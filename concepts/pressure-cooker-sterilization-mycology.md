# Pressure Cooker Sterilization for Mycology

[[sterilization]] is the most critical step in [[antimicrobial-compounds-mushroom-mycelium]] and ruin the crop. The pressure cooker (autoclave) is the most accessible and effective sterilization tool for home and small-scale cultivators, achieving temperatures high enough to kill bacterial endospores that survive boiling.

## Why Pressure Sterilization Is Necessary

[[bacillus-subtilis]]* form extremely heat-resistant spores that can survive hours of boiling at 100°C. These are the primary target of pressure sterilization.
- **Mold spores**: Fungal contaminants including *Trichoderma*, *Aspergillus*, *Penicillium*, and *Cladosporium* produce abundant airborne spores that readily colonize exposed substrates.
- **Yeast**: Wild yeasts can rapidly colonize nutrient-rich substrates, consuming nutrients and creating conditions unfavorable for mushroom mycelium.
- **Insect eggs and larvae**: Some substrate contaminants include tiny insects or their eggs that survive non-pressurized treatments.

Boiling water at atmospheric pressure (100°C/212°F) is insufficient to kill bacterial endospores. A pressure cooker raises the boiling point of water by increasing pressure, typically achieving temperatures of 121°C (250°F) at 15 psi — the standard sterilization temperature used in laboratory autoclaves.

## Pressure Cooker Specifications for Mycology

### Minimum Requirements
- **Pressure rating**: Must reach at least 15 psi (103 kPa) above atmospheric pressure. This produces steam at approximately 121°C (250°F), the standard autoclave temperature.
- **Size**: Must accommodate the substrate containers. For the boil-a-bag method, the cooker needs to fit 2 bags at a time (approximately 4-6 liter capacity minimum).
- **Materials**: Stainless steel is preferred for durability and corrosion resistance. Aluminum cookers work but may pit over time from acidic substrates.

### Recommended Features
- **Weighted pressure regulator (jiggler)**: Simple, reliable, and doesn't require calibration. Maintains constant pressure without electricity.
- **Pressure gauge**: Allows verification that the correct pressure is being maintained. Not essential but very helpful.
- **Gasket seal**: Creates the airtight seal necessary for pressure buildup. Must be maintained in good condition.
- **Rack or trivet**: Keeps bags off the bottom of the cooker to prevent scorching and allows steam circulation.

### Popular Models
- **Presto 23-quart**: The most popular pressure canner for mycology. Large enough for 4-10 quart jars or 2-3 substrate bags. Affordable and widely available.
- **All-American**: Heavy-duty, gasketless design with metal-to-metal seal. More expensive but extremely durable and reliable.
- **Instant Pot**: Electric pressure cookers can work for small batches but typically only reach 10-12 psi. The "sterilize" mode on newer models may reach higher pressures. Generally not recommended for serious cultivation due to limited size and pressure.

## The Sterilization Process

### Pre-Sterilization Preparation

1. **Clean the pressure cooker**: Wash thoroughly with soap and water. Check the gasket for cracks or deformation.
2. **Add water**: Place 1-2 inches of water in the bottom of the cooker. Some methods use a towel at the bottom to prevent bag contact with the metal.
3. **Load substrate bags**: Place filled, sealed bags in the cooker. Do not overpack — steam must be able to circulate around all surfaces.
4. **Ensure bag sealing**: Bags should be sealed with the filter can assembly in place and secured with tape. Ensure the filter can is positioned so that the top (filter side) is accessible to steam penetration.

### Running the Sterilization Cycle

The Jarrold method specifies sterilization at maximum pressure (15 psi) for 60 minutes. This can be done in two 30-minute stages if preferred:

1. **Heat up phase**: Place the cooker on the stove over high heat. It will take 15-30 minutes for steam to build and the pressure regulator to begin rocking (or the gauge to reach 15 psi).
2. **Sterilization phase**: Once 15 psi is reached, reduce heat to maintain steady pressure (the regulator should rock gently, about 1-4 times per minute). Begin timing the 60-minute sterilization period.
3. **Cool down phase**: After 60 minutes, turn off the heat and allow the cooker to depressurize naturally. Do not use the quick-release valve or run cold water over the cooker — rapid depressurization can cause bags to burst due to the pressure difference between the hot interior and cooler exterior.
4. **Wait for full depressurization**: The pressure indicator (or gauge) must return to zero before opening. This typically takes 30-60 minutes after turning off the heat.
5. **Remove bags**: Open the cooker carefully (steam will escape). Remove bags using clean or gloved hands and transfer to the inoculation area.

## Critical Points for Success

### Avoiding Bag Blowouts
The most common problem during pressure sterilization of bags is bags bursting or popping:

- **Do not seal bags airtight**: Bags must allow some steam penetration while preventing contamination. The filter can assembly serves this purpose.
- **Leave headspace**: Do not fill bags completely. Leave room for the filter can and for substrate expansion during heating.
- **Do not rapid-cool**: Allow natural depressurization. Rapid cooling creates a vacuum that can collapse or burst bags.
- **Use appropriate bags**: Only use bags rated for boiling/pressure cooking (polypropylene). Standard polyethylene bags will melt.

### Ensure Complete Sterilization

- **Verify pressure**: Make sure the regulator is actively rocking and the gauge reads 15 psi throughout the sterilization period. If pressure drops below target, increase heat slightly. If pressure exceeds target, reduce heat. Consistent pressure is more important than exact timing.
- **Check water level**: Ensure the cooker does not boil dry. A dry cooker can damage the equipment and create a fire hazard. Most cookers have a minimum water line marked inside. If unsure, add extra water at the start.
- **Timer accuracy**: Use a separate timer rather than relying on memory or the stove clock. Under-sterilization is one of the most common causes of contamination in home cultivation. Set the timer only after the cooker has reached full pressure.
- **Positioning**: Ensure bags are not pressed tightly against each other or the cooker walls. Steam must reach all surfaces of the substrate for effective sterilization. Use the rack or trivet to elevate bags off the bottom.
