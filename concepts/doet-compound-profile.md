# DOET (HECATE) — 2,5-Dimethoxy-4-Ethylamphetamine

## Overview

DOET (PIHKAL #66), also known by the code name HECATE, is a psychedelic amphetamine in the DOx family. The 4-ethyl substituent makes DOET less potent but reportedly more empathogenic than its methyl and halogenated siblings. Shulgin noted that the ethyl group imparts a "warmer" quality to the experience, bridging the character of the DOx psychedelics with the more connective qualities of compounds like [[mdma-compound-profile]].

## Chemical Identity

- **Chemical name:** 2,5-dimethoxy-4-ethylamphetamine
- **PIHKAL number:** #66
- **Code name:** HECATE
- **Class:** Substituted amphetamine (DOx family)
- **HCl melting point:** 190-191 °C (194-195 °C recrystallized)

## Dosage and Duration

| Parameter | Range |
|-----------|-------|
| **Threshold** | ~2 mg |
| **Common dose** | 2-6 mg |
| **Strong dose** | 6-10 mg |
| **Duration** | 14-20 hours |
| **Onset** | 1-3 hours |

## Effects

- Classic psychedelic visuals with enhanced color perception
- Notable empathogenic qualities — more "connected" feeling than other DOx compounds
- Introspective and philosophical thought patterns
- Moderate stimulant effects
- Less intense body load than [[doi-compound-profile]] or [[doc-compound-profile]]
- Long duration characteristic of the DOx family

## Synthesis (from PIHKAL)

Prepared from 2,5-dimethoxy-4-ethylbenzaldehyde via nitrostyrene condensation with nitroethane and subsequent LAH reduction. The aldehyde precursor is made from asarone through ethylation and Vilsmeier formylation.

## Pharmacology

The ethyl group at the 4-position provides moderate lipophilicity and receptor affinity, placing DOET between [[dom-compound-profile]] (methyl) and higher homologues in potency. The larger alkyl chain may influence receptor conformation in ways that promote serotonergic effects associated with empathy and emotional openness. See [[phenethylamine-receptor-pharmacology]].

## Experience Reports (from PIHKAL)

The PIHKAL entries for DOET document a wide range of subjective experiences across a 1.0–7.0 mg dosage spectrum:

**At 1.0 mg:** "Very gentle, relaxing level, no psychedelic effects apparent. Easy, relaxed. But cramps and irritability completely knocked out." — A predominantly physical effect at the lowest dose tested, with mood stabilization but no perceptual changes.

**At 2.5 mg:** "Much, too much, movement with eyes closed. Awful lot with eyes open. Movement on concrete floor was too much — almost sea-sick. Reality problems — cannot find centering point of reference. Legs twitching, crawling sensation. In the morning still ++. This is definitely not my thing." — This dose produced significant discomfort and vestibular disturbance, suggesting individual sensitivity varies considerably.

**At 4 mg:** "Surprised by awareness of effects — had forgotten I took something. At second hour, real but subtle. As a psychotomimetic or STP-like thing, very little there. But as a mood energizer, really a ++ or more. Worked at cleaning up the office until 11 PM. Slept well. None of the LSD or STP seriousness." — An unexpectedly functional experience, with productive energy rather than classical psychedelic effects.

**At 6 mg:** "Onset slow and subtle. Effects fully there in about three hours. Everything smelled was vivid, all colors and shapes clean, beautiful, serenely self-contained. No visual movement. Eyes-closed fantasy images extremely rich. No dark corners. Might be possible to be creative with this, no body depletion." — A rich, contemplative psychedelic experience without the intensity or body burden typical of the DOx family.

**At 7 mg:** "Unbelievably lovely erotic-to-divine, deep loving, open, not much visual, eyes-closed form-image-symbol. Intellect and feeling-emotion area intact at all times. Next morning still at plus one. Incredible material. Perhaps best at 6 to 7 milligrams, no higher due to body load." — Described by Shulgin as perhaps the optimal range, combining emotional depth with cognitive clarity.

## Naming History

DOET's original Shulgin code was **DOE** (DO for desoxy + E for ethyl). However, this created a conflict: DOE was already an established abbreviation for desoxyephedrine, which is methamphetamine. To resolve this ambiguity, the code was changed to **DOET**.

Interestingly, the same logic would have required [[dom-compound-profile]] to become **DOME**. But by the time the naming inconsistency was recognized, DOM had already become too firmly established in both the scientific literature and popular usage to justify a change. The STP episode (see [[dom-compound-profile]]) had cemented the DOM name in the public consciousness. See [[pihkal-introduction-and-methodology]] for more on Shulgin's coding system and [[phenethylamine-chemistry-basics]] for the chemical naming conventions underlying the DOx series.

## Comparison with DOM

DOET and [[dom-compound-profile]] are immediate structural homologues, differing only at the 4-position substituent (ethyl vs. methyl). Despite this small change, several notable differences emerge from the PIHKAL reports:

- **Potency:** DOM is approximately 2-3x more potent by weight than DOET, consistent with the general trend that smaller 4-position substituents increase activity in the DOx series. See [[shulgin-dosage-guidelines]] for comparative dosing.
- **Character:** DOM produces more intense visual effects and stronger stimulant properties. DOET is described as warmer, more relaxed, and less likely to produce anxiety or visual overwhelm.
- **Body load:** Both compounds share the long-acting body burden typical of DOx compounds, but DOET may be somewhat gentler at equivalent effect levels.
- **Duration:** Both compounds share the characteristic 14-20+ hour DOx duration.
- **Abuse potential:** Shulgin noted that while DOM's original publications "clearly documented the compound as being a psychedelic with sizeable potential for abuse," the studies of DOET "at lowest levels" showed "no disturbing hints of abusability." This suggests the ethyl substituent may reduce the compulsive redosing drive.

The 4-position substituent comparison extends across the full DOx series: [[doi-compound-profile]] (iodo, most potent), [[doc-compound-profile]] (chloro), DOM (methyl), and DOET (ethyl). The related [[2c-e-compound-profile]] family compounds follow similar SAR principles — see that page for the phenethylamine (non-amphetamine) analogue with the same ethyl substitution.

## Harm Reduction

- Standard DOx precautions apply — long duration (14-20 hours), plan accordingly
- Milligram-scale accuracy required
- Never redose due to long onset period
- Less commonly encountered than DOM or DOI, but same safety principles apply
- See [[dom-compound-profile]] for comprehensive DOx harm reduction guidance
- Individual sensitivity appears to vary significantly — start at the low end of the dosage range
- The 6-7 mg range appears optimal for full effects; doses above this increase body load without proportional benefit
- Do not combine with other serotonergic substances due to risk of serotonin syndrome
