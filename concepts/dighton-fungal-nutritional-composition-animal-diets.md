## Digestibility Constraints

Despite favorable nutrient profiles, much of the nitrogen in fungi occurs in
complex forms within indigestible cell walls (Cork and Kenagy, 1989a).
Animals require complex communities of gut symbionts to effectively break
down and access fungal nutrients. Experiments with ground squirrels showed
weight decline when fed entirely on fungal fruit bodies, likely due to these
digestibility limitations.

Lichen hemicellulose contains xylan and lichen starch in beta-1-4 and
beta-1-3 glucoside linkages. Mathiesen et al. (2000) suggest this composition
induces increased bacterial fermentation in the reindeer gut, resulting in
development of food-absorptive papillae on the gut wall. This morphological
adaptation enables reindeer to subsist on an otherwise difficult-to-digest
lichen diet during winter months.

## Comparison with Plant Food Sources

Compared to available plant parts, many fungi have similar food value but
less fat content for herbivorous small mammals. This nutritional profile
positions fungi as complementary food sources that provide protein and
minerals that may be limiting in herbivorous diets, particularly during
seasons when fresh plant material is unavailable.

The lower fat content of fungi compared to seeds and nuts means that
mycophagous animals may need to supplement fungal diets with higher-energy
food sources during periods of high metabolic demand. This may explain why
many animals consume fungi as part of a mixed diet rather than exclusively.
