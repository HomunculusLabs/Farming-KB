## Cosmic Life Force Beyond Biology

Fukuoka extends his argument into a cosmic framework. If living
things arose from nonliving things, there is no sharp dividing
line between them: "If the living possess life, then so do the
nonliving." Biological evolution cannot be discussed without
considering "the colossal evolution of all things in the natural
world." The source of cosmic life "permeates nonliving and
living things alike" and could be called the "Holy Spirit" or
ultimately "God." Nucleic acids are "not the cause or the source
of life, but just a manifestation of the result."

## Critique of Scientific Authority

Fukuoka argues that scientists "abuse their authority" by
commenting on natural phenomena they cannot truly understand.
"Although it may be permissible for scientists to report natural
events as they occur, they have no right to comment or criticize."

Scientists today are "under the illusion that they can create
perfect organisms," when in fact "human actions and knowledge are
effective only within a narrow range and produce anti-natural
effects." Regardless of how superior artificial organisms may
appear, "these can only be anti-natural progeny" leading to
"karmic tragedy." Anti-natural things are invariably incomplete
and will either meet with failure or proceed along a lonely path.

## Connection to Insect Rice Breeding

This hypothesis emerged from Fukuoka's observation that insects in
his fields play a role in creating new rice varieties through
natural cross-pollination. Grasshoppers, locusts, and crickets
bite holes in rice husks more skillfully than he could with
scissors. Snails, pill bugs, slugs, and cutworms eat the anthers,
allowing cross-pollination between neighboring heads.

This led Fukuoka to conclude that "what we think of as crop pests
have all along been genetically upgrading rice, playing in effect
a role in advancing the evolution of rice." In his unplowed,
pesticide-free field of thirty-five years, grain created by
insects may ripen in autumn and fall naturally to the ground,
passing the winter hidden and safe. By adding a little help to
this rice, Fukuoka got about a hundred new rice lines.

## Darwin as Temporary Hypothesis

Fukuoka concludes that "Darwin's theory, then, never really
amounted to more than a temporary hypothesis." While it has
helped clarify biogenetic lineage showing what organisms arose
and vanished from the distant past to the present, "the
theoretical analysis is a biased West European view that
misreads the true state of the natural world."

He believes it has "dealt the order of human society a blow of
incalculable force over the past hundred years" by providing a
framework where humans discern superiority and inferiority in
nature and accept these as facts of life.
