# Intermediate Species Sink-or-Float Hypothesis

Fukuoka's alternative to Darwinian gradualism proposes that when two
different organisms cross, they produce tens to hundreds of offspring,
each differing slightly from the others. Because they have continuous
traits, they form what Fukuoka calls a "discontinuous continuity."
Most intermediate species disappear, while only organisms at either
extreme or which are special in some way survive.

This hypothesis was developed to explain the conspicuous absence of
transitional fossils in the geological record. Rather than positing
that transitional forms never existed, Fukuoka argues they existed
in vast numbers but were not preserved or are not visible to the
methods of conventional science.

## The Ocean and Snowfall Analogies

Fukuoka offers two vivid analogies for why transitional fossils are
missing. First, all organisms are continuous on the ocean floor,
invisible to man, who sees only the islands floating above. The
great majority of potential species disappear "under the surface
of the sea." Second, a heavy snowfall buries all but the largest
rocks and trees; what lies buried remains invisible. Both
emphasize that absence of evidence is not evidence of absence.

## Why Intermediate Species Disappear

Fukuoka identifies multiple distinct reasons for the disappearance
of intermediate organisms, noting that the process is neither
directed nor purposeful:

- Many species disappear before having had a chance to establish
  themselves at all, perishing in early developmental stages
- Others that have established themselves later fall out of sorts
  with changing environmental conditions
- In some cases, a lack of fertility may prevent reproduction,
  cutting off the line entirely
- Offspring may be conceived but fail to thrive due to genetic
  incompatibilities or developmental failures
- Competition with more specialized forms at either extreme may
  squeeze out the intermediates

The process is, in Fukuoka's view, essentially "accidental." Only
a small number of the great body of potential species have managed
to survive and establish themselves on the face of the earth.

## Rejection of Darwinian Selection

Fukuoka explicitly rejects each pillar of Darwin's theory. Natural
selection through adaptation is a misreading: "When acacia seeds
fall thickly to the ground, which seeds will survive? The intellect
cannot tell whether it is the organism that selects nature or
nature that winnows the organisms."

"The question of superiority and inferiority, of suitability and
unsuitability, never existed in the natural world." These are
"near-sighted conclusions arrived at through the relativistic
views of man." "To the human observer, the lion appears stronger
than the rabbit, but nature most likely sees no such difference
in strength." From nature's vantage point, "nothing is in
conflict" and "no distinctions are made, such as between insect
pests and beneficial insects."

## Cosmic Life Force Beyond Biology

Fukuoka extends his argument into a cosmic framework. If living
things arose from nonliving things, there is no sharp dividing
line between them: "If the living possess life, then so do the
nonliving." Biological evolution cannot be discussed without
considering "the colossal evolution of all things in the natural
world." The source of cosmic life "permeates nonliving and
living things alike" and could be called the "Holy Spirit" or
ultimately "God." Nucleic acids are "not the cause or the source
of life, but just a manifestation of the result."

## Critique of Scientific Authority

Fukuoka argues that scientists "abuse their authority" by
commenting on natural phenomena they cannot truly understand.
"Although it may be permissible for scientists to report natural
events as they occur, they have no right to comment or criticize."

Scientists today are "under the illusion that they can create
perfect organisms," when in fact "human actions and knowledge are
effective only within a narrow range and produce anti-natural
effects." Regardless of how superior artificial organisms may
appear, "these can only be anti-natural progeny" leading to
"karmic tragedy." Anti-natural things are invariably incomplete
and will either meet with failure or proceed along a lonely path.

## Connection to Insect Rice Breeding

This hypothesis emerged from Fukuoka's observation that insects in
his fields play a role in creating new rice varieties through
natural cross-pollination. Grasshoppers, locusts, and crickets
bite holes in rice husks more skillfully than he could with
scissors. Snails, pill bugs, slugs, and cutworms eat the anthers,
allowing cross-pollination between neighboring heads.

This led Fukuoka to conclude that "what we think of as crop pests
have all along been genetically upgrading rice, playing in effect
a role in advancing the evolution of rice." In his unplowed,
pesticide-free field of thirty-five years, grain created by
insects may ripen in autumn and fall naturally to the ground,
passing the winter hidden and safe. By adding a little help to
this rice, Fukuoka got about a hundred new rice lines.

## Darwin as Temporary Hypothesis

Fukuoka concludes that "Darwin's theory, then, never really
amounted to more than a temporary hypothesis." While it has
helped clarify biogenetic lineage showing what organisms arose
and vanished from the distant past to the present, "the
theoretical analysis is a biased West European view that
misreads the true state of the natural world."

He believes it has "dealt the order of human society a blow of
incalculable force over the past hundred years" by providing a
framework where humans discern superiority and inferiority in
nature and accept these as facts of life.
