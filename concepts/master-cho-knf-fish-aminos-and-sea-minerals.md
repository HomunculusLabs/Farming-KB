# Master Cho's Fish Aminos and Sea Minerals

## Fish Amino Acid (FAA)

Fish Amino Acid is a liquid made from fish waste, of great value to both plants and microorganisms. It contains abundant nutrients and various amino acids. FAA is nitrogen-rich and enhances crop growth during vegetative periods.

### Blue-Back Fish

Blue-back fishes are the preferred material: mackerel, Spanish mackerel, horse mackerel, saury, garfish, herring, yellowtail, sardine, salmon, sweetfish, bluefin tuna, skipjack tuna, and anchovy. They contain excellent proteins, amino acids, and fatty acids including DHA and EPA (unsaturated fatty acids that lower cholesterol and prevent adult diseases).

Blue-back fish also contain abundant vitamins: Vitamin A (prevents night-blindness, strengthens immune system), Vitamin B complex (prevents anemia and beriberi, helps cell regeneration), and Vitamin E (slows aging by blocking peroxidized lipids). These effects are also excellent in plants.

### How to Make FAA

**Tools**: Clay jar, Japanese cedar box, or PE container; mosquito net, rubber band.

**Materials**:
- Blue-back fish waste (head, bone, intestines) -- fresh only, never salted
- Brown sugar (1:1 weight ratio with fish waste)
- IMO #4, OHN, Mineral A, rice straw

**Environment**: 23-25 deg C, cool and shaded, minimal environmental change.

**Method**:
1. Put fish waste and brown sugar in clay jar at 1:1 weight ratio. Fill to 2/3 of jar volume.
2. Pile fish waste and brown sugar in alternating layers. More sugar near the top. Add a little IMO #4 with brown sugar to decompose fish oil and meat.
3. Scatter IMO #4 on top and cover with rice straws. Adding OHN and Mineral A increases effectiveness.
4. Cover opening with mosquito net, lay sticks on rim, cover with lid (leaving air gap).
5. In 3-4 days, fish waste begins liquidizing through osmotic pressure and fermentation.
6. Takes at least 6 months for well-fermented FAA. Matured FAA has a smell; extract only the liquid.

**Container installation hint**: Put various stones 10cm high on the bottom, then ingredients. Cover with mosquito net. Do not seal -- air must flow through. This allows FAA to contain Mineral A and acts as a built-in filter.

### How to Use FAA

- **Basic dilution**: 1:1,000 with water.
- Rich in nitrogen; good fertilizer for both soil and foliage during vegetative growth when used with other KNF inputs.
- Activates microorganisms when added to IMO or mixed compost (after diluting).
- For leafy vegetables, can be used continuously to increase yield, taste, and fragrance.
- **Not recommended during reproductive growth** -- may induce overgrowth.
- Fish oil from fermentation can be used as insect repellent (e.g., army worm moths become helpless at the slightest contact).
- Mackerel FAA is effective against mites and greenhouse whitefly -- dilute and spray on both leaf sides.
- Bones left from FAA production can be put in brown rice vinegar (BRV) at 1:10 ratio to produce water-soluble calcium phosphate.

## Seawater and Fermented Seawater

Fish live off seawater microbes. The surface 3cm of seawater swarms with microbes. Deeper seawater has higher salt content, so surface seawater is preferred. For storage, pour seawater in a large bowl and leave for a day to let airborne microbes enter -- this develops fermented seawater.

### Making Fermented Seawater

Mix diluted seawater (x1/30) with rice-washed water (x1/200) and FPJ of pear, Japanese mugwort, and dropwort (x1/500). Keep for a half day in summer (2-3 days if cloudy). Use when it becomes whitish like haze.

Fermented seawater cannot be stored -- when it goes bad, the haze gathers like a cotton ball. Use immediately when whitish.

### Why Fermented Seawater Works

The combination of land microbes with sea minerals and microbes creates an effect similar to a brackish zone where freshwater meets seawater. Brackish water provides favorable conditions for microbes, supporting plankton and fish. This combination provides abundant energy to crops and livestock.

Rice-washed water acts like yeast. Adding FPJ and OHN brings out the best properties of seawater.

### How to Use Seawater and Fermented Seawater

- **Increasing fruit sugar content**: Apply seawater with water-soluble calcium 15-20 days before harvest.
- **Seedling treatment** (onions, leeks, garlic): Apply seawater (x1/20) with FPJ and BRV. Salt water dilute to 0.2%.
- **Preventing chicken feather-loss in summer**: Provide seawater (x1/10 to x1/15, or salt water 0.1%), BRV (x1/200), and water-soluble calcium (x1/500) every other day.
- **Anthracnose control**: Add liquid IMO #4, OHN, and water-soluble calcium phosphate to fermented seawater for increased microbial activity.
- **Enhancing ripening**: Use seawater at x1/30, x1/25, or x1/20.
- **Hot weather and tropical nights**: Fermented seawater is particularly effective.
- **Fungal disease control**: Controls scab, rust, and anthracnose.
