### Mycorrhizal Responses to Elevated CO₂

Effects differ among mycorrhizal fungal species:
- **Klironomos et al. (1997, 1998)**: Two *Glomus* species increased plant growth under elevated CO₂, while *Acaulospora denticulata* and *Scutellospora calospora* caused no growth difference
- **Sanders et al. (1998)**: No increased P uptake despite increased AM hyphal extension under elevated CO₂
- Increased hyphal mass contributes to below-ground carbon stores and soil aggregation

### Glomalin and Carbon Sequestration

- AM fungi produce the glycoprotein **glomalin** (Wright and Upadhyaya, 1996)
- Glomalin may account for **30-60%** of carbon in undisturbed soils (Treseder and Allen, 2000)
- Glomalin content increased in grassland soils under long-term elevated CO₂ (Rillig et al., 2000)
- This carbon is protected in soil aggregates -- a potential long-term carbon sink
