# Inactive Probes and Pharmacological Falsification in PiHKAL

**Updated:** 2026-04-18

## Overview

Throughout PiHKAL, Shulgin documented numerous compounds that proved inactive or minimally active in human trials. Far from being failures, these "negative results" served as critical pharmacological probes that helped define the boundaries of psychoactivity and, in several cases, revealed unexpected drug interactions.

## The Concept of the Inactive Probe

An inactive probe is a compound that produces no significant subjective effects at reasonable dosages, yet can still modify the activity of other drugs. These compounds are pharmacologically valuable because they help distinguish between different receptor mechanisms and can reveal synergistic or antagonistic interactions that would otherwise go unnoticed.

## Key Inactive Probes

### MDPR (N-propyl-MDA)
The paradigmatic inactive probe. At up to 180 mg, MDPR showed no standalone psychedelic activity. However, at this "inactive" level, it proved to be "a rather fantastic enhancer of LSD action." This paradox -- a compound that does nothing alone but dramatically potentiates another drug -- suggests that MDPR binds to a relevant receptor site without activating it, but in a way that modifies the receptor's response to LSD.

### MDAL (N-allyl-MDA)
The structural near-twin of MDPR (differing only by having an unsaturated allyl group rather than a saturated propyl chain). Despite being inactive on its own at up to 180 mg, MDAL "not only does not enhance, but actually seems to somewhat antagonize the action of LSD." The fact that MDPR enhances LSD while MDAL antagonizes it -- with only a couple of hydrogen atoms difference -- demonstrates extraordinary sensitivity of these interactions to minor structural changes.

### MDBU (N-butyl-MDA)
Inactive at up to 40 mg. Part of a consistent pattern: N-alkyl chains longer than two carbons on MDA produce inactive compounds.

### MDMC (ethylenedioxy-N-methyl-MDMA)
Required 200+ mg for threshold effects. Despite extensive testing, "there was no way of guessing what an active level might be, or how consistent that level might be between different people."

### SB (Symbescaline)
Inactive at up to 240 mg. Shulgin used this as an example of the difficulty of assigning precise thresholds: "One cannot say that it is inactive, for there might well be something at 400 or 800 or 1200 milligrams."

## The Ambiguity of "Inactive"

Shulgin was careful about declaring compounds inactive. His discussion of SB illustrates the problem:

"There was no convincing evidence for much effect at levels that would clearly be active for mescaline. This is the kind of result that puts some potentially ambiguous numbers in the literature. One cannot say that it is inactive, for there might well be something at 400 or 800 or 1200 milligrams. But since it has been tried only up to 240 milligrams, I have used the phrase that the activity is greater than 240 milligrams. This will be interpreted by some people as saying that it is active, but only at dosages higher than 240 milligrams. What is meant, is that there was no activity observed at the highest level tried."

## Falsification of Theoretical Predictions

Several compounds falsified theoretical predictions about psychedelic activity:

### TA (2,3,4,5-tetramethoxyamphetamine)
Shulgin noted: "The structure of this molecule has everything going for it. It is an overlay of TMA (active) and TMA-2 (even more active) so it is completely reasonable that it should be doing something at a rational dosage." But activity was not convincingly demonstrated even at 35 mg, with only "definite threshold" and "foul headache" reported. This contradicted the simple structural additivity model.

### The 3,4-DMA Catecholamine Hypothesis
The expectation that 3,4-substituted compounds should be the most psychedelic (because dopamine, norepinephrine, and epinephrine all have 3,4-substitution) was not supported by the data. 3,4-DMA required "a few hundred milligrams" for any effect, while 2,5-DMA (with supposedly "less interesting" substitution) served as a precursor to highly potent compounds like DOB.

### IDNNA Fluorination
The expectation that radio-fluorination would work as well as radio-iodination was falsified: iodination gave 40% isotope incorporation while fluorination gave only 2%.

## Scientific Value of Negative Results

Shulgin's careful documentation of negative results serves several scientific purposes:
1. They prevent duplication of effort by future researchers
2. They define the boundaries of the structure-activity relationship
3. They reveal unexpected interactions (MDPR/LSD enhancement, MDAL/LSD antagonism)
4. They challenge overly simple theoretical models
5. They demonstrate the extraordinary sensitivity of biological systems to minor structural changes
