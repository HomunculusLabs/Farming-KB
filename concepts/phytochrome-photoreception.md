## Phytochrome Photoreception

Phytochromes are a family of photoreceptor proteins [[cannabis-seed-germination-methods]] to flowering time, and enable plants to
sense the quality, quantity, and duration of light in their environment.

## Molecular Structure

Phytochromes exist as dimers, each monomer consisting of two major domains. The
N-terminal photosensory module contains a covalently attached chromophore
called phytochromobilin, which is a linear tetrapyrrole molecule structurally
related to the heme group found in hemoglobin. This chromophore absorbs light
and undergoes a conformational change, switching the protein between two
stable states.

The C-terminal output module contains a histidine kinase-related domain and
two PAS domains. In some phytochromes, this module functions as a light-
regulated serine/threonine kinase, though the full signaling mechanism remains
an area of active research.

## Pr and Pfr States

Phytochromes interconvert between two photoisomers. The Pr form absorbs red
light (around 660 nm) and converts to the Pfr form. The Pfr form absorbs
far-red light (around 730 nm) and converts back to Pr. Pfr is generally
considered the biologically active form, though both states participate in
signaling cascades.

This reversible switching allows plants to act as sophisticated light sensors.
Under canopy shade, the ratio of red to far-red light drops because chlorophyll
in overlying leaves absorbs red light but transmits far-red. Phytochromes
detect this shift and trigger [[phototropism-and-shade-avoidance]] responses, including stem
elongation, accelerated flowering, and reduced leaf expansion.

## Ecological Significance

Phytochrome-mediated shade avoidance is one of the most ecologically important
plant behaviors. Plants growing in dense stands compete for light, and the
ability to detect neighbors through red/far-red ratios provides a competitive
advantage. This system explains why weeds growing at the edge of a crop row
often elongate dramatically and reproduce earlier than those in open fields.

The phytochrome system also enables plants to detect changes in canopy
architecture over time, adjusting their growth strategies in response to the
competitive environment. This informational capacity represents a form of
environmental awareness that blurs the line between simple stimulus response
and more complex sensory processing.

## Light Quality and Photomorphogenesis

Beyond simply detecting light quantity, phytochromes encode information about
the spectral composition of ambient light. Sunlight contains a relatively high
ratio of red to far-red light, while light filtered through vegetation is
enriched in far-red wavelengths. Phytochromes can distinguish between these
different light environments and activate distinct developmental programs
accordingly.

This ability to assess light quality enables plants to optimize their growth
strategy for the specific light environment they occupy. Under direct
sunlight, plants invest in thicker leaves and shorter stems, while under
shade they elongate rapidly and produce thinner leaves to maximize light
