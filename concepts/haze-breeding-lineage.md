# Haze Breeding Lineage
The Haze family represents arguably the most influential [[cannabis-sativa-breeding-evolution]] lineage
in cannabis history. Originating in Northern California in the 1960s and 1970s,
Haze became the genetic backbone for countless modern sativa-dominant hybrids
and remains a benchmark for psychedelic, cerebral cannabis effects. The story of
Haze is fundamentally the story of how a small group of dedicated breeders
combined multiple [[african-landrace-sativa-modern-hybrids]] varieties to create something entirely new.
## Origins in Santa Cruz and Hayward
The creation of Haze is generally attributed to a group of breeders known as
the Haze Brothers working in the Santa Cruz Mountains and Hayward area of
Northern California during the late 1960s and early 1970s. The breeders were
working outdoors, as [[cannabis-sativa-indoor-cultivation-techniques]] technology was not yet developed. Their
goal was to create a sativa-dominant hybrid that combined the best
characteristics of several premium landrace varieties available at the time.
The [[cervantes-breeding-program-design-goal-setting]] involved multiple generations of selection and
stabilization, eventually producing a line that exhibited extraordinary potency,
complex aromatics, and a distinctive psychoactive profile that was unlike
anything that had come before in the cannabis world.
## Parental Landrace Genetics
The foundational cross that created Haze combined genetics from four major
landrace regions. Colombian Gold contributed structure, yield potential, and
sweet citrus notes. A Mexican sativa, variously described as Oaxacan or
Acapulco, added [[cannabis-glandular-trichome-types-resin-production-clarke]] and earthy undertones. Thai genetics provided
the intense psychoactive head high that became Haze's signature characteristic.
A South Indian or South Asian variety rounded out the cross, contributing
additional flowering vigor and complexity to the terpene profile. The
combination of these four geographically diverse gene pools created
extraordinary hybrid vigor but also extreme variability in the offspring.
## The Haze Phenotype
True Haze exhibits a distinctive set of morphological and chemical
characteristics. The plants grow tall with elongated internodes, producing
sparse but extremely resinous flowers. The buds are often described as
foxtailed, long cylindrical calyx formations that can appear airy compared
to dense indica buds but are loaded with trichomes. The aroma profile
typically combines sweet, spicy, citrus, and earthy notes with an underlying
pungency. The effect is profoundly cerebral, often described as psychedelic,
uplifting, and creatively stimulating. The flowering period is extremely long,
typically 12-16 weeks or more, which has historically limited commercial
viability but contributed to its legendary status among dedicated growers.
## Cultural Impact and Spread to Europe
Haze gained cult status in the 1970s and 1980s among cannabis connoisseurs,
particularly in Northern California. The Rev recalls that he never even heard
the word Haze until the mid-1980s but immediately recognized it as a Southeast
Asian type with an intense and wicked powerful high. As Dutch seed companies
gained prominence in the 1980s and 1990s, Haze genetics traveled to the
Netherlands where they were further developed. Neville Schoenmaker of
Neville's Seeds and the breeders at Green House Seed Co. were instrumental in
stabilizing and popularizing Haze for the European market.
## Major Haze-Descended Strains
The Haze lineage has spawned an enormous family of descendant strains. Super
Silver Haze, a cross of Haze, Skunk, and Northern Lights, won multiple
Cannabis Cup awards. Amnesia Haze combines Haze with Southeast Asian and
Jamaican genetics and became one of the best-selling strains in European
coffee shops. Amnesika 2.0 from Philosopher Seeds represents a further
refinement, crossing Super Silver Haze with G-13 Haze and Skunk. Mango Haze
from Mr. Nice Seedbank combines Haze with Northern Lights #5, producing a
tropical-flavored sativa with improved yield characteristics for commercial
growers seeking Haze-like effects with more manageable cultivation requirements.
## Indoor Adaptation Challenges
One of the primary challenges breeders have worked to address with Haze
genetics is their unsuitability for indoor cultivation. The extreme height,
long flowering period, and sensitivity to environmental conditions make pure
Haze difficult for most indoor growers. Various strategies have been employed
to adapt Haze for indoor environments: crossing with indica varieties to
reduce height and flowering time, using Low Stress Training (LST) and Screen
of Green (ScrOG) techniques to manage canopy height, and selecting for
autoflowering variants. Royal Queen Seeds recommends packing as much light
as possible into grow rooms for Amnesia Haze, with indoor yields of
approximately 600 grams per square yard under 600 watts of lighting.
## Haze in Modern Breeding Programs
Contemporary breeders continue to work with Haze genetics, though the
approach has shifted from preserving pure Haze lines to incorporating Haze
traits into more commercially viable hybrids. The goal is typically to
capture the cerebral high, complex terpenes, and visual appeal of Haze
while reducing flowering time and improving yield. Breeders like Bodhi Seeds,
Sannie's Seeds, and Dynasty Seeds have been particularly active in this
space. The enduring appeal of Haze lies in its unique psychoactive profile.
Many experienced consumers report that no other cannabis variety replicates
the specific combination of euphoria, creativity, and perceptual enhancement
that authentic Haze genetics provide.
