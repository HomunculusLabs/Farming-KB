## Overview

[[contamination-prevention-in-mushroom-cultivation]], "aborts" are mushroom primordia (baby mushrooms) that
stop growing and begin to deteriorate before reaching maturity. The PF Tek
method, developed by Robert McPherson (Psilocybe Fanaticus), specifically
recommended harvesting and consuming aborts, noting their reportedly high
potency relative to their small size.

The phenomenon of concentrated alkaloids in aborted mushrooms raises
interesting questions about fungal biochemistry and the dynamics of
[[baeocystin-and-norbaeocystin-in-psilocybin-biosynthesis]] during [[bloomfield-mushroom-formation-fruiting-body-development-cultivation]].

## What Are Aborts?

Aborts are mushroom primordia that have initiated development but have ceased
growing for various reasons. They are easily identified by their appearance:
they remain small, typically the size of a pinhead or small pea, and their
caps often darken or become discolored as the tissue begins to break down.

Several factors can cause aborts in a PF Tek cultivation:

**Insufficient moisture** at the substrate surface prevents primordia from
accessing the water needed for cellular expansion and growth. This is one of
the most common causes, particularly [[psilocin-emergence-in-later-flushes]] when substrate moisture
has been depleted.

**High carbon dioxide levels** in the growing environment can inhibit normal
fruiting body development. Mushrooms require fresh air exchange to support
metabolic processes, and elevated CO2 levels can cause primordia to stall.

**Genetic factors** mean that not every primordium will develop into a mature
mushroom. Cultures typically produce more primordia than the substrate can
support to maturity, and some are naturally selected out through abortion.

**Physical damage** to developing primordia, whether from handling, falling
condensation, or contact with other mushrooms, can trigger developmental
arrest and subsequent abortion.

## The Potency Claim

The PF Tek documentation asserts that aborts are significantly more potent
than mature mushrooms on a weight-for-weight basis. This claim is based on
McPherson's extensive personal experience and reports from the cultivation
community, rather than on controlled chemical analysis.

The reported potency difference is substantial — some sources claim that
aborts can be 2-3 times more potent per dry gram than fully mature mushrooms.
If accurate, this would make aborts a particularly efficient use of the
cultivation effort, as they are harvested earlier and require no additional
growing time.

However, rigorous scientific quantification of this potency difference is
limited in the published literature. Most evidence remains anecdotal, drawn
from the subjective reports of cultivators and consumers.

## Biochemical Hypotheses for Increased Potency

Several biochemical mechanisms have been proposed to explain the potential
potency concentration in aborts:

**Concentration by dehydration.** Aborts that have ceased growing begin to
lose water through normal metabolic processes without replacing it through
growth-related water uptake. This natural dehydration could concentrate the
existing alkaloid content per unit of remaining tissue, similar to how
drying concentrates alkaloids in mature mushrooms.

**Alkaloid reallocation.** When a mushroom primordium aborts, the fungal
organism may attempt to recover the resources invested in the failed fruiting
body. However, the biosynthetic precursors of psilocybin (tryptophan,
tryptamine) may not be efficiently recovered, leaving them to accumulate in
the aborting tissue as more complex alkaloids are synthesized.

**Developmental stage alkaloid profile.** The ratio of psilocybin to
psilocin may differ between immature and mature fruiting bodies. If early-
stage fruiting bodies contain a higher proportion of the more potent
psilocin relative to psilocybin, this could account for the perceived
potency difference.

**Stress-induced alkaloid production.** Environmental stresses that cause
abortion (moisture deficit, CO2 elevation) may also trigger upregulation of
the fungal alkaloid biosynthetic pathway as a stress response, resulting
in higher alkaloid concentrations in the affected tissue.

## Harvesting Technique

Aborts should be harvested promptly once identified. Leaving them on the
substrate serves no purpose — they will not resume growing and will eventually
decompose, potentially creating a focus for contamination.

The recommended technique is to grasp the abort gently at its base and twist
or pull it free from the substrate. A clean tweezers or forceps can be helpful
for very small aborts that are difficult to grip with fingers.

Harvested aborts should be dried using the same methods as mature mushrooms.
Due to their small size, they dry very quickly — often within 24-48 hours
using desiccant drying. Their small size also means they can be dried without
slicing, which preserves more of the alkaloid content.

## Community Observations and Reports

The cultivation community has extensively discussed abort potency over decades
of online forums and discussion groups. While rigorous controlled studies are
lacking, several consistent observations have emerged:

Many cultivators report a noticeably stronger experience from consuming
aborts compared to an equivalent weight of mature mushrooms. The effects are
often described as more intense, more visual, and of shorter duration.

Some cultivators preferentially harvest and save aborts for personal use
while selling or sharing mature mushrooms, treating the aborts as a premium
product. This practice is widespread enough to have become an established
norm within the cultivation community.

There is general agreement that aborts from the first flush are the most
potent, with subsequent flushes producing aborts of somewhat lower but still
significant potency.

## Scientific Evidence Gaps

The scientific literature contains limited data on the alkaloid content of
aborted versus mature psilocybin mushroom fruiting bodies. Most published
analyses focus on mature, properly dried specimens from controlled or wild
collections.

A comprehensive study would need to:

1. Cultivate a single strain under [[psilocybe-cubensis-strain-potency-variability-controlled-conditions-bigwood-beug]]
2. Harvest aborts and mature mushrooms from the same grow simultaneously
3. Analyze both sets for psilocybin, psilocin, baeocystin, and norbaeocystin
4. Account for water content differences between aborts and mature specimens
5. Repeat across multiple flushes and genetic lines

Until such controlled studies are conducted, the potency claims regarding
aborts must be considered preliminary but supported by extensive anecdotal
evidence from experienced cultivators.
