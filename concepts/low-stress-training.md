# Low-Stress Training (LST)

Low-Stress Training (LST) is a [[query-how-does-no-till-cannabis-cultivation-work]] technique that involves bending and securing branches to create an even, flat canopy without cutting or damaging plant tissue. Cervantes describes bending as a training method that "alters the flow of hormones" and is "much easier on plants than pruning." LST is the foundation of many other [[cannabis-training-techniques-comparison]] including [[main-lining]], and is considered the safest and most forgiving approach to canopy management.

## Core Concept

LST works by manipulating the plant's natural hormone distribution (primarily auxins) through physical positioning rather than tissue damage. The apical meristem at each branch tip produces growth-inhibiting auxins that suppress lateral branching. When a branch is bent horizontally, the auxin concentration at the tip is partially neutralized, allowing lateral buds along the branch to develop more vigorously. The bent branch continues to grow, but now multiple bud sites receive direct light instead of just the single tip.

Unlike pruning or [[supercropping]], LST causes minimal stress to the plant. Cervantes states: "Bending is much easier on plants than pruning" and "it is easy to prune too much, but it is hard to over bend." This makes LST ideal for beginners and for strains that are sensitive to stress.

## How LST Works

The fundamental LST process:

1. Start when plants have 3-5 nodes (sets of branches) during the [[comparison-cannabis-vegetative-stage-vs-flowering-stage]]
2. Select a flexible branch and gently bend it away from the center of the plant
3. Secure the branch in its new position using soft ties, wire ties, or string
4. Anchor the tie to the rim of the container or a stake in the soil
5. Repeat with additional branches, pulling outer branches down and center branches outward
6. Adjust ties every few days as the plant grows, gradually increasing the bend angle
7. Continue training until a flat, even canopy is achieved
8. Stop training 1-2 weeks before switching to flowering

## Bending Technique

Cervantes provides detailed guidance on proper bending technique:

- "To bend, lean a branch in the desired direction and tie it in place"
- "Branches can take a lot of bending before they fold over or break"
- "Even if a branch folds, tie it in place; if necessary, use a wooden splint"
- "The stem will heal itself"
- "Young, supple branches take bending much better than old, stiff ones"
- "Be gentle when bending, even though cannabis can take much abuse"

When a branch folds over or a crotch separates during bending, the damage can be repaired. Cervantes advises using "a small wooden splint snugly secured with wire ties or duct tape to support the split and broken stem."

## Tools and Materials

- Soft wire ties (bread bag ties) -- inexpensive, easy to find at nurseries
- Plastic-coated electronic or telephone wire -- rigid, allows breathing room for stems
- Garden twist ties -- flexible with wire core, coated in soft material
- Soft string or garden twine -- gentle but may need frequent re-tying
- Bamboo stakes or wooden dowels -- for anchoring ties in the [[cervantes-green-algae-growing-medium]]
- Pipe cleaners -- soft and flexible, good for delicate seedlings

Cervantes specifically warns about wire ties: "if applied too tightly around a stem, the liquids cannot flow, and death could result." Always leave room for the stem to expand as it grows.

## Hormonal Effects of Bending

Cervantes explains the science behind bending:

- "Bending efficiently neutralizes the effect of the growth-inhibiting hormone"
- "Bending a growing tip changes hormone concentrations less than pruning"
- The auxin that normally suppresses lateral growth is partially disrupted
- Lateral buds along the bent branch receive more light and grow more vigorously
- Each bud "will turn into an impressive top, because they all receive more light"

The key difference from pruning is degree: "pruning affects the plant more drastically than bending." LST shifts hormones gently, while cutting the meristem causes a more dramatic hormonal disruption.

## Advantages Over Pruning

- No open wounds: eliminates disease and pest entry points
- No recovery time: the plant continues growing normally without pause
- Reversible: ties can be adjusted or removed if the bend is too extreme
- No hormone rebuilding period: unlike pruning, which takes a month for hormone concentrations to recover
- Works on autoflowers: suitable for strains with limited vegetative time
- Suitable for all experience levels: nearly impossible to cause serious harm

### Combined with Pruning

"Growers also combine bending and pruning." The combination allows for more aggressive canopy shaping while minimizing overall plant stress. Pruning is used sparingly for strategic cuts, while bending handles the bulk of canopy management.
