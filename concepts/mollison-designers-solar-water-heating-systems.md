## Batch Heaters (Integral Collector Storage)

The simplest solar water heater is the batch heater (also called a
breadbox or ICS collector). A water tank painted black and placed
inside an insulated, glazed box absorbs solar radiation directly. Cold
water enters the tank, heats throughout the day, and is drawn off for
use. No pumps or controls are needed.

Batch heaters are cheap to build from salvaged materials -- an old
water heater tank, a glass pane, and insulation. They work well in
warm, sunny climates but lose heat rapidly at night and during cloudy
periods. Mollison recommends them as entry-level holmgren-renewable-resources-detailed
principles.

## Evacuated Tube Collectors

Evacuated tube collectors consist of rows of parallel glass tubes,
each containing a vacuum-sealed absorber. The vacuum eliminates
convective heat loss, making these collectors highly efficient
(70-80%) even in cold, cloudy, or windy conditions. They can heat
water to higher temperatures than flat plate collectors.

Mollison views evacuated tubes as appropriate where high temperatures
are needed (radiant floor heating, commercial applications) or where
climate conditions limit flat plate performance. Higher cost is the
main disadvantage.
