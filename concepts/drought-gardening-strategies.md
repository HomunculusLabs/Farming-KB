# Drought Gardening Strategies

Water management is increasingly important for gardeners facing dry spells, water restrictions, or simply wishing to conserve this precious resource. The organic gardener has several strategies available to reduce water demand, improve soil water retention, and select plants that cope with dry conditions.

## Soil Water Management

### Improving Water Retention

The single most effective drought strategy is to improve the soil's ability to hold moisture. This is achieved by adding generous quantities of organic matter — compost, well-rotted [[hamilton-animal-manure-types-and-use]], or other organic materials. Organic matter acts like a sponge, absorbing and holding water that would otherwise drain away.

On sandy or chalky soils, which drain rapidly and lose nutrients through leaching during wet weather, organic matter is particularly important. These light soils benefit enormously from regular additions of compost and from being kept covered with [[fukuoka-natural-cover-grass-green-manure]] when vacant.

### Mulching to Reduce Evaporation

A thick layer of mulching-techniques|mulch on the soil surface dramatically reduces water loss through evaporation. Bark chippings, compost, grass cuttings, or well-rotted manure all serve this purpose. A 3in (7cm) layer of compost mulch not only retains moisture but also suppresses weeds that would otherwise compete with garden plants for water.

### Keeping the Ground Covered

Bare soil exposed to sun and wind loses moisture rapidly. Keeping the ground covered at all times — with crops, green manures, or mulches — is a fundamental principle of drought-conscious gardening. The [[fukuoka-white-clover-ground-cover]] also encourages beneficial soil organisms that improve soil structure and water-holding capacity.

## Watering Wisely

### Water at the Right Time

Water in the early morning or late evening to minimize evaporation. Avoid watering in the heat of the day, when much of the water is lost before it reaches the roots.

### Water Deeply and Infrequently

Frequent shallow watering encourages roots to stay near the surface, making plants more vulnerable to drought. A thorough soaking that penetrates deeply into the soil encourages roots to grow downward, where moisture is more available. Most plants do better with a thorough soaking followed by a period before the next watering.

### Target Watering

Direct water to the roots of plants rather than spraying the foliage. A hose with a rose attachment or a watering can with a long spout allows precise delivery. In the greenhouse-growing|greenhouse, capillary matting or trickle irrigation systems provide efficient, targeted watering.

### Use Warm Water for Seedlings

In early spring, water from the tap may be too cold for tender seedlings. Fill a watering can and leave it in the greenhouse to warm to ambient temperature before use. Better still, keep a tank of water inside the greenhouse.

## Choosing Drought-Resistant Plants

Some plants are naturally more tolerant of dry conditions:

- **Clover lawns** are more drought-resistant than grass
- **Mediterranean herbs** (rosemary, thyme, lavender) thrive in dry conditions
- **Established deep-rooting plants** can access water from lower soil levels
- **Native wildflowers** are often adapted to local rainfall patterns

## Water Collection

- Install water butts to collect rainwater from greenhouse and shed roofs
- Use greywater from the household where appropriate (avoid on edible crops)
- A permanent water tank inside the greenhouse, under the staging if space is limited, provides a convenient supply

## Specific Situations

### Containers and Hanging Baskets

Container-grown plants are especially vulnerable to drought. In hot weather, they may need watering twice daily. Use larger containers, which hold more moisture, and add water-retaining granules to the compost. [[comparison-cold-frame-gardening-vs-container-gardening-guide]] requires vigilant attention to watering.

### New Plantings

Newly planted trees, shrubs, and perennials need regular watering until their root systems are established. A thorough soak once or twice a week is better than daily sprinkling. Mulch around the base to retain moisture.

### Climbers on Walls

Plants growing against walls receive less rainfall and the wall radiates heat, increasing water loss. These plants need extra watering, especially in dry spells.
