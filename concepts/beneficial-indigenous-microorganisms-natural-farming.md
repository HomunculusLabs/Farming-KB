## Bokashi in Natural Farming

Beneficial Indigenous Microorganisms (BIM) are naturally occurring microorganisms
harvested from local environments and cultured for use in [[han-kyu-cho]]'s [[lactic-acid-bacteria]] (Lacto Bacilli)

[[bionutrients-lab-lactic-acid-bacteria-serum]] are the primary workhorse microorganism in the BIM toolkit.
These Gram-positive bacteria produce lactic acid as a metabolic byproduct,
creating an acidic environment that suppresses pathogenic organisms. Their
applications span multiple domains:

- **Composting**: Added to compost piles, lacto bacilli accelerate decomposition,
  reduce unpleasant odors, and produce higher-quality finished compost by
  inhibiting putrefactive bacteria that cause foul smells.
- **Natural Piggery**: In odor-free piggery systems, lacto bacilli are sprayed on
  bedding and manure to control ammonia and hydrogen sulfide odors, improving
  animal welfare without [[biological-wastewater-treatment]] infrastructure.
- **Aquaculture**: In fish ponds and shrimp farms, lactic acid bacteria improve
  water quality by breaking down organic waste and suppressing pathogens,
  reducing mortality in cultured stock.
- **Foliar Spray**: Diluted solutions colonize leaf surfaces, creating a
  protective barrier against pathogens and supporting plant immune responses.
- **Animal Feed Additive**: Mixed into feed, lacto bacilli promote digestive
  health, improve nutrient absorption, and reduce enteric diseases.

### Antibacterial Properties

Lactic acid bacteria exhibit potent antibacterial activity against human and
animal pathogens. Field practice has demonstrated effectiveness in controlling
Salmonella and Escherichia coli populations in animal housing, feed, and
digestive systems. Mechanisms include competitive exclusion, bacteriocin
production, and creation of a low-pH environment hostile to gram-negative
pathogens.

### Culturing Lactic Acid Bacteria

The standard method uses rice wash and milk. Rice wash water from rinsing raw
rice serves as a nutrient medium, left at room temperature for several days
until slightly sour. Milk is added at approximately a 1:10 ratio and the mixture
ferments for five to seven days. Lacto bacilli consume lactose and coagulate
milk proteins, forming a curd that separates from a yellowish liquid — the crude
lactic acid bacteria serum. This is strained and can be stored or diluted for
use. No laboratory equipment is required.

## Forest Beneficial Microorganisms

Old-growth forests contain rich [[cannabis-rhizosphere-microbial-communities]] in their humus layers.
Practitioners place cooked rice in a container and bury it beneath leaf litter in
an undisturbed forest area. After three to five days, the rice becomes colonized
by fungi and bacteria attracted to the carbohydrate source. The microbial-covered
rice is collected and mixed with crude sugar or molasses and water to create a
microbial concentrate valued for its diversity and adaptability.

## Bamboo Microorganisms

Bamboo stands are targeted because bamboo roots exude sugary compounds supporting
a distinct [[cannabis-cultivar-microbial-community-effects]]. The same cooked-rice bait method is used, placing
rice at the base of bamboo clumps or within bamboo litter. The resulting culture
is rich in cellulolytic organisms capable of breaking down tough plant fibers,
making it useful for [[bionutrients-fermented-plant-extracts]] (FPE), serve as food for
beneficial microorganisms. They are prepared by fermenting fast-growing plants
(often weeds such as purslane, mugwort, or taro leaves) with crude sugar or
molasses. The resulting liquid contains sugars, amino acids, minerals, and growth
factors that sustain and multiply beneficial microbes when applied alongside BIM
cultures, creating a self-reinforcing biological system in the soil.

## Eastern vs. Western Approaches

A philosophical distinction exists between Eastern and Western approaches to
[[mushroom-cultivation-for-sustainable-agriculture]]. Western organic farming emphasizes compost, nutrient
management, cover cropping, and mechanical weed control. Eastern natural farming
focuses on microorganisms, enzymes, and the energetic concepts of chi (life force)
and yin-yang balance. While Western standards regulate prohibited inputs, Eastern
natural farming asks practitioners to understand and support living biological
processes that sustain fertility. Both share ecological sustainability goals but
differ in primary methodology.

## Practical Applications

BIM cultures have been applied across diverse agricultural contexts. In
free-range poultry, BIM sprays reduce ammonia in bedding and support gut health.
Odor-free piggeries using BIM demonstrate that intensive livestock can be managed
without [[bloomfield-environmental-pollution-overcollection-impact-fungi]]. Orchid growers use BIM foliar sprays to resist fungal infections, while salad green growers report reduced pest pressure and improved shelf life of harvested produce.
