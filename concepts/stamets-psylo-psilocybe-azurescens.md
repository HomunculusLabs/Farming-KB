# [[psilocybe-azurescens]]

**Authority**: Stamets and Gartz

**Common Names**: Astoriensis, flying saucer mushroom, indigo Psilocybe

## Description

One of the most potent psilocybin mushrooms in the world, Psilocybe
azurescens was first described by [[paul-stamets]] and [[jochen-gartz]]. This
species is known for its exceptionally strong bluing reaction, with flesh
actually becoming indigo-black where damaged.

### Cap (Pileus)

3-10 cm broad. Conic to convex, expanding to broadly convex and eventually
flattening with age with a pronounced, persistent broad umbo. Surface
smooth, viscid when moist, covered by a separable gelatinous pellicle.
Chestnut to ochraceous brown to caramel in color, often becoming pitted
with dark blue or bluish black zones. Hygrophanous, fading to dingy brown
in drying. Strongly bruising blue when damaged. Margin even, sometimes
irregular and eroded at maturity, slightly incurved at first, soon
decurved, translucent striate and often pale azure tinted.

### Gills (Lamellae)

Attachment ascending, sinuate to adnate. Brown, often stained indigo black
where injured. Close, with two tiers of lamellulae, mottled. Edges
whitish. Spore print dark purplish brown to purplish black in mass.

### Stem (Stipe)

90-200 mm long by 3-6 mm thick. Silky white, dingy brown from the base or
in age. Hollow at maturity. Composed of twisted, cartilaginous, silky
white fibrous tissue. Base thickening downwards, often curved, and
characterized by coarse white aerial tufts of mycelium, often with azure
tones combined with dense, thick, silky white rhizomorphs that
tenaciously attach to wood chips or dead grass. Strongly bruising bluish
upon disturbance.

### Veil

White, cortinate, often leaving a fibrillose annular zone in the superior
regions of the stem.

### Taste and Odor

Taste is extremely bitter. Odor none to slightly farinaceous.

## Microscopy

- **Spores**: Dark purplish black in deposit, 12-13.5 by 6.5-8 um,
  ellipsoid
- **Basidia**: 4-spored
- **Pleurocystidia**: Abundant, fusoid-ventricose, tapering to a narrow
  but short neck, bluntly papillate, 23-35 by 9-10 um
- **Cheilocystidia**: Nearly identical to pleurocystidia, 23-28 by 6.5-8
  um
- **Tissue**: Notably awash with bluish tones

## Habitat

Cespitose to gregarious on deciduous wood chips and/or in sandy soils
rich in lignous debris. This mushroom naturally grows, often prolifically,
along the northern Oregon coast near Astoria, favoring the land adjacent
to the shoreline. P. azurescens has a strong affection for dune grasses,
especially Ammophila maritima, with which it is closely associated.
Growing an extensive, dense, and tenacious mycelial mat, P. azurescens
causes the whitening of wood. An adaptive species, outdoor beds have been
established with ease in the United States.

## Distribution

Naturally found along the northern Oregon coast. Established in outdoor
beds elsewhere in the United States.

## Season

Fruitings begin in late September and continue well after the first frost,
often into late December and early January. A cold weather-tolerant species.

## Dosage and Potency

One of the most potent species in the world. Containing up to 1.78%
psilocybin, 0.38% psilocin, and 0.35% baeocystin (Stamets and Gartz
1995). After six months of storage, analyses revealed that this species
retained most of its original potency. This species is unique not only in
its potency but also in its relatively high baeocystin content, roughly on
par with [[psilocybe-semilanceata]].

## Commentary

The silky white stem, caramel-colored cap, relatively large stature,
non-undulating cap margin, and broad but pronounced umbo are
representative features. P. azurescens is one of the most potent species
known and exhibits one of the strongest bluing reactions Stamets has ever
seen.

The extremely bitter taste of this mushroom is notable and may limit
consumption. At higher doses, some people report a loss of muscular
control and strength that can persist into the following day.

## Similar Species

- **[[psilocybe-cyanescens]]**: Similar but macroscopically distinguished by
  much smaller stature and characteristically sine-wave (wavy) margin
- **[[psilocybe-cyanofibrillosa]]**: Related species from the Pacific coast
- **Psilocybe serbica**: European species with similar potency
- **[[psilocybe-subaeruginosa]]**: Australian species in the same alliance
- **Psilocybe venenata**: Asian species with similar features
