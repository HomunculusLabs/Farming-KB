## Palliative Management Through Cannabinoid Therapy

Night sweats — episodes of excessive perspiration during sleep that soak
bedding and nightclothes — are a distressing symptom experienced by many
cancer [[blesching-cannabis-cancer-night-sweats]] patients. Unlike the mild perspiration associated with warm sleeping
environments, clinical night [[blesching-cannabis-cancer-night-sweats]] sweats are drenching episodes that disrupt sleep,

weight loss, and fatigue. (see [[cannabis-and-fibromyalgia]]).

### Mechanisms of Cancer-Induced Night Sweats

Several mechanisms contribute to excessive sweating in cancer patients:

- **Cytokine-mediated**: Tumo [[psilocybin-and-the-logos]] [[blesching-cannabis-immune-system-modulation]] e system release
  pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1, IL-6, TNF-alpha)[[plutarch-and-cicero-on-the-eleusinian-mysteries]] act on the
  hypothalamus to raise the body's thermoregulatory set point, triggering
  heat-dissipating responses including sweating
- **Hormonal fluctuations**: Certain tumors (particularly lymphomas and
  some solid tumors) produce hormones or hormone-like substances that
  disrupt normal thermoregulation
- **Febrile responses**: Low-grade fevers from infection, tumor necrosis,
  or treatment-related neutropenia often manifest predominantly as night
  sweats before becoming overtly febrile during the day
- **Medication effects**: Chemotherapy, hormonal therapies (tamoxifen,
  aromatase inhibitors), and some targeted therapies can trigger sweating
- **Autonomic dysfunction**: Cancer or its treatment can affect autonomic
  nervous system control of sweat gland activity
- **Menopausal symptoms**: In breast cancer patients, treatment-induced
  menopause causes hot flashes and night sweats that may be severe

### Night Sweats as a Diagnostic Indicator

Night sweats are one of the classic "B symptoms" of lymphoma, along with
unexplained fever and weight loss. The presence of drenching night sweats
affects cancer staging (upstaging to "B" designation in Hodgkin and
non-Hodgkin lymphoma) and carries prognostic significance. Persistent
unexplained night sweats should always prompt thorough medical evaluation. (see [[blesching-cannabis-fibromyalgia-tai-chi-and-trauma]]).

### Impact on Cancer Patients

The consequences of chronic night sweats extend beyond discomfort:

- **Sleep disruption**: Repeated awakening to change clothes or bedding
  severely fragments sleep architecture, contributing to cancer-related
  fatigue
- **Psychological distress**: The combination of sleep deprivation,
  anxiety about disease progression, and the visceral unpleasantness of
  drenching sweats creates a significant psychological burden
- **Practical difficulties**: Frequent linen changes, skin irritation from
  persistent moisture, and social embarrassment affect daily functioning
- **Treatment compliance**: Poor sleep and reduced quality of life may
  contribute to reduced adherence to demanding treatment regimens

### Conventional Management

Standard approaches to managing cancer-related night sweats include:

- **Treating the underlying cause**: Antibiotics for infection, disease-
  specific therapy for tumor-associated symptoms
- **Antipyretics**: Acetaminophen and NSAIDs for cytokine-mediated fevers
- **Anticholinergics**: Glycopyrrolate, oxybutynin, and similar agents to
  reduce sweat gland activity; limited by side effects including dry mouth,
  constipation, urinary retention, and cognitive effects
- **Hormonal therapies**: For treatment-induced menopause, low-dose
  antidepressants (venlafaxine) or gabapentin
- **Cooling measures**: Environmental modifications, moisture-wicking
  sleepwear, and cooling mattress pads

These approaches often provide incomplete relief and carry their own side
effect burdens, creating a need for alternative or adjunctive therapies.

### Cannabis and Night Sweats — Evidence Base

Cannabinoids have been studied for their potential to manage sweating and
thermoregulatory dysfunction in cancer patients:

**Dronabinol (Synthetic THC) Studies**: Dronabinol, an FDA-approved synthetic
THC formulation, has been investigated for cancer-associated anorexia and
cachexia. During these trials, patients consistently reported reductions in
sweating episodes as a secondary benefit. The thermoregulatory effects of
cannabinoids on the hypothalamic temperature set point appear relevant to
the pathophysiology of cytokine-mediated night sweats.

**Hypothalamic Modulation**: THC and other cannabinoids influence hypothalamic
function through CB1 receptor activation. The hypothalamus is the body's
primary thermoregulatory center, and cannabinoid-mediated modulation of this
region may help normalize the dysregulated temperature set point that drives
cancer-related sweating episodes.

**Anti-cytokine Effects**: Both THC and CBD have demonstrated the ability to
modulate cytokine production, potentially reducing the pro-inflammatory
cytokine cascade that triggers night sweats in cancer patients. This immune-
modulating effect may address the root cause rather than merely suppressing
symptoms.

**Sleep Enhancement**: Beyond direct effects on sweating, cannabis's known
sedative and anxiolytic properties may help patients fall back asleep more
quickly after sweating episodes, reducing the overall sleep disruption
associated with night sweats.

**Patient Experience**: Cancer patients using medical cannabis frequently
report improvement in night sweats among other benefits, including better
sleep, reduced anxiety, and improved appetite. While anecdotal, these
consistent reports across diverse cancer types warrant clinical attention.

### Practical Considerations for Use

For cancer patients considering cannabis for night sweat management:

- **Timing**: Evening or bedtime administration aligns with symptom pattern
- **Strain selection**: Indica-dominant strains with sedating properties
  may offer dual benefits of sweat reduction and sleep promotion
- **Delivery method**: Oral or sublingual preparations provide longer-lasting
  effects appropriate for overnight symptom control
- **Integration with conventional care**: Cannabis should complement, not
  replace, standard oncological treatment and symptom management

### Summary

Cancer-induced night sweats represent a significant source of suffering that
is often inadequately addressed by conventional therapies. The hypothalamic
effects, anti-cytokine properties, and sleep-enhancing benefits of cannabinoids
provide multiple mechanisms through which cannabis may help. While formal
clinical trials focused specifically on night sweats are limited, consistent
patient reports and the known pharmacology of cannabinoids support further
investigation.
