# Cold Climate Growing Reference

Detailed growing techniques, variety selection, indoor growing, winter production, and food storage for cold climates. See [[cold-climate-gardening]] for cold-hardy crops, season extension, and succession planting schedules.

## Growing Techniques for Cold Climates

### Starting Seeds Indoors

- Start 6-10 weeks before last frost date
- Use seed-starting mix (not garden soil)
- Heat mat: 70-80F (21-27C) for germination (peppers, tomatoes, eggplant)
- Grow lights: 14-16 hours per day, 2-3 inches above seedlings
- Harden off: 7-10 days before transplanting -- gradually expose to outdoor conditions
- See [[seed-starting]] for detailed guidance

### Raised Beds

- Raised beds warm faster in spring (soil 5-10F warmer than ground level)
- Build beds 8-12 inches tall
- Fill with compost, topsoil, and aged manure
- Cover with row cover or cold frame for earliest planting
- See [[raised-bed-gardening]] for detailed construction

### Soil Warming

- Clear plastic (not black -- clear heats soil better) laid on beds 2-3 weeks before planting
- Solarization: kills weed seeds and soil pathogens
- Remove plastic before planting
- Combines well with raised beds and row covers

### Wind Protection

- Plant a windbreak of evergreens on the north and west sides of the garden
- Perennial hedge: arborvitae, juniper, or spruce (leave gaps for air flow)
- Temporary: straw bales or burlap screens
- Reduced wind = higher temperatures and less plant stress

### Microclimate Creation

- South-facing walls absorb heat during day, radiate at night
- Stone walls and rocks act as thermal mass
- Water features moderate temperature swings
- See [[edge-effect-and-microclimate]] for detailed microclimate strategies

## Cold-Climate Variety Selection

Choose short-season varieties (look for "days to maturity" under 70 days for warm-season crops):

| Crop | Recommended Short-Season Varieties |
|------|-----------------------------------|
| Tomato | Early Girl (52 days), Stupice (55 days), Sub Arctic Plenty (42 days) |
| Pepper | Ace (55 days), Gypsy (60 days), Jimmy Nardello (65 days) |
| Corn | Early Sunglow (63 days), Tuxedo (68 days) |
| Squash | Early Prolific Straightneck (50 days), Gold Rush (50 days) |
| Cucumber | Marketmore (58 days), Bush Pickle (45 days) |
| Bean | Provider (50 days), Tendergreen (55 days) |
| Watermelon | Sugar Baby (80 days), Yellow Doll (68 days) |
| Melon | Minnesota Midget (60 days), Earli-Dew (65 days) |
| Basil | Genovese (68 days), Spicy Globe (60 days) |

## Winter Growing

### Indoor Growing

- Grow lights: LED panels, 14-16 hours per day
- Best indoor crops: herbs (basil, cilantro, parsley), lettuce, microgreens, sprouts
- Windowsills: supplement natural light with grow lights in winter
- Sprouts: alfalfa, mung bean, radish -- ready in 3-7 days, no light needed
- Microgreens: harvest in 10-21 days, high nutrient density
- See [[mushroom-indoor-cultivation]] techniques for more detail

### Winter Cold Frame Crops

These will grow (slowly) in an unheated cold frame in zones 5-6:

- Spinach
- Mache (corn salad)
- Claytonia (miner's lettuce)
- Winter lettuce
- Radishes
- Scallions
- Asian greens (tatsoi, mizuna, bok choy)
- Kale (will survive but grow very slowly)
- Carrots (planted in fall, harvested through winter)

## Food Storage for Cold Climates

- Root cellar: 32-40F for root vegetables (see [[food-preservation]])
- Cool pantry: 50-60F for squash, onions, garlic
- Freezing: blanch and freeze vegetables at peak freshness
- Canning: pressure can low-acid vegetables, water-bath high-acid
- Drying: herbs, fruits, jerky
- Fermentation: sauerkraut, kimchi, pickles (see [[food-fermentation]])
