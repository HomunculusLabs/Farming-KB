# THC and CBD Therapeutic Profiles

Tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) and cannabidiol (CBD) are the two most abundant and most studied cannabinoids in the cannabis plant. Despite sharing a common biosynthetic origin, they have profoundly different pharmacological profiles, receptor affinities, and [[cannabis-for-motion-sickness]].
- **Appetite stimulation**: THC stimulates appetite through hypothalamic CB1 activation, used for AIDS-related anorexia/cachexia and cancer-related weight loss.
- **Neuroprotection**: THC demonstrates neuroprotective properties via CB1 receptors, potentially beneficial in Alzheimer's disease, ALS, [[cannabis-for-cachexia-and-appetite-stimulation]].
- **Nabilone (Cesamet)**: Synthetic THC analog, FDA-approved for chemotherapy-induced nausea.
- **Sativex**: A whole-plant cannabis extract mouth spray containing approximately equal parts THC and CBD, approved in many countries for MS spasticity and cancer pain.
- **Limitation**: With the exception of Sativex, pharmaceutical THC products lack the other biologically active cannabis components (CBD, terpenes, minor cannabinoids) that contribute to therapeutic effects through the entourage effect.

## Cannabidiol (CBD)

### Chemical Properties

- **Non-psychoactive**: CBD does not produce the euphoria, altered perception, or cognitive impairment characteristic of THC. This makes it suitable for patients who need therapeutic benefits without intoxication.
- **Receptor profile**: CBD has a greater affinity for CB2 receptors than CB1 receptors, but much of its therapeutic activity occurs through non-cannabinoid receptor mechanisms.

### Mechanism of Action

CBD's therapeutic effects operate through multiple pathways beyond direct cannabinoid receptor activation:

- **FAAH inhibition**: CBD suppresses the enzyme FAAH (fatty acid amide hydrolase) that breaks down anandamide. By inhibiting FAAH, CBD keeps anandamide active at higher concentrations and for longer durations, indirectly enhancing endocannabinoid system signaling.
- **THC modulation**: CBD tames the psychoactive effects of THC, allowing patients to tolerate higher THC doses while maintaining cognitive clarity. This antagonistic relationship between CBD and THC at CB1 receptors is one of CBD's most clinically important properties.
- **5-HT1A receptor activation**: CBD activates serotonin 5-HT1A receptors, contributing to its anxiolytic and antidepressant effects.
- **TRPV1 activation**: CBD activates the TRPV1 (vanilloid) receptor involved in pain and inflammation signaling.
- **PPAR-γ activation**: CBD activates peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma, contributing to anti-inflammatory effects.

### Therapeutic Applications

CBD is considered one of the most promising cannabinoids for medical use, with particular efficacy in:

- **Neurodegenerative diseases**: CBD has the highest therapeutic prospect for treating conditions like Alzheimer's disease, Parkinson's disease, and Huntington's disease through its neuroprotective and anti-inflammatory properties.
- **Epilepsy**: CBD (as Epidiolex) received FDA approval in 2018 for treatment-resistant epilepsy (Dravet syndrome and Lennox-Gastaut syndrome), based on strong clinical evidence.
- **[[cannabis-for-anxiety-disorders]]**: CBD demonstrates anxiolytic effects through 5-HT1A receptor activation, without the sedation or dependency risks of benzodiazepines.
- **Psychosis and schizophrenia**: CBD shows antipsychotic properties and may mitigate THC-induced psychotic symptoms, with a more favorable side-effect profile than traditional antipsychotics.
- **Inflammatory conditions**: CBD's anti-inflammatory effects through CB2 and PPAR-γ pathways make it useful for arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease, and other inflammatory conditions.
- **Cancer**: CBD has demonstrated anti-tumor properties in preclinical studies, including induction of apoptosis and inhibition of metastasis across multiple cancer types.

### Safety Profile

- **Wide therapeutic window**: CBD has an excellent safety profile with no known lethal dose. Side effects at therapeutic doses are generally mild (drowsiness, dry mouth, gastrointestinal upset).
- **Drug interactions**: CBD can inhibit cytochrome P450 enzymes (particularly CYP3A4 and CYP2C19), potentially increasing blood levels of other medications metabolized by these enzymes.
- **No abuse potential**: CBD does not produce rewarding effects or dependence, and the WHO has determined it has no potential for abuse.
