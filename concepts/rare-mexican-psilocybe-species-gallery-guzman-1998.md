## Psilocybe angustipleurocystidiata Guzman

P. angustipleurocystidiata is a species described by Guzman, named for its
distinctively narrow pleurocystidia. This morphological feature distinguishes
it from closely related species in the same section. The species was included
in the 1998 worldwide checklist and was illustrated among the published
figure plates. It is known only from Mexico and represents an endemic element
of the country's neurotropic fungal flora.

The discovery and description of species like P. angustipleurocystidiata
illustrates the pattern observed by Guzman that new species of neurotropic
fungi continue to be found wherever targeted [[mexican-neurotropic-fungi-diversity]]. The existence of
two recognized varieties within this species reflects the morphological
variability that complicates the taxonomy of Psilocybe in Mexico. Guzman
noted that taxonomic challenges in the genus are particularly acute in
Mexico, where the high species diversity leads to frequent morphological
overlap between closely related taxa.

## Psilocybe laurae Guzman

P. laurae is another endemic Mexican Psilocybe species documented in the
1998 checklist. It was illustrated in the figure plates of the publication
and is known from limited collections in Mexico. Like other rare Mexican
endemics, P. laurae contributes to the country's position as the global
leader in neurotropic fungal diversity. The species was listed among the
endemic taxa that are found nowhere else in the world.

Endemic species like P. laurae are of particular biogeographic interest
because they represent lineages that have evolved in isolation within
Mexico's diverse range of vegetation types. The concentration of endemic
neurotropic species in Mexico supports the hypothesis that the country's
complex topography and varied climate have created numerous ecological
niches that promoted speciation within the genus Psilocybe.

## Psilocybe chiapanensis Guzman

P. chiapanensis is described from Chiapas, southern Mexico, a state with
exceptional fungal biodiversity at the junction of Mexican and Central
American tropical forests. The species is included as one of the endemic
Mexican neurotropic taxa and reflects the continuous forest corridor that
facilitates species distribution between Mexico and Central America.

## Psilocybe muliercula (Heim) Guzman

P. muliercula is a small species whose name means "little woman" in Latin,
reflecting its diminutive stature. It is found in the Nevado de Toluca
region of Mexico, where it is used alongside Cordyceps species in
traditional indigenous ceremonies. The Trique Indians of Alta Mixteca
(Oaxaca) refer to P. muliercula and the related Cordyceps species as
"hombrecitos" (little men) and "mujercitas" (little women), respectively.

P. muliercula grows in high-altitude habitats in central Mexico and is
associated with the Elaphomyces truffle hosts that serve as the substrates
for the Cordyceps species used in the same ceremonies. The species
illustrates the deep integration of specific neurotropic fungi into
indigenous ceremonial practices in Mexico.

## Habitat and Vegetation Associations

The rare Mexican Psilocybe species profiled here share common ecological
patterns. The majority occur in mesophytic (cloud) forests, which Guzman
identified as the primary habitat for Mexican neurotropic Psilocybe.
These forests, characterized by high humidity, moderate temperatures, and
persistent cloud cover, provide ideal conditions for the growth and
fruiting of Psilocybe species. Some species extend into tropical forests
at lower elevations, and a few are found in coniferous forests at higher
altitudes.
