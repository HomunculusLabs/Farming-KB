# Spore Syringe Preparation

**Source:** PF TEK - [[mushroom-spore-syringes]] are the primary [[brassinosteroid-growth-promotion-psilocybe-cubensis]]* spores suspended in sterile water, drawn into a hypodermic syringe for delivery into substrate jars. The syringe method allows precise, controlled inoculation of multiple jars from a [[macrofungal-documentation-photography-spore-prints]] completes the [[shirota-4-hydroxyindole-acetyl-protection-psilocin-synthesis-starting-material]] for syringe preparation. It is made by placing a mature mushroom cap gill-side down on a clean surface and allowing the spores to drop and collect.

### Making a Spore Print

1. **Select a mature specimen** — Choose a mushroom with an open cap and dark gills. The cap should be fully expanded but not deteriorating.
2. **Prepare the surface** — Use a clean piece of paper, aluminum foil, or glass. Aluminum foil is preferred because it can be folded and stored.
3. **Remove the stem** — Cut the stem off close to the cap with a sterile knife.
4. **Place the cap** — Set the cap gill-side down on the prepared surface.
5. **Cover** — Place a glass or bowl over the cap to reduce airflow and prevent contamination while retaining humidity.
6. **Wait** — Leave undisturbed for 12-24 hours. A visible deposit of purplish-brown to black spores will accumulate on the surface beneath the gills.
7. **Remove cap** — Carefully lift the cap. The spore print should be a dense, even deposit of dark spores.

### Spore Print Quality

- **Color:** *P. cubensis* spore prints are dark purplish-brown to nearly black
- **Density:** A good print has a thick, even deposit. Thin prints may not yield enough spores.
- **Purity:** No visible mold growth or discoloration on the print. Contaminated prints should be discarded.
- **Storage:** Fold foil prints and store in a dry, cool, dark place. Spores remain viable for months to years when properly stored.

## Preparing the Spore Syringe

### Materials Needed
- Spore print (on foil or glass)
- Sterile syringe (10 cc capacity)
- Sterile water (distilled or boiled and cooled)
- Sterile needle
- Alcohol lamp or lighter for [[pf-tek-dry-vermiculite-contaminant-barrier]] barrier instead of reaching the nutritious substrate below, resulting in failed germination.

### Between Jars

Between each jar, flame-sterilize the needle to prevent cross-contamination. There may be a few "pops" of boiling water from the needle during flaming, but the spores within the syringe body remain safe.

## Syringe Storage

- Store syringes in a **dark, cool place** (refrigerator is ideal)
- Spore syringes can survive freezing but extreme low temperatures are probably destructive
- Syringes remain viable for many months when properly stored
- Re-sterilize the needle immediately before each re-use
- If leftover solution remains, replace the needle guard and store for later use
