# Alliums Growing Guide

updated: 2026-04-18

The edible bulbs of the onion family (Allium) are in fact compacted layers of swollen leaf bases in which the plant stores food. They prefer a rich soil so are particularly suited to organic cultivation. The bulbs are among the easiest of all vegetables to grow and most of them store well, so it is not difficult to maintain a year-round supply.

## Soil Preparation for Bulb Vegetables

- All bulb vegetables prefer sunshine and a soil rich in organic matter
- Dig in plenty of well-rotted [[organic-composting-methods]] or manure
- If necessary, add lime to raise the soil pH above 6.5
- If using a [[crop-rotation-benefits]] plan, grow bulb vegetables in Plot A
- Keep rows weed free; support stems in exposed sites to prevent wind breakage

## Garlic (Allium sativum)

Garlic is one of the easiest of all vegetables to grow, provided you have a suitably sunny site. Sometimes classified as an herb, it is sown from cloves (the individual segments of the bulb) and can be dried and stored for year-round use.

**Varieties:** Grown from cloves. California White grows well in most climates, while Silver Rose stores very well. Elephant Garlic produces big, beautiful bulbs.

**Planting:** Separate the cloves and plant them, pointed end up, in holes 1in (2.5cm) deep and 6in (15cm) apart. Do this in late winter.

**Deep beds:** Plant cloves 6in (15cm) apart in staggered rows.

**Maintenance:** Keep rows weed free. Support stems in exposed areas to prevent wind breakage.

**Harvesting:** Dig up bulbs in summer, clean them and dry in the sun before stringing or storing in nets in a frost-free place. See [[root-crops-growing-guide]] for related storage techniques.

**Pests and Diseases:** Garlic is generally trouble free.

## Leeks (Allium porrum)

A superb winter vegetable that is hardy in all but the very coldest climates. Leeks are easy to grow, undemanding, and a valuable source of fresh greens in winter. They may run to seed if planted too early.

**Varieties:** Musselburgh — popular, very hardy with thick stems. Titan — very long stem and good blanched base. Lyon Prizetaker — excellent flavor, very hardy.

**Sowing and Planting:** Leeks can be multiple sown in seed boxes started in the greenhouse in mid-spring at about 60F (15C), or sown in a seedbed outside in a shallow row 6in (15cm) apart. For dibber planting: starting in early summer, make holes with a dibber about 6-8in (15-20cm) deep and 6in (15cm) apart, with 12in (30cm) between rows. Trim the roots of the leeks by about two-thirds and the tops by about half, and drop a plant into each hole. Do not refill the holes, but pour a little water into each to wash some soil over the roots.

**Deep beds:** Plant in staggered rows with 6in (15cm) between plants each way, or multiple sow and plant 12in (30cm) apart each way.

**Maintenance:** Hoe to keep rows weed free. As leeks grow, pull a little soil around the base of each stem to blanch them, taking care to prevent soil from getting between the leaves.

**Harvesting:** Leeks can usually be left in the ground until required. If very cold weather threatens to make soil too hard to dig, remove a few plants and store in a box of moist peat until needed.

## Onions (Allium cepa)

Onions store well so you can eat them all year-round if a combination of main crop and other varieties are planted.

**Varieties:** Choose main crop varieties that store well, like Walla Walla and Spanish.

**Sets vs Seed:** If your soil is heavy and wet, buy onion sets — tiny onion bulbs specially treated to produce good-sized bulbs. Cut off any long growths of old foliage at the tips to prevent birds pulling the sets out, and plant bulbs at the same distances with tips just below soil level. Do not push sets into the ground as they may push themselves out again when roots grow. For seed sowing: sow outside in shallow rows 12in (30cm) apart in early spring. Use a stale seedbed so tiny seedlings are not swamped by weeds. Thin to 2in (5cm) apart. Sow other varieties in late summer and spread a handful of dried blood per square yard/meter in spring.

**Deep beds:** Sow or plant 2in (5cm) apart in staggered rows.

**Maintenance:** Keep onions weed free and water in dry weather.

**Harvesting:** In late summer, the onion foliage turns brown and withers. Dig the roots and leave bulbs to dry in the sun. Remove tops and store in nets in a frost-free spot, or tie bunches together with string and hang.

**Pests and Diseases:** Onion fly, onion eelworm, neck rot, white rot, and storage rot. See [[companion-planting-deterrents]] for pest prevention strategies.

## Shallots (Allium ascalonicum)

Earlier to mature and with a milder flavor than main crop onions. Shallots require similar growing conditions, are easy to grow from "sets," rarely troubled by diseases, and store well for winter use.

## Green Onions (Allium cepa)

Green (salad) onions are picked before the mature bulb forms. They have a milder flavor than larger types and do not store well. White Lisbon and Ishikuro are recommended varieties. Sow under cloches in late winter, and in the open at three-week intervals beginning in early spring. They will not need thinning. In deep beds, scatter seeds in a wide row.
