# How Do I Grow Alliums (Garlic, Onions, Leeks, Shallots) Successfully?

Quick-reference FAQ for planting, growing, harvesting, and saving seed for the main allium crops in a permaculture or small-farm setting.

---

## When Should I Plant Alliums by Climate Zone?

**Garlic:** Plant in fall (October–November in most zones). Hardneck garlic needs 4–8 weeks of vernalization (temps below 40°F / 4°C) to form proper bulbs — plant 4–6 weeks before ground freezes. Softneck garlic tolerates milder winters; in zones 9–10, plant in November–December. In zones 3–4, plant by late September.

**Onions (bulbing):** Plant sets or transplants 4–6 weeks before your last average frost date (March–April in most zones). Long-day varieties (14–16 hr daylight) are for zones 4–6; short-day (10–12 hr) for zones 7–10; intermediate-day for zones 5–8. Direct-seed short-day onions in fall in the South; sow long-day types indoors in late January.

**Leeks:** Sow indoors 10–12 weeks before last frost; transplant out 2–3 weeks before last frost. For winter leeks in mild zones, direct-seed in midsummer and overwinter. In cold zones (3–5), choose a hardy variety like 'Bandit' or 'Bleu de Solaise' and mulch heavily.

**Shallots:** Plant sets in fall (October–November) in zones 5–9 for overwintering, or in early spring (March) in colder zones. Seed-grown shallots are typically spring-planted.

> **Quick rule:** If you're unsure, garlic and shallots go in the ground in fall; most onions and leeks go in early spring.

## What Soil Preparation Do Alliums Need?

All alliums share the same soil preferences:

- **Well-drained, loose loam** with pH 6.0–7.0 (6.5 is ideal). Soggy soil causes rot.
- **Dig in 2–3 inches of compost** and a balanced [[complete-organic-fertilizer-cof-recipe-solomon]] (or bone meal for phosphorus) before planting. Alliums are moderate feeders — excess nitrogen produces lush tops but small bulbs.
- **[[comparison-raised-beds-vs-hugelkultur]] or mounded rows** are strongly recommended in heavy clay or high-rainfall areas. Even 4–6 inches of elevation helps.
- **Remove rocks and debris** — bulbs need unrestricted expansion. A fine tilth to 6–8 inches depth is the minimum target.
- **Avoid fresh manure** — it can introduce allium pests (maggots) and burn cloves/sets.

## What Are the Most Common Mistakes When Growing Alliums?

| Mistake | Why It's a Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Planting too shallow** | Garlic cloves and onion sets should be 2–3 inches deep (3–4 inches in sandy soil). Shallow planting causes heaving, small bulbs, and cloves pushing out of the ground. | Use a dibber or trowel; measure from the base of the clove. |
| **Wrong planting time** | Spring-planted garlic rarely bulbs well; fall-planted long-day onions bolt. See the timing chart above. | Follow your zone and daylight-hour requirements. |
| **Poor drainage** | Alliums rot in standing water. Basal rot and fusarium are common in waterlogged soil. | Raise beds, add sand/compost, avoid low spots. |
| **Weed competition** | Alliums have shallow root systems and can't outcompete aggressive weeds. | Mulch with 3–4 inches of straw after planting; weed regularly early on. |
| **Over-fertilizing with nitrogen** | Big green tops, tiny bulbs. Harvest is delayed and storage life suffers. | Side-dress lightly at 3 weeks and 6 weeks only; use a balanced fertilizer. |
| **Crowding** | Bulbs stay small when competing for space. | Garlic: 6 inches apart. Onions: 4 inches apart. Leeks: 6 inches. Shallots: 6–8 inches. |

## How Can I Use Alliums for Pest Control via Interplanting?

Alliums release sulfur compounds ([[allicin]] and others) that deter a wide range of pests. Key companion pairings:

- **With brassicas (cabbage, kale, broccoli):** Deters cabbage moths, aphids, and cabbage maggots. Interplant garlic or onion sets every 12–18 inches among brassicas.
- **With carrots:** Classic "carrots love onions" pairing. Onions deter carrot rust fly; carrots deter onion maggot. Interplant in alternating rows or scattered blocks.
- **With tomatoes and peppers:** Garlic repels [[cervantes-spider-mites-identification-control]] and aphids. Plant garlic cloves around the perimeter of tomato beds.
- **Around [[comparison-pruning-fruit-trees-vs-grafting-fruit-trees]]:** Garlic planted at the drip line deters borers and aphids. Garlic chives work well here too.
- **General [[foliar-pest-deterrence-aact-mechanisms]]:** Interplanting alliums throughout the garden reduces overall pest pressure. Avoid planting alliums near legumes (beans, peas) — some evidence suggests they stunt each other.

## How Do I Know When Garlic and Onions Are Ready to Harvest?

**Garlic:**
- Hardneck: Watch for the scape (flower stalk) to curl fully, then straighten. Cut scapes about 2 weeks before expected harvest to redirect energy to the bulb.
- Both types: Harvest when roughly 50–60% of lower leaves have yellowed and dried. Don't wait until all tops fall over — over-mature bulbs split and don't store well.
- Dig — don't pull — with a garden fork. Start 4–6 inches away to avoid slicing bulbs.
- Timing is usually mid-June to mid-July in most zones.

**Onions:**
- Harvest when the tops naturally fall over and turn brown. Don't bend/knock them over prematurely — this reduces bulb size and storage life.
- Once 50–75% of tops have fallen, lift the bulbs and let them dry in place for 1–2 days if weather is dry.
- Harvest in mid-summer (July–August for spring-planted sets).

## What's the Basic Process for Curing Alliums?

Proper curing is essential for storage life. The goal is to dry the outer wrapper layers and neck slowly.

1. **Curing location:** Dry, well-ventilated, shaded area. A covered porch, barn loft, or garage with a fan works. Do NOT cure in direct sun — it cooks the bulbs.
2. **Temperature:** Ideal is 70–80°F (21–27°C) with 60–70% humidity.
3. **Method:** Tie in bundles of 6–10 and hang, or spread on wire racks/screens in a single layer. Good airflow around every bulb is critical.
4. **Duration:** Garlic: 2–3 weeks. Onions: 2–4 weeks. Shallots: 2–3 weeks. Leeks are generally not cured — use fresh or store damp in a root cellar.
5. **Finished when:** Outer skins are papery and rustle, necks are completely dry and shrunk, and no moisture squeezes from a squeezed bulb.
6. **After curing:** Trim roots to ½ inch and tops to 1 inch (or braid softneck garlic). Store garlic at 55–60°F; onions at 35–40°F in a cool, dark, dry place with good ventilation.

## How Do I Save My Own Allium Seed?

**Garlic — plant your own cloves:**
- Select the largest, healthiest bulbs from your harvest. These produce the biggest plants next year.
- Break bulbs apart no more than 48 hours before planting — separated cloves dry out and lose vigor.
- Plant the outer cloves (they're larger); use inner cloves for eating.
- Save 10–15% of your harvest for replanting. Garlic adapts to your conditions over 2–3 seasons of saved-seed selection.
- Do NOT plant supermarket garlic — it's often treated with sprout inhibitors and may carry disease.

**Onions — save seed from bolted onions:**
- Allow 2–3 strong, healthy onions to bolt (flower) in year one. This is easiest with biennial onions overwintered in the garden or stored at 35–40°F and replanted in spring.
- Onion flowers are insect-pollinated — isolate by 800+ feet from other flowering onions to maintain variety purity (or hand-pollinate in cages).
- Harvest seed heads when they dry and blacken. Seeds are small and black. Dry thoroughly, then store in paper envelopes in a cool, dark place. Onion seed loses viability quickly — use within 1–2 years.

**Leeks — overwinter for seed:**
- Leave 3–5 of your best leeks in the ground through winter. Mulch heavily in cold zones.
- In spring, they will send up a tall flower stalk (4–5 feet). Leeks are cross-pollinated by insects — isolate similarly to onions.
- Seed heads mature in late summer. Harvest when dry, thresh, and store. Leek seed lasts 2–3 years.

**Shallots — replant bulbs:**
- Simply save the best bulbs and replant at the normal time. Each bulb divides into multiple cloves, so seed saving is essentially cloning. Select for size, flavor, and disease resistance each generation.
