# Cannabis Chemotype Classification and Cannabinoid Level Breeding

## Overview

Cannabinoid chemotype refers to the characteristic profile of cannabinoids produced by a Cannabis plant. The relative amounts of THC, CBD, CBN, and other cannabinoids define the chemotype, which is genetically determined and passed from parent to offspring. Understanding chemotype classification is essential for both breeding programs and predicting psychoactive effects.

## Small's Four Chemotypes

Small (1975) divided genus Cannabis into four distinct chemotypes based on the relative amounts of THC and CBD:

1. **Drug-type (high THC:low CBD)**: Selected for psychoactive potency. Includes most recreational and medicinal strains.
2. **Intermediate-type**: Moderate levels of both THC and CBD.
3. **Fiber-type (low THC:high CBD)**: Hemp varieties selected for fiber production. CBD is the primary cannabinoid with little THC.
4. **Ruderal-type**: Wild or weedy types, typically low in both THC and CBD.

## Genetic Control of Cannabinoid Production

### Inheritance Patterns
- Crosses between high THC:low CBD strains and low THC:high CBD strains yield offspring with cannabinoid content intermediate between the two parents (Beutler and der Marderosian, 1978)
- The F1 offspring of controlled crosses (C. sativa Mexico x C. ruderalis Russia) fell into two groups intermediate between parents in THC level
- THC production is most likely controlled by more than one gene (polygenic inheritance)
- F1 hybrids with lower THC (resembling the staminate parent) were twice as frequent as higher THC hybrids (resembling the pistillate parent)

### Biochemical Mechanism
- Drug strains possess an enzyme system that quickly converts CBD-acid to THC-acid, favoring THC-acid accumulation
- Fiber strains lack this enzyme activity, so CBD-acid accumulation is favored since there is little conversion to THC-acid
- These same enzyme systems are probably sensitive to changes in heat and light
- Drug strains are characterized by Delta1-THC as the primary cannabinoid, with low levels of THCV, CBD, CBC, and CBN

### Environmental vs. Genetic Factors
According to Small (1979):
- The cannabinoid ratios of strains grown in northern climates reflect the cannabinoid ratio of the pure, imported parental strain
- This indicates cannabinoid phenotype is genetically controlled
- The levels of total cannabinoids are determined by environment
- Complex highs from various strains may be blended by careful breeding, but total psychoactivity depends on environment
- Unconscious breeding with undesirable low-THC parents can rapidly lead to degeneration of a drug strain

## Breeding for Cannabinoid Content

### Challenges
- Breeding for cannabinoid content without chromatographic analysis is "subjective guesswork"
- Selection of staminate (pollen) parents is always problematic because staminate plants usually express the same cannabinoid ratios as pistillate counterparts but in much lower quantities
- Staminate plants are rarely allowed to reach full maturity for fear of seeding the crop

### The Bioassay Method
A simple bioassay for THC content of staminate plants:
1. Leave numbered bags of leaves and tops from prospective pollen parents along with rolling papers in locations frequented by steady crowds of marijuana smokers
2. The bag consumed first is considered the most desirable and possibly most psychoactive
3. This is effectively an unstructured panel evaluation averaging unbiased opinions
4. Such bioassay results can inform staminate parent selection

### Analytical Requirements
- A chromatographic analysis system allows selection of specific cannabinoid types, especially staminate pollen parents
- Accurate characterization of varying highs requires measuring cannabinoid levels in prospective parents
- Variation in cannabinoid profile is subtle, making objective single-person selection nearly impossible

## Cannabinoid Profile and the Type of High

The type of high associated with different strains results from varying levels of cannabinoids:
- THC is the primary psychoactive ingredient
- CBN, CBD, and other accessory cannabinoids act synergistically with THC
- Terpenes and other aromatic constituents may also potentiate or suppress the effect of THC
- Cannabinoid levels establish phenotypes that are heritable from parent to offspring
- Therefore, cannabinoid levels are in part determined by genes

## Breeding History and Achievements

- Warmke (1942) and Warmke and Davidson (1943-44) demonstrated they could significantly raise or lower cannabinoid levels by selective breeding
- Modern drug strains are the result of generations of selection for high THC content
- Triploid and tetraploid strains of Cannabis with up to twice the potency of diploid strains have been produced
- Medicinal Cannabis of the late 1800s and early 1900s was usually Indian ganja
