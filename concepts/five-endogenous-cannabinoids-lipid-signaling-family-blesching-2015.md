# Five Endogenous Cannabinoids — The Complete Lipid Signaling Family

## Overview

The human body produces at least five distinct endogenous cannabinoids
(endocannabinoids) that serve as signaling molecules within the endocannabinoid
system (ECS). While anandamide (AEA) and 2-AG are the most studied, Blesching's
*[[blesching-cannabis-health-index-autoimmune-conditions]]* catalogs the full set of five identified lipid
mediators that interact with [[cannabinoid-receptors-cb1-cb2]]. Each has unique binding
profiles, biosynthetic pathways, and [[hemenway-mulch-makers-and-plant-functional-roles]].

## The Five Endocannabinoids

### 1. Anandamide (N-arachidonoylethanolamine, AEA)
Anandamide was the first endocannabinoid discovered and is often called the
"[[anandamide-bliss-molecule]]" (from the Sanskrit *ananda*, meaning bliss). It binds with
relatively equal affinity to both CB1 and CB2 receptors. Anandamide enhances
pleasure, has been implicated in mitigating acute schizophrenia episodes,
demonstrates anti-cancer properties, and suppresses cough reflex. It is rapidly
degraded by the enzyme fatty acid amide hydrolase (FAAH), giving it a short
half-life that limits its duration of action.

### 2. 2-Arachidonoyl Glycerol (2-AG)
2-AG is the most abundant endocannabinoid in the body, present at
concentrations orders of magnitude higher than anandamide in many tissues. It
is a full agonist at both CB1 and CB2 receptors. 2-AG is produced on-demand
from membrane phospholipids by the enzyme diacylglycerol lipase (DAGL) and is
degraded primarily by monoacylglycerol lipase (MAGL). 2-AG plays critical
roles in retrograde synaptic signaling, immune function, and inflammatory
regulation.

### 3. Noladin Ether (2-Arachidonoyl-glyceryl-ether)
Noladin ether was isolated in 2001 and shown to bind to CB1 receptors. It is
structurally similar to 2-AG but contains an ether linkage rather than an ester
bond, which confers greater metabolic stability. Noladin ether has been
implicated in pain modulation and may play a role in ocular function,
particularly intraocular pressure regulation. Its pharmacological profile
remains less characterized than anandamide or 2-AG.

### 4. Virodhamine (O-Arachidonoyl-ethanolamine)
Virodhamine was identified in 2002. The name derives from the Sanskrit
*virodha*, meaning opposition or antagonism — reflecting its initial
characterization as a partial agonist/antagonist at CB1 receptors with lower
efficacy than anandamide. Virodhamine exists in equilibrium with anandamide
through a thioester exchange mechanism, suggesting the two molecules may serve
as a physiological push-pull system for modulating CB1 tone. Virodhamine also
acts as a full agonist at CB2 receptors.

### 5. N-Arachidonoyl-dopamine (NADA)
NADA was discovered in 2000 and is unique among endocannabinoids because it
contains a dopamine moiety, creating a molecular bridge between the
endocannabinoid and dopaminergic systems. NADA is an agonist at both CB1 and
vanilloid TRPV1 receptors (the capsaicin receptor), making it a dual-function
signaling molecule. This dual activity links pain perception, thermoregulation,
and neuroinflammation through a single endogenous compound.

## Biosynthetic and Degradative Enzymes

The five endocannabinoids share a common biosynthetic origin: they are all
derivatives of arachidonic acid, a polyunsaturated omega-6 fatty acid
incorporated into cell membrane phospholipids. Their production is "on-demand"
rather than storage-based — they are synthesized and released in response to
specific physiological signals and rapidly degraded by dedicated enzymes:

| Endocannabinoid | Primary Synthesis Enzyme | Primary Degradation Enzyme |
|---|---|---|
| Anandamide (AEA) | NAPE-PLD | FAAH |
| 2-AG | DAGL (α and β isoforms) | MAGL |
| Noladin Ether | Unknown (possibly DAGL) | Unknown |
| Virodhamine | NAPE-PLD (same pathway as AEA) | FAAH |
| NADA | Unknown | FAAH |

The shared reliance on FAAH for degradation means that FAAH inhibitors
(elevated indirectly by CBD) can simultaneously increase the levels of
multiple endocannabinoids, not just anandamide.

## Non-CB1/CB2 Receptors

Blesching notes that scientists suspect at least three additional
endocannabinoid receptors beyond CB1 and CB2, referred to as "non-CB1 and
non-CB2" receptors. These may include:

- **GPR55**: An orphan G-protein coupled receptor activated by several
  cannabinoids, sometimes called CB3
- **GPR18**: Activated by abnormal cannabidiol (Abn-CBD) and NADA
- **GPR119**: Activated by oleoylethanolamide (OEA), a related lipid mediator

The existence of additional receptors means the five endocannabinoids likely
exert effects beyond what CB1/CB2 binding alone can explain, particularly in
tissues with low CB1/CB2 density.

## Clinical Significance

Understanding the full family of endocannabinoids has practical implications
for therapeutic strategies:

- **[[blesching-cbd-faah-inhibition-anandamide-preservation-mechanism]]** (via CBD or synthetic inhibitors) boosts multiple
  endocannabinoids simultaneously, producing broader [[cbd-therapeutic-effects]] than
  targeting any single compound
- **NADA's dual CB1/TRPV1 activity** makes it relevant to pain conditions
  where both cannabinoid and vanilloid pathways are involved
- **The anandamide-virodhamine equilibrium** may represent an intrinsic
  homeostatic mechanism for fine-tuning CB1 receptor activation
- **2-AG's abundance** suggests it is the workhorse of retrograde synaptic
  signaling, while anandamide may serve more specialized modulatory roles
