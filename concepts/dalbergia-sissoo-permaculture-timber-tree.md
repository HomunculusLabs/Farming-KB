# Dalbergia Sissoo — Permaculture Timber and Forage Tree

Dalbergia sissoo, commonly known as sissoo, is a fast-growing leguminous tree that plays a significant role in permaculture systems across semi-arid and [[riparian-zone-restoration]]
- Gully stabilisation
- Bank protection along waterways
- Slope stabilisation in [[charcoal-production-for-cooking]]
- Can be managed in coppice rotation for sustainable fuel production

## Fodder Value

Dalbergia sissoo provides supplementary forage for livestock:

- **Young branches and leaves** are browsed by deer and cattle
- Young plants are particularly palatable
- Provides protein-rich fodder during dry periods when other forage is scarce
- Can be managed as a pollarded or coppiced fodder bank

## Permaculture Design Applications

### Agroforestry Systems
- **Alley cropping**: Sissoo rows can provide [[hemenway-backyard-wildlife-habitat]]

### Coppice Management
- Responds well to coppice rotation for sustainable fuelwood production
- Suckering habit ensures continuous regeneration without replanting
- Can be managed on short (5–10 year) rotations for firewood or longer (15–25 year) rotations for timber

### Windbreaks and Shelterbelts
- Fast growth provides rapid wind protection
- Hardy to a wide range of temperatures and moisture conditions
- Deciduous nature allows winter sun penetration while providing summer shade

### Nitrogen Fixation
- As a legume, sissoo fixes atmospheric nitrogen through rhizobial symbiosis
- Improves soil fertility for companion plantings
- Reduces or eliminates need for nitrogen fertilisers in associated crops

## Companion Planting

Sissoo combines well with:
- **Grasses and cereals** in agroforestry systems (benefiting from nitrogen fixation)
- **Fruit trees** as a nitrogen-fixing nurse tree
- **Other multi-purpose trees** in mixed woodlots
- **Forage grasses** in silvopastoral systems

## Limitations and Considerations

- **Heavy clay soils**: Does not perform well; select lighter, well-drained soils
- **Suckering**: The vigorous suckering habit can be undesirable near infrastructure or in formal landscapes
- **Frost**: While frost hardy, severe or prolonged frost may damage young growth
- **Size**: Reaching up to 30 m, it requires adequate space for full development

## Propagation

- Primarily propagated from seed
- Direct seeding or nursery-grown seedlings
- Hard seed coat may require scarification for uniform germination
- Fast early growth allows rapid establishment

## Summary

Dalbergia sissoo is one of the most versatile multi-purpose trees available for permaculture in semi-arid and tropical regions. Its combination of valuable timber, nitrogen fixation, erosion control, livestock forage, and coppice resilience makes it an excellent choice for integrated land management systems seeking to maximise yields while building soil and ecosystem health.
