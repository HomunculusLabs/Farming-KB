## AACT Fungal Dominant Tea

Updated: 2026-04-18

Fungal dominant compost tea is an actively [[aact-disease-suppression-mechanisms-ingham]] against mildew and other fungal pathogens, and building soil structure in mature ecosystems.

## When to Use Fungal Tea

Fungal dominant tea is appropriate when:

- Managing fungal diseases such as mildew, botrytis, and fusarium
- Growing perennial plants, trees, shrubs, and vines (which prefer fungal soils)
- Building soil structure in perennial systems (fungal hyphae bind soil aggregates)
- Transitioning from [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungal-diversity-patterns-distribution]] partners
- Managing root-feeding pests (certain fungi are predators of root-feeding nematodes)

## The Oxygen Critical Threshold

As oxygen concentration drops below 5-6 ppm (depending on species composition), facultative anaerobic bacteria begin to attack fungal hyphae and kill them. This is why adequate aeration is absolutely critical for fungal tea. Machines that do not maintain adequate oxygen will lose fungal biomass even if it was extracted successfully.

## Filtering and Application

Fungal hyphae are large (up to 100 micrometers) and easily filtered out. Use mesh sizes of 300-400 micrometers for filtration. Fine organic matter will clog smaller openings and take fungi with it. When using spray equipment, ensure nozzle openings are at least 200 micrometers, preferably 400 micrometers, to allow fungal colonies to pass through.

Spray pressure and impact kill organisms -- it is the impact of organisms hitting a surface, not pressure in the line, that causes damage. Keep spray pressure low relative to distance (20-40 psi at 1-2 feet; 100 psi at 6 feet).

## Disease Suppression

Fungal tea provides disease suppression through multiple mechanisms:
- Direct competition for leaf surface and root zone space
- Production of antibiotics and enzymes that inhibit pathogens
- Physical occupation of infection sites
- Trichoderma species actively parasitize pathogenic fungi
