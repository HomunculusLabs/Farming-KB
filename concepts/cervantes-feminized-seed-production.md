# Feminized Seed Production

Feminized seeds are produced by obtaining pollen from one female
individual and subsequently fertilizing another female plant. The result
is a [[cervantes-cannabis-seed-purchasing-storage]] industry.

## Chromosome Basis of Sex in Cannabis

Cannabis has 20 chromosomes in each cell of the plant. The 10th pair
of chromosomes, the smallest pair, are the sex chromosomes. Female
cannabis plants have two copies of the X chromosome, therefore their
genotype is XX. Male plants have only one copy of the X chromosome and
a Y chromosome instead of a second X. The genotype of male plants in
terms of the sex chromosomes is XY.

When pollen is created within the plant, one of each of the chromosome
pairs is packaged into the cells that develop into pollen. Each pollen
grain or ovule contains 10 chromosomes, one copy of each pair. When
pollen deposits genetic material into the ovule, the 10 chromosomes
from the pollen and the ovule unite to make a total of 20 chromosomes.

## Punnett Square Analysis

From a typical male (XY) x female (XX) cross, a Punnett square shows
half the plants are XY (male) and half are XX (female).

From a female (XX) x female (XX) cross, the Punnett square shows
female:female breeding schemes produce only female (XX) offspring. This
is the genetic basis of feminized seed production.

## Methods of Producing Feminized Seeds

### Method 1: Stress-Induced Intersex Pollen (Problematic)

Some growers intentionally use pollen from intersex plants to fertilize
females. They have found that the seeds and subsequent offspring
produced from this union will be predominantly female. The major
problem with this technique is that these plants will have intersex
tendencies. By selecting parent plants that have intersex tendencies,
we ensure that some of the progeny will also have intersex tendencies.
Using pollen from an intersex or hermaphrodite plant is an intentional
selection for intersexuality -- like begets like.
