## Warming Soil Strategies

Solomon describes several practical methods for warming garden soil earlier in the season:

### Raised Beds

Raised beds warm faster than flat ground because:
- The increased surface area exposes more soil to solar radiation
- Drainage is improved, reducing the cooling effect of water-saturated soil
- Air circulation around bed edges provides additional warming

Beds raised 6 to 12 inches can be 5 to 10 degrees Fahrenheit (3 to 6 degrees Celsius) warmer than surrounding flat ground in early spring.

### Plastic Mulch

Clear or black plastic laid over prepared beds in early spring creates a greenhouse effect:
- Clear plastic warms soil faster but promotes weed growth beneath it
- Black plastic suppresses weeds while still providing significant warming
- Infrared-transmitting plastic (IRT) combines weed suppression with soil warming nearly as effective as clear plastic
- Lay plastic 2 to 3 weeks before intended planting date for maximum warming

### Cloches and Row Covers

Temporary covers protect plants from cold nights while allowing daytime warming:
- Floating row covers provide 2 to 4 degrees Fahrenheit (1 to 2 degrees Celsius) of [[fungal-nitrogen-mineralization-pathways]]**: The conversion of organic nitrogen to plant-available forms is temperature-dependent. Cold soil means slow nitrogen release, potentially starving plants even when [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi]] that extend root nutrient-absorbing capacity are inactive in cold soil
- **[[comparison-biological-nitrogen-fixation-vs-synthetic-fertilizer]]**: Legume root nodules fix atmospheric nitrogen less efficiently in cold soil
