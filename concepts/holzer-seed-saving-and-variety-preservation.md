# Holzer Seed Saving and Variety Preservation

The preservation of old [[the-ultimate-goal-of-farming]] and [[sepp-holzer-method]]. Holzer's experiences consistently show that [[holzer-old-varieties-and-seed-saving]] of cereal, vegetable, and potato make the best crops -- better flavour, less demanding, and far better adapted to local conditions than modern overbred varieties.

## Why Old Varieties

In earlier times, every country, indeed every region, had its own cereal, vegetable, potato, and herb varieties. These were bred over generations through selection to become plants best suited to local conditions. Often they had no names of their own, having been propagated on a single farm. Key advantages:

- Better flavour and higher nutritional value
- Less demanding of soil and inputs
- Better adapted to local climatic conditions
- Greater disease resistance through natural adaptation
- Can cope with the poorest soils and most extreme temperatures
- Nutritional value often far exceeds that of monoculture-grown modern varieties

## Against Hybrid and GM Seeds

Holzer is emphatic that [[heirloom-vs-hybrid-seeds-comparison]] (F1 varieties) are entirely unsuited to permaculture systems:

- Most can no longer reproduce -- they do not breed true
- Must always be bought new ("much to the satisfaction of seed companies")
- Come from homozygous inbred lines, frequently sterile
- Not suited to varying local conditions
- Give good yields but are more susceptible to disease in monoculture
- Lead to pesticide dependence

He considers [[holzer-climate-zone-microclimate-creation]] Holzer creates serve [[cervantes-seed-production-storage]] by allowing spatial isolation of varieties. Multi-layered, structured habitats with hedges and tall raised beds between seed crops protect wind-pollinated varieties from cross-contamination.
