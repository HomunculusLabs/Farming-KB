# Duggar Mushroom Spawn Preparation Techniques

Duggar devoted an entire chapter to spawn-making, documenting both the
historical empirical methods and his revolutionary pure culture
approach that transformed the American mushroom industry.

## The Nature of Spawn

Spawn is the visible, extensive growth of mycelium -- the vegetative
phase of the mushroom. Although spores are the reproductive bodies,
they cannot be used directly in cultivation. Under favorable conditions,
spores germinate to produce germ tubes that develop into threadlike
mycelium, which penetrates the substratum in all directions. The
fresh, undried spawn of Agaricus campestris has a pleasing aroma of
fresh mushrooms combined with a slight almond character.

## Virgin Spawn: The Chance Method

For centuries, growers depended on "spontaneous" or "native"
occurrences of spawn in pastures, compost piles, or elsewhere. This
was called "virgin" spawn, presumably from accidental spore
germination. In England, "mill-track" virgin spawn was originally
found in the well-trodden soil and manure of horse-driven mill paths
and later became merely a trade name.

The traditional procedure involved finding natural mushroom occurrences
in a favorable locality, removing the sod, preparing small trenches
filled with good stable manure, and returning the sod. Spawn would
grow into the manure over several weeks. This material served either
to inoculate beds directly or to produce "crop spawn" in larger
quantities.

In France, virgin spawn was used to heavily inoculate small beds of
compost where the mycelium was permitted to spread without fruiting;
the bed was then broken down to make flake spawn. In England, virgin
spawn inoculated compost bricks for brick spawn production. Near Paris,
some persons made a business of searching for and selling virgin spawn
at high prices, becoming adept at distinguishing Agaricus campestris
and its varieties by growth character and odor.

## The Running-Out Problem

Successive transfer of spawn from bed to bed was known to cause
complete "running-out" or deterioration after about three years. No
transfer should be made from a bed that has begun to bear mushrooms
vigorously. This biological limitation made virgin spawn essential
for periodic renewal under the old system.

## Types of Commercial Spawn

Two principal types dominated international trade:

- **English Brick Spawn**: Dried, compressed blocks of manure
  permeated with mushroom mycelium. Commonly used in England and
  the United States. Bricks were broken into 9-12 pieces (hen's egg
  size), one piece per square foot of bed space.

- **French Flake Spawn**: Fresh, moist manure containing active
  mycelium, sold in bulk. Monopolized the French market. Most large
  French growers prepared their own using a "starter" of virgin
  spawn. This type rapidly lost vitality under conditions of
  importation to America.

## The Spawn Reliability Crisis

From 1901 to 1904, Duggar purchased samples of commercial spawn in
various American cities and tested them at Columbia, Missouri. An
unexpectedly large majority proved dead. Imported spawn suffered from
additional shipping time, storage conditions, and sometimes being
carried over from one season to the next. English and French growers
did not experience comparable difficulties.

## Pure Culture Method: The Tissue Culture Revolution

In 1899, working in his laboratory, Duggar and Miss Ferguson
investigated spore germination conditions but encountered
difficulties. Meanwhile, Duggar discovered that fragments of growing
mushroom tissue, obtained under aseptic conditions, could serve as
starting points for pure cultures. A small piece of inner tissue from
a fresh mushroom, placed on suitable sterile nutrient medium, promptly
develops mycelium.

### Making Pure Cultures

The procedure, described in Bureau of Plant Industry Bulletin 85,
involved:

1. Sterilizing large test tubes or wide-mouthed bottles with cotton
   plugs in a dry oven at 150 C for an hour, or preliminarily with
   dry heat.

2. Partially filling tubes (about two-thirds) with manure or
   half-decayed leaves.

3. Sterilizing in a steam boiler (1 hour on each of 2-3 successive
   days at 100 C) or in an autoclave (15 pounds pressure for 15-30
   minutes).

4. Selecting desirable mushrooms from beds in full bearing --
   considering size, quality, color, form, and prolificness. Plants
   with the veil still intact were especially desirable.

5. Removing small pieces of internal tissue with a sterilized
   scalpel, transferring to tubes without contacting unsterilized
   objects.

6. Incubating at room temperature. Slight growth appeared in several
   days, becoming very evident in a week, with mold-like mycelium
   spreading through the medium in three weeks.

## Selection and Propagation

The pure culture method enabled, for the first time in centuries of
cultivation, the selection and maintenance of specific varieties.
One could select for long bearing period, resistance to higher
temperatures, or other characteristics. The contents of one large
tube could spawn several bricks. Duggar advised no more than one
transfer from the initial culture tubes to bricks, as continuous
transference was injurious to vigor.

## Impact

By 1913-14, 80-90 percent of American mushrooms were grown from
pure culture spawn. Poor spawn had been largely driven from markets.
The success of this approach was so great that American spawn was
exported to Europe. Several reliable firms mastered the necessary
bacteriological and mechanical details, launching successful
enterprises, though some irresponsible firms also exploited the new
movement to sell inferior product.
