# Cannabis Strain Selection and Receptor Targeting

## Overview

Selecting the right cannabis strain requires understanding how cannabinoids
interact with CB1 and CB2 receptors. These receptors function like locks,
with cannabinoids serving as keys. When binding occurs, signals produce
physiological, mental, and emotional changes. Over 111 cannabis-based
cannabinoids have been isolated, each with unique receptor affinities.

## CB1 Receptor Distribution and Function

CB1 receptors are concentrated in the central nervous system (CNS),
including the brain and spinal cord. Key regions include:

- **Hippocampus** — memory, learning, and neurogenesis
- **Cerebellum** — movement and motor control
- **Basal ganglia** — reward, motivation, habit formation
- **Cerebral cortex** — cognition, perception, consciousness
- **Amygdala** — fear and anxiety processing

CB1 receptors are also found in peripheral nerves, liver, and GI tract.
Activation is primarily responsible for psychoactive effects. CB1
engagement initiates neurogenesis, neuroprotection, and modulates mood,
pain perception, and appetite.

## CB2 Receptor Distribution and Function

CB2 receptors are concentrated in peripheral tissues and immune system:

- **B cells and T cells** — adaptive immune response
- **Macrophages** — innate immune defense and tissue repair
- **Microglia** — CNS immune cells
- **Spleen and tonsils** — immune system organs

CB2 activation does not produce psychoactive effects. Instead, it
initiates potent anti-inflammatory actions, modulates immune responses,
and provides peripheral pain relief. CB2 receptors are increasingly
recognized as therapeutic targets for inflammatory and autoimmune
conditions.

## Cannabinoid Receptor Affinity Profiles

| Cannabinoid | CB1 | CB2 | Psychoactive |
|-------------|-----|-----|-------------|
| THC | High | High | Yes |
| Anandamide (AEA) | High | High | Endogenous |
| CBD | Low | Moderate | No |
| CBG | Moderate | Moderate | Minimal |
| CBN | Low | High | Mild |
| CBC | None known | None known | No |
| HU210 | Very High | Moderate | Yes |
| WIN55,212-2 | Moderate | High | Yes |
| (E)-beta-caryophyllene | None | High | No |

CBD exerts much of its therapeutic influence by suppressing FAAH, the
enzyme that breaks down anandamide, keeping "the Bliss Molecule" active
at higher concentrations and longer duration.

## Strain Selection Framework

### Sativa-Dominant Strains

Sativas have higher THC:CBD ratios, favoring CB1 activation:

- Cerebral, stimulating, energizing effects
- Enhanced creativity, focus, mood elevation
- Daytime use suitability
- Sought for depression, fatigue, ADHD, mood disorders

### Indica-Dominant Strains

Indicas have lower THC:CBD ratios, favoring CB2 activation:

- Body-centered, sedating, relaxing effects
- Pain relief, muscle relaxation
- Anti-inflammatory and immune modulation
- Nighttime use suitability
- Sought for chronic pain, insomnia, anxiety, spasms

### Hybrid Strains

Hybrids combine sativa and indica characteristics. The genetic ratio
influences whether effects lean cerebral or physical.

## The Entourage Effect

Cannabis contains 70+ cannabinoids and hundreds of non-cannabinoid
constituents including terpenoids and flavonoids. These may work in
concert to produce therapeutic effects exceeding the sum of individual
cannabinoid actions. Whole-spectrum cannabis activates both CB1 and CB2
through combined action of multiple cannabinoids.

Pharmaceutical isolates like Dronabinol contain only THC and lack
whole-plant balancing effects. Sativex is a plant-derived exception.

## (E)-beta-Caryophyllene: The Dietary Cannabinoid

An international research team (Switzerland, Germany, Italy, U.S.)
reported that spice plants contain a non-psychoactive CB2 agonist called
(E)-beta-caryophyllene or (E)-BCP, considered a food-based cannabinoid
with FDA GRAS approval. Key sources:

- **Black Ashanti pepper** — 58% (E)-BCP content
- **White Ashanti pepper** — 52%
- **Indian bay-leaf** — 25%
- **Black pepper** — 7-19%
- **Cinnamon, rosemary, clove, oregano, basil** — also contain (E)-BCP

Cannabis sativa ranges from 12-35% (E)-BCP content by comparison.

## Practical Strain Selection Guide

1. Identify the target condition — neurological/CNS (CB1) or
   immune/inflammatory (CB2)?
2. Match receptor profile to strain type — sativa for CB1, indica for CB2
3. Consider the entourage effect — whole-plant products may outperform
   isolates
4. Start low, go slow — individual responses vary significantly
5. Combine with dietary cannabinoids — spices provide additional CB2
   activation

## Key Takeaways

- CB1 receptors (brain/CNS) produce psychoactive effects and modulate
  mood, memory, and pain
- CB2 receptors (immune/peripheral) provide non-psychoactive anti-
  inflammatory and immune-modulating effects
- Sativa-dominant strains favor CB1 for cerebral effects; indica-dominant
  favor CB2 for body effects
- The entourage effect suggests whole-plant cannabis outperforms isolates
- Common spices provide dietary CB2 activation via (E)-BCP
