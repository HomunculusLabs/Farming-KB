Because humanity has no way of recognizing that its accumulated knowledge is fundamentally unknowable and mistaken, it founders ever deeper:

> "Human knowledge is merely discriminating knowledge. Having no way of knowing that this knowledge is really unknowable, man founders ever deeper into confusion through the collection and advancement of unknowable and mistaken knowledge."

The result is spiritual derangement — a civilization that knows more and more about less and less, while losing sight of the whole. This derangement constitutes the first collapse: the breakdown of the human capacity to perceive truth.

### 2. Destruction of Life and Matter

The second stage is ecological. The Earth, which Fukuoka describes as a living system of organic synthesis, is being systematically broken down by human activity. The natural world is deprived of its equilibrium. Ecosystems are destroyed. The natural order — the self-regulating harmony that sustains all life — unravels.

In this condition, humanity faces two possible fates. It may gradually lose its biological adaptability and self-destruct through accumulated degradation. Or, more dramatically, it may:

> "succumb to instant ruin under a slight pressure from without, like an inflated rubber balloon ruptured by a small needle."

Civilization, inflated by artificial energy inputs and stripped of natural resilience, becomes brittle. A small shock — climate disruption, resource shortage, pandemic — could trigger catastrophic failure.

### 3. Failure of Purpose

The third stage is institutional and economic. Modern industrial activity, Fukuoka argues, is not purposeful production but a campaign to promote energy consumption. The target of industry is not human welfare but the senseless waste of energy. As materials and resources dry up, the self-contradiction at the heart of the system accumulates toward explosive levels. The result will be drastic and unavoidable changes in political, economic, and social institutions — not reforms, but ruptures.

## Energy Efficiency Decline

The most concrete evidence Fukuoka offers for this self-contradiction is the measurable decline in energy efficiency across the history of human technology:

> "Self-contradiction is most evident in the decline in energy efficiency."

Consider the progression of energy technologies. The fireplace converts fuel to warmth with minimal overhead. The water wheel harnesses gravitational energy with modest infrastructure. Thermal power generation requires vast extraction, transportation, and conversion chains. Nuclear power demands mining, enrichment, plant construction, waste management, and decommissioning — an apparatus of staggering complexity. When total energy input (including all ancillary processes) is measured against useful output, efficiency worsens exponentially in precisely this order:

fireplace → water wheel → thermal power → nuclear power

Each "advance" consumes more energy to deliver less net benefit. Yet humanity persists in the illusion of progress:

> "Because he refuses to acknowledge this, internal contradiction continues to accumulate and will soon reach explosive levels."

Even technologies celebrated as clean solutions — solar panels, wind turbines — are non-polluting only in their operational phase. Their manufacture, installation, maintenance, and disposal continue the broader pattern of declining efficiency. Far from reversing the trajectory, they accelerate the speed toward destruction by adding new layers of industrial complexity.

## Only Natural Farming Lies in the Future

Fukuoka"s prognosis is severe, but it is not nihilistic. He identifies a clear path of survival — though it requires a fundamental inversion of values:

> "Until man notices that scientific truth is not the same as absolute truth and turns his system of values on its head, he will continue to rush blindly onward toward self-destruction."

The inversion required is this: doing nothing becomes the highest form of action. Non-intervention becomes the most sophisticated technology. After the collapse of scientific civilization, there will be no grand projects, no technological salvations:

> "There will then be nothing for him to do except sustain an attitude that enables him to survive without doing anything."

Life will simplify radically:

> "Man"s only work then will consist of the barest of farming essential for sustaining life."

Fukuoka ranks [[fukuoka-textdoc-three-stages-of-human-collapse-and-natural-farming-future]] — which requires no external inputs, no plowing, no chemicals, no machinery beyond the human body — achieves the highest energy efficiency of all.

This is not a regression to primitivism. Natural farming, for Fukuoka, is paradoxically the most advanced form of agriculture:

> "Only natural farming lies in the future. Natural farming is the only future for man."

It occupies an "immutable and unshakable position that transcends time and space." It is always simultaneously the oldest and the newest form of farming — the practice of the first humans and the inevitable practice of the last. In this sense, natural farming stands at the very leading edge of modern agriculture, not behind it but ahead of it, waiting for civilization to complete its arc and return.
