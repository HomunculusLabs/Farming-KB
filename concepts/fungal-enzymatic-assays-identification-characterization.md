# Fungal Enzymatic Assays for Identification and Characterization
Updated: 2026-04-18
Fungal enzymatic assays are biochemical tests used to differentiate
fungal species, detect metabolic capabilities, and characterise
functional traits. These tests complement morphological and molecular
[[forensic-identification-methods-psilocybin-mushrooms-tlc-gc-hplc]], and are particularly valuable for
distinguishing morphologically similar species and assessing ecological
roles.

## Major Enzyme Assays

### Laccase Assay

Laccases (benzenediol:oxygen oxidoreductases, EC 1.10.3.2) are
multi-copper oxidases produced by many fungi, especially white-rot
basidiomycetes. They catalyse the oxidation of phenolic and
non-phenolic compounds. Detection methods:

- **Gum guaiac test:** Plates are flooded with 1% gum guaiac in 95%
  ethanol. Laccase-positive cultures develop an orange-brown colour
  within 5 minutes as guaiacol oxidises.
- **Syringaldazine test:** More specific for laccase; 0.1%
  syringaldazine in ethanol applied to cultures. Positive reaction:
  pink to purple colour change.
- **ABTS (2,2'-azino-bis(3-ethylbenzothiazoline-6-sulfonic acid)):**
  Green colour development indicates laccase activity.
- **TMB (3,3',5,5'-tetramethylbenzidine):** Blue-green colour
  development.
Laccase production is characteristic of white-rot fungi and is
important in lignin degradation, bioremediation, and fungal
identification keys. See [[fungal-enzymatic-capabilities]] for a
comprehensive survey of fungal enzyme systems.

### Tyrosinase Assay

Tyrosinases (monophenol monooxygenase, EC 1.14.18.1) catalyse the
oxidation of monophenols to o-diphenols and their further oxidation to
o-quinones. Detection:

- **Catechol test:** Plates flooded with 1% catechol solution.
  Tyrosinase-positive cultures develop brown to black colouration as
  catechol is oxidised to o-quinone and further polymerised.
- **L-DOPA test:** L-3,4-dihydroxyphenylalanine used as substrate;
  positive reaction produces dark melanin-like pigments.
- **Tyrosine test:** L-tyrosine as substrate; melanin production
  indicates tyrosinase activity.
Tyrosinase is important in [[fungal-melanin-biosynthesis-ecological-functions]] and is characteristic
of many dematiaceous (dark-pigmented) fungi.

### Lignin Peroxidase (LiP) Assay

Lignin peroxidases (EC 1.11.1.14) are heme-containing peroxidases
involved in lignin degradation, primarily produced by white-rot
basidiomycetes. Detection:

- **Azure B decolorisation:** Azure B dye (a polymer model for lignin)
  incorporated into agar. Clear halos around colonies indicate lignin
  peroxidase activity.
- **Poly R-478 decolorisation:** Synthetic dye decolorisation as a
  proxy for ligninolytic activity.
- **[[fungal-lignin-peroxidase-veratryl-alcohol]] oxidation:** Measured spectrophotometrically at
  310nm.
LiP is one of the key enzymes distinguishing white-rot from brown-rot
fungi. See [[fungal-enzymatic-diversity-decomposition]] for the role
of these enzymes in ecosystem [[fungal-decomposition-processes]].

### Lipase Assay

Fungal lipases (triacylglycerol acylhydrolase, EC 3.1.1.3) hydrolyse
ester bonds in lipids. Detection:

- **Tween 80 opacity test:** Tween 80 incorporated into agar at 1%.
  Lipase-positive colonies develop an opaque precipitate (calcium
  oleate) around the colony within 24-48 hours. This is one of the
  simplest and most widely used lipase tests.
- **Tributyrin agar:** Clear halos around colonies indicate lipase
  activity.
- **Spirit blue agar:** Blue dye complexed with triglycerides; lipase
  activity releases dye, producing a dark blue halo.
Lipase activity is useful for identifying dermatophytes, Malassezia
species, and other lipid-dependent fungi.

### Cellulase Assay

Cellulases are enzyme complexes that hydrolyse cellulose to glucose.
They are critical for understanding the ecological role of fungi in
plant litter decomposition. Detection:

- **Carboxymethyl cellulose (CMC) agar:** Clear zones around colonies
  after flooding with Congo red indicate cellulase activity.
- **Filter paper degradation:** Weight loss of filter paper strips

## Diazonium Blue B (DBB) Reaction

DBB is a histochemical stain that differentiates Ascomycota from
Basidiomycota. When applied to fungal hyphae or fruiting body tissue,
ascomycetes produce a red to cherry-red colour, while basidiomycetes
remain unstained (yellow to brownish). This test is valuable for
uncertain taxonomic placements.
**CRITICAL SAFETY WARNING:** Diazonium blue B is extremely carcinogenic
and sensitive to oxidation above 0C. It must be stored desiccated at
-20C. Handle with extreme care using appropriate personal protective
equipment and fume hoods. Do not prepare solutions in advance; make
fresh for each use.

## Application in Species Identification

Enzymatic profiles are used in dichotomous keys for various fungal
groups:

- Distinguishing dermatophyte species (keratinase, lipase, urease
  patterns)
- Differentiating yeasts (assimilation and fermentation profiles of
  various carbohydrates)
- Identifying white-rot vs. brown-rot decay fungi (laccase, LiP,
  Mn-peroxidase patterns)
- Characterising extremophilic fungi (thermostable enzyme profiles)
- Assessing bioremediation potential of fungal isolates (enzyme
  activity against specific pollutants)

## Molecular Methods Complementing Enzymatic Assays

Enzymatic assays are increasingly used alongside molecular techniques
for comprehensive fungal identification:

- **DNA barcoding:** [[dna-barcoding-fungal-identification]] using
  ITS regions provides species-level identification that enzymatic
  profiles alone cannot achieve
- **Enzyme gene expression:** Quantitative PCR can measure expression
  levels of specific enzyme genes under different conditions,
  providing insight into fungal [[arbuscule-isolation-metabolic-activity-assays]]
- **Metagenomic enzyme screening:** Environmental DNA samples can be
  screened for enzyme gene diversity without cultivation
- **Proteomic profiling:** Mass spectrometry of fungal secretomes
  reveals the full complement of [[fungal-extracellular-enzymes]] produced
The combination of enzymatic assays with molecular methods provides
both functional and taxonomic information, which is especially
valuable for [[fungal-biodiversity-assessment-methods]] in
complex environmental samples.
