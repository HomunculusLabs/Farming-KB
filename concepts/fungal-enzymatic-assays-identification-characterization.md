# Fungal Enzymatic Assays for Identification and Characterization

Updated: 2026-04-18

Fungal enzymatic assays are biochemical tests used to differentiate fungal species, detect metabolic capabilities, and characterize functional traits. These tests complement morphological and molecular identification methods, and are particularly valuable for distinguishing morphologically similar species and assessing ecological roles.

## Major Enzyme Assays

### Laccase Assay
Laccases (benzenediol:oxygen oxidoreductases, EC 1.10.3.2) are multi-copper oxidases produced by many fungi, especially white-rot basidiomycetes. They catalyze the oxidation of phenolic and non-phenolic compounds.

Detection methods:
- **Gum guaiac test:** Plates are flooded with 1% gum guaiac in 95% ethanol. Laccase-positive cultures develop an orange-brown color within 5 minutes as guaiacol oxidizes.
- **Syringaldazine test:** More specific for laccase; 0.1% syringaldazine in ethanol applied to cultures. Positive reaction: pink to purple color change.
- **ABTS (2,2'-azino-bis(3-ethylbenzothiazoline-6-sulfonic acid)):** Green color development indicates laccase activity.
- **TMB (3,3',5,5'-tetramethylbenzidine):** Blue-green color development.

Laccase production is characteristic of white-rot fungi and is important in lignin degradation, bioremediation, and fungal identification keys.

### Tyrosinase Assay
Tyrosinases (monophenol monooxygenase, EC 1.14.18.1) catalyze the oxidation of monophenols to o-diphenols and their further oxidation to o-quinones.

Detection:
- **Catechol test:** Plates flooded with 1% catechol solution. Tyrosinase-positive cultures develop brown to black coloration as catechol is oxidized to o-quinone and further polymerized.
- **L-DOPA test:** L-3,4-dihydroxyphenylalanine used as substrate; positive reaction produces dark melanin-like pigments.
- **Tyrosine test:** L-tyrosine as substrate; melanin production indicates tyrosinase activity.

Tyrosinase is important in melanin biosynthesis and is characteristic of many dematiaceous (dark-pigmented) fungi.

### Lignin Peroxidase (LiP) Assay
Lignin peroxidases (EC 1.11.1.14) are heme-containing peroxidases involved in lignin degradation, primarily produced by white-rot basidiomycetes.

Detection:
- **Azure B decolorization:** Azure B dye (a polymer model for lignin) incorporated into agar. Clear halos around colonies indicate lignin peroxidase activity.
- **Poly R-478 decolorization:** Synthetic dye decolorization as a proxy for ligninolytic activity.
- **Veratryl alcohol oxidation:** Measured spectrophotometrically at 310nm.

LiP is one of the key enzymes distinguishing white-rot from brown-rot fungi.

### Lipase Assay
Fungal lipases (triacylglycerol acylhydrolase, EC 3.1.1.3) hydrolyze ester bonds in lipids.

Detection:
- **Tween 80 opacity test:** Tween 80 incorporated into agar at 1%. Lipase-positive colonies develop an opaque precipitate (calcium oleate) around the colony within 24-48 hours. This is one of the simplest and most widely used lipase tests.
- **Tributyrin agar:** Clear halos around colonies indicate lipase activity.
- **Spirit blue agar:** Blue dye complexed with triglycerides; lipase activity releases dye, producing a dark blue halo.

Lipase activity is useful for identifying dermatophytes, Malassezia species, and other lipid-dependent fungi.

## Diazonium Blue B (DBB) Reaction

DBB is a histochemical stain that differentiates Ascomycota from Basidiomycota. When applied to fungal hyphae or fruiting body tissue, ascomycetes produce a red to cherry-red color, while basidiomycetes remain unstained (yellow to brownish). This test is valuable for uncertain taxonomic placements.

**CRITICAL SAFETY WARNING:** Diazonium blue B is extremely carcinogenic and sensitive to oxidation above 0C. It must be stored desiccated at -20C. Handle with extreme care using appropriate personal protective equipment and fume hoods. Do not prepare solutions in advance; make fresh for each use.

## Application in Species Identification

Enzymatic profiles are used in dichotomous keys for various fungal groups:
- Distinguishing dermatophyte species (keratinase, lipase, urease patterns)
- Differentiating yeasts (assimilation and fermentation profiles of various carbohydrates)
- Identifying white-rot vs. brown-rot decay fungi (laccase, LiP, Mn-peroxidase patterns)
- Characterizing extremophilic fungi (thermostable enzyme profiles)
- Assessing bioremediation potential of fungal isolates (enzyme activity against specific pollutants)

## Standardized Protocols

For reproducible results, assays should be performed:
- On standardized media at controlled temperatures
- With appropriate positive and negative controls
- At consistent incubation periods (typically 5-14 days depending on test)
- With documented inoculum density

Multiple tests should be run in combination to build an enzymatic profile, as single tests are rarely definitive.
