# Fukuoka World of Nothing

## Overview

In the closing philosophical chapters of "The Road Back to Nature," Fukuoka describes his own experience of the "world of nothing" -- a state of being he first tasted after quitting his post at Yokohama Customs. He contrasts this authentic experience with the false paths people take in seeking God through knowledge, ritual, and intellectual pursuit.

## The Taste of Nothing

"I never knew such complete ease and happiness. I lived in a world of great joy. Ever since, I have merely lived my life while recalling those days. I found no joy in anything else." No matter what Fukuoka did, the happiest moments were those when he had nothing at all on his mind. To this day, everything he has done in many places has been mere diversions.

In Tokyo, he has been treated to Japan's best buckwheat noodles, but to him the coarse fare in his orchard huts is plenty. If he works up an appetite on his hill, then buckwheat grown on that same hill suits him just fine.

## The Richest Life

The discovery that the world of nothing has everything is central to Fukuoka's philosophy:

- One needs nothing; it is enough to be able to eat, work, and sleep

- Why watch all sorts of famous movies when it is enough to open the window and look out at the green world outside

- Without learning any instruments or knowing the musical scale, one can enjoy the bird calls and the croaking of the frogs -- although noisy perhaps, these mix in their own way to form an orchestra

- By renouncing absolutely everything and breaking away from the world of man, one can discover that the world with nothing at all has everything and enables one to lead the richest life possible
