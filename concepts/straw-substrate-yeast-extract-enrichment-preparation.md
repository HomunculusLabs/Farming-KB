## Chopping and Preparation

The first step in substrate preparation is chopping the straw to an appropriate length:

- **Target size**: Straw should be chopped to lengths of approximately 3 to 10cm (1.5 to 4
inches). This size range provides a balance between surface area for colonization and
structural integrity for gas exchange. Pieces that are too long create dense mats with poor
aeration; pieces that are too short compact excessively and can restrict airflow.

- **Source options**: Pre-cut straw of appropriate length can be purchased from many pet
stores, where it is sold as animal bedding. Alternatively, whole straw bales can be obtained
from agricultural suppliers or farm stores and chopped manually using scissors, shears, or a
mechanical chopper. Hay should not be used as a substitute, as it contains seed heads that
can sprout and compete with the [[duggar-mushroom-life-cycle-spore-germination-fruiting]] and
mycelial growth:

- **Preparation**: One heaped tablespoon of Marmite (or equivalent yeast extract product such
as Vegemite) is added to a pouring jug and dissolved in a small amount of boiling water. The
yeast extract solution is then poured into a bucket, and approximately 6 liters of additional
boiling water is added to the bucket.

- **Soaking process**: The chopped straw is placed in a large watertight container, and the
yeast extract solution is poured over it. The straw should be fully submerged. A weight (such
as another container filled with water) is placed on top of the straw to keep it submerged as
it absorbs the liquid. After one hour, the water level should be checked to ensure the straw
remains fully covered; additional boiling water should be added if necessary, and the soak
continued for one more hour.

- **Purpose of yeast extract**: Yeast extract is a rich source of B vitamins, amino acids, and
minerals that serve as germination stimulants for mushroom spores. Jarrold specifically notes
that yeast extract is used "to help quicken the germination time of the spores." The B vitamin
complex in yeast extract is particularly important, as these vitamins serve as cofactors in
the metabolic enzymes that drive spore germination and early mycelial growth.

- **Pasteurization effect**: The use of boiling water serves a dual purpose — it hydrates the
straw and provides a degree of pasteurization by killing many of the competitor organisms
present on the straw. While this initial hot water treatment does not achieve full
sterilization (which is accomplished later in the [[double-layer-tinfoil-jar-covering-pressure-cooker-method]]), it significantly reduces
the initial microbial load on the substrate.

## Draining

After the two-hour soak, the straw must be thoroughly drained before supplementation and bag
filling. Jarrold describes turning the soaked straw out into a bathtub and leaving it to drain
for one hour. After the initial drain period, the straw should be spread out evenly in the tub
and checked for excessive moisture. If the straw is still too wet, the cultivator should use
their hands to physically squeeze surplus water from the straw. Proper moisture content is
critical — substrate that is too wet will promote bacterial contamination and restrict gas
exchange, while substrate that is too dry will slow mycelial growth.
