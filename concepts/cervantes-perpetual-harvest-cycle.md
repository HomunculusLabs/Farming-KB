# Perpetual Harvest Cycle for Cannabis

A perpetual harvest system maintains multiple growth stages simultaneously,
enabling growers to harvest cannabis on a regular, ongoing basis rather than
waiting for a single crop to complete its full cycle. Jorge Cervantes
describes this approach as the most efficient method for consistent production
in indoor gardens.

## How Perpetual Harvest Works

The concept is straightforward: maintain separate rooms or zones for
different growth stages, each with appropriate lighting and environmental
conditions. As one batch of plants moves from [[cervantes-vegetative-phase]] to [[cervantes-sex-determination-pre-flowering]], the next batch
moves into the vegetative space. This creates a continuous pipeline of plants
at various stages of development.

### The Three-Room Model

1. **Clone/mother room** - Maintains [[cervantes-mother-plants]]
   and roots new cervantes-cloning|clones under 18/6 or 24/0 lighting
2. **Vegetative room** - Grows established clones under 18/6 lighting until
   they reach desired size for flowering
3. **Flowering room** - Induces flowering with 12/12 lighting; plants
   flower for six to ten weeks before harvest

With this setup, a grower can harvest every four to eight weeks depending on
variety flowering time, rather than waiting three to four months for a single
crop cycle.

## Scheduling by Flowering Time

The harvest frequency depends on the flowering duration of the chosen
varieties:

- **Fast-flowering indicas (6-7 weeks)**: Harvest every 6-7 weeks
- **Indica/sativa crosses (8 weeks)**: Harvest every 8 weeks
- **Longer-flowering sativas (9-12 weeks)**: Harvest every 9-12 weeks

For example, with an eight-week flowering variety, a grower would move a new
batch of plants into the flowering room every two weeks to maintain an
eight-week stagger. This means having four groups of flowering plants at
different stages at any given time.

## Benefits of Perpetual Harvesting

- **Consistent supply** - Regular harvests provide a steady, predictable
  supply rather than large intermittent yields
- **Efficient space use** - Grow rooms are always productive; no downtime
  between crops
- **Risk management** - If one batch has problems, others are at different
  stages and not equally affected
- **Better for biological controls** - Gardens with perpetual harvests are
  ideal for [[hamilton-beneficial-insects-and-biological-pest-control]] since they provide continuous habitat
- **Continuous learning** - Frequent harvests provide more opportunities to
  refine technique

## Requirements for Success

### Separate Spaces

Each growth stage requires its own controlled environment. The most critical
separation is between vegetative and flowering spaces, as light leaks during
the dark period can disrupt flowering or cause accidental
[[cervantes-rejuvenation-re-vegging]]. A clone chamber can be a
simple enclosed space within the vegetative room.

### Consistent Mother Plants

Maintaining healthy [[cervantes-mother-plants]] is the
foundation of any perpetual system. Mothers must be kept in continuous
vegetative growth under 18/6 or 24/0 lighting. Plan for adequate container
size - see [[cervantes-container-gardening-pot-size]] for guidance on long-term mother plant care.

### Clone Management

A reliable cloning operation ensures a steady supply of new plants. Clones
typically need two to three weeks to root before moving to the vegetative
room. Build in extra capacity so that clone failures do not disrupt the
schedule.

### Strain Selection

Choose varieties with predictable, consistent flowering times for easier
scheduling. Mixing strains with very different flowering durations complicates
the perpetual cycle. Most growers select one or two primary varieties and
optimize their system around those genetics.

## Environmental Considerations

Each room requires independent climate control:
- **Clone room**: Higher humidity (70-100%), moderate light intensity,
  warmer temperatures
- **Vegetative room**: Moderate humidity (50-70%), strong light intensity
- **Flowering room**: Lower humidity (40-55%), maximum light intensity,
  strict dark period integrity

## Harvest Staggering

To achieve a staggered harvest, introduce new plants to the flowering room at
regular intervals. For an eight-week flowering strain:
- Week 1: Move batch A to flowering
- Week 2: Move batch B to flowering
- Week 3: Move batch C to flowering
- Week 4: Move batch D to flowering
- Week 5: Harvest batch A; move batch E to flowering
- And so on continuously

This approach also eases the workload at harvest time, as only a portion of
the total crop is harvested at any given time. Drying and [[cervantes-curing-storage]] space requirements are also spread out.

[[aeroponics-cannabis]] offers an alternative growing method that pairs well with perpetual harvest scheduling for growers seeking faster growth rates.

[[cannabis-indica]] and [[cannabis-sativa]] lineages differ significantly in flowering time, directly affecting harvest frequency in perpetual systems.

## Limitations

- Higher initial setup cost for multiple rooms/zones
- More complex management and scheduling
- Less flexibility to change varieties quickly
- Requires disciplined adherence to the schedule
- More total plants under cultivation at any time
