## When to Harvest

Harvesting is the act of reaping your rewards and is without a doubt
the most fun you will have with your garden. Follow the breeder's
guidelines on flowering times. At the end of the flowering time,
examine your bud and learn to identify maturity.

### Harvest Readiness Indicators

- 50% to 70% of the pistils change color
- Your plant stops producing crystals
- Your plant stops producing resin
- Fan leaves and lower leaves turn yellow and start dropping off
- The smell has reached a peak
- Bud mass has not increased in the past few days

Some of these indicators do not appear on every strain, but multiple
signs confirm readiness.

## Indica Harvest Procedure

1. Hack the 1-4 foot plant at the base.
2. Hang upside down in a cool room with no light and fresh air.
3. Remove as many fan leaves as possible with clippers.
4. Remove secondary leaves and put in a separate pile.
5. Gather trim (small resin-covered leaves from the bud).
6. You now have 4 grades: fan leaf (okay smoke), middle leaves
   (better), trim (very good), and bud (prime).

Leave the plant hanging until a branch can break easily between your
fingers. This takes about 2-3 weeks.

## Sativa Harvest Procedure

Much like Indica but potentially more labor-intensive. Outdoor Sativa
plants can grow up to 12 feet carrying over 20 oz of bud.

1. Chop the plant at the base and spread on a canvas.
2. Roll the canvas up and tie down tight for transport.
3. Hang upside down in a cool room with no light and fresh air.
4. Large plants may need branches cut and hung separately.
5. Remove leaves and trim as described in the Indica section.

### Light Warning

Light degrades THC quantities. Do not expose your harvest to any
direct light. The room does not need to be as light-proof as the grow
room, but avoid direct light entirely.

## Drying Fan Leaves, Leaves, and Trim

These are cured by leaving them dry on a flat surface, away from direct
light with plenty of fresh air. Leaves will dry after 3 weeks and are
smokable at that stage.

**Do NOT speed up drying with ovens, microwaves, or heat.** Let them
dry normally and you will cure a much better smoke.

## The Curing Process (Canning)

As soon as branches are brittle, begin canning:

1. Find a can or jar with a removable lid (more is better).
2. Using scissors, clip bud from the branch into the can.
3. Branches and stems go in the bin (they contain only small THC
   quantities).
4. Place the can in a dark room or cupboard.
5. Every day open the lid for several hours (6 hours is good).
6. Close the lid again.
7. Shake the bud around every couple of days.

### Curing Timeline

- **3-4 weeks canning:** Ready for initial sampling
- **8 weeks total (including hang-dry):** Peak quality -- the pinnacle
  point of cured bud
- **1 year:** Vintage but may lose some potency

Fresh bud (8 weeks canned curing) is the pinnacle. After that, some
THC cannabinoids change composition and become different cannabinoid
compounds. Fresh bud is far better than aged bud.

If you dried plants for 3 weeks hanging upside down, subtract that
time from the canning time. Although you can have good bud 2 weeks
after harvest, it is better to wait 4 weeks or more.

## Making Hash from Trim

Trim (the small resin-covered leaves removed during harvest) can be
used to make hash. The trim is very good quality because of the resin
content. Hash-making involves gathering the stalked capitate trichomes
through screening processes (discussed in Chapter 17 of the source).

## Harvest Quality Grades

| Grade | Material | Quality |
|-------|----------|---------|
| Prime | Bud (colas) | Best quality, highest high |
| High | Trim (sugar leaves) | Very good, resin-rich |
| Medium | Secondary leaves | Decent smoke |
| Low | Fan leaves | Okay smoke, lowest potency |
| Processing | Stems/branches | Minimal THC, mostly discarded |

## Indica vs. Sativa Harvest Differences

| Factor | Indica | Sativa |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Plant size | 1-4 feet | Up to 12 feet (outdoor) |
| Harvest difficulty | Simple | Can be labor-intensive |
| Transport | Easy | Needs canvas/wrapping |
| Branch handling | Hang whole plant | May need to separate branches |
| Bud weight | Lighter per plant | Can carry 20+ oz |

## Common Harvesting Mistakes

1. Harvesting too early -- reduces potency and yield
2. Exposing harvested bud to direct light -- degrades THC
3. Speeding up drying with heat -- ruins taste and smoothness
4. Skipping the curing process -- harsh smoke, reduced quality
5. Not opening curing jars daily -- mold risk, incomplete cure
6. Throwing away trim -- valuable for hash production

## Post-Harvest Checklist

- [ ] Bud hung upside down in cool, dark, ventilated area
- [ ] Fan leaves removed with clippers
- [ ] Trim separated for hash-making or smoking
- [ ] Branches tested for brittleness (snap test)
- [ ] Bud clipped into curing jars
- [ ] Jars opened daily for 6 hours
- [ ] Bud shaken in jars every couple days
