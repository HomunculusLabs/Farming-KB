# Mulching Techniques

Mulching is the practice of covering the soil surface with a layer of organic or inorganic material. It is one of the most useful techniques in the organic garden, serving to suppress weeds, retain moisture, protect soil structure, moderate soil temperature, and encourage beneficial soil organisms.

## Benefits of Mulching

- **Weed suppression**: A thick mulch blocks light, preventing weed seeds from germinating and smothering existing small weeds
- **Moisture retention**: Mulch reduces evaporation from the soil surface, reducing the need for watering — important for [[teaming-with-microbes-compost-mulch-tea]] with the added advantage of higher nutrient content. However, it is essential that the manure is fully rotted — fresh manure will scorch young shoots and may introduce weed seeds.

### Grass Cuttings

Grass cuttings can be used as mulch but require care. A depth of at least 6in (15cm) is needed to be effective, but such a thick layer can create anaerobic conditions as the grass rots, producing an unpleasant smell and potentially harming plant roots. It is generally better to compost grass cuttings first and use the resulting compost as mulch.

### Other Organic Mulches

- **Straw**: Excellent for strawberry beds and around vegetables; suppresses weeds and keeps fruit clean
- **Leaf mold**: Decomposed leaves make an excellent, nutrient-rich mulch
- **Seaweed**: Rich in trace elements; rinse to remove salt before use
- **Shredded prunings**: A good way to recycle garden waste

## How to Apply Mulch

1. **Prepare the ground**: Remove all perennial weeds before mulching. Annual weeds can be covered if the mulch is thick enough.
2. **Water the soil**: Apply mulch to moist soil. Mulching dry soil can actually prevent water from reaching plant roots.
3. **Apply thickly**: A minimum depth of 2-3in (5-7cm) is needed to be effective. For weed suppression, 3-4in (7-10cm) is better.
4. **Keep away from stems**: Leave a small gap around plant stems to prevent rotting.
5. **Top up as needed**: Organic mulches break down over time and will need replenishing annually.
