## Grifola frondosa (Maitake / Hen of the Woods) — Research Summary

Maitake (Grifola frondosa) is a large polypore mushroom native to temperate hardwood forests of North America, Europe, and Japan. Known as "hen of the woods" for its appearance, and as "dancing mushroom" (mai-take) in Japanese folklore because people reportedly danced with joy upon finding it.

### Active Compounds

**Beta-glucans** (primary immunologically active components):
- **D-fraction** (MD-fraction): a standardized beta-1,6/1,3-glucan extract developed by Dr. Hiroaki Nanba, the most researched maitake preparation
- **Grifolan**: a high-molecular-weight beta-1,3-glucan with beta-1,6 branching, isolated from maitake mycelium
- **X-fraction**: an alpha-glucan with insulin-sensitizing properties

**Other**: various polysaccharides, proteins, lectins, sterols, fatty acids

### Mechanism of Action

**Immune activation** (primary mechanism):
- Activates macrophages, NK cells, and cytotoxic T cells through Dectin-1 and complement receptor 3 (CR3) recognition of beta-glucans
- Enhances cytokine production: TNF-alpha, IL-1, IL-6, IL-12, interferon-gamma
- Promotes dendritic cell maturation and antigen presentation

**Anti-tumor activity**:
- Immune-mediated tumor cell killing is the primary mechanism
- In animal models, D-fraction showed tumor growth inhibition and metastasis reduction
- Synergistic effects with conventional chemotherapy: D-fraction enhanced the anti-tumor effect of mitomycin-C while reducing side effects
- Apoptosis induction in cancer cells demonstrated in vitro

**Metabolic effects**:
- X-fraction and other maitake components improve insulin sensitivity and glucose metabolism
- Reduces blood glucose and triglycerides in animal studies
- Alpha-glucan fraction activates insulin receptors and AMPK pathway

**Anti-inflammatory**:
- Reduces inflammatory cytokines (TNF-alpha, IL-6)
- Modulates NF-kappaB signaling

### Clinical Evidence

**Cancer**:
- Animal studies consistently show tumor inhibition, particularly for breast, liver, lung, and prostate cancers
- Human clinical studies (primarily Japanese) show immune parameter improvement in cancer patients
- D-fraction used as an adjunct to chemotherapy, reporting improved immune function and quality of life
- One notable study showed 50-70% tumor regression or significant improvement in patients receiving D-fraction alongside conventional treatment

**Immune function**:
- Increased NK cell activity documented in multiple human trials
- Enhanced overall immune competence in both healthy and immunocompromised subjects

**Metabolic syndrome**:
- Improved insulin sensitivity and blood glucose control in human studies
- Reduction in blood pressure and triglyceride levels reported

### Dosage

- D-fraction: 0.5-1 mg/kg/day in studies, roughly 35-70 mg/day for adults
- Dried mushroom: 3-5 g/day
- Mushroom powder extract: 500 mg - 2 g/day (standardized extracts)
- Often combined with whole mushroom powder for synergistic effect

### Traditional Use

Used in Japanese and Chinese traditional medicine for: immune support, digestive health, fatigue reduction, and as a general tonic. In traditional Kampo medicine, prescribed for "spleen and stomach" disorders. Valued as both food and medicine — one of the most commercially cultivated medicinal mushrooms in Japan.
