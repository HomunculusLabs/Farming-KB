# KNF Fish Amino Acid (FAA)

Fish Amino Acid is a liquid fertilizer made from fish waste and brown sugar through osmotic pressure fermentation. It is rich in nitrogen and various amino acids, providing great value to both plants and microorganisms.

## Best Fish to Use

Blue-back fishes are ideal because they contain good proteins, amino acids, fatty acids, DHA, EPA, and various vitamins:
- Mackerel, Spanish mackerel, horse mackerel
- Saury, garfish, herring, yellow tail
- Sardine, salmon, sweet fish
- Bluefin tuna, skipjack tuna, anchovy

Use fresh waste only. Do NOT use salted fishes. Fish heads, bones, and intestines are all used.

## Ingredients

- Fish waste: 1 part by weight
- Brown sugar/jaggery: 1 part by weight (1:1 ratio)
- Optional additions: IMO-4 (small amount to decompose fish oil and meat), OHN, Mineral A, rice straw

## How to Make FAA

1. Put fish waste and brown sugar in clay jar, wooden box, or PE container at 1:1 weight ratio
2. Fill jar to 2/3 of volume
3. Pile fish waste and sugar in alternating layers; cover top with extra sugar
4. Add small amount of IMO-4 with sugar near the top
5. Scatter IMO-4 on top, cover jar with rice straws
6. Cover opening with mosquito net, lay sticks on rim, cover with lid (leaving air space)
7. In 3-4 days, osmotic pressure from sugar begins liquidizing the fish waste
8. Fermentation takes at least 6 months for well-fermented FAA
9. Matured FAA has a smell; extract and use only the liquid

## Hints for Installation

- Put stones 10 cm high on bottom of container (for drainage)
- Cover with mosquito net (not sealed); air must flow through
- This arrangement filters FAA naturally

## Dilution and Use

- Basic dilution ratio: 1:1,000
- Rich in nitrogen -- excellent for vegetative growth period
- Good for both soil and foliar application
- For leafy vegetables: can use continuously to increase yield, taste, and fragrance
- Can be added when making IMO or mixed compost to activate microorganisms

## When NOT to Use

- NOT recommended during reproductive growth period -- may induce overgrowth
- Some FAA types have distinctive odors
- Fish oil from fermentation can be used as insect repellent (army worm moths become helpless with slightest contact)

## Pest Control Bonus

- Mackerel FAA is very effective against mites and greenhouse whitefly
- Dilute and spray on both sides of leaves

## Recycling Bones

Put leftover bones from FAA production into brown rice vinegar (BRV) at 10:1 ratio (BRV to bones). Bones will decompose and produce good quality [[knf-water-soluble-phosphoric-acid|water-soluble calcium phosphate]].

## Storage

- Optimum temperature: 23-25C
- Cool place, no direct sunlight
