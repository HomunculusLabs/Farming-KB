## The Food Web in Detail

Plants growing on the earth are fed upon by bird and beast. Some become prey, others
succumb to disease or age. Their wastes and remains are broken down by microorganisms
which proliferate and die, returning nutrients taken up once more by plants.

Among the microorganisms are bacteria, fungi, [[savory-living-organisms-as-tools]] on earth. If self-governing mechanisms regulating one
type of bacterium were to cease for just several days, the earth would become a morass
of bacterial remains.

Yet this never happens. The balance between multiplication and destruction, the
equilibrium between production and consumption, carries out both rapidly and harmoniously
for untold millions of years. No species of bird should become extinct. Earthworms must
not vanish. Nor should mice and spiders proliferate too greatly. Tens of millions of
species live in perfect harmony without increase or decrease; they are born and die
unseen by man.
