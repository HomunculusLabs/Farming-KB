## School Survey Data (ESPAD 2003)

The [[health-risks-of-hallucinogenic-mushrooms]] equaled or exceeded lifetime
use of ecstasy in nine of the surveyed countries. This finding was significant
because it positioned mushrooms as a competitor to ecstasy in the youth drug
market, despite their different pharmacological profiles and effects.

Analysis across 13 countries demonstrated that students were much more likely to
have used hallucinogenic mushrooms if they had also used LSD, ecstasy,
amphetamines, or cocaine, compared to those who had only used cannabis or legal
substances. Male students generally had higher prevalence rates than females,
consistent with broader gender patterns in illegal drug use.

## Clubbing and Dance Music Settings

Targeted surveys in dance music settings revealed dramatically higher prevalence
than general population surveys. Lifetime prevalence estimates ranged from 9% of
clubbers in Budapest to 55% in France. These figures underscore the importance
of the dance music and clubbing scene as a context for hallucinogenic mushroom
use.

A 2000 survey of 760 clubbers from six south-east England nightclubs found 24%
lifetime prevalence overall. However, among current drug users in the sample
(those who had used any drug in the past month), prevalence rose to 44%. This
compared to only 11% among 16-19 year olds in the general population.

Within club populations, polydrug use tended to cluster in the same individuals.
The common factor across all non-probability clubbing surveys was that
substantially higher drug prevalence estimates appeared compared to general or
school population surveys, suggesting that the dance music scene functions as a
key environment for both the normalization and diffusion of hallucinogenic
mushroom use.
