# Cottage Garden Design Principles

For the average gardener with a moderately sized garden, the
"cottage-garden" design offers distinct advantages over other styles.
Geoff Hamilton advocated this approach as an ideal way to combine
productive food growing with ornamental beauty in an organic system.

## Origins and Philosophy

The cottage-garden style was developed by old English cottagers as a
way of combining a productive garden with ornamental plants. It was
later developed and romanticized by the Victorians in the 19th century.
The style is endlessly adaptable and suits modern architecture and
[[natural-building-materials-guide]] just as well as it did old stone cottages with
roses around the door and windows.

## Advantages of the Cottage Garden

The first advantage is that it allows you to grow a mixture of
ornamental and vegetable plants in the same beds, making maximum use
of any available space. In a very small garden, combine vegetables and
flowers in mixed borders. You can also use the

## Color and Design Principles

In cottage garden design, large areas of concentrated color are created
by dividing plants into bold clumps. Dramatic combinations of purple,
pink, and red work well together. The key is to plant in drifts rather
than single specimens, creating a natural, flowing effect. Self-seeding
plants are welcome in the cottage garden, filling gaps and creating
an informal, relaxed atmosphere that looks better with each passing
year.
