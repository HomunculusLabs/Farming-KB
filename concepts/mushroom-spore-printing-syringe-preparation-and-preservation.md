# Mushroom Spore Printing, Syringe Preparation, and Preservation

## Overview

Spore collection, syringe preparation, and preservation are foundational
skills in [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]]. The PF TEK methodology provides detailed
protocols for each stage, emphasizing [[cultivator-sterile-technique-and-agar-culture]] to prevent
contamination. Proper execution determines the success of subsequent
cultivation cycles and long-term viability of genetic material.

## Mature vs. Immature Specimens

Understanding specimen maturity is critical. Mature mushrooms are required
for [[spore-production-geotropic-sensitivity-coupling-basidiomycete-badham-1982]], though they tend to have weaker potency compared to
specimens harvested earlier. As a mushroom matures, its cap upturns and
expands, revealing the gill structure. Dark-colored gills visible through
the upturned cap signal sporulation is underway. Purple spore deposits on
surfaces below provide definitive evidence of active spore release.
Cultivators must balance maximum spore production (full maturity) against
optimal potency (peaking earlier in the fruiting cycle).

## Spore Printing Equipment

The spore printing process requires specific equipment:
- **KERR wide mouth canning jar** - sterile printing chamber
- **Cuticle scissors** - for harvesting mushroom caps
- **Alcohol lamp or lighter** - for [[flammability-hazard-alcohol-lysol-flame-sterilization-mycology]] of tools
- **Isopropyl rubbing alcohol** (70%) - for [[challenge-microorganisms-microwave-surface-sterilization]]

The KERR jar is preferred for its wide mouth accommodating various cap sizes
while providing an enclosed, controllable environment for spore collection.

## Sterile Technique

Maintaining sterility is the single most important factor for success:
- **Hand washing** - thorough washing with antibacterial soap
- **HEPA air cleaner** - operating in a room with HEPA filtration
- **Still air environment** - avoiding drafts during critical procedures
- **Surface sterilization** - wiping surfaces with rubbing alcohol
- **Flame sterilization** - passing metal tools through alcohol flame

## Cap Harvesting Procedure

1. Flame-sterilize cuticle scissors until metal glows red-orange
2. Allow scissors to cool briefly (avoid heat-damaging spores)
3. Select a mature specimen with upturned cap and visible dark gills
4. Grasp the mushroom by the base of the stem (pin handling technique)
5. Cut the stem cleanly, separating cap from stem
6. Place cap gill-side-down inside the KERR jar
7. Set the lid loosely on top (not sealed tightly)
8. Place jar in a draft-free, dark location for approximately 2 days

The dark environment encourages spore release while preventing light
exposure that could degrade [[pf-tek-spore-viability-degradation]] during printing.

## Dehumidification Before Sealing

After the 2-day printing period, the cap is removed and discarded. The jar
is left open briefly in still air to allow excess moisture to evaporate
before sealing. This prevents condensation inside the sealed jar, which
could promote bacterial growth or premature [[growing-gourmet-spore-germination-strain-isolation]].

## Spore Viability

Spore viability is time-sensitive and follows a predictable decline:
- **Fresh spores** - germinate massively and quickly, producing vigorous
  [[mycelial-colonization]]
- **Several months** - viability begins gradually decreasing
- **1.5 years** - viability greatly reduced, lower germination rates

For best results, spores should be used as fresh as possible. The dramatic
difference between fresh and aged spore performance underscores the
importance of proper preservation and timely use.
