# Solar Oven Cooking

Solar ovens use concentrated sunlight to cook food without fuel, electricity,
or flame. They are a practical off-grid cooking solution, especially in sunny
climates, and can reach temperatures of 250-400F (121-204C) depending on
design. This guide covers the three main types, construction, cooking
techniques, and recipes.

## How Solar Cooking Works

Solar ovens concentrate sunlight into an insulated cooking chamber using
reflective surfaces. The greenhouse effect traps heat inside the chamber,
while insulation minimizes heat loss. Key principles:

- **Reflectors** — direct additional sunlight into the cooking chamber
- **Absorber plate** — dark surface (black) absorbs maximum solar radiation
- **Insulation** — minimizes conductive and convective heat loss
- **Glazing** — transparent cover (glass or plastic) allows light in but
  prevents convective heat loss

## Types of Solar Ovens

### Box Oven

A insulated box with a dark interior, a transparent lid, and reflective
panels that fold out to direct light into the box.

**Characteristics:**
- Temperature range: 200-300F (93-149C)
- Cooking style: slow, similar to a crock pot
- Advantages: stable temperature, easy [[query-how-to-use-cover-crops-for-heavy-clay-soil-improvement]], good for baking
- Disadvantages: heavier, slower to heat up
- [[query-what-are-the-different-phytoremediation-mechanisms-and-which-plants-are-best-for-each]]: breads, casseroles, roasts, grains, soups

### Panel Cooker

Flat reflective panels that surround a dark cooking pot, focusing sunlight
onto the pot from multiple angles. The pot sits inside a heat-resistant bag
to retain heat.

**Characteristics:**
- Temperature range: 150-250F (66-121C)
- Cooking style: moderate speed, portable
- Advantages: lightweight, foldable, inexpensive, portable
- Disadvantages: less stable in wind, lower maximum temperature
- Best for: rice, vegetables, stews, reheating, water pasteurization

### Parabolic Cooker

A parabolic reflector dish that concentrates sunlight to a single focal
point where the cooking pot is positioned.

**Characteristics:**
- Temperature range: 300-400F (149-204C)
- Cooking style: fast, similar to a stovetop; requires frequent repositioning
- Advantages: highest temperature, fastest cooking, can fry and grill
- Disadvantages: requires constant adjustment to track the sun, can cause
  burns if misused, expensive
- Best for: frying, boiling water quickly, searing, stir-frying

## Building a Box Solar Oven (DIY)

### Materials
- Cardboard box (inner) — medium size, roughly 18x18x12 inches
- Cardboard box (outer) — 2-3 inches larger on all sides
- Aluminum foil — [[oner-peptide-limestone-city-seeds-heavy-duty-fruity-sweet-tooth-durban-poison]], for reflective panels
- Black spray paint — high-heat rated, for interior
- Glazing — tempered glass or oven-safe polycarbonate
- Insulation — crumpled newspaper, foam board, or wool
- Duct tape, scissors, ruler

### Construction Steps
1. Cut the inner box to fit inside the outer box with a 2-inch gap on all
   sides for insulation
2. Paint the interior [[mckenna-food-of-the-gods-exopheromones-and-plant-communication]] inner box black
3. Fill the gap between boxes with insulation (crumpled newspaper works well)
4. Cut and attach reflective flap panels from cardboard and aluminum foil
   to the outer box lid — these hinge open at a 45-degree angle
5. Attach the glazing (glass) over the top of the inner box with tape or
   a simple frame
6. Add a prop stick to hold the reflector panels at the correct angle

### Cost
A basic cardboard box oven costs $5-15 in materials and can be built in 1-2
hours. More durable versions using plywood or sheet metal cost $50-150.

normal cooking time.

### General Guidelines
- Preheat the oven for 20-30 minutes before adding food
- Position the oven facing due south ([[query-what-are-the-benefits-of-straw-mulch-in-the-garden-and-how-do-i-use-it]] Northern Hemisphere)
- Adjust the reflector angle every 30-45 minutes to track the sun
- Use dark, thin-walled pots with tight-fitting lids for fastest results
- Cast iron works well due to its heat retention
- Start cooking early — the sun is strongest between 10AM and 2PM

## Recipes for Solar Cooking

### Solar Bread
- 3 cups flour, 1 packet yeast, 1.5 cups warm water, 1 tbsp salt, 1 tbsp oil
- Mix, knead, let rise 1 hour in the sun
- Place [[query-how-can-ducks-help-with-pest-control-and-fertility-in-a-garden]] greased dark bread pan, cover with lid
- Bake in solar oven at 250-300F for 1-1.5 hours
- Internal temp should reach 190F

### Solar Chicken and Vegetables
- Season chicken pieces with salt, pepper, herbs
- Arrange in a dark pot with chopped potatoes, carrots, onions
- Add 1/2 cup water or broth
- Cover tightly and place in solar oven
- Cook 2-3 hours until chicken reaches 165F internal temperature

### Solar Rice Pilaf
- 1 cup rice, 2 cups water, 1 tbsp butter, salt, raisins, cinnamon
- Combine all in dark pot, cover tightly
- Place in solar oven for 45-60 minutes
- Let stand 10 minutes before opening
