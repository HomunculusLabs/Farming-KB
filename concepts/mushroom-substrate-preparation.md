# Mushroom Substrate Preparation

## Overview

Substrate is the nutritive medium on which mushroom mycelium grows and from
which fruiting bodies are produced. The PF TEK substrate — a mixture of
brown rice flour (BRF), vermiculite, and water — is the most widely used
beginner substrate for [[substrate-preparation]]:

- Distilled or spring water is preferred
- Tap water may contain chlorine that can inhibit mycelial growth
- If using tap water, let it sit uncovered for 24 hours to off-gas chlorine
- The water-to-dry-ingredient ratio is critical: too wet promotes
  bacterial contamination; too dry slows colonization

## Mixing Procedure

The standard PF TEK mixing ratio per half-pint jar:

1. Measure **1/2 cup fine vermiculite** into a mixing bowl
2. Add **1/4 cup water** and mix thoroughly — the vermiculite should be
   damp but no water should pool at the bottom of the bowl
3. Add **1/4 cup brown rice flour** and mix until evenly distributed
4. The final mixture should be fluffy, moist, and hold together when
   squeezed without dripping water

**Critical moisture test**: Squeeze a handful of substrate. It should
form a ball that holds its shape. A few drops of water may appear, but
the substrate should not drip or feel soggy.

## Loading Jars

Proper jar loading ensures successful colonization and easy cake removal:

1. Fill each jar with substrate to within **1/2 inch of the rim**
2. Gently tap the jar on the counter to settle the substrate and eliminate
   large air pockets
3. Wipe the inside rim of the jar clean with a paper towel — any
   substrate on the rim can provide a contamination pathway
4. Fill the remaining 1/2 inch at the top with **dry vermiculite** — this
   "barrier layer" is a critical innovation of the PF TEK that filters
   airborne contaminants during inoculation
5. Cover with the prepared lid (4 nail holes, tape over holes)

## Why the Dry Vermiculite Barrier Matters

The dry vermiculite barrier layer at the top of the jar serves multiple
functions:

- **Contaminant filter**: Airborne mold spores and bacteria that land on
  the dry surface cannot germinate without moisture
- **Moisture shield**: Prevents wet substrate from wicking up to the lid
- **Gas exchange**: Loose dry vermiculite allows CO2 to escape and fresh
  air to reach colonizing mycelium
- **Easy removal**: After colonization, the dry layer is simply scraped
  off before fruiting

## Sterilization Methods

### Steam Sterilization (Basic PF TEK)

- Place jars in a large pot with a tight-fitting lid
- Put a folded towel at the bottom to prevent jars from cracking
- Fill with water to halfway up the jars
- Bring to a gentle boil and maintain for **60–90 minutes**
- Keep the lid on throughout; do not remove to check on jars
- After sterilization, turn off heat and let jars cool in the pot with
  the lid on — rapid cooling can create vacuum pulling contaminated air
  through inoculation holes

### Pressure Sterilization (Recommended for Reliability)

- Pressure canner at **15 PSI** for **30–45 minutes**
- More reliable at killing resistant bacterial endospores
- Not strictly necessary for PF TEK but significantly reduces
  contamination rates

## Common Substrate Problems

### Too Wet

- Water pooling at the bottom of jars after sterilization
- Colonization stalls or is very slow
- Bacterial contamination (sour smell, slimy texture) is common
- Solution: Use less water; squeeze excess moisture from vermiculite
  before adding flour

### Too Dry

- Substrate crumbles and won't hold together when squeezed
- Mycelium grows very slowly or fails to colonize completely
- Solution: Add more water incrementally during mixing

### Compacted

- Substrate is dense with no air pockets
- Colonization is very slow or stalls
- Solution: Mix more gently; don't pack substrate into jars
