## The "Paddo" Delivery Culture
In the Netherlands, hallucinogenic mushrooms were known as "paddos," and a
distinctive delivery culture emerged. "Paddo cabs" made home deliveries of
hallucinogenic mushrooms, paralleling the cannabis delivery services already
operating in Dutch cities. This normalized the substance as a consumer product
available on demand. A 2001 study among representative Dutch young people aged
12 and above found that 64 percent of those aged 18 and older who had used
magic mushrooms in the past year purchased them in smartshops.

## The UK Market Before the Ban
Prior to July 2005, an estimated 300 shops and market stalls across the UK
sold hallucinogenic mushrooms. The UK Treasury collected sales tax estimated at
up to 175,000 GBP annually on a turnover of approximately 1 million GBP. The
2004 Mixmag survey found that 54.6 percent of users purchased their mushrooms
from clubs, shops, or stalls, 16 percent obtained them from friends, and 4.2
percent bought them online. Camden Market in London was a notable retail
location for fresh mushroom sales.
