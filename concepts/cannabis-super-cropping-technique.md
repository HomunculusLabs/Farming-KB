Super cropping is a high-stress training (HST) technique that involves intentionally damaging the stem to create a knuckle joint. This strengthens the branch, controls height, and can increase overall yield by creating a more even canopy and promoting lateral growth.

## What Is Super Cropping

Super cropping is the practice of gently crushing and bending the stem of a cannabis branch until the inner tissue softens and the branch bends over at a 90-degree angle. The plant heals the damaged tissue by forming a thickened "knuckle" at the bend point, which is significantly stronger than the original stem.

## Benefits

1. **Stronger branches**: The knuckle joint at the bend point becomes much stronger than the original stem, better supporting heavy buds
2. **Height control**: Bending tall branches down reduces overall plant height without removing any plant material
3. **Better light distribution**: Horizontal branches expose more bud sites to light
4. **Increased yield**: More bud sites receive adequate light, producing more even and abundant bud development
5. **No plant material removed**: Unlike topping, all plant matter stays on the plant

## When to Super Crop

- **Timing**: During the [[comparison-cannabis-vegetative-stage-vs-flowering-stage]], typically 3–6 weeks into growth
- **Plant readiness**: The plant should be healthy and actively growing with stems that are flexible but established
- **Branch thickness**: Select branches that are thick enough to handle the technique — pencil thickness or greater
- **Do NOT super crop**: Seedlings, sick or stressed plants, or plants in flowering (though mild bending is possible in early flower)

## How to Super Crop Step by Step

### Preparation

1. Ensure the plant is well-hydrated (water 1–2 hours before)
2. Do this during the first few hours of the lights-on period when stems are most flexible
3. Wash your hands before handling

### The Technique

1. **Select the branch**: Choose a branch you want to bend down
2. **Find the bend point**: Pick a spot on the stem where you want it to bend — usually in the middle section
3. **Squeeze and twist**: Firmly pinch the stem between your thumb and index finger. While squeezing, gently roll/twist the stem back and forth between your fingers.
4. **Feel for softening**: You will feel the inner tissue soften and become pliable. The outer skin may wrinkle slightly.
5. **Bend slowly**: Once the stem is soft, slowly bend it downward and over to your desired angle (typically 90 degrees)
6. **Support if needed**: If the branch does not stay in position on its own, use soft plant ties, string, or pipe cleaners to hold it in place
7. **Healing**: The plant will form a knuckle at the bend point within 3–7 days. The branch will stiffen and strengthen.

### What You Should Feel and See

- **Correct**: The stem softens under your fingers and bends without breaking the outer skin. A slight pop or crack of the inner tissue is normal.
- **Too rough**: The stem snaps completely, breaking the outer skin and exposing the inside. The branch may die above the break point.
- **Not enough**: The stem does not soften and does not bend — you need to squeeze/twist more firmly

## Fixing a Snapped Branch

If you accidentally snap a branch completely:
1. Do not remove it — plants can often heal broken branches
2. Realign the broken ends carefully
3. Wrap the break point tightly with electrical tape, duct tape, or plant tape
4. Support the branch with a stake or tie
5. The branch may heal in 1–3 weeks. It will form a thick knuckle at the repair point.
6. If the broken section turns brown and dies, prune it off above the break point.

## Common Mistakes

- **Snapping the stem**: Too much force breaks the outer tissue. Squeeze and twist gradually.
- **Doing it too early**: Thin, young stems cannot handle super cropping and will snap
- **Super cropping sick plants**: Stressed plants should recover before any HST
- **Bending too close to the tip**: Bend in the middle of the branch for the best effect
- **Overdoing it**: Do not super crop every branch on the plant. Select branches that need height control or strengthening.

## Super Cropping in Flowering

Green advises against super cropping during flowering, but notes:
- Light bending is acceptable in the first 1–2 weeks of flowering
- The plant is less flexible during flowering and more prone to snapping
- Any damage during flowering can slow bud development and reduce yield
- Focus on proper training during vegetative stage to minimize need for adjustment during flowering
