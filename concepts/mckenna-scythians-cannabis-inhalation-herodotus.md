## Cannabis in the Classical World

Dioscorides, writing during the first century, gave an excellent
description of cannabis and mentions its use in rope making and
medicine, but says nothing of its intoxicating properties. Pliny the
Elder reproduces a fragment from Democratus concerning a plant called
thalassaegle or potamaugis that many scholars consider a reference to
cannabis: "Taken in drink it produces a delirium, which presents to the
fancy visions of a most extraordinary nature."

Because the climate favored the growth of hemp and Islam encouraged its
use over alcohol, in the Near Eastern and Arab worlds cannabis became
the intoxicant of choice. This predilection was already very old at the
time of the Prophet, which explains why alcohol is explicitly forbidden
to the faithful but hashish is a matter of theological disputation.

## Significance in McKenna's Argument

For McKenna, the Scythians illustrate a broader pattern: that cannabis
has been a continuous part of the human family longer than almost any
other plant, spread throughout the world by human agency. The Scythian
cannabis practices represent one of the earliest documented links in
this chain of transmission. Their methods show that the psychoactive
properties of cannabis were recognized and valued in Central Asia long
before the plant reached the Mediterranean world.

The Scythian failure to invent the pipe, despite discovering inhalation,
also illustrates McKenna's theme that drug delivery technologies evolve
slowly and unevenly across cultures, with major innovations often
arising in unexpected places and spreading along unpredictable pathways.
