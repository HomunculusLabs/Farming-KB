# Fertilizer Pollution and Environmental Impact

The environmental consequences of excessive fertilizer use represent one of the most serious and widespread pollution problems in agriculture and gardening.

## The Gulf of Mexico Dead Zone

### Mechanism

1. Runoff from 31 U.S. states and 2 Canadian provinces enters the Mississippi River and flows to the Gulf of Mexico
2. Excessive nitrogen and phosphorus from synthetic fertilizers leach into the river system
3. In spring, warm freshwater runoff creates a barrier layer atop the saltier Gulf water
4. This barrier cuts off the lower saltwater from atmospheric oxygen
5. Nitrogen and phosphorus pollution cause massive algal blooms in the freshwater layer
6. When algae die, they sink and decompose, consuming the limited oxygen in the lower layer
7. The resulting hypoxia (oxygen depletion) causes fish and other organisms to flee or die in massive numbers
8. Cooler winter temperatures disrupt the barrier layer, but the cycle repeats each spring

### Scale

The dead zone forms each summer along the coasts of Mississippi, Louisiana, and eastern Texas — a vast area rendered uninhabitable by nutrient pollution. At its peak, the Gulf dead zone can cover over 8,000 square miles, roughly the size of New Jersey.
