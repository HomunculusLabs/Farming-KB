# Plant Nursery Establishment in Permaculture Systems

The plant nursery is the engine of permaculture establishment. In
*Permaculture: A Designers' Manual*, Bill Mollison repeatedly
emphasises that a small plant nursery is necessary to supply the
4,000-8,000 plants that can be placed on a hectare in a designed
system. Without nursery capacity, the designer cannot implement the
complex species assemblies that characterise mature permaculture.

## Why a Nursery Is Essential

When we construct a system of mixed tree, shrub, and vegetable crop
utilising livestock as foragers, so many species and individuals of
each species are needed that it is usually necessary to first create
a small plant nursery to supply them. While these are growing in
their pots, we can fence and prepare the soil, and then plant them
out to a carefully-designed long-term plan.

Every plantation needs a mulched, shaded nursery, no matter how
modest. Shade is most cheaply provided by light-foliaged legumes at
wide spacing (such as Acacia or Albizia). The nursery should be
established early in the planning stages as one of the critical
first priorities, along with access ways, shelter, and water supply.

## Nursery Design Considerations

### Shade and Protection
Shade is essential for tender seedlings and cuttings. In tropical
and subtropical areas, 30-50% shade cloth or living shade from
light-foliaged legumes is appropriate. In cooler climates, a simple
glasshouse or cold frame provides both warmth and protection from
wind. The [[mollison-greenhouse-design-solar-inventions]] and
mollison greenhouse cool climates mollison provide further
design guidance.

### Propagation Media
Seedlings and cuttings need well-drained, sterile propagation media.
Standard mixes include combinations of sand, peat, perlite, or
compost. For [[mollison-designers-seed-pelleting-and-pretreatment]],
potting soils should incorporate soil from nodulating legume trees
to provide inoculant.

### Irrigation
A reliable water supply is critical. Hand watering, mist systems,
or gravity-fed drip irrigation all serve. Wastewater from the house
can be directed to nursery beds, connecting to [[mollison-greywater-domestic-water-recycling]].

### Hardening Off
Seedlings raised in protected nursery conditions need gradual
exposure to full sun, wind, and temperature fluctuations before
planting out. This process of hardening off over 1-2 weeks reduces
transplant shock significantly.

## Sourcing Plant Material

The best source of seed and plants is always neighbours, public
nurseries, or forestry departments. From the early planning stages,
it pays to collect seed, pots, and hardy cuttings for the site,
just as it pays to forage for second-hand bricks, wood, and roofing.
This approach builds [[mollison-seed-resources-and-sovereignty]]
and reduces establishment costs.

Many useful plants can be propagated from cuttings, divisions,
or seed collected locally. Willow water from [[mollison-designers-willow-water-and-natural-rooting-propagation]] can assist with
rooting. Advanced, vigorous plants from nurseries or other
properties give the best results for tree establishment.

## The Nursery as Enterprise

Mollison identifies the nursery as a key rural enterprise that
supports broader permaculture development. A bioregion that is
integrated and cooperative can support many different nursery
enterprises without competition:

- Bamboo nursery together with canegrasses and large clump
  grasses
- Aquatic and edge plant nursery including fish forages,
  insectary species, and marshland perennials
- Berry fruit and vine nursery for the many species suited to
  local climates
- Bee fodder plants, butterfly and insectivorous bird
  attractors, hedgerow and forage species
- Hedgerow tree species specific to the district, pioneer tall
  grasses, and vines for coastal "fedges"
- Conservation and reforestation trees for extending natural
  forests
- Speciality nurseries for herbs, salt-affected areas, smog
  tolerance, medicinals, food dyes, and stabilisers

None of these enterprises are competitive. Troubles occur only
where large investors create large areas of identical crops, usually
from out-of-region investment.

## Integration with System Establishment

The nursery is the starting point for [[mollison-designers-pioneer-species-for-exhausted-tropical-soils]] and the broader process of
system evolution. Mollison's establishment sequence is clear:

1. Design the site thoroughly on paper
2. Set priorities based on economic reality
3. Locate and trade for components locally or cheaply
4. Establish the nursery and begin raising plants
5. Develop a nucleus completely before expanding
6. Expand on information and area using species proved suited
   to site

The nursery connects to all subsequent planting: [[mollison-designers-coppicing-management-of-legume-trees]], [[mollison-designers-legume-tree-inoculation-and-nitrogen-distribution]],
[[mollison-designers-shelterbelt-economics-for-livestock-and-croplands]], and the full range of tree crop establishment.

## Transplanting and Establishment

Mollison notes that transplant shock from nursery stock can be
reduced when soil around the root ball is carefully preserved.
Radish or turnip planted with tree seedlings controls grasses until
the small tree provides its own grass control by shading. Mulching,
watering, and protection from livestock all improve establishment
success rates.

## Starting Small

The emphasis throughout Mollison's treatment is on starting small
and developing thoroughly. It is far better to occupy a quarter
hectare thoroughly than to scatter trees over 2 hectares, as
production is higher and maintenance less, moisture is conserved,
and frost excluded. The nursery makes this concentrated approach
possible by providing the large number of plants needed for dense
planting within a manageable area.
