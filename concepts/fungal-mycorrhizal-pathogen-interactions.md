## Implications for Ecosystem Dynamics

Mycorrhizal-pathogen interactions have cascading effects on plant community composition and ecosystem dynamics. By differentially affecting plant species depending on their mycorrhizal dependency and susceptibility to pathogens, these interactions can shift competitive hierarchies among plant species.

The Janzen-Connell mechanism promotes tree species diversity in tropical forests by preventing any single species from dominating. In successional contexts, mycorrhizal networks can facilitate seedling establishment in forest gaps while root pathogens prevent competitive exclusion under parent trees. The [[mycorrhizal-helper-bacteria-mycorrhizosphere-interactions]] in the mycorrhizosphere can amplify these defensive effects through production of antifungal antibiotics and competition with pathogens for root exudates.

## Ectomycorrhizal vs. Arbuscular Mycorrhizal Protection

The mechanisms of pathogen protection differ between ectomycorrhizal (ECM) and arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) associations, reflecting the fundamentally different architectures of these symbioses. ECM fungi form a dense fungal sheath (mantle) around short roots and a Hartig net between root cortical cells, creating a physical barrier of 20 to 40 cell layers that pathogens must penetrate. This mantle also produces antimicrobial compounds including sesquiterpenes, phenolics, and low-molecular-weight proteins that directly inhibit pathogen growth.

AM fungi, lacking a protective mantle, rely more heavily on biochemical defense mechanisms and systemic signaling. Their intracellular arbuscules interface directly with plant cells, creating intimate metabolic connections that facilitate rapid defense signaling. AM-mediated protection has been demonstrated against a wide range of soil-borne pathogens including Fusarium, Rhizoctonia, Pythium, Phytophthora, Verticillium, and Gaeumannomyces species. The implications for forest ecology are particularly significant. In mixed-species forests, CMNs connecting trees of different species may serve as an early warning system, allowing uninfected individuals to activate defenses before pathogen spread reaches them. This network-mediated defense could contribute to the maintenance of forest biodiversity by preventing any single species from being eliminated by a host-specific pathogen.
