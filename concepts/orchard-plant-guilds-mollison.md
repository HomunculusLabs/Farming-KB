# Orchard Plant Guilds (Mollison)

Source: Permaculture: A Designers' Manual, [[bill-mollison]],
Section 12.9 (pp. 423-425)

## Cool Temperate Orchard Species

Cool temperate humid orchard species include pome fruits
(apple, pear, quince, medlar), stone fruits (cherry, peach,
plum, apricot), nuts (filbert, walnut, chestnut, hickory),
and hardy evergreens such as olive, loquat, and Feijoa.

## Selecting Orchard Varieties

### Adaptation to the Region
Search local nurseries, gardens, town plantings, and older
homesteads to list species yielding reliably in region.

### Resistance to Disease
Older hardy apple varieties resist fungal and insect damage,
yielding sound fruit without chemical biocides.

### Site Selection
Well-drained, sun-facing slopes in cool areas; shaded slopes
where late frosts are expected to reduce freeze/thaw damage.

## The Enemy: Grass

The enemy of deciduous orchards is grass. Non-grass ground
cover below tree canopies is essential for tree health.

## Guild Plant Groups

### Spring Bulbs (Narcissus, Hyacinth)
Flower and die back by early summer, creating grass-free
zones. They yield bulbs, flowers, and honey. Iris and
tuberous-rooted flowers also assist [[mollison-geese-grass-control-and-integrated-poultry]].

### Spike Roots (Comfrey, Dandelion, Globe Artichoke)
Cover ground, encourage worms, yield mulch and crop. Soil
below foliage is soft, free-draining, and cool.

### Insectary Plants
Umbelliferae: fennel, dill, Queen Anne's lace, tansy.
Attract tachinid wasps, robber flies, ladybirds, and
pollinators. Quillaja, Photinia, and Acacia serve as
insectary trees in windbreaks and interplant rows.

### Herb Layer Insectary
Catnip, fennel, dill, small daisies, Phyla nodosa, and
flowering [[permaculture-plants-food-forest-ground-covers-bamboos]] attract wasps, bees, and birds.

### Nitrogen and Nutrient Crops
Clovers, tagasaste, and Acacia provide root-level nitrogen.
Crotalaria and marigolds help control root nematodes.

## Maximising the Guild Effect

Maximise flowering components, reduce grasses, attract
pollinators and predators, provide [[fukuoka-citrus-orchard-ground-cover-cultivation]] for frogs
and lizards. Kniphofia, Fuchsia, and Salvia attract
insectivorous birds. Small ponds breed frogs. Nasturtium
prevents soil drying and adds mulch.

## Pest Reduction Strategy

Pest species can be reduced to 4-7% of monocrop levels through:
disease resistant stock, minimal pruning, predation by birds
and insects, leguminous interplants, windbreaks and mulch,
and poultry for windfall cleanup.

## Animals in the Orchard

Bantams at 120-240/ha scavenge windfalls and reduce codlin
moth and [[fukuoka-australia-fruit-fly-incident]]. Pigs handle windfalls when trees reach
3-7 years. Sheep and cattle graze pruned orchards aged 7-20
years as older trees tolerate grass competition.
