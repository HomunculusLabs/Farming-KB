## Fungal Metabolism of Phenols

Phenols are metabolized to catechol by fungi, which then undergoes further degradation. Key pathways and organisms:

- **Phanerochaete chrysosporium**: Depletes 90% of phenol at high concentration (500 mg/L) after 5 days at 37°C. The pathway involves 2-hydroxylation of phenol to catechol (1 day), ortho-fission to muconic acid (3 days), and oxidation to β-keto-adipate (5 days)
- **Trichosporon cutaneum**: Possesses two phenol uptake systems—an inducible high-affinity system and a constitutive low-affinity system. Oxidizes phenol to catechol as sole carbon source
- **Candida tropicalis**: Degrades phenol at 1000 mg/L in 32 hours and 1800 mg/L in 59 hours
- **Rhodotorula glutinis**: Degrades phenol (5 mM), 4-CP, and 3-CP (0.5 mM each) to completion in 18–24 hours with stoichiometric chloride release
- **Graphium sp. FIB4**: Secretes catechol 1,2-dioxygenase and phenol hydroxylase; oxidizes catechol by ortho-type ring fission
- **Candida maltosa**: Utilizes phenol and catechol as sole carbon/energy sources at concentrations up to 1.7 g/L and 1.5 g/L

Coprinus species (*C. cinereus*, *C. micaceus*) metabolize most phenolic compounds efficiently. *C. cinereus* produces peroxidases and *C. micaceus* mostly laccases. Phenolic compounds with –OH or –COOH substituents are easier to metabolize than those with –OCH₃ groups.

## Fungal Metabolism of Chlorophenols

- **P. chrysosporium**: Complete degradation of meta- and para-chlorophenol after 15 days; ortho-position is the preferred attack site. Acclimated mycelium enhances degradation rates and reduces positional preference. Degraded 2,6-DCP within 3 days at >99% rate in both N-limited and N-sufficient media
- **Pleurotus species**: *P. ostreatus*, *P. pulmonarius*, *P. eryngii*, and *P. sajor-caju* all achieve 100% degradation of 2,4-DCP (100 μM) within 10–24 hours
- **Rhodotorula glutinis**: Forms catechol and muconolactone during phenol assimilation, indicating ortho-fission as the preferred mechanism
- **Pleurotus cornucopiae**: Reduced 25% of 2,4,5-TCP or 2,4,6-TCP within 4–5 days; 56% of [¹⁴C]-2,4,5-TCP mineralized to CO₂

## Fungal Metabolism of Pentachlorophenol

- **Oidiodendron echinulatum**: 64% PCP removal (100 mg/L) after 5 days, largely through adsorption
- **Calcarisporium arbuscula**: 88% PCP removal
- **Absidia and Cunninghamella** (Zygomycetes): 69–88% removal, most effective genera among 70 strains tested
- **Polyporus**: Exhibited best PCP-absorbing capacity (31 g/kg dry mycelium)
- **Armillaria**: Highest breakdown capacity (13 g/kg)
- **White-rot strain Wd1**: 68% PCP degradation in 16 days; enhanced by Mn(II), H₂O₂, and glucose

## Fungal Biosensors

Biosensors for phenol detection employ fungal enzymes and cells:

- **Mushroom tyrosinase**: Detection limits of 40–100 nM for phenols using thick-film electrodes
- **Trametes versicolor laccase**: Catechol detection at 1–90 μM/L on carbon fiber electrodes
- **Trichosporon cutaneum phenol hydroxylase**: Phenol detection at 0.5 μM using Clark-type electrode
- **Saccharomyces cerevisiae**: Immobilized cells detect phenol at 0.1 mM/L

Co-immobilized tyrosinase–laccase biosensors detect phenol, catechol, cresol, and hydroquinone at 1 mM sensitivity.

## Factors Affecting Degradation

- **Culture conditions**: Static vs. agitated cultures affect laccase production (10–12 U/mL static vs. 1.5 U/mL shaken in *N. crassa*)
- **Culture age**: Older cultures (60–70 hours) show increased PCP degradation under N-deficient conditions
- **Carbon source**: Glucose at 5–10 g/L supports best PCP depletion
- **Nitrogen**: N-sufficient conditions support slightly greater PCP dehalogenation than N-deficient conditions
- **Fluid depth**: Surface-area-to-volume ratio (oxygen transfer) significantly affects PCP removal by white-rot fungi
- **Light**: 4800 lux improved PCP depletion by *Phoma glomerata* (22% vs. 14% in dark after 24 hours)

## Physiological Alterations by Phenols

Phenolic substrates induce physiological changes in certain Micromycetes:
- 50–68% of fungi produce normal fructifications on catechol, protocatechuic, vanillic, ferulic, and syringic acids
- Phenol (29%) and guaiacol (40%) support the lowest fructification rates
- Catechol, guaiacol, and syringic acid induce the strongest pigment formation
- ~4.4% of strains secrete viscous substances on protocatechuic acid
- ~8.6% show morphological abnormalities, especially in *Alternaria*, *Cylindrocarpon*, and *Fusarium*
- No Zygomycete fructification occurs in phenol presence, indicating sexual reproduction sensitivity

## Taxonomic Distribution of Phenol-Utilizing Fungi

Among 809 Micromycete strains, five groups of phenol-degrading activity were identified:
1. **Zygomycetes**: Most efficient (especially *Absidia* and *Cunninghamella*)
2. **Basidiomycetes**: Moderate to high activity
3. **Ascomycetes**: Heterogeneous results
4. **Coelomycetes**: Lower activity
5. **Tuberculariales**: Variable performance

Among 1044 strains, the highest proportion of phenol-degrading fungi belong to Melanconiales, Mucedinaceae (*Penicillium*), Tuberculariales, and Sphaeropsidales. *Trichosporon* species within Tremellomycetidae utilize phenol and similar substrates. No correlation exists between phenol oxidase production and PCP depletion across all Micromycete classes.
