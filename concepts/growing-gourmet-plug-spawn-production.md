# Plug Spawn Production for Log Cultivation

Plug spawn consists of wooden dowels that have been sterilized and
inoculated with mushroom mycelium. These dowels are driven into
holes drilled in logs or stumps, providing a direct method for
establishing mushroom cultures in natural wood substrates. Plug

For [[growing-gourmet-reishi-cultivation]], the mycelium can be

fermentation vessels. This liquid inoculum is then used to generate
grain spawn, empowering the mushroom mycelium with prior knowledge
of its destination substrate.

## Log Selection and Preparation

Logs for plug spawn inoculation should be freshly cut, preferably
within 2-6 weeks of felling. The wood should be from living,
healthy trees. Logs that have been sitting for extended periods
become colonized by competitors, reducing the likelihood of
successful mushroom establishment. The species of wood should match
the preferences of the mushroom being cultivated. Oak is the
preferred wood for Shiitake, while many Oyster species are more
adaptable.

Log diameter should ideally be 4-8 inches for most species. Larger
logs take longer to colonize but can produce for many more years.
The logs are drilled with holes spaced in a diamond pattern, and
the plug spawn dowels are tapped firmly into each hole. The holes
are then sealed with wax to prevent desiccation and contamination.
