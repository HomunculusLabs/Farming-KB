     9|# Agar Culture and Isolation Techniques
    10|
    11|Agar culture is the foundamushroom-cultivationhroom cultivation. Workimyceliummycelium on nutrient agar plates allows cultivators to isolate clean cultures,
    12|select for desirable traits, store genetics long-term, and expand inoculum
    13|without the genetic variability inherent to spore-based methods. Mastery of
    14|agar technique is the single most impactful skill upgrade for any mushroom
    15|cultivator.
    16|
    17|## What Is Agar?
    18|
    19|Agar is a gelatinous substance derived from red algae. It serves as a solid
    20|growth medium when combined with nutrients and water. Agar melts at 185°F
    21|(85°C) and solidifies at approximately 100-110°F (38-43°C), allowing it to
    22|be sterilized as a liquid and poured into plates where it sets into a firm
    23|surface for mycelium growth.
    24|
    25|## Agar Recipes
    26|
 [[malt-extract-agar]]Malt Extract Agar (MEA)
    28|
    29|The standard, most widely used recipe:
    30|- 10g light malt extract (DME)
    31|- 10g agar agar powder
    32|- 500ml water
    33|
    34|Provides balanced nutrition suitable for most mushroom species. Simple,
    35|reliable, and inexpensive.
    36|
    37|### Potato Dextrose Agar (PDA)
    38|
    39|A classic mycological medium:
    40|- 200g sliced potatoes (boiled in 500ml water for 30 minutes, broth saved)
    41|- 10g dextrose (or substitute with 10g light malt extract)
    42|- 10g agar agar powder
    43|- Water to 500ml total
    44|
    45|Rich in starches and sugars, promoting vigorous mycelial growth. Slightly
    46|more labor-intensive than MEA.
    47|
    48|### Dog Food Agar (DFA)
    49|
    50|20g dog food kibble, 10g agar, 500ml water. Boil kibble 20 minutes, strain,
    51|add agar. Produces very fast, rhizomorphic growth. Good for reviving old
    52|cultures.
    53|
    54|### Yeast Malt Agar (YMA)
    55|
    56|3g yeast extract, 10g malt extract, 15g agar, 500ml water. Enhanced nutrition
    57|for demanding species like *Ganoderma*.
    58|
    59|## Preparation and Sterilization
    60|
    61|1. Mix dry ingredients with water in a flask or bottle.
    62|2. Heat with stirring until agar fully dissolves (microwave or stovetop).
    63|3. Pour into media bottles or jars (no more than 2/3 full).
    64|4. Sterilize at 15 psi for 20-30 minutes in a pressure cooker.
    65|5. Cool to ~120°F (49°C), then pour ~20-25ml per 90mm plate in a still air
    66|   box or laminar flow hood.
    67|6. Allow to solidify with lids slightly ajar to release condensation.
    68|7. Seal and store at room temperature; use within 2-4 weeks or refrigerate
    69|   (sealed in bags) for up to 2 months. Dehydrated plates should be discarded.
    70|
[[inoculation-methods]]oculation Methods
    72|
    73|### Spore Swab or Print
    74|
    75|Spores from a print or swab are streaked across the agar surface using a
    76|sterile loop or inoculation needle. Germination typically occurs within 3-10
    77|days depending on species and spore viability. Each spore produces a
    78|genetically unique mycelium, resulting in a multispore culture with genetic
    79|variability.
    80|
    81|### Tissue Culture
    82|
    83|A small piece of inner tissue from a fresh fruiting body is placed on the agar
    84|surface, capturing the exact parent genetics (clonal propagation). Sterilize
    85|the outer surface, tear (not cut) the mushroom open, transfer a rice-grain
    86|piece of inner tissue with flame-sterilized tweezers, seal, and incubate.
    87|
    88|### Liquid Culture Transfer
    89|
    90|A small amount of liquid culture is dropped onto the agar surface. This is
    91|primarily used to test liquid culture for contamination or to isolate sectors
    92|from a known culture. Liquid culture should not be the sole method of storage
    93|due to risk of senescence and contamination over time.
    94|
    95|## Isolation Techniques
    96|
    97|### Sectoring
    98|
    99|When mycelium grows on agar, it often displays sectors — areas of visibly
   100|different morphology. Fast-growing, ropey (rhizomorphic) sectors are generally
   101|more vigorous fruiters than slow, cottony (tomentose) ones. Isolate a sector
   102|by cutting a wedge from the leading edge and transferring to a fresh plate.
   103|Repeat 2-4 times for a clean, isolated strain.
   104|
 [[contamination-identification]]on Identification and Elimination
   106|
   107|Common agar contaminants and their appearance:
   108|
   109|- **Bacteria**: Milky, glossy, iridescent colonies on the agar surface.
   110|  Often smell sour or rotten. Mycelium may grow around but not through
   111|  bacterial colonies.
   112|- **Mold** (Aspergillus, Penicillium, Trichoderma): Fast-growing, colored
   113|  colonies (green, blue-green, black). Produce spores that can spread rapidly.
   114|  Affected plates should be discarded in a sealed bag.
   115|- **Yeast**: Creamy, raised, circular colonies. Often white or pinkish.
   116|  Slower growing than molds but persistent.
   117|
   118|## Storage and Preservation
   119|
   120|### Slant Cultures
   121|
   122|Agar is poured into small tubes at an angle, creating a slanted surface.
   123|Inoculated slants are sealed and stored at refrigeration temperature
   124|(35-39°F / 2-4°C), where mycelium growth slows to near-zero. Slants preserve
   125|viable cultures for 1-3 years. Master slants serve as the "seed bank" and are
   126|never opened for routine use; they inoculate working slants for production,
   127|preventing loss of the original genetics.
   128|
   129|## Agar-to-Grain Transfer
   130|
   131|Once a clean isolate is established, it is expanded to grain spawn. A full agar
   132|plate or wedge is transferred to a sterilized grain jar in a still air box or
   133|laminar flow hood. The mycelium colonizes the grain over 7-21 days depending
   134|on species and grain type.
   135|
   136|## Equipment
   137|
   138|- **Petri dishes**: 90mm disposable plastic or reusable glass. Pre-sterilized
   139|  disposable plates eliminate a preparation step.
   140|- **Still air box (SAB)**: A clear plastic container with two arm holes.
