Cannabinoids demonstrate anti-tumor activity against liver cancer (hepatocellular carcinoma) through apoptosis induction and tumor growth inhibition. The [[blesching-cannabis-health-index-cannabinoid-terpene-synergy]] assigns a CHI value of 4 from 2 studies reviewed.

## Understanding Liver Cancer

Liver cancer (hepatocellular carcinoma, HCC) is a malignancy of the liver. Hepatocellular carcinoma is the most common form. Signs and symptoms include:
- Right upper abdominal distention, tenderness, pain (may be radiating)
- Jaundice (yellow skin, sclera)
- Brown urine
- Weight loss
- Nausea and vomiting

Risk factors include: male gender, chronic hepatitis B or C, cirrhosis, fatty liver, diabetes, toxins, alcoholism, L-carnitine deficiency, and obesity.

Conventional treatments include chemotherapy, radiation, surgery (tumor removal or transplant), cryoprobe (cold applications), radiofrequency ablation (heat), and percutaneous ethanol injection.

## Cannabis and Liver Cancer

### Synthetic Cannabinoid WIN55,212-2

A 2009 University of Palermo, Italy study investigated WIN55,212-2 (a synthetic cannabinoid receptor agonist) against liver cancer cells. Results:
- WIN55,212-2 produced liver cancer cell death (apoptosis)
- Effects were both dose-dependent and time-dependent
- Authors concluded: "the results seem to indicate a potential therapeutic role of WIN, a synthetic cannabinoid, in hepatic cancer treatment"

### THC and JWH-015

A 2011 Alcala University, Madrid, Spain study investigated THC and JWH-015 (a synthetic cannabinoid) on various liver cancer cell lines. Results:
- Both cannabinoids inhibited liver cancer tumor growth in animal models
- THC activates both CB1 and CB2 receptors
- JWH-015 has higher affinity for CB2 than CB1

## Strain-Specific Considerations

Both WIN55,212-2 and JWH-015 bind with higher affinity to CB2 than CB1, suggesting that CB2 receptor activation may be the primary anti-cancer mechanism for liver cancer. THC activates both receptors relatively equally.

- **Indica/indica-heavy strains**: Lower THC:CBD ratio; may favor CB2 activation; preferred based on study evidence
- **Sativa strains**: Higher THC:CBD ratio; activate CB1 more prominently
- Both receptor types may contribute to anti-cancer effects
