# IMO Four-Stage Cultivation System

**Source:** [[monster-cropping]] (KNF Recipe Book, Sections 1-2)
**Updated:** 2026-04-18

## Overview

The Indigenous Microorganism (IMO) cultivation system is the foundation of Korean Natural Farming. It consists of four progressive stages, each building upon the previous one, that transform wild microorganisms collected from the local environment into a potent soil amendment. The four stages are:

- **IMO #1**: Collection of indigenous microorganisms from the field
- **IMO #2**: Foundation stock (preservation with brown sugar)
- **IMO #3**: Pure stock (multiplication on rice bran)
- **IMO #4**: Final product (soil-based cultivation for field application)

## Stage 1: IMO #1 -- Collection

### Materials
- A wooden lunch box (avoid plastic or metal)
- Cooked rice (not overcooked)
- Container box (to protect from animals and mark the location)

### Method
1. Fill the wooden lunch box about two-thirds full with cooked rice
2. Place the box in a location appropriate for collecting the desired microorganisms:
   - **Bamboo grove floor**: for general-purpose microbes
   - **Forest floor under deciduous trees**: for leaf-decomposing microbes
   - **Field margins**: for crop-associated microbes
3. Cover the box to protect from rain and animals
4. Leave for several days (typically 5-7 nights) depending on temperature

### What Happens
Microorganisms from the surrounding environment colonize the rice. A white cottony growth (fungal mycelium) indicates successful collection. Different colors indicate different microbial communities:

- White: beneficial fungi (desired)
- Black, green, or other colors: unwanted organisms (avoid these locations)

### Important Notes
- Do not use rice from a new crop immediately after harvest; use rice that is at least one week old
- The wooden box is important because it breathes and does not interfere with microbial colonization

## Stage 2: IMO #2 -- Foundation Stock

### Method
1. Mix IMO #1 with brown sugar in a 1:1 ratio by weight
2. Place the mixture in a clay jar (porous earthenware, not airtight)
3. Cover with porous paper secured with a rubber band
4. Store in a cool, shaded place

### What Happens
The brown sugar acts as a preservative, drawing moisture from the microorganisms through osmotic pressure and creating a stable, storable product. The microorganisms enter a dormant state but remain viable.

### Storage
IMO #2 can be stored for extended periods. It serves as the "seed stock" from which all subsequent IMO is propagated. This means that once a farmer successfully collects IMO #1, they can perpetuate the culture indefinitely without needing to recollect from the wild.

## Stage 3: IMO #3 -- Pure Stock

### Materials
- IMO #2 (foundation stock)
- Rice bran
- Nutrient liquids: OHN (1/1,000), FPJ of Japanese mugwort or dropwort (1/500), BRV (1/500)

### Method
1. Dilute IMO #2 by 500 to 1,000 times with water
2. Mix with rice bran
3. Add nutrient liquids (OHN, FPJ, BRV) to activate the microorganisms
4. Stack the rice bran mixture evenly 35-40 cm high
5. Cover with straw mats or similar material
6. Monitor temperature (should not exceed 50 degrees Celsius)

### Fermentation Process
- First 2-3 days: Temperature rises as microbes multiply rapidly
- Day 3-4: Temperature peaks, then begins to stabilize
- Day 5-7: White mycelium appears throughout the mixture
- When fully cultivated, the mixture forms a cohesive lump with white fungal growth throughout

### What Happens
The rice bran provides carbohydrates and nutrients for the microorganisms to multiply. The nutrient liquids provide vitamins, minerals, and growth factors that support diverse microbial communities. The controlled fermentation selects for beneficial aerobic organisms while suppressing pathogens.

### Storage
Keep IMO #3 in a ventilated container with rice straw or fallen leaves at the bottom. Maintain moisture but avoid waterlogging.

## Stage 4: IMO #4 -- Field-Ready Product

### Materials
- IMO #3 (pure stock)
- OHN (1/1,000)
- Fermented plant juice, BRV, and other nutrient liquids
- Soil mixture: field soil 50%, mountain soil and loess 50% (or available local soil)

### Method
1. Mix IMO #3 with the soil mixture
2. Add nutrient liquids and water to achieve 65-70% moisture content
3. Stack 35-40 cm high and cover
4. Ferment using the same process as IMO #3 (monitor temperature, maintain moisture)

### Application
- Scatter IMO #4 over the field so that the top soil is barely covered
- Apply 2-3 hours before sunset and make the soil foundation immediately after
- Soil treatment should be done 14 days before seeding
- Apply 2-3 times: before plowing, after plowing, and at seeding

### Effects on Soil
- Soil becomes warm and soft
- Balanced microbial community is maintained
- Improves soil structure and water retention
- Enhances nutrient cycling and availability

## The Philosophical Principle

The IMO system embodies a core KNF principle: use what is locally available and adapt to local conditions. Microorganisms collected from the immediate environment are already adapted to the local climate, soil, and ecosystem. By culturing and multiplying these local organisms, the farmer works with nature rather than imposing foreign biology onto the land.
