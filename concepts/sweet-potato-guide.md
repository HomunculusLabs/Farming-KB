# Sweet Potato Growing Guide

Sweet potatoes ([[ipomoea-batatas]]) are a warm-climate perennial grown as an annual in most of the US. They produce large, calorie-dense tubers that store for months. Unlike regular potatoes ([[solanum-tuberosum]]), sweet potatoes are in the morning glory family and have very different growing requirements. This guide covers slips, soil, planting, hilling, curing, and storage.

## Understanding Sweet Potatoes

- **Not yams** — true yams (Dioscorea species) are a completely different plant from Africa/Asia. American "yams" are just orange-fleshed sweet potatoes.
- **Not related to regular potatoes** — different family, different growing needs, different nutrient profile.
- **Vining plant** — produces long trailing vines that can root at nodes.
- **Tropical/subtropical origin** — needs warmth above all else.

### Most Recommended for Home Gardens
- **Beauregard** — most widely grown, reliable, good yield, excellent storage.
- **Covington** — sweet, uniform, great for roasting, good storage.
- **Georgia Jet** — short-season (90 days), good for northern growers.
- **Jewel** — classic, deep orange, high yield.

## Slips (Growing Your Own Plants)

Sweet potatoes are NOT grown from seed or seed pieces. They are grown from "slips" — sprouts that grow from a stored sweet potato.

### Producing Slips

**Method 1: Water rooting**
1. 6-8 weeks before last frost, select a healthy organic sweet potato (not from supermarket — may be treated with sprout inhibitor).
2. Suspend the potato in a jar of water, submerging the bottom half (use toothpicks).
3. Place in a warm, sunny window (70-80°F).
4. Change water every 2-3 days.
5. Slips will emerge in 2-4 weeks and grow 6-12 inches tall.
6. When slips are 6 inches tall with roots, gently twist or cut them off the mother potato.

**Method 2: Soil/rooting box method (preferred)**
1. Fill a shallow container (flat, boot tray) with 2-3 inches of moist potting soil or sand.
2. Lay sweet potatoes on their sides, half-buried.
3. Cover with 1 inch of soil.
4. Keep at 75-85°F in a warm location.
5. Water to keep moist but not soggy.
6. Slips emerge in 2-4 weeks.
7. When 6-8 inches tall, cut slips from the potato and root them in water or moist soil for 1 week before planting.

### Purchasing Slips
- Mail-order slips from reputable nurseries are the easiest option.
- Shipped at the right planting time for your zone.
- Varieties not available locally can be ordered.
- Plant slips immediately on arrival — they dehydrate quickly.

## Soil Requirements

Sweet potatoes are more forgiving about soil than many crops, but certain conditions are critical:

- **Texture**: Sandy or sandy loam is ideal — heavy clay produces misshapen roots. Raised beds with loose, well-drained soil work well.
- **pH**: 5.0-6.5 (slightly acidic). Do NOT lime — sweet potatoes are sensitive to high pH.
- **Fertility**: LOW to moderate nitrogen. Excess nitrogen produces huge vines and small tubers. Sweet potatoes need phosphorus and potassium more than nitrogen.
- **Organic matter**: Moderate — avoid overly rich soil (too much [[compost]] = lush foliage, few tubers). Sandy soil with moderate compost is ideal.
- **Preparation**: Loosen soil to 12 inches deep. Remove rocks and debris that can cause deformed roots.

## Planting

### Timing
- Plant slips AFTER all frost danger has passed and soil has warmed to at least 65°F (70°F is better).
- Sweet potatoes cannot tolerate any frost.
- Typical planting: 2-4 weeks after last frost date.
- [[soil-temperature]] can be raised with black plastic mulch.

### How to Plant Slips
1. Trim slips to 6-8 inches if longer. Remove lower leaves, leaving 2-3 leaves at the tip.
2. Optional: root slips in water for 3-5 days before planting (helps survival rate).
3. Dig a hole 3-4 inches deep.
4. Plant slip at an angle or vertically, burying 2-3 leaf nodes underground (roots form at the buried nodes).
5. Leave 2-3 leaves above ground.
6. Firm soil gently around the slip and water in well.
7. Space plants 12-18 inches apart in rows 3-4 feet apart.
8. Water daily for the first week to establish roots.

### Black Plastic Mulch
- Lay black plastic over the bed 1-2 weeks before planting to warm soil.
- Cut holes and plant slips through the plastic.
- Plastic conserves moisture, suppresses weeds, and increases soil temperature.
- Remove plastic at harvest.

## Hilling

- Unlike regular potatoes, sweet potatoes do NOT require hilling for tuber production.
- However, light hilling or adding a thin layer of soil/sand once during the growing season can:
  - Protect shallow tubers from sun exposure (green spots are bitter).
  - Improve drainage around the root zone.
  - Reduce weeding in the row.
- Do NOT hill deeply or pile heavy soil — sweet potatoes form near the surface.
- Vines that root at nodes can be lifted periodically to prevent them from drawing energy away from the main root.

## Care During Growth

### Watering
- 1-1.5 inches per week.
- Critical during the first month (establishment) and during tuber formation (last 6-8 weeks).
- Reduce watering 3-4 weeks before harvest — this concentrates sugars and cures the skin.
- [[drip-irrigation]] is ideal.
- Avoid overhead watering — promotes fungal disease on leaves.

### Fertilizing
- Apply a balanced low-nitrogen fertilizer at planting (e.g., 5-5-10 or similar organic equivalent).
- If vines look pale yellow, side-dress lightly with compost or kelp meal (not nitrogen-heavy amendments).
- Stop all fertilization 2 months before harvest.

### Weed Control
- Sweet potato vines shade out most weeds once established.
- Weed carefully by hand for the first 4-6 weeks until vines cover the bed.
- Mulch between rows with straw or grass clippings.

### Pests
- **Sweet potato weevil**: Most serious pest; larvae tunnel into tubers. Rotate crops, remove crop debris, avoid planting near previous sweet potato beds.
- **Flea beetles**: Chew holes in leaves. Generally not damaging enough to treat.
- **Wireworms**: Bore into tubers. Rotate crops, avoid planting after grass sod.
- **Deer**: Love sweet potato vines. Fence or use repellent.

## Harvesting

### Timing
- Harvest before first frost — frost kills vines and damages tubers in the ground.
- Days to maturity: 90-120 days depending on variety.
- Look for yellowing leaves as a sign of maturity.
- Lift a test potato to check size — if they are the size you want, harvest.

### How to Harvest
- Cut vines 2-3 inches above ground and remove.
- Use a digging fork, starting 12-18 inches from the plant center.
- Dig deep and wide — sweet potatoes can spread far.
- Lift gently — sweet potatoes skin easily; bruised tubers won't store well.
- Do NOT wash — brush off loose soil only.
- Cure damaged or cut tubers separately (use them first).
