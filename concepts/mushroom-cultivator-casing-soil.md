# Casing Soil Techniques in The Mushroom Cultivator

Source: The Mushroom Cultivator (Stamets & Chilton, 1983), Chapter VIII
Updated: 2026-04-18

## Overview

Casing is the practice of covering the colonized substrate surface with a layer of moist material having specific structural characteristics. Developed by Agaricus growers who found that [[bloomfield-mushroom-formation-fruiting-body-development-cultivation]] was stimulated by covering their compost with such a layer, casing has become an integral part of [[duggar-mushroom-growing-history-and-methods]] methodology. It encourages fruiting and enhances yield potential in many, but not all, cultivated mushroom species.

## Functions of the Casing Layer

1. **Protects from drying**: Mushroom mycelium is extremely sensitive to dry air. The casing layer shields the exposed cropping surface.
2. **Provides humid microclimate**: The casing is where mycelium develops an extensive network supporting [[growing-gourmet-primordia-formation-pinning]] and [[pf-tek-mushroom-growth-timeline]].
3. **Water reservoir**: Enlargement of pinheads into mature mushrooms is strongly influenced by available water. The casing supplies this for heavy flushes.
4. **Supports beneficial microorganisms**: Select groups of bacteria (especially Pseudomonas putida) in the casing result in increased primordia formation, earlier cropping, and higher yields.

## Properties of Ideal Casing

1. **Water Retention**: Must absorb and release substantial quantities of water for successive generations of fruitbodies
2. **Structure**: Surface must be porous and open, with small moist cavities protecting developing primordia and allowing gas diffusion
3. **Microflora**: Should support beneficial bacteria; sterilized casing lacks these and has little resistance to contaminants
4. **Low Nutritive Value**: A nutritive casing supports broader range of competitor molds -- wood fragments should be screened out
5. **pH**: Agaricus brunnescens prefers 7.0-7.5; casing gradually falls to ~6.0 by end of cropping due to mycelial acids
6. **Hygienic Quality**: Must be free of pests, pathogens, nematodes, and insect larvae

## Casing Materials

### Peat-Based Casings (Preferred)

Sphagnum peat has practically eliminated the use of soil in [[mazatec-mushroom-culture-and-velada]]:
- Highly decomposed plant matter, pH 3.5-4.5 (acidic, which precludes many contaminants)
- Coarse fibrous peat is preferred -- holds structure better than fine peat
- Must be buffered with calcium carbonate to reach target pH

**Buffering Agents:**
- **Limestone flour**: Most widely used in the US (97% CaCO3, <2% magnesium)
- **Chalk**: Used extensively in Europe, soft texture, continuous long-lasting buffering
- **Limestone grit**: Excellent structural additive (#9 recommended), low buffering
- **Oyster shell**: Good structural contribution but low solubility -- should not be sole buffering agent

## Casing Formulas

### Formula 1
- Coarse peat: 4 parts
- Limestone flour: 1 part
- Limestone grit: 1/2 part
- Water: approximately 2-2.25 parts

### Formula 2
- Coarse peat: 2 parts
- Chalk or Marl: 1 part
- Water: approximately 1-1.25 parts

Up to 1/2 to 1 part coarse vermiculite can be added to improve water retention. Optimum moisture content is normally 2-4% below saturation; peat-based casings are balanced to 70-75% moisture.

## Application

**Depth Guidelines:**
- Substrates 6-8 inches deep: case 1.25-1.5 inches
- Substrates deeper than 8 inches: case 1.5-2 inches
- Sterilized grain: 3/4 to 1 inch
- **Minimum on bulk substrates: 1 inch**
- General rule: more mushrooms expected per sq ft = deeper casing

The casing surface should be rough and porous (small "mountains and valleys") -- this provides more surface area for pinhead formation and allows gas diffusion.

## Casing Colonization

Environmental conditions after casing should match spawn running:
- Substrate temperature in optimum range for [[brassinosteroid-psilocybe-cubensis-mycelial-growth-research]]
- Relative humidity: 90-100%
- Fresh air kept to minimum (CO2 buildup is beneficial)
- Plastic sheet over casing prevents moisture loss

Within 3 days, mycelium should be growing into the casing. Water gradually up to optimum moisture capacity via light mistings over 2-4 days. **Critical**: waterings must not damage the surface structure -- heavy watering can "pan" the surface, sealing all pore spaces.

## Casing Moisture and Mycelial Appearance

- **Optimum moisture**: Thick white rhizomorphic strands branching into finer capillaries; casing held firmly together but separates easily; soft and pliable
- **Overly dry**: Lack of rhizomorphs, fine capillary mycelia; casing becomes hard and compact; susceptible to "overlay"; mushrooms form only along edges
- **Overly wet**: Coarse stringy mycelium with little branching; slow sparse growth; casing largely uncolonized; may leach onto substrate surface

## Overlay and Its Causes

Overlay is a dense mycelial growth covering the casing surface with little or no inclination to form pinheads. Caused by:
- Dry casing
- High CO2 levels
- Low humidity
- Air temperature too high

Overlay can be counteracted by "patching" shallow areas and ruffling the surface, but the underlying cause must be corrected.
