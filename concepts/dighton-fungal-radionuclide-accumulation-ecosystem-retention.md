---# Fungal Radionuclide Accumulation and Ecosystem Retention

Following the development of nuclear weapons and nuclear energy
industries, considerable concern has arisen regarding safe storage of
radionuclide waste and hazards of radiation pollution from nuclear
detonations and releases from atomic energy plants. The Chernobyl
disaster in 1986 focused attention on accumulation of radioactive
fallout in terrestrial ecosystem components, including fungi, which
serve as both [[laccaria-amethystina]] and Cortinarius armillatus), up
to 80 times the safe consumption limit.

Fungi accumulate a range of radionuclides beyond cesium, including 7Be,
60Co, 90Sr, 95Zr, 95Nb, 100Ag, 125Sb, 144Ce, 226Ra, and 238U. Potassium
(40K) occurs naturally at 0.15 to 11.7% of fungal dry weight and must
be accounted for when measuring total radioactivity.
