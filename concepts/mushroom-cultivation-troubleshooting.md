# Mushroom Cultivation Troubleshooting

"Preventing is better than solving problems." This principle underlies all
[[mushroom-cultivation]] practices. However, when problems do arise, prompt
identification and appropriate response are essential to minimize losses.
This page covers the most common problems, their causes, and recommended
solutions based on the FAO [[appropriate-technology]] solutions for water purification.

### Bad Substrate
Check substrate quality. Spread and remix, then repackage. Ensure all raw
materials are good and fresh. Pasteurize immediately after bagging to
prevent fermentation gas from slowing or stopping [[mycelium]] growth.

### Bad Pasteurization
Release all air before starting. Ensure continuous steam at proper
temperature (90 to 100 degrees C for 3 to 4 hours for country-style, or
121 degrees C for commercial systems). Verify the chamber door is not
opened too quickly after pasteurization.

### Substrate Too Hot During Inoculation
Ensure substrate bags have cooled sufficiently before inoculation. The
temperature must be comfortable to the touch.

### Bad Strain or Contaminated Spawn
Obtain younger strains of known vitality. Pasteurize and reinoculate
with good spawn if contamination is suspected.

## Poor Mycelium Spread and Contamination

### Pasteurization Chamber Cooling Too Fast
Slowly decrease temperature. Do not open the cover too quickly.
Check that cotton plugs are tightly closed.

### Inoculation Hygiene Problems
Inoculate in hygienic conditions with no air movement. Use a laminar
flow cabinet and sterilize all tools near an alcohol lamp flame.

### Overcrowding in Incubation
Spread substrate bags and improve ventilation. Monitor temperature to
maintain 25 to 35 degrees Celsius. Do not exceed 5 percent carbon dioxide.

### Mycelium Growing in Patches
Substrate is not evenly prepared and some parts have more nutrients.
Mix substrate thoroughly before bagging to ensure uniform distribution.

### Bacteria or Other Fungi Contamination
Check all processes causing contamination. Separate contaminated bags
immediately. Remix substrate separately, remake bags, and pasteurize
for a longer time. For serious cases, stop using the area for 1 to 2
weeks to break the contaminant [[cervantes-sticky-traps-pest-monitoring]] to decrease insects.

### Mite Contamination
Immediately separate contaminated bags and repasteurize. Continue normal
process with remaining bags.

## Mycelium Grows But Fails to Produce Mushrooms

### Substrate Formula Issues
Adjust the formula. Check pH, sawdust quality, and additive ratios.

### Environmental Toxins
Remove any source of toxins including solvents, gas, or chlorine near
the mushroom house.
