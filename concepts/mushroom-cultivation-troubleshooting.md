# Mushroom Cultivation Troubleshooting

"Preventing is better than solving problems." This principle underlies all
mushroom cultivation practices. However, when problems do arise, prompt
identification and appropriate response are essential to minimize losses.
This page covers the most common problems, their causes, and recommended
solutions based on the FAO training manual for mushroom cultivation.

## Mycelium Fails to Form

### Improper Initiation Strategy
Consult growth parameters. Alter moisture, temperature, light, and
carbon dioxide levels. If substrate is too moist, decrease moisture
content before retrying.

### Chlorinated or Contaminated Water
Use activated charcoal water filters to eliminate chemical contaminants
or employ other [[appropriate-technology]] solutions for water purification.

### Bad Substrate
Check substrate quality. Spread and remix, then repackage. Ensure all raw
materials are good and fresh. Pasteurize immediately after bagging to
prevent fermentation gas from slowing or stopping mycelium growth.

### Bad Pasteurization
Release all air before starting. Ensure continuous steam at proper
temperature (90 to 100 degrees C for 3 to 4 hours for country-style, or
121 degrees C for commercial systems). Verify the chamber door is not
opened too quickly after pasteurization.

### Substrate Too Hot During Inoculation
Ensure substrate bags have cooled sufficiently before inoculation. The
temperature must be comfortable to the touch.

### Bad Strain or Contaminated Spawn
Obtain younger strains of known vitality. Pasteurize and reinoculate
with good spawn if contamination is suspected.

## Poor Mycelium Spread and Contamination

### Pasteurization Chamber Cooling Too Fast
Slowly decrease temperature. Do not open the cover too quickly.
Check that cotton plugs are tightly closed.

### Inoculation Hygiene Problems
Inoculate in hygienic conditions with no air movement. Use a laminar
flow cabinet and sterilize all tools near an alcohol lamp flame.

### Overcrowding in Incubation
Spread substrate bags and improve ventilation. Monitor temperature to
maintain 25 to 35 degrees Celsius. Do not exceed 5 percent carbon dioxide.

### Mycelium Growing in Patches
Substrate is not evenly prepared and some parts have more nutrients.
Mix substrate thoroughly before bagging to ensure uniform distribution.

### Bacteria or Other Fungi Contamination
Check all processes causing contamination. Separate contaminated bags
immediately. Remix substrate separately, remake bags, and pasteurize
for a longer time. For serious cases, stop using the area for 1 to 2
weeks to break the contaminant life cycle. Spray area with chemicals if
needed. Use black-light traps or sticky traps to decrease insects.

### Mite Contamination
Immediately separate contaminated bags and repasteurize. Continue normal
process with remaining bags.

## Mycelium Grows But Fails to Produce Mushrooms

### Substrate Formula Issues
Adjust the formula. Check pH, sawdust quality, and additive ratios.

### Environmental Toxins
Remove any source of toxins including solvents, gas, or chlorine near
the mushroom house.

## Mushrooms Form But Abort or Delay

### Poor Fruiting Conditions
Check temperature and humidity. Open or close doors and windows to
adjust conditions. For severe contamination cases, use half a teaspoon
of sulfur in 3.5 liters of water and mist bags and mushroom surfaces.

### Inadequate Light or Excessive Carbon Dioxide
Increase or adjust light to correct wavelength. Increase air exchange by
opening doors or windows at the correct times.

### Deformed or Decaying Mushrooms
Clean substrate surface. Increase air circulation and reduce humidity to
prescribed levels. Never use chemicals during the fruiting stage.

## Pest and Insect Management

### Natural Pest Occurrence
Place lemongrass plants around the mushroom house. Spread lime on
shelves, poles, and ground. Keep the house clean and free of waste.
Use black-light with water or sticky traps to decrease insects.

### Ants
Mix detergent with water and place on ant paths. Do not apply directly
to mushrooms.

### Mushroom Waste Attracting Pests
Use waste as fertilizer or recycle promptly. Do not allow spent substrate
to accumulate around the production area.

## Post-Harvest Quality Issues

### Mushrooms Spoil Quickly
Harvest when younger. Chill mushrooms before packaging. Reduce humidity
several hours before harvesting. Sell mushrooms faster.

### Bacterial Rot (Pseudomonas tolaasii)
Control humidity at 80 to 85 percent. Give enough time for water to
evaporate from mushroom surfaces before further watering. For severe
cases, use 113 grams chlorine mixed in 45 liters of water.

### Light-Weight Mushrooms
Check humidity levels. Shortage of water produces lighter mushrooms.

### First Flush Only, No Subsequent Flushes
Review substrate nutrition and reformulate if needed. Check hygiene,
adjust light, temperature, humidity, air, and ventilation. Improve
growing house management practices. Consider acquiring a new strain.

## Hygiene Best Practices

Maintain hygiene management at every step: clean all persons, areas,
tools, equipment, and surroundings. Stop using contaminated areas for 1
to 2 weeks to break contaminant life cycles. For serious contamination,
spray with appropriate chemicals. Use black-light traps with water or
sticky traps to decrease insects. Always separate contaminated bags
immediately upon detection.
