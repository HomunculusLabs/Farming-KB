# Cannabis Tinctures, Infused Oils, and Topical Preparations

## Overview

In *The Cannabis Health Index*, Blesching describes several cannabis preparation methods that allow patients to consume cannabinoids without inhaling burned plant material. Tinctures, infused oils, and topical preparations represent versatile delivery systems that offer advantages in precise dosing, discretion, and targeted application. These preparations form the basis for a wide range of medicinal products, from oral drops to skin creams and suppository formulations.

## Tinctures

### Definition and Production

Tinctures are liquid extracts created by dissolving cannabis plant material in a solvent medium. Blesching describes the production process: "Alcohol or glycerin may be used as a medium to dissolve plant material and produce a tincture useful in oral or topical preparations. This is often called 'green dragon.'"

### Solvent Media

| Medium | Properties | Advantages | Limitations |
|--------|-----------|------------|-------------|
| **Ethanol (alcohol)** | High solvent power for cannabinoids and terpenes | Efficient extraction; long shelf life; rapid sublingual absorption | Strong taste; alcohol-sensitive patients should avoid |
| **Glycerin** | Sweet, viscous liquid; food-grade | Pleasant taste; suitable for children and alcohol-averse patients | Lower cannabinoid solubility; shorter shelf life |
| **MCT oil** | Medium-chain triglyceride oil (coconut-derived) | Good bioavailability; neutral taste; well-absorbed | Not as effective at extracting as alcohol |

### The Green Dragon Method

The colloquial name "green dragon" refers to cannabis tinctures made with alcohol as the primary solvent. The basic process involves:

1. Decarboxylating cannabis flower (heating to activate THC and CBD from their acid forms)
2. Submerging the material in high-proof alcohol (typically 190-proof or Everclear)
3. Steeping for a period ranging from hours to weeks, depending on desired potency
4. Straining the plant material, leaving behind the cannabinoid-rich liquid
5. Optionally evaporating some alcohol to increase concentration

### Dosing with Tinctures

Blesching provides clear guidance on tincture dosing: "Infused oils or tinctures often come in dropper bottles, which allow for precise dosing. As always, start slowly with a few drops, and wait at least one hour to feel the effect. Then slowly increase the dosage by a couple of drops, repeating the process until you have achieved the desired therapeutic effect."

Key dosing principles from the Cannabis Health Index:

- **Start low** — Many patients begin with 3–4 drops
- **Go slow** — Wait at least one hour before increasing
- **Track your response** — Document dose, timing, and effects
- **Recalibrate with each batch** — Since most products are not standardized, each new bottle may differ in potency
- **Consistency is key** — Once an effective dose is found, maintain it

### Routes of Tincture Administration

1. **Sublingual (under the tongue)** — Fastest absorption; cannabinoids enter the bloodstream through the mucous membranes, bypassing first-pass metabolism in the liver. Effects typically begin within 15–30 minutes.

2. **Oral ingestion** — Tinctures can be added to food or beverages. Absorption occurs through the gastrointestinal tract, producing delayed onset (45–90 minutes) but longer duration of effects.

3. **Topical application** — Tinctures can be applied directly to the skin for localized effects.

## Cannabis-Infused Oils

### Production Methods

Cannabis-infused oils are made by extracting cannabinoids into a carrier oil through gentle heating. Blesching notes: "hemp oil, almond oil, or coconut oil (preferably of the virgin, organic variety) may be mixed with cannabis as an additive to create topical skin creams and food products."

Common carrier oils include:

- **Coconut oil** — High in saturated fat, which efficiently absorbs cannabinoids; solid at room temperature; anti-inflammatory properties
- **Olive oil** — Readily available; good bioavailability; rich in beneficial fatty acids
- **Hempseed oil** — Optimal balance of omega-3 and omega-6 fatty acids; complementary to cannabis therapeutics
- **Almond oil** — Light texture; good skin penetration for topical use
- **MCT oil** — Rapid absorption; neutral flavor; long shelf life

### Culinary Applications

Blesching explains that cannabis-infused oils can be incorporated into cooking: "Cannabis is usually added to recipes in the form of an herbed butter, infused oils, or tinctures." A critical instruction is to "add the oil or tincture after the cooking process is completed, and at the right dose specific to your therapeutic window," since excessive heat can degrade cannabinoids.

### Cannabis-Infused Honey

Blesching describes a specific preparation combining cannabis with honey: "Modern medicine has rediscovered honey's ancient use in the care of infected wounds." Honey provides antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, and wound-healing properties. While no formal studies examine the combined effects of honey and cannabis, Blesching notes that "many of the therapeutic properties of honey also exist in cannabis — most notably, anti-inflammatory and pain-reducing properties."

The Cuban Ministry of Health recommends honey preparations as:
- Skin-protective agents
- Antimicrobial topical creams
- Alcohol tinctures
- Oral drops

### Cannabis-Infused Hempseed Oil

A specific preparation combining hempseed oil with cannabinoid-rich cannabis creates a dual-action topical: "Oil made from hempseed is void of any mind-altering cannabinoids and has historically been used for the treatment of dry skin, as well as age-related skin blemishes and wounds." It provides omega-3 and omega-6 fatty acids that reduce dryness, itching, and inflammation in [[blesching-cannabis-dermatological-conditions|dermatological conditions]] like atopic dermatitis.

Patient reports of combined cannabis-hemp oil topical benefits include:
- Shrinking of moles and age spots
- Localized and systemic pain relief
- Therapeutic relaxation of tight muscles and cramps
- Deeper and more restful sleep
- Soothing of inflammation
- Subtle increase in libido

## Topical Preparations

### Forms and Applications

Topical cannabis preparations deliver cannabinoids directly to the skin for localized therapeutic effects. Blesching describes the range: "Tinctures extracted using alcohol, glycerin, or oil form the basis of various medicinal creams, balms, and lotions."

Common topical forms include:

- **Salves and ointments** — Oil-based preparations for localized pain, inflammation, and skin conditions
- **Creams and lotions** — Emulsified preparations that combine oil-soluble cannabinoids with water-soluble ingredients for better skin penetration
- **Balms** — Thicker preparations for joint and muscle application
- **Patches** — Transdermal patches that provide sustained cannabinoid delivery over hours

### Pharmacokinetics of Topical Application

Blesching notes that "absorption rates may be similar to that of ingestion but tend to be less systematic and more local." This is a crucial distinction: topically applied cannabinoids primarily affect the area of application rather than producing systemic (whole-body) effects. This makes topicals ideal for:

- Localized [[blesching-cannabis-arthritis|arthritis]] pain in specific joints
- [[blesching-cannabis-muscle-spasticity|muscle spasms]] and cramps
- [[blesching-cannabis-dermatological-conditions|skin conditions]] (eczema, psoriasis, dermatitis)
- [[blesching-cannabis-post-surgery-wounds|post-surgical wound care]]
- [[blesching-cannabis-neuropathic-pain|peripheral neuropathy]] in specific areas

## Rectal Administration (Suppositories)

Blesching briefly describes an alternative delivery route: "Some patients who want to deliver cannabinoids to the lower half of the intestinal tract use suction-bulbs filled with cannabis-infused oils inserted into the rectum similar to a suppository."

This route offers several unique advantages:

1. **Bypasses first-pass metabolism** — Cannabinoids absorbed rectally avoid initial processing by the liver, resulting in higher bioavailability than oral ingestion
2. **Targeted GI delivery** — Direct application to the lower intestinal tract for conditions like [[blesching-cannabis-inflammatory-bowel-disease|inflammatory bowel disease]], [[blesching-cannabis-hemorrhoids|hemorrhoids]], or [[blesching-cannabis-colon-cancer|colon cancer]]
3. **Rapid absorption** — The rectal mucosa has a rich blood supply that facilitates quick uptake
4. **Non-psychoactive option** — At appropriate doses, rectal administration may produce therapeutic effects with minimal psychoactivity

## Raw Cannabis Juice

Blesching describes a unique preparation method: "Fresh cannabis leaf is consumed by many self-growing patients as a salad mixed with other greens. Fresh raw leaves can also be juiced; this is often diluted with other vegetable juices to disguise its bitter taste."

A critical pharmacological distinction exists for raw cannabis: "Fresh cannabis contains CBD and THC in their acid forms. As THC is not psychoactive in this form, fresh raw cannabis is ideal for patients advised to consume large quantities of CBD for its numerous therapeutic and preventative purposes."

Key points about raw cannabis juice:

- **THCA (THC-acid)** — Non-psychoactive precursor to THC; has its own anti-inflammatory and neuroprotective properties
- **CBDA (CBD-acid)** — Non-psychoactive precursor to CBD; may have enhanced bioavailability compared to decarboxylated CBD
- **Nutrient density** — Raw cannabis leaves contain vitamins, minerals, enzymes, and fiber
- **Large-dose potential** — Without psychoactive effects, patients can consume much larger quantities

## Practical Guidance

### Storage and Stability

- Alcohol-based tinctures have the longest shelf life (years when stored properly in dark glass bottles)
- Oil-based preparations should be stored in cool, dark conditions and typically last 3–6 months
