# Biointensive Close Plant Spacing

Close plant spacing is one of the eight principles of [[comparison-biointensive-vs-square-foot-gardening]] farming. Plants are grown in hexagonal (offset) patterns at densities 2-4 times closer than conventional row gardening, creating a "living mulch" that maximizes photosynthesis per square foot.

## The Hexagonal Grid

Instead of planting in rows with wide spacing between plants, GB uses an equilateral triangular (hexagonal) grid:

- Each plant is equidistant from its six nearest neighbors
- This arrangement is the most efficient packing of circles in a plane
- It typically allows 20-40% more plants per unit area than square grid planting
- The leaf canopy closes quickly, creating a microclimate

## Spacing Calculation

The key principle: plants are spaced so that their leaves just touch at maturity. This means:

- The soil surface is completely shaded by foliage
- Evaporation is minimized (reducing water needs by 60-80%)
- Weed competition is naturally suppressed by the closed canopy
- Photosynthesis is maximized because all leaf area is intercepting light

## Benefits of Close Spacing

1. **Water efficiency**: Closed canopy reduces evaporation by 60-80% compared to bare soil between rows
2. **Weed suppression**: Dense planting leaves no room/light for weeds
3. **Soil protection**: Soil surface is never exposed to sun, rain impact, or wind erosion
4. **Microclimate creation**: Humidity is higher, temperature fluctuations are moderated
5. **Yield maximization**: More photosynthesis per unit area means more total biomass production
6. **Carbon return**: More plant material per area means more organic matter returned to the soil

## Relationship to Double-Digging

Close spacing only works because [[biointensive-bed-preparation]] creates a deep, loose root zone. Without the loosened subsoil, closely spaced plants would compete too aggressively for nutrients and water in the compacted upper layer. The deep root zone allows plants to access resources at different depths, reducing direct competition.

## Transplanting vs. Direct Seeding

GB strongly favors transplanting over direct seeding:

- Transplants are started in flats 4-8 weeks before planting out
- This allows the bed space to be used continuously (no "waiting for germination")
- Transplants are placed at exact spacing in the hexagonal pattern
- Seedlings are hardened off before transplanting
- Transplanting reduces the time the bed is exposed/unshaded

## Yield Calculations

Yields in GB are measured in pounds per 100 square foot bed. Jeavons provides master charts with expected yields for each crop, including:

- **Average yield** per 100 sq ft under normal conditions
- **Optimum yield** under ideal conditions
- **Calorie yield** for [[biointensive-carbon-farming]] calculations
- **Biomass yield** for [[biointensive-carbon-farming]] calculations

Typical GB yields are 2-6 times higher per square foot than conventional row gardening.

## Common Mistakes

- **Too close**: If plants are spaced tighter than recommended, they compete excessively, resulting in stunted plants and lower total yield
- **Too wide**: Defeats the purpose; weeds fill the gaps, water is wasted, yields drop
- **Not accounting for variety**: Some varieties grow larger than others; adjust spacing accordingly
- **Ignoring soil fertility**: Close spacing demands high soil fertility from regular biointensive-composting|compost applications
