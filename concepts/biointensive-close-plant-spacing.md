# Biointensive Close Plant Spacing

Close plant spacing is one of the eight principles of [[biointensive-bed-preparation]] creates a deep, loose root zone. Without the loosened subsoil, closely spaced plants would compete too aggressively for nutrients and water in the compacted upper layer. The deep root zone allows plants to access resources at different depths, reducing direct competition.

## Transplanting vs. Direct Seeding

GB strongly favors transplanting over direct seeding:

- Transplants are started in flats 4-8 weeks before planting out
- This allows the bed space to be used continuously (no "waiting for germination")
- Transplants are placed at exact spacing in the hexagonal pattern
- Seedlings are hardened off before transplanting
- Transplanting reduces the time the bed is exposed/unshaded

## Yield Calculations

Yields in GB are measured in pounds per 100 square foot bed. Jeavons provides master charts with expected yields for each crop, including:

- **Average yield** per 100 sq ft under normal conditions
- **Optimum yield** under ideal conditions
- **Calorie yield** for [[biointensive-carbon-farming]] calculations

Typical GB yields are 2-6 times higher per square foot than conventional row gardening.

## Common Mistakes

- **Too close**: If plants are spaced tighter than recommended, they compete excessively, resulting in stunted plants and lower total yield
- **Too wide**: Defeats the purpose; weeds fill the gaps, water is wasted, yields drop
- **Not accounting for variety**: Some varieties grow larger than others; adjust spacing accordingly
- **Ignoring soil fertility**: Close spacing demands high soil fertility from regular biointensive-composting|compost applications
