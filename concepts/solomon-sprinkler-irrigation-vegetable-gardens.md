# Sprinkler Irrigation for Vegetable Gardens

## Overview

Steve Solomon's *Gardening When It Counts* includes a detailed "gardener's textbook of sprinkler irrigation." While dry gardening is ideal, most gardeners [[query-how-to-grow-and-use-oca-in-temperate-climates]] need supplemental irrigation, and properly designed sprinkler systems offer the most practical solution for extensive row gardens.

## The Case Against Intensive Irrigation

### Why Intensive Beds Demand Constant Watering

Intensive raised-bed systems place plants close together in deeply dug, super-fertile beds. This dense planting:

- **Sucks soil dry rapidly**: High plant density means transpiration exceeds natural rainfall
- **Requires near-daily watering**: Limited soil volume per plant cannot buffer dry periods
- **Creates dependency**: The gardener cannot leave for even a week in summer without crop risk
- **Wastes water per unit of food**: More water evaporates from the canopy than reaches roots

Solomon notes yield per gallon of water is actually lower with intensive systems, because so much water is lost to canopy interception and surface evaporation.

### The Widstoe Definition of Drought

Solomon quotes John Widstoe's 1911 *Dry Farming*, redefining drought as human failure rather than meteorology: crops fail due to insufficient water supply not because of Providence, but because of the gardener's choices in spacing, [[coleman-winter-greens-variety-selection]], soil preparation, and water management.

## Four Spacing Systems and Their Water Needs

### Column 1: Intensive Raised Beds (Jeavons)
Closest spacing, highest density. Requires abundant daily irrigation. Least efficient water use per unit of food. Best for gardeners with unlimited water and tiny spaces.

### Column 2: Semi-Intensive (Solomon's Compromise)
Wider spacing on raised beds. Moderate irrigation, 2-3 times per week. Better water efficiency. Good balance for suburban gardeners with moderate water access.

### Column 3: Extensive with Good Rainfall
Wide spacing on raised rows or flat ground. Minimal supplemental irrigation in regions with 20+ inches of growing-season rain. Plants develop larger root systems and produce more per plant.

### Column 4: Extensive with Little Rain
Widest spacing, suitable for dry farming or minimal irrigation. Plants spaced far enough to survive weeks without rain. Fertigation (fertilizer dissolved in irrigation water) becomes essential when water is applied. Solomon's preferred method for hard-time gardening.

## Sprinkler System Design

### Types of Garden Sprinklers

1. **Impact (impulse) sprinklers**: Classic tractor-style, Solomon's recommended choice for row gardens. Adjustable partial-circle, large droplet size reduces misting/evaporation, durable and inexpensive.

2. **Oscillating sprinklers**: Fan-shaped back-and-forth spray. Lower delivery rate, better for rectangular beds, newly seeded areas, and windy conditions.

3. **Stationary spray heads**: Fixed-pattern for small gardens or permanent installations requiring precise coverage.

### Water Pressure and Flow Rate

Understanding your water system's capacity is essential:

- **Static pressure**: Water pressure with no flow (measured with gauge)
- **Dynamic pressure**: Always lower than static when water flows
- **Flow rate**: Volume delivered per unit time (GPM or LPM)
- Most municipal systems deliver 30-60 PSI static, 5-15 GPM flow

Key principle: run only as many sprinklers simultaneously as your flow rate allows. Too many drops pressure below minimum needed for proper coverage, creating dry spots.

### Calculating Application Rate

1. Place straight-sided containers in a grid within the sprinkler's coverage area
2. Run the sprinkler for a known time (e.g., 30 minutes)
3. Measure water depth in each container
4. Average measurements and scale to inches per hour

Repeat at different times and wind conditions, as both affect coverage uniformity.

## Fertigation: Watering with Fertilization

### What Is Fertigation?

Dissolving soluble fertilizer in irrigation water delivers moisture and nutrients simultaneously. Solomon considers this essential for Column 4 gardeners:

- **COF extract**: Steeping [[complete-organic-fertilizer-cof]] in water creates a nutrient-rich irrigation solution
- **[[solomon-foliar-feeding-fish-emulsion-and-kelp]] kelp**: Commercial liquid organics added directly to irrigation water
- **[[aact-compost-extract-leachate-tea-distinctions-ingham]]**: Simple steeping serves double duty
