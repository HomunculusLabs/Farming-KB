## Orchard Pest Management

Detailed IPM guidance for fruit tree pests and diseases in zone 7-8 homestead orchards. See [[orchard-management]] for site selection, planting, pruning, and seasonal care.

## Pest Management (IPM)

### Codling Moth (Apples, Pears)
- Granulosis virus (Cyd-X): Highly specific, apply at egg hatch
- Spinosad (Entrust): Spray at egg hatch, 2-3 applications per generation
- Trunk banding: Corrugated cardboard traps larvae
- Remove dropped fruit promptly

### Cedar Apple Rust
- Resistant varieties: Best long-term solution
- Sulfur: Preventive spray starting at green tip, every 7-10 days
- Neem oil: Some preventive efficacy

### Apple Maggot
- Red sphere traps with Tanglefoot: Very effective for small orchards
- Kaolin clay (Surround): Particle film barrier
- Remove dropped fruit

### Plum Curculio
- Surround (kaolin clay): Most effective organic option
- Jarring: Shake branches early morning, collect adults
- Remove dropped fruit

### Brown Rot (Stone Fruits)
- Sanitation: Remove mummified fruit, prune cankers
- Canopy management: Open pruning increases airflow
- Blossom sprays with sulfur or biologicals

### Fire Blight (Apples, Pears)
- Resistant varieties: Essential in prone areas
- Prune 12-18" below visible infection during DRY weather
- Avoid excess nitrogen

### Peach Leaf Curl (Peaches, Nectarines)
- Single dormant spray: Copper or lime sulfur in late fall or late winter
- If infected: Tree will usually releaf; rake and destroy fallen leaves

### General IPM Strategies
- Scout weekly during growing season
- Preserve beneficial insects (lady beetles, lacewings, parasitic wasps)
- Good sanitation, proper pruning, adequate nutrition
- Accept some damage — perfect fruit isn't necessary for home use
- See [[cannabis-pest-management]] for general IPM principles applicable across crops

## Companion Planting for Pest Control

Companion planting strategies from [[bill-mollison]]'s [[permaculture-design]] work provide ecological approaches to orchard pest management. The concept of "plant defense guilds" (Atsatt & O'Dowd, 1976) identifies three mechanisms by which companion plants reduce pest pressure:

### Insectary Plants (Breeding Predators)
Plants that attract or feed predatory insects which then range across the orchard eating pests:
- **Phacelia** planted in orchards reduces Prospatella pest incidence
- **Umbellifers** (dill, fennel, cilantro) attract lady beetles, parasitic wasps, lacewings, and syrphid flies — these feed on aphids, codling moth larvae, and other orchard pests
- **Strawberries** planted near peach orchards
- **Wild blackberry** near grapes helps support generalist predators
- **Comfrey** beneath fruit trees attracts beneficial insects and accumulates nutrients

### Repellent Plants
Plants that chemically deter pests through phenols and other compounds:
- **Marigolds** prevent nematode attack on citrus roots
- **Garlic and alliums** repel certain insect pests when interplanted
- **Tansy** repels codling moth and Japanese beetles
- **Wormwood** deters aphids and flea beetles

### Trap Cropping (Decoy Plants)
Plants that draw pests away from fruit trees:
- **Nasturtiums** attract aphids away from fruit trees
- **Sunflowers** attract stink bugs and plant bugs as a trap crop
- **Solanum nigrum** (black nightshade) attracts Colorado potato beetle egg masses as a lethal decoy

The key insight from permaculture research is that "a little powerful diversity" of the right kind is the key component of stability. Dispersing plants among varied species leads to far less pest infestation in all cases studied.

## Organic Spray Recipes and Materials

### Neem Oil Spray
- 2 tbsp pure neem oil + 1 tsp liquid soap (as emulsifier) per gallon of water
- Effective against aphids, mites, codling moth, and fungal diseases
- Apply at dusk to avoid leaf burn; repeat every 7-14 days
- Disrupts insect hormone systems, reducing feeding and reproduction

### Kaolin Clay (Surround WP)
- 3–4 tbsp kaolin clay per gallon of water
- Forms a particle film barrier that irritates and deters insects
- Most effective organic option for plum curculio and apple maggot
- Must be washed off fruit at harvest; apply when leaves are dry
- Reapply after heavy rain

### Garlic-Chili Spray
- Blend 1 head garlic + 1 tbsp cayenne powder + 1 quart water; strain
- Add 1 tsp liquid soap as surfactant
- Repels a wide range of chewing and sucking insects
- Apply every 5-7 days or after rain

### Dormant Oil + Copper Spray
- 2-4 tbsp horticultural oil + copper sulfate per gallon of water
- Applied during dormancy (late winter before bud break)
- Smothers overwintering eggs of mites, scale, and aphids
- Copper provides disease suppression for bacterial canker and fire blight

## Seasonal IPM Calendar

| Timing | Action | Target |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Dormant (late winter) | Copper/sulfur spray, dormant oil | Overwintering pests & disease |
| Green tip | Sulfur spray begins (cedar apple rust) | Fungal diseases |
| Pink/Petal fall | Surround (kaolin clay), spinosad | Plum curculio, codling moth |
| June drop | Remove fallen fruit | Codling moth, apple maggot |
| Mid-summer | Hang red sphere traps, monitor | Apple maggot |
| Post-harvest | Sanitation, remove mummified fruit | Brown rot, disease carryover |
| Late fall | Copper or lime sulfur spray | Peach leaf curl |

## Ecological Pest Management Principles

Following [[regenerative-agriculture-history]] principles, the most sustainable pest management relies on building ecosystem resilience rather than reactive spraying:

1. **Biodiversity** — diverse plantings support complex food webs that naturally suppress pest outbreaks
2. **Soil health** — healthy trees grown in nutrient-rich, biologically active soil resist pests and diseases more effectively (see [[soil-food-web-nutrient-cycling]])
3. **Habitat for predators** — maintain hedgerows, wild areas, and insectary plants to support lady beetles, lacewings, parasitic wasps, and birds
4. **Cultural practices** — proper pruning (see [[fruit-tree-pruning]]), adequate spacing, and [[mulching-guide]] reduce disease pressure
