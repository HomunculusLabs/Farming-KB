# Beer Brewing Basics

Home brewing is a natural fit for the homesteader, especially if you grow your own grains and hops. Beer is essentially water, malted grain, hops, and yeast. Understanding the basics of each ingredient and the brewing process allows you to create quality beer at a fraction of commercial prices.

## Two Main Approaches

### Extract Brewing (Beginner)
Uses malt extract (concentrated wort) instead of whole grains. Simplifies the process significantly -- no mashing required. Good for learning fermentation, sanitation, and bottling before investing in all-grain equipment. Most home brewers start here.

### All-Grain Brewing (Intermediate-Advanced)
Brews from raw malted grain. More complex process (mashing, sparging) but offers complete control over the beer. Lower ingredient cost per batch. Required for using homestead-grown grains. Produces fresher, more complex flavors.

## Ingredients

### Water
Beer is 90-95% water. Mineral content affects flavor and mash chemistry. If your tap water tastes good, it is probably fine for brewing. Soft water (low in minerals) is ideal for most styles. Avoid heavily chlorinated water -- let it sit uncovered for 24 hours or use a carbon filter.

### Malt (Malted Barley)
The backbone of beer. Malted barley provides fermentable sugars. During malting, barley is soaked, germinated, and dried to activate enzymes that convert starches to sugars during mashing.

- Base malts (2-row, pale ale, pilsner): provide the majority of fermentable sugars. Light in color and flavor.
- Crystal/caramel malts: add sweetness, body, and color. Not mashed -- steeped.
- Roasted malts (chocolate, black patent): add color and roasted/coffee/chocolate flavors. Used in stouts and porters.
- Specialty malts ( Munich, Vienna, wheat): add specific flavor profiles.

### Hops
Provide bitterness (to balance malt sweetness), flavor, and aroma. Also act as a natural preservative.

- Bittering hops: added early in the boil (60-90 minutes). [[fukuoka-ideal-rice-plant-form-dimensions-high-yield]] alpha acid varieties (Magnum, Chinook, Columbus).
- Flavor hops: added mid-boil (15-30 minutes). Moderate alpha acid (Citra, Centennial, Willamette).
- Aroma hops: added late in the boil (0-5 minutes) or during dry-hopping. High aromatic oils (Cascade, Simcoe, Mosaic, Saaz).
- Hop forms: pellet (most common), whole leaf, or plug.

### Yeast
Converts sugars to alcohol and CO2. The single most important ingredient for beer quality.

- Ale yeast (Saccharomyces cerevisiae): top-fermenting, works at 60-75F. Produces fruity esters. Ferments in 1-2 weeks.
- Lager yeast (Saccharomyces pastorianus): bottom-fermenting, works at 45-55F. Cleaner, crisper flavor. Requires cold fermentation and lagering (cold storage for weeks to months).
- Dry yeast: convenient, long shelf life, good for beginners. Rehydrate before pitching.
- Liquid yeast: wider variety of strains, fresher, more expensive. Requires a starter for high-gravity beers.

## Essential Equipment

### Extract Brewing Setup ($75-150)
- Brew kettle (5-gallon minimum, stainless steel or aluminum)
- Fermenter (6.5-gallon food-grade bucket or carboy with airlock)
- Siphon and tubing (food-grade, no autosiphon needed but convenient)
- Sanitizer (Star San is the standard -- no-rinse, food-safe)
- Hydrometer and test jar (measures sugar content and tracks fermentation)
- Thermometer
- Bottling bucket with spigot
- Bottles (pry-off, not twist-off), caps, and capper
- Spoon (long-handled, stainless steel)

### All-Grain Brewing Addition ($100-300)
- Mash tun (converted cooler with false bottom, or dedicated vessel)
- Hot liquor tank (for heating sparge water)
- Propane burner (outdoor brewing)
- Grain mill (crush grains before brewing)
- Wort chiller (immersion or counterflow)

## The Brewing Process

### Extract Brewing Steps

1. Sanitize everything that will contact cooled wort. This is the most important step.
2. Heat 2-3 gallons of water to boiling in the brew kettle.
3. Remove from heat and stir in malt extract until fully dissolved.
4. Return to boil. Add bittering hops at the start of the boil.
5. Boil for 60 minutes, adding flavor and aroma hops at their scheduled times.
6. Cool the wort to 65-70F as quickly as possible (ice bath or wort chiller).
7. Transfer to the fermenter and top up to 5 gallons with cold, clean water.
8. Pitch (add) yeast. Seal with an airlock.
9. Ferment at the appropriate temperature for 1-2 weeks.
10. Prime with corn sugar (3/4 cup dissolved in water) and bottle.
11. Condition at room temperature for 2-3 weeks for carbonation.

### All-Grain Brewing Steps

1. Heat strike water (1.25-1.5 quarts per pound of grain) to 165F.
2. Add grain to the mash tun. Target mash temperature: 148-158F depending on desired body (lower = drier, higher = fuller).
3. Mash for 60 minutes. Enzymes convert starches to fermentable sugars.
4. Sparge: rinse the grain bed with 168F water to extract remaining sugars. Collect 6-7 gallons of wort.
5. Boil the wort for 60-90 minutes. Follow hop schedule.
6. Cool, transfer, pitch yeast, and ferment as in extract brewing.

## Fermentation

- Primary fermentation: 1-2 weeks. Most active fermentation occurs in the first 48-72 hours.
- Secondary fermentation (optional): transfer to a clean fermenter for additional clarification and conditioning. Common for lagers and high-gravity beers.
- Temperature control is critical: too warm = off-flavors (fusel alcohols, esters). Too cold = stalled fermentation.
- Fermentation is complete when gravity is stable for 3 consecutive days (measure with hydrometer).

## Bottling and Carbonation

- Boil 3/4 cup of corn sugar (priming sugar) in 2 cups of water. Cool and add to the bottling bucket.
- Siphon beer into the bottling bucket (mixing gently with the priming solution).
- Fill bottles to within 1 inch of the top.
- Cap immediately.
- Store at room temperature (65-75F) for 2-3 weeks.
- Refrigerate for 24 hours before serving.
- Alternative: kegging with a CO2 [[blue-light-phototropism-red-light-photoperiodism-dual-plant-vision-system]] provides faster carbonation (2-3 days) and eliminates bottling labor.

## Growing Your Own Ingredients

- Hops: perennial vines, hardy in zones 4-8. Plant rhizomes in spring. Harvest cones in late summer. Dry and freeze for brewing. See [[small-scale-grain-growing]] for grain production.
- Grain: barley and wheat can be grown on the homestead. Malting requires controlled germination and kilning -- possible at home but labor-intensive.

## Common Problems

- Off-flavors: usually caused by poor sanitation, oxidation, or fermentation temperature issues
- Stalled fermentation: check temperature, consider re-pitching yeast
- Low carbonation: insufficient priming sugar, old yeast, or not waiting long enough
- Cloudy beer: chill haze is normal; persistent cloudiness may indicate starch haze or yeast not settling
