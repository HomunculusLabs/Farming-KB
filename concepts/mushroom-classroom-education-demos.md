# Mushroom Classroom Education Demonstrations

[[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]] offers a powerful platform for science education across
all age groups. Tradd Cotter developed a comprehensive framework of
classroom activities organized by grade level and subject track, designed to
teach students about [[gadd-fungal-ecology-endophytes]], cultivation, recycling, mycoremediation,
and scientific methodology. The hands-on nature of [[duggar-mushroom-growing-history-and-methods]] makes
abstract biological concepts tangible and memorable.

## Pedagogical Philosophy

Cotter emphasizes that educators should fruit several batches of mushrooms
before introducing them to the classroom. If the simplest lessons fail to
produce fruitbodies, students quickly become disinterested. Showing
successfully fruiting cultures in advance piques student interest and helps
the educator decide which steps to do as prep work versus classroom
activities. Since colonization takes weeks, the waiting period can be filled
with supporting material like outdoor fungal ecology walks.

## Grades K-4 Activities (Ages 5-10)

### Mushroom Art and Spore Prints

Store-bought mushrooms with gills or pores can be dipped into dyes for print
art, and their outlines drawn and labeled with basic parts (base, stem, cap).
Making [[oyster-mushroom-growing-house-types]] spawn and pack it into cups. Cups are labeled and placed
in an aquarium or terrarium with water at the bottom for humidity. Students
mist daily and observe mycelium spreading within days. Within two weeks,
cups are capable of fruiting. Students measure the daily doubling in
mushroom size and harvest mature specimens for spore prints.

### Coffee Cultivator

Spent [[oyster-mushroom-coffee-grounds-cultivation]] are collected from the school or a local coffee shop,
placed in a bucket, and inoculated with an [[cotter-oyster-mushroom-cultivation]] starter culture.
Students observe the bucket daily, treating it like a pet. The gas exchange
needs of mushrooms are explained by analogy to human respiration. See
[[turkey-tail-trametes-versicolor]] can be blended with water into a
slurry and formed into sheets of paper, connecting mycology with materials
science and art.

## Grades 5-8 Activities (Ages 10-14)

### Gas Exchange and Plant Symbiosis

Building on the fruiting cups concept, half the cups are placed in an empty
aquarium and half in an aquarium containing low-growing plants. Mushrooms in
the plant aquarium develop shorter, stouter stems because plants scrub carbon
dioxide and exchange it for oxygen, while mushrooms without plants develop
elongated stems searching for fresh air. Students measure and graph stem
length and cap width differences, developing hypotheses about the observed
variation.

### Entomopathogenic Fungi

Cordyceps species that colonize insects and steer their behavior make
compelling subjects for this age group. Photos of ants with mushrooms
emerging from their heads capture student imagination while teaching the
fungal life cycle and parasitic ecology.
