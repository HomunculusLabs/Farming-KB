## The Samoan Parallel

### Teenage Discovery

During the 1970s, a group of teenagers in Samoa independently discovered the psychoactive effects of Panaeolus cyanescens. At first, police took measures to suppress the practice. However, persecution was halted when it became apparent that ingestion did not pose significant health risk.

### Parental Response

The teenagers" parents considered the experience "a foolish, but totally harmless episode and a part of normal teenage development." This pragmatic parental response contrasts sharply with the legal approaches taken in Western countries and recalls the ancient European designation of psychoactive mushrooms as "fool"s mushrooms."
