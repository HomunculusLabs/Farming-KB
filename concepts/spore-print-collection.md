## Making a Spore Print on Paper

### Materials
- Fresh mushroom specimen (approaching maturity — convex cap, not yet flat)
- White paper (for dark-spored mushrooms)
- Colored paper (for white-spored mushrooms like shiitake, oyster)
- Cup or bowl (for smaller mushrooms to prevent drying)
- Dust mask (caution: spore masses can trigger allergies)

### Steps
1. **Choose a mushroom**: Pick a specimen approaching maturity. Convex caps work best; flattened caps have usually passed peak spore production.
2. **Select paper color**: White paper for mushrooms with non-white gills; colored paper for white-gilled mushrooms.
3. **Sever cap from stem**: Place the cap gills/pores down on the paper.
4. **Cover smaller mushrooms**: Use a cup or bowl to slow evaporation and prevent drying.
5. **Wait at least 12 hours**: Spores will collect in delicate, mandala-like patterns.
6. **Lift the cap carefully**: The spore pattern mirrors the gills/pores beneath.

### Tips
- A single cap can yield up to 6 distinct prints over a week before it dries or succumbs to contamination
- If the mushroom seems wet, replace paper daily to remove excess moisture
- Place the mushroom on a bottle cap to stay elevated off paper (produces smudgier but still usable prints)
- Write the date, species, location, and notes on the print

## Spore Prints on Glass

Stamets' preferred method for long-term storage:

1. Place mushroom cap, gills down, on a clean pane of glass
2. Wait for spores to fall (12+ hours)
3. Carefully remove the cap
4. Overlay with another pane of glass
5. Tape edges all the way around to seal
6. Result: a glass-enclosed "spore gallery" that can be stored at room temperature for years

### Advantages
- Spores easily observed without risk of contamination
- Glass protects against damaging UV waves
- To retrieve spores: cut binding tape, open gallery, scrape spores with a razor blade
- Can be mailed with few ill effects

## The Bag Technique

For mushrooms that cannot easily print on paper (morels, lion's mane) — they broadcast spores in all directions:

1. Enclose the mushroom upright in a paper, wax paper, or plastic bag
2. Tent the bag upside-down so insects and debris fall out
3. Paper bags are biodegradable; plastic bags give cleaner collection but may collect condensation
4. For morels, Stamets prefers paper sacks — they can be sandwiched into cardboard to create cardboard spawn

## Collecting Spores from Airstreams

Mushrooms orient into the wind (aerotropism), creating a pressure drop under the cap that helps spores enter ambient airstreams:

### Aquarium Pump Method
1. Place a sporulating mushroom gills-up in a spore-collection chamber
2. Use an aquarium pump or peristaltic air pump to suck air from the chamber
3. Aim spore-enriched air into a paper bag, onto glass, or into water for a spore slurry
4. "Gazillions" of spores can be amassed this way

### Forced-Air Dryer Method
1. Place a nylon air sock around the exhaust tube of a forced-air mushroom dryer
2. As mushrooms dry, spores flow into the sock
3. Set dryer at lowest heat setting to prevent too-rapid drying

### Growing Room Ductwork Method
1. Contact local exotic mushroom growers for spores from their air ducts
2. Spores collect en masse in plastic ductwork and coarse air filters
3. Take down ductwork, shake spores into an airtight gallon jar
4. Up to 1 liter of spores can be gathered from a 1,000 sq ft growing room every few months
