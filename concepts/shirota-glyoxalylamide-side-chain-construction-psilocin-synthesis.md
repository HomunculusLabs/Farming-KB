# Glyoxalylamide Intermediate Route for Psilocin Side Chain Construction

## Overview

A critical step in the Shirota, Hakamata, and Goda (2003) [[concise-large-scale-synthesis-of-psilocin-and-psilocybin]] is the construction of the 3-(2-aminoethyl) side chain through a two-step acylation-amidation sequence that proceeds via a glyoxalylamide intermediate. This route, from 4-acetylindole (4) through the glyoxaloyl intermediate (5) to the 3-dimethylaminooxalyl-4-acetylindole (6), provides an efficient and high-yielding path to install the N,N-dimethyltryptamine side chain that is essential to the pharmacological activity of both [[psilocybin-tryptamine-chemistry-fungi]]. Several approaches have been explored:

- **Direct alkylation**: Reaction of indole with halogenated amines, which typically gives poor regioselectivity and low yields due to competing reactions at the indole nitrogen.
- **Grignard or organometallic approaches**: Installation of a two-carbon chain followed by functional group interconversion, which requires multiple steps and protecting group manipulations.
- **Reissert-type reactions**: Introduction of a cyano or formyl group followed by elaboration, which can be efficient but requires careful optimization.
- **Glyoxalylamide approach**: Introduction of a glyoxaloyl (oxalyl) group followed by amidation and reduction — the route chosen by Shirota et al.

### Advantages of the Glyoxalylamide Route

The glyoxalylamide approach offers several advantages for large-scale synthesis:

1. **Regioselectivity**: [[acetyl-protection-route-psilocin-psilocybin-synthesis]]**: The [[shirota-glyoxalylamide-intermediate-oxalyl-chloride-acylation-pathway]] acylation conditions are compatible with the 4-O-acetyl protecting group, eliminating the need for additional protection/deprotection steps.
5. **Direct reduction to target**: The glyoxalylamide can be reduced directly to psilocin in a single step using LiAlH4, simultaneously removing the acetyl protecting group.

## Step 1: Oxalyl Chloride Acylation (4 → 5)

### Reaction Conditions

The conversion of 4-acetylindole (4) to 3-glyoxaloylindole (5) is described in the experimental section:

1. **Substrate**: 4-Acetylindole (17.6 g, 100 mmol) dissolved in anhydrous [[diethyl-ether]] (100 mL).
2. **Reagent**: Oxalyl chloride (13 mL, 146 mmol), added with stirring in an ice bath.
3. **Precipitation**: After stirring for 15 minutes, n-hexane (200 mL) is added.
4. **Crystallization**: The reaction flask is placed in a freezer and stored overnight.
5. **Isolation**: The resulting yellow crystals of compound 5 are separated by filtration.

The use of oxalyl chloride (COCl)₂ as the acylating agent introduces a glyoxaloyl (—CO—COCl) moiety at the indole 3-position. The low temperature (ice bath) prevents over-acylation and decomposition. The crystallization from ether/hexane provides the product as yellow crystals suitable for direct use in the next step.

### Reaction Mechanism

The reaction proceeds through electrophilic aromatic substitution at the indole 3-position:

1. Oxalyl chloride acts as an electrophile, with the carbonyl carbon being attacked by the electron-rich 3-position of the indole ring.
2. The acyl chloride moiety is retained on the introduced group, providing a reactive handle for the subsequent amidation step.
3. The 4-O-acetyl group remains intact under the reaction conditions, as the mild conditions do not promote deacetylation.

### Significance of the Yellow Crystal Intermediate

The yellow crystalline nature of compound 5 is noteworthy:

- **Color**: The yellow color is characteristic of the extended conjugation in the glyoxaloyl-substituted indole system.
- **Crystallinity**: The crystalline form enables efficient isolation and purification without chromatography.
- **Stability**: The crystalline solid is stable enough for storage and handling, allowing the two-step sequence to proceed through isolated intermediates.

## Step 2: Dimethylamine Amidation (5 → 6)

### Reaction Conditions

The conversion of 3-glyoxaloylindole (5) to 3-dimethylaminooxalyl-4-acetylindole (6) proceeds through nucleophilic acyl substitution:

1. **Substrate**: Compound 5 dissolved in anhydrous tetrahydrofuran (100 mL).
2. **Reagent**: 2 M dimethylamine in THF solution (60 mL, 120 mmol).
3. **Base**: Pyridine (10 mL, 123 mmol), added with stirring in an ice bath over 15 minutes.
4. **Workup**: Additional anhydrous ether is added to compensate for solidification. After stirring for 15 minutes at room temperature, the product is separated by filtration and washed with n-hexane, ethyl acetate, and water.
5. **Yield**: 22.0 g (80.0%) as an ivory white crystalline powder.

### Product Characterization

The glyoxalylamide (6) is characterized by:

- **Appearance**: Ivory white crystalline powder
- **1H NMR** (CDCl3, 400 MHz): δ 10.40 (1H, br s, H-1), 7.52 (1H, d, J = 3.2 Hz, H-2), 7.15 (1H, t, J = 8.0 Hz, H-6), 7.06 (1H, d, J = 8.0 Hz, H-7), 6.91 (1H, d, J = 8.0 Hz, H-5), 3.02 (3H, s, NCH3), 2.92 (3H, s, NCH3), 2.50 (3H, s, OCOCH3)
