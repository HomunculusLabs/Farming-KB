## Systematic Gaps in Retailer Information
A snapshot of 21 online shops viewed in January 2006 revealed significant variability in the
quality and completeness of risk information provided to consumers:

### What Most Sites Provided
- Warnings not to use hallucinogenic mushrooms when taking medication
- Warnings about use in combination with alcohol or other drugs such as stimulants
- Information on sought-after effects and expected experiences
- General product descriptions and strain information

## Information Quality Assessment
The overall pattern shows that information from retailers is biased towards positive effects:
- Positive experience descriptions are prominently featured
- Negative effects and risks are downplayed or omitted entirely
- The extent of information varies considerably between retailers
- There is no standardised labelling requirement across jurisdictions
- Information leaflets generally provide no data on maximum shelf life, possible side-effects,
  or active substance proportions

## Legal Status of Retailer Warnings
The legal requirements for retailer-provided information vary across EU Member States. In the
Netherlands, the CAM 2000 risk assessment report noted that the quality and amount of information
provided by those selling the product varies. German law changed its definition of controlled
substance from "plant" to "organism" in March 2005 to close the mushroom loophole, but
information requirements for remaining legal products remained inconsistent.

## Comparison with Official Prevention Materials
Official prevention materials provide more balanced information but have limited reach:
- Trimbos Institute (Dutch Ministry of Health) published a flyer on psilocybin effects and
  mushroom consumption
- Berlin Drug Working Group produced an information brochure on hallucinogenic mushrooms
- UK organisation Lifeline published "Magic Mushrooms - Frequently Asked Questions"
- These official resources provide more comprehensive risk information than retailer materials
