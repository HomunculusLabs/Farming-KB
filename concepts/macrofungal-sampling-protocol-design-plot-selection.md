## Plot Design for Small Woody Debris

Small to medium woody debris (1.0 to 15.0 cm diameter) is
sampled using randomly or regularly spaced subplots.

Each piece of debris within the plot is examined for
macrofungi producing recognisable fruiting structures.

One specimen of every species is collected from each piece
of substratum within the plot.

Portions of hosts that protrude into a subplot are also
sampled to avoid edge effects.

After sampling, substrata are returned to their original
positions for future resampling.

For inconspicuous species like small ascomycetes, all woody
debris should be collected and examined under a dissecting
microscope in the laboratory.

## Plot Size Selection

Circular plots are usually the most efficient to set up and
sample, requiring only a premeasured string and center pole.

For small ascomycetes on branches and twigs, 0.5 square metre
circular plots positioned within larger plots have proven
effective.

Plots 10 metres in diameter work for medium-size wood pieces.

The practical upper limit for circular plot diameter is 10 to
20 metres (78.5 to 314 square metres).

If subplots will be resampled repeatedly, they should be
divided into halves or quadrants, with debris collected from
each sector only once to allow recovery.

## Large Woody Debris and Standing Trees

For fungi fruiting on large logs and standing trees, the
entire tree or log serves as the sample unit rather than
a plot.

Each visible fruiting structure is recorded from base to top,
with the type and decay class of the wood noted.

Collection sites can be tagged or mapped and resampled
periodically to track species turnover over time.

The point quarter method can estimate density of fallen or
standing dead trees along transect lines.

GPS coordinates and GIS mapping are recommended for large
survey areas to facilitate specimen relocation.
