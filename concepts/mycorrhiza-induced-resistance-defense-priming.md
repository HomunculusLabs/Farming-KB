## Priming vs. Constitutive Defense

A key feature of MIR is that it represents a primed state rather than
constitutively activated defense. Priming prepares the plant for a
faster and stronger response to pathogen attack without the metabolic
cost of maintaining fully activated defenses in the absence of
pathogens. This is analogous to the biopriming effect described for
PGPR, where microorganisms prime plant resistance mechanisms through
biochemical signaling. The quicker and upper activation of host defense
pathways in mycorrhizal plants allows more effective pathogen
containment upon infection, as demonstrated by reduced infection loci
and lower intraradical pathogen development in pre-colonized roots.
