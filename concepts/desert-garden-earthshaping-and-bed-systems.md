## Desert Garden Earthshaping and Bed Systems

Home gardens return so much in health, cash, and quality of life that they deserve intensive, bed-by-bed planning, in which companion plants, seasonal succession, bed soil treatments, and a permanent watering method are all designed together. In desert environments, the physical shaping of garden beds and the method of planting into them are critical determinants of success.

## Earthshaping Techniques

A wide variety of earthshaping methods can be employed within desert gardens, adapted to available materials and local conditions:

- **Cool mulched pits** for trees
- **Circle gardens** arranged around a central mulch hole
- **North-south ridges** to shade seedlings from intense lateral sun
- **Mulch "baskets"** at clump plantings to concentrate moisture and nutrients
- **Log boxes** of mulch over alkaline sands
- **Broad flood bays** for carrot crops and other root vegetables
- **Top-watered raised beds** for saline water supply (up to 1,200 ppm)

## Irrigation Without Pipes

Where plastic irrigation pipes are absent or expensive, adequate substitutes can be made from:

- **Unglazed pots** buried near plant root zones
- **Gravel beds** for subsurface water distribution
- **Hand-moulded concrete** channels on or below the surface, covered with tiles or any flat material
- **Bamboo pipes**, drilled to leak water slowly
- **Flagons or bottles** suspended to drip
- **Simple bucket systems**, albeit with more labour

## Planting Categories

Four main categories of plants require different establishment techniques:

### Advanced Perennials and Trees in Pots

Shrubs, flowers, and advanced tomatoes should be kept under shade until rains have fallen, then transplanted in wet weather. Holes should be dug to accept the full root system, the hole soaked, and a small handful of fertiliser added (lime in acid areas, [[compost]], dibble a hole for the seedling. If in thick mulch, pour about one-quarter of a bucket (a litre) of good soil into a hole made in the mulch, place a flat stone upwind, and plant in the soil hole.

### Tubers and Bulbs

Potatoes, yams, sweet potatoes, flower bulbs, and sunroot can be greened and, if possible, sprouted. Tubers and tuber shoots can be pushed down into mulch, or buried and then mulched in damp conditions.

### Large or Fine Seeds

Two strategies exist for direct seeding:

1. **Broadcast method**: Scatter seeds over a fine-tilth bed of sieved compost, wet and press down. Sometimes hessian (burlap) is laid above the bed before watering and removed on day 3 or 4 after the first signs of germination appear. This suits larger crops.

2. **Lens method**: A lens of fine soil 4–5 cm thick is patted down over a thick (12 cm) base mulch, and fine seeds sprinkled on this, forming patches of crop in a general mulch. This suits repetitive sowings in home gardens.

Such "instant gardens" have commonly been established in a few hours. They give satisfactory results in the first year, but as with any garden, do not form good garden soils until a few years of continuous gardening have passed, when the biological content of the soil is stabilised and crumb structures have formed.

## Specific Crop Bed Designs

### Carrots and Onions

Three to six beds (4 metres long × 1 metre wide) should be used for carrots, depending on family size. Another 2–3 beds should be allotted to chives, garlic, and shallots to ensure some Alliums throughout the year. All these beds can be built raised but dead level by levelling with a straight-edge, either sunken below a built-up rim or heaped and levelled off.

Both onions and carrots prefer free sandy soils with moderate drainage and modest fertility (pH 5.5–7.5). These beds receive old compost and dry fine organic material such as powdered grazing-animal manures mixed at a ratio of 1:9 or 1:8 (organic material to soil). Seeds are sprinkled over the levelled surface, previously mixed with sawdust or coloured sand to show seed coverage, and the bed is flooded to the rim with water not exceeding 1,100 ppm salt.

For carrots, a scatter of small salad radish breaks the soil crust and "thins" the crop as the radishes mature in a few weeks and are removed. For onions, gladioli inhibit most pests and provide a small cash crop. For both crops, a nearby border of sunflower, [[keyhole-bed]] is a particularly efficient [[desert-garden-design]]: a raised bed, densely planted, with hardy sunflower windbreak and high shade. It provides easy access with minimum path-to-bed ratio or wasted space. Plants do well when set out as strong seedlings in soil pockets in deep mulch.

## Economic Returns

Planning a garden repays itself quickly. A good gardener expects to amortise establishment costs (fences, beds, seed, mulch, micronutrients) in 6–8 months, thereafter gaining a profit in health, food, and cash.
