# Gottlieb Psilocybin Cultivation Strain Selection Guide

Adam Gottlieb's "The Psilocybin Producer's Guide" (1976) provides detailed
data on psilocybin-bearing mushroom species, with [[psilocybe-cubensis]] as
the primary recommended species. The guide gives habitat, cultivation
parameters, potency, and contamination resistance for each species.

## Primary Recommended Species

### Psilocybe cubensis var. cyanescens (formerly Stropharia cubensis)

- Common name: San Ysidro mushroom.
- Reason for recommendation: most easily obtainable, most readily
  cultivated, most disease-resistant, and psychoactively strongest species.
- Habitat: cow/horse dung, rich pasture soil, straw, sawdust/dung mixtures.
- Range: Mexico, Cuba, Florida, other southern US states, Australia,
  Southeast Asia.
- Season: February to November (warm rainy periods).
- Optimum growth on MEA: 86 degrees F.
- Carpophores appear: 4-8 weeks.
- Thermal death: 104 degrees F.
- Fruiting: vegetable compost in clay pots with silica sand/limestone
  casing, 4-6 weeks in daylight at 68 degrees F. Does poorly in darkness.
- Contamination resistance: very high.
- Potency: potent mushroom.

## Secondary Species Data

### Conocybe cyanopes

- Habitat: dense shade among mosses, wet soil near bogs/swamps/ditches.
- Range: northwestern USA to Michigan.
- Season: May through September.
- Substrate pH: 7-8 (sphagnum moss).

### Copelandia cyanescens

- Habitat: cow dung or rich soil.
- Range: Florida and other southern states.
- Season: early summer through late autumn.
- Spores germinate easily on all agar media.
- Optimum growth on MEA: 80 degrees F.
- Fruiting: uncased compost or rye.

### Panaeolus subalteatus

- Habitat: open ground, manured lawns, straw piles, compost, dung piles,
  roadsides.
- Range: Ontario and throughout USA (especially MA, MD, NY, OH, MI, WA, OR).
- Season: spring to autumn.
- Optimum growth on MEA: 86 degrees F.
- Occasionally occurs as a weed mushroom in commercial mushroom houses.

### [[psilocybe-baeocystis]]

- Habitat: earth, lawns, mulch, decomposing forest wood near conifers.
- Range: western Oregon and Washington.
- Season: autumn and winter.
- Optimum on agar: 77 degrees F.
- Potency: potent. Contains psilocybin, psilocin, baeocystin, and
  nor-baeocystin. Most visually hallucinogenic of the psilocybin mushrooms.
- Caution: unconfirmed 1960 report of a child fatality; proceed with
  graduated dosing.

### [[psilocybe-caerulescens]]

- Habitat: soil, sugar cane mulch, turned earth, stream banks.
- Range: Alabama, northern Florida, Mexico.
- Optimum on MEA: 81 degrees F. Thermal death: 95 degrees F.
- Fruiting: vegetable compost, dim light, 55-85 days incubation.
- Contamination: resistant to white mold but prone to other diseases.
- Potency: not one of the more potent species.

### Psilocybe mexicana

- Habitat: altitudes 4500-5500 feet, limestone regions, mosses, herbs,
  roadsides, humid meadows, cornfields, near pine forests.
- Range: Mexico.
- Season: May to October.

### [[psilocybe-pelliculosa]]

- Habitat: humus and debris in or near conifer forests.
- Range: northwestern USA to Marin County, California.
- Season: September to December.
- Potency: small but potent species.

### Psilocybe quebecencis

- Habitat: sandy soil with vegetable debris in flooded river areas,
  decomposing wood (birch, alder, fir, spruce).
- Range: Quebec area.
- Season: summer to late October.
- Temperature: thrives at lower temperatures than other psilocybes; fruits
  at 43-59 degrees F air temperature.

### [[psilocybe-semilanceata]]

- Habitat: soil, grasses, clearings, pastures, meadows, forest edges, open
  conifer woodlands, roadsides. Never on dung.
- Range: New York, northern USA, British Columbia, Europe.
- Season: August through September.
- Potency: generally less potent, but sometimes quite potent.

### Psilocybe strictipes

- Habitat: soil or decomposing wood on conifers.
- Range: northwestern USA (especially Oregon).
- Season: October.
- Potency: resembles P. baeocystis (likely contains baeocystin alkaloids).

## Broader Taxonomy

Gottlieb notes the total recognized psilocybin-bearing genera:
- Conocybe: ~40 species (forests, pastures, dung, sandy soil, ant hills,
  decayed wood, charcoal; cosmopolitan).
- Panaeolus: ~20 species (soil and dung; cosmopolitan).
- Psilocybe: ~40 species (soil, moss, organic substrata from arctic to
  tropics).
- Stropharia: ~9 species (soil, dung, leaf mulch, rotting wood; fairly
  cosmopolitan).

## Strain Degradation and Maintenance

- Continuous reinoculation from crop cultures causes gradual strain weakening
  (senescence factor).
- Mitigation: alternate stock culture media between PDA and MEA to maintain
  vigor.
- Always select the most vigorous (largest, fastest-growing) uncontaminated
  mycelia for stock culture transfer.
