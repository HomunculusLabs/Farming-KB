## Implications for Drug Policy

The criminological evidence from this period reveals a
market in transition. Prior to 2005, seizure data and
offence statistics were relatively modest, suggesting that
the hallucinogenic mushroom trade operated largely within
the boundaries of legal tolerance or exploitation of
legislative loopholes. The wave of new legislation between
2001 and 2006 created new criminal categories and
correspondingly increased enforcement activity.

However, the seizure data also demonstrate the limitations
of relying solely on criminal justice statistics to
understand the scale of an emerging drug trend. The
absence of systematic seizure monitoring, inconsistent
reporting standards across countries, and the relatively
low law enforcement priority assigned to mushrooms mean
that the available data likely underestimates the true
extent of the trade.
