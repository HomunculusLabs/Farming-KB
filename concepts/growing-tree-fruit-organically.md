# Growing Tree Fruit [[pruning-fruit-trees-and-bushes]] Organically

Rootstocks limit tree size; fans and espaliers are suitable for walls; and even tubs work for patios. Choosing the right rootstock, form, and variety is essential for organic success.

## Apples

**Soil:** pH 6.0-6.5; lime only if below 6.0.

**Rootstocks:** M106 is the standard semi-vigorous rootstock.

**Forms:** Standard (20ft spacing), bush (20ft), dwarf pyramid (5ft), fan (15ft), espalier (15ft), cordon (2ft 6in spacing), stepover (15ft), festoon (5ft).

**Varieties -- cooking:** Bramley's Seedling, Grenadier. **Late-flowering:** Howgate Wonder, Royal Jubilee.

**Planting:** Plant bare-root in early winter. Prune at planting.

**[[query-how-to-manage-pests-and-diseases-in-mushroom-cultivation]]:** Codling moth, woolly aphid, canker, apple scab, [[cervantes-powdery-mildew-prevention-treatment]]. Choose resistant varieties where possible. Encourage birds and predatory insects.

## Pears

Same forms as apples. Need a pollinator nearby. Susceptible to fireblight -- avoid nitrogen-rich feeds that produce soft growth.

## Cherries

**Sweet cherries:** Grow as fans on a south wall. Rootstock Gisela 5. Need deep, well-drained loam. Prune in midsummer -- remove dead, diseased, and crossing branches. Fan-trained cherries pruned like plums.

**Acid cherries:** More tolerant; can grow on a north wall.

**Pests:** Birds (netting essential), aphids. Protect with physical barriers.

## Plums

Easy to grow with heavy crops. Risk from late-spring frosts.

**Rootstocks:** St. Julien A (semi-dwarfing, for pyramids and fans), Pixy (new dwarfing).

**Varieties:** Stanley is recommended.

**Fan-training:** A 5-step process -- establish a [[comparison-central-leader-vs-open-center-fruit-tree-training]], train branches at 45-degree angles, tie to wires, shorten extension growth by half, repeat annually.

**Pruning:** Prune in midsummer for established trees to avoid silver leaf disease infection.

Plums can grow on north-facing walls; they fruit later but still produce well.

## Peaches and Nectarines

Need well-drained soil and full sun. Best grown as fans on a south wall.

**Pests:** Birds, aphids, red spider mite, sawfly, rust, silver leaf, bacterial canker. Hand pollination needed as they flower early when few pollinators are active.

## Apricots

Prefer light soils with added [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]], pH around 6.0. Very sunny, sheltered position essential. Varieties: Alfred, Moorpark. Hand pollination needed (flower early). Same pest/disease profile as peaches.

## Figs

Any well-drained yet moisture-retentive soil. Very hardy once established.

## Citrus

**Kumquat, lime, lemon:** 15ft bush, 7-8 years to crop. **Orange:** 25ft, needs light sandy soil. **Grapefruit:** 30ft, acid well-drained soil. All need winter protection in temperate climates.

## Quince

Sunny position, well-drained deep soils. 2-3 years to first crop.

## Olives

Only for warmer areas. Produces twisted, gnarled appearance.

## Mulberries

Large, slow-growing trees. Self-pollinating.

## General Care

- Apply one bucket of compost or manure per square yard/meter as mulch in spring
- Feed with general [[complete-organic-fertilizer-cof-recipe-solomon]] in spring if not mulched
- Ensure good drainage and [[stamets-growing-room-air-circulation-co2-management]]
- Net all fruit against birds
- Prune at appropriate times (stone fruits in summer to avoid disease; apples/pears in winter)
- Choose disease-resistant rootstocks and varieties
