# Pulp and Paper Mill Effluent Fungal Treatment

The pulp and paper industry generates highly colored wastewaters
containing lignin and its derivatives from pulping, bleaching, and
chemical recovery operations. Fungi, particularly white-rot species,
have demonstrated exceptional ability to degrade lignin-related phenolic
compounds and decolorize these recalcitrant effluents through their
[[stable-carbon-isotope-signature-fatty-acids-mycorrhizal-carbon-tracking]], chlorinated hydrocarbons, and hypochlorite
  degradation products
- **E1-stage effluent (first alkaline extraction)**: Highly colored,
  contributing 80% of total color, 30% of BOD, and 60% of COD from the
  total mill discharge despite lower volume

Lignin converts to thiolignin and alkali lignin during the Kraft process
and to lignosulfonate during the Sulfite process. These derivatives resist
degradation due to diphenyl linkages and other bonds. Color is also
attributed to double bonds, quinone methides, and quinone groups.

## Fungal Species for Effluent Decolorization

Of 110 pure cultures screened, 10 strains decolorize 75 to 85% of
extraction-stage effluent within 24 to 48 hours. Key species include:

### White-Rot Fungi
- **[[fungal-treatment-of-distillery-and-brewery-wastes]] as carbon
  cosubstrate; combined ozone-fungal treatment removes more color than
  either method alone
- **[[stamets-reishi-ganoderma-lucidum-cultivation]]**: Demonstrated decolorization capacity for kraft
  mill effluents
- **[[pulp-paper-effluent-treatment]]; reduces lignin 86%, color 95%, AOX
  67%, phenol 63%, and COD 88% after 3 days in sequential reactor
- **[[schizophyllum-commune]]**: Achieves nearly 80% color reduction in 5
  days with bagasse pith addition

## Bioreactor Configurations

### Batch and Continuous Systems
Stirred-tank reactors have been used since the 1980s for fungal
decolorization. Batch operation remains most common, though continuous
systems offer advantages for large-scale treatment.

### Immobilized Bioreactors
Immobilization on polyurethane foam, nylon cubes, and other supports
enhances operational stability and enables repeated-batch processing.
Immobilized systems maintain fungal activity over extended operational
periods.

### Combined Treatment Approaches
- **Photochemical-fungal treatment**: Preirradiation of effluent for 10
  to 15 minutes followed by fungal treatment achieves effective
  decolorization in 48 hours; eliminates color specificity issues
- **Sequential fungal-bacterial systems**: Fungal pretreatment followed
  by Pseudomonas aeruginosa achieves 82% color reduction versus 68%
  by fungus alone
- **Ozone-fungal combinations**: Partial ozone pretreatment followed by
  fungal treatment provides more effective color removal
