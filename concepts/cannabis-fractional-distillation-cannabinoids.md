# Cannabis Fractional Distillation

Fractional distillation is an [[cervantes-hash-cannabis-oil-production]] into its component cannabinoids based on differences in their boiling points under reduced pressure. This process can yield products of significantly higher purity than simple extraction and winterization, potentially reaching nearly pure THC. Fractional distillation represents one of the most sophisticated techniques available in [[pf-tek-ethyl-alcohol-extraction-liqueur-concentration]].

Adams' method involved several stages. First, the ethanolic extract was concentrated by flashing off solvent under vacuum, never raising the temperature above 50 degrees Celsius. The concentrated solution contained approximately 23.1 grams of solids per 100 milliliters of ethanol.

The red oil was then obtained by distilling off the remaining ethanol from a Claisen flask filled with glass wool. The temperature was raised gradually from 90 to 140 degrees Celsius as pressure was diminished. After all ethanol was removed, the temperature was raised to 200 degrees Celsius. The bath was then cooled to 170 degrees Celsius and the pressure reduced to 2-5 millimeters of mercury. Under these conditions, the residual product distilled between 100 and 220 degrees Celsius, with the bath temperature at 170-310 degrees Celsius, yielding 180-200 grams of crude red oil.

This crude red oil was dissolved in petroleum ether and washed with water. The ether layer was then fractionally distilled through a column with an outside heating unit. The first fraction boiled at 115-120 degrees Celsius (yielding 70-80 grams), and the second fraction at 150-175 degrees Celsius (yielding 25-30 grams). The material remaining in the flask was further processed to yield 90-110 grams of purified red oil.

## Temperature and Pressure Parameters

The critical parameters in cannabis fractional distillation are temperature and pressure. These must be carefully coordinated to vaporize the desired cannabinoids without degrading them. Typical operating pressures range from 0.1 to 5 millimeters of mercury, depending on the specific apparatus and the fraction being collected.

At these reduced pressures, THC vaporizes at temperatures approximately 100-150 degrees lower than its atmospheric boiling point. The exact vaporization temperature depends on the degree of vacuum, with lower pressures producing lower boiling points. A well-calibrated vacuum gauge and precise temperature control are essential for reproducible results.

Foaming is a significant challenge in cannabis oil distillation. The oil tends to foam when heated under vacuum, which can cause material to carry over into the condenser and contaminate the collected fraction. Careful temperature ramping and the use of anti-foaming agents or boiling chips can help manage this problem.

## Equipment Requirements

Fractional distillation of cannabis oil requires specialized glassware and equipment that goes well beyond basic extraction apparatus. A vacuum distillation setup includes a distillation flask (typically a Claisen flask with a fractionating column), a condenser, receiving flasks, a vacuum pump capable of achieving the required pressure, a cold trap to protect the pump, and a thermometer or thermocouple for precise temperature monitoring.

A heating mantle or oil bath provides controlled heating. The oil bath is preferred because it distributes heat more evenly than a direct heating mantle, reducing the risk of local hot spots that can cause degradation. A magnetic stirrer in the distillation flask helps prevent bumping and ensures uniform heating.

The vacuum pump must be capable of achieving pressures in the range of 0.1 to 5 millimeters of mercury. Two-stage rotary vane pumps or diffusion pumps are typically required. A cold trap filled with liquid nitrogen or dry ice placed between the distillation apparatus and the vacuum pump protects the pump from solvent vapors and prevents pump oil from backstreaming into the product.

## Fraction Collection

Different cannabinoids vaporize at different temperatures under a given pressure, allowing them to be collected as separate fractions. The first fraction to distill typically contains lighter compounds, solvents, and monoterpenes. Subsequent fractions contain increasingly heavier cannabinoids and sesquiterpenes.

## Safety at Vacuum Pressures

Operating at high vacuum introduces safety considerations beyond those of atmospheric pressure distillation. The apparatus must be rated for vacuum service, as glassware not designed for vacuum can implode. The vacuum pump must be protected from solvent vapors by a cold trap, and the operator must be aware that the low pressure inside the apparatus creates a significant pressure differential with the atmosphere.

Leak detection is important, as air leaking into the system can introduce oxygen that promotes cannabinoid degradation at elevated temperatures. A properly sealed system should maintain its vacuum for extended periods without active pumping. Leak-checking with a vacuum gauge before heating ensures the system integrity needed for successful distillation.
