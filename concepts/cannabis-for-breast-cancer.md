# Cannabis for Breast Cancer

By 2004, reported global estimates of breast cancer deaths exceeded 500,000 victims. This type of cancer most commonly originates in the milk ducts from corrupted breast cells, which left unchecked can invade surrounding breast tissue with devastating results. While often benign in origin, a dreaded lump in the breast can develop into cancerous growth.

## Risk Factors

One controllable risk factor for breast cancer is high levels of estrogen. The development of breast cancer remains a serious effect of hormone replacement therapy. Body fat is another source of high estrogen levels, as women's fat cells store excess estrogen after menopause. Women who breast-feed longer tend to develop greater protection against breast cancer.

Exposure to ionizing radiation is another known risk factor. Research has suggested that past exposure to ionizing radiation, primarily medical x-rays, may be responsible for a significant proportion of breast cancer cases in the [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]].

## Evidence Base

The [[blesching-cannabis-health-index-autoimmune-conditions]] (CHI) rates the evidence for cannabis in breast cancer at a CHI value of 11, based on 6 studies reviewed. The evidence demonstrates multiple anti-breast cancer mechanisms through different cannabinoids.

## Anandamide and Breast Cancer Cell Growth

A 1998 team from Naples, Italy, discovered that the endogenous cannabinoid anandamide blocks human breast cancer cell growth in vitro. By 2000, researchers discovered a potential mechanism: cannabinoids suppressed certain growth factors and prolactin receptors, leading to inhibition of certain types of breast cancer cell lines.

This finding is significant because it suggests the body's own endocannabinoid system may play a natural role in regulating breast cell proliferation.

## CBD as Most Potent Anti-Breast Cancer Cannabinoid

A 2006 Italian team compared the anti-tumor properties of five individual cannabinoids (cannabidiol, cannabigerol, cannabichromene, cannabidiol acid, and THC acid) to an extract of whole cannabis. Cannabidiol emerged as the most potent anti-breast cancer substance, on par with the cannabidiol-rich whole plant extract.

The authors wrote that CBD's effect is due to its capability of inducing apoptosis via elevation of intracellular calcium and reactive oxygen species. This mechanism selectively targets cancer cells for destruction while leaving healthy cells unharmed.

## THC and CB2-Mediated Effects

A 2006 Spanish team found that cannabinoids are involved in breast cell proliferation, differentiation, and survival. THC, through activation of CB2 cannabinoid receptors, reduces human breast cancer cell proliferation by blocking cell cycle progression and inducing apoptosis.

The Spanish researchers concluded that these data might set the basis for a cannabinoid therapy for the management of breast cancer. The CB2-mediated mechanism is particularly relevant because CB2 activation does not produce psychoactive effects.

## CBD and Aggressive Breast Cancer Metastasis

In 2007, a team from [[shipibo-san-francisco-ceremonial-center-ucayali]] examined cannabinoids in cases involving rapid spread of aggressive breast cancer cells. They reported that CBD could down-regulate gene (Id-1) expression in aggressive human breast cancer cells. The researchers concluded that CBD represents the first nontoxic exogenous agent that can significantly decrease Id-1 expression in metastatic breast cancer cells, leading to down-regulation of tumor aggressiveness.

This finding is especially important because metastatic breast cancer is the most lethal form of the disease, and current treatments for metastasis have significant toxicity.

## Genetic Regulation Discovery

Perhaps the most exciting research comes from Spain, where scientists discovered for the first time that cannabinoids therapeutically influence a genetic component involved in breast cancer progression. THC was found to regulate transcription factor jun-D, which when activated reduces the proliferation of cancer cells.

This identification of a specific genetic target (jun-D) points to a new molecular target for inhibiting breast cancer progression and opens avenues for developing cannabinoid-based treatments with precise molecular mechanisms.

## Multiple Cannabinoid Advantage

The evidence suggests that different cannabinoids may work through different mechanisms to combat breast cancer. CBD induces apoptosis through calcium and reactive oxygen species elevation, THC works through CB2-mediated cell cycle arrest, and anandamide suppresses growth factors and prolactin receptors.

Using multiple cannabinoids simultaneously, as in whole-plant cannabis preparations, may provide a multi-pronged attack on breast cancer cells through several complementary mechanisms.

## Strain Considerations

CBD has emerged as the most potent individual anti-breast cancer cannabinoid. CBD-dominant strains or preparations may be most appropriate for breast cancer patients. However, THC's contribution through CB2-mediated mechanisms and genetic regulation suggests that balanced THC:CBD preparations may offer additional benefits.

## Key Research Summary

Anandamide blocks breast cancer cell growth (1998). CBD emerged as the most potent anti-breast cancer cannabinoid, inducing apoptosis through calcium and ROS elevation (2006). THC reduces breast cancer cell proliferation through CB2 activation (2006). CBD down-regulates Id-1 gene expression in metastatic breast cancer (2007). THC regulates jun-D genetic factor to reduce cancer cell proliferation.

## Limitations and Future Directions

Most evidence comes from laboratory and animal studies. [[mushroom-cancer-clinical-trials-overview]] examining cannabinoids in breast cancer patients are needed. The identification of CBD as the most potent individual anti-breast cancer cannabinoid, combined with THC's complementary mechanisms, suggests that whole-plant or multi-cannabinoid preparations should be studied. The metastasis-inhibiting effects of CBD are particularly promising and deserve urgent clinical investigation.
