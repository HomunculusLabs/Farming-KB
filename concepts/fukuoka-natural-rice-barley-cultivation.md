### Key Innovations

**Straw mulch:** Fukuoka was the first to spread fresh rice straw on fields in autumn, defying agricultural experts who forbade this practice for fear of disease. He confirmed through experiment that rice straw decomposes entirely by spring, leaving no trace of pathogenic microbes. The straw mulch provides:

- Weed suppression (the primary function)
- Moisture retention
- Organic matter as it decomposes
- Microbial habitat

**No nursery beds:** Rice is direct-seeded, eliminating the entire process of seedling raising and transplanting.

**No ridges:** Both rice and barley grow on level fields, the most natural arrangement for grass family crops.

## Results of No-Tillage Cultivation

Fukuoka documented the following results from more than ten years of continuous no-tillage rice/winter grain succession cropping:

1. **Soil improvement:** No-tillage cultivation does not degrade the land; it actually improves and enriches it. The soil becomes darker, richer, and more aggregated each year.
2. **Complete germination:** Broadcasting achieves total germination when combined with proper timing and mulch.
3. **Weed control:** The combination of straw mulch and clover ground cover provides effective weed suppression.
4. **Higher yields:** The method is higher-yielding than conventional tillage-based methods.
5. **Minimal labor:** The labor requirement is a tiny fraction of conventional methods.

## Clay Pellet Seed Coating

For natural rice cultivation to work, the seed must survive on the soil surface through winter until conditions favor germination. Fukuoka developed a method of coating rice seeds in clay pellets:

- Mix rice seed with clay and water to form small pellets (approximately 1 cm diameter)
- The clay coating protects the seed from:
  - Being eaten by birds and rodents
  - Desiccation during dry periods
  - Rotting during wet periods
  - Freezing during cold weather
- The clay pellet eliminates the need for chemical pesticide protectants that conventional agriculture uses on pre-germinated seed
- When conditions are right (adequate moisture and temperature), the seed germinates through the clay coating
