# Tree Biomass System

## The Tree as a Total Entity

A tree is not merely a standing stem and crown — it is a **total biomass system** encompassing many interconnected zones:

- **The stem and crown** — the visible tree above ground
- **The detritus and humus** — the tree at the soil surface boundary, where decomposition and [[aact-nitrogen-forms-microbial-nutrient-cycling-ingham]] occur
- **The roots and root associates** — the underground tree, including [[mollison-designers-mycorrhizal-fungi-and-soil-microbiology]] their networks

Like all living things, a tree sheds its weight many times over to earth and air, and has built much of the soil it stands in. The living tree stands in a zone of decomposition, where [[fukuoka-textdoc-composting-critique-futility-prepared-organic-matter]] is continuously transferred, reborn, and reincarnated into grasses, bacteria, fungi, insects, birds, and mammals.

## The Guild: Tree Associates

Many life forms "belong with" the tree and still function as part of it. These associations form what permaculture calls a **guild** — a community of plants and animals that interact with the tree:

- **Animals as agents**: Blue jays, currawongs, and squirrels bury acorns (recovering only ~80%) and thus act as seed dispersal agents for the oak. Squirrels and wallabies dig up the columella (fruiting body) of mycorrhizal fungi, guided by a garlic-like smell, then swallow and deposit spores elsewhere — inoculating new trees with their fungal partners.

- **Root fungi as energy translators**: Mycorrhizal fungi intercede with water, soil, and atmosphere to manufacture cell nutrients for the tree. They are essential conduits for mineral uptake.

- **Insects as pruners and decomposers**: Myriad insects carry out summer pruning, decompose surplus leaves, and activate essential soil bacteria for nutrient flow. The rain of insect faeces may be crucial to forest and prairie health.

- **Animals as messengers and distributors**: Animals distribute seed, nutrients, and energy — entering the tree system, interacting, and leaving changed.

As Australian Aboriginal peoples understood, a certain shrub, the fire that germinates it, and the wallaby that feeds off it are all called by the same "skin name" — recognizing the inseparability of these life forms. The tree can only be understood as its **total entity** which reaches into all things.

## Trees as Collections of Genetic Individuals

A large tree has from **10,000 to 100,000 growing points** (meristems), each capable of individual mutation. Unlike mammals, trees produce seed from multitudinous flowers, meaning any one main branch can be a genetic "individual." Some deciduous poplars may produce a single evergreen branch. Seedlessness in fruit or a specific ripening time may belong only to one branch.

Grafts and cuttings perpetuate these isolated characteristics, so the tree itself must be understood as a **collection of compatible genetic individuals**, each with persistent characteristics that may differ from place to place on the tree, and each responding differently to energy and stimuli.

Like ourselves, trees are a **cooperative amalgam** of many individuals — some of the tree body, but most free-living agents.

## Wind Effects on Tree Form

Wind profoundly shapes tree architecture. As wind speed increases, leaves and branches deform to steadily reduce exposed leaf area. At very high winds (>32 m/sec), survival becomes secondary to light interception, water use, and heat dissipation.

**Root strategies for wind resistance:**
- Heavy, rigid trees spread wide root mats, relying on their mass
- Other trees insert gnarled roots deep into rock crevices, anchoring literally to the ground

**Forest dynamics:**
- Each species bends and sways with its own amplitude
- Special wood cells bear tension and compression
- Edge trees grow thick and sturdy as windbreaks
- If a tree is tethered halfway up, it stops thickening below the tether

Some leaves twist and reverse to show a white underside to the wind, reflecting light energy — these strikingly light-colored leaves are found only in forest edge species.

## Wind as a Nutrient Conveyor

Forests provide a **nutrient net** for materials blown by wind or gathered by birds foraging from edges. Migrating salmon die in headwaters after spawning, and thousands of tons of fish remains are deposited by birds and predators in surrounding forests. Beyond these nutrient sources, trees actively **mine base rock and soils** for minerals.

## Forest Edges as Essential Protection

The forest edge — with its thick-stemmed, wind-adapted trees — buffers the frontline attack of wind. Cutting a windward forest edge removes these defences, allowing windburn by salt, dust abrasion, or plain wind force to kill the inner forest of weaker stems. This phenomenon is called **"edge break."**

Conversely, establishing a forest begins by planting tough, [[permaculture-plants-fire-resistant-trees-shrubs]] as windbreak to protect subsequent downwind plantings. **Forest edges are essential and permanent protection and should never be cut or removed.**

## Ecological Significance
The principles described in relation to tree biomass system
are fundamental to understanding how natural systems maintain equilibrium.
Forests and vegetation play critical roles in water cycling, temperature
regulation, and atmospheric processes that sustain regional climate patterns.
