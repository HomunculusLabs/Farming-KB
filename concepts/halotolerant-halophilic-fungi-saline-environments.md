# Halotolerant and [[fungi-stressful-environments-thermophilic-xerophilic-halophilic]],
[[marine-mycelial-fungi]]. The
diversity of halophilic fungi in extreme saline habitats
remains poorly documented and likely represents a
significant reservoir of [[undescribed-fungal-taxa]] species.

### Halotolerant Fungi

Halotolerant fungi can grow in the presence of elevated
salt concentrations but do not require salt for growth.
They typically grow best at moderate or low salinities
and can tolerate salt as an environmental stress factor.
Most fungi isolated from saline environments are
halotolerant rather than truly halophilic.

### Halophilic Fungi

True halophilic fungi require elevated salt concentrations
for optimal growth and may show reduced growth at low
salinities. These are relatively rare among fungi and are
typically found in stable hypersaline environments where
salt concentrations remain consistently high.

### Facultatively Halophilic Fungi

Some species are facultatively halophilic, capable of
growing over a wide range of salinities without strong
preference. These species are ecologically versatile and
can colonize both saline and non-saline habitats.

### Compatible Solutes

The primary mechanism of osmotic adaptation in halophilic
fungi is the intracellular accumulation of compatible
solutes, [[fungal-volatile-organic-compounds-communication]] that do not interfere with
normal cellular metabolism even at high concentrations.
These include:
- **Glycerol**: The most common compatible solute in
  fungal cells, accumulating in response to osmotic stress.
- **Mannitol and other sugar alcohols (polyols)**: Commonly
  accumulated by marine and halophilic fungi. Mannitol
  is used in [[agar-culture-media]] to maintain osmotic conditions
  for isolating [[marine-fungi-diversity-sampling]].
- **Erythritol, arabitol, and other polyols**: Less common
  but reported from some halophilic species.

### Ion Exclusion and Compartmentation

Halophilic fungi maintain low intracellular concentrations
of sodium and chloride ions through [[diffusion-osmosis-and-active-transport-in-plants]]
mechanisms that exclude these ions from the cytoplasm or
sequester them in vacuoles. This prevents the disruption
of enzyme function and protein structure that would occur
if intracellular ion concentrations matched those of the
external environment.

### Cell Wall Modifications

The [[chitin-chitosan-fungal-cell-wall]] may be modified in response to saline
conditions, with changes in the composition and cross-linking
of wall polysaccharides. These modifications can affect the
permeability of the wall to water and solutes and may
contribute to the structural integrity of the cell under
osmotic stress.

### Marine and Estuarine Environments

Marine environments provide the largest habitat for
halotolerant fungi. The mycota of marine and estuarine
habitats includes both obligate marine species and
facultative marine fungi. Estuarine environments, where
salinity fluctuates with tides and freshwater input,
select for euryhaline species capable of tolerating rapid
changes in salt concentration.
