# The Morning Glory Complex: Ipomoea and Turbina corymbosa as Psychedelic Substitutes

The highlands of Mazatec Mexico are home to two species of morning
glory whose seeds have been used for centuries as a visionary
sacrament.
Ipomoea purpurea and Turbina corymbosa (formerly classified as Rivea
corymbosa) contain ergoline alkaloids closely related to those found
in ergot fungus and were employed by Mazatec shamans as preferred
substitutes when psilocybin-containing mushrooms were unavailable.
The morning glory complex represents one of the oldest and most
continuous relationships between humans and psychoactive plants.
