## The Bwiti Cult

The Bwiti cult represents one of the most important indigenous religious
movements in Central Africa. The cult uses iboga as its central sacrament in
elaborate initiation ceremonies that can last for days. During these
ceremonies, initiates consume large quantities of iboga root bark to enter
a visionary state in which they encounter the spirit of the plant and the
ancestors. The Bwiti ceremony, known as the "Payment of the Ancestors," takes
place between two shrubby bushes of *Tabernanthe iboga* and involves complex
rituals including dancing, chanting, and the consumption of the sacred root.

The Bwiti cult serves multiple social functions beyond its religious role. It
provides a framework for community cohesion, conflict resolution, and the
transmission of cultural knowledge. The cult's resistance to both Christian
and Islamic conversion efforts speaks to its deep cultural roots and the
power of the iboga experience in reinforcing traditional belief systems. The
ceremonial use of iboga creates intense shared experiences that bind
community members together and provide a direct, experiential basis for
their cosmological beliefs.
