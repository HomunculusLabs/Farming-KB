## Common Supplements

### Calcium-Based Amendments

**Gypsum (Calcium Sulfate, CaSO4)**
- Used at 2-5% of substrate dry weight
- Provides calcium and sulfur, essential nutrients
- Improves substrate structure and aeration
- Buffers pH alongside calcium carbonate
- Reduces sectoring on grain when pH is maintained at 6.5
- Standard component of perlite spawn formulation (6g per 120ml water)

**Calcium Carbonate (CaCO3 / Chalk)**
- Used to buffer pH upward
- Combined with gypsum for optimal pH buffering to 6.5
- Used at 0.2% in [[shiitake-cultivation-guide]] and [[shiitake-cultivation-guide]] at 2% supplementation rate
- Rich in B vitamins and amino acids
- Promotes vigorous mycelial growth

**Soybean Meal**
- Highly effective for [[shiitake-cultivation-guide]] at 2%
- Provides proteins, fats, and lactose
- Must be used at proper rates to avoid contamination

**Molasses**
- Used at 1.5% for [[growing-gourmet-enoki-cultivation]] at 5%
- Inhibits pellet formation in liquid culture when combined with malt

**Malt / Malt Extract**
- Primary carbon source in MEA (Malt Extract Agar) at 20g per liter
- Used in liquid culture for enoki at 2%
- Provides fermentable sugars and growth factors

**Dextrose**
- Added to Potato Dextrose Yeast (PDY) agar at 10g per liter
- Simple sugar for rapid mycelial growth
- Must not exceed sterilization temperature of 250°F to avoid caramelization

## Species-Specific Supplementation Data

### Lentinus edodes (Shiitake)
Best supplements ranked by effectiveness:
1. Soybean meal — 5%
2. Milk powder — 2%
3. Yeast powder — 2%
4. Molasses — 1.5%
5. Rice bran — 10% (standard baseline)

### Flammulina velutipes (Enoki)
- Liquid culture with 5% corn starch + 2% malt inhibits pellet formation
- Sawdust/bran spawn at 4:1 ratio

### Agaricus Species
- Gypsum and calcium carbonate for pH buffering on grain spawn
- Compost-based substrates already contain complex nutritional profiles
- Post-composting supplements (soybean meal, cottonseed meal) can boost yields but increase contamination risk

## Supplementation Risks

Higher nutritional content increases contamination risk. Supplements must be thoroughly integrated into the substrate and properly sterilized. Over-supplementation can select for competitor organisms rather than the intended mushroom species. The composting process for Agaricus already concentrates nutrients, making additional supplementation less critical than for wood-decomposing species grown on sawdust.
