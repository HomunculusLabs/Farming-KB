# Cometabolic Degradation Pathways

Cometabolism is a process in which microorganisms transform a compound that they cannot use as a source of carbon or energy for growth.

The transformation occurs incidentally through enzymes that are produced for the metabolism of other substrates, known as primary substrates or growth substrates.

Cometabolism is a critical concept in environmental microbiology and bioremediation because many environmentally persistent pollutants, including chlorinated solvents, [[bioaugmentation-strategies]] for recalcitrant contaminant treatment.

## Fundamental Mechanisms of Cometabolism

In cometabolic degradation, a microorganism produces enzymes for its primary metabolic activities, and these enzymes fortuitously recognize and transform a non-growth substrate.

The cometabolic substrate may bind to the active site of an enzyme that evolved for a different reaction, leading to partial transformation of the cometabolite.

Common cometabolic enzymes include monooxygenases, dioxygenases, and peroxidases, which have broad substrate specificity and can act on structurally similar compounds.

Monooxygenases are particularly important in cometabolism because they introduce a single oxygen atom into the substrate molecule, often initiating degradation of recalcitrant compounds.

Methane monooxygenase (MMO), produced by methanotrophic bacteria for methane oxidation, is one of the most studied cometabolic enzymes because it cometabolizes a wide range of chlorinated aliphatic compounds including trichloroethylene (TCE), dichloroethylene (DCE), and vinyl chloride.

Toluene monooxygenase and toluene dioxygenase, produced by Pseudomonas and related bacteria for toluene degradation, also cometabolize TCE and other chlorinated ethenes.

Fungal enzymes including cytochrome P450 monooxygenases, described in [[fungal-lignin-peroxidase-veratryl-alcohol]] of [[fungal-manganese-peroxidase-remediation]], also produced by white-rot fungi, oxidizes PAHs indirectly through the generation of Mn(III) chelates and lipid peroxidation intermediates that diffuse into the soil matrix and attack sorbed PAH molecules.

The herbicide atrazine is cometabolized by bacteria expressing enzymes for the metabolism of other s-triazine compounds.

Organophosphate insecticides can be cometabolized by phosphotriesterases produced by soil bacteria for the metabolism of naturally occurring organophosphate esters.

Explosive compounds including TNT (2,4,6-trinitrotoluene) and RDX (hexahydro-1,3,5-trinitro-1,3,5-triazine) are also degraded through cometabolic pathways.
