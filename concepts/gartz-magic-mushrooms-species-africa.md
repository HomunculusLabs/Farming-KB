From 9,000 to 7,000 years ago, the Sahara -- between Tassili (southern
Algeria), Acacus (Libya), and Ennedi (Chad) -- was populated by people
who created magnificent rock drawings. At an altitude of 6,500 feet,
artists depicted mythical beings with anthropomorphic and zoomorphic
attributes reminiscent of early Mexican images: tiny horned dancers
alongside mushrooms, deities with masks and horns holding mushrooms, and
anthropomorphic beings with mushroom-like heads.

The most striking image at Tin-Tazarift, Tassili District, shows masked
beings engaged in ecstatic dancing with dashed lines connecting a mushroom
to the center of the head -- representing a "flow of energy" and the
mushroom's influence on the human soul. The rock drawings consistently
show two kinds of mushroom shapes: one resembling [[psilocybin-mushroom-image-guide-lookalike-species]]. Some mushrooms were drawn in blue, possibly representing the
bluing phenomenon.

Gartz notes that as early as 9,000-7,000 years ago, the head was
apparently considered the seat of consciousness -- a remarkably early
insight. The likely species that grew in the area thousands of years ago
include relatives of P. cubensis and P. cyanescens (dung-inhabiting), P.
semilanceata (nitrophilic), and P. cyanescens (wood-debris inhabiting).

## Documented African Species

- **[[psilocybe-natalensis]]** Gartz, Reid, Ecker & Smith: Discovered in
  January 1994 by Gartz and M. Smith in Natal Province, South Africa --
  the first psychotropic Psilocybe species ever found in the area.
  Generally whitish in color without an annular ring. Comparable in size
  to P. cubensis but does not grow directly on dung in cow pastures.
  Currently being published as a new species.

- **Panaeolus africanus** Ola'h: A strongly psychoactive Panaeolus
  species described in Ola'h's 1960s monograph on Panaeolus mushrooms.

- **Panaeolus tropicales** Ola'h: Another strongly psychoactive
  Panaeolus species from Africa, also described by Ola'h.

- **Psilocybe cubensis**: Under sufficiently wet conditions, P. cubensis
  can be found in African pastures up to 30 degrees north and south of
  the equator. The yellow Stropharia species from the Kenyan Highlands
  that caused intoxications may have been P. cubensis or a close
  relative.

## Documented Intoxications

- **Kenya, 1945**: E.R. Cullinan and D. Henry described 22 cases of
  typical hallucinatory intoxication from Nairobi (July 1945) and Nakuru
  (May 1949). Symptoms began one hour after ingestion, peaked within
  three hours, and persisted for 24-48 hours. Symptoms included emotional
  imbalance, mirthful laughter alternating with depressive moods, pupil
  dilation, visual hallucinations, and euphoria with delusions.

- **Algeria**: Maire's collections at Chrea Pass confirm the presence of
  bluing Psilocybe species in North Africa.

- **South Africa**: The 1994 discovery of P. natalensis in Natal Province
  supports the hypothesis of psychoactive species on the continent.

## Research Status

Gartz emphasizes that Africa's mycoflora has been studied only
peripherally and remains largely unknown. The intoxication reports and
species discoveries confirm that psychoactive species do occur in Africa,
supporting the hypothesis of an ancient mushroom cult. However,
additional work is needed on African species' areas of distribution,
taxonomic classification, and the nature of their active ingredients.
The continent -- as the cradle of humanity -- likely holds many more
undiscovered species and archaeological evidence of early mushroom use.
