# Hill Planting for Large Vegetables

Hills are small, highly fertilized mounds used to grow large vegetable
plants. They concentrate fertility in a compact zone, giving plants a
strong start that carries benefits throughout the entire [[extending-growing-season]].

## When to Use Hills vs. Raised Beds

The choice between hills and [[solomon-raised-beds]] depends on plant size and
whether the garden is irrigated.

### Plants Suited to Hills

Large plants that benefit most from hill planting include:

- Kale
- Brussels sprouts
- Broccoli
- Tomatoes
- [[growing-sweet-corn-organically]]
- Cucurbits (squash, melons, cucumbers, pumpkins)

Small vegetables — beets, carrots, lettuce, bush beans — are better
grown in [[solomon-raised-beds]] or raised [[raised-beds-wide-rows-hills-garden-layout-solomon]], where the slightly elevated
surface makes it easier to create a fine seedbed for tiny seeds.

### Sweet Corn: An Exception

Sweet corn needs to grow in a large cluster for wind pollination, so it
does better "on the flat" rather than in individual hills.

## Non-Irrigated vs. Irrigated Gardens

- **Non-irrigated gardens:** Use hills and single rows, except for
  small vegetables in raised wide rows. See [[drought-gardening]].
- **Irrigated gardens:** [[solomon-raised-beds]] work well for most crops,
  including tomatoes and sweet corn.

## Hill Dimensions

Hills for large plants are typically:

- **Size:** 24 x 30 inches (60 x 75 cm), sometimes 24 x 24 inches
- **Spacing between hills:** Usually 4 feet (120 cm) center-to-center
- **Dry conditions:** Increase to 5–6 feet (150–180 cm) apart
- **Squash and melons:** Hills can be up to 8 feet (240 cm) apart
  where rainfall is low, because these cucurbits spread so aggressively

## How to Make Hills

1. Prepare the whole area first. Spread lime, compost, manure, and/or
   [[complete-organic-fertilizer-cof]] (COF) evenly across the entire plot.
2. Then concentrate additional fertilizer where each hill will be. Place
   a shovelful or two of compost or manure in a little pile, then dig
   a circular spot about 12–18 inches (30–45 cm) in diameter, working
   the amendments in down to the full depth of the spade blade.
3. Blend the amendments thoroughly. You should end up with a small
   mound of highly fertile soil.
4. If the mound is not prominent enough after blending, scrape a bit
   of earth from the surrounding area onto the hill.

For smaller plants on raised beds, use about half as much fertility and
dig a smaller mound — roughly 10 inches (25 cm) in diameter.

## The Principle of Band Placement

The core idea behind hill planting is what agronomists call **band
placement** — placing fertilizer in a concentrated zone so that as
young plants put out roots, they immediately discover a zone or band of
highly fertile soil. See band placement.

This practice gets plants growing fast from the very beginning, so they
outgrow environmental menaces — insects, slugs, and other threats that
can take a huge chunk out of a newly emerged seedling and may even kill
it from a single feeding. Once a plant becomes 50 times larger, the
initial head start still provides meaningful protection.

## Raised Beds: Quick Reference

When [[solomon-raised-beds]] are the better choice:

- **Maximum width:** 4 feet (120 cm) — any wider makes it hard to
  reach the center from the path
- **Path width:** About 1 foot (30 cm)
- **Height:** Only 2–3 inches (5–8 cm) is needed; deeper digging
  produces little additional benefit
- **Bed width range:** 3–4.5 feet (90–140 cm) depending on the
  gardener's flexibility and arm reach

## Raised Wide Rows: Quick Reference

Raised wide rows are narrow raised beds suited to unirrigated gardens:

- **Width:** 20–24 inches (50–60 cm), about two shovel-blades wide
- **Path width:** About 1 foot (30 cm) between rows
- **Row center spacing:**
  - With irrigation: ~36 inches (90 cm) between centers
  - Without irrigation: 36–90 inches (90–230 cm) depending on
    expected rainfall and crop
- In dry summer climates (e.g., western Oregon), row centers of 4–5
  feet (120–150 cm) may be needed

## Summary

| Feature | Hills | Raised Beds | Raised Wide Rows |
|---------|-------|-------------|-----------------|
| Best for | Large plants | Small plants | Non-irrigated gardens |
| Width | 24–30 in (60–75 cm) | ≤4 ft (120 cm) | 20–24 in (50–60 cm) |
| Spacing | 4–8 ft apart | ~1 ft paths | 3–7.5 ft centers |
| Irrigation needed | Low | Moderate | Low |
