# [[psilocybe-azurescens]] — Description and Habitat

Psilocybe azurescens Stamets and Gartz is among the most potent psilocybin
mushrooms known. Originally discovered along the northern Oregon coast near
Astoria, it is a cold weather-tolerant species that exhibits one of the
strongest bluing reactions of any Psilocybe.

## Macroscopic Description

Cap 3-10 cm broad, conic to convex, expanding to broadly convex and eventually
flattening with age with a pronounced, persistent broad umbo. Surface smooth,
viscid when moist, covered by a separable gelatinous pellicle. Chestnut to
ochraceous brown to caramel in color, often becoming pitted with dark blue or
bluish black zones, hygrophanous, fading to dingy brown in drying. Flesh
rapidly bruising blue, then darkening to deep caerulean blue and eventually
indigo-black. Margin even, sometimes irregular and eroded at maturity,
translucent-striate and often pale azure tinted.

Gills attachment ascending, sinuate to adnate, brown, often stained indigo
black where injured, close, with two tiers of lamellulae, mottled, edges
whitish. Spore print dark purplish brown to purplish black in mass.

Stem 90-200 mm long by 3-6 mm thick, silky white, dingy brown from the base
or in age, hollow at maturity. Composed of twisted, cartilaginous, silky white
fibrous tissue. Base thickening downwards, often curved, characterized by
coarse white aerial tufts of mycelium with azure tones combined with dense,
thick, silky white rhizomorphs that tenaciously attach to wood chips or dead
grass, strongly bruising bluish upon disturbance.

Veil white, cortinate, often leaving a fibrillose annular zone in the superior
regions of the stem. Taste is extremely bitter. Odor none to slightly
farinaceous.

## Microscopic Features

Spores dark purplish black in deposit, 12-13.5 by 6.5-8 microns, ellipsoid.
Basidia 4-spored. Pleurocystidia abundant, fusoid-ventricose, tapering to a
narrow but short neck, bluntly papillate, 23-35 by 9-10 microns.
Cheilocystidia nearly identical to pleurocystidia, 23-28 by 6.5-8 microns.
Tissue notably awash with bluish tones.

## Habitat and Distribution

Cespitose to gregarious on deciduous wood chips and/or in sandy soils rich
in ligninous debris. This mushroom naturally grows, often prolifically, along
the northern Oregon coast near Astoria, favoring the land adjacent to the
shoreline. P. azurescens has a strong affinity for dune grasses, especially
Ammophila maritima, with which it is closely associated. Forming an extensive,
dense, and tenacious mycelial mat, P. azurescens causes the whitening of wood.

Fruitings begin in late September and continue well after the first frost,
often into late December and early January. An adaptive species, outdoor beds
have been established with ease in the United States, particularly in coastal
regions of the Pacific Northwest. The species has also been successfully
cultivated in other temperate regions including parts of Europe.

## Potency

Containing up to 1.78% psilocybin, .38% psilocin, and .35% baeocystin
(Stamets and Gartz 1995), P. azurescens is one of the most potent species
in the world. After six months of storage, analyses revealed that this species
retained most of its original potency — a notable feature shared with
P. semilanceata.

This species is unique not only in its potency but also in its relatively high
baeocystin content, roughly on par with [[stamets-mushrooms-world-semilanceata]]. The flesh actually becomes indigo-black where
damaged, and it exhibits one of the strongest bluing reactions Stamets has
seen in any Psilocybe.

## Distinguishing Features

The silky white stem, caramel-colored cap, relatively large stature,
nonundulating cap margin, and broad but pronounced umbo are representative
features. [[stamets-mushrooms-world-cyanescens]] is
similar to P. azurescens but can be macroscopically distinguished by its much
smaller stature and characteristically sine-wave (wavy) cap margin.

## Cultivation and Spread

P. azurescens can be established in outdoor beds with relative ease, making it
one of the more accessible potent species for dedicated cultivators. Its
tolerance for cold temperatures and frost sets it apart from most other
Psilocybe species, which typically require milder conditions.

## Related Species
