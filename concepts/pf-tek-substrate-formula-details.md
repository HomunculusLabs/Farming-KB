# PF Tek Substrate Formula Details

The Psylocybe Fanaticus Tek (PF TEK) revolutionized amateur mushroom
cultivation with its simple, reliable substrate formula. This page
covers the exact ratios, ingredient specifications, and rationale
behind the PF substrate.

## The Basic Formula

The standard PF TEK substrate formula per jar:

| Ingredient | Amount | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Brown rice flour (BRF) | 60 mL (¼ cup) | Nutrient source |
| Vermiculite | 120 mL (½ cup) | Water retention and aeration |
| Water | 60 mL (¼ cup) | Moisture for colonization |
| Dry vermiculite (top layer) | 15-20 mL | Contamination barrier |

This produces a 1:2:1 ratio of BRF to vermiculite to water by volume.
The formula fills a standard 240 mL (½ pint) wide-mouth canning jar
approximately two-thirds full, leaving headspace for air exchange.

## Brown Rice Flour Specifications

BRF quality significantly impacts results:

- **Source**: Commercially milled brown rice flour from health food
  stores or Asian markets. Some cultivators mill their own from
  whole brown rice using a coffee grinder for maximum freshness.
- **Fineness**: Finer flour colonizes faster because the mycelium
  can access nutrients more readily. Coarse or chunky flour slows
  colonization and may leave uncolonized pockets.
- **Freshness**: Stale flour may have reduced nutritional value.
  Store opened flour in the refrigerator or freezer.
- **Type**: Short-grain or medium-grain brown rice flour is
  preferred. Long-grain varieties work but may have slightly
  different nutritional profiles.
- **Avoid**: Do not use white rice flour (nutrients removed), rice
  bran alone (too fine, compacts), or instant rice products.

## Vermiculite Specifications

Vermiculite is the structural backbone of the PF substrate:

- **Grade**: Fine or medium-fine horticultural vermiculite. Coarse
  grades hold less water and provide less surface area for
  [[sterilization]]
- **Avoid** softened water (high sodium), mineral water with
  excessive calcium, or water with known contaminants
- **Temperature**: Room temperature or slightly warm water mixes
  more easily than cold water

## Mixing Procedure

The PF TEK mixing procedure, in Fanaticus's own words:

1. Measure 120 mL fine vermiculite into a mixing bowl
2. Add 60 mL water and mix thoroughly until the vermiculite is
   evenly moistened but not dripping
3. Add 60 mL brown rice flour and mix until evenly distributed
4. The final mixture should be moist but not wet — when squeezed
   gently, a few drops of water should appear but no streams
5. Load the mixture into jars, filling to approximately 60-70% of
   jar volume
6. Wipe the inside rim of each jar clean with a paper towel
7. Top with a 15-20 mL layer of dry vermiculite — this is the
   critical contamination barrier
8. Cover with foil before sterilization

## The Dry Vermiculite Barrier

The dry vermiculite top layer is a key innovation of the PF TEK:

- Creates a physical barrier between the sterile substrate and
  the inoculation point
- Any contaminant spores that land on the surface must penetrate
  the dry vermiculite to reach the moist, nutritious substrate
- The dry layer lacks moisture and nutrients, preventing most
  contaminant germination
- This barrier allows inoculation through the foil without a
  glovebox or flow hood in many cases
- The barrier should be loose, not packed, and level with or
  slightly below the jar rim

## Common Problems and Solutions

| Problem | Likely Cause | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Substrate too wet | Too much water | Add more vermiculite |
| Substrate too dry | Too little water | Add water sparingly |
| Slow colonization | Coarse BRF or vermiculite | Use finer ingredients |
| Contamination | Barrier too thin | Increase dry layer depth |
| Compacted substrate | Packed too firmly | Load loosely, tap to settle |

## Scaling the Formula

For larger jars or bulk preparation:

- Maintain the 1:2:1 BRF:vermiculite:water ratio by volume
- A standard 1-pound bag of BRF makes approximately 10 half-pint
  jars
- Large bags of vermiculite are far more economical per jar
- Pre-mix dry ingredients in bulk, then add water per batch to
  avoid moisture loss
