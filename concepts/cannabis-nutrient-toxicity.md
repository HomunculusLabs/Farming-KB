# Cannabis Nutrient Toxicity

## Overview

Nutrient toxicity occurs when any essential element is present in excess, causing [[cannabis-growth-stage-checklist]]. Toxic conditions can be as damaging as deficiencies and are often more difficult to diagnose because excess of one nutrient typically causes a deficiency of another. Overfertilization is one of the biggest problems for indoor growers.

## General Principles

- Toxic levels of nutrients change the soil chemistry
- Growth is rapid and lush until toxic levels are reached
- Chance of overfertilization is greater in small containers with limited soil
- Micronutrients can easily reach toxic levels despite being needed only in minute amounts
- Always follow manufacturer instructions when applying micronutrients

## Macronutrient Toxicities

### Nitrogen Toxicity

- Excessively lush, soft foliage susceptible to insect and fungal attacks
- Weak stems that fold over easily
- Vascular transport tissue breaks down; water uptake restricted
- Leaves turn brownish-copper, dry, and fall off
- Roots develop slowly, darken, and may rot
- Flowers are smaller and sparse
- Ammonium toxicity most common in acidic soils; nitrate toxicity in alkaline

### Phosphorus Toxicity

- May take several weeks to surface
- Many cannabis varieties tolerate high phosphorus levels
- Excess interferes with calcium, copper, iron, magnesium, and zinc uptake
- Manifests as deficiency of other nutrients, most commonly zinc

### Potassium Toxicity

- Difficult to diagnose; mixed with other [[knf-calcium-deficiency-symptoms]] of these nutrients

## Secondary Nutrient Toxicities

### Magnesium Toxicity

- Rare and difficult to discern with the naked eye
- Conflicts with other fertilizer ions, usually calcium
- More common in [[query-how-to-identify-and-fix-cannabis-iron-deficiency]] symptoms

### Copper Toxicity -- Extremely Dangerous

- Toxic even in minor excess
- Slows overall plant growth
- Causes interveinal iron chlorosis and stunted growth
- Fewer branches; roots become dark, thick, and slow growing
- Toxic conditions accelerate quickly in acidic soils
