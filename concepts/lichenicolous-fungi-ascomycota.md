# Lichenicolous Fungi of the Ascomycota

## Definition

**[[lichenicolous-fungi]]** (also called lichen-inhabiting or lichenicolous ascomycetes) are fungi that live **on or within lichens**, using the lichen thallus or its constituent mycobiont and photobiont as substrate. They form a diverse ecological guild that includes obligate parasites, facultative parasites, commensals, and saprotrophs. Unlike lichenized fungi, lichenicolous species do not form their own functional thallus with a photobiont partner, though some may retain the ancestral capacity for lichenization.

The term encompasses both sexually reproducing (teleomorphic) ascomycetes and their asexual (anamorphic) counterparts. Lichenicolous fungi are found across numerous orders of the [[ascomycota]] and represent one of the most poorly known yet ecologically significant components of fungal biodiversity.

## Survey by Order

### Dothideales / Pleosporales

This group contains some of the most diverse lichenicolous assemblages.

**Tubeufiaceae** — Primarily fungicolous rather than strictly lichenicolous, with taxa recorded on:
- Meliolaceous leaf-inhabiting ascomycetes (18 spp)
- Phyllachoraceous leaf-inhabiting ascomycetes (9 spp)
- [[rust-fungi]] (3 spp)
- Decaying wood (20 spp)

**Dacampiaceae** — Exclusively lichenicolous genera with substantial species diversity:

| Genus | Species Count |
|-------|--------------|
| *Polycoccum* | ~30 |
| *Pyrenidium* | ~15 |
| *Weddellomyces* | 8 |
| *Clypeococcum* | 6 |
| *Dacampia* | 3 |
| *Kalaallia* | 1 |

**Microthyriaceae** — *Lichenopeltella* species colonize macrolichens.

Additional important lichenicolous genera within Dothideales/Pleosporales include *Buelliella*, *Cercidospora*, *Didymellopsis*, *Endococcus*, *Lichenostigma*, *Sagediopsis*, *Sphaerellothecium*, and *Zwackhiomyces*.

### Arthoniales

Arthonialean fungi are among the most conspicuous lichenicolous organisms, with:
- **~45 lichenicolous *Arthonia* species**
- **35 lichenicolous *Opegrapha* species**
- **23 lichenicolous *Plectocarpon* species**
- **3 lichenicolous *Lecanographa* species**

Many of these produce distinctive fruiting bodies (ascomata or pycnidia) that are readily visible on host lichen thalli, often causing characteristic discoloration, galls, or deformation.

### Caliciales

Species in Caliciales exhibit remarkable ecological plasticity — individual taxa can be lichenized, lichenicolous, fungicolous, or saprotrophic. Key lichenicolous genera:

- ***Chaenothecopsis*** — High proportion of lichenicolous species
- ***Microcalicium*** — High proportion of lichenicolous species; *M. arenarium* associates with *Psilolechia* or *Stichococcus*
- ***Sphinctrina*** — High proportion of lichenicolous species

The ecological versatility within this order complicates delimitation of strictly lichenicolous lineages and suggests multiple evolutionary transitions between nutritional modes.

### Lecanorales

Several lecideoid and other genera within Lecanorales include lichenicolous representatives:
- ***Toninia*** — Parasitic on other lichens
- ***Buellia***, ***Carbonea***, ***Catillaria*** — Occasional lichenicolous taxa
- ***Rhizocarpon***, ***Tephromela***, ***Thelocarpon*** — Scattered lichenicolous species
- ***Phacopsis*** — **14 species exclusively lichenicolous on Parmeliaceae**, representing one of the most host-restricted lichenicolous genera known

### Ostropales

- ***Odontotrema*** (including *Lethariicola*) — 15 lichenicolous species
- ***Nanostictis*** — 3 described + 2 undescribed lichenicolous species

### Verrucariales

- ***Verrucaria*** — Includes species with parasitic (lichenicolous) lifestyles
- Non-lichenized lichenicolous genera:

| Genus | Species Count |
|-------|--------------|
| *Stigmidium* | ~70 |
| *Muellerella* | 12 |
| *Merismatium* | 9 |
| *Adelococcus* | 3 |
| *Norrlinia* | 2 |

*Stigmidium* is the largest exclusively lichenicolous genus in Verrucariales and one of the most species-rich lichenicolous genera overall.

### Genera Incertae Sedis

- ***Abrothallus*** — 20+ species, predominantly on Parmeliaceae macrolichens; one of the most familiar and frequently collected lichenicolous genera
- ***Dactylospora*** — 30 lichenicolous species

## Anamorphic (Conidial) Lichenicolous Fungi

Over **100 lichenicolous conidial fungi** are currently known, though this number is considered a significant underestimate given the scarcity of focused surveys.

A key anamorph-teleomorph connection has been established:
- ***Acremonium* sect. *Lichenoidea*** — 9 lichenicolous species recognized as the **anamorphic (asexual) states of *Pronectria***, linking hyphomycete morphology to teleomorph taxonomy

These connections are critical for understanding life cycles and completing species concepts, but many anamorphs remain unlinked to their sexual morphs.

## Host Specificity Patterns

Lichenicolous fungi display a broad spectrum of host specificity:

1. **Oligophagous / specialist** — Restricted to a single host genus or family. *Phacopsis* (14 spp on Parmeliaceae) and *Abrothallus* (20+ spp on Parmeliaceae) are exemplars, showing co-radiation with their foliose macrolichen hosts.

2. **Polyphagous / generalist** — Found on multiple unrelated lichen hosts across different families.

3. **Substrate-specific but not host-specific** — Some taxa colonize specific lichen growth forms (e.g., crustose vs. foliose) or thallus microhabitats (e.g., apothecia, soralia, cephalodia) rather than particular host species.

Host specificity data remain fragmentary for most genera, complicating both biodiversity estimation and conservation assessments.

## Parasitic vs. Saprotrophic Lifestyles

Lichenicolous fungi occupy a continuum of interactions with their host lichens:

- **Obligate biotrophic parasites** — Derive nutrition exclusively from living lichen tissue; may cause chlorosis, necrosis, or galling. Many Arthoniales and some Verrucariales representatives fall here.
- **Facultative parasites** — Can switch between parasitic and saprotrophic modes depending on host condition.
- **Necrotrophic parasites** — Kill host tissue ahead of colonization.
- **Commensals / epiphytes** — Occupy the lichen surface without apparent harm.
- **Saprotrophs on dead lichen thalli** — Decompose senescent lichen material, particularly on decaying wood-inhabiting lichens.

The Caliciales illustrate this continuum especially well, with individual genera containing species spanning multiple nutritional strategies.

## Biodiversity Estimates

Known lichenicolous fungal diversity totals **several hundred described species** distributed across all major ascomycete orders that include lichenized members. However, these numbers represent a fraction of the probable total:

- Lichenicolous fungi are routinely undercollected and underreported
- Many tropical and Southern Hemisphere lichen floras remain essentially unsurveyed for lichenicolous associates
- Molecular phylogenetic studies consistently reveal cryptic species complexes
- Anamorphic diversity is particularly undersampled, with the 100+ known conidial species likely representing a small proportion of the actual diversity

Conservative estimates suggest that true global diversity of lichenicolous fungi could exceed **1,000–2,000 species**, with many awaiting discovery and description.

## Ecological Significance

Lichenicolous fungi are ecologically important for several reasons:

- **Regulation of lichen communities** — Parasitic species can significantly reduce host lichen fitness, abundance, and competitive ability, shaping lichen community composition.
- **Bioindicators** — The presence and diversity of lichenicolous fungi may serve as indicators of lichen community health, air quality, and habitat continuity.
- **Drivers of lichen evolution** — Host-parasite coevolutionary dynamics may promote diversification in both lichenized and lichenicolous lineages.
- **Nutrient cycling** — Saprotrophic lichenicolous fungi contribute to decomposition of lichen biomass in forest and tundra ecosystems.
- **Conservation relevance** — Specialized lichenicolous fungi face elevated extinction risk when their host lichens decline, necessitating their inclusion in conservation assessments.
