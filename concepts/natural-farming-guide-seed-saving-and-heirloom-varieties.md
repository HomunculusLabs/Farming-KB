# Seed Saving and Heirloom Varieties

Seed saving is a critical skill for self-reliant farming and a core practice in permaculture. It preserves [[biodiversity-fungal-genetic-diversity]], reduces dependence on commercial seed suppliers, and allows farmers to select for traits adapted to their specific conditions. Seed sharing is identified as one of the foundational activities of permaculture culture, alongside organic agriculture, [[comparison-pruning-fruit-trees-vs-grafting-fruit-trees]] are more likely to be disease-resistant. Seed saving from hybrids is explicitly cautioned against: "You can save seed from your second crop of grain (unless you're using a hybrid)." [[cannabis-seed-selection-and-strain-types]] and storage:

1. Save seed from your second crop of grain (not the first, and not from hybrids)
2. Select the best seed heads: unbroken and healthy specimens only
3. Let seed dry in the shock (stook) for at least one month until totally dry
4. Thresh and remove all leaves and twigs to ensure clean seed
5. Store in rodent-proof containers in the house (not the shed), with cats to deter mice
6. Add bay leaves to stored grain to help deter pests
7. Keep containers in a cool, dry place
8. Check periodically for mold, bugs, or signs of rodents
9. Once grain is ground, it must be used immediately (it does not store after milling)

### Protecting Stored Seed from Insect Pests

Two methods for dealing with bugs in stored grain:

Dry ice method: Use 1 tablespoon of dry ice per 5 gallons of grain. Place in an airtight container, put the ice on the bottom, pour the grain on top, wait one hour, then seal. The carbon dioxide produced kills the bugs without affecting the grain.

Heat method: For edible grain only (not seed grain, as heat kills viability). Spread a quarter-inch layer on a pan and heat in the oven at 140 degrees F (60 degrees C) for 30 minutes.
