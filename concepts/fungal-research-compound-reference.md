## Fungal Research Compound Reference

Research summary of psilocybin-related fungal biology compiled from published papers. See [[mushroom-growing-reference]] for cultivation applications and [[psychedelic-compounds-reference]] for pharmacology.

## Metabolic Pathway of Psilocybin Biosynthesis

Psilocybin is produced via the Shikimate pathway in fungal cytosol:

1. **Shikimate pathway**: Phosphoenolpyruvate + eryrose-4-P -> chorismate (via 7 enzymatic steps)
2. **[[tryptophan]] branch**: Chorismate -> anthranilate -> tryptophan (via 6 steps)
3. **[[decarboxylation]]**: Tryptophan -> tryptamine (via [[tryptophan-decarboxylase]] — this step is self-regulated)
4. **Hydroxylation + methylation**: Tryptamine -> psilocin -> psilocybin (exact intermediates still unclear; baeocystin and norbaeocystin are produced as phosphorylated intermediates)

### Tryptophan Decarboxylase Inhibition

| Inhibitor | Type | % Inhibition |
|-----------|------|-------------|
| N,N-dimethyltryptamine | Competitive | 65% |
| Tryptamine | Unknown mechanism | 62% |
| Indole-3-acetic acid | Competitive | 60% |
| Indole-3-acetaldehyde | Unknown mechanism | 50% |
| 5-Hydroxytryptamine | Unknown mechanism | 45% |
| 5-Methoxy-DMT | Non-inhibitor | 0% |
| 5-Methoxytryptamine | Non-inhibitor | 0% |

## Tryptamine Biotransformation (Gartz 1989)

Adding tryptamine HCl (25mM) to P. cubensis substrate dramatically alters alkaloid profile:

| Flush | Psilocin (with Trp) | Psilocin (control) | Psilocybin (with Trp) | Psilocybin (control) |
|-------|--------------------|--------------------|-----------------------|---------------------|
| 1 | 2.1% | 0% | 0.01% | 0.55% |
| 2 | 3.3% | 0.01% | 0.02% | 0.48% |
| 3 | 2.8% | 0.02% | 0.2% | 0.51% |
| 4 | 3.1% | 0.09% | 0.07% | 0.46% |
| 5 | 2.9% | 0.15% | 0.13% | 0.61% |

Key findings: Fed tryptamine produced up to 3.3% psilocin (highest ever reported in mushrooms), while psilocybin dropped to near zero. This suggests the phosphorylation step (psilocin -> psilocybin) is downregulated by excess tryptamine.

## Psilocybin/Psilocin Variation by Flush (Bigwood & Beug 1982)

Amazon strain of P. cubensis on rye grain (mg/g dry weight):

| Flush | Miniculture 1 Psilocybin | Psilocin | Miniculture 2 Psilocybin | Psilocin | Miniculture 3 Psilocybin | Psilocin |
|-------|------------------------|----------|------------------------|----------|------------------------|----------|
| 1 | 8.3 | 0.5 | 5.1 | 0 | 7.6 | 0 |
| 2 | 6.5 | 1.5 | 7.0 | 0 | 6.2 | 0 |
| 3 | 13.3 | 1.0 | 4.7 | 1.7 | 5.3 | 0.9 |
| 4 | 4.8 | 2.6 | 3.7 | 2.9 | 3.2 | 1.8 |
| 5 | 5.2 | 2.2 | 5.2 | 2.2 | 6.7 | 1.7 |

Key findings: Psilocin absent in first 1-2 flushes, peaks at flush 4. Psilocybin varies unpredictably (factor of 4) with no trend. Caps contain ~2x psilocybin of stems.

## P. azurescens Alkaloid Profile (Gartz 1995)

Highest known alkaloid concentrations of any [[psilocybin-mushroom]]:

| Sample | Psilocybin (%) | Psilocin (%) | Baeocystin (%) |
|--------|---------------|-------------|----------------|
| 1 | 1.40 | 0.31 | 0.28 |
| 2 | 1.56 | 0.30 | 0.32 |
| 3 | 1.68 | 0.28 | 0.38 |
| 4 | 1.71 | 0.34 | 0.41 |

Total alkaloids: up to ~2.5% dry weight (25 mg/g).

## Brassinosteroid Growth Promotion (Gartz, Adam & Vorbrodt 1990)

22S,23S-homobrassinolide at 0.01 ppm in P. cubensis culture:

| Parameter | Control | With Brassinosteroid |
|-----------|---------|---------------------|
| Mycelial growth rate | Baseline | 2-3x faster |
| Time to first flush | 4-5 weeks | 3-3.5 weeks |
