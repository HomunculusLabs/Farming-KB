## Insect Pests

### Mushroom Gnats

Several species of small flies, including representatives of the genus
Sciara, commonly infest mushroom houses. The mushroom is injured by
these gnats in the larval or maggot stage. The insects may appear in
enormous quantity following the preparation of the beds, when the
temperature is on the decline. They multiply with great rapidity during
any period when the temperature is high enough, depositing their eggs
at the base of the mushroom stem. From this point the larvae may pass
up the stem and ultimately riddle the cap.

In properly regulated cellars this condition is most infrequent, since
these insects are practically inactive at temperatures below 55 degrees
F. The adult flies are readily killed by fumigation with tobacco or
nicotine fumigants. Carbon bisulphide (2 to 4 pounds per 1,000 cubic
feet) is also effective but expensive. Sulphur or hydrocyanic acid
fumigation may be used before spawning.
