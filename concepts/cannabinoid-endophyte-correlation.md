## Overview

Research on the Cannabis microbiome has revealed significant correlations between cannabinoid concentrations in flowering buds and the structure of bacterial communities in the root endorhiza. Winston et al. (2014) found that both cannabinoid composition and soil edaphic variables independently correlated with endorhiza community structure.

## Cultivar as an Integrating Variable

Cannabis cultivar represents the integrated effect of host genetics on both cannabinoid production and root microbiome selection. A cultivar that produces high THC may simultaneously produce root exudates and root-tissue cannabinoids that favor specific bacterial communities.

The two-tier selection model predicts that after initial soil-driven community assembly in the rhizosphere, host genotype determines the final endorhiza community structure. Cultivar identity encompasses both the genetic capacity for cannabinoid biosynthesis and the genetic determinants of root-microbe signaling.

This makes it challenging to separate cannabinoid effects from broader host genetic effects on microbiome assembly. The correlation observed by Winston et al. likely reflects this integrated genotype effect rather than a direct causal influence of any single cannabinoid.

## Antimicrobial Properties of Cannabinoids

Cannabinoids possess documented antimicrobial activity against both Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria. THC and CBD have shown bacteriostatic and bactericidal effects in controlled laboratory assays.

These antimicrobial properties could act as a selective filter in the endorhiza environment. Bacteria colonizing root tissue may need tolerance to cannabinoid compounds, effectively screening out sensitive taxa while allowing resistant ones to persist.

The degree of this selective pressure would depend on local cannabinoid concentrations in root tissue. If concentrations vary between cultivars, the resulting selective landscapes would differ, contributing to cultivar-specific microbiome assembly.
