## Clearing Uncultivated Ground

When starting a new garden or incorporating a new area, the first stage
is to make the ground as clean as possible. Begin by digging over the
whole site and removing as much weed root as possible. Annual weeds can
go on the compost heap provided they have not been allowed to seed.
Perennial weed roots (dandelions, docks, bindweed, couch grass) should
be burned or thrown away -- never put them on the compost heap.

## Planting a Cleaning Crop

After digging, plant a "cleaning crop" to further clean the soil.
Potatoes are the best choice for this purpose. They help clean soil of
weeds in two ways: their cultivation requires turning the soil three
times per year (planting, earthing up, and harvest), and they grow a
dense canopy of leaves that excludes light from any weeds attempting to
compete. The combination of light starvation and soil disturbance
encourages most weeds to give up.

Some weeds survive even this treatment. Bindweed climbs through the
potato canopy to reach sunlight and maintains an extensive root system.
In the second year these persistent weeds will still need attention, but
on a small scale they are not difficult to overcome.

## Hoeing

The hoe is the most effective tool in your armory and should be used
regularly, preferably during dry weather. By pulling it through the top
layer of soil you uproot weeds before they establish.

### Dutch Hoe
Best for weeding. Use walking backward to avoid treading on uprooted
weeds. Walking forward pushes them back into the soil and effectively
transplants them. Keep the blade no more than half an inch below the
soil surface to avoid damaging shallow crop roots.

### Wheel Hoe
A hand-pushed tool with a single wheel and cutting blade. Much quicker
than a Dutch hoe once the soil is loosened. Adjust [[fukuoka-dutch-priest-vegetable-garden-spiritual-renewal]]. Can be covered with gravel or soil for

### Paper and Biodegradable Materials
Tough brown paper is effective as a biodegradable mulch. Lay it several
sheets thick and cover with a layer of organic material to hold it down
and improve appearance.
