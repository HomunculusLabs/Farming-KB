# Living Plant Systems and Insectary Gardens

Toby Hemenway's guide to creating gardens that support [[query-how-to-build-healthy-garden-soil]] depends on a complex web of relationships between plants, insects, birds, and other creatures. Without pollinators, many crops fail. Without predators, pest populations explode. The ecological garden intentionally creates habitat for these essential partners.

## The Insectary Garden

An insectary is a garden specifically designed to attract and sustain beneficial insects. It can be a dedicated bed, a border planting, or integrated throughout the garden.

### Plant Categories for Insectaries

1. **Nectar plants** — provide food for adult beneficial insects
2. **Pollen plants** — essential protein source for many predators
3. **Host plants** — plants where insects lay eggs and larvae develop
4. **Shelter plants** — provide overwintering habitat and protection from weather

### Key Insectary Plants

Many of the best insectary plants are common herbs and flowers:

- **Umbellifers** (dill, fennel, cilantro/coriander, parsley, Queen Anne's lace, yarrow): Small, shallow flowers perfect for tiny parasitic wasps and hover flies
- **Composite flowers** (cosmos, sunflowers, asters, zinnias, marigolds): Provide landing platforms and accessible nectar
- **Mint family** (lavender, rosemary, thyme, oregano, bee balm, catnip): Long-blooming, attractive to bees and other pollinators
- **Legumes** (clover, vetch, peas, beans): Attract predatory wasps and provide [[savory-growing-season-and-non-growing-season-management]]. Plan for:

- **Early spring:** Crocus, snowdrops, early-blooming bulbs, winter-blooming heathers
- **Spring:** Clover, phacelia, alyssum, candytuft, early-blooming herbs
- **Summer:** Dill, fennel, yarrow, cosmos, zinnias, buckwheat, sunflowers
- **Late summer/fall:** Asters, goldenrod, sedum, late-blooming herbs, chrysanthemums

## Butterfly Gardens

Butterfly gardens require specific host plants for caterpillars:

- **Monarch:** Milkweed (Asclepias species)
- **Swallowtail:** Dill, fennel, parsley, carrot family plants
- **Painted lady:** Thistle, hollyhock, mallow
- **Sulfur:** Clover, alfalfa

Adult butterflies need nectar from flat-topped or tubular flowers in bright colors (purple, red, orange, pink).

## Food Hedges and Hedgerows

Hedgerows are linear plantings of shrubs, trees, and herbs that serve multiple functions:

- **Wind protection** for gardens and livestock
- **Wildlife corridors** connecting habitat patches
- **Berry and nut production** for humans and wildlife
- **Insectary function** when including flowering shrubs
- **Privacy screening** and boundary definition
