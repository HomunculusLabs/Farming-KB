# Biblical Sacrament and the Living Waters

## Overview

A central argument in *[[mushrooms-and-mankind]]* is that the sacramental substances described throughout the Bible — including manna, the flesh and blood of Christ, the hidden manna, the tree of life, and the fountain of living waters — all refer to a single entheogenic substance, most likely the **[[amanita-muscaria]]** mushroom. This interpretation challenges orthodox Christian theology by arguing that the original sacrament was a physically active psychoactive plant, later replaced by symbolic substitutes that lack the transformative properties described in the texts.

## Manna as Mushroom

In ancient Hebrew, the word **manna** literally means "mushroom." The [[biblical-manna-and-amanita-muscaria]] — the food provided by God to the Israelites during their desert wanderings — is reinterpreted in this framework as an entheogenic substance. Key passages from Revelation reinforce this identification:

- **Revelation 2:7**: "To him that overcometh will I give to eat of the tree of life, which is in the midst of the paradise of God."
- **Revelation 2:17**: "To him that overcometh will I give to eat of the hidden manna, and will give him a white stone, and in the stone a new name written."

The mushroom is described as simultaneously being the fruit of the tree of life, the hidden manna, the white stone, the comforter, and the "door" — all referring to the same entheogenic substance through different metaphorical frameworks.

## The Flesh and Blood of Christ

The Gospel of John contains what are perhaps the most direct statements about the physical nature of the sacrament:

- **John 6:53**: "Except ye eat the flesh of the Son of man, and drink his blood, ye have no life in you."
- **John 6:54**: "Whoso eateth my flesh, and drinketh my blood, hath eternal life; and I will raise him up at the last day."
- **John 6:55**: "For my flesh is meat indeed, and my blood is drink indeed."
- **John 6:56**: "He that eateth my flesh, and drinketh my blood, dwelleth in me, and I in him."

The phrase "flesh indeed" and "drink indeed," combined with the statement that the substance enters inside the body, is argued to leave little room for purely symbolic interpretation. The text emphasizes this is not a reference to cannibalism but to the ingestion of a specific psychoactive substance that produces the described spiritual effects.

## The Fountain of Living Waters

The biblical God declares "I am the Fountain of Living Waters." When understood literally, this description — alive, made of water, in the similitude of a fountain — is argued to describe a mushroom. The "Waters of Life" is an ancient term connected to:

- **Soma** (Vedic tradition): The elixir of the gods conferring enlightenment
- **Amrta** (Hindu/Buddhist tradition): The nectar of immortality
- **The churning of the Milky Ocean**: A Hindu myth describing the production of this substance
- **The blood of the god**: In sacramental analysis, synonymous with the living waters

Jesus is identified as the "revealer of the Fountain of Living Waters," with Revelation 21:6 promising to give the living waters to those who are athirst. All of these terms — living waters, blood, manna, flesh — are argued to represent "the one and same thing represented in different ways": a phenomenal substance producing multi-dimensionality, spiritual learning, and communion with the divine.

## The Broken Cistern: Jeremiah's Prophecy

The Book of Jeremiah contains a powerful metaphor for the replacement of the original sacrament:

- **Jeremiah 2:13**: "For my people have committed two evils; they have forsaken me the fountain of living waters, and hewed them out cisterns, broken cisterns, that can hold no water."

The "broken cisterns" are interpreted as the symbolic substitutes — bread and wine — that replaced the original [[biblical-entheogenic-sacrament]]. The argument proceeds: if a light bulb does not work, it is broken; if a substance that is supposed to produce specific spiritual effects does not produce them, it is "broken and actually worthless." This is characterized as a "forsaking" of the true fountain — a loss of genuine communion with the divine.

## [[transubstantiation]] and the Placebo Problem

The Catholic doctrine of **transubstantiation** — the magical transformation of bread and wine into the body and blood of Christ — is challenged on practical grounds. If Jesus stated that "unless you eat and drink, you have no life in you," then replacing the active substance with a placebo would seem to condemn the practice. The text argues that those who convince themselves they feel something after eating priest-blessed bread may be "creating an illusion that is eventually something that is damaging to them beyond comprehension."

## The Mushroom Cap Aura in Religious Art

A recurring motif in religious artwork shows figures of spiritual attainment with a mushroom-shaped form behind their heads. This is commonly interpreted as a "glowing aura" but may represent:

- The underside of a mushroom cap with gills extending outward
- A symbol distinguishing those who have "tasted of the heavenly gift" from those who have not
- Sometimes depicted in red with white spots, directly referencing [[allegro-colour-and-consistency-of-the-amanita-muscaria]]

The visual difference between "knowers" and "non-knowers" in religious art is marked by the presence or absence of this mushroom-cap halo, suggesting the artists understood the distinction between symbolic and actual spiritual experience.

## Two Baptisms: Water and Fire

Christian theology recognizes two baptisms:

1. **Baptism by water**: The traditional purification ritual
2. **Baptism by fire**: The transformational baptism — death, journey to the heavens, and rebirth

The mushroom has a long association with fire, partly due to its red coloration. Moses conversed with God in the "Burning Bush" on the mountaintop — a location where mushrooms naturally grow at high elevations. The baptism by fire is interpreted as the [[pineal-gland-dmt-entheogenic-experience]] that produces genuine spiritual transformation, as opposed to the purely symbolic water baptism.

## Levels of Understanding

The text describes a spectrum of public awareness regarding entheogenic sacraments:

- **Complete ignorance**: Most people have never encountered this information, and their paradigm of thought may not allow consideration of it.
- **Passing interest**: Some find it interesting but never pursue it further.
- **Personal research**: A smaller group finds it familiar and conducts independent research.
- **Academic familiarity**: Many mycologists and scholars have encountered the theory but never personally experienced the mushrooms.
- **Partial experience**: Some have eaten the mushrooms but, unfamiliar with dosage levels, experienced only minimal effects and abandoned the practice.
- **Full experience**: The rarest level — those who have experienced the full spectrum of entheogenic effects described in religious texts.

## The Revelation Passage on the Living Waters

In Revelation 7, even after physical death, the multitudes before the throne of God are led to "living fountains of waters" by the Lamb. This is significant because it suggests that even in the spiritual realm, the entheogenic substance retains its importance — it is not merely a physical tool but has significance at every level of existence. The scene also describes a "great healing," connecting the living waters to restoration and spiritual wholeness.

## Implications for Religious Practice

The entheogenic interpretation of biblical sacraments raises fundamental questions about the validity of modern religious practice. If the original sacramental substances produced genuine transformative experiences, and these were replaced with inert symbols, then centuries of religious practice may have been built on a foundation that "does not profit." The urgency conveyed in the biblical texts about eating and drinking the sacred substances suggests the authors considered this matter of extreme spiritual importance — not a matter of metaphorical interpretation but of literal physical ingestion with concrete spiritual consequences.
