# Maitake Growth Parameter Management

Comprehensive management of four key growth parameters—temperature, relative humidity,
light, and ventilation (O₂ vs CO₂)—is critical for successful commercial cultivation of
*Grifola frondosa* (maitake). This page summarizes parameter recommendations across the
three major growth stages as practiced in China, Japan, and North America, based on
Alice W. Chen's 1999 practical guide to synthetic-log cultivation.

## Overview of Growth Parameters

- **Temperature** — Fruiting body development requires a distinctly lower temperature
  range than the spawn run. Temperature fluctuation should be minimized to avoid
  condensation buildup on substrate surfaces and bags, which can promote contamination
  and impair primordia formation. Gradual transitions between stages are preferred.

- **Relative Humidity (RH)** — Must be progressively increased from spawn run through
  fruiting to support tissue development and prevent drying of delicate primordia.
  Overly high humidity during spawn run can encourage bacterial contamination, while
  insufficient humidity during fruiting causes cracking and reduced yield.

- **Light** — Continuous low-level illumination (50 lux) during spawn run helps maintain
  circadian rhythm; intensity increases at primordia initiation and fruiting to guide
  morphological development. Light directionality influences fruiting body orientation.

- **Ventilation (CO₂ Control)** — Mycelia tolerate higher CO₂ during spawn run, but
  primordia differentiation and fruiting require progressively lower CO₂ levels.
  Elevated CO₂ (>0.15%) during fruiting produces small, thin caps with poor market
  quality. Proper air exchange also helps regulate temperature and humidity.

## Cultivation Facility Design

In China and Japan, spawn run (combined with primordia initiation) and fruiting body
development are conducted in **separate rooms**, each optimized for the distinct
environmental requirements of those stages. This separation allows precise control
without compromise between conflicting stage requirements.

Primordia initially form inside **enclosed bags**, while fruiting bodies develop with the
bag top opened. After 2–3 days of adjustment to the fruiting environment, cotton plugs
are removed, the bag top is opened, or slits are cut to provide oxygen access. This
gradual introduction of fresh air prevents shock to developing primordia. Some growers
also punch holes in the bottom of bags for drainage of excess moisture, which can
accumulate during high-humidity fruiting conditions.

## Stage 1: Spawn Run

The spawn run is the longest stage, during which mycelium fully colonizes the synthetic
log substrate. Bags remain sealed throughout to maintain high CO₂ and protect against
contamination.

| Parameter | China | Japan | North America |
|-----------|-------|-------|---------------|
| Temperature | 20–25°C (~25°C typically used) | 22–23°C air (≈24–25°C in substrate) | Similar to China/Japan |
| Relative Humidity | 60–70% | 70% air; 60–80% at bag surface | Similar to China/Japan |
| Light | 50 lux throughout | Not applied / minimal | Variable |
| CO₂ | Mycelia tolerate higher; ventilate 5–6×/day | <0.3% (3000 ppm) | Moderate tolerance |
| Duration | 30–40 days | 30–35 days | 30–40 days |
| pH | 5.5–6.5 | Not specified | 5.5–6.5 |
| Spacing | — | Bags 3–4 cm apart | Variable |

During spawn run, higher CO₂ is tolerated and bags remain sealed. In Japan, precise
substrate temperature is monitored (≈2°C above ambient due to metabolic heat). Ventilation
in China is managed by opening doors or vents 5–6 times per day, balancing CO₂
dissipation with humidity retention.

## Stage 2: Primordia Initiation

Primordia initiation is a critical transition stage requiring careful adjustment of
temperature and humidity. This stage may occur in the same room as spawn run or in a
dedicated initiation room.

| Parameter | China | Japan |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| Temperature | 18–22°C (22–25°C sometimes used) | 22–23°C (23°C optimal for differentiation) |
| Relative Humidity | 80–90% | Same as spawn run (70–80%) |
| Light | 200 lux | 50 lux |
| CO₂ | — | <0.3% (3000 ppm) |
| Duration | 15–20 days | 40 days (stage I) + 7 days (stage II) |

Japan divides this stage into two sub-stages: stage I (40 days, mycelial maturation and
initial primordia signaling within enclosed bags) and stage II (7 days, early primordia
emergence). China uses a shorter window with higher light intensity (200 lux) to
accelerate differentiation. Bags remain enclosed throughout to maintain the high-CO₂
environment that promotes primordia formation.

## Stage 3: Fruiting Body Development

This stage demands the narrowest environmental tolerances, especially for temperature
and CO₂. Small deviations can significantly impact yield and quality.

| Parameter | China | Japan |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| Temperature | 15–20°C (narrow range) | 16–18°C |
| Relative Humidity | 90–95% | 85–95% |
| Light | 200–500 lux (~300 lux typical) | 200–500 lux |
| CO₂ / O₂ | High O₂ required; ventilate 5–6×/day | 0.1% CO₂ (1000 ppm); >0.15% causes malformed caps |
| Duration | 14–21 days | 20–25 days |

Bags are opened after 2–3 days of acclimation to the fruiting room. CO₂ above 0.15%
(1500 ppm) in the Japanese system produces small, thin-capped fruiting bodies with
reduced commercial value. China's protocol emphasizes frequent ventilation (5–6 times
daily) to maintain high O₂ levels. Fruiting temperature is consistently lower than spawn
run temperatures across all regions.

## Soil Casing

Soil casing is reported to significantly increase yield. Recommended soil sources:

- **Humus-rich hardwood forest soil** — preferred for balanced mineral content
- **Garden soil** — widely available alternative
- **Rice paddy soil** — common in Asian cultivation regions

Optimal soil particle diameter is **0.5–0.8 cm**. Sandy soils and clay are not
beneficial. Soil must be treated (pasteurized or sterilized) before application.
Casing is applied after primordia initiation, before or during transition to the
fruiting room.

## Yield Benchmarks

- **China:** 150–300 g/bag without casing; 300–500 g/bag with soil casing (~2×
  improvement)
- **Japan:** ~3.5 months total production cycle from inoculation to harvest
- **North America:** Comparable to Chinese ranges with similar protocols

## Factors Affecting Parameter Interpretation

Published parameter ranges should be interpreted with awareness of variables that
influence actual optimal conditions:

- **Strain differences** — Genetic variation can shift optimal temperature and timing
  by several degrees or days
- **Substrate composition and size** — Sawdust type, supplement ratio, and bag
  dimensions affect internal temperature, moisture dynamics, and CO₂ retention
- **Bag design** — Filter patch type, bag thickness, and sealing method influence gas
  exchange rates and humidity equilibrium
- **Spawn quality and distribution** — Even colonization and vigorous spawn reduce
  vulnerability to parameter deviations
- **Log arrangement and spacing** — Bag spacing affects air circulation, temperature
