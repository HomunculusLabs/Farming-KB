# Fungal Production of Plant Growth Regulators

Fungi synthesize a diverse array of plant growth-regulating substances
including gibberellins, auxins, cytokinins, abscisic acid, and ethylene
precursors. These fungal-produced phytohormones influence plant growth,
development, and stress responses, contributing significantly to the beneficial
effects of mycorrhizal and endophytic fungal associations.

## Gibberellins from Fungi

The gibberellin story began with the discovery that Gibberella fujikuroi (now
reclassified as Fusarium fujikuroi) caused bakanae ("foolish seedling") disease
in rice through overproduction of gibberellins. This finding led to the
isolation and structural elucidation of gibberellic acid (GA3) and ultimately
to the recognition of gibberellins as a major class of plant hormones.

Fusarium species remain the most prolific fungal producers of gibberellins,
with documented production of over 100 GA intermediates and metabolites.
Industrial fermentation of Fusarium moniliforme produces GA3 commercially for
agricultural applications including fruit set enhancement, seedless grape
production, and malting barley modification.

Endophytic Fusarium species isolated from various plants produce
gibberellin-like substances that promote host plant growth. The gibberellin
biosynthetic pathway in fungi shares early steps with the plant pathway but
diverges in later stages, reflecting convergent evolution of hormone
production. Fungal GAs include bioactive forms (GA1, GA3, GA4, GA7) that are
perceived by plant GID1 receptors, triggering DELLA protein degradation and
growth-promoting gene expression.

## Fungal Auxin Production
