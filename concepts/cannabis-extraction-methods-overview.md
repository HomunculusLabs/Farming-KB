# Cannabis Extraction Methods Overview

Cannabis extraction concentrates the plant's therapeutic compounds (cannabinoids, terpenes, and other constituents) into more potent and versatile forms. Different extraction methods produce varying levels of purity, safety, and preservation of the plant's full spectrum of compounds. Understanding these methods is essential for patients and practitioners selecting appropriate medicinal preparations.

## Concentrated Cannabis Forms

### Kief
The collected resin crystals of mature cannabis flower buds in their loose form. Separation is typically accomplished using a sieve or ice water extraction. Kief contains a high concentration of trichomes (the resin glands producing cannabinoids and terpenes).

### Hashish
Pressed and heated kief. The application of heat and pressure transforms loose resin crystals into a cohesive, concentrated block. Hashish represents one of the oldest forms of concentrated cannabis.

## Extraction Methods

### 1. Mechanical/Solventless Extraction

**Dry Sifting (Sieving):**
- Uses mesh screens of varying micron sizes to separate trichomes from plant material
- No solvents required
- Produces kief of varying grades based on screen size
- Preserves full spectrum of cannabinoids and terpenes
- Lower yield but highest safety profile

**Ice Water Extraction (Bubble Hash):**
- Uses ice-cold water and agitation to separate trichomes
- Trichomes sink while plant material floats
- Multiple "micron bag" filtration stages produce different grades
- Produces high-quality, full-spectrum hashish
- Considered one of the safest extraction methods
- Water-soluble impurities are removed, leaving pure resin

### 2. Alcohol/Glycerin Extraction (Tinctures)

**Ethanol Extraction:**
- Uses food-grade ethanol as a solvent
- Effective at extracting both cannabinoids and terpenes
- Produces tinctures for oral or sublingual use ("green dragon")
- Can be performed at home safely (ethanol is food-safe)
- May extract chlorophyll (bitter taste), though cold extraction reduces this
- Tinctures allow precise dropper-based dosing

**Glycerin Extraction:**
- Uses vegetable glycerin as solvent
- Sweeter taste than alcohol-based tinctures
- Less efficient at extracting cannabinoids than ethanol
- Suitable for patients who avoid alcohol
- Produces preparations for oral or topical use

### 3. Hydrocarbon Extraction (Butane/Propane)

**Butane Extraction (BHO - Butane Hash Oil):**
- Uses butane as a solvent to dissolve cannabis constituents
- Produces the highest concentration of cannabinoids
- Creates various consistencies: shatter, wax, budder, crumble, oil
- **Significant safety risks** due to butane flammability
- Explosions have led to serious injuries and deaths
- Solvent residue may linger in concentrate (toxic concern)
- Professional closed-loop systems are safer than open-blasting
- Highly efficient but requires careful purging of residual solvent

**Propane Extraction:**
- Similar to butane but with different solubility properties
- Often combined with butane (PHO - propane hash oil)
- May extract a slightly different terpene profile

### 4. CO2 (Supercritical Carbon Dioxide) Extraction

**Supercritical CO2:**
- Uses carbon dioxide at supercritical state (between gas and liquid)
- Extremely safe - CO2 is non-toxic and non-flammable
- Produces very clean concentrates with no residual solvent
- Temperature and pressure can be precisely controlled
- Can target specific cannabinoids (tunable extraction)
- Expensive equipment limits accessibility
- Widely used in commercial/medical cannabis production
- Preserves terpenes better than many solvent methods

**Subcritical CO2:**
- Lower temperature and pressure than supercritical
- Gentler extraction that may preserve more delicate terpenes
- Lower yield but potentially fuller spectrum

### 5. Oil-Based Extraction (Infused Oils)

- Uses carrier oils (coconut, olive, hemp, almond) to extract cannabinoids
- Usually involves gentle heating (decarboxylation activates THC from THC-acid)
- Produces culinary and medicinal oils for ingestion
- Lower cannabinoid concentration than solvent-based methods
- Safe for home preparation
- Basis for edibles and capsules
- Virgin, organic coconut oil preferred for its health benefits

### 6. Rosin Press (Heat and Pressure)

- Uses heat and pressure to squeeze resin from cannabis flower or hash
- Completely solventless
- Produces high-quality, full-spectrum concentrate
- No risk of residual solvent contamination
- Accessible for home use with affordable equipment
- Growing popularity among medical patients

## Comparison of Extraction Methods

| Method | Safety | Purity | Full Spectrum | Cost | Accessibility |
|--------|--------|--------|--------------|------|---------------|
| Dry Sifting | Excellent | Moderate | Yes | Low | High |
| Ice Water | Excellent | High | Yes | Low | High |
| Ethanol Tincture | Good | Moderate-High | Mostly | Low | High |
| Hydrocarbon | Poor | Very High | Variable | Moderate | Low |
| CO2 | Excellent | Very High | Tunable | High | Low |
| Oil Infusion | Excellent | Low-Moderate | Mostly | Low | High |

## Pharmaceutical Cannabis Preparations

- **Dronabinol:** Synthetic THC (isomer of Delta-9-THC), available in capsule form
- **Nabilone:** Synthetic cannabinoid, primarily used as antiemetic
- **Sativex:** Standardized whole-plant cannabis extract (THC:CBD) delivered as oromucosal spray; essentially a plant-derived tincture

## Key Safety Note

Hydrocarbon extraction poses significant fire and explosion hazards. Professional-grade closed-loop systems with proper ventilation and safety equipment are essential. Patients should be aware that improperly purged concentrates may contain residual solvents. For medical use, CO2-extracted or solventless preparations generally offer the safest profiles.
