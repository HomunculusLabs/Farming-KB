## Cannabis Extraction Methods Overview
Cannabis extraction encompasses a diverse range of techniques for separating desirable compounds — cannabinoids, terpenes, and flavonoids — from plant material. Each method produces different types of extracts with distinct characteristics regarding potency, flavor, purity, safety, and intended use. The [[cannabis-extraction-basics]] page covers fundamental concepts; this page provides a comparative overview of all major extraction methods and their practical considerations.

## Mechanical Methods
Mechanical extraction uses physical force rather than chemical solvents, producing what are considered the most "natural" concentrates. **Dry sifting** passes cannabis over graduated screens to separate trichome heads (kief) from plant material. Screens range from 25-150 microns, with the finest screens collecting the highest-quality material. **Ice water extraction** (bubble hash) combines ice-cold water with mechanical agitation to make trichome heads brittle so they detach from the plant. The resulting slurry is filtered through a series of mesh bags (typically 220, 160, 120, 90, 73, and 25 micron) to collect hash of different grades. **Rosin pressing** applies heat (180-220°F) and pressure (500-2000 psi) to flower or hash, squeezing out a golden, solventless extract. Rosin has gained enormous popularity for its clean flavor and solvent-free processing.

## Solvent-Based Methods
Solvent extraction uses chemical solvents to dissolve cannabinoids and terpenes from plant material. **Ethanol extraction** is the industry standard for large-scale commercial production due to ethanol's safety profile and regulatory acceptance. **Butane/propane extraction (BHO/PHO)** produces high-potency extracts with excellent terpene preservation and is the preferred method for artisanal concentrates like live resin. **Supercritical CO2 extraction** offers tunable selectivity and produces clean extracts with no solvent residue, but requires expensive equipment. The [[cannabis-extraction-solvent-properties-and-safety]] page provides detailed solvent comparisons. Post-extraction processing varies by method but typically includes winterization (wax removal), decarboxylation (THCA to THC conversion), and distillation for purification.

## Comparison of Extract Types
The form of the final extract depends on the extraction method and post-processing: **Shatter** is a translucent, glass-like extract produced by butane extraction with careful purging; **Wax/Budder** has a softer, opaque consistency from whipping during purging; **Crumble** is a dry, crumbly texture from lower-temperature purging; **Live resin** is extracted from fresh-frozen plant material, preserving the maximum terpene content; **Distillate** is a highly refined, nearly pure cannabinoid product (95%+) with minimal flavor; **Rosin** is the premier solventless extract, available in press-from-flower and press-from-hash varieties. The [[cannabis-hashish-guide]] covers traditional hash-making alongside modern concentrate production.

## Quality Assessment
Extract quality is assessed through several metrics: potency (total cannabinoid percentage, THC:CBD ratio); terpene profile (which terpenes are present and in what proportions); purity (absence of residual solvents, pesticides, heavy metals); and sensory characteristics (flavor, aroma, appearance, consistency). Licensed testing laboratories analyze extracts using HPLC or GC for cannabinoid content and GC-MS for terpene and residual solvent analysis. The [[cannabis-testing-and-quality]] framework establishes standards that all commercial products must meet.

## Choosing an Extraction Method
The choice of extraction method depends on scale, budget, desired product type, and regulatory environment. Home cultivators typically use dry sifting, ice water extraction, or rosin pressing due to their safety and simplicity. Small commercial operations may use ethanol extraction for its versatility and safety. Large licensed operations often employ CO2 or ethanol extraction at scale. Artisanal producers favor hydrocarbon extraction for the superior flavor profiles it produces, especially for live resin. Each method has trade-offs between cost, safety, quality, throughput, and product characteristics that must be weighed against business goals and market demands.
