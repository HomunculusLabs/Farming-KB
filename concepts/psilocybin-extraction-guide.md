# Psilocybin Extraction Guide
## Overview
Psilocybin (4-PO-DMT) and psilocin (4-HO-DMT) are the primary psychoactive compounds found in "magic mushrooms" — over 200 species of fungi in the genus Psilocybe and related genera. Psilocybin is a prodrug — it is rapidly dephosphorylated in the body to psilocin, the active compound that acts on serotonin 5-HT2A receptors.
This guide covers methods for extracting and concentrating psilocybin from mushroom material, primarily using ethanol extraction. These methods are presented for educational and harm-reduction purposes. Psilocybin is a controlled substance in many jurisdictions — see [[psychedelic-plants-legal-status]].
## The Chemistry
### Psilocybin vs. Psilocin
- **Psilocybin (4-PO-DMT)**: Stable, water-soluble, not soluble in nonpolar solvents. The primary compound in dried mushrooms. Prodrug that converts to psilocin in the body.
- **Psilocin (4-HO-DMT)**: Unstable in air (oxidizes to blue quinone compounds, causing bruising), water-soluble. The actual active compound at receptors.
- Both are tryptamines, structurally similar to DMT and serotonin
### Solubility
- Both psilocybin and psilocin are soluble in water, ethanol, methanol, and other polar solvents
- Insoluble in nonpolar solvents (naphtha, hexane, etc.)
- This makes polar solvent extraction straightforward
## Mushroom Sources
### Common Cultivated Species
- **Psilocybe cubensis** — most common cultivated species, moderate potency (0.5–1.5% psilocybin+psilocin by dry weight)
- **Psilocybe cyanescens** ("wavy caps") — higher potency (1.0–2.5%), wood-loving species
- **Psilocybe azurescens** — among the most potent known (up to 3.0%), native to the Pacific Northwest coast
- **Psilocybe semilanceata** ("liberty caps") — widespread in temperate grasslands, moderate-high potency
### Potency Variation
Potency varies enormously based on:
- Species and strain
- Growing conditions (substrate, temperature, light)
- Harvest timing (before or after veil breaks)
- Drying method
- Storage conditions
## Method 1: Ethanol Extraction (Tincture)
The simplest extraction method using food-grade ethanol:
### Materials
- Dried mushroom material: 10–50 g
- High-proof ethanol (190 proof / 95%): enough to cover material by 2–3 cm
- Glass jar with tight lid
- Fine mesh strainer or cheesecloth
- Amber glass bottle for storage
### Procedure
1. Grind dried mushrooms to a fine powder using a coffee grinder
2. Place the powder in a glass jar
3. Cover with ethanol (190 proof preferred; 151 proof vodka works but is less efficient)
4. Seal and shake vigorously
5. Store in a cool, dark place for 1–2 weeks, shaking daily
6. After extraction, strain through cheesecloth, squeezing to extract maximum liquid
7. Optionally do a second extraction with fresh ethanol (combine with first extract)
8. Transfer to an amber glass bottle
9. Store in a cool, dark place — shelf life of 6–12 months
### Dosage
The tincture concentrates the actives into a liquid form. Calculate dosage based on starting material:
- If 10 g dried mushrooms extracted into 20 ml ethanol, each ml contains approximately 0.5 g equivalent
- Standard dose of P. cubensis: 1–3.5 g dried
- Therefore, 2–7 ml of this tincture would be a standard dose
## Method 2: Quick Ethanol Extraction (Heat-Assisted)
A faster method using gentle heat:
1. Powder dried mushrooms
2. Place powder in a glass jar with ethanol (1 g mushroom per 10 ml ethanol)
3. Place the jar in a water bath at 60–70°C (140–158°F) — do not exceed 78°C (ethanol boiling point)
4. Simmer gently for 2–3 hours
5. Strain and squeeze through cheesecloth
6. Reduce the volume by gentle evaporation if a more concentrated product is desired
7. Store in amber glass
This method extracts in hours rather than weeks but may extract more unwanted compounds (fats, pigments).
## Method 3: Water Extraction (Tea)
The simplest method of all — no chemistry required:
1. Finely chop or powder dried mushrooms (5–10 g)
2. Bring 1–2 cups of water to a gentle simmer (not a rolling boil — heat can degrade psilocybin)
3. Add the mushroom material
4. Simmer for 15–20 minutes
5. Add a tea bag (mint, ginger, chamomile) for flavor
6. Strain and drink
7. For maximum extraction, do a second steep and combine
Water extraction is efficient because psilocybin is highly water-soluble. The primary drawback is short shelf life — consume within a few hours.
## Method 4: Psilocybin Crystallization (Advanced)
Converting mushroom extract into purified psilocybin crystals:
### Overview
1. Perform ethanol extraction as described above
2. Filter the extract thoroughly
3. Evaporate the ethanol completely using a water bath or rotary evaporator, leaving a dark residue
4. The residue contains psilocybin, psilocin, and various impurities
5. Purify through recrystallization:
   - Dissolve the residue in minimal hot ethanol
   - Filter while hot to remove insoluble impurities
   - Cool slowly to room temperature, then refrigerate
   - Psilocybin crystals may precipitate
   - Collect crystals by filtration
### Challenges
- Psilocybin is difficult to crystallize in pure form — it tends to form amorphous solids
- Psilocin degrades rapidly during processing
- Home crystallization typically yields a partially purified product, not pharmaceutical-grade material
- Yields are low — expect 50–70% recovery
## Potency Testing
### Reagent Testing
- **Ehrlich reagent**: Turns purple/violet in the presence of indole compounds (psilocybin, psilocin, DMT, LSD)
- **Hofmann reagent**: Similar function for tryptamines
- Cannot distinguish between psilocybin and psilocin or quantify potency
- Cannot detect contaminants
### Quantitative Testing
- HPLC (high-performance liquid chromatography) is the gold standard
- GC-MS (gas chromatography-mass spectrometry) also used
- Services like Miraculix and Energy Control offer affordable potency testing
- Some at-home testing kits using colorimetric methods are becoming available
## Storage and Stability
- Dried mushrooms: store in airtight container with desiccant, in a cool dark place — potency stable for 1–2 years
- Ethanol tincture: 6–12 months in amber glass, refrigerated
- Psilocybin degrades with heat, light, and oxygen exposure
- Oxidation of psilocin is what causes mushrooms to turn blue when bruised
## Safety Considerations
- Start with low doses — potency is unpredictable without testing
- A sitter is recommended, especially at higher doses
- Screen for SSRIs or other serotonergic medications (serotonin syndrome risk)
- Avoid if personal or family history of psychosis or schizophrenia
- Set and setting are critical for a positive experience
