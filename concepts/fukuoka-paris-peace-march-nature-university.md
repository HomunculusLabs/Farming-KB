## Fukuoka Paris Peace March and International Nature University

In the European section of "The Road Back to Nature," Fukuoka recounts his encounter with the anti-nuclear peace movement in Paris and the founding vision for an International Nature University. These experiences reveal his conviction that peace, nature, and farming are inseparable concerns.

## The Paris Peace March

In 1979, an anti-nuclear peace march that had started from Oslo reached Paris the same day as Fukuoka. The march began when four women decided they had to do something for peace and simply started walking. As they proceeded south through Europe, people from each country joined them. By Paris, the marchers numbered an estimated 8,000 people.

Fukuoka was invited to speak at the evening gathering in a large renovated warehouse. Speaking through three interpreters into English, German, and French, he addressed the roots of peace and war. The slow translation process gave him time to think and deliver a more serious message.

## The American Indian Perspective

The American Indian chief's son who proposed the university embodied a perspective that resonated deeply with Fukuoka. Indigenous peoples around the world maintained sustainable relationships with their environments for thousands of years before being disrupted by colonial agriculture.

Fukuoka saw in this man's Paris garden -- [[natural-farming-methods-comparison]] in the heart of a European capital -- proof that his philosophy could take root anywhere. The scattering of vegetable seeds in urban settings was itself an act of ecological restoration and cultural bridge-building.
