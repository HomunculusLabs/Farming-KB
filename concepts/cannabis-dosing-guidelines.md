# cannabis dosing guidelines

## Overview

Cannabis dosing is inherently more complex than dosing conventional pharmaceuticals. The [[cannabis-drug-interactions-and-contraindications]] is critical. There is no single "correct dose" -- the goal is to find the minimum effective dose that produces the desired therapeutic or recreational effects with minimal side effects.

## Why Cannabis Dosing Is Challenging

Several factors make standardized cannabis dosing difficult:

- **Variable potency**: Cannabis flower potency ranges from less than 5% to over 30% THC. Concentrates can exceed 90% THC. Without lab testing, potency is unknown
- **Individual sensitivity**: Genetic differences in [[set-and-setting]]**: Psychological state, environment, and expectations significantly influence the experience
- **Plant chemistry**: Different cannabinoid and terpene profiles produce different effects even at the same THC dose

## Consumption Methods and Bioavailability

### Inhalation (Smoking/Vaporizing)

- **Onset**: 1-5 minutes
- **Peak effects**: 15-30 minutes
- **Duration**: 2-4 hours
- **Bioavailability**: 10-35% (varies with smoking technique)
- **Notes**: Easiest to titrate because effects are felt almost immediately. Vaporizing is preferred over smoking as it avoids combustion byproducts. Dose by counting puffs rather than measuring weight.

### Sublingual (Tinctures)

- **Onset**: 15-45 minutes
- **Peak effects**: 1-2 hours
- **Duration**: 4-8 hours
- **Bioavailability**: 12-35% (higher than oral, lower than inhalation)
- **Notes**: Absorbed through mucous membranes under the tongue. Hold under tongue for 30-60 seconds before swallowing for maximum absorption.

### Oral (Edibles, Capsules)

- **Onset**: 30 minutes to 2 hours (sometimes up to 4 hours on a full stomach)
- **Peak effects**: 2-4 hours
- **Duration**: 6-12 hours (sometimes longer)
- **Bioavailability**: 4-20% (lowest of all routes due to first-pass metabolism in the liver)
- **Notes**: THC is converted to 11-hydroxy-THC by the liver, which is significantly more psychoactive. This is why edibles feel stronger and last longer than smoked cannabis. Start very low and wait at least 2 hours before redosing.

### Topical

- **Onset**: 15-60 minutes
- **Peak effects**: 1-2 hours
- **Duration**: 4-6 hours
- **Bioavailability**: Local only (does not enter bloodstream significantly)
- **Notes**: No psychoactive effects. Used for localized pain, inflammation, and skin conditions. See [[cannabis-topical-applications]] for details.

## THC Dosing Guidelines

### Microdose (0.5 - 2.5 mg THC)

- Sub-perceptual to very mild effects
- May enhance mood, creativity, and focus without impairment
- Used for anxiety reduction, mild pain, and daytime functional use
- Many experienced users report this as their preferred daily dose
- Excellent starting point for new or sensitive users

### Low Dose (2.5 - 10 mg THC)

- Mild to moderate psychoactive effects
- Noticeable euphoria, relaxation, sensory enhancement
- Therapeutic for moderate pain, insomnia, appetite stimulation
- Standard dose in many legal market edibles (5-10 mg per serving)
- May cause mild anxiety in sensitive individuals

### Moderate Dose (10 - 25 mg THC)

- Strong psychoactive effects
- Significant euphoria, altered perception, impairment of motor skills
- Effective for severe pain, nausea, muscle spasms
- Not recommended for driving or operating machinery
- May cause anxiety, paranoia, or discomfort in inexperienced users
- This is considered a "high" dose for most occasional consumers

### High Dose (25 - 50+ mg THC)

- Very strong psychoactive effects
- Significant impairment of cognitive and motor function
- May cause anxiety, paranoia, nausea, disorientation
- Used therapeutically only by highly tolerant patients (e.g., chronic pain, cancer)
- Not recommended for casual or new users
- Risk of unpleasant or overwhelming experience is high

## CBD Dosing Guidelines

CBD does not produce psychoactive effects and has a much wider therapeutic window than THC. The optimal dose varies widely depending on the condition being treated.

### General Wellness (5 - 25 mg CBD/day)

- Stress reduction, mild anxiety relief
- Anti-inflammatory support
- Better sleep quality
- Can be taken throughout the day

### Moderate Conditions (25 - 75 mg CBD/day)

- Anxiety disorders, depression
- Chronic pain, arthritis
- Inflammation
- Epilepsy (as adjunct therapy)
- Often split into 2-3 doses per day

### Serious Conditions (75 - 300+ mg CBD/day)

- Severe epilepsy (Epidiolex is FDA-approved at 10-20 mg/kg/day for Dravet and Lennox-Gastaut syndromes)
- Severe chronic pain
- Cancer-related symptoms
- Parkinson's disease, multiple sclerosis symptoms
- Should be monitored by a healthcare professional

### Important Notes on CBD

- CBD can interact with many pharmaceuticals by inhibiting CYP450 liver enzymes, particularly the blood thinner warfarin and anti-seizure medications
- Full-spectrum CBD (containing trace THC and other cannabinoids) may be more effective than CBD isolate due to the entourage effect
- CBD attenuates some of the psychoactive effects of THC -- a 1:1 CBD:THC ratio produces a much milder experience than THC alone
- Side effects at high doses may include drowsiness, dry mouth, diarrhea, and changes in appetite or weight

## The THC:CBD Ratio

The ratio of THC to CBD significantly affects the experience:

- **High THC, no CBD (20:1 or pure THC)**: Maximum psychoactive effect. Higher risk of anxiety and paranoia. Best for experienced users and recreational use
- **Balanced THC:CBD (1:1)**: Moderate psychoactive effect with reduced anxiety. Good for pain management, spasticity, and patients who want therapeutic benefit with minimal intoxication
- **CBD-dominant (1:20 or higher)**: Minimal to no psychoactive effect. Best for anti-inflammatory, anti-anxiety, and neuroprotective applications. Suitable for daytime use and those sensitive to THC
