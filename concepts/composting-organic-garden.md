# Composting in the Organic Garden

Every garden must have a compost heap. Composting is the ideal
way to return as much [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]] as possible to the soil,
following nature's example. Decomposing vegetation provides a
home for millions of soil organisms, opens up the soil,
improves drainage, and eases the way for [[soil-moisture-management-and-root-growth]]. It also
helps over-drained soils hold water and nutrients.

## Why Composting Matters

Plant remains gathered from the garden in the form of waste
leaves, stems from vegetables, grass cuttings, and annual
flowers all contain plant food and should not be wasted.
However, if dug in immediately while still green, the
decomposing bacteria will draw nitrogen from the soil, leaving
growing plants short of food. If the material is turned into
compost first, it will actually [[query-how-do-legumes-add-nitrogen-to-soil]] because bacteria
called Azotobacter [[query-how-to-fix-nitrogen-toxicity-in-cannabis]] from the air into a form
usable by plants.

A successful organic garden should have at least two compost
heaps. One can rot down while the other is being filled.

## Conditions for Good Composting

Good compost requires five key ingredients:

### Air Circulation
Air is vital in the compost heap. Without it, anaerobic
bacteria develop and turn material into stinking slime. The
container should allow air circulation, and material should
be mixed rather than packed down. Mix fine material like
grass cuttings with larger items such as shredded newspaper
or straw to create natural air spaces.

### Nitrogen
Bacteria in the heap need nitrogen as fuel. [[fukuoka-textdoc-animal-manure-human-waste-natural-cycle-heretic]]
is ideal. If unavailable, use organic compost activators,
dried sewage sludge, [[comparison-fish-aminos-vs-kelp-meal]], or dried blood. A fine
dusting every 12in (30cm) of compost is sufficient.

### Lime
Adding lime keeps the compost sweet by neutralizing acidity.
However, a good mixture of materials creates a sweet heap
without added lime. Apply a slightly heavier dusting of lime
than of the nitrogen activator every 12in (30cm).

### Water
This is essential. Green material usually provides enough
moisture, but in hot summers the edges may dry out. Straw
needs thorough wetting before adding. Cover the heap with
plastic or old carpet in winter to retain moisture and
warmth while still allowing the heap to breathe.

### Heat
Decomposition is much faster when warm. In summer, usable
compost is ready in two to three months; in winter the
process slows and compost may not be ready until spring.

### Bacteria
Millions of bacteria exist in clumps of earth on weed
roots. Adding soil layers throughout the heap is completely
unnecessary and makes compost less concentrated.

## What Not to Compost

- Material infected with persistent diseases such as
  clubroot or blight; always burn these
- Top growth of main crop potatoes (blight risk)
- Prunings from woody plants (too slow to rot)
- Cooked kitchen scraps (attract vermin)
- Roots of pernicious weeds such as couch grass, ground
  elder, bindweed, and creeping buttercup
- Weed seeds: the heap rarely gets hot enough to kill them

## Hard Materials

Woody prunings should be composted separately because bark
contains lignin, which is difficult for bacteria to break
