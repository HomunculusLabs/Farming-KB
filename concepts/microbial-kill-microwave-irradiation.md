Microwave irradiation can destroy microorganisms through a
combination of thermal and non-thermal mechanisms. The
effectiveness depends on microwave parameters (frequency,
intensity, duration), the presence of water, and the type and
physiological state of the target organisms. NASA research
established that 2.45 GHz microwaves can achieve complete
sterilization of surfaces contaminated with bacteria, yeasts,
and molds when applied with controlled water presence.

## Thermal Mechanism

The primary kill mechanism involves thermal inactivation. When
microwaves at 2.45 GHz interact with water molecules, the
dipolar water molecules rotate rapidly in response to the
oscillating electromagnetic field, generating heat through
molecular friction. In microbial cells, this intracellular
heating denatures proteins, disrupts lipid membranes, and
inactivates essential enzymes.

Vegetative cells of bacteria, yeasts, and molds contain
substantial free water (typically 70-90% of cell mass),
making them highly susceptible to microwave-induced thermal
damage. The rapid heating achieved by microwave absorption
is particularly effective because it outpaces the cell's
ability to mount a heat-shock response, leading to rapid
and irreversible cellular damage.
