# Reciprocal Roof Frame

A reciprocal roof is a self-supporting roof structure where each rafter rests on and supports the next, forming a spiraling or circular arrangement. No central column or ridge beam is needed — the rafters mutually support each other through an interlocking pattern. This creates a striking, open interior space with a central oculus (opening) at the peak.

## How It Works

The principle is simple: each rafter sits on top of the one before it and supports the one after it. The weight of the structure is distributed through the entire ring, with each rafter transferring its load to the next in sequence. The resulting structure is a closed loop of mutual support.

### The Basic Pattern

1. Start with the lowest-numbered rafter (rafter 1), which sits on the wall plate
2. Rafter 2 rests on top of rafter 1 and also on the wall plate, slightly offset clockwise
3. Rafter 3 rests on rafter 2 (and the wall plate), and so on
4. Each successive rafter laps over the previous one and is notched or secured
5. The final rafter (rafter N) rests on rafter N-1 and its own weight locks the entire assembly

The number of rafters depends on the building diameter and rafter dimensions. Common configurations use 8-16 rafters.

### The Central Opening (Oculus)

The central opening is a defining feature of reciprocal roofs. It can be:

- **Open:** Left as a skylight for natural light and ventilation
- **Glazed:** Fitted with a custom glass or polycarbonate cap
- **Living roof:** A soil and plant layer built up over the roof surface with the oculus left open or glazed

The oculus size is determined by the rafter length and the number of rafters. Longer rafters or fewer rafters produce a larger opening.

## Roundwood Timber

Reciprocal roofs are especially well-suited to roundwood (unmilled, whole logs or branches) construction. Roundwood is:

- **Locally available:** Thinnings from woodland management, coppiced poles, or fallen trees
- **Stronger than milled timber per cross-sectional area:** The natural grain is intact, without the weakening effect of sawing
- **Lower embodied energy:** No milling, drying, or transport of dimensional lumber
- **Beautiful:** Each pole is unique, creating organic, sculptural spaces

### Selecting Roundwood

- **Species:** Locust, oak, larch, Douglas fir, and sweet chestnut are naturally rot resistant. Pine and spruce work but need more protection.
- **Diameter:** 4-8 inches at the butt end for most small to medium structures
- **Length:** Determined by the building radius plus overhang
- **Straightness:** Rafters should be reasonably straight; slight curves add character but must be accounted for
- **Condition:** Sound wood, no rot, active bark beetle galleries, or significant cracks

### Peeling and Preparation

- Peel bark using a drawknife while the wood is green (fresh cut) — bark comes off much more easily
- Allow poles to air-dry for several weeks to months before installation to reduce weight and shrinkage
- Remove side branches flush with the trunk
- Mark the top (crown) and butt (root) end — butt end typically goes at the wall plate

## Green Woodworking

Working with green (freshly cut) wood has advantages for reciprocal roof construction:

- **Ease of shaping:** Green wood cuts, carves, and bends much more easily than dried wood
- **Shrinkage as an advantage:** As green wood dries, it shrinks. In a reciprocal roof, this tightening can make the joints tighter over time, especially when combined with pegged connections.
- **Steam bending:** Green roundwood can be steamed and bent to follow the reciprocal curve if needed

### Tools for Green Woodworking

- **Drawknife:** The primary tool for peeling and shaping roundwood
- **Spokeshave:** Fine shaping and smoothing
- **Froe and mallet:** Splitting green wood along the grain
- **Axe (felling and broad):** Rough shaping
- **Treenails (wooden pegs):** Green hardwood pegs that dry and shrink in place, locking joints tight

## Construction Process

### Design and Calculations

1. **Determine building diameter and wall height:** This sets the rafter length
2. **Choose number of rafters:** More rafters = smaller oculus, closer spacing. Fewer = larger opening.
3. **Calculate rafter length:** For a given diameter (D) and desired oculus size (d), rafter length ≈ (D - d) / 2 + overhang
4. **Determine rafter spacing at the wall plate:** Circumference / number of rafters

### Site Preparation

- Level and compact the foundation or floor area
- Install the wall plate (a continuous ring beam) — can be a roundwood pole ring or a masonry/earthbag circle
- The wall plate must be level and strong enough to support the roof loads
- Common wall types: earthbag, cordwood, cob, stone, roundwood pole frame

### Raising the Reciprocal Roof

1. **Prepare all rafters:** Peel, trim to length, mark seating notches
2. **Position temporary center support:** A tall pole or tripod in the center to hold the first few rafters
3. **Place rafter 1:** Rest butt end on wall plate, tip supported by center post
4. **Place rafter 2:** Rest on wall plate, lap over rafter 1, tip supported by center post
5. **Continue around:** Each rafter laps the previous and supports the next
6. **Final rafter:** Remove center post — if calculations are correct, the structure is self-supporting
7. **Secure joints:** Add pegs, screws, or lashings at each rafter-to-rafter contact point
8. **Test the assembly:** The structure should feel solid with no wobble

### Safety Considerations

- Work with a crew — reciprocal roof raising requires coordination
- Secure each rafter as it is placed before moving to the next
- Have a clear plan for the temporary center support removal
- Do not climb on the roof structure until all joints are secured and the roof decking is in place

## Living Roofs on Reciprocal Frames

The reciprocal roof's round or polygonal shape and often organic geometry pair beautifully with living (green) roofs.

### Layer Assembly (top to bottom)

1. **Growing medium:** 3-6 inches of lightweight soil mix (compost, sand, lightweight aggregate)
2. **Root barrier:** Pond liner or heavy-duty polyethylene
3. **Drainage layer:** Gravel or drainage mat
4. **Filter fabric:** Prevents soil from clogging drainage
5. **Waterproof membrane:** Pond liner or EPDM rubber (most critical layer — must be continuous)
6. **Substrate:** Rough sawn boards, poles, or plywood decking

### Planting

- **Sedums:** Low-maintenance succulents that handle drought well
- **Wildflowers:** Native meadow species for pollinators
- **Grasses:** Ornamental or native grasses for texture and wind resistance
- The oculus allows rainwater to enter and can be used for a garden bed or patio below

### Weight Considerations

- Wet soil and plants add significant weight — typically 25-40 lbs per square foot
- The reciprocal frame and wall system must be engineered to support this load
- Consult an engineer for living roof designs
