# PF TEK Spore Syringe Boiling Sterilization and Storage Lifecycle

## Overview

The [[query-spore-syringes-vs-liquid-culture]] that avoids the need for a [[mushroom-spore-printing-syringe-preparation-pf-tek]] accessible to home cultivators using only common kitchen equipment. The method covers syringe preparation, sterilization, spore loading, inoculation, and storage practices that together define a full lifecycle management system for fungal inoculant.

## Syringe Sterilization Method

### Equipment Required

- Plastic syringes (10cc minimum, with 18 gauge 1.5 inch needles)
- Large syringes (20cc to 65cc) preferred for higher yield
- Extra-long needles advantageous for deep substrate inoculation
- Standard kitchen pot with boiling water
- Tin foil for wrapping

### Boiling Sterilization Procedure

The [[pf-tek]] specifies a straightforward boiling-water approach:

1. **Initial flushing**: Draw boiling water into a syringe and squirt it out several times. This removes manufacturing residues and pre-heats the syringe body.

2. **Boiling bath**: Fill the syringe with boiling water, replace the needle guard, and wrap the entire syringe in tin foil. Multiple syringes can be prepared simultaneously using this method.

3. **Extended boil**: Drop the foil-wrapped syringes directly into a pot of boiling water and maintain a rolling boil for one full hour. This extended duration ensures thorough heat penetration to all internal surfaces of the syringe and needle.

4. **Cooling**: Allow syringes to cool to room temperature before use. Hot syringes will kill spores on contact during loading.

The boiling method works because syringes are thin-walled plastic devices with narrow internal channels and minimal thermal mass. Unlike substrate jars containing dense organic material, a syringe has no insulation barriers. One hour at 100 degrees Celsius is sufficient to sterilize all internal surfaces. PF offered a money-back guarantee for contaminated syringes, demonstrating confidence in this approach.

## Syringe Loading from Spore Print

### Lid Preparation for Spore Extraction

A specialized two-hole lid is prepared for the spore print jar:

- **Center hole**: Drilled to accept a Pyrex glass stirring rod, used to scrape spores into sterile water
- **Edge hole**: Punched near the rubberized edge to fit a syringe needle, used for drawing the spore solution

Both holes must be covered with tin foil or sterile surgical tape before and after the procedure to maintain sterility throughout the process.

### Loading Sequence

1. Inject sterile water into the spore print jar through the edge needle hole
2. Flame-sterilize the glass stirring rod, allow it to cool briefly, then insert through the center hole to scrape spores into the water
3. Insert the sterile syringe needle through the small edge hole, tilt the jar until the spore-water suspension reaches the needle tip, and draw the spore solution into the syringe

The critical principle is minimizing exposure of the jar interior to room air. Every transition between steps must be protected. PF emphasized that experience is the best teacher for this procedure.

## Inoculation Technique with Sterile Syringe

### Pre-Inoculation Protocol

- Ensure the substrate jar is cool to the touch before proceeding
- Verify the jar lid is tight
- Shake the syringe well to redistribute spores throughout the water column
- Remove tape from both the syringe needle guard and the jar needle holes

### Injection Method

- Insert the needle through one of the lid holes
- Tilt the syringe body back toward the center of the lid with the needle tip touching the glass
- This angle distributes the spore solution down the side of the jar, inoculating the substrate cake along its full depth
- Inoculate a few drops through each needle hole (4 holes per lid)
- Observe the needle tip against the inside glass; release plunger pressure as soon as water appears
- Shake the syringe between holes to keep spores in suspension
- Use approximately 1 cc per jar, allowing one syringe to inoculate 10 jars

### Troubleshooting Needle Plugging

If the syringe needle plugs during insertion into the substrate, drawing the needle back slightly will unplug it. The vermiculite-based substrate is loose enough that repositioning resolves blockage without losing inoculation depth.
