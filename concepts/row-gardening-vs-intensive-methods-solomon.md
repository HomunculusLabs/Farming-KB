The widest spacing of all, designed for situations with minimal irrigation or

taps deep subsoil moisture reserves.

## Raised Beds: Practical Considerations

Raised beds should not be wider than about four feet (120 cm), so a person
standing on the adjacent path can reach the center while bending over. Paths
between beds are about one foot (30 cm) wide. There is no point in raising
beds more than two to three inches (5–8 cm) unless the gardener is inflexible
and cannot squat or bend over.

The main advantages of slightly raised beds are: (1) it is far easier to
create a fine seedbed for small seeds when you can rake clods off to the sides
and into the paths; and (2) raised beds tend to stay looser because nobody
walks on them, which avoids compacting the root zone.

## When to Use Each System

- **Intensive beds**: Rarely recommended. Only for gardeners with very limited
  space who are willing to invest heavily in water, compost, and daily labor.
- **Semi-intensive raised beds**: Best general-purpose system for irrigated
  gardens. Good balance of yield, quality, and manageable labor.
- **Extensive with good rainfall**: Ideal for most temperate climates with
  reliable summer rainfall. Minimizes irrigation needs.
- **Extensive dry gardening**: For water-scarce situations or gardeners
  without irrigation infrastructure. Requires wider spacing and careful
  variety selection.
