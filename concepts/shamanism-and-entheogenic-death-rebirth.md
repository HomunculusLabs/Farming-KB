# Shamanism and the Entheogenic Death-Rebirth Experience

## Overview

Shamanism represents humanity's oldest and most indigenous form of natural spirituality, rooted in the direct experiential exploration of consciousness through entheogenic plants. At its core, shamanic practice centers on the journey into the death experience — a deep psychological introspection and judgment through plant-induced states, undertaken in preparation for physical death. The practice is fundamentally about conquering death spiritually so that when the physical body dies, the spirit "wakes," having already rehearsed the transition through guided visionary experience.

## Historical Origins

Before organized religion, before written language, early humans who encountered consciousness-expanding plants became aware of something beyond normal physical reality. This realization developed a new contemplation of death and a desire for individual immortality. World mythologies universally contain claims of beings ascending beyond death into a spiritual dimension, attainable through a quest for an elixir — and this cosmopolitan pattern across ancient documents points to a common historical source: the indigenous natural practice of shamanism.

[[allegro-colour-and-consistency-of-the-amanita-muscaria]] mushroom is identified as the cornerstone of shamanism, producing the quintessential [[shamanism-death-and-rebirth]] experience. Despite shamanism's rejection of patriarchal religious structures, it is paradoxically the foundational source from which all major religions ultimately derive their myths, rituals, and symbolism.

## The Core Practice: Journey into Death

The shamanic death-rebirth experience involves several key elements:

- **Induction of a death-like trance** through the consumption of entheogenic substances
- **Complete psychological introspection and self-judgment**, where every event of one's life surfaces into consciousness
- **Experience of heaven and hell**, and all points between — the "hell" being primarily the recognition and rejection of deceptions embedded in one's cosmological belief system through years of societal conditioning
- **Confrontation with culturally embedded beliefs** that contradict one's naturally experienced indigenous spiritual nature
- **Stripping away of belief systems** in conflict with the individual seeker's natural spiritual makeup

The experience follows what is described as an "elevator" pattern — reaching indefinable realms of the heavens, followed by a sudden plunge to the depths, then an up-sweeping again toward ascent. This unpredictable cycling between extremes is characteristic of the Amanita muscaria experience specifically.

## Spiritual Evolution and Reincarnation

Shamanism operates on an individual basis, with teachings that stand in direct opposition to societal, religious, and political norms. The practice is connected to broader spiritual evolutionary frameworks found in Hindu and Buddhist texts, where advancement through reincarnation occurs according to knowledge gained and key tools for enlightenment discovered on different levels of the path.

The Hindu concept of **Samsara** (the wheel of life) — the revolving door of incarnation — is described as being driven by the inability to let go of repressive belief at death. This conflict between deeply conditioned beliefs and the truth experienced during the death transition is what forces beings into repeated reincarnation. The indigenous shamanic teaching recognizes this repression of the human soul on a global level, embedded into the psyche through past-life experiences of oppression.

## The Soma Hypothesis: A Multi-Plant Elixir

A significant portion of shamanic scholarship concerns the identity of **Soma**, the legendary elixir of the gods described in Vedic texts. Rather than identifying Soma with a single plant, evidence from medieval artwork suggests it was an admixture of several plants working in combination:

1. **Amanita muscaria** (red) — produces the "quickening of the spirit"
2. **Psilocybe species** (blue) — opens the "third eye"
3. **Syrian Rue / [[mckenna-food-gods-peganum-harmala-soma]]** — contains Telepathine, an MAO inhibitor that increases the properties of other compounds, paralleling Pinoline naturally produced by the Pineal Gland
4. **Opium Poppy** — induces euphoria enabling one to relax and fully experience the intensive visionary state
5. **Green plants including Cannabis** — prevents nausea, enhances effects, and reactivates neural pathways opened by entheogens

This combination mimics the DMT-Pinoline combination naturally produced by the pineal gland, associated with naturally occurring psychic experiences and visionary states during sleep (somnambulism). The linguistic connection between Soma and [[papaver-somniferum]], as well as the word somnambulism itself, is noted as significant.

The text identifies a systematic pattern of suppression directed at shamanic and indigenous spiritual practices:

- **[[mckenna-caffeine-coffee-and-industrial-revolution]] and patriarchal systems** consciously attempted to stamp out [[ethnobotanical-use-of-sacred-mushrooms-in-indigenous-cultures]] under the guise of "civilizing the savage"

- **Five natural human drives** are identified as specifically targeted for repression: (1) expanding consciousness, (2) heightening awareness, (3) experiencing plant substances, (4) freely enjoying sexuality, (5) engaging in rhythmic dance and trance
- These drives are replaced with societal models of moral judgment condemning them as sin

## The Shaman as Hero

The nature of the hero's journey toward immortality is associated with the search for ultimate truth, which is a necessary step toward that goal. In shamanism, the entheogenic plants are known to teach this truth to those able to understand. The teaching strips away beliefs in conflict with the individual's natural indigenous spiritual nature, tuning the mind toward higher frequency vibrations necessary to conquer death on both spiritual and physical levels.

The hero's quest is also consistently about freeing friends, family, and the world from an oppressive tyrant or government — a pattern that appears across mythological traditions worldwide. The degree to which one accepts this task is determined by awareness of surroundings, recognition of the repressive nature of one's environment, and possession of the desire and courage to act.
