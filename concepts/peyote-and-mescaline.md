    10|# Peyote and Mescaline
    11|
    12|Peyote (Lophophora williamsii) is a small, spineless cactus native to the
    13|Chihuahuan Desert that contains the phenethylamine alkaloid mescaline as its
    14|primary psychoactive [[2c-t-7-compound-profile]]. Peyote has been used in ceremonial contexts by
    15|indigenous peoples of North America for thousands of years and remains central
    16|to the religious practices [[allegro-star-of-the-morning-venus-and-the-sacred-mushroom]] Native American Church. Mescaline was the
    17|first classical psychedelic to be chemically identified and characterized.
    18|
    19|## Botanical Background
    20|
    21|Peyote is a perennial cactus that grows in a limited range spanning southern
    22|Texas through central Mexico. It typically grows as a flattened, blue-green
    23|button-shaped crown above ground, with the majority [[allegro-colour-and-consistency-of-the-amanita-muscaria]] plant consisting
    24|of an extensive underground root system. The above-ground portion, known as
    25|the crown or button, contains the highest concentration of mescaline and
    26|related alkaloids.
    27|
    28|The plant is extremely slow-growing, taking 5 to 10 years to reach maturity.
    29|Wild populations have been depleted by overharvesting, leading to conservation
    30|concerns. Peyote is legally protected in some jurisdictions, though
    31|harvesting for religious use by members [[allegro-names-of-the-gods-and-their-fertility-origins]] Native American Church is
    32|permitted under specific legal exemptions [[allegro-death-and-resurrection-in-the-mushroom-cult]] United States.
    33|
    34|## Chemistry of Mescaline
    35|
    36|Mescaline (3,4,5-trimethoxyphenethylamine) is a naturally occurring
    37|phenethylamine alkaloid and one [[allegro-fertility-philosophy-of-the-ancient-near-east]] four principal classical psychedelics,
    38|alongside psilocybin, LSD, [[ayahuasca-additive-plants-and-dmt-synergy]]. It is structurally distinct from the
    39|[[tryptamine]] class of psychedelics, consisting of a phenethylamine backbone
    40|with three methoxy groups at the 3, 4, and 5 positions of the aromatic ring.
    41|
    42|The effective oral dose of mescaline is approximately 200 to 500 milligrams,
    43|which is relatively high compared to other classical psychedelics. The onset
    44|of effects occurs within one to two hours, peaks at three to five hours, and
    45|lasts eight to twelve hours. Mescaline is metabolized primarily through
    46|oxidative deamination by monoamine oxidase, with subsequent conjugation and
    47|renal excretion.
    48|
    49|## Pharmacological Mechanism
    50|
    51|Mescaline acts primarily as a partial agonist at serotonin 5-HT2A receptors,
    52|the same receptor subtype that mediates the effects of other classical
    53|psychedelics. This shared mechanism of action underlies the phenomenological
    54|similarities between mescaline and other psychedelic compounds, including
    55|altered perception, synesthesia, enhanced pattern recognition, and changes
    56|in the sense of self and time.
    57|
    58|Mescaline also shows affinity for 5-HT2C, 5-HT1A, and alpha-1 adrenergic
    59|receptors, contributing to its distinct subjective profile. Users frequently
    60|report a greater emphasis on vivid visual hallucinations with mescaline
    61|compared to other psychedelics, along with a pronounced quality of emotional
    62|warmth and connectedness.
    63|
    64|## Indigenous Ceremonial Use
    65|
    66|The ceremonial use of peyote in North America dates back at least 5,000 years,
    67|based on archaeological evidence including peyote buttons found in cave
    68|deposits in Texas. The modern Native American Church, formally incorporated
    69|in 1918, uses peyote as a sacrament in ceremonies that typically involve
    70|all-night prayer, singing, drumming, and the consumption of peyote buttons.
    71|
    72|Peyote ceremonies are structured rituals conducted by experienced practitioners
    73|known as roadmen. The ceremony follows a well-defined sequence of prayers,
    74|songs, and communal activities designed to facilitate healing, spiritual
    75|guidance, and community bonding. Participants report that the peyote
    76|experience enhances emotional insight, promotes moral reflection, and
    77|strengthens social bonds.
    78|
    79|## Legal Status
    80|
    81|In the United States, peyote is classified as a Schedule I controlled
    82|substance under the Controlled Substances Act. However, the American Indian
    83|Religious Freedom Act Amendments of 1994 specifically protect the use of
    84|peyote by members of the Native American Church for bona fide religious
    85|ceremonies. This exemption has been upheld by multiple court decisions.
    86|
    87|Mescaline itself is also a Schedule I substance, and its synthetic
    88|preparation and possession are prohibited outside of approved research
    89|contexts. Despite its Schedule I status, mescaline is not considered to have
    90|significant abuse potential, and physical dependence has not been documented.
    91|
    92|## Clinical Research Interest
    93|
    94|There has been renewed scientific interest in mescaline in recent years as
    95|part of the broader renaissance in psychedelic research. However, clinical
    96|studies of mescaline have lagged behind those of psilocybin and MDMA,
    97|partly due to the longer duration of action and partly due to supply
    98|constraints. Contemporary research is exploring its potential applications
    99|in depression, anxiety, and substance use disorders, and modern studies are
   100|beginning to characterize its subjective effects using standardized
   101|measurement tools.
   102|
   103|## Comparison with Other Classical Psychedelics
   104|
   105|Mescaline occupies a distinctive position among classical psychedelics due to
   106|its phenethylamine structure, relatively high effective dose, and long
   107|duration of action. Compared to psilocybin (6-8 hours) and DMT (15-60 minutes
   108|when vaporized), mescaline's 8-12 hour duration makes it one of the longest-
   109|acting naturally occurring psychedelics. This extended duration has both
   110|advantages and disadvantages in therapeutic contexts.
   111|
   112|The subjective quality of mescaline experiences is often described as more
   113|visual and less cognitive than psilocybin, with users reporting richly
   114|detailed hallucinations of geometric patterns, organic forms, and landscapes.
   115|The emotional tone is frequently characterized as warm and benevolent, with
   116|a strong sense of connection to nature and the natural world. These qualities
   117|have contributed to mescaline's enduring appeal in both indigenous ceremonial
   118|and modern recreational contexts.
   119|
   120|## Pharmacokinetic Variability
   121|
   122|The pharmacokinetics of mescaline show considerable interindividual
   123|variability, influenced by factors including body weight, metabolic rate,
   124|gastric pH, and genetic polymorphisms in metabolizing enzymes. This
   125|variability can make dose determination challenging, particularly in
   126|non-clinical settings. The compound is metabolized primarily by hepatic
   127|monoamine oxidase, with subsequent conjugation and renal excretion.
   128|
   129|Food intake significantly affects absorption rates, with mescaline taken
   130|on an empty stomach typically producing more rapid and intense effects.
   131|The relatively high effective dose compared to other psychedelics means that
   132|dose-dependent adverse effects, including nausea, vomiting, and anxiety, are
   133|more common with mescaline than with more potent compounds.
