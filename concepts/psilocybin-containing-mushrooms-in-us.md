# [[psilocybe-cubensis]] and other Psilocybe species, commonly known as
"[[guzman-magic-mushrooms-african-species]]," contain the hallucinogenic compounds psilocybin
(4-phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) and [[hallucinogenic-plants-native-to-united-states]],
[[paul-stamets]] provide
comprehensive identification resources.

## Geographic Distribution in the US

In the United States, psilocybin-containing mushrooms are
coprophagous, meaning they grow on animal dung, particularly cow and
horse manure. Their distribution includes:

- **Gulf States**: Florida through the southern Gulf States to Texas,
  where mushrooms grow in pastures and manured fields.
- **Pacific Northwest**: Pasture and grain fields, and along the
  coastline from California into Canada.
- **Other regions**: Scattered populations may occur wherever suitable
  substrates and climate conditions exist.

The warm, humid conditions of the southeastern US and the temperate
rainy seasons of the Pacific Northwest provide ideal growing
environments for these fungi.

## Misidentification Risks

Misidentification of wild mushrooms is the most significant danger
associated with [[emcdda-psilocybin-mushroom-dose-and-effects]] use. Many [[growing-gourmet-poisonous-mushroom-lookalikes]]
species grow in the same habitats as Psilocybe species, and
inexperienced foragers risk consuming toxic look-alikes. This risk
can result in serious illness or death from liver failure or other
organ damage caused by truly poisonous species such as Galerina
species, which can resemble small Psilocybe mushrooms.

false identification and potential poisoning from wild-sourced
specimens.

## Preparation and Dosage

[[forensic-identification-methods-psilocybin-mushrooms-tlc-gc-hplc]] are typically dried and eaten directly. They may
also be brewed into a tea to reduce nausea. Fresh mushrooms can be
consumed, though potency is lower due to water weight.

Approximately 40 micrograms of psilocybin per kilogram of body weight
produces a threshold intoxication. Typically, 1 to 2 grams of dried
mushroom is ingested, yielding 4 to 20 mg of psilocybin depending on
the species and potency. The intoxication peaks within the first 2
hours and diminishes over the subsequent 3 to 4 hours.

Cross-tolerance occurs between LSD and psilocybin/psilocin, though
not as readily as between LSD and mescaline.

## Cultivation and Spore Prints

Although Psilocybe species mushrooms, psilocybin, and psilocin are
Schedule I controlled substances in the United States, [[emcdda-hallucinogenic-mushroom-spore-distribution-home-cultivation]]
prints remain legal to possess and distribute in most states
(California is an exception). A small industry continues to supply
mycologists and those interested in obtaining hallucinogens with the
necessary materials to grow these mushrooms. Home cultivation removes
the risk of misidentification associated with wild foraging.

## Historical and Cultural Context

Some indigenous tribes in the Oaxaca region of Mexico still consider
these mushrooms sacred. Sacramental use extends historically back to
the reign of the Aztec civilization and earlier. The Mazatec people
of Mexico have a particularly well-documented tradition of using
psilocybin mushrooms for divination and healing ceremonies.

## Health and Safety

Psilocybin mushrooms themselves are not known to be physically
harmful in the way that many other drugs are. They do not cause
organ damage and have no known lethal dose in humans. The primary
risks include:

- **Misidentification**: Ingestion of toxic mushroom species
  mistaken for Psilocybe mushrooms.
- **Psychological distress**: Anxiety, paranoia, or panic during
  intense experiences, particularly in unprepared users or
  inappropriate settings.
- **Accidental injury**: Impaired judgment during intoxication.

## Legal Status

Psilocybin and psilocin are Schedule I controlled substances under
federal law in the United States. Possession, cultivation, and
distribution of psilocybin-containing mushrooms are illegal. Spore
prints, which do not contain psychoactive compounds, are legal in
most states.
