# KNF FMC Floor and Temperature Control

Fermented Mixed Compost (FMC) is a core [[korean-natural-farming]] input
that promotes micronutrient absorption, enhances plant hormone
effectiveness, and provides vitamins. Proper floor selection and
temperature management during fermentation are critical to producing
high-quality FMC rather than decomposed waste. For storage guidelines once FMC is complete, see [[knf-input-storage-guidelines]].

## FMC Overview

FMC is similar to farmyard manure or ordinary compost, but the quality
is completely different due to the use of [[query-how-do-i-use-beneficial-indigenous-microorganisms]] and other NF inputs
during the fermentation process. The result is a biologically active
compost rich in hormones, enzymes, and bioavailable micronutrients.

Two seasonal considerations affect FMC production:
- Hot and humid summer weather favors bacterial multiplication
- Cool and dry weather between late fall and winter favors fermenting
  microbes, which is why FMC should ideally be made in late fall

## Floor Requirements: Soil vs Concrete

The choice of floor surface is critical for successful FMC:

### Soil Floor (Required)
- All FMC mixing must be done on a soil floor, never on concrete
- Soil floor allows microbial exchange between the compost and the
  earth, supporting diverse microbial communities
- IMO-4 application and mixing should occur directly on soil
- The floor should be covered with one foot of soil if it is
  currently bare

### Concrete Floor (Never Use)
When FMC becomes hot during fermentation, it emits moisture in all
directions. On a concrete floor:
- The concrete gets wet from the moisture
- This trapped moisture causes FMC to decay rather than ferment
- The fertilizing value of the FMC is significantly decreased
- Beneficial microorganisms are killed by the anaerobic conditions

### Site Selection
Choose a location with:
- Little wind to prevent excessive drying
- Good drainage to prevent waterlogging
- Reliable water access for moisture adjustment
- For greenhouses, install shade rather than transparent vinyl film
  to protect from direct sunlight

## Temperature Management During Fermentation

Temperature control is the most critical factor in FMC production.
Effective fermentation is dependent on maintaining the right
temperature range throughout the process.

### Heap Height by Season
- Hot weather: Heap to a height of 40 cm to allow oxygen to
  penetrate and control temperature naturally
- Cold weather: Heap to 80 cm to generate and retain sufficient
  heat for fermentation

### Temperature Monitoring and Turning Schedule
The temperature during the process must be carefully controlled:

- Day 2: Temperature rises to approximately 50 degrees C. At this
  point, use a shovel or machine to turn the material over. Mix
  inside out to lower temperature and allow better aeration.

- Days 5 to 6: Second turning. The temperature will have risen
  again as microbial activity peaks. Turn to redistribute heat
  and moisture evenly throughout the pile.

- Days 8 to 10: Third turning. Final mixing to ensure complete and
  even fermentation throughout the entire compost mass.

### Critical Temperature Warning
If temperature reaches 70 degrees C, nutrients and beneficial liquids
in the ingredients will evaporate, destroying the value of the FMC.
The specific turning schedule is designed to prevent temperatures
from reaching this destructive threshold.

## Moisture Management

Moisture level should be adjusted to approximately 60 percent:
- Use diluted (about 500 times) FPJ, FAA, OHN, and other NF inputs
  for moisture adjustment (see [[knf-input-storage-guidelines]] for storage of these inputs)
- The matter should be slightly wet to the hand
- When squeezed, it should barely maintain its shape
- Adding powdered oyster, crab, shrimp, or eggshells is very good for
  both calcium enrichment and moisture management

## Covering and Protection

Cover the FMC heap with rice straw mat to:
- Control moisture evaporation
- Maintain stable temperature
- Control the propagation of microorganisms
- Protect from direct sunlight and rain

## Hormone Production in FMC

A well-made FMC contains a rich mixture of plant growth hormones
produced during fermentation:
- Auxin from yeast and filamentous fungus
- Gibberellins from red fungus
- Cytokines from germs and yeast

Cytokine hormone promotes leaf and branch growth, cell division,
embryo and seed formation, flower bud formation, germination, and
prevents aging. These hormones make FMC far more valuable than
ordinary compost.

## Quality Indicators

- Good FMC has a sweet smell, indicating proper fermentation
- Bad-smelling FMC indicates deterioration, not fermentation
- In worst cases with poor turning or excessive water, maggots may
  appear, signaling complete failure of the process

## Storage and Application

- After 20 days, FMC is complete and ready for use
- Put FMC in sacks or gunny bags for storage
- Keep in cool shade for up to one year of storage
- Apply to field 2-3 hours before sunset to avoid UV damage to
  microorganisms
