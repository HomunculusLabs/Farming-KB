## Black Stem Rust: Puccinia graminis

Black stem rust attacks wheat through a life cycle requiring two hosts:
a cereal grass and the barberry bush ([[bloomfield-rust-germ-tube-tactile-sensing-leaf-surface-navigation]] and sends out a germ
tube programmed to find stomata. The tube grows across the leaf, rising
over microscopic ridges formed by epidermal cell junctions and falling
into intervening valleys. Because only one in five or fewer lines of
epidermal cells contain stomata, growing across the leaf dramatically
increases the odds of finding an entry point.

Cornell plant pathologist Harvey Hoch demonstrated that germ tubes
recognize stomatal lips using physical cues alone. He fabricated plastic
replicas of leaf surfaces and showed that germ tubes recognized and
swelled over model stomata. Further experiments with films bearing
microscopic ridges of defined height showed that the fungus recognized
ridges matching the height of stomatal lips (0.5 micrometers) but
crawled over lower or higher ones. Hoch even manufactured microscopic
mazes for rusts, predicting growth patterns before seeding surfaces
with spores.

germination: both cells produce short hyphae, and meiosis packages four
daughter nuclei into bean-shaped spores at the tips of spikes.
