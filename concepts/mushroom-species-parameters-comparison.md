# Mushroom Species Parameters Comparison

paul stamets provides detailed species-specific cultivation parameters in The Mushroom Cultivator, covering temperature ranges, substrate preferences, spawn media, and yield expectations for numerous cultivated species. These parameters serve as essential reference data for cultivators.

## Agaricus Species

### Agaricus bitorquis
- **Spawn media**: Grain (rye, millet, wheat)
- **Spawn temperature**: 84-86°F (29-30°C)
- **Fruiting temperature**: 75-77°F (24-25°C)
- **Substrate**: Composted straw/horse manure
- **Casing**: Required
- **Light**: Not required for fruiting
- **CO2 tolerance**: Moderate
- **Notable**: More tolerant of warm temperatures than other Agaricus species; partial veil provides nearly contaminant-free spores

### Agaricus brunnescens (White Button)
- **Spawn temperature**: 75-81°F (24-27°C)
- **Fruiting temperature**: 60-64°F (15-18°C)
- **Substrate**: Composted straw/horse manure
- **Casing**: Required
- **Light**: Not required for fruiting
- **Notable**: The most commercially important cultivated mushroom worldwide

## Pleurotus Species

### Pleurotus ostreatus (Oyster Mushroom)
- **Spawn media**: Grain or sawdust/bran
- **Spawn temperature**: 75-85°F (24-29°C)
- **Fruiting temperature**: 55-65°F (13-18°C)
- **Substrate**: Straw, sawdust, or supplemented substrates
- **Casing**: Not required
- **Light**: Required for normal fruiting
- **Fresh air**: High requirements — one of the most CO2-sensitive species
- **Notable**: Among the most vigorous mushroom species; produces in the wild year-round

### Pleurotus eryngii (King Oyster)
- **Fruiting temperature**: 55-65°F (13-18°C)
- **Notable**: Large, thick-stemmed fruiting bodies with culinary appeal

## Psilocybe Species

### Psilocybe cubensis
- **Spawn temperature**: 84-86°F (29-30°C)
- **Fruiting temperature**: 74-78°F (23-26°C)
- **Substrate**: Grain, straw, or composted manure
- **Casing**: Not required but beneficial
- **Light**: Required for normal fruiting
- **Notable**: One of the easiest species to cultivate; most widely grown psilocybin mushroom

### Psilocybe cyanescens
- **Spawn temperature**: 75-80°F (24-27°C)
- **Fruiting temperature**: 50-60°F (10-15°C)
- **Substrate**: Wood chips, sawdust
- **Notable**: More difficult to cultivate indoors; prefers outdoor woodchip beds

## Lentinus edodes (Shiitake)
- **Spawn media**: Sawdust/bran (preferred over grain)
- **Spawn temperature**: 72-77°F (22-25°C)
- **Fruiting temperature**: 50-60°F (10-15°C)
- **Substrate**: 90% hardwood sawdust, 10% rice bran, 0.2% CaCO3
- **Casing**: Not required
- **Light**: Required — 550 lux for highest yields
- **Vegetative cycle**: 12 weeks for maximum yield
- **Notable**: Two thousand years of cultivation history in Japan and China

## Flammulina velutipes (Enoki)
- **Spawn temperature**: 72-77°F (22-25°C)
- **Fruiting temperature**: 35-55°F (2-13°C)
- **Substrate**: Sawdust-based
- **Light**: Required
- **Yield**: 160-220g per 800ml bottle average, max approximately 600g
- **Water content**: 92%
- **Protein (dry)**: 18-31%
- **Notable**: Widely cultivated in Japan; elongated stems and small caps produced in commercial conditions

## Volvariella volvacea (Paddy Straw Mushroom)
- **Spawn temperature**: 90-95°F (32-35°C) — highest of all cultivated species
- **Fruiting temperature**: 85-90°F (29-32°C)
- **Substrate**: Rice straw or cotton waste
- **Notable**: Tropical species requiring very warm conditions; popular in Southeast Asian cuisine

## Coprinus comatus (Shaggy Mane)
- **Fruiting temperature**: 55-65°F (13-18°C)
- **Substrate**: Composted materials
- **Notable**: Deliquesces (dissolves into ink) within hours of spore release — must be harvested immediately

## Key Parameters Across All Species

### Spawn-Running Phase (Common Requirements)
- High humidity (85-100%)
- Moderate to elevated CO2
- Dark or low light conditions
- Clean, draft-free environment

### Fruiting Phase (Common Requirements)
- Temperature drop from spawn-running optimum
- High humidity (85-95%)
- Frequent fresh air exchange
- Light for most species (exceptions: Agaricus)
- Reduced CO2 levels
