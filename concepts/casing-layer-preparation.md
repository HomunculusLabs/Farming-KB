## Moisture Assessment

The correct moisture content is critical and challenging to achieve:
- **Too dry:** Mycelium will not grow through the casing. Surface appears
  dusty and crumbly. Pinning is delayed or absent.
- **Too wet:** Casing becomes compacted and anaerobic, promoting
  bacterial contamination. Surface appears muddy.
- **Correct:** Casing holds together when squeezed but releases water
  slowly, not in streams. Surface appears uniformly dark and moist.

Water needed depends on the peat's initial moisture, which varies by
brand and season. Exact volumes cannot be prescribed universally;
cultivators must develop a feel for correct consistency through
experience.

## The Sterilization Debate

A significant point of contention among cultivators is whether casing
soil should be sterilized, pasteurized, or left untreated:

**Arguments against sterilization (conventional wisdom):**
- Sterilization kills beneficial microorganisms naturally present in
  peat, such as *Pseudomonas* and *Trichoderma* species, which are
  believed to stimulate pinning and suppress competitor molds.
- Pasteurization (heating to 60 to 70 degrees C for 1 to 2 hours) is
  said to kill most contaminants while preserving beneficial organisms.

**The source material's position (pro-sterilization):**
- The authors explicitly state they have had only bad experiences with
  untreated or pasteurized casing soils.
- Full sterilization in the pressure cooker has consistently produced
  better results in their practice.
- They acknowledge the conventional argument but emphasize empirical
  results over theoretical benefits of beneficial organisms.

This debate remains unresolved in the broader cultivation community.
The source material provides a clear practical recommendation: when
cultivating on grass seed in home conditions, sterilize the casing soil
for the most reliable results.

## Application to Cased Trays

Once the colonized grass seed is transferred to 1-litre disposable
plastic trays and the surface is levelled, the casing is applied:

- **Depth:** 1.5 to 2.0 cm of casing soil over the grain surface
- **Surface texture:** Keep the surface even while maintaining a rough
  quality with small valleys and hills. This roughness increases the
  surface area available for pin formation.
- **Covering:** After casing, cover the tray with tin foil and incubate
  at 20 to 25 degrees C until mycelium grows visibly through the
  casing surface (typically a few days).
- **Timing:** Readiness for fruiting depends on strain. Some strains
  fruit earlier and more readily than others.
