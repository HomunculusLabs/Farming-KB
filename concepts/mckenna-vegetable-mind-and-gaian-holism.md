## Gaian Holism

Gaian holism is McKenna's term for "a sense of the unity and balance
of nature and of our own position within that dynamic, evolving
balance." It is explicitly a plant-based view, requiring the
deconstruction of dominator cultural values and their replacement with
an awareness that places the ego within the larger context of planetary
life and evolution. McKenna saw this as the essence of the Archaic
Revival, and credits Marshall McLuhan with correctly predicting that
planetary human culture would be tribal in character. The next great
step, he argues, is "the partial merging of the technologically
transformed human world with the Archaic matrix of vegetable
intelligence that is the Transcendent Other."

## Implications for Human Evolution

McKenna characterizes the dawning awareness of Gaian holism as
religious in nature, involving a full exploration of dimensions revealed
by plant hallucinogens. Careful exploration of these plants will, he
argues, probe the most archaic and sensitive level of the drama of the
emergence of consciousness: the plant-human quasi-symbiotic relationship
that characterized archaic society. This return means trading the point
of view of the egoistic dominator for the "intuitional, feeling-toned
understanding of the maternal matrix." The implication is that
understanding planetary purpose may be humanity's major contribution to
the evolutionary process, achievable only through renewed partnership
with the plant kingdom.

## Psychedelic Exopheromones

A key element of McKenna's framework is the concept of "psychedelic
exopheromones," compounds produced by plants that regulate the
symbiotic relationship between humans and the plant kingdom. Just as
insect pheromones mediate [[psilocybin]] and related tryptamines
function as exopheromones mediating communication across species
boundaries. Without awareness of these exopheromones and the
relationships they encode, McKenna argues, humanity stands "outside
of an understanding of planetary purpose." This concept bridges the
gap between biochemistry and cosmology, suggesting that the chemical
interactions between humans and psychoactive plants are not accidental
but reflect a deeper ecological and evolutionary intelligence.

## Criticism and Limitations

on several grounds. Some scholars note the lack of empirical evidence
for the specific historical claims about mushroom use in human
