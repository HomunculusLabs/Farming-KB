# Plants Tolerant of Alkaline Soils

Alkaline [[alkaline-soil-tolerant-plants-permaculture|permaculture]]|[[alkaline-soil-plants|soil]] s, characterised by high pH (above 7.0) and often high calcium carbonate content, present significant challenges for plant growth. Nutrient availability decreases at higher pH, particularly for iron, manganese, zinc, and phosphorus. Despite these limitations, a remarkable diversity of plant species have adapted to alkaline conditions, ranging from Australian native shrubs to Mediterranean trees and ornamental species. This page catalogues plants known to tolerate or prefer alkaline soils, drawing primarily from Australian permaculture plant selections.

## Acacia Species (Wattles)

The Acacia genus contains perhaps the largest number of species adapted to alkaline conditions, particularly the calcareous soils of arid and semi-arid Australia. Wattles are nitrogen-fixing legumes, making them doubly valuable for alkaline soil improvement.

### Key Alkaline-Tolerant Wattles

- **Acacia ampliceps** (salt wattle, jila jila) — NW Australia
- **Acacia aneura** (mulga) — One of Australia's most widespread arid zone trees; extremely alkaline-adapted
- **Acacia bivenosa** (two-veined acacia) — Calcareous soils
- **Acacia cuspidifolia** (spine-tipped wattle) — Calcareous adapted
- **Acacia cyclops** (coastal wattle) — Coastal calcareous sands
- **Acacia harpophylla** (brigalow, spearwood) — SE NSW to N Queensland
- **Acacia ligulata** (sandhill wattle) — All mainland states
- **Acacia notabilis** (Flinders wattle) — Calcareous rangelands
- **Acacia pycnantha** (Australian golden wattle) — SE Australia; notable for 40% tannin content in bark
- **Acacia ramulosa** (horse mulga) — Arid calcareous zones
- **Acacia salicina** (cooba) — Important fodder and timber tree on alkaline soils
- **Acacia saligna** (golden wreath wattle) — Widely planted for rehabilitation of alkaline and saline lands
- **Acacia sclerosperma** (large seeded cooba) — Alkaline adapted
- **Acacia sibilans** (whispering myall) — Calcareous soils
- **Acacia stenophylla** (river cooba) — From tropics to central Victoria
- **Acacia transluscens** — NW Australia
- **Acacia xiphophylla** — NW Australia

## Eucalyptus Species

Many Eucalyptus species tolerate alkaline soils, particularly those from the mallee regions and semi-arid interior of Australia where calcareous soils are widespread:

- **[[eucalyptus-camaldulensis]]** (river red gum) — One of the most widely planted eucalypts; tolerates a range of soil pH
- **Eucalyptus citriodora** (lemon-scented gum)
- **Eucalyptus diversifolia**
- **Eucalyptus erythrocorys** (illyarrie)
- **Eucalyptus gomphocephala** (tuart)
- **Eucalyptus lansdowneana** (sub-alpine mallee)
- **Eucalyptus leucoxylon** (yellow gum)
- **Eucalyptus nutans** (jilarno)
- **[[eucalyptus-occidentalis]]** (swamp yate) — Notably salt and alkaline tolerant
- **Eucalyptus platypus** (moort)
- **Eucalyptus spathulata** (swamp mallet)
- **Eucalyptus torquata** (coral gum)
- **Eucalyptus woodwardii** (Lemon-flowered gum)

## Casuarina Species (Sheoaks)

Casuarinas are nitrogen-fixing trees that tolerate alkaline and calcareous soils:

- **Casuarina cristata** (black sheoak) — Calcareous adapted; valuable timber
- **Casuarina cunninghamiana** (river sheoak) — Riparian; tolerant of alkaline alluvial soils
- **Casuarina humilis** — Hardy, evergreen; salt spray resistant
- **Casuarina obesa** (swamp sheoak) — Highly tolerant of alkaline and saline conditions

## Eremophila Species (Emu Bushes)

Eremophilas are a genus of Australian native shrubs remarkably well-adapted to alkaline, arid conditions. Many have traditional medicinal uses:

- **Eremophila glabra** (tarbush) — Hardy evergreen shrub
- **Eremophila longifolia** (berrigan) — Hardy evergreen shrub; important Australian Aboriginal cure for colds and sores
- **Eremophila oppositifolia** (twin-leaf emu bush) — Hardy evergreen bush
- **Eremophila santalina** — Alkaline-adapted species

## Grevillea Species

Grevilleas provide both ornamental value and bee forage on alkaline soils:

- **Grevillea ilicifolia** (holly grevillea) — Frost resistant evergreen shrub; honey source
- **Grevillea leucopteris** (plume grevillea) — Hardy evergreen shrub; honey source
- **Grevillea pauciflora** — Frost resistant evergreen shrub; honey source
- **Grevillea vestita** — Hardy evergreen shrub

## Melaleuca Species (Paperbarks)

Several paperbarks tolerate alkaline conditions:

- **Melaleuca lanceolata** (western tea tree) — Honey and pollen production
- **Melaleuca nesophila** — Alkaline adapted
- **Melaleuca wilsonii** — Alkaline adapted

## Ornamental and Useful Trees

### Maples (Acer spp)

Several maple species tolerate alkaline soils, providing autumn colour and timber:

- **Acer negundo**, syn. Negundo aceroides (box elder maple) — Hardy; also noted for other uses
- **Acer platanoides** (Norway maple)
- **Acer pseudoplatanus** (sycamore maple)

### Oaks (Quercus spp)

Oaks are notably tolerant of alkaline soils, particularly:

- **Quercus cerris** (Turkey oak)
- **Quercus ilex** (holm oak, holly oak) — Mediterranean evergreen; extremely alkaline adapted
- **Quercus robur** (English oak)

### Conifers

- **Araucaria heterophylla** (Norfolk Island pine) — Coastal; salt tolerant to beach front
- **Callitris columellaris** (Murray pine) — Hardy evergreen
- **Callitris preissii** (Rottnest Island pine) — Hardy evergreen
- **Cedrus atlantica** (Mt. Atlas cedar) — Tolerates alkaline soils
- **Pinus griffithii**, syn. P. wallichiana (Indian blue pine)
- **Pinus johannis** (Johannis piñon)

### Other Trees

- **Aesculus spp** (horse chestnuts and buckeyes)
- **Albizia lophantha** (crested wattle, spiked acacia)
- **Allocasuarina verticillata** — Alkaline adapted
- **Arbutus unedo** (Irish strawberry tree)
- **Colophospermum mopane** (mopane) — African legume tolerating alkaline clay
- **Cornus mas** (Cornelian cherry) — Deciduous tree with edible fruit
- **Crataegus cordata** (Washington thorn) — Close relative of hawthorn; scarlet berries persist into winter; prefers cool climate
- **Crataegus oxyacantha** (English hawthorn) — Honey; berries as poultry feed; seedlings woven into hedges
- **Derris indica** (Indian beech) — Alkaline tolerant
- **Fagus sylvatica** (common beech, European beech)
- **Fraxinus excelsior** (European ash) — Frost resistant deciduous tree to 40 m; winged fruits pickled; edible manna
- **Fraxinus ornus** (manna ash) — Frost resistant deciduous tree to 16 m
- **Gleditsia triacanthos** (honey locust) — Nitrogen-fixing; alkaline tolerant
- **Melia azedarach** (cape lilac, white cedar)
- **Morus nigra** (black mulberry)
- **Phoenix dactylifera** (date palm) — Naturally grows in highly alkaline desert soils
- **Phyllostachys bambusoides** (Madake giant timber bamboo)
- **Pistacia atlantica** (butum, Mt. Atlas pistachio) — Native to calcareous mountain soils
- **Populus alba** (white poplar) — Alkaline tolerant
- **Sorbus aria** (white beam) — Good for windswept coastal and high pollution areas; fruit for jellies, brandy, and vinegar

## Shrubs and Small Plants

### Australian Native Shrubs

- **Agonis flexuosa** (peppermint tree) — Honey production
- **Atriplex nummularia** (old man saltbush) — Salt and alkaline tolerant forage shrub
- **Banksia ashbyi** — Nectar in spring
- **Banksia integrifolia** (coast banksia) — Good dark honey in autumn to winter
