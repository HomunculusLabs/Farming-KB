## Making Connections Between Components

To enable a design component to function, we must put it in the right place
and arrange connections. The essentials are:

- That the inputs needed by the component are supplied by other components
  in the system
- That the outputs of the component are used by other components

For living components, right placement may be enough (ducks in a swamp may
take care of themselves). For non-living components, we must also arrange
connections (a solar collector linked by pipes to hot water storage).

## Placement and Linking Strategies

Having listed all information on a component, we proceed to placement and
linking strategies posed as questions:

- Of what use are the products of this component to the needs of other
  components?
- What needs of this component are supplied by other components?
- Where is this component incompatible with other components?
- Where does this component benefit other parts of the system?
- What connections need to be made between this and other components?
- Where is the best placement for this component?

## Self-Regulation as the Goal

Once components are placed and connected, we can relax and let the system
self-regulate. Regulation may involve confining or insulating the component,
or guiding it by fencing, hedging, or one-way valves. The goal is to
minimise ongoing work while maximising system function.

## Elements of a Total Design

A total design includes:

- **Social components:** Legal aids, people, culture, trade and finance
- **Energy components:** Technologies, structures, sources, connections
- **Landscape components:** Soil, water, vegetation, animals, earthworks

All must be analysed for inputs, outputs, and intrinsics, then placed in
relation to each other for mutual benefit.

## The Ground Strategy of Relative Placement

The overall strategy is one of relative placement: positioning every
component where it best serves and is best served by other components. This
eliminates pollution (unused outputs) and extra work (unsupplied inputs) from
the system, creating a self-regulating whole.

## Practical Considerations

Successful implementation of Design by Analysis requires attention to
several practical factors including environmental conditions,
resource availability, and timing. Careful monitoring and
adaptive management help optimize outcomes across varying
conditions. Integration with other system elements enhances
overall effectiveness and creates beneficial synergies that
improve resilience and productivity over time.
