# Smartshops

Smartshops are retail outlets that specialise in psychoactive herbal substances, vitamins,
mineral supplements, and other health products. They are most closely associated with the
Netherlands, where they emerged in the late 1990s as a significant commercial force in the
distribution of [[emcdda-hallucinogenic-mushrooms-emerging-trend-europe]] and other natural psychoactive products. Smartshops
played a pivotal role in the emergence of hallucinogenic mushroom use as a widespread drug
trend in Europe during the late 1990s and early 2000s.

## Role in the Hallucinogenic Mushroom Market

Smartshops in the Netherlands were instrumental in kick-starting the hallucinogenic mushroom
trend. By offering psilocybin-containing [[query-how-can-i-grow-mushrooms-alongside-garden-vegetables]] other natural products in a
retail environment, they created a market infrastructure that normalised access to these
substances. It is estimated that there were approximately 120 to 150 smartshops in the
Netherlands, concentrated particularly in Amsterdam. According to the Dutch Risk Assessment
Report, hallucinogenic mushrooms accounted for approximately 50% of smartshop turnover,
making them the single most important product category.

A 2001 study of young people aged 12 and above in the Netherlands found that 64% of those
aged 18 and older who had used magic mushrooms in the previous year had purchased them in
smartshops. Smartshops also sold related products including sclerotia (marketed as
philosopher's stones or truffles), particularly from [[psilocybe-tampanensis-entity]]. Some coffee shops, headshops (which sell drug paraphernalia),
and grow shops (for home [[query-how-does-no-till-cannabis-cultivation-work]]) also sold hallucinogenic mushrooms. In some
areas, paddo cabs operated home delivery services for hallucinogenic mushrooms, reflecting
the degree of commercial sophistication in the Dutch market.

The term philosopher's stone is used commercially to describe the sclerotia of certain

## Online Expansion

Beginning in the late 1990s, many smartshops and related retailers expanded to online sales,
dramatically increasing the geographic reach of their products. A January 2006 snapshot
survey using Google and AltaVista search engines identified 39 online shops selling
hallucinogenic [[cotter-mushroom-products-and-value-added-applications]], with 82% based in the Netherlands. The remaining shops
were primarily in Austria (4 shops selling growkits and spore prints), Germany (2 sites),
and the UK (1 site linked to a Dutch online shop).

These online shops offered international shipping, multiple language versions (primarily
English, French, and German) to attract foreign customers, and a range of products from
fresh mushrooms to cultivation supplies. Website statistics for one Dutch online shop
