## Psilocybe ovoideocystidiata Species Profile

## Overview

Psilocybe ovoideocystidiata is a bluing, wood-inhabiting Psilocybe
species native to the eastern [[forensic-identification-methods-psilocybin-mushrooms-tlc-gc-hplc]], it
colonizes decaying hardwood debris in riparian and floodplain
environments. Its distinctive ovoid to lemon-shaped cheilocystidia
give the species its name and provide a key microscopic
identification feature.

## Taxonomic Background

Psilocybe ovoideocystidiata was first formally described in the late
20th century, distinguishing it from similar species in the section
Omphocystis. It is closely related to other lignicolous bluing
Psilocybe species including P. caerulipes, P. cyanescens, and P.
subaeruginosa. The species name derives from the characteristically
ovoid shape of its cheilocystidia, which are swollen at their apices --
a feature shared with P. cubensis and P. cyanescens but particularly
pronounced in this species.

## Macroscopic Features

**Cap (Pileus):** 1.5-5 cm broad at maturity. Conic to campanulate
when young, expanding to broadly convex or nearly plane with age,
often retaining a pronounced umbo. Surface is viscid when moist from a
gelatinous pellicle, translucent-striate nearly to the disc. Color
ranges from chestnut brown to reddish brown when young, becoming
paler brown to ochraceous with drying. The cap is strongly
hygrophanous, fading dramatically as it dries. Flesh bruises
prominently blue to blue-green where injured.

**Gills (Lamellae):** Adnate to adnexed, subdistant to close, with
one to two tiers of intermediate gills (lamellulae). Grayish when
young, developing purplish brown tones, eventually becoming dark
chocolate brown with [[partial-veil-tropism-protection-before-spore-release]] is
cortinate, leaving a fragile annular zone that may persist as
fibrillose patches or disappear entirely. Flesh is firm, stuffed with
pith when young, becoming hollow with age. Base may develop white
rhizomorphs anchored to woody substrate.

**Flesh:** Whitish to pale brown, bruising blue to blue-green.
Odor is farinaceous (mealy or flour-like). Taste is mild to
slightly bitter.

## Microscopic Features

**[[comparison-spore-print-collection-vs-spore-syringe-production]]:** Dark purplish brown to nearly black in deposit.

**Spores:** Subellipsoid to ellipsoid in side view, ovoid to
subrhomboid in face view. Wall thickness is moderate. Spore
dimensions typically fall in the range of 7-9 by 4-6 micrometers,
with a distinct germ pore.

**Basidia:** 4-spored, though occasional 2-spored basidia may
occur. Clavate, bearing four sterigmata.

**Cheilocystidia:** Very distinctive -- the namesake feature.
Ventricose-rostrate with conspicuously swollen apices, giving a
mucronate to ampullaceous appearance. These swollen-headed cystidia
are 15-36 by 5-10 micrometers, with necks 2.5-4.8 micrometers
thick.

**Pleurocystidia:** Present, similar to cheilocystidia but generally
less abundant. Ventricose-rostrate with swollen apices, dimensions
12-35 by 9-15 micrometers.
