## Supporting Evidence

The authors assembled evidence from multiple domains:

- **Mythological**: The Demeter-Persephone myth describes a grain goddess who withdraws her gifts and then restores them. The grain used in the kykeon was grown in the Rarian plain, where Demeter taught agriculture. The name "Nysa" derives from the pre-Greek language. The narcissus (narkissos) is explicitly linked to narcotic properties.

- **Archaeological**: The telesterion was a vast, darkened hall with tiered seating -- architecture suited to a group visionary experience, not theatrical performance. No expenditures for actors or stage apparatus appear in sanctuary accounts. ## The Comparative Framework: Eleusis and Mexico

A particularly powerful dimension of the theory is its systematic comparison between the Eleusinian Mysteries and the Mazatec mushroom velada. The parallels are extensive:

- **Fasting and purification**: Both required initiates to abstain from certain foods (notably eggs were taboo in both traditions) and to fast before the night of the experience.
- **Darkness and silence**: Both took place at night, in darkness, with strict injunctions against speaking of what transpired.
- **Shared potion**: The kykeon at Eleusis corresponds to the mushroom tea or chocolate-spiked mushroom preparation in Mexico.
- **Physical symptoms**: The described onset -- fear, trembling, nausea, cold sweat, followed by brilliant light and visions -- matches the onset profile of both lysergic acid amides and psilocybin.
- **Bond of fellowship**: Both traditions produced a lasting bond of friendship (cofradía) among those who shared the experience. Greek sources speak of this bond explicitly.
- **Secrecy**: In both cases, the experience was considered ineffable -- "words are useless" to convey what was revealed. The silence was self-enforcing, not merely legally imposed.
- **Single lifetime experience**: At Eleusis, the initiate took the potion only once. In Mexico, while some took mushrooms repeatedly, the experience was always treated as grave and delicate.

A crucial difference: the Mazatec mushrooms served also as oracles, consulted on serious matters, whereas the Eleusinian hierophants saw too many initiates each year to serve as consultants. The Eleusinian experience was oriented toward collective redemption rather than individual divination.

## The 415 BCE Profanation Scandal

The theory draws significant support from the scandal of 415 BCE, when numerous aristocratic Athenians were discovered reproducing the Eleusinian experience at private dinner parties. This incident, recorded by Thucydides and Andocides, demonstrates that the experience was pharmacologically reproducible outside the sanctuary -- the kykeon's effects were not dependent on the unique atmosphere or belief system of the telesterion, but on its chemical properties. The subsequent crackdown with harsh penalties shows how seriously the Athenian state regarded the protection of the Mysteries' secret.
