## The Orchard Hut Investigation

Finding it hard to bear the sight of large red pines near his citrus
groves rapidly dying one after another, Fukuoka set up a small
makeshift lab in one of his orchard huts, gathering test tubes and
petri dishes. He used a basket steamer as his sterilizer and a
kotatsu heater as his room heater.

## The Nematode Misconception

Pine wood nematodes feed on molds that entered through bark wounds,
multiplying explosively. Together with invading molds, they destroy
the resin ducts within the tree, creating severe wilting that kills
it during a hot, dry summer. But the nematodes are secondary invaders,
not the primary cause. At least two to three years before nematodes
invade, the roots have already begun to decay, with few external signs
because the leaves remain green.

## Imported Pathogens

Most pine wood-decaying molds involved in pine rot are not native
to Japan, appearing to have entered on imported lumber. During
his 1979 U.S. visit, Fukuoka ascertained the presence of both
these organisms and pine wood nematodes in American trees. The
identical disease pattern was observable in California, where
almost all pines were already dead, hit approximately ten years
earlier than in Japan.
