# McKenna on Catalytic Psychedelics and Culture

The concept of psychedelics as cultural catalysts is the unifying theoretical
framework of [[psychedelic-mushroom-set-and-setting]] argues that psychoactive plants do not cause consciousness
but rather catalyze processes already present, dramatically accelerating the
emergence of language, religion, social bonding, and cultural innovation. This
"catalytic model" underpins his [[mckenna-transcendent-other-and-exopheromones]]
-- chemical messengers that act across species lines rather than within a
single species. If hallucinogens function as interspecies chemical messengers,
then the dynamic between primate and hallucinogenic plant is one of
information transfer from one species to another. The benefits to the mushroom
arise out of hominid domestication of cattle and hence the expansion of the
mushroom's niche.

Where plant hallucinogens do not occur, cultural innovation occurs very
slowly, if at all. In their presence, a culture is regularly introduced to
ever more novel information, sensory input, and behavior, and thus is
bootstrapped to higher and higher states of self-reflection. The shamans are
the vanguard of this creative advance.

## The Interrupted Symbiosis

McKenna frames modernity as the consequence of an interrupted psychophysical
symbiosis between humans and visionary plants. The severing of this
connection -- beginning with the suppression of partnership societies around
5000-3000 B.C. and culminating in the destruction of the Eleusinian
Mysteries in A.D. 268 -- produced the alienation, warfare, and environmental
destruction characteristic of dominator culture. An interrupted psychophysical
symbiosis between ourselves and visionary plants is the unrecognized cause
of the alienation of modernity.

The loss of catalytic psychedelic input left Western civilization with a
steadily narrowing epistemology. The phonetic alphabet reinforced the shift
from gestalt pictographic awareness toward linear, dominator-style thinking.
Science and rationalism became both the triumph and the prison of Western
consciousness. Monotheism became the carrier of the dominator model, and
the psychological development of Western civilization diverged markedly from
the Eastern traditions that maintained techniques of ecstasy.
