## Cold Greenhouse Winter Harvest

## Overview

The cold greenhouse is an unheated structure designed to [[coleman-french-historical-winter-vegetable-production]] ecologically sound and economically viable.

## Cold Houses vs. Heated Greenhouses

Traditional greenhouse production depends on maintaining warm temperatures
year-round through propane, oil, or electric heating. These heated structures
allow warm-season crops to grow through winter, but energy costs are
substantial. Coleman distinguishes three temperature regimes:

- **Cold house**: Completely unheated. Temperature follows outdoor conditions
  with only passive solar gain and thermal mass buffering. Suitable for
  cold-hardy vegetables that tolerate freezing.
- **Cool house**: Minimally heated to maintain temperatures just above freezing
  (approximately 32°F). Provides [[coleman-greenhouse-covering-materials-comparison]] and was among the first researchers to document the
effectiveness of a second inner layer for frost protection. His work
demonstrated that the inner air layer provided insulation comparable to
traditional glass greenhouses, at a fraction of the cost. Coleman credits
Emmert's research as the intellectual starting point for his own winter
harvest system.

## Economic Advantages

The economic case for cold greenhouse production is compelling:

- **Lower capital investment**: Hoop houses with polyethylene covers cost a
  fraction of glass or rigid-panel greenhouses.
- **Reduced operating costs**: No fuel bills, no heating system maintenance,
  no ventilation fans for winter [[cold-hardy-winter-vegetables]] — Species selection for winter production
- [[mobile-greenhouse-system]] — Crop rotation with movable structures
