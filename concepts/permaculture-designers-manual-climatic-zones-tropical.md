# Climatic Zones: Tropical
**Source:** Permaculture: A Designers' Manual, Bill Mollison (1988)
## Overview
Chapter 10 of the Designers' Manual covers tropical and subtropical
climates in detail. Mollison divides the tropics into several sub-types,
each with distinct design challenges and species assemblages. This page
covers the humid tropics, wet-dry tropics, and monsoon tropics.
## Humid Tropics
### Characteristics
- High rainfall (150-400+ inches annually), evenly distributed
- High temperatures year-round (25-35 C / 77-95 F)
- High humidity; persistent cloud cover
- Rapidly leached, low-nutrient soils (except on recent volcanics)
- Dense [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi-in-tropical-forest-restoration]] as climax vegetation
- Major river systems with extensive floodplains
### Design Essentials
The village complex model (Section 10) provides a comprehensive checklist
for humid tropical settlement:
- Housing around compound with processing, storage, threshing, meeting
  house, cooperative store, fuel depots
- Well, piped water, tanks, dam
- Plant nursery
- 0.25-1.0 hectare home garden per house
- Border ranges for chickens, guinea pigs, ducks, rabbits, small pigs,
  pigeons
- Fish ponds under [[permaculture-designers-manual-animal-housing-detailed]]
- Fuelwood plantation with windbreak
### Evolving a Polyculture
Start by ripping, swaling, pitting, damming from the highest point.
Co-plant:
- Tree legumes at 3x3m spacing
- Bananas at 2x2m
- Fruits at 5x8m
- Palms at 10x10m
- [[permaculture-plants-food-forest-ground-covers-bamboos]]: [[comparison-cassava-vs-taro-vs-sweet-potato-vs-yam]], cassava, arrowroot, comfrey at 1-1.5m
- Seed holes: nasturtium, Dolichos, clover, broad bean, buckwheat,
  Umbelliferae, lupin, vetch, dun peas, chilies, pigeon pea
Aim to carpet and overshade ground in 18-20 months. Complex polycultures
work best at small scale with close human attention.
### Key Species
- **Tree crops:** Coconut, breadfruit, mango, citrus, avocado, durian,
  jakfruit, rambutan, lychee, mangosteen
- **Legumes:** Leucaena, Gliricidia, Acacia, Inga, Albizia, Erythrina,
  Sesbania
- **Ground covers:** Sweet potato, cassava, taro, yam, kang kong,
  xanthosoma
## Wet-Dry Tropics (Savannah)
### Characteristics
- Distinct wet and dry seasons
- High temperatures in wet season; cooler in dry
- Erratic rainfall; drought common in dry season
- Soils generally more fertile than wet tropics (less leached)
- Savannah and grassland with [[mollison-designers-gallery-forests-and-valley-bottomland-systems]] along waterways
- Fire is a major ecological factor
### Staples and Production
- Corn, millet, wheat, beans, potatoes, cucurbits, peanuts
- Cattle, goats, sheep, and game products
- Plantation crops: sugar, cotton, peanuts, pineapple, sisal
### Design Essentials
- Small domestic water storage and reticulation
- Hedgerow against winds
- In-crop tree legumes (especially Acacia albida)
- Improved stock varieties and stock management
- Mulch use of grasses; decreased fire frequency
- No-tillage (cut and mulch) grain techniques
- Low bunds for water retention; keyline techniques of flood control
- Tree forage hand-fed to domestic stock
- Reclamation of eroded lands using pioneer species
## Monsoon Tropics
### Characteristics
- Confined to Indo-Thailand region, northern Australia, East Java, Timor,
  southern New Guinea
- Late summer heating causes onshore winds and heavy rains
- Dry (winter) season reverses winds from cool interiors
- 60% of rain falls in summer; 102-1016 cm (40-400 inches) total
- Floods and droughts equally unpredictable
### Vegetation
- Dry-deciduous broadleaves; teak and bamboo once extensive
- Tree canopy less dense than wet tropics; dense understory
- Mangroves at river mouths and low coasts
### Design Essentials
Similar to wet-dry tropics, with emphasis on:
- Water storage for the long dry season
- Shelterbelts for hot dry winds
- Multiple water sources (dams, tanks, wells)
- Building design for monsoon rain (steep roofs, wide eaves, good
  drainage)
## Tropical Soils and Restoration
### Key Soil Problems
- Old, deeply leached soils (not renewed by glaciation)
- Low silica and calcium supply
- Aluminium ions substitute for silica in clays
- Once cleared, humic particles leach to 30% of prior levels
### Restoration Strategies
1. **Restore humus** with green crop and perennials (especially Leucaena
   and tree legumes); grow plants with intercrop to avoid cultivation
   losses
2. **Add small quantities of superphosphate** at frequent intervals; use
   fine crushed basalt, cement powder, and shredded bamboo or cane mulches
   for silica and calcium
3. **Light, frequent fertiliser applications** at 6-week intervals;
   substitute perennial for annual crop; never practice frequent
   cultivation
4. **Use basalt, cement powder, coral, and bamboo mulch** to supply
   essential nutrients and increase soil pH
