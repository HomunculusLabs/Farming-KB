# Climatic Zones: Tropical

**Source:** Permaculture: A Designers' Manual, Bill Mollison (1988)
**Updated:** 2026-04-18

## Overview

Chapter 10 of the Designers' Manual covers tropical and subtropical climates in detail. Mollison divides the tropics into several sub-types, each with distinct design challenges and species assemblages. This page covers the humid tropics, wet-dry tropics, and monsoon tropics.

## Humid Tropics

### Characteristics
- High rainfall (150-400+ inches annually), evenly distributed
- High temperatures year-round (25-35 degrees C / 77-95 degrees F)
- High humidity; persistent cloud cover
- Rapidly leached, low-nutrient soils (except on recent volcanics)
- Dense tropical forest as climax vegetation
- Major river systems with extensive floodplains

### Design Essentials
The village complex model (Section 10) provides a comprehensive checklist for humid tropical settlement:
- Housing around compound with processing, storage, threshing, meeting house, cooperative store, fuel depots
- Well, piped water, tanks, dam
- Plant nursery
- 0.25-1.0 hectare home garden per house
- Border ranges for chickens, guinea pigs, ducks, rabbits, small pigs, pigeons
- Fish ponds under animal housing
- Fuelwood plantation with windbreak
- Strong yards for cattle and pigs at commercial level

### Evolving a Polyculture
Start by ripping, swaling, pitting, damming from the highest point. Co-plant:
- Tree legumes at 3x3m spacing
- Bananas at 2x2m
- Fruits at 5x8m
- Palms at 10x10m
- Ground covers: sweet potato, cassava, arrowroot, comfrey at 1-1.5m spacing
- Seed holes: nasturtium, Dolichos, clover, broad bean, buckwheat, Umbelliferae, lupin, vetch, dun peas, chilies, pigeon pea

Aim to carpet and overshade ground in 18-20 months. Complex polycultures work best at small scale with close human attention.

### Key Species
- **Tree crops:** Coconut, breadfruit, mango, citrus, avocado, durian, jakfruit, rambutan, lychee, mangosteen
- **Legumes:** Leucaena, Gliricidia, Acacia, Inga, Albizia, Erythrina, Sesbania
- **Ground covers:** Sweet potato, cassava, taro, yam, kang kong, xanthosoma
- **Understorey:** Banana, papaya, pineapple, choko, passionfruit
- **Canopy:** Oil palm, coconut, tall timber species

## Wet-Dry Tropics (Savannah)

### Characteristics
- Distinct wet and dry seasons
- High temperatures in wet season; cooler in dry
- Erratic rainfall; drought common in dry season
- Soils generally more fertile than wet tropics (less leached)
- Savannah and grassland with gallery forests along waterways
- Fire is a major ecological factor

### Staples and Production
- Corn, millet, wheat, beans, potatoes, cucurbits, peanuts
- Cattle, goats, sheep, and game products
- Plantation crops: sugar, cotton, peanuts, pineapple, sisal
- Exports: cattle and sheep products, hardwoods from gallery forests
- Herding of low-yield large herds is a major erosion hazard

### Design Essentials
- Small domestic water storage and reticulation
- Hedgerow against winds
- In-crop tree legumes (especially Acacia albida)
- Improved stock varieties and stock management
- Natural herding system of local herd species
- Mulch use of grasses
- Increased tree crop of high forage value
- Decreased fire frequency
- No-tillage (cut and mulch) grain techniques
- Low bunds for water retention
- Keyline techniques of flood control
- Soakage pits and impoundment of run-off
- Tree forage hand-fed to domestic stock
- Development of domestic fuelwood systems near villages
- Reclamation of eroded lands using pioneer species

## Monsoon Tropics

### Characteristics
- Confined to Indo-Thailand region, northern Australia, East Java, Timor, southern New Guinea
- About 8-10% of world's land surface, but large human populations
- Late summer heating causes onshore winds and heavy rains
- Dry (winter) season reverses winds from cool interiors
- 60% of rain falls in summer; 102-1016 cm (40-400 inches) total
- Floods and droughts equally unpredictable

### Vegetation
- Dry-deciduous broadleaves; teak and bamboo once extensive
- Tree canopy less dense than wet tropics; dense understory
- Mangroves at river mouths and low coasts
- Monsoon Australia: scattered eucalypt and Acacia, riverine forests
- Large marsupials, feral buffalo, marsh waterfowl abundant in Australia

### Design Essentials
Similar to wet-dry tropics, with emphasis on:
- Water storage for the long dry season
- Shelterbelts for hot dry winds
- Multiple water sources (dams, tanks, wells)
- Building design for monsoon rain (steep roofs, wide eaves, good drainage)

## Tropical Soils (Section 10.3)

### Key Problems
- Old, deeply leached soils (not renewed by glaciation)
- Low silica and calcium supply
- Aluminium ions substitute for silica in clays
- Once cleared, humic particles leach to 30% of prior levels
- Cation exchange capacity (CEC) affected by pH

### Restoration Strategies
1. **Restore humus** with green crop and perennials (especially Leucaena and tree legumes); grow plants with intercrop to avoid cultivation losses
2. **Add small quantities of superphosphate** at frequent intervals; use fine crushed basalt, cement powder, and shredded bamboo or cane mulches for silica and calcium
3. **Light, frequent fertiliser applications** at 6-week intervals; substitute perennial for annual crop; never practice frequent cultivation
4. **Use basalt, cement powder, coral, and bamboo mulch** to supply essential nutrients and increase soil pH

### Coral Cay Soils
Calcium-rich sands bind phosphate to form insoluble calcium triphosphate (platin). Fine rock phosphate yields more slowly and provides long-term benefits. Return crop wastes as mulch to reduce pH from 8-9 to 6.5-7.

## Animal Barriers in Tropics

Corrals or cattle-goat-sheep barriers strengthened with thorny or distasteful shrubs, planted as a hedge or coppice crop cut into a thorn fence (boma). These provide:
- Stock control without expensive fencing
- Additional forage from coppiced hedge species
- Wind protection and microclimate modification
- Wildlife habitat
