## Mechanical Soil Conditioning Techniques

[[geoff-wallace]] developed a particularly effective soil conditioner. A circular coulter first slits the ground — the soil must be neither too dry nor too wet for this to work properly. The slit is then followed by a steel shoe that opens the ground to form an air pocket without turning the soil over. Seed can be dropped in the thin furrows created by this process, and crops such as beans or corn seeded in this way grow through the existing grass cover without any fertiliser or top-dressing. The beneficial effect comes entirely from the entrapped air beneath the earth surface and the follow-up work of soil organisms and plant roots on the reopened soil.

## The Rehabilitation Sequence

### Initial Treatment

At the end of winter or in autumn after rain, when the soil will support a tractor, a chisel plough is pulled 5–10 cm (2–4 inches) deep over the area. The passes are made on contour parallels or on low slopes, starting in the high valley bottoms and driving slightly downhill toward the ridges. Unless there are absolutely no legumes or grasses already growing, no extra seed is applied. The immediate response is increased root penetration, seed germination, and top-growth of pasture.

### The Graze-and-Chisel Cycle

The rehabilitation proceeds through a repeated cycle:

1. **Chisel** at the initial shallow depth
2. **Hard-graze** the new growth, or cut it and leave it to lie on the surface
3. The shocked plants lose most of their root mass and seal their wounds
4. Dead roots add compost to the soil, as does the cut foliage or animal droppings
5. Soil bacteria and earthworms multiply, feeding on the [[acacia-albida]] and Glyricidia
- **New Guinea**: Casuarina is the primary species
- **Mediterranean**: Farmers use Tamarix

On difficult terrain including boulder fields, dunes, steep slopes, and laterites, mixed forests of legume and non-legume species (citrus, olive, pine, oak) provide the best permanent solution to soil conservation.

## Maintaining Gains

No matter how soil is aerated — whether with a garden fork, a daikon radish, or mechanical power — the advantage of looseness and penetrability can be quickly lost through overstocking, cropping, heavy traffic, or heavy-hoofed animals on wet ground. All of these compress the soil back into a solid state. The only lasting solution lies in following conditioning with permanent, deep-rooted plant systems (forests or prairies) and maintaining good management practices including minimum tillage.

Intense fire, intense stocking, intense cropping, and intensive production all threaten soil structure. Mechanical soil rehabilitation can be either a one-time beneficial process or another way to waste energy every year — the difference depends entirely on the land management practices that follow.
