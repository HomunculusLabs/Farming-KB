## Medicinal Mushrooms Overview and Cancer Treatment Potential

This comprehensive report (Smith, Rowan and Sullivan, University of Strathclyde, May 2002) examines the therapeutic properties of [[medicinal-mushrooms]] with special emphasis on cancer treatments. Commissioned by the Cancer Research Campaign, it reviews Oriental and Western literature on mushroom-derived bioactive compounds.

## Historical Context

### From Plants to Fungi
Many clinically important drugs (aspirin, digitoxin, progesterone, cortisone, [[morphine]]) derive from higher plants. Equally important but less recognized are fungal-derived drugs: antibiotics (penicillin, griseofulvin), ergot alkaloids, and cyclosporin.

### Traditional Use
Chinese Pharmacopeias document the use of well over 100 species of mushroom by practitioners of traditional Chinese medicine for a wide range of ailments. Many mushroom-derived medicinal products are now produced by major Japanese, Korean, and Chinese pharmaceutical companies.

## The Immune System Connection

"During the last two decades there has been an increasing recognition of the role of the human immune system for maintaining good health." Diseases associated with immune dysfunction include:
- Cancer
- Chronic fatigue syndrome
- AIDS/HIV
- Hepatitis
- Autoimmune conditions

Mushrooms have had long and successful medicinal use especially in traditional Chinese clinical medicine for many forms of immune disorders.

## Key Bioactive Compounds

### Mushroom-Derived Polysaccharides
Considered compounds able to modulate animal and human immune responses and inhibit certain tumour growths:
- **Mushroom glucans**: Mostly non-cytotoxic
- **Glucan-protein complexes**: Not necessarily non-cytotoxic

### Immune System Activation
These compounds potentiate both innate (non-specific) and acquired (specific) immune responses, activating:
- **Host cells**: Cytotoxic macrophages, monocytes, neutrophils, natural killer cells, dendritic cells
- **Chemical messengers**: Cytokines (interleukins, interferons, colony stimulating factors) triggering complement and acute phase responses
- **Lymphocytes**: B-cells (antibody production) and T-cells (cell-mediated cytotoxicity)

They can be considered **multi-cytokine inducers** able to induce gene expression of various immunomodulatory cytokines and cytokine receptors.

## Main Products in Clinical Testing

| Compound | Source | Status |
|----------|--------|--------|
| **Lentinan** | Lentinus edodes fruit-bodies | Phase I, II, III trials |
| **Schizophyllan** | [[schizophyllum-commune]] mycelial broth | Phase I, II, III trials |
| **PSK** | Trametes versicolor mycelial cultures | Phase I, II, III trials |
| **PSP** | Trametes versicolor mycelial cultures | Phase I, II, III trials |
| **Grifron-D** | [[grifola-frondosa]] fruit-bodies | Phase I, II, III trials |

All have been through clinical trials mainly in Japan and China, now also in the US. Several are used extensively as **adjuncts to standard radio- and chemotherapy**.

### Mixed Extract Approach
Recent Japanese studies show that mixtures of extracts from several known medicinal mushrooms, taken as supplements, have shown beneficial effects on quality of life for some advanced cancer patients.

## Most Encouraging Observation

"Perhaps the most encouraging observations from most of these studies is the ability of the mushroom-derived polysaccharides when taken prior to and during radiotherapy and/or chemotherapy to significantly reduce the side-effects of these treatments."

## Safety Profile

"Safety criteria for the mushroom polysaccharides have been exhaustively studied with little evidence of any toxicity." In Phase I clinical trials, these compounds demonstrate remarkably few adverse reactions. Several purified mushroom polysaccharides have been in clinical use in Japan, Korea, China and more recently in the USA for several years with no reports of short-term or long-term toxicity.

## Pharmacological Properties

Some of the most recently isolated compounds from medicinal mushrooms show promising:
- Immunomodulatory properties
- Antitumour activity
- Cardiovascular benefits
- Antiviral activity
- Antibacterial activity
- Antiparasitic activity
- Hepatoprotective effects
- Antidiabetic properties

## Modern Scientific Context

"Mushrooms are not a taxonomic group but do include well over 12,000 species which have macroscopic fruit-bodies." Modern scientific studies have expanded exponentially during the last two decades, primarily in Japan, Korea and China but also in the USA.

## Functional Foods Perspective

Mushrooms are increasingly viewed as:
- **Functional foods**: Nutritious with health-benefiting properties
- **Sources of non-invasive medicines**: Physiologically beneficial compounds
- Some are purely medicinal (non-edible) but considered sources of beneficial compounds

## Clinical Efficacy Factors

Will depend on understanding:
- Precise scope of activity verifiable through in vitro and in vivo tests
- Dose range
- Extraction methods
- Source and purity of raw fungal material
- Duration and frequency of administration
- Accuracy in matching extracts to each particular patient based on traditional and modern diagnostic methods

## Western Medicine Gap

"To date, Western medicine has made little use of these products in part due to their complex structure and lack of acceptable pharmaceutical purity." Many products are used worldwide by holistically oriented physicians, chiropractors, herbalists, and naturopathic physicians, but Western oncology has been slow to assess them.

## Report Scope

The report covers:
- Nature of fungi with emphasis on mushrooms
- Medicinally important mushrooms (detailed species profiles)
- Technology of [[mushroom-cultivation]]
- Extraction, development and chemistry of anti-cancer compounds
- Immunomodulatory activities in animals and humans
- Role of polysaccharides in cancer
- Additional medicinal properties
- Regulatory and safety criteria
- Overview of human immune system
- Standard antitumour activity testing
- Cancer chemoprevention

## Holistic Conclusion

"From a holistic consideration, the consumption of whole edible medicinal mushrooms or extracts or concentrates (dietary supplements) may well offer novel, highly palatable, nutritious and health benefiting ingredients to the Western diet as functional foods."
