## Beauveria bassiana: Biological Warfare Agent

Beauveria bassiana has attracted intense interest as a biological control agent
against insect pests. The fungus naturally infects over 200 species of insects
and is considered one of the most promising alternatives to chemical pesticides.
When conidia land on an insect's cuticle, they germinate, produce a penetration
peg that bores through the exoskeleton, and then proliferate within the hemocoel
(body cavity). The insect dies within days, and the fungus erupts from the
cadaver to produce new conidia.

The fungus has been investigated for controlling:
- Colorado potato beetles
- Coffee berry borers
- Termites
- Bedbugs
- Mosquito vectors of malaria and dengue

Research has also explored the potential of Metarhizium species as biological
control agents for locusts in Africa, where swarms devastate crops. Fungal
spores can be formulated into oil-based sprays that are applied from aircraft
over infested areas.

## Mechanisms of Behavioral Manipulation

How do fungi manipulate insect behavior? Research on Ophiocordyceps unilateralis
has revealed that the fungus does not invade the insect's brain directly.
Instead, it forms a network of hyphae throughout the body cavity and surrounds
muscle fibers, effectively taking over the motor system from the periphery. The
fungus produces compounds that interact with the insect's neuromuscular junctions,
triggering the climbing behavior and death grip without needing to enter the
central nervous system. This peripheral takeover is arguably more sophisticated
than direct brain control.
