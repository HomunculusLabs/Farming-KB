# Psilocybe cubensis Street Sample Potency Variability

## Overview

The potency of Psilocybe cubensis obtained from informal ("street") sources
varies enormously, making it impossible for users to predict their dosage
with any accuracy. Bigwood and Beug (1982) analyzed five street samples of
P. cubensis and found psilocybin levels varying by over a factor of ten —
from 0.7 mg/g to 6.2 mg/g dry weight. Psilocin levels were consistently
low across all street samples, ranging from 0 to 0.3 mg/g.

## Factors Contributing to Street Sample Variability

Multiple factors contribute to the observed variability in street sample
potency, many of which are difficult or impossible to control in informal
distribution chains:

**Genetic variation.** Different strains of P. cubensis produce different
alkaloid profiles. Bigwood and Beug found that caps contained roughly twice
the psilocybin of stems in the M.R. and Equadorian strains, while the
Amazon strain showed equal cap-stem distribution. Without strain
identification, users cannot predict potency from species name alone.
