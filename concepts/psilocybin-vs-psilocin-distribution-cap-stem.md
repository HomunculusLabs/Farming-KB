# Psilocybin vs Psilocin Distribution in Cap and Stem

The distribution of [[psilocybe-cubensis]]* is not uniform.

Bigwood and Beug (1982) found that caps generally contain approximately twice as much psilocybin as stems, while psilocin shows a more complex and strain-dependent distribution pattern.

Understanding this tissue-specific distribution is important for both pharmacological research and practical cultivation.

## Strain-Specific Distribution Patterns

Three strains were analyzed for cap-versus-stem [[sandoz-psilocybin-distribution-program]] between cap and stem.

Unlike the other two strains, psilocin appeared only in the cap.

## General Patterns

Despite strain-specific differences, several general patterns emerge:

### Psilocybin Concentration

Caps generally contain more psilocybin per gram of dry tissue than stems.

The cap-to-stem ratio ranges from approximately 1:1 to 2.3:1 depending on strain.

The cap is the primary site of spore production, which may relate to alkaloid accumulation.

### Psilocin Distribution

Psilocin distribution is inconsistent between strains.

In two strains (M.R. and Ecuadorian), psilocin was exclusively in the stems.

In the Amazon strain, a trace of psilocin was found only in the cap.

The higher psilocybin concentration in caps may reflect the metabolic demands of spore production.

**Spore development**: Tryptamine compounds could serve as precursors or regulators in sporogenesis.

**Pigmentation**: Some tryptamine derivatives are involved in fungal pigment biosynthesis.
