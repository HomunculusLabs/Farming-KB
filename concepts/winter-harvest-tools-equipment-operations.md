# Winter Harvest Tools, Equipment, and Farm Operations

## Overview

The practical success of a year-round [[winter-harvest-system]] depends as much on the right tools and efficient farm operations as on crop selection and [[greenhouse-design]]. The Winter Harvest Handbook provides detailed guidance on hand tools, mechanized equipment, weed control methods, and operational workflows that together enable a small farm team to maintain productive harvests through the most challenging months of the year. [[eliot-coleman]]'s decades of experience are distilled into specific, practical recommendations.

## Essential Hand Tools

### Cultivation and Weed Control Tools

**Hoes** — The primary weed control tools for a winter harvest operation:
- **Collinear hoes** (154, 156) — The preferred hoe design for efficient weeding between crop rows. The collinear blade lies parallel to the soil surface, allowing the operator to slice weeds just below the surface with minimal soil disturbance
- **Wire weeders** (84, 154-156) — Specialized tools for precision weeding close to crop plants without damaging roots
- **Functionality principle** — Every tool should be designed for maximum functionality (192, 194); Coleman emphasizes choosing tools that work efficiently and reduce operator fatigue

**Flame weeders** (100, 151-154) — A propane-powered weed control tool that passes a flame over young weeds, killing them through thermal shock without disturbing the soil. Particularly valuable for:
- Pre-emergent weed control in direct-seeded crops like carrots and onions
- Organic weed management without soil disturbance
- Quick, efficient weed control in large planting areas
- Effective on small weed seedlings; less effective on established weeds

### Soil Preparation Tools

**Broadforks** (135-137) — A heavy-duty U-shaped tool with long tines used to deeply aerate soil without inversion. The broadfork:
- Loosens compacted soil to improve drainage and root penetration
- Preserves soil structure by not turning the soil over
- Allows air and water to penetrate deep into the soil profile
- Is the primary tool for bed preparation in the organic system
- Requires significant physical effort but produces superior soil conditions

**Tillers and the Tilther** (134-137) — While power tillers are available, Coleman has developed preferences:
- The **Tilther** is a specialized shallow-tilling tool for surface soil preparation
- Conventional tillers can be used but may damage soil structure if overused
- The principle is to minimize soil disturbance while maintaining good tilth

**Rakes** (137) — Essential for leveling beds, incorporating amendments, and creating fine seedbeds for direct seeding

**Rollers** (143, 145, 149-150) — Used after seeding to ensure good seed-to-soil contact, which improves germination rates. Particularly important for small-seeded crops like lettuce and carrots.

### Planting Tools

**Dibbles** (83-84) — Hand tools for making planting holes for transplants. Used extensively for leeks and other crops that are transplanted at specific spacing intervals.

**Pinpoint seeders** (141-143, 145-147) — Precision hand seeders that allow accurate placement of individual seeds at desired spacing:
- Dramatically reduce seed waste compared to broadcast seeding
- Eliminate or greatly reduce the need for thinning
- Enable accurate in-row spacing for optimal plant density
- Multiple seed plate options accommodate different seed sizes

**Six-row seeders** (147, 149-150) — A multi-row seeder that can plant six rows simultaneously, significantly increasing planting efficiency for larger plantings.

### Harvesting Tools

**Knives** — Victorinox knives (158) are specifically recommended for harvesting greens:
- Sharp, high-quality knives make clean cuts that minimize damage to remaining plants
- Essential for [[cut-and-come-again]] harvesting of salad mix and braising mix
- Clean cuts heal faster and reduce disease entry points

**Forks** (194) — Garden forks for lifting root crops (potatoes, carrots, beets) and for turning compost

**Trowels** (189-192) — Hand trowels for transplanting, weeding in tight spaces, and various small-scale operations

**Scythes** (139, 156, 192) — Used for cutting cover crops, managing field edges, and general vegetation management

## Floating Row Cover Systems

### Types and Properties

Row covers are a critical component of the winter harvest system, providing 2-8°F of frost protection within greenhouses:

- **Spun-bonded polypropylene** — The standard [[floating-row-cover]] material; lightweight, breathable, and reusable
- **Polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) row covers** (62-63, 158) — An alternative material with different thermal and light transmission properties
- **Aluminized cloth row covers** (60-61) — Reflective covers that provide additional thermal benefits by reflecting ground heat back toward plants

### Support Systems

- **Wire wickets** (5-6, 29, 60-61, 129) — Bent wire supports that hold row covers above the crop, preventing abrasion and allowing air circulation
- **Clothespins** (60-61) — Simple but effective fasteners for securing row covers to wire supports

### Cost Considerations

Row covers represent a significant annual expense (61, 63), but their contribution to frost protection and crop quality justifies the investment. They are reusable for multiple seasons with proper care.

## Quick Hoop Construction

Quick hoops are the most economical season-extension structure in the winter harvest system:

### Components
- **PVC conduit supports** (125) — Bent into hoop shapes and inserted into the soil
- **Floating row covers** — Stretched over the conduit hoops
- **Sand bags** (125-127) — Used to secure the cover material along the base of each hoop

### Applications
Quick hoops are used for protecting:
- Carrots, onions, scallions, radishes (122, 124, 126-130)
- Spinach and lettuce (126-127)
- Multiple succession plantings throughout fall and winter

### Advantages
- Extremely low cost per linear foot of protected growing space
- Quick to install and remove
- Highly portable between beds
- Compatible with existing drip irrigation systems

## Greenhouse Infrastructure

### Wiggle Wire Channels

Also known as spring lock channels or poly-lock channels, wiggle wire channels (110, 113, 116) are the standard system for securing plastic greenhouse covers:
- Aluminum channel is fastened to the greenhouse frame
- A flexible wire (the "wiggle wire") is pressed into the channel, gripping the plastic film tightly
- Allows easy installation and removal of covers for ventilation
- Creates a weathertight seal against wind and rain

### Plastic Covers

Greenhouse plastic is the standard covering material (57-59, 70):
- More affordable than glass
- Provides good light transmission
- Can be easily replaced when damaged or degraded
- Different grades available for different durability requirements

### Ventilation Systems

Proper ventilation is essential in all greenhouse types (63-65, 106, 112-113, 116-117):
- Prevents overheating on sunny winter days
- Reduces humidity and associated disease pressure
- Provides CO2 exchange for active plant growth
- Mobile greenhouses can be positioned to optimize natural ventilation

## Sowing and Seed Management

### Seed Sowing Practices

The handbook provides specific guidance on seed sowing (4, 90, 130, 141-143, 145-147, 149-150):
- **Timing** — Sowing dates are critical and must be calibrated to the specific planting site (outdoor, early house, late house, cool house)
- **Depth** — Proper planting depth varies by seed size; rollers ensure good soil contact
- **Spacing** — Both in-row and between-row spacing must be optimized for each crop
- **Germination rates** — Vary significantly by crop, season, and conditions (102, 127, 130, 137, 139-140); overseeding accounts for expected germination failure

### Succession Planting Schedules

Succession planting is the key to continuous harvest:
- **Salad mix** — Succession planted every 2-3 weeks for continuous supply (75, 85)
