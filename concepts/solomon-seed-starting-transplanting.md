## Grow Your Own: The Simple Approach

Solomon recommends raising transplants only for species that benefit from every possible frost-free day: tomatoes, peppers, and eggplants. In short-season areas, also a few melon or [[solomon-potting-mix-and-seedling-soil-recipe]]:
1. Half-fill a 5-gallon bucket with garden soil
2. If not clayey, blend in ~1.5 gallons well-rotted manure or compost
3. If clayey, mix in an equal volume of sphagnum moss (peat moss)
4. Add 1 cup COF per 3-4 gallons of mix
5. End result: ~12 pint pots worth of fertile medium

Using your own garden soil means seedlings that thrive in it from the start are adapted to your conditions. A few losses to soil diseases are acceptable and even beneficial (they eliminate weaker seedlings).

## Seed Quality and Germination Testing

As a former [[seed-company-supplier-reference]] owner, Solomon emphasizes buying from reputable regional seed houses. Mass-market packets from discount stores often have low germination rates or old seed. Test germination by sprouting 10 seeds between moist paper towels; if fewer than 7 sprout, buy fresh seed.
