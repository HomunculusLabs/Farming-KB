- **Corrosiveness:** Acetic anhydride is a strong acylating agent that can cause chemical burns

Because of these hazards, the conversion must be performed inside a purpose-built **safety box** (glove box) with the following features:

- Thick front panel with safety glass window for observation
- Hinged side panels for equipment access
- Long gloves sealed to the front shield for manipulation
- Sparkless electric exhaust fan to evacuate fumes
- Internal fire extinguisher
- Nitrogen gas inlet for creating an inert (oxygen-free) atmosphere
- Three work stations: equipment staging, reaction, and cleanup/sink

### Safety Box Design

The safety box is constructed with:

- **Walls:** Thin plywood, hinged at the top — serves as both access panels and explosion vents
- **Front:** Thick reinforced boards protecting the operator; explosion force is directed through the lighter side panels
- **Atmosphere control:** Anhydrous nitrogen displaces oxygen through an inlet near the top and an exhaust valve near the bottom. When a match cannot be struck inside, oxygen has been sufficiently removed
- **Three stations:**
  - **Right station:** Equipment and chemical staging
  - **Center station:** Active reaction
  - **Left station:** Sink and cleanup

The operator assembles equipment at the right station, transfers it to the center for the reaction, and can process waste at the left. Chemicals are handled exclusively through the sealed gloves after the atmosphere has been purged with nitrogen.

### Acetylation Procedure

#### Equipment

- 500 ml Pyrex round-bottom boiling flask with ground glass fitting
- Tubular condenser with matching ground glass male fitting
- Metal pot (≥2000 ml) for hot oil bath
- Thermometer for oil bath monitoring
- Sparkless electric hotplate
- Rheostat for external temperature control
- Ring stand with clamps
- [[cottonseed-oil]] for the bath
- Acetic anhydride
- Immersible water pump, bucket, and hoses for condenser cooling

#### Process Summary

1. **Assembly:** Equipment is assembled at station one inside the safety box. The cannabis extract is placed in the boiling flask before entry.

2. **Acetic anhydride addition:** After the box is sealed and purged with nitrogen, acetic anhydride (three times the volume of cannabis oil) is poured from a graduated beaker into the flask through the gloves.

3. **Reflux:** The apparatus is moved to the center station. Ice water circulates through the condenser via hoses passing through holes in the box. The cottonseed oil bath is heated slowly via externally-controlled hotplate and rheostat. Refluxing continues for **three hours** at the temperature where acetic anhydride condenses and returns to the flask.

4. **Cooling:** After three hours, the hotplate is turned off and the solution cools to room temperature with ice water still circulating through the condenser (minimum two hours). The apparatus sits for an additional hour.

5. **Distillation of excess acetic anhydride:** The reflux apparatus is converted to a distillation setup. A distillation flask replaces the boiling flask. As the solution is heated, acetic anhydride vaporizes, travels through the condenser, and is collected in an ice-water-cooled receiving flask. Collection continues until no more acetic anhydride evaporates.
