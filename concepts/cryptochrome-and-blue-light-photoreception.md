# Cryptochrome and Blue Light Photoreception

## Overview

Blue light photoreception is one of the most fundamental sensory systems in plants.

It governs directional growth toward light (phototropism), circadian clock
entrainment, stomatal opening, and inhibition of stem elongation. The primary
photoreceptors responsible for blue [[arabidopsis-thaliana]] identified mutants defective in blue

in parallel: cryptochromes and phototropins.

## Phototropins: Directional Light Sensors

Phototropins are serine/threonine kinases that undergo autophosphorylation upon
absorbing blue light. They contain two LOV (Light, Oxygen, or Voltage) domains
that non-covalently bind flavin mononucleotide (FMN) chromophores, plus a
C-terminal kinase domain.

Arabidopsis has two phototropin genes: phot1 mediates phototropism under low
to moderate light, while phot2 functions under high light and additionally
mediates chloroplast relocation and stomatal opening.

When blue light strikes the LOV domains, the FMN undergoes a reversible
cysteine-dependent photochemical reaction activating the kinase domain. This
phosphorylates NPH3 (NON-PHOTOTROPIC HYPOCOTYL 3) family proteins, which
recruit auxin redistribution machinery. Auxin accumulates on the shaded side of
the stem, promoting [[staycare-degradation-of-plant-cell-wall-polymers-by-fungi]] acidification and
expansin activation, causing the plant to bend toward light.

This explains Darwin's experiment at the molecular level: the coleoptile tip
is enriched in phototropin expression, and lateral auxin redistribution from
the tip drives asymmetric growth.

## Cryptochromes: Blue Light Regulators of Development

Cryptochromes are flavoprotein photoreceptors that evolved from DNA photolyase
enzymes, ancient proteins that repair ultraviolet-damaged DNA. Unlike
phototropins, which primarily mediate directional growth responses,
cryptochromes regulate a broad range of blue-light-dependent developmental
processes. They function as central integrators of blue light signals into the
plant's gene regulatory network.

Key cryptochrome-mediated responses include:

- **Circadian clock entrainment**: Cryptochromes absorb blue light at dawn and

  the circadian clock and photoperiod pathway, affecting CONSTANS and FLOWERING
  LOCUS T expression in leaves.
- **Stomatal opening**: Activate plasma membrane H+-ATPases in guard cells,
  driving ion fluxes and water movement for stomatal opening.
