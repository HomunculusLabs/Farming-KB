## Wastewater Composition

A typical analysis of a powdered detergent or a soap could include:
- Sodium or potassium salts or polysulphates
- Silicates, sulphates or bicarbonates
- Borates
- Residual biocides (concentrated in animal fats) e.g. DDT, Dieldren, Hexachlor from dairy cattle
- Additives such as resins (hardeners), scents, dyes, and brighteners
- Faecal bacteria and viral or worm pathogens from washing

This data is from Kevin Handreck, CSIRO Division of Soils (pers. comm. 1979).

## Soil and Plant Interactions

Of the typical greywater constituents, most can be dealt with by soil organisms. However, specific concerns include:

### Sodium and Potassium Salts
- If the basic water supply is already saline, these salts can add to the problem
- At concentrations greater than 1,000 ppm, they can deflocculate soil clays or damage leaves
- Best directed to tree systems rather than vegetable gardens

### Borates
- At greater than 0.5 ppm, can create excessive boron concentration in soils
- Above 1.0 ppm, harmful to soil life and plants
- Avoid using boron-containing detergents where greywater is used on gardens

## Design Strategies

### Soap Selection

Plain soaps should be used on crops wherever possible. More complex pollutants should be routed to tree systems (well-monitored), as woody perennials can cope better than garden vegetables and allow more time for decomposition of long-term pollutants.

In critical areas, especially in arid or delicate environments:
- Create special soaps from unpolluted oils, potash or sodium
- Plant special crops which remove excesses (many water plants)
- Route greywater through biological treatment before passing to soils and streams

### Routing Priorities

1. **Kitchen sink water** - divert to garden and use productively
2. **Handbasin water** - route to banana circles or mulch pits
3. **Shower water** - route to wet food patches
4. **Laundry water** - route to garden or tree systems
5. **Toilet water** - handle separately (septic, biogas, or composting toilet)

### Dirty Water Patch

Sewage can be fed to a mulched 5 x 8m stone pit surrounded by useful species. Mulch can be grown in the pit itself. This creates a productive nutrient-cycling system from waste.

### Subsurface Irrigation

Greywater can be directed through subsurface systems:
- Slot-pipe systems with nylon mesh filters
- Domestic waste channels with plastic sheet bases
- Arbor system half-pipes for tree irrigation

## Irrigation with Greywater

Greywater irrigation is ideal for mulch in home gardens. Mollison notes that months of growth in greywater produces abundant vegetable yields. The key is to know the composition of both soils and soaps to ensure compatibility.

Six months' growth in greywater produces abundant vegetable yields when proper management is maintained. Systems sustained by waste wash-water and grey water can be highly productive.

### Wastewater Ponds

Food and tank overflow plus greywater can irrigate a "wetland" mulch and water plant system. Such systems create habitat for wildlife while processing nutrients.

## Commercial Applications

Greywater use extends to commercial orchard systems. An intensive biomass production system using wastewater can support large-scale tree crops and forage production.

## Design Considerations

There is no blanket policy for greywater use, only specific cases where we can expect to gain yield and also clean up water if we know the composition of soils and soaps. Key considerations:
- Test soil types and drainage capacity
- Analyze detergent and soap constituents
- Match water quality to plant tolerance
- Monitor system performance regularly
- Use buffer zones and biological filtration where needed
- Route the most contaminated water to the most tolerant plants

## Health and Safety

Biological treatment of common contaminants is stressed. The only water safe for us is also safe for other living things. For millennia we have existed on water supplies containing healthy plants and fish, and if we keep natural waters free of faecal and industrial contaminants, we can continue to do so.
