# PF TEK Mushroom Growth Timeline

## Overview

The [[pf-tek]] cultivation cycle follows a predictable chronological sequence from spore inoculation through fruiting and harvest. The complete process spans approximately 4 to 6 weeks under optimal conditions. Understanding each phase and its expected duration allows cultivators to anticipate milestones, diagnose problems, and time their interventions for maximum yield and potency.

## Phase 1: Spore Germination (Days 1 to 7)

After inoculation, the spore solution sits within the wet substrate, and viable spores begin to hydrate and germinate. At approximately 70 degrees Fahrenheit, germination typically begins within 3 to 5 days, though a full week may pass before visible signs appear.

### What to Expect

Germinating spores first appear as small white fuzzy spots against the darker substrate. These spots quickly develop cottony white growth and strandy rhizomorphs that spread outward. The rhizomorphic growth pattern indicates healthy, vigorous colonization.

### Temperature Considerations

Room temperature is generally acceptable for germination. The critical rule is to avoid overheating rather than to provide warmth. Cold temperatures slow germination but do not prevent it. If indoor temperatures are low, overhead lighting directed onto the jar tops can provide gentle supplemental warmth, but temperature must be monitored with a thermometer to ensure it does not exceed 70 degrees Fahrenheit.

### Light Requirements

Only indirect light is needed during this phase. Direct sunlight should be avoided, as it can overheat the jars and potentially damage developing mycelium.

## Phase 2: Colonization (Weeks 1 to 3)

After germination, the mycelium spreads through the substrate, consuming the brown rice flour and colonizing the vermiculite matrix. Complete colonization of the cake typically takes 2 to 3 weeks from inoculation.

### Colonization Progress

The mycelium expands radially from each inoculation point. With four inoculation sites, the expanding fronts eventually merge, creating a fully white cake. The vermiculite structure allows the mycelial threads to stretch across air spaces, creating a spongy, well aerated fungal mat rather than the dense mass that forms in grain only substrates.

### Monitoring for Problems

During colonization, cultivators should watch for:

- Colored growths (green, blue, black, pink) indicating contamination
- Foul sour odors detectable through the dry vermiculite layer, signaling bacterial contamination
- Halted growth without visible contamination, suggesting substrate drying from overheating during sterilization
- Excess water pooling at the jar bottom under a tight lid

During this phase, the cultivator must decide whether to keep lids tight or loose based on [[pf-tek-maximum-fruiting-substrate-formula]] formula with its higher water content typically requires a loose lid during incubation.

## Phase 3: Fruiting Initiation and Pinning (Weeks 3 to 5)

After the substrate surface turns fully white with mycelium, the jars are left in indirect light. The mycelium continues to infiltrate the substrate until it accumulates sufficient resources to trigger the fruiting cycle. This transition period can range from less than a week to several weeks.

### Pinning

Tiny white pin like structures begin to appear on the cake surface. This pinning phase represents the shift from vegetative mycelial growth to reproductive [[fungal-fruiting-body-formation-environmental-triggers]]. The pins are the earliest visible expression of the [[dual-chambered-terrarium-design]], the fruiting cycle enters its most active phase, lasting approximately 2 weeks.

### Early Fruit Body Development

The first mushrooms to form are often abhorts: convoluted caps, gnarly stems, and stunted growth forms. Despite their unusual appearance, abhorts are exceptionally high in psychoactive alkaloids. Alongside abhorts, mutant forms may appear as blobs of fungus with little or no cap formation, also suitable for harvest.

### Normal Sporocarp Development

Healthy specimens develop with recognizable [[pleurotoid-mushroom-morphology-gilled-fungi]]: expanding caps, elongating stems, and eventually gill development. These stately specimens are visually impressive but are not as potent as the smaller immature forms.

### The Initial Flush

The initial flush is the primary fruiting event. After it completes, the mycelium cake begins to turn blue and no further mushrooms form. If the cake is thoroughly cleaned after the initial fruiting, secondary fruit bodies may form, but they are usually sparse and small.

### Harvest Timing and Potency

The secret to potent mushrooms is harvesting them young. Scientific evidence shows that small immature specimens are significantly more potent than larger mature ones. Harvesting before the veil under the cap breaks ensures maximum [[abort-harvesting-alkaloid-concentration-pf-tek]]. Mature specimens that have begun sporulation, with darkened caps and purple spore deposits, are past their prime for potency but remain useful for spore collection.
