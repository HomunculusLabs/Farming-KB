## Contamination After Inoculation

When contamination appears after successful inoculation of properly sterilized jars, there are two primary causes:

### Contaminated Syringe

The spore syringe itself may carry contaminant organisms. This can occur from:

- Improper [[flammability-hazard-alcohol-lysol-flame-sterilization-mycology]] between jars
- Syringes that have been stored too long or at improper temperatures

If multiple jars inoculated from the same syringe show similar contamination patterns at similar times, the syringe is the likely source.

### Breached Vermiculite Barrier

The dry vermiculite contaminant barrier may have been disturbed during inoculation, allowing airborne contaminants to reach the wet substrate. This can happen when:

- The needle is inserted too deeply and disrupts the barrier from below
- The needle is withdrawn at an angle that pulls vermiculite aside
- The syringe plunger pressure forces substrate upward through the barrier
- The top layer is accidentally disturbed when removing or replacing tape

If contamination appears at specific inoculation points while other points remain clean, a localized barrier breach is the probable cause.

## Non Germination of Spores

When spores fail to germinate entirely, the problem may not be contamination but rather one of several environmental or procedural factors:

### Insufficient Inoculation Depth

The spore solution may have been absorbed by the non nutritive top vermiculite layer rather than reaching the substrate below. This occurs when the needle tip is not inserted deep enough. The solution must flow down along the sides of the substrate cake below the dry vermiculite barrier.

### Heat Killed Spores

If jars are inoculated while still hot from sterilization, the spores are killed on contact. Jars must be cool to the touch before inoculation. This is one of the most common beginner mistakes.

### Transit Heat Damage

Spore syringes can be exposed to killing temperatures during shipping. Rare but possible causes include:

- Overheated airplane cargo holds during intense heat waves
- Superheated mail trucks parked in direct sun all day
- Syringe packages left inside a sun heated mailbox

### Extreme Cold

While spore syringes can survive freezing, extreme low temperatures below freezing are probably destructive to the spores. Syringes should be stored in cool, dark conditions but not subjected to deep freezing.

### Spore Age and Viability

[[psilocybe-cubensis]] spores begin to degrade a few months after collection. After approximately 1.5 years, germination will be greatly reduced or will not occur at all. Fresh spores germinate massively and quickly, making spore age an important factor in non germination cases.
