# Garden Planning for Calorie Production

## The Half-Acre Benchmark

Steve Solomon begins with a rough gauge: about 2,700 square feet (250 square meters) of growing beds can produce approximately half the daily calories for two adults, provided the garden is in a climate similar to the United Kingdom. Britain's cool, cloudy summers mean most vegetables grow more slowly than in the US, but mild winters allow year-round harvesting of frost-hardy crops.

The British wartime allotment of 2,700 square feet was sized so that a family [[query-how-to-protect-plants-from-frost-and-extend-the-growing-season]] is effectively year-round.

**Continental climates** (most of [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]] east of the 98th meridian): Growing seasons are compressed. No winter garden without glass. This means roughly doubling the growing area to compensate -- about 700 additional square feet (65 square meters) per adult in the family. No irrigation needed in the rainy eastern half.

**Dry climates** (western North America): Irrigation is needed. This increases by about 500 more square feet (45 square meters) per adult to account for the area required for irrigation paths, and to grow irrigation-demanding crops like tomatoes, peppers, and corn that would not succeed without watering.

## The Potato: King of Calorie Crops

Solomon identifies the potato as the single most important calorie crop for home-scale food production. He states that it "produces many times more actual nutrition per acre than any other staple crop except rice." The potato's role in European history demonstrates its efficiency: it allowed a family to feed itself on less than an acre, driving rapid population growth.

Potatoes can contain up to about 11 percent protein (dry weight), matching the protein content of human breast milk. However, the [[coleman-winter-greens-nutritional-quality-cold-sweetening]] depends on variety and soil fertility:

- **Starchy "chipper" varieties** grown with lots of moisture and excess potassium produce larger yields of lower-protein, lower-mineral spuds.
- **Waxy "boiling" varieties** (often yellow-fleshed) grown with reduced irrigation after tuber formation begins, on mineral-rich but low-potassium soil, produce somewhat smaller but considerably more nutritious potatoes.

## Calorie Production Efficiency

Solomon compares the land efficiency of different approaches:

- Vegetables (including potatoes) can produce about 500-1,000 calories per square foot per growing season, depending on crop and climate.
- Cereal grains would require five to ten times as much land per person to produce the same nutrition -- making them impractical for most home gardeners.
- The healthful potato is "really the thing" for home-scale calorie production.

## The Full Self-Sufficiency Calculation

For a family seeking to produce nearly all their calories from the garden:

1. Start with 2,700 square feet (250 square meters) as the baseline for two adults.
2. Double it if the soil freezes solid in winter (no winter garden without glass).
3. Add 500 square feet per adult if irrigation is needed.
4. Factor in the need for crop rotation: Solomon puts roughly one-third of his garden in grass/clover ley at any time, resting and rebuilding soil. This doubles the total enclosed area from about 2,000 to over 4,000 square feet of growing beds, requiring about 6,500 square feet (600 square meters) inside a wildlife-proof fence.
5. Add space for fruit trees, berry bushes, asparagus, and perennial herbs.

Total: roughly 7,000-8,000 square feet (650-750 square meters) per two-adult family for full caloric self-sufficiency.

## Garden Layout for Maximum Calorie Production

Solomon recommends allocating garden space roughly as follows:

- **50-60 percent** to potatoes and other root crops (calorie staples).
- **15-20 percent** to legumes (beans, peas for protein).
- **15-20 percent** to brassicas and leafy greens (vitamins and minerals).
- **5-10 percent** to all other crops (tomatoes, cucumbers, squash, onions, herbs).

This allocation prioritizes calories over variety. For gardeners not seeking full self-sufficiency, the proportions shift toward more variety and less emphasis on calorie staples.

## Practical Considerations

Solomon warns that his calculations assume several conditions:

- Deep, reasonably fertile soil (at least 3 feet/90 cm deep).
- A skilled gardener (experience matters enormously).
- Adequate water supply, whether from rainfall or irrigation.
- Climate-appropriate [[solomon-root-cellaring-food-preservation]] for preserving the calorie harvest
