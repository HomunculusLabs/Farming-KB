## Desert Revegetation

**Desert revegetation** is the process of restoring plant cover to arid and
semi-arid lands that have lost their native vegetation. Masanobu Fukuoka
proposed a distinctive approach to desert revegetation based on scattering
clay-encased [[cervantes-wind-protection-outdoor-cannabis]] for subsequent succession.

The multi-species approach was deliberate. Fukuoka acknowledged that the
specific conditions of any given site could not be predicted in advance. By
including many species in each pellet, some portion would be suited to whatever
conditions actually prevailed. The survivors would then modify the environment
in ways favorable to other species, initiating natural succession.

## Large-Scale Proposals

Fukuoka proposed that seed pellets could be scattered from aircraft over
thousands of square kilometers of degraded land at relatively low cost. He
advocated starting with the deserts of Africa, the Middle East, and the
American Southwest. The approach required no irrigation, no soil preparation,
and no ongoing maintenance — in stark contrast to conventional reforestation,
which typically involves nursery seedlings, irrigation systems, and years of
tending.

He argued that speed was essential. The conventional approach of first studying
desertification scientifically, identifying causal chains, and designing
targeted interventions would take decades and might never produce actionable
results, because ecological causation is too complex and interconnected for
human analysis to fully comprehend. Better, he argued, to act immediately with
a simple intervention that aligns with natural processes and let nature select
the appropriate outcomes.
