# Bionutrients and [[fermented-plant-extract]]s

Bionutrients are naturally derived plant and animal-based preparations used in natural farming to supply essential macro- and micronutrients, growth hormones, and beneficial microorganisms to crops and livestock. Unlike synthetic fertilizers, bionutrients are produced through simple fermentation processes using locally available materials—making them accessible, affordable, and ecologically sustainable. They form a core pillar of [[korean-natural-farming]] (KNF) and Philippine natural farming traditions.

## Fermentation vs. Simple Extraction

A critical distinction in natural farming is between **fermented extracts** and **simple teas or infusions**. A tea or infusion involves steeping plant material in water to draw out water-soluble compounds; this is passive and extracts only a fraction of the available nutrients. Fermentation, by contrast, is an active biological process where microorganisms break down plant or animal tissues over several days to weeks, releasing a far broader spectrum of active compounds—including amino acids, enzymes, organic acids, and growth hormones—that would otherwise remain locked inside cellular structures. Fermented extracts are significantly more potent, stable, and bioavailable than simple teas.

## General Fermentation Formula

The universal baseline formula for producing fermented plant or animal extracts is straightforward: combine the raw material with **crude sugar or molasses at a ratio of 1 part sugar to 3 parts material by weight**. The sugar serves as both an osmotic extractant—drawing juices and active compounds out of the raw material—and as a food source for beneficial microorganisms that drive fermentation. Molasses is often preferred over crude sugar not only for its lower cost, but because it helps suppress foul odors and contributes additional trace minerals. The mixture is typically left to ferment in a sealed container at ambient temperature for 5 to 14 days, depending on the material, then strained and diluted before use.

## Papaya Fermented Extract

Papaya is a versatile material for producing fermented plant extract. The procedure is as follows:

1. **Harvest** ripe or near-ripe papaya fruit.
2. **Chop thin**—slice the papaya into small, thin pieces to maximize surface area for extraction. Do **not wash or peel** the fruit; the skin and any surface microorganisms contribute to the fermentation process.
3. **Mix with sugar**—combine the chopped papaya with crude sugar or molasses at the standard 1:3 ratio (1 part sugar to 3 parts papaya by weight).
4. **Ferment** for approximately **one week** (7 days) in a sealed container away from direct sunlight.
5. **Strain** the liquid extract from the solid residue. The liquid is your fermented papaya extract.
6. **Dilute** at a ratio of **1:20** (one part extract to twenty parts water) before spraying on plants or applying as a soil drench.

Papaya extract is valued for its enzyme content (particularly papain) and broad spectrum of nutrients.

## Kangkong (Water Spinach) — Growth Promotant

Kangkong (*Ipomoea aquatica*), also known as water spinach, is used in natural farming primarily as a **plant growth promotant**. It is a fast-growing aquatic or semi-aquatic vegetable, and its rapid vegetative growth signals high concentrations of natural growth hormones. In scientific terms, these include:

- **Gibberellins** — promote stem elongation and cell division
- **Auxins** — regulate root development and apical dominance
- **Cytokinins** — stimulate cell division and delay senescence

Plants that exhibit exceptionally fast growth—such as kangkong, kelp, and mugwort—naturally accumulate greater concentrations of these hormones. Other good materials for growth-promoting fermented extracts include the axillary buds of cucumber, squash, and watermelon. Once fermented using the standard sugar method, the extract can be sprayed as a foliar feed or watered into the soil, producing noticeable improvements in plant vigor and vegetative growth.

## Banana-Squash-Papaya (BSP) Fermented Extract — Flowering and Fruiting

The **BSP fermented extract** is one of the most important preparations for supporting the **flowering and fruiting stages** of plants, especially vegetables. Banana is notably high in **potassium**, while squash and papaya contribute **beta carotene** and additional micronutrients. In conventional Philippine agriculture, potassium nitrate is used to induce flowering in mangoes; natural farmers have achieved similar results using BSP extract, seaweed extract, and other potassium-rich fermented materials. The high potassium content in these fruits mirrors the nutrient demands of plants entering their reproductive phase.

For experimentation in other regions, substitutes such as **comfrey, squash, and carrot** can be used in place of the traditional BSP combination. The guiding principle is to ferment materials that are rich in potassium and natural growth hormones.

## [[fish-amino-acid]] — Nitrogen Source

**Fish Amino Acid (FAA)** is a primary nitrogen source in natural farming. The fundamental rule is straightforward: **the higher the protein content of the raw material, the higher the nitrogen yield** when fermented. Fish scraps—heads, bones, entrails, and offal—are extremely protein-dense and produce a potent nitrogen-rich extract.

**Procedure:**
1. Combine fish scraps with crude sugar or **molasses** at the standard 1:3 ratio.
2. Molasses is strongly preferred over sugar because it significantly reduces the characteristic fishy odor and adds trace minerals.
3. Adding **[[lactic-acid-bacteria]] (LAB)** during fermentation further suppresses foul odors and accelerates the breakdown of proteins into amino acids.
4. Ferment for 2–4 weeks, then strain. The resulting liquid is a concentrated fish amino acid solution.

FAA is used as a foliar spray or soil drench to supply rapidly available nitrogen during the vegetative growth stage. It is analogous to commercially available fish emulsion but produced on-farm at minimal cost.

## Calcium Phosphate — Nutrioperiodism and the Changeover Period

**Calcium phosphate** occupies a uniquely important role in natural farming due to the theory of **Nutrioperiodism**, developed by Japanese horticulturist **Yasushi Inoue** in the 1930s. This theory holds that plants and animals require specific nutrients at precise stages of their development:

| Stage | Plant | Animal Analog | Key Nutrient |
|-------|-------|---------------|--------------|
| Vegetative | Leaf/stem growth | Infantile/juvenile | Nitrogen |
| Changeover | Transition to flowering | Juvenile → adult | Calcium phosphate |
| Reproductive | Flowering/fruiting | Adulthood | Potassium |

The **changeover period**—the transitional phase between vegetative growth and reproductive flowering—is considered the **most critical stage** in determining the quality of the final harvest. During this period, plants experience a kind of "morning sickness" analogous to early pregnancy, where additional nutritional support is essential. Calcium phosphate is the specific nutrient demanded at this juncture. Ash from **soybean stems** is noted as an excellent traditional source.

**Procedure — Eggshell Calcium Phosphate:**
1. Collect **eggshells** and **roast** them until they produce good ash.
2. Soak the roasted eggshells in approximately **equal volume of vinegar**.
3. Allow the mixture to sit for **2 weeks** until the eggshells are fully broken down by the acetic acid.
4. Dilute the resulting liquid extract at **1:20** (one part extract to twenty parts water).
5. Apply as a foliar spray or soil drench specifically during the **changeover period**.

Proper timing of calcium phosphate application during this narrow window improves overall plant health, disease resistance, and final fruit or flower quality.

## Ginger-Garlic Extract — Natural Antibiotic

**Ginger-garlic extract** serves as a broad-spectrum natural antibiotic and fungicide for both plants and animals. Garlic's high **sulfur content** gives it potent antifungal properties, while ginger contributes additional antimicrobial and medicinal compounds.

The original Korean natural farming recipe incorporates Chinese herbs (*Angelica acutiloba, Glycyrrhiza uralensis, Cinnamomum loureirii*) valued for their digestive benefits, but a simplified version uses only equal parts ginger and garlic:

1. **Chop** ginger and garlic into small pieces.
2. **Soak** in **beer or wine** for 12 hours (overnight) to begin extracting active compounds.
3. Add **crude sugar** at the standard 1:3 ratio.
4. **Ferment** for **5–7 days**.
5. Add **alcohol (at least 40% proof / 20% ABV)** to stabilize and arrest fermentation, similar to preparing an herbal tincture in homeopathy. The alcohol performs the final extraction of active ingredients from the plant material.

Applications are extensive: it is used preventively and therapeutically on poultry and livestock, as a treatment for fungal infections on plants, and even for ailments like rheumatism in animals. It functions as both a protective and curative agent.

## Plant Part Potency Hierarchy

The potency of any fermented plant extract depends heavily on **which part of the plant** is used. Natural farmers observe a clear hierarchy of energy and nutritional concentration:

1. **Seed** — most potent; contains the full genetic blueprint and stored energy for germination
2. **Fruit** — second most potent; concentrates nutrients and sugars for seed development
3. **Leaf** — third; active in photosynthesis but less nutrient-dense than seed or fruit
4. Other parts (stem, root, bark) — lower concentrations

This hierarchy directly affects **dilution rates**. For example, fermented grain seed extract is diluted at **1:1000**, whereas leaf-based extracts are typically diluted at **1:500**. More diluted preparations can be applied more frequently—there is no rigid rule, and farmers are encouraged to experiment, observe, and adjust based on their specific conditions and crop responses.

## Designer Compost (Bokashi)

**Designer compost**, known as **bokashi** in Japan, is an improved, nutrient-fortified compost that integrates bionutrients and biodiversed [[beneficial-indigenous-microorganisms]] (BIM) into a concentrated organic soil amendment. Unlike traditional composting, bokashi is an **anaerobic fermentation** process that preserves more nutrients and produces a more potent end product.

### Philippine Base Recipe

| Ingredient | Amount |
|------------|--------|
| Rice Bran | 10 kg |
| Copra Meal (coconut residue) | 20 kg |
| Coco Peat | 20 kg |
| Chicken Manure | 30 kg |
| Charcoal Dust | 20 kg |
| BIM (Beneficial Indigenous Microorganisms) | 1 liter |
| Molasses | 1 kg |
| Bionutrient (custom blend) | 1 liter |

### General Formulation Principle

The recipe follows a fundamental ratio analogous to animal feed formulation:

- **80% carbon source** (carbohydrate equivalent) — rice bran, coco peat, charcoal dust
- **17% nitrogen source** (protein equivalent) — copra meal, chicken manure
- **3% trace elements** (vitamin/mineral equivalent) — rock dusts, bionutrient blends

### Substitutions for Other Regions

Every ingredient can be adapted to locally available materials:
- **Rice bran** → wheat bran or any inexpensive grain bran
- **Copra meal** → corn meal, soybean meal, or any legume meal
- **Coco peat** → peat moss, sawdust, or grain hay (carbon/lignin-rich materials)
- **Chicken manure** → any animal manure (chicken is preferred for its balanced NPK and micronutrient profile)
- **Charcoal dust** → any biochar (serves as a substrate for microbial proliferation)
- **Bionutrient** → customize based on crop goals (e.g., potassium-rich BSP extract for fruiting crops, fish amino acid for nitrogen-demanding crops)

The **real key** to effective bokashi is providing a large, diverse population of beneficial microorganisms supported by ample carbon and organic matter. Molasses feeds the microbial community, while the bionutrient blend tailors the compost to specific crop needs. This enriched compost also produces excellent compost tea for foliar or soil applications.
