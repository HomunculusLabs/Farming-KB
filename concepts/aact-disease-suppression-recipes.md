# AACT Disease Suppression Recipes

Updated: 2026-04-18

Dr. Elaine Ingham's approach to disease suppression using actively aerated compost tea (AACT) is based on restoring the full soil food web so that natural biological controls prevent disease organisms from gaining a foothold. Different diseases require different biological approaches.

## The Fundamental Principle

Disease suppression with compost tea works by establishing beneficial organisms on leaf surfaces and in the root zone that occupy the space and resources that pathogens would otherwise use. When the beneficial biology is present in adequate biomass and diversity, pathogens cannot establish. The approach is preventive rather than curative -- establish the biology before disease pressure arrives.

## Bacterial Diseases: Black Spot

Black spot on roses is controlled primarily by bacterial tea. The bacteria occupy the leaf surface and prevent the black spot fungus from establishing infection sites.

Recipe approach:
- Brew bacterial dominant tea (molasses-based, no humic acid)
- Apply as foliar spray at 5 gallons per acre per month during growing season
- Start applications 2 weeks before bud break
- Continue monthly through the growing season
- If disease outbreak occurs, apply immediately every 3-5 days until resolved
- If disease alert is issued by extension service, apply weekly until alert stops, then continue every 10-14 days until harvest

## Fungal Diseases: Mildew

Mildew requires fungal biomass in the tea. Bacterial tea alone is generally not effective against mildew.

Recipe approach:
- Brew fungal dominant tea (humic acid-based, minimal molasses)
- Ensure adequate fungal biomass through pre-activation of compost with oatmeal
- Apply as foliar spray starting early in the season
- "Getting fungal compost tea on later in the season is the only way not to have problems with mildew"
- Apply weekly when mildew alerts are issued
- If outbreak occurs, apply immediately every 3-5 days until resolved

## Root Diseases

Root diseases are managed through soil drench applications that establish beneficial biology in the root zone:

- Apply 15 gallons of compost tea per acre as a soil drench
- Use a balanced tea with both bacterial and fungal biomass
- Include protozoa to cycle nutrients and maintain bacterial populations
- Apply 2 weeks before planting, then as a top-dress drench on compost in fall
- For established disease, apply every 3 days until resolved

## Thatch Decomposition in Turf

Thatch buildup in turf is a fungal decomposition problem:

- Apply fungal compost tea to decompose thatch instead of physical removal
- Use fungal dominant recipe
- Apply following aeration treatments
- Fill aeration plugs with 30% compost/wormcastings and 70% sand, water in with compost tea
- Overseed with compost tea spray to promote rapid germination

## General Disease Prevention Program

Ingham's general prevention protocol:

1. Fall: Apply 1-5 tons per acre of compost and/or wormcastings as top dressing
2. Pre-planting: Apply 15 gallons of compost tea per acre as soil drench 2 weeks before planting
3. Growing season: Apply 5 gallons per acre foliar spray monthly starting 2 weeks before bud break
4. Disease alerts: Apply weekly until alert stops, then every 10-14 days until harvest
5. Disease outbreak: Apply immediately every 3-5 days until resolved
6. Post-pesticide: If any pesticides are used, apply compost tea 3-5 days later to reintroduce beneficial organisms

## Nutrient Addition During Disease Response

When nutrient deficiencies contribute to disease susceptibility:

- Do a petiole or tissue analysis to identify deficient nutrients
- Add the lacking nutrients directly to the compost tea
- The organisms in the tea increase the plant's ability to take up nutrients faster
- This is especially useful in the first 1-2 years while nutrient cycling is recovering

## Pesticide Recovery

When pesticides (including sulfur-based fungicides) must be used:

- Apply compost tea 3-5 days after pesticide application
- Sulfur always kills some beneficial organisms; increasing sulfur applications leads to more disease unless biology is restored
- By maintaining the food web with compost and wormcastings, no pesticides should be necessary after 4-5 years
- For herbicides: apply compost tea 3-5 days later; fewer herbicides will be needed over time as crops outcompete weeds

## Monitoring and Adjustment

All compost teas should meet SFI minimum standards for bacterial and fungal biomass. Testing before and after application, and leaf surface assays, confirm that the tea is achieving its purpose. Adjust recipes based on test results and plant response.
