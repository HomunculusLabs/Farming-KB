## Overview

**Psilocin Levels:** Notably, the street samples showed consistently low
psilocin levels. In controlled conditions, psilocin reached maximum
concentrations by the fourth flush (up to 2.9 mg/g). In the street samples,
psilocin was either absent (Samples 1 and 2) or present at only 0.3 mg/g.
This may indicate that many street samples come from earlier flushes, where
psilocin has not yet accumulated to peak levels.

## Analytical Methodology

The street samples were analyzed using the same reversed-phase high
performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) methodology as the controlled
cultures. Quantification was performed with a Hewlett-Packard 8380A reporting
integrator-plotter calibrated against standards from the National Institute
on Drug Abuse. The method demonstrated a linear relationship with 10%
repeatability between concentration and peak area from 0.2 to 3 micrograms
of total psilocybin or psilocin, with a detection limit of approximately
0.01 micrograms. Results were qualitatively confirmed by thin-layer
chromatography (TLC) using butanol-acetic acid-water (12:3:5).

## Practical Implications for Dosage

The ten-fold variability in street sample potency means that consumers have
no reliable way to predict the dose they are ingesting based on dry weight
alone. A person consuming 2 grams of Sample 2 would receive approximately
12.4 mg of psilocybin, while the same weight of Sample 3 would deliver only
1.4 mg — a nearly nine-fold difference in active compound intake.
