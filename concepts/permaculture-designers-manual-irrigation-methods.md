# Irrigation Methods
# Permaculture Designers Manual - Garden [[irrigation-systems]]

updated: 2026-04-18

## Overview

Water use in unguarded or unsheltered garden plots is wasteful, as a 10% increase in evaporation is possible on the edges of such plots. There is no doubt that trickle, drip, or seepage irrigation is the most effective dryland garden watering method. The permaculture approach to irrigation emphasizes efficiency, water conservation, integration with [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-aquaculture-and-water-systems]], and the use of [[appropriate-technology]] matched to site conditions and resources.

## Drip Irrigation Systems

### Advantages

Drip irrigation water use, especially in orchard and tree establishment, can be from 10-50% of sprinkler use. The particular advantages are:
- Only low water head/pressure needed
- Water is placed right at the plants
- Reduced evaporation losses
- Compatible with mulch systems
- Can be automated with timers and sensors

### System Types

There are at least two basic drip system types:

**Solid Line with Emitters**
- Extruded plastic hose with drip emitters of various efficiency and flow rate
- Emitters ranging from small sprinklers to individual drip points
- Nylon mesh insert filters (or old stockings in a larger pipe) used as filters
- Automatic, soil-sensing, and nutrient addition or timing systems can be added
- 7- or 8-line sequences possible with automatic timers

**Twin-Pipe "Bi-wall" System**
- A high-pressure pipe joined and perforated to a low-pressure pipe
- The low-pressure pipe is perforated at intervals to suit row crop
- More economical for broadscale row cropping
- Widely available from manufacturers globally

### Salt Considerations

Drip irrigation should be used with caution in waters of more than 800 ppm salt on bare ground, as local salts and carbonates accumulate at the plants. For trees, this effect is overcome by:
- Letting drips fall into stand pipes which let water pool at root level
- Not allowing water to evaporate to air at the soil surface

## Subsurface Irrigation Systems

Underground drip and seep systems, if 12cm or more below the soil surface, are the most conservative method of irrigation. These are best suited to arid sites where solar evaporation is a significant factor.

### Micropore or Seepage Lines

These are designed for valuable row-crop, and have many slits which open under slight pressure. They can be laid:
- Below root level for deep-rooted crops
- At surface for shallow-rooted crops
- Worth the effort for crops such as strawberry, medicinals, and special seeds

### Trickle Irrigation Plus Pot or Pipe

A very successful way to water arid-area tree seedlings:
- Shallow pipe can be used initially
- Replaced with deep pipe to 0.5m as tree grows and root zone extends
- Ideal for primary windbreak or ridge forest establishment
- Species that later stand alone should be used

### Domestic Waste Channels

Successfully used off desert houses:
- A sheet of plastic laid 30cm below the soil surface from a wastewater outlet
- Best in sands or very permeable soils
- Preferably 60cm or less wide
- Non-root crop planted above the plastic
- The channel periodically extended so no surface water appears
- Slotted pipe can carry wastewater along the plastic base

### Arbor System

Commercially available in South Australia:
- Wastewater pipes will not fill with tree roots
- Can be used without gravel seepage beds in ordinary soils
- Ideal for productive trees like citrus, apricot, and palms
- Half-pipes of 20-30cm diameter with crosswalls at 1.25m spacings

### Pot System

Excellent for village gardens, with or without dripline. Combines well with circle gardens. Uses unglazed earthen jars central to small circle gardens, or inverted bottles with leaky tops.

## Nutrient Film and Injection Techniques

These techniques prevent water loss by soil absorption and are essentially hydroponic-intensive:

**Direct Infusion (Israel)**
- Direct infusion of water and nutrients into tree or vine stems (nutrient drip)

**Plastic Film Technique (India)**
- Used to grow Napier grass (Pennisetum) for forage
- Part of root mass in earth, part in nutrient solution in plastic gullies

**Wick Systems**
- Slotted pipe using hessian or nylon wicks to nearby plant roots
- Little or no water lost to soil
- Plant roots explore both soil and nutrient solution

## Flood Irrigation

Flood irrigation of swales, or flood and soak regimes to orchards, can be effective where water is abundant. For every irrigated orchard, there is a [[permaculture-designers-manual-ponds-and-dams]] system that provides flood irrigation capacity. A pan system (a dish that fills up and holds water) is a great help in flood-irrigated orchards.

Flood irrigation under conditions that leach salt is one of two methods used to reclaim saline soils. Both methods involve either leaching salts below root zone or planting salt-tolerant species.

## Sprinkler Systems

Conventional sprinkler systems are the least efficient method but have their place:
- Useful for establishing ground covers and pasture
- Necessary for some frost protection strategies
- Combined with pattern design (circle gardens, spiral gardens), sprinkler efficiency increases dramatically
- A 4m row of crop needs three 1.2m sprinklers, while a circle of 0.6m radius (4m circumference) needs only one 1.2m sprinkler, saving 2/3 of water

## Automatic Irrigation

In areas where electronic sensors are impractical or expensive, permaculture systems use:
- Timer taps on simple driplines
- Float valves on tanks
- Gravity-fed systems from elevated tanks
- Wicking beds and self-watering containers
- Swales and earthworks that distribute water passively

## Water Sources for Irrigation

Irrigation water can come from multiple integrated sources:
- [[permaculture-designers-manual-rainwater-harvesting-detailed]]
- [[permaculture-designers-manual-ponds-and-dams]]
- [[permaculture-designers-manual-crafts-and-income]]
- Well or bore water
- Stream diversion
- Swale storage

## Water Conservation Summary

The most water can be saved by:
- Using the least possible in gardens (shrubberies instead of lawns, sub-surface trickle irrigation, mulch, and shade) - saves about 50% total water use
- Saving toilet flushing water (hand-basin water fed to low-flush cistern) - saves 40% of remaining water use
- Diverting bathwater to pre-soak trough for clothes
