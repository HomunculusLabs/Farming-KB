# Azurescens, Cyanescens, and Woodlovers Comparison

The [[permaculture-designers-manual-temperate-climate-design]] species that grow on decaying hardwood debris in half-shaded environments.

## Species Comparison Table

| Feature | P. azurescens | P. cyanescens | P. subaeruginosa |
|---------|--------------|---------------|------------------|
| Discovery | 1979, Stamets & Gartz | Long known | Long known |
| Native Range | Columbia River basin, Oregon coast | Pacific NW, now worldwide | Australia, New Zealand |
| Natural Habitat | Coastal dune grass, hardwood/conifer chips | Decaying alder chips, [[comparison-ramial-wood-chip-mulch-vs-composted-wood-chips]] mulch |

| Stem Length | 90-200 mm | 40-90 mm | Variable |

| Total Alkaloids | Up to ~25 mg/g (highest known) | High | High |
| Cultivation Difficulty | More difficult | Moderate | Robust, prolific |

### Key Differences

| Parameter | Azurescens Guide | Woodlovers Guide |
|-----------|-----------------|------------------|
| Spawn ratio | 1:5 (4x more spawn) | 1:20 |
| Container | Jars | Bags |
| Sterilization | 1+ hours | 3 hours |
| Bed cover | Cardboard + plastic | Plastic only |
| Supplementation | None mentioned | Oatmeal/grass seed |
| Spawn chip size | Fresh cut hazelnut | Small beech bedding |
| Bed chip size | Fresh cut hardwood | Large beech bedding |

The azurescens guide's higher spawn ratio likely reflects the species' slower colonization compared to more aggressive woodlovers. The cardboard layer in the azurescens method provides both moisture retention and an additional colonizable medium.
