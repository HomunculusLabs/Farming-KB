---# Sequestrate Fungi Taxonomy and Geographic Distribution

The [[sequestrate-fungi]] occur in three major groups:

**Zygomycota**: Endogonales (Endogone), Glomales (Glomus,
Sclerocystis), and Mucorales (Modicella)

**Ascomycetes**: Elaphomycetales (Elaphomyces), Glazieliales
(Glaziella), and primarily Pezizales. The Pezizales includes
Tuberaceae (Tuber, Terfezia, Choiromyces), Pezizaceae
(Tirmania, Hydnoplicata, Sphaerozone), and several other
families with sequestrate genera

**Basidiomycetes**: Primarily Agaricales (Cortinariaceae,
Russulaceae, Tricholomataceae, Boletaceae, Strobilomycetaceae),
Lycoperdales (including Mesophelliaceae), Phallales
(Hysterangiaceae), and Sclerodermatales

Many genera do not fit into described families and are listed
under "uncertain status." Numerous undescribed genera are known
from field collections.

## Host Specificity and Richness

Presence of ectomycorrhizal host plants is strongly associated
with sequestrate fungal richness. Because many sequestrate fungi
are specific to individual host genera, a diversity of host
genera in a region contributes to richness. Moist forests
generally support greater diversity than dry ones.

Australia and North America provide a striking comparison of
parallel evolution. The Suillus-Gastrosuillus-Truncocolumella-
Rhizopogon line is host-specific to Pinaceae, absent from
Australia. The Descolea-Setchelliogaster-Descomyces line appears
host-specific to Eucalyptus, absent from North America. The
Russula-Macowanites-Gymnomyces, Lactarius-Arcangeliella-
Zelleromyces, and Cortinarius-Thaxterogaster-Protoglossum
lines occur on both continents, although no species are shared.
