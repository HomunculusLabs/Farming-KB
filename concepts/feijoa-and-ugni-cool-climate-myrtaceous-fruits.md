# Feijoa and Ugni — Cool-Climate Myrtaceous Fruits

## Overview

Two small, evergreen shrubs from the family Myrtaceae offer exceptional cold tolerance and unique fruit flavors for permaculture systems in cool temperate and subtropical regions: the feijoa or pineapple guava (*[[holzer-fruit-growing-marginal-climates]].

## Feijoa (*Feijoa sellowiana*)

### Botanical Characteristics

The feijoa, also known as pineapple guava, is a bushy evergreen shrub reaching 1–6 meters in height. Native to southern Brazil, northern Argentina, western Paraguay, and Uruguay, it is cultivated in the highlands of Chile, New Zealand, and Australia.

### Environmental Requirements

- **Soil**: Prefers deep, rich organic soil kept moist in summer
- **Drought**: Tolerant but produces little fruit under drought stress
- **Frost**: Remarkably cold-hardy, tolerating temperatures to -11°C
- **Root system**: Shallow and fibrous — benefits from heavy mulching
- **Climate preference**: Prefers a cool season; produces better-flavored fruit in cooler regions

### Fruit

The fruit is variable depending on the tree and season, with the characteristic pineapple-guava flavor suggested by its common name. It is delicious eaten fresh and is a valuable addition to fruit salads. The fruit is high in pectin and makes excellent jelly.

### Historical Note

The Chilean guava was reportedly Queen Victoria's favorite fruit, and she ordered it grown in all royal gardens — a testament to its exceptional flavor despite its modest size.

### Fruit

The small reddish-purple berries (approximately 1–2 cm diameter) have an intensely aromatic, sweet-tart flavor often compared to a combination of strawberry and pineapple. They are eaten fresh or used in preserves, jellies, and desserts.

## Permaculture Applications

Both species serve similar design functions:

1. **Climate extension**: Provide quality fruit production in areas too cold for citrus, mango, and other subtropical species
2. **Hedge and windbreak**: Dense growth habit creates effective wind protection
3. **Edible landscaping**: Both are attractive ornamental shrubs suitable for residential food gardens
4. **Pollinator support**: Flowers provide nectar and pollen for bees
5. **Low maintenance**: Minimal care requirements once established
6. **Shade understorey**: Can be positioned beneath taller canopy trees in food forests
7. **Soil protection**: Shallow root systems with dense foliage provide ground cover [[dighton-fungal-drought-tolerance-plant-water-relations]]
- Both species benefit from mulching due to shallow root systems
- Feijoa is not reliably self-fertile — plant 2–3 for cross-pollination
- The iodine content in feijoa fruit is notable and may be nutritionally significant
