# PF TEK Inoculation and Incubation Techniques

## Overview

The inoculation and incubation phases of the PF TEK are where [[cultivator-sterile-technique-and-agar-culture]]
is most critical. Properly executed, the spore syringe introduces viable spores
to the sterile substrate, and the [[mycelium-running-parasitic-mushrooms-forest-blights|mycelium]] colonizes the cake over 2–3 weeks.
Contamination at this stage is the most common cause of failure.

## Pre-Inoculation Checks

Before inoculating, verify:

1. **Jars are cool to the touch** — hot jars kill spores on contact
2. **Lid bands are tight** — ensure proper seal after cooling
3. **Syringe is viable** — shake well to redistribute [[mushroom-spawn-preparation|spores]] in suspension
4. **Workspace is clean** — minimize airborne contaminants (still air preferred)

## Spore Syringe Preparation

A standard 10 cc syringe can inoculate **10 jars** at 1 cc per jar. Shake well
before each use. Store unused syringes **dark and cool** with needle guard on.
Syringes survive freezing but extreme cold kills spores. Resterilize the needle
in an alcohol flame before each reuse.

## Inoculation Procedure

### Step 1: Preparation
Remove masking tape from the jar lid's needle holes. Remove the syringe needle
guard. Ensure the jar lid is tight.

### Step 2: Insertion
Insert the needle through a lid hole. **Tilt the syringe body back toward
the center** of the lid so the needle tip touches the inside glass surface.
This positioning ensures the spore solution flows **down the side of the jar**,
distributing spores along the substrate cake rather than into the non-nutritive
vermiculite barrier layer.

### Step 3: Inoculation
Press the syringe plunger slowly. Watch the needle tip against the glass — as
soon as water appears around the tip, release pressure. Inoculate **a few drops
down each of the 4 holes**. Shake the syringe gently between holes to keep
spores distributed.

### Step 4: Sealing
After all 4 holes are inoculated, retape the needle holes with masking tape.
Tighten the lid band.

### Critical Detail: Needle Depth
The spore solution must flow **below the dry [[vermiculite-cannabis|vermiculite]] barrier layer**. If
the solution is absorbed by the dry top layer instead of reaching the wet
substrate, spores cannot germinate. Ensure the needle tip penetrates past the
barrier.

## Alcohol Flaming Technique

If the syringe needle is touched or contaminated, flame-sterilize it:

1. Place a tequila shotglass **upside down**
2. Use an eyedropper to place a few drops of **denatured alcohol fuel** on the
   hollow bottom
3. Touch with a match or lighter — produces a clean blue flame
4. An **alcohol flame** is preferred over butane lighters, which leave soot
5. Heat the needle in the flame for a few seconds
6. A few "pops" of boiling water may spurt from the needle — the spores
   inside the syringe remain safe

## Lid Management: Loose vs. Tight

During incubation, the lid can be either loose or tight depending on moisture:

| [[pf-tek-substrate-moisture-diagnosis-and-correction]] | Lid Position | Rationale |
|--------------------|-------------|-----------|
| High (max fruiting formula) | Loose | Prevents water pooling at jar bottom |
| Low | Tight | Preserves moisture content |

Water pooling at the jar bottom is to be avoided. The balance depends on:
- Water content of the formula
- Jar size and available air space
- Type of vermiculite (fine holds more water)
- Ambient humidity

**Only direct experimentation and comparison can find the right balance for a
given set of conditions. Take notes and go with what fruits the best.**

## Incubation Conditions

After inoculation:
- Place jars in a **safe place out of direct sunlight**
- **Indirect light** is all that is required
- Optimal temperature: **~70°F (21°C)**
- Germination begins within **3–5 days** at this temperature
- Cool temperatures are never a problem — **do not overheat**
- If indoor temperatures are low, a clamping lamp with reflector shining on
  jar tops provides gentle heat (monitor with thermometer)

## Germination and Colonization Timeline

| Phase | Timeframe | Appearance |
|-------|-----------|------------|
| Spore germination | 3–5 days | Small white fuzzy spots |
| Early colonization | 1 week | Cottony white growth with rhizomorphs |
| Full colonization | 2–3 weeks | Entire substrate surface turns white |
| [[stamets-pinning-initiation-stages-environmental-control]] | <1 week after colonization | Tiny white "pin" structures |
| [[growing-gourmet-primordia-formation-pinning]] | Within a week after pins | Worm-like structures with reddish heads |

## Troubleshooting: Non-Germination

If no growth appears after 1–2 weeks, possible causes:

1. **Spore solution too shallow** — absorbed by the dry vermiculite barrier
   instead of reaching the substrate. Solution: angle the needle to deposit
   solution along the jar wall below the barrier.

2. **Hot jars** — spores killed by residual heat. Always verify jars are cool
   to the touch before inoculating.

3. **Heat-killed syringes** — rare but possible. Syringes can be exposed to
   killing heat during transit (overheated cargo holds, sun-heated mailboxes).
   Store syringes in cool locations upon receipt.

4. **Extreme cold** — syringes can survive freezing but extreme low
   temperatures are destructive to spores.

## Contamination Identification

Contaminants appear in various colors from pastels to black:

| Type | Indicators | Source |
|------|-----------|--------|
| Bacteria | Sour foul odor within 2 days, no germination | Contaminated syringe or breached barrier |
| Mold | Colored growth (green, blue, pink, black) | Airborne spores entering through barrier |
| Yeast | Slimy or bubbly appearance | Poor [[boil-a-bag-pressure-sterilization-technique]] |

**Bacteria-contaminated jars should be handled with care** — do not inhale
near them, wash hands after contact, and dispose of the substrate safely.
