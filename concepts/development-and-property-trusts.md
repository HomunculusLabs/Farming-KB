# Development and Property Trusts

## Overview

Development and property trusts represent a critical financial mechanism within permaculture for redirecting capital away from destructive investments and toward truly rehabilitative ends. These trusts pool the resources of many small investors to purchase, improve, and manage real estate for [[tropical-rainforest-seedling-mycorrhiza-ecological-restoration]], [[permaculture-village-development]], and community benefit. By operating within the trust framework, they provide tax advantages, asset protection, and a structure that allows ordinary people to actively participate in land stewardship and sustainable development.

## The Problem with Conventional Investment

No investment in glamour stocks — coal, oil, uranium, properties, paper pulp, agrochemicals, mining — is likely to yield anything to investors but more pollution and to hasten global collapse. The evidence on acid rain alone (well documented even at the time of this writing) should convince any sane person that further "progress and development" along conventional lines will cause social and environmental upheaval. Development and property trusts offer an alternative pathway: turning money resources toward accelerated reafforestation, preservation of existing forests, sane village development, and the rehabilitation of eroded and misused lands.

## How Property Trusts Work

A property trust purchases real estate for improvement, lease, or rental on behalf of many small investors who cannot afford to individually own or develop such properties. By improving the properties so purchased, their value increases. Under present rulings in many jurisdictions, taxation is not incurred on that increase in value if the property is held for 10 or more years, nor is the trust itself taxed on its income from investors.

The management group obtains backing from investors via a public prospectus to float the property trust on the investment market. The prime purpose is to give every person a chance to do more than merely object to or protest inappropriate land sales to overseas investors, land misuse, and poor planning. Instead, they can actively invest in saving critical or endangered national resources (such as wildlife and forests) while actively rehabilitating eroded lands.

## Investment Structure

### Capital Raising

In the first trust of this type, the aim can be to stop accepting investors at $2 to $5 million, which will develop a property or properties as listed in the prospectus. A low unit price (e.g., $100) enables even poorer people to invest. A single unit can be held by a partnership, society, or other corporate group so that even less money need be contributed per person, enabling participation by unemployed people or those on low incomes. There need be no limit to the number of units held by any person.

### Investor Involvement

Unlike conventional property trusts, investors in permaculture-oriented development trusts should be given every opportunity to involve themselves in their investments through:

- **On-site work**: Volunteering for planting, building, or maintenance activities
- **Consultancy**: Contributing professional skills in design, engineering, ecology, or management
- **Leasehold opportunities**: Renting trust land for farming, forestry, or business purposes
- **Tree nursery supply**: Providing seedlings or planting materials for trust projects
- **Preference in village title sales**: Priority access to housing or land titles in trust-developed villages
- **Access to products and services**: Purchasing produce, timber, or other outputs from trust lands
- **Controlled recreational access**: Visiting and enjoying trust properties for leisure purposes

The trust informs investors of any opportunity for their involvement at any level, from volunteer or recreational use to paid consultancy, building, or leaseholds.

### Fund Allocation

Funds are used for setting up the trust deed and management company, paying for the work of the trustee, paying management a retainer, and paying for normal running costs including office expenses, printing, bookkeeping, accounting, valuation, and management travel to properties.

The precise amount used for administration should not exceed 4 to 8 percent of funds (based on figures from other property trust expenses), and the remainder is devoted to the purchase and development of properties as outlined in the prospectus. Administrative costs reduce as trust income grows from developed properties.

### Surplus Funds

Any surplus or unused funds accumulating in the trust can be invested in ethical systems, including housing cooperatives, inventory for development projects, and shares in ethical businesses. This creates a virtuous cycle where trust capital continuously supports the broader permaculture economy.

## Project Areas for Ethical Trusts

Development trusts can operate across a wide range of specific project areas:

### Wildlife and Forest Habitat Protection
Purchase of threatened wildlife and forest habitat, usually with a specific plant or animal group in mind, managed for the aims stated in a specific prospectus. This provides direct conservation outcomes while generating returns through eco-tourism, sustainable timber, or carbon credits.

### Land Rehabilitation
Purchase and development of eroded, salted, deforested, or misused land for ecological rehabilitation. This turns degraded land into productive, valuable property while providing environmental benefits such as improved water quality, carbon sequestration, and biodiversity recovery.

### Sustainable Village Development
Development of energy-efficient, sustainable villages on trust lands, either for sale as developed properties or pre-sale to raise capital for development. This creates communities designed according to [[permaculture-principles]] from the outset.

### Special Interest Developments
Group developments such as trout fisheries, aquaculture operations, or seed orchards for lease or sale. These combine [[solomon-gardening-aikido-pest-philosophy-ecological-management]] with productive enterprise.

### Bioregional Development
Purchase of community resources in a specific region for use by residents, community groups, trust unit holders, or by residents buying trust properties in that area. This creates integrated bioregional economies.

### Overseas Assistance
Purchase of selected properties for assisting [[low-cost-mushroom-growing-house-construction-developing-countries]] through overseas outpost programs, extending the trust model internationally.

### Clean Energy and Transport
Bioregional clean energy systems or clean transport methods, reducing dependence on fossil fuels while generating returns.

## The Development Staging Process

Property trusts follow a staged development process:

1. **Formation**: Set up the trust deed, register the trustee company, and establish the management structure
2. **Prospectus**: Write and publish the investment prospectus, detailing properties, development plans, and expected returns
3. **Capital raising**: Accept investor funds up to the target amount ($2-5 million or as determined)
4. **Property acquisition**: Purchase identified properties using raised capital
5. **Design and planning**: Commission permaculture designs and development plans for each property
6. **Development**: Execute the development plan in stages as funds allow
7. **Management**: Manage developed properties for income generation and ecological outcomes
8. **Re-evaluation**: Annual re-evaluation of property values and trust performance by an independent valuer
9. **Return or reinvestment**: Surplus income can be paid out to investors or reinvested in new projects
10. **Disposal**: Possible sale or disposal of trust properties after 10 years, with the increase in real value benefiting unit holders

## Community Involvement Beyond Investment

The development trust model creates a pathway for community involvement that goes far beyond passive investment. Investors are encouraged to assist in developments and sales, creating a community of active participants rather than passive shareholders. This involvement strengthens the social fabric around trust properties and creates the kind of engaged, knowledgeable community that is essential for long-term ecological stewardship.

## Tax Advantages

The property trust structure offers significant tax advantages:

- No capital gains tax on property value increases if held for 10+ years (in many jurisdictions)
- Trust income from investors is not taxed at the trust level
- Non-profit trading trusts associated with the property trust can gift profits tax-free to charitable entities
- Donations to associated charitable trusts may be tax-deductible

These tax advantages make [[ethical-investment-and-earth-banking]] through property trusts financially competitive with conventional investment options, removing one of the main barriers to redirecting capital toward ecological and community benefit.

## Integration with Broader Permaculture Strategy

Development and property trusts do not operate in isolation. They integrate with bioregional organizations that identify priority areas for conservation and development, extended family networks that provide the human capital and social infrastructure, charitable trusts that hold core assets and receive profits from trading operations, and village development projects that create the physical communities where [[mollisonian-permaculture-principles]] are lived out daily. Together, these structures form a comprehensive framework for redirecting capital, land, and human energy toward truly sustainable outcomes.
