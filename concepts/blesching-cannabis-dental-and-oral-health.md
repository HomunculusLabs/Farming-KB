---# Cannabis and Dental and Oral Health

## Overview

Oral disease and infection of the mouth are the most common diseases of mankind,
according to the World Health Organization. Over 75% of Americans have some
degree of gum inflammation or infection, and an estimated 80% of American adults
have periodontal disease by age 45. This is significant because periodontal
disease and cardiovascular disease are statistically linked, and oral bacteria
have been directly implicated in arterial plaque deposits.

Dental plaque, composed of colonies of harmful microorganisms, accumulates when
teeth are not regularly cleaned. This leads to gingivitis (inflamed, bleeding
gums) and later periodontitis (supporting bone loss). Cannabis smoking has
historically been linked to poor dental health, though much of this data fails
to distinguish smoking from other oral hygiene factors.

## Cannabis Research Findings

### Antibacterial Effects on Oral Pathogens

A 2020 review article from the University of Ljubljana, Slovenia, published in
Molecules, found that cannabinoids possess potent antibacterial activity. The
review covered 13 in-vitro studies examining cannabinoids' efficacy against a
range of Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria, including several oral
pathogens. CBD showed the highest antibacterial activity of all cannabinoids
tested, and Gram-positive bacteria were more sensitive to cannabinoid treatment
than Gram-negative species.
