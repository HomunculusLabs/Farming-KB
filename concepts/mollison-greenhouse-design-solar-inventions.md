## Species Profiles for Protected Cultivation

Mollison's species selections prioritize high-value plants that are difficult or impossible to grow outdoors in most climates.

- **Vanilla (Vanilla planifolia):** The world's most expensive spice by weight. Requires warm, humid conditions with filtered light. Growing up the rear wall of a heated greenhouse allows the vine to climb into the warmest air stratum. Hand pollination is required outside its native range (no natural pollinators), which is labor-intensive but manageable at household scale.

- **Cinnamon (Cinnamomum verum):** Coppices readily, producing 4 new stems per cut on a 2-year cycle. A single potted plant supplies enough cinnamon bark for an entire community. The leaves are a high-value nitrogen-rich fertilizer when composted, and cinnamon oil can be distilled from leaf material for additional income.

- **Tea (Camellia sinensis):** Surprisingly cold-hardy, tea can be grown in greenhouse environments well outside its tropical highland range. Five to six mature plants can supply 20-30 households with fresh tea leaves. The bush benefits from regular harvesting, which encourages new growth.

- **Black pepper and other tropical spices:** Many tropical spices can be grown in heated greenhouses in temperate climates, providing significant value per square foot compared to conventional greenhouse crops.

## Practical Tips for Greenhouse Operators

- **Ventilation management:** Overheating kills more greenhouse plants than cold. Automated vent openers (wax-filled cylinders that expand with heat) are inexpensive and reliable.

- **Thermal mass placement:** Water barrels, stone walls, and concrete floors absorb heat during the day and release it at night. Position thermal mass where it receives direct sunlight during the day.

- ** succession planting:** Maintain continuous production by starting new seedlings before harvesting existing crops. The controlled greenhouse environment allows year-round growing calendars.

- **Pest management:** Beneficial insects (ladybugs, lacewings, parasitic wasps) can control greenhouse pests without chemicals. The enclosed environment makes biological pest control particularly effective.
