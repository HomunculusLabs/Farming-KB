# Psilocybe subcubensis

Psilocybe subcubensis is a psilocybin-containing mushroom species
described by [[psilocybin-mushroom]] [[psilocybin-mushroom-image-guide-habitat-clues]]
includes photographs by John W. Allen showing P. subcubensis
specimens from the Koh Samui type locality.

## Taxonomy

The species authority is Psilocybe subcubensis Guzman. It was
described as a distinct species closely allied to P. cubensis but
separated on the basis of morphological differences, most notably
[[spore]] size. The specific epithet "subcubensis" literally means
"below cubensis," reflecting its close relationship to and smaller
stature compared to P. cubensis.

The taxonomic status of P. subcubensis has been the subject of some
debate among mycologists. Some authorities have suggested it may
represent a tropical form or subspecies of P. cubensis rather than a
fully distinct species. However, the consistently smaller spore size
and other morphological features support its recognition as a
separate taxon.

## Description

### Cap (Pileus)

The cap of Psilocybe subcubensis measures 1.5 to 4 cm in diameter,
making it generally smaller than P. cubensis. When young, the cap
is hemispherical to convex, expanding to broadly convex or nearly
plane with age. The surface is smooth and slightly viscid when
moist, with a hygrophanous quality typical of the genus.

The cap color is golden brown to light brown when fresh and moist,
fading to a paler tan or yellowish-white as it dries. The center of
the cap (the disc) often retains a darker coloration even when dry.
The margin is initially incurved, becoming straight or slightly
uplifted with maturity. A [[partial-veil-tropism-protection-before-spore-release]] is present, connecting the
cap margin to the stem, which typically ruptures as the cap expands
and may leave a fragile annulus on the stem.

### Gills (Lamellae)

The gills are adnate to slightly adnexed, with moderate spacing.
They are initially grayish but darken to deep purplish-brown or
nearly black as the spores mature. The gill edges remain paler than
the faces, creating a noticeable contrast. This dark spore coloration
is typical of the genus Psilocybe and helps distinguish these
mushrooms from genera with lighter [[macrofungal-documentation-photography-spore-prints]].

### Stem (Stipe)

The stem is 3 to 7 cm long and 3 to 5 mm thick, typically more
slender than that of P. cubensis. It is cylindrical, slightly
enlarged at the base, and has a smooth surface. The color is
whitish to pale buff, sometimes with bluish tints where bruised.
The stem is hollow and fibrous. A fragile annular zone may be
present from the remnants of the partial veil.

### Spores and Microscopy

The most diagnostic feature separating P. subcubensis from P.
cubensis is spore size. Spores of P. subcubensis are consistently
smaller than those of P. cubensis, measuring approximately 10 to 13
micrometers in length, compared to the 12 to 17 micrometer range
typical of P. cubensis. The spores are ellipsoid to broadly
ellipsoid, with a prominent germ pore, and are smooth and
thick-walled. The spore print is dark purplish-brown.
