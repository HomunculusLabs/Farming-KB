# Tryptamine Oral Activity and MAO Inhibition

## The [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-caribbean-central-america]] Problem

The single [[query-what-are-the-most-important-medicinal-mushrooms-and-how-do-i-choose-one]] factor determining whether a tryptamine is orally active is its susceptibility to **monoamine oxidase (MAO)** -- an enzyme [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-classification-system]] that oxidatively deaminates primary and secondary amines, rendering them pharmacologically inactive.

Simple tryptamines like DMT are orally inactive because MAO in the gut and liver efficiently destroys them before they can reach the brain. This is the fundamental pharmacological barrier that explains why [[guzman-allen-gartz-sacred-puffballs-gasteromycetes-indigenous-cultures]] developed [[ayahuasca]] (combining DMT-[[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]] [[dmt-maoi-plants-united-states]] with MAOI-[[dmt-containing-plants-and-maoi-combination]]).

## How MAO Inhibition Works

Some drugs are metabolized by the removal of the needed amine function. This deamination results from the action of an enzyme system called **monoamine oxidase (MAO)**. If this enzyme system is inhibited, the drug would be destroyed to a lesser extent and would have greater potency. The material that protects the drug from this erosion is called a **monoamine oxidase inhibitor (MAOI)**.

As a result, drugs that show no oral activity (such as DMT) become available when the oxidizing enzymes are made dysfunctional by an inhibitor.

## Strategies for Achieving Oral Activity

### 1. MAO Inhibitors (Exogenous)

Combining orally inactive tryptamines with MAO inhibitors:

| Tryptamine | Without MAOI | With MAOI |
|------------|-------------|-----------|
| DMT | Inactive at 350 mg orally | Active at 35-120 mg with harmaline |
| 5-MeO-DMT | Inactive at 35 mg orally | Active at 10-25 mg with harmaline |
| DET | Not orally active | Orally active |

The harmaline dose of 150 mg is identified as "highly effective as an MAO blocker."

### 2. Alpha-[[query-how-does-one-carbon-metabolism-affect-methylation]] ([[serotonin-psilocin-structural-similarity-hallucinogenic-mechanism]] Protection)

Adding a methyl group to the alpha-carbon (adjacent to the amine) physically blocks MAO from accessing the vulnerable amine:

- **DMT** (inactive orally) becomes **2-Me-DMT** (orally active)
- **DET** (not orally active) becomes **2-Me-DET** (orally active)
- **5-MeO-DMT** (not orally active) becomes **5-MeO-TMT** (orally active)

This works consistently across the three major parenterally-only active psychedelics. Shulgin describes it as "that bit of bulk got in the way of the destructive amine oxidases, and protected the molecule from its expected first-pass metabolic destruction."

### 3. N-Substitution

Modifying the terminal amine with increasingly bulky groups:
- Simple tryptamine: weak, not orally active
- N-methyl (NMT): not orally active
- N,N-dimethyl (DMT): not orally active
- N,N-diethyl (DET): some oral activity at ~300 mg
- N,N-diisopropyl ([[dipt]]): orally active
- N-ethyl-N-isopropyl (EIPT): orally active

However, excessive bulk destroys activity, and the pattern is not perfectly predictable.

### 4. Ring Substitution (The Psilocin Pathway)

Adding a 4-hydroxy group (the "psilocin signature"):
- 4-HO-DMT (psilocin): orally active at 10-20 mg
- 4-HO-DET: orally active
- 4-HO-DIPT: orally active
- 4-HO-MET: orally active

The 4-hydroxyl group provides "some measure of oral activity."

### 5. The O-Methyl + Alpha-Methyl Strategy (alpha,O-DMS)

For serotonin-like compounds, two obstacles block brain entry:
1. The 5-hydroxy group is too polar (blocks blood-brain barrier passage)
2. The exposed amino group is vulnerable to MAO

**alpha,O-Dimethylserotonin** overcomes both: the O-methyl removes polarity, the alpha-methyl protects from enzymatic attack. The result is one of the most potent tryptamines known, orally active.

## MAO Inhibitors as Psychedelic Substances

A critical correction to popular belief: MAOIs are NOT themselves without pharmacological action. Shulgin explicitly states:

"There is a general inference that the MAOI is, itself, without action and this is simply not correct."

Harmaline alone at 150-500 mg produces significant intoxication, visual disturbances, motor impairment, and nausea. Several prescription antidepressants work by exactly this MAO inhibition mechanism.

## Tryptamines That Are Themselves MAOIs

Some tryptamines are effective MAO inhibitors:
- **[[alpha-methyltryptamine]] (alpha-MT)**: Effective MAOI, clinically studied as an antidepressant (Indopan in the USSR, Monase in the US)
- **alpha,N-DMT**: Effective MAOI
