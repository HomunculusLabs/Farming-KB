# Sachamama and Forest Spirits

The Amazonian rainforest is inhabited by a vast hierarchy of spiritual beings that exist alongside and sometimes interpenetrate the physical world. In [[amaringo-ayahuasca-visions-shipibo-iconography]], these beings range from the colossal [[sachamama]] — a serpent so large it supports an entire ecosystem on its back — to the trickster [[icaros]]. Understanding these entities is fundamental to the practice of vegetalismo.

## The Sachamama (Mother of the Forest)

The Sachamama is one of the most formidable beings in Amazonian cosmology. This enormous snake is so ancient and so massive that trees and weeds grow upon its body, making it virtually indistinguishable from the surrounding terrain. A person can climb on top of it without realizing they are standing on a living, dangerous creature.

### Physical Description and Behavior

The Sachamama rarely moves, remaining in the same place for perhaps hundreds of years. Its breast is described as being like the scraper blade of a bulldozer, capable of knocking down trees and plants in its path. When it does move to a new location, it throws down the trees on its back and creates a path by knocking down other trees as well, producing strong winds with lightning and heavy rain.

The creature possesses a powerful magnet in its head. Any living being that crosses its path is immediately magnetized and drawn to its mouth, where it is swallowed whole. Beside the Sachamama are found all kinds of animal and even human bones — testimony to its lifetime of predation. Notably, the Sachamama expels meat through its rectum but vomits the bones.

### Hunting the Deer

In one vision, the Sachamama is shown hypnotizing a deer in order to swallow it. The deer, mesmerized by the serpent's magnetic gaze, walks willingly toward its doom. This demonstrates that the Sachamama does not need to chase prey — its magnetic power is sufficient to draw victims to it.

### Ecological Role

A small pond lies to the left of the Sachamama from which it drinks. The creature serves as an ecosystem in miniature, with vegetation growing on its back creating habitat for birds, insects, and small animals. The Sachamama represents the forest itself — ancient, seemingly immobile, yet capable of tremendous destruction when stirred.

### Defensive Capabilities

When a person notices the presence of the Sachamama, they must leave swiftly. The creature produces strong wind, lightning, and heavy rain capable of knocking down trees. Lightning strikes may accompany its movements, making escape perilous.

## The Chullachaki (Forest Spirit)

The Chullachaki is a forest spirit whose name derives from Quechua: chulla means "uneven" or "one-footed" and chaki means "foot." This being is the guardian and master of wild animals, responsible for their care and protection.

### The Supay-chacra (Devil's Garden)

The Chullachaki presides over the Supay-chacra, a supernatural garden in the forest. At its center stands a caimitillo tree (Duroia hirsuta), flanked by Supay-caspi and Supay-anallosacha trees, all marked by distinctive circles at the base of their leaves. The Chullachaki is depicted dressed in royal gowns, reading a parchment — a decree on the care of wild animals.

### The Royal Assembly

The shapshicos or sacharunas (forest people) attend the reading, all dressed in brilliant-colored royal clothes that make nearby leaves shine as brightly as light bulbs. The gathering is described as a Bacchanalian feast to which all wild animals are invited. The animals set up a circus show with dances, euphoric music, and entrancing singing. One can hear "the most complex sounds and lullabies of the purity of the forest, of the intricate web of the vegetal esoteric world."

### Animal Participants

The following animals are called through telepathic vibration via the instinct of the psychic cord:

- **Otorongo** (jaguar), **manco-puma**, **lluhuichio-puma** (jaguar varieties)
- **Huasca** (monkey), **maquisapa** (Ateles sp., spider monkey)
- **Squirrel**, **casha-cushillo** (porcupine)
- **Sachavaca** (tapir), **armas** (Priodontes maximus, giant armadillo)
- **Majas** (Agouti paca), **achuni** (Nasua nasua, coati)
- **Mantona** (boa constrictor), **shushupi** (Lachesis muta, bushmaster)
- **Pucanacanaca** (coral snake), **sparrow-hawk**, **toucan**
- **Paufil** (Crux sp., curassow), **partridge**, **paucarcillo**
- **Canaoro**, **urcutu** (owl), **puka-cunga** (Penelope jacquacu, piping guan)
- **Trompetero** (Psophia leucoprocta, trumpeter)

### Hidden Knowledge

The vision of the Chullachaki carries a profound message: "Within vegetalismo there is so much knowledge that could benefit man. Perhaps some day the hidden secret of longevity will be known, because it is certain that this is to be found within the plants of the forest."

## Vision of the Snakes

Snakes occupy a central position in Amazonian spiritual ecology. They serve as healers, guardians, and dangerous entities depending on the species and context. All snakes respond to the singing of the snakes' icaro, gathering under and around the healing house during ceremonies.

### Huairamama (Mother of the Wind)

The Huairamama is a colossal snake that moves with great wind. Jungle legend holds that when this snake bathes, a sound of thunder is heard among the clouds, but no water falls to the ground — a phenomenon called supay-cato (ghost bath). When a healer calls out to the Huairamama during trance, she arrives with a great wind born instantaneously, without any clouds, passing like a whirlwind. The sound can be heard by everyone present, whether they have taken the purge or not.

The Huairamama throws its rainbow to the jungle to give strength to the medicine a healer prepares for a patient bitten by a snake. Her eyes shine with white lights, and her mouth radiates violet-colored waves that make those present feel like giants capable of sensing anything nearby.

### The Snake Tamer Canessa

Canessa, the great tamer of snakes and jungle animals, is accompanied by her princesses Uzela and Vedina who attack with magic nets. Her presence represents the shaman's ability to command and negotiate with dangerous serpent forces.

### Quiruma-supay (Tree Stump Spirit)

This aquatic genie always emerges late at night in silent places. Witch doctors kidnap people with the help of the quiruma-supay, taking victims to the bottom of the river where the black yakurunas live — from where rescue is extremely difficult.

### Yana-yaku-lobos (Black Water Wolves)

These animals serve as guards of the aquatic arkanas because they move very swiftly in water. They represent the defensive animal allies available to healers working in aquatic contexts.

### Paiche Machaco

A creature that is simultaneously a paiche fish (Arapaima gigas) and a snake with a poisonous bite. It inhabits the Amazonian lowlands and is called upon in curing snake bites — a classic example of Amazonian "like cures like" logic where the venomous creature itself becomes the medicine.

### Mermaids with Two Tails

These beings only emerge during sublime trances to cure sicknesses of the water — afflictions caused by the [[metzner-spirit-beings-multiple-realities-entheogens]].

## The Puma-runa and Huya-runa Snakes

The huya-runa snakes fight against Puma-runa (jaguar-man), whose paws and head are black. This represents the eternal struggle between different classes of animal-spirit powers in the Amazonian cosmological landscape.

## The Supay-caiman

The supay-caiman (devil caiman) possesses marble quills. Its icaro is sung to cure the mal aire del agua (illness produced by an evil breeze from the water), demonstrating that even ostensibly demonic beings can be called upon for healing when approached with proper knowledge and respect.
