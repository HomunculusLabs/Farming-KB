# Proteobacteria and Actinobacteria Enrichment in Cannabis Endorhiza: The Nutrient-Driven Shift

## Overview

One of the most striking findings from the Winston et al. (2014) [[cannabis-rhizosphere-microbial-communities]] transition from bulk soil through the rhizosphere and into the Cannabis endorhiza (root interior). The two phyla that most consistently increased in relative abundance within the endorhiza were Proteobacteria and Actinobacteria — a pattern predicted by the two-tier selection model and consistent with observations across diverse plant systems.

## The Phylum-Level Transition Pattern

As microbial communities move from bulk soil into the root environment, predictable taxonomic shifts occur:

- **Acidobacteria** dramatically decrease (most significant OTU shift, Bonferroni-corrected ANOVA: p = 1.12 × 10⁻⁷)
- **Proteobacteria** substantially increase, particularly Gammaproteobacteria and Alphaproteobacteria
- **Actinobacteria** increase, especially Actinomycetales
- **Bacteroidetes** show more variable responses, with some orders increasing

This pattern was consistent across all five Cannabis cultivars tested (Sour Diesel, Bookoo Kush, Burmese, White Widow, and Maui Wowie) and in both soil types, suggesting it represents a fundamental feature of Cannabis root colonization rather than a cultivar- or soil-specific artifact.

## Proteobacteria: The Dominant Endorhiza Phylum

### Why Proteobacteria Dominate Root Interiors

Proteobacteria are well-adapted to the root interior environment for several reasons:

1. **Nutrient richness**: The endorhiza provides a carbon-rich environment through root exudates (sugars, amino acids, organic acids). Many Proteobacteria are copiotrophs — organisms that thrive in nutrient-rich conditions. This contrasts with Acidobacteria, which are typically oligotrophs adapted to nutrient-poor soils.

2. **Competitive colonization**: Proteobacteria possess diverse mechanisms for colonizing plant tissues, including chemotaxis toward root exudates, biofilm formation on root surfaces, and the ability to breach the root epidermis.

3. **Plant signaling compatibility**: Many Proteobacteria produce or respond to signaling molecules that facilitate plant-microbe communication, including quorum-sensing molecules and phytohormone mimics.

### Key Proteobacterial Orders in Cannabis Endorhiza

The study identified several Proteobacterial orders that were significantly enriched or differentiated between Cannabis strains:

- **Rhizobiales** (Alphaproteobacteria): Well-known plant-associated bacteria that increased in the endorhiza relative to the rhizosphere. Multiple Rhizobiales OTUs showed significant abundance differences between Cannabis strains, making this order a key contributor to cultivar-specific [[proteobacteria-dominance-cannabis-endorhiza]], found consistently across all cultivars. Pseudomonas species are among the most well-studied plant growth-promoting rhizobacteria (PGPR).

- **Sphingomonadales** (Alphaproteobacteria): Showed strain-specific abundance patterns. Notably, *[[weighted-unifrac-strain-clustering-cannabis-endorhiza-community-structure]] shared across all five cultivars. These filamentous bacteria possess several traits advantageous for root colonization:

- **Hyphal growth**: Their filamentous morphology allows them to physically penetrate root tissues and explore the root interior more effectively than non-filamentous bacteria
- **Antibiotic production**: Many Actinomycetales produce [[endorhiza-endophytic-bacteria]], primarily within Gammaproteobacteria and Alphaproteobacteria
- **Consistent with other plant systems**: The dominance of Proteobacterial [[brown-rot-fungi-cellulose-degradation-wood-decay]] capacity within root cell walls
- A role in root tissue turnover or nutrient cycling within the root
- An opportunistic colonization strategy facilitated by the unique cell wall composition of Cannabis roots

Interestingly, Cellvibrio was later identified as a potential biomarker for root decay in post-harvest Cannabis samples, suggesting its cellulolytic activity may become problematic after root death.

## Strain-Specific Proteobacteria: Beyond the Core

While the core community was conserved across cultivars, the strain-specific OTU differences were also predominantly Proteobacterial. This dual role of Proteobacteria — as both the conserved core and the differentiating variable — underscores their importance in Cannabis root biology. The cultivar-specific Proteobacterial enrichment likely reflects differences in:

- Root exudate profiles between cultivars (different [[antifungal-secondary-metabolites-coprophilous-fungi]], sugar compositions, organic acid ratios)
- Root anatomy and tissue architecture that create different colonization niches
- Immune response variation that selectively permits or excludes specific bacterial taxa
