# Cannabis High-Volume Extraction

High-volume cannabis extraction is the scaled-up application of solvent-based extraction techniques, designed to process large quantities of cannabis material efficiently and safely. The apparatus and methods described in classical cannabis alchemy literature, particularly in Ben Lemon's Cannabis Alchemy, draw directly from the tradition of illicit alcohol distillation, adapting moonshiner techniques for the extraction and refinement of cannabis oils.

## Historical Context

The development of high-volume extraction methods was driven by the high potential profits from smuggling concentrated cannabis oil, which is far more potent per unit weight than raw plant material. The United States Drug Enforcement Administration documented various large-scale extraction devices they had seized, noting the new problems these operations posed for law enforcement. The situation was described as reminiscent of the traditional moonshiner-revenuer dynamic.

The apparatus designs reflect practical ingenuity in adapting commonly available industrial materials for cannabis processing. The use of 55-gallon oil drums, hardware store components, and surplus equipment made these systems accessible to operators without access to professional laboratory apparatus.

## Apparatus Design

The high-volume extraction apparatus is constructed using two 55-gallon oil drums and equipment purchased from hardware or surplus stores. The lower drum serves as the boiling vessel, heated by a water bath in a large tub. The upper drum functions as a condenser, filled with circulating ice water that causes solvent fumes to recondense into liquid and fall back into the lower drum.

The condenser drum is constructed by drilling holes at four-inch grid intersections on both the top and bottom surfaces. Pieces of one-inch copper tubing are run through the drum lengthwise, protruding about one inch from each surface. Watertight seals are soldered or welded around each tube where it passes through the drum surface. Inlet and outlet fittings for circulating ice water coolant are fitted to the top.

The lower drum has sturdy legs attached to maintain space between the drum bottom and the boiling water bath. A copper drain tube with an on/off valve allows solvent-cannabis solution to be drained by siphon. A fitting with an on/off valve is installed in the side of the drum approximately halfway between top and bottom for solvent collection.

## Safety Features

The large volumes of flammable solvents used in high-volume extraction pose serious fire hazards. Multiple safety features are incorporated into the apparatus design. A pressure relief valve system is created by leaving one of the copper tubes unsealed at the top, with a hose leading to a jug placed in a safe location. If any solvent fumes or liquid appear in the jug during operation, it indicates excessive pressure and the operator immediately removes the heat source and adds ice water to the condenser.

The crack between the two drums is sealed with a large polyethylene trash bag (bottom cut out) slipped over the apparatus and secured with rubber bands made from inner tubes. This arrangement acts as an additional safety valve, as any pressure buildup causes the plastic to puff out before reaching dangerous levels.

All heating is done indirectly through a water bath rather than by applying direct heat to the solvent drum. This eliminates the risk of localized overheating and prevents the solvent from reaching its flash point at the point of contact with the heat source. Three heavy-duty sparkless electric hotplates heat the water bath.

## Refluxing Procedure

The cannabis material is prepared by grinding to a fine powder. The lower drum is filled approximately one-third full with powdered cannabis, and solvent is added to half-fill the drum. The upper drum is secured to the lower and sealed. The apparatus is placed in the tub resting on the electric hotplates.

An immersion pump in a tub of ice water supplies coolant through the condenser drum. The hotplates heat the water bath to boiling, which in turn heats the solvent-cannabis mixture. Solvent fumes rise, contact the copper tubes and condenser drum surface, recondense into liquid, and fall back into the boiling mixture. Refluxing continues for approximately three hours.

## Soxhlet Extraction at Scale

After initial refluxing, the apparatus is cooled and the oil-saturated solvent is drained through the bottom valve. The damp cannabis material is replaced in the drum, and the collected solvent is added back for additional extraction cycles. Multiple extraction cycles ensure maximum oil recovery from the plant material.

A soxhlet chamber can be incorporated for continuous extraction. The soxhlet chamber is fitted between the falling recondensed solvent drops and the boiling solvent solution. As the pure solvent condenses and drips through the cannabis material in the soxhlet, it continuously washes out remaining oil. After several hours of soxhleting, virtually all the oil is dissolved in the solvent.

## Solvent Recovery and Collection

Solvent is removed from the oil-solvent solution by catching the recondensed solvent drops in a funnel rather than allowing them to fall back into the boiling mixture. A tube leads from the funnel through the soxhlet chamber to the valve on the side of the lower drum, transferring the pure distilled solvent to a receiving barrel outside the apparatus.

This solvent recovery system allows the operator to reuse solvent across multiple extraction batches, significantly reducing operating costs and the total volume of flammable solvent that must be handled and stored.

## Operational Requirements

An overhead chain winch or locking block and tackle is needed for lifting and positioning the heavy drum components. An immersion pump and large pail for ice water are required for the cooling system. Large, thick polyethylene trash bags and rubber bands seal the drum junction. A large funnel nearly matching the drum diameter and connecting tubing complete the solvent collection system.

The entire operation requires continuous monitoring. Such a device must always be tended during operation, and measures to shut it down must be ready at all times. Even though the apparatus has many safety features, the large scale of a potential accident warrants all possible caution.

## Scaling Considerations

The principles of each operation in the high-volume system parallel those in basic kitchen-scale extraction using household equipment. The chemistry is identical, but the engineering challenges of managing large volumes of flammable solvents require proportionally greater attention to safety, cooling capacity, and structural integrity.

Modern commercial extraction facilities have largely replaced these improvised systems with purpose-built closed-loop extraction equipment that provides even greater safety, efficiency, and solvent recovery. However, the fundamental principles of reflux extraction, soxhlet washing, and solvent recovery remain the same.

## Throughput and Economics

A 55-gallon drum system can process several pounds of cannabis material per extraction cycle. With a three-hour reflux period plus time for heating, cooling, draining, and solvent recovery, a complete cycle takes approximately six to eight hours. Multiple extraction cycles per day are possible with careful scheduling and adequate solvent recovery capacity.

The primary economic advantage of high-volume extraction is the dramatic concentration of value. Raw cannabis flower has a THC content typically ranging from 10 to 25 percent, meaning 75 to 90 percent of the plant material is non-psychoactive. Extraction concentrates the psychoactive components into an oil that represents a small fraction of the original weight while retaining the full psychoactive value. The concentrated oil is far easier to transport, store, and conceal than equivalent amounts of bulk plant material.

Solvent recovery significantly reduces operating costs. In a well-designed system, 80 to 90 percent of the solvent can be recovered and reused across batches. Given that high-quality ethanol or petroleum ether represents a significant expense, efficient solvent recovery is essential for the economic viability of large-scale operations.

## Environmental and Safety Regulations

Modern commercial extraction facilities must comply with fire codes, environmental regulations, and occupational safety standards that did not exist when the classical high-volume methods were developed. Explosion-proof electrical equipment, ventilation systems rated for flammable vapor atmospheres, fire suppression systems, and solvent storage requirements add significant cost and complexity to operations that were originally designed for illicit production.

Environmental regulations govern the disposal of spent cannabis biomass, solvent-contaminated waste, and wastewater from the extraction process. Proper disposal prevents soil and groundwater contamination and avoids legal penalties. Many jurisdictions require extraction operations to obtain specific permits and undergo regular inspections.

## Comparison with Modern Closed-Loop Systems

Modern commercial extraction has moved toward closed-loop hydrocarbon extraction systems that recirculate butane or propane through the cannabis material in a sealed system. These systems offer several advantages over the classical drum-based approach: more precise temperature and pressure control, higher extraction efficiency, reduced solvent consumption, and inherently safer operation due to the closed system design.

However, the fundamental chemistry of solvent extraction remains unchanged. Whether using a 55-gallon drum apparatus or a computer-controlled closed-loop system, the process involves dissolving cannabinoids in a solvent, separating the solvent from the plant material, and recovering the purified oil by removing the solvent. The classical methods described in cannabis alchemy literature remain valuable for understanding the underlying principles.
