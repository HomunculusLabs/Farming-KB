## Overview of the Third Bardo

The Third Bardo (Sidpa Bardo) is the final phase of Leary's psychedelic adaptation of the Tibetan Book of the Dead. It represents "the period in which the consciousness makes the transition from transcendent reality to the reality of ordinary waking life." The Tibetans call it the Bardo of "seeking rebirth."

A person enters the Third Bardo when they are "incapable of holding on to the knowledge that the [[leary-peaceful-and-wrathful-visions]] visions were projections of his own mind, but became attracted to or frightened by one or more of them." The key struggle is regaining routine reality and reconstructing the ego — a process the manual frames as choosing a "post-session personality" or "re-entry robot."

The manual emphasizes that the types of re-entry made "can profoundly color the person's subsequent attitudes and feelings about himself and the world, for weeks or even months afterwards." A predominantly negative session can still be turned to great advantage provided the re-entry is positive and conscious. Conversely, "a happy and revelatory experience can be made valueless by a fearful or negative re-entry."

Approximately fifty percent of the entire [[leary-psychedelic-experience-bardo-mapping]] is spent in the Third Bardo by most normal people, according to Tibetan estimates.

## Seven Signs of Re-Entry

The manual identifies seven characteristic signs that a person has entered the Third Bardo, each requiring recognition rather than reaction.

### Enhanced Perception

The first recognition point is "the feeling of supernormal perception and performance" — clairvoyance, telepathy, or ESP-like experiences. The manual warns against fascination with these heightened powers: "until the selfish, game-involved nature of man is completely mastered is he safe in using them." This parallels the yogic teaching against pursuing siddhis (psychic powers) for their own sake.

### Panic and Persecution Visions

Experiences of panic, torture, and persecution distinguished from Second Bardo wrathful visions by their involvement of "the person's own skin-encapsulated ego." Mind-controlling figures and demons appear — "Where Tibetans saw demons and beasts of prey, a Westerner may see impersonal machinery grinding, or depersonalizing and controlling devices of different futuristic varieties." The person may plead for antidotes or tranquillizers, or see themselves falling down "deep, terrifying precipices."

These symbolize the "evil passions which, like narcotic drugs, enslave and bind mankind to existence in game-networks": anger, lust, stupidity, pride or egoism, jealousy, and control-power.

### Restless Wandering

A third sign involves "restless, unhappy wandering which may be purely mental or may involve actual physical movement." The person feels "driven by winds (winds of karma) or shunted around mechanically." There may be a desperate desire to phone family, doctor, or friends to "pull you out of the state." The manual urges resisting this impulse: "One should not try to involve others in one's hallucinatory world."

### Feelings of Stupidity

The person may "feel stupid and full of incoherent thoughts, whereas everyone else seems to be perfectly knowing and wise." This leads to feelings of guilt, inadequacy, and in extreme form to the Judgment Vision. The manual attributes this to "the limited perspective under which the consciousness is operating in this Bardo."

### Feelings of Death and Isolation

A fifth sign is "the feeling of being dead, cut off from surrounding life, and full of misery." The person may awaken from a trance-like swoon and experience themselves and others as "lifeless robots, performing wooden meaningless gestures." This contrasts with the First and Second Bardos, where "such complaints are never uttered" in the true state of ego-death.

### Constriction and Entrapment

The sixth sign involves "feeling oppressed or crushed or squeezed into cracks and crevices amidst rocks and boulders. Or the person may feel that a kind of metallic net or cage may encompass him." This symbolizes "the attempt prematurely to enter an ego-robot which is unfitting or unequipped to deal with the expanded consciousness."

### Grey Twilight Light

A seventh sign is "a kind of grey twilight-like light suffusing everything," in marked contrast to the brilliant radiating lights of earlier phases. "Objects, instead of shining, glowing and vibrating, are now dully colored, shabby and angular."

## The Six Levels of Re-Entry

The Tibetan manual conceives of the voyager returning to one of six worlds of game existence (sangsara), representing different personality types or levels of consciousness.

The three higher levels are: (1) devas — saints, sages, divine teachers (Gautama Buddha, Lao Tse, Christ), (2) asuras — titans or heroes with more-than-human power and vision, and (3) normal human beings, "struggling through game-networks, occasionally breaking free."

The three lower levels are: (4) primitive and animalistic incarnations — the dog and cock symbolizing hyper-sexuality with jealousy, the pig symbolizing lustful stupidity, the ape full of "rampaging primitive power," the bird soaring freely, (5) neurotics — "frustrated lifeless spirits forever pursuing unsatisfied desires," and (6) hell or psychosis.

Less than one percent of ego-transcendent experiences end in either sainthood or psychosis. Most persons return to the normal human level.

## The Influence of Thought

A critical section of the Third Bardo addresses how thoughts during re-entry determine the level of return. Attachment to material possessions, old games, or jealousy toward others enjoying one's former role will "affect the psychological balance in such a way that even if destined to return at a higher level, you will actually re-enter on a lower level."

The manual instructs: "abandon weakness and attachment to them; cast them away wholly; renounce them from your heart." When observing improper behavior in others during the session, the voyager should think: "What? How can the words of a Buddha be inappropriate? It is like the reflection of blemishes on my own face which I see in a mirror; my own thoughts must be impure."

## The Judgment Vision

The Judgment Vision is the Third Bardo "blame game." Drawing on the Tibetan image of white and black pebbles counting good and evil deeds, Leary notes Westerners are most likely to see this in the Christian version. The Judge or Lord of Death "symbolizes conscience itself in its stern aspect of impartiality and love of righteousness," and the "Mirror of Karma" is simply memory.

Different parts of the ego come forward with excuses, accusations, and justifications. The instruction is stark: "Be not frightened, tell no lies, face truth fearlessly." The manual reminds the voyager that "your personality is a collection of thought-patterns and void. It cannot be harmed or injured. Swords cannot pierce it, fire cannot burn it."

## Sexual Visions and Re-Entry

Sexual visions are "extremely frequent during the Third Bardo." The person may see or imagine males and females copulating, hallucinate orgies, and experience both desire and shame. Leary notes the exact correspondence to the psychoanalytic theory of the Oedipus Complex and the "primal scene."

If the person attempts to enter an old ego through sexual attraction or repulsion, "he will re-enter on an animal or neurotic level." The instruction is to "withhold yourself from action or attachment. Have faith and float gently with the stream." Visions of sexual union may be followed by birth visions — conception, intra-uterine life, and emergence from a womb, cocoon, or shell.

## Four Methods of Preventing Re-Entry

When the person is still wandering in the Third Bardo and the experience could be prolonged for greater benefit, four meditative methods are offered: (1) meditation on the Buddha or guide, (2) concentration on good games, (3) meditation on illusion, and (4) meditation on the void. Each attempts to lead the voyager "back to the First Bardo central stream of energy from which he has been separated by game involvements."

The manual notes that due to "the increased suggestibility and openness of the mind in the psychedelic state these methods can be used by anyone, regardless of intellectual capacity, or proficiency in meditation."
