## The Common Belief

The conventional gardening wisdom holds that watering in the evening
leaves foliage wet overnight, promoting [[extending-growing-season]]. During summer, dew forms as air temperatures drop and
relative humidity rises toward saturation. Plants quickly dry after
morning sunlight arrives. Watering plants just before dark mimics this
natural pattern. If plants are not harmed by natural dew — and they are
not — they will not be harmed by irrigation that follows the same
timing. This single observation undermines the entire argument against
night watering for most climates.

### Minimal Evaporation Loss

When sprinkling is done during calm, low-light conditions, little or
no water is lost to evaporation before it enters the soil. Daytime
watering, especially during hot sunny weather, can lose a substantial
fraction of the applied moisture to evaporation before it ever reaches
the root zone. Night watering eliminates this waste entirely, meaning
less total water is needed to achieve the same soil moisture level.
For gardens on limited well water, this efficiency gain is critical.

The critical requirement for safe night watering is that the sprinkler
application rate must be low enough to prevent leaching. Solomon

This means that for an eight-hour night window with a 0.45 in/hr
nozzle, the total application would be approximately 3.6 inches —
more than enough to fully recharge even a very dry clay soil profile
without any leaching risk.

## The Practical Advantage: Well Water Competition

For rural homes with limited-output wells, nighttime offers a crucial
practical advantage: there is no competition for water from household
use. No one is taking showers, running dishwashers, flushing toilets,
or washing clothes. The full well output is available for irrigation.

The optimal window runs from bedtime to breakfast. During this period
there is rarely any wind, temperatures are at their lowest, and
household water demand is at its minimum. This convergence of favorable
conditions makes nighttime the most efficient time to irrigate.

## When Night Watering is Not Ideal

Night watering works best under these conditions:

- The soil is heavy (clay or clay-loam) with good water retention
