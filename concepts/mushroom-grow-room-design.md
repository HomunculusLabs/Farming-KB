## Overview

A dedicated grow room provides controlled environmental conditions for [[mushroom-cultivation]] at scale. While a single monotub can be managed in a closet or spare room, growers running multiple tubs or bags benefit from a purpose-built space that maintains stable temperature, humidity, and fresh air exchange. Good grow room design reduces daily maintenance, improves consistency, and maximizes yields.

## Environmental Parameters

### Temperature

Psilocybe cubensis thrives at 70-78F (21-26C) during both colonization and fruiting. Temperature requirements:

- **Colonization**: 75F (24C) is optimal. Temperatures above 80F promote bacterial growth without significantly speeding mycelial growth. Below 65F, colonization slows dramatically.
- **Fruiting**: 70-75F (21-24C) is ideal. A slight drop from colonization temperature (3-5 degrees) can help trigger fruiting.
- **Avoid temperature swings**: Rapid changes stress mycelium. Stable temperatures produce more consistent results.

### Humidity

- **Colonization**: Ambient humidity is fine (40-60%). Jars and tubs maintain their own internal humidity.
- **Fruiting**: 85-95% relative humidity is required for proper mushroom development. The monotub's internal humidity is maintained by the substrate moisture and evaporation, supplemented by misting.
- **Grow room ambient**: Maintaining 50-60% ambient humidity in the grow room reduces substrate moisture loss and lessens the need for misting.

### Fresh Air Exchange (FAE)

- **Colonization**: Minimal FAE needed. High CO2 is acceptable and even beneficial during this phase.
- **Fruiting**: Critical. Mushrooms are obligate aerobes — they require oxygen and produce CO2. Elevated CO2 causes elongated stems, small caps, and reduced yields. The grow room itself should have adequate ventilation; individual tubs handle their own FAE through polyfill-stuffed holes.

### Lighting

- **Colonization**: Not required. Ambient room light or darkness is fine. Light does not harm colonizing mycelium.
- **Fruiting**: 12 hours on / 12 hours off cycle. Indirect light is sufficient — mushrooms do not photosynthesize. Light serves as a directional cue and triggers the fruiting response.
- **Recommended**: 6500K daylight spectrum LED strips or bulbs. Avoid direct sunlight (causes heating and drying). A single LED strip mounted above the tubs is sufficient for a small grow room.

## Room Selection and Preparation

### Ideal Characteristics

- **Size**: Minimum 4x4 feet for 2-4 monotubs. Larger rooms can accommodate 10+ tubs.
- **Flooring**: Easy to clean. Tile, concrete, or vinyl is ideal. Avoid carpet (harbors contaminants).
- **Walls**: Smooth, cleanable surfaces. Painted drywall is fine.
- **Ventilation**: Some natural air exchange. A room with a door and at least one window or HVAC vent.
- **Power**: Access to multiple outlets for lights, humidifiers, and fans.
- **Water source**: Nearby access for misting and cleaning.

### Preparation Steps

1. Clean the room thoroughly — floors, walls, ceiling, corners.
2. Seal any major air leaks around windows and doors.
3. Paint walls with a mold-resistant paint if possible.
4. Install shelving — wire shelving allows air circulation and is easy to clean.
5. Set up a thermometer/hygrometer with a remote sensor to monitor conditions.
6. Install lighting on a timer.

## Environmental Control Equipment

### Heating

- **Space heater**: The simplest solution for cold rooms. Use a thermostat-controlled model placed away from the tubs. Avoid radiant heaters that create hot spots.
- **Seedling heat mat with thermostat**: Can be placed under tubs during colonization, but risks overheating the substrate. Not recommended for fruiting.
- **In-line duct heater**: For rooms with existing ventilation systems.
- **Aquarium heater**: Some growers place jars in a water bath warmed by an aquarium heater during cold months.

### Cooling

- **Air conditioning**: The most effective cooling method. Even a small window unit can maintain optimal temperatures.
- **Exhaust fan**: Venting warm air out of the room.
- **Avoid**: Swamp coolers — they raise humidity too high.

### Humidification

- **Ultrasonic humidifier**: The standard choice for grow room humidification. Produces a cool mist without raising temperature. Place on a timer or hygrostat for automatic control.
- **Evaporative humidifier**: Less precise but adequate for maintaining ambient humidity.
- **Important**: Use distilled or RO water in humidifiers to prevent mineral buildup and white dust.
- **Placement**: Humidifiers should not blow directly onto tubs — this can cause localized over-humidification. Position them to raise room ambient humidity evenly.

### Fresh Air Exchange

- **Passive**: Open a door or window slightly. Adequate for small setups.
- **Active exhaust**: A small inline fan venting to the outside or another room. Pair with an intake vent for makeup air.
- **CO2 monitoring**: A CO2 meter (available for $20-50) helps ensure levels stay below 1000 ppm during fruiting. Above 1500 ppm, fruiting quality degrades noticeably.

## Shelving and Layout

### Shelving Options

- **Wire shelving units**: The gold standard. Open design allows air circulation on all sides of the tubs. Easy to wipe down and disinfect. Adjustable shelf height accommodates different tub sizes. 48x18 inch units are common.
- **Plastic shelving**: Budget option. Less airflow but adequate for small setups.
- **Wood shelving**: Not recommended — porous surface harbors contaminants and is hard to clean.

### Layout Principles

- Maintain at least 6 inches between tubs for air circulation.
- Place larger tubs on lower shelves (stability).
- Leave walkway space for maintenance (misting, harvesting, cleaning).
- Position lighting to reach all tubs evenly.
- Keep humidifiers and heaters away from direct contact with tubs.

### Capacity Planning

- A 48x18 inch wire shelf can hold 2-3 standard monotubs per level.
- A 4-shelf unit can accommodate 8-12 tubs.
- Each 66-quart monotub requires approximately 4 square feet of floor space including clearance.

## Contamination Prevention in the Grow Room

- **Clean regularly**: Wipe shelves and surfaces with 10% bleach solution or 70% alcohol weekly.
- **HEPA air purifier**: Run continuously or on a timer to reduce airborne spore count.
- **Separate colonization and fruiting areas**: If possible, colonize jars in a separate space from fruiting tubs. Fruiting produces massive spore loads that can contaminate colonizing cultures.
- **Remove contaminated materials immediately**: Do not open contaminated tubs or jars in the grow room. Remove them sealed and dispose of them outside.
- **No carpet**: If the room has carpet, cover it with a plastic tarp or sheet that can be wiped down.
- **Shoe protocol**: Remove shoes or wear dedicated indoor shoes in the grow room.
- **Negative pressure**: Slightly negative air pressure (more air leaving than entering) prevents spore-laden air from escaping to the rest of the house.

## Automation

- **Inkbird or similar controllers**: Dual-stage temperature and humidity controllers can automate heaters and humidifiers.
- **Timers**: For lighting (12/12), humidifiers (on/off cycles), and fans.
- **Smart plugs**: WiFi-enabled plugs allow remote monitoring and control via smartphone.
- **Data logging**: Some hygrometers log temperature and humidity over time, useful for identifying patterns and problems.

## Budget Considerations

A basic grow room setup can be assembled for $100-300:
- Shelving: $50-100
- Thermometer/hygrometer: $15-30
- LED light strip with timer: $15-25
- Small humidifier: $20-40
- Space heater (if needed): $20-40
- HEPA air purifier: $50-100
