# EMCDDA Hallucinogenic Mushroom Cultivation and Growkit Market

## Overview

While hallucinogenic mushrooms grow wild in much of Europe, the EMCDDA 2006
thematic paper noted that most recreationally used mushrooms were cultivated
rather than picked wild. The development of home cultivation methods and the
commercial growkit market represented a significant supply channel for
hallucinogenic mushrooms, enabling users to produce their own supply
independently of retail outlets. Cultivation products were sold through both
smartshops and online retailers across Europe.

## Why Cultivate Rather Than Forage?

Several factors favoured cultivated mushrooms over wild-harvested ones for
recreational use:

- **Consistent supply**: Cultivated mushrooms can be produced year-round,
  free from seasonal constraints that affect wild species.
- **Known species**: Commercial cultivation primarily uses Psilocybe cubensis
  varieties, which do not grow wild in Europe, ensuring species consistency.
- **Ease of cultivation**: Cubensis varieties are easy to grow in
  terrariums, making them accessible to novice cultivators.
- **Shelf life**: Cultivated mushrooms possess a low water content that
  allows them to stay fresh for 7 to 10 days (Levitt et al, 2006).
- **Dose predictability**: Cultivated mushrooms from known strains offer
  more consistent potency than wild-harvested specimens.

## Home Cultivation Products

The EMCDDA identified several product types available for home cultivation
of hallucinogenic mushrooms:

### Spore Prints and Spore Syringes

Spore prints are created by pressing the gills of a mature mushroom onto
paper, capturing the spore pattern. Spore syringes are created by
transferring spores from a print into a syringe under sterile conditions.
The spores are then used to inoculate a substrate of nutrients free of
bacteria and molds. Just as a house plant is grown in soil, mushrooms can
be grown on a cake of substrate material. Spore prints are available for a
wide variety of magic mushroom species.

### Spawnbags

Spawn is the pure culture of mycelium already grown through a substrate.
The substrate typically consists of woodchips. Mushroom spawn bags contain
sterile hardwood chips inoculated with mushroom mycelium, providing a
head start for the growth process. Spawnbags for species such as
Psilocybe azurescens were available through online retailers.

### Growkits

Growkits represent the most accessible cultivation method for beginners.
They are available for various types of magic mushrooms, including
Psilocybe cubensis and Psilocybe tampensis. A typical growkit contains:

- A box with colonised substrate (the growing medium already inoculated
  with mycelium).
- A bag with an air filter for humidity control.
- Paperclips for securing the bag.
- Instructions for the cultivation process.

A growkit can produce multiple flushes (harvests) of mushrooms, making them
a cost-effective option for regular users.

## The Free Spore Ring Europe

The "Free Spore Ring Europe" (FSRE) was identified as a non-profit making
organisation that distributed various strains of hallucinogenic mushroom
spores. Users could obtain strains of species such as Psilocybe cubensis
and Panaeolus cyanescens for a price of approximately 1 EUR per print. This
organisation represented an informal distribution network operating outside
conventional commercial channels.

## Online Cultivation Resources

Instructions on growing hallucinogenic mushrooms using spore prints and
a substrate could be easily found on the internet. Websites provided
step-by-step guides covering sterile technique, substrate preparation,
environmental conditions (temperature, humidity, light), harvesting and
drying techniques, and troubleshooting common cultivation problems.

The widespread availability of cultivation information online lowered the
barriers to home production, contributing to the diffusion of
hallucinogenic mushroom use beyond countries with legal retail markets.

## Pricing of Cultivation Products

Prices for cultivation products sold by online shops in January 2006
varied by product type:

| Product                          | Price Range (EUR) |
|----------------------------------|--------------------|
| Spawnbag (Psilocybe azurescens)  | 24.00 - 25.00     |
| Growkits (varies by species)     | 25.00 - 100.00    |
| Spore prints/syringes            | 9.00 - 28.00      |
| Free Spore Ring Europe prints    | ~1.00 each         |

## Terrarium Cultivation

The commercial cultivation of Psilocybe cubensis varieties was primarily
conducted in terrariums, mostly in the Netherlands. Terrarium cultivation
allows precise control of environmental conditions including temperature,
humidity, and air exchange, producing reliable yields for the market.

## Legal Status of Cultivation Materials

The legal status of cultivation materials varied across jurisdictions:

- In some countries, spore prints themselves were not controlled (as they
  contain no psilocybin or psilocin), while the resulting mushrooms were.
- Other countries specifically criminalised cultivation of hallucinogenic
  mushrooms (e.g., Estonia clarified this in May 2005).
- Germany's 2005 legal amendment changed the definition of controlled
  substance from "plant" to "organism" partly to address cultivation of
  mushrooms.

## Impact of Legal Changes on Cultivation Market

Following the UK ban in July 2005, internet sites stopped selling growkits.
One site (allsalvia.co.uk) was "sold out" in July 2005 with a notice that
growkits were about to become Class A controlled drugs. The Irish online
headshop THC stopped offering mushroom products from January 2006. Despite
legal restrictions, spore prints and cultivation information remained
accessible online, raising enforcement challenges.
