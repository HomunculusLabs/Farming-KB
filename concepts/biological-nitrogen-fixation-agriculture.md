## The Nitrogenase Enzyme Complex

Nitrogenase consists of two metalloprotein components. The **Fe protein**
(dinitrogenase reductase, encoded by *nifH*) is a homodimer (~60–70 kDa)
containing a [4Fe-4S] cluster that serves as the obligate electron donor,
binding one ATP per subunit. The **MoFe protein** (dinitrogenase, encoded by
*nifD*/*nifK*) is an α₂β₂ heterotetramer containing the **P-cluster**
([8Fe-7S]) and the **FeMo-cofactor** ([Mo-7Fe-9S-C-homocitrate]) — the actual
N₂ reduction site with a central carbon interstitial. Three isozyme types exist:
Mo-nitrogenase (100% activity, *nifHDK*), V-nitrogenase (~50%, *vnfHDGK* under
Mo-limitation), and Fe-only nitrogenase (~25%, *anfHDGK* under combined Mo/V
limitation). At least 1 H₂ is produced per N₂ reduced; uptake hydrogenase
(*hup* genes) recaptures some, improving efficiency by 10–15%. The catalytic
mechanism follows the Lowe-Thorneley scheme: electrons accumulate on the
FeMo-cofactor through transient Fe protein docking (2 ATP per electron), N₂
binds at an Fe atom, and undergoes sequential hydrogenation through diazene
and hydrazine intermediates to yield 2 NH₃. The *nif* regulon is controlled by
**NifA**, regulated by N-status sensing (NtrC/NtrB, GlnD/GlnK) and, in
rhizobia, by the FixL/FixJ oxygen-sensing system.
