# Biological Monitoring and Control

Biological monitoring is the essential feedback mechanism in Allan Savory's
[[savory-water-cycle-management]], [[savory-water-cycle-management]], [[savory-holistic-resource-management-brittle-vs-nonbrittle-environments]] worldwide, the
procedure involves finding and marking severely grazed plants alongside
ungrazed plants of the same species as livestock leave each paddock. A two
or three-foot wire carrying a fluorescent flag makes relocating sample
plants simple, and a kink in the wire records the grazed-down height.

Periodically return to the marked plants and assess recovery using the
nearby ungrazed plants as a yardstick. If plants have barely grown and
livestock are due back soon, the rotation is too fast for prevailing slow
growth and overgrazing will occur.
If plants are already indistinguishable from ungrazed plants but livestock
are not due back for weeks, the move is too slow and livestock are held
longer than necessary on fouled ground.

## The Recovery Question

When has a plant recovered? Research confirms overgrazing as a function of time, but few have investigated
the point of full recovery. In practice this matters little because so many
factors obscure the question: myriad species with different growth rates,
slope, soil variations, shade, and aeration. One considers severely grazed
plants recovered when they resemble ungrazed plants alongside them under
virtually identical conditions.

## Fast Growth/Fast Move, Slow Growth/Slow Move

The general rule of thumb is simple: fast growth calls for fast moves,
slow growth for slow moves. When some grasses grow slowly and others fast
simultaneously (as with warm-season and cool-season species overlapping
in spring), the flexibility of the holistic approach allows pencil-and-paper
analysis of the situation to determine the best course for each paddock.

## Implementing Control

Depending on monitoring results, either continue applying tools as planned
or make adjustments. When a tool is not producing the expected tendency:
1. Apply the same tool but in a different manner
2. Apply another tool altogether
3. Apply a combination of available tools

If diagnosis shows that the needed tool is one excluded by the goal itself,
the goal needs reexamination. Tools should never be mentioned in goals
(beyond brief reference to forms of production), as this can eliminate
options essential to achieving the landscape goal.
