# Cannabis Vegetative Growth Stage

## Overview

The vegetative stage is the primary growth phase of the cannabis plant,
during which it develops the structural framework that will support flower
production. During this cycle, the plant begins to grow quickly and produce
more leaves and new branches. The stem also grows thicker. This is when the
plant begins to look like a mature cannabis specimen. Understanding and
managing this stage properly is essential for maximizing yield and plant
health.

## Transition from Seedling to Vegetative Growth

After the seedling stage, when the plant has developed its first set of
true leaves and a stable root system, it transitions into vegetative growth.
The seedling is moved to its permanent pot and placed under grow lights on
an 18-6 or 24-hour light cycle. During this transition, the grower should
refrain from feeding for about a week after transplanting to allow roots to
establish. Some transplant feeding products are available but are not
strictly necessary.

## Light Requirements

Your plant needs all the light it can get during vegetative growth. Leave
lights on 24 hours a day for maximum growth, or use an 18 hours on, 6 hours
off cycle which some growers prefer as it may reduce electricity costs while
maintaining robust growth. Metal halide (MH) lights are ideal for
vegetative growth due to their blue spectrum, though high-pressure sodium
(HPS) lamps can also be used.

Adjust lights carefully as the plant grows. Some cannabis plants can grow
as fast as an inch per day, so monitor the distance between the light and
the plant canopy. The closer the light is to the plant, the more light it
receives, but if too close it will burn the leaves. If you can keep your
hand at a certain area under the light without discomfort, the plants should
do okay too. Use common sense and adjust accordingly.

## Watering Practices

Water plants every second or third day, or better still when they dry out.
Never let the soil dry out completely for long periods. A practical method
involves a cyclical pattern: Day A (watering), Day B (let dry), Day C (let
dry), Day D (check soil and water if needed). This depends heavily on pot
size.

You can judge watering needs by weight. Pick up the pot when dry and again
when watered to feel the difference. Watch plants for 2-3 days after
watering. If fan leaves point outward to receive light, the plant is happy.
If they wilt downward and look like claws, you may be over-watering or
under-watering. Check the soil moisture to determine which.

Over-watering kills plants if it continues. If it happens, let the soil dry
out and hope for the best. Use a fan near the soil surface to improve
evaporation if needed. You may need to water daily if your light discharges
significant heat and your soil drains quickly.

## Plant Structure and Training

During vegetative growth, the plant develops its branching architecture.
Indica plants tend to have short internodes (3 inches or less between
branches) with rounded leaves and marble-like patterns. Sativa plants have
long internodes (3 to 6 inches between branches) with pointy leaves and
no markings. Ruderalis plants have very short internodes with much
branching and small, thick leaves.

Training techniques during vegetative growth include topping (cutting the
main stem tip to encourage lateral branching), low-stress training (LST,
bending stems to create an even canopy), and screen of green (SCROG, using
a screen to train branches horizontally). These techniques increase the
number of main colas and improve light penetration through the canopy.

Fans should be positioned to circulate air around the plants. This helps
develop stronger stems and branches through wind stimulation, prevents hot
spots under lights, reduces humidity buildup, and strengthens the overall
plant structure for the flowering stage.

## Soil and pH Management

Cannabis plants like a steady pH of around 7 when grown in soil. Check pH
at least once per week and a day or two after feeding. The removal of
nutrients and addition of waste material by the plant may cause pH to shift.
If pH moves outside the 6-8 range, correction is needed. Small pH meters
can be purchased in most grow shops for monitoring.

## Pot Size and Transplanting

Cannabis plants are best kept in pots that are somewhat large (1.5 to 3
gallon pots) because the plant grows long, winding roots. Always use one pot
per plant so that if problems arise with soil, watering, or nutrients, only
one plant is affected. Pots should have perforations at the bottom for
drainage, sitting on dishes to catch runoff that should be emptied.

Double-potting is a technique that helps create thicker stems. The bottom
of the top pot is cut away and placed on top of a new pot with fresh soil.
The roots grow down into the lower pot, progressively building a stronger
root system. Clean all pots thoroughly before use.

## Environmental Monitoring

Maintain temperatures between 70-85 degrees F (21-29 degrees C) during the
day and slightly cooler at night. Relative humidity should be between 50-70%
during vegetative growth. Monitor CO2 levels if supplementing, as enriched
CO2 can dramatically increase growth rates when all other conditions are
optimized. Good air circulation and ventilation are essential for healthy
vegetative growth.
