# Clove as a CB2-Active Medicinal Botanical

## Overview

Clove (*Syzygium aromaticum*) is a culinary spice with an extraordinarily broad medicinal profile. The Cannabis Health Index (CHI) identifies clove as containing approximately 4% (E)-beta-caryophyllene, making it a dietary CB2 receptor activator. Beyond its cannabinoid activity, clove's essential oil contains eugenol and eugenine, compounds with demonstrated antiviral, antibacterial, anticancer, analgesic, and anti-inflammatory properties supported by multiple peer-reviewed studies.

## Botanical Profile and CB2 Content

Clove is the dried flower bud of *Syzygium aromaticum*, a tree native to the Maluku Islands (Spice Islands) of Indonesia. A tested sample of clove contained almost 4% (E)-beta-caryophyllene, placing it among the CB2-activating spices catalogued in the CHI's "Spice Power" section. While its (E)-BCP content is lower than Ashanti pepper (52 to 58%) or Indian bay-leaf (25%), clove compensates with an exceptionally wide range of additional bioactive compounds, most notably eugenol and eugenine.

## Comprehensive Medicinal Properties

Studies have shown the essential oil of clove to work across an impressive spectrum of therapeutic categories:

- **Analgesic**: pain-relieving properties
- **Anti-inflammatory**: reduces inflammation through multiple pathways
- **Antioxidant**: combats oxidative stress and free radical damage
- **Antimicrobial**: effective against a range of pathogenic microorganisms
- **Antifungal**: combats fungal infections
- **Antiviral**: active against Herpes simplex I and II and hepatitis C
- **Antibacterial**: effective against several multi-drug-resistant *Staphylococcus epidermidis* strains
- **Anticancer**: induces apoptosis in cancer cells
- **Cancer protective**: demonstrated protective properties for skin and lung tissue
- **Antidiabetic**: insulin-like effects beneficial for diabetes treatment
- **Insect repellent**: natural pest deterrent
- **Aphrodisiac**: contains aphrodisiac properties

## Antiviral Research: Eugenine and HSV

Virologists from the Toyama Medical and Pharmaceutical University in Sugitani, Japan conducted a series of experiments on clove's antiviral properties. They determined that eugenine, a compound purified from clove extracts, inhibits viral DNA synthesis in several strains of herpes (I and II), including acyclovir-phosphonoacetic acid-resistant HSV-I.

This finding is particularly significant because acyclovir-resistant herpes strains represent a growing clinical challenge. The fact that a natural compound from clove can inhibit DNA synthesis in drug-resistant viral strains positions clove as a potential adjunct or alternative when conventional antivirals fail.

## Antiviral Research: Hepatitis C

A Tunisian study reported in the National Library of Medicine determined that essential oil of clove extract has antiviral activity against hepatitis C virus. This same study also confirmed clove's antibacterial properties, including efficacy against several multi-drug-resistant strains of *Staphylococcus epidermidis*. A separate Tunisian study specifically confirmed clove's antiviral effects against hepatitis C in the context of digestive health conditions.

The hepatitis C findings are notable because hepatitis C remains a significant global health burden with limited treatment options in many regions. Clove's dual antiviral and antibacterial activity against drug-resistant organisms makes it a subject of ongoing research interest.

## Anticancer Properties: Lung and Skin

Research from Kolkata, India examined the properties of aqueous solution of clove and found it produced apoptosis (programmed cell death) of lung cancer cells in mice. The same study identified additional possible cancer-protective properties.

A second Indian study determined that aqueous solution of clove might also have protective properties against skin papillomas (skin tumors). These findings have been replicated across multiple disease entries in the CHI, appearing in the "Let Food Be Thy Medicine" sections for both lung cancer and skin cancer.

The mechanism of clove's anticancer activity is thought to involve multiple pathways: (E)-BCP's CB2-mediated anti-inflammatory action, eugenol's antioxidant properties, and direct induction of apoptotic pathways in malignant cells.

## Clove for Chronic Anal Fissures

A clinical study on clove's application in proctology demonstrated striking results. Patients suffering from chronic anal fissures were given a clove oil 1% cream preparation. Healing occurred in five times as many patients as in the control group. Additionally, the 1% clove cream patients had a greater reduction in resting anal pressure than those in the control group.

This finding represents one of the most clinically concrete applications of clove in the CHI, with a measurable fivefold improvement over control conditions. The combination of clove's anti-inflammatory, analgesic, and wound-healing properties likely contributes to this effect.

## Antidiabetic Potential

The CHI reports that researchers explored clove because it has insulin-like effects, which may prove beneficial in the treatment of diabetes. Data revealed that clove functions in ways analogous to insulin, opening possibilities for complementary diabetes management strategies, particularly in regions where conventional diabetes medications are unavailable or unaffordable.

## Clove as an Antioxidant

The essential oil of clove has demonstrated potent antioxidant properties. Oxidative stress is implicated in the pathogenesis of numerous chronic diseases including cardiovascular disease, neurodegenerative conditions, and cancer. Clove's antioxidant capacity, combined with its CB2-mediated anti-inflammatory action through (E)-BCP, creates a dual protective mechanism against oxidative damage.

## Integration with Cannabis Therapy

Within the CHI framework, clove represents a complementary botanical that can be used alongside cannabis therapy. Since clove activates CB2 receptors through its (E)-BCP content without producing psychoactive effects, it can supplement cannabinoid therapy's anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory actions. The potential synergy between clove's eugenol/eugenine compounds and cannabis-derived cannabinoids represents an area worthy of further investigation.

## Safety Considerations

While clove has an excellent safety profile as a culinary spice used worldwide for centuries, the concentrated essential oil should be used with appropriate caution. Clove oil applied topically at 1% concentration has been clinically validated for anal fissure treatment. Internal use of concentrated clove oil may cause gastrointestinal irritation in some individuals. As with all complementary therapies, clove should be discussed with healthcare providers when used as part of a treatment regimen.
