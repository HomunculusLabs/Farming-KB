# Insectary Plants Guide

Insectary plants attract, feed, and shelter beneficial insects -- pollinators, predators, and parasitoids that provide essential ecological services in any permaculture system. A year-round supply of nectar and pollen is the foundation of biological pest control and crop pollination. This guide covers bee forage systems, hedgerow design for beneficial insects, and specific pest-repelling plants.

## The Bee Forage System

Commercial apiarists may transport hives up to 1000 km to follow the honey flow. The expense is enormous, the hidden cost prohibitive -- essentially converting fossil fuel to honey with very poor efficiency. Alternatively, a year-round honey flow can be established for permanent hive sites.

The bee forage system should be integrated within a whole property design. Where possible, nectar (N) and pollen (P) producing plants are used in preference to other utility plants having no benefit to bees. Bees can effectively travel 2 miles from the hive.

## Hedgerow Bee Forage (Trees and Large Shrubs)

## Remnant Forest and Woodlot Trees

Supplement native forest with multipurpose species, especially those producing out-of-season with natives. Notable species:

- **Eucalyptus melliodora** (yellow box): Considered the best honey of all eucalypts; spring, summer
- **Eucalyptus salmonophloia** (salmon gum): Choice honey; summer, autumn
- **Eucalyptus microcarpa** (grey box): Good honey; summer, autumn, winter
- **Eucalyptus platypus** (moort): Good bee plant; winter, spring, summer
- **Eucalyptus maculata** (spotted gum): Major source of winter/spring nectar
- **Eucalyptus stellulata** (black Sally): Flowers most of the year
- **Eucryphia lucida** (leatherwood): Extended nectar flow
- **Banksia spp**: Banksia amula (winter), B. ericifolia (winter through summer), B. grandis (spring), B. integrifolia (autumn, winter, spring), B. serrata (summer), B. spinulosa (autumn, winter, spring)
- **Dryandra spp**: Autumn, winter

## Insectary Plants for Pest Control

### Cape Lilac (Melia azedarach) -- Natural Insecticide
The leaves, seeds, and fresh fruit contain azadarachtin, a naturally occurring systemic insecticide. Most insects starve rather than eat treated plants. Also repellent to nematodes and effective protecting stored food from pests.

Pest applications:
- **Cabbage worm**: Dust leaves with powdered fruit or 50-100% water extract spray
- **Grasshoppers and locusts**: 2% powdered fruit in water as antifeedant; dried seed dusting inhibits feeding
- **Mosquito larvae**: Seed oil kills at 250 ppm
- **Nematodes**: Green leaves applied to soil
- **Rice weevil**: 2.5-5 parts powdered fruit per 100 parts grain protects stored grain for 4 months
- **Tobacco cutworm**: Extracts of fruit
- **Fungi**: Cold water leaf extract inhibits growth

### Neem (Azadirachta indica) -- Natural Insecticide
Close relative of cape lilac with similar azadarachtin content. In India, leaves are dried and placed in books, grain, and clothes to repel insects. Twigs used as toothbrushes. Bark decoction treats fevers, nausea, liver complaints, and jaundice.

### Indian Beech (Derris indica) -- Stored Grain Protection
Dried leaves repel insects and protect stored grain. Also ploughed into soil as green manure; said to repel nematodes. Presscake is an effective insecticide and good poultry feed.

### Companion Plants for Pest Deterrence
Many garden plants repel pests or attract predators. See [[garden-plant-groupings]] for detailed companion planting lists.

Key insectary companions:
- **Marigolds**: Repel nematodes, attract pollinators
- **Nasturtiums**: Repel aphids, attract predatory insects
- **Garlic**: Repels codling moth, Japanese beetles
- **Rosemary**: Repels cabbage moth, bean beetles, carrot fly
- **Thyme**: Repels cabbage worm
- **Borage**: Attracts pollinators and predatory wasps
- **Dill**: Attracts parasitic wasps, swallowtail butterflies
- **Fennel**: Attracts beneficial insects (but can inhibit some crops)
