# Complete Organic Fertilizer (COF)

## Overview

Steve Solomon's Complete Organic Fertilizer (COF) is a custom-blended organic
fertilizer mix designed to provide balanced plant nutrition that produces
nutrient-dense vegetables. Solomon developed COF after decades of gardening
experience and considerable research into soil science. He concluded that
most organic gardeners rely too heavily on manure and compost alone, which
can produce bulky yields but fail to deliver optimal [[coleman-winter-greens-nutritional-quality-cold-sweetening]].

The core insight behind COF is that the nutritional content of vegetables
depends on the completeness and balance of minerals available in the soil.
Solomon observed that pushing potassium to high levels — as both commercial
chemical and organic growers commonly do — increases bulk yield in the form
of starch and fiber, but the resulting vegetables have proportionally less
protein, vitamins, enzymes, and minerals. COF is formulated to avoid this
imbalance, prioritizing nutritional quality over sheer tonnage of harvest.

## The COF Recipe

Solomon's formula uses volume measurements (parts by scoop, bucket, or jar),
not weight, because the bulk densities of ingredients vary too much for
weight-based proportioning to work reliably. The base recipe calls for:

- **4 parts** any kind of seed meal (except copra meal), OR **3 parts**
  any seed meal plus **1 part** tankage (blood-and-bone, meatmeal)
- **1 part** ordinary agricultural lime (finely ground)
- **1/2 part** gypsum (if omitted, double the agricultural lime)
- **1/2 part** dolomite lime
- **1 part** phosphorus source: rock phosphate, bonemeal, or guano
- **1 part** kelp meal, OR **1 part** basalt dust

The higher-nitrogen option (3 parts seed meal plus 1 part tankage) is
slightly better for leafy crops in spring. Seed meals — byproducts of
making vegetable oil from cottonseed, linseed, sunflower, soybean, or
canola — typically analyze at approximately 6-4-2 NPK. Copra meal
(from coconuts) is about one-third weaker. Tankage, a slaughterhouse
byproduct, typically analyzes around 10-4-0.

## Ingredient Details

**Seed meal** is the nitrogen-rich base. Buy whichever seed meal offers
the most protein per dollar. All seed meals also contain calcium,
magnesium, and trace nutrients beyond their NPK label. Store dry in a
sealed container with a tight lid.

**Agricultural lime** provides calcium carbonate. **Dolomitic lime**
contains both [[dynamic-accumulator-plants-calcium-and-magnesium]] carbonates. **Gypsum** is calcium
sulfate. A mixture of all three produces the best results, but any
single type can serve. Never use quicklime, burnt lime, or hydrated
lime. Lime products are inexpensive in bulk from agricultural dealers.

**Rock phosphate, bonemeal,** and **guano** supply phosphorus. Bonemeal
is rich in trace elements. Solomon notes that nutrient content in US
vegetables has been declining for decades, attributing this to trace
element depletion in agricultural soils.

**Kelp meal** adds a broad spectrum of trace minerals. It may seem
costly, but Solomon argues the health benefits justify the expense.
Basalt dust is a lower-cost alternative that also supplies trace minerals.

## Application Rates

Solomon's recommendations vary by crop demand category:

- **Low-demand vegetables:** 4-6 quarts COF per 100 sq ft, plus 50
  lb lime(s) per 1,000 sq ft
- **Medium-demand vegetables:** same COF rate plus 1/8-inch layer of
  composted chicken manure
- **High-demand vegetables:** same COF rate plus 1/4-inch layer of
  composted chicken manure

COF is potent — do not overapply. However, exceeding compost or manure
amounts by half again or double is fine. Never apply more than double
the recommended organic matter, as this risks unbalancing soil minerals.

## Soil pH Controversy

Solomon takes a firm and controversial position on soil pH: he advises
gardeners to forget about it entirely. Liming solely to adjust pH is a
practice borrowed from conventional chemical farming and is unnecessary
in a well-managed organic garden. If a soil test shows low pH, Solomon
says don't lime to correct it — just add what his standard program calls
for each year. Over time, pH corrects itself, more from the organic
matter than from calcium and magnesium additions.

Even when pH is acceptable, Solomon still recommends his full lime
regimen because vegetables need calcium and magnesium in the right
balance. COF is formulated so that at the recommended application rate,
it automatically distributes about 50 pounds of lime per 1,000 square
feet per year. Gardeners who routinely use COF may never need to lime
separately.

## Slow-Release Mechanism

Unlike [[cannabis-chemical-fertilizers]] that dissolve rapidly in water, COF works
through the complex ecology of living soil creatures. Organic materials
release nutrients only as they decompose. The rate of decomposition
roughly doubles for each 10°F (5°C) increase in soil temperature.
Complete decomposition of COF takes around two months in warm soil, with
nutrients steadily released throughout. Gardeners in hot climates get a
bigger result from smaller applications, though the effect is shorter-
lived. This slow-release mechanism feeds plants gradually, more closely
matching the pace at which vegetables absorb nutrients, and nutrients are
far less likely to wash out during heavy rain.

## Side-Dressing Technique

For medium- and high-demand vegetables, Solomon recommends side-dressing
during [[query-how-to-protect-plants-from-frost-and-extend-the-growing-season]] — applying additional COF or chicken manure
compost alongside established plants for a mid-season nutrient boost.
This is particularly important for heavy-feeding crops like tomatoes,
corn, and brassicas with extended growing seasons and continued nutrient
demands after initial fertilization.

## Key Points

- COF is a custom-blended organic fertilizer designed for nutritional
  completeness, not just bulk yield
- The recipe centers on seed meal for nitrogen, with lime, phosphate,
  and kelp or basalt dust for minerals and trace elements
- Application rate is 4-6 quarts per 100 square feet by crop demand
- Solomon advises ignoring soil pH tests; let [[savory-soil-organic-matter-and-decomposition-dynamics]] COF's
  balanced lime correct pH naturally over time
- COF works through slow biological decomposition over approximately
  two months, not rapid chemical dissolution
- Overapplying COF is harmful; exceeding compost/manure by double is
  the safe upper limit
