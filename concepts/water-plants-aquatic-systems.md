# Water Plants for Aquatic Systems

Water plants are essential components of any aquatic system in
[[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-permaculture-design-principles]]. They provide direct food for humans and fish,
oxygenate water, filter excess nutrients, and create habitat for
aquatic organisms. No aquatic system is complete without a diverse
range of plants filling different ecological niches.

## Plant Categories

Water plants can be classified into four broad groups:

- **Submerged plants**: Roots act only as anchors; [[arbuscule-isolation-and-nutrient-exchange-mechanisms]]
  occurs across the entire plant surface. Valuable for fish production
  as they oxygenate water directly
- **Floating-leaved plants**: Often useful to humans but less so for
  fish. Found in calm waters; some anchor to the bottom while others
  float freely
- **Emergent plants**: Roots and stems below water, leaves and flowers
  above. Shallows in ponds can be dedicated to these species
- **Margin plants**: Grow at the water's edge, roots may be
  seasonally submerged

## Utility Submerged Plants

**Plankton** (algae, bacteria, flagellates, diatoms) form the base of
the aquatic food web. A reasonable balance can be established by
introducing water from an established aquatic system such as a lake,
creek, or large pond.

## Floating Plants

### Azolla spp

A small, perennial, free-floating fern that hosts a microscopic alga
(Anabaena azolla) in its leaves. The alga fixes nitrogen from the
atmosphere, making it available to the Azolla as ammonia. Key benefits:

- Used extensively in Vietnam as a companion in rice paddies
- Feeds tilapia and other fish in [[fukuoka-textdoc-creating-rice-paddy-natural-methods]] systems
- Valuable food for pigs, poultry, and waterfowl
- Controls mosquitoes and weeds by covering water surface
- Excellent nitrogen-rich mulch when dried; similar water-holding
  properties to [[query-what-is-the-difference-between-coco-coir-and-peat-moss]]
- Research shows potential for hydrogen production from water

### Duckweed (Lemnaceae Family)

The 40+ species of free-floating duckweed are among the most vigorously
growing plants on the planet, some doubling their numbers every three
days. They are valuable for removing excess nutrients from water and can
concentrate boron, aluminium, manganese, iron, titanium, copper, and
cobalt from water into their tissue.

**[[wolffia-arrhiza]]**: The smallest flowering plant on Earth. Edible to
humans with 20% protein, 44% carbohydrate, 5% fat, plus vitamins A, B2,
B6, and G. Cultivated in Burma, Laos, and Thailand as a vegetable.
Annual yield: 265 tons wet weight or 10.5 tons dry weight/ha/year.

**Spirodella spp**: Can produce the equivalent feed value of 60 ha of
soybeans in just 1 ha. Protein 37-45%, nitrogen 6-7%, phosphorous
1.4-3%, potassium 1.5-3%.

### Neptunia oleracea (Water Mimosa)

A floating, leguminous plant native to Asia, Africa, and tropical
America. Grown in tanks in Thailand and Vietnam. Leaves and stems are
eaten in salads, seed pods are cooked and eaten, and the whole plant can
be cooked as greens.

### Trapa spp (Water Chestnut)

A perennial floating plant native to Europe, India, and eastern Asia.
The kernels of the spiny fruit are eaten. Note: has become a pestilent
weed in some regions.
