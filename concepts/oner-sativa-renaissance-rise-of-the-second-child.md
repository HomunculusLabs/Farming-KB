# The Sativa Renaissance — Rise of the Second Child

## Sativa as the Overlooked Sibling

For the last couple of decades of [[comparison-cannabis-indoor-growing-basics-vs-cannabis-outdoor-growing]], which favored the short
stature, hardiness, and heavy yields of indica plants, pushed sativa
into the background. While indica dominated the market with compact,
fast-flowering plants suited to small grow spaces, sativa was left
waving its arms trying to get attention.

The reasons for this neglect were practical. Many growers simply
lack the [[cannabis-sativa-indoor-cultivation-techniques]] created a fundamental mismatch with
sativa genetics. Indoor grow rooms, closets, and small tents are
designed for plants that stay short and finish quickly -- precisely
the opposite of sativa characteristics. Sativa plants are unruly
and difficult to raise indoors, and they are not slow to show their
displeasure when they do not receive the conditions they prefer.

Their tendency to stretch toward light sources, their long flowering
periods of 10 to 16 weeks, and their often lower yields per square
foot made them economically unattractive for commercial indoor
operations. The vast majority of growers, especially home growers,
simply did not have the space nor the opportunity to give sativas
what they needed to thrive.

## The Breeding Response

While most of the cannabis world was focused on indica-dominant
hybrids, sativa lovers were working quietly to bring their beloved
plants back to the forefront. Two parallel breeding strategies
emerged. The first approach involved making sativas more accessible
to the general public by breeding them with indicas to reduce
height, increase yield, and shorten flowering times.

Haze plants were crossed with indicas, Colombian landraces were
paired with various Kush strains, and the best breeders began
creating sativa-dominant hybrids that could perform well indoors.
