# Coastal Trees and Salt-Tolerant Forage Plants

Coastal and saline environments demand specially adapted tree species and forage plants capable of surviving salt spray, periodic inundation, and sandy or waterlogged soils. This page covers key tree species for coastal permaculture systems and salt-tolerant forage plants for livestock integration in challenging environments.

## Coastal Trees

### Grey Mangrove (*Avicennia marina*)
The common mangrove tree of estuaries, river banks, and sheltered coasts of Australia. These remarkable trees take their oxygen from specialised roots (pneumatophores) which thrust up from the mud. The soil is typically soft, moist, saline, and has no profile.

**Edible uses**: The seed, after three separate boilings, looks and tastes similar to avocado. The golden-yellow flowers yield nectar that makes a light, amber-coloured, pleasant honey from mid-autumn to early winter. The leaves are palatable to stock.

**Companion species**: *Casuarina glauca* (swamp oak) in the south on less inundated margins, and *Ceriops tagal* (small stilted mangrove) in the north. It is tolerant of some frost.

**Timber**: Extremely tough and hard wood with conspicuous brown growth rings, density about 880 kg/cu m. Has been used for knees and elbows in boat building.

### Grey Birch (*Betula populifolia*)
Also known as white birch or oldfield birch. A tree to 9 m that thrives on sandy or swampy soil. Extremely frost hardy. Best planted in groves for mutual protection and effective windbreaks.

### Cocoa Palm / Icaco Palm (*Chrysobalanus icaco*)
An extremely hardy shrub or tree (to 12 m) with branches that creep in all directions. Native to south Florida and the Caribbean, it grows in dense clumps on the sea shore and in both fresh and saline swamps, extending well inland and into mountain regions — it may well prove frost tolerant.

**Food uses**: The fruit was an important food of the Seminole Indians. It can be eaten raw, stewed with sugar, dried like prunes, or made into jellies. Seed kernels are roasted and eaten. In preserves, the fruit is pierced right through to the kernel so the flavour penetrates into the seed, which is then taken from the shell and eaten.

### Bohemian Olive / Russian Olive (*Elaeagnus angustifolia*)
Also known as trebizond date or oleaster. A deciduous small tree to 12 m and about 2 m wide, sometimes quite shrubby. Native to the Mediterranean through to SW Asia.

**Hardiness**: Extremely tough seaside plant, hardy to frost and very cold temperatures (−34°C). Grows right to the sea in France. Has a symbiotic relationship with *Frankia* spp. nitrogen-fixing soil microbes.

**Growing conditions**: Prefers dry, well-drained, alkaline soils in a sunny, open position. A good pioneer species, but has thorns. Occurs naturally with *Tamarix gallica*.

**Food uses**: Yellow, sweet berries eaten fresh or made into pies, sauces, jams, jellies, sherbets, and wines. Berries provide poultry forage in late summer.

**[[biodiversity-of-fungi-ecosystem-services-agriculture]]**: Good windbreak and hedge species providing bird habitat. Used [[comparison-vetiver-grass-vs-bamboo-for-erosion-control]].

### New Zealand Christmas Tree (*Metrosideros tomentosa*)
A large tree (to 10 m), somewhat frost hardy. Commonly found growing with its roots dangling into the sea. Hardy to salt spray.

### Myoporum (*Myoporum laetum*)
Native to New Zealand, this fast-growing evergreen tree (to 6 m) is very hardy in exposed places. Recommended for planting by the sea.

### Black Tupelo (*Nyssa sylvatica*)
Also known as pepperidge, black gum, or sour gum. A frost-hardy tree to 30 m. The fruit is edible raw and useful in preserves. Also *Nyssa aquatica* and *Nyssa ogeche* would be worthy of trial in coastal systems.

### Red Bay / Bull Bay (*Persea borbonica*)
An evergreen, frost-hardy tree occurring in low areas near the sea along the eastern coast of USA. A relative of the avocado, the leaves are used as flavouring similar to bay leaves. Can also be made into a tea.

### European Aspen (*Populus tremula*)
A small, suckering, open-headed tree native to Europe that withstands full exposure to the sea. Hardy to frost and extremely resistant to salt.

### Soapberry (*Sapindus saponaria*)
A small evergreen tree (to 8 m) native to Europe, which grows well in places where salt water overflows frequently.

### West Indian Mahogany (*Swietenia mahagoni*)
Also known as Madeira redwood. An evergreen tree to 24 m native of southern USA and the Caribbean. Common in low-lying limestone swamps close to the sea, where it produces wood of exceptional quality. Survives just above saline water. Considered frost tender.

### Bald Cypress (*Taxodium distichum*)
A swamp tree of the Everglades. Has been found to be quite frost hardy despite its tropical origins.

## Salt-Tolerant Forage Plants

### Palmer Saltgrass (*Distichlis palmeri*)
A perennial to about 0.5 m growing in tidal flats and salty marshlands off the Gulf of California. Thrives with tidal inundation of seawater. The seed falls to the water and is washed ashore where it is traditionally harvested by the Seri Indians.

Once established, this plant should not need replanting. It is fast-growing and extremely dense as the plant spreads by means of rhizomes. Field tests with hybrid cultivars are producing 1 tonne of grain to the hectare — a promising salt-tolerant grain crop.

### Saltgrass (*Distichlis spicata*)
There are distinct inland and sea shore ecotypes. Some 20,000 hectares of salt flats are under cultivation near Mexico City for cattle fodder, demonstrating the commercial viability of this species.

### Russian Thistle (*Salsola iberica*)
A salt- and drought-tolerant annual with crude protein of 15–20% and a similar amino acid breakdown to alfalfa. Useful as annual forage in saline, arid environments.

### Kallar Grass (*Leptochloa fusca*)
A highly salt-tolerant perennial forage species. Grows best on permanently wet soils in tropical and southern Africa. Valuable for integrating livestock into saline land rehabilitation.

## Integration Strategies for Coastal Permaculture

1. **Mangrove buffers**: Use *Avicennia marina* and companion species as frontline [[query-why-are-mangroves-critical-for-climate-change-adaptation-and-coastal-protection]], with their pneumatophore root systems stabilising shorelines
2. **Graduated plantings**: Zone from salt-tolerant pioneers (Russian olive, sea grape) to more demanding species as shelter improves
3. **Livestock integration**: Salt-tolerant forages (Palmer saltgrass, kallar grass, Russian thistle) enable grazing on marginal saline land while plants rehabilitate soil
4. **Nitrogen fixation**: Species like *Elaeagnus angustifolia* with *Frankia* symbiosis [[query-how-does-rock-dust-improve-soil-fertility]] in coastal sands
5. **Honey production**: Many coastal trees and shrubs (mangrove, tea tree, melaleuca) provide nectar flows for beekeeping
6. **Multi-crop orchards**: Species like icaco palm and sea grape provide fruit while serving as windbreaks
