# Trichoderma Biocontrol Fungi in AACT

**Source:** Elaine Ingham, *The Field Guide for [[actively-aerated-compost-tea]] Compost Tea*
**Domain:** Biocontrol → Beneficial Fungi → Disease Suppression

---

## Overview

Trichoderma species are among the most important biocontrol fungi
incorporated into compost tea systems. These fast-growing, opportunistic
saprotrophs provide multiple mechanisms of disease suppression including
mycoparasitism, antibiosis, competition for nutrients and space, and
[[endophytic-mycorrhizal-induced-systemic-resistance]] resistance in plants. Ingham's field guide devotes
extensive coverage to Trichoderma biology, selection, preactivation
methods, and incorporation into AACT brewing.

Trichoderma species are naturally present in most healthy soils and
mature composts, but their populations can be boosted dramatically
through intentional inoculation. They are particularly effective against
soil-borne fungal pathogens including Fusarium, Rhizoctonia, Pythium,
Sclerotinia, and Verticillium.

## Key Species

The most commonly used Trichoderma species in biocontrol include:

- **[[trichoderma-harzianum]]**: The most widely studied and commercially
  available biocontrol species. Effective against a broad range of
  soil-borne pathogens. Strain T-22 is the most well-known commercial
  preparation.
- **Trichoderma viride**: Early commercial biocontrol strain with
  strong cellulolytic activity.
- **[[trichoderma-koningii]]**: Effective against Rhizoctonia and
  Sclerotinia species.
- **Trichoderma hamatum**: Good activity against Fusarium species.

## Mechanisms of Action

**Mycoparasitism**: Trichoderma directly attacks pathogenic fungi. It
detects [[query-how-do-plants-use-chemical-signals-to-defend-against-insects]] from host fungi, grows toward them, coils
around the hyphae, and secretes cell-wall-degrading enzymes
(chitinases, glucanases, proteases) that lyse the pathogen's cells.
This process is highly specific and energy-intensive but devastatingly
effective at the point of contact.

**Antibiosis**: Trichoderma produces [[antifungal-secondary-metabolites-coprophilous-fungi]] metabolites:
- Trichodermin and trichodermol (trichothecene antibiotics)
- Harzianic acid
- 6-Pentyl-alpha-pyrone (6-PAP, volatile antifungal)
- Gliotoxin and gliovirin (peptidyl antibiotics)

These create inhibition zones that suppress pathogen germination.

**Nutrient competition**: Trichoderma is an aggressive competitor for
carbon and nitrogen sources. It rapidly colonizes organic matter and
root surfaces, consuming nutrients that would otherwise be available to
pathogens. Its high growth rate allows it to outcompete most pathogenic
fungi in compost tea and soil environments.

**Induced Systemic Resistance (ISR)**: Root colonization triggers
systemic defense responses, producing elevated defense compounds even
in distant tissues, making the whole plant more resistant.

**Enzyme production**: Exceptionally high cellulase and chitinase levels
attack pathogen cell walls and break down organic matter, releasing
nutrients for plants and beneficial organisms.

## T-22 Commercial Strain

T-22 is a specific strain of T. harzianum developed at Cornell
University. It is the most widely available commercial Trichoderma
product and has been proven effective in numerous field trials. Key
properties:

- Effective against Rhizoctonia, Fusarium, Pythium, and Sclerotinia
- Enhanced rhizosphere competence through selection for root
  colonization ability
- Compatible with most organic production systems
- Available as granules, wettable powder, or pre-mixed into compost

Ingham notes that T-22 fungal biomass can be increased through proper
preactivation before adding to AACT brews.

## Preactivation Protocol

Trichoderma inoculum must be preactivated before adding to compost tea
to ensure vigorous growth. The preactivation process brings dormant
spores or conidia out of dormancy and into active growth:

**Basic preactivation:**
1. Prepare an activation medium (warm water with a food source)
2. Add Trichoderma inoculum (spores, granules, or colonized substrate)
3. Maintain at appropriate temperature (65-75°F / 18-24°C)
4. Provide gentle aeration if in liquid medium
5. Incubate 24-48 hours until visible growth
6. Add the actively growing culture to the AACT brew

**Activation mediums** (tested for effectiveness):
- Warm water with small amount of molasses
- Compost extract with additional fungal foods
- Diluted potato or grain extract
- Commercial Trichoderma-specific activation media

The test for successful preactivation is visible hyphal growth under
low magnification (40-100×). If only spores are visible after the
activation period, conditions were not favorable.

## Incorporation into AACT

Trichoderma should be added to compost tea during the brewing process
rather than after:

- Add preactivated culture 6-12 hours into a standard 24-hour brew
- Ensure adequate fungal food sources (humic acids, fish hydrolysate)
- Maintain dissolved oxygen above 6 ppm to support fungal growth
- Monitor for off-smells indicating anaerobic conditions that would
  favor pathogens over Trichoderma

Fungal-dominant teas require different brewing parameters than
bacterial-dominant teas: slightly higher temperature, more fungal food,
and longer brewing times (up to 36 hours in some protocols).

## Fungal Foods for Trichoderma

Specific food sources promote Trichoderma biomass in compost tea:
- Humic acids (preferred fungal food)
- Fish hydrolysate (protein source for fungal growth)
- Kelp meal (micronutrients and growth stimulants)
- Small amounts of simple sugars (molasses, but not excessive — osmotic
  stress can inhibit fungi)
- Alfalfa meal
- Paper or cardboard (cellulose source for Trichoderma cellulases)

The key is providing enough food for Trichoderma without overfeeding,
which shifts the tea toward bacterial dominance and can create anaerobic
conditions.

## Limitations and Considerations

- Temperature-dependent; activity drops below 50°F (10°C)
- Rare cases of opportunistic pathogenicity on stressed plants
- Excessive sugar can inhibit through osmotic stress
- UV light kills exposed spores; apply early morning or evening
- Requires aerobic conditions; anaerobic tea won't support growth
