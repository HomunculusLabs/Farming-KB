# Fungal Interactions with Living Plant Surfaces
Fungi colonize living plant surfaces in diverse ways, ranging from
obligate biotrophic parasites to saprobic epiphytes. The above-
ground portions of plants — leaves, stems, flowers, and fruits —
provide distinct habitats for specialized [[dighton-mycorrhizal-diversity-ecosystem-function]], and
agricultural productivity.
## Powdery Mildews (Erysiphaceae)
### Biology
The Erysiphaceae are a family of Ascomycetes whose species are
obligate parasites on above-ground parts of higher plants. They are
nourished by haustoria and develop a characteristic white powdery
conidial stage on the host surface.
### Diversity and Distribution
- 19-22 currently accepted genera; approximately 400 species
  (majority in genus Erysiphe)
- Hirata (1986) listed 9,838 angiosperm host species across 44
  orders, 169 families, and 1,617 genera
- 93% of hosts are dicotyledons; 634 of 662 monocotyledonous hosts
  are Gramineae
- No powdery mildews occur on gymnosperms, pteridophytes, or lower
  plants
- Greatest abundance and damage in California and Israel (warm,
  rain-free summers, intensive agriculture)
- North-temperate regions have far more host species than tropical,
  subtropical, arctic, or subarctic regions
- Herbaceous hosts (8,250) far exceed tree hosts (2,645)
### Recognition and Collection
- White, dusty conidial state coats leaf and stem surfaces
- Infected tissues may be stunted or chlorotic
- Black, globose perithecia less than 1mm diameter scattered over
  [[ascomycota]]).
More than 1,580 species; approximately 1,400 in genus Meliola.
Dark, superficial, circular colonies with characteristic hyphopodia.
Found from Chile to southern US, South Africa to Scotland, Japan to
Tasmania. More than 90% of hosts are dicotyledonous angiosperms.
## Fly-Speck Fungi
Belong to Microthyriaceae (Microthyriales). Named for dark,
flattened, shield-shaped ascomata appearing as small dark dots on
leaf surfaces. 447 species in 45 genera (Batista 1959). Largest
genus: Micropeltis (100+ species). Mostly tropical and subtropical.
Mycelium usually entirely superficial with haustoria in epidermal
cells.
