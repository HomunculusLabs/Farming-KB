# Pacific and Australian Neurotropic Mushroom Species

## Overview

The Australasian and Pacific regions harbor a significant and distinctive
neurotropic mycoflora. Australia, New Zealand, and New Guinea together
support numerous species, many of which show close biogeographic
relationships to American taxa, supporting the hypothesis of Gondwanan
or trans-Pacific distribution patterns.

## Australia

Australia has a moderately rich neurotropic fungal flora, documented
primarily by Hall (1973), Southcott (1974), Margot and Watling (1981), and
Allen et al. (1991).

### Australian Psilocybe Species

- **P. subaeruginosa**: The most common and widespread Australian
  neurotropic Psilocybe; found in southern Australia from autumn through
  winter; grows on decaying wood and wood chips; confirmed to contain
  psilocybin (Picker and Richards, 1970); extensively studied by
  Chang and Mills (1992)
- **P. australiana**: Described from Australia by Margot and Watling
  (1981); related to the subtropical species complex
- **P. cubensis**: Common in tropical and subtropical regions of
  Australia; found on bovine dung in northern regions
- **P. cyanescens**: Present in Australia (Margot and Watling, 1981)
- **P. semilanceata**: Found in southeastern Australia; confirmed by
  multiple studies (Allen et al., 1991)
- **P. eucalypta**: Described from Australia; associated with
  Eucalyptus forests
- **P. collybioides**: Found in Australia; however, Guzman (1983)
  noted this is not the same species as reported from Argentina;
  Australian material is synonymous with P. zapotecorum
- **P. subcubensis**: Present in Australia
- **P. tasmaniana**: Named for Tasmania; found in Tasmania and
  southeastern Australia

### Australian Non-Psilocybe Species

- **Amanita muscaria**: Introduced and naturalized; documented by
  Cleland (1976)
- **Copelandia cyanescens**: Present; confirmed by Pollock (1976)
- **[[gymnopilus-spectabilis-entity]]** and **G. purpuratus**: Both present
- **Panaeolina foenisecii**: Widespread; documented by Southcott (1974)
- **Panaeolus** species: P. ater, P. fimicola, P. olivaceus, P.
  papilionaceus, P. retirugis, P. sphinctrinus, P. subbalteatus
- **Claviceps paspali** and **C. purpurea**: Both present
- **[[gerronema-fibula]]**: Documented from Australia

## Tasmania

Tasmania supports several neurotropic species:
- **P. australiana**: Found in Tasmania
- **P. cubensis**: Present in Tasmania
- **P. semilanceata**: Confirmed from Tasmania
- **P. subaeruginosa**: Common in Tasmania (Picker and Rickards, 1970)
- **P. tasmaniana**: Named for and found in Tasmania
- **Copelandia cyanescens**: Present in Tasmania (Allen et al., 1991)

## New Zealand

New Zealand has a notable neurotropic mycoflora documented by Allen et al.
(1991), Guzman et al. (1991, 1993b), and Johnston and Buchanan (1995).

### New Zealand Endemic and Notable Species

- **P. aucklandii**: Endemic to New Zealand; described by Guzman,
  Bandala, and King (1991); closely related to P. zapotecorum from
  Mexico and South America, demonstrating trans-Pacific biogeographic
  connections
- **P. makarorae**: Endemic to New Zealand; described by Johnston and
  Buchanan (1995); a bluing species unique to New Zealand
- **P. kumaenorum**: Also found in New Guinea; demonstrates New
  Zealand-New Guinea connection

### Other New Zealand Species

- **P. australiana**: Shared with Australia
- **P. collybioides**: Present in New Zealand (synonymous with P.
  zapotecorum)
- **P. cubensis**: Present in New Zealand
- **P. eucalypta**: Shared with Australia
- **P. semilanceata**: Confirmed from New Zealand
- **P. subaeruginosa**: Present in New Zealand (Johnston and Buchanan,
  1995)
- **P. subcubensis**: Present in New Zealand
- **P. tasmaniana**: Shared with Australia and Tasmania
- **Amanita muscaria**: Present in New Zealand (introduced)
- **Copelandia cyanescens**: Present in New Zealand
- **[[gymnopilus-purpuratus]]** and **G. spectabilis**: Both present
- **Panaeolina foenisecii** and **Panaeolus subbalteatus**: Present

## Oceania and Pacific Islands

### Hawaii
- **Copelandia cyanescens**: Abundant; confirmed by Heim et al. (1966a)
- **C. anomala**, **C. bispora**, **C. cambodginiensis**, **C.
  tropicalis**: All present
- **Amanita muscaria**: Present (Merlin and Allen, 1993)
- **Panaeolus subbalteatus** and **P. sphinctrinus**: Present
- **Psilocybe cubensis**: Questionably reported (Allen, 1998)

### Other Pacific Islands
- **Fiji**: C. cyanescens and P. cubensis
- **Samoa**: C. cyanescens used traditionally (Cox, 1981)
- **Bonin Islands**: C. tropicalis (Hongo, 1977a)
- **New Caledonia**: Hypholoma neocaledonica (endemic)
- **Solomon Islands**: Gerronema fibula
- **Macquarie Island (sub-Antarctic)**: Panaeolus mollearius,
  one of the southernmost neurotropic records

## Biogeographic Significance

The Australasian-Pacific neurotropic mycoflora reveals important
biogeographic connections:
1. The close relationship between P. aucklandii (New Zealand) and P.
   zapotecorum (Mexico/South America) suggests ancient Gondwanan
   connections or trans-Pacific dispersal
2. P. samuiensis (Thailand) is closely related to P. mexicana (Mexico)
3. P. makarorae (New Zealand) represents a unique southern hemisphere
   lineage
4. The presence of P. subaeruginosa in Australia and its absence from
   South America suggests independent evolution in the southern
   hemisphere
