# Shirota Large-Scale Psilocin and Psilocybin Synthesis Pathway

## Overview

In 2003, Osamu Shirota, Wataru Hakamata, and Yukihiro Goda at the National Institute of Health Sciences in Tokyo published a concise large-scale synthesis of [[psilocin]] (1) and [[psilocybin]] (2), the principal hallucinogenic constituents of magic mushrooms. Their approach achieved gram-scale production of both compounds without any chromatographic purification, representing a significant practical advance over earlier methods that reported yields of only 20% and 47% for [[psilocybin]]. The key innovation was the isolation of a novel zwitterionic dibenzyl phosphate intermediate, which enabled simple filtration-based purification.

## Background and Motivation

### Regulatory Context

Since June 6, 2002, fungi containing [[psilocybin]] and [[psilocin]] have been regulated by the Narcotics and Psychotropic Control Law in Japan. The identification of hallucinogenic mushrooms using morphologic and microscopic analyses is difficult without expert knowledge, so chromatographic methods including TLC, GC, and HPLC are typically employed. These analytical methods require pure standard compounds.

### Challenge of Isolation from Natural Sources

Isolating [[psilocybin]] and [[psilocin]] from mushrooms on a gram scale for use as analytical standards is problematic because [[psilocin]] easily decomposes and [[psilocybin]] has high polarity. Several syntheses of psilocin had been published, but reports on psilocybin synthesis were few, with historically low yields.

### Structural Context

[[Psilocybin]] and [[psilocin]] are structurally similar to the neurotransmitter [[serotonin]] (5-hydroxytryptamine or 5-HT). Psilocybin is 4-phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine (4-PO-DMT), the phosphate ester of psilocin (4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine or 4-HO-DMT). Minor constituents [[baeocystin]] and [[norbaeocystin]] may also be present in mushroom material.

## Synthesis of Psilocin (1)

### Step 1: Protection of 4-Hydroxyindole

The synthesis starts from commercially available [[4-hydroxyindole]] (3). The hydroxyl group is protected by acetylation with acetic anhydride and pyridine in dichloromethane at 0 degrees Celsius to room temperature. The reaction is stirred for approximately 2 hours, then worked up by adding water, evaporating, and recrystallising from ethyl acetate. This yields 4-acetylindole (4) as ivory white crystals in constant yield.

The acetyl protecting group was preferred over the benzyl ether protection also reported in the literature because the benzyl route required a separate deprotection step. The acetyl group is removed later in the synthesis during the reduction step.

### Step 2: Formation of Glyoxalylamide Intermediate

4-Acetylindole (4) is treated with oxalyl chloride in anhydrous diethyl ether at 0 degrees Celsius, then n-hexane is added and the reaction flask placed in a freezer overnight. The resulting yellow crystals of the oxalyl chloride adduct (5) are separated by filtration and dissolved in anhydrous tetrahydrofuran.

A 2M dimethylamine tetrahydrofuran solution and pyridine are added over 15 minutes in an ice bath. The reaction product is separated by filtration and washed to afford 3-dimethylaminooxalyl-4-acetylindole (6) as an ivory white crystalline powder in over 80% yield. The 4-O-benzyl derivative of 4-hydroxyindole was found to be somewhat unstable without careful reaction control and was not isolated in crystal form.

### Step 3: Reduction to Psilocin

The glyoxalylamide (6) is reduced using lithium aluminium hydride (LiAlH4) in anhydrous tetrahydrofuran under argon atmosphere. The solution is dropwise added over 2 hours, then refluxed for 2 hours. After cooling, anhydrous sodium sulphate and saturated sodium sulphate are added carefully.

The product is filtered through an aminopropyl silica gel pad and concentrated to afford [[psilocin]] (1) as white crystals in over 85% yield. The reduction simultaneously cleaves the acetyl protecting group and reduces the glyoxalylamide to the desired 4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine.

## Synthesis of Psilocybin (2)

### Phosphorylation Method Selection

For the synthesis of psilocybin from psilocin, several phosphorylation methodologies were evaluated. Most methods did not consume psilocin effectively. Two promising approaches were identified:

- The phosphoryl iodide method using tribenzyl phosphite, iodine, and DMAP
- The pyrophosphate method using tetrabenzylpyrophosphate and n-butyllithium

The pyrophosphate method was selected for large-scale production because of its easy handling and reagent stability.

### Phosphorylation Reaction

Psilocin (1) is dissolved in anhydrous tetrahydrofuran at minus 78 degrees Celsius. A 2.6M solution of n-butyllithium in n-hexane is added, followed after 5 minutes by tetrabenzylpyrophosphate (prepared from dibenzyl phosphate). The reaction is stirred for 1 hour while warming to 0 degrees Celsius.

### Discovery of the Zwitterionic Intermediate

After the usual aqueous workup, the 1H NMR spectrum of the remaining substance showed complicated signals. TLC revealed an additional spot at the origin, and the whitish material no longer dissolved in dichloromethane. This observation had been previously reported by Nichols and Frescas, who concluded that hydrolytic cleavage of one O-benzyl group had occurred.

Purification by preparative reversed-phase HPLC afforded a single compound (8). The 1H and 13C NMR spectra in methanol-d4 showed signals for two sets of benzyl groups and a psilocin core. Critically, the proton signal of one benzyl group methylene was shifted to high field (delta H 4.56), consistent with attachment to a quaternary ammonium ion rather than an oxygen. The 31P NMR spectrum confirmed the presence of a phosphate moiety.

### Structural Elucidation of the Zwitterionic Intermediate

Full structural identification of compound 8 was achieved using 2D NMR analyses:

- **HMBC experiment**: Revealed that one benzyl group was directly linked at the nitrogen of the N,N-dimethyl group, forming a quaternary ammonium ion
- **NOESY spectrum**: Supported the assigned linkages
- The compound was identified as a zwitterionic N,O-dibenzyl phosphate derivative

This intramolecular conversion occurs when the dibenzyl phosphate derivative (7) is suspended in dichloromethane overnight, with one benzyl group migrating from oxygen to nitrogen.

### Practical Significance of the Zwitterion

The zwitterionic nature of compound 8 enabled its isolation on large scale by simple filtration in over 85% yield. Excess dibenzyl phosphate was removed by washing with dichloromethane. This represented a dramatic simplification compared to previous methods requiring anion-exchange resin chromatography.

### Final Deprotection to Psilocybin

Catalytic hydrogenolysis of the zwitterionic intermediate (8) using 10% palladium-activated carbon in methanol under a hydrogen atmosphere led to [[psilocybin]] (2) as a crystalline product. After approximately 2 hours, water was added to the mixture (to address product deposition) and stirring continued for one more hour. The product was filtered through a Celite pad and concentrated to afford psilocybin as a white needle crystalline powder.

The isolated yield of psilocybin from psilocin was greater than 72%, even for gram-scale production, representing a substantial improvement over the previously reported yields of 20% and 47%.

## Characterisation Data

### Psilocin (1)

- Melting point: 169 to 174 degrees Celsius (decomposing), literature value 173 to 176 degrees C
- UV (MeOH) lambda max: 222.5, 268.0, 284.5, 294.0 nm
- ESIMS m/z: 205.1 [M + H]+
- HRESIMS m/z: 205.1303 [M + H]+ (calculated for C12H17N2O: 205.1341)

### Psilocybin (2)

- Melting point: 190 to 198 degrees Celsius, literature values 185 to 195 degrees C and 210 to 212 degrees C
- UV (MeOH) lambda max: 221.0, 267.5, 278.5, 290.0 nm
- 31P NMR (CD3OD): delta minus 4.48 (OPO3H2)
- ESIMS m/z: 285.1 [M + H]+
- HRESIMS m/z: 285.0991 [M + H]+ (calculated for C12H18N2O4P: 285.1004)

## Significance and Applications

This synthesis pathway is significant for several reasons:

1. **No chromatographic purification**: The entire sequence avoids column chromatography, making it practical for laboratories without advanced purification equipment
2. **Gram-scale production**: Both psilocin and psilocybin can be produced in gram quantities suitable for analytical standard preparation
3. **Novel intermediate**: The zwitterionic N,O-dibenzyl phosphate represents a newly identified compound class in tryptamine chemistry
4. **High overall yield**: The route provides substantially higher yields than previously available methods
5. **Reagent availability**: All starting materials and reagents are commercially available

The synthesis is directly relevant to forensic and analytical chemistry applications where pure [[psilocybin]] and [[psilocin]] standards are required for the identification and quantification of hallucinogenic mushrooms through chromatographic methods.
