# Bacterial Endophyte Isolation and Detection in Plant Roots

## Overview

Endophytic bacteria live inside plant tissue without causing substantive harm to the host. They may benefit the host through competition for habitat (antibiotic production), induction of plant defense mechanisms, production of plant growth stimulators, and improving soil quality. Endophytes represent an abundant source of biologically important and chemically novel bioactive compounds with pharmaceutical and agricultural potential.

## Plant Material Selection

With approximately 30,000 plant species, strategic selection is essential for bioprospecting endophytes (Strobel and Daisy 2003):

1. Plants from unique environmental niches with unusual biology and novel survival strategies
2. Plants with ethnobotanical history related to specific applications of interest
3. Endemic plants with unusual longevity or from ancient land masses
4. Plants growing in areas of great biodiversity (potential for housing diverse endophytes)

Always select healthy, disease-free plant material to avoid isolating localized pathogenic endophytes.

## Surface Sterilization Protocol

### Pre-Washing
Thorough pre-washing removes adhering soil particles and dust to reduce surface flora load:
- Vigorous washing with tap water, distilled water, or dilute saline buffers (pH 7.2)
- Sonication may be used to dislodge soil and organic matter before sterilization
- Root tissue should be freshly collected and processed immediately

### Sterilization Agents
Common surface-sterilizing chemicals:
- Sodium hypochlorite
- Mercuric chloride
- Ethanol (70%)
- Hydrogen peroxide
- Chloramine T
- Propylene oxide vapor (less common)
- Formaldehyde (less common)

Nonionic detergents (Tween 20, Triton X-100, Tween-80) improve penetration of sterilizers into niches beyond epidermal cells.

### Standard Three-Step Method
1. 70% ethanol treatment
2. Sodium hypochlorite treatment
3. 70% ethanol treatment

Alternative: ethanol followed by mercuric chloride followed by ethanol. Concentration, combination, and incubation period vary depending on plant and tissue type.

## Vital Staining Detection (TTC Method)

The tetrazolium dye method (Patriquin and Dobereiner 1978) demonstrates bacteria inside plant tissue by detecting metabolic activity:

### Principle
Bacteria inside surface-sterilized root tissue utilize malic acid as carbon source and reduce 2,3,5-triphenyl tetrazolium chloride (TTC) to form water-insoluble red-colored formazans. Actively respiring (viable) bacterial cells deposit formazans as discrete dark red to purple granules visible by light microscopy.

### Protocol
1. Prepare 50 mM potassium phosphate buffer (pH 7) with 0.0625 g malic acid; autoclave 15 min
2. Add 0.15 g TTC to the sterile malate phosphate buffer aseptically
3. Dip surface-sterilized roots in 20 ml TTC solution, incubate overnight at 30 +/- 2 degrees C
4. Cut longitudinal or cross sections (hand or cryotome)
5. Prepare wet mounts on glass slides with phosphate buffer, seal with nail polish
6. Examine at 100x magnification -- bacterial cells stain red to purple

### Counterstaining
- **Aniline blue (1%)**: Counterstain TTC-treated sections for 1 min to enhance contrast
- **Acridine orange**: Fluorescent cationic dye for direct counting of endophytes in situ via epifluorescence microscopy. Stain sections 3-5 min at room temperature. Counterstaining TTC-treated cells with acridine orange differentiates bacteria from small particles.

## Cultivation of Endophytes

Most endophytic bacteria can grow on routine laboratory bacterial growth media. After surface sterilization:
- macerate or grind surface-sterilized tissue
- Plate serial dilutions on appropriate media
- Incubate and identify colony types
- Verify endophytic status by demonstrating bacteria originated from inside tissue (not surface contamination)

## Significance

Understanding endophytic bacterial flora opens new biotechnological opportunities:
- Biocontrol agents against plant pathogens
- Plant growth-promoting microorganisms
- Sources of novel bioactive compounds (antibiotics, anticancer agents)
- Improved understanding of plant-microbe interactions
- Integration with mycorrhizal symbiosis for comprehensive plant health management
