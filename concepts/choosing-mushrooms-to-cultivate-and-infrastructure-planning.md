## Choosing Mushrooms to Cultivate and Infrastructure Planning

From [[tradd-cotter]]'s *[[organic-mushroom-farming-and-mycoremediation-overview]] and Mycoremediation*, guidance on selecting mushroom species, matching them to available infrastructure, and planning cultivation operations.

## Choosing a Mushroom Species

### Beginner Recommendations
"Begin with the easiest species and the most readily available substrates":
1. **Shiitakes on logs** — classic outdoor cultivation
2. **King stropharia on wood chips** — low maintenance, garden integration
3. **[[oyster-mushrooms]] on agricultural waste** — fastest results, indoor or outdoor

### Key Selection Factors
- **Ease of cultivation** — most important for beginners
- **Available substrates** — logs, wood chips, straw, compost, agricultural waste
- **Climate compatibility** — match species to local conditions
- **Infrastructure** — indoor vs. outdoor, scale of operation
- **Market demand** — if growing commercially

### Species Difficulty Spectrum
| Difficulty | Species Examples |
|-----------|-----------------|
| **Easy** | Oyster mushrooms (Pleurotus), king stropharia |
| **Moderate** | Shiitake ([[lentinula-edodes]]) |
| **Advanced** | Maitake ([[grifola-frondosa]]), lion's mane (Hericium) |
| **Expert** | Morel (Morchella), chanterelle |

## Infrastructure Planning

### Small-Scale Setup
"An extra room, closet, or bathroom can serve as both colonization room and fruiting room."
- Hand misting for humidity control
- Clear plastic bag with holes as humidity tent
- Modular shelving wrapped in clear plastic as fruiting chamber
- **Ventilation**: Holes near top and base — heavier CO₂ seeps out, pulling fresh O₂ in at top

### Commercial Operations
"Commercial farms that devote separate rooms to every batch of mushrooms maintain a higher carbon dioxide level up to pinning, introduce fresh air throughout the mushroom maturation process while maintaining high humidity, and then cut back on watering altogether during the resting stage."

### Workflow Optimization
"Stage your operation in a way that minimizes the number of steps — steps of the process and actual physical steps."
- Position equipment in assembly-line sequence
- Organize rooms in order of use
- "Automation magnifies your problems as well as your strengths"

### Retrofitting Existing Structures
"Old barns, chicken houses, indoor hog farms, warehouses, and textile mills can be surprisingly well suited for mushroom growing."
- Must withstand high humidity
- Wood-framed structures need waterproof covering (plastic wall panels, low-VOC waterproofing paint)

## Log Cultivation

### Tree Species Selection

**Hardwoods** (preferred for most [[fungi-cultivated-mushrooms-pathogens-competitors]]):
- Oak, maple, beech, birch, poplar, ironwood, hophornbeam, alder, sweetgum, cottonwood, elm

**Conifers** (limited suitability):
- [[cauliflower-mushroom-cultivation-sparassis]] (Sparassis crispa)
- Tufted conifer lover ([[growing-gourmet-hypholoma-capnoides-cultivation]])
- Some [[chicken-of-the-woods]] strains ([[laetiporus-conifericola]])

**Caution**: "Some mushrooms grown on conifers can have a sap-like flavor and may cause gastric upset."

### Bark Importance
"Mushrooms form between the inner and outer layers of bark." The space serves as a nursery for primordia development.
- **Bark must be intact** all the way around
- Avoid species that drop bark when cut (e.g., elms)
- Mushrooms can exert pressure greater than **80 psi** to break through bark

### Log Fruiting Timeline
"A beautiful, meaty shiitake emerging from the bark surface eight months after inoculation. These logs will fruit for five to eight years!"

### Plug Spawn Technique
- Drill holes 1 to 1½ inches deep with 5/16-inch bit
- Tap plugs in gently
- Cover with wax to preserve moisture and exclude contamination/insects
- Wax "gives the mycelium enough time to drill its way into the cavity and begin colonization"

## Spawn Types

### Purchased Spawn
- Available in sawdust, grain, or wooden dowel forms
- Best for beginners developing skills

### Wild/Naturalized Spawn
"Spawn that has been transplanted or grown from the bases of wild mushrooms is called naturalized or wild spawn."

### Sourcing Wood
- Use locally available timber to minimize costs
- Fresh-cut logs preferred (within 2-6 weeks of felling)
- Avoid logs with signs of other fungal colonization

## Compost-Based Cultivation

### Manure-Based Compost
For secondary decomposers (button mushrooms, etc.):

**Target nitrogen content**: 1.5 to 1.7 percent of total dry weight

**Gypsum**: Commonly used as calcium source; helps coat particles for aeration; does not alter pH.

**Nitrogen calculation example**:
- Total nitrogen weight (0.94 lb) ÷ total dry weight (59.75 lb) = 1.57% nitrogen

**Raw material databases**: Use agricultural databases for livestock feed nutrient breakdowns to calculate nitrogen content of local materials.

### Compost Process
1. Determine compost "ingredients" from locally available wastes
2. Ensure starting nitrogen content in correct range (1.5-1.7%)
3. Periodic sampling and nitrogen testing via soil test kit or university analysis
4. Adjust ratios as needed based on test results
