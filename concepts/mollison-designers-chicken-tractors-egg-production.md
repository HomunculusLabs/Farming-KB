Chicken tractors are mobile [[query-how-to-design-a-chicken-tractor-system]] is a lightweight, floorless enclosure that confines
chickens to a specific area of ground while allowing them access to
soil, vegetation, insects, and sunlight. The tractor is moved regularly
to fresh ground, distributing manure evenly and preventing the buildup

  - Weather protection: A solid or semi-solid roof section providing

Typical dimensions are 2-3 meters wide by 3-6 meters long, providing
enough space for 10-25 birds depending on the stocking density used.

## Daily Management and Moving

The core principle of chicken tractor management is frequent relocation
to fresh ground. In intensive systems, the tractor is moved daily or
every second day. The move distance depends on the stocking density
and the desired ground impact:

  - Daily moves of 1-2 meters: Birds lightly scratch and fertilize
    the surface, suitable for pasture renovation before planting
  - Moves every 2-3 days: Moderate soil disturbance and manure
    deposition, good for general garden integration
  - Weekly moves: Heavier manure concentration, useful for building
    fertility in depleted soils

The move schedule must balance the benefits of manure deposition and
weed/pest control against the risk of over-concentration of nitrogen
and potential soil compaction from concentrated scratching.

## Stocking Density

Mollison references stocking densities appropriate to chicken tractor
systems. Recommended densities range from 4-8 birds per square meter
for short-duration stays (1-2 days) to 2-4 birds per square meter for
longer stays (up to 7 days). Overstocking leads to bare, compacted soil,
ammonia buildup, and increased disease pressure. Understocking reduces
the effectiveness of weed control and fertilization.

For a typical 6-bird flock in a home garden system, a tractor of 2m x
3m (6 square meters) provides ample space. Commercial-scale operations
may use larger tractors holding 25-50 birds, moved with the aid of
tractors or vehicle hitches.
