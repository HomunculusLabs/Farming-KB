# Four Families of Indole Hallucinogens

## Overview

[[terence-mckenna]], in Food of the Gods, identifies four families of indole
compounds that function as strong visionary hallucinogens and occur
naturally in plants. These are: the LSD-type compounds found in morning
glories and ergot; the tryptamine hallucinogens including DMT, psilocin,
and psilocybin; the beta-carbolines such as harmine and harmaline; and
the ibogaine family from African and South American tree genera. McKenna
considered this classification to contain "the most important and
exciting information that human beings have gathered concerning the
natural world since the long-forgotten birth of science."

The indole nucleus is central to each of these compound families. Indoles
are not merely secondary metabolic by-products but play dynamic roles in
plant chemistry, fluctuating in both concentration and metabolic decay
rate. They are formed in greatest amounts in tissues most active in
overall metabolism, suggesting they are essential to the survival
strategy of the organisms that produce them. McKenna proposed that some
of these compounds may function as exopheromones - chemical messengers
that act across species lines to influence entire biomes.

Understanding these four families provides a framework for grasping the
relationship between psychoactive plants and human consciousness. Each
family interacts with human neurochemistry in distinct ways: LSD-type
compounds require large doses for full visionary effects; tryptamines
produce brief but intensely visual experiences; beta-carbolines inhibit
the enzyme systems that would otherwise deactivate DMT; and ibogaine
offers prolonged visionary and emotional journeys unique among
psychedelics.

## Key Concepts

### LSD-Type Compounds

The LSD hallucinogens are rare in nature, found in several related genera
of morning glories and in ergot fungi. They became the best known of all
hallucinogens because millions of doses were manufactured during the
1960s. LSD is a psychedelic that requires relatively large doses to
elicit the "paradis artificiel" of vivid, transmundane hallucinations
that DMT and psilocybin produce at traditional doses. McKenna emphasized
the nonhallucinogenic effects of LSD: mind expansion, increased speed of
thought, and the ability to understand complex behavioral patterns and
decision-making networks. LSD has shown therapeutic promise for treating
chronic alcoholism, though media hysteria has limited research.

### Tryptamine Hallucinogens

Tryptamine hallucinogens - especially DMT, psilocin, and psilocybin -
are found throughout higher plant families, including legumes, and in
mushrooms. DMT also occurs endogenously in the human brain, leading
McKenna to suggest it should not be thought of as a drug at all. DMT
intoxication is the most profound and visually spectacular of visionary
hallucinogens, remarkable for its brevity, intensity, and nontoxicity.
Psilocybin-containing mushrooms, particularly Stropharia cubensis, are
pandemic throughout tropical regions wherever zebu-type cattle graze.

### Beta-Carbolines

Beta-carbolines such as harmine and harmaline can be hallucinogenic at
near-toxic levels but are most important for their role as monoamine
oxidase inhibitors. They prevent the body's enzyme systems from
deactivating DMT-type hallucinogens, thereby prolonging and intensifying
visual hallucinations. This DMT-beta-carboline combination is the basis
of the Amazonian brew ayahuasca. Beta-carbolines are legal and were
virtually unknown to the general public until recently. [[peganum-harmala]],
the Syrian rue, is rich in harmine and occurs wild across Mediterranean
North Africa.

### The Ibogaine Family

Ibogaine occurs in two related tree genera, Tabernanthe and
Tabernamontana. Tabernanthe iboga is a small, yellow-flowered bush with
a history of use as a hallucinogen in tropical West Africa, particularly
among the Fang people of Gabon in the Bwiti religion. Its active
compounds bear a structural relationship to beta-carbolines. Ibogaine is
known as both a powerful aphrodisiac and a hallucinogen capable of
inducing profound visionary and emotional experiences. The root bark is
consumed in prodigious amounts to "open one's head."

### Exopheromones and Cross-Species Communication

McKenna proposed that psychoactive plant compounds may function as
exopheromones - chemical messengers that act across species lines
rather than within a single species. Some exopheromones allow a small
group of individuals to affect an entire community or biome. This
suggests nature operates as an organismic whole that mediates its own
development through chemical messages, challenging the classical
Darwinian view of nature as endless warfare among competing species.

### The Ur Plant Hypothesis

By a process of elimination, McKenna argued that psilocybin-containing
mushrooms - specifically Stropharia cubensis - were the most likely
catalyst for the emergence of human consciousness. The mushroom is
pandemic wherever zebu cattle graze, requires no preparation, is
continuously available, and confers immediate tangible benefits.
Archaeological evidence from Non Nak Tha in Thailand (15,000 B.P.)
shows zebu bones coincident with human graves where the mushroom is
common today.

## Applications

### Psychotherapeutic Applications

LSD has demonstrated efficacy in psychotherapy and alcoholism treatment.
Ibogaine has shown promise for treating addiction. These applications
remain largely unexplored due to legal restrictions that McKenna
criticized as preventing research into potentially valuable treatments.

### Ethnobotanical Conservation

Understanding the botanical sources of these compounds supports
conservation of indigenous knowledge and the plant species that produce
them. The Bwiti religion's use of iboga, Amazonian ayahuasca practices,
and Mesoamerican mushroom traditions all represent irreplaceable
cultural-biological heritage.

### Neuroscience Research

The endogenous presence of DMT in the human brain raises fundamental
questions about consciousness and brain chemistry. Research into how
these indole compounds interact with serotonin receptors continues to
advance understanding of perception, cognition, and mental health.
