# Homestead Energy Conservation

Energy conservation is the practice of reducing energy consumption through design, efficiency improvements, and behavioral changes. For off-grid or grid-tied homesteaders, conservation is the first and most cost-effective strategy — every watt saved is a watt that doesn't need to be generated, stored, or purchased. The cheapest energy is the energy you never use.

## The Conservation Hierarchy

1. **Reduce demand**: Eliminate unnecessary energy use
2. **Increase efficiency**: Use less energy for the same result
3. **Generate cleanly**: Meet remaining demand with renewable sources
4. **Store wisely**: Size storage for actual needs, not wasteful habits

This order matters — reducing demand before sizing generation saves thousands of dollars.

## Passive Solar Design

### Site Orientation

- Orient the longest side of the house south (in the Northern Hemisphere)
- South-facing windows capture winter sun (low angle) and are shaded by roof overhang in summer (high angle)
- Deciduous trees on the south side provide summer shade and winter sun after leaf drop
- Evergreen windbreaks on north and west sides reduce heating costs by 10-30%

### Thermal Mass

Materials that absorb, store, and slowly release heat:
- Concrete floors and walls
- Stone and brick
- Tile
- Earthen construction (cob, adobe, [[rammed-earth]])

**How it works**: Sun heats thermal mass during the day; mass releases heat slowly at night, moderating indoor temperature swings.

### Insulation

Proper insulation is the single most impactful energy conservation measure.

| Area | Recommended R-Value | Notes |
|------|--------------------|-------|
| Attic/Ceiling | R-49 to R-60 | Most cost-effective area to insulate |
| Walls | R-23 to R-30 | Closed-cell spray foam or dense-pack cellulose |
| Floor over crawlspace | R-25 to R-30 | Also seals against moisture and pests |
| Basement walls | R-15 to R-19 (interior) | Continuous insulation preferred |
| Rim joists | R-25+ | Major source of air infiltration |

### Air Sealing

Before adding insulation, seal air leaks — insulation is useless if air moves through it:

- Caulk around window and door frames
- Weatherstrip all exterior doors
- Seal electrical outlets and switch plates on exterior walls
- Seal plumbing penetrations, ductwork, and chimney flashing
- Use spray foam for gaps over 1/4 inch
- Professional blower door test identifies leaks (costs ~$150-300)

## Heating and Cooling

### Wood Heating

- EPA-certified wood stoves: 70-80% efficient vs 40-50% for older models
- Cordwood: 1 cord = ~20 million BTU (heats 1200-1500 sq ft for a season)
- Wood is carbon-neutral when sustainably harvested
- See [[rocket-stove]] for ultra-efficient wood heating

### Passive Cooling

- Stack ventilation: Open low windows on the cool side, high windows on the warm side
- Whole-house fan: Exhaust hot air through attic in the evening
- Shade south and west windows in summer
- Light-colored roof reflects heat
- Attic ventilation reduces cooling load

### Minimize Refrigeration Load

Refrigerators are the largest single energy consumer on many homesteads:

- Choose efficient models (SunDanzer, Sundanzer DC, or high-efficiency AC units)
- Locate away from heat sources (stove, direct sun)
- Ensure adequate ventilation behind and above the unit
- Keep coils clean
- Consider root cellar alternatives for foods that don't require refrigeration
- Convert chest freezer to refrigerator using external thermostat (uses 1/4 the energy)

## Water Heating

Water heating accounts for 15-25% of home energy use:

- **Solar thermal**: Batch heaters or evacuated tube collectors — free hot water for 6-9 months
- **Wood-fired**: [[thermosiphon]] systems using wood stove heat
- **On-demand**: Tankless water heaters only heat water when needed (eliminate standby losses)
- **Insulate**: Tank blanket and pipe insulation reduce losses 25-40%
- **Lower temperature**: 120°F is adequate for most uses; 140°F for dishwasher sanitizing

## Lighting

- Replace ALL incandescent bulbs with LED (uses 75-85% less energy)
- LED lifespan: 15,000-50,000 hours vs 1,000 for incandescent
- Task lighting: Light only the area you need, not the whole room
- Motion sensors for outdoor and utility lighting
- Daylighting: Maximize natural light with windows, skylights, and light tubes
- Solar-powered pathway and security lights eliminate wiring

## Appliances

### High-Energy Devices to Avoid or Minimize

- Electric resistance heating (baseboard, electric furnace)
- Electric water heater
- Electric clothes dryer (use clothesline instead)
- Air conditioner (use passive cooling first)
- Electric range (use wood cookstove, propane, or solar oven)

### Efficient Alternatives

| Standard | Efficient Alternative | Savings |
|----------|----------------------|---------|
| Electric dryer | Clothesline, spin-dryer | 100% |
| Electric water heater | Solar thermal, on-demand propane | 60-80% |
| Electric range | Propane, wood cookstove, solar oven | 50-100% |
| Standard fridge | DC fridge, chest freezer conversion | 50-75% |
| Desktop computer | Laptop | 80-90% |

## Cooking Efficiency

- **Solar ovens**: Reach 250-350°F on sunny days, zero fuel
- **Pressure cookers**: Reduce cooking time and fuel by 60-70%
- **Haybox cooking**: Bring to boil, insulate, let residual heat finish cooking
- **Rocket stoves**: Very efficient wood combustion for cooking
- **Thermal cookware**: Vacuum-insulated pots maintain temperature without fuel

## Water Conservation

Saving water saves energy (pumping, heating, treating):

- Low-flow showerheads: 1.5 GPM vs standard 2.5 GPM
- Dual-flush toilets or [[composting-toilets]]
- Fix all leaks immediately (a dripping faucet wastes 3000+ gallons/year)
