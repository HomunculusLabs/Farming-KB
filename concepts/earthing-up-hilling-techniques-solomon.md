## The Problem with Deep Planting

Farmers typically plant potato seed pieces deeply in loose soil, which
leads to a compacted soil column above the seed piece. As the plant
grows, the soil above the seed settles and compacts under its own
weight and from rain impact. This compaction limits tuber formation
because potatoes need loose soil to expand into. Additionally, portions
of tubers forming near the surface will green from light exposure and
become inedible. Green potatoes contain solanine, a glycoalkaloid
toxin that makes them bitter and potentially harmful if consumed in
quantity. The farmer's approach trades yield quality for mechanization
efficiency.

## The Gardener's Solution: Shallow Planting Plus Hilling

Gardeners can avoid both compaction and greening by planting seed
shallowly and then hilling up progressively as the vines grow. This
provides a loose, uncompacted medium for the potatoes to form in,
resulting in smoother tubers and easier harvesting. The key insight:
all potatoes will form above the original seed placement, never below
it. This means the original planting depth only determines how quickly
the vines emerge — the entire crop will develop in the hilled soil
above the seed piece. The deeper you plant, the more compacted soil
the tubers must push through.
