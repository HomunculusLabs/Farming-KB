# Metal Halide Lighting Systems

Metal halide (MH) lamps are a type of High Intensity Discharge (HID)
light that produce a broad, blue-heavy spectrum ideal for vegetative
growth. They are one of the most popular lighting choices for indoor
cannabis cultivators and have been a staple since the 1970s. See also
[[cervantes-lighting-systems]], [[cervantes-high-pressure-sodium-lighting]],
and [[cervantes-ballasts-hid-electrical]].

## Construction and Operation

Metal halide lamps produce light by passing or arcing electricity through
vaporized argon gas, mercury, thorium iodide, sodium iodide, and scandium
iodide within the quartz arc tube. After the chemicals reach their proper
concentrations in the arc tube, the characteristic bright white light is
emitted. This process takes about three to five minutes from startup.

The metal halide arc system is very complex and requires a seasoning
period of 100 hours of operation for all components to stabilize. If a
power surge occurs and the lamp goes out or is turned off, it will take
five to fifteen minutes for the gases inside the arc tube to cool before
the lamp can restart.

The outer bulb functions as a protective jacket that contains the arc
tube and starting mechanism, keeping them in a constant environment as
well as absorbing ultraviolet radiation. Protective glasses that filter
out ultraviolet rays are recommended if you spend much time in the grow
room, or if you are prone to staring at the HID lamp.

When the lamp is started, incredible voltage is necessary for the
initial ionization process. Turning the lamp on and off more than once
a day causes unnecessary stress on the HID system and will shorten its
life. It is best to start the lamp only once a day, and always use a
timer.

## Operating Position

Metal halides operate most efficiently in a vertical position within
15 degrees of vertical. When operated in positions other than plus or
minus 15 degrees of vertical, lamp wattage, lumen output, and life
all decrease. The arc bends, creating non-uniform heating of the arc
tube wall, resulting in less efficient operation and shorter bulb life.

There are special lamps made to operate in horizontal or any other
position. These bulbs have "HOR" stamped on the crown or base, which
refers to horizontal operation.

## Lumen Output and Efficiency

A 1000-watt standard metal halide emits from 80,000 to 110,000 initial
lumens and 65,000 to 88,000 average (mean) lumens. Super halides emit
115,000 initial lumens and 92,000 mean lumens. One lumen is equal to
the amount of light emitted by one candle that falls on one square foot
of surface one foot away.

Originally developed in the 1970s, metal halides were characterized by
one main technical limitation: the larger the bulb, the higher the
lumens-per-watt conversion. For example, a 1000-watt MH produces about
12 percent more light than a 400-watt MH. Scientists overcame this
barrier when they developed "pulse start" metal halides, which are
brighter and much more efficient than their predecessors.

## Lumen Maintenance and Life

The average life of a halide is about 12,000 hours, almost two years
of daily operation at 18 hours per day. Many will last even longer.
The lamp reaches the end of its life when it fails to start or come up
to full brilliance. This is usually caused by deterioration of lamp
electrodes over time, loss of transmission of the arc tube from
blackening, or shifts in the chemical balance of the metals in the arc
tube.

Do not wait until the bulb is burned out before changing it. An old
bulb is inefficient and costly. Replace bulbs every 10 to 12 months or
5000 hours of use. Electrode deterioration is greatest during start-up.

## Stroboscopic Effect

The halide may produce a stroboscopic (flashing) effect. The light
will appear bright, then dim, bright, dim, etc. This flashing is the
result of the arc being extinguished 120 times every second.
Illumination usually remains constant, but it may pulsate a little.
This is normal and nothing to worry about.

## Ballasts

The ballast for a 1000-watt halide will operate standard, clear, and
phosphor-coated and super, clear, and phosphor-coated halides on a
120 or 240-volt current. Different ballasts are required for each lamp
wattage: 150, 250, 400, 1000, 1100, and 1500. Each ballast must be
specifically designed for the particular wattage because their starting
and operating requirements are unique.

## Bulb Types

Universal metal halide bulbs designed to operate in any position,
vertical or horizontal, supply up to ten percent less light and often
have a shorter life.

SunMaster Warm Deluxe Grow Lamps emit balanced light similar to a
3000 Kelvin source. The enhanced orange-red component promotes
flowering, stem elongation, and germination while a rich blue content
assures healthy vegetative growth.

Venture manufactures the AgroSun for Hydrofarm, an enhanced metal
halide bulb with more yellow/orange in the spectrum for improved
flowering performance.

## Kelvin Temperature Ratings

The Kelvin scale helps growers select the right spectrum:
- 6000K: Daylight Metal Halide
- 4000K: Standard Clear Metal Halide
- 3200K: Warm Metal Halide
- 2200K: High Pressure Sodium (for comparison)

## Best Uses

Metal halide lamps are excellent for the [[cervantes-vegetative-phase]]
due to their blue-heavy spectrum. They promote compact, bushy growth
with short internodes. Many growers use MH for vegetative growth and
switch to [[cervantes-high-pressure-sodium-lighting]] during the
[[cervantes-flowering-phase]] for the red-heavy spectrum that promotes
bud development.
