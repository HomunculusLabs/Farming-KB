# Mulberry (Morus spp)
## Description
- Family: Moraceae
- Small to medium, long-lived (300 years), deciduous trees
- Hardy, drought resistant, frost tolerant, suited to cool areas
- Tolerant of shade; suited to most soils and sites
- Heavy bearers: 19-24 t/ha; regular fruiting
- Good companion plant for grapes; forms trellis for them

Mulberries are among the most versatile [[permaculture-climate-zones]]
and [[permaculture-animal-integration]] principles, reducing labour
while cycling nutrients. This approach is central to
[[permaculture-grazing-and-pasture]] design.

## Species

### Morus alba (White Mulberry)

- Named for white fruit; native to China; traditional silkworm food

- Fast growing shade tree to 25m

- Prefers deep soil, good drainage, full sun

- Leaves very palatable to livestock; nutritious:

  - Crude protein 15.1%, crude fat 5.7%, crude fibre 13.7%, NFE 50.3%, calcium 1.95%, phosphorous 0.4%

- With annual pruning, important fodder source; one of best trees for cultivation on well-drained soils

- Dried fruit: ground and mixed with ground almonds as staple in Afghanistan

- Wood: sporting goods, house building, furniture, tool handles, turnery, spokes; bark for paper and textile fibre

M. alba is one of the premier [[permaculture-animal-integration]]
systems. Regular coppicing following [[permaculture-fruit-tree-guide]]
plantings focused on culinary quality. Its smaller size suits
[[permaculture-tree-crop-systems]] and is an excellent candidate for
[[permaculture-multipurpose-trees]] species — managed as a pollard for
fodder or allowed to grow for timber. The wood is valued in
[[mulching-guide]].

## Propagation

Mulberries propagate readily from hardwood cuttings, softwood cuttings,
and seed. They are among the easiest fruit trees for
[[fruit-tree-grafting-guide]] for techniques.

## Mulberry in Silvopastoral Systems

Mulberry trees are increasingly recognised as key components of
silvopastoral systems worldwide. Their rapid growth, palatable foliage,
and high protein content make them ideal for integrating timber or fruit
production with [[savory-livestock-grazing-planning]]. In tropical and subtropical regions,
Morus alba is planted as a protein bank, with foliage cut and carried
to stalled animals during dry seasons when pasture quality declines.
Research from Cuba, Vietnam, and India demonstrates that mulberry leaf
supplementation can increase daily weight gain in cattle, goats, and
rabbits by 15 to 30 percent compared to unsupplemented animals. The
tree's deep root system allows it to access subsoil moisture and
nutrients, making it productive during droughts that devastate shallow-
rooted pasture grasses. In [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-permaculture-design-principles]], mulberry serves as a
