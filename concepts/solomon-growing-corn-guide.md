### Pest Control

After pollen drops, the silks become attractive to the corn earworm moth.
This is the time to dust the silks with Bt if that pest is a problem in
your area. See [[solomon-seed-saving-vegetable-breeding]].

## Saving Corn Seed

Corn is wind-pollinated. The pollen is heavy and will not blow more than
about one mile (1,600 m) except in a gale strong enough to knock corn
plants flat. Isolating varieties by a quarter mile (400 m) will usually be
good enough for home-garden seed, provided the second patch is not upwind
in the direction of prevailing winds.

### Selection Method

It is possible to determine quality by nibbling a portion of the hooking
ear to see how it tastes, thus making selection while the main ear
develops. Allow the seed-making plants to stand until they have fully
dried. If the season is not long enough, harvest the ears shortly after
the first frost. Pull back the wrappers without tearing them off and plait
them, hanging the ears to finish drying.

To produce truly pure seed of a corn variety, out of 100 or more plants
requires a patch of at least 1,600 square feet (150 sq m). Solomon has
also produced acceptable OP sweet corn seed from as few as 50 desirable
plants, though this risks narrowing the [[cannabis-gene-pool-preservation-acclimatization-clarke]] excessively.
