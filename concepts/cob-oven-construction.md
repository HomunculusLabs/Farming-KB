# Cob Oven Construction

A cob oven (also called an earth oven, clay oven, or wood-fired bread oven) is an outdoor baking structure built from natural materials: clay, sand, and straw. Cob ovens reach 700-900F and bake excellent bread, pizza, roasts, casseroles, and more. They cost very little to build, require no special skills, and can last for decades with basic maintenance. A cob oven pairs naturally with a [[rocket-stove-design]] for complementary cooking infrastructure.

## Cob Basics

Cob is an ancient [[fungal-building-material-deterioration]] made from:

- **Clay** — the binder. Acts like glue. Source from your subsoil (dig 12-24 inches below topsoil) or purchase powdered clay.
- **Sand** — the aggregate. Provides structure and prevents cracking. Sharp sand (builder's sand) works best.
- **Straw** — the fiber reinforcement. Prevents cracking and adds tensile strength. Use clean, dry straw (not hay — hay contains seeds and decomposes).

The ratio is typically 1 part clay to 2-3 parts sand by volume, with straw mixed in by handful. Test your soil: fill a jar 1/3 full of soil from your dig site, add water, shake vigorously, and let settle for 24 hours. Sand settles first, then silt, then clay. You want at least 20% clay content for good cob.

## Site Selection

- Level, well-drained ground — do not build in a low spot where water collects
- At least 10 feet from any combustible structure
- Accessible from the kitchen — you will carry food back and forth
- Wind-protected — wind affects the fire and your comfort while tending it
- Consider a simple roof or shelter to protect the oven from rain

## Foundation

The oven is heavy (500-1000+ pounds). A proper foundation prevents settling and cracking.

### Simple Foundation (Temporary Installations)
1. Dig a shallow pad 4-6 inches deep, slightly wider than the oven base
2. Fill with compacted gravel or crushed rock
3. Top with a layer of [[comparison-mulch-types-and-effects-vs-landscape-fabric-alternatives]] and 2-3 inches of sand, leveled

### Permanent Foundation
1. Pour a concrete pad or lay concrete blocks (CMUs)
2. Size: 48-60 inches diameter, 4-6 inches thick (concrete pad) or a block platform
3. Include a 2-inch layer of insulating material on top of the foundation (perlite, pumice, or a dry mix of 1 part cement to 10 parts perlite). This insulates the hearth from the ground.
4. Level carefully in all directions

## Materials List

- Clay soil (20-50 gallons, depending on oven size)
- Sharp sand (40-100 gallons)
- Straw (1-2 bales)
- Firebricks for the hearth floor (30-40 standard firebricks, 4.5x9 inches)
- Recycled brick or stone for the oven base walls (optional but attractive)
- Newspaper (for insulation layer)
- Optional: ceramic fiber blanket for additional insulation
- Optional: lime wash or earthen plaster for the finished exterior

## Construction Steps

### Step 1: Build the Base (if raised)
A raised base brings the oven to a comfortable working height (36-40 inches hearth height). Build with:
- Concrete blocks, stone, or urbanite (recycled concrete chunks)
- Fill the base with gravel or rubble for stability
- Leave an opening in the base for wood storage or as a warming chamber
- Top the base with a level platform

### Step 2: Install the Hearth
1. Lay a 2-inch bed of fine sand or fireclay on the insulated foundation
2. Set firebricks tightly together on the sand bed, flat side up
3. Tap each brick level with a rubber mallet
4. The hearth should be perfectly flat and level — this is your baking surface
5. Do not use mortar between the hearth bricks. The sand bed allows them to expand and contract with heat without cracking.

### Step 3: Build the Insulation Layer
1. Cover the hearth bricks with a sheet of damp newspaper (so the oven dome does not stick to the hearth)
2. Mound damp sand on the newspaper to form the dome shape. This is a temporary form — the oven dome is built over it and the sand is removed later.
3. Dome dimensions: 18-22 inches high, 24-30 inches wide for a family-sized oven
4. Cover the sand dome with a layer of wet newspaper or plastic sheeting (keeps the sand clean during removal)

### Step 4: Build the Oven Door
Set a wooden form (a piece of 2x6 or similar cut to your desired door opening) into the sand dome at the front. The door opening should be:
- 63% of the dome height (this ratio ensures good draw)
- 10-12 inches wide for a medium oven
- Slightly wider at the bottom than the top (trapezoidal shape improves airflow)

### Step 5: Mix and Apply the Thermal Layer (First Cob Layer)
1. Mix cob: combine clay and sand in roughly 1:2 ratio on a tarp. Add water gradually and mix by folding the tarp and stomping with bare feet. When the mix holds together when squeezed but does not ooze water, the consistency is right. Add straw and mix until well distributed.
2. Build the thermal layer 3-4 inches thick over the sand dome. Start from the base and work up, pressing firmly to eliminate air pockets.
3. Do not cover the door opening.
4. Smooth the surface with wet hands.
5. Let this layer dry for several hours (not overnight — it should still be tacky for the next layer to bond).

### Step 6: Add the Insulation Layer (Second Layer)
1. Mix a looser, lighter cob with more straw and less clay. Alternatively, use a mix of sawdust and clay slip.
2. Apply 4-6 inches over the entire thermal layer. This layer keeps heat inside the oven rather than radiating it to the outside.
3. For maximum insulation, wrap the thermal layer in ceramic fiber blanket before applying the insulating cob.

### Step 7: Apply the Protective Outer Layer
1. Mix a final layer of cob (similar to the thermal layer mix) and apply 2-3 inches thick.
2. This layer protects the insulation from weather and physical damage.
3. Shape and smooth to your aesthetic preference.
4. Optional: apply a lime wash (1 part lime to 3 parts water) for weather protection and a white finish.

### Step 8: Remove the Sand Form
1. Let the entire oven dry for several days to a week (longer in humid climates)
2. Remove the door form
3. Reach in and scoop out the sand through the door opening
4. Pull out the newspaper layer
5. Your oven interior is revealed — the firebricks form the baking floor

## The Door

Build a wooden door to fit the oven opening:
- Use hardwood (oak, maple) — softwoods will char and burn
- Cut to fit the opening with a slight taper (wider at the bottom)
- Add a handle and insulation (a layer of ceramic fiber blanket or wet newspaper on the oven-facing side)
- The door is used after the fire is removed to seal the oven for retained-heat baking

## Firing the Oven

### First Fire (Curing)
- Build a very small fire and let it burn for 2-3 hours
- Gradually increase fire size over 3-4 sessions to prevent cracking from thermal shock
- The oven will steam as moisture escapes — this is normal
- After 4-5 curing fires of increasing intensity, the oven is ready for full use

### Regular Firing
1. Build a fire in the back of the oven using small-diameter dry wood
2. Let the fire burn for 1-2 hours, adding wood to maintain a hot fire
3. The oven walls will turn from black to white as they reach full temperature (this indicates soot is burning off — the oven is ready)
4. Push the fire to one side or remove it entirely
5. Sweep the hearth with a damp rag
6. Insert the door and wait 10-15 minutes for temperatures to equalize
7. Bake! Bread bakes in 2-5 minutes at 700-800F. Pizza in 90 seconds to 2 minutes.
8. As the oven cools (700F down to 200F over 4-8 hours), you can bake successive items: bread, then roasts, then casseroles, then granola, then dehydrate herbs.

## Maintenance

- Cover with a tarp or build a roof to protect from prolonged rain exposure
- Repair cracks with fresh cob (wet the crack first, then press in new cob)
- Re-apply lime wash annually for weather protection
- The hearth bricks may eventually crack from thermal cycling — replace individual bricks as needed
- A well-maintained cob oven can last 20+ years
