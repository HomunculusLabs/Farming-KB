# Chemical Modifications of LSD and Derivative Compounds

## The Logic of Chemical Modification

When a new type of active compound is discovered in pharmaceutical
research, whether by isolation from a plant or animal organ, or through
synthetic production as in the case of LSD, the chemist attempts through
alterations in its molecular structure to produce new compounds with
similar, perhaps improved activity, or with other valuable properties.
Of the approximately 20,000 new substances produced annually in
pharmaceutical-chemical research laboratories worldwide, the overwhelming
majority are modification products of proportionally few types of active
compounds. The discovery of a truly new type of active substance, new
with regard to chemical structure and pharmacological effect, is a rare
stroke of luck.

## Correcting the Lysergic Acid Structure

In 1949, Hofmann and his colleagues succeeded in correcting the
structural formula for lysergic acid proposed by American researchers.
They specified the valid structure of this common nucleus of all ergot
alkaloids, including LSD. The investigations of peptide alkaloids of
ergot led to complete structural formulas published in 1951. Their
correctness was confirmed through the total synthesis of ergotamine,
realized ten years later in collaboration with Dr. Albert J. Frey and
Dr. Hans Ott.

Dr. Paul A. Stadler was largely responsible for developing this
synthesis into a process practicable on an industrial scale. The
synthetic production of peptide [[ergot-fungus]] in tanks has great economic
importance. This procedure produces the [[lsd-molecule-of-perfection]], differentiated by the prefix "iso-" and the letters
D and L. Besides D-lysergic [[lsd-lysergic-acid-diethylamide-experience-guide]] (iso-LSD), L-lysergic acid
diethylamide (L-LSD), and L-isolysergic acid diethylamide (L-iso-LSD).
All three showed no psychic effects up to a dose of 0.5 mg, which
corresponds to a 20-fold quantity of a still distinctly active LSD dose.
This demonstrated the extreme stereospecificity of LSD's action on the
human psyche.
