# Soybean Oil Wastewater Fungal Treatment

## Overview

Wastewater from soybean oil manufacturing plants is characterized by high oil content (88% oils, 3% carbohydrates, 3% proteins, 6% unknown substances) and presents a significant treatment challenge. Fungal treatment using oil-decomposing yeasts has demonstrated effective removal without the need for chemical pretreatment, as part of [[protein-fat-wastewater-fungi]] strategies.

## Wastewater Characteristics

Soybean oil manufacturing wastewater exhibits:

- **COD range:** 24,000-61,000 mg/L
- **BOD range:** 12,000-27,200 mg/L
- **Hexane extract concentration:** 6,700-17,000 mg/L
- **pH:** 6.5-7.5
- **Total nitrogen:** 401-497 mg/L
- **Suspended solids:** 5,990-16,600 mg/L (mostly oils)

The London Dumping Treaty (1996) prohibits ocean disposal of treatment wastes, increasing the importance of effective biological treatment alternatives.

## Oil-Decomposing Yeast Isolation

Nine yeast strains were isolated from decomposing oil using enrichment culture techniques. Seven belong to the Candida genus, consistent with its reputation as a high lipase producer. Key findings:

- **Strain N9:** Removed 78% of hexane extracts and 94% of glycerides
- **Strain N1:** Removed 65% of hexane extracts
- **Mixed culture of all nine strains:** Superior to any single strain in both beta-oxidation capability and lipase activity
- **Synergism** demonstrated among mixed strains of oil-decomposing yeasts

## Treatment Process

### Optimal Conditions

- **Temperature:** 30-35 degrees C
- **pH:** 2.5-6.5 (without pH control in pilot operation at 25 degrees C)
- **Removal order** (most to least efficient): linoleic acid > soybean oil > raw wastewater

### Laboratory Scale

A 50-L tank reactor inoculated with all oil-decomposing yeasts removed more than 98% of hexane extracts at a daily load of 0.5 kg hexane extracts per kg yeast, with approximately 150 mg/L remaining. An activated sludge aftertreatment achieved the sea discharge target of 30 mg/L.

### Pilot Plant Operation

A pilot plant operated for one year with the following configuration:

1. Equalization tank
2. Yeast reactor tank (1.0 m3 capacity)
3. Yeast settling tank
4. Activated sludge tank (1.5 m3 capacity)
5. Final settling tank

Results during operation:
- **Yeast reactor:** 99% oil removal (0.3-0.9 kg hexane extract/kg yeast/day at 0.6-1.4 kg BOD load/kg yeast/day)
- **Activated sludge aftertreatment:** Reduced hexane extract to 2 mg/L
- **Treated water stability:** Maintained at approximately 100 mg/L within loading range of 0.30-0.75 kg hexane extract/kg yeast/day

### Dominant Yeast Species During Pilot Operation

- Candida hellenica (most dominant)
- Candida schatavii
- Candida fluviatilis

## Oil Decomposition Assay Methods

Two standardized tests evaluate oil-decomposing ability:

1. **Synthetic medium assay:** Lipase and beta-oxidation activities measured with 2% soybean oil as sole carbon source over 7 days, tracking hexane extract weight, acid value, and saponification value
2. **Comparative removal test:** Hexane extract removal rates compared between raw wastewater and synthetic media containing soybean oil or linoleic acid

## Related Applications

Similar yeast treatment approaches have been applied in dried food and fish meal industries, demonstrating the broad applicability of fungal oil decomposition technology across food processing sectors. The principles overlap with [[dairy-wastewater-fungal-treatment]] and [[protein-fat-wastewater-fungi]] fungal treatment approaches.
