## Mycofiltration Overview

Using mycelial mats to filter water — removing silt, pathogens, chemicals, and excess nutrients. One of the four branches of [[mycorestoration]]. From Chapter 8 of *Mycelium Running* by [[paul-stamets]].

## What Mycofiltration Can Remove

| Target | Mechanism | Species |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| **Silt/sediment** | Physical mesh of mycelium | Most saprophytes |
| **E. coli / coliforms** | Antibacterial secretions | Oyster, Stropharia |
| **Pseudomonas fluorescens** | Competitive displacement by Bacillus subtilis | Oyster |
| **Nitrates/phosphorus** | Metabolic uptake | Stropharia, oyster |
| **Hydrocarbons** | Enzymatic degradation | Oyster, turkey tail |
| **Heavy metals** | Bioaccumulation in fruiting bodies | Aspergillus, shiitake |
| **Pfiesteria** | Antibacterial secretions | Oyster |

## The Stropharia E. coli Discovery

[[paul-stamets]] discovered that garden giant mycelium (Stropharia rugoso-annulata) causes a dramatic die-off of E. coli:

1. Farm runoff carrying high E. coli from cattle manure
2. Stropharia mycelial mats placed downslope
3. Water samples before mycofilter: millions of coliforms
4. Water samples after passing through mycelium: undetectable
5. The mycelium was shown to selectively kill fecal coliforms while promoting beneficial bacteria

This has profound implications for agriculture — mycofilters could replace expensive water treatment systems at the edge of feedlots and manure ponds.

## Mycofilter Design

### Layered Construction (From Bottom to Top)
1. **Gravel base** (2-3 inches) — drainage
2. **Sawdust/wood chips** (3-4 inches) — primary substrate
3. **Spawn layer** (sawdust spawn at ~1/4 lb/sq ft)
4. **Corncobs** (4 inches) — aeration and rapid colonization
5. **Second spawn layer**
6. **Waste cardboard** — moisture retention, weed suppression
7. **Straw** (4-6 inches) — shade, moisture retention

### Key Design Principles
- Surface area should be several times the contamination source
- Corncobs are ideal — fibrous structure provides excellent aeration
- Water should percolate slowly through, not channel around
- Annual replenishment of organic material needed
- Replace straw bales every 1-3 years as mycelium degrades substrate

## Bunker Spawn for Filtration

Burlap sacks filled with inoculated sawdust:
- Stack like sandbags to create walls, berms, or channels
- Mycelium knits through the burlap, creating a living filter membrane
- Excellent for:
  - Edge of manure ponds and lagoons
  - Road runoff channels
  - Construction site perimeters
  - Stream bank stabilization
  - Edge of septic drain fields

## Road Reclamation (Tahuya State Forest Case Study)

- 500-foot stretch of abandoned logging road on hillside above salmon stream
- Road was primary vector of siltation into spawning grounds
- Treatment: 6 inches of waste wood (bark + chips + fir needles) + oyster spawn + wheat straw + mycorrhizal inoculum
- After 3 years: mantle of contiguous mycelia formed at wood chip/gravel interface
- Road surface stabilized; erosion eliminated; salmon habitat protected
- Cost: approximately 50% of conventional decommissioning (tank traps + scarification)

## Log Road Decommissioning Strategy

Stamets recommends:
1. Add fungal spores to chainsaw/bar chipper lubrication oil — fungi colonize wood as it's cut
2. Spread myceliated wood chips on abandoned road surfaces
3. A mesh of fungal networks develops, slowing silt passage
4. As fungi decompose chips, they nourish native flora
5. Roads transform into nature trails

Benefits over conventional methods (tank traps, ditches, scarification):
- Sediment reduction into streams and fisheries
- Enhanced moisture retention
- Reestablishment of native mycoflora
- Reduction of hydrocarbon contaminants from road use
- Filtration of coliform bacteria and pathogens
- Cooling of water flowing into streams
- Low impact on existing ecosystems
- Long-term investment protection (road subsurface reusable)
- Educational value as accessible restoration sites

## Agricultural Applications

- **Feedlot runoff**: Mycofilters downslope from manure ponds reduce coliform, nitrate, phosphorus
- **Septic systems**: Mycofiltration around drain fields reduces pathogen loading
- **Irrigation water**: Filter canal water before field application
- **Dairy operations**: Filter milk parlor wash water
- **Swine effluent**: Aspergillus niger removed 91% copper and 70% zinc (Price et al. 2001)

## Human Hair as Oil Sorbent + Mycelial Substrate

- Human hair naturally soaks up petroleum (5x its weight)
- Hair mats deployed on water to absorb oil (Matter of Trust organization)
- After saturation, hair mats can be saprophytized by oyster mushroom mycelium
- Mycelium metabolizes the petroleum while the hair provides structure
- This creates a two-stage cleanup: physical absorption → biological degradation
