## Village Financial Pool System

The schematic of capital flow within a village aims to:
- Keep money circulating productively within the system
- Reduce taxation through legal strategies
- Reduce external energy costs through conservation
- Reduce external trade by developing local resources
- Return resources to income and consumer production within the village

### Flow Components

1. **Local Currency** — created within the community for internal exchange
2. **Village Financial Pool** — reserves and sub-loan inventory for community investment
3. **Business Services** — salaries and wages paid within the community
4. **External Income** — from markets, tourism, and specialized services
5. **Export** — community surplus goods traded externally
6. **Import** — materials, services, and vehicles not available locally
7. **Barter/Sales** — goods and services exchanged through work groups, markets, cooperatives
8. **Consultancy** — expertise shared within and beyond the community

## Cooperative Enterprise Support Services

Essential administrative services for a bioregional cooperative:

- **Secretarial:** Communication, writing, presentation, filing, mail order
- **Bookkeeping:** Banking, cost accounting, summary analyses, billing
- **Accounting:** Tax assessment, trust advice, network planning
- **Legal:** Legal forms, leases, litigation, land conveyancy, trust structure design
- **Communication:** Phone, fax, photocopy, answering services, travel
- **Gift/Tithe/Public Service:** Funds for aid, tree planting, trust services
- **Education:** Seminars, speakers, instructing new members
- **Skills Register:** File on key people for special advice
- **Research:** Retrieval and basic research for region and projects

## Cooperatives and Leasing

Any cooperative or village can run a **leasing service** for seldom-used capital equipment (photocopiers to trucks). The user-pays principle applies: charges cover running costs, repairs, and replacements for all users — businesses, administration, trusts, and individuals.

## Special Co-investment

Community members can co-invest in shared projects:
- Group water storages or energy systems
- Group refrigeration facilities
- Co-ownership of fishing vessels or holiday homes

These save money and provide wider resource access than individual funding. The investing group decides expense, location, and use payments.

several practical factors including environmental conditions,

improve resilience and productivity over time.
