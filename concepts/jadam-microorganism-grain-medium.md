# JADAM Microorganism Grain Medium

Grains serve as an excellent medium for culturing microorganisms in JADAM.
The JMS method uses steamed rice or other grains placed in the field to capture
local [[lactic-acid-bacteria-knf]] and yeasts -- over pathogens.

## Grains Suitable for JMS Culture

### Primary: Rice
- Most commonly used grain in JADAM
- Steamed white rice is the standard medium
- Readily available worldwide
- Excellent moisture retention when steamed
- Mild pH favors diverse microbial colonization

### Secondary: Barley
- Barley works as an alternative to rice
- Similar starch content and structure
- May select for slightly different microbial populations
- Good in temperate climates where barley is abundant

### Other Options
- Wheat, oats, millet, sorghum can all be used
- Any starchy grain works as microorganism medium
- Choose based on local availability and cost

## Preparation: Steaming the Grain

### Rice Method
1. Wash rice thoroughly
2. Soak in water for 30 minutes to 1 hour
3. Steam (not boil) until grains are cooked but still firm
4. Rice should be sticky but not mushy
5. Each grain should be separate, not clumped together
6. Spread on a bamboo basket or steamer tray
7. Steam over boiling water for 20-30 minutes

### Key Points
- **Steaming is critical** -- boiling washes away nutrients
- Do not add salt or seasonings
- Rice should be slightly undercooked (al dente) -- too soft and it
  becomes anaerobic, too hard and microbes cannot access starch
- Let cool to [[ultra-low-cost-jadam-microorganism-solution-and-soil-foundation]]

## Grain-to-JMS Ratios Summary

| Stage | Ratio | Time |
|-------|-------|------|
| Steamed grain to field | Place as-is | 5-14 days |
| Fermented grain to water | 1:10 by weight | 3-7 days |
| Final JMS dilution for field use | 1:10 to 1:20 | Apply directly |

## Tips for Success

- Always use clean containers and tools
- Use non-chlorinated water (well water, rain water)
- Avoid placing grain medium in direct sunlight
- If grain dries out, mist with water -- do not flood
- Different locations yield different microbial populations
- Experiment with multiple locations to find the best microbial communities
