---# Cannabis and Thyroid Cancer

## Overview

Guided by hormones from the pituitary and hypothalamus glands, the thyroid
(Greek for "shield") is an integral part of the human endocrine system. Its
butterfly-like shape shields the front of the Adam's apple in males and females
alike. The thyroid produces hormones mainly triiodothyronine (T3) and
thyroxine (T4), intricately involved in physiological development, growth, and
cellular metabolism. The thyroid also enhances catecholamine effects of
adrenalin (increasing alertness and fight-or-flight responses).

Minor thyroid diseases fall into two basic categories:
- Hypothyroidism (underproduction of T3 and T4)
- Hyperthyroidism (overproduction of T3 and T4)

The thyroid produces both T3 and T4 by utilizing dietary iodine. A major
historical cause of thyroid cancer was orthodox medical radiation procedures on
necks and heads of children from 1940 to 1960. Ten to thirty years later, many
of those children developed thyroid cancer, which affects women at twice the
rate of males. Thyroid's affinity for iodine also makes it vulnerable to
radioactive iodine isotopes from nuclear accidents.

## Signs and Symptoms

Symptoms may include throat pressure, lumps or nodules, voice changes, swollen
lymph nodes, and difficulty swallowing. These may coexist with or be preceded
by hyperthyroidism or hypothyroidism, both of which may lead to autoimmune
difficulties.

### Hyperthyroidism vs. Hypothyroidism Symptoms

Hyperthyroidism: weight loss with increased appetite, feeling hot, rapid
heartbeat (tachycardia), toxic goiter, protruding eyes, sweating, diarrhea,
nervousness, tremors, difficulty sleeping, mood swings, light menses.

Hypothyroidism: weight gain with poor appetite, feeling cold, slow heartbeat
(bradycardia), dry hair and skin, brittle nails, non-toxic goiter, puffy face,
decreased sweating, constipation, depression, joint and muscle pains, heavy
menses.
