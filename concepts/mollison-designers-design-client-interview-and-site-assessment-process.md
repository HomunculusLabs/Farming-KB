## The Designer-Client Relationship

Mollison approaches the designer-client relationship as a collaborative
partnership rather than a top-down consultation. The designer's role is to
help clients clarify their goals, understand their site's potentials and
constraints, and develop a design that is ecologically sound, economically
viable, and personally fulfilling.

### Initial Contact and Scope Definition
The first contact between designer and client establishes the framework for
the entire project:

- **Client's motivation**: Understanding why the client is seeking a
  [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-permaculture-design-principles]] is essential. Are they motivated by food production,
  environmental restoration, energy independence, [[urban-permaculture-community-building-social-design]], aesthetic
  goals, economic return, or a combination? Different motivations lead to
  different design priorities.
- **Property details**: Size, location, legal tenure (ownership, lease,
  community title), existing infrastructure, current use, and any constraints
  (covenants, easements, zoning).
- **Budget and resources**: Available financial resources, labour (family,
  volunteers, contractors), equipment, and timeline. Mollison's
  mollison-design-report-structure
  varies accordingly.

## The Client Interview Process

Mollison recommends a structured but conversational interview process that
covers the following domains:

### Lifestyle and Values Assessment
Understanding how the client lives and what they value determines the design
approach:

- **Household composition**: Number of people, ages, dietary preferences,
  health considerations, pets, livestock interests. This directly informs
  mollison-zone-one-garden-design scale.
- **Time availability**: How many hours per week can the client devote to
  garden and property management? This determines the appropriate balance
  between intensive zone 1-2 systems and self-maintaining zone 3-5 systems.
  The [[mollison-permaculture-design-course-curriculum]] provides baseline expectations.
- **Long-term vision**: Where does the client see themselves and the property
  in 5, 10, 20 years? Is this a retirement property, a family homestead, a
  commercial farm, an educational facility, or a transition step?
- **Cultural and aesthetic preferences**: Food preferences, building styles,
  landscape aesthetics, and cultural practices all influence design choices.

### Resource Assessment
A thorough inventory of available resources guides realistic design:

- **Financial resources**: Capital available for establishment, annual
  maintenance budget, expected income from the property.
- **Water resources**: Existing water sources (mains, bore, spring, stream,
  rain), water rights or restrictions, water quality. See
  [[mollison-designers-home-energy-conservation-and-solar-heating]].
- **Material resources**: Existing buildings, fencing, tools, machinery,
  timber, stone, and other on-site materials.
- **Human resources**: Family labour, community networks, WWOOFers, student
  groups, local tradespeople. The mollison-seed-resources-and-sovereignty.
