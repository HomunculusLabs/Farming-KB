# Liberty Cap — Psilocybe semilanceata History and Biology

The Liberty Cap (Psilocybe semilanceata) is the most widely distributed and commonly consumed [[emcdda-psilocybin-non-addictive-profile-tolerance-dynamics]] in the temperate world. Its name derives from the distinctive Phrygian bonnet shape of its cap, associated with the French revolutionaries who stormed the Bastille.

## Distribution and Habitat

The Liberty Cap appears in autumn months, growing in great abundance in "troops."
It is found in pastures across the British Isles, especially in acid upland pastures — the wet, chilly sheep fields of Wales, the Pennines, Devon and Cornwall, and Scotland.
Despite being thought of as "British," it actually grows in many temperate regions worldwide:
- Western Europe: Scandinavia to the Spanish Picos mountains
- Eastern Europe: Czech Republic to Russia
- North America: Pacific Northwest
- [[guzman-allen-gartz-africa-southern-hemisphere-underexplored-neurotropic-fungi]]: New Zealand and Tasmania

## Chemistry and Pharmacology

The Liberty Cap contains a high and predictable concentration of psilocybin: about 1 per cent.

It also contains trivial amounts of psilocin but significant amounts (0.36 per cent) of baeocystin (4-phosphoryloxy-N-methyltryptamine).

The stability of these concentrations is critical — were they not so consistent, dosage would be impossible to gauge. Any twenty mushrooms picked in different parts of the world will have, on average, the same concentration of active ingredients and therefore the same pharmacological effect.

This predictability contrasts sharply with other psychoactive fungal species whose active compound concentrations vary considerably, making them impractical for intentional use.

## Modern Legal Status

The legal status of Liberty Caps has fluctuated across jurisdictions. In Britain, fresh mushrooms were legal until the Drugs Act 2005, which closed a loophole that had permitted their sale.
