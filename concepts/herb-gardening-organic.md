# Herb Gardening Organic

Updated: 2026-04-18

Herbs should always be included in the organic garden, for both practical and aesthetic reasons. They are generally not difficult to grow and most are extremely decorative plants that help attract useful predators, as well as pollinating insects like bees. They can be planted around shrubs as ground cover to aid [[staycare-spent-mushroom-compost-bioremediation]] compost is ideal.

The one thing most herbs cannot stand is bad drainage. If your soil is wet and heavy and cannot be improved by deep digging or the inclusion of coarse sand and [[holzer-frost-protection-techniques]]. Dig, divide, and replant every four years as the flavor deteriorates with age.

### Basil
Plant in a sunny, sheltered place in the best possible soil. Sow in small pots inside in early spring, harden off, and plant 12in (30cm) apart when all danger of frost has passed. Pinch off flower buds to maintain growth. Leaves can be dried quickly in a hot oven or frozen in ice cubes.

### Rosemary
This aromatic evergreen shrub is frost-tender in severe winters. In temperate areas, provide a sheltered position. Propagate from semi-ripe cuttings in late summer. Harvest fresh sprigs throughout the year.

### Sage
Hardy shrub reaching 2ft (60cm). Very drought-tolerant once established. Prune annually after flowering to prevent the plant from becoming leggy. Replace plants every 4-5 years as they become woody.

### Fennel
A vigorous hardy perennial reaching 5ft (1.5m) with finely divided, feathery green leaves and bright-yellow flowers. Do not plant near coriander, caraway, or dill as they may cross-pollinate. Sow seeds 2ft (60cm) apart in fall or spring. Dig, divide, and replant established plants every three years. Harvest fresh leaves as required and collect seeds by hanging flower heads upside down.

### Lovage
A shrubby, hardy perennial that dies down each year, growing extremely tall. Needs a lot of space -- best at the back of a large herb garden. Tolerates partial shade but prefers sun. Needs moist soil with plenty of organic matter. Sow seeds in spring or plant divided clumps in early spring or fall. Set plants 3ft (90cm) apart. Dig, divide, and replant as needed. All parts of the plant have a strong celery-like flavor.

### Chives
Attractive border edging with distinctive globular lilac flowers. Sow seeds outside 12in (30cm) apart in early spring, or divide established plants in spring or fall. Dig up clumps every three years in early or mid-fall, divide with a knife, and replant in fresh soil. Cut leaves with scissors to about 1/2in (1cm) -- they can be frozen in ice cubes. The bulbs can be pickled in wine vinegar.

### Parsley
Sow an early crop inside in late winter, transfer to open ground in mid-spring at 6in (15cm) apart. Alternatively, sow directly outside in mid-spring and again in midsummer. Seeds take a long time to germinate, so be patient. Parsley prefers some shade and soil enriched with well-rotted manure or compost. Remove weeds and water during dry weather. Cut leaves as needed without completely defoliating the plant.

### Chervil
A self-seeding hardy annual with a spicy, aniseed flavor. It requires a shaded position in moist soil, or it will run to seed as soon as the sun shines. Sow seeds in early or mid-spring, 9in (23cm) apart. Keep plants well-watered in dry weather and pinch off flowers to delay seeding. Harvest fresh leaves from the outside of the plants. Leaves can be frozen or dried.

### Sorrel
Hardy perennial with a sharp taste -- use sparingly. Prefers partial shade and moist soil enriched with organic matter. Plant seeds or roots about 12in (30cm) apart in fall. Remove flower heads as soon as they appear. Keep weed-free and well-watered. Dig up, divide, and replant the outer young offsets every three years. Dry leaves flat on a wire tray for best results.
