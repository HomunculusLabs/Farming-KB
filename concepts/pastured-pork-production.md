# Pastured Pork Production

Pasture-raised pork comes from pigs raised outdoors with access to living vegetation, fresh air, and space to root and forage. Unlike confinement operations, pastured pigs produce well-marbled, flavorful meat with superior nutritional profiles and strong market appeal.

## Why Pastured Pork

Benefits over confinement:
- Higher vitamin D (sunlight exposure)
- More omega-3 fatty acids from forage
- Better animal welfare (natural behaviors: rooting, wallowing, exploring)
- Lower feed costs (20-40% of diet from pasture/forage)
- Superior meat flavor and fat quality
- Lower input costs, higher per-pound sale price

## Breed Selection

Heritage breeds are preferred for pasture systems:
- Berkshire: excellent marbling, dark meat, premium market price, calm
- Tamworth: "bacon pig" -- long body, lean, excellent foragers
- Large Black: docile, excellent foragers, good mothers, black skin (sun tolerant)
- Gloucestershire Old Spot: hardy, good mothers, flavorful meat
- Duroc: fast growth, good muscling, red skin (sun tolerant), common cross
- Red Wattle: disease resistant, hardy, good foragers, lean meat
- Kunekune: small, hairy, excellent grazers (need less grain), good for homesteads

Crossbreeding: Berkshire x Duroc or Tamworth x Berkshire crosses combine fast growth with meat quality.

## Forage Crops for Pigs

Pigs are omnivores and will consume a wide variety of forage:

### Permanent Pasture Species
- White clover, [[red-clover]], alfalfa (high protein)
- Orchardgrass, ryegrass, fescue
- Chicory (deep-rooted, drought-tolerant, high mineral content)
- Plantain (broadleaf, palatable, mineral-rich)

### Annual Forage Crops
- Winter rye and oats for cool-season grazing
- Cowpeas and soybeans for summer protein
- Turnips and radishes for fall/winter root crops
- Field peas (spring planting)
- Sorghum-[[sudangrass]] for summer bulk

### Supplemental Forage
- Pumpkins and squash: excellent fall feed, pigs eat entire fruit
- Apples, pears, windfall fruit
- Garden waste and vegetable trimmings
- Acorns (limit to 25% of diet -- tannin toxicity risk)
- Dairy waste (whey, skim milk) -- excellent protein source
- Spent brewers grain -- high protein, available from local breweries

## Feed Management

Pigs cannot survive on pasture alone -- grain supplementation is required.

### Feed Ratio
- Pasture/forage: 20-40% of diet (varies by quality and season)
- Grain ration: 60-80% of diet
- Grower ration: 16% protein
- Finisher ration: 13-14% protein
- Feed conversion: 3-4 lbs feed per 1 lb gain (pastured) vs. 2.5-3.0 (confinement)

### Feeding Strategy
- Free-choice grain in self-feeders, rotated with pasture
- Alternatively, split-feed: grain morning, pasture afternoon
- Never restrict water -- pigs need 2-3 gallons/day
- Provide mineral supplement free-choice (especially selenium, zinc, copper)

## Finishing Timeline

| Stage | Weight Range | Duration | Feed Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weaning | 12-15 lbs (8 weeks) | -- | Creep feed before weaning |
| Nursery | 15-50 lbs | 6-8 weeks | 18-20% protein starter |
| Grower | 50-150 lbs | 8-10 weeks | 16% protein grower |
| Finisher | 150-250 lbs | 8-12 weeks | 13-14% protein finisher |
| Slaughter | 220-280 lbs live | 6-8 months total | Market weight |

Slaughter age: 6-8 months for heritage breeds (slower), 5-6 months for commercial crosses.

Ideal slaughter weight: 240-280 lbs live (175-200 lbs hanging weight).

## Fencing and Infrastructure

- Electric fencing is essential: 2-3 strands at 6, 12, and 24 inches
- Train pigs to electric fence in a small enclosure first
- Portable fencing (polywire + step-in posts) allows rotational grazing
- Shelter: simple 3-sided hoop house or A-frame, 8x10 ft minimum per 3-4 pigs
- Water: nipple waterers or heavy rubber tubs (they'll tip lightweight ones)
- Wallows: pigs need access to mud in summer for cooling and sun protection
- Winter: deep bedding (straw, hay) in shelter -- pigs will compost it themselves

## Pasture Rotation

- Move pigs every 1-4 weeks depending on stocking density
- Rest paddocks minimum 60 days (ideally a full growing season)
- Follow pigs with poultry to break parasite cycles
- Pigs root deeply -- use this intentionally on areas needing renovation
- Permanent pastures: use nose rings on heavy rooters if you want to preserve sod
- Sacrifice paddock for winter feeding -- keep pigs off wet pastures

## Carcass Quality and Cuts

Pastured pork carcass characteristics:
- Firmer, redder meat than confinement pork
- Cream-colored fat with better flavor
- Higher vitamin D and omega-3 content
- Smaller loin eye area (heritage breeds)
- More belly (bacon yield) in Tamworth and similar breeds

Average yield from 250 lb live pig:
- Hanging weight: 180-190 lbs (72-76% dress)
- Retail cuts: 120-140 lbs (65-75% of hanging weight)
- Bacon: 12-18 lbs per side
- Pork chops: 20-30 lbs
- Ground pork: 20-30 lbs
- Roasts: 15-25 lbs

## Economics

### Costs Per Pig
- Feeder pig (40 lbs): $80-150
- Feed: $200-350 (6-8 months)
- Mineral/supplements: $15-25
- Processing: $250-400 (butcher + cut/wrap/cure/smoke)
- Miscellaneous: $25-50

### Revenue Per Pig
- Live sale: $400-600 ($1.80-2.40/lb live)
- Custom cuts retail: $700-1,200 ($5-8/lb average retail)
- Premium retail (farmers market): $900-1,500 ($6-10/lb)

### Profitability
- Net profit per pig (retail): $200-600
- Selling live: $50-150 profit (thin margins)
- Key is direct marketing -- see [[farmers-market-selling-guide]]
- Annual production of 6-10 pigs can generate $2,000-6,000 net income
