# [[mycorrhizal-fungi]] in Heavy Metal [[phytostabilization]]

## Overview

[[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi]] (AMF) form symbiotic associations with
the roots of most terrestrial plants and play a central role in heavy
metal phytostabilization, as extensively documented in the Singh source.
AMF enhance plant establishment on metalliferous soils, reduce metal
bioavailability, and promote soil aggregation, collectively reducing
the environmental risk posed by contaminated sites. This approach is
increasingly recognized as a sustainable, low-cost alternative to
excavation and chemical immobilization.

## Arbuscular [[mycorrhizal-symbiosis]]

AMF belong to the phylum Glomeromycota and form intracellular
arbuscules within root cortical cells, creating an extensive
extraradical mycelial network in the surrounding soil. This hyphal
network extends the effective depletion zone of plant roots by up to
several centimeters, enhancing nutrient and water uptake. The symbiosis
is obligate for the fungus and largely beneficial for the host plant,
which exchanges photosynthetically fixed carbon (up to 20% of total)
for mineral nutrients, particularly phosphorus.

## Heavy Metal Tolerance Mechanisms

The Singh source identifies several mechanisms by which AMF confer
heavy metal tolerance to host plants:

- Extracellular immobilization: Fungal cell walls contain chitin,
  glucans, and melanin with abundant functional groups (carboxyl,
  amino, hydroxyl, phosphate) that bind metal cations through
  biosorption. Glomalin-related soil protein (GRSP) released by
  AMF hyphae also sequesters metals.

- Intracellular compartmentalization: Metals that enter fungal
  hyphae can be sequestered in vacuoles or bound to metallothioneins
  and phytochelatins, reducing cytoplasmic toxicity.

- Reduced translocation: AMF colonization alters root membrane
  transport selectivity, limiting metal uptake and translocation
  from roots to shoots. This is critical for phytostabilization,
  where the goal is to immobilize metals belowground.

- Altered rhizosphere chemistry: AMF exudates modify soil pH and
  redox potential, and release organic acids that can precipitate
  metals as insoluble compounds.

## Metals Addressed

AMF-mediated phytostabilization has been demonstrated for:

- Cadmium (Cd): [[glomus-intraradices]] and G. mosseae reduce Cd
  translocation to shoots in various crop species.

- Lead (Pb): Extraradical hyphae adsorb Pb through cell wall binding;
  Funneliformis mosseae effectively immobilizes Pb in contaminated
  soils.

- Zinc (Zn): AMF improve Zn tolerance by enhancing phosphorus
  nutrition, which competitively inhibits Zn uptake.

- Copper (Cu): Mycorrhizal plants show reduced Cu phytotoxicity
  through chelation and compartmentalization.

- Arsenic (As): AMF can suppress As uptake in some plant species
  by modulating phosphate transporter expression.

- Nickel (Ni) and Chromium (Cr): AMF associations mitigate toxicity
  in serpentine and tannery-affected soils.

## Plant Growth Promotion Under Metal Stress

AMF enhance plant fitness on contaminated soils through multiple
mechanisms beyond metal immobilization:

- Improved phosphorus nutrition compensates for reduced root
  function under metal stress.

- Enhanced water uptake through the hyphal network increases
  drought tolerance, which is often co-limiting on degraded sites.

- Production of phytohormones (auxins, cytokinins) and suppression
  of ethylene stress signaling promote root and shoot growth.

- Protection against root pathogens that may be more aggressive
  in stressed plant communities.

## Field Applications

The Singh source describes field trials where AMF inoculation of
native or crop plants achieved significant metal immobilization on
mine tailings, smelter-impacted soils, and industrially contaminated
land. Key considerations include:

- Selection of indigenous AMF isolates adapted to local soil
  conditions and metal assemblages.

- Compatibility of AMF with selected host plants, prioritizing
  native, metal-tolerant species.

- Soil amendments (organic matter, lime, phosphate) to support
  plant and fungal establishment.

- Long-term monitoring to confirm persistent metal immobilization
  and ecosystem recovery.
