## Free-Range Poultry Forage Systems Design

Free-range poultry systems in [[permaculture]] integrate birds with multi-layered plant communities designed to supply year-round forage, eliminating or reducing the need for stored, husked grains. Mollison presents the most detailed poultry forage species list in permaculture literature, with integrated system design for homesteads.

## The Forage System Concept

"By planting and observing a variety of forage species, we greatly reduce (perhaps even obviate) the need for stored, husked grains, and should therefore be ahead in terms of energy "economics.'"

The plant list doubles as a bee forage system and would evolve into a cattle forage situation over time. Many species also withstand sea winds, salted soils, and frosts, suiting coastal and desert situations.

## Integrated System Components

### Four Broad-Scale Systems (2,000 m²+)
1. **Hardy free-range system of Zone II**: Plus normal orchard species; stored foods gathered here
2. **Stryards**: For seasonal forage, gleaning, and production of stored seeds (deep-mulched, gate enclosures for emergency food from grains and pulses)
3. **Throwover pens**: For more tender greens and highly-selected browse species (chard, etc.)
4. **Storage bins or sheds**: For hard foods during spring "hungry gap" when seeds and berries are few

### Broadscale Possibilities
Pigeons, quail, or pheasant in field shelters harvesting crops. Spacing of field shelters dictated by:
- Keeping pure-bred flocks separated
- Normal ranging habits of the birds
- Scale of operation (time available to harvest eggs or meat)

## Establishment

As for orchards:
1. Control browsing herbivores
2. Sow to herbal leys
3. Establish nitrogenous or leguminous plants as manure crop
4. Slower-evolving trees, shrubs, herbs, and root crops follow

### Initial Costs
- Fencing
- Initial mulching of strawyards
- Buildings, water supply
- Stock provision (plant and animal species)

### Evolution
Species such as fungi and insects soon begin to complicate the system and may provide resources beyond those planned. Because of high manurial and mechanical turnover in strawyards, rough bracken, cornstalk, hedge clippings, and straw residues quickly shred and decay to mulch.

### Stocking Timeline
- Small species (ducks, hens, guinea-fowl) from the beginning
- Geese added after first year or so
- Weed control achieved by regulating time and density of stocking rates

## Forage Storage

### Sources
1. Hard seedpods and seed heads from strawyards
2. Gathered or raked windfalls from walnut, oak, carob, and similar trees

### Storage Needs
- Racks, dry shelves, bins
- Wires for corn cobs (cribs)
- Drying floors or pits for acorns and chestnuts
- Overhead hooks or racks for sunflower heads
- Pest-proofing from rats and sparrows essential
- Small hammer mill a great asset

## Regulation of Yield

### Steady Forage Yield (Reduce Storage Needs)
| Season | Forage Available |
|--------|-----------------|
| Early summer | Hard seed ([[acacia]], Robinia, Curanga) drops mainly; some residue all year |
| Late summer–autumn | Berries follow; persist to late winter |
| Autumn–spring | Large seeds (walnuts, acorns) persist; all year if gathered and dried |
| Spring–early summer | Greens and annuals carry over |

### Regular Egg Yield
- **Dorkings** for winter
- **Ducks** for continuity
- **Heavy breeds** for spring
- **Leghorns** for summer laying
- **Pigeon and quail**: yield most of the year if age classes kept mixed
- **Wyandottes and/or silky bantams**: for brooding eggs of other species (avoid mechanical brooders)

## Poultry Forage Days (P.F.D.)

"The value of certain trees and plants can be expressed in terms of P.F.D. or "poultry forage days'."

### Known Equivalents
- **Mature tree lucerne**: ~2.5 kg of seed = 30 P.F.D. (keeps a hen for 30 days)
- **Black walnut at maturity**: keeps 8 hens all year = 365 × 8 = **2,920 P.F.D.**
- **Carob (17-year-old)**: 3–6 bean daily supplement for 3 goats all year = **1,000+ G.F.D.** (goat forage days) concentrate value

"Plants of high value, even if they are slow to grow, can be preferentially planted."

## Poultry Forage Species by Season

### Summer Seed and Pod Species

#### Shrubs and Trees
- **Tree Lucerne** (Chaemocytisus proliferus): Early to mid-summer seed drop; foliage also edible; hardy
- **Lespedeza** (L. bicolor, L. cytobotrya, L. sericea, L. striata): Similar
- **Atriplex** (salt-bushes): Wind, salt, and pest resistant
- **Black Locust** ([[robinia-pseudoacacia]]): High protein pods; leaves browsed; timber value
- **Honey Locust** ([[gleditsia-triacanthos]]): Edible pods; good timber
- **Carob** ([[ceratonia-siliqua]]): 68% sugar beans; major forage and human food crop
- **Mesquite** (Prosopis spp.): Edible pods; nitrogen-fixing
- **Acacia spp.**: A. aneura (mulga), A. decurrens, A. melanoxylon, A. pycnantha
- **Albizia** (A. julibrissin, A. lophantha): Summer seed; foliage browsed
- **Tagasaste** (Chamaecytisus proliferus): As tree lucerne
- **Sesbania**: Quick-growing; edible pods

#### Annuals and Grasses
- **Sunflower**: Heads fed entire to poultry; good oil and food for humans
- **Sorghum, millets, sudax**: Grain and forage
- **Corn (maize)**: Cobs stored; interplant with beans
- **Amaranthus**: Seed and leaf; high protein
- **Chenopodium** (goosefoot): Seed and leaf
- **Panicum, Eragrostis**: Native millets
- **Lamb's quarters**: Self-seeding annual

### Autumn–Winter Berry and Fruit Species
- **Mulberry** (Morus spp.): First-class poultry fodder
- **Coprosma** (C. repens, C. robusta): Persistent berries; salt/wind resistant
- **Pyracantha** (firethorn): Persistent berries
- **Holly** (Ilex): Winter berries
- **Cotoneaster**: Persistent berries
- **Elaeagnus** (oleaster, goumi): Autumn berries; nitrogen-fixing
- **Hippophae** (sea buckthorn): Persistent berries
- **Rosa** (rose hips): Winter forage
- **Crataegus** (hawthorn): Persistent haws
- **Prunus** (cherry, plum): Fruit for poultry
- **Diospyros** (persimmon): Late fruit
- **Ficus**: Fig fruit
- **Pistacia**: Late seed
- **Owenia** (emu berry): Native berry
- **Carthanium** (native currant): Fruit and foliage

### Spring Greens
- **Comfrey** (Symphytum): High protein; cut-and-come-again
- **Lab-lab** (Dolichos): Climbing bean; high protein
- **Broad beans**: Pods and seed; resprout if hard-cut
- **Tree lucerne**: Fresh foliage
- **Lucerne**: Grazing and cut forage
- **Chicory**: Deep-rooted; persistent greens
- **Dandelion**: Early spring greens
- **Plantain**: Persistent greens
- **Borage**: Bee forage; leaves edible
- **Mustard**: Quick spring crop
