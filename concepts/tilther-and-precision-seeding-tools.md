## The Tilther and Precision Seeding Tools

## Overview

The Tilther and precision multi-row seeders form the backbone of soil preparation and planting in Eliot Coleman's [[coleman-winter-harvest-greenhouse-temperature-management]] system. These tools were developed or adopted out of necessity — when no suitable commercial alternatives existed for intensive, indoor, year-round [[coleman-french-historical-winter-vegetable-production]]. Together with complementary tools like the broadfork, Austrian hay rake, and mesh roller, they enable a complete bed-preparation-to-planting workflow that is quiet, fume-free, and gentle on soil structure.

## The Tilther

### Design and Operation

The Tilther is a custom-designed, electrically powered shallow tiller created by Eliot Coleman when no commercial electric tiller was available on the market. It is specifically engineered for greenhouse use, where noise and exhaust fumes from gas-powered equipment are unacceptable in enclosed growing spaces.

The tool is powered by a cordless drill that sits on top of the housing. The drill drives a shaft connected by a chain to the tine shaft below, which rotates to cultivate the soil surface. A rope wrapped around the drill's trigger is pulled from the handlebars, allowing the operator to control the tines from a comfortable standing position while walking down the bed.

A key design feature is the side-mounted gearbox, which eliminates the center strip of untilled soil that conventional tillers leave down the middle of the bed. This ensures uniform cultivation across the entire 30-inch bed width.

The Tilther works only the top 2 inches of soil — a deliberately shallow depth that serves an important agronomic purpose (see below). It produces little noise and no fumes, making it ideal for indoor greenhouse use. A battery charger is kept in the greenhouse so fresh batteries are always available.

### The Rationale for Shallow Tillage

The 2-inch working depth of the Tilther is not a limitation but a deliberate strategy. Weed seeds rarely germinate from deeper than 2 inches below the soil surface. By limiting cultivation to this shallow zone, the Tilther:

- **Preserves soil structure** in the lower profile, maintaining the natural aggregation and [[cannabis-rhizosphere-microbial-communities]] that develop over time
- **Avoids bringing dormant weed seeds to the surface**, where light and moisture would trigger germination
- **Incorporates amendments and compost** only where they are most effective — in the zone where seeds germinate and young roots establish

This shallow-tillage philosophy is central to the low-weed, high-germination success of the [[winter-harvest-system]].

## Soil Aeration with the Broadfork

Before using the Tilther, the soil is aerated by making a pass down the bed with a broadfork. The broadfork is a manual soil-loosening tool with:

- **Two handles**, each 4 feet long, sitting upright at either end of a 30-inch-wide metal crossbar
- **Seven tines**, each 10 inches long, attached to the crossbar for soil penetration

### Step-and-Pull Technique

The operator uses a specific rhythm to aerate the bed:

1. Apply body weight with one foot to press the tines fully into the soil
2. Pull back on the handles just enough to lift and loosen the lower soil slightly — without fully inverting it
3. Raise the tines out of the soil
4. Step forward 6 inches and repeat the sequence

This 6-inch progression ensures thorough, even aeration of the entire bed. The broadfork loosens compacted subsoil and improves drainage and root penetration without the destructive effects of mechanical deep tilling.

## Crop Debris Removal

Old crops are removed on a bed-by-bed basis before replanting. A clean seedbed is essential because decomposing green matter can inhibit germination of the subsequent crop.

### Techniques

- **Twisting out lettuce plants**: When harvesting head lettuce, debris is removed simultaneously by twisting the plants out of the ground so the fat lower stem is partially extracted from the soil
- **Scythe mowing**: For larger areas of baby-leaf salad crops, the old growth is shaved off with a scythe held right down along the soil surface to leave no stubble. A file is carried for frequent blade re-sharpening
- **Greens harvester**: The powered greens harvester can also be used by running its blade along the soil surface with a forward motion, collecting spent plants directly into its cloth basket. Frequent re-sharpening is required here as well

### Root Mass Strategy

With all of these techniques, the root mass of the previous crop is deliberately left in the ground. Decomposing roots do not have the same inhibitory effect on germination as incorporated green-leaf residues would. The roots break down in place, contributing organic matter and soil structure.

## Compost Application Methodology

### Surface Application Philosophy

After broadforking and tiltering, compost is applied to the bed. Coleman's system has found that compost applications in the greenhouse are most effective when left on the surface and mixed only shallowly into the top 1/2 inch of soil.

This technique works exceptionally well, likely because it mimics nature: in undisturbed soils, the highest concentration of organic matter is at the surface. Exceptional germination rates result from keeping compost in this surface layer. The compost is eventually incorporated more deeply at the end of each crop cycle when the bed is prepared for the next planting.

### Bed Finishing Tools

1. **Austrian hay rake** (30-inch wide): Used to rake the bed smooth and mix compost shallowly into the top 1/2 inch. A wooden-tooth grading rake from a hardware store is a good alternative
2. **Mesh roller** (30-inch wide): A single light pass firms the bed surface, providing purchase for the toothed wheels of the [[precision-seeders]]

This finishing sequence produces a fine, firm, evenly composted seedbed optimized for precision seeding.

## Precision Seeders

### The Need for Multi-Row Precision

Greenhouse plantings must be far more intensive than field plantings because protected growing space is limited and expensive. Economically viable winter harvest production requires multi-row precision seeders capable of close row spacing — tools that did not exist in the American market when Coleman began commercial winter harvesting.

Coleman initially improvised by bolting five Earthway seeders together side by side to plant five rows at 4-inch spacing. While functional, this homemade rig was awkward and heavy, motivating the search for a better solution.

### The Pinpoint Seeder

The "pinpoint seeder" is a lightweight four-row precision seeder made in Germany. Despite its simplicity, it proved to be an extremely accurate and precise implement for greenhouse sowing. Key specifications:

- **Four rows** per pass
- **2.5-inch row spacing** between rows
- **Three passes** required to plant twelve rows across a standard 30-inch bed

The 2.5-inch spacing, while tighter than conventional field practice, is standard in European [[economics-of-winter-greenhouse-production]] and is essential for maximizing yield per square foot in protected culture.

### The Six-Row Seeder

When the German manufacturer discontinued the pinpoint seeder in 2004, Coleman collaborated with other interested growers to commission a new manufacturer to produce an improved six-row seeder. This successor incorporated many features that had been found lacking in the original pinpoint model. Key improvements:

- **Six rows** per pass instead of four
- **Two passes** to plant twelve rows on a 30-inch bed (versus three with the pinpoint)
- Refined design features for easier operation and more consistent seed placement

Both seeders remain in use, and most operational guidance for the pinpoint seeder applies equally to the six-row model. All sowing in the winter harvest system is done with one of these precision seeders.

## Complete Bed Preparation Sequence

The full soil preparation and planting workflow proceeds in this order:

1. **Remove crop debris** from the previous planting
2. **Broadfork** the bed for deep soil aeration
3. **Tilther** to incorporate soil amendments (e.g., alfalfa meal) in the top 2 inches
4. **Apply compost** to the surface
5. **Rake smooth** with the Austrian hay rake, mixing compost into the top 1/2 inch
6. **Firm the surface** with a mesh roller
7. **Seed** with the pinpoint or six-row precision seeder

This sequence preserves soil structure, minimizes weed pressure, maintains high organic matter at the surface, and produces the precise, clean seedbed that intensive greenhouse production demands.
