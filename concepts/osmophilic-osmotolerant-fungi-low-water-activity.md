# Osmophilic and Osmotolerant Fungi

Osmophilic and osmotolerant fungi are specialized organisms
capable of growth at low water-activity (aw) values resulting
from high concentrations of dissolved solutes such as sugars
and salts. They are critical agents of food spoilage in
concentrated sugar and salt products and represent an important
ecological group within [[biodiversity-xerophilic-fungi-dry-environments]] grow where low water
activity results from the matrix potential of the substratum
itself (drying). The effects of low water activity on growth
may be similar for both groups, but the causes differ.

## Mechanism of Osmotic Tolerance

High osmotic concentrations outside a cell cause it to lose
water, potentially killing the fungus or preventing spore
germination. The ability to preserve foods with highly
concentrated sugar or salt solutions depends on this principle.
In environments with naturally high salt concentrations (salt
brines, some soils), fungi must cope with both high ionic
strengths and little available water.
