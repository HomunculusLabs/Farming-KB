## Genes Induced During Trap Formation

The microarray data revealed several categories of genes dramatically upregulated during trap
formation:

### Adhesion Proteins
Genes encoding extracellular adhesive proteins were among the most strongly induced. These
include hydrophobins (small cysteine-rich proteins that self-assemble at hydrophobic/hydrophilic
interfaces), lectins (carbohydrate-binding proteins that may mediate initial nematode
recognition), and serine proteases that may modify the nematode cuticle surface.

### Signaling and Regulatory Genes
G protein-coupled receptors, mitogen-activated protein (MAP) kinase cascade components, and
cyclic AMP signaling genes were activated early in trap induction, suggesting these pathways
transduce the nematode-derived signal that triggers trap morphogenesis. Transcription factors
including Zn(II)2Cys6 binuclear cluster proteins were strongly upregulated.

### Cell Wall Remodeling Enzymes
Chitin synthases, glucanases, and other cell wall-modifying enzymes were induced, consistent
with the substantial cell wall rearrangements required to build complex trapping structures from
ordinary vegetative hyphae.
