## Overview
"Strain" in the context of Psilocybe cubensis cultivation refers to a selectively propagated variety with distinct visual and growth characteristics. Unlike botanical strains, mushroom strains are not genetically distinct subspecies — all P. cubensis strains can interbreed and are the same species. The differences between strains are analogous to dog breeds: the same species with varying traits selected for by cultivators. Choosing the right strain depends on your goals: yield, potency, ease of cultivation, visual appeal, or personal preference.
## Important Terminology
- **Variety**: The more accurate botanical term for what cultivators call "strains."
- **Isolate**: A specific genetic individual isolated from [[mushroom-spore-syringe-preparation|spore]] genetics. True isolates are genetically unique and produce consistent results.
- **Multi-spore (MS)**: Inoculation from spores containing many different genetic individuals. Results vary between grows.
- **Clone**: Tissue culture taken directly from a fruiting body. Genetically identical to the parent mushroom.
## Popular P. cubensis Strains
### Golden Teacher (GT)
The most widely known and cultivated P. cubensis variety. A reliable, forgiving strain that is often recommended for beginners.
- **Appearance**: Medium-sized mushrooms with golden-brown caps that lighten with age. Dense, meaty stems.
- **Cultivation**: Easy. Colonizes grain and bulk substrate quickly. Tolerant of minor environmental fluctuations. Consistent yields.
- **Potency**: Moderate. Typical P. cubensis potency (0.6-1.0% psilocybin by dry weight).
- **Yield**: Good. First flush yields of 2-4 oz dry from a standard monotub.
- **Growth characteristics**: Thick, rhizomorphic mycelium. Fruits readily without complicated environmental triggers.
- **Best for**: Beginners, cultivators seeking reliability and consistency.
### B+
One of the oldest and most widely distributed varieties. Originally collected in Florida.
- **Appearance**: Large mushrooms with caramel-colored caps. Can grow very large under optimal conditions. Caps often develop a pronounced "nipple" (umbo) at the center.
- **Cultivation**: Very easy. Extremely robust and forgiving. One of the most cold-tolerant varieties — reported to fruit at temperatures as low as 55F (13C).
- **Potency**: Moderate to low-moderate.
- **Yield**: High. Large fruiting bodies produce good dry weight.
- **Growth characteristics**: Very aggressive colonizer. Can sometimes overlay if FAE is insufficient.
- **Best for**: Beginners, outdoor cultivation, cold-weather grows.
### Penis Envy (PE)
A distinctive and highly sought-after variety known for its unusual appearance and reportedly high potency.
- **Appearance**: Thick, dense, bulbous stems with small, underdeveloped caps. The overall shape is phallic, hence the name. Often produces "blob" mutations — dense masses of tissue without clear cap/stem differentiation. Colors range from pale to deep blue.
- **Cultivation**: Difficult. Slower colonization than most varieties. Often produces fewer but much larger mushrooms. Requires patience and good environmental control. Higher contamination risk due to slow early growth.
- **Potency**: High to very high. Widely reported as 1.5-3x more potent than standard cubensis varieties, though scientific verification is limited. Some sources cite 1.3-1.8% psilocybin by dry weight.
- **Yield**: Lower by count but potentially higher by total weight due to dense tissue. Biological efficiency may be similar or slightly below average, but potency per gram is significantly higher.
- **Growth characteristics**: Very slow colonizer (often 30-50% longer than GT). Dense mycelium that can look bacterial due to its thick, wet appearance.
- **Subvarieties**: Penis Envy Uncut (caps rarely open), Melmac, Albino Penis Envy (APE), and Texas Penis Envy are all related variants with similar characteristics.
- **Best for**: Experienced cultivators seeking maximum potency. Not recommended for beginners.
### Amazonian
Collected in the Amazon region of South America.
- **Appearance**: Medium to large mushrooms with reddish-brown caps. Dense fruits.
- **Cultivation**: Easy to moderate. Fast colonizer. Produces well on CVG and manure-based substrates.
- **Potency**: Moderate to high. Often reported as above average for cubensis.
- **Yield**: Good to high. Consistent multi-flush performer.
- **Growth characteristics**: Aggressive rhizomorphic mycelium. Pins readily.
- **Best for**: Intermediate cultivators seeking a balance of yield and potency.
### Cambodian
Collected near the Angkor Wat temple complex in Cambodia.
- **Appearance**: Tall, slender mushrooms with medium brown caps. Often produces abundant small-to-medium fruits rather than fewer large ones.
- **Cultivation**: Easy. Fast colonizer. Fruits prolifically — often produces many small fruits rather than fewer large ones.
- **Potency**: Moderate.
- **Yield**: High by fruit count, moderate by dry weight.
- **Growth characteristics**: Very fast colonization and fruiting. Often the first to pin and mature in side-by-side comparisons.
- **Best for**: Beginners wanting quick results, cultivators who prefer many smaller fruits.
### Ecuadorian
Collected in the Ecuadorian highlands.
- **Appearance**: Large, thick-stemmed mushrooms with golden-brown caps. Classic cubensis morphology.
- **Cultivation**: Easy. Reliable and consistent. Tolerant of a range of conditions.
- **Potency**: Moderate.
- **Yield**: Good. Large fruits produce respectable dry weight.
- **Growth characteristics**: Thick, healthy mycelium. One of the most reliable fruiters.
- **Best for**: Beginners, those seeking classic cubensis appearance.
### Thai (Koh Samui, Lipa Yai)
Southeast Asian varieties known for fast growth and abundant fruiting.
- **Appearance**: Tall, thin mushrooms with light golden caps. The Koh Samui strain is notable for producing sclerotia (truffles) under certain conditions, though it is primarily grown for mushrooms.
- **Cultivation**: Easy. Very fast colonization and fruiting.
- **Potency**: Moderate.
- **Yield**: Good. High fruit count.
- **Growth characteristics**: Extremely fast and aggressive. Pins in clusters.
- **Best for**: Quick grows, cultivators who prefer many smaller fruits.
### Albino Varieties (AA+, APE, Albino Treasure Coast)
Lack of pigmentation resulting in white or near-white fruiting bodies.
- **Appearance**: White to bluish-white caps and stems. Bruise vividly blue. Often visually striking.
- **Cultivation**: Varies. APE is difficult (see Penis Envy). AA+ is easier but still more finicky than pigmented varieties.
- **Potency**: Generally higher than their pigmented counterparts. APE is particularly potent.
- **Yield**: Variable. Often lower than standard varieties due to the genetic mutation causing albinism.
- **Best for**: Experienced cultivators and those interested in unique aesthetics.
### McKennaii
Named in honor of Terence McKenna (though not directly connected to his work).
- **Appearance**: Large, dense mushrooms with thick stems and broad caps. Can grow very large.
- **Cultivation**: Moderate. Slower than GT but generally reliable.
- **Potency**: High. Consistently rated above average by the community.
- **Yield**: Good. Large fruits produce good dry weight.
- **Best for**: Intermediate cultivators seeking above-average potency.
## Selection Criteria
### For Beginners
Choose strains known for ease of cultivation and forgiveness: Golden Teacher, B+, Ecuadorian, or Cambodian. These varieties colonize quickly, fruit reliably, and tolerate minor mistakes in technique or environment.
### For Maximum Potency
Penis Envy (and its variants), McKennaii, and Albino Penis Envy are consistently reported as the most potent. However, potency varies significantly between individual grows and genetics, even within the same variety.
### For Maximum Yield
B+, Amazonian, and Golden Teacher produce the most consistent yields. Proper cultivation technique matters far more than strain selection for yield.
### For Speed
Cambodian and Thai varieties are the fastest colonizers and fruiters, often completing a full grow cycle in 4-6 weeks from inoculation to harvest.
### For Outdoor Cultivation
B+ is the most cold-tolerant and resilient variety for outdoor beds. Amazonian also performs well outdoors in warm climates.
## The Importance of Genetics Over Strain
It is important to understand that strain selection is less important than genetic quality within that strain. A well-isolated, vigorous culture of Golden Teacher will outperform a multi-spore grow of any "premium" strain. Agar isolation (see [[mushroom-agar-work]]) to select for fast, rhizomorphic growth will improve results more than strain shopping.
