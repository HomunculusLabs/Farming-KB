# Hallucinogenic Mushroom Acute Psychological Effects and Bad Trips

## Overview

The acute psychological effects of hallucinogenic mushrooms containing psilocybin and psilocin are highly variable, depending on dose, individual sensitivity, previous experiences, and the setting in which they are consumed. User reported experiences range from profoundly positive to severely distressing, with the negative experiences commonly referred to as "bad trips." Understanding the full spectrum of acute psychological effects is essential for both harm reduction and clinical practice.

## Factors Influencing Effects

Mushroom effects are dependent on multiple interacting variables:

- **Dose** - the quantity and potency of mushrooms consumed
- **Individual reaction and sensitivity** - personal neurochemistry and psychological makeup
- **Previous experiences** - familiarity with psychedelic states affects interpretation
- **Set and setting** - the user's mindset and the physical and social environment
- **Species and variety** - different species contain varying concentrations of active compounds
- **Preparation method** - whether mushrooms are eaten fresh, dried, or brewed as tea

## Positive Subjective Effects

Users describe a range of sought after psychological effects that are typically associated with what they term "good trips":

- **Mild relaxation** - feelings comparable to those produced by cannabis
- **Giddiness and euphoria** - uncontrollable laughter, joy, and elevated mood
- **Energy** - increased physical and mental energy
- **Visual enhancement** - colours appear brighter and more vivid
- **Mental stimulation** - heightened thinking and cognitive engagement
- **New insights** - often about the nature of time, self, and relationships
- **Quickly changing emotions** - described by users as "lots of fun and laughter"

## Perceptual Distortions

The perceptual effects of hallucinogenic mushrooms are more accurately described as distortions than true hallucinations, though the effects extend well beyond simple perception changes:

- **Visual disturbances** - moving surfaces, waves, geometric patterns
- **Altered perception of real events** - reinterpretation of familiar surroundings
- **Altered perception of images and faces** - facial features may appear to change
- **Changes in thought processes** - racing thoughts, unusual associations
- **Altered mood** - rapid and unpredictable emotional shifts
- **Personality integration effects** - changes in self awareness and ego boundaries
- **Impaired judgement of time and distance** - minutes may feel like hours
- **Sense of unreality** - feeling that the world or oneself is not real

## Depersonalisation and Dissociation

Some users experience depersonalisation, a state in which they feel detached from their own body or identity. This can range from a mild sense of floating detachment to profound feelings of being outside oneself. While some experienced users seek these states, they can be extremely frightening for inexperienced users who may interpret them as signs of mental breakdown.

## Bad Trips

Bad trips represent the most clinically significant acute psychological effects of hallucinogenic mushroom use. The term encompasses a range of severely distressing experiences:

### Anxiety and Panic

Anxiety is the most common feature of bad trips. Users may experience escalating anxiety that develops into full panic reactions. A UK clubbing magazine survey conducted in 2005 found that nearly a quarter of those who had used hallucinogenic mushrooms in the last year had experienced a panic attack.

### Confusion and Disorientation

During bad trips, individuals are typically extremely anxious, severely agitated, confused, and disoriented, with impaired concentration and judgement. They may be unable to recognise familiar people or surroundings and may not understand what is happening to them.

### Psychotic Episodes

In serious cases, acute psychotic episodes may occur during bad trips. These episodes can include:

- Bizarre and frightening images
- Severe paranoia
- Total loss of reality
- Delusions of persecution or grandeur
- Visual, auditory, or tactile hallucinations in the true sense

These psychotic episodes can lead to accidents, self injury, or suicide attempts. The risk is elevated when mushrooms are combined with alcohol or other substances.

### Comparison with LSD

Users and clinicians note that flashbacks following hallucinogenic mushroom use do occur, but less frequently than with LSD. These episodes are generally perceptual alterations or pseudo hallucinations rather than full psychotic episodes.

## Post Trip Psychological Effects

A bad trip is usually followed by a period of psychological aftereffects:

- **Faintness** - physical weakness and lightheadedness
- **Sadness and depression** - can persist for hours to days
- **Paranoid interpretations** - lingering suspicious thoughts about the experience
- **Sleep disturbance** - difficulties falling asleep lasting 2 to 6 hours after effects
- **Anxiety about future use** - reluctance or fear about repeating the experience

These symptoms may persist for days, weeks, or even months in some cases. Some of these persistent symptoms may be associated with the concurrent use of other controlled substances.

## Limiting Appeal for Regular Use

The unpredictable nature of mushroom effects serves as a natural barrier to frequent use. Qualitative research in the Netherlands reports that users do not find hallucinogenic mushrooms a particularly pleasant experience compared to other substances. The experience lacks the positive mood altering entactogenic effects that ecstasy provokes, making it less appealing in social situations.

User accounts suggest that hallucinogenic mushrooms may not be viewed in a sufficiently favourable light to repeat the experience or to promote the trend. The negative effects, including nausea, panic attacks, and lack of sociable effects, may all contribute to limiting recreational use.

## Clinical Management

When individuals present to emergency services during bad trips, the primary clinical approach involves:

- Reassurance and emotional support
- Prevention of self harm or harm to others
- Creating a calm, quiet environment
- Administration of benzodiazepines, reported to be the safest medication of choice, effective for most patients

## Fatal Outcomes

While rare, fatal outcomes have been documented. Notable cases include:

- A March 2004 case in Manchester, UK, where a 31 year old man died after leaping from a tower block window after consuming Hawaiian psilocybin containing mushrooms in combination with alcohol
- A 2005 case in Dun Laoghaire, Ireland, where a 33 year old man died after falling from the fourth floor of a building after consuming hallucinogenic mushrooms
- A suicide in the Czech Republic in 2004 where the presence of hallucinogenic mushrooms was detected in the autopsy report

In both the Manchester and Irish cases, coroner inquests confirmed the contributory role of mushrooms, particularly when combined with alcohol.
