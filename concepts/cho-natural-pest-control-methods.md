# Cho's Natural Pest Control Methods

Dr. Cho's Korean Natural Farming approaches pest control through preventive
plant health management and targeted physical interventions. The system
avoids chemical pesticides entirely, relying instead on soil health, balanced
nutrition, and biological methods.

## Philosophy

Cho's pest control rests on several principles:

1.  **Healthy plants resist pests** — Soil managed with
    [[cho-indigenous-microorganisms-collection-and-cultivation]] produces naturally resistant plants.
2.  **Nutrient balance prevents susceptibility** — Over-fertilization or
    imbalance makes plants vulnerable. The [[cho-plant-growth-stage-treatments]] ensures appropriate nutrition at each stage.
3.  **Microbial diversity suppresses pathogens** — A diverse soil
    microbiome prevents any single pathogen from dominating. IMOs produce
    antibiotic substances and enzymes that suppress diseases.
4.  **Minimal intervention preferred** — Physical traps and biological
    methods are used only when necessary.

## Soil Health Foundation

In Cho's "Non-Chemical" approach, farmers nurture the soil rather than
feed plants directly. IMOs perform two critical functions for pest
management:

1.  **Decomposition** — Breaking complex organic compounds into
    absorbable nutrients for vigorous, resistant growth.
2.  **Pathogen suppression** — Producing antibiotic substances, enzymes,
    and lactic acids that suppress diseases.

The [[cho-seed-seedling-solution-treatment]]
applied before planting establishes this protective microbial foundation.

## Method A: Aromatic Insect Attractant (AIA)

A passive trapping method using fermented liquids to lure and drown
moths and butterflies during their egg-laying period.

### Materials and Preparation

-   PET bottle, rice wine, [[cho-fermented-plant-juice-preparation]]
    diluted 300 times, and [[master-cho-knf-ffj-fermented-fruit-juice-guide]] (most
    attractive to insects).

### Installation

1.  Fill a PET bottle with rice wine and diluted FPJ.
2.  Install at the height of target fruits or leaves.
3.  Timing is critical — deploy during egg-laying season before insects
    lay eggs in the field.
4.  For vegetables, hang at plant height. For fruit trees, install 4-6
    bottles per tree.

## Method B: Fluorescent Insect Attractant (FIA)

Uses light to attract nighttime-flying insects to a water trap.

### Materials

-   Zinc sheet (L-shaped), fluorescent light, roofing, basin with water,
    gasoline or kerosene (a few drops to ensure insects drown).

### Construction

1.  Hang fluorescent light vertically on the corner of the zinc sheet.
2.  Add roof to protect from rain; place water basin beneath.
3.  Install at target crop height. Most effective during peak
    reproductive growth of fruit-bearing plants.

## Oriental Herbal Nutrient in Pest Management

[[knf-oriental-herbal-nutrient]] functions as both plant tonic and
pest deterrent. It is used in making IMO-3/IMO-4, soil treatment
solution, seed/seedling treatment, and foliar applications at 1:500 to
1:1000 dilution throughout all growth stages.

## Lactic Acid Bacteria for Disease Suppression

[[bionutrients-lab-lactic-acid-bacteria-serum]]
create a protective microbial coating on plant surfaces. When IMO mixed
with LAB is sprayed on crops, the resulting layer makes plants resistant
to fungal establishment. LAB also helps recover digestive function in
livestock.

## Yeast for Disease Recovery

Yeast is deployed as emergency response when viral or bacterial diseases
occur, plants become weak, branches are damaged by wind, or photosynthesis
is reduced during rainy seasons. Dilute 1:1000 and spray with other
natural farming materials.

## Integration with Growth Stage Treatments

Pest management is integrated into the
[[cho-plant-growth-stage-treatments]]. Each stage
treatment includes inputs that simultaneously support plant health and
provide pest resistance. The crack prevention treatment, for example,
combines OHN, BRV, FPJ, LAB, and seawater to strengthen tissues against
both physiological disorders and pathogen entry.

## Key Takeaways

-   Prevention through soil health and balanced nutrition is primary.
-   Physical trapping (AIA and FIA) provides targeted insect control.
-   All natural farming inputs contribute to pest resistance indirectly.
-   The system avoids the chemical dependency cycle of conventional
    agriculture.
