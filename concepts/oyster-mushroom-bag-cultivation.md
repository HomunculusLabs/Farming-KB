## Advantages

1. Much smaller risk of crop failure compared to other methods
2. Possible inside houses or unused structures
3. Possible with small initial investment
4. Easy to control pests and diseases
5. Quick return of capital
6. Production possible all year round

## Substrate Materials

The substrate is to mushrooms like soil is to plants. Common materials:

- **Sawdust**: oak, poplar, alder, maple, birch, wild cherry, mango, elm
- **Cottonseed hulls**: higher yields than sawdust, but low moisture retention (max 55-58%); must be mixed with other materials
- **Cereal straw**: rich in lignin and cellulose
- **Corncob**, sugar cane bagasse, other high-cellulose plant fibers

Avoid: pine (resins inhibit growth), cedar, redwood (resistant to colonization).

Supplements (nitrogen sources): wheat bran, rice bran, sorghum, millet, corn meal, cottonseed meal, alfalfa meal, soybean meal, canola meal.

Additives: gypsum, limestone, chalk (pH buffers); sugar, molasses.

Typical ratio: substrate to nitrogen supplement = 4:1.

## Sawdust Preparation

- Mix sawdust with water and supplements (manually or mechanically)
- Ensure even water distribution throughout
- Target moisture: 65%
- **Palm test**: squeeze a fistful tightly — a few drops of water = proper content
- Too wet impedes air flow; too low prohibits mycelial growth

**Country examples:**
- Thailand: rubber tree sawdust
- Bangladesh: mango sawdust + wheat bran (4:1)
- America: oak, poplar, alder, maple, birch, wild cherry; supplements at 4:1 ratio
- Korea: oak, poplar, broad-leaf trees; ribbon mixer for mixing

## Straw Preparation

- Cut into 1-2 inch pieces (straw choppers, shredders, garden chippers)
- Soak in water 1-2 hours, rinse 2-3 times in clean water
- Drain excess water for 3-4 hours
- Check moisture with palm test

**Country examples:**
- India: paddy straw, chopped to 3-5cm
- Nepal: rice straw + wheat/rice bran supplement
- Vietnam: soak in limewater (2kg slacked lime per 100kg dry straw), ferment 7-10 days, turn every 3 days
- South Africa: chopped wheat straw + sugarcane bagasse + lime + gypsum; available in round bales (250kg), square bales (350kg), small bales (12kg)
- Zimbabwe: wheat straw, grass, or banana leaves (banana leaves = higher yield but delayed break)

## Filling and Compressing

- Fill prepared substrate into bags before heat treatment (recommended to minimize contamination)
- Alternative: pasteurize/sterilize in bulk, then fill bags
- Compress bags for fast mycelial colonization
- Make 2-3cm diameter center hole for deep inoculation and oxygen supply
- Growers who don't compact don't always need inoculation hole

## Closing the Bags

- Seal with rubber bands, plastic caps, or cotton plugs
- Proper sealing prevents contamination while allowing gas exchange
