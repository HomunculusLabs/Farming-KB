# High-Volume Cannabis Extraction and Advanced Refinement

## Overview

Large-scale cannabis extraction requires industrial-scale apparatus built from 55-gallon oil drums and heavy-duty equipment, capable of processing large quantities of marijuana or [[mushroom-spawn]] including [[cannabis-oil-capsules-preparation]], combined with enforcement pressures, created demand for large-scale extraction equipment. The apparatus described here is built from readily available hardware and surplus store materials, specifically designed to handle the unique problems of high-volume work while incorporating necessary safety features.

### Core Construction: Dual Drum System

The apparatus uses two 55-gallon oil drums in a stacked configuration:

- **Lower drum:** Serves as the boiling vessel where solvent/cannabis solutions are heated via a boiling water bath
- **Upper drum:** Functions as a giant condenser, filled with circulating ice water so that solvent fumes contacting its inner surfaces recondense and fall back into the lower drum

The tops of the connecting copper tubes (except one acting as a pressure relief valve) are closed with rubber stoppers. Solvent fumes also condense inside the tubes themselves, adding to the total condensation surface area.

### Condenser Drum Construction

1. A **four-inch grid** is marked on both the top and bottom surfaces of the oil drum
2. A hole is drilled at each grid intersection
3. Pieces of **one-inch copper tubing** are run through the drum lengthwise, protruding about one inch from each surface
4. **Watertight seals** are soldered or welded around each tube where it passes through the drum surfaces
5. **Inlet and outlet fittings** for circulating ice water coolant are fitted to the top of the barrel
6. **Metal straps** with chain holes are attached to the top for lifting with a small overhead crane

### Lower Drum Preparation

1. The top is removed
2. **Sturdy legs** are attached to the bottom, maintaining clearance above the boiling water bath
3. A **copper drain tube with on/off valve** is attached to the bottom for siphoning the solvent/cannabis solution
4. **Four metal straps** protruding above the drum rim secure the upper drum
5. A **side fitting with on/off valve** is installed approximately halfway between top and bottom

### Soxhlet Chamber

A removable chamber is placed between the falling recondensed solvent drops and the boiling solution. This chamber holds the cannabis material during the soxhlet (wash) phase:

- Many holes are punctured in the bottom with a nail
- A hole is cut in the side slightly higher than the lower drum's side valve
- A plate closes the hole when not in use

### Additional Operating Equipment

1. **Large deep tub** — at least twice the diameter of the drums, for the boiling water bath
2. **Immersion pump and large pail** — for ice water circulation
3. **Long hose** — runs from a copper tube to an empty jug (safety monitoring)
4. **Rubber stoppers** — for sealing unused copper tube tops
5. **Large polyethylene trash bags and inner-tube rubber bands** — for sealing the drum junction
6. **Large funnel** — nearly the same diameter as the drum, for collecting recondensed solvent during distillation
7. **Overhead chain winch or block and tackle** — for lifting heavy components
8. **Three heavy-duty sparkless electric hotplates** — for heating the water bath

## High-Volume Operations

### Sealing and Safety

The junction between upper and lower drums is sealed with a large trash bag (bottom cut out) slipped over the apparatus, positioned at the joining point, and secured with inner-tube rubber bands both above and below. This arrangement functions as a **pressure relief valve** — if internal pressure builds, the plastic puffs outward visibly.

One copper tube is left open with a hose running to a large jug in a safe location. Under normal conditions, no fumes or liquid appear at the hose. If any do, it signals condenser failure — the boiling water must be immediately removed and replaced with ice water.

### Refluxing Procedure

1. Powdered cannabis fills the lower drum to approximately **one-third**
2. Solvent is added to **half-fill** the lower drum
3. The side valve is closed; the soxhlet chamber and funnel are not yet used
4. The upper drum is secured and sealed
5. Ice water circulation begins through the condenser drum
6. The three hotplates heat the water bath to boiling (~212°F)
7. The solvent boils, fumes condense on the copper tubes and condenser surface, and liquid falls back
8. **Refluxing continues for three hours**
9. After cooling (ice water in the tub for at least 30 minutes), the oil-saturated solvent is drained via the siphon pump through the bottom drain tube

### Soxhleting Phase

1. The damp powdered cannabis is returned to the drum with fresh clean solvent
2. The soxhlet chamber (with filter paper fitted to the bottom) is filled with the cannabis
3. Clean solvent is added until the cannabis is saturated and several inches collect in the drum bottom
4. The apparatus is assembled and the water bath brought to a boil
5. Recondensed solvent drips through the cannabis, washing out remaining oil
6. After several hours, all oil is dissolved in the solvent
7. The solution is distilled and collected as before

### Solvent Distillation and Collection

1. A funnel (held by the soxhlet chamber) catches recondensed solvent drops
2. Liquid runs through a hose to the side fitting, then through an external hose to a receiving barrel
3. When all solvent is evaporated, a heavy oil film coats the lower drum bottom
4. The oil is redissolved in a small amount of solvent and removed for storage

All oil solutions from each extraction pass are combined in a large, pre-weighed metal pot for further processing.

## Advanced Refinement Techniques

### Overview

Translucent amber oil from charcoal-filtered, isomerized extraction typically contains 30–60% THC. Modern chemistry techniques can further refine this oil through [[psilocybe-cubensis-strain-potency-variability-controlled-conditions-bigwood-beug]]:

1. **Vacuum reduction:** The oil is heated under reduced pressure created by a vacuum pump, lowering the boiling points of the cannabinoid compounds and preventing thermal degradation
2. **Selective vaporization:** Different cannabinoids vaporize at different temperatures — the desired THC fraction is collected by selecting the appropriate temperature and pressure
3. **Condensation:** Cannabinoid vapors condense back into oil on contact with a cooled surface
4. **Impurity separation:** Many impurities do not vaporize at the selected conditions and remain behind in the heating flask

### Historical Reference: Adams (1940)

The foundational laboratory method for cannabinoid refinement was documented by [[ethanol]] per crock
- Countercurrent transfers: 40 liters withdrawn per transfer, 20 liters retained by the cannabis
- One to two transfers per day
- Most concentrated extract: approximately 2 gm solids per 100 cc
- Temperature never exceeded 50°C; evaporation carried out at 30°C
- Concentrated solution: 23.1 gm solids per 100 cc ethanol; 1 cc represented 4.13 gm hemp

#### Red Oil Production

1. Ethanolic extract was placed in a 1-liter Claisen flask (short, wide neck) filled with glass wool to two-thirds capacity
2. Bath temperature raised gradually from 90°C to 140°C as pressure was slightly diminished
3. Distilled ethanol was discarded; flask refilled to two-thirds
4. Process repeated until 1600 cc of extract had been processed and alcohol removed
5. Temperature raised to 200°C; when ethanol traces ceased, bath lowered to 180°C
6. Pressure reduced to 30 mm Hg for [[cannabis-fractional-distillation-cannabinoids]]

### Pure THC Production

Totally pure THC — a thin transparent oil — can be produced by:

1. **Chemically isolating pure cannabidiol** from the crude extract
2. **Isomerizing the pure [[cbd-to-thc-isomerization-chemistry]]** under controlled conditions
