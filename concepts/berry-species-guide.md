# Berry Species Guide

Detailed profiles for each berry type. For overview, planning, IPM, calendar, and preservation, see [[berry-growing]].

## Strawberries (*Fragaria × ananassa*)

### Types
| Type | Bearing | Pros | Cons |
|------|---------|------|------|
| June-bearing | One large crop (3-4 weeks) | Biggest berries, best for preserving | All-at-once harvest |
| Everbearing | 2-3 crops per season | Extended harvest | Smaller berries |
| Day-neutral | Continuous (summer-frost) | Longest season, great for containers | Smaller berries, shorter lifespan |

### Planting
- **Timing:** Spring (zone 7-8) or early fall. Bareroot crowns at soil level — too deep = rot, too shallow = dry out
- **Spacing:** 12-18 inches in rows 3-4 feet. Matted row (let runners fill) or spaced row (remove runners)
- **Renovation (June-bearing):** After harvest, mow foliage to 1 inch, narrow rows to 12 inches, thin to 5-6 inches, fertilize and water

### Care
- Remove flowers in year 1 to build crown strength
- Mulch with straw in late fall for winter protection; pull back in spring
- Replace plants every 3-4 years
- **Nutrients:** Side-dress with compost in spring and after renovation. [[animal-byproducts]] — blood meal at bloom, bone meal at fruiting
- **Common issues:** Spider mites, slugs, botrytis, verticillium wilt, tarnished plant bug

### Varieties (Zone 7-8)
- June-bearing: Chandler, Earliglow, Honeoye, Allstar
- Everbearing: Ozark Beauty, Quinault
- Day-neutral: Seascape, Albion, Tribute

## Blueberries (*Vaccinium* spp.)

### Types
| Type | Size | Chill Hours | Best For |
|------|------|-------------|----------|
| Highbush (Northern) | 5-8 ft | 800-1000 | Zone 4-7 |
| Southern Highbush | 4-6 ft | 200-500 | Zone 7-10 |
| Rabbiteye | 6-10 ft | 350-700 | Zone 7-9 |
| Lowbush | 6-24 in | 1000+ | Zone 2-6 |

### Planting
- **Timing:** Early spring or fall
- **pH critical:** 4.5-5.5 — amend with elemental sulfur (takes 6-12 months) or peat moss. Never use lime
- **Spacing:** 4-6 ft (highbush), 6-8 ft (rabbiteye)
- **Pollination:** Self-fertile but better with cross-pollination. Plant 2+ varieties
- **Mulch:** 3-6 inches pine needles or pine bark — maintains acidity

### Care
- **Water:** 1-2 inches/week, consistent moisture critical (shallow roots)
- **Pruning:** Late winter — remove dead, diseased, crossing canes. Remove oldest canes (6+ yr). Keep 6-8 canes per plant
- **Fertilizer:** Ammonium sulfate (lowers pH + provides N). Split doses: budbreak, petal fall, early fruit, post-harvest
- **Birds:** Net when fruit colors. Scare devices limited effectiveness
- **Common issues:** Mummy berry, botrytis, SWD, Japanese [[beekeeping-basics]]tles, chlorosis (pH too high)

### Varieties (Zone 7-8)
- Southern Highbush: Legacy, Sunshine Blue, O'Neal, Emerald
- Rabbiteye: Brightwell, Premier, Tifblue, Climax

## Raspberries (*Rubus idaeus*)

### Types
| Type | Bearing | When | Notes |
|------|---------|------|-------|
| Summer-bearing (floricane) | One crop | Mid-summer | 2-year-old canes |
| Fall-bearing (primocane) | Two crops or one | Late summer + fall OR fall only | 1-year canes |

### Planting
- **Timing:** Early spring (bareroot) or fall (potted)
- **Support:** Trellis essential — T-bar or V-trellis, wires at 3-4 ft and 5-6 ft
- **Spacing:** 18-24 inches in rows 8-10 feet

### Care
- **Pruning (summer-bearing):** After harvest, cut fruited canes to ground. Thin primocanes to 4-6/linear ft
- **Pruning (fall-bearing, single crop):** Cut ALL canes to ground in late winter
- **Suckering:** Remove canes outside 12-18 inch hedgerow
- **Common issues:** SWD, anthracnose, cane borers, spider mites, crown rot, viruses (plant certified virus-free)

### Varieties (Zone 7-8)
- Summer: Nova, Prelude, Canby (thornless), Tulameen
- Fall: Heritage, Caroline, Joan J (thornless), Autumn Bliss

## Blackberries (*Rubus* spp.)

### Types
| Type | Growth Habit | Notes |
|------|-------------|-------|
| Erect | Self-supporting | Benefit from trellis |
| Semi-erect | Need trellis | Larger berries, more productive |
| Trailing | Must be trellised | Most productive, least cold-hardy |

### Planting
- **Timing:** Early spring. Spacing: 3-4 ft in rows 8-10 ft
- **Support:** T-wire trellis at 3 ft and 5-6 ft

### Care
- **Pruning:** Same pattern as raspberries. Semi-erect: tip primocanes at 3-4 ft in summer
- **Primocane varieties:** Mow all in winter for simplified management
- **Mulch:** 4-6 inches to suppress weeds and retain moisture
- **Common issues:** SWD, orange rust, cane blight, Japanese beetles, stink bugs, rosette

### Varieties (Zone 7-8)
- Erect (thornless): Navaho, Arapaho, Ouachita
- Semi-erect: Triple Crown, Chester (thornless), Hull
- Primocane: Prime-Ark Freedom, Prime-Ark Traveler

## Elderberries (*Sambucus nigra/canadensis*)

Hardy, productive shrubs (8-12 ft) thriving in partial shade and wet soils. Excellent for [[food-forest-design]]s and hedgerows. Medicinal flowers and berries — high antioxidants and vitamin C.

### Planting
- **Spacing:** 6-10 ft apart. Spring or fall
- **Pollination:** American elders (S. canadensis) partially self-fertile, better with 2 varieties. European (S. nigra) needs cross-pollination
- **Soil:** Adaptable — tolerates wet, clay, poor soils. pH 5.5-6.5

### Care
- **Pruning:** Remove dead + oldest canes (>3 yr) in late winter. Best on 1-3 yr wood
- **Harvest:** Flowers June (cordial, fritters), berries Aug-Sep (syrup, jelly, wine)
- **Propagation:** Softwood/hardwood cuttings, division — see [[seed-saving-techniques]]
- **Caution:** Green parts contain cyanogenic glycosides — cook berries. Avoid red-berried elder (S. racemosa)

### Varieties (Zone 7-8)
- American: Adams, York, Johns, Nova
- European: Haschberg, Samdal, Marge

## Gooseberries and Currants (*Ribes* spp.)

Cold-hardy shrubs (3-5 ft) tolerating partial shade and poor soils. **Check local regulations** — banned in some states (white pine blister rust host). See [[homesteading-legal-considerations]].

### Planting
- **Spacing:** 3-5 ft in rows 6-8 ft. Early spring. pH 5.5-7.0
- **Care:** Keep 6-8 main stems, remove oldest (>4 yr) in winter. Mulch 3-4 inches
- **Common issues:** Powdery mildew, sawfly, currant worm
- **Harvest:** Gooseberries — underripe for pies, ripe for fresh. Currants — full color

### Varieties (Zone 7-8)
- Gooseberries: Invicta, Hinnonmaki Red/Yellow, Pixwell
- Black currants: Consort, Crandall, Titania
- Red currants: Red Lake, Cherry, Rovada

## Grapes (*Vitis* spp.)

Vigorous perennial vines for fences, arbors, and food forest canopy gaps. Wine, juice, jelly, raisins, fresh eating.

### Planting
- **Spacing:** 6-8 ft in rows 8-10 ft. Early spring
- **Support:** Sturdy trellis — end posts need anchoring. Kniffen system (2 wires), high cordon, or umbrella Kniffen
- **Soil:** Deep, well-drained, pH 5.5-6.5. Drought-tolerant once established
- **Pollination:** Self-fertile

### Care
- **Training:** Main trunk to wire year 1-2, cordons/spurs year 3+
- **Pruning:** CRITICAL — fruit on 1-year-old wood. 40-60 buds/vine in late winter. Spur (2-3 buds) or cane prune (8-12 buds)
- **Canopy management:** Summer shoot thinning, positioning, leaf removal around clusters
- **Common issues:** Japanese beetles, grape berry moth, black rot, powdery/downy mildew, phylloxera, birds

### Varieties (Zone 7-8)
- Table: Concord, Niagara, Reliance, Himrod (seedless)
- Wine: Chambourcin, Norton (Cynthiana), Vidal Blanc, Traminette

## Hardy Kiwi (*Actinidia arguta*)

Grape-sized, smooth-skinned kiwifruit. Hardy to -25°F. Dioecious — need male + female (1:8 ratio). Excellent for pergolas and fences.

### Planting
- **Spacing:** 8-15 ft apart. Spring after frost. VERY vigorous
- **Support:** Strong trellis/arbor (6-7 ft T-bar). Male + female (1:8 ratio)
- **Soil:** Well-drained, fertile, pH 5.5-7.0

### Care
- **Pruning:** Winter — remove dead, thin crowded. Summer — tip-prune vigorous shoots. Fruit on current season from 1-yr canes
- **Harvest:** Late Sept-Oct. Pick when soft — doesn't ripen well off vine
- **Caution:** EXTREMELY invasive in some regions — check local lists
