# Cannabidiol (CBD)

Cannabidiol (CBD) is a non-psychoactive cannabinoid sourced from cannabis. It has emerged as one of the most therapeutically promising compounds in the [[cannabis-and-multiple-sclerosis]], where CBD has demonstrated the ability to reduce neuroinflammation and oxidative stress in neural tissues.

### ALS and Huntington's Disease

CBD shows therapeutic potential in amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS, Lou Gehrig's disease) and Huntington's disease, both through neuroprotective mechanisms and through modulation of excitotoxicity and neuronal death pathways. In ALS models, CBD combined with THC has shown promise in delaying disease progression and extending survival. For Huntington's disease, CBD's antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties may help mitigate the neurodegenerative cascade caused by mutant huntingtin protein accumulation.

## Psychological and Psychiatric Applications

CBD's therapeutic potential in psychological disorders is based on three primary pharmacological effects:

- **Antipsychotic**: CBD is able to reduce symptoms of acute paranoid schizophrenia, performing as effectively as the pharmaceutical drug Amisulpride, but without Amisulpride's significant adverse side effects
- **Anxiolytic**: CBD calms autonomic responses to stress, including rapid heart rates, by engaging receptors that selectively enhance serotonin signaling to achieve a calming effect
- **Antidepressant**: CBD has demonstrated measurable antidepressant effects in preclinical studies, likely mediated through both endocannabinoid and serotonergic pathways

The combination of antipsychotic, anxiolytic, and antidepressant properties makes CBD unique among therapeutic compounds, as most pharmaceutical agents target only one of these domains. Patients with treatment-resistant anxiety and PTSD have reported significant improvements with CBD therapy, often at doses much lower than those used for seizure management.

## Anti-Inflammatory and Immune Modulation

CBD has been shown to have a clear and measurable therapeutic impact on inflammatory and anti-inflammatory regulation mechanisms. Documented applications include:

- **[[blesching-cannabis-rheumatoid-arthritis]] and osteoarthritis respond to CBD's anti-inflammatory action, with reductions in joint swelling and pain
- **Periodontitis**: CBD reduces inflammatory markers in gum disease
- **Atherosclerosis**: CBD may slow the progression of arterial plaque formation through anti-inflammatory pathways
- **Diabetes-related inflammation**: CBD may reduce pancreatic islet inflammation and protect against diabetic complications
- **Multiple sclerosis**: CBD reduces spasticity and inflammatory damage in MS models

The anti-inflammatory mechanism operates through multiple pathways: CB2 receptor activation, suppression of pro-inflammatory cytokines (including TNF-alpha, IL-1 beta, and IL-6), reduction of reactive oxygen species, and modulation of immune cell migration and proliferation. This multi-pathway approach means CBD may be effective where single-mechanism anti-inflammatory drugs fail, particularly in chronic inflammatory conditions where multiple inflammatory cascades are simultaneously active.

## Anti-Cancer Properties

As regards cancer, CBD is able to produce significant anti-tumor activity both in vitro and in vivo. Key findings include:

- **Selective oxidative stress**: CBD produces oxidative stress selectively in cancer cells, triggering apoptosis (programmed cell death) without impacting normal, healthy cells
- **Anti-proliferative effects**: CBD inhibits the growth and spread of various cancer cell lines including breast, lung, prostate, and colon cancers
- **Angiogenesis inhibition**: CBD may prevent the formation of new blood vessels that feed tumors
- **Metastasis suppression**: Some studies suggest CBD can reduce the migration and invasion of cancer cells to distant sites
- **Synergy with conventional treatment**: CBD may enhance the efficacy of radiation therapy and certain chemotherapy agents while reducing their side effects

The selectivity of CBD's anti-cancer action is particularly noteworthy — it appears to distinguish between malignant and healthy cells, targeting only the former. This selectivity is a significant advantage over many conventional chemotherapy agents that damage both cancerous and healthy tissue indiscriminately.
