# Bjerkandera and Pleurotus Species in Pollutant Degradation

## Bjerkandera in PAH Degradation

Bjerkandera sp. strain BOS55 can oxidize a variety of PAHs including
benzo[a]pyrene. The fungus was able to metabolize three- and four-ring
PAHs in solvent extracts of polluted soil. High-molecular-weight PAHs
(five and six aromatic rings) were poorly degraded, mainly because the
water-miscible solvent acetone used to solubilize PAHs was toxic to the
fungal cells at high concentrations. This limitation highlights the
importance of [[bioavailability-in-fungal-bioremediation]] bioremediation.

Non-ionic surfactants such as Tween 80 increase the bioavailability of
PAHs for Bjerkandera sp. BOS55, enhancing solubility and increasing
oxidation of anthracene, pyrene, and benzo[a]pyrene by ligninolytic
enzymes up to fivefold. Bjerkandera sp. BOS55 combined with indigenous
bacteria from activated sludge or forest soil showed stimulation in the
breakdown of benzo[a]pyrene and a significant decrease in its mutagenicity.

## Pleurotus ostreatus: The Oyster Mushroom

Pleurotus ostreatus has been shown to be an efficient PAH degrader with
considerable practical potential. Unlike P. chrysosporium, P. ostreatus
does not produce LiP activity, but its abilities to degrade lignin and
PAHs are linked with laccase and MnP activities. The fungus grows well in
soil and can compete with indigenous soil bacteria, making it suitable
for practical soil remediation applications.

Mycelia on solid substrates such as straw and spent mushroom waste can be
used as economical sources of inoculum for P. ostreatus. The fungus has
demonstrated the ability to degrade 80% of total PAHs in soil within 35
days. In aged creosote-contaminated soil, P. ostreatus degraded
approximately 40% of the benzo[a]pyrene present after 12 weeks of
incubation.

## Dual Enzymatic Pathways in Pleurotus

P. ostreatus possesses multiple [[fungal-pah-degradation-pathways]] for PAH metabolism.
Cytochrome P450 monooxygenase and epoxide hydrolase are responsible for
initial enzymic attack, while free radical attack by extracellular
oxidative enzymes occurs to form quinones. Phenanthrene is metabolized to
trans-9,10-dihydrodiol and 2,2'-diphenic acid under non-ligninolytic
conditions. Oxygen-18 incorporation experiments indicate that P.
ostreatus initially oxidizes phenanthrene stereoselectively, using
cytochrome P450 and epoxide hydrolase to form dihydrodiols predominantly
as the (9R,10R) enantiomer.
