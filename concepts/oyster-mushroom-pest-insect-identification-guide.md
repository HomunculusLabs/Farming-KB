Identification guide for the five major fly species and two mite types that are the primary pests of [[cotter-oyster-mushroom-cultivation]]. Oyster mushroom cultivation beds provide ideal conditions for pests: abundant food, warm temperatures, and high humidity.

## Sciarids (Lycoriella mali)

The most important pests of oyster mushroom.

- **Adults**: ~2mm long with long thread-like antennae
- **Larvae**: 6-12mm long with distinct black head capsule
- **Damage**: larvae feed on mycelia, pin-heads, and large mushrooms; cause cuts in mycelium, reduced primordia formation, cavities in stipes and caps
- **Adults**: spread diseases and mites between bags/beds
- **Reproduction**: females lay 100-130 eggs at a time on cultivation beds; eggs hatch after 4-5 days at 20°C
- **Temperature sensitivity**: growth delayed or poor below 15°C or above 30°C
- **Control**: most critical to control during spawn run (early flies cause peak populations later in crop cycle)

## Scaptosids (Coboldia fuscipes)

- **Occurrence**: mainly during summer crop cultivation
- **Damage**: larvae feed on mycelium causing substrate rotting and yield loss
- **Vector**: both adults and larvae transfer mites and diseases
- **Temperature**: larvae grow fast above 25°C; development much slower below 20°C
- **Seasonal pattern**: favored by high temperatures during summer cultivation

## Cecids (Mycophila sp.)

- **Adults**: very small, less than 1mm — difficult to see inside growing rooms
- **Larvae**: 1-3mm, orange-colored; suck nutrients from hyphae and attack stipes and caps
- **Rapid reproduction**: reproduce by paedogenesis — each larva releases 14-20 daughter larvae every 6 days
- **Visual indicator**: mushroom bags or beds turn orange when huge numbers of larvae are present
- **Disease vector**: larvae transfer various bacteria causing mushroom breakdown
- **Control difficulty**: extremely rapid population growth makes early detection essential

## Phorids (Megaselia tamiladuensis)

- **Adults**: 2-4mm; move quickly by hopping on substrate
- **Larvae**: 4-6mm long, white and transparent body, no distinct black head
- **Damage**: larvae feed on mycelia and create cavities in fruiting bodies
- **Season**: usually occur during summer cultivation
- **Severity**: normally cause less damage than other fly species

## Mycetophils (Mycetophila sp.)

- **Adults**: large and yellowish
- **Larvae**: 15-20mm long, grayish-brown; construct cocoons with threads on substrates or mushrooms
- **Damage**: young fruiting bodies turn brown and stop growing; large cavities in stipes
- **Identification**: distinctive cocoon structures visible on substrate

## Mites (Tarsonemus sp. and Histiostoma sp.)

- **Classification**: belong to class Arachnida (not Insecta)
- **Size**: small and invisible to the naked eye
- **Damage**: feed on mycelia and fruiting bodies; yield loss and quality decrease
- **Vector**: carry pathogens and nematodes
- **Human impact**: can cause itchy rashes among growers
- **Control**: thorough pasteurization is very important for mite control

## General Control Measures

- Sanitation and hygiene are the most important control methods
- Clean and disinfect mushroom houses thoroughly before cultivation
- Remove waste, weeds, mushroom debris, and water containers inside/outside houses
- Exclude flies with mesh apertures not greater than 0.5-0.6mm on air inlets
- Keep doors closed during spawning and mycelium growth phase
- Maintain low fly population during spawn run — early flies cause peak infestations later
- Pasteurize substrates thoroughly (especially for mite control)
- Burning mosquito coils is effective against adult flies inside houses
- Remove mushroom stumps shortly after harvest to eliminate breeding sites
- Do not dump waste near mushroom houses (attracts flies)
