# Erosion and Land Deterioration

In [[savory-ecosystem-foundation-blocks]]. Savory argues that the standard approach to erosion
control, which typically involves mechanical earthworks and structural
interventions, addresses symptoms rather than causes. True [[permplant-willows-salix-erosion-control-fodder]]
requires restoring the biological processes that maintain soil stability.

## Erosion as Symptom, Not Cause

The conventional approach to erosion has been to build check dams,
contour banks, and other structures to slow water flow and catch sediment.
While these measures may provide temporary relief, they do not address the
underlying cause. Savory observed that erosion continued to advance on
ranches that had received extensive mechanical erosion control treatments
because the biological health of the land continued to decline.

Erosion is the result of an ineffective [[savory-brittle-environments]].

conditions for both water and wind erosion that no structural intervention
can fully address.

## Riparian Erosion

Stream banks and riparian areas are particularly susceptible to erosion.
Where livestock concentrate at water points without planned movement,
trailing creates paths that wash out during rains. Steep stream banks
erode when vegetation is lost.

However, properly managed livestock can cure the very erosion they might
cause. Very high [[savory-land-monitoring]] should track erosion indicators
including gully formation, soil deposition patterns, exposed roots, pedestal
plants, rill formation, and changes in stream bank stability. Photographic
records at fixed points provide the most accessible means of tracking
erosion trends over time.
