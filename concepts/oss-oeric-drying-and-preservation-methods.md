# Oss & Oeric: Drying and Preservation Methods

The 1976 guide provides specific instructions for harvesting,
drying, and preserving [[psilocybe-cubensis]] mushrooms to maintain
maximum potency over extended storage periods. Proper preservation
is essential because the active compounds, particularly psilocybin,
are susceptible to degradation when exposed to heat, light, moisture,
and oxygen over time. The authors tested and refined their methods
through practical experience.

## Harvest Timing

Mushrooms are ready for harvest when the cap enlarges and becomes
more globular in shape. The key indicator is veil rupture: as the
cap enlarges, the partial veil connecting the cap margin to the
stipe detaches to form an annulus (ring) on the stem. Harvesting
should occur as soon as veil rupture is observed. If a spore print
is desired, the mushroom should be allowed to mature further until
the cap flattens to an umbrella shape.

The authors emphasize grasping the stalks firmly when harvesting
and removing soil from the bottom of the stems before drying.

## Drying Methods

**Gas Oven Drying (Recommended):** The preferred method is drying
in a gas oven at low heat (140 degrees F or less) for 6-10 hours.
The low temperature is sufficient to remove moisture without
significantly degrading the active alkaloids.

**Alternative Methods:** Mushrooms may also be dried under a heat
lamp, or placed on a screen and dried over a heating vent. Any
method that provides gentle, consistent warmth and good air
circulation is suitable. The key is to avoid high temperatures
that could destroy the active compounds.

## Dryness Testing

Mushrooms are considered fully dried when they are "hard to the
touch, like crackers, with no spongy feel at all." Any residual
sponginess indicates incomplete drying, which will lead to
degradation during storage. Incomplete drying is the most common
cause of potency loss. The internal tissue should snap cleanly
when bent, rather than folding or bending.

## Packaging and Storage

The authors recommend a multi-layer packaging approach for maximum
preservation. Dried mushrooms are sealed in 5-gram increments in
plastic bags from which the air has been withdrawn (heat-sealed).
These bags are then placed inside a tightly sealed glass vessel or
other moisture-proof container, which is frozen for long-term
storage.

This triple barrier (plastic bag, glass container, frozen
environment) protects against moisture reabsorption, oxidation,
and thermal degradation. The 5-gram portions correspond
approximately to a single dose, allowing convenient use without
repeated exposure of the entire stock to temperature fluctuations.

## Critical Warnings

**Air Exposure:** Dried mushrooms left in the open air quickly
lose their potency. Even brief exposure to ambient humidity can
begin the degradation process. The authors stress that mushrooms
must be sealed promptly after drying is confirmed complete.

**Freezing Fresh Mushrooms:** Fresh mushrooms should never be
frozen without drying first. Freezing fresh mushrooms "will turn
them into a black, gooey mess" as the water content expands and
ruptures cellular structures, destroying both the physical
integrity and the active compounds.

**Refrigerator Storage of Fresh Mushrooms:** Fresh mushrooms can
be preserved in a plastic bag in the vegetable compartment of a
refrigerator for approximately one to two weeks. Fresh mushrooms
older than this should either be eaten or dried to prevent
spoilage. The vegetable crisper provides adequate humidity to
prevent desiccation while the cool temperature slows degradation.

## Long-Term Stability

The authors note that when properly dried and stored, mushrooms
can retain potency for years or even decades. The key factors are
complete desiccation, airtight packaging, and cold or frozen
storage. Each of these factors independently contributes to
preservation, and the combination provides maximum protection.
Regular inspection of stored mushrooms for any signs of moisture
absorption or mold growth is advisable.

## Potency Considerations

The dried mushrooms contain 0.2 to 0.4 percent psilocybin by
weight, with some strains reported to contain up to 0.5 percent.
Psilocin is present only in trace amounts in dried specimens.
Fresh mushrooms are reported to be somewhat stronger than dried
ones, likely due to the presence of psilocin which degrades more
rapidly during the drying process than the more stable psilocybin.

The authors note that psilocybin is one of the least toxic of all
hallucinogens, with an LD50 (lethal dose for 50% of test
subjects) in mice of 280 mg/kg of body weight, compared to a
minimum effective dose of approximately 10 mg for humans. This
wide therapeutic index means that the margin of safety between
an effective and a lethal dose is approximately 28-fold, which
is considered favorable compared to many pharmaceutical agents.

## Comparison of Preservation Methods

The gas oven method recommended by the guide is effective but
has been largely superseded by food dehydrators, which provide
more consistent temperature control. Desiccant-based drying
(using silica gel or DampRid in sealed containers) is another
modern approach that works well for small quantities and avoids
any heat exposure. All methods share the same endpoint: the
mushroom must be thoroughly desiccated to preserve potency.
