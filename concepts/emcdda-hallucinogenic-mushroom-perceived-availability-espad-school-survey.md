# ESPAD School Survey: Perceived Availability of Magic Mushrooms

The European School Survey Project on Alcohol and Other Drugs (ESPAD) provides standardized data on substance use and perceived availability among 15 to 16 year old school students across EU Member States and candidate countries. The 2003 ESPAD survey, conducted in 22 EU Member States plus Norway and four candidate countries (Croatia, Bulgaria, Turkey, and Romania), included questions about perceived ease of obtaining hallucinogenic mushrooms.

## Perceived Availability Rates

The ESPAD surveys reported that rates of students who perceived hallucinogenic mushrooms as "very" or "fairly easy" to obtain ranged from 4% to 28% across participating countries. This wide variation reflects differences in actual market availability, legal frameworks, [[hallucinogenic-mushroom-markets]].

### High Availability Countries (Over 20%)

Five countries reported perceived availability above 20% among 15 to 16 year old students:

- **Czech Republic**: Consistently one of the highest prevalence and availability countries in the EU, reflecting a well-established market and cultural acceptance of hallucinogenic mushrooms.
- **Ireland**: Despite later prohibition, perceived availability was high during the survey period, consistent with reports of seasonal wild mushroom consumption in regions like Kilkenny.
- **Italy**: Substantial perceived access among adolescents, though actual prevalence data suggested more moderate use.
- **Poland**: High availability consistent with reports of young people actively hunting wild [[cotter-mushroom-products-and-value-added-applications]].

### The Netherlands Anomaly

The Netherlands presented a notable anomaly. Despite the absence of legal sanctions controlling the sale of fresh hallucinogenic mushrooms (prior to the 2002 Supreme Court ruling on prepared products), only 16% of Dutch school students reported easy access to hallucinogenic mushrooms. This gender gap was particularly pronounced among students who had used other hallucinogens or ecstasy. Among male students who had used LSD or other hallucinogens, lifetime prevalence of hallucinogenic mushroom use exceeded 40%, while the corresponding figure for females was somewhat lower. The gender disparity was less pronounced among students who had only used cannabis, suggesting that the factors driving male-female differences in hallucinogenic mushroom use were related to the broader pattern of gendered drug experimentation rather than to mushroom-specific factors.

## Foraging as an Availability Channel

Perceived availability was not solely a function of retail market presence. In countries where hallucinogenic mushrooms grew wild, foraging represented an independent availability channel that could inflate perceived access even in the absence of formal retail markets.
