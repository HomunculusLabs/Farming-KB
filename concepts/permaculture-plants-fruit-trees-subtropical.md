# Permaculture Fruit Trees for Subtropical Climates

Subtropical fruit trees bridge the gap between temperate and tropical species, often offering exceptional nutritional value, medicinal properties, and productivity. Many are evergreen, providing year-round canopy cover and multiple harvests. This page covers the key subtropical species from the source.

## Cherimoyas and Custard Apples (Annona spp)

Family: ANNONACEAE

### Cherimoya (A. cherimola)
Andean native, semi-evergreen tree to 9 m. Said to be the best of the related species but only fruits under specific conditions -- does best in warm areas outside the tropics, needs high humidity during flowering. Young trees susceptible to frost; older trees tolerant to about -3C. Prefers deep, fertile, light-textured soil. Hand pollination common.

Fruit is green, variable in size, flesh is white with flavour between pineapple and strawberry. Ripens autumn to early winter. First crop at 3 years (grafted), 4 years (seedling).

Nutritional per 100g: Water 73.5%, Energy 94 cal, Carbohydrate 24.0g, Protein 1.3g, Vitamin A 10 IU, Ascorbic acid 9mg.

### Custard Apple (A. atemoya)
Also called bullock's heart. Deciduous, to 8-10 m. Susceptible to wind damage, will not grow on poorly drained soils. Fruit yellow becoming red or brown, flesh yellow-white, more solid than other annonaceous fruits.

### Sugar Apple (A. squamosa)
Slow-growing, bushy, deciduous tree to 5 m. [[mollison-designers-legume-shade-tree-pasture-systems]]. Less frost tolerant (0C). L pulchella and [[mollison-designers-bee-forage-tree-planting]]. Propagated from seed or cuttings.

Nutritional per 100g: Water 82.7-87.8%, Carbohydrate 10.3g, Protein 1.5g, Vitamin A 540 IU, Ascorbic acid 23.3-33.9mg.

## Passionfruit (Passiflora spp)

Family: PASSIFLORACEAE

More than 40 edible species, mostly tropical to subtropical, woody fruiting vines climbing by tendrils. Can inhibit supporting tree growth by reducing sunlight -- provide a clump of trees so the vine can spread. Fruit falls to ground when ripe.

### Purple Passionfruit (P. edulis)
By far the best known, native to Brazil. Subtropical, fairly densely growing, evergreen vine. Prefers moist, well-drained, fertile, neutral to slightly alkaline soil. Withstands occasional light frost.

### Other notable species:
- **P. ligularis** (sweet granadilla)
- **P. quadrangularis** (giant granadilla) -- largest of the passionfruit
- **P. mollissima** (banana passionfruit) -- more cold tolerant

## Acerola / Barbados Cherry (Malpighia glabra)

Family: MALPIGHIACEAE

Also called West Indian cherry, native cherry. Very attractive, slow-growing, evergreen shrub to ~6 m with glossy dark green leaves. Very tough and versatile -- grows in any soil under almost any conditions. Prefers moist soil and responds to mulching. Does well on limestone, marl, and clay if well-drained. Moderately drought and salt spray tolerant. Frost hardy to -2C.

Fruit cherry-like, 1.25-2.5 cm diameter, red to crimson. Extremely high in Vitamins C and A (4,500 mg ascorbic acid in green fruit, 2,000 mg in ripe). Used fresh, in drinks, and dried. Wine retains 60% of ascorbic acid. Good [[permaculture-plants-fruit-trees-selection]]
