# Permaculture Fruit Trees for Subtropical Climates

Subtropical fruit trees bridge the gap between temperate and tropical species, often offering exceptional nutritional value, medicinal properties, and productivity. Many are evergreen, providing year-round canopy cover and multiple harvests. This page covers the key subtropical species from the source.

## Cherimoyas and Custard Apples (Annona spp)

Family: ANNONACEAE

### Cherimoya (A. cherimola)
Andean native, semi-evergreen tree to 9 m. Said to be the best of the related species but only fruits under specific conditions -- does best in warm areas outside the tropics, needs high humidity during flowering. Young trees susceptible to frost; older trees tolerant to about -3C. Prefers deep, fertile, light-textured soil. Hand pollination common.

Fruit is green, variable in size, flesh is white with flavour between pineapple and strawberry. Ripens autumn to early winter. First crop at 3 years (grafted), 4 years (seedling).

Nutritional per 100g: Water 73.5%, Energy 94 cal, Carbohydrate 24.0g, Protein 1.3g, Vitamin A 10 IU, Ascorbic acid 9mg.

### Custard Apple (A. atemoya)
Also called bullock's heart. Deciduous, to 8-10 m. Susceptible to wind damage, will not grow on poorly drained soils. Fruit yellow becoming red or brown, flesh yellow-white, more solid than other annonaceous fruits.

### Sugar Apple (A. squamosa)
Slow-growing, bushy, deciduous tree to 5 m. Drought tolerant. Fruit yellow-green with pale yellow, firm, sweet custard-like flesh. Fruiting at 3 years (seedling), 1 year (grafted). Very productive.

### Soursop (A. muricata)
Small upright tree to 5 m. Drought tolerant, makes a good windbreak/shade tree. Less frost tolerant (0C). L[[macadamia]] the custard apples, more acid -- often best stewed. Juice abundant and sweet, makes good drinks. Seeds have insecticidal properties.

Nutritional per 100g: Water 81.7%, Energy 65 cal, Carbohydrate 16.3g, Ascorbic acid 20mg.

## Macadamia (Macadamia spp)

Family: PROTEACEAE

Small to medium, long-lived, evergreen tree. Two main species:

### M. integrifolia (Smooth-shelled)
### M. tetraphylla (Rough-shelled)

Native to Australian subtropical rainforests. Requires rainfall 1000-3000 mm and protection from frost when young. Mature trees tolerate light frost. Prefers well-drained, slightly acidic, organically rich soil. Trees begin bearing at 4-7 years from seed, sooner from grafted trees.

The nuts are highly nutritious, with high oil content (65-75% in kernel), and command premium prices. Excellent companion to [[acacia]] pulchella and [[permaculture-tagasaste]].

## Guavas (Psidium spp)

Family: MYRTACEAE

### Common Guava (P. guajava)
Small, branching, evergreen Mexican native tree, 2-10 m. Susceptible to frost (tolerant to -2C) but recovers by suckering. Does best in high rainfall but tolerates 4-5 months of drought. Also tolerant of flooding and somewhat salt tolerant. Avoids heavy clay soils.

Fruit highly scented, yellow when ripe. Contains 2-5x the Vitamin C of fresh orange juice and good Vitamin A. Best eaten fresh, also made into jams, jelly, paste, juice, nectar. Seedling trees bear at about 2 years. Minimum spacing 3 m.

Nutritional per 100g: Water 83%, Energy 62 cal, Carbohydrate 15g, Ascorbic acid 242mg, Vitamin A 280 IU.

Medicinally: Roots, bark, leaves, and immature fruits used for gastroenteritis, diarrhoea, dysentery. Crushed leaves applied to wounds, ulcers, rheumatism. Leaves chewed for toothache. Leaf decoction for coughs, throat/chest ailments, mouth ulcers, vermifuge, and promoting menstrual flow.

### Strawberry Guava (P. cattleianum)
Compact evergreen shrub 3-6 m. Fruit small (~2 cm), deep wine-red (also yellow variety). Juicy, strawberry-like texture and flavour. Hardier than common guava (to -5C) but less drought and flood tolerant.

### Brazilian Guava (P. guineense)
Slow-growing, evergreen 1-7 m. Widest natural distribution of all guavas (southern Mexico to northern Argentina). Fruit lacks musky aroma of common guava, said to make the best jelly of all guavas. Bark rich in tannin, used for tanning. Bark/root decoction for urinary diseases, diarrhoea, varicose veins.

## Lillipillis and Related Myrtaceous Fruits

### Lillipilli (Eugenia smithii / Acmena smithii)
Most widely distributed Australian Eugenia. Medium to tall tree (up to 30 m). Purplish or white fruit, edible though not outstanding in flavour. Bears large quantities over long period of autumn and winter. Potential as pig and poultry food. Good addition to fruit forests, especially those designed for schools and children.

### Brazil Cherry / Surinam Cherry (Eugenia uniflora)
South American evergreen shrub to small tree (up to 3.5 m). Useful fruiting hedge. Tolerant of drought, shade, wind, salt spray, and frosts to -2C. Often bears two crops per year in warmer climates. Fruit ripens a few weeks after flowering. Bark rich in tannin; in Brazil, leaves spread on floors release aroma that repels flies. Seeds are toxic.

### Grumichama (Eugenia dombeyi)
Evergreen, slender, erect tree to 10.5 m from coastal southern Brazil. Withstands temperatures to -3C. Very slow growing. Fruits at 4-5 years. Fruit deep crimson to almost black, 1.25-2 cm wide, with soft melting flesh.

### Rose Apple (Syzygium jambos)
Attractive evergreen tree native to East Indies and Malaysia, to 13 m. Frost and drought tender. Pale yellow, crisp, sweet fruits with distinctive rose flavour. Distilled 4 times to make first-grade rose water.

### Java Plum (S. cumini)
Fast-growing evergreen from Indonesia and northern Queensland, 5-30 m. Frost tender, drought tolerant when established. Purple-black, olive-shaped, somewhat astringent fruits eaten raw and in jams, jellies, preserves, liquors, vinegar, pies. Wine resembling port made from the fruit.

## Sapodilla (Manilkara zapota)

Family: SAPOTACEAE

Also called sapota, Santo Domingo apricot, naseberry, nispero, chiku. Large, slow-growing, attractively shaped, evergreen tree, 5-18 m. Good shade or windbreak species. Fruits at about 8 years (seed) or 3-4 years (graft/cutting). Prefers moist, well-drained soil but adaptable. Drought tolerant, reasonably tolerant of salt spray, flooding, and wind. Mature trees tolerate temperatures to -4.5C.

Fruit produced in pairs, resembles quince, russet-brown when ripe. Must be picked fully ripe -- immature fruits contain tannin and milky inedible juice. Honey-coloured pulp is juicy, tender, very sweet.

Nutritional per 100g: Water 76.1%, Energy 89 cal, Carbohydrate 21.8g, Fat 1.1g, Ascorbic acid 14mg.

Produces latex (chicle) by tapping the bark -- commercial basis for chewing gum. Good strong, durable, termite-resistant timber used for furniture, banisters, cabinet making, railway sleepers, flooring, tool handles. Some Mayan temple lintels are still intact.

## White Sapote (Casimiroa edulis)

Family: RUTACEAE

Originating in Mexico, not related to the sapotes but belongs to the citrus family. Tall evergreen tree, 10-15 m. Subtropical but grows surprisingly well in cooler regions. Moderately drought and wind tolerant. Mature trees frost tolerant to -4C.

Fruit greenish-yellow when ripe, sweet, sometimes slightly resinous. Best eaten fresh, also made into jams, jellies, drinks. Fruits at about 3 years (grafted), 3-8 years (seed). Ripens mainly early autumn. Good bee forage. Minimum spacing 6 m.

Nutritional per 100g: Water 78.3%, Carbohydrate 19.7g, Ascorbic acid 30.3mg.

Medicinal: Fruit claimed to induce sleep; bark/leaf/seed extracts used as tranquillisers and sedatives in Mexico. Effective in lowering blood pressure. Fruit eaten to relieve rheumatism/arthritis pain. In Costa Rica, leaf decoction treats diabetes.

## [[tamarillo]] (Cyphomandra betacea)

Family: SOLANACEAE

Also called tree tomato. Native to temperate Andes of South America. Suited to small gardens in tropical and subtropical climates; grows in cooler areas if sunny and sheltered. Erect, shrubby, fast-growing evergreen to 4 m. Fairly short-lived. Needs rich, moist, well-drained soil; very shallow-rooted so heavy mulching recommended. Slightly frost tolerant (to -2C).

Fruits egg-shaped, ~5 cm long, yellow, red, or dark red. Yellow fruits sweetest, red more acid. Best eaten fresh, also made into jams, jellies, preserves. Skin easily removed by dipping in hot water. Some value as bee forage. Propagated from seed or cuttings.

Nutritional per 100g: Water 82.7-87.8%, Carbohydrate 10.3g, Protein 1.5g, Vitamin A 540 IU, Ascorbic acid 23.3-33.9mg.

## Passionfruit (Passiflora spp)

Family: PASSIFLORACEAE

More than 40 edible species, mostly tropical to subtropical, woody fruiting vines climbing by tendrils. Can inhibit supporting tree growth by reducing sunlight -- provide a clump of trees so the vine can spread. Fruit falls to ground when ripe.

### Purple Passionfruit (P. edulis)
By far the best known, native to Brazil. Subtropical, fairly densely growing, evergreen vine. Prefers moist, well-drained, fertile, neutral to slightly alkaline soil. Withstands occasional light frost.

### Other notable species:
- **P. ligularis** (sweet granadilla)
- **P. quadrangularis** (giant granadilla) -- largest of the passionfruit
- **P. mollissima** (banana passionfruit) -- more cold tolerant

## Acerola / Barbados Cherry (Malpighia glabra)

Family: MALPIGHIACEAE

Also called West Indian cherry, native cherry. Very attractive, slow-growing, evergreen shrub to ~6 m with glossy dark green leaves. Very tough and versatile -- grows in any soil under almost any conditions. Prefers moist soil and responds to mulching. Does well on limestone, marl, and clay if well-drained. Moderately drought and salt spray tolerant. Frost hardy to -2C.

Fruit cherry-like, 1.25-2.5 cm diameter, red to crimson. Extremely high in Vitamins C and A (4,500 mg ascorbic acid in green fruit, 2,000 mg in ripe). Used fresh, in drinks, and dried. Wine retains 60% of ascorbic acid. Good animal forage and hedge/pioneer.

Nutritional per 100g: Vitamin A 408-1000 IU, Ascorbic acid 2,000-4,500mg (varies by ripeness).

Bark contains 20-25% tannin. Fruit gargled for sore throat. Beneficial for coughs, colds, diarrhoea, liver complaints. Leaves have tiny stinging hairs.
