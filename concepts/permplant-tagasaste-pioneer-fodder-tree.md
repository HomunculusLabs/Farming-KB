## Tagasaste as Pioneer Fodder Tree in Permaculture

Tagasaste (Chamaecytisus palmensis), also known as tree lucerne, is a
nitrogen-fixing legume native to the Canary Islands and one of the most
useful [[fukuoka-low-toxicity-pesticide-deception]], and a high degree of palatability. See forage-and-fodder-trees-permaculture-systems to allow recovery from grazing and to avoid the time-consuming
cut-and-carry method. Well-fenced hedge rows can be constantly grazed.

Tagasaste is readily eaten by all types of stock including pigs, horses,
and rabbits, and can comprise a major portion of their diet. The seed
is also valuable as free-range [[comparison-nut-trees-vs-grain-farming-for-caloric-staple-production]], trees growing
in close association with tagasaste (as close as 1 m) consistently grew
better than controls without tagasaste.

When grown close to timber trees, tagasaste forces them upward for
light, producing straight, long timber. For fruit or nut trees where a
