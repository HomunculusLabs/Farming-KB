# Anaconda Sorcery and Supernatural Pregnancy

## Overview

Anaconda sorcery and supernatural pregnancy constitute a significant class of spiritual illness within the Shipibo-Conibo and broader [[pablo-amaringo]] in *Ayahuasca Visions*. The central phenomenon—**boa chichuchishca** (literally "begot by a boa")—describes a condition in which a woman is supernaturally impregnated by an anaconda through sorcerous means. This malady sits at the intersection of [[icaros]] (healing songs).

Amaringo's Visions 32, 33, and 34—titled "Pregnant by an Anaconda," "Campana Ayahuasca," and "Maituch Iscami" respectively—form a triptych that explores sorcery, healing, and the restoration of magical power from complementary angles. Together they reveal a cosmological framework in which serpent spirits operate as both agents of illness and catalysts for the healer's development of mastery.

### Icaros and Spirit Invocation

The icaros form the core of the healing process. The vegetalista calls upon specific spirit allies to assist in extracting the boa:

- **Conmachaco**: A meandering serpent adorned with golden disks, summoned to help coax the boa from the womb. The conmachaco operates as a serpent of higher order—a spirit that can communicate with and command the invading boa through serpentine kinship.
- **The Rainbow (*Kuychi*)**: Invoked as a bridging force between worlds, its spectrum of colors representing the full range of healing frequencies. The rainbow is a universal symbol in Andean and Amazonian cosmology, associated with the ancestor spirits and the connection between the lower, middle, and upper worlds.
- **Icaros of Diamond, Gold, Silver, and Precious Stones**: These are sung to call upon the mineral spirits—embodiments of earth's crystalline energies that lend [[harner-amazonian-ayahuasca-shamanism]], where indigenous animistic beliefs coexist with and incorporate extraterrestrial and interdimensional imagery. The Aponian spacecraft functions as a cosmic guarantor of the healer's authority, ensuring that the balance of power favors the curing side. Its presence suggests that even the most powerful terrestrial sorceries are subject to oversight from higher cosmic orders.

### The Extraction and the Father Boa's Hypnotism

When the boa finally emerges from the womb, the father boa (a larger, more powerful ancestral serpent) exerts a hypnotic influence that causes all present—family members, assistants, observers—to fall into a deep sleep. Only the *sublime vegetalista* remains conscious, sustained by his electromagnetic powers. This detail underscores the hierarchy of spiritual power: the father boa's hypnotism is nearly absolute, yet the fully initiated healer, charged with electromagnetic energy through years of dieting plants and receiving *mariri*, transcends its influence. The extraction is thus both a medical procedure and a demonstration of the vegetalista's mastery over formidable supernatural forces.

## Campana Ayahuasca and Vibrational Healing

### The Bell-Shaped Vision

Vision 33 introduces **Campana Ayahuasca**, centered on a bell-shaped object that represents the vibration of hearing and the heartbeat. The *campana* (bell) symbolizes the primordial sound—the rhythmic pulse that underlies all creation. In this context, healing is understood as a process of restoring the patient to harmonic resonance with the cosmic pulse, aligning the heartbeat of the physical body with the heartbeat of the universe. The bell's shape—a form that produces pure, sustained tones when struck—mirrors the ideal state of the healed body: resonant, clear, and in tune with its environment.

### Spirit Guardians of the Mariri

Lilac-colored spirits with red eyes, called **papalluses**, serve as guardians of the *mariri* and *huansi*. The *mariri* is a concept of central importance in Amazonian vegetalismo—it refers to the phlegm-like substance that a healer accumulates through years of plant dieting, containing concentrated spiritual power. The *huansi* relates to the healing force or energetic charge that the healer channels through singing and blowing. The papalluses' role as guardians indicates that these sacred energies require protection from sorcerous interference. Their unusual appearance—lilac coloring with red eyes—marks them as beings from a specific spiritual frequency, visible only to those with sufficiently developed visionary capacity.

### Spirits of the Ayahuman

The *ayahuman* (*Couroupita guianensis*, the cannon ball tree) contributes spirits that assist in curing gastritis and stomach ache. This tree, named for its large spherical fruits resembling cannonballs, is regarded as a powerful medicinal plant in the Amazonian pharmacopoeia. Its spirits' association with digestive healing connects to the broader understanding that many sorcery illnesses manifest in the gastrointestinal tract, the body's primary site of energetic exchange with the external world.

### Subaquatic Cities and the Queen's Schacapa

Vision 33 also reveals armed beings with four faces who inhabit subaquatic cities—spiritual realms beneath rivers and lakes that are accessible through ayahuasca visions. A queen within this underwater cosmology assists in curing, using a *schacapa* (a bundled leaf rattle) as her healing instrument. The schacapa is one of the most common tools of the vegetalista, used during ceremonies to cleanse patients' energy fields and call beneficial spirits. The subaquatic realm represents the hidden dimension of Amazonian cosmology—the world beneath the water's surface, which mirrors and influences the visible world above.

## Maituch Iscami: Restoration of Twisted Magical Powers

### The Condition

Vision 34 addresses **Maituch Iscami**, derived from the Quechua *maytuy* ("to twist") with the suffix *-shcami* indicating a completed or inflicted action. *Maituchishcami* thus means "one's magic powers are twisted"—a condition in which a practitioner's or patient's spiritual energy has been distorted, knotted, or inverted through sorcery. Unlike boa chichuchishca, which targets the physical body through reproductive invasion, maituch iscami attacks the subtle body, compromising the victim's capacity for healing, protection, and spiritual perception. This condition can afflict not only laypeople but also vegetalistas themselves, making it one of the most insidious forms of sorcery.

### The Sumiruna's Intervention

A *sumiruna* (a high-level vegetalista or master healer) addresses this condition by blowing *alli-huaira* ("good wind") to form a great tornado that disperses the sorcery. The *alli-huaira* is the purified, beneficial breath of the healer—charged with plant spirits and protective intention. The tornado imagery conveys the sweeping, decisive nature of the cure: the twisted energies are not gently unraveled but forcefully scattered and neutralized. The fact that only a sumiruna can effectively perform this intervention highlights the hierarchy of healing knowledge in the tradition.

### Prince Quekaltec and Cosmic Travel

Prince Quekaltec, a cosmic traveler, visits sanctuaries across the universe, with primary connections to Mongol and Tiahuanaco cultures. This figure embodies the tradition's expansive cosmology, linking Amazonian shamanism to civilizations across the globe and beyond. His mode of travel is a snake called the *kori-machacu* (golden viper), which functions as his spaceship. The kori-machacu represents the synthesis of animal spirit and advanced technology—a living vessel that navigates interdimensional space, paralleling the Aponian spacecraft from the boa chichuchishca treatment.

### Serpent Spaceships and the Cosmic Serpent Motif

The recurring image of serpents as vehicles of cosmic travel—both the kori-machacu and the Aponian spacecraft—points to a deep structural motif in Amaringo's cosmology. The serpent is not merely a terrestrial animal spirit but the archetype of connection and movement between worlds. Whether coiled in a woman's womb or stretched across intergalactic space, the serpent represents the same fundamental principle: the power to traverse boundaries. The healer's work, in this framework, involves learning to navigate the same serpentine pathways that sorcerers exploit, but in service of curing rather than harm.

## The Mariri: Concentrated Healing Power

The concept of the *mariri* appears across multiple visions and deserves special attention as a unifying element. The mariri is the accumulated spiritual substance that a vegetalista builds through years of strict dietary regimes (*dietas*) with teacher plants. It is experienced as a phlegm-like material in the throat that the healer can project through blowing (*soplar*) to cleanse, protect, or attack. The papalluses who guard the mariri in Vision 33 underscore its vulnerability—without protection, the mariri can be stolen or corrupted by sorcerers, leaving the healer powerless. The mariri is thus both the healer's greatest asset and his most exposed liability.

The *huansi*, often mentioned alongside the mariri, represents the active healing charge or frequency that the healer emits during ceremony. If the mariri is the battery, the huansi is the current. Together they form the energetic infrastructure that makes the vegetalista's work possible. Loss or corruption of either renders the healer unable to diagnose or treat sorcery effectively.

- **Energetic restoration** (maituch iscami) addresses the aftermath of sorcery—the twisting of one's innate magical capacities—and requires intervention at the highest level of the vegetalista hierarchy.

Several cross-cutting themes emerge from this triptych. First, the importance of **hierarchy**—from the vegetalista to the sumiruna, from the boa to the father boa, from earthly spirits to cosmic overseers. Second, the role of **[[plant-response-to-sound-and-vibration]]** as primary healing media, whether through icaros, the campana, or the schacapa rattle. Third, the **syncretic breadth** of the tradition, which freely incorporates elements from Andean, Mesoamerican, and even extraterrestrial cosmologies into a unified healing framework.

Together, they illustrate a worldview in which illness and healing operate across multiple dimensions simultaneously—physical, botanical, spiritual, and cosmic—requiring the vegetalista to command knowledge of plants, songs, spirit allies, and interstellar forces to practice effectively. The serpent, as both pathogen and healer's ally, embodies the dual nature of power in this tradition: the same forces that cause illness, when properly understood and directed, become the instruments of cure.
