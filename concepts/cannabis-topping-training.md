# Cannabis Topping and Training Techniques

Plant training is the practice of manipulating cannabis growth patterns to increase yield, improve light penetration, manage canopy height, and create an even bud distribution. These techniques range from simple pruning cuts to elaborate trellis systems.

## Why Train Cannabis Plants

Untrained cannabis plants typically grow as a single central cola (main stem) with smaller subordinate branches. This "Christmas tree" shape means the top canopy receives the most light while lower branches are shaded and underdeveloped. Training creates a wider, flatter canopy that maximizes light exposure across more bud sites.

Benefits of training:

- Increased yield per plant by 20-50% or more
- Better light distribution to all bud sites
- More manageable plant height for indoor spaces
- Improved airflow through the canopy (reducing mold risk)
- Larger, more uniform colas rather than one dominant top

## Topping

Topping is the removal of the apical meristem (the very top growing tip of the main stem). This breaks apical dominance and forces the plant to develop two new main colas from the nodes immediately below the cut.

**When to top**: After the plant has developed 4-6 true nodes (not counting cotyledons). The plant should be healthy and in active vegetative growth.

**How to top**: Locate the newest growth at the top of the main stem. Use clean, sharp scissors or a scalpel to cut through the stem just above the highest set of full leaves. Two new growth tips will emerge from the leaf axils below the cut site.

Recovery takes 3-7 days. You can top multiple times (topping each new leader) to create 4, 8, or even 16 main colas.

## FIM (Fuck I Missed)

FIM is a variation of topping where instead of cleanly removing the entire apical tip, you cut roughly 75% of it, leaving a small portion behind. This often results in 3-4 new colas instead of 2, though results are less predictable than a clean top.

**Technique**: Pinch the new growth tip and cut at an angle through the upper portion. The cut is intentionally imprecise — "FIM" got its name from a grower who missed a clean topping cut.

## Low-Stress Training (LST)

LST involves physically bending and tying down branches to alter the plant's shape without cutting. This is the gentlest training method and can begin very early in vegetative growth.

**Materials**: Soft plant ties (pipe cleaners, garden wire with rubber coating, twist ties, or zip ties with padding).

**Method**:

1. Gently bend the main stem down and away from the center of the pot.
2. Tie it down to the rim of the container or to a stake in the soil.
3. As new branches grow upward, continue bending and tying them outward.
4. Over weeks, this creates a flat, even canopy.

LST can be combined with topping for maximum effect. Always bend slowly and gradually to avoid snapping branches.

## Supercropping

Supercropping is a high-stress training (HST) technique that involves deliberately softening and bending stems to create knuckle-like joints. This increases nutrient flow to the bend site, strengthens the branch, and allows horizontal reorientation of growth.

**Technique**:

1. Select a branch that is tall enough to warrant manipulation.
2. Pinch the stem firmly between thumb and index finger, rolling it back and forth to crush the inner tissue (collenchyma) without breaking the outer skin.
3. The branch becomes pliable — gently bend it to the desired angle.
4. The "knuckle" that forms at the bend point becomes structurally stronger than before.
5. Support the bent branch with a tie if needed for the first few days.

## ScrOG (Screen of Green)

ScrOG uses a horizontal screen (usually chicken wire or plastic trellis netting) stretched above the plants. As branches grow through the screen, you weave them laterally to create an even canopy.

**Setup**: Position the screen 12-20 inches above the base of the plants depending on available vertical space. Fill the screen during vegetative growth. Switch to flowering once the screen is roughly 70-80% full — plants will stretch and fill remaining gaps during early flower.

**Benefits**: Extremely even light distribution, maximizes yield per square foot, ideal for small grow spaces.

## Mainlining

Mainlining (also called "manifolding") is a systematic approach that combines topping with LST to create a perfectly symmetrical plant with a specific number of evenly spaced main colas (typically 8 or 16).

**Process**:

1. Top the seedling at the 3rd node (creating 2 main branches).
2. Top each of those 2 branches at the 3rd node from each tip (creating 4 branches).
3. Continue the pattern to reach 8 or 16 main colas.
4. LST all branches outward into a symmetrical fan shape.

This produces highly uniform plants with consistent cola sizes. It requires a longer vegetative period but delivers predictable, high-quality results.

## Defoliation

Defoliation is the selective removal of leaves — usually fan leaves — to improve light penetration and airflow. There are two main schools of thought:

**Pre-flower defoliation**: Remove large fan leaves and lower growth 1-2 weeks before switching to 12/12. This exposes bud sites that will become future colas.

**Mid-flower defoliation**: Some growers remove fan leaves during weeks 2-3 of flowering to redirect energy toward bud development and improve airflow. This is controversial — some argue it stresses the plant unnecessarily.

**Guidelines**: Never remove more than 20-30% of foliage at once. Focus on leaves that are blocking light to bud sites, damaged, or yellowing. Leave healthy leaves on the upper canopy unless they are severely shading lower growth.

## Choosing a Training Method

Different methods suit different situations:

| Method | Difficulty | Best For | Vegetative Time Needed |
|--------|-----------|----------|----------------------|
| LST | Beginner | Small spaces, limited plant count | 2-4 weeks |
| Topping | Beginner | All setups | 2-3 weeks recovery |
| FIM | Beginner | Quick canopy expansion | 1-2 weeks |
| Supercropping | Intermediate | Tall, stretchy strains | 3-5 days recovery |
| ScrOG | Intermediate | Maximizing yield per sq ft | 4-6 weeks |
| Mainlining | Advanced | Symmetrical, even canopy | 6-8+ weeks |
| Defoliation | Beginner-Adv | Canopy management | N/A (maintenance) |

**Key principles regardless of method**:

1. **Start during vegetative growth**: Most structural training should be complete before switching to 12/12 flowering. Light stress during early flowering can cause hermaphroditism in sensitive strains.
2. **Be patient**: Plants need time to recover from stress. Don't apply multiple HST techniques at once — wait for recovery.
3. **Support heavy colas**: As buds develop, trained branches may need support with yo-yos, bamboo stakes, or garden wire to prevent branches from snapping under weight.
4. **Observe plant response**: Some strains respond enthusiastically to training; others are sensitive and may stunt. Adjust your approach based on the plant's reaction.
5. **Clean tools**: Always use clean, sharp scissors or blades. Sanitize between plants to prevent disease transmission.
