## Overview

Poultry (primarily chickens) are among the most integrated animal systems in permaculture design. They provide eggs, meat, manure, pest control, orchard cleanup, and soil cultivation through the "chicken tractor" system. See also [[faires-plant-communities-and-guilds]], [[poultry-housing-guide]], and Permaculture Water Systems.

## Breed Selection

### Light Breeds (Mediterranean origin)
- **Ancestry:** Spanish, Indo-Malaysian jungle fowl
- **Characteristics:** Flighty, poor mothers, lay white eggs (spring/summer), cold/wind sensitive, prefer sandy/light soils, high pH
- **Examples:** Anconas, Andalusians, Minorcas, Leghorns, Hamburghs
- **Traits:** Long legs, full combs, four toes, lightly feathered, white ear lobes, fly well
- **Foraging:** Excellent foragers — can obtain most or all food on free range with hedgerow and pasture
- **Egg production:** 160-180 flock average per hen per year

### Heavy Breeds (Chinese origin)
- **Ancestry:** Chinese fowl via Langshan
- **Characteristics:** Non-fliers (if not chased), excellent mothers, lay brown eggs (summer/autumn through winter), cold/wind hardy, tolerate clay and acid soils
- **Examples:** Scots Dumpy, Orpington, Dorking, Wyandotte, Rhode Island Red, Plymouth Rock, Australorp
- **Traits:** Heavy feathering, short/feathered legs, short combs or rose combs, five toes
- **Foraging:** Forage well but need supplementary food, especially in winter
- **Egg production:** 130-150 flock average (up to 200 in rare cases)

### Utility Breeds
- Selected for both egg and meat production
- 60-100 breeds available for specific soils, sites, and foodstuffs

## Flock Management

### Natural Flock Size
- Wild flocks seldom exceed 20-30 individuals (2-3 cockerels, rest hens)
- Surplus cockerels driven out or killed (fall prey to predators)
- Larger flocks of 40-60 will break up into two flocks on their own

### Longevity
- Unstressed free-range flocks continue laying for 4-6 years
- Caged birds under forced regimes (artificial day length, hormones, pelleted food with additives) must be replaced every 18-20 months

### Population Density
- **800-900/ha:** Unstressed, housed as ~20 small flocks in sheds ~2m per side
- **350-400/ha:** Allows sheep and cattle to also use the range
- **120-180/ha:** Needed to clean up windfalls and supply fertilizer to orchards
- **Household:** One flock of 20-30 hens as food supplement

## Chicken Tractor / Pen Rotation System

### Method
1. Flocks moved every 6 weeks through a series of pens
2. Followed by vegetable or small fruit crop
3. Vines on fences add yield
4. Bared areas limed, raked, sown to crop
5. Full rotation cycle: 12-16 pens on one hectare every 18 months

### Weed Control
- Chickens remove all weeds, surface bulbs, weed seeds, and grasses
- Effective against nutgrass, Kikuyu, onion weed, Oxalis, and most persistent weeds
- Ground limed after chickens moved, then planted to vegetable or orchard crops

### Forage Crops for Rotation
- Buckwheat, wheat, sunflower, millet, black nightshade, cleavers, chard, mustard
- Allow 2-3 months to mature before chickens return
- Separate pen of comfrey, arrowroot, chard, and sunroot as drought forage

## Forage Systems

### On Range (65% of diet from foraging)
- 65% of food from insects, invertebrates, greens, and grains
- 30-35% supplied as concentrated grain products (weed seed, cracked/undersized grains from threshers)
- Table scraps, fallen fruit and seeds, garden wastes

### Tree and Shrub Forage
- Coprosma repens, Caragana, tagasaste, boxthorn (Lycium spp.)
- Pigeon pea, Solanum species with berries, mulberry (5-6 varieties)
- Acacias with edible seed, Leucaena, clovers, grasses
- "Weeds": cleavers, chicory, comfrey, Oxalis, dandelion

### Insect Farming for Poultry
- Piled manure for flies to lay in, chickens eat maggots
- Mulch piles for cockroaches
- Termites bred in sunken pits
- Compost piles to breed insect foods
- Mealworms in bran and root mixtures

### Grain Processing
- Large acorns, chestnuts, or starchy seeds sprouted or rolled to crack
- Garden roller on hard surface for cracking
- In hot periods, grains sprouted for 2-3 days
- In winter, unsprouted grains and grain legumes preferred

## Health Maintenance
- Dry dust baths with diatomaceous earth, dried bracken, or neem leaves for ectoparasites
- Wormwoods for intestinal worm control
- Abundant shell grit or calcium (limestone) gravel for eggshells
- Hard silica grit to grind food in the crop
- Clean water, good shade, predator protection

## Integration with Other Systems

- **Orchards:** Clean windfalls, supply fertilizer, control pests
- **Dairy:** Combined mixed farm economy
- **Garden:** Process kitchen and garden wastes
- **Tropics:** Abundant fruit like papaya supplies most needs
