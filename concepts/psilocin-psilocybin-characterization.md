# Spectroscopic Characterization of [[psilocybin]]

## Overview

The complete spectroscopic characterization of psilocin and psilocybin by
Shirota, Hakamata, and Goda provided essential reference data for the
forensic identification of magic mushrooms. Since morphological and
microscopic identification of psilocybin-containing fungi is difficult without
experts, chromatographic methods including TLC, GC, and HPLC are usually
employed, and these require authentic standard compounds. The detailed
spectroscopic data reported in this synthesis serves as a definitive reference
for analytical laboratories.

## Psilocin Spectroscopic Data

### Physical Properties
Psilocin is obtained as white crystals with a melting point of 169-174
degrees (decomposing), consistent with the literature value of 173-176
degrees (dec). The [[indole]] chromophore and
distinguishes psilocin from its 5-hydroxy isomer [[serotonin]], which has a
different UV profile.

### Infrared Spectroscopy
The IR spectrum (KBr pellet) shows a broad N-H/O-H stretch at 3285 cm-1,
aromatic C=C stretches at 1620 and 1588 cm-1, and C-N stretches at 1473
and 1345 cm-1. The 4-hydroxy C-O stretch appears at 1258 cm-1 with an
associated C-O bending mode at 1232 cm-1. Characteristic indole ring modes
appear at 1044 and 833 cm-1, and a C-H out-of-plane bend at 722 cm-1.

### 1H NMR Spectroscopy
The 1H NMR spectrum of psilocin in CDCl3 at 400 MHz shows the indole N-H
proton as a broad singlet at delta 7.90. The four aromatic protons of the
indole ring appear as a doublet at delta 7.05 (H-6), a doublet of doublets
at delta 6.86 (H-7), a doublet at delta 6.84 (H-2), and a doublet of
doublets at delta 6.56 (H-5). The ethylamine chain protons appear as a
multiplet at delta 2.94 (H2-1', alpha to the indole ring) and delta 2.70
(H2-2', alpha to the nitrogen). The N,N-dimethyl protons appear as a
singlet at delta 2.38.

### 13C NMR Spectroscopy
The 13C NMR spectrum in CDCl3 at 100 MHz shows twelve carbon signals
consistent with the C12H16N2O molecular formula. The quaternary C-4 bearing
the hydroxyl group appears at delta 152.1. The C-7a quaternary carbon
appears at delta 139.0. The four aromatic CH carbons appear between delta
106.4 and 123.5. The C-3 quaternary carbon of the indole ring is at delta
114.6, while the C-3a quaternary carbon appears at delta 117.5. The
ethylene chain shows signals at delta 61.6 (C-2') and 25.1 (C-1'). The
N,N-dimethyl carbon appears at delta 45.3.

### Mass Spectrometry
ESIMS shows the protonated molecular ion at m/z 205.1 [M+H]+ and the
sodiated ion at m/z 227.1 [M+Na]+. The base peak is the fragment at m/z
160.1 [M-NMe2]+, resulting from loss of the dimethylamine group. High-
resolution ESIMS confirmed the molecular formula C12H17N2O with a measured
[m/z]+ of 205.1303 versus a calculated 205.1341.

## Psilocybin Spectroscopic Data

### Physical Properties
Psilocybin is obtained as white needle-like crystals with a melting point
of 190-198 degrees C, consistent with literature values of 185-195 and
210-212 degrees C. The higher melting point compared to psilocin reflects
the additional phosphate ester group, which increases molecular weight and
intermolecular hydrogen bonding.

### Ultraviolet Spectroscopy
The UV spectrum of psilocybin in methanol shows absorption maxima at
221.0 nm (log epsilon 4.44), 267.5 nm (3.66), 278.5 nm (3.57), and
290.0 nm (3.42). The pattern is very similar to that of psilocin, as
expected, since the chromophore is the same 4-hydroxyindole system. The
slight bathochromic shift and decreased intensity of the longer wavelength
bands reflect the electronic effect of the phosphate ester at the 4-position.

### Infrared Spectroscopy
The IR spectrum (KBr pellet) shows a broad absorption at 3266 cm-1
corresponding to O-H stretches of the phosphate group and N-H stretch. The
P=O stretch appears at 1154 cm-1, and P-O-C stretches appear at 1103 and
1061 cm-1. Aromatic ring modes are visible at 1620 and 1580 cm-1, with
C-H bending at 1439 and 1352 cm-1. The phosphate O-H bending mode appears
at 1298 cm-1, and P-O stretches at 1244 cm-1. Additional fingerprint
region absorptions appear at 926, 858, and 804 cm-1.

### 1H NMR Spectroscopy
The 1H NMR spectrum of psilocybin in D2O at 400 MHz shows the four
aromatic protons of the indole ring: a doublet at delta 7.22 (H-7, J =
7.6 Hz), a singlet at delta 7.18 (H-2), a triplet at delta 7.13 (H-6, J =
7.6 Hz), and a doublet at delta 6.98 (H-5, J = 7.6 Hz). The ethylamine
chain shows the characteristic ABCX pattern of a phosphorylated ethanolamine
moiety: a triplet at delta 3.44 (H2-2', J = 7.2 Hz) and a triplet at delta
3.28 (H2-1', J = 7.2 Hz). The N,N-dimethyl protons appear as a singlet at
delta 2.86.

### 13C NMR Spectroscopy
The 13C NMR spectrum in D2O with a drop of methanol at 100 MHz shows
phosphorus-coupled signals for C-4 at delta 146.4 and for C-3a and C-5a.
The C-7a quaternary carbon appears at delta 139.4. The aromatic CH carbons
appear between delta 108.4 and 124.8. The C-3 quaternary carbon appears at
delta 108.6. The phosphorylated ethylene chain shows signals at delta 59.7
(C-2') and 22.4 (C-1'). The N,N-dimethyl carbon appears at delta 43.4.

### 31P NMR Spectroscopy
The 31P NMR spectrum in CD3OD at 162 MHz shows a single phosphate signal
at delta -4.48, consistent with a monoalkyl phosphate ester. This chemical
shift is characteristic of a primary phosphate ester and serves as a useful
diagnostic for distinguishing psilocybin from other phosphorylated indole
alkaloids.

### Mass Spectrometry
ESIMS shows the protonated molecular ion at m/z 285.1 [M+H]+ and the
sodiated ion at m/z 307.1 [M+Na]+. Key fragment ions include m/z 240.0
[M-NMe2]+, m/z 205.1 [M-H2O3P+H]+ (corresponding to psilocin), and m/z
160.1 [M-H2O3P-NMe2]+. The presence of the psilocin fragment at m/z 205.1
is analytically significant, as it allows indirect detection of psilocybin
through its characteristic decomposition product. HRMS confirmed the
molecular formula C12H18N2O4P with a measured [M+H]+ of 285.0991 versus
a calculated 285.1004.

## Analytical Applications

The comprehensive spectroscopic data for both psilocin and psilocybin
provides essential reference standards for forensic laboratories, regulatory
agencies, and research institutions involved in the identification and
quantification of these compounds in mushroom samples. The clear
distinguishing features between the two compounds — particularly the
phosphate-specific signals in psilocybin's NMR and IR spectra — enable
unambiguous identification even in complex matrix samples.
