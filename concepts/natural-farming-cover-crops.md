# Natural Farming Cover Crops and Green Manure

In [[green-manure-crops]] that take the place of undesirable weeds
and are beneficial to crops, then manual weeding becomes unnecessary.
Fukuoka found that weeding in orchards is not only useless but
positively harmful.

## Green Manure Crop Selection

Fukuoka developed specific recommendations for green [[green-manure-crops-detailed]]
suitable for different purposes:

### Leguminous Green Manures
The pea family provides excellent green manures including kudzu,
common vetch, bur clover, and various bean species. Leguminous green
manure suppresses summer weeds and enriches the soil through nitrogen
fixation. These crops are particularly valuable because they address
both weed suppression and soil fertility in a single planting.

### Combination Planting
Rotational cropping in combination with various green manure crops is,
in a sense, a mimicry of nature. Green manure crops combined with root
vegetables can improve and maintain soil fertility. When a field has
lost its fertility, rotation with green manure crops and root
vegetables can gradually restore productivity. A leguminous green
manure suppresses summer weeds and enriches the soil while the root
crops penetrate and aerate the soil profile.

### Orchard Floor Management
For fruit [[holzer-sweet-chestnut-and-nut-trees]], Fukuoka recommended a diverse [[fukuoka-citrus-orchard-ground-cover-cultivation]]
of clover and vegetables. The green manure crops that take the place of
undesirable weeds enrich the soil and prevent erosion. In the
orchard environment, the no-weeding principle applies equally well.
Trees and their understory cover crops form a symbiotic community.

## Soil Building Through Ground Covers

Without grasses growing over the surface of the ground, rainwater
washes away tons of topsoil each year. In gently sloping areas, this
can result in the loss of several tons to over a hundred tons of soil
per year. In twenty to thirty years, topsoil washes entirely away,
reducing soil fertility to essentially zero. A ground cover of green
manure crops and the combination planting of vegetables and grains
maintains soil structure and prevents this catastrophic loss.

The roots of grasses and green manure crops work the soil down to
twelve inches or more, creating channels for air and water
penetration that no mechanical tillage can match. When combined with
the return of all crop residues as mulch, cover crops build soil
[[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]] continuously without external inputs.

## Practical Application in Grain Fields

Fukuoka's rice-barley succession in a clover cover exemplifies the
practical application of cover crop principles. He seeded barley
together with clover over standing heads of rice, then scattered rice
seed and green manure while the barley was up. This approach more
nearly approximates nature and eliminates weeding entirely. Rice and
barley can be grown without weeding, and vegetables can be grown in a
state that allows them to go wild, without fertilizer or weeding, yet
attain yields comparable to conventional methods.

## Transition to Natural Farming

For farmers wanting to transition to natural farming, Fukuoka
suggested sowing clover and grain in turned soil, then allowing nature
to take over. The key insight is that rotational cropping in
combination with various green manure crops mimics natural plant
succession. By selecting appropriate cover crop species for each season
and purpose, the farmer works with natural processes rather than
against them, gradually reducing and eventually eliminating the need
for all external inputs.
