**Orchid Mycorrhizal Fungi:** Primarily Basidiomycetes in the
Sebacinales and Tulasnellales, these fungi are required for orchid seed
germination and early seedling growth. Orchid seeds are microscopic and
lack endosperm, depending entirely on fungal carbon supply during
germination. Some orchid species remain fully mycoheterotrophic throughout
their entire life cycle, never photosynthesizing.

## Diversity Patterns

AMF diversity was historically considered very low, with fewer than 300
described morphospecies. Molecular studies have dramatically revised this
estimate upward, suggesting several thousand species exist, many of which
are cryptic and may have global distributions. The Glomeromycotina
contain at least 10-15 major clades, each potentially harboring hundreds
of genetically distinct species that are morphologically indistinguishable.

EMF diversity is better documented but still incompletely known. A single
temperate forest stand can host several hundred EMF species. Dominant
genera include *Russula*, *Cortinarius*, *Lactarius*, *Amanita*, *Boletus*,
*Tricholoma*, *Laccaria*, and *Inocybe*, each with hundreds of described
species. Tropical EMF communities remain especially poorly documented,
despite the fact that many tropical tree families form ectomycorrhizal
associations that are critical for forest structure and dynamics.

## Host Specificity and Network Structure

Mycorrhizal fungi vary enormously in host specificity. Many AMF are
generalists, associating with hundreds of plant species across diverse
habitats and continents. Others show preferences for particular plant
families or soil conditions. EMF tend to be more host-specific, with many
species associating primarily with a single tree genus or family, though
some generalist EMF species associate with multiple host families.
