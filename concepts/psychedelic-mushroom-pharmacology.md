# Psychedelic Mushroom Pharmacology

## Overview

The pharmacology of psychedelic mushrooms is defined primarily by the action of psilocin — the active metabolite of [[psilocybin]] — on serotonergic neurotransmitter systems in the central nervous system. Understanding these mechanisms is essential for explaining both the therapeutic potential and the subjective effects of these compounds.

## Primary Mechanism: 5-HT2A Receptor Agonism

The principal mechanism of action of psilocin is agonism (activation) of the serotonin 2A receptor (5-HT2A), a Gq/11-coupled G-protein-coupled receptor (GPCR). Psilocin acts as a full or near-full agonist at this receptor, with an estimated Ki (inhibition constant) of approximately 6-20 nM, making it a high-affinity ligand.

### Receptor Binding Profile

Psilocin interacts with multiple serotonin receptor subtypes:

- **5-HT2A**: Primary target; full agonist — responsible for psychedelic effects, hallucinations, perceptual changes
- **5-HT2C**: Partial agonist — may contribute to anxiety, changes in appetite, and some cognitive effects
- **5-HT1A**: Partial agonist — may contribute to anxiolytic and mood-elevating effects
- **5-HT1B**: Moderate affinity — role not fully characterized
- **5-HT5A, 5-HT6, 5-HT7**: Lower affinity — potential contributory roles

The psychedelic effects are overwhelmingly mediated through 5-HT2A activation. This has been confirmed by pharmacological blockade studies: pretreatment with ketanserin (a selective 5-HT2A antagonist) completely abolishes the subjective and behavioral effects of psilocybin.

### Intracellular Signaling Cascades

5-HT2A receptor activation triggers several downstream signaling pathways:

- **Gq/11-PLC-IP3 pathway**: Activates phospholipase C, generating inositol triphosphate (IP3) and diacylglycerol (DAG), leading to calcium release from intracellular stores
- **MAPK/ERK pathway**: Activation of mitogen-activated protein kinase cascades, involved in gene expression changes and neuroplasticity
- **mTOR pathway**: Activation of the mechanistic target of rapamycin pathway, which is critical for synaptic plasticity and the growth of new neural connections
- **β-arrestin signaling**: Biased signaling through β-arrestin-2 may contribute to specific aspects of the psychedelic experience

## Pharmacokinetics

### Absorption

After oral ingestion, psilocybin is rapidly absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract. The phosphate group is cleaved by alkaline phosphatases in the intestinal mucosa and liver, converting psilocybin to psilocin. Peak plasma concentrations of psilocin are reached in approximately 40-90 minutes.

### Distribution

Psilocin is lipophilic enough to cross the blood-brain barrier readily. It distributes throughout the body, with highest concentrations in the liver, kidneys, and brain. Plasma protein binding is moderate (~40-50%).

### Metabolism

Psilocin is primarily metabolized in the liver through two pathways:

1. **Oxidative deamination** by monoamine oxidase (MAO), primarily MAO-A, producing 4-hydroxy-indoleacetic acid (4-HIAA)
2. **[[glucuronidation]]** by UDP-glucuronosyltransferases (UGTs), producing psilocin-O-glucuronide, the primary metabolite excreted in urine

The plasma half-life of psilocin is approximately 2-3 hours. Subjective effects typically last 4-6 hours, depending on dose.

### Elimination

Psilocin and its metabolites are primarily excreted renally. Approximately 50-65% of an administered dose is recovered in urine within 24 hours, mostly as glucuronide conjugates. Complete elimination occurs within 48 hours.

## Brain Regions Affected

Neuroimaging studies (fMRI, PET) have identified several brain regions where psilocin produces measurable changes in activity and connectivity:

### Default Mode Network (DMN)

The default mode network — comprising the medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC), posterior cingulate cortex (PCC), and angular gyrus — shows decreased activity and reduced functional connectivity under psilocybin. The DMN is associated with self-referential thinking, ego, and the narrative sense of self. Its suppression correlates strongly with:

- Ego dissolution experiences
- Feelings of unity and interconnectedness
- Reduced rumination and self-focused thought
- Increased cognitive flexibility

### Prefrontal Cortex

The prefrontal cortex shows altered connectivity patterns, including increased cross-talk with sensory and emotional processing regions. This may underlie the blending of cognitive and perceptual experiences characteristic of the psychedelic state.

### Visual Cortex

Increased activity and altered connectivity in visual processing areas (V1 through higher-order visual cortices) correlate with visual hallucinations, geometric patterns, and enhanced color perception.

### Amygdala

Psilocybin reduces amygdala reactivity to emotional stimuli, particularly negative emotional faces and images. This effect may underlie the emotional processing benefits observed in clinical settings.

### Hippocampus

Changes in hippocampal activity and connectivity may contribute to memory retrieval, autobiographical recollection, and the vivid imagery common during psychedelic experiences.

## Neuroplasticity

One of the most significant findings in modern psychedelic research is the promotion of structural and functional neuroplasticity:

### Dendritic Growth and Spinogenesis

In animal models, psilocybin and related 5-HT2A agonists promote:

- **Increased dendritic spine density** in cortical pyramidal neurons within 24 hours of administration
- **Dendritic arbor growth** — increased branching and length of dendrites
- **Synaptogenesis** — formation of new functional synaptic connections
- **Upregulation of BDNF** (brain-derived neurotrophic factor) and other neurotrophic factors

These structural changes are mediated through the 5-HT2A-mTOR signaling pathway and can persist for weeks to months after a single administration. This neuroplastic effect is hypothesized to be a key mechanism underlying the lasting therapeutic benefits observed in clinical trials.

### Functional Connectivity Changes

Psilocybin produces a transient but dramatic increase in global brain connectivity — the entropy of functional brain networks increases, meaning more regions communicate with each other than under normal waking consciousness. This "entropic brain state" is characterized by:

- Increased cross-network integration
- Reduced modularity (decreased segregation of functional networks)
- Enhanced connectivity between normally distinct brain regions
- A more flexible, less rigid information processing architecture

This increased connectivity may allow the brain to escape entrenched patterns of thought and behavior, facilitating therapeutic insight and behavioral change.

## Tolerance

Rapid and significant tolerance develops to the effects of psilocybin:

- **Cross-tolerance** occurs with other serotonergic psychedelics (LSD, DMT, mescaline)
- Tolerance is largely gone within 7-14 days of abstinence
- The mechanism involves 5-HT2A receptor downregulation and desensitization
- No evidence of physical dependence or withdrawal syndrome
- Psychological dependence is extremely rare

## Drug Interactions

Important pharmacological interactions:

- **SSRIs/SNRIs**: Chronic use of serotonergic antidepressants significantly attenuates psychedelic effects due to receptor downregulation. Discontinuation of 2-4 weeks is typically recommended before psychedelic therapy
- **MAOIs**: Monoamine oxidase inhibitors slow psilocin metabolism, potentially increasing intensity and duration. Caution is advised
- **Lithium**: Concurrent use with psychedelics has been associated with an increased risk of seizures and psychotic reactions
- **Antipsychotics**: 5-HT2A antagonists (risperidone, olanzapine, etc.) block psychedelic effects
- **Tramadol**: May increase seizure risk when combined

## Toxicology

Psilocybin has a very high safety profile:

- **LD50** (lethal dose 50%) in rodents: approximately 285 mg/kg IV — far exceeding any reasonable human dose
- **No known lethal overdose** in humans from [[psilocybin-mushrooms]] alone
- The primary risks are psychological (acute anxiety, panic, psychosis in vulnerable individuals) and accidental (misidentification leading to toxic species ingestion)
- Cardiovascular effects include mild, transient increases in heart rate and blood pressure
