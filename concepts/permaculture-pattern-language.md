## Why Patterns?

Nature is organized into patterns: branching (rivers, trees, blood vessels), spirals (shells, galaxies, pinecones), nets (spider webs, leaf veins, [[mycorrhiza]]l networks in soil
- Food webs

### Wave (Meander)
Waves and meanders slow flow, increase edge, and create diversity.

**Applications:**
- Swale design on contour
- Meandering garden paths
- Stream restoration (adding meanders)
- Winding planting rows

### Circle
Circles enclose space efficiently (maximum area for minimum perimeter) and create a sense of wholeness.

**Applications:**
- Keyhole gardens
- Mandala gardens
- Circular chicken runs
- Herb spirals (viewed from above)
- Zone planning (concentric circles from house)

### Lobes (Lobe and Scatter)
Lobes and bulges increase edge while maintaining overall organization.

**Applications:**
- Pond shorelines (irregular edges increase habitat)
- Garden bed shapes
- Food forest canopy openings
- Community layout

### Scatter (Random Distribution)
Random distribution mimics natural plant communities and avoids the predictability of grid layouts.

**Applications:**
- Naturalistic plantings
- Mixed species planting
- Wildflower meadows
- Forest garden understory

### Fractal (Self-Similar at Multiple Scales)
Fractal patterns repeat at different scales -- the branching pattern of a tree resembles the branching pattern of its veins, which resembles the branching pattern of a river system.

**Applications:**
- Design at multiple scales (detail patterns echo the overall design pattern)
- Zone planning repeated at regional, property, and garden scales
- Guild design repeated at orchard, tree, and herbaceous levels

## Pattern Application Process

### From Pattern to Detail
Permaculture design moves from broad patterns to specific details:

1. **Identify the overall pattern** for the design (branching, spiral, etc.)
2. **Apply the pattern at the property scale** (layout of zones, paths, water)
3. **Apply the pattern at the garden scale** (bed shapes, planting arrangements)
4. **Apply the pattern at the detail scale** (individual plant placement, structures)

### Combining Patterns
Most effective designs combine multiple patterns:
- A branching path network connecting circular keyhole beds
- A spiral herb garden at the center of a branching pathway system
- Meandering swales creating net-like water distribution
- Lobed pond edges creating maximum edge within a circular food forest

## Specific Garden Patterns

### Mandala Garden
Circular garden with radiating paths creating pie-shaped wedges. Maximum edge, beautiful form, all plants accessible from central point.

### Keyhole Bed
Circular bed with a keyhole-shaped path. Variation on the mandala for smaller scale.

### Herb Spiral
Three-dimensional spiral combining circular and spiral patterns. See [[permaculture-principle-design-from-patterns-to-details]]
