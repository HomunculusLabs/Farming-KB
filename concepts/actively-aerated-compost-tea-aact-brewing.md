## Actively Aerated Compost Tea (AACT) Brewing

Actively Aerated Compost Tea (AACT) is a water extract of high-quality compost that is actively aerated during brewing to support the growth and reproduction of beneficial aerobic microorganisms. Dr. Elaine Ingham's field guide presents AACT as a method for rapidly multiplying and distributing the soil foodweb organisms found in mature compost, providing a liquid biological amendment that can be applied to soil, foliage, [[fungi-convention-on-biological-diversity]] of AACT.

### Compost Extract

Compost extract is a simple slurry made by mixing compost with water and immediately straining. It contains the organisms present in the original compost but does not allow them to multiply. Extract is useful when biological material is needed quickly but brewing time is not available.

## The Science of Brewing

### What Happens During Brewing

The AACT brewing process extracts organisms from compost into water and provides conditions for them to reproduce. The key processes include:

1. **Extraction:** Agitation and water movement dislodge organisms from compost particles into suspension
2. **Reproduction:** Aerobic bacteria reproduce rapidly (doubling every 20–30 minutes under optimal conditions), while fungi grow and produce hyphae
3. **Food web development:** Protozoa and other predators multiply as [[fungal-elicitors-enhanced-secondary-metabolite-production]]:** Microorganisms produce enzymes, vitamins, growth factors, and antibiotics during active growth

The goal is to produce a tea with high organism diversity and biomass reflecting the source compost community, but at much higher concentrations.

### Dissolved Oxygen Requirements

Maintaining adequate dissolved oxygen (DO) is the critical variable in AACT production. Ingham specifies:

- **Minimum DO during brewing:** 6 mg/L (above the 5.5 mg/L threshold where facultative organisms shift to anaerobic metabolism)
- **Target DO:** 6–8 mg/L throughout the brew cycle
- **Brewing temperature:** 65–75°F (18–24°C), as temperature affects both organism growth rates and oxygen solubility

If DO drops below 5.5 mg/L during brewing, the [[ingham-anaerobic-organisms-role-soil]], producing alcohol, phenols, and other compounds that can be phytotoxic. Ingham considers this the most common cause of brewing failure.

## Essential Equipment

### Brewers

AACT brewers range from simple 5-gallon bucket systems to commercial 500+ gallon units. The essential components are:

- **Brewing vessel:** Food-grade plastic or stainless steel container
- **Aeration system:** Air pump(s) rated for the brew volume, connected to diffuser stones or bubblers
- **Compost basket or bag:** Mesh bag (400 micron or finer) to contain compost while allowing organism extraction
- **Extraction mechanism:** Some method of agitating the compost-water mixture, whether through bubbling action, mechanical stirring, or recirculation

Ingham emphasizes that the design must ensure all parts of the compost receive adequate water flow and aeration. Dead zones produce anaerobic conditions that can contaminate the entire brew.

### Monitoring Tools

- **Dissolved oxygen meter:** Essential for verifying adequate aeration throughout the brew cycle
- **Thermometer:** Monitor brewing temperature
- **Microscope:** Assess organism populations in finished tea (40× minimum, 400× preferred)
- **pH meter:** Less critical than DO, but useful for monitoring brewing dynamics

## Ingredients

### Compost

The quality of the source compost is the single most important determinant of tea quality. Ingham specifies that compost used for AACT must be:

- **Aerobic throughout:** No anaerobic pockets or foul odors
- **Biologically diverse:** Containing the full range of soil foodweb organisms
- **Fully mature:** At least 6 months old, with no recognizable feedstock materials
- **Free of contaminants:** No pesticide residues, heavy metals, or human/animal pathogens
