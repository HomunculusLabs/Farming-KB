## Psilocybe Fanaticus on Potency

Fanaticus provides specific guidance for P. cubensis growers on maximizing
potency, based on his cultivation experience and the research of Jochen Gartz:

### Key Potency Principles

1. **Substrate quality**: P. cubensis can achieve a baseline psilocybin content
   of 1% (similar to P. semilanceata) when grown on [[pf-tek-brown-rice-vermiculite-substrate-method]], but this
   depends on multiple factors.

2. **Age and maturity**: Young specimens are significantly more potent than
   mature ones. The critical factor is harvesting before or just as the cap
   begins to open.

3. **Sporulation cycle**: Pre-sporulation specimens (those that have not yet
   begun dropping spores) are more potent than post-sporulation ones.

4. **Harvest and preservation**: Cool-dried specimens (dried using desiccant
   rather than heat) retain higher potency.

### Recognizing Over-Mature Specimens

Fanaticus identifies clear visual indicators of over-mature, weakly potent
mushrooms:
- Caps have turned darker colors
- Cap margins have turned up beyond plain (like an inverted umbrella)
- The cap has fully opened
- The veil has torn away from the stem
- Gills are dark brown
- Purple spore deposits are visible gathering on the stem

These characteristics indicate that the mushroom is past its prime for
psychedelic use, and Fanaticus states that such specimens are "indeed weakly
potent and not worth taking."
