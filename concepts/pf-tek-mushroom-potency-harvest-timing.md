# PF TEK Mushroom Potency and Harvest Timing for Maximum Strength

## Overview

One of the most practical and frequently discussed aspects of home mushroom
cultivation is the relationship between harvest timing and mushroom potency. The
PF TEK, authored by Psilocybe Fanaticus, includes detailed guidance on
maximizing the psychoactive potency of cultivated [[brassinosteroid-morphological-effects-psilocybe-cubensis-fruiting]] through
strategic harvesting decisions. This guidance draws on both Fanaticus's own
cultivation experience and published research by Jochen Gartz, and is
supplemented by extensive dosage and potency excerpts from Paul Stamets's
"[[forensic-identification-methods-psilocybin-mushrooms-tlc-gc-hplc]] of the World."

## Stamets Dosage Guidelines (Excerpted in PF TEK)

The PF TEK reproduces key passages from Chapter 5 of Stamets's book regarding
dosage and potency, providing a framework for understanding individual
sensitivity and dosage ranges:

**Individual Sensitivity**: Stamets notes significant individual variation in
sensitivity to psilocybin mushrooms. One mycologist reportedly responds to only
1-2 grams (dried) as if he had consumed 3-4 times that dose. At the other
extreme, a woman reported feeling no effects whatsoever at doses exceeding 5
grams (dried), despite being well-read on mushroom history and eagerly
anticipating the experience.

**Baseline Dosing with P. semilanceata**: Using liberty caps (which average about
1% psilocybin/psilocin content) as the standard, Stamets recommends 1-2 grams
dried for first-time users, equivalent to 10-20 mg of psilocybin. This produces
moderate effects lasting 4-5 hours in most individuals.

**Dose Escalation**: Experienced users can increase dosage in single dried-gram
increments. At 2 grams, the first colorful geometric patterns appear with
noticeable changes in auditory perception. At 3 grams, some users experience
"visual waves" — a phenomenon described as the air appearing to be in a liquid
state with tidal movements distorting the scenery. At 4 grams, effects become
more intense over 4-5 hours. At 5 grams, the experience can be nearly
overwhelming, spanning 6 hours.

**High-Dose Considerations**: Stamets recommends against doses above 7 grams
dried, even for experienced users. At higher doses, some users report loss of
muscular control and strength persisting into the following day. This has been
reported with P. azurescens, P. cyanescens, and [[gartz-panaeolus-subbalteatus-taxonomy-and-intoxication-history]].

## Psilocybe Fanaticus on Potency

Fanaticus provides specific guidance for P. cubensis growers on maximizing
potency, based on his cultivation experience and the research of Jochen Gartz:

### Key Potency Principles

1. **Substrate quality**: P. cubensis can achieve a baseline psilocybin content
   of 1% (similar to P. semilanceata) when grown on brown rice, but this
   depends on multiple factors.

2. **Age and maturity**: Young specimens are significantly more potent than
   mature ones. The critical factor is harvesting before or just as the cap
   begins to open.

3. **Sporulation cycle**: Pre-sporulation specimens (those that have not yet
   begun dropping spores) are more potent than post-sporulation ones.

4. **Harvest and preservation**: Cool-dried specimens (dried using desiccant
   rather than heat) retain higher potency.

### Recognizing Over-Mature Specimens

Fanaticus identifies clear visual indicators of over-mature, weakly potent
mushrooms:
- Caps have turned darker colors
- Cap margins have turned up beyond plain (like an inverted umbrella)
- The cap has fully opened
- The veil has torn away from the stem
- Gills are dark brown
- Purple spore deposits are visible gathering on the stem

These characteristics indicate that the mushroom is past its prime for
psychedelic use, and Fanaticus states that such specimens are "indeed weakly
potent and not worth taking."

### Harvesting Aborts and Primordia

One of the most important potency recommendations in the PF TEK is the
harvesting of abhortive (aborted) specimens and primordia — the tiny
mushroom formations that stop developing or fail to grow into normal
specimens. These appear as small round blobs without well-formed caps. While
they seem tiny individually, Fanaticus notes that several cakes will produce
quite a pile of these small specimens, totaling several dried grams.

These immature specimens are described as "the source of the potent magic."
Their high potency-to-weight ratio makes them particularly valuable for
experienced users seeking strong effects from smaller quantities of material.

### The Potency-Yield Tradeoff

Fanaticus explicitly acknowledges that harvesting only young and abhortive
specimens produces significantly lower total yield but excellent quality:
"By harvesting only the young and abhortive specimens, the yield will be
significantly lower, but the quality will be excellent and the potency can
be similar to P. semilanceata."

This tradeoff between quantity and quality is a fundamental consideration
for home cultivators. A grower seeking maximum total dried weight would
allow mushrooms to mature fully, while a grower prioritizing potency per
gram would harvest early and include all primordia.

## Consumption Advice

The PF TEK recommends consuming dried mushrooms as a deliberate, controlled,
and methodical ritual. Key advice includes:
- Eat all fungi at one sitting rather than piece-mealing the dose
- The lag time in onset can defeat peak performance if the dose is spread out
- Being methodical and deliberate in consumption supports a more predictable
experience

## Species Potency Comparison

The PF TEK contextualizes P. [[psilocybe-cubensis-potency-harvest-timing]] within the broader landscape of
psychoactive species. While P. semilanceata is often cited as the most potent
commonly available species (averaging ~1% psilocybin), Gartz's research
demonstrated that P. cubensis can achieve comparable potency levels when
cultivated under optimal conditions and harvested at the right time. This
finding is significant because P. cubensis is by far the most commonly
cultivated species due to its ease of growth.
