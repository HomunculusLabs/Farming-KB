# Transplanting and Root-Bound Management

Transplanting is one of the most critical physical interventions a cannabis
grower performs during the early stages of a plant's life. Moving a plant
from one container to a larger one, or from a starter medium into the main
grow environment, carries inherent risks but is often necessary to support
healthy root development and vigorous [[cannabis-vegetative-growth-stage]]. Greg Green's "The
[[green-cannabis-grow-bible-breeding-genetics-and-true-breeding]] Bible" covers transplanting as a core pre-production technique
that bridges the gap between [[green-cannabis-grow-bible-hydroponics-and-the-bubbler]]. There is not much of a problem when transferring the cube and
seedling to soil -- simply dig a small hole, place the cube in, and cover
it with soil.

## Transplanting Techniques

### Method 1: Pot-in-Pot

One approach that avoids disturbing the root ball entirely is to cut away
the base of the small pot and place this pot directly into the bigger pot
of soil. The roots will naturally grow down through the bottom hole of the
old pot and into the new one. Roots will always find their way downward.

### Method 2: Soil Ball Extraction

The second method requires letting the soil dry out for a couple of days
so that it settles hard around the root mass. Then use a clean knife to
cut around the inside of the pot. Cut deep but do not damage the roots.
Push your fingers down into the side and lift the plant and soil out. Some
breakaway soil is acceptable as long as there is not too much lost. Quickly
place the plant into the larger pot, fill with soil, and give the plant
some water so it will take to the new soil.

## Critical Rules

- Never lift the plant by the stem. Even though the stem may look safe
  and strong, this nearly always causes problems down the line.
- Always maintain a firm grip on the soil when transplanting.
- Some people like to clean the roots during transplant, but this is not
  recommended for cannabis.
- If your soil is very compact, you may be able to turn the pot upside
  down and tap the whole medium out as one solid mass.

## Transplant Shock

During some transplants the cannabis plant may go into shock, even if the
transplant was clean and perfect. Transplant shock is caused by a
disturbance of the roots. If the roots are cut or fall away during the
process, the plant does not respond well.

Key recovery strategies include:

- If the plant has been well maintained it should survive the shock.
  Poorly cared for plants may fail quickly.
- Refrain from feeding for at least one week after transplanting.
- Transplant feeding products that work as hormones are available and
  may help the plant recover.
- Plants usually take about two weeks to get back to full health after
  a significant transplant shock event.

## Root-Bound (Pot-Bound) Condition

A root-bound condition exists when a potted plant has outgrown its
container. The entire root mass grows to its maximum capacity within the
pot, which causes stress and a variety of secondary problems including
[[hamilton-nutrient-deficiencies-and-treatment]], stunted growth, and general decline.

The only cure for a root-bound plant is a bigger pot. This underscores the
importance of proactive potting-up before the plant shows severe symptoms.
