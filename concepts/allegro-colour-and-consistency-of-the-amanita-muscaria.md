# Colour and Consistency of the Amanita Muscaria

Allegro argues that the distinctive colouring of the Amanita muscaria — the deep red cap contrasting with the white stem and the white or yellowish "warts" from the broken volva — is the key to identifying it as the specific subject of the [[allegro-the-sacred-mushroom-amanita-identification]] cult, traceable through a vast web of nomenclature, mythology, and biblical allusion.

## The Golden Fleece: Red Cloak of the Mushroom

The most famous classical myth derived from the mushroom's colour is the quest for the Golden Fleece by Jason and the Argonauts. By "golden" we must think of red gold most common in the ancient world. The ram's hair was of great importance for weaving. In Sumerian the same word DARA means both "ram" and "hair dyed red." From the reversed combination DARA-SIG came Greek thrix, "hair," properly "red hair." Dionysus was a Thracian god, and his frantic Maenads were called Threiciae — referring not primarily to Thrace but to the "red-cloaked" Amanita muscaria.

## Jacob and Esau: Stem and Canopy

The story of smooth-skinned Jacob tricking his red, rough-skinned brother Esau out of his birthright is a presentation of the "red-cloaked" mushroom theme. Esau's name derives from Sumerian "raised canopy," a fitting epithet for the cap of the Amanita muscaria. His brother Jacob (Sumerian "pillar") represents the mushroom stem. At their birth: "the first came forth all red, all his body like a hairy mantle" (Gen 25:25) — a perfect description of the red, white-flecked mushroom cap.

## The Amanita Name: "Breast-Shaped"

The Greek name Amanita derives from Sumerian AGAN, "breast," referring to the hemispherical cap. The Sumerian word GAN means a cone or hemispherical shape, the lid of a bowl, or a woman's breast. The Hebrew word for the red cap on ewes (khinun) derives from GAN-NU, "red dye" — itself referring to the red top of the Amanita. Such a red cap described the canopy of the sacred fungus perfectly.

## Purple: The Colour of the Cap

The striking colour of the Amanita cap gave its name to red or purple dyes. The Greek phoinix ("Phoenix"), name of the palm tree, the bird, and the Levantine coast, derives from a Sumerian phrase meaning "mighty man holding up the sky." The Latin tablion, denoting the purple fringe of authority, derives from Sumerian "double-cone," the two halves of the split mushroom volva. The Greek KUR-KUR, a name of the Holy Plant, means "two cones."

## Bdellium and the Panther

The Hebrew bedolakh (Latin Bdellium), included among the sources of comparison for the heaven-sent Manna (Num 11:7), has a Sumerian origin meaning "spotted, variegated skin." Pliny says Bdellium contains "a number of white spots, like fingernails" — matching the mushroom's appearance. The Greek panther derives from Sumerian BAR-DARA, "spotted, variegated skin," describing the Amanita's characteristic markings and also the closely related Amanita pantherina.

## The Paideros: Opal and "Beloved Son"

The stone Opal (Latin Opalus) is related to the mushroom through its distinctive colouring. The Greeks called it Paideros, meaning "red and white spotted skin." Pliny assumed it derived from pais ("boy, son") and erotis ("beloved"), connecting it to paiderastia. The New Testament plays on this understanding when the voice at Jesus' baptism declares "this is my son, the beloved" (Matt 3:17) — precisely the false etymology of Paideros.

Similarly, "the disciple whom Jesus loved" (pais-erotis) is the Paideros — "red and white spotted skin." At the Last Supper, the "dipping" of the morsel given to Judas plays on TAB-BA-LI, the "double-cone" mushroom name, and the Semitic root "dip, dye."

## The "Scabby One" and Leprosy

The Arabs call the mushroom "the scabby one." The peeling, wart-like surface of the Amanita reminded the myth-makers of leprosy and skin diseases. Isaiah warns the "daughters of Zion" engaged in witchcraft that "the Lord will make your crowns scabby" (Isa 3:17). The prophet puns on justice (mishpat) and fungi/scabs (mispakh): "I looked for justice, but behold, bloodshed; for righteousness, but behold, a cry!" becomes "I planted you as a vine... and hoped to reap justice, but received fungi (scabs)" (Isa 5:7).

## Lazarus and Lapis Lazuli

The name Lazarus plays on "Lazuli," the blue mineral containing flecks of gold, "tinged with purple." The Persian form traces to Sumerian "brightly shining variegated (stone)." The writer added the "scabby, ulcerous" appearance given by the warty surface of the cap: "moreover, the dogs came and licked his sores" (Luke 16:21). Even the name "Barnabas" ("Son of Consolation") derives from the Sumerian ZA-GIN ("flecked stone"), source of "sapphire" and the Aramaic nabusa ("red woolly caterpillar"), connected to the "red flecked with white" motif.
