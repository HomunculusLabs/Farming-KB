## Bokashi Fermentation

Bokashi is an anaerobic fermentation process that preserves organic waste using effective microorganisms. Unlike composting, bokashi uses lactic acid bacteria and yeast to pickle food scraps and organic matter. The result is pre-digested material that breaks down rapidly when buried in soil.

## What Bokashi Is (and Isn't)

- **IS**: Anaerobic lactic acid fermentation (like making sauerkraut)
- **IS NOT**: Aerobic thermal composting — no heat, no rotting
- **Preserves** all nutrients (nothing is lost to off-gassing or leaching)
- **Processes** ALL food scraps including meat, dairy, bones, citrus, onions
- **Works** in any temperature (even freezing — fermentation just slows)

## Making Bokashi Bran (Inoculant)

### Materials
- Wheat bran or rice bran: 10 kg
- [[effective-microorganisms-em]] (EM-A activated): 200ml
- Unsulfured molasses: 200ml
- Chlorine-free water: 2-3 liters
- Large tarp for mixing

### Process
1. Dissolve molasses in warm water
2. Add EM-A to the molasses water
3. Spread bran on tarp
4. Sprinkle EM solution evenly over bran while mixing
5. Moisture target: 40-50% (squeeze test: clumps but no drip)
6. Pack tightly into airtight bags or buckets (press out all air)
7. Ferment at room temperature (70-100F ideal) for 7-14 days
8. Ready when sweet-sour fermented smell, white mold on surface OK
9. Dry in shade and store, or use immediately

## Using a Bokashi Bin (Kitchen System)

### Setup
- Two bokashi bins (rotate between them)
- Bokashi bran (store in airtight container)
- Kitchen scraps of all kinds

### Daily Process
1. Add food scraps to bin, chopping large pieces
2. Press down firmly to remove air pockets
3. Sprinkle thin layer of bokashi bran over scraps
4. Seal bin airtight (drain spigot if available)
5. Drain any liquid (bokashi tea) every 2-3 days

### Bokashi Tea (Leachate)
- Dilute 1:100 for soil drench — excellent microbe boost
- Dilute 1:500 for foliar spray
- Use undiluted in drains (prevents clogs, controls odor)
- Use within 24 hours — bacteria die off quickly

### Completion
- Ferment 2-4 weeks (bin is full)
- Ready when: white mold present, sweet-pickled smell, food structure preserved
- If it smells rotten, add more bran and reseal

## Burial (Final Composting)

Bokashi doesn't finish composting — it pre-digests. The final step is burial:

1. Dig trench 8-12 inches deep in garden bed
2. Spread bokashi material in trench (don't layer too thick — max 2-3 inches)
3. Cover with 6+ inches of soil
4. Material decomposes into humus in 2-4 weeks (faster in warm soil)
5. Plant directly over buried bokashi after 2 weeks

## Bokashi for Living Soil Beds

- Excellent pre-planting amendment for [[cannabis-living-soil-guide]]
- Can inoculate bokashi with [[indigenous-microorganisms-imo]] instead of commercial EM
- Fermented material adds both organic matter and lactic acid bacteria
- Combine with [[vermicomposting]] and compost for complete soil biology
- Works well in [[no-till-gardening-method]] systems as layered amendment

## Tips

- More bran = faster fermentation (but don't overdo it)
- The tighter packed, the better — anaerobic conditions are essential
- Any color of mold except black/green is acceptable (white, yellow, orange = OK)
- Citrus, onions, and garlic work fine in bokashi (they don't in aerobic compost)
- Meat and bones break down completely — chop bones small
- Two-bin system means one is always fermenting while the other fills
