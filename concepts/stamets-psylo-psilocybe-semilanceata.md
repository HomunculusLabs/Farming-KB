    10|# [[psilocybe-semilanceata-profile]]
    11|
    12|**Authority**: (Fries) Kummer
    13|
    14|**Common Name**: Liberty cap
    15|
    16|## Description
    17|
    18|The liberty cap is perhaps the most iconic psilocybin mushroom and one of
    19|the most common and easily identified active Psilocybes in temperate
    20|regions. Although petite in size, it is one of the more potent
    21|psilocybin-producing mushrooms known. P. semilanceata is to temperate
    22|grasslands what Psilocybe mexicana is to subtropical grasslands.
    23|
    24|### Cap (Pileus)
    25|
    26|0.5-2.5 cm broad. Conic to obtusely conic to conic-campanulate to
    27|campanulate with an acute umbo. Margin translucent-striate, incurved and
    28|sometimes undulated in young fruiting bodies, often darkened by spores.
    29|Color variable, extremely hygrophanous. Usually dark chestnut brown when
    30|moist, soon drying to a light tan or yellow, occasionally with an olive
    31|tint. Surface viscid when moist from a separable gelatinous pellicle.
    32|The sharp umbo is a key identification feature.
    33|
    34|### Gills (Lamellae)
    35|
    36|Attachment mostly adnexed, close to crowded, narrow. Color pallid at
    37|first, rapidly becoming brownish and finally purplish brown with the
    38|edges remaining pallid. Spore print dark purplish brown.
    39|
    40|### Stem (Stipe)
    41|
    42|40-100 mm long by 0.75-2 (3) mm thick. Slender, equal, flexuous, and
    43|pliant. Pallid to more brownish towards the base, where the attached
    44|mycelium may become bluish tinged, especially during drying. Surface
    45|smooth overall. Context stuffed with a fibrous pith.
    46|
    47|### Veil
    48|
    49|Partial veil thinly cortinate, rapidly deteriorating, leaving an obscure
    50|evanescent annular zone of fibrils, usually darkened by spores. Often
    51|entirely absent. This is a key feature: liberty caps typically lack a
    52|visible ring.
    53|
    54|## Microscopy
    55|
    56|- **Spores**: Dark purplish brown in deposit, ellipsoid, 12-14 by 7-8 um
    57|- **Basidia**: 4-spored
    58|- **Pleurocystidia**: Few to absent
    59|- **Cheilocystidia**: 18-35 by 4.5-8 um, lageniform with an extended
    60|  and flexuous neck, often forked
    61|
    62|## Habitat
    63|
    64|Scattered to gregarious in the fall in pastures, fields, lawns, or other
    65|grassy areas, especially rich grasslands grazed by sheep and cows. This
    66|mushroom is especially abundant in and about clumps of sedge grass in the
    67|damper zones of fields. The close relationship of P. semilanceata to the
    68|rhizomes of grasses has been demonstrated ecologically (Keay and Brown
    69|1990). Not found directly on dung.
    70|
    71|## Distribution
    72|
    73|West of the Cascades from northern California to British Columbia in fall
    74|to early winter, and to a lesser degree in the spring along coastal areas
    75|of Oregon and Washington. Europe (France, Holland, Italy, Norway,
    76|Switzerland), South Africa, Chile, northern India, Australia, Tasmania.
    77|First reported from New Zealand by Guzman, Bandala, and King (1993), from
    78|high-altitude grasslands of the South Island.
    79|
    80|## Season
    81|
    82|Late summer through late fall in northern latitudes. Spring fruiting
    83|along coastal Pacific Northwest. May in New Zealand.
    84|
    85|## Dosage and Potency
    86|
    87|Rating: Moderately active to extremely potent. This species has the
    88|highest psilocybin content yet reported:
    89|
    90|- Gartz (1993): Average 1% psilocybin, range 0.2-2.37% (highest ever
    91|  reported)
    92|- Cultivated: maxima of 1.12% psilocybin, no psilocin, 0.21% baeocystin
    93|- Gartz (1994): Up to 0.98% psilocybin
    94|- Stijve and Kuyper (1985): One specimen at 1.7%
    95|- Baeocystin: 0.36% (relatively high)
    96|- Christiansen et al. (1981): Variation of 0.17-1.96% in dried
    97|  specimens, youngest specimens most potent
    98|
    99|The high psilocybin and low psilocin accounts for long storage life.
   100|Psilocybin content is not adversely affected by the drying process (Gartz
   101|1986a).
   102|
   103|Important: Since P. semilanceata is low in psilocin, a co-indicator of
   104|bluing, it rarely bruises bluish. The strength of the bluing reaction is
   105|NOT an indication of activity in this species, unlike most potent species.
   106|
   107|## Commentary
   108|
   109|Several distinct varieties can be encountered. The archetypal variety is
   110|easy to recognize macroscopically. One unusually large form with a
   111|pronounced incurved margin and contorted bell-shaped cap occasionally
   112|surfaces late in the season in the Pacific Northwest. Perhaps the most
   113|unusual form looks dangerously close to a Galerina: a denuded, nearly
   114|sporeless, orange form where the color of the overlying translucent flesh
   115|shows yellowish orange instead of its normal purplish brown.
   116|
   117|Woodland Galerinas can coexist in the same habitat as grassland Psilocybes,
   118|especially in lands recently converted to pastures. Readers should be
   119|aware of this dangerous overlap.

   121|## Similar Species
   122|

   124|  distinctive umbo

   126|- **Psilocybe silvatica**: Conifer woodland species, more yellow in caps
   127|- **Psilocybe subfimetaria**: Related species
   128|- **Galerina species**: Deadly look-alikes in similar habitats; rusty
   129|  brown spores distinguish them
   130|- **Psilocybe samuiensis**: Subtropical equivalent
