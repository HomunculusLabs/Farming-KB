title: Psilocybe semilanceata - Species Profile
tags: [[psychedelic-mushroom-set-and-setting]]

**Authority**: (Fries) Kummer

**Common Name**: Liberty cap

## Description

The liberty cap is perhaps the most iconic psilocybin mushroom and one of
the most common and easily identified active Psilocybes in temperate
regions. Although petite in size, it is one of the more potent
psilocybin-producing mushrooms known. P. semilanceata is to temperate
grasslands what Psilocybe mexicana is to subtropical grasslands.

### Cap (Pileus)

0.5-2.5 cm broad. Conic to obtusely conic to conic-campanulate to
campanulate with an acute umbo. Margin translucent-striate, incurved and
sometimes undulated in young fruiting bodies, often darkened by spores.
Color variable, extremely hygrophanous. Usually dark chestnut brown when
moist, soon drying to a light tan or yellow, occasionally with an olive
tint. Surface viscid when moist from a separable gelatinous pellicle.
The sharp umbo is a key identification feature.

### Gills (Lamellae)

Attachment mostly adnexed, close to crowded, narrow. Color pallid at
first, rapidly becoming brownish and finally purplish brown with the
edges remaining pallid. Spore print dark purplish brown.

### Stem (Stipe)

40-100 mm long by 0.75-2 (3) mm thick. Slender, equal, flexuous, and
pliant. Pallid to more brownish towards the base, where the attached
mycelium may become bluish tinged, especially during drying. Surface
smooth overall. Context stuffed with a fibrous pith.

### Veil

Partial veil thinly cortinate, rapidly deteriorating, leaving an obscure
evanescent annular zone of fibrils, usually darkened by spores. Often
entirely absent. This is a key feature: liberty caps typically lack a
visible ring.

## Microscopy

- **Spores**: Dark purplish brown in deposit, ellipsoid, 12-14 by 7-8 um
- **Basidia**: 4-spored
- **Pleurocystidia**: Few to absent
- **Cheilocystidia**: 18-35 by 4.5-8 um, lageniform with an extended
  and flexuous neck, often forked

## Habitat

Scattered to gregarious in the fall in pastures, fields, lawns, or other
grassy areas, especially rich grasslands grazed by sheep and cows. This
mushroom is especially abundant in and about clumps of sedge grass in the
damper zones of fields. The close relationship of P. semilanceata to the
rhizomes of grasses has been demonstrated ecologically (Keay and Brown
1990). Not found directly on dung.

## Distribution

West of the Cascades from northern California to British Columbia in fall
to early winter, and to a lesser degree in the spring along coastal areas
of Oregon and Washington. Europe (France, Holland, Italy, Norway,
Switzerland), South Africa, Chile, northern India, Australia, Tasmania.
First reported from New Zealand by Guzman, Bandala, and King (1993), from
high-altitude grasslands of the South Island.

## Season

Late summer through late fall in northern latitudes. Spring fruiting
along coastal Pacific Northwest. May in New Zealand.

## Dosage and Potency

Rating: Moderately active to extremely potent. This species has the
highest psilocybin content yet reported:

- Gartz (1993): Average 1% psilocybin, range 0.2-2.37% (highest ever
  reported)
- Cultivated: maxima of 1.12% psilocybin, no psilocin, 0.21% baeocystin
- Gartz (1994): Up to 0.98% psilocybin
- Stijve and Kuyper (1985): One specimen at 1.7%
- Baeocystin: 0.36% (relatively high)
- Christiansen et al. (1981): Variation of 0.17-1.96% in dried
  specimens, youngest specimens most potent

The high psilocybin and low psilocin accounts for long storage life.
Psilocybin content is not adversely affected by the drying process (Gartz
1986a).

Important: Since P. semilanceata is low in psilocin, a co-indicator of
bluing, it rarely bruises bluish. The strength of the bluing reaction is
NOT an indication of activity in this species, unlike most potent species.

## Commentary

Several distinct varieties can be encountered. The archetypal variety is
easy to recognize macroscopically. One unusually large form with a
pronounced incurved margin and contorted bell-shaped cap occasionally
surfaces late in the season in the Pacific Northwest. Perhaps the most
unusual form looks dangerously close to a Galerina: a denuded, nearly
sporeless, orange form where the color of the overlying translucent flesh
shows yellowish orange instead of its normal purplish brown.

Woodland Galerinas can coexist in the same habitat as grassland Psilocybes,
especially in lands recently converted to pastures. Readers should be
aware of this dangerous overlap.
