## DMT as an Endogenous Compound

DMT is present in small amounts in the human brain, a fact that distinguishes
it from virtually all other classic hallucinogens. LSD, mescaline, and
psilocybin are all exogenous compounds with no known natural presence in human
neurochemistry. DMT, by contrast, is synthesized endogenously from
tryptophan through the enzymatic pathways that also produce serotonin and
melatonin. Its presence in the brain suggests that the neural machinery for
processing this molecule already exists as part of normal neurochemistry,
even if the concentrations required for hallucinogenic effects are never
reached under ordinary circumstances.

Psilocybin is 4-phosphoraloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine, while serotonin is
5-hydroxytryptamine. The structural similarity is striking: both are
substituted [[tryptamine]] molecules differing only in the functional group
attached to the indole ring. McKenna emphasized this relationship as central
to understanding the quality of the [[leary-psychedelic-experience-bardo-mapping]], arguing that
the brain's familiarity with the tryptamine skeleton is what allows these
compounds to produce experiences that feel "real" rather than artificial or
pharmacologically imposed.

## The Significance of Rapid Onset

When DMT is smoked, the onset of effects begins in approximately fifteen
seconds, with full breakthrough occurring within thirty seconds. The entire
experience lasts roughly five minutes. This pharmacokinetic profile is
dramatically faster than that of any other major hallucinogen. LSD takes
thirty to ninety minutes to reach peak effects and lasts eight to twelve
hours. Psilocybin takes twenty to forty minutes to reach peak effects and
lasts four to six hours.

McKenna interpreted the rapid onset as evidence that "the brain is
absolutely at home with this compound." The speed with which DMT produces
its effects suggests that no novel metabolic processing is required. The
molecule enters the brain, binds to its target receptors, and produces
its effects immediately because the receptor sites, signal transduction
pathways, and downstream neural circuits are all already configured to
respond to tryptamine-based signaling. In pharmacological terms, DMT has
a very high "receptor on-rate," meaning it binds quickly and efficiently to
its targets.

The contrast with LSD is instructive. LSD is retained in the body for an
extended period, and its effects build gradually. McKenna suggested that
this extended duration reflects the brain's relative unfamiliarity with the
[[lysergic-acid]] scaffold, requiring more complex metabolic processing and
producing a qualitatively different type of experience. The DMT experience,
by contrast, is immediate and total because the brain already knows how to
handle this molecule.

## Subjective Familiarity and Authenticity

Users of DMT consistently report that the experience has a quality of
authenticity or reality that distinguishes it from experiences produced by
synthetic hallucinogens. McKenna argued that this subjective quality of
familiarity is a direct consequence of DMT's endogenous status. The brain
does not interpret DMT as a foreign substance imposing artificial patterns
on neural activity. Instead, it processes DMT as an amplified version of
a signaling modality that is already part of normal neurochemistry.

This interpretation is supported by the observation that the experiential
content of DMT states, while extraordinary in intensity, often has a
quality of recognition or familiarity that is absent from experiences
produced by compounds with no endogenous analog. McKenna described
