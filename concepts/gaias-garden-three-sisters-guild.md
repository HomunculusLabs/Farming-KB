## Yield Advantages

Together, the Three Sisters produce more food, with less water and fertilizer, than a similar area planted to any one of these three crops in isolation. Research by agronomist Jane Mt. Pleasant at Cornell University, who blended her Iroquois heritage with her research, has shown that yields of this polyculture, measured in calories, are about 20 percent higher than corn grown alone in an equal-sized plot. This calorie advantage comes from the complementary nutritional profiles: corn provides carbohydrates, beans supply protein, and squash adds vitamins and oils.

## The Fourth Sister

In the Southwest, a fourth "sister" is traditionally found in the guild: Rocky Mountain bee plant (Cleome serrulata). Often growing near former Anasazi settlements, it is virtually an indicator plant for ancient ruins.
