# Alternatives to Political Systems

## Overview

Current systems of government are based on self-interest, economic pragmatism, belief, impractical theory, and power-centred minorities --- whether religious, military, capitalist, communist, familial, or criminal. Almost all such groups establish competitive and adversary-oriented systems. The [[permaculture]] framework proposes replacing power-centred politics and political hierarchies with a far more flexible, practical, and information-centred system responsive to research and feedback, with long-term goals of stability.

## The Problem with Adversarial Politics

The world needs a new, non-polarised, and non-contentious politic --- one not made possible by those in situations that promote left-right, black-white, capitalist-communist, believer-infidel thinking. Such systems, whether intentional or not, promote antagonism and destroy cooperation and interdependence. Confrontational thinking, operating through political or power systems, has destroyed cultural, intellectual, and material resources that could have been used, in a life-centred ethic, for earth repair.

## Creative Use of Difference

It is always possible to agree with most people of any race or creed on the basics of life-centred ethics and common-sense procedures across all cultural groups. It matters not that one group eats beef and another regards cows as holy, providing they agree to cooperate in areas of mutual concern and respect the origins of their differences as a chance of history and evolution --- not assessing differences as due to personal perversity.

Differences can be used creatively and designed for, rather than eliminating one group or another as infidels. Belief is not so much a difference as a refusal to admit the existence of differences; this easily transposes into the antagonistic attitude of "who is not with me is against me" --- a coercive and illogical attitude likely to classify all others as enemies when they are merely living according to their own history and needs.

Most human communities function in relation to long-term sustainability precisely because they differ from others. What is possible for an Inuit is not possible for a forest pygmy. It is not differences themselves that are important; it is how all groups relate to the basic rules of the local ecology that permits them to function on a long-term basis. Belief, like religion, is a basically private and non-global characteristic and should not be subject to comparisons.

## Failure of Current Political Systems

It has long been apparent that current political, economic, and land-use systems cannot solve long-term and worsening problems such as soil degradation, groundwater pollution, forest decline, the spread of poverty, unemployment, and malnutrition. Despite good scientific prognoses and assessments, effective ground strategies are lacking. The temporary nature of political systems is an impediment to effective action.

All Western political systems can be described as those of competing belief elites --- whether self-described as communist, socialist, capitalist, or democratic, they all function in ways that are essentially short-term. By their nature, political systems seek to impose policy control over as wide an area of influence as possible, are power-centred rather than life-centred, and are often composed of very few families or one family. The continuing and long-sustained programmes necessary to reverse forest loss and soil decline are usually sacrificed for the short-term policies of an elite maintaining power.

## Personal Action as Political Change

The argument for simplicity is never a political argument --- when people practice it in their lives, they don't even need any politics. This statement also refers to the adoption of an [[ethical-basis-of-alternative-nation]] to action, to the placement of money and resources, and to the determination to act in accordance with one's beliefs. All of these can occur independently of political change and can be lifelong personal actions of great effect.

People can act independently of political theory, which rarely covers questions of ethics, simplicity, local autonomy, or life-oriented action. Such changes come about through education and information. When enough people change, political systems may follow --- or become as irrelevant as they now appear to be in terms of real solutions. The place to start change is first with the individual (oneself), and second in one's region or neighbourhood.

## The Right Not to Be in Debt

Some of the most charming and climatically appropriate houses on earth are built without bank loans, architects, metals, concrete, or contractors. They are built in areas where trade unions, building surveyors, health officials, and local or state governments do not impede the home builder or the community providing shelter for themselves.

Stone, mud, bamboo, round timbers, rope, thatch, and even baked brick and tiles are the age-old durable [[gadd-fungal-biodegradation-concrete-building-materials]] of humankind. All can be locally produced if energy from community forests and people is provided. Even cement and mortar can be made using kilns fired by wood, as can pottery, bricks, and roof tiles. None of this needs money if people work together.

The real cause of a lack of shelter in any country is not finance but restrictive practices by a regulatory bureaucracy. State or private ownership of forests, small mines, and lands is devoted to state or corporate profits, which denies basic biological [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-earthworking-and-earth-resources]] to the people who work to produce or mine them.

## True Basic Rights

In modern societies, basic "rights" are to vote, form unions, protest, or go to law --- effectively supporting professional classes. Truly basic rights to grow or protect forests, build shelter, grow food, or provide water from roof areas are commonly denied by local or state regulations. Effective local group action restores the true basic rights: personal responsibility for sustenance on earth, and responsibility to the earth itself.

While "natural law" demands a fair return for every gift received, the laws of power demand gifts without thought of return --- this is called "economic growth" and means unlimited resource exploitation and the concomitant exploitation of people. The wealth of any area lies not in banks or cities, but in those basic resources, skills, and natural systems developed by its peoples.

## Political Affiliations

There are two ways to ensure political changes that bring ecological improvements. The first is to mobilise ground support in every electorate where a candidate of any party takes a stand on good ecology or against nuclear and polluting industry. The second is to form a local Ecological or [[green-party-movement-mollison]], or a bioregional group.

A common policy statement sums up the skills of all groups and leaves room for local issues while providing strong guiding principles. The broad aims of such a party are ecological, socially egalitarian, grassroots democratic, and non-growth.

## Evolving Policy

A common global policy starts with a general ethic and proceeds to specific policies for specific cultures, regions, and landscapes. The structure for gathering common policy follows these steps:

1. Define the problem or concern, weighting priorities on a scale from 1 to 10
2. State the intent of policy for the region --- what it is intended to achieve
3. Collect strategies that have been proven to work --- a set of case histories
4. Frame policies based on all successful strategies
5. Set policy priorities weighted for urgency, public cost, general spread, long-term effect, and threats to basic resources or life systems

The emphasis should be on broad principles rather than purely local or trivial matters, unless presented as a case history applicable to a broad principle.

## Philosophical Foundations
These concepts draw on indigenous wisdom, ecological ethics, and
systems thinking to propose alternative governance and social structures
that work with natural processes rather than against them.

## Practical Applications
Community-level implementation involves consensus decision-making,
[[mycelial-network-resource-sharing-plants]] agreements, and long-term stewardship planning.
These approaches emphasize local self-reliance and ecological integrity.

## Related Movements
These ideas connect to broader movements including bioregionalism,
ecological economics, and indigenous sovereignty frameworks.
