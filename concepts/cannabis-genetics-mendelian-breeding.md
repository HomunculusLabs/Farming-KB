# Cannabis Genetics and Mendelian Breeding

All information transmitted from generation to generation is contained in the pollen of the staminate parent and the ovule of the pistillate parent. Individual plants have two identical sets of genes (2n) in every cell except the gametes. In Cannabis, the haploid (n) number of chromosomes is 10 and the diploid (2n) number is 20.

## Basic Mendelian Genetics

### Dominance and Recessiveness

If cross-pollination of two plants with a shared genetic trait results in offspring that all exhibit the same trait, the strain is "true-breeding" for that trait. When a cross produces offspring that do not breed true, the parents have genes that segregate or are hybrid.

Example: Crossing a true-breeding webbed-leaf strain (ww) with a true-breeding compound-pinnate leaf strain (WW) produces 100% compound-pinnate offspring. The W gene masks expression of the w gene; W is dominant and w is recessive. Crossing two F1 offspring produces approximately a 3:1 ratio in the F2 generation (three compound-pinnate to one webbed).

### Mendel's First Law

Each of the genes in a related pair segregate from each other during gamete formation.

### Back-Crossing

Crossing one of the F1 progeny back to one of the true-breeding P1 parents produces a 1:1 ratio of phenotypes, proving the parental genotypes. The back-cross phenotype is a direct representation of the four possible gametes produced by the F1 hybrid.

### Mendel's Second Law (Polyhybrid Crosses)

Unrelated pairs of genes are inherited independently of each other. For two independently assorting gene pairs with complete dominance, the F2 generation shows a 9:3:3:1 ratio of four phenotypes. With incomplete dominance for both pairs, nine phenotypes appear in a 1:2:1:2:4:2:1:2:1 ratio. Recombination creates phenotypes that differ from both parents and grandparents.

### Gene Interaction

When two or more gene pairs control a single trait, genotype ratios remain the same but phenotype ratios are altered. Example: Two dominant gene pairs Pp and Cc controlling anthocyanin pigmentation in Cannabis. If P is present alone, only leaves exhibit purple color. If C is present alone, the plant remains green. If both are present, calyxes also turn purple. The phenotype ratio becomes 9:3:4 instead of the expected 9:3:3:1.

## Summary of Essential Breeding Points

1. Genotypes are controlled by genes passed on unchanged from generation to generation.
2. Genes occur in pairs, one from each parent.
3. When gene pair members differ in effect, the plant is hybrid or heterozygous.
4. When gene pair members are equal in effect, the plant is true-breeding or homozygous.
5. Pairs of genes controlling different traits are (usually) inherited independently.
6. Dominance relations and gene interaction can alter phenotypic ratios.

## Polyploidy

Polyploidy is the condition of multiple sets of chromosomes within one cell. Cannabis has 20 chromosomes in the diploid (2n) condition. Triploid (3n) and tetraploid (4n) individuals are termed polyploids. Polyploidy does not occur naturally in Cannabis but may be induced with colchicine.

### Colchicine Treatment

Colchicine is a poisonous compound extracted from Colchicum species roots. It inhibits chromosome segregation and cell wall formation, resulting in larger cells with multiple chromosome sets. Seed treatment is the most effective and safest application method.

### Effects of Polyploidy

**Tetraploid (4n) Cannabis:**
- Height often exceeds diploid plants by 25-30%
- Intensely colored with dark green leaves and stems
- Vegetative growth cycles extended by 30-40%
- Increased height and vigorous growth vanish in subsequent generations
- Plants often revert to diploid condition

**Triploid (3n) Cannabis:**
- Formed by crossing tetraploids with diploids
- Proved inferior to both diploids and tetraploids in many cases

### Cannabinoid Effects

De Pasquale et al. (1979) treated Cannabis with 0.25% and 0.50% colchicine solutions at the primary meristem. Results showed:
- 166-250% increase in THC compared to controls
- 30-33% decrease in CBD
- 39-65% decrease in CBN
- In plants with deformed leaf lamina, 90% of cells were tetraploid (4n=40)

### Aneuploids

The transformation to tetraploid inevitably produces some plants with unbalanced chromosome sets (2n+1, 2n-1, etc.). Aneuploid Cannabis is characterized by extremely small seeds (7-9 grams per 1,000 seeds vs. 14-19 grams for normal diploid plants) and is inferior to polyploids in every economic respect.

### Practical Considerations

- Colchicine is nearly always lethal to Cannabis seeds; the line between polyploidy and death is very fine
- If 1,000 viable treated seeds give rise to only 3 surviving plants, those 3 are very likely polyploid
- Frequent testing is needed to determine if ploidy is changing in subsequent generations
- Greenhouses and artificial lighting can extend the season for testing polyploid strains
