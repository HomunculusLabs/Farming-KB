### AIDS-Related Anorexia

AIDS-related anorexia is defined as a lack or loss of appetite leading to weight loss of both fat and muscle tissues due to appetite-depressing disease progression. HIV/AIDS directly affects metabolism and can cause secondary infections that further compromise nutritional status. The wasting associated with AIDS was one of the earliest recognized indications for medical cannabis.

## Endocannabinoid Mechanisms in Appetite Regulation

Research has established that endocannabinoid CB1 receptors play a central role in the regulation of eating behavior. An animal study conducted at the University Complutense of Madrid in 2002 provided initial evidence that peripheral CB1 receptors modulate feeding behavior. Subsequent research at Columbia University in New York confirmed that reducing CB1 receptor expression produces a measurable reduction in appetite, suggesting that CB1 blockers could be explored as weight-loss products, and conversely, that CB1 agonists stimulate eating.

### THC in Cancer-Related Anorexia

A study at the Cleveland Clinic Foundation in Ohio evaluated the impact of THC on cancer patients with anorexia. Patients received 2.5 mg of THC orally, three times per day, one hour after meals for four weeks. Of the 18 patients who completed the course, 13 reported an increase in appetite. The study concluded: "THC is an effective appetite stimulant in patients with advanced cancer. It is well tolerated at low doses."

### Nabilone for Cancer Symptoms

Scientists at the William Osler Health Center in Toronto, Canada, collected data from 82 cancer patients who received Nabilone for 53 days for cancer-related pain and other symptoms. Results indicated potential therapeutic benefit for cancer-related anorexia with improvements seen in pain levels, nausea, depression, anxiety, insomnia, and night sweats.

### Cannabis in Hepatitis C Treatment

Researchers at the University of Ottawa hypothesized that cannabinoids might mitigate the severe side effects of antiviral therapy used to treat hepatitis C. In a 2009 study, 64% of patients undergoing interferon-ribavirin therapy experienced symptom improvements when also administered cannabinoids, suggesting a role for cannabis in supporting treatment adherence for difficult antiviral regimens.

### Mixed Results in Cancer Anorexia

A 2006 Swiss study at Cantonal Hospital in St. Gallen divided 164 cancer patients into three groups receiving a six-week course of cannabis extract (2.5 mg THC and 1 mg CBD), oral THC alone, or placebo. This randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial found no significant difference in appetite or quality of life between the groups. Importantly, no reports of toxicity were noted.
