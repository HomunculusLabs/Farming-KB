## The No-Compost Approach

"It is by no means necessary for a high-yield garden to have a compost heap." Mulching throughout the year and the skilful use of polycultures mean that additional organic fertiliser is not necessary.

### Why Holzer Avoids Conventional Composting
- Bare areas of soil are left defenceless against environmental effects
- The "tidy garden" aesthetic requires constant weeding, watering, and manuring
- Watering washes away nutrients, creating a vicious cycle requiring more manure
- Obsession with tidiness undermines soil health

## Alternative Composting Method: Raised Bed Trench Composting

For those who do want to compost, Holzer describes an unconventional, easy-to-maintain method:

### Construction
1. Build two [[cervantes-raised-beds-mound-planting]] running parallel, positioned as close together as you can walk between them
2. Build beds at as steep an angle as possible while still holding together (60-70 degrees)
3. Leave organic waste between the two beds each day
4. Each time, cover waste with a spade's worth of earth, straw, leaves, or similar material
5. Gradually fill until organic material reaches 60% of raised bed height
6. Cover top layer with earth and plant with vigorous vegetables (pumpkins, cucumbers, turnips)
7. Start at one end; work toward the other over the course of a year
8. Next year, begin on the opposite side
9. Throw the high-quality compost from the previous year onto the beds with a spade

### Materials
Any organic material that decomposes is suitable:
- Grass clippings
- Chipped material
- Leaves
- Hay and straw
- Algae or mud from pond
- Kitchen waste
- Cardboard

### Principles
- Smaller material + more active soil life = faster humus production
- Space between raised beds is protected from drying out and retains heat
- Plants on beds chosen so compost gets enough light but is protected from full sun
- Partial shade creates optimal decomposition conditions
- Earthworms breed abundantly -- be careful when digging

## Mulching

"Mulching is nothing other than surface composting -- it involves spreading a layer of organic material over the soil to serve as [[fukuoka-citrus-orchard-ground-cover-cultivation]]."

### Functions
- Protective cover preventing drying, erosion, and extreme weather effects
- Constant decomposition process turns mulch into high-quality fertiliser
- Oxygen reaches soil through loose layer
- Prevents unwanted plant growth
- Earthworms thrive underneath

### Materials
- Leaves
- Straw
- Cardboard
- Plants pulled up while weeding
- [[comparison-green-manure-guide-vs-mulch-types-and-effects]] plants (clover, lupins, mustard) are particularly good

### Application Thickness
- Moist/wet material: spread thinly to rot slowly without mouldering
- Dry material (straw, hay): can be spread 20cm+ because it is looser with better [[stamets-growing-room-air-circulation-co2-management]]
- Never pack down tightly

### Against Shredding
Holzer does not shred mulch material despite expert advice. Reasons:
- Unshredded material releases nutrients more slowly
- Mulch layer less likely to compact
- Better for long-term soil building

### Working With Mulch
- In spring, scoop mulch aside to sow or plant
- Sown areas remain free of rivals; other areas stay protected
- With good [[lowenfels-mulch-cover-cropping]], hardly any weeding needed
