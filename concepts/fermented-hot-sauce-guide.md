# Fermented Hot Sauce Guide

Lacto-fermented hot sauce develops complex flavor through controlled bacterial fermentation. Unlike vinegar-based hot sauces, fermented sauces use salt and time to encourage Lactobacillus growth, producing lactic acid that preserves and flavors the peppers.

## Lacto-Fermentation Basics

Lacto-fermentation relies on Lactobacillus bacteria naturally present on produce. In a salty anaerobic environment, these bacteria convert sugars into lactic acid, lowering pH below 4.6 -- the threshold for safe preservation.

Key principles:
- Salt concentration: 2-3% of total weight (peppers + water)
- Anaerobic environment: submerge peppers below brine
- Temperature: 65-75F (18-24C) ideal
- Time: 1-4 weeks depending on flavor preference
- Airlock or burping daily to release CO2

## Pepper Selection

Common varieties by heat level (Scoville Heat Units):
- Mild: Anaheim (500-2,500), Poblano (1,000-2,000), Jalapeno (2,500-8,000)
- Medium: Serrano (10,000-23,000), Cayenne (30,000-50,000)
- Hot: Habanero (100,000-350,000), Scotch Bonnet (100,000-350,000)
- Extreme: Carolina Reaper (1,500,000-2,200,000), Ghost Pepper (800,000-1,000,000)

Blend varieties for complex flavor. A typical mix: 60% mild/medium for bulk and sweetness, 30% hot for kick, 10% extreme for heat.

## Pepper Mash Method

The mash method produces concentrated, restaurant-quality hot sauce.

### Ingredients
- 2 lbs fresh peppers (stems removed, keep seeds)
- 2-3% kosher salt by total weight (about 15-19g)
- Optional: 2-4 cloves garlic, 1 tbsp coriander seeds, 1 sliced onion

### Process
1. Weigh peppers after destemming
2. Blend peppers (and aromatics) into a rough puree -- do not over-process
3. Weigh the puree, calculate salt at 2.5% of total weight
4. Mix salt thoroughly into the puree
5. Pack into a clean fermenting vessel (mason jar, crock, or fermentation vessel)
6. Press down to remove air pockets; puree should be submerged in its own juice
7. If insufficient liquid, add a 2.5% salt brine to cover
8. Cover with a lid + airlock, or a loose lid with daily burping
9. Ferment at room temperature (65-75F) for 2-4 weeks
10. Taste weekly; it's ready when flavor is complex and tangy

## Brine Method (Whole Peppers)

For sauces where you want identifiable pepper pieces:

1. Pack whole or halved peppers into a jar
2. Prepare a 3% brine: 30g salt per liter of non-chlorinated water
3. Pour brine over peppers, ensuring full submersion
4. Add a weight (fermentation weight, boiled stone, ziplock with water) to keep peppers down
5. Ferment 2-4 weeks with airlock

## Blending and Bottling

After fermentation:

1. Transfer mash/peppers to a blender
2. Add vinegar (white distilled, apple cider, or rice vinegar) at 10-25% of total volume for additional safety and tang
3. Blend until smooth
4. Strain through a fine mesh sieve or food mill if desired (smooth sauce) or leave chunky
5. Taste and adjust: add more vinegar for tang, salt for seasoning, sugar or honey to balance heat
6. Bottle in sterilized glass bottles

## pH Safety

Critical safety parameters:
- Final pH must be 4.6 or below (test with calibrated pH meter)
- Target pH: 3.4-4.0 for best flavor and safety
- Water-bath can the finished sauce for shelf stability (10 min for half-pints)
- Refrigerated fermented sauce keeps 6-12 months un-canned
- Canned sauce keeps 12-18 months

## Aging

Hot sauce improves with age. Store bottled sauce in a cool, dark place for 1-3 months before consuming. Flavors mellow and integrate. Some producers age sauces 6-12 months.

## Troubleshooting

- White film (kahm yeast): skim off, not harmful but affects flavor
- Mold (fuzzy, colored): discard entire batch -- do not risk
- Soft/rotting peppers: too little salt or not fully submerged
- Too salty: dilute with vinegar and water when blending
- Too thin: reduce with light simmering after blending, or add xanthan gum (0.1%)

## Equipment

- Fermentation vessels (glass, ceramic, or food-grade plastic)
- Airlocks and silicone lids
- pH meter (calibrated)
- Digital kitchen scale
- Immersion blender or standard blender
- Funnel and bottles
- Labels
