# Endorhiza Endophytes

## Overview

Endorhiza endophytes [[bacterial-endophytes-plant-roots]] are bacteria that colonize the interior of plant root
tissue, residing within the endorhiza [[endorhiza-endophyte-bacteria-plant-roots]] — the zone encompassing both the
rhizoplane (root surface) and the root cortex. Unlike rhizosphere bacteria [[endorhiza-endophyte-bacteria-plant-roots]]
that inhabit the soil immediately surrounding roots [[bacterial-endophytes-plant-roots]], endorhiza bacteria have
established themselves within the plant itself, forming intimate associations
that can profoundly influence plant health, growth, and stress tolerance.

## Definition and Distinction from Rhizosphere Bacteria

The term "endorhiza" refers specifically to root-inhabiting microorganisms,
distinguishing them from:

- **Bulk soil microbiota**: Free-living bacteria not directly influenced
  by plant roots
- **Rhizosphere microbiota**: Bacteria in the soil zone directly influenced
  by root exudates, typically 1–2 mm from the root surface
- **Rhizoplane microbiota**: Bacteria attached to the root surface
- **Endorhiza (endophytes)**: Bacteria that have penetrated and colonized
  root tissue itself

This distinction matters because the selective pressures acting on each
community differ dramatically. Bulk soil communities are shaped primarily by
edaphic factors (pH, moisture, nutrients), while endorhiza [[methylophilus-methylotrophic-bacteria-cannabis-endorhiza]] communities face
the additional selective pressure of the host plant's immune system and
biochemical environment.

## Functions of Endorhiza Bacteria

### Growth Promotion

Endorhiza bacteria [[methylophilus-methylotrophic-bacteria-cannabis-endorhiza]] provide phytohormones that directly stimulate plant growth:

- **Indole-3-acetic acid (IAA)**: An auxin promoting root elongation and
  lateral root formation
- **Cytokinins**: Hormones promoting cell division and delaying senescence
- **Gibberellins**: Compounds promoting stem elongation and germination

### Nutrient Acquisition

- **Nitrogen fixation**: Some endophytes convert atmospheric N₂ into
  ammonia the plant can assimilate
- **Phosphate solubilization**: Organic acids and enzymes solubilize
  insoluble phosphates in soil
- **Iron chelation**: Siderophore production captures iron for plant use

### Disease Suppression

- **Antibiotic production**: Antimicrobial compounds inhibit pathogens
- **Induced systemic resistance (ISR)**: Priming the plant's immune
  defenses against future attack
- **Competitive exclusion**: Occupying niche space prevents pathogen
  establishment
- **Enzyme production**: Chitinases and glucanases attack fungal pathogens

### Stress Tolerance

Endorhiza bacteria help plants cope with environmental stresses including
phytotoxicants, heavy metals, drought, and salinity through production of
osmoprotectants and exopolysaccharides.

## Colonization Process

Root colonization by endorhiza bacteria follows a general sequence:

1. **Attraction**: Root exudates attract chemotactic bacteria toward
   the root zone
2. **Attachment**: Bacteria adhere to the rhizoplane using adhesins,
   pili, and exopolysaccharide matrices
3. **Entry**: Bacteria penetrate through natural openings (lateral root
   emergence sites, root tips) or wounds
4. **Establishment**: Bacteria multiply in the root apoplast, often
   forming biofilm-like aggregates
5. **Systemic spread**: Some endophytes move into aboveground tissues
   via the vascular system

## Core Endorhiza Community in Cannabis

Winston et al. (2014) identified a conserved core bacterial community in
the Cannabis endorhiza across multiple cultivars, including:

- **Pseudomonas**: Diverse Gammaproteobacteria genus; many species are
  known plant growth-promoting rhizobacteria (PGPR)
- **Cellvibrio**: Aerobic cellulolytic bacterium assisting in degrading
  plant cell wall material
- **Oxalobacteraceae**: Betaproteobacteria family common in plant-
  associated environments
- **Xanthomonadaceae**: Gammaproteobacteria family
- **Actinomycetales**: Includes Streptomyces known for antibiotic
  production
- **Sphingobacteriales**: Bacteroidetes order

With the exception of Cellvibrio, all prevalent core members are
well-known endophytic bacteria within Gammaproteobacteria and
Alphaproteobacteria, consistent with observations from other plant systems.

## Cultivar Specificity

A key finding in Cannabis microbiome research is that endorhiza communities
are significantly more cultivar-specific than rhizosphere or bulk soil
communities. While soil type determines the available bacterial pool, the
host plant's genotype acts as a selective filter determining which bacteria
successfully colonize root tissue. This suggests different Cannabis cultivars
may benefit from tailored microbial inoculants optimized for their specific
endorhiza preferences.

## Relationship to Terroir

Endorhiza bacteria may contribute to the localized "flavor" or terroir of
crop plants. Just as wine characteristics are influenced by vineyard-specific
microbial communities, Cannabis secondary metabolite profiles may be partially
shaped by distinct endorhiza communities influencing cannabinoid and terpene
production.
