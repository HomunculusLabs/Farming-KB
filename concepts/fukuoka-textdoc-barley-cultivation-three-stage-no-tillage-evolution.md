## Stage Two: Green Manure and Cover Crop Integration

The second stage addressed the fertility and weed competition problems that remained unresolved in the first stage. Fukuoka began integrating cover crops, particularly clover, into his barley-rice rotation. Clover served as a living mulch that fixed nitrogen from the atmosphere, suppressed weeds by competing for light and space, and protected the soil surface from erosion and desiccation.

The timing of clover management became critical. Clover had to be managed so that it did not overwhelm the barley seedlings but was sufficiently established to provide its benefits. Fukuoka learned to allow the clover to grow thickly through the winter and then suppress it in early spring by trampling or mowing just as the barley was beginning its most rapid growth phase.

The suppressed clover then served as a green manure, decomposing on the soil surface and releasing nutrients that fed the growing barley. This stage also involved refinements to the straw mulch technique. Fukuoka discovered that scattering rice straw over the barley field immediately after seeding was essential.
