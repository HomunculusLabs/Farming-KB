# Cob Building Basics Detailed

Cob is a natural [[fungal-building-material-deterioration]] made from subsoil, sand, straw, and water. Mixed by foot on a tarp and applied in thick layers by hand, cob creates monolithic walls that are sculptural, incredibly durable, and made from materials found on most homestead sites. Cob buildings have survived for hundreds of years in England, Yemen, and other regions with wet climates.

## What Is Cob

Cob (from Old English "cumb" meaning lump or mass) is an ancient building technique where a wet mixture of earth, sand, and straw is sculpted into walls. Unlike adobe (formed into bricks and dried) or rammed earth (compacted in forms), cob is built up in layers while wet, creating a continuous monolithic structure with no mortar joints.

## Cob Mix Ratio

The ideal cob mix contains approximately:

- Subsoil: 50-75% (must contain 15-30% clay)
- Sand: 25-50%
- Straw: handfuls per batch (roughly 5-10% by volume)
- Water: enough to make a workable, stiff mix

### Testing Your Soil

**Jar Test:**
1. Fill a clear jar 1/3 full with your subsoil
2. Fill the rest with water and a drop of dish soap
3. Shake vigorously and let settle for 24-48 hours
4. Sand settles in seconds, silt in minutes, clay in hours/days
5. Ideal soil has visible layers of all three

**Ribbon Test:**
1. Moisten a handful of soil to putty consistency
2. Form a ball and squeeze upward into a ribbon
3. If it forms a 2+ inch ribbon before breaking: too much clay, add sand
4. If it crumbles at 1 inch or less: too little clay, add clay
5. Ideal: ribbon of 1-2 inches before breaking

**Dry Brick Test:**
1. Make a 2x4x8 inch brick of your mix
2. Let dry in sun for 3-7 days
3. Drop from waist height onto hard ground
4. Should hold together but show some cracking — if it shatters, too much sand; if it deforms, too much clay

## Mixing Methods

### Foot Mixing on Tarp (Traditional)

1. Spread a tarp on flat ground
2. Spread a layer of sand (2-3 buckets)
3. Spread a layer of clay soil (2-3 buckets) on top
4. Fold tarp edges over and stomp with bare feet
5. Add water gradually while stomping
6. When ingredients are combined, spread flat
7. Add straw by the handful, spreading evenly
8. Fold and stomp again until straw is thoroughly mixed in
9. The mix is ready when it holds together but does not stick excessively to hands

### Tarp Rolling (Efficient)

1. Combine sand, soil, and water on tarp
2. Two people lift tarp edges and roll the pile back and forth
3. Add straw and continue rolling
4. Faster and easier on the back than foot stomping

### Cement Mixer (Large Projects)

1. Add dry ingredients first (soil, sand)
2. Add water gradually
3. Add straw last — only mix briefly to avoid chopping straw fibers
4. A gasoline-powered mixer can produce cob much faster for large walls

## Building with Cob

### Foundation

Cob walls must be elevated above ground on a stone or concrete foundation:

- Rubble trench foundation with concrete stem wall (preferred)
- Width: 6-8 inches wider than the cob wall on each side
- Height: Minimum 12-18 inches above grade
- Cap with a water-resistant layer (slate, concrete, tar paper)

### Building Up

1. Start by applying a 4-6 inch layer of cob directly onto the foundation
2. Each subsequent layer (called a "lift") is 12-18 inches high
3. Let each lift firm up (dry for 1-3 days) before adding the next
4. While building, press cob firmly into the previous layer
5. Use thumbs to create a rough surface texture on each lift for bonding
6. Work the cob with your hands, pressing and sculpting

### Bonding Between Lifts

When starting a new lift on a partially dried previous lift:
1. Wet the surface of the previous lift (not soaked, just dampened)
2. Score horizontal grooves with a stick or cob tool
3. Press fresh cob firmly into the scored, dampened surface
4. Poor bonding between lifts is the most common cause of structural failure

### Window and Door Openings

- Install wooden frames (bucks) as you build
- Build cob around the frames, embedding them into the wall
- Lintel required above every opening — wood beam or stone arch
- Allow for settling — cob shrinks slightly as it dries

### Wall Thickness

- Standard cob walls: 18-24 inches thick
- Load-bearing walls: minimum 18 inches
- Non-load-bearing partitions: 12-15 inches acceptable
- In cold climates, thicker walls (24+ inches) provide more insulation

## Arches and Niche Construction

Cob's sculptural quality allows beautiful built-in features:

### Arches

1. Build a temporary form from wood or straw/sand
2. Layer cob over the form, building from both sides toward the center
3. Allow to dry thoroughly (1-2 weeks) before removing the form
4. The keystone (top center) is critical — it locks the arch together

### Niches and Shelves

- Sculpt recesses directly into the wall during building
- Support shelves with cob corbels (built-out ledges)
- Create built-in benches, window seats, and storage alcoves

## Waterproofing

Cob is vulnerable to water damage. Protect your walls:

### Roof Overhang

- Minimum 2-foot overhang on all sides (3 feet in wet climates)
- Wider overhangs protect walls from driving rain
