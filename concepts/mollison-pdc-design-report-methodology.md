## Property Finding Criteria

When finding property for a client, Mollison establishes six criteria:

1. Client has own water supply potential on site
2. Site suits what the client wants to do
3. Site in present usage has been undervalued
4. Multi-titled or without structural limitations
5. Designer can improve the property to a high level
6. Not valued for subdivision (highest price, lowest opportunity)

## Report Structure

The PDC specifies a complete report structure:

### General Description
Begin with a brief description of the site: orientation, existing
vegetation, water, soils. Draw a main map breaking the site into
identifiable areas, each numbered for reference.

### Whole-Site Themes
Address themes affecting the entire property: fire [[permaculture-frost-protection-strategies]],
legal structures (trusts, land trusts), community development, and
commercial crop strategies.

### Area-by-Area Detail
Starting with area #1 (nearly always the house site and intensive
garden), work through each numbered area. Define house design
principles, garden placement, roof water collection, and specific
planting and management systems.

### Standard Designs
Insert relevant standard designs with bridging text. Designers can
develop their own standard designs for common situations and earn
royalties from publication.

### Priority Staging and Cost Analysis
"You must never omit this -- never hand this report to the client with
all these lovely ideas, while he has no idea where to start." Show
where to begin, approximate costs, and a logical sequence of
development.

### Bibliography and Plant List
Include all referenced books plus relevant additional titles. Provide
a documented plant list, avoiding illegal or potentially invasive
species. Include a resource list of local suppliers and contacts.

## Fee Structure

The PDC provides specific guidance on professional fees:

- Normal jobs (up to 40 acres): one day on site, 15-20 page typed and
  illustrated report
- Large jobs (100+ acres): quote on time basis, group design work
- Design cost: 1.5% to 2% of total implementation investment
- Hourly rate: approximately $35/hour (1981 basis)
- Property finding: 0.5% of purchase price to find, plus 1.5% if
  client buys
- Land management: $2,000 to $5,000 per year depending on workload

## Type-One Errors to Avoid

Mollison catalogs ten critical design errors:

1. Undertaking designs for environmentally destructive aims
2. Not explaining reasons for recommendations
3. Recommending technology beyond the client's ability
4. Not supplying adequate management directions
5. Writing with a depersonalized approach
6. Being vague and nonspecific
7. Poor patterning of edges and linkages
8. Failing to recommend essential pre-treatments
9. Recommending illegal plant species
10. Failing to define on-site resources
