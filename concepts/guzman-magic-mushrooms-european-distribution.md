## Widely Distributed European Species

Several neurotropic species are found across much of Europe with no specific country-level restriction:

- [[amanita-pantherina]]: Widely distributed across temperate Europe
- [[claviceps-purpurea]]: The ergot fungus, extremely common on cereals and grasses throughout Europe
- [[psilocybe-semilanceata]]: The "liberty cap," Europe's most common and widespread psychoactive Psilocybe, found in temperate and boreal grasslands
- [[panaeolus-subbalteatus]]: A common dung-inhabiting species across much of Europe

## Comparison with Other Continents

Europe's relatively low Psilocybe diversity (14 species) is notable given the intensity of mycological study the continent has received. By comparison, Africa has only 6 to 8 known Psilocybe species, but this is attributable to poor exploration rather than genuine absence. Europe's well-studied status means its 14-species count more accurately reflects true diversity. The absence of tropical and subtropical Psilocybe sections (Cubensiae, Zapotecorum) from Europe limits its diversity compared to tropical regions.

## Species of Particular European Interest

Several European species have been the focus of intensive chemical and pharmacological investigation:

- [[galerina-steglichii]]: Described from Germany by Besl (1994), this is one of the few Galerina species confirmed to contain psilocybin. It is notable because many Galerina species contain deadly amatoxins rather than psilocybin, making correct identification critical.

- [[psilocybe-serbica]]: Originally described from Yugoslavia, this species is found in southeastern and central Europe and represents one of the more potent European Psilocybe species.
