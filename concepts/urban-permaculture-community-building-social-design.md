Urban permaculture is intensely social. Everything you do is within
sight, sound, or touch of a neighbour or an inspector. This intense
interaction is one of the defining and charming features of urban
life. Rather than privatising and controlling daily activities as
modern design does, urban permaculture seeks to increase, open up,
and share the diverse paths, places, and patterns of urban
conviviality.

## People Guilds and Neighbourhood Design

Just as plant guilds are designed in gardens, people guilds can be
designed in neighbourhoods. The goal is to put people in touch with
one another, match somebody's needs to another person's skill and
surplus, and connect the cycles and flows of human energy. This
means letting everyone join in and have fun.

## Guerrilla Planting in Public Spaces

Foraging and guerrilla planting in city parks extends permaculture
beyond private property. Native plants can survive on local rainfall
and bring the real world into the heart of the city. Abandoned lots,
rubble fields, and neglected public spaces present opportunities
for restoration without formal permission.

The concept of conferring particularity and identity to place is
central. Converting a lawn to a garden gives it particularity and
identity. A front garden says something about who the inhabitants
are and what nature can be. Its diverse interactions evoke the
conviviality of home and neighbourhood.
