# Spore Syringe Water Sterilization and Preparation

In D.H. Jarrold's boil-a-bag [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]] method, preparing a sterile
spore syringe is a critical step that directly determines the success or
failure of the grow. The process involves sterilizing water, a syringe and
needle, and the spore collection jar, then combining them under strict
aseptic conditions to create a clean spore solution for inoculating
[[oil-drum-sterilizer-mushroom-substrate-bags]].

## Equipment Required

The following items are needed for spore [[gottlieb-psilocybin-cultivation-spore-syringe-preparation]]:

- Glass pyrex container (for sterilizing water)
- Syringe (typically 10-20ml capacity)
- Syringe needle (standard 4cm or custom long needle from stainless
  steel tubing)
- Tin foil (for covering containers during sterilization)
- Spore print jar (glass jar with metal screw lid)
- Spirit burner or alcohol lamp (for [[flammability-hazard-alcohol-lysol-flame-sterilization-mycology]])
- Propyl alcohol (isopropyl alcohol for [[challenge-microorganisms-microwave-surface-sterilization]])
- Flat-ended metal scraper
- Metal tweezers and scissors
- Protective breathing mask or clean handkerchief

## Water Sterilization

The first step is preparing sterile water that will serve as the carrier
fluid for the spores. The pyrex container is filled with approximately
80ml of water and covered with a tin foil lid. The container, along with
the syringe and needle wrapped in tin foil, is placed in a [[double-layer-tinfoil-jar-covering-pressure-cooker-method]]
and brought to full steam at 15 psi for 20 minutes.

This sterilization step is essential because any contaminant organisms
present in the water will be injected directly into the sterile substrate
bags during inoculation, potentially ruining the entire batch. The 20-minute
sterilization at 15 psi is sufficient to kill bacteria, fungal spores, and
other contaminant organisms.

## Spore Jar Sterilization

Before the spore syringe can be loaded, the spore print jar must also be
sterilized. Jarrold recommends placing clean glass jars with their metal
screw lids on a metal baking tray under the grill (broiler) of an oven for
30 minutes at half maximum heat. After cooling, the lids are loosely placed
on each jar, the tray is removed from the grill, and the lids are tightened.

This oven-based sterilization is an alternative to [[jarrold-pressure-cooker-sterilization-boil-bag-mushroom-cultivation]]
sterilization for glass jars and is effective for eliminating contaminant
organisms from the jar surfaces and lids.

## Loading the Spore Syringe

Once all components are sterile and cooled, the spore syringe is assembled
and loaded through the following steps:

1. The sterile water is carefully added to the spore jar containing the
   spore print. At this stage, the spores do not readily mix with the
   water and tend to clump at the bottom of the jar.

2. The flat-ended metal scraper is flame sterilized using the spirit
   burner, held in the flame until red hot, then allowed to cool briefly.

3. The sterilized scraper is inserted into the spore jar and used to
   gently scrape and loosen the spores from the bottom and sides of the
   jar. This action disperses the spores into the water, creating a
   spore suspension suitable for drawing into the syringe.

4. The syringe is assembled with the needle attached and passed through
   the flame of the spirit burner to sterilize the needle. This flame
   sterilization must be performed every time the syringe is refilled
   from the spore jar and before each [[inoculation-point-taping-technique-sterile-bag-inoculation-jarrold]].

5. The [[sterile-spore-print-collection-glass-jar-technique]] solution is drawn into the syringe from the jar.
   The jar lid must be replaced immediately after each refill to prevent
   airborne contamination.
