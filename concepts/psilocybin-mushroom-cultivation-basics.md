## Overview

Psilocybin mushroom cultivation refers to the process of growing mushrooms in the genus Psilocybe, most commonly Psilocybe cubensis, for their psychoactive compounds psilocybin and psin. The practice has evolved from simple home experiments in the 1970s to a well-documented set of techniques accessible to motivated beginners. The core principle is straightforward: create a sterile environment, introduce mushroom mycelium to a nutritious substrate, and provide conditions that trigger fruiting.

## Core Cultivation Methods

### PF Tek (Psilocybe Fanaticus Technique)

The PF Tek is the foundational beginner method, developed in the early 1990s. It uses half-pint wide-mouth mason jars filled with a substrate of brown rice flour and vermiculite. The mixture provides both nutrition (rice flour) and water retention (vermiculite). Jars are sterilized in a pressure cooker, inoculated with a spore syringe or liquid culture, and left to colonize at room temperature. Once fully colonized (white mycelium visible through the glass), cakes are removed from the jars and placed in a simple fruiting chamber — often a plastic bin with damp perlite on the bottom for humidity.

Advantages of PF Tek:
- Minimal equipment investment
- Good for learning sterile technique fundamentals
- Visual feedback — you can watch colonization through the glass
- Low risk of catastrophic failure

Limitations:
- Low yields per jar (typically 1-3g dry per cake)
- Labor-intensive at scale
- Brown rice flour is not an ideal long-term substrate

### Monotub Method

The monotub is the most popular method for intermediate and advanced growers. A large plastic storage bin (typically 50-110 quart) serves as both the colonization vessel and fruiting chamber. The substrate is a bulk mixture of coco coir, vermiculite, and gypsum (CVG), inoculated with grain spawn at a ratio of 1:2 or 1:3 (spawn to substrate by volume). See [[monotub-cultivation]] for the complete process.

### Grain Spawn

Grain spawn is the living mushroom mycelium grown on sterilized grains such as rye berries, whole oats, or wild bird seed. The grain provides dense nutrition for rapid mycelial expansion. Grain spawn is the bridge between a tiny inoculant (spores or agar culture) and a large bulk substrate. See [[grain-spawn-preparation]] and [[mushroom-spawn-making]] for full details.

## The Cultivation Lifecycle

1. **Spore collection or acquisition** — spore print or spore syringe. See [[mushroom-spore-printing-and-storage]].
2. **Agar isolation** — germinate spores on agar plates to obtain clean mycelium and isolate strong genetics. See [[agar-work-guide]].
3. **Grain inoculation** — transfer agar wedge or liquid culture to sterilized grain jars. See [[grain-spawn-preparation]].
4. **Grain colonization** — wait 2-4 weeks for mycelium to fully colonize grain.
5. **Spawn to bulk** — mix colonized grain with pasteurized bulk substrate (CVG). See [[bulk-substrate-preparation]].
6. **Bulk colonization** — wait 7-14 days for the mycelium to colonize the bulk substrate.
7. **Fruiting initiation** — introduce fresh air exchange, maintain high humidity (90%+), and provide indirect light (12 hours on/off).
8. **Harvest** — pick mushrooms at the right time. See [[mushroom-harvesting-potency]].

## Fruiting Chambers

A fruiting chamber provides the environmental conditions mushrooms need to form and grow fruiting bodies. Key parameters:

- **Humidity**: 85-95% relative humidity. Mushrooms are 90%+ water and need high moisture to develop properly.
- **Fresh Air Exchange (FAE)**: CO2 levels must stay low. High CO2 triggers long, stringy stems and small caps. Passive FAE through holes stuffed with polyfill or micropore tape is the standard monotub approach.
- **Temperature**: 70-75F (21-24C) is optimal for P. cubensis fruiting.
- **Light**: Indirect ambient light on a 12/12 schedule. Mushrooms do not photosynthesize but use light as a directional cue. A 6500K daylight LED is common.

## Contamination

Contamination is the single biggest challenge in mushroom cultivation. Unwanted molds (Trichoderma, Aspergillus), bacteria, and competing fungi can ruin entire grows. Prevention through [[mushroom-sterile-technique]] is far more effective than treatment after the fact. See [[mushroom-contamination-troubleshooting]] for identification and response strategies.

## Equipment Essentials

- Pressure cooker (minimum 15 PSI, 23-quart Presto is the standard entry-level)
- Wide-mouth mason jars (quart and half-pint)
- Spore syringe or agar culture
- Substrate materials (brown rice flour, vermiculite, coco coir, gypsum)
- Isopropyl alcohol (70%) for surface sterilization
- Nitrile gloves and a lighter or butane torch
- Fruitng chamber (plastic bin or monotub)
- Hygrometer/thermometer for monitoring conditions

## Legal Considerations

The cultivation of psilocybin-containing mushrooms is illegal in many jurisdictions. Cultivators should research their local laws before proceeding. In some areas, spores are legal to possess because they do not contain psilocybin, but the act of germinating them may constitute a crime.

## Yields and Expectations

A beginner using PF Tek might harvest 10-30g dry from a 12-jar batch. An experienced monotub grower can harvest 3-6 oz dry (85-170g) from a single 66-quart tub on the first flush. Total biological efficiency (dry weight of mushrooms divided by dry weight of substrate) typically ranges from 100-200% for P. cubensis. See [[mushroom-harvesting-potency]] for strategies to maximize output.
