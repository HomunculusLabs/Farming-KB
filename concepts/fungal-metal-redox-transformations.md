# Fungal Metal Redox Transformations Beyond passive biosorption, fungi actively
transform metals through oxidation-reduction reactions,
methylation-demethylation, precipitation, and organic acid-mediated dissolution.
These biogeochemical transformations alter metal mobility, toxicity, and
bioavailability -- processes critical to both environmental contamination and
remediation. ## Oxidation-Reduction Reactions **Chromium:** Fungi reduce highly
toxic Cr(VI) to less toxic Cr(III). Rhizopus nigricans dead biomass biosorbs
Cr(VI) at 212 mg/g (APTS-treated) and 160 mg/g (PEI-treated). Reduction
mechanisms involve cell surface electron transfer and intracellular enzymatic
reduction. Chromium reduction by yeasts may involve oxidation-reduction
reactions, interaction with cellular organelles, and formation of protein-DNA
and Cr-DNA adducts. **Manganese:** Fungi oxidize Mn(II) to Mn(III) and Mn(IV).
MnP produces Mn(III) as a diffusible oxidant that extends the oxidative reach of
the fungal enzyme system beyond the hyphal surface. Mn(III) chelated by organic
acids (oxalate, malonate) acts as a key mediator in lignin and pollutant
degradation. Some fungi also precipitate manganese as Mn(IV) oxides on hyphal
surfaces. **Iron:** Fungi reduce Fe(III) to Fe(II) through organic acid
production and enzymatic mechanisms. Neurospora crassa mutants showed
significant Fe(II) pickup capacities. Iron redox cycling affects availability of
both iron and other metals whose behavior is coupled to iron chemistry.
**Selenium and tellurium:** Filamentous fungi reduce selenium oxyanions [Se(IV),
Se(VI)] and tellurite [Te(IV)]. Reduced selenium accumulates intracellularly or
is volatilized. Reduction products and cellular location influence tolerance
mechanisms. Baker's yeast demonstrates different biosorption capacities for
different oxidation states of selenium and antimony, confirming that oxidation

## Glomalin as Metal Stabilizer

Glomalin, an insoluble glycoprotein produced by VAM fungi, sequesters
potentially toxic elements:
- Extracted from polluted soils containing Cu, Cd, and Pb
- Glomalin from Gigaspora rosea hyphae sequestered up to 28 mg Cu/g in vitro
- Can act as a biostabilizer for long-term remediation of polluted soils
- Persists in soil for years, providing durable metal immobilization

## Methylation and Volatilization Pathways

Fungi methylate several metal and metalloid species, converting ionic forms into
volatile organic compounds. This methylation can serve either detoxification or
environmental dispersal:

- **Arsenic methylation**: Fungi convert inorganic arsenic [As(III), As(V)]
through methylation steps to volatile trimethylarsine. *Scopulariopsis* and
*Penicillium* species are particularly active arsenic methylators.
Volatilization
reduces soil arsenic but may contaminate the atmosphere.
- **Mercury methylation**: Certain fungi can methylate mercury to methylmercury,
though bacterial methylation dominates in most environments. Fungal
demethylation
of methylmercury back to inorganic mercury has been documented as a
detoxification pathway.
- **Tin and lead methylation**: Fungi produce volatile methylated tin and lead
compounds, though ecological significance remains poorly understood.

## Enzymatic Mechanisms of Metal Transformation

Fungal enzymes mediate many metal redox transformations:

- **Laccases**: Copper-containing oxidases that reduce oxygen to water while
oxidizing substrates including metal ions. Laccases from white-rot fungi can
reduce uranium U(VI) to U(IV), precipitating it as insoluble uraninite.
- **Peroxidases**: Manganese peroxidase (MnP) and lignin peroxidase (LiP)
oxidize Mn(II) and other metals as part of their catalytic cycles. The Mn(III)
generated by MnP acts as a diffusible redox mediator.
- **Nitrate reductases**: Reduce metals including uranyl and chromate as
incidental side reactions during nitrogen metabolism.
- **Cell surface reductases**: Membrane-bound electron transport chains transfer
electrons from intracellular NAD(P)H to extracellular metal acceptors.

## Applications in Bioremediation and Bioleaching

Fungal metal-transforming capabilities are exploited in bioremediation and
bioleaching. In bioleaching of low-grade ores, fungi such as Aspergillus niger
and Penicillium species produce organic acids that solubilize metals from
mineral matrices, offering a low-energy alternative to conventional extraction.
This has been applied to recovery of copper, nickel, cobalt, and rare earth
elements from mine tailings and electronic waste.

In chromium remediation, fungal Cr(VI) reduction offers an in-situ treatment
option. The reduction of soluble, toxic Cr(VI) to insoluble Cr(III) dramatically
reduces chromium mobility. [[comparison-constructed-wetlands-vs-bioswales]]
incorporating Cr(VI)-reducing fungi have been proposed for passive treatment of
tannery and electroplating effluents.
[[fungal-immobilization-bioreactor-systems]]
that exploit both biosorptive capacity and redox-transforming enzymes achieve
more complete metal removal than either mechanism alone. Hybrid systems using
killed biomass combined with enzyme addition represent a promising engineering
approach for [[ligninolytic-enzyme-production-bioremediation]].
