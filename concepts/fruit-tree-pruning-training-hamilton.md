# Fruit Tree Pruning and Training (Hamilton Method)

Updated: 2026-04-18

Pruning is the deliberate cutting back of plants, usually applied to trees and shrubs. It controls size, trains to shape, and encourages flower or fruit bud formation.

## Essential Pruning Terminology

- **Sideshoot**: A shoot arising from the main stem of the plant
- **Secondary shoot**: A shoot arising from a sideshoot
- **Leading shoot**: The main stem that extends the branch system
- **Fruiting spur**: A collection of shoots on which fruit is produced, made by cutting sideshoots and secondary shoots back hard
- **Downward-facing bud**: Buds can be downward-, outward-, inward-, or upward-facing. A bud grows in the direction it faces, used to advantage when training to shape.

## Making a Correct Pruning Cut
A pruning cut should be angled away from the bud and made slightly above it. Do not leave too much stem above the bud as this "snag" will rot. Do not cut too close to the bud.

## Training Forms

### Dwarf Pyramid
A free-standing tree shaped like a Christmas tree. Final shape is conical.

1. Immediately after planting, cut the stem back to a bud within 24in (60cm) of ground level
2. In the second winter, select five evenly spaced lower branches with a wide angle to the stem. Cut back to a downward- or outward-facing bud, leaving 10in (25cm). Remove other branches at this level. Cut branches above back to 6in (15cm) to form a second tier. Cut main stem tip to leave it 12in (30cm) above the top branch
3. Every summer, cut back the tip of each branch to leave 6in (15cm) of the current year's growth. Prune sideshoots to 4in (10cm) and secondary shoots to 2in (5cm)
4. Every winter, prune the leading shoot to leave 8in (20cm) of last season's growth
5. Once the tree reaches required height, prune back twice as hard: new growth to 3in (7cm), sideshoots to 2in (5cm), secondary shoots to 1in (2.5cm)

Plant dwarf pyramids 5ft (1.5m) apart.

### Cordon
Cordon-grown fruit takes very little room and becomes a decorative feature. Grow against post-and-wire supports at 2ft (60cm) intervals, or on an existing fence. Plant trees 2ft 6in (75cm) apart; bushes 12in (30cm) apart.

1. Immediately after planting, cut the leading shoot back to remove one-third of that year's growth. Cut any sideshoots to a downward-facing bud, leaving each 3in (7cm) long
2. In the first summer, prune sideshoots from the main stem to 3in (7cm). Prune secondary shoots to 1in (2.5cm)
3. In the second winter, prune the leading shoot, cutting off one-third. Continue pruning in this way until the end of the cane is reached
4. Every summer, prune sideshoots to 3in (7cm) and secondary shoots to 1in (2.5cm). When the tree is as long as desired, prune the main shoot in summer to 3in (7cm)

### Espalier
Suitable for small gardens, espaliers make a decorative feature of bare walls. Plant trees at least 12ft (3.6m), preferably 15ft (4.5m), apart, against a wall or fence strung with wires at 2ft (60cm) intervals.

1. Immediately after planting, prune to 2in (5cm) above the first wire (2ft/60cm above ground). Ensure three good buds below the pruning position
2. In spring, train the resulting shoots onto canes fixed to the wires. Train sideshoots at a 45-degree angle from the main stem
3. In the first summer, cut any side branches on the main stem to 3in (7cm)
4. In the second winter, tie the two branches to the lower wire. Remove one-third of previous season's growth. Prune secondary shoots to 1in (2.5cm). Cut the main stem to 2in above the second wire
5. Every summer, repeat the process for as many tiers as desired
6. At required height, select only two buds and train horizontally as the final tier. After that, prune each arm as for cordon training

### Stepover
A modern single-tier espalier variation for small gardens. Grow on wires on short posts 2in (30cm) above ground. Plant trees 12-15ft (3.5-4.5m) apart.

1. Immediately after planting, prune main stem to 2in (5cm) above the wire, ensuring buds on either side
2. In spring, train shoots out along the wires
3. Each arm is then pruned as for cordon training

### Festooned Tree
Branches are bent into severe curves to restrict sap flow and encourage fruiting buds.

1. At the end of the first summer, pull the main shoot downward, bending it into a hoop. Secure by tying the end to the base with soft string
2. In the second summer, prune new shoots on top of the curve in summer like cordons
3. Select more shoots and bend them into hoops, securing to the main stem
4. In subsequent summers, prune all fruiting spurs like cordons

## Winter vs Summer Pruning
- **Winter pruning**: Used for initial shaping and establishing form (dormant season)
- **Summer pruning**: Used to restrict growth and encourage fruiting buds. By cutting in summer, the tree does not waste energy on unwanted growth
- Fan-trained cherry and plum trees should be pruned in summer only (winter pruning increases disease risk)
