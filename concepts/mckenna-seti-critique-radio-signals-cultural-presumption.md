## The Radio Signal Assumption

The dominant approach to detecting extraterrestrial intelligence — scanning the
sky for artificial radio signals — presupposes that any technologically advanced
civilization would communicate using electromagnetic radiation in the radio
spectrum. McKenna characterized this as a fundamentally culture-bound
presumption:

> To search expectantly for a radio signal from an extraterrestrial source is
> probably as culture bound a presumption as to search the galaxy for a good
> Italian restaurant.

The analogy is deliberately provocative. Just as the expectation of finding
Italian cuisine on a distant planet reveals the searcher's cultural frame rather
than any objective feature of the cosmos, so too does the expectation that
aliens would use radio waves reveal the technological assumptions of a
mid-twentieth-century civilization that had recently discovered radio
communication.

## The Noise Problem

McKenna argued that the universe is already saturated with "signals" from other
dimensions, but that our scientific framework has ruled these signals
inadmissible a priori. He pointed to the vast population of people worldwide
whose subjective experiences — psychics, shamans, mystics, and schizophrenics
— are filled with information from what they experience as non-ordinary
realities:

> Meanwhile, there are people all over the world — psychics, shamans, mystics,
> schizophrenics — whose heads are filled with information, but it has been
> ruled a priori irrelevant, incoherent, or mad. Only that which is validated
> through consensus via certain sanctioned instrumentalities will be accepted
> as a signal.

The problem, as McKenna framed it, is not a lack of incoming information but
an excess of it: "We are so inundated by these signals — these other dimensions
— that there is a great deal of noise in the circuit." The challenge is not
detection but discrimination — separating genuine signal from noise in an
information-saturated environment.
