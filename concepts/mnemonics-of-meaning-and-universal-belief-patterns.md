# Mnemonics of Meaning and Universal Belief Patterns

Across cultures and throughout history, human societies have developed mnemonic systems—chant, symbol, and ritual—that encode profound understanding of natural patterns and cosmic relationships. These systems often converge on remarkably similar core insights, suggesting that deep observation of the natural world leads to universal truths about the interconnectedness of all phenomena.

## The Buddhist Mantra: Om Mani Padme Hum

The Tibetan Buddhist chant "Om mani padme hum" (pronounced "Aum ma-ni pay-may hung") serves as a comprehensive mnemonic of pattern understanding, with each syllable encoding a layer of meaning:

### Aum (Om) — The Awakening Harmonic
The fundamental harmonic representing all stillness and all sound across time. It recalls the universe itself—the beginning state from which all phenomena emerge. As the "signing on" element, it represents creation, potential, and the unity underlying all diversity.

### Ma-Ni — The Unchanging Essence
The diamond jewel or thunderbolt at the core of all phenomena—truth itself. Represented visually as a blue orb, radiant jewel, or source of lightning and fire. This is the implicate order, the unchanging reality that persists through all transformation. In Buddhist iconography, the diamond (vajra) symbolizes that which cannot be destroyed or altered.

### Pay-May — The Unfolding of Events
"Enfolded in the heart of the lotus" (mani enfolded). This represents the visible, everyday unfolding of events—petals, patterns, and processes—that progressively reveal the essential unchanged core to human understanding. The lotus is the quintessential symbol of this principle: rooted in mud, growing through water, opening to light, yet its essential nature remains unchanged throughout.

### Hung — The Declaration of Presence
"It is here, now." A declaration of belief in the words being spoken, and a preface to the beginning of the next chant cycle. This syllable encodes the concept of implicate time: all events are present now and forever repeated in their form. It closes one cycle while simultaneously opening the next, reflecting the non-linear nature of time itself.

The realization of this whole—the jewel in the heart of the lotus—is *nirvana*, the ideal state of Buddhism.

## Dorje: The Primordial Thunderbolt

The Tibetan figure Dorje (Dorje-chang), who holds the dorje or thunderbolt (represented as a radiant stone symbolizing cosmic energy), is the primordial Buddha of Tibet who began the great succession of reincarnations. His color is blue, representing eternity, and he carries a bell signifying the voiceless wisdom of the inanimate—the sound of the void.

This figure has direct parallels across cultures:
- **Thor** of the Norsemen (whose hammer Mjölnir lends its name through linguistic evolution to "Mollison")
- **Durga** of Hindu tradition
- **Thunderers** of numerous tribal peoples worldwide

The persistence of this archetype across disconnected civilizations suggests it emerges independently from observation of the same natural phenomena—lightning, storm, and cosmic energy.

## The Universal Core of Belief Systems

Many world beliefs share an essential core derived from contemplation of natural events. These systems:

- **Precede individualism** as a philosophical framework
- **Transcend disciplinary boundaries** between science, art, and spirituality
- **Express a way of life and philosophy** rather than dogma or measurement
- **Arise from direct observation** of natural patterns—rainbows, serpents, song cycles, tidal rhythms

The concept of "the jewel in the heart of the lotus" recurs in Amerindian and Australian tribal cultures through different symbolic vocabularies but expressing essentially the same understanding: that an unchanging core of truth underlies the apparent diversity and flux of experienced reality.

## The Drift from Nature-Based Systems

Mollison identifies a historical trajectory in which nature-based, universal systems drift toward:
- **Personalized or humanoid gods** replacing the recognition of impersonal cosmic patterns
- **Dogma** replacing open inquiry and direct observation
- **Fanaticism** replacing contemplative understanding
- **Symbols without meaning** that serve to exclude rather than include understanding
- **Secretive, centralized belief systems** that destroy open knowledge

This drift transforms belief from a shared understanding of natural patterns into instruments of social control. The fanatic "cares not what is destroyed if it empowers the repressive hierarchy that is then imposed." Book-burning and image-breaking represent the reaction of the intellectually deprived to accumulated ancestral wisdom.

## Patterns of Social Hierarchy

Mollison's studies of social behavior patterns in Tasmania (1969–1974), sampling approximately 35,000 people across military, corporate, religious, and business organizations, revealed a consistent "boat-shaped" hierarchical pattern with distinct occupational strata:

### Low Occupational Level (Resource Area)
- Manual and unskilled urban workers
- General dearth of material resources, low status
- Notable sex ratio imbalance: approximately 140 males per 100 females
- Large families, serial polyandry common

### Central Level (Middle Class)
- Adequate resources, nine-to-five employment, some job tenure
- "Normal" birth ratio: 104 males per 100 females
- Mixed white-collar and skilled technical workers
- Monogamy as ideal, often expressed as serial monogamy

### Upper Levels
- Few people, extensive resources, flexible self-set schedules
- High proportion of female children: approximately 100 females to 70 males
- Urban professionals and managers, small families
- Effective polygyny via concubines or mistresses

### Very High Levels
- Executive directors and landed nobility
- Lateral intermarriage for economic alliances, facilitated by exclusive schools and resorts
- Preponderance of female children

The sex ratio imbalances across strata ensure **genetic turnover or diffusion between classes**—a streaming of genetic material between levels across generations, preventing rigid stratification.

## Implications for Permaculture Design

The convergence of ancient belief systems on core truths about natural patterns validates the permaculture approach of designing from pattern to detail. The mnemonic systems described here demonstrate that:

- **Complex information** can be encoded in simple, memorable patterns
- **Universal principles** emerge independently across cultures from direct observation
- **Integration of knowledge** (rather than fragmentation into disciplines) reflects actual reality
- **Accessible, shared understanding** is more robust than specialized, centralized knowledge
