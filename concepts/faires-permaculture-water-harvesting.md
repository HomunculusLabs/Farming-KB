## Diversion Drains

Unlike swales (which stop water), diversion drains carry water away. They direct flow to swales, ponds, or irrigation systems. When sending water to a dam, drains should be built of rock or concrete. ## Pond Design

Ponds can segregate fish of different ages when several are connected in succession. Alternatively, build parallel ponds to prevent disease/parasite transmission between them. Each pond should have connected marshes supplying food.

## Irrigation Design

Permaculture irrigation is half species choice and half terrain. Only a small part involves piping or pumps. Gravity directs water to gardens, and species are chosen for specific [[comparison-desert-gardening-vs-drought-gardening]] success.

In the orchard, sprinklers can mist over roots but only in shady tree areas to avoid evaporation problems.

## Integrated Water Systems

Dams at the top of slopes connect to mid-slope dams via diversion drains, feeding swales and reserve ponds. Ponds may have irrigation pipes to gardens and orchards. Irrigation should supplement natural flow, not enable inappropriate plantings like watermelons in the desert.

A well-developed water diversion system can make even a small property very self-reliant. Dams at top of slopes lead down to mid-slope dams connected with diversion drains, collecting rainwater into swales and reserve ponds. Irrigation should never be used for growing watermelons in the desert or for growing lawns and washing cars.

## Graywater Systems

Graywater (from sinks, tubs, washing machines, showers) can be piped into marshes or container gardens to grow water-loving plants (cattails, rushes, reed grasses, horsetails). It cannot be spouted directly into food gardens.

Sizing: one cubic foot of wetlands filters roughly 1 gallon of water. Marsh surface area should handle about 1/3 of daily graywater production, 2 feet deep. For a frugal household of five using ~940 gallons/week: 134 gallons/day divided by 3 equals 45 sq ft of marsh.
