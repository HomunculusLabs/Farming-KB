# JADAM Nutritional Balance Philosophy

## Overview

JADAM's approach to soil fertility and fertilizer management is radically simpler than conventional [[cervantes-fertilizer-application-strain-feeding]], and design separate fertilizer programs for each growth stage (high-N for vegetative growth, high-P for flowering, high-K for fruit enlargement, high-Ca for maturing).

JADAM argues this method:
- Is nearly impossible for farmers to practice correctly
- Does not exist in nature -- no plant in nature grows this way
- Exists primarily as a marketing tool for chemical fertilizer sales
- Has not delivered higher yield or quality despite decades of use
- Makes farmers dependent on "experts" and corporations
- Drains money from farms into the pockets of service providers

## The Danger of Unbalanced Organic Matter

Simply putting organic matter into soil does not make you an organic farmer. Common [[fungi-around-the-world-species-distribution]] had inferior taste and quality compared to conventional produce." The reason: failure to provide the optimum nutritional balance for the specific crop.

## The Simple Solution

The answer to perfect fertilizer: "Give plants what is same (or similar) as the plants."

### For Base Fertilizer
- Use cover crops and crop residues
- Add manure from animals that ate grass
- Focus on growing abundant cover crops to serve as base fertilizer

### For Additional Fertilizer
Every time you water, apply this combination (per 0.1 ha):
- JMS: 500 L (132 gal)
- Seawater: 20 L (5.3 gal) or sea salt: 500 g (1.1 lb)
- Phyllite solution: 20 L (5.3 gal)
- GC-JLF (grass/crop JLF): diluted x100

Do this 3-4 times per month. Observe growth and adjust GC-JLF dilution.

### To Accelerate Growth
Add food waste JLF, urine/feces JLF, or fish JLF when growth appears weak.

## The Nutrient Loop

Without human harvesting, a crop could grow on the same soil for generations without external inputs. The nutrient loop is naturally closed -- organic matter from fallen leaves and dead grass suffices for the crop's needs. Harvesting removes nutrients from this loop, so the farmer must return what was taken.

## The Plant Tissue Optimum

The optimum levels of elements in plant tissue (by Epstein 1972, 1990):
- Macronutrients: N (1.5%), K (1.0%), Ca (0.5%), Mg (0.2%), P (0.2%), S (0.1%)
- Micronutrients: Cl (100ppm), Fe (100ppm), B (20ppm), Mn (50ppm), Na (10ppm), Zn (20ppm), Cu (6ppm), Ni (0.1ppm), Mo (0.1ppm)

It is nearly impossible to adjust individual element quantities to achieve this balance. "Don't bother trying to do the impossible" -- use crop residues and wild grass instead.

## Why Traditional Farming Worked

Korean farmers for 5,000 years fed the peninsula without complicated fertilizer programs, back-breaking compost turning, sophisticated pest control, difficult books, or trainings. Their method made soil richer by the season. They were self-sufficient down to the seeds, completely free from commercial capital.

## The Marx Quote

Youngsang Cho quotes Karl Marx: "When commercial capital occupies a position of unquestioned ascendancy, it everywhere constitutes a system of plunder." JADAM sees this system of plunder now evidenced in every corner of agriculture, where corporate interests have taken over agro-technology.
