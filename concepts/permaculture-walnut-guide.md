## Harvesting and Processing

Walnuts are typically ready for harvest in late September through October
when the green hulls begin to split and the nuts drop naturally. For
English walnuts, nuts can be shaken from the tree once the packing
tissue between the kernel and shell turns brown. Black walnuts require
hull removal soon after harvest to prevent dark staining of the shell.
Nuts should be dried at 95-105°F (35-40°C) for 2-4 weeks until kernel
brittleness indicates adequate moisture content (below 8%). Properly
dried walnuts store for over a year at 32-38°F (0-3°C). Mature trees
can yield 100-200 pounds of nuts per year, with yields increasing as
the tree matures over its first 30-40 years.

## Juglone Toxicity and Companion Planting

Black walnut roots, leaves, and nut hulls produce juglone (5-hydroxy-
1,4-naphthoquinone), an allelopathic compound that inhibits growth of
many plant species. Sensitive plants include tomatoes, potatoes, peppers,
eggplants, and lilacs. Tolerant species include black raspberry, cherry,
elderberry, pawpaw, and many native woodland plants. See
[[black-walnut-juglone-plant-toxicity]] for a comprehensive compatibility
list. In permaculture design, walnut trees are best placed at the edge
of growing areas where juglone-sensitive plants are not expected.
