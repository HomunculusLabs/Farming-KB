## Net Pots and Growing Slabs in Hydroponics

Net pots and growing slabs are fundamental containers in hydroponic
[[cannabis-deep-water-culture-guide]] to top-feed
slab systems. Understanding the proper selection and use of these
containers is essential for healthy root development and efficient
nutrient delivery.

## Net Pots

Net pots are perforated plastic containers, typically cylindrical or
conical, with open sides and bottoms that allow roots to grow
through into the [[fukuoka-aerial-seeding-clay-pellets-desert-revegetation-technique]], rockwool, or other growing medium. The net
pots are nestled in holes in a lid that covers the reservoir. The
roots of seedlings and cuttings dangle down into the nutrient
solution. Cervantes describes this setup as simple and productive,
with no timer required because pumps run 24 hours a day.

### Use in Top-Feed Buckets

Self-contained top-feed buckets often feature a net pot suspended
in the lid of a five-gallon (19 L) bucket/reservoir. The roots hang
down into the reservoir while an air stone in the bottom aerates the
nutrient solution. A separate pump cycles irrigation to the
container. This design creates a 100-percent humid environment for
roots before they grow into the nutrient solution.

### Net Pot Best Practices

- Screen the drain in DWC reservoirs so roots do not block it
- Fill net pots loosely enough to allow root penetration but firmly
  enough to support the plant
- Use expanded clay pellets for maximum aeration and drainage
- Position net pots so the water level just touches the bottom of
  the pot initially, allowing roots to grow down seeking moisture
- Clean and sterilize net pots between crops to prevent disease
  carryover
