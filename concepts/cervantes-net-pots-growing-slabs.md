# Net Pots and Growing Slabs in Hydroponics

Net pots and growing slabs are fundamental containers in hydroponic
[[query-how-does-no-till-cannabis-cultivation-work]]. Jorge Cervantes describes their use across
multiple hydroponic system types, from [[cannabis-deep-water-culture-guide]] to top-feed
slab systems. Understanding the proper selection and use of these
containers is essential for healthy root development and efficient
nutrient delivery.

## Net Pots

Net pots are perforated plastic containers, typically cylindrical or
conical, with open sides and bottoms that allow roots to grow
through into the [[cannabis-nutrient-solution-preparation]] or moist air surrounding them.
They come in various sizes, most commonly 2-inch, 3-inch, and
6-inch diameters. Small net pots are used in cloning systems and
aeroponics, while larger net pots support mature flowering plants.

### Use in Deep Water Culture (DWC)

In DWC systems, seedlings and clones are held in net pots full of
expanded [[fukuoka-aerial-seeding-clay-pellets-desert-revegetation-technique]], rockwool, or other growing medium. The net
pots are nestled in holes in a lid that covers the reservoir. The
roots of seedlings and cuttings dangle down into the nutrient
solution. Cervantes describes this setup as simple and productive,
with no timer required because pumps run 24 hours a day.

### Use in Top-Feed Buckets

Self-contained top-feed buckets often feature a net pot suspended
in the lid of a five-gallon (19 L) bucket/reservoir. The roots hang
down into the reservoir while an air stone in the bottom aerates the
nutrient solution. A separate pump cycles irrigation to the
container. This design creates a 100-percent humid environment for
roots before they grow into the nutrient solution.

### Net Pot Best Practices

- Screen the drain in DWC reservoirs so roots do not block it
- Fill net pots loosely enough to allow root penetration but firmly
  enough to support the plant
- Use expanded clay pellets for maximum aeration and drainage
- Position net pots so the water level just touches the bottom of
  the pot initially, allowing roots to grow down seeking moisture
- Clean and sterilize net pots between crops to prevent disease
  carryover

## Growing Slabs

Growing slabs are rectangular blocks of compressed growing medium,
most commonly rockwool or [[comparison-coco-coir-cannabis-vs-peat-free-gardening]], wrapped in plastic. They serve
as growing containers for commercial and large-scale hydroponic
operations.

### Rockwool Slabs

Rockwool slabs covered in plastic serve as growing containers in
top-feed systems. Nutrient solution is delivered via spaghetti tubes
from the top of the slab. An emitter attached to the spaghetti tube
doses a specific measure of nutrient solution to each plant. The
solution is aerated as it is applied, absorbed by the medium, and
drains back to the reservoir. Rockwool slabs fit in individual
containers or are lined up on drainage tables.

### Coco Coir Slabs

Coco is compressed into slabs and packaged in plastic wrapping.
Similar to rockwool, nutrient solution is delivered from above via
spaghetti tubes or drip emitters. Coco slabs retain more moisture
than rockwool and have natural buffering properties that can help
stabilize pH. Cervantes notes that under normal conditions, the EC
in coco slabs should be monitored alongside the EC of the water and
runoff to maintain proper nutrient balance.
