# Topping (Cannabis)

Topping is a fundamental cannabis pruning technique that involves removing the apical meristem -- the main growing tip at the top of the plant -- to force the plant to develop two or more main colas instead of a single central one. Cervantes describes the technique in his pruning guidance: "Pinching back or pruning tops (branch tips) causes the two growing shoots just below the cut to grow stronger and bigger. This increases the number of top or main buds."

## Core Concept

Cannabis plants naturally exhibit apical dominance, meaning the main central stem grows taller and faster than side branches. The apical meristem at the very top produces auxin hormones that suppress lateral branching below it. When this dominant tip is removed, auxin production at the cut site stops, and the two highest branch tips below the cut begin to grow vigorously as they compete to become the new dominant tips. The result is two main colas of similar size instead of one.

Cervantes explains the hormonal mechanism: "Cutting the meristem (top growth tip) of a cannabis plant will diffuse auxins and cause greater concentrations in lower branch tips." This hormonal shift is what drives the plant to redirect energy from vertical growth into lateral branching.

## How Topping Works

1. Wait until the plant has 3-5 sets of true leaves (branches), typically at 3-4 weeks of age
2. Locate the apical meristem -- the newest growth at the very top of the main stem
3. Using sterilized scissors or pruners, cut the stem just above the highest set of branches
4. The cut should leave a small stump above the top branch node
5. The two branches directly below the cut will accelerate growth and become the new main colas
6. Allow 1-2 weeks for recovery before additional training or a second top

## Pinching vs Cutting

Cervantes distinguishes between cutting and pinching:

- "To pinch back a branch tip, simply snip it off below the last set or two of leaves"
- "Pinching off tender growth with your fingers helps seal the wound and is often less damaging to plants than cutting"
- Fingertip pinching crushes the soft tissue and seals the wound naturally, reducing the risk of infection

For clean cuts with tools, Cervantes advises: "Always use clean instruments when pruning. A straight razor, single-edge razor blade, a sharp pair of pruners, or a pair of scissors all work well. Sanitize clippers and blades between cuts by dipping in rubbing alcohol."

## Effects on the Plant

### Increased Branching

When the terminal bud is removed, "all lower branches develop more rapidly." The further a branch is from the hormones at the plant tip, the less effect the auxins have. Removing the tip releases this suppression across the entire plant.

### Diffused Floral Hormones

"Pruning tops also diffuses floral hormones. These hormones (auxins) prevent the lateral buds from growing very fast." Topping redistributes these hormones to multiple growing points, creating a bushier plant with more potential bud sites.

### Delayed Flowering

"Pruning shortly before flowering delays peak maturation." Cervantes warns that "if heavily pruned shortly before flowering, peak maturation is delayed for a week or longer." It takes a month or more for hormones to build up to pre-pruning concentrations. For this reason, topping should be completed well before the switch to 12/12 flowering.

### Reduced Top Bud Size

"Most growers do not pinch plants back, because it diminishes the yield of prime, dense tops." Topping trades one large central cola for two smaller ones. The total yield may increase, but the individual top bud quality and size decrease. Some growers prefer the single large cola approach for maximum quality per bud.

## Multiple Toppings

Each topped branch can be topped again, creating an exponential increase in branch points:

- First top: 1 cola becomes 2
- Second top (each branch): 2 colas become 4
- Third top: 4 colas become 8
- Fourth top: 8 colas become 16

However, Cervantes cautions that "pruning too much over time may alter hormonal concentrations, causing spindly growth." There is a practical limit to how many times a plant can be topped before the repeated stress and hormone disruption causes diminishing returns. Most growers top 1-3 times maximum.

## Topping for Specific Goals

### Even Canopy

Topping is used to prevent one plant from dominating the canopy height. In a mixed garden, taller plants can be topped to match shorter ones, ensuring even light distribution across all plants.

### Four-Branch Manifold

Cervantes describes a specific topping approach: "Remove all but the four main branches. The meristem (central stem) is removed just above the four lowest branches." This creates a four-cola plant where floral hormones are concentrated in the remaining branches. See [[main-lining]] for the full manifold technique.

### Bush Training

"Continually pinching back, as when taking clones from a mother, causes many more little branches to form below the pruned tips. Eventually, the plant is transformed into a hedge-like shape." This is useful for creating bushy mother plants for clone production.

## Timing

- Perform topping during the [[cannabis-vegetative-stage]] only
- Best done at 3-5 weeks of age when the plant has 3-5 node pairs
- Complete all topping at least 4 weeks before switching to flowering
- Do not top during flowering -- energy is diverted from bud production to wound healing
- Do not top stressed, unhealthy, or recently transplanted plants

## Cervantes on Not Topping

Cervantes provides a counterpoint: "Most successful growers do not prune at all, especially if growing a short clone crop that is only two to three feet (61-91 cm) tall. Short clone crops require no pruning to increase light to bottom leaves or to alter their profile." He notes that "not pruning has several advantages" because "floral hormones are allowed to concentrate in tips of branches causing buds to grow stronger and denser."

## Topping and the 20 Percent Rule

Cervantes' broader pruning guidance applies to topping as well: "Pruning all the branches or removing more than 20 percent of the foliage in a short time frame stresses plants too much and diminishes harvest." While topping typically removes only a small portion of the plant, combining topping with other pruning on the same day can exceed this threshold. Space out training activities to keep total stress manageable.

## Topping in Different Growing Styles

### SOG Topping

In [[sea-of-green]] setups, topping is generally unnecessary and may be counterproductive. SOG relies on the natural single-cola structure of short, untrained clones. Topping would delay flowering and create uneven canopy heights in a system designed for uniformity.

### SCROG Topping

In [[screen-of-green]] setups, topping is commonly used to create more branch points that can be trained through the screen. A single top at the right time provides 2-4 branches per plant instead of 1, filling the screen more quickly.

### Bush Training

Cervantes describes a bush training method where plants are progressively pruned to create wide, bushy specimens. Combined with topping, this approach maximizes lateral branching while controlling height. The method involves pruning half the branches during week 3 of vegetative growth, then pruning the remaining half during weeks 4-5.

## Topping Tools

Cervantes recommends several tools for topping:

- Fingertips (pinching): best for soft new growth, seals the wound naturally
- Straight razor or single-edge razor blade: very clean cut, disposable
- Sharp pruners: fast and efficient for multiple plants
- Scissors: adequate for small seedlings

All tools should be sterilized with rubbing alcohol between cuts and between plants. "Use indoor pruners only in the indoor garden. Pruners used outdoors have everything from spider mites to fungus spores on them."

## After Topping Care

- Allow 1-2 weeks for new growth to emerge from the nodes below the cut
- Maintain adequate nitrogen levels to support new vegetative growth
- Monitor for signs of infection at the cut site
- Once new branches are 2-3 inches long, consider LST to position them
- Do not top the same branch again until new growth is well established (2+ weeks)
