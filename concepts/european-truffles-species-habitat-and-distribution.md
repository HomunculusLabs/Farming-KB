## European Truffles: Species, Habitat, and Distribution

## Overview

Truffles are subterranean fungi of the family Tuberaceae, remarkable for their structure, unique habitat, and unequaled flavor and aroma. They constitute one of the most valuable fungal products in European commerce, with a history of culinary use extending back to ancient Greece and Rome. The Périgord truffle (*[[european-truffles]] came into use in the courts of France and Italy prior to the 14th century. The earliest French references concern the **summer truffle**, *[[tuber-magnatum]]** Pico — The only really important species of the lighter-colored group. Fruit body yellow (resembling a spherical yam), smooth but somewhat irregular. Internal tissue lighter in color than the black truffles. Fruit bodies average larger than any other species. The flavor is somewhat onion-like. Highly esteemed in Italy, where it is known as "fiorini" or "tartufo bianco." Particularly abundant in Lombardy and far southward towards Rome.

## Distribution and Geography

### Geographic Range

Truffles are found in the temperate and warm temperate regions of the western part of southern Europe — from Italy westward, and from the 40th to the 48th parallel. France is practically the only country in which truffles constitute an article of commerce, although they are marketed to some extent in Italy and Spain.
