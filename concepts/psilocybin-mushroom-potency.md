# Psilocybin Mushroom Potency and Dosage Guide

Comparative potency data and dosage guidelines for psilocybin-containing mushrooms, from [[paul-stamets]]' Psilocybin Mushrooms of the World. See [[psychedelic-compounds-reference]] for broader compound data, [[mushroom-species-guide]] for cultivation information, and [[fungal-research-compound-reference]] for the complete metabolic pathway (Shikimate pathway → chorismate → tryptophan → tryptamine → psilocin → psilocybin). The key regulatory step is tryptophan decarboxylase, which is self-inhibited by product accumulation — explaining why tryptamine feeding (Gartz 1989) increases total alkaloid output.

## Potency Rating Scale (Using P. semilanceata as Baseline)

| Rating | Combined Max % |
|--------|---------------|
| Not active | 0% |
| Weakly active | < 0.25% |
| Moderately active | 0.25-0.75% |
| Highly active / potent | > 0.75% |

Within any species, there can be a tenfold or greater range in psilocybin/psilocin production from one collection to the next.

## Dosage Guidelines

Using P. semilanceata as the standard species:

| Dried Grams | Effect |
|-------------|--------|
| 0.5g | Threshold -- noticeable changes in colour perception and auditory acuity |
| 1g | Subtle visual enhancement, mood elevation |
| 2g | Visual waves begin -- air appears liquid, tidal movements distort scenery |
| 3-4g | More intense experience, lasting 4-5 hours |
| 5g | Nearly overwhelming, spans ~6 hours |
| 7g+ | Maximum recommended. Not recommended above 7g even for experienced users |

## Important Dosage Notes

- Always take half the intended dose first, wait 1.5 hours before increasing
- At higher doses, some people report loss of muscular control persisting into the following day (reported with P. azurescens, P. cyanescens, Panaeolus subbalteatus)
- Tryptamine precursors in substrate can potentiate psilocybin content (Gartz 1989) -- substrate composition significantly affects potency
- Potency varies enormously between collections; actual potency is likely less than the maximum percentages listed above
- Psilocybin dephosphorylates to psilocin during digestion -- you are effectively experiencing psilocin (the active metabolite)

## Set and Setting

From Stamets' extensive field experience:

- **Plan well in advance** -- set aside an entire day and evening
- **Choose a power spot** -- natural beauty, dimensional views, boulders, flowing water, open sky
- **Trip with someone you love** -- strangers are a major risk factor
- **Tell a confidant** -- someone NOT tripping who knows where you are, with whom, and when you will be back
- **Start with half the intended dose** and wait 1.5 hours
- **The experience will come, plateau, and gently descend** -- panic guarantees it gets worse
- **Best assistance for a bad trip**: love, reassurance, and especially touch
- **No matter what happens, you will come down** -- this understanding should be reached beforehand
- Those with heart conditions, kidney/liver disorders, or mental health issues are ill-advised to experiment
- Dogs make excellent trip companions -- their heightened awareness and instinctive protectiveness are valuable

## Identification Safety

Psilocybe and Panaeolus genera are generally safe to identify in that there are **no known poisonous species in those two genera**. However:

- Conocybe and Inocybe contain several poisonous species that could damage or kill you
- Galerina marginata (deadly, contains amatoxins) mimics Psilocybe and grows on wood
- Mycena species with bluing reactions exist but analysis has not consistently detected psilocybin
- The bluing reaction is useful but not definitive on its own -- always combine with spore print color (dark purplish brown to black)



## Tryptamine Potentiation (Gartz 1989)

Adding tryptamine HCl to the substrate dramatically alters the alkaloid profile of *P. cubensis*, producing the highest psilocin concentrations ever reported in any mushroom. Full data in [[fungal-research-compound-reference]].

### How It Works

P. cubensis mycelia naturally produce psilocybin de novo via the Shikimate pathway. When fed exogenous tryptamine (a direct biosynthetic precursor), the mycelia hydroxylate and methylate it to produce psilocin. The phosphorylation step (psilocin → psilocybin) appears to be **downregulated** by excess tryptamine — psilocybin drops to near zero while psilocin skyrockets.

### Key Results (25mM tryptamine HCl in substrate)

| Metric | With Tryptamine | Control |
|--------|----------------|---------|
| Peak psilocin (flush 2) | **3.3%** dry mass | 0.01% |
| Psilocybin (flush 2) | 0.02% | 0.48% |
| Psilocin absent in 1st flush? | No (2.1%) | Yes |
| Total flushes tested | 5 | 5 |

### Substrate Used

Dried cow dung/rice-grain mixture (2:1) with twice the amount of water. No casing needed. First sporocarps in 3-4 weeks, five flushes total, average 3g dry mass per 10g substrate.

### PF TEK Application Notes (Psylocybe Fanaticus 1997)

- **Original formula**: 1/2-2/3 cup vermiculite + 1/8 cup brown rice powder + 45ml water with 0.16g tryptamine HCl per 1/2 pint jar
- **Issue**: PF substrate is lighter/thinner than Gartz's cow dung/rice, so 0.16g was too much — fruits grew dwarfed, some jars failed to fruit
- **Revised formula**: Use 0.08-0.1g tryptamine HCl per jar (1/2 to 3/4 of original)
- **Bioassay**: At least 3x usual potency despite dwarfed size
- **Preservation critical**: Use cool desiccation — dry in refrigerator under cold conditions. Store dried fungi in airtight container with desiccant in freezer. Psilocin degrades faster than psilocybin

### Important Caveats

- Tryptamine HCl is a controlled precursor in many jurisdictions
- Potency boost comes with significantly reduced fruit body size
- The psilocin:psilocybin ratio is inverted compared to normal mushrooms — this may affect subjective experience since psilocybin must dephosphorylate to psilocin during digestion, while free psilocin is immediately active
- Results may vary significantly by strain and substrate composition

## Flush-by-Flush Potency Variation (Bigwood & Beug 1982)

From controlled cultivation of an Amazonian strain of P. cubensis (Bigwood & Beug, Journal of Ethnopharmacology):

**Key findings:**
- **Psilocin** is generally absent in the first flush, increases to maximum by the 4th flush, then declines
- **Psilocybin** varies unpredictably across flushes (factor of ~4) but shows no upward or downward trend
- Psilocybin is nearly always at least 2x the psilocin level
- Street samples showed tenfold variation in psilocybin levels (0.7-6.2 mg/g dry weight)
- Users have no way to predict dosage from dry weight alone

### Cap vs Stem Distribution

In three strains tested, caps generally contained **twice as much psilocybin** as stems. The small amount of psilocin present was primarily in the stems (except the Amazon strain where a trace was in the cap). See [[mushroom-drying-storage]] for harvesting and preservation of mushrooms.
