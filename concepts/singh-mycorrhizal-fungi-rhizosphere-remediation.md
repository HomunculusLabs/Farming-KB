## Classification of Mycorrhizal Fungi

Seven types of mycorrhizal associations (Smith and Read, 1997):
1. **Arbuscular (VAM)**: most ancient; ~2/3 of plants colonized; ~150 taxa in Order Glomales
2. **Ectomycorrhizal (ECM)**: second most common; more specialized in nutrient capture
3. **Ectendomycorrhiza**: intermediate type
4. **Ericoid (ERM)**: associated with Ericaceae; important for phenolic degradation
5. **Arbutoid**
6. **Orchid**
7. **Monotrophic**

ECM fungi are extremophiles, occurring in habitats with extreme temperature, pH, salt/metal concentration, and drought. Mycorrhizas are obligate symbionts with little independent growth ability.

## Mycelial Functions in Soil

- Mycelial lengths up to 2000 m/m root documented
- Surface area up to 47-fold increase over roots alone
- Mycelia can exceed 300 m2 area
- Proliferation in patches/mats captures nitrogen and phosphorus
- ECM fungi mobilize essential nutrients from rock by excreting organic acids
- Reduce bacterial activity near [[singh-mycelial-mats-and-biological-soil-crusts]] of ECM fungi enhance pollutant oxidation (Gramss, 1997)

## Petroleum Hydrocarbon Degradation

ECM fungi survive in petroleum-contaminated soils and utilize crude oil as nutrient:
- 90% of spruce root tips remain mycorrhizal at contamination time (Nicolotti and Egli, 1998)
- Six morphotypes identified; Laccaria spp. show best colonization
- VAM fungi (Glomus aggregatum, G. mosseae) occur in both polluted and nonpolluted soils
- Mycorrhizal fungi establish degradative consortia in root zone
- Parenchymatous patches support hydrocarbon-degrading bacterial biofilms
- Suillus bovinus + Pseudomonas fluorescens: degrades m-toluate

## PAH Degradation

### ECM Fungi PAH Capabilities (Braun-Lullemann et al., 1999)
- 16 species (27 strains) tested against phenanthrene, chrysene, pyrene, benzo[a]pyrene
- [[allegro-amanita-muscaria-properties-and-pharmacology]], Paxillus involutus, Suillus grevillei: ~50% phenanthrene utilization
- Multiple species removed ~50% benzo[a]pyrene in 4 weeks
- S. grevillei: ~50% pyrene removal
- Boletus edulis, A. muscaria: 35% chrysene removal
- No correlation between aromatic ring number and degradation rate
- Rate of benzo[a]pyrene metabolism slow but efficient (correlates with white-rot fungi)

### Gramss et al. (1999) -- 58 Fungi
- Only 1 of 21 species does not degrade at least one PAH
- Over half degrade all five PAHs tested
- Oxidative enzymes may have ecological role in ECM fungi
- Active oxygen important for aromatic compound oxidation by field-grown ECM mats

## TNT Degradation

- Paxillus involutus, Pisolithus tinctorius, [[suillus-variegatus]], Tylospora fibrillosa transform TNT
- S. variegates: 600% higher transformation by intact mycelium vs cultural filtrate (extracellular enzymes)
- Suggests multiple pathways in TNT transformation
- TNT EC50 for S. variegatus: 2-10 ug/ml (comparable to P. chrysosporium at 12 mg/g soil)
- Biotransformation requires sufficient carbon (consistent with symbiotic plant association)

## Lignin and Phenolic Degradation

### ECM Fungi
- Five ECM species metabolize 14C-labeled lignin/lignocellulose (rate lower than white-rot fungi)
- Paxillus involutus (facultative ECM): degrades lignin more readily than obligate ECM (Suillus bovinus, Rhizopogon roseolus)
