# Psilocin Accumulation Across Flushes

Psilocin, the dephosphorylated and more labile psychoactive compound found in [[psilocin-biosynthetic-delay-hypothesis-successive-flushes]] that differs markedly from [[psilocybin-psilocin-biosynthetic-timing-first-flush-absence]] pathway may be upregulated later in the fruiting cycle.

### Why Absent in Early Flushes?

Several hypotheses could explain the absence of psilocin in early flushes:

1. **Enzyme maturation**: The phosphatase or biosynthetic enzyme may require time to accumulate to detectable levels
2. **Substrate availability**: Early flushes may deplete a precursor or cofactor required for psilocin production
3. **Regulatory suppression**: Early in the fruiting cycle, metabolic resources may be directed toward [[gottlieb-psilocybin-production-extraction-methods]], with psilocin biosynthesis suppressed until later
4. **Oxidative degradation**: Psilocin is more susceptible to oxidation than psilocybin; early flush conditions may favor degradation
5. **Compartmentalization**: Psilocin may be produced but rapidly converted or sequestered in early [[cap-and-stem-alkaloid-distribution-in-psilocybe-cubensis-strains]] tissues:

### First Flush Distribution

| Tissue | M.R. Strain | Ecuadorian | Amazon |
|--------|------------|------------|--------|
| Caps psilocin | 0 mg/g | 0 mg/g | 0.1 mg/g |
| Stems psilocin | 0.35 mg/g | 0.4 mg/g | 0 mg/g |

Key observations:
- In M.R. and Ecuadorian strains, psilocin was found **exclusively in stems**
- The Amazon strain showed a trace of psilocin in caps but none in stems
- This compartmentalization difference may reflect strain-specific expression of biosynthetic enzymes
- Stem-localized psilocin could serve a protective function (e.g., antimicrobial defense of structural tissue)
