## Terminology for Nonordinary Dimensions

Shamanic, esoteric, and magical traditions across cultures have long referred to
realms beyond ordinary sensory experience using various names:

- The **"inner world"** suggests a landscape within the psyche accessible
  through introspection
- The **"spirit world"** implies a dimension inhabited by nonmaterial beings
- The **"upper or lower world"** of shamanic cosmology maps a vertical axis of
  consciousness with different realms above and below the ordinary plane
- The **"faerie world"** of Celtic tradition points to an otherworld contiguous
  with but distinct from ordinary reality
- The Australian Aboriginal **"dreamtime"** describes a creative dimension where
  ancestral beings shaped the world
- The general term **"otherworld"** captures realms existing alongside ordinary
  reality

The anthropologist Michael Harner introduced the term "nonordinary reality" to
describe these realms from a cross-cultural perspective, providing neutral
terminology that does not privilege any particular cultural framework.

## Phenomenology of World Access

The access to otherworlds takes different phenomenological forms consistently
reported across cultures and throughout history.

### The Journey Model

One common pattern involves a kind of journey to another world, perhaps
traveling on the back of an animal or being carried by a large bird. This model
emphasizes movement through space and time, with the traveler experiencing a
transition from ordinary to nonordinary reality. The sense of actually traveling,
of leaving one place and arriving at another, is pervasive in shamanic accounts.

### The Perceptual Model

Alternatively, one may feel that one can see into the spirit world without
physically moving, while still remaining aware of the ordinary present world.
This model involves an expansion or intensification of ordinary vision rather
than displacement from it. The practitioner remains seated in the ceremony but
perceives layers of reality ordinarily invisible.

### Bidirectional Permeability

In some experiences, the boundaries between worlds become permeable in both
directions. Scenery and beings of the other world may appear in our world,
superimposed on or interpenetrating ordinary reality. This suggests that the
worlds are interconnected dimensions of a larger, more complex reality rather
than completely separate realms.
