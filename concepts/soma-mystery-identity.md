## Soma Mystery and Identity

Soma is the divine sacrament of the Vedic religion, celebrated in over
120 hymns of the Rigveda as a plant that conferred immortality, inspired
ecstatic visions, and served as the central ritual substance of ancient
Indo-Aryan worship. Despite its paramount importance in one of the
world's oldest religious traditions, the botanical identity of Soma has
remained one of the enduring mysteries of ethnobotany and religious
history. No consensus has ever been reached among scholars.

## The Vedic Evidence

The Rigveda describes Soma in exuberant but contradictory terms. It is
called "the pillar of the sky," "the tongue of the gods," and "the
swift conqueror." It grew on mountains, was pressed between stones to
extract its juice, was filtered through wool, and was consumed by
priests to produce visionary experiences and a sense of divine union.

The physical descriptions are simultaneously specific and elusive. Soma
was described as having no leaves, no blossoms, and no fruit. It was
yellow or tawny in color. It grew in the mountains, was brought down
by traders, and was consumed fresh — dried Soma was considered
ineffective. These details have fueled centuries of speculation about
its identity.
