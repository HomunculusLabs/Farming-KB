## Gypsum, Lime, and pH Management in Substrates

Proper management of substrate chemistry, particularly pH and
mineral content, plays a critical role in successful mushroom
cultivation. Gypsum (calcium sulfate) and lime (calcium hydroxide)
are the two most commonly used amendments. Understanding their
distinct functions helps cultivators optimize substrate conditions
for each mushroom species.

## Gypsum (Calcium Sulfate)

Gypsum serves multiple important functions in [[brassinosteroid-psilocybe-cubensis-mycelial-growth-research]] of Shiitake in [[growing-gourmet-straw-pasteurization-methods]]. By immersing straw into
water baths high in hydrated lime, competitor fungi and bacteria
are largely rendered inactive from the drastic change in pH.

Two to four pounds of lime is added for every 50 gallons of water,
equivalent to about 0.5-1.0% by weight. The pH of the water
skyrockets to 9.5 or higher. Once dissolved, chopped straw is
immersed into this highly alkaline bath. Under these caustic
conditions, pH-sensitive microorganisms soon die.

After an overnight soaking, the water is drained and discarded.
It is not unusual for the straw to achieve a pH of 8.5 or higher
after soaking. [[brassinosteroid]]
