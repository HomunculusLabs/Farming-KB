# Mushroom Culinary Preparation and Cooking

Duggar's 1915 treatise on [[duggar-mushroom-growing-history-and-methods]] devotes an entire chapter to
mushrooms for the table, reflecting the era's growing appreciation for the
cultivated mushroom as a kitchen staple. His guidance on selection,
preparation, and [[solar-cooking-methods]] offers a window into early twentieth-century
culinary practice and remains remarkably relevant to [[modern-mushroom-festival-culture]] cookery.

## Selection and Freshness

The impression that [[fungi-cultivated-mushrooms-pathogens-competitors]] must be consumed immediately is
partly mistaken. When properly gathered and marketed in relatively small,
well-aerated containers, mushrooms keep for two to three days even in warm
weather. In a cold, dry place or a properly maintained refrigerator, they
last considerably longer. The critical precaution, applicable to nearly any
fresh food, is never to keep mushrooms in a tight box during warm weather,
as this accelerates spoilage.

For home-grown mushrooms gathered in small quantities, cleanliness is
typically excellent. Cutting off the stem end may be the only preparation
required. Sprinkling the beds, however, can spatter soil onto the caps,
making brushing advisable at harvest time. Mushrooms purchased from market
vendors will generally require washing, though soaking must be strictly
avoided, as it results in a significant loss of flavor.

## Peeling and Trimming Practices

Different mushroom species demand different preparation approaches. For
puffballs, boletes, and fully mature agarics, peeling is usually necessary.
The stems of cap-bearing forms should be scraped or peeled depending on
their texture. Buttons of [[agaricus-campestris-morphology-and-life-cycle]]
and other delicate species need not be peeled at all, and in the highest
quality commercial production, buttons often reach giant size with skins
so tender that only light scraping is needed. Older specimens require
peeling from the margin inward toward the center.

Morels, clavarias, and branched forms of Hydnum cannot be peeled under any
circumstances, and all parts of these mushrooms are edible. This
distinction between species that benefit from peeling and those that do not
reflects fundamental differences in cuticle structure across mushroom
families.

## Stem Utilization

Many recipes call exclusively for mushroom caps, leaving the cook to wonder
about the stems. In higher quality commercial mushrooms, stems are not so
elongated as to be tough, and they may always be used in dressings,
stuffings, and soups. For soup-making, stems are actually more desirable
than the gill surfaces because they contribute body without clouding the
broth. This complete utilization of the mushroom reflects the economical
approach characteristic of the era's household cookery.

## Cooking Principles

Mushroom cookery in Duggar's time followed several well-established
principles that modern chefs would recognize. Mushrooms should be cooked
quickly over high heat to preserve their texture and develop their flavor
through the Maillard reaction. Overcooking renders them rubbery and
watery, as their high water content is expelled into the pan. The addition
of butter or cream complements their earthy flavor, while a squeeze of
lemon juice brightens the finished dish.

The mushroom's own juices, released during cooking, form the basis of
sauces and reductions. These natural liquids carry the mushroom's
characteristic umami flavor, which was not yet understood chemically
in 1915 but was appreciated instinctively by experienced cooks.

## Historical Context

The cultivation of [[agaricus-bisporus-cultivation-biology]] in the early
[[coleman-louis-savier-twentieth-century-maraicher]] was still largely a specialized enterprise, and the
majority of mushrooms reaching urban markets came from dedicated growers
operating cellars and caves near major cities. The growing appreciation
for mushroom cookery was part of a broader movement toward more
sophisticated American cuisine, influenced by French culinary traditions
where mushrooms had long been a staple.
