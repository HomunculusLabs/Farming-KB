# Permaculture System Establishment and Design Principles

System establishment in [[permaculture-design]] should achieve:
1. **Reduced need to earn** through conservation of food and energy costs
2. **Repair and conservation** of degraded landscapes, buildings, soils, and species at risk
3. **Sustainable production** across short, medium, and long timeframes
4. **A unique, preferably essential service or product** for the region
5. **Right livelihood** — meaningful work for occupants in services or goods
6. **Sound legal status** for occupiers
7. **A harmonious, productive landscape** without wastes or poisons
8. **Cooperative, information-rich participation** in regional society

## Foundational Design Principles

### Definition of Permaculture Design
Permaculture design is a system of assembling conceptual, material, and strategic components in a pattern which functions to benefit life in all its forms. It seeks to provide a sustainable and secure place for living things on earth.

### Functional Design
Every component of a design should function in many ways. Every essential function should be supported by many components. This principle of **functional redundancy** ensures resilience — if one component fails, others continue to provide the essential service.

### Principle of Self-Regulation
The purpose of a functional and self-regulating design is to place elements or components so that each serves the needs and accepts the products of other elements. This creates closed-loop systems where outputs become inputs, wastes become resources, and external inputs are minimized.

## The Nucleus Philosophy

Starting with a nucleus and expanding outward is the most successful, morale-building, and easily achieved approach. Broadscale systems incur broadscale losses and inefficiencies. The recommended sequence:

1. Design the whole system on paper first
2. Develop a small, complete nucleus
3. Observe results and gather information
4. Expand using proven species and techniques
5. Maintain the relationship of each nucleus plan to the larger whole

## Management and Adaptation

Design methodologies must incorporate flexibility in management. Success depends on:
- Steering based on trial results
- Acting on new information as it emerges
- Continuing to observe without discrimination
- Remaining open to diverse techniques

The success of any design ultimately depends on how it is accepted and implemented by the people who live with it. This explains why grand centralized schemes more often result in ruins and monuments than in stable, occupied, well-maintained ecologies.

Responsible design arises from recommending to others the way you have found it possible to work or live in a similar situation. It is more effective to educate people to plan for themselves than to employ a permanent, expensive corps of planners who lead lives unrelated to the conditions or people they design for.

## Pioneer Designers in New Regions

Designers who are pioneers in a region should capitalize on that position by providing resources for newcomers: species as nursery stock, educational resources, food processing services, or lease arrangements. The first successful design becomes a demonstration and resource hub for the broader community.
