# Fungal Metabolism of Polycyclic Aromatic Hydrocarbons

Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) are ubiquitous environmental contaminants formed during incomplete combustion of organic matter. Fungi metabolize PAHs through both ligninolytic enzyme systems (in white-rot fungi) and cytochrome P450 monooxygenase systems (in non-ligninolytic fungi), with generally slower mineralization rates than bacteria but important ecological roles in soil.

## Fungal Metabolic Strategies

### White-Rot Fungi (Ligninolytic Pathway)
White-rot fungi such as Phanerochaete chrysosporium and Pleurotus ostreatus use extracellular lignin-modifying enzymes ([[lignin-peroxidase-mechanism]], [[lignin-peroxidase-mechanism]], [[laccase-mediator-system-dye]]) to oxidize PAHs. This nonspecific radical-based attack can occur on sorbed PAHs and is not limited by bioavailability constraints.

### Non-Ligninolytic Fungi (Cytochrome P450 Pathway)
Cunninghamella elegans, Aspergillus niger, and Syncephalastrum racemosum use intracellular cytochrome P450 monooxygenase systems that initiate PAH oxidation through epoxidation or hydroxylation, similar to mammalian liver metabolism.

## Key PAH Metabolism Findings

### Naphthalene (2-ring PAH)
- Candida tropicalis converts naphthalene to 1-naphthol, trans-1,2-dihydroxy-1,2-dihydronaphthalene, and 1,4-naphthoquinone
- Metabolites conjugated as sulfate esters and glucosides

### Anthracene and Phenanthrene (3-ring PAHs)
- Pleurotus ostreatus metabolizes both through initial epoxidation at K-region (C9,10)
- Phenanthrene 9,10-dihydrodiol is a major metabolite from T. versicolor, Kuehneromyces mutabilis, Agrocybe aegerita, and Flammulina velutipes
- Phase II conjugation produces sulfates and glucuronides of phenanthrols
- White-rot fungi oxidize the K-region; other fungi produce C1,2, C3,4, and C9,10 substituted metabolites

### Fluorene (3-ring PAH with five-membered ring)
- 96% degradation by Cunninghamella strains (mean rate)
- Key metabolites: 9-fluorenol and 9-fluorenone (monooxygenation of aliphatic ring)
- Pleurotus ostreatus metabolizes 96% of fluorene in Basidiomycetes-rich medium
- Surfactant Tween 80 enhances biodegradation (efficiency: Tween 80 > Triton X-100 > SDS)

### Fluoranthene (4-ring PAH)
- Most abundant tetracyclic aromatic hydrocarbon in environmental samples
- Cunninghamella strains show 86-98% degradation range (most efficient group)
- Of 39 Micromycete strains, 16 degraded more than 60% in 4 days
- Better degraded through cometabolism than as sole carbon source

### Pyrene (4-ring PAH)
- Initial oxidation by P. ostreatus produces pyrene-4,5-oxide, then trans-4,5-dihydrodiol
- Mineralization by fungi is generally low despite extensive initial oxidation

## Bioavailability Enhancement

Surfactants significantly improve PAH biodegradation rates:
- Tween 80 > Triton X-100 > sodium dodecyl sulfate (efficiency ranking)
- Mechanism: micellar solubilization increases PAH aqueous concentration
- Effectiveness varies among fungal species

## Ecological Significance

Fungi contribute to PAH degradation in soil through:
- Extracellular enzymatic attack on PAHs sorbed to soil particles
- Oxidation of high-molecular-weight PAHs recalcitrant to bacteria
- Cometabolic degradation alongside lignocellulose decomposition
- Mycelial exploration of soil that increases contact with contaminants
