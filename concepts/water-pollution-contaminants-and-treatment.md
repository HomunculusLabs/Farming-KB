# Water Pollution Contaminants and Treatment

> The only long-term insurance of a good water supply is the rigorous control of a forested catchment, with a total ban on biocides and metallic processing.
> — [[bill-mollison]], *Permaculture: A Designers' Manual*

## Water Purification Philosophy

Water quality management in permaculture rests on a foundational ethic: **prevention of pollution is far more important than treatment**. The biological treatment of common contaminants is strongly emphasised over chemical or technological fixes.

Key principles include:

- **Forested catchments** provide the only reliable long-term insurance of clean water, provided biocides and metallic processing are strictly banned within them.
- **Water safe for humans must also be safe for all other living things** — the standard cannot be anthropocentric.
- The **30–40 additives** commonly introduced into municipal water supplies are themselves often pollutants, undermining the very purpose of treatment.
- A basic hydrological reality: **the sewage of upstream settlements is the drinking water of downstream areas**.
- Every user has a duty to release only water of **sufficiently good quality to be safely useable by others** downstream.

Biological approaches to water purification are preferred because they work in harmony with natural systems, are self-maintaining, and avoid introducing secondary contaminants.

## Problem Contaminants

### Turbidity

- Silt and fine particles suspended in water reduce light penetration and carry adsorbed pollutants.
- Often the most visible sign of degraded catchment conditions, especially where vegetation has been removed.

### Bacterial and Organic Contaminants

- Sewage decay products and faecal coliforms (*E. coli*).
- Disease organisms including viral and protozoan pathogens (e.g., *Giardia*, *Cryptosporidium*).
- Parasitic worm eggs, particularly in regions with inadequate sanitation.
- Organic loads deplete dissolved oxygen, creating anaerobic conditions harmful to aquatic life.

### Metallic Pollutants

- **Chromium, cadmium, lead, mercury** — cumulative toxins that bioaccumulate in food chains.
- Even trace concentrations are dangerous because metals are persistent and concentration increases through biological magnification.

### Biocides and Persistent Organic Pollutants

- Chlorinated hydrocarbons: Aldrin, Dieldrin, DDT, dioxin, PCB.
- Herbicides: 2,4-D, 2,4,5-T.
- Organophosphates and halogenated hydrocarbons.
- Many are extremely stable in the environment and resist biological breakdown.

### Excessive Fertiliser

- Nitrogenous compounds (nitrates, ammonia, nitrites).
- Phosphates promoting eutrophication and algal blooms.
- Sodium and potassium salts from agricultural runoff altering water chemistry.

### Acids

- Water with **pH below 5.5** dramatically increases the solubility and toxicity of metallic pollutants.
- Acid rain is a primary driver of dissolved metal contamination in otherwise clean catchments.

## Contaminant Interactions and Synergies

Contaminants rarely act in isolation; their interactions often produce effects far worse than the sum of individual impacts:

- **Acid rain** dissolves poisonous forms of aluminium, mercury, lead, cadmium, and selenium from rocks and soil, and leaches copper, nickel, and lead from storage tanks and pipes.
- **Biological conversion of metals**: Organisms in water convert inorganic mercury into organic forms (methylmercury) that are readily absorbed by the human body. The Minamata disaster in Japan is the classic example.
- **Sewage in water aids the conversion** of metals to biologically active, assimilable forms.
- **Mercuric fungicide on seeds** causes direct poisoning of consumers and accumulates in soil, contaminating future crops.
- **Excessive artificial fertiliser** increases aquatic biological activity, which in turn increases metal uptake in already acidic waters — a compounding cycle.
- **Aldrin prevents DDT excretion** from the body; the combination is far more deadly than either alone.
- **DDT is a stable residual poison** that co-distils with water, meaning conventional distillation will **not** remove it.
- **Asbestos-cement pipes** used in public water supplies pose a threat of stomach and bladder cancer through fibre release.

## Aluminium and Cooking

Water treatment commonly involves adding ferric or aluminium sulphate, salt, and lime to flocculate suspended particles. This practice carries hidden dangers:

- As **pH increases due to acid rain**, aluminium goes into solution rather than precipitating out.
- Dissolved aluminium **binds with lead and cadmium to proteins** in vegetables and meat, especially during boiling or steaming.
- **Cooking may increase the metal content of food by a factor of 5** due to protein binding, making metals far easier for the body to assimilate.
- Cooking **acidic substances in aluminium pots** (tomatoes, vinegar, citrus) significantly worsens aluminium leaching.
- Aluminium leached by acidic rain is a **major cause of tree dieback and lake death** in affected regions.
- **Ferric sulphate** may be a safer coagulant choice, especially if water is alkaline.
- **Any inorganic or metallic salt added to water deserves cautious use**; the cure may be worse than the disease.

## Common Water Treatments

### Aeration

- Methods: wind-driven aerators, mechanical pumps, turbulent flow features, trickle columns, vegetation and phytoplankton activity, injected compressed air.
- Increases dissolved oxygen, supports aerobic bacterial decomposition, and strips volatile organic compounds.

### Settling

- Spreading flow across still-water ponds or rush beds allows suspended particles to settle by gravity.
- Simple, low-energy, and highly effective for turbidity removal when adequate retention time is provided.

### Skimming and Sieving

- Removes large organic particles, leaf litter, and surface debris before finer treatment stages.
- First line of defence in multi-stage treatment systems.

### Filtration

- **Sand beds**: slow sand filtration removes bacteria and fine particles through biological and physical processes.
- **Charcoal-fibre columns**: adsorb organic chemicals and some metals.
- **Soil filtration**: water passed through healthy soil is naturally purified by microbial communities.
- **Aquatic plant roots**: constructed wetlands and reed beds provide continuous biological filtration.

### Coagulation and Flocculation

- **Chemical methods**: lime, salt, ferric sulphates cause particles to clump and settle — but introduce their own contaminants.
- **Organic methods**: bacterial gels achieve similar results without adding metallic salts, aligning with permaculture's biological approach.

### Biological Removal

- **Bacteria** break down organic wastes and some pollutants.
- **Phytoplankton** absorb nutrients and metals, though blooms must be managed.
- **Higher aquatic plants** (reed beds, water hyacinth, duckweed) actively concentrate and sequester contaminants.

### pH Adjustment

- Adding calcium compounds (lime, agricultural limestone) raises pH toward neutral, reducing metal solubility.
- Sulphur compounds can lower excessively alkaline water.
- Maintaining pH between 6.5–8.0 is critical for minimising metallic pollutant mobility.

## Summary

The permaculture approach to water quality is fundamentally preventive: protect the catchment, eliminate pollution at source, and use biological treatment methods that work with natural processes rather than against them. Chemical treatments should be regarded with suspicion, as they frequently introduce new problems while addressing old ones. The ultimate test of water quality is whether it supports healthy ecosystems — not merely whether it passes human drinking standards.
