## Greenhouse Heating and Temperature Control

providing just enough heat to keep frost at bay, the greenhouse becomes
far more productive. Half-hardy perennials like fuchsias and geraniums can
be overwintered, and tender plants can be started much earlier.

## Controlling Temperatures

When heating a greenhouse, the key statistics are the minimum temperature
at night and the maximum during the day. In a closed greenhouse, spring
temperatures can reach 95 degrees F (35 degrees C), while nights may be
much colder. Such fluctuations are disastrous for young seedlings. Use a
maximum/minimum thermometer to record daily extremes and try to even out
the differences through careful ventilation and heating adjustment.

## Heating Methods

### Electricity

Using electricity is one of the cheapest options and certainly the most
convenient. Temperature control is very accurate and cost-effective.
Portable fan or convection heaters keep air circulating well throughout
the greenhouse. Permanent electric heating systems must be professionally
installed with special waterproofing of all equipment. Electric soil-
heating cables can warm root zones directly, reducing the need to heat
the air.

### Gas

Gas heating is more expensive than solid fuel but very convenient. It can
use portable propane or butane cylinders or be piped from the household
supply. Gas heaters are efficient since they can be controlled with a
thermostat and do not require constant refuelling. However, they need a
fairly large initial investment and can emit fumes harmful to plants.
Burners must be trimmed regularly to prevent harmful emissions.
