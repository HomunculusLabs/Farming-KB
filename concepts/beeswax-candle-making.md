# Beeswax Candle Making

## Overview

Beeswax candles are made from the wax produced by [[honey-extraction-and-processing]] into a valuable, practical product.

## Properties of Beeswax

Beeswax has unique physical and chemical properties that make it an excellent candle material:

- **Melting point**: 62-65°C (144-149°F) — significantly higher than tallow (40-50°C) and comparable to harder paraffin grades. This high melting point means beeswax candles hold their shape well in warm conditions and resist dripping
- **Density**: 0.95-0.97 g/cm3 — slightly less dense than water, causing candles to float
- **Color**: Natural beeswax ranges from pale yellow to deep amber, depending on the flowers the bees foraged on and the age of the wax. Cappings wax (freshly removed during honey extraction) is typically the lightest in color. Old comb wax darkens with age and brood rearing
- **Scent**: Natural beeswax has a characteristic warm honey scent. The intensity varies with wax color and source. Lighter wax has a subtler scent; darker wax has a more pronounced honey aroma
- **Hardness**: Beeswax is relatively hard and brittle at room temperature, which contributes to excellent drip resistance and long burn times but makes it prone to cracking if bent sharply
- **Smoke and soot**: Beeswax candles produce virtually no smoke when properly wicked. The flame is warm and slightly larger than a comparable paraffin candle
- **Ion emission**: When burned, beeswax releases negative ions that bind to positively charged particles in the air (dust, pollen, mold spores, odors), potentially improving indoor [[greg-green-odor-control-and-air-filtration]], it is a unique benefit of beeswax candles

## Candle Wicking for Beeswax

Beeswax requires larger wicks than paraffin or soy because beeswax is denser and more viscous when molten:

- **Square-braided cotton**: The standard wick for beeswax pillars and tapers. The square braid provides a slight curl that promotes even burning and self-trimming
- **Wick sizing guide**: 2-3 cm diameter candle = #2 square braid; 4-5 cm = #3 square braid; 5-7 cm = #4 square braid; 7-10 cm = #5 or #6 square braid. Undersized wicks produce small, dim flames and tallow pooling; oversized wicks produce large, smoky flames and excessive wax consumption
- **Pre-waxing wicks**: Dip raw cotton wicks in melted beeswax and straighten them while still warm. Pre-waxed wicks are stiffer, easier to thread through molds, and burn more consistently
- **Wick tabs**: Small metal bases used for container candles. Crimp a pre-waxed wick into a tab and adhere to the container bottom with a drop of melted wax or a hot glue gun

## Candle Making Methods

### Rolled Beeswax Candles

The simplest method, requiring no heat. Uses commercially available beeswax sheets:

1. Lay a beeswax sheet flat on a clean surface
2. Place a pre-cut wick along one edge, extending 1 cm above and below the sheet
3. Fold the edge of the sheet over the wick and press firmly to seal
4. Roll the sheet tightly and evenly, applying gentle pressure to ensure good contact between layers
5. Seal the outer edge by pressing firmly or warming briefly with a hair dryer
6. Trim both ends and the wick

Rolled candles are quick to make, require no equipment, and are an excellent introduction to candle making. Beeswax sheets come in various colors (natural and dyed) and honeycomb or smooth textures.

### Dipped Beeswax Candles

Traditional method producing elegant, hand-dipped tapers:

1. Melt beeswax to 70-75°C (158-167°F) in a tall container. The wax must be deep enough to fully submerge the desired candle length
2. Cut wicks to desired length plus 10 cm for hanging. Tie weights to the bottom ends
3. Dip the wick into the wax and withdraw slowly and steadily. Speed of withdrawal affects the thickness of each layer — slower withdrawal builds thicker layers
4. Hang to cool for 2-3 minutes. Beeswax solidifies quickly due to its relatively high melting point
5. Repeat dipping 15-30 times, depending on desired thickness. Alternate hanging direction (flip 180°) every few dips for even thickness
6. For a smooth finish, roll the still-warm candles on a smooth, clean surface after every 5-6 dips
7. Allow to cool completely, then trim the bottoms flat and wicks to 6-10 mm

### Molded Beeswax Candles

Produces consistent, professional-looking candles in various shapes:

1. Prepare molds by cleaning and applying a light coat of mold release (silicone spray or vegetable oil)
2. Thread pre-waxed wicks through the mold, securing the bottom with mold sealer or rubber putty
3. Melt beeswax to 75-80°C (167-176°F) — slightly hotter than for dipping, to ensure smooth pouring and reduce surface imperfections
4. Pour slowly and steadily into the mold to minimize air bubbles. Fill to the top
5. Beeswax shrinks significantly on cooling (more than paraffin). Poke relief holes near the wick after the surface has skinned over (about 30 minutes), then pour additional melted wax to fill the depression
6. Allow to cool completely at room temperature for 6-8 hours or refrigerate for 2-3 hours
7. Remove from molds. If stuck, place in a freezer for 10-15 minutes — the contraction will release the candle from the mold
