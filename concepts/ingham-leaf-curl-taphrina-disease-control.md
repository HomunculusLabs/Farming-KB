### Spring Soil Drench

Apply another soil drench in the spring, before bud break. This re-establishes beneficial organisms in the soil after winter conditions may have reduced populations. The soil organisms will continue decomposing any remaining infected leaf debris and provide a biological buffer around the root zone.

### Pre-Budbreak Foliar Spray

Apply AACT as a foliar spray **two weeks before budbreak**. This timing is critical. The tea must be on the plant surfaces before the buds begin to open and the new, vulnerable leaves emerge. The beneficial organisms need time to establish themselves so they are already present and functioning when the new growth appears.

### Growing Season Maintenance

During the growing season, apply compost tea as a foliar spray at minimum once per month. For more severe disease pressure, increase frequency to every 10 to 14 days. The specific interval depends on disease severity, environmental conditions, and the overall health of the soil foodweb.

## Understanding Why Leaf Curl Responds to Fungi

The mechanism of leaf curl suppression illustrates a general principle of biological disease control. *Taphrina*, like many foliar pathogens, has relatively weak enzymatic capability. It cannot break down the cell walls or consume the food resources that beneficial organisms are already using. When the leaf surface is occupied by a diverse community of bacteria and fungi, the pathogen has nowhere to germinate, no available food, and no exposed leaf surface to infect.

This is fundamentally different from chemical disease control, which relies on toxic compounds that kill the pathogen directly.
