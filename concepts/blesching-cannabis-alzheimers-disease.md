# Cannabis and Alzheimer's Disease

## Overview

Alzheimer's disease (AD) is a chronic degenerative illness affecting the mind
and brain, partially characterized by selective neuronal loss (AD kills brain
cells) and cognitive deficits. Early symptoms include forgetfulness or decreased
cognitive function, eventually progressing to loss of communication abilities and
significant behavioral changes. The patient becomes completely dependent and
bedridden, with death usually from secondary sources such as trauma or chronic
infections. AD affects more women than men, typically beginning between ages 50
and 60, and is the main culprit for dementia.

The modern medical system lacks a cure or effective treatment for AD. Management
involves multidisciplinary supportive care, pharmaceutical medications, exercise,
nutrition, and patient safety supervision. Risk factors include lack of exercise,
obesity, hypertension, diabetes, depression, and smoking. One hypothesis proposes
beta-amyloid plaque as a factor, while another involves tau protein that
tangles and twists, preventing [[solomon-fertigation-bucket-drip-water-nutrient-delivery]] to brain cells. Scientists
believe these plaques and tangles induce neurochemical and inflammatory changes
responsible for disease development.
