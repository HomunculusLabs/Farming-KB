## The Scythians and the Sweat Lodge

The Scythians, a nomadic central Asian barbarian group who entered eastern Europe
around 700 B.C., are the people who brought the use of cannabis to the European world.
Herodotus describes their novel method of self-intoxication, a kind of cannabis sweat
lodge: they took hemp seed, crept under the cloths of the sweat lodge, and put the
seed on red-hot stones. The burning seed produced such steam that no Grecian
vapour-bath would surpass it. The Scythians, transported by the vapour, shouted
aloud.

Elsewhere Herodotus comments on another method: the Scythians discovered trees that
produced fruit of a peculiar kind which, when thrown on the fire and inhaled, caused
intoxication comparable to wine. The more fruit thrown on, the more intoxicated they
became, until they rose up to dance and betake themselves to singing. The passage
makes clear that though the Scythians had discovered that inhaling the smoke of
cannabis was the most effective way to enjoy it, they were nevertheless unable to
make the creative leap to the invention of the pipe or chelum. The Greek herbalist
and natural scientist Dioscorides also described cannabis, but until effective
smoking practices were adopted, it made no inroads into European and American
cultures.

## India and China

Chinese tradition holds that hemp cultivation began as early as the twenty-eighth
century B.C., when the emperor Shen-Nung taught the cultivation of hemp for fiber.
Around A.D. 220, the physician Hoa-tho evidently recommended hemp preparations in
wine as an anaesthetic: "After a certain number of days or the end of a month the
patient finds he has recovered without having experienced the slightest pain during
the operation."
