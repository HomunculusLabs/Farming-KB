## Fungal Decomposition of Lignin and Cellulose

## Overview

Fungi are the primary decomposers of lignocellulose—the
structural polymer complex that constitutes plant cell
walls and accounts for the bulk of terrestrial organic
carbon. Lignocellulose consists of cellulose (40-50%),
hemicellulose (25-35%), and lignin (15-30%). While
cellulose and hemicellulose are carbohydrate polymers
relatively accessible to enzymatic breakdown, lignin is
an irregular, cross-linked phenolic polymer that is among
the most recalcitrant organic molecules in nature. Fungi,
particularly basidiomycetes, are virtually alone in their
ability to efficiently degrade lignin, making them
indispensable agents in global carbon cycling.

co-substrate, generated by fungal oxidases including
glyoxal oxidase, aryl alcohol oxidase, and methanol
oxidase. Laccases use molecular oxygen directly, making
them potentially more efficient in oxygen-limited
environments. The regulation of these enzyme systems
responds to nutrient limitation—particularly nitrogen
starvation triggers ligninolytic gene expression.

## Ecological Significance

Wood [[holmgren-soil-humus-carbon-storage-rebuilding]]: brown rot leaves
more recalcitrant lignin residues that accumulate as
soil organic matter, while white rot more completely
mineralizes woody carbon to CO2.
