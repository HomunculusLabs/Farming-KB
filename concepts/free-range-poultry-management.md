# Free-Range Poultry Management

Free-range poultry and pig systems are integral components of [[rhode-island-red]],
Plymouth Rock, and Australorp.

### Other Breeds

The **Araucana** originates from South America and occupies its own distinct lineage. For
incubation and brooding, **Wyandottes** or **Silky Bantams** serve as reliable broody hens.
Hardy bantam varieties can breed and rear their own chicks directly on range.

## Egg Production

- **Heavy breeds**: 130–150 eggs per hen per year (rarely 180–200)
- **Light breeds**: 160–180 eggs per hen per year

A key advantage of free-range systems is longevity. Unstressed free-range flocks remain
productive for 4–6 years, whereas caged birds typically require replacement after only
18–20 months.

## Flock Structure and Behaviour

The natural flock size is **20–30 individuals**, including 2–3 cockerels. When flocks exceed
this size they naturally break into two separate groups. Light breeds, particularly
Hamburghs and Leghorns, are excellent foragers and can obtain most of their food on free
range. Heavy breeds require supplementary feeding, especially through winter.

## Feeding and Nutrition

In a well-managed free-range system, approximately **65% of food** comes from insects,
invertebrates, greens, and grains found on range, with **30–35%** supplied as concentrated
grain products.

### Range Forage Plants

A diverse planted range provides balanced nutrition:

- **Shrubs and trees**: [[chicken-tractor-system]]** is particularly effective for land preparation. Birds clear
weeds, bulbs, seeds, grasses, nutgrass, Kikuyu, onion weed, and Oxalis as they are rotated
through pens.

## Forage Crops After Chickens

After chickens have worked a section, sow these crops 2–3 months before birds return:

- Buckwheat, wheat, sunflower, millet, black nightshade, cleavers, chard, and mustard

These crops mature quickly, restoring soil organic matter and providing the next rotation
with fresh forage.

## Health and Disease Management

Diseases are rare on well-managed free systems. Essential health provisions include:

- **Dust baths** with [[diatomaceous-earth]], dried bracken, or neem leaves for ectoparasites
- **Wormwoods** for intestinal worm control
- **Shell grit and calcium gravel** for eggshell formation
- **Silica grit** for digestion
- **Clean water**, shade, and shelter
- **Predator protection** via secure housing and vigilant management

## Integration with Other Farm Systems

Poultry combines naturally with orchard, dairy, and grain farm economies. Mollison notes
that **no soft fruit orchard thrives without poultry foragers** — the birds control pests,
consume fallen fruit that would harbour disease, and cycle nutrients.

### Waterfowl Integration

- **Ducks**: kept alongside chickens; require marsh plants, snails, and shallow water;
  produce approximately 200 eggs per year
- **Geese**: traditional small grazers that replace lawnmowers in orchards

## Commercial Scale

A viable free-range egg enterprise requires approximately **13 hectares** and supports a
full-time family operation with around **3,000 birds**. Field-selected chickens typically
reach 3.5–4 kg live weight.

## Free-Range Pig Systems

### Site Selection

Ideal pig range sites are wooded, well-drained, and dry. Wet or muddy soils encourage worms
and disease. Electric fencing provides effective containment.

### Health and Parasite Control

- **Rubbing posts** wrapped in hessian and treated with sump oil, neem, or pyrethrum for
  lice control
