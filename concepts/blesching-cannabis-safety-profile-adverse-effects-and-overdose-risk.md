## Mortality Data: Zero Attributable Deaths

Blesching cites multiple U.S. government sources covering the period January 1997 through June 2005, which recorded **zero deaths attributable to the exclusive use of cannabis**. For context, he provides comparative CDC mortality estimates for the same general period:

An FDA report compared marijuana to seventeen common FDA-approved pharmaceutical drugs used to treat similar symptoms. Between 1997 and 2005, the seventeen pharmaceuticals were associated with 10,008 deaths, while cannabis recorded zero.

### Lethal Dose Calculations

Blesching cites two laboratory estimates of cannabis's lethal dose:

- **1973 study:** Median lethal oral dose of THC in rats was 800-1,900 mg/kg, extrapolating to approximately **200 grams of herb per kilogram of body weight** in humans. A 70 kg (154 lb) person would need to consume roughly **14 kg (31 lbs)** of cannabis to approach a fatal dose.
- **2004 study:** Even more conservative, estimating that **628 kg of cannabis would need to be smoked in 15 minutes** to induce a lethal effect — a physical impossibility.

As former U.S. Surgeon General Joycelyn Elders stated: "Unlike many of the drugs we prescribe every day, marijuana has never been proven to cause a fatal overdose."

## Adverse Effects

Blesching categorizes adverse effects by severity and mechanism:

### Common and Mild
- Increased appetite ("munchies")
- Reduced attention span
- Reddening of the sclera (bloodshot eyes)
- Dry mouth (cottonmouth)

### Less Common, Dose-Dependent
- Ataxia (unsteady gait)
- Aphasia (difficulty speaking clearly)
- Unusual sensory perceptions including hallucinations
- Anxiety (addressable by reassurance)
- Slight increase in heart rate
- Subtle blood pressure shifts depending on body position
- Panic upon first-ever use (generally self-limiting)
