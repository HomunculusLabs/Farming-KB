## Nature and Identification of Spawn

The spawn of cultivated mushrooms consists of fine, white, thread-like strands of mycelium that spread through the compost in all directions. These threads absorb the necessary water as well as organic and inorganic food materials required for growth. The mycelia of Agaricus campestris and related species are adapted to invade a great variety of substrata, including rich earth, moist forest floor leaves, fallen timber, and even the trunks of living trees. Each species has its own general habitat requirements, and the substratum must match these needs for vigorous mycelial growth.

## Accumulation of Nutrient Reserves

A critical feature of the mycelial phase is the accumulation of food materials within the mycelial network. The mycelium absorbs not only what it needs for its own growth but also builds up a considerable surplus of nutrients. This stored reserve is what enables the relatively rapid production of mushrooms once the fruiting process begins. The speed with which mushrooms appear after pin formation is a direct reflection of this accumulated energy reserve.

## Spawn Storage and Handling

Proper storage of mushroom spawn is critical for maintaining viability:

- Store at 2-4°C (refrigeration temperature) to slow mycelial metabolism
- Use within 2-3 months for grain spawn, longer for plug or sawdust spawn
- Avoid freezing, which damages cell walls and kills mycelium
- Check regularly for contamination signs (colored patches, unusual odors)

## Factors Affecting Mycelial Growth Rate

Several environmental factors influence how quickly mycelium colonizes substrate:

- **Temperature**: Each species has optimal growth temperature range
- **Moisture**: Substrate moisture content of 60-70% is typically ideal
- **pH**: Most species prefer slightly acidic conditions (pH 5.5-7.0)
- **Nutrition**: Carbon-to-nitrogen ratio affects colonization speed
- **Gas exchange**: Adequate fresh air exchange prevents CO2 buildup

## Commercial Spawn Production

Modern spawn production involves laboratory-scale sterile technique:

1. Select and maintain pure cultures on agar plates
2. Transfer mycelium to sterilized grain (rye, millet, or sorghum)
3. Incubate at species-appropriate temperature until fully colonized
4. Quality test for contamination and vigor before distribution

The spawn industry has developed specialized facilities with laminar flow hoods,
autoclaves, and climate-controlled incubation rooms to ensure consistent quality.
