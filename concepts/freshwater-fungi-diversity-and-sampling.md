# Freshwater Fungi Diversity and Sampling Methods

Source: Biodiversity of Fungi (Hyde et al., Ch. 22)

Freshwater habitats harbor a remarkable diversity of fungi including chytrids, hyphochytrids, peronosporomycetes (formerly oomycetes), aquatic hyphomycetes, and other groups. Many are microscopic and require specialized collection techniques.

## Chytrids and Hyphochytrids

### Distribution and Ecology
- Chytrid species appear distributed worldwide, with occurrence determined by local conditions rather than geography (Sparrow 1968)
- Endemism not known among chytrids and hyphochytrids
- Batrachochytrium dendrobatidis, a chytridialean pathogen of amphibians, may have recently spread to several continents (molecular evidence)
- In northern North America, conifers produce annual pollen showers; chytrid zoospores attacking pollen peak during the 2 weeks following peak pollen (Ulken & Sparrow 1968, MPN method)

### Collection Methods
Chytrids are microscopic and cannot be collected by unaided sight. "Baits" provide a fresh substratum for colonization:

**Common baits**:
- **Chitin**: Purified shrimp exoskeleton, insect wings
- **Cellulose**: Cellophane, lens paper, white onion skin, bleached corn straw
- **Keratin**: Defatted baby hair, snake skin, defatted wool fibers
- **Pollen**: Pine (Pinus), spruce (Picea), sweetgum (Liquidambar)
- **Larger baits** (seeds, fruits, twigs): Attract Blastocladiales

**Bait preparation**: All baits except pollen boiled 3-5 minutes before use.

### In Situ Baiting
Baits enclosed in weighted nylon net bags placed in habitat for ~1 week (>20°C) to ~1 month (<10°C). Pollen wrapped in lens paper to prevent loss. Retrieved into sterile containers.

### Laboratory "Gross Culture"
Small organic debris + habitat water added to deep glass Petri plate or finger bowl. Two pieces each of cellophane, onion skin, and chitin plus thin pollen shower added. Mostly water with small bait and debris — excess organic matter encourages bacteria over chytrids. Chytrids appear on pollen after 1-2 days; on other baits after several days. Cellulosic chytrid generation times: 4-7 days.

### Natural Substrata
- Algae (senescent leaves especially productive)
- Waterlogged wood (supports Monoblepharidales and Blastocladiales)
- Aquatic insect exuviae
- Eriocaulon, Typha, Utricularia, Potamogeton

### Isolation
- Place colonized substratum on 9cm nutrient agar plate (PmTG medium + antibiotics)
- Push sporangium through agar with fine needle to remove bacteria
- After 4-6 sporangia cleaned, seal plate with Parafilm
- Check daily by inverting plate onto compound microscope
- Transfer bacteria-free colonies to agar slants (20mm x 125mm screw-cap tubes)
- Most temperate isolates viable at least 3 months refrigerated
- Many retain viability after freezing in liquid nitrogen

### Peronosporomycetes (Formerly Oomycetes)
Snake skin, fruit, and seed baits attract peronosporomycetes which can rapidly overgrow chytrids. These are better placed in separate gross cultures when baiting for chytrids.

## Quantitative Assessment
- Bacteriological MPN and limnological methods for particulate substrata
- Crude abundance ranked by bait bags yielding target species
- Sampling during all seasons of several years and many microhabitats increases probability of finding most species
