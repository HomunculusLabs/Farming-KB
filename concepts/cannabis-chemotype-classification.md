# Cannabis Chemotype Classification
Cannabis chemotype classification organizes the species by its chemical
constituents rather than morphological traits or folk taxonomy. This
approach, pioneered by Ernest Small in the 1970s and refined by modern
analytical chemistry, provides a reproducible framework for understanding
the therapeutic and psychoactive properties of different cannabis
varieties.

## Modern Multidimensional Classification
Contemporary chemotaxonomy expands beyond the THC:CBD binary to
incorporate the full spectrum of cannabinoids and terpenes. A
multidimensional approach considers:
**Primary cannabinoid profile**: The relative proportions of THC, CBD,
CBC, CBG, THCV, CBDV, and their acidic precursors. Modern analytical
methods can quantify 15-20 individual cannabinoids simultaneously.
**Dominant terpene group**: The primary terpene or terpene cluster that
characterizes the aroma and modifies cannabinoid effects. Terpene
dominance patterns provide more reliable prediction of subjective effects
than cannabinoid ratios alone.
**Minor constituent profile**: Flavonoids (cannaflavins A and B),
alkylamides, and other [[cannabis-terpene-entourage-effect-profiles]] but are rarely quantified in standard testing.
**Total cannabinoid content**: The aggregate concentration of all
cannabinoids, which determines overall potency regardless of the specific
profile.
## Terpene-Based Functional Classification
An emerging classification system groups cannabis by dominant terpene
profile rather than cannabinoid content:
- **Type I (Myrcene-dominant)**: Sedative, relaxing, analgesic
  profile. Typically high in THC with myrcene comprising over 30% of
  the terpene fraction.
- **Type II (Limonene-dominant)**: Energizing, anxiolytic, mood-elevating
  profile. Limonene exceeding 15% of terpenes with supportive pinene and
  caryophyllene.
- **Type III (Caryophyllene-dominant)**: Anti-inflammatory, analgesic
  with minimal sedation. High caryophyllene with humulene and moderate
  myrcene.
