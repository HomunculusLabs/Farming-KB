# Kraft Pulp Bleach Effluent Fungal Decolorization

The pulp and paper industry releases large volumes of intensely coloured
wastewater, with bleach plant effluents (BPEs) being among the most
problematic. These effluents contain chlorophenols, chlorolignols, lignin
sulfonates, chlorolignins, and various lower molecular weight phenolic
compounds and their partly oxidized derivatives such as quinones. Many of
these materials are toxic to aquatic life and to microorganisms, making
effective treatment essential before discharge. The colour in pulping
effluents results primarily from dissolved derivatives or fragments of lignin
produced during the pulping and bleaching processes.

## Ligninolytic Enzyme Systems in BPE Treatment

Two principal fungal species have dominated research on fungal BPE treatment:
[[fungal-manganese-peroxidase-remediation]] (MnP) plays the primary role in BPE
decolorization. Lackner, Srebotnik, and Messner confirmed that MnP-mediated
oxidation of BPE is catalyzed by manganese(III) chelated to lactate or other
organic acids. Purified P. chrysosporium MnPs catalyzed BPE decolorization
in the presence of lactate, manganese(II), and [[fungal-lignin-peroxidase-veratryl-alcohol]] activity, leading to approaches
such as rotating biological contactors, percolating filters, and packed beds.
More recently, fluidized beds using mycelial pellets have been shown to be
effective. [[singh-fungal-degradation-endocrine-disrupting-compounds]].

## Performance Metrics and Scale-Up

Most authors routinely report 60 to 90 percent colour removal from pulp
effluents, with reports exceeding 90 percent achievable under optimized
conditions. Decolorization is typically complete in 3 to 6 days, though
longer periods may be required for some effluents. Large-scale demonstrations
include a 200-liter packed bed reactor that successfully treated Kraft
effluent. White rot mycelia are rugged enough for repeated use over many
cycles of decolorization covering periods of several weeks to months, and can
be stored at 4 degrees Celsius for up to 4 months while retaining full
decolorization activity. This storage stability is a valuable property for
industrial treatment applications requiring rapid deployment capability.
