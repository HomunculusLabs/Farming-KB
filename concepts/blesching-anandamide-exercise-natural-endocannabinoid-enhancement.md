# Anandamide, Exercise, and Natural Endocannabinoid Enhancement

**Source:** The Cannabis Health Index (Blesching, 2015)

## Overview

Anandamide (N-arachidonoylethanolamide, AEA) is an endogenous cannabinoid and
neurotransmitter produced in human cell membranes. Called the "Bliss Molecule,"
it is structurally similar to THC and binds at the same CB1 and CB2 receptor
sites, producing comparable effects at appropriate concentrations. Unlike THC,
anandamide is produced naturally by the body and can be enhanced through
specific lifestyle practices.

## Anandamide Biochemistry

- **Production:** Synthesized in human cell membranes on demand
- **Receptor affinity:** Binds relatively equally to both CB1 and CB2 receptors
- **Degradation:** Broken down by fatty acid amide hydrolase (FAAH); inhibiting
  FAAH keeps anandamide active longer
- **Emotional keyword:** "I am at ease" — encompassing bliss, relaxation, and
  social connection

## The Runner's High: Anandamide, Not Endorphins

The traditional explanation for the "runner's high" attributed the effect to
endorphins. However, high-intensity endurance running has been shown to
significantly increase anandamide levels, not endorphins. This discovery
reframed exercise as an endocannabinoid-enhancing activity.

### The Cortisol-Anandamide-Exercise Connection
The presence of corticosteroids (released during exercise) stimulates the body's
production of anandamide. This may account for the way anandamide influences
the neuropathic and antidepressant effects of exercise. The mechanism operates
as a positive feedback loop:

1. Physical exertion triggers cortisol release from adrenal glands
2. Corticosteroids stimulate anandamide production in cell membranes
3. Anandamide activates CB1 and CB2 receptors throughout the body
4. The result: reduced pain perception, elevated mood, and the characteristic
   sense of ease and well-being associated with sustained exercise

## Anandamide's Therapeutic Potential

### Mental Health
Anandamide may reduce hypertension, depression, fear, and anxiety. It
contributes to social play behavior and may be relevant in treating psychiatric
disorders characterized by impairments in social behavior, including autism
spectrum conditions.

### Pain Management
Both THC and synthetic cannabinoids reduce pain by inducing release of
endogenous opioids (dynorphin A and B). Anandamide similarly reduces pain but
through a potentially novel mechanism not yet fully understood — one that
does not depend on opioid receptor activation.

### Cancer
Anandamide inhibits the movement of cancer cells and may prevent metastasis
without negative effects to the patient's immune system. This finding suggests
that supporting natural anandamide levels may have anti-cancer implications.

### Norepinephrine Regulation
Anandamide inhibits norepinephrine levels, contributing to stress reduction.
This is particularly relevant for patients with anxiety disorders and PTSD,
where norepinephrine dysregulation is common.

## Natural Anandamide Enhancement Strategies

### Exercise
High-intensity endurance running is the most well-documented method for
boosting anandamide. The effect is dose-dependent: longer and more intense
exercise produces greater anandamide increases. Other forms of aerobic exercise
likely produce similar effects.

### Social Connection
Meaningful social interactions may increase anandamide levels naturally. This
finding aligns with research showing that social isolation reduces endocannabinoid
tone while positive social bonding enhances it. The anandamide-oxytocin
connection suggests these systems work synergistically.

### Meditation
Meditation practices may increase anandamide levels naturally. Combined with
the stress-reducing effects of meditation on cortisol, this creates a dual
pathway for endocannabinoid enhancement through contemplative practice.

### FAAH Inhibition
Certain foods and compounds may inhibit FAAH, the enzyme that breaks down
anandamide. By slowing anandamide degradation, these compounds extend its
duration of action:
- Dark chocolate contains compounds that may modestly inhibit FAAH
- Kaempferol (found in apples, grapes, and green tea) has FAAH-inhibiting
  properties
- Maca root has been studied for potential FAAH inhibition

### Cannabinoid Therapy
A balanced CB1:CB2 activating cannabinoid approach may support the body's
endogenous anandamide system by:
- Providing receptor stimulation that upregulates natural anandamide
  production
- Reducing the stress hormones that deplete endocannabinoid reserves
- Creating a positive cycle where plant cannabinoids and endogenous
  cannabinoids work synergistically

## Clinical Implications

### Exercise as Medicine
The anandamide-exercise connection provides a biological mechanism for
exercise's antidepressant and anxiolytic effects. For patients using cannabis
for mood disorders, combining cannabinoid therapy with regular exercise may
produce synergistic benefits and reduce the cannabis dose needed for
therapeutic effect.

### FAAH Inhibitor Pharmaceuticals
The pharmaceutical industry has pursued FAAH inhibitors as alternatives to
cannabinoid medicines. While early trials showed promise, some FAAH inhibitors
have caused serious adverse effects, underscoring the complexity of
endocannabinoid modulation compared to whole-plant cannabis approaches.

### The Bliss Deficit
Chronic stress, trauma, and certain genetic profiles (particularly FAAH gene
polymorphisms) may result in chronically low anandamide levels — a "bliss
deficit." This concept helps explain why some individuals are more vulnerable
to anxiety, depression, and social withdrawal, and why they may respond
particularly well to both cannabinoid therapy and exercise-based interventions.
