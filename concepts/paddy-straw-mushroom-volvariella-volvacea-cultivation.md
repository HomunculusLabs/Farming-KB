# Paddy Straw Mushroom Cultivation (Volvariella volvacea)

Updated: 2026-04-18

Paddy straw mushroom (Volvariella volvacea) is the most popular mushroom in rural areas of the Philippines and throughout Southeast Asia. Naturally growing on paddy straw and decomposing banana leaves, it has been cultivated for generations. Known locally as kabuteng dayami or kabuteng saging in the Philippines, it thrives in tropical temperatures (30-35C).

## Production Methods

### Outdoor Cultivation (Traditional Bed Method)

Uses agricultural wastes: dried paddy straw, rice stubbles, water lily, banana leaves, and stalks. The choice depends on local availability.

**Site selection:** Area free from ants, termites, and rodents. Preferably under trees with wide canopy. Spread rice hulls and burn to ash to eliminate pests and soil-borne pathogens.

**Bed preparation:**
1. Sun-dry collected bedding materials
2. Trim and bundle substrates into 12-inch lengths x 2-inch diameter bundles
3. Soak bundles for 12 hours in clean water
4. Drain to 65% moisture content
5. Pile bundled substrates into bed forms (ideal: 6 layers, 3 meters long)
6. Sprinkle spawn thinly on top of every layer

**Incubation:** Cover entire bed with plastic sheet to maintain 30-35C temperature for mycelial ramification and 28-30C for fruiting. First harvest in 10-14 days. Harvest button stages by pulling clusters from bed.

### Indoor Cultivation (Improved Method)

Three salient features: composting, pasteurization, and cultivation inside a mushroom house.

**Soaking:** Rice straw (any type) or rice stubbles soaked 12 hours in clean water.

**Composting:**
1. Pile soaked substrates, sprinkle with 1% molasses and 0.5% complete fertilizer
2. Cover with plastic sheets
3. On day 7: turn pile with spading fork; add 1% agricultural lime
4. Continue composting for total of 14 days

**Crating and steaming:**
1. Dispense composted substrate into 12x24x18 inch wooden crates (open on all sides)
2. Verify 65% moisture content (no drippings when squeezed)
3. Pack compactly into crates
4. Stack in steaming room
5. Steam for 4-6 hours at 60-80C

**Spawning:** Next morning, verify substrate temperature has dropped to 30C (higher temperatures will harm mycelia).

**Incubation:** Seal mushroom house. Maintain 30-35C with no ventilation and no light. Mycelial spread takes 7-10 days. When fruiting initials appear, lower temperature to 28C by sprinkling water on floor. First harvest 3-5 days after fruiting initials appear.

## Spawn Production

Multiple locally available substrates are used for spawn:

**Tobacco midrib (Northern Luzon):** Waste from cigar industry, soaked 3 days, washed, air-dried, mixed with sawdust. Placed in mayonnaise bottles, autoclaved.

**Rice hull (Central Luzon):** Waste from rice milling. Moistened, mixed with 10% corn meal or rice bran. Dispensed in heat-resistant polypropylene bags or microwaveable plastic trays.

**Leguminous tree leaves (Gliricidia, Leucaena):** Dried leaves soaked 3 days, air-dried, mixed with sawdust and rice bran at 7:3:1 ratio.

**Coffee hulls:** Used in coffee-growing areas.

All preparations at 65% moisture content.

## Economic and Technical Feasibility

### Economics
Fresh paddy straw mushroom sells at 150-180 pesos/kg (USD 3-3.50). Supply is erratic, making it a luxury food. The mushroom is preferred by Filipino consumers, and its nutriceutical properties are increasingly recognized.

### Environmental Conditions
Philippine climate: maximum temperature 30-35C, rainfall 55-225mm. Substrates (paddy straw, water lilies, banana leaves) abundant year-round. Similar conditions exist throughout most of tropical Southeast Asia.

### Zero Farm Wastes Integration

Spent substrate from paddy straw mushroom production can be further utilized:
- Substrate for [[pleurotus-tuberregium-sclerotia-cultivation]], Auricularia, Ganoderma, and Collybia
- Fishpond fertilizer for tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus)
- Feed for broiler chickens

This integrated approach creates a zero-waste system linking mushroom, crop, and animal production.

## Nutriceutical Benefits

Beyond table consumption, paddy straw mushroom shows immunobiological activity. Studies report beneficial effects as functional food, supporting its use as a nutriceutical product for additional market value.

## Challenges

- Most Philippine production remains backyard-scale, "hit and miss"
- Lack of controlled-environment facilities for stable production
- Small-scale growers lack business management training
- No strong cooperative tradition among growers (unlike Japan)
- Intensified rice production (two cropping seasons) has reduced straw availability
- Pleurotus mushrooms are increasingly outpacing V. volvacea in new plantings
