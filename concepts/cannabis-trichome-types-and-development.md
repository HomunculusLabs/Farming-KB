# Cannabis Trichome Types and Development

Trichomes are hair-like epidermal outgrowths found on the surface of cannabis plants. Several types of trichomes are present on cannabis, and the glandular (secretory) varieties are responsible for producing the cannabinoid-rich resin that gives cannabis its therapeutic and psychoactive properties. Understanding trichome types and their development is essential for both breeders selecting for potency and growers determining optimal harvest timing.

## Trichome Classification

Cannabis trichomes are divided into two broad categories: non-glandular and glandular. Non-glandular trichomes serve primarily structural and protective functions — they give the plant its characteristic fuzzy appearance and may help deter insect herbivory through physical barrier effects. These non-glandular hairs do not produce cannabinoids or significant amounts of terpenes.

Glandular trichomes are the resin-producing structures and are the primary source of all cannabinoids, terpenes, and other bioactive compounds found in cannabis. These glandular trichomes are further classified into three distinct types based on their morphology and secretory capacity.

## Bulbous Trichomes

Bulbous trichomes are the smallest of the glandular types, typically only 15-30 micrometers in length. They are visible only under magnification and appear as tiny bumps or bulges on the plant surface. These trichomes consist of a short stalk and a small glandular head that produces minimal amounts of resin.

Bulbous trichomes are the first glandular type to appear during plant development and are found on all above-ground parts of the plant, including stems, leaves, and floral structures. Despite their ubiquity, they contribute relatively little to the overall cannabinoid and terpene content of the plant due to their small size and limited secretory capacity.

## Capitate-Sessile Trichomes

Capitate-sessile trichomes are intermediate in size and feature a slightly larger glandular head sitting directly on the epidermal surface without an elevated stalk. The head is typically 25-100 micrometers in diameter and contains secretory cells that produce moderate amounts of cannabinoids and terpenes.

These trichomes begin to develop early in the floral stage and increase in number as the plant matures. They are more numerous than capitate-stalked trichomes during the early and mid-floral stages. While they contribute to the overall resin profile, their cannabinoid output is significantly less than that of the larger capitate-stalked type.

## Capitate-Stalked Trichomes

Capitate-stalked trichomes are the largest and most important glandular structures on cannabis. They consist of a multicellular stalk elevated above the plant surface, topped by a large, globular glandular head containing secretory cells. The head can range from 50-500 micrometers in diameter, making them visible to the naked eye as the crystalline "frost" or "sugar" coating on cannabis flowers.

These trichomes are apparently responsible for the intense secretion of cannabinoid-laden resins and are the primary determinants of a plant's psychoactive potency. Research has demonstrated a positive correlation between the number of capitate-stalked trichomes on leaves and calyxes and the cannabinoid content of the floral clusters. Plants with a high density of capitate-stalked trichomes consistently show higher THC levels.

The number and type of trichomes is easily characterized by observation with a small hand lens (10x to 50x magnification). Breeders of drug cannabis should select for high densities of capitate-stalked trichomes as a primary selection criterion, since many capitate-stalked trichomes reliably indicates higher cannabinoid production.

## Trichome Development Through Floral Stages

### Premature Stage

During the premature floral stage, the surface of the calyxes is lightly covered with fuzzy, hair-like non-glandular trichomes, but only a few bulbous and capitate-sessile glandular trichomes have begun to develop. Resin secretion is minimal, indicated by small resin heads and few if any capitate-stalked glandular trichomes. There is essentially no drug yield from plants at this stage since THC production is extremely low.

Terpene production starts as the glandular trichomes begin to secrete resin, but premature floral clusters have no terpene aromas or tastes. Total cannabinoid production is low but basic cannabinoid phenotypes based on relative amounts of THC and CBD can already be determined, as the plant has established its chemotype by this point.

### Early Floral Stage

During the early floral stage, calyx production increases and internode length decreases. Older calyx pairs begin to swell and the pistils darken as they lose fertility. Some resin secretion is observed in trichomes along the veins of the calyx, but newly produced calyxes show few if any capitate-stalked trichomes. As a result of low resin production, only a slight terpene aroma and psychoactivity are detectable.

### Peak Floral Stage

At peak floral stage, elongation growth of the main floral stem ceases. Young pistillate calyxes are rapidly producing capitate-stalked glandular trichomes to protect the unfertilized ovule. Since capitate-stalked trichomes now predominate, resin and THC production increase substantially. The elevated resin heads appear clear since fresh resin is still being actively secreted.

THC acid production is at its peak during this stage, and CBD acid levels remain stable as molecules are rapidly converted to THC acids. Terpene production is also nearing its peak and the floral clusters are beautifully aromatic. Many cultivators prefer to harvest some strains during this stage for a clear, cerebral psychoactive effect with low sedative properties.

### Late Floral Stage

In the late floral stage, production of additional capitate-stalked glandular trichomes is rare, although some existing trichomes may still be elongating and secreting resins. As previously secreted resins mature, they change color. The polymerization of small terpene molecules produces longer chains and a more viscous, darker-colored resin.

The transparent amber color of mature resin is usually indicative of high THC content. Many cultivators agree that transparent amber resins are a sign of high-quality drug cannabis. Particularly potent cannabis from various regions of the world is often encrusted with transparent amber colored rather than clear resin heads.

Some late-maturing strains develop opaque, white resin heads as a result of terpene polymerization and THC decomposition. Opaque resin heads are usually a sign that the floral clusters are over-mature and cannabinoid content is beginning to decline.

## Resin Color as Maturity Indicator

Resin color is a useful indicator of trichome maturity and cannabinoid content. Clear resin heads indicate the glandular trichome is still actively secreting fresh cannabinoids. Transparent amber resin suggests peak maturity with high THC content. Translucent or opaque resin heads indicate that biosynthesis has slowed or ceased and degradation is occurring.

Transparent resins, regardless of color, are a sign that the plant is actively carrying out resin biosynthesis. When biosynthesis ceases, resins turn opaque as cannabinoid and aromatic levels decline. This color progression provides growers with a visual tool for determining optimal harvest timing based on their desired cannabinoid profile.

## Trichome Density as Breeding Criterion

For breeders, trichome density and type distribution are among the most important selection criteria when developing drug cannabis varieties. The number of glandular trichomes on leaves and calyxes correlates positively with cannabinoid content, making visual trichome assessment a rapid field screening method for identifying high-potency individuals.

Strains may also be bred for resin tenacity — the degree to which resin heads adhere to the plant surface. Strains bred for hashish production benefit from resin heads that release easily with moderate agitation, while strains intended for marijuana use benefit from extremely tenacious resin heads that remain attached during handling and packaging.
