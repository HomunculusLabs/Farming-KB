# King Oyster Cultivation

## Overview

The King Oyster (Pleurotus eryngii), also known as Eryngii, is the largest member of the [[oyster-mushroom-species-guide|oyster mushroom genus]]. It produces thick, meaty stems and small caps that make it one of the most commercially valuable specialty mushrooms. Its texture and flavor are often compared to abalone or scallops, making it popular in high-end cuisine.

## Species Description

King Oyster produces large, thick-stemmed fruiting bodies with stems 4-10 cm in diameter and caps 5-15 cm across. Unlike other [[query-how-to-grow-oyster-mushrooms-at-home]] that develop thin stems and large caps, the King Oyster naturally produces the opposite morphology: a massive, fleshy stem with a relatively small cap. The color ranges from white to light tan, depending on strain and growing conditions.

Natural habitat: saprophytic on the roots and buried stems of Apiaceae family plants (Eryngium, Ferula, Thapsia) in Mediterranean and Central Asian grasslands. This specific ecological niche distinguishes it from other Pleurotus species.

## Substrate Preparation

### Supplemented Sawdust Blocks

The standard method for King Oyster cultivation uses [[gourmet-mushroom-enriched-sawdust-cultivation]] substrates:

- **Base substrate:** Hardwood sawdust (oak, beech, poplar, or mixed hardwoods)
- **Supplementation:** 20-30% wheat bran or rice bran -- King Oyster benefits from higher supplementation rates than many other species
- **Moisture content:** 62-68%
- **pH:** 6.0-7.0 (slightly higher than most oyster species)
- **Common formula:** 3 parts hardwood sawdust : 1 part wheat bran

### Alternative Substrates

- **Straw:** Wheat or rice straw (lower yields but economical)
- **[[oyster-mushroom-coffee-grounds-cultivation]]:** Mixed with sawdust or straw; high nitrogen content supports good growth
- **Corn cob:** Viable substrate in areas where corn processing waste is available
- **Paper/cardboard:** Experimental; can supplement other substrates

### Sterilization

Autoclave at 15 psi for 60-90 minutes. King [[mushworld-oyster-substrate-materials-worldwide-survey]] is often packed into larger bags (5-10 lbs) for commercial production.

King Oyster mycelium is white, dense, and vigorous. It colonizes quickly and produces a thick mycelial mat on the substrate surface.

## Fruiting Conditions

### The Key: CO2 Control and Cool Temperatures

King Oyster morphology is highly responsive to environmental conditions, particularly CO2 levels and temperature:

- **High CO2 produces long, thick stems with small caps** (commercially desirable form)
- **Low CO2 produces shorter stems with large, open caps** (like other oyster species)
- **Cool temperatures produce denser, whiter, higher-quality fruiting bodies**

### Fruiting Parameters

| Parameter | Range | Optimum |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| Temperature | 50-68°F (10-20°C) | 55-62°F (13-17°C) |
| Humidity | 85-95% RH | 90-95% RH |
| Fresh Air Exchange | Low to Moderate | Controlled |
| Light | Moderate | 500-1000 lux |

### Commercial Morphology Management

To produce the premium long-stemmed, small-capped form:

1. After colonization, cut a small opening (2-3 inches) in the bag above the substrate surface
2. Maintain elevated CO2 (1000-3000 ppm) by limiting ventilation
3. Keep temperature cool (55-62°F / 13-17°C)
4. The mushroom will grow upward through the opening, developing a thick stem and small cap

### Multiple Growing Styles

- **Upright (columnar):** Restricting the opening produces tall, thick stems -- premium market form
- **Shelf formation:** Horizontal cuts in the bag allow lateral fruiting with larger caps
- **Whole-surface fruiting:** Removing the bag entirely produces many smaller, cap-dominated mushrooms

## Harvest and Post-Harvest

### When to Harvest

Harvest when the cap has reached desired size but before the margin begins to curl upward significantly. For premium stem development, harvest when stems are thick and the cap is still relatively small and convex. Typically 7-14 days after primordia appear.

### Harvesting Technique

Cut cleanly at the base with a sharp knife. Handle carefully to avoid bruising, which shows as brown discoloration on the white stem surface.

### Yield

- **[[stamets-biological-efficiency-yield]]:** 100-180% on supplemented sawdust (very high)
- **Flushes:** 2-3 flushes per block; first flush is typically 60-70% of total
- **Typical yield per 5 lb block:** 2.5-4.5 lbs fresh weight

### Storage

Refrigerate at 34-38°F (1-3°C). King Oyster has an excellent shelf life of 2-3 weeks when properly stored, owing to its dense, low-moisture flesh.

## Culinary Qualities

King Oyster is valued for its:
- **Meaty, firm texture** that holds up to grilling, roasting, and sauteing
- **Mild, savory, umami-rich flavor**
- **Versatility:** Can be sliced into "scallops," grilled whole, or used in stir-fries
- **Minimal water loss** during cooking compared to other mushrooms

## Common Problems

- **Short stems / large caps:** CO2 too low; increase restriction around growing area
- **Browning:** Temperature too high, physical damage, or bacterial contamination
- **Cracked stems:** Rapid humidity fluctuations or temperature stress
- **Pest issues:** [[query-how-to-control-fungus-gnats-organically]] and phorid flies are attracted to the high-nutrient substrate
