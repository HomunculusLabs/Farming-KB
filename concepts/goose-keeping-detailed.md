# Goose Keeping Detailed Guide

Geese eat grass and weeds and in return fertilize the soil while leaving crops and mulch alone. They protect property from predators and provide eggs, meat, and feathers. They should only be allowed into well-established areas so they will not squash young shoots.

## Integration

- Seven geese per acre when over eight weeks old, grazing until spring sprouts emerge
- Fence at least 3 feet (0.9 m) high
- Can live in Zone 2, especially if you also keep ducks
- Remove from garden before it ripens (they will eat fruit and vegetables)
- Can be let into Zone 1 [[knf-green-grass-liquid-fertilizer]]/weeds, duck food, grit
- At 5-6 weeks: can survive on large pasture (1 acre per 20-40 geese), add grain optional
- Butcher before winter when pinfeathers are not growing in (they grow in cycles)
- Goose grease traditionally used for frying, pastry, and hand salves (greasier than other meats)

## Breeds

- White breeds: easier to feather when butchering (hence traditional white goose imagery)
- Dark breeds: harder to feather when butchering
- Separate egg breeds and meat breeds exist
