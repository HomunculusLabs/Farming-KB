# Cannabis and Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD)

## Overview

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is characterized by narrowing of
the airways, which decreases gas exchange in and out of the lungs. In orthodox
medicine, cigarette smoking is identified as the primary culprit. Constant
exposure to toxic gases produces initial low-grade lung inflammation that
slowly progresses to chronic bronchitis with chronic coughing. Carbon deposits
destroy alveoli (lung structures that exchange gases with blood), leading to
emphysema. At advanced stages, even slight exertion causes shortness of breath.

## Cannabis Health Index Assessment

- Total Number of Studies Reviewed: 2
- CHI Value: 3

## Key Research Findings

- A 2005 German case study enrolled 18 COPD patients with secondary weight
  loss and limited exertion potential. Patients received twice-daily oral
  cannabis oil containing 3.3-4.2 mg THC. After 16 days, results showed an
  average weight gain of 1.5 kg and a 36% average increase in walking
  distance -- a significant achievement compared to orthodox protocols (CHI 3)
- A 2009 Canadian population-based study from St. Paul's Hospital and the
  University of British Columbia examined the effects of tobacco, tobacco with
  cannabis, and cannabis alone on COPD risk. Key finding: "Smoking both
  tobacco and marijuana synergistically increased the risk of respiratory
  symptoms and COPD. Smoking only marijuana was not associated with an
  increased risk of respiratory symptoms or COPD" (CHI 0)

## Mechanisms of Action

The endocannabinoid system plays a protective role in chronic obstructive
pulmonary disease, acute lung injury, acute respiratory distress syndrome,
and allergic asthma. Its therapeutic action works through prevention or
modulation of inflammation and oxidative stress. THC binds to both CB1 and CB2
receptor sites relatively equally, providing both anti-inflammatory and
antioxidant effects.

## Strain and Form-Specific Considerations

The human case study from Germany used THC dissolved in oil. Sativa and
sativa-prominent strains contain higher THC:CBD ratios. Organic sativa oil
extracts can be made easily or purchased at dispensaries. Crucially, the
research indicates that oral administration (rather than smoking) avoids the
respiratory risks associated with combustion while delivering therapeutic
cannabinoids.

## Non-Cannabis Adjunctive Therapies

Research suggests turmeric may play a protective role in COPD and acute lung
injury through its anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties. Scientists
have recommended clinical trials using turmeric for chronic and acute lung
disorders. This aligns with the broader anti-inflammatory mechanisms shared by
both cannabis and curcumin.

## Mind-Body Medicine and COPD

The barrel chest common in emphysema patients may be interpreted
metaphorically: an impressive, hyper-inflated, dominant body part mimics
someone inflating their chest to appear bigger, stronger, and more in control.
Vulnerability is often feared -- especially sadness, grief, hurt, love, or fear
-- and these feelings may be held in rather than expressed. The un-exhaled
breath becomes a metaphor for unexpressed emotion.

## Important Considerations

The finding that smoking cannabis alone does not increase COPD risk should not
be interpreted as an endorsement of smoking as a delivery method for patients
with existing lung disease. Non-combustion methods such as oral oils,
tinctures, or vaporization may be preferable for COPD patients to avoid any
additional respiratory irritation.
