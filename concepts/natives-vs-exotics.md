## Natives vs Exotics

One of the most contentious debates in gardening and ecological
restoration is whether to use native or exotic plants. The native-plant
movement has gathered tremendous momentum, with compelling arguments on
both sides. Ecological gardening takes a nuanced middle path.

## The Case for Native Plants

Native plants offer several genuine benefits. They have coevolved with
local insects, birds, and other wildlife, often providing superior
habitat. For example, research by entomologist Doug Tallamy has shown
that native oaks support over 500 species of Lepidoptera (butterflies
and moths), while exotic ginkgos support virtually none. Native plants
are adapted to local climate and soils, often requiring less water and
care once established. They also tend to be more resistant to local
[[biodiversity-fungal-genetic-diversity]]. They
connect our gardens to the broader landscape, allowing wildlife to move
through corridors of familiar habitat. They provide a sense of place
that generic, globally-sourced landscapes cannot match. For many
ecological purposes, natives are excellent choices.
