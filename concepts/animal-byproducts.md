# Animal Byproducts in Homesteading Systems

A core permaculture principle is that every output of one system becomes an input for another. Animal byproducts — manure, bones, eggshells, blood, feathers — are high-value fertility inputs for the homestead when properly processed and applied.

## Duck Manure

- **NPK:** ~0.6-1.4% N, 0.4-0.6% P, 0.4-0.6% K (fresh, with bedding)
- **C:N ratio:** 8-12:1 (nitrogen-rich; needs carbon amendment)
- **Hot composting:** Mix with straw, leaves, or wood chips at 25-30:1 overall ratio; reaches 130-150F in 1-3 days; maintain for 15+ days with turning every 2-3 days to ensure pathogen kill; finished in 2-3 months
- **Cold composting:** Layer into existing [[composting-systems]]s at ~1 in per 4 in of carbon material; takes 6-12 months; lower temperatures mean some pathogens may survive — do not use on root crops consumed raw
- **Direct application rates:** Side-dress established plants at 0.5-1 lb/sq ft (incorporate into soil); avoid direct contact with foliage or young seedlings
- **Manure tea:** Fill a burlap sack with 5 lbs manure, steep in 5 gallons water for 24-48 hours; dilute 1:10 for foliar spray or soil drench; do not steep longer than 72 hours (goes anaerobic)
- **Safety:** Always compost duck manure before applying to food crops; 90-120 days before harvest for crops touching soil, 120+ days for crops eaten raw

## Goat Manure

- **NPK:** ~0.7-1.0% N, 0.3-0.5% P, 0.6-0.9% K
- **C:N ratio:** 12-20:1 (relatively balanced; easiest manure to compost)
- **Composting:** Pile at 3x3x3 ft minimum; moisten to wrung-sponge consistency; turn every 3-5 days; reaches thermophilic temperatures readily; finished in 2-4 months
- **Direct application:** One of the few manures safe to apply fresh (in moderation) due to lower nitrogen content; spread 0.5 in deep around established perennials; work into annual beds 2-4 weeks before planting
- **Bedding integration:** Goats on straw bedding produce manure-straw mixtures that compost readily — this is the ideal starting material for a hot compost pile
- **Volume:** Apply 1-2 in of finished compost as a top dressing per growing season; 2-3 lbs/sq ft of composted goat manure for heavy feeders

## Eggshells

- **Composition:** ~94% calcium carbonate (CaCO3), plus small amounts of magnesium, phosphorus, and trace minerals
- **Preparation:** Rinse, dry, crush, then bake at 250F for 20 minutes to sterilize; powder in a blender or mortar and pestle
- **Uses:**
  - **[[water-soluble-calcium-wca]] input:** Powdered eggshells + [[brown-rice-vinegar-brv]] produce water-soluble calcium for KNF applications; 10g eggshell powder per 100ml BRV
  - **[[soil-fertility-management]]:** Direct incorporation into soil or compost at 0.5-1 lb/100 sq ft for calcium; slow-release (takes months to break down in soil); works best when powdered
  - **Chicken/duck feed supplement:** Bake, crush, and offer free-choice to laying poultry for calcium recycling; coarse particles preferred (birds retain coarse grit in gizzard for calcium mobilization)
  - **Seedling starter:** Sprinkle powder in planting holes for transplants (tomatoes, peppers) to prevent blossom end rot
  - **Slug deterrent:** Sharp crushed shells around vulnerable plants create a physical barrier
- **Yield:** One dozen chicken eggshells yields ~12-15g of dry powder

## Bones

- **Composition:** ~30% organic matrix (collagen), ~65% hydroxyapatite (calcium phosphate), ~5% water; rich in phosphorus (12-16% P2O5) and calcium (20-25%)
- **Uses:**
  - **[[water-soluble-calcium-phosphate-wcp]] input:** Charred bones (not ashed) + BRV produce water-soluble calcium phosphate; essential KNF input for the changeover period in the [[nutritive-cycle-theory]]
  - **Bone char production:** Char bones at 400-600F in a covered container (not open flame — that produces ash); charred bones retain phosphorus structure while becoming porous; crush to 0.5-1 in pieces for WCP extraction
  - **Bone meal:** Dry bones thoroughly, then grind in a heavy-duty blender or hammer mill; apply at 1-2 lbs per 100 sq ft for slow-release phosphorus and calcium; NPK approximately 3-15-0
  - **Stock pot broth:** Large bones (goat, poultry) simmered 12-24 hours for mineral-rich broth; remaining bones can then be charred or composted
  - **Vermicompost enhancer:** Crushed bones added to [[vermicomposting]] bins provide slow-release calcium and phosphorus for worms
- **Safety:** Always char or compost bones; raw bones attract pests; bone meal from cooked/slaughtered animals is safe; avoid bones from diseased animals

## Blood Meal

- **NPK:** ~12-15% N, 1-2% P, 0.5-1% K (fastest-release organic nitrogen source)
- **Source:** Slaughter byproduct (duck processing, goat kidding/culling); collect fresh blood, dry into cakes, then grind into powder
- **Application:** 1-2 lbs per 100 sq ft; incorporate into soil (not surface-applied — attracts animals); use sparingly (overapplication burns plants)
- **Best timing:** Side-dress heavy feeders (corn, brassicas) at planting and mid-season; excellent compost activator (sprinkle into pile when turning)
- **KNF alternative:** [[fish-amino-acid-faa]] provides similar nitrogen with additional micronutrients and amino acids

## Feather Meal

- **NPK:** ~12-14% N, 0.5-1% P, 0.5-1% K (very slow-release nitrogen over 3-6 months)
- **Source:** Duck/goat feathers from molting or processing; feathers are ~90% keratin protein
- **Processing:** Dry feathers, then grind into meal; or ferment with brown sugar (1:1 by weight) for 6 months to create a partially broken-down nitrogen source
- **Application:** 2-4 lbs per 100 sq ft mixed into soil before planting; ideal for long-season crops; too slow for quick green-up needs
- **Compost activator:** Excellent nitrogen source to balance high-carbon compost piles

## Manure Tea

- **Aerated (AACT-style):** 5 gallons water + 2 cups composted manure + 1 tbsp unsulfured molasses; aerate with aquarium pump for 24-36 hours; dilute 1:5 for soil drench, 1:10 for foliar spray; see [[compost-tea-aact]] for full methodology
- **Non-aerated (steeped):** 5 gallons water + 5 lbs manure in burlap sack; steep 24-48 hours; dilute 1:10; simpler but less effective (fewer beneficial microbes, potential for anaerobic pathogens)
- **Application timing:** Apply in early morning or evening (UV kills microbes); avoid spraying during flowering (can promote mold on flowers); soil drench is generally more effective than foliar for manure teas

## Deep Litter Systems

- **Principle:** Layer fresh carbon material (straw, leaves, wood shavings) over manure daily; the pack heats up and composts in place; provides warmth in winter (composting temperatures reach 90-110F)
- **For ducks:** 4-6 in starting depth; add 1-2 in of fresh bedding weekly; clean out fully 2-4x/year; finished material is excellent compost
- **For goats:** 6-12 in starting depth in shelter; add carbon as needed to keep surface dry; clean out annually in spring
- **Management:** Keep top layer dry (add more carbon); monitor ammonia smell (add more carbon); ensure drainage; occasional turning with a pitchfork speeds decomposition
- **Output:** Rich, partially decomposed material that finishes quickly in a compost bin; high in nitrogen and microbial activity; ideal for [[vermicomposting]] pre-composting or direct garden incorporation after curing

## Integration with KNF Preparations

- **WCA input:** Eggshells + BRV → water-soluble calcium; see [[water-soluble-calcium-wca]]
- **WCP input:** Charred bones + BRV → water-soluble calcium phosphate; see [[water-soluble-calcium-phosphate-wcp]]
- **FAA alternative:** Blood (instead of fish) + brown sugar at 1:1 ratio; ferment 2-3 months; provides nitrogen plus amino acids; use at same dilution rates as fish FAA
- **[[oriental-herbal-nutrient-ohn]] synergy:** Animal byproduct inputs (manure teas, blood meal) complement oriental herbal nutrient applications — OHN builds plant immunity while byproduct inputs provide raw nutrients
- **[[indigenous-microorganisms-imo]]:** Areas with active animal integration (manure, decaying organic matter) are ideal IMO-1 collection sites — diverse microbial communities thrive in these zones
- **BRV production:** Brown rice for vinegar production can be grown in animal-manure-amended beds for higher nutrient density in the grain
