    10|# Seasonal Garden Tasks Calendar
    11|updated: 2026-04-18
    12|Gardening is far from an exact science, and the gardener who makes rules and sticks to them rigidly is bound to suffer disappointment. Of more concern are the existence of different regions with varying climatic conditions and soil types. These all affect plant growth, the kinds of plants that can be grown, and the timing of gardening duties. The calendar is divided into four seasons: spring (March to end of May), summer (June to end of August), autumn (September to end of November), and winter (December to end of February).
    13|
    14|## Spring
    15|
    16|### Throughout Spring
    17|- Heavy soils: rake soil for sowing
    18|- Light soils: dig in overwintered green-mancover-cropsrops in time to allow soil to settle
    19|- Start hoeing regularly
    20|- Mulch between plants to control weeds and help water retention
    21|- Water regularly in dry weather, particularly newly planted items
    22|- Check worm [[composting-organic-garden]]; put outside if overwintered inside
    23|- Start spraying with insecticides or fungicides as necessary but never on open flowers
    24|- Construct a [[gaias-garden-net-and-pan-water-harvesting]]
    25|- Carry out grafting
    26|### Early Spring — Ornamental Garden
    27|- Rake lawn with spring-tine rake
    28|- Start mowing lawn when grass is growing well
    29|- Sow new seed lawns and resow patches
    30|- Lay turf lawns
    31|- Feed lawns and borders with blood, fish, and bone meal
    32|- Lift and divide perennials
    33|- Prune hybrid bush roses, cutting away frost damage
    34|- Finish planting bare root deciduous trees, shrubs, and hedges
    35|- Start outside sowing of hardy annuals and many shrubs and climbers
    36|- Start planting summer-flowering bulbs and perennials if ground is workable
    37|- Start planting sweet peas
    38|- Take softwood cuttings of heathers and layer shrubs
    39|### Early Spring — Vegetable Garden
    40|- Feed spring cabbages with dried blood or kelp meal
    41|- Start sowing peas and beets outside under cloches, and green beans in greenhouse
    42|- Sow leeks, Brussels sprouts, and cabbages in a stale seedbed
    43|- Start sowing chard outside
    44|- Plant sea kale, shallots, garlic, onion sets, potatoes, and Jerusalem artichokes
    45|- Plant out peas and early spinach sown in greenhouse in late winter
    46|### Early Spring — Fruit Garden
    47|- Feed red currants and white currants with rock potash; black currants with blood, fish, and bone meal
    48|- Check netting on fruit bushes
    49|- If figs were protected for winter, remove straw and sacking, retrain branches
    50|- Graft apples, pears, and plums
    51|- Hand-pollinate blossoms covered with fabric
    52|### Mid-Spring — Ornamental Garden
    53|- Plant bare root evergreen trees, shrubs, and hedges
    54|- Prune shrubs that flower on one-year-old wood immediately after flowering
    55|- Prune shrubs that flower on wood made in the same season
    56|- Prune heathers as they finish flowering
    57|- Plant floating, submerged oxygenating and marsh plants in and around pond
    58|- Finish planting sweet peas
    59|- Continue planting perennials; dig up and divide crowded clumps
    60|- Continue sowing herbs; thin those sown in early spring
    61|- Take cuttings of half-hardy perennials overwintered inside
    62|- Plant tubs and hanging baskets for spring and summer color
    63|- Start spraying roses if black spot has been a problem
    64|### Mid-Spring — Vegetable Garden
    65|- Plant asparagus crowns
    66|- Sow sweet corn outside under polyethylene and in greenhouse in pots
    67|- Sow main crop Florence fennel, peas, okra, turnips, kohlrabi, salsify, scorzonera, carrots, beets, cauliflower, broccoli, kale, and Swiss chard outside
    68|- Plant out globe artichokes, green beans, and onions sown inside; finish planting potatoes
    69|- Set up canes for runner beans
    70|- Plant eggplant and peppers under cloches
    71|### Mid-Spring — Fruit Garden
    72|- During day, remove some cloches protecting strawberries for pollinator access
    73|- Continue hand-pollinating blossoms
    74|### Late Spring — Ornamental Garden
    75|- Finish planting bare root evergreen plants
    76|- Support tall perennials
    77|- Trim ground cover after flowering
    78|- When all frost danger has passed, plant out half-hardy annuals and perennials
    79|- If half-hardy annuals in seed trays look yellow, feed with liquid kelp fertilizer
    80|- Dig and heel in spring-flowering bulbs if space is needed
    81|- Start clipping fast-growing hedges
    82|- Prune early flowering climbers; start tying in climbers
    83|- Plant deep-water aquatic plants
    84|- Sow hardy perennials and biennials in a seedbed
    85|- Start taking softwood cuttings
    86|- Look out for caterpillars on roses and other ornamentals
    87|- Control aphids
    88|### Late Spring — Vegetable Garden
    89|- Finish planting squash and fruiting vegetables under cloches
    90|- Mound soil around potatoes
    91|- Plant out leeks
    92|- Transplant celery, sweet corn, green and runner beans, fruiting vegetables, and celeriac
    93|- Pinch off tops of broad beans if affected by blackfly
    94|- Place carpet pad around newly planted cabbages to protect against cabbage root fly
    95|- Erect carrot fly barriers around carrots
    96|### Late Spring — Fruit Garden
    97|- Water fruit bushes and trees
    98|- Mulch under strawberry plants with straw; cover with netting against birds
    99|- Start tying in and pinching off wall-trained fruit
   100|- Pull up unwanted raspberry shoots growing into paths
   101|- Thin gooseberries for large dessert fruits; use thinnings for cooking
   102|- Put out codling moth traps
   103|- Leave blossoms uncovered in daytime for insect pollination
   104|## Summer
   105|
   106|### Throughout Summer
   107|- Water garden in dry weather but do not water ripe fruit
   108|- Continue hoeing; many weeds will be seeding — very important to cut down perennial weeds
   109|- Continue mulching to control weeds and retain moisture
   110|- Top off worm compost regularly (worms work fast now); water if necessary
   111|- Watch continually for signs of pests and diseases; treat immediately
   112|- Best season for softwood cuttings and budding
   113|- Start layering
   114|### Early Summer — Ornamental Garden
   115|- Remove rose suckers
   116|- Cut back early flowering perennials
   117|- Continue to dig up and heel-in spring-flowering bulbs
   118|- In cold areas, start planting half-hardy perennials and annuals
   119|- Plant deep-water aquatic plants
   120|- Transplant to nursery rows seedlings of perennials and biennials sown in early spring
   121|- Continue sowing biennials; continue propagating climbers by layering
   122|### Early Summer — Vegetable Garden
   123|- In colder areas, plant out tender vegetables: sweet corn, outdoor tomatoes, green beans, zucchini
   124|- Transplant autumn and winter cole crops; protect from cabbage root fly
   125|- Finish transplanting leeks
   126|- Support asparagus ferns with stakes in exposed areas
   127|- Harvest and store shallots
   128|- Tie cucumbers to stakes if growing on tripod frames
   129|- If growing sweet corn under plastic, cut slits for leaves
   130|- Plant out sweet corn raised in greenhouse
   131|- Sow chicory outside
   132|- Earth up main crop potatoes; harvest early potatoes
   133|### Early Summer — Fruit Garden
   134|- Protect cherries against birds; pick when ripe
   135|- Start summer pruning trained soft fruit to encourage fruiting branches
   136|- Continue tying in and pinching off wall-trained trees
   137|- Feed black currants if necessary
   138|- Pin down strawberry runners if wanted for forcing, or cut them off
   139|### Mid-Summer — Ornamental Garden
   140|- Trim fast-growing hedges regularly
   141|- Cut back herbaceous plants that have finished flowering (shred woody stems before composting; leave a few for seed)
   142|- Transplant biennials and perennials raised from seed into nursery rows
   143|- Plant autumn-flowering bulbs
   144|- Harvest herbs for drying before full bloom
   145|### Mid-Summer — Vegetable Garden
   146|- Put straw under bush tomatoes to keep fruit clean
   147|- Start late sowings of turnips and carrots for maturing outside
