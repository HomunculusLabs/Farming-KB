## Sprinkler Nozzle Selection for Vegetable Gardens

## Overview

The choice of sprinkler nozzle diameter has a significant impact on garden
health beyond simple water delivery. Nozzle size determines application rate,
droplet size, soil compaction risk, and whether a soil crust forms. Most
consumer sprinklers sold for lawns use large nozzles that are poorly suited
to vegetable gardens, where soil tilth and gentle water application are
critical to success. Solomon argues that nozzle selection is one of the most
overlooked aspects of garden irrigation design.

## Nozzle Diameter and Application Rate

Sprinkler performance is primarily determined by nozzle diameter and
operating pressure. Solomon provides a comparison of four common nozzle
sizes operating at typical residential water pressures:

| Nozzle Diameter | Operating Pressure (PSI) | Application Rate (in/hr) |
|-----------------|--------------------------|-------------------------|
| 1/30 inch       | 30                       | 0.45                    |
| 1/30 inch       | 60                       | 0.79                    |
| 3/64 inch       | 30                       | 1.94                    |
| 1/16 inch       | 60                       | 2.66                    |

The 1/16-inch nozzle at 60 PSI applies water at nearly 6 times the rate
of the 1/30-inch nozzle at the same pressure (2.66 vs 0.45 inches per
hour). Even at 30 PSI, the 3/64-inch nozzle applies over 4 times the
volume of the smallest nozzle. This means that in the same amount of
time, a high-rate sprinkler delivers vastly more water — and with it,
vastly more kinetic energy to the soil surface.

## Why Low Application Rate Matters
