## Permaculture Plant Index C-E

This index covers plants from the letter C through E as catalogued in *Permaculture Plants: A Selection*. Entries are organized by common or scientific name with their primary uses and page references to the source text.

## C

### Cacti and Succulents
- **Cooba** (*Acacia salicina*) — pages 21, 93, 134. Drought-hardy Australian wattle used for timber, fodder, and soil stabilization in arid landscapes.
- **Common purslane** (*[[portulaca-oleracea]]*) — page 133. Edible ground cover with high omega-3 content; useful as a salad green and living mulch.
- **Coral tree** (*Erythrina* spp.) — page 112. Nitrogen-fixing legume tree, excellent for fodder, living fences, and [[green-manure]].
- **Cress** — page 114. Fast-growing salad green suitable for aquatic and terrestrial cultivation.

### Coppice and Timber Species
- **Coppice** systems are referenced extensively throughout the text (pages 18-130). The following species are specifically noted for coppice suitability: cooba (21), crested wattle (22), various eucalypts (17-23), sheoaks (24), and poplars (86). Coppicing provides sustainable timber, fuelwood, and craft materials on short rotation cycles.
- **Cork oak** (*Quercus suber*) — pages 82, 113. Primary source of commercial cork; slow-growing but productive over centuries. Drought tolerant once established.
- **Common hornbeam** (*[[maintaining-organic-matter-in-vegetable-gardens]].
- **Curry plants** — page 90. Various species used in traditional cooking across tropical and subtropical regions.
- **Cowpea** (*Vigna unguiculata*) — page 116. Nitrogen-fixing legume for food, fodder, and green manure in warm climates.

### Companion Planting
- **Companions** — page 118. Companion planting is a core permaculture strategy for pest management, nutrient cycling, and spatial efficiency. The text references companion planting throughout, particularly in vegetable and herb garden designs.

### Cosmetics and Oils
- **Cosmetics** — pages 45, 87, 103, 105. Several species provide oils, waxes, and extracts used in cosmetics: jojoba (45), neem (87), and various palms (103, 105).
- **Coquilla nut** (*Attalea funifera*) — page 99. Brazilian palm producing hard nuts used for buttons, carving, and small craft items.

### Crops and Food Production
- **Corn** (*Zea mays*) — page 65. Staple grain crop; in permaculture systems often intercropped with beans and squash (three sisters method).
- **Custard apple** (*Annona reticulata*) — page 70. Tropical fruit tree producing sweet, creamy-textured fruits.
- **Cumbungi** (*Typha* spp.) — pages 124, 134. Aquatic reed with multiple uses: food (rhizomes, pollen), thatch, and water filtration.

### Construction and Craft
- **Construction** — pages 23, 24, 66, 85, 89, 92, 94-97, 102, 105. Timber species for building include eucalypts (92-96), bamboo (97), poplars (86), oaks (80-82), and various palms (98-108).
- **Crates** — pages 26, 28, 37, 106. Lightweight timber from fast-growing species used for shipping crates and containers.
- **Craft** — pages 17, 19, 20, 22-24, 84, 97. Basketry, carving, weaving, and other crafts use willow (84), bamboo (97), and various wattles and palms.

## D

### Dairy and Livestock Fodder
- **Cows and cattle** — pages 22, 26, 32. Fodder species include tagasaste/tree lucerne (26), honey locust (28), and various grasses and legumes.
- **Ducks** — pages 67, 105, 121. Duckweed (*Lemna* spp., page 121) serves as high-protein duck fodder; also feeds fish in aquaculture systems.

### Dates and Palms
- **Date palm** (*Phoenix dactylifera*) — pages 106, 136. Desert-adapted palm producing edible dates; also provides building materials, fiber, and shade.
- **Date plum** (*Diospyros lotus*) — page 60. Caucasian persimmon species with sweet fruit when fully ripe.
- **Double coconut** (*[[carob-ceratonia-siliqua-mediterranean-leguminous-tree]]*) — page 79
- Pistachios (*Pistacia vera*) — page 40
- Pines (*Pinus* spp.) — pages 42-45
- Various saltbushes (*Atriplex* spp.) — page 128

### Ducks and Aquaculture
- **Duckweed** (*Lemna* spp.) — page 121. Tiny floating aquatic plant that doubles in biomass every 2-3 days. Exceptional protein content (25-45% dry weight) makes it ideal for poultry and fish feed. Also sequesters nutrients from wastewater.
- **Duck potatoes** (*Sagittaria latifolia*) — page 124. Aquatic plant producing edible tubers; native to North American wetlands.

### Dye Plants
- **Dye** — pages 74, 75, 99, 101, 136. Natural dye sources include indigo (74, 75), various palms (99, 101), and numerous bark and leaf sources (136).
- **Dyeing** — pages 27, 72, 87. The text references traditional dyeing techniques using plant extracts throughout.

## E

### Eucalypts (*Eucalyptus* spp.)

Eucalypts are one of the most extensively covered genera in the text, referenced across pages 17-23, 92-96, 116, 130, 135. They are the dominant hardwood timber trees in Australia and widely planted worldwide.

#### Timber Eucalypts
- **Eucalyptus regnans** (Mountain ash) — page 18. The tallest flowering plant in the world; premium construction timber.
- **Eucalyptus marginata** (Jarrah) — pages 23, 93, 95, 96, 116. Extremely durable Western Australian hardwood for flooring, furniture, and railway sleepers.
- **Eucalyptus diversicolor** (Karri) — pages 95, 116. Tall West Australian eucalypt with straight grain; excellent structural timber.
- **Eucalyptus obliqua** (Messmate) — pages 20, 95, 117. Versatile hardwood for general construction.
- **Eucalyptus delegatensis** (Alpine ash) — page 18. Cold-tolerant mountain eucalypt for high-elevation timber production.
- **Eucalyptus microcorys** (Tallowwood) — page 94. Naturally durable timber resistant to decay and termites.

#### Ornamental and Small Eucalypts
- **Eucalyptus ficifolia** (Red flowering gum) — page 116. Spectacular red flower displays; nectar source for birds and bees.
- **Eucalyptus erythrocorys** (Illyarrie) — page 135. Ornamental mallee with bright yellow and red flowers.
- **Eucalyptus torquata** (Coral gum) — page 135. Ornamental species with pink to coral-colored flowers.
- **Eucalyptus woodwardii** (Lemon-flowered gum) — page 135. Showy ornamental with distinctive lemon-yellow blossoms.

#### Drought-Tolerant Eucalypts
- **Eucalyptus camaldulensis** (River red gum) — pages 21, 93, 94, 116, 135. One of the most widely distributed eucalypts; excellent for riparian zones and saline soils.
- **Eucalyptus microtheca** — pages 19, 20, 21. Extremely drought hardy; used in arid zone forestry and windbreaks.
- **Eucalyptus eremophila** (Tallerack) — pages 93, 116. Mallee eucalypt of arid Western Australia; silvery foliage and ornamental flowers.
- **Eucalyptus salmonophloia** (Salmon gum) — pages 94, 95, 96, 117. Hard, durable timber tree of Western Australia's wheatbelt.
- **Eucalyptus salubris** (Gimlet) — pages 94, 95, 96, 117. Exceptional woodworking timber; drought adapted.
- **Eucalyptus wandoo** (Wandoo) — pages 94, 96, 117. Hard, dense timber; excellent for flooring and furniture.

#### Eucalypts for Essential Oils
- **Eucalyptus citriodora** (Lemon-scented gum) — page 135. High citronella content; insect repellent and fragrance uses.
- **Eucalyptus dives** (Broad-leaved peppermint) — page 18. Commercial source of eucalyptus oil.
- **Eucalyptus radiata** (Narrow-leaved peppermint) — pages 18, 20. High-quality medicinal eucalyptus oil.
- **Eucalyptus smithii** (Gully gum) — page 20. Eucalyptus oil production.
- **Eucalyptus globulus** (Tasmanian blue gum) — page 20, 116. Major species for both timber and essential oil production worldwide.

#### Eucalypts for Firewood and Fuel
- **Eucalyptus cladocalyx** (Sugar gum) — pages 94, 116. Dense wood; excellent firewood with high heat output.
- **Eucalyptus leucoxylon** (Yellow gum) — pages 94, 135. Good firewood and ornamental flowering tree.
- **Eucalyptus sideroxylon** (Red ironbark) — pages 94, 96. Extremely dense, durable timber; premium firewood.
- **Eucalyptus maculata** (Spotted gum) — pages 94, 112, 116. Strong structural timber; good firewood.

### Edible Plants
- **Edible bean** — page 28. Various legume species produce edible beans in [[comparison-vetiver-grass-vs-bamboo-for-erosion-control]] include wattles (21-23), poplars and willows (83, 84), vetiver grass (131), various palms (104, 106, 107), and salt-tolerant species (133).

### Essential Oils
- **Essential oils** — pages 91, 129, 136. Eucalypts (92), melaleucas/tea trees (129), lavender (136), and other aromatic plants provide essential oils for medicine, fragrance, and insect control.

### Estuarine and Wetland Plants
- **Estuaries** — page 23. Mangroves and other estuarine plants stabilize coastlines, filter water, and provide nursery habitat for fish and crustaceans.
- **Eelgrass** (*Zostera* spp.) — page 134. Submerged seagrass of coastal waters; important for coastal stabilization and habitat.
