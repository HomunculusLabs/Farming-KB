## Partnership Societies

Partnership societies, as described by Eisler and adopted by McKenna, are defined by several key characteristics.

**Egalitarianism**: social structures that distribute power and responsibility broadly rather than concentrating it in a ruling elite. These societies did not lack hierarchy entirely, but their hierarchies were based on age, wisdom, experience, and spiritual accomplishment rather than coercion, inheritance, and military power.

**Maternalistic orientation**: cultural values centered around nurturing, fertility, and the maternal principle — a reverence for the life-giving power of the feminine expressed in both social organization and religious practice.

**Nature reverence**: a worldview that sees human beings as embedded within, rather than separate from or superior to, the natural world. The earth is not a resource to be exploited but a living being to be respected.

**Absence of warfare**: conflict resolution through social mechanisms, ritual, and negotiation rather than organized violence and conquest.

The partnership model is fundamentally about **linking** rather than **ranking** — about connection, mutuality, and reciprocity rather than domination and submission.

## Dominator Cultures

In stark contrast, dominator cultures are characterized by four features that mirror and invert the partnership model.

**Hierarchical structures**: rigid social stratification with power concentrated at the top and enforced through violence or the threat of violence.

**Paternalistic values**: male-dominated value systems that privilege control, authority, and the systematic subordination of the feminine in every domain of social life.

**Materialistic worldviews**: philosophical frameworks that reduce reality to what can be measured, owned, and exploited — seeing nature as dead matter awaiting human dominion.

**Male dominance**: systematic subordination of women and feminine values across every domain of social life, from the family to the state.

The dominator model is organized around **ranking** — who is above whom, who commands and who obeys.
