## In-Vitro Sclerotia Production Timeline

## Overview

Sclerotia (colloquially "magic truffles" or "stones") are dense, hardened masses of mycelial tissue that serve as survival structures for certain *Psilocybe* species. Unlike fruitbody cultivation, sclerotia can form directly within colonized substrate inside sealed jars, without the need for casing, fruiting chambers, or environmental manipulation beyond basic incubation.

## Species Capable of Sclerotia Production

The primary sclerotia-producing species in cultivation are:

- ***Psilocybe tampanensis*** — The most prolific sclerotia producer in cultivation. Originally collected from Tampa, Florida by Steven Pollock in 1977, this species reliably forms substantial sclerotial masses on [[pf-tek-dual-chambered-terrarium-design-humidity-management]], fresh air exchange, or light cycles are needed.

## Sclerotia in Cased Trays

Sclerotia of *P. mexicana* and *P. tampanensis* also form within the casing layer of cased trays that are intended for mushroom fruiting. This means that even when the cultivator's goal is mushroom production, sclerotia can be harvested as a secondary yield from the casing layer when the substrate is spent after fruiting.

## Yield Expectations

Typical yields from grass seed substrate:

| Species | Substrate Weight | Sclerotia Yield (wet) | Yield Ratio |
|---------|-----------------|----------------------|-------------|
| *P. tampanensis* | 110g seed | 30-90g | 27-82% |
| *P. mexicana* | 110g seed | 15-40g | 14-36% |

Yields vary significantly based on genetics, substrate quality, and incubation conditions. *P. tampanensis* consistently outperforms *P. mexicana* in sclerotial biomass production.
