# White Clover Ground Cover

## Fukuoka's Clover Revolution

Masanobu Fukuoka called his no-till rice and barley system the "clover revolution" because it enabled farming without any pesticides or [[cervantes-chemical-fertilizers-guide]]. White clover (ladino clover) serves as a permanent living ground cover in his direct-seeding, no-tillage rice/[[direct-seeded-no-tillage-rice-barley-succession-in-clover]] system.

## How It Works

### Annual Cycle

1. **October:** Sow clover seeds (1 lb per quarter-acre) over the standing heads of rice
2. **Late October:** Sow barley seed about two weeks before rice harvest
3. **Spring:** A thick layer of clover grows at the foot of the maturing barley. Beneath the clover, rice seedlings begin to emerge.
4. **Late May:** Cut the barley. The clover is cut together with the barley but does not interfere with harvesting. Barley straw is scattered uncut over the entire field. Rice seedlings emerge through the straw and clover grows back.
5. **Early June:** When clover appears about to choke out young rice seedlings, hold water in the field for 4-7 days to weaken the clover. Then surface-drain to grow hardy plants.
6. **Rice season:** Irrigate intermittently. Soil moisture of 80% is adequate. A soil moisture level of eighty percent is adequate.
7. **October:** Cycle begins again

### Benefits of the Clover Cover

- **[[comparison-biological-nitrogen-fixation-vs-synthetic-fertilizer]]:** Clover fixes atmospheric nitrogen, feeding companion crops
- **Weed suppression:** Dense cover prevents winter weeds from establishing
- **Soil protection:** Ground cover shields soil from rain and erosion
- **Moisture retention:** Shade holds moisture in the soil
- **Organic matter:** Clover and straw mulch build soil humus
- **No-till:** The system eliminates ploughing entirely

### Seeding Method

Broadcasting directly over the maturing heads of rice requires less work and is beneficial for germination, seedling growth, and weed control. When seeded this way, clover and barley readily germinate because of the high soil moisture, and winter weeds have not yet appeared.

## Hemenway's Perspective

Toby Hemenway describes Fukuoka's method as "a great example of [[gaias-garden-stacking-functions-detailed]]":
- Greenery suppresses weeds
- Shade holds moisture in the soil
- Blossoms attract [[comparison-beneficial-insects-guide-vs-integrated-pest-management]]
- Nitrogen fixed by the clover boosts the growth of other crops

To plant crops, Fukuoka simply opens up small areas in the clover and places seeds or transplants in the resulting gaps.
