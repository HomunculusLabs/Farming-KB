## Fungal Treatment of Olive Oil Mill Wastewater

Olive oil mill wastewaters (OMW), also known as "alpechin" or "olive
milling effluent," are dark-coloured, highly phytotoxic effluents
generated during the extraction of olive oil. These waste streams are
produced in enormous volumes in Mediterranean countries where olive oil
production is concentrated, and their disposal represents a significant
environmental challenge. White rot fungi have been investigated as
biological agents for the detoxification and decolorization of these
effluents, which contain high concentrations of recalcitrant polyphenolic
compounds.

## Composition and Properties of Olive Oil Mill Wastewater

Olive oil mill wastewater is characterized by several problematic
properties that complicate conventional treatment:

- **Intense dark colour**: The characteristic dark brown to black colour
  results from polyphenolic compounds and their oxidation products,
  including tannins, anthocyanins, and flavonoids derived from olive
  [[cannabis-seed-germination-methods]] and plant growth at
  relatively low concentrations. This phytotoxicity limits the
  direct use of OMW for irrigation or land application.
- **Antimicrobial activity**: Some polyphenolic compounds in OMW
  possess [[biochemistry-of-ligninolytic-fungi-bioremediation]] in OMW treatment.

## White Rot Fungi for OMW Treatment

Several white rot fungal species have been evaluated for their ability
to decolorize and detoxify olive oil mill wastewater. The approach
relies on the non-specific oxidative enzyme systems of these fungi to
degrade the polyphenolic chromophores and reduce the phytotoxicity of
the effluent.
