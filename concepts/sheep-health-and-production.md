# Sheep Health and Production

Health management, vaccination schedules, shearing, wool processing, growth data, and homestead integration for sheep. See [[sheep-husbandry]] for breed selection, housing, feeding, and breeding.

## Health

### Common Health Issues

| Problem | Symptoms | Prevention/Treatment |
|---------|----------|---------------------|
| Internal parasites | Anemia, diarrhea, bottle jaw | FAMANCHA scoring, rotational grazing, deworming |
| Footrot | Lameness, foul-smelling feet | Foot baths (zinc sulfate), dry footing |
| Flystrike | maggots in wool/flesh | Crutching, shearing, fly traps |
| Caseous lymphadenitis | Abscesses on lymph nodes | Isolate affected animals, hygiene |
| Enterotoxemia (overeating) | Sudden death | CDT vaccination |
| Pneumonia | Coughing, nasal discharge | Good ventilation, dry housing |
| Pregnancy toxemia | Weakness, staggering in late pregnancy | Adequate nutrition in late gestation |
| Scrapie | Neurological degeneration | Genetic testing, select resistant breeding stock |

### Health Schedule

| Timing | Procedure |
|--------|-----------|
| 1-2 weeks | CDT vaccination #1, dock tails, castrate |
| 4-6 weeks | CDT booster, selenium injection (if deficient area) |
| Spring | Shear wool sheep, trim hooves, deworm (FAMANCHA) |
| Fall | Breeding, body condition scoring, deworm as needed |
| Pre-lambing | CDT booster (ewe), increase nutrition |
| Annually | Hoof trimming (2-4 times per year) |

### FAMANCHA Scoring (Parasite Check)

Score the inner eyelid color:
- 1: Red -- healthy, no deworming needed
- 2: Pink-red -- marginal, monitor
- 3: Pink -- deworm
- 4: White-pink -- deworm immediately
- 5: White -- severe anemia, emergency treatment

Only deworm sheep that score 3 or above (targeted selective treatment) to slow parasite resistance.

## Shearing and Wool

### Shearing

- Once per year (spring, before lambing)
- Professional shearer or learn to do it yourself (takes practice)
- Keep sheep dry for 24 hours before shearing
- Skirt the fleece: remove belly, leg, and contaminated wool
- Package in paper bags (not plastic -- wool needs to breathe)

### Wool Processing

1. Skirt and sort fleece
2. Wash in hot water with detergent (do not agitate -- causes felting)
3. Rinse thoroughly
4. Dry on racks in shade
5. Pick or card for spinning
6. Spin into yarn
7. Can be dyed before or after spinning

### Wool Products

- Handspun yarn for knitting/crocheting
- Felted wool for insoles, pads, insulation
- Raw wool for mulch, compost nitrogen source
- Wool insulation for [[natural-building]]
- See homestead-crafts for wool processing crafts

## Growth and Production

| Age | Weight | Notes |
|-----|--------|-------|
| Birth | 5-12 lbs | Varies by breed |
| 30 days | 25-40 lbs | Creep feeding |
| 60 days (weaning) | 50-70 lbs | Transition to pasture + supplement |
| 90 days | 70-100 lbs | Market weight (hair sheep) |
| 6 months | 100-130 lbs | Market weight (wool breeds) |
| Maturity | 120-250 lbs | Depends on breed |

- Dressing percentage: 45-55% of live weight
- Lamb is ready for market at 100-130 lbs live weight

## Integration with Homestead Systems

- Sheep graze areas unsuitable for goats (close-cropped grass)
- Manure fertilizes pasture; compost for garden beds
- Wool provides insulation material for [[natural-building]]
- Multi-species grazing with goats and cattle reduces parasite load
- Sheep can be trained to electric fencing quickly
- Effective at clearing brush and invasive plants
- See [[homesteading-livestock-comparison]] and [[pasture-management]]
