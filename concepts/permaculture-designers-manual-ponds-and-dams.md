# Permaculture Designers Manual - Ponds and Dams

updated: 2026-04-18

Ponds, dams, and water systems are covered across Chapters 7 and 13, forming a critical nexus between water conservation, aquaculture, energy production, and landscape stability. Mollison presents water as the most productive medium for biological systems, with 4-20 times the yield possible from the same inputs compared to adjoining land.

## The Case for Aquaculture (Section 13.2)

Water cultures have long-tested stability, many persisting without external inputs for thousands of years. Aquaculture yields are superior to terrestrial cultures given the same energy or nutrient inputs because:

- Water supply is constant for plant and animal growth
- Plant nutrients are available in soluble, easily assimilable form
- Water and nutrient flow boost production (not represented in fields)
- Aquatic organisms need little energy for movement, largely free of gravitational effects
- Light, nutrient, and plants occupy a three-dimensional medium with complex edges and surfaces
- Polyculture has been recognised from the beginning in water cultures
- Energies lost in cultivation are eliminated or reduced
- Impounded water provides multiple products: energy, recreation, irrigation, transport

## Pond Design Principles

### Edges, Interfaces, and Gradients

The edge effect in water is more pronounced than on land. Stocking rates for fingerlings can use surface area or margin length interchangeably, so a narrow sinuous canal holds as many fish as a broadwater several times its surface area. Maximum edge is assured by swale, canal, or chinampa systems, which can be sinuous even in flat landscapes.

Edges occur in great variety at the land/water interface: forest, shrubbery, reed-bed, mudflat, gravel, marsh, ice and snow all produce unique habitat. The water surface supports striding, floating, and sucking organisms; the mud surface is critical to nutrient retention where phosphates and nitrogenous products are stored.

### Water Quality

General oxygen level of 5 ppm is very satisfactory in ponds. At 1 ppm, many fish species die except for air-breathing tropical species (snakeheads, walking catfish, giant gourami, climbing perch). Derelict swampy ponds stocked with polyculture of air-breathers can yield 2,400 kg protein/ha/year.

Ponds are classified as:
- Aerobic: oxygen well-supplied by wind overturn, turbulent flow, aerators
- Facultative: surface may be aerated but pond base becomes anaerobic in cold periods
- Anaerobic: low (1 ppm or less) oxygen status; primary sewage ponds

### Acidity-Alkalinity

Pond pH ideally 6.0-8.5. Lethal limits for most fish: pH 3.7 (acid) and 10.5 (alkaline). A pH mosaic in pond series is ideal, as soft (acid) water suits some food organisms while hard (alkaline) water suits fish, molluscs, and freshwater lobsters. Crushed dolomite, marble chips, hard limestone gravels, and oyster shells give slow calcium release.

### Stocking Rates

A baseline figure: 200 kg fish/ha/year is a modest yield, achievable as:
- 200 x 1 kg fish; 400 x 500 g; 800 x 250 g; or 1000 x 125 g
- 300 g is minimum pan-fish size; below this, fish can be dried or made into paste

A good strategy: keep a few predatory fish in a cage to control breeding, and rear fry separately in small covered ponds before release as fingerlings.

### Fertilisers

Pig, duck, and second-stage sewage are all used in fish ponds. Heavily fertilised ponds suit algae-feeding milkfish or prawns; higher vegetation ponds suit grass carp or tilapia; almost clear-water ponds suit bass, perch, and salmonids. In polyculture, a 5:1 herbivore:predator ratio is usual.

Modestly fertilised ponds alter yield by factors of 2-10 times. Waterfowl, perches for birds, pigeon lofts, pig pens, chicken roosts over water, and bat roosts all bring complex plant nutrients to ponds. Flow-on pond water doubles land crop yields (melons, tomatoes, fuel forests).

### Temperature and Thermal Strategies

Temperature stratification creates thermoclines utilised by different species. Deep "kettles" in shallow ponds prevent fish dying in freezes or excessive heat, and assist capture in draw-down. Solar ponds below fish ponds can provide accessory heat. Shade helps maintain high oxygen levels.

### Aeration

Many commercial aerators can be solar-powered. Where water has a head of 2 m or more, fountains, showers, and flowforms oxygenate ponds. A considerable energy saving is achieved if only part of the pond is aerated at critical periods; fish will gather there in times of need.

## Dam Design (Chapter 7)

Dams serve multiple functions beyond water storage:
- Fire control barrier
- Heat storage and microclimate moderation
- Run-off absorption and flood control
- Pollutant cleansing
- Transport system
- Recreation asset
- Energy storage (elevated dams as "batteries")
- Irrigation accessory
- Aquaculture production
- Wildlife habitat

Key principles from Chapter 7:
- Dams should be sited to catch maximum run-off from stable catchments
- Earthworks should minimise erosion and maximise water retention
- Spillways must handle extreme events safely
- Evaporation can be reduced by deep design, surface covers, or shade trees on the poleward side
- Series of small dams are generally more productive and stable than one large dam
