# Biodynamic Agriculture and Steiner

Biodynamic agriculture, developed by [[rudolf-steiner]] in the 1920s and
advanced by his follower Dr. Ehrenfried Pfeiffer, represents a holistic
approach to farming that recognizes living forces in soil and plants
beyond what conventional chemistry can detect. Through unique compost
preparations, sensitivity crystallization testing, and chromatographic
analysis, biodynamic methods have demonstrated remarkable results in
soil restoration, [[fukuoka-hotel-analogy-crop-yield-model]] improvement, and plant quality enhancement
that conventional [[knf-vs-chemical-farming-comparison]] cannot explain.

## Overview

In the 1920s, Rudolf Steiner gave a series of esoteric lectures at the
Silesian estate of Count Keyserling for agronomists concerned about
declining crop productivity. He asked Dr. Ehrenfried Pfeiffer to find
a reagent that would reveal what he called "etheric formative forces"
in living matter. After months of testing, Pfeiffer discovered that a
copper chloride solution with plant extracts, allowed to evaporate slowly
over fourteen to seventeen hours, produced crystallization patterns
determined by the nature and quality of the plant.

Strong, vigorous plants produce beautiful, harmonious, clearly formed
crystal arrangements radiating to the outer edge. Weak or sick plants
produce uneven pictures with thickening or incrustation. When a forester
sent Pfeiffer two pine seeds, crystallization tests revealed one would
be a fine specimen while the other had a serious defect. Photographs
confirmed one tree was mast-straight while the other was too crooked
for lumber.

## Key Concepts

- Steiner's "etheric formative forces" are non-material energies that
  shape and organize [[savory-living-organisms-as-tools]], beyond what [[challenges-neurotropic-fungi-chemical-analysis-taxonomic-identification]]
  can detect
- Pfeiffer's copper chloride crystallization method can reveal the
  quality and vitality of plants, soils, and foods
- Chromatographic analysis with silver nitrate shows that natural
  vitamins have stronger vitality patterns than synthetic equivalents
- The whole of a natural organism contains factors undetectable by
  analyzing its component parts
- Biodynamic compost uses homeopathic preparations buried in cow horns,
  deer bladders, and other natural containers to concentrate specific
  forces
- Weeds serve [[fungal-melanin-biosynthesis-ecological-functions]]; dandelions transport calcium from
  deep soil layers, while sorrels and docks indicate excess acidity

## Pfeiffer's Testing Methods

Pfeiffer developed a simpler chromatographic method using circular filter-
paper discs with silver nitrate solution. From the brilliant-colored
concentric patterns he disclosed new secrets of life. Testing natural
vitamin C from rose hips, he established the pattern of vitality was far
stronger than from artificial ascorbic acid. Rudolf Hauschka suggested
vitamins are not [[neurotropic-fungi-chemical-compounds]] but "primary cosmic formative forces."

Pfeiffer's chromatograms revealed the quality of soils and the stages of
fermentation. A cranberry bog with 18 percent [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]] was shown to
be biologically inert, which standard analysis could not reveal. Adobe
soil from California, though mineral-rich, had no microflora and was
infertile. Plants in mineral-only soil are "like people forced to eat
salty foods," absorbing excess moisture, looking lush but becoming
disease-prone.

The chromatograms also established scientific proof of plant symbiosis.
Beans and cucumbers grow better together, but beans and fennel fare
badly. Apples and potatoes stored together rob each other of vital
properties. Pfeiffer showed that camomile stimulates heavier wheat
growth, but only at a ratio of one to one hundred, confirming age-old
Russian peasant wisdom about cornflowers and rye.

## Biodynamic Compost

Pfeiffer developed a Biodynamic compost starter composed of some fifty
different microorganisms from outstanding soils worldwide, each with a
particular mission. What makes it inscrutable to average scientists is
the presence of homeopathic quantities of vital elements, enzymes, and
growth substances working at dilutions up to one billion to one.
