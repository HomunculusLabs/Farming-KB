# Zoroastrian Mazda Religion, Haoma/Soma, and Christian Influence

## Zoroastrian Religion Overview

Zoroaster (also known as Zarathustra) did **not** create a new religion from nothing. He
reformed the existing Vedic religion practiced by the Indo-European peoples who had migrated
into the Iranian plateau. The Indo-European tribes who invaded Iran were closely related —
both linguistically and culturally — to those who invaded India, and they shared a common
religious heritage stretching back thousands of years.

Zoroastrian theology — it is a divine, cosmic entity. According to *Encyclopædia
Britannica* (1991, vol. 26, pg. 789), Haoma is described as a divine being who occupies a
central role in the cosmic order, a mediator between the divine and the human, and the
bestower of immortality and healing.

The very names Haoma and Soma derive from the Indo-European roots *su* or *bu*, meaning
"to squeeze" or "to press" — a direct reference to the pressing ceremony that lies at the
core of both Vedic and Zoroastrian worship. The ritual act of pressing the sacred plant
and consuming its juice is the origin point from which both religious traditions flow.

## Key Zoroastrian Concepts That Precede Christianity

Zoroaster's teachings contain a remarkable number of concepts that would later become
central to Christianity — and they appear in Zoroastrianism **centuries before** they
appear in any other religion:

- **Resurrection of the dead** — Zoroaster taught that the dead would be physically
  resurrected at the end of time, a concept with no precedent in earlier Near Eastern
  religions.
- **A last judgment** — All souls would be weighed in a final cosmic judgment, separating
  the righteous from the wicked.
- **The coming of various saviors** — Zoroastrianism prophesied the coming of future
  saviors (*saoshyants*) who would aid in the renovation of the world.
- **An apocalyptic end of times** — The world would undergo a final transformation, a
  cosmic battle between good and evil culminating in the renewal of creation.
- **Angels and demons** — Zoroastrianism developed an elaborate hierarchy of spiritual
  beings serving Ahura Mazda (the Wise Lord) or Angra Mainyu (the destructive spirit).
- **Heaven and Hell** — Zoroaster taught that souls would proceed to paradise or
  punishment based on their earthly conduct.

All of these concepts are found in Zoroaster's teachings long before they emerge in
Judaism or Christianity. The chronological priority is well-established by scholars.
