## Fungal BTEX and Chlorinated Monoaromatic Biodegradation

Fungi degrade monoaromatic compounds including BTEX (benzene, toluene,
ethylbenzene, and m-, o-, p-xylenes) and chlorinated monoaromatics through both
intracellular and [[ligninolytic-enzymes]] for nonspecific
oxidation of aromatic compounds. These systems generate highly reactive radicals
that attack aromatic rings without requiring prior modification. The broad
substrate specificity of ligninolytic systems is a major advantage for treating
mixed-contaminant sites. The system includes lignin peroxidase, manganese-
dependent peroxidase, laccase, and auxiliary enzymes that generate hydrogen
peroxide, operating synergistically to oxidize diverse aromatic substrates.

### Cytochrome P450 Systems
Non-ligninolytic fungi use intracellular cytochrome P450 monooxygenases for
initial ring oxidation, following pathways similar to mammalian liver
metabolism. These systems introduce hydroxyl groups via arene oxide
intermediates, which rearrange to form phenolic compounds. The cytochrome P450
pathway is particularly important for fungi that do not produce ligninolytic
enzymes.

### Monooxygenase-Mediated Side Chain Degradation
Toluene, ethylbenzene, and xylenes can be degraded at their alkyl side chains
by monooxygenase enzymes. During toluene metabolism by Cladophialophora sp.
strain T1, hydroxylation to benzyl alcohol and transformation to benzoic acid
occurred prior to ring hydroxylation and cleavage. This pathway is important
for alkylated benzenes where the side chain is more accessible to enzymatic
attack than the ring itself.

## Key Degrading Organisms

### Cladophialophora sp. Strain T1
This soil fungus does not metabolize benzene, but alkylated benzenes are
degraded by assimilation and cometabolism. Para-xylene degradation does not
occur in complex BTEX mixtures, but sixty percent is depleted with toluene.
NMR metabolic profiles revealed phthalate formation as end products of ortho-
and meta-xylenes, illustrating the complexity of fungal aromatic metabolism.

### White-Rot Fungi
[[cancer-medicinal-mushrooms-pleurotus-ostreatus]], and [[staycare-cunninghamella-elegans-in-bioremediation]] is extensively studied for aromatic ring hydroxylation.
Exophiala jeanselmei degrades toluene, Cladosporium sphaerospermum degrades
toluene and ethylbenzene, and Trichosporon veenhuisii assimilates aromatic
compounds. Various Bjerkandera, Trametes, and Pleurotus species degrade
chlorinated phenols and related compounds.
