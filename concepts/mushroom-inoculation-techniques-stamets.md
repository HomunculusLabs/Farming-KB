## Agar-to-Agar Transfers

### Purpose

Agar-to-agar transfers are used for isolating pure [[sterilization]], the
other is being used for transfers. This reduces waiting time and improves
efficiency.

### Selecting Transfer Material

Always cut from the leading edge of actively growing mycelium. This is the
youngest, most vigorous growth and least likely to harbor hidden
contaminants. Avoid the outer margin of petri dishes where contaminants
most commonly enter, and avoid areas showing any discoloration or abnormal
growth.

### Isolating Away from Contaminants

When contamination is present on a plate, it may still be possible to
isolate clean mycelium. Cut out segments of emerging mushroom mycelia away
from contaminant colonies. Since many common contaminants are sporulating
molds, avoid jarring the culture or creating air currents that could spread
spores during the transfer.

### Post-Inoculation Shaking

After completing the transfer set, tightly secure lids and shake each jar
thoroughly. Each wedge travels throughout the grain, leaving mycelial
fragments adhering to kernels. If a wedge sticks to the glass, distribution
is hampered. This typically indicates agar that was too thinly poured or
has dehydrated.

## Grain-to-Grain Transfers

### The Rolling Wrist Pour

The grain-to-grain transfer uses a technique Stamets describes as the
"rolling wrist pour":

1. Loosen lids on receiving jars so they can be removed with one hand
2. Gently shake the master jar until spawn separates into individual
   kernels
3. Hold the master in the preferred hand
4. Remove the master's lid, then the first receiving jar's lid
5. With a rolling of the wrist, pour one-tenth of the master into the jar
6. Replace the lid and continue sequentially
