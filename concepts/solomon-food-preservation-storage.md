### Making a Root Cellar

Many homes have basements suitable for cellaring. If not, options include:
- Wall off and insulate a section of an existing basement
- Dig an outdoor cellar (difficult during heavy snow periods)
- Defunct chest freezers can serve as small root-storage compartments
- No permits generally required for simple [[permaculture-plants-root-crops-and-tubers]]
- Do not wash before storing; brush off loose soil only

**Cabbage and leafy crops**:
- Harvest immediately before hard freezes (after some light frosts but before serious freezing)
- Dig up with roots intact, shake off earth, remove damaged outer leaves
- Transplant into cellar beds of moist soil
- Heads blanch during storage, becoming milder and more tender
- Brussels sprouts can be stored on the stalk

**Potatoes**:
- Ideal: 38-40F (3-4C), humid, dark
- Colder than 38F and the starch converts to sugar (undesirable texture)
- Warmer than 40F and they start sprouting
- Solomon's method: wood boxes stacked in tightly built unheated outbuilding, with old woolen blankets to regulate temperature
- Check regularly; rub off sprouts as they appear
- Potatoes stored this way last for months past their prime, still usable after sprouting begins

**Sweet potatoes**:
- Cure first: 85F (29C), high humidity for 1-2 weeks
- Storage: 55-60F (13-15C), humid -- warmer than regular potatoes
- Below 50F causes chilling injury and rotting

**Onions and garlic**:
- Cure by drying in warm, airy location for 2-3 weeks
- Store at cool room temperature in mesh bags
- Need lower humidity than root vegetables
- Inspect regularly; remove any developing mold

**[[cover-crops]]**:
- Cure in warm, dry conditions for 1-2 weeks
- Store at 50-55F (10-13C), dry conditions
- Acorn squash: shorter storage (1-2 months)
- Butternut and Hubbard: 3-6 months under proper conditions

## In-Garden Storage

Many crops can overwinter in the garden itself where the ground does not freeze solid:

- **Kale**: survives well below freezing; harvest continues through winter
- **Brussels sprouts**: stand in the garden; harvest after frosts for sweetest flavor
- **Leeks**: survive with soil mounded around stems
- **Parsnips**: flavor improves after frost; can be dug through winter
- **Rutabagas**: stand in the garden through winter in mild climates
- **Chicories**: survive if soil does not freeze solid
