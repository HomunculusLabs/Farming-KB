# Plants and Human Consciousness

## Marcel Vogel's Research

Marcel Vogel, an IBM research chemist in Los Gatos, California, became fascinated by Backster's plant experiments after initially dismissing them as charlatanism. His systematic investigation revealed that plants respond profoundly to human emotional states and focused consciousness.

Vogel found that not only plants but their individual leaves had unique "personalality and individuality." Leaves with high water content worked best. Plants went through phases of activity and inactivity -- "full of response at certain times of the day or days of the month, sluggish or morose at other times."

## The Communication Protocol

Vogel developed a precise technique for establishing plant-human communication:

1. Attach electrodes to a philodendron using a mucilaginous paste (agar with karri gum and salt) to eliminate random electromagnetic frequencies
2. Stand before the plant completely relaxed, breathing deeply, almost touching it with outspread fingers
3. Shower the plant with affectionate emotion, "the same kind of affectionate emotion he would flow to a friend"
4. The chart records ascending oscillations; Vogel felt "an outpouring from the plant of some sort of energy" on his palms
5. After 3-5 minutes, the plant "discharges all its energy" and further emotion evokes no response

Vogel described this interreaction as being "on the same order as that evoked when lovers or close friends meet."

## Consciousness as Circuitry

Vogel discovered that a "certain focused state of consciousness" was integral to the monitoring circuitry. The plant could be awakened from somnolence to sensitivity only when Vogel gave up his normally conscious state and focused "a seemingly extra-conscious part of his mind" on the plant's wellbeing. In this state, "man and plant seemed to interact, and, as a unit, pick up sensations from events, or third parties, which became recordable through the plant."

When Vogel cut a leaf from one plant, a second wired plant responded -- but only when Vogel was paying attention to it. "It was as if Vogel and the plant were lovers on a park bench, oblivious of passers-by until the attention of one lover became distracted from the other."

## The Effect of Skepticism

Vogel found that hostile observers produced dramatic negative effects on his experiments. "The feeling of hostility, of negativity, in an audience is one of the main barriers to effective communication." Skeptics could cause the plant and equipment to "go dead" until a positive tie could be reestablished.

## Psychic Energy and Leaf Preservation

Vogel's friend Vivian Wiley demonstrated that focused human attention could keep leaves alive far beyond their normal lifespan. She placed two saxifrage leaves in separate rooms, focusing daily loving attention on one while ignoring the other. After a month, the attended leaf was "radiantly vital and green, just as if freshly plucked," while the ignored leaf was flaccid, brown, and decaying. Wiley later kept a leaf green and alive for two full months.

## Entering the Plant

Vogel found that exceptionally sensitive individuals could "get into" a plant -- a phenomenon reported of the 16th-century mystic Jakob Boehme, who described mingling with plants and "rejoicing with a joyously growing leaf." Subject Debbie Sapp, wired to Vogel's equipment, described entering a philodendron:

"I was becoming part of, and filling out, a broad expansive surface. This seemed to me to be describable only as pure consciousness. I felt acceptance and positive protection by the plant. There was no sense of time, just a feeling of unity in existence and in space."

The chart showed "an abrupt stop" when she "came out" of the plant. On later occasions, she accurately described cellular structures she could not have known about, including a burned spot on a leaf confirmed by electrode inspection.

## The Life Force Concept

Vogel concluded that a "Life Force, or Cosmic Energy" surrounding all living things is "sharable among plants, animals, and humans." Through this sharing, "a person and a plant become one." He stated unequivocally: "It is fact: man can and does communicate with plant life. Plants are living objects, sensitive, rooted in space. They may be blind, deaf, and dumb in the human sense, but there is no doubt in my mind that they are extremely sensitive instruments for measuring man's emotions."

## Consciousness-Driven Remote Experiments

Vogel's colleague demonstrated that thought alone, relayed to a plant, could drive a model car by remote control. Another experiment purportedly steered a plane in flight "by transmitting a thought to a plant." Vogel concluded that "focused thought can exert a tremendous effect" on [[savory-living-organisms-as-tools]].

## Teaching Children Plant Communication

Because Vogel knows that "among all humans, children are the most open-minded," he began teaching children how to interact with plants. He asks them to feel a leaf, describe its temperature, consistency, and texture in detail. Next, he lets them bend leaves and become aware of their resilience before petting the leaves gently.

If his pupils take pleasure in describing the sensations, Vogel asks them to take their hands away and try to feel a force or energy emanating from the leaves. Many children instantly described a rippling or tingling sensation. Those who felt the strongest sensations were wholly engrossed in what they were doing.

"This is basic training," says Vogel, "to develop an expanded awareness of a force which is not visible. The awareness established, they see they can operate with this force."

## The Mechanistic Approach Fails

Adults are much less successful than children, which leads Vogel to surmise that many scientists will not be able to repeat his or Backster's experiments. "If they approach the experimentation in a mechanistic way, and don't enter into mutual communication with their plants and treat them as friends, they will fail."

"Hundreds of laboratory workers [[fungi-around-the-world-species-distribution]] are going to be just as frustrated and disappointed until they appreciate that the empathy between plant and human is the key. No amount of checking in laboratories is going to prove a thing until the experiments are done by properly trained observers. Spiritual development is indispensable."

## Reading Thoughts Through Plants

Vogel considered it possible to read a person's thoughts through a plant. He asked a nuclear physicist to mentally "work" on a technical problem. As the man cogitated, Vogel's plant registered tracings for 118 seconds. When the tracing fell back to baseline, Vogel informed his friend that he had stopped his train of thought. The friend corroborated.

## American Indian Knowledge

Vogel noted that [[yage-common-themes-south-american-indians]] "were keenly aware of these faculties. When in need, they would go into the woods. With their arms extended, they would place their backs to a pine tree in order to replenish themselves with its power."
