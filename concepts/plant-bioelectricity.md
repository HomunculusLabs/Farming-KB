# Plant Bioelectricity

Plants generate, conduct, and respond to [[tompkins-cleve-backster-primary-perception]]'s
1966 observation that his dracaena plant produced galvanometer patterns
similar to human emotional responses. The fundamental premise is that all
living matter generates and responds to electrical phenomena. Bose
demonstrated that the electrical response curves of metals, animal muscles,
and plant tissues are strikingly similar, suggesting a fundamental unity
in how matter responds to stimulation.

The Galvanic Skin Response (GSR) measured in humans attached to
electrodes has its botanical counterpart: the Plant Galvanic Response
(PGR). When Backster attached electrodes to his dracaena, the galvanometer
responded to the plant's thirst for water not with decreased resistance
(as expected from greater conductivity) but with a downward trending
sawtooth pattern indicating a complex electrical reaction. Vogel later
refined the measurement technique with agar-based electrode paste that
eliminated spurious signals and produced perfectly straight baselines.

## Key Concepts

- Plants generate measurable electrical potentials that fluctuate in
  response to environmental stimuli, threats, and human intentions
- The Plant Galvanic Response (PGR) parallels the Galvanic Skin
  Response (GSR) measured in humans during emotional arousal
- Plant bioelectric responses occur in all plant parts, not merely
  in motile organs like the [[electromagnetic-spectrum-plant-light-perception]]
- Individual cells including sperm, amoeba, and paramecium exhibit
  bioelectric responses suggesting cellular-level consciousness
- The bioelectric field around plants can be sensed by humans as a
  cooling sensation followed by electrical pulses

## Electrical Responses in Plant Tissues

Bose demonstrated that when plants are mechanically stimulated, scraped,
burned, or exposed to chemicals, they produce electrical responses
analogous to those in animal tissues. These responses include an initial
spike followed by recovery, diminished response with repeated stimulation
(fatigue), and restoration of responsiveness after rest. Chloroform
anesthetizes plants just as it does animals, suppressing electrical
response until fresh air revives the plant.

The response and recovery pattern in plants mirrors that in metals and
muscles, a finding that astonished the scientific establishment. When
Sir Michael Foster of the Royal Society viewed Bose's recordings, he
identified them as muscle response curves, only to be told they were
from metallic tin. The president of the Linnean Society concluded that
"all parts of plants, not merely those which are known to be motile,
are irritable, and manifest their irritability by an electrical response
to stimulation."

## The Bioelectric Field and Human Interaction

Vogel's work established that plants and humans share a bioelectric
field that can be brought into resonance. When Vogel stood before a
wired philodendron, relaxed and breathing deeply, showering it with
affectionate emotion, ascending oscillations appeared on the chart. He
tangibly felt an outpouring of energy from the plant on his palms. The
interreaction appeared analogous to the energy dynamic between lovers
or close friends, with a surge of mutual response until energy was
expended and needed recharging.
