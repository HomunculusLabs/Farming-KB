## Red Wax Scale

Red wax scale, another major pest, follows the same pattern. Originally controlled
with a pine rosin mixture, wartime rosin shortages forced growers to stop. Parasitic
wasps emerged naturally and established themselves as effective predators, making
chemical extermination unnecessary. After the war, a fluorine pesticide was
introduced: "Severe outbreaks of the pest arose at once." When the agent was banned,
"Infestation by the scale declined almost immediately, demonstrating that the most
intelligent way of controlling this particular pest is not to spray."

## Foreign Pests Warning

Fukuoka closes with a sobering warning about foreign pests introduced through
international trade. The Mediterranean fruit fly attacks all major fruit and vegetable
crops; the codling moth ravages rose family fruits. "Extermination of these will be
difficult if not impossible; once they have entered Japan, they may very well cause
incredible damage." Larval worms and maggots bury deep into fruit, making outside
spraying or fumigation ineffective once established.

His warning is philosophical as much as agricultural: "I would simply like to warn
that the free movement of fruit may satisfy the fleeting desires of people, but the
price we will have to pay will be enormous."

## Summary of Principles

- **Select resistant varieties** closer to natural ancestors rather than weakened
  modern cultivars.
- **Rely on natural predators** --- ladybugs, parasitic wasps --- rather than
  broad-spectrum chemicals that destroy them.
- **Improve tree form and microenvironment** through pruning and strategic planting
  to create conditions unfavorable to pests.
- **Accept minor cosmetic damage** rather than pursuing total extermination.
- **Avoid strong pesticides** that kill natural enemies and trigger the very
  outbreaks they are meant to prevent.
- **Recognize that most pest problems originate in human actions**, not in the pests
  themselves; often the most intelligent control is to do nothing at all.
