# KNF Yeast Preparation

**Updated:** 2026-04-18

Yeast is a Korean [[comparison-natural-farming-vs-permaculture]] microorganism input responsible for the final step of fermentation. It breaks sugars into alcohol and CO2, enhances metabolism, and helps plants recover from stress, disease, and damage. In KNF, cultivated yeast is particularly valued for its ability to resynthesize decomposed organic matter into forms directly beneficial to plants.

## What Is Yeast

Yeast is a microorganism (a single-celled fungus) that performs the last step of fermentation -- breaking sugars down into alcohol and carbon dioxide (ethanol fermentation). In KNF, cultivated yeast serves multiple plant health functions and is particularly useful as a recovery treatment for stressed or damaged plants.

## Functions of Yeast

1. **Completes the fermentation process** by converting sugars to alcohol and CO2
2. **Enhances metabolism** and expands capillary vessels in plants, improving [[mycelial-network-nutrient-transport-imaging-gadd]]
3. **Resynthesizes organic substances** decomposed by various microbes into amino acids, hormones, and vitamins beneficial to crops
4. **Produces vitamins, nucleic acids, minerals, hormones, and fatty acids** that support plant growth
5. **Produces 8 essential amino acids** that organisms cannot synthesize on their own
6. **Decomposes [[cervantes-chemical-fertilizers-guide]]** -- extremely useful for transitioning conventional farms to natural methods
7. **Naturally occurs abundantly** on fruit surfaces, especially on grapes -- making it easy to collect

The resynthetic function is particularly important: while bacteria and other microbes break down organic matter, yeast rebuilds these breakdown products into plant-available forms. This makes yeast a critical bridge between decomposition and plant nutrition.

## Cultivation Guidelines

### Important Rules

- Do NOT wash fruits in water -- use them as-is with their native yeast populations intact on the surface
- All tools must be sterilized before use to prevent contamination
- Do NOT seal the container -- cover loosely with lid to allow gas exchange
- Keep in a separate area to prevent unwanted microbes and odors from contaminating
- Maintain optimum temperature: 23-25C and humidity: 65-70%
- Shake 1-2 times daily to protect from fungi during cultivation (prevents surface mold)
- Store cultivated yeast in refrigerator at 1-15C
- Maximum storage: 1 month, but recommended to use within 1 week for maximum viability

### Best Culture Mediums

- **Grapes** -- yeast exists specifically and abundantly on grape surfaces; the standard and preferred choice. Wild grapes are ideal.
- **Strawberries** -- also a good yeast culture medium, especially when grapes are unavailable
- Other fruits with white bloom on their surface (indicating wild yeast presence) can be used

### Cultivation Process

1. Select ripe grapes or strawberries with visible white bloom (yeast) on surface
2. Do NOT wash the fruit -- the yeast is on the skin
3. Crush the fruit lightly and mix with brown sugar (approximately 1:1 ratio by weight)
4. Place in a clean container (clay jar preferred)
5. Cover loosely (not airtight) to allow CO2 to escape
6. Maintain at 23-25C with 65-70% humidity
7. Shake 1-2 times daily to prevent fungal growth on surface
8. Fermentation is complete in 5-7 days (indicated by reduced bubbling and alcohol aroma)
9. Strain and collect the liquid yeast culture

## Usage

### Dilution

- Standard dilution ratio: 1:1,000 with water
- Effective when sprayed on leaves together with other KNF inputs such as [[knf-oriental-herbal-nutrient]]

### When to Use Yeast

1. When the plant becomes weak or lacks vigor -- yeast restores [[arbuscule-isolation-metabolic-activity-assays]]
2. When viral or bacterial disease occurs -- yeast supports the plant's immune response
3. When branches or stems are heavily damaged by strong wind -- accelerates recovery
4. When photosynthate production is low during rainy or high-humidity seasons -- compensates for reduced photosynthesis
5. When livestock lose appetite or vigor -- added to feed or water at appropriate dilution
6. When transitioning from chemical to natural farming -- yeast helps decompose residual chemical fertilizers in the soil

### Crop-Specific Applications

- **Rice:** Apply during [[comparison-cannabis-vegetative-stage-vs-flowering-stage]] to promote tillering and leaf growth
- **Fruit trees:** Apply after pruning or when trees show reduced vigor
- **Vegetables:** Apply to seedlings for accelerated establishment
- **Flowering plants:** Apply before flowering to support bud development (reduce near flowering to avoid excessive vegetative growth)
