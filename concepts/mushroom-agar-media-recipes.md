# Mushroom Agar Media Recipes

## Overview

Agar media are gelatinous, nutrient-rich substrates used for culturing mushroom mycelium in the laboratory. They serve essential functions in mushroom cultivation: isolating pure strains from spores or tissue, expanding mycelium for [[mushroom-cultivation-grain-spawn|grain spawn]] production, and maintaining [[mushroom-genetics-strain-selection|strain collections]]. Different formulations suit different purposes and species preferences.

## Basic Principles

All agar media share these components:

- **Agar:** A gelling agent derived from red algae (Gelidium or Gracilaria species), typically used at 1.5-2.0% concentration (15-20 g per liter). Provides a solid but moist surface for mycelial growth
- **Nutrient source:** Provides carbohydrates, nitrogen, minerals, and vitamins for fungal growth
- **Water:** Distilled or deionized; typically 1 liter per recipe
- **pH adjustment:** Most mushroom mycelium prefers slightly acidic conditions (pH 5.0-6.5)

## Standard Recipes

### Potato Dextrose Agar (PDA)

The most widely used general-purpose mushroom culture medium. Suitable for most cultivated species.

**Ingredients:**
- 200-300 g potatoes (peeled, diced)
- 20 g dextrose (glucose) or white sugar
- 15-20 g agar powder
- 1 liter distilled water

**Preparation:**
1. Boil diced potatoes in 1 liter water for 30 minutes
2. Strain the broth through cheesecloth, discarding the potato solids
3. Return broth to 1 liter by adding distilled water (water lost during boiling)
4. Add dextrose and agar, stirring until dissolved
5. Adjust pH to 5.6-5.8 (optional but recommended)
6. Pour into media bottles or Erlenmeyer flasks (about 200 mL per 500 mL flask)
7. Sterilize at 15 psi for 20-30 minutes

### Malt Extract Agar (MEA)

An excellent all-purpose medium, often preferred over PDA for its consistency and ease of preparation. Particularly good for [[basidiomycete-phylum-guide|basidiomycetes]].

**Ingredients:**
- 20 g malt extract powder (light malt extract, not dark)
- 2 g yeast extract (optional but beneficial)
- 15-20 g agar powder
- 1 liter distilled water

**Preparation:**
1. Dissolve malt extract and yeast extract (if using) in 1 liter water with gentle heating
2. Add agar and stir until fully dissolved
3. Adjust pH to 5.4-5.6
4. Dispense and sterilize at 15 psi for 20-30 minutes

### Dog Food Agar (DFA)

A versatile, nutrient-rich medium popularized by Paul Stamets for its ability to support a wide range of fungal species. Uses commercially available canned dog food as the nutrient base.

**Ingredients:**
- 1 can (approximately 400 g) meat-based canned dog food (avoid fish-based varieties)
- 15-20 g agar powder
- 1 liter distilled water

**Preparation:**
1. Blend dog food with 1 liter water in a blender until smooth
2. Transfer to a pot and bring to a gentle boil, stirring frequently
3. Simmer for 15-20 minutes
4. Strain through cheesecloth or a fine mesh strainer to remove solids
5. Return liquid to 1 liter volume
6. Add agar, stir until dissolved
7. Adjust pH to 5.6-6.0
8. Dispense and sterilize at 15 psi for 30 minutes (the organic content requires slightly longer sterilization)

### Dextrose Yeast Extract Agar (DYEA)

A simple, defined medium suitable for routine culture work and species that do not require complex nutrients.

**Ingredients:**
- 10 g dextrose
- 5 g yeast extract
- 15-20 g agar powder
- 1 liter distilled water

### Malt Yeast Agar (MYA)

Combines the benefits of malt extract and yeast extract:

**Ingredients:**
- 10 g malt extract
- 2 g yeast extract
- 15-20 g agar powder
- 1 liter distilled water

## Specialty and Modified Recipes

### Antibiotic Agar

For isolating fungi from contaminated samples:

- Prepare PDA or MEA as described
- After autoclaving and cooling to 50-55°C (warm but not hot enough to denature antibiotics), add:
  - 50 mg streptomycin per liter, OR
  - 30 mg chloramphenicol per liter, OR
  - 25 mg penicillin + 50 mg streptomycin per liter

### Wood-Based Agar

For species that prefer wood substrates (shiitake, maitake, reishi):

- 200 g hardwood sawdust (oak or beech)
- 20 g malt extract
- 15-20 g agar
- 1 liter distilled water
- Boil sawdust in water for 30 minutes, strain, then proceed as with PDA

### Low-Nutrient Agar

For isolating slow-growing fungi that might be overgrown on richer media, or for encouraging fruiting on agar:

- 5 g malt extract (half the standard amount)
- 15-20 g agar
- 1 liter distilled water

### Nutrient Yeast Extract Agar with Peptone (NYEA)

A richer medium for fastidious species:

- 5 g nutrient broth
- 5 g yeast extract
- 10 g peptone
- 20 g dextrose
- 15-20 g agar
- 1 liter distilled water

## Pouring Plates

1. Allow sterilized media to cool to approximately 50-55°C (comfortable to touch but still liquid)
2. Pour 20-25 mL into each sterile Petri dish inside a laminar flow hood
3. Cover immediately and allow to solidify (15-30 minutes)
4. Invert plates and store at room temperature; use within 2-4 weeks
5. Before use, check for contamination -- any plates showing microbial growth should be discarded

## Storage

- Prepared plates: store inverted at room temperature (65-75°F) in sealed bags; use within 4 weeks
- Prepared but unpoured media: store sterilized bottles at room temperature; remelt to pour as needed
- Dry agar powder: store in airtight containers in a cool, dry place; stable for years
