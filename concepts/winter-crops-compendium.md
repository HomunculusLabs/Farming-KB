## Winter Crops Compendium

[[eliot-coleman]]"s [[winter-harvest-system]] encompasses a remarkable diversity of crops — over 30 species that can be grown and harvested through the cold months using unheated or minimally heated greenhouses. The following compendium catalogs every crop referenced in *The [[eliot-coleman-and-the-winter-harvest-handbook]]*, organized by category with notes on their roles in the [[coleman-beet-greens-cold-house-winter-production]] system.

## Salad Greens

These form the backbone of winter production, harvested as [[cut-and-come-again]] crops:

### Arugula (*Eruca vesicaria*)
- Used in crop rotations
- Flame weeded before planting
- Harvest season extends through winter
- Component of salad mix
- Winter crop — one of the most cold-hardy greens
- Sowing dates span from early September through December in protected environments

### Lettuce (*Lactuca sativa*)
- Crop debris management critical to prevent disease carryover
- Included in crop rotations for soil health
- [[coleman-flame-weeding-preemergence-technique]] used for weed control
- [[coleman-winter-greens-nutritional-quality-cold-sweetening]] peaks in cool conditions
- Grown in quick hoops and greenhouses
- Core component of salad mix
- Winter crop — requires slightly more protection than arugula
- Temperatures around 70°F (21°C) ideal for growth; cool houses at ~50°F maintain quality without bolting

### Spinach (*Spinacia oleracea*)
- Susceptible to aphids — the primary pest concern
- Component of braising mix and salad mix
- Heavy feeder; requires adequate fertilization (nitrogen)
- Included in extensive crop rotations
- Customer demand is strong — one of the most requested winter vegetables
- [[coleman-winter-harvest-planting-schedule]]: outdoor sowings in September, greenhouse sowings through October
- Winter crop — extremely cold-hardy; can survive repeated freezing

### Mâche / Corn Salad (*Valerianella locusta*)
- Harvest season spans winter
- Core component of salad mix
- Sown in fall [[us-climate-zones-for-winter-harvest]]
- Perhaps the quintessential winter green — thrives in short-day, cold conditions

### Claytonia / Miner's Lettuce (*Claytonia perfoliata*)
- Included in crop rotations (noted for relationship with boron fertility)
- Harvest season through winter
- Photographed and featured in the handbook
- Component of salad mix
- Winter crop — one of the most cold-tolerant species in the system
- Sown in fall, spacing approximately 4-6 inches

### Radicchio (*Cichorium inty*)
- Winter crop — develops sweeter flavor after frost
- Component of salad mix
- Susceptible to specific diseases requiring rotation
- Sown in late summer for winter harvest

### Frisée / Endive (*Cichorium endivia*)
- Includes varieties like Bianca Riccia and golden frisée
- Winter crop
- Component of salad mix
- Nutritional quality noted for anthocyanin content

### Escarole (*Cichorium endivia var. latifolia*)
- Winter crop
- Related to endive with similar growing requirements

### Watercress (*Nasturtium officinale*)
- Winter crop — extremely cold-hardy perennial
- Component of salad mix
- Likes moist conditions; can be grown in beds or along water channels

## Asian Greens

A diverse category that expands the winter salad repertoire:

### Mizuna (*Brassica juncea var. japonica*)
- Winter crop — highly cold-tolerant
- Component of salad mix
- Cut-and-come-again harvesting
- Included in crop rotations
- One of the most reliable winter Asian greens

### Tatsoi (*Brassica rapa var. rosularis*)
- Winter crop — forms tight rosettes that protect the growing point
- Component of salad mix and Stir-Fry Paks
- Cut-and-come-again harvesting

### Pak Choi / Bok Choy (*Brassica rapa var. chinensis*)
- Winter crop — moderate cold tolerance
- Component of salad mix and Stir-Fry Paks
- Included in crop rotations
- Can be grown in quick hoops
- Strong customer demand

### Mustard Greens (*Brassica juncea*)
- Winter crop
- Component of salad mix and braising mix
- Adds spicy flavor to winter salads

## Braising Mix Components

These heartier greens are harvested for cooking rather than raw salad:

### Chard (*Beta vulgaris subsp. cicla*)
- Harvest season extends through winter
- Component of both braising mix and salad mix (young leaves)
- Winter crop — can survive heavy frost though outer leaves may be damaged
- Sown in late summer

### Collards (*Brassica oleracea var. viridis*)
- Winter crop — extremely cold-hardy, similar to kale
- Withstands repeated freeze-thaw cycles

### Kale (*Brassica oleracea var. sabellica*)
- Includes dinosaur (Tuscan/Lacinato) and other varieties
- Winter crop — flavor sweetens after frost
- Component of salad mix (baby leaves) and braising mix
- Included in crop rotations
- One of the most productive winter greens

### Dinosaur Kale
- Specific variety noted for tenderness and flavor
- Component of salad mix
- Photographed in the handbook

## Root Crops

### Carrots (*Daucus carota subsp. sativus*)
- Varieties noted: Napoli, Nelson
- In crop rotations — follow heavy feeders like tomatoes
- Flame weeding used before emergence
- Customer demand is strong — "sweet winter" or "candy" carrots command premium prices
- Quick hoops used for protection
- Sown in August for winter harvest
- Spacing approximately 2-3 inches
- Winter crop — develops high sugar content after frost exposure
- Nutritional quality enhanced by cold

### Turnips (*Brassica rapa subsp. rapa*)
- Includes Hakurei variety — noted for salad-quality eating
- Component of Stir-Fry Paks
- Harvest season through winter
- Winter crop
- Sown in late summer
