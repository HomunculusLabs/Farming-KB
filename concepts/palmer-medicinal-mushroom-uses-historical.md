## Amanitine Toxicology and Absorption

Palmer developed a detailed theory of amanitine absorption that was
remarkably advanced for its time. He argued that the alkaloid entered
the system "by absorption through the villi or lining of the intestinal
canal, so that were we to remove every vestige of the mushroom, the
system might possibly be left so charged with poison as to refuse to
rally." This meant that emetics and purgatives, standard treatments
for other types of poisoning, were "worse than useless" against
amanitine, because they could not remove poison already absorbed into
the bloodstream.

Palmer documented personal experiments demonstrating that absorption
could occur through skin contact, inhalation, and even mucous membrane
exposure. He described holding Amanita specimens in his closed hand
and experiencing "fearful nausea," and on another occasion merely
smelling specimens in a closed box and suffering vomiting and
oppression three hours later. These observations suggested that the
poison operated through general physiological absorption rather than
requiring ingestion.
