# JADAM Farm Economics and Market Viability

## Overview

JADAM's ultra-low-cost agriculture (ULC) system is built on the economic principle that sustainable farming must first be economically viable. With [[coleman-deep-organic-farming-philosophy]] can be both high-quality and financially accessible, challenging the assumption that organic means expensive.

## The Economic Problem of Modern Organic Farming

Any organic farming that relies on buying inputs from the corporate sector will face a rising cost. The resulting expensive products can then only serve the very few wealthy. The majority of consumers (and farmers) will eventually turn their backs.

JADAM is skeptical that the current system of agriculture will have any future beyond the next ten or twenty years. Organic farming has become too expensive and, if the trend cannot be reversed, it will have no role in empowering farmers, fostering healthier food production, or rehabilitating the environment.

## Two Paths to Profit

There are two ways to make profit in farming: to increase revenue or to reduce cost. JADAM argues that the latter is safer and more logical for farmers. Too many farmers put themselves in danger of bankruptcy by investing large sums of money and trying to sell at a high price. JADAM organic farming is friendly for the planet, empowering for farmers, and ultra-low-cost.

## The 100 Dollars Per Acre Achievement

After decades of research and experiments, JADAM has not just lowered the cost marginally; it has lowered it radically to an ultra-low level. Production cost now stands at 100 dollars per acre per year. When farmers begin to make their own inputs, farming can finally declare independence from the corporations. Ultra-low-cost farming is possible only when farmers are the masters of their farming.

## Market Viability

JADAM's economic model addresses market viability at multiple levels:

**For farmers**: Low production costs mean lower financial risk, less debt, and greater resilience to market fluctuations. A farmer does not need to achieve premium prices to be profitable.

**For consumers**: Lower production costs can translate to more affordable organic products, expanding the market beyond wealthy consumers. The majority of consumers who currently cannot afford organic food become potential customers.

**For the organic movement**: Cost-competitive organic farming can become mainstream rather than a niche market, leading to broader environmental benefits.
