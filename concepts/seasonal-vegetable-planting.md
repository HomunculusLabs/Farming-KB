# Seasonal Vegetable Planting Guide

Making sure you always have fresh vegetables in the garden
requires planning and attention to seasonal timing. Weather
variations make rigid timetables impractical, but keeping a
garden diary helps establish reliable patterns over two or
three years.

## Spring Tasks

### Early Spring
- Protect emerging potato shoots with garden fabric if
  frost is likely
- Feed spring cabbages with dried blood or kelp meal
- Start successional sowing of hardy vegetables
- Plant asparagus crowns, shallots, garlic, onion sets,
  and Jerusalem artichokes
- Sow peas and beets outside under cloches
- Sow leeks, Brussels sprouts, and cabbages in a stale
  seedbed for later transplanting

### Mid to Late Spring
- Sow [[growing-globe-artichokes]], green beans, and early
  spinach
- Set up canes for runner beans
- Transplant celery, sweet corn, fruiting vegetables,
  and celeriac once frost risk has passed
- Pinch off broad bean tops if affected by blackfly
- Erect carrot fly barriers around carrot rows
- Mulch under strawberry plants with straw

## Summer Tasks

### Early Summer
- Start planting out tender vegetables: sweet corn,
  outdoor tomatoes, green beans, and zucchini
- Continue successional sowing until late summer
- Put straw under bush tomatoes to keep fruit clean
- Transplant autumn and winter brassicas, protecting from
  cabbage root fly

### Midsummer
- Start late sowings of turnips and carrots for autumn
- Sow Swiss chard for [[holzer-soft-fruit-bushes-and-berries]] trees during dry spells

### Late Summer
- Sow spring cabbages in a stale seedbed
- Sow quick-maturing lettuce varieties for late crops
- [[savory-bare-ground-and-soil-exposure]] with compost as it becomes available
- Sow vacant ground with mustard or phacelia green-manure
  [[savory-growing-season-and-non-growing-season-management]] by protecting from frost and warming
the soil for earlier sowing. Remove covers during the day
