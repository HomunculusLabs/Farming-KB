     9|     9|# [[mushroom-geotropism-spore-formation]] and Spore Formation
    10|    10|
    11|    11|[[negative-geotropism-in-mushroom-fruiting]] mushrooms is the upward bending of the stipe (stem) in response to gravity, which becomes dominant at the onset of spore production.
    12|    12|
    13|    13|In *[[basidiocarp-four-stage-development-classification-psilocybe-cubensis-badham-1982]]*, this geotropic response is intimately linked to spore formation — sterile mutants that cannot produce spores do not exhibit negative geotropism.
    14|    14|
    15|    15|This connection was demonstrated by Badham (1982) using fertile and sterile strains in controlled wind tunnel experiments.
    16|    16|
    17|    17|## Developmental Stages
    18|    18|
    19|    19|Badham defined four developmental stages for *P. cubensis* basidiocarps:
    20|    20|
    21|    21|**Stage 1**: Slight swelling of the pileus (cap).
    22|    22|
    23|    23|The primordium has emerged but the cap shows minimal expansion.
    24|    24|
    25|    25|**Stage 2**: Pileus is nearly spherical.
    26|    26|
    27|    27|The cap has expanded but remains closed.
    28|    28|
    29|    29|[[evaporative-demand-gradient-stipe-elongation-mechanism-badham-1982]] is at its peak during this stage.
    30|    30|
    31|    31|**Stage 3**: Maturity — pileus is an open hemisphere.
    32|    32|
    33|    33|Spore production has begun.
    34|    34|
    35|    35|The gills (lamellae) are exposed.
    36|    36|
    37|    37|**Stage 4**: Late maturity — pileus becomes more or less plane.
    38|    38|
    39|    39|Maximum spore production.
    40|    40|
    41|    41|The cap is fully expanded.
    42|    42|
    43|    43|## The Geotropic Transition
    44|    44|
    45|    45|Between Stage 2 and Stage 3, a dramatic shift in tropic behavior occurs.
    46|    46|
    47|    47|During Stages 1–2, the stipe is primarily influenced by anemotropism (wind response) and grows toward air flow.
    48|    48|
    49|    49|At Stage 3, when spores begin to form, a negative geotropic response emerges.
    50|    50|
    51|    51|The pileus turns upward from the direction of the wind, and the lamellae face downward.
    52|    52|
    53|    53|This recurvation — bending back upward — is one of the most visually striking behaviors in mushroom development.
    54|    54|
    55|    55|## Evidence from Sterile Mutants
    56|    56|
    57|    57|The connection between geotropism and spore formation is supported by experiments with sterile (sporeless) mutant strains of *P. cubensis*.
    58|    58|
    59|    59|Sterile basidiocarps exhibit normal anemotropic growth during Stages 1–2.
    60|    60|
    61|    61|However, at Stage 3 when fertile mushrooms would recurve upward, sterile mutants continue to grow in the direction of the wind.
    62|    62|
    63|    63|They do not exhibit the negative geotropic curvature.
    64|    64|
    65|    65|This demonstrates that the geotropic response is not a general property of stipe tissue.
    66|    66|
    67|    67|Instead, it is specifically linked to the onset of spore production.
    68|    68|
    69|    69|## Role of Light
    70|    70|
    71|    71|Light plays a necessary but possibly indirect role in the geotropic response.
    72|    72|
    73|    73|The recurvature does not occur in the absence of light.
    74|    74|
    75|    75|However, Badham noted that light may be necessary primarily because it is required for spore production itself.
    76|    76|
    77|    77|If spore formation requires light, and geotropism requires spore formation, then the apparent light requirement for geotropism may be secondary.
    78|    78|
    79|    79|The experiments showed that recurvature occurs when light is incident from either above or below the culture.
    80|    80|
    81|    81|This rules out a simple phototropic mechanism (growth toward light).
    82|    82|
    83|    83|Instead, it supports the hypothesis that light enables spore formation, which in turn triggers the geotropic response.
    84|    84|
    85|    85|## Rotation Experiments
    86|    86|
    87|    87|When basidiocarps were rotated horizontally (parallel to the airflow) at 3 rpm under constant illumination, the stipes elongated and the pileus opened into the wind.
    88|    88|
    89|    89|Gravity effects were equalized by rotation, confirming that the horizontal growth direction in this condition is wind-determined.
