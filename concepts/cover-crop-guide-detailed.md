# Cover Crop Guide Detailed

Cover crops are plants grown primarily to benefit the soil rather than for harvest. They protect soil from erosion, suppress weeds, fix nitrogen, improve soil structure, and feed soil biology. A well-planned cover crop program is foundational to any regenerative homestead.

## Why Grow Cover Crops

- Prevent soil erosion from wind and rain
- Suppress weed growth through competition and allelopathy
- Fix atmospheric nitrogen (legumes only)
- Scavenge residual soil nutrients and prevent leaching
- Build soil organic matter
- Break compaction layers with deep taproots
- Attract beneficial insects and pollinators
- Cycle nutrients from deep soil layers to the surface
- Provide biomass for [[hot-composting-vs-vermicompost-vs-bokashi]] production

## Cover Crops by Season

### Spring Cover Crops

**Crimson Clover (Trifolium incarnatum)**
- Hardiness: annual, dies at 15F
- Seeding rate: 15-20 lbs/acre or 1/4 lb per 1000 sq ft
- nitrogen fixation: 70-150 lbs N/acre
- Benefits: Excellent nitrogen fixer, beautiful red flowers attract pollinators, winter-hardy in zone 7+
- Termination: Mow or crimp at early bloom, or till in
- Good for: Preceding corn, tomatoes, peppers, squash

**Field Peas (Pisum sativum)**
- Hardiness: semi-hardy annual
- Seeding rate: 100-150 lbs/acre
- Nitrogen fixation: 90-150 lbs N/acre
- Benefits: Fast growth, vigorous nitrogen fixation, good biomass producer
- Termination: Mow or incorporate at early pod stage
- Good for: Preceding heavy-feeding crops

**Oats (Avena sativa)**
- Hardiness: killed by hard frost (convenient for no-till)
- Seeding rate: 80-120 lbs/acre
- Benefits: Quick establishment, winter-kill leaves dead mulch on surface, scavenges nutrients, fibrous roots build soil structure
- Termination: Winter kill naturally — no mechanical termination needed
- Good for: [[no-till-gardening-method|No-till]] gardens where you want a dead mulch in spring

**Buckwheat (Fagopyrum esculentum)**
- Hardiness: tender annual, killed by frost
- Seeding rate: 40-60 lbs/acre or 1/3 lb per 1000 sq ft
- Benefits: Extremely fast germination (7-10 days), smothers weeds, attracts beneficial insects, phosphorus scavenger, tender leaves decompose quickly
- Termination: Mow at flowering (before seed set) or incorporate
- Growth period: 35-45 days from seed to flower
- Good for: Quick cover between crops, phosphorus scavenging, bee forage

### Summer Cover Crops

**Sorghum-Sudangrass**
- Hardiness: warm-season annual
- Seeding rate: 30-50 lbs/acre
- Benefits: Massive biomass production (can reach 8+ feet), deep root system breaks compaction, drought tolerant, allelopathic weed suppression
- Termination: Mow repeatedly to prevent seed set, incorporate when 3-4 feet tall
- Caution: Prussic acid toxicity — do not graze during drought or after frost
- Good for: Building large amounts of organic matter, subsoiling

**Cowpeas (Vigna unguiculata)**
- Hardiness: warm-season annual
- Seeding rate: 40-60 lbs/acre
- Nitrogen fixation: 100-150 lbs N/acre
- Benefits: Excellent heat and drought tolerance, good nematode suppression, produces edible beans
- Termination: Mow or incorporate at flowering
- Good for: Summer nitrogen fixation in hot climates

### Fall Cover Crops

**Winter Rye (Secale cereale)**
- Hardiness: extremely cold hardy (survives to -30F)
- Seeding rate: 60-120 lbs/acre or 3-4 lbs per 1000 sq ft
- Benefits: Most cold-tolerant grain cover crop, extensive root system prevents erosion, scavenges leftover nitrogen, suppresses weeds through allelopathy, provides winter grazing
- Termination: Mow or crimp at boot stage, or incorporate before seed set
- Good for: The most reliable fall-planted cover crop in cold climates

**Hairy Vetch (Vicia villosa)**
- Hardiness: winter hardy to -20F
- Seeding rate: 20-30 lbs/acre or 1/2 lb per 1000 sq ft
- Nitrogen fixation: 90-200 lbs N/acre (one of the highest)
- Benefits: Excellent nitrogen fixer, climbing growth habit works well with rye as a companion, good weed suppression, drought tolerant once established
- Termination: Mow or crimp at full bloom, or incorporate
- Good for: Maximum nitrogen fixation, pairing with winter rye

**Daikon Radish / Tillage Radish**
- Hardiness: dies at 20F (winter-kill)
- Seeding rate: 8-12 lbs/acre
- Benefits: Massive taproot (can reach 3+ feet) breaks hardpan and compaction, winter-kill leaves channels for water infiltration, stores nutrients in root, decomposes quickly in spring
- Termination: Winter kill naturally
- Good for: Breaking compaction, improving drainage, no-till systems

## Cover Crop Mixes

Multi-species mixes outperform monocultures in most situations. A basic fall mix for cold climates:

- Winter rye: 2 lbs per 1000 sq ft (structure, erosion control)
- Hairy vetch: 0.5 lbs per 1000 sq ft (nitrogen fixation)
- Crimson clover: 0.25 lbs per 1000 sq ft (nitrogen fixation, pollinator attraction)
- Daikon radish: 0.25 lbs per 1000 sq ft (compaction breaking)

## Termination Methods

### No-Till Methods
- **Crimping**: Use a roller-crimper at flowering to kill cover crops without tilling
- **Mowing**: Mow low at flowering; may need multiple passes
- **Solarization**: Cover with tarps after mowing to accelerate kill
- **Winter kill**: Plant species that die with frost (oats, buckwheat, radish)

### Tillage Methods
- Incorporate with a rototiller or broadfork before the cover crop sets seed
- Allow 2-3 weeks for decomposition before planting the next crop

## Integration with Living Soil Systems

Cover crops are an excellent way to maintain soil biology between cash crop cycles. The root exudates feed [[soil-food-web-nutrient-cycling]] and mycorrhizal networks even during fallow periods. Legume cover crops provide nitrogen that living soil beds can cycle efficiently.
