# Wind Patterns, Harmonics, and the Beaufort Scale

## Overview

Wind is both one of the most powerful forces shaping landscapes and one of the most challenging elements for [[permaculture]] designers to manage. Unlike precipitation and temperature, wind offers the least potential for storage or generation, but its behaviour can be significantly modified on-site through windbreaks, shelterbelts, and strategic landform design. Understanding wind direction, speed categories, seasonal patterns, and the phenomenon of wind harmonics is essential for productive site design.

## Wind Speed Categories and Effects

### The Beaufort Scale

The [[beaufort-scale]] is the standard system for reporting wind speed and its effects:

| Beaufort No. | Knots | km/h | MPH | Description | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | <1 | <1 | <0.5 | Calm | Negligible |
| 1 | 1–3 | 1–5 | 1–3 | Light airs | Slight, no damage |
| 2 | 4–6 | 6–11 | 4–7 | Light breeze | Slight, no damage |
| 3 | 7–10 | 12–19 | 8–12 | Gentle breeze | Damage to very susceptible species |
| 4 | 11–16 | 20–28 | 13–17 | Moderate breeze | Damage to susceptible species; useful energy produced |
| 5 | 17–21 | 29–38 | 18–24 | Fresh breeze | Mechanical damage to crops, some structural damage |
| 6 | 22–27 | 39–49 | 25–31 | Strong breeze | Increasing damage |
| 7 | 28–33 | 50–61 | 32–38 | Near gale | Severe structural and crop damage begins |
| 8 | 34–40 | 62–74 | 39–46 | Gale | Severe damage to structures and crops |
| 9 | 41–47 | 75–88 | 47–55 | Strong gale | Severe damage to windmills |
| 10 | 48–55 | 89–102 | 56–64 | Storm | Catastrophic structural damage |
| 11 | 56–63 | 103–117 | 65–73 | Violent storm | Extreme damage |
| 12 | 64+ | 118+ | 74+ | Hurricane | Devastation |

### Critical Wind Speed Thresholds for Agriculture

- **8 km/h:** Harmless to crops
- **24 km/h:** Reduces crop production and causes weight loss in animals
- **32–40 km/h:** Sheer mechanical damage to plants exceeds all other effects—plants may be uprooted and blown along like tumbleweeds
- **Cyclonic/hurricane winds:** Become the overriding design modification around which all other factors must be arranged

## Wind Directions and Seasonal Patterns

### Global Wind Patterns by Latitude

**Latitudes 0°–35° (both hemispheres):** Winds are bi-modal and seasonal:
- Southern hemisphere: Southeast or northwest
- Northern hemisphere: Southwest or northeast
- Windward and leeward coast phenomena are almost universal
- Locally modified by landscape features

**Latitudes 35° to occupied coasts:**
- Westerlies prevail in winter
- Easterlies occur sporadically as high or low pressure systems pass
- Cold winds blow from continental interiors in winter
- Chilling winter winds also arrive from the seas

### Tropical Island and Peninsula Patterns

**Latitudes 0°–28°:** Islands and peninsulas experience two main wind modes:

1. **Winter winds (trade winds):** Southeast in southern hemisphere, northeast in northern hemisphere
2. **Monsoon winds:** Cross-equatorial, deflecting to blow from the northwest in the southern hemisphere and as southwest monsoons in the northern hemisphere during summer

Two short periods of relative calm or shifting wind systems occur between the trade wind and monsoon seasons. Southeast Asia and Pacific/Indian Ocean islands are most predictably affected by these bi-modal systems.

### Reading Wind History from the Landscape

Wind-flagging and wind-pruning of older trees reveal the site's summed total wind effects over time—a living record of prevailing wind direction and intensity that every landscape designer should learn to read.

## Wind Harmonics

### The Pulsing Nature of Wind

Ground winds do not blow steadily but vary as gusts and calms in a predictable, locality-specific way. [[frank-bethwaite]] (New Zealand-born Australian boat designer, pilot, and sailor) outlined the key characteristics:

- Regular variations can be easily timed
- **49–60 minute frequency** of gusting is typical of mid-latitudes
- Gusts are approximately **40% stronger** than lulls
- During lulls, wind direction changes—light crosswinds at about **15° to the main wind direction**

### Visual Indicators of Wind Harmonics

Wind "waves" are made visible in several ways:

- **Grasslands:** Wave fronts may pass every 14 seconds
- **Water surfaces:** Viewed from cliffs, wave patterns reveal periodicity
- **Cloud formations:** "Rank and file" cloud systems reflect gusts (cloud ranks) and lulls (spaces between ranks with differently-aligned light clouds)

### Locality-Specific Wind Periods

| Location | Gust Period |
|---|---|
| Bahrain | 5.25 minutes |
| Sydney | 6–12 minutes |
| Toronto | 10 minutes |
| Sea waves (normal) | 5–12 per minute |
| Sea waves (storms) | Longer period |

Both frequencies, durations, and amplitudes can be measured using combinations of stopwatches, anemometers, and wind vanes, or recorded on automatic equipment.

### Prevalent Winds vs. Energy Winds

In the westerly wind belts, two distinct categories are recognized:

- **Prevalent winds:** 8–24 km/h, blow for five out of seven windy days
- **Energy winds:** 16–40 km/h, blow on the other two days, coming from 15°–20° off the direction of prevalent winds

This distinction is critical for wind energy planning and windbreak design.

## Wind-Transported Materials and Organisms

### Inorganic Materials

- **Salt spray** on warm sea coasts is the limiting factor on species selection—only plants with fibrous or waxy surfaces escape death or deformation
- **Dry sulphur and nitrogen particles** carried by wind convert to acid in misty rains, burning gardens, forests, and even pitting paints and roofing
- **Plutonium and other radioactives** are carried downwind of nuclear waste stores, tests, and accidents (Colorado, Virginia, Utah, the Urals)
- **Sand** in deserts and coastal dunes buries fences, buildings, trees, and crops through wind transport

### Organic Materials

Wind transports organisms ranging from:
- **Impalpable spores** of fungi and ferns
- **Heavy insects** such as plague locusts, swept aloft by heated air columns and carried as frozen swarms to down-draught areas
- **Mosquitoes, fruit flies, wasps, and spiders** that deliberately spin aerial floatlines to migrate over mountains and oceans
- **Migratory bird flocks** that take advantage of windstreams during global circuits

## Wind Effects on Plant Physiology

Flow of air over leaf surfaces promotes rapid transpiration, especially when combined with high light intensity. Plants have evolved several protective mechanisms:

- **Whitish leaf undersides** are presented to light as wind blows, maintaining dynamic balance between light and wind factors
- **Leaf angle alteration** to reflect light, trap air, or reduce exposed leaf area
- **Pigmentation changes** and **stomatal closure** to prevent moisture loss
- **Wind-flagging and wind-pruning** shape trees into characteristic streamlined forms

## The Chill Factor

The **chill factor**—the removal of heat from surfaces and evaporation of fluids—creates cool to cold climates in the tropics at lower altitudes than adiabatic or altitude factors alone would indicate. This factor:

- Retards plant growth
- Lowers efficiency of solar devices and insulation
- Is the primary mechanism making wind exposure a design concern even in warm climates
