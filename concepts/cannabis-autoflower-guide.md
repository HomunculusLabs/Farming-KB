# Cannabis Autoflower Guide

## Overview

[[autoflowering-cannabis]] strains automatically transition from vegetative growth to flowering based on age rather than light cycle. This trait comes from [[cannabis-ruderalis]], a subspecies that evolved in extreme northern latitudes with very short [[jeavons-climate-adaptation-growing-seasons]]. Incorporating ruderalis genetics into [[marijuana-strain-guide-sativa|sativa]] and [[marijuana-strain-guide-indica|indica]] lines has created a category of fast-finishing, low-maintenance plants popular with both beginners and experienced growers.

## Ruderalis Genetics

### Origins

Cannabis ruderalis is native to Central and Eastern Europe, Russia, and Central Asia. Key characteristics of pure ruderalis:

- Very short stature (1-2 feet)
- Automatic flowering at 3-4 weeks of age regardless of light cycle
- Low THC content (typically under 3%)
- High CBD content in some populations
- Extreme hardiness and disease resistance
- Ability to survive harsh climates and short seasons

Ruderalis was largely ignored by cannabis breeders until the early 2000s, when its autoflowering trait was recognized as valuable for creating faster, more discreet growing options.

### The Joint Doctor and Lowryder

The modern autoflower movement began with "The Joint Doctor," a Canadian breeder who created Lowryder in the early 2000s. Lowryder crossed ruderalis with Northern Lights #2 and William's Wonder to create the first commercially viable autoflowering strain. While Lowryder's potency was modest, it proved the concept and launched extensive [[cannabis-breeding-basics|breeding programs]] focused on autoflowers.

## How Autoflowering Works

### Photoperiod Independence

Regular cannabis (photoperiod strains) requires a shift from 18+ hours of light to 12 hours of light to trigger flowering. This mimics the natural shortening of days in late summer and fall.

Autoflowers, by contrast, flower based on an internal genetic timer. After 2-4 weeks of vegetative growth (depending on the specific strain), the plant will begin flowering regardless of the light schedule. This is caused by specific genes inherited from ruderalis that trigger the flowering hormone cascade independently of photoreceptor signals.

### Growth Timeline

A typical autoflower life cycle:

- **Week 1-2:** Seedling stage
- **Week 2-4:** Vegetative growth (shorter than photoperiod plants)
- **Week 3-4:** Pre-flowering begins (automatic transition)
- **Week 4-8:** Flowering stage
- **Week 8-10:** Harvest (some strains up to 12 weeks)

Total grow time from seed to harvest: 8-12 weeks for most modern autoflowers, compared to 14-20+ weeks for photoperiod strains.

## Pros of Autoflowering Strains

### Speed

The most significant advantage is the compressed timeline. From seed to harvest in 8-10 weeks means more harvests per year. In [[cannabis-indoor-growing-basics|indoor setups]], growers can achieve 4-6 harvests annually.

### Light Schedule Flexibility

Autoflowers can be grown on any light schedule, including:

- 18/6 (most common — 18 hours light, 6 hours dark)
- 20/4 (maximum growth with minimal dark period)
- 24/0 (continuous light — some debate about plant health)
- 12/12 (compatible with photoperiod plants in the same space)

This flexibility allows autoflowers and photoperiod plants to share the same [[cannabis-indoor-growing-basics|grow space]].

### Compact Size

Most autoflowers stay under 3 feet tall, making them ideal for:

- Small grow spaces and closets
- Stealth growing
- Balcony and patio cultivation
- SOG ([[sea-of-green]]) setups with many small plants

### Hardiness

Ruderalis genetics contribute exceptional resilience:

- Cold tolerance down to near-freezing temperatures
- Pest and disease resistance
- Ability to recover from stress (overwatering, light stress, nutrient issues)
- Less sensitive to light leaks during dark periods

### Multiple Harvests

In [[cannabis-outdoor-growing|outdoor environments]], autoflowers can produce 2-3 harvests per growing season in most climates. Stagger planting every 2-3 weeks for continuous harvests.

## Cons of Autoflowering Strains

### Lower Yields

Autoflowers typically produce 1-4 ounces per plant, compared to 4-16+ ounces for photoperiod plants. The shorter vegetative period limits the plant's size and bud production capacity.

### Reduced Training Options

Autoflowers have a limited recovery window due to their fixed life cycle. High-stress training (HST) techniques like [[query-what-is-the-difference-between-topping-and-fimming-cannabis]] can stall growth and reduce yield. Recommended [[mckenna-shamanic-identification-signs-and-training-methods]] include:

- Low-stress training (LST) — gentle bending and tying
- Defoliation (light, conservative)
- Trellis netting for support
- Avoid topping, super cropping, or transplanting after establishment

### No Cloning

Autoflowering clones inherit the biological age of the mother plant. A clone taken from a 4-week-old autoflower will flower immediately, regardless of its size. This makes cloning impractical and means every grow starts from seed.

### Smaller Plant Size Limitations

While compact size is an advantage for small spaces, it limits the potential for large-scale production. Commercial growers typically prefer photoperiod plants for higher yields per plant.

### Less Potency (Historically)

Early autoflowers had significantly lower THC than photoperiod strains. Modern breeding has largely closed this gap, with many current autoflowers reaching 20-25% THC. However, the absolute highest-potency strains remain photoperiod varieties.

## Best Autoflower Varieties

### High-THC Autoflowers

- **Gorilla Glue Auto:** 20-25% THC, resin-heavy indica-dominant
- **Amnesia Haze Auto:** 20-22% THC, sativa-dominant with cerebral effects
- **[[girl-scout-cookies]] Auto:** 21-25% THC, balanced hybrid effects
- **Bruce Banner Auto:** 22-26% THC, potent and fast-finishing

### High-CBD Autoflowers

- **CBD Auto Charlotte's Angel:** 15-20% CBD, less than 1% THC
- **CBD Solomatic Auto:** High CBD medicinal strain
- **Harlequin Auto:** Balanced CBD:THC ratio for therapeutic use

### Beginner-Friendly Autoflowers

- **Royal Dwarf:** Extremely compact, easy to grow, forgiving
- **Easy Bud:** One of the simplest autoflowers to cultivate
- **Quick One:** Fast finishing (8 weeks seed to harvest)
- **Northern Light Auto:** Classic genetics, resilient, consistent

## Growing Autoflowers Successfully

### Starting from Seed

Direct sowing is preferred over starting in small pots. Autoflowers do not handle transplanting well due to their short life cycle. Plant seeds directly in their final container (3-5 gallon fabric pots are common).
