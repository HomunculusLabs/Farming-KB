# Getting a Natural Diet

## Overview

Fukuoka presents his practical philosophy of natural eating, which parallels
his approach to [[comparison-natural-farming-vs-permaculture]]. A true natural diet is a way of eating where
one feeds randomly with an undiscriminating attitude on food taken from the
wild, crops grown by [[cho-loess-powder-in-natural-farming]], and fish and shellfish caught using
[[fukuoka-textdoc-creating-rice-paddy-natural-methods]]. Natural diet [[fukuoka-textdoc-three-stages-of-human-collapse-and-natural-farming-future]] are not separate ideas but
united intimately as one whole.

## The Foundation of Natural Diet

Natural farming consists of adapting to true nature, that is, nature
understood with non-discriminating knowledge. In the same way, a true natural
diet means feeding on food taken from the wild and from naturally farmed
crops. One must abandon an artificial diet designed on the basis of
discriminating scientific knowledge and, gradually liberating oneself from
philosophical constraints, ultimately deny and transcend these.

## The Role of Traditional Crops

[[fungal-endophytes-agricultural-crops]] which for many thousands of years have merely adapted to
the environment and survived through natural selection to become fixtures of
human society may be thought of as foods that arose naturally. This does not
apply to crops developed recently through breeding programs that are
considerably alienated from nature. These, along with artificially bred fish
and livestock, should be firmly excluded from the diet.

## The Unity of Food, Clothing, Shelter, and Spirit

Natural diet and natural farming are united intimately as one whole. They are
one too with natural fishing and [[holzer-natural-animal-husbandry]]. Man's food, clothing, and
shelter, and his spiritual existence must all be blended together with nature
in perfect harmony. To separate diet from farming, or food from spirit, is to
commit the fundamental error that characterizes modern civilization.

## Eating with the Seasons

The food that farmers and fishermen have taken locally for thousands of years
is a splendid example of natural diet in accordance with the laws of nature.
The seven herbs of spring emerge early in the new year from the slumbering
brown earth. As the farmer enjoys their flavors, he meditates joyfully on
having survived a harsh winter. Nature provides shellfish as a complementary
brown food at exactly the right time.

In summer, loquats glistening in the early rains are a fruit the body craves.
All fruits ripen at the right time of year, and that is when they are most
delicious. The bitter and sour flavors of oleaster berry, plum, and apricot
suit the season perfectly. In early autumn, cereal grains, soybeans, and
adzuki beans become available. The ripened grains of rice that have fully
absorbed the yang of summer provide a food staple rich in calories for winter.

## The Seasonal Round of Delicacy

Spring brings bracken, osmund, young cherry and persimmon leaves, bamboo
shoots delicious with rockfish, and red sea bream at the barley harvest. In
summer, one may eat melon, drink milk, and lick honey in the cool shade. In
autumn, millet dumplings under the moonlight, taros and green soybeans,
matsutake mushrooms and rice, chestnuts and rice. In winter, mallard and wild
game served with welsh onions, pickled vegetables from the fall, oysters, sea
urchins, and sea cucumbers.

## Beyond Yin and Yang

Fukuoka incorporates but transcends both Western [[fukuoka-critique-western-dietetics-nutritional-science]] and the
[[fukuoka-western-eastern-philosophy-agriculture]] of yin and yang. Summer is hot and yang, winter is cold and
yin. The diet maintains a balance: in summer one should eat yin foods, in
winter yang foods. Vegetables are green, seaweed is blue, cereal grains are
yellow, and meat is red. Grains, being intermediate, serve as the staple with
yin vegetables, while meat consumption, essentially cannibalism, is avoided.

However, even if these principles are essential medically or in the treatment
of disease, too much concern over whether something is yin or yang, acidic or
alkaline, leads one right back into the realm of science and discriminating
knowledge. The mandala of foods shows that as long as man accepts and lives
in accordance with the divine dispensation, a complete natural diet will arise
of itself without his needing to know anything.
