# Earthmoving Machinery

Moving earth is fundamental to [[permaculture-zones]] system.

## Swivel and Scoop Buckets

Fixed loading buckets are often fitted to wheeled tractors for lifting and tipping loose material up to 4 m — useful in quarries and nurseries.

Swivel or scoop bucket machines can scoop out material, swivel around, and deposit it elsewhere. Some can dig wells to 9 m (30 feet) using hydraulic extension arms, and all can dig sharp-edged pits or drains. They normally reach out 6–10 m (20–32 feet) and, standing on dry land, can remove silt from canals or ponds.

## Dragline

For reaching beyond the range of bucket machines, the dragline is a crane-like device that casts out a loose or tractor-tethered bucket dredge up to 18–24 m (60–80 feet) and hauls it back full. A two-tractor dragline system uses one tractor to haul an endless rope with a bucket across even greater distances.

## Spinner

The spinner is a large wheel with small peripheral buckets spun at high speed, throwing earth out as small clods over a considerable distance. Much used in the Netherlands to drain polders, it is the most economical and speedy machine for flatland shallow drains. Crucially, the spinner distributes spoil as small clods as it travels, so no banks form beside spinner drains — the excavated material is spread rather than piled.

## Trench Digger

A chain-bucket system (commercially called a "Ditch-Witch" or "Trench-Wench") continuously digs narrow trenches for pipe-laying or foundation insulation.

## Animal-Drawn Scoops

Scoops drawn behind horses or oxen can excavate small ponds or clean silt from existing ponds. Unless fitted with a rear spill-door or chain tipper, they require considerable manual effort to empty and can be exhausting to operate.

## Machine Selection Principles

- **Small tractors** are fuel-efficient and can accomplish large earthworks given sufficient time
- **Bulldozers** are time-efficient and handle difficult terrain
- **Graders** produce the finest surfaces for roads and long drains
- **Scrapers** handle the largest area-leveling tasks
- **Bobcats** are ideal for finishing work and swale construction in intensively managed zones
- **Draglines** extend reach for canal and pond work
- **Spinners** are optimal for flatland drainage without creating spoil banks
