# PDA Medium Production and Mushroom Tissue Culture

PDA (Potato Dextrose Agar) is the standard growth medium used in [[mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities-training-program]] isolating, propagating, and maintaining pure mycelium cultures. This page covers the complete process of producing PDA medium, performing tissue culture from fresh [[aquarium-humidity-chamber-mushroom-fruiting]] bodies, and transferring mycelium between PDA bottles for multiplication.

## PDA Medium Production

### Materials

| Material | Quantity | Notes |
|----------|----------|-------|
| Potatoes | 200 g | Fresh, free of spots or rot |
| Dextrose | 20 g | Commercial grade |
| Agar powder | 20 g | Commercial grade |
| Water | 1 liter | Clean, potable |
| Cotton (gauze) | As needed | For plugging bottles |
| Small flat bottles | As needed | Whiskey bottles work well |

### Production Process

1. **Inspect the potatoes.** Check each potato carefully for dark spots, soft areas, or signs of rot. Only use clean, healthy potatoes — contaminated raw material will ruin the entire batch.

2. **Wash and cut the potatoes.** Wash thoroughly under running water to remove soil and debris. Cut into approximately 1 cm cubes. The skin may be left on or peeled off — both methods work, though peeling can produce a clearer broth.

3. **Clean the small flat bottles.** Wash all bottles with soap and hot water, rinse thoroughly, and allow to air dry. Any residue left inside can harbor contaminants.

4. **Simmer the potatoes.** Place the potato cubes into 1 liter of water in a clean pot. Bring to a simmer and cook for 15–20 minutes until the potatoes are soft. Do not boil vigorously.

5. **Extract the broth.** Remove the potatoes from the pot, straining through gauze or a fine sieve. Keep the broth as clear as possible — cloudy broth can obscure contamination later. Add fresh water to bring the total volume back up to 1 liter.

6. **Add dextrose and agar.** Return the broth to the stove. Add the 20 g of dextrose first, then the 20 g of agar powder. Stir continuously over heat until both ingredients are fully dissolved. This is critical — undissolved agar will result in an uneven medium.

7. **Pour into bottles.** While still liquid, pour the hot PDA medium into the cleaned flat bottles. Fill each bottle to a height of 5–10 mm — this is shallow enough to allow slanting later while providing enough medium for growth.

8. **Plug the bottles.** Insert a cotton (gauze) plug into the neck of each bottle. The plug must be firm enough to stay in place but loose enough to allow gas exchange during sterilization and incubation.

9. **Sterilize (autoclave).** Place all plugged bottles into the autoclave. Sterilize at 121 °C for 20–30 minutes. After the cycle completes, allow the bottles to cool gradually to approximately 37 °C before handling.

10. **Set the slant.** While the PDA is still soft but cooled, place each bottle in a slanted position. This creates an inclined surface that maximizes the area available for mycelium growth. The PDA should extend close to the neck of the bottle but must not touch the cotton plug, as wet cotton can wick in contaminants.

11. **Inspect for contamination.** After the PDA has fully solidified and cooled, examine each bottle carefully. Look for dark spots, discolored lines, or any unusual growth on the surface. Discard any contaminated bottles before proceeding to tissue culture.

## Tissue Culture Procedure

### Materials

- **Special needle** with an insulated handle (for safe handling after flame sterilization)
- **Alcohol lamp** (continuous flame source for sterile technique)
- **Alcohol** (70% ethanol for surface disinfection)
- **Cotton** (for swabbing and cleaning)
- **Matches or lighter** (to light the alcohol lamp)
- **Prepared PDA bottles** (sterilized, slanted, and uncontaminated)
- **Laminar flow cabinet** (provides a sterile workspace with filtered airflow)
- **UV lamp** (for pre-sterilization of the laminar flow cabinet interior)

### Step-by-Step Procedure

1. **Select a strong, healthy mushroom.** Choose a fruiting body that is robust and free of visible contamination. The mushroom should not be too mature or too young. Ideally, perform tissue culture 2–3 hours after watering so the mushroom is not overly humid. Look for a stiff stalk and a clean specimen collected from an area far from known [[stamets-contamination-sources-laboratory-hygiene]].

2. **Clean the workspace and tools.** Wipe down the entire room, the laminar flow cabinet interior, and all tools with 70% alcohol. Transfer the cleaned materials into the laminar flow cabinet.

3. **Activate the UV lamp.** Turn the UV lamp on inside the laminar flow cabinet for 10–15 minutes to sterilize the air and surfaces. After this period, turn the UV lamp off but keep the laminar flow fan running continuously throughout the procedure.

4. **Disinfect hands and bottles.** Thoroughly clean your hands with alcohol. Also wipe down the exterior of each PDA bottle that will be used.

5. **Sterilize the needle.** Hold the special needle at a 45° angle over the alcohol lamp flame. Heat the tip until it glows red. The insulated handle protects your fingers from burns.

6. **Allow the needle to cool.** Wait 15–20 seconds after removing the needle from the flame. A needle that is too hot will kill the tissue sample on contact.

7. **Open the mushroom.** Tear the mushroom open lengthwise by hand. **Do not use a knife** — cutting with a blade can drag surface contaminants into the interior tissue, defeating the purpose of aseptic technique.

8. **Excise the tissue sample.** Using the cooled, sterilized needle, cut a small piece of fleshy tissue approximately 2 mm × 2 mm from the inside of the mushroom. The ideal location is in the middle zone between the cap and the stalk, where the tissue is cleanest and most viable.

9. **Open the PDA bottle.** Pass the mouth of the PDA bottle through the alcohol lamp flame to sterilize the rim. Remove the cotton plug while holding the bottle mouth as close to the flame as possible.

10. **Place the tissue on the PDA.** Insert the needle through the bottle mouth and gently deposit the tissue sample onto the middle of the PDA surface. Avoid touching the sides of the bottle or the cotton plug.

11. **Close the bottle.** Immediately reinsert the cotton plug while keeping the bottle mouth near the flame. This prevents airborne contaminants from entering.

12. **Label the bottle.** Write the following information on each bottle: the date of inoculation, the mushroom species or type, and the mother spawn number for tracking purposes.

13. **Maintain sterile posture.** Throughout the procedure, keep the bottom of the bottle lower than its mouth, and keep the mouth oriented toward the flame. This ensures that gravity and the upward convection of the flame work together to prevent contamination from falling into the open bottle.

## PDA to PDA Transfer

### Why Transfer?

The initial tissue culture step carries a high risk of contamination because the [[shirota-4-hydroxyindole-acetyl-protection-psilocin-synthesis-starting-material]] (a whole mushroom) is not sterile. For this reason, it is best to inoculate only a few bottles in the first round. Once clean mycelium growth is confirmed on those bottles, the mycelium can be multiplied through PDA-to-PDA transfers, which carry a much lower contamination risk since the source culture is already pure.

### Transfer Technique

- Cut a piece of actively growing mycelium approximately 5 mm × 5 mm from an uncontaminated PDA bottle.
- Use the same sterile transfer technique described in the tissue culture procedure: flame-sterilize the needle, work inside the laminar flow cabinet, keep the bottle mouth near the flame, and plug immediately after inoculation.
- Place the mycelium piece in the center of the fresh PDA surface.

### Incubation and Storage

- Full [[mycelial-colonization]] of a slanted PDA bottle typically takes **10–15 days**, depending on the mushroom species and incubation conditions.
- Once fully colonized, mature mycelium cultures can be stored in a cool place or in the vegetable compartment of a refrigerator to slow growth and extend viability.
- **Check every bottle daily** for signs of infection: off-color growth, unusual textures, or dark spots. Immediately separate and discard any contaminated bottles to prevent spread.
- Keep **detailed observation notes** for each bottle, recording the date of inoculation, growth progress, any anomalies observed, and the final outcome. This documentation is essential for troubleshooting and quality control.

## Key Principles

- **Sterility is everything.** The single most important factor in successful tissue culture is maintaining a sterile environment throughout every step.
- **Work quickly but carefully.** The longer a bottle is open, the greater the contamination risk.
- **Start small, then multiply.** A few clean tissue culture bottles can produce hundreds of transfer bottles if handled correctly.
- **Document everything.** Detailed records enable you to trace problems back to their source and improve your technique over time.
