## Effects of Hydrocarbon Pollution on Mycorrhizal Fungi

Hydrocarbon contamination significantly reduces mycorrhizal propagule density and colonization. Field studies have demonstrated:

- **Reduced propagule density**: AMF propagule density was 7.5× lower in petroleum-contaminated soils in Argentina (Cabello, 1997) and 5× lower in Germany, compared to uncontaminated reference sites.
- **Reduced colonization**: Plants in polluted soils consistently show lower mycorrhizal colonization percentages. However, some studies paradoxically found 150% higher mycorrhizal root colonization in contaminated soils, suggesting certain mycorrhizal species may be stimulated or selectively enriched by pollution conditions.
- **Vesicle degeneration**: In contaminated soils, vesicles show signs of degeneration due to the utilization of lipid reserves by the fungus, indicating physiological stress.

## Plant-Fungal Partnerships for Hydrocarbon Degradation

### Alfalfa and Glomus mosseae

A landmark 12-month study (Cabello, 1995) demonstrated the protective and degradative role of AMF inoculation in petroleum-contaminated soils:
- **Non-inoculated alfalfa**: Colonization increased from only 5% to 15% over 12 months
- **Inoculated alfalfa (G. mosseae)**: Colonization increased from 41% to 70% over the same period
- Inoculated plants showed significantly better growth and survival in contaminated soil

### ECM Fungi and Conifer Systems

ECM fungi colonize spruce plants effectively even at the time of contamination, with up to 90% of root tips becoming mycorrhizal (Nicolotti and Egli, 1998). Six distinct ECM morphotypes were identified, with Laccaria species showing the best colonization matching. Key ECM species involved include *Amanita pantherina*, *Cenococcum geophilum*, *Hebeloma crustuliniforme*, *Tricholoma vaccinum*, *Pisolithus tinctorius*, *Laccaria bicolor*, *Laccaria amethystea*, and *Paxillus involutus*.

### Bacterial-Fungal Consortia

A particularly effective mycorrhizal system involves *Paxillus involutus* or *Suillus bovinus* with *Pinus sylvestris*, which forms parenchymatous patches on hydrocarbon-contaminated soil supporting a hydrocarbon-degrading bacterial biofilm (Sarand et al., 1998). When this rhizosphere association was inoculated with a *Pseudomonas fluorescens* strain carrying a toluene-degrading plasmid, it degraded meta-toluate (Sarand et al., 1999). ## Field Applications

### Agricultural and Forest Soils

The long-term environmental consequences of oil spills in agricultural and forest areas are generally less severe than in coastal ecosystems, likely due to the presence of fungi and bacteria in soil that catalyze bioremediation. VAM fungal species *Glomus aggregatum* and *G. mosseae* are identified from both contaminated and uncontaminated soils, indicating their resilience.
