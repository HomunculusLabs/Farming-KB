# Polyculture Design (Holzer Method)

Sepp Holzer's approach to polyculture is central to his permaculture method. Rather than growing plants in monoculture, he advocates for maximum plant diversity and mixed cultivation systems.

## Core Principles

### Plant Diversity

Holzer describes his method as "working with nature, not against it." A diverse mixture of plants is the foundation. By growing different plants together, you create a self-regulating system where plants support and protect each other.

### Old Varieties

Holzer strongly advocates for using old, local plant varieties. These are significantly hardier, more resistant to disease and pests, and better adapted to local conditions than modern over-bred varieties. They have evolved over centuries to thrive in their specific environments.

### Green Manure

Green manure crops play a central role in Holzer's polyculture systems:
- Improve soil fertility naturally
- Prevent erosion
- Suppress weeds
- Fix nitrogen (leguminous plants)
- Attract beneficial insects
- Provide organic matter when incorporated into soil

Green manure is not a one-off measure — it must play a continuous role in cultivation because fertile and healthy soil is the key to success.

## Soil Fertility Approach

The foundation of all polyculture design is soil health. Rather than applying chemical fertilisers, Holzer builds soil fertility through:
- Green manure crops
- Mulching
- Earthworm activity
- Mixed plantings that feed the soil
- Avoiding bare soil
- Permanent soil cover

Chemical fertilisers are never used. Fertilised trees, for instance, do not lignify well enough and are not frost resistant.

## Practical Polyculture Tips

### Avoid Competition

Plants that grow to different heights can be positioned so that no competition arises. An arrangement of plants at staggered heights achieves the best use of space.

### Continuous Cropping

Mixed crops should be selected so as to avoid large areas being harvested all at once. This maintains permanent soil cover and protects earthworms and soil life.

### Supporting Plants

When establishing new plantings (e.g. fruit trees on new terraces), sow various supporting plants around them: sunflowers, Jerusalem artichokes, buckwheat, oilseed rape, scorpion weed, among others. These improve growing conditions as green manure and provide grazing opportunities.

### Distraction Planting

Problem plants can be regulated by sowing desirable companion plants (e.g. peas, beans or sweetcorn) between them to attract livestock to concentrate grazing there.

### Wild Plant Integration

Wild plants are not treated as weeds to be eradicated. Many have valuable functions in the ecosystem and should be integrated into the polyculture.

## Species Integration

Holzer's polycultures integrate:
- Vegetables and herbs
- Fruit trees and berry bushes
- Grain crops
- Green manure plants
- Wild flowers and native plants
- Livestock (for grazing, manure, and pest regulation)

## Benefits

- Reduced disease and pest problems through natural balance
- Improved soil health and fertility
- Multiple yields from the same area
- Reduced maintenance over time
- Resilience against extreme weather
- Self-regulating ecosystem
