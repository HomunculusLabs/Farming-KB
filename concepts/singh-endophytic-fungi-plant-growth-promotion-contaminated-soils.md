# Endophytic Fungi and Plant Growth Promotion in Contaminated Soils

## Overview

Endophytic fungi colonize plant tissues internally without causing
disease symptoms, forming mutualistic relationships that enhance host
plant performance under abiotic stress. The Singh source documents the
emerging role of endophyte-assisted phytoremediation, where fungal
endophytes improve plant establishment, growth, and pollutant
degradation capacity on contaminated sites. This approach combines
the advantages of phytoremediation with the biochemical capabilities
and stress-mitigating effects [[query-what-is-the-role-of-endophytic-fungi-in-plant-health]].

## Endophyte-Host Interactions

Endophytic fungi reside in intercellular spaces, vascular tissues,
and individual cells of aboveground and belowground plant organs.
Key genera include:

- Dark [[dark-septate-endophytes-root-colonizing-fungi]] (DSE): Exophiala, Leptodontidium, and
  Cadophora species are common in stressed and contaminated
  environments. They form melanized hyphae and microsclerotia in
  roots.

- Neotyphodium and Epichloe: Clavicipitaceous endophytes of grasses
  that produce alkaloids conferring herbivore resistance and stress
  tolerance.

- Fusarium, Trichoderma, and Piriformospora: Non-clavicipitaceous
  endophytes found in diverse plant species, including those growing
  on metal-contaminated or hydrocarbon-impacted soils.

- Serendipita indica (formerly [[linum-album-piriformospora-indica-co-culture-system]]): A versatile
  root endophyte that colonizes a broad host range and promotes growth
  under nutrient stress and metal contamination.

## Stress Tolerance Mechanisms

The Singh source describes how endophytic fungi enhance plant
resilience to contaminant-induced stress:

- Osmotic adjustment: Endophytes accumulate compatible solutes
  (trehalose, mannitol, proline) that stabilize cellular structures
  under drought and [[singh-mycorrhizal-response-under-salinity-stress]], which frequently accompany
  contamination.

- [[blesching-cannabis-oxidative-stress-and-antioxidant-defense]]: Endophyte-colonized plants show elevated
  activity of superoxide dismutase, catalase, and peroxidase enzymes,
  reducing [[reactive-oxygen-species-and-oxidative-stress]] generated by pollutant exposure.

- Metal chelation: Some endophytes produce metallothioneins and
  phytochelatins that bind heavy metals, reducing metal toxicity to
  both the fungus and the host plant.

- Detoxification: Endophytic fungi metabolize organic pollutants
  within plant tissues, reducing phytotoxic effects. Laccase,
  peroxidase, and cytochrome P450 enzymes have been detected in
  endophyte-infected plant tissues growing on contaminated soils.

## ACC Deaminase Activity

A key mechanism by which endophytic fungi [[query-what-are-pgpr-and-how-do-they-promote-plant-growth]] under
stress is the production of 1-aminocyclopropane-1-carboxylate (ACC)
deaminase:

- ACC is the immediate precursor of ethylene in plants. Under stress
  conditions (contaminant exposure, drought, salinity), ethylene
  production increases dramatically, leading to growth inhibition,
  leaf senescence, and ultimately plant death.

- ACC deaminase (EC 4.1.99.4) cleaves ACC to alpha-ketobutyrate and
  ammonia, reducing the ethylene precursor available to the plant and
  thereby lowering stress ethylene levels.
