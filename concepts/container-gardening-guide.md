# Container Gardening Guide

Container gardening grows plants in pots, planters, and other vessels rather than in the ground. It enables gardening on patios, balconies, rooftops, and in areas with poor or contaminated soil. It is a key technique for [[edible-landscaping]] in urban and small-space settings.

## Pot Sizing

Container size directly affects root development, water retention, and yield. Bigger is almost always better for edibles.

### Size Guide by Crop

| Crop | Minimum Pot Size | Ideal Pot Size | Depth |
|------|-----------------|----------------|-------|
| Herbs (basil, thyme, parsley) | 6 inch | 10-12 inch | 8+ inches |
| Lettuce, spinach, Asian greens | 8 inch | 12-14 inch | 6-8 inches |
| Peppers (one plant) | 5 gallon | 10 gallon | 12+ inches |
| Tomatoes (determinate) | 5 gallon | 10 gallon | 12+ inches |
| Tomatoes (indeterminate) | 10 gallon | 15-20 gallon | 18+ inches |
| Cucumbers (bush type) | 5 gallon | 10 gallon | 12+ inches |
| Eggplant | 5 gallon | 10 gallon | 12+ inches |
| Squash (summer, bush) | 5 gallon | 10 gallon | 12+ inches |
| Carrots | 10 inch diameter, 12+ inch deep | 14 inch | 12-14 inches |
| Potatoes | 10 gallon | 15-20 gallon | 15+ inches |
| Strawberries | 6 inch | 12-14 inch | 8+ inches |
| Blueberries | 15 gallon | 20-25 gallon | 18+ inches |

### Key Sizing Principles
- Undersized pots stunt growth, reduce yields, and require constant watering
- Depth matters as much as volume — deep pots support deeper roots
- Taller pots dry out slower than wide, shallow pots (less surface area evaporation)
- Self-watering containers reduce watering frequency significantly

## Soil Mix

Container plants depend entirely on the soil you provide — they cannot reach into the ground for nutrients or moisture.

### Basic Container Mix Recipe
- 1/3 compost (homemade or quality commercial)
- 1/3 coconut coir or peat moss (moisture retention)
- 1/3 perlite, vermiculite, or pumice (drainage and aeration)

### Additions
- 1 cup worm castings per 5 gallons of mix (boosts microbial life)
- 2 tablespoons bone meal per 5 gallons (phosphorus for flowering/fruiting)
- 1 tablespoon kelp meal per 5 gallons ([[dynamic-accumulator-plants-trace-minerals]])
- Mycorrhizal inoculant at planting (especially for peppers and tomatoes)

### Commercial Options
- Quality bagged [[solomon-potting-mix-and-seedling-soil-recipe]] (avoid "garden soil" in bags — too heavy for containers)
- Add perlite if the mix seems too dense
- Replenish with [[coleman-compost-and-organic-fertility-management]] fertilizer each season; replace mix every 2-3 years

## Drainage

Proper drainage is the single most important factor in container gardening success.

### Essential Practices
- Every container must have drainage holes — no exceptions
- Minimum 3-4 holes for a 5-gallon pot; more for larger containers
- Holes should be 1/2 inch diameter minimum
- Elevate pots on pot feet, bricks, or a tray to allow water to escape freely
- Do not add gravel or pot shards at the bottom — this actually reduces drainage (perched water table effect)

### Self-Watering Containers
- Built-in reservoir below the soil chamber
- Soil wicks moisture up from the reservoir via fabric or soil bridge
- Refill reservoir every 3-7 days depending on heat and plant size
- Excellent for tomatoes, peppers, and vacation-prone gardeners
- DIY: Use 5-gallon bucket with perforated inner bucket as soil chamber

## Best Vegetables for Containers

### High Performers
- **Tomatoes**: Choose determinate (bush) types for smaller pots; indeterminate for large containers with trellis support. Cherry tomatoes are prolific in containers.
- **Peppers**: Both sweet and hot peppers thrive in 5-10 gallon pots. Need warm soil and full sun.
- **Lettuce and greens**: [[cut-and-come-again]] varieties provide months of harvest. Shallow roots suit smaller pots. Partial shade extends harvest in hot weather.
- **Radishes**: Ready in 25-30 days. Excellent for filling gaps between slower crops.
- **Beans (bush)**: No trellis needed, produce in 50-60 days. Pole beans need support but produce longer.
- **Cucumbers (bush type)**: Bush cucumbers bred for container culture; full-size types need large pots with trellises.
- **Eggplant**: Needs consistent heat and full sun; 5+ gallon pots.
- **Strawberries**: Grow in [[hamilton-hanging-baskets-organic]], strawberry jars, or regular pots; need acidic soil (pH 5.5-6.5).

### Crops That Struggle in Containers
- Sweet corn (needs blocks of plants for pollination, deep soil)
- Pumpkins and large winter squash (massive root systems and vine spread)
- Watermelon (enormous plants, heavy fruit)
- Asparagus (perennial, needs deep permanent beds)

## Watering

Container gardens dry out much faster than in-ground gardens, especially in summer.

### Watering Guidelines
- **Check daily**: Stick your finger 1-2 inches into soil; if dry, water
- **Water deeply**: Until water runs from drainage holes
- **Water the soil, not the foliage**: Reduces disease pressure
- **Morning watering preferred**: Less evaporation, foliage dries before evening
- **In hot weather**: May need to water twice daily (morning and evening)

### Reducing Watering Frequency
- Use self-watering containers
- Add water-retaining crystals (hydrogel) to soil mix
- Apply 1-2 inches of mulch on the soil surface
- Group containers together to reduce evaporation
- Use larger pots (larger soil volume holds more water)
- Move to shadier spots during heat waves

## Fertilizing

Container soils get depleted faster than garden soil since roots cannot explore beyond the pot.

- **At planting**: Mix slow-release organic fertilizer into soil (fish bone meal, kelp meal)
- **During growth**: Liquid feed every 2-3 weeks with compost tea, fish emulsion, or [[comparison-seaweed-extract-vs-fish-hydrolysate]]
- **Heavy feeders** (tomatoes, peppers, eggplant): More frequent feeding — weekly once fruiting begins
- **Light feeders** (herbs, lettuce): Less frequent — monthly or not at all if soil is rich

## Season Extension

- Move containers to sheltered spots during cold snaps
- Use cloches, row covers, or frost blankets over containers
- Black pots absorb heat and warm soil faster in spring
- Group pots against a south-facing wall for extra warmth
- Bring tender herbs indoors before first frost
