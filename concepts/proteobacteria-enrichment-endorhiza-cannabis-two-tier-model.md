# Proteobacteria Enrichment in the Cannabis Endorhiza: Evidence for the Two-Tier Selection Model

The two-tier selection model of root [[edaphic-factors-cannabis-endorhiza-microbiome-assembly]] predicts a
consistent pattern of taxonomic shifts from bulk soil through the rhizosphere
to the endorhiza (internal root tissues). Specifically, the model predicts
that Acidobacteria — a phylum predominantly associated with oligotrophic
(acidic, low-nutrient) soil conditions — should decline in relative abundance
moving from soil to root, while Proteobacteria — a metabolically versatile
phylum that thrives in nutrient-rich environments — should increase. Winston
et al. (2014) confirmed both predictions in the Cannabis root microbiome.

## The Two-Tier Selection Model

The two-tier selection model proposes that root microbiome assembly proceeds
through two sequential selective filters. The first filter operates at the
soil-to-rhizosphere transition, where root exudates create a carbon-rich
microhabitat that favors copiotrophic (fast-growing, nutrient-responsive)
organisms over oligotrophic ones. The second filter operates at the
rhizosphere-to-endorhiza transition, where plant immune responses and
tissue-specific conditions further restrict community membership.

This model generates specific, testable predictions about taxonomic
composition at each compartment. Because root exudates dramatically increase
carbon availability relative to bulk soil, organisms adapted to low-nutrient
conditions (Acidobacteria) should be progressively filtered out, while
organisms capable of rapid growth on labile carbon (Proteobacteria) should be
progressively enriched. The Cannabis microbiome data provided by Winston et
al. (2014) offered a clear test of these predictions.

## Acidobacteria Decline: The Most Significant Taxonomic Shift

The strongest taxonomic signal in the Winston et al. (2014) dataset was the
decline of Acidobacteria from bulk soil to endorhiza. The Acidobacteria order
iii1-15 showed the most significant decrease of any taxonomic group, with a
p-value of 1.12 x 10^-7. This extraordinarily significant result confirms
that Acidobacteria are actively excluded from the root endosphere, consistent
with their known physiology as oligotrophic organisms adapted to low-nutrient
soil conditions.

Acidobacteria are among the most abundant phyla in most terrestrial soils but
are consistently underrepresented in root-associated communities across plant
species. Their decline in the Cannabis endorhiza mirrors patterns documented
in Arabidopsis, maize, and other model systems, supporting the generality of
the two-tier model. The high statistical significance of the iii1-15 decline
suggests that this filtering is a robust and reproducible feature of Cannabis
microbiome assembly.

## Proteobacteria Enrichment: 17 of 51 Increasing OTUs

Of the 51 OTUs that showed significant increases in relative abundance from
bulk soil to the endorhiza, 17 were classified within the phylum
Proteobacteria. This represents approximately one-third of all
endorhiza-enriched OTUs, making Proteobacteria the single most enriched
phylum in the transition from soil to root interior.

Several of the enriched Proteobacterial OTUs were classified within the order
Rhizobiales, a group that includes well-known plant-associated bacteria such
as Rhizobium, Bradyrhizobium, and Methylobacterium. Rhizobiales are
frequently identified as root endophytes in diverse plant species and are
known for their ability to colonize root tissues intracellularly. Their
enrichment in the Cannabis endorhiza is consistent with established patterns
of root-microbe association.

The remaining Proteobacterial OTUs were distributed across multiple classes
including Alpha-, Beta-, and Gammaproteobacteria, reflecting the broad
metabolic versatility of this phylum. This taxonomic diversity within the
enriched Proteobacteria suggests that multiple independent colonization
strategies may be operating simultaneously, with different Proteobacterial
lineages exploiting different niches or resources within the root.

## Quantifying the Selective Filter: OTU Abundance Correlations

Winston et al. (2014) quantified the strength of selective filtering at each
compartment transition by computing the correlation of mean OTU abundances
between pairs of compartments. The results revealed a progressive weakening
of correlation moving away from the soil source:

Bulk soil vs. rhizosphere: r = 0.92
Rhizosphere vs. endorhiza: r = 0.63
Bulk soil vs. endorhiza: r = 0.42

The very high correlation between bulk soil and rhizosphere (r = 0.92)
indicates that the first selective filter is relatively permissive. The
[[cannabis-rhizosphere-community-structure-edaphic-factors]] is largely a slightly modified version of the bulk soil
community, with most taxa retained at similar relative abundances. Root
exudates enrich certain copiotrophic taxa but do not fundamentally reshape
the community.

The moderate correlation between rhizosphere and endorhiza (r = 0.63)
indicates that the second selective filter is more stringent. Entry into root
tissues imposes additional constraints that substantially restructure
[[core-endorhiza-bacterial-community-composition-cannabis]]. Many rhizosphere taxa cannot or do not colonize the
root interior, and those that do may experience dramatic shifts in relative
abundance due to the very different nutritional and environmental conditions
within root tissues.

The lowest correlation, between bulk soil and endorhiza (r = 0.42), reflects
the cumulative effect of both selective filters. The endorhiza community is
only moderately predictable from soil composition alone, as two rounds of
selection have significantly altered the community from its soil starting
point.

## Context Within Cannabis Microbiome Research

The Proteobacteria enrichment and Acidobacteria decline pattern provides a
framework for understanding how the Cannabis root microbiome is assembled
from [[endorhiza-rhizosphere-bulk-soil-microbial-communities]]. The progressive correlation decline (0.92
to 0.63 to 0.42) quantifies the degree to which each compartment transition
restructures the community, offering a metric that can be compared across
plant species and [[darwin-five-seedling-phototropism-experimental-conditions]].

The identification of Rhizobiales among the enriched Proteobacteria is
particularly noteworthy for Cannabis agriculture. Rhizobiales include
nitrogen-fixing bacteria and other plant growth-promoting organisms, and
their natural enrichment in the Cannabis endorhiza suggests that the plant
may be actively recruiting beneficial partners. This opens possibilities for
soil [[weed-management-strategies]] that promote Rhizobiales populations, potentially
enhancing the beneficial functions they provide.
