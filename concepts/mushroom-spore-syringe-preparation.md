## Overview

A spore syringe is a suspension of mushroom spores in sterile water, loaded into a syringe for easy inoculation of grain jars or agar plates. Spore syringes are the most common starting point for beginner cultivators. Pre-made syringes are widely available from spore vendors (legal in many jurisdictions for microscopy purposes), but many cultivators learn to make their own from spore prints. This skill allows for genetic preservation, strain sharing, and independence from vendors.

## Understanding Spores

Mushroom spores are the microscopic reproductive cells produced by the gills (or pores) of mature fruiting bodies. A single mushroom cap can release millions of spores. When deposited on a growth medium under the right conditions, spores germinate to form haploid mycelium, which eventually mates with compatible hyphae to form dikaryotic mycelium — the actively growing organism that colonizes substrates and produces mushrooms.

Key facts:
- Psilocybe cubensis spores are approximately 11-17 x 7-12 micrometers.
- Spores are extremely durable and can survive for years in cool, dry, dark storage.
- Spores do NOT contain psilocybin or psilocin — the psychoactive compounds are produced by the mycelium and fruiting body.
- Each spore contains a unique genetic complement — germinating spores produces a wide range of genetic variation.

## Making a Spore Print

Before you can make a spore syringe, you need a spore print.

### Materials

- Mature mushroom with open veil (gills fully exposed)
- Clean sheet of paper, aluminum foil, or glass slide (foil is preferred for storage)
- Drinking glass or bowl (to cover the cap)
- Clean scissors or scalpel

### Process

1. **Harvest timing**: The best time to take a print is when the veil has just torn and the gills are fully exposed but the cap has not flattened completely. Earlier = too few spores. Later = spores may be contaminated from the growing environment.
2. **Preparation**: Work in a clean environment. Wipe all surfaces with 70% alcohol. Wash hands thoroughly.
3. **Cut the stem**: Using a flame-sterilized scalpel or clean scissors, cut the stem as close to the cap as possible. Avoid touching the gills with anything.
4. **Place the cap**: Set the cap gills-down on the foil or paper. Cover with the glass or bowl to prevent air currents from disturbing the spores.
5. **Wait**: Leave undisturbed for 6-24 hours. A dense, dark deposit of spores on the foil indicates a good print.
6. **Remove and dry**: Carefully lift the cap. Allow the print to dry in a clean location for several hours. A fan on low speed can speed drying but should not blow directly on the print.
7. **Store**: Fold the foil with the spore print inside, place in a ziplock bag with a desiccant packet, and store in a cool, dark place. Refrigerated spore prints can last 2-5 years.

## Making a Spore Syringe from a Print

### Materials

- Spore print (on foil)
- Sterile water (distilled water, boiled for 10 minutes, or autoclaved)
- Sterile syringe (10-20cc)
- Sterile needle (18-20 gauge)
- Small sterile jar or shot glass
- Butane torch or alcohol lamp
- Isopropyl alcohol (70%)
- Still air box (SAB) or [[laminar-flow-hood]]

### Process

1. **Sterilize the water**: Boil distilled water for 10 minutes. Draw the hot water into the syringe while still hot (this also sterilizes the syringe barrel). Let the syringe cool to room temperature. Alternatively, sterilize a small jar of water in the pressure cooker for 30 minutes.
2. **Prepare the workspace**: Set up the SAB or flow hood. Wipe all surfaces and tools with 70% alcohol.
3. **Open the spore print**: Inside the sterile workspace, unfold the foil with the spore print facing up.
4. **Deposit sterile water**: Using the sterile syringe, carefully squirt a small amount of sterile water (2-3 cc) onto the spore print. Gently scrape the spores with the needle tip to loosen them from the foil.
5. **Draw up the spore solution**: Slowly draw the dark spore-water mixture back into the syringe. You may need to repeat steps 4-5 to pick up more spores. A dark-colored syringe indicates a heavy spore load.
6. **Top off if needed**: If you need more volume, add sterile water from a second sterile syringe or jar.
7. **Cap and store**: Attach the needle cap. Label the syringe with the strain, date, and source. Store at 35-45F (2-7C) in the refrigerator.

### Yield

A single dense spore print can produce 10-50 spore syringes depending on the size and density of the print. A standard 10cc syringe should contain enough spores for 5-10 grain jar inoculations.

## Agar Work from Spore Syringe

Dropping spore solution directly onto agar plates is the preferred method for serious cultivators. This allows you to:

- See germination and select the fastest, most rhizomorphic growth
- Isolate away from contamination (spore prints are never perfectly clean)
- Perform strain isolation for desirable traits (potency, speed, fruiting characteristics)
- Create a master culture that can be expanded indefinitely

### Process

1. Prepare agar plates (see [[agar-work-guide]]).
2. Inside the SAB or flow hood, flame the syringe needle.
3. Squirt a single drop (0.1-0.5 cc) of spore solution onto the agar surface. A single drop can contain thousands of spores.
4. Gently tilt the plate to spread the drop across the surface, or leave it as a concentrated spot.
5. Incubate at 75-78F. Germination typically appears in 3-7 days.
6. Transfer the healthiest growth to fresh plates.

## Spore Syringe Quality

A good spore syringe:
- Appears dark purple to black when held up to light (indicating heavy spore load)
- Contains no visible particles other than spores
- Does not have a foul odor (bacterial contamination)
- Produces visible germination on agar within 3-7 days

A poor spore syringe:
- Appears clear or very light (few spores)
- Contains floating debris or clumps (possible contamination)
- Shows no growth on agar after 10+ days (dead spores or contamination)

## Storage and Viability

- **Refrigerated (35-45F / 2-7C)**: Spore syringes remain viable for 6-12 months, sometimes up to 2 years.
- **Room temperature**: Viability drops significantly after 3-6 months.
- **Frozen**: Not recommended — ice crystal formation damages spores.
- **Light exposure**: Store in the dark. UV light degrades spores over time.

For long-term genetic preservation, agar slants or cultures stored in mineral oil are superior to spore syringes. Spores represent sexual reproduction and introduce genetic variability with each germination — they are not clones of the parent mushroom.

## Legal Status

Spore syringes and spore prints are legal to possess and sell in many jurisdictions because the spores themselves do not contain controlled substances. However, the act of germinating spores with the intent to cultivate psilocybin-containing mushrooms is illegal in many places. Laws vary widely by country, state, and municipality. Always research local regulations before acquiring or using spore materials.
