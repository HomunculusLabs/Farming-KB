## Causal Agents

### OMIV-I and OMIV-II

Two isometric viruses have been identified as causal agents of viral disease in oyster mushrooms:

- **OMIV-I** (Oyster Mushroom Isometric Virus I): An isometric virus particle approximately 30 nm in diameter, containing specific coat proteins and double-stranded RNA (dsRNA) segments that distinguish it from related viruses.
- **OMIV-II** (Oyster Mushroom Isometric Virus II): Also isometric and 30 nm in diameter, OMIV-II shares the same particle size as OMIV-I but differs in its coat protein composition and dsRNA content, indicating it is a distinct viral entity rather than a strain variant.

### Cryptic Virus

In addition to the two pathogenic viruses, a third isometric virus of the same 30 nm size has been found in apparently healthy oyster mushroom tissue. This cryptic virus is considered non-pathogenic and may represent an asymptomatic persistent infection, though its ecological role and potential interactions with pathogenic viruses remain poorly understood. ### Spawn Hygiene

- **Use healthy, virus-free spawn**: All spawn should be prepared from virus-free stock cultures that have been verified through molecular testing or at minimum through culture-based screening for vigor and colony morphology.
- **Maintain clean stock cultures**: Mother cultures and spawn master cultures should be stored separately from production areas and handled with dedicated tools to prevent cross-contamination.
- **Avoid subculturing from infected material**: Never attempt to rescue mycelium from a crop showing viral symptoms for use as spawn stock, even if the mycelium appears partially healthy.
