## Forest Structure: The Seven Layers

Williams describes forests as having seven distinct structural layers, each supporting different ecological functions:

### 1. Canopy Layer (Overstory)
The tallest trees forming the uppermost canopy. In natural forests these are species like Douglas fir and oak. In food forests, the canopy is composed of the tallest fruit and nut trees. The canopy creates the microclimate conditions for all layers below through shade, wind reduction, and moisture modulation.

### 2. Understory Layer (Sub-Canopy)
Smaller versions of overstory trees plus shade-tolerant species such as dogwood or hornbeam. In food forests, this layer contains shorter fruit and nut trees that can thrive in the partial shade of the canopy.

### 3. Shrub Layer
Woody shrubs including gooseberry, coralberry, and highbush cranberry. In food forests, berry-producing shrubs occupy this layer, providing abundant food in a relatively small footprint.

### 4. Herbaceous Layer
The most diverse layer, composed mostly of non-woody species -- grasses, sedges, and wildflowers.
