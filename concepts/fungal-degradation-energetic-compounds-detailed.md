## Fungal Degradation of Energetic Compounds (Explosives) Energetic compounds -- primarily nitroaromatic and nitramine explosives -- represent some of the most persistent and toxic contaminants at military sites worldwide. Fungi, particularly white-rot basidiomycetes, offer unique enzymatic capabilities for degrading these recalcitrant molecules. ## The Contamination Problem Military explosives contamination affects sites globally, with residues persisting from World War II and the Korean conflict through to present-day training ranges. Key contaminants include: - **TNT (2,4,6-trinitrotoluene)**: The most widely used military explosive, with estimated worldwide production of 10 to the 8th power kg per year - **RDX (Royal Demolition eXplosive, cyclotrimethylenetrinitramine)**: A major component of many munitions - **HMX (High Melting eXplosive)**: Used in detonators and plastic explosives - **Dinitrotoluenes**: Byproducts of TNT manufacturing - **Nitroguanidine**: An ingredient in triple-base propellants These compounds resist degradation because increasing nitro-substitution renders the aromatic ring electron-deficient, preventing it from acting as a substrate for electrophilic oxygenation mechanisms that bacteria typically use. ## Why Fungi for Explosive Degradation? The interest in fungal degradation of energetic compounds stems from several unique capabilities: - Non-specific [[staycare-phanerochaete-chrysosporium-in-bioremediation]] can catalyze the oxidation of many xenobiotic compounds, making them candidates for degrading hazardous organonitro explosives.

## TNT Degradation by Phanerochaete chrysosporium

The most extensively studied system is TNT degradation by P. chrysosporium:

- Early screening found that 183 of 190 fungal strains from 98 genera could transform 100 ppm TNT
- Most strains reduce TNT at the 4-nitro position to 4-hydroxylamino-2,6-dinitrotoluene (4HADNT) then to 4-amino-2,6-dinitrotoluene (4ADNT)
- Under nitrogen-limiting conditions (which induce lignolytic enzyme expression), approximately 35% of radiolabelled TNT is mineralized to carbon dioxide
- TNT is inhibitory to spore germination at concentrations greater than 5 ppm
- The well-characterized pathway involves nitro group reduction, oxidation, and aromatic ring cleavage

## RDX Degradation

RDX presents different challenges from TNT. While less studied than TNT degradation, research has shown that certain white-rot fungi can also transform RDX through their ligninolytic enzyme systems. The cyclic nitramine structure of RDX requires different enzymatic approaches than the nitroaromatic TNT.

## HMX and Nitroguanidine

HMX and nitroguanidine have received less research attention than TNT and RDX, but the same ligninolytic enzyme systems that attack nitroaromatics are expected to contribute to their transformation. ## Toxicity Reduction and Intermediate Metabolites

A critical consideration in fungal degradation of energetic compounds is the
fate and toxicity of transformation intermediates. The reductive pathway that
converts TNT to 4ADNT and 2ADNT (amino-dinitrotoluenes) reduces the parent
compound's mutagenicity but the amino metabolites can be more mobile in
groundwater and may form covalent bonds with soil organic matter through
oxidative coupling reactions catalyzed by [[ligninolytic-enzyme-systems-white-rot-fungi]]
expression. Phytoremediation combined with mycoremediation shows particular
promise: plants such as smooth brome and switchgrass grown in contaminated soil
