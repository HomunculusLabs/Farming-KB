# Iboga Root Bark Preparation

## Overview

Tabernanthe iboga is a perennial rainforest shrub native to Gabon, Cameroon, and the Republic of Congo in West-Central Africa. The root bark of iboga contains ibogaine — a complex indole alkaloid with unique psychoactive properties. Unlike classical psychedelics (which primarily act on serotonin receptors), ibogaine acts on multiple receptor systems simultaneously, including NMDA receptors, kappa opioid receptors, and the serotonin transporter.

Iboga has been used for centuries in the Bwiti spiritual tradition of the Fang people of Gabon. In the West, ibogaine has gained attention for its reputed ability to interrupt substance addiction, particularly opioid dependence — though this application remains experimental and unapproved by major medical regulatory bodies.

## The Bwiti Tradition

Bwiti is a syncretic spiritual practice that combines elements of Fang ancestral worship with Christian symbolism. Iboga is the central sacrament:

- Initiation ceremonies involve consuming large doses of iboga root bark
- The experience is understood as a death and rebirth — the initiate "dies" to their old self and is "reborn" into the Bwiti community
- Ceremonies are conducted in a temple (mbandja) by a spiritual leader (nganga)
- Participants see visions of ancestors and receive guidance
- The ceremony involves drumming, singing, and dancing throughout the night
- Iboga is also used in smaller doses for healing, divination, and community rituals

## Active Compounds

### Ibogaine
- Primary psychoactive alkaloid — typically 0.3–1.5% of dry root bark weight
- Complex pharmacology: NMDA antagonist, kappa opioid agonist, serotonin reuptake inhibitor
- Long duration: 12–36 hours for acute effects, with aftereffects lasting days to weeks
- Metabolized in the liver to noribogaine (active metabolite with longer half-life)

### Other Alkaloids
- Ibogamine — lower psychoactivity
- Tabernanthine — related iboga alkaloid
- Voacangine — precursor compound found in Voacanga species
- Iboxygaine, iboluteine — minor alkaloids

## Root Bark Preparation

### Sourcing Root Bark

Iboga root bark (often abbreviated "iboga RB" or "EB" for écorce de racine) is available as:

- Whole root bark pieces — brown, fibrous, wood-like shavings
- Powdered root bark — ground to a fine or coarse powder
- Extracted ibogaine HCl — purified white crystalline powder (synthesized from root bark or from voacangine)

### Raw Root Bark Consumption

Traditional Bwiti practice involves consuming raw or minimally processed root bark:

1. Measure dose by weight (see dosage section below)
2. The bark can be chewed directly, though it is extremely bitter and fibrous
3. More commonly, the bark is powdered and mixed with water into a slurry
4. Some people fill capsules with the powder to avoid the taste
5. Ginger, honey, or fruit juice can help mask the bitterness

### Flood Dose Protocol

A "flood dose" is a large, single dose intended to produce the full visionary experience. This is the traditional initiation approach:

**Preparation Phase (1–2 weeks before):**
- Discontinue all psychoactive substances (critical safety requirement)
- Stop SSRIs at least 2 weeks prior (serotonin syndrome risk)
- Discontinue opioids — withdrawal should be managed before the flood dose
- Light, healthy diet
- Medical screening essential (see Safety section)

**The Experience:**
- Typical flood dose: 15–25 g of powdered root bark (approximately 10–20 mg/kg ibogaine)
- Ingested on an empty stomach after an overnight fast
- Onset: 1–3 hours
- Peak: 3–8 hours
- Acute effects: 12–36 hours
- Ataxia (loss of motor coordination) is universal and intense — lying down is mandatory
- Visual experiences often involve reviewing one's life in detail
- Nausea and vomiting are common and expected

**Aftercare:**
- 24–72 hours of rest after the experience
- Noribogaine metabolite continues acting for days to weeks
- Integration support is important
- Light diet, no alcohol or drugs

### Microdosing / Boosting

Lower, more frequent doses used for addiction interruption:

- "Booster" doses: 500 mg – 2 g of root bark, taken every few days
- Typically follows a flood dose to extend the anti-addictive effects
- Much milder experience — no significant visionary effects
- Used for 1–6 months after a flood dose

## Extraction Methods

### Basic Ibogaine Extraction (Overview)

This is a simplified overview of the general process — detailed procedures are available from specialized sources:

1. Root bark is powdered and extracted with a weak acid solution
2. The acidic solution is basified with sodium hydroxide to precipitate alkaloids
3. The precipitate is collected and further purified
4. Multiple purification steps yield ibogaine hydrochloride (HCl) as a white powder
5. This is a complex chemistry procedure requiring laboratory equipment and knowledge

### Voacanga as a Source

Voacanga africana seeds contain voacangine, which can be chemically converted to ibogaine. This is the industrial source for most purified ibogaine HCl on the market.

## Safety Screening

Ibogaine carries significant medical risks and requires thorough screening:

**Absolute contraindications:**
- History of heart arrhythmia, prolonged QT interval, or recent cardiac events
- Active substance use (especially SSRIs, opioids, stimulants)
- Known liver disease or significantly impaired liver function
- Psychosis or active schizophrenia
- Pregnancy

**Required screening:**
- ECG (electrocardiogram) — QTc interval must be within normal range
- Liver function tests (AST, ALT, bilirubin)
- Complete blood count
- Blood pressure and heart rate monitoring
- Medical history review
- Psychological screening

**During the experience:**
- Continuous cardiac monitoring is strongly recommended
- A trained medical attendant should be present
- Blood pressure and heart rate should be checked regularly
- Emergency medical equipment should be available

Ibogaine has been associated with cardiac fatalities — the primary risk mechanism is prolongation of the QT interval leading to torsades de pointes, a potentially fatal arrhythmia.

## Legal Status

Ibogaine is classified as a Schedule I substance in the United States. It is illegal in several other countries but unscheduled or available for medical use in others (e.g., Brazil, New Zealand has limited medical provisions).
