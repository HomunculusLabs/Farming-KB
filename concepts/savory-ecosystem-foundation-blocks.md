## Water Cycle

The water cycle determines how effectively precipitation is converted
into usable soil moisture. In arid and semiarid regions, Savory
observed that typically only five or six inches out of fourteen received
were actually effective, meaning they soaked in and became available to
plants and soil organisms. The rest ran off or evaporated.

An effective water cycle directs most water either up through plants
to the atmosphere or down to underground supplies. A noneffective cycle
produces flooding, erosion, and rapid evaporation. The effectiveness
of the water cycle depends primarily on the condition of the soil
surface: its cover, porosity, crumb structure, and organic content.

## Mineral Cycle

A good mineral cycle implies a biologically active living soil with
good aeration and sufficient energy to sustain abundant organisms in
continuous contact with atmospheric nitrogen, oxygen, and carbon.
These organisms require energy derived from sunlight but obtain it
indirectly through decomposing plant and animal residues.
