# Seed Production and Storage

Seed production requires careful planning, selection of parent plants, and
proper pollination techniques. Understanding the genetics of sex determination
is essential for producing feminized seeds. Proper storage ensures seed
viability for years or even decades. For broader propagation methods see
[[vegetative-propagation-methods-mollison]] and [[tissue-culture-cloning]].

## Seed Production Basics

Any method where pollen comes into contact with a pistil will result in seeds.
For small-scale seed production, individual pollen sacs can be collected from
male plants and applied selectively to specific branches using a small brush.
For larger scale production, breeders place multiple males (or clones from a
single father donor plant) in the seed production grow room with chosen females.
Placing these males in a well-ventilated room and allowing full release of
pollen ensures the crop will be completely pollinated and produces a vast amount
of seeds per plant.

## Feminized Seed Production

All-female (gynoecious) seeds are produced by obtaining pollen from one female
individual and subsequently fertilizing another female plant. This exploits the
fact that female cannabis plants have two X chromosomes (XX genotype), while
males have XY. A female-to-female cross produces only XX (female) offspring.
See also [[autoflowering-breeding]] for related breeding approaches.

The process involves chemically inducing male flower development on a female
plant by applying ethylene-inhibiting agents (such as silver thiosulfate or
colloidal silver) as the plant enters flowering. This causes the female to
produce pollen sacs containing only X chromosomes. This pollen is collected and
used to fertilize another female.

Some seed companies produce feminized seeds by collecting pollen from carefully
selected, latent, stress-induced hermaphrodites. However, using pollen from
intersex plants selects for intersexuality in progeny -- like begets like.
Progeny from intersex pollen parents will often show intersex tendencies under
the same stresses that caused intersexuality in the pollen parent.

The ethylene hormone regulates sex determination in cannabis. Applying high
concentrations of ethylene to male plants in flowering causes pistil formation.
Conversely, applying ethylene-inhibiting agents to female plants entering
flowering results in stamen formation in place of pistils.

## Seed Crop Care

Seed production plants benefit from a complete balanced diet throughout the seed
gestation period, unlike sinsemilla crops that use high-phosphorus, low-nitrogen
flowering formulas. Many cannabis-specific flowering fertilizers are low on
nitrogen, which may not support proper seed development. Growers may combine
vegetative and flowering fertilizers to ensure all nutrients required for proper
seed development are available.

Complete, balanced, organic-based soil mixes produce the most healthy, viable
seeds. Organic soils contain various bacterial populations that break down and
digest amendments to make them usable by plants. These organic bacterial
populations provide some benefit to plant health and thus produce more mature,
healthy, viable seeds.

## Seed Maturation and Harvest

Seeds are ready to plant immediately after harvest, but initial germination
rates may be low. Germination rates increase by drying seeds post-harvest,
leaving them in a cool, dark, well-ventilated area for a few weeks, then placing
them in the refrigerator for one or two months before sprouting. This after-
ripening period allows the seed to fully mature and enter dormancy before
germination.

Seeds should be harvested when fully mature -- dark in color with hard shells
and visible striping or mottling. Immature green or white seeds have low
viability.

## Long-Term Seed Storage

Proper seed storage is critical for maintaining genetic lines. Seeds should be
stored in a cool, dark, dry environment. A refrigerator maintains humidity
levels below five percent and temperatures around 40°F (4°C), ideal for medium-
term storage (1-5 years). For long-term storage (5-20+ years), freezing at 0°F
(-18°C) in airtight, moisture-proof containers with desiccant packets is
recommended.

Seeds must be thoroughly dry before freezing. Moisture content below 8% prevents
ice crystal formation that can damage cellular structures. Vacuum-sealing seeds
in moisture-proof bags before refrigeration or freezing extends viability
significantly.

## Genetic Considerations

Cannabis inherits 10 chromosomes from the pollen parent and 10 from the seed
mother, for a total of 20 chromosomes (2 full genomes). Every cell in the plant
carries a copy of this unique DNA. Inbred lines for hybrid seed production are
developed through repeated self-pollination or sibling crosses over many
generations to fix desirable traits. Hybrid seed production has benefits for
breeders: by producing F1 hybrids of their pure lines, seed banks ensure
uniform, vigorous offspring that outperform either parent (hybrid vigor).
Related techniques for plant training and management include [[topping-cannabis]],
[[supercropping]], and [[training-cannabis]].

## Pollen Collection and Storage Techniques

Collecting and preserving viable pollen is essential for controlled breeding
programmes, allowing breeders to make specific crosses without maintaining live
male plants year-round. The timing and technique of pollen collection
significantly affect its viability and the success rate of subsequent
pollination. Male cannabis plants typically begin releasing pollen 2–4 weeks
after the onset of flowering, when pollen sacs (anthers) swell, change colour
from green to pale yellow, and begin to open. The optimal collection window is
brief — pollen is most viable when collected from sacs that have just begun to
open but have not yet fully dehisced, as fully open sacs may have already
released much of their pollen into the air.

Several collection methods are commonly employed. The simplest involves placing
a dry, clean container (such as a glass jar or paper bag) beneath opening pollen
sacs and gently tapping or shaking the branch to release pollen. More controlled
methods involve cutting entire branches with mature pollen sacs and suspending
them over a clean collection surface — typically a sheet of glass, parchment
paper, or a mirror — inside a dry, still environment. Over 24–48 hours, the sacs
will dehisce and release pollen onto the collection surface. The pollen can then
be carefully transferred to a storage container using a small brush or by
tapping the collection surface. Some breeders place a fine mesh screen between
the branch and the collection surface to filter out plant debris and anther
fragments, producing cleaner pollen.

Moisture is the primary enemy of pollen viability. Freshly collected pollen
contains residual moisture that must be removed before storage. The standard
drying procedure involves spreading the collected pollen in a thin layer on a
non-stick surface and allowing it to air-dry in a low-humidity environment
(below 30% relative humidity) for 24–48 hours. A desiccant chamber — a sealed
container with silica gel packets or indicating desiccant — provides more
controlled drying. Once dry, pollen should feel like a fine, free-flowing powder
with no clumping. Over-drying should be avoided, as excessively desiccated
pollen may lose viability more rapidly than pollen dried to an optimal
intermediate moisture content.
