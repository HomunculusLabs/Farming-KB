## Seed Production and Storage

Seed production requires careful planning, selection of parent plants, and
proper pollination techniques. Understanding the genetics of [[vegetative-propagation-methods-mollison]] see

[[maitake-fruiting-body-success-rate-management]] of subsequent
pollination. Male cannabis plants typically begin releasing pollen 2–4 weeks
after the onset of flowering, when pollen sacs (anthers) swell, change colour
from green to pale yellow, and begin to open. The optimal collection window is
brief — pollen is most viable when collected from sacs that have just begun to
open but have not yet fully dehisced, as fully open sacs may have already
released much of their pollen into the air.

Several collection methods are commonly employed. The simplest involves placing
a dry, clean container (such as a glass jar or paper bag) beneath opening pollen
sacs and gently tapping or shaking the branch to release pollen. More controlled
methods involve cutting entire branches with mature pollen sacs and suspending
them over a clean collection surface — typically a sheet of glass, parchment
paper, or a mirror — inside a dry, still environment. Over 24–48 hours, the sacs
will dehisce and release pollen onto the collection surface. The pollen can then
be carefully transferred to a storage container using a small brush or by
tapping the collection surface. Some breeders place a fine mesh screen between
the branch and the collection surface to filter out plant debris and anther
fragments, producing cleaner pollen.

Moisture is the primary enemy of pollen viability. Freshly collected pollen
contains residual moisture that must be removed before storage. The standard
drying procedure involves spreading the collected pollen in a thin layer on a
non-stick surface and allowing it to air-dry in a low-humidity environment
(below 30% relative humidity) for 24–48 hours. A desiccant chamber — a sealed
container with silica gel packets or indicating desiccant — provides more
controlled drying. Once dry, pollen should feel like a fine, free-flowing powder
with no clumping. Over-drying should be avoided, as excessively desiccated
pollen may lose viability more rapidly than pollen dried to an optimal
intermediate moisture content.
