# Mushroom Compost Phase I and Phase II for Agaricus Cultivation

Agaricus species including button mushrooms, portobellos, and creminis require
composted substrates rather than raw or pasteurized materials. The composting
process transforms raw organic ingredients into a selectively nutritive medium
that favors Agaricus mycelium while being inhospitable to most competitors.
This two-phase process is fundamental to commercial [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]].

### The Composting Process

1. **Mixing**: Ingredients are blended thoroughly using mechanical turners
2. **Building the pile**: Compost is piled in windrows, 4 to 6 feet high
3. **Turning schedule**: Piles are turned every 2 to 3 days to reintroduce
   oxygen and redistribute moisture throughout the pile
4. **Temperature management**: Internal temperatures reach 160 to 180 degrees
   Fahrenheit driven by microbial metabolism
5. **Ammonia generation**: Microbial decomposition of proteins releases ammonia
   that accumulates in the compost matrix

### Phase I Completion Indicators

Phase I is complete when the compost is dark brown to chocolate in color with
a soft pliable texture. Straw fibers should break easily. Nitrogen content
should measure 1.5 to 2.0% on a dry weight basis with moisture at 68 to 72%.
Strong ammonia presence with a pH of 7.5 to 8.0 should be detectable.

## Phase II: Pasteurization and Conditioning

Phase II takes place indoors in a controlled environment such as composting
tunnels or growing rooms. It serves two critical functions: killing remaining
pests and pathogens through pasteurization, and converting ammonia into protein
through controlled microbial activity known as conditioning.

### Pasteurization Stage

The compost is filled into beds, trays, or bulk tunnels and subjected to
140 degrees Fahrenheit using steam injection for 8 to 12 hours. Fresh air is
introduced to supply oxygen and remove excess carbon dioxide. This pasteurization
kills nematodes, insect eggs, and competitive fungi that survived Phase I.
