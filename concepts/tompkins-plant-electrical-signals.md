## The Mimosa Reflex Arc

Bose demonstrated that the mimosa plant exhibited a "reflex arc"
identical in principle to the human withdrawal reflex from a hot stove.
When he electrically shocked the stem, the base of the nearest petiole
collapsed within seconds, followed by the folding of the leaves. An
electrical disturbance traveled between points of reaction -- the same
principle as an animal nerve carrying an impulse to a muscle.

Bose showed that the isolated vegetal nerve is "indistinguishable from
animal nerve." He maintained that "the discovery of a given responsive
characteristic in one case has proved a sure guide to its observation in
the other."

## Death and Electrical Discharge

Bose invented a morograph (death recorder) to determine the exact
temperature at which death occurred. Many plants died at 60 degrees
Centigrade, with individual variations depending on history and age. At
death, the plant "threw off a huge electrical force." Five hundred
green peas could develop five hundred volts, said Bose, "enough to
fulminate a cook but for the fact that peas are seldom connected in
series."

Plants could be anesthetized with chloroform just like animals, and
revived when the vapor was blown away. They became intoxicated with
whiskey or gin, swayed like drunkards, passed out, and revived with
signs of a hangover.

## Reception and Legacy

Bose's findings were received with "veiled hostility" by botanists.
Nature magazine wrote that his work "would be recommended as of great
value if it did not continually arouse our incredulity." The reviewer
was "limited by the compartmentalized science of his day" and had "no
way of knowing he was dealing with a genius half a century ahead of his
time."
