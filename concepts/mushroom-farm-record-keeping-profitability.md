# Mushroom Farm Record Keeping and Profitability

Step 20 of the [[goose-keeping-detailed]] financial records throughout the farming process. Without accurate
record keeping, a farmer cannot know whether the operation is truly profitable or
where improvements can be made.

## Why Keep Records

There are three primary reasons to maintain thorough records:

1. **Verify profit margins** — Know exactly how much each bag costs to produce and
   what revenue it generates. Without this, you may believe you are making money
   when you are actually losing it.

2. **Identify where to reduce expenses without reducing quality** — Records reveal
   which inputs are the most costly and whether cheaper alternatives exist that
   would not compromise [[ubon-ratchathani]] between
February and October 2000:

- **Average yield:** 250g per bag over a 3-month fruiting period
- **Average [[comparison-chaga-vs-oyster-mushroom]] price:** 25 Baht/kg
- **Total income per bag:** 6.25 Baht over the 3-month fruiting cycle

Profit depends on whether the farmer buys ready-made spawn bags or produces them:

- **Buying spawn bags** at 3.75 Baht each → Profit of approximately **2.50 Baht
  per bag**
- **Making own bags** at approximately 2.25 Baht each → Profit of approximately
  **4.00 Baht per bag**

## Key Cost Justifications (Annex 5)

The following calculations support the per-bag material costs:

- **Sawdust:** 982.39 Baht/ton. Every 100 kg produces ~130 bags = 0.756 Baht/bag.
- **PP Bags:** 47 Baht/kg. ~200 bags per kg = 0.235 Baht/bag.
- **Rice bran:** 5 Baht/kg. Standard formula uses 6 kg per 100 kg sawdust.
- **Spawn:** 5 Baht/bottle. Each bottle inoculates ~30 bags = 0.16 Baht/bag.
- **Fuel:** Corn cob 900 Baht/ton + wood 2,000 Baht/ton. Across 11,216 total bags
  = 0.259 Baht/bag.
