## Overview

Endophytic fungi are microorganisms that live within plant tissues without causing visible disease symptoms. Among the most ecologically significant endophytes are the **clavicipitaceous fungi** that form symbiotic associations with grasses, including many of the world"s most important forage and turf grasses. These fungi—particularly species of *Epichloë* (asexual Neotyphodium)—produce a remarkable array of **alkaloid compounds** that protect their host plants from herbivorous insects, grazing mammals, and even other fungi. In exchange, the fungus receives shelter, nutrition, and vertical transmission through the host's seeds. This mutualistic relationship represents one of the most widespread and economically important plant-fungal symbioses on Earth, affecting livestock management, pasture ecology, and the turfgrass industry.

## The Endophytic Lifestyle

Endophytes differ from pathogens in their relationship with the host: while pathogens extract nutrients at the expense of plant health, endophytes maintain a balanced coexistence. The fungus grows **intercellularly**—between the cells of the host plant's leaf sheaths, stems, and sometimes inflorescences—without penetrating the cells themselves. In grasses infected with *Epichloë* and its asexual relatives, the fungal mycelium is distributed throughout the above-ground tissues, forming a systemic infection that persists for the lifetime of the plant.

### Transmission Strategies

Endophytic grass fungi employ two distinct transmission strategies:

1. **Vertical transmission**: The fungus grows into the developing seeds of the host grass, ensuring that every seedling inherits the endophyte. This maternal inheritance is highly efficient and maintains the symbiosis across generations without requiring external infection. Virtually 100% of seeds from an infected mother plant carry the fungus.
2. **Horizontal transmission**: In sexual *Epichloë* species (the "choke" fungi), the fungus eventually erupts from the plant"s inflorescence, forming a stroma that envelops the developing seed head—literally "choking" the grass"s reproduction. Flies visit the stroma, transfer gametes between fungal individuals, and subsequently disperse fungal spores to uninfected grass plants. This strategy sacrifices the host's current reproductive output but allows the fungus to colonize new hosts.

The existence of both strategies within the same genus (*Epichloë*) illustrates a fundamental evolutionary trade-off: vertical transmission guarantees perpetuation but limits genetic diversity, while horizontal transmission promotes genetic recombination but risks the endophyte being lost if the host is not successfully infected.

## The Chemical Arsenal: Ergot Alkaloids and Lolines

The ecological significance of grass endophytes lies primarily in the **bioactive alkaloids** they produce. These compounds are synthesized by the fungus within the plant tissues and accumulate to concentrations sufficient to deter or poison herbivores.

### Ergot Alkaloids

Endophytic fungi produce a range of **ergopeptine alkaloids** related to those found in *[[savory-livestock-grazing-planning]].

### Lolitrem B

Lolitrem B is a **tremorgenic indole-diterpene** alkaloid responsible for **ryegrass staggers** in sheep and cattle. It causes a neurological disorder characterized by tremors, ataxia, and staggering gait, particularly when animals are excited or stressed. The compound acts on GABA receptors in the mammalian brain, disrupting inhibitory neurotransmission.

## Fescue Toxicosis: The Double-Edged Sword

The most economically significant consequence of grass endophytes is **fescue toxicosis**, caused by ergovaline produced by *Epichloë coenophiala* (formerly *Neotyphodium coenophialum*) infecting tall fescue (*Festuca arundinacea*). Tall fescue is the most widely planted cool-season forage grass in the United States, covering approximately 35 million acres. An estimated 80–90% of this acreage is infected with the endophyte.

### Symptoms

Fescue toxicosis manifests in three overlapping syndromes:

1. **Fescue foot**: Ergovaline-induced vasoconstriction reduces blood flow to the extremities, causing tissue necrosis, sloughing of hooves and tail tips, and gangrene in severe cases
2. **Summer slump**: Reduced feed intake, weight loss, poor milk production, and elevated body temperature during hot weather, as the vasoconstrictive effects of ergovaline impair the animal's ability to dissipate heat
3. **Reproductive problems**: Reduced conception rates, prolonged gestation, thickened placentas, agalactia (failure to produce milk), and increased calf mortality

The economic impact of fescue toxicosis in the United States alone has been estimated at over **$1 billion annually** in lost livestock productivity.

### The Agronomic Paradox

The paradox of fescue toxicosis is that the same endophyte that causes livestock losses also confers enormous agronomic benefits. Endophyte-infected tall fescue is:

- More drought-tolerant than uninfected grass
- More resistant to insect pests and nematodes
- More competitive against weed species
- More tolerant of heavy grazing
- More persistent under poor soil conditions

Removing the endophyte (by planting endophyte-free seed) eliminates fescue toxicosis but also eliminates these benefits, often resulting in stand failure within 2–3 years. The agricultural industry has responded by developing **novel endophytes**—strains of *Epichloë* that produce peramine and lolines (for insect resistance) but not ergovaline or lolitrem B (the mammalian toxins). These "friendly" endophytes offer the agronomic benefits of the wild-type symbiosis without the toxicity to livestock.
