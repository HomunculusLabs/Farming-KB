## Perennial Vegetables

Perennial vegetables produce harvests year after year without replanting. They reduce soil disturbance, build [[fukuoka-soil-building-method]] organic matter over time, and are central to [[food-forest-design]] and [[permaculture-design]] systems. Once established, they provide reliable yields with minimal annual labor.

## Asparagus (Asparagus officinalis)

The king of perennial vegetables, asparagus can produce for 15-25 years from a single planting.

### Planting
- **Varieties**: Jersey series (all-male, high yield), Mary Washington (heirloom), Purple Passion
- **Timing**: Plant dormant 1-year crowns in early spring as soon as soil is workable
- **Site**: Full sun, well-drained soil (pH 6.5-7.5); asparagus hates wet feet
- **Bed preparation**: Dig trenches 12 inches deep, incorporate compost and rock phosphate
- **Spacing**: 12-18 inches apart in rows 4-5 feet apart

### Care and Harvest
- Do not harvest in the first year; harvest lightly (2-3 weeks) in year two
- Full harvest from year three: cut spears at ground level when 6-8 inches tall
- Stop harvesting when spears are pencil-thin (usually mid-June)
- Let ferns grow through summer to build crown energy
- Cut ferns to ground after frost, mulch heavily for winter
- Fertilize with compost or balanced [[complete-organic-fertilizer]] each spring

## Rhubarb (Rheum rhabarbarum)

A hardy perennial grown for its tart leaf stalks. Technically a vegetable used as a fruit.

### Planting
- **Varieties**: Victoria, Canada Red, Chipman's (red-stalked varieties are sweeter)
- **Timing**: Plant dormant crowns in early spring or divisions in fall
- **Site**: Full sun to part shade, deep, fertile soil
- **Spacing**: 3-4 feet apart

### Care and Harvest
- Do not harvest in the first year after planting
- From year two, pull stalks (do not cut) from the base when they reach 12-18 inches
- Never eat the leaves — they contain toxic oxalic acid
- Remove flower stalks immediately to direct energy to leaf production
- Divide every 5-7 years to maintain vigor
- Mulch in fall; hardy to -40F

## Globe Artichokes (Cynara scolymus)

Perennial in zones 7-10; grown as annuals in colder zones. Related to thistles.

### Planting
- **Varieties**: Green Globe, Imperial Star (annual-friendly), Purple of Romagna
- **Timing**: Start indoors 8-12 weeks before last frost; transplant after danger passes
- **Site**: Full sun, rich well-drained soil, protected from strong winds
- **Spacing**: 3-4 feet apart

### Care and Harvest
- Harvest flower buds before they open, when tight and 3-5 inches across
- Cut stem 1-2 inches below the bud
- Side buds will form after main bud is harvested
- In cold zones: cut to ground after frost, mulch heavily (6-12 inches), or lift and store like dahlias
- Feed heavily with compost and nitrogen-rich amendments

## Sorrel (Rumex spp.)

Tangy, lemon-flavored greens that emerge early in spring and persist through fall.

### Varieties
- **French sorrel (Rumex scutatus)**: Shield-shaped leaves, milder flavor, preferred for culinary use
- **Garden sorrel ([[rumex-acetosa]])**: Larger leaves, more tart, very vigorous
- **Red-veined sorrel (Rumex sanguineus)**: Ornamental and edible, milder flavor

### Planting and Care
- Direct sow in spring or early fall, or transplant seedlings
- Prefers partial shade and moist, fertile soil
- Space 12-18 inches apart
- Harvest outer leaves continuously; remove flower stalks to extend leaf production
- Divide every 3-4 years
- Hardy and long-lived once established

## Walking Onions (Allium proliferum)

Also called Egyptian onions or tree onions. They reproduce by forming bulbils at the top of the stalk that weigh it down, "walking" to new positions.

### Planting and Care
- Plant bulbils or bulblets in fall or early spring, 1-2 inches deep, 6-8 inches apart
- Full sun to part shade, any well-drained soil
- Use bulbils as green onions, or let them mature into small bulb onions
- The underground bulbs multiply and can be harvested like shallots
- Virtually indestructible — will naturalize if allowed
- Harvest bulbils in summer when tops topple; plant immediately or store cool and dry

## Additional Perennial Vegetables

- **Sea kale ([[crambe-maritima]])**: Blanched shoots in spring, zone 4-8
- **Good King Henry (Chenopodium bonus-henricus)**: Spinach-like greens, zone 3-8
- **Skirret (Sium sisarum)**: Sweet, parsnip-like roots, zone 4-8
- **[[comparison-jerusalem-artichoke-vs-potato]] ([[helianthus-tuberosus]])**: Productive but invasive — confine with barriers
- **Stinging nettle (Urtica dioica)**: Nutritious spring greens (harvest with gloves), tea plant
- **Hostas**: Young shoots are edible, similar to asparagus

## Design Considerations

- Place perennial vegetables in dedicated beds separate from annual crop rotation areas
- Combine with [[edible-landscaping]] in ornamental borders
- Use as understory plants in [[food-forest-design]] systems
- Plan for long-term soil building — these beds accumulate organic matter over decades
