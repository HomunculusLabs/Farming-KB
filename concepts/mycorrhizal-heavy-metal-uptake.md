## Radionuclide Transport

### Radiocesium (137Cs)

Mycorrhizal fungi play a significant role in radiocesium dynamics in forest ecosystems:

- About 22% of 137Cs bound by fungal mycelia is in the top 5 cm of coniferous forest soil
- Major fractions of 137Cs in Swedish forests are associated with fungal components
- Horizontal transport occurs through actively expanding mycelia (Schizophyllum commune) via bulk flow
- Plants inoculated with specific mycorrhizal fungi show increased radiocesium uptake, useful for phytoextraction
- Compartment models can quantify radiocesium transport in soil-plant-fungal systems

### Transport Mechanisms

Radiocesium translocation occurs through:
- Bulk flow in actively expanding mycelia
- Diffusion in established mycelial networks
- Cytoplasmic streaming within hyphae
- These mechanisms enable both accumulation and redistribution of radionuclides

## Genetics of Metal Tolerance

No field studies have been conducted on the genetics of ECM fungal adaptation to contaminated soils. Key findings from laboratory studies:

- Pisolithus tinctorius isolates from contaminated coal mines show varied Al tolerance (EC50: 0.2-74 mM) vs. wild-type (0.04-0.07 mM)
- Genetic variation in Suillus luteus populations is larger at polluted sites
- Oidiodendron maius isolates from polluted soils group in different genetic clusters
- Screening of sensitive and insensitive genotypes from mine and wild-type populations is needed
