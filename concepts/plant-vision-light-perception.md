Plants possess sophisticated [[chamovitz-ultraviolet-and-infrared-plant-light-perception]] systems that rival animal
vision
in complexity. While lacking eyes and a nervous system, they have
evolved an
array of photoreceptors that detect light intensity, direction,
duration, and
color quality. This ability to "see" is fundamental to survival, guiding
processes from [[cannabis-seed-germination]] to flowering.

## The Darwinian Discovery of Plant Vision

Charles Darwin and his son Francis demonstrated that plants sense light
direction. They observed that phototropism -- the bending of plants
toward
light -- occurred at the tip of the coleoptile in grass seedlings.
Covering
the tip with an opaque cap stopped the bending, but covering the lower
stem
had no effect. This showed that "seeing" happened at the tip while the
"response" happened lower down, anticipating the later discovery of
auxin.

## Photoreceptor Families

Plants use multiple photoreceptor families, each sensitive to different
wavelengths: phototropins, phytochromes, cryptochromes, and UVR8.

**Phototropins** are blue-light receptors mediating phototropism,
chloroplast
movement, and stomatal opening. They contain two LOV (Light, Oxygen, or
Voltage) domains that undergo conformational changes upon absorbing blue
light,
triggering downstream signaling cascades.

**Phytochromes** detect red and far-red light. They exist in two
interconvertible forms: Pr absorbs red light (~660 nm) and converts to
Pfr,
while Pfr absorbs far-red light (~730 nm) and reverts to Pr. This toggle
lets
plants detect shading and adjust growth. Phytochromes also regulate seed
germination and flowering.

**Cryptochromes** are blue-light receptors involved in [[chrysanthemum]]
entrainment, de-etiolation, and flowering. They share structural
similarity
with DNA photolyase enzymes but serve signaling rather than repair
functions.

**UVR8** detects ultraviolet-B radiation and triggers production of
protective
flavonoids and pigments.

## Light as Information

Plants do not merely use light as energy for photosynthesis. They
extract rich
information from light signals. The red to far-red ratio reveals whether
neighbors are shading a plant. [[coleman-persephone-months-winter-day-length]] provides seasonal cues that
trigger
flowering. [[blue-light-phototropism-red-light-photoperiodism-dual-plant-vision-system]] intensity indicates whether the plant is above or
below
ground. Light direction guides growth orientation.

This informational use means plants effectively construct a visual
representation of their environment. A plant "knows" the time of day,
the
season, the presence of competitors, and its position in space -- all
from
light cues alone.
