## The Japanese Bantam vs. White Leghorn

Fukuoka uses a compelling comparison between two chicken production
systems to illustrate the difference between natural and artificial
approaches to livestock.

The Japanese Bantam is a native breed that lays just one small egg
every other day when left to roam freely. By conventional productivity
metrics, this is low output. But take a breeding pair, let them nest
every so often, and within a year the original pair grows to a flock
of ten or twenty birds that together lay many times as many eggs each
day as the best variety of White Leghorn.

The Bantams are efficient calorie producers because they feed
themselves and lay eggs on their own — literally producing something
from nothing. As long as the flock size remains appropriate for the
available space, raising chickens this way does not harm the land.

By contrast, genetically-upgraded White Leghorns raised in cages lay
one large egg a day. To achieve this, they must be given feed grain
having **twice the caloric value of the eggs produced**. Such artificial
methods are basically counterproductive — instead of increasing
calories, they actually cut the number in half.
