## Ladino Clover: Features and Management

### Six Key Advantages

1. When used as a cover crop, this eliminates weeds.
   Annual weeds are displaced in one year, and biennials
   disappear in two years. After two to three years, almost
   all garden weeds have vanished.
2. Improves soil down to a depth of sixteen to eighteen
   inches.
3. Seed does not have to be sown again for another six to
   eight years.
4. Does not compete strongly with fruit trees for fertilizer
   or moisture.
5. Grows back easily after being cut, and remains healthy and
   hardy even when trampled upon.
6. Does not hinder farming operations.

### Seeding and Management

The seed should be drilled the first autumn. Delayed seeding
invites insect damage. Seeds should not be covered with soil
as this often hampers germination; merely firm the soil after
drilling.

Clover does not choke out other vegetation but gradually
becomes dominant by growing so thickly as to prevent the
germination of other weeds. If, after the clover takes well
and flourishes, it is left alone, it becomes excessively
luxuriant, leaving it open to attack by diseases such as leaf
spot and the reemergence of weeds in five to six years.

Areas where perennial weeds such as sorrel and dandelion, or
twining plants such as bindweed grow in abundance should be
cut more frequently than other places, and wood ashes or coal
ashes scattered.
