## Identifying the Amanita Family

The three universal distinguishing marks that Palmer emphasized for public safety:

1. **Volva (wrapper)** at the base of the stem — a cup-like sheath from which the young plant emerges
2. **Ring (annulus)** around the stem, about two-thirds up from the ground
3. **Scurfy top** with warts or scales that rub off easily, leaving the smooth skin intact

Additional features: pure white gills and stem, variable cap color (white, yellow, red, green, drab), soft and sticky when moist. The most common variety near Boston had a yellow central spot shading to white at the margin.

## Historical Context

Palmer noted that this poison has been known since the days of the Roman Empire. The Emperor Claudius was reportedly poisoned by a boletus, and Czar Nicholas allegedly died from Amanita muscarius. Despite this long history, no certain antidote existed until the late 19th century experiments with atropine.
