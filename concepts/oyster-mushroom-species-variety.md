# Oyster Mushroom Species and Varieties

The genus Pleurotus encompasses over 30 described species of gilled mushrooms,
commonly known as oyster mushrooms for their shelf-like growth pattern resembling
oyster shells. Several species within this genus are commercially cultivated
worldwide, each with distinct characteristics in appearance, flavor, growth
requirements, and substrate preferences. Understanding these differences is
essential for cultivators selecting appropriate species for their conditions and
markets. For cultivation techniques and growing parameters by species, see [[stamets-pleurotus-oyster-cultivation]].

## Pleurotus ostreatus

The classic oyster mushroom and the most widely cultivated species globally.
Fruiting bodies have smooth, fan-shaped caps ranging from 5 to 25 centimeters
across. Color varies by strain from pale grey to dark blue-grey, with some
strains browning with age. The flesh is white, firm, and mild in flavor with a
subtle anise aroma.

Pleurotus ostreatus is a cold-tolerant species that fruits optimally at 12 to 18
degrees Celsius. It can fruit at temperatures as low as 5 degrees C, making it
suitable for cultivation in cool climates and during winter months in temperate
regions. It colonizes a wide range of substrates including straw, sawdust, paper,
and agricultural residues. Biological efficiency on supplemented straw typically
ranges from 75 to 150 percent.

## Pleurotus pulmonarius

Known as the Phoenix oyster or Indian oyster, this species is more heat-tolerant
than P. ostreatus, fruiting well at 18 to 28 degrees C. It has a faster
colonization rate and shorter cropping cycle, often producing the first harvest
within 14 to 18 days of spawning. Caps are generally smaller and thinner than P.
ostreatus, with a white to pale brown coloration.

P. pulmonarius is popular in tropical and subtropical regions where warm
temperatures make P. ostreatus cultivation difficult. Its rapid growth cycle
allows for more flushes per year, compensating for somewhat lower individual flush
weights. It also performs well on coffee grounds and other nitrogen-rich waste
substrates.

## Pleurotus eryngii

The King oyster mushroom is the largest species in the genus, producing
impressive fruiting bodies with thick, meaty stems up to 10 centimeters in
diameter and caps up to 15 centimeters across. Unlike other Pleurotus species,
the stem is the primary edible portion, with a texture and flavor often compared
to abalone or scallops when cooked. This unique characteristic commands premium
prices in many markets.

P. eryngii is native to the Mediterranean basin and parts of Central Asia, where
it grows as a weak parasite on the roots of Apiaceae plants (Eryngium, Ferula,
Thapsia). Cultivation requires sterilized supplemented sawdust substrate and
cooler fruiting temperatures of 12 to 18 degrees C. It has a longer cultivation
cycle (40 to 60 days from spawning to harvest) but yields are high and the crop
has excellent shelf life.

## Pleurotus citrinopileatus

The Golden oyster mushroom is one of the most visually striking cultivated
species, producing clusters of bright yellow to golden-orange caps. Native to
eastern Asia, it has gained popularity for both its ornamental appearance and its
intense, slightly nutty flavor. Caps are smaller than P. ostreatus, typically 3
to 8 centimeters, growing in dense clusters.

P. citrinopileatus prefers warmer temperatures, fruiting at 20 to 30 degrees C.
It has a relatively short shelf life compared to other oyster species, with caps
drying and wrinkling quickly after harvest. This limits its commercial distribution
radius but makes it excellent for direct-to-consumer and farmers market sales.

## Pleurotus djamor

The Pink oyster mushroom produces vivid pink to salmon-colored caps that retain
some color even after cooking. It is one of the most heat-tolerant species,
fruiting well at 25 to 35 degrees C, making it ideal for tropical cultivation.
Growth is extremely fast: pinning occurs within days of introducing fruiting
conditions, and mushrooms reach harvest size in 3 to 5 days.

Pink oysters have a shorter shelf life than most species and the color fades as
mushrooms mature. They are best harvested young when the caps are still vibrant
and the texture is firm. The flavor is slightly stronger and more aromatic than
grey oyster mushrooms, with tropical fruit notes in some strains.

## Pleurotus cornucopiae

The Branching oyster mushroom produces antler-like or coral-shaped fruiting bodies
when grown in high CO2 environments. Under proper fresh air exchange, it develops
more typical shelf-like forms but with a characteristic ruffled, irregular cap
margin. It is less commonly cultivated commercially but is valued by specialty
growers for its unusual appearance and firm texture.

## Substrate and Species Matching

Different Pleurotus species show distinct substrate preferences. P. ostreatus and
P. pulmonarius are generalists that grow on nearly any lignocellulosic material.
P. eryngii prefers hardwood sawdust, particularly from deciduous trees. P.
citrinopileatus performs well on straw and cottonseed hulls. P. djamor excels on
sugarcane bagasse and rice straw in tropical environments. Matching species to
locally available and affordable substrates is a key factor in cultivation
economics. See also [[oyster-mushroom-fruiting-conditions]], [[fruiting-conditions]], and [[oyster-mushroom-species-comparison]]
