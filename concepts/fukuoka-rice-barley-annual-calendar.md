# Fukuoka Rice-Barley Annual Calendar

Masanobu Fukuoka's direct-seeded, no-tillage rice/barley succession produces ~22 bushels (1,300 lbs) of rice and 22 bushels of barley per quarter-acre -- matching conventional Japanese yields at a fraction of the labor. The entire year's planting for one field is finished by New Year's Day. See [[natural-farming-fukuoka]] for the full method overview and [[fukuoka-philosophy-in-practice]] for the philosophical context.

## Annual Cycle

### Early October
- **Broadcast white clover** among standing rice (~1 lb/quarter-acre)
- **Broadcast barley/rye seed** (~6.5-13 lbs/quarter-acre; more for hard/poor soil or beginners)
- Clover and winter grain sprout 1-2 inches before rice harvest
- Seed rates can be reduced over years as soil improves and farmer gains experience

### Late October / Early November
- **Harvest rice** while trampling young barley/rye seedlings underfoot -- they recover immediately
- **Thresh rice** on site
- **Scatter ALL rice straw uncut** over the field -- do NOT chop or arrange neatly
- **Add chicken manure** (650-900 lbs/quarter-acre) to help decompose straw
- **Form clay pellets** containing rice seed (4.5-9 lbs/quarter-acre); see [[natural-farming-seed-balls]]

### Mid-November to Mid-December
- **Broadcast rice seed balls** among the young barley/rye plants
- Alternatively, broadcast in spring (check germination of fall-sown seed in late April; broadcast more pellets as needed)
- Thin layer of chicken manure spread over field
- **Year's planting is now complete**

### Winter (December-March)
- Clover and barley/rye grow together; clover fixes nitrogen
- Rice seed sits dormant inside clay pellets, protected from rodents and birds
- No field work required

### Late April
- **Check rice germination** from fall-sown pellets
- **Broadcast supplemental rice seed balls** if germination is sparse
- Rice seedlings are 1-2 inches tall by this time

### Late May (~May 20th)
- **Harvest barley/rye** (rice seedlings are still only 1-2 inches tall; they survive trampling)
- **Thresh** on site
- **Scatter ALL barley/rye straw uncut** over the field
- **Add dried chicken manure** (450-900 lbs/quarter-acre)
- Rice seedlings emerge through the straw mulch

### Early June
- **Plaster levees with mud** to prevent water leakage
- **Flood field for 4-7 days** -- this weakens clover and weeds, gives rice seedlings a chance to establish
- Do NOT kill the clover -- only weaken it; it recovers when water is drained
- Drain as soon as possible after the week

### June-July
- **No irrigation needed** -- even in dry years, soil stays moist under straw and green manure mulch
- Rainfall alone is sufficient
- If extremely dry, light irrigation is acceptable
- Rice plants develop strong roots in unflooded soil

### August
- **Fresh water run through field about once a week** -- never allow it to stand
- Standing water promotes weak, leggy growth and disease
- Ideal soil moisture: 60-80% of water-holding capacity

### September-October
- **Rice matures and is harvested** -- cycle begins again
- Compact plants produce heavy grain: 2,000 lbs straw yields 2,000+ lbs rice (good harvest: 1,650 lbs/quarter-acre)

## Key Quantities

| Input | Rate (per quarter-acre) | Notes |
|-------|------------------------|-------|
| White clover seed | ~1 lb | Sown early October |
| Barley/rye seed | 6.5-13 lbs | Reduce as soil improves |
| Rice seed (in pellets) | 4.5-9 lbs | Check and supplement in spring |
| Chicken manure (fall) | 650-900 lbs | Helps decompose rice straw |
| Chicken manure (spring) | 450-900 lbs | Helps decompose barley straw |
| Straw (rice) | ALL from harvest | Scatter uncut |
| Straw (barley/rye) | ALL from harvest | Scatter uncut |
| Ducklings (alternative to manure) | 10 per quarter-acre | Patrol for insects and weeds |

## Water Management Details

Fukuoka's water regime is the opposite of conventional paddy farming:

1. **June**: Flood 4-7 days only (weakens clover/weeds)
2. **June-July**: No irrigation at all -- soil stays moist under mulch
3. **August**: Run fresh water through weekly, never let it stand
4. **Never hold water more than 5 days** at any point

Conventional farming keeps fields flooded all season to suppress weeds. Fukuoka suppresses weeds with straw mulch and clover instead. The result: rice plants develop stronger roots, are more disease-resistant, and sunlight reaches the base of every plant.

## The "No Gap" Principle

A critical insight: timing seeding so there is **no interval between succeeding crops** gives grain a great advantage over weeds:
- Winter weeds sprout after rice harvest, but winter grain already has a head start
- Summer weeds sprout after barley harvest, but rice is already growing strongly
- The preceding crop provides physical cover (hides seeds from birds) and mulch (suppresses weed germination)

## Straw Mulch Rules

- **Always scatter uncut** -- chopped straw does not work as well
- **Never arrange neatly** -- toss "every which way, just as though the stalks had fallen naturally"
- **Cross-species mulching is safe**: rice straw on barley field (fall), barley straw on rice field (spring)
- **Do NOT use same-species fresh straw**: fresh winter grain straw should not mulch other winter grain (disease risk)
- **Rice straw decomposes completely** by the time rice sprouts the following spring
- **All straw and hulls** from threshing go back to the field -- nothing is removed

## Expected Yields

| Crop | Average Yield | Best Yield |
|------|--------------|------------|
| Rice | ~22 bushels/quarter-acre (1,300 lbs) | ~27.5 bushels (1,650 lbs) with old glutinous varieties |
| Barley/rye | ~22 bushels/quarter-acre (1,300 lbs) | Up to 29 bushels |

Yields continue to increase and soil becomes richer every year under this system.

## Transition from Conventional Farming

For fields currently under conventional cultivation:
1. **Year 1**: Some weeding, composting, or pruning may still be necessary
2. **Gradually reduce** each intervention year by year
3. **For leaky paddies**: Start with light 2-inch tilling, transition to full no-till after 1-2 years
4. **Use more seed initially** (up to 22 lbs/quarter-acre for winter grain) -- reduce as soil improves
5. The most important factor is "the state of mind of the farmer" -- patience and observation over intervention

## Adapting to Other Climates

Fukuoka noted this method could be adapted:
- Where water is scarce: upland rice, buckwheat, sorghum, millet
- Instead of white clover: other clover varieties, alfalfa, vetch, or lupine
- "Natural farming takes a distinctive form in accordance with the unique conditions of the area in which it is applied"
