# Compost Bins and Containers

Compost containers are useful not only because they keep the compost
moist right up to the edges but also because they keep it tidy. Every
garden must have a compost heap. This is the ideal way to return as
much organic matter as possible to the soil, following nature's
example. Whether you build your own bin or buy one, make sure that it
enables you to get to the compost easily when it comes to putting it
on the garden.

## Types of Manufactured Compost Containers

### Wooden Compost Bins

Commercial wooden bins normally come in kit form and you assemble them.
Stacking bins, like the beehive model, enable you to build the heap
gradually one section at a time and allow easy access to the compost.
Wooden bins look good and are relatively cheap and easy to make.

### Plastic Compost Bins

This type of bin is useful in a small garden. A small amount of air is
allowed in through the bottom and a lid keeps the contents dry and the
heat in. Plastic bins are lightweight, durable, and available in
various sizes to suit different gardens.

## Homemade Compost Containers

### Wire-and-Post Container

This method is suitable only if you can put the compost heap somewhere
it cannot be seen. Hammer four stakes into the ground to make a 3ft
(1m) square. Staple about 12ft (4m) of wire mesh, 3ft (1m) deep, to
the outside of the stakes. Tie large pieces of cardboard to the inside
of the wire to help retain moisture and heat.

### Plastic Barrel Container

Large plastic barrels used for fruit concentrates make ideal compost
containers. Cut off the top and bottom with a sharp knife. Keep one of
the cut ends and use it as a lid. Drill 1in (2.5cm) holes around the
barrel, about one hole every 1 sq ft (30 sq cm), to allow air
circulation.

### Brick-Built Compost Bin

This method is suitable only if you are never going to move the heap.
Stagger the bricks so that air can get into the compost heap. The
front should be made of wooden slats for easy access. Fix wood shims
down the inside of the walls and slide the slats in.

## Making a Wooden Compost Container

This is a very simple procedure possible by even the most reluctant
DIY enthusiast. The most important factor is to take the time to be
precise, as the bin looks good enough to admire and not hide away.

### Materials Needed

- 4 x 3ft (1m) lengths of 2 x 4in (5 x 10cm) wood for the uprights
- 19 x 3ft (1m) lengths of wood for the sides
- 5 x 2ft 6in (75cm) lengths of wood for the front panels
- 4 x 2ft 6in (75cm) wood shims
- 2 small pieces of wood
- Strong nails, about four per panel

### Construction Steps

1. Place two of the uprights on the ground, parallel to each other
   and 2ft 6in (75cm) apart. Place one of the side planks across them
   3in (7.5cm) from the bottom of each post and nail it into
   position. Nail five more planks between the uprights, ensuring they
   all butt up against each other. Make another wall to match.

2. Stand the two walls up parallel to each other and at right angles
   to a wall. Nail a piece of wood to the top of each upright to hold
   them in position. Working from the bottom upward, nail six pieces
   of wood across the back, level with those on each side.

3. Remove the support panel, turn the box around, and make the front
   wall. Nail a board across the front of the uprights 3in (7.5cm)
   from the bottom.

4. Nail two shims onto the side edge of each upright, making sure
   they are far enough apart to slide the front panels between them.
   Nail a small piece of wood across the bottom to prevent panels from
   sliding out when filling.

5. Slide all front panels into the bin to check the fit, then paint
   the entire container, including the cut edges and front panels,
   with a water-based wood preservative. Let air dry.

6. Tie a piece of string across the top of the container to prevent
   the sides from bulging outward when you fill it.

## The Need for Two Containers

A successful organic garden should have at least two compost heaps or
bins. That way, the contents of one can be left to rot down properly
while the other is being filled up. In a hot year, from each bin you
should get two good binfuls in the summer and another in the spring.
