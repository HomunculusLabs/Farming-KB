# Enterprise Overhead and Gross Margin Analysis

Gross margin analysis, derived from Cambridge economist David Wallace,
helps determine the strongest links of product conversion in the chain
from sunlight energy to solar dollar. It assists in finding the
enterprise or combination of enterprises that brings in the most income
for the least additional cost each year, essential for ranch  [[integrate-rather-than-segregate]] grown to free
land for livestock and decrease capital per animal unit?

## When to Apply Gross Margin Analysis

Enterprises benefit from analysis at three points during the year:

1. **Evaluating new enterprises** or when market shifts affect
  existing ones.
2. **Annual financial planning** when the main strategy takes shape.
3. **Year's end** when actual results feed back into future plans.

## Accounting for Risk

Advance analysis must consider major risk variables. Project worst,
average, and best cases, starting with the variable least within
control. For a dry farmer, weather is critical -- compute margins at
low, average, and high yields at average price. For irrigated farming,
price may be the critical variable.

## Integration with Other Guidelines

Gross margin analysis must be used with other testing guidelines: the
cause and effect guideline, [[animal-tractoring]] guideline, and the holistic goal itself. The ideal is
finding the enterprise combination where all tools pass all tests,
creating a holistically sound chain from sunlight to solar dollar.

## The Supplemental Feeding Example

Supplemental feed and salt expenses may have no justification at all.
The same funds invested in fencing to increase paddock divisions would
yield a higher marginal reaction. Supplements seldom pass the marginal
reaction test compared to fencing until paddock levels reach thirty or
more per herd with good biological planning.
