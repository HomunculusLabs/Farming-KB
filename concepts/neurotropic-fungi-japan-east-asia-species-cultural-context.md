## Japan's Neurotropic Fungi Diversity

Japan has one of the most comprehensively documented neurotropic fungal
floras in Asia, thanks to the systematic work of Japanese mycologists
including Hongo, Imazeki, Yokoyama, and Koike.

### Psilocybe Species

Japan hosts several Psilocybe species, some of which are endemic to East
Asia:
- **Psilocybe argentipes**: Originally described from Japan by Yokoyama
  (1976, 1985), this species was also studied by Koike et al. (1981),
  Guzmán (1983), Singer (1986), Imazeki et al. (1988), Gartz (1996), and
  Stamets (1996). It is one of the most significant East Asian endemic
  neurotropic species.
- **Psilocybe subaeruginascens var. subaeruginascens**: Confirmed from
  Japan by Koike et al. (1981), Guzmán (1983), Imazeki et al. (1988),
  and Stamets (1996). This variety also occurs in Indonesia (Java).
- **[[allegro-amanita-muscaria-properties-and-pharmacology]]**: Wasson (1968), Singer (1978) from the Himalaya.
- **Amanita muscaria**: Heim (1978) from Tibet.

The Himalayan A. muscaria is particularly significant in the context of
Wasson's Soma hypothesis, which proposed that A. muscaria was the
identity of the sacred Soma plant described in the Rig Veda (Wasson,
1968).
