## Overview

Tuberculosis (TB) is a serious infectious disease caused by the
Mycobacterium tuberculosis bacterium. It primarily affects the lungs but
can also affect other parts of the body. TB has been a major global
health concern for centuries and remains one of the leading causes of
death from infectious disease worldwide.

### The Antibiotic Resistance Crisis

As Blesching documents across multiple conditions, bacteria have
increasingly developed resistance to standard antibiotic treatments.
While this is most prominently discussed in the context of gonorrhea
and MRSA, the same trend affects tuberculosis, with multi-drug-resistant
TB (MDR-TB) and extensively drug-resistant TB (XDR-TB) representing
major global health threats.

## Cannabis Research on Bacterial Pathogens

Five major cannabinoids (THC, CBD, CBG, CBC, and CBN) have been
discovered to be potent against bacteria, including multi-drug-resistant
bacteria. While the primary research cited in The Cannabis Health Index
focuses on MRSA and gonorrhea, the broad-spectrum antibacterial
properties of cannabinoids suggest potential relevance to tuberculosis
as well.

The precise mechanism through which plant constituents are able to
destroy even antibiotic-resistant bacteria remains elusive and is an
active area of scientific investigation.

## Historical Use of Cannabis for Bacterial Infections

Prior to the discovery of penicillin, cannabidiolic acids were commonly
prescribed for bacterial infections. Arabic and Indian Ayurvedic
physicians long applied the diuretic, sedative, and anti-inflammatory
properties of cannabis to treat bacterial conditions. A Japanese team
of scientists (2008) discovered that CBD-acid selectively inhibits COX-2,
an enzyme responsible for inflammation and pain.
