# Psilocybin Mushroom Species Field Guide

Field identification and cultivation data for psilocybin-bearing mushroom species. Based on Adam Gottlieb's 1976 Psilocybin Producer's Guide. See also [[psilocybin-mushroom-distribution]] for global distribution data and [[psilocybe-genus-classification]] for taxonomic classification.

## Safety Warning

There is NO reliable field test for psilocybin mushrooms. The blueing reaction is NOT definitive -- the poisonous Eastwood Boletus also blues, and any mushroom containing indolic substances may respond. Always use an identification key (e.g., Shaffer's "Keys to Genera of Higher Fungi") and verify with an expert mycologist.

## Species Identification Data

### Conocybe cyanopes
- Season: May-September
- Habitat: Dense shade, scattered among mosses, wet soil around bogs/swamps/ditches
- Range: Northwestern USA to Michigan
- Cultivation: Carpophores grow on sphagnum moss, pH 7-8

### Copelandia cyanescens
- Season: Early summer through late autumn
- Habitat: Cow dung or rich soil, scattered/grouped/clustered
- Range: Florida and other southern states
- Cultivation: Spores germinate easily on all agar media. Optimum on MEA at 80F. Carpophores on uncased compost or rye.

### Panaeolus foenisecii (Haymower's Mushroom)
- Season: Late spring-early summer; also July-September in cool wet seasons
- Habitat: Lawns, pastures, grassy places; large groups
- Range: Throughout USA, Quebec
- Note: Washington specimens showed no psilocybin; eastern specimens were potent

### Panaeolus sphinctrinus
- Season: Summer-autumn
- Habitat: Small groups on cow dung, forests, pastures, fields, roadsides
- Range: Many temperate parts of the world

### Panaeolus subalteatus
- Season: Spring-autumn
- Habitat: Rings up to 2 feet diameter on open ground, manured lawns, straw piles, compost, dung
- Range: Ontario, throughout USA (esp. MA, MD, NY, OH, MI, WA, OR)
- Cultivation: Optimum on MEA at 86F. Occasionally occurs as weed mushroom in commercial mushroom houses

### Pholiotina cyanopoda
- Season: August-September
- Habitat: Solitary to clustered on lawns
- Range: NY, WA, CO

### Psilocybe baeocystis
- Season: Autumn-winter
- Habitat: Solitary/grouped/clustered on earth, lawns, mulch, decomposing forest wood near scattered trees (esp. conifers)
- Range: Western Oregon and Washington
- Cultivation: Does well on agar at 77F. Thermal death at 95F.
- Potency: Very high. Contains psilocybin, psilocin, baeocystin, and nor-baeocystin. Most visually hallucinogenic species. Start with small doses due to baeocystin alkaloids.

### Psilocybe caerulescens
- Season: Summer (rainy season)
- Habitat: Grouped/clustered in shady places on soil, sugar cane mulch, turned earth, stream banks
- Range: Alabama, northern Florida, Mexico. Mexican var. "Durrumbe" found among landslides near corn/coffee plantations.
- Cultivation: Mycelium best on MEA at 81F. Thermal death at 95F. Very difficult to produce carpophores on sterilized rye. Can grow on vegetable compost in dim light (55-85 day incubation). Resistant to white mold but long incubation = disease prone. Not very potent.

### Psilocybe caerulipes
- Season: Summer, occasionally autumn
- Habitat: Solitary/clustered on decomposing logs/debris of hardwood trees (esp. birch, maple)
- Range: NY, New England, OH, MI, NC, TN, Ontario

### Psilocybe cubensis var. cyanescens (San Ysidro)
- Season: February-November
- Habitat: Compact groups on cow dung, horse dung, rich pasture soil, straw, sawdust/dung mixture
- Range: Mexico, Cuba, Florida, other southern states, Australia, SE Asia
- Cultivation: Grows well on MEA at 86F. Carpophores in 4-8 weeks. Thermal death at 104F. Does poorly in darkness. Larger carpophores via compost in clay pots, silica sand/limestone casing, 4-6 weeks at 68F in daylight.
- Potency: Potent and very resistant to contaminants. Most easily cultivated species.

### Psilocybe cyanescens
- Season: Autumn
- Habitat: Scattered/grouped/clustered in woods, on earth, among leaves/twigs, occasionally decomposing wood
- Range: Northwestern USA

### Psilocybe mexicana
- Season: May-October
- Habitat: Isolated or sparse, altitudes 4500-5500 feet (esp. limestone regions), among mosses/herbs, roadsides, humid meadows, cornfields, near pine forests
- Range: Mexico

### Psilocybe pelliculosa
- Season: September-December
- Habitat: Scattered/grouped/clustered on humus/debris in or near conifer forests
- Range: Northwestern USA to Marin County, CA
- Potency: Small but potent species

### Psilocybe quebecensis
- Season: Summer-late October
- Habitat: Shady forest edges, sandy soil with vegetable debris (regularly flooded by rivers), decomposing wood/debris (esp. birch, alder, fir, spruce)
- Range: Quebec area
- Cultivation: Thrives at lower temperatures; produces carpophores at 43-59F

### Psilocybe semilanceata (Liberty Cap)
- Season: August-September
- Habitat: Large groups on soil, among grasses, clearings, pastures, meadows, forest edges, conifer woodlands, roadsides -- NEVER on dung
- Range: NY, northern USA, British Columbia, Europe
- Potency: Generally regarded as less potent but sometimes quite potent

### Psilocybe strictipes
- Season: October
- Habitat: Clustered on soil or decomposing wood/debris on conifers and some other trees
- Range: Northwestern USA (esp. Oregon)
- Potency: Closely resembles P. baeocystis but longer stem. Similar baeocystin alkaloids; very visual.

### Psilocybe sylvatica
- Season: September-October
- Habitat: Small compact groups in woods on leaf mold/debris (esp. beech wood), around stumps/logs (not usually on them)
- Range: NY to Michigan, Quebec and Ontario
- Note: Often mistaken for P. pelliculosa

## Broader Taxonomy

Known psilocybin-bearing genera and approximate species counts:
- Conocybe: ~40 species (forests, pastures, gardens, dung, sandy soil, ant hills, charcoal)
- Panaeolus: ~20 species (soil and dung, cosmopolitan)
- Psilocybe: ~40 species (soil, moss, dung, rotting wood, bagasse, peat; arctic to tropics)
- Stropharia: ~9 species (soil, dung, leaf mulch, rotting wood, cosmopolitan)

## Cultivation Notes

P. cubensis is recommended for beginners due to: easiest to obtain, most readily cultivated, most disease resistant, psychoactively strongest. Other species require more specific conditions. See [[psilocybin-mushroom-cultivation]] for cultivation methods.
