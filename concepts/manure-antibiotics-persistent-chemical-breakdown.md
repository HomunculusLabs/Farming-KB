# Manure Antibiotics and Persistent Chemical Breakdown

## Overview

When [[composting]] manure from livestock that have been treated with wormers, antibiotics, or other veterinary chemicals, special considerations are needed to ensure these contaminants are adequately broken down. The biological approach to decontamination relies on encouraging the right bacteria and fungi to decompose these compounds.

## Hierarchy of Composting Problems

Based on practical experience, the following ranking of contaminants from most to least problematic in composting:

1. **Strongylid medicine (de-wormer)** — the most significant problem, hardest to break down
2. **Herbicide residues** — particularly persistent synthetic herbicides
3. **Insecticides**
4. **Fungicides**
5. **Copper** — accumulates over repeated applications
6. **Sulfur** — can lower pH excessively
7. **Antibiotics** — generally the easiest to decompose, as long as concentrations are not overwhelming

## Antibiotic Decomposition

Antibiotics are generally easy to decompose through biological composting, provided the system is not overwhelmed with high concentrations. The key is maintaining a healthy, diverse microbial community that can metabolize these compounds.

## Detecting Contamination Problems

The primary indicator of contamination problems in a compost pile is **abnormal temperature response**:

- Temperature stays **too low for too long** after starting a windrow or pile
- This indicates that the microbial community is being inhibited by contaminants
- Normal composting should show rapid temperature rise to thermophilic range (55-65 C / 131-149 F)

## Remediation Protocol

When contamination is suspected based on poor temperature response:

### Step 1: Add Nitrogen
Add **[[fish-hydrolysate]]** as a nitrogen source. This is especially important if de-wormer residues are suspected, as the extra nitrogen gives organisms the energy to decompose these recalcitrant compounds.

### Step 2: Add Simple Carbon
Add **molasses** as an energy source. Molasses provides readily available sugars that fuel rapid microbial growth, which in turn accelerates [[decomposition]] of contaminants.

### Step 3: Add Specialized Inoculum (If Needed)
If temperature still does not respond normally, add commercial compost starters containing specialized bacteria known to degrade persistent chemicals. Examples include:

- **EM inoculum** (Effective Microorganisms)
- **Biostart**
- **Organica**
- **Agri-Energy compost starters**

These products contain bacteria specifically selected for their ability to decompose nasty materials that standard compost organisms cannot handle.

## Grass Clipping Awareness

Grass clippings are a common source of chemical contamination in compost. Many lawns receive regular applications of herbicides, insecticides, and fungicides. Pay close attention to monitoring temperature at the start of composting when grass clippings are part of the feedstock.

If temperature does not start normally, contaminants are likely present and additional energy sources (like molasses) should be added to give organisms the fuel to decompose compounds such as:

- **Dieldrin** — organochlorine insecticide
- **Diazinon** — organophosphate insecticide
- **Furans** — persistent organic pollutants
- **DDT** — organochlorine insecticide (historical but persistent in soils)

## Clopyralid

Clopyralid is a synthetic auxin herbicide that is **not broken down during conventional composting**. This is a critical distinction from other contaminants:

- There is no known bacteria or fungi that routinely occur on grass clippings capable of decomposing clopyralid
- The organisms that might decompose it are not present in typical compost starting materials
- While unknown decomposers may exist, they do not occur routinely in common feedstocks
- Clopyralid-contaminated compost will damage or kill broadleaf plants when applied as a soil amendment

### Prevention

The only effective strategy with clopyralid is **prevention**:

- Do not compost grass clippings from lawns treated with clopyralid-containing herbicides
- Ask lawn care companies what products they use
- Common clopyralid products include Confront, Lontrel, Curtail, and Transline
- Many municipalities now restrict clopyralid use because of composting contamination issues

## Chlordane

Chlordane is another persistent organochlorine insecticide. While there are bacteria and fungi that can decompose chlordane, they are **not always found in compost starting materials**. This means:

- Some compost piles may successfully break down chlordane if the right organisms happen to be present
- Other piles may retain chlordane residues because the necessary decomposers are absent
- The presence or absence of degrading organisms depends on the source and history of the compost feedstocks

## Brix as a Plant Health Indicator

### What Is Brix?

Brix is a measure of **sugar content** (dissolved solids) in plant sap, measured with a refractometer. Higher brix readings are often associated with healthier plants.

### Nutrient Quality Differences

Research has shown that vegetables, grapes, and strawberries grown with healthy soil food webs contain **3 to 10 times more protein** than plants grown in conventional systems. This nutritional difference is attributed to the improved nutrient cycling and availability provided by a complete [[soil-food-web]].

### Free Amino Acids and Pest Attraction

If free amino acids accumulate in plant tissue and do not get incorporated into protein synthesis, they can attract pests. Understanding plant physiology is critical:

- Free amino acids sitting around doing nothing are not beneficial
- Excess free amino acids signal a breakdown in normal plant metabolic processes
- Protein must be moved into structural and functional forms within the plant

### Brix Measurement Limitations

There are practical challenges to using brix as a reliable health indicator:

- Plants naturally fluctuate in brix readings throughout the day
- Readings change from day to day based on environmental conditions
- It is difficult to establish a reliable baseline for comparison
- A standard curve of hourly readings through the day is needed to make meaningful comparisons
- Without a baseline, it is impossible to say whether treated plants are "better" or "worse"

### Alternative Interpretations

Alternative hypotheses about elevated brix deserve consideration:

- **Immune response hypothesis**: Elevated sugar might mean the plant is mounting an immune reaction to whatever was sprayed, not necessarily indicating improved health
- **Metabolic shift hypothesis**: If brix drops, it could mean sugars are being converted to other materials the plant needs

## The Bacteria-Then-Fungi Composting Cycle

### Decomposition Sequence

Bacteria and fungi play distinct and sequential roles in decomposition:

- **Bacteria start the process** by consuming easy-to-use sugars and simple, rapid-growth food resources
- **Fungi finish the process** by producing complex "waste products" that become humus, or more specifically, [[humic-acid]]

### Humus vs. Humic Acid

These terms describe different levels of organic matter complexity:

**Humus** includes all of the following:
- Living organisms (bacteria, fungi, protozoa, nematodes, roots)
- Simple organic matter (simple sugars, amino acids, proteins, simple carbohydrates, lipids, and thousands more simple compounds)
- Complex organic matter (polysaccharides, amino-sugars, hormones, fats, lipopolysaccharides, fulvic acids)
- All of the above branched and bound together in intermediate molecular weight fractions

**Humic acids** are all of the above bound together in very complex tertiary (three-dimensional) binding complexes.
