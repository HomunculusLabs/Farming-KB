# EMCDDA Hallucinogenic Mushroom Internet Information Sources

## Overview

Since the late 1990s, extensive internet repositories of information on
hallucinogenic mushrooms have emerged, contributing significantly to the
accessibility of information on mushroom description, use, effects, and the
exchange of experiences among users. The EMCDDA 2006 thematic paper identified
this online ecosystem as a key factor facilitating the diffusion of
hallucinogenic mushroom use across Europe, alongside the expansion of smartshop
markets and a broader "return to nature" trend.

## Major International Websites

Several US-based websites serve as comprehensive repositories of information
on hallucinogenic mushrooms and other psychoactive substances:

- **Erowid**: One of the most well-known drug information vaults, providing
  detailed information on effects, dosages, chemistry, and user experience
  reports for hallucinogenic mushrooms and many other substances.
- **Shroomery**: A dedicated mushroom-focused community site offering
  identification guides, cultivation advice, trip reports, and discussion
  forums.
- **Mycotopia**: An online community focused on mycology and the cultivation
  and use of psychoactive mushrooms.
- **Lycaeum**: An online library of psychoactive substance information.
- **MushroomJohn**: A resource for mushroom identification and information.
- **The Entheogen Review**: A publication and resource covering entheogenic
  substances including hallucinogenic mushrooms.

These international sites typically provide information on effects, dosages,
chemistry, mushroom classification, legal status, and user-submitted trip
reports. Many include photo galleries of various hallucinogenic mushroom
species and offer guidance on cultivation or collection of fresh mushrooms.

## Types of Information Provided

These websites are typically created by non-profit organisations, mushroom
users, or lobby groups. They commonly provide:

- **Effects and dosage information**: Detailed descriptions of expected
  effects at various dose levels, including subjective experiences.
- **Chemistry and classification**: Information on active compounds
  (psilocybin, psilocin), chemical properties, and taxonomic classification.
- **Legal information**: Current legal status in various jurisdictions, though
  this information may become outdated.
- **Trip reports**: First-hand accounts of user experiences, contributing to
  collective knowledge about effects and risks.
- **Identification guides**: Photographic and descriptive guides for
  identifying hallucinogenic mushroom species in the wild.
- **Cultivation advice**: Instructions on growing mushrooms at home using
  spore prints, growkits, or other methods.
- **Location sharing**: Some sites allow users to share information about
  known locations where hallucinogenic mushrooms grow wild.

## Community Features

Many internet information sources include community-oriented features that
enable interaction among users:

- **Discussion forums**: Spaces for users to ask questions, share
  experiences, and exchange practical advice.
- **Events listings**: Information about gatherings, festivals, or other
  events related to psychedelic culture.
- **Supply channel information**: Forums where users discuss purchasing
  options, though this raises concerns about facilitating drug access.
- **Photo galleries**: Collections of mushroom photographs for
  identification and educational purposes.

## Information Quality and Concerns

The EMCDDA noted that the quality and reliability of information on these
websites is variable. Key concerns include:

- Information may be biased toward positive effects, with insufficient
  attention to risks and negative outcomes.
- Dosage recommendations may not account for individual variation in
  sensitivity.
- Legal information can become outdated as legislation changes.
- Cultivation instructions may facilitate illegal activity in jurisdictions
  where mushrooms are controlled.

Despite these concerns, these internet resources have played a significant
role in democratizing access to information about hallucinogenic mushrooms,
filling a gap where formal drug education and prevention programmes have
been slow to address this emerging trend.

## Role in the Emerging Trend

The internet has been identified as one of the key factors in the emergence
and diffusion of hallucinogenic mushroom use in Europe. Alongside smartshop
marketing and lifestyle trends toward natural products, online information
sources have lowered barriers to knowledge about identification, effects,
and acquisition of hallucinogenic mushrooms. The availability of detailed
cultivation guides online has also contributed to the growth of home
cultivation as an alternative source of supply.
