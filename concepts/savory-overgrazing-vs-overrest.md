# Overgrazing Versus Overrest

One of the most revolutionary insights in Allan Savory's work is the
recognition that overgrazing and overrest are two distinct and equally
destructive forces that commonly occur simultaneously on the same land.
Conventional [[mollison-pdc-range-management-wildlife]] has historically blamed all grassland
deterioration on overgrazing, but Savory's observations revealed that
overrest is often the more insidious and less recognized culprit,
especially in brittle environments.

## The Nature of Overgrazing

Overgrazing is not caused by having too many animals. André Voisin's
pioneering research, which Savory drew upon extensively, demonstrated
that overgrazing is fundamentally a function of **time**, not numbers.
A plant is overgrazed when animals remain on it too long during a
single grazing period, or when they return to it before it has had
adequate time to recover from the previous defoliation.

The key insight is that overgrazing applies to **individual plants**, not
to areas of land. When conventional managers apply the label "overgrazed"
to an entire ranch or paddock, they miss the critical fact that within
any grazed area some plants may be overgrazed while others are
overrested, and still others may be healthy.

Animals naturally select their preferred plants and tend to concentrate
on the most palatable species. In a set-stocked situation where cattle
remain on the same ground year-round, they repeatedly bite the same
favoured plants before recovery can occur, while less palatable species
accumulate old growth. Over time, the most desirable grasses weaken and
disappear while the community shifts toward less productive species.

## The Problem of Overrest

Overrest is the accumulation of old, dead plant material on grass plants
that blocks sunlight from reaching basal growing points. In brittle
environments, dead material breaks down extremely slowly through
chemical oxidation and physical weathering rather than rapid biological
decay. Tips of leaves and stems weather away first, leaving the basal
growth points shaded and obstructed for years.

Several years of accumulated old growth can actually kill a perennial
grass plant. In rested brittle environments, closely-spaced grass
plants that established after some disturbance begin to kill each other
as old growth shades even the edges of neighboring clumps. Whole
handfuls of dead grass can be pulled out by the roots with ease.

The consequences of overrest are most visible on protected lands. At
the Sevilleta Wildlife Refuge in New Mexico, grasslands rested for
decades show wide bare spaces between vestigial perennial plants whose
centers are dead. Despite years of [[cervantes-seed-production-storage]] by surrounding
plants, no seedlings have established on the bare, undisturbed surface.

## How Overgrazing and Overrest Compound

The standard practice of set-stocking low numbers of livestock scattered
widely across large areas produces both overgrazing and overrest
simultaneously. Cattle concentrate on palatable plants near water and
shade, overgrazing those individuals, while the majority of the land
sits in a state of partial rest with insufficient [[savory-animal-impact-and-herd-effect]].

Savory observed this [[psilocin-accumulation-pattern-across-flushes]] Africa, the Americas, and the
Middle East. On inspection, lands labelled "overstocked" almost always
contained both overgrazed and overrested plants, along with bare ground
from partial rest. The blanket label "overgrazed" means nothing and
offers no guide to a solution when overrested plants are also present.

Even gross overgrazing can maintain a grassland community longer than
total rest in brittle environments. At Chaco Canyon National Monument,
fifty years of rest destroyed most grass while adjacent land that had
been heavily overgrazed for the same period still had green, growing
plants at every footstep.
