## The Broader Principle: Microclimate Creation

The rock terrace technique illustrates a broader
principle in [[ecological-garden-design]]: the
deliberate creation of favorable microclimates. By
using thermal mass, wind protection, and water
management, gardeners can modify local conditions
to suit a wider range of plants than the regional
climate would normally allow.

Hemenway discusses several related microclimate
techniques:

### Suntraps

U-shaped or horseshoe-shaped plantings that open
toward the sun create warm, sheltered pockets.
The reflecting surfaces bounce additional light
onto plants, while the surrounding vegetation
blocks wind.

### Windbreaks

Hedges, walls, and dense plantings reduce wind
speed, which lowers evaporative cooling and wind
chill. Even a low wall or fence can create a
significant sheltered zone extending many times
its height downwind.

### Water Bodies

Ponds and water features moderate temperature
through thermal mass. Water heats and cools more
slowly than air or land, creating stable
microclimates in their vicinity. A pond can also
reflect light onto nearby plantings.
