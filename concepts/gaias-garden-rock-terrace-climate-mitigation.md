# Rock Terraces and Climate Mitigation

## The Jerome Osentowski [[food-forest-design-guide]]

[[hemenway-accelerated-succession]] presents Jerome Osentowski's garden
in Aspen, Colorado, as a remarkable case study in
using rock terraces to mitigate harsh mountain
climates. At 7,400 feet above sea level, where
frosts can occur during any month, Jerome has
created a productive forest garden that defies the
conventional limits of his climate zone.

The rock terraces are essential to this success.
They demonstrate how intelligent use of thermal
mass and shelter can extend the growing range of
many species far beyond their expected hardiness
zones.

## How Rock Terraces Work

### [[urban-water-catchment-cistern-thermal-mass]] and Heat Storage

The mass [[allegro-star-of-the-morning-venus-and-the-sacred-mushroom]] stones absorbs heat during the day
and releases it slowly at night. This heat storage
has several critical effects:

- **Softens temperature swings**: The diurnal
  temperature range is reduced, preventing the
  extreme highs and lows that stress plants.

- **Warms plants at night**: Released heat
  protects against frost damage during the growing
  season.

- **Fights frost**: The radiant heat from rocks can
  raise air temperature by several degrees around
  frost-sensitive plants.

### Seasonal Benefits

The rock terraces are important in helping young
plants survive. They get shaded in summer but still
store heat in winter and especially in spring when
plants need it most. During winter, the rocks,
snow cover, [[using-accumulators-in-compost-and-mulch]] combine to prevent the ground
from freezing even when air temperatures swing
below zero.

This ground-level warmth allows slug-eating snakes
[[allen-gymnopilus-and-other-psychoactive-genera]] beneficial wildlife to survive the cold
season, maintaining predator populations that keep
pests in check year-round.

### Soil Improvement

Rock terraces also function as soil-building
structures. The spaces between rocks accumulate
organic matter, leaves, and moisture, creating
fertile microsites where plants can establish.
Terrace walls prevent erosion on slopes and allow
the gardener to create level planting surfaces on
otherwise difficult terrain.

## Building Rock Terraces

### Design Considerations

- **Orientation**: Terraces should face the sun
  to maximize heat absorption. South-facing
  terraces [[allegro-death-and-resurrection-in-the-mushroom-cult]] Northern Hemisphere capture
  the most solar energy.

- **Height**: Walls should be substantial enough
  to provide meaningful thermal mass but not so
  tall that they shade the planting area.

- **Stone selection**: Dense, dark-colored stones
  absorb and retain the most heat. Local stone
  is ideal, both ecologically and aesthetically.

- **Drainage**: Ensure [[water-management]] can flow through or
  around terrace walls rather than pooling behind
  them.

### Construction Tips

- Use the largest stones at the base for
  stability
- Fit stones tightly to maximize contact and
  heat transfer
- Leave small gaps for drainage and wildlife
  habitat
- Backfill with rich [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-soil-and-humus-management]] organic matter
- Mulch the surface to retain moisture and
  suppress weeds

## The Broader Principle: Microclimate Creation

The rock terrace technique illustrates a broader
principle in [[ecological-garden-design]]: the
deliberate creation of favorable microclimates. By
using thermal mass, wind protection, and water
management, gardeners can modify local conditions
to suit a wider range of plants than the regional
climate would normally allow.

Hemenway discusses several related microclimate
techniques:

### Suntraps

U-shaped or horseshoe-shaped plantings that open
toward the sun create warm, sheltered pockets.
The reflecting surfaces bounce additional light
onto plants, while the surrounding vegetation
blocks wind.

### Windbreaks

Hedges, walls, and dense plantings reduce wind
speed, which lowers evaporative cooling and wind
chill. Even a low wall or fence can create a
significant sheltered zone extending many times
its height downwind.

### Water Bodies

Ponds and water features moderate temperature
through thermal mass. Water heats and cools more
slowly than air or land, creating stable
microclimates in their vicinity. A pond can also
reflect light onto nearby plantings.
