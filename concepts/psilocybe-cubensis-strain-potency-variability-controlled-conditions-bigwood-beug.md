# Strain Potency Variability in Psilocybe cubensis

## Overview

The Bigwood and Beug (1982) study provided some of the earliest quantitative evidence that *[[psilocybe-cubensis]]* strains produce wildly different alkaloid levels even when grown under identical, carefully controlled conditions. Their data demonstrated a **fourfold variation** between minicultures of the same Amazonian strain, and a **tenfold variation** between unknown street samples—all grown on the same basic rye-grain substrate. These findings fundamentally challenge the assumption that strain name or source provides reliable information about potency.

## Controlled-Culture Variation (Same Strain)

### Three Parallel Minicultures

Bigwood and Beug grew three independent minicultures from the same Amazonian spore print (originally collected near Pucalpa, Peru). Despite identical protocols, psilocybin levels varied substantially:

| Flush | Miniculture 1 (mg/g) | Miniculture 2 (mg/g) | Miniculture 3 (mg/g) |
|-------|----------------------|----------------------|----------------------|
| 1     | 8.3                  | 5.1                  | 7.6                  |
| 2     | 6.5                  | 7.0                  | 6.2                  |
| 3     | 13.3                 | 4.7                  | 5.3                  |
| 4     | 4.8                  | 3.7                  | 3.2                  |
| 5     | 5.2                  | 5.2                  | 6.7                  |
| 6     | 6.8                  | —                    | —                    |

The highest single reading (13.3 mg/g in Miniculture 1, Flush 3) was more than **four times** the lowest (3.2 mg/g in Miniculture 3, Flush 4). This variation occurred despite:
- Identical spore origin (same Amazonian print)
- Same substrate (rye grain, 10 g + 15 ml water)
- Same casing mix (peat : calcium carbonate : perlite/vermiculite)
- Same environmental conditions
- Same handling, drying, and analysis protocols

### Sources of Intra-Strain Variation

Several factors may explain the variation even under controlled conditions:

1. **Genetic heterogeneity**: Spore prints contain millions of individual spores with genetic variation. Each miniculture was inoculated from mycelium ultimately derived from different spores, meaning each jar represented a genetically unique individual organism, not a clone.

2. **Micro-environmental gradients**: Despite efforts at consistency, subtle differences in temperature, gas exchange, moisture distribution, or light exposure within and between jars could influence alkaloid biosynthesis.

3. **[[fungal-biogeography-distribution-patterns]]:

| Tissue | M.R. Strain (mg/g) | Equadorian (mg/g) | Amazon (mg/g) |
|--------|--------------------|--------------------|----------------|
| Caps — Psilocybin | 6.7 | 7.6 | 5.7 |
| Caps — Psilocin | 0 | 0 | 0.1 |
| Stems — Psilocybin | 4.2 | 4.7 | 5.7 |
| Stems — Psilocin | 0.35 | 0.4 | 0 |

Key observations:
- **M.R. and Equadorian strains**: Caps contained approximately **1.6× more psilocybin** than stems; psilocin was found **only in stems**
- **Amazon strain**: Equal [[mycorrhizal-phosphatase-activity-elf97-detection]]

## Street Sample Variation (Unknown Strains)

Five street samples of *P. cubensis*, all reportedly cultivated on rye-grain substrate, showed dramatic potency differences:

| Sample | Psilocybin (mg/g) | Psilocin (mg/g) |
|--------|--------------------|-----------------|
| 1      | 5.6                | 0               |
| 2      | 6.2                | 0               |
| 3      | 0.7                | 0.3             |
| 4      | 0.7                | 0.3             |
| 5      | 1.3                | 0.3             |

### Analysis of Street Sample Data

The tenfold range (0.7 to 6.2 mg/g psilocybin) is nearly triple the fourfold range seen in controlled cultures. [[psilocin-emergence-in-later-flushes]]
- **Post-harvest handling**: Drying method, storage temperature, and duration all affect alkaloid stability, particularly for the less stable psilocin
- **Substrate variations**: Despite all being described as rye-grain grown, exact [[bulk-substrate-preparation]] likely varied
- **Sample age**: Older samples may have degraded alkaloid content
