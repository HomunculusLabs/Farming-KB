# Chytridiomycota Diversity

The Chytridiomycota comprises approximately 800 described
species of [[anaerobic-zoosporic-fungi]], defined traditionally by the
presence of a single posteriorly inserted smooth flagellum
(Barr 2001). As the only fungi that retain a flagellated
stage in their [[bloomfield-rust-fungi-life-cycles-barberry-wheat]], chytrids represent the most
basal lineage of the [[fungal-taxonomy-and-kingdom-fungi]], consistent with a
choanoflagellate-like ancestor. At the time [[biodiversity-of-fungi-pcr-molecular-methods-fungal-diversity]]
came into use, some mycologists doubted that chytrids were
true fungi, but their inclusion in the kingdom has been
confirmed based on rDNA evidence (Forster et al. 1990; Bowman
et al. 1992; Bruns et al. 1993).

## Phylogenetic Position and Monophyly

Subsequent molecular studies with increased taxon sampling
have shown that the Chytridiomycota, as traditionally defined,
is not monophyletic. However, chytrid groups are consistently
basal in phylogenetic trees (James et al. 2000; Tanabe et al.
2000), and a core group of Chytridiomycota does form a clade.
Some analyses place the genus Basidiobolus (traditionally a
zygomycete) among the core chytrids, though this may be an
artifact of rDNA-based analyses. The Blastocladiales may
represent a lineage independent of the other orders of
Chytridiomycota.

## Orders and Classification

Barr and Desaulniers (1988) established five orders of
chytrids based on the ultrastructure of zoospores:

- **Blastocladiales**: A distinctive lineage that may be
  independent of other chytrid orders. Some analyses place
  this group outside the core Chytridiomycota, suggesting
  it represents a separate lineage of flagellated fungi.
- **Monoblepharidales**: A well-supported monophyletic
  clade consistent with zoospore ultrastructure
  characteristics.
- **Neocallimastigales**: Anaerobic chytrids found in the
  guts of herbivorous mammals, particularly ruminants. These
  fungi play an essential role in fiber digestion and are of
  significant interest for biofuel research.
- **Spizellomycetales**: A monophyletic group consistent
  with zoospore ultrastructural groupings.
- **Chytridiales**: The best-sampled members of the phylum.
  Members can be grouped using rDNA data, and the resulting
  clades are consistent with zoospore ultrastructure.

These five orders, plus four small independent groups of
chytrids, form the major taxonomic framework. Their
relationships, however, have not been fully resolved. The
zoospore ultrastructure-based classification is largely
congruent with molecular phylogenies based on mitochondrial
DNA (Paquin et al. 1997).

## Undescribed Diversity

James and colleagues (2000) predicted that substantial
additional chytrid diversity will be uncovered when more
chytrids with unique zoospore types are included in
phylogenetic analyses. Some chytrids with unique zoospore
morphologies have not been classified in previously existing
orders. Notably, [[batrachochytrium-dendrobatidis]], the chytrid
pathogen associated with global [[bloomfield-amphibian-decline-fungal-disease-climate-interactions]], does not
fall within any of the groups defined on the basis of DNA
characters. This finding underscores how little is known
about chytrid diversity and the need for comprehensive
sampling efforts.

## Amphibian Decline and Ecological Importance

A resurgence of interest in determining the diversity of
Chytridiomycota has followed the discovery that chytrids
may be an important factor in the global decline of amphibian
populations (Daszak et al. 1999, 2000; Longcore et al. 1999).
This ecological crisis has highlighted how poorly understood
 chytrid diversity remains, despite the phylum's fundamental
importance in [[bloomfield-chytrid-fungi-early-fungal-evolution]] and ecosystem functioning.
