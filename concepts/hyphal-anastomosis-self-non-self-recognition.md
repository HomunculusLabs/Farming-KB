## Self-Non-Self Recognition Systems

### Vegetative Compatibility Groups (VCGs)

Most filamentous fungi can undergo hyphal fusion only with genetically compatible individuals. Populations are subdivided into **vegetative compatibility groups (VCGs)** — also called vegetative (or somatic) compatibility (VC) groups — within which hyphal fusion occurs freely, but between which fusion is rejected.

VCG determination is controlled by multiple genetic loci:

- **Heterokaryon incompatibility (het) loci**: Typically 5–15 independent loci per species. Fusion between individuals that differ at any het locus triggers incompatibility.
- **Vic (vegetative incompatibility) loci**: Functionally equivalent to het loci; the nomenclature varies among fungal groups.
- **Mat (mating-type) loci**: In some species, the mating-type locus contributes to vegetative incompatibility.

The het loci are highly polymorphic, with dozens to hundreds of alleles per locus in natural populations. This extreme allelic diversity ensures that most encounters between genetically distinct individuals result in incompatibility, effectively maintaining genetic individuality.

### Molecular Mechanisms of Incompatibility

When incompatible hyphae undergo fusion, the incompatibility reaction is triggered:

1. **Het gene product interaction**: When het alleles from different individuals are brought into common cytoplasm, their protein products interact. In some systems, het proteins form heterodimeric complexes that are toxic.
2. **Programmed cell death (PCD)**: Incompatible fusion triggers a PCD-like response. Vacuolar collapse, cytoplasmic coagulation, and septal plugging occur within minutes to hours of fusion.
3. **[[neurospora-crassa]]*, the best-characterized het system involves the het-c and het-6/vic-2 loci:

- **HET-C**: A glycine-leucine-rich (GLR) protein with a HET domain. Different HET-C alleles encode variant GLR repeats.
- **HET-6**: A protein containing a HET domain and a coiled-coil region.
- **Incompatibility mechanism**: When HET-C and HET-6 from incompatible alleles interact, they trigger a death pathway involving the PIN-C terminal (HET) domain, which oligomerizes to form pores in membranes, causing ion imbalance and cell death.
