## Earthmoving Machinery and Tools

People have always moved earth — to reach water in dry river beds, mine pigments, excavate food as bulbs, grubs, or fungi, and bury waste or their dead. The archetypal tool is the digging stick, which provides food of greater variety and nutrition than the spear. The basic hoe, rake, and shovel exist today in most cultures, and hand tools have moved most of the earth we see today, including the vast mountain rice terraces of Asia.

## Hand Tools for Earth Moving

Hand tools remain effective for many earth-moving tasks, particularly where labour is plentiful or for fine work. Cooperative digging methods can be remarkably efficient:

- **Two-person shovel** — one digging in, the other pulling over in a see-saw motion. Rhythm and cooperation make this surprisingly productive for making miles of low irrigation banks or unloading gravels
- The technique works best with a steady rhythm — workers often sing to maintain pace

## Earthmoving Machine Attachments

The basic earth-moving attachments, each suited to particular landscape tasks, include:

### Blade
Mounted on almost any vehicle or towed. Includes "V" blades and delvers. Ideal for levelling, benching, and terracing. Can lift/drop, angle, and tilt.

### Bucket
For lifting and loading loose material. Comes in narrow, toothed versions for hard ground, or special shapes for drains. Includes swivel and scoop variants.

### Bucket Chain
For foundations, pipelines, narrow deep ditches, and underwater dredging. Continuously digs and lifts material.

### Scoop
Horse or bullock-drawn, or articulated on a hydraulic or telescopic arm. Used for pond excavation and silt removal.

### Ripper
For breaking up compacted soils. Usually towed or rear-mounted on tractors. Essential preparation before other earth-moving in hard ground.

### Delver
For one-pass drains. Often mounted on a grader or towed on a frame behind a bulldozer.

### Spinner
Rear-mounted on a special tractor. A fast-revolving disc of approximately 2-3 m diameter with peripheral buckets. Throws earth as small clods over considerable distance.

### Borers and Drills
For holes, fences, explosives, pipes, wells, bores. Range from hand post-hole diggers to large-bore mine shaft machines.

### Jet Pumps
To pump out silt and sand in wet places. Also used for drilling water bores in sands, gravels, or deep soils.

### Explosives
Of special use in marshes, swamps, and for loosening rock in quarries.

## Blade Machines

Blade earthmovers are the primary machines for levelling and benching or terracing. They should ideally be able to **lift (and drop), angle, and tilt**.

### Mounting Positions
- **Forward-mounted (bulldozer)** — for sight and control reasons
- **Centre-mounted (grader)** — for even spreading
- **Rear-mounted (wheel tractors)** — for levelling operations
- Some small blades **rotate 180°** about their axis (swivel blades)

### Blade Operations

| Operation | Effect |
|--------|--------|
| **Tilt** | Cut channels, bench slopes, road crown slopes |
| **Angle** | Cast earth to one side (side-casting), make long shallow drains |
| **Lift** | Pile up or level loads, spread and release material |
| **Drop** | Delve out drains and ditches |

### The Bulldozer

The bulldozer is a blade machine primarily for pushing and planing earth. It is of greatest use in roading and for dams. An excellent machine for:

- Putting up, rolling solid, and spreading earth
- Digging large shallow holes
- Moving small hills and benching slopes
- Side-cutting roads
- Pushing up earth walls

Even a small tractor (17-25 hp), patiently worked, can make very large dams and terraces at less fuel cost (but greater time cost) than a bulldozer. Large bulldozers are the most economical of time and can work difficult, steep, or stony sites while moving very large objects such as boulders.

### The Grader

The grader has a mid-mounted blade for even spreading. It can make long drains of shallow angle (often miscalled spoon drains, but effectively more angled than spooned in section). Best suited for long flat road or levelling runs.

### The Scraper

A large, self-filling bucket or land dredge that can both fill and empty itself to plane off or dig out large areas. All large machines can now be **laser-guided** to accurately level and grade fields at a pre-set slope. Lasers can automatically work the hydraulics to lift and drop earth. Land forming is mainly restricted to large flattish [[soil-ph|soil]]
- Cannot swivel the blade separately from the body (unlike some dedicated bucket machines)

The **Bobcat** — a small wheeled machine — serves as an excellent finishing tool for light work or for making swales in Zones 2 and 3.

## Selecting the Right Machine

For all normal bench work, a **bulldozer** is most useful. For long flat road or levelling runs, a **grader** is better. Machine selection depends on:

- **Scale** of the operation
- **Terrain** — steep, stony sites require tracked machines
- **Precision** needed — laser-guided machines for exact grades
- **Material type** — rippers needed for compacted soils, buckets for loose material
- **Access** — light swamp machines for marsh work, draglines for distance reach
