# Quick Hoops: Low-Cost Protected Cultivation Structures

## Overview

Quick hoops are low-cost, low-profile tunnel structures developed by Eliot Coleman as an economical alternative to full greenhouses for [[coleman-greenhouse-covering-materials-comparison]] the same area, quick hoops have become a cornerstone of small-scale intensive farming.

## Structural Design

### Hoop Supports
The framework consists of 10-foot lengths of conduit bent into half-circles:

- **Material options**: Either plastic or metal electrical conduit, 1/2-inch diameter
- **Plastic conduit**: Can be bent by hand as each support is installed — no special tools needed
- **Metal conduit**: Must be pre-bent to the ideal shape using a tubing bender
- **Hoop spacing**: One hoop every 5 feet along the bed length (beds typically 50 or 100 feet long)
- **Heavy snow areas**: When using plastic conduit, spacing should be reduced to 2.5 feet apart
- **Insertion depth**: Each end of the conduit is pushed about 10 inches into the soil

### Dimensions
The hoops cover two 30-inch beds side by side with a path between them, forming a tunnel approximately 30 inches high at the midpoint. Three rows of quick hoops cover the same area as a 22-by-48-foot greenhouse at a fraction of the cost.

### Covering System
Quick hoops use a dual-layer covering system that changes with the seasons:

1. **Initial cover (mid-October)**: 10-foot-wide spun-bonded [[clay-seed-pellets-direct-seeding]]
- **Scallions**: Harvested from the intermediate rows of overwintering onion beds

### Planting Timeline
- **Overwintering onions**: Planted last week of August
- **Soil block seeding for spinach/lettuce**: October 1
- **Transplant to field**: Mid-October (two weeks after seeding)
- **[[coleman-winter-greens-variety-selection]] is critical — must choose hardy enough varieties to withstand overwintering stress
- Transplants may outperform direct seeding for fall planting due to spotty outdoor germination conditions

### Spring Ventilation
When spring arrives:

- Remove a few sandbags along the southern edge
- Insert a notched prop to hold up the edges of the plastic and fabric
- By late March, when fabric alone provides sufficient protection, remove the plastic layers and store them for next winter

## Economic Analysis

### Cost Comparison
Quick hoops can be erected to cover 1,000 square feet (the area of a 22-by-48-foot greenhouse) for approximately one-twentieth (5%) of the cost of building a greenhouse. This makes them one of the most cost-effective season-extension tools available.

### Multi-Season Utility
The economic case for quick hoops extends beyond winter:

- Quick hoops used for overwintering can be disassembled and relocated to cover other crops later in spring
- Their simplicity and long season of use have completely replaced curved wire wickets on Coleman's farm
- The dual-layer system (row cover + plastic) provides different levels of protection as seasons change
