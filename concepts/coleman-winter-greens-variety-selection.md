# Winter Greens Variety Selection

Eliot Coleman's [[coleman-claytonia-winter-harvest-cultivation]] system relies on a carefully curated
selection of cold-hardy greens that thrive in unheated high tunnels
during the coldest months. These species have evolved or been bred to
survive freezing temperatures and continue producing harvestable leaves
through the short days of winter.

## Mache (Corn Salad)

Mache (Valerianella locusta) is one of the most cold-tolerant salad
greens available. It forms small rosettes of tender, nutty-flavored
leaves that remain succulent even after repeated freezing. Coleman
considers mache the king of winter salads and a cornerstone of his
winter production.

Key varieties include 'Vit' and 'Coquille de Louviers,' both of which
have been selected for [[coleman-cold-hardiness-testing-ratings]] and uniform rosette formation.
Mache germinates best in cool soil and is direct-seeded in early to
mid-fall. It grows slowly through winter but can be harvested
repeatedly as a cut-and-come-again crop.

## Claytonia (Miner's Lettuce)

Claytonia perfoliata produces distinctive round leaves surrounding
delicate white flowers. It is exceptionally cold-hardy and retains
its crisp texture and mild flavor even after hard freezes. Claytonia
is native to western [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]] and is naturally adapted to cool,
moist conditions.

Coleman seeds claytonia in September for winter harvest. It grows
vigorously in the [[darkness-low-light-facilitation-entheogenic-ceremonies]] of winter and produces abundant leaves
from November through March. The stems are also edible and add a
pleasant crunch to salad mixes. 'Giant Winter' is a preferred
variety for its larger leaf size and productivity.

## Minutina (Buckshorn Plantain)

Minutina (Plantago coronopus) produces narrow, deeply toothed leaves
with a mild, slightly salty flavor. It is extremely cold-hardy and
reliable in unheated tunnel production. Minutina grows in a low rosette
and can be harvested as individual leaves or whole small plants.

This crop is less well-known than mache or claytonia but fills an
important niche in the winter salad mix. It tolerates both extreme
cold and relatively warm conditions, making it productive across a
wide range of winter temperatures. See [[coleman-row-cover-fabric-management]].

## Arugula

Wild arugula (Diplotaxis tenuifolia) is more cold-hardy than standard
cultivated arugula and maintains its spicy flavor throughout winter.
'Sylvetta' is the most common wild-type variety. Standard arugula
(Eruca vesicaria) is also productive but may bolt or suffer more cold
damage in severe conditions.

Coleman includes arugula in his winter salad mix for its peppery
flavor that contrasts with the milder tones of mache and claytonia.
Successive plantings every two to three weeks ensure continuous supply
through the winter harvest period.

## Additional Winter Greens

Other cold-hardy greens in Coleman's winter lineup include:
- **Scallions:** 'Evergreen Hardy White' survives reliably in tunnels.
- **Turnip greens:** 'Hakurei' and 'Purple Top White Globe' produce
  tender greens through winter.
- **Tatsoi:** Forms compact rosettes that tolerate heavy freezing.
- **Mizuna:** Frilly leaves add visual appeal and mild mustard flavor.
- **Endive and escarole:** 'Blonde Full Heart' endive and 'Giant
  Fringed' escarole add bitterness to winter salads.
- **Lettuce:** Selected cold-hardy varieties like 'Winter Density'
  and 'Rouge d'Hiver' can produce in protected culture.
