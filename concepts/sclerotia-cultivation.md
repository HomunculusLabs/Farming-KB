# Sclerotia Cultivation

Sclerotia (singular: sclerotium) are dense, hardened masses of mycelium that serve as survival structures for certain fungi. In cultivation, sclerotia-producing species of *Psilocybe* can form these "truffles" directly in sealed jars without ever requiring fruiting conditions, making them one of the easiest methods for indoor cultivation. See [[psilocybin-mushroom-cultivation]] for fruiting body methods and [[mushroom-substrates]] for substrate details.

## Sclerotia-Producing Species

| Species | Potency | Sclerotia Notes | Native Range |
|---------|---------|----------------|--------------|
| *P. tampanensis* | Low (0.16% psilocybin) | Best sclerotia producer; "philosopher's stones" | Florida (originally), now cultivated worldwide |
| *P. mexicana* | Moderate | Produces both sclerotia and mushrooms; one big flush + small second | Mexico, Central America |
| *P. galindoi* | Moderate-High | Excellent sclerotia producer, higher potency than tampanensis | Mexico (high altitude grasslands) |

## Grass Seed Substrate

Grass seed (particularly rye grass, *Lolium perenne*) is the preferred substrate for sclerotia cultivation. It provides excellent nutrition, water retention, and loose structure that allows mycelium to easily colonize and form sclerotia between kernels.

### Formula (per 720 mL jar)

| Ingredient | Quantity |
|-----------|----------|
| Rye grass seed | 110 grams |
| Water | 180 mL |

### Key Considerations

- **Fungicide-free seed is essential** — most lawn seed is treated with fungicides. Buy from animal feed stores (sold as birdfeeding seed) or agricultural suppliers. The label must not indicate fungicide treatment.
- **Water absorption varies** — different seed varieties and even different batches absorb water differently. Too much water produces a slimy, unshakeable clump; too little produces dry substrate with poor yields. Experiment and adjust.
- **Pre-soak alternative** — soaking grass seed overnight before loading jars produces more homogenous hydration but requires draining before loading.
- **Mixed grass species** work well — rye grass seed blends with other species have been used successfully.

### Preparation

1. Combine grass seed and water in jars (720 mL wide-mouth jars work well)
2. Place lids on loosely — **do not screw tight** (vacuum will form during cooling if tight)
3. Cover lids with double layer of crumpled aluminum foil
4. Sterilize in pressure cooker at 15 psi for **1 hour** (2 hours for 1.5L jars or spawn bags)
5. Remove from cooker, shake jars vigorously to mix wet and dry kernels
6. Check for cracked jars before shaking
7. Allow to cool to room temperature with lids loose

## Inoculation and Colonization

- Inoculate with spore syringe, mycelium syringe, or agar squares (see [[mushroom-sterile-technique]])
- Work clean — even a cardboard box hood prevents drafts and contamination
- Flame-sterilize needles and scalpels before use
- After inoculation, close lids, shake jars, then **loosen lids again** for gas exchange
- Colonization at 20°C or slightly higher: **5-20 days** depending on species and inoculation method
- Shake jars when mycelium starts growing in only a few spots to redistribute — this dramatically speeds colonization

### Metabolite Warning

If jars are incubated too long or at too high a temperature, mycelium excretes yellowish metabolites. This indicates stress — case or harvest immediately when this occurs.

## Sclerotia Formation

Unlike fruiting body cultivation, sclerotia form **directly in the colonized jar** — no casing, no fruiting chamber, no light required.

- After full colonization, place jars in a clean, preferably dark location
- Room temperature is adequate (18-24°C)
- Sclerotia continue to enlarge for up to **4 months** after inoculation
- Yield increases significantly with time — harvest at 3-4 months for maximum yield
- No need to open jars until harvest
- Sclerotia also form in casing layers if the grain is cased for mushroom production

## Casing and Fruiting (Optional)

The same grass seed substrate can also be used to grow mushrooms by casing the colonized grain:

### Casing Soil Recipe

| Ingredient | Parts |
|-----------|-------|
| Peat | 10 |
| Vermiculite | 5 |
| Limestone (Marl) | 2 |

Mix ingredients dry, then add water while stirring until maximum moisture without turning to mud. Sterilize for 1 hour in the pressure cooker (the authors found sterilized casing outperformed pasteurized or untreated).

### Fruiting Setup

- Level colonized grain into 1L disposable plastic trays
- Cover with 1.5-2.0 cm casing soil, keeping surface rough (small valleys and hills)
- Cover with foil, incubate at 20-25°C until mycelium shows through casing
- Expose to air and light; use clear plastic bins with polyethylene sheeting and holes for air exchange
- 5 cased trays per bin; evaporation from casing surface provides humidity
- **Cold shock** harder-to-fruit strains (refrigerate overnight) — not needed for *P. mexicana*, *P. tampanensis*, or *P. subbalteatus*
- Mist daily; never let casing dry out
- Increase watering directly after each flush
- First pinheads appear 6-15 days after exposure
- Mushrooms mature in 5-7 days
- Expect 3 flushes (up to 5-6 with proper watering)
- Remove dead pinheads (aborts) immediately — they attract mold

## Sclerotia vs Fruiting Body Comparison

| Factor | Sclerotia (in-jar) | Fruiting Bodies |
|--------|-------------------|-----------------|
| Equipment needed | Jars, pressure cooker | Jars + fruiting chamber, casing supplies |
| Space | Compact (jars stack) | Requires bins/chamber |
| Time to harvest | 3-4 months | 1-2 months |
| Active compounds | Same species, lower per-weight | Higher per-weight potency |
| Contamination risk | Very low (sealed jars) | Higher (open air fruiting) |
| Effort | Minimal after inoculation | Daily misting, monitoring |
| Yield per jar | Moderate (varies by species) | Multiple flushes possible |
| Species available | P. tampanensis, P. mexicana, P. galindoi | All psilocybin species |

## Harvesting and Storage

- Open jar, separate sclerotia from grain by hand
- Rinse briefly with cold water to remove substrate debris
- Pat dry with paper towels
- Store fresh in refrigerator for 1-2 weeks
- For long-term storage: dehydrate at low temperature (30-35°C) until hard, store in airtight container with desiccant. See [[mushroom-drying-storage]] for methods.
- Sclerotia have firm, nut-like texture when fresh — unlike the fragile fruiting bodies
