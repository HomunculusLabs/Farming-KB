## Psilocybe Southern Hemisphere Origin Hypothesis

Guzman, Allen, and Gartz (1998) proposed that the genus Psilocybe and
related [[neurotropic-fungi]] may have originated in the southern hemisphere,
primarily in South America, based on the markedly higher species diversity

states with suitable habitat (Arizona, Colorado, Illinois, Maryland,
Vermont, Massachusetts, New Hampshire, Pennsylvania) also lack records,
likely due to limited mycological survey effort rather than genuine
absence.

## Disjunct Species and Trans-Pacific Relationships

Some of the most compelling evidence for southern hemisphere origins
comes from disjunct species pairs that link Mesoamerica/South America
with Southeast Asia, Australia, and New Zealand through shared ancestry:

### Section Mexicanae Connections

- **Psilocybe samuiensis** (Thailand) and **P. makarorae** (New Zealand)
  are both closely related to **P. mexicana** (Mexico and Guatemala).
  All three belong to Section Mexicanae, suggesting an ancient
  distribution that predates continental drift or was facilitated by
  long-distance dispersal.

### Section Zapotecorum Connections

- **Psilocybe aucklandii** (New Zealand) is closely related to
  **P. zapotecorum** from Mexico and South America. Both species belong
  to Section Zapotecorum.
