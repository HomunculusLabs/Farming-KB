# Cannabidiol (CBD) Therapeutic Profile

## Overview

Cannabidiol (CBD) is a non-psychoactive cannabinoid sourced from the cannabis
plant. Unlike THC, CBD does not produce the characteristic cannabis "high" but
exhibits a remarkably broad therapeutic profile. CBD has a greater affinity for
CB2 receptors than CB1 receptors, but much of its therapeutic influence stems
from its ability to suppress the enzyme fatty acid amide hydrolase (FAAH) that
breaks down anandamide, thus keeping the "Bliss Molecule" active at higher
concentrations and for a longer duration. At the same time, CBD tames the
psychoactive influence of THC, allowing patients to focus on THC's other
therapeutic powers at higher concentrations.

## Mechanism of Action

While the full complexity of how CBD interacts with the body's endocannabinoid
system is yet to be revealed, CBD operates through multiple complementary
mechanisms beyond direct [[psilocybin-pharmacology-and-serotonin-receptors]] to achieve
  a calming effect on autonomic stress responses such as rapid heart rates.
- **TRPV1 modulation**: CBD interacts with the vanilloid receptor system,
  influencing pain perception and inflammatory signaling.
- **PPAR-gamma activation**: CBD activates nuclear receptors involved in
  lipid metabolism and inflammation regulation.

## Neurological Applications

CBD is considered a very promising agent with the highest prospect for
therapeutic use in the treatment of neurodegenerative illness. Key neurological
applications include:

### Epilepsy and Seizure Disorders

An oil-based solution of CBD has been documented as effective for pediatric
patients suffering from epileptic seizures who failed to respond to traditional
pharmaceutical anti-seizure medications. This represents one of the most
well-documented clinical applications of CBD and has led to the FDA approval
of Epidiolex for certain forms of childhood epilepsy.

### Neuroprotection

CBD may also be useful in preventing nerve damage associated with alcohol
poisoning, demonstrating its capacity to protect neural tissue from toxic
insult through multiple neuroprotective mechanisms including antioxidant
activity and anti-inflammatory signaling.
