# Mycoremediation of PAH and PCB Degradation

## Overview

[[singh-fungal-biodegradation-of-polycyclic-aromatic-hydrocarbons]] hydrocarbons (PAHs) and polychlorinated biphenyls (PCBs) are among the most persistent and toxic environmental contaminants. PAHs are generated by incomplete combustion of [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]] (vehicle exhaust, industrial processes, [[rocket-stove-design]], grilled food), while PCBs were manufactured as industrial insulating fluids and plasticizers before being banned in most countries by the late 1970s. Both compound classes are hydrophobic, tend to accumulate in sediments and organic matter, and resist biological degradation due to their stable aromatic ring structures.

White-rot fungi are uniquely capable of degrading these recalcitrant compounds through their extracellular ligninolytic enzyme systems, which are non-specific oxidizers capable of attacking the carbon-carbon bonds in aromatic rings.

### Degradation Mechanisms
- **Laccase oxidation:** Laccases from Trametes, Pleurotus, and Coriolopsis species can oxidize low-molecular-weight PAHs (2-3 rings) directly. Higher-molecular-weight PAHs (4-5 rings) require mediator compounds
