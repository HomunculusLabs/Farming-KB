# Oyster Mushroom Fruiting Chamber Environment and Conditions

## From the Mushroom Growers' Handbook 1

After substrate colonization completes, oyster mushrooms require specific
environmental conditions to trigger fruiting — the transition from vegetative
mycelial growth to reproductive mushroom formation. Managing these conditions
in a fruiting chamber or growing house is the primary operational challenge
in commercial mushroom cultivation.

## The Fruiting Trigger

Several environmental cues signal mycelium to initiate fruiting. The primary
triggers are: a drop in temperature (typically 5-10°C below colonization
temperature), introduction of fresh air with elevated CO2 exchange, exposure
to light (even low levels suffice), and high relative humidity (85-95%).
These cues mimic the natural conditions that would trigger fruiting in the wild.

The handbook emphasizes that all four cues should be applied simultaneously
or in rapid sequence for best results. Applying only partial cues produces
abnormal fruiting bodies — long stems, small caps, or no fruiting at all.

## Temperature Management

Different Pleurotus species have different fruiting temperature ranges.
P. ostreatus fruits at 10-20°C, P. pulmonarius at 20-30°C, P. sajor-caju at
25-35°C, and P. cystidiosus at 20-28°C. Selecting the right species for local
climate conditions is essential for low-cost production without climate control.

Temperature fluctuations of more than 5°C within a 24-hour period can cause
deformed mushrooms or aborting pins. The handbook recommends insulated growing
structures and passive ventilation for small-scale operations, with active
heating or cooling reserved for commercial facilities.

## Humidity Control

Relative humidity must remain between 85-95% throughout the fruiting period.
Below 80%, pin formation fails and existing mushrooms crack or dry out.
Above 95%, condensation promotes bacterial blotch disease and excessive
moisture on mushroom surfaces reduces shelf life and market quality.

Common humidification methods include fogging nozzles, misting systems,
[[bloomfield-mushroom-evaporative-cooling-and-thermal-regulation]] pads, and simple manual spraying. The handbook describes
a low-cost system using wetted burlap walls that maintains adequate humidity
in tropical growing houses without mechanical equipment.

## Fresh Air Exchange

Oyster mushrooms are extremely sensitive to CO2 concentration. During
colonization, high CO2 (5,000-10,000 ppm) encourages mycelial growth.
During fruiting, CO2 must drop below 800-1,000 ppm. Elevated CO2 during
fruiting produces the characteristic long-stemmed, small-capped morphology
that reduces market value and yield.

Fresh air exchange also provides evaporative cooling, reduces surface moisture
on mushrooms, and helps prevent disease. The challenge is providing sufficient
air exchange while maintaining high humidity — a fundamental tension in
[[mushroom-house-design]].

## Light Requirements

Oyster mushrooms need light for normal fruiting body development, but
requirements are modest. Indirect ambient light (50-200 lux) or 8-12 hours
of fluorescent light per day is sufficient. Direct sunlight should be avoided
as it causes overheating and drying. Light influences cap color, stem length,
and direction of growth — mushrooms grow toward light sources.

## Pinning and Fruiting Stages

After [[fungal-fruiting-body-formation-environmental-triggers]] are applied, primordia (tiny mushroom initials
called pins) form within 3-7 days. This pinning stage is the most sensitive
period — temperature fluctuations, drafts, or physical disturbance can abort
pins. The handbook recommends minimal intervention during pinning.

Pins develop into harvestable mushrooms in 5-10 days depending on species
and conditions. Most Pleurotus species produce 2-4 flushes (harvest waves)
with diminishing yields per flush. Total cropping period spans 4-6 weeks.

## Growing House Design Principles

The handbook describes several growing house designs suited to different scales
and budgets. The simplest design is a modified bamboo or plastic tunnel house
with adjustable side ventilation. More sophisticated designs incorporate
insulated walls, evaporative cooling pads, exhaust fans, and automated
humidity control systems.

Regardless of design complexity, the handbook identifies three non-negotiable
features: adequate drainage (prevents waterlogging), adjustable ventilation
(for CO2 control without excessive humidity loss), and shading (prevents
overheating while allowing ambient light). The total cost of a basic growing
house for 500-1,000 bags ranges from NPR 15,000 to 50,000 in the Nepal
context.

## Harvesting Technique

Oyster mushrooms should be harvested when the cap margin begins to uncurl
from downward to upward — typically when caps are 5-15 cm diameter depending
on species. Harvesting too early reduces yield; too late compromises texture
and shelf life. The handbook recommends twisting or cutting at the base rather
than pulling, which can damage the substrate surface and reduce subsequent
flush yields.

Post-harvest handling significantly affects market quality. Mushrooms should
be cooled immediately and kept at 2-4°C for maximum shelf life (7-14 days).
Packaging in perforated plastic bags or cartons prevents moisture loss while
allowing respiration. The handbook notes that oyster mushrooms deteriorate
rapidly at room temperature, losing market value within 24-48 hours.

## Pest and Disease Management

Major pests include fungus gnats, phorid flies, and mites. The handbook
recommends integrated pest management combining physical barriers (screened
vents, sealed doors), sanitation (removing spent substrate promptly), and
biological controls ([[bacillus-thuringiensis]] for larval fly control). Chemical
pesticides are strongly discouraged due to residue concerns on food products.

The most common diseases are bacterial blotch ([[pseudomonas-tolaasii-entity-the-mushroom-cultivator]]),
Trichoderma green mold contamination, and various mycogone infections.
Prevention through proper environmental management — especially humidity
control and fresh air exchange — is more effective than treatment after
infection appears.
