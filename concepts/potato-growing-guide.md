# Potato Growing Guide

Potatoes ([[solanum-tuberosum]]) are a high-yielding, calorie-dense staple crop well-suited to home gardens. This guide covers seed potato selection, hilling methods, container growing, harvest timing, storage, and popular varieties.

## Seed Potatoes

### What Are Seed Potatoes?
- Small potatoes or potato pieces grown specifically for planting.
- Certified disease-free seed potatoes from a reputable supplier are essential.
- Do NOT plant supermarket potatoes — they may carry disease and are often treated with sprout inhibitors.

### Chitting (Pre-Sprouting)
- 2-4 weeks before planting, place seed potatoes in a warm (60-70°F), bright location.
- Eyes will develop short, green sprouts.
- Chitting gives a 1-2 week head start and improves yield.
- Place potatoes "rose end up" (end with the most eyes facing up).
- Egg cartons or shallow trays work well for chitting.

### Cutting Seed Potatoes
- Large seed potatoes (2+ ounces) can be cut into pieces.
- Each piece must have at least 2-3 eyes.
- Cut 2-3 days before planting; let cut surfaces dry and form a callus (reduces rot).
- Dust cut surfaces with sulfur powder or wood ash to prevent fungal infection.
- Small seed potatoes (under 2 oz) can be planted whole.

## Planting

### Timing
- Plant 2-4 weeks before average last frost date.
- [[soil-temperature]] should be at least 45°F.
- Potatoes are frost-tolerant; new shoots may die back from a light freeze but will regrow.

### Soil Preparation
- Loose, well-drained, slightly acidic soil (pH 5.0-6.0 is ideal).
- Avoid lime — higher pH promotes scab disease.
- Work in 2-3 inches of compost before planting.
- Avoid fresh manure — promotes scab.

### Planting Depth and Spacing
- Dig a trench 4-6 inches deep.
- Place seed potatoes 10-14 inches apart in the trench, eyes facing up.
- Rows 24-36 inches apart.
- Cover with 3-4 inches of soil.

## Hilling Methods

Hilling is essential — potatoes form on stolons (underground stems) ABOVE the seed piece, not below it. If tubers are exposed to sunlight, they turn green and produce solanine (toxic).

### Traditional Hilling (In-Ground)
1. When plants are 6-8 inches tall, mound soil around the base to cover all but the top 2-3 inches of foliage.
2. Repeat every 2-3 weeks as plants grow.
3. Final hill should be 8-12 inches above the original soil level.
4. Use a hoe, rake, or draw hoe to pull soil from between rows.

### Straw Hilling (No-Dig)
- Instead of soil, hill with 6-12 inches of straw or hay as plants grow.
- Tubers form in the clean straw — no digging required at harvest.
- Fewer pest problems (Colorado potato beetles have more trouble in straw).
- Easier harvest — simply pull back straw.
- Risk: if straw is too thin, tubers may green-expose; mice may nest in straw.

### Container Growing
Potatoes grow very well in containers — ideal for small spaces and patios.

**Containers:**
- Grow bags (fabric pots), 10-20 gallon minimum.
- Buckets (5 gallon for 1-2 plants; drill drainage holes).
- Wire cages lined with straw.
- Tires (stacked and filled with soil — avoid if tires are old and may leach toxins).

**Container method:**
1. Add 4-6 inches of soil/compost mix to bottom of container.
2. Place 1-3 seed potatoes on surface, eyes up.
3. Cover with 3-4 inches of soil.
4. As shoots grow 6-8 inches, add more soil until container is full.
5. Water consistently — containers dry out fast.
6. Harvest by dumping the container when plants die back.

## Care During Growth

- **Watering**: 1-2 inches per week. Consistent moisture is critical during tuber formation (when plants flower). Irregular watering causes knobby or split tubers.
- **Fertilization**: Potatoes are moderate feeders. Side-dress with compost or a balanced organic fertilizer when hilling. Avoid excess nitrogen — promotes foliage over tubers.
- **Pest management**: Colorado potato beetle is the most common pest. Hand-pick adults and larvae. Neem oil or spinosad for heavy infestations. Floating row covers prevent early-season infestation.
- **Disease**: Early blight and [[late-blight]] are the main threats. Remove affected foliage. Do not plant potatoes near tomatoes (shared disease complex).

## Harvesting

### New Potatoes
- Can be harvested 7-8 weeks after planting (when plants flower).
- Gently dig into the side of the hill and remove a few tubers without disturbing the plant.
- Smaller, thinner-skinned, sweeter — best eaten immediately.

### Mature Potatoes (Main Crop)
- Wait until foliage has died back and turned brown (90-120 days from planting).
- Stop watering 2 weeks before harvest to cure skins.
- Harvest on a dry day.
- Use a digging fork, starting 12-18 inches from the plant base.
- Lift gently — avoid spearing tubers.
- Let potatoes dry on the ground for 1-2 hours in a shaded spot (do not leave in direct sun).

## Curing and Storage

### Curing
- Cure unwashed potatoes in a dark, humid (85-90%), warm (55-65°F) location for 2 weeks.
- Curing thickens the skin for storage and heals minor cuts.
- A basement, garage, or shed works well. Cover loosely with burlap or newspaper.
- Do NOT wash potatoes before curing or storage — moisture promotes rot.

### Storage
- After curing, move to a cool (38-42°F), dark, well-ventilated space.
- Humidity: 85-95% (prevents shriveling).
- Do NOT refrigerate — cold converts starches to sugar, affecting flavor and cooking.
- Do NOT store near onions — onions release gases that cause potatoes to sprout.
- Check monthly; remove any sprouting or rotting potatoes immediately.
- Storage life: 2-6 months depending on variety and conditions.

## Common Problems

| Problem | Cause | Prevention |
|---------|-------|------------|
| Green potatoes | Sunlight exposure | Hill adequately, keep tubers covered |
| Scab (brown corky spots) | High [[soil-ph]], fresh manure | Keep pH 5.0-6.0, avoid lime, use aged compost |
| Knobby tubers | Inconsistent watering | Water consistently, especially during tubering |
| Hollow heart | Rapid growth after dry period | Consistent watering |
| Wireworm holes | Wireworms in soil | Rotate crops, avoid planting after grass sod |
| Late blight | [[phytophthora-infestans]] | Fungicide (copper), remove infected plants, good airflow |
