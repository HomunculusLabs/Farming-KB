Solomon recommends digging a test hole at least 36 inches (90 cm) deep. Observe:

- Topsoil layer (usually darker, 6-12 inches): the fertile surface layer
- Subsoil: typically lighter in color, may be clayey
- Root penetration: can roots from nearby vegetation reach deep?
- Layer barriers: hardpans or dense layers that block moisture and roots

The subsoil type is critical. A sandy topsoil over open, penetrable clay subsoil can hold large moisture reserves. A hardpan or dense layer acts as a complete barrier to capillary moisture rise.

## Tools and Sharpening

Only three tools are essential: a combination shovel (or spade), a bow rake, and a hoe. A 10-inch mill bastard file for sharpening.

Sharpening a shovel:
- Hold at 15-degree angle, file from center outward to each side
- Create a chisel-like bevel
- Remove the wire edge with light strokes on the back at 5 degrees
- A sharp shovel cuts into soil with body weight, not brute force

Discount-store tools are false economy. Buy professional-grade tools from landscapers' or nursery supply houses. They cost more but last for years of daily use.

## Raised Beds: Solomon's Approach

Solomon uses 4-foot-wide (1.25 m) raised beds for most of his garden. These are created by digging, incorporating amendments, and raking soil up from paths into beds. The beds are 4-6 inches higher than the paths.

He explicitly does NOT recommend:
- Deep double-digging (as promoted by Jeavons) -- unnecessary effort for most soils
- Imported raised beds filled with purchased soil mixtures -- expensive and can have mineral imbalances
- [[fukuoka-textdoc-composting-critique-futility-prepared-organic-matter]], loosening compaction -- helps the soil warm faster.
