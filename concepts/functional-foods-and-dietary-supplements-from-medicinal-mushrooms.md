# Functional Foods and Dietary Supplements from Medicinal Mushrooms

The intersection of nutrition science, traditional medicine, and modern pharmacology has positioned [[medicinal-mushrooms]] as both functional foods and sources of pharmaceutical-grade bioactive compounds. This concept explores the scientific framework, key species, and bioactive chemistry that underpins their therapeutic potential.

## Functional Food Science

### Definition and Paradigm
"Scientific evidence supports the view that diet controls and modulates many functions of the human body and, accordingly, participates in the maintenance of the state of good health or homeostasis."

Functional food science has evolved from awareness of the diet-disease relationship. A food is functional if it "contains a food component (whether a nutrient or not) which affects one or more identified functions in the body in a positive manner." The US Academy of Science defines functional foods as those that "encompass potentially healthful products" including "any modified food or food ingredient that may provide a health benefit beyond the traditional nutrients it contains."

### Distinction from Pharmaceuticals
Functional food science is distinct from medical/pharmaceutical sciences:
- **Functional food science**: Maintain good health, improve homeostasis, create conditions for disease risk reduction
- **Pharmaceutical science**: Cure or control diseases

"Conventional medicine seeks to eliminate disease rather than to fortify the patient."

### Dietary Supplement Definition
Formally defined by US DSHEA 1994: a product intended to supplement the diet to enhance health, including minerals, amino acids, vitamins, herbs or other botanicals, in capsule, powder, softgel or gel cap form.

### Nomenclature
Functional foods come under many names: dietary supplements, nutraceuticals, medical foods, vita foods, pharmafoods, phytochemicals, mycochemicals, biochemopreventatives, designer foods. The term "dietary supplement" is now most widely accepted.

## Mushroom Cultivation

### Commercial Scale
About 35 mushroom species cultivated commercially, ~20 on industrial scale. Most are both edible and possess medicinal properties. Cultivation involves: strain [[solomon-garden-tools-selection-and-maintenance]], [[spawn-production]], mushroom production (log culture and enriched sawdust culture), crop management. Mycelium production by liquid tank fermentation increasingly used for uniform medicinal products.

## Polysaccharide Chemistry

### Structural Classes
The anti-tumour polysaccharides isolated from mushrooms are:
1. **Water-soluble β-D-glucans**: Linear or branched glucose polymers
2. **β-D-glucans with heterosaccharide chains**: Of xylose, mannose, galactose or uronic acid
3. **β-D-glucan-protein complexes (proteoglycans)**: Protein-bound glucans

Some are orally bioavailable.

### Extraction and Purification
Methods are now well worked out. The main medically important polysaccharides that have undergone clinical trials include:
- **Lentinan** (Lentinus edodes)
- **Schizophyllan** (Schizophyllum commune)
- **PSK and PSP** (Trametes versicolor)
- **Grifron-D** (Grifola frondosa)

## Immunomodulation Mechanisms

### Innate Immune Activation
Mushroom compounds potentiate innate (non-specific) immune responses by activating:
- **Cytotoxic macrophages**: Enhanced phagocytosis and tumour cell killing
- **Monocytes**: Cytokine production
- **Neutrophils**: Bactericidal/tumouricidal activity
- **Natural killer (NK) cells**: Cytotoxicity against tumour/virus-infected cells
- **Dendritic cells**: Antigen presentation

### Chemical Messengers
- **Cytokines**: Interleukins, interferon, colony stimulating factors
- **Complement system**: Opsonization and cell lysis
- **Acute phase response**: Systemic inflammatory response

### Acquired Immune Activation
- **B-cells**: Antibody production
- **T-cells**: Cell-mediated cytotoxicity
- **Multi-cytokine induction**: Gene expression of immunomodulatory cytokines and cytokine receptors

### T-Cell Oriented Immunopotentiators
**Lentinan and Schizophyllan**: Require functional T-cell component. Work by increasing helper T-cell production, increased macrophage production, stimulation of acute phase proteins and colony stimulating factors, affecting proliferation of macrophages/neutrophils/lymphocytes, and activating complement system.

### Broad Immunostimulators
**PSK and PSP**: Potent activity for T-cells and antigen-presenting cells (monocytes, macrophages). Characterized by increased white blood cell counts, interferon-γ and interleukin-2 production, and delayed type hypersensitivity reactions.

## Clinical Applications

### Dual Mode of Action
- **In vivo**: Strong evidence for immunomodulating mode of action
- **In vitro**: Some polysaccharides show direct cytotoxic effects on cancer cells, but not all

### Route of Administration
While all proprietary mushroom polysaccharides are effective by IV route, several can also be effective orally.

## Safety Advantages Over Herbal Products

1. **Commercially cultivated** (not wild-gathered): Guarantees proper identification and relatively pure, unadulterated products
2. **Vegetatively propagated**: Kept to one clone; mycelium stored long-term; genetic and biochemical consistency checked over time
3. **Fermenter-grown**: Controlled conditions with improved product purity—important future trend

## Regulatory Landscape

Current laws on dietary supplements discussed for Europe, Japan, and US. Safety cannot be guaranteed simply from centuries of historical use—modern cautionary approach adopted while indicating the way forward for expanding mushroom dietary supplement and pharmaceutical products.
