# Compost Biology and Organisms

Updated: 2026-04-18

Compost is a whole universe of diverse [[bacterial-vs-fungal-soil-dynamics]]).

### Bacterial Compost

Bacterial compost is made with high-nitrogen "green" materials:
- Grass clippings
- Kitchen scraps (vegetable)
- Green manures
- Coffee grounds
- Young, succulent plant material

These materials have lower C:N ratios and support rapid bacterial growth.
Bacterial compost is preferred for vegetables, annuals, and lawns -- plants
that prefer nitrate nitrogen.

### Fungal Compost

Fungal compost requires more "brown" materials:
- Autumn leaves
- Wood chips and sawdust
- Shredded bark
- Straw and hay
- Branches and woody material

These materials are higher in lignin and cellulose, which fungi excel at
breaking down. Fungal compost is preferred for trees, shrubs, and perennials
-- plants that prefer ammonium nitrogen.

## Composting Stages

### Mesophilic Stage (68-104F / 20-40C)

Moderate-temperature bacteria begin the process, breaking cellulose chains
into smaller glucose units (depolymerization). Brown rot fungi and certain
bacteria (Bacillus, Heliospirillum) start breaking down resistant materials.

### Thermophilic Stage (104-155F / 40-68C)

High-temperature organisms take over. Complex carbohydrates and proteins are
fully decomposed. Hemicelluloses are broken down. The pile should be kept
between 140-150F for at least a few days to kill pathogens and weed seeds.
Never exceed 155F or carbon starts burning off.

### Maturation Stage

As food supplies diminish, temperatures drop and mesophilic organisms
reassert themselves. Lignin decomposition is completed by fungi. Nematodes,
springtails, and centipedes arrive, grazing on bacteria and fungi and causing
their populations to increase. Worms colonize the cooling pile. This stage
produces the humus that gives finished compost its dark, crumbly texture.

## Compost as Inoculant

Compost's primary role in soil food web gardening is as an inoculant. The
organisms in applied compost spread life through the soil. Compost can be used
to introduce, maintain, or alter the soil food web in a particular area. By
choosing the right type of compost (bacterial or fungal), you can match the
soil biology to the plants you are growing.

## Key Requirements

Successful composting requires:
- Heat (generated by microbial metabolism, primarily bacterial)
- Water (for microbial transport and life functions)
- Air (beneficial decomposers are aerobic; anaerobic conditions produce
  alcohols toxic to plants at just 1 ppm)
- Organic materials with appropriate C:N ratio (around 30:1 is ideal)
