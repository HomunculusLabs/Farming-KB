## The Absence of No-Fertilizer Tests in Agricultural Science

## The Missing Baseline

Fukuoka identifies a fundamental methodological flaw in [[comparison-grafting-vs-budding-fruit-trees]] without fertilizer, and tests on rice and other grains used no-fertilizer conditions only as controls — not as the primary subject of investigation.

This is not an accidental oversight. It reflects a deep structural bias in how agricultural science defines its domain of inquiry.

## Why No-Fertilizer Tests Are Not Performed

The reason is both ideological and institutional. Scientists work from the basic premise that "crops are to be grown with fertilizer." Within this framework, no-fertilizer cultivation is self-evidently absurd. Fukuoka quotes the implicit attitude: "Why experiment with such an idiotic and dangerous method of cultivation?"

This circular logic ensures that the assumption (fertilizer is necessary) is never tested:

1. We assume fertilizer is necessary
2. Therefore, we only design experiments that use fertilizer
3. These experiments show results consistent with fertilizer use
4. This confirms our original assumption
5. Why would we test something so obviously wrong?

The question of whether crops can grow without fertilizer — the most fundamental question in plant nutrition — is simply never asked because the answer is presumed in advance.

## The Wrong Standard of Comparison

The standard against which fertilizer experiments should be measured is the no-fertilizer condition. This is the null hypothesis of agriculture: can plants grow on their own? But instead, agricultural science uses three-element tests (nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium) as its standard, comparing different fertilizer formulations and doses against each other.

This means the entire edifice of fertilizer science rests on comparisons between different artificial conditions, none of which are compared against the natural condition they claim to improve upon.

## Flawed No-Fertilizer Experiments

The few no-fertilizer experiments that have been conducted are deeply compromised by their methodology:

### Dead Soil Conditions
Crops are planted in small earthenware pots or artificial concrete enclosures. The soil in such containers is dead soil — devoid of the [[living-soil-cannabis]].

### Root Confinement
The growth of trees whose roots are boxed in by concrete is "highly unnatural." A tree"s root system in a pot bears no resemblance to its root system in natural soil, where roots can extend many meters in multiple directions, interacting with countless soil organisms.

### Invalid Extrapolation
From these impoverished conditions, scientists conclude that plants grown without fertilizer produce only about half as much growth, and that yields are "terrible — on the order of one-third that obtained with fertilizers." But this extrapolation from dead potted soil to living field conditions is methodologically indefensible.

Fukuoka writes: "It is unreasonable to claim that because plants grown without fertilizer in such an enclosure grow poorly, they cannot be grown without fertilizers."
