## Permaculture Nursery Management

Permaculture nursery management is the establishment and operation of plant nurseries within permaculture systems for the propagation, cultivation, and distribution of food plants, timber trees, nitrogen fixers, [[permplant-willows-salix-erosion-control-fodder]], and the establishment of sustainable agricultural systems.

## Nursery Siting and Design

The nursery should be located in Zone 1, close to the house for easy daily attention. Access to water is essential, as nursery plants require regular irrigation. Protection from strong winds is important, as young seedlings are vulnerable to desiccation and physical damage. Good drainage prevents waterlogging of pots and beds. Proximity to composting facilities reduces the labour of moving potting media materials.

The nursery layout should include a propagation area for seed sowing and cuttings under cover, a growing-on area with shade protection for developing seedlings, a hardening-off area where plants are gradually exposed to full sun before planting out, a potting and mixing area with storage for media components, a storage area for pots, tools, labels, and supplies, and an irrigation system with fine nozzles or misters for seedling care.

## Propagation Structures

Propagation structures provide the controlled environment needed for successful plant propagation. Cold frames are simple, unheated structures that protect seedlings and cuttings from frost and wind while allowing natural light. A cold frame consists of a bottomless box with a transparent lid, typically made from recycled windows or polycarbonate sheet.

Hot beds use the heat generated by decomposing [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi-biology-symbiosis]], which can be added to the potting mix as a soil inoculant from established forest soil.

Compost for nursery use should be fully decomposed, fine-textured, and free of weed seeds and pathogens. Sieving compost through a mesh removes coarse material and produces a uniform fine medium. Worm castings from vermicompost systems provide an excellent potting media component with high nutrient content and beneficial microbial populations.

## Irrigation Systems

Nursery irrigation must deliver water gently and evenly to avoid displacing seeds, damaging seedlings, or creating waterlogged conditions. Overhead misting systems provide fine droplets that maintain high humidity around cuttings and seedlings. Drip irrigation delivers water directly to the root zone of each pot, reducing water waste and foliage wetting.

Hand watering with a fine-rose watering can is appropriate for small nurseries. The rose attachment breaks the water stream into gentle droplets. Watering should be done in the morning to allow foliage to dry before nightfall, reducing fungal disease risk.

Water quality matters for nursery plants. Rainwater collected from nursery roofs is generally the best quality. Bore or mains water may contain salts or chlorine that [[query-how-to-prevent-transplant-shock-when-moving-cannabis-plants]] and improves survival rates.

## Record Keeping

Nursery record keeping is essential for managing the propagation calendar and tracking plant performance. Records should include species name and source, date of sowing or striking, germination rate and date, potting-up dates, growth observations, planting-out date and location, and performance in the field. This information builds a knowledge base that improves nursery efficiency over time.

## Income Generation

Permaculture nurseries can generate income through plant sales to the community. Surplus seedlings of vegetables, herbs, fruit trees, and ornamental plants can be sold at farmers markets, through community networks, or at the nursery gate. Speciality plants including rare food species, medicinal herbs, and indigenous plants often command premium prices. Nursery income can contribute to household economics and fund further permaculture development.
