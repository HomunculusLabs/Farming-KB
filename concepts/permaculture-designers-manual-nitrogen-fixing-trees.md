# Nitrogen-Fixing Trees

> Source: Bill Mollison, *Permaculture: A Designers' Manual* (1988), Chapters 5, 8, and 10

Updated: 2026-04-18

## Overview

The amount of nitrogen fixed has usually been underestimated, at 75-100 kg of nitrogen/ha/year, but efficient legumes such as lucerne (alfalfa) may provide 250-500 kg of nitrogen/ha/year, and tree legumes such as Albizzia as much in quite poor sandy soils.

Every part of such legumes as Leucaena, Acacia, Albizia, Gliricidia and Tephrosia may contain high nitrogen levels; one can actually smell the ammonia from the trees in rain or when the roots are crushed.

## Nitrogen Release Mechanisms

As nitrogen-fixing organisms and the roots to which they attach are in a constant process of death and replacement over a growing season, much of the nitrogen is also released for use by other plant species. Clover and tree legumes perform the same benefit for pastures. The rain tree (Samanea saman) can preserve green grass below even in dry seasons.

Some shrubs and trees lay down about a 9-year supply of nitrogen, and if cut or ringbarked, the slow decay of the roots gives up nitrogen for 6 years or so. Nitrogen, if supplied artificially, quickly leaches in warm rains, so legumes, with their slow nitrogen release, are of critical importance in any tropical crop situation.

## Green Mulch Application

Cut green material from nitrogen-fixing trees supplies much of the nitrogen for crops. It is necessary to make sure the trees are inoculated as seed with the correct root associates, in the nursery or in the field. Most agricultural departments can supply a list of strains of inoculants. Soil from nodulating trees can be washed in around newly planted trees, or mixed with potting soils.

## Key Nitrogen-Fixing Tree Species

### Acacia Species
- Acacia albida: African dryland nitrogen fixer, used as biological "chisel plough"
- Acacia mearnsii: tropical pioneer, rapid establishment
- Acacia tortilis: ideal for cut-and-build fences, established from cuttings even in arid conditions
- Multiple uses: mulch, forage, windbreak, soil conditioning

### Leucaena leucocephala
- Premier tropical nitrogen-fixing tree
- High leaf nitrogen, coppices readily
- Forage and mulch production
- Interplant with elephant grass (Pennisetum purpureum)

### Albizia Species
- Albizzia: can fix as much nitrogen as lucerne in poor sandy soils
- Large Albizia: provides high shade for pens and crops
- Used in savannah forestry systems

### Other Tree Legumes
- **Gliricidia**: nitrogen-fixing, green mulch, forage
- **Tephrosia**: yielding 13.5 t/ha in 4 cuts; leaf nitrogen about 20-30 kg/t, giving 1,000-1,500 kg/ha/year
- **Prosopis**: arid zone nitrogen fixer, forage pods
- **Erythrina**: nitrogen-fixing, mulch, forage
- **Casuarina**: biological chisel plough, nitrogen fixer, coastal pioneer
- **Caragana arborescens** (Siberian pea shrub): important tree forage legume of cool and cold climates
- **Elaeagnus umbellata** (autumn olive): turkey forage, berry production, nitrogen fixer
- **Tagasaste**: cool temperate forage and mulch, 1m x 2m spacing, yields up to 7m mulch trimmings/year

## Inoculation and Nodulation

Nodulation of nitrogen-fixing bacteria is greatly increased by soil aeration (via chisel ploughs). Keyline chisel plough patterns create S-curves directing water to ridges, also increasing nitrogen fixation.

Conditions for fixing nitrogen are enhanced by growing nitrogen-fixing plants inoculated with the appropriate rhizobia. Much higher levels of nitrogen than were previously estimated can be achieved.

## Management Considerations

### Spacing
- With shrubs and small trees, 0.5 m apart is best for foliage production
- Trimming height of 0.5-1.5 m is recommended

### Seasonal Timing
- Only in frost-free tropics can trimming occur all year (4-5 cuts)
- Where cold is seasonal, two months of growth must be allowed to harden the plant before winter
- Trees in drought can be part-trimmed

### Forage Caution
Young coppice shoots may have higher levels of metabolic poisons than 2-3 year old shoots. If stock do not thrive, this factor should be assessed.

### Shelter Spacing
- Trees more widely spaced for root nitrogen, seed production (poultry and bees), and in-crop shelter
- Flowering and seed production better at 2-20 m spacing
- Full canopy needed to reduce or eliminate frost

### Replacement
- Many trees coppice for 4-30 years
- Any sign of loss of vigour indicates need to replant
- Small shrub replant may be necessary every 2-3 years

## Total Nitrogen Assessment

To assess total nitrogen yield:
- Soil nitrogen from mycelia: approximately 200 kg/ha/year
- Leaf and slash nitrogen/ha from legume mulch
- Tephrosia example: 1,000-1,500 kg/ha/year from leaf nitrogen alone

Phosphate and potash levels in green mulch are also satisfactory for crop production. This is the whole rationale for avenue cropping and legume mulch.
