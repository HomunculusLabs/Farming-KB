## Preparation Methods

### Stump Selection
Ideal stumps are freshly cut (within one to six months), from healthy trees, with intact
bark. Diameters of 30 cm or greater provide sufficient wood volume. Cut close to ground
level to maximize inoculation surface area.

### Drilling and Inoculating
1. **Drilling:** Using a 12 mm bit, create holes in a grid on the top face, spaced
   10–15 cm apart, 4–5 cm deep, angled slightly outward. For large stumps, also drill
   into sides near the cambium.
2. **Spawn placement:** Firmly pack holes with sawdust spawn (preferred) or plug spawn.
   Sawdust spawn offers faster colonization and better substrate contact.
3. **Sealing:** Cover each hole with melted cheese wax or food-grade paraffin to seal
   moisture and exclude competitors. For exposed stumps, wax the entire cut face.

### Inoculation Timing
Inoculate as soon as possible after felling, ideally within the first growing season.
Spring inoculation (after last frost) aligns colonization with natural sap flow.

## Site Selection

### Shade and Moisture
Locate stumps in partial to full shade with 60–80% canopy cover. North-facing slopes
offer cooler, moister conditions. Sites near streams or in ravines with high ambient
humidity are preferred. Avoid standing water. Mulch around the base with leaves or wood
chips to retain soil moisture.

### Tree Species Compatibility
The felled tree's species determines mushroom performance. Oaks are universal substrates
supporting shiitake, maitake, oyster, and reishi. Maples, beeches, birches, and sweetgum
are good hardwood hosts. Conifer stumps suit reishi and certain oyster species.

## Seasonal Considerations

### Inoculation and Colonization
Early spring is optimal, when soil temperatures exceed 5°C and are rising. Fall
inoculation is possible in mild climates but risks winter kill. Colonization lasts six
to eighteen months depending on species, stump size, and conditions. Maintain moisture
and shade throughout this period.

### Fruiting and Harvest
Most species fruit in response to cool, wet spring and autumn conditions. Shiitake and
oyster produce two to three flushes per year; maitake tends toward a single large autumn
flush. Harvest when caps are fully expanded but before margins curl upward.

## Advantages Over Log Culture

- **Extended productive life:** Root-connected stumps fruit 10–15 years versus 2–5 for
  logs.
- **Reduced labor:** No log handling, soaking, or stacking. Stumps remain in place.
- **Greater moisture resilience:** Ground contact and roots buffer against drought.
- **No substrate cost:** Stumps are forestry byproducts requiring no wood purchase.
- **Larger fruiting surface:** A single stump offers far more inoculation area than
  individual logs.
