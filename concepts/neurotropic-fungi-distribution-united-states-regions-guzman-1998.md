# Neurotropic Fungi Distribution Across United States Regions

## Source
Guzmán, Allen & Gartz (1998) — *A worldwide geographical distribution of the neurotropic fungi, an analysis and discussion.* Ann. Mus. civ. Rovereto, Vol. 14, pp. 189-280.

---

## Overview

The Guzmán, Allen and Gartz (1998) catalogue provides a detailed state-by-state inventory of neurotropic fungal species across the United States. This distribution data reveals distinct regional patterns, with the [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]] and Southeast showing the highest species diversity, while other regions show more limited representation.

## Pacific Northwest (California, Oregon, Washington, British Columbia)

The Pacific Northwest is the most species-rich region for neurotropic fungi in North America, serving as a global biodiversity hotspot for *Psilocybe*:

### California
- *Amanita muscaria*, *A. pantherina*
- *Copelandia cyanescens*, *C. tropicalis*
- *Gymnopilus aeruginosus*, *G. liquiritae*, *G. luteofolius*, *G. sapineus*, *G. spectabilis*
- *[[hypholoma-popperianum]]*
- *[[guzman-panaeolina-foenisecii-psilocybin-controversy-conflicting-chemical-analyses]]*, *Panaeolus fimicola*, *P. papilionaceus*, *P. retirugis*
- *Psilocybe azurescens*, *P. baeocystis*, *P. cyanescens*, *P. cyanofibrillosa*, *P. mairei*, *P. pelliculosa*, *P. semilanceata*, *P. stuntzii*

California hosts the highest number of neurotropic species of any US state, including the extremely potent *P. azurescens* which is restricted to the Pacific Northwest coast.

### Oregon and Washington
- Similar species assemblage to California
- *P. azurescens* primarily documented from the Oregon coast
- *P. stuntzii* known from Washington and Oregon
- Rich *Gymnopilus* diversity including *G. validipes*

## Southeastern United States (Florida, Georgia, Louisiana, Mississippi)

The subtropical Southeast hosts several species not found elsewhere in the US:

### Florida
- *Copelandia chlorocystis*, *C. cyanescens*, *C. westii*
- *Gymnopilus liquiritae*, *G. luteofolius*, *G. sapineus*
- *Psilocybe caerulescens* var. *caerulescens*, *P. cubensis*, *P. mammilata*, *P. tampanensis*

Florida is the only US state with documented *P. cubensis*, *P. tampanensis*, and multiple *Copelandia* species — all species more typical of tropical and subtropical regions.

### Georgia
- *Amanita muscaria*, *A. pantherina*
- *Psilocybe weilii* — a species first identified from Georgia

### Louisiana and Mississippi
- *P. cubensis* (Gulf Coast distribution)
- *P. tampanensis* documented from Mississippi
- *Copelandia cyanescens* in Louisiana

## Northeastern United States (New York, New England, Mid-Atlantic)

The Northeast shows primarily temperate neurotropic species:

### New York
- *Amanita muscaria*, *A. pantherina*
- *[[conocybe-cyanopus]]*
- *Gymnopilus liquiritae*, *G. luteofolius*, *G. luteus*, *G. spectabilis*, *G. validipes*
- *Panaeolina foenisecii*, *Panaeolus castanaeifolius*, *P. fimicola*, *P. papilionaceus*, *P. subbalteatus*, *P. retirugis*
- *Psilocybe caerulipes*, *P. semilanceata*, *P. silvatica*

### New England (Maine, Massachusetts, New Hampshire, Vermont)
- *Amanita muscaria* (widespread)
- *Gymnopilus* species (*G. liquiritae*, *G. sapineus*, *G. spectabilis*)
- *Panaeolus papilionaceus*, *P. subbalteatus*
- *Psilocybe caerulipes* (Maine, New York)
- *Claviceps purpurea* (Iowa, Nebraska — ergot species in grain-growing states)

## Great Lakes and Midwest (Michigan, Illinois, Ohio, Wisconsin)

### Michigan
Michigan shows remarkable neurotropic diversity for a non-coastal state:
- *Amanita muscaria*, *A. pantherina*
- *[[conocybe-smithii]]*
- *Gymnopilus aeruginosus*, *G. luteofolius*, *G. luteus*, *G. liquiritae*, *G. sapineus*, *G. spectabilis*, *G. validipes*
- *Panaeolus subbalteatus*
- *Pluteus salicinus*
- *Psilocybe caerulipes*, *P. liniformans* var. *americana*, *P. silvatica*

### Illinois
- *Panaeolus subbalteatus* (reported by Stein, 1959)
- *Pluteus salicinus*

## Mountain West and Southwest (Colorado, New Mexico, Idaho)

### Colorado
- *Amanita muscaria*, *A. pantherina*
- *Conocybe cyanopus*
- *Gymnopilus sapineus*
- *Panaeolus papilionaceus*

### New Mexico
- *Gymnopilus liquiritae*, *G. luteofolius*, *G. sapineus*, *G. spectabilis*
- *Panaeolus papilionaceus*
- *Psilocybe azurescens* (documented from New Mexico)

## Regional Distribution Patterns

### Biogeographic Zones

1. **Pacific Northwest Temperate**: Highest *Psilocybe* diversity; *P. azurescens*, *P. baeocystis*, *P. stuntzii* as regional endemics
2. **Southeastern Subtropical**: *P. cubensis*, *Copelandia* spp., *P. tampanensis* — tropical elements reaching their northern limit
3. **Northeastern Temperate**: *P. caerulipes*, *P. semilanceata*, widespread *Gymnopilus* and *Amanita*
4. **Midwestern Continental**: Limited diversity, primarily *Gymnopilus*, *Panaeolus*, and *Pluteus*
5. **Southwestern Montane**: Mix of temperate and subtropical elements

### Widespread Species

Several species occur across most US regions:

- *Amanita muscaria*: Nearly ubiquitous, found coast to coast
- *Amanita pantherina*: Nearly as widespread as *A. muscaria*
- *Gymnopilus sapineus*: Documented from most eastern and central states
- *Panaeolus papilionaceus*: Widespread across eastern and central US
- *Panaeolus subbalteatus*: Eastern and central US

### Species with Disjunct US Distributions

Some species show unexpected disjunct distributions within the US:

- *Psilocybe azurescens*: Primarily Oregon coast, but also documented from New Mexico
- *Psilocybe semilanceata*: Northeast and Pacific Northwest, absent from central US
- *Conocybe cyanopus*: Scattered reports from Colorado, Michigan, New York — no continuous range

### Comparison with Mexico and Canada

The US has 21 reported neurotropic taxa, compared to 44 in Mexico and approximately 15-20 in Canada. The lower US diversity (despite much larger area) partly reflects less intensive [[guzman-allen-gartz-mycological-exploration-bias-and-underexplored-regions]] in tropical and subtropical regions of the southern US, and the concentration of species in specific biodiversity hotspots (Pacific Northwest, Florida).
