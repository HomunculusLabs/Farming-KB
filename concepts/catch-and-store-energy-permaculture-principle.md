## The Energy Basis of Life

All life on earth depends on the capture of solar energy through photosynthesis.
This fundamental process, along with minor contributions from wind, wave, and
geothermal energy, is the basis for all biological productivity. In the context
of [[permaculture-design]] is clear: designing systems that mimic
natural patterns of energy capture and storage will be more productive and
sustainable over the long term.

## Soil as Energy Storage

Soil is one of the most important energy storage media in terrestrial
ecosystems. Fertile arable soils in temperate zones contain over 10% humus,
while the majority now contain less than 5%. Humus acts as a slow-release
energy and nutrient store, supporting soil biology and plant growth. Building
soil organic matter is therefore a primary strategy for catching and storing
energy in permaculture systems.

Key strategies for building soil energy stores include:
- Minimising soil disturbance to protect existing organic matter
- Using deep-rooted perennial plants to cycle nutrients from subsoil
- Applying organic mulches and composts to feed soil biology
- Managing grazing to stimulate root exudation and microbial activity
- Using appropriate mineral amendments to correct imbalances

## Genetic Resources

The storage of genetic diversity through seed saving, heritage variety
preservation, and the maintenance of living collections is another expression of
this principle. The Seed Savers Network, Diggers Seeds, and heritage fruit
groups represent practical applications. Agribusiness multinationals have made
enormous efforts to control seed supplies through plant patenting and genetic
engineering, making independent seed-saving an act of cultural and ecological
resistance.
