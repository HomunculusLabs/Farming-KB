## Overview

Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), also known as Lou Gehrig's disease, is a progressive neurodegenerative disease affecting motor neurons. It leads to muscle weakness, paralysis, and ultimately respiratory failure. Cannabis research suggests neuroprotective and symptom-management benefits. See also [[blesching-cannabis-alzheimers-disease]], [[cannabis-for-parkinsons-disease]], and [[cannabis-and-multiple-sclerosis]].

## Clinical Evidence

### CHI Score: 19 (Moderate Evidence)

Seven studies support cannabis for ALS — one of the higher CHI values among neurological conditions.

### Key Findings

**Neuroprotection**
- The endocannabinoid system is involved in protecting motor neurons from degeneration
- CB1 and CB2 receptors are both implicated in neuroprotective pathways
- The US HHS patent on cannabinoids as neuroprotectants (US6630507, 2003) is directly relevant to ALS

**Disease Progression**
- Preclinical evidence suggests cannabinoids may slow the progression of motor neuron degeneration
- Anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties may protect neurons from oxidative stress
- Modulation of glutamate excitotoxicity — a key mechanism in ALS — via CB1 receptors

**Symptom Management**
- Spasticity and muscle cramps: cannabinoids reduce muscle spasticity
- Pain: neuropathic and musculoskeletal pain common in ALS, responsive to cannabinoids
- Appetite stimulation: THC promotes appetite in wasting conditions
- Sleep improvement: indica strains may help with insomnia
- Sialorrhea (excessive saliva): THC may reduce saliva production
- Depression: mood effects of cannabis may help with psychological burden

**Anti-inflammatory Effects**
- Microglial activation and neuroinflammation are features of ALS progression
- CB2 receptor activation on microglia reduces inflammatory cytokine production
- This may slow disease progression by reducing neuroinflammatory damage

## Mechanism of Action

- CB1 receptors on motor neurons modulate neurotransmitter release (reducing glutamate excitotoxicity)
- CB2 receptors on microglia reduce neuroinflammation
- Antioxidant properties protect neurons from oxidative damage
- Anti-apoptotic effects may extend motor neuron survival

## Therapeutic Considerations

### Strain Selection
- Balanced THC:CBD preparations for comprehensive symptom management
- Indica-dominant for spasticity, sleep, and pain
- Sativa-dominant for daytime energy and mood (if tolerated)
- High-CBD strains for neuroprotection without psychoactive effects

### Administration
- Oral preparations (tinctures, capsules) for consistent baseline dosing
- Vaporization for breakthrough symptom relief
- Start low and titrate slowly — ALS patients may be sensitive to psychoactive effects

### Dosing Strategy
- Regular daily dosing likely needed for neuroprotective effects
- Symptom-based dosing for breakthrough pain and spasticity
- Monitor respiratory function — smoking is contraindicated; use vaporization or oral routes

## Integrative Approaches

- Riluzole (conventional ALS drug) — no known negative cannabinoid interactions
- Physical therapy and assistive devices
- Nutritional support — weight maintenance critical
- Respiratory support as disease progresses
- Psychological support and counseling

## Safety

- Monitor for respiratory depression when combining with other CNS depressants
- Use non-smoking administration routes given respiratory compromise
- Psychoactive effects should be monitored in patients with cognitive changes

# Cannabis for ALS (Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis)

ALS, also known as Lou Gehrig's disease, is a chronic degenerative neurological illness involving the selective loss of motor neurons in the brain and spinal cord. The name derives from Greek: "a-myo-trophic" meaning no-muscle-nourishment, "lateral" referring to muscles on either side of affected nerves, and "sclerosis" meaning hardening. Without nourishment, muscles waste away along with the ability to initiate movement.

## Disease Overview

ALS often begins spontaneously in otherwise healthy adults, with initial symptoms including isolated muscle weakness, twitching, difficulty speaking, muscle cramps and spasms, and periods of uncontrollable laughter or crying. These progress to advanced symptoms including difficulty swallowing and eventual respiratory failure.

While late-stage ALS patients descend into total paralysis, their minds largely remain unaffected. About two people per 100,000 die of the disease annually in the U.S. The majority of ALS patients die within three to five years of symptom onset, though some survive up to ten years.

Orthodox medicine does not know the exact causes of ALS and offers no cure. Higher incidence has been observed in military veterans, especially Gulf War veterans (twice as likely to develop ALS), and in populations from Guam during the 1950s, leading to hypotheses involving environmental nerve toxin exposure.

Doctors diagnose ALS through neurological examination, symptom progression review, and nerve/muscle function tests. The only available pharmaceutical, riluzole, reduces glutamate levels and may extend lifespan by up to two months, but does not reverse nerve damage and can be liver-toxic.

## Cannabis and ALS

The number of studies examining the [[cannabis-endocannabinoid-system]] in the context of ALS continues to grow. Researchers suggest that [[cannabis-for-neurological-conditions]], with its pain-killing, muscle-relaxing, bronchodilating, saliva-reducing, appetite-stimulating, sleep-inducing, antioxidative and neuroprotective properties, may be a practical therapeutic agent in ALS management.

A landmark 2010 Seattle trial on mice led scientists to suggest that an optimal ALS treatment regimen would include glutamate antagonists, antioxidants, a centrally acting anti-inflammatory agent, microglial cell modulators (including TNF-alpha inhibitors), an antiapoptotic agent, neurotrophic growth factors, and a mitochondrial function-enhancing agent. Remarkably, cannabis appears to have activity in ALL of those areas.

## Evidence Summary (CHI Value: 19)

A 2010 Swiss study on 27 ALS patients receiving 5 mg THC twice daily found no change in cramps. However, a 2010 Seattle study on mice found cannabinoids exhibited prolonged neuronal cell survival, delayed ALS symptom onset, and slower disease progression.

A 2010 review from Rome confirmed cannabinoids regulate immune responses and protect nerve cell function and integrity. A 2007 review from the University of Rome found the [[cannabis-endocannabinoid-system]] is involved in modulating neurodegeneration and neuroinflammation.

A 2005 University of Washington study delivering CBN via osmotic mini-pumps to mice significantly delayed disease onset by more than two weeks. A 2004 study on mice found cannabinoids delayed disease progression and extended survival.

## Mechanism of Action

ALS patients possess higher levels of glutamate in their spinal fluid, contributing to excitotoxic motor neuron death. Cannabis addresses this through multiple pathways:

[[cannabis-thc-therapeutic-profile]] acts as a glutamate antagonist, reducing excitotoxic damage to motor neurons. The anti-inflammatory properties of cannabinoids, particularly through CB2 receptor activation, help modulate the immune response and reduce neuroinflammation.

The antioxidant properties of cannabinoids protect neurons from oxidative stress, a key contributor to neurodegeneration. The muscle-relaxant effects may help reduce cramps and spasms, while appetite stimulation addresses the nutritional challenges faced by ALS patients.

Bronchodilation helps maintain respiratory function, and sleep-inducing properties address the sleep disturbances common in ALS. The neuroprotective effects work across multiple pathways to slow the progression of motor neuron death.

## Strain-Specific Considerations

Given the multi-target nature of ALS pathology, both [[cannabis-cannabinoid-receptor-pharmacology]] and CB2 activation appear beneficial. Full-spectrum preparations that engage both receptor pathways may offer the most comprehensive therapeutic approach.

Indica or indica-heavy hybrids with their lower THC:[[cannabis-cbd-therapeutic-profile]] ratio may favor CB2 activation and provide additional anti-inflammatory and muscle-relaxant effects. Patients may benefit from the [[blesching-cannabis-cannabinoid-terpene-entourage-effect]] of whole-plant preparations.
