# Seed Germination, Stratification, and Photoperiodism

## Overview

Successful [[philodendron-selloum]]** store fats that are metabolically "burned" to create heat
- Philodendrons may register 46°C (115°F) when ambient air is 4°C (39°F)
- **Crocuses** heat up to 15°C (27°F) above ambient air temperature
- **[[symplocarpus-foetidus]]*) generates 20–25°C of extra heat to melt spring snow, protect blooms, and create a warm incubator for early growth

The skunk cabbage has a remarkable additional adaptation: its first leaf shape creates a wind-driven vortex contained within the hot leaf that carries pollen down to unpollinated lower flowers, achieving fertilization in cold winds without insects.

Understorey clumps of such thermogenic species may assist frost-tender, fly-pollinated, or heat-starved plants in cold-climate designs.

## Integrated Germination Strategy for Permaculture

A comprehensive approach to seed propagation combines these factors:

1. **Know your climate zone and local day length** to select appropriate species and varieties
2. **Measure soil temperature** before planting to ensure it exceeds species-specific minimums
3. **Apply appropriate pre-treatment** (stratification, heat, or digestion) for dormant seeds
4. **Match light requirements** to planting depth—surface-sow light-requiring seeds
5. **Use microclimate manipulation** (cold frames, mulch, thermal mass) to extend growing seasons
6. **Select local seed sources** to ensure photoperiod-appropriate flowering and seed set
