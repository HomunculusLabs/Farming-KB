Screen of Green (SCROG) is a training technique that uses a horizontal screen or net (a "scrog net") to create an even, flat canopy of bud sites. It maximizes light efficiency and yield by ensuring every bud site receives direct light exposure.

## Core Concept

A screen (typically chicken wire, trellis netting, or string grid) is installed above the plants at a fixed height. As plants grow through the screen, branches are woven and spread horizontally across it. This creates a flat, even canopy where every bud site sits at the same level, all receiving equal light intensity.

## Advantages

- **Maximizes light utilization**: No buds are shaded by other buds — every site gets direct light
- **Dramatically increases yield**: Exposes far more bud sites to direct light compared to untrained plants
- **Fewer plants needed**: Works with fewer, larger plants compared to SOG (better for plant-count-limited grows)
- **Better airflow**: Horizontal spreading opens up the canopy, reducing mold risk
- **Height control**: Keeps tall plants manageable in height-restricted spaces
- **Larger individual plant yield**: Each trained plant produces significantly more than an untrained plant

## Disadvantages

- **Labor intensive**: Daily attention needed during vegetative phase to tuck and train branches
- **Longer vegetative phase**: Requires 4–8 weeks of vegetative growth to fill the screen before flowering
- **Difficult to move plants**: Once trained into the screen, plants cannot be easily repositioned
- **Requires some experience**: Proper technique takes practice to learn

## Screen Setup

### Screen Materials

- **PVC-coated garden trellis netting**: 2×2 inch or 3×3 inch grid — most popular and readily available
- **Chicken wire**: Works but can be sharp and may damage stems
- **String grid**: DIY option using string or wire tied to a frame
- **Purpose-made scrog nets**: Available from grow supply stores

### Screen Height

- Position the screen 8–20 inches above the container rim, depending on:
  - Available vertical space (account for light height above screen and stretch during flowering)
  - Strain stretch ratio (sativas stretch more than indicas)
  - Typically 12–18 inches is a good starting point
- The screen should leave enough room above it for the flowering stretch

### Screen Frame

- Build a frame from PVC pipe, wood, or similar materials
- Frame should be slightly smaller than your grow space footprint
- Ensure it is sturdy enough to support the weight of your plants
- The frame can be tied to or suspended from grow tent poles

## Step-by-Step SCROG Training

### 1. Vegetative Growth Phase

1. **Start plants in their final containers** — you will not be transplanting after training begins
2. **Grow normally** until plants reach the screen height
3. When a branch tip grows 2–3 inches above the screen, **tuck it back under** the next square of the screen, weaving it horizontally
4. **Continue daily**: As new growth pushes through the screen, tuck it back under and spread it into empty screen squares
5. **Fill the screen**: The goal is to have every square of the screen filled with a branch tip before switching to flowering
6. **Vegetative time**: Expect 4–8 weeks to fill the screen, depending on plant count, strain vigor, and grow conditions

### 2. Training Technique

- **Weave, don't cut**: Unlike topping, SCROG training involves bending and weaving, not cutting
- **Spread evenly**: Distribute branch tips evenly across the screen — do not let one area get too dense
- **Tuck growing tips under**: Always pull the newest growth back under the screen and toward empty areas
- **Remove lower growth**: Prune branches and leaves below the screen that won't receive light — they waste plant energy
- **Be gentle**: Bend branches slowly to avoid snapping. See [[cannabis-super-cropping-technique]] for handling stubborn branches.

### 3. Switch to Flowering

- **Flip to 12/12** once the screen is approximately 70–80% full (the remaining 20–30% will fill in during the flowering stretch)
- Most strains stretch 1.5–3x their vegetative height during early flowering
- Plan accordingly: if the screen is at 18 inches and you have 3 feet of headroom above the screen, account for 1.5–3 feet of stretch

### 4. Flowering Management

- **Early stretch (weeks 1–3 of flower)**: Continue tucking any branches that push too far above the screen
- **After week 3**: Stop training — let the plant focus energy on bud development
- **Support heavy colas**: As buds develop weight, they may need support. Use garden wire, soft ties, or additional netting above the screen
- **Defoliation**: Consider removing large fan leaves that shade bud sites, especially in the middle of the canopy

## Plant Count for SCROG

- **Single plant SCROG**: One large plant trained across the entire screen. Longest vegetative time but dramatic results.
- **2–4 plants**: Good balance of vegetative time and screen fill speed
- **4–9 plants**: Faster screen fill, moderate vegetative time. Common in a 3×3 or 4×4 foot space.

## SCROG vs SOG Comparison

| Feature | SCROG | SOG |
|---------|-------|-----|
| Plant count | Few (1–9) | Many (16–50+) |
| Vegetative time | 4–8 weeks | 2–3 weeks |
| Training effort | High | Minimal |
| Yield per plant | High | Low |
| Yield per space | Very high | High |
| Per-plant yield | Excellent | Poor |
| Best strains | Any (adjust for stretch) | Indica-dominant, compact |
