# Tagasaste (Chamaecytisus palmensis)

Tagasaste, also known as tree lucerne, is a nitrogen-fixing legume (Fabaceae/Papilionaceae) native to the Canary Islands. One of the most useful [[permaculture-pioneer-species]] available.

## Description

- Evergreen shrub/tree to 7m height

- Fairly short lived, but regular cutting or browsing extends life expectancy

- Naturalised coloniser of Australian wastelands

- Survives wide range of soils

- 50,000 ha planted in Western Australia by 1996; estimated 1.5M ha could benefit

- Grows in areas of low rainfall (300mm minimum)

- Drought tolerance improved with phosphorous availability (rock phosphate at planting)

- Nitrogen-fixing bacteria usually present from associated Acacia, Albizia, Cassia species

Tagasaste exemplifies the [[permaculture-multipurpose-trees]] ideal, providing fodder, soil improvement, wind protection, and [[permaculture-bee-forage-system]] services simultaneously. Its nitrogen-fixing ability is central to [[legume-nitrogen-dynamics]] and [[permaculture-nitrogen-fixing-trees-guide]] strategies in low-rainfall environments.

## Fodder Value

Fast growing, highly productive stock feed with high nutrition, low toxicity, high palatability:

| Constituent | Tagasaste | Lucerne | Rye Grass | Wheat Grain |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Crude Protein | 17-31% | 21% | 16% | 11% |
| Carbohydrate | 38-58% | 40% | 46% | 82% |
| Crude Fat | 2-6% | 3% | 4% | 2% |
| Crude Fibre | 14-30% | 26% | 24% | 3% |
| Calcium | 0.48-1.62% | 2.2% | 1.0% | 0.04% |

The crude protein content of 17-31% rivals or exceeds lucerne, making tagasaste one of the highest-quality [[urban-permaculture-fodder-trees-livestock]] species available. This exceptional nutrition supports [[permaculture-livestock-forage-systems]] even on marginal land.

| Pasture yield (t DM/ha/yr) | 5.5 | 5.5 | 4.0 | 2.0 | 1.0 | 1.0 |

On near-useless sandy soils (700kg wheat yield), tagasaste yielded feed equivalent of 1100kg wheat. Ewes on tagasaste grew 30% more wool.

These figures demonstrate tagasaste's capacity to transform unproductive land into valuable [[permaculture-grazing-and-pasture]] systems. The synergy between tagasaste and pasture supports [[permaculture-animal-integration]] by providing year-round feed security.

## Other Uses

- Bee forage: flowers in winter when little else blooms; cutting different plants at different times extends flowering

- Poultry forage: prolific seed producer; seeds are 25% protein, 12% fat

- Also eaten by pigs, horses, rabbits

- Nurse crop: NZ trials showed trees growing 1m from tagasaste consistently outperformed controls

- Timber: good firewood; hard, light wood for tool handles and craft

- Can lift farm profit from $6/ha to $30/ha

The winter flowering period fills a critical nectar gap in [[permaculture-bee-forage-system]] design. As a [[nurse-crop-species-permaculture]], tagasaste accelerates [[permaculture-forest-garden-establishment]] by sheltering and nurturing young food trees. See [[permaculture-windbreak]] for shelterbelt applications.

## Management

Trim at very early age to encourage bushy habit rather than erect tree. Keeps foliage accessible to stock and prevents ring barking. Arrange into lock-up [[urban-permaculture-fodder-trees-livestock]] systems with well-fenced hedgerows for constant grazing.

Early training is essential — unmanaged trees grow tall and become inaccessible to stock. Management follows [[permaculture-grazing-and-pasture]] rotational principles. Cut material can be fed directly to stock or used as [[permaculture-green-manure-crops]] surface mulch, supporting [[permaculture-soil-strategies]].

## Propagation

Seed requires scarification or hot water treatment before sowing to break dormancy. Direct seeding is possible but protection from grazing animals is essential. See [[permaculture-plant-propagation-guide]] for techniques. Seedling establishment benefits from [[permaculture-sheet-mulch-detailed-guide]] moisture conservation.

## Establishment in Permaculture Systems

Tagasaste is a key component of [[permaculture-pioneer-species]] strategies for site preparation and [[permaculture-succession]] management. It supports [[permaculture-tree-crop-systems]] as a nurse crop for species like [[permaculture-macadamia]] and [[permaculture-chestnut]]. Its integration follows [[permaculture-food-forest-design-principles]] where pioneers create the conditions for productive canopy trees.

## Climate Adaptation

Tagasaste thrives in [[permaculture-climate-zones]] with as little as 300mm annual rainfall when established with rock phosphate. Its drought tolerance makes it valuable for [[permaculture-difficult-sites-plant-guide]] where few other fodder trees survive. Frost tolerance is moderate — young plants may need [[permaculture-frost-protection-strategies]] in colder areas.
