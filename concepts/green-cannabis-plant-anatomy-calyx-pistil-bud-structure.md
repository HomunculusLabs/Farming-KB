# [[cannabis-plant-anatomy]]: Calyx, Pistil, and Bud Structure

Understanding the physical structure of the cannabis plant is essential
for growers who need to identify sex, assess maturity, and maximize yield.
[[greg-green]]'s "The Cannabis Grow Bible" provides detailed descriptions of
each plant part and its role in growth and reproduction. The cannabis
plant can be divided into six main sections: bud, stem, branches, nodes,
leaves, and main cola. Beyond these broad categories, the flower
structures -- calyx, pistil, and resin glands -- are the most important
anatomical features for growers to understand.

## The Six Main Plant Sections

### Bud

The bud is the prime smokable part of the cannabis plant and contains the
highest concentration of THC and other cannabinoids. Buds develop at the
nodes and at the top of the plant during the [[green-cannabis-grow-bible-flowering-phase-and-harvest]]. Non-pollinated female buds (sinsemilla) produce the largest and
most potent flowers.

### Stem

The stem provides structural support and serves as the transport pathway
for water, nutrients, and sugars between roots and leaves. While stems
contain some THC, the quantities are small compared to the bud. Stems are
normally discarded after harvest, though they can be used for extraction.

### Branches

Branches grow from the stem at intervals called nodes and support the
leaves and buds. Branch architecture determines the overall shape of the
plant and can be manipulated through [[green-cannabis-grow-bible-plant-training-pruning-and-yield]].

### Nodes

Nodes are the junction points where branches meet the stem. They are
critically important because this is where pre-flowers first appear,
allowing growers to determine plant sex before committing to the
flowering phase.

### Leaves

Leaves are the lesser potent part of the plant next to the stem and
roots. Fan leaves are large and serve primarily to gather light for
photosynthesis, producing sugars used in bud production. Smaller sugar
leaves are found within the buds and are often coated in trichomes.

### Main Cola

The main cola (also spelled kola) is the large central bud cluster at
the very top of the plant. It typically receives the most light and
produces the largest single mass of bud. See
[[green-cannabis-cola-development-bud-architecture]] for detailed
coverage of cola development.

## Flower Anatomy: Calyx

The calyx is the first visible indicator of pre-flowering. During the
pre-flowering stage, the plant starts to show a calyx which appears where
the branches meet the stem (at the nodes). The calyx is a small teardrop-
shaped structure that encloses the reproductive organs of the flower.

### Sexing by Calyx Examination

Growers can determine plant sex by closely examining the calyx with a
magnifying glass:

- If the calyx is raised on a small short stem (a pedicel), it is
  probably a male flower.
- If the calyx is not raised on a small short stem, it is probably a
  female flower.

This distinction is crucial for identifying and removing males before
they release pollen. The calyx is described as the ultimate pre-flowering
indicator for cannabis sex determination.

### Female Calyx Development

In female plants, the calyx forms the base of each flower. As flowering
progresses, clusters of calyces swell and pack tightly together, forming
the dense bud structures that growers harvest. Each calyx contains the
ovule and, if pollinated, will produce a seed.

## Flower Anatomy: Pistil (Stigma)

The pistil is the hair-like structure that emerges from the calyx on
female plants. These white or creamy-colored hairs are technically called
stigmas and serve to catch pollen from male plants. Key characteristics
of pistils through the flowering cycle:

- Early flowering: Pistils are white and begin to curl slightly,
  growing longer and thicker.
- Mid flowering: Pistils become sticky to the touch as they are covered
  in resin containing THC. The top cola should carry the most pistils.
- Late flowering: Pistils swell up at the flower tips, then change
  color from white to orange, red, or brown depending on the strain.
- Harvest indicator: When 50% to 70% of pistils have changed color, the
  plant is generally ready for harvest.

The pistil color change is one of the primary visual indicators growers
use to determine harvest readiness. However, breeder-recommended
flowering times should also be consulted.

## Male Flower Structures

Male plants produce small round pollen sacks clustered together like
grapes at the nodes. When these sacks are ripe they burst open and
scatter pollen to female plants. Males can be smoked or made into hash
oil, but they contain low levels of THC and are considered vastly inferior
to female plants for consumption purposes.

## Resin Glands (Trichomes)

The surface of the flowers is covered in tiny stalked resin glands called
stalked capitate trichomes. These are the primary cannabinoid-producing
structures on the plant. The gland heads secrete an oil-like substance
containing THC and other cannabinoids, commonly referred to as resin.

Additional gland types include:

- Bulbous glands: Found on leaves, producing cannabinoids in minor
  quantities compared to stalked capitate trichomes.
- The stem itself also produces small amounts of cannabinoids.

The stalked capitate trichomes are the target of
[[green-cannabis-grow-bible-hash-making-and-trichome-extraction]] processes.
