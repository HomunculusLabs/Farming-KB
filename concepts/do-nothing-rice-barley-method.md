# Do-Nothing Rice Barley Method

The do-nothing rice/barley method is [[masanobu-fukuoka]]'s signature [[boil-a-bag-mushroom-cultivation-technique]],
formally known as "direct-seeded, no-tillage, winter grain/rice succession in a clover
cover." This method produces roughly 22 bushels (1,300 pounds) each of winter grain and
rice per quarter-acre without plowing, [[cannabis-chemical-fertilizers]], prepared compost, or
pesticides. Fukuoka's goal was to eventually take in 33 bushels per quarter-acre. The
labor of one or two people is more than enough to grow crops on a quarter-acre.

Fukuoka practiced this method for over thirty years on the same field, never plowed or
turned. He called it the "clover revolution" in rice and barley cropping, voicing his
opposition to modern [[fukuoka-illusions-of-scientific-farming]] with its use of chemicals and large machinery.

## The Core Principle

The method is built on the symbiosis of rice and barley or wheat in clover. Fukuoka
summarized it: "Were I to say that all my method of farming boils down to is the
symbiosis of rice and barley or wheat in clover, I would probably be reproached: 'If
that's all there is to growing rice, then farmers wouldn't be out there working so hard
in their fields.'" Yet this simple combination produces robust harvests while the soil
improves rather than degrades.

## Annual Cycle: Fall Operations

### Sowing Clover and Winter Grain

In early or mid-October, clover seeds are sown over the standing heads of ripening rice.
About two weeks before harvest, barley or wheat seed is broadcast over the same field.
Both germinate and begin growing beneath the rice canopy before the rice is harvested.

### Rice Harvest

The rice is harvested while the farmer treads on the young shoots of newly sprouted
winter grain. After drying for three days, the rice is threshed and the straw scattered
uncut over the entire field. Chicken droppings or decomposed organic matter are applied
if available.

### Sowing Rice Seed

Clay pellets containing seed rice are scattered over the straw before the New Year. With
the winter grain growing and the rice seed sown, there is nothing left to do until the
winter grain harvest. This completes the sowing of rice and barley for the coming year.

## Annual Cycle: Spring and Summer Operations

### Winter Grain Harvest

In late May, while harvesting the winter grain, the clover is growing luxuriantly and
small shoots have emerged from the rice seed in the clay pellets. After harvesting,
drying, and threshing, all straw is scattered uncut over the field with chicken manure.

### Managing the Clover-Rice Transition

In early June, when the clover appears about to choke out the young rice seedlings, the
field is flooded for four to seven days to weaken the clover and give rice shoots a
chance to break through. The field is then surface-drained to grow hardy plants. Water
may be passed briefly over the field once every week to ten days during the first half
of the rice growing season.

### Summer Water Management

In June and July the field is left unirrigated. In August, water is run through the
drainage ditches once every week or ten days. After heading, the rice requires lots of
water, but Fukuoka made it a point not to hold water for more than five days at a
stretch. A soil moisture level of eighty percent is adequate.

## Drainage Preparation

The first step when preparing a paddy field for this method is digging drainage channels.
Two or three weeks before rice is cut, a water outlet is cut through the levee and the
field surface drained. A drainage channel about 8 inches deep and 8 inches wide is
shaped along the perimeter. After rice harvest, similar channels are dug at intervals of
12 to 15 feet. Once dug, these channels can be used for many years.
