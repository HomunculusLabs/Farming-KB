# High Pressure Sodium Lighting

High Pressure Sodium (HPS) lamps are the most efficient HID lamps
available for indoor [[query-how-does-no-till-cannabis-cultivation-work]]. They emit an orange-tinged
glow comparable to the harvest sun, making them ideal for the
[[cervantes-metal-halide-lighting-systems]],
[[cervantes-conversion-bulbs-hid-lighting]].

## Overview and Efficiency

The most impressive fact about the 600-watt HPS lamp is that it
produces 90,000 initial lumens. Watt for watt, a 600-watt HPS produces
seven percent more light than the 1000-watt HPS. Incandescent bulbs
are the least efficient at converting electricity to light; 600-watt
HPS lamps are the most efficient.

HPS lamps come in 35, 50, 70, 100, 150, 200, 250, 310, 400, 600, and
1000 wattages. Nearly all of the HPS bulbs used in grow rooms are
clear. All HP sodium vapor lamps have their own unique ballast.

## Manufacturers

High pressure sodium lamps are manufactured by GE (Lucalox), Sylvania
(Lumalux), Westinghouse (Ceramalux), Philips (Son Agro), Iwasaki (Eye),
and Venture (High Pressure Sodium). American growers use 1000 and
600-watt HP sodiums most often, while European growers favor 400 and
600-watt HPS lamps.

## Spectrum Characteristics

High pressure sodium lamps emit an orange-tinged glow that could be
compared to the harvest sun. The color spectrum is highest in the
yellow, orange, and red end. For many years, scientists believed this
spectrum promoted flower production. However, with new PAR technology,
scientists are rethinking old theories.

Marijuana's light needs change when flowering; it no longer needs to
produce as many vegetative cells. [[cannabis-vegetative-growth-stage]] slows and
eventually stops during blooming. All the plant's energy and attention
is focused on flower production to complete its annual [[bloomfield-fungal-life-cycle-spiral-versus-circle]].
Light from the red end of the spectrum stimulates floral hormones
within the plant, promoting flower production.

## Combined Lighting Strategy

According to some growers, flower volume and weight increase when using
HP sodium lights. Other evidence shows enhanced metal halides to be
superior. Many growers using a 10 x 10-foot room retain the 1000-watt
halide and add a 1000-watt sodium during flowering. Flowering plants
need more light to produce tight, full buds.

Adding an HPS lamp not only doubles available light, it increases the
red end of the spectrum. A 1:1 ratio of one halide and one HPS is a
popular combination for flowering. This combined approach leverages
the strengths of both [[cervantes-ballasts-hid-electrical]]. Light distribution can be
further optimized with proper [[cervantes-grow-room-electricity-basics]].
