# Permaculture Grain Crops for Small Scale

While modern agriculture grows grains in vast monocultures, permaculture integrates grain crops into diverse polyculture systems at homestead scale. The [[permaculture-useful-plants-reference]] highlights several grain-producing species suited to integrated systems, the wiki's [[small-scale-grain-growing]] page provides detailed cultivation guidance, and the [[permaculture-plant-catalog]] lists many more grain options.

## Grain-Producing Aquatic Plants

### Floating Rice (Oryza sativa)
Family: POACEAE. Floats on water up to 6 m deep, although seed is usually broadcast onto dry or damp soil before flooding. Plants withstand flooding for up to 30 days. In permaculture systems, grow rice in paddies integrated with:
- [[edible-aquatic-plants]] as a living nitrogen source
- Duckweed for water nutrient management
- Fish for integrated permaculture-aquaculture|aquaculture
- Taro and water chestnut in rotation

This integrated approach eliminates the need for synthetic nitrogen while producing multiple yields from the same water area. See also [[permaculture-aquaculture-guide]] and [[permaculture-water-strategies]].

### Great Water Grass (Glyceria maxima)
Dense clumping perennial grass to 2 m spreading by creeping rhizomes. Leaves are good fish food. Seed flour makes good quality bread, thickens soups and stews, and makes puddings. Useful for aquatic zone edges in [[holzer-livestock-philosophy-pig-breeds]].

### Floating Manna Grass (Glyceria fluitans)
Perennial grass to 120 cm in stagnant or slow-moving water. Insect larvae and small animals shelter in the loosely floating leaves -- supporting [[mollison-designers-insectary-plants-and-beneficial-insect-habitat]]. Seed used for human food.

## Grain-Producing Trees

### Oak Acorns (Quercus spp)
About 600 species across Europe, Asia, and North America. Some evergreen, most deciduous, usually large and long-lived (500-700 years). White oak group acorns contain less tannin and are more palatable. Acorns must be leached of tannins before eating -- traditionally ground into meal for breads, soups, and stews. All oaks have low combustibility and serve as fire barriers in landscape design.

Key edible species:
- **Q. alba** (white oak) -- acorns leached, boiled, eaten like chestnuts. Bark used medicinally. Timber prized for barrels.
- **Q. bicolor** (swamp white oak) -- native to SE Canada and eastern USA. At home in wetlands. Acorns roasted, ground into meal.
- **Q. ilex** (holm oak) -- evergreen, drought tolerant, grows on limestone. Acorns used for fodder.
- **Q. cerris** (Turkey oak) -- fastest growing oak. Acorns ground for bread. Branches yield manna-like substance (gaze) boiled into syrup.
- **Q. frainetto** (Hungarian oak) -- remarkably fast growing on light, fertile soil.
- **Q. suber** (cork oak) -- produces cork; grows as companion to Q. ilex.

### Macadamia (Macadamia spp)
Family: PROTEACEAE. Long-lived evergreen tree, first commercial tree crop native to Australia. Hardy to -4C, fairly drought tolerant. Bears at 4-6 years; 15-year tree produces up to 40 kg nuts. Extremely hard-shelled (parrot-proof), stay fresh for 2 years. Spacing 9m x 9m -- interplant with [[nitrogen-fixing-trees-and-shrubs]] until trees fill space. A mix of grafted cultivars and seedlings recommended for cross-pollination and diversity.

Nutrition per 100g: 691 cal, 71.6g fat, 7.8g protein, 48mg calcium, 264mg potassium. Mostly goes to confectionery but increasingly eaten fresh.

### Carob (Ceratonia siliqua)
Family: CAESALPINIACEAE. Largest of Europe's leguminous trees (to 15 m), native to the Mediterranean. Minimum rainfall 250 mm. Life span 500+ years. Pods are fleshy beans to 300 mm, both flavoursome and nutritious: protein 2.25-21%, sugars 37.1-56.6%. Excellent stockfeed, commercial chocolate substitute. Seed contains industrial gums. Potential for alcohol fuel. Yields of 1 tonne pods from a single tree reported in good seasons; ~8 t/ha more typical. Dioecious (needs male + female).

Tap roots traced to 20 m. Seedlings develop 250 mm taproot in first two weeks. Direct seeding possible with protective markers. Scarify seed with boiling water. Good companion to grapes.

## Fodder Grasses as Lockup Feed

### Pampas Grass (Cortaderia selloana)
Clumping perennial to 3 m. NZ estimate: 1 acre at 1.8m x 1.8m spacing equals 3000 bales of hay in reserve. Deep roots bring up trace elements controlling grass tetany. Companion to poplars. See [[permaculture-ground-cover-plants]].

### Elephant Grass (Pennisetum purpureum)
Clumping perennial to 4 m, native to tropical Africa. Tolerant of different soil conditions, remarkably drought tolerant for a high-rainfall species. Very fast recovery from browsing. Propagated by placing small branches into damp soil. "Hungry" for nutrients but does not like waterlogging.

### Bana Grass (Pennisetum purpureum x americanum)
Hybrid with broader leaf, less aggressive. Produces little seed -- propagated from cuttings. Very easy: lay whole stems (up to 4 m) into shallow trenches in damp soil. Roots strike from each node.

## Additional Grain Crops

### Quinoa (Chenopodium quinoa)
Family: AMARANTHACEAE. Perennial to ~0.5 m growing in tidal flats and salty marshlands off the Gulf of California, thriving with tidal inundation of sea water. Seed falls to water and is washed ashore where traditionally harvested. Fast growing, once established should not need replanting. An exceptional grain for saline coastal [[permaculture-difficult-sites-plant-guide]] permaculture systems.

### Pearl Millet (Pennisetum typhoides)
Important grain crop for arid and semi-arid regions. Very drought tolerant. Used as both human food and livestock fodder. The hybrid "Bana Grass" (Pennisetum purpureum x americanum) is a cross between elephant grass and pearl millet used primarily for fodder. See [[fodder-growing-guide]] for integration with [[pasture-management]].

### Azolla in Rice Paddies
[[edible-aquatic-plants]] pinnata is used extensively in Vietnam as a companion in rice paddies, doubling as nitrogen fixer and fish food. Azolla fixes atmospheric nitrogen through symbiotic cyanobacteria, reducing or eliminating the need for synthetic nitrogen fertilizers. It also serves as food for pigs, poultry, and waterfowl. Connects to [[green-manure-cover-crops]] and [[soil-fertility-management]] strategies.

## Design Principles

- Integrate grain crops into polycultures rather than monoculture blocks
- Use tree crops (oaks, macadamias, carob) for long-term, low-maintenance staple production
- Aquatic grains (rice, water grasses) fit into [[faires-livestock-integration-systems]]
- Fodder grasses serve dual purpose: livestock feed and soil stabilization
- Lockup fodder systems (pampas, elephant grass, bana grass) provide drought insurance
- Combine grain production with [[nitrogen-fixing-trees-and-shrubs]] for sustainable fertility
- See [[small-scale-grain-growing]] for detailed cultivation methods and [[permaculture-tuber-crops]] for complementary staple crops
- Build [[query-how-do-mushrooms-contribute-to-soil-health]] with mulching-guide|mulching and ground covers (see [[permaculture-ground-cover-plants]])
- Select grain crops suited to your [[mollison-permaculture-two-zone-planning-and-site-layout]] for best results
- Use [[jeavons-green-manures-cover-crops]] in rotation with grain crops to maintain [[soil-fertility-management]]

See also: [[fodder-growing-guide]], [[permaculture-plant-catalog]], and [[pasture-management]].
