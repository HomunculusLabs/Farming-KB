# Photosynthesis and Chlorophyll in Cannabis

Photosynthesis is the fundamental biological process by which
cannabis plants convert light energy into chemical energy,
producing the sugars and compounds needed for growth,
[[cervantes-cannabinoids-thc-cbd-chemistry]] synthesis. Understanding this process is
essential for optimizing [[cannabis-grow-room-setup]] conditions and maximizing
plant health and yield.

## The Photosynthesis Process

[[query-what-causes-cannabis-leaves-to-curl-and-how-to-fix-it]] function as solar panels, capturing light
energy and using it to drive photosynthesis. The process
works as follows:

1. Light is captured by chlorophyll and other pigments in
   leaf cells
2. Light energy converts carbon dioxide (CO2) and water
   into glucose and oxygen
3. Glucose fuels all plant metabolic processes including
   growth, [[cannabis-glandular-trichome-types-resin-production-clarke]], and reproduction
4. Oxygen is released as a byproduct through stomata

For photosynthesis to occur, the leaf's interior tissue must
remain moist. The tiny breathing pores called stomata, found
primarily on leaf undersides, must open to allow CO2 intake
and oxygen release. When stomata open, water vapor also
escapes in a process called transpiration.

## Stomata and Gas Exchange

Stomata are microscopic pores on leaf surfaces that regulate
gas exchange and water loss. They play a critical role in
several plant processes:

- CO2 from the air enters through stomata for
  photosynthesis
- Oxygen produced by photosynthesis exits through stomata
- Water vapor exits during transpiration, creating the
  transpiration stream that pulls water and nutrients up
  from roots
- Stomata open and close in response to environmental
  conditions including light, humidity, CO2 concentration,
  and [[cervantes-nutrient-stock-solutions-concentrates]]
  supply, especially nitrogen and iron, is essential for
  chlorophyll synthesis
- Proper [[cervantes-reservoir-temperature-management]] affects
  the rate of photosynthetic reactions
- [[cervantes-light-spectrum-photomorphogenesis]]
  should be optimized for maximum chlorophyll absorption
- CO2 concentration: Atmospheric CO2 (approximately 400
  ppm) limits photosynthesis. Enrichment to 1200-1500 ppm
  can significantly increase the rate
- Temperature: Photosynthesis proceeds faster in warmer
  conditions up to an optimal range around 75-85F
  (24-29C), beyond which enzymes become less efficient
- Water availability: Drought stress causes stomata to
  close, reducing CO2 intake and photosynthesis
- Nutrient status: All essential nutrients play roles in
  maintaining the photosynthetic machinery

## Transpiration and the Transpiration Stream

Transpiration is the process by which water evaporates from
leaves through stomata. This creates a negative pressure
(pull) that draws water and dissolved nutrients up from the
roots through the xylem, called the transpiration stream.
The transpiration stream is responsible for:

- Delivering water from roots to all plant tissues
- Transporting dissolved [[cervantes-light-spectrum-photomorphogenesis]]
  for maximum chlorophyll absorption
- Supplement CO2 to increase photosynthetic rate during

- Maintain optimal humidity to keep stomata open without
  causing excessive water stress
- Ensure nutrients like nitrogen, magnesium, and iron are
  available for chlorophyll production
