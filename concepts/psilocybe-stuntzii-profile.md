# Psilocybe stuntzii Profile

psilocybe stuntzii profile Guzman & Ott is a strongly bluing psychoactive mushroom species native to the Pacific Northwest of North America. First identified in 1972 at the University of Washington in Seattle, it rapidly became one of the most significant species in terms of recreational usage in the region, second only to [[psilocybe-semilanceata]].

## Discovery and Naming

The species was first documented in the fall of 1972 when large numbers of a strongly bluing gilled mushroom with a distinct ring pattern were found at the University of Washington campus in Seattle. The fruiting bodies grew on bark mulch that had been spread widely across the campus by gardeners from a central distribution point.

Due to the distinctive bluing reaction, students at the university assumed the mushroom contained [[query-what-is-the-difference-between-psilocybin-and-psilocin]], a belief later confirmed through biochemical analysis. The sudden appearance of massive numbers of fruiting bodies inspired students to use the mushroom as a hallucinogen.

In 1976, the species was formally named Psilocybe stuntzii by Guzman and Ott, in honor of Daniel E. Stuntz, the mycologist who first collected the specimens.

### Origin Debate

It remains uncertain whether the mushroom appeared spontaneously or whether it fruited on bark debris because the mulch had been previously mixed with spawn derived from fruiting bodies originating elsewhere. Gartz notes that either scenario is possible, but the sudden mass appearance at a specific location with imported mulch suggests introduction rather than natural occurrence.

## Slang Names

- **"Blue veil"** -- Refers to the distinctive ring (veil) and blue-staining reaction
- **"Stuntzees"** -- Playful diminutive of the scientific name

## Macroscopic Description

- **Cap:** Conic to convex with a distinct umbo; chestnut brown when moist, fading to yellowish-brown when dry; develops blue to blue-green stains when handled or injured
- **Ring:** Distinct annular ring on the stem, a key identifying feature that distinguishes it from many other Pacific Northwest species
- **Bluing reaction:** Strong and rapid blue staining, particularly at the base of the stem
- **Habitat:** Bark mulch, lawns in parks, golf courses, football fields, and gardens

## Distribution and Habitat

### Primary Range

The species is concentrated in the Pacific Northwest, specifically:

- **Washington State:** Most common, particularly in the Seattle area
- **British Columbia, Canada:** Present in the Vancouver area
- **Oregon:** Western portions of the state

### Habitat Preferences

Psilocybe stuntzii is remarkably adaptable:

- Bark mulch in landscaped areas
- Lawns in parks and gardens
- Golf courses
- Football fields
- Garden beds

The species' ability to colonize bark mulch is particularly notable, as this is a relatively uncommon substrate for psychoactive mushrooms. This adaptability may explain its rapid spread across the Pacific Northwest after initial introduction.

### Fruiting Season

Under favorable conditions, Psilocybe stuntzii can fruit year-round, making it unique among Pacific Northwest psychoactive species. Most other species in the region fruit only during fall and early winter, ceasing when temperatures drop below freezing.

## Chemistry

Psilocybe stuntzii contains both [[query-what-is-the-difference-between-psilocybin-and-psilocin]] and [[query-what-is-the-difference-between-psilocybin-and-psilocin]], with the characteristic blue staining reaction indicating the presence of these alkaloids. The bluing reaction is notably strong and rapid compared to other species.

## Significance and Usage

### Regional Importance

By the early 1980s, Psilocybe stuntzii had become the second most important psychoactive mushroom species in the Pacific Northwest in terms of usage, after [[psilocybe-semilanceata]] (Liberty Cap). [[panaeolus-subbalteatus-profile]] ("red cap") is another regionally important species, though users believe it to be slightly more poisonous.

### Ecological Spread

The species' expansion across the Pacific Northwest represents a notable case study in the spread of psychoactive mushrooms through human activity. The bark mulch trade -- where wood chips are processed at central distribution points and spread across diverse locations -- served as an unwitting vector for dispersal of the species.

### Safety Record

Despite widespread usage in the Pacific Northwest, no fatalities or serious physical damage have been reported from consumption of Psilocybe stuntzii. Local newspapers have only mentioned occasional panic reactions that subsided as the acute effects wore off.
