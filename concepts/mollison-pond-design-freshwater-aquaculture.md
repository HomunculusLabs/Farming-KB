# Mollison on Pond Design and Freshwater Aquaculture

**Source:** Bill Mollison, Permaculture Design Course, Pamphlet XI (Water in Permaculture), 1981

## Overview

Mollison provides detailed practical guidance for productive pond design, species selection, nutrient management, and integrated aquaculture systems. This extends [[mollison-aquatic-polyculture-systems]] and [[mollison-fish-pond-construction]]. Freshwater aquaculture represents one of the highest-yielding food production systems per unit area, and Mollison's designs integrate ponds into the broader farm and landscape ecosystem.

## Pond Zonation and Planting

- **Edge zone**: duck potatoes (Sagittaria, 80+ species, wide climate range), arrowheads, mint (black mint for menthol distillation -- 2-3 acres = $70,000 income), blueberries, cattails

- **Shallow water**: water lilies (plant in tire with manure bag, punch holes)

- **Deeper water**: wild rice (plant seeds in mud balls; 18 inches to 3 feet depth; needs some water movement; 3-week dropping period for harvest; superb duck food)

- **Chinese water chestnut**: lives as a mud rush; Indian water chestnut floats with anchor

- **Water hyacinth**: usable in restricted locations for pollution cleanup (dangerous in large slow rivers in warm climates)

- **Fly catching plants**: Venus fly trap, pitcher plants, sundews at water's edge in soaked soils

## Nutrient Management

- **pH**: 6-8 (100-200x less acidic than garden soils); lime the whole base when constructing; monitor regularly

- **Secchi disk test**: white disc disappears at 2.5 feet = well-manured; at 1 foot = over-manured; visible at 5 feet = under-manured (add chicken manure)

- **Mussels**: filter ~200 gallons/day each; pump nitrogen and phosphorus into mud; superior phosphorus fixer; harvest modestly for chicken grit

- **Hay bales**: strew half in, half out around edge for diatom production; diatoms like hay; kick more in as it rots; ducks add manure

- **Pond fertilization**: traditional Hawaiian/Japanese method = let bark and limbs rot on pond edges (mimics beaver)

- **Dryland cycling**: drain pond every few years; shift animals to forage pond; grow heavy feeders first year, taper off, roll down and re-flood

## Fish and Animal Systems

- **Ducks**: 8 per quarter-acre; eggs plus manure production; they accelerate energy turnover in pond; wild rice grows above duck height; need island or fence+net for fox protection

- **Catfish**: low on trophic ladder; good eating; excellent for small ponds

- **Trout**: extensive (not intensive); need shaded deep escape zones; optimal >60F but active lower; eat smelt and algae; UV light + fan over pond drops insects for feed

- **Crawfish/yabbies**: 3 feet deep ponds with brush piles; use beer cans on ping-pong balls as traps; restricted-burrow species preferred (yabbies tunnel 25 feet and can bore through dam walls); 30 quarter-acre ponds = family income of $20-30K/year

- **Stickleback**: breed by millions; can convert to trout food in high-algae conditions; keep in separate forage ponds with trickle grilles

## Polyculture Pond Design

- Multiple small ponds at different pH for different species

- Trickle systems from forage ponds to main trout pond (through grilles so fish can't escape upstream)

- Sequence: duck marsh with mussels and wild rice (18" deep) --> rice/paddy --> shrimp ponds --> trout pond

- Even very small ponds (6 foot diameter) are productive -- watercress, cattails as good as any land vegetables

## Pond Construction

- Ideal: sloping or stepped floor; fully drainable; drain into another pond for dryland cycle

- After gleying, gley perpetuates itself in soil

- Six-foot sheet metal mold ponds cost ~$40; very good production units

- Sewage: holding pond --> marsh with mints/bog plants (phenomenal sewage demand) --> final pond (no solids, dissolved nutrients only)

## Integration with Permaculture Earthworks

Pond design connects deeply with [[permaculture-earthworks]] and [[backyard-water-harvesting]] principles.

- **Swale-connected ponds:** Ponds placed at the base of alexander shulgin systems receive consistent water input without the need for pumped irrigation. Swales overflow into ponds during heavy rain events, providing both flood control and water storage.

- **Dam and embankment construction:** Pond walls serve dual purposes as access roads, zone boundaries, and windbreaks. The earth excavated from the pond basin becomes the dam material, following the permaculture principle that every element should serve multiple functions.

- **Keyline integration:** [[keyline-design]] principles can guide pond placement to maximize water capture from the landscape while minimizing earthworks. Ponds sited at keypoint locations receive the full catchment runoff.

- **Cascade systems:** A series of ponds at descending elevations creates a gravity-fed cascade system. Water flows from upper to lower ponds through trickle grilles, transferring nutrients and forage organisms without requiring pumping.

## The Dryland Cycle

One of Mollison's most important practical innovations is the periodic dryland cycling of ponds.

- **Every 3-4 years:** Drain the pond and allow the nutrient-rich bottom sediments to dry.

- **Year 1 (dry):** Plant heavy-feeding crops (tomatoes, corn, squash) in the exposed pond bed. The accumulated pond sediments are extraordinarily fertile, producing exceptional yields without additional fertilization.

- **Year 2 (dry):** Plant lighter feeders or cover crops, allowing residual nutrients to be partially depleted.

- **Year 3:** Re-flood and re-stock the pond. The dryland cycle has renewed the pond bottom and broken disease and parasite cycles.

- **Parallel ponds:** Maintain at least two pond systems so that one can be in the dryland phase while the other remains flooded, ensuring continuous food production.

## Species Selection by Climate

- **Temperate climates:** Trout, catfish, crawfish, bluegill, perch, duckweed, watercress, cattails, wild rice.

- **Subtropical climates:** Tilapia, bass, catfish, Chinese water chestnut, water hyacinth (controlled), lotus.

- **Tropical climates:** Tilapia, carp, walking catfish, taro, water spinach, kangkong, lotus, water lilies.

## Economic Yields

- **Crawfish:** 30 quarter-acre ponds can generate $20,000-30,000/year family income.

- **Mint distillation:** 2-3 acres of black mint can produce $70,000 worth of menthol.

- **Wild rice:** Premium market prices; excellent duck forage that doubles as human food.

- **Duck eggs:** 8 ducks per quarter-acre produce eggs and manure simultaneously.

- **Mussels:** Free water filtration service plus harvestable chicken grit.
