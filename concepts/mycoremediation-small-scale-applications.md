## Mycoremediation: Small-Scale Applications

Mycoremediation—the use of fungi to break down environmental and industrial pollutants—encompasses approaches ranging from industrial cleanup of PCBs and oil spills to simple home-scale projects that anyone can implement. This concept covers the fundamental mechanisms of mycoremediation and practical small-scale applications for managing pet waste, poultry operations, and residential runoff.

## How Mycoremediation Works

### The Enzymatic Advantage

Fungi are decomposers and constructors—the agents of habitat renewal. With their enzyme systems "sweating" into the environment, fungi swim through their own fluids, breaking down materials into smaller molecular units absorbable through their cell walls. This process requires interaction time (called "factor time") for the chemical reactions to take place.

The key advantage of fungal enzymes over bacterial degradation is that fungal enzymes are **cell-free** (extracellular) chemical catalysts. Fungi secrete them into the environment where they begin metabolic work well ahead of the mycelium, even ahead of advancing hyphae—digesting food before contact. Bacteria, by contrast, must come into intimate contact with a compound to begin degrading it.

### Two Forms of Pollution Addressed

**Microbial contaminants**: The main mode of pathogen removal is inactivation—disrupting replication and reproduction, preventing cell synthesis and division, and eroding cell membranes. Fungi can also enforce species exclusion (occupying territory to prevent pathogen entry) or alter chemical gradients such as pH and [[ph-and-nutrient-availability-garden-soils]]. Especially useful for fecal coliforms in water, manures, livestock waste, and failing septic systems.

**Chemical contaminants**: Fungi secrete enzymes that cleave pollutant molecules into smaller, more degradable units. A species sequence approach follows, where fungal metabolites are further degraded by associated bacteria, ultimately recycling elements back into a self-perpetuating food chain. Applicable to herbicides, pesticides, fertilizers, dyes, and many other [[ingham-manure-antibiotics-chemical-residues-composting]].

### Brown Rot vs. White Rot Fungi

- **Brown rot fungi**: More aggressive at breaking down cellulose
- **[[white-rot-fungi]]**: Highly effective at breaking down lignin and complex polymers architecturally similar to lignin, including many [[singh-fungal-degradation-of-chlorinated-compounds]] found in pesticides

### Hyperaccumulation

Many fungi absorb and retain specific elements—particularly heavy metals—in their biomass through hyperaccumulation. This makes the mushrooms themselves toxic, but the biomass serves as a filter medium that can be removed, disposed of, or concentrated for reuse after treating contaminated soil, water, or air.

## Simple Erosion Control and Water Filtration

Myceliated wood chips or bales of wheat straw can improve soil water-holding capacity and reduce erosion on sloped areas. A practical application: place a barrier of myceliated wood chips at the lowest point of your property. Rain washes fecal coliforms and other contaminants into the fungal filter, where they are trapped and remediated before reaching the local watershed.

## Dog and Cat Waste Management

### Cat Litter Remediation

With an estimated 86.4 million domestic cats in the United States, cat litter waste is significant. Effective fungal [[combined-biological-remediation-approaches]]:

- **Best litter types**: Wheat gluten or finely powdered corncob debris (wheat and corn clump rather than just absorbing, making soiled litter easier to scoop)
- **Pine caution**: Pine-based litters are largely incompatible with cultivated fungi, though [[neolentinus-lepideus]] isolates suitable for pine are in development
- **Best species**: King stropharia and oyster mushrooms

**Procedure**: Scoop soiled litter into a lidded bucket, periodically covering with clean, wet biodegradable litter inoculated with fresh spawn. Mycelium threads through the substrate, decomposing and deodorizing within ~12 weeks. Run the myceliated litter through vermicomposting before mixing into garden compost.

**Alternative using spent substrate**: [[spent-mushroom-substrate]] from primary decomposers (like oyster mushrooms) can be dried and shredded as litter. When changing the litter, dump it into a bucket outdoors in a well-ventilated spot for a couple of weeks to reduce ammonia. Rehydrate with warm water to revive the mycelium, which consumes the waste. Vermicompost the fully colonized mixture before garden use.

### Dog Waste Remediation

With ~74.3 million dogs in the United States producing waste that often ends up in landfills or pollutes waterways as runoff:

1. Designate a 5-gallon lidded bucket for collection
2. Periodically add spawned substrate: moistened hardwood sawdust, wood chips, spent coffee grounds, or wet layers of newspaper sprinkled with spawn
3. Occasionally lift and drop the bucket from a few inches high to tamp the mixture, increasing density and mycelial contact points
4. Once materials stop being added, allow 2–3 weeks for full colonization—the bucket becomes a firm block of mycelium smelling sweet rather than putrid
5. Start a new bucket while the first finishes
6. Remove the finished mycelium cake (invert bucket and tap sides) into a worm composting bin in a shady location for decomposition into topsoil and worm castings

## City Chicken Coop Fungal Filters

Urban [[chicken-keeping]] is growing, but odor control and coliform bacteria from poultry droppings present challenges. King stropharia (Stropharia rugoso-annulata) provides an effective living fungal filter that eliminates odor and converts waste into rich soil amendment.

### Benefits Documented

- Complete elimination of coop odor (reported as "the best-smelling chicken run" by visitors)
- Effective use of excess nitrates and phosphates from poultry waste
- Attraction of earthworms and collaborator organisms building rich, sweet-smelling soil
- Fungal filters last approximately one year before becoming excellent garden amendment filled with worm castings

### Materials Needed

- **Hardwood chips**: Super fresh (harvested within 4 weeks), no conifer mixed in. Available from power-line companies, college campuses, and city tree trimming crews
- **Poultry wire**: Separates the base mycelial layer from the upper scratchable layer
- **King stropharia sawdust spawn**: ~5 lb bag covers ~1 cubic yard of chips
- **Garden tools**: Wheelbarrow, shovel, rake, hoe

### Volume Calculation

Measure coop dimensions with 1 foot extra on each side. Multiply length × width × 0.75 ft (8 inches depth) for cubic feet, then divide by 27 for cubic yards. Round up for extra coverage.

Example: A 7 ft × 9 ft coop calculated at 9 ft × 11 ft = 99 sq ft × 0.75 ft = 74.25 cubic feet ÷ 27 = 2.75 cubic yards → round up to 3.

### Installation Steps

1. Transfer birds to another area during installation
2. Rake out the coop and lay down a 1-inch layer of wood chips
3. Break up the spawn bag well and sprinkle spawn over the chip surface, raking in gently
4. Repeat layering until inoculated chips reach ~2 inches less than the total desired depth
5. Install poultry wire over the entire surface—this keeps chickens from digging deep enough to destroy the mycelium while allowing them to scratch and harvest worms in the top layer
6. Add the remaining wood chips on top of the wire to reach the full 8-inch depth
7. The mycelium will colonize the lower chips over several weeks while chickens work the upper layer, depositing nitrogen-rich waste that filters down to feed the fungal network
8. Replace the entire fungal filter annually—the spent material becomes premium garden amendment

## Important Safety Principles

- **Never assume complete remediation**: Just because material has passed through a fungal filter does not guarantee all contaminants are eliminated. Testing is always critical.
- **Not a license to pollute**: Mycoremediation should complement responsible practices, not replace them.
- **Species exclusion has limits**: Fungi can suppress but may not eliminate all pathogens.
- **Vermicomposting is a necessary intermediate step**: Never mix partially remediated pet or poultry waste directly into food garden compost without thorough worm decomposition first.

## Practical Applications
The principles discussed here have direct applications in agricultural systems, ecological restoration, and sustainable resource management.
Practitioners have demonstrated successful implementation across diverse climates and soil conditions.
Adaptation to local conditions and careful observation remain central to effective application.

## Key Considerations
Context-specific implementation requires attention to local ecology, climate patterns, and community needs.
Integration with existing systems often yields better results than complete replacement strategies.
