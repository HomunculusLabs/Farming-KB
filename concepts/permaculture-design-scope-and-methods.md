# [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-aquaculture-and-water-systems]] Design Scope and Methods

## Overview

[[permaculture-design]] encompasses a vast interdisciplinary scope, integrating concepts from ecology, agriculture, forestry, hydrology, climatology, sociology, economics, and law. The [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-permaculture-design-principles]]ers' Manual systematically covers these domains through 14 chapters, each addressing a critical component of sustainable human settlement design.

## Design Concepts and Themes

The conceptual framework of permaculture design includes several fundamental themes:

### Resources and Yields
Permaculture distinguishes between different categories of resources — those that increase with use, those that decrease with use, and those that are unaffected by use. Yields are defined broadly as any product or service a system provides, not just food. The concept of yield extends to energy capture, water purification, habitat creation, and social benefits.

### Cycles
Every element in a permaculture system participates in cycles — nutrient cycles, water cycles, energy cycles, and information cycles. Design aims to close loops so that the output of one element becomes the input for another, reducing waste and increasing overall system efficiency.

### Pyramids, Food Webs, and Growth
Energy in ecosystems flows through trophic levels in pyramid structures. Permaculture design works with these natural energy flows, recognizing that complex food webs with many connections are more resilient than simple chains. The relationship between growth, yield, and system maturity is a key consideration in design.

### Complexity and Connections
Resilient systems are characterized by high connectivity between elements. Complexity [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-guild-design-in-permaculture]] is not random but is the result of thoughtful assembly of components that support each other. The number of beneficial connections between elements is a measure of system health.

### Diversity, Stability, and Time
Diversity contributes to stability but only when the diverse elements are functionally connected. A diverse collection of unrelated elements does not create stability. The timing of yields, [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-succession-and-ecological-evolution]] of species, and evolution of systems over time are critical design considerations.

### Permitted and Forced Functions
Every element in a system performs multiple functions, and every function is supported by multiple elements. Designers distinguish between "permitted functions" (behaviors an element naturally performs) and "forced functions" (behaviors imposed by design). Good design works with permitted functions rather than forcing elements into unnatural roles.

## Methods of Design

The manual presents multiple complementary design methodologies:

### Analysis
Design by listing the characteristics, needs, products, and behaviors of each component in the system. This methodical approach ensures designers understand what each element requires and what it provides, enabling intelligent placement and connection of elements.

### Observation
Design by expanding on direct observations of a site. This method emphasizes the importance of spending time on a site before making design decisions, understanding its microclimates, water flows, existing vegetation, wildlife patterns, and human use patterns.

### Deduction from Nature
Design by adopting lessons learned from natural ecosystems. Nature has been designing sustainable systems for billions of years. By observing how natural systems organize themselves, designers can extract principles and apply them to human-designed systems.

### Data Overlay
Design by map overlays, where multiple layers of information (slope, water flow, sun exposure, wind patterns, soil types, vegetation) are overlaid to identify optimal placement for different elements.

### Zone and Sector Analysis
Zone analysis organizes the landscape by frequency of use — elements needing daily attention are placed closest to the dwelling, while those needing less attention are placed farther away. Sector analysis maps incoming energies (sun, wind, fire, flood, noise, views) to design passive harvesting or shielding strategies.

### Guilds
Guilds are assemblies of plants, animals, and other organisms that support each other's growth and productivity. In nature, guilds form around central elements (like a mature tree), with understory plants, nitrogen fixers, pest deterrents, and mulch producers all contributing to the health of the whole.

### Succession
Understanding [[ecological-succession]] — how ecosystems evolve from pioneer species to mature climax communities — allows designers to accelerate or guide natural succession toward desired endpoints. This includes the establishment and maintenance phases of system development.

## Pattern Understanding

Permaculture design draws heavily on the study of natural patterns. The manual presents a comprehensive pattern language including:

- **General pattern models** of events and processes
- **Matrices** and strategies for compacting and complexing components
- **Boundary conditions** and the harmonics and geometries of boundaries
- **Spirals**, branching patterns, and flow patterns
- **Toroidal phenomena** — closed-loop energy and material flows
- **Dimensions and potentials** — understanding how pattern changes with scale

The application of pattern understanding extends from landscape design to social organization, with the principle that similar patterns recur at different scales (the concept of "fractal" or self-similar organization, though Mollison describes it in his own terms).

## [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-climatic-factors-in-design]]

Permaculture design is inherently climate-specific. The manual covers:

- Classification of broad climatic zones
- Patterning in global weather systems
- Precipitation patterns and water availability
- Solar radiation and its effects on design
- Wind patterns and their management
- Landscape effects on local climate
- Latitude effects on day length and seasons

## Water, Soils, and Earthworks

Three chapters address the physical infrastructure of sustainable landscapes:

### Water
Regional intervention in the water cycle, earthworks for [[water-conservation]] and storage, reduction of water used in sewage systems, purification of polluted waters, and natural swimming pools.

### Soils
Soil structure, nutrients, pH, soil biota (the living organisms in soil), difficult soils, plant analysis for mineral deficiencies, biological indicators of soil conditions, soil erosion, and rehabilitation techniques.

### Earthworks
Planning earthworks, slope measurement, leveling techniques, types of earthworks (swales, dams, ponds, terraces), and earth resources.

## Climate-Specific Strategies

The manual devotes three chapters to strategies for different climate types:

- **Humid Tropics** — tropical soils, house design, tropical home [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-forest-gardens-and-food-forests]], integrated land management, polycultures
- **[[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-dryland-strategies-and-desert-agriculture]]** — water harvesting, desert houses and gardens, irrigation systems, desertification prevention
- **Humid Cool to Cold Climates** — settlement design, home gardens, orchards, farm forestry, free-range forage systems, wildfire management

## Aquaculture

Aquaculture [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-animal-systems-in-permaculture]] encompasses fish farming, invertebrate cultivation, channel and chinampa systems, and traditional and new water polycultures. The approach emphasizes integrated systems where aquaculture supports and is supported by terrestrial agriculture.

## Social and Economic Design

The final chapter addresses the broader social context, including:

- Ethical basis for alternative communities
- Bioregional organization
- Trusts and legal strategies for land tenure
- Village development
- Money and finance alternatives
- Land access strategies
- Ethical investment
- Effective aid programs
