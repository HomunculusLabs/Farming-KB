# The Logos and Translinguistic Matter

Central to [[mckenna-tryptamine-hallucinogens-and-consciousness]].

## Translinguistic Matter and Hyperspace

McKenna coined the term "translinguistic matter" to describe the substance
or medium through which the Logos communicates. In the DMT state, one
encounters objects that are "feeling-toned, meaning-toned, three-dimensional
rotating complexes of transforming light and color" that convey information
through a language that cannot be translated into ordinary speech.

This translinguistic matter operates at a level prior to and more
fundamental than ordinary language. McKenna described it as "the assembly
language that lies behind language," comparable to [[mckenna-glossolalia-and-primal-language-tryptamine-states]] in The [[mckenna-philo-judaeus-logos-hearing-to-beholding]]'s description of the Logos: a transition
"from things heard to things beheld." In the tryptamine state, language
becomes visible. One does not merely hear the Logos; one sees it
manifesting as three-dimensional forms, as evolving surfaces, as
self-transforming geometric patterns that carry meaning and intent.

This synesthetic quality of the experience is crucial. The Logos
communicates through a medium that is simultaneously auditory, visual,
and cognitive. The entities one encounters speak in sounds "like music,
like language," but these sounds "pass without any quantized moment of
distinction" from hearing to seeing. The communication is holistic rather
than sequential.

## Glossolalia and Primal Language

The tryptamine experience sometimes induces glossolalia, but unlike the
classical speaking-in-tongues phenomenon studied in religious contexts,
this hallucinogen-induced version is described by McKenna as a brain state
that allows expression of the assembly language underlying all language.
The speaker is not in a trance of possession but is consciously
experimenting with vocal output, discovering that one can "make the
extra-dimensional objects" respond to sound.

This suggests that human language may be a shadow or derivative of a more
fundamental form of communication that is directly accessible in the
tryptamine state. McKenna speculated that a human language might be
possible in which the intent of meaning is actually beheld in
three-dimensional space, accessible to cultural development over
thousands of years.

## The Mushroom Consciousness and the Logos

McKenna connected the Logos to what he called "the mushroom consciousness,"
describing it as "the consciousness of the Other in hyperspace, which means
in dream and in the psilocybin trance, at the quantum foundation of being,
in the human future, and after death." All of these domains, normally
considered separate, are revealed in the tryptamine state to be part of a
single continuum unified by the Logos.
