# Psychedelic Experience Preparation

Proper preparation is the single most important factor in determining the quality and safety of a psychedelic experience. Turner emphasizes that the main reason for negative experiences is lack of preparation, rather than anything inherent in the person or in the substance. One's chances of having a bad trip, or not being able to reintegrate afterwards, can be sharply reduced by learning about the experience beforehand.

## Physical Preparation

### Dosage Approach
- Start with a small dose and work up when working with a new substance
- Monitor how body and mind respond to determine which dosage works
- Some people have powerful reactions to amounts that produce only minimal activity in most
- LSD has a wide margin between active dose and toxic dose; ecstasy can exhibit toxic effects at just twice the average dose
- Never assume dose accuracy with street substances — blotter, tablets, and capsules may contain unknown amounts

### Health Considerations
- Anyone with health problems, or taking prescription or over-the-counter medicines, should exercise caution
- Most psychedelics increase pulse rate and blood pressure
- Pregnant women should consult a knowledgeable doctor prior to use
- See [[mao-inhibitors-and-psychedelics]] for critical MAOI interaction warnings — 5-MeO-DMT and Harmala alkaloids are MAO inhibitors

### Body State
- Psychedelics are best enjoyed when the body and mind are in good health, properly nourished, exercised, and rested
- Taking psychedelics when exhausted tends to produce more negative experiences
- Exhaustion disrupts normal patterns of appetite and sleep, which psychedelics further disrupt
- Fasting or dietary restriction prior to tripping can reduce or eliminate nausea
- Indigenous people say Peyote and Ayahuasca act as a "purge," cleansing body and soul

### Practical Safety
- Never drive, fly a plane, or operate machinery while under the influence
- A large dose produces massive changes in consciousness — one may not recall their own name
- Plan for a full day devoted to the experience without transportation needs
- Have an extra day after the trip to recuperate before returning to work
- Avoiding familiar routines for a day allows retention of more from the journey

## Mental Preparation

### Understanding Set and Setting

Much of each person's experience is based on SET and SETTING:

- **Set**: A person's mental and emotional makeup, expectations about the experience, cultural upbringing, etc.
- **Setting**: The immediate environment and stimulus present during the experience — outdoors or indoors, with friends or alone, presence or absence of music and artwork

### The Ideal Setting

Turner's recommendation for the most conducive setting: one that most closely resembles the time-honoured methods of using these substances:

- Taking a natural psychedelic like mushrooms or Peyote
- Outdoors in nature, away from the noise and activity of any city
- With a group of respected friends or family
- For novices: guided by trusted and experienced "elders"

### Understanding the Psychedelic Process

Psychedelics dissolve one's identity and perceptual framework — a process commonly called "ego death." What follows is a predictable model:

1. **Dissolution**: Identity and perceptual framework are dissolved
2. **Raw energies**: Experience of raw, undifferentiated energies flowing through the senses from a formless state
3. **Creative exploration**: Tapping into vast banks of mental imagery, generating limitless visions of intricacy, beauty, and meaning
4. **Trying on filters**: Experiencing various perceptual frameworks, hallucinations, or personalities
5. **Re-assembly**: Building a new personality based on the old one, hopefully improved

### Preparation for Ego Death

- Be willing to let go of your former identity as it is being dissolved
- Don't hold on to beautiful feelings or visions if they start to fade
- Don't be afraid of letting go
- If having a frightful experience, don't run away from it — look at it and see what you can learn
- There is no need to spend an entire psychedelic session wrapped up in one issue or feeling

### Know the Intensity Scale

Turner assigns intensity levels from 1 to 10 for each psychedelic. The wiser users gradually move from milder to more intense substances. A high dose of LSD can produce level 10 intensity. Since the experience is in the mind, one can even have a high intensity trip with a small dose.

| Substance | Intensity Range |
|-----------|----------------|
| Ecstasy | 1-2 |
| Mescaline | 3-8 |
| LSD | 3-7 |
| Psilocybin | 3-7 |
| 2C-B | 3-7 |
| Ketamine | 5-10 |
| DMT | 9-10 |

## Psychological Readiness

### Who Should Be Cautious
- People with schizophrenia or any mental disorder beyond common neurosis should exercise extreme caution
- When psychedelic research was widespread until 1966, there were many successful results treating mental patients
- However, people with mental imbalances have greater risk of a negative experience, especially without therapist guidance

### Natural vs. Synthetic
- Turner notes he has never heard of anyone experiencing long-term adverse effects through natural psychedelics (psilocybin mushrooms, mescaline cacti)
- When disturbing experiences occur with natural substances, users seem to take it in stride and return to center
- Synthetic substances (ecstasy, Ketamine, LSD) carry somewhat more risk of overuse

### Integration Mindset
- People who try to manifest the lessons learned through psychedelic use benefit the most
- Examples: using psychedelics to modify personality or thinking, as part of a healing process, or with creative outlets (art, music)
- People who do not go through personal change find their experiences tend to stagnate and get repetitive
- Stopping for a while, pursuing other interests, then returning later can break the stagnation

## Frequency and Sustainability

- How often one can effectively use psychedelics varies from person to person
- Users must monitor their own mind, body, and life, as well as listening to friends
- Traditionally, only shamans use psychedelics daily — in service of healing their communities
- For the shaman, the psychedelic experience is a well from which to bring back "healing water" and knowledge
- A small percentage of users "abuse" psychedelics by taking more than their psyche or body can integrate

## Expecting Difficult Experiences

- Anyone who regularly uses psychedelics stronger than ecstasy can expect an occasional unpleasant experience
- Even experienced trippers will occasionally have a bad experience
- Turner has had several trips during which he thought he was dead, or could not tell if alive or dead
- If using psychedelics frequently, one will eventually confront nearly all the skeletons buried within their psyche
- Approach with willingness to deal with whatever issues come up
- One generally learns more from unpleasant trips than from the average psychedelic session

## Nausea and Purging

- Nausea is common on many psychedelics
- If feeling the urge to vomit, don't resist it — users generally feel better afterwards
- Nausea is not an indication that something is wrong with the trip
- Indigenous people consider the purge as cleansing the body and soul
