# Bushman Scrabbling Existence Is Not True Nature

## Overview

Masanobu Fukuoka used the example of the African Bushman to challenge the romantic notion that primitive peoples live in harmony with nature. While praising the Bushman's character and spirit, Fukuoka argued that the barren environment they inhabit is evidence not of [[comparison-natural-farming-vs-permaculture]] aspires.

## The Bushman's Character

Fukuoka was unequivocally impressed by the Bushman as a person:

> "The Bushman himself is fine. That dazzlingly bright, cheerful face says it all. I felt as if I knew where it came from. Civilized society may mock and make fun of him, but all that is of no concern to him. This is the face of a natural man able to immerse himself in his own joy."

The Bushman's cheerfulness and authenticity stood in stark contrast to what Fukuoka had observed in New York City, where affluent people wore "tragic, cornered" expressions. The Bushman, despite material poverty, displayed a spiritual wealth that civilized society had lost.

## The Problem: Barren Environment

But Fukuoka was troubled by the environment in which the Bushman lived:

> "I saw him digging up some kind of root with a stick. He had to go quite a ways to find that root and also to fetch firewood. That means that nature is scarce there."

He observed that the Bushman had to travel great distances to find basic necessities — roots and firewood. This effort was not the leisurely gathering of a person living amid abundance, but the desperate scrabbling of someone eking out an existence in a depleted landscape.

## Not a Primitive Life but an Unnatural One

Fukuoka rejected the idea that the Bushman represented a window into pristine, pre-agricultural nature:

> "His seems to be a natural existence but is nothing of the sort. The nature in the background is just too poor. I would say in fact that this is an unnatural existence."

He argued that if the Bushman truly led a primitive life in a functioning [[cho-loess-powder-in-natural-farming]], "there would be a greater abundance of nature around."
