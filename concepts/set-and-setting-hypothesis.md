## Set and Setting Hypothesis

## Overview

The set and setting hypothesis is the foundational principle governing the
nature and quality of psychedelic experiences. It holds that the primary
determinants of a psychedelic experience are not the pharmacological
properties of the drug itself, but rather the internal psychological state

- **Motivation**: The deeper psychological drives that lead a person to
  seek a psychedelic experience — curiosity, therapeutic need, spiritual
  seeking, or social bonding.
- **Emotional state**: The person's mood and emotional condition at the
  time of ingestion, including unresolved anxieties, traumas, or joys.
- **Personality structure**: Long-standing psychological patterns, defenses,
  and cognitive styles that shape how experiences are processed.

## Definition of Setting

"Setting" refers to the external context in which the psychedelic experience
occurs:

- **Physical environment**: The room, building, or natural setting where the
  experience takes place — its comfort, beauty, safety, and freedom from
  intrusion.
- **Social context**: The presence of other people, their roles (guide,
  friend, stranger), their behavior, and the quality of interpersonal
  dynamics during the experience.
- **Music and sound**: Auditory environment, including music selection,
  singing, drumming, or silence.
- **Lighting**: Brightness, color, and quality of light — darkness
  facilitates visions while bright light tends to ground the experience
  in ordinary reality.
