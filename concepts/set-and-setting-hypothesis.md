# Set and Setting Hypothesis

## Overview

The set and setting hypothesis is one of the most important theoretical
frameworks in psychedelic research and practice. Introduced by [[ralph-metzner]] and their colleagues at Harvard
University in the early 1960s, it holds that the primary determinants of a
psychedelic experience are the internal set (intention, expectation,
motivation) and the external setting or context, including the presence of a
guide or therapist — rather than the pharmacological properties of the drug
itself.

## Historical Development

The concept was formalized by Leary, Litwin and Metzner in 1963, based on
their research with [[set-and-setting-framework]] is closely related to the amplifier analogy
that has been used by researchers in both the psycholytic and psychedelic
traditions. According to this analogy, the psychedelic drug functions as a
nonspecific amplifier of psychic contents — it amplifies whatever is present
in the mind of the experiencer, whether that is fear or love, trauma or
transcendence.

The amplification may occur in part through a lowering of sensory thresholds —
what [[aldous-huxley]] called "a cleansing of the doors of perception" — and in
part through not-yet-understood central processes involving one or more
neurotransmitters. The set and setting determine what material is available
to be amplified.

## The Microscope Metaphor

Complementary to the amplifier analogy is the microscope metaphor. It has
been repeatedly said that psychedelics could play the same role in psychology
as the microscope does in biology — opening up to direct, repeatable,
verifiable observation realms and processes of the human mind that have
hitherto been largely hidden or inaccessible.

Just as the microscope revealed an entire previously invisible world of cells
and microorganisms, psychedelics reveal previously inaccessible dimensions of
consciousness, emotional processing, and cognitive function. The set and
setting determine what aspect of this inner world is brought into focus.

## Shamanic Parallels

The set and setting model finds a natural parallel in indigenous shamanic
practices, where the ritual framework serves precisely the function of
structuring the set and setting for entheogenic experiences. Shamanic rituals
involving hallucinogens are, in Metzner's formulation, "the intentional
arrangement of the set and the setting for purposes of healing and
divination."

The three distinctive features of traditional shamanic ceremonies — minimal
talking among participants, essential role of singing or chanting, and
performance in darkness or low light — all serve to optimize the set and
setting for therapeutic and divinatory purposes. These features have been
incorporated into modern hybrid therapeutic-shamanic rituals.
