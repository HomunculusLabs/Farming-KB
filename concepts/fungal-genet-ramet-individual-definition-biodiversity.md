## Implications for Sampling Design

### Population and Community Metrics

Population and community metrics must accommodate data that are not based solely on the delineation of individuals. For analyses of fungal biodiversity, each investigator must establish a unit by which presence-absence, biomass, density, or frequency of component species can be estimated over space and time.

Key requirements:
- Biomass, density, or frequency must be based on the same unit of collection
- Data must be obtained in the same manner throughout the spatial and temporal domain
- The operational definition of "individual" must be clearly stated and relevant to the taxon of interest
- Comparisons among ecosystems require consistent definitions

### Operational Definitions by Ecological Group

The operational definition for an individual will differ for different taxonomic or ecological groups of fungi. The Biodiversity of Fungi provides recommended operational definitions for each major group:

- **Ectomycorrhizal fungi:** Often defined by root tip colonization or sporocarp production
- **Saprotrophic microfungi:** Typically defined by colony formation on isolation plates
- **Wood-inhabiting macrofungi:** Counted as sporocarps per unit area or substrate piece
- **Leaf litter fungi:** Isolates per unit mass of leaf material
- **Soil fungi:** Colonies per dilution plate or DNA sequence per soil sample
