## The Source of the Religious Impulse

Powell suspects that the very historical source of Homo sapiens' religious
impulse lies in our ancestors' primeval encounters with raw entheogenic
species like the psilocybin mushroom, which is effective without the need for
elaborate preparation. This scenario does not lessen religion; it gives it
an unstoppable impetus created through the effect of visionary alkaloids in
opening up the boundless capacities of the human mind. However, once detailed
knowledge of the plant or fungus is lost in time, any lingering memory of its
original entheogenic power becomes mere words, an echo of a once living
mystery.

## A Naturalistic Framework for Religious Experience

The greatest reason to embrace an entheogenic explanation for the rise of
the religious impulse is that it is couched in wholly naturalistic terms,
lending itself to scientific study. If a person claims to have had a
life-changing theophany, that is one thing; but if they bear in their hand
the very method by which they attained such an experience, researchers are
obliged to explore and verify the means. In this way, psychedelic plants and fungi
must be taken seriously in their role in the development of religious
ideology. Their historical influence can never be overestimated.
