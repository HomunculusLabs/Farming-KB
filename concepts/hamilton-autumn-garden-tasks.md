# Autumn Garden Tasks

Autumn is one of the busiest and most important seasons in the gardening
year. The work done now lays the foundations for next year's successes,
from soil preparation and planting to protection and tidying. It is a
time of harvest and reflection, of clearing away the old and making
ready for the new. The [[hamilton-seasonal-gardening-calendar]] places enormous emphasis on autumn tasks across the
ornamental garden, vegetable plot, fruit garden, and greenhouse.

## Soil and Bed Preparation

Autumn is the prime time for [[hamilton-digging-and-cultivation]]
heavy soils. Work in manure or [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]]
as soon as land becomes vacant after harvesting. Keep off wet soil —
cover an area with plastic sheeting so you can cultivate it even when
the rest of the garden is too wet to work.

For lighter soils, sow [[hamilton-green-manure-crops-and-cover-cropping]] to cover vacant land through winter, preventing nutrient
leaching and protecting soil structure from rain damage. Dig in any
spring-sown green manure crops that have finished.

Start collecting fallen leaves for [[hamilton-leaf-mold-making-using]] — either compost them separately or mix with shredded prunings.
There is usually an enormous quantity of compost material available at
this time of year. If bins are full, use some as sheet compost spread
directly on vacant beds.

## The Ornamental Garden

Continue watering borders if the weather is very dry, especially newly
planted specimens. Lightly fork over any vacant ground in the borders
and apply a mulch of compost or [[hamilton-animal-manure-types-and-use]].

Finish planting spring-flowering bulbs (other than tulips, which are
best left until November). Plant lilies, hardy perennials, alpines, and
heathers. Sow sweet peas in a [[hamilton-cold-frames-and-hardening-off]]. Start taking [[hamilton-taking-cuttings-softwood-hardwood]] of deciduous shrubs.

Prune climbing and rambling roses and weeping standards. Trim
coniferous trees and [[hamilton-hedges-and-windbreaks]]. Dig up,
divide, and replant perennials that are old, crowded, or restricting
neighbouring shrubs. Dig up and store half-hardy perennials and tender
bulbs such as gladioli.

Begin planting bare-root deciduous trees, shrubs, and hedging plants.
If the ground is frozen when plants arrive, heel them in temporarily or
cover the roots with moist material and wrap in burlap. Continue
planting as conditions allow throughout late autumn.

## The Vegetable Garden

Keep leaves and vegetable debris cleaned up to prevent the spread of
disease. Start digging and storing root vegetables, except parsnips
which improve in flavour after frost. Protect late fruiting vegetables
with [[hamilton-cloches-and-frost-protection]]. Earth up celery,
leeks, and celeriac for blanching.

Lift and store main crop onions and potatoes. Burn potato foliage if
there has been any sign of blight. Transplant spring cabbages to their
winter positions. Cut down the tops of runner beans and [[hamilton-pod-seed-vegetables-organic]]
plants, leaving the roots in the soil to release their fixed nitrogen.

In late autumn, sow broad beans outside, secure bird netting over
winter brassicas, and protect cauliflowers from frost by bending outer
leaves over the heads. Remove yellowing leaves of cole crops and
compost them. Take up all stakes and poles, clean them, and store for
next year.

## The Fruit Garden

Complete summer pruning of all trained trees. Mulch autumn-fruiting
strawberries with straw and protect with bird netting. Pick and store
late apples, pears, and quinces. Winter-prune currants and gooseberries
after leaf fall.

Put greasebands around apple and cherry trees to trap winter moth.
Take [[hamilton-taking-cuttings-softwood-hardwood]]
of currants and gooseberries. Check apple, pear, and quince trees for
canker and cut out any affected wood. Plant bare-root trees, bushes,
and rootstocks.

Inspect fruit in storage regularly and remove any showing signs of
deterioration. Check and repair fruit cage netting. Cover soft fruit
bushes with bird netting as berries ripen.

## Greenhouse Tasks

Reduce ventilation and spraying down. Check that heaters are working
before cold weather arrives. Start closing the greenhouse at night and
begin heating if necessary. Continue sowing winter lettuce in trays.

Take fuchsia and geranium cuttings. Sow cyclamen. If the greenhouse is
heated and large enough, bring the wormery inside — the worms will
keep working through winter and provide a steady supply of
[[hamilton-worm-composting-manure]]. Start planting vines,
peaches, and nectarines under glass.

## Summary

Autumn is the garden's most critical maintenance season. Soil
preparation, planting, protection, and tidying all converge in a busy
few weeks that determine much of next year's success. Prioritise
digging and manuring heavy soils, collecting leaves for compost,
planting bare-root trees and shrubs, and protecting vulnerable crops
from early frosts. A well-executed autumn programme ensures the garden
enters winter in good heart and emerges in spring ready to grow.

### Tool Maintenance and Shed Organisation

Autumn is the ideal time to clean, sharpen, and oil garden
tools before winter storage. Hamilton stresses cleaning soil
from secateurs, spades, and forks with a wire brush, then
wiping metal parts with an oiled rag to prevent rust. Wooden
handles benefit from a rub with linseed oil to prevent
cracking. Blunt tools damage plants and make work harder,
so sharpening edges on a whetstone is time well spent.
