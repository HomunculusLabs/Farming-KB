Holmgren's treatment of livestock goes beyond the typical permaculture discussion of integration to address the deeper question of what role animals play in systems adapted to [[permaculture-retrofit-suburbia]].

## Animals in Energy Descent

During energy descent, plant and animal species that have been marginalised by industrial agriculture will regain importance. The "chook tractor" exemplifies the permaculture approach: the natural scratching action of fowl cultivates soil, replaces insecticides through foraging, and produces manure that replaces energy-intensive fertilisers. See [[duck-keeping-guide-detailed]].

## The Melliodora Animal System

At Holmgren's [[holmgren-design-patterns-details-detailed]] demonstration site, animals are integrated through careful design:

- Black Australorp poultry chosen for their ability to be contained by standard-height fences, reducing damage to newly planted vegetables

- Nitrogen-fixing tagasaste and wattle shrubs hard-pruned for goat fodder and tree mulch, preventing excessive moisture competition with vegetables and fruit trees

- Movable electric mesh fencing controls grazing of goats and foraging of poultry in the orchard (actual annual electricity use less than that for a regularly used light globe)

- Grazing animals gradually replacing the motorised slasher as converters of seasonal pasture surplus

## The Complementarity of Technology and Biology

At Melliodora, the approach is not anti-technology but wisely complementary:

- A motorised slasher serves as a powerful tool for initial conversion of brambles, weeds and rough pasture to mulch and worm food

- Over time, biological systems (grazing animals) gradually replace the mechanical solution

- Electric fencing provides precise control with minimal energy input

## Working Animals

Before industrialisation, domesticated beasts of burden -- especially horses, but also dogs, bullocks, donkeys, camels and elephants -- were as important as pyrotechnic skills in increasing human well-being and power to tap natural resources. Unlike fire, which appeared to come from human brilliance, working animals required humans to care and respect nature in a way that could be as intimate and empathetic as the relationships between people.

The value of a well-bred, well-trained working bullock or horse could be greater than that of a human worker. The loss of these working relationships represents a significant reduction in both practical capacity and human connection to animal nature. See [[holmgren-obtain-a-yield-detailed]].

## Conservation vs Use

Those working to combine traditional skills and knowledge with the latest understanding in non-violent training of animals, and who make creative use of readily available modern technology in combination with animals, exemplify Permaculture in action as much as the horticulturalist saving heirloom varieties or maintaining the skills of grafting.

The current situation where most indigenous wildlife species have achieved the dubious distinction of being both worthless and "sacred" is problematic. When wild resources have low value, the cheapest and most wasteful harvesting methods are used and there is little care or management to sustain the resource. Sanctity in indigenous cultures was founded on the premise of very high material value of wild resources generally -- the most valuable became sacred.

## Sustainable Hunting Principles

The principles of sustainable wild yield apply equally to wildlife management:

- Young, barely mature animals (especially males) should be culled to maintain genetic vigour

- Dominant breeding animals should be preserved as the genetic and knowledge repository of the population

- No farmer would kill his prize bull for meat, yet this behaviour has often prevailed in culling wild animals

- Traditional rabbit trappers "farmed" rabbits by not setting traps on breeding mounds

## Livestock and the Future Farm

In energy descent, more diverse and integrated uses of farm land will develop, which will be much more labour-intensive. Animals will play essential roles in nutrient cycling, pest control, vegetation management, and food production. The industrial model of concentrated animal feeding operations (CAFOs) will become untenable as energy costs rise and the environmental consequences of concentration become unbearable.

Small-scale, diversified livestock systems integrated with cropping and forestry represent the permaculture model for animal agriculture. Poultry in orchards, pigs in woodland, cattle on pasture with rotational grazing, ducks in rice paddies -- all these integrated systems produce multiple yields while reducing inputs and building soil fertility. See [[duck-rice-paddy]] and [[fukuoka-duck-rice-fish-polyculture]].

## Fodder Trees and Shrubs

Tree fodder systems provide another integration point. Holmgren's own work on Trees on the Treeless Plains demonstrated that tagasaste, wattle, and other nitrogen-fixing shrubs can provide significant livestock fodder while improving soil fertility. The hard pruning of these shrubs serves multiple functions: fodder production, mulch generation, moisture management, and light regulation for understory crops.

## The Ethics of Animal Use

The permaculture ethics of earth care, people care, and fair share provide a framework for considering animal use that goes beyond both industrial exploitation and absolutist abolitionism. Animals in permaculture systems are valued for their services, their products, and their role in ecological cycles. The question is not whether to use animals but how to design systems where animals can express their natural behaviours while contributing to system productivity.
