## Failure on Sandy Soils

On sandy soils, drip systems are particularly ineffective. Water applied through drip emitters moves straight down through the sand profile without spreading laterally. This means almost no horizontal wetting of the soil occurs. Roots in the dry zones between emitters receive no moisture, and the overall root development of the crop is severely restricted. On sandy soil, overhead sprinkling is far more effective because it wets the entire surface uniformly, allowing water to penetrate broadly.

## When Drip May Be Justified

Solomon concedes that high-quality, long-lasting drip lines might be useful for permanent plantings such as raspberries in heavier soils. In perennial plantings where the drip lines can be left undisturbed and the plants are always in the same position, some of the vulnerabilities are mitigated. However, even in this scenario he would choose sprinklers over drip if given the option.

Two important cautions apply:

- **Uniformity**: Be wary about getting uniform water application across the system. Variations in emitter output can create wet and dry spots.
- **Filtration**: Use very effective filters. The nozzles in microirrigation systems are extremely fine and clog easily without excellent filtration.

## Comparison With Sprinkler Systems

For the home vegetable garden, sprinkler systems offer several decisive advantages over drip:

- They wet the entire soil surface, supporting [[cannabis-seed-germination-methods]]
- They are not damaged by routine cultivation
- They are far less expensive to install
- They provide more uniform coverage
- They work effectively on all soil types including sand
- They do not require point-by-point inspection before each use

## Water Volume and Pressure Requirements

Drip systems require clean water at consistent pressure. Even small fluctuations in pressure can cause uneven emission rates across the system. In gardens relying on low-yielding wells or municipal water with variable pressure, maintaining consistent drip performance adds another layer of complexity.

Sprinkler systems, by contrast, are far more forgiving of pressure variations. A slight drop in pressure may reduce the radius of throw but will not create dry spots between emitters. This robustness makes sprinklers better suited to the variable water supplies typical of homesteads.
