# Acid Mine Drainage Passive Treatment

Acid mine drainage (AMD) is acidic, metal-rich water generated when sulfide minerals, primarily pyrite (FeS2), are exposed to oxygen and water during mining operations.

AMD is one of the most significant environmental impacts of the mining industry, affecting thousands of kilometers of streams and rivers worldwide.

Passive treatment systems use natural and enhanced biological and chemical processes to treat AMD without continuous energy input or chemical addition, offering sustainable long-term treatment alternatives for abandoned and post-closure mine sites.

Open limestone channels allow AMD to flow over limestone surfaces with enhanced oxygenation, promoting iron oxidation and precipitation alongside limestone dissolution.

Diversion wells actively mix AMD with crushed limestone in a well or tank, providing rapid neutralization and allowing frequent limestone replenishment.

The combination of limestone-based alkalinity generation with anaerobic sulfate reduction provides the most robust passive treatment for moderate to high acidity AMD.

## Bioremediation Components of Passive Treatment

Microbial communities play essential roles in all passive treatment systems, even those that are primarily designed around geochemical processes.

Iron-oxidizing bacteria (Acidithiobacillus ferrooxidans, Leptospirillum ferrooxidans) accelerate iron oxidation in aerobic systems, promoting metal precipitation.

Sulfate-reducing bacteria are the key organisms in anaerobic treatment systems, generating sulfide for metal precipitation and bicarbonate for pH adjustment.
