# Phenethylamine Chain Length Effects

**Updated:** 2026-04-18

## Overview

One of the most systematic structure-activity relationship studies in PiHKAL concerns the effect of aliphatic chain length on psychoactivity in phenethylamine-type compounds. Shulgin explored the continuum from 2-carbon (phenethylamine) through 3-carbon (amphetamine) to 4-carbon and beyond, documenting how each additional methylene unit affects potency, duration, and qualitative character.

## The Chain Length Continuum

### Two-Carbon Chain (2C Series / Phenethylamines)

The basic phenethylamine structure has a two-carbon chain between the aromatic ring and the amine nitrogen. These compounds:
- Are generally less potent than their amphetamine counterparts
- Often have shorter durations
- Tend to have a "cleaner" or more purely psychedelic character with less stimulant overlay
- Include the entire 2C-X family (2C-B, 2C-I, 2C-D, 2C-E, etc.)

The most dramatic potency loss is seen with 2C-D: its amphetamine counterpart DOM is active at approximately 3 mg, while 2C-D requires 20-60 mg -- roughly one-tenth the potency.

### Three-Carbon Chain (Amphetamines)

The addition of a single alpha-methyl group (creating the amphetamine structure) dramatically increases potency in most cases:
- DOM: active at 3-10 mg (vs 2C-D at 20-60 mg)
- DOB: active at 1-3 mg (vs 2C-B at 12-24 mg)
- DOI: active at 1.5-3 mg (vs 2C-I at 12-25 mg)
- MDA: active at 80-160 mg (vs MDPEA at ~300 mg)

The alpha-methyl group also typically increases duration and adds stimulant qualities.

### Four-Carbon Chain

Extending to four carbons produces varied results. The compound J (alpha,alpha-dimethyl-3,4-methylenedioxyphenethylamine) and related 4-carbon homologues show intermediate or reduced activity. The aminoindanes and aminotetralins (where the chain is "bent back" to the ring) are a special case -- they "react in animal discrimination studies identically to MDMA" and "appear to be free of neurochemical toxicity," despite having the chain length incorporated into a ring structure.

### Extended Chains (5+ Carbons)

Beyond four carbons, activity generally drops off sharply. The Muni-Metro series (K, L, etc.) with progressively longer chains showed decreasing psychoactivity.

## The Muni-Metro Encoding System

Shulgin developed the "Muni-Metro" naming convention (named after the San Francisco transit system's lettered lines) for the chain-length homologues of the MD-family:
- H (homopiperonylamine) = the phenethylamine (2-carbon) counterpart of MDA
- I = standard amphetamine (3-carbon, the standard MDA chain length)
- J = 4-carbon chain (alpha-ethyl)
- K, L = 5-carbon and 6-carbon chains

Each letter represents an additional methylene unit. The "bending back" of J to form an aminoindane, and of a further-extended chain to form an aminotetralin, represents rigidification of the otherwise flexible chain.

## N-Substitution vs Chain Length

Chain length effects interact with N-substitution patterns:
- N-methyl (as in MDMA) modifies the activity profile significantly from the parent MDA
- N-ethyl (as in MDE) further shifts the character toward "stoning intoxication"
- N-substitution with chains longer than two carbons generally eliminates psychoactivity (e.g., N-butyl MDA is inactive)

## Alpha-Ethyl vs Alpha-Methyl

The replacement of the alpha-methyl with an alpha-ethyl group (4-carbon chain) has been explored in several series. The results are variable:
- In some cases, potency is reduced
- In others, a qualitatively different profile emerges
- The compound ARIADNE (alpha-ethyl homologue of DOB) represents this exploration in the DO-series

## The 4-Position: Primary vs Secondary vs Tertiary Carbon

The nature of the carbon bearing the amine group matters:
- Primary carbon (benzylamine) -- reduced activity
- Secondary carbon (amphetamine, the standard) -- optimal for most psychedelic amphetamines
- Tertiary carbon -- generally inactive

Moving the amine from the beta- to alpha- position (benzylamines like ALPHA) or to the gamma-position (propylamines like GAMMA) produces compounds with reduced potency and altered character.

## Theoretical Implications

The systematic exploration of chain length effects provides evidence that:
1. The optimal chain length for psychedelic activity is 3 carbons (amphetamine)
2. The alpha-methyl group confers a specific pharmacokinetic advantage (metabolic stability)
3. The flexibility of the aliphatic chain may be important for receptor interaction
4. Rigidified analogues (indanes, tetralins) can retain activity while potentially reducing toxicity
