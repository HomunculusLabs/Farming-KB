# Log Cultivation of Mushrooms

Log cultivation is the oldest and most natural method of [[growing-gourmet-mushrooms-civilization-history]] and is well-suited to shiitake, oyster, lion mane, and reishi.

## Wood Selection

The choice of log species is critical for success and quality.

### Preferred Species

- **Oak (Quercus spp.)**: Premier log species for shiitake. Dense, nutrient-rich, slow to decompose. Oak logs produce for 4-7 years with consistent yields
- **Maple (Acer spp.)**: Excellent all-around choice with good nutrient density and moderate decay rate. Widely available and easy to inoculate
- **Ironwood (Ostrya/Carpinus)**: Extremely dense and long-lasting, producing for 6-10 years, but difficult to inoculate due to hardness
- **Beech (Fagus spp.)**: Good for shiitake and maitake with moderate decay rate between oak and maple
- **Birch (Betula spp.)**: Fast-colonizing but decomposes quickly. Excellent for [[coleman-claytonia-winter-harvest-cultivation]]** is optimal. Trees have stored nutrients in the wood, and bark is tight and intact for moisture retention
2. **Cut logs 4-8 inches diameter, 3-4 feet long**. Smaller logs colonize faster but produce fewer years; larger logs take longer but last much longer
3. **Do not let logs dry out** after cutting. End-seal with wax and inoculate within 2-6 weeks of felling
4. **Avoid logs with visible decay** or existing fungal fruiting bodies

## Inoculation Methods

### Spawn Plug Method

Most accessible for small-scale cultivators. Colonized wooden dowels are inserted into drilled holes:

1. Drill holes 1 inch deep, 5/16 inch diameter, spaced 6 inches apart in a diamond pattern
2. Insert spawn plugs and tap flush with or slightly below the bark
3. Seal each plug and log ends with food-grade wax to prevent desiccation

A typical 4-foot log requires 30-50 plugs. Inoculate in early spring.

### Sawdust Spawn Method

Preferred by commercial cultivators for efficiency and faster colonization:

1. Drill larger holes -- 3/8 inch diameter, 1.5 inches deep, spaced 4-6 inches apart
2. Pack sawdust spawn tightly into each hole
3. Seal with wax

Sawdust spawn colonizes 20-40% faster than plugs and costs less per inoculation point.

### Totem Method

For stump-forming species like maitake and reishi. A log section is placed on a buried stump with spawn layered between, mimicking natural stump colonization. This method is particularly effective for species that naturally fruit from buried wood.

## Colonization

After inoculation, logs colonize for 6-18 months. Stack logs in a shaded, humid location using a crib stack (log cabin style) for good airflow or a lean-to stack in rainy areas. Maintain moisture by watering thoroughly once or twice per week in dry periods. Most species colonize at 10-25C; growth stops near freezing and heat above 30C can kill mycelium.

Full colonization is indicated by white mycelium visible at log ends and slightly loose bark. Protect from direct sun, wind, and competing fungi.

## Forced Fruiting

Natural fruiting produces 1-2 flushes per year. Forced fruiting increases to 3-5:

1. Soak the log in cold water (10-15C) for 12-24 hours, simulating heavy rain
2. Stand the log upright in a shaded, humid fruiting area with spacing for air circulation
3. Maintain 80-90% humidity during pinning. Mist regularly if dry
4. Mushrooms appear within 7-14 days of soaking
5. Allow 6-8 weeks rest between soaking cycles for mycelial energy recovery

## Yield and Longevity

Typical yields range from 0.5-2 kg fresh mushrooms per log per year. Shiitake on oak averages 1-1.5 kg per log per year over 4-6 productive years. Total lifetime yield is approximately 20-40% of the log original fresh weight.

## Advantages and Limitations

**Advantages**: Low cost after setup; minimal equipment; premium quality; natural conditions; productive for years; integrates with gardens and [[cannabis-sativa-indoor-cultivation-techniques]]; vulnerable to competing fungi and insects.
