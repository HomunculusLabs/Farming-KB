# Log Cultivation of Mushrooms

Log cultivation is the oldest and most natural method of growing mushrooms,
involving inoculation of freshly cut hardwood logs with mushroom spawn. The
mycelium colonizes the wood over months, then fruits in response to seasonal
temperature and moisture changes. This method produces some of the highest-
quality gourmet mushrooms and is well-suited to shiitake, oyster, lion's mane,
and reishi.

## Wood Selection

The choice of log species is critical for success and quality.

### Preferred Species

- **Oak (Quercus spp.)**: Premier log species for shiitake. Dense, nutrient-
  rich, slow to decompose. Oak logs produce for 4–7 years.
- **Maple (Acer spp.)**: Excellent all-around choice with good nutrient
  density and moderate decay rate.
- **Ironwood (Ostrya/Carpinus)**: Extremely dense and long-lasting, producing
  for 6–10 years, but difficult to inoculate due to hardness.
- **Beech (Fagus spp.)**: Good for shiitake and maitake with moderate decay
  rate between oak and maple.
- **Birch (Betula spp.)**: Fast-colonizing but decomposes quickly. Excellent
  for oyster mushrooms, producing for 2–4 years.

### Species to Avoid

- **Conifers** (pine, spruce, fir, cedar): Resins and phenolic compounds
  inhibit most cultivated species.
- **Black walnut/butternut**: Juglone is toxic to mycelium.
- **Eucalyptus**: Essential oils inhibit fungal growth.
- **Black locust**: Extremely rot-resistant, very difficult to colonize.

## Log Harvesting and Preparation

1. **Fall or early winter harvest** is optimal. Trees have stored nutrients in
   the wood, and bark is tight and intact for moisture retention.
2. **Cut logs 4–8 inches diameter, 3–4 feet long**. Smaller logs colonize
   faster but produce fewer years; larger logs take longer but last much
   longer.
3. **Do not let logs dry out** after cutting. End-seal with wax and inoculate
   within 2–6 weeks of felling.
4. **Avoid logs with visible decay** or existing fungal fruiting bodies.

## Inoculation Methods

### Spawn Plug Method

Most accessible for small-scale cultivators. Colonized wooden dowels are
inserted into drilled holes:

1. Drill holes 1 inch deep, 5/16 inch diameter, spaced 6 inches apart in a
   diamond pattern. Stagger rows 2–3 inches apart around the log.
2. Insert spawn plugs and tap flush with or slightly below the bark.
3. Seal each plug and log ends with food-grade wax (cheese wax, beeswax, or
   soy wax) to prevent desiccation and contamination.

A typical 4-foot log requires 30–50 plugs. Inoculate in early spring.

### Sawdust Spawn Method

Preferred by commercial cultivators for efficiency and faster colonization:

1. Drill larger holes — 3/8 inch diameter, 1.5 inches deep, spaced 4–6
   inches apart.
2. Pack sawdust spawn tightly into each hole.
3. Seal with wax.

Sawdust spawn colonizes 20–40% faster than plugs and costs less per
inoculation point.

### Totem Method

For stump-forming species like maitake and reishi. A log section is placed on
a buried stump with spawn layered between, mimicking natural stump
colonization.

## Colonization

After inoculation, logs colonize for 6–18 months:

- **Stack logs** in a shaded, humid location. Crib stack (log cabin) provides
  good airflow; lean-to stack is simpler in rainy areas.
- **Maintain moisture** — water thoroughly once or twice per week in dry
  periods. Mulch or shade cloth helps retain moisture. Avoid waterlogging.
- **Temperature**: Most species colonize at 10–25°C. Growth stops near
  freezing; heat above 30°C can kill mycelium.
- **Protection**: Shield from direct sun, wind, and competing fungi. A
  north-facing slope under deciduous canopy is ideal.

Full colonization is indicated by white cream-colored mycelium visible at log
ends and slightly loose bark.

## Forced Fruiting

Natural fruiting produces 1–2 flushes per year. Forced fruiting increases to
3–5:

1. **Soak the log** in cold water (10–15°C) for 12–24 hours, simulating heavy
   rain. Use a clean tank or container.
2. **Stand the log upright** in a shaded, humid fruiting area with spacing
  for air circulation.
3. **Maintain 80–90% humidity** during pinning. Mist regularly if dry.
4. **Mushrooms appear within 7–14 days** of soaking.

Allow 6–8 weeks rest between soaking cycles for mycelial energy recovery.

## Yield and Longevity

Typical yields: 0.5–2 kg fresh mushrooms per log per year. Shiitake on oak
averages 1–1.5 kg per log per year over 4–6 productive years. Total lifetime
yield is approximately 20–40% of the log's original fresh weight.

## Advantages and Limitations

**Advantages**: Low cost after setup; minimal equipment; premium quality;
natural conditions; productive for years; integrates with gardens and forests.

**Limitations**: Long lead time to first harvest; heavy labor; requires outdoor
space; weather-dependent; lower yield per time vs. indoor cultivation;
vulnerable to competing fungi and insects.
