# Cannabis Propagation: Sexual vs. Asexual Methods (Clarke)

## Fundamental Principles

Cannabis can be propagated either sexually or asexually. The function of propagation is to preserve special genotypes by choosing the proper technique to ensure replication of the desired characteristics.

## Sexual Propagation (Seed Production)

Sexual propagation requires the union of staminate pollen and pistillate ovule, the formation of viable seed, and the creation of individuals with newly recombinant genotypes.

### Genetics
- Cannabis has 10 chromosome pairs (2n = 20)
- Pollen and ovules formed by meiosis (reduction division), producing haploid cells (n = 10)
- Diploid condition restored upon fertilization, with a haploid set from each parent
- Offspring may resemble staminate parent, pistillate parent, both, or neither
- Considerable variation in offspring is to be expected

### Homozygous vs. Heterozygous
- **Homozygous**: Genes controlling a trait are identical on both chromosomes; plant will "breed true" for that trait
- **Heterozygous**: Genes on homologous chromosomes differ; offspring may not possess parental traits and will probably differ from each other
- Imported Cannabis strains usually exhibit great seedling diversity for most traits

### Advantages
- Produces genetic variation for selection and breeding
- Seeds are easy to store and transport
- Allows combination of desirable traits from different parents
- Essential for breeding programs

### Disadvantages
- Offspring are not identical to parents
- Desirable parental combinations may not be reproduced in subsequent generations
- F1 hybrids are far from true-breeding; F2 generation shows great variation

## Asexual Propagation (Cloning)

Vegetative methods of propagation (cloning) such as cuttage, layerage, or division of roots allow exact replication of the parental plant without genetic variation. In theory, strains can be preserved unchanged through many seasons and hundreds of individuals.

### Genotype vs. Phenotype
The unique characteristics of a plant result from the combination of genes in chromosomes (genotype). The expression of a genotype, as influenced by the environment, creates visible characteristics (phenotype). Two clones from the same plant placed in different environments (shade vs. sun) will have identical genotypes but different phenotypes -- the shaded clone grows tall and slender and matures late, while the sun-grown clone remains short and bushy and matures earlier.

### Advantages
- Exact replication of desirable genotypes
- Preserves strain characteristics indefinitely
- All individuals are identical, ensuring uniform crop quality
- No genetic variation to manage
- Faster to harvest (no seedling stage needed)

### Disadvantages
- No genetic variation means no adaptation potential; entire clone population is susceptible to the same [[query-how-to-manage-pests-and-diseases-in-mushroom-cultivation]]
- Requires maintaining mother plants
- More labor-intensive than seed production
- Limited transport options compared to seeds

## Sinsemilla Cultivation

Sinsemilla (seedless drug Cannabis) cultivation eliminates pollination and subsequent seed production. By removing all staminate plants from a patch, the pistillate plants produce massive clusters of unfertilized flowers.

### Theories on Sinsemilla Potency
Various theories explain the unusually potent psychoactive properties of unfertilized Cannabis, centered on the extraordinarily long, frustrated struggle of the pistillate plant to reproduce. In reality, seeding cuts the life of the plant short, preventing continued calyx production and decreasing cannabinoid yield. THC does not have enough time to accumulate in seeded plants.

### Seed Production in Sinsemilla Operations
- Modern fiber farmers use commercially produced monoecious strains of even maturation
- For drug crops, special techniques are needed to produce viable seed without compromising flower quality
- A mature pistillate plant can produce tens of thousands of seeds if freely pollinated
- Seeds falling during harvesting, drying, and processing can be collected for future planting

## Life Cycle Considerations

- A wild Cannabis plant grows from seed through seedling, prefloral juvenile, to adult
- Fiber crops are usually harvested in the juvenile or prefloral stage (before viable seed)
- Sinsemilla cultivation eliminates the natural reproductive pathway
- Both fiber and drug production interfere with natural inheritance pathways
- [[cannabis-controlled-pollination-seed-production]] is needed for seed production while maintaining product quality
