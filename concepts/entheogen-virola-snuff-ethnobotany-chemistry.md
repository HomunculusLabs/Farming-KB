up to 8 percent tryptamines by weight, making it one of the most
potent natural psychoactive preparations known. The primary active
compound is 5-methoxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine (5-MeO-DMT), a
tryptamine that is significantly more potent than N,N-DMT itself.

Two beta-carboline alkaloids have also been found in the resin. These
function as monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs) and make it possible
for the tryptamines to take effect when the resin is taken orally as
pellets or other oral preparations. This MAOI activity is significant
because it allows Virola resin to be used in multiple ways -- as a
snuff, as oral pellets, and even as a crude licked preparation -- with
different routes of administration producing different onset times and
experience characteristics.

## Ceremonial Context

Among the Waika (Yanomami) tribes of the Brazil-Venezuela frontier,
epena or nyakwana snuff is not restricted to shamans but may be
taken ceremonially by all adult males. A snuff-taking ceremony is
conducted annually to memorialize those who have died the previous
year. The ceremony involves blowing huge amounts of snuff through
long tubes into each other's nostrils, followed by dancing, running,
and ritual combat. Despite the physical violence of the combat, the
narcotic effects are so strong that participants do not flinch or
show signs of pain.

Among other groups, such as those in eastern Colombia, Virola snuff
(called yakee or parica) is restricted to shamans, who use it to
induce a trance for diagnosing and treating illness. Effects begin
within minutes and include increasing excitability, numbness of the
limbs, facial twitching, lack of coordination, and macropsia -- the
sensation of seeing things greatly enlarged.

## Pharmacological Significance

Virola resin represents one of the most concentrated natural sources
of psychoactive tryptamines in nature distribution ecology and evolutionary origins known to science. The combination of
5-MeO-DMT with beta-carboline MAOIs in a single plant resin is
pharmacologically noteworthy, as it means the resin is effective via
both intranasal and oral routes of administration. The Witoto, Bora,
and Muinane of Colombia prepare little pellets from the resin for
oral consumption, and the Maku nomads simply scrape and lick crude
resin from the bark, demonstrating the versatility of this
entheogenic preparation.

## Distribution and Related Species

The use of Virola snuffs spans a vast geographic area encompassing
parts of Colombia, Venezuela, and northwestern Brazil. Different
indigenous groups have developed distinct preparation methods and
ceremonial contexts for the same basic pharmacological material,
illustrating how a single genus can support diverse ethnobotanical
traditions. Related Myristicaceae genera used in the Amazon include
Osteophloeum and Iryanthera, though these are less well documented.
The high tryptamine content of Virola resin has attracted interest
from phytochemists investigating the biosynthetic pathways of
indole alkaloids in [[mollison-designers-moringa-and-multipurpose-tropical-trees]].
