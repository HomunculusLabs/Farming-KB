# Mushroom Fruiting Chamber Design and Environmental Parameters

A fruiting chamber is an enclosed space where environmental conditions are
controlled to trigger and support [[bloomfield-mushroom-formation-fruiting-body-development-cultivation]]. The transition from
colonization to fruiting is governed by specific environmental cues that signal
the [[oyster-mushrooms]].

FAE is provided through passive ventilation (filtered holes), active
ventilation (fans), or a combination. Passive systems are simpler but require
careful sizing of intake and exhaust ports.

### Humidity

Relative humidity should be maintained between 85 and 95 percent. Below 80
percent, the substrate surface dries out and primordia abort. Above 95 percent,
water condenses on surfaces and promotes [[growing-gourmet-bacterial-blotch-disease-management]]. Humidification is
achieved with ultrasonic foggers or evaporative pan humidifiers. The key is
maintaining humidity through [[bloomfield-mushroom-evaporative-cooling-fungal-frigidity]] rather than direct spraying.

### Temperature

Fruiting temperature generally runs 5 to 10 degrees Fahrenheit lower than the
optimal colonization temperature. Common ranges: oyster 55-75F, shiitake
50-75F, lion's mane 60-75F, reishi 70-85F. Temperature consistency matters more
than hitting the exact midpoint.

### Light

Most species require light to orient and develop normal fruiting bodies. A
12-hour-on, 12-hour-off cycle of 6500K daylight-spectrum LED lighting at 500
to 1000 lux is standard. Direct sunlight should be avoided. Reishi and enoki
are exceptions that fruit well in very low light.

### Evaporation from the Substrate

Evaporation from the substrate surface is perhaps the most important and most
overlooked fruiting trigger. As moisture evaporates, it creates a microclimate
at the substrate surface where primordia first form. Maintaining evaporation
requires balancing [[pf-tek-substrate-moisture-diagnosis-and-correction]] with adequate air movement.

## Fruiting Chamber Designs

### Shotgun Fruiting Chamber (SGFC)

A plastic tote with numerous small holes drilled on all six sides, filled with
damp perlite for humidification. The SGFC works well for small-scale grows
using cakes or small substrate blocks.

### Mono Tub

A modified plastic tote with filtered ventilation holes near the substrate
surface. The substrate itself provides humidity through evapotranspiration.
Mono tubs are popular for their simplicity and scalability.

### Martha Tent

A commercial greenhouse shelf enclosed in a plastic tent with a cool-mist
humidifier piped in at the base and an exhaust fan at the top. Martha tents
accommodate multiple shelves of substrate blocks.

### Greenhouse / Walk-in Chamber

Commercial operations use insulated rooms with automated environmental controls.
Humidity, temperature, CO2, and lighting are managed by programmable logic
controllers.
