## Pigeons as Phosphate Factories

Mollison repeatedly emphasises that phosphorus is one of the critical
limiting resources for human civilisation, second only to clean water.
Phosphorus is found concentrated in the bones of vertebrates,
especially in seed-eating birds. Historically, most farms kept a flock
of pigeons as their phosphate factory. Every thinking farmer who keeps
pigeons builds their own phosphate production system as a pigeon loft.

The connection is direct: pigeons eat seeds (which concentrate
phosphorus), and their droppings return concentrated phosphate to the
soil beneath and around the loft. In a permaculture system, this
phosphate-rich manure can be directed to orchards, gardens, and
compost systems, closing a nutrient loop that conventional agriculture
leaves open.

In aquatic systems, phosphorus was traditionally recovered from the mud
of ponds stocked with mussels and from fish and waterfowl wastes.
Bat guano is another favoured source. Even a modest perch in a bare
field will attract a few perching birds to leave their phosphates at a
tree or along a crop line.

## Seed Dispersal and Forest Establishment

Mollison identifies pigeons as active agents of forest establishment.
Free-ranging pigeons and pheasants will plant trees by distributing
seeds of loquats and other fruits. Bears plant small fruits, and
cattle distribute hard-seeded species. Pigeons contribute to this
natural reforestation process by carrying and depositing seeds of
berry-producing and fruiting trees throughout the landscape.
