# Pigeons and Squab Production in Permaculture Systems

Bill Mollison's permaculture systems. Their role extends far beyond squab production to
include phosphate recycling, seed dispersal, and the maintenance of
nutrient cycles within the designed landscape.

## Pigeons as Phosphate Factories

Mollison repeatedly emphasises that phosphorus is one of the critical
limiting resources for human civilisation, second only to clean water.
Phosphorus is found concentrated in the bones of vertebrates,
especially in seed-eating birds. Historically, most farms kept a flock
of pigeons as their phosphate factory. Every thinking farmer who keeps
pigeons builds their own phosphate production system as a pigeon loft.

The connection is direct: pigeons eat seeds (which concentrate
phosphorus), and their droppings return concentrated phosphate to the
soil beneath and around the loft. In a permaculture system, this
phosphate-rich manure can be directed to orchards, gardens, and
compost systems, closing a nutrient loop that conventional agriculture
leaves open.

In aquatic systems, phosphorus was traditionally recovered from the mud
of ponds stocked with mussels and from fish and waterfowl wastes.
Bat guano is another favoured source. Even a modest perch in a bare
field will attract a few perching birds to leave their phosphates at a
tree or along a crop line.

## Pigeons in the Zone 1 Garden

In Mollison's [[mollison-course-zone-planning-and-design]] system, pigeons are classified as small,
quiet domestic animals suitable for Zone 1. Placed within 6 metres of
the home alongside rabbits, guinea pigs, and [[holzer-medicinal-and-culinary-herbs-compendium]], pigeons
require frequent observation but minimal management.

Their loft can be integrated with the house structure, placed above
animal sheds, or elevated on pole structures. Pigeons are most easily
protected from predators by elevation on pole structures or over
shallow ponds, making them ideal for small residential properties.

## Seed Dispersal and Forest Establishment

Mollison identifies pigeons as active agents of forest establishment.
Free-ranging pigeons and pheasants will plant trees by distributing
seeds of loquats and other fruits. Bears plant small fruits, and
cattle distribute hard-seeded species. Pigeons contribute to this
natural reforestation process by carrying and depositing seeds of
berry-producing and fruiting trees throughout the landscape.

This seed-dispersal function connects pigeons directly to
mollison-soil-improvement-and-rehabilitation systems.

## Foraging Range and Behaviour

Pigeons naturally forage the streets and open areas around human
settlements. In [[pdc-urban-permaculture-design-principles]] systems, feral pigeons are already
performing a foraging and waste-recycling function, though their
unmanaged populations can become a nuisance. The permaculture approach
is to design managed pigeon systems that capture the benefits of this
foraging behaviour while controlling population and location.

Pigeons forage on seeds, grains, and green matter. Their preference
for scattered seeds makes them useful for weed-seed management in
cropped areas, as they consume weed seeds that might otherwise persist
in the soil seed bank.
