Geese are versatile livestock for the homestead, serving as grazers, guardians, egg producers, and meat birds. They are low-maintenance compared to many other livestock and can live entirely off pasture.

## How Geese Fit into a Permaculture System

Geese provide multiple functions:

- **Weed and [[mollison-geese-grass-control-and-integrated-poultry]]**: They eat grass and weeds while leaving crops and mulch alone
- **Soil fertilization**: Their droppings fertilize the land as they graze
- **[[fukuoka-natural-predator-protection]]**: Geese act as watchdogs — alert and somewhat aggressive toward intruders
- **Eggs and meat**: Provide food for the homestead
- **Feathers**: Traditional use for pillows, bedding, and writing quills

Geese should only be allowed into well-established areas so they do not squash young shoots. They eat fruit and vegetables, so remove them before gardens ripen.

## Grazing and Space Requirements

- **[[savory-stock-density-vs-stocking-rate]]**: 7 geese per acre when over 8 weeks old, grazing until spring sprouts appear
- **Fencing**: At least 3 feet high around the field to prevent access to other gardens
- **Grazing season**: Let geese graze until spring when new sprouts emerge
- **Pasture living**: Geese can live entirely off grass pasture, and unlike ducks, they eat older grass
- **Water access**: Geese can live with a small pond; 6 geese maximum per acre of water surface
- **Winter feeding**: Dried grass, hay (not alfalfa), corn fodder, grain, and kitchen scraps

## Housing

- **Space**: 10 square feet (0.9 square meters) per goose in the house
- **Yard space**: 30–40 square feet (2.8–3.7 square meters) per goose
- **Construction**: A simple, very dry shed — does not need to be fancy
- **Feeder**: Box feeder located inside the house
- **Water trough**: Located outside under an awning
- **Bedding**: Clean bedding such as chopped straw on the floor
- **Winter needs**: Ensure the house remains dry and well-bedded

## Feeding

- **Protein requirement**: 15% protein (same feed as ducks)
- **Pasture-based**: Geese are among the most efficient pasture-based livestock — they thrive on grass alone
- **Avoid alfalfa**: Geese generally will not touch it unless very hungry
- **Supplements**: Grain can be added but is not always necessary if pasture is adequate

## Sexing

- **Difficult**: Hard to tell males from females without practiced vent examination
- **Visual clues**: Ganders (males) have broader heads, longer necks, and more aggressive behavior
- **Call**: Ganders have a deeper, louder call
- **Mating behavior**: Watch the flock during mating season for confirmation

## Breeding

- **Gander ratio**: One large gander can service 2–3 geese; a smaller gander can handle 4–5
- **Mating fidelity**: Once a gander picks his mates, he sticks with the same females every year and helps raise goslings
- **Breeding age**: Wait until geese are two years old for best egg quality
- **Introduce ganders**: Let ganders in with hens in late fall or early winter
- **Separate ganders**: Keep them separate when not breeding

### Egg Collection and Hatching

- **Frequency**: Gather eggs twice daily
- **Brooding**: Geese prefer to brood outside — provide an old tire with straw or a small brooding house
- **Egg management**: When the hen starts laying, leave two eggs and take the rest. Once the nest is full, she will begin setting.
- **Incubation**: 28–35 days
- **Day 20 care**: Spray eggs completely with warm water and turn them yourself

## Gosling Care

- **Brooder space**: 1.5 square feet per gosling until 7 weeks; 2.5 square feet after that
- **Heat**: Maximum 25 goslings per 250-watt heat lamp
- **Feeding**: 4 times per day, enough food for 15 minutes of eating. Includes tender [[knf-green-grass-liquid-fertilizer]]/weeds, some duck food, and grit.
- **Pasture transition**: At 5–6 weeks, goslings can survive completely on a large pasture (1 acre per 20–40 geese), optionally supplemented with grain
- **Butchering**: Before winter when pinfeathers are not growing in
- **Goose grease**: Traditionally used for frying, pastry, and hand salves

## Wing Clipping

- **Frequency**: After each molting cycle
- **Method**: Clip 5 inches off the feathers of one wing only
