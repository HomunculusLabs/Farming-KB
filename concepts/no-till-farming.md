# No-Till Farming

Agricultural practices that preserve soil structure and fungal networks by avoiding mechanical disturbance. Referenced throughout *Mycelium Running* by [[paul-stamets]].

## Why Tilling Destroys Soil Biology

- Tilling severs mycorrhizal networks — the fungal internet connecting plants
- It exposes soil biology to UV radiation, killing surface organisms
- It releases stored CO2 from soil organic matter (soil carbon loss)
- It accelerates erosion by breaking soil aggregates
- It disrupts the layered soil architecture built by fungi over years
- Each tilling event can take decades of soil building to recover from

## The Fungal Connection

- [[mycorrhizal-networks]] are the primary casualty of tilling
- Tilled soil selects for bacterial-dominated communities (weeds thrive in disturbed soil)
- No-till soil selects for fungal-dominated communities (perennials, woody plants thrive)
- Fungal:bacterial ratio is a key indicator of soil health and succession stage
- [[fungal-ecology]] explains how fungi build and maintain soil structure

## Sheet Mulching (No-Till Bed Preparation)

The no-till method for creating garden beds, as described in *Mycelium Running*:

1. **Smother layer**: Cardboard or newspaper (4-6 sheets), overlapping
2. **Organic layer**: 2-4 inches of compost, manure, or green material
3. **Spawn layer**: Sawdust spawn (if adding mushrooms — see [[garden-mushroom-growing]])
4. **Carbon layer**: 4-6 inches of straw, wood chips, or leaves
5. **Moisture**: Water thoroughly; let sit for weeks to months

Benefits:
- No soil disturbance — fungal networks preserved
- Weed suppression through smothering
- Moisture retention
- Slow nutrient release as layers decompose
- Can add mushroom spawn for food production

## Mycoremediation Meets No-Till

Mycoremediation naturally works best with no-till approaches:
- Mycelial mats are laid on top of contaminated soil, not tilled in
- Sheet mulching over contaminated sites creates living remediation membranes
- The mycelium processes toxins without disturbing the soil profile
- See [[mycoremediation-techniques]] for deployment methods

## Conservation Agriculture Principles

1. **Minimal soil disturbance** — no-till or reduced tillage
2. **Permanent soil cover** — mulch, cover crops, living roots
3. **Crop rotation** — breaks pest cycles, diversifies root exudates
4. **Integration of fungi** — mycorrhizal inoculants, mushroom cultivation

## Carbon Sequestration

- Tilled soil loses 30-50% of its organic carbon within decades of cultivation
- No-till farming can rebuild soil carbon at 0.1-0.5 tons/acre/year
- Mycorrhizal fungi produce **glomalin** — a carbon-rich protein that persists in soil for decades
- Glomalin accounts for 27% of soil carbon in some ecosystems
- Preserving fungal networks = preserving carbon sequestration capacity
