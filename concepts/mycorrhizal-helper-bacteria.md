# Mycorrhizal Helper Bacteria

Mycorrhizal helper bacteria (MHB) are a functional group of rhizosphere bacteria that stimulate mycorrhiza formation -- ectomycorrhiza, arbuscular mycorrhiza, or ectendomycorrhiza -- without entering into a symbiotic relationship with plant roots. The concept was first defined by Garbaye (1994) to describe bacteria that selectively promote the establishment of mycorrhizal symbiosis. MHB have been documented to influence fungal growth, root colonization, plant nutrition, and plant health.

MHB represent a critical but often overlooked component of the mycorrhizal symbiosis. While the plant-fungal partnership has been studied intensively, the bacterial associates that facilitate this relationship add an additional layer of complexity to the underground interactions that sustain terrestrial ecosystems. These bacteria occupy the mycorrhizosphere -- the narrow zone of soil influenced by both root exudates and fungal metabolites -- where they interact with both symbiotic partners. See [[beneficial-indigenous-microorganisms-bim]] for related concepts in beneficial soil microbiology.

## Types of Mycorrhizal Helper Bacteria

MHB belong to diverse taxonomic groups. The most extensively studied MHB are streptomycetes (filamentous actinobacteria) isolated from the hyphosphere of ectomycorrhizal fungi. Key organisms include:

- Streptomyces strain AcH 505, isolated from the hyphosphere of Amanita muscaria in a spruce stand (Schonwitz et al., 1987; Maier et al., 2004)

- Pseudomonas fluorescens BBc6, associated with Laccaria bicolor

- Various Bacillus, Paenibacillus, and Burkholderia species

The taxonomic diversity of MHB reflects the functional diversity of mechanisms by which they promote mycorrhization.

## Mechanisms of Action

### Direct Effects on the Fungus

MHB can modify the morphology of the fungal partner. Streptomyces AcH 505 was shown to modify the mycelial morphology of Amanita muscaria, stimulate fungal growth, and enhance mycorrhiza formation on Norway spruce (Picea abies). In dual culture, specific bacteria were classified as:

1. **Positive MHB**: Promote fungal growth and/or mycorrhiza formation

2. **Negative MHB**: Suppress fungal growth, often through antibiosis

3. **Neutral**: No measurable effect on fungal growth

The classification of MHB effects is context-dependent; a bacterium that promotes one fungal species may inhibit another. This specificity highlights the co-evolutionary nature of MHB-fungal partnerships.

### Secondary Metabolite Production

The streptomycete AcH 505 produces at least two antagonistic secondary metabolites affecting A. muscaria:

- **Auxofuran**: A growth-promoting compound that stimulates A. muscaria hyphal extension at concentrations around 15 uM

- **WS-5995 B**: An antifungal cyclopeptide that retards fungal growth at 60 uM concentrations

- **7-dehydroxyauxofuran**: A related compound with variable effects

The dual role of AcH 505 in both promoting and inhibiting fungal growth demonstrates the complexity of MHB-fungal interactions, where the net effect depends on metabolite concentrations and timing.

### Mycorrhiza Synthesis Enhancement

MHB enhance mycorrhiza synthesis through multiple pathways:

- Reducing the time required for mycorrhiza formation

- Increasing the percentage of short roots colonized

- Stimulating lateral root formation in host plants

- Modifying the rhizosphere chemical environment

- Producing volatile organic compounds that stimulate fungal growth

Volatile organic compounds (VOCs) produced by MHB can diffuse through soil air spaces and influence fungal behavior at a distance.

## MHB in Arbuscular Mycorrhizal Systems

While MHB were first characterized in ectomycorrhizal systems, they have also been identified in association with arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi:

- **Paenibacillus brasilensis** promotes AMF colonization of maize and sorghum roots

- **Azospirillum** species synergistically interact with AMF to enhance plant growth beyond the effects of either organism alone

- **Bacillus** species can stimulate AMF spore germination and hyphal elongation in the rhizosphere

The interaction between MHB and AMF is particularly relevant for [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi]], as these bacteria may help overcome barriers to AMF establishment in degraded or disturbed soils where natural mycorrhizal communities have been depleted.

## Methodology for MHB Identification

The standard approach for isolating MHB from ectomycorrhizal environments involves:

1. Collecting soil from the hyphosphere (fungal-hyphae-containing organic layer)

2. Heat-treating soil at 60C for 40 min to select for actinomycetes

3. Membrane filtration (0.3 um pore) to isolate actinomycetes from other bacteria

4. Dual culture assays on solid media with cellophane separation

5. Measuring fungal colony radius and mycelial density after 3-6 weeks

Modern molecular approaches complement classical culture-based methods, including 16S rRNA gene sequencing, metagenomic analysis, and quantitative PCR to track MHB population dynamics.

## Ecological Significance

MHB appear to play important roles in forest ecosystems by:

- Facilitating the establishment of ectomycorrhizal symbiosis on seedlings

- Enhancing nutrient cycling in the mycorrhizosphere

- Contributing to plant disease resistance

- Influencing the composition of fungal communities

- Mediating interactions between mycorrhizal fungi and other soil microorganisms

The concept of the "mycorrhizosphere" as a distinct microbial habitat underscores the importance of MHB.

## Applications in Agriculture and Restoration

- **Nursery inoculation**: Combining MHB with mycorrhizal inoculum can improve seedling establishment and outplanting success

- **Soil restoration**: MHB may accelerate the recovery of mycorrhizal communities in degraded soils

- **Sustainable agriculture**: MHB consortia can reduce fertilizer dependence by enhancing mycorrhizal nutrient acquisition

- **Biocontrol**: Some MHB exhibit antagonistic activity against plant pathogens, providing dual benefits of mycorrhization promotion and disease suppression
