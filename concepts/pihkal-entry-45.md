# 2C-T-9 — 2,5-Dimethoxy-4-(t)-butylthiophenethylamine

## Overview

2C-T-9 is a member of the 2C-T family of [[pihkal-beta-substituted-phenethylamines]],
distinguished by a tert-butylthio group at the 4-position of the
2,5-dimethoxyphenethylamine scaffold. It was synthesized and evaluated by
[[alexander-shulgin]] as part of his exploration of bulky sulfur-containing
substituents. The compound requires relatively high doses (60–100 mg) for
activity and produces an experience dominated by strong physical/body effects
with minimal visual phenomena, lasting 12–18 hours. Shulgin considered the
compound to have an unfavorable cost-to-benefit ratio due to the significant
body burden it imposes relative to its psychoactive rewards.

## Chemical Classification

2C-T-9 belongs to the 2C-T series of 2,5-dimethoxy-4-alkylthiophenethylamines.
The compound features the characteristic 2,5-dimethoxy substitution on the
phenyl ring with a tert-butylthio group at the 4-position. The tert-butyl
group is a highly branched, three-carbon alkyl chain with all three carbons
attached to the central carbon (C(CH3)3), making it one of the most sterically
demanding substituents explored in the 2C-T series. This extreme bulk
distinguishes it from the straight-chain alkylthio substituents found in most
other 2C-T compounds. Molecular formula: C14H23NO2S. The fully branched
structure creates a nearly spherical substituent with significantly different
steric and electronic properties compared to linear alkyl chains.

## Synthesis

The synthesis of 2C-T-9 employs a notably different first step compared to
other 2C-T compounds. Rather than starting from 2,5-dimethoxythiophenol, the
synthesis begins with hydroquinone dimethyl ether (p-dimethoxybenzene), which
is lithiated using butyllithium and N,N,N',N'-tetramethylethylenediamine in
petroleum ether under helium atmosphere. The aryl lithium intermediate reacts
with di-(tert)-butyl disulfide to install the tert-butylthio group, yielding
2,5-dimethoxyphenyl tert-butyl sulfide as a white mobile liquid. This
intermediate was characterized via a crystalline nitration derivative (mp
92–93°C). Vilsmeier-Haack formylation (POCl3/N-methylformanilide) produced
the benzaldehyde, which proved difficult to crystallize and was complex by TLC.
Condensation with nitromethane/ammonium acetate yielded the beta-nitrostyrene
as fine lemon-yellow crystals. Final reduction with LiAlH4/THF (H2SO4-moderated)
gave the free base, which was distilled and converted to the hydrochloride
salt as white crystals.

## Dosage and Duration

Active dosage: 60–100 mg. Duration: 12–18 hours. The high dose requirement
likely reflects steric bulk of the tert-butyl group interfering with receptor
binding. Onset is slow, with peak effects developing over 2–5 hours. The
extended duration is among the longest in the 2C-T series, producing
significant difficulty with sleep even after subjective effects begin to wane.

## Qualitative Effects

At 90 mg: the taste was described as resembling old crankcase motor oil.
Effects developed to above a plus-two by the third hour. Notably, no visual
phenomena were observed despite the significant dose. The experience was
primarily somatic — described as doing "more to the body than to the head."
Effects seemed to diminish by the sixth hour, but a strong "body memory" made
sleeping very difficult. Sleep was eventually achieved after midnight with the
aid of wine.

At 125 mg: steady climb to a +++ over the first two hours. The body was
peaceful with no strong energy push initially, though the subject treated the
stomach with "quiet respect." By the fifth hour, body energy became very
strong, requiring channeling into activities such as writing or exercise to
manage restlessness. Described as a "murderously difficult experience" for a
novice. Sleep was eventually achieved but a continuing awareness of bodily
residue persisted for a couple of days, described as a "low-level poisoning."
The subject concluded there was insufficient excitement or creativity to
justify the physical cost.

## Pharmacological Notes

The pharmacological profile is unusual among 2C-T compounds in producing
predominantly physical effects with minimal visual or cognitive alterations.
This suggests either preferential activity at non-5-HT2A receptor subtypes,
partial 5-HT2A agonism, or significant activity at adrenergic receptors. The
absence of visual effects even at doses well above threshold is notable for a
classical phenethylamine psychedelic. The strong body load, restlessness, and
prolonged physical aftereffects suggest significant peripheral sympathetic
activation. The 12–18 hour duration and multi-day physical residue indicate
slow metabolic processing, likely due to steric hindrance of the tert-butyl
group impeding oxidative metabolism of the sulfur-containing [[shirota-glyoxalylamide-side-chain-construction-psilocin-synthesis]].

## Structure-Activity Relationships

2C-T-9 occupies a unique position due to the extreme steric bulk of the
tert-butyl group. The fully branched C(CH3)3 moiety creates a spherical,
three-dimensional substituent substantially larger than even the longest
straight-chain alkyl groups in the series. The fact that this bulky group
produces an active compound, albeit requiring high doses, indicates the
receptor can accommodate substantial steric bulk at the 4-position. However,
the predominance of physical over visual/cognitive effects and the high dose
suggest the tert-butyl group orients the sulfur atom suboptimally for
psychedelic receptor interaction. The compound demonstrates that steric bulk
alone does not predict psychedelic potency — the shape and flexibility of the
substituent are critical determinants of both character and quality of effects.

## Extensions and Commentary

2C-T-9 served as a gateway to several additional synthetic explorations. The
successful lithiated dimethoxybenzene/disulfide reaction inspired immediate
investigation of three additional disulfide reagents: 2,2-dipyridyl disulfide
(for 2C-T-10, the pyridylthio analogue), di-(4-bromophenyl) disulfide (for
2C-T-11, the bromophenylthio analogue), and N,N-dimorpholinyl disulfide (for
2C-T-12). Neither 2C-T-10 nor 2C-T-11 was pursued to completion, and the
morpholinyl reaction produced virtually no product. An additional attempt to
create a sulfur-nitrogen bonded analogue via the sulfonyl chloride/diethylamine
route also failed, as the resulting sulfonamide was totally refractory to all
reduction efforts. None of the higher-numbered 2C-T compounds have had their
ALEPH (amphetamine) counterparts synthesized.

## Safety Considerations

The [[blesching-cannabis-safety-profile]] is characterized by disproportionate physical toll relative
to psychoactive effects. At active doses, the compound produces strong body
energy, restlessness, and sleep difficulty extending well beyond psychological
effects. The 125 mg dose produced multi-day "low-level poisoning" sensations,
suggesting cumulative toxicity or very slow clearance. The 12–18 hour duration
increases risk of sleep deprivation. The high dose requirement (60–100 mg)
compared to most other 2C-T compounds increases risk of accidental overdose,
as users familiar with more potent members might underestimate the dose
needed. The profile suggests greater physical risk than psychological benefit,
particularly for inexperienced users.
