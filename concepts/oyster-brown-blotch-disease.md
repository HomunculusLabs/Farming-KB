# Brown Blotch Disease in Oyster Mushrooms

Brown blotch disease is a serious condition affecting oyster mushroom crops,
causing significant quality and yield losses. The disease manifests in two
forms: bacterial brown blotch caused by [[pseudomonas-tolaasii-entity-the-mushroom-cultivator]] and fungal brown
blotch suspected to be caused by Verticillium fungicola. Both forms result in
discoloration and deformation [[bloomfield-asterophora-and-mycoparasites-of-fruiting-bodies]], reducing marketability. The
disease is very common in mushroom houses, particularly in Korea.

## Bacterial Brown Blotch Disease

### Pathogen

The major causal agent of bacterial brown blotch is Pseudomonas tolaasii.
Pseudomonas agarici has also been reported as a causal agent, but its
importance as a pathogen of brown blotch disease in oyster mushrooms remains
questionable. These bacteria are widespread in the environment and can be
introduced through contaminated water, substrate, tools, or insect vectors.

### Symptoms

Bacterial brown blotch presents with various symptoms depending on severity
and environmental conditions:

- **Typical symptoms**: Brown spots on caps and stipes that enlarge and
  coalesce. Affected areas become sunken and covered with sticky material
  producing a distinctive rotten fish smell.
- **Advanced symptoms**: Entire fruiting body discolored with reddish brown
  color, appearing waterlogged.
- **Early infection**: Young fruiting bodies covered by clear, glossy
  material that stop growing entirely.
- **Mixed infection**: Various symptom combinations possible when multiple
  pathogens are present simultaneously.

Pseudomonas tolaasii can be isolated from all diseased mushroom specimens,
confirming its primary role in the disease complex.

### Environmental Triggers

Rapid changes in humidity caused by excessive ventilation are strongly
conducive to bacterial brown blotch development. Yellowing of fruiting bodies
can easily result from environmental stress factors even without bacterial
infection, making accurate diagnosis important. The disease is favored by:

- Fluctuating [[greg-green-temperature-and-humidity-control]] in [[mushroom-growing-houses]]
- Free water on fruiting body surfaces
- Excessive watering of cultivation beds
- Poor [[stamets-growing-room-air-circulation-co2-management]] combined with high moisture

### Control Measures

Sanitation is the fundamental control measure for bacterial brown blotch:

- Follow strict Oyster [[mushroom-contamination-control]] protocols at all times
- Pasteurize substrates thoroughly and use only healthy spawn
- [[environmental-control-mushroom-growing]] flies, which are well-known vectors of the pathogen
- Maintain constant humidity and temperature in growing houses
- Avoid free water on mushroom surfaces by ventilating after watering
- Do not water excessively; brown blotch is favored by high moisture
- Use chlorinated water: 5 ppm active chlorine for routine prevention,
  20 ppm when brown blotch is observed

Chlorine compounds used include sodium hypochlorite (NaOCl), calcium
hypochlorite [Ca(OCl)2], and Biosoft (sodium dichloroisocyanurate). Active
chlorine content varies among formulas, and chlorine vaporizes easily, so
fresh solutions must be prepared regularly.
