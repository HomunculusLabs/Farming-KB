## Earthworks Earthworks are human-made modifications to the shape and contour of the land. Since so much of the ecology is affected by the shape of the land, earthworks are among the most powerful tools available to the permaculture designer. The shape of the ground affects the local hydro-cycle, it creates micro-climates, it determines what plants can grow where, and through these effects it shapes animal populations. ## Why Earthworks Matter Just as soup is contained within a bowl, the ecology is contained within geological features such as hills, valleys, grottos, crevices, and soil composition. A permaculture designer cannot take the geo-morphology of the site for granted. Any permaculture design will probably require some extent of earthworks, some more than others. The point of permaculture earthworks is not simply moving earth around for the sake of moving it. You are not a painter and the earth your canvas. The goal is to create geo- morphological conditions that increase the ecological health of the site. This can be accomplished by a host of design strategies. ## Terracing

Terracing is another common strategy with earthworks. Just
like swales, terraces help reduce water flow and create
garden space on slopes and hills. Berms and swales can be
used to slow surface water flow, and terraces should be used
when you need to create garden space or otherwise need a
large flat plane of ground for some reason.

Terraces turn otherwise unusable sloped land into productive
[[fungi-around-the-world-species-distribution]], from the rice terraces of Southeast
Asia to the Andean agricultural terraces of [[permaculture-plants-food-forest-ground-covers-bamboos]], and possibly some
edibles as well.

## Leveling Swales

You will want to use a level as you dig your swale to make
sure that it is level. A typical technique is to get the
longest architects level you can and two bricks. As you dig
out the swale you place the bricks in it, and then lay the
level on top of the bricks. In this way you will be able to
determine if your swale is level across its length.

Another great method for leveling swales is to create a water
level. A water level is essentially a long tube filled with
water that uses the principle that water seeks its own level.
You can also use an A-frame level, a simple tool made from
three pieces of wood that can be built on site.

## Caution: Earthworks Are Transformative

More so than any one other element, earthworks is probably
the single most transformative, or destructive, element in
any design. As you put in berms and swales for your gardens
and foundations for your structures you must constantly be
aware of the effect all of this earthmoving will have on the
overall ecological health of your site. Poorly planned
earthworks can cause erosion, drainage problems, and even
destabilize slopes. Always observe the site thoroughly before
beginning any earthworks, and start small.
