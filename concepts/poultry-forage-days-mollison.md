# Poultry Forage Days and Forage Valuation

updated: 2026-04-18

Permaculture Two (1979) Chapter 8 introduces the concept of "Poultry Forage Days" (P.F.D.) and "Goat Forage Days" (G.F.D.) as metrics for valuing perennial plants in integrated livestock systems.

## The P.F.D. Concept

"When we reach more sophisticated levels, it will be possible to express the 'value' of certain trees and plants in terms of P.F.D. or 'poultry forage days'. That is, the value of a mature tree lucerne may be about 2.5 kilos of seed, which would keep a hen for 30 days or so on free range."

## Documented Examples

### Black Walnut
Smith states that a black walnut at maturity will keep 8 hens all year. Therefore:

- Single tree value = 365 x 8 = 2,920 P.F.D.

This is one of the highest documented forage values for any tree species in temperate climates.

### Carob
A friend in Perth (W.A.) reports that his 17-year-old carob yields a 3-6 bean daily supplement for 3 goats all year (gathering only some of the beans). This is sufficient sugars and "concentrates" for them to deal with on rough range.

- Single carob tree = at least 1,000 G.F.D. (goat forage days) concentrate value

### Tree Lucerne
A mature tree lucerne produces about 2.5 kg of seed, keeping a hen for 30 days or so.

- Single tree lucerne = approximately 30 P.F.D. from seed alone (plus foliage value)

## Application to Design

This valuation system enables rational planting decisions:

1. Plants of high value, even if slow to grow, can be preferentially planted
2. The equivalent of cattle-day assessments used by Turner for herbal leys and pastures can be applied to poultry and small livestock
3. Fig. 4.2 in Permaculture Two was worked out on a "cattle-day" basis by the Victorian Agricultural Dept.

## The Cattle-Day Model

The cattle-day model (Victorian Dept. of Agriculture) and Turner's herbal ley assessments provide the precedent for P.F.D. calculations. Both measure the carrying capacity of specific plantings in terms of animal-days of forage.

## Implications for Species Selection

### Priority Planting

Species with highest P.F.D./G.F.D. values should be given prime planting positions (Zone I and II), best soils, and most reliable water access.

### Long-Term Investment

Black walnut takes many years to reach maturity but achieves 2,920 P.F.D. This justifies planting even slow-growing species as long-term investments in the forage system.

### Multiple Products

Trees valued primarily for P.F.D. may also provide timber, shade, wind protection, bee forage, and human food, increasing their total system value beyond the P.F.D. metric.

## Relationship to Existing Systems

This concept parallels the grazing day calculations used in [[broadscale-permaculture-techniques-mollison]]. It provides a quantitative framework for comparing different forage species and making informed planting decisions.

## Limitations

- P.F.D. values are still approximate and need local calibration
- Seasonal variation in forage availability affects real-world carrying capacity
- Competition between poultry species and other livestock must be considered
- The system does not yet account for protein quality, mineral content, or palatability

## Future Development

Mollison anticipates that as more data accumulates, comprehensive P.F.D. tables will enable designers to calculate the carrying capacity of proposed plantings before installation, similar to how engineers calculate load-bearing capacity of structures.
