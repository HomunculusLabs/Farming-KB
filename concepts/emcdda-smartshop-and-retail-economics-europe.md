# EMCDDA Smartshop and Retail Economics in Europe

## Overview

The EMCDDA's 2006 case study provides detailed economic data on the
retail market for [[emcdda-hallucinogenic-mushrooms-emerging-trend-europe]] in Europe, revealing a
commercial ecosystem that expanded rapidly during the late 1990s and
early 2000s before being curtailed by legislative changes in multiple
EU member states.

## The Dutch Smartshop Market

### Scale and Concentration

The Netherlands served as the epicentre of the European hallucinogenic
mushroom trade. The EMCDDA estimated approximately 120-150 smartshops
operating in the country, though the exact number was unknown. These
shops were heavily concentrated in Amsterdam, where tourism and drug
policy created favourable conditions for retail.

### Revenue Significance

According to the Dutch Risk Assessment Report (CAM, 2000), magic
mushrooms constituted approximately 50% of total smartshop turnover.
This figure underscores the economic importance of hallucinogenic
mushrooms to the broader smartshop ecosystem, which also sold vitamins,
mineral supplements, and other health products alongside psychoactive
herbal substances and new synthetic drugs not yet under legal control.

### Distribution Channels

The Dutch market employed multiple distribution channels:

- **Smartshops**: Primary retail venues selling legal natural products
  including magic mushrooms
- **Coffee shops**: A subset also sold [[query-how-can-i-grow-mushrooms-alongside-garden-vegetables]] cannabis
- **"Paddo" cabs**: Home delivery services, indicating sophisticated
  market development

### Product Types Sold

Dutch retailers primarily sold Psilocybe cubensis varieties,
most notably Psilocybe mexicana, none of which grow wild in Europe.
Products were available as:

- Fresh mushrooms (stay fresh for 7-10 days due to low water content
  of cultivated cubensis varieties)
- Dried mushrooms
- Spore prints and spore syringes
- Spawn bags (sterile hardwood chips inoculated with mycelium)
- Growkits (complete cultivation kits with colonised substrate,
  filter bag, and paperclips)

## The UK Retail Market

### Market Size Before Prohibition

An estimated 300 shops and market stalls across the UK sold
hallucinogenic mushrooms, including dedicated smartshops, market
stalls (particularly Camden, London), and headshops.

### Economic Impact

The UK Treasury collected sales tax estimated at up to £175,000
(~€255,000) annually on a total turnover of approximately £1 million
(~€1.46 million). Source: UK Home Office Drugs Bill Regulatory
Impact Assessment, 2004.

### Purchase Patterns

The Mixmag 2004 readership survey provided insight into UK purchase
channels:

- **54.6%** purchased their last batch from a club, shop, or stall
- **16%** obtained mushrooms from a friend
- **4.2%** purchased via the internet

### The Prohibition Effect

Following reclassification of hallucinogenic mushrooms as Class A drugs
in July 2005, the market collapsed. Internet sites stopped selling
mushrooms, with some (like AllSalvia.co.uk) simultaneously claiming to
be "sold out" and posting notices about the impending legal change.
The Mixmag 2005 survey found 67% of respondents reported that
mushrooms had become less available than previously.

### Market Substitution

The prohibition triggered immediate [[emcdda-herbal-highs-market-product-substitution-bans]] by retailers.
UK online headshops like EveryOneDoesIt.com and AllSalvia.co.uk
switched sales to:

- **[[allegro-amanita-muscaria-properties-and-pharmacology]]** (fly agaric) and closely related Amanita
  pantherina (panther cap)
- **Purple Ohms capsules** containing [[purple-ohms-argyreia-nervosa-legal-mushroom-substitutes]] (Hawaiian
  Baby Woodrose) seeds with lysergic acid amide (LSA), marketed as
  having "similar effects to LSD"

## Online Market Snapshot (January 2006)

### Methodology

The EMCDDA conducted a systematic search via Google and AltaVista using
keywords: "magic mushrooms shop," "buy magic mushrooms," "psilocybe
cubensis shop," and "buy psilocybe cubensis." The first 300 results
per search were analysed.

### Results

A total of **39 unique online shops** were identified:

- **82%** based in the Netherlands
- **18%** included four Austrian, two German, one UK (linked to Dutch),
  and one Polish site (primarily cannabis)

showed access originating primarily from the Netherlands (73%),
followed by Belgium (14%), Germany (2%), and France (2%).
