# Psilocybin Phosphorylation via Zwitterionic Intermediate

## Overview

The key innovation in the Shirota, Hakamata, and Goda synthesis of [[hmbc-noesy-nmr-analysis-zwitterionic-phosphate-intermediate-psilocybin]] — a compound that forms spontaneously during the phosphorylation of psilocin and that enables large-scale purification without chromatography.

This intermediate, designated compound 8, was "completely identified by means of 2D NMR analyses" and represents a novel chemical entity not previously described in the literature.

Its zwitterionic nature (carrying both positive and negative charges) makes it insoluble in dichloromethane, allowing isolation by simple filtration — a dramatic simplification compared to the chromatographic methods required by earlier syntheses.

## The Phosphorylation Challenge

Converting psilocin to psilocybin requires adding a phosphate group to the 4-hydroxy position of the [[psilocin-psilocybin-synthesis-shirota]], with earlier reports yielding only 20%, 47%, or requiring ion-exchange resin purification.

Several phosphorylation methodologies were applied before the successful route was found:

- Most methods did not consume the starting psilocin at all.
- The phosphoryl iodide method (using tribenzyl phosphite, I₂, and DMAP) appeared promising.
- The pyrophosphate method (using tetrabenzylpyrophosphate and n-BuLi) was ultimately selected for its easy handling and reagent stability.

## The Phosphorylation Reaction

Psilocin is dissolved in anhydrous THF and cooled to -78°C.

A solution of n-butyllithium in n-hexane is added, and after stirring for 5 minutes, tetrabenzylpyrophosphate is added all at once.

Stirring continues for 1 hour while the temperature is allowed to warm to 0°C.

The reaction produces the dibenzyl-protected phosphate derivative (compound 7), which was the intended product.

However, after the usual aqueous workup, the ¹H NMR spectrum of the remaining substance showed complicated signals.

Rechecking the TLC revealed an additional spot at the origin, and the whitish material no longer dissolved in CH₂Cl₂.

## Discovery of the Zwitterionic Intermediate

The insolubility of the worked-up reaction mixture pointed to the formation of an unexpected product.

Nichols and Frescas had previously reported that "hydrolytic cleavage of one of the O-benzyl groups rapidly occurred and the resulting zwitterionic O-monobenzyl phosphate was obtained as a mixture."

Purification by preparative reversed-phase HPLC afforded a single compound (8), which was analyzed by NMR.

The ¹H and ¹³C NMR spectra in CD₃OD showed signals for two sets of benzyl groups and a psilocin core.

Critically, the proton signal of the methylene on one benzyl group was shifted to high field (δ 4.56) compared to the other (δ 4.98 and 4.96).

The proton signals of the N,N-dimethyl and methylene groups on the psilocin core were shifted to low field compared to psilocin itself.

The ³¹P NMR spectrum confirmed the presence of the phosphate moiety.

## Structural Elucidation by 2D NMR

The structural assignment was confirmed using two-dimensional NMR experiments:

### HMBC (Heteronuclear Multiple Bond Correlation)

The HMBC spectrum revealed that one benzyl group was directly linked at the nitrogen of the N,N-dimethyl part, forming a quaternary ammonium ion.

This was unexpected — the benzyl group had migrated from the phosphate oxygen to the nitrogen during workup.

### NOESY (Nuclear Overhauser Enhancement Spectroscopy)

The NOESY spectrum supported the linkages identified by HMBC.

### Structure of Compound 8

The data established compound 8 as a **zwitterionic N,O-dibenzyl phosphate derivative**:

- One benzyl group remains on the phosphate oxygen (O-benzyl).
- The other benzyl group has migrated to the dimethylamino nitrogen, forming a quaternary ammonium cation (N-benzyl).
- The phosphate group carries a negative charge.
- The quaternary ammonium carries a positive charge.

The overall structure is thus: **{Benzyl[2-(4-oxyindol-3-yl)ethyl]dimethylammonio}-4-O-benzyl phosphate**.

## Practical Advantages of the Zwitterion

The zwitterionic nature of compound 8 is the key to the practical success of this synthesis.
