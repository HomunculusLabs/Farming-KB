## Overview

Stinkhorns, [[stinkhorn-cage-fungi-reproductive-biology]], and certain flowering plants have independently
evolved remarkably similar strategies for attracting insects: producing
foul odors that mimic rotting flesh, dung, or carrion. This parallel
evolution demonstrates how unrelated organisms — fungi and angiosperms
— converge on the same solution when faced with the same ecological
problem: how to lure flies and beetles as vectors for reproductive
dispersal.

## The Stinkhorn Strategy

[[stinkhorns-and-cage-fungi]] fungi (order Phallales) produce [[bloomfield-asterophora-and-mycoparasites-of-fruiting-bodies]]
that are essentially insect traps disguised as food:

- The [[comparison-fruiting-body-vs-mycelium-extracts-medicinal-mushrooms]] emerges from an underground egg as a stalk
  topped with a slimy, foul-smelling [[growing-gourmet-spore-mass-inoculation-techniques]] called the gleba
- The odor — variously described as resembling rotting meat,
  excrement, or death — is produced by [[fungal-volatile-organic-compounds-communication]] compounds
  including dimethyl disulfide, dimethyl trisulfide, and various
  amines
- Flies are attracted to the odor, land on the gleba, become coated
  in spore slime, and carry spores to new locations

Different species of flies are "lured by particular scents and
personalized [[cannabis-harvest-timing-visual-cues]]," suggesting that the diversity of stinkhorn
morphologies reflects adaptive radiation into different fly-dispersal
niches. Each species has evolved a specific scent profile and visual
display optimized for its preferred insect vector.

## The Corpse Flower Parallel

The Sumatran giant *Amorphophallus titanum* — the corpse flower —
represents a nearly identical strategy deployed by a completely
unrelated organism. Its enormous inflorescence produces a stench
that Money describes as "stinkhorny," attracting carrion beetles
and flies that normally feed on dead animals. The parallel extends
beyond odor to include:

- Heat production (thermogenesis) to volatilize scent compounds
  more effectively
- Dark red or purple coloration that mimics the appearance of
  meat
- A temporary bloom period synchronized with insect activity

## Convergent Evolution vs. Shared Ancestry

The similarity between [[stinkhorn-fungi-and-insect-spore-dispersal]] carrion flowers cannot be
explained by shared ancestry. Fungi are more closely related to
animals than to plants, and the Phallales diverged from the
angiosperm lineage hundreds of millions of years before either group
evolved carrion mimicry. The parallel represents genuine convergent
evolution — independent arrival at the same solution through
different evolutionary pathways.

The convergence is particularly striking because it involves
multiple trait dimensions simultaneously:

- **Chemical convergence**: both groups synthesize similar volatile
  sulfur compounds and amines to create a carrion-like odor
- **Visual convergence**: dark reds, purples, and fleshy textures
  reinforce the olfactory signal
- **Temporal convergence**: both groups time their display to
  coincide with peak insect activity
- **Ecological convergence**: both target the same insect guilds —
  carrion-feeding flies and beetles
