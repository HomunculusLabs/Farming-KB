# Quail Husbandry

Quail are the most space-efficient and fastest-producing homestead livestock. They mature in 6 weeks, begin laying eggs at 7-8 weeks, and require minimal space, feed, and infrastructure. Ideal for small properties, urban settings, or as a supplement to other livestock. Drawn from [[nicole-faires]]' comprehensive manual.

## Breed Selection

| Breed | Mature Weight | Eggs/Year | Egg Color | Characteristics |
|-------|--------------|-----------|-----------|-----------------|
| Coturnix (Japanese) | 5-7 oz | 250-300 | Speckled brown | Most common, fastest maturity, calm |
| Jumbo Coturnix | 10-14 oz | 200-250 | Speckled brown | Larger meat bird, good egg producer |
| Bobwhite | 6-8 oz | 100-150 | White | Wild-type, flighty, harder to raise |
| Button | 1-2 oz | 50-80 | Speckled | Tiny, ornamental, not for production |
| Texas A&M | 10-14 oz | 250-300 | White | Jumbo strain, white eggs, docile |

### Choosing

- Best overall: Coturnix (easy, prolific, fast)
- Meat production: Jumbo Coturnix or Texas A&M
- Small space/urban: Coturnix (quiet, small)
- Beginners: Coturnix (hardy, forgiving)
- Avoid: Bobwhite for beginners (flighty, lower production)

## Housing

### Space Requirements

- Minimum cage space: 1 square foot per bird
- Colony floor space: 2 square feet per bird (with 1 male per 4-5 females)
- Height: 12-15 inches minimum (quail fly straight up when startled)

### Cage Design

- Wire mesh floor (1/2 inch hardware cloth): allows droppings to fall through
- Tray beneath for manure collection (or deep litter if floor-raised)
- Wire: 16-gauge welded wire, 1x2 inch mesh for walls
- Roof: solid to prevent escape and protect from rain
- Door: hinged panel for access, secure latch
- Feeder and waterer inside the cage

### Hutch/Rack System

- Stackable cages: 3-4 tiers high
- Each tier: 24x36 inches (holds 12-15 birds)
- Manure trays slide out for cleaning
- Floor space per tier: 6 square feet
- Total height for 4-tier system: approximately 5-6 feet

### Floor Housing (Colony)

- Not recommended for beginners (easier for disease, pecking, cannibalism)
- If using: 2 sq ft per bird, deep litter (pine shavings), low light (reduces cannibalism)
- Provide hiding spots: small boxes, branches, hay bales
- Males must be separated (ratio 1:4-5 females) or fights will occur

### Outdoor Considerations

- Quail tolerate cold well if dry and out of wind
- Summer heat is the bigger threat: provide shade, fans, frozen water bottles
- Protect from predators: rats, weasels, raccoons, hawks, owls
- Wire must extend to the ground (or use hardware cloth on the floor)
- Cover outdoor runs with wire or netting (hawks are a major threat)

## Feeding

### Feed Requirements

| Stage | Protein | Feed Type | Amount (per bird/day) |
|-------|---------|-----------|----------------------|
| Chick (0-5 weeks) | 24-28% | Game bird starter or turkey starter | Free choice |
| Grower (5-7 weeks) | 20-22% | Game bird grower | Free choice |
| Layer (7+ weeks) | 18-20% | Game bird layer or turkey layer | 0.8-1.0 oz |
| Breeder | 20-22% | Breeder ration | 1.0-1.2 oz |

- Feed consumption: approximately 20-25 pounds of feed per bird per year
- A 50 lb bag of game bird feed feeds approximately 2-3 birds for one year
- Provide grit (fine sand or chick grit) free choice
- Provide oyster shell free choice for layers (prevents thin-shelled eggs)
- Fresh water always available (quail dehydrate quickly)

### Supplements

- Mealworms: excellent protein supplement (10-15% of diet)
- Green feeds: grass, dandelion, clover (chopped)
- Vegetable scraps: lettuce, kale, cucumber (chopped small)
- Scratch grains: small amount as treat (not more than 10% of diet)
- Avoid: avocado, chocolate, onion, citrus, salty foods

## Breeding

### Natural Breeding

- Coturnix quail do not go broody (they will not incubate their own eggs in captivity)
- For natural incubation: Bobwhite or other wild-type quail will sometimes set
- If a hen does go broody, provide a quiet, dark nesting area with 10-12 eggs

### Artificial Incubation

1. Collect eggs daily, store pointed-end down at 55-65F for up to 7 days
2. Pre-warm incubator to 99.5F (37.5C) and 50-55% humidity for 24 hours
3. Place eggs on their sides or in an automatic turner
4. Turn eggs 3-5 times daily (or use automatic turner)
5. Day 1-14: 99.5F, 50-55% humidity, turn regularly
6. Day 15-17 (last 3 days): stop turning, increase humidity to 65-70% ("lockdown")
7. Hatch day: day 17-18 for Coturnix (23-24 days for Bobwhite)
8. Do not open incubator during hatching
9. Leave chicks in incubator 12-24 hours after hatch to dry

### Hatchling Care

- Brood at 95F for first week
- Reduce temperature 5F per week until reaching ambient temperature (approximately 4 weeks)
- Use a heat lamp or brooder plate
- Starter feed (24-28% protein) free choice from day 1
- Water: marbles in the water dish to prevent drowning
- Non-slippery surface (paper towels over shavings for first week)
- 1 square foot per 10 chicks in brooder


## Egg Production

Coturnix begin laying at 6-8 weeks, producing 250-300 eggs/year (9-12g each, ~1/5 chicken egg). Peak production at 10-20 weeks with 14-16 hours light. Below 12 hours light, production stops. Productive life: 1-2 years. 3-4 quail eggs = 1 chicken egg.

## Processing (Meat)

- Processing age: 6-8 weeks for Coturnix (optimal feed-to-meat conversion)
- Weight at processing: 5-7 oz (Coturnix), 10-14 oz (Jumbo)
- Dressing percentage: approximately 70-75% of live weight
- Yield per bird: 3-5 oz meat (Coturnix)

### Common Issues

| Problem | Prevention |
|---------|------------|
| Cannibalism | Reduce light, provide space, greens |
| Coccidiosis | Clean water, dry conditions |
| Respiratory | Good ventilation, dry conditions |
| Pasty vent | Clean water, proper temp |
| Mites/Lice | Clean housing, DE |
| Egg binding | Calcium/oyster shell |

### Preventive Care

Keep housing clean and dry, provide fresh water, avoid overcrowding, quarantine new birds 2 weeks, provide oyster shell, monitor for cannibalism (biggest killer), clean waterers weekly, remove sick birds immediately.

## Integration and Economics

Quail manure (NPK ~3.5-1.5-1.5) is an excellent compost activator. Can be raised in garage/basement/apartment. Quail eggs sell for $4-8/dozen. Break-even at first egg (~7 weeks). Startup for 10 birds + cage: $50-100. Feed: ~$8-12/bird/year.
