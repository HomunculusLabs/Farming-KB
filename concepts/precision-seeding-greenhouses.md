# Precision Seeding in Greenhouses

Precision seeding is one of the most impactful techniques a winter-harvest grower can adopt. By sowing seeds at exact spacings with mechanical seeders, growers can practically double their yields per square foot compared to single-row or homemade multi-row seeders. In the intensive greenhouse environment where every square foot of protected growing space commands a premium, the efficiency gains from precision seeding are substantial.

## Row Spacing and Crop Density

The standard row spacing for most greenhouse salad and root crops is 2¼ inches between rows. This tight spacing makes optimal use of the valuable greenhouse footprint. Some crops require more room — spinach, claytonia, and turnips are planted at 4½-inch row spacing by filling only every other hopper on the seeder. Baby-leaf salad crops, radishes (at 2 square inches per plant), and carrots (at 4 square inches per plant) all thrive on the standard 24-inch spacing.

The economic rationale is straightforward: in a greenhouse where heating, covering, and maintaining the structure represent fixed costs, maximizing output per unit area is the primary lever of profitability. [[precision-seeders]] that are ten to fifteen times less expensive than large European multi-row greenhouse seeders can deliver comparable results when used skillfully.

## The Pinpoint Seeder

The Pinpoint Seeder is a hand-pushed tool (not a machine) whose effectiveness depends on the skill of the operator. It uses two seed-selection mechanisms:

1. **Four seed-hole sizes on a movable axle** — identified as A (smallest), B, C, and D (largest). The axle is positioned so the desired hole size is centered under the hoppers.
2. **Adjustable brushes in each hopper** — set with a thumbscrew to T (tight), M (medium), or L (loose) to control how many seeds pass through each hole per rotation.

The rolling circumference of the wheel is approximately 9 inches, and sizes A, B, and C have nine holes per rotation — delivering roughly one seed per inch. Size D has seven holes per rotation. For even greater flexibility, an extra axle can be machined with deeper seed holes (D by ⅛ inch, A/B/C by 1/16 inch) to accommodate larger seeds like beet and chard.

### Soil Preparation Requirements

Precision seeders demand finely prepared soil. Rocks, undecomposed compost chunks, stringy weeds, or root residues will jam the mechanism and produce erratic results. The extra production per square foot more than justifies the additional care in seedbed preparation. Beds should be raked smooth and lightly rolled afterward — a 30-inch-wide mesh-surfaced roller is ideal because it compacts without glazing the soil surface.

### Record-Keeping System

A simple notation system tracks ideal settings for each seed lot. Settings are written directly on the seed packet using a code that begins with the numeral identifying the seeder type. For example:

- **4BT** — Four-row seeder, hole size B, brush tight (baby-leaf lettuce in greenhouse)
- **4BM** — Four-row seeder, hole size B, brush medium (same lettuce outdoors, slightly higher rate)
- **4AM** — Four-row seeder, hole size A, brush medium (arugula in lower light of winter)
- **4BT** — Same seeder, tighter brush (arugula in summer)

Three exceptionally small seeds — claytonia, minutina, and sylvetta — are so fine that even the smallest hole drops multiple seeds. The brushes are set tightly for these, and the extra seedlings are tolerated since these crops tolerate close growing. A modification with liquid solder to seal the seams in the seed hoppers prevents these tiny seeds from falling through gaps.

## Guiding and Operating the Seeder

Several operational techniques ensure consistent results:

- **Walk backward** while pulling the seeder so you can watch the seed drop and immediately correct any problems (wheels not turning, seeds not dropping, rocks knocking the seeder off line).
- **Depth control** is achieved by adjusting the handle angle — raising the handle for shallower sowing, lowering for deeper. This becomes second nature but requires conscious thought when switching seed types.
- **Furrow openers** are designed so soil falls back quickly after seed drop. Light irrigation after sowing washes additional soil over the seeds, or the mesh roller can be used to firm them in while leaving a smooth surface ideal for close-cut mesclun harvesting.
- **Alignment** uses the tooth marks left by the seeder wheels as guides for the next set of rows. On the four-row model, the wheel teeth run 2⅞ inches beyond the outside rows, so they may track over previously sown rows during successive passes — this has not proven problematic.

### Moisture Management

If the seeder surfaces are wet, seeds will stick. In cold, moist conditions, the seeder can be warmed slightly in front of a heater to prevent condensation. For especially damp greenhouses where condensed moisture drips from the roof, a form-fitting Plexiglas cover over the seed hoppers provides protection.

## The Six-Row Seeder

The six-row seeder represents an evolution of the pinpoint design, optimized for larger areas with several key improvements:

- **Integrated mesh rollers** — front roller levels soil, rear roller drives the seed axle and presses seeds into the soil, eliminating the need for separate rolling passes.
- **Three drive pulleys with a belt** — allow seed delivery at one per inch, one every two inches, or one every four inches.
- **Six rows instead of four** — a 30-inch-wide bed can be planted in two passes (one down, one back).
- **Screw-knob seed-depth adjustment** on the front roller for quick, precise depth changes.
- **Pushed rather than pulled** — the operator maintains pressure on the rear roller to ensure axle rotation.

The six-row seeder dramatically speeds up the sowing process: no separate bed-rolling passes needed, and only two passes to sow a full 30-inch bed instead of three.

## Key Takeaways

| Factor | Recommendation |
|--------|---------------|
| Standard row spacing | 2¼ inches for salad/root crops |
| Wide-row crops | 4½ inches (spinach, claytonia, turnips) |
| Soil prep | Fine, rock-free, lightly rolled |
| Yield improvement | ~2× over single-row seeders |
| Record keeping | Write settings on each seed packet |
| Storage | Dry location; loosen brushes before storing |
