# Fodder Growing Guide

Growing fodder — crops specifically cultivated for livestock feed — reduces feed costs, improves animal nutrition, and increases homestead self-sufficiency. Fodder systems range from simple field crops to intensive sprouting operations, each suited to different scales and livestock types.

## Types of Fodder Systems

### Field Fodder Crops
Traditional fodder crops grown in the field and harvested for storage:

- **Hay** — Dried grasses and legumes (alfalfa, clover, timothy, orchardgrass). The backbone of winter feeding programs.
- **Silage** — Fermented, high-moisture forage stored in anaerobic conditions. Corn silage is most common, but grass and small-grain silages work well.
- **Haylage** — Intermediate between hay and silage; baled at higher moisture than hay and wrapped for fermentation.
- **Root crops** — Mangel beets, turnips, rutabagas, and sweet potatoes. Excellent winter feed for cattle, sheep, and pigs.

### Sprouted Fodder (Hydroponic)
Growing cereal grains in trays for 7-10 days to produce green fodder:

- **Grains used:** Barley (most common and productive), wheat, oats, sunflower seeds
- **Yield:** 1 lb of seed produces 6-8 lbs of fodder
- **Space:** 100 sq ft of tray space feeds 2-3 adult goats or 1 cow daily
- **Cycle:** 7-10 days from seed to feed; harvest daily for continuous supply
- **Infrastructure:** Trays, rack system, automatic watering, grow lights (optional)

## Planning Fodder Production

### Calculate Annual Needs
- **Dairy cow:** 3-5 tons of hay per year plus supplemental grain
- **Dairy goat:** 0.5-1 ton of hay per year
- **Sheep:** 0.3-0.5 tons of hay per year
- **Meat rabbits:** Minimal hay needs; primarily pellets and garden greens
- See [[livestock-feed-management]] for detailed feed requirements by species

### Crop Selection by Purpose
- **Ruminants** — Grass-legume hay, corn silage, root crops
- **Poultry** — Grain crops (corn, oats, barley), sprouted fodder, vegetable greens
- **Pigs** — Root crops, corn, garden surplus, sprouted barley
- **Rabbits** — Grass hay, sprouted fodder, garden greens (see [[rabbit-husbandry-guide]])

## Field Fodder Production

### Hay Making
1. **Select forage species** — Mix grasses (timothy, orchardgrass, brome) with legumes (alfalfa, clover) for balanced nutrition
2. **Time cutting** — Early bloom for legumes, boot stage for grasses (maximum nutrition)
3. **Curing** — Spread to dry to 15-18% moisture. Turn regularly for even drying.
4. **Baling** — Small square bales (40-60 lbs) are practical for homesteads; round bales require equipment
5. **Storage** — Keep dry and off the ground. Barn storage is ideal; tarped stacks work if well-drained.

### Silage Making
- **Corn silage:** Plant at high density (30,000+ plants/acre), harvest at 65-70% moisture
- **Chop to 3/4" length** for good packing
- **Pack tightly** in silo, bunker, or wrapped bales to exclude air
- **Fermentation:** 3-4 weeks minimum before feeding
- **Small-scale option:** Use 55-gallon drums or garbage bags for small batches

### Root Crops for Fodder
- **Mangel beets** — The traditional winter fodder root. Can produce 15-20 tons/acre. Store in root cellar.
- **Fodder beets** — Similar to mangels, higher sugar content.
- **Turnips** — Fast-growing (60-90 days), can be grazed in place or harvested.
- **Rutabagas** — Cold-hardy, store well, good for cattle and sheep.
- **Sweet potatoes** — Excellent for pigs and poultry. Store warm (55-60°F).

## Sprouted Fodder Systems

### System Components
1. **Seed soaking** — Soak barley seeds for 12-24 hours in water with a splash of vinegar
2. **Draining and rinsing** — Drain and rinse 2-3 times daily during the first 3 days
3. **Tray placement** — Spread soaked seeds evenly in trays with drainage holes (about 2 lbs per 10" x 20" tray)
4. **Watering** — Automatic misting or manual watering 2-3 times daily
5. **Growing period** — 7-10 days; sprouts grow to 6-8 inches
6. **Harvest** — Pull the entire mat from the tray and feed whole

### Pros and Cons
| Aspect | Pros | Cons |
|--------|------|------|
| Nutrition | Fresh green feed year-round | High water content (85%+) |
| Cost | Reduces purchased feed 30-50% | Seed cost adds up |
| Space | Very efficient per sq ft | Needs dedicated area |
| Labor | 15-20 min/day | Daily commitment required |
| Reliability | Consistent daily output | Power outage = crop loss |

### Livestock Suitability
- **Ruminants** — Good supplement, not a complete replacement for roughage
- **Poultry** — Excellent; reduces grain costs significantly
- **Pigs** — Good supplement; reduces feed costs 20-30%
- **Rabbits** — Excellent; natural part of their diet
