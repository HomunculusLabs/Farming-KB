# Cannabis Outdoor Growing

## Overview

Outdoor cannabis cultivation leverages natural sunlight and environmental conditions to produce crops with lower costs and often superior quality compared to [[cannabis-indoor-growing-basics]]. Sun-grown cannabis benefits from the full spectrum of natural light, producing robust [[sativa-terpene-profiles-and-aromatic-diversity]] complex cannabinoid expression. This guide covers seasonal timelines, companion planting, climate considerations, and techniques for successful outdoor cultivation.

## Advantages of Outdoor Growing

- Free light source — no electricity costs for lighting or cooling
- Full-spectrum sunlight produces superior terpene development
- Larger plants and potentially higher per-plant yields
- Lower startup costs than indoor operations
- Natural environmental cues promote vigorous growth
- Living soil and organic methods integrate naturally
- Lower carbon footprint and environmental impact

## Disadvantages

- Dependent on weather and seasonal timing
- Pest and disease pressure from outdoor environment
- Limited to one harvest per year (in most climates)
- Security and privacy concerns
- Odor control is difficult outdoors
- Subject to [[cannabis-regulations-by-state]] on outdoor cultivation
- Longer vulnerability window (5-8 months vs. 3-4 months indoor)

## Climate Zones

### Mediterranean (Zone 8-10)

Ideal cannabis climate: warm, dry summers with mild temperatures and low humidity. Northern California, Southern Europe, parts of Australia. Long growing season allows for multiple planting strategies and large plants.

### Temperate (Zone 5-7)

Shorter growing season with distinct seasons. Northeast US, Northern Europe, parts of Canada. Frost-free period typically May-October. Requires fast-finishing strains (8-week flowering time). Autoflowers extend the growing window.

### Tropical and Subtropical (Zone 10-12)

Year-round growing potential in areas without frost. Hawaii, Southeast Asia, [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-caribbean-central-america]], parts of South America. High humidity requires mold-resistant strains and good airflow management. [[marijuana-strain-guide-sativa]] strains are naturally adapted to these conditions.

### Arid and Semi-Arid

Low rainfall regions with intense sunlight. Desert Southwest US, parts of the Middle East and Central Asia. Requires irrigation infrastructure. Low humidity reduces mold risk. [[marijuana-strain-guide-indica]] and Afghan [[cannabis-landrace-strains]] thrive in these conditions.

### Cold Climates (Zone 3-4)

Very short growing season. Scandinavia, high altitude, northern Canada. Greenhouse or high tunnel required. Autoflowering strains are essential. Consider light deprivation (light dep) greenhouse techniques.

## Seasonal Timeline

### Spring Preparation (February-April)

- Select strains appropriate for your climate and season length
- Source genetics from [[cannabis-seed-banks-and-genetics]]
- Prepare growing site (soil amendment, bed building, irrigation)
- [[query-what-is-seed-starting-and-when-should-i-start-seeds-indoors]] or in a greenhouse (4-6 weeks before last frost)
- Harden off seedlings before transplanting
- Plant companion plants

### Early Vegetative (May-June)

- Transplant seedlings after last frost date
- Establish irrigation and protection from wind
- Apply mulch layer for moisture conservation
- Begin training (LST, tying down for even canopy)
- Monitor for pests (slugs, aphids, caterpillars)
- Top-dress soil with compost and amendments

### Mid-Season Vegetative (June-July)

- Peak vegetative growth — plants can add inches per day
- Continue training for even light exposure
- Prune lower growth that won't receive adequate light
- Manage plant height (satias can exceed 10 feet)
- Monitor soil moisture and amend as needed
- Apply compost tea every 2-4 weeks
- Scout for pests and disease weekly

### Pre-Flowering (Late July-August)

- Plants begin showing pre-flowers (gender identification)
- Remove any males if growing sinsemilla (seedless)
- Reduce nitrogen inputs, increase phosphorus and potassium
- Install support structures (trellis, bamboo stakes)
- Monitor for bud rot in humid conditions
- Continue pest management

### Flowering (August-October)

- Buds develop and swell
- Peak water demand
- Monitor for bud rot, powdery mildew, and pests
- Reduce watering in final 1-2 weeks (flushing debate)
- Protect from rain and excessive moisture
- Harvest indicator: trichomes turn cloudy/amber

### Harvest and Processing (September-November)

- Harvest based on trichome maturity
- Hang-dry in controlled environment (60-70F, 50-60% humidity)
- Trim buds (wet trim or dry trim)
- Cure in airtight containers (burp daily for 2 weeks, then weekly)

## Site Selection, Companion Planting, and Guerrilla Growing

### Site Selection Criteria
- **Sunlight:** Minimum 6-8 hours direct sun daily (south-facing slopes ideal)
- **Wind protection:** Natural windbreaks or planted barriers
- **Water access:** Reliable irrigation source
- **Soil quality:** Well-draining, 12+ inches depth for in-ground
- **Security:** Privacy from neighbors, roads, aerial observation
- **Frost pockets:** Avoid low-lying areas where cold air settles

### Companion Planting

**Pest deterrents:** Marigolds (nematodes), lavender (moths, aphids), basil (thrips, mosquitoes), chrysanthemums (pyrethrins), nasturtiums (aphid trap crop).

**Beneficial insect attractors:** Yarrow, dill, sunflowers, and clover attract ladybugs, parasitic wasps, and hoverflies.

**Soil builders:** Clover (nitrogen-fixing ground cover), comfrey (deep mineral miner), alfalfa (nitrogen fixation), buckwheat (phosphorus accumulator).

**Nutrient accumulators:** Daikon radish (breaks compacted soil), borage (calcium, potassium), nettle (iron, nitrogen).

### Guerrilla Growing

Cultivating cannabis in remote, hidden locations. Use autoflowering strains for smaller profile, mix with surrounding vegetation, visit at varying times, use natural fertilizers, and plant in clusters of 3-5. Risks include theft, wildlife, law enforcement, and inconsistent care.

## Outdoor Pest Management

### Common Outdoor Pests

- **Aphids:** Control with ladybugs, neem oil, insecticidal soap
- **Spider mites:** Control with predatory mites, increased humidity, neem oil
- **Caterpillars:** Hand-pick or use Bt ([[bacillus-thuringiensis]])
- **Slugs/snails:** Diatomaceous earth, beer traps, copper barriers
- **Deer/rabbits:** Physical fencing barriers

### Organic Pest Control

Neem oil, insecticidal soap, diatomaceous earth, Bt, beneficial insect releases (ladybugs, lacewings, predatory mites), and companion planting.
