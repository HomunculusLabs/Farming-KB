# Pricking Out Seedlings

Pricking out is the GROW BIOINTENSIVE technique of transplanting
seedlings from a germination flat into a second flat with fresh soil.
This process is central to the "Breakfast-Lunch-Dinner" concept
developed by Alan Chadwick, where seedlings receive progressively
better nutrition as they develop, minimizing transplant shock and
stimulating uninterrupted growth.

## The Breakfast-Lunch-Dinner Concept

The GROW BIO. If seedlings are raised in very good soil with good
nutrients and good structure, only to be transplanted into an area
with few nutrients and poor structure, the plants will suffer root
shock.

Results are better when seedlings are pricked out from a first flat
with a good planting mixture "breakfast" into a second flat with a
"lunch" consisting of fresh flat soil. The plant will forget the
trauma of being pricked out when it tastes the delectable new lunch
treats in the second flat. This process minimizes shock and even
fosters growth. Finally, a splendid GROW BIOINTENSIVE "dinner"
greets the plant in the growing bed.

With this kind care and stimulated healthy plant growth, there is
less likelihood of insect and disease damage. In the GROW BIOINTENSIVE
method, pricking out and transplanting can stimulate growth rather
than slowing it down.

## When to Prick Out

Seedlings from broadcast seed are ready to be pricked out after their
cotyledons (the first "seed leaves" that appear, although they are
not true leaves) have appeared and before their roots are too long
to handle easily. Timing is important because waiting too long allows
roots to become tangled, making separation difficult and increasing
damage.

A second pricking out, if called for, should be done when the
seedlings' leaves have just begun to touch each other. This prevents
competition for light and resources among crowded seedlings.
