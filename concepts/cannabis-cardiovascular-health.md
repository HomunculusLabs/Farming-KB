# Cannabis for Cardiovascular Health

Research reveals that the [[cannabis-endocannabinoid-system]] plays a significant role in cardiovascular function. Both CB1 and CB2 receptors are naturally present in heart tissue, and the endogenous cardiac cannabinoid system is involved in several phenomena associated with cardioprotective effects. The endocannabinoid system operates as a biological regulatory mechanism throughout the cardiovascular system, influencing vasodilation, cardiac protection, blood pressure control, and inflammatory responses in vascular tissue.

## Heart Disease

CHI Value: 16 (8 studies reviewed)

### Cardioprotective Effects
Multiple pre-clinical studies have demonstrated cannabinoids' ability to protect heart tissue:

- **CBN and THC** significantly reduced heart attack size in mice; neuroprotective effects of THC were mediated via CB1 receptors (Fukuoka, Japan, 2004)
- **THC** protects heart cells against hypoxia damage by induction of nitric oxide (Ramat-Gan, Israel, 2006)
- **Endogenous cannabinoid system** in heart tissue is involved in cardioprotective effects, including reduction of infarct size after induced ischemia (Montreal, 2006)
- **CBD** induced substantial in vivo cardioprotective effect from ischemia, representing "a promising novel treatment for myocardial ischemia" (Jerusalem, 2007)
- **CB2 activation** reduced infarct size and oxidative stress in ischemic heart cells (Geneva, 2009)
- **WIN55,212-2** binds to both CB1 and CB2 receptors but with higher affinity to CB2, relevant for cardiovascular anti-inflammatory effects

### Meta-Analysis Findings
A New Zealand meta-analysis (2007) revealed cannabinoids positively influence vasodilation, cardiac protection, modulation of baroreceptor reflex for systolic blood pressure control, inhibition of endothelial inflammation, and inhibition of atherosclerosis progression. This multi-target cardiovascular action is consistent with the endocannabinoid system's role as a whole-body regulatory network.

### Stress Response
CBD calms autonomic responses to stress, including rapid heart rates, by engaging serotonin-selective receptors (Sao Paulo, Brazil, 2009). This is particularly relevant because psychological factors -- including hostility, pessimism, and chronic stress -- are recognized contributors to cardiovascular disease progression. Cannabis's combined anti-inflammatory and anxiolytic effects may offer dual cardiovascular protection.

### The Mind-Heart Connection
The Blesching source documents a case from Cornell Medical Center of a 43-year-old woman whose severe, treatment-resistant hypertension was linked to repressed memories of sexual assault. After decades of suppression, nightmares triggered by a family encounter led to the emergence of traumatic memories. Through counseling enabling emotional processing -- moving from numbness to feeling powerlessness, betrayal, and rage, and ultimately to identifying as a survivor -- her blood pressure normalized over 18 months with gradual reduction of pharmaceuticals. This case illustrates the deep psychosomatic connections between unresolved trauma and cardiovascular disease, and suggests that therapies modulating the stress response (including cannabinoid-based approaches) may have systemic cardiovascular benefits.

## Hypertension

CHI Value: 5 (2 studies reviewed)

### Blood Pressure Reduction
- **THC inhalation** (2.8%): Patients responded with increased heart rate followed by substantial drop in both systolic and diastolic blood pressure, while maintaining adequate cardiac perfusion (Howard University Hospital, 1976)
- **Anandamide** (intravenous): Lowered blood pressure in hypertensive rats through vasodilation, without affecting rats with normal blood pressure (Nottingham, UK, 2009). This selective action on elevated blood pressure is significant -- it suggests anandamide acts as a homeostatic regulator rather than a blanket blood pressure reducer.
- **WIN55,212-2**: Synthetic cannabinoid also lowered high blood pressure through vasodilation

Both CB1 and CB2 receptors are implicated in activating vasodilation. Non-CB1/non-CB2 cannabinoid receptors in arterial blood vessel lining cells also suggest the system's involvement in maintaining healthy blood pressure. The endocannabinoid system's involvement in blood pressure regulation represents a potentially novel therapeutic avenue, as anandamide reduces hypertension through a mechanism distinct from conventional antihypertensive medications.

## Atherosclerosis

CHI Value: 6 (3 studies reviewed)

- **WIN55,212-2** significantly reduced the size of aortic atherosclerotic lesions in mice (China, 2010)
- **CBD and its derivatives** inhibit 15-LOX enzyme activity, which oxygenates LDL and contributes to plaque formation (Japan, 2009). CBDD (CBD-2',6'-dimethyl ether) had the strongest enzyme inhibitory effects and could be a useful prototype for producing medicines for atherosclerosis.
- **Cannabinoid regulation** of the endocannabinoid circuitry shows therapeutic potential for atherosclerosis (Boston, 2006)
- **CB2 receptor activation** ameliorates atherosclerosis through suppression of adhesion molecules, which are critical in the development of arterial plaques

Habitual cannabis use has been shown to positively affect the human immune system, and regulation of the endocannabinoid circuitry can impact almost every major function associated with the immune system -- including the inflammatory processes that drive atherosclerosis. The enzyme 15-LOX can oxygenate low-density lipoprotein (LDL), leading to oxidized LDL, which plays a factor in developing atherosclerosis. Cannabinoids that inhibit this enzyme represent a targeted therapeutic approach.

## Stroke

The U.S. government patent on cannabinoids as neuroprotectants notes their application in limiting neurological damage following ischemic insults such as stroke. CB2 activation may protect ischemic brain cells during cerebrovascular events. The neuroprotective properties of cannabinoids -- including anti-inflammatory action, antioxidant activity, and anti-excitotoxic effects -- are all relevant to minimizing stroke damage.

## Strain and Form Considerations

- **Indica strains** (lower THC:CBD ratio): Generally preferred for cardiovascular conditions due to greater CBD content and CB2 activation, particularly for atherosclerosis
- **CBD-rich preparations**: CBD shows particular promise for cardiovascular protection without psychoactive effects. Indica and indica-heavy strains tend to possess a lower THC:CBD ratio, thus favoring CB2 expression.
- **Raw cannabis juice**: Non-psychoactive CBD-acid provides cardiovascular benefits without cognitive effects
- **(E)-Beta-caryophyllene**: This dietary cannabinoid found in black pepper, clove, cinnamon, and other spices activates CB2 and may complement cardiovascular cannabinoid therapy. Researchers suggest that activation of CB2 receptors via (E)-beta-caryophyllene-containing spices may present a new and additional therapeutic strategy in treating cardiovascular diseases associated with inflammation and oxidative stress.
- **Tinctures and sublingual administration**: Allow precise dosing control, which is essential given the dose-dependent nature of cannabinoid cardiovascular effects

## Safety Considerations

- Acute cannabis inhalation may temporarily increase heart rate, which could be a concern for patients with severe cardiovascular disease
- The temporary heart rate increase is followed by blood pressure reduction
- Patients with existing heart conditions should consult cardiologists before using cannabis
- Dose titration is essential -- start low and go slow. A San Diego randomized controlled trial demonstrated this vividly: a medium THC dose significantly decreased capsaicin-induced pain, while a high dose actually increased it, showing the narrow therapeutic window.
- It is not safe to drive or operate heavy machinery after taking any psychoactive form of cannabis

## Complementary Cardiovascular Support

Cacao (dark chocolate), cardamom, coconut, garlic, rosemary, and turmeric all show cardiovascular protective properties that may complement cannabinoid therapy. The Blesching source also recommends considering the role of positive emotions -- a study published in 2005 by researchers from London showed that positive emotions such as happiness can lower cortisol levels, reduce heart rate, and decrease fibrinogen stress responses, producing beneficial physiological results for cardiovascular health.

Age-related macular degeneration (AMD) is a painless eye disease characterized by loss of accurate sight in the center of the field of vision. Most common in seniors, AMD is due to damage of the retina (the tissue lining the inner surface of the eye). The [[cannabis-endocannabinoid-system]] plays an important role as a potential therapeutic agent in neurodegenerative diseases. A 2009 study from Shanghai, China, showed that retinal pigment epithelial cells indeed contain cannabinoid receptors [[cannabis-cannabinoid-receptor-pharmacology]] and CB2.
