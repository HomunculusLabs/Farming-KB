# Solvent Properties and Selection Guide

## Overview

The solvents discussed here are common in the research literature on
THC. Benzene and toluene appear with respect to
[[lemon-cannabis-alchemy-isomerization]]. Most others are used in
extraction and chromatography. Petroleum ether is the solvent of
choice for laboratory extraction, but the consensus among amateur
alchemists is that the fire hazard is too high. Every solvent has
serious drawbacks: low-boiling solvents are nearly explosive, while
nonflammable solvents are more poisonous. The researcher appears to
face the choice of risking death by fire or by liver and kidney
damage, preceded by narcosis in both cases.

## Critical Storage Warning

One alchemist's pamphlet recommends storing petroleum ether in the
refrigerator to keep it cold. Unless the refrigerator is designed for
solvent storage, this approximates a bomb. A refrigerator is closed to
air circulation; a slight leak in the cap seal allows the
refrigerator to saturate with petroleum ether vapor (flash point -32
degrees C / -24 degrees F). A spark from the motor or defroster
could then ignite the confined mixture.

## Solvent Effects on THC Stability

The effect of solvent type on the stability of marijuana solutions to
decomposition has been reported (Bonuccelli, 1979). Chloroform and
other chlorine-containing solvents promote significant decomposition
of THC on storage, speeded up by light. In sunlight, a chloroform
solution lost 25-35% of its THC in 30 minutes. Ethanol is better for
storage, but slight decomposition still occurs. Storage in cold and
darkness is recommended. Keeping samples under inert atmosphere
(nitrogen) retards decomposition further.

## Solvent Descriptions from Chapter One

### Methanol (Methyl Alcohol, Wood Alcohol)

Boiling point: 64 degrees C. Commonly employed and does a fine job
when used correctly. Available at pharmacies and industrial chemical
supply companies; also as paint thinner (seldom pure). Methanol fumes
are toxic and explosive. Inhalation causes sickness with pronounced
body ache; continued inhalation of even small amounts may cause
permanent damage. Any traces remaining in the oil are hazardous.
Methanol evaporates at approximately 190 degrees F and does not
extract a lot of water-soluble, non-psychoactive tars.

### Isopropyl Rubbing Alcohol

Most rubbing alcohol is 70% isopropyl alcohol and 30% water. Several
advantages: available at low price in many stores, much less toxic
and explosive than methanol. However, the water content extracts many
water-soluble, non-psychoactive substances. Oil yield is twice that
of methanol but proportionally less potent. Water-soluble tars give
undesirable taste and burning qualities. If the oil will be
re-extracted later with a more selective solvent, this matters less.
The water causes evaporation at much higher temperature; after
alcohol evaporates, remaining water takes a long time to evaporate in
a boiling water bath. An oil bath may be needed instead.

### Ethanol (Ethyl Alcohol, Pure Grain Alcohol)

Boiling point: 78.5 degrees C. Very desirable solvent with
extraction properties similar to methanol but less toxic. Very
difficult to obtain since it is heavily taxed as a liquor ingredient.
Pure ethanol may be produced from liquor or fermented material.
Denatured ethanol from hardware stores contains non-removable poisons
which evaporate at the same temperature as pure ethanol, making it
unfit for consumption.

### Petroleum Ether

Boiling point: 30-60 degrees C. A light solvent much more selective
than any alcohol. Produces oil twice as potent by weight as
alcohol-extracted oil. Due to its highly explosive nature, cannabis
is first extracted with alcohol and then re-extracted with ether,
requiring much less of the dangerous solvent. Usually available only
through chemical supply companies.

## Key to Table Values

**Water Solubility:** misc = miscible; v = >50g/100ml; s = 5-50g/100ml;
d = <5g/100ml; i = insoluble; decomposes on contact with water.

**NFPA Hazard Identification Signals** (from NFPA No. 704-M):

Health (Blue): 4 = death on short exposure; 3 = serious injury; 2 =
temporary incapacitation; 1 = irritation only; 0 = no hazard beyond
ordinary combustibles.

Flammability (Red): 4 = vaporizes readily at normal temperature and
burns; 3 = ignitable under almost all conditions; 2 = requires
moderate heating; 1 = requires preheating; 0 = will not burn.

Reactivity (Yellow): 4 = detonates at normal temperatures; 3 =
detonates with strong initiating source; 2 = normally unstable; 1 =
normally stable; 0 = stable even under fire conditions.

## Selection Guidance for [[lemon-cannabis-alchemy-thc-extraction-purification]]

For initial extraction from plant material, methanol or ethanol are
practical choices with manageable risk profiles. Petroleum ether
offers superior selectivity for re-extraction of purified oil but
demands extreme fire precautions. Chloroform should be avoided for
storage due to rapid THC degradation, especially in light. Ethanol
is the best choice for long-term storage of THC solutions when kept
cold and dark. See also
[[lemon-cannabis-alchemy-reflux-apparatus-and-laboratory-setup]] for
safe handling procedures applicable to all solvents.
