## Mechanism of Fungal Biobleaching

The major colour in pulping effluents results from dissolved derivatives or fragments of lignin, including lignin sulfonates, chlorolignins, chlorinated phenolics, and their partly oxidized derivatives such as quinones. White rot fungi degrade these lignin-derived chromophores through the same non-specific radical-based mechanisms used in natural [[lignin-peroxidase-mechanism]] -- demonstrated to be the most important enzyme for pulp delignification

- [[brown-rot-fungi-biodegradation]] -- induced by growth on pulping effluent in some species, notably Trametes versicolor

The radical-based attack on lignin is inherently non-specific, which is both its strength and its challenge. The enzymes cannot distinguish between lignin in wood and lignin-derived chromophores in effluent, making them broadly effective. However, this non-specificity also means that enzyme activity can be diverted by competing substrates in complex effluent streams. ## Role of Laccase

Laccase activity can be dose-dependently increased by the presence of pulping liquor in growth medium for T. versicolor (Manzanares, Fajardo and Martin, 1995). Laccase production was induced by growth on olive mill wastewater. In P. flavido-alba, both laccase and MnP were implicated in decolorization. The role of laccase is significant because it uses molecular oxygen as the oxidant (unlike peroxidases which require H2O2), potentially simplifying bioreactor design.

Laccase-mediator systems (LMS) represent a promising approach for industrial biobleaching. When combined with mediators such as HBT (1-hydroxybenzotriazole) or N-hydroxyacetanilide (NHA), laccase can oxidize non-phenolic lignin structures that constitute the majority of residual lignin in Kraft pulp. This dramatically extends the practical delignification capability of laccase beyond its native phenolic substrate range. ## Nutritional Requirements

Chromophores in pulping effluents cannot be degraded without a carbonaceous co-substrate. Glucose or sucrose are the most usual (1-10 g/L), though other sugars, polysaccharides, glycerol, and ethanol have also been used. Nitrogen requirements vary: P. chrysosporium ligninolysis is repressed by high nitrogen, while T. versicolor does not show nitrogen repression of effluent decolorization. Interestingly, high nitrogen concentrations repressed P. chrysosporium decolorization with glucose as co-substrate but promoted it with cellulose.

The co-substrate requirement represents a significant cost factor for industrial application. Research has explored using less expensive carbon sources including molasses, corn steep liquor, and effluent organic matter itself. The nitrogen effect differs among species because ligninolytic regulation pathways vary, with some fungi producing ligninolytic enzymes constitutively or under different metabolic triggers. These nutritional considerations are central to [[chlorophenol-degradation-by-white-rot-fungi]]. Reductive dechlorination appears to occur alongside oxidative degradation, with quinone intermediates serving as electron acceptors. The simultaneous removal of chlorine atoms and aromatic ring structures is a distinctive advantage of fungal treatment over conventional approaches, which often address only one aspect of effluent toxicity.

## Bioreactor Configurations for Biobleaching

Biobleaching has been demonstrated in multiple reactor types:

- **Rotating biological contactors (RBCs)** -- discs partially submerged in effluent, providing alternating exposure to wastewater and atmosphere

- **Packed-bed reactors** -- fungal biomass immobilized on solid supports through which effluent flows

- **Fluidized-bed reactors** -- suspended carriers with attached biomass in upflowing liquid

- **Continuous stirred-tank reactors (CSTR)** -- well-mixed systems suitable for pellet-forming fungi

- **Trickle-bed reactors** -- effluent percolated over fixed fungal mats with passive aeration

Each configuration offers distinct advantages for different treatment scales and effluent characteristics. Reactor selection is a key component of [[fungal-immobilization-bioreactor-systems]].

## Performance

Most studies report 60-90% colour removal in 3-6 days, with some reports exceeding 90% (Archibald, Paice and Jurasek, 1990a). Effective treatment has been demonstrated in multiple bioreactor configurations including rotating biological contactors, packed-bed reactors, fluidized-bed reactors, and continuous stirred-tank reactors.
