# Psilocybin Extraction with Ethyl Alcohol

Psilocybin can be extracted from dried mushroom material using pure ethyl alcohol (ethanol) as a solvent. This technique produces a concentrated [[pf-tek-ethyl-alcohol-extraction-psilocybin-liqueur]] that can be stored long-term and dosed with precision. The process is relatively simple, requiring only mushroom material and alcohol, and avoids the use of toxic industrial solvents.

## Why Ethyl Alcohol?

Pure ethyl alcohol (200 proof, anhydrous) is the preferred solvent for [[psilocybin-extraction]] for several important reasons:

- **Safety**: Unlike methanol or other industrial solvents, ethanol is a drinkable spirit that is non-toxic to humans. Any residual solvent remaining in the final extract poses no health risk.
- **Availability**: In the United States, 200 proof ethanol can be purchased at well-stocked liquor stores, either over the counter (in some states) or with a simple permit from the state liquor board.
- **Effectiveness**: Ethanol efficiently dissolves [[hplc-analysis-of-psilocybin-and-psilocin]] from [[hplc-analysis-psilocybin-psilocin-mushroom-tissue]]. While 190 proof (95% ethanol, 5% water) is acceptable, 200 proof is preferred because the absence of water means less volume to evaporate during concentration.

## The Extraction Process

### Step 1: Preparation and Pulverization

Begin with thoroughly dried mushroom material — the mushrooms should be rock-hard to the touch. Any residual moisture reduces extraction efficiency and introduces water into the final product. Use at least several grams of dried material to make the process worthwhile.

To pulverize the dried mushrooms, place them in a heavy-duty freezer bag, cover with a magazine or towel for protection, and crush with the rubber heel of a shoe or a similar heavy object. The goal is a fine powder, which maximizes the surface area exposed to the solvent and therefore the extraction efficiency.

### Step 2: Initial Soak

Transfer the pulverized material to a bottle with a tight-fitting, leak-proof cap. Add enough alcohol to cover the mushroom material and create a loose slurry. Seal the bottle and shake vigorously. Allow the mixture to soak for 24 hours, shaking periodically to agitate the slurry and improve extraction.

### Step 3: Filtration

After 24 hours, filter the alcohol from the mushroom solids. Inexpensive dust-pollen masks (available at hardware and drug stores) serve as effective filters. Fashion the mask over the mouth of a drinking glass, shake the slurry bottle, pour it into the filter, and squeeze to extract the alcohol. Collect the filtered alcohol extract in a clean bottle.

### Step 4: Secondary Extraction

The filtered mushroom material still contains residual psilocybin. Return the solids to the original bottle, add fresh alcohol, and repeat the 24-hour soak and filtration process. Combine both alcohol extracts.

## Evaporation and Concentration

The combined extracts are then concentrated by evaporating some of the alcohol. Pour the extract into a glass and place a small electric fan so that it directs airflow directly into the glass, causing the surface to ripple. This accelerates evaporation. The process takes several hours depending on the volume.

As the alcohol level drops, wash any residue adhering to the inside of the glass back into the solution. Keep all flames away from the working area — pure ethanol is highly flammable. The fumes produced are harmless since ethanol is non-toxic.

### Target Concentration

The evaporation should be continued until the desired concentration is reached. A common benchmark is 1 to 3 or more grams of dried mushroom material per 1 cc (mL) of final extract. This produces a potent brew where only a small volume needs to be consumed for a full dose.

The concentrated extract will have a pungent, mushroomy aroma. It should be stored in small screw-cap bottles or vials in the freezer. Ethanol does not freeze solid at standard freezer temperatures, so the extract will remain liquid and stable.

## Precise Dosing

A 10 cc syringe is recommended for accurate dosing. Precision is important because the concentrated nature of the extract means that even a small miscalculation can result in a significantly stronger experience than intended. Starting with a test dose and adjusting upward is the prudent approach.

## Sourcing 200 Proof Ethyl Alcohol

Full-service liquor stores in the United States supply hospitals and laboratories with 200 proof anhydrous ethyl alcohol. If a particular store does not stock it, the staff can usually direct you to one that does.

In states requiring a permit, contact the state liquor board (typically located in the state capital) and request an application for an ethyl alcohol permit. The fee is nominal (typically $5–10). The application will ask about the intended use — acceptable responses include "non-toxic surface sterilizing," "herb extraction," "preservation," "tincture making," and "perfume making."
