# Krameterhof - Sepp Holzer's Model Farm

updated: 2026-04-18

The Krameterhof is the 45-hectare (110-acre) family farm of [[sepp-holzer-terrace-systems-and-raised-beds]], located in the Lungau region of Salzburg province, Austria, at an elevation of 1,100 to 1,500 metres above sea level. It serves as the primary demonstration site for Holzer permaculture and is one of the most influential alternative farms in the world.

## Location and Climate

The farm occupies a steep south-facing mountainside in the Austrian Alps, spanning an elevational range of 400 metres. The climate is harsh by conventional agricultural standards: short growing seasons, severe winters with heavy snowfall, and cool summer temperatures. The soil is predominantly loam, though it varies considerably across the site. Despite these challenging conditions -- conditions that conventional agriculture would deem marginal or unworkable -- the Krameterhof produces an extraordinary diversity of food, fibre, and other products.

The elevational range is critical to Holzer's approach. Different crops and animals are positioned according to the microclimates created by the slope, aspect, water features, and shelterbelts. The lower areas near streams and ponds provide warmer, more sheltered sites for demanding crops, while the higher slopes accommodate hardy species adapted to exposure and cold.

## Landscape Design

The Krameterhof is not a farm in any conventional sense but rather an intricately designed ecosystem. From above, it appears as a complex mosaic of [[sepp-holzer-terrace-systems-and-raised-beds]], [[sepp-holzer-terrace-systems-and-raised-beds]], [[holzer-tamera-water-landscape-project]], waterways, wetlands, tracks, shelterbelts, and mixed plantings. Every element serves multiple functions, and the connections between elements are as important as the elements themselves.

The design follows natural contours and works with gravity to move water, nutrients, and heat across the landscape. Ponds at different elevations create a cascading water system that can generate power, breed fish and crayfish, grow aquatic plants, provide irrigation, and create microclimates. The water system was designed so that water flows from the upper boundary of the farm to the lowest point through a network of ponds, wetlands, and ditches.

Approximately 14,000 fruit trees of many different varieties are planted throughout the Krameterhof, integrated with other crops and livestock rather than segregated into orchards. Wild fruit trees are interspersed with cultivated varieties to provide pollination partners and genetic diversity.

## Water Systems

Water management is central to the Krameterhof design. The farm contains numerous ponds of varying sizes and depths, connected by waterways and wetlands. These serve multiple purposes simultaneously:

- **Aquaculture**: Breeding of fish species including trout, tench, pike, wels catfish, zander, koi carp, roach, rudd, and minnows, as well as European crayfish
- **Aquatic plant cultivation**: Different pond zones support different plant communities
- **Microclimate creation**: Open water moderates temperature extremes in surrounding areas
- **Irrigation**: Gravity-fed water distribution across the farm
- **Power generation**: Water power is harnessed through elevation differences between ponds
- **Recreation and aesthetics**: Ponds provide bathing areas and scenic beauty
- **Wildlife habitat**: The ponds and wetlands support amphibians, waterfowl, and other wildlife

Deeper pond zones allow fish to hibernate and survive winter freezing. Shallow zones warm quickly in spring and support warm-water species and aquatic plants. This zonation within individual ponds multiplies the number of ecological niches available.

## Raised Bed Systems

The [[sepp-holzer-terrace-systems-and-raised-beds]] at the Krameterhof are among its most distinctive features. They are not the neat, rectangular structures found in many gardens but large, organic forms that follow the landscape contour. The beds are constructed from a wide variety of materials including logs, branches, chipped wood, green material, and earth. Some are built from whole fallen trees placed directly on the ground and covered with soil.

The raised beds serve as windbreaks, water harvesters, and growing spaces simultaneously. Beds on the windward side of the system are planted with fruit bushes and tall-growing plants to provide additional shelter. The heat generated by decomposing organic matter within the beds extends the growing season and protects plants from frost.

## Animal Integration

Livestock at the Krameterhof includes cattle, [[mangalitza-pig-breeds]], Duroc pigs, Turopoije pigs, poultry, and other species. Animals are managed using [[holzer-livestock-philosophy-paddock-system]] where different species rotate through areas, each contributing different benefits. Pigs serve as living ploughs, turning and aerating soil while fertilizing it. Cattle graze on diverse pastures and contribute manure. Poultry scratch through manure and crop residues, controlling pests and distributing fertility.

The animal shelters at the Krameterhof use [[holzer-roundwood-shelter-construction]] and earth-sheltered design, providing warmth in winter and coolness in summer without energy inputs.

## Mushroom Cultivation

Holzer has conducted extensive [[holzer-mushroom-cultivation-methods]] experiments at the Krameterhof, growing edible fungi including Judas' ear fungus (Auricularia auricula-judae) on inoculated logs. Mushrooms appear in the most unlikely places across the farm, demonstrating how fungal networks develop naturally when habitat is provided through diverse plantings, dead wood, and moist microclimates.

## Plant Diversity

The plant diversity at the Krameterhof is extraordinary. Holzer grows every conceivable cultivated plant suited to the site, from vegetables and grains to fruit trees and herbs. He also cultivates many wild plants, including alpine species such as gentians. Old and heritage varieties are preferred over modern commercial cultivars, as they are better adapted to local conditions and require fewer inputs.

The farm demonstrates that a polyculture approach can produce more total food per hectare than monoculture, while simultaneously building soil, conserving water, supporting biodiversity, and reducing or eliminating the need for external inputs such as fertilizers, pesticides, and imported feed.

## Global Influence

The Krameterhof has become a pilgrimage site for permaculture practitioners and alternative farmers from around the world. Holzer offers guided tours and courses at the farm, and his methods have been adapted to diverse climates and scales through his consulting work on every inhabited continent. The farm demonstrates that what he calls "alternative agriculture" is not a hobby or experiment but a viable, productive, and economically sound approach to food production.

## Key Principles Demonstrated

- Working with natural processes rather than against them
- Creating and exploiting microclimates
- Using water as a design element for multiple functions
- Integrating animals as essential components of the farm ecosystem
- Maximising edge and diversity
- Choosing plant varieties adapted to site conditions
- Building soil through natural processes
- Designing for self-sufficiency and resilience
