# Mycorrhizal Colonization Thresholds

## Overview

Mycorrhizal colonization percentage is a key indicator of root-zone health. Research from [[soil-foodweb]] Inc. (SFI) shows clear thresholds that correlate with disease pressure, root-feeding pest activity, and overall plant vigor. Compost and compost tea play a central role in supporting [[mycorrhizal-fungi]] and moving colonization into healthy ranges.

## Colonization Thresholds and Their Meaning

| Range | Interpretation | Recommended Action |
|-------|---------------|-------------------|
| Below 10-15% | Critically low colonization | Apply mycorrhizal inoculum directly; add humic acids to the root zone to accelerate colonization |
| 15-40% | Moderate — below optimal | Add compost, compost tea, or humic acids to boost fungal foods and push colonization higher |
| Above 30-40% | Healthy colonization | Root disease problems, root-feeding nematodes, and other root pests begin to go away |
| Below 40% | Vulnerable zone | Root disease problems and root-feeding nematodes are consistently observed |

The key transition occurs around 30-40% colonization. Below this threshold, plants are susceptible to root diseases and root-feeding nematodes. Above it, these problems diminish significantly. The data comes from SFI's microscopy-based assessment of VAM (vesicular-arbuscular mycorrhizal) colonization in root samples.

When VAM colonization is less than 40%, root disease problems, root-feeding nematodes, and other root pests are consistently observed. Once VAM or ectomycorrhizal colonization gets above 30-40%, these root problems begin to go away. If colonization falls below 10-15%, a direct inoculum of mycorrhizal fungi is needed to jump-start the relationship.

## How Compost and Compost Tea Support Mycorrhizae

Compost and compost tea support mycorrhizal fungi through three primary mechanisms:

1. **Food resources**: Compost and tea serve as nutrient sources that VAM, ectomycorrhizal, ericoid, and other mycorrhizal types can pull from. This is especially important when the plant is not actively supplying the carbohydrates the fungi need. Mycorrhizal fungi can draw on compost and tea as alternative food resources during periods when plant exudates are insufficient.

2. **Moisture barrier**: Compost application keeps soil moist and provides an evaporation barrier, maintaining the moisture conditions mycorrhizal hyphae require to function. Hyphae are sensitive to desiccation; consistent moisture is essential for their nutrient-transport capacity.

3. **Macroaggregate building**: Compost provides building blocks that help mycorrhizal fungi construct macroaggregates — a critical component of soil structure that improves water infiltration, root penetration, and microbial habitat. Mycorrhizal fungal hyphae physically bind soil particles together, and compost supplies the organic matter that acts as a glue in these aggregates.

Humic acids, present in both compost and compost tea, function as fungal foods. When applied to the root zone alongside mycorrhizal inoculum, they significantly accelerate the rate and extent of colonization on root systems. Colonization becomes much more rapid and extensive when humic acids reach the root zone.

## Natural Growth Regulators

Root growth hormones are produced by the bacteria and fungi naturally present in a healthy root system. This is not a single-organism function — it emerges from the community:

- The correct set of bacteria and fungi in the root zone produce root growth hormones as part of their normal [[arbuscule-isolation-metabolic-activity-assays]]. Having the right community composition is essential.
- Any excess hormones produced are consumed by other organisms in the root system, creating a self-regulating feedback loop that prevents hormonal imbalances.
- Commercial root hormone products are fundamentally microbial in origin — they are cultured from the organisms that naturally perform this function. This is why biological inoculation can replace synthetic hormone applications.
- If using commercial hormone products, be aware that preservatives in those products can damage the soil biology. Compost tea applications can help repair this damage by replacing the organisms impacted by preservatives.

## Organism Diversity Principles

### Diversity as a Biomass Indicator

Higher total bacterial and total fungal biomass generally correlates with better organism diversity. While not a perfect relationship, biomass measurements serve as a practical proxy:

- Low biomass typically signals low diversity and increased vulnerability to disease outbreaks.
- Applying compost or compost tea delivers an inoculum of organisms that builds diversity over time. Each application adds to the community and can fill ecological gaps.
- Compost tea is particularly useful because it can deliver both organisms and food to plant surfaces — above ground and below ground, in soil, ponds, pools, lakes, and other environments.
- Disease outbreaks can often be predicted and detected by monitoring diversity and biomass levels in soil or compost tea samples.

### Single Species vs. Whole Community

Two approaches exist for biological inoculation:

**Whole-community inocula (preferred for general use)**:
- Well-made local compost supplies tens of thousands of species of bacteria, fungi, protozoa, and nematodes.
- Thermal, worm (vermicompost), and static compost can all harbor complete communities when correctly made under aerobic conditions.
- This approach provides [[functional-redundancy-fungal-communities]] — if one species fails to establish, others fill the ecological niche.
- Local compost is especially valuable because it supplies organisms already adapted to regional conditions.

**Single-species inocula (for targeted interventions)**:
- Useful when you know the precise organism needed and the specific condition you want to control.
- Requires matching the environmental isolate to your soil conditions (temperature, moisture, humidity must be right for it to grow).
- You should first confirm whether the beneficial organism is already present in your soil, compost, or tea before adding more.
- Best used when precise, targeted control is needed rather than general soil health improvement.
- SFI has planned assays to help growers assess whether specific beneficial organisms are already present.

## Compost vs. Compost Tea Tradeoffs

| Factor | Compost | Compost Tea |
|--------|---------|-------------|
| Food resources | More total food content | Less food per volume, but sufficient for foliar and soil application |
| Transport | Heavy, bulky, harder to move | Liquid, easier to transport and apply |
| Application cost | Higher (spreading equipment needed) | Lower (can be sprayed, injected, or applied through irrigation) |
| Coverage | Excellent for soil incorporation | Excellent for foliar coverage and hard-to-reach root zones |
| Feeding drip compatibility | Solid application | Ideal for summer feeding through drip irrigation |

Compost tea is especially valuable in summer when temperatures are warm; a feeding drip delivery system works well. In cool temperatures, the additional food in solid compost may be less critical because biological activity slows.

## Disease Control and Compost Tea Standards

Tea machines should demonstrate that they extract the right organisms for the intended purpose. A few testimonials are not adequate to claim a machine makes good tea. Key questions any manufacturer should answer:

- What organisms does the machine extract, and how reliably?
- How repeatable are results when the same conditions are used multiple times?
- How good does the compost quality need to be to produce tea with adequate bacterial coverage for disease prevention?

SFI has done testing showing what sets of organisms on leaf surfaces can compete with disease organisms, inhibit pathogens, and consume unwanted material. The goal is to establish [[actively-aerated-compost-tea]] standards using solid scientific approaches — if you demonstrate the biology is present, disease control follows.

## Chelation Biology

Chelation — the process of neutralizing positive charges on mineral ions — does not happen spontaneously in soil:

- **EDTA is not a natural soil compound**: Synthetic chelating agents like EDTA are not found naturally in soil. Chelation requires biological energy to produce the organic molecules that wrap around positively charged cations. There is no evidence that EDTA occurs naturally in soil to any significant degree.
- **Charge matters for uptake**: Positively charged ions get "stuck" at cell membranes. Materials need to be electrically neutral to enter and move through plant and microbial cells. This is why chelation is essential for [[aact-calcium-fungi-interaction-nutrient-bioavailability-ingham]].
- **Chelated minerals are safer**: Chelated minerals are less likely to leach from the root zone and less likely to cause toxicity or harm to plants compared to non-chelated nutrients. No regulator prevents applying chelated minerals, but tissue testing is recommended first.
- **Biological chelation is the norm**: In healthy soil biology, bacteria and fungi produce natural chelators (organic acids, siderophores) that make minerals bioavailable. This is one of the many reasons maintaining living soil biology is essential.

## Practical Management Based on Colonization %

1. **Test first**: Get VAM colonization assessed through root sample microscopy before applying interventions. Without baseline data, management is guesswork.
2. **Below 15%**: Direct [[mycorrhizal-inoculation]] is warranted. Pair with humic acids (fungal foods) for fastest colonization. Colonization will be much more rapid and extensive when humic acids reach the root zone.
3. **15-40%**: Focus on feeding existing mycorrhizae with compost, compost tea, or humic acid applications. The fungi are present but need support to reach protective thresholds.
4. **Above 40%**: Maintenance mode — continue practices that support the biology but prioritize other soil health factors.
5. **Disease pressure observed alongside low colonization**: The low colonization is likely a contributing factor. Address the mycorrhizal deficiency and the disease pressure should decrease.
