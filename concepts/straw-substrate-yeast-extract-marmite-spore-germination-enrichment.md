## The Role of Yeast Extract in Mycology

### What Is Yeast Extract?

Yeast extract is a concentrated product made by breaking down yeast cells through autolysis (self-digestion of intracellular enzymes). The resulting paste or powder contains:

- **Amino acids**: All 20 proteinogenic amino acids, providing a complete nitrogen source
- **B vitamins**: Particularly rich in thiamine (B1), riboflavin (B2), niacin (B3), pyridoxine (B6), and folate (B9)
- **Minerals**: Including zinc, magnesium, manganese, and iron
- **Nucleotides**: RNA and DNA breakdown products that serve as growth factors
- **Peptides**: Short protein fragments that are more bioavailable than intact proteins

### Why Marmite Specifically?

Jarrold specifically recommends Marmite, Vegemite, or generic yeast extract. These products are:

- **Widely available**: Sold as food spreads in supermarkets, making them accessible to home cultivators
- **Water-soluble**: Dissolve readily in boiling water for even distribution through the straw
- **Sterilization-stable**: The nutrients survive the pressure cooking sterilization step
- **Cost-effective**: A single heaped tablespoon is sufficient for six bags of substrate, making it extremely economical

### Spore Germination Enhancement

Yeast extract accelerates spore germination by providing nutrients that fungal spores need during their most vulnerable life stage. [[hyphal-branching-regulation-nutrient-sensing-gadd]]**: The spore detects available nutrients in the environment

4. **Germ tube emergence**: A hyphal tube emerges and begins growing
