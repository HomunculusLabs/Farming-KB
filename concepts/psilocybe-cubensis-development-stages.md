## Psilocybe cubensis Development Stages

Psilocybe cubensis (Earle) Singer undergoes a well-defined sequence of [[bloomfield-mushroom-formation-fruiting-body-development-cultivation]], following the formation of the primordium (knot of hyphae) on the substrate surface.

### Morphological Characteristics

- The pileus shows a slight, barely perceptible swelling at the apex of the primordium.
- The stipe is very short and indistinct from the developing cap.
- Overall height of primordia at this stage is typically less than 15 mm.
- The entire structure is pale, with no visible color differentiation between cap and stem.

### Morphological Characteristics

- The pileus opens into a hemispherical (dome-shaped) configuration.
- The gills (lamellae) are fully developed and exposed, with spores visible on their surfaces.
- The stipe has reached its maximum or near-maximum height.
- The partial veil typically ruptures during this stage, leaving an annulus (ring) on the stipe in species that possess one.

### Growth Behavior and Tropism

- At this stage, the negative [[spore-formation-geotropic-response-psilocybe-cubensis-badham-1982]] becomes dominant over anemotropism.
- The pileus turns upward from the direction of the wind, with the lamellae facing downward—the optimal orientation for spore release.
- This recurvation occurs when light is incident from above or below, but NOT in the absence of light (due to the light requirement for spore production).
- Bending is less pronounced if upright cultures are placed in a wind tunnel at Stage 2 or later, suggesting that the tropic responses established during early development set the trajectory for later growth.

### Spore Dispersal

- Active spore release begins at Stage 3.
- The downward-facing gills are positioned to maximize the distance spores fall before hitting the ground, improving wind dispersal efficiency.
- Spore production continues to increase through this stage.
