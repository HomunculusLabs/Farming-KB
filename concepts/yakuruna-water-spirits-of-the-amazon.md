## Yakuruna: Water Spirits of the Amazon

The yakuruna are aquatic [[amaringo-ayahuasca-visions-shipibo-iconography]] as humanoid figures, sometimes with green hair, who rest in serpent-hammocks among groves of golden trees. They are allies of the [[muraya]] (high-degree shamans) and serve as teachers, guides, and protectors.

## Types of Yakuruna

### Masha Yakuruna

An ally of the murayas who teaches them how to rescue people lost in the watery depths. The masha yakuruna smokes a pipe made from an encanto (magical stone) in the form of a shoe, with a stem made from the bone of the tuyuyo (Jabiru mycteria, the jabiru stork). This type of yakuruna is associated with rescue and safe passage through underwater realms.

### Taksha Yakuruna (Little Yakuruna)

Recognizable by green hair, the taksha yakuruna carries a boa. According to Amazonian legend, this yakuruna emerges to feed between midnight and dawn. If captured by a vegetalista, the taksha yakuruna must remain on the earthly plane and teach the shaman how to hunt in the waters.

### Yana-Sacraruna (Black Evil People)

A dark practitioner of black magic among the yakuruna. The yana-sacraruna's sole purpose is to kill, and it takes pleasure in causing riverbanks to collapse. This type lives in the deepest, darkest caves of the underwater world and uses a red conch as a fan to cast evil spells. The yana-sacraruna is an ally of sorcerers, not healers.

### Puka-Ninaruna (People of the Red Flame)

This yakuruna type lives in the largest of the underwater cities. It is invoked by murayas as an ally to control the anguila mama (electric eel), sea monsters, thunder, and storms. The puka-ninaruna represents the fire element within the water realm.

### Black Yakuruna

Allies of sorcerers, these dark yakuruna inhabit the deepest underwater regions alongside the pato-bufeos (duck-dolphins) and black mermaids. The Shetebo sorcerer works with the black yakurunas, the yana-sirenas (black mermaids), the black boa, and other dark beings.

## The Underwater World

The yakuruna inhabit a multi-layered underwater reality:

1. **First region**: Fair-haired mermaids, pink and black dolphins, white yakurunas
2. **Second region**: Red yakurunas, Hitrodos, blue and red mermaids
3. **Third region**: Black yakurunas, pato-bufeos, black mermaids

Beyond these regions lie realms of great splendor:
- Enchanted underwater forests of aquatic plants and seashells with pearls and aquamarines
- Cities of the huiracucha mermaids (white people), home to the most sublime and beautiful mermaids with great magical power
- The miraculous oasis of youth, with aromatic fragrances and a love-chant icaro
- The great bath of the sumis, with gilded crystal stairways and sapphire-blue waters
- Sumillakta, the beautiful city of the sumis, where great magical parapsychic arts are learned

## Yakuruna as Guardians

The muraya uses the [[yakumama]] (water mother serpent) to descend to the deepest parts of the river, where she transforms into a submarine. The Yakumama is distinct from the yakuruna -- she is the great serpent that guards the underwater cities. Yakurunas serve as her subjects and the muraya's guides within these domains.

## Relationship to the Muraya

The muraya's power is specifically associated with dominion over the water realms. Through their yakuruna allies, murayas can:
- Navigate to underwater cities
- Rescue people lost in the depths
- Control storms and sea monsters
- Access subaquatic treasures and knowledge
- Command the electric eel and other dangerous aquatic beings

The Yakuruna represent a fundamentally different aspect of Amazonian cosmology than the terrestrial and aerial [[metzner-spirit-beings-multiple-realities-entheogens]]. While beings like [[sachamama]] and [[huairamama]] are tied to specific physical locations, the Yakuruna inhabit an entire parallel dimension — the underwater world. This cosmological framework reflects the Amazon's own geography, where rivers and waterways are as central to life as the forest itself.

The aquatic realm is viewed as a source of primordial knowledge, accessible only to the most advanced practitioners. In ceremonies, the Yakuruna are sometimes perceived as bringing gifts of underwater plants, stones, or substances that possess unique healing properties not found in the terrestrial world.
