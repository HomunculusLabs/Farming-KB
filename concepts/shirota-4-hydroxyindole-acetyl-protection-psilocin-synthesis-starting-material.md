# 4-Hydroxyindole Starting Material and Acetyl Protection Strategy for Psilocin Synthesis

## Overview

The Shirota, Hakamata, and Goda (2003) synthesis of [[tryptamine]] structure.

## 4-Hydroxyindole as Starting Material

### Commercial Availability

4-Hydroxyindole (compound 3 in the Shirota scheme) is commercially available from chemical suppliers including Tokyo Kasei Kogyo Co., Ltd. (TCI), where it is sold in bottles containing more than 25 grams (>185 mmol). This commercial availability at kilogram-adjacent quantities makes it suitable for large-scale synthesis without requiring preliminary synthesis of the starting material itself.

### Structural Significance

4-Hydroxyindole provides the complete indole ring system with the critical 4-hydroxy substituent that is essential to the pharmacological activity of [[serotonin]], psilocin, and psilocybin, and the 4-hydroxy position corresponds to the 5-hydroxy position in serotonin (due to different numbering conventions between tryptamine and indole systems).

The choice of 4-hydroxyindole means that the synthesis must:
1. Protect the 4-hydroxy group to prevent interference with subsequent reactions
2. Introduce the ethylamine [[aminopropyl-silica-gel-chromatography-indole-alkaloid-purification]]/Celite pad.
4. **Yield**: 34 g of 4-acetylindole (compound 4) as ivory white crystals.

### Acetyl vs. Benzyl Protection

The Shirota synthesis specifically addresses the choice between acetyl and benzyl protection. Earlier approaches, including that of Nichols and Frescas, used benzyl ether protection of the 4-hydroxy group. Shirota et al. note that "protection by benzyl ether was also utilized; however, a separate step was needed for its deprotection." This requirement for an additional deprotection step reduces the overall efficiency of the synthesis.

The benzyl protection route also presented stability issues: "the 4-O-benzyl derivative of 3 was somewhat unstable, without careful control of the reaction conditions, and was not isolated in crystal form." The instability of the benzyl-protected intermediate necessitated careful reaction control and prevented crystallization-based purification.

In contrast, the acetyl protection strategy offers several advantages:

1. **Single-step deprotection**: The acetyl group is removed during the LiAlH4 reduction step that converts glyoxalylamide (6) to psilocin (1), eliminating the need for a separate deprotection step.
2. **Crystalline intermediates**: The acetyl-protected intermediates (4, 5, and 6) are all obtained as crystalline solids, enabling efficient purification by crystallization rather than chromatography.
3. **Stability**: The acetyl-protected intermediates are stable under the reaction conditions, eliminating the stability concerns associated with the benzyl-protected derivative.
4. **No chromatographic purification**: The combination of crystalline intermediates and efficient reactions allows the entire synthesis to proceed without any chromatographic purification step.

## Scale Considerations

### Commercial Scale Availability

The synthesis was designed for gram-scale production. The commercial availability of 4-hydroxyindole in quantities exceeding 25 grams per bottle means that the starting material limitation is not a bottleneck for laboratory-scale production of psilocin and psilocybin reference standards.

### Cost Effectiveness

4-Hydroxyindole is a relatively inexpensive starting material compared to more complex indole derivatives. The simplicity of the acetyl protection step (using inexpensive reagents — acetic anhydride and pyridine) further reduces the cost of the overall synthesis.

### Regulatory Context

Shirota et al. (2003) note that "since June 6, 2002, fungi containing [psilocin and psilocybin] have been regulated by the Narcotics and Psychotropic [[hplc-analysis-of-psilocybin-and-psilocin]]. The large-scale synthesis described was developed in part to meet this analytical need.
