# Forest Spirits and the Subaquatic World

## Overview

In Amazonian vegetalismo as documented by [[ayahuasca-preparation-pablo-amaringo-ucayali-shamanism]], the spirit world is organized into interconnected realms: the forest (land spirits), the subaquatic world (water spirits), and the sky. The forest is inhabited by a vast hierarchy of beings — from the colossal [[sachamama]] to the mischievous Chullachaki — while beneath the rivers lie golden cities inhabited by yakurunas (water people), mermaids, and extraterrestrial visitors. These realms are accessed through [[amaringo-ayahuasca-visions-shipibo-iconography]] and are central to the healing and protective practices of vegetalistas.

## Major Forest Spirits

### The Sachamama (Mother of the Forest)

The Sachamama is a colossal serpent that rarely moves, sometimes remaining in the same place for hundreds of years. Trees and weeds grow on its back, making it appear as part of the landscape. It is so large that people can accidentally climb on top of it without realizing they are on a dangerous animal.

**Hunting mechanism**: The Sachamama possesses a very powerful magnet. No living being that crosses its path can escape — it immediately magnetises and swallows anything passing by its head. It has a small pond from which it drinks, and beside it lie the bones of all kinds of animals and even humans that it has vomited during its lifetime. It expels meat through its rectum and vomits the bones.

**Movement and destruction**: When the Sachamama moves to another place, it throws down the trees on its back and knocks down other trees to create a path. It produces a strong wind with lightning and heavy rain capable of felling trees.

**Protective function**: Vegetalistas invoke the Sachamama for protection during healing sessions. A rainbow emanating from her mouth represents her power over the elements. When she moves, she produces strong winds, rain, and thunder.

**Related beings**: The yana-puma (black jaguar) and other animals are said to have the power to make people sleep in order to devour them, similar to the Sachamama's hypnotic capabilities.

### The Chullachaki (Forest Spirit)

The Chullachaki (from Quechua: chulla = one-sided, deformed; chaki = foot) is a forest spirit that presides over the Supay-chacra (devil's garden), a gathering place for all wild animals.

**The Supay-chacra**: In the middle stands a caimitillo tree (*Duroja hirsuta*), flanked by Supay-caspi and Supay-anallosacha trees with distinctive circles at the base of their leaves. The Chullachaki dresses in royal gowns and reads from a parchment — a decree on the care of wild animals. The shapshicos or sacharunas (forest people) listen to the reading, as they bear responsibility for the animals.

**The gathering**: The Chullachaki's garden hosts a Bacchanalian feast to which all wild animals are invited. Animals perform circus-like shows with dances, music with euphoric rhythm, and entrancing singing. The sounds and lullabies of the forest's purity and the intricate web of the vegetal esoteric world can be heard here.

**Animal summons**: The animals are called through telepathic vibration using the instinct of the "psychic cord." Animals that attend include otorongo (jaguar), manco-puma, lluhuichio-puma, monkey, squirrel, casha-cushillo (porcupine), sachavaca (tapir), deer, armas (*Priodontes maximus*), maids (*Agouti paca*), achuni (*Nasua nasua*), mantona (boa constrictor), shushupi (*Lachesis muta*), nacanaca, sparrow-hawk, maquisapa (*Ateles sp.*), paufil (curassow), partridge, owl, toucan, and trompetero (*Psophia leucoprocta*).

**Extraterrestrial connections**: In the upper left corner, two dazzling spaceships from the Pleiades make contact. In a pond, yana-huarmis (black women) sit atop ivory towers with nets to catch spaceships, wishing to take crew members to live in luxurious aquatic palaces. Another ship with a melodious [[icaro]] comes from the Kima constellation, emanating wisdom as heavenly light.

**Hidden knowledge**: The text suggests that within vegetalismo lies knowledge that could benefit humanity, including the hidden secret of longevity, believed to be found within the plants of the forest.

### The Huairamama (Mother of the Wind)

The Huairamama is a great snake that moves with a powerful wind. According to jungle legend, when this snake takes a bath, thunder is heard among the clouds but no water falls — a phenomenon called supay-cato (ghost bath). The Huairamama can be called by a healer during trance and arrives with a sudden great wind born from one moment to the next, without any visible clouds, passing like a whirlwind. It can be heard by both those taking the purge and those who have not.

**Physical description**: Its eyes shine with white lights and its mouth radiates violet-colored waves that make perceivers feel like giants capable of sensing anything approaching.

**Associated beings**: On the Huairamama ride sylphs who teach sidereal defense and esoteric sciences. One sylph cleverly corners enemies and delivers them to another sylph dressed in yellow who kills them. A princess instructs in esoteric sciences, and a king's goats (chusco-huacra), jumping and running, produce the wind that accompanies this snake. Four flying objects always accompany the sylphs as guardians.

**Healing function**: The Huairamama throws its rainbow to the jungle to give strength to the medicine a healer prepares for a patient bitten by a snake.

## Vision of the Snakes (Vision 17)

The snake vision presents an elaborate ecosystem of serpent beings and their associated healers and protectors:

### Key Serpent Beings

- **Huairamama**: Throws rainbow to strengthen medicine
- **Sachamama**: Throws rainbow charged with waves of light
- **[[yakumama]]**: Throws magnet upward; murayas use her to descend to the deepest river parts, where she transforms into a submarine
- **Purahua**: Also throws a rainbow from its mouth; the Yakumama that becomes a steamboat to walk on river and lake waters

### Healers and Princes

- **Abuelito Casiano**: A great tamer with wisdom in psychometric sciences
- **Mu Ni Ju**: A great Chinese guru from the family of the great Sakias, studying mystic subjects including sacred Sanskrit, astronomical knowledge, and sublime powers for difficult cures. Carries a magic healing stick and sceptre of cosmic occult conjurations.
- **Prince Harvadek**: Plays the harp, enrapturing listeners with persuasive and melodious ultrasound of esoteric songs with rhythmic modulations never heard in simple songs
- **Canessa**: The great tamer of snakes and jungle animals, accompanied by princesses Uzela and Vedina with magic nets

### Aquatic Guardians and Dangers

- **Yana-yaku-lobos** (black water wolves): Used by healers as guards of aquatic arkanas, moving very swiftly in water
- **Huancahui** (*Herpetotheres cachinnans*, laughing falcon): Its icaro stuns snakes and defeats them, neutralizing their bites. The huancahui-sacha plant serves as an emetic and purgative.
- **Quiruma-supay** (tree stump spirit): Emerges late at night in silent places; witch doctors kidnap people with the help of these aquatic genies, taking victims to the bottom of the river where black yakurunas live
- **Mermaids with two tails**: Emerge only in sublime trances to cure water sicknesses caused by the Yakumama, yaku-caballo, rayamama, dolphin, or anguila mama
- **Paiche machaco**: A creature like the paiche fish (*Arapaima gigas*) but also a snake with a poisonous bite, used in curing snake bites

### The Snakes' Icaro

All snakes respond to the singing of the snakes' icaro. When this icaro is performed, snakes gather under and around the house, making it dangerous to go outside. After curing, the healer must sing another icaro to make the snakes disperse. Failure to do so leaves the area permanently dangerous.

## The Muraya Entering the Subaquatic World (Vision 18)

### Journey to the Golden City

A muraya (a type of shaman specializing in aquatic realms) travels to the bottom of the river, received by two mermaids who serve as guides to a golden city beneath the waters. Guardian animals precede him: a tibemama (*Osprey, Pandion haliaetus*), a sarara (*Anhinga anhinga*), and a tibe negro (*Neotropic cormorant, Phalacrocorax olivaceus*).

### The City's Defenses

The city gate is guarded by two Yakumamas with magnetic power in their tongues, capable of capturing anyone who tries to enter by force. The town of witches is surrounded by a great serpent called purahua, whose tunnel-like mouth serves as the only entrance. The town has well-situated radars preventing entry from above. Arrivals must come by boat and pass through the serpent's mouth to be received by guardians.

### Extraterrestrial Airport

In the middle of the subaquatic city lies an airport for extraterrestrial spaceships from various planets:

- A ship from Jupiter descends to land
- A ship from Ganymede sits in the centre
- Ships from Venus (right), Saturn (left), and Mars (back)

### Princesses and the Distant City

Sukuacra-huarmi (princesses or nymphs) dance to the music of their [[icaros]], coming from a distant city in the midst of the river — a beautiful place full of aromatic perfumed air, where the most rapid ships (such as the supay-lancha) can be found.

### Royal Palace

A great royal palace above houses muses who dance pompously back and forth while musicians play lutes producing charming and emotional melodies. A queen and her court-ladies observe. This occurs on Callisto, a satellite of Jupiter. A monument honours Hashi, a great juggler. Nearby is the spirit of the papastrueno plant (*Dioscorea sp.*), an Inca called Yana-huya — a person who ingests this plant can summon rain or prevent it from falling.
