# Sourdough Bread Guide

Sourdough bread is made using a wild yeast and lactic acid bacteria culture (a sourdough starter) rather than commercial baker's yeast. The result is a bread with complex flavor, a chewy crumb, a crisp crust, and improved digestibility due to the pre-fermentation of gluten and phytic acid breakdown.

## Creating a Sourdough Starter

A sourdough starter is a symbiotic culture of wild yeast (Saccharomyces exiguus and others) and lactic acid bacteria (Lactobacillus species). It requires only flour and water, plus daily attention for 7-14 days.

### Day-by-Day Method

Use whole grain flour (rye or whole wheat) for the first few days -- wild yeast and LAB are more abundant on whole grain surfaces.

Day 1: Combine 50g whole grain flour and 50g non-chlorinated water in a glass jar. Stir until no dry flour remains. Cover loosely with a cloth or lid set ajar.

Day 2: You may see a few bubbles. Discard half the starter. Add 50g flour and 50g water. Stir.

Day 3-5: Repeat the discard-and-feed cycle. By day 3-4, the starter should show consistent bubbling and rise. A sour smell develops. If you see a layer of liquid (hootch) on top, the starter is hungry -- feed more frequently.

Day 5-7: Switch to bread flour (higher protein). Continue discard-and-feed at 50g/50g. The starter is ready when it doubles in size within 4-6 hours of feeding and has a pleasant sour aroma.

### Maintenance Feeding

Once established, feed the starter at a 1:1:1 ratio (equal weights starter, flour, water). A typical maintenance feed: 50g starter + 50g flour + 50g water. Feed daily if kept at room temperature, or weekly if refrigerated (with a feeding before baking).

## Flour Selection

- Bread flour (12-14% protein): Primary flour for sourdough. Strong gluten network.
- Whole wheat flour: Adds flavor, nutrients, and complexity. Use at 10-30% of total flour.
- Rye flour: Adds flavor, moisture, and helps fermentation. Use at 5-20%.
- All-purpose flour: Works but produces less open crumb than bread flour.
- Spelt, einkorn, emmer: Heritage grains can be used in part but have weaker gluten.

A typical blend: 80% bread flour, 20% whole wheat. Or 75% bread flour, 15% whole wheat, 10% rye.

## Hydration

Hydration is the ratio of water to flour in a recipe, expressed as a percentage (baker's percentage).

- 60-65% hydration: Firm dough, easier to handle, tighter crumb. Good for beginners.
- 70-75% hydration: Standard artisan range. Good balance of open crumb and workability.
- 80%+ hydration: High hydration, very open crumb, sticky dough. Requires advanced technique.

## Basic Sourdough Recipe

This yields one round boule loaf:

Levain (prepare 8-12 hours before mixing):
- 20g active starter
- 50g bread flour
- 50g water

Dough:
- 450g bread flour
- 50g whole wheat flour
- 350g water (78% hydration)
- 10g salt (2%)
- All of the levain

## Mixing and Bulk Fermentation

1. Mix flour and water until no dry spots remain (autolyse). Rest 30-60 minutes.
2. Add levain and salt. Squeeze and fold to incorporate.
3. Bulk ferment at 75-80 F for 4-6 hours.
4. During bulk ferment, perform stretch-and-folds every 30 minutes for the first 2 hours (4 sets total).
   - Wet hands, grab underside of dough, stretch up, and fold over itself. Rotate 90 degrees and repeat.
5. After folds, let dough rest undisturbed until it has grown 50-75% in volume and feels airy with visible bubbles.

## Shaping

1. Turn dough out onto a lightly floured surface.
2. Pre-shape into a loose round. Rest 20-30 minutes.
3. Final shape: Use a bench scraper to pull dough toward you, creating surface tension. Rotate and repeat until the ball is tight and smooth.
4. Place seam-side up in a floured banneton (proofing basket) or bowl lined with a floured towel.

## Cold Retard (Optional but Recommended)

Cover shaped dough and refrigerate for 12-24 hours. This slows fermentation, develops flavor, and makes scoring easier. The cold dough also holds its shape better during baking.

## Scoring

Scoring (cutting the dough before baking) controls where the bread expands. Use a razor blade or lame. Common patterns:

- Single slash down the center (ear): Classic
- Cross pattern: Four-way expansion
- Leaf pattern: Decorative
- Grid pattern: For square Dutch ovens

Score at a 30-45 degree angle, cutting about 0.25-0.5 inches deep. Cut quickly and confidently.

## Baking

Dutch oven method:
1. Preheat oven to 500 F with Dutch oven inside for 45-60 minutes.
2. Transfer dough to Dutch oven (score side up).
3. Cover and bake at 500 F for 20 minutes.
4. Remove lid, reduce to 450 F, bake 20-25 more minutes until deep golden brown.
5. Internal temperature should reach 200-210 F.
6. Cool on a wire rack for at least 1 hour before slicing.

## Troubleshooting

- Dense bread: Under-fermented. Extend bulk ferment or use warmer temperature.
- Flat, spreading loaf: Over-fermented. Reduce bulk ferment time or use cooler temperature.
- No sour flavor: Feed starter less frequently before baking or extend cold retard.
- Gummy interior: Under-baked. Check internal temperature. Extend baking time.
- Poor oven spring: Under-shaped dough, dull scoring blade, or insufficient steam.

## Equipment

- Kitchen scale (essential for accuracy)
- Glass jars for starter
- Banneton (proofing basket) or bowl with towel
- Dutch oven or covered baking vessel
- Bench scraper
- Lame or razor blade for scoring
- Oven thermometer
