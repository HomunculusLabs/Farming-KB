# Organic Greenhouse Pest Management

Greenhouse pests can build up rapidly because the warmth and humidity necessary for healthy plant growth are also ideal for [[query-how-to-manage-pests-and-diseases-in-mushroom-cultivation]]. Vigilance, scrupulous cleanliness, and biological controls are the organic gardener's primary weapons.

## Prevention Through Cleanliness

The most important defense is keeping the greenhouse spotlessly clean:

- Remove any plant debris where pests and diseases may hide
- At the end of the season, wash the house thoroughly with warm, soapy water, brushing into every corner
- If you empty the house for winter, leave it open for frost to help sterilize it
- Always keep the glass clean -- dirty panes reduce light and weaken plants
- Before reusing pots or seed trays, wash thoroughly in boiling water to kill disease spores and insect eggs
- [[query-fabric-pots-vs-plastic-pots-vs-air-pots-for-cannabis]] and trays are much easier to clean than clay (which is porous and harbors disease)
- Remove any damaged plant material as soon as you see it

## Common Greenhouse Pests and Organic Controls

### Aphids (Greenfly/Blackfly)
Small green or black flies that weaken plants by sucking sap, transmit virus diseases, and excrete sticky honeydew that attracts sooty mold.

**Controls:**
- Plant French marigolds to attract hoverflies (aphid predators)
- Introduce parasitic wasp Aphidius colemani
- Spray with pyrethrum or insecticidal soap in severe cases (3 times at 6-day intervals; last spray at least 1 day before introducing biological controls)
- Rub stems with fingers to squash early infestations

### Whiteflies
Serious and persistent pests, these small white flies weaken plants by sucking sap and are often found on leaf undersides.

**Controls:**
- Introduce parasitic wasp Encarsia formosa (highly effective)
- Hang grease-coated yellow cards (whiteflies are attracted to yellow)
- As last resort, spray with insecticidal soap at 5-day intervals (do not spray if Encarsia has been introduced)
- Ensure no garden debris is left for overwintering

### Red Spider Mites
Invisible to the naked eye, these tiny pale green or red mites cover plants with webs and cause fine mottling on leaves.

**Controls:**
- They thrive in dry atmospheres -- increase humidity through damping down
- Introduce predatory mite Phytoseiulus persimilis
- For small infestations, spray with insecticidal soap (3 times at 6-day intervals; last spray at least 1 day before introducing Phytoseiulus)

### Scale Insects
Small, disclike insects that cling to leaves and stems, sucking sap and secreting honeydew. Plants weaken and turn yellow.

**Controls:**
- Introduce [[fukuoka-natural-predator-protection]] Metaphycus helvolus
- Scrape insects off with a piece of wood

### Vine Weevils
Small grubs with white bodies and brown heads invade [[hamilton-potting-compost-recipes-and-growing-media]] and eat roots. Severely affected plants may keel over.

**Controls:**
- Use biological control with nematodes (apply to compost)
- Knock off all compost from affected plants and repot, ensuring all larvae are removed

### Thrips
Tiny insects that cause silvering and distortion of leaves and flowers.

**Controls:**
- Blue sticky traps
- Introduce [[comparison-predatory-mites-vs-ladybugs-biocontrol]]
- Increase humidity

## Common Greenhouse Diseases

### Botrytis (Gray Mold)
Dirty white or gray-brown mold on leaves and stems. Thrives in low temperatures and poor ventilation.

**Controls:**
- Increase temperature and ventilation
- Remove and burn infected material immediately
- Improve air circulation

### Mildew
White, powdery coating on leaves causing puckering and distortion. More likely when soil is dry.

**Controls:**
- Remove affected leaves immediately
