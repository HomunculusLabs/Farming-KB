# Grain-to-Grain Transfer

Grain-to-grain transfer is one of the most efficient methods of [[mushroom-grain-spawn-preparation]] in [[outdoor-mushroom-cultivation]]. Described in detail in The Mushroom Cultivator, this technique enables exponential expansion of [[mycelium-network-intelligence]] mass, transforming a single agar culture into thousands of colonized grain jars through successive generations of transfer.

## The Transfer Process

The grain-to-grain technique follows a specific protocol:

1. A petri dish two-thirds covered with mycelium is used to inoculate 6-8 quart jars of grain via the "double wedge" transfer method, where a single triangular wedge of mycelium is cut in half and both pieces inserted into a grain jar. Loosening lids prior to inoculation facilitates speedy transfers.

2. Once fully colonized, these become "master cultures" (G-1). Masters must be transferred within a few days of full colonization; otherwise the myceliated kernels do not break apart easily.

3. Each master jar is carefully scrutinized for signs of contamination: heavy growth, regions of sparse growth, slimy or wet kernels (bacteria indication), exploded kernels, unusual colorations, or sour "rotten apple" odors. Suspect jars are discarded.

4. Masters are shaken against a tire or palm to break grain into individual kernels. Each jar can amply inoculate ten to twelve quart jars or seven to nine half-gallon jars.

5. A critical waiting period of 12-24 hours follows shaking. The jars are set aside because some spawn may not recover, usually from bacterial contamination. Had these jars been used immediately, contamination would have been multiplied by a factor of ten.

6. After inspection confirms renewed growth, one-tenth of each master's contents is poured by rolling the wrist into receiving jars (G-2), which are then shaken and incubated.

## Exponential Expansion

The grain-to-grain technique enables geometric expansion of mycelial mass:

- 1 agar culture inoculates 10 jars (G-1)

- G-1 jars inoculate 100 jars (G-2)

- G-2 jars inoculate 1000 jars (G-3)

It is critical that the first set of spawn masters be absolutely pure, as they may ultimately inoculate as many as 1,000 jars. Inoculations beyond the third generation are not recommended. If contamination exceeds 10% at the second generation, G-2 should be considered a terminal stage.

Each generation risks accumulating hidden contaminants or senescence (genetic aging of the mycelium). The third generation should ideally be used directly to inoculate [[mushroom-cultivation-bulk-substrates]] rather than being expanded further. Experienced cultivators sometimes skip G-3 entirely and move from G-2 directly to bulk substrates or fruiting blocks to maintain strain vigor.

## Species-Specific Timelines

Different mushroom species colonize grain at very different rates, which affects how quickly each generation can be expanded:

- Fast colonizers (Psilocybe cubensis and other tropical species): 7-10 days per generation

- Moderate colonizers (oyster species): 10-14 days per generation

- Slow colonizers (Agaricus brunnescens and other temperate species): up to 2.5 weeks per generation

For slower-growing species, a common shaking schedule is on the 5th and 9th days after inoculation to redistribute mycelium and speed colonization. Fast colonizers may only need a single shake on day 4 or 5.

## Grain Types and Preparation

The choice of grain substrate affects colonization speed, spawn quality, and contamination resistance:

- **Rye grain** -- the gold standard for most species. Kernels are uniform in size, hold moisture well, and provide excellent nutrition

- **Millet** -- very small kernels provide thousands of inoculation points per jar, making it ideal for spawning to bulk substrates

- **Wild bird seed** -- a cost-effective mix of millet, milo, and sunflower seeds. The oil content of sunflower seeds can sometimes promote contamination

- **Brown rice** -- works well but kernels tend to clump together, making shaking difficult

- **Wheat berries** -- widely available and inexpensive, though slightly less consistent than rye

Proper grain preparation is essential regardless of grain type. Grains must be hydrated to the correct moisture content (typically achieved by simmering or soaking followed by a brief boil), then drained and loaded into jars before sterilization. See [[mushroom-grain-spawn-preparation]] for detailed grain preparation protocols.

## Contamination Prevention

Grain-to-grain transfer inherently multiplies any contamination present in the master jar, making [[contamination-identification-prevention]] absolutely critical at every stage:

- Work in a still-air box or under a laminar flow hood whenever possible

- Flame-sterilize all tools (scalpels, spoons, jar rims) between every transfer

- Wear gloves and a face mask to minimize airborne contaminants

- Keep the work area clean and free of drafts

- Inspect master jars thoroughly before committing to expansion

- Never transfer from a jar that shows even slight signs of contamination

The 12-24 hour waiting period after shaking masters is one of the most important quality control steps. Jars contaminated with bacteria often fail to recover after shaking, revealing themselves before they can contaminate dozens of receiving jars.

## Comparison to Other Methods

Grain-to-grain transfer is preferred by most commercial spawn laboratories. For creating large quantities of spawn, it is far superior to agar-to-grain for both ease and speed. However, every cultivator must ultimately return to [[agar-culture-techniques-detailed]] in order to maintain the purity of the strain.

The [[mushroom-liquid-culture]] technique offers an alternative approach, using suspended mycelial fragments in sterile nutrient broth to inoculate up to 100 jars from a single plate, more than ten times the number inoculated by traditional wedge transfer. Liquid culture also allows for more precise dosing and faster colonization since mycelium is already in a growth state when it contacts the grain.

## Alternative Spawn Media

Some species do not grow well on grain and are better suited to alternative spawn media. [[shiitake-mushroom-cultivation]] and [[growing-gourmet-enoki-cultivation]] ([[enoki-mushroom-cultivation]]) prefer sawdust/bran spawn (4 parts hardwood sawdust to 1 part rice or wheat bran). Perlite spawn (40g perlite, 50g wheat bran, 6g gypsum, 1.5g calcium carbonate per 120ml water) is another option, sterilized at 15 psi for one hour.

Sawdust spawn is generally preferred for [[shiitake-mushroom-cultivation]] because shiitake mycelium naturally colonizes wood substrates more aggressively than grain. The sawdust spawn also provides a smoother transition when inoculating logs or supplemented sawdust blocks, reducing the competitive disadvantage the mycelium faces during the spawn run.
