# KNF Livestock Feeding Natural

updated: 2026-18

Korean Natural Farming (KNF) emphasizes homemade livestock feed that is carefully balanced according to the [[knf-nutritive-cycle-theory]]. Rather than relying on commercial feed mixes that contain chemicals, antibiotics, and imported ingredients, KNF farmers prepare feed from locally available materials. The feeding protocol is designed to develop strong digestive systems, promote natural health, and reduce costs.

## The Nutritive Cycle Theory for Livestock

Dr. Cho's Nutritive Cycle Theory states that plants and livestock need different nutrients during different stages of growth. Like humans, animals also need different kinds of food at different stages. Just as a baby can only eat baby food, young animals can only be fed with nutrients appropriate for their age. The Nutritive Cycle guides farmers in obtaining the best results from their livestock and avoiding disease through proper nutrient management.

The growth stages follow the same pattern observed in plants:

- **Vegetative growth stage (youth):** Animals consume carbohydrates (C) and convert them to nitrogen (N), their main growth requirement. Protein-rich inputs are emphasized.
- **Cross-over period (puberty/maturity):** Animals need "sour nutrients" in the form of phosphoric compounds (P) for reproductive development.
- **Reproductive growth stage (adulthood):** Animals need calcium (Ca) and potassium (K) for production (eggs, milk, meat) and proper development of offspring.

## KNF Poultry Feeding Protocol

NF emphasizes the use of homemade chicken feed. More importantly, the nutrient content of the feed given is carefully balanced. Feed is given normally once a day, 2 hours before sunset.

### Initial Feeding: Brown Rice and Bamboo Leaves

Newly hatched chicks are fed **whole brown rice grains** in unlimited quantity instead of enriched commercial feed. This is a radical departure from conventional practice, which provides soft powdered feed to chicks with "fragile stomachs."

After three days for layers and one day for broilers, **bamboo leaves** are added. On Day 50, rice husk is added. Gradually, the proportion of rice husk is increased until it consists of 20 to 25 percent of the total feed at 6 months, when the egg-laying rate reaches 60 percent.

This approach toughens the intestines and makes the chicken healthy. Giving soft and over-nutritious feed prevents chicks from developing strong digestive organs, leading to the excretion of undigested nutrition, which causes smell and disease.

### Homemade Feed Composition

Any material except mineral matter can be given as feed:

- **Fresh green grass** makes up approximately one-third of the total feed for adult chickens
- Feed can be found on the housing floors too -- along with soil rich in microorganisms, the fermented products of chicken feces can make up 7 to 10 percent of total feed
- **Rice husks** can be used at 15 to 20 percent of feed from late February to mid-May to hold down the laying rate
- To maintain the laying rate at 65 to 70 percent for three years, rice husks can make up as much as 25 percent of total feed

### KNF Supplements for Livestock

KNF natural farming inputs are regularly added to livestock feed and water:

- **[[knf-oriental-herbal-nutrient]]:** Provides herbal medicine benefits, boosts immunity, and prevents disease
- **[[knf-fish-amino-acid]]:** Provides protein and amino acids, especially important during growth stages
- **[[lactic-acid-bacteria-knf]]:** Added to drinking water to maintain gut health, prevent diarrhea, and improve digestion
- **[[water-soluble-calcium-wca]]:** Essential for bone development and eggshell formation; given to pregnant or breeding animals
- **[[knf-water-soluble-calcium-phosphate]]:** Used as drinking water (1:500 dilution) for livestock that are pregnant or ovulating
- **[[knf-seawater-and-fermented-seawater]]:** Provides trace minerals; diluted 1:30 with water
- **[[knf-brown-rice-vinegar]]:** Aids calcium absorption, supports liver function, prevents disease; added to water at specified dilutions

### Feeding by Growth Stage

The feeding protocol adjusts based on the animal's growth stage:

**Early stage (vegetative growth):** Emphasis on nitrogen-rich inputs. [[master-cho-knf-ffj-fermented-fruit-juice-guide]] and [[master-cho-knf-ffj-fermented-fruit-juice-guide]] provide sugars and growth-promoting compounds. FAA provides protein. OHN strengthens the immune system.

**Cross-over period:** Phosphorus-rich inputs become important. [[knf-water-soluble-phosphoric-acid-wspa]] and WS-CaPo support reproductive development.

**Reproductive/mature stage:** Calcium and potassium are emphasized. WS-Ca strengthens bones and eggshells. [[knf-water-soluble-potassium-detailed]] supports overall production and quality.

## General Livestock Principles

The KNF approach to livestock feeding extends beyond poultry to all animals:

- Feed should be appropriate to the animal's age and physiological state
- Over-nutrition is considered as harmful as under-nutrition -- Dr. Cho states that providing nutrients in excess will only bring disease
- The right material at the right amount and at the right stage is the guiding principle
- Locally available materials should be used rather than expensive commercial feeds
- The microbial health of the animal's gut is maintained through LAB and other biological inputs
- Animals should have access to soil contact for natural mineral supplementation and microbial exposure

## Cost Benefits

The KNF feeding approach dramatically reduces feed costs because:

- Most ingredients are produced on-farm or gathered locally
- No expensive commercial feed mixes are needed
- Waste products (fermented feces from the floor) supplement feed
- Green forage replaces purchased protein supplements
- Animals are healthier and require fewer veterinary interventions
- Feed conversion is improved because digestive systems are stronger
