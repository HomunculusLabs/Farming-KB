## King Stropharia Cultivation

King stropharia (Stropharia rugoso-annulata), also known as the wine cap or
garden giant, is one of the top three [[holzer-mushroom-cultivation-outdoor-beds]].
Its sweet-smelling mycelium and resilience make it ideal for gardens,
community plots, and [[yellow-metabolite-excretion-mycelial-stress-indicator-grain-spawn]]
on overcolonized substrates with a characteristically sweet smell. The cap is
tan to dark burgundy, centered on the stem, with smaller buttons being darker
and more firm. As mushrooms mature, pigments spread and the cap lightens to
reddish brown. The spore print is dark purple-black. Wild strains are easily
collected and cloned or propagated through [[growing-gourmet-primordia-formation-pinning]], and more abundant fruitings result from careful
observation of watering schedules. Reduce watering as mushrooms mature since
larger specimens rot quickly, with the cap liquefying if kept too wet. Harvest
at mid-button stage as the cap separates from the stem for firm, bug-free
mushrooms. The taste resembles potatoes cooked in a light wine marinade, with
stems reminiscent of steamed asparagus.

## Outdoor Wood Chip Bed Cultivation

Build beds by layering cardboard, hardwood chips, and spawn to a depth of at
least 6 to 8 inches. The deeper the mulch, the larger the mushrooms will grow.
Use fresh chips cut no more than one month before inoculation to ensure the
mushroom outcompetes wild fungi. Cover beds with mesh or cardboard to prevent
wildlife from destroying them; turkeys, squirrels, and other animals are
attracted to the sweet mycelium smell and earthworm masses. When colonization
is complete (typically three to six months), dress beds with 2 to 3 inches of
microbial casing soil.

Water beds daily for the first week, every other day for weeks two through four,
then once monthly thereafter unless rainfall is sufficient. Wood chip beds
fruit for one to two years and can be extended by adding more chips at the end
of the year. However, worms often infiltrate beds and consume mycelium in the
second year, so refurbish beds completely if no visible mycelium is detected.
Never add fresh chips when mushrooms are about to fruit or it will delay the
flush as mycelium redirects energy to colonizing new substrate.

Step-by-step: build a frame from 6 to 8 inch diameter hardwood logs (which can
also be inoculated with log-loving species). Cover the area with saturated
cardboard, sprinkle [[comparison-grain-spawn-vs-sawdust-spawn]]
indoors. Expect two rapid flushes several weeks apart with average yields.

Recycled nursery pots work well for indoor king stropharia. Fill pots tightly
with spawned wheat straw, leaving 2 inches at top for casing soil. Once
colonized, separate pots, apply pasteurized potting soil as casing, and mist
to keep moist. The mycelium drives upward into the soil, creating a dense mat
just beneath the surface where primordia form. Growing indoors in containers
offers predictable yields and sizes for market purposes.

## Spawn Expansion and Storage

King stropharia is one of the easiest wild mushrooms to isolate on cardboard.
Wrap stem bases, full of thick mycelium and beneficial microbes from soil and
wood chips, in wet cardboard and place in a ziplock bag in the refrigerator.
The stem bases revert to mycelial state, quickly colonizing the cardboard.
