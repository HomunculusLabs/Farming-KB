# Agar-to-Grain Wedge Transfer Technique

The agar-to-grain transfer is the critical bridge between [[contamination-identification-prevention]] for contamination awareness.

## Standard Single Wedge Transfer

A flame-sterilized scalpel is used to cut a triangular wedge of mycelium-covered agar from the petri dish. With careful, deliberate movements, the wedge is transferred to an awaiting grain jar, exposing the grain for a minimal amount of time. For each transfer, the scalpel is reflame-sterilized. The more mycelia transferred, the faster colonization proceeds and the lower the contamination risk.

## Double Wedge Transfer Method

Stamets recommends a "double wedge" technique for superior results. A single triangular wedge of mycelium is cut from the agar plate, then cut in half. Both pieces are speared on the scalpel simultaneously and inserted into the grain jar. Jars inoculated with this double wedge method grow out far faster than those receiving a single wedge, because two inoculation points establish growth from separate positions within the grain mass.
