# Lentinan Clinical Research

Lentinan is a purified β-(1→3)-D-glucan with β-(1→6) branches extracted from the fruiting bodies and mycelium of [[mushroom-training-shiitake-cultivation]] (shiitake mushroom). It is one of the most extensively studied mushroom-derived immunomodulators and has been approved as an adjuvant cancer therapy in Japan since 1985.

## Discovery and Development

Lentinan was first isolated and characterized by Chihara and colleagues at the National Cancer Center Research Institute (Tokyo) in the late 1960s. The compound is produced commercially by Ajinomoto Co. (Japan) under the trade name Lentinan. It was approved for clinical use by the Japanese Ministry of Health and Welfare for treatment of gastric cancer.

## Chemical Properties

- **Type**: β-(1→3)-D-glucan with β-(1→6) branching branches every 3-5 glucose units
- **Molecular weight**: ~400-800 kDa
- **Structure**: triple helix in solution (essential for biological activity)
- **Solubility**: water-soluble; insoluble in ethanol, methanol, acetone
- **Source**: extracted from both fruitbodies and submerged cultured mycelium of shiitake

## Mechanism of Action

Lentinan does not directly attack cancer cells. Instead, it produces antitumour effects through host immune potentiation:

- **Macrophage activation** — enhances phagocytosis, ROS production, and tumoricidal activity
- **T-cell restoration** — restores depressed T-cell function in cancer patients
- **NK cell enhancement** — increases natural killer cell cytotoxicity
- **Cytokine induction** — stimulates IL-1, IL-2, TNF-α, IFN-γ production
- **Complement activation** — activates the alternative complement pathway via C3
- **Langerhans cell infiltration** — increases antigen-presenting cell infiltration into tumours

## Clinical Applications

### Gastric Cancer

The primary approved indication. Multiple clinical trials have demonstrated:
- Prolonged survival when combined with chemotherapy (tegafur, mitomycin C, 5-FU)
- Improved immune parameters (increased CD4+/CD8+ ratios)
- Enhanced quality of life measures
- Reduced postoperative recurrence

### Other Cancers

Clinical studies have shown benefit in:
- Colorectal cancer — improved survival with combination therapy
- Hepatocellular carcinoma — tumour marker reduction
- Pancreatic cancer — limited evidence of benefit
- Cervical cancer — increased Langerhans cell infiltration

## Administration

- **Route**: intravenous injection (primary); subcutaneous possible
- **Dosage**: typically 1-2 mg per administration, 1-2 times weekly
- **Duration**: maintenance therapy over months to years
- **Combination**: always used with conventional chemotherapy, not as monotherapy

## Immune Activities in Humans

| Activity | Animal Studies | Human Studies |
|----------|---------------|---------------|
| Macrophage activation | Strong | Confirmed |
| NK cell enhancement | Strong | Confirmed |
| T-cell restoration | Strong | Confirmed (in immunocompromised) |
| Cytokine induction | Strong | Confirmed (IL-2, IFN-γ) |
| Complement activation | Strong | Confirmed |
| Direct cytotoxicity | Not observed | Not observed |

## Limitations

- **No direct cytotoxicity** — relies entirely on host immune function; ineffective in severely immunocompromised patients
- **IV administration required** — limits outpatient use; oral bioavailability is poor for the intact high-MW polymer
- **Cost** — production and quality control are expensive
- **Variable trial quality** — many studies lack proper blinding or randomization
- **Geographic availability** — approved in Japan but not in US or EU as a pharmaceutical
