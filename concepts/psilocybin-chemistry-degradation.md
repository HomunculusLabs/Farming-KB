# Psilocybin Chemistry and Degradation in Fungal Specimens

## Overview

The psychoactive properties of neurotropic fungi derive primarily from **tryptamine
alkaloids** — indole compounds structurally related to serotonin (5-HT). The
principal active compounds are **psilocybin**, **psilocin**, **baeocystin**, and
**norbaeocystin**. Understanding their chemistry, distribution, and degradation
is critical for both [[challenges-neurotropic-fungi-chemical-analysis-taxonomic-identification]] and understanding indigenous use
practices.

## Principal Compounds

### Psilocybin
Chemical name: **4-phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine (4-PO-DMT)**. This is the
phosphate ester of psilocin. Psilocybin is more stable in air and is water-soluble,
making it the predominant form in fresh fungal tissue. It serves as a prodrug —
the body dephosphorylates it to produce the active compound psilocin.

### Psilocin
Chemical name: **4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine (4-HO-DMT)**. This is the
pharmacologically active metabolite. Psilocin acts as a **5-HT2A post-synaptic
agonist or partial agonist** in the serotonin system. It is less stable than
psilocybin and oxidizes more readily, contributing to the characteristic blue
bruising reaction in active species.

### Baeocystin and Norbaeocystin
These are N-desmethyl tryptamine analogs — baeocystin is 4-PO-NMT and
norbaeocystin is 4-OH-NMT. They are present in many psilocybin-containing fungi
but are considered **less potent** than [[ingham-tannins-terpenes-phenolic-compounds]]) by enzymes
present in the fungal tissue. When cell walls are ruptured:

1. Psilocybin is enzymatically dephosphorylated to psilocin
2. Psilocin is oxidized by polyphenol oxidases
3. The oxidized products form blue-colored quinone-like compounds

Not all bluing species are psychoactive, and at least one psychoactive species
(*P. atrobrunnea*) reportedly does not blue. However, bluing remains the single
most reliable visual indicator in the field.

## Degradation Over Time

One of the most important findings in the chemical study of neurotropic fungi
is that **active compounds degrade significantly with specimen age**. This has
profound implications for both chemical analysis and traditional use.

### Evidence from Chemical Analysis

Repke et al. (1977) demonstrated that baeocystin varies according to specimen
age. In *P. baeocystis* and *P. cyanescens*, no trace of indoles was detected
in specimens analyzed 20–60 days after collection.

Ohenoja et al. (1987) quantified psilocybin loss in [[fungal-btex-volatile-compound-degradation]].

Guzmán (personal observation, 1958) confirmed this volatility experientially:
while sleeping in a small room filled with freshly collected and semifermented
specimens of multiple *Psilocybe* species (*P. caerulescens*, *P. cordispora*,
*P. cubensis*, *P. mexicana*, *P. zapotecorum*), he experienced colored
hallucinations from airborne exposure alone. The effect ceased immediately upon
leaving the room, suggesting volatile indole compounds in the air.
