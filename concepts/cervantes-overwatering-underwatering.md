# Overwatering and Underwatering Cannabis

Proper watering is one of the most important skills in
[[query-how-does-no-till-cannabis-cultivation-work]]. Jorge Cervantes identifies
overwatering as one of the most common mistakes made by
both novice and experienced growers. Understanding when
and how much to water is essential for healthy root
development and vigorous plant growth.

See also: [[cervantes-growing-medium]]

## When to Water

Cervantes provides clear guidelines for irrigation
timing:

- **Irrigate soil and soilless mixes** when they are
  dry one-half inch (1.5 cm) below the surface.
- **Small plants** with small root systems in small
  containers must be watered frequently, as soon as
  the soil surface dries out.
- **Larger plants** in vegetative and flowering stages
  should also be irrigated when soil is dry one-half
  inch below the surface.
- **Flowering marijuana** uses high levels of water
  for rapid floral formation. Withholding water
  stunts flower development.

As long as drainage is good, it is difficult to
overwater fast-growing cannabis. Four-week-old clones
flowering in 2 to 3 gallon containers typically need
irrigation once or twice daily.

## Watering Technique

Cervantes recommends these best practices for effective
watering:

- **Use plenty of water** and allow up to 10 percent
  runoff during each watering to prevent fertilizer
  buildup in the soil.
- **Water early in the day** so excess water
  evaporates from the soil surface and foliage.
  Leaving foliage and soil wet overnight invites
  fungal attack.
- **Use a water wand with a breaker head** that mixes
  air with irrigation water and splits it into
  multiple gentle streams, preventing soil erosion
  and compaction.
- **Learn to judge by weight:** After developing skill,
  check how heavy pots are simply by tipping them.
  Light pots need water; heavy pots do not.
