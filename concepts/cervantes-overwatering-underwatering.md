# Overwatering and Underwatering Cannabis

Proper watering is one of the most important skills in
cannabis cultivation. Jorge Cervantes identifies
overwatering as one of the most common mistakes made by
both novice and experienced growers. Understanding when
and how much to water is essential for healthy root
development and vigorous plant growth.

See also: [[cervantes-growing-medium]]

## When to Water

Cervantes provides clear guidelines for irrigation
timing:

- **Irrigate soil and soilless mixes** when they are
  dry one-half inch (1.5 cm) below the surface.
- **Small plants** with small root systems in small
  containers must be watered frequently, as soon as
  the soil surface dries out.
- **Larger plants** in vegetative and flowering stages
  should also be irrigated when soil is dry one-half
  inch below the surface.
- **Flowering marijuana** uses high levels of water
  for rapid floral formation. Withholding water
  stunts flower development.

As long as drainage is good, it is difficult to
overwater fast-growing cannabis. Four-week-old clones
flowering in 2 to 3 gallon containers typically need
irrigation once or twice daily.

## Overwatering

Overwatering is a common problem, especially with small
plants. Too much water drowns the roots by cutting off
their supply of oxygen. Without adequate oxygen in the
root zone, roots cannot absorb nutrients properly and
may begin to rot.

### Signs of Overwatering

- Leaves curled downward and yellowed
- Waterlogged, soggy soil
- Fungal growth on the soil surface
- Slow growth rate
- Drooping foliage that does not recover
- Algae growth on medium surface
- Root rot in severe cases

Symptoms of overwatering are often subtle, and
inexperienced gardeners may not see blatant symptoms
for a long time. One of the main causes of
overwatering is poor air ventilation. Plants need to
transpire water into the air. If there is nowhere for
humid air to go, moisture is locked in the grow room.

### Solutions for Overwatering

- **Buy a moisture meter** to take the guesswork out
  of irrigating. Meters cost less than $30 and
  measure exactly how much water the soil contains at
  any level.
- **Cultivate the soil surface** gently to allow water
  to penetrate evenly and prevent dry pockets where
  water runs down the gap between pot and soil.
- **Improve drainage** by adding perlite, pumice, or
  other amendments to the growing medium.
- **Increase ventilation** to promote transpiration
  and carry away humid air.
- **Remove excess water** from catch trays using a
  turkey baster, large syringe, or sponge.

## Underwatering

Underwatering is less common but still occurs,
particularly when small 1 to 2 gallon pots are used and
the grower underestimates the water needs of rapidly
growing plants. Small containers dry out quickly and
may require daily watering.

### Signs of Underwatering

- Wilting, especially during the hottest part of the
  day
- Bone-dry soil, even in pockets below the surface
- Crispy, dry leaf edges and tips
- Stunted growth
- Light, easy-to-lift containers

### Solutions for Underwatering

When root hairs dry out, they die. It seems to take
forever for roots to grow new root hairs and resume
rapid growth after drought stress. To prevent this:

- **Water consistently** before plants show wilt
- **Add a few drops of biodegradable liquid soap**
  (one drop per pint) to irrigation water as a
  wetting agent to help water penetrate soil evenly
  and prevent dry pockets
- **Water in stages:** Apply about one-quarter to
  one-half as much water as the plant needs, wait 10
  to 15 minutes for it to soak in, then apply more
  until the soil is evenly moist
- **Use larger containers** that hold more moisture
- **Apply mulch** on the soil surface to reduce
  evaporation

## Watering Technique

Cervantes recommends these best practices for effective
watering:

- **Use plenty of water** and allow up to 10 percent
  runoff during each watering to prevent fertilizer
  buildup in the soil.
- **Water early in the day** so excess water
  evaporates from the soil surface and foliage.
  Leaving foliage and soil wet overnight invites
  fungal attack.
- **Use a water wand with a breaker head** that mixes
  air with irrigation water and splits it into
  multiple gentle streams, preventing soil erosion
  and compaction.

  check how heavy pots are simply by tipping them.
