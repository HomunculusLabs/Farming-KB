# Chinese Tallow Tree (Sapium sebiferum)

updated: 2026-04-18

The Chinese tallow tree, also known as the popcorn tree or vegetable tallow tree, is a deciduous tree native to China and Southeast Asia. It is primarily valued for the high-quality vegetable tallow and oil produced from its seeds, and for its ornamental qualities. It is a fast-growing, multi-purpose tree for subtropical and warm temperate permaculture systems.

## Botany and Growth Habit

A fast-growing deciduous tree reaching 10-15 m. It is notable for its attractive autumn foliage, which turns vivid shades of orange, red, and purple, making it one of the most colourful autumn trees in warm climates. The tree has a rounded crown and smooth bark.

## Climate and Soil

The Chinese tallow tree is adaptable to a wide range of conditions. It tolerates both waterlogged and drought-prone soils, though it prefers moist, well-drained sites. It is frost tolerant once established, making it suitable for warm temperate as well as subtropical climates. It grows well on poor soils and disturbed sites, acting as a pioneer species.

**Important note**: The Chinese tallow tree is considered an invasive species in some regions, particularly the southeastern United States and parts of Australia. Before planting, check local regulations and assess the risk of uncontrolled spread. In permaculture systems where active management prevents seed dispersal, the invasiveness risk can be managed.

## Seed and Oil Production

The seeds are the primary economic product. Each seed contains both a solid vegetable tallow coating and a liquid oil in the kernel:

- **Outer tallow**: A white, waxy solid used historically for candle and soap making. The tallow is of high quality, producing clean-burning candles with a pleasant aroma
- **Kernel oil**: A drying oil similar to linseed oil, used in paints, varnishes, and industrial applications
- **Seed yield**: A mature tree can produce substantial quantities of seed

The combination of two different products from a single seed is unusual and gives the tree economic versatility. The tallow can be used as a biofuel, and the seed cake remaining after oil extraction serves as fertiliser or animal feed.
