# Sterilizing Grow Systems and Mediums

Sterilizing used [[cervantes-growing-mediums-comparison]] and hydroponic systems between crops
is essential for preventing [[hamilton-pest-and-disease-control-organic]] carryover. [[jorge-cervantes]]
covers sterilization in detail in Chapter 12 of *Marijuana
Horticulture* (page 313).

## Why Sterilize

To reuse a [[cervantes-green-algae-growing-medium]], it must be sterilized to remove destructive
[[query-how-to-manage-pests-and-diseases-in-mushroom-cultivation]]. Sterilizing is less expensive and often easier than
replacing the growing medium entirely.

Once sterilized, the medium is free of harmful microorganisms including
bacteria and fungi, plus pests and their eggs.

## Which Mediums Can Be Sterilized

Sterilizing works best on **rigid [[cannabis-growing-mediums]]** that do not lose
their shape:
- Gravel
- Expanded clay (Hydroton)
- Mica
- Lava rock
- Sand

### Mediums to Avoid Reusing

Avoid sterilizing and reusing substrates that compact and lose
structure:
- Rockwool
- Coconut coir
- [[query-what-is-the-difference-between-coco-coir-and-peat-moss]]
- Perlite
- Vermiculite

These substrates develop problems with compaction and decaying root
matter. Once used indoors, reuse rockwool and biodegradable coco in
the outdoor garden instead.

## Root Removal Process

Before sterilizing, remove all roots from the growing medium:

1. **Manually remove** the mat of roots entwined near the bottom of
   the bed. Shake loose any attached growing medium. It may be easier
   to add more medium than to pick it from between matted roots.
2. **Screen method:** Pour growing mediums such as expanded clay and
   gravel through a screen placed over a large bucket. Most roots
   will stay on the screen.
3. **Air dry:** Lay growing medium out on the floor and train an
   oscillating fan on it to dry out remaining roots.

A three-to-four-month-old marijuana plant has a root mass about the
size of an old desk telephone. Fewer decaying roots cause fewer pest
and disease problems, and decrease incidence of clogged feeder tubes.

## Chemical Sterilization Method

1. **Wash** substrate with plenty of fresh water to remove built-up
   salts and dead roots. Use a large container such as a barrel or
   bathtub; lighter substrates like expanded clay float while roots
   can be skimmed off.
2. **Soak** in a 5% laundry bleach (calcium or sodium hypochlorite)
   solution for at least one hour. Alternatively, mix hydrochloric
   acid (the kind used in hot tubs and swimming pools).
3. **Drain** or pump off the sterilant.
4. **Flush** with plenty of fresh water. A bathtub with shower nozzle
   is ideal. Fill the tub with fresh water and drain several times to
   remove all residual sterilants.
