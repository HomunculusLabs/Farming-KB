# Water-Soluble Calcium Phosphate (WCP)

**Updated:** 2026-04-18

Water-Soluble Calcium Phosphate (WCP) is a [[natural-farming-knf-overview]] input that provides both calcium and phosphorus in immediately bioavailable forms. Made by extracting minerals from animal bones using [[knf-brown-rice-vinegar]] (BRV) as a solvent, WCP supports root development, flowering, fruit set, and reproductive growth. Phosphorus is critical for energy transfer (ATP), root establishment, and reproductive development in plants.

## Why Calcium Phosphate Matters

Phosphorus is one of the three primary macronutrients (N-P-K) and plays essential roles:

- **Root development:** Phosphorus is fundamental for root growth and establishment, especially during early growth stages
- **Energy transfer:** Phosphorus is a component of ATP (adenosine triphosphate), the energy currency of all living cells
- **Flowering and fruiting:** Essential for flower formation, pollen viability, seed development, and fruit maturation
- **Cell division:** Required for DNA and RNA synthesis, which drives cell division and tissue growth
- **Calcium role:** The calcium component strengthens cell walls and supports nutrient transport to reproductive organs

Combined calcium and phosphate are especially important during the crossover period when plants transition from vegetative to reproductive growth.

## Preparation from Animal Bones

### Materials

- Animal bones (beef, pork, chicken, or fish bones)
- [[knf-brown-rice-vinegar]] (BRV)
- A fire source (wood fire, charcoal grill, or gas burner)
- A metal container or metal sheet for burning
- Tongs or a shovel
- A glass or clay container for extraction
- Water

### Step 1: Cleaning the Bones

1. Collect bones from kitchen waste or slaughter byproducts
2. Remove all meat, fat, and connective tissue from the bones
3. Rinse the bones thoroughly with clean water
4. Boil the bones for 30-60 minutes to remove any remaining organic material in the marrow
5. Scrape the bones clean after boiling
6. Dry the cleaned bones completely in the sun or a well-ventilated area

### Step 2: Charring (Low-Temperature Burning)

This is the most critical step. The bones must be charred at low temperature to remove organic material while preserving the mineral structure.

1. Place the dried bones on a metal sheet or in a metal container
2. Burn at LOW temperature -- the goal is slow carbonization, not complete combustion
3. The bones should turn black (charcoal-like) but should NOT turn white or gray
4. Burning at too high a temperature converts the calcium phosphate to calcium oxide (quicklime), which is caustic and harmful to plants
5. The proper result is bone charcoal -- black, lightweight, and crumbly
6. Allow the charred bones to cool completely

**Why Low Temperature Matters:**
- Low-temperature charring (approximately 300-500 degrees C) removes organic matter while converting calcium compounds to forms that are soluble in BRV
- High-temperature combustion (above 800 degrees C) converts calcium phosphate to tricalcium phosphate or calcium oxide, which are insoluble or caustic
- The charred bone retains a porous structure that allows BRV to penetrate and extract minerals efficiently

### Step 3: Extraction with BRV

1. Break the charred bones into small pieces (2-3 cm)
2. Place the bone charcoal in a glass or clay container
3. Pour BRV over the bones -- use enough BRV to submerge the bones completely with extra liquid above (approximately 2:1 ratio of BRV to bone volume)
4. Cover loosely with cloth or paper
5. Store in a cool, shaded place at 23-25 degrees C
6. Allow extraction to proceed for 7-10 days

### Monitoring the Extraction

- Bubbles will appear as the acetic acid reacts with the bone minerals
- The bone pieces will gradually become lighter and more porous
- The liquid will develop a slightly cloudy or amber appearance

### Step 4: Completion

1. When the bone pieces are fluffy and floating, extraction is complete
2. Strain the liquid through cloth or a fine mesh
3. The resulting liquid is Water-Soluble Calcium Phosphate (WCP)

## Dilution and Application Rates

### Standard Dilution
- Dilute WCP at 1:500 to 1:1000 (1-2 ml per liter of water)

### Stage-Specific Application

- **Early growth/germination:** Apply at 1:1000 to support initial root development
- **Vegetative stage:** Apply at 1:1000 as a supplement to nitrogen inputs
- **Crossover/transition stage (vegetative to reproductive):** Apply at 1:500 to 1:1000 to support the transition to flowering. This is the most critical timing for WCP application.
- **Flowering/fruiting stage:** Apply at 1:500 to support flower development and fruit set
- **Late fruiting/harvest:** Apply at 1:500 to improve fruit quality and maturation

### Livestock Application

- Dilute WCP at 1:500 and provide as drinking water supplement
- Especially important for pregnant and lactating animals
- Supports bone development in young livestock
- Can be combined with [[knf-water-soluble-calcium]] for comprehensive mineral supplementation

## Application Guidelines

- Apply as a foliar spray 2-3 hours before sunset
- WCP is most effective during the crossover period when plants shift from vegetative to reproductive growth
- Can be tank-mixed with [[knf-oriental-herbal-nutrient]], [[knf-fish-amino-acid]], and [[knf-water-soluble-calcium]]
- For soil drench, dilute at 1:1000 and apply to the root zone
- Apply every 7-14 days during critical growth stages

## Storage

- Store in glass or clay containers at 1-15 degrees C
- Keep in a dark location, away from direct sunlight
- Airtight seal for long-term storage
- Shelf life: 1-2 years when properly stored

## Quality Indicators

- **Color:** Amber to dark amber
- **Aroma:** Mild vinegar smell with mineral/earthy notes
- **Taste:** Sour with a distinct mineral quality
- **Failure signs:** Putrid smell (incomplete bone cleaning or charring); white or gray bone residue (burned at too high temperature)

## WCA vs WCP: When to Use Which

| Property | Water-Soluble Calcium (WCA) | Water-Soluble Calcium Phosphate (WCP) |
|----------|---------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| Source | Eggshells, oyster shells | Animal bones |
| Primary nutrient | Calcium only | Calcium + Phosphorus |
| Main use | Growth regulation, fruit firmness | Root development, flowering, fruit set |
| Key timing | Fruiting stage, harvest | Crossover/transition stage |
| Dilution | 1:500 to 1:1000 | 1:500 to 1:1000 |

Both inputs can be used together in a tank mix when both calcium regulation and phosphorus supplementation are needed, such as during the transition from vegetative to reproductive growth.

## Relationship to KNF Nutritive Cycle

In the [[knf-nutritive-cycle-theory]], WCP occupies a critical position during the plant's transition from vegetative to reproductive growth. While [[knf-fish-amino-acid]] and [[knf-fermented-fruit-juice]] drive vegetative growth through nitrogen supply, and [[knf-water-soluble-calcium]] regulates excessive growth, WCP provides the phosphorus needed for the plant to shift energy into reproductive development. This balanced approach prevents both overgrowth (too much nitrogen) and stunted reproductive development (insufficient phosphorus).
