## Rebuilding Household Storages of Energy

One permaculture strategy is to encourage household and local economic self-reliance. This rebuilds traditional forms of energy storage in food, fuel and other resources that complement the landscape storages of energy. At Melliodora, Holmgren identifies these traditional storages:

- **The seedbox:** Order and diversity reflect efforts to save, exchange and purchase the potential for future abundance
- **The cellar full of produce:** Captures the essence and substance of a bountiful season, provides insurance against a bad one
- **The stack of firewood:** Green wood added at one end, two-year seasoned wood used from the other — represents both natural abundance and honest work maturing through weather and sunshine

These household energy storages are by their nature:

- **Diverse:** Multiple types of food, fuel, and resources
- **Small:** Human-scale and manageable
- **Dispersed:** Distributed across many households rather than concentrated
- **Easily used:** Accessible without complex infrastructure
- **Unattractive to thieves:** Not rich or portable enough to attract those seeking to monopolise wealth

A nation full of such wealth is much more secure and stable than one dependent on the high-throughput, centralised systems of the supermarket and electricity grid.

## The Nuclear Family Problem

Holmgren identifies a critical structural problem: the nuclear family is too small in scale for many aspects of ecological living. The dwelling and the human household are analogous to the nucleus of the cell, providing control, management, and information for reproduction. But in the Western world, households are too small to be efficient in food production and preparation, as well as other functions.

The more traditional extended family household of between five and fifteen members was large enough to efficiently support many functions of permaculture self-reliance:

- Food production, processing, and preservation at scale
- Child care and education
- Building maintenance and construction
- Animal husbandry
- Water and energy management
- Skill sharing and knowledge transmission

Despite the challenges in recreating community, the expanding interest in eco-villages and co-housing as part of the permaculture vision is implicit recognition of this problem.

## Systems Under Stress: The Household as Last Resort

Holmgren's analysis of disaster response reveals a profound truth about the household economy. In social unrest and famine, most social and economic functions break down while the family and the household manage to persist as the fundamental units of society. Experience in African famines suggests that, at the extreme, family relationships break down, but the last to fail is that between mother and child.

This dynamic illustrates a more general principle: very powerful systems can insulate themselves against small disasters, but this leads to lack of fitness for large disasters. In nature, small stresses keep systems fit and adapted. Modern society has lost or actively dispensed with most of its informal fallback strategies. The defence forces are a costly and formal remnant of what were once deep and broad structures of back-up protection at every level of society.

## Reuse and the Self-Reliant Household

Free or cheap industrially produced containers, which are rubbish for the average consumer household, are a bonus for those involved in self-reliant living. This abundance can be seen as a subsidy to the minority who adopt the lifestyle of energy descent.

Examples of container reuse at Melliodora:

- Seal-top bottles for hot preserves and pickles
- Old-style beer bottles for making beer and juice
- Egg cartons for egg storage and sale
- Cardboard and polystyrene boxes for fruit storage
- Film containers for seed storage
- Milk cartons as plant pots and tree guards
- 200-litre drums for grain storage and liquid manure
