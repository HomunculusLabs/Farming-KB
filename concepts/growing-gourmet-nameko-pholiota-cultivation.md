---# Nameko Mushroom Cultivation (Pholiota nameko)

The Genus Pholiota is not known for its abundance of deliciously
edible species. Many species are characterized by a glutinous, slimy
veil coating the surface of the cap. However, the Japanese discovered
that Pholiota nameko is a superior gourmet mushroom. It is one of the
most popular [[growing-gourmet-enoki-cultivation]].

## Taxonomy and Common Names

- Nameko or Namerako (Japanese for "Slimy Mushroom")
- Slime Pholiota, Viscid Mushroom
- Synonymous with Pholiota glutinosa Kawamura
- Formerly placed in Collybia and Kuehneromyces
- Cap 3-8 cm, hemispheric to convex, surface covered with
  orangish glutinous slime that thickly encapsulates the mushroom
- Gills white to yellowish, becoming brown with maturity
- Spores cinnamon brown, ellipsoid, small, 4-7 x 2.5-3.0 microns
- Unique: produces a conidial stage allowing single-spore
  homokaryotic mycelium to generate mushrooms directly

## Distribution and Habitat

Common in the cool temperate highlands of China, Taiwan, and
throughout the islands of northern Japan. Not known from Europe or
North America. Grows on broad-leaf hardwood stumps and logs in
temperate forests, especially deciduous oaks and beech (Fagus
crenata).

## Mycelial Characteristics

Whitish, longitudinally radial, becoming light orangish or tawny
from the center as the mycelium ages. On sterilized grain the
mycelium is densely cottony white and becomes speckled with
yellowish to orangish zones at maturity. Fragrance is musty and
farinaceous, not pleasant.

## Outdoor Log Cultivation

On logs of broad-leaf hardwoods, especially beech, poplar and
assorted deciduous oaks, using methods parallel to Shiitake
cultivation. Because of the high moisture requirement, partially
burying logs in a high peat moss soil base is recommended.

## Indoor Sawdust Cultivation

### Spawn Production
Spawn is quickly generated with liquid inoculation of grain from
petri dish cultures. Grain spawn can be expanded two generations
using standard grain-to-grain inoculations. Intermediate sawdust
spawn can then be produced for final inoculation into supplemented
hardwood sawdust.

### Substrate Recommendations

### Fruiting Without Casing
Should a casing layer not be applied, the block must be exposed to a
"condensing-fog" environment during the [[bloomfield-mushroom-formation-fruiting-body-development-cultivation]].

### Harvesting
Rather than removing the entire bag, cut off the top portion, leaving
3-4 inch side walls of plastic. These walls collect moisture and
enhance primordia formation. The mushroom stems elongate to exactly
the height of these walls, facilitating harvest.
