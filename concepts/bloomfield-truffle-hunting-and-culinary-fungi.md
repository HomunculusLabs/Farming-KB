## Truffle Hunting and Culinary Fungi

The human fascination with edible fungi spans millennia, from ancient mushroom
foragers to modern commercial truffle hunters who comb Italian forests with
specially trained dogs. [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]].

People "who would never venture into the woods for any other reason will get
up before dawn to collect morels in the pouring rain." Morels are not abundant
every year but appear according to long-term cycles that are often
misrepresented in local folklore. An elderly Ohio woodsman, drawing on eighty
years of experience, assured Money that every three years brought forth a
magnificent harvest. Four years later, Money was still waiting.

The reality is biological: mycelia are exhausted by a year of exuberant fruiting
and demand time to restore their biomass. The odds of encountering a bumper
crop rise with the passage of an increasing number of barren seasons. Money
observes that many who profess love for morels are "greatly prejudiced by the
cream sauces in which they are simmered, rather than their scalloped heads and
pale gray flesh."

Morel fruiting bodies are ascomycetes -- stalked like mushrooms, the head is an
enormous cup covered with asci that line the gouges in its surface. The asci
shoot their spores in a roughly horizontal direction, continually creating a
spore cloud a few millimeters from the surface, which is dissipated by wind
gusts.
