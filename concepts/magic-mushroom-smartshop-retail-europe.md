# Magic Mushroom Smartshop Retail in Europe

The commercial retail of [[forensic-identification-methods-psilocybin-mushrooms-tlc-gc-hplc]] occupied a legal
  grey area. While psilocybin itself was scheduled under the
  Opium Act, fresh mushrooms were not explicitly listed. Dried
  mushrooms were treated as a preparation and were illegal.
- **Licensing**: Smartshops operated as regular retail businesses
  with local government permits. They were subject to standard
  business regulations but not pharmaceutical or drug-specific
  controls.
- **Product range**: Shops typically stocked 10-20 mushroom
  varieties, sold by weight (fresh, in sealed containers). Prices
  ranged from approximately €7-15 per 10-15g portion.
- **Staff guidance**: Shop employees provided dosage advice and
  information about effects. Some shops distributed printed guides.
- **Customer base**: Primarily tourists, with domestic users
  constituting a smaller segment. Young adults (18-25) were the
  largest demographic.

use across EU countries based on general population surveys:

| Ireland | 4.2% |
| Germany | 1.9-3.2% |
| France | 1.0-1.7% |
| Belgium | 0.6-1.5% |

Prevalence was highest among young adults (15-24), with the UK
consistently showing the highest rates in Europe.

## Online Market Expansion

As internet commerce grew, the mushroom market expanded beyond
physical smartshops:

- **Online retailers**: Websites based in the Netherlands and
  other countries began shipping mushroom grow kits, spore
  syringes, and dried material. Jurisdictional ambiguity made
  enforcement difficult.
- **Grow kits**: Pre-sterilized substrate jars and grow kits
  became a major product category. These contained legal materials
  (substrate, spores) but enabled [[sclerotia-maturation-timeline-psilocybe-tampanensis-mexicana]]* and
  related species) remained legal for several more years before
  also being restricted

## Patterns of Use

Survey data from the EMCDDA revealed consumption patterns:

- Most users consumed mushrooms infrequently (1-5 times per year)
- Group settings were the most common context (parties, nature,
  festivals)
- The UK [[emcdda-clubbing-survey-prevalence-data-polydrug-use-patterns-mushrooms]] found 5.2% of clubbers used mushrooms
  in the past month
