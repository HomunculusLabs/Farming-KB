### The Five-Kingdoms Principle

The ZERI approach to waste conversion models natural ecosystem function. In healthy ecosystems, the waste of one kingdom of life (bacteria, protists, plants, animals, fungi) becomes a nutrient or energy source for species of another kingdom. Resources never become exhausted and the ecosystem never pollutes itself -- a model of true sustainability. See [[fungal-lignin-degradation-enzymology]] for detailed coverage.

## Lignocellulosic Waste Substrates

### Straw and Crop Residues
Wheat and rice straw are used for mushroom cultivation (*Pleurotus* spp., *Agaricus bisporus*, *Lentinula edodes*). *Pleurotus* spp. require no composting or casing layer and can decompose lignocellulose efficiently without pretreatment. Biological delignification of straw by white-rot fungi improves digestibility for ruminant feed.

### Sugar Cane Bagasse
Contains 30–35% hemicelluloses extractable with dilute acids. Used for single-cell protein (*Candida blankii*), xylitol production (*Candida guilliermondii*), and xylanase production (*A. tamarii*). Maximum theoretical xylitol yield from D-xylose is 0.917 g/g.

### Coffee Pulp
Annual production of ~2.76 × 10⁶ tons worldwide. Contains carbohydrates, proteins, minerals, tannins, polyphenols, and caffeine. *A. niger* under SSF improves total amino acid content and reduces cell wall constituents. Five *Aspergillus* strains and two *Penicillium* strains degrade nearly 100% of caffeine.

### Sugar Beet Pulp
~10⁷ tonnes produced annually in the EU. Comprises cellulose, hemicellulose, pectic substances, and lignin. White-rot fungi improve nutritional upgrading for animal feed. Low lignin content enhances suitability for biotransformation.

### Corn Cobs
Used for cellulase-free xylanase production (*T. lanuginosus*, *A. tamarii*) and enzymic conversion to glucose (*T. viride*). *Pleurotus ostreatus* grown on corn cobs increased dietary fiber content up to 78% and significantly prevented colon cancer development in rats.

### Okara (Soybean Residue)
~700,000 tons produced annually in Japan. Fermented by *Rhizopus oligosporus* and *A. oryzae* to improve nutritional qualities as high-fiber, low-energy foodstuff. Also used for citric acid production (*A. niger*, *A. terreus*).

### Apple, Tomato Pomace, and Other Substrates

- **Apple pomace**: Used for ethanol and citric acid production via SSF; *Thamnidium elegans* produces γ-linoleic acid as high-value fungal oil
- **Tomato pomace**: *Geotrichum candidum* under SSF increases protein content and digestibility; saccharification yields glucose, fructose, xylose, and cellobiose
- **Canola meal**: *Trichoderma reesei* produces xylanase, acetyl-xylan esterase, and xylosidase at yields similar to or better than with refined substrates

## The Role of Fungi in Composting

When wastes cannot serve as pure culture substrates, composting is the treatment of choice. The three phases of composting are:

1. **Mesophilic phase** (5–37°C): Dominated by bacteria; *Geotrichum* spp. predominant among fungi
2. **Thermophilic phase** (45–70°C): Thermophilic fungi (*Talaromyces emersonii*, *Thermoascus aurantiacus*, *T. lanuginosus*, *Chaetomium thermophilium*) degrade lignocellulose; laccase purified from compost during this phase shows high catalytic activity toward phenolic substrates
3. **Cooling phase**: Mesophilic and thermotolerant fungi reappear; *Aspergillus* spp., *Panaeolus* spp., *Corticium coronilla* isolated from mature compost

Laccase activity drives the polymerization of aromatic compounds during humification. Compost amendments suppress soilborne plant pathogens through antagonistic microorganism populations, including *Trichoderma hamatum* and *Gliocladium virens*.
