# Cannabis for Epilepsy and Seizure Disorders

Epilepsy is a neurological disorder characterized by recurrent, unprovoked
seizures resulting from excessive synchronous neuronal activity in the brain.
Status epilepticus (SE) is a life-threatening emergency defined as continuous
seizure activity lasting longer than five minutes, or multiple seizures
without regaining consciousness between
episodes.

## Mechanisms of Cannabinoid Anticonvulsant Action
The anticonvulsant mechanisms of cannabinoids involve multiple pathways within
the endocannabinoid system and beyond. CBD acts on several molecular targets
that are relevant to seizure control: it enhances signaling at the 5-HT1A
serotonin receptor, which has anticonvulsant properties; it acts as a positive
allosteric modulator of the GABA-A receptor, enhancing the inhibitory
neurotransmission that counteracts the excessive excitation underlying
seizures; and it antagonizes GPR55, an orphan receptor whose overactivation
may promote epileptogenesis. Additionally, CBD inhibits the reuptake of
adenosine, a neuromodulator with anticonvulsant effects, and activates the
transient receptor potential vanilloid type 1 (TRPV1) channel, which modulates
neuronal
excitability.

THC's anticonvulsant effects are mediated primarily through CB1
receptor
activation. When THC binds to CB1 receptors on presynaptic neurons, it reduces
calcium influx and inhibits the release of excitatory neurotransmitters such
as glutamate. This presynaptic inhibition reduces overall neuronal
excitability and can raise the seizure threshold. However, at higher doses,
THC can be proconvulsant, particularly in certain seizure types, which is why
CBD -- which does not bind CB1 receptors directly and lacks psychoactivity --
has become the primary cannabinoid of interest for epilepsy
treatment.
## Epidiolex and Modern Clinical Development
The most significant development in cannabis-based epilepsy treatment has been
the FDA approval of Epidiolex (cannabidiol) in June 2018. Epidiolex is a
purified, plant-derived CBD oral solution approved for the treatment of
seizures associated with Lennox-Gastaut syndrome and Dravet syndrome, two
severe, treatment-resistant forms of childhood epilepsy. The approval was
based on three randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials involving a
total of 516 patients. In these trials, patients receiving Epidiolex
experienced a median reduction in seizure frequency of 37-44 percent compared
to 17-22 percent in the placebo
group.

Dravet syndrome is a rare genetic epilepsy caused by mutations in the
SCN1A
gene, affecting voltage-gated sodium channels in neurons. It typically begins
in the first year of life and is characterized by frequent, prolonged seizures
that are resistant to conventional medications. Lennox-Gastaut syndrome is
another severe epilepsy that typically emerges between ages 3-5, featuring
multiple seizure types including drop attacks (atonic seizures). The fact that
CBD showed statistically significant efficacy in these hardest-to-treat
populations has generated considerable scientific interest in understanding
its mechanisms more
fully.
## Dosing Considerations and Pharmacokinetics
CBD dosing for epilepsy has evolved considerably as clinical experience has
grown. In the Epidiolex trials, patients received doses of 10 mg/kg/day or 20
mg/kg/day, with the higher dose generally producing greater seizure reduction
but also more side effects. The most common [[blesching-cannabis-safety-adverse-effects-evidence]] included
somnolence, decreased appetite, diarrhea, elevated liver enzymes, and fatigue.
Notably, CBD interacts with several antiepileptic drugs through the cytochrome
P450 enzyme system, particularly CYP3A4 and CYP2C19, which can increase blood
levels of drugs like clobazam, valproate, and
stiripentol.
