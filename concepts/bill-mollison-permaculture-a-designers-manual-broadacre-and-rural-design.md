## Rotational Grazing Systems

Mollison strongly advocated for rotational grazing as an alternative to
continuous set-stocking. In rotational systems, livestock are moved frequently
through a series of paddocks, allowing each paddock an extended rest period
for recovery. High [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-water-harvesting-and-earthworks]], are applied at farm scale through
keyline farm planning. The farm is divided into land units based on keyline
contours, and tree planting, road placement, and fencing all follow keyline
patterns. This ensures that water is distributed from ridges into valleys,
that tree belts follow contour for maximum water capture, and that farm
infrastructure works with the natural shape of the land rather than against
it. Keyline plowing of pastures and croplands fractures compacted subsoil
layers, improving root penetration and water infiltration without disturbing
the valuable topsoil.

## Contour Strip Cropping

On sloping land, Mollison recommended contour strip cropping where different
crops are planted in alternating strips along the contour. Perennial strips
such as grasses or legumes alternate with annual crop strips, creating
barriers that slow runoff, reduce erosion, and trap sediment. The perennial
strips also provide habitat for [[cannabis-seed-banks-and-genetics]] through the
preservation of locally adapted tree provenances.

## Water Infrastructure on Farms

Farm-scale water infrastructure includes dams, reticulation systems, and
irrigation distribution. Mollison recommended designing water systems to use
gravity wherever possible, with storage located high on the property and
distribution flowing downhill to points of use. Multiple water sources
including dams, bores, and roof catchment provide redundancy. Irrigation
should be designed for efficiency, using drip systems, micro-sprinklers, or
flood irrigation from contour channels rather than wasteful overhead
sprinklers. Farm dams should be designed as multi-purpose systems combining
