# Psilocybin-Bearing Mushroom Species — Identification, Habitat, and Cultivation

## Overview

Approximately **109 species** across four genera are known to contain psilocybin and/or psilocin: 40 species of *Conocybe*, 20 of *Panaeolus*, 40 of *Psilocybe*, and 9 of *Stropharia*. These species have a cosmopolitan range from the Arctic to the tropics, found on soil, dung, rotting wood, moss, and various organic substrates. The most commonly cultivated species is *[[psilocybe-cubensis]]*, valued for its ease of cultivation, disease resistance, and potency. This guide summarizes key species data and mycelial [[cannabis-sativa-indoor-cultivation-techniques]].

## Key Species

### *Psilocybe cubensis* var. *cyanescens* (San Ysidro)
- **Most easily obtainable, most readily cultivated, most disease-resistant, and psychoactively strongest** species.
- Found February–November on cow/horse dung, pasture soil, straw, sawdust/dung mixtures.
- Range: Mexico, Cuba, Florida, other southern states.
- Optimum growth on [[malt-extract-agar]] (MEA): 86°F.
- Carpophores appear in 4–8 weeks. Thermal death at 104°F.
- Produces larger carpophores in cultivation than in the wild.
- Does poorly in darkness; requires some light.

### *Psilocybe semilanceata* (Liberty Cap)
- Found August–September in large groups on soil, among grasses, in pastures, meadows, forest edges.
- **Never on dung** — a key distinguishing feature.
- Range: New York, northern USA, British Columbia, Europe.
- Generally regarded as one of the less potent species, but occasionally quite potent.

### *Psilocybe cyanescens*
- Found in autumn, scattered/grouped/clustered in woods, on earth, among leaves and twigs, occasionally on decomposing wood.
- Range: Northwestern USA.
- Notable for its blueing reaction upon bruising.

### *Psilocybe mexicana*
- Found May–October at 4500–5500 feet altitude, especially in limestone regions, among mosses and herbs.
- Range: Mexico (roadsides, humid meadows, cornfields, near pine forests).
- The species from which Albert Hofmann first isolated psilocybin (1958).

### *Psilocybe baeocystis*
- Found autumn/winter on earth, lawns, mulch, decomposing forest wood near conifers.
- Range: Western Oregon and Washington.
- Contains **psilocybin, psilocin, baeocystin, and nor-baeocystin** — the most visually hallucinogenic of the psilocybin mushrooms, possibly due to baeocystin alkaloids.
- A 1960 report of a child fatality after consuming large quantities; proceed with caution.
- Optimum growth on agar: 77°F.

### *Psilocybe caerulescens*
- Mexican variety *P. caerulescens* var. *mazatecorum* — known locally as "Durrumbe" ("landslides").
- Found among landslides, near corn or coffee plantations.
- Optimum on MEA: 81°F. Thermal death: 95°F.
- Almost impossible to produce carpophores on sterilized rye.
- Long incubation period (55–85 days) makes it prone to contamination.

## Mycelial Cultivation

### The Mycelium

The mycelium is the fibrous [[teaming-with-microbes-fungi-the-underground-network]] of the mushroom. It contains the same active compounds ([[hplc-analysis-of-psilocybin-and-psilocin]]) as the carpophores (stem and cap) and can be readily grown in Mason jars in a low-cost medium.

### Pure Culture Technique

The most critical aspect of cultivation is maintaining sterile conditions:

- Work in a clean, dust-free room with disinfected surfaces.
- Scrub arms, hands, and nails with disinfectant soap.
- Wear clean short-sleeve clothing, surgical mask, and hair cover.
- Eliminate drafts; close doors; exclude animals and unnecessary people.
- Only sterilized equipment may contact medium or inoculum.

### Sterilization (Autoclaving)

1. Fill containers no more than half full with medium.
2. Place in pressure cooker at **15–20 lbs pressure (250°F) for 30 minutes**.
3. Higher temperatures or longer periods cause dextrose/maltose sugars to caramelize, inhibiting growth and psilocybin production.
4. Cool completely before opening.
5. Hold at room temperature for 3 days to check for contamination.

### Spore Print Method

- Spore prints serve for identification (colour) and microscopic study (spore shape).
- A single cap produces millions of spores.
- Spores are germinated on agar media to begin cultivation.

### Cultivation Timeline

1. Germinate spores on agar media (stock culture).
2. Inoculate broth medium with mycelium fragments from stock culture.
3. Incubate 10–12 days at 70–75°F (for *P. cubensis*).
4. Shake jars every 2–3 days to aerate and disperse mycelium.
5. Monitor with a **saccharimeter** — maximum growth and psilocybin content occurs ~4 days after all broth sugar is consumed.
6. Harvest mycelium at peak.
7. Filter through flannel cloth, dry in oven at ≤200°F.

### Psilocybin Testing

**Paper chromatography** can detect psilocybin and psilocin:

1. Dry and pulverize mushroom sample.
2. Extract into unheated methanol (shake 30 minutes).
3. Spot on chromatography paper; develop with water-saturated butanol.
4. Spray with p-dimethylaminobenzaldehyde in alcohol, then 1N HCl.
5. **Psilocybin**: reddish colour. **Psilocin**: blue-violet zone.

### Blueing Reaction

Most psilocybin-bearing mushrooms develop a **blue colour** when flesh is exposed to air, caused by oxidation of indole-based substances. However, this is not a definitive test — some non-psilocybin species (e.g., *Boletus eastwoodiae*) also blue.

## Legal Status

- Psilocybin and psilocin are Schedule I [[controlled-substances-act-and-psychedelic-research]] in the US (Title 21, Section I, (C)).
- Only *Psilocybe mexicana* is specifically named in law — many other species were not included due to legislative ignorance.
- Possession of any psilocybin-bearing mushroom is theoretically equivalent to possessing the alkaloid, but prosecution practices vary.
