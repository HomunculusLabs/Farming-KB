# Organic Lawn Care

updated: 2026-04-18

Sowing seed is the cheapest way to lay a new lawn and allows you to control the different types of grasses making up the lawn. The only disadvantage is that a seed lawn takes three months to establish if sown in spring; if sown in fall, it should not be used until the following spring. A turf lawn is an "instant lawn" — it looks good right away and is ready for use about six weeks after laying if put down in spring.

## Sowing a Seed Lawn

Grass seed should be sown in early spring or early fall, when some wet weather can be expected. Avoid the driest months.

**Step 1 — Prepare a stale seedbed.** Rake the area roughly with the back of a fork to take out any local undulations before consolidating the soil.

**Step 2 — Improve soil.** If soil is light or lacks organic matter, apply a 2in (5cm) layer of [[organic-composting-methods]] and rake it in. If soil is heavy, use coarse sand instead.

**Step 3 — Fertilize.** Rake in two handfuls of blood, fish, and bone meal every square yard/meter. To consolidate the soil, walk over the whole area with your weight on your heels — this prevents the soil from sinking later.

**Step 4 — Level the area.** Rake the area level. Step back from time to time, crouch down, and squint across the surface to see undulations, though a completely level lawn is rarely necessary.

**Step 5 — Sow seed.** Sow at 1-1.5 oz (25-35g) per square yard/meter (about two handfuls). Stand with feet as wide apart as possible and lean forward as far as possible to cover about 1 square yard/meter per scatter.

**Step 6 — Cover and protect.** Rake the seed in, aiming to cover about half with soil. To speed germination and protect from birds, cover with perforated plastic. Remove when the first seed germinates.

## Maintaining a New Seed Lawn

**Watering:** With only a limited root system, a new lawn is very vulnerable in dry weather. Water using a lawn sprinkler to apply a fine spray at regular intervals. If sowing grass seed along the edge of paving, raise the level of the lawn above the paving to ensure easy mowing.

**Mowing:** When grass is about 3in (7cm) high, it is ready for cutting. First, roll with the mower blades right off the ground to push in any loose stones. This also bruises the grass stems, causing the buds at their base to grow out, greatly thickening up the lawn. Then raise mower blades as far as they will go and trim the tips. Lower blades progressively with each cut until about 0.5in (1cm) above soil surface. Any weeds growing through the new grass will be sliced off regularly. Annuals will disappear quickly. Only rosetted weeds will survive regular mowing and these can be dealt with later.

## Laying a Turf Lawn

Prepare the soil (as for seed lawn) and rake flat. Always work from boards — never walk on the turf you have just laid or the leveled soil. Scaffolding boards are ideal.

**Step 1:** Place a board along the longest straight edge and lay the first row of turf. Tap down strips with the back of a rake to put them into close contact with the soil.

**Step 2:** Lay a board on the first row of turf. Standing on the board, lay a second row of turf parallel to the first and pull it into the first with the back of the rake. No need to bond pieces together.

**Step 3:** When you reach the end of a row, butt the last turf up to the edging turf by laying it on top and cutting off the excess with a penknife.

**Step 4:** When the whole lawn is down, put a sprinkler on the area. Keep pieces well watered after laying to prevent shrinking. Once grass has established its roots, water less frequently. Shape the edges with an edging knife.

## Maintaining a New Turf Lawn

**Watering:** Make sure the sod is never short of water. It takes only a little drying out to cause pieces to shrink, leaving ugly gaps. Use the sprinkler little and often.

**Mowing:** You will know the grass has rooted when it starts to look greener and stands up. Cut in the same way recommended for a seed lawn.

## Established Lawn Care

If you already have a lawn, you cannot dig in organic matter, but you can care for the grass to keep it in good condition.

### Drainage

Proper drainage is necessary to get grass growing really well. Improve by adding sand or organic matter. If drainage is poor, the soil becomes waterlogged, preventing air from reaching the roots. If the lawn drains too quickly, the grass soon becomes brown and unhealthy.

**Improving drainage:** Work over the lawn with a hollow-tined fork, removing cores of soil. If soil is heavy, put sand into the holes to fill them. For light soil, use garden compost.

### Feeding

Lawns can be fed with slow-release blood, fish, and bone meal once or at most twice a year — in early spring and again in early summer. Alternatively, for instant greening results, use a liquid manure feed high in nitrogen.

### Weed Control

If you keep the grass growing well and cut it regularly, you will not be bothered by many weeds. But inevitably some rosetted weeds (like dandelions or daisies) creep in. They can be kept under control by cutting them out regularly with a knife, or by dropping ordinary table salt on the growing point — this causes them to shrivel up and die overnight. Alternatively, cut them out with a penknife.

### Repairing Coarse Patches

If patches of coarse grass appear, they can be repaired by hatching and reseeding. Score the coarse patch in a criss-cross fashion with a penknife and reseed using an equal-parts mixture of seed, moist peat, and good soil.

### Mowing Technique

Mow only during the growing season — grass stops growing in winter and does not need cutting then. Begin by raking any earthworm casts around the lawn, otherwise the mower flattens them, making perfect seedbeds for weeds.

- Cut in opposite directions on alternate cuts. First cut once around the entire edge, then cut diagonally across the lawn. Mark your starting point so you can work in the opposite direction next time.
- Never mow too close — this encourages bare patches colonized by moss and weeds.
- Always leave grass 0.5in (1cm) long and remove cuttings to prevent build-up of dead material and fungus disease attack.
- Grass cuttings are the best [[organic-composting-methods]]ing material possible — never throw them away.
- Rake the lawn with a spring-tine rake at least once a year in spring to remove dead grass before you start mowing.
- Trim edges with long-handled shears after mowing, taking clippers as close to the edge as possible.
