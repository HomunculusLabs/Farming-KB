## Making Good Compost

Making good compost is something of an art because each batch of
starting materials is different. Key requirements for both methods:
- Maintain 5.5 ppm O2 or higher during compost cycle
- Pleasant smell (no anaerobic conditions)
- Moisture 45-75%
- The required set of organisms to compete with, inhibit, and
  consume diseases or pest organisms
- Match the fungal:bacterial ratio to the requirements of the
  target plant

To make compost, you have to have the right starting materials
and process correctly -- worm, thermal, or static. They can all
make great [[bacterial-vs-fungal-compost-teas]].

## Compost Longevity

If kept in warm conditions with good moisture (50%) and protected
from contaminants, compost should remain good for up to 2 years.
Adding foods to compost keeps organisms happy and growing. Good
fungal compost is particularly valuable and should be preserved.

## Testing Requirements

For thermal compost: turning times, daily temperature, end
moisture, and daily CO2 or O2 data for each batch.

For vermicompost: worm numbers per unit volume, temperature, and
oxygen data.

Both must meet chemistry and pathogen testing standards at city,
county, state, and federal levels.
