## Ololiuhqui: The Aztec Morning Glory

Ololiuhqui is the Aztec name for the seeds of certain climbing plants
of the Convolvulaceae (morning glory family) that were used in
pre-Columbian times by the Aztecs and neighboring peoples in religious

The Zapotec utilize both species: the original ololiuhqui (badoh) from
Turbina corymbosa and badoh negro, the seeds of Ipomoea violacea.

## The Astonishing Chemical Discovery

Hofmann and Tscherter advanced the working hypothesis that the active
principles of ololiuhqui could be indole compounds, the same chemical
class as LSD, psilocybin, and psilocin. Extracts of ololiuhqui seeds
gave the blue coloration characteristic of indole compounds with the
appropriate reagent. The identification led to an astonishing result.

The active principles from the ancient Mexican magic drug proved to be
identical with substances already present in Hofmann's laboratory. They
were identical with alkaloids obtained in the course of decades-long
investigations of ergot. Lysergic acid amide, lysergic acid
hydroxyethylamide, and closely related alkaloids were established as
the main active principles. Also present was ergobasine (ergometrine),
whose synthesis had constituted the starting point of Hofmann's
investigations on ergot alkaloids.

## Skeptical Reception

When Hofmann presented these findings at the IUPAC Natural Products
Congress in Sydney, Australia, in 1960, colleagues received the talk
with skepticism. Some suspected that ololiuhqui extracts could have
been contaminated with traces of lysergic acid derivatives from the
laboratory. There was another reason for doubt: the occurrence in higher
plants (morning glory family) of ergot alkaloids known only from lower
fungi contradicted the experience that certain substances are
restricted to respective plant families.

Results were confirmed by independent laboratories in the United States,
Germany, and Holland. The suspicion that seeds could have been infected
with alkaloid-producing fungi was ruled out experimentally.

## Pharmacological Effects

Lysergic acid amide evokes a dreamlike condition, but only with a
tenfold to twenty-fold greater dose than [[turner-lsd]] substitute. The boom
lasted only a short time, as the seeds tasted very bad, were difficult
to digest, and produced less desirable effects than LSD.
