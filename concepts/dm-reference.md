# N,N-Dimethyltryptamine (DMT) Reference

## Overview

N,N-Dimethyltryptamine (DMT) is a naturally occurring tryptamine compound found in many plants, animals, and fungi. It is a potent serotonergic psychedelic and one of the most widely distributed psychoactive compounds in nature. DMT has been used for centuries in indigenous South American ayahuasca ceremonies and has become the subject of intensive clinical research for its potential therapeutic applications. This page covers chemistry, natural sources, pharmacology, effects, dosage, and safety.

## Chemistry

### Structure and Properties

- **Chemical name**: N,N-Dimethyltryptamine
- **Chemical formula**: C12H16N2
- **Molecular weight**: 188.27 g/mol
- **IUPAC name**: N,N-dimethyl-1H-indole-3-ethanamine
- **Structure**: Tryptamine backbone (indole ring + ethylamine chain) with two methyl groups on the terminal amine nitrogen
- **Appearance**: White to off-white crystalline solid (freebase); crystalline powder (salt forms)

### Physical Properties

- **Freebase**: Melting point 44-49C; insoluble in water; soluble in organic solvents; vaporizes at ~60-80C
- **Fumarate salt**: Water-soluble; stable at room temperature; melting point ~150C
- **Hydrochloride salt**: Water-soluble; hygroscopic

### Chemical Relatives

- **5-MeO-DMT** (5-methoxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine): More potent, different subjective profile
- **Bufotenin** (5-OH-DMT): Found in Anadenanthera seeds and toad venom
- **Psilocybin/Psilocin**: 4-substituted tryptamines; see [[morel-mushroom-guide]] for context on fungal psychoactives
- **DPT** (N,N-dipropyltryptamine): Synthetic tryptamine
- **DET** (N,N-diethyltryptamine): Synthetic tryptamine

## Natural Sources

### Plants

- **Psychotria viridis** (chacruna): Primary DMT source in ayahuasca; 0.1-0.6% DMT
- **Diplopterys cabrerana** (chaliponga): Up to 1% DMT
- **Mimosa tenuiflora** (M. hostilis / jurema): Root bark contains 0.57-1.0% DMT
- **Acacia species**: A. confusa, A. obtusifolia, A. maidenii — bark contains DMT
- **Anadenanthera peregrina** (yopo) and A. colubrina (vilca): Seeds contain DMT, 5-MeO-DMT, bufotenin
- **Virola species**: V. theiodora, V. sebifera — bark resin used as snuff

### Animals

- DMT detected in human brain, CSF, blood, and urine at trace levels (nanomolar)
- Found in rat, mouse, rabbit brain tissue
- Present in Bufo alvarius (sonoran desert toad) venom as 5-MeO-DMT
- Endogenous DMT function debated: trace amine signaling, sigma-1 receptor modulation

### Fungi

- DMT is not a major fungal metabolite (psilocybin/psilocin are the primary psychoactive fungal tryptamines)
- Trace amounts detected in some fungal species

## Extraction Methods

Note: This information is provided for educational and harm reduction purposes. DMT is a Schedule I controlled substance in many jurisdictions. Extraction of controlled substances may be illegal.

### Plant Material Extraction (General Overview)

The most common extraction follows an acid-base (A/B) protocol:

1. **Plant material preparation**: Dried and powdered plant material (e.g., Mimosa root bark)
2. **Acidification**: Material is soaked in an acidic aqueous solution (vinegar or dilute HCl) to convert DMT to water-soluble DMT salt
3. **Defatting**: Non-polar solvent (naphtha) washes remove fats, oils, and non-alkaloid plant material
4. **Basification**: The aqueous solution is basified (NaOH) to pH 11-13, converting DMT to freebase form (insoluble in water, soluble in non-polar solvents)
5. **Pull**: Non-polar solvent (naphtha) is added to extract the DMT freebase
6. **Separation**: The solvent layer containing DMT is separated and collected
7. **Precipitation**: The solvent is frozen or evaporated to precipitate DMT crystals
8. **Purification**: Recrystallization from hot solvent to improve purity

### Ayahuasca Preparation

Ayahuasca is traditionally prepared by combining:
- **DMT-containing plant**: Psychotria viridis or Diplopterys cabrerana
- **MAOI-containing plant**: Banisteriopsis caapi (ayahuasca vine), which contains harmine, harmaline, and tetrahydroharmine (beta-carboline MAO inhibitors)

The beta-carbolines inhibit monoamine oxidase (MAO) in the gut and liver, preventing the rapid oxidative deamination of DMT and allowing it to be orally active. Without the MAOI, orally consumed DMT is inactive because MAO-A rapidly breaks it down before it reaches the brain.

## Pharmacology

### Receptor Pharmacology

DMT is primarily a serotonin (5-HT) receptor agonist:

- **5-HT2A**: Primary receptor responsible for psychedelic effects (full agonist)
- **5-HT2C**: Moderate agonism; may contribute to anxiety and perceptual effects
- **5-HT1A**: Partial agonism; may contribute to anxiolytic aspects
- **Sigma-1 receptor**: Agonism; may modulate neuroprotection and neuroplasticity
- **TAAR1**: Trace amine-associated receptor agonism
- **SERT**: Acts as a substrate/inhibitor at the serotonin transporter

### Pharmacokinetics

**Smoked/vaporized (freebase)**: Onset 10-30s, peak 2-5 min, duration 15-30 min, bioavailability 40-60% estimated. Crosses blood-brain barrier rapidly.

**Intravenous**: Onset immediate, peak 1-2 min, duration 15-30 min.

**Oral (with MAOI, as ayahuasca)**: Onset 20-60 min, peak 60-120 min, duration 3-6 hours. MAO inhibition extends oral bioavailability from ~0% to ~50%+.

- **Intranasal**: Less common; onset in minutes, duration 30-60 minutes

### Metabolism

- Primary: Oxidative deamination by MAO-A to DMT-aldehyde, then indoleacetic acid
- Secondary: N-demethylation to N-methyltryptamine (NMT)
- Half-life: ~15 minutes (IV); longer when MAO inhibited

## Effects

### Subjective Experience

- Intense visual hallucinations (geometric patterns, colors, entities, landscapes)
- Altered sense of time and space
- Ego dissolution or "ego death" at high doses
- Mystical or spiritual experiences
- Emotional catharsis
- Synesthesia
- Altered body perception
- Encounters with perceived entities or beings

### Physiological Effects

- Increased heart rate and blood pressure (transient)
- Pupillary dilation
- Increased body temperature
- Nausea and vomiting (common with oral/ayahuasca use; the "purge")
- Dizziness
- Potential for HPPD (Hallucinogen Persisting Perception Disorder) in rare cases

### Dosage

**Smoked/Vaporized (Freebase)**: Threshold 2-5 mg, Light 10-20 mg, Moderate 20-40 mg, Strong 40-60 mg, Breakthrough 60-100+ mg.

**Oral (Ayahuasca)**: Typical ceremonial dose 50-100 mg DMT with 50-150 mg harmine/harmaline. Effects depend heavily on MAOI dose and individual MAO activity.

## Safety

### Physical Safety

- DMT has very low toxicity; no known lethal dose in humans
- Primary risks are psychological, not physiological
- Avoid with cardiovascular conditions (transient BP/HR increase)
- Avoid with personal/family history of psychosis
- Serotonin syndrome risk with SSRIs or other serotonergic drugs

### Psychological Safety

- Set and setting are critical for safe experiences
- Integration support recommended after intense experiences
- Contraindicated for schizophrenia, bipolar, or psychotic disorders
- "Bad trips" can occur — preparation and a trusted guide reduce risk

### Legal Status

- Schedule I in the United States (illegal to possess, manufacture, or distribute)
- Controlled substance in most countries worldwide
- Ayahuasca legally protected for religious use in some jurisdictions (Brazil, Peru, certain US exemptions)
- DMT-containing plants have varying legal status depending on preparation and intent

## Clinical Research

- Ongoing research at institutions including Johns Hopkins, Imperial College London, and Usona Institute
- Investigating DMT for treatment-resistant depression, anxiety related to life-threatening illness, and substance use disorders
- Ayahuasca research for depression, PTSD, and addiction
- Neuroscience studies of brain mechanisms (default mode network disruption, increased global connectivity)
