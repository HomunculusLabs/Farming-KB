## Key Distinguishing Features of LSD Psychotherapy

Several features distinguish LSD-assisted psychotherapy from conventional
pharmacotherapy:

- **Experience-dependent**: Unlike antidepressants or tranquilizers where the patient
  takes a pill and goes home, LSD psychotherapy inherently involves a profound
  experiential state that forms the basis of therapeutic change
- **Amplifier function**: LSD acts as a nonspecific amplifier of psychic contents,
  partly through lowering sensory thresholds ("cleansing the doors of perception") and
  partly through not-yet-understood central processes involving one or more
  neurotransmitters
- **Microscope analogy**: LSD can play the same role in psychology as the microscope
  in biology -- opening hidden realms to direct, repeatable, verifiable observation
- **Guide requirement**: The personal experience of the therapist is an essential
  prerequisite; without prior experience, communication between therapist and patient
  in a psychedelic state is likely to be severely limited
