## Mechanism of Action

Most hallucinogens produce their effects primarily through agonism at the
5-HT2A [[serotonin-receptor-interference-psychedelic-mechanism]] subtype. Salvinorin-A has no appreciable affinity
for serotonin receptors. Instead, it is a highly potent and selective kappa-
opioid receptor (KOR) agonist (Roth et al., 2002). KOR activation produces
dissociative effects, including feelings of unreality, perceptual
distortions, and profound alterations in consciousness that differ
qualitatively from serotonergic hallucinogens.

## Traditional Use

The Mazatec Indians of Oaxaca, Mexico, traditionally consume Salvia
divinorum by chewing fresh leaves or by drinking the juice of crushed
leaves. The salvinorin-A is absorbed through the oral mucosa during
chewing, producing a less intense intoxication lasting up to approximately
1 hour. This traditional method emphasizes the ceremonial and divinatory
context rather than recreational intoxication.

## Modern Use and Availability

Salvia divinorum plants are now grown and sold in the [[magic-mushroom-preparation-and-consumption-methods]] include:

- **Smoking dried leaves**: Produces rapid onset of effects within 30–60
  seconds, with psychoactivity lasting approximately 15 minutes at doses
  of 200–500 μg of salvinorin-A.
- **Smoking concentrated extracts**: Enhanced-potency extracts (5x, 10x,
  20x, up to 100x) are sold through Internet-based companies and smoke
  shops, producing dramatically more intense experiences.
- **Sublingual tinctures**: Liquid extracts held under the tongue for
  oral mucosa absorption, closer to the traditional method.

## Subjective Effects

Salvia divinorum produces an experience qualitatively different from other
hallucinogens. Common effects include:

- Uncontrollable laughter
- Sensation of merging with objects or environments
- Vivid and sometimes bizarre visual and auditory hallucinations
- Profound dissociation and feelings of unreality
- Altered sense of time and body image
- Temporary loss of motor coordination

The experience is typically short-lived (5–15 minutes when smoked) but can
be extremely intense during its peak. Many users describe it as
unpleasant or overwhelming, which may limit its abuse potential.

## Abuse Potential and Safety

Salvinorin-A has no reinforcing properties, meaning it does not produce
dependence or addiction. The DEA has monitored Salvia divinorum as a
possibly emerging drug of abuse but has not scheduled it at the federal
level. The primary risks come from the intense dissociative effects
themselves — falls, accidents, or dangerous behavior during intoxication.
Setting is critical: using Salvia in unsafe environments (e.g., near
stairs, while driving) poses the greatest danger.
