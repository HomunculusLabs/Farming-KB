## The A-Frame Level

The A-frame is the simplest and most accessible contour-finding tool. It
consists of two legs of equal length joined at the top to form an A shape,
with a cross-brace for stability. A plumb line (string with a weight) hangs
from the apex, and a horizontal mark on the cross-brace indicates the level
position. When the plumb line aligns with the mark, the two feet of the
A-frame are at exactly the same elevation.

### Construction

To build an A-frame: cut two straight pieces of timber 1.5 to 2 metres long
for the legs. Join them at the top with a bolt, hinge, or lashing that allows
the legs to pivot slightly. Fit a cross-brace about one-third from the bottom.
Hang a plumb line from the apex. With the A-frame standing on level ground,
mark where the plumb line crosses the cross-brace. This is the level mark.

### Use

To find contour: place one foot of the A-frame at your starting point. Swing
the other foot uphill or downhill until the plumb line aligns with the level
mark. Mark the position of both feet with pegs. Move the A-frame so one foot
is at the second peg, and repeat. The series of pegs marks a [[bloomfield-buller-drop-surface-tension-spore-catapult-basidiospore-discharge]].
