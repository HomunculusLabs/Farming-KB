# [[fungal-metal-transformations]]

Fungi transform metals through a diverse array of mechanisms that affect metal mobility, bioavailability, toxicity and speciation. These transformations are central to geomycology and have profound implications for environmental remediation, soil fertility, biogeochemical cycling, and even the integrity of human-built structures including nuclear waste containment systems.

## Metal Solubilization and Mobilization

### Mechanisms of Metal Release

Fungi solubilize metals from rocks and minerals through the same biochemical weathering mechanisms that drive mineral dissolution: acidolysis, complexolysis and redoxolysis. The primary fungal impact on biogeochemical cycling results from acidolysis and complexolysis. Key metal-mobilizing metabolites include:

- **Organic acids** (oxalic, citric, gluconic, malic, succinic, formic): provide protons for acidolysis and chelating anions for complexolysis
- **Siderophores**: high-affinity iron-chelating compounds that also mobilize other metals
- **Amino acids and phenolic compounds**: additional metal-complexing agents excreted during metabolism

[[mycorrhizal-fungi]] excrete low-molecular-mass carboxylic acids contributing to "heterotrophic leaching" of metals from soil minerals. In podzol E horizons under European coniferous forests, weathering of hornblendes, feldspars and granitic bedrock has been attributed to oxalic, citric, succinic, formic and malic acid excretion by ectomycorrhizal hyphae producing micro- to millimolar concentrations at micropores (3-10 micrometres) in weatherable soil minerals. The ectomycorrhizal fungus Piloderma extracted K and/or Mg from biotite, microcline and chlorite and precipitated mycogenic calcium oxalate on the hyphae.

### Mobilization of Toxic Metals

During lithospheric weathering, non-essential toxic metals (Cs, Al, Cd, Hg, Pb) are mobilized alongside essential nutrients. Metals can exert toxic effects through multiple mechanisms:

- Blocking functional groups of essential enzymes
- Displacing essential metal ions from their normal binding sites
- Disrupting cellular and organellar membranes
- Interacting with systems that normally protect against free radicals
- Generating reactive oxygen species through Fenton-like reactions

Ericoid mycorrhizal and ectomycorrhizal fungi dissolve cadmium-, copper-, zinc- and lead-bearing minerals, including metal phosphates. The ectomycorrhizal fungi [[suillus-granulatus]] and Paxillus involutus released elements from apatite and wood ash (K, Ca, Ti, Mn, Pb) and accumulated them in mycelia, especially rhizomorphs. This demonstrates that mycorrhizal fungi can actively accumulate toxic metals, which has implications for both phytoremediation and food chain contamination.

## Metal Immobilization and Detoxification

### Biosorption

Fungi can be highly efficient bioaccumulators of soluble and particulate forms of metals (Ni, Zn, Ag, Cu, Cd, Pb), especially from dilute external concentrations. Metal binding occurs through multiple components:

- **Cell wall components** (chitin, glucans, melanin) providing abundant binding sites with diverse chemical properties
- **Extracellular polysaccharides** in the mucilaginous sheath surrounding hyphae
- **Pigments** including melanin, which has high metal-binding capacity due to its phenolic and quinone groups
- **Intracellular accumulation** via transport across cell membranes into the cytoplasm

Metal binding is an important passive process in both living and dead fungal biomass, making dead fungal material useful for biosorption applications in water treatment and waste remediation.

### Extracellular Precipitation

Fungi immobilize metals through precipitation of secondary mycogenic minerals spanning diverse chemical classes:

- **Metal oxalates**: Ca, Cd, Co, Cu, Mn, Sr, Zn, Ni, Pb oxalates formed by diverse fungal species
- **Carbonates**: calcite (CaCO3) precipitation, important for soil aggregation and carbon sequestration
- **Oxides and hydroxides**: Fe and Mn oxides precipitated from reduced mineral sources
- **Other minerals**: birnessite, ferrihydrite, goethite, moolooite, montmorillonite, and many more

Immobilization turns metals into chemically more inert forms, resulting in detoxification. The ability of fungi to immobilize metals via biosorption, accumulation within biomass and extracellular precipitation is the basis for many fungal bioremediation strategies.

### Redox Transformations

Fungi mediate important redox transformations of metals:

- **Reduction**: precipitation of elemental Ag (black colonies), Se (red), Te (black) from ionic forms
- **Oxidation**: Mn and Fe oxidation from siderite (FeCO3) and rhodochrosite (MnCO3) to oxide forms
- **Chromium reduction**: fungi with chromate-reducing activity can reduce highly toxic Cr(VI) to less toxic Cr(III), useful for Cr-polluted soil treatment

## Mycorrhizas and Metal Transformations

Mycorrhizal fungi play critical roles in metal transformations in the rhizosphere through multiple simultaneous mechanisms:

- Ectomycorrhizal mycobionts filter toxic metals in the hyphal sheath or Hartig net by sorption
- They restrict metal mobility due to hydrophobicity of the fungal sheath
- They complex metals with released organic acids and other substances
- Arbuscular mycorrhizas reduce toxic metal (e.g., Zn) uptake by plants
- Metal-resistant mycobionts most efficiently ameliorate metal toxicity to host plants

The Paxillus involutus/Pinus sylvestris ectomycorrhizal association demonstrated that zinc phosphate dissolution, phosphorus acquisition and zinc accumulation depended on mycorrhizal status, zinc tolerance of the fungal strain, and phosphorus status. Under phosphorus-replete conditions, zinc-tolerant ectomycorrhiza employed a toxic metal avoidance strategy restricting metal entry and protecting plant tissues from excessive zinc accumulation.

## Environmental Applications

### Bioremediation Potential

The ability of fungi to transform toxic metals and metalloids has potential for treatment of contaminated land:

- Immobilization via biosorption and precipitation turns metals into inert forms
- Metal dissolution can be used in cleaning soils, solid industrial wastes, low-grade ores and metal-bearing minerals
- Phosphate-induced metal stabilization may be compromised by fungal mineral-solubilizing activity
- Fungi with Cr(VI)-reducing activity treat Cr-polluted soils
- Mycorrhizas enhance plant growth on contaminated soils for re-vegetation and phyto/rhizo-remediation
- Metal-resistant mycorrhizal associations show greatest promise for re-vegetation and re-forestation programmes

### Deterioration of Built Structures

Fungal metal transformations also cause significant deterioration of human infrastructure:

- Any building material, concrete and cement can be degraded by fungi through the same mechanisms used for natural rock weathering
- Fungal deterioration of concrete may be more efficient than bacterial attack
- Fungi colonize and corrode barrier concrete, avoiding granite-filled areas
- Microfungi adapt to severe radioactive contamination at Chernobyl, where melanized Alternaria, Cladosporium and Aureobasidium colonize highly radioactive concrete
- Myco-corrosion may reduce the theoretically calculated service life of concrete barriers for nuclear waste, causing potential risk from release of radionuclides
