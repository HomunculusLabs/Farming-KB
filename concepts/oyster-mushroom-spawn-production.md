# Oyster Mushroom Spawn Production

Spawn is the mycelium-rich material used to inoculate bulk substrates for [[mushroom-cultivation]]. Producing high-quality spawn is the foundation of successful mushroom growing. Poor spawn = poor or no mushrooms, regardless of substrate quality.

## Types of Spawn

### Grain Spawn (Most Common)
- Mycelium grown on sterilized grain (rye, millet, sorghum, wheat)
- Each grain kernel is colonized with mycelium — hundreds of inoculation points per handful
- Standard for both hobby and commercial cultivation
- Advantages: Fast colonization, many inoculation points, easy to produce
- Disadvantages: Requires sterilization, contamination risk

### Sawdust Spawn
- Mycelium grown on sterilized hardwood sawdust + bran
- Preferred for log inoculation (sawdust plugs)
- Longer shelf life than [[grain-spawn]]
- Slower to colonize than grain spawn

### Liquid Culture
- Mycelium grown in nutrient broth
- Inoculated by syringe into grain bags or substrate
- Fastest expansion rate
- Requires more sterile technique than other methods

## Grain Spawn Production

### Step 1: Grain Preparation
- **Rye grain**: Most common choice. Good moisture retention, kernels separate easily
- **Millet**: Small grain, many inoculation points. Preferred by many commercial growers
- **Sorghum**: Excellent, round grains, good moisture
- **Wheat**: Works but kernels sometimes stick together

### Step 2: Hydration
- Soak grain in water for 12-24 hours at room temperature
- Alternative: simmer for 30-45 minutes until grains are plump but not burst
- Drain thoroughly (grain should not be wet on surface)

### Step 3: Loading Jars/Bags
- Fill jars 2/3 full with hydrated grain
- Add filter lid or polyfill plug for gas exchange
- For bags: fill 2-3 lbs per bag, add filter patch

### Step 4: Sterilization
- Pressure canner: 15 PSI (121°C) for 90 minutes
- Critical: full sterilization is essential — any surviving spores will contaminate
- Allow to cool completely (overnight) before inoculation

### Step 5: Inoculation
- Work in front of a [[laminar-flow-hood]] or in a still-air box
- Flame-sterilize inoculation tool between transfers
- Transfer 1-2 tablespoons of clean spawn (or [[liquid-culture]]) per jar
- Shake jar to distribute inoculum throughout grain

### Step 6: Incubation
- Temperature: 70-80°F (21-27°C) for [[oyster-mushrooms]]
- Darkness preferred (not required)
- Shake jars at 20-30% colonization to redistribute and speed up
- Full colonization: 10-21 days depending on species and conditions
- Signs of healthy colonization: white, fluffy mycelium spreading from inoculation points, mushroom smell

### Step 7: Quality Check
- Healthy spawn: uniform white mycelium, grainy texture, mushroom smell
- Contaminated spawn: any color other than white (green, blue, pink, black), slimy texture, sour or rotten smell
- Discard contaminated jars immediately (don't open indoors)

## Strain Selection for Oyster Mushrooms

### Common Cultivated Species
- **[[pleurotus-ostreatus]]** (Pearl oyster): Most common, versatile, 55-75°F
- **Pleurotus pulmonarius** (Phoenix oyster): Similar but warmer, 65-85°F
- **Pleurotus eryngii** (King oyster): Large, thick stems, 55-65°F
- **Pleurotus citrinopileatus** (Golden oyster): Yellow, tropical, 70-85°F
- **Pleurotus djamor** (Pink oyster): Tropical, 70-90°F, very fast

### Sourcing Strains
- Commercial spawn suppliers (Field & Forest, Mushroom Mountain, North Spore)
- Culture banks (ATCC, CBS)
- Cloning from fresh grocery store mushrooms (use tissue culture on agar first)

## Storage
- Refrigerated (35-40°F): grain spawn lasts 2-3 months
- Room temperature: use within 2-4 weeks
- Liquid culture: refrigerated, lasts 6-12 months
- Don't freeze spawn (kills mycelium)
