# Aquaculture Pond Construction and Size Classes

Fish ponds range from tiny garden containers of a few square metres to commercial impoundments of hundreds of hectares. Each size class has distinct applications, construction requirements, and management considerations that make it suited to particular roles in a permaculture system.

## Construction Principles

Pond or terrace construction on a small scale (up to 0.2 ha) can be hand-tuned for drainage, levelling, and spillways. Larger constructions arise either from years of hand labour built to suit the landscape, or from modern survey and machine work.

### Sealing and Materials

- **Still ponds** require an essential compact clay base
- **Tidal and diversion ponds** can tolerate some seepage losses
- Earth, clay, and stone-faced earth walls suffice for most ponds up to 2–3 m depth
- Concrete-sealed ponds are used for intensive trout and fast-flow channel systems
- By far the most productive, common, and economical ponds are clay-based

A build-up of organic mulch and algae in ponds assists sealing over time, so that even ponds in sandy loams may gradually evolve to reduce water losses. Long uncompacted canals, however, need good clay bases to prevent transit water loss, although seepages can be captured by planting land crops and trees to utilize the water.

### New Pond Assessment

New ponds need careful assessment for evaporation rates, seepage potential, water source reliability, sealing requirements, and structural stability.

## Size Classes

### Ponds from 1–10 Square Metres

Very small garden ponds of 2–60 cm depth can be made from old baths, stock watering tanks, or plastic-lined holes with protective clay or earth covers. Pre-cast ponds in plastic, fibreglass, and concrete are sold commercially.

In shallow ponds, productive species include Chinese water chestnut (Eleocharis dulcis), kangkong (Ipomoea aquatica), watercress (Rorippa aquatica), taro (Colocasia esculentus) — a square metre yields 20–30 kg of starchy food — frogs (excellent garden predators), and small fish as forage stocks.

At approximately 10 square metres and 2 m deep, clear fibreglass or Kalwall® ponds (as used at the New Alchemy Institute) produce fish and products valued at $4.50 to $17 per square foot (1984), amortizing costs in 3–5 years. They yield fish, shrimp, and enriched water for semi-hydroponic crops, while providing useful heat storage for night and winter heating.

### Ponds from 10–100 Square Metres

Ponds of this size serve multiple functions: rearing fish fry for sale or stocking, breeding forage fish, producing large quantities of vegetable crop, supplying aquaculture nurseries and aquariums, and creating fire-breaks, water reserves, and heat moderation.

One special pond of this size can supply house heating and hot water (the solar pond), or be built to heat a larger pond. An intensively-managed 100 square metre fish pond comes close to providing a full protein and vegetable resource for a family, yielding 300–2,000 kg of protein. These ponds can form part of waste food disposal and total wastewater systems.

### Ponds from 100–500 Square Metres

Chakroff (1982) and others regard ponds of this size as **ideal for fish culture**. Advantages include easy harvesting, netting, or draining, and the capacity to hold specific age, size, or species assemblies with management flexibility.

Even one 500 square metre pond can provide income from baitfish, aquarium fish, special plant crops, and intensively managed high-value fish such as prawns. Incomes of approximately $30,000 were achievable from open-air ponds of this size stocked with prawns (Hawaii, 1983).

### Ponds from 500 Square Metres to 5 Hectares

About 5 ha is estimated to provide a full affluent family income in high-value product. Ten to twenty prawn ponds (with ducks, mussels, and edge plants) constitute a full-time job akin to — but more easily managed than — a market garden. These are characterized as **semi-intensive** systems. Sewage ponds slightly exceeding 5 ha provide safe disposal for small towns.

### Ponds Greater than 5 Hectares

Impoundments of 50–500 hectares are now not uncommon as extensive farms. Even a single impoundment designed for easy management gives a living to one or more families. Innovative leaseholds, recreational fishing, and new scales of planning (village development, holiday homes, boating, water sales) may become feasible at this scale.

## Pond Wall Construction

Where clay is in short supply, a tamped clay cut-off core provides an economical seal for pond walls. Earth walls with clay cores every 20–30 m create effective containment. For larger ponds, stone rip-rap facing on earth walls provides erosion protection while maintaining the clay seal.
