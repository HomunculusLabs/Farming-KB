# Halophilic Fungi
Halophilic and halotolerant fungi are adapted to environments with
elevated salt concentrations, ranging from moderately saline soils to
hypersaline salt pans and marine environments. These fungi occupy a
specialized but ecologically important niche at the interface of
terrestrial and aquatic systems.

## Classification of Salt Tolerance

Fungi are categorized by their response to NaCl in the growth medium
(Kushner 1978):

- **Nonhalophiles:** grow best in media containing less than 0.2 M NaCl
- **Halotolerant:** can tolerate salt to the point of saturation of
  the medium
- **Borderline extreme halophiles:** grow best in media containing
  1.5-4.0 M NaCl
- **Extreme halophiles:** grow best in media containing 2.5-5.2 M NaCl

No extreme halophiles have been found among the eukaryotes. Among
fungi, most salt-tolerant species fall into the halotolerant category.
Microorganisms tolerant of aw values between 0.75 and 0.981 that result
from high NaCl concentrations are considered halophilic or halotolerant
(Truper and Galinski 1986).

## Halotolerant Soil Fungi

Halotolerant fungi including species of *Alternaria*, *Aspergillus*,
*Penicillium*, *Myrothecium*, *Stachybotrys*, and *Trichoderma* are
commonly isolated from arid and saline soils (Malik et al. 1982).
Specific halophilic species include:

- **Basipetospora halophila:** grows optimally at salinities of 2.5 M
  NaCl; grows better on NaCl media than on media with other
  osmotically active solutes such as glucose or glycerol; may be
  restricted to salted foods
- **Hortaea werneckii:** facultatively halotolerant black yeast
  associated with hypersaline saltpans of 15-30% salinity
- **Phaeotheca triangularis:** an obligate halophile
- **Aspergillus halophilicus:** isolated from saline and arid-region
  soils
- **Scopulariopsis halophilica:** halophilic species from saline soils
