Perennials maintain living roots in the soil year-round, continuously exuding sugars and carbohydrates that feed soil bacteria, fungi, and other organisms. This constant food source supports a robust underground ecosystem -- the bacteria, fungi, protozoa, nematodes, earthworms, and other organisms that cycle nutrients and build soil structure.

Annual vegetables, by contrast, leave bare soil for months each year, interrupting these underground food webs and causing soil life populations to crash. When the soil is tilled for annual planting, fungal networks are destroyed and aerobic organisms are exposed to lethal conditions. The soil must rebuild its biology from scratch each season.
