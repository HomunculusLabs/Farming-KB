# Lichen Biogeochemistry

## What Are Lichens?

[[lichens]] are fungi that exist in facultative or obligate symbioses with
one or more photosynthesizing partners. The lichen [[fungal-biogeochemistry]]).

## Global Distribution

Lichens are among the most successful organisms on Earth:

- An estimated **6% of Earth's land surface** is covered by
  lichen-dominated vegetation
- They grow on practically all surface terrestrial environments
- Commonly thought of as pioneer colonizers of fresh rock outcrops
- Possibly one of the earliest life forms to occupy Earth's land
  surfaces

## Biogeochemical Roles

### Soil Formation
Lichens play a critical role in pedogenesis (soil formation):

- Physical breakdown of rock surfaces through hyphal penetration and
  thallus expansion
- Chemical dissolution of rock minerals through organic acid exudation
- Accumulation of organic matter as lichen biomass dies and decomposes
- Creation of microhabitats for other organisms to colonize

### Nutrient Retention and Distribution
Lichens play important roles in:

- **Carbon cycling**: Photosynthetic carbon fixation and subsequent
  carbon release through decomposition
- **[[fukuoka-textdoc-fertilizer-acidification-trace-element-deficiency]] cycling**: Retention and distribution of essential and
  non-essential elements

### Rock Weathering
Lichens contribute to rock weathering through:

- **Biomechanical action**: Physical penetration of hyphae into rock
  substrates along crystal boundaries and cracks
- **Biochemical action**: Exudation of oxalic acid and other organic
  acids that dissolve minerals
- **Colloidal complexation**: Metal-organic complexes formed by lichen
  substances enhance mineral dissolution

### Metal Accumulation
Lichens accumulate metals from their environment, including:

- **Essential metals**: Required for metabolic processes
- **Toxic metals**: Lead (Pb), copper (Cu), and others of environmental
  concern
- **Radionuclides**: Lichens can bioaccumulate radioactive isotopes,
  making them useful biomonitors for [[radioactive-contamination-and-fungi]]
