# Electrokinetic-Fungal Remediation

Electrokinetic-fungal remediation represents an innovative coupling of
electrokinetic soil processing with [[staycare-composting-as-a-fungal-bioremediation-strategy]] capabilities.
This hybrid technology applies low-intensity electric fields to contaminated
soils to enhance pollutant mobility and bioavailability while simultaneously
delivering fungal inocula, nutrients, and electron acceptors to targeted
treatment zones. Singh's treatment of this emerging approach highlights its
particular promise for low-permeability soils where conventional remediation
technologies fail.

## Electrokinetic Principles

Electrokinetics applies direct current (typically 1 to 2 V/cm) across soil
electrodes, generating three primary transport phenomena:

- **Electromigration**: Movement of charged ions toward oppositely charged
  electrodes. Metal cations migrate toward the cathode, while anions
  including chromate and cyanide migrate toward the anode.
- **Electroosmosis**: Bulk fluid flow from anode to cathode driven by the
  electrical double layer at soil particle surfaces. This phenomenon
  transports neutral and charged species through low-permeability soils.
- **Electrophoresis**: Movement of charged colloidal particles and
  microorganisms, including fungal spores and hyphal fragments, through
  the soil matrix.

These phenomena can be exploited to move contaminants toward collection
zones, distribute fungal inocula throughout the soil, and deliver
remediation amendments to otherwise inaccessible subsurface regions.

## Coupling Electrokinetics with Fungal Bioremediation
