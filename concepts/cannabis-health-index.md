# Cannabis Health Index

The Cannabis Health Index is a therapeutic framework developed by Uwe Blesching, Ph.D., that maps specific cannabis preparations, cannabinoid profiles, and terpene combinations to particular health conditions. Rather than treating cannabis as a monolithic therapy, the index takes an entourage-effect approach, recommending specific THC:CBD ratios, terpene profiles, and delivery methods for different conditions. This page summarizes key therapeutic applications and principles.

## Core Principle: The Entourage Approach

The Cannabis Health Index is built on the entourage effect — the principle that cannabis therapeutics are most effective when multiple cannabis compounds (cannabinoids, terpenes, flavonoids) work synergistically rather than as isolated molecules:

- **THC alone** is less therapeutic than whole-plant cannabis with equivalent THC
- **CBD alone** misses the benefits of THC and terpene synergies for many conditions
- **Specific terpene profiles** enhance or modify cannabinoid effects for particular therapeutic targets
- **Individual variation** means the optimal preparation varies between patients

See [[cannabis-terpene-profile]] for detailed terpene information and [[cannabis-thc-therapeutic-profile]] and [[cannabis-cbd-therapeutic-profile]] for individual cannabinoid profiles.

## THC:CBD Ratio Guide by Condition

| Condition | THC:CBD Ratio | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Chronic pain (general) | 1:1 to 2:1 | Balanced analgesia with reduced psychoactivity |
| Neuropathic pain | 1:1 | CBD enhances THC analgesia; anti-inflammatory |
| Inflammatory pain | 1:1 to 1:2 | Higher CBD for anti-inflammatory effect |
| Acute pain | 2:1 to 4:1 | Higher THC for rapid pain relief |
| Severe insomnia | 4:1 to 10:1 (THC dominant) | THC sedation; indica-dominant strains |
| Mild sleep difficulty | 1:1 | Balanced approach without heavy sedation |
| Anxiety (general) | 1:2 to 1:4 (CBD dominant) | CBD anxiolytic effect; minimal THC psychoactivity |
| Social anxiety | 1:4 to CBD only | Avoid THC-induced anxiety |
| PTSD | 1:1 to 1:2 | Nightmares (THC); anxiety and hypervigilance (CBD) |
| Depression | 2:1 to 1:1 | THC mood elevation; CBD neuroprotection |
| Epilepsy (seizures) | CBD dominant to CBD only | CBD anticonvulsant properties well-established |
| Cancer (symptom management) | 1:1 to 2:1 | Pain, nausea, appetite stimulation |
| Cancer (anti-tumor potential) | 1:1 | Some evidence for synergistic anti-tumor effects |
| Nausea/vomiting | 4:1 to 10:1 (THC dominant) | THC antiemetic is well-established |
| Appetite stimulation | 4:1+ (THC dominant) | THC directly stimulates appetite |
| Autoimmune conditions | 1:2 to 1:4 (CBD dominant) | CBD immunomodulation and anti-inflammatory |
| Fibromyalgia | 1:1 | Balanced approach for pain, sleep, mood |
| Migraine | 1:1 to 2:1 | During acute attack; CBD for prevention |
| IBD/Crohn's | 1:1 to 1:2 | Anti-inflammatory + gut motility effects |
| Arthritis | 1:1 to 1:2 | Topical application with balanced ratio |

## Delivery Methods by Condition

### Inhalation (Smoking, Vaporizing)
- **Onset**: Seconds to minutes
- **Duration**: 2-4 hours
- **Best for**: Acute symptoms — breakthrough pain, acute anxiety attacks, nausea, migraine onset
- **Advantages**: Rapid onset, easy dose titration
- **Disadvantages**: Respiratory irritation (smoking); shorter duration requires frequent re-dosing

### Oral/Sublingual (Tinctures, Edibles, Capsules)
- **Onset**: 30 min - 2 hours (oral); 15-45 min (sublingual)
- **Duration**: 4-8 hours (oral); 2-4 hours (sublingual)
- **Best for**: Chronic conditions requiring sustained relief — chronic pain, insomnia, anxiety, autoimmune
- **Advantages**: Longer duration, precise dosing with tinctures/capsules
- **Disadvantages**: Delayed onset makes titration harder; oral bioavailability is low (~6-20%)

### Topical (Creams, Salves, Balms)
- **Onset**: 15-45 minutes
- **Duration**: 2-6 hours
- **Best for**: Localized pain, inflammation, skin conditions, arthritis
- **Advantages**: No psychoactive effects, targeted application
- **Disadvantages**: Limited to surface-level conditions

### Suppositories
- **Onset**: 10-30 minutes
- **Duration**: 4-8 hours
- **Best for**: Pelvic pain, GI conditions, patients who cannot tolerate oral administration
- **Advantages**: High bioavailability (up to 70%), no first-pass metabolism, avoids psychoactive peak

## Conditions Treated: Summary

The Cannabis Health Index identifies cannabis as potentially therapeutic for over 100 conditions. The most evidence-supported categories:

1. **Pain**: Chronic pain, neuropathic pain, inflammatory pain, cancer pain, migraine
2. **Mental health**: Anxiety, PTSD, depression, insomnia, OCD
3. **Neurological**: Epilepsy (especially CBD for Dravet/Lennox-Gastaut syndromes), multiple sclerosis spasticity, Parkinson's, tremor
4. **Gastrointestinal**: IBD, Crohn's disease, nausea/vomiting, appetite loss
5. **Autoimmune/inflammatory**: Rheumatoid arthritis, fibromyalgia, lupus
6. **Cancer support**: Pain, nausea, appetite, sleep, anxiety; possible anti-tumor effects under investigation
7. **Dermatological**: Psoriasis, eczema, acne, pruritus (topical application)

## Drug Interactions

### Cytochrome P450 Interactions
Cannabis compounds (especially CBD) inhibit and induce cytochrome P450 enzymes, particularly CYP3A4 and CYP2C19:

- **Warfarin (blood thinner)**: CBD can increase warfarin levels — bleeding risk
- **Clobazam (anticonvulsant)**: CBD increases clobazam metabolite levels — sedation risk (this is actually leveraged therapeutically in some epilepsy protocols)
- **Valproate**: Possible liver toxicity when combined with CBD
- **Opioids**: Cannabis may enhance opioid analgesia, allowing dose reduction — but respiratory depression risk increases
- **Benzodiazepines**: Enhanced sedation
- **SSRIs**: Possible increased risk of sedation and serotonin syndrome
- **Immunosuppressants**: CBD may alter metabolism of tacrolimus and other immunosuppressants

### General Principles
- Start with low cannabis doses when combining with other medications
- Consult with a physician or pharmacist knowledgeable about cannabis-drug interactions
- Monitor for increased side effects of concurrent medications
- CBD has more drug interactions than THC due to CYP450 effects
- Grapefruit warning: If a medication carries a grapefruit interaction warning, it likely interacts with cannabis (same enzyme system)

## Important Limitations

- Individual responses vary dramatically — these are guidelines, not prescriptions
- The Cannabis Health Index synthesizes available research but many conditions lack large-scale clinical trials
- Cannabis should complement, not replace, conventional medical care
- Quality and consistency of cannabis products varies widely
- Legal status affects research quality and availability of standardized preparations
