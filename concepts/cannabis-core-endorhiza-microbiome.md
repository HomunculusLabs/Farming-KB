# Cannabis Core Endorhiza Microbiome

## Overview

The core endorhiza microbiome of cannabis (Cannabis sativa L.) refers to the
set of bacterial taxa that are consistently found within the root interior
across all studied cultivars, regardless of genotype, chemotype, or growing
conditions. This core community represents the stable, reproducible foundation
of the [[cannabis-root-microbiome]] and is thought to be primarily shaped by
soil-driven selection — the first tier of the two-tier selection model for
root [[edaphic-factors-cannabis-endorhiza-microbiome-assembly]]. Identification of these core taxa provides a baseline
for understanding which bacterial groups are reliably associated with cannabis
roots and which represent cultivar-specific additions reflecting host genotype
effects operating at a secondary level of community selection.

## Core Taxa Composition

The core [[Proteobacteria]] of cannabis includes representatives from several
major bacterial groups: Pseudomonas, Cellvibrio, Oxalobacteraceae,
Xanthomonadaceae, Actinomycetales, and Sphingobacteriales. These taxa were
present in the endorhiza of every cultivar examined in controlled studies,
including chemically distinct varieties such as Bookoo Kush, Burmese, Sour
Diesel, and Maui Wowie. Their consistent presence across genetically diverse
cultivars grown in identical soil strongly suggests that they are recruited
from the soil microbial pool through conserved plant-microbe interaction
mechanisms that are shared across all cannabis genotypes rather than being
specific to any particular chemotype or morphological variant of the species.

## Pseudomonas as a Dominant Core Member

Pseudomonas is one of the most prominent genera in the cannabis core endorhiza
community and is well-represented across all studied cultivars. Members of this
genus are known for their metabolic versatility, producing a wide range of
[[antifungal-secondary-metabolites-coprophilous-fungi]] including phenazines, pyoluteorin, and siderophores that
can influence both plant health and microbial [[dighton-aquatic-hyphomycete-conidia-community-dynamics]]. In the
cannabis root context, Pseudomonas species may contribute to plant defense
against soil-borne pathogens through competitive exclusion and the production
of [[antimicrobial-compounds-mushroom-mycelium]]. Their consistent enrichment across all cultivars
suggests that cannabis roots provide a favorable niche for Pseudomonas
colonization, possibly through the release of organic acids and amino acids
in root exudates that serve as carbon and nitrogen sources for these bacteria.

## Cellvibrio: The Non-Endophytic Anomaly

Cellvibrio occupies a unique position within the cannabis core endorhiza
community as the only member that is not typically considered an endophyte.
Cellvibrio species are primarily known as free-living soil bacteria and plant
biomass degraders, producing a suite of carbohydrate-active enzymes (CAZymes)
including cellulases, xylanases, and pectinases that break down plant cell
wall polysaccharides. The consistent detection of Cellvibrio within the root
interior of all cannabis cultivars is somewhat unexpected for a genus that is
not classically endophytic. One possibility is that Cellvibrio colonizes the
root apoplast — the space between plant cell walls — where it may have access
to partially degraded cell wall components released during root growth and
lateral root emergence. Its [[fungal-enzymatic-capabilities]] for polysaccharide
degradation may allow it to exploit this niche effectively in a way that
fewer specialized organisms can match.

## Phylogenetic Distribution: Gammaproteobacteria Dominance

The core [[actinobacteria-enrichment-cannabis-endorhiza]] community is phylogenetically skewed toward
Gammaproteobacteria and Alphaproteobacteria. Pseudomonas, Cellvibrio, and
Xanthomonadaceae are all Gammaproteobacteria, while the Sphingomonas-related
taxa found in some cultivars belong to Alphaproteobacteria. This
proteobacterial dominance is consistent with root microbiome profiles observed
in many other plant species, where Proteobacteria — particularly Alpha- and
Gammaproteobacteria — are consistently overrepresented in the root interior
relative to bulk soil. The reasons for this phylogenetic bias are not fully
understood but may relate to the metabolic flexibility of proteobacteria,
their ability to rapidly respond to root exudate signals, or specific
receptor-ligand interactions that facilitate root entry through natural
openings or wound sites in the root epidermis.

## Actinomycetales and Sphingobacteriales in the Endorhiza

Actinomycetales, particularly members of the genus Streptomyces and related
taxa, are also part of the cannabis core endorhiza community. These
filamentous bacteria are renowned for their production of bioactive secondary
metabolites and their role in suppressing soil-borne plant diseases. Their
presence in the cannabis root interior may contribute to [[mycorrhizal-pathogen-resistance-mechanisms]]
and could influence the overall chemical ecology of the root microbiome.
Sphingobacteriales, meanwhile, are represented by taxa that include
environmental generalists capable of degrading complex organic matter. Their
inclusion in the core community suggests that they fill a consistent functional
role in the cannabis root environment, possibly related to the degradation of
organic compounds or participation [[comparison-soil-protozoa-vs-nematodes-in-nutrient-cycling]] processes within the
root interior.
