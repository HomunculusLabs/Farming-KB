## Overview

The core endorhiza microbiome of cannabis (Cannabis sativa L.) refers to the
set of bacterial taxa that are consistently found within the root interior
across all studied cultivars, regardless of genotype, chemotype, or growing
conditions. This core community represents the stable, reproducible foundation
of the [[edaphic-factors-cannabis-endorhiza-microbiome-assembly]]. Identification of these core taxa provides a baseline
for understanding which bacterial groups are reliably associated with cannabis
roots and which represent cultivar-specific additions reflecting host genotype
effects operating at a secondary level of community selection.

## Cellvibrio: The Non-Endophytic Anomaly

Cellvibrio occupies a unique position within the cannabis core endorhiza
community as the only member that is not typically considered an endophyte.
Cellvibrio species are primarily known as free-living soil bacteria and plant
biomass degraders, producing a suite of carbohydrate-active enzymes (CAZymes)
including cellulases, xylanases, and pectinases that break down plant cell
wall polysaccharides. The consistent detection of Cellvibrio within the root
interior of all cannabis cultivars is somewhat unexpected for a genus that is
not classically endophytic. One possibility is that Cellvibrio colonizes the
root apoplast — the space between plant cell walls — where it may have access
to partially degraded cell wall components released during root growth and
lateral root emergence. Its [[actinobacteria-enrichment-cannabis-endorhiza]] community is phylogenetically skewed toward
Gammaproteobacteria and Alphaproteobacteria. Pseudomonas, Cellvibrio, and
Xanthomonadaceae are all Gammaproteobacteria, while the Sphingomonas-related
taxa found in some cultivars belong to Alphaproteobacteria. This
proteobacterial dominance is consistent with root microbiome profiles observed
in many other plant species, where Proteobacteria — particularly Alpha- and
Gammaproteobacteria — are consistently overrepresented in the root interior
relative to bulk soil. The reasons for this phylogenetic bias are not fully
understood but may relate to the metabolic flexibility of proteobacteria,
their ability to rapidly respond to root exudate signals, or specific
receptor-ligand interactions that facilitate root entry through natural
openings or wound sites in the root epidermis.

## Actinomycetales and Sphingobacteriales in the Endorhiza

Actinomycetales, particularly members of the genus Streptomyces and related
taxa, are also part of the cannabis core endorhiza community. These
filamentous bacteria are renowned for their production of bioactive secondary
metabolites and their role in suppressing soil-borne plant diseases. Their
presence in the cannabis root interior may contribute to [[comparison-soil-protozoa-vs-nematodes-in-nutrient-cycling]] processes within the
root interior.
