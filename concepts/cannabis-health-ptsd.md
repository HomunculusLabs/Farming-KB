## Scientific Evidence

A 2009 study from Haifa, Israel, demonstrated that the fear-processing
center of the brain (amygdala) contains a significant number of
endogenous cannabinoid receptors (CB1). When the synthetic cannabinoid
WIN55,212-2 was injected into the amygdala of rats, it modulated
anxiety responses, especially extinction learning via regulation of the
hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis. The study showed that
microinjecting WIN55,212-2 into the basolateral amygdala before
exposing rats to a stressor reversed the enhancing effects of stress on
inhibitory avoidance conditioning and its impairing effects on
extinction. This may explain why cannabinoids can modulate panic
responses both before and after traumatic events.

## Key Symptoms Addressed by Cannabis

PTSD manifests through a cluster of symptoms that cannabis has shown
potential to address. These include re-experiencing symptoms such as
nightmares, flashbacks, and intrusive thoughts, as well as hyperarousal
symptoms including hypervigilance, exaggerated startle response,
irritability, and sleep disturbance. The Nabilone study demonstrated
particular strength in addressing sleep-related symptoms, with patients
reporting cessation or reduction of nightmares, elimination of night
sweats, improved sleep duration and quality, and decreased daytime
flashbacks. These improvements in sleep quality are especially
significant since sleep disruption is one of the most debilitating and
persistent aspects of PTSD.

## Extinction Learning Mechanism

The endocannabinoid system's role in extinction learning is central to
its [[cannabis-health-thc-tetrahydrocannabinol]], binding with both CB1 and CB2 relatively
equally. Rimonabant is a CB1 antagonist known to reduce learned fear
responses. While the human case study relied on Nabilone, many patients
prefer indicas or indica-dominant hybrids with a lower THC:CBD/CBN
ratio, favoring the more relaxing and grounding properties of these
strains for managing PTSD symptoms.
