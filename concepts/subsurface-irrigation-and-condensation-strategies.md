### 5. Pit Evaporation Systems

Used as survival strategies in deserts, these can be adapted to grow useful plants. Green leafy material in a pit gives off water which condenses at night on the underside of a plastic cone (perforated at the centre to allow rain through). By adding fresh leaves, dry water, or urine, a nearby hardy tree can be sparsely watered, or enough freshwater for drinking obtained.

### 6. Closed Recycling Systems

When it is feasible to totally enclose plants, as in glasshouse and plastic tunnel systems, water losses are limited to ventilation. Condensation on inside surfaces can be led back to plants, and salt water flows can be evaporated and the vapour cooled to produce potable water for irrigation.

## Sea and Brackish Water Use

Extremely salty water or seawater can be evaporated under plastic to water root-zone plants via sand and peat layers. This approach is warranted only in extreme conditions in arid areas where no fresh water can be spared for irrigation.
