# MDMA

## Overview

MDMA (3,4-methylenedioxymethamphetamine), commonly known as "Ecstasy" or
"Molly," is the most widely recognized compound from Shulgin's research.
Documented in PIHKAL as compound #109, MDMA is an empathogen-entactogen
producing its characteristic effects primarily through [[serotonin]] release,
with additional effects on dopamine and norepinephrine. Its discovery and
popularization by Shulgin and his wife Ann represents one of the most
consequential events in psychoactive substance history.

First synthesized by Merck in 1912, MDMA's psychoactive properties remained
undiscovered until Shulgin resynthesized it in 1976. After finding its
effects remarkable, he introduced it to psychotherapists who recognized its
[[metzner-ayahuasca-therapeutic-potential]]. For several years before criminalization, MDMA was
used by hundreds of therapists for PTSD, anxiety, and relationship issues.

MDMA's effects are distinct from classical psychedelics: rather than
primarily visual hallucinations, it produces profound emotional openness,
feelings of love and connection, enhanced tactile sensation, and well-being.
Shulgin described it as a "window" rather than a "door," suggesting MDMA
allows users to examine their emotional landscape with clarity rather than
transporting them to an entirely different consciousness.

## Chemical Structure and Classification

MDMA's IUPAC name is 1-(1,3-benzodioxol-5-yl)-N-methylpropan-2-amine. It
consists of an amphetamine backbone with a methylenedioxy bridge at
positions 3 and 4 and an N-methyl group. The methylenedioxy bridge is the
defining feature of the MDxx family and is critical for empathogenic
activity.

The N-methyl group distinguishes MDMA from MDA and reduces direct 5-HT2A
receptor agonism, which is why MDMA produces less pronounced psychedelic
effects than its parent compound. MDMA's relationship to other compounds is
extensive: it is the N-methylated derivative of MDA and shares structural
features with numerous substituted amphetamines.

## Dosage and Administration

Shulgin's recommended oral dosage is 80 to 150 milligrams, with 100 to 120
mg considered standard. Onset occurs within 30 to 45 minutes, peaking at 1.5
to 2 hours. Total duration ranges from 3 to 6 hours, with primary
empathogenic effects concentrated in the first 2 to 3 hours.

Some users experience a post-use "crash" with mild depression, anxiety, and
fatigue for 1 to 3 days. Redosing is common but produces diminishing returns
as serotonin stores deplete. A common pattern is an initial dose followed by
a smaller booster at 1.5 to 2 hours, but more than two doses per session is
discouraged due to increased neurotoxicity risk.

## Effects Profile

MDMA's empathogenic effects are its hallmark: profound feelings of love,
connection, and emotional openness. Enhanced tactile sensation makes
physical contact particularly enjoyable, and the compound reduces social
anxiety while promoting deep, meaningful conversation.

Cognitive effects include enhanced music and art appreciation, reduced
defensiveness, increased willingness to examine emotional issues, and mental
clarity. Unlike classical psychedelics, MDMA does not typically produce
significant visual hallucinations, though some mild visual enhancement may
occur.

Physical effects include pronounced pupil dilation, jaw clenching, elevated
heart rate, increased blood pressure, mild hyperthermia, increased energy,
and appetite suppression.

## Shulgin's Commentary

Shulgin's PIHKAL commentary on MDMA is among the most extensive and
personally significant. He described MDMA as his "penultimate" achievement,
believing it had genuine therapeutic potential. His famous "window" vs.
"door" metaphor captures what made MDMA unique: observing and processing
emotions with unusual clarity rather than escaping into altered reality.
