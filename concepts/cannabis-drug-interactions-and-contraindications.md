## Cannabis Drug Interactions and Contraindications

Understanding how cannabis interacts with pharmaceutical medications and when it should be avoided is essential for safe therapeutic use. The [[blesching-cannabis-health-index-autoimmune-conditions]] introduces the concept of the "subjective therapeutic window" -- the dose range at which [[adverse-effects-and-harm-reduction-psilocybin]]. This window is:
- **Highly individual**: What works for one person may be too much or too little for another
- **Condition-dependent**: The optimal range differs for pain, anxiety, sleep, etc.
- **Time-variable**: The window can shift with tolerance, stress, food intake, and other factors
- **Narrow for some**: Particularly for anxiety, PTSD, and in [[fukuoka-stem-borer-thinning-effect-natural-pest-balance]]) and bleeding risk. Close INR monitoring is essential.
- **Clopidogrel**: CBD may interfere with activation of this antiplatelet drug.

### Sedatives and CNS Depressants
- **Benzodiazepines** (diazepam, lorazepam, alprazolam): Additive sedation; cannabis inhibits metabolism, increasing blood levels
- **Opioids**: Additive sedation and respiratory depression. However, cannabis may allow opioid dose reduction (opioid-sparing effect). Monitor carefully.
- **Sleep medications** (zolpidem, eszopiclone): Additive sedation risk
- **Alcohol**: Additive CNS depression; increased impairment

### Antidepressants
- **SSRIs/SNRIs**: CBD inhibits CYP2D6 and CYP2C19, potentially increasing levels. Watch for serotonin syndrome (rare but possible).
- **Tricyclic antidepressants**: CYP2D6 inhibition may increase blood levels and side effects
- **MAOIs**: Theoretical interaction risk; limited clinical data

### Antipsychotics
- **Clozapine**: CBD inhibits CYP1A2, which metabolizes clozapine. Blood levels may increase, raising seizure risk.
- Other antipsychotics metabolized by CYP2D6 may also be affected.

### Cardiovascular Medications
- **Beta-blockers**: CBD inhibition of CYP2D6 may increase levels
- **Calcium channel blockers**: CYP3A4 inhibition by THC may increase levels
- **Statins**: Some (simvastatin, lovastatin) metabolized by CYP3A4; levels may increase

### Anti-seizure Medications
- **Clobazam (Onfi)**: CBD significantly increases clobazam and its active metabolite levels. Dose reduction of clobazam is often needed when adding CBD.
- **Valproate**: CBD may increase valproate metabolite levels; liver function monitoring recommended.
- **Other AEDs**: Complex interactions possible; epilepsy patients using cannabis require close neurologist supervision.

### Immunosuppressants
- Cannabinoids have immunomodulatory effects that may interact with immunosuppressive drugs
- CBD may alter metabolism of tacrolimus and cyclosporine through CYP3A4 inhibition

### Chemotherapy Agents
- Many chemotherapy drugs are metabolized by CYP450 enzymes
- CBD may increase or decrease chemotherapy drug levels
- Coordination with oncologist is essential

## Contraindications

### Absolute Contraindications
- **Severe cardiovascular disease**: THC increases heart rate and may cause orthostatic hypotension
- **Psychotic disorders**: THC may exacerbate psychosis, schizophrenia, and other psychotic conditions
- **Pregnancy** (relative): THC crosses placenta and affects fetal development; CBD effects on fetal development are not fully known

### Relative Contraindications (Use with Caution)
- **History of substance use disorder**: Cannabis has lower addiction potential than many substances but dependence is possible
