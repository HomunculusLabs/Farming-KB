# Free-Range Poultry Systems

Free-range poultry systems integrate chickens, ducks, geese, and other fowl
into the permaculture landscape as active contributors to soil fertility, pest
control, and food production. In the Permaculture Designers Manual, Mollison
emphasizes that poultry are not merely livestock to be housed and fed but are
mobile processing units that convert forage, insects, and food waste into eggs,
meat, and manure. When properly managed within a designed system, poultry
reduce the need for external inputs, cycle nutrients through the landscape,
and perform essential ecological functions such as weed suppression, insect
control, and soil aeration.

## Poultry Functions in Permaculture

Every element in a permaculture system should perform multiple functions, and
poultry exemplify this principle. Chickens scratch and aerate soil, consume
insect pests and weed seeds, produce nitrogen-rich manure, and provide eggs
and meat. Ducks control snails and aquatic weeds in wetland areas and around
[[mollison-designers-sewage-aquaculture-systems]]. Geese graze on grassy weeds
in orchards and can serve as alarm animals. Guinea fowl are exceptional tick
and insect controllers. By selecting appropriate species and breeds for each
function, the designer creates a complementary poultry guild that services
multiple needs across the property.

## Chicken Tractors

### Design and Construction

A chicken tractor is a lightweight, movable enclosure that concentrates
poultry on a specific area of ground for a controlled period. It typically
consists of a framed structure with wire mesh walls and a partially or fully
covered roof, sized to house 4 to 12 birds. The tractor is moved daily or
every few days to fresh ground, where the birds scratch, manure, and clean
the soil surface. Mollison describes chicken tractors as one of the most
effective tools for converting rough pasture or weed-infested ground into
productive garden beds.

### Integration with Garden Beds

Chicken tractors can be run over future garden beds before planting,
incorporating manure and removing weed seeds and soil-dwelling pests. After
the tractor has processed an area for 1 to 2 weeks, the bed is ready for
planting with no additional fertilization required. This sequence integrates
directly with [[mollison-designers-house-garden-integration]] by preparing
Zone 1 beds efficiently. Tractors can also be moved through established
orchards in Zone 2 to clean up fallen fruit and control codling moth and
other pest populations.

### Forage Systems for Poultry

Mollison advocates designing dedicated forage systems that reduce or
eliminate the need for purchased grain feed. Perennial forage plants such
as comfrey (Symphytum spp.), [[tagasaste]] (Chamaecytisus proliferus), mulberry
(Morus spp.), and chicory (Cichorium intybus) provide high-protein green
fodder. Seed-producing annuals and perennials like amaranth, sunflower,
millet, and quinoa supply grain. A well-designed poultry forage system can
provide 30 to 70 percent of nutritional requirements depending on species
and season, significantly reducing feed costs and improving egg nutrition.

## Predator Protection

### Fencing and Shelter

Predator control is one of the most critical challenges in free-range
poultry management. Mollison recommends a layered defense strategy. The
primary barrier is a well-constructed perimeter fence with either buried
wire or an electric offset wire at the base to prevent digging predators.
Overhead netting may be necessary where raptors are a significant threat.
Night shelters should be secure, lockable enclosures with solid floors or
wire mesh buried below ground level to prevent access by foxes, raccoons,
and other nocturnal predators.

### Livestock Guardian Animals

Integration with guardian animals provides an additional layer of
protection. Dogs trained as livestock guardians, geese (which are naturally
vigilant and aggressive toward intruders), and donkeys or alpacas in larger
systems can significantly reduce predation losses. Mollison notes that the
permaculture approach favors biological solutions over physical barriers
where possible, and guardian animals fit this principle by adding another
productive element to the system rather than relying solely on fencing
infrastructure.

## Species and Breed Selection

Breed selection should match the system design and local conditions. For
egg production in forage-based systems, hardy dual-purpose breeds such as
Rhode Island Red, Australorp, Sussex, or Plymouth Rock perform well. Light
breeds like Leghorns are efficient converters but are less suited to rough
conditions. For meat production on pasture, Cornish Cross hybrids grow
rapidly but may require more supplementation. Heritage breeds are preferred
for long-term self-reliance as they reproduce naturally, forage actively,
and maintain genetic diversity.

Ducks such as Khaki Campbell and Indian Runner are excellent foragers in
wet areas and around water features. Muscovy ducks are effective insect
control specialists and require minimal water access. Geese are efficient
grassland managers and can maintain lawns and orchard floors with minimal
supplementary feeding.

## Nutrient Cycling and System Outputs

Poultry manure is one of the most concentrated and readily available sources
of nitrogen and phosphorus in a permaculture system. A single chicken
produces approximately 30 to 40 kilograms of manure per year, rich in NPK
and micronutrients. When distributed through chicken tractor rotations or
deep litter systems in poultry houses, this manure becomes the primary
fertility source for garden beds and orchards. The integration of poultry
with [[mollison-designers-fuelwood-forestry-systems]] through poultry forage
in alley cropping or silvopastoral systems creates additional nutrient
cycling pathways across the property.

## Summary

Free-range [[poultry-systems-in-permaculture]] design integrate chickens, ducks,
geese, and other fowl as multi-functional elements that provide eggs, meat,
manure, pest control, and soil cultivation. Chicken tractors, designed
forage systems, layered predator protection, and appropriate breed selection
are the key design tools. When properly integrated with gardens, orchards,
and broader landscape elements, poultry become essential drivers of nutrient
cycling and productive efficiency in the permaculture system.
