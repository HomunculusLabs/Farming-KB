## Citrus Tree Natural Form Discovery
The natural form of citrus trees, and especially the Satsuma orange,
was more difficult to determine. Fukuoka first tried applying natural
[[natural-farming-methods-comparison]] to an established grove of Satsuma orange trees that
had been trimmed into a wineglass shape about six or seven feet tall.
When he simply discontinued pruning, large numbers of scaffold
branches and laterals grew out at once, crisscrossing and growing in
strange twisted shapes. Disease sites formed where branches tangled,
and one dying branch caused others to wither. The tree produced a full
crop only every other year. Abandoning the trees to their own devices
was a sure path to ruin.
He then tried heavy pruning and thinning, but this also failed. The
natural forms shown in technical journals, all hemispherical with
scaffold branches meandering upwards, were not true natural forms at
all but shapes of abandoned trees. To discover the true form, Fukuoka
planted citrus seed directly and observed the trees. He also looked at
hardier [[query-best-citrus-varieties-for-different-climates]] such as summer orange and shaddock, which
clearly showed a central leader type form. When he reported his
findings that the natural form of citrus is a central leader type,
this created a stir among specialists but was laughed off by farmers.
The natural form varies by variety: early-ripening Satsuma orange
takes a low pyramidal form, late-ripening Satsuma a tallish cypress-
like conical form, and summer orange, shaddock, persimmon, chestnut,
pear, apple, and loquat all take a tall, cedar-like conical form.
## Practical Non Pruning Orchard Management
The natural form provides several practical advantages for orchard
management. First, it permits tree growth best suited to cultivation
conditions. No branch or leaf is wasted, enabling maximum sunlight
exposure and maximum yields. Second, the erect central trunk causes
little entanglement with neighboring trees, so pruning gradually
decreases and disease pressure remains minimal.
Third, oblique sunlight penetrates into the interior of conical
central leader trees, whereas open-center systems with inverse
triangle crowns reduce light penetration to the base, inviting
withering of branches and disease. Fourth, the natural form provides
the best distribution of nutrients to scaffold branches, giving a
full fruit harvest each year. Fifth, the root system closely resembles
the aboveground portion, creating a deep, healthy, resistant tree.
Fukuoka acknowledged that simply abandoning a tree does not work.
Trees must be protected from the local environment so they assume
their natural shape. This means shielding young trees from wind,
pests, and competing vegetation. The goal is to draw a mental picture
of the natural form and make every effort to protect the tree so it
thrives and puts out good fruit year after year. Pruning only creates
a need for more pruning, but if the grower is determined to grow
trees not in need of pruning, they will bear fruit without it.
## Comparison of Garden vs Mountain Trees
The confusion between natural form and neglected form is central to
understanding why non-pruning has been dismissed. Many people have a
vague idea of the natural form as something akin to the shape of a
neglected tree. But there is a world of difference between the two.
A low, twisted pine on a windswept seashore is not the natural form.
A lone cryptomeria with branches drooping in all directions may not
show its true form either. Plants grow under an incredible diversity
of conditions and respond to injury from bird, beast, and insect.
