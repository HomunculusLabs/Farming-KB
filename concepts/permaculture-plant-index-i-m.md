## Permaculture Plant Index I-M

This index covers plants from the letter I through M as catalogued in *Permaculture Plants: A Selection*. Entries are organized by common or scientific name with their primary uses and page references.

## I

### Inga and Legume Trees
- **[[inga-edulis]]** (Ice cream bean) — pages 28, 136. Amazonian nitrogen-fixing tree with sweet edible pulp. Widely used in agroforestry for shade and soil improvement.
- **Inga spp.** — page 28. Diverse genus of neotropical legume trees forming the backbone of traditional shade-grown [[mollison-designers-shade-grown-coffee-and-cacao-systems]].

### Ivies and Climbers
- **Ivy** (*[[hedera-helix]]*) — pages 114, 117. Evergreen woody climber for walls, ground cover, and wildlife habitat. Provides year-round shelter for insects and birds.

## J

### Jujube and Japanese Fruits
- **Jujube** (*Ziziphus jujuba*, syn. *Z. zizyphus*) — pages 61, 118, 137. Also called Chinese date. Deciduous fruit tree producing sweet, date-like fruits when dried. Extremely drought and heat tolerant; medicinal properties in traditional Chinese medicine.
- **Japanese chestnut** (*Castanea crenata*) — page 33. Important chestnut species in East Asian cultivation; resistant to chestnut blight.
- **Japanese loquat** (*Eriobotrya japonica*) — page 62. Evergreen fruit tree with large fuzzy leaves and sweet yellow-orange fruits. Medicinal leaves used in traditional Asian medicine.
- **Japanese medlar** — page 62. See loquat.
- **Japanese raisin tree** (*Hovenia dulcis*) — pages 77, 117. Deciduous tree whose swollen peduncles (fruit stalks) are sweet and edible, resembling raisins.
- **Japanese sawtooth oak** (*Quercus acutissima*) — page 113. Asian oak species for timber and acorn production.
- **Japanese maple** (*Acer palmatum*) — page 111. Ornamental tree prized for autumn foliage; small-scale timber and craft use.
- **Jams** — pages 33, 59, 62, 63, 65, 66, 67, 69, 72, 74, 76, 77, 131, 133. Fruit preserves made from numerous species including cherry, mulberry, loquat, guava, and citrus.

### Jarrah and Australian Timbers
- **Jarrah** (*Eucalyptus marginata*) — pages 93, 95, 96, 116. Premium Western Australian hardwood: extremely durable, termite resistant, and dimensionally stable. Used for flooring, furniture, and outdoor construction.

### Jellies and Confections
- **Jellies** — pages 59, 61, 62, 65, 66, 68, 69, 74, 75, 76, 77, 106, 108, 131, 133, 137. Fruit jellies from quince, crabapple, guava, feijoa, and other high-pectin fruits.

### Jojoba
- **Jojoba** (*[[simmondsia-chinensis]]*) — page 45. Desert shrub producing liquid wax esters used in cosmetics, lubricants, and pharmaceuticals. Extremely drought tolerant; important dryland crop.

### Juglans (Walnuts)
- **Juglans australis** (Argentine walnut) — page 32. South American walnut species.
- **Juglans boliviana** (Bolivian walnut) — page 32. Tropical walnut of the Andean foothills.
- **Juglans hindsii** (Northern California black walnut) — pages 32, 112. Rootstock species for commercial walnut orchards; also produces edible nuts.
- **Juglans neotropica** (Ecuadorian/Andean walnut) — page 32. High-altitude tropical walnut for timber and nuts.
- **Juglans nigra** (Eastern black walnut) — pages 32, 112. Premium North American timber and nut tree. Rich-flavored nuts; valuable lumber for furniture.
- **Juglans regia** (English/Persian walnut) — pages 33, 42, 112. The most widely cultivated walnut species worldwide; excellent nuts and timber.
- **Juglans venezuelensis** — page 33. Venezuelan walnut species.
- **JUGLANDACEAE** — pages 32, 38. The walnut and hickory family, encompassing valuable timber and nut-producing trees.

### Juniper
- **Juniper** (*[[juniperus-horizontalis]]*) — page 136. Low-growing ground cover juniper for slopes and rock gardens.

## K

### Karkalla and Coastal Plants
- **Karkalla** (*Carpobrotus rossii*) — page 131. Australian native pigface producing edible fruits and leaves; coastal ground cover and sand stabilizer.
- **Kanji** — page 89. Traditional fermented food or drink; referenced in context of food preservation.

### Karri
- **Karri** (*Eucalyptus diversicolor*) — pages 95, 116. One of the tallest trees in the world; premium Western Australian timber with straight grain and rich color.

### Kang Kong and Aquatic Vegetables
- **Kang kong** (*[[leucaena-leucocephala]]** (River tamarind/Lead tree) — page 24. Fast-growing nitrogen-fixing legume tree for fodder, green manure, and firewood. Pods and leaves provide high-protein livestock feed. Widely planted in tropical agroforestry.
- **Leucaena diversifolia** — page 25. Cold-tolerant Leucaena species for higher elevations.
- **Leucaena esculenta** — page 25. Edible-seeded species consumed as food in Mexico.
- **Leucaena glauca** — page 24. Synonym for L. leucocephala.
- **Leucaena lanceolata** — page 26. Australian native Leucaena species.
- **Leucaena macrophylla** — page 25. Large-leaved species for tropical reforestation.
- **Leucaena pulverulenta** — page 26. Powdery-leaved species for semi-arid conditions.
- **Leucaena retusa** — page 26. Round-leaved Leucaena species.
- **Leucaena shannoni** — page 26. Species from the Yucatan Peninsula.
- **Leucaena trichodes** — page 26. Hairy-podded species.

### Lucerne and Legumes
- **Lucerne** (*Medicago sativa*, alfalfa) — pages 15, 65, 114, 116, 118. Premier perennial forage legume; deep taproot drought tolerance; nitrogen fixation; bee forage. The "queen of forages."
- **Lupins** (*Lupinus* spp.) — page 116. Ornamental and agricultural legumes; some species produce edible seeds. Blue lupin (*L. angustifolius*) is a significant grain crop in Australia.

### Livistona Palms
- **[[macadamia-integrifolia]]** — page 31. The primary commercial macadamia species; smooth-shelled nuts with high oil content.
- **Macadamia tetraphylla** — page 31. Rough-shelled macadamia species; more cold tolerant than M. integrifolia.
- **Macadamia spp.** — page 31. Australian native nut trees producing the highest-value commercial nut crop per hectare.
- **PROTEACEAE** — page 31. Plant family including macadamia, grevillea, banksia, and hakea.

### Melaleuca (Tea Trees)
- **Melaleuca spp.** — pages 96, 117, 129. Australian genus of aromatic trees and shrubs; many produce valuable essential oils.
- **Melaleuca uncinata** (Broombush) — page 19, 129. Important species for arid land restoration; also harvested for brush fencing.
- **Melaleuca lanceolata** — pages 112, 129, 136. Variable species from coastal heath to inland mallee; essential oil production.
- **Melaleuca cymbifolia** — page 129. Aromatic melaleuca species.
- **Melaleuca ericifolia** — page 136. Swamp paperbark; essential oil production.
- **Melaleuca nesophila** — page 136. Showy-flowered melaleuca for ornamental hedges.
- **Melaleuca stypheloides** (Prickly paperbark) — page 129. Riverine species with distinctive papery bark.

### Moringa
- **[[comparison-moringa-oleifera-vs-moringa-stenopetala]]** (Horseradish tree) — page 90. Multi-purpose tropical tree: leaves are 25% protein with all essential amino acids; pods edible as vegetables; seeds purify water; drought tolerant; fast growing. Called the "miracle tree" for its nutritional density.

### Mulberry
- **Mulberry** (*Morus* spp.) — pages 59, 118. Deciduous fruit trees producing sweet berries; essential food source for silkworms.
- **Morus alba** (White mulberry) — page 59. Primary species for silkworm cultivation; sweet fruit.
- **Morus nigra** (Black mulberry) — page 59, 136. Rich, tart fruit considered the finest for culinary use.
- **Morus rubra** (Red mulberry) — page 59. North American native with deep red-purple fruit.

### Mallee Eucalypts
- **Mallee** — page 95. Growth form where eucalypts produce multiple stems from a lignotuber; adapted to harsh conditions.
- **Mallee honey myrtle** — page 129. See melaleuca species.

### Medicinal Plants (M Section)
- **Medicinal** — pages 102, 136. Numerous species have documented medicinal properties including neem (87), moringa (90), jujube (61), and various palms (102).
- **Meliodogyne javanica** — page 88. Root-knot nematode; major pest managed through crop rotation, resistant varieties, and biological controls.
