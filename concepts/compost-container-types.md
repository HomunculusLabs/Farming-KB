# Compost Container Types

## Overview

Compost containers keep compost moist right up to the edges and keep the area tidy. Whether you build or buy, the key requirement is easy access to the compost when it is ready for the garden. Several types are available, from manufactured bins to simple homemade solutions.

## Manufactured Containers

### Wooden Compost Bin (Beehive/Stacking)
- Commercial kits assembled on site
- Stacking bins enable gradual building one section at a time
- Allow easy access to finished compost

### Plastic Compost Bin
- Best for small gardens
- Small amount of air enters through the bottom
- Lid keeps contents dry and retains heat

### Plastic Barrel Container
- Made from large plastic fruit-concentrate barrels
- Cut off top and bottom with a sharp knife; keep one cut end as a lid
- Drill 1in (2.5cm) holes around the barrel — about one per sq ft (30 sq cm)

## Homemade Containers

### Wire-and-Post Container
- Suitable only where the heap can be hidden from view
- Hammer four stakes to make a 3ft (1m) square
- Staple ~12ft (4m) of wire mesh, 3ft (1m) deep, to the outside of the stakes
- Tie large pieces of cardboard to the inside of the wire for insulation

### Brick-Built Compost Bin
- Suitable only for permanent placement (never to be moved)
- Stagger bricks to allow air into the heap
- Front made of wooden slats (like the homemade wooden bin)
- Fix wood shims inside walls; slide slats in for front panel removal

### Homemade Wooden Compost Container

**Materials needed:**
- 4 x 3ft (1m) lengths of 2x4in (5x10cm) wood for uprights
- 19 x 3ft (1m) lengths of wood for the sides
- 5 x 2ft 6in (75cm) lengths for the front panels
- 4 x 2ft 6in (75cm) wood shims
- 2 small pieces of wood + strong nails (~4 per panel)

**Construction steps:**
1. Lay two uprights parallel, 2ft 6in (75cm) apart. Nail a side plank 3in (7.5cm) from the bottom of each post. Add five more planks, all butting up against each other. Build a second matching wall.
2. Stand walls parallel at right angles to a wall. Nail a support piece across the top of each upright. Working from the bottom up, nail six pieces across the back.
3. Remove the support panel, turn the box around, and nail the front board across the uprights 3in from the bottom.
4. Nail two shims onto each upright's side edge, spaced to slide front panels between them. Nail a small piece across the bottom to prevent panels from sliding out.
5. Slide all front panels in to test fit; cut down if necessary.
6. Paint the entire container with water-based wood preservative, including cut edges. Let air dry.
7. Slide front panels into position. Tie string across the top to prevent sides from bulging outward when filled.

Use rough-sawn lumber (cheaper and looks fine). Old floorboards work well for sides. Sources include demolition contractors (often half the price of new), but avoid reclaimed lumber which can be overpriced.

## Managing Your Compost

- Good compost is brown and crumbly with a sweet smell (like woods in fall)
- Reality is often more variable with semi-rotted fibrous material — still improves soil, just takes longer
- Grass cuttings must always be mixed with coarser material (weeds, shredded newspaper, straw) to prevent slime
- Straw is excellent for mixing with grass cuttings — soak thoroughly for an hour before adding
- Newspaper: cut into 1in strips, soak in diluted seaweed before use; use ~1 part newspaper to 4 parts grass
- Kitchen scraps: nothing cooked (avoids vermin); cut large root pieces smaller; cover with grass cuttings or weeds
- Old natural-fiber clothes can go on the heap if cut into strips
- In a hot year, expect two binfuls from each bin in summer plus one in spring
