## Overview

One-carbon metabolism is the network that transfers single-carbon units among folate cofactors, methionine cycle intermediates, nucleotide synthesis reactions, and methylation systems.
It links nutrition to DNA synthesis, amino acid metabolism, redox balance, neurotransmitter production, detoxification, development, and epigenetic regulation.
The phrase does not refer to a single linear pathway; it names an integrated set of cytosolic, mitochondrial, and nuclear reactions that handle carbon units at several oxidation states.
Folate cofactors carry one-carbon groups as methyl, methylene, methenyl, formyl, or formimino units, each suited to different biosynthetic reactions.
Methionine metabolism converts those carbon units into S-adenosylmethionine, the major methyl donor for DNA, RNA, protein, lipid, and small-molecule methylation.
Because the network depends on folate, vitamin B12, vitamin B6, riboflavin, choline, betaine, serine, glycine, and methionine, it is highly sensitive to diet and genetic variation.

## Folate Cofactors

Dietary folates and synthetic folic acid are converted into tetrahydrofolate derivatives that act as carriers of one-carbon units.
Tetrahydrofolate can accept a carbon from serine, glycine, histidine catabolism, formate, or mitochondrial metabolism.
5,10-methylene-tetrahydrofolate is used for thymidylate synthesis, which converts dUMP to dTMP and supports DNA replication and repair.
10-formyl-tetrahydrofolate donates carbon atoms during de novo purine synthesis, contributing positions in the purine ring.
5-methyl-tetrahydrofolate donates a methyl group to homocysteine through the vitamin B12-dependent enzyme methionine synthase.
The interconversion of folate forms is controlled by enzymes such as serine hydroxymethyltransferase, methylenetetrahydrofolate dehydrogenase, and methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase.
Folate trapping can occur when vitamin B12 deficiency prevents 5-methyl-tetrahydrofolate from remethylating homocysteine, functionally limiting other folate pools.

## Methionine Cycle

The methionine cycle begins when methionine is adenylated by methionine adenosyltransferase to form S-adenosylmethionine, often abbreviated SAM.
SAM donates methyl groups to many acceptors through methyltransferase reactions and becomes S-adenosylhomocysteine.
S-adenosylhomocysteine is hydrolyzed to homocysteine and adenosine, but the reaction is reversible and high homocysteine can inhibit methylation by raising SAH.
Homocysteine can be remethylated to methionine by methionine synthase using 5-methyl-tetrahydrofolate and methylcobalamin.
In liver and kidney, betaine-homocysteine methyltransferase provides a folate-independent remethylation route using betaine derived from choline.
The balance between SAM and SAH is sometimes used as a methylation potential, although local enzyme activity and compartmentation also matter.
Methionine restriction, high methyl demand, vitamin deficiency, or inherited enzyme defects can shift this cycle in clinically significant ways.

## Transsulfuration and Redox Links

Homocysteine is not only recycled; it can also enter the transsulfuration pathway.
Cystathionine beta-synthase condenses homocysteine with serine to form cystathionine, a vitamin B6-dependent reaction.
Cystathionine gamma-lyase then forms cysteine, alpha-ketobutyrate, and ammonia.
Cysteine supports protein synthesis, taurine synthesis, hydrogen sulfide signaling, and especially glutathione production.
Glutathione is a major cellular redox buffer and conjugating agent in xenobiotic metabolism, so one-carbon metabolism indirectly affects oxidative-stress defense and detoxification.
SAM can activate cystathionine beta-synthase, linking high methyl donor status to increased disposal of homocysteine through transsulfuration.
This coupling helps explain why methylation, sulfur amino acid metabolism, and redox biology are difficult to separate experimentally.

## Mitochondrial One-Carbon Flux

Mitochondria generate one-carbon units from serine, glycine, sarcosine, dimethylglycine, and other substrates.
Mitochondrial serine hydroxymethyltransferase and the glycine cleavage system are major sources of one-carbon units in many proliferating cells.
Formate produced in mitochondria can exit to the cytosol and feed purine synthesis, thymidylate synthesis, or methionine-cycle remethylation.
This compartmental separation lets cells coordinate energy metabolism with nucleotide production and methylation demand.
Cancer cells often increase serine uptake, serine synthesis, mitochondrial folate flux, and glycine cleavage to support rapid biomass production.
However, one-carbon flux varies by tissue, diet, oxygen availability, mitochondrial function, and genetic background.
Mitochondrial impairment can therefore appear as altered nucleotide pools, redox stress, or abnormal methylation rather than simply as low ATP.

## Nucleotide Synthesis

One-carbon metabolism is essential for making DNA because both purines and thymidylate require folate-derived carbon units.
Thymidylate synthase uses 5,10-methylene-tetrahydrofolate to methylate dUMP into dTMP, producing dihydrofolate as a byproduct.
Dihydrofolate reductase regenerates tetrahydrofolate, which is why antifolate drugs can block DNA synthesis by depleting reduced folate cofactors.
Purine biosynthesis uses 10-formyl-tetrahydrofolate in two transformylation steps, building carbon atoms into the purine ring.
Rapidly dividing tissues such as bone marrow, intestinal epithelium, embryos, tumors, and immune cells are especially vulnerable to folate disruption.
Folate or B12 deficiency can produce megaloblastic anemia because DNA synthesis slows while cell growth continues.
This is a central reason the pathway matters in pharmacology, oncology, pregnancy, and toxicology.

## Methylation Biology

SAM-dependent methyltransferases modify DNA, histones, RNA, phospholipids, creatine precursors, catecholamines, arsenic species, and many other substrates.
DNA methylation can regulate gene expression, imprinting, transposon silencing, and developmental programs, although methylation changes must be interpreted in context.
Histone methylation can either activate or repress chromatin depending on the residue and degree of methylation.
RNA methylation affects translation, splicing, stability, and stress responses.
Phosphatidylethanolamine methylation contributes to phosphatidylcholine synthesis, especially in liver.
Because methylation reactions consume SAM and produce SAH, high demand or impaired homocysteine clearance can feed back on many methyltransferases.
One-carbon metabolism therefore provides biochemical currency for epigenetic regulation without being a simple on-off switch for gene expression.

## Pharmacology and Toxicology

Antifolate drugs exploit the dependence of proliferating cells and microbes on folate-mediated nucleotide synthesis.
Methotrexate inhibits dihydrofolate reductase and is used in cancer, autoimmune disease, and ectopic pregnancy at different dosing regimes.
Trimethoprim and pyrimethamine target microbial or protozoal folate pathways with selective toxicity based on enzyme differences.
5-fluorouracil forms a complex with thymidylate synthase and reduced folate, blocking thymidylate production.
Leucovorin can rescue normal cells after high-dose methotrexate or enhance fluoropyrimidine activity by stabilizing the thymidylate synthase inhibitory complex.
Nitrous oxide can inactivate vitamin B12-dependent methionine synthase, creating acute one-carbon disruption in susceptible patients.
Arsenic methylation, acetaminophen glutathione demand, and other detoxification processes can intersect indirectly with methyl donor and sulfur amino acid status.

## Clinical and Nutritional Relevance

Folate deficiency, vitamin B12 deficiency, and some inborn errors of metabolism can elevate homocysteine and disturb DNA synthesis or methylation.
Neural tube defect prevention with folic acid supplementation is one of the most successful public-health applications of one-carbon biology.
Vitamin B12 deficiency can cause neurological injury even when folate supplementation corrects anemia, so treating folate status alone can mask the underlying problem.
MTHFR variants, especially common reduced-function alleles, can alter folate distribution and homocysteine levels, but their clinical interpretation is often overstated in consumer testing.
Choline and betaine can support remethylation independently of folate in selected tissues, making dietary context important.
Assessment may include complete blood count, serum B12, methylmalonic acid, folate measures, homocysteine, and clinical signs rather than one isolated marker.
Population fortification policies must balance prevention of deficiency against concerns about unmetabolized folic acid, cancer context, and masking B12 deficiency.

## Common Misinterpretations

One-carbon metabolism is often described as methylation, but methylation is only one output of the network.
High homocysteine is a risk marker in many settings, but lowering homocysteine does not automatically reverse disease mechanisms.
More methyl donor intake is not always better because nucleotide synthesis, redox balance, cell proliferation, and epigenetic state can respond differently.
Genotype alone rarely predicts pathway status without diet, renal function, medications, age, pregnancy, inflammation, and microbiome context.
Pathway diagrams can imply simple directionality, while real cells use reversible reactions, compartmentalized pools, enzyme regulation, and substrate competition.
These caveats make one-carbon metabolism a systems problem rather than a supplement checklist.
