## Overview

One-carbon metabolism is the network that transfers single-carbon units among folate cofactors, methionine cycle intermediates, nucleotide synthesis reactions, and methylation systems.
It links nutrition to DNA synthesis, [[cho-fish-amino-acid-preparation]] metabolism, redox balance, neurotransmitter production, detoxification, development, and epigenetic regulation.
The phrase does not refer to a single linear pathway; it names an integrated set of cytosolic, mitochondrial, and nuclear reactions that handle carbon units at several oxidation states.
Folate cofactors carry one-carbon groups as methyl, methylene, methenyl, formyl, or formimino units, each suited to different biosynthetic reactions.
Methionine metabolism converts those carbon units into [[taurine]] synthesis, hydrogen sulfide signaling, and especially glutathione production.
Glutathione is a major cellular redox buffer and conjugating agent in xenobiotic metabolism, so one-carbon metabolism indirectly affects oxidative-stress defense and detoxification.
SAM can activate cystathionine beta-synthase, linking high methyl donor status to increased disposal of homocysteine through transsulfuration.
This coupling helps explain why methylation, sulfur amino acid metabolism, and redox biology are difficult to separate experimentally.

## Mitochondrial One-Carbon Flux

Mitochondria generate one-carbon units from serine, glycine, sarcosine, dimethylglycine, and other substrates.
Mitochondrial serine hydroxymethyltransferase and the glycine cleavage system are major sources of one-carbon units in many proliferating cells.
Formate produced in mitochondria can exit to the cytosol and feed purine synthesis, thymidylate synthesis, or methionine-cycle remethylation.
This compartmental separation lets cells coordinate energy metabolism with nucleotide production and methylation demand.
Cancer cells often increase serine uptake, serine synthesis, mitochondrial folate flux, and glycine cleavage to support rapid [[dighton-fungal-biomass-production-aquatic-ecosystems]].
However, one-carbon flux varies by tissue, diet, oxygen availability, mitochondrial function, and genetic background.
Mitochondrial impairment can therefore appear as altered nucleotide pools, redox stress, or abnormal methylation rather than simply as low ATP.

## Nucleotide Synthesis

One-carbon metabolism is essential for making DNA because both purines and thymidylate require folate-derived carbon units.
Thymidylate synthase uses 5,10-methylene-tetrahydrofolate to methylate dUMP into dTMP, producing dihydrofolate as a byproduct.
Dihydrofolate reductase regenerates tetrahydrofolate, which is why antifolate drugs can block DNA synthesis by depleting reduced folate cofactors.
Purine biosynthesis uses 10-formyl-tetrahydrofolate in two transformylation steps, building carbon atoms into the purine ring.
Rapidly dividing tissues such as bone marrow, intestinal epithelium, embryos, tumors, and immune cells are especially vulnerable to folate disruption.
Folate or B12 deficiency can produce megaloblastic anemia because DNA synthesis slows while cell growth continues.
This is a central reason the pathway matters in pharmacology, oncology, pregnancy, and toxicology.

## Methylation Biology

SAM-dependent methyltransferases modify DNA, histones, RNA, phospholipids, creatine precursors, catecholamines, arsenic species, and many other substrates.
DNA methylation can regulate gene expression, imprinting, transposon silencing, and developmental programs, although methylation changes must be interpreted in context.
Histone methylation can either activate or repress chromatin depending on the residue and degree of methylation.
RNA methylation affects translation, splicing, stability, and stress responses.
Phosphatidylethanolamine methylation contributes to phosphatidylcholine synthesis, especially in liver.
Because methylation reactions consume SAM and produce SAH, high demand or impaired homocysteine clearance can feed back on many methyltransferases.
One-carbon metabolism therefore provides biochemical currency for epigenetic regulation without being a simple on-off switch for gene expression.

## Pharmacology and Toxicology

Antifolate drugs exploit the dependence of proliferating cells and microbes on folate-mediated nucleotide synthesis.
Methotrexate inhibits dihydrofolate reductase and is used in cancer, autoimmune disease, and ectopic pregnancy at different dosing regimes.
Trimethoprim and pyrimethamine target microbial or protozoal folate pathways with selective toxicity based on enzyme differences.
5-fluorouracil forms a complex with thymidylate synthase and reduced folate, blocking thymidylate production.
Leucovorin can rescue normal cells after high-dose methotrexate or enhance fluoropyrimidine activity by stabilizing the thymidylate synthase inhibitory complex.
