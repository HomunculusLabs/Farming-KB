# Medicinal Mushroom Cardiovascular and Metabolic Effects

Beyond their well-documented immunomodulatory and anticancer properties, medicinal mushrooms demonstrate significant cardiovascular, cholesterol-lowering, antidiabetic, and antithrombotic activities. These effects have been supported by both animal studies and limited clinical trials, and align closely with traditional Chinese medicine applications.

## Cholesterol-Lowering Effects

### Mechanisms of Action

Two primary mechanisms underlie mushroom hypocholesterolemic activity:

1. **HMG-CoA reductase inhibition** — the rate-limiting enzyme in cholesterol biosynthesis. [[medicinal-mushroom-guide-cordyceps]] produces mevinolin (lovastatin), a potent HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor, particularly concentrated in the lamellae/gills
2. **Dietary fibre effects** — mushroom β-glucans and chitin act as dietary fibres (10-50% dry matter) that absorb carcinogenic substances and promote their excretion, while also binding bile acids

### Species-Specific Evidence

Six mushroom species have demonstrated total cholesterol reduction:
- [[medicinal-mushroom-guide-cordyceps]] — via ganoderic acids Mf, B, and T-O
- [[medicinal-mushroom-guide-cordyceps]]
- [[mushroom-training-shiitake-cultivation]] — via eritadenine (see below)
- [[medicinal-mushroom-guide-cordyceps]] — via lovastatin production; 4% dried mushroom in high-cholesterol diet reduced serum and liver cholesterol in rats
- [[medicinal-mushroom-guide-cordyceps]]
- [[tremella-fuciformis]]

### Eritadenine (Lentinus edodes)

Eritadenine from shiitake lowers blood serum cholesterol through a distinct mechanism:
- Does not inhibit cholesterol biosynthesis
- Accelerates excretion of ingested cholesterol and its metabolic decomposition
- Lowers the phosphatidylcholine (PC) to phosphatidylethanolamine (PE) ratio in liver microsomes
- May impair VLDL cholesterol secretion at high dosages
- Clinical studies in Japan showed positive cholesterol decreases in young women and people over 60

### Clinical Evidence

Limited clinical trials with 15-20 g dried [[medicinal-mushroom-guide-cordyceps]] supplement daily for one month reduced hypercholesterolemia in many (but not all) patients. [[psk-psp-polysaccharide-peptides]] also causes decreases in LDL cholesterol in hyperlipidemia patients.

## Blood Pressure Effects

Three species demonstrated arterial blood pressure lowering:
- [[medicinal-mushroom-guide-cordyceps]] — via ACE-inhibitory ganoderic acids B, D, F, H, K, S, Y
- [[medicinal-mushroom-guide-cordyceps]]
- Tricholoma mongolicum — via a lectin compound

## Antiplatelet and Antithrombotic Effects

Six species demonstrated reduced blood platelet binding (in vitro):
- [[medicinal-mushroom-guide-cordyceps]] — activity due to adenosine
- [[medicinal-mushroom-guide-cordyceps]]
- Calyptella sp. — via 5-hydroxy-3-vinyl-2(5H)-furanone
- Kuehneromyces sp. — via kuehneromycine B
- Neolentinus adhaereus — via 2-methoxy-5-methyl-1,4-benzoquinone
- Panus sp. — via panudial and nematolon

**Caution**: [[mushroom-training-shiitake-cultivation]] water extracts lessen blood platelet effectiveness in coagulation. People who bleed easily or take anticoagulants should exercise caution with shiitake extracts or LEM.

## Antidiabetic Effects

Six species showed glycemia reduction in animal models:

### Insulin-Dependent Diabetes
- [[psilocybin-mushroom-distribution-worldwide]]
- Agrocybe aegerita — via two polysaccharides: AG-HN1 (high MW, glucose) and AG-HN2 (low MW, fructose/galactose/glucose/mannose)
- Cordyceps sinensis — via CS-F30 polysaccharide (galactose/glucose/mannose)
- Tremella aurantia — via TAP (Tremella acidic polysaccharide)

### Non-Insulin-Dependent Diabetes
- [[medicinal-mushroom-guide-cordyceps]] — reduces glycemia, insulinemia, and blood triglycerides

### Non-Diabetic Animals
- [[coprinus-comatus-detailed]]

Mushroom extracts of [[mushroom-training-shiitake-cultivation]], [[medicinal-mushroom-guide-cordyceps]], and Phellinus linteus clearly demonstrated blood glucose and triglyceride lowering in streptozotocin-induced diabetic rat models.

## Nutritional Profile

Mushrooms are nearly ideal for cardiovascular-preventive diets due to:
- High dietary fibre content
- High protein content
- Low fat content and low calorific value
- Rich in sterols and microelements
- These properties align with traditional Chinese medicine recommendations for cardiovascular health
