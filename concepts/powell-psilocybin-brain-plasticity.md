# Psilocybin, the Brain, and Neural Plasticity

Powell dedicates significant portions of *The [[powell-psilocybin-solution-ecological-cognition-theory]]* to explaining the neurological
basis of the psilocybin experience, framing it as a window into understanding how the brain
constructs consciousness. His treatment, while written for a general audience, provides a detailed
account of neuronal architecture, neurotransmission, and the serotonergic system.

## The Neuronal Brain

Powell describes the brain as consisting of approximately 13 billion individual information-
processing nerve cells, or neurons — comparable to the number of stars in our galaxy and more
than twice the number of people alive on the planet. These neurons form the essential "wetware"
of the brain and, massed together with other cells that provide support and energy, they form
the spongy grey matter residing within our skulls.

He emphasises that the association between this grey matter and consciousness is indisputable:
scramble the brain through trauma and consciousness similarly becomes scrambled; electrically
excite the brain of a patient undergoing surgery and definite experiences are evoked; introduce
certain chemical substances into the brain and consciousness is altered. What distinguishes
*Homo sapiens* from primate cousins is the sheer size of our brains and the mental abilities
they grant: self-awareness, language, complex social behaviour, foresight, [[maitake-problem-solving-troubleshooting]],
and metaphysical musing.

## The Tree-Like Structure of Neurons

Structurally, each neuron has four main components. Powell uses the analogy of a big tree
suspended in mid-air: the roots are the dendrites, which receive signals from other neurons;
the lower bulbous trunk is the soma (cell body), where signals are integrated; the long thin
upper trunk is the axon, which transmits the integrated result; and the topmost branches are
the terminal fibres, which pass signals onward to other neurons.

The dendrites serve to receive information in the form of signals from other neurons. These
neuronal signals travel to the soma where they are integrated. The singular result of this
integration is then passed to the axon, which in turn passes the information to the terminal
fibres. Neurons transmit informational impulses in an orderly, well-defined manner — signals
progress through the neuronal architecture in one direction only.

## Digital Firing and Information Processing

Powell explains that neurons operate digitally: they can either fire or not fire, with no
intermediate state. There is no room for doubt or indecision, only a logically determined
discrete firing or non-firing signal according to what other neurons in their vicinity are
doing. Firing means the neuron sends an electrochemical impulse — a rapidly travelling wave
of electrical excitation — down its axon to its terminal fibres.

This binary operation is compared to the "bit" components inside computers, which store
information by being either on or off, active or inactive. Neurons thus appear to operate
digitally, processing information embodied in their electrochemical activity — their state
of either firing or not firing.

## Synaptic Transmission

The synapse is the gap between the terminal fibres of one neuron and the dendrites of the
next. When an electrochemical impulse reaches the terminal fibres, it causes a neurotransmitter
substance to be released into the synaptic cleft. Powell notes that a few tens of thousands of
neurotransmitter molecules are released, cross the gap, and bind to receptors on the receiving
neuron's dendrites.

If enough receptors are activated, the receiving neuron fires its own electrochemical impulse,
continuing the chain of information transmission. This is the fundamental mechanism by which
information is processed in the brain, and it is precisely at these serotonergic synapses that
psilocybin exerts its effects. Powell acknowledges that a major conceptual flaw in our
understanding of neurons is the distinct lack of detail about what actually happens at the
synaptic gap.

## Serotonin and Environmental Chemistry

Psilocybin acts upon neurons which utilise serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine) as their
neurotransmitter. Serotonin is a chemical messenger allowing individual neurons to communicate
with one another so that information can be transmitted and processed. The various compounds
employed by brains have evolved over millions of years and are determined by chemicals available
in the environment, particularly from raw materials in food.
