## Anti-Cancer Polysaccharides from Medicinal Mushrooms

The main antitumour compounds isolated from [[medicinal-mushrooms]] have been identified as water-soluble β-D-glucans with heterosaccharide chains or β-D-glucan-protein complexes (proteoglycans). This concept explores the chemistry, [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-spore-extraction-methods]], and structure-activity relationships of these bioactive polysaccharides.

## Overview

"Hot water extracts of many mushrooms used in traditional Chinese medicine have long been said to be efficacious in the treatment of various diseases including many forms of cancer." It is only within the last three decades that chemical technology has been able to isolate the relevant compounds.

"Many species of mushrooms have been found to be highly potent immune system enhancers, potentiating animal and human immunity against cancer." While at least **30 mushroom species** have yielded compounds with pronounced anticancer actions in xenografts, only a small number have undergone objective clinical assessment in humans.

## Polysaccharide Types

### β-D-Glucans (Water-Soluble)
The most widely distributed and studied class. Composed of repetitive structural units of β-D-glucose. Water-soluble β-D-glucans with heterosaccharide chains of xylose, mannose, galactose, or uronic acid.

### β-D-Glucan-Protein Complexes (Proteoglycans)
Polypeptide chains or small proteins linked to polysaccharide β-D-glucan chains. Often show enhanced bioactivity compared to pure glucans.

## Structure-Activity Relationships

### Molecular Weight
- Antitumour activity is directly related to **molecular weight**
- Higher molecular weight molecules show greater immunomodulatory activity
- Only higher molecular weight molecules apparently form **triple helical structures**

### Branching
- Degree of branching significantly affects activity
- β-D-glucopyranosyl branch units stabilize triple helical structures (Saito et al., 1991)
- The (1→3)-β-glucan backbone with (1→6) branches is the most common active configuration

### Solubility
- **Increased water solubility favours enhanced antitumour activity**
- Location of substituent groups on the helix surface is important
- Hydrophilic groups on the outside surface of the helix appear critical

### Helical Conformation
"There is good evidence to propose that both [[lentinan]] and Schizophyllan are active only when they exist in a single helical structure." The antitumour activity resides in the helical conformation of the glucan backbone, possibly triple-stranded.

"Chemical modification can be achieved by oxido-reductohydrolysis (Smith degradation) and also by formolysis." Some positive improvements in activity have been recorded but it is still at a very early stage—comparable to the early penicillin story.

## Key Clinical Polysaccharide Compounds

### Lentinan (from Lentinus edodes / Shiitake)
- Water-soluble β-D-glucan from [[bloomfield-fungal-fruiting-body-morphology-ecological-function]]
- Active only in helical conformation
- Degraded by internal β-glucanase activity during storage at room temperature
- Widely used in Japan as cancer immunotherapy

### Schizophyllan (from Schizophyllum commune)
- β-glucan produced from [[liquid-culture]] broth
- Also known as Sonifilan or SPG
- Considerable anti-cancer activity in xenograft and clinical practice
- Active only in helical conformation

### PSK ("Krestin") (from Trametes versicolor)
- Protein-bound polysaccharide from [[stamets-forest-mycelial-biomass-topsoil-douglas-fir]]
- Acts both directly on tumour cells and indirectly through host immunity
- Accounted for 25% of total anti-cancer agent expenditure in Japan (1987)

### PSP (from Trametes versicolor)
- Polysaccharide-peptide from mycelial biomass
- Powerful immunostimulant and anti-cancer agent
- Different from PSK but derived from the same species

### Grifolan (from Grifola frondosa / Maitake)
- β-glucan from fruiting body
- Additional compounds from mycelial biomass: heteroglucan protein, manogalactofucan

## Extraction Methods

### Fractional Preparation (Mizuno, 1999)
Sequential extraction from fruiting body or mycelium:
1. **80% ethanol treatment** → separates low molecular weight substances
2. **Water extraction** → neutral polysaccharides (F I)
3. **1% ammonium oxalate** → acidic polysaccharides (F II)
4. **5% NaOH** → alkali-soluble polysaccharides (F III)
5. **pH adjustment and ethanol precipitation** → purified fractions

### Purification by Chromatography
1. **Ion-exchange chromatography** (DEAE-Cellulose column) → separates neutral and acidic polysaccharides
2. **Gel filtration** (Toyopearl HW-65F) → size-based separation
3. **Affinity chromatography** (Con A-Toyopearl column) → separates α-glucans from β-glucans

### Improved Lentinan Extraction (Yap and Ng, 2001)
- Ethanol precipitation and freeze-drying in liquid nitrogen
- 87.5% purity achieved
- Less time-consuming, more efficient, relatively [[coleman-quick-hoops-low-cost-season-extension-structures-design]] compared to original methods

## Concentration Changes During Growth and Storage

### Developmental Stage Effects
"Concentration of polysaccharides in certain medicinal mushroom species can be related to the stage of development of the mushroom fruitbody."

- **L. edodes and G. frondosa**: Increase in immunomodulating activity during [[cytokinin-plant-growth-and-development]], followed by decrease at final stages of maturation
- Activities paralleled by concentration changes in Lentinan and Grifolan respectively

### Post-Harvest Storage Effects
"Immunomodulating activities of extracts from L. edodes decreased rapidly when the mushrooms had been stored at 20°C for 7 days while no decrease occurred at low temperature storage (1°C and 5°C)."

The decrease was related to degradation of Lentinan by internal **β-glucanase activity**.

### Practical Implications
"It becomes imperative that medicinal mushrooms should be harvested at the optimum β-glucan concentration in the fruitbody and also that the harvested fruitbodies should be stored at correct temperature conditions before processing or consumption."

"These results must surely compromise the use of medicinal mushrooms derived from distant parts which must involve inadequate environmental conditions and subsequent loss of β-glucans."

### Fermentation Advantage
"Where polysaccharides are produced by fermentation processes it is much easier to harvest at optimum production points as is already practised in other fermentations such as with antibiotics."
