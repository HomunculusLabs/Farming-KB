Seed quality is the foundation of farming success in Cho's Natural
Farming system. A strong seed produces a healthy plant throughout
its entire life cycle. Cho observed that seeds grown under harsh
conditions develop superior toughness, strength, and adaptability
compared to seeds bred in controlled artificial environments.

Modern commercial seeds, by contrast, are bred in artificial
conditions and treated with chemicals. Cho argued that these
seeds cannot grow without human protection and fail to reach
their full potential even when given scientific treatments. They
have been weakened by dependency on chemical inputs and
controlled environments that shield them from natural selection
pressures.

## The Problem with Commercial Seeds

Cho identified several problems with the conventional seed
industry. Seeds in the market are bred for uniformity and
yield under chemical-intensive conditions, not for resilience
or nutritional quality. They are soaked in chemical treatments
that suppress natural microbial associations and create
dependency on synthetic fertilizers and pesticides.

The conventional practice of soaking seeds for long periods
before broadcast causes seeds to lose nutrients through
leaching. Seeds that have been pre-soaked in water for extended
durations lose vitality because essential compounds dissolve
into the soak water. This is a particular problem for farmers
who follow traditional seed preparation methods without
understanding the biochemistry involved.

Cho's assessment was that market seeds are "almost chemical
products" that need treatment to recover their natural powers.
The goal of Natural Farming seed treatment is not to add
foreign substances but to restore the seed's innate vitality
that has been suppressed by industrial processing.

## The Seed/Seedling Solution (SES)

Natural Farming addresses seed vitality through a brief
treatment using a Seed/Seedling Solution (SES). The key
innovation is the short treatment period compared to
conventional long-soaking methods. This brief exposure is
sufficient to restore microbial associations and prime the
seed for germination without causing nutrient loss.

The base SES formula combines four core Natural Farming inputs
diluted in one liter of water. FPJ at two milliliters provides
growth enzymes and amino acids from fermented plant material.
BRV at two milliliters supplies organic acids that help break
seed dormancy and stimulate metabolic activity. OHN at one
milliliter contributes herbal nutrients that boost disease
resistance. The dilution ratio is intentionally precise.

## Conditional Additions to the Base Formula

Cho specified two conditional additions that farmers apply based
on the condition of the seedlings being treated. When seedlings
are small and weak, one milliliter of FAA (Fish Amino Acid) is
added to the base solution. The amino acids in FAA provide a
readily assimilable nitrogen source that helps weak seedlings
build initial biomass without the metabolic cost of synthesizing
nitrogen compounds from simpler nutrients.

When seedlings are overgrown and soft, one milliliter of
Water-Soluble Calcium (WsCa) is added instead. The calcium
strengthens cell walls and promotes structural rigidity in
plants that have grown too rapidly under high nitrogen
conditions. This corrective application prevents lodging and
improves the seedling's ability to withstand transplanting
stress and environmental challenges.

These conditional additions illustrate Cho's principle of
diagnosing the plant's condition before applying inputs. The
same base formula serves different purposes depending on what
the seedling needs, demonstrating the flexibility within the
Natural Farming system.

## Treatment Duration Guidelines

The duration of SES treatment varies by seed type. Cho
provided specific guidance for different categories of seeds,
though the exact duration tables depend on the seed size,
hardness of the seed coat, and the crop species. The
fundamental principle is brevity: treatment should be long
enough to restore vitality but short enough to prevent
nutrient leaching from the seed.

This contrasts sharply with conventional practices that
involve soaking seeds for twelve to twenty-four hours or
longer. Cho's research indicated that extended soaking
depletes the seed's stored reserves, particularly water-
soluble vitamins, enzymes, and growth regulators that are
essential for vigorous early growth.

## Soil Treatment Solution (SOS) Protocol

Complementing the seed treatment, Cho developed a Soil
Treatment Solution (SOS) that prepares the planting
environment. Good, fertile soil is the basis of productive
land. While conventional farming relies on deep tilling and
soil conditioners, Natural Farming uses earthworms for
tillage, straw-and-leaves mulching for weed suppression,
and IMOs for soil health management.

The SOS formula for one acre includes IMO at 500 ml, FPJ
at 500 ml, OHN at 500 ml, BRV at 500 ml, LAB at 300 ml,
WCaP at 300 ml, and FAA at 300 ml. These are combined with
fifty kilograms of charcoal, five hundred kilograms of soil,
three liters of seawater, two hundred liters of water, and
one thousand kilograms of farm yard manure.

When using IMO-4, compost is scattered over the field so
that the topsoil is barely covered with a minimum of 1500
kilograms per acre. Cho emphasized that deeper soil is
richer, so compost should not be buried. IMO-4 should be
applied in small lumps to help field soil build aggregated
structure rapidly.
