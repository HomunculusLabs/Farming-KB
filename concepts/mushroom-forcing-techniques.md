### The Improved Forcing Method

Cuthill's key discovery was spawning and finishing beds as
soon as heat reached 100 degrees or more. The spawn was
inserted into the dung, the mould immediately applied,
trodden hard, watered, and plastered. By placing cold mould
on the surface and treading hard, the remaining heat was
driven downwards. By morning, heat had nearly subsided to
the bottom, injuring no spawn.

### Advantages

- Ammonia and manure strength were not wasted
- Latent heat could be revived with hay or straw coverings
- Crops were produced two weeks earlier than the old plan
- Beds were more productive over their entire bearing period

Great care was required not to force the crop sooner than six
weeks, even with this accelerated method.

## Half-Quantity Mould Forcing

For a very early crop, Cuthill recommended spawning at 90 to
100 degrees and covering with only half the usual mould
quantity. This reduced insulation allowed faster development,
forcing mushrooms in four to five weeks. The trade-off was
that the bed would not be lasting. For sustained production,
full moulding was superior.
