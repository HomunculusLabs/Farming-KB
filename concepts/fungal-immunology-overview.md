## Fungal Immune Evasion Mechanisms

Pathogenic fungi have evolved sophisticated strategies to evade host immune detection, relevant to both agricultural and human contexts:

- **Capsule formation** (Cryptococcus) -- masks fungal PAMPs from immune recognition
- **Dimorphic switching** -- alters surface antigen expression
- **Melanin production** -- scavenges reactive oxygen species produced by immune cells
- **Biofilm formation** -- creates physical barrier against immune cells and antifungal agents
- **Hyphal transition** -- Candida albicans switches from yeast to invasive hyphal form
- **Thermotolerance mechanisms** -- allowing human-pathogenic fungi to survive at body temperature

These evasion strategies highlight the evolutionary arms race between fungi and host immune systems. In agricultural settings, fungal pathogens such as Fusarium and Verticillium species use many of these same mechanisms to overcome plant defenses, making this knowledge relevant to understanding disease resistance in [[soil-food-web-nutrient-cycling]]. The underground fungal networks that connect plants can serve as conduits for chemical signals that warn neighboring plants of pathogen attack, triggering preemptive immune responses before infection occurs. This "wood wide web" of inter-plant communication depends on the same fungal immune signaling pathways studied in human mycology, illustrating the deep evolutionary conservation of fungal immunological interactions across kingdoms.
