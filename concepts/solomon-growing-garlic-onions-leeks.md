# Solomon on Growing Garlic, Onions, and Leeks

From [[solomon-gardening-when-it-counts-index]] (2005) by Steve Solomon.

## Garlic

### Overview

Garlic is one of the most rewarding homestead crops. It requires minimal attention, stores for months, and provides both culinary and medicinal value. There are many varieties of garlic; some only suit tropical or subtropical climates and don't perform well farther from the equator.

### Planting

Garlic is planted in autumn (1-2 months before the ground freezes) in most temperate climates. Plant individual cloves 2-3 inches deep, pointed end up, about 6 inches apart in rows 12 inches apart. Each clove produces one bulb. Solomon recommends selecting the largest cloves from the best bulbs for planting stock.

### Growing

Garlic is relatively low-demand. Mulch heavily after planting to suppress weeds and insulate through winter. In spring, remove mulch to let soil warm, then reapply as the plants grow. Garlic benefits from moderate fertility but does not need heavy nitrogen, which can promote excessive leaf growth at the expense of bulb formation.

### Harvest

Harvest when the lower 3-4 leaves have died back but 5-6 green leaves remain. If you wait until all leaves are brown, the cloves may begin separating and the bulb won't store well. Dig (don't pull) carefully, then cure in a warm, dry, well-ventilated place for 2-3 weeks.

### Storage

After curing, trim roots and stems. Store at cool room temperature (50-60 degrees F) with moderate humidity. Properly cured garlic stores 6-8 months. Softneck varieties generally store longer than hardneck types.

### Seed Stock

Always save your own planting stock from the best bulbs. Commercial garlic is often treated to prevent sprouting. Over years of selection, your garlic will become increasingly adapted to your specific soil and climate.

## Onions

### Bulbing Onions

Onions are daylength-sensitive. Choosing the right variety for your latitude is critical:

- **Long-day varieties** (above 35 degrees latitude): Form bulbs when days reach 14-16 hours.
- **Short-day varieties** (below 35 degrees latitude): Form bulbs at 12-14 hours.
- **Intermediate-day varieties**: Work in a middle range.

Planting the wrong type for your latitude results in lush green tops but no bulbs, or premature bolting.

### Growing from Seed vs Sets

Solomon prefers growing from seed rather than sets. Onion sets are small bulbs grown from seed the previous year; they often bolt (go to seed) the first year, producing a tough central stalk and a bulb that doesn't store well. Seed-grown onions are more reliable.

Start onion seeds indoors 8-10 weeks before last frost, or direct-seed in the garden as early as soil can be worked.

### Spacing

Solomon recommends wider spacing than conventional guides: 4-6 inches between plants in rows 12-18 inches apart. This produces larger bulbs that store better.

### Fertilizer

Onions are medium-demand. They need steady moisture and consistent nutrition, especially in their early growth stages. Solomon recommends applying COF before planting.

### Harvest and Storage

Harvest when tops fall over naturally. Lift and cure in a warm, dry place with good air circulation for 2-3 weeks. Onions that don't develop a dry papery skin won't store well. Store at cool temperatures (35-40 degrees F) with low humidity.

### Topsetting and Potato Onions

Topsetting (walking) onions and potato onions are perennial multiplier onions that reproduce by division rather than seed. They are extremely hardy, disease-resistant, and reliable. Potato onions produce clusters of bulbs at the base; topsetting onions produce bulbils at the top of the stalk that bend over and plant themselves. These are excellent low-maintenance crops for the self-sufficient garden.

## Leeks

Leeks are grown for their thick, white stems. They are a cool-season crop that tolerates frost. Start seed indoors 8-10 weeks before last frost, or direct-seed in early spring. Transplant into trenches 6-8 inches deep; as the leek grows, gradually fill in the trench (hilling up) to blanch the stems.

Leeks are medium-demand. They need steady moisture and fertile soil. Harvest from autumn through winter. In mild climates, leeks can overwinter in the garden. In cold climates, mulch heavily or store in a root cellar.
