In the 1970s, Mollison and Holmgren began to develop a set of
techniques for holistic landscape designs modeled after nature yet
including humans. Permaculture's vision is of people participating in
and benefiting from an abundant, nurturing natural world.

## Core Principles

Though permaculture practitioners design with plants, animals,
buildings, and organizations, they focus less on those objects
themselves than on the careful design of relationships among them.
Interconnections are what turn a collection of unrelated parts into a
functioning system, whether it's a community, a family, or an
ecosystem.

The aim of permaculture is to create ecologically sound, economically
prosperous human communities. It is guided by ethical principles:
- Care for the earth
- Care for people
- Sharing the surplus

From these stem design guidelines. Some are based on understanding of
nature, such as: "Each element should perform several functions," and
"Use natural plant succession to create favorable sites and soils."
Others are borrowed from stable, long-term societies: "Use renewable
resources," and "Begin the garden at your doorstep."

## Key Design Concepts

### Multifunctionality
Nothing in nature does just one thing. This principle, where each
interconnected piece plays many roles, distinguishes an ecologically
designed garden from conventional ones. A tree chosen for shade can
also offer nuts or fruit for people and wildlife, attract pollinators,
harvest rainwater, pull dust from the air, and build soil when its
leaves fall.

### Redundancy
Each role in a natural landscape is supported by many players. Each
insect pest is pursued by a hungry army of natural predators. If one
predator falls down on the job, others pick up the slack. This
redundancy shrinks the risk of failure.

### Connectedness
In nature each piece not only plays many roles, each role is supported
by many players. The pieces are all ready and waiting to be linked
together using nature's interconnectedness as a model.

## Ecological Gardens

What the author calls "ecological gardens" draw much from permaculture.
These gardens meld the best features of wildlife gardens, edible
landscapes, and conventional flower and vegetable gardens. They are
based on relatively new concepts such as permaculture and ecological
design, yet use time-tested techniques honed by indigenous people,
restoration biologists, organic farmers, and cutting-edge landscape
designers.

Ecological gardens combine low environmental impact, low maintenance
once established, and high yields with elegant aesthetics. They are
filled with beautiful plants that have many uses, providing fruit and
vegetables, medicinal and culinary herbs, colorful blossoms, soil-
building mulch, protection from pests, and habitat for wildlife.

With good design, these gardens need only infrequent watering, and the
soil renews itself rather than demanding heavy fertilizing. These are
living ecosystems, designed using nature's rules, and boasting the
lushness and resilience of the natural environment.

## Beyond Gardening

Permaculture is about much more than gardening. Since permaculture
emphasizes the role of plants and animals in human life, many people
have come to it through gardening and agriculture. But the principles
apply to the design of towns, cultures, and economies as well.

Most of the gardeners featured in Gaia's Garden consider themselves
permaculturists, and many of the techniques described were first
assembled in Mollison's books on permaculture. The ideas encourage
gardeners to reduce their ecological impact, and lead the way, through
beautiful, lush landscapes, for others to do the same.

## The Role of the Gardener

In a permaculture-designed garden, the gardener works with nature
rather than against it. By building strong connections among the
plants, soil life, beneficial insects and other animals, and the
gardener, the gardener weaves a resilient, natural web. Each organism
is tied to many others. Nature has a broad back, and with a little
ingenuity and a change in viewpoint, a gardener can shift plenty of
labor to this willing partner.

The goal is to restore the natural cycles that have been broken by
conventional landscape design and agriculture. A forest or meadow
looks perfect and stays nearly disease free with no care at all,
while a garden demands arduous hours of labor. This happens because
most gardens ignore nature's rules. Permaculture gardens follow
those rules instead.

## A Practical Example

A deer-deflecting hedge demonstrates permaculture thinking in action.
Instead of a single-purpose fence, a food hedge combines bush cherries,
Manchurian apricots, currants, wild plums, and gooseberries. On the
outside, wildlife plants feed deer. On the inside, domestic fruit
varieties are grafted onto wild rootstock. Nitrogen-fixing Siberian
pea shrub and buffaloberry build soil. Deep-rooted chicory, yarrow,
and daikon radish pull nutrients from the subsoil. Mulch-producing
comfrey and cardoon conserve water. Every element serves multiple
functions.
