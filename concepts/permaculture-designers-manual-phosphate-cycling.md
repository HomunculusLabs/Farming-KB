About 45 ppm phosphate in soil is needed for grains (optimum pH 6.0-6.5). Pelleting seeds with basic superphosphate provides phosphorus. Phosphorus deficiency reduces growth in animals by depressing their appetite for herbage.

## The Phosphate Crisis

### Global Inequity
Europe and North America consume 75% of all phosphatic fertilisers used (and get least return from this input because of overuse, over-irrigation, and poor soil economy). Most western soils are now over-supplied, with a very large unused soil bank of phosphorus. If we really wanted to reduce world famine, the redirection of these surplus phosphates to the poor soils of Africa and India would do it.

### Losses from Agriculture
- Uncut forests may lose 0.1 kg/ha/year
- Clear-cropping can lose 100 kg/ha/year or more -- a 1000 times increase
- These losses are never accounted for in logging and clearing

### Non-Agricultural Waste
About 15-20% of the inessential use of phosphorus is in detergents. The older potash soaps are the answer, or the recovery of phosphorus from greywater rather than a one-way trip via a sewer to the sea.

## Conservation Approaches

Conservation farming loses about one-half to one-third the phosphorus of contemporary agriculture, even without non-tilage. Non-tilage farming would lose even less.

### Permaculture Strategies
- Bioregional farming with wastes returned to soils
- Home gardening with organic residues
- Regional food supply with waste recycling
- Devoting 30% of land surface to trees for sustainable phosphate cycling
- Seabird and salmon recycling (we tend to reduce their numbers by denying them breeding grounds)
- Recovery of phosphorus from greywater systems
- Use of pigeon flocks, fish ponds, and bone meal

## Seabird and Salmon Recycling

Seabirds and salmon do try to recycle phosphorus back to us, but we tend to reduce their numbers by denying them breeding grounds. The preservation of seabird colonies and salmon runs is therefore a critical phosphate conservation strategy.
