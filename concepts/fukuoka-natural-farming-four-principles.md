# Fukuoka Natural Farming Four Principles

Masanobu Fukuoka distilled natural farming into four foundational principles. Together, they describe an agricultural system that produces food without human intervention beyond planting and harvesting.

## Principle 1: No Cultivation (No Plowing/Tilling)

### Why
- Tilling destroys soil structure, kills beneficial organisms, and exposes organic matter to oxidation
- Plowed soil is vulnerable to erosion, compaction, and weed seed germination
- Nature doesn't plow — forests and prairies build soil without disturbance

### How It Works
- Earthworms, roots, and soil organisms create channels naturally
- Soil aggregates form from fungal glues and root exudates
- Mulch layer protects soil surface and feeds organisms

### In Practice
- [[comparison-seed-starting-indoors-vs-direct-seeding-techniques]] into untilled soil
- Broadcast seeds onto mulched surface
- Use seed balls (clay + seeds + compost) for direct broadcasting

## Principle 2: No Chemical Fertilizer (No Added Fertilizer)

### Why
- [[cervantes-chemical-fertilizers-guide]] bypass the soil food web, creating dependency
- [[hamilton-organic-fertilizers-and-plant-nutrition]] still represent an external input
- Natural systems cycle nutrients through decomposition

### How It Works
- Returning all plant residues to the soil surface maintains fertility
- Green manure crops (clover, vetch) fix nitrogen from air
- Mycorrhizal fungi make phosphorus and micronutrients available
- Decomposing mulch releases nutrients slowly as plants need them

### In Practice
- Leave all crop residues on the field as mulch
- Grow white clover as a living ground cover (fixes nitrogen)
- Rotate crops to balance nutrient demands
- Accept lower but stable yields

## Principle 3: No Weeding by Tillage or Herbicide

### Why
- Weeds are not enemies — they protect soil, cycle nutrients, and indicate soil conditions
- Tilling to remove weeds creates ideal conditions for new weed seeds to germinate
- Herbicides kill soil life and create resistant weeds

### How It Works
- Dense planting and ground covers (white clover) suppress weeds naturally
- Straw mulch prevents weed seed germination by blocking light
- In rice paddies: flooding suppresses most weeds naturally

### In Practice
- Maintain a permanent ground cover of clover or straw
- Don't worry about all weeds — some are beneficial
- Cut weeds at base rather than pulling roots (preserves soil structure)

## Principle 4: No Pesticides

### Why
- Pests appear when the natural balance is disrupted by human intervention
- Healthy plants in healthy soil resist pest damage naturally
- Pesticides kill [[comparison-beneficial-insects-guide-vs-integrated-pest-management]] along with pests, creating dependency

### How It Works
- Biodiversity provides natural pest control (predator-prey balance)
- Healthy plants produce their own defensive compounds
- A diverse ecosystem is inherently pest-resistant

### In Practice
- Create [[query-how-do-i-design-habitat-for-beneficial-insects]] insects (insectary plants, hedgerows)
- Maintain biodiversity through polyculture
- Accept some pest damage as natural and acceptable

## The Fifth Principle: No Pruning
Often added as an unofficial fifth principle:
- Fruit trees should grow naturally
- Pruning creates wounds and stimulates excessive regrowth
- Natural tree forms are more productive and disease-resistant

## Results
Fukuoka's rice yields matched or exceeded conventional farms. His method required 1/10th the labor and no purchased inputs. The soil improved every year rather than degrading.
