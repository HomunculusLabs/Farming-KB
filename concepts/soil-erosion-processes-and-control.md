## Soil Erosion Processes and Control

Soil erosion and degradation represent one of the most critical threats to agricultural productivity and ecological stability. In [[green-manure]], grass leys in rotation, hedgerows, and minimal cultivation are not merely beneficial practices — they are imperative for long-term soil survival.

## Design Implications

In landscape design, soil erosion repair takes priority wherever erosion is occurring. No other design consideration can succeed if the foundational soil resource is being lost. A conservative approach to land use planning encompasses the concept of permitted or restricted uses for different soil types, carefully plotted according to analyses of slope, soil stability, minimal forest clearing (or active reafforestation), and permitted maximum levels of crop production or livestock density.

The assessment of any site for design purposes must include a thorough evaluation of erosion risk and existing erosion features. Water lines and soil types form the basis for determining appropriate land uses, and any design must prioritize the establishment and maintenance of soil stability before other productive elements are introduced.
