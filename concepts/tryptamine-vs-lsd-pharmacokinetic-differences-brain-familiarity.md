# Tryptamine vs LSD Pharmacokinetic Differences and Brain Familiarity

## Overview

While psilocybin, DMT, and LSD are often grouped together as "classical hallucinogens"
by the uninformed public and legal systems, each compound represents a phenomenologically
distinct universe of experience. A critical distinction lies in their pharmacokinetic
profiles — how the body absorbs, distributes, metabolizes, and eliminates each compound —
and the degree to which the brain appears evolutionarily adapted to process them.

## DMT: The Endogenous Compound

Dimethyltryptamine (DMT) is distinguished from other hallucinogens by several remarkable
pharmacokinetic properties:

### Onset and Duration

When smoked or vaporized, DMT produces a fully immersive altered state within 15-45
seconds of inhalation. The experience reaches peak intensity within approximately 30
seconds and lasts roughly 5-15 minutes before subsiding. This extraordinarily rapid
onset and brief duration are unique among classical hallucinogens.

### Endogenous Presence

DMT is present in small but measurable quantities in the human brain, cerebrospinal
fluid, blood, and urine. It is synthesized endogenously through the enzyme indolethylamine
N-methyltransferase (INMT), which catalyzes the methylation of tryptamine to N-
methyltryptamine (NMT) and then to DMT.

The rapid onset of DMT when administered exogenously suggests that the brain is
"absolutely at home with this compound." The neural infrastructure for processing
DMT already exists — the receptors, the metabolic pathways, the signal cascades are
all part of normal brain chemistry. Exogenous DMT simply amplifies or redirects
an existing endogenous system.

### MAO Vulnerability

DMT is rapidly degraded by monoamine oxidase (MAO) enzymes in the gut and liver,
making it orally inactive unless combined with a monoamine oxidase inhibitor (MAOI).
This is the basis of ayahuasca, the Amazonian brew that combines DMT-containing plants
with Banisteriopsis caapi, which contains harmine and harmaline — beta-carboline
MAOIs. This pharmacological requirement for oral activity has shaped the entire
cultural and botanical framework of Amazonian shamanism.

## LSD: The Synthetic Stranger

LSD (lysergic acid diethylamide) presents a pharmacokinetic profile strikingly
different from that of the [[mckenna-endogenous-tryptamines-quantum-consciousness]]:

### Extended Duration and Retention

LSD is retained in the body for considerably longer than DMT or psilocybin. A single
dose produces effects lasting 8-12 hours, and the compound can be detected in the
body for days after administration. This extended duration reflects LSD's high
lipophilicity, its tight binding to serum albumin, and its resistance to metabolic
degradation.

The prolonged action of LSD stands in contrast to the brief, intense visitation of
DMT. Where DMT acts like a rapid key-turn in a lock the brain already possesses,
LSD acts more like a sustained foreign presence that the body processes slowly.

### Non-Endogenous Status

LSD has no known endogenous counterpart in human biochemistry. While it shares
[[metzner-shamanic-ceremony-versus-psychotherapy-structural-features]] with ergoline alkaloids produced by the ergot fungus Claviceps
purpurea, it is a semisynthetic compound not found in nature. The brain has no
evolutionary history with this molecule.

### Receptor Profile Complexity

LSD acts as a partial agonist at multiple [[serotonin-receptor-interference-psychedelic-mechanism]] subtypes (5-HT1A,
5-HT2A, 5-HT2B, 5-HT2C), as well as at dopamine D2 receptors and adrenergic
receptors. This broad receptor profile contributes to the complexity and
unpredictability of the LSD experience compared to the more focused tryptamine
action.

## Psilocybin: The Bridge Compound

Psilocybin occupies a pharmacological middle ground between DMT and LSD:

### Prodrug Metabolism

Psilocybin is a prodrug — it is rapidly dephosphorylated by alkaline phosphatase
enzymes to psilocin (4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine), which is the actual
psychoactive compound. This dephosphorylation occurs primarily in the digestive
system and liver, meaning that psilocybin is orally active without requiring an
MAOI, unlike DMT.

### Onset and Duration

After oral ingestion, psilocybin produces effects within 20-60 minutes, with peak
effects occurring at approximately 90-120 minutes. The total experience duration is
typically 4-6 hours. This intermediate timeline reflects the additional metabolic
step of dephosphorylation.

### Tryptamine Core

Like DMT, psilocin shares the basic indole tryptamine structure with serotonin
(5-hydroxytryptamine). Psilocin is 4-hydroxy-DMT, while serotonin is 5-hydroxy-
tryptamine. The close [[serotonin-psilocybin-structural-relationship-hallucinogenic-mechanism]] to the brain's primary neurotransmitter
suggests that the brain is evolutionarily familiar with this molecular template,
though the specific 4-hydroxy substitution may produce effects that diverge from
normal serotonergic signaling.

## The Brain Familiarity Hypothesis

The pharmacokinetic data suggest a hierarchy of "brain familiarity" among classical
hallucinogens:

1. **DMT**: Endogenous compound, rapid onset, brief duration — the brain processes
it through native, evolutionarily conserved pathways
2. **Psilocybin/Psilocin**: Close structural analog of serotonin, orally active
prodrug, moderate duration — familiar molecular template with novel substitution
3. **LSD**: Non-endogenous, broad receptor profile, extended duration — the brain
has no evolutionary preparation for this molecule

This hierarchy may explain qualitative differences in the phenomenology of each
compound. The tryptamine experiences (DMT, psilocybin) tend to be described in
terms of encountering alien intelligence, information download, and dimensional
transit — experiences that may reflect the activation of latent endogenous
information-processing systems. The LSD experience, by contrast, is often described
in more visual, synesthetic, and emotionally expansive terms — possibly reflecting
the broader receptor engagement of a molecule the brain processes as a novel
presence rather than an amplified native signal.

## Risk Considerations

The differing pharmacokinetic profiles carry distinct risk profiles:

- **DMT**: Rapid onset can be psychologically overwhelming; brief duration limits
opportunity for therapeutic integration during the experience
- **Psilocybin**: Moderate duration allows for more gradual onset and some
therapeutic processing during the experience; dose-response is relatively
predictable
- **LSD**: Extended duration means that a difficult experience lasts many hours;
extreme potency (active at microgram doses) increases risk of accidental
overdose; broad receptor engagement introduces more unpredictable interactions
