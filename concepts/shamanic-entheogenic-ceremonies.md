# Shamanic Entheogenic Ceremonies

## Overview

Shamanic entheogenic ceremonies are traditional healing and divination rituals
that employ psychoactive plants within carefully structured ceremonial contexts.
Found in indigenous cultures worldwide, these ceremonies represent what Ralph
Metzner described as "the intentional arrangement of the set and the setting for
purposes of healing and divination." Unlike Western psychedelic psychotherapy,
shamanic ceremonies operate within a worldview that accepts the reality of
multiple worlds and spirit beings, and they emphasize the role of the healer
or shaman who ingests the medicine to perceive the causes of illness and
determine appropriate remedies.

## The Shamanic Worldview

Two fundamental assumptions distinguish shamanic from Western approaches:

1. **Multiple realities**: Shamanic practitioners perceive and interact with
   multiple worlds or dimensions of reality beyond the physical plane. These
   have been referred to as the "inner world," "spirit world," "upper or lower
   world," "faerie world," "dreamtime," or "nonordinary reality" (Michael
   Harner's term). The boundaries between these worlds become more permeable
   during ceremonial states.

2. **Spirit beings**: The beings encountered in dreams and visions are
   considered just as real as physical organisms. Spirits are associated with
   particular animals (jaguar, serpent), plants, trees, places (rivers,
   rainforests), deceased ancestors, and other nonordinary entities
   (extraterrestrials, elves). They function as allies, power animals,
   guardians, or helpers in healing and divination.

## Common Ceremonial Features

Across diverse cultures, shamanic entheogenic ceremonies share several
consistent features:

**The guide or healer**: Always central and essential. This person has
extensive personal experience with the medicines and has typically undergone
lengthy initiation and training under an experienced elder. The guide does
much or all of the singing, which profoundly shapes the experience.

**Minimal talking**: Traditional ceremonies involve very little verbal
communication among participants, except during preparatory phases or
afterward to clarify teachings and visions.

**Singing and chanting**: Invariably considered essential to success. The
shamanic singing typically has a rapid rhythmic beat similar to shamanic
drumming, providing psychical support for moving through the flow of visions
and minimizing the likelihood of getting stuck in frightening or seductive
experiences.

**Darkness or low light**: Almost always done in darkness, which facilitates
the emergence of visions. The exception is peyote ceremonies conducted around
a fire, where participants may see visions while staring into the flames.

**Small groups**: Typically six to twelve participants, though the healer may
work with one or two afflicted individuals.

## Three Variations of Shamanic Healing

Metzner identified three main variations of shamanic healing with entheogens:

1. **Extraction**: Removal of a toxic psychic residue or "sorcerous implant"
   that is believed to cause illness. The shaman may use sucking methods or
   other techniques to extract the toxin.

2. **Soul retrieval**: Recovery of a split-off psychic fragment or "soul"
   that has been lost through trauma, shock, or spiritual interference. The
   shaman journeys to find and return the lost portion.

3. **Dismemberment and reconstitution**: The experience of being destroyed
   and then reconstituted with a healthier, stronger "body." This death-
   rebirth pattern is found in shamanic traditions worldwide.

## Major Ceremonial Traditions

**Peyote ceremonies (Native American Church, North America):** Participants
sit in a circle on the ground around a blazing central fire in a tipi. The
ceremony lasts all night, conducted by a "roadman" with assistants. A staff
and rattle are passed; participants sing traditional peyote songs with rapid
rhythmic beats. Ceremonies are legally protected for members with 25% or more
Native American ancestry.

**Huichol peyote ceremonies (Mexico):** Follow the shamanic healing model
with careful apprenticeships for curanderos. After a long group pilgrimage
to Wirikuta to find the sacred cactus, ceremonies involve singing and
telling of creation myths around a fire.

**Mazatec mushroom veladas (Mexico):** Participants sit or lie in a very
dark room with a small candle. The healer sings almost uninterruptedly
throughout the night, weaving the names of Christian saints, spirit allies,
and nature spirits into the chants.

**Ayahuasca ceremonies (Amazon):** A small group sits in a circle in
semidarkness while initiated healers sing icaros through which healing
and diagnosis take place. The songs have a rapid rhythmic pulse. Shamanic
sucking methods for extracting toxic residues are sometimes employed.

**San Pedro ceremonies (Andes):** Sometimes conducted around a fire with
singing; sometimes the curandero sets up an altar with figurines
representing light and dark spirits.

**Bwiti iboga ceremonies (Gabon and Zaire):** Involve an altar with
ancestral images. Initiates undergo a powerful death-rebirth experience
after which they converse with ancestor spirits. A mirror in the assembly
room allows initiates to "see" their ancestral spirits.

## Divination and Plant Teachers

A central feature of shamanic entheogenic practice is divination — access to
hidden knowledge. Indigenous healers refer to entheogenic plants as "plant
teachers," believing that an intelligence associated with the medicine
communicates in an interior way to the person who ingests it. This divination
serves the same function as diagnosis in Western medicine: determining the
source of illness and the appropriate remedy.

## Comparison with Western Psychotherapy

Both approaches recognize the centrality of an experienced guide and the
importance of intention and setting. Both can produce healing and insight.
However, the underlying paradigms of illness and treatment are fundamentally
different. Western therapy operates within a materialist biomedical framework,
while shamanic healing operates within an animistic framework that accepts the
reality of spirit worlds and nonmaterial beings.
