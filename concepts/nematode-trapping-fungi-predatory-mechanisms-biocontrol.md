## Nematode-Trapping Fungi: Predatory Mechanisms and Biocontrol

## Overview

Nematode-trapping fungi are a specialized group of predatory soil fungi that capture and digest nematodes, playing a critical role in soil food web dynamics and offering significant potential as biological control agents against plant-parasitic nematodes. These fungi have evolved remarkable morphological and biochemical strategies to detect, trap, and consume nematodes, representing one of nature's most sophisticated predator-prey systems at the micro scale.

## Taxonomy and Diversity

[[nematophagous-fungi]] are distributed across two major phyla:

- **Ascomycota**: The majority of trapping fungi, including *Arthrobotrys*, *Monacrosporium*, *Dactylellina*, and *Drechslerella* genera. These are the most well-studied and commercially relevant groups.
- **Basidiomycota**: Some wood-decay basidiomycetes produce nematotoxic compounds (e.g., *[[pleurotus-ostreatus]]* produces toxocysts that paralyze nematodes).
- **Zygomycota**: Certain species in the order Zoopagales are obligate parasites of nematodes.

Over 200 species of nematophagous fungi have been described. Molecular phylogenetics has revealed that trapping structures evolved independently multiple times, suggesting strong convergent evolution driven by the ecological advantage of [[arthrobotrys-oligospora]]* and *Arthrobotrys conoides*.

**Adhesive Knobs**
- Short, bulbous branches on hyphae, produced by species like *Monacrosporium cionopagum*.
- Each knob secretes a droplet of adhesive material that binds to the nematode cuticle on contact.
- Some species produce stalked knobs that elevate the adhesive surface into the path of nematodes.

**Adhesive Columns**
- Erect, non-constricting hyphal columns covered with adhesive material, found in species such as *Dactylellina ellipsospora*.

### Mechanical Traps

**Constricting Rings**
- Among the most dramatic trapping structures, formed by species such as *Drechslerella stenobrocha*.
- Three-celled ring structures that inflate rapidly (within 0.1 seconds) upon mechanical stimulation.
- The inner surface of the ring cells swells inward, constricting the nematode body.
- Triggered by physical pressure, not chemical signals. Requires considerable metabolic energy.

### Non-Trapping Endoparasitic Strategies

- **Spore-forming endoparasites**: Species like *Catenaria anguillulae* produce zoospores that actively swim toward and attach to nematodes, germinating inside the host.
- **Adhesive spore traps**: *Meristacrum asterosperma* produces sticky spores that adhere to nematode cuticles and penetrate directly.

## Sensing and Induction of Trap Formation

### Chemical Signals
- Nematode-derived [[plant-signaling-pathways]] regulate trap morphogenesis, involving enzymes similar to yeast FUS3/KSS1.
- Hundreds of genes are differentially expressed during trap formation, including:
  - Adhesion proteins and lectins
  - Subtilisin-like serine proteases
  - Extracellular matrix components
  - Signal transduction proteins

## Nematode Capture and Digestion Process

1. **Capture**: Physical contact triggers immediate adhesion or constriction. The nematode cannot escape.
2. **Penetration**: Within minutes to hours, the fungus produces penetration pegs that breach the nematode cuticle using mechanical pressure and enzymatic degradation (proteases, collagenases, chitinases).
3. **Colonization**: Hyphae proliferate rapidly inside, forming infection bulbs. The nematode dies within 24-48 hours.
4. **Assimilation**: [[fungal-extracellular-enzymes]] digest internal tissues, converting nematode biomass into fungal biomass.
5. **Reproduction**: Conidia are produced from the nematode cadaver and dispersed.

## Biocontrol Applications

### Target Pests
- Root-knot nematodes (*Meloidogyne* spp.)
- Cyst nematodes (*Globodera* and *Heterodera* spp.)
- Lesion nematodes (*Pratylenchus* spp.)
