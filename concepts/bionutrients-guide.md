# Bionutrients Guide

Bionutrients are fermented plant and animal extracts used in Philippine/Asian natural farming as foliar sprays, soil drenches, and animal feed supplements. Developed by Gil Carandang (Herbana Farms, Laguna) based on Dr. Han Kyu Cho's KNF principles, they differ from standard KNF preparations in their use of locally available tropical materials, simpler fermentation methods, and focus on self-reliant farmer-made inputs. See [[knf-preparations]] for Cho's original Korean formulations.

## Core Principle: Fermentation Over Extraction

Fermentation is superior to simple extraction (boiling, tea infusion) because microorganisms break down complex plant materials, releasing more active ingredients. The general formula: mix plant/animal material with 1/3 crude sugar or molasses by weight, allow 50-75% air gap, cover loosely, ferment 5-7 days. Dilute extract 1:20 for use at 2-4 tbsp per gallon.

**Key rules**:
- Never use refined sugar (chemically bleached, harms microbes)
- Crude sugar or molasses feeds microbes during fermentation
- Energy concentration: seed > fruit > leaf (seed extracts dilute 1:1000, leaf 1:500)
- Materials should be unwashed and unpeeled (surface microbes aid fermentation)
- If materials are dry, add water just enough to moisten

## Fermented Plant Juice (FPJ) — Local Plant Selections

### Kangkong (Water Spinach) FPJ — Growth Promotant
Kangkong grows extremely fast in freshwater, indicating high concentrations of natural growth hormones (gibberellins, auxins, cytokinins). Use axillary buds of kangkong, or substitute with fast-growing plants like kelp, mugwort, cucumber, squash, and watermelon tips. Spray/water plants to accelerate vegetative growth.

### Banana-Squash-Papaya (BSP) FPJ — Flowering/Fruiting
High in potassium (banana) and beta-carotene (papaya, squash). Used to induce flowering and fruiting in vegetables. In the Philippines, conventional mango growers use potassium nitrate; BSP is the natural alternative. Substitutes for temperate climates: comfrey + squash + carrot.

### Papaya FPJ — General Nutrient
Rich in enzyme papain, beta-carotene, and vitamin C. Good general-purpose plant extract.

### General FPJ Method
1. Harvest plant material at peak growth (before flowering for veg, during flowering for reproductive)
2. Chop as thinly as possible — unwashed, unpeeled
3. Mix with 1/3 crude sugar/molasses by weight
4. Pack in container with 50-75% air gap
5. Cover loosely, ferment 5-7 days
6. Strain liquid — dilute 1:20 for use

## Fish Amino Acid (FAA)

Fish scraps fermented as a high-nitrogen source. The higher the protein of source material, the higher the nitrogen in the final extract.

**Recipe differences from KNF**:
- Carandang prefers molasses over crude sugar for FAA (better odor control)
- Adds lactic acid bacteria during fermentation to arrest foul odors
- Fish emulsion is the US commercial equivalent but lacks microbial diversity

**Process**: Mix fish scraps with 1/3 molasses by weight, add LAB serum, ferment 5-7 days. Strain and dilute 1:20. See [[fish-amino-acid-faa]] for KNF's detailed FAA recipe.

## Calcium Phosphate (CA)

Critical for the "changeover period" (vegetative to reproductive transition). Based on Yasushi Inoue's Nutrioperiodism theory (1930s): plants need specific nutrients at specific developmental stages. Calcium phosphate is the key input for the transition — the plant's "morning sickness" phase.

**Method**:
1. Roast eggshells until good ashes form
2. Dip roasted eggshells in equal volume of vinegar
3. Let sit 2 weeks until eggshells break down from acetic acid
4. Dilute 1:20, spray/water during changeover period

**Note**: Soybean stem ash is also excellent for calcium phosphate. See [[water-soluble-calcium-wca]] and [[water-soluble-calcium-phosphate-wcp]] for KNF's brown rice vinegar-based methods.

## Oriental Herbal Nutrient (OHN) — Natural Antibiotic

The original Korean recipe uses ginger, garlic, and Chinese herbs (Angelica acutiloba, Glycyrrhiza uralensis, Cinnamomum loureirii) — all selected for digestive benefits. Carandang's simplified version uses equal parts ginger and garlic.

**Key difference from standard FPJ**: OHN uses alcohol extraction as the final step (like herbal tinctures in homeopathy):
1. Soak chopped ginger + garlic in beer or wine overnight (12 hours)
2. Add 1/3 crude sugar, ferment 5-7 days
3. Add 40% proof alcohol to stabilize and arrest fermentation
4. Alcohol extracts the final active ingredients

**Uses**: Natural antibiotic/fungicide for plants and animals. Garlic's high sulfur content makes it a potent fungicide. Used for chicks, chickens, fungal plant problems, and even human rheumatism. See [[oriental-herbal-nutrient-ohn]] for the full 5-herb KNF version.

## Lactic Acid Bacteria (LAB) Serum

The "major workhorse" of the bionutrient system. LAB constitutes 50% of the total Beneficial Indigenous Microorganism (BIM) blend.

**Collection**: Rice wash fermented 5-7 days at 20-25C in a container with 50-75% air gap. Lactic acid bacteria gather when rice bran separates and floats as a thin film with sour smell.

**Purification**: Add 10 parts milk (fresh best, skim/powdered OK). After 5-7 days, carbohydrate/protein/fat float, leaving pure yellow LAB serum. Discard solids to compost or animal feed.

**Storage**: Add equal amount crude sugar/molasses. Do not use refined sugar. Store at room temperature.

**Use**: Dilute 1:20 basic concoction. 2-4 tbsp per gallon for spray, animal water, or compost activation. See [[lactic-acid-bacteria-lab]] for detailed KNF procedure.

## Bamboo Vinegar and Activated Charcoal

**Bamboo microorganisms**: Bamboo roots exude sugary substances that attract powerful beneficial microbes. Collect by burying cooked rice in bamboo litter (same method as forest IMO collection). Bamboo microbes are particularly useful for fermenting animal feeds.

**Bamboo vinegar (mokusaku)**: Not detailed in Carandang's paper but referenced in broader Asian natural farming. Produced by pyrolysis of bamboo. Contains acetic acid and phenolic compounds. Used as a soil conditioner, pest deterrent, and microbial stimulant.

**Activated charcoal**: Used in designer compost (bokashi) as a carbon substrate for microbial proliferation. Charcoal dust provides surface area for beneficial microorganisms to colonize.

## Designer Compost (Bokashi)

Carandang's enhanced bokashi formula (Philippine version):

| Material | Amount | Role |
|----------|--------|------|
| Rice bran | 10 kg | Carbon source (80% carb target) |
| Copra meal | 20 kg | Protein source |
| Coco peat | 20 kg | Carbon/lignin |
| Chicken manure | 30 kg | NPK + micronutrients |
| Charcoal dust | 20 kg | Microbial substrate |
| BIM | 1 liter | Microbial inoculant |
| Molasses | 1 kg | Microbe food |
| Bionutrient | 1 liter | Targeted nutrients |

**Substitutions for temperate climates**: wheat bran for rice bran, corn/soy meal for copra, peat moss for coco peat, any animal manure for chicken manure.

**Key**: 80% carbon : 17% nitrogen : 3% trace elements ratio. Adjust bionutrient blend based on crop needs (high-K for flowering, high-N for vegetative). See [[bokashi]] and [[fermented-mixed-compost-fmc]] for related methods.
