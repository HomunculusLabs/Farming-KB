# [[indigenous-microorganisms]] (IMO)

## Overview

Indigenous Microorganisms (IMO) are the cornerstone of Dr. Cho Han Kyu's
Korean [[natural-farming]] system. IMO refers to beneficial bacteria, fungi,
actinomycetes, and other microorganisms native to a specific local
environment. Rather than purchasing commercial microbial inoculants or
synthetic fertilizers, natural farming practitioners collect, multiply,
and apply these locally adapted organisms to build living, fertile soil.

## Why Indigenous Microorganisms?

Dr. Cho's key insight was that the most effective microorganisms for any
given farm are already present in the surrounding natural environment.
Forest soils teem with diverse microbial communities that have evolved
to decompose organic matter, fix nutrients, suppress pathogens, and
build soil structure. These organisms are already adapted to the local
climate and soil type. By collecting IMO from nearby forests, practitioners
harness locally adapted biology rather than relying on exogenous
organisms that may not survive in the target environment.

## The Four-Stage IMO Collection Process

### Stage 1: IMO-1 Collection from the Forest

IMO-1 is the initial collection of microorganisms from a natural forest.
A healthy forest with rich, humus-littered soil is selected. A wooden box
(typically 30 cm x 30 cm x 10 cm) is filled with steamed rice, covered
with breathable paper, and buried slightly in the forest floor leaf litter.
After 5 to 7 days, the rice becomes colonized by fungi and bacteria from
the surrounding soil. White, fuzzy fungal mycelium indicates success.
Colors such as white, yellow, or gray are acceptable, while dark green or
black molds indicate undesirable organisms and the collection should be
restarted.

### Stage 2: IMO-2 Multiplication with Rice Bran

IMO-2 expands the collected microorganisms using a nutrient-rich substrate.
The colonized rice from IMO-1 is mixed with crude rice bran at
approximately 1:10 ratio. The mixture should be moist enough to hold
together when squeezed but not dripping. It is placed in a breathable
container, covered, and kept warm and shaded for 5 to 7 days. Temperature
should reach 40 to 50 degrees Celsius as microbial activity peaks. A
sweet, fermented smell indicates success. The final product is fluffy,
aromatic, and free from foul odors.

### Stage 3: IMO-3 Soil Incorporation

IMO-3 acclimates microorganisms to a soil environment. The IMO-2 mixture
is combined with garden soil at approximately 1:1 and spread in a thin
layer (about 3 cm thick) in a shaded, protected area. It is covered with
straw or leaves and kept moist for 7 to 10 days. The resulting material
is dark, crumbly, earthy-smelling compost rich in beneficial microbes.

### Stage 4: IMO-4 Final Field Preparation

IMO-4 is the final preparation for field application. The IMO-3 material
is mixed with an equal volume of completed Fermented Mixed Compost and
allowed to mature for 7 to 14 days. This product can be applied directly
to garden beds, mixed into potting soil, or used as top dressing. Standard
rates are 1 to 2 kg per 10 square meters, or 100 to 200 kg per hectare
for larger operations.

## Storage and Shelf Life

Fresh IMO preparations are most effective when used soon after production.
IMO-2 can be stored for several weeks in a cool, dry, shaded location.
For longer storage, IMO-2 can be sun-dried and kept in sealed containers
for several months. However, viability decreases over time, and fresh
preparations should be prioritized whenever possible.

## Applications of IMO

### Soil Building

The primary application of IMO is soil building. Bacteria break down
simple organic compounds, fungi decompose complex materials like lignin
and cellulose, and actinomycetes produce antibiotics that suppress
soil-borne pathogens. Together they create a living soil ecosystem.

### Seed and Seedling Treatment

IMO is used in the Seed/Seedling treatment (SES) protocol. Seeds or
seedling roots are dipped in a dilute IMO solution before planting to
inoculate them with beneficial organisms from the very start.

### Compost Acceleration

Adding IMO to compost piles dramatically accelerates decomposition. The
diverse microbial community ensures all types of organic material are
efficiently broken down, producing higher-quality compost in less time.

### Livestock Bedding

IMO is mixed into livestock bedding to control odor, reduce pathogens,
and accelerate manure decomposition. This is a key component of Dr. Cho's
zero-emission poultry system.

## Quality Indicators

Good IMO-1 shows abundant white fungal growth with a pleasant, sweet or
earthy aroma. Good IMO-2 is fluffy, warm during fermentation, and smells
of sweet fermentation. Good IMO-3 and IMO-4 are dark, crumbly, and smell
like rich forest soil. Any foul, putrid, or ammonia-like odors indicate
anaerobic conditions or contamination, and the batch should be discarded.

## Common Mistakes

The most common mistake is over-wetting the substrate, which creates
anaerobic conditions favoring putrefying bacteria. Collecting from
degraded or polluted sites should be avoided. Using polished rice bran
instead of crude bran can fail because processing removes essential
nutrients for beneficial microorganisms.
