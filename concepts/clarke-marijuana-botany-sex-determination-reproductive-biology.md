# Cannabis Sex Determination and Reproductive Biology

Cannabis is fundamentally a dioecious plant, meaning that male and female
flowers develop on separate plants, although monoecious examples with
both sexes on one plant are also found. The development of branches
containing flowering organs varies greatly between males and females.
Understanding the mechanisms of sex determination, pollination biology,
and sexual reproduction is essential for any Cannabis breeding program.

## Staminate and Pistillate Flower Morphology

Female Cannabis flowers and plants are referred to as pistillate, and
male flowers and plants are referred to as staminate. This botanical
convention is more accurate and makes examples of complex aberrant
sexuality easier to understand. Male flowers hang in long, loose,
multi-branched, clustered limbs up to 30 centimeters long, while female
flowers are tightly crowded between small leaves.

The female flowers appear as two long white, yellow, or pink pistils
protruding from the fold of a very thin membranous calyx. The calyx is
covered with resin-exuding glandular trichomes. Pistillate flowers are
borne in pairs at the nodes, one on each side of the petiole behind the
stipule of bracts. The calyx measures 2 to 6 millimeters in length and
closely contains the ovary.

In male flowers, five petals approximately 5 millimeters long make up the
calyx and may be yellow, white, or green in color. Five stamens emerge,
consisting of slender anthers splitting upwards from the tip and
suspended on thin filaments. The exterior surface of the staminate calyx
is covered with non-glandular trichomes. The pollen grains are nearly
spherical, slightly yellow, and 25 to 30 microns in diameter with a
smooth surface exhibiting 2 to 4 germ pores.

## Sexual Differentiation and Primordia Development

The first sign of flowering in Cannabis is the appearance of
undifferentiated flower primordia along the main stem at the nodes,
behind the stipule. In the prefloral phase, the sexes are
indistinguishable except for general trends in shape. Soon the males can
be identified by their curved claw shape, followed by the differentiation
of round pointed flower buds having five radial segments. Females are
recognized by the enlargement of a symmetrical tubular calyx and are
easier to recognize at a young age than male primordia.

Female plants tend to be shorter with more branches than males. Female
plants are leafy to the top with many leaves surrounding the flowers,
while male plants have fewer leaves near the top with few if any leaves
along the extended flowering limbs. Before flowering begins, the
phyllotaxy reverses from decussate to alternate, and the number of
leaflets per leaf decreases until a small single leaflet appears below
each pair of calyxes.

## Factors Affecting Sex Ratios

Under average conditions with a normal inductive photoperiod, Cannabis
will bloom and produce approximately equal numbers of pure staminate and
pure pistillate plants with a few hermaphrodites. Under conditions of
extreme stress, such as nutrient excess or deficiency, mutilation, and
altered light cycles, populations have been shown to depart greatly from
the expected one-to-one staminate to pistillate ratio.

Staminate plants tend to flower up to one month earlier than pistillate
plants. However, pistillate plants often differentiate primordia one to
two weeks before staminate plants. Soon after dehiscence, the staminate
plant dies, while the pistillate plant may mature up to five months after
viable flowers are formed if little or no fertilization occurs.

## The Biology of Pollination

Pollination begins with dehiscence, the release of pollen from staminate
flowers. Millions of pollen grains float through the air on light
breezes, and many land on the stigmatic surfaces of nearby pistillate
plants. Just prior to dehiscence, the pollen nucleus divides to produce a
small reproductive cell accompanied by a large vegetative cell, both
contained within the mature pollen grain.

Germination occurs 15 to 20 minutes after contact with a pistil. As the
pollen tube grows, the vegetative cell remains in the pollen grain while
the generative cell enters the pollen tube and migrates toward the ovule.
The generative cell divides into two gametes as it travels the length of
the pollen tube. Fertilization may take up to two days in cooler
temperatures.

## Seed Production and Life Cycle

Pollination of the pistillate flower results in the loss of the paired
pistils and a swelling of the tubular calyx where the ovule is
enlarging. After approximately 14 to 35 days the seed is matured and
drops from the plant. Fresh seeds approach 100 percent viability, but
this decreases with age. The hard mature seed is partially surrounded by
the calyx and is variously patterned in grey, brown, or black. The seed
measures 2 to 6 millimeters in length and 2 to 4 millimeters in maximum
diameter.

The normal Cannabis life cycle is 4 to 6 months but may take as little
as 2 months or as long as 10 months. A mature pistillate plant can
produce tens of thousands of seeds if freely pollinated. Sinsemilla
marijuana is grown by removing all staminate plants, eliminating every
pollen source, and allowing the pistillate plants to produce massive
clusters of unfertilized flowers.

## Controlled Pollination Techniques

Controlled hand pollination consists of two basic steps: collecting
pollen from the anthers of the staminate parent and applying pollen to
the receptive stigmatic surfaces of the pistillate parent. Since Cannabis
is a wind-pollinated species, enclosures are employed which isolate the
ripe flowers from wind while allowing enough light penetration and air
circulation for pollen and seeds to develop.

Paper and tightly woven cloth are the most suitable materials for
pollination bags. Coarse cloth allows pollen to escape and plastic
materials tend to collect transpired water and rot the flowers. Light-
colored opaque or translucent reflective materials remain cooler in the
sun than dark or transparent materials. Breathing synthetic fabrics such
as Gore-Tex are used with great success for this purpose.

## Sinsemilla and the Reproductive Struggle

Various theories have arisen to explain the unusually potent psychoactive
properties of unfertilized Cannabis. The central theme involves the
extraordinarily long, frustrated struggle of the pistillate plant to
reproduce. Upon fertilization the plant's energies are channeled into
seed production instead of increased resin production. Sinsemilla plants
continue to produce new floral clusters until late fall, while seeded
plants cease floral production. It is suspected that capitate-stalked
trichome production might cease when the calyx is fertilized.
