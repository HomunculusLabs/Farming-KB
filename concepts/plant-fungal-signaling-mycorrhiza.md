- Originally identified as germination stimulants for root parasitic plants (Striga, Orobanche)
- Synthesized from carotenoid precursors; their production increases under [[beta-glucans]]) can trigger defense responses
- The plant response to these elicitors is typically weak and transient compared to pathogen-derived elicitors
- Chitinase activity increases in roots during both AMF and EMF colonization
- This "defense paradox" — activation of defense-like responses during mutualistic symbiosis — is a recurring theme

## Symbiotic Signaling (During Colonization)

### Hormonal Signaling

#### Auxin

- AMF colonization modifies root auxin levels and sensitivity
- Some mycorrhizal fungi produce IAA directly (e.g., [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-colonization-patterns]]
- High ethylene levels can suppress AMF colonization
- Some PGPR promote mycorrhization partly by reducing ethylene levels through ACC deaminase activity
- The ethylene pathway intersects with jasmonic acid signaling in modulating the symbiosis

#### Jasmonic Acid and Salicylic Acid

- JA/SA signaling pathways are involved in the balance between defense and mutualism
- JA signaling may be required for proper arbuscule development
- SA-mediated SAR generally antagonizes mycorrhizal colonization
- The crosstalk between JA and SA pathways determines the outcome of the plant-fungus interaction

### Calcium Signaling

- Calcium spiking in the nucleus is the earliest detectable plant response to fungal signals
- The calcium/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase CCaMK/DMI3 decodes calcium oscillations
- Calcium signaling coordinates the expression of symbiosis-specific genes
- Calcium influx also occurs in response to pathogen elicitors, creating specificity challenges

## Defense Regulation in Symbiosis

### The Mutualism-Parasitism Continuum

Molecular events in mycorrhizal signaling overlap with plant-pathogen interactions:

- Signal perception, [[fungal-environmental-sensing-signal-transduction-read]], and defense gene activation occur in both
- In mycorrhizal symbiosis, these responses are characteristically weak and transient
- The low intensity may reflect limited capacity of AMF to trigger defense
- Alternatively, the fungus may actively suppress defense responses through effector proteins

### Transient Defense Response

The defense response during mycorrhizal colonization shows characteristic temporal dynamics:

- Early phase: Defense gene activation (peroxidases, chitinases, PR proteins, GSTs)
- Mid phase: Suppression of defense as compatibility is established
- Late phase: Re-emergence of some defense markers during arbuscule senescence
