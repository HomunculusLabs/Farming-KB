# Psilocybe cubensis Miniculture Technique

The miniculture technique is a small-scale cultivation
method for [[psilocybin]] and [[mycelial-colonization]] and multiple fruiting
flushes while keeping individual cultures small and
manageable for experimental purposes. The jar is
then autoclaved to achieve full sterilization
before any biological material is introduced.

## Inoculation and Colonization

Under sterile conditions, each jar is inoculated
with a mycelium culture grown on agar. The agar
inoculation ensures a clean start with known
genetic material, which is critical for controlled
analytical studies. Once inoculated, the jars enter
a 28-day colonization period during which the
mycelium spreads through the rye grain substrate.

## Shaking Schedule

Throughout the 28-day colonization phase, the jars
are shaken every four days to distribute the growing
mycelium evenly across the grain. This regular
redistribution prevents localized dense clumping of
mycelium and promotes uniform colonization of the
entire substrate. By day 28, the mycelium has fully
covered the grain, indicating readiness for the
casing step.

## Casing Layer

After full colonization, the jars are opened and
the grain is cased with a layer approximately 2 cm
deep. The casing mixture consists of:

- 2 parts peat
- 1 part calcium carbonate
- 2 parts perlite and/or vermiculite

This casing layer serves multiple purposes: it
retains moisture at the substrate surface, provides
a humid microclimate for primordia formation, and
supplies calcium to buffer pH. The combination of
perlite and vermiculite ensures adequate aeration
and water retention properties in the casing.

## Watering Regimen

The minicultures are watered once every two days
with 1 ml of sterile water delivered via syringe.
This precise watering method prevents contamination
that could arise from pouring or spraying non-sterile
water onto the exposed substrate and casing layer.
Sterile technique is maintained throughout the
entire cultivation process.

## Fruiting Timeline

The first flush of mushrooms occurs four to five
weeks after inoculation, which corresponds to
approximately two weeks after casing is applied.
This timeline is consistent across multiple
minicultures. The cultures continue to produce
mushrooms for at least 20 weeks total, provided
they remain uncontaminated. Up to five or six
flushes can typically be harvested from a single
miniculture before the mycelium exhausts the
substrate or contamination sets in.

## Yield

Each miniculture yields an average of 2.7 grams
dry weight of mushrooms over its productive
lifespan. This yield is modest compared to
larger cultivation methods but is sufficient
for analytical purposes. The small scale allows
researchers to maintain tight control over growing
conditions and to track alkaloid content across
individual flushes from a single culture.

## Relevance to Alkaloid Research

The technique was designed to study how psilocybin
and psilocin levels vary across successive flushes.
Bigwood and Beug found that psilocin is generally
absent in the first one or two flushes, reaches
maximum levels by the fourth flush, then declines.
Psilocybin levels showed no clear trend across
flushes but varied by a factor of four among
cultures grown under identical conditions.

## Adaptations and Provenance

The rye-grain medium was initially described by
San Antonio (1971) for cultivating Agaricus
bisporus from cased grain spawn. Oss and Oeric
(1976) refined the technique for Psilocybe
cubensis in their grower's guide. Bigwood and Beug
scaled it to a single half-pint jar for controlled
scientific study of alkaloid production across
repeated flushes.
