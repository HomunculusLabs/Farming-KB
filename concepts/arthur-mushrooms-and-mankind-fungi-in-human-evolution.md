# Fungi in Human Evolution: Mushrooms and Mankind

James Arthur's "Mushrooms and Mankind: A Brief Introduction to Ethnomycology" presents the thesis that psychoactive mushrooms -- particularly **[[allegro-amanita-muscaria-properties-and-pharmacology]]** -- have been central to the development of human religious thought, mythology, and social organization. The work argues that entheogenic fungi served as the hidden sacramental basis for many of the world's major religions and cultural traditions.

## Core Thesis: The Symbiotic Relationship

Arthur posits that humanity has maintained a **symbiotic relationship with psychoactive plants and fungi** for thousands, perhaps millions, of years. Beyond providing food, certain plants expanded human consciousness into mystical and spiritual states. Every indigenous culture had plant-knowers -- shamans, priests, healers, sages -- whose insight was largely derived from their understanding of pharmacopoeia (the use of plants) and the visionary experiences these substances produced.

The field studying this relationship is called **ethnomycology**, a term coined by Gordon Wasson, specifically dedicated to mushrooms with consciousness-expanding qualities and their deep roots in religious traditions, writings, and indigenous knowledge.

## Amanita Muscaria as the Primordial Sacrament

Arthur identifies **Amanita muscaria** -- the iconic red-and-white mushroom -- as the single most important entheogenic fungus in human history. Key arguments include:

- **Christmas symbolism**: The mushroom grows in mycorrhizal association with pine trees; the tradition of bringing pine trees indoors and placing red-and-white gifts underneath directly mirrors the mushroom's natural habitat. Santa Claus is traced to Siberian shamans who dressed in red and white, entered through the roof opening (chimney), and carried mushrooms in bags.

- **The "Fruit of the Tree"**: The mushroom's [[comparison-fruiting-body-vs-mycelium-extracts-medicinal-mushrooms]] grows from underground mycelium attached to tree roots, making it literally the "fruit of the tree." Arthur connects this to the [[allegro-garden-of-eden-and-delight]] narrative, the Tree of Knowledge, and the Tree of Life.

- **Virgin birth symbolism**: The mushroom's apparent seedless growth (spores are microscopic) appeared miraculous to ancient peoples, giving rise to virgin birth motifs.

- **Soma/Haoma**: Arthur aligns with the Wasson hypothesis identifying Vedic Soma as Amanita muscaria, noting the Rig-Veda's references to ceremonial urine drinking -- consistent with muscimol being excreted unchanged.

- **Manna as mushroom**: Arthur draws on John Allegro's philological work ("The [[allegro-the-sacred-mushroom-amanita-identification]] and the Cross") arguing that biblical Manna means "mushroom," noting the botanical description fits: small, round, appearing after dew, breeding worms and stinking if left in the sun.

## The Hidden Sacrament Across Religions

Arthur traces mushroom symbolism through numerous traditions:

- **Christianity**: The Eucharist ("This is my body/Soma") is interpreted as the ritual ingestion of the mushroom. The Holy Grail is identified with the mushroom's cup-shaped mature form. The doctrine of Transubstantiation is characterized as a later substitution replacing the actual entheogenic sacrament with a placebo.

- **Egyptian religion**: Osiris mythology, the Djed pillar, the Eye of Horus, and the Ankh are all linked to mushroom imagery and the concept of rebirth (the mushroom's lifecycle from spore to fruiting body).

- **Norse/Germanic mythology**: Thor's mushroom-shaped hammer, Odin's sky-chariot (the Big Dipper), and flying horses whose blood-saliva spawns mushrooms are interpreted as encoded mushroom references.

- **Hindu/Buddhist traditions**: Soma, Amrita, and the [[amanita-muscaria-feathered-serpent-phoenix]] motif are connected to the mushroom's lifecycle stages (egg, serpent-like stem, shedding the universal veil, upturned cap with gill-feathers).

## The Role of the Shaman

Arthur describes the shaman (also called healer, priest, mage, sage, yogi, or magician) as a universal figure across cultures whose authority derived from knowledge of pharmacopoeia -- the use of psychoactive plants. This knowledge provided insight through direct experience rather than received doctrine. Arthur notes that "pharmacopoeia" is the root of the English "pharmacy" or "pharmacist," and has also been translated as "witchcraft."

Different tribes and peoples used different entheogens, largely determined by local availability. The social power, respect, reverence, and authority held by the "pharmacopoeia-practicing" native shaman was a major problem for government and religion. Arthur identifies this as the underlying motivation for campaigns to demonize and discredit shamanism, portraying it as something of the devil.

## Gnosis and the Pineal Gland

Arthur introduces the concept of **Gnosis** -- the joining of human consciousness with universal consciousness -- as the direct experience that entheogens facilitate. He draws attention to the pineal gland:

- The pineal gland is shaped like a pinecone (hence the name Pine-al)
- It is described as an autonomous part of the brain, floating in the dead center, not attached to any other part
- It has been considered the "seat of the soul" in various traditions
- Arthur notes that due to cultural conditioning, the pineal gland atrophies during youth and begins to calcify during puberty, reducing endogenous secretions

This atrophy corresponds temporally with the "day of reckoning" when children are told Santa Claus is not real -- a moment Arthur interprets as a psychological defense mechanism against consciousness-expanding knowledge.

## The Suppression of Entheogenic Knowledge

A central argument is that religious and governmental institutions actively suppressed knowledge of entheogenic mushrooms to maintain control over spiritual authority:

- The Spanish Inquisition and witch hunts (described as the murder of over eight million people) targeted shamans and pharmacopoeia practitioners.
- An estimated twenty million indigenous Central Americans were murdered, bringing about the near extinction of Aztec/Inca/Mayan peoples.
- Indigenous North Americans suffered a similar fate.
- Modern drug prohibition is characterized as a continuation of this suppression, lumping all substances together to deny individuals direct spiritual experience.

Arthur argues that the lumping together of all psychoactive substances -- from heroin and crack to plant entheogens -- is deliberate propaganda. The highly addictive drugs serve the interests of corrupt institutions through profit, while natural entheogens are suppressed because they "make you think," which is the last thing those in power want.

Arthur quotes Roger Q. Mills of Texas (1887): "Prohibition was introduced as a fraud; it has been nursed as a fraud. It is wrapped in the livery of Heaven, but it comes to serve the devil."

## Critical Perspective

Arthur's work is explicitly speculative and interdisciplinary, drawing on philology, art history, comparative religion, archaeology, and mycology. While many individual connections are intriguing, the overall thesis that Amanita muscaria is the singular hidden sacrament behind all world religions is not widely accepted in academic circles. The work is most valuable as a survey of mushroom symbolism across cultures and as an accessible introduction to ethnomycological thinking.

## Synonymous Names for the Mushroom

Arthur compiles a list of names and symbols he identifies as referring to Amanita muscaria across cultures, arguing that the Egyptian concept of "the God of a thousand names" begins to make sense:

- Soma (Hindu plant-god; also Greek for "body")
- Amrita (Buddhist magical sacrament)
- Ambrosia (Greek, "food of the gods")
- The Holy Grail (cup-shaped, containing the "blood of God")
- Fruit of the Tree of Life / Tree of Knowledge
- The Golden Fleece
- The Fountain of Youth
- Haoma (Islamic sacrament)
- Manna (Hebrew; means "mushroom" -- there are two kinds in the Bible)
- Bread of Life / Loaf of Bliss
- Fountain of Living Waters (the mushroom is 90% water, shaped like a fountain)
- Hidden Manna (Revelation 2:17)
- The Cosmic Egg / Easter Egg (the mushroom hunt)
- The Prima Materia / Philosopher's Stone of the Alchemists
- The Flesh of the God / Flesh of Jesus
- The Hammer of Thor (mushroom-shaped; lightning creates the mushroom)
- The Small White Stone (infant button stage)
- The Elixir of Immortality
- The Feathered Serpent (cosmopolitan symbol)
- The Phoenix (ashes/spores to egg to bird to dissolution and rebirth)
- The Ankh (waters and life in Egypt)
- Rudra (Hindu red god of the forest)
- The Djed pillar of Osiris
- The Eye of Horus (Djed-Eye)
- The World Tree
- Celestial Food (Egyptian Book of the Dead)
- Fly Agaric (Arthur suggests "fly" refers to spiritual flight, not houseflies)

Arthur notes that "Fly Agaric" is commonly associated with the house fly, but evidence suggests the name also refers to the act of "flying" -- taking spiritual flight. Many of these representations are used severally in single depictions, creating layered symbolic meaning.
