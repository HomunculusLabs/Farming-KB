# Psilocybin and Psilocin: Chemistry and Pharmacology

## Overview

Psilocybin (4-phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine, or 4-PO-DMT) and its active
metabolite psilocin (4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine, or 4-HO-DMT) are the
primary psychoactive compounds found in [[emcdda-eu-legislative-timeline-hallucinogenic-mushrooms-2001-2006]]. These indolealkylamine
tryptamines are structurally similar to the neurotransmitter serotonin
(5-hydroxytryptamine, 5-HT) and produce their effects primarily through agonism
at the 5-HT2A receptor. Alongside [[baeocystin-and-norbaeocystin-in-psilocybin-biosynthesis]], they constitute
the four major tryptamine alkaloids identified in psilocybian fungi.

## Chemical Structures

### Psilocybin

Psilocybin is the phosphate ester of psilocin. It is more stable in air and
water-soluble, making it the primary storage form in fungal tissue. The phosphate
group makes psilocybin more polar and less lipid-soluble than psilocin, affecting
its pharmacokinetic properties. Upon ingestion, psilocybin is rapidly dephosphorylated
by alkaline phosphatase enzymes in the body to produce psilocin, which is the
pharmacologically active compound.

Chemically, psilocybin is classified as an indolealkylamine, placing it in the
same broad chemical group as LSD, DMT, and 5-MeO-DMT. This grouping reflects
their shared structural basis in the tryptamine molecule and similar receptor
binding profiles, though their potencies and durations differ substantially.

### Psilocin

Psilocin is the dephosphorylated, pharmacologically active form. It acts primarily
as a post-synaptic agonist or partial agonist at serotonin 5-HT2A receptors, which
are densely concentrated in the cerebral cortex, particularly in layer V pyramidal
neurons. This receptor activation is responsible for the characteristic perceptual
distortions, altered thought patterns, and changes in self-awareness associated
with the [[leary-psychedelic-experience-bardo-mapping]].

### Baeocystin and Norbaeocystin

Two additional tryptamines — baeocystin (4-phosphoryloxy-N-methyltryptamine) and
norbaeocystin (4-phosphoryloxytryptamine) — are present in many psilocybian fungi
but are considered less active than psilocybin and psilocin. Their contribution
to the overall psychoactive effects is not fully understood but they are typically
present in smaller concentrations.

## Pharmacological Mechanism

### Serotonin Receptor Activation

Psilocin's primary mechanism of action is as a 5-HT2A receptor agonist. This
receptor is a G-protein-coupled receptor that, when activated, modulates glutamate
release onto cortical pyramidal neurons. The resulting cascade of neural effects
produces the characteristic alterations in perception, mood, and cognition.

The structural similarity between psilocin and serotonin allows it to bind to
multiple serotonin receptor subtypes, though the 5-HT2A receptor is considered
primary for psychedelic effects. Additional binding to 5-HT1A, 5-HT2C, and other
receptors may contribute to the complex phenomenology of the mushroom experience.

### Chemical Classification of Hallucinogens

Hallucinogens can be classified by chemical structure into three major groups:
- **Indolealkylamines (tryptamines)**: LSD, psilocin, psilocybin, DMT
- **Phenylethylamines**: mescaline
- **Cannabinoids**: THC and related compounds

Chemically related substances within these groups tend to exhibit similar
pharmacological effects, though significant differences exist in potency,
duration, and subjective character of experience.

## Occurrence in Nature

### Species Distribution

Psilocybin-containing fungi belong mainly to four families:
- **Strophariaceae** — Psilocybe genus (dominant)
- **Bolbitiaceae** — Conocybe genus
- **Coprinaceae** — Copelandia and Panaeolus genera
- **Cortinariaceae** — Inocybe genus

The genus Psilocybe is predominant in recreational use, with Psilocybe cubensis
(also known as Stropharia cubensis) being the most commonly cultivated and marketed
variety. Other frequently encountered species include P. semilanceata (liberty
caps) and P. cyanescens (wavy caps).

### Varietal Forms

Marketed cubensis varieties are often named by geographic origin: Mexican, Thai,
Colombian, and Amazonian. Some species such as P. mexicana and P. tampanensis form
sclerotia — hardened masses of mycelium resistant to adverse conditions — marketed
as "truffles" or "philosopher's stones." Sclerotia are a [[fukuoka-textdoc-disease-as-natures-self-defense-mechanism]] against
dryness, cold, heat, or excessive moisture.

### The Bluing Reaction

The primary field characteristic distinguishing psilocybin-containing mushrooms
from inactive, poisonous, or edible look-alikes is that they bruise blue when
handled or damaged. This blueing is an oxidative reaction involving the
degradation of psilocin and related compounds. However, not all psilocybian species
exhibit strong blueing, and the reaction is not exclusive to psychoactive species.

## Dose and Effects

### Potency Variation

Potency varies considerably between species, within species, and even between
individual mushrooms. [[factors-affecting-mycoremediation-efficiency]] potency include species, age at harvest,
growing conditions, substrate, and post-harvest handling. The EMCDDA (2006) noted
that unpredictable potency is one factor limiting the appeal of regular
recreational mushroom use.

### Route of Administration

Mushrooms are typically chopped and ingested directly or brewed as tea. Onset of
effects occurs within 20-60 minutes, with peak effects at 1-3 hours and total
duration of 4-6 hours. The experience is characterized by perceptual distortions
(especially visual), altered time perception, changes in thought patterns, and
modified self-awareness. True hallucinations (perceptions without external stimuli)
are rare except at very high doses; the effects are more accurately described as
perceptual distortions.

### Acute Effects

Psychological effects include altered perception, euphoria or anxiety, introspective
thoughts, and synesthesia. Physiological effects include dilated pupils, increased
heart rate, elevated blood pressure, nausea, and increased body temperature. Nausea
and panic attacks are among the most commonly reported negative effects that limit
the appeal of repeated use.
