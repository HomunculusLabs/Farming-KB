# Oomycete Phylogeny and Straminipile Relationships
The Oomycota (oomycetes) and related heterotrophic
straminipiles were long classified as fungi based on their
filamentous growth habit and ecological roles as decomposers
and snow mold fungi. Molecular phylogenetics has revealed,
however, that these organisms belong to the Kingdom
Straminipila (also known as Chromista or Heterokonta), a
major eukaryotic lineage distinct from the Kingdom [[symbiotic-fungi-mycorrhizal-carbon-trading]]. This reclassification is one of the
most significant and paradigm shifts in modern mycology.
## The Straminipila
The Straminipila includes certain algae (Phaeophyceae -- brown
algae, diatoms, golden-brown algae, chrysophytes) and a group
of heterotrophs that previously was classified in the fungi
(Oomycota), along with a variety of "protozoan" groups. The
kingdom name "Straminipila" has not been formally described
but is commonly recognized. Various names have been applied
in conflicting ways, including Chromista and Heterokonta.
## Defining Characteristics
The Phaeophyta, Oomycota, and other straminipiles are
characterized by:
- **Zoospores with two flagella** -- one smooth (whiplash)
  and one bearing tubular tripartite hairs; the flagella are
  anteriorly or laterally inserted
- **Tubular tripartite hairs** -- a synapomorphic character
  of the group, also found as ornamentation on certain
  zoospore cyst walls
- **Chlorophylls a and c** -- in photosynthetic members
- **Cell wall composition** -- cellulose rather than chitin,
  distinguishing straminipiles from true fungi
In some instances the smooth flagellum or even both flagella
have been lost evolutionarily.
## Separation from True Fungi
The separation of oomycetes from the Kingdom Fungi was
confirmed by molecular characters including ribosomal DNA
sequences. However, mycologists had long recognized
morphological and biochemical traits that distinguished
straminipiles from true fungi, including:
- Flagellation patterns (biflagellate with tripartite hairs
  versus uniflagellate with smooth flagellum in chytrids,
  or non-flagellate in most other fungi)
- Cell wall composition (cellulose versus chitin)
- Pesticide sensitivity (differences in response to
  fungicides)
- Biochemical pathways (lysine biosynthesis via
  diaminopimelate pathway versus the alpha-aminoadipate
  [[shikimate-pathway-in-fungi]])
## Phylogenetic Hypotheses for Oomycetes
Phylogenetic hypotheses for oomycetes (Class
Peronosporomycetes) define two major clades:
### Saprolegniomycetidae (Water Molds)
The Saprolegniomycetidae includes primarily aquatic or
semi-aquatic organisms. The Saprolegniales appears to be
monophyletic, although the large genus Achlya within this
order is polyphyletic. These organisms are commonly found in
freshwater habitats and include:
- Saprolegnia -- common saprobes and parasites of fish
  eggs and aquatic invertebrates
- Achlya -- water molds found in freshwater habitats
- Leptomitus -- sewage fungus indicator organisms
### Peronosporomycetidae (Plant and Animal Parasites)
The Peronosporomycetidae includes the most economically
important oomycetes as plant pathogens. Major clades include:
- **Pythium clade** -- soil-borne pathogens causing cervantes root rot and root rots; Pythium species are important in
  agriculture and horticulture
- **Phytophthora clade** -- devastating plant pathogens
  including Phytophthora infestans (cause of the Irish
  potato famine), P. ramorum (sudden oak death), and
  P. cinnamomi (root rot of many tree species)
- **Peronospora clade** -- the downy mildews, obligate biotrophic parasites of flowering
  plants with more than 300 described species
- **Albugo clade** -- white rusts, obligate parasites that
  cause white blister-like symptoms on above-ground plant
  parts
The taxonomic placement of the order Rhipidiales, a group
with unusual obligate fermentative metabolism, remains
uncertain. It may represent an independent lineage within the
oomycetes of equivalent rank to the two established subclasses,
or may be included within Peronosporomycetidae.
## Hyphochytriomycetes
The Hyphochytriomycetes possess cells with a single anteriorly
inserted flagellum bearing tubular tripartite hairs. Their
phylogenetic position among the straminipiles as the sister
group of oomycetes was long assumed based on ultrastructural
comparisons and has been confirmed by analyses of rDNA from
three species of hyphochytrids.
## Labyrinthulales and Thraustochytriales
These primarily marine and brackish-water organisms associate with marine plants and bivalves, often as parasites. Several
investigators have placed the Labyrinthulales in the
straminipiles based on rDNA analyses. A broader question
concerning the monophyly of all heterotrophic members of the
straminipile clade has not yet been resolved.
Results of SSU and LSU rDNA sequence analyses are consistent
with, but do not strongly support, the monophyly of
Labyrinthulales and Thraustochytriales with oomycetes and
hyphochytrids. A third lineage composed of two species of
Labyrinthuloides was identified as basal to Labyrinthulales
and Thraustochytriales.
