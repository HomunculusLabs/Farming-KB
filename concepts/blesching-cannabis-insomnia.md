# Cannabis and Insomnia

Insomnia is difficulty or inability to fall or stay asleep. While many possible
causes exist for insomnia, it is interesting to note that its preceding
symptoms mirror the condition's effects. Depending on the severity, insomnia
affects mental and physical performance as well as emotional expressions
(sufferers become moody or irritable). Insomnia may produce symptoms of
anxiety, depression, low energy and affect, fatigue, hallucinations, lowered
immunity, and hormone disruption, each with its own set of possible
complications such as hypertension or heart disease.

## Types and Causes

If insomnia follows acute pain, jet lag, or a new work schedule, it is usually
self-correcting with time. Insomnia due to other causes is more complex.
Insomnia caused by [[blesching-cannabis-anxiety-disorders]] or use of addictive
substances requires more time and active intervention to resolve.

## Orthodox Treatment

Orthodox medicine's approach to insomnia includes diagnostics to determine
underlying causes. Common pharmacological treatments for insomnia include:

- Psychoactive benzodiazepines such as Valium (can be addictive)
- Sedative-hypnotic drugs like Ambien (can be addictive)
- Opiates for the co-treatment of pain (can be addictive)
- Antidepressants (possible serious side effects)

While these agents help manage the symptoms, none of these medications cure
chronic insomnia, which usually returns once the medication is stopped.

## Cannabis and Insomnia

Like cannabis, the majority of common pharmacological treatments for insomnia
affect both body and mind. Most studies on the effects of cannabis and sleep
took place in the 1970s and 1980s. These early studies revealed that cannabis
had a varied impact on sleep. The plant seemed to act like a sedative in some
ways but also reduced deep sleep.

However, the Farnborough study (2004) revealed an apparent self-balancing
mechanism between CBD and THC:

- Dose-dependent THC appears to have sedative effects
- Dose-dependent CBD appears to have some alerting properties

This finding has important implications for using cannabis as a sleep aid,
suggesting that strain selection and cannabinoid ratios matter significantly.

## Strain-Specific Considerations

Practical reports from patients using cannabis to improve their sleep have
shown that the use of indica and indica-dominant strains with their particular
mix of cannabinoid ratios, namely a relatively lower THC:CBD combination,
encourages sedation, relaxation, and grounding effects.

This aligns with the Farnborough study findings: since THC has sedative
properties and CBD has alerting properties, indica strains with lower THC and
higher CBD ratios might actually be counterproductive for sleep. Patients
seeking sleep benefits may want to experiment with THC-dominant indica strains
while monitoring their individual response. See
[[blesching-cannabis-dosing-strategies]] for detailed guidance.

## Mind-Body Medicine and Insomnia

Researchers discovered that insomniac patient populations appear to have two
characteristic traits that may make them vulnerable to developing the disorder
but also provide a potential basis for therapeutic interventions:

- A poor mechanism for managing stress
- States of cognitive-emotional hyperarousal (exaggerated mental-emotional
  responses and/or tensions)

Since both insomnia and hyperarousal are frequently present in
[[blesching-cannabis-ptsd-anxiety]], some clinicians suggest a potential similar
connection with past traumatic events in cases of insomniacs.

Whatever the physical reasons for insomnia may be, a commonly observed thread
is the inability to let go and relax. The mind is constantly engaged in
thinking to a point where it could be considered excessive, suggesting a
belief that forces the mind to control and dominate the thinking process
itself. Regular, deep, and restful sleep usually returns once the person
learns to trust the process of life.

Aggravating factors include chronic stress, poor stress management, unresolved
past traumatic events, fear-based beliefs, or inappropriate trust.

Powerful questions:

- What is going to happen if I let go and fall asleep?
- I cannot trust myself because...?
- I cannot trust life because...?
- Can I let go of these definitions or beliefs?
- What must I believe to have a peaceful slumber and restful sleep?

Suggested affirmation: "The day is over, this day is gone. I release the day
and all the good and bad that may have come with it."
