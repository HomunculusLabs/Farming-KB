## Seaweed Cultivation

Seaweed (macroalgae) is one of the most productive mariculture crops.
Mollison notes that seaweeds can produce 10-20 times more biomass per
hectare than terrestrial crops, requiring no freshwater, fertilizer, or
arable land. [[biodiversity-of-fungi-ecosystem-services-agriculture]] including water
filtration, [[fungal-ecosystem-processes-overview]]. Mollison distinguishes between
intensive aquaculture (which he critiques for pollution and feed
dependency) and extensive or integrated systems:

  - Polyculture: Multiple fish species occupying different trophic
    levels in the same pond or enclosure, such as tilapia (herbivore)
    with catfish (omnivore) and carp (detritivore)
  - Silvofishery: Integrating mangrove trees with fish and shrimp
    ponds, where mangroves provide habitat, water filtration, and
    leaf litter as feed
  - Sea ranching: Releasing hatchery-reared fish into natural
    environments and harvesting them as they return to spawn or
    reach harvestable size
  - Integrated multi-trophic aquaculture (IMTA): Combining fish
    farming with seaweed and shellfish culture so that waste
    nutrients from fish are absorbed by seaweeds and filtered by
    shellfish, creating a balanced production system
