## New United Nations and Alternatives to Political Systems

[[bill-mollison]]"s permaculture framework extends beyond land design into social and political restructuring. Two central concepts emerge: a reimagined "United Nations" based on shared ethics rather than nation-state representation, and a fundamental critique of existing political systems that calls for decentralised, life-centred alternatives.

## Critique of the Existing United Nations

The existing United Nations is characterised as neither united nor representative of true nations. Many genuine nations — such as the Iroquois confederation, Basques, Tartars, Kurds, Palestinians, Hawaiians, Hopi, Tibetans, Pitjatjantjara, Misquito, Aranda, Basarwa, and Herrero — are not represented. Most nations within the UN are described as repressing a majority of the world"s peoples.

## Redefining "Nation"

Mollison proposes defining a nation as a people subscribing to a common ethic and aspiring to a similar culture. Such nations may not share a common land base or language, but share a minimal common ethic:

- **Care for the earth**: to repair and conserve
- **Seek peace**: and guard human rights everywhere
- **Invest all resources**: capital, intelligence, goodwill, and labour toward these ends

Thousands of organisations, affinities, tribes, bioregions, and non-governmental organisations already aspire to these ends. In every continent, a majority of people — the "ethical majority" — want peace, a clean and forested earth, cessation of torture, malnutrition, and oppression, and the right to work towards these ends.

## Decentralised Organizational Structure

Unlike the existing United Nations, this alternative does not require a world centre or paid administrators. Instead, groups meet as affinity groups — through alternative economic summits, [[bioregional-congresses-and-global-nation-permaculture]], tribal conferences, and garden and farm design groups — to deal with specific areas of interest, making affinities global in scope.

By avoiding centralised administrations, power blocs are avoided. By avoiding tax funding, inefficiency is reduced. Fees for a regional secretariat arise from annual fees forwarded by participant groups. Once continental and global groups have allied, these congresses can bring in less informed or remote groups to share resources in a humane alliance.

Global seed exchanges, technology groups, gardening forums, and regional groups already meet and are increasing in cooperation. The concept of a global nation is well-developed in such groups, where the idea of war or oppression across race, language, or territory is anathema to those allied in good works.

## Critique of Political Systems

### Power-Centred vs. Life-Centred Systems

Systems of government are described as based on self-interest, economic pragmatism, belief, impractical theory, and power-centred minorities (religious, military, capitalist, communist, familial, or criminal). Almost all set up competitive and adversary-oriented systems.

The world needs a non-polarised, non-contentious politic — not one made possible by those in situations promoting left-right, black-white, capitalist-communist, believer-infidel thinking. Such systems promote antagonism and destroy cooperation and interdependence.

### The Short-Term Problem

Political systems are inherently temporary, creating an impediment to effective long-term action on problems like soil degradation, groundwater pollution, forest decline, poverty, unemployment, and malnutrition. Despite good scientific prognoses, effective ground strategies are lacking because continuing and long-sustained programmes necessary to reverse environmental decline are sacrificed for short-term policies of an elite maintaining power.

### Creative Use of Differences

It is always possible to use differences creatively and design to use them rather than eliminate groups as infidels. Belief is a basically private and non-global characteristic that should not be subject to comparisons. What matters is how all groups relate to the basic rules of the local ecology that permit them to function on a long-term basis.

## The Right Not to Be in Debt

### Shelter Without Financial Systems

Some of the most appropriate houses on earth are built without bank loans, architects, metals, concrete, or contractors. Stone, mud, bamboo, round timbers, rope, thatch, baked brick, and tiles are age-old durable building materials that can all be locally produced if energy from community forests and people is provided.

The real cause of lack of shelter in any country is not finance but restrictive practices by a regulatory bureaucracy. State or private ownership of forests, small mines, and lands denies basic biological and earth resources to the people who work to produce or mine them.

### Personal Action Over Political Theory

"The argument for simplicity is never a political argument... when people practice it in their lives... they don"t even need any politics." Changes in people come about by education and information, and when enough people change, political systems may follow — or become irrelevant. The place to start change is first with the individual, and second in one's region or neighbourhood.

## Practical Implications for Permaculture Design

This social framework directly informs permaculture practice: community-based land access, local economic systems, [[bioregional-organisation]], and the emphasis on self-reliance over dependency on centralised political and economic structures. The transition to a cooperative global society must be creative rather than destructive, ethical rather than coercive, and local in origin while global in aspiration.
