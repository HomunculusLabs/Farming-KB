# Mushroom [[spawn-production]] — Grain Spawn and Tissue Culture

Complete spawn production workflow from the FAO training manual: PDA medium preparation, tissue culture selection, PDA-to-PDA transfer, and grain spawn multiplication on sorghum seeds. This is the most technically demanding part of mushroom cultivation.

See also: [[mushroom-training-adapted-cultivation]], [[mushroom-training-oyster-straw-cultivation]], [[mushroom-training-pest-disease-management]], [[gourmet-medicinal-mushroom-spawn-grain-production]]

## Overview

Spawn production involves three stages:
1. **PDA medium** (Potato Dextrose Agar) — the growth medium
2. **Tissue culture** — isolating pure mycelium from a mushroom
3. **Grain spawn** — multiplying mycelium on sorghum seeds for inoculating substrate bags

Not all trainees need to master spawn production. Many buy inoculated bags ready for fruiting. However, understanding the full process enables future expansion and higher income (spawn bags can be sold for supplemental income). Expected contamination loss: ~3%.

## Stage 1: PDA Medium Production

### Materials
- Potatoes: 200g
- Dextrose: 20g
- Agar powder: 20g
- Water: 1 liter
- Cotton (gauze)
- Small flat bottles (whiskey bottles work well)

### Procedure
1. Visually check potatoes for spots or rot; buy commercial-grade dextrose and agar
2. Wash and cut potatoes into 1cm cubes (skin on or off)
3. Clean bottles thoroughly
4. Simmer potatoes in 1 liter water for 15-20 minutes
5. Remove potatoes, keep broth as clear as possible; add water to reach 1 liter
6. Add dextrose then agar; stir continuously at regular speed until fully dissolved
7. Pour into bottles to 5-10mm depth
8. Plug with cotton
9. Autoclave at 121C for 20-30 minutes
10. Cool to ~37C
11. Place bottles at slant to increase medium surface area (PDA should approach neck but not touch cotton)
12. Transfer to clean shelf in clean room
13. Check for contamination (dark spots or lines = contaminated)
14. Wait 2-3 days; if no contamination, PDA is ready for tissue culture

## Stage 2: Tissue Culture Selection

### Materials
- Special needle (insulated handle)
- Alcohol lamp, alcohol, cotton, matches/lighter
- PDA bottles
- Laminar flow cabinet (or protected clean environment)
- UV lamp

### Procedure
1. Select a strong, healthy mushroom for culture:
   - Not too mature, not too young
   - Not too humid (wait 2-3 hours after watering)
   - Stiff stalk
   - Clean, far from any contaminated mushrooms
2. Clean room, tools, and laminar flow cabinet with alcohol
3. Place all materials in laminar flow; run UV lamp 10-15 minutes, then turn off UV (leave laminar flow on)
4. Clean hands and bottles with alcohol; insert hands into cabinet
5. Hold needle with 2 fingers at 45-degree angle; flame until red hot
6. Let needle cool 15-20 seconds (do not touch any surface)
7. Tear mushroom lengthwise with fingers — DO NOT use a knife
8. Cut a small piece (2mm x 2mm) of fleshy tissue from inside the mushroom (between cap and stalk)
9. Flame around bottle mouth; remove cotton plug near flame
10. Insert needle, place tissue piece in middle of PDA surface
11. Close bottle immediately near flame with cotton plug
12. **Critical**: bottle bottom always lower than mouth; mouth stays near flame at all times
13. Label: date, mushroom type, mother spawn number

### PDA-to-PDA Transfer

Because tissue culture has high contamination risk, do it in only a few bottles first. Then multiply from clean mycelium:

1. Cut 5mm x 5mm piece of mycelium from clean PDA
2. Transfer to new PDA bottle using same sterile technique
3. Incubation to full mycelium: 10-15 days (species dependent)
4. Check daily for infection; monitor growth rate
5. Store mature mycelium in cool place or refrigerator (vegetable section)
6. Keep detailed observation notes

## Stage 3: Grain Spawn on Sorghum Seeds

### Grain Selection

Various grains can be used: sorghum, millet, wheat. Requirements:
- Recently harvested
- Few broken kernels
- Little contamination
- No fungi, no insects
- No more than 12% humidity

Sorghum is preferred in tropical Asian cultivation.

### Materials
- Sorghum seeds
- Flask-type bottles
- Cotton, paper squares (7cm x 7cm), rubber bands
- Alcohol lamp, alcohol

### Procedure

**Preparation:**
1. Soak sorghum overnight: 2 liters water per 1 kg grain
2. Wash and strain to remove all water
3. Steam 30-45 minutes to soften grains (cook ~25%)
4. Drain and spread to cool, decrease moisture
5. Fill bottles 3/4 full with sorghum seeds
6. Tightly plug with cotton

**Sterilization:**
7. Transfer to sterilization chamber
8. Release all air, then maintain 15 lb/sq.inch (121C) for 30 min (small) or 45 min (medium chamber)
9. Let cool

**Inoculation:**
10. Transfer bottles to clean cool place
11. Clean laminar flow with alcohol; UV lamp 10-15 minutes
12. Clean hands with alcohol
13. Flame needle until red, let cool
14. Cut 5mm x 5mm square of PDA mycelium (white)
15. Close PDA bottle immediately, keep near flame
16. Flame around sorghum bottle mouth; open near flame
17. Inoculate by placing PDA piece in middle of sorghum bottle
18. Close immediately; mouth higher than bottom at all times
19. Place paper over cotton, tie with rubber band
20. Label: date, spawn number, reference, inoculation time

**Incubation:**
21. Full mycelium growth: 10-15 days (species dependent)
22. Store mature spawn in cool place or refrigerator
23. Check for infection regularly
24. Remove contaminated bottles immediately
25. Clean contaminated bottles as normal glassware
26. Observe and collect data; expected ~3% loss
