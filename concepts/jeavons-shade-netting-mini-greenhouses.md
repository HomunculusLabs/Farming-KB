# Shade Netting and Mini-Greenhouses

Shade netting and mini-greenhouses are important tools in the [[jeavons-grow-biointensive-method-overview]]
method for protecting transplants, extending the [[savory-growing-season-and-non-growing-season-management]], and managing
the garden microclimate. Both structures are simple to build and use standard
materials.

## Shade Netting

### Purpose and Timing

After watering a newly planted bed in hot weather, the area should be covered
with 30-percent shade netting from approximately 10 A.M. to 5 P.M. The shade
netting protects young transplants from excessive heat and sun while they
become established.

Shade netting also serves as bird protection for newly transplanted grains in
the fall and winter. In this case, the netting is left on for 10 days. After 10
days, the shade netting is removed because at that point the plants are less
tasty to birds and therefore not attractive.

### Construction and Installation

Use shade netting that is 3 feet wider and 3 feet longer than the growing bed,
so the netting can drape down around the edges to provide shade on the sides as
well as the top.

The support structure uses 3-foot-long pieces of 1-inch by 1-inch wood inserted
on a 45-degree angle into the soil at the four corners of the bed and every 5
feet along and perpendicular to the sides. Headless nails are hammered part
way into the top end of the sticks so the shade netting can be hooked in place.

### Daily Routine

At 5 P.M., the netting is unhooked from the long eastern side of the bed and
hooked over the nails on the other side several times to secure it and keep it
out of the paths and the bed. This process is reversed at 10 A.M. the next
day. The nails should not jut into the path where they can be a hazard.

### Bird Protection Setup

For bird protection, long pieces of 1/2-inch rebar are used to hold down the
edges of the netting so birds cannot enter the growing area. The 1-inch by
1-inch sticks are adjusted so the netting edges lie on the ground with the
rebar along the edges.

## Mini-Greenhouses

### Design and Performance

A mini-greenhouse made from plastic sheeting and wood can increase the
temperature of the soil and the air surrounding plants. This allows an early
start on the growing season in spring and lengthens the growing season in
autumn.

The Ecology Action design features double-walled construction, which can keep
the inside temperature above freezing when the outside temperature falls as
low as 20 degrees Fahrenheit. This makes the unit a good season-extender for
crops. Detailed plans are available in Ecology Action's Backyard Homestead,
Mini-Farm and Garden Log Book.

### Uses

Mini-greenhouses serve multiple purposes in the GROW BIOINTENSIVE garden:

For spring set-out, they protect seedlings from late frost and cold nights. In
areas with very cold winters, seedlings can be grown in a good mini-greenhouse
9 to 12 weeks before the last spring frost.

For fall and winter growing, double-walled mini-greenhouses protect crops
during cold weather. Certain varieties are specifically noted for winter
growing under these conditions.

For hardening off seedlings, a greenhouse or miniature greenhouse can be used
when weather conditions require. [[hamilton-cold-frames-and-hardening-off]] serve a similar purpose for
two-day hardening periods when seedlings are almost ready to transplant.
