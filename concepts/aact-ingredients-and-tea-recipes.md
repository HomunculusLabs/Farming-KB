# AACT Ingredients and Bacterial vs Fungal Tea Recipes

## Overview

The composition of [[actively-aerated-compost-tea]] (AACT) is determined by two primary factors: the **compost** used as the inoculum source and the **food resources** added during brewing. Different crops and soil conditions require different microbial balances — specifically the ratio of bacteria to fungi — and the ingredients selected for brewing directly influence which organisms proliferate. Understanding how to formulate teas for bacterial dominance, fungal dominance, or balanced communities is essential for achieving desired results.

## The Foundation: Quality Compost

### Compost as the Living Inoculum

The compost used in AACT brewing is the seed source for all microbial life in the tea. No amount of food resources can compensate for poor compost. Requirements for AACT-quality compost include:

- **Diversity** — Must contain the full range of [[ingham-aact-beauveria-fruit-fly-beneficial-bacteria-inoculant]], fungi, protozoa, nematodes, and microarthropods appropriate for the target crop
- **No pathogens** — Compost must reach adequate temperatures (131-150°F / 55-65°C) for sufficient duration to kill human and plant pathogens
- **No toxic residues** — Free from persistent herbicides (clopyralid, aminopyralid), pesticides, and heavy metals
- **Active biology** — Organisms must be alive and capable of reproducing; compost should not be dried, frozen, or stored for extended periods
- **Proper C:N ratio** — Finished compost should have a C:N ratio between 15:1 and 25:1

### Thermal Compost vs. Vermicompost

| Property | Thermal Compost | Vermicompost |
|----------|----------------|-------------|
| Temperature | Reaches 131-150°F (55-65°C) | Ambient (60-80°F) |
| Fungal diversity | Higher (especially if woody materials used) | Moderate |
| Bacterial diversity | High | Very high |
| Protozoa | Present | Abundant |
| Nematodes | Present (if not killed by heat) | Present |
| Pathogen killing | Effective (heat kills pathogens) | Limited (relies on competition) |
| Best use in AACT | General purpose, fungal teas | Bacterial teas, rapid cycling |

| Organism Group | For Row Crops/Vegetables | For Vines/Shrubs | For Trees |

## AACT Food Ingredients

### Core Ingredients List

The following ingredients are commonly used in AACT brewing, each serving specific microbial feeding purposes:

#### Bacterial Foods (Promote Bacterial Growth)

| Ingredient | Function | Typical Rate (per 50 gal) |
|-----------|----------|--------------------------|
| Unsulfured [[blackstrap-molasses]] | Simple sugars (sucrose), minerals (Ca, Mg, K, Fe) | 1-4 oz (30-120 ml) |

| Flaked Alfalfa Meal | Nitrogen, growth regulators (triacontanol) | 1-2 oz |

| Oatmeal, Wheat Bran | Complex carbs + protein | 1-2 oz |

## Bacterial vs. Fungal Tea Recipes

### Understanding Bacterial vs. Fungal Dominance

Different plant communities evolved with different soil microbial balances:

- **Annual crops, grasses, brassicas** evolved in bacterial-dominated soils (bacteria:fungi ratio of 10:1 to 100:1)
- **Shrubs, vines, perennials** evolved in balanced soils (bacteria:fungi ratio of 1:1 to 10:1)
- **Coniferous and deciduous trees** evolved in fungal-dominated soils (bacteria:fungi ratio of 1:10 to 1:1000)

Applying the wrong microbial balance can actually increase disease susceptibility. For example, applying bacterial tea to an established forest ecosystem can disrupt the fungal network that trees depend on for nutrient cycling.

### Recipe 1: Bacterial-Dominated Tea

**Purpose**: Row crops, vegetables, lawns, early succession plants, annuals

**Compost source**: Vermicompost or bacterial thermal compost

**Per 50 gallons of water**:
- 2-5 lbs (1-2.5 kg) high-quality compost
- 4 oz (120 ml) unsulfured [[ingham-blackstrap-molasses-compost-tea-concentration-osmotic-effects]]
- 2 oz (60 ml) cold water kelp extract
- 2 oz (60 ml) fish hydrolysate
- 1 oz (30 ml) blood meal or alfalfa meal (optional)

**Brewing conditions**:
- Temperature: 65-75°F (18-24°C)
- Aeration: Vigorous, fine bubbles
- Duration: 24 hours
- Target: Active bacteria 150-300 µg/ml, fungi 5-15 µg/ml

### Recipe 2: Fungal-Dominated Tea

**Purpose**: Trees, shrubs, vines, perennials, orchards, timber

**Compost source**: Fungal compost (made with 40-60% woody materials)

**Per 50 gallons of water**:
- 5-10 lbs (2.5-5 kg) fungal compost
- 1 oz (30 ml) unsulfured blackstrap molasses (minimal — too much favors bacteria)
- 4 oz (120 ml) humic acid or liquid humus
- 2 oz (60 ml) fish hydrolysate
- 2 oz kelp meal (coarse, not extract)
- 1-2 oz rock dust
- 1 cup oatmeal or wheat bran

**Brewing conditions**:
- Temperature: 65-75°F (18-24°C)
- Aeration: Gentle, larger bubbles (avoid shearing fungal hyphae)
- Duration: 24-48 hours (longer brew allows fungal growth)
- Target: Active fungi 50-200+ µg/ml, bacteria 100-200 µg/ml
