## Winter Forest Foraging and Wildlife Cooperation

Mollison's treatment of winter foraging in temperate forest systems emphasises
cooperation between humans and wildlife rather than competition. By designing
food forests with shared winter resources, both wild species and domestic
animals can thrive through the lean season.

## Tree Crop Foraging in Winter

Hard mast from deciduous trees forms the backbone of winter foraging systems:

- **Acorns:** Once leached of tannins, acorns provide a carbohydrate staple
  comparable to grain. Mollison notes that many oak species in the northern
  hemisphere produce reliably even in poor years. Select low-tannin varieties
  (white oak group) to reduce processing time. Acorns store well in dry
  conditions through spring.

- **Filberts (hazelnuts):** Extremely reliable bearers, filberts produce
  annually with minimal care once established. Their small size makes them
  ideal for wildlife and [[polypore-fungi-eastern-north-america]] American forest zone.
  Mollison recommends planting them on contour for [[mollison-designers-cool-humid-climate-design-overview]],
branch piles on north-facing slopes retain moisture longest, supporting
