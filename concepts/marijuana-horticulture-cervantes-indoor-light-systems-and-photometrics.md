## Indoor Light Systems and Photometrics for Cannabis

## Photosynthetically Active Radiation (PAR)

Plants use only a specific portion of the light spectrum for photosynthesis — the region between 400 and 700 nanometers (nm), called the Photosynthetically Active Radiation (PAR) zone. The most important colors for maximum chlorophyll production and photosynthetic response are in the blue and red ranges.

"PAR watts" measures the actual amount of specific photons a plant needs to grow. Photons are discrete packets of light energy; photosynthesis is activated by their assimilation. Blue photons are worth more PAR watts than red photons, though the exact differential remains difficult to quantify. Each color of light activates different plant functions — blue light controls phototropism (leaf orientation toward light) while red light drives flowering responses.

Most commercial light meters measure in foot-candles, lux, or lumens — metrics designed for human vision (most sensitive at 525-625 nm), not plant photosynthesis. Plants utilize large portions of the spectrum not measured by these instruments. Despite this limitation, foot-candle readings still provide a useful approximation of light distribution in a grow space.

## Light Measurement Units

- **Foot-candle**: Illumination equal to one candle's intensity at one foot distance
- **Lux**: 10.76 lux = 1 foot-candle
- **Lumen**: Total light output from a source (weighted to human vision)
- **PAR watt**: Photon energy usable by plants (400-700 nm)
- **Kelvin (K)**: Color temperature of emitted light (3000-6500 K optimal for cannabis)
- **CRI** (Color Rendering Index): Higher CRI = better for growing

## HID Lamp Selection

High Intensity Discharge (HID) lamps are the standard for serious indoor cultivation. Available wattages: 150, 175, 250, 400, 600, 1000, and 1100 watts.

### Metal Halide (MH)

Metal halide lamps produce a blue-dominated spectrum ideal for vegetative growth. The blue light promotes compact, leafy growth with short internodes. MH lamps are the preferred choice for mother plants, clones, and the vegetative phase. They produce approximately 100-115 lumens per watt depending on the specific model.

### High Pressure Sodium (HPS)

HPS lamps emit an orange-red dominated spectrum that drives flowering and fruiting. They are more efficient than MH, producing 120-150 lumens per watt. HPS is the standard for flowering rooms. The warm spectrum (2700-3000K) triggers and supports bud development. Many growers use MH for vegetative growth and switch to HPS for flowering, though HPS alone can sustain the full lifecycle.

### Wattage and Coverage

| Wattage | Coverage Area | Best Use |
|---------|--------------|----------|
| 150-250W | 3×3 ft (0.8 m²) | Closets, small spaces |
| 400W | 4×4 ft (1.5 m²) | Small grow rooms |
| 600W | 5×5 ft (2.3 m²) | Medium rooms (most efficient g/W) |
| 1000W | 6×6 ft (3.3 m²) | Large rooms |

Light intensity diminishes substantially beyond 4 feet from the bulb. A single 1000W metal halide can support enough vegetative plants to fill a 4000W flowering room.

## Grow Room Design

### Room Setup Protocol

1. **Choose space**: Out-of-the-way area with minimal traffic. Minimum 5-foot ceiling height (account for container height and lamp clearance).
2. **Enclose**: Remove non-garden items. Plywood walls or reflective plastic. Ensure no light leaks visible from outside.
3. **Reflective surfaces**: Flat white paint or Mylar on walls, ceiling, and floor. Reflective coverage increases effective lamp output by 10-20%.
4. **Ventilation**: At least one fresh-air vent. Oscillating fan for air circulation — not fixed directly on plants to avoid windburn.
5. **Light placement**: Hang lamps with adjustable height chains for each growth phase.

### Production Models

**Single room**: Simplest approach. Grow vegetative → flower → harvest → clean → start over. Involves downtime between crops.

**Two rooms** (recommended): Vegetative/clone room (¼ the size of flowering room) + dedicated flowering room. When the flowering room is harvested, plants move from [[cervantes-reservoir-temperature-management]] is critical:

- Day temperature: 70-75°F (21-24°C)
- Night temperature: 55-60°F (13-16°C)
- Air should be pumped in from below, cooling plants and lights as it rises
- Heated air rises to the ceiling and is exhausted through a vent/filter
- Wing-style reflectors (e.g., Adjust-a-Wings) can be positioned close or far to manage heat at the canopy

## Reflectors

Reflector design profoundly affects light distribution and intensity at the canopy:

- **Standard horizontal reflectors**: Adequate for basic setups, direct light downward
- **Parabolic reflectors**: Distribute light more evenly over a wider area
- **Adjustable wing reflectors**: Most versatile — can be positioned close for intensity or far for even coverage. Include spreaders to eliminate hot spots.

Case study: Nigel and Terry achieved a 137% improvement in grams-per-watt by upgrading reflectors and optimizing light placement in their second crop (0.63 g/W → 1.49 g/W).
