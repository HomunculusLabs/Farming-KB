# Herbal First Aid

Herbal first aid is the application of plant-based remedies for acute injuries and conditions that require immediate attention. A well-stocked herbal first aid kit can address most common minor injuries and acute illnesses encountered at home, in the garden, or on homesteads. Herbal first aid is complementary to, not a replacement for, conventional emergency medical care for serious injuries.

## Building a Herbal First Aid Kit

A comprehensive herbal first aid kit should contain preparations for the most common acute situations: wounds, burns, bites and stings, sprains and strains, digestive upset, respiratory congestion, allergic reactions, fever, pain, and anxiety or shock.

The kit should include both topical preparations (salves, oils, clays, poultice materials) and internal preparations (tinctures, teas, glycerites). Commercially prepared remedies are convenient, but homemade preparations are equally effective when properly made and stored. See [[herbal-salves-and-balms]] for salve recipes.

## Topical First Aid Remedies

Calendula salve is the single most important topical remedy in the herbal first aid kit. Calendula (Calendula officinalis) is effective for virtually all minor skin injuries including cuts, abrasions, burns, scrapes, rashes, and insect bites. Its combination of antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and tissue-healing properties makes it the first-line topical treatment for skin injuries. ## Burn Care

Burns are common injuries that respond well to herbal treatment. For minor burns (first degree and small second degree), immediate herbal treatment can reduce pain, prevent infection, and promote healing.

Aloe vera gel is the first-line treatment for fresh burns. The cool, soothing gel provides immediate pain relief and creates a protective barrier over damaged skin. Fresh gel from a living aloe plant is preferred. Apply generously and reapply as the gel dries.

Lavender [[permaculture-designers-manual-essential-oil-plants]] applied neat (undiluted) to minor burns is a traditional French aromatherapy remedy. A few drops applied immediately after a burn can reduce pain, blistering, and scarring. This treatment is supported by clinical observation though not by large controlled trials.

Calendula salve or St. John's Wort oil can be applied after the initial acute phase (24 to 48 hours) to promote tissue repair and reduce scarring. Honey, particularly Manuka honey, can be used as a wound dressing for more serious burns to prevent infection and promote healing.

For severe burns (large area, deep tissue, or face, hands, feet, or genitals), seek immediate medical attention. Herbal remedies are complementary to, not replacements for, professional burn care.

## Insect Bites and Stings

Plantain is the first remedy for insect bites and stings. Fresh leaves chewed and applied as a poultice provide immediate relief from pain and itching. The astringent and drawing properties help neutralize venom and reduce swelling.

Baking soda paste (mixed with water) is a simple but effective treatment for bee and wasp stings. Applied immediately, it neutralizes the acidic venom and reduces pain and swelling.

For tick bites, the primary concern is Lyme disease and other tick-borne illnesses. After removing the tick, apply antimicrobial herbs (tea tree oil, thyme oil diluted in carrier oil) and monitor the bite site for the characteristic bullseye rash. Echinacea tincture taken internally may support immune response to potential infection.

For spider bites of unknown species, clean the area, apply a drawing poultice (plantain or clay), and seek medical attention if severe symptoms develop. Herbal treatment is supportive but should not delay professional care for potentially dangerous bites.

## Fractures, Sprains, and Strains

Comfrey (Symphytum officinale) is traditionally known as "knitbone" for its ability to accelerate healing of broken bones and torn tissues. Comfrey poultices or compresses applied to sprains, strains, and fractures (after professional setting) promote tissue repair through allantoin's cell-proliferative effects. Apply only to intact skin due to pyrrolizidine alkaloid concerns.

Arnica is used alongside comfrey for its anti-inflammatory and pain-relieving effects on musculoskeletal injuries. Arnica reduces bruising and swelling while comfrey promotes tissue repair.

Turmeric taken internally provides systemic anti-inflammatory support for acute musculoskeletal injuries. The anti-inflammatory action of curcumin complements the topical treatments.
