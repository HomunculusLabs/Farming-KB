# Fungi on Living Plants — Anthracnose, Ergot, and Fruit Rots
Fungi that infect living plant tissues — leaves, stems, flowers, and fruits
— include some of the most destructive plant pathogens known. They range
from biotrophs that can only grow on living tissue to facultative saprobes
that kill tissue then sporulate.

## Obligate vs. Facultative Biotrophs

### Obligate Biotrophs
- Cannot be cultured away from living host
- Obtain nutrients only from living cells via haustoria
- Examples: rusts, powdery mildews, downy mildews

### Facultative Biotrophs
- Cause disease in living plants but can sporulate after plant tissue dies
- Often cultured easily in vitro
- Sporulation occurs in dead tissue islands or after host death
- Symptoms can be confused with obligate biotrophs

## Ergot (Claviceps — Clavicipitaceae)

### The Pathogen
Clavicipitaceae (Hypocreales, Ascomycota): 27 genera, mostly systemic
endophytes or insect parasites. Claviceps contains ~35 species worldwide.

### Life Cycle and Host Range
Ergot fungi infect grasses and cereals, replacing the ovary with a dark
sclerotium (ergot body) rich in alkaloids including ergotamine and
ergocristine. The life cycle begins when airborne ascospores infect
flowering grass heads during the receptive period. Infection requires
synchrony between [[ergot-and-lysergic-acid-history]] derivatives from ergot led
directly to the synthesis of LSD by Albert Hofmann in 1938.
