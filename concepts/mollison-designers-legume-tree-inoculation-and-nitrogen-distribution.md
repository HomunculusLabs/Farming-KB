## Nitrogen Yield Assessment

To assess total nitrogen yield, Mollison advises evaluating both
soil nitrogen from mycelia (approximately 200 kg/ha/year) and leaf
and slash nitrogen per hectare. In crops such as Tephrosia, yielding
13.5 tonnes per hectare in 4 cuts, leaf nitrogen should be about
20-30 kg per tonne, or 1,000-1,500 kg/ha/year. This is several
factors higher than the root nitrogen yield alone.

Phosphate and potash levels in green mulch are also satisfactory for
crop production, making legume mulch a complete nutrient package
rather than simply a nitrogen source.

## Management Considerations for Legume Tree Crops

### Spacing for Foliage Production
With shrubs and small trees, 0.5 m apart is best for foliage
production, with a trimming height of 0.5-1.5 m recommended.

### Spacing for Shelter and Seed
Trees can be more widely spaced at 2-20 m for root nitrogen, seed
production for poultry and bees, and for in-crop shelter. Flowering
and [[cervantes-seed-production-storage]] is better at wider spacing depending on tree
size. A full canopy may be needed to reduce or eliminate frost.

### Seasonal Timing
Only in frost-free tropics can trees be trimmed all year (4-5 cuts).
Wherever cold is a seasonal problem, two months of growth must be
allowed to harden the plant before winter, or it will weaken.

### Replacement
Many trees will coppice for 4-30 years, but any sign of loss of
vigour indicates the need to replant. Replant for small shrubs may
be necessary every 2-3 years.
