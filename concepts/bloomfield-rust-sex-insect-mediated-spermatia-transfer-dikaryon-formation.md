After several days of growth within the barberry leaf tissue, the
mycelium forms tiny flask-shaped structures called spermagonia
(singular: spermagonium) that rupture through the upper epidermis of
the leaf. Thin receptive hairs protrude through the opening of each
spermagonium, and the structure exudes a sugary nectar along with
masses of spherical cells called spermatia. The spermagonia are
embedded within the leaf tissue, with only their openings and the
protruding hairs visible on the leaf surface. The nectar they produce
is sufficiently attractive to draw numerous insect visitors despite the
microscopic scale of the structures.
