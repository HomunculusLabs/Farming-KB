### Flake Spawn (French Type)

In France, "flake" spawn held a monopoly of the market, and most large French growers prepared all the material they used by means of a "starter" of "virgin" spawn. This method opened the way not merely for the production of high-grade mushroom spawn in the United States, but also for its production by a **selection method**—the ability to choose specific desirable varieties and propagate them reliably.

### Overcoming Skepticism

English growers had offered the information that spawn-making in the United States would not be successful on account of climatic conditions. Similarly, the author had been assured in France that extreme conditions would render mushroom culture unsatisfactory. The pure culture method proved both assertions wrong.

### Implementation and Impact

The perfection of the method and its application by spawn growers required a few years, and still further time was needed for this to react upon commercial mushroom production. The experimental pure culture spawn made and distributed under the auspices of the Department of Agriculture was very successful.

Unfortunately, certain irresponsible firms took advantage of the new movement to advertise and sell quantities of poor product, creating utter confusion among amateurs. In time, however, several reliable and painstaking growers mastered all necessary bacteriological (pure culture) and mechanical details, ultimately launching highly successful enterprises.

### Market Transformation

The impact of pure culture spawn was transformative:

- **Poor spawn was largely driven from the markets**
- **Quality of English imported spawn advanced** through successful competition with superior American product
- **80 to 90 percent** of mushrooms grown during the 1913–1914 season were reported to be from American spawn of pure culture origin
- **American spawn was actually exported to Europe**—a complete reversal of the earlier dependency

## Spawn-Making as a Special Enterprise

Modern spawn production is based on a **selection principle** and involves knowledge of laboratory pure culture methods and bacteriological technique. Spawn-making is a special industry, and it is not practicable for every mushroom grower to make their own spawn satisfactorily unless prepared to acquire necessary training and accept initial failures.

The grower should, however, understand the general process to better appreciate the necessity of good spawn and to be justified in demanding good quality and tested varieties.

## The Traditional "Chance" Method

### Virgin Spawn

From the earliest records of mushroom culture, growers depended upon "spontaneous" or "native" occurrence of spawn in pastures, compost piles, or elsewhere. This naturally occurring spawn was known as **"virgin" spawn** and presumably came from the accidental germination of spores. The grower was forced to use this spawn without adequately testing its productiveness and often without even knowing the variety secured.

As mushroom growing developed, the demand for virgin spawn increased to such extent that its location and sale became almost a special industry. In England, **"mill-track" virgin spawn**—originally the spawn found in the well-trodden soil and manure of horse paths at animal-driven mills—was much sought, though it later became merely a trade name.

### The Traditional Propagation Method

The traditional grower whose source of spawn was the pasture proceeded by:

1. Finding a natural occurrence of mushrooms in a favorable locality
2. Removing the sod and preparing small trenches filled with good stable manure
3. Returning the sod over the trenches
4. Allowing spawn to grow and spread vigorously over several weeks
5. Using this material for spawn-making or mushroom growing

Virgin spawn was used either directly to inoculate mushroom beds, or (more frequently) to spawn compost intended to yield a large amount of "crop spawn."

### The Problem of Deterioration

Experience showed that continuously transferring spawn from one bed to another was unprofitable if carried too far. The practice resulted in complete **"running out" or deterioration** of the spawn if persisted in for about three years. No transfer of spawn should be made from a bed which has begun to bear mushrooms vigorously—whether by direct transfer of growing spawn into fresh beds or by transfer after drying and preserving until the next season.

### Limitations of the Chance Method

Any method of spawn-making involving an unknown strain or variety is essentially haphazard. By such chance procedure there can be:

- No progress in selection and maintenance of desirable strains or varieties
- No accumulation of benefits from breeding
- No reliable knowledge of the worth of the strain without preliminary testing

These fundamental limitations motivated the development of pure culture methods that would enable systematic variety selection and reliable propagation.
