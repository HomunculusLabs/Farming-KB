# Fungal Plant Pathogens

Devastating fungal diseases of crops and forests — rusts, blasts, blights, and mycotoxins from Money's "Mr. Bloomfield's Orchard."

## Black Stem Rust (Puccinia graminis)

The most studied fungal pathogen. Causes more crop damage than any other disease. Its complex life cycle requires two hosts:

1. **Wheat (primary host)**: produces uredospores (red pustules) and teliospores (black pustules)
2. **Barberry (alternate host)**: produces spermatia and aeciospores

The eradication of barberry from wheat-growing regions retarded the evolution of new rust strains by eliminating the sexual stage. Control relies on resistant cultivars and fungicides (triazoles like triadimefon inhibit ergosterol biosynthesis).

## Coffee Rust (Hemileia vastatrix)

Produces uredospores, teliospores, and basidiospores but lacks spermatium and aeciospore stages — an "edited" life cycle because it has no alternate host. Control limited to fungicide spraying and resistant cultivars. Devastating to economies in South and Central America, Africa, and Asia.

## Rice Blast (Magnaporthe grisea)

The most powerful mechanical pathogen on Earth. Infection mechanism:

1. Pear-shaped conidium lands on rice leaf and sticks with mucilage
2. If leaf is wet, germ tube forms and produces an appressorium
3. Appressorium becomes melanized (pigmented), fills with glycerol, pressurizes to 80+ atmospheres
4. A penetration hypha is forced through the leaf surface

The appressorium can puncture bulletproof vest material. Pressure exceeds that of a 400-pound dancer on pointe, or a woman in stiletto heels. Rice blast causes more crop loss than any other disease and is worsened by nitrogen fertilizer application. The fungus genome (7 chromosomes) is being sequenced for disease-resistance gene discovery.

## Potato Blight (Phytophthora infestans)

The most infamous fungal pathogen, responsible for the Irish famine (1845-1852). Caused $3 billion in annual losses. Phytophthora is an oomycete (water mold) with a 250 billion nucleotide genome — sixfold larger than Magnaporthe. Triggered mass starvation in Ireland: population halved from 8 million (1 million dead, 1+ million emigrated). Effects persist — Ireland's population remains below 4 million.

## Mycotoxins

Fungi produce toxic secondary metabolites that contaminate food:

- **Aflatoxins** (Aspergillus flavus, A. parasiticus): most potent natural carcinogens, contaminate peanuts and corn, cause liver cancer
- **Ergot alkaloids** (Claviceps purpurea): contaminate rye, cause ergotism (St. Anthony's Fire) — hallucinations, convulsions, gangrene. Historical epidemics may have triggered the Salem witch trials
- **Vomitoxin/deoxynivalenol** (Fusarium graminearum): contaminates wheat, causes illness in humans and livestock
- **Fumonisins** (Fusarium verticillioides): contaminate corn, linked to esophageal cancer and neural tube defects

## Mycopesticides

Fungi as biocontrol agents against insect pests:
- **Metarhizium anisopliae**: infects and kills insects, commercialized for locust and termite control
- **Beauveria bassiana**: entomopathogenic fungus used against whiteflies, thrips, and other pests
- **Entomophthora**: "insect destroyer" fungus, causes epizootics in aphid populations

These offer alternatives to chemical pesticides and are central to integrated pest management.
