# Neem (*Azadirachta indica*)

## Overview

Neem (*Azadirachta indica*, family Meliaceae) is a deep-rooted, broad-leaved tree, usually evergreen but deciduous during drought. Native to the dry forests of India through to Burma, it is potentially among the most valuable of arid land trees. Neem is renowned for its insecticidal, medicinal, and timber properties. The seeds and leaves yield azadirachtin, one of the most important natural insecticides known, while the wood is similar to Cuban mahogany and tougher than teak.

## Environmental Requirements

- **Rainfall**: Typically 450–1,150 mm; tolerates as low as 130 mm annually
- **Drought**: Can tolerate long dry seasons and extreme heat
- **Frost**: Not frost hardy — seedlings are killed by frost
- **Soil**: Grows on most soils including dry, stony, clay, and shallow soils. Not tolerant of seasonally waterlogged soils (taproot rots) or deep dry sands where water table exceeds 18 m. Roots extract nutrients from even the most leached sandy soils
- **Soil pH**: Optimum 6.2, but grows well at pH 5 and actually brings soil toward neutral through leaf litter
- **Salinity**: Not tolerant

## Azadirachtin and Pest Control

The seeds and leaves contain azadirachtin, a complex limonoid that acts as a powerful natural insecticide, antifeedant, and growth disruptor for hundreds of insect pest species while being relatively non-toxic to [[fukuoka-natural-farming-in-tropical-regions]] where termites are devastating to most timber species. The tree coppices readily, producing straight, strong poles.

## Firewood

Neem is a valuable firewood source with a calorific value almost as high as coal.

## Traditional Medicinal Uses

In India, neem has been called "the village pharmacy" for its extensive traditional uses:

- **Twigs**: Used as toothbrushes (neem chewing sticks)
- **Bark decoction**: Used for fevers, nausea, liver complaints, and jaundice
- **Bark poultice**: Applied to wounds and skin diseases
- **Bark oil**: Used as a rub for rheumatism
- **Fruit**: Steeped or crushed for urinary complaints, piles, and worms; considered a purgative and emollient
- **Seed oil**: Applied topically for skin diseases

## Cultural Significance

In India, neem is one of five sacred trees planted in temple groves, where it is planted as a companion to mango and *Ficus religiosa*. This cultural reverence has preserved genetic diversity that may prove invaluable for future breeding programs.

## Propagation

By seed only, but seed is very short-lived (2–3 weeks viability). Seedlings are killed by frost and fire and need to be kept free of weeds during establishment.

## Permaculture Applications

1. **[[botanical-pesticides-and-natural-pest-control]]
2. **Arid zone forestry**: Thrives where most trees fail
3. **Timber production**: Premium, decay-resistant, termite-proof timber
4. **Soil improvement**: Leaf litter raises pH of acidic soils toward neutral
5. **Firewood**: High calorific value suitable for fuelwood systems
6. **Medicinal tree**: Traditional remedies from bark, leaves, seeds, and oil
7. **Grain protection**: Leaves in storage repel insects without chemicals

## Design Considerations

- Not frost hardy — restrict to frost-free zones
- Seed viability is extremely short — propagate immediately
- Deep taproot allows interplanting with shallower-rooted crops without significant competition
- Buffer zone of nitrogen-fixing trees recommended between neem and sensitive crops (as with all deep-rooted, water-extracting species)
