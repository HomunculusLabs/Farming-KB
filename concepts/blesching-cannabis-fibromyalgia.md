# Cannabis and Fibromyalgia

Fibromyalgia is an illness characterized by chronic pain combined with some form
of psychiatric diagnosis. It still lacks an observable underlying pathology in
conventional medicine. The disease picture of fibromyalgia usually includes
widespread chronic pains in muscles and connective tissue, joint stiffness,
general weakness, exhaustion, depression, anxiety, and insomnia. Nearly 2
percent of the general population in the United States suffers from
fibromyalgia, the majority of them being middle-aged females.

## Diagnosis and Conventional Treatment

No specific test exists to determine an exact diagnosis of fibromyalgia.
Symptoms are often similar to those of rheumatism, osteoporosis, or arthritis.
Physicians diagnose by process of elimination. Diagnostic criteria established
by the American College of Rheumatology include: a history of diffused and
chronic pain for more than three months, present in all four quadrants of the
body, and the presence of pain in at least 11 of 18 specific trigger points.

Orthodox pharmaceutical treatment includes muscle relaxants, pain medication
(opioids), antidepressants, anti-seizure medication, dopamine agonists, and
cannabinoids.

## Cannabis and Fibromyalgia: The Evidence

The Cannabis Health Index (CHI) assigns fibromyalgia a value of 14 based on 3
reviewed studies, indicating moderate therapeutic potential.

### Key Clinical Studies

In 2007, researchers in Winnipeg, Canada, enrolled 40 fibromyalgia patients to
measure the effects of Nabilone (a synthetic cannabinoid similar to THC) on
pain and quality of life. This randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled
trial showed that "Nabilone appears to be a beneficial, well-tolerated
treatment option for fibromyalgia patients, with significant benefits in pain
relief and functional improvement."

A 2008 crossover clinical trial from Montreal, Canada, examined the effects of
Nabilone on insomnia in 29 fibromyalgia patients. Patients received either
Nabilone (0.5 to 1.0 mg before bedtime) or the tricyclic antidepressant
amitriptyline (10 to 20 mg before bedtime). While both medications
significantly improved sleep, Nabilone was superior.

A 2009 meta-analysis from the University of Massachusetts Medical School found
that "all classes of cannabinoids, including endogenous cannabinoids such as
anandamide, related compounds such as the elmiric acids (EMAs), and non-
cannabinoid components of cannabis, show anti-inflammatory action." The
analysis demonstrated effectiveness in reducing pain from post-surgery
conditions, rheumatism, rheumatoid arthritis, chronic neuropathic pain, and
fibromyalgia.

### Strain-Specific Considerations

Two of the reviewed studies used primarily the synthetic cannabinoid Nabilone
(similar to THC), which binds relatively equally to CB1 and CB2 receptors.
Both sativa and indica strains contain cannabinoids that activate CB1 and CB2.
However, sativas or sativa-heavy strains tend to produce higher THC:CBD ratios
than indica strains.

## Mind-Body Medicine and Fibromyalgia

Functional brain-imaging studies, neurotransmitter studies, and brain anatomy
studies reveal that "fibromyalgia patients have alterations in central nervous
system (CNS) anatomy, physiology, and chemistry that potentially contribute to
the symptoms experienced by these patients."

The frequent comorbidity of fibromyalgia with stress-related disorders such as
chronic fatigue, [[blesching-cannabis-ptsd-anxiety]], irritable bowel syndrome, and depression, as well as the similarity
of many CNS abnormalities, suggests at least a partial common substrate for
these disorders.

Contributing factors may include:
- Early life stress or prolonged severe stress
- Low levels of positive emotions (affect), especially during stressful times
- Increased risk of victimization, particularly adult physical abuse
- Sexual, physical, and emotional trauma

Research revealed that fibromyalgia patients experienced improved
psychological well-being and a reduction in pain and fatigue through emotional
expression of personal traumatic experiences, writing about them for 20
minutes, three times per week.

## Complementary Approaches: Tai Chi

A single-blind, randomized trial of classic Yang-style tai chi showed that of
66 enrolled patients, those in the tai chi group demonstrated measurable
clinical improvement and a general increase in quality of life. Benefits were
still present after six months, with no adverse effects observed.
