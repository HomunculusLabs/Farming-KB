# Mycelium Running — Techniques & Spawn Preparation

Practical cultivation methods from *Mycelium Running* by [[paul-stamets]]. Covers inoculation, spawn preparation, and deployment for both food production and mycorestoration.

## Inoculation Methods

### From Spores
- Spores are mushroom "seeds" — each produces one of several possible genetic combinations
- Spore prints: place cap gills-down on paper, cover overnight
- Spore slurries: blend spore prints with water, pour over substrate
- Less predictable than cloning — each spore mating expresses a different phenotype
- Best for: low-tech inoculation, genetic diversity

### From Tissue (Cloning)
- Take a piece of internal flesh from a fresh mushroom
- Place on sterilized agar media in a petri dish
- Captures the exact genetic composition of the parent mushroom
- Requires sterile technique and a clean workspace
- Best for: replicating exceptional specimens, commercial production

### From Stem Butts
- Bury the base portion of a freshly harvested mushroom in substrate
- Mycelium regrows from the stem tissue into surrounding material
- Simplest method — no lab required
- Best for: Stropharia, Pleurotus, garden inoculation

### From Purchased Spawn
- **Sawdust spawn** — sterilized sawdust inoculated with mycelium; versatile
- **Grain spawn** — sterilized grain (rye, millet) colonized with mycelium; fast-growing
- **Plug spawn** — wooden dowels infused with mycelium; for logs/stumps
- **Rope spawn** — hemp rope colonized with mycelium; for wrapping stumps

## Outdoor Cultivation Methods

### Straw Cultivation
1. Pasteurize straw by soaking in hot water (160-170°F) for 1-2 hours
2. Drain and cool to below 90°F
3. Mix with spawn (10% by weight)
4. Pack into bags, beds, or tubes
5. Keep at 70-75°F for colonization (2-4 weeks)
6. Introduce fresh air, light, and humidity drop to trigger fruiting

### Log Cultivation
1. Cut fresh hardwood logs (oak, maple, alder, cottonwood) 3-6" diameter, 3-4' long
2. Drill holes 1" deep, spaced 4-6" apart in diamond pattern
3. Insert plug spawn, seal with wax
4. Stack in shade, keep moist
5. Colonization: 6-18 months depending on species and conditions
6. Shock logs (soak in cold water 24 hours) to initiate fruiting

### Wood Chip Beds (Garden Giants, Wine Caps)
1. Prepare site: partial shade, well-drained
2. Lay down 4-6 inches of fresh hardwood chips
3. Spread sawdust spawn at ~1/4 lb per square foot
4. Cover with 2-4 more inches of chips
5. Mulch with straw to retain moisture
6. Keep moist; colonization in 2-6 months
7. Mushrooms fruit after soaking rains when temperatures are 60-80°F

### Stump Inoculation (for Mycoforestry)
1. Drill holes into fresh-cut stumps
2. Insert plug spawn or fill with sawdust spawn
3. Seal with wax to prevent desiccation
4. Mycelium colonizes the stump over 1-2 years
5. Mushrooms fruit from the stump for several years
6. Species that combat Armillaria blight: Sparassis crispa, Hypholoma capnoides, Trametes versicolor

## Mycofilter Installation

Layered construction for filtering water (see [[mycoremediation-techniques]]):
1. Bottom: 3-4 inches sawdust or wood chips
2. First spawn layer: inoculated sawdust, 1/4 lb per sq ft
3. Middle: 4 inches corncobs
4. Second spawn layer
5. Cover: waste cardboard
6. Top: 4-6 inches straw for shade and moisture
7. Water until established; then natural rainfall should suffice

## Sterile Technique Essentials

For tissue cloning and grain spawn production:
- Work in a still-air box or laminar flow hood
- Flame-sterilize tools between transfers
- Use 70% isopropyl alcohol for surface sterilization
- Maintain cleanliness — one contaminated jar can ruin a batch
- Monitor for contamination: green molds (Trichoderma), bacterial slime, off-colors
