Source: The Cannabis Grow Bible, Greg Green (Ch. 5, 6)

Container selection directly impacts root health, plant size, watering frequency, and ultimately yield. Greg Green covers pot sizing as part of the [[comparison-cannabis-indoor-growing-basics-vs-cannabis-outdoor-growing]] setup.

## Container Types

### Standard Pots
[[query-fabric-pots-vs-plastic-pots-vs-air-pots-for-cannabis]] are the most common choice. They are inexpensive, lightweight, and available in many sizes. The main drawback is poor drainage compared to fabric pots.

### Fabric Pots (Smart Pots)
Fabric pots allow air pruning of roots, preventing the root circling that occurs in solid containers. This promotes a healthier root mass and better oxygen exchange. They dry out faster than solid pots, requiring more frequent watering.

### Air Pots
Similar to fabric pots but with rigid plastic walls perforated with many small holes. These provide excellent drainage and air pruning while maintaining their shape.

## Sizing Guide

### By Grow Stage
| Stage | Container Size | Notes |
|-------|---------------|-------|
| Seedling | Small propagation cube or solo cup | Enough for first 1–3 weeks |
| Early veg | 1–2 gallon (4–8 liter) | Transplant when roots show at drainage holes |
| Late veg / early flower | 3–5 gallon (11–19 liter) | Standard for most indoor grows |
| Full-term plant | 5–10 gallon (19–38 liter) | For large plants or long veg periods |

### By Growing Method
- **SOG (Sea of Green)**: Smaller containers (1–2 gallon) since plants flower young and small
- **ScrOG ([[cannabis-screen-of-green-scrog-guide]])**: 3–5 gallon pots to support extended [[cannabis-vegetative-growth-stage]]
- **Single large plant**: 5–10+ gallons for maximum root mass and plant size
- See [[cannabis-transplanting-guide]] for transplanting solutions

## Transplanting Strategy

Greg Green recommends transplanting seedlings through progressively larger containers rather than placing them directly into a large pot. This encourages the root system to develop more densely:

1. Start seedlings in small containers or propagation cubes
2. Transplant to a 1-gallon pot once established
3. Move to final container (3–5+ gallons) once roots fill the intermediate pot
4. Minimize [[query-how-to-prevent-transplant-shock-when-moving-cannabis-plants]] by watering well before and after

Transplant during the [[comparison-cannabis-vegetative-stage-vs-flowering-stage]] only — avoid transplanting during flowering, which causes significant stress.

## Drainage

Proper drainage is critical:
- All containers must have drainage holes at the bottom
- Use saucers or trays to catch runoff
- Do not allow plants to sit in standing water, which promotes root rot
- Add a layer of perlite or gravel at the bottom of solid pots for additional drainage
- Elevate pots slightly above their saucers using small blocks or pot feet

## Soil Volume and Watering

Larger pots retain moisture longer, which means:
- Less frequent watering (every 2–3 days vs. daily for small pots)
- More stable root zone temperature
- Greater buffer against pH and nutrient fluctuations
- More forgiving of occasional over- or underwatering

Smaller pots:
- Dry faster, requiring closer attention
- Allow more control over feeding (less soil to flush)
- Better suited to SOG and fast-turnover grows
- Space-efficient for many plants in a small area

## Potting Up vs. Starting Large

**Potting up** (gradual transplanting):
- Pros: Better root development, more efficient use of soil and nutrients
- Cons: More labor, risk of transplant shock each time

**Starting in final large container**:
- Pros: No transplant shock, less labor
- Cons: Seedling may struggle in large volume of soil, watering can be tricky in early stages (easy to overwater the small root zone)

A compromise is to start in a 1-gallon pot and move directly to the final container, skipping intermediate steps.
