## Fungal Biomass Stoichiometry

Fungal biomass has characteristic C:N:P ratios that differ from those of bacteria and
plants. The C:N ratio of fungal biomass is typically around 6:1 to 15:1, with the model
of Swift et al. (1979) suggesting thresholds of approximately 15:1 for phosphorus and 6:1
for nitrogen in decomposer fungi. These ratios are significantly narrower than the C:N
ratios of most plant litter, which commonly range from 20:1 to over 300:1.

The relatively narrow C:N ratio of fungal biomass means that decomposition of high C:N
ratio substrates requires net nitrogen immobilization into fungal tissue until the
substrate C:N ratio approaches that of the decomposer. Only then does net mineralization
of nitrogen occur. This immobilization phase can last weeks to months depending on
initial substrate quality and environmental conditions.

## Carbon:Nitrogen Ratios and Decomposition

The carbon:nitrogen ratio is one of the most widely used predictors of decomposition rate
and nutrient dynamics. Where C:N or lignin:N ratios are high, there are reduced rates of
decomposition compared to resources containing lower ratios (Melillo et al., 1982).
During decomposition of Pteridium aquilinum litter, the C:N ratio decreased from
approximately 200:1 to 30:1 as succession proceeded and recalcitrant materials were
progressively decomposed (Frankland, 1992).

The lignin:N ratio is an even stronger predictor of decomposition rate than C:N alone,
with regression coefficients of 0.68 for mass loss and 0.42 for nitrogen mineralization
(Vanlauwe et al., 1997). The combined (lignin + polyphenol):N ratio provides the
strongest prediction, with regression coefficients of 0.77 for mass loss and 0.68 for
nitrogen mineralization.

## Litter Quality and Mycorrhizal Associations

The C:N ratio of plant litter is strongly associated with the dominant mycorrhizal type.
Plants with ericoid mycorrhizae produce litter with the highest C:N ratios and longest
decomposition times. Ectomycorrhizal plants produce litter with intermediate C:N ratios
and decomposition rates. Arbuscular mycorrhizal plants produce litter with the lowest C:N
ratios and fastest decomposition rates. This association between mycorrhizal type, litter
quality, and decomposition rate creates a major feedback between plant communities and
soil carbon storage.

## Resource Quality and Enzyme Investment

Fungi modulate their enzyme production in response to the stoichiometric mismatch between
their biomass requirements and the elemental composition of available substrates. Where
nutrient elements are less available relative to carbon, fungi expend greater amounts of
energy to produce nutrient-acquiring enzymes such as phosphatases and proteases. This
relationship between substrate stoichiometry and enzyme investment is a key mechanism
linking litter quality to [[fungal-carbon-storage-mycorrhizal-pathways]]. This
stoichiometrically-driven pathway is particularly important in boreal and heathland
ecosystems where mycorrhizal-mediated nutrient removal significantly slows decomposition.
