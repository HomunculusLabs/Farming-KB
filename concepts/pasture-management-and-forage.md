## Developing a Tree-Based Forage System

### Timeline

1. **Year 1**: Plant about 10% of the land in [[savory-recovery-and-grazing-periods]]
3. **Year 5–10**: Gradually extend grazing periods as trees mature and canopy provides adequate forage
4. **Year 10+**: Full integration of grazing animals with tree forage system

### Forage Trees

- **Fig**: Excellent forage and fruit
- **Poplar**: Fast-growing, palatable to livestock
- **Willow**: Good browse, grows well near water
- **Chestnut**: Nut production for winter feed
- **Oak**: Acorns for fattening livestock
- **Pine**: Winter browse when other forage is scarce
- **Bamboo**: Can serve as either forage or timber crop

### Tree Forage Benefits

- Saves tremendous energy compared to cutting and hauling hay
- Cattle are healthier and happier
- Higher yields
- Drought resilience (deep-rooted trees access water unavailable to grasses)
- Soil building [[query-role-of-fungi-in-soil-aggregation-and-erosion-prevention]]
- Windbreak and shelter for livestock

## Large Pasture Development

For 50 acres or more:

1. **Fence off** a small area to develop first (electric fence works well)
2. **Rehabilitate soil**: Chisel plow and apply lime as needed
3. **Plant center trees**: Small group of windbreak and forage trees in the center; mulch and fertilize well. Protect seedlings with shelters (tires with mulch work well)
4. **Introduce waterfowl**: Ducks and geese in the area — monitor for damage
5. **Expand**: When the area establishes itself, move fences to develop the next section
6. **Cull trees**: Cut out poorly performing trees, keeping only the strongest and highest-yielding specimens

## Fencing for Grazing Areas

### Double-Fence Perimeter

- **Outer fence**: Standard perimeter fencing
- **Inner fence**: Built inside the outer fence
- **Space between**: Plant trees, hedges, and spiny shrubs
- **Benefits**: Protection, windbreak, fruit, nuts, wood, and forage for sheep, bees, and birds
