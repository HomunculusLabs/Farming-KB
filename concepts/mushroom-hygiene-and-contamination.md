### Mold Contamination

Molds are fungi that compete with mushroom mycelium for nutrients and space:

- **Trichoderma** (green mold): The most notorious mold contaminant in
  mushroom cultivation. Appears as rapidly expanding patches of green
  sporulation. Trichoderma harzianum is the species most commonly encountered.
  It produces powerful antifungal compounds that kill mushroom mycelium and can
  spread aggressively through cultivation areas. Trichoderma contamination
  often indicates insufficient sterilization, contaminated inoculant, or poor
  hygiene practices.

- **Aspergillus** and **Penicillium**: Produce distinctive colored mold
  colonies — Aspergillus niger appears black, Aspergillus flavus is yellow-
  green, and Penicillium species produce blue-green colonies with a powdery
  texture. Some Aspergillus species produce aflatoxins, making contaminated
  materials a health hazard.

- **Cobweb mold** (Hypomyces / Dactylium): Appears as a fine, grayish-white
  webbing over the substrate surface or fruiting bodies. It is often mistaken
  for mushroom mycelium but grows faster and more aerial than mushroom mycelium.
  Cobweb mold thrives in stagnant, high-humidity conditions with poor fresh air
  exchange.

- **Neurospora** (pink mold): Produces bright orange-pink growth that spreads
  rapidly. It is particularly problematic in warm conditions and can release
  vast quantities of spores that contaminate entire growing areas.
