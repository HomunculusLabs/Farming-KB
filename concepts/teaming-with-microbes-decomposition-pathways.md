Decomposition is the process by which [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]] is broken down into
simpler compounds, ultimately forming [[teaming-with-microbes-soil-food-web-trophic-structure-and-succession]].

## The Hierarchy of Decomposability

Plant compounds vary enormously in how difficult they are to decompose.
The general order from easiest to most resistant is:

1. **Sugars and simple carbohydrates**: Digested almost instantly by
   bacteria and fungi
2. **Proteins and amino acids**: Rapidly decomposed by many organisms
3. **Hemicellulose**: Moderately resistant polysaccharide; decomposed
   during thermophilic composting
4. **[[fungal-decomposition-ecology]]**: Complex,
   three-dimensional polymer that binds and protects cellulose; the
   most resistant plant component
6. **Cutin, waxes, and suberin**: Protective surface coatings on
   leaves, stems, and roots; extremely resistant to decomposition
7. **Tannins and phenols**: Secondary defense compounds that inhibit
   microbial activity; slow decomposition significantly

## Cellulose Decomposition

Cellulose is the most abundant organic polymer on Earth, forming the
structural framework of plant cell walls. It consists of long, straight
chains of glucose molecules linked by beta-1,4-glycosidic bonds.

### Bacterial Role

Bacteria are particularly adept at depolymerization -- breaking the long
cellulose chains into shorter glucose chains. Many common soil bacteria
including Bacillus, Pseudomonas, and Cellulomonas species produce
cellulase enzymes. In compost, bacterial depolymerization begins even
in the mesophilic stage (68-104F).

### Fungal Role

Fungi excel at cellulose decomposition because of their [[bloomfields-orchard-hyphal-growth-and-invasive-biology]]
strategy. Using apical growth (extension at the [[gadd-hyphal-tip-growth-and-branching-mechanisms]]), fungi can
physically penetrate plant cell walls and access cellulose that bacteria
cannot reach. [[comparison-brown-rot-vs-white-rot-bioremediation]] fungi (basidiomycetes) are particularly
important cellulose decomposers in wood and forest litter.

## Lignin Decomposition

Lignin is a complex, non-carbohydrate polymer that provides structural
rigidity to plant cell walls. The bonds holding lignin's chains of
alcohols together are among the strongest in biological materials.

### White Rot Fungi

White rot basidiomycetes are the primary lignin decomposers. They
produce phenol oxidase, a powerful enzyme that dissolves lignin. White
rot fungi are unique in their ability to completely mineralize lignin to
carbon dioxide and water.

### Brown Rot Fungi

Brown rot fungi primarily attack cellulose but also modify lignin,
leaving behind a brown, crumbly residue. They use a non-enzymatic
Fenton chemistry ([[cervantes-hydrogen-peroxide-sterilization]] plus iron) to depolymerize
cellulose.
