# Casing Moisture Management

## Overview

Moisture within the casing layer has a direct effect on the diameter and
degree of branching in growing mycelium. These characteristics serve as
visual indicators of moisture content and can guide the cultivator in proper
watering practices. Proper casing moisture management is critical for
successful primordia formation and sustained flush production.

## Water Retention Properties

The casing must have the capacity to both absorb and release substantial
quantities of water. Not only does the casing sustain vegetative growth, but
it must also supply sufficient moisture for successive generations of
fruitbodies. The following materials vary in their water retention capacity:

| Material           | Absorption (ml water/g) | Retention |
|--------------------|------------------------|-----------|
| Vermiculite        | 5.0                    | 84%       |
| Peat               | 2.5                    | 79%       |
| Potting Soil       | 0.7                    | 76%       |
| Loam               | 0.3                    | 25%       |
| Chalk              | 0.6                    | 37%       |
| Limestone Grit     | 0.2                    | 15%       |
| Sand               | 0.2                    | 18%       |

Values vary according to source and quality of material. Vermiculite is the
most effective water-retaining material available for casing amendments.

## Determining Moisture Saturation

An important reference point is the moisture saturation level. To determine
this, completely saturate a casing sample and allow it to drain. Cover and
wait 30 minutes. Weigh out 100 grams and dry in an oven at 200 degrees F for
two to three hours until completely dry. The difference in weight reveals the
percent moisture at saturation. Optimum moisture content is normally 2-4%
below saturation. Peat-based casings are typically balanced to 70-75%
moisture content.

## Three Moisture Conditions

### Optimum Casing Moisture

Under ideal moisture conditions, mushroom mycelium thrives in a moist humid
casing, sending out minute branching networks that expand and grow, absorbing
water, CO2 and oxygen from the near-saturated environment. This mycelial
growth is characterized by:

- Many thick, white rhizomorphic strands
- Branching into mycelia of smaller diameters
- Fine capillary networks
- Lush, dense overall appearance
- Casing holds firmly together when examined but separates with little effort
- The casing remains soft and pliable

### Overly Dry Casing

In a dry casing, the mycelium shows distinctly different characteristics:

- Lack of rhizomorphs and abundance of fine capillary-type mycelia
- Fine growth totally permeates the casing layer
- Casing becomes hard, compact, and unreceptive to water
- Puddles commonly form on a dry casing that has just been watered
- Rarely permits primordia formation due to arid microclimate
- Susceptible to "overlay" (dense mycelial mat covering the surface)
- Mushrooms, if they occur, frequently form only along tray edges

Dry casing directly results from insufficient watering, high evaporation
rates, or low room humidity. Overlay is a secondary consequence where the
mycelium forms a dense mat that shows little inclination to form pinheads.

### Overly Wet Casing

In a saturated casing, mycelial growth is compromised in the opposite
direction:

- Mycelium grows coarse and stringy with very little branching
- Few capillaries form
- Mycelial growth is slow and sparse
- Casing remains largely uncolonized
- Saturated casing leaches onto substrate surface, causing waterlogging
- Further growth is inhibited and contamination is promoted
- Subsequent drying may reactivate mycelium, but yield reduction is expected

## Casing Preparation for Optimum Moisture

To prepare a casing of proper moisture content, Stamets recommends this
method: remove 10-20% of the volume of the dry mix, then saturate the
remaining 80-90%. Add the reserved dry material back, which brings the
moisture content to near optimum. Some growers prefer letting the casing sit
for 24 hours to fully absorb water, then thoroughly mix again before
application.

Vermiculite can be added (one-half to one part per casing mixture) to
improve water-retaining capacity. When used, it must be presoaked to
saturation before mixing with other ingredients.

## Watering During Casing Colonization

After casing application, environmental conditions should match spawn run
parameters: 90-100% humidity, minimal fresh air. Within three days the
mycelium should be growing into the casing. Once mycelial growth is
established, the casing is gradually watered up to optimum moisture holding
capacity through a series of light waterings with a misting nozzle over a two
to four day period.

Critical warning: waterings must not damage the surface structure. Heavy
direct watering can "pan" the casing surface, closing all pore spaces and
effectively sealing it. The growing mycelium becomes trapped within the
casing layer and may not break through at all.

## Evaporation and Humidity Balance

Water in the casing moves by capillary action to the surface where it is
drawn into the air by evaporation. This constant movement slowly depletes
the moisture needed to protect pinhead development. Therefore, room humidity
must be held at 95% during the casing run and primordia formation. Lower
humidities must be accompanied by light but regular waterings. The higher
the humidity, the less water will be lost to evaporation.
