# Foliar Feeding Cannabis

Foliar feeding is the practice of misting fertilizer
solution directly onto plant foliage, where nutrients
are absorbed through the leaves. Jorge Cervantes
describes foliar feeding as a valuable technique for
delivering a fast fix for nutrient deficiencies and
supplementing regular root feeding programs.

See also: [[cervantes-macronutrient-deficiency-toxicity]]

## How Foliar Feeding Works

Plants can absorb nutrients through their leaves. The
stomata on leaf surfaces, along with the waxy cuticle
layer, allow certain nutrient solutions to penetrate
when applied as a fine mist. Foliar feeding delivers
nutrients directly to where they are needed, bypassing
the root uptake pathway. This makes it an effective
rapid-response treatment for nutrient deficiencies.

Foliar feeding should turn a struggling plant around
in less than a week. A second spray may be necessary
at the end of the week to ensure the correction holds.

## When to Use Foliar Feeding

Foliar feeding is most useful in these situations:

- **Rapid correction of nutrient deficiencies** when
  symptoms appear suddenly and need immediate
  attention
- **Providing immobile nutrients** such as calcium,
  boron, iron, manganese, molybdenum, and zinc that
  do not translocate well within the plant
- **Supplemental feeding during flowering** when
  nutrient uptake through roots slows, particularly
  for boron, calcium, iron, and potassium
- **Reversing foliar nitrogen** by misting mother
  plants with plain water to leach nitrogen from
  leaves and build carbohydrate levels before taking
  cuttings
- **Emergency situations** where root uptake is
  compromised by root damage, disease, or
  overwatering

## Nutrient Mobility Considerations

Understanding nutrient mobility is critical for
effective foliar feeding:

- **Mobile nutrients** (nitrogen, phosphorus,
  potassium, magnesium, zinc) move freely within the
  plant. Apply them sparingly, as they translocate
  effectively.
- **Immobile nutrients** (sulfur, boron, copper,
  iron, manganese, molybdenum) move slowly and once
  deposited, stay in place. These often require two
  or three applications for adequate coverage.

Calcium and boron are poor candidates for foliar
feeding because they translocate poorly. However,
urea nitrogen applied as a spray in high humidity
penetrates almost instantly into leaves. Be careful
with urea-based fertilizers and keep them diluted.
Urea also carries other nutrients into the plant
when used as a carrier.
