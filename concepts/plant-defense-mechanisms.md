# Plant Defense Mechanisms

Plants are not passive victims of pests and disease. They have evolved sophisticated defense systems that work through chemistry, signaling, and partnerships with [[soil-food-web-nutrient-cycling]]. Understanding these mechanisms is key to [[insect-allies]]-free gardening.

## Chemical Defenses

### Constitutive Defenses (Always Present)
- **Alkaloids** — nicotine, caffeine, solanine (bitter, toxic compounds)
- **Terpenoids** — essential oils that repel insects (mint, rosemary, lavender)
- **Phenolics** — tannins that make plant tissue indigestible
- **Cyanogenic glycosides** — release cyanide when tissue is damaged (cassava, apple seeds)
- **Saponins** — disrupt insect cell membranes

### Induced Defenses (Activated by Attack)
- **Jasmonic acid pathway** — activated by herbivore damage; produces protease inhibitors that make leaves indigestible to insects
- **Salicylic acid pathway** — activated by pathogen attack; triggers systemic acquired resistance (SAR)
- **Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs)** — released when damaged; warn neighboring plants AND attract predatory insects

### The VOC Warning System
When a plant is attacked by caterpillars, it releases VOCs that:

1. **Warn nearby plants** to pre-activate their own defenses
2. **Attract parasitic wasps** that lay eggs in the caterpillars
3. **Signal mycorrhizal networks** to share resources with stressed plants

This is why companion planting partially works — nearby plants can "eavesdrop" on each other"s alarm signals.

## Biological Defenses

### Mycorrhizal Protection
Mycorrhizal fungi provide multiple layers of defense (see [[mycorrhizal-fungi]]):

1. **Physical barrier** — fungal hyphae form a dense network around roots that blocks pathogen access
2. **Chemical warfare** — fungi produce antibiotics and antifungal compounds
3. **Early warning** — the fungal network detects pathogen presence before the plant does
4. **Nutrient bolstering** — well-nourished plants have stronger immune responses

### Endophytic Fungi and Bacteria
Plants host beneficial microbes inside their tissues (endophytes):

- **Endophytic fungi** in grasses produce alkaloids that deter grazing insects
- **Endophytic bacteria** (Pseudomonas, Bacillus) produce antibiotics against pathogens
- These are transmitted through seeds and the environment
- Sterile potting mix lacks these protective endophytes

### Rhizosphere Defense Zone
The [[soil-food-web-nutrient-cycling]] around plant roots creates a biological shield:

- Beneficial bacteria outcompete pathogens for root exudates
- Predatory nematodes and protozoa consume pathogenic bacteria and fungi
- Antibiotic-producing actinomycetes suppress soil-borne diseases
- See [[compost-tea-aact]] for how to inoculate this zone

## Physical Defenses

- **Thorns and spines** — mechanical deterrents
- **Trichomes (leaf hairs)** — impede insect movement and trap pests
- **Thick cuticles** — resist penetration by fungi
- **Laticifers** — produce latex that gums up insect mouthparts
- **Silica accumulation** — in grasses, makes tissue abrasive and difficult to chew

## Nutritional Defenses

### Brix as a Defense Metric
Steve Solomon and others argue that high-brix (high sugar/mineral) plants resist pests because:

1. **Insects can't digest high-sugar sap** — many insects lack the enzymes to process concentrated sugars
2. **Thick cell walls** from adequate calcium make penetration difficult
3. **Complete nutrition** produces complete defensive chemistry
4. Insects are the "quality control department" — they attack the weakest plants

See [[nutrient-dense-gardening]] for how to maximize brix.

### Silicon (Silica)
Often overlooked as a plant nutrient, silicon:

- Deposits in cell walls, creating a physical barrier against fungi and insects
- Enhances resistance to powdery mildew
- Strengthens stems against lodging
- Available through horsetail tea, diatomaceous earth, or soluble silicon products

## How to Support Plant Defenses

1. **Complete mineral nutrition** — plants need all 13 nutrients to synthesize defensive compounds
2. **Healthy soil biology** — [[soil-food-web-nutrient-cycling]] provides biological protection
3. **Mycorrhizal inoculation** — especially in disturbed or tilled soils
4. **Avoid synthetic pesticides** — they kill beneficial insects and microbes that provide defense
5. **Appropriate stress** — mild stress (hardening off, water stress) can prime defenses
6. **Biodiversity** — polycultures support the predator-prey cycles that keep pests in check
