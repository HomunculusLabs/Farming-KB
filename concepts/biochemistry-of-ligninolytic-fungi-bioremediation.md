The biochemistry of ligninolytic fungi is central to [[fungal-bioremediation]] of aromatic pollutants. White-rot basidiomycetes possess extracellular oxidative enzyme systems that degrade lignin and, by extension, a wide range of recalcitrant aromatic pollutants including [[fungal-pah-degradation-pathways]], chlorophenols, and other xenobiotics.

## Relevance to Bioremediation

Three groups of aromatic compounds constitute substantial pollutants:
1. **Polyaromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs)**: Carcinogenic compounds formed during organic combustion, concentrated at industrial sites, petroleum facilities, and wood-preserving operations (creosote contains 85% PAHs by weight)
2. **BTEX compounds**: Benzene, toluene, ethyl benzene, xylene (bacterial remediation often more efficient)
3. **Synthetic substituted aromatics**: Typified by chlorophenols

PAHs with two or more fused benzene rings are insoluble and stable. Angular ring structures (phenanthrene, benzo[a]pyrene, pyrene) are more stable than linear arrangements (anthracene, benz[a]anthracene). Two- and three-ringed structures are more biodegradable than four-, five-, and six-membered rings.

## The Bacterial Limitation

Bacteria use intracellular dioxygenases and cytochrome P450 monooxygenases for PAH activation. These enzymes cannot process PAH structures larger than two or three rings because:
- Larger PAHs are extremely insoluble in water
- They cannot be taken up through the microbial cell wall
- The intracellular enzymes cannot access them

White-rot basidiomycetes overcome this limitation through **extracellular** oxidative enzyme systems that initiate degradation of higher-molecular-weight polymeric xenobiotics.

## Parallels Between Lignin and PAH Degradation

Both lignin and PAHs require initial extracellular depolymerization or aromatic ring-opening before constituent rings can be metabolized. Key similarities:
- Both are hydrophobic and highly insoluble
- Both pose challenges for water-soluble, stereospecific enzymes
- White rot fungi tackle both using small diffusible oxidizing agents generated by extracellular enzymes

## Laccase: The Blue Multicopper Oxidase

Laccase (benzenediol:oxygen oxidoreductase, EC 1.10.3.2) is the most intensively studied [[fungal-laccase-mediator-systems]]. It belongs to the blue multicopper oxidase family, containing four or more copper atoms that reduce dioxygen completely to water.

### Structure and Properties
- Secreted glycoproteins in ligninolytic fungi
- Monomeric (unlike dimeric ascorbate oxidase)
- Highly specific binding pocket for oxygen
- Shallow, relatively non-stereospecific reducing substrate binding pocket
- Substrate oxidation governed primarily by redox potential differences between substrate and type I copper

### Redox Potential Range
Laccases vary markedly in substrate oxidation ability:
- Standard redox potential up to 0.8 V/SCE (1.042 V/NHE)
- Some laccases oxidize non-phenolic compounds with higher redox potentials
- Laccase secretion with MnP is a common pattern among ligninolytic fungi

### Reactions Catalyzed
Laccase catalyzes single-electron oxidation of phenolic compounds to (cation) radicals, similar to MnP and LiP. With aromatic lignin-like phenolics, oxidation frequently results in carbon-carbon and carbon-oxygen coupling (polymerization) or depolymerization depending on conditions.

Historically, the role of laccase in lignin degradation was controversial because:
1. Not all ligninolytic fungi produce laccase
2. Not all laccase-minus mutants showed reduced ligninolytic activity
3. Purified laccases generally polymerized rather than depolymerized lignin
4. Laccases could not oxidize non-phenolic lignin model compounds

However, the discovery of laccase-mediator systems and the recognition of variable redox potentials among different laccase isozymes has revised this view.

## Key Fungal Species

The most studied ligninolytic fungi for bioremediation include:

- **[[singh-cytochrome-p450-fungal-bioremediation]]**: Produces primarily LiP and MnP; model organism for lignin degradation research
- **[[staycare-trametes-versicolor-bioremediation-applications]]**: Produces all three major ligninolytic enzymes (LiP, MnP, laccase); widely used in bioremediation programs
- **[[cotter-mycoremediation-overview]]**: Edible oyster mushroom with significant ligninolytic capability
- **Phlebia radiata**: Produces laccase and MnP
- **Bjerkandera adusta**: Notable for versatile enzyme production

## Hydrogen Peroxide Generation

Both LiP and MnP require H2O2, which must be generated by the fungus. Fungal H2O2-producing enzymes include:
- Glucose oxidase
- Glyoxal oxidase
- Aryl alcohol oxidase
- MnP itself can generate H2O2 through oxidation of organic acids
