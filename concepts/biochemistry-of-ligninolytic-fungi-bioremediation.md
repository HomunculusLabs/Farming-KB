## Relevance to Bioremediation

Three groups of aromatic compounds constitute substantial pollutants:
1. **Polyaromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs)**: Carcinogenic compounds formed during organic combustion, concentrated at industrial sites, petroleum facilities, and wood-preserving operations (creosote contains 85% PAHs by weight)
2. **BTEX compounds**: Benzene, toluene, ethyl benzene, xylene (bacterial remediation often more efficient)
3. **Synthetic substituted aromatics**: Typified by chlorophenols

PAHs with two or more fused benzene rings are insoluble and stable. Angular ring structures (phenanthrene, benzo[a]pyrene, pyrene) are more stable than linear arrangements (anthracene, benz[a]anthracene). Two- and three-ringed structures are more biodegradable than four-, five-, and six-membered rings.

## The Bacterial Limitation

Bacteria use intracellular dioxygenases and cytochrome P450 monooxygenases for PAH activation. These enzymes cannot process PAH structures larger than two or three rings because:
- Larger PAHs are extremely insoluble in water
- They cannot be taken up through the microbial [[comparison-brown-rot-vs-white-rot-bioremediation]] fungi tackle both using small diffusible oxidizing agents generated by extracellular enzymes
