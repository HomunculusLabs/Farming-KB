The biochemistry of ligninolytic fungi is central to [[fungal-pah-degradation-pathways]], chlorophenols, and other xenobiotics.

## Relevance to Bioremediation

Three groups of aromatic compounds constitute substantial pollutants:
1. **Polyaromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs)**: Carcinogenic compounds formed during organic combustion, concentrated at industrial sites, petroleum facilities, and wood-preserving operations (creosote contains 85% PAHs by weight)
2. **BTEX compounds**: Benzene, toluene, ethyl benzene, xylene (bacterial remediation often more efficient)
3. **Synthetic substituted aromatics**: Typified by chlorophenols

PAHs with two or more fused benzene rings are insoluble and stable. Angular ring structures (phenanthrene, benzo[a]pyrene, pyrene) are more stable than linear arrangements (anthracene, benz[a]anthracene). Two- and three-ringed structures are more biodegradable than four-, five-, and six-membered rings.

## The Bacterial Limitation

Bacteria use intracellular dioxygenases and cytochrome P450 monooxygenases for PAH activation. These enzymes cannot process PAH structures larger than two or three rings because:
- Larger PAHs are extremely insoluble in water
- They cannot be taken up through the microbial [[staycare-degradation-of-plant-cell-wall-polymers-by-fungi]]
- The intracellular enzymes cannot access them

White-rot basidiomycetes overcome this limitation through **extracellular** oxidative enzyme systems that initiate degradation of higher-molecular-weight polymeric xenobiotics.

## Parallels Between Lignin and PAH Degradation

Both lignin and PAHs require initial extracellular depolymerization or aromatic ring-opening before constituent rings can be metabolized. Key similarities:
- Both are hydrophobic and highly insoluble
- Both pose challenges for water-soluble, stereospecific enzymes
- [[comparison-brown-rot-vs-white-rot-bioremediation]] fungi tackle both using small diffusible oxidizing agents generated by extracellular enzymes

## Laccase: The Blue Multicopper Oxidase

Laccase (benzenediol:oxygen oxidoreductase, EC 1.10.3.2) is the most intensively studied [[singh-cytochrome-p450-fungal-bioremediation]]**: Produces primarily LiP and MnP; model organism for [[query-how-does-lignin-degradation-by-fungi-work]] research
- **[[cotter-mycoremediation-overview]]**: Edible [[cotter-oyster-mushroom-cultivation]] with significant ligninolytic capability
- **Phlebia radiata**: Produces laccase and MnP
- **Bjerkandera adusta**: Notable for versatile [[ligninolytic-enzyme-production-bioremediation]]

## Hydrogen Peroxide Generation

Both LiP and MnP require H2O2, which must be generated by the fungus. Fungal H2O2-producing enzymes include:
- Glucose oxidase
- Glyoxal oxidase
- Aryl alcohol oxidase
- MnP itself can generate H2O2 through oxidation of organic acids
