# Permaculture Community Services and Enterprise

## Overview

[[permaculture]] villages require organised community services, diversified local economies, and multiple [[homesteading-income-streams]]. Mollison outlines governance structures for community services, methods for generating communal income, and a comprehensive analysis of how village occupants can self-employ in service to both the village and surrounding districts.

## The Community Services Council

### Purpose and Scope

A Community Services Council is elected to administer policy on publicly owned or common assets. Its responsibilities include:

- Administering policy on publicly owned or common assets
- Collecting lease monies on utility plots
- Administering funds for schools and medical services
- Ensuring that rates are used in maintaining roads, water supplies, and other public services

### Areas of Responsibility

Community services in a [[permaculture-village-development]] encompass:

| Service Area | Examples |
|---|---|
| Fire control | Fire prevention, suppression, equipment |
| Land leasing | Leasing common properties for business use |
| Public reserves | Sports grounds, recreation areas |
| Health services | Ambulance, health services, cemetery administration |
| Education | Schools, educational facilities |
| Community centre | Use and hire of shared space |
| Utilities | Power and water supply |
| [[mollison-course-coppice-forest-management]] | Sustainable timber, firewood, ecosystem |
| Tourism | Promotion and visitor management (if desired) |

### Governance Structure

- Council should be comprised of people actively involved in the above areas, not an uninvolved group
- Each area of action has a basically independent management sub-group
- Sub-groups report to Council regularly
- Each sub-group appoints one representative to Council
- Council meets monthly or even less frequently
- Sub-groups handle everyday business within their budget and allotted areas
- Council can call for and act on submissions or reports relating to specific policies
- Sub-groups can raise their own funds (as well as access public funds)
- Sub-groups may have an active business management role in income-producing areas
- Sub-groups supply workers to carry out businesses

## Income Generation for Community Services

A community needs income for continued upgrading of public services. Several complementary methods are recommended:

### Levy System

A yearly levy on each family or property, collected by the Community Council.

- **Pros**: Predictable income stream
- **Cons**: Probably in addition to normal Shire Council rates; may create hardship on some households; flat rate regardless of earning power

### Tithe System (Recommended)

Individuals, groups, and businesses (including cooperatives) tithe 10% of net profit to public services.

- **Model**: All [[mondragon-cooperatives]] fund schools, libraries, churches, and recreational grounds this way
- **Advantage**: Rates are on a sliding scale, not a flat rate regardless of earning power
- **Fairness**: Those who earn more contribute more proportionally

### Local Currency

The Community Services Council can print its own currency, backed by a valuable community resource (firewood, clean bottled water) to fund projects and new buildings.

- Provides local economic circulation
- Keeps wealth within the community
- Can be used for local trading and services
- A timber forest planted early and harvested at various stages can produce most income needed for subsequent years (social forestry model)

### User Charges

Charges by the school, community centre, and health services offset costs on a user-pays basis for tuition, facility use, and medical needs.

- Should be used in conjunction with another model
- Covers only those facilities not universally used
- Universally used services are usually pro-rated

## Potential Enterprises and Occupations

### Economic Analysis for a 50-House (100 Adult) Village

Costs are highest in three areas: food, energy, and transport. The following analysis shows how residents can earn their living:

### Food Sector (5–7 families)

About 20 adults can support 1 adult providing food supply. Potential livelihoods include:

- Open-air market gardens
- Glasshouse crop production
- Cooperative store (with local trade)
- Domestic livestock and fishery enterprise
- Part-time livelihood from café, food processing, baked goods
- All surplus sold to visitors or locals via a village market

### Energy Sector (2–5 families)

Energy needs include space heating, cooking, electric, hot water, gas, and appliances. Livelihoods include:

- [[fire-resistant-house-design-and-siting]] retrofit (with contract work outside the village)
- Hardware and appliance sales to the village and district
- Installing, repairing, and tending [[village-energy-systems-and-conversion-efficiency]]
- Most systems amortise in 3–7 years
- Over time, local manufacturing enables balanced trade in appliances

### Vehicle Sector (2–4 families)

Vehicles are the most costly item and need careful, planned approach — better on a village network level than a one-village system:

- Service, repairs, and maintenance of village vehicles
- Bulk fuel and oil supply
- Growing, distilling, or fermenting fuels for engines
- Fleet lease and insurance
- Special vehicle lease, tractor lease
- Village alliances can establish special engine or chassis manufacture

### Financial Sector (2 people)

Handling income, loans, accounting of enterprises, and running a credit union:

- Computer-based accounting and management
- Community credit union holding insurance money and incomes
- Funds enterprises such as energy systems, dairies, food processing centres

### Medical and Pharmaceutical (1–2 people)

- Medical and pharmaceutical services to village and area
- Prescription, massage, counselling, local treatment
- Some medications made and sold more widely
- Specific remedies grown or manufactured
- Community health based on prevention

### Building (1 person)

A plumber-builder-mechanic serves village establishment and later maintenance needs:
- Produces useful furnishings and sale items
- Extra income from woodworking area
