## Herbal Medicine — Homestead Apothecary

Growing medicinal herbs provides self-reliance for common ailments, connects you to plant medicine traditions, and integrates well into a [[food-forest-design]] or [[permaculture-useful-plants-reference]] planting. This page covers the top herbs to grow, harvest timing, preparation methods, and building a home apothecary.

## Top 20 Herbs for a Homestead Apothecary

### Immune Support
1. **Echinacea (E. purpurea, E. angustifolia)** — Root and aerial parts. Immune stimulant, anti-inflammatory. Full sun, well-drained soil. Harvest root in fall of 3rd year. Zones 3-9.
2. **Elderberry (Sambucus nigra/canadensis)** — Berries and flowers. Antiviral, immune tonic. Shrub, 6-12 ft. Harvest flowers in June, berries Aug-Sep. Zones 3-8.

### Nervous System / Stress
3. **Chamomile (Matricaria recutita)** — Flowers. Calming, digestive, anti-inflammatory. Easy from seed, self-sows. Harvest flowers at full bloom. Annual.
4. **Lemon Balm (Melissa officinalis)** — Leaves. Calming, antiviral (herpes), digestive. Vigorous perennial — contain in pots or raised beds. Harvest before flowering. Zones 4-9.
5. **Valerian (Valeriana officinalis)** — Root. Sedative, sleep aid, muscle relaxant. Tall perennial (4-5 ft). Harvest root in fall of 2nd year. Zones 3-7.
6. **Holy Basil / Tulsi (Ocimum sanctum)** — Leaves and flowers. Adaptogen, stress relief, immune support. Tender annual — start indoors. Multiple harvests per season.

### Skin / Wound Healing
7. **Calendula (Calendula officinalis)** — Flowers. Anti-inflammatory, wound healing, lymphatic. Prolific bloomer, self-sows. Harvest flower heads at peak bloom. Annual.
8. **Plantain (Plantago major, P. lanceolata)** — Leaves. Wound healing, drawing (splinters, bites), digestive. Common "weed" — learn to ID, don't eradicate. Harvest leaves anytime.
9. **Yarrow (Achillea millefolium)** — Flowers and leaves. Wound styptic, fever reducer, digestive bitter. Drought-tolerant perennial. Harvest flowers at peak. Zones 3-9.

### Digestive / Respiratory
10. **Peppermint (Mentha x piperita)** — Leaves. Digestive, cooling, headache relief. AGGRESSIVE spreader — containers only. Harvest before flowering. Zones 3-8.
11. **Sage (Salvia officinalis)** — Leaves. Antimicrobial, sore throat, digestive. Perennial shrub. Harvest leaves before or at flowering. Zones 4-8.
12. **Thyme (Thymus vulgaris)** — Leaves and flowering tops. Antimicrobial (esp. respiratory), expectorant. Low woody perennial. Zones 5-9.
13. **Mullein (Verbascum thapsus)** — Leaves and flowers. Expectorant, respiratory tonic, ear oil (flowers). Biennial — harvest leaves in year 1, flowers year 2. Zones 3-9.
14. **Rosemary (Rosmarinus officinalis)** — Leaves. Circulatory stimulant, cognitive, antimicrobial. Tender perennial in zones 7-10; overwinter indoors in colder zones.

### Adaptogens / Tonics
15. **Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera)** — Root. Adaptogen, stress, thyroid support. Tender perennial — grow as annual in most zones. Harvest root in fall. Requires long season (200+ days).
16. **Garlic (Allium sativum)** — Bulbs. Antimicrobial, cardiovascular, immune. Plant cloves in fall, harvest next summer. Zones 3-8.

### Aromatic / Culinary-Medicinal
17. **Ginger (Zingiber officinale)** — Rhizome. Anti-inflammatory, digestive, anti-nausea. Tropical — grow in containers, bring indoors below 50°F. 8-10 month season.
18. **Turmeric (Curcuma longa)** — Rhizome. Anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, liver support. Same culture as ginger. 8-10 month season.
19. **Lavender (Lavandula angustifolia)** — Flowers. Calming, antimicrobial, burn relief. Full sun, well-drained soil (hates wet feet). Harvest flowers at 50% bloom. Zones 5-9.

## Propagation

- **Seed**: Echinacea, chamomile, calendula, holy basil, ashwagandha, mullein, yarrow. Stratify echinacea and ashwagandha (30-60 days cold).
- **Division**: Peppermint, lemon balm, valerian, elderberry, plantain. Divide in early spring or fall.
- **Cuttings**: Rosemary, sage, thyme, lavender. Softwood cuttings in spring, semi-hardwood in fall.
- **Bulbs/Cloves**: Garlic. Plant individual cloves 2" deep, 6" apart, in Oct-Nov.

## Harvest Timing by Plant Part

| Plant Part | When to Harvest | Notes |
|-----------|----------------|-------|
| Leaves | Before flowering (morning, after dew) | Highest volatile oil content |
| Flowers | At peak bloom, mid-morning | Calendula, chamomile, yarrow, elder |
| Seeds | When dry and brown | Fennel, holy basil |
| Roots | Fall (after dormancy) or early spring | Echinacea, valerian, ashwagandha, garlic |
| Bark | Spring (sap rising) or fall | Slippery elm (ethical sourcing) |

## Drying and Storage

- **Air dry** — Hang bundles or lay on screens in warm, dark, well-ventilated space. Ideal temp: 70-90°F, humidity below 50%.
- **Dehydrator** — 95-105°F for leaves and flowers; 125°F for roots. Never exceed 135°F (volatile oils destroyed).
- **Storage** — Glass jars, dark cabinet. Label with herb name, date, plant part. Whole leaves retain potency longer than powdered. Shelf life: leaves 1-2 years, roots 2-3 years, flowers 1 year.

## Preparations

**Tinctures** — Alcohol extraction (1:5 herb:liquid by weight). Vodka (80-100 proof) for most herbs. Macerate 4-6 weeks, shake daily, strain. Dose: 1-3 mL, 2-3x/day. Glycerites use vegetable glycerin instead of alcohol (for children or alcohol-sensitive).

**Infused Oils** — Dry herb covered in oil (olive, jojoba). Solar infusion: 2-4 weeks in sunny window. Heat method: low simmer 2-4 hours (double boiler). Base for salves. Use FULLY dry herbs only (moisture = mold).

**Salves** — Infused oil + beeswax (1:4 to 1:5 ratio). Melt together, pour into tins. Excellent for calendula wound salve, plantain drawing salve.

**Teas / Decoctions** — Infusion (pouring hot water over leaves/flowers, steep 10-15 min). Decoction (simmering roots/bark 20-30 min). Drink 2-3 cups/day.

**Syrups** — Strong decoction + honey or sugar (2:1 liquid:sweetener). Elderberry syrup is the classic. Refrigerate, lasts 2-3 months. Shelf-stable with honey at 1:1 ratio.

**Capsules** — Dried, powdered herb in gelatin/vegetarian capsules. Convenient but less traditional. Use fresh powder (loss of potency over time).

## Basic Safety and Contraindications

- **Always research** each herb individually. This is a reference, not medical advice.
- **Pregnancy**: Avoid ashwagandha, yarrow, sage (internally in medicinal doses), valerian. Chamomile and ginger are generally safe.
- **Medication interactions**: Echinacea can interact with immunosuppressants. Valerian potentiates sedatives. Ginger thins blood.
- **Allergies**: Aster family (echinacea, chamomile, calendula) — avoid if ragweed allergic.
- **Start low**: Always begin with small doses. Discontinue if adverse reaction.
- **Know your ID**: Never wildcraft without positive identification. See [[mushroom-identification]] principles apply to plants too.
- Consult a qualified herbalist or physician for serious conditions, persistent symptoms, or if pregnant/nursing.

## Building a Home Apothecary

**Start with 5-10 herbs** and expand as you learn. Recommended starter set: calendula, chamomile, peppermint, plantain, yarrow, elderberry, garlic, sage.

**Essential equipment**:
- Glass mason jars (4 oz, 8 oz, 16 oz)
- Amber dropper bottles (1 oz, 2 oz) for tinctures
- Small tins (1-2 oz) for salves
- Fine mesh strainer or cheesecloth
- Kitchen scale (digital, 0.1g precision)
- Labels and permanent marker
- Mortar and pestle or herb grinder

**Organize by system**: Immune, digestive, nervous/calm, skin/[[homestead-first-aid-guide]], respiratory.
