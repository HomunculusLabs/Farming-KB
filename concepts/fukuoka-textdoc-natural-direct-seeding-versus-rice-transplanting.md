## Why Transplanting Is Unnatural

In nature, rice shatters as easily as other grasses, the grains falling in a
certain order starting at the top of the panicle and going downward. The
chances of seed surviving are infinitesimally small, but the enormous
fecundity of grasses ensures that enough survive to perpetuate the species.
Nature plants without sowing seed through this mechanism of natural shedding.

Modern rice and other cultivated grains have been genetically improved for
centuries and are no longer truly natural. They cannot simply return to wild
conditions. Yet, Fukuoka argued, making use of an unnatural method of
cultivation just because a cultivar is unnatural only moves the rice even
further from nature and evokes stronger natural repercussions.

Transplanting also creates a cascade of dependent practices. Once a farmer
commits to growing seedlings in a nursery bed, transplanting them into a
flooded paddy, the entire sequence of plowing, puddling, leveling, and flood
management becomes necessary. Each step creates the conditions that justify
the next step in an endless cycle of human intervention.
