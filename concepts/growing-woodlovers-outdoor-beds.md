## Growing Woodlovers Outdoors — Wood Chip Bed Cultivation

## Overview

"Woodlovers" are lignicolous (wood-decomposing) [[psilocybin-mushrooms]] including *Psilocybe azurescens*, *P. cyanescens*, *P. cyanofibrillosa*, *P. allenii*, and related species. Outdoor cultivation on wood chip beds is the most practical method for these species, which are difficult or impossible to fruit indoors. The technique involves preparing grain spawn, expanding onto sterilised wood chips, and establishing outdoor beds.

## Cultivation Cycle

1. **Spring**: Prepare spawn on sterilised wood chips.
2. **Spring**: Inoculate outdoor beds with colonised wood chips.
3. **Spring–Summer**: Cover beds with plastic; water regularly.
4. **Autumn**: Remove plastic; increase watering.
5. **Late autumn/early winter**: Fruiting occurs.

## Why Not Direct Grain Inoculation?

Although some growers use colonised grain directly in outdoor beds, this is **not recommended** because:

- Grain attracts **bugs and rodents**.
- Grain is more likely to **contaminate** than properly prepared wood chip spawn.
- Wood chip spawn establishes more robustly and persists longer.

## Wood Chip Substrate

### Preparation

- Use **beech** (*Fagus sylvatica*) wood chips (sold as animal bedding).
- **Small chips** for spawn preparation; **larger chips** for bed substrate.
- Soak chips in water for **48 hours** (no longer, or fungi may start growing on the wood).
- Drain thoroughly after soaking.

### Sterilisation for Spawn

- Wood chips for spawn must be **sterilised** (autoclaved) before inoculation.
- Load into jars or bags; sterilise at 15 psi for 60–90 minutes.

## Outdoor Bed Establishment

1. Select a **shaded or partially shaded** location.
2. Lay out a bed of fresh (non-sterile) wood chips.
3. Mix in colonised wood chip spawn (typically 10–20% spawn by volume).
4. Cover with plastic sheeting to retain moisture.
5. Water regularly during dry periods.

## Fruiting Conditions

- Woodlovers require **cool temperatures** to initiate fruiting (typically 7–12°C / 45–54°F).
- Natural fruiting occurs with autumn rain and temperature drops.
- Remove plastic covers in autumn to allow rain exposure.
- Beds may produce for **many years** if properly maintained.

## Maintenance

- Top up beds with fresh wood chips annually.
- Keep beds moist but not waterlogged.
- Avoid direct sunlight and strong winds.

## Suitable Species

- *Psilocybe azurescens* — Most potent; native to Pacific Northwest.
- *Psilocybe cyanescens* — Widespread in temperate regions.
- *Psilocybe allenii* — Pacific Northwest.
- *Psilocybe cyanofibrillosa* — Pacific Northwest.
- *Psilocybe subaeruginosa* — Australia/New Zealand.
