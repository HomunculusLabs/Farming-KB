## Overview and Structural Foundation
The psychedelic effects of psilocin and its phosphate prodrug psilocybin are fundamentally rooted in their
structural resemblance to serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine, 5-HT), the endogenous neurotransmitter responsible
for regulating mood, perception, cognition, and various physiological processes throughout the central
nervous system.
Both psilocin and serotonin share a common 4-hydroxyindoleethylamine core scaffold, meaning that the indole
ring system bearing a hydroxyl group at the 4-position is coupled to a two-carbon ethylamine side chain that
terminates in a primary amine.
This near-identical molecular architecture allows psilocin to function as a serotonin mimetic, binding to
and activating serotonin receptors with high affinity and producing the characteristic alterations in
perception, mood, and cognition that define the .
The Shirota synthesis paper (J. Nat. Prod., 2003) explicitly notes this structural similarity, stating that
"these compounds resemble serotonin" and that their hallucinogenic effects are "likely due to interference
with the serotonergic system," a conclusion aligning with decades of pharmacological investigation into
classical psychedelics and their receptor-level mechanisms of action.
## Detailed Structural Comparison with Serotonin
Serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine) possesses the molecular formula C10H12N2O and consists of an indole
heterocycle with a hydroxyl substituent at the 5-position of the benzene ring, connected via an ethylamine
bridge to a primary amino group at the terminal position.
Psilocin (4-hydroxy-N,N-, C12H16N2O) is distinguished from serotonin by two critical
modifications: the hydroxyl group is shifted from the 5-position to the 4-position of the indole ring, and
the terminal amine is dimethylated rather than existing as a primary amine.
These seemingly minor substitutions have profound pharmacological consequences, as the 4-hydroxy placement
and N,N-dimethylation together enhance the molecule's affinity for the 5-HT2A receptor subtype while
reducing its activity at other serotonin receptor families.
The dimethylated ethylamine side chain increases lipophilicity compared to serotonin, facilitating more
efficient passage across the blood-brain barrier and allowing psilocin to reach central serotonergic neurons
at pharmacologically relevant concentrations following oral or intravenous administration.
Psilocybin (O-phosphoryl-4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) represents a phosphate ester prodrug of psilocin,
wherein the 4-hydroxyl is masked with a phosphate group that is cleaved by endogenous alkaline phosphatases
in vivo, yielding the active psilocin molecule with dramatically improved metabolic stability.
## 5-HT2A Receptor Agonism as the Primary Mechanism
The prevailing pharmacological model for psychedelic action centers on partial agonism at the serotonin 5-HT2A
receptor, a G-protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) predominantly expressed in cortical layer V pyramidal neurons.
Psilocin binds to the 5-HT2A receptor with high affinity (Ki values typically in the low nanomolar range),
where it acts as a partial agonist, activating the receptor with sufficient efficacy to trigger downstream
signaling cascades that alter neuronal excitability, gene expression, and network-level oscillatory dynamics.
The receptor activation engages Gq/11 proteins, which stimulate phospholipase C (PLC), leading to inositol
trisphosphate (IP3) and diacylglycerol (DAG) production, subsequent intracellular calcium release, and
activation of protein kinase C (PKC).
These intracellular events ultimately modulate thalamocortical gating, disrupting the normal filtering of
sensory and cognitive information and producing the synesthetic, hallucinatory, and perceptually expansive
effects characteristic of the psilocybin experience.
The critical role of 5-HT2A has been demonstrated through antagonism studies using ketanserin and risperidone,
which reliably block psychedelic effects when administered prior to psilocybin, and through knockout studies
in mice lacking the 5-HT2A receptor, which show no behavioral response to psychedelic administration.

provides accurate molecular weight confirmation and fragmentation patterns that distinguish psilocin (m/z 205

tryptamines encountered in forensic contexts.
