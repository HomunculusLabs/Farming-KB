# Psychedelic Harm Reduction Guide

## Overview

Harm reduction is a pragmatic approach to psychoactive substance use that prioritizes minimizing negative outcomes rather than enforcing abstinence. For psychedelics, harm reduction means providing accurate information so people who choose to use these substances can do so with the lowest possible risk. This guide covers the essential principles and practices for safer psychedelic experiences.

## Dosage Guidelines

### Start Low, Go Slow

The single most important harm reduction principle. Effects scale non-linearly with psychedelics -- doubling a dose can multiply the intensity by 4-10x rather than 2x.

### General Thresholds (approximate, substance-dependent)

- **Threshold**: Minimum dose for perceptible effects
- **Light**: Noticeable changes in perception and mood, manageable
- **Moderate**: Strong visual and cognitive effects, significant alteration of thought patterns
- **Strong**: Intense experience, potential for [[psychedelic-safety-and-contra-indications]])
- History of severe psychotic episodes triggered by psychedelics

### Strong Caution

- Personal or family history of schizophrenia or bipolar disorder
- Active PTSD (without professional support)
- Borderline personality disorder (can be destabilizing)
- Cardiovascular conditions
- Current substance use disorder
- Underage (brain still developing)

### Situational Caution

- Unstable living situation
- Unsupportive or hostile people nearby
- Inability to commit to a full experience (time pressure, responsibilities)
- Recent trauma or loss
- Forced or coerced use (never use psychedelics against your will)

## Red Flags During an Experience

- Rapid heart rate, chest pain, or difficulty breathing (medical emergency -- call for help)
- Complete inability to distinguish the experience from reality for extended periods
- Self-harm ideation or behavior
- Violent or dangerous behavior
- Seizure
- Prolonged unconsciousness
