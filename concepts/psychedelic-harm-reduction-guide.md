# Psychedelic Harm Reduction Guide

## Overview

Harm reduction is a pragmatic approach to psychoactive substance use that prioritizes minimizing negative outcomes rather than enforcing abstinence. For psychedelics, harm reduction means providing accurate information so people who choose to use these substances can do so with the lowest possible risk. This guide covers the essential principles and practices for safer psychedelic experiences.

## Dosage Guidelines

### Start Low, Go Slow

The single most important harm reduction principle. Effects scale non-linearly with psychedelics -- doubling a dose can multiply the intensity by 4-10x rather than 2x.

### General Thresholds (approximate, substance-dependent)

- **Threshold**: Minimum dose for perceptible effects
- **Light**: Noticeable changes in perception and mood, manageable
- **Moderate**: Strong visual and cognitive effects, significant alteration of thought patterns
- **Strong**: Intense experience, potential for ego dissolution, requires experience and preparation
- **Heroic**: Very high dose, complete ego dissolution, only for very experienced users with extensive preparation

Always research substance-specific dosing ranges from trusted sources (Erowid, PsychonautWiki) before use. Individual sensitivity varies enormously due to body weight, metabolism, stomach contents, and natural variation.

### Weight and Potency Considerations

- Weigh doses with a milligram scale, never eyeball
- Natural products (mushrooms, cactus, ayahuasca) vary in potency; start with less than you think you need
- Tolerance builds rapidly; wait at least 1-2 weeks between psychedelic experiences (longer for some substances)

## Substance Testing

Never consume a substance without verifying its identity. See [[psychedelic-drug-testing-and-adulterant-screening]] for detailed testing procedures.

### Reagent Testing

- Use reagent test kits (Marquis, Mecke, Mandelin, Ehrlich, Hofmann) to verify substance identity
- No single reagent identifies everything; use multiple reagents for better confidence
- Reagents indicate presence only, not purity or dosage

### Fentanyl Test Strips

- Essential for any substance not obtained directly from a trusted source
- Fentanyl contamination has been found in cocaine, MDMA, and other non-opioid substances
- Test every batch, every time

## Knowing Your Source

The safest approach is to know and trust the person who synthesized or grew the substance. When this is not possible:

- Prioritize sources with reputation systems and user reviews
- Avoid substances with no testing history
- Be especially cautious with pressed pills (high risk of adulterants)
- Natural materials (whole mushrooms, cactus, plant matter) are generally lower risk than synthetic powders and pills, though natural does not mean safe

## Set and Setting

The context of a psychedelic experience profoundly shapes its outcome. "Set" refers to mindset (mental state, expectations, intentions) and "setting" refers to physical and social environment.

### Set Preparation

- Enter the experience with a clear, positive intention (not an escape from problems)
- Be well-rested, fed, and in good physical health
- Resolve or set aside immediate life stressors if possible
- Avoid psychedelics during acute grief, instability, or major life transitions
- Journal about your intentions beforehand

### Setting Preparation

- Choose a comfortable, familiar, private space
- Prepare the space in advance: blankets, water, snacks, music, art supplies
- Remove potential hazards (sharp objects, open windows, stairs)
- Control who is present: only trusted, supportive people
- Consider the season, weather, and time of day
- Have a plan for the full duration plus aftercare

## Knowing When NOT to Use

Certain conditions significantly increase risk:

### Absolute Contraindications

- Active psychosis or schizophrenia
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding
- Currently on certain medications (SSRIs, MAOIs, lithium -- see [[psychedelic-safety-and-contra-indications]])
- History of severe psychotic episodes triggered by psychedelics

### Strong Caution

- Personal or family history of schizophrenia or bipolar disorder
- Active PTSD (without professional support)
- Borderline personality disorder (can be destabilizing)
- Cardiovascular conditions
- Current substance use disorder
- Underage (brain still developing)

### Situational Caution

- Unstable living situation
- Unsupportive or hostile people nearby
- Inability to commit to a full experience (time pressure, responsibilities)
- Recent trauma or loss
- Forced or coerced use (never use psychedelics against your will)

## Red Flags During an Experience

- Rapid heart rate, chest pain, or difficulty breathing (medical emergency -- call for help)
- Complete inability to distinguish the experience from reality for extended periods
- Self-harm ideation or behavior
- Violent or dangerous behavior
- Seizure
- Prolonged unconsciousness

For managing difficult experiences, see [[psychedelic-crisis-response-protocol]].

## Integration

The period after a psychedelic experience is as important as the experience itself. Integration practices include:

- Journaling about the experience within 24-48 hours while details are fresh
- Talking with trusted friends, a therapist, or a psychedelic integration specialist
- Making concrete changes based on insights (diet, habits, relationships, creative practices)
- Being gentle with yourself -- avoid major life decisions for at least a week
- Regular meditation, nature time, and creative expression
- Avoid immediately repeating the experience; allow time for processing
