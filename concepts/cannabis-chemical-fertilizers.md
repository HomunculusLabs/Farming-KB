# Cannabis Chemical Fertilizers

## Overview

Chemical (synthetic/soluble) fertilizers are an excellent choice for indoor container cultivation. They dissolve in water, are easy to control, and can be easily added or washed (leached) out of the growing medium. Soluble-chemical fertilizers allow precise control over the exact amounts of nutrients available to plants in an available form.

## Types of Chemical Fertilizers

### Soluble Fertilizers

Soluble fertilizers dissolve in water and offer the most control for cannabis cultivation:

- Easy to measure and apply
- Easy to flush from growing medium if problems arise
- Can be applied as a water solution onto soil
- High-quality hydroponic fertilizers using food-grade nutrients are the best value
- Avoid low-quality fertilizers that do not list all necessary micronutrients on the label

### Granular Fertilizers

Chemical granular fertilizers work well but carry significant risks:

- Can easily be over-applied, creating toxic soil
- Almost impossible to leach out fast enough to save the plant
- Not recommended for containerized cannabis cultivation

### Time-Release Fertilizers (e.g., Osmocote)

- Convenient; only require one application every few months
- Exact control is lost
- Best suited for ornamental containerized plants
- Not recommended for cannabis where precise nutrient management is needed

## Understanding Fertilizer Labels

### N-P-K Numbers

Fertilizers show the N-P-K in big letters on the front of the package. These three numbers always appear in the same order:

- **N** = cannabis-nitrogen|Nitrogen (total combined elemental)
- **P** = cannabis-phosphorus|Phosphorus (listed as phosphoric anhydride P2O5, understating actual P by 44%)
- **K** = cannabis-potassium|Potassium (listed in potash form K2O)

### WSN and WIN

- **WSN** (water-soluble nitrogen) -- Dissolves readily; fast-release nitrogen source
- **WIN** (water-insoluble nitrogen) -- Does not dissolve easily; often organic form; slow-release

## Application Methods

### Top-Dressing

Apply fertilizer evenly over the entire garden bed area. Best for outdoor gardens.

### Side-Dressing

Apply fertilizer around the bases of individual plants.

### Fertigation

Mix fertilizer with water and apply with each watering. Use a siphon applicator (typically 1:15 ratio) to meter concentrated fertilizer into the water supply.

### Foliar Feeding

Spray liquid fertilizer solution on foliage. See [[cannabis-foliar-feeding]] for detailed guidelines.

## Stage-Specific Fertilizer Programs

### Germination and Seedling Stage

High phosphorus intake for root development.

### Vegetative Growth Stage

Use a general-purpose or "grow" fertilizer with high nitrogen content. Larger amounts of nitrogen for green-leaf growth; phosphorus and potassium also necessary in substantial levels.

### Flowering Stage

Use a "super bloom" fertilizer with less nitrogen and more potassium, phosphorus, and calcium to promote fat, heavy, dense buds. Cannabis needs some nitrogen during flowering but very little -- with no nitrogen, buds do not develop to full potential.

## Preventive Maintenance

Leach soil with 1-2 gallons (4-8 liters) of mild nutrient solution per gallon of soil every month. This is the best form of preventive maintenance against toxic salt buildup.

## Advantages Over Organic Fertilizers

- Easy to measure exact amounts for each growth stage
- Easy to flush from growing medium
- Precise EC and pH management possible
- Consistent nutrient content in every batch
- Faster plant response to corrections

## Choosing a Fertilizer

The diversity of hydroponic fertilizers is extensive. Local shop owners know which work best in the local climate and water. Read the entire fertilizer label and follow directions. Always choose fertilizers that list a specific analysis for each trace element.
