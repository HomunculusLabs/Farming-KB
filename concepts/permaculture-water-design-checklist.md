# Permaculture Water Design Checklist

The water design checklist is a comprehensive framework for assessing, planning, and implementing [[permaculture-plants-aquatic-systems-water-plants|plants]] or trees without irrigation. This reduces long-term water demand and creates resilient landscapes.

## Landscape-Scale Water Planning

### Dam Site Selection
Soil samples revealing **40% or more clay content** indicate sites suited to earth-dam [[query-what-are-the-different-fruit-tree-training-systems-and-which-should-i-use|fruit]] trees and timber species benefit from greywater irrigation
- **Common effluent schemes** — for villages, design on-site cleanup through maximum-use systems

### Village-Scale Maximum-Use Design
For community-scale systems, design a cascade of uses:

1. **Methane production** — anaerobic digestion of organic wastes
2. **Plant production** — aquatic and terrestrial crops using nutrient-rich effluent
3. **Irrigation** — final-stage water application to food or fuel crops

## Dam Construction Guidelines

### Professional Supervision
Get good advice on, and **supervise construction of**, all dams. Dam failure can be catastrophic.

### Environmental Protections
- Do **not** impede normal stream flow
- Do **not** block fish migration
- Site houses out of the way in case of dam failure

### Spillway Design
Allow **adequate stable spillway flow** for "worst case" rain intensity. This is the most critical safety feature of any dam.

## Soil and Water Integration

### Tree Planting on Earth Storages
Make sure all earth storages, and in particular **swales**, are planted with trees to:

- Remove infiltrated water (prevent waterlogging)
- In arid areas, prevent **salting problems** caused by rising water tables

### Cloud Seeding Considerations
Before recommending cloud-seeding:

- Ensure the area to be affected is **warned**
- Design dams and swales to cope with potential increase of up to **30%** in rainfall

## Forest and Windbreak Design

### Forested Ridges
Design for **forested ridges** and maximize forest cover on strategic uplands. Forests are the most effective water management systems:

- Intercept rainfall and reduce runoff velocity
- Increase infiltration through root systems
- Reduce evaporation through canopy shading
- Release water slowly through baseflow

Do not lend skills to **high-country deforestation** or any deforestation.

### In-Crop Windbreaks
Windbreaks and in-crop trees are **essential** to reduce water loss in croplands through:

- Reducing wind speed across soil surfaces
- Decreasing evapotranspiration rates
- Providing microclimate moderation

## Soil Introduction: Foundation for Water Management

Soils are the interface between water and land. Understanding soil properties is essential to effective water design because:

### Soil Complexity
Soils defy precise treatment due to constantly varying:

- **Structure and permeability** — changes with depth, treatment, and time
- **Organic content** — variable across micro-elevations
- **Gaseous components** — derived from atmosphere, soil processes, and deep sediments
- **Minerals** — complex mixtures from varied rock types
- **pH and solute status** — fluctuating with biological and chemical activity

### Soil Formation Factors
The key factors in soil formation are:

1. **Rock type** — the parent material determines mineral composition
2. **Climate** — temperature and precipitation drive weathering rates
3. **Topography/landform** — slope and position affect water movement and soil depth

Water plays a central role in rock breakdown:

- Combines with felspars in rock to swell and fracture the rock
