## Overview

The flowering stage is the reproductive phase where cannabis plants produce resinous buds. It is triggered by switching the light cycle to 12 hours of light and 12 hours of uninterrupted darkness (12/12). Flowering typically lasts 7-12 weeks depending on genetics, with indica-dominant strains finishing faster and sativa-dominant strains requiring longer.

## The 12/12 Flip

Switching from 18/6 to 12/12 initiates a hormonal change within the plant. The extended dark period triggers the production of florigen, a flowering hormone. Key considerations:

- **Dark period must be uninterrupted** — even brief light leaks can cause hermaphroditism or delayed flowering
- Use light-proofing tape, sealed ducting, and verified dark rooms
- The flip can be stressful; ensure plants are healthy before switching

## Week-by-Week Development

### Pre-Flower / Stretch (Weeks 1-3)

After the flip, plants enter a rapid vegetative stretch phase, often growing 50-200% taller depending on genetics.

- **Week 1**: Plant shows sex — pistils (white hairs) appear at branch nodes. Calyx development begins.
- **Week 2**: Rapid vertical growth. Bud sites become visible as small clusters. The plant is still putting energy into stem and leaf growth.
- **Week 3**: Stretch begins to slow. Bud sites start forming distinct shapes. Pistil production increases. This is the last major window for defoliation — removing large fan leaves that block light to lower bud sites.

Managing height during stretch is critical. Techniques include:

- Tying down tall branches with LST
- Super cropping overly vertical shoots
- Adjusting light height to manage stretch rate

### Bud Development (Weeks 4-6)

- **Week 4**: Buds noticeably fattening. Trichome production becomes visible to the naked eye (frosty appearance). Aroma intensifies. Plants are at peak nutrient demand.
- **Week 5**: Buds become dense and resinous. Pistils begin changing from white to amber/brown. Trichomes are developing but mostly clear.
- **Week 6**: Major bulking phase. For many indica strains, buds approach final size. Trichomes start transitioning from clear to milky.

### Late Flowering / Ripening (Weeks 7-12)

- **Week 7-8**: Pistils continue browning. Trichomes shift from clear to milky/opaque. Terpene profile peaks.
- **Week 9-12** (strain dependent): Trichomes begin turning amber, signaling THC degradation to CBN. This produces a more sedative effect.

## Trichome Development and Harvest Timing

Trichomes are the clearest indicator of harvest readiness. Use a jeweler's loupe (30x-100x) or digital microscope to inspect:

| Trichome State | Effect Profile | Visual |
|---|---|---|
| Mostly clear | Uplifting, cerebral, less potent | Transparent, glass-like heads |
| 50-70% milky / cloudy | Peak potency, balanced effect | Opaque white heads |
| Mixed milky + amber | Balanced with body relaxation | Mix of white and golden heads |
| Mostly amber | Sedative, couch-lock | Dark amber/golden heads |

Most growers harvest when 50-70% of trichomes are milky with some amber beginning to appear. Waiting too long into full amber reduces THC content significantly.

## Nutrient Management During Flowering

### Phosphorus and Potassium

During flowering, plants shift from nitrogen-heavy to phosphorus and potassium-heavy feeding:

- **Early flower (weeks 1-3)**: Transition from veg nutrients. Reduce nitrogen, increase P and K.
- **Mid flower (weeks 4-6)**: Peak P-K demand. N should be minimal but not zero.
- **Late flower (weeks 7+)**: Reduce all nutrients. Many growers taper off entirely in the final 1-2 weeks.

### Nutrient Burn in Flower

Overfeeding during flowering causes:

- Tip burn and leaf clawing
- "Fox-tailing" — buds produce airy, abnormal calyx growth
- Harsh or chemical taste in final product
- Reduced terpene production

When in doubt, feed less. Plants need less than most growers assume during late flower.

## Flushing

Flushing involves feeding only plain water (or lightly amended water) for the final 1-2 weeks before harvest. The intent is to allow the plant to use stored nutrients, producing a smoother smoke.

- **Debate**: Scientific evidence on flushing's effectiveness is mixed. Many growers report smoother flavor; controlled studies show minimal difference in ash color or combustion quality.
- **Living soil**: Flushing is generally unnecessary in [[cannabis-living-soil-guide]] beds where the plant feeds on-demand from the biology. The soil buffer naturally tapers nutrient availability.
- **Hydroponics**: More important in inert media where nutrient salts accumulate. Run plain water for 5-7 days minimum.

## Environmental Conditions in Flower

### Temperature

Ideal flower temperatures are 68-80 F (20-27 C) lights on. Night temps should not drop below 60 F (15 C) to prevent slowed metabolism. Warmer temps (up to 85 F) during late flower can increase terpene vaporization but also boost mold risk.

### Humidity

Humidity must be progressively lowered during flowering to prevent bud rot and powdery mildew:

- **Early flower (weeks 1-3)**: 55-65% RH
- **Mid flower (weeks 4-6)**: 45-55% RH
- **Late flower (weeks 7+)**: 40-45% RH (critical to avoid mold on dense buds)

Dehumidifiers are often essential during late flower. See [[cannabis-environment-control]] for VPD-targeted humidity management.

### Light Spectrum

Many growers shift to warmer light spectrums (more red/far-red, less blue) during flowering. This mimics the autumn sun and promotes bud development. Some LED fixtures have a dedicated "bloom" switch or channel for this purpose.

## Common Flowering Issues

- **Hermaphroditism**: Caused by light leaks, stress, or genetics. Remove pollen sacs immediately or discard the plant if heavily affected.
- **Bud rot (botrytis)**: Gray mold inside dense buds. Cut away affected areas, increase airflow, reduce humidity. See [[cannabis-disease-guide]].
- **Foxtailing**: Can be genetic or caused by light stress and heat. Reduces bag appeal but bud is still usable.
- **Slow development**: Often caused by low temperatures, excessive defoliation, or nutrient lockout.

## Transition to Harvest
