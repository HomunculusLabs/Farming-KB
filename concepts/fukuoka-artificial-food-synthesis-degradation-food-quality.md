# Artificial Food Synthesis and the Degradation of Food Quality

## Core Concept

Fukuoka traces the progressive degradation of food quality from open-field cultivation through hothouse horticulture to soil-less culture and ultimately to total [[fukuoka-critique-scientific-agriculture-civilization]]: the belief that food quality can be maintained or improved by replacing natural systems with artificial ones.

## The Stages of Agricultural Degradation

### Stage One: Open Field to Hothouse

The destruction of agriculture can be seen in the transition by farmers from the open cultivation of vegetables to hothouse horticulture. This began innocently enough with seeding and growing melons and tomatoes in soil within hot beds or vinyl houses arranged in neat rows.

### Stage Two: Soil Replacement with Inert Media

The next stage was sand culture and gravel culture, using sand or gravel in place of soil because these materials have fewer bacteria and are thus considered cleaner. This was accompanied by a fundamental change in thinking — replacing the notion of forming rich soil with that of administering nutrients. The [[fukuoka-natural-fertility-cycle]]. The sand and gravel served only to support the plant, so simpler materials were sought: plastic or polymer netting and containers in which seeds are planted and roots extend in all directions.

### Stage Three: Total Environmental Control

In the sealed growing chamber, the environment is completely sterile, eliminating insect damage or blight at first. Since root absorption of nutrients dissolved in water is inefficient, the nutrient solution is sprayed on a regular basis over the entire plant. Nutrients are taken in through roots and leaf surfaces simultaneously, resulting in a higher [[carbon-dioxide-retention-jar-lid-mushroom-cultivation]] is sprayed, and oxygen is pumped in, making plant growth several times faster than in field cultivation. The plant becomes a biological machine operating in a factory, not a living organism participating in an ecosystem.

### Stage Four: Total Chemical Synthesis

The final stage is the total synthesis of food, rendering farms and gardens unnecessary:

- **Urea synthesis** has enabled man to produce any organic material desired
- **Protein synthesis** enables man-made meat to be fabricated from various materials
- **Butter and cheese** can be made from petroleum
- **Starch synthesis** from photosynthesis research will allow carbohydrate production
- **Saccharification of wood and oil** offers further pathways to [[fukuoka-textdoc-nature-must-not-be-dissected-limits-scientific-analysis]] cannot detect — qualities that relate to the food's connection to living systems, seasonal cycles, and the total environment in which it was produced.

Nature has struck back fiercely against this affront by technology, in the form of increased insect damage. The response by man has been an agriculture increasingly dependent on pesticides and fertilizers — a classic vicious cycle where each technological intervention creates the need for further intervention.

## The Oil Patch Replaces the Paddy

Fukuoka coined a striking phrase to describe the transformation: crops are no longer grown in the paddy but in the oil patch. The use of oil-based energy in agriculture has reached the point where one could almost talk of growing rice in the oil patch rather than in the paddy.

Farming under the open skies has disappeared. Agriculture has been degraded to the manufacture of petroleum-derived foods, and the farmer has become a seller of false goods called nutritional food. The farmer who once worked hand in hand with nature has capitulated to the pressures of society and become a subcontractor to the oil industry.

## Why Artificial Environments Fail

### The Vegetable Factory Fallacy

Suppose an entrepreneur decided to grow rice and vegetables in a large building at the center of a major city, making full three-dimensional spatial use of the building with central heating, air conditioning, artificial lighting, and automatic spraying devices for carbon dioxide and nutrient solutions.

Such a vegetable factory cannot be built and run without considerable outlays for capital and materials, so the vegetables produced are inevitably expensive. However efficient and modern the facility, it cannot possibly grow produce more cheaply than crops grown naturally with sunlight and soil.

### The Fundamental Principle

Nature produces without calling for supplies or remuneration, but human effort always demands payment in return. The more sophisticated the [[fukuoka-textdoc-production-costs-not-coming-down]], yet costs have skyrocketed.

### Microorganism Food Fallacy

Equally mistaken is the notion that food can be produced cheaply and in large quantity with microorganisms such as chlorella and yeast. Science cannot produce something from nothing. Invariably, the result is a decrease in production rather than an increase, yielding a high-cost product.
