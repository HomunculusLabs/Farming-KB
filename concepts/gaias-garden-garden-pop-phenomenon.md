## The Early Establishment Phase

Before the pop, the garden goes through what can
feel like a frustrating period of sluggish growth
and imperceptible improvement. This early phase
can last several years, during which:

- Trees and shrubs are small and struggling
- Soil improvement seems barely noticeable
- Insects and wildlife are sparse
- Weeds constantly threaten to overwhelm plantings
- The gardener may feel that the whole project is
  failing

This is not failure -- it is the necessary
foundation-building phase. During this time, the
garden is assembling its underground infrastructure:
[[soil-life]]. Thousands of species of soil
organisms arrive with this mulch, drift in on air
currents and raindrops, or are present but dormant.
In the welcoming habitat, they spring to life,
rapidly enlarging the decomposer component of the
critical producer-consumer-decomposer cycle.

As the decomposers ramp up, they pump out an
enormous surplus of nutrients, throwing off food
and energy in wild abundance. More plants and
animals, both in diversity and sheer numbers, can
thrive. The garden becomes an organism rather than
a collection of parts.
