## Wind Interactions

- Wind completely disappears within an effective forest within 1,000 meters, except at the crown

- Forest forces 60% of wind upward, creating high pressure windward and decreased evaporation

- Rainfall increases 15-20% on windward side (measured in Holland and Sweden)

- Cutting forest decreases regional rainfall by 10-30%

- Wind creates secondary spiral patterns causing alternating wet/dry belts transverse to wind direction

- Need about 5 trees wide for effective windbreak; single belt at 40% penetrability has effect

- Wind energy is absorbed and converted by trees -- possibly to wood (stem diameter increases with wind)

## Humidity and Precipitation

- Forest reduces humidity in air by ~50% within 100 meters on foggy nights (positive interception)

- Trees catch 86% of precipitation; only 14% falls as rain in some coastal forests (Tasmania)

- Organic particles released by forests are more effective precipitation nuclei than inorganic particles

- 60% of inland rain falls from forest clouds, not sea clouds

- Up to 80 miles out in the Atlantic, pollen from American forests coated ship decks

## Water Storage and Soil

- 86% of forest mass is water; 96% of leaves and twigs are water

- Forest floor humus stores 6-12 inches of water; every foot of mineral soil holds another inch

- 30 inches of soil absorbs 3-inch rainfall with zero runoff

- Forest soils are "bored out" by old roots, creating conduits for deep percolation

- No significant evaporation of through-fall water occurs from forest soils

- No upward migration of salts occurs under forest cover

- Forest delays snow melt by at least a month

## Air Quality

- Negative ions increase sharply in air passing through forests

- Forests absorb tons of inorganic materials and release tons of organic particles

- A single small plant can handle CO2 and CO problems in an office

- Dark trees become radiators; birches are reflectors

- Forests act as enormous vertical water tanks affecting earth load

## Key Quotes

"By the removal of ridge forests alone, we can produce deserts in any climate. By the removal of forests alone, we can remove soils."

"The removal of the forest has been the main cause of the collapse of nations. Because when the forests go they just haven't the water, the soil, or the climate quality to sustain human life thereafter."

## Forest Microclimate Creation

Forests create distinct microclimates that extend well beyond their boundaries, influencing the productivity and livability of surrounding areas.

- **Temperature moderation:** Forest canopies reduce ground-level temperature fluctuations by providing shade during the day and reducing radiative cooling at night. The diurnal temperature range under a forest canopy is significantly narrower than in open areas, creating more stable growing conditions for understory species.

- **Frost protection:** Forests reduce frost frequency and severity by trapping long-wave radiation and reducing radiative heat loss. Orchards and sensitive crops planted within or adjacent to forest margins benefit from this frost protection effect.

- **Evapotranspiration feedback:** Forests release enormous quantities of water vapor through transpiration. This moisture enters local air circulation patterns, increasing humidity and the likelihood of precipitation downwind. The 60% figure for inland rain originating from forest clouds underscores the scale of this effect.

- **Wind speed reduction:** The elimination of wind within 1,000 meters of forest edge protects crops, livestock, and structures from wind damage, desiccation, and heat loss. This is the basis of [[permaculture-housing-design]].

## Implications for Permaculture Design

The forest-climate data has profound implications for landscape design decisions.

- **Ridge forest preservation:** Mollison's statement that ridge forest removal alone can create deserts makes forest preservation on ridgelines a non-negotiable design principle. Ridge forests generate the precipitation that waters lower slopes and valleys.

- **Reforestation as climate restoration:** Tree planting is not merely an aesthetic or conservation activity -- it is climate engineering. Restoring forests restores rainfall patterns, moderates temperature extremes, and rebuilds soil water storage capacity.

- **Forest placement for maximum effect:** Strategic forest placement can optimize beneficial climate effects. Windward forests increase rainfall. Riparian forests protect water quality. Ridge forests generate orographic precipitation. Each placement serves a specific climate function.
