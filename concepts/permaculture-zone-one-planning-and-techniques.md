# Permaculture Zone One Planning and Techniques

updated: 2026-04-18

Zone one in [[urban-permaculture-edge-effect-and-design-principles]] is the area of most intensive management, immediately adjacent to the dwelling. It contains the kitchen garden, culinary herbs, frequently harvested vegetables, small ponds, and greenhouse. Bill Mollison's Permaculture Design Course (Pamphlet IX) provides detailed practical guidance for zone one design.

## House Layout for Zone Efficiency

The house setup must follow zone logic: mud room, pantry, kitchen on the entry side; bedrooms on the opposite side. The most intensive garden section goes around the garden entries. House orientation should follow sun-time (e.g., 8 AM to 3 PM), not compass alignment.

## The Herb Spiral and Key Herbs

Place a small herb spiral and a great mass of parsley near garden entries. Two critical herbs:
- **Chives**: Several varieties (Chinese chives, ordinary with purple tops, fine-leaf blue)
- **Parsley**: Start a bed, let it go to seed, shake seed heads throughout the garden. Once parsley is thick, you never need to replant.

Other common herbs: tarragon, thyme, rosemary, sage, mint (in pots to contain spread), dill.

## Vegetable Layout

From closest to farthest:
1. **Immediately adjacent**: Lettuce, cabbage, celery, New Zealand spinach, broccoli, zucchini
2. **Mid-range**: Main crops -- winter squashes, corn, carrots, main crop onions, parsnips
3. **Outer edge**: Potatoes (in permanent straw boxes), asparagus, comfrey

## Companion Planting Systems

### Corn-Bean-Squash (Three Sisters)
Create a band of corn about four feet across, hollow in the middle. Place manure pile and plant cucurbits in the hollow. Plant climbing beans around the outside. This creates an edge effect. You can pick all corn and beans; when pumpkins are ready, the corn is finished and accessible.

### Tomato System
- Jerusalem artichoke windbreak around the outside
- Basil on the hot (south-facing) side
- Parsley on the cool side
- Marigolds in random clumps for pest control
- Gooseberries outside on the cool side for specific pest control
- Nasturtium for white fly control (essential root contact)
- Never re-buy tomato seed -- save from volunteer seedlings under mulch
- Tomatoes grow better on the same spot (no rotation)

### Potato Straw Box
Board up an area (8' x 5'), throw in straw or seaweed, set potatoes 9" x 9", scatter ashes, fill with straw. No soil, no bottom. Keep it ticking all the time -- 12+ years possible. Comfrey leaf under each potato provides nutrients.

## Mulch vs. Compost

You get about 1/12th the nutrient from compost compared to mulch. Composting consumes nutrients (many go into the air). Sheet mulching spreads materials and lets decomposition occur slowly on the soil surface; leach loss goes into soil. If you want maximum value, sheet mulch.

## Kitchen Waste Management
- Pick up a handful of mulch, scatter garbage around, put mulch back
- In winter, freeze kitchen wastes into blocks
- Banana peels and tea leaves placed on mulch disappear overnight
- Hay goes through chicken pens first (they eat seeds, shred it, add manure)
- Never put thick mulch of wet/slimy material (creates anaerobic conditions)

## Sheet Mulching for Lawn Conversion
To convert lawn to garden (one day's work):
1. Never dig it
2. Use old tick mattresses or thick newspaper, flop onto lawn
3. Cut little holes, drop potatoes through
4. Put a handful of hay over them

## Ponds

Place a small pond (4 feet across, 18 inches deep) in the herb area:
- Produces 200-300 frogs twice per summer
- Frogs live in cabbages, lettuces, and mulch; return to pond to breed
- Build pond slightly higher than surrounding soil, paved with stones
- Sweet alyssum and thyme between stones for frog exit ramps
- Garlic bulbs squeezed into water kill mosquito larvae (100% kill, harmless to tadpoles)
- Backswimmers for additional mosquito control

## Trellis Systems

- **Deciduous** around kitchen windows: hops, grapes, runner beans (summer shade, winter sun)
- **Evergreen** for wind control: jasmine, honeysuckle, lilac
- **Horizontal** trellis for summer heat reduction, winter sun penetration
- **Stone walls** need 40% penetrability; avoid turbulence with flat stones
- Hops as a "noble vine" -- makes light pillows for children

## Greenhouse Crops

Beyond extending seasons for ordinary crops, grow critical and income species:
- **Vanilla**: Hardy orchid, grows up greenhouse walls. Hand-pollinate with a feather. One plant produces hundreds of beans -- better commercial crop than tomatoes
- **Cinnamon**: Propagates from cuttings, coppices, two-year cycle. One pot produces cinnamon for 100+ households
- **Tea** (Camellia sinensis): Cold-hardy shrub. One plant supplies all tea needs. Grows in open up to latitude 40 degrees
- **Coffee**: Shade-tolerant indoor shrub. Produces heavy berry crops. Eat or roast berries
- **Pineapple**: Needs ethylene to set fruit -- enclose with apple. Dandelion provides constant ethylene production
- **Turmeric**: Equally easy to grow as ginger
- **Bananas**: Two plants supply a family. Dwarf cavendish recommended for greenhouses

## Perennial Vegetables

- **Celery**: Perennial, not annual. Wild lovage tastes like celery and is perennial
- **Leeks**: Permanent -- let some go to seed, set bulbs straight out
- **Chard**: Scatter along pathways, stands a long time
- **Cross-cut cabbages**: Cut stem crosswise to get four good little heads

## Mollison's Inventions

### Black Sand Solar Heater
Run water pipes through a box of black sand with glass on top. Far more efficient than metal collectors -- endless hot water at no cost. Could be built on top of water tanks.

### Titanium Oxide Nitrogen Fixer
White sand + titanium oxide (common paint pigment) + sunlight = catalyzes atmospheric nitrogen into ammonia. Endless nitrogen fertilizer for gardens and sand dune restoration.

### Parabolic Solar Cooking
A house front designed as a focusing system with sun-reflecting mirrors under eaves, focusing 8 feet off ground. Can melt a car (copper pipe in a hole) for winter heating.
