## Why Humidity Matters

Mushroom fruiting bodies are composed of approximately 90% water. During fruiting, the mycelium transfers large volumes of water from the substrate to the developing mushrooms. If the ambient humidity is too low, the mushrooms lose water faster than the mycelium can supply it, causing them to dry out, crack, or abort entirely. High humidity reduces transpiration rates and allows the mushrooms to develop properly.

The optimal relative humidity for fruiting varies by species but generally falls within the 85-95% range. [[query-how-do-i-grow-figs-in-cold-climates]] or those cultivating during winter, a warm humid air delivery system can be constructed:

### Design

- A sealed container serves as both the humidity reservoir and delivery system
- The container is partially filled with several inches of water
- A water heater with a timer thermostat maintains the water temperature
- One large duct serves as the main warm humid air input
- Multiple smaller ducts around the container serve as distribution outlets
- A fan pushes the warm humid air through the system

### Setup Considerations

- The water heater timer requires calibration — it may take several hours to find the right setting that maintains the desired [[cold-shock-fruiting-trigger-species-specificity-psilocybe-panaeolus]]
- Ensure all connections are sealed to prevent air leaks that would compromise humidity
