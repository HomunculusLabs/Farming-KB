# Cannabis for Anxiety Disorders

The relationship between cannabis and anxiety is complex and dose-dependent. Low doses of THC and CBD can reduce anxiety, while high doses of THC can paradoxically increase it. This biphasic response is one of the most important concepts in therapeutic cannabis use.

## The Dosing Paradox

### Low Doses (1-5mg THC)
- Reduces anxiety via CB1 receptor activation
- Enhances endocannabinoid tone
- Produces mild relaxation without significant psychoactive effects

### High Doses (15mg+ THC)
- Can trigger panic attacks, paranoia, and increased anxiety
- Overstimulation of CB1 in the amygdala
- The "bad trip" phenomenon in susceptible individuals

### CBD (any dose)
- Consistently anxiolytic across dose ranges
- Does not produce the biphasic response that THC does
- Counteracts THC-induced anxiety when co-administered

## Evidence by Condition

### Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD)
- Evidence level: Moderate
- CBD-dominant products preferred (15-25mg CBD, minimal THC)
- Best used as daily maintenance rather than acute relief
- Comparable efficacy to some pharmaceuticals in early trials

### Social Anxiety Disorder
- Evidence level: Moderate to strong for CBD
- CBD reduces performance anxiety in simulated public speaking tests
- Optimal dose: 300mg CBD before anxiety-provoking event
- THC generally NOT recommended (can worsen social evaluation anxiety)

### PTSD
- Evidence level: Promising, growing
- Nightmares: THC before sleep reduces nightmare frequency
- Hyperarousal: CBD reduces physiological stress responses
- Emerging research on MDMA + cannabis combinations (not standard)

### OCD
- Evidence level: Limited
- High-CBD strains may reduce compulsive behaviors
- Anecdotal evidence stronger than clinical trials

## Recommended Approach

1. **Start with CBD-only products** — no risk of anxiety increase
2. **If THC is needed**, use very low doses (1-2.5mg) with CBD in 1:1 or higher CBD ratio
3. **Avoid high-THC strains** if anxiety is the primary condition
4. **Strain selection**: High-linalool, high-caryophyllene strains (e.g., ACDC, Harlequin, Cannatonic)
5. **Terpenes matter**: Linalool (anxiolytic), limonene (mood elevation), myrcene (sedation)

## Contraindications
- Personal or family history of psychosis
- Current use of benzodiazepines (additive CNS depression)
- Bipolar disorder (THC may trigger manic episodes)
