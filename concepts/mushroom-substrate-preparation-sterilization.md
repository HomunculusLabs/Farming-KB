## Common Mistakes

Overpacking the pressure vessel restricts steam circulation and creates cold
spots where substrate is not fully sterilized. Bags should not be stacked
tightly against each other or against the vessel walls.

Insufficient hydration before sterilization leads to dry substrate that
colonizes poorly. Substrate should be hydrated 12 to 24 hours before loading to
allow even water distribution.

Skipping the cool-down phase and inoculating while substrate is still hot will
kill or severely stress the spawn. Substrate must cool to below 90 degrees
Fahrenheit (32 degrees Celsius) before inoculation.

## Bulk Substrate vs. Spawn Substrate

Spawn substrate (grain) is sterilized because grain is extremely nutritious and
contamination-prone. Bulk substrate (straw, sawdust) can often be pasteurized
because its lower nutrient density makes it less attractive to competitors. The
distinction matters because pasteurization is simpler and cheaper than
sterilization.

## Scaling Up

Small-scale growers use pressure canners holding 4 to 10 substrate bags.
Commercial operations use retort autoclaves processing hundreds of bags per
cycle. Regardless of scale, the principles remain identical: clean materials,
proper hydration, adequate sterilization time and temperature, and sterile
handling post-process.
