# Degradation of Plant Cell Wall Polymers by Fungi

## Overview

Processes of natural bioremediation of lignocellulose involve a range of
organisms, but predominantly fungi. The annual terrestrial production of
lignocellulose-rich plant cell wall material is approximately 100
gigatonnes, of which lignin alone accounts for 20 gigatonnes. The
Basidiomycota and Ascomycota are responsible for decomposition of a high
proportion of this material. Understanding lignocellulose degradation
mechanisms is important for modifying and enhancing the degradation of
industrial pollutants such as chlorophenols, nitrophenols, and polyaromatic
hydrocarbons by these fungi.

## Primary Cell Wall Structure

Every plant cell is surrounded by a primary wall that undergoes plastic
extension as the cell grows. The primary wall is composed of cellulose
fibrils, hemicellulose, and protein with large amounts of pectin forming a
viscous matrix that cements the wall together. An approximate dry weight
ratio is 30% cellulose, 25% hemicellulose, 35% pectin, and 10% protein.
Cellulose is formed by polymerization of D-glucose molecules linked in the
beta-1,4 position, resulting in flat, linear chains. Hydrogen bonding
between chains leads to the formation of microfibrils up to 3 nm in
diameter.

Hemicelluloses are mixed polymers of different neutral and acidic
polysaccharides that adhere to cellulose microfibrils by hydrogen bonding.
Pectic polysaccharides are covalently bound to hemicelluloses. The protein
components include hydroxyproline-rich glycoproteins (extensins) involved
in cell wall architecture and plant disease resistance.

## Secondary Cell Wall Structure

As some cell types mature, a secondary cell wall is deposited between the
primary wall and the plasmalemma. Wood contains the most mature cell walls,
with several layers (S1, S2, S3) in the secondary wall. These are composed
of approximately 35% cellulose, 25% hemicellulose including pectin, and
up to 35% lignin depending on the plant species. Lignin gives rigidity to
the wall, while the orientation and crystallinity of cellulose
microfibrils determine strength.

The dense secondary wall structure makes cells impenetrable to
microorganisms without prior degradation of wall polymers. Permeability
studies indicate that molecules above 2000 Da are unable to penetrate
wood cell walls. In wheat cell walls, pores with radii of 1.5-3 nm
predominate, below the size that would allow free penetration by
degrading enzymes.

## Lignin Chemistry

Lignin is a three-dimensional aromatic polymer composed of up to three
monomeric units of cinnamyl alcohols: coumaryl alcohol (mainly in
grasses), coniferyl alcohol (major unit in gymnosperm wood), and sinapyl
alcohol (predominant in angiosperm wood). Polymerization occurs by free
radical reaction catalyzed by peroxidase, resulting in a variety of bonds.
The predominant bond linkage is the beta-O-4 bond between the aromatic
ring and the beta-carbon of the side chain.

The high proportion of ether bonds in lignin gives it unique structural
properties as a strong, resistant polymer. Lignin is not a symmetrical
well-ordered polymer with a single repeating bond, making it difficult for
any single enzyme to depolymerize. Hemicelluloses in the secondary wood
wall vary between plant species, with xylan the predominant polymer.
Hardwood xylans are primarily glucuroxylan type, while softwood xylans
are glucuronoarabinoxylans.

## Cellulase Systems

Cellulase is a complex of enzyme activities including exo-
(cellobiohydrolases) and endocellulases, with beta-glucosidases, acting
in synergy to depolymerize cellulose fibrils, releasing glucose and
cellobiose. The most prolific sources of cellulases are Trichoderma spp.
When enzymes from wood-rotting basidiomycetes have been screened, their
composition has closely resembled those of [[trichoderma-reesei]], with five
endoglucanases, one exoglucanase, and two beta-1,4-glucosidases.

Feedback control of exo- and endocellulase production is exerted by
glucose and sucrose with catabolite repression, but induction occurs with
cellobiose or cellulose. Cellobiohydrolases are composed of a cellulose-
binding domain linked to a catalytic domain through a proline and OH-amino
acid linker region. Genes for exo- and endocellulases have been isolated
and characterized from multiple fungal species.

## Hemicellulase Systems

Enzymes for hydrolysis of hemicellulose have been identified in many
wood-rotting fungi. Hardwoods contain xylan, and fungal species colonizing
them produce xylanases. Other hemicelluloses are hydrolyzed by mannanases,
galactosidases, and glucosidases. These enzymes have very similar
characteristics to the cellulase complex in that different enzymes attack
exo- and endo-hemicellulose positions. The coordinated action of
cellulases and hemicellulases is essential for complete degradation of
the polysaccharide components of plant cell walls.

## Brown Rot vs White Rot Mechanisms

A fundamental difference exists in the mechanism of cellulose hydrolysis
between brown rot and white rot fungi. Brown rots produce complete
breakage of amorphous cellulose fibrils while white rots cause progressive
decay from fibril surfaces. The mechanism that brown rots use to access
cellulose in the wood cell wall involves generation of hydroxyl radicals
from the reaction of H2O2 with Fe2+ in the Fenton reaction. Brown rot
basidiomycetes may have the most efficient mechanisms for obtaining
glucose from lignocellulose as they extract glucose from cellulose without
expending energy on lignin degradation.

## Ecological Considerations

Most studies on lignocellulose-degrading fungi have focused on a few model
organisms, primarily from northern temperate forests. However, the
biodiversity of decomposer fungi is much higher in tropical ecosystems.
The tropical forest biome contains 400-450 gigatonnes of plant biomass
compared with 120-150 gigatonnes for temperate and boreal forest biomes.
It is estimated that there are three times more taxa of higher fungi in
tropical ecosystems than in other forest ecosystems. Fungi in little-
explored tropical forests may yet prove to have better bioremediation
capabilities than the temperate organisms currently studied.
