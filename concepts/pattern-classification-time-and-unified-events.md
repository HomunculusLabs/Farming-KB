# Pattern Classification, Time, and Unified Events

## Overview

In [[permaculture-design]] theory, all phenomena — from seeds to stars, from ideas to ecosystems — can be classified within a unified framework based on their nature, stage of development, dimension, and location in space. This classification system provides designers with a language for describing and comparing fundamentally different events using common categories. Coupled with an understanding of time as an enfolded dimension rather than a linear progression, this framework supports holistic design thinking that recognizes the interconnectedness of all phenomena.

## Classification of Events

All events, regardless of scale or type, can be classified along four axes:

### A. Nature (What kind of event?)

| Category | Description | Examples |
|----------|-------------|----------|
| **A1** | Explosive, disintegration, erosion, impact | Bomb detonation, landslide, erosion |
| **A2** | Growth, integration, construction, translation | Tree growing, building construction, seedling |
| **A3** | Conceptual, idea, creative thought, insight | Scientific theory, design concept, revelation |

### B. Stage (Where in its lifecycle?)

| Category | Description | Examples |
|----------|-------------|----------|
| **B1** | Potential only | Ungerminated seed, unexplored idea, unexploded bomb |
| **B2** | In process of evolution | Growing sapling, developing theory, flowing river |
| **B3** | Completed (growth ceased), articulated | Mature tree, published theory, stabilized landform |
| **B4** | Decaying, disintegrative | Rotting log, obsolete theory, eroding cliff |

### C. Dimension (How many dimensions does it occupy?)

| Category | Description | Examples |
|----------|-------------|----------|
| **C1** | One-dimensional (linear) | Curves, streams, paths, timelines |
| **C2** | Two-dimensional (surface) | Tessellations, dendrites, patterns on planes |
| **C3** | Three-dimensional (solid) | Trees, rocks, organisms |
| **C4** | Four-dimensional (moving solid + time) | Falling objects, flowing water, living organisms |
| **C5** | More dimensions (conceptual) | Models of particles, force fields, energy states |

### D. Location (Where and how is it situated?)

| Category | Description | Examples |
|----------|-------------|----------|
| **D1** | Generating across equi-potential surfaces | Storms at sea, wave propagation |
| **D2** | Within media | Weather frontal systems, currents within water |
| **D3** | Through surfaces at ~90° | Trees growing from ground, springs emerging |
| **D4** | Englobements (enveloping) | Some explosions, organisms expanding into space |
| **D5** | Out of normal space-time dimensions | An idea, a concept, an abstract principle |

### Using the Classification System

By combining these categories, any event can be given a compact annotation:

- **A sapling**: A2 (growth), B2 (evolving), C3 (3D solid), D3 (through surface)
- **A falling bomb**: A1 (explosive), B1 (potential), C4 (4D moving), D2 (within media)
- **A seed in soil**: A2 (growth), B1 (potential), C3 (3D), D3 (through surface)
- **A design idea**: A3 (conceptual), B1 (potential), C5 (multi-dimensional), D5 (abstract)

This framework allows designers to rapidly categorize and compare elements in a design, identifying similarities and differences across seemingly unrelated phenomena.

## Time and Relativity in the Pattern Model

### The Present as Origin

The conventional view of time places the past behind us and the future ahead — a linear progression. The permaculture pattern model offers a fundamentally different perspective: **the present is the origin of both past and future**.

Consider a tree: the seed is the origin of the tree, but we can broaden our view to see the tree as the "current time-focus of its own genealogy." Before it lie its ancestors; after it, its progeny. The tree exists on the plane between past and future, and — like the seed — determines by its expression the forms of both, while being in turn determined by them.

### The Enfolded Nature of Time

This view parallels the **Buddhist concept of implicate order** — all time is enfolded in the present, and current events are part of a total sequence, all of which are present in the current manifestation. The tree's growth rings encapsulate its history, just as universal time encapsulates the tree within evolutionary history.

Applied to human experience: we stand in the plane of the present. We are the sum of all our ancestors and the origin of all our descendants. We exist as an ever-changing origin on the boundary of past and future. Simultaneously, we are spinning with the Earth, spiraling with the galaxy, and expanding or contracting with the universe — all with characteristic pulse rates.

### Design Implications of Time Understanding

- **Transport across time-fronts**: Designers can "move sideways in the waves of time" — transporting seeds between continents, permitting natural or induced hybridization to weave alternative futures
- **Humanity as translator**: Mankind is described as "an active translator of life, and, of course, of death"
- **Present action determines past and future**: "How we behave now may determine not only the future, but the past"
- **Personal responsibility**: The individual is "really where it's at" — the point where all important action occurs

### The Genealogical Pattern

In all core models, including genealogy, the point where important action occurs is through the point of origin, always in the present. All rivers, erosion cells, and glaciers originated at the central stem of their courses and built their patterns both ways along the kinetic gradient of their flow. This symmetry — building backward into the past and forward into the future from the present — is a fundamental property of pattern in time.

## The World as a Tessellation of Events

### Unity of Pattern

All observed phenomena — volcanic craters, trees, nesting birds, wind spirals, day-night cycles, life-death sequences — are described as "a unity of patterns long repeated and based on one master pattern, each one preparing for new evolutions and dissolutions." The number and complexity of such cycles are what create life opportunities, and life is identified as "the only integrative force in this part of the universe."

### Bohm's Implicate Order

David Bohm's concept (1980) urges seeing all things as "projections of a single totality." The word "implicate" comes from the Latin "enfolded." When we separate individuals, effects, or disciplines from this enfolded order, we must recognize that we have only part of an unknowable totality, not truth itself. "There are no opposites, just phases of the one phenomena."

### Converging Perspectives

Several thinkers contribute converging perspectives on the unity of phenomena:

- **Lovelock (1979)**: The [[gaia-hypothesis]] — Earth behaves as a self-regulating, self-generated construct akin to a single organism
- **Birch and Cobb (1984)**: Deny the validity of separate organisms; all exist in a field of events as expressions of one life force
- **Capra (1976)**: Theoretical physics contributes to unified worldviews of interrelated events
- **Fox**: "We must view the cosmos as an infinite complex of interrelated events"

These perspectives, while in conflict with current political, educational, and economic ethics, contribute to "an increasing effort to unify and cooperate in a common ethic of earth-care, without which we have no meaning to the universe."

## Design Synthesis

For the permaculture designer, pattern understanding provides:

1. **A classification tool** for analyzing and comparing diverse phenomena
2. **A temporal framework** that places the designer as the active origin point
3. **A philosophical foundation** of interconnectedness supporting earth-care ethics
4. **A recognition that patterns are the template** into which all design elements fit — "The pattern is design, and design is the subject of permaculture"

Understanding that we are "the universe attempting to define its processes" — or as a Kalahari bushman would say, "the dreams of a dreamer" — places design work within the largest possible context while grounding it in the practical reality of the present moment.
