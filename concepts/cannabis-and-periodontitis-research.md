# Cannabis and Periodontitis Research

**Source:** [[blesching-cannabis-health-index-autoimmune-conditions]] (2017)

## Condition Overview

Periodontitis is inflammation of tissues supporting the teeth, caused by oral microbes and overzealous immune response. Leads to tissue reduction and alveolar bone loss through resorption (bone cells leak substances and weaken). May be implicated in heart attacks, strokes, and atherosclerosis.

**Symptoms:** painful swollen gums, tissue reduction, deep pockets around teeth, bad breath (halitosis), loose teeth.

**Risk factors:** financial difficulties, grief, caregiving for spouse with dementia, depressed mood, chronic stress. Physiological: elevated pro-inflammatory cytokines, reduced salivary flow.

## Cannabis Research Findings

**First known study** examining CBD effects on resorption-produced periodontitis (Brazil, 2009):

- CBD-treated rats showed **reduction of alveolar bone loss**

- "CBD may be useful to control bone resorption during progression of experimental periodontitis"

## Pharmacological Mechanisms

The endocannabinoid system plays a significant role in bone metabolism, with both CB1 and CB2 receptors expressed on osteoblasts (bone-forming cells), osteoclasts (bone-resorbing cells), and osteocytes (mature bone cells embedded in the bone matrix).

- **CB2 receptor-mediated bone protection:** CB2 receptor activation on osteoclasts inhibits bone resorption -- the process by which minerals are released from bone into the bloodstream. In periodontitis, excessive osteoclast activity causes alveolar bone loss, leading to tooth mobility and loss. CBD's preference for CB2 receptors makes it a targeted agent for inhibiting this destructive process.

- **Osteoclast inhibition:** CBD suppresses osteoclast differentiation and activity through CB2-dependent and independent pathways. This directly addresses the primary mechanism of bone loss in periodontitis.

- **Anti-inflammatory effects on periodontal tissue:** Periodontitis is driven by chronic inflammation of the gingival tissues in response to bacterial biofilm (plaque). CBD modulates the immune response by suppressing pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1beta, TNF-alpha, IL-6) while promoting anti-inflammatory mediators (IL-10).

- **Osteoblast stimulation:** Some evidence suggests that CB2 receptor activation may also promote osteoblast activity and new bone formation, potentially helping to restore bone that has been lost to periodontal disease.

- **Periodontal ligament effects:** The periodontal ligament contains [[endogenous-opioids-pain-modulation-endocannabinoid-interaction]]:** Periodontitis causes significant oral pain. CB1 and CB2 receptors in the trigeminal nerve pathways modulate dental pain signaling, providing analgesic benefits beyond the disease-modifying effects.

## Clinical Evidence

The clinical evidence for cannabis in periodontitis is very limited but mechanistically promising, with a CHI value of 2.

- **Alveolar bone loss reduction (2009, Uberaba, Brazil):** The University of Uberaba study was the first to examine CBD's effects on experimentally induced periodontitis. Rats treated with CBD showed significant reduction in alveolar bone resorption compared to untreated controls, establishing proof-of-concept for cannabinoid-based periodontal therapy.

- **CB2 [[fukuoka-pine-rot-root-cause]] by reducing the stress-induced inflammatory cascade that exacerbates periodontal disease.

- **Limitations:** Only one animal study exists. Human [[blesching-cannabis-strain-selection-receptor-targeting]]:** CBD-rich indica strains are most consistent with the evidence base. Whole-plant extracts containing myrcene (anti-inflammatory terpene) and beta-caryophyllene (CB2 agonist terpene) may provide enhanced bone-protective effects.

- **THC consideration:** THC may provide additional anti-inflammatory benefits through CB1 receptor activation in periodontal tissues, but the primary evidence supports CBD. A low-THC, high-CBD ratio (1:5 to 1:10) is recommended.
