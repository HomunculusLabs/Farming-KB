# Mycorrhizosphere Bacteria and Mycorrhiza Helper Bacteria

The mycorrhizosphere is the soil volume influenced by mycorrhizal fungal
hyphae, analogous to the rhizosphere around plant roots. Because
mycorrhizal hyphae release substantial amounts of plant-derived carbon
into the soil, they create a unique microbial habitat enriched with
diverse [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungal-diversity]].

## The Mycorrhizosphere Concept

Roots release 10 to 20 percent of total net fixed carbon as low
molecular weight compounds, forming the basis for the rhizosphere
[[fukuoka-textdoc-biological-control-fallacy-pheromone-critique]], or showing direct effects
on soil quality. The mycorrhizosphere thus functions as an extended
symbiotic interface where plant, fungus, and bacteria interact.

## Types of Mycorrhizosphere Bacteria

Mycorrhizosphere bacteria occupy several ecological niches. Intrahyphal
bacteria have been found within ectomycorrhizal hyphae, and intra-spore
bacteria within arbuscular mycorrhizal fungal spores. Additional
bacterial species colonize the surfaces of fungal hyphae and mycorrhizal
roots. These diverse bacterial communities include both Gram-negative
and Gram-positive species, with the latter including important genera
such as Streptomyces and other actinomycetes.

## Mycorrhiza Helper Bacteria (MHB)

Mycorrhiza helper bacteria are a functional group of mycorrhizosphere
bacteria that promote mycorrhiza formation. The concept was formalized
by Garbaye in 1994. A strong correlation exists between the induction
of fungal growth in dual laboratory cultures and the mycorrhization
helper effect, making dual culture assays a common screening method.
MHB include diverse Gram-negative species such as Pseudomonas
fluorescens and Gram-positive species including Streptomyces isolates.

## Streptomyces AcH 505: A Model Mycorrhiza Helper Bacterium

Streptomyces sp. AcH 505, isolated from the rhizosphere of a Norway
spruce (Picea abies) stand, has been extensively characterized as a
model MHB. This actinomycete significantly promotes mycelial growth and
mycorrhization rate of the ectomycorrhizal fungus [[allegro-amanita-muscaria-properties-and-pharmacology]] while
simultaneously suppressing the mycelial extension of plant pathogens
Armillariella obscura and Heterobasidion annosum. This dual activity,
promoting beneficial symbiosis while suppressing pathogens, illustrates
an important application potential for [[mycorrhization-helper-bacteria]] in
forestry and agriculture.

## Factors Influencing Bacterium-Fungus Interactions

The outcome of bacterium-fungus interactions depends on multiple factors.
Media composition and the distance between and size of microbial inocula
are critically important. Microorganisms commonly activate secondary
metabolism during stress or competition, so interaction-relevant
substances may be produced primarily during direct contact. The
interaction between Streptomyces AcH 505 and Amanita muscaria changes
from mutualistic to antagonistic when the medium is changed from MMN to
ISP2, because AcH 505 grows better on ISP2 and produces higher
concentrations of antifungal metabolites.
