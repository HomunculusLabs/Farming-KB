## Overview

[[fukuoka-textdoc-non-causality-rice-blast-disease]], one of the most devastating diseases of cultivated rice worldwide. Rice is the staple diet of approximately half the global human population, and every year between 11% and 30% of the rice harvest is destroyed by this pathogen. The functional genomics of M. grisea, as presented by Jenkinson, Wilson, Cartwright, Soanes, Kershaw, and Talbot in "Fungi in the Environment," has provided unprecedented insights into the molecular mechanisms by which this fungus infects its host, offering potential targets for disease [[fungi-in-the-environment-rice-blast-magnaporthe-grisea]] is a heterothallic, phytopathogenic ascomycete capable of infecting over 50 species of grass, though rice is the most economically important host. Key facts about this pathogen:

- **Disease severity:** Despite modern advances including fungicides and resistant rice cultivars, annual crop losses range from 11% to 30% globally.
- **Epidemic potential:** A serious blast epidemic in Bhutan in 1995 destroyed 1,090 tonnes of rice, with some farmers experiencing 100% crop losses.
- **Biosecurity concern:** The American CDC has classified rice blast disease as a significant biological weapon that could be deployed in agricultural bioterrorism.
- **Disease manifestations:** Blast causes distinct pathologies affecting stems, leaves, and panicles. Stem node infections can cause rot before seed maturation. Leaf infections reduce photosynthetic capacity and can kill seedlings.
- **Symptom appearance:** Brown necrotic lesions on leaves, taking 4-5 days to develop after initial infection.
