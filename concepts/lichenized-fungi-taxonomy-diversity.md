# Lichenized Fungi Taxonomy and Diversity Source: Biodiversity of Fungi (Hyde et al., Ch. 9) Lichenized fungi represent one of the most remarkable symbiotic associations in nature. Lichenization has evolved independently several times during fungal history. Of the 46 orders of Ascomycota, 14 include lichen-forming representatives, and five of those comprise lichenized species exclusively. ## Growth Forms Three of five common growth forms (Figure 9.1 from Hyde et al.): - **Crustose**: Several layers, medullary fungal hyphae attached directly to substratum - **Foliose**: Distinct upper and lower cortex layers, attached by rhizines (rootlike fungal hyphae) - **Fruticose**: Stalked, tufted, or pendulous, uniform outer layer (e.g., Ramalina menziesii on Quercus garryana) ## Orders of Lichen-forming Ascomycetes (Table 9.1) | Order | Families | Species | Notes | |-------|----------|---------|-------| | **Lecanorales** | 34 of 40 | ~7,150 | Majority of lichen-forming fungi; includes Acarosporaceae, Cladoniaceae, Lecanoraceae, Parmeliaceae, Physciaceae, Ramalinaceae, Stereocaulaceae; mainly with Chlorococcales and Pleurastrales (e.g., Trebouxia) | | **Ostropales** (incl. Graphidales) | 6 of 7 | ~1,600 | Mainly with Trentepohlia species | | **Arthoniales** (incl. Opegraphales) | 4 | ~1,000 | Mostly with Trentepohlia species | | **Verrucariales** | 1 of 2 | ~700 | Verrucariaceae; mainly with green algae | | **Teloschistales** | 3 | ~570 | Almost all with green algae, commonly Trebouxia | | **Peltigerales** | 4 | ~510 | With green algae or cyanobacteria; sometimes multiple photobionts in same thallus | | **Pertusariales** | 1 | ~300 | All lichenized with green algae | | **Pyrenulales** | 4 of 5 | ~500 | Mainly with Trentepohlia | | **Lichinales** | 3 | ~280 | All lichen-forming with cyanobacteria | | **Trichotheliales** | 1 | ~240 | With Phycopeltis or trentepohlioid green algae | | **Gyalectales** | 1 | ~100 | Especially with Trentepohlia | | **Agyriales** | 3 | ~100 | Mostly lichenized, some saprobic on wood | | **Dothideales sensu lato** | 3 | ~120 | Status of many Arthopyreniaceae members unclear | ### Basidiomycete Lichens A few Basidiomycota form lichens, particularly in Agaricales (Omphalina), Cantharellales (Multiclavula), and Stereales (Dictyonema). ### Families of Uncertain Position 15 families including Aphanopsidaceae, Baeomycetaceae, Gomphillaceae, Strigulaceae, Thelenellaceae, Thelocarpaceae, Umbilicariaceae. ## Taxonomic Principles Scientific names refer to the fungal partner (mycobiont); photobionts have independent names. Officially, the lichen association itself has no name under the International Code of Botanical Nomenclature. When more than one species of photobiont occurs with the same fungus, the same fungal name is used for all resulting lichen thalli even if they have different morphologies. Orders, families, and genera delimited primarily by characters of fruiting bodies. Molecular approaches bring significant modifications to classical morphological and chemical species concepts. Secondary chemistry used extensively for identification. ## Ecological Roles - Important winter foods for reindeer, caribou, deer in boreal forest and arctic tundra - Emergency food during hard winters for many large mammalian herbivores - Nest material for birds and mammals - Significant proportion of autotroph biomass in desert and tundra ecosystems - Fix significant nitrogen in Pacific Northwest conifer forests - Buffer heavy metals from atmospheric deposition - Industrial melanism in peppered moths: classic example of indirect lichen impacts on animals ## Major Collections - University of Uppsala (UPS): ~500,000 lichen specimens - Natural History Museum London: ~400,000 specimens - Smithsonian Institution: ~250,000 specimens (largest in USA) - About one-third of lichenized fungal species tested have been cultured successfully on first attempt ## See Also - lichen-diversity-estimates-and-geography - mycorrhizal fungi biodiversity - [[freshwater-fungi-diversity-and-sampling]]
