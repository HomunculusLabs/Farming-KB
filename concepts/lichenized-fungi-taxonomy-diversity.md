# Lichenized Fungi: Taxonomy and Diversity

Source: [[biodiversity-of-fungi-biodiversity-patterns-ecosystems]] (Hyde et al., Ch. 9)

Lichenized fungi represent one of the most remarkable symbiotic
associations in nature. Lichenization has evolved independently several
times during fungal history. Of the 46 orders of Ascomycota, 14 include
lichen-forming representatives, and five of those comprise lichenized
species exclusively.

## Growth Forms

Three of five common growth forms (Figure 9.1 from Hyde et al.):

- **Crustose**: Several layers, medullary fungal hyphae attached
  directly to substratum
- **Foliose**: Distinct upper and lower cortex layers, attached by
  rhizines (rootlike fungal hyphae)
- **Fruticose**: Stalked, tufted, or pendulous, uniform outer layer
  (e.g., *Ramalina menziesii* on *[[quercus-garryana]]*)

### Basidiomycete Lichens

A few Basidiomycota form lichens, particularly in Agaricales
(*Omphalina*), Cantharellales (*Multiclavula*), and Stereales
(*Dictyonema*).

### Families of Uncertain Position

15 families including Aphanopsidaceae, Baeomycetaceae,
Gomphillaceae, Strigulaceae, Thelenellaceae, Thelocarpaceae,
Umbilicariaceae.

## Taxonomic Principles

Scientific names refer to the fungal partner (mycobiont); photobionts
have independent names. Officially, the lichen association itself has no
name under the International Code of Botanical Nomenclature. When more
than one species of photobiont occurs with the same fungus, the same
fungal name is used for all resulting lichen thalli even if they have
different morphologies.

Orders, families, and genera delimited primarily by characters of
fruiting bodies. [[genetic-improvement-agaricus-bisporus-molecular-approaches]] bring significant modifications
to classical morphological and chemical species concepts. Secondary
chemistry used extensively for identification.

## Ecological Roles

- Important winter foods for reindeer, caribou, deer in boreal forest
  and arctic tundra
- Emergency food during hard winters for many large mammalian herbivores
- Nest material for birds and mammals
- Significant proportion of autotroph biomass in desert and tundra
  ecosystems
- Fix significant nitrogen in [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]] conifer forests
- Buffer heavy metals from atmospheric deposition
- Industrial melanism in peppered moths: classic example of indirect
  lichen impacts on animals
