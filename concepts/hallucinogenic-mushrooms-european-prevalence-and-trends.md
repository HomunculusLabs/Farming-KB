## Prevalence Data

### General Population (Ages 15-24)

Surveys across 12 EU Member States found that lifetime prevalence of hallucinogenic
mushroom use among 15-24 year olds ranged from less than 1% to 8%. The highest
rates were reported in the Netherlands, Czech Republic, UK, Germany, and Ireland.
The lowest prevalence was found in Lithuania, Hungary, and France (though France's
2000 general population survey likely underestimated current use).

A 2003 survey of 17-18 year old French residents found higher rates than the older
general population survey: 4.3% lifetime, 2.9% last year, and 1% last month
prevalence (Beck, Legleye, Spilka, 2004). The proportion of current users among
those who have ever used is lower for hallucinogenic mushrooms than for cannabis
and ecstasy, suggesting limited appeal for regular use.

### School Surveys (ESPAD, Ages 15-16)

The 2003 ESPAD school survey across 22 EU Member States, Norway, and candidate
countries found lifetime prevalence among 15-16 year olds ranging from 0% to 8%.
The Czech Republic, Netherlands, France, and Belgium had the highest estimates.
Three countries — Cyprus, Finland, and Romania — reported zero lifetime prevalence.

Notably, lifetime use of hallucinogenic mushrooms equaled or exceeded lifetime
ecstasy use in nine of the surveyed countries, indicating the significance of
mushrooms within the adolescent drug landscape.

### Clubbing Surveys

Non-probability surveys in dance music settings revealed substantially higher
prevalence than general or school populations. Estimates ranged from 9% of
clubbers in Budapest to 55% in France. Among current drug users in UK nightclub
samples, lifetime prevalence for mushroom use rose to 44%, compared to 11% among
16-19 year olds in the general population.

## Correlates of Use

### Polydrug Use Patterns

Analysis across 11 EU Member States demonstrated that school students aged 15-16
were much more likely to have used hallucinogenic mushrooms if they had used LSD,
ecstasy, amphetamines, or cocaine than if they had used only cannabis or legal
substances. Male students generally had higher prevalence rates than females,
following the common gender distribution for illegal drug use.

### Lifestyle Association

The EMCDDA identified a "return to nature" trend as a key driver, with interest
in natural hallucinogens facilitated by the rapid expansion of internet sales and
information. Norway reported an organic lifestyle music milieu characterized by
woodland parties, cannabis use, and mushroom use among economically disadvantaged
people aged 25-40. However, this milieu was considered unattractive by under-25s,
limiting its diffusion potential.

## Market and Availability

### Retail Channels

Multiple retail channels emerged across Europe:

- **Smartshops** (Netherlands) — specialized retail outlets selling psychoactive
  herbal substances, vitamins, and novel synthetic drugs not yet controlled
- **Internet shops** — online vendors selling fresh and dried mushrooms, spore
  prints, spawn bags, and grow kits
- **Market stalls** — informal retail at markets and festivals

Most recreationally used mushrooms in Europe were cultivated rather than wild-
harvested, despite the [[guzman-allen-gartz-psilocybe-semilanceata-temperate-cosmopolitan-distribution]]** (liberty caps) — the most common wild species, found
