# Coco Coir for Cannabis

Coconut coir (also called coco peat or coconut fiber) is an excellent [[query-what-is-light-deprivation-cannabis-growing-and-how-does-it-work]] Mediums for [[query-how-does-no-till-cannabis-cultivation-work]]. It is a natural, renewable product derived from the fibrous husk of coconuts. Coco coir is versatile enough to be used in passive [[cervantes-passive-wick-hydroponic-systems]], and as a soil amendment in outdoor gardens.

## What is Coco Coir

Coconut coir is the processed fibrous material from between the outer husk and the inner shell of coconuts. Washed, pressed blocks or bricks of coir are virtually inert. Bricks typically weigh about 1.3 to 2.2 pounds (0.6 to 1 kg) when dry. The pH of quality coconut coir ranges between 5.5 and 6.8, making it naturally suitable for cannabis cultivation.

## Source and Quality

Some of the best coconut coir comes from the interior of the Philippine Islands, where the environment is not packed with coastal salts. Quality coconut coir should have a sodium content of less than 50 ppm. Coir sourced from coastal areas may contain elevated salt levels that can harm plants. Always check the source and quality specifications before purchasing.

## Preparing Coco Coir

Dry bricks of coconut coir must be expanded before use:
1. **Hand-flaking**: Flake dry bricks apart by hand to break up compressed fibers.
2. **Soaking method**: Soak bricks in a bucket of water for 15 minutes to expand and wet them fully.
3. **Expansion ratio**: One brick will expand to about nine times its original size when hydrated.

Rinse expanded coir with fresh water before use to remove any residual salts or dust.

## Properties and Characteristics

- **Porosity**: Coco coir has approximately 90% porosity, providing excellent air-to-water ratio.
- **Air space**: Approximately 10% air space within the fiber structure.
- **Water retention**: High water-holding capacity while still maintaining good aeration.
- **pH**: Naturally between 5.5 and 6.8, well within the ideal range for cannabis.
- **Inert**: Does not react with nutrient solutions or alter their chemical composition.
- **CEC**: Has some cation-exchange capacity, though lower than peat moss.

## Coco Coir in Different Systems

### Hydroponic Systems

Coco coir is widely used in [[coleman-drip-irrigation-and-water-management]], and top-feed hydroponic systems. In drip irrigation, coco coir is typically irrigated four or five times daily. It works well in both recirculating and run-to-waste configurations.

### Slab Systems

Coco is compressed into slabs and packaged in plastic. The plastic wrapper is pierced or cut to plant into the slab. Slabs are placed on drainage tables or in individual trays with nutrient solution delivered via spaghetti tubes from the top.

### Wick Systems

## Coco Coir in Soil Mixes

Outdoor soil mixes that incorporate garden soil, compost, manure, coco peat, and rock powders grow some of the best plants. Coco coir-based soil mix recipes include:

- 0.5 compost + 0.5 soilless mix
- 0.3 compost + 0.3 soilless mix + 0.3 coco coir
- 0.5 compost + 0.5 coco coir
- 0.3 compost + 0.3 soilless mix + 0.16 worm castings + 0.16 [[perlite-cannabis]]

## Coco Coir Containers

Woven coco fiber pots are available from suppliers like General Hydroponics. These breathable containers allow air pruning of roots and provide excellent drainage. Grow bags are another economical, lightweight option for coco coir cultivation.

## Advantages of Coco Coir

- **Renewable and sustainable**: A byproduct of coconut processing.
- **Excellent water retention**: Holds moisture well while maintaining aeration.
- **Near-ideal pH**: Naturally within the optimal range for cannabis.
- **Disease resistant**: Naturally resistant to many soil-borne pathogens.
- **Reusable**: Can be reused for multiple grows, though sterilization between uses is recommended.
- **Versatile**: Works in hydroponic, soilless, and soil-amended systems.

## Disadvantages

- **Can stay too wet**: May require extra ventilation and air circulation.
- **Variable quality**: Low-quality coir may contain high sodium or be improperly processed.
- **No buffering**: Like most hydroponic media, coco has limited nutrient buffering capacity.
- **Compaction**: Can compact over time, reducing aeration.
