## The Black Hole Effect

McKenna describes a "black hole effect" surrounding DMT: the more precisely one articulates the experience, the less others are able to understand it. He draws an explicit analogy to astrophysical black holes, where no signal can escape. ## Shamanism and the Phenomenological Tradition

McKenna reflects extensively on the nature of shamanism as practiced in indigenous societies. He distinguishes it sharply from the popular image of shamans as "embedded in a tradition," arguing that in practice shamans are "the true phenomenologists of this world" who "know plant chemistry" yet call these energy fields "spirits." He insists that "spirit" as used by shamans is a technical gloss for a very complicated concept, comparable to how a quantum physicist might use the term "charm."

McKenna describes the signature qualities that identify potential shamans in traditional societies: oddness or uniqueness, epilepsy, survival of unusual ordeals such as lightning strikes, recovery from near-fatal illnesses after extended indeterminate states, and hypersensitivity to trance states. The distinguishing characteristic he observed from his world travels was "an extraordinary centeredness" combined with intellectual sophistication.
