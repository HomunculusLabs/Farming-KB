# Cannabis for Addiction Recovery

Cannabis occupies a unique and sometimes controversial position in the context of addiction medicine. The [[bay-11-medical-sativa-granddaddy-purp-pain-relief-oner]]
- **Withdrawal management**: Cannabinoids may ease opioid withdrawal symptoms including anxiety, insomnia, muscle aches, and gastrointestinal distress
- **Craving reduction**: CBD has shown potential to reduce drug-seeking behavior and cravings in pre-clinical studies
- **Reward pathway modulation**: The endocannabinoid system interacts with the dopaminergic reward system involved in addiction

### Alcohol Dependence

Cannabis has been explored as a substitute for alcohol in individuals seeking to reduce alcohol consumption:
- CBD may reduce alcohol-related cravings and anxiety
- Cannabis may provide relaxation and stress relief without the organ toxicity of alcohol
- Some individuals report successfully transitioning from alcohol to cannabis use
- However, substituting one substance for another requires careful clinical oversight

### Benzodiazepine Tapering

Patients attempting to discontinue benzodiazepines may benefit from cannabis:
- Anxiolytic properties of CBD may help manage the anxiety that accompanies benzodiazepine withdrawal
- Sleep-promoting effects of THC may address insomnia during tapering
- The risk of life-threatening benzodiazepine withdrawal (seizures) makes medical supervision essential regardless of cannabis use

## CBD for Addiction Treatment

CBD has emerged as a particularly promising cannabinoid for addiction treatment due to its non-psychoactive nature:

- **Anti-anxiety**: Reduces anxiety that triggers substance use
- **Anti-craving**: May reduce cravings for multiple substances
- **Neuroprotective**: May help repair neural damage from substance abuse
- **[[cannabis-chronic-pain-management]] patients seeking to reduce opioid use
- Individuals with alcohol use disorder seeking harm reduction
- Patients with anxiety-related substance use
- Those with insomnia related to substance withdrawal
- PTSD patients using substances to self-medicate

### When Caution Is Needed
- Individuals with personal or family history of substance use disorder
- Adolescent patients (cannabis may affect brain development)
- Patients currently using multiple substances
- Those with psychotic disorders (THC may exacerbate symptoms)
- Patients in early recovery who have not developed stable coping skills
