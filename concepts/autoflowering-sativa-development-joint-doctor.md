# Auto-Flowering Sativa Development and the Joint Doctor

## The Auto-Flowering Revolution

The development of auto-flowering [[cannabis-microbiome-otu-abundance-vs-presence-cannabis-strains]] represents one of the most
significant advances in [[cannabis-breeding-basics]] history. Auto-flowering plants switch from
vegetative growth to flowering based on age rather than photoperiod, meaning they
do not require a change in light cycle to begin producing buds. This trait comes
from [[cannabis-ruderalis]], a subspecies that evolved in the short-summer regions of
Central and Eastern Europe and Russia.

## The Joint Doctor and Lowryder

Canada's the Joint Doctor is credited with creating one of the most influential
auto-flowering strains in cannabis history: Lowryder. According to Cannabis Sativa
Volume 3, Lowryder "comes up in conversation so regularly" that the author had
memorized the Joint Doctor's website URL. The impact of Lowryder on the worldwide
cannabis community is described as impossible to overstate.

The original Lowryder demonstrated that auto-flowering genetics could produce
meaningful quantities of quality cannabis in a fraction of the time required by
traditional photoperiod strains. This opened up cultivation possibilities for
growers in climates with short [[jeavons-climate-adaptation-growing-seasons]] and those without the space or
equipment for light-cycle management.

## Easy Ryder: A Case Study in Auto-Flowering Sativa Breeding

Cannabis Sativa Volume 3 documents Easy Ryder from High Bred Seeds (by the Joint
Doctor) as a prime example of how auto-flowering sativa breeding has advanced. Easy
Rider is a stabilized F1 cross of Automatic AK-47 and Lowryder #2, demonstrating
how breeders have combined auto-flowering genetics with established sativa-dominant
strains to create plants that offer both convenience and quality.

### Genetics and Development

Easy Ryder combines:
- **Auto AK-47** — An auto-flowering version of the legendary AK-47 strain
- **Lowryder #2** — A specifically selected phenotype of the second-generation
  Lowryder, chosen for its "phenomenal amount of [[cannabis-resin-production-and-quality-traits-clarke]] a fruity
  aroma"

The cross is described as "a stabilized F1 cross" that brings "the very best traits
of the two parent strains" into a "harmonious relationship." The selection of a
specific Lowryder #2 phenotype rather than using a generic Lowryder line shows the
precision involved in modern auto-flowering breeding.

### Growth Characteristics

Despite being slightly sativa-dominant, Easy Ryder reaches a maximum height of only
about 2 feet (60 cm). This compact stature makes it ideal for indoor growers with
limited vertical space—a common challenge when growing traditional sativas, which
can reach 6-14 feet.

Key [[mushroom-cultivator-shiitake-growing-parameters]] include:
- **Flowering time:** 56 days
- **Total life cycle:** Approximately 70 days from germination to harvest
- **Recommended light schedule:** 20 hours light / 4 hours darkness throughout
  the entire life cycle
- **Indoor/outdoor:** Performs well in both environments
- **Outdoor window:** Best time is May through August, allowing multiple harvests
  per season

### The 20/4 Light Schedule

The book notes that auto-flowering strains like Easy Ryder need "a change in light
cycle to flip into flower" but that "the more light you can throw at it, the more
bud you'll get in the end." The recommended 20/4 schedule means the plant receives
maximum light exposure throughout its short life, maximizing photosynthesis and
growth without the complications of managing a separate vegetative and flowering
light cycle.

### Phenotype Expression

The Auto AK-47 parent contributes "a very typically indica style of growing" with
a squat stature and extensive side branching. The Lowryder #2 phenotype contributes
exceptional resin production and fruity aromatics. The result is a plant that
defies simple classification—sativa-dominant genetics in a compact, indica-like
growth pattern with enhanced resin characteristics.

### Yields and Quality

Easy Ryder is described as producing "a huge yield per harvest" relative to its
small size. Growers who can manage multiple outdoor cycles between May and November
can achieve substantial total harvests. The finished product offers "a nice berry,
fruity taste" with a "wonderfully balanced high" described as having "enough buzz
and energy to make this super fun weed made easy."

## The Significance of Auto-Flowering Sativas

The development of auto-flowering sativa strains addresses several long-standing
challenges:

### Time Efficiency
Traditional sativas require 12-16 weeks of flowering alone, plus 4-8 weeks of
vegetative growth. Auto-flowering sativas like Easy Ryder complete their entire
life cycle in approximately 70 days—less time than many indicas spend just
flowering.

### Space Constraints
Sativa strains are notoriously tall and difficult to manage indoors. Auto-flowering
sativas remain compact regardless of light conditions, making them accessible to
growers who lack the ceiling height or vertical space for traditional sativas.

### Climate Limitations
Outdoor sativa cultivation is limited to regions with long growing seasons. Auto-
flowering strains can be grown successfully in much shorter seasons and at higher
latitudes, and can produce multiple harvests per year in favorable climates.

### Simplicity
Auto-flowering strains eliminate the need for light-cycle management, making them
ideal for novice growers who may struggle with the timing and complexity of
photoperiod-dependent cultivation.

## The Ruderalis Contribution

Cannabis ruderalis, the subspecies that provides the auto-flowering trait, was
long considered essentially worthless by cannabis breeders and consumers. The
plants were small, low-potency, and produced minimal resin. However, the auto-
flowering trait proved to be an extraordinarily valuable genetic contribution when
properly combined with the potency and resin production of sativa and indica
varieties through careful breeding and selection.

## Future Directions

The trajectory of auto-flowering sativa breeding suggests continued improvements in
both potency and yield. As breeders refine their understanding of ruderalis genetics
and develop more sophisticated breeding programs, the gap between auto-flowering and
photoperiod strains continues to narrow. The Joint Doctor's work with Easy Ryder
represents just one milestone in what has become a rapidly evolving field within
cannabis breeding.
