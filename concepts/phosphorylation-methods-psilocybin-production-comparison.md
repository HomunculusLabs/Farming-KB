## The Chemical Challenge

Psilocybin is 4-phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine, meaning the
phosphate ester is attached to the phenolic oxygen at the 4-position
of the indole ring. The molecule also contains a tertiary
dimethylamine on the shirota-glyoxalylamide-side-chain-construction-psilocin-synthesis. Both the phenol and the amine are
nucleophilic sites that can potentially react with phosphorylating
agents, creating a selectivity problem.

Additionally, psilocybin's high polarity makes it difficult to
isolate from reaction mixtures using standard techniques. The
phosphate group introduces both charge and hydrogen-bonding
capacity that complicate extraction and crystallization. These
properties are what make isolation from natural sources difficult
and what make chromatographic purification of synthetic product
challenging at scale.

## Method 1: Phosphite/Iodine/DMAP

The phosphoryl iodide method uses tribenzyl phosphite, iodine, and
DMAP (4-dimethylaminopyridine) as the phosphorylating system. This
method appeared in the literature as a potential route to
phosphorylated tryptamines. However, Shirota reports that "most of
the phosphorylation methods did not consume 1" (psilocin), meaning
the reaction either did not proceed or proceeded only partially
under the conditions tested.

The failure of this method likely reflects the relatively low
reactivity of the phosphite/iodine system toward phenolic
substrates in the presence of competing amine nucleophilicity.
The dimethylamino group may intercept the phosphorylating agent
before it reaches the phenol, or the conditions may simply be
too mild to activate the phenolic oxygen toward phosphorylation.

## Method 2: Pyrophosphate with n-Butyllithium

The method ultimately selected by Shirota uses tetrabenzyl-
pyrophosphate [(BnO)2PO]2O with n-butyllithium in THF at -78°C
to 0°C. Tetrabenzylpyrophosphate is prepared in "almost 100%
yield from zwitterionic-n-o-dibenzyl-phosphate-intermediate" using a literature procedure with
modifications. The reaction proceeds via deprotonation of the
phenolic OH by n-BuLi, generating a phenoxide that attacks the
pyrophosphate to form the O,O-dibenzyl phosphate derivative (7).

The pyrophosphate method was selected over alternatives for two
practical reasons: "easy handling and the reagent stability."
Tetrabenzylpyrophosphate is a stable crystalline solid that can
be prepared in advance and stored, unlike some phosphorylating
agents that require preparation immediately before use or are
sensitive to moisture and air.

## Method 3: Phosphoramidite (Literature Alternative)

While not explicitly tested by Shirota, the phosphoramidite method
described by Nichols and Frescas represents another approach. This
method uses a phosphoramidite reagent followed by oxidation to
introduce the phosphate group. Nichols reported a yield of 47% for
psilocybin using this route, but required anion-exchange resin
purification to isolate the product.
