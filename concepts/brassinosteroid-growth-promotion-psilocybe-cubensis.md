# Brassinosteroid Growth Promotion in Psilocybe Cubensis Brassinosteroids are a class of steroidal plant growth regulators that were first
recognized as a distinct group of phytohormones in the late 1970s. By 1990, approximately 30 members had been detected across a wide variety of higher
plants, including both Angiospermae and Gymnospermae, as well as in a green alga. Their strong growth-promoting activity involves complex [[emcdda-psilocybin-cardiovascular-risks-acute-physiological-effects]],
including increases in cell elongation and cell division. Promising results in [[chamovitz-photoperiodism-agricultural-applications]] to produce higher crop yields and
anti-stress effects had been reported, but no studies had examined the occurrence and activity of brassinosteroids on fungi until this pioneering work by Gartz,
Adam, and Vorbrodt.

## The Brassinosteroid Compound

22S,23S-homobrassinolide is a brassinosteroid with a specific stereochemistry at the 22 and 23 positions, both in the S configuration.
The compound was synthesized from stigmasterol, a plant-derived sterol, through established chemical routes documented by Adam and Marquardt in their 1986
Phytochemistry publication. This particular brassinosteroid was chosen for its documented potency in plant
growth promotion assays and its availability through synthetic production. The synthetic preparation from stigmasterol is noteworthy because it demonstrates
that the active compound does not need to be extracted from natural sources, making potential commercial application more feasible.
Stigmasterol is itself abundant in plant tissues, particularly in legumes and unrefined vegetable oils, providing an inexpensive [[shirota-4-hydroxyindole-acetyl-protection-psilocin-synthesis-starting-material]] for
brassinosteroid synthesis.

## Results: Mycelial Growth on Agar

On malt agar, the brassinosteroid-treated cultures exhibited dramatically accelerated mycelial growth, expanding two to three times faster than the control
series without steroid. This represents one of the most pronounced growth-promoting effects ever documented
for a phytohormone applied to a non-plant organism. The speed differential was readily observable and consistent across all ten
experimental runs, providing strong statistical confidence in the result. The fact that the growth enhancement was observed on a simple defined medium (malt
agar) suggests that the brassinosteroid is not merely improving [[ph-and-nutrient-availability-garden-soils]] or [[growing-gourmet-mushrooms-species-sequencing-substrate-utilization]] but is directly influencing the fungal
metabolic or regulatory machinery responsible for hyphal extension. This distinction is important because it points to a direct biological mechanism
rather than an indirect nutritional effect.

## Results: Fruiting on Dung/Grain Substrate

The effects on the dung and grain substrate were equally striking. The first flush of fruit bodies appeared remarkably earlier in the
brassinosteroid-treated cultures. In the control group, the first sporocarps were produced within 4 to 5 weeks.
With the addition of brassinosteroid, fruiting of the mycelia occurred in just 3 to 3.5 weeks, representing a 25 to 30% reduction in the time to first harvest.
For commercial [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]], where time to harvest directly impacts profitability, this acceleration would represent a significant economic advantage.
The time reduction is particularly valuable because it shortens the period during which cultures are vulnerable to contamination.
Faster colonization means less exposure to competing microorganisms, potentially reducing crop losses in commercial settings where contamination rates can be a
major factor limiting production efficiency.

## Enhanced Dry Mass

The steroid-treated cultures also afforded a remarkably higher dry mass compared to the control cultivations.
Treated cultures yielded 3.4 to 3.9 grams of dry material per 10 grams of substrate, while control cultures produced only 2.3 to 2.9 grams per 10 grams of substrate.
This represents approximately a 40 to 50% increase in dry mass yield, which is a commercially significant improvement [[grass-seed-substrate-for-mushroom-cultivation]].
The dry mass increase indicates that the brassinosteroid does not merely accelerate water uptake or create a visual impression of larger growth, but actually increases
the biosynthetic output of the fungal metabolism. More fungal tissue is being produced per unit of substrate consumed, suggesting
improved metabolic efficiency or enhanced access to substrate nutrients facilitated by the hormone treatment.

## Suppression of Incomplete Fruit Bodies

One of the most notable observations was that the formation of incomplete fruit bodies, which are normally present in [[emcdda-hallucinogenic-mushroom-cultivation-and-growkit-market]] represent wasted
biological effort, was almost completely suppressed by the brassinosteroid. This suggests that the phytohormone helps coordinate the developmental program more
effectively, ensuring that initiated fruit bodies complete their development to maturity rather than aborting partway through.
The suppression of abortive fruit body formation has both biological and practical significance.
Biologically, it implies that brassinosteroids may stabilize the developmental commitment of primordia, reducing the sensitivity of developing fruit bodies to
environmental fluctuations that might otherwise trigger abortion. Practically, it means that more of the biological resources invested in fruit body
initiation are carried through to harvest, improving overall cultivation efficiency.
