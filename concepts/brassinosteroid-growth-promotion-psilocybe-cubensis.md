## Brassinosteroid Growth Promotion in Psilocybe Cubensis Brassinosteroids are a class of steroidal plant growth regulators that were first
recognized as a distinct group of phytohormones in the late 1970s. By 1990, approximately 30 members had been detected across a wide variety of higher
plants, including both Angiospermae and Gymnospermae, as well as in a green alga. Their strong growth-promoting activity involves complex [[chamovitz-photoperiodism-agricultural-applications]] to produce higher crop yields and
anti-stress effects had been reported, but no studies had examined the occurrence and activity of brassinosteroids on fungi until this pioneering work by Gartz,
Adam, and Vorbrodt.

## The Brassinosteroid Compound

22S,23S-homobrassinolide is a brassinosteroid with a specific stereochemistry at the 22 and 23 positions, both in the S configuration.
The compound was synthesized from stigmasterol, a plant-derived sterol, through established chemical routes documented by Adam and Marquardt in their 1986
Phytochemistry publication. This particular brassinosteroid was chosen for its documented potency in plant
growth promotion assays and its availability through synthetic production. The synthetic preparation from stigmasterol is noteworthy because it demonstrates
that the active compound does not need to be extracted from natural sources, making potential commercial application more feasible.
Stigmasterol is itself abundant in plant tissues, particularly in legumes and unrefined vegetable oils, providing an inexpensive [[ph-and-nutrient-availability-garden-soils]] or [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]], where time to harvest directly impacts profitability, this acceleration would represent a significant economic advantage.
The time reduction is particularly valuable because it shortens the period during which cultures are vulnerable to contamination.
Faster colonization means less exposure to competing microorganisms, potentially reducing crop losses in commercial settings where contamination rates can be a
major factor limiting production efficiency.

## Enhanced Dry Mass

The steroid-treated cultures also afforded a remarkably higher dry mass compared to the control cultivations.
Treated cultures yielded 3.4 to 3.9 grams of dry material per 10 grams of substrate, while control cultures produced only 2.3 to 2.9 grams per 10 grams of substrate.
This represents approximately a 40 to 50% increase in dry mass yield, which is a commercially significant improvement [[emcdda-hallucinogenic-mushroom-cultivation-and-growkit-market]] represent wasted
biological effort, was almost completely suppressed by the brassinosteroid. This suggests that the phytohormone helps coordinate the developmental program more
effectively, ensuring that initiated fruit bodies complete their development to maturity rather than aborting partway through.
The suppression of abortive fruit body formation has both biological and practical significance.
Biologically, it implies that brassinosteroids may stabilize the developmental commitment of primordia, reducing the sensitivity of developing fruit bodies to
environmental fluctuations that might otherwise trigger abortion. Practically, it means that more of the biological resources invested in fruit body
initiation are carried through to harvest, improving overall cultivation efficiency.
