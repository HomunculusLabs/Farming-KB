# Water-Soluble Calcium (WS-Ca)
Water-Soluble Calcium (WS-Ca) is a [[korean-natural-farming]] input made
by extracting calcium carbonate from eggshells or seashells using
brown rice vinegar. Calcium is one of the most common substances in
the world next to oxygen and silicon, and the majority of calcium
exists as calcium carbonate. In Natural Farming, this calcium
carbonate is converted to a water-soluble form that can be quickly
absorbed by crops for immediate benefit.
## The Importance of Calcium in Plants
Calcium is as important to plants as it is to humans. It contributes
to better utilization of carbohydrates and protein, is the major
component in forming cell membranes, and enables smooth cell
division. Calcium removes harmful substances by binding with organic
acids and plays several critical roles:
- It prevents crops from overgrowing, balancing vegetative and
  reproductive development.
- It makes fruits firm and prolongs the storage period.
- It promotes absorption of phosphoric acid and nutrient accumulation.
- It carries nutrients like carbohydrates from branches and leaves
  to final storage organs.
- It plays a very important role in maintaining overall plant health.
## Extraction Principle
Calcium carbonate, the main component of eggshells, reacts with acids
to produce carbon dioxide. When calcium carbonate reacts with any
acid, it produces CO2. However, acetic acid (the major component of
vinegar) is a weak acid, so it reacts very slowly and emits an
imperceptible amount of CO2. A fluffy egg shell indicates that CO2
is dissolving into the vinegar.
This slow reaction is advantageous because it produces a gentle,
controlled extraction of calcium into a water-soluble form that
plants can readily absorb through foliar application.
## Preparation of WS-Ca
Materials needed:
- Eggshells or seashells
- Crushing or pounding tool
- Brown rice vinegar (BRV)
- Jar or polyethylene container
- Porous paper and rubber band
Steps for preparation:
1. Collect eggshells and remove the inside peel. Taking out the
   peelings clears the eggshells of organic matters other than
   calcium.
2. Crush or pound the shells into small pieces (not into powder
   form). Smaller pieces speed the process and make the product
   more effective.
3. Lightly roast the shells to remove any organic substances that
   may rot and deteriorate during the extraction process.
4. Put the roasted shells in a container filled with brown rice
   vinegar (BRV). The eggshells will move up and down, emitting
   bubbles and melting to become a neutralized liquid.
5. When there is no more movement or bubbles, the process is
   complete and the solution is saturated.
Important precautions:
- Put roasted eggshells into the vinegar little by little and slowly.
  If too many are added at once, bubbles can overflow from the
  vigorous reaction.
- Eggshells that still have calcium carbonate may sink and remain at
  the bottom when the solution reaches saturation. In this case,
  take out the solution and add more vinegar or BRV.
## When to Use WS-Ca
WS-Ca has numerous specific applications:
1. Very effective during the cross-over period (vegetative to
   reproductive transition).
2. Spray on leaves after fruits become large to prevent overgrowth
   and yield solid fruits.
3. Leads nutrients to accumulate in flower buds and fruits.
4. Use when plants overgrow to restore balance.
5. Use when initial growth is poor or leaves discolor and lack luster.
6. Use when flower buds show poor differentiation.
7. Use when physiological fruit drop is severe.
8. Use when fruit enlargement is slow or sugar content decreases.
9. WS-Ca should NOT be given when vigorous vegetative growth is
   needed.
## Dilution and Combined Use
WS-Ca is used with a basic dilution ratio of 1:1000 in water. For
enhanced effects, WS-Ca can be combined with other inputs:
- WS-Ca with WCaP, FPJ, OHN, and seawater produces better taste and
  more aromatic fruits.
- Multiple separately prepared WS-Ca batches may be mixed together
  to enhance the overall calcium effect.
- Spray on leaves after fruits have become large for best results.
## Symptoms of Calcium Deficiency
The source details calcium deficiency symptoms:
- Underdeveloped roots and feeble root hairs.
- Leaves discolor, become brownish, and dry out.
- Appearance of empty bean pods.
- Poor ripening with low sugar content and softening of fruit flesh.
- Leafy vegetables may contact Rhizoctonia disease.
- Root vegetables become pithy and hollow with short storage life.
- Rice and barley suffer from low starch accumulation and low
  resistance to insects and diseases.
## Environmental Conditions
- Optimum temperature: 23 to 25 degrees Celsius.
- Cool and shaded place with no direct sunlight for preparation and
  storage.
## Key Principles
- Eggshells are a cost-effective and readily available source of
  calcium carbonate.
- Brown rice vinegar slowly converts calcium carbonate into a
  water-soluble form that plants can absorb.
- WS-Ca prevents overgrowth and promotes firm, sweet fruit.
- Do not apply WS-Ca when vigorous vegetative growth is desired.
- Calcium is critical during the transition from vegetative to
  reproductive growth stages.
