# KNF IMO Collection Methods

Indigenous Microorganisms (IMOs) can be collected using three distinct
methods described in Cho's Global Natural Farming. Each method targets
different microbial communities from specific environments, enabling
farmers to build broad microbial diversity in their preparations.

## Overview of IMO Collection

Dr. Cho advocates collecting microorganisms from different areas and
under different weather conditions to maximize microbial diversity.
Organisms found under the heat of the sun differ from those in shaded
areas such as under bamboo trees. The best material for culturing IMOs
is steamed rice that is not too soft or too sticky, as aerobic
microorganisms do not prefer overly moist substrates.

Key material principles:
- Use leftover rice rather than freshly cooked, overly sticky rice
- Avoid plastic containers; use wood or bamboo instead
- Collect from locations with abundant decomposed leaf molds
- Gather from diverse environments (sunny, shaded, high altitude)

## Method 1: Collection from Native Soil

This is the standard method for collecting IMOs from healthy forest or
field soil where microbial life is abundant and diverse.

### Materials Needed
1. Wooden box (natural wood, bamboo, or cedar)
2. Hard-cooked rice with less moisture (for aerobic microbes)
3. Porous paper (paper towel)
4. Rubber band or thread
5. Container box or basket made of bamboo
6. Jaggery or brown sugar (unrefined sugar)

### Procedure
1. Fill the wooden box with steamed rice at moderate moisture. Do not
   stuff rice higher than 3 inches and do not press it, as adequate
   air circulation is essential for aerobic microorganisms.
2. Cover with white plain paper (avoid newspaper). Secure with rubber
   band or thread.
3. Mark a 12-inch by 8-inch area in soil, excavate 2 inches, and
   place the box in the pit. Choose a location where IMOs abound,
   such as a forest or site with decomposed leaf molds.
4. Cover with dry leaves to protect from sun and rain.
5. After 3 days (5 days in winter), the rice will be covered with
   microorganisms indicating successful collection.
6. Transfer the IMO-covered rice to a clay pot or glass jar (IMO-1).
7. Mix jaggery with IMO-1 in 1:1 ratio (e.g., 1 kg jaggery to 1 kg
   IMO-1) to create IMO-2. Cover and store in a cool, shaded place.

Note: White refined sugar is not recommended. Jaggery is best. Black
molds indicate the process has gone too far.

## Method 2: Collection from Bamboo Stump

Bamboo groves harbor unique microbial communities due to the bamboo's
natural juices and the shaded, humid microclimate they create.

### Materials Needed
1. Wooden box (natural wood, bamboo, or cedar)
2. Hard-cooked rice with less moisture
3. White plain paper and rubber band
4. Sharp cutting tool and jaggery or brown sugar

### Procedure
1. Choose a bamboo bush in the center of a bamboo grove for the
   richest microbial diversity.
2. Cut a healthy bamboo shoot approximately 10 cm from the base.
   Cut diagonally to prevent bamboo juice from leaking out.
3. Fill the bamboo cavity with boiled rice of low moisture content.
   The rice level should be higher than the edge of the stump.
4. Cover the rice-filled stump with the wooden box, then dry leaves,
   then a plastic sheet for rain protection.
5. After 3 to 5 days, microorganisms will have colonized the rice.
   Bamboo juice provides additional beneficial compounds.
6. Cut the stump, transfer rice to a jar (IMO-1), mix with jaggery
   in 1:1 ratio to create IMO-2.

Unique advantage: Bamboo juice provides additional nutrients and the
grove microclimate favors diverse fungal species not found in open
soil environments.

## Method 3: Collection from Paddy Field

Paddy fields after harvest provide field-adapted microbes particularly
suitable for crop application.

### Materials Needed
1. Wooden box filled three-quarters with steamed rice
2. Steel wire net (to prevent mice and rats)
3. Plastic sheet and jaggery or brown sugar

### Procedure
1. Immediately after paddy harvest, cover rice stumps with the rice-
   filled wooden boxes facing downward so rice contacts the stumps.
2. Cover with steel wire net to prevent rodent access.
3. Cover with plastic sheet for rain protection while allowing some
   air circulation.
4. After approximately 4 days, fungal growth on the rice indicates
   successful collection.
5. Transfer the fungi-covered rice to a clay pot (IMO-1), mix with
   jaggery in 1:1 ratio to create IMO-2.

Unique advantage: Microbes are already adapted to agricultural soil
conditions and integrate more readily into crop soils.

## Combining Collection Methods

Dr. Cho recommends collecting IMOs using multiple methods and from
different environments to maximize microbial diversity. Farmers should
aim to collect from all four directions of their field, and from
varied environments including mountains, summits, valleys, and
trenches. Samples from high mountains or uncontaminated regions
introduce particularly resilient microbes into the mixture. The
principle of mixing microorganisms from various sources ensures a
broad spectrum of beneficial organisms capable of thriving under
diverse field conditions throughout the growing season.
