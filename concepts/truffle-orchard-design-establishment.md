## Site Assessment

### Soil Analysis

Comprehensive soil analysis is the essential first step in truffle orchard planning. Soil properties directly affect both tree growth and truffle fungus establishment and fruiting.

Key soil parameters to assess include: pH (Tuber melanosporum requires pH 7.5-8.3; T. aestivum tolerates pH 6.5-8.0), texture (well-drained sandy loam to loamy soil is ideal; heavy clays and pure sands are problematic), drainage (truffles require excellent drainage; waterlogging is fatal), calcium content (calcicheous soils with free calcium carbonate are preferred for most European species), organic matter content (moderate levels of 2-5% organic matter are optimal; high organic matter favors competing fungi), and depth (soil should be at least 50 cm deep to a restrictive layer).

Soil samples should be collected from multiple depths (0-15 cm, 15-30 cm, 30-50 cm) and from multiple locations across the proposed site. Professional soil testing laboratories can provide detailed analysis of these parameters.

### Climate Assessment

Truffle-producing regions have characteristic climate patterns: moderate winters without prolonged hard freezing, warm but not excessively hot summers, adequate and reasonably distributed rainfall (typically 600-1000 mm annually), and a distinct seasonal cycle that triggers fruiting.

Tuber melanosporum requires warm summers (average July temperature 18-22 C) and cool winters with occasional frosts. Tuber aestivum is more cold-tolerant and can fruit in regions with colder winters. Extreme heat (sustained temperatures above 30 C) during summer can stress trees and reduce truffle production.

Climate data from the proposed site should be compared with the climate profiles of successful truffle-producing regions. Microclimate effects (slope orientation, elevation, frost pockets, wind exposure) can significantly modify the local climate and should be assessed during site visits.
