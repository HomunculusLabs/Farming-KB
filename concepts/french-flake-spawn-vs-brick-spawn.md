# French Flake Spawn Versus English Brick Spawn

## Two Traditions of mushroom-spawn was produced and traded in two fundamentally different forms.

The English tradition manufactured spawn in the form of hard, dry, solid bricks of compressed manure.

The French tradition produced spawn as loose flakes of half-rotted strawy manure.

Both forms were completely permeated with mushroom [[mycelium]], the vegetative body of the fungus.

These two traditions represented not merely different packaging methods but genuinely different approaches to the propagation and preservation of [[antimicrobial-compounds-mushroom-mycelium]].

The choice between them was a matter of ongoing debate among growers.

Falconer's 1891 treatise discussed both types in considerable detail, reflecting their importance to the [[mushroom-industry-african-countries]].

## English Brick Spawn

Brick spawn was commonly known as English spawn.

What was imported into the [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]] was made in England, mostly around London.

The bricks made by different manufacturers varied somewhat in size and weight.

In some cases ten bricks went to the bushel, in others fourteen, and in others sixteen.

Sixteen to the bushel was the commonest size.

Each brick weighed exactly one pound.

It measured approximately eight and one-half inches long, five and one-fourth inches wide, and one and one-fourth inches thick.

The London spawn makers called this a 9x6x2 inch brick, but it shrank in drying.

In retailing brick spawn in America, it was sold by weight and not by measure.

The bricks were dry, hard, and solid — qualities that made them durable for shipping and storage.

The mycelium within the bricks appeared as a network of fine white threads or a bluish-white mold permeating the manure mass.

## Mill-Track Spawn — The Original Natural Spawn

Mill-track [[cuthill-mushroom-spawn-preparation-historical]] was advertised by some American seedsmen.

But what they sold under this name was only ordinary English brick spawn.

One prominent seed firm admitted: "Genuine mill-track spawn used to be the best in England, but it has been superseded."

The real mill-track spawn was natural spawn that had spread through thoroughly amalgamated horse droppings in mill-tracks.

It was the cleanings from the pathways where horses had trodden and mixed their droppings with the soil over many years.

It was usually sold in large, irregular, somewhat soft lumps.

It was much esteemed by spawn makers for impregnating their bricks.

But horses had given place to steam as motive power in mills, so there was no further supply of mill-track spawn.

Contemporary manufacturers produced spawn that was considered as good as the old mill-track natural spawn.

## French Flake Spawn

The flake spawn was generally known as French spawn.

It was imported into the [[dmt-maoi-plants-united-states]] from France.

The manufacture of "French" spawn for sale was not strictly confined to France, however.

It was put up in two forms.

The first was nicely packed in thin wooden boxes, each containing two or three pounds of spawn.

The second was sold loose in bulk, by weight or measure.

Flake spawn consisted of pieces of half-rotted strawy manure permeated with white mycelium.

It was softer and more perishable than brick spawn.

It had to be handled more carefully and kept under more [[psilocybe-cubensis-strain-potency-variability-controlled-conditions-bigwood-beug]].

But many growers considered it more vigorous and productive than brick spawn.

The Parisian cave growers were the primary producers and users of flake spawn.
