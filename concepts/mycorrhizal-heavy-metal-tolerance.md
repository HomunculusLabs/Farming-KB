# Mycorrhizal Heavy Metal Tolerance

[[mycorrhizal-fungi]] play a critical role in protecting host plants from heavy
metal toxicity and enabling plant survival in contaminated soils. The
symbiosis provides multiple tolerance mechanisms including extraradical metal
immobilization, reduced metal translocation to host tissues, and metal
sequestration within fungal structures. Research on mycorrhizal responses to
Zn, Cd, Pb, and Al has revealed significant interspecific variation in
tolerance and complex interactions among multiple metal contaminants.

## Ectomycorrhizal Responses to Specific Metals

### Zinc and Cadmium

Hartley et al. (1997a) established EC50 values for five ectomycorrhizal
species, showing that tolerance varies considerably: [[suillus-granulatus]] was
the most Cd-tolerant (EC50 = 12.6 mmol m-3), while [[suillus-variegatus]] was
among the most sensitive (EC50 = 0.008 mmol m-3). For zinc, tolerance
ranged from 100 mmol m-3 in [[lactarius-deliciosus]] to 343 mmol m-3 in S.
variegatus. Hartley et al. (1999b) identified Cd as the most toxic of five
metals tested (Cd, Pb, Zn, Sb, Cu) to ectomycorrhizal Scots pine seedlings.

Colpaert and Van Assche (1992, 1993) investigated Zn and Cd toxicity in
[[pinus-sylvestris]] inoculated with ectomycorrhizal fungi, demonstrating that
mycorrhizal colonization ameliorated the effects of these metals on host
plant growth. Hartley-Whitaker et al. (2000a) found effective concentrations
reducing root colonization by 50% were 3.7 mg g-1 for Paxillus involutus
and 2.3 mg g-1 for Suillus variegatus.

### Lead

Chappelka et al. (1991) demonstrated that total ectomycorrhizal colonization
of Loblolly pine decreased with increasing soil Pb (0-480 mg kg-1), though
plant biomass was unaffected. Vodnik et al. (1998) showed that Pisolithus
tinctorius took up only 1.1% of total Pb compared to 6.2% for Laccaria
laccata and 5.4% for Suillus bovinus. Internal translocation also differed:
45% of Pb taken up by L. laccata was released distally versus only 10% by
S. bovinus.

### Aluminum

Egerton-Warburton and Griffin (1995) documented differential Al responses
among Pisolithus tinctorius isolates. Tam (1995) showed that P. tinctorius,
Hymenogaster sp., and Scleroderma sp. tolerated Al up to 200 mg L-1, while
Thelephora terrestris and Cenococcum geophilum were inhibited at 10 mg L-1.

## Mechanisms of Mycorrhizal Metal Amelioration

Denny and Wilkins (1987a, b), using electron microscopy with EDAX, identified
adsorption of heavy metals onto the extraradical hyphal network, fungal
sheath, and Hartig net, preventing translocation into host vascular tissue.
Their data on Paxillus involutus mycorrhizae on birch showed Zn at 192
mmol g-1 in extraradical hyphae versus only 15 mmol g-1 in the stele of
mycorrhizal plants (26 mmol g-1 in non-mycorrhizal steles). Denny and
Ridge (1995) identified high Zn binding in extracellular slime of P.
tinctorius hyphae. Turnau et al. (1993, 1994) demonstrated metals complex
to polyphosphate granules in fungal vacuoles, polysaccharides, and
cysteine-rich proteins in the outer pigmented cell wall. Cadmium was
abundant in fungal vacuoles, suggesting active detoxification.

## Interspecific Variation and Adaptive Tolerance

Blaudez et al. (2000) tested 39 isolates of five ectomycorrhizal species,
showing that P. tinctorius, Suillus luteus, and S. variegatus were more
tolerant of Cu, Cd, and Zn than Paxillus involutus, though Paxillus was
more Ni-resistant. Hartley et al. (1997a) observed ameliorative metal
interactions: Pb and Sb reduced Cd toxicity to S. granulatus, and Cd + Pb +
Zn was less toxic to L. deliciosus than individual metals. Two mechanisms
were proposed: competition between elements of similar valency for ion
transporters, and an induced physiological response to Zn decreasing
sensitivity to both Zn and Cd.

Adaptive evolution of metal tolerance remains unresolved. No relationship
between resistance and prior exposure was found by Denny and Wilkins or
Colpaert and Van Assche. However, Hartley et al. (1997a) suggest that in
acidic, peaty soils where heavy metals are common, plants may have
coevolved with mycorrhizal endophytes, citing ericoid associations of
Calluna and Vaccinium as examples.

## Multiple Metal Interactions and Community Effects

Hartley et al. (1999a) showed heavy metals affect both pine seedling
performance and cross-colonization ability of ectomycorrhizal fungi.
Hartley-Whitaker et al. (2000b) found decreased colonization in Cd- and
Zn-contaminated soils, though seedling growth was similar. Significant
community shifts could have consequences not apparent in short-term
experiments. Ruhling and Soderstrom (1990) found ectomycorrhizal species
more metal-tolerant than saprotrophs along a Swedish pollution transect,
with Laccaria laccata the most tolerant species.

## Arbuscular Mycorrhizae and Heavy Metals

Leyval et al. (1997) reviewed arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) responses, noting
AM colonization can be high in polluted spoil soils (863 mg kg-1 Zn, 456 mg
kg-1 Pb), but correlations with extractable metal are often weak. Joner
and Leyval (1997) showed AM fungi enhanced Cd uptake into clover but
retained Cd within the fungal component, reducing transfer to host tissue.
Call and Davies (1988) showed AM inoculation increased survival, growth,
and nutrients of grasses on lignite mine overburden.

## Bioremediation Potential and Metal Solubilization

Donnelley and Fletcher (1994) reviewed mycorrhizal fungi as bioremediation
agents, emphasizing the dual advantage: the fungus protects the host from
toxic metals while the host may enhance fungal survival. Many
ectomycorrhizal fungi produce enzymes degrading aromatic compounds, aiding
co-contaminated site restoration. Effective restoration requires
pre-inoculation of all planted trees rather than relying on cross-colonization
from a few inoculated individuals.

Dixon-Hardy et al. (1998) demonstrated that C, N, and P availability
significantly influences solubilization of insoluble metal compounds by live
fungal biomass. Using Aspergillus niger, they showed nutrient sources modulate
fungal capacity to solubilize metals, indicating metal binding is greatly
influenced by environmental conditions.
