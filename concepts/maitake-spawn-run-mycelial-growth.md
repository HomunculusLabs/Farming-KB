## Maitake Spawn Run and Mycelial Growth

The spawn run is the foundational vegetative phase in Grifola frondosa cultivation, during which mycelium colonizes the substrate and prepares for fruiting body formation. This phase encompasses both active growth and a critical maturation period with distinct visual markers.

## Overview

The spawn run is the vegetative mycelial phase leading to primordia initiation. G. frondosa has a notably prolonged spawn run compared to many other cultivated mushrooms, during which the substrate is fully colonized by the spawn. The entire period includes both a growth phase and a maturation phase characterized by metabolic activities.

## Timeline of Mycelial Development

### Day 1–9: Initial Colonization

- Undifferentiated white mycelia begin to colonize the substrate
- Growth is initially slow as the mycelium establishes itself
- The substrate appears largely unchanged from the outside

### Day 17 (~2½ Weeks): Surface Penetration

- White young mycelia penetrate throughout the surface of the substrate within the sealed bags
- The surface of the substrate becomes visibly white
- The bag contents show uniform mycelial coverage

### Day 30 (~1 Month): Metabolic Maturation

- Orange-brown exudates appear, indicating active metabolic processes
- These exudates cause discoloration of the previously white mycelia
- This discoloration is a normal and expected part of the maturation process
- The mycelium is considered mature at this stage

### Day 40–42: Surface Coat Formation and Primordia

- Tighter mycelial growth at the substrate surface gives rise to a surface mycelial coat
- The topography of the mycelial surface becomes uneven with grayish amorphous masses
- Grayish primordia reaching 2.54–5.08 cm (1–2 inches) in diameter form on the substrate surface
- These primordia form while the bag is still closed
- At this point, bags should be transferred to a fruiting facility

## Environmental Parameters for Spawn Run

### Commercial Cultivation Parameters

| Parameter | Growth Period | Maturation Period |
|-----------|--------------|-------------------|
| Temperature | 21–24°C | 21–24°C |
| Relative humidity | 95–98% | 95–98% |
| CO₂ concentration | 20,000–40,000 ppm (2–4%) | 20,000–40,000 ppm |
| Light | 0–1 lux | 14–30 lux |
| Duration | ~3 weeks | ~4 weeks (to day 30) |

### Home Grower Parameters

| Parameter | Condition |
|-----------|-----------|
| Temperature | 20–25°C |
| Relative humidity | High (within sealed bags) |
| Light | Low level, intermittent |
| Ventilation | Not specifically required (sealed bags with microfilters) |
| Total duration | 4–5 weeks |

## Role of Light During Spawn Run

Although G. frondosa mycelia can grow in the complete absence of light, a low level of light (approximately 50 lux) throughout the spawn run period has been found to facilitate primordia initiation (Wu et al., 1997). Light management during spawn run is critical for several reasons:

- **Too much light** during early and mid spawn run produces a grayish-brown surface coat that is undesirable for initiating light-induced, highly pigmented primordia
- **No light** during a prolonged spawn run may lead to excessive thickening of the surface mycelial coat, which can delay or prevent primordia formation
- **Low light (~50 lux)** throughout the period provides the best balance for forming a proper surface mycelial coat conducive to primordia initiation

## Surface Mycelial Coat

The surface mycelial coat is a critical structure that forms during the later stages of the spawn run. Its quality directly affects primordia initiation success:

- The coat should **not** be grayish-brown or too thick
- A properly formed coat is white to light gray with moderate thickness
- Proper light levels and appropriate spawn run duration (not excessively long) are essential
- An overly thick coat on the substrate surface may delay or prevent primordia formation entirely

## Transition to Primordia Initiation

After approximately 42 days, when grayish primordia of sufficient size have formed:

1. Transfer sealed bags to a fruiting facility
2. Wait 2–3 days for acclimation
3. Open the tops of the bags for increased access to light and oxygen
4. Increase light to approximately 200 lux
5. Ensure ample oxygen and high humidity within the sealed bag environment

## Key Differences from Other Cultivated Mushrooms

G. frondosa spawn run has several distinctive characteristics:

- **Prolonged duration**: Many strains require 60–90+ days; fast-growing strains complete it in ~30 days
- **No cold shock required**: Unlike some other species, maitake does not need cold shock for primordia initiation
- **High CO₂ tolerance**: The mycelium thrives at 20,000–40,000 ppm CO₂ during spawn run
- **Metabolic exudates**: Orange-brown discoloration during maturation is a normal indicator of metabolic activity, not contamination
