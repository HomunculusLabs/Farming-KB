Salicylic acid (SA) is a phenolic phytohormone that functions as a
central regulator of plant defense against biotrophic and hemibiotrophic
pathogens. First identified for its role in inducing systemic acquired
resistance (SAR) in tobacco, SA is now recognized as a master signaling
molecule that orchestrates both local and whole-plant immune responses.
Beyond pathogen defense, SA modulates abiotic [[endophyte-mediated-plant-stress-tolerance]], flowering
time, thermogenesis in certain Araceae, and senescence pathways.

## Biosynthesis and Metabolism

Plants synthesize salicylic acid through two primary biosynthetic
pathways. The isochorismate (IC) pathway, operating primarily in
chloroplasts, converts chorismate to isochorismate via isochorismate
synthase (ICS), which is then cleaved to produce SA. This pathway
dominates in [[arabidopsis-thaliana]] and most dicots, contributing
approximately 90% of pathogen-induced SA accumulation.

The phenylalanine ammonia-lyase (PAL) pathway represents the
alternative route, converting phenylalanine through cinnamic acid and
benzoic acid intermediates to yield SA. This pathway is more prominent
in monocots such as rice and contributes to basal SA levels even in
species where the IC pathway is dominant. The relative contribution of

each pathway varies by species, tissue type, and the nature of the
stimulus triggering SA biosynthesis.

SA is conjugated to inactive forms through glucosylation (SAG) and
methyl esterification (MeSA) for transport and storage. SA
beta-glucoside (SAG) serves as a mobile storage form, while methyl
salicylate functions as a volatile airborne signal that can activate
defense in neighboring plants and distant tissues.

## Signaling Mechanism and NPR1

The SA signal is transduced through a pathway centered on the
regulatory protein NPR1 (Nonexpressor of Pathogenesis-Related genes
1). In uninfected cells, NPR1 exists as an oligomeric complex
stabilized by intermolecular disulfide bonds. SA accumulation triggers
changes in the cellular redox environment, reducing these disulfide
bonds and causing NPR1 to dissociate into active monomers.

Monomeric NPR1 translocates to the nucleus, where it interacts with
TGA-family transcription factors to activate expression of pathogenesis-
related (PR) genes. NPR1 also recruits chromatin remodeling complexes
that modify histone acetylation at PR gene promoters, facilitating
transcriptional activation. The NPR1-TGA complex regulates hundreds
of defense-related genes, creating a broad-spectrum antimicrobial state
characterized by PR protein accumulation and enhanced cell wall
fortification.

## Systemic Acquired Resistance

Systemic acquired resistance (SAR) is a long-distance signaling
mechanism that immunizes uninfected plant tissues after a localized
pathogen attack. SA is essential for SAR establishment. Following
local infection, SA levels rise dramatically at the infection site,
triggering a wave of SA production that spreads through the vasculature
to distal organs over 24-72 hours.

The mobile signal responsible for SAR propagation has been
characterized as a derivative of SA, with methyl salicylate (MeSA)
and azelaic acid both implicated as candidate phloem-mobile signals.
In distant tissues, MeSA is converted back to active SA by
methylsalicylate esterase, reactivating the defense program. This
conversion step provides spatial control, ensuring that defense
activation occurs specifically in tissues receiving the mobile signal
rather than indiscriminately throughout the plant.

SAR provides broad-spectrum protection lasting days to weeks, effective
against diverse pathogens including fungi, bacteria, and viruses. The
epigenetic component of SAR involves histone modifications at defense
gene loci that prime faster and stronger responses upon subsequent
challenge, a phenomenon known as [[mycorrhiza-induced-resistance-defense-priming]].

## Crosstalk with Other Hormonal Pathways

SA signaling exists within a complex network of hormonal crosstalk that
determines the specificity and magnitude of the plant immune response.
The most significant interaction is the antagonistic relationship
between SA and [[jasmonic-acid-plant-defense-signaling]] (JA) [[plant-signaling-pathways]]. SA-mediated
defenses are effective against biotrophic pathogens that feed on living
tissue, while JA-mediated defenses target necrotrophic pathogens that
kill host cells.

This antagonism operates through multiple molecular mechanisms. NPR1
represses JA-responsive gene expression by interacting with the JA
co-repressor JAZ proteins and recruiting histone deacetylases to JA-
responsive promoters. Conversely, JA signaling can suppress SA
accumulation through the transcription factor MYC2. The SA-JA
antagonism allows the plant to allocate defensive resources
efficiently, activating the pathway most appropriate to the attacking
pathogen's lifestyle.

Ethylene signaling intersects with JA to modulate necrotrophic defense,
while [[abscisic-acid-plant-stress-hormone]] generally suppresses both SA and JA pathways to
favor abiotic [[fungal-stress-responses]]. Auxin signaling antagonizes SA through
the transcriptional repressor AUX/IAA proteins, providing a mechanism
by which rapidly growing tissues temporarily suppress defense
activation.

## Role in Abiotic Stress Tolerance

Beyond biotic defense, SA confers tolerance to multiple abiotic
stresses including drought, salinity, heat, cold, and heavy metal
toxicity. SA pretreatment enhances antioxidant enzyme activity
(superoxide dismutase, catalase, peroxidase), stabilizes membrane
integrity, and upregulates osmoprotectant synthesis under stress
conditions.

In drought stress, SA modulates stomatal closure through regulation
of guard cell ion channels and [[cervantes-hydrogen-peroxide-sterilization]] signaling. Under
salt stress, SA reduces sodium accumulation by modulating root
transport systems and enhances potassium retention. Heat tolerance
is improved through SA-mediated induction of heat shock proteins
and maintenance of photosynthetic [[electron-transport-chain-mitochondrial-respiration]]. These roles suggest SA functions as a general stress signaling
hub integrating diverse environmental cues.

## Agricultural Applications

SA and its functional analog acibenzolar-S-methyl (ASM, sold as
Actigard) are applied as foliar sprays to induce SAR in crops
including wheat, rice, and tomato. ASM provides reduced disease
severity against downy mildew, bacterial spot, and various fungal
pathogens. [[fukuoka-critique-genetic-engineering]] targeting SA biosynthetic genes (ICS1)
or receptor proteins (NPR3, NPR4) represents a potential strategy for
developing disease-resistant [[heritage-and-landrace-crop-varieties-for-small-farmers]].
