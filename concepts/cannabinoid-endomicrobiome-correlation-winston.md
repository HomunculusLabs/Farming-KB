# Cannabinoid Profile and Endorhiza Microbiome Correlation

## Overview

A notable finding from the Winston et al. (2014) [[winston-cannabis-microbiome-study-design]]
was the significant correlation between cannabinoid concentration and
composition with endorhiza [[edaphic-factors-microbial-community-structure]]. This relationship
raises the possibility that the root microbiome may influence, or be
influenced by, the plant's [[fungal-elicitors-enhanced-secondary-metabolite-production]]. However, the
confounding effects of soil physicochemical variables make it difficult to
establish causation from these correlational data alone.

## Study Design and Cannabinoid Analysis

Cannabinoid data was collected from the second experiment, sampling buds of
three White Widow plants and one Maui Wowie plant. Analysis was performed by
Delta-9-Technologies (Santa Ana, California) using standard chromatographic
methods. The cannabinoid profile included delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol (THC),
cannabidiol (CBD), and other cannabinoid compounds. The plants were sampled
two weeks prior to harvest, during late flowering when cannabinoid production
is at its peak.

## Mantel Test Results

Mantel tests correlating cannabinoid data with endorhiza community structure
revealed:

- **Significant correlation between cannabinoid profiles and strain
  differences** (unweighted r-stat: 0.863, p = 0.001). This strong
  correlation indicates that the endorhiza microbial community structure
  clusters according to the cannabinoid-producing genotype of the host plant.

- **THC concentration and composition were also significantly correlated
  with soil edaphic variables**. Plants from one of the two soil types showed
  both higher THC composition and concentration, creating a confounding
  variable where THC effects on the microbiome cannot be disentangled from
  soil physicochemical effects.

## The Causation Problem

The significant correlation between cannabinoids and endorhiza community
structure is subject to three competing interpretations:

### 1. Microbiome Influences Cannabinoid Production

Root-associated microbes may modulate cannabinoid biosynthesis through
several mechanisms:

- **Nutrient availability** - Microbial mineralization of organic matter
  affects nitrogen, phosphorus, and micronutrient availability, all of which
  influence secondary metabolite production. Nitrogen limitation, for example,
  is well-known to increase terpenoid and alkaloid production in many plants.

- **Phytohormone signaling** - Endorhiza bacteria produce auxins,
  cytokinins, gibberellins, and ethylene precursors that interact with the
  plant's hormone signaling networks. These hormones regulate the expression
  of genes in the cannabinoid biosynthetic pathway, including
  tetrahydrocannabinolic acid synthase (THCAS) and cannabidiolic acid
  synthase (CBDAS).

- **Elicitor molecules** - Microbe-associated molecular patterns (MAMPs)
  such as flagellin, lipopolysaccharide, and chitin oligomers can trigger
  defense responses in plants that divert metabolic flux toward secondary
  metabolite production, potentially including cannabinoids.

- **Precursor supply** - Microbial metabolism in the rhizosphere may
  influence the availability of precursors for cannabinoid biosynthesis,
  including geranyl pyrophosphate (GPP) and olivetolic acid.

### 2. Cannabinoid Production Influences the Microbiome

Cannabinoids and other [[antifungal-secondary-metabolites-coprophilous-fungi]] exuded from roots may directly
shape the microbial community:

- **Antimicrobial activity** - THC, CBD, and other cannabinoids have
  demonstrated [[medicinal-mushroom-antimicrobial-properties]] against both Gram-positive and
  Gram-negative bacteria. Root exudation of these compounds could selectively
  filter the endorhiza community.
- **Terpene exudation** - The terpene fraction of cannabis resin shares
  biosynthetic pathways with cannabinoids. Terpenes are well-documented
  antimicrobial compounds that could influence root microbial composition.
