## No-Tillage Systems

### Fukuoka's Stacked Rotation
- Sows into the preceding crop using white clover as permanent base
- "Stacked" grain/legume system and citrus/wattle system
- Controls weeds by natural means (frogs, spiders, straw, flooding, cutting)
- "Order in time by apparent disorder in space"

### No-Tillage Crop Sequence
- Rye and wheat broadcast into soybean crops when leaves begin to fall (hiding seed from birds)
- Soybeans/legumes broadcast into stubble of oats, barley, wheat, rye
- Peas planted after corn; green peas followed by corn
- Other no-tillage crops: cucumber, watermelon, tomato, cotton, tobacco, sugar beet, pepper, vetch, sunflower

### Key Principle
- "Spring" straw cannot be strewn over seed of same crop (disease transmission)
- Straw applied in autumn or mid-summer rots before spring crop shoots
- "There is no doubt in my mind that we can evolve several permanent no-tillage rotations with modest trials"

## Yield Distribution Strategy

The concentration of yields into one short period is "a fiscal, not an environmental strategy" creating feast/famine. Permaculture disperses yield by:
- Selecting early, mid, and late season varieties
- Planting same variety in early/late-ripening situations
- Selecting long-yielding species
- Increasing diversity (leaf, fruit, seed, root all as product yields)
- Using self-storing species (tubers, hard seeds, nuts, rhizomes) dug on demand
- Preserving, drying, pitting, cool storage
- Regional trade or land at different altitudes/latitudes

## Hedgerow Plants

- **Tree Lucerne** (Chamaecytisus proliferus): small tree to 4m, perennial legume, abundant seed for poultry, wide soil range, useful hedge plant, prunings for mulch

## Oil Plants

- Peanuts: plant after last frost, 33 kg/ha, 120-140 days, mulch essential for harvest
- Intercropping: peanut/potato with lupins as green manure, mulch with straw/seaweed, Russell lupin as windbreak
