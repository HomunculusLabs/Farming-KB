## Period 3: Scientific Investigations (1950s Onward)

The third period began with Wasson's studies in the 1950s, which followed
the path initially laid by Schultes. The key scientific figures included
Wasson and Wasson (1957), Heim (1956a, b, 1957a, c, 1958a, b), Singer
(1949, 1958, 1959, 1960a), Singer and Smith (1958), and Guzman
(1959, 1977a, 1978b, 1982, 1983, 1990a, b). These investigations
established the modern framework for understanding neurotropic fungi.

Guzman (1990a, b) divided the development of Mexican mycology into two
important time periods based on this era: 1) before Wasson, Heim, and
Singer's studies (before 1954-1958), and 2) after their studies. The
research on neurotropic fungi played such an important role that it
stimulated interest in all mycological studies throughout Mexico.

This period also saw the isolation and identification of psilocybin by
Hofmann, and the chemical characterization of many species. The number of
recognized neurotropic species grew from approximately 20 to over 100
during this period.

## Period 4: [[guzman-allen-gartz-recreational-use-degeneration-underground-neurotropic-fungi-trade]] and Cultural Degeneration (1960s)

The fourth period involved the utilization of neurotropic fungi as
recreational drugs, accompanied by a degeneration of traditional use,
mainly in Mexico. The spread of recreational psychedelic use in the 1960s
led to the development of an underground market and illegal trade of
these fungi across the USA, Canada, Europe, and Australia.

Because of this trade, it was frequently reported that the tropical fungus
Psilocybe cubensis was found in Europe, though these were actually
illegally cultivated or dried specimens imported from tropical countries.
The recreational drug trade forced governments of many countries to forbid
the use and commerce of these fungi.
