# Jasmonic Acid: Plant Defense and Wound Signaling Hormone

Jasmonic acid (JA) and its derivatives (collectively called jasmonates) are oxylipin-derived plant hormones that play a central role in plant defense against herbivores and necrotrophic pathogens, mediate responses to mechanical wounding, and regulate diverse developmental processes including male fertility, tuber formation, [[soil-moisture-management-and-root-growth]]. Jasmonates function as the primary alarm signal in plant-insect interactions, activating both direct defenses (toxic compounds) and indirect defenses (volatile signals that attract predators of herbivores).

## Chemical Nature and Biosynthesis

Jasmonic acid (C₁₂H₁₈O₃) is a cyclopentanone derived from α-linolenic acid (18:3), an omega-3 polyunsaturated fatty acid released from membrane lipids by phospholipase A (PLA) enzymes upon tissue damage. The biosynthetic pathway is known as the **octadecanoid pathway** or **lipoxygenase (LOX) pathway**:

1. **Lipid release**: Phospholipase A releases α-linolenic acid from chloroplast membrane galactolipids (MGDG and DGDG)
2. **Oxidation**: 13-lipoxygenase (13-LOX) adds molecular oxygen to C-13, forming 13-hydroperoxyoctadecatrienoic acid (13-HPOT)
3. **Allene oxide formation**: Allene oxide synthase (AOS) converts 13-HPOT to an unstable allene oxide
4. **Cyclization**: Allene oxide cyclase (AOC) produces 12-oxo-phytodienoic acid (OPDA), the first cyclic intermediate
5. **Reduction and β-oxidation**: OPDA is transported to peroxisomes, reduced by OPDA reductase (OPR3), and subjected to three rounds of β-oxidation to yield jasmonic acid

The pathway occurs partly in chloroplasts (steps 1-4) and partly in peroxisomes (step 5). OPDA itself has biological activity independent of JA and can trigger a subset of jasmonate-responsive genes, suggesting it functions as a signaling molecule in its own right.

## Active Forms and Conjugation

Jasmonic acid is not the most bioactive form. The key active derivative is **jasmonoyl-isoleucine (JA-Ile)**, formed by conjugation of JA with isoleucine by the enzyme JASMONATE RESISTANT 1 (JAR1). JA-Ile is the primary ligand that binds the jasmonate receptor complex. Other biologically relevant forms include:

- **Methyl jasmonate (MeJA)**: A volatile ester that functions in interplant communication and airborne defense signaling. MeJA is more volatile than JA and can diffuse to neighboring tissues or plants
- **12-OH-JA and 12-OH-JA-Ile**: Inactive catabolic products formed by cytochrome P450 enzymes (CYP94 family)
- **JA-glucoside**: An inactive conjugate that may serve as a storage form
- **Cucurbic acid and related compounds**: Side products with uncertain biological significance

The homeostatic balance between active JA-Ile and inactive catabolic forms (12-OH-JA, JA-glucoside) is tightly regulated and determines the duration and intensity of jasmonate signaling.

## Jasmonate Signaling Pathway

The jasmonate signaling pathway is built around a mechanism conceptually similar to the animal jasmonate/COI1 system, involving an SCF-type E3 ubiquitin ligase complex:

1. **Perception**: JA-Ile (not JA itself) is the active ligand. It forms a molecular glue between the F-box protein COI1 (CORONATINE INSENSITIVE 1) and JAZ (JASMONATE ZIM-DOMAIN) repressor proteins.

2. **Coronatine**: A phytotoxin produced by [[pseudomonas]] syringae pathovars that structurally mimics JA-Ile but binds COI1 with much higher affinity. Coronatine hijacks the jasmonate pathway to suppress plant defense and promote bacterial infection by opening stomata for bacterial entry.

3. **JAZ repressors**: In the absence of JA-Ile, JAZ proteins bind and repress transcription factors (primarily MYC2, MYC3, and MYC4) through interaction with the adaptor protein NINJA and the co-repressor TOPLESS. The JAZ family contains 13 members in Arabidopsis with partially redundant functions.

4. **SCFCOI1 ubiquitination**: When JA-Ile is present, the COI1-JA-Ile-JAZ ternary complex recruits SCF (Skp1-Cullin-F-box) E3 ubiquitin ligase activity, leading to polyubiquitination of JAZ proteins.

5. **26S proteasome degradation**: Ubiquitinated JAZ proteins are degraded by the 26S proteasome, releasing MYC2 transcription factors.

6. **Gene activation**: Freed MYC2/MYC3/MYC4 activate transcription of jasmonate-responsive genes, including [[fungal-elicitors-enhanced-secondary-metabolite-production]] biosynthetic genes, protease inhibitors, and additional JAZ genes (negative feedback loop).

This mechanism allows rapid activation of defense (within minutes of wounding) and equally rapid deactivation when the signal fades (JAZ proteins are resynthesized).

## Defense Against Herbivores

Jasmonates are the master regulators of anti-herbivore defense in plants. When an insect feeds on plant tissue:

1. Mechanical damage and insect oral secretions trigger JA biosynthesis
2. Fatty acid-amino acid conjugates (FACs) in insect saliva (e.g., volicitin) amplify the JA response
3. Systemin (an 18-amino-acid peptide in Solanaceae) or analogous signals propagate the wound response systemically
4. JA accumulation (typically peaking 1-3 hours after wounding) activates defense gene expression

### Direct Defenses (toxic/anti-digestive compounds)
- **Protease inhibitors (PIs)**: Including trypsin inhibitors, chymotrypsin inhibitors, and cysteine protease inhibitors that impair insect digestion. Serine PI expression is strongly JA-dependent
- **Glucosinolates and myrosinases**: In Brassicaceae, JA induces glucosinolate biosynthesis and myrosinase (TGG) expression, creating a "mustard bomb" system activated upon tissue damage
- **Alkaloids**: [[nicotine]] biosynthesis in Nicotiana is JA-regulated; caffeine, [[strychnine]], and other alkaloid pathways show JA responsiveness
- **Terpenoids**: Including volatile monoterpenes and sesquiterpenes with insecticidal or repellent properties
- **[[aact-tannins-terpenes-phenolic-compounds-wood-composting-ingham]]**: Tannins, flavonoids, and other phenylpropanoid-derived defenses
- **Defensins**: Small cysteine-rich antimicrobial peptides with activity against insects and fungi

### Indirect Defenses (tritrophic interactions)
- **Herbivore-Induced Plant Volatiles (HIPVs)**: JA induces the production of [[chamovitz-plant-scent-communication-volatile-organic-compounds]] (VOCs) including:
  - (E)-β-ocimene, linalool, and other terpenoids
  - (Z)-3-hexenol (green leaf volatile)
  - Methyl salicylate
  - Indole
- These volatiles attract natural enemies of herbivores, including [[parasitic-wasps-biological-pest-control]], [[comparison-predatory-mites-vs-ladybugs-biocontrol]], and predatory bugs
- HIPVs can also prime neighboring plants for enhanced defense (interplant communication via eavesdropping)
- The parasitic wasp Cotesia glomerata, for example, uses HIPVs from Brassica to locate caterpillar-infested plants

## Defense Against Necrotrophic Pathogens

Jasmonates, often in concert with ethylene, mediate defense against necrotrophic pathogens (fungi and bacteria that kill host tissue and feed on dead matter):

- [[botrytis-cinerea]] ([[cervantes-gray-mold-botrytis-prevention]]): JA/ET signaling activates defensins, thionins, and chitinases effective against this pathogen
- Alternaria brassicicola: JA-dependent defenses include camalexin biosynthesis and PDF1.2 defensin expression
- JA signaling is antagonistic to salicylic acid (SA) signaling, which defends against biotrophic pathogens
- This JA/SA antagonism creates a defense trade-off: plants optimized for one type of defense may be more susceptible to the other
