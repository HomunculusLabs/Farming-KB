## Designer's Climate Checklist

This checklist provides a systematic approach to gathering and applying climatic data for permaculture site design. It is extracted from the end of the climate chapter of Permaculture: A Designer's Manual and represents the essential field observations and data collection steps every designer should follow.

## Climate Data Collection

### Rainfall and Temperature
- Check data on **average rainfall, temperature, and wind speed and direction** for the region (often available from the Bureau of Meteorology)
- Find out about **flood locations and periodicity, rain intensity, temperature extremes, and seasonal rainfall patterns**
- Design for extremes (e.g., no rain in summer) — do not design only for averages

### Hardiness Assessment
- Ascertain the general **hardiness zone** for plants and animals, based on temperature with frost as the limiting factor
- Survey plants growing in the area, noting **special circumstances** surrounding "marginal" plants — what technique or microclimate allows them to grow?

### Precipitation Accounting
Consider **total precipitation** from all sources:
- Snow, hail, rain, fog, condensation, and dew
- Design ways to **trap and store moisture** in dry climates
- Design ways to **dispose of excess moisture** in wet climates

### Light Availability
- Note light availability, especially on foggy coasts where **light becomes the limiting factor** for flowering plants

## Continental and Maritime Effects

- **Continental climates** produce more temperature extremes
- **Maritime climates** buffer severe heat or cold through oceanic moderation
- Account for both when selecting species and designing structures

## Altitude Considerations

- Every 100 m of altitude ≈ 1° of latitude
- A property containing both hills and flats can support a **wide variety of plants**
- In the subtropics, even temperate-area plants can be grown on **high islands or hills**
