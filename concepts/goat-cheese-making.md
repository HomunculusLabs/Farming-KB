# Goat Cheese Making

Goat milk cheese (chevre) is one of the most accessible and rewarding homestead dairy products. Goat milk is naturally homogenized (smaller fat globules), making it easier to curdle and produce a creamy cheese. This guide covers the fundamental cheeses that can be made at home: fresh chevre, feta, and aged gouda.

## Milk Quality Fundamentals

The quality of cheese depends entirely on the quality of the milk.

-  **Freshness** — Use milk within 24-48 hours of milking. The closer to milking, the better the cheese quality.
-  **Handling** — Chill milk to 4 C (40 F) within 1 hour of milking. Strain through a fine-mesh filter to remove debris and hair.
-  **Sanitation** — All equipment (pots, spoons, molds, cheesecloth) must be sanitized before use. Use a dairy sanitizer or a bleach solution (2 tablespoons per gallon), rinse thoroughly.
-  **Milk source** — Cheese works best with milk that has not been ultra-pasteurized (UHT). Raw milk or normally pasteurized milk produces superior cheese. See [[raw-milk-guide]] for handling raw milk safely.
-  **Avoid off-flavors** — Strong-flavored feed (cabbage, onions, wild garlic) can taint the milk and cheese. Feed grain or quality hay before milking if goats have access to browse with strong flavors.

## Essential Equipment

-  **Stainless steel pot** — 8-12 quart, non-reactive. Avoid aluminum.
-  **Dairy thermometer** — Accurate from 0-110 C (32-230 F). A digital probe thermometer works.
-  **Cheesecloth or butter muslin** — For draining curds. Butter muslin (tighter weave) is preferred for soft cheeses.
-  **Colander** — For draining curds over a pot or sink.
-  **Cheese molds** — For shaping. Commercial molds or DIY from PVC pipe with drainage holes.
-  **Long knife** — For cutting curds. A pastry knife or long-bladed knife.
-  **Slotted spoon** — For ladling curds.
-  **pH meter or strips** — Optional but highly recommended for consistent results.
-  **Cheese press** — Required for hard cheeses (gouda). See [[homestead-dairy-equipment]].

## Chevre (Fresh Goat Cheese)

Chevre is the simplest and most popular goat cheese. Ready to eat in 12-24 hours.

### Ingredients

- 4 liters (1 gallon) goat milk
- 1 packet mesophilic culture (MA 4001 or similar)
- 1-2 drops liquid rennet (or 1/4 rennet tablet diluted in 1/4 cup cool water)
- 1-2 teaspoons non-iodized salt (to taste)

### Process

1.  **Heat milk** to 22 C (72 F) in the stainless steel pot
2.  **Add culture** — Sprinkle the mesophilic culture over the surface. Let rehydrate for 2 minutes, then stir gently for 30 seconds
3.  **Add rennet** — Stir the diluted rennet into the milk with 20 gentle up-and-down strokes. Do not over-stir
4.  **Ripen** — Cover and let sit at room temperature (20-22 C) for 12-18 hours. The milk will set into a soft curd with a layer of whey on top
5.  **Ladle curds** — Gently ladle the curd into cheesecloth-lined colander. Do not pour — pouring breaks the delicate curd
6.  **Drain** — Let drain for 6-12 hours at room temperature, or 12-24 hours in the refrigerator
7.  **Salt and flavor** — Unwrap the curd, add salt to taste (start with 1 teaspoon per gallon), and mix gently. Add herbs, garlic, pepper, or honey if desired
8.  **Shape** — Form into logs, balls, or press into molds. Refrigerate and consume within 1-2 weeks

### Yield

1 gallon of goat milk produces approximately 450-600 g (1-1.3 lbs) of chevre.

## Feta

Feta is a brined cheese with a tangy, salty flavor. It stores well and improves with age in the brine.

### Ingredients

- 4 liters (1 gallon) goat milk
- 1 packet mesophilic culture (MA 4001)
- 1/2 teaspoon liquid rennet diluted in 1/4 cup cool water
- 2 tablespoons non-iodized salt for brine
- 1/2 cup non-iodized salt for dry salting

### Process

1.  **Heat milk** to 30 C (86 F)
2.  **Add culture** — Sprinkle and stir as for chevre
3.  **Add rennet** — Stir gently, cover, and let set at 30 C for 45-60 minutes until a clean break is achieved (cut the curd with a knife; it should separate cleanly with whey filling the gap)
4.  **Cut curds** — Cut into 1.3 cm (1/2 inch) cubes. Let rest 5 minutes
5.  **Cook curds** — Slowly raise temperature to 36 C (97 F) over 20 minutes, stirring gently every few minutes
6.  **Drain** — Ladle curds into cheesecloth-lined colander. Drain for 2 hours
7.  **Salt** — Cut the drained curd into blocks (5x5x10 cm). Place blocks on a draining mat and salt all surfaces with 1/2 cup salt total. Let drain 24 hours at room temperature, flipping and re-salting every 8 hours
8.  **Brine** — Make a saturated brine (dissolve salt in water until no more dissolves, approximately 250 g per liter). Submerge cheese blocks in brine and refrigerate
9.  **Age** — Feta improves with 1-4 weeks in the brine. It keeps for 3-6 months refrigerated

## Gouda (Aged Hard Cheese)

Gouda is a washed-curd cheese that requires more equipment and time but produces an excellent aged cheese.

### Ingredients

- 8 liters (2 gallons) goat milk
- 1 packet thermophilic culture (TA 61 or similar)
- 1/4 teaspoon liquid rennet diluted in 1/4 cup cool water
- 2 tablespoons non-iodized salt
- Cheese wax or vacuum sealer for aging

### Process

1.  **Heat milk** to 32 C (90 F)
2.  **Add culture** — Sprinkle thermophilic culture, rehydrate 2 minutes, stir
3.  **Ripen** — Cover and hold at 32 C for 30 minutes
4.  **Add rennet** — Stir gently, cover, hold at 32 C for 45-60 minutes until clean break
5.  **Cut curds** — Cut to 1.3 cm cubes. Rest 5 minutes
6.  **Cook** — Raise temperature to 38 C (100 F) over 30 minutes, stirring gently. Hold at 38 C for 15 minutes
7.  **Wash curds** — Drain off 1/3 of the whey. Replace with equal volume of 38 C water. This washing reduces acidity and creates the sweet, nutty Gouda flavor
8.  **Drain** — Pour curds into a cheesecloth-lined colander. Drain 10 minutes
9.  **Press** — Place curds in a cheese mold lined with cheesecloth. Press at 10 lb (4.5 kg) for 20 minutes, flip, press at 20 lb (9 kg) for 20 minutes, flip, press at 50 lb (23 kg) for 12 hours
10. **Brine** — Submerge pressed cheese in saturated brine for 12 hours per pound of cheese
11. **Dry** — Air-dry the cheese at 10-12 C (50-54 F) and 80-85% humidity for 2-4 weeks. Turn daily
12. **Wax or vacuum seal** — Apply cheese wax in 2-3 thin coats, or vacuum seal
13. **Age** — Age at 10-13 C (50-55 F) and 80-85% humidity. Minimum 2 months; 4-12 months for full flavor development. Turn weekly

### Yield

2 gallons of goat milk produces approximately 900-1100 g (2-2.4 lbs) of gouda.

## Cultures and Rennet

### Starter Cultures

-  **Mesophilic** — Work at 20-30 C. Used for chevre, feta, cottage cheese. Common strains: MA 4001, MM 100, Flora Danica.
-  **Thermophilic** — Work at 35-45 C. Used for gouda, cheddar, parmesan. Common strains: TA 61, LH 100, Thermophilic B.
-  **Direct-set (DVI)** — Single-use packets added directly to milk. Most convenient for beginners.
-  **Mother culture** — Perpetuated from batch to batch. More economical for frequent cheesemakers but requires sterile technique.

### Rennet

-  **Animal rennet** — Traditional, from calf stomach. Strongest coagulation. Preferred for aged cheeses.
-  **Vegetable rennet** — From cardoon thistle or fig sap. Weaker coagulation. Required for vegetarian cheese.
-  **Microbial rennet** — Fermentation-produced. Consistent, affordable, vegetarian. Good all-purpose choice.
-  **Junket tablets** — Available at grocery stores. Weak but work for soft cheeses.

### Storage

- Cultures: Freeze in sealed packets. Last 1-2 years frozen.
- Liquid rennet: Refrigerate. Lasts 6-12 months. Check strength before use — old rennet produces weak curds.

## Troubleshooting

-  **Curd won't set** — Check rennet freshness, verify milk temperature, ensure milk wasn't UHT pasteurized
-  **Bitter cheese** — Too much rennet, excess moisture in curd, or insufficient aging
