# Old Rice Variety Yields and the Critique of Scientific Agriculture

One of the most provocative practical claims in *The Road Back to Nature* is
Fukuoka's report of growing an old Tokugawa-era glutinous rice variety using

## The Experiment with Tokugawa-Era Glutinous Rice

Fukuoka grew an old variety of glutinous rice that he believed dated to the
Tokugawa Period (1600-1868). His purpose was twofold:

1. To determine the yields obtainable with an heirloom variety rather than the
   new high-yielding varieties developed by modern breeders
2. To evaluate whether the old rice tasted good, testing the common assumption
   that newer varieties are superior in flavor

The results were striking. When he examined the rice, the grain count per head
averaged 250 grains. Based on his calculations — which he reported checking
and rechecking — this rice could yield up to 50 bushels per quarter-acre. Since
the top theoretical yield of rice grown under open sun has been calculated at
50 to 70 bushels, his naturally-farmed heirloom rice was approaching the
theoretical maximum.

## The Growing Method

The method Fukuoka used for this rice was the same he had employed for
twenty-two or twenty-three years:
