# Mushroom Polysaccharide Chemistry and Anti-Cancer Compounds

The main anti-tumour compounds isolated from [[comparison-chaga-vs-turkey-tail-medicinal-mushrooms-comprehensive]] are
water-soluble polysaccharides with remarkable structural diversity. Understanding
their chemistry is essential for explaining their biological activity and
optimizing extraction methods. Research in this field has produced several
clinically approved pharmaceuticals, primarily in Japan and China.

## Polysaccharide Structural Diversity

Polysaccharides are structurally diverse biological macromolecules -- polymers
of monosaccharide residues joined by glycosidic linkages. They differ from
proteins and nucleic acids in having the greatest potential for structural
variability. While four amino acids can form only 24 different permutations,
four different sugar monosaccharides could form up to 35,560 unique
tetrasaccharides. This enormous variability allows precise regulatory mechanisms
for cell-cell interactions in higher organisms.

## Types of Anti-Tumour Polysaccharides

Anti-tumour polysaccharides from mushrooms occur as: water-soluble beta-D-
glucans; beta-D-glucans with heterosaccharide chains of xylose, mannose,
galactose, or uronic acid; and beta-D-glucan-protein complexes (proteoglycans).
Protein-linked glucans generally have greater immunopotential activity than
corresponding glucans alone.

## Key Structural Features Affecting Activity

Anti-cancer activity levels relate to molecular weight, degree of branching,
solubility in water, and glycosidic linkage types. The most pharmacologically
active forms are typically beta-(1,3)-D-glucans with beta-(1,6)-D branches.
See beta-glucan [[tihkal-receptor-binding-serotonin]] for details on
how these structures interact with immune cells.

## Major Clinically Relevant Compounds

### Lentinan (Lentinus edodes / Shiitake)

Pure polysaccharide containing only carbon, oxygen, and hydrogen. A beta-1,3
glucan with beta-1,6 branches. Effective only by intravenous or intraperitoneal
administration due to large molecular size.

### Schizophyllan (Schizophyllum commune)

Beta(1,3)-D glucan with beta-(1,6)-D glucan side-chains. Also known as
Sonifilan or Sizofiran. Large molecules administered intramuscularly or
intraperitoneally. See [[schizophyllum-commune-schizophyllan-cancer|Schizophyllan research]] for clinical data.

### PSK (Trametes versicolor / Turkey Tail)

A polysaccharide-protein complex (proteoglycan) isolated in Japan in the late
1960s. Molecular weight approximately 100 kDa. Contains fucose. Marketed as
Krestin, approved as an adjuvant cancer therapy in Japan since the 1970s.

### PSP (Trametes versicolor)

Similar to PSK but lacks fucose and contains arabinose and rhamnose instead.
Developed in China from 1983. Used extensively in Chinese hospitals as
complementary cancer treatment.

### Grifron-D (Grifola frondosa / Maitake)

Highly purified beta-1,6 glucan branched with beta-1,3 linkage. Notably orally
bioavailable, distinguishing it from Lentinan and Schizophyllan.

### AHCC (Proprietary Blend)

Low molecular weight alpha-1,3 glucan structures -- contrasting with the
beta-glucans of other compounds. Retains tumour-static activity and is one of
the most commercially successful medicinal mushroom supplements worldwide.

## Extraction and Purification

Hot water extraction is the traditional method aligned with Chinese medicine
preparation. The first definitive anti-cancer studies in the late 1960s by
Ikekawa et al. and Chihara et al. demonstrated that active compounds were
easily extracted with hot water. See [[medicinal-mushroom-compound-extraction| extraction methods]] for practical procedures.

### Source Materials

Anti-tumour polysaccharides can be derived from fruit-bodies (generally highest
level and variety), submerged cultured mycelial biomass, or [[comparison-liquid-culture-vs-grain-spawn]]
broth. Reshetnikov et al. (2001) listed 650 species from 182 genera containing
pharmacologically active polysaccharides.

### Oral Bioavailability Challenge

Large glucan molecules like Lentinan and Schizophyllan are only effective by
IV/IP administration. However, acid treatment of beta-1,6 glucan from Agaricus
blazei produced approximately 10 kDa fragments that were orally active in mice,
suggesting a path to improved oral bioavailability for other large beta-glucans.

## Commercial Production in Japan

Several polysaccharide anti-tumour agents have been developed commercially:
Lentinan (multiple manufacturers), Schizophyllan/Sonifilan (multiple Japanese
pharmaceutical companies), PSK/Krestin (Kureha Chemical Industry), and
Grifron-D (Yukiguni Maitake Co.).
