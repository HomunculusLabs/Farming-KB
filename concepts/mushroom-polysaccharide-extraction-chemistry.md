# Mushroom Polysaccharide Chemistry and Anti-Cancer Compounds

The main anti-tumour compounds isolated from medicinal mushrooms are water-soluble polysaccharides with remarkable structural diversity. Understanding their chemistry is essential for explaining their biological activity and optimizing extraction methods.

## Polysaccharide Structural Diversity

Polysaccharides are structurally diverse biological macromolecules composed of repetitive structural features -- polymers of monosaccharide residues joined by glycosidic linkages. They differ from proteins and nucleic acids in having the greatest potential for structural variability.

Key structural principle: while four amino acids can form only 24 different permutations, four different sugar monosaccharides could form up to 35,560 unique tetrasaccharides. This enormous variability allows precise regulatory mechanisms for cell-cell interactions in higher organisms.

## Types of Anti-Tumour Polysaccharides

Anti-tumour polysaccharides from mushrooms occur as:

1. Water-soluble beta-D-glucans
2. Beta-D-glucans with heterosaccharide chains of xylose, mannose, galactose, or uronic acid
3. Beta-D-glucan-protein complexes (proteoglycans)

Protein-linked glucans generally have greater immunopotential activity than corresponding glucans alone.

## Key Structural Features Affecting Activity

Anti-cancer activity levels relate to:
- Molecular weight
- Degree of branching
- Solubility in water
- Glycosidic linkage types: (1-3), (1-6)-beta-glucans and (1-3)-alpha-glucans

## Major Clinically Relevant Compounds

### Lentinan (Lentinus edodes)
Pure polysaccharide containing only carbon, oxygen, and hydrogen. A beta-1,3 glucan with beta-1,6 branches. Effective only by intravenous or intraperitoneal administration due to large molecular size.

### Schizophyllan (Schizophyllum commune)
Beta(1,3)-D glucan with beta-(1,6)-D glucan side-chains. Also known as Sonifilan, Sizofiran. Large molecules administered intramuscularly or intraperitoneally.

### PSK (Trametes versicolor)
A polysaccharide-protein complex (proteoglycan) isolated in Japan in the late 1960s. Molecular weight approximately 100 kDa. Contains fucose in its structure.

### PSP (Trametes versicolor)
Similar to PSK but lacks fucose and instead contains arabinose and rhamnose. Developed in China from 1983.

### Grifron-D (Grifola frondosa)
Highly purified beta-1,6 glucan branched with beta-1,3 linkage. Orally bioavailable, distinguishing it from Lentinan and Schizophyllan.

### AHCC (Proprietary)
Low molecular weight alpha-1,3 glucan structures -- contrasting with the beta-glucans of other compounds. Should theoretically have lower immunopotentiating activity but retains tumour-static activity.

## Extraction and Purification

Hot water extraction is the traditional method aligned with Chinese medicine preparation. The first definitive anti-cancer studies in the late 1960s by Ikekawa et al. and Chihara et al. demonstrated that active compounds were easily extracted with hot water.

### Source Materials
Anti-tumour polysaccharides can be derived from:
- Fruit-bodies (generally highest level and variety)
- Submerged cultured mycelial biomass
- Liquid culture broth

Reshetnikov et al. (2001) listed 650 species and 7 intraspecific taxa from 182 genera of higher Hetero- and Homo-basidiomycetes containing pharmacologically active polysaccharides.

### Oral Bioavailability Challenge
Large glucan molecules like Lentinan and Schizophyllan are only effective by IV/IP administration. However, acid treatment of beta-1,6 glucan from Agaricus blazei produced ~10 kDa molecular mass fragments that were orally active in mice -- suggesting a path to improved oral bioavailability for other large beta-glucans.

## Commercial Production in Japan

Several polysaccharide anti-tumour agents have been developed commercially in Japan:
- Lentinan (multiple manufacturers)
- Schizophyllan/Sonifilan (multiple Japanese pharmaceutical companies)
- PSK/Krestin (Kureha Chemical Industry)
- Grifron-D (Yukiguni Maitake Co.)
