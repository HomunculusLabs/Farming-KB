# Phylogenetic Species Recognition in Fungi

## Overview

Phylogenetic species recognition (PSR) uses DNA sequence data from multiple loci to identify genetically isolated lineages — offering a more objective and reproducible alternative to traditional morphological species concepts. PSR has revealed extensive cryptic speciation in fungi, where morphologically identical populations represent distinct evolutionary lineages. Multi-locus sequence typing (MLST) and multi-locus microsatellite typing (MLMT) are the primary tools, and their application has reshaped our understanding of [[fungal-biodiversity]], biogeography, and emerging infectious diseases.

## Multi-Locus Sequence Typing (MLST)

### Principle

- Sequence multiple unlinked DNA regions (typically 5–7 gene fragments) from individual fungal isolates.
- Identify groups of individuals that share identical or nearly identical sequences at all loci.
- These groups represent **phylogenetic species** — independently evolving lineages.

### Advantages Over Single-Locus Approaches

- Single gene trees can be misleading due to incomplete lineage sorting, horizontal gene transfer, or gene duplication.
- Concordance across multiple unlinked loci provides strong evidence for species boundaries.
- MLST schemes gain power as more researchers contribute data.

## Multi-Locus Microsatellite Typing (MLMT)

### Principle

- Microsatellites are highly polymorphic repetitive DNA sequences.
- Multiple microsatellite loci are genotyped across isolates.

### Caveats

- **Single microsatellites can be misleading** due to hypervariability and attendant homoplasy (convergent evolution of repeat number).
- Demonstrated in both *Coccidioides* (Fisher et al., 2000) and *Neurospora* (Dettman & Taylor, 2004).
- **Best employed to extend multi-locus sequence studies from species into populations** — not for primary species recognition.
- If aiming to accomplish species recognition and population characterization simultaneously, **many microsatellites should be used** (Fisher et al. used 20 in their study of *[[penicillium-marneffei]]*).

## Challenging Morphological Species Recognition

### *Neurospora* — A Model System

*Neurospora* has been central to developing and testing PSR:

- **929 crosses (1858 matings)** between outbreeding *Neurospora* species were performed to test biological species recognition (BSR).
- A reproductive success rating of 6 was used to delineate biological species.
- **Phylogenetic species often correspond to biological species**, confirming the utility of PSR.
- **Hybridization is rare** in *Neurospora*, in contrast to agriculturally important fungi where hybridization is more common.

### *Aspergillus flavus*

- PSR with five DNA regions found **at least four species-level clades** in what was thought to be one morphological species (Geiser et al., 1998).
- Once phylogenetic species are identified, new phenotypes correlating with genetically isolated groups can be discovered.

### *Coccidioides*

- MLST and MLMT have been applied to distinguish *C. posadasii* from *C. immitis*.
- Population structure and biogeography can be resolved using these tools.

## *Cryptococcus gattii* — Biogeography and Emergence

### The VGIIa Outbreak

- The *C. gattii* outbreak in the Pacific Northwest of North America involves primarily the VGIIa molecular type.
- If the clade originated from a **single Latin American A individual**, it must have existed for at least **1.7 million years** (Kasuga et al., 2003).
- If it originated by transportation of several genetically different Latin American individuals to Europe, the date could be much more recent.

### Speculation on Introduction

- The largest assemblage of clonal individuals from horses and equids is found in the **Eurasian clade**.
- It is "not completely ludicrous to speculate" that the fungus was brought from Latin America to Eurasia by animals owned by the **first European explorers of the New World**, ~500 years ago.
- If a variety of genotypes were brought across the Atlantic on horses or donkeys, there is no evidence they persisted.

## A Posteriori Correlation of Phenotype and Genotype

### The Pattern

1. Traditional morphological concepts are often proven wrong when phylogenetic data become available.
2. Once phylogenetic species are defined, **new phenotypes correlating with genetically isolated groups** can be found.

### Significance

- PSR does not discard morphology — it redefines which morphological characters are phylogenetically informative.
- Enables the discovery of **cryptic species** with distinct ecological, pathological, or biochemical properties.
- Facilitates accurate species identification in clinical, agricultural, and environmental contexts.

## Applications

| Field | Application |
|---|---|
| Medical mycology | Identifying pathogenic species, tracking outbreaks, understanding emergence |
| Plant pathology | Recognizing host-specialized pathogen lineages, tracking crop disease spread |
| Ecology | Resolving fungal community composition, understanding biogeography |
| Biosecurity | Detecting introduced or emerging fungal pathogens |
| Conservation | Assessing fungal biodiversity in threatened ecosystems |

## Limitations

- Requires culturing (though environmental DNA approaches are emerging).
- Multiple loci must be sequenced — more expensive than single-gene barcoding.
- Incomplete lineage sorting can complicate recently diverged species.
- Horizontal gene transfer can create conflicting phylogenetic signals.
