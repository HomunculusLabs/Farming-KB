# Schizophyllan Research

Schizophyllan (also known as Sonifilan, SPG, or Sizofiran) is a β-(1→3)-D-glucan with β-(1→6) branches produced by the fungus Schizophyllum commune. It was the third mushroom-derived polysaccharide approved for clinical use in Japan, following [[lentinan-pre-clinical-antitumour-models]] and [[psk-psp-polysaccharide-peptides]].

## Chemistry

- **Source**: Schizophyllum commune (a common wood-decomposing bracket fungus)
- **Type**: extracellular β-(1→3)-D-glucan with β-(1→6) branching
- **Molecular weight**: ~450-600 kDa (native)
- **Production**: produced by submerged fermentation of S. commune, extracted from culture broth
- **Structure**: triple helix in aqueous solution; denatures to single strand in DMSO
- **Trade names**: Sonifilan, Sizofiran, SPG
- **Producer**: Taito Co. and Kaken Pharmaceutical (Japan)

## Immunomodulatory Activities

Schizophyllan demonstrates broad immune-enhancing properties:
- Potent activation of macrophages and enhancement of phagocytosis
- Activation of the alternative complement pathway
- Enhancement of NK cell activity
- Stimulation of cytokine production (IL-1, IL-2, TNF-α, IFN-γ)
- Promotion of T-cell proliferation and differentiation
- Activation of Langerhans cells (important for cervical cancer response)

## Clinical Trials

### Cervical Cancer

The most extensively studied indication:
- Randomized controlled trial with radiotherapy: Schizophyllan significantly prolonged overall survival of Stage II cervical cancer patients
- Stage III patients did not show significant benefit (possibly due to more advanced disease burden)
- Intratumoral injection produced significant infiltration of Langerhans cells and T-cells into the tumour

### Head and Neck Cancers

- Schizophyllan demonstrated increased overall survival in clinical studies
- Combination with radiotherapy and chemotherapy showed additive effects

### Gastric Cancer

- Early study: combination with tegafur or mitomycin C + 5-FU in 367 patients showed significant increase in median survival
- However, a similar follow-up study failed to confirm this result
- Subgroup analysis showed patients with >10% activated CD4+ cells and >25% activated CD8+ cells had highly significant survival benefit

### Large Combination Trial

A prospective randomized trial of 312 patients treated with various combinations of surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy (fluorouracil), and Schizophyllan found:
- Patients receiving Schizophyllan had better overall survival
- The variety of treatment regimens reduced statistical power
- Subgroup analyses were more informative than the overall result

## Administration

- **Route**: subcutaneous injection or intratumoral injection
- **Dosage**: typically 20-40 mg, 2-3 times per week
- **Duration**: maintenance therapy concurrent with other treatments
- **Commercial status**: produced by several Japanese pharmaceutical companies

## Significance

Schizophyllan's clinical history illustrates both the promise and challenges of mushroom-derived immunomodulators:
- Evidence of immune activation is robust across multiple systems
- Clinical benefit appears strongest when patients retain baseline immune competence
- Patient selection (immune status as a biomarker) may be critical for clinical success
- The mixed gastric cancer trial results highlight the need for well-designed, adequately powered studies

## Mechanism of Action: Molecular Details

Schizophyllan's immunomodulatory effects are mediated through specific receptor interactions:
- **Dectin-1 receptor**: Schizophyllan binds to Dectin-1 (a C-type lectin receptor) on macrophages and dendritic cells, triggering Syk/CARD9 signaling cascades that activate NF-κB and production of pro-inflammatory cytokines
- **TLR-2 co-stimulation**: Concurrent engagement of TLR-2 synergizes with Dectin-1 signaling, amplifying the immune response through MyD88-dependent pathways
- **Complement receptor 3 (CR3)**: Schizophyllan binds to CR3 on NK cells and neutrophils, enhancing antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) and promoting tumor cell lysis
- **Langerhans cell activation**: In cervical cancer specifically, schizophyllan promotes the migration and activation of Langerhans cells (skin-resident dendritic cells) that infiltrate the tumor microenvironment and present tumor antigens to T-cells, bridging innate and adaptive immune responses

## Pharmacokinetics

Understanding schizophyllan's behavior in the body is essential for clinical application:
- **Absorption**: After subcutaneous injection, schizophyllan is absorbed slowly into the systemic circulation, maintaining detectable serum levels for 48-72 hours post-injection
- **Distribution**: The polysaccharide accumulates preferentially in reticuloendothelial tissues — liver, spleen, and lymph nodes — consistent with its immune-targeting mechanism
- **Metabolism**: Schizophyllan is not metabolized by human enzymes. It is gradually broken down by macrophage phagocytosis and lysosomal degradation
- **Elimination**: Excretion occurs primarily through the kidneys as smaller degradation fragments. The elimination half-life ranges from 4-6 hours for intact molecules, though immune effects persist well beyond detectable serum levels

## Adverse Effects and Safety Profile

Schizophyllan has a favorable safety profile compared to conventional cancer therapies:
- **Local reactions**: Mild injection-site erythema, induration, or pruritus occurs in approximately 10-15% of patients. These reactions are self-limiting and do not require treatment discontinuation
- **Systemic reactions**: Transient fever, chills, or fatigue may occur within hours of injection, typically resolving within 24 hours
- **No bone marrow suppression**: Unlike chemotherapy, schizophyllan does not cause myelosuppression, making it suitable for combination regimens where maintaining immune cell counts is critical
- **No hepatotoxicity or nephrotoxicity**: Liver and kidney function tests remain stable during schizophyllan treatment, even with prolonged administration over months

## Current Research Directions

- **Nanotechnology drug delivery**: Schizophyllan's triple helix can encapsulate hydrophobic molecules, making it a candidate for targeted drug delivery systems. Its natural affinity for Dectin-1 receptors on immune cells allows selective delivery to immune tissues
- **Conjugate vaccines**: Schizophyllan-protein conjugates are being investigated as vaccine adjuvants, where the polysaccharide enhances antigen presentation and immune memory formation
- **Wound healing**: Topical schizophyllan accelerates wound closure in animal models by promoting macrophage-mediated tissue remodeling and collagen deposition
