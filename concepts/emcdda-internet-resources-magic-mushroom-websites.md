# EMCDDA Internet Resources and Magic Mushroom Information Websites

## Overview

The emergence of the internet as a major information resource for psychoactive
substance users was a defining feature of the late 1990s and early 2000s. The EMCDDA
thematic paper on hallucinogenic mushrooms (2006) documented the extensive network
of websites that had developed specifically to provide information on magic
mushrooms, including identification guides, cultivation instructions, dosage
information, legal resources, trip reports, and community forums. This online
ecosystem played a significant role in the diffusion of knowledge about
hallucinogenic mushrooms across Europe and globally.

## Major International Resources

The EMCDDA identified several major US-based websites that served as comprehensive
repositories of information on hallucinogenic mushrooms:

- **Erowid**: Perhaps the most well-known psychoactive substance information
  resource globally. Erowid provided detailed information on mushroom species,
  effects, dosages, chemistry, trip reports, and legal status across multiple
  jurisdictions. It served as a primary reference for both casual users and
  researchers.
- **Lycaeum**: An early online community and information resource focused on
  psychoactive plants and fungi. Alongside Erowid, Lycaeum was one of the
  foundational internet resources for entheogenic knowledge.
- **Mycotopia**: A dedicated mycological community website focused on mushroom
  cultivation, identification, and discussion.
- **Shroomery**: One of the largest and most active online communities dedicated
  to [[forensic-identification-methods-psilocybin-mushrooms-tlc-gc-hplc]], featuring extensive cultivation guides, identification
  resources, and user forums.
- **MushroomJohn**: A specialized website providing information on mushroom
  species and their effects.
- **The Entheogen Review**: A publication and website covering a range of
  entheogenic substances including hallucinogenic mushrooms.

## European National and Regional Websites

In addition to the major international resources, the EMCDDA documented numerous
country-specific websites that focused on magic mushrooms in their local and
regional contexts. These websites reflected the linguistic and cultural diversity
of European mushroom interest:

- **Copenhagen Mushroom Link** (mushroom.dk) — Denmark
- **Champis** (champis.fr.tc) — France
- **Daath** (daath.hu) — Hungary
- **Delysid** (gratisweb.com/delysid) — Spain
- **Enteogeneos** (enteogeneos.com.sapo.pt) — Portugal
- **Kouzelné houbičky** (drogy.jinak.cz/houbicky) — Czech Republic
- **Norshroom** (norshroom.org) — Norway
- **Planetahongo** (planetahongo.tripod.com) — Spain
- **Shroommap** (shroommap.tk) — United Kingdom
- **Svampinfo** (knarkkorven.magiskamolekyler.org/svampinfo) — Sweden
- **Taikasieniforum** (trippi.info/taikasieniforum) — Finland

## Content and Features

These websites typically provided a range of information and community features:

**Educational Content**: Most sites offered detailed information on the effects of
hallucinogenic mushrooms, recommended dosages, chemistry of active compounds
(psilocybin and psilocin), taxonomic classification of mushroom species, and legal
status in various jurisdictions. This information served both harm reduction and
educational purposes.

**Trip Reports**: Many websites hosted collections of user-submitted experience
reports, allowing visitors to read about the effects of different mushroom species,
dosage levels, and set and setting considerations. The website magic-mushrooms.net
was specifically cited as an example of a site hosting trip reports.

**Cultivation Guides**: Some sites provided detailed instructions on how to
cultivate hallucinogenic mushrooms at home, including techniques derived from the
PF TEK method and other cultivation approaches. This information contributed to
the availability of mushrooms beyond natural sources.

**Photo Galleries**: Visual identification resources including photographs of
various [[emcdda-hallucinogenic-mushroom-species-classification-and-chemistry]] in their natural habitats, aiding in field
identification for foragers.

**Community Features**: Discussion forums, events listings, and other community
tools enabled visitors to share personal experiences, cultivation tips,
information about local mushroom seasons, and supply channel information.

**Legal Information**: Many sites included updates on the evolving legal status of
hallucinogenic mushrooms in different countries, particularly relevant during the
period when several European nations were moving to schedule or restrict mushroom
sales.

## Role in the Emerging Trend

The EMCDDA identified the internet as one of the key [[emcdda-hallucinogenic-mushroom-contributing-factors-megatrends]] to the
emergence of hallucinogenic mushrooms as a drug trend in Europe. The rapid
expansion of internet sales and information was noted as facilitating interest in
natural hallucinogens, alongside the "return to nature" trend of the late 1990s.
The availability of online information reduced barriers to knowledge about
[[psilocybin-mushroom-identification-guide]], effects, and acquisition.

The relationship between online information and the smartshop market was
particularly notable. Websites provided complementary information to products
available through retail channels, and online forums allowed users to share
experiences with specific products available in smartshops. The internet also
facilitated the development of an online market for mushroom products, with
the UK Mixmag 2005 survey reporting that 67% of respondents found mushrooms less
available after legal restrictions were imposed, suggesting the internet had
been a significant supply channel.

## Impact on Harm Reduction

The online ecosystem of mushroom information resources had both positive and
negative implications for public health. On the positive side, these websites
provided accurate dosage information, species identification guidance, and
experience reports that could help users make informed decisions and avoid
dangerous misidentifications. On the negative side, cultivation guides and
supply channel information could facilitate access to psychoactive substances.

The EMCDDA noted that the information available on these websites was generally
more detailed and accurate than information available through traditional drug
education channels, creating a complex dynamic where informal online resources
served as primary sources of drug information for many users.

## Historical Significance

The period documented by the EMCDDA (late 1990s to mid-2000s) represents a
transitional phase in drug information culture. The emergence of dedicated
mushroom websites predated and paralleled the broader social media revolution,
representing an early example of peer-to-peer health information sharing on the
internet. Many of the websites listed by the EMCDDA have since evolved, merged,
or been replaced by newer platforms, but their role in establishing the online
knowledge base for psychoactive mushroom information remains historically
significant.
