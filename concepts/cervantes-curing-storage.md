# Curing and Storage

Curing is the slow drying and aging process that transforms freshly harvested cannabis into smooth, flavorful, potent medicine. Proper curing breaks down chlorophyll, converts non-psychoactive cannabinoids into active forms, and develops complex flavors and aromas. Storage preserves these qualities for long-term use.


## Drying

After harvest, buds must be dried slowly to preserve potency and prevent mold. Hang manicured branches or place buds on drying racks in a dark, well-ventilated room. Maintain temperature at 60-70 degrees F (15-21 degrees C) and humidity at 45-55 percent. A small oscillating fan provides air circulation without blowing directly on buds. Drying typically takes 5-10 days depending on bud size, humidity, and temperature.

Drying too fast (high heat, low humidity) makes buds brittle and harsh-smoking. Drying too slowly (high humidity, poor ventilation) invites mold. The goal is slow, even drying that preserves trichomes and allows internal moisture to migrate to the surface gradually. Stems should snap rather than bend when buds are properly dry.


## Jar Curing (Burping)

Once buds are dry to the touch but still have slight internal moisture, transfer them to airtight glass jars. Fill jars 3/4 full to allow some air space. Seal jars and store in a cool, dark place. Open jars (burp) two to three times a day for the first week, allowing moisture to escape and fresh air to enter. Each burping session should last 10-15 minutes.

During the first few days of jar curing, you may notice that buds feel moist again on the surface. This is normal; internal moisture is redistributing. If buds feel soggy, leave jars open longer during burping. If buds feel overly dry, add a small humidity pack (such as Boveda) to the jar. Continue burping daily for 2-4 weeks, then reduce to once every few days.


## Curing Duration

Minimum curing time is two weeks for noticeable improvement. Optimal curing takes 4-8 weeks. Some connoisseurs cure for six months or longer for the smoothest, most complex flavor profiles. During curing, chlorophyll breaks down, reducing the harsh "green" taste. Non-psychoactive THCA converts to active THC through decarboxylation. Terpenes develop and mellow, creating more nuanced aromas.


## Monitoring During Cure

Check jars daily during the first two weeks of curing. Look for signs of mold (white fuzzy patches, musty smell) and address immediately if found. Buds should feel slightly springy when gently squeezed, not brittle or wet. The aroma should improve progressively as curing progresses. If a musty or ammonia smell develops, the buds are too moist and need more drying time before continuing the cure.


## Mold Prevention

Mold is the biggest enemy during curing and storage. Prevent mold by ensuring buds are adequately dry before jarring, burping jars regularly, and maintaining proper storage conditions. Keep storage area clean and free of mold spores. Do not crowd buds in jars. Use glass jars with rubber seals for the best airtight seal. Discard any buds that show signs of mold immediately; mold can spread to surrounding buds.

Temperature above 77 degrees F (25 degrees C) and humidity above 65 percent create conditions favorable for mold growth. Store curing jars in a cool, dark place with stable temperature. A closet or cabinet works well. Avoid storing near heat sources or in direct sunlight.


## Vacuum Sealing

Storing cannabis in an airtight environment will preserve aroma, taste, and potency. Use a vacuum sealer to evacuate air in glass jars. Inexpensive vacuum sealers are available but may lose vacuum after a few days. More expensive models work much better. When properly vacuum-packed, buds will stay as fresh as the day they were sealed.


## Refrigeration and Freezing

Vacuum-seal jars and place them in the refrigerator for long-term storage. Refrigeration slows decomposition. Remember that refrigerators have high humidity (about 65 percent), so containers must be sealed airtight. Do not place buds in the freezer; freezing draws moisture to the surface of buds, which can harm resin glands. If freezing is necessary for very long-term storage, vacuum-seal thoroughly first.


## Moisture Content

To find the approximate moisture content of dry buds, weigh a specific bud upon harvest when it is wet. Weigh it again during drying and curing. In general, a dry bud will weigh 75 percent less than its wet weight at harvest. Properly cured buds should have a moisture content of 8-12 percent. Too dry and buds crumble and lose flavor; too moist and mold risk increases.


## Long-Term Storage

For storage longer than six months, vacuum-seal in glass jars and keep in a cool, dark place. Some growers use food-grade moisture barrier bags with oxygen absorbers. Avoid plastic bags for long-term storage; they allow air exchange and can impart unwanted flavors. Cedar boxes provide a traditional storage method that may help repel insects. Always store away from light, heat, and moisture. Properly stored cannabis can maintain potency for one to two years.


## Effects of Improper Curing

Improperly cured cannabis has a harsh, chlorophyll-laden taste that burns the throat. Rapid drying creates brittle buds that crumble and lose potency. Too-slow drying promotes mold and ammonia-like odors. Buds that are not adequately dried before jarring will develop mold. Over-dried buds lose terpenes and become flavorless. Proper curing is the difference between mediocre and exceptional cannabis.


## Humidity Control Products

Two-way humidity control packs (such as Boveda) are invaluable during curing and storage. These packs maintain a specific relative humidity (62 percent is ideal for cannabis) by adding or removing moisture as needed. They prevent overdrying during storage and help maintain consistent curing conditions. Replace packs every 2-3 months or when they become rigid.


## Vacuum Sealing for Long-Term Storage

For storage periods exceeding a few months, vacuum sealing is an excellent option. Vacuum-sealed bags remove oxygen, which is the primary enemy of cannabinoids and terpenes during storage. Store vacuum-sealed cannabis in a cool, dark place such as a refrigerator or cellar. Avoid freezing cannabis, as trichomes can become brittle and break off when frozen buds are handled. For the best long-term preservation, combine vacuum sealing with humidity control packs to maintain ideal moisture content throughout extended storage.
