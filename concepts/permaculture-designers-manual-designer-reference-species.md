# Permaculture Designers Manual - Designer Reference Species

updated: 2026-04-18

The Designers Manual contains extensive species references scattered across all chapters. Rather than a single species list, species are presented in context: climate-specific planting tables, bee forage matrices, mulch trial results, windbreak compositions, and aquaculture stocking guides. This page aggregates the key species mentioned for rapid design reference.

## Bee Forage Species (Table 12.1)

Bee forage is tabulated by season and honey type for cool temperate climates:

| | Early Season | Mid Season | Late Season |
|---|---|---|---|
| Classic Honeys | Rosemary | Sage, thyme, catmint | Leatherwood (Eucryphia) |
| Bulk Honeys | Echium fastuosum | White clover, Acacia, Blackberry | Echium, Eucalypt |
| Special and Crop | Gooseberry | Small fruits | Mustard |

Additional classical honeys: pine tree, basswood (Tilia spp.), marsh tupelo (subtropics), citrus. Special pollens from Acacia and goldenwood may cause allergic reactions if inhaled but prevent them when eaten.

## Mulch Species and Crop Response (Table 10.1)

Mulch trial data from Okigbo and Lal (Montreal, 1978) for tropical crops:

- Legume wastes gave highest maize yield (4.3 t/ha vs 3.0 t/ha bare ground)
- Legume and rice husk mulches gave highest cassava yields (20.5 and 15.5 t/ha)
- Sawdust performed well for cassava (20.5 t/ha)
- All mulches outperformed bare ground for every crop tested
- Maize responded best to legume straws or water plant (Pistia) mulch
- Legumes themselves responded well to grass and sawdust mulch

Key mulch species: Pennisetum grass, elephant grass, millet straw (Panicum), legume wastes, rice husks, sawdust.

## Windbreak and Shelter Species

The manual recommends windbreaks composed of multiple layers:
- Outer windward row: hardy, tall evergreens (conifers in temperate zones)
- Middle rows: mixed deciduous and evergreen species
- Inner leeward rows: shorter, denser species for fine wind filtration
- Understorey: shrub species for wildlife habitat and ground-level protection

In temperate zones: mixed hardwoods and conifers for year-round shelter. In the tropics: bamboo, Casuarina, and mixed native species.

## Legume Species for Nitrogen Fixation

### Tree Legumes
- Acacia species (tropical and subtropical nitrogen fixers)
- Leucaena leucocephala (fast-growing tropical fodder and fuel)
- Albizia species (shade trees, nitrogen fixers)
- Gliricidia sepium (living fence posts, green manure, fodder)
- Robinia pseudoacacia (temperate black locust, fence posts, nectar)

### Pasture Legumes
- White clover (Trifolium repens) -- temperate pasture nitrogen fixer, bee forage
- Lucerne/alfalfa (Medicago sativa) -- deep-rooted drought-resistant fodder
- Lupins -- acid soil tolerant green manure
- Cowpeas (Vigna unguiculata) -- tropical green manure and food crop

### Shrub Legumes
- Caragana species -- cold-hardy windbreak and nitrogen fixer
- Cytisus (broom) -- temperate nitrogen-fixing shrub
- Indigofera -- tropical dye plant and nitrogen fixer

## Aquaculture Species (Chapter 13)

### Fish Species
- Carp (Cyprinus carpio) -- primary temperate pond fish, detrivore
- Tilapia -- tropical pond fish, rapid growth
- Trout -- cool-water species for high-quality streams
- Catfish -- warm-water scavenger species
- Perch -- predatory species for population control

### Aquatic Plants
- Duckweed (Lemna species) -- high-protein pig and poultry feed, rapid multiplication
- Water hyacinth (Eichhornia crassipes) -- water purification, livestock feed (caution: invasive)
- Water chestnut -- food crop for wetland edges
- Taro (Colocasia esculenta) -- padi and wetland staple
- Iris species -- pond edge water filtration

### Pond Margin Species
- Willow (Salix) -- water-retaining bank plantings
- Bamboo -- pond-edge stability
- rushes and reeds -- water purification, nutrient absorption

## Fuel Wood Species

### Temperate
- Oak (Quercus spp.) -- dense firewood, long coppice rotation
- Ash (Fraxinus spp.) -- excellent firewood, quick establishment
- Hazel (Corylus avellana) -- coppice poles, 7-15 year rotation
- Black locust (Robinia pseudoacacia) -- fast-growing, high heat value

### Tropical
- Leucaena leucocephala -- fast coppice, nitrogen fixer
- Casuarina equisetifolia -- coastal windbreak and fuel
- Gliricidia sepium -- multi-purpose fuel and fodder
- Acacia nilotica -- desert fuel species

## Key Design Trees Referenced

### Temperate
- Walnut (Juglans regia) -- high-value nut crop, timber
- Chestnut (Castanea sativa) -- carbohydrate staple tree crop
- Apple, pear, plum -- standard temperate orchard species
- Oak, ash, maple -- timber, fuel, wildlife habitat

### Tropical
- Banana/plantain (Musa) -- staple carbohydrate, shade for understorey
- Coconut (Cocos nucifera) -- multi-purpose: food, oil, timber, thatch
- Mango (Mangifera indica) -- fruit, shade, timber
- Avocado (Persea americana) -- fruit, windbreak
- Citrus species -- fruit, windbreak, bee forage

### Multi-Climate
- Ficus species (figs) -- food, shade, wildlife
- Morus species (mulberry) -- poultry and pig fodder, fruit, silk production
- Bamboo species -- building material, erosion control, windbreak
