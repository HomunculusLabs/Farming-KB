## Argyreia Nervosa (Hawaiian Baby Woodrose)

Argyreia nervosa is a perennial climbing vine native to the Indian subcontinent but now widely cultivated in tropical and subtropical regions worldwide. The plant produces large, heart-shaped leaves and attractive purple flowers, making it popular as an ornamental. The seeds are the primary psychoactive part, containing approximately 0.14% LSA by dry weight.

Five to ten seeds of Argyreia nervosa yield an average dose of LSA. The seeds are brown and woody in appearance. Commercially available seeds may be coated with pesticides or anti-fungal treatments that should be removed by washing before preparation.

Common preparation methods include grinding the seeds and soaking them in water, crushing the seeds and consuming the pulp directly, or performing a simple cold water extraction. Some users scrape off the fuzzy seed coating before grinding, believing it contains compounds that contribute to nausea.

The experience from Argyreia nervosa seeds is consistently described as more sedating and physically heavy than LSD. Nausea is common during the onset phase. Visual effects are generally mild, with more pronounced changes in thought patterns, emotional sensitivity, and sensory perception than in visual hallucination.

## Legal Status

LSA itself is a Schedule III controlled substance in the [[controlled-substances-act-and-psychedelic-research]]. However, the plants that contain LSA are generally legal to grow, possess, and sell. The seeds of Argyreia nervosa and Ipomoea species are widely available from garden suppliers and online vendors.

This legal paradox, where a controlled substance occurs naturally in an uncontrolled plant, creates a gray area. The seeds themselves are not scheduled, but extracting the LSA would constitute manufacturing a controlled substance. Most users who prepare LSA-containing seeds are technically in violation of the law, though prosecution for personal use of commonly available garden seeds is essentially unknown.
