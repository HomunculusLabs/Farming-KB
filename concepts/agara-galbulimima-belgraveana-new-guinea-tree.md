# Agara (Galbulimima belgraveana)

Agara is the name given to Galbulimima belgraveana, a tall forest tree of
Malaysia and Australia. In Papua New Guinea, natives prepare a drink by
boiling the leaves and bark with the leaves of ereriba (an undetermined
species of Homalomena). When consumed, this preparation causes violent
intoxication, eventually leading to a deep sleep during which the user
experiences visions and fantastic dreams. Some 28 alkaloids have been
isolated from this tree, and although they are biologically active, the
specific psychoactive principle remains unknown. Agara is one of four
species of Galbulimima and belongs to the Himantandraceae, a rare plant
family related to the magnolias.

## Geographic Distribution

Galbulimima belgraveana is a tall forest tree native to the rainforests of
Malaysia and Australia, with its most significant ethnobotanical use
documented in Papua New Guinea. The tree is one of four species in the
genus Galbulimima, all of which are found in [[tropical-rainforest-seedling-mycorrhiza-ecological-restoration]] habitats
in the Australasian region. The genus is distributed across New Guinea,
northeastern Australia, and parts of the Malay Archipelago, where it
occupies a place in the canopy or subcanopy of primary and secondary
rainforests.

The wide [[geographic-distribution-of-psilocybe-species]] Galbulimima across multiple countries
and indigenous cultural areas suggests that its psychoactive properties may
have been discovered independently by different peoples. However, detailed
documentation of traditional use outside of Papua New Guinea is limited,
and it is possible that the preparation described by Schultes represents
a localized tradition specific to the peoples of Papua New Guinea.

## Method of Preparation and Use

In Papua New Guinea, the traditional method of preparing the agara drink
involves boiling the leaves and bark of Galbulimima belgraveana together
with the leaves of ereriba, an undetermined species of Homalomena (family
Araceae). The use of an admixture plant is a common feature of traditional
psychoactive preparations worldwide, and the specific role of ereriba in the
agara preparation is unclear. Ereriba may contribute its own psychoactive
compounds, modify the effects of Galbulimima alkaloids, improve the taste
of the preparation, or serve a symbolic or ritualistic purpose.

The effects of the drink are described as beginning with violent
intoxication, followed by a deep sleep during which the user experiences
visions and fantastic dreams. This progression from initial agitation or
disorientation through sedation to a visionary dream state is reminiscent
of the effects produced by several other psychoactive plant preparations,
particularly those containing [[mckenna-witch-ointments-tropane-alkaloids-flying-salves]] tropane alkaloids flying salves tropane alkaloids [[schultes-witches-ointments-flying-salves]], which typically produce an
initial period of delirium and agitation before settling into a state of
sedation and hallucination.

## Chemistry: 28 Alkaloids and Counting

The chemical investigation of Galbulimima belgraveana has revealed the
presence of some 28 alkaloids, making it one of the most chemically complex
psychoactive plants documented in the ethnobotanical literature. Despite
this extensive alkaloid profile, the specific psychoactive principle
responsible for the plant's visionary effects remains unidentified. All of
the isolated alkaloids are reported to be biologically active, meaning they
produce measurable pharmacological effects, but which specific compound or
combination of compounds produces the reported intoxication and dream-like
visions is not yet known.

The Galbulimima alkaloids belong to several structural classes, with
himbacine and related compounds being among the most extensively studied.
Himbacine has been investigated for its muscarinic receptor antagonism and
has shown potential as a lead compound for the development of Alzheimer's
disease therapeutics. However, the psychoactive effects of himbacine and
related alkaloids at the doses consumed in traditional preparations remain
incompletely characterized.
