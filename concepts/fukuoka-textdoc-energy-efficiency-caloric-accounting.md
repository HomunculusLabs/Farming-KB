## The Japanese Bantam vs White Leghorn Paradox

Fukuoka illustrates the counterintuitive nature of true efficiency with
chickens. The native Japanese Bantam lays just one small egg every other
day — "low productivity" by conventional standards. But a breeding pair
left to roam freely will hatch a clutch of chicks and grow to a flock of
ten or twenty birds within a year, collectively laying many times as many
eggs as the best White Leghorn. The Bantams are supremely efficient
because they feed themselves, produce something from nothing, and do not
degrade the land.

Genetically-improved White Leghorns in cages lay one large egg daily, but
require feed grain having twice the caloric value of the eggs produced.
