# Synthetic Cannabinoid Pharmaceuticals

## Overview

Several pharmaceutical preparations containing cannabinoids have received FDA approval for
specific medical conditions. These synthetic and semi-synthetic cannabinoid drugs represent
the pharmaceutical industry's attempt to standardize and isolate the therapeutic compounds
found in the cannabis plant. While they offer dosing precision and regulatory approval, they
fundamentally differ from whole-plant cannabis in their therapeutic profiles.

## FDA-Approved Cannabinoid Pharmaceuticals

### Dronabinol (Marinol)
Dronabinol is a synthetic form of delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) approved by the FDA.
It is available in capsule form and is indicated for:

- **AIDS-related [[blesching-cannabis-multiple-sclerosis]] spasticity**: Reducing muscle stiffness and spasms
- **Cancer pain**: Managing neuropathic and cancer-related pain
- **Neuropathic pain**: Various chronic pain conditions

## Critical Limitations of Isolated Cannabinoid Pharmaceuticals

Blesching highlights a fundamental limitation shared by most synthetic cannabinoid drugs:
with the exception of Sativex, pharmaceuticals that contain THC do not contain any of the
other biologically active components of cannabis. This is a significant therapeutic
shortcoming because:

### Missing Entourage Effect
The cannabis plant contains over 111 identified cannabinoids, along with terpenoids,
flavonoids, and other compounds that may play important therapeutic roles. The "entourage
effect" hypothesis suggests that these compounds work synergistically, with the combined
effect being greater than the sum of individual components. Isolated THC lacks these
complementary compounds.

### Narrow Therapeutic Spectrum
Single-cannabinoid pharmaceuticals address only one mechanism of action. Whole-plant
cannabis simultaneously engages CB1, CB2, and non-CB1/non-CB2 receptors through multiple
cannabinoids, terpenoids, and their metabolites. This broader engagement may explain why
many patients report better results with whole-plant preparations.

### Absence of Terpenoid Co-factors
Researchers are increasingly recognizing that terpenoids (aromatic compounds responsible
for cannabis's distinctive smell) function as important co-factors in inducing therapeutic
effects. Synthetic pharmaceuticals lack these naturally occurring [[cannabis-pharmaceutical-cannabinoids-synthetic-research]] are used to treat a range of conditions:
- AIDS-related anorexia/cachexia
- Nausea and vomiting secondary to chemotherapy
- Neurological disorders (particularly spasticity in multiple sclerosis)
- Inflammatory conditions
- PTSD (post-traumatic stress disorder)
- Chronic pain syndromes
