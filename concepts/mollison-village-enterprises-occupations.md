# Village Enterprises and Occupations

In Permaculture: A Designers' Manual, Bill Mollison presents a
detailed analysis of how village occupants can self-employ in
service to the village itself and to nearby districts. The
framework for village enterprise is grounded in the principle
that costs are highest in three areas: food, energy, and
transport, and that local production can address all three.

## Food Provision

About 20 adults can support 1 adult providing a food supply;
thus, 5 to 7 families can earn a living from food provision.
This includes open air market gardens, glasshouse crop,
cooperative store (with local trade), domestic livestock and
fishery enterprise, and a part-time livelihood from cafe, food
processing, and baked goods. All surplus can be sold to visitors
or locals from outside the village proper via a village market.

Cut flowers, herbs, pharmaceutical products, processed dairy
products, fish and aquaculture represent additional land-based
enterprise opportunities.

## Energy Services

Energy needs include space heating, cooking, electric, hot
water, gas, and appliances. To establish energy-efficient
systems, plans need to include prior excellent house design,
retrofit of inefficient houses, insulation, and good appliances.
Most systems amortise in 3-7 years. Two to five families can
earn a living from house design and retrofit, hardware and
appliance sales, and installing, repairing, and tending a
village energy system.

## Medical and Pharmaceutical Services

One or two people can offer medical and pharmaceutical services
to the village or area, including prescription, massage,
counselling, and local treatment. Some medications can be made
and sold more widely, and specific remedies grown or
manufactured. Community health should always be based on
prevention of most illnesses.

## Building and Construction

For some time, a plumber-builder-mechanic can serve village
establishment and later maintenance needs locally, and can
produce useful furnishings and sale items for extra income, if
a woodworking area is made available. Craft products from
metal or wood workshops, pottery, and art provide additional
income streams.

## Education and Consultancy

Teachers are needed for children, adult education, and applied
workshops on site. Consultancy for other sites in architecture,
landscape, and design is possible, as is implementation and
provision of plant materials from a nursery on site. Publishing
is greatly assisted by computer word processing. A small
business service centre may be needed in the mid-term, employing
3-4 people.

## Nursery and Plant Supply

A nursery on site can further develop special crop for site,
fire control, bees, orchards, or forages for animals. Some
people may cooperate in an animal-breeding programme for special
poultry, pigeon, sheep, or goat breeds as a small stud. Computer
services to a network, programming, and databases on special
subjects are in demand.

## Vehicle and Transport

These are perhaps the most costly item and need a careful and
planned approach. Two to four livings are indicated in service,
repairs, maintenance of village vehicles, bulk fuel and oil
supply; in growing, distilling, or fermenting fuels for engines;
and in fleet lease and insurance, special vehicle lease, tractor
lease. Village alliances can establish special engine or chassis
manufacture.

## Financial Services

Handling the income, loans, accounting of enterprises, and
running a credit union is an essential job. A computer is
certainly needed, as are accounting and managerial skills. Two
people could perhaps handle the financing. A community credit
union, holding insurance money as well as incomes, can fund
enterprises such as energy systems, dairies, and food processing
centres.

## Income Sources for Community Services

Several methods are identified for funding community services:

- LEVY: A yearly levy on each family or property collected by
  the Community Council.
- TITHE: Individuals, groups, and businesses tithe 10% of net
  profit to public services.
- LOCAL CURRENCY: The Community Services Council can print its
  own currency, backed up by a valuable community resource.
- CHARGES: User-pays basis for tuition, use of facilities,
  medical needs.

## Enterprise Dependencies

These enterprises depend on two basic factors: capital (enough
money to start up and develop), and management (careful
accounting, forward planning, market research and development,
product development, sensible costing and staffing, correct
lease and rental agreements, appropriate legal structures). A
small business service centre is economical and necessary for
20 or more businesses.

Many small enterprises yielding products or services can
pre-sell their wares for initial capital. Some need no capital
to start up but may need time to develop. A very reliable early
income can be made from food, energy, and transport services
to the local community.
