## Basic Electrical for Homestead

Electrical work on a homestead ranges from wiring a simple shed to installing a full residential service panel. Electricity is the most dangerous system a homeowner can work with — mistakes cause fires, electrocution, and death. This page covers wiring fundamentals, circuit basics, outlet installation, GFCI protection, and critical safety guidelines. It also covers when to hire a licensed electrician.

## Safety First

**Non-negotiable rules:**
- Always shut off power at the breaker before working on any circuit
- Use a non-contact voltage tester to verify power is off — test it on a known live circuit first
- Never work on live circuits unless absolutely necessary and you are trained
- Never stand in water or on wet surfaces when working with electricity
- Use insulated tools rated for electrical work
- Wear safety glasses and dry gloves

**Electrical burns are deceptive** — they may not look serious externally but can cause deep tissue damage. Arc flash can cause instant third-degree burns and blindness.

## Wiring Basics

### Wire Types

**NM-B (Romex):**
- Non-metallic sheathed cable for interior residential wiring
- Contains 2-3 insulated conductors plus a bare ground wire
- Cannot be used outdoors, in wet locations, or where exposed to physical damage
- 14/2 NM-B = 2 conductors (14 AWG) + ground, for 15-amp circuits
- 12/2 NM-B = 2 conductors (12 AWG) + ground, for 20-amp circuits

**UF-B (Underground Feeder):**
- Water-resistant sheathing for direct burial or wet locations
- Same gauge rules as NM-B
- Must be buried at least 24 inches deep (18 inches with GFCI protection)

**THHN/THWN:**
- Individual conductors used in conduit
- THHN for dry locations, THWN for wet locations
- Required for outdoor runs, garages, and outbuildings
- Most modern wire is dual-rated THHN/THWN

### Wire Gauge and Circuit Rating

| Gauge (AWG) | Max Ampacity | Typical Use |
|---|---|---|
| 14 AWG | 15 amps | Lighting, general outlets |
| 12 AWG | 20 amps | Kitchen outlets, bathroom, small appliances |
| 10 AWG | 30 amps | Water heaters, AC units, dryers |
| 8 AWG | 40 amps | Ranges, large AC units |
| 6 AWG | 55 amps | Subpanels, electric vehicle chargers |
| 2 AWG | 95 amps | Service entrance conductors |

**Critical rule:** Never undersize wire. If a circuit is on a 20-amp breaker, every piece of wire in that circuit must be 12 AWG minimum. Undersized wire overheats and causes fires.

## Circuits and Panels

### Circuit Breaker Panel

The main distribution point where power enters and is divided into individual circuits. Modern homesteads should have a minimum 200-amp main service.

**Panel layout principles:**
- Dedicated circuits for major appliances (water heater, range, dryer, AC)
- General lighting/outlet circuits distributed throughout the home
- Label every breaker clearly
- Keep a panel directory updated
- Leave 20% spare capacity for future expansion

### Circuit Types

**120V Circuits (standard):**
- 15-amp: Lighting, general outlets, bedrooms, living areas
- 20-amp: Kitchen countertop outlets, bathroom, garage, outdoor

**240V Circuits (double-pole breaker):**
- Required for large appliances: water heaters, electric ranges, dryers, well pumps
- Use double-pole breakers matching the wire gauge

**Dedicated Circuits:**
- Refrigerator (15-amp)
- Microwave (20-amp)
- Dishwasher (20-amp)
- Garbage disposal (15-amp)
- Sump pump (15-amp)
- Furnace (15-amp)

## Outlets and Switches

### Standard Outlet Installation

- White wire to silver screw (neutral)
- Black wire to brass screw (hot)
- Bare copper to green screw (ground)
- Wrap wires clockwise around screws for a tight connection
- Use stab-in connections only on high-quality commercial-grade receptacles
- Use the correct box size — minimum 2.5-inch deep for standard devices

### GFCI Protection (Ground Fault Circuit Interrupter)

GFCIs detect current leaking to ground and shut off power in milliseconds, preventing electrocution. Required by code in:
- Bathrooms
- Kitchens (all countertop outlets)
- Garages and outbuildings
- Outdoor outlets
- Laundry areas
- Crawl spaces and unfinished basements
- Within 6 feet of any water source

**Installation:**
- Connect line wires (from panel) to the LINE terminals
- Connect load wires (to downstream outlets) to the LOAD terminals for protection of the entire circuit
- Test GFCI monthly using the test/reset buttons
- Replace any GFCI that fails to trip when tested

### AFCI Protection (Arc Fault Circuit Interrupter)

Required in modern construction for bedrooms, living rooms, and other habitable spaces. AFCIs detect dangerous arcing conditions that standard breakers miss. Most modern panels use combination AFCI/GFCI breakers.

## Common Troubleshooting

### Tripped Breaker

- Reset once. If it trips again immediately, do NOT keep resetting — there is a fault
- Unplug all devices on the circuit, then reset. Plug in one at a time to identify the culprit
- Common causes: overloaded circuit, short circuit, ground fault, failed appliance

### Dead Outlet

- Check the breaker first
- Check for a tripped GFCI on the same circuit (may be in another room)
- Test the outlet with a receptacle tester
- Check wire connections at the outlet — loose connections are the most common cause

### Flickering Lights

- Loose bulb — tighten or replace
- Loose wire connection at the switch or fixture
