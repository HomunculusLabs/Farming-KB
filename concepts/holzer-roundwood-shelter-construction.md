# Holzer Roundwood Shelter and Earth Cellar Construction

Earth is the cheapest and best insulation. The temperature of the soil balances out fluctuations and provides a steady room temperature, which benefits both livestock [[solomon-root-cellaring-and-crop-storage]]. These structures can be built anywhere in the world using local materials.

## Earth Shelter Pigsties (Simplest Design)

1. Dig a 2-3 meter wide, 1-2 meter deep ditch at the foot of a slope (location must stay dry)
2. Lay tree trunks over the ditch to form a roof, gently sloped for water runoff
3. Place sheeting or bitumen shingles on top for insulation
4. Cover with a little soil or brushwood
5. Logs must have at least 1 meter excess length (pigs dig around them)
6. Low side walls for stability
7. One side open for pig entry/exit
8. Throw straw or hay into the entrance -- pigs distribute it themselves
9. Pigs are clean animals: they go outside to relieve themselves if given space to move

## Roundwood Shelters (Larger Design)

### Orientation
- Closed on three sides, entrance faces east
- Rising sun wakes the animals in the morning
- Sheltered from midday sun in summer; pleasant cool temperature inside

### Site Preparation
- Dig into a terrace/slope (not straight down) to minimize digging
- Excavator digs narrow ditch at back and sides, approximately 1 meter deep
- Logs for walls placed in this ditch

### Wall Construction
1. Choose timber with large dimensions (thicker = longer lasting)
2. Best wood types: larch and robinia (longest lasting)
3. Timber quality (knots, [[dighton-fungus-cultivation-ants-termites-bark-beetles]]) is minor importance -- low-quality timber works fine
4. Can use timber from windbreak plantings
5. Lean logs against inside of slope during construction
6. Place all logs for each wall and fill around them in one go (for evenness)
7. Logs for 2m-high shelter must reach 1m into soil (minimum 3m long timber)
8. Cut all logs to same height when wall complete
9. Back wall should be somewhat higher to fit closely with roof top edge
10. Hold individual logs together with a cap log (log cut in two lengthways, laid on top, nailed)
11. Fill in the ditch after wall is complete

### Roof Construction
1. Lift logs onto shelter with excavator
2. Notch logs with chainsaw and nail to walls
3. Notches improve fit between roof and walls, prevent sides from giving way
4. Layer the roof:
   a. Roofing membrane on the logs
   b. Thin layer of fine earth (fill spaces between logs, even the surface, direct runoff)
   c. Sheeting (pond liner is most hard-wearing) -- earth underneath must contain NO stones
   d. Additional membrane layer on top for security
   e. Neither membrane nor sheeting should be taut (could tear under heavy load)
   f. Cover with earth (not more than half a meter; less for heavy loam soil)
   g. Pack soil around side walls
   h. Put buttresses against the sides for additional stability

### Dimensions and Cost
- Width: maximum 4 meters (roof load limitation); few limits on depth/length
- Example: 3m wide x 6m long x 2m high
- Buildable with excavator + 1 person in one day
- Excavator rental: 400-500 euros/day (10 work hours)
- Total cost (with timber already available): 700-800 euros (sheeting, nails, labor)
- Lifespan: ~30 years with larch or robinia logs of 30-40cm diameter

## Use as Storage Room

### Converting Livestock Shelter to Storage
1. Remove straw or hay with tractor fork
2. Pour in a tractor bucket of sand if necessary
3. Tip crops in with tractor
4. Block entrance with straw for winter insulation
5. For permanent storage: install a door

### Storage Conditions
- [[sepp-holzer-earth-cellars-and-philosophy]] maintain 80-90% humidity (ideal for crops)
- Steady temperature between 8-10C using only soil warmth
- Modern heated cellars with concrete floors are disastrous for [[root-cellaring-and-crop-storage]] (too dry -- apples wither and wrinkle)
- High humidity + steady temperature = ideal for the majority of crops
