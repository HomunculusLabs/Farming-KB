## Harvesting Technique

The best pickers are meticulous, unhurried, and treat the mushrooms with
care. Carelessness, when multiplied by hundreds of cultures, can be
costly. Equipped with a basket and short-bladed paring knife, the picker
grasps the base of the stem and, with a twisting motion, pulls the
mushroom from the casing layer, being careful not to disturb neighboring
pinheads.

The stem base is then trimmed, removing only flesh to which casing or
substrate is attached. Mushrooms having thin stems are best cleaned
using a knife in a downward scraping motion. All trimmings should be
placed in a sealed plastic bag and removed from the cropping area.

## Handling Clusters

Mushrooms growing in clumps or clusters should be broken apart and
harvested individually when possible. Special care must be taken with
clumps containing both mature and immature mushrooms. Immature mushrooms
should be left attached to the casing layer or substrate to ensure
continued growth. Premature removal of developing pins destroys their
growth potential.

## Labeling and Safety

No matter the technique, fresh mushrooms are preferred. Young mushrooms
should be selected over old ones. Each container should be labeled with
species, grower or identifier, date, and place of origin.

A general rule recommended by all mycologists: when eating wild mushrooms
for the first time, always save a specimen in case illness ensues and a
mycologist or doctor needs to be consulted.
