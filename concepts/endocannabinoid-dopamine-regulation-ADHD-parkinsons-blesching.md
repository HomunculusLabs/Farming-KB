## Dopamine and the Endocannabinoid System
Dopamine modulates neurons in the substantia nigra (a portion of the mid-brain) via
dopamine receptors and CB1 [[plant-signaling-pathways]] converge. Disrupted dopamine signaling in this brain region is one of
the most consistently replicated findings in ADHD neurobiology. The ECS modulates
dopamine release through CB1 receptors located on presynaptic terminals, creating a
feedback loop that can either amplify or dampen dopaminergic signaling depending on
context.

## Parkinson's Disease
Parkinson's disease is characterized by the progressive degeneration of
dopamine-producing neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta. This loss leads to
the characteristic motor symptoms: tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia (slowness of
movement), and postural instability. The non-motor symptoms include depression, sleep
disturbances, and cognitive decline.

Since CB1 receptors are co-localized with dopamine receptors in the basal ganglia
circuits, the ECS is positioned to influence both the motor and non-motor symptoms of
Parkinson's. Sufficient dopamine availability reduces risk of developing Parkinson's
disease, and maintaining healthy dopaminergic tone through lifestyle, diet, and
targeted cannabinoid therapy may have neuroprotective implications.

## Extended Attention Span Training (EAST)
NASA-developed EAST and its derivative SMART represent biofeedback approaches that use
real-time neurofeedback to train sustained attention. These systems measure
electroencephalographic (EEG) signals and provide visual or auditory feedback when the
user maintains focused attention states. Over repeated sessions, users develop greater
capacity for sustained attention, which is particularly relevant for ADHD management.
