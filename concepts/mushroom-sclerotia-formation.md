# Mushroom Sclerotia Formation

Sclerotia (singular: sclerotium) are compact, hardened masses of mycelium that
serve as survival structures for certain fungi. In cultivation, they are
sometimes called "truffles" or "philosopher's stones." The ability to produce
sclerotia is relatively rare among mushrooms and is of particular interest in
the genera Psilocybe and Panaeolus.

## What Are Sclerotia?

Sclerotia are dense, nutrient-storage structures composed of packed hyphal
cells. They function as a survival mechanism, allowing the fungus to persist
through unfavorable environmental conditions (drought, temperature extremes,
nutrient depletion). When conditions improve, sclerotia can germinate to
produce mycelium and ultimately fruiting bodies.

Unlike mushrooms (which are the reproductive structures), sclerotia are
entirely vegetative. They contain the same active compounds as the fruiting
bodies of the same species, often in comparable concentrations per dry weight.

### Optimal Conditions

- **Temperature**: Room temperature (20–22°C) is sufficient
- **Darkness**: A clean, preferably dark location is recommended (light is not
  harmful but is unnecessary for sclerotia production)
- **Duration**: Unlike [[aquarium-humidity-chamber-mushroom-fruiting]] which occurs in discrete flushes,
  sclerotia grow continuously. Longer incubation generally means larger
  sclerotia, with diminishing returns after approximately 4 months
- **Gas exchange**: Lids should remain loose or fitted with filtered gas
  exchange ports throughout the entire incubation period

## Factors Affecting Sclerotia Yield

### Substrate Moisture Content

Proper water content is critical. Too dry substrate limits sclerotia size; too
wet substrate promotes bacterial contamination and prevents sclerotia
formation. The grass seed should be hydrated but not waterlogged.
