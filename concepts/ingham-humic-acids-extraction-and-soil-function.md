## Definitions: Humus, Humic Acids, and Fulvic Acids

Humus is the broad term for the total stable [[teaming-with-microbes-humic-and-fulvic-acids]] acids in soil fertility is their
role as natural chelators. Chelation is the process by which organic
molecules bind to mineral ions (particularly micronutrients like iron,
manganese, zinc, copper, and boron), holding them in a plant-available form
that resists precipitation and lock-up in the soil.

Without humic substances, many micronutrients would quickly form insoluble
compounds with soil minerals, becoming unavailable to plants. Humic acids
wrap around these mineral ions, keeping them dissolved in the soil solution
and accessible to plant roots. This chelation effect is particularly
important in alkaline soils where iron and other micronutrients commonly
become unavailable.

Fulvic acids are especially effective chelators due to their small size and
high density of functional groups (carboxyl, phenolic hydroxyl, and quinone
groups). They can transport chelated minerals directly across cell membranes,
delivering nutrients into plant root cells.

## Extraction from Compost

Humic acids can be extracted from finished compost through alkaline
extraction. The process involves soaking mature compost in a solution
adjusted to pH 10-12 using potassium hydroxide (KOH) or sodium hydroxide
(NaOH). After 12-24 hours of agitation, the dark humic-acid-rich liquid is
separated from the solid compost material by filtration or settling.
