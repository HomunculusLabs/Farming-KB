# Shiitake (Lentinus edodes) Lentinan Cancer Therapy

## Overview

Lentinus edodes (shiitake) is indigenous to Japan, China, and other Asian temperate-climate countries, growing on fallen deciduous trees. It is the second most cultivated mushroom worldwide. Lentinan, a purified polysaccharide isolated from shiitake fruit-bodies by Chihara et al. (1970), is one of the most extensively studied mushroom-derived anticancer compounds. It has been approved as a drug in Japan for many years and is manufactured by several pharmaceutical companies.

## Active Compounds

### Lentinan
A pure polysaccharide composed only of carbon, oxygen, and hydrogen atoms. It is a (1-3)-beta-D-glucan with (1-6) beta-D-glucopyranoside branches, forming a right-handed triple spiral helix structure essential for antitumour activity.

### LEM and LAP
Glycoproteins derived from L. edodes mycelium and culture media respectively, containing glucose, galactose, xylose, arabinose, mannose, and fructose. LEM also contains nucleic acid derivatives, vitamin B compounds, and ergosterol. Both demonstrate strong antitumour activity via intraperitoneal and oral administration in animals and humans. LEM is prepared from hot water extract of powdered mycelia, incubated 50-60 hours at 40-50 degrees C and partially hydrolysed by endogenous enzymes. An immunoactive substance EP3, a water-soluble lignin with carboxyl groups, has been further fractionated from LEM.

### KS-2
An alpha-mannan peptide from cultured mycelium hot water extracts containing serine, threonine, alanine, and proline.

**Tegafur Combination Study**: A randomised controlled study compared tegafur alone (n=68) versus Lentinan plus tegafur (n=77) in 145 patients. Results:
- 50% survival time: 92 days (tegafur alone) vs. 173 days (Lentinan + tegafur)
- Sub-group analysis by tumour extension, histology, and Borrman classification consistently showed Lentinan significantly prolonged 50% survival
- 19.5% survived more than one year, 10.4% more than two years, 6.5% more than three years
- Response rate by Japan Society for Cancer Therapy criteria: 14.9% (Lentinan group) vs. 2.0% (control)

### Administration and Classification

In Japan, Lentinan is classified as a medicine and is widely used as an intraperitoneal adjuvant treatment for certain cancers. LEM and LAP are classified as food supplements (nutriceuticals). Numerous clinical trials have been conducted in Japan, though none have been placebo-controlled and double-blinded.

## Current Status

Lentinan continues to be approved for clinical use in Japan. It is one of only three mushroom-derived compounds (along with PSK and PSP) that have been used in extensive clinical trials involving hundreds of cancer patients. Its use as an adjuvant to conventional chemotherapy, particularly for gastric cancer, remains the primary clinical application.
