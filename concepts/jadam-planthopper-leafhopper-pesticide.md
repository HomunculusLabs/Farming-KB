# Citrus Planthopper and Leafhopper Pesticide (JADAM Recipe #8)

## Overview

JADAM pesticide recipe #8 specifically targets citrus flatid planthopper, leafhoppers, mulberry suckers, and spot clothing wax cicada. It uses [[jadam-herb-solution-jhs]] 15 L (4 gal)
- Water to make 500 L (132 gal)

## Target Pests

- Citrus flatid planthopper (adult and nymph)
- Ricanid planthopper
- Mulberry sucker
- Spot clothing wax cicada (adult and nymph)
- Rice green leafhopper
- Wooly apple aphid
- Slug moth larvae (secondary coverage)

## Biology of Target Pests
Understanding the biology of the insects targeted by recipe #8 is essential for effective application timing. Citrus flatid planthoppers (Metcalfa pruinosa) are sap-sucking insects that feed on phloem tissue, extracting large quantities of plant sap and excreting honeydew that promotes the growth of sooty mould on leaves and fruit. The nymphs are covered in white, waxy filaments that give them a cottony appearance and provide some protection against contact pesticides. This waxy coating is one reason why the [[jadam-pest-control-recipes]] to prevent the development of insect resistance. Recipe #8 should not be used continuously throughout the season against the same pest population. Instead, JADAM recommends alternating between recipes with different modes of action — for example, rotating recipe #8 (ginkgo-based) with [[cannabis-pest-management-guide]] programme.
This rotation strategy is grounded in the same principles that underpin conventional integrated pest management. When a single pesticidal compound is applied repeatedly, surviving individuals that possess genetic resistance traits reproduce preferentially, leading to a resistant population within as few as 5-10 generations. By rotating among compounds with different biochemical targets — ginkgolic acids that disrupt mitochondrial function, canola oil that suffocates by physical means, and red clay that desiccates through abrasion — the selection pressure for any single resistance mechanism is reduced, extending the useful life of all the materials in the rotation.

One of the advantages of JADAM's herbal pesticide system is its generally favourable profile toward beneficial insects compared to synthetic broad-spectrum insecticides. The sprayer should be agitated frequently during application to keep the red clay particles in suspension and ensure uniform distribution of all components.
Complete coverage is essential for effective control. Planthoppers and leafhoppers are highly mobile and will quickly re-infest plants that have untreated areas. The applicator should spray both the upper and lower leaf surfaces, the stems, and the growing tips, paying particular attention to the sheltered areas where nymphs congregate. For large-scale applications, JADAM recommends spraying in a systematic pattern that ensures no areas are missed — for example, walking each row in both directions or using a flagging system to mark the boundaries of the sprayed area. After spraying, the sprayer should be cleaned thoroughly with clean water to prevent corrosion and residue buildup that could affect the accuracy of subsequent applications.
