## Stump Cultivation

Stumps are exceptionally suitable for growing [[cannabis-breeding-traits-selection-criteria-clarke]]**: Partially or fully shaded stumps are better than full-sun; stumps with mosses/lichens/ferns indicate good microclimate; presence of competitor fungi generally disqualifies a candidate
- **Timing**: Inoculate before the first season of wild mushrooms. With each mushroom season, spore-laden air increases competition. Old stumps (not inoculated within several months of cutting) are poor candidates
- **Species for stumps**: [[stamets-reishi-ganoderma-lucidum-cultivation]]), maitake-cultivation|Maitake ([[growing-gourmet-saprophytic-mushrooms-decomposers]] must move to remain healthy. When mycelium reaches geographic or nutritional boundaries, a resting period ensues. If not triggered into fruiting, over-incubation leads to "die-back."

Signs of healthy mycelium:
- Tenacious, holding substrate particles together (especially true with Stropharia and Oyster mushrooms)
- Continuous, sheet-like growth pattern

Signs of decline:
- Spotty growth with shrinking islands of mycelium
- Loss of ability to hold substrate together
- Invasion by secondary decomposers (weed fungi) and insect predators
