## Slime Molds

Slime molds are a diverse assemblage of amoeboid protists traditionally studied
by mycologists due to their fungal-like fruiting bodies and spore-based
dispersal. Despite this historical association, molecular evidence conclusively
demonstrates that slime molds are **not true fungi**. They belong to the
supergroup Amoebozoa, completely separate from Kingdom Fungi. Their continued
study within mycology persists because their [[biodiversity-of-fungi-freshwater-survey-collection-methods]] overlap substantially with those of
true fungi.

## Phylogenetic Position

The classification of slime molds has undergone dramatic revision with the advent
of molecular systematics. Historically placed within Kingdom Fungi or as a
separate phylum (Mycetozoa) closely allied to fungi, slime molds are now
understood to be protists with no direct evolutionary relationship to the fungal
lineage. This reclassification is one of the most significant taxonomic shifts
in mycology — slime molds are absent from [[physarum-polycephalum]]*. This clade is the core "slime mold" lineage within
  Amoebozoa.

- **Plasmodiophorales** are phylogenetically unrelated to the other groups.
  Gene sequence analyses place them among alveolate protozoans within Cercozoa.
  Their only resemblance to other slime molds is a multinucleate plasmodial
  stage.

- **Acrasid slime molds** are likewise separated. GAPDH gene analysis groups
  them with Heterolobosa (Vahlkamfiidae), heterotrophic flagellates.

"Slime mold" is thus a term of convenience — except for the core
myxomycete–protostelid–dictyostelid clade.

## Myxomycetes (Plasmodial Slime Molds)

Myxomycetes are the largest and most conspicuous group, producing elaborate
macroscopic fruiting bodies visible to the naked eye. Their life cycle is
dominated by a **plasmodium** — a large, multinucleate, coenocytic mass of
protoplasm that creeps over surfaces engulfing bacteria, spores, and organic
particles. The plasmodium is brightly colored in many species (yellow, white,
pink, or red) and exhibits remarkable cytoplasmic streaming driven by
actin-myosin networks, achieving rates of up to 1 cm per hour.

## Dictyostelids (Cellular Slime Molds)

Dictyostelids are soil-dwelling organisms best known for their remarkable
**social behavior**. Individual amoebae feed on bacteria and divide while food
is abundant. When the bacterial supply is depleted, starving cells secrete
cyclic AMP in pulsatile waves, creating a chemotactic gradient attracting
neighboring cells.

Tens of thousands of amoebae aggregate into a differentiated **slug**
(pseudoplasmodium) that migrates toward light and heat. The slug exhibits
tissue-level organization: prestalk cells occupy the anterior, prespore cells
the posterior. Upon reaching a suitable site, anterior cells vacuolate to form
a stalk through programmed cell death, lifting viable spores for dispersal.

This altruistic differentiation has made *Dictyostelium discoideum* a premier
model system for studying cell signaling, chemotaxis, and the evolution of
multicellularity.
