## The Maori Question

Several persons have suggested that the Maori (the indigenous race) may have
used psilocybin mushrooms. R. Gordon Wasson, on a visit to NZ, mentioned this
in relation to the theory that concepts of divinity may have arisen in
primitive peoples out of psychedelic drug experiences. However, there is no
evidence of any sort to support the use of these mushrooms by the
pre-conquest Maori.

The Maori are distinctive for having used no consciousness-altering drugs of
any kind, and were highly skilled at treating food to remove toxic substances.
This conclusion was supported by all of NZ's Professors of Maori studies and
many scholars of Maori culture in consultations carried out in 1988. The Maori
culture represents at least one instance of a complex theology and mythology,
involving priests (tohungas) and frequent invocation of deities in daily life,
which did not require the use of consciousness-altering chemicals.

## Health and Safety

In a study of 150 people carried out between 1982 and 1989, the only case
requiring emergency room treatment was a musician who, due to impaired
coordination, had fallen and cut his head. Three out of 150 cases (approximately
2%) suffered prolonged psychological difficulties. No deaths from psilocybian
mushroom ingestion have been reported in NZ.
