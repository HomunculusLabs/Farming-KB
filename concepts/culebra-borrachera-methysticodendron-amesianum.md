# Culebra Borrachera (Methysticodendron amesianum)

Culebra borrachera (Methysticodendron amesianum) is a tree reaching a height
of 25 feet, known only from cultivated specimens in the Kamsa Indian town
of Sibundoy in southern Colombia. The Kamsa also call it mitskway
borrachera, meaning "snake intoxicant." This tree is the only species of
its genus and may represent an extremely aberrant form of a tree species of
Datura. An infusion of its leaves is said to be more potent and dangerous
to use than similar preparations of Datura, due to its extraordinary
alkaloid profile: approximately 80 percent of its tropane alkaloid content
is scopolamine.

## The Valley of Sibundoy

The significance of Methysticodendron amesianum is intimately connected to
its geographic and cultural context. The tree is known only from cultivated
specimens in the Sibundoy Valley of southern Colombia, a region Schultes
characterizes as "the most narcotic conscious area of the New World." This
highland valley, inhabited primarily by the Kamsa (also spelled Kamsa or
Coaiquer) and Inga indigenous peoples, has an extraordinary concentration
of psychoactive plant use that is unparalleled in the ethnobotanical
literature.

The Sibundoy Valley sits at a cultural and ecological crossroads between
the Amazonian lowlands and the Andean highlands, and its inhabitants have
developed one of the most sophisticated traditions of psychoactive plant
cultivation and use anywhere in the world. The Kamsa medicine men are
renowned for their knowledge of hallucinogenic and medicinal plants, and
the valley contains a remarkable diversity of species used in traditional
medicine and shamanic practice.

## Taxonomic Status: An Aberrant Datura?

Methysticodendron amesianum is the only species in its genus, and its
taxonomic placement has been a subject of botanical debate. Schultes
suggests it may represent an extremely aberrant form of a tree Datura
species, noting similarities to the tree daturas of the genus Datura (often
treated as the separate genus Brugmansia). The tree's 11-inch white flowers
differ from those of the tree daturas in having their bell-shaped corolla
split nearly to the base, a distinctive morphological feature that sets it
apart from its presumed relatives.

The possibility that Methysticodendron represents a cultigen, a plant that
has been selectively bred or maintained by human cultivation over many
generations, is significant. Many of the most important psychoactive plants
in the Sibundoy Valley are cultigens that do not occur in the wild and are
maintained exclusively through vegetative propagation by indigenous
cultivators. If Methysticodendron is indeed a cultigen derived from a tree
Datura ancestor, it would represent a remarkable example of human-directed
plant evolution for psychoactive purposes.

## The Name: Culebra and Snake Intoxicant

The Spanish name "culebra borrachera" combines "culebra" (snake) with
"borrachera" (intoxicant or drunkenness), paralleling the Kamsa name
"mitskway borrachera" ("snake intoxicant"). The snake imagery in both the
Spanish and indigenous names may refer to the plant's association with
shamanic practices involving serpentine symbolism, a common motif in
Amazonian and Andean cosmology. Snakes are frequently associated with
healing, transformation, and the spirit world in indigenous [[chiric-caspi-brunfelsia-south-american-hallucinogen]]
cultures.

The term "borrachera" is widely used throughout South America to refer to
plants in the nightshade family (Solanaceae) that produce intoxicating
effects, particularly those containing tropane alkaloids. Tree daturas,
Brunfelsia species, Iochroma species, and other Solanaceous hallucinogens
are all commonly called borrachera in various regions.

## Chemistry: Extraordinary Scopolamine Content

The chemical composition of Methysticodendron amesianum explains its
reputedly extreme potency. Approximately 80 percent of the several typical
tropane alkaloids present in the plant is scopolamine (also known as
hyoscine). This is an extraordinarily high proportion of scopolamine
compared to other tropane-containing plants. Most Datura species, for
example, contain a mixture of scopolamine and hyoscyamine in roughly equal
proportions, with hyoscyamine often predominating.

Scopolamine is a potent anticholinergic compound that, even in small doses,
may cause excitement, hallucinations, and delirium. At higher doses it can
produce dangerous and even life-threatening effects including tachycardia,
hyperthermia, and respiratory failure. The extremely high scopolamine
content of Methysticodendron makes it significantly more dangerous than
other tropane-containing hallucinogens, and its use requires exceptional
skill and knowledge on the part of the practitioner.

## Use by Kamsa Medicine Men

The trees of Methysticodendron amesianum are the special property of certain
Kamsa medicine men who employ the drug in difficult cases of disease
diagnosis, divination, prophecy, or witchcraft. This restricted access to
the plant reflects both its danger and its perceived power. Only
experienced practitioners with extensive training in the use of psychoactive
plants are entrusted with its preparation and administration.

The specific applications of culebra borrachera in Kamsa medical practice
include diagnosing difficult illnesses, foretelling future events, and
performing acts of spiritual intervention or witchcraft. These uses are
consistent with the broader pattern of tropane alkaloid hallucinogen use in
South America, where deliriant plants are typically employed for divinatory
and diagnostic purposes rather than for recreational or communal religious
use.

## Preparation and Administration

An infusion of the leaves is the reported method of preparation. The leaves
are likely steeped in water to produce a tea that is then consumed orally.
Schultes notes that this preparation is said to be more potent and
dangerous to use than similar preparations of Datura species, which is
consistent with the plant's extraordinarily high scopolamine content. The
dosage and preparation methods are closely guarded secrets of the Kamsa
medicine men who cultivate the trees.

## Comparison with Tree Daturas

Methysticodendron amesianum occupies a position alongside the tree daturas
(genus Datura, sometimes segregated as Brugmansia) as one of the most
potent New [[old-world-hallucinogenic-plants-overview]] plants of the nightshade family. Both
produce deliriant effects through tropane alkaloids, and both are
cultivated and carefully managed by indigenous peoples in the northern
Andes. However, the exceptional scopolamine content of Methysticodendron
sets it apart even from the most potent tree Datura varieties.
