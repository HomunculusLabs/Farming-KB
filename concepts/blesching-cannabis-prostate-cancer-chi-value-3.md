# Cannabis for Prostate Cancer — Evidence Review

Prostate cancer is the second most common cancer in men worldwide and the fifth leading cause of cancer death. The endocannabinoid system has emerged as a potential therapeutic target for prostate cancer, with [[cannabis-for-prostate-cancer]] for prostate cancer, reflecting three peer-reviewed studies that have been systematically evaluated.

## CHI Value: 3 (Three Reviewed Studies)

The Cannabis Health Index (CHI) rating system evaluates therapeutic claims based on the number of peer-reviewed studies and their evidence quality. A CHI Value of 3 indicates a modest but growing evidence base. While this is not yet sufficient to form the basis of a standard [[plant-defense-mechanisms]].

This study established a strong rationale for exploring cannabinoids as targeted therapy for prostate cancer, as the cancer cells essentially "advertise" their vulnerability through receptor overexpression.

## Study 3: CB1 Receptor in Healthy Prostate Epithelium (2009, Debrecen, Hungary)

**Location**: University of Debrecen, Hungary
**Year**: 2009

This study mapped the expression of CB1 receptors in healthy prostate tissue, finding CB1 receptors present in both prostate epithelial cells and smooth muscle cells. This finding is important because:

1. It confirms that the endocannabinoid system is a normal physiological component of prostate tissue, not merely an artifact of cancer.
2. It provides a baseline for understanding how CB1 receptor expression changes during malignant transformation (as demonstrated by the 2004 Wisconsin study).
3. The presence of CB1 in smooth muscle cells suggests additional roles for the ECS in prostate function beyond cancer — potentially in regulating prostate contractility and secretory function.

## Mechanisms of Anti-Cancer Action

The three studies collectively suggest several mechanisms by which cannabinoids may combat prostate cancer:

### Anti-Proliferative Effects
Anandamide and plant-derived cannabinoids (THC, CBD) have demonstrated the ability to inhibit the proliferation of prostate cancer cells, essentially slowing or stopping tumor growth. This anti-proliferative effect appears to be mediated through CB1 and CB2 receptor activation, which triggers intracellular [[blesching-cannabis-beta-caryophyllene-cb2-spices-complementary-therapy]] alongside — not a replacement for — established prostate cancer treatments:

### Active Surveillance
For men on active surveillance (monitoring low-risk prostate cancer without immediate treatment), cannabis may help modulate the endocannabinoid system's natural anti-cancer mechanisms.

### Surgery and Radiation
Cannabis may help manage the side effects of prostate cancer surgery (radical prostatectomy) and radiation therapy, including pain, inflammation, nausea, and anxiety. Its potential direct anti-cancer effects could provide additional therapeutic benefit.

### Androgen Deprivation Therapy (ADT)
ADT for advanced prostate cancer often causes significant side effects including hot flashes, loss of libido, bone density loss, and mood changes. Cannabis may help alleviate several of these side effects while potentially complementing the anti-cancer effects of hormone therapy.

### Chemotherapy
For advanced prostate cancer treated with chemotherapy (docetaxel), cannabis's anti-nausea, appetite-stimulating, and pain-relieving properties are well-established. The potential for direct anti-cancer synergy with chemotherapy agents warrants further investigation.
