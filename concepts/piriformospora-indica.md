# Piriformospora indica

## Introduction and Classification

*Piriformospora indica* (Hymenomycetes, Basidiomycota, Sebacinaceae) is a
cultivable root endophytic fungus that colonizes roots of a wide range of
plant species. It was discovered as the only cultivable endophyte that forms
arbuscular mycorrhiza-like structures in roots. Unlike obligate mycorrhizal
fungi, *P. indica* can be grown on artificial media, making it an invaluable
[[comparison-soil-food-web-vs-arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi]] fungi, which cannot be grown in the absence
of a living plant. The ability to culture *P. indica* axenically opens
possibilities for biotechnological applications that have been restricted for
other mycorrhizal fungi due to their obligate biotrophic nature.

## Colonization Pattern

The fungus forms inter- and intracellular hyphae in the root cortex, often
differentiating into dense hyphal coils (arbuscule-like structures), spore
and vesicle-like structures. As with AM fungi, hyphae multiply within the
host cortical tissues and never traverse through the endodermis. Likewise,
they do not invade the aerial portions of the plant (stem and leaves).

Fungal colonization increases with root tissue maturation. The root tip
meristem shows no colonization, the elongation zone shows mainly
intercellular colonization, and the differentiation zone is heavily
infested by inter- and intracellular hyphae and intracellular
chlamydospores. The majority of hyphae are present in dead rhizodermal and
cortical cells that become completely filled with chlamydospores.

## Host Cell Death and Proliferation

*P. indica* requires host cell death for proliferation in differentiated
barley roots. Seven days after inoculation, expression of barley BAX
inhibitor-1 (HvBI-1), a gene capable of inhibiting plant cell death, was
attenuated. Fungal proliferation was strongly inhibited in transgenic barley
overexpressing GFP-tagged HvBI-1. The endophyte appears to either actively
kill cells or sense cells undergoing endogenous programmed cell death, and
interferes with the host cell death program to form a mutualistic interaction.

## Plant Growth Promotion

*P. indica* vastly improves growth and overall [[bacopa-monnieri]]*, *[[ashwagandha-withania-somnifera]]*, *Spilanthes calva*, *Adhatoda vasica*, and
*Chlorophytum borivilianum*. A pronounced growth-promotional effect has
also been seen with terrestrial orchids.

Arabidopsis plants colonized with *P. indica* produce 22% more seeds than
uncolonized plants. The fungus stimulates nitrate assimilation in roots and
solubilizes insoluble phosphatic components in soil, enhancing nutrient
availability to host plants.
