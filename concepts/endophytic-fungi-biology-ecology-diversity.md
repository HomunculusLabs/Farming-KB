# Endophytic Fungi — Biology, Ecology, and Diversity

Endophytic fungi are fungi capable of symptomless occupation of apparently healthy plant tissue. They represent a vast reservoir of undiscovered fungal diversity and blur the boundaries between mutualism, commensalism, and parasitism.

## Definition

Endophytic fungi colonize living plant tissue without causing any immediate, overt negative effects. The term encompasses virtually the entire spectrum of symbiotic interactions in which fungi and plants participate: parasitism, commensalism, and mutualism.

## Distribution

Fungal surveys during the past 20+ years have demonstrated that endophytic colonization of land plants is ubiquitous:
- Known from tropical, temperate, and boreal forests
- Herbaceous plants from various habitats, including extreme arctic, alpine, and xeric environments
- Occur in mosses, liverworts, ferns, and pteridophytes
- Found in bark, wood, roots, and foliage

## Biology and Ecology

### Episodic Growth
A defining feature of endophytes is episodic growth — a prolonged, inconspicuous period in which growth and colonization cease temporarily, resuming after a physical or maturational change in the host. This distinguishes endophytes from continuously growing pathogens.

### Ecological Roles

1. **Protective Mutualists or Saprobic Commensals:** Grass endophytes (primarily Neotyphodium anamorphs of Balansiae) provide hosts with protection against herbivory and pathogens, increasing fitness. Sporulation on the host is suppressed completely, and host and fungus function essentially as a single organism.

2. **Latent, Quiescent Pathogens:** Many fungi commonly reported as endophytes are regarded by forest pathologists as minor or secondary pathogens. The behavioral differences between "endophytic" and "latent pathogen" may simply reflect differences in the duration of the latent phase and degree of host injury.

3. **Nutritional Metamorphoses:** A large proportion of endophytic species become saprobic on host death, allowing immediate utilization of dead tissue. Some enter an intermediate necrotrophic stage as tissue becomes moribund.

### Grass Endophytes

For grass hosts (Poaceae), endophytes denote a particular type of systemic, nonpathogenic symbiosis:
- Colonize leaf, culm, and root tissues of cool-season grasses
- Transmitted through host seeds
- Provide protection against herbivory and pathogens
- Interspecific hybrid strains of Neotyphodium

## Types of Endophytes

- **Foliar endophytes:** Internal tissues of foliage
- **Bark endophytes:** Young and old bark
- **Xylem endophytes and wood decomposers:** Internal wood tissues
- **Root endophytes:** Including Ingoldian hyphomycetes
- **Epiphytes:** Foliar and twig surfaces (sometimes grouped with endophytes)
- **Rhizosphere fungi:** Associated with root zones
- **Non-systemic grass endophytes:** Limited rather than systemic colonization
- **Endophytic Penicillia:** Widespread but poorly documented

## Methods

### Isolation and Culture
- Surface sterilization of plant tissue followed by culturing on agar media
- Selective isolation agents to target specific groups
- Microdissection for precise tissue sampling
- Molecular sequence approaches for identification

### Sampling Considerations
- Spatial and temporal distribution of endophytes within hosts
- Tissue age affects colonization patterns
- Tissue specificity varies among endophyte species
- Screening can be done on field populations, herbarium specimens, or collected samples

## Global Species Diversity

Endophytes likely represent one of the largest components of undiscovered fungal diversity. Higher plants furnish complex, multilayered, spatially and temporally diverse habitats supporting species-rich microfungal assemblages. The species composition of the internal mycobiota is distinct for various hosts, organs, and tissues.
