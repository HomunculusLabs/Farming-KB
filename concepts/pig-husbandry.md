# Pig Husbandry

Pigs are extraordinarily productive homestead livestock. They grow fast,
convert feed efficiently, and produce excellent meat. Their rooting behavior
can be harnessed for land clearing and tillage, and their manure is among
the most nutrient-rich available. Pigs are intelligent and require secure
fencing and thoughtful management.

## Breed Selection

### Heritage Breeds (Recommended for Homesteads)
- **Berkshire** — Excellent meat quality, marbled, dark-fleshed. Known as
  Kurobuta in Japan. Good mothers, moderate growth rate.
- **Tamworth** — Long-bodied, bacon-type. Excellent foragers, hardy, good
  mothers. Red-gold color.
- **Large Black** — Docile, excellent foragers, good lard-type pigs. Black
  skin resists sunburn. Excellent mothers.
- **Gloucestershire Old Spot** — Good foragers, docile, good mothers.
  Traditionally raised on orchard windfalls.
- **Red Wattle** — Fast-growing, hardy, disease-resistant. Good meat quality.
  Critically rare breed worth preserving.
- **Duroc** — Fast-growing, good feed efficiency, reddish-brown. Good
  terminal sire for crossbreeding.
- **Yorkshire (Large White)** — Large, good mothers, lean. Common maternal
  breed in commercial systems.
- **Hereford** — Red with white, hardy, docile. Good for small-scale operations.
- **Mulefoot** — Rare, black, syndactyl (single-toed). Excellent foragers,
  good lard-type.

### Commercial Breeds (Less Suited to Homestead)
- Landrace, Hampshire, Pietrain — selected for confinement operations, less
  hardy on pasture.

## Housing

### Pasture-Based Systems
- Moveable hoop houses or A-frame shelters work well. Rotate with paddocks.
- Minimum 8-10 ft of shelter space per pig. Pigs need dry bedding.
- Deep bedding with straw or hay — pigs will burrow into it. Add carbon
  regularly.
- Wallows or mud areas are essential in summer for thermoregulation.
- Provide shade — pigs cannot sweat and are extremely heat-sensitive.

### Confinement (Not Recommended for Homesteads)
- Concrete floors, slatted flooring, climate control. Higher capital cost,
  more disease pressure.

### Farrowing Housing
- Individual farrowing crates or pens (5x7 ft minimum) for sows with piglets.
- Heat lamps or creep areas for newborn piglets (need 85-95°F for first week).
- Farrowing rails prevent sow from lying on piglets.

## Fencing

Pigs are notorious for testing and destroying fencing.

- Woven wire (hog panels — 34 inches tall, 4-inch squares) with an electric
  wire at nose height is the standard. See [[livestock-fencing-guide]].
- Two or three strands of hot wire at 6, 12, and 24 inches can work for
  older, trained pigs.
- Pigs will root under fences. Bury wire 6-12 inches or place logs/rocks
  along the bottom.
- Electric fencing must be powerful — minimum 6 joules for perimeter.
- Portable electric net fencing works for rotational grazing with pigs.
- Train piglets to electric fence early with a dedicated training paddock.

## Feeding

### Pasture and Forage
- Pigs are omnivores and excellent foragers. On good pasture, they can meet
  30-50% of their nutritional needs from grazing and rooting.
- Excellent foragers on: clover, alfalfa, turnips, beets, field peas,
  pumpkins, acorns, hickory nuts, apples, and food waste.
- Rooting can be destructive — confine to designated areas or use nose
  rings (controversial) to limit damage.

### Grain Rations
- Grower diet: 16% protein from weaning to ~120 lbs.
- Finisher diet: 14% protein from 120 lbs to market weight (220-280 lbs).
- Common grains: corn (energy), soybean meal (protein), barley, oats, wheat.
- Feed conversion ratio: 2.5-4 lbs of feed per lb of gain (heritage breeds
  are less efficient, 3.5-4.5:1). See [[livestock-feed-formulation]].

### Alternative Feeds
- Dairy: whey, skim milk, clabber — excellent protein source.
- Kitchen scraps and garden waste — up to 30% of diet if balanced.
- Sprouted grains, fodder systems.
- Composted food waste (not meat products in most areas — check regulations).

### Minerals
- Free-choice loose mineral mix formulated for swine.
- Calcium and phosphorus balance is critical for growing pigs.

## Health Care

### Common Issues
- Respiratory disease (pneumonia) — ventilation is critical. Wet, ammonia-
  filled environments are the primary cause.
- Parasites — internal (worms) and external (lice, mange). Rotate pastures,
  deworm strategically.
- Erysipelas — bacterial disease causing diamond-shaped skin lesions,
  arthritis, sudden death. Vaccinate.
- Leptospirosis — can transmit to humans. Vaccinate breeding stock.
- Scours (diarrhea) in piglets — often caused by E. coli, rotavirus, or
  coccidiosis. Maintain clean farrowing environment.

### Vaccinations
- Erysipelas — core vaccine for breeding stock and growing pigs.
- Leptospirosis (6-way) — for breeding stock.
- Circovirus (PCV2) — recommended for growing pigs.
- CD&T — not standard for pigs (clostridial diseases less common), but
  available.

### Biosecurity
- Quarantine new animals for 30 days minimum.
- All-in/all-out management for farrowing and finishing groups.
- Control rodents and wild birds — disease vectors.

## Breeding

- Puberty: 5-8 months. Do not breed gilts until 8-10 months and 250-300 lbs.
- Estrous cycle: every 21 days, lasts 2-3 days.
- Gestation: 114 days (3 months, 3 weeks, 3 days).
- Litter size: 8-12 piglets (heritage breeds), 10-16 (commercial breeds).
- Sow-to-boar ratio: 1 boar per 15-20 sows for natural service.
- Weaning: 6-8 weeks on homestead (4 weeks commercial). Earlier weaning
  allows faster rebreeding.
- Breeding life: sows productive for 5-7 litters (3-5 years). Boars: 3-5
  years.

## Farrowing

- Prepare farrowing pen 1 week before due date. Clean, dry, bedded deeply.
- Signs of farrowing: restlessness, nesting behavior, vulva swelling, milk
  letdown (12-24 hours before).
- Farrowing typically takes 2-6 hours. Piglets born every 10-20 minutes.
- Dry each piglet immediately, clear airways, ensure first colostrum.
- Dip navels in iodine.
- Process piglets (iron injection, clip needle teeth, dock tails, castrate
  males) within first 3 days.
