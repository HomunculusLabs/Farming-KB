# Calea Zacatechichi (Dream Herb)

Calea zacatechichi is an inconspicuous shrub ranging from Mexico to Costa
Rica, recognized as a recently discovered hallucinogen employed almost
exclusively by the Chontal people of Oaxaca, Mexico. It holds a distinctive
place among psychoactive plants as one of the few verified oneirogens:
substances traditionally used specifically to enhance and alter dream
experiences. The plant is commonly known as zacatechichi, from the Aztec
(Nahuatl) word meaning "bitter grass," a reference to its intensely bitter
taste that has defined its medicinal and ceremonial uses since pre-Columbian
times.

## Ethnobotanical Use Among the Chontal

The Chontal Indians of Oaxaca use Calea zacatechichi for two primary purposes:
to "clarify the senses" during waking life and to enable communication with the
spirit world through dreams. The traditional method of preparation involves
crushing the dried leaves of the shrub and brewing them into a tea. After
drinking the infusion, the practitioner lies down in a quiet, dark place and
smokes a cigarette made from the dried leaves of the same plant. The user
knows that the correct dose has been reached when drowsiness sets in and they
begin to hear their own pulse and heartbeat, indicating the onset of the
plant's psychoactive effects.

This practice of combining oral and smoking administration is relatively
unusual among hallucinogenic plant traditions and may reflect the specific
pharmacological profile of Calea zacatechichi. The emphasis on auditory
phenomena, particularly the amplification of internal body sounds, is a
noteworthy characteristic that distinguishes this plant from other
hallucinogenic species.

## Traditional Medicinal Applications

From earliest recorded times, the intensely bitter taste of zacatechichi has
made it a favorite folk medicine throughout its range in Mexico and Central
America. Traditional healers have employed preparations of the plant for
treating fevers, nausea, and various gastrointestinal complaints. The bitter
principle likely stimulated interest in the plant long before its psychoactive
properties were fully appreciated, as bitterness is widely associated with
medicinal potency in traditional Mesoamerican pharmacopeia.

The plant's reputation as a fever remedy and digestive aid extends across
multiple indigenous groups in southern Mexico, suggesting a long history of
use that predates its more specialized application as a dream enhancer among
the Chontal specifically.

## Chemistry and Pharmacology

Chemical investigation of Calea zacatechichi has revealed the presence of
several constituents, but research is still in relatively early stages. The
most significant finding noted by Schultes is the presence of an unidentified
alkaloid that may be responsible for the auditory hallucinations reported by
users. The auditory dimension of the experience, particularly the perception
of one's own heartbeat and pulse in amplified form, suggests a mechanism
distinct from the [[serotonin-receptor-interference-psychedelic-mechanism]] agonism characteristic of classical
hallucinogens like [[psilocybin]] or [[mescaline]].

Subsequent research beyond the [[golden-guide-amanita-muscaria-and-soma]] has identified additional compounds
in Calea zacatechichi, including sesquiterpene lactones and flavonoids, though
the precise psychoactive constituents remain a subject of ongoing
investigation. The plant does not appear to contain any of the major known
hallucinogenic alkaloid classes (tryptamines, isoquinolines, or lysergamides),
which adds to its pharmacological interest.

## Botanical Classification

Calea zacatechichi belongs to the daisy family, Compositae (also known as
Asteraceae). There are approximately one hundred or more species in the genus
Calea, distributed across open or scrubby hillsides in tropical America.
Several species of Calea enter into folk medicine in various parts of Latin
America, but C. zacatechichi appears to be unique in its psychoactive
applications. The genus is native to the Americas and is most diverse in
Mexico and [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-central-america-caribbean]].
