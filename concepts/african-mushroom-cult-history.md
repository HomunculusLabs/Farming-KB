## Iconography of the Mushroom Cult

On a Sahara plateau at an altitude of 6,500 ft., pictures depict mythical beings with anthropomorphic and zoomorphic attributes reminiscent of early Mexican images.

Many scenes show tiny horned dancers alongside mushrooms.

Deities with masks and horns hold mushrooms in their hands; sometimes the mushrooms are shown attached directly to body parts.

Stone Age artists created images of anthropomorphic beings with mushroom-like heads.

The most striking renditions at Tin-Tazarift, Tassili District (Algeria), show masked anthropomorphic beings engaged in ecstatic dancing.

Dashed lines connect the mushroom with the center of the head, representing a flow of energy and perhaps the mushrooms' influence on the human soul.

As early as 9,000-7,000 years ago, the head was apparently considered the seat of consciousness.

Mushrooms were drawn in white, several shades of ochre, and occasionally in blue colors, possibly representing the bluing phenomenon of psilocybin-containing species.

The rock drawings consistently show two kinds of mushroom shapes: one resembling [[psychedelic-mushroom-set-and-setting]] (a nitrophilic species), and [[global-psychedelic-mushroom-species-diversity]] was discovered in Natal Province, South Africa, the first psychotropic Psilocybe species ever found in the area.

## Documented Intoxications

In 1945, Cullinan and Henry described 22 cases of intoxication in Nairobi from a yellow Stropharia species mistaken for a culinary mushroom.

Symptoms started one hour after ingestion, peaked within three hours, and persisted for 24-48 hours.

Symptoms included emotional imbalance, fits of mirthful and irresponsible laughter alternating with depressive moods, insomnia with nightmarish feelings, and rational speech despite the experience.

In 1957, A.D. Charters reported cases from Nakuru (1949) where a man and his wife ate mushrooms for lunch and experienced pupil dilation, euphoria, delusions, visions of pink colors, and feelings of passing into the next life.

The wife had laughing fits alternating with happiness and depression, and feared she was dying.

Both patients recovered completely within six hours after medical intervention.
