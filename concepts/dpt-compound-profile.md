## DPT Compound Profile

DPT (N,N-dipropyltryptamine) is a synthetic tryptamine described in TIHKAL as compound #9. It has been used in clinical [[ayahuasca-tourism-legal-status-by-country]]**: Schedule I in US (since 2004), controlled in many countries

## Dosage by Route
- **Oral**: 100-250 mg, duration 2-4 hours
- **Smoked**: 30-100 mg, duration 15-30 minutes
- **Intramuscular (IM)**: 80-100 mg, duration 1-2 hours
- **Intravenous (IV)**: 12-36 mg, duration 15-30 minutes

## Effects

### Physical
- Nausea (especially at onset)
- Pupil dilation
- Body load
- Temperature dysregulation
- Possible vomiting at higher doses

### Mental
- [[metzner-psycholytic-psychedelic-therapy-models]])

### Comparison with Related Compounds
- Longer acting than DMT but shorter than psilocybin
- More potent than DET but less potent than DMT
- Smoked DPT: rapid onset similar to smoked DMT
- Oral route has significant first-pass metabolism (variable effects)
- IM route preferred in clinical settings for reliable dosing

## Clinical Use History

### Psychotherapy Research
- Used by Claudio Naranjo in the 1960s-70s for psychedelic therapy
- Studied for treatment of alcoholism
- Used in research with terminal cancer patients
- Temple of the True Inner Light in NYC has used DPT as a religious sacrament
- Research was part of the broader psychedelic therapy movement before 1970 scheduling

### Advantages in Clinical Context
- Shorter duration than LSD (more manageable sessions)
- Reliable IM dosing (precise control)
- Less visual intensity than DMT (more verbal/processing possible)
- Good balance of introspective and visual effects

## Duration Timeline (Oral)
- **Onset**: 30-90 minutes
- **Peak**: 1-2 hours
- **Come down**: 1-2 hours
- **Total**: 2-4 hours

## Synthesis
Synthesized from tryptamine or indole via standard reductive amination with dipropylamine. Straightforward synthesis using lithium aluminum hydride or sodium borohydride reduction.

## Safety Notes
- Limited modern [[lsd-set-and-setting-framework]] critical (especially for IM/IV routes)
- Long-term effects unknown
