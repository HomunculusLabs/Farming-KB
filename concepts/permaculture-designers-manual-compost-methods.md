## Cold Composting

Cold heaps (pitted or silage) do not lose nitrogen, but neither do they kill weed seeds. They produce a slower, cooler decomposition that preserves more nutrients.

## Care in Selection of Compost Materials

Even using organic residues or "natural" wastes, soil problems can arise from a concentration of nitrates in manures, or toxic mineral residues.

### Mineral Contamination Risks (Kevin Handreck, Organic Growing, Autumn 1987)
- Galvanized, copper, or brass containers containing wet residues of manures or manurial teas; clay, stainless steel, glass, or iron are preferable
- Zinc and copper are produced in excess from galvanized or copper/brass containers
- Manures from pigs, poultry, cattle, or sewage sludges can add excessive copper, zinc, nickel, boron, lead, or cadmium to soils (especially acid sandy soils)
- Excess zinc can be built up by using earthworm casts from contaminated pig, sheep, or domestic wastes
- Animals penned in galvanized areas can produce excess zinc in pen wastes

### Leaf Material
Leaf material cut and mulched (or composted) green will contain more nutrients than fallen leaves, although the latter are still useful for humus production.

## Greenhouse Compost Integration

The areas below plant benches, if used to house rabbits, guinea pigs, poultry, or any small domestic animals at night will provide considerable winter heat, as will long compost boxes along the poleward wall, or external to but below the level of the greenhouse itself.

## Mulch vs Compost

### Mulching Defined
Covering the soil surface with 15 cm or more of organic material, as a loose (uncompacted) mulch; 8 cm of tight-rolled sawdust does not qualify.

### Mulch Benefits
- Adds plant nutrients (the "cool" decay loses little nitrogen)
- Buffers soil temperatures (cooler by day, warmer at night)
- Prevents erosion
- Promotes soil life (earthworms increase only under mulch)
- Restores soil structure
- Enhances water absorption (11% better infiltration on mulched plots)
- Seed germination enhanced
- Major nutrients (N, P, K) remain at satisfactory levels over long term

### Plastic Mulches
Black plastic for heat and weed control, silver for aphid repellancy. These have a more limited role, important in the short-term, but often expensive or impractical in poor countries.

### Time Frame
It can take 3-5 years to create a balanced soil under mulch from a compacted or mined-out soil. Even longer periods are necessary to develop humus in permanent crops on the broad scale.

## Soil Humus Content

Soils in nature can vary from a humus content of 2% to close to 100% (as peats). In gardens, 40% or more humus helps block heavy metal uptake by plants. Many compost-fed or mulched garden soils contain 10-30% humus.

### Humus Loss Factors
- Ploughing/aerating soils turns up humus and oxidises it to carbon dioxide
- Burning vegetation is worse, with additional pollutants (terpenes, creosotes, nitrogen, dust particles)
- Where soils are not tilled or burned, soil humus lasts a long time (hundreds or even thousands of years)

### Humus Sources
On the broad scale, humus can only be provided by the root and above-ground mass of grasses, trees, and plants. Prairie grasses and broadleaf trees are particularly effective.
