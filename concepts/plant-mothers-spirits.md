## Plant Mothers and Spirits

In [[pablo-amaringo]], the renowned Shipibo painter and former
vegetalista, stated: "Every tree, every plant, has a spirit. People may
say that the plant has no mind. I tell them that the plant is alive and
conscious. A plant may not talk, but there is a spirit in it that is
conscious, that sees everything, which is the soul of the plant, its
essence, what makes it alive."

## The Concept of Plant Mothers

The "mother" of a plant is its governing spirit — a supernatural being
who protects the species, controls its [[amaringo-ayahuasca-visions-shipibo-iconography]]," describe
numerous plant mothers and their characteristics:

**Remocaspi** (*Aspidosperma excelsum*): Its spirit appears as a wise
old Chinese king wearing a golden crown, holding a scepter of death.
He bestows sublime wisdom upon those who have properly dieted, but
brings down death upon those who have not followed the strict dietary
regime. The plant's cathartic medicine facilitates knowledge of magical
and esoteric sciences.

**Puka-lupuna** (*Cavanillesia hylogeiton*, red lupuna): Its spirit is
a dark-complexioned woman with cat eyes and a gold chain. She is
associated with sorcery and rarely helps curanderos who heal. The tree
demands a strict and rigorous diet; immediate death awaits the
unprepared who ingest it without proper preparation.

**Ajosquiro** (*Gallesia corozoana*): Its spirit is a very small man
with curly hair, wearing a red cape and red clothes. Few people ingest
this plant because its required diet is extremely severe, symbolized by
the alicomachines — ferocious monkeys that hang by their tails and jump
wildly. The medicine provides defense against enemies and causes the
ingester to feel constantly hot, wanting to bathe.

**Renaquilla** (*Ficus* sp.): Its spirit appears as a Shipibo woman
who teaches healing through dreams to those who diet correctly. This
plant is unmatched for treating fractures and other lesions, and can be
applied raw or cooked.
