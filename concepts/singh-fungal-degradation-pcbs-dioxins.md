**Effects of chlorination:**
- Mineralization decreases with increasing degree of chlorination
- Mineralization order: biphenyl (23%) > 2-chlorobiphenyl (16%) > 2,2',4,4'-tetrachlorobiphenyl (10%)
- Penta- and hexachlorobiphenyls removed at slower rates than tri- and tetrachlorobiphenyls
- Lower chlorinated congeners decrease on percentage basis; higher chlorinated congeners dehalogenate to lower chlorinated forms

**Mechanisms:**
- Two mechanisms operate in P. chrysosporium: LiP-catalyzed one-electron oxidation producing aryl cation radicals, and cytochrome P450 hydroxylation
- Free radical attack mechanism is relatively non-specific for chlorine substitution position
- No linear correlation between biodegradation/mineralization and LiP/MnP activities, suggesting unknown enzymatic systems also contribute

### Role of Manganese
- Mn(IV) oxide addition improves production, enzymatic activity, and stability of LiP in P. chrysosporium
- Mn(IV) enhances MnP and H2O2 production, improving Aroclor 1254 degradation
- About 84% of Aroclor eliminated in cultures with 57.5 mM MnO2 within 6 days

### PCB Bioremediation in Soils
- U.S. EPA issued first R&D permit for PCB bioremediation using white-rot fungi (P. chrysosporium)
- Fungal growth does not occur below 15C in soil
- 90% degradation achieved with young P. chrysosporium mycelial inoculum grown on sugarcane bagasse pith
- Up to 50% of penta- and hexachlorobiphenyls degraded; lower chlorinated congeners not always degraded
- Proper homogenization and contact between mycelia and soil aggregates is critical
- Lignosulfonate addition does not improve PCB degradation in soil

## Fungal Degradation of Dioxins

Dioxins (polychlorinated dibenzo-p-dioxins, PCDDs) and furans (PCDFs) are among the most toxic synthetic compounds:

- 2,3,7,8-Tetrachlorodibenzo-p-dioxin (TCDD) is the most toxic congener
- Produced as by-products of combustion, chlorine bleaching, and pesticide manufacturing
- Extremely persistent in the environment due to their chemical stability

White-rot fungi can degrade dioxins through ligninolytic enzyme systems:
- P. chrysosporium degrades 2,7-dichlorodibenzo-p-dioxin and 2,3,7,8-TCDD
- Degradation mechanism involves initial hydroxylation followed by ring cleavage
- Non-specific peroxidase systems attack the aromatic rings
- Complete mineralization is limited but partial transformation reduces toxicity

## Comparison with Bacterial Systems

| Feature | Fungal Systems | Bacterial Systems |
|---|---|---|
| Enzymatic mechanism | Non-specific free radical attack (LiP, MnP, laccase) | Specific dioxygenases |
| Co-substrate requirement | Often required for enzyme induction | Biphenyl co-substrate often needed for PCB degradation |
| Chlorine substitution | Non-specific; attacks ortho, meta, and para positions | Often limited by chlorine substitution pattern |
| Higher chlorinated PCBs | Degraded through reductive dechlorination | Generally require anaerobic dechlorination first |
| Soil application | Filamentous growth penetrates soil aggregates | Limited soil exploration |
| Mineralization | Partial; typically 10-25% to CO2 | Can achieve higher mineralization rates |
| Temperature range | 15-30 C for most species | 15-35 C |

## Agricultural Relevance

PCBs and dioxins affect agricultural environments through:
- Contaminated soils on former industrial or military sites converted to farmland
- Atmospheric deposition on cropland
- Sewage sludge application containing PCB residues
- Fungal bioremediation offers a low-cost approach for restoring contaminated agricultural land
