## Farming with Air, Water and Sunlight

"Farming with Air, Water and Sunlight" is a foundational principle in [[double-digging]] in Biointensive Mini-Farming

One of the most direct applications of this principle is found in the **Biointensive
mini-farming** technique of **[[comparison-double-digging-vs-no-till]]**. In conventional agriculture, soil is typically
loosened to a depth of only 8 to 10 inches using plows or tractors. Double digging loosens
soil to a depth of **24 inches** — nearly three times deeper. This dramatic increase allows
far more air to pass through the soil profile, creating an aerobic environment that supports
vigorous [[root-development-and-plant-competition]] beneficial microbial activity. Plants in compacted soil struggle
because air cannot reach their root systems effectively.

## Harnessing Water

Water is frequently taken for granted, yet without adequate moisture — whether through
irrigation or natural rainfall — plants do not grow well and often die. In [[natural-farming]],
consistent and clean water availability is ensured for both crops and livestock. Water
facilitates [[fukuoka-textdoc-composting-critique-futility-prepared-organic-matter]] [[companion-planting]]

In planting beds oriented **north to south**, interplanting or [[cervantes-companion-planting-pest-deterrent]] is employed.
Shorter plants are positioned towards the northern portion to maximize sunlight exposure and
strategic shading. The sun inclines toward the northern side despite rising east and setting
west, making this orientation effective when combined with thoughtful plant height arrangement.

### Moon Phase Planting

The moon, as a reflection of the sun, also influences plant growth. Transplanting a few days
before the **new moon** or during the **full moon** effectively increases available light
energy reaching plants. During the full moon, additional nighttime luminosity stimulates
plant activity. This practice, rooted in biodynamic traditions, recognizes that lunar cycles
affect moisture levels, sap flow, and patterns that influence germination and growth.

## Application to Free-Range Chicken Housing

The "farming with air, water and sunlight" principle extends directly to livestock, especially
in the design of housing for **free-range chickens**:

### Air: Ventilation and Substrate Depth

Air movement and ventilation are mandatory. An **exhaust ventilation system** ensures
continuous air exchange. The substrate — composed of loose sawdust, wood shavings, and compost
— is maintained at a depth of **one yard (approximately 36 inches)**. This deep loose litter
allows air to circulate through both the housing structure and the litter substrate, preventing
[[aact-ph-and-anaerobic-conditions]] that breed pathogens.

### Sunlight: Orientation and Transparent Roofing

Chicken housing is oriented **east to west** to maximize sunlight exposure. A portion of the
roofing is made **transparent** to allow direct sunlight penetration into the housing interior,
ensuring chickens receive natural sunlight essential for vitamin D synthesis and disease
resistance.

### Water: Clean and Continuous Access

Clean water must be provided at all times. Water quality directly affects animal health,
growth rates, and disease susceptibility. [[cho-loess-powder-in-natural-farming]], water access is a fundamental
requirement, not an afterthought.

### The Conventional Contrast

Conventional chicken production creates conditions that undermine these principles: **high
density crowding**, **foul decomposing manure smells**, and **practically no sunlight
penetration**. These conditions breed disease, making routine **antibiotic use** imperative.
The natural free-range method allows chickens to freely access air, water, and sunlight —
eliminating the need for chemical interventions and producing healthier animals.

## Learning from Nature

The deeper philosophy behind this principle is that **Nature is complete**. When farmers learn
to observe and work with natural systems rather than against them, Nature reveals her
principles and answers. The instruction is to "speak" with the soil, with plants and animals —
to listen and observe what they tell us.

A telling story illustrates this: when a farm help reported his vegetables were not growing
well despite applying all taught techniques, he was asked to look to the sky and observe. He
discovered a shrub tree overshadowing his plant bed, blocking sunlight. The answer came not
from a fertilizer bag or pesticide bottle — it came from observing Nature herself.

## Key Takeaways

- **96% of growth** comes from air, water, and sunlight; only 4% from nutrients
- **Double digging** loosens soil to 24 inches (vs. 8-10 inches conventional) for air access
- **East-west orientation** maximizes direct sunlight exposure on planting beds
- **North-south orientation** with companion planting uses height gradients for light
- **Moon phase planting** (new moon and full moon) extends light energy availability
- **Free-range chicken housing** requires exhaust ventilation, deep substrate, transparent
  roofing, and continuous clean water
- **Conventional farming** relies on NPK chemicals, antibiotics, and high-density crowding
