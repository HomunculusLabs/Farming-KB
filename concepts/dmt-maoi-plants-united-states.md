# DMT and Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitor Plants

## Overview

N,N-dimethyltryptamine (DMT) is a potent hallucinogenic tryptamine found in
numerous plant species growing throughout the [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]]. When taken orally,
DMT is normally inactive because monoamine oxidase (MAO) enzymes in the gut
and liver rapidly metabolize it. However, when co-administered with reversible
[[mao-inhibitors-and-psychedelics]] (MAOI), DMT becomes orally active, producing a 3–4 hour
intoxication. This combination is the basis of the Amazonian brew ayahuasca,
but both DMT and MAOI-containing plants also grow independently within the
[[hallucinogenic-plants-native-to-united-states]].

## Pharmacology of DMT

DMT is a structural analog of serotonin and melatonin. When smoked, inhaled,
or injected, DMT is rapidly psychoactive without need for [[tihkal-oral-activity-mao-inhibition]]:
peak effects occur within 2–5 minutes and clear over 20–30 minutes. The
typical smoked dose is 40–50 mg, with anecdotal reports ranging from 20 to
100 mg. The effective intravenous dosage range is estimated at 0.1–0.4 mg/kg,
while 0.6 mg/kg was described as excessive.

Orally, DMT requires MAO inhibition to be active. The MAOI prevents
first-pass metabolism in the gut and liver, allowing DMT to reach systemic
circulation and cross the blood-brain barrier. This pharmacological
requirement is the reason traditional [[schultes-ayahuasca-preparations]] combine
DMT-containing plants with MAOI-containing plants.

## DMT-Containing Plants in the United States

Several common [[growing-gourmet-north-american-reishi-species]] plants contain appreciable amounts of DMT:

### Phalaris Species (Canary Grass)

[[phalaris-arundinacea]], Phalaris tuberosa, and [[phalaris-aquatica]] are
widespread in the United States, found in fallow fields, sidewalk cracks,
and lawns across the entire country. DMT can be obtained through simple
juice extraction of these grasses. However, DMT content is highly variable
between strains, making dose prediction unreliable. High-percentage strains
can be ordered through the Internet.

### Desmanthus illinoensis (Prairie Bundleflower)

This prairie legume has a wide American distribution. The root bark has been
found to contain 0.34% DMT by dry weight. As a nitrogen-fixing legume, it
is also valued in permaculture and restoration plantings.

### Psychotria viridis

While not native to the United States, this is the traditional DMT source
in [[harner-amazonian-ayahuasca-preparation-methods]]. Its DMT content ranges from 0.1% to 0.66% by dry
weight. It is included here for context as the classical ayahuasca DMT
component.

## MAOI-Containing Plants in the United States

### Peganum harmala (Syrian Rue)

Introduced into the western United States and now found growing wild. It
contains 2–4% MAOI content by dry weight — primarily the beta-carbolines
harmaline, harmine, and tetrahydroharmine. These compounds also have mild
sedative and hallucinogenic properties of their own.

### Passiflora incarnata (Passionflower)

Grows throughout the southeastern United States and much of the Midwest.
Contains approximately 1% MAOI content by dry weight. Widely used in herbal
preparations for its sedative properties, and bears an edible fruit.

### Banisteriopsis caapi

The traditional MAOI source in ayahuasca, native to the Amazon. Its
beta-carboline content ranges from 0.05% to 1.95% by dry weight. The
[[turner-harmala-alkaloids-preparation-and-dosage]] in this vine are the classical MAOI component.

## Ayahuasca Composition

A typical 100 mL dose of ayahuasca contains approximately 24 mg DMT,
107 mg tetrahydroharmine, 20 mg harmaline, and 170 mg harmine (Callaway
et al., 1996). However, alkaloid content varies considerably across
batches and specific preparation recipes.
