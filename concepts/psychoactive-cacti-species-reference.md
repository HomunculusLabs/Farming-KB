## Psychoactive Cacti Species Reference

Comprehensive reference to psychoactive cacti species, their alkaloid profiles, identification, and traditional use. Based on Adam Gottlieb's 1977 guide.

## Major Psychoactive Cacti

### Peyote (Lophophora williamsii)
- Type: Spineless, tufted, blue-green button-like cactus
- Range: Central Mexico to southern Texas (Chihuahuan Desert)
- History: Known since at least 300 BC; standardized ceremonial use by 1800s
- Mescaline content: Up to 6% dried weight (typically ~1%); average button ~2g dried
- Active alkaloids: Mescaline, hordenine, N-methylmescaline, N-acetylmescaline, pellotine, anhalinine, anhalonine, anhalidnine, anhalonidine, anhalamine, O-methylanhalonidine, tyramine, lophophorine
- Effects: ~6-12 hours; physical and visual; first effects in ~30 minutes
- Dose: 6-10 dried buttons (~350 mg mescaline)
- Harvest: Decapitate above ground level, leave roots intact for regeneration
- Two forms recognized by Huichol: "Peyote of the Gods" (larger, more potent) and "Peyote of the Goddesses" (smaller, milder)

### Lophophora diffusa
- Discovered 1967 near Queretaro, south-central Mexico
- Yellow-green, soft, ribless
- Different alkaloid profile with far less mescaline than L. williamsii

### San Pedro (Trichocereus pachanoi)
- Type: Large, multi-branched columnar cactus to 10-15 feet
- Range: Andes of Peru and Ecuador; altitudes from sea level to 9000 feet
- Mescaline content: 0.3-1.2% (less than peyote but larger plant = more total)
- Active alkaloids: Mescaline, tyramine, hordenine, 3-methoxytyramine, anhalaninine, anhalonidine, and several other sympathomimetic compounds
- Effects: More pleasant than peyote; less physical, more tranquil; less initial nausea
- Dose: 4-8 inches of fresh cutting (3-4 inch diameter)
- Ritual use dates back to ~1000 BC; used by Peruvian curanderos as "Cimora"
- Hardy; tolerates cold climates; grows in almost any soil with decent drainage

### Other Mescaline-Bearing Trichocereus
- T. bridgesii
- T. macrogonus
- T. terscheckii
- T. werdermannianus

## Minor Psychoactive Cacti (Central Mexico)

### Doñana (Coryphantha macromeris)
- Range: Northern Mexico
- Active compound: Macromerine (~1/5 potency of mescaline)
- Other alkaloids: Normacromerine, N-formylnor-macromerin, tyramine, N-methyltramine, hordenine, metanephrine, synephrine
- Content: ~0.1% macromerine; extraction required (1+ kg dried cactus needed)
- Related species with macromerine: C. pectinada, C. elephantideus, C. runyonii, C. cornifera var. echinus

### Peyotillo (Pelecyphora aselliformis)
- Range: San Luis Potosi, central Mexico
- Also known as "hatchet cactus" (flattened tubercles)
- Contains traces of mescaline (too minute for effect) plus: anhalidine, anhadine, hordenine, N-methylmescaline, pellotine, and several other alkaloids

### Tsuwiri / False Peyote (Ariocarpus retusus)
- Huichol name means "False Peyote"; believed to cause madness if consumed
- Contains: Hordenine, N-methyltryamine (~0.02%), traces of N-methyl-3,4-dimethoxy-B-phenethylamine
- Alkaloid amounts insufficient for psychopharmacological response
- Contains flavone retusin (3,3',4',7-tetramethoxy-5-hydroxyflavone)

### Sunami (Ariocarpus fissuratus)
- Also known as "living rock" or "dry whiskey"
- Used in Mexican folkloric medicine
- Contains: Mostly hordenine, less N-methyl-tyramine, some N-methyl-3,4-dimethoxy-B-phenethylamine
- Two varieties: var. lloydii and var. fissuratus (similar chemistry)
- Related species with similar alkaloids: A. kotschoubeyanus, A. trigonus

### Dolichothele species
- Species used: D. baumii, D. longimamma, D. melalenca, D. sphaerica, D. surculosa, D. uberiforma
- Contains: N-methylphenethylamine, B-O-methylsynephrine, N-methyltryamine, synephrine, hordenine, dolichotheline (N-isovalerylhistamine)

## Cactus Alkaloid Dictionary

Key alkaloids found across psychoactive cacti:

- Mescaline (3,4,5-trimethoxy-B-phenethylamine) -- main psychoactive component of peyote and San Pedro
- Hordenine (N,N-dimethyltyramine) -- mild sympathomimetic, antiseptic properties
- Tyramine -- mild sympathomimetic, some antiseptic activity
- Pellotine (1,2-dimethyl-6,7-dimethoxy-8-hydroxy tetrahydroisoquinoline) -- tetrahydroisoquinoline alkaloid
- Anhalidine (2-methyl-6,7-dimethoxy-8-hydroxy tetrahydroisoquinoline) -- tetrahydroisoquinoline
- Macromerine (N,N-dimethyl-3,4-dimethoxy-B-hydroxy-B-phenethylamine) -- ~1/5 mescaline potency, found only in Coryphantha
- Synephrine (N-methyl-4-hydroxy-B-phenethylamine) -- known sympathomimetic, probable macromerine precursor
- Homoveratrilamine (3,4-dimethoxy-B-phenethylamine) -- no activity alone, may alter mescaline experience
- Dolichotheline (N-isovalerylhistamine) -- imidazole alkaloid, found only in Dolichothele and Gymnocactus

## Harvesting Principles

- Best time: after long dry spell (alkaloids accumulate during drought)
- Worst time: during or after rain (alkaloid content can drop below 50%)
- Nitrogen-rich soil correlates with higher alkaloid content
- For peyote: decapitate above ground, leave roots; 3-inch button may be 20+ years old
- Alkaloids accumulate with age in these cacti
- Phenolic alkaloids (hordenine, tyramine) provide antibacterial activity
