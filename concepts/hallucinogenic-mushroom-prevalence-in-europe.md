### Countries with Highest Prevalence

The highest reported lifetime prevalence figures among 15–24 year-olds are found in the
Netherlands, the Czech Republic, the United Kingdom, Germany, and Ireland. The
Netherlands, with its long-standing smartshop culture and historical tolerance of
psychoactive mushroom sales, consistently reports elevated prevalence. The Czech Republic
also reports relatively high use, possibly reflecting more permissive cultural attitudes
toward certain drug categories. In the UK and Ireland, the ready availability of mushrooms
through head shops and online retailers prior to legislative changes contributed to
measurable use levels.

### Countries with Lowest Prevalence

At the lower end of the spectrum, Lithuania, Hungary, and France report lifetime
prevalence below 1% among 15–24 year-olds. In these countries, limited retail availability,
stricter enforcement, and different drug market dynamics appear to suppress use. Cultural
factors, including less established recreational drug subcultures, may also play a role in
the lower observed prevalence figures.

## School Survey Data (ESPAD 2003)

### ESPAD Methodology

The European School Survey Project on Alcohol and Other Drugs (ESPAD) collects
standardised data from students aged 15–16 across participating European countries. The
2003 survey round included questions on hallucinogenic mushroom use, providing a valuable
snapshot of adolescent experimentation with these substances. Because school surveys
capture younger respondents than general population surveys, they tend to yield lower
lifetime prevalence figures but can indicate emerging trends among adolescents.
