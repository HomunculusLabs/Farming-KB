## Fire Mandalas

Fire may be trying to get in (to a house or town) or out (from a public fireplace). The orientation does not much matter; what matters is that structures and vegetation are integrated and designed to block fire.

A fireproof array of plants consists of:

- All "sappy" perennials that will not burn unless permeated by grasses
- All species green in midsummer
- Sunflowers included as an annual
- Most annual gardens resist fire
- Damp mulch as a base layer

If such systems are fenced and poultry stocked after midsummer, their scratching and browsing greatly decrease the fire risk represented by grasses. If the system is closely planted and attended to, the chance of fire damaging any plants or structures behind such a barrier is slight.

## Integrated Fire Control Through Design

### Slope-Based Fire Protection

Fire is most intense advancing upslope. Dwellings should be sited behind lower dams for fire protection. Small standby fire pumps at high level provide emergency water access. Ridge-top storage dams ("turkey nests") serve as both gravity-fed water supply and fire-fighting reserves.

### Zone II Forest Placement

High-slope forests coupled with "thermal belt" create remarkable difference to midslope climate and soil temperatures. If forests are above Zones I and II, they present little or no fire danger to the dwelling. The warm down-draught from high forests on frosty nights is an additional benefit.

### Non-Inflammable Species

Some species with non-inflammable bark and foliage:

- Black locust (Robinia pseudoacacia) -- spiny leaves are toxic to stock but bark and foliage will not burn
- Tamarisk (Tamarix spp.) -- self-mulching, fire resistant
- Coprosma spp. -- resist fire
- Tree lucerne (Chamaecytisus proliferus) -- fire and frost resistant
- Mulga (Acacia aneura) -- fire resistant
