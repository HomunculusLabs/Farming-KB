# Psilocybe azurescens Species Guide

## Overview

psilocybe-azurescens Stamets and Gartz is among the most potent
psilocybin-containing mushrooms in the world. Known by common names
including "Astoriensis," "flying saucer mushroom," and "indigo
Psilocybe," this species was first identified near Astoria, Oregon
along the northern coast. P. azurescens is notable for its exceptional
potency, relatively high baeocystin content, and one of the strongest
bluing reactions of any known Psilocybe. The flesh actually becomes
indigo-black where damaged, a hallmark feature of this species. It was formally described by Stamets
and Gartz and is considered one of the most significant mycological
discoveries in the Pacific Northwest region.

## Macroscopic Features

**Cap:** 3-10 cm broad. Conic to convex, expanding to broadly convex
and eventually flattening with age, with a pronounced, persistent
broad umbo. Surface smooth, viscid when moist, covered by a separable
gelatinous pellicle. Chestnut to ochraceous brown to caramel in color,
often becoming pitted with dark blue or bluish black zones, hygrophanous,
fading to dingy brown in drying. Strongly bruising blue when damaged.
Margin even, sometimes irregular and eroded at maturity, slightly
incurved at first, soon decurved, flattening with maturity, translucent
striate and often pale azure tinted.

**Flesh:** At center 3-6 mm thick, cottony and whitish to pallid brown
at the stipe/pileus junction before bruising. Flesh rapidly bruising
blue, then darkening to deep caerulean blue and eventually indigo-black.

**Gills:** Attachment ascending, sinuate to adnate, brown, often
stained indigo black where injured. Close, with two tiers of lamellulae,
mottled, edges whitish. Spore print dark purplish brown to purplish
black in mass.

**Stem:** 90-200 mm long by 3-6 mm thick. Silky white, dingy brown
from the base or in age. Hollow at maturity. Composed of twisted,
cartilaginous, silky white fibrous tissue. Base thickening downwards,
often curved, characterized by coarse white aerial tufts of mycelium,
often with azure tones combined with dense, thick, silky white
rhizomorphs that tenaciously attach to wood chips or dead grass. The
stem strongly bruises bluish upon disturbance.

**Veil:** White, cortinate, often leaving a fibrillose annular zone in
the superior regions of the stem. Taste is extremely bitter. Odor none
to slightly farinaceous.

## Microscopic Features

Spores are dark purplish black in deposit, 12-13.5 by 6.5-8 microns,
ellipsoid. Basidia are 4-spored. Pleurocystidia are abundant,
fusoid-ventricose, tapering to a narrow but short neck, bluntly
papillate, 23-35 by 9-10 microns. Cheilocystidia are nearly identical
to pleurocystidia, measuring 23-28 by 6.5-8 microns. Tissue is notably
awash with bluish tones.

## Habitat and Distribution

P. azurescens grows cespitose to gregarious on deciduous wood chips
and/or in sandy soils rich in ligninous debris. This mushroom naturally
grows, often prolifically, along the northern Oregon coast near
Astoria, favoring the land adjacent to the shoreline. The species has
a strong affection for dune grasses, especially Ammophila maritima,
with which it is closely associated. By forming extensive, dense, and
tenacious mycelial mats, P. azurescens causes the whitening of wood.

Fruiting begins in late September and continues well after the first
frost, often into late December and early January. As an adaptive
species, outdoor beds have been established with ease in the United
States and elsewhere. It is a cold weather-tolerant species, one of the
hardiest of the active Psilocybes.

## Potency and Chemistry

P. azurescens is one of the most potent species in the world. Reported
alkaloid concentrations include up to 1.78% psilocybin, 0.38%
psilocin, and 0.35% baeocystin (Stamets and Gartz 1995). After six
months of storage, analyses revealed that this species retained most of
its original potency. The relatively high baeocystin content is roughly
on par with psilocybe-semilanceata.

Ingesting P. azurescens can produce profoundly intense experiences. At
higher doses, some people report a loss of muscular control and
strength that can persist into the following day. These effects have
also been reported with psilocybe-cyanescens and panaeolus-subbalteatus.

## Distinguishing Features from Related Species

The silky white stem, caramel-colored cap, relatively large stature,
non-undulating cap margin, and broad but pronounced umbo are
representative features. psilocybe-cyanescens is similar but can be
macroscopically distinguished by its much smaller stature and
characteristically sine-wave margin. A variety sometimes called
"[[psilocybe-cyanescens]] Ossip" from the Astoria area bears resemblance
to P. azurescens.
