# Schizophyllan SPG Clinical Cancer Trials

Schizophyllan (also known as Sonifilan, Sizofiran, or SPG) is a
polysaccharide derived from [[schizophyllum-commune]], a common
Basidiomycete mushroom. It is a beta(1,3)D glucan with beta(1,6)D
glucan side-chains. As with all glucan preparations, Schizophyllan
molecules are not homologous in terms of molecular weight but consist
of molecules with a wide range of molecular weights. The molecules
are large and are normally administered in the clinical setting by
the intramuscular or intraperitoneal route.

Schizophyllan has been approved for clinical use in Japan and is
currently produced commercially by several Japanese pharmaceutical
companies. Despite its clinical availability, it remains less well
known internationally than PSK or Lentinan.

## Pre-Clinical Activity

Schizophyllan has been shown to be cytostatic in Sarcoma 180 tumour
xenografts. The survival of Sarcoma 180 xenografts was not affected by
pre-treatment with Schizophyllan, while combined pre- and post-treatment
and post-treatment alone resulted in increased survival. Notably,
Schizophyllan had no effect on the survival of Sarcoma 37, Ehrlich
carcinoma or Yoshida sarcoma ascites tumours (Wasser and Weis, 1999),
indicating tumour-type specificity in its mechanism of action.

## Gastric Cancer Trials

Early clinical studies with Schizophyllan in combination with
conventional chemotherapy (tegafur or mitomycin C and 5-fluorouracil)
in a randomised controlled study of 367 patients with recurrent and
inoperable gastric cancer found a significant increase in median
survival (Furne, 1985). However, a similar study by Fugimoto et al.
(1984) was unable to confirm this apparent success, highlighting the
inconsistency of results in early clinical trials.

The conflicting results between these two large trials underscore the
importance of rigorous trial design and the challenges inherent in
evaluating immunomodulatory agents where patient immune status varies
considerably.

## Head and Neck Cancer

Schizophyllan has been shown to increase overall survival of patients
with head and neck cancers (Kimura et al., 1994). This finding has
been replicated and represents one of the more consistent clinical
results for Schizophyllan across different study centres.

## Cervical Cancer and Radiotherapy Combination

In a randomised controlled study of Schizophyllan in combination with
radiotherapy, Schizophyllan significantly prolonged overall survival
of Stage II cervical cancer patients but not Stage III (Okamura et
al., 1986, 1989). The staging-specific benefit suggests that immune
function plays a greater role in earlier-stage disease where tumour
burden is lower and the immune system may be more responsive to
immunostimulation.

## Multi-Modal Cancer Trial

In a prospective, randomised clinical trial involving 312 patients
treated with surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy (fluorouracil) and
Schizophyllan in various combinations, patients treated with
Schizophyllan had better overall survival than those who had not
received the polysaccharide (Miyazaki et al., 1995). However, the
variety of treatment regimes significantly reduced the value of these
results.

Importantly, separate analyses of patients with 10% or more activated
CD4+ cells out of their total CD4+ population and with more than 25%
activated CD8+ cells before treatment showed that in this group the
Schizophyllan-induced increase in survival was highly significant.
This suggests that patient immune status is a critical predictor of
response to mushroom polysaccharide immunotherapy.

## Intratumoral Injection and Local Immune Response

When Schizophyllan is injected intratumorally into cervical cancers,
there is a significant infiltration of Langerhans cells and T-cells
(Nakano et al., 1996). This local immune cell infiltration represents
a direct mechanism of antitumour action at the tumour site. Langerhans
cells are a type of dendritic cell resident in epithelial tissues that
play a key role in antigen presentation and T-cell activation.

## Limitations

Various clinical trials have been carried out in Japan, although many
are not blinded. The lack of placebo-controlled, double-blinded trials
limits the strength of evidence. The requirement for intramuscular or
intraperitoneal administration (due to large molecular size) is a
practical limitation compared to orally bioavailable alternatives such
as PSK, PSP or Maitake D-fraction.
