## 2. Nutrient Cycling

### Aerobic Tea

Aerobic compost teas contain the full spectrum of organisms needed for nutrient cycling:

- **Bacteria and fungi** immobilize nutrients in their biomass, preventing leaching
- **Protozoa** (flagellates and amoebae) consume bacteria and release plant-available nitrogen
- **Nematodes** consume fungi and bacteria, cycling additional nutrients
- If no plants are present, other bacteria and fungi consume released nutrients, maintaining the cycle

This creates a self-regulating [[comparison-soil-protozoa-vs-nematodes-in-nutrient-cycling]]. Beneficial fungi, protozoa, and nematodes are all killed. Nutrients become locked in bacterial biomass with no predators to release them. Nitrogen is lost as ammonia and nitrous oxide, sulfur is lost as hydrogen sulfide gas (the rotten egg smell), and pH drops into the acidic range. Nutrient cycling stops entirely and anaerobic brews actively deplete soil nutrients.

## 3. Soil Nutrient Levels

Aerobic teas add both soluble nutrients and living organisms to soil, extracting nutrients from compost and amendments (kelp, fish hydrolysate, yucca). Organisms in the tea retain nutrients in the root zone and make them more available through chelation and mineralization. Anaerobic processes have the opposite effect: nitrogen, sulfur, and phosphorus are converted to gaseous forms and lost, resulting in net nutrient depletion when anaerobic brews are applied.

## 4. Soil Structure Building

Soil structure is built through biological processes that only aerobic organisms can perform. Bacteria produce glues that form microaggregates, fungi produce glues (including glomalin) that form macroaggregates, and protozoa, nematodes, and microarthropods create pores and passageways as they move through soil. These functions create hallways for water and air movement, with larger pores holding water against gravity as reservoirs for dry periods. Anaerobic conditions destroy soil structure by killing aerobic bacteria (destroying microaggregate glues), attacking fungi, and eliminating protozoa, nematodes, and microarthropods.

## 5. Toxin Decomposition

### Aerobic Tea

Aerobic compost teas add the specific organisms needed to break down and remove toxic materials:

- Certain bacteria decompose pesticide residues, herbicide carryover, and hydrocarbon contamination
- Fungi produce enzymes that break down complex toxic compounds
