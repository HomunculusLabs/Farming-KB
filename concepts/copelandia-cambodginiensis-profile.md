# Copelandia cambodginiensis Profile

## Taxonomy and Synonyms

Panaeolus cambodginiensis Ola'h and Heim is a potently active
mushroom also known by its synonym Copelandia cambodginiensis
(Ola'h and Heim) Singer and Weeks. The species was originally
described from Cambodia. It belongs to the section Copelandia
within the genus [[stamets-panaeolus-cyanescens-copelandia-identification-ecology]], which is distinguished from other
Panaeoli by a strong bluing reaction, a dung-loving habitat, and
large pleurocystidia. See also [[stamets-panaeolus-cyanescens-copelandia-identification-ecology]] and
[[panaeolus-tropicalis]] for closely related Copelandian species
that share many morphological and ecological characteristics.

## Macroscopic Features

### Cap

The cap measures 1.2 to 2.5 cm broad. It is conic-convex at first,
soon becoming hemispheric and expanding to broadly convex, and
eventually nearly plane. The surface is smooth, moist to viscid
when wet, soon drying, and often cracking in age. The cap color is
nearly chocolate brown, quickly fading in maturity to yellowish
brown, often with olive greenish gray tones along the cap margin.
The flesh is pale but quickly bruises bluish upon injury. The
margin is incurved when young, often irregular, rarely with fine
remnants of the veil but not truly appendiculate.

### Gills

The gills are pallid at first, soon becoming grayish black to black
with age and mottled from the uneven ripening of spores, a pattern
characteristic of the genus [[stamets-panaeolus-cyanescens-copelandia-identification-ecology]]. Gill attachment is
ascending and uncinate, with several tiers of intermediate gills
inserted. The mottled appearance at maturity is a key feature for
field identification that this species shares with all Panaeoli.

### Stem

The stem measures 5 to 95 mm long by 3.5 to 5 mm thick. It is
centrally attached, even, and swelling towards the base, and can
be difficult to separate from the cap without gill fragments. The
upper regions of the stem are irregular with horizontal fissures.
Young primordia sometimes show a fragile annular zone, though this
is not persistent. The stem is whitish to cream in color, brown
near the base, and rapidly bruises bluish when injured, a hallmark
of the [[psilocybin-mushroom-bluing-reaction-chemistry]] found in
Copelandian Panaeoli.

## Microscopic Features

Spores are blackish brown in deposit, lemon-shaped, smooth, and
measure 10.5 to 12 by 6.5 to 9 micrometers. They have a relatively
large, centrally located germ pore and are dark brown under the
microscope, non-translucent. The basidia are 4-spored.
Pleurocystidia are present, fusoid-ventricose with a sharp
elongated apex, measuring 48 to 60 by 13 to 19 micrometers.
Cheilocystidia are lageniform to clavate, measuring 12 to 14
(occasionally 20) by 2.5 to 5 (occasionally 7.5) micrometers. The
large pleurocystidia are a defining feature of the Copelandian
section.

## Habitat and Distribution

Panaeolus cambodginiensis grows scattered to gregarious on the dung
of water buffalo. It was originally described from Cambodia and is
thought to be widespread throughout the Asian subtropics. Merlin
and Allen (1993) reported this species from Kahalu'u, O'ahu,
Hawaii, indicating a broader distribution in the Pacific. The
species likely occurs wherever suitable large herbivore dung is
available in tropical and subtropical climates, including
throughout Southeast Asia.

## Chemical Constituents and Potency

This is a strongly bluing, potently active species. Merlin and
Allen (1993) reported the presence of psilocybin and psilocin at
0.55% and 0.6%, respectively, making it comparable in potency to
[[stamets-panaeolus-cyanescens-copelandia-identification-ecology]]. The strong bluing reaction is consistent
with the relatively high psilocin content. The golden color of the
cap, especially towards the disc, is noted by Ola'h (1969) as a
distinguishing feature from close relatives.

## Comparison with Related Species

According to Ola'h (1969), P. cambodginiensis is larger in stature
than its close relative Panaeolus tropicalis and generally smaller
than [[stamets-panaeolus-cyanescens-copelandia-identification-ecology]]. However, Stamets notes that the
composition of the dung substrate likely affects the size of the
fruitbodies, making size-based distinctions of doubtful taxonomic
significance. Panaeolus bispora is virtually identical macroscopically
except that it has exclusively 2-spored basidia.

## Cultivation and Spread

The late Steven H. Pollock successfully cultivated this species in
a garden in Texas in the 1970s. From there, it may have escaped
into the Gulf Coast mycoflora, potentially establishing naturalized
populations in the southern United States. This history highlights
the possibility of anthropogenic dispersal of Copelandian Panaeoli
beyond their native tropical and subtropical ranges.

## Ethnobotanical Context

Copelandia cambodginiensis has likely been used traditionally in
Southeast Asia, where several dung-inhabiting Copelandian Panaeoli
are known to be consumed for their psychoactive properties. The
original description from Cambodia, combined with its reported
occurrence across the Asian subtropics, suggests a long history
of human interaction with this species. The potent activity level
of 0.55% psilocybin and 0.6% psilocin places it among the more
powerful naturally occurring psilocybin mushrooms, comparable to
[[tropisms-psilocybe-cubensis-gravitropism]] and [[stamets-psi-psilocybe-azurescens-species-guide]] in overall
psychoactive strength.

## Key Identification Features Summary

The combination of dung habitat, small stature, chocolate brown
cap fading to yellowish brown, strong bluing reaction, lemon-shaped
spores, and large fusoid-ventricose pleurocystidia with sharp
apices distinguish C. cambodginiensis from other Copelandian
Panaeoli. The olive greenish gray tones along the cap margin are
an additional subtle feature. Collectors in tropical and
subtropical regions should be familiar with this species and its
close relatives, as the potential for misidentification among
Copelandian Panaeoli is significant without microscopic
examination.
