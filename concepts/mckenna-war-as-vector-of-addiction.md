## Morphine and the American Civil War

The pattern begins with morphine, isolated in 1805 by German chemist Friedrich
Serturner and named after Morpheus, the Greek god of dreams. Morphine use
remained restricted until the invention of the hypodermic syringe by Alexander
Wood in 1853. The syringe arrived just in time to be used to inject morphine
into soldiers wounded in the American Civil War.

The result was significant addict populations in both Europe and the United
States. So many Civil War veterans returned home addicted to injectable morphine
that journalists called morphine addiction "the soldier's disease."

## The Franco-Prussian War

The same pattern repeated during the Franco-Prussian War (1870-1871), where
morphine was widely used for battlefield injuries, creating another wave of
addiction among veterans. The syringe, originally a medical innovation, had
become an instrument of mass addiction through wartime deployment.

## The Opium Wars: State-Sponsored Drug Trafficking

The most egregious example of war as drug vector was the British Opium Wars
against China (1838-1842, 1856-1860). The pattern began with the prohibition of
tobacco smoking in China by the last Ming emperor (1628-1644), which led
frustrated tobacco addicts to experiment with smoking opium. By 1793, opium and
tobacco were being routinely smoked together throughout China.

Despite Chinese prohibition of opium importation beginning in 1729, imports
continued to rise, reaching more than 25,000 chests annually by 1830. English
financial interests manipulated the situation into open warfare:

- The East India Company grew opium in India and sold it at auction in Calcutta.
- After the auction, the Company "abjured all responsibility for the drug."
- Chinese Commissioner Lin destroyed 20,000 chests of opium in Canton harbor.
- The British declared war in 1840, bombarded Chinese forts, and captured
  Chusan.
- China was forced to cede Hong Kong and pay a massive indemnity.
- The Treaty of Tientsin eventually legalized the Chinese opium traffic.

McKenna called this "capitalistic terrorism and true drug enslavement on a mass
scale." The pattern established by England's opium diplomacy became the model
for twentieth-century government collusion in international drug trafficking.

filled by a more potent, more dangerous substitute.
