# Spectroscopic Characterization of Synthetic Psilocin and Psilocybin as Forensic Standards

## Overview

The Shirota, Hakamata, and Goda (2003) synthesis of psilocin and psilocybin provides comprehensive spectroscopic data that serves as reference standards for [[australian-magic-mushroom-common-names]]" materials. The difficulty of isolating psilocin (due to its instability) and psilocybin (due to its high polarity) from fungal material on a gram scale makes synthetic standards essential for chromatographic methods including TLC, GC, and HPLC used in forensic laboratories worldwide.

## Regulatory Context

Since June 6, 2002, fungi containing psilocin and psilocybin have been regulated under the Narcotics and Psychotropic Control Law in Japan. The morphological and [[ceremonial-shamanic-use-psychoactive-mushrooms]] is extremely difficult without expert mycological knowledge, necessitating chromatographic confirmation. This regulatory need was the primary motivation for developing reliable gram-scale syntheses of reference standards.

## UV-Visible Spectroscopy

### Psilocin (1)

The UV spectrum of synthetic psilocin measured in methanol showed four absorption maxima:

- λmax 222.5 nm (log ε = 4.55)
- λmax 268.0 nm (log ε = 3.72)
- λmax 284.5 nm (log ε = 3.62)
- λmax 294.0 nm (log ε = 3.58)

This four-band pattern is characteristic of the 4-hydroxyindole chromophore with an electron-donating substituent at the 4-position. The most intense absorption at 222.5 nm corresponds to the π→π* transition of the indole ring system, while the longer wavelength bands arise from vibronic progression.

### Psilocybin (2)

Psilocybin in methanol displayed a similar but slightly shifted UV pattern:

- λmax 221.0 nm (log ε = 4.44)
- λmax 267.5 nm (log ε = 3.66)
- λmax 278.5 nm (log ε = 3.57)
- λmax 290.0 nm (log ε = 3.42)

The modest bathochromic shift of the longest wavelength band (from 294.0 to 290.0 nm) reflects the electron-withdrawing effect of the phosphate ester group at the 4-oxygen, which reduces the electron-donating character of the hydroxyl substituent. The molar absorptivities are somewhat lower than psilocin, consistent with the phosphate group reducing overall chromophore intensity.

## Infrared Spectroscopy

### Psilocin

The IR spectrum of psilocin as a KBr pellet showed characteristic bands at:

- 3285 cm⁻¹ (N-H stretch, indole hydrogen bonding)
- 2959 cm⁻¹ (C-H stretch, aliphatic and aromatic)
- 1620 cm⁻¹ (C=C ring stretch, indole)
- 1588 cm⁻¹ (aromatic C=C stretch)
- 1473 cm⁻¹ (N-CH₃ deformation)
- 1258 cm⁻¹ (C-O stretch, phenolic)
- 1232 cm⁻¹ (C-N stretch, amine)
- 833 cm⁻¹ (aromatic C-H out-of-plane)
- 722 cm⁻¹ (aromatic ring deformation)

The phenolic C-O stretch at 1258 cm⁻¹ confirms the presence of the free hydroxyl group at the 4-position.

### Psilocybin

The IR spectrum of psilocybin showed:

- 3266 cm⁻¹ (O-H stretch, phosphate)
- 3034 cm⁻¹ (aromatic C-H stretch)
- 2731 cm⁻¹ (N-CH₃ C-H stretch)
- 1620 cm⁻¹ (indole C=C)
- 1580 cm⁻¹ (aromatic C=C)
- 1439 cm⁻¹ (P-O-C stretch)
- 1352 cm⁻¹ (P=O stretch)
- 1298 cm⁻¹ (P-O stretch)
- 1244 cm⁻¹ (C-O-P stretch)
- 1103 cm⁻¹ (P-OH deformation)
- 926 cm⁻¹ (P-O-C aromatic)
- 804 cm⁻¹ (aromatic substitution pattern)

The phosphate moiety introduces distinctive bands in the 1400-900 cm⁻¹ region, particularly the P=O stretch at 1352 cm⁻¹ and the P-O-C stretches that differentiate psilocybin from psilocin.
