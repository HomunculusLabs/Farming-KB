# The Tilther — Electric Greenhouse Tillage Tool

## Overview

The Tilther is a custom-designed electric tillage tool created by [[azurescens-outdoor-bed-preparation-woodchips]] between succession crops in winter-harvest greenhouses. Powered by a cordless drill, the Tilther produces no fumes and minimal noise, addressing the practical problems of using gas-powered rotary tillers in enclosed greenhouse spaces.

## Design Rationale

### The Problem with Conventional Tillers

Greenhouse beds require frequent replanting — often within 24 hours of harvesting a previous crop. Outdoors, Coleman uses a ten-horsepower walk-behind rotary tiller for soil preparation, but this tool is unsuited to greenhouse use for several reasons:

- It is awkward to maneuver for preparing just one or two beds at a time
- Gas engine fumes accumulate dangerously in [[fukuoka-textdoc-composting-critique-futility-prepared-organic-matter]] is at the surface

## Mechanical Design

### Power Source

The Tilther is powered by a cordless drill that sits on top of the housing. A rope around the drill's trigger is pulled from the handlebars, providing throttle control. A charger for the cordless drill batteries is kept in the greenhouse so batteries can be rotated as needed during work sessions.

### Drive System

The gearbox is mounted on the side of the housing rather than in the center. This side-mounted configuration is critical because it eliminates the untilled strip that center-gearbox tillers leave down the middle of the bed. Power is transmitted from the drill to a shaft connected by a chain to the tine shaft below.

### Tines

The tines are designed to thoroughly incorporate amendments into just the top 2 inches of soil. They chop and mix without digging deeply, maintaining the soil stratification that is important for greenhouse soil health.

## Place in the Soil Preparation Sequence

The Tilther occupies a specific step in Coleman's greenhouse bed preparation process, which proceeds in this order:

1. **Remove old crop debris** — Pull out spent plants or mow off residues with a scythe
2. **Broadfork the bed** — Make a pass down the bed with a broadfork to aerate the lower soil, penetrating 10 inches deep and lifting slightly to loosen compaction
3. **Apply amendments** — Spread alfalfa meal, dried crab waste, or other soil amendments as needed
4. **Tilther** — Use the Tilther to incorporate amendments into the top 2 inches
5. **Apply compost** — Spread a layer of finished compost on the surface
6. **Rake smooth** — Use a 30-inch-wide Austrian hay rake to mix compost shallowly into the top half inch and create a smooth seedbed
7. **Firm the bed** — Make one pass with a 30-inch-wide mesh-surfaced roller to provide purchase for the seeder wheels

## Compost Application Technique

Coleman has found that compost applications in the greenhouse are most effective when left on the surface and mixed only shallowly into the top half inch.
