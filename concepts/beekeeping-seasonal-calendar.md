# Beekeeping Seasonal Calendar

Successful beekeeping requires aligning management tasks with the natural
annual cycle of the colony. This calendar provides month-by-month guidance
for [[fukuoka-natural-farming-temperate-climates]] (USDA zones 5-7). Adjust timing by 2-4 weeks for
warmer or cooler zones.

## Late Winter (February - March)

### February
- Order packages, nucs, or queens for spring
- Check hive weight — heft hives from behind; feed sugar cakes or dry sugar
  if light (colonies can starve in late winter)
- Ensure entrances are clear of dead bees and debris
- Monitor weather forecasts for rapid warm-ups triggering cleansing flights

### March
- First full inspection on a calm day above 55F (13C)
- Assess colony strength — look for brood pattern, queen presence, food stores
- Perform oxalic acid vapor treatment for Varroa if broodless period exists
- Begin syrup feeding (1:1 ratio) to stimulate brood production
- Reverse brood boxes if the cluster has moved up and the bottom box is empty
- Clean bottom boards and scrape propolis

## Spring Build-Up (April - May)

### April
- Continue 1:1 syrup feeding until nectar flow is strong
- Install package bees or nucs when available
- Monitor for queen cells — first sign of swarming intent
- Add drawn comb supers when 6-8 frames of brood are present
- Perform Varroa monitoring with alcohol wash or sugar roll
- Begin [[swarm-capture-guide]] measures

### May
- Primary swarming season — inspect weekly for queen cells
- Perform splits to control swarming and increase colony count
- Rotate in fresh foundation or drawn comb
- Super colonies ahead of the main nectar flow
- Plant [[cover-crops]] for mid-summer gaps
- Monitor for American foulbrood, European foulbrood, and chalkbrood

## Summer Nectar Flow (June - August)

### June
- Main nectar flow in most regions — keep supers on
- Reduce or stop syrup feeding when natural nectar is abundant
- Continue weekly swarm checks early in the month
- Harvest early honey if frames are capped (at least 80%)
- Monitor Varroa levels — treat if threshold exceeded (3% mite load)
- Ensure adequate ventilation — prop up inner covers or use screened boards

### July
- Peak [[faires-beekeeping-and-honey-production]] in many areas
- Harvest honey as supers fill
- Begin queen rearing if [[queen-rearing-basics]]
- Watch for dearth periods — feed if no nectar available
- Monitor for small hive beetle and wax moth in weaker colonies
- Provide water source near hives (shallow pan with pebbles)

### August
- After the main flow, harvest remaining supers
- Assess colony health and strength for winter
- Begin fall Varroa treatment — critical for winter survival
- Combine weak colonies; requeen failing ones
- Start feeding 2:1 syrup to build winter stores if needed

## Fall Preparation (September - October)

### September
- Complete Varroa treatments before winter bees are raised
- Feed 2:1 syrup heavily — colonies need 60-80 lbs of honey for winter
  [[query-how-do-i-grow-figs-in-cold-climates]]
- Reduce entrance size with entrance reducers to deter robbing and mice
- Install mouse guards
- Remove queen excluders to allow the queen to move into winter stores
- Treat for nosema with Fumidil-B if warranted

### October
- Final feeding — stop when colonies refuse syrup or temps drop below 50F
- Insulation wraps may be needed in extreme cold zones (zone 4 and below)
- Elevate hives slightly to improve bottom ventilation and prevent moisture rot
- Document colony status: queen age, strength rating, stores estimate
- Store extracted supers with para-moth crystals to prevent wax moth damage

## Winter (November - January)

### November
- Hives should not be opened except for emergency feeding
- Monitor entrance activity on warm days — dead bee removal is normal
- Ensure upper ventilation to prevent condensation on the cluster
- Wrap hives with black roofing paper or commercial wraps in cold zones
- Maintain quiet around the apiary — avoid bumping hives

### December - January
- Minimal intervention — let the cluster manage itself
- Order equipment, read, and plan for the coming season
- Build or repair frames, boxes, and other gear
- Attend beekeeping association meetings and workshops
- Monitor hive weight by hefting on warm days; emergency feed with sugar
  cakes above the inner cover if light

## Seasonal Varroa Management Cycle

Varroa destructor is the most serious threat to honey bee colonies. A
seasonal [[comparison-beneficial-insects-guide-vs-integrated-pest-management]] approach:

1. **Late winter** — oxalic acid vapor (broodless window)
2. **Spring** — monitor only; avoid treatments during buildup
3. **Summer** — monitor; treat if threshold exceeded (formic or amitraz)
4. **Early fall** — aggressive treatment before winter brood is raised

See [[beekeeping-problems-and-solutions]] for detailed [[hamilton-pest-and-disease-control-organic]]
management, and [[cover-crops]] for ensuring adequate nutrition
throughout the season.
