# Plant Diseases and Remedies

The home gardener has no effective cure for plant diseases once they take hold. However, diseases can often be prevented by growing healthy, naturally disease-resistant plants using appropriate varieties and making highly fertile soil. When disease does strike, some practical remedies can slow its progress and save the crop.

## The Fundamental Truth

When some plants are getting diseased or when conditions are unfavorable for certain types of vegetables, almost any variety will become sick. Acceptance is possible, and growing a diverse garden that contains half-hardy vegetables almost certain to produce something in any weather provides resilience.

## Preventive Approaches

### Compost Tea Spray

Spray your garden with liquid kelp or compost tea, which provides [[soil-trace-elements-and-micronutrients]] other fortifying elements that improve plants' resistance to disease.

### The Elaine Ingham Method

Dr. Blaine (Elaine) Ingham, an Australian at Oregon State University, discovered that compost teas brewed from high-quality composts contain [[forest-and-bamboo-beneficial-microorganisms]] that, applied to foliage, can prevent disease-causing organisms from establishing on leaf surfaces. A week or so after spraying, they prevent disease from gaining a foothold.

**Important**: This is not simply a matter of dumping compost into a barrel and brewing "tea." The compost has to be quality stuff — the ratio of manure to vegetation, and the quality of that vegetation (how much woody matter vs. green stuff) determines which diseases the tea will control.

### Regular Foliar Feeding

Regular (weekly) foliar feeding of vegetables is a good idea in any case. It doesn't cost much. Every gardener should experiment with:
- Homebrewed compost/manure teas
- Liquid kelp sprays
- Fish emulsion sprays
- [[hydroponic-nutrient-management]] solutions as foliars (for non-organic gardeners)

## Specific Diseases

### Powdery Mildew

**Symptoms**: Covers the photosynthetic surfaces of the leaf with a grey cast and interferes with food-making. Quickly kills most plants it attacks.

**The Baking Soda Remedy** (effective but short-lasting):
- 1 measured teaspoon (5 ml) baking soda per quart (liter) of water
- Add just enough liquid soap or dishwashing detergent to make droplets spread out and cover the leaf instead of beading up
- A spray instantly kills the mildew

**Limitations**:
- The remedy is short-lasting and requires painstaking attention
- PMD usually occurs when conditions are unfavorable to the plant
- After spraying it away, PMD will reappear in a few days
- If PMD occurs during a short spell of unseasonably unfavorable weather, stopping it for a few days can save the day

**Enhancement**: Mix kelp tea and fish emulsion into the spray tank along with the baking soda/soap solution for added plant nutrition while treating.

### Blight (General)

**Late blight** of tomatoes and potatoes can destroy entire crops during damp, cool summers. The infection spreads rapidly in humid conditions.

**Management**:
- Choose resistant varieties
- Ensure good air circulation between plants
- Remove and destroy infected plant material immediately
- Don't save seed from diseased plants
- Crop rotation helps break disease cycles

### Bacterial Wilt

Commonly seen on cucurbits, bacterial wilt causes sudden wilting of individual runners or entire plants. It can be confused with squash bug damage, but plants don't recover from true bacterial wilt.

**Management**:
- Control cucumber beetles (which transmit the bacteria)
- Remove and destroy infected plants immediately
- Rotate crops away from cucurbits for 2-3 years

### Blossom End Rot

A physiological disorder (not a pathogen) of tomatoes and peppers caused by [[knf-calcium-deficiency-symptoms]] or irregular watering.

**Management**:
- Maintain consistent soil moisture
- Don't let soil dry out between waterings
- Ensure adequate calcium in soil (lime if needed)
- Some varieties are more susceptible than others

### Club Root

A soil-borne disease affecting brassicas that causes swollen, distorted roots and stunted growth.

**Management**:
- Raise soil pH to 7.0+ with lime (club root thrives in acid soil)
- Practice long rotation (7+ years)
- Remove and destroy infected roots
- Resistant varieties are available

## The Gardening Aikido Approach to Disease

### Accept Losses Gracefully

Some years the weather is simply unfavorable. Blight may blacken tomatoes, powdery mildew may cover cucumbers, and there's nothing to be done. A diverse garden with many different crops ensures that even in a bad year, something will produce.

### Plant for Redundancy

Plant twice as much as you need. If diseases wipe out half your crop, you still have enough. This is especially important for expensive transplants.

### Focus on Soil Health

The organic doctrine holds that a truly healthy plant will either be unassailable or will outgrow disease. Making perfectly fertile soil is the key defense against most plant diseases.

## End-of-Season Sanitation

Thorough cleanup at season's end is one of the most important [[cervantes-damping-off-seedling-disease-prevention]] practices:

- Remove all diseased plant material from the garden
- Burn or hot-compost infected material (regular composting may not kill all pathogens)
- Clean up all plant residue, boards, and debris where [[mushroom-pests-and-diseases]] can overwinter
- Don't leave root masses in the ground — dig them up and compost them
