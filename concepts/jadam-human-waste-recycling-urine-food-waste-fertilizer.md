# JADAM Human Waste Recycling — Urine and Food Waste as Liquid Fertilizer

## Overview

JADAM advocates for the use of human urine, human manure, and food by-products as liquid fertilizer ingredients, arguing that these materials are superior to livestock manure and represent an underutilized resource for ultra-low-cost agriculture.

## The Human Fertilizer Argument

### Against Livestock Dependency

"Many argue that organic farming should be combined with organic livestock farming. The reason is that they want to promote the use of livestock manure as fertilizer. I do not understand why you would need animals when your family can produce better quality fertilizer!"

Cho challenges the assumption that organic farming requires livestock. Human waste, he argues, is a superior fertilizer source that eliminates the need for animal husbandry.

### The Body as Fertilizer Machine

"Our body is the ultimate fertilizer producing machine." Every human produces a continuous supply of nitrogen-rich fertilizer in the form of urine and organic matter in the form of food waste. JADAM captures this resource through simple collection and fermentation systems.

### Production Volume

A single person can produce "over 2 T (528 gal) of human manure liquid fertilizer, over 2 T (528 gal) of urine fertilizer, and 2 T (528 gal) of food by-product liquid fertilizer." This represents a substantial volume of high-quality fertilizer at zero input cost.

## Urine JADAM Liquid Fertilizer

### Nitrogen Richness

"Urine JLF is over 30 times richer in nitrogen compared to wild grass JLF." Human urine is one of the most concentrated sources of nitrogen available to farmers, making it an excellent replacement for chemical nitrogen fertilizers.

### Collection and Fermentation

"Collect urine all the time, throw in leaf mold occasionally, keep lid closed to limit evaporation." The process is simple: collect urine in a sealed container with leaf mold as a microbial inoculant, and allow it to ferment for at least 6 months.

### Dilution and Application

"Dilution: 50-500 times. When to apply: Early to mid-growth." The high nitrogen concentration requires significant dilution to avoid burning plants. JADAM recommends using urine JLF primarily during the vegetative growth phase when nitrogen demand is highest.

### Regulatory Compliance

"Organic farming regulations says it has to be used after full fermentation and dilution." JADAM acknowledges regulatory requirements for human waste fertilizers, which typically mandate extended fermentation and dilution to eliminate pathogens.

### Supplemental Use

"When growth seems weak, use urine JLF diluted ×100 to supplement N." Urine JLF is used as a supplement to other liquid fertilizers rather than as the sole fertilizer source.

## Food Waste Liquid Fertilizer

### Cherry Tomato JLF

"Gather cherry tomatoes that were unsold, left-over, diseased, ugly, malformed, etc. Fill half the container with water, put in a handful of leaf mold. Put in the cherry tomato residues as you gather them."

JADAM provides specific recipes for liquid fertilizer made from crop residues and unsold produce, turning waste products into valuable inputs.

### The Egg Analogy

"Remember that fruits are like eggs—the perfect food." The nutritional density of fruits makes them excellent fertilizer ingredients when fermented.

### Purslane JLF

"Purslane is one of the fastest disintegrating wild grasses." Wild plants like purslane, which grow abundantly without cultivation, provide free and effective fertilizer material.

## Nutritional Analysis

JADAM provides detailed nutritional analysis tables for each liquid fertilizer type, including pH, electrical conductivity (EC), organic matter content, total carbon and nitrogen, C/N ratio, macronutrients (P₂O₅, K₂O, CaO, MgO), and micronutrients (Fe, Mn, Zn, Cu, B, Mo).

The analysis demonstrates that JADAM liquid fertilizers provide a complete nutrient profile comparable to or better than commercial organic fertilizers—at a fraction of the cost.
