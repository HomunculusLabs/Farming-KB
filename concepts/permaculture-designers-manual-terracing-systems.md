# Terracing Systems
# Permaculture Designers Manual - Earth-Shaping for Slope Management

updated: 2026-04-18

## Overview

Terracing is one of the most ancient and effective earth-shaping techniques for managing sloped land. In permaculture design, terraces serve multiple functions: soil conservation, water retention, microclimate creation, and the establishment of productive growing areas on otherwise difficult terrain. From the wet rice terraces of Asia to dry terrace grain systems, terracing has sustained civilizations for millennia.

Terraces are integral to [[soil-erosion-control]], [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-soil-and-humus-management]], and food production systems worldwide. The Ifugao terraces of the Philippines, for example, have been in continuous production for up to 5000 years.

## Types of Terraces

### Broad Contour Banks Evolving to Terraces

On gentle slopes (2-8 degrees) in the wet-dry tropics, a series of large contour banks or swales have an excellent soil preservation effect. Coupled with the gradual development of a terrace, the retention of wet-season water, and mulch-providing hedgerow, this ensures a stable situation. Between the main hedges, mulch hedgerow and borders can be developed in crop.

### Dry Terraces

Where no streams exist to feed the terrace system, dry terraces hold soil against erosion in cropped areas. Lacking streams, a deep [[permaculture-designers-manual-mulch-systems]] keeps terrace soils moist. Dry terraces are suited to:
- Millet (summer or dry periods)
- Dryland rice (spring-summer)
- Barley, wheat, rye (winter and cool periods, spring wheat varieties, brassicas, fava beans)
- Amaranth (summer grains, spring greens)
- Quinoa (summer grains)
- Rape/mustard (winter oils and oil seed)
- Lentils, peas (intercrop and nitrogen fixing grain legumes)
- Grams and pulses (intercrop and nitrogen fixing)

### Wet Terraces (Padi)

Where a stream or part of a stream can be led to upper terraces, wet crops such as rice, taro, watercress, kangkong, and water chestnuts can be cultivated in water-level controlled padi. This is the rich wet terrace culture of Asia and Oceania. Water is continuously led into the top terrace of the series, and each has a drain and sump to regulate water level. Fish may be grown in the deeps of such terraces.

## Garden Terraces

### Hand-Made Terraces

Hand-made slope terraces need to be narrower (3.5-6.5m, or 12-15 feet) than machine-made systems. Garden terraces on very steep humid slopes must be kept narrow and in sets of 6-8 downslope, otherwise instability may result. Borders should be kept vegetated with trees.

### Construction Specifications

- Terrace walls typically at 1:2.5 ratio (height to base width)
- Pits dug at intervals to absorb rain
- Paths slope alternately to spill excess runoff
- Paths should be mulched
- Twice annually, path mulch is shifted to terrace beds
- Successfully used on very steep slopes (30-35 degrees or more)

### Specific Growing Situations on Terraced Lands

- Banks and bunds: the rim of the terraces and stepped bunds made for tree crop
- Slope faces and walls
- On trellis out from bunds
- In and around ditches and drains
- On steep (unterraced) slopes
- The flat area of the terrace itself

## Machine-Made Terraces

Blade machines (bulldozers, graders) are the machines for levelling and benching or terracing. They should be able to lift (and drop), angle, and tilt. Even a small tractor (17-25 hp), patiently worked, can make very large dams and terraces at less fuel cost (but greater time cost) than a bullddozer.

Large machines can now be laser-guided to accurately level and grade fields at a pre-set slope. However, land forming is mainly restricted to large flattish irrigation areas or civil works, and is normally contracted out to specialists with large machines.

## Terrace Stability

### Mulch Tree Coverage

Essential for terrace stability: about one-half to one-third of the total terraced area should be devoted to mulch tree crop providing fodder for livestock or direct leaf and branch mulch to terraces. Ideally:
- The upper one-third of hills and very steep slopes (30 degrees or greater)
- Terrace side-borders
- Outer faces and crowns of bunds (walls)

All should be planted to productive and mulch-producing tree and ground crop. This adds to terrace stability and provides local manurial-mulch crop for terrace cultures. Included in such mulches are the crop wastes of the preceding crop.

### Terraces Can Be Flooded

The terraces can be flooded seasonally for irrigated crops, providing additional water storage and nutrient distribution capacity.

## Check Dams and Gully Control

Terrace-building combined with check dams built of stone-filled wire mesh gabions in stream beds effectively check and stabilize soil erosion. Both help to absorb runoff water. At Assomada (Cape Verde) in the Sahel:
- Some 2000 hectares have been reclaimed in two target valleys
- Terrace-building and gabion check dams have stabilized erosion
- Silt brought down by erosion has built terraces 6-8m deep and about 200m wide across eroded valleys
- Copious spillways let floods flow over
- A change from sugar cane cropping to tree and vegetable culture also assists soil conservation

### Weir Construction

A series of weirs 0.5m high traps sediment for tree establishment. Weirs can be made from:
- Well-braced wire-mesh fences
- Low stone bunds across gully floors
- Stone-filled wire baskets (gabions) placed to spread floodwater flow

Weirs must always extend up the gully sides, well above any flood level. Stone weirs are more easily maintained and restored than wire fences, and more permanent. All weirs need Vetiver grass or similar tough plant barrier to hold erosion.

## Slope Management Priorities

Almost every slope benefits from earth-shaping for soil conservation. The sequence of slope management is:
1. [[permaculture-designers-manual-site-development-sequence]] and planning
2. Contour mapping and layout
3. Windbreak establishment as priority, preceding planting
4. Essential earthworks (terraces, swales, diversion drains)
5. Mulch tree planting on terrace borders and steep slopes
6. Terrace cropping with appropriate species
7. Continuous assessment, feedback, and innovative trials

## Fodder Production on Terraces

Fodder species for terrace systems include:
- [[tagasaste]]
- Banna grass
- Comfrey
- Leucaena
- Crop wastes and straw

## Avalanche and Snow Management

Terraces play a role in snow country management. Snow slides at velocities of 4-5 m/sec can be arrested by terraces. Cross-slope terraces, walls, and barriers have been used in alpine areas, though snow barriers on plateaus above the slope can more effectively hold snow and lessen the load.
