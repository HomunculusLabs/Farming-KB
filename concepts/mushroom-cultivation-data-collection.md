Spawn media formulation, including grain type, water volume, and any additives such as gypsum. The exact recipe matters because small variations in moisture content dramatically affect results.

Shaking schedule — for grain spawn, the timing of shaking or mixing interventions affects colonization speed and uniformity.

Observations — any anomalies in growth rate, color, smell, or morphology should be noted. These become invaluable for future troubleshooting.

Temperature tracking should be recorded on a chart showing both the set point and actual air temperature over time. Temperature excursions during incubation are one of the most common causes of poor spawn performance.

## Substrate Preparation Records

For each batch of substrate, document the substrate formula by weight and volume of each ingredient. Record moisture content if measured. Note the [[mushroom-substrate-comparison]] details if applicable.

Record the inoculation date, the date colonization was complete, and any observations about the colonization pattern — whether it was uniform, patchy, fast, or slow compared to previous batches.

## Yield Analysis

Yield data should be analyzed in terms of biological efficiency: the weight of fresh mushrooms harvested divided by the dry weight of the substrate, expressed as a percentage. This standardized metric allows comparison across different batches, substrates, and growing methods regardless of scale.

Record yield per flush, total yield, and the distribution of yield across flushes. Some strains front-load production in the first two flushes while others produce more evenly across four or more flushes. This pattern affects harvest scheduling and economic returns.
