# Cultivar-Specific Root Microbial Communities

## Overview

Cultivar-specificity in root microbial communities refers to the phenomenon
where genetically distinct plant varieties (cultivars) of the same species
select and maintain significantly different bacterial assemblages within
their root tissues, even when grown in identical soil conditions. Winston et
al. (2014) demonstrated this effect in Cannabis across five cultivars,
providing one of the clearest examples of host genotype-mediated
microbiome selection.

## Distinguishing Community Composition vs. Structure

A critical distinction in understanding cultivar-specificity is between
community **composition** (which taxa are present or absent) and community
**structure** (the relative abundances of taxa). In the Cannabis microbiome
study:

- **Strain had zero significant effect on OTU presence/absence** (unweighted
  g-test with FDR correction): 0 significant OTUs
- **Strain had 71 significant effects on OTU abundances** (weighted ANOVA
  with FDR correction)

This means that different Cannabis cultivars do not harbor unique bacterial
species in their roots. Instead, they modulate the relative proportions of
the same soil-derived bacterial pool. The same bacteria are available to all
cultivars, but each cultivar creates a different internal root environment
that favors different population sizes for different taxa.

## Evidence Across Two Experiments

### Experiment 1: Three Cultivars, One Soil

Three cultivars (Burmese, Bookoo Kush, Sour Diesel) were grown in
minimally variable soil in Vista, California. Key findings:

- Division of all communities by strain was not significant for weighted
  (R² = 0.11, p = 0.25) or unweighted (R² = 0.11, p = 0.15) analyses
  across all sample types
- However, **[[cannabis-rhizosphere-endorhiza-communities]] divided by strain were highly
  significant** for both weighted (R² = 0.59, p = 0.004) and unweighted
  (R² = 0.39, p = 0.003) analyses
- This pattern supports the [[two-tier-selection-model-plant-microbiome]]:
  cultivar effects are only detectable in the root interior, not in bulk
  soil or rhizosphere

### Experiment 2: Two Cultivars, Two Soils

White Widow and Maui Wowie were grown in two distinct soil types (Mo-Bio and
Orange County soils) with significant edaphic variation. Key findings:

- **Soil type** was the dominant factor for unweighted analysis (R² = 0.32,
  p = 0.001), while **strain dominated weighted analysis** (R² = 0.27,
  p = 0.001) for endorhiza communities
- This demonstrates that when edaphic variation is substantial, it
  determines community composition (who is present), while cultivar
  determines [[edaphic-determinants-cannabis-microbiome-community-structure]] (how abundant each member is)

## Strain-Specific OTU Signatures

Significant OTU abundance differences between Cannabis strains were
composed primarily of Proteobacteria:

- **Pseudomonadales** — diverse metabolic capabilities, many known plant
  growth-promoting species
- **Burkholderiales** — includes both plant-beneficial and pathogenic taxa
- **Sphingomonadales** — associated with biodegradation and plant
  protection
- **Rhizobiales** — nitrogen-fixing and plant-associated bacteria

Beyond Proteobacteria, Bacteroidetes orders were also responsible for
strain-specific differences:

- **Sphingobacteriales** — involved in polysaccharide degradation
- **Flavobacteriales** — associated with [[nutrient-cycling-in-soil]] rhizosphere
  environments
