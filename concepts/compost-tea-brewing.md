# Compost Tea Brewing Guide

Aerated compost tea (ACT) is a liquid extract of finished compost, brewed with oxygen to multiply beneficial microorganisms. Applied as a soil drench or foliar spray, ACT introduces diverse bacteria, fungi, protozoa, and nematodes to the soil and plant surfaces, boosting nutrient cycling and disease suppression. (see [[compost-tea-guide]]).

## ACT vs. Non-Aerated Compost Tea

| Feature | Aerated (ACT) | Non-Aerated (Steep) |
|---------|---------------|---------------------|
| Oxygen | Continuous aeration | No aeration |
| Microbe diversity | High (aerobic organisms) | Lower, may go anaerobic |
| Brewing time | 24-48 hours | 1-14 days |
| Purpose | Soil inoculation, foliar protection | Quick nutrient extraction |
| Risk | Low if brewed correctly | Higher (pathogens if anaerobic) |
| Equipment needed | Air pump, airstone | Just a bucket |

This guide focuses on aerated compost tea (ACT). (see [[compost-tea-recipe-guide]]).

## 5-Gallon ACT Recipe

### Equipment
- 5-gallon bucket (food-grade, clean)
- Aquarium air pump (minimum output: 0.5 L/min)
- Airstone or air diffuser (coarse bubble)
- Tubing (food-grade, non-toxic)
- Cheesecloth or 400-micron mesh bag (for compost)
- Dechlorinated water (see below)

### Ingredients
- 4-5 gallons dechlorinated water (room temperature, 65-75°F)
- 1-2 cups high-quality, finished compost (vermicompost preferred)
- 1 tablespoon unsulfured blackstrap molasses (microbe food)
- Optional: 1/4 tsp kelp meal or 1 tsp fish hydrolysate (additional nutrients)

### Step-by-Step Process

1. **Dechlorinate water**: Fill bucket 24 hours before brewing and let sit uncovered, or use a chlorine filter. Chlorine kills the microbes you are trying to grow. Chloramine (used by some municipalities) requires ascorbic acid (1 tsp per gallon) or a Campden tablet.

2. **Set up aeration**: Place airstone in bucket, run tubing to pump. Turn on pump and verify vigorous bubbling. Aerate for 15-20 minutes before adding compost to saturate water with oxygen.

3. **Fill compost bag**: Place 1-2 cups compost in mesh bag. Do NOT pack tightly — microbes need surface area exposed. Tie bag securely.

4. **Submerge compost**: Lower bag into bucket. Do not let it sit on the bottom where aeration is weakest; suspend it mid-water column.

5. **Add molasses**: Stir in 1 tbsp unsulfured blackstrap molasses. Sulfured molasses contains sulfur dioxide, which is antimicrobial and defeats the purpose. Optional additions: kelp meal, fish hydrolysate, humic acid.

6. **Brew**: Run pump continuously for 24-36 hours.
   - Ideal water temperature: 65-75°F.
   - If room is cool, wrap bucket in a towel or blanket for insulation.
   - You should see a frothy foam on top — this indicates microbial activity.

7. **Harvest**: Remove compost bag (return contents to compost pile or garden). Use tea immediately — within 4 hours of stopping aeration.

### Brewing Time Guidelines

| Duration | Result |
|----------|--------|
| 12-18 hours | Mostly bacteria, some protozoa |
| 24-36 hours | Peak bacterial and protozoan diversity |
| 36-48 hours | Protozoa and fungal hyphae increasing |
| Beyond 48 hours | DO NOT USE — oxygen depleted, anaerobic, potential pathogens |

If tea smells bad (sewer, sulfur, vomit), it has gone anaerobic — discard on non-edible plants or compost pile. Good tea smells earthy, sweet, or like fresh forest floor.

## Application Rates

### Soil Drench
- Dilute 1:1 with water (optional but stretches volume).
- Apply 1-2 cups per plant at the base.
- For beds: 2-5 gallons per 100 sq ft.
- Water soil lightly before and after application to help microbes colonize.
- Apply every 2-4 weeks during the growing season.

### Foliar Spray
- Dilute 1:5 to 1:10 (tea to water) to avoid clogging sprayer.
- Spray in early morning or late evening — UV light kills microbes.
- Coat both tops and undersides of leaves.
- Do not spray in full sun or above 85°F.
- Apply every 2-3 weeks for disease prevention.
- Good for: powdery mildew suppression, botrytis prevention, general plant health.

## Compost Quality Matters

The quality of your starting compost directly determines tea quality:
- **Vermicompost** (worm castings) — highest microbe diversity, gold standard.
- **Thermophilic compost** — good if fully finished, earthy-smelling, dark brown.
- **Bad compost** — smells foul, still hot, recognizable feedstock pieces. Do NOT use.
- **Mushroom compost** — can work but may be sterilized; test results vary.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Cause | Solution |
|---------|-------|----------|
| Foul smell | Anaerobic conditions | Ensure pump is strong enough; reduce brew time |
| No foam | Inactive compost or cold water | Use fresh vermicompost; warm water to 65°F |
| Clogged sprayer | Compost particles in tea | Use finer mesh bag (400 micron); strain before spraying |
| Brown slime on surface | Anaerobic bacteria | Discard; improve aeration next batch |
| Tea has no effect | Poor compost or chlorinated water | Source quality compost; properly dechlorinate |

## Safety and Hygiene

- Clean all equipment between batches with hot water and vinegar (no soap residue).
- Never brew in direct sunlight — UV kills microbes and heats water too high.
- Label brew date on bucket.
- Use food-grade buckets — avoid buckets that held chemicals, paint, or drywall compound.
