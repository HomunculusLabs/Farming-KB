# Permaculture Difficult Sites Plant Guide

Not all land is equally suited to cultivation. Permaculture design matches species to site conditions, including saline soils, alkaline soils, coastal exposure, and waterlogged areas. The [[permaculture-useful-plants-reference]] provides extensive species lists for challenging conditions, turning marginal land into productive systems.

## Saline and Salt-Tolerant Species

### Salt-Tolerant Fodder Species
- **Acacia ampliceps** (salt wattle) -- tolerates highly saline and waterlogged conditions; good firewood
- **Acacia maconochieana** -- highly tolerant of salt and waterlogging; high palatability to cattle (41% digestibility, 12% protein)
- **Acacia cyclops** (coastal wattle) -- tolerates saline and waterlogged soils; withstands wind and salt spray
- **Casuarina obesa** (swamp sheoak) -- occupies low-lying swampy flats above high-tide limits
- **Casuarina equisetifolia** (horsetail tree) -- salt-tolerant, absorbs heavy winds; wood density 1000 kg/cu m
- **Casuarina stricta** -- tolerates frost, dry ridges, poor coastal sands, salty soils, wind, and salt spray
- **Tagasaste** -- tolerates a wide range of soils; 50,000 ha planted on mostly marginal farmland in WA by 1996

### Salt-Tolerant Trees
- **Acacia ligulata** -- moderately salt-tolerant, occurs on edges of saline depressions
- **Acacia cuspidifolia** -- tolerates saline, alkaline, and stony compacted soils
- **Populus euphratica** (Euphrates poplar) -- occurs naturally in soils with salinity up to 3000 ppm
- **Populus alba** (white poplar) -- tolerant of soil salinity and salt spray
- **Carob** -- some degree of salt tolerance observed along waterways and low-lying areas
- **Taro** -- some cultivars highly salt tolerant

### Plants Surviving Right to the Ocean
Species that tolerate direct salt spray and coastal exposure:
- Casuarina equisetifolia
- Casuarina stricta
- Acacia cyclops
- Populus alba
- Irish strawberry tree (Arbutus unedo) -- moderately salt-spray tolerant

## Alkaline Soil Species

Many plants that struggle in acid soils thrive in alkaline conditions:
- **Acacia bivenosa** -- neutral to alkaline, less fertile soils
- **Acacia harpophylia** (brigalow) -- tolerant of heavy clay, often highly alkaline or saline
- **Casuarina cristata** -- grows on sandy soils over limestone or calcrete
- **Honey locust** (Gleditsia triacanthos) -- tolerant of both alkaline and acid soils
- **Fig** (Ficus carica) -- periodic lime applications beneficial; dislikes alkaline or salty soils (note: thrives with lime but not in strongly alkaline soil)
- **Oaks**: Q. ilex grows on limestone subsoils; Q. cerris excellent in chalk soils

## Waterlogged and Flood-Tolerant Species

- **Alder** (Alnus glutinosa) -- flourishes in areas frequently inundated by salt or brackish water; wood hardens in water
- **Willows** (Salix spp) -- tolerate prolonged flooding; roots above winter groundwater table; used for gully and slump erosion
- **Poplars** (Populus spp) -- some species tolerate prolonged flooding; P. deltoides is a pioneer on flooded soils
- **Acacia maconochieana** -- highly tolerant of waterlogging
- **Elephant grass** -- does not like waterlogged conditions (notable exception among tropical grasses)
- **Chinese tallow tree** -- tolerates poor drainage; survived more than 2 years in areas flooded by dam construction

## Drought-Tolerant Species

- **Carob** -- minimum rainfall 250 mm; taproots traced to 20 m
- **Acacia aneura** (mulga) -- survives 200-500 mm annual rainfall
- **Casuarina obesa** -- drought tolerant to 200 mm
- **Casuarina stricta** -- rainfall as low as 250 mm
- **Honey locust** -- deep root system withstands drought
- **Moringa** -- drought tolerant (frost tender)
- **Pampas grass** -- drought reserve
- **Chinese tallow tree** -- drought tolerant; brief lows to -10C
- **Macadamia** -- fairly drought tolerant; produces better with irrigation

## Fire Retardant Species

Species with low fire potential for integration into fire-safe landscape design:
- **Oaks** (Quercus spp) -- generally low combustibility; incorporate as fire barriers
- **Acacia glandulicarpa** -- fire retardant properties
- **Acacia cyclops** -- fire retardant properties
- **Acacia iteaphylla** -- fire retardant properties
- **Figs** (Ficus spp) -- generally good fire retardant trees
- **Carob** -- reported not to burn easily; generally recovers when scorched
- **Box elder maple** (Acer negundo) -- fire retardant properties
- **Leucaena** -- stands 10m+ wide suppress fire spread; plants soon recover and regenerate

## Design Principles

- Match species to existing site conditions rather than trying to change conditions to suit species
- Use [[nitrogen-fixing-trees-and-shrubs]] to rehabilitate degraded land before planting longer-term crops
- Buffer between demanding crops and difficult conditions using tolerant species
- Use salt-tolerant trees around salt scalds with trials on soil mounds within scalds
- Plant fire retardant species as strategic barriers in fire-prone landscapes
- See [[drought-tolerant-planting-design]] for additional drought strategy and [[permaculture-plants-ground-covers-and-accumulators]] for soil stabilization on difficult sites
