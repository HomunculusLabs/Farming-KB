# PF Substrate

**Source:** PF TEK - [[pf-tek-psilocybe-fanaticus]]
**Category:** Mycology
**Tags:** mushroom-cultivation, substrate, vermiculite, brown-rice, pf-tek

## Overview

The PF substrate is the foundational growing medium developed by Psilocybe Fanaticus for cultivating *[[brassinosteroid-growth-promotion-psilocybe-cubensis]]* mushrooms. It consists of a simple three-ingredient mixture of brown rice powder, horticultural vermiculite, and water, loaded into half-pint canning jars and steam sterilized. The substrate's innovation lies in using vermiculite as a structural base rather than growing solely on grain, which provides superior aeration and mimics the airy substrate conditions found in nature.

## Composition

### Standard Formula (per half-pint jar)
- **1/8 cup** brown rice powder
- **1/2 to 2/3 cup** horticultural vermiculite (medium grade)
- **40-45 cc** (milliliters) water — slightly less than 1/4 cup

### Maximum Fruiting Formula (per half-pint jar)
- **1/4 cup** brown rice powder
- **1/2 cup** vermiculite
- **60 cc** water

The maximum fruiting formula increases the nutrient content but requires careful [[mushroom-casing-moisture-management]]. If using the finer type of vermiculite with this formula, the jar lid should be kept loose during incubation to prevent excess moisture accumulation.

## The Role of Vermiculite

Vermiculite is the "magic ingredient" of the PF TEK. When mycelium is cultured in grain alone, it forms a dense mass with little air space. But when grown with vermiculite, the mycelial threads stretch across the porous spaces between vermiculite particles. This creates a loose, well-aerated substrate that:

- Allows mycelium to colonize rapidly throughout the entire volume
- Provides excellent gas exchange during colonization
- Holds moisture while maintaining structure
- Enables easy removal of the colonized cake from the jar

### Vermiculite Grades

Not all vermiculite is the same. The coarseness varies considerably between brands:

- **Fine vermiculite:** Particles average ~1 mm across. Holds more water. Recommended.
- **Coarse vermiculite:** Particles average 4-5 mm across, up to 8 mm. Holds less water.

The coarser type will hold less water than the finer type, which alters water-holding capacity. If a formulation results in a really wet or sloppy substrate, reduce water. Keep notes on formulas for replicating the substrate that fruits best.

## Brown Rice Powder

Freshly ground brown rice powder is recommended over prepackaged types. To make it at home, place regular brown rice in a small canister-type coffee bean grinder and grind to a fine powder. The freshness can make a significant difference in colonization speed and vigor.

## Dry Vermiculite Contaminant Barrier

A key innovation of the PF substrate is the dry vermiculite layer placed on top of the wet substrate inside the jar:

1. After filling the jar loosely with wet substrate (leaving 1/2 to 3/4 inch at the top), wipe the inside of the jar down to the substrate level
2. Fill the remaining top space with **plain dry vermiculite** and level it off
3. This upper layer protects the wet substrate from airborne contaminants
4. It also absorbs and regulates moisture transpiration and condensation within the jar

This dry vermiculite barrier is a Psilocybe Fanaticus original discovery. It acts as a passive filter that allows gas exchange while preventing contaminant spores from reaching the nutritious wet substrate below.

## Jar Preparation

### Suitable Containers
Jars must be **1/2 pint capacity** (8 ounces / 250 ml) with **tapered sides and no shoulders**, otherwise the fungus cakes won't easily come out. Appropriate jars include:

- KERR wide mouth half-pint canning jar
- BALL regular mouth half-pint canning jar
- BALL half-pint jelly jar
- 1/2 pint (250 ml) drinking glasses with tapered sides

Note: Even though regular mouth BALL and KERR half-pints look similar, the KERR is not tapered. Jars somewhat larger than 1/2 pint are unreliable for the PF TEK and fail easily.

### Preparation Steps

1. **Mix:** Place 1/2 cup vermiculite in a mixing bowl. Place brown rice powder on top. Slowly add water directly onto the brown rice powder, wetting it first. Thoroughly mix. The mixture should feel damp and cohesive.
2. **Load:** Fill the jar very loosely. Leave 1/2 to 3/4 inch space at top. Level the substrate.
3. **Barrier:** Wipe jar insides down to substrate. Fill top with plain dry vermiculite, level off.
4. **Lid:** Place lid with rubberized edge up. Punch 4 holes inside the periphery of the rubber sealing edge with a sharpened 3-penny nail. Cover holes with professional-grade masking tape.
5. **Sterilize:** Steam at lowest possible boil for 1 hour in a tightly covered pot with a vegetable steamer insert to keep jars off the pot bottom.

## Moisture Balance

Getting the water content right is critical. Signs of incorrect moisture:

- **Too wet:** Substrate is sloppy; water collects at jar bottom (especially with tight lid)
- **Too dry:** Spores germinate but mycelium growth halts at a certain point; substrate appears dry

The balance depends on the water needs of the mycelium, jar size, available air space, and vermiculite type. Only through direct experimentation can the right balance be found. Take notes and go with what fruits the best.

## Measurement Accuracy

If measuring cup specs aren't true, the formulas will be off, potentially causing failure or diminished growth. Verify: 1 cup = 237 ml = 1/2 pint = 8 liquid ounces. There are 2 cups in a pint, 2 pints in a quart, and 4 cups in a quart.

## Why It Works: Copying Nature

The PF TEK substrate copies nature in a fundamental way. Instead of the usual cloning of mushroom tissue and [[falconer-growing-mushrooms-in-sheds]] from that cloned culture, a mass spore inoculation is employed directly to the [[pf-tek-maximum-fruiting-substrate-formula]]. This means the complete genotype remains intact through every generation.

Senescence (mutating and ceased fruiting) is no longer a problem with this approach. The spores ensure a never-ending succession of fungus, with all the power of the spores' reproductive ability intact. This is in contrast to tissue cloning methods, where successive generations of cloned mycelium gradually lose vigor and fruiting capacity.

The vermiculite-based substrate also mimics the natural growing conditions of *Psilocybe cubensis*, which in the wild colonizes nutrient-poor, well-aerated substrates like cow manure and grassy soils. The substrate provides just enough nutrition for the mycelium to fully colonize, then triggers fruiting — a pattern that mirrors natural fungal life cycles.

## Common Problems and Solutions

### Substrate Too Wet
- Reduce water in the formula by 5-10 cc
- Use coarser vermiculite if available
- Keep jar lid loose during incubation to allow evaporation
- Ensure the pot lid is tight during steaming to prevent water from boiling into jars

### Substrate Too Dry
- Increase water content in the formula
- Ensure steaming time is not too long (dries substrate)
- Use finer vermiculite which holds more water
- Keep jar lid tight during incubation to preserve moisture

### Slow Colonization
- Check that the brown rice powder is fresh (freshly ground is best)
- Verify incubation temperature is around 70°F (don't overheat)
- Ensure the substrate was mixed loosely, not packed down
- Consider using the maximum fruiting formula for higher nutrient content

### Substrate Shrinks or Pulls Away from Jar
- This is normal to some degree during colonization
- Excessive shrinkage indicates the substrate was too wet initially
- Mycelium consumes substrate and the vermiculite structure settles

## Relationship to Other Substrate Types

The PF substrate differs significantly from grain-based substrates used in more advanced cultivation:

| Property | PF Substrate | Whole Grain (Rye/WBS) |
|---|---|---|
| Base | Vermiculite + BRF | Whole grains |
| Aeration | Excellent (porous) | Moderate (grain gaps) |
| Sterilization | Steam (1 hour) | [[pressure-cooker-sterilisation-fungal-substrate]] required |
| Contamination resistance | High (dry barrier) | Lower (no barrier) |
| Shaking | Not possible | Yes (speeds colonization) |
| Yield per jar | Lower | Higher |

The PF substrate's main advantages are its simplicity, low equipment requirements (no pressure cooker needed), and high contamination resistance. Its main disadvantage is lower yield compared to grain-based methods, and the inability to shake jars to speed colonization.
