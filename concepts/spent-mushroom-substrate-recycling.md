## Nutritional Composition

SMS from oyster mushroom cultivation is rich in primary plant nutrients. Analysis of *Pleurotus* compost shows:

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Nitrogen (N)** | 1.70% |
| **Phosphorus (P2O5)** | 0.61% |
| **Potassium (K2O)** | 1.13% |

These values are notably **higher than human manure** (N 0.30%, P2O5 0.16%, K2O 0.30%), **pig manure** (N 0.60%, P2O5 0.60%, K2O 0.50%), and **cow manure** (N 0.59%, P2O5 0.28%, K2O 0.14%).

## Recycling Applications

### 1. Soil Amendment and Fertilizer

Spent substrate from sawdust, cotton-waste, or straw cultivation can be used as an organic fertilizer or soil conditioner. In practice, Thai growers age their spent substrate **outdoors for over one year** before reuse to allow complete decomposition and pathogen die-off. Aged SMS improves soil structure, water retention, and nutrient availability.

### 2. Animal Feed

Mushroom mycelia are rich in protein, containing **up to 38% protein** by weight. The spent substrate, now partially digested by fungal enzymes, has increased crude protein content compared to the raw starting material. Studies show:

- **4 kg of vegetable or fungal protein** produces approximately **1 kg of pig meat**
- For cattle farming, the conversion ratio is approximately **7:1**
- Poultry, buffalo, and cattle feeding trials have demonstrated the viability of SMS as a feed supplement

### 3. Bioremediation

SMS has shown remarkable capacity for environmental cleanup:

- **Pentachlorophenol removal** — spent oyster mushroom substrate performs better than many living mushroom mycelia in removing this biocide from contaminated environments
- **Creosote-contaminated soil** — SMS from *Pleurotus ostreatus* cultivation is effective for bioremediation of creosote-contaminated soils
- **Olive oil mill wastewater** — the ligninolytic fungus *Pleurotus ostreatus* reduces phenol content and toxicity in olive oil mill waste waters using spent substrate

### 4. Vermiculture

SMS serves as an excellent food base for earthworms. Worms convert the spent substrate into nutrient-rich humus that can be reapplied to agricultural soils. This creates a closed-loop system where nothing is wasted.

## ZERI Project Case Studies

The Zero Emissions Research and Initiatives (ZERI) Foundation has pioneered integrated biosystems using SMS:

### Colombia: Coffee Waste Integration

Coffee farm organic wastes contain biochemicals that normally prevent their reuse as cattle feed. However, **mushroom enzymes neutralize these biochemicals** during cultivation. After harvesting mushrooms from the coffee substrate:

- The spent substrate becomes an excellent additive to cattle and pig feed
- 100 pigs produce enough manure daily to generate calorific energy equivalent to **10 liters of petroleum** (via biogas digester)
- Biogas energy is used to pasteurize/sterilize new substrate, creating a self-sustaining energy loop

### Africa: Water Hyacinth Conversion

The invasive water hyacinth (*Eichhornia crassipes*) chokes waterways across southern and East Africa. ZERI demonstrated that dried water hyacinth is an excellent mushroom substrate:

- **1 ton of dried water hyacinth** generated **1.1 tons of mushrooms** — a biological efficiency exceeding 100%
- After mushroom harvesting, the spent substrate becomes rich protein feed for cattle
- Residual substrate is used for earthworm farming, producing humus for soil recovery
- Earthworms themselves serve as chicken feed
