# The Willow Basket Hotte: Manure Transport System

## Narrow Paths and Human-Scale Logistics

The [[falconer-horse-manure-varieties]] (for
hotbed heat generation) or [[coleman-french-maraicher-horse-manure-hotbed-system]] from the city stables of Paris was brought to the
gardens daily by the wagons that had delivered vegetable crates to
the markets that morning. On the return trip, the empty wagons
stopped at stables to collect fresh manure, creating a remarkably
efficient recycling loop.

The manure was delivered to the garden entrance, where it was
transferred into hottes for distribution throughout the beds. This
human-powered last-mile delivery was necessary because the narrow
paths precluded the use of carts or wheelbarrows within the garden.

### Two Types of Manure, Two Purposes

The system distinguished between two forms of horse manure:

1. **Fresh manure**: Packed into hotbeds for heat generation. The
   microbial activity of decomposing fresh manure produced
   temperatures sufficient to protect cold-sensitive crops through
   winter.

2. **Composted manure**: Spent hotbed material that had finished
   decomposing, now a crumbly, dark compost. This was also
   carried in hottes and spread as a soil amendment on the growing
   beds.

Both materials required the same delivery infrastructure, making
the hotte the central tool of the [[coleman-british-adoption-of-french-intensive-gardening]] tradition: the substitution of skilled human labor
