# Holmgren Appropriate Non-Renewable Resources

Updated: 2026-04-19

David Holmgren addresses the appropriate use of non-renewable resources within permaculture design in *Permaculture: Principles and Pathways Beyond Sustainability* (2002), arguing that non-renewables can play a constructive role if used strategically to build long-term renewable capacity.

## The Transition Argument

Non-renewable resources — fossil fuels, minerals, and metal ores — represent a one-time inheritance from geological processes that took millions of years. In the [[holmgren-tripartite-altruism]] framework, the wise use of these resources during the transition from growth to descent is a critical design question.

The transition to declining energy availability provides a unique strategic opportunity to make the best use of existing wealth and non-renewable resources to rebuild natural and human capital. This perspective connects to [[consumer-versus-conserver-society]] and the argument that we should shift from a consumer to a conserver society during the energy-wealth peak.

## Use Non-Renewables to Build Renewables

The core principle is using non-renewable resources to establish systems that will continue yielding after the non-renewables are exhausted. Examples include:

- Using fossil fuel energy to plant long-lived perennial food forests

- Using industrial metals to install water harvesting infrastructure

- Using manufactured tools to establish soil-building systems

- Using fossil fuel knowledge to develop seed-saving and breeding programs

## The Establishment Principle

In general, the best use for non-renewable resources and technology should be to establish a system, rather than to maintain or harvest it, even if the "establishment" process is a gradual one that takes place as a transition over a lifetime (or even generations). For example, a passive solar house uses the high-embodied energy in glass to capture solar energy. If it does this while providing the other functions of glass in buildings, such as natural lighting and views, this is good use of embodied non-renewable energy.

## Earthworks as Strategic Investment

Bulldozers and other large earth-moving equipment are perhaps the most dramatic example of appropriate use of fossil fuels and technology promoted in permaculture. They are used to make well-designed systems to harvest, store and distribute water, as well as creating vehicle access and house sites that increase the productivity of urban and rural landscapes. These earthworks are capable of being maintained indefinitely, by human labour if necessary.

This connects to [[permaculture-earthworks-guide]] and the understanding that one-time investment of fossil energy in landscape modification can yield benefits for centuries.

## Solar Cells as Transitional Technology

In the [[holmgren-solar-cell-emergy-critique]], Holmgren suggests that solar cells may be appropriate as a way to make use of already embodied fossil fuel energy in manufacturing capacity to contribute to electric power production during the transition. They are especially appropriate for small remote demand systems and sunny urban environments.

## Avoiding the Trap of Substitution

A common error is assuming that non-renewable resources can be directly substituted one for another. Holmgren emphasizes that the unique energy density and versatility of fossil fuels cannot be replicated by any combination of renewables. The goal should not be maintaining current systems with alternative inputs but redesigning systems to need less energy overall.

## Mineral Fertilizers as Transition

Rock minerals and mineral fertilizers can be understood as non-renewable inputs used to rebuild biological soil capital. Once the soil ecosystem is functioning effectively, biological mineral cycling through deep-rooted plants and mycorrhizal associations can maintain fertility with much lower external inputs. This connects to [[holmgren-mineral-fertilizers-soil-building]].

## The Y2K Warning

The vulnerability of modern systems that depend entirely on continuous non-renewable energy flows was highlighted by the Y2K computer bug concern. Systems with no stored reserves are extremely fragile. Building redundancy and storage capacity using non-renewable resources now provides insurance against future disruptions.

## Honest Assessment of Limits

Holmgren insists on honest assessment of what non-renewable resources can and cannot achieve. Claims that renewable technologies can maintain current consumption levels are as dangerous as denial of energy descent altogether. The appropriate use framework demands clear-eyed assessment of net energy yields, embodied energy costs, and long-term sustainability.

## Manufacturing Resources

We are surrounded by wastes from agriculture, forestry, and industry — organic matter, building materials, containers, and more. These manufactured resources are not necessarily renewable but can be tapped in the transition to low-energy systems, connecting to [[permaculture-principle-produce-no-waste]] and [[holmgren-produce-no-waste-detailed]].

## Idealism versus Pragmatism

In practice, it is hard and maybe unwise to completely avoid using the generous energy subsidy that comes with the power and convenience of fossil fuels for day-to-day living. Nevertheless, in making use of coal-generated electricity, petrol in the car or lucerne hay (produced with fossil fuel subsidy) for garden mulch, we should never take these underpriced resources for granted. Instead, we should design our systems as if these resources were much more highly priced.

At Melliodora, connected to mains electricity with use at less than 3 kilowatt hours a day (less than one-fifth of typical household use), the energy-demanding functions are provided with renewable energy. By buying the slightly more expensive renewable energy tariff, they contribute to stimulating development of renewable sources, but this is less important than their low consumption of electricity.
