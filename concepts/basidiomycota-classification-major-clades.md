# Basidiomycota Classification and Major Clades

The Basidiomycota is one of the two large phyla of the
Kingdom [[fungal-speciation-evolution]].

## Defining Features

All Basidiomycota share the following synapomorphies:

- **Basidium** -- the cell on which meiosis occurs and
  basidiospores are produced
- **Basidiospores** -- sexual spores formed externally on
  sterigmata projecting from the basidium
- **Dolipore septum** -- a specialized [[fungal-woronin-bodies-and-septal-pore-organization]] structure
  with a swollen rim and perforated septal cap, unique to
  the Basidiomycota
- **Clamp connections** -- hyphal branches that ensure
  proper distribution of nuclei in dikaryotic hyphae
  (present in many but not all groups)
- **Dikaryotic phase** -- an extended phase in which cells
  contain two genetically distinct nuclei

## Major Subphyla and Classes

### Ustilaginomycotina (Smuts)

The Ustilaginomycotina contains the [[rust-fungi-biodiversity-life-cycles]],
which are among the most economically important plant
pathogens. Features include:

- Up to five distinct spore stages in complex [[bloomfield-rust-fungi-life-cycles-barberry-wheat]]
- Many species are heteroecious (requiring two unrelated
  host species to complete their life cycle)
- Over 7,000 species described, predominantly on
  angiosperms
- Major pathogens include Puccinia graminis (wheat stem
  rust) and P. striiformis (wheat stripe rust)

### Agaricomycotina (Mushroom-Forming Fungi)

The Agaricomycotina is the largest subphylum and contains
the familiar mushroom-forming fungi, bracket fungi, puffballs,
and their allies. It is subdivided into three classes:

1. **Agaricomycetes** -- the largest class, containing the
   typical gilled mushrooms, boletes, polypores, and
   corals; most produce macroscopic fruiting bodies
2. **Dacrymycetes** -- "jelly fungi" with forked basidia
3. **Tremellomycetes** -- jelly fungi and yeasts,
   including some human pathogens

## Hymenomycetes

The Hymenomycetes is a traditional grouping (now largely
superseded by molecular classifications) that included
basidiomycetes with exposed hymenophores -- the spore-
producing surface. This group encompassed most of the
mushroom-forming fungi and was divided into several major
categories:

### Agaricales (Gilled Mushrooms)

The Agaricales is the best-known order of fungi, containing
over 10,000 described species. It includes:

- **Cortinariaceae** -- Cortinarius, the largest genus of
  agarics with over 2,000 species, many forming
  [[ectomycorrhizal-fungi]] tree associations
  associations
- **Boletaceae** -- boletes with poroid hymenophores
  (Boletus, Suillus, Leccinum)
- **Amanitaceae** -- Amanita, including both deadly
  poisonous and edible species
- **Tricholomataceae** -- a large and diverse family
  including Tricholoma, Laccaria, and Armillaria

### Polyporales (Bracket Fungi and Poroids)

The Polyporales contains the bracket fungi and their allies,
many of which are wood decomposers. Important genera include:

- Ganoderma (reishi/lingzhi)
- Trametes ([[comparison-chaga-vs-turkey-tail-medicinal-mushrooms-comprehensive]])
- Fomes and Fomitopsis (conk fungi)
- Lentinula (shiitake mushroom)

### Aphyllophorales

The traditional order Aphyllophorales encompassed diverse
non-gilled basidiomycetes including coral fungi (Clavaria,
Ramaria), tooth fungi (Hericium), and crust fungi. Molecular
phylogenetics has shown this group to be polyphyletic.

## Resupinate and Sequestrate Forms

The Basidiomycota includes fungi with reduced or modified
[[comparison-fruiting-body-vs-mycelium-extracts-medicinal-mushrooms]] morphologies:

- **Resupinate fungi** -- crust-like fruiting bodies that
  grow flat on the substrate surface
- **Secotioid fungi** -- intermediate forms between
  typical mushrooms and fully [[dighton-hypogeous-fungi-mammal-spore-dispersal]], with
  partially enclosed caps
- **Gasteroid fungi** -- puffballs, earthstars, and
  stinkhorns in which the spore-producing tissue is
  enclosed at maturity
- **Truffle-like basidiomycetes** -- hypogeous fungi
  including [[comparison-truffle-cultivation-basics-vs-mushroom-indoor-cultivation]] methods techniques (Tuber) in the Ascomycota and their
  basidiomycete analogues (Hymenogaster, Melanogaster)
