## [[swale-design]] Guidelines

For effective swale installation:

- **Always dig on contour** -- use an A-frame level or laser level to mark exact contour lines
- **Space swales according to slope** -- steeper slopes need more closely spaced swales
- **Size to rainfall** -- larger swale capacity for higher rainfall areas
- **Plant immediately** -- never leave bare swale berms; plant trees and ground covers as soon as the earthwork is done
- **Plan overflow** -- every swale needs a spillway for extreme rain events to prevent blowout
- **Start at the top** -- work from the top of the slope downward so upper swales don't overload lower ones

## Urban Applications

Swales can be scaled to any property:

- **Backyard food forests** -- A single swale across a gentle slope captures enough water to support [fruit trees
- **Rain gardens** -- miniature swales in urban yards that capture roof runoff
- **Curb cuts** -- redirecting street runoff into sidewalk planting strips via small swale-like basins
- **Contour hedgerows** -- planting dense hedges on contour with a slight swale behind them

Even on flat urban lots, the principles apply: slow, spread, and sink water rather than letting it run off. Every drop of water captured on-site is a drop that does not need to be imported.
