## Cannabis Cultivar Specificity

Cannabis cultivar specificity refers to the phenomenon whereby different Cannabis cultivars (strains) harbor distinct microbial communities in their root tissues, even when grown in identical soil conditions. This host-genotype-dependent selection of the endorhiza microbiome has been demonstrated across multiple cultivars and growing environments, providing evidence for the second tier of the two-tier selection model.

## Mechanisms of Cultivar Specificity

### Root Exudate Profiles

The primary mechanism underlying cultivar specificity is believed to be differential root exudation. Each cultivar releases a unique cocktail of organic compounds from its roots, including:

- **Sugars**: Glucose, fructose, sucrose, and other carbohydrates that serve as carbon sources for rhizosphere bacteria. The relative proportions of different sugars vary between cultivars.
- **Amino acids**: The amino acid profile of root exudates differs between cultivars and can selectively feed or inhibit different microbial groups based on their nitrogen metabolism capabilities.
- **Organic acids**: Citric acid, malic acid, oxalic acid, and other organic acids affect local pH and chelate minerals, creating microenvironments that favor certain bacteria.
- **Secondary metabolites**: Cannabis-specific compounds including terpenes and their precursors, which may have antimicrobial properties that differentially affect bacterial taxa.
- **Cannabinoids**: While primarily produced in aerial tissues, some cannabinoid-related compounds are found in roots and may influence microbial colonization patterns.

### Immune System Variation

Cultivars differ in their immune receptor repertoires and signaling pathways, which affects which bacteria are permitted to colonize internal root tissues. Plants use a two-tiered immune system:

1. **Pattern-triggered immunity (PTI)**: Activated by detection of conserved microbial molecules (MAMPs). The sensitivity and specificity of PTI receptors may vary between cultivars.
2. **Effector-triggered immunity (ETI)**: Activated when pathogen effectors are detected inside plant cells. ETI is often associated with programmed cell death at the infection site.

Differences in either PTI or ETI between cultivars can create distinct filtering effects on the endorhiza community, allowing different bacterial assemblages to establish.

### Root Architecture

Physical differences in root structure between cultivars influence colonization patterns. Factors include root branching density, cortex thickness, the presence and extent of aerenchyma (air channels), and the composition of root cell walls. Dense root networks with more lateral roots provide more colonization sites, while thicker cell walls may present greater barriers to bacterial entry.

## Limitations of Current Research

- Sample sizes in existing studies are relatively small, limiting the statistical power to detect subtle cultivar effects.
- Most studies have examined only the bacterial component of the microbiome; fungal and viral communities may show different patterns of cultivar specificity.
- The temporal stability of cultivar-specific associations across the full growing season has not been thoroughly characterized.
- Controlled experiments with grafted plants (same rootstock, different scion genotypes) would help disentangle root-specific from shoot-specific effects on the microbiome.
- The interaction between cultivar-specific microbiomes and mycorrhizal fungal partners has been largely unexplored, despite the known importance of mycorrhizae [[two-tier-selection-model]]
