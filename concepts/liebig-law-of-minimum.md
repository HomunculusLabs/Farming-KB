## Liebig's Law of Minimum

**Liebig's law of minimum** (also known as the law of the minimum) states that plant growth is limited not by total resources available, but by the single resource that is scarcest relative to the plant's needs. Formulated by German chemist Justus von Liebig in the mid-nineteenth century, the law became the theoretical foundation for chemical agriculture and the modern fertilizer industry.

### The Escalation of Inputs

Perhaps the most damaging consequence is the escalating spiral of inputs. When a farmer identifies and corrects one limiting factor, growth increases until the next limiting factor becomes apparent. This new factor must then be addressed with another input. The process never ends because the system is being managed by external intervention rather than internal self-regulation.

Fukuoka observed that this creates a dependency on chemical inputs. As the biological systems that naturally supply nutrients atrophy under chemical management, the farmer becomes increasingly reliant on external inputs. The soil, once a self-sustaining living system, becomes a lifeless medium that requires constant chemical supplementation.
