# Cannabinoid Receptors CB1 and CB2: Tissue Distribution

## Overview

Cannabinoid receptors are specialized [[plant-cell-membrane-transport-proteins]] proteins that function as the "locks"
in the endocannabinoid system's lock-and-key mechanism. When cannabinoids (the "keys") bind
to these receptors, they trigger signaling cascades that produce diverse physiological and
[[hallucinogenic-mushroom-acute-psychological-effects-and-bad-trips]]. Large numbers of cannabinoid receptors are embedded throughout the
human body, and their tissue distribution determines the [[cervantes-terpenoid-pharmacology-therapeutic-effects]] of both
endogenous and exogenous cannabinoids.

## The Lock-and-Key Model

If cannabinoid receptors function as a lock, cannabinoids are the key. Scientific knowledge
of cannabis and its most potent constituents is expanding exponentially, reflective of the
far-reaching implications for human health and medicine. The two most common types of
cannabinoid receptors are CB1 and CB2. Scientists suspect there are at least three more
endocannabinoid receptors whose locations and functions will be more fully understood after
additional research. For the time being, these other receptors are referred to as non-CB1
and non-CB2.

## CB1 Receptor Distribution

### Central Nervous System
CB1 receptors are concentrated primarily in the central nervous system (CNS), including the
brain and spinal cord. They are among the most abundant G-protein-coupled receptors in the
brain. Key CNS locations include:

- **Cerebral cortex**: Involved in higher cognitive functions, perception, and consciousness.
  CB1 activation here modulates thinking, memory, and sensory processing.

- **Basal ganglia**: Important for movement control and reward pathways. CB1 receptors here
  influence motor coordination and may explain both therapeutic and adverse motor effects
  of cannabinoids.

- **Cerebellum**: Critical for balance, coordination, and fine motor control. High CB1
  density here contributes to the motor effects of cannabis.

- **Hippocampus**: Essential for memory formation. CB1 receptors in the hippocampus mediate
  the well-documented short-term memory effects of THC.

- **Hypothalamus**: Regulates appetite, body temperature, and hormonal balance. CB1
  activation here is responsible for the appetite-stimulating (munchies) effect of cannabis.

### Peripheral Nervous System
CB1 receptors are also found in peripheral nerves, where they modulate pain signaling.
This peripheral distribution is relevant to the analgesic properties of cannabinoids and
suggests potential for [[cannabis-chronic-pain-management]] that avoids central psychoactive effects.

### Other Tissues
CB1 receptors have been identified in:
- **Reproductive organs**: Influencing fertility and hormonal regulation
- **Gastrointestinal tract**: Modulating motility and secretion
- **Liver**: Affecting lipid metabolism and glucose homeostasis
- **Adipose tissue**: Influencing fat storage and energy balance
- **Cardiovascular system**: Modulating heart rate and blood vessel function

## CB2 Receptor Distribution

### Immune System
CB2 receptors are primarily concentrated in the immune system and peripheral tissues. Key
locations include:

- **Spleen**: Major immune organ with high CB2 density, modulating immune cell activation
  and inflammatory responses.

- **Tonsils**: Lymphoid tissue involved in immune surveillance at mucosal surfaces.

- **Thymus**: Where T-cells mature; CB2 receptors may influence immune cell development.

### Immune Cells
CB2 receptors are found on the surface of various immune cell types:
- **B-cells**: Antibody-producing lymphocytes
- **T-cells**: Cell-mediated immunity regulators
- **Natural killer cells**: Innate immune surveillance
- **Macrophages**: Phagocytic immune cells
- **Monocytes**: Circulating precursors to macrophages

### Peripheral Tissues
CB2 receptors are also present in:
- **Bone**: Modulating bone formation and resorption, relevant to osteoporosis
- **Skin**: Influencing inflammation and immune responses in [[blesching-cannabis-dermatological-conditions]]
- **Gastrointestinal tract**: Affecting inflammation in conditions like Crohn's disease
