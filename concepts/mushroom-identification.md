# Mushroom Identification

Safe wild mushroom foraging requires systematic observation of multiple features. No single rule (peelability, silver spoon, color) reliably separates edible from poisonous species (Ramsbottom, 1945). The only safe method is learning to identify each species individually.

## Key Identifying Features

### Cap (Pileus)
- Shape: convex, flat, conical, funnel, umbonate
- Surface texture: smooth, scaly, viscid, dry, hairy
- Color and color changes with age or bruising
- Margin: rolled under, curved upward, striate

### Gills / Pores / Teeth
- Gill attachment: free, attached, decurrent, notched
- Gill spacing: close, distant
- Gill color: white, pink, brown, rusty; spore color develops on mature gills
- Pores (Boletaceae): tube layer on underside; pore color and bruising reaction
- Teeth (Hericium): hanging spines from underside

### Stem (Stipe)
- Solid vs hollow (cut lengthwise to check)
- Presence/absence of ring (annulus) from partial veil
- Volva (cup at base) -- critical Amanita feature
- Surface texture: smooth, scaly, fibrous

### Ring and Veil
- Partial veil: tissue connecting cap margin to stem; tears at maturity
- Universal veil: tissue enclosing entire young mushroom; leaves volva at base
- Ring can be persistent, evanescent, or double

### Spore Print Color
Essential diagnostic tool. Place cap gills-down on half white/half black paper, cover with bowl, wait 4-24 hours.
- White: Amanita, Tricholoma, Lepiota
- Pink/brown: Agaricus (edible), Entoloma (poisonous)
- Dark brown/chocolate: Psilocybe, Galerina (deadly lookalike)
- Rust/brown: Cortinarius
- Cream/yellow: Chanterelles
- Purple-brown: some Boletus

### Bruising Reaction
- Blue bruising: Psilocybe (psychoactive), some Boletus (non-toxic)
- Red/black bruising: some Boletus, some Russula
- No bruising: many species (not diagnostic alone)

### Habitat, Season, Substrate
- Growing on wood, dung, soil, leaf litter, grass
- Mycorrhizal vs saprophytic
- Season: spring morels, summer chanterelles, fall hen-of-the-woods
- Geographic range and associated tree species

## Common Edible Lookalikes of Poisonous Species

- **Amanita phalloides (Death Cap)** looks like **paddy straw mushroom** (Volvariella) and some **Agaricus** species. Key difference: Death Cap has white gills, volva at base, and spore print is white (not brown)
- **Galerina marginata** (contains amatoxins, deadly) mimics **Psilocybe** species. Both grow on wood, have brown spore prints, and similar stature. Key difference: Galerina has a persistent ring and brown rusty gills; Galerina does NOT bruise blue
- **Amanita muscaria** (Fly Agaric) -- bright red cap with white warts is distinctive, but young buttons with universal veil can resemble **puffballs** when sliced open. Always slice puffballs vertically to check for embryonic gill structure

## Major Poisonous Genera

### Amanita
- A. phalloides, A. verna, A. virosa: contain amatoxins; cause delayed onset (6-24 hr) liver/kidney failure; frequently fatal. Odourless, white spore print
- A. muscaria, A. pantherina: ibotenic acid/muscimol; nervous system effects; bright colors but not all Amanita are colorful

### Galerina
- G. marginata (Deadly Galerina): amatoxins identical to Death Cap; grows on decaying wood; brown spore print; easily confused with Psilocybe or Kuehneromyces

### Cortinarius
- Several species contain orellanine (kidney toxin); delayed onset (days to weeks)
- Rust-brown spore print; cobwebby partial veil (cortina)
- Notoriously difficult to identify; best avoided entirely by beginners

## Basic Microscopy for ID

- **Spore size and shape**: measure with micrometer slide at 400-1000x magnification
- **Cheilocystidia**: sterile cells on gill edge; shape and size are diagnostic features
- **Basidia**: spore-bearing cells; typically 4-spored (some species 2-spored)
- A compound microscope with 400x minimum is entry-level for serious identification

## When to Forage

- After rain events following dry periods (triggers fruiting)
- Late summer through fall for most temperate species
- Spring for morels (late March-May depending on region)
- Avoid areas with industrial contamination or heavy pesticide use
- Never eat an unidentified mushroom; always cross-reference multiple features
