# DMT — N,N-Dimethyltryptamine

N,N-Dimethyltryptamine (DMT) is a potent, naturally occurring tryptamine
hallucinogen found in numerous plant species across the globe and present
endogenously in mammalian brains, including humans. It is structurally
related to serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine) and psilocybin (4-
phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine), differing only by the substitution
pattern on the indole ring. DMT is remarkable for its extraordinary
pharmacokinetic profile — when smoked or injected, onset occurs within
fifteen to forty-five seconds, the peak experience lasts approximately five
minutes, and the total subjective experience resolves within twenty to thirty
minutes. Despite its brevity, the intensity of the DMT experience is widely
regarded as the most powerful of any known psychedelic compound.

## Chemistry and Structure

DMT (C₁₂H₁₆N₂) is an indolethylamine alkaloid belonging to the tryptamine
family. Its molecular structure consists of an indole ring linked to a
dimethylated ethylamine side chain. The compound is a crystalline solid at
room temperature, with a free base that vaporises at relatively low
temperatures, making it suitable for inhalation. It is soluble in organic
solvents and poorly soluble in water as a free base; the salt forms (fumarate,
hydrochloride) are water-soluble and suitable for injection.

The structural similarity of DMT to serotonin (5-HT) is central to its
pharmacological activity. Both compounds are agonists at the 5-HT₂A serotonin
receptor subtype, which is the primary receptor mediating the hallucinogenic
effects of classical psychedelics. DMT also has affinity for other serotonin
receptor subtypes, including 5-HT₁A, 5-HT₂C, and 5-HT₇, as well as sigma-1
receptors and the trace amine-associated receptor (TAAR).

## Natural Occurrence

DMT is biosynthesised by a remarkably wide range of organisms. In the plant
kingdom, it is found in *Psychotria viridis* (chacruna) and *Diplopterys
cabrerana* — the two most common DMT-containing admixture plants used in
ayahuasca preparations — as well as in *Mimosa tenuiflora* (jurema),
*Acacia* species, *[[yopo-anadenanthera-peregrina-snuff]]* (yopo), and numerous other
leguminous and rubiaceous plants. The compound is particularly abundant in
the bark, leaves, and roots of these species.

Endogenously, DMT has been detected in human blood, urine, cerebrospinal
fluid, and brain tissue. The enzyme indolethylamine N-methyltransferase
(INMT), which catalyses the methylation of tryptamine to DMT, is expressed
in the human brain, particularly in the pineal gland and cerebral cortex.
The functional significance of endogenous DMT remains debated; hypotheses
include roles as a neurotransmitter or neuromodulator, a regulator of
near-death experiences, and a contributor to dream imagery.

## Pharmacology and Pharmacokinetics

DMT is orally inactive as a hallucinogen because it is rapidly deaminated by
monoamine oxidase (MAO) enzymes in the gut and liver before reaching the
brain. This is why [[harner-amazonian-ayahuasca-preparation-methods]] preparations combine DMT-containing
plants with *Banisteriopsis caapi*, which contains [[turner-harmala-alkaloids-preparation-and-dosage]] that
act as MAO inhibitors (MAOIs), allowing the DMT to survive first-pass
metabolism and become psychoactive when consumed orally.

When vaporised and inhaled, DMT crosses the blood-brain barrier within
seconds, producing a rapid and overwhelming onset. The subjective
experience is characterised by:

- **Auditory phenomena**: a characteristic "ripping cellophane" or buzzing
  sound, often described as a tearing sensation, followed by an ascending
  tone.

- **Visual phenomena**: intense, rapidly morphing geometric patterns and
  hyperdimensional spaces described as "self-transforming machine elves"
  (McKenna's terminology) — dynamic, contorting topological modules that
  appear as autonomous entities within a continuously transforming
  background.

- **Ego dissolution and transcendence**: the experience consistently
  includes a sense of contact with an alien intelligence or a
  transdimensional realm. McKenna described arriving in "a place that defies
  description, a space that has a feeling of being underground, or somehow
  insulated and domed."

- **Translinguistic phenomena**: the experience frequently involves
  glossolalia (speaking in tongues) and the perception of a language of
  alien meaning that "cannot be Englished." McKenna described this as
  "a [[mckenna-primal-ursprache-kabbalistic-zohar-and-white-goddess-glossolalia]] that comes out of oneself," a pre-linguistic assembly
  language underlying normal speech.

## The DMT Experience and Quantum Mechanics

McKenna argued that hallucinatory states induced by tryptamines arise at the
[[mckenna-quantum-mechanical-correlates-hallucinogenesis]] level. He cited the work of John Smythies and Alexander
Shulgin demonstrating that quantum mechanical correlates exist for
hallucinogenesis: moving a single atom on the molecular ring of an inactive
compound can render it highly psychoactive. McKenna interpreted this as
"perfect proof of the dynamic linkage at the formative level between
quantum mechanically described matter and mind."

The speed of DMT's onset and offset is pharmacologically significant. The
fact that the brain can enter and exit these extraordinary states within
minutes suggests that the neural circuitry for these experiences is
intrinsic to the brain and can be rapidly modulated. McKenna described the
DMT state as "transtemporal" — "an anticipation of the future" — and
connected it to Plato's metaphor of time as "the moving image of eternity,"
suggesting the experience represents "a stepping out of the moving image
and into eternity."

## Safety and Risk Profile

DMT has a relatively favourable safety profile compared to many other
psychedelics. It is not considered physically addictive, and no deaths
attributed to DMT overdose have been documented in the clinical literature.
The LD₅₀ in rodents is extremely high relative to the psychoactive dose.
However, the intensity of the experience means that psychological risks —
including anxiety, panic, and lasting ontological shock — are significant,
particularly at high doses. McKenna noted that "a touch of terror gives the
stamp of validity to the experience because it means, 'This is real.'"

## Legal Status

DMT is classified as a Schedule I controlled substance in the United States
and is similarly restricted in most countries worldwide. Exceptions exist
for certain indigenous religious practices, most notably the União do
Vegetal (UDV) and Santo Daime churches in Brazil, which have won legal
recognition for their sacramental use of ayahuasca.
