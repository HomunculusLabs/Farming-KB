# DMT — N,N-Dimethyltryptamine

N,N-Dimethyltryptamine (DMT) is a potent, naturally occurring tryptamine
hallucinogen found in numerous plant species across the globe and present
endogenously in mammalian brains, including humans. It is structurally
related to serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine) and psilocybin (4-
phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine), differing only by the substitution
pattern on the indole ring. DMT is remarkable for its extraordinary
pharmacokinetic profile — when smoked or injected, onset occurs within
fifteen to forty-five seconds, the peak experience lasts approximately five
minutes, and the total subjective experience resolves within twenty to thirty
minutes. Despite its brevity, the intensity of the DMT experience is widely
regarded as the most powerful of any known psychedelic compound.

## Chemistry and Structure

DMT (C₁₂H₁₆N₂) is an indolethylamine alkaloid belonging to the tryptamine
family. Its molecular structure consists of an indole ring linked to a
dimethylated ethylamine side chain. The compound is a crystalline solid at
room temperature, with a free base that vaporises at relatively low
temperatures, making it suitable for inhalation. It is soluble in organic
solvents and poorly soluble in water as a free base; the salt forms (fumarate,
hydrochloride) are water-soluble and suitable for injection.

The structural similarity of DMT to serotonin (5-HT) is central to its
pharmacological activity. Both compounds are agonists at the 5-HT₂A serotonin
receptor subtype, which is the primary receptor mediating the hallucinogenic
effects of classical psychedelics. DMT also has affinity for other serotonin
receptor subtypes, including 5-HT₁A, 5-HT₂C, and 5-HT₇, as well as sigma-1
receptors and the trace amine-associated receptor (TAAR).
