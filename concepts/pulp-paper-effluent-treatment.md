# Pulp and Paper Effluent Treatment

## Overview

The pulp and paper industry is one of the primary users of wood resources globally. Chemical pulping (Kraft process) dissolves lignin from cellulose fibers using sodium hydroxide and sodium sulfide, yielding <50% pulp that requires extensive bleaching. Conventional bleaching employs chlorine species, producing chlorinated phenols, chlorinated hydrocarbons, and other toxic byproducts. The resulting effluents are highly colored, with the first alkaline extraction stage (E1) contributing ~80% of color, 30% of BOD, and 60% of COD of total mill discharge.

This topic extends [[growing-gourmet-turkey-tail-trametes-versicolor-cultivation]]*, *[[staycare-lentinula-edodes-in-bioremediation]]*, *Ceriporiopsis subvermispora*, *[[fungal-manganese-peroxidase-remediation]] (MnP)**: H₂O₂-dependent oxidation of phenolic lignin compounds; requires Mn(II) and organic acid chelators (oxalate, malonate)
- **Laccase**: Multicopper oxidase catalyzing one-electron oxidation of diphenols and aromatic amines; many white-rot and marine fungi secrete laccase responsible for effective decolorization

## Bioreactor Systems

### Batch and Continuous Bioreactors
- *T. versicolor* (pellets): 93% color removal in 48 hours (fermentor) or 38 hours (packed glass column); 92% color and 69% COD removal from E1 effluent in 7 days
- *Rhizopus oryzae*: 92–95% color removal, 50% COD removal from E1 effluent in 24 hours
- *Ceriporiopsis subvermispora*: 90% color, 45% COD removal in 48 hours

### Upflow Column Bioreactors
- *Penicillium camemberti*: 60% color and 76% AOX removal in 7–8 hours; operated 1.5 years continuously

### Immobilized Bioreactors
- **Ca-alginate T. versicolor**: 76–83% continuous decolorization; 43–59% AOX reduction
- **Polyurethane foam T. versicolor**: 69% decolorization, 73% COD removal
- **Jute-rope P. chrysosporium**: 86% color, 54% COD removal in 3 days
- **RBC with R. pusillus**: 71% color and COD removal, 55% AOX removal in 72 hours
- **RBC with C. versicolor**: 53% color, 59% COD removal
- **MyCor process**: P. chrysosporium fixed film on RBC discs, continuous operation >35 days
- **MyCOPOR process**: Polyurethane-immobilized trickling filter; 50% color/AOX removal in 3–6 hours, 80% toxicity reduction

### Combined and Sequential Systems
- **Photo-fungal treatment**: Preirradiation + *L. edodes* achieved effective decolorization in 48 hours; LiP and MnP enhanced fourfold
- **Two-step sequential**: *Paecilomyces* sp. (68% color) followed by *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* achieved 82% color removal
- **Fungal + activated sludge**: Fungal pellets (80% decolorization) followed by activated sludge for residual COD mineralization

## Mechanisms of Color Removal

Color removal involves both **adsorption** and **biodegradation**:

- *T. versicolor*: ~50% adsorption, ~30% degradation (of 80% total decolorization)
- *C. versicolor*: Adsorption and biodegradation combined
- *R. pusillus*: Primarily adsorption
- *R. oryzae*: 87% biodegradation, 13% adsorption (for *L. edodes*)

## Factors Affecting Decolorization

- **Carbon cosubstrate**: Glucose most effective; required for most white-rot fungal activities
- **Nitrogen**: Increased nitrogen inhibits lignin biodegradation; N-limiting conditions favor LiP production
- **pH**: Optimal range 4.0–5.0 for most fungi
- **Temperature**: 25–35°C optimal; thermotolerant *Rhizomucor pusillus* shows 85% higher decolorization at 55°C than 35°C
- **Inoculum dose**: Higher doses improve color removal but may cause viscosity/oxygen transfer issues
- **Effluent dilution**: Dilution improves treatment efficiency but increases volume
- **Agitation**: Aeration every 8 hours significantly improves decolorization (*Schizophyllum commune*)
