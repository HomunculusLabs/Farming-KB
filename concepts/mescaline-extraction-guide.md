# Mescaline Extraction Guide

## Overview

Mescaline (3,4,5-trimethoxyphenethylamine) is a naturally occurring phenethylamine alkaloid found in several cactus species, most notably peyote ([[lophophora-williamsii]]), San Pedro ([[trichocereus-pachanoi]]), and Peruvian Torch ([[trichocereus-peruvianus]]). It is one of the oldest known psychoactive compounds — evidence of peyote use dates back over 5,000 years.

This guide covers extraction methods for isolating mescaline from cactus material. Mescaline is a Schedule I controlled substance in the United States and many other countries. This information is provided for educational and harm-reduction purposes.

## The Chemistry

Mescaline is a phenethylamine — structurally related to amphetamine and MDMA, but with a distinct pharmacological profile. It is a serotonin 5-HT2A receptor agonist, like psilocybin and LSD, but also has significant activity at other [[serotonin-receptor-interference-psychedelic-mechanism]] subtypes.

### Chemical Properties
- Molecular formula: C11H17NO3
- Molecular weight: 211.26 g/mol
- Melting point (freebase): 35°C
- Melting point (hydrochloride): 181°C
- **Basic compound** — like DMT, mescaline's solubility depends on its salt/freebase form

### Solubility Profile
- **Mescaline freebase**: Soluble in nonpolar solvents (xylene, toluene, naphtha), insoluble in water
- **Mescaline hydrochloride (HCl)**: Soluble in water and polar solvents, insoluble in nonpolar solvents
- **Mescaline sulfate**: Water-soluble, forms crystalline precipitate from solution
- **Mescaline acetate**: Water-soluble, can be crystallized from acetone

## Cactus Sources

### Mescaline Content by Species
- **Lophophora williamsii** (peyote): 0.4–6.0% fresh weight (varies enormously)
- **Trichocereus pachanoi** (San Pedro): 0.1–2.0% dried tissue
- **Trichocereus peruvianus** (Peruvian Torch): 0.3–2.4% dried tissue
- **[[trichocereus-bridgesii]]** (Bolivian Torch): 0.5–2.0% dried tissue

Note: Published mescaline content figures vary widely between sources. Potency depends on genetics, growing conditions, age, and part of the cactus used. The green outer tissue has the highest concentration.

## Method 1: Acid-Base Extraction (A/B Tek)

The standard chemistry approach for isolating mescaline from cactus material.

### Materials
- Dried cactus material: 100–500 g (powdered)
- Distilled water
- Hydrochloric acid (HCl) or citric acid
- Sodium hydroxide (NaOH / lye)
- Xylene, toluene, or naphtha (nonpolar solvent)
- Glass jars, separatory funnel (if available)
- pH testing strips or meter
- Safety equipment: gloves, goggles, ventilation, fume hood if possible

### Step 1: Acid Extraction
1. Powder dried cactus material as finely as possible
2. Add to a large pot with acidified water (pH ~3–4 using HCl or citric acid)
3. Simmer for 2–3 hours, stirring occasionally
4. Strain through a cheesecloth or t-shirt, saving the liquid
5. Repeat 2–3 more times with fresh acidified water
6. Combine all liquid extracts

### Step 2: Defatting (Optional but Recommended)
1. Cool the combined extract to room temperature
2. Add a nonpolar solvent (naphtha or xylene)
3. Shake vigorously and allow layers to separate
4. Discard the nonpolar layer (contains fats, waxes, and other non-alkaloid material)
5. Repeat 2–3 times until the nonpolar layer comes out clean

### Step 3: Basify
1. Add sodium hydroxide slowly to the aqueous extract to raise the pH to 11–13
2. The solution will become cloudy as mescaline freebase precipitates
3. The freebase will dissolve into a nonpolar solvent when added

### Step 4: Nonpolar Extraction
1. Add xylene or toluene (naphtha works but is less efficient for mescaline)
2. Shake vigorously for several minutes
3. Allow layers to separate completely
4. Collect the nonpolar (top) layer — this contains mescaline freebase
5. Repeat 3–4 times for maximum extraction

### Step 5: Salt Formation
1. Combine all nonpolar solvent pulls
2. Bubble dry HCl gas through the solution (advanced) OR:
   - Add a small amount of dilute HCl solution and shake
   - The mescaline HCl will migrate to the aqueous layer
3. Collect the aqueous layer containing mescaline HCl

### Step 6: Evaporation
1. Evaporate the aqueous solution to dryness using a water bath or gentle heat
2. The resulting crystals are mescaline hydrochloride

## Method 2: Tea Method (Non-Extraction)

For those who do not want to work with solvents, the traditional tea method is effective:

1. Clean and chop cactus (30–60 cm of San Pedro)
2. Simmer in acidified water (add lemon juice) for 4–6 hours
3. Strain and repeat 2–3 times
4. Combine extracts and reduce to drinkable volume
5. Consume as a bitter tea

See [[san-pedro-cactus-preparation]] for detailed tea preparation instructions.

## Method 3: Mescaline Acetate

A simpler salt form that is easier to crystallize:

1. Perform the A/B extraction through Step 4 (nonpolar extraction of freebase)
2. Instead of converting to HCl, add distilled white vinegar (acetic acid) to the nonpolar solution
3. Mescaline acetate will form in the aqueous layer
4. Separate and evaporate the aqueous layer
5. Mescaline acetate crystals form as the solution concentrates

Mescaline acetate is a white crystalline powder, less hygroscopic than HCl.

## Method 4: Mescaline Sulfate

Mescaline sulfate is another common salt form:

1. After basifying the cactus extract (Step 3 of A/B), instead of nonpolar solvent extraction:
2. Add sulfuric acid to re-acidify to pH ~7
3. Mescaline sulfate has limited solubility in water and will precipitate as the solution is concentrated
4. Filter and dry the crystals

Mescaline sulfate forms larger, more beautiful crystals than HCl but is slightly less potent by weight due to the higher molecular weight of the sulfate salt.

## Dosage

All doses below are for mescaline hydrochloride:
- **Threshold**: 100–150 mg
- **Light**: 150–250 mg
- **Common**: 200–400 mg
- **Strong**: 400–600 mg
- **Heavy**: 600+ mg (not recommended without experience)

Note: Mescaline sulfate and acetate have different molecular weights, so adjust doses accordingly.

Onset: 45–90 minutes. Peak: 2–4 hours. Duration: 8–12 hours.

## Safety Considerations

- Mescaline produces a long-duration experience — plan for 12+ hours
- Nausea during onset is common and sometimes severe
- Cardiovascular effects include increased heart rate and blood pressure
- Not recommended for people with heart conditions, schizophrenia, or [[blesching-cannabis-bipolar-disorder]]
