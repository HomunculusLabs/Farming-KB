## 2C-B (4-Bromo-2,5-Dimethoxyphenethylamine)

## Overview

2C-B is a synthetic psychedelic phenethylamine first synthesized and studied by Alexander Shulgin. It is compound #20 in PiHKAL (Phenethylamines I Have Known And Loved). The name "2C" refers to the two-carbon chain of the phenethylamine skeleton, distinguishing it from the three-carbon amphetamine analogues (such as DOB, DOI). 2C-B gained significant popularity in the 1990s and early 2000s as a recreational substance and has been used in psychotherapeutic settings.

## Chemical Identity

- **Chemical name**: 4-Bromo-2,5-dimethoxyphenethylamine
- **Molecular formula**: C10H14BrNO2
- **Molecular weight**: 244.12 g/mol
- **Structure**: Phenethylamine core with bromine at the 4-position and methoxy groups at the 2 and 5 positions on the aromatic ring
- **Appearance**: White crystalline hydrochloride salt
- **Melting point**: 237-239°C (hydrochloride, with decomposition)
- **Solubility**: Free base picks up CO2 from air rapidly; hydrochloride salt is stable

## Dosage and Duration

- **Dosage range**: 12-24 mg orally
- **Threshold**: 4-8 mg
- **Light experience**: 8-12 mg
- **Common experience**: 12-18 mg
- **Strong experience**: 18-24 mg
- **Duration**: 4-8 hours
- **Onset**: 20-60 minutes

2C-B is considered a moderately potent phenethylamine. Its dosage range is relatively narrow, and the difference between a light and strong experience can be as little as 8-10 mg. Shulgin noted that the effects are dose-responsive with clear progression from mild perceptual enhancement at lower doses to full [[cultural-evolution-and-psychoactive-substances]] sold in a legal gray area. Eventually, most countries adopted analogue or blanket scheduling laws to address the proliferation of 2C compounds.

## Historical Context

2C-B was first synthesized by Shulgin in 1974. During the 1980s, it saw limited use in the [[savory-short-duration-grazing-and-cell-systems]] and controllable intensity compared to LSD or psilocybin. Therapists found that patients could engage with difficult material without being overwhelmed, and the shorter duration made it practical for outpatient sessions.

In the early 1990s, 2C-B became popular in the rave and nightclub scene, where its empathogenic and stimulant qualities at lower doses made it a MDMA alternative. It was often sold in tablet form, sometimes misrepresented as MDMA, which created risks for users expecting MDMA's effects.
