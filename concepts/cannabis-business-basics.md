# Cannabis Business Basics

## Overview

The legal cannabis industry represents one of the fastest-growing economic sectors in the United States, with annual revenues exceeding $30 billion nationally as of 2026. However, starting and operating a cannabis business involves unique challenges including federal prohibition, complex state regulations, limited access to banking, and intense competition. This guide covers the fundamentals of entering the legal cannabis market.

## Business Models

### Cultivation

Growing cannabis for wholesale distribution to processors, manufacturers, and retailers.

**Types:**
- Indoor cultivation: Highest quality, highest costs, controlled environment
- Greenhouse cultivation: Balance of quality and cost, uses natural light supplemented with artificial
- Outdoor cultivation: Lowest cost per pound, seasonal, weather-dependent
- Mixed-light/greenhouse: Increasingly popular for balancing quality and economics

**Key Considerations:**
- High upfront capital requirements for equipment and facility buildout
- Strict [[cannabis-regulations-by-state|state licensing]] and compliance requirements
- Environmental controls (HVAC, lighting, irrigation)
- Security requirements (cameras, access control, vaults)
- [[cannabis-testing-and-quality|Testing]] requirements for all harvested product
- Staffing for cultivation, processing, and management

### Dispensary / Retail

Direct sales to consumers through licensed retail locations.

**Types:**
- Medical dispensary: Serves registered medical patients only
- Adult-use retail: Serves consumers 21+ (and medical patients where programs overlap)
- Dual-licensed: Serves both medical and recreational customers
- Delivery-only: No storefront, delivery to registered addresses

**Key Considerations:**
- Location selection (zoning restrictions are common)
- Inventory management across product categories
- Staff training on product knowledge and customer service
- Compliance with packaging, labeling, and purchase limits
- Point-of-sale systems designed for cannabis (age verification, purchase limits)
- Security and cash management (most dispensaries are cash-only due to banking restrictions)

### Manufacturing / Processing

Creating value-added cannabis products including concentrates, edibles, topicals, and tinctures.

**Types:**
- Extraction: Producing oils, waxes, shatter, live resin, and other concentrates
- Edibles: Infused foods and beverages
- Topicals: Balms, salves, lotions, transdermal patches
- Tinctures: Sublingual liquid products
- Pre-rolls: Pre-rolled joints

**Key Considerations:**
- Equipment costs (extraction machines, commercial kitchens)
- Food safety compliance for edible products
- Dose consistency and testing requirements
- Product development and formulation expertise
- Packaging and labeling compliance

### Ancillary Businesses

Businesses that support the cannabis industry without directly handling the plant:

- **Consulting:** Compliance, cultivation, business strategy
- **Technology:** POS systems, seed-to-sale tracking, compliance software
- **Packaging:** Child-resistant packaging, branding, design
- **Security:** Installation, monitoring, guard services
- **Legal:** Specialized cannabis law firms
- **Testing laboratories:** [[cannabis-testing-and-quality|Analytical testing services]]
- **Real estate:** Cannabis-friendly property leasing
- **Media and marketing:** Industry publications, advertising agencies, influencers

Ancillary businesses avoid many of the licensing and compliance burdens of plant-touching businesses but may still face banking and regulatory challenges.

## Licensing

### Application Process

Obtaining a cannabis license is typically competitive and expensive:

1. **Research available license types** in your target jurisdiction
2. **Prepare application materials:**
   - Business plan
   - Financial projections and proof of capital
   - Security plan
   - Operational plan
   - Compliance plan
   - Background checks for all owners and key personnel
   - Real estate documentation (lease or ownership)
   - Community impact assessment
3. **Submit application** with application fees (often $5,000-$50,000+)
4. **Competitive scoring:** Many states score applications and award limited licenses
5. **Compliance verification:** Site inspections and operational readiness reviews
6. **License issuance:** Upon approval and payment of licensing fees

### License Costs

- **Application fees:** $1,000-$50,000+ depending on state and license type
- **Annual license fees:** $5,000-$100,000+ depending on state, type, and size
- **Renewal fees:** Similar to annual fees
- **Modification fees:** For changes to licensed premises, ownership, or operations

## Compliance

### Seed-to-Sale Tracking

Most states require seed-to-sale tracking systems that monitor cannabis from cultivation through sale:

- **Metrc:** The most widely used tracking platform (used by most legal states)
- **Leaf Data Systems:** Alternative platform used in some states
- All plants must be tagged and tracked from clone/seed
- All transfers, processing, and sales must be recorded
- Regular audits and inventory reconciliation required
- Penalties for discrepancies can include license suspension or revocation

### Security Requirements

- Video surveillance (30-90 day retention), access control, alarm systems
- Safe or vault for cash and product storage
- Background checks for all employees

### Packaging and Labeling

- Child-resistant packaging (ASTM certified)
- THC content, serving size, government warnings
- License number, batch number, [[cannabis-testing-and-quality|testing]] results
- Strain name and type (indica/sativa/hybrid)

### Advertising Restrictions

- No advertising to minors, no unverified medical claims
- Restrictions near schools, churches, and playgrounds
- Social media platforms often prohibit cannabis content
