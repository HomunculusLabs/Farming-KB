# Cannabis for Musculoskeletal Conditions

Musculoskeletal conditions including arthritis, fibromyalgia, and related disorders are among the most common reasons patients seek medical cannabis. The [[cannabidiol-therapeutic-mechanisms]].

## Arthritis (Osteoarthritis)

CHI Value: 6 (3 studies reviewed)

Osteoarthritis is the most common form of arthritis, affecting millions worldwide. It involves progressive cartilage degradation and associated inflammation.

### Evidence Base
- Meta-analysis (2009, University of Massachusetts Medical School) confirmed that all types of cannabinoids (endogenous, plant-based, and synthetic) effectively reduce pain from inflammation in rheumatism and chronic neuropathic pain
- Laboratory studies (2006, Boston) suggested [[blesching-cannabis-inflammatory-diseases]]
- CB2 receptors in joint tissue mediate anti-inflammatory effects

### Mechanism of Action
- CB2 receptor activation reduces pro-inflammatory cytokine production in joint tissue
- CB1 receptor activation provides analgesic effects
- Anti-inflammatory action reduces joint swelling and stiffness
- Cannabinoids may slow cartilage degradation through anti-inflammatory pathways

## Rheumatoid Arthritis

CHI Value: 10 (3 studies reviewed)

RA is the most crippling form of arthritis, deforming joints and bending bodies. It is considered an autoimmune disorder where the immune system attacks healthy joint tissue.

### Key Clinical Findings
- **2006 (UK Multi-institutional)**: First controlled trial of cannabis-based medicine in RA. 58 patients in double-blind, placebo-controlled study. Sativex (roughly equal parts THC 2.7 mg and CBD 2.5 mg) produced significant analgesic effect and suppressed disease activity. Patients slept better and experienced pain reduction.
- **2006 (Boston)**: Endocannabinoid circuitry regulation can "impact almost every major function associated with the immune system," suggesting therapeutic potential for inflammatory diseases including RA.
- **2009 (Worcester, MA)**: Meta-analysis confirmed all types of cannabinoids and non-cannabinoid plant parts effectively reduce pain from RA inflammation.

### Strain Considerations
Sativa and sativa-prominent hybrids contain relatively higher THC:CBD ratios, activating both CB1 (analgesic) and CB2 (anti-inflammatory) receptors. The roughly equal THC:CBD ratio of Sativex aligns with many balanced hybrid strains.

## Fibromyalgia

CHI Value: 14 (3 studies reviewed)

Fibromyalgia involves widespread musculoskeletal pain, fatigue, sleep disturbance, and cognitive difficulties. It frequently co-occurs with other conditions and may involve immune dysregulation and central sensitization.

### Key Clinical Findings
- **2007 (Winnipeg)**: Nabilone provided significant pain relief and functional improvement in fibromyalgia patients
- **2008 (Montreal)**: Nabilone was superior to amitriptyline (a standard fibromyalgia medication) for improving sleep quality
- **2009 (Meta-analysis)**: All cannabinoids show anti-inflammatory action relevant to fibromyalgia

### Psychosomatic Connections
Research links fibromyalgia to early life stress affecting brain modulatory circuitry of pain and emotions in genetically susceptible individuals. The mind-body connection is particularly strong in fibromyalgia, and addressing psychological factors alongside cannabinoid therapy may improve outcomes.

## Muscle Spasms and Cramps

Cannabis's antispasmodic properties are relevant for:
- **[[blesching-cannabis-spinal-cord-injuries]] injury**: May reduce spasticity below the level of injury
- **Menstrual cramps**: Historical and clinical use for dysmenorrhea
- **Exercise-induced cramps**: Anecdotal reports of relief
- **Parkinson's disease**: Reduces muscle rigidity

### Mechanism
CB1 receptors in the spinal cord and peripheral nervous system modulate muscle tone. THC activates CB1 receptors to reduce excessive muscle firing and spasm.

## Osteoporosis

CB2 receptors are present in bone tissue and play a role in bone metabolism. The endocannabinoid system influences:
- **Bone formation**: CB2 activation may promote osteoblast (bone-building cell) activity
- **Bone resorption**: May reduce osteoclast (bone-removing cell) activity
