## Pistils

Pistils are the white, hair-like structures that emerge from calyxes.
They are the reproductive organs of the female flower, designed to
catch male pollen. Female flowers initially appear as two small,
one-quarter to one-half inch (6-12 mm) fuzzy white hairs forming a
"V" shape. The set of pistils is attached at the base to an ovule
contained within the calyx. Pistil-packed calyxes form dense clusters
or buds along stems.

As flowering progresses, pistils change color -- typically from white
to amber, and eventually to reddish brown. This color change is one
of the visual indicators growers use to assess harvest readiness,
though trichome clarity is considered more reliable.

## Bracts and Stipules

The seed bract (also called the stipule) is the small, leaf-like
structure at the base of the calyx. It is often the first part of the
pre-flower to form, appearing as a light green protrusion behind
which pistils later emerge. The bract partially encloses and protects
the developing calyx. In some references, the terms bract and stipule
