## Mycorrhizal Helper Bacteria and Tripartite Symbioses

## Overview

Mycorrhizal helper bacteria (MHB) are a functional group of rhizosphere bacteria that stimulate the establishment, development, and function of mycorrhizal symbioses. These bacteria form the third partner in a tripartite association between plants, arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) or ectomycorrhizal (ECM) fungi, and bacteria. The concept was first formalized by Garbaye (1994), though the phenomenon had been observed decades earlier.

## Definition and Functional Group

MHB are defined by their functional effect rather than taxonomy:
- They specifically promote mycorrhiza formation (not merely plant growth).
- The effect is strain-specific, not species-specific — related bacterial strains may differ dramatically in MHB activity.
- They are distinct from general plant growth-promoting rhizobacteria (PGPR), though some bacteria fulfill both roles.
- They are found in diverse taxa including *Pseudomonas*, *Bacillus*, *Streptomyces*, *Rhizobium*, *Burkholderia*, and *Azospirillum*.

## Mechanisms of Action

### Pre-Symbiotic Effects (Before Mycorrhizal Contact)

**Fungal Spore Germination Stimulation**
- Some MHB produce [[staycare-fungal-organic-acid-production-metal-leaching]] (gluconic, citric, oxalic acids).
- Others mineralize organic phosphorus through phosphatase enzyme production.

### Post-Symbiotic Effects (During Mycorrhizal Function)

**Enhanced Nutrient Exchange**
- MHB in the mycorrhizosphere may facilitate more efficient nutrient transfer between fungus and plant.
- Bacterial activity at the fungal-plant interface may stimulate the expression of plant phosphate transporters.

**Protection Against Stress**
- MHB can protect the mycorrhizal association from environmental stresses including drought, salinity, and heavy metal toxicity.
- Some MHB produce ACC deaminase, which reduces stress ethylene levels in the plant, benefiting both plant growth and mycorrhizal function.

**Disease Suppression**
- MHB contribute to the biocontrol activity of mycorrhizal associations against soil-borne pathogens.
- Multiple mechanisms may be involved: antibiosis, competition for nutrients and niches, [[cannabis-endorhiza-bacterial-communities]] in the rhizosphere.
- MHB may produce compounds that modulate strigolactone signaling, creating a feedback loop.
- The common symbiosis signaling pathway (CSSP) in plants, originally characterized for rhizobium-legume symbiosis, also governs AM fungal colonization and may be modulated by bacterial signals.

### Genomic Insights
- Metagenomic studies of the mycorrhizosphere reveal distinct bacterial communities compared to bulk soil or non-mycorrhizal rhizosphere.
- The mycorrhizosphere (soil influenced by both root and fungal exudates) harbors a bacterial community that is enriched for genes related to nutrient cycling, stress response, and plant growth promotion.
- Specific bacterial operational taxonomic units (OTUs) are consistently associated with mycorrhizal roots across different plant species and soil types.

## Types of Mycorrhizal Associations Affected

### Arbuscular Mycorrhizal Fungi
- Most MHB research has focused on AM fungi (*Rhizoglomus irregularis*, *[[funneliformis-mosseae]]*, etc.).
- Bacterial effects on AM include increased spore germination, enhanced hyphal elongation, accelerated root colonization, and improved arbuscule formation.
- The MHB effect is particularly important in disturbed or low-fertility soils where natural mycorrhizal establishment is slow.
