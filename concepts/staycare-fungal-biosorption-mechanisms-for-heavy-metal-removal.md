# [[biosorption-isotherms]] and Kinetics

Biosorption equilibrium data are commonly described by the Langmuir and
Freundlich isotherm models. The Langmuir model assumes monolayer sorption
on a homogeneous surface with finite identical sites, while the Freundlich
model describes sorption on heterogeneous surfaces. Many fungal biosorption
systems follow Langmuir behavior, suggesting a relatively homogeneous
distribution of binding sites. Biosorption kinetics typically follow pseudo-
second-order models, indicating that chemisorption is the rate-limiting
step. Rapid initial uptake is common, with equilibrium often achieved
within 30-120 minutes for most fungal-metal systems.

## Dead versus Living Biomass

Both living and dead [[bioremediation-fungal-biomass-biosorbent-material]] can function as effective biosorbents.
Dead biomass offers several practical advantages: it does not require
nutrient supply, is not affected by toxic metal concentrations, can be
stored for extended periods, and can be regenerated and reused through
desorption with acids or chelating agents. Living biomass provides the
additional mechanism of bioaccumulation, where metals are actively
transported intracellularly. However, living cells may be inhibited or
killed by high metal concentrations. Pretreatment of dead biomass with
alkalis, acids, or heat can significantly enhance biosorption capacity by
exposing additional binding sites or removing masking substances.
