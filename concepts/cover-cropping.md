# Cover Cropping, Green Manures, and Living Mulches

Cover crops are plants grown primarily to benefit the soil and ecosystem rather than for harvest. They are one of the most powerful tools in regenerative agriculture, performing the work of fertilization, weed suppression, erosion control, and soil building that would otherwise require chemical inputs and machinery.

## Why Cover Crop

Cover crops provide multiple simultaneous benefits:

- **Nitrogen fixation**: Legumes (clover, vetch, peas, beans) convert atmospheric nitrogen into plant-available form through symbiosis with rhizobia bacteria
- **Soil protection**: Prevent erosion from wind and rain by holding soil in place with roots
- **Weed suppression**: Dense plantings outcompete weeds for light, water, and nutrients
- **Organic matter**: When terminated and incorporated (or left as mulch), cover crops add biomass that feeds soil organisms
- **Soil structure**: Deep taproots (daikon radish, tillage radish) break compaction and create channels for water infiltration
- **Water retention**: Living roots and surface cover reduce evaporation and increase water infiltration
- **Pest disruption**: Break pest and disease cycles by interrupting monoculture patterns
- **Pollinator habitat**: Many cover crops (buckwheat, clover, phacelia) provide excellent nectar and pollen

## Major Cover Crop Families

### Legumes (Nitrogen Fixers)
Best for adding nitrogen to the soil. Inoculate with the appropriate rhizobium strain for best results.

| Crop | When to Plant | Notes |
|------|--------------|-------|
| Crimson clover | Fall (zone 6+) | Beautiful red flowers, good bee forage, winter hardy |
| White dutch clover | Spring or fall | Excellent living mulch, low growing, tolerates foot traffic |
| Red clover | Spring or fall | Biennial, deep taproot, good forage for animals |
| Hairy vetch | Fall | Vigorous, very winter hardy, high nitrogen production |
| Austrian winter pea | Fall | Quick growing, good nitrogen fixer, winter kills in cold zones |
| Cowpeas | Summer | Heat tolerant, fast growing, good in warm climates |
| Field peas | Early spring | Cool season, good for spring planting before summer crops |
| Soybeans | Summer | Dual purpose -- can harvest beans or terminate as green manure |
| Alfalfa | Spring | Deep taproot (6+ feet), perennial, very high nitrogen production |

### Grasses (Soil Builders and Scavengers)
Best for adding biomass, preventing erosion, and scavenging excess nutrients.

| Crop | When to Plant | Notes |
|------|--------------|-------|
| Winter rye | Fall | Extremely winter hardy, vigorous, good for fall planting |
| Oats | Early spring or fall | Fast growing, winter kills in cold zones (easy termination) |
| Annual ryegrass | Fall or spring | Dense root system, good for erosion control |
| Barley | Early spring or fall | Fast growing, less competitive than rye |
| Buckwheat | Summer | Very fast (6 weeks to flower), excellent bee forage, heat tolerant |
| Sorghum-sudangrass | Summer | Massive biomass production, deep roots, allelopathic (suppresses weeds) |

### Brassicas (Soil Conditioners)
Best for breaking compaction and scavenging nutrients.

| Crop | When to Plant | Notes |
|------|--------------|-------|
| Daikon/tillage radish | Late summer/fall | Giant taproot drills through compacted soil, winter kills |
| Mustard | Spring or fall | Biofumigant -- releases compounds that suppress soil pathogens when terminated |
| Rapeseed/canola | Fall | Deep taproot, good nutrient scavenger |

### Broadleaves (Diversity Builders)
| Crop | When to Plant | Notes |
|------|--------------|-------|
| Phacelia | Spring or summer | Excellent pollinator plant, quick growing |
| Sunflower | Spring | Tall, bee forage, nutrient accumulator |
| Chicory | Spring | Deep taproot, drought tolerant, good livestock forage |

## Cover Crop Strategies

### Fall-Winter Cover (Most Important)
After main crops are harvested, sow a fall cover crop to protect soil through winter. In cold climates:
- Winter rye + hairy vetch (classic combination)
- Crimson clover + winter rye
- Oats + field peas (winter kills, leaves bare soil covered with dead mulch for easy spring planting)

### Living Mulch
Maintain a permanent ground cover beneath or between crops:
- White clover between rows of vegetables or fruit trees
- Perennial ryegrass in [[orchard-management]] rows
- Clovers and grasses in pathways between raised beds

This is central to [[no-till-farming]] -- you never leave soil bare.

### Green Manure Crops
Grow a crop specifically to terminate and incorporate into the soil:
- Buckwheat in summer (terminate at flowering, 6 weeks)
- Cowpeas in warm season (terminate before seed set)
- Crimson clover in fall (terminate in spring at flowering for maximum nitrogen)

### Chop-and-Drop
Cut cover crops at the base and leave them on the soil surface as mulch. No tilling needed -- the mulch suppresses weeds, retains moisture, and feeds soil organisms as it decomposes. This is the preferred method in [[no-till-farming]] systems.

### Animal Integration
Let animals graze or "tractor" over cover crops:
- Chickens in a chicken tractor over buckwheat or clover
- [[duck-keeping]] grazing clover patches
- [[goat-keeping]] browsing taller covers like sorghum-sudangrass
- Animals fertilize while they graze, closing the nutrient loop

## Termination Methods

- **Crimp-and-roll**: Use a roller/crimper to flatten the crop (works for rye, vetch at maturity)
- **Chop-and-drop**: Cut with hand tools or weed whacker, leave as mulch
- **Mow and leave**: Lawn mower on high setting, leave clippings as mulch
- **Solarization**: In hot climates, cover with clear plastic for 2-4 weeks
- **Grazing**: Let animals eat it down
- **Winter kill**: Plant oats, buckwheat, or sorghum in fall -- they die naturally in cold weather

## Using Cover Crops on a Homestead

- Never leave garden beds bare between crops -- always have something growing
- Overseed vegetables with clover as a living mulch
- After garlic or onion harvest in summer, immediately sow buckwheat
- In fall, sow rye + vetch in all empty beds
- Under fruit trees, maintain a permanent clover ground cover
- Use duck/goat manure to boost cover crop growth
- In no-till beds, terminate covers by cutting and leaving as surface mulch
- Use cover crop residues as [[composting-systems]] inputs when needed
