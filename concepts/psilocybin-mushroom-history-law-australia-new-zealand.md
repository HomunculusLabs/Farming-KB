# History and Law of Psilocybin Mushrooms in Australia and New Zealand

## Scientific Discovery Timeline in Australia

- **Pre-1934:** First suspected hallucinogenic mushroom intoxication in Australia (Cleland's colleague's parents ate dung-inhabiting mushrooms, experienced hilarity)
- **1941–1945:** Numerous reports of "hysteria fungus" poisonings attributed to *Panaeolus ovatus* — actually likely *[[copelandia-cyanescens]]* (which is not psychoactive)
- **1958:** Aberdeen and Vogt documented *[[psilocybe-cubensis]]* as the first known psychoactive mushroom in Australia (Springbrook, Qld)
- **1970:** Picker and Rickards found psilocybin (but no psilocin) in *P. subaeruginosa* — second confirmed species
- **1970:** Perkel et al. developed HPLC method for quantifying psilocin/psilocybin from dried *P. subaeruginosa* specimens
- **1978:** Guzmán and Watling described three new species (*P. eucalypta*, *P. australiana*, *P. tasmaniana*) based on intense bluing reactions
- **1980:** Australian Government Royal Commission report on drugs addressed mushroom abuse

## Accidental Intoxication Case Histories

### Early Reports
- **1934 (Cleland):** Parents ate dish of mushrooms, "gradually became more and more hilarious, the most simple remarks giving rise to peals of laughter"
- **1941–1945:** "Hysteria fungus" (*P. ovatus*) reports — symptoms included drowsiness, staggering gait, uncontrollable laughter, euphoria
- **1963 (Stocks, Brisbane):** 11 patients admitted to Princess Alexandra Hospital over 6 years; 5 definitely caused by *P. cubensis*

### Notable Case Histories
1. **Female patient (Stocks):** Dark/green clouds passing across eyes, thick tongue, paralysis sensation, resolved in 12 hours
2. **Male patient (Stocks):** TV "changed color" to steel-blue, objects appeared alternately too large/small, sharp images of dragons in oriental settings
3. **1969 Forster, NSW:** Professional artist couple experienced violent hallucinations for 3+ hours; woman felt skin peeling off hands, believed she was dying
4. **1969 Gold Coast family picnic:** Whole family affected — euphoria, depression, inappropriate speech, visual hallucinations, ataxia, vomiting, urinary incontinence; "respectable family man ran naked through hospital trying to molest nurses"
5. **1971 Adelaide:** 17-year-old female on *C. cyanescens* thought she was a banana and someone was trying to skin her
6. **1972 Adelaide Hills:** 3-year-old girl experienced repeated hallucinations (colored lights, phantom cats, feeling larger than normal); Southcott attributed to *P. foenisecii* (later confirmed non-psychoactive)

## Clinical Effects Timeline (Hollister et al., 1962)

| Time | Effects |
|------|---------|
| 0–30 min | Nausea, giddiness, abdominal discomfort, weakness, muscle twitches, shivering, anxiety, numbness of lips |
| 30–60 min | Visual effects (brighter colors, sharper outlines), increased hearing, sweating, facial flushing, depersonalization, dreamy state |
| 60–120 min | Colored patterns (mostly eyes closed), wave-motion of surfaces, euphoria, slowed time perception |
| 120–240 min | Waning effects; normal within 4–12 hours |

Duration: **3–6 hours** (vs. 8–12 hours for LSD, mescaline, MDA). Onset: **15–30 min** (fresh), **5–10 min** (tea/soup).

## Treatment for Psilocybin "Bad Trips"

1. **"Talk-down" technique** — non-moralizing, comforting personal support (preferred method)
2. **Limit external stimulation** — reduce light and sound; soft music
3. **Diazepam** — 0.1 mg/kg (children), up to 10 mg (adults) for seizures only
4. **Do NOT use** chlorpromazine or other anti-cholinergics — mushrooms have anti-cholinergic effects of their own
5. **Gastric lavage/emesis** — generally NOT recommended once psychological effects have commenced; not helpful and may worsen aggression. Only warranted if toxic mushroom species suspected or patient is a young child

Dr. Jansen's classification of medical presentations:
- (a) Adolescents with little knowledge, without experienced sitters
- (b) Falls due to impaired balance/muscle weakness
- (c) Acute anxiety/panic, depression, paranoia, disorientation
- (d) Idiosyncratic physical reactions (e.g., cyanosis)
- (e) Post-experience phenomena including prolonged psychosis

## Legal History

### Australia
- **1969, July 11:** First psilocybin possession conviction — 4 men aged 20–22 fined $200 each near Nambour, Qld (found with box of mushrooms in van)
- **1972:** 60–74 persons charged with psilocybin offenses across Australia
- **1973:** Only 27 persons charged (decline in use attributed to extreme depression/lethargy reports)
- **Queensland:** Only state to specifically name *P. cubensis* as a prohibited "plant" under Health Act 1937–1971
- **Tasmania:** Psilocybin/psilocin listed as Schedule 3 drugs (Dangerous Drugs Order 1965); later Schedule 1 (International Convention 1971)
- **1980:** Government Royal Commission report documented mushroom availability across multiple states

### New Zealand
- **Misuse of Drugs Act 1975:** Declared *P. mexicana* and *P. cubensis* prohibited plants — neither has ever been found growing in NZ
- Psilocybin and psilocin listed as Class A substances
- **Early prosecutions failed** — judges ruled mushrooms were not "substances" (chemicals)
- **1988 Amendment:** Declared ALL members of genera *Psilocybe* and *Panaeolus* prohibited plants
- Sentences continue to be mild; police generally avoid involvement unless called by farmers
- **Agricultural spraying** represents greater public health threat than mushrooms themselves — farmers and enforcement authorities have sprayed fungicides on paddocks, causing respiratory muscle weakness lasting 18+ hours

## Flashbacks
- Hall (1973): Reported recurring flashbacks similar to LSD-associated phenomena
- Jansen: "No firm evidence that mushroom flashbacks can occur"
- English study (1978–1981): 21 of 318 intoxication cases experienced flashback phenomena for up to 4 months; some attributed to drug synergy/polydrug abuse

## New Zealand Specifics
- Awareness spread from early 1980s via surfers recognizing *C. cyanescens* at New Plymouth sand dunes and a UK botanist finding *P. semilanceata* on Otago Peninsula
- 1982 media reports paradoxically provided precise location instructions while exaggerating dangers
- Jansen study (1982–1989): 150 users interviewed; only 1 ER visit (musician cut head from fall); 2% experienced prolonged psychological difficulties (severe paranoid psychosis with obvious predisposing features)
- **No deaths** from [[psilocybin-mushrooms]] in NZ; no reported cases in NZ medical literature
- **No evidence** of pre-conquest Māori use of psychoactive mushrooms — Māori culture used no consciousness-altering drugs of any kind
- 5 indigenous Psilocybe species identified, 4 undescribed; *P. novo zealandiae* (Guzmán, 1983) is not psychoactive

## Toxicity Data
- **LD₅₀ (psilocybin, mice, oral):** 280 mg/kg
- **Calculated lethal dose (70 kg human):** ~19.6 g pure psilocybin
- Jansen caution: Mouse-to-human LD₅₀ extrapolation is not valid due to different metabolic rates
- **Guzmán et al. (1976):** "Psychoactive mushroom use as it normally occurs does not constitute a drug abuse problem or a public health hazard"
- Thompson et al. (1985): College student survey found "low frequency and few negative effects... abuse does not present a social problem"

## Ingestion Methods
- Fresh (reportedly most powerful/intensely visual)
- Dried and consumed later
- Frozen for storage
- Capsules for resale
- Mixed in milkshakes ("mushroom smoothies") or tea
- Cooked in soup, stew, or omelette
- Boiled in water to extract actives (water-soluble psilocybin); liquid used for rice/soup
- Packed in honey for preservation/transport (method dating back to Aztec ceremonies)
- Chocolate (also used in Aztec ceremonies)

## Fatal Misidentification Risk
The primary physical danger is **confusing toxic species with psychoactive ones**:
- *[[galerina-autumnalis]]* / *G. venenata* — deadly poisonous, share habitat with *Psilocybe* species in mulched gardens
- *Conocybe filaris* — extremely poisonous, same habitat
- *[[chlorophyllum-molybdites]]* ("green gills") — toxic (not deadly); common in Australia; >90% of Florida mushroom poisonings from this species being mistaken for *P. cubensis*
- **1981 Whidbey Island, WA:** 16-year-old died after eating *Galerina autumnalis* she thought was *Psilocybe*; delayed seeking medical help for 2 days due to fear of prosecution
