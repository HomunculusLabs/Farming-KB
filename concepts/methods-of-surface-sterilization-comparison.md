## Gamma Irradiation

### Principle
Gamma rays (high-energy photons from radioactive isotopes like Cobalt-60 or Cesium-137) damage microbial DNA through direct ionization and indirect free-radical generation. Doses of 25–40 kGy are standard for sterilization.

### Advantages
- Excellent penetration through materials — can sterilize sealed packages
- No significant temperature increase during treatment
- Effective against all known microorganisms at sufficient dose
- No [[blesching-cannabis-apoptosis-and-cancer-cell-death]]. UV is a surface phenomenon — penetration into materials is negligible.

### Advantages
- Chemical-free with no residues
- Rapid treatment (seconds to minutes)
- Low operating cost
- Effective against a wide range of bacteria, viruses, and fungi
- Simple equipment requirements

### Limitations
- **Line-of-sight only** — cannot sterilize shadowed areas, recesses, or [[comparison-laminar-flow-hood-vs-fruiting-chamber]] hoods and clean rooms to maintain air sterility between uses. However, they cannot sterilize tools or surfaces that are shadowed from the light source.

## Chemical Disinfectants

### Ethylene Oxide (EtO)
A gaseous alkylating agent that penetrates packaging and equipment. Highly effective but toxic, carcinogenic, and requires extensive aeration periods (12–48 hours) after treatment to remove residues. Increasingly regulated and being phased out in many applications.

### Alcohols (Ethanol, Isopropanol)
Rapid broad-spectrum activity at 60–90% concentration. Evaporates quickly leaving no residue. However, does not reliably kill bacterial spores and cannot penetrate biofilms. Flammable. Commonly used for surface wipes and tool sanitization in mycology laboratories.

### Quaternary Ammonium Compounds (Quats)
Cationic surfactants that disrupt cell membranes. Good for routine environmental disinfection but ineffective against spores and mycobacteria. Can leave residues that may be toxic to sensitive biological systems.

### Hydrogen Peroxide
Available as liquid (3–6%), vapor, and plasma (cold sterilization) forms. Broad-spectrum activity including spore kill at sufficient concentration. Breaks down to water and oxygen, leaving no toxic residues. Vapor-phase hydrogen peroxide (VHP) is used for whole-room decontamination.

### Elemental Iodine
Broad-spectrum antimicrobial effective against bacteria, fungi, viruses, and some spores. Used in solution (tincture of iodine, povidone-iodine) for skin and surface disinfection. Can leave staining residues and has limited penetration into complex geometries.

## Microwave Irradiation

### Principle (from MSC-22484)
2.45 GHz microwave energy couples with dipolar water molecules on contaminated surfaces, generating localized heating and potentially non-thermal effects that destroy microorganisms.

### Advantages
- No chemical residues — only heat and steam as byproducts
- Can potentially sterilize complex geometries through antenna design
- Rapid treatment compared to autoclaving
- Portable systems are feasible
- Targeted application to specific surfaces within assembled systems
- Moisture-dependent mechanism allows spatial control

### Limitations
- Requires controlled moisture on target surfaces (~9 μL/cm²)
