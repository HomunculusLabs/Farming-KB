## The Problem

"Bare soil is damaged soil and occurs only where man or introduced animals have interfered with the natural ecological balance." The conventional plough not only damages soil life processes but may cause more extensive soil losses. Pest plants (lantana, capeweed, blackberry, mullein, thistle) are "the attempts of damaged soil to produce a plant mat that will prepare the way for forest or crop."

## Three Approaches to Minimal Soil Loss
1. Growing forests and shrubberies to protect soil
2. Using ploughs that do not turn the soil (soil conditioning)
3. Encouraging life forms (especially worms) to aerate compacted soils

All produce the same result: soil aeration and safe nutrient addition.

## Broadscale Soil Conditioning

### Keyline (Yeomans) and Wallace's Soil Conditioner
Both developed tools for rehabilitating compacted, unproductive, sterile soil:
- Compacted soil is lifted gently (not turned over or reversed), aerated, and loosened
- Rain penetrates and is absorbed; soil temperatures rise
- Roots grow and die to make humus
- Weak carbonic acid from air, rain, and roots dissolves nutrients

### Wallace's Method
- Circular coulter with the ground (not too dry or wet)
- Slit followed by a wedge which opens the ground to form an air pocket
- No fertilizer or top-dressing needed — only beneficial effect of trapped air
- Temperature rises of up to 11 degrees C recorded under reconditioned forest
- No more than 100mm depth in first treatment, 150-225mm in subsequent treatments

### Herring-Bone Pattern
"Drive your tractor or team slightly downhill, making herring-bones of the land: the spines are the valleys and the ribs slope out and down-slope." Soil channels become the easiest way for water to move, spreading out from valleys below the soil surface.

## Results of Soil Rehabilitation
- Living soil: earthworms add alkaline manure and act as living plungers
- Friable and open soil through which water penetrates easily
- Aerated soil that stays warmer in winter and cooler in summer
- Absorbent soil as a water-retaining blanket, preventing runoff
- Dead roots as plant and animal food, creating air spaces and nitrogen
- Easy root penetration for new plantings
- Permanent change if not again trodden, rolled, pounded, or chemicalized

## No-Till Grain Cropping

### Fukuoka's Influence
"Until I read Fukuoka, there was no satisfactory basis for including grain and legume crops in permaculture." The system combines the usual rotation of legume/grain/root crop/pasture/fallow into a **single grain/legume mixed crop**.

### Key Principles
- Continuous mulch (with clover) plus double-cropping using winter and spring sown grains
- Small areas (400 m2 or less) can support a family of five on grain
- Grain/legume diet gives complete protein
- Simultaneous rotation: legume/grain/root/pasture/fallow in one planting

### Annual Cycle (Rice-Based System)
- **April**: Broadcast seed below mulch (rice, white clover, rye/barley/millet/wheat/oats). Thin chicken manure layer.
- **May**: Harvest last year's rice. Return all straw and husks to field. Resow rice within a month.
- **June-September**: Winter crop grows. Light grazing by sheep or geese. 100 ducks per ha reduce pests and add manure.
- **November**: Harvest rye/barley. Return all straw to fields, rotating straw types across plots.
- **December-March**: Rice grows. Summer weeds weakened by flooding.

### Results
Fukuoka maintained this no-dig cycle for 25 years with soil improving, no fertilizer other than chicken and duck manure, no sprays, no herbicides. Good yields: 3,000-4,000 kg/ha rice.

## Practical Considerations

Successful implementation of Mollison Soil Improvement and Rehabilitation requires attention to
several practical factors including environmental conditions,
resource availability, and timing. Careful monitoring and
adaptive management help optimize outcomes across varying
conditions. Integration with other system elements enhances
overall effectiveness and creates beneficial synergies that
improve resilience and productivity over time.
