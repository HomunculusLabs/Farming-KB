### Symptoms Across Cases

The most commonly reported symptoms in accidental ingestion cases include:

- Visual hallucinations (most consistent symptom)
- Pupillary dilation
- Laughter (in some cases)
- Confusion and disorientation
- Anxiety or panic (particularly when effects are unexpected)
- Nausea (less common with properly cooked mushrooms)
- Rapid heart rate
- Cold extremities

### The Role of Panic

In many accidental ingestion cases, the most harmful element was not the psilocybin itself but the panic response of the affected person and their family:

- The 1929 Japanese physician panicked and ran for help
- The 1799 London family was alarmed by the child's symptoms
- Modern emergency room visits for accidental ingestion are typically driven by anxiety rather than actual medical danger

This pattern underscores the importance of education: knowing that psilocybin mushrooms are not physically dangerous can prevent unnecessary emergency interventions.

## Comparison with Actual Poisonous Mushrooms

### Why Accidental Ingestion is Usually Benign

Accidental psilocybin ingestion is usually benign because:

1. **Psilocybin is not organ-toxic:** It does not damage the liver, kidneys, or other organs (unlike amatoxins)
2. **Psilocybin has a high [[bloomfield-destroying-angel-amanita-virosa-amatoxin-poisoning-clinical-course]]** (destroying angel) -- Similar to death cap
- **Gyromitra esculenta** ([[bloomfield-false-morel-gyromitrin-volatile-toxin-cooking-risk]]) -- Contains gyromitrin, can be fatal
- **Cortinarius species** -- Contain orellanine, cause kidney failure

The confusion between these genuinely deadly species and psilocybin-containing species has caused unnecessary fear of the latter.
