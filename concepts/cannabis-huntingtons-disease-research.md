## Cannabis and Huntington's Disease

**Source:** The [[mushroom-cancer-clinical-trials-overview]].

- **Endocannabinoid system review (2011, Madrid):** Activation of CB2 receptors leads to slower neurodegeneration in both Parkinson's and HD. Cannabinoids with antioxidant properties and CB2 activation capability are promising therapeutic agents.

- **CBD neuroprotection (2007, Madrid):** CBD provides neuroprotection against striatal damage through its antioxidant properties, relevant to HD's preferential loss of striatal projection neurons.

- **CBD placebo-controlled trial (1991, Arizona):** 15 neuroleptic-free HD patients received CBD 10 mg/kg/day for 6 weeks. CBD proved no more effective than placebo in this trial.

### Strain-Specific Considerations

CBD has greater affinity for CB2 receptors. THC binds relatively equally to CB1 and CB2. Sativex is a standardized extract containing THC and CBD in ratios similar to cannabis flowers. Indica and indica-dominant strains contain lower THC:CBD ratios, favoring CB2 activation.

## Dosing Considerations

Dosing for Huntington's disease should prioritize neuroprotection and symptom management while minimizing psychoactive effects that could complicate cognitive symptoms. CBD-dominant preparations are generally preferred, with doses of 10-20 mg/kg/day based on the clinical trials reviewed.

For motor symptom management (chorea, dystonia), THC in low doses (2.5-5 mg) may provide additional benefit through CB1 receptor activation. A ratio of approximately 1:5 THC:CBD may balance efficacy with tolerability. Sativex-style preparations (roughly 1:1 THC:CBD) can be considered for patients who tolerate THC well.

Oral administration (tinctures, capsules) is preferred for consistent dosing in HD patients. Edibles may be suitable for patients with dysphagia (swallowing difficulties), a common HD symptom. Vaporizing is generally not recommended due to motor coordination difficulties that may make inhalation devices challenging to use.

Dosing should begin low and increase gradually, with particular attention to orthostatic hypotension (noted as an adverse effect in the 1986 study). Patients on neuroleptic medications should have doses adjusted carefully due to potential additive sedative effects.

## Safety and Interactions

HD patients often take multiple medications for motor symptoms, psychiatric manifestations, and cognitive decline. CBD may interact with neuroleptics (haloperidol, tetrabenazine, risperidone) through CYP450 enzyme inhibition, potentially increasing blood levels and side effects. Drug levels should be monitored when initiating CBD alongside existing HD medications.

THC may exacerbate psychiatric symptoms in HD patients, particularly depression, anxiety, and psychosis-like symptoms. Patients with significant psychiatric comorbidity should use THC cautiously or avoid it entirely. CBD has demonstrated antipsychotic and anxiolytic properties that may be more appropriate for this population.

The progressive nature of HD means that dosing needs may change as the disease advances. Early-stage patients may benefit from neuroprotective strategies, while later-stage patients may prioritize symptom management (chorea reduction, sleep improvement, appetite stimulation). Caregiver involvement in dosing decisions is essential as cognitive decline progresses.

## Cannabis for Huntington's Disease

Huntington's disease (HD), or Huntington's chorea (abnormal twisting and writhing movements), is a degenerative genetic disease affecting the brain and nervous system, leading to loss of muscle control and dementia. A mutated form of the Huntington gene, found significantly more in Western Europeans, is thought responsible for disease development.

## Genetics and Inheritance

The Huntington gene can be passed to offspring. One parent with the mutation on one DNA strand pair is associated with a 50 percent chance that any offspring will develop the disease. If both parents have a single pair mutation, the risk increases to 75 percent. If both parents have dual pair mutation, development of Huntington's is a virtual certainty.

Parents who may carry this gene should consider genetic testing. Researchers continue to investigate fundamental questions about HD: How does the disease start? What causes or maintains the mutation? What activates the gene? Why does HD develop at a certain time after years of apparent dormancy?

## Disease Progression

Some scientists believe the mutated gene codes for a protein that causes protein aggregation. These aggregated proteins clump together, collect, and eventually corrupt certain portions of the brain, causing characteristic symptoms.

Symptoms usually begin with lack of coordination and ataxia (unsteady gait), soon progressing to chorea, dystonia (muscle contraction and twisting), dementia, behavioral difficulties, and eventually death. Leading causes of death include pneumonia, heart disease, nutritional deficiencies, and accidents. No treatment exists within orthodox medicine for HD.

## Evidence Base

The Cannabis Health Index (CHI) rates the evidence for cannabis in Huntington's disease at a CHI value of 10, based on 5 studies reviewed. The evidence focuses primarily on CBD's neuroprotective properties.
