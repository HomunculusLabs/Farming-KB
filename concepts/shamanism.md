# Shamanism

Shamanism is an ancient spiritual and healing practice found in indigenous
cultures worldwide. Characterized by the practitioner's ability to enter
altered [[metzner-spirit-beings-multiple-realities-entheogens]],
  animal guides, ancestors, and deities to gain knowledge, power, or
  assistance in healing.
- **Healing function**: A central role of the shaman is diagnosing and treating
  illness, which in shamanic cosmology is often attributed to soul loss,
  spiritual intrusion, or imbalance.
- **Community role**: Unlike mystics who seek personal enlightenment, shamans
  serve their community, acting as intermediaries between the human and spirit
  worlds for collective benefit.
- **Mastery of spirits**: The shaman does not merely communicate with spirits
  but exercises control or influence over them, distinguishing shamanic
  practice from passive mediumship or spirit possession.

## Cross-Cultural Distribution

Shamanic practices have been documented across every inhabited continent, from

universal elements exist across all shamanic traditions regardless of cultural

- **Lower World**: An underworld associated with ancestral spirits, animal
  guides, and deep psychological material. Accessed by journeying downward
  through an opening in the earth such as a cave, roots, or a hole in the
  ground.

The World Tree or cosmic axis connects these realms, and the shaman travels
along this axis during trance states. This cosmological structure parallels
Jungian ideas about the layers of the psyche, with the upper world
corresponding to the superego or collective unconscious, and the lower world
to the personal unconscious and shadow.
