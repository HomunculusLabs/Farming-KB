## Structure

### Basic Construction
- Wood or concrete block with cement floor
- Drainage provisions (water collects on floor during cropping cycle)
- Wood floors covered with heavy gauge plastic
- Interior walls, ceilings, and exposed wood treated with marine enamel or epoxy-plastic paint
- White color enhances lighting and exposes contaminating molds

### Insulation (Critical)
- Walls: R=11 or R=19 insulation
- Ceiling: R=30 insulation
- Fiberglass or styrofoam work well but must be protected from humidity
- 2-4 mil plastic vapor barrier between insulation and interior wall
- The most important feature is the ability to maintain constant temperature

### Airtightness
- Essential feature: prevents insects and spores from entering
- Gives cultivator full control over fresh air supply
- All cracks, seams, and joints must be carefully sealed during construction

### Room Options
- Dedicated mushroom house
- Modified basement, porch, garage, or shed
- Plastic tent/envelope room framed with 2-inch PVC pipe inside a larger room
- Insulated plastic greenhouse (galvanized metal pipe frame, heavy plastic inner/outer skin, fiberglass insulation between)
- Mini-culture chambers: styrofoam ice chests, aquariums, plastic-lined cardboard boxes

### Mini-Culture
For limited space: small environmental chambers need minimal air exchange. Enough air exchanged during daily misting. Clear perforated plastic covering maintains humidity. Heat from outer room or heating coils/light bulb on rheostat mounted at base.

## Growing Systems

### Shelf System
- Most common indoor method
- Framework of upright posts with cross bars at each level
- Constructed of wood or non-corrosive tubular metal
- Preservative-treated softwood shelves, 6 inches wide with 1 inch spaces between boards
- Side boards 6-8 inches high depending on fill depth
- Shelf boards placed unattached for easy filling, emptying, and cleaning
- Center pole design (single central support) suited for growing in plastic bags
- Metal storage shelves: impervious to disease, appropriate for sterilized substrates in small containers

### Tray System
- Developed by Dr. James Sinden
- Individual mobile cropping units (vs. anchored shelves)
- Enables mechanization: filling, spawning, casing done on separate tray lines
- Uses separate room for [[mushroom-contamination-control-strategies]] for maintaining a clean growing environment
