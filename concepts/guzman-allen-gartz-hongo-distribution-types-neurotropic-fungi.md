# Hongo Distribution Types Applied to Neurotropic Fungi

## Background

Akira Hongo (1978) proposed nine types of geographical distribution for Japanese fungi, a biogeographic framework that Guzmán, Allen, and Gartz (1998) applied to [[neurotropic-fungi]] worldwide. This classification system provides a structured way to understand the [[health-risks-of-hallucinogenic-mushrooms]] and related species.

## The Nine Distribution Types

### Type 1: Cosmopolitan Species

Cosmopolitan species are found across multiple continents in diverse habitats. In the neurotropic fungi, this category includes:

- *Panaeolus spp.* — Most species in this genus have wide distribution, particularly *P. subbalteatus* and *P. cyanescens* (note: not the same as *Psilocybe cyanescens*).
- *[[polypore-fungi-eastern-north-america]] and eastern Asia, separated by the intervening dry grasslands and deserts of central North America and central Asia:

- *Psilocybe graveolens*, *P. muliercula*, *P. pintonii*, and *P. zapotecorum* from the Americas are very closely related to *P. argentipes* and *P. subcaerulipes* from Japan. This pattern parallels well-known plant disjunctions (e.g., ginseng, tulip trees) between eastern North America and eastern Asia.

### Type 5: Far Eastern Species

Species endemic to Japan and the Korean peninsula, from the perspective of Hongo's original Japanese-centered framework:

- Several species of *Panaeolina* described by Hongo from Japan.
- *Psilocybe subcaerulipes* — endemic to Japan's temperate forests.

### Type 6: Southeastern Asiatic Species

Species from the tropical and subtropical forests of Southeast Asia:

- *Psilocybe subaeruginascens* var. *subaeruginascens* — Known from Japan and Java, Indonesia.
- *Psilocybe subaeruginascens* var. *septentrionalis* — Restricted to Japan only.
- *Psilocybe samuiensis* — From Koh Samui, Thailand.

### Type 7: Tropical and Subtropical Species

Species restricted to warm climates between approximately 23.5°N and 23.5°S, though some extend into warm temperate zones:

- *Psilocybe cubensis* — The quintessential pantropical neurotropic fungus, found in cow pastures throughout the tropics.
- *Psilocybe subcubensis* — Closely related to *P. cubensis*, also tropical.
- *Copelandia cyanescens* — With some exceptions, primarily tropical and subtropical.
- *Copelandia tropicalis* — As the name suggests, tropical distribution.
- *Gerronema fibula* — Reported from Malaysia, New Guinea, Solomon Islands, and South America, also known from Europe, representing a complex distributional history.

### Type 8: Arctic and Alpine Species

Species of extreme cold environments:

- *Psilocybe aztecorum* var. *aztecorum* — Restricted to subalpine and alpine habitats in the high mountains of central Mexico, growing at elevations up to 4,000 m. This species represents a remarkable example of neurotropic fungi adapting to extreme conditions.
- *Panaeolus moellerianus* and *P. olivaceus* from the Faeroe Islands — Examples of northern cold-adapted species.

### Type 9: Endemic Species

Species known from only a single country, region, or very restricted geographic area:

**Mexico endemics:**
- *Conocybe siliginoides* — Collected only once in 1955 by Wasson in Oaxaca; never re-found despite extensive searching.
- *Hypholoma naematoliformis*
- *Psilocybe muliercula*, *P. chiapanensis*, *P. laurae*, and many others.

**Colombia endemics:**
- *P. columbiana*, *P. guatapensis*, *P. pintonii*.

**Brazil endemics:**
- *P. brasiliensis*, *P. paulensis*.

**Europe endemics:**
- *Psilocybe hispanica* — Spain.
- *Psilocybe serbica* — Central Europe.

**Other regional endemics:**
- *P. portoricensis* — Puerto Rico.
- *Panaeolina* species described by Hongo from Japan and by Natarajan and Raman from India.

## Amphitropical and Bipolar Distributions

Beyond Hongo's nine types, several neurotropic fungi show amphitropical distributions (found in both Northern and Southern temperate zones but not in the tropics between):

- *Psilocybe cyanescens*, *P. fimetaria*, *P. pelliculosa*, *P. semilanceata*, and *P. silvatica* are common in both North America and Europe.
- *Psilocybe stuntzii* is only known in the Pacific Northwest of North America.
- *Psilocybe coprinifacies* and *P. serbica* are only European.
