## Seed Planting Fundamentals

A seed needs an entire microcosm: air, warmth, moisture, soil, nutrients, and microorganisms. The elements fall into two categories:

- **Terrestrial:** Soil and nutrients
- **Celestial:** Air, warmth, moisture

These overlap — plants can take in air through their roots as well as through their leaves, and nutrients can be borne on air currents.

### Planting Depth

Seeds should be planted as deep as the thin vertical dimension of each side. Lima and fava beans may be planted on their sides (root systems emerge from an eye and grow straight down). Seeds should be covered with humus-containing flat soil to stimulate germination.

### Spacing

Seeds in beds or flats should be planted in a **diagonally offset (hexagonal) spacing pattern** with equal distance between each seed. ## Pricking Out (Transplanting Between Flats)

The GROW BIOINTENSIVE method embodies the "Breakfast-Lunch-Dinner" concept:

- **Breakfast:** First flat with good planting mixture
- **Lunch:** Second flat with fresh flat soil (pricking out minimizes shock and fosters growth)
- **Dinner:** The splendid GROW BIOINTENSIVE growing bed

### Pricking Out Process

1. Fill a 3-inch or 6-inch-deep flat with flat soil, mounding slightly.
2. Use a widger or kitchen knife to lift one seedling at a time by its cotyledons, keeping soil on roots.
3. Make a hole in the second flat with the widger at a slight backward angle.
4. Drop the seedling by its roots, placing slightly deeper than in the first flat.
5. Let soil fall around the seedling; watering will settle it.
6. Arrange seedlings on **offset (hexagonal) centers** to maximize space.

Seedlings are ready to prick out after cotyledons appear and before roots are too long to handle.
