# Permaculture Plant Index F-I

This index covers plants from the letter F through I as catalogued in *Permaculture Plants: A Selection*. Entries are organized by common or scientific name with their primary uses and page references.

## F (continued)

### Fungi and Plant Health
- **Fusarium wilt** — page 68. Soil-borne fungal disease affecting bananas, tomatoes, and other crops; managed through resistant varieties and biological controls.

### Furniture and Fine Wood
- **Furniture** — pages 17, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24, 25, 26, 27, 28, 59, 65, 66, 67, 75, 81, 82, 83, 85, 86, 88, 89, 92, 95, 97, 98, 100, 101, 102, 104, 106, 130. Premium furniture timbers include walnut (32-33), oak (80-82), mahogany, jarrah (93), and various eucalypts (92-96).

## G

### Grasses and Grazing
- **Grass** — page 98. Perennial grasses for pasture, erosion control, and thatch include pampas grass (*[[cortaderia-selloana]]*), elephant grass (*Pennisetum purpureum*), and giant reed (*Arundo donax*).
- **Grasses** — page 27. Native and introduced grasses form the basis of grazing systems in permaculture design.
- **Grasshoppers** — page 88. Pest insect managed through habitat manipulation, poultry integration, and biological controls.
- **Great water grass** (*Glyceria maxima*) — page 123. Tall aquatic grass for riparian stabilization and biomass production.
- **Grazing systems** — page 101. Silvopastoral systems integrate trees with livestock grazing for mutual benefit.

### Grevilleas (*Grevillea* spp.)
- **Grevillea robusta** (Silk oak) — pages 112, 115. Fast-growing ornamental and shade tree; excellent nectar source for birds and bees.
- **Grevillea spp.** — page 117. Australian genus with over 350 species; diverse forms from ground covers to tall trees. Most produce nectar-rich flowers attractive to wildlife.
- **Grevillea ilicifolia** (Holly grevillea) — page 135. Low-growing species with holly-like leaves; ornamental ground cover.
- **Grevillea leucopteris** (Plume grevillea) — page 135. Large flowered grevillea with distinctive white plumes.
- **Grevillea pauciflora** — page 135. Small-flowered species; ornamental.

### Ground Covers
- **Ground covers** — page 130. Low-growing plants that suppress weeds, retain moisture, and protect soil. The text references ground covers extensively for slope stabilization and mulch production.

### Guava and Tropical Fruits
- **Guava** (*Psidium guajava*) — pages 72, 118. Tropical fruit tree producing vitamin C-rich fruit; used fresh, in juices, and preserves.
- **Guava, strawberry** (*[[psidium-cattleianum]]*) — pages 72, 118. Smaller-fruited relative with sweeter, strawberry-flavored fruit.
- **Guava, pineapple** (*Acca sellowiana*) — pages 73, 118. See feijoa.
- **Grumichama** (*Eugenia brasiliensis*) — page 74. Brazilian cherry relative producing dark purple, sweet fruit.

### Gums and Resins
- **Gum** — pages 21, 41. Various trees produce gums used in food, industry, and medicine.
- **Gum arabic** — from *Acacia senegal*. Important food additive and industrial thickener.
- **Gum myrtle** — page 111. Australian species producing aromatic resin.

### Garlic and Companion Plants
- **Garlic** (*Allium sativum*) — pages 114, 118, 119. Essential culinary allium; companion plant repelling pests [[maintaining-organic-matter-in-vegetable-gardens]]. Also has medicinal properties.
- **Golden rain wattle** (*Acacia podalyriifolia*) — page 21, 111. Ornamental Australian wattle with golden flower clusters; nitrogen fixing.
- **Golden bamboo** (*Phyllostachys aurea*) — page 97. Running bamboo for poles, erosion control, and ornamental use.
- **Golden chinquapin** (*Chrysolepis chrysophylla*) — page 34. Western American evergreen chestnut relative producing edible nuts.

### Gourds and Groundnuts
- **Ginkgo** (*Ginkgo biloba*) — page 112. Ancient deciduous tree; edible nuts (female trees) and medicinal leaves. Extremely hardy and pollution tolerant.
- **Globe artichoke** (*Cynara scolymus*) — page 115. Perennial thistle-like plant producing edible flower buds; excellent ornamental and food plant.
- **Gladioli** — page 119. Ornamental corms for cut flowers; corms also edible in some species.

### H

### Hakeas (*Hakea* spp.)
- **Hakea spp.** — page 117. Australian genus closely related to Grevillea; diverse woody plants producing distinctive woody seed capsules.
- **Hakea eriantha** — page 117. Large-flowered species with woolly buds.
- **Hakea francisiana** — page 117. Western Australian species with red flower clusters.
- **Hakea salicifolia** — pages 112, 115. Willow-leaved hakea; ornamental hedging species.
- **Hakea suaveolens** — pages 112, 135. Sweet-scented hakea with fragrant flowers.

### Haws and Hawthorns
- **Hawthorn** (*Crataegus* spp.) — pages 112, 114, 115. Hedgerow shrubs/small trees producing edible red haws for jelly and wine. Thorny branches make effective livestock barriers.
- **Hackberry** (*Celtis* spp.) — pages 77, 111. Tough hardwood trees producing small sweet fruits; excellent for difficult sites.
- **Hazel** (*Corylus* spp.) — pages 37, 114. Nut-producing shrubs; important understory component in food forests and agroforestry systems.

### Hedgerow and Hedge Plants
- **Hedge** — pages 62, 63, 73, 74, 77, 83, 90, 96, 97, 133, 134, 135. Hedgerows serve multiple functions: windbreak, livestock barrier, wildlife corridor, and microclimate creation. Common hedge species include hawthorn (112), osage orange (28), leucaena (24), and bamboo (97).
- **Hedgerow** — page 115. Traditional European farming practice integrating trees and shrubs into agricultural landscapes.

### Herbs and Healers
- **Hazel** — page 114. See hazelnut.
- **Heather** (*Calluna vulgaris*) — page 131. Low evergreen shrub of acid soils; traditional thatch material and bee forage.
- **Helianthemum spp.** (Rock rose) — pages 114, 136. Low-growing flowering plants for rock gardens and ground cover.
- **Henon bamboo** (*Phyllostachys nigra* 'Henon') — page 97. Large timber bamboo for construction and crafts.

### Honey and Nectar Sources
- **Honey** — pages 22, 27, 28, 43, 75, 77, 91, 92, 93, 94, 95, 100, 114, 129, 132, 134, 135, 136. Key honey and nectar sources include eucalypts (92-96), wattles (22), melaleucas (129), clovers, and various ornamental species.
- **Honey locust** (*[[gleditsia-triacanthos]]*) — pages 28, 135. Nitrogen-fixing legume tree producing sweet pods for livestock fodder; excellent shade and fence tree.

### Horses and Hay
- **Horses** — pages 16, 26, 106. Livestock fodder species for equines include lucerne (15), various grasses, and tree fodder.
- **Hay** — page 21, 98. Dried grass and legume forage for winter feeding; produced from permanent pastures and dedicated hay crops.
- **Horseradish** (*[[armoracia-rusticana]]*) — pages 90, 119. Pungent root crop; companion plant for pest deterrence in orchards.
- **Horseradish tree** (*Moringa oleifera*) — page 90. Multi-purpose tropical tree: leaves, pods, and seeds all edible; drought tolerant; fast growing. Medicinal and nutritional powerhouse.

### Hollies and Evergreens
- **Holly** (*Ilex aquifolium*) — pages 112, 114. Evergreen shrub with spiny leaves and red berries; traditional ornamental and hedge plant.
- **Holly oak** (*Quercus ilex*) — pages 80, 136. Evergreen oak of the Mediterranean; fire resistant; excellent for agroforestry and livestock shelter.

## I

### Ice Cream Bean and Inga Species
- **Ice cream bean** (*Inga edulis*) — pages 28, 136. Nitrogen-fixing legume tree producing sweet, cotton-candy-flavored pulp surrounding large seeds in long pods. Popular in Amazonian agroforestry.
- **Inga spp.** — page 28. Genus of tropical legume trees widely used in shade coffee and cacao systems for [[legume-nitrogen-fixation-and-green-manure]] shade.

### Iriartea Palms
- **Iriartea setigera** — page 103. South American palm used for construction timber and thatch.
- **Iriartea ventricosa** — page 103. Amazonian palm producing straight, durable stems for house posts and spears.

### Irish Strawberry Tree
- **Irish strawberry tree** (*Arbutus unedo*) — pages 77, 134. Evergreen Mediterranean shrub producing strawberry-like orange-red fruits; ornamental and edible.

### Ivy and Climbers
- **Ivy** (*Hedera helix*) — pages 114, 117. Evergreen climbing plant for walls and ground cover; wildlife habitat. Note: can become invasive in some regions.

### Italian Species
- **Italian alder** (*Alnus cordata*) — pages 26, 111. Fast-growing nitrogen-fixing tree for timber, shelter, and soil improvement.
- **Italian maple** — page 111. Ornamental maple species.
- **Italian stone pine** (*Pinus pinea*) — page 44. Mediterranean pine producing large edible pine nuts; iconic landscape tree.
