# Cannabis and Chronic Non-Malignant Pain

## Overview

Chronic non-malignant pains (CNMPs) are defined as non-cancerous pains lasting
longer than three months yet significantly impairing quality of life. A study
conducted in Germany surveying available data from 1995 to 2009 discovered that
about 17% of all pain falls into this category.

The most common forms of CNMP that are non-responsive to pharmaceutical and
non-drug treatment include musculoskeletal pains of the neck, shoulder, and
back. These conditions represent a significant portion of the chronic pain
burden worldwide.

According to the Cannabis Health Index, the evidence for cannabis in treating
chronic non-malignant pain is based on 2 studies reviewed with a total CHI
value of 5, yielding a CHI score of 2.50.

## Cannabis and Chronic Non-Malignant Pain: The Evidence

### Animal Study on CB1/CB2 Agonists (2008, Warsaw, Poland)

Researchers from the Medical University of Warsaw discovered that cannabinoid
agonists activate both endocannabinoid receptors CB1 and CB2. Cannabinoids
reduced sensitivity to pain in a dose-dependent fashion. Furthermore, it was
discovered that COX-1 inhibitors such as indomethacin (a non-steroidal
anti-inflammatory drug commonly used to reduce fever, pain, and stiffness)
might increase these cannabinoid properties at low dosages.

This finding is significant because it suggests potential synergistic
interactions between cannabinoids and commonly used NSAIDs, which could allow
for lower doses of both medication classes and consequently fewer side effects.

### Human Case Study on Dronabinol (2008, Jerusalem, Israel)

A case study from the Department of Anesthesiology at Hadassah-Hebrew
University Medical Center examined Dronabinol (the synthetic version of THC)
in the context of CNMP. Researchers observed: "Cannabinoids have been used
for pain relief for centuries, and recent studies have investigated their
analgesic and anti-inflammatory mechanisms, as well as clinical efficacy, in
treating chronic pain."

Out of 13 participating patients with CNMP unresponsive to conventional
pharmacotherapy, five reported an adequate response while eight patients
reported an inadequate or no response to Dronabinol. The authors concluded
that "oral THC may be a valuable therapeutic option for selected patients with
CNMP that are unresponsive to previous treatments, though further research is
warranted to characterize those patients."

## Strain-Specific Considerations

Dronabinol is a synthetic prescription medication that is an isomer (same
molecular formula but different structural formula) of delta-9-
tetrahydrocannabinol (THC). THC binds relatively equally with both CB1 and
CB2 receptors.

Sativa and sativa-dominant strains usually contain higher THC:CBD ratios.
However, for chronic pain conditions that involve inflammation, indica or
indica-dominant strains with a lower THC:CBD ratio may provide additional
benefits through enhanced CB2 activation.

The dose-dependent nature of cannabinoid analgesia is important to note:
too little may be sub-optimal while too much can actually increase pain
sensitivity. Starting with low doses and titrating upward slowly is the
recommended approach.

## The Endocannabinoid System and Pain

CB1 receptors are located in the central nervous system (brain and spinal
cord) and regulate motor control, attention, emotion, and memory. Notably,
CB1 receptors are absent in the medulla oblongata (which regulates heart and
respiratory function), making cannabinoid pain management safer than opiates,
which can cause fatal respiratory depression.

CB2 receptors cluster in the periphery, especially in immune tissue. CB2
engagement is important for chronic pain associated with autoimmune diseases.
Cannabinoids reduce inflammation and can produce synergy when combined with
reduced amounts of opiates, lowering the risk of adverse effects and habit-
forming potential.

## Mind-Body Medicine and Chronic Pain

Pain gets our attention. It can save lives and prevent damage. Beyond the
positive attributes of pain as feedback, man-made beliefs in punishment demand
pain, as does guilt with its constant insistence on using pain as a means of
making amends.

Pain begins as an emotional reality. If addressed and healed in the non-
physical realm, its existence will dissolve. If ignored, denied, masked, or
avoided, the emotion will seek healing through more forceful means. For
instance, avoided pain born of betrayal may develop into an autoimmune disease
where the body's own defense mechanism turns on itself.

### Aggravating Factors

- Guilt and beliefs in punishment
- Avoiding pain through numbing medications, judgments, or depression
- Running from pain through addiction or obsession
- Avoiding pain through focusing on blame and betrayal

### Suggested Approaches

- Release guilt, grief, pain, and hurt in the emotional realm
- Employ forgiveness
- Understand that pain demands change and points to where attention is needed
