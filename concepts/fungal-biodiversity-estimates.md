# Fungal Biodiversity Estimates

Fungal biodiversity remains one of the most poorly quantified frontiers in
biology. Unlike plants and vertebrates, fungi are microscopic for much of their
life cycle, making traditional [[environmental-dna-sequencing-fungi]], particularly metabarcoding of environmental
samples, has generated data-driven estimates that consistently exceed
morphology-based surveys. Molecular surveys reveal vast numbers of operational
taxonomic units that do not correspond to any described species. Studies of
individual forest plots have recovered thousands of fungal taxa from a handful
of soil cores.

Extrapolation from metabarcoding data is complicated by several factors. The
relationship between sequence variants and biological species is not one-to-one.
Sampling effort varies enormously between studies. Many sequences represent
dormant or dead propagules rather than active community members. Despite these
caveats, molecular data consistently support higher diversity estimates than
morphology-based surveys alone.
