# Oyster Mushroom Clean Room Design for Spawn Production

A clean room provides a hygienically sterile enclosed airspace where mushroom mycelium can be isolated from competing organisms. This page describes a simple, low-cost clean room design suitable for teaching, learning, and experimenting with [[mushroom-cultivation-fundamentals]] and [[mushroom-cultivation-grain-spawn]] production.

## Purpose and Scope

This design is intended as a place to teach, learn, and experiment with mushroom sterile culture. It is **not capable of withstanding the demands of commercial mushroom spawn production**, but is excellent for small-scale growers producing their own spawn.

The clean room can be used to:
- Inoculate agar plates from culture slants or tissue samples
- Conduct agar-to-agar, agar-to-grain, and grain-to-grain transfers
- Produce sawdust-to-grain and sawdust-to-sawdust transfers
- Work with standard domestic pressure cookers for sterilization

## Key Equipment

| Equipment | Specification |
|---|---|
| Laminar flow bench (HEPA filter/fan) | Fan output: 400–500 CFM; HEPA filter depth ≤ 6" (15 cm) |
| Polythene sheeting | Clear, 120 gauge (8 mil, 0.5 mm), 50m × 2.5m roll |
| Saw timber | 2× 1" (50 × 25mm), treated |
| Fasteners | 2.5" (60mm) counter-sunk wood screws, tacks, or staples |
| Sealant | Silicone caulking |
| Disinfectant | 5% household bleach (sodium hydroxide) solution |
| UV lamp (optional) | For additional surface sterilization |

## Room Dimensions and Airflow

The recommended clean room size is **2m long × 2m wide × 2m high** (cubic air capacity: ~141 cubic feet). This size is directly correlated to the cubic airflow capacity of the laminar flow bench.

For effective contamination control, all air in the room must pass through the HEPA filter **once every 60–80 seconds**. The fan produces 400–500 CFM input, delivering 125–150 CFM off the face of the filter.

## Construction

### Frame Assembly

1. Build two **side frames**: 2m long × 2m high, with a bracing bar at 1m height
2. Build two **end frames**: 2m wide × 1m tall, one containing a door opening
3. Line all frames with polythene sheeting (cut 2.5× the frame length, folded back and tacked)
4. Run a 10mm bead of silicone sealant 10mm from the edge of each frame
5. Join frames at right angles with screws; apply additional silicone along all joins
6. Cover the top with a 2.5m × 1.5m polythene sheet with a bracing bar

### Door and Sealing

The door is constructed along the same design as the frames. Household window insulation foam makes a good seal around the doorway. The goal is an airtight enclosure.

### Floor

Best sited on a concrete base. Alternatives include linoleum or butyl rubber sheet.

## Room Preparation and Disinfection

Before use:
1. Disinfect all surfaces with **10% lime solution** and **3–5% blue vitriol** (copper sulfate)
2. Clean work surfaces with **5% household bleach solution**
3. Perform cleaning **one hour before** intended use
4. Workers must **step in lime solution** before entering the room

## Worker Hygiene Protocol

- Shower before entering the clean room
- Wear **newly laundered clothes** (ideally not exposed to outside air since washing)
- Tie back long hair or use a hair net
- Clean hands with isopropyl alcohol at regular intervals
- Do not breathe over open culture dishes
- Minimize time spent in the clean room to reduce contamination risk

## Operation

- The laminar flow bench should be left in **continual operation** to keep the room dry and free from mould growth
- All work is performed in front of the laminar flow filter face
- HEPA filter should be replaced after **12 months** of operation
- Any holes in polythene must be immediately sealed with adhesive tape
- Silicone sealant should be checked monthly and replaced as needed

## Grain Spawn Production Workflow

### 1. Substrate Preparation
- Use rye or sorghum grain; optimum moisture content ~50%
- Pre-cook grain for ~30 minutes (firm but squashable)
- Add gypsum (CaSO₄) at ~4g per 1kg dry grain to prevent clumping
- Fill jars to ¾ capacity or bags to ⅔ capacity

### 2. Sterilization
- Sterilize at **15 psi (250°F/121°C)** for **1–1.5 hours** (105 min for 1kg bags)
- Smaller quantities (400g in 1L jars) need ~1 hour
- Let pressure return to zero before opening; cool for at least 2 hours

### 3. Inoculation
- Perform all inoculation under the laminar flow bench
- Each colonized petri dish can inoculate up to 20 containers
- For fastest colonization, use ½ to 1 whole dish per 1kg grain jar

### 4. Incubation
- Incubate at **24–25°C** in **complete darkness**
- First growth visible within 2–3 days
- **Shake containers after 7 days** to distribute colonized grain kernels
- Full colonization typically in **15–20 days**
- Use spawn immediately when fully colonized (white/off-white)

## Maintenance Schedule

| Task | Frequency |
|---|---|
| Surface cleaning with bleach | Before each use |
| Check polythene for holes | Weekly |
| Inspect silicone sealant | Monthly |
| Replace HEPA filter | Every 12 months |
