# Names of the Gods and Their Fertility Origins

John Allegro's central philological discovery is that the principal god-names of the [[allegro-fertility-philosophy-of-the-ancient-near-east]] East — across Greek, Semitic, and Indo-European traditions — derive from a common Sumerian vocabulary of fertility and sexuality. This chapter-by-chapter analysis of divine nomenclature reveals that behind the apparent diversity of ancient religion lay a unified conception of the deity as a life-giving, fecundating power.

## Zeus, Yahweh, and the Single Deity

The principal gods of the Greeks and Hebrews, Zeus and Yahweh (Jehovah), have names derived from Sumerian meaning "juice of fecundity," spermatozoa, "seed of life." The phrase is composed of IA (ya, dialectally za), "juice" (literally "strong water"), and U, the most important phoneme in all Near Eastern religion, at whose root lies the idea of "fertility."

This finding undermines the old evolutionary theory that religion progressed from primitive polytheism to monotheism. Moses is portrayed as a monotheist; the Church divided its Godhead into three. The Bible cannot illustrate "primitive" religion. Behind Judaism and Christianity, and indeed all the Near Eastern fertility religions, lies this single phoneme "U."

## Phallic Divine Titles

Many Sumerian, Greek, and Semitic gods carry phallic designations. The Sumerian word for "shepherd" is SIPA, literally "stretched horn" or "penis." This appears in the biblical phrase Yahweh Sabaoth, from SIPA-UD, "penis of the storm." The Sumerian storm-god Iskur has a name meaning "mighty penis." Among the Semites he was known as Adad, "Mighty Father."

The name Joseph means "Yahweh's penis," a shortened form of Yahweh Sabaoth. The Phrygian deity Sabazios is composed of the same SIPA plus ZI, "erect." Hercules, the great "club-bearer," was named after the grossness of his sex organ, as was the Hebrew tribal ancestor Issachar.

## Baal: The Phallus God

Perhaps the best known of the Canaanite fertility gods, Baal, derives his name from a Sumerian verb AL, "bore," which combined with a preformative BA gave words for "drill" and "penis" — and gave Latin and English the word "phallus." In Semitic, ba'al is not only the divine name but has the general meaning of "lord, husband." Hosea makes a play on this when Yahweh says to Israel: "in that day you will call me 'my man' and you will no more call me 'my baal'" (Hos 2:16).

## The Sun as Glans Penis

More than any other heavenly body, the sun commanded respect as the embodiment of god — the Creator, the fecundator of the earth. The ancients saw the glowing orb as the tip of the divine penis, rising to white heat at zenith, then turning deep red (characteristic of the fully distended glans penis) as it plunged into the earthly vagina. In the cultic centres this ritual was enacted by the priest entering the god's house (temple).

## Kings as Divinely Endowed

Kings and priests were "pourers of bounty," lawgivers and teachers, in their capacity as the god's earthly representatives. They were reckoned especially endowed with divine "grace," the word for which in both Hebrew and Greek refers to the flowing of seed. They were "shepherds" of their people. When this important faculty deserted a king, he could be deposed. King David, whose name means "lover," when his manly prowess seemed to fail, sought stimulation from a young virgin, Abishag.

## The Origin of "Christ"

The word "Christ" (Greek Christos, Hebrew Mashiah) means "anointed one," referring to the smearing of the body with sacred substances representing the god's semen. The oil of anointing contained aromatic gums and spices — myrrh, aromatic cane, cinnamon, and cassia — all representing the powerful semen of the god. The "Christian" was literally one smeared with the divine seed for access to heavenly knowledge.

## Angels as Drug Plants

Such very special kinds of vegetation were "angels" — the word in Greek and Hebrew means "workers of miracles" or "messengers." They had access to both the heavenly and subterranean founts of knowledge. To know their names was to have power over them. A large part of magical lore was devoted to maintaining this vital knowledge of the names of the angels.

## The Sacred Seed and Sin

The processes and balance of nature demanded the effective use of the god's seed, since without it there could be no life or regeneration. The words for "curse" and "sin" have their roots in the idea of "seed running to waste." The seed of God was supremely holy, whether appearing as rain, sap, resin, or seminal emission. Kings wore purple garments because the colour derived from the association of purple with menstrual blood — another form of the creative substance.
