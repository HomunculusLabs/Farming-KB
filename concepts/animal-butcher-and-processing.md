# Animal Butcher and Processing
On-farm slaughter and processing is a fundamental [[homesteading]] skill that
provides complete control over meat quality, animal welfare at harvest, and
food safety. Processing your own animals eliminates processing fees and
allows full utilization of every part of the animal. This guide covers the
general process applicable to most homestead livestock (poultry, rabbits,
sheep, goats, pigs, and cattle).

## Legal Considerations

- On-farm slaughter for personal consumption is legal in all 50 US states.
- You cannot sell meat from on-farm slaughter without inspection (USDA or
  state-inspected facility required for commercial sales).
- Some states allow "custom exempt" processing (butcher for the owner, not
  for resale).
- Check local zoning and regulations before starting.
- Humane dispatch is both an ethical and legal obligation.

## Pre-Harvest Preparation

### Planning
- Choose harvest date based on species, age, and weight targets.
- Arrange help — processing is much easier and safer with 2-3 people.
- Gather all tools and supplies in advance (see equipment list).
- Check weather — cool weather (below 50°F) is ideal for processing.
  Avoid hot days.

### Equipment List
- Killing equipment: killing cone (poultry), captive bolt or firearm
  (large livestock), sharp knife for exsanguination.
- Scalding equipment: large pot, propane burner, thermometer (poultry,
  pigs).
- Plucking equipment: plucking fingers or hand plucking (poultry).
- Skinning equipment: skinning knife, gambrel and pulley system, spreader.
- Evisceration: gut hook knife, saw (for large animals), sealing rings.
- Cooling: large coolers with ice, or refrigerator.
- Cutting: boning knife, breaking knife, cleaver, cutting board, meat saw.
- Packaging: vacuum sealer, freezer paper, tape, marker, scale.
- Sanitation: multiple cutting boards, bleach solution (1 tbsp/gallon),
  hot water, disposable gloves.

### Animal Preparation
- Remove feed 12-24 hours before harvest (water OK). This empties the gut
  for cleaner evisceration.
- Handle animals calmly to minimize stress (stress affects meat quality —
  dark cutting, PSE pork, tough meat).
- Process early in the morning when possible.

## Slaughter and Dispatch

### Poultry and Rabbits
- **Cervical dislocation:** Quick, effective for birds and rabbits under
  5 lbs. Hold the bird/rabbit, dislocate the neck with a swift pull and
  twist. Ensure immediate unconsciousness before proceeding.
- **Killing cone:** Bird is placed head-down in a cone, neck extended.
  Sever the carotid arteries and jugular vein on both sides of the neck
  with a sharp knife. Allow the bird to bleed out completely (2-3 minutes).
- **Broomstick method (rabbits):** Place a bar behind the rabbit's ears,
  step on the bar, and pull the hind legs upward sharply.

### Sheep, Goats, and Pigs
- **Captive bolt:** Penetrate the skull, causing immediate unconsciousness.
  Follow with exsanguination (cut throat) to ensure death.
- **Firearm:** .22 LR or .22 Magnum for sheep and goats (aim at the
  intersection of two lines from eyes to opposite ears). .22 LR or
  larger for pigs (aim at center of forehead, slightly above eyes).
  Follow with exsanguination.

### Cattle
- **Captive bolt:** Preferred method. Penetrate the skull at the correct
  anatomical site. Follow with exsanguination.
- **Firearm:** .22 Magnum, .38 Special, or rifle (.223, .308) for
  emergency situations. Aim at center of forehead, 2 inches above eye
  line, angled toward the brainstem.
- Ensure the animal is down and unconscious before any further steps.

## Skinning

### General Procedure
- Hang the carcass by the hind legs on a gambrel.
- Make initial cuts around each hind leg below the hock. Slit between the
  legs along the inside of the thighs to the pelvic area.
- Begin pulling the hide down from the hind legs, using your knife only to
  separate the skin from the fascia (connective tissue). Keep the blade
  flat against the skin to avoid cutting meat.
- Work around the tail and down the body. Pull the hide downward using
  your body weight.
- Cut around the front legs at the knee. Skin the front legs.
- Pull the hide over the shoulders and neck. Cut around the head and
  remove the hide completely.
- For pigs: scalding (rather than skinning) is preferred to preserve the
  skin for crackling. Scald at 145-150°F for 3-5 minutes, then scrape.

### Tips
- A sharp knife is safer than a dull knife (less force required, less
  slipping).
- Keep the blade angled away from the meat.
- Cool the carcass if skinning takes longer than 30 minutes.

## Gutting (Evisceration)

### Procedure
- Open the abdominal cavity: make a shallow cut through the abdominal wall
  starting at the pelvis (avoid cutting the intestines). Use your free
  hand to guide the knife and protect the organs.
- Cut down the midline to the breastbone.
- Split the pelvic bone (with a saw or knife) to free the bung (rectum).
- Tie off the bung with a string, cut around it, and pull it into the
  abdominal cavity (keeps feces contained).
- Reach in and pull out the intestines, stomach, and other organs. Let
  them drop into a clean container.
- Separate the liver (save it), heart (save it), and kidneys (save them).
  Discard the rest of the entrails or save for other uses.
- Open the chest cavity: split the breastbone with a saw.
- Remove the lungs and windpipe (pluck).
- Trim any contamination, blood clots, or loose tissue from the carcass.

### Safety Notes
- Wear disposable gloves throughout.
- Avoid puncturing the intestines, gallbladder, or urinary bladder.
- If contamination occurs, wash with clean, cold water immediately.
- Work quickly to cool the carcass.

## Cutting and Wrapping

### Chilling
- Chill the carcass for 12-24 hours (poultry: 4-6 hours, rabbits: 2-4
  hours) before cutting. Cold meat cuts cleanly.
- Target temperature: 33-40°F internal.
- Large animals: hang in a cool, dry, well-ventilated space. Small animals:
  submerge in ice water.

### Basic Cuts (Beef/Lamb/Goat/Pork)
- **Primal cuts:** Chuck, rib, loin, round, flank, brisket, plate (beef).
  Shoulder, rack, loin, leg, shank (lamb/goat). Shoulder, loin, belly,
  leg, ham (pork).
- **Sub-primal cuts:** Break primals into roasts, steaks, chops, stew meat,
  and ground meat.
- **Cutting order:** Remove legs first, then separate chuck/shoulder from
  rib/loin, then break individual cuts.

- **Ground meat:** Trim and grind trimmings. Mix with fat (pork fat or
  beef fat) for proper consistency. 80/20 or 75/25 lean-to-fat ratio.
