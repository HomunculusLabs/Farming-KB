## rRNA Gene Organization and Isolation

In eukaryotes, genes coding for large (25S-28S), small (18S), and 5.8S
rRNAs occur as tandem repeats with as many as 100 to 200 copies. The
separately transcribed 5S rRNA gene is often included in the repeat as
part of the intergenic spacer (IGS) or nontranscribed spacer (NTS).
Each rRNA size class has been examined for phylogenetic information.
The large quantities of rRNAs in cells make isolation relatively easy
despite ubiquitous RNAases. Procedures of Chirgwin and colleagues
(1979) and modifications by Kurtzman and Liu (1990) are generally
satisfactory for rRNA isolation. Protocols for rDNA sequencing using
PCR and specific oligonucleotide primers were established by White and
colleagues (1990) and Vilgalys and Hester (1990).
