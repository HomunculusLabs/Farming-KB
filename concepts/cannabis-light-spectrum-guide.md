# Cannabis Light Spectrum Guide

Light is the fundamental driver of cannabis growth and development. Understanding the relationship between light spectrum, intensity, and photoperiod is essential for maximizing yield, potency, and terpene production in indoor cultivation. This guide covers the key metrics (PAR, PPFD, DLI), LED spectrum design, photoperiod management, and light stress techniques.

## Key Light Metrics

### PAR (Photosynthetically Active Radiation)

PAR is the range of light wavelengths that plants use for photosynthesis: 400-700 nanometers (nm). This spans from blue (400-500 nm) through green (500-600 nm) to red (600-700 nm). PAR is the plant-specific measure of useful light — it excludes UV (below 400 nm) and far-red (above 700 nm), though both of these ranges have biological effects on cannabis.

### PPFD (Photosynthetic Photon Flux Density)

PPFD measures the intensity of PAR light reaching the plant canopy, expressed in micromoles per square meter per second (μmol/m²/s). This is the primary metric growers use to set light intensity.

Typical PPFD targets for cannabis:
- Seedlings/clones: 100-300 μmol/m²/s
- Early vegetative: 300-500 μmol/m²/s
- Late vegetative: 500-800 μmol/m²/s
- Early flowering: 600-900 μmol/m²/s
- Peak flowering (weeks 3-6): 800-1200 μmol/m²/s
- Late flowering (weeks 7+): 600-900 μmol/m²/s (reducing to prevent bleaching)

Measuring PPFD requires a quantum PAR meter — lux meters are not accurate for grow lights because they weight wavelengths based on human vision, not plant response.

### DLI (Daily Light Integral)

DLI is the total amount of PAR light delivered to the plant over a 24-hour period, expressed in moles per square meter per day (mol/m²/d). It combines PPFD with photoperiod (hours of light) into a single cumulative number.

DLI = PPFD (μmol/m²/s) × photoperiod (hours) × 0.0036

Target DLI for cannabis:
- Vegetative: 12-20 mol/m²/d
- Flowering: 20-40 mol/m²/d (under 12-hour photoperiod, this requires very high PPFD)

DLI is the most complete single metric for evaluating whether plants are receiving adequate light. It accounts for both intensity and duration.

## Light Spectrum and Plant Response

### Blue Light (400-500 nm)

Effects on cannabis:
- Compact, short internode growth (prevents stretching)
- Enhanced chlorophyll production
- Strong root development
- Increased terpene and cannabinoid production (stress response)
- Essential for phototropism (plant movement toward light)
- Stomatal regulation

Use in cultivation:
- Higher blue ratios during vegetative stage promote compact, bushy growth
- Excessive blue during flowering can reduce stretch and yield
- Blue light is the primary signal for photomorphogenesis

### Red Light (600-700 nm)

Effects on cannabis:
- Drives stem elongation and leaf expansion
- Primary driver of photosynthetic efficiency
- Promotes flowering initiation (phytochrome response)
- Increases biomass production
- Flowering hormones are regulated by red/far-red ratio

Use in cultivation:
- Higher red ratios during flowering promote bud development and yield
- Red-dominant spectrum during late flower maximizes calyx size
- Too much red without blue balance causes excessive stretching

### Green Light (500-600 nm)

Long considered unimportant for photosynthesis, but research shows:
- Green light penetrates deeper into the canopy than red or blue
- Drives photosynthesis in lower leaves that receive little direct light
- Involved in shade avoidance responses
- Some green light in the spectrum improves overall canopy penetration
- Pure green light can trigger stem elongation (shade avoidance)

Use in cultivation:
- 5-15% green light in the spectrum improves lower canopy photosynthesis
- Excessive green may cause stretching
- Most full-spectrum LEDs include some green naturally

### UV-A (315-400 nm) and UV-B (280-315 nm)

- UV-B triggers trichome production as a defense/stress response
- Increases THC and terpene production
- UV supplementation in final 2-4 weeks of flowering can boost potency
- Start with 15 min/day UV-B, increase to 30-60 min over 2 weeks
- Both UV types blocked by most greenhouse glass

### Far-Red (700-800 nm)

- Triggers phytochrome response regulating flowering
- End-of-day far-red treatment (5-15 min at lights-out) shortens flowering time
- Promotes stem elongation (shade avoidance)
- Emerson Enhancement Effect: far-red + red increases photosynthetic efficiency

## LED Spectrum Types

### Full-Spectrum (White) LEDs

- Broad spectrum covering 380-750 nm
- Most closely mimics natural sunlight
- Good balance of blue, green, red
- Easier on the eyes for working in the grow space
- Adequate for all growth stages without spectrum switching
- Examples: Samsung LM301B/LM301H diodes, various COB arrays

### Blue-Heavy Spectrum

- Higher ratio of blue (450 nm) to red (660 nm)
- Promotes compact vegetative growth
- Often used in dedicated vegetative rooms
- Can reduce internode spacing and promote lateral branching
- Less efficient for flowering

### Red-Heavy Spectrum

- Dominated by 630-660 nm red diodes
- Often includes some blue (typically 10-20%)
- Very efficient for photosynthesis (red is the most photosynthetically active wavelength)
- Best for flowering stage
- Can cause excessive stretching if used during vegetative stage

### Tunable Spectrum LEDs

- Fixtures that allow real-time adjustment of spectrum
- Can shift from vegetative-optimized to flowering-optimized spectrum
- Most expensive but offer maximum control
- Examples: various smart LED systems with app control
- Can incorporate UV and far-red on separate channels

## Photoperiod Management

### Standard Photoperiod Schedule

Vegetative stage:
- 18 hours light / 6 hours dark (18/6) — most common
- 20/4 or 24/0 — faster vegetative growth but some strains show stress at 24/0
- Plants need a dark period for metabolic recovery and root starch translocation

Flowering stage:
- 12 hours light / 12 hours dark (12/12) — triggers flowering in photoperiod-sensitive strains
- Dark period must be completely uninterrupted — light leaks cause stress, hermaphroditism, or re-veg
- Total darkness is critical: no indicator lights, no light under doors

### Light Stress Techniques

Light stress training (LST using light):
- Light deprivation (shortening photoperiod below 12/12) can accelerate flowering
- Flashing light during dark period can disrupt flowering (avoid this)
- Light intensity ramping: gradually increase PPFD over the first week of flower to acclimate plants

UV stress:
- Low-dose UV-B supplementation in late flower increases trichome production
- Start with 15 min/day and increase to 30-60 min over 2 weeks
- Monitor for leaf curling or burning — reduce if damage appears
- Not recommended for beginners

## Light Distance and Canopy Management

- Follow manufacturer recommendations for hanging height
- Use a PAR meter to measure PPFD at canopy level and adjust height accordingly
- Maintain even canopy with SCROG (Screen of Green) or LST
- Use multiple smaller lights rather than one large light for better coverage
- Reflective walls (Mylar, white paint) improve light distribution
- Replace lights when output degrades (LEDs lose ~5-10% output over 50,000 hours)

## Energy Efficiency

- LED is significantly more efficient than HPS, MH, or CMH
- Look for μmol/J rating: 2.0+ is efficient, 2.5+ is excellent
- Calculate actual power draw (watts at the wall), not rated wattage
- LEDs produce less heat than HID, reducing cooling costs
- Account for the full system cost: light + electricity + cooling + replacement
