## Zero Emissions Research and Initiatives (ZERI)

## Overview

Zero Emissions Research and Initiatives (ZERI) is an international organization founded by visionary eco-designer Gunter Pauli. Pauli, selected as one of "100 Global Leaders of Tomorrow" by the World Economic Forum, has been a pioneer of sustainability since the 1980s. ZERI's core philosophy is encapsulated in a single principle: **"Zero emissions means zero waste."**

ZERI applies **systems thinking** — a holistic approach that considers feedback loops in complex dynamic systems — to design projects applicable to all human needs including food, shelter, livelihood, self-esteem, and community, all implemented in a sustainable manner.

## Five Kingdoms Design Philosophy

The design principles of ZERI are modeled on the natural world, drawing instruction from the relationships among the five kingdoms of life on Earth:

- **Bacteria**
- **Protists** (e.g., algae)
- **Plants**
- **Animals**
- **Fungi**

The fundamental observation is that the waste — or even the toxin — of one kingdom is often a nutrient or energy source for species of another kingdom. A prime example is the saprophytic relationship between fungi and plants: without fungi, we would be smothered by mountains of dead plant material.

When the five kingdoms work together harmoniously in a healthy ecosystem, they process organic matter to maximize productivity, biodiversity, and system resilience. An ecosystem endures indefinitely as long as climate and environment remain stable — resources are not exhausted, nor does the ecosystem pollute itself. It is, by definition, **sustainable**.

## The Namibian Brewery Project

The flagship ZERI project was initiated in Tsumeb, Namibia, centered on a brewery. This project perfectly illustrates ZERI's integrated design principles.

### The Problem

In traditional breweries, the beer-making process generates enormous quantities of organic waste and lost energy for every unit of beer produced. Only a small percentage of nutrients in the grain are utilized, and the protein remains almost untouched. The spent grain becomes waste despite containing considerable nutritional potential.

Traditional disposal methods are problematic:
- **Landfill disposal**: Wastes all embedded nutrients
- **Animal feed**: Animals find spent grain difficult to digest, producing significant amounts of methane (a greenhouse gas), and manure runoff may pollute watersheds

### The ZERI Solution: A Cascading Value Chain

1. **Spent grain to Oyster mushrooms (*Pleurotus ostreatus*)**: With a relatively small investment, spent grain serves as a prime substrate for mushroom cultivation, yielding nutritious and marketable oyster mushrooms plus local jobs.

2. **[[spent-mushroom-substrate]] to Livestock feed**: After fruiting, the substrate has increased protein content from the mushroom mycelium. Livestock find it palatable, resulting in increased animal growth and better quality meat.

3. **Spent grain to Bread**: Additional grain can be incorporated into bread production — more bread to feed more people.

4. **Brewery wastewater to Manure flushing**: Wastewater from brewing flushes manure and organic matter from animal pens.

5. **Runoff to Anaerobic digester**: The organic-rich but pathogen-containing runoff is fed into an airtight digester where anaerobic bacteria produce methane, which is collected and burned to provide heat for sterilizing spent grain for mushroom substrate.

6. **Digester effluent to Oxidation ponds**: Pathogen-free but nutrient-rich effluent flows to oxidation ponds where algae flourish through photosynthesis.

7. **Algae to Fish food**: Harvested algal biomass feeds fish in adjacent ponds.

8. **Algae pond water to Crop fertilizer**: Still nutrient-rich water fertilizes gardens and crops, producing higher quality organic plant crops without synthetic fertilizers.

This single brewery project integrates all five kingdoms of life into a seamless, zero-waste system.

## Earthworm Integration (Vermicomposting)

ZERI trainee Mary Appelhof ("Wormwoman") implemented complementary projects utilizing earthworms within the ZERI framework. Her system added another layer:

- **Spent mushroom substrate to Earthworms**: Worms transmute mushroom mycelium into more worm biomass
- **Worms to Fish or chicken feed**: Creating another protein source
- **Remaining substrate to Vermicompost**: A nutrient-rich natural fertilizer and soil conditioner
- **Earthworms to Medicinal enzymes**: Lumbrokinase, a potent fibrinolytic enzyme extracted from earthworms, dissolves blood clots and is used to treat strokes and coronary thrombosis

## Sustainable Communities / ZERI-New Mexico (SCZ-NM)

SCZ-NM, located near Santa Fe, New Mexico, operates under the U.S. Forest Service Collaborative Forest Restoration Program. Its missions include:

### Forest Fire Hazard Reduction
- Thinning overgrown National Forests creates mountains of woody scrap
- Woody refuse is used as substrate for oyster and shiitake mushrooms
- Even wood digested by mushrooms can be incorporated into digestible feed for cows, sheep, and bison
- This aligns with Paul Stamets's concept of **mycoforestry**

### Native Fungal Culture Bank
- Founded by SCZ co-founder Lynda Taylor
- Native fungi are collected and maintained on agar plates
- Fungi are tested for growth on various small-diameter tree species (ponderosa, pinon, juniper) and invasive species (salt cedar, Russian olive)

### Mycoforestry Principles (from Paul Stamets)
- Matching native fungi to native tree species
- Chipping woody debris from forest operations and inoculating with fungi
- Mixing spores into chainsaw and wood chipper oil for dispersal
- Using myceliated chips in depressions and roadside ditches for **mycofiltration** — reducing erosion and silt flow by binding substrate into a dense matted network

## Coffee Waste Project (Chinchina, Colombia)

[[carmenza-jaramillo-lopez]] conducted a ZERI project in Chinchina, Colombia, a coffee-growing region with mostly low-income peasants facing volatile coffee prices. Just as in brewing, coffee beans represent only a small fraction of the agricultural starting material. Traditionally, coffee waste was discarded. The Chinchina project utilizes coffee waste biomass as a substrate for shiitake cultivation at a remarkable **Biological Efficiency of 75%**, providing additional income and nutrition for coffee growers.

## Key Principles Summary

| Principle | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Zero waste | Every output becomes an input for another process |
| Five kingdoms integration | Bacteria, protists, plants, animals, and fungi all participate |
| Systems thinking | Holistic consideration of feedback loops |
| Value-added cascading | Each processing step increases economic and nutritional value |
| Local empowerment | Projects managed by families or small communities |
| Low-tech accessibility | Simple, affordable methods suitable for developing regions |
