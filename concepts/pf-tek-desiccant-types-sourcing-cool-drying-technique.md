# PF TEK Desiccant Types, Sourcing, and Cool Drying Technique

## Overview

The PF TEK identifies desiccant-based cool drying as the overall best technique for preserving harvested mushrooms. Using chemical desiccants to absorb moisture at or near room temperature avoids the alkaloid degradation caused by heat-based drying methods (ovens, hot air dryers). The technique preserves [[hplc-analysis-of-psilocybin-and-psilocin]] content, maintaining the mushrooms in their prime condition for months.

## Desiccant Types and Sources

### 1. Drierite (Calcium Sulfate)

Drierite is described as "the universal lab desiccant." It is available from chemical and science supply retailers. Drierite (anhydrous calcium sulfate) is widely used in laboratory settings for moisture control and is considered the gold standard for desiccant drying.

**Key properties:**
- Highly efficient moisture absorption
- Can be regenerated by heating
- Available with and without indicating cobalt chloride
- Standard laboratory-grade desiccant

### 2. Silica Gel Granules

Silica gel is available from chemical and science supply stores. It is one of the most commonly available desiccants and works well for mushroom drying applications.

**Key properties:**
- Granular form allows even distribution
- Can be regenerated by oven heating
- Widely available from multiple retail sources
- Effective at room [[duggar-mushroom-environmental-factors-temperature-moisture-light]] absorption

### 3. Damp Gone (Moisture Absorbing Granules)

Damp Gone is described as looking like kitty litter and is designed for drying closets and damp places in the home. It is available at well-stocked hardware stores.

**Key properties:**
- Inexpensive compared to laboratory-grade desiccants
- Works as effectively as any other desiccant for mushroom drying
- Readily available at consumer hardware stores
- Large granule size suitable for layering under drying screens

## Safety Precautions

PF notes that desiccant products may carry toxicity warnings:
- Do not breathe the dust
- Try not to touch it directly — it dries skin
- Follow manufacturer safety instructions

However, PF emphasizes that when desiccant is used in an airtight box under a screen, it does nothing to the fungi except dry them. The setup is completely safe for mushroom preservation because the desiccant never comes into direct contact with the mushroom material.

## Desiccant Preparation

Before first use, desiccant must be pre-dried because it is usually somewhat damp when purchased from the store, which would thwart its moisture-absorbing function:

1. Heat the desiccant in an oven according to manufacturer instructions
2. This drives off any absorbed moisture and restores full drying capacity
3. Store pre-heated desiccant in an airtight container until ready to use

## Desiccant Reuse

Desiccant can be reused indefinitely:
1. After a drying cycle, the desiccant has absorbed moisture from the mushrooms
2. Re-heat the desiccant in an oven as directed by the manufacturer
3. Allow to cool in an airtight container
4. The desiccant is ready for another drying cycle

This makes desiccant drying a highly economical preservation method over time, despite the initial purchase cost.

## Cool Desiccation Procedure

### Materials Needed

- Desiccant (Drierite, Silica Gel, or Damp Gone)
- Wire screen (for holding mushrooms above desiccant)
- Plastic tub or container (airtight)
- Plastic bag with tie-off (to seal the container)

### Setup

1. Place a 1-inch layer of desiccant on the bottom of the container
2. Place mushrooms on a wire screen laid on top of the desiccant
3. Put the container into a plastic bag
4. Close the bag with a wire tie (a clear plastic bag allows observation of drying progress)

### Drying Timeline

- After 24 hours: slight shriveling of the mushrooms is visible
- After 4–5 days: mushrooms are dried rock hard
- **Doneness test**: the stem should snap cleanly when bent

### Optional Pre-Drying Step

Pre-drying mushrooms in the air on a wire screen works well if room humidity is not high. After a couple of days of air drying, the shriveling fungus can be quickly and completely finished in the desiccant box. This two-stage approach can reduce the load on the desiccant and speed overall drying time.

### Refrigerated Drying for Maximum Alkaloid Preservation

For the best alkaloid preservation technique, the desiccant box can be placed into the refrigerator. Mushrooms dried at near-freezing temperatures experience minimal chemical degradation. The cold temperature slows any residual enzymatic activity while the desiccant removes moisture.

## Post-Drying Storage

Once properly desiccant-dried:
- Store mushrooms by sealing in plastic bags
- Alternatively, store in canning jars with rubber-edged canning lids tightened
- The freezer is a good place for long-term preservation
- Ensure fungi are tightly sealed in containers to protect against moisture in the freezer

PF emphasizes that mushrooms dried in this way lose hardly any [[guzman-magic-mushrooms-chemical-constituents]]. Their truly desiccated state preserves them in their prime for months when properly stored.

## Contrast with Other Drying Methods

PF explicitly warns against:
- **Oven drying**: heat leaches chemical constituents and reduces quality
- **Hot air dryers**: same problem as oven drying

PF notes that frost-free (dehumidifying) refrigerators work for drying but are time-consuming, and not everyone has access to one. Desiccant drying is recommended as the most accessible and effective method overall.

## Why Not Vermiculite or Perlite?

PF specifically uses desiccant rather than perlite or vermiculite for the final drying stage. While perlite is excellent for humidification during fruiting (see pf-tek-perlite-humidification-technique.md), it is not suitable for desiccation because:
- Perlite holds water through [[bloomfield-buller-drop-surface-tension-spore-catapult-basidiospore-discharge]] and slow evaporation
- It does not actively absorb moisture below ambient humidity levels
- Desiccants create a much lower moisture equilibrium than perlite
