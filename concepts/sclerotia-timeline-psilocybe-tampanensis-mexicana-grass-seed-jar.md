## Inoculation and Colonization

Once cooled, jars are inoculated with the chosen *Psilocybe* species using spore syringe,
liquid culture, or agar-to-grain transfer. After inoculation, jars should be **shaken
thoroughly** to distribute the inoculant evenly — this ensures multiple colonization points
across the substrate, significantly accelerating the colonization phase.

### Colonization Phase

Jars are incubated at **20°C** (68°F) in a clean location. Colonization typically completes
within **5 to 20 days**, depending on several factors:

- **Temperature** — Temperatures closer to 20°C are optimal; higher temperatures increase
  the risk of bacterial contamination.
- **Species** — *P. mexicana* may colonize slightly faster than *P. tampanensis*.
- **Inoculation method** — Liquid culture or agar wedges colonize faster than spore
  syringes due to the presence of already-active mycelium.
- **Shake quality** — More even distribution leads to faster, more uniform colonization.

Full colonization is indicated by complete visual coverage of the substrate with no
uncolonized seed visible. The mycelium binds the grass seed into a cohesive white mass.

## Sclerotia Formation Phase

After full colonization, the mycelium shifts from [[cannabis-vegetative-growth-stage]] to sclerogenesis — the
biological process of forming sclerotia. This transition occurs spontaneously and does not
require any change in environmental conditions, substrate addition, or casing application.

### Conditions for Sclerotia Development
