# Shotgun Fruiting Chamber Design

The shotgun fruiting chamber (SGFC), popularized by RogerRabbit on the Shroomery cultivation forums, is one of the most widely used fruiting chambers for small-scale Psilocybe cubensis cultivation. Its name derives from the pattern of holes drilled throughout all six sides of the container, which provides continuous fresh air exchange (FAE) through natural convection. The design is inexpensive, requires no mechanical equipment, and is well-suited to beginners.

## Design Principles

The shotgun fruiting chamber operates on the principle of passive air exchange driven by temperature differentials between the inside and outside of the chamber. Warm air generated by the metabolic activity of colonizing mycelium rises and exits through upper holes, while cooler fresh air is drawn in through lower holes. This constant gentle circulation provides both fresh air exchange and evaporative cooling without fans or pumps. The chamber relies on a thick layer of moist perlite at the bottom to maintain high humidity.

## Construction

The standard SGFC is built from a clear or translucent plastic storage tub, typically 54-66 quart capacity. The construction process involves drilling 1/4 inch holes on all six sides of the tub in a grid pattern spaced approximately 2 inches apart. This includes holes in the lid, the bottom, and all four sides. The bottom holes are essential because they allow air to flow through the perlite layer, picking up moisture as it rises.

The perlite layer is 4-5 inches deep and thoroughly moistened before installation. Perlite should be rinsed to remove fine dust, then soaked and drained until it is at [[monotub-cultivation]].

## Limitations

The SGFC is designed primarily for small-scale cultivation using the [[monotub-cultivation]] designs or equipped grow rooms with active fresh air exchange are more appropriate.

The SGFC also struggles in environments with very low ambient humidity. In arid climates or during dry winter months, the perlite may not be able to maintain adequate humidity, requiring supplementary room humidification. Conversely, in very humid environments, the SGFC may provide insufficient evaporation from the substrate surface, which is an important pinning trigger.

## Advantages Over Alternatives

Compared to monotub designs, the SGFC provides superior fresh air exchange for individual cakes because all six sides are perforated. Monotubs rely on fewer, larger holes and can develop CO2 pockets that affect fruiting quality. The SGFC also allows easy inspection of individual cakes from all angles and permits removal of contaminated cakes without disturbing others. Its simplicity and lack of mechanical parts make it virtually maintenance-free apart from occasional misting.
