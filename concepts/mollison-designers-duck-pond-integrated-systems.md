# Duck Pond Integrated Systems

Bill Mollison describes duck pond systems as among the most productive and
easily managed integrated components in [[permaculture-design]]. Ducks combine
the functions of pest control, nutrient cycling, egg and meat production,
and [[orchard-management]] within a single biological system centred on a
pond or series of ponds.

## The Duck-Orchard Model

The classic permaculture duck system integrates a pond with a surrounding
food forest or orchard. Ducks forage in the orchard during the day,
consuming slugs, snails, codling moth larvae, grasshoppers, and fallen
fruit (preventing pest carryover). They return to the pond for drinking,
bathing, and night-time safety.

This integration provides multiple yields simultaneously:

- Eggs and meat from ducks
- Pest control in the orchard (ducks are voracious predators of snails
  and slugs that damage fruit trees)
- Fertiliser: duck manure enriches both the pond water (feeding aquatic
  plants and fish) and the orchard floor
- Weed control: ducks graze ground cover vegetation, reducing competition
  around tree roots
- Water plants harvested from the pond provide mulch, compost material,
  and supplementary duck fodder

## Pond Design for Ducks

Duck ponds in permaculture systems are designed at a scale appropriate to
the flock size and landscape:

- Minimum depth: 30-60cm for bathing; deeper zones of 1-2m provide
  refuge and support fish and aquatic plants
- Shallow edges: gradual slopes (1:3 to 1:5) allow ducks to walk in and
  out easily and provide planting zones for marginal species
- Island features: small islands or partially submerged logs provide
  safe nesting sites protected from land predators
- Drainage: ponds should have a drain pipe for periodic cleaning and
  nutrient harvesting; drained nutrient-rich water irrigates the orchard
- Size: allow 1-2 square metres of water surface per duck for adequate
  space and water quality

## Water Plants for Duck Fodder

The pond surface is planted with productive aquatic species that serve as
duck fodder, water filters, and nutrient cyclers:

- Duckweed (Lemna spp.): fastest growing higher plant on Earth, doubling
  biomass every 2-3 days; up to 40% protein when grown in nutrient-rich
  water; ducks consume it directly
- Azolla (Azolla spp.): aquatic fern that fixes atmospheric nitrogen in
  symbiosis with cyanobacteria; high protein fodder for ducks and excellent
  nitrogen-rich mulch when scooped from the pond
- Water hyacinth (Eichhornia crassipes): extremely productive in warm
  climates; provides duck fodder and compost material; must be managed
  aggressively as it can choke waterways
- Kangkong (Ipomoea aquatica): edible aquatic spinach that ducks graze;
  also harvested for human consumption
- Water chestnut, lotus, and watercress provide additional food plants for
  both ducks and humans

## Nutrient Cycling Dynamics

Duck pond systems create a tight nutrient cycle:

1. Duck manure enters pond water, providing nitrogen and phosphorus
2. Aquatic plants absorb these nutrients, growing rapidly
3. Ducks eat aquatic plants, converting plant nutrients into eggs and meat
4. Excess plants are harvested for mulch or compost in the orchard
5. Pond water enriched with duck manure is periodically drained to
   irrigate and fertilise orchard trees
6. Trees produce fruit and fallen fruit feeds ducks

This cycle can operate with minimal external inputs once established. The
pond acts as a nutrient reservoir that captures, transforms, and
redistributes fertility across the system.

## Duck Species Selection

Mollison notes that different duck breeds suit different system scales:

- Indian Runner ducks: tall, upright birds that are excellent foragers in
  orchards; prolific egg layers (200-300 eggs/year); minimal flying ability
  so they stay close to the pond
- Khaki Campbell: hardy, prolific layers adapted to varied climates; good
  foragers
- Muscovy ducks: large, quiet birds that roost in trees; excellent mothers
  that brood their own young; prefer warmer climates but tolerate cool
  conditions; heavy meat birds
- Mallard-derived breeds: adaptable and widely available; moderate egg
  production; can fly so may require wing clipping

For integrated orchard systems, Indian Runners and Khaki Campbells are
preferred for their egg production, foraging behaviour, and ease of
management.

## Fish Integration

Duck ponds can support fish alongside ducks, though management requires
attention to water quality. Duck manure at moderate levels feeds
phytoplankton and zooplankton that support small fish. Overstocking ducks
leads to excessive nutrient loading and oxygen depletion.

Suitable fish for duck ponds include tilapia (warm climates), carp,
goldfish, and native minnow species. Fish provide an additional protein
yield and help control mosquito larvae.

## System Management

- Stock 3-6 ducks per 100 square metres of pond surface
- Harvest duckweed and azolla weekly to prevent complete surface coverage
  and maintain light penetration
- Drain and clean ponds annually or biannually, applying nutrient-rich
  sediment to orchard trees
- Provide supplementary grain feeding during winter when aquatic plant
  growth slows
- Protect ducks from predators (foxes, raptors, snakes) with fencing or
  night-time housing near the pond

## Multiple Pond Series

For larger systems, Mollison suggests a series of ponds connected by
overflow channels. The upper pond receives duck manure directly and grows
nutrient-loving plants like duckweed. Overflow water passes through a
planted channel to a second pond with cleaner water supporting fish and
a wider range of aquatic plants. A third pond may provide irrigation
storage with the cleanest water.

This staged nutrient stripping maximises the use of duck manure while
preventing downstream pollution.
