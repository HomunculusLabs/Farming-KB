## Grain Yields in Small Spaces

One hundred square feet of grain may yield 4, 8, 12, or more pounds
of edible seed. This productivity makes grain growing feasible even
in very small gardens. The goal with wheat is to eventually achieve
two 26-pound crops in an 8-month period from only 100 square feet —
yielding approximately one 1-pound loaf of bread per week for the
entire year.

### Current Achievements

Ecology Action"s highest wheat yield is about 21 pounds per
100-square-foot bed, using about 10 inches of water for the whole
season with compost grown on-site and minimal purchased fertilizer.
The Zulus in South Africa use a similar technique and grow grains
with natural rainfall alone, demonstrating adaptability to many
climates without irrigation infrastructure.

## Protein Crops

GROW BIOINTENSIVE techniques can be used to grow important protein
crops. Experiments with wheat, soybeans, grains, beans, and other
seeds have worked well. For cooler climates, varieties such as peanut,
yellow-eye, and cranberry beans are recommended.

### Evaluating Protein Crop Practicality

Not all protein crops are equally practical for the mini-farm. Sesame
seeds illustrate the need to evaluate total practicality:
- Very high in nutrition
- Usually have low yields compared to other protein crops
- Somewhat difficult to harvest
- Can exhaust the soil
- On a per-square-foot sustainable nutrition yield basis, not
  necessarily superior to other protein sources

A large sesame harvest would require a very large growing area. It
is important to examine each crop's total practicality, not just its
nutritional profile.

## Nutrient Considerations

When producing intermediate yields, the quantity of nutrients each
crop takes from the soil becomes a critical factor. Many "heavy
givers" of nitrogen can exhaust the soil of other nutrients over
time. Soybeans, for example, are "heavy giving" legumes, but
continuous cropping of them has been demonstrated to wear out the
soil.

It is essential to develop and work within natural sustainable
cycles. The [[biodiversity-fungal-genetic-diversity]], see Ecology Action"s Self-Teaching
