# Swales and Water Conservation

A swale is a long, shallow ditch about 3-5 feet across that stops and channels water flow into the soil. Unlike a regular ditch (which carries water away), the swale is designed to help water absorb directly into the ground. Swales are perhaps the most effective method of water conservation in both dry and humid climates.

## How Swales Work

- Swales lie across the land, especially across slopes
- Water is forced into the soil and soaked up by trees planted along the swales
- Can be filled with rock, gravel, or gypsum for better water penetration
- Work on steep slopes, flat prairies, and in urban areas
- Can capture road and roof runoff in urban settings

## Building Swales

1. Dig a shallow ditch a few feet deep along the contour of the slope
2. The swale catches water in a pool as rain runs in; it gradually soaks in
3. If overflowing, widen the swale or improve drainage
4. Add mulch over time; grass will likely grow naturally
5. As trees mature and shade the swale, other species will spring up

## Swale Spacing

Always install two or more swales together along the contour. The second swale is placed based on annual rainfall:

| Annual Rainfall | Distance Between Swales |
|----------------|------------------------|
| 5 inches | 60 feet |
| 10 inches | 50 feet |
| 20 inches | 40 feet |
| 30 inches | 30 feet |
| 40 inches | 20 feet |
| 50 inches | 10 feet |

## Planting Strategy

After a couple of rainfalls that soak deeply into the soil, plant trees directly behind the hump (berm) of dirt:

- **Wet climate:** Heavily plant the entire area between swales
- **Dry climate:** Only plant the edges of the swales

It takes 3-10 years for trees to fully mature and begin their job of:
- Shading the ground
- Mulching the soil with leaf drop
- Absorbing salts from the soil
- Soaking up water runoff
- Preventing waterlogging and evaporation
- Producing valuable products and food
- Improving temperature and microclimate

## Swale vs. Ditch

| Feature | Swale | Ditch |
|---------|-------|-------|
| Purpose | Absorb water into soil | Carry water away |
| Direction | Across slope, on contour | Along slope, downhill |
| Liner | Often filled with rock/gravel | Usually bare earth |
| Vegetation | Trees planted on berm | Usually kept clear |
| Result | Recharges groundwater | Discharges water off-site |

This planting step is the most important part of the whole swale strategy. Without trees, the swale is incomplete.
