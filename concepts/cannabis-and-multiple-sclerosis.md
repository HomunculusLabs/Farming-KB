# Cannabis and Multiple Sclerosis

updated: 2026-04-18

Multiple sclerosis (MS) is the condition with perhaps the most extensive clinical evidence for cannabis-based medicine. Research from The Cannabis Health Index documents over 30 studies examining cannabinoids for spasticity, neuropathic pain, bladder dysfunction, and other MS symptoms. Sativex (THC:CBD oromucosal spray) was developed primarily for MS and is approved in many countries.

## Key Clinical Studies

### Spasticity
**Collin et al. (2007)**: "Randomized controlled trial of cannabis-based medicine in spasticity caused by multiple sclerosis." European Journal of Neurology 14(3):290-96.

**Pryce and Baker (2007)**: "Control of spasticity in a multiple sclerosis model is mediated by CB1, not CB2, cannabinoid receptors." British Journal of Pharmacology.

Spasticity relief is mediated through CB1 receptors, not CB2. This distinguishes MS spasticity from inflammatory conditions where CB2 dominates.

### Neuropathic Pain
**Rog et al. (2005)**: "Randomized, controlled trial of cannabis-based medicine in central pain in multiple sclerosis." Neurology 65(6):812-19.

**Rog et al. (2007)**: "Oromucosal delta9-tetrahydrocannabinol/cannabidiol for neuropathic pain associated with multiple sclerosis." Clinical Therapeutics 29(9):2068-79.

### Bladder Dysfunction
**Freeman et al. (2006)**: "The effect of cannabis on urge incontinence in patients with multiple sclerosis." International Urogynecology Journal.

**Brady et al. (2004)**: "An open-label pilot study of cannabis-based extracts for bladder dysfunction in advanced multiple sclerosis." Multiple Sclerosis 10(4):425-33.

### Broad Symptom Relief
**Wade et al. (2004)**: "Do cannabis-based medicinal extracts have general or specific effects on symptoms in multiple sclerosis?" Multiple Sclerosis 10(4):434-41.

A double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled study on 160 patients found improvements in pain, spasticity, tremor, and overall symptom scores.

### Endocannabinoid System Dysregulation
**Centonze et al. (2007)**: "The endocannabinoid system is dysregulated in multiple sclerosis and in experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis." Brain 130(Pt 10):2543-53.

**Di Filippo et al. (2008)**: "Abnormalities in the cerebrospinal fluid levels of endocannabinoids in multiple sclerosis."

These studies found that MS patients have abnormal endocannabinoid levels, suggesting a deficiency that cannabis may correct.

### Adhesion Molecules
**Mestre et al. (2009)**: "A cannabinoid agonist interferes with the progression of a chronic model of multiple sclerosis by downregulating adhesion molecules."

Cannabinoids reduce the adhesion molecules that allow inflammatory immune cells to cross the blood-brain barrier -- a key event in MS lesion formation.

### Early Clinical Evidence
- **Meinck, Schonle, and Conrad (1989)**: Effect of cannabinoids on spasticity and ataxia in MS
- **Petro and Ellenberger (1981)**: Treatment of spasticity with delta-9-THC
- **Ungerleider et al. (1987)**: Delta-9-THC for spasticity in MS
- **Martyn, Illis, and Thom (1995)**: Nabilone in the treatment of MS

## Psychosomatic Research

### Stress and MS Exacerbation
**Ackerman et al. (2002)**: "Stressful life events precede exacerbations of multiple sclerosis." Psychosomatic Medicine 64:916-20.

**Philippopoulos, Wittkower, and Cousineaum (1958)**: "The etiologic significance of emotional factors in onset and exacerbations of multiple sclerosis." Psychosomatic Medicine 20:458-74.

### Psychological Process in MS
**Mei-Tal, Meyerowitz, and Engel (1970)**: "The role of psychological process in a somatic disorder: Multiple sclerosis." Psychosomatic Medicine 32:67-86.

## Food-as-Medicine

### Curcumin and MS
**Natarajan and Bright (2002)**: "Curcumin inhibits experimental allergic encephalomyelitis by blocking IL-12 signaling through Janus kinase-STAT pathway in T lymphocytes."

Curcumin (turmeric) inhibited EAE (the animal model of MS) by blocking the IL-12 signaling pathway -- a key driver of the autoimmune attack on myelin.

## Dosing and Strain Selection

### Sativex Model
The Sativex formulation is roughly 1:1 THC:CBD, delivered as an oromucosal spray. This ratio is well-studied for MS and provides:
- THC for spasticity relief (via CB1)
- CBD for anti-inflammatory and neuroprotective effects (via CB2)
- CBD also moderates THC psychoactivity

### For Daytime Use
Balanced THC:CBD strains at low doses. Start with 1-2 sprays of tincture sublingually. Harlequin (roughly 1:1) approximates the Sativex ratio.

### For Severe Spasticity
Indica-dominant strains with higher THC for nighttime. Granddaddy Purple, Northern Lights.

### For Cognitive Clarity
CBD-dominant strains to avoid THC-induced cognitive effects. ACDC, Cannatonic.

## Practical Considerations

### Starting Dose
Begin very low and go slow. MS patients are often sensitive to THC. Start with CBD-only preparations, then add small amounts of THC.

### Route of Administration
- **Sublingual tinctures**: Fast onset, controllable dosing
- **Oromucosal spray**: Most studied (Sativex model)
- **Oral capsules**: Longer duration but delayed onset
- **Vaporization**: Avoid smoking due to lung concerns

### Drug Interactions
MS patients often take multiple medications. Monitor for interactions with:
- Disease-modifying drugs (interferons, glatiramer)
- Muscle relaxants (baclofen, tizanidine)
- Bladder medications
