# Ethylene: The Ripening and Stress Response Hormone

Ethylene (C₂H₄) is the simplest naturally occurring plant hormone — a gaseous hydrocarbon at [[jadam-ambient-temperature-principle]]. Despite its molecular simplicity, ethylene regulates some of the most commercially important processes in agriculture, including fruit ripening, flower senescence, leaf abscission, and responses to pathogen attack and flooding stress. Its gaseous nature means it can diffuse freely between cells and tissues, and it can also affect neighboring plants.

## Discovery

The effects of ethylene on plants were observed long before the hormone itself was identified. Ancient Chinese and Egyptian cultures practiced burning incense in closed rooms to ripen fruit, unknowingly exploiting ethylene's effects. In the 19th century, street lamps leaking illuminating gas (which contained ethylene) were noticed to cause nearby trees to defoliate. In 1901, the Russian botanist Dimitry Neljubov identified ethylene as the active component of illuminating gas that caused the "triple response" in etiolated seedlings. The compound was definitively identified as a natural plant hormone by Richard Gane in 1934, who showed that apples produce ethylene autonomously.

## Biosynthesis of Ethylene

Ethylene biosynthesis follows the **Yang Cycle** (methionine or Yang cycle), named after Shang Fa Yang who elucidated the pathway:

1. **Methionine → [[s-adenosylmethionine]] (SAM)**: Catalyzed by SAM synthetase
2. **SAM → 1-Aminocyclopropane-1-Carboxylic Acid (ACC)**: Catalyzed by ACC synthase (ACS), the rate-limiting step
3. **ACC → Ethylene**: Catalyzed by ACC oxidase (ACO), an oxygen-dependent enzyme

The regulation of ethylene biosynthesis occurs primarily at the level of ACC synthase, which is encoded by a multigene family with different members induced by different stimuli (auxin, wounding, pathogen attack, flooding). ACC oxidase activity is also regulated and requires oxygen and ferrous iron as cofactors.

An alternative pathway for ethylene production exists in certain conditions (particularly under stress) involving the formation of ethylene from cyanide and other precursors, but the ACC-dependent pathway is dominant in most plant tissues.

Key features of ethylene biosynthesis:
- ACC can be conjugated to malonyl-ACC (MACC), an inactive form that serves as a regulatory pool
- ACC can be transported from roots to shoots through the xylem, allowing ethylene production at distant sites
- The methionine moiety is recycled through the Yang cycle, making the pathway metabolically efficient
- Ethylene production is autoinhibitory in many tissues but autocatalytic in ripening climacteric fruit

## Ethylene Signaling Pathway

Ethylene is perceived by membrane-bound receptors with similarity to bacterial two-component histidine kinases. The signaling pathway in Arabidopsis involves:

1. **Receptors (ETR1, ETR2, EIN4, ERS1, ERS2)**: Endoplasmic reticulum-localized proteins that bind ethylene. In the absence of ethylene, these receptors actively suppress ethylene responses through interaction with CTR1.

2. **CTR1 (Constitutive Triple Response 1)**: A Raf-like serine/threonine kinase that negatively regulates ethylene signaling. CTR1 interacts with the receptors and phosphorylates downstream negative regulators (EIN2) in the absence of ethylene.

3. **EIN2 (Ethylene Insensitive 2)**: A positive regulator. When ethylene binds receptors, CTR1 is inactivated, allowing EIN2 to be processed. The C-terminal end of EIN2 is cleaved and translocates to the nucleus.

4. **EIN3/EIL1 (Ethylene Insensitive 3 / EIN3-Like 1)**: Nuclear transcription factors stabilized by the EIN2 C-terminal fragment. They activate transcription of ethylene-responsive genes, including ERF transcription factors.

5. **ERFs (Ethylene Response Factors)**: AP2/EREBP-family transcription factors that bind to GCC-box elements in promoters of ethylene-responsive genes and activate or repress their expression.

A unique feature of this pathway is negative regulation: in the absence of ethylene, the receptors actively suppress the response. Ethylene binding relieves this suppression. This is unusual among hormone signaling systems.

## The Triple Response

The triple response is the classic bioassay for ethylene activity, observed in etiolated (dark-grown) dicot seedlings exposed to ethylene:

1. **Inhibition of stem elongation**: The hypocotyl stops growing in length
2. **Thickening of the stem**: Lateral cell expansion increases, causing the hypocotyl to swell
3. **Horizontal growth**: The seedling adopts an exaggerated apical hook and grows horizontally rather than vertically

This response is an adaptation that helps seedlings navigate around obstacles in soil. Mutants that do not show the triple response include ein2, ein3, and ctr1, which have been instrumental in dissecting the ethylene signaling pathway.

## Fruit Ripening

Ethylene is the primary trigger for fruit ripening in climacteric fruits (those that show a burst of respiration during ripening):

**Climacteric fruits** (ripening regulated by ethylene): tomato, apple, banana, mango, avocado, pear, peach, melon, kiwifruit

**Non-climacteric fruits** (ripening independent of ethylene): citrus, grape, strawberry, pineapple, cherry, pepper

In climacteric fruits, ethylene triggers a developmental switch from maturation to ripening through:
- Autocatalytic ethylene production (System 2 ethylene synthesis replaces the basal System 1)
- [[chitin-chitosan-fungal-cell-wall]] degradation: upregulation of polygalacturonase, pectin methylesterase, cellulase
- Chlorophyll degradation and carotenoid synthesis (color change from green to ripe)
- Starch-to-sugar conversion (sweetening)
- [[fungal-organic-acid-metal-solubilization]] metabolism (reduction in acidity)
- [[fungal-btex-volatile-compound-degradation]] synthesis (flavor and aroma production)
- Conversion of ACC synthase from regulated to constitutive expression

## Commercial Applications

### Ethylene Promotion
- **Fruit ripening**: Ethylene gas or ethephon (2-chloroethylphosphonic acid, which decomposes to release ethylene) is used to ripen bananas, tomatoes, and other climacteric fruits during transport and retail
- **Latex flow**: Ethylene stimulates latex production in rubber trees (Hevea brasiliensis), applied as ethephon to increase rubber yield
- **Pineapple flowering**: Ethephon treatment induces uniform flowering in pineapple plantations
- **Leaf abscission**: Used to promote leaf drop in cotton before mechanical harvesting

### Ethylene Inhibition
- **1-Methylcyclopropene (1-MCP)**: A competitive inhibitor of ethylene receptors, widely used commercially to extend [[ingham-aact-holding-time-shelf-life-decay]] of apples, kiwifruit, cut flowers, and other perishables. Marketed as SmartFresh, EthylBloc, and similar products
- **Hypobaric storage**: Reduced atmospheric pressure slows ethylene accumulation
- **Potassium permanganate scrubbers**: Remove ethylene from storage atmospheres
- **Controlled atmosphere storage**: Low O₂ and elevated CO₂ suppress ethylene synthesis and action
