# Greenhouse Ventilation Design for Mobile and Sliding Houses

This design provides effective ventilation while maintaining the structural rigidity needed for houses that are physically pushed or pulled between sites.

## Rolling House End-Wall Design Improvement

The move to wheeled greenhouses running on pipe rails enabled a significant improvement in end-wall ventilation design. Since the rail system provides directional stability during moves, the structural crossbar used in sliding houses is no longer necessary. This freed up the entire end wall for better ventilation design.

### Wide Door Opening

The wheeled houses feature a 12-foot-wide opening at both ends, dramatically improving ventilation capacity compared to the narrower door arrangement of sliding houses. Additionally, a narrow 2-foot-wide door is positioned between the large opening and the sidewall for winter access when full ventilation is not needed.

### Roll-Up End Wall

The wide doorway is covered with a plastic sheet attached at the top and sides using the wiggle-wire channel system. The bottom edge is secured with sandbags, following the same approach used for the bottom edges of the rolling houses. Crucially, the plastic sheet has a pipe attached at both the top and bottom, making it function essentially as a roll-up (or roll-down) end wall.

### Three-Mode Ventilation

This design enables three distinct ventilation modes:

**Winter mode:** The wiggle wire is unclipped across the top and upper sides of one or both ends, and the plastic is dropped to open as large an area as required. Because the opening is at the top and upper sides, neither snow nor cold air can blow in directly across the crops at soil level. The openings are closed in the evening.

**Early spring mode:** Once snow is no longer a concern, the door is rolled up from the bottom during the day and rolled down at night, functioning like a conventional roll-up sidewall. When cold air drafting across the crops might be detrimental, a 2-foot-wide piece of plastic is stretched horizontally across the bottom of the door opening as an air barrier.

**Summer mode:** The doors at both ends are rolled up entirely and tied in place, ensuring maximum ventilation for summer crops and easy access for harvesting or [[soil-health-indicators]] for the subsequent crop.

## Integration with the Wiggle-Wire System

The ventilation designs at Four Season Farm all depend on the wiggle-wire (wire-lock) plastic attachment system. This system's ability to quickly secure and release plastic sheeting makes it possible to open and close large ventilation areas as conditions change. Without this attachment method, the adaptive ventilation management that characterizes the winter harvest system would be impractical.

The best wiggle-wire channels are deep enough to accept two or more wires simultaneously. This allows a single channel to serve double duty: securing the end-wall plastic and the top-covering plastic at the overlap point. This detail may seem minor but it simplifies construction and reduces the number of attachment points that could fail.

## Pipe Rail Systems and Ventilation

The transition from sled-runner houses to wheeled houses running on pipe rails enabled ventilation improvements beyond the end-wall design. The pipe rails provide directional stability that allows the removal of structural crossbars, which in turn opens up the entire end wall for ventilation. This is an example of how one design decision cascades through the system, creating possibilities (and eliminating constraints) in unexpected areas.

The pipe rails themselves are made from 20-foot sections connected by smaller-diameter pipe inserted inside. For houses that move only between two adjacent sites, 100 feet of rail per side suffices. For houses that move to four or more sites, the full 200 feet of rail per side eliminates the need to pick up and relay rail sections with each move. Coleman notes that the additional expense of full-length rails was well worth eliminating what he calls the "hassle factor" of relay rails.

## Structural Tradeoffs

Every ventilation design involves tradeoffs between competing needs:

- Wider openings provide better ventilation but require more robust wind bracing
- Higher openings prevent cold-air injury to crops but are harder to seal against winter drafts
- Roll-up mechanisms are convenient but vulnerable to snow and ice
- Removable panels provide flexibility but require storage space and handling time
- Permanent vents simplify management but cannot adapt to changing conditions

The Four Season Farm approach has been to accept some complexity in exchange for adaptability. The three-mode ventilation system of the rolling houses (winter drop-down, spring roll-up, summer full open) requires more management than a simple fixed-vent design, but it allows the same structure to perform optimally across all four seasons.

## Design Principles

The evolution of ventilation design at Four Season Farm illustrates several principles:

- Ventilation openings should be positioned above plant height to prevent cold air from flowing directly across crops
- The same structural features that enable house movement (rigid end walls, secure anchoring) can be designed to serve ventilation needs as well
- Plastic attachment systems (wiggle wire) that allow quick release and re-securing are essential for adaptive ventilation management
- Multiple ventilation modes (winter, shoulder season, summer) should be designed into the structure from the start
- Wind stabilization of large plastic surfaces is necessary for winter reliability
- Structural decisions in one area (like choosing pipe rails over sled runners) have cascading effects on capabilities in other areas (like ventilation design)

These ventilation innovations are integral to making mobile greenhouses practical for year-round use, ensuring that the benefits of winter protection do not come at the cost of inadequate ventilation during warmer periods. The continued evolution of these designs reflects the broader truth that the winter harvest system itself is not static but continues to be refined with each growing season.
