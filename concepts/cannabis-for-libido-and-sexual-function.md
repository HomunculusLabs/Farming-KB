Cannabis has a complex and paradoxical relationship with sexual function, capable of both enhancing and diminishing libido depending on dose, strain, individual psychology, and context. The [[cannabis-subjective-therapeutic-window-dosing]] window:
- **Optimal dose**: Heightened sensation, increased intimacy, enhanced pleasure
- **Too high a dose**: Can deepen anxieties, reduce sensations, and impair performance
- Effects fade as the body metabolizes the plant

User experience teaches to err on the side of caution, beginning with low to medium doses gently and increasing over time. The paradox may be dose-dependent: low doses stimulate while high doses inhibit.

## Strain-Specific Considerations

Anandamide activates CB1 and CB2 receptors relatively equally. Sativas and sativa-heavy hybrids present with higher THC:CBD/CBN ratios, which similarly activate both receptors relatively equally. The balanced receptor activation may contribute to both the sensory enhancement and the emotional intimacy aspects.

Indica strains, with their generally relaxing effects, may be preferred by those whose sexual difficulties are primarily anxiety-driven. Sativa strains, with their stimulating and sensory-enhancing properties, may benefit those seeking heightened physical sensation.

## Mind-Body Medicine and Libido

An ebb and flow of libido is normal and natural. Asexuality is not an illness. However, if a person experiences unwanted loss of libido, underlying causes should be explored.

Research findings:
- Men who suppress anger or hostility toward their partner or women in general have lower testosterone profiles and lower libido
- Sexual dysfunction may surface suppressed emotional tendencies
- Performance anxiety creates a vicious cycle of failure and avoidance
