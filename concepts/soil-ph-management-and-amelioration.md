# Soil pH Management and Amelioration

## Overview

[[permaculture]] designers must understand pH dynamics to make informed decisions about soil amendments, crop selection, and site management. This concept covers the pH scale, acid-alkaline classification of rocks and soils, nutrient availability across pH ranges, and practical amelioration strategies for different soil types and climates.

## The pH Scale

pH is measured on a **logarithmic scale** in which each point represents a 10-fold change in hydrogen ion concentration:

- pH 8 (alkaline) is 10,000 times less acidic than pH 4 (10 × 10 × 10 × 10)
- pH 3 is 10 times more acidic than pH 4
- pH 7 is neutral
- Below 7 is acidic; above 7 is alkaline

This logarithmic nature means that seemingly small pH changes have dramatic effects on soil chemistry and nutrient availability.

## Rock Classification by Silica Content

Parent rocks of soils are classified by their silica (SiO₂) content:

| Classification | Silica Content |
|---------------|---------------|
| **Acid rocks** | 64% or more SiO₂ |
| **Intermediate rocks** | 50–64% SiO₂ |
| **Basic rocks** | 40–50% SiO₂ |
| **Ultrabasic rocks** | Less than 40% SiO₂ |

This classification helps predict the inherent pH tendencies of soils derived from different parent materials.

## Acid and Alkaline Waters and Soils

### Hard vs. Soft Water

- **"Hard" water** (alkaline): Contains calcium (Ca²⁺) or [[iron-cobalt-nickel-copper-zinc-in-soils|Iron]], zinc, aluminium, copper

### Non-metals (release negative OH⁻ ions):
- [[gypsum]]** | Calcium sulphate |
| **Magnesite** | Magnesium carbonate |
| **Dolomite** | Calcium magnesium carbonate |

These are all carbonates, sulphates, or oxides of calcium or magnesium, or both (as in dolomite). Chalk, limestone, calcite, dolomite, magnesite, and gypsum are rocks and minerals giving rise to hard water.

## Nutrient Availability and pH

The availability of plant nutrients varies dramatically across the pH range. Some key relationships:

### More Available in Acid Soils (pH < 6.5):
- Iron (Fe)
- Manganese (Mn)
- Zinc (Zn)
- Copper (Cu)
- Boron (B)

### More Available in Near-Neutral to Slightly Alkaline Soils (pH 6.0–7.5):
- Phosphorus (P) — immobilised by calcium, iron, and aluminium; released at pH 6–7.5
- Nitrogen (N) — general availability is optimal near neutral
- Sulphur (S) — adjusts pH toward acid

### Less Available in Alkaline Soils (pH > 7.5):
- Iron — deficiency causes interveinal leaf yellowing
- Manganese — deficiency in grain crops and vegetables
- Zinc — critical for [[tree-establishment-in-deserts]]
- Copper — deficiency on coastal sands and basalts
- Boron — deficiency in brassicas and beets

## Soil Amelioration Guide

### Sandy Soils

| Condition | Alkaline | Acid |
|-----------|----------|------|
| **General** | Add magnesium sulphate as spray at 10 g/L, or dolomite at 100–300 g/m². Neutralise with lime at 20 g/m² | Add dolomite (for Ca, Mg) |
| **Humid** | Add ammonium sulphate or fine dolomite. Add zinc every 7 years at 1 g/m². Add water-retaining polymers | Add water-retaining polymers, gypsum, sulphur. Use copper-based sprays sparingly |
| **Non-wetting** | Add 10 g/m² bentonite or montmorillonite, clays, polymers | Add bentonite, polymers |
| **Deserts** | Add ferrous sulphate (for Fe) at 23 g/L as foliar spray. Add polymers | Add dolomite, gypsum, polymers |

### Coastal Sands

| Condition | Alkaline | Acid |
|-----------|----------|------|
| **General** | Add copper sulphate every 7–14 years at 1 g/m² if cropped. Add zinc sulphate every 7–14 years at 1 g/m² | Add neutralised copper sulphate every 7–14 years at 1 g/m². Add zinc sulphate 2:1 with lime at 1 g/m² every 7–14 years. Do not add sulphur unless absent |

### Loams

| Condition | Alkaline | Acid |
|-----------|----------|------|
| **Tropics** | Add silicate or spray silica solutions | Add cement (calcium silicate) at 100–400 g/m² for coarse textures |
| **Deserts** | Add dolomite, gypsum | Add dolomite, gypsum |
| **General** | Add potash if cropped. Add zinc sulphate | Add potash if cropped |
| **Coasts** | Add copper sulphate 1 g/m² every 7–14 years | Add neutralised copper sulphate every 7–14 years at 1 g/m² |
