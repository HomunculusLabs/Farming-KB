## Harvesting and Propagation

Trichocereus cacti are relatively quick growing and easy to propagate
from cuttings. Each plant may produce one or more doses worth per year.
When harvesting, the top four inches of growing tip is cut off for
replanting. A length beneath this is cut for consumption. The remaining
stub continues to grow, frequently putting out several branches where
it was cut. The tip cutting should be laid on its side in the sun until
the exposed flesh calluses, then placed upright in a half soil/half
sand mixture with good drainage.

## Preparation Method

Turner's preparation method for [[mescaline-peyote-and-san-pedro-guide]]:

1. Cut out the spines, which is fairly easy on most Trichocereus
   species.
2. Cut the skin off in "V" strips, cutting from the outside of each
   rib into the central section of the cactus.
3. Save these V strips as they contain the most potent flesh. The
   skin itself is thick, waxy, and not edible.
4. The dark green flesh next to the skin is the most potent part and
   should be eaten first.
5. Flatten out the fleshy sections for efficient consumption.

The taste ranges from very bitter to tasteless, with less tasty
varieties having a slimy consistency. The texture is the main obstacle
to consuming large quantities. Turner chewed the cactus to a pulp and
washed it down with liquid, eating full-grain bread to absorb excess
stomach liquid. The process is "definitely not fun" but tolerable for
experienced users.
