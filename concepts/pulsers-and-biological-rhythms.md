## Pulsers and Biological Rhythms

## Overview

Pulsers are fundamental organizing phenomena in nature that regulate the timing, duration, and cessation of flows and processes. From the beating of the human heart to the 11-year solar cycle, pulsing is a universal characteristic of living and physical systems. Understanding pulsers is essential in [[dictyostelium]]* uses this pattern to move towards a pivot point where individuals construct a multi-cellular organism that crawls away to complete the life cycle — a process resembling the precursor of hormonal control in the nervous system. Some such process may assemble more complex multi-species organisms.

## Pulsers in the Human Body

### Heart and Circulation
Pulsers act in concert to create peristaltic or heart contractions. When they receive correct signals, they maintain the synchronized rhythm essential for pumping blood. If they receive wrong signals, pulsers can move out of phase and send the organ into seizure.

### Ventricular Fibrillation
Ventricular fibrillation — a potentially fatal quivering of the heart — may derive from the spasm effect distributed over heart or nerve tissue, causing "ineffectual churning." Spasms can damage blood vessel cells and cause build-up of scar tissue or cholesterol at injury sites, or hard waste products in muscle tissue.

### Heartbeat Quota
All mammals have an allotted number of heartbeats in relation to their body size. When these are exhausted, the organism dies. This fundamental relationship between body size, metabolic rate, and lifespan is a direct consequence of pulsing as a life-limiting mechanism.

## Shapers and Timers

Species and individual organisms need both **shapers** and **timers** to achieve a specific size and form:

- **Shapers (DNA)**: Encode the spatial pattern — the correct angles, placements, and proportions of parts such as fingers and toes. Branching patterns must have correctly encoded main branch angles and placements, with room for sideshoots and forks but not for interweaves or cross-points that would damage function.
- **Timers (biological clocks)**: Regulate the temporal dimension — when growth starts, proceeds, and stops. They determine that a toe will stop at being a toe, not grow into a monstrous appendage or stay as a midget toe.

The two must work synchronously to achieve correct proportions. Both are critical to the organism.

## Circadian Rhythms

### The Pineal Gland and Melatonin
The 24-hour rhythm (circadian) of birds is controlled by the [[pineal-gland]], which secretes a regular nocturnal pulse of the hormone **melatonin**. The changing levels of melatonin trigger the annual cycles of breeding and nest-building in birds. Visual perception of light changes and day lengths regulate melatonin production in the pineal gland.

Even small pieces of the pineal gland in isolation will respond to light, and can be disrupted by flashes of light such as lightning at night. This reveals that not only expansion but **discharge phenomena** such as lightning or sudden shock disrupt or trigger initiatory reactions in life rhythms, introducing irregularities in cycles or pulsers.

## Solar Pulsing

The sun itself "pulses" approximately every 11 years, affecting ozone and climatic factors such as rainfall. Our own pulses have characteristic or normal resting rates, as do peristaltic or visceral movements. The universality of pulsing suggests it is a fundamental property of energy flow in all systems, from molecular to planetary scale.

## The Growth Question

Mollison relates a telling anecdote: when his four-year-old son pointed to his toes in the bath and asked "Why do these toes stop at being toes? Why don't they get bigger and bigger or longer and longer?" — the question goes to the heart of what limits size and growth.

The answer lies in the interaction of pulsers and patterns:
1. **Pulsers limit duration**: Growth proceeds for a predetermined period then stops
2. **Patterns limit form**: DNA-encoded templates define the final shape and proportions
3. **The two are synchronized**: Correct form requires both correct timing and correct spatial encoding

## Atmospheric and Planetary Pulsing

Counter-rotating spirals serve both centripetal and centrifugal functions in the atmosphere as high and low pressure cells. This same pulsing pattern appears at every scale:
- **Molecular**: Chemical reaction spirals in petri dishes
- **Cellular**: Peristaltic motion in single-celled organisms
- **Organ**: Heart contraction, gut motility
- **Organism**: Circadian rhythms, breeding cycles
- **Planetary**: Atmospheric circulation cells, solar cycles

## Design Implications

For permaculture designers, understanding pulsers suggests several principles:

1. **Work with natural timing**: Plant, harvest, and manage systems in accordance with natural cycles rather than against them
2. **Design for pulse duration**: Systems should have built-in periods of activity and rest
3. **Respect synchrony**: Components in a design must have compatible timing — mismatched rhythms cause stress and failure
4. **Use pulse disruption carefully**: Sudden shocks (mechanical, chemical, thermal) can reset or disrupt biological rhythms with consequences for growth and health
5. **Size-life relationships**: Smaller organisms pulse faster and live shorter lives; this affects succession planning and system design timescales

The interplay of shapers and timers is what gives every living event its characteristic time-shape memory. Rivers, volcanoes, and the sun itself appear to carry similar temporal-structural memories encoded in their pulsing patterns.
