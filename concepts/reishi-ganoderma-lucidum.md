## Taxonomy

Ganoderma lucidum is the type species of the genus Ganoderma. It grows on
oaks and other hardwoods. Two close relatives, G. tsugae (growing on
hemlocks) and G. oregonense (Pacific Northwest conifers), have been
questionably separated based primarily on habitat. In culture, G. lucidum
and G. tsugae readily fruit on both hardwood and conifer sawdust mixtures,
and all three species can develop elongated stems under environmental
manipulation, making habitat-based delineation suspect. The spore size of G.
lucidum is smaller (typically less than 13 x 7.5 micrometers) compared to
G. oregonense and G. tsugae (13-17 x 7.5-10 micrometers).

### Environmental Responsiveness
Ganoderma lucidum is remarkably responsive to environmental conditions.
Stem elongation is promoted under elevated CO2 levels above 20,000 ppm,
while cap development and hymenial formation activate when CO2 falls below
2,000 ppm. This allows cultivators to intentionally shape the fruitbody,
producing either conk forms (low CO2, high [[growing-gourmet-growing-room-ventilation-air-exchange]]) or antler forms
(high CO2, restricted air exchange). The antler form is preferred in
traditional Chinese medicine and has a sweeter, more soothing flavor.
