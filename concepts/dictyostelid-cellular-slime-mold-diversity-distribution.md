## Ecological Significance

### Role in Soil Food Webs

Dictyostelids are significant bacterial predators in soil ecosystems. As heterotrophic amoebae, they consume bacteria and contribute to nutrient cycling and energy flow through soil food webs. Their population dynamics are closely linked to bacterial abundance and the availability of suitable microhabitats.

### Indicators of Soil Health

Because dictyostelid diversity correlates with soil moisture, organic matter content, and general biological activity, they have been proposed as potential bioindicators of soil health and ecosystem integrity. Changes in dictyostelid community composition may reflect shifts in soil conditions due to land use, pollution, or climate change.

### Model Organisms

Dictyostelium discoideum has become one of the most important model organisms in cell and developmental biology. Research on this species has contributed to understanding of:

- Chemotaxis and signal transduction
- Cell-type differentiation
- Programmed cell death
- **Evolution of multicellularity and social behavior**
- Kin discrimination and altruism

## Knowledge Gaps

Several major gaps remain:

1. **Tropical regions**: Despite highest diversity, tropical forests remain undersampled
2. **Southern Hemisphere**: Australia, Africa, and South America need comprehensive surveys
3. **Ecological interactions**: Predator-prey dynamics with bacteria and between dictyostelid species are poorly known
4. **Molecular systematics**: DNA-based approaches are revealing [[soil-nutrient-cycling]] is quantified only roughly
6. **Climate sensitivity**: Responses to warming, drying, and land-use change are largely unknown
