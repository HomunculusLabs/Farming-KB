## Fungal Degradation of Synthetic Dyes

Synthetic dyes are widely used in textile dyeing, paper printing, colour
photography, and petroleum products. Azo dyes, anthraquinone dyes,
triarylmethane dyes, and phthalocyanine dyes represent the [[mollison-designers-wastewater-treatment-wetlands]] and
persist in the environment, making [[fungal-lignin-peroxidase-veratryl-alcohol]] (LiP) preparations decolorized all
dyes tested except Congo Red, suggesting involvement of enzymes other
than LiP in the degradation of that particular dye.

The azo dyes 4-phenylazophenol, Disperse Yellow 3, Disperse Orange 3,
Solvent Yellow 14, and several N,N-dimethyl derivatives were extensively
degraded by P. chrysosporium under nitrogen-limiting conditions, as
demonstrated by radiolabelling of ring carbons. Notably, some dyes were
also degraded under nitrogen-sufficient non-ligninolytic conditions,
indicating the involvement of non-lignin-modifying enzymes in the
degradation of at least some azo dyes.

## Lignin Peroxidase in Dye Decolorization

Disperse Yellow 3, a carcinogenic azo dye, was oxidized by LiPs and
manganese peroxidases (MnPs) to yield 4-methyl-1-2-benzoquinone,
acetanilide, and a dimer of the parent dye. LiP from Bjerkandera adusta
mediated efficient decolorization of industrial dyes Reactive Violet 5,
Reactive Black 5, Reactive Orange 96, Reactive Red 198, and Reactive Blue
38 and 15. Efficient decolorization of azo and phthalocyanine dyes by
LiP from B. adusta occurred in the presence of veratryl alcohol and not
in its absence, indicating the mediator requirement for this enzyme
system.
