# Willow Growing Guide

Willows (Salix spp.) are versatile, fast-growing trees with extensive root systems that make them excellent for [[cervantes-rooting-hormones-cloning]] (salicylic acid) that promote root development. Willow water (made by soaking willow cuttings in water) is used as a natural rooting solution for other plant cuttings.

## Medicinal Uses

Willow bark has been used medicinally for thousands of years, dating back to ancient Egyptian, Greek, and Chinese herbal traditions. The active compound salicin is metabolized in the body to salicylic acid, the same anti-inflammatory compound found in aspirin. White willow (*Salix alba*) bark is the preferred medicinal species, containing the highest concentrations of salicin. Traditional preparations involve steeping dried bark shavings in hot water for pain relief, fever reduction, and inflammation. Modern herbalists recommend standardised extracts containing 15% salicin. Willow bark preparations are generally gentler on the stomach than synthetic aspirin, though they should be avoided by people with aspirin sensitivity.
