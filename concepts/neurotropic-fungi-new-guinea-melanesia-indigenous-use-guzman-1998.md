## Gasteromycetes in New Guinea

Six species of gasteromycetes (puffballs and related fungi from the orders
Lycoperdales and Phallales) in three genera are listed as sacred fungi from
New Guinea and Melanesia. These include the striking **cage fungi**
(*Clathrus*, *Ileodictyon*) and **stinkhorns** (*Phallus*), which are
among the most morphologically dramatic fungi in the tropics.

## Biogeographical Context: The South Pacific Connection

New Guinea's position in the southwestern Pacific places it at the
intersection of the Asian and Australian biogeographical realms. The
island's extraordinary biodiversity — both floral and faunal — extends to
its mycota. Horak (1983) noted that South American and Mesoamerican agarics
and boletes are often closely related to those of southeastern Asia, New
Guinea, Indonesia, and Australia.

This South Pacific distribution pattern is reflected in the *Psilocybe*
species of the region. The close relationships between New Guinean/New
Zealand species and Mexican/Central American species (e.g., *P. samuiensis*
— Thailand ↔ *P. mexicana* — Mexico; *P. aucklandii* — New Zealand ↔
*P. zapotecorum* — Mexico/South America) suggest ancient dispersal events
or vicariance patterns that predate modern continental configurations.

## Underexplored Status

Despite the foundational work of Singer, Heim, and Wasson, the mycota of
New Guinea remains severely underexplored. The island contains some of the
world's most extensive tropical rainforests and montane cloud forests —
precisely the habitat types most associated with *Psilocybe* diversity in
other parts of the world. The true number of neurotropic species in New
Guinea is likely far higher than currently documented, and additional
indigenous communities may maintain mushroom traditions that have not yet
been recorded by science.
