## Plant Stacking and Plant-Animal Interactions in Permaculture

Plant stacking (vertical diversity) and the synergistic interactions between plants, animals, and inorganic elements represent the biological core of [[permaculture-design]]. These concepts distinguish [[permaculture]] from conventional agriculture by treating every element as part of a complex web of relationships rather than as an isolated production unit.

## The Stacking of Plants

Mollison quotes Williams' evocative description of Mediterranean multi-tier agriculture:

> "The long serpentine trunks of the palm tree rise above every village and about every field... It is the first line of defence against the sun in the open fields, and in its shade grows the olive tree. Under the olive, the fig grows, and under the fig, the pomegranate and vine, then the grain and vegetables."

This is not a crowded forest where plants compete for light and moisture, but a carefully structured system where each layer occupies a distinct niche.

### Strategies for Increasing Plant Density

Three strategies allow designers to fit more plants into an area:

1. **Preventing water loss**: Building high shade, hedge and trenched wall systems that reduce evaporation by wind
2. **Introducing drought-resistant species**: Cacti and other drought-adapted plants, especially as windbreak hedges, have greater resistance than normal useful plants
3. **Vertical moisture barriers**: Buried sheet-plastic barriers between the garden and dry adjacent soil

### The Key Principle

"An environment can in this way be made to accommodate many species without competition between them" (Watt). The main modification—a very important one—is that a well-occupied system resists invasion by rampant forms (blackbirds and blackberries). Initial diversity plus lack of disturbance preserve the diversity-stability dynamism.

### The Importance of Full Occupation

"I cannot stress too much the importance of keeping a small area fully occupied with plants, as a strategy to reduce work." A fully occupied system resists weed invasion and reduces maintenance. Mollison notes that systems need intervention only when they begin to "simplify" themselves—approximately 60 years for long-lived elements, shorter periods for small or short-lived ones.

### Evolved Order vs. Imposed Order

Neil Douglas gardens on a droughted Victorian foothill where trees are placed to cast shade on, rather than compete with, the myriad plant species. Such gardens arrive after years of trials where species indicate their preferences, "often in defiance of the dictates of literature. It is fortunate indeed that plants cannot read!"

## Plant-Plant Interactions

### Seven Types of Plant Relationships

1. **Trellis**: Grape on mulberry, fig, boxthorn
2. **Screening and shade**: Coffee under palms
3. **Nutrient provision**: [[comfrey]] leaves for potato sets
4. **Cross-fertilization**: Different varieties of plums and nuts
5. **Obligate relationships**: Fungi under oaks and pines
6. **Rejection or acceptance**: [[companion-planting]] interactions
7. **Spare parts (grafting)**: Apple, pear, nut species

### Plant Defense Guilds

Drawing from Atsatt and O'Dowd's "plant defense guilds" research (Science, 1976), Mollison identifies three categories:

**1. Plants that breed predators for other plants' pests**
- Phacelia in orchards reduces Prospatella pest incidence
- Sorghum or lucerne interplanted with cotton
- Umbelliferae (dill, fennel) with brassicas (cabbage, cauliflower)
- Strawberries near peach orchards
- Wild blackberry near grapes
- Conversely, Berberis carries rust disease from grain and acts as re-infection reservoir

**2. Plants that repel browsers or pests**
- Ranunculus protects grasses from cattle (lactone ranunculin irritates herbivore mucous tissue)
- Trifolium fragiferum protects white clover from hares
- Haplopappus tenuirostris deters or kills feral donkeys and horses
- Rhododendron and azalea shoots kill feral goats

**3. Attractant or decoy plants**
- Nepenthes (catmint) has fatal attraction to cats
- Solanum (S. nigrum) with potatoes attracts Colorado beetle egg masses as lethal decoy
- [[wallaby]] in Tasmania attracted to Pittosporum bicolor
- Meat flies attracted to Arum maculatum ("dead donkey" lily)
- Gladiolus traps and clears onion rot by preventing fungus sporulation

### Dispersal vs. Monoculture

"Plants scattered amongst others appear scattered to pests, but (once pests breed on them) the 'scattered' plants, surrounded as they are by other and insectary species, appear to the predator as concentrated food sources." Dispersal of plants among varied species "leads to far less pest infestation in all cases studied."

### Root and Gaseous Interactions

- Apples and dandelions release ethylene gas that causes pineapples and bananas to ripen
- Quandong germinates and establishes only in root association with another plant
- Marigolds prevent nematode attack on citrus roots
- Deep shade under Coprosma prevents weed establishment
- Phenols from bracken, walnuts, and pine trees act as growth inhibitors
- Grasses secrete substances that kill young trees

### Manurial Factors

- Comfrey and coprosma provide potash for potatoes (as mulch or trenched leaves)
- [[canary-island-tree-lucerne-mollison]] and Leucaena provide essential nitrogen as mulch
- Smaller legumes provide nitrogen through root nodulation
- Deep-rooted and mat-rooted perennials probe below leached upper soil levels

## Animal Functions in Permaculture

Animals provide ten categories of useful function:

1. **Producers**: Meat, fibres, eggs, down from materials of little direct use to man
2. **Manure providers**: High quality manures from man's wastes
3. **Pollinators and foragers**: Collecting dispersed materials across the permaculture
4. **Heat sources**: Body heat for greenhouses and barns
5. **Gas producers**: CO₂ and methane for enclosed systems
6. **Living tractors**: Poultry, pigs, and fish as soil-turning, weeding, manuring machines
7. **Draught animals**: Operating pumps and vehicles
8. **Pioneer bulldozers**: Clearing and manuring difficult areas prior to planting
9. **[[beneficial-insects-and-biological-pest-control]]**: Devouring pupae and eggs of pests in fallen fruit and shrubs
10. **Nutrient concentrators**: Nitrogen and phosphates from flies and wasps
11. **Water cleansing filters**
12. **Short grazers**: Aiding in fire control

## Population Management

All [[fungal-pathogens-animal-populations]] produce surplus from natural increase when managed as breeders. Like crowded forests, too many animals become non-productive. Dense populations are self-thinned by starvation, crowding stress, or epidemic mortality.

### The Fishery Fallacy

Mollison challenges "minimum size" regulations in fisheries: "We should throw back the breeders and utilize the small fish. No farmer could survive if he killed breeding cattle, and no fishery will thrive under the same mismanagement." Selection pressure should operate on old animals, not young ones.

### The Role of Grazing Animals

"To exclude animals from agricultural areas is to kill them in the long term." In the tropics, animals are essential to "detour" tree leaves and monsoon grasses to nutrient-rich, available manures. Rank grasses may swamp gardens if not controlled by browsing species.

Mollison notes that religious proscriptions against cattle slaughter "have (in their native climate) a sound ecological basis, in that cattle in monsoon areas are an essential adjunct to agriculture." However, such restrictions "do not transplant well to every climate, and we should adopt a local morality."

## The Design Challenge

"Permaculturists can look on garden design as an intellectual exercise, with many more moves and infinitely more pieces than man or computer can deal with." The goal is to "design and strengthen the plant defense guilds which naturally exist" by understanding how they work.

"Plants without animal foragers are like strawberries without cream, or men without women." The interplay between plants and animals is always beneficial when properly designed. "Just as we can assemble plant sequences, so we can assemble beneficial associations of animal species, and integrate plant with animal systems to mutual advantage."
