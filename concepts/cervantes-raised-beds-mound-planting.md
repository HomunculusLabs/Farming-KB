# Raised Beds and Mound Planting for Cannabis

Raised beds and mound planting are essential
techniques for outdoor cannabis growers dealing
with poor native soil, inadequate drainage, or
challenging terrain. These methods allow growers
to create optimal growing conditions even in
clay-heavy, compacted, or waterlogged ground.

## Why Use Raised Beds

Raised beds solve several common outdoor growing
problems. They improve drainage in heavy clay
soils, prevent waterlogging around roots, allow
soil to warm faster in spring, reduce soil
compaction from foot traffic, and give growers
total control over the soil mix. In areas where
native soil is poor, raised beds let you import
the perfect growing medium without excavating
large areas.

## Raised Beds for Clay Soil

Raised beds are an excellent option for clay soil.
Till the clay when it is damp and workable, and
add manure and compost in heaps; plant directly
in the mounds. The elevated position improves
drainage immediately, while the organic amendments
lighten the soil structure and promote root growth.

Pile subsoil in a ring around the plant, making a
bowl shape to catch rain water. This technique
combines the benefits of raised bed drainage with
passive water collection, reducing the need for
frequent irrigation visits to remote guerrilla
patches.

## Soil Mix for Raised Beds

Once you have amended soil so it holds plenty of
water and nutrients but still drains well, you can
take it easy. The ideal raised bed soil combines
the moisture retention of clay with the drainage
and workability of sand, similar to loam found in
ancient river bottoms and lake beds where
sedimentary soil builds up.

For remote or guerrilla gardens, prepare raised
beds by digging holes two to three feet square
(60-90 cm) and mounding amended soil above ground
level. This creates a well-drained planting area
with enriched soil, separated from the surrounding
native ground. Allow amended soil to sit for a
month or longer before planting if possible.

## Containers as Portable Raised Beds

Container growing is essentially raised bed
cultivation on a small scale. Using large containers
or grow bags lets you control the soil mix
completely and move plants if necessary. Ensure
containers have adequate drainage holes. See
[[cervantes-container-gardening-pot-size]] for
detailed container guidance.

## Mound Planting Technique

Mound planting is a simpler alternative to formal
raised beds. Create mounds of amended soil directly
on the ground surface. The mound height can vary
from a few inches to a foot or more, depending on
drainage needs. This technique is especially
effective in:

- Bog and marsh environments where the water table
  is high. Cut a square yard of moist sod, turn it
  over, and plant in the exposed soil.

- Clay-heavy areas where surface drainage is poor.
  The mound elevates roots above the slow-draining
  subsurface layer.

- Rocky or thin mountain soils. Mounds of imported
  soil provide enough depth for root development.

- Areas with compacted or hardpan layers that would
  otherwise block root penetration.

## Mulching Raised Beds

Apply mulch to raised beds and mounds to conserve
moisture, suppress weeds, and regulate soil
temperature. Organic mulches like straw, leaves,
or compost break down over time, adding to the
soil's organic content. Rock or gravel mulch can
also moderate soil temperature and prevent erosion
on sloped beds. See
[[cervantes-mulch-outdoor-moisture-conservation]]
for detailed mulching guidance.

## Amendments for Raised Beds

Backfill raised beds and planting mounds with a
rich mix of native soil, compost, aged manure, and
organic amendments. For mountain and alpine
gardens, use a mix of peat moss, soil, polymer
crystals, and slow-release organic fertilizer to
help plants deal with stress from cold, wind, and
poor native soil fertility.
