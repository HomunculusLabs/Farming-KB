# Mushroom Species Selection Guide
Choosing which mushroom species to cultivate is one of the most consequential
decisions a grower makes. The overall challenge depends on skill level,
available infrastructure, climate, and substrate access. An incremental
approach that builds complexity over time is the most reliable path to success
in [[mushroom-pests-woodlice]].
## Key Selection Criteria
### Ease of Cultivation
For beginners, three species on three basic substrates are recommended:
shiitakes on logs, king stropharia on wood chips, and oyster mushrooms on
agricultural waste. Practicing with these simple combinations builds
confidence, capabilities, and intuition before attempting more demanding
species like [[cotter-morel-cultivation-research]] or maitake.
Species like maitake (Grifola frondosa) require supplemented sterilized
sawdust, a mild cold shock, high carbon dioxide during initiation, and a
high-oxygen maturation phase. Morels add flooding, freezing, and a sclerotia
stage. Each mushroom has unique "pickiness" factors that determine its
difficulty ranking.
### Temperature Range and Sensitivity
Mushroom strains vary in their ability to fruit or survive at specific
temperature thresholds. Seeking local ecotypes already acclimated to regional
conditions is the best strategy. Attempting indoor cultivation of a mushroom
unsuited to your climate requires expensive year-round temperature control,
which can be unsustainable.
### Broad vs. Specific Substrates
Many mushrooms produce a spectrum of enzymes enabling them to break down
diverse substrates. Others are more limited in their enzymatic capabilities.
Species often have strains and ecotypes evolved for particular ecological
niches with specific substrate affinities. Culturing native ecotypes on
locally available materials has proven the most successful strategy.
### Time to Maturation and Yield
Fast-fruiting species with high yields are more economical since they occupy
less space for shorter periods. Slower, lower-yielding species generally
command higher market prices, reflecting the greater cost of production.
Pricing should account for both cultivation difficulty and the time and space
dedicated to each crop.
### Infrastructure Requirements
Methods range from completely natural outdoor cultivation to tightly
controlled indoor environments with precise gas exchange requirements.
Infrastructure influences and is influenced by cultivation method. Begin
with whatever equipment is available and scale up as skills develop.
## Temperature Classification of Species
### Cold-Tolerant Species (32-59F / 0-15C)
Cold-tolerant species suitable for logs and stumps include birch polypore,
brick top, cauliflower, chicken of the woods, enoki, lion's mane, maitake,
nameko, cold-tolerant oysters, cold-tolerant shiitakes, and tiger sawgill.
For sawdust cultivation, enoki, lion's mane, maitake, and nameko are options.
Blewits and shaggy manes work well on composts.
### Temperate Species (60-85F / 16-29C)
This is the widest category. Logs and stumps support beefsteak, black poplar,
cauliflower, chicken of the woods, hairy panus, temperate oysters, reishi,
scaly lentinus, shiitakes, tiger sawgill, turkey tail, and wood ear. Wood
chips support king stropharia. Composts suit almond portabella, king
stropharia, parasol, and shaggy mane. Agricultural waste works for elm oyster,
king stropharia, parasol, and temperate oysters.
### Tropical Species (86-100+F / 30-38+C)
Logs support hairy panus, tropical oysters, reishi, scaly lentinus, tropical
shiitakes, turkey tail, and wood ear. Agricultural waste suits giant
macrocybe, tropical oysters, and paddy straw mushrooms. Tropical species are
excellent candidates for low-tech cultivation in developing regions.

## Strategic Considerations
### Primary vs. Secondary Decomposers
[[cotter-mushroom-life-cycle-mycelium-biology]] function as
primary or secondary decomposers. Primary decomposers like oyster mushrooms
efficiently break down lignin and cellulose from raw substrates. Secondary
decomposers like Agaricus species prefer partially decomposed material.
Cascading substrates by growing primary decomposers first, then secondary
decomposers on spent media, maximizes resource efficiency.
### Ecotype Matching
Within each species, ecotypes adapted to local conditions perform far better
than imported strains. Local ecotypes are familiar with regional climate
fluctuations and are most compatible with native substrates. This principle
applies whether cultivating in temperate, tropical, or subtropical regions.
### Starting Strategy
The recommended progression begins with three foundation species (shiitake,
king stropharia, oyster) on their preferred substrates. As skill and
confidence grow, introduce one or two moderately difficult species. Only
after achieving consistent results should growers attempt the most
challenging species. This incremental approach minimizes costly failures
while building the intuition essential for advanced [[mushroom-cultivation-in-kenya]].
