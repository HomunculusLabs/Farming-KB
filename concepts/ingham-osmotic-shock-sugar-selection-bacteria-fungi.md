## Overview

## The Osmotic Shock Mechanism

As sugar concentration increases in a solution, the water molecules become increasingly bound to the sugar molecules through osmotic forces. This effectively reduces the availability of free water to microorganisms. Fast-growing bacteria, which rely on rapid water uptake for cell division, are disproportionately affected by this water restriction.

Beneficial fungi possess cell wall structures and metabolic mechanisms that allow them to tolerate higher osmotic conditions. As sugar concentration rises, bacteria face increasing difficulty obtaining water, while fungi continue to function. This gives fungi a competitive edge at elevated sugar concentrations.

## Concentration Zones and Their Effects

### Low Concentration (below 0.5 percent)

At low sugar concentrations, there is insufficient energy to drive significant microbial growth. Both bacteria and fungi grow modestly. Oxygen demand remains low and most brewing systems can easily maintain aerobic conditions. The resulting tea tends to have moderate bacterial biomass with limited fungal growth.

### The Danger Zone (0.5 to 3 percent)

This concentration range presents the greatest risk of anaerobic conditions. There is enough sugar to fuel explosive bacterial growth, but not enough osmotic pressure to inhibit the rapidly-dividing bacteria. The bacterial bloom consumes [[compost-tea-brewing]] wing.
