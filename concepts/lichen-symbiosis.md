## Nitrogen Fixation

When the photobiont is a cyanobacterium rather than an alga, lichens
gain the ability to fix atmospheric nitrogen, converting it into forms
usable by other organisms. Cyanolichens are particularly important in
nitrogen-poor environments such as arctic tundra and old-growth forests,
where they contribute significantly to the nitrogen budget of the
ecosystem. This nitrogen-fixing capability makes them ecological
cornerstones in habitats where few other organisms can access this
essential nutrient.

## Reproduction and Dispersal

The fungal nature of lichens is most evident during reproduction, when
cup-shaped apothecia develop on the surface and blast ascospores into
the air. Lichens also reproduce vegetatively through fragmentation or
through specialized dispersal structures called soredia and isidia,
which contain both [[fungal-hyphae]] and algal cells, allowing the
complete symbiotic partnership to be dispersed together.
