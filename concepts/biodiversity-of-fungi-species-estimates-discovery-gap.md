# Fungal Species Estimates and the Discovery Gap

The fungal kingdom represents one of the most significant yet poorly documented
domains of biodiversity on Earth. Current estimates suggest that between 74,000
and 120,000 fungal species have been formally described, yet the total number of
extant species is conservatively estimated at approximately 1.5 million (Hawksworth
1991). This means that at most only about 5% of all fungal species have been
discovered and named. Some authorities consider even the 1.5 million figure to be
too low, with evidence from tropical plant studies and molecular investigations
suggesting the actual number could be substantially higher.
