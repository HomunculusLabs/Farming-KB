## Chemical Structure and Classification

### Psilocybin and Psilocin

Psilocybin and psilocin are indolealkylamines (tryptamines), placing them in the same broad chemical class as LSD and DMT. Their structural similarity to the neurotransmitter serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine, or 5-HT) is the basis for their pharmacological activity:

- **Psilocybin:** 4-phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine (4-PO-DMT) — the phosphate ester of psilocin
- **Psilocin:** 4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine (4-HO-DMT) — the pharmacologically active compound

Psilocybin is more stable in air and is water soluble, making it the primary form found in fresh and properly dried mushrooms. After ingestion, psilocybin is rapidly dephosphorylated by alkaline phosphatase enzymes to produce psilocin, which is the actual psychoactive compound that acts on the brain.

Psilocybin-containing mushrooms fall exclusively within the tryptamine class, sharing their primary mechanism of action with LSD despite having a different chemical structure.

### Hallucinogens vs. Pseudo-Hallucinogens

An important distinction made in the pharmacological literature:
- **True hallucinogens:** Produce visual disturbances and perceptual distortions through 5-HT2A agonism (e.g., psilocybin, LSD, mescaline)
- **Pseudo-hallucinogens:** Produce psychotic and delirious effects without classic visual disturbances (e.g., anticholinergic compounds like scopolamine)

Psilocybin is classified as a true hallucinogen, with its effects primarily mediated through the serotonin system rather than through anticholinergic or dopaminergic pathways.

## Pharmacokinetics

### Absorption and Metabolism

After oral ingestion, psilocybin is absorbed in the GI tract, converted to psilocin by alkaline phosphatase enzymes, crosses the blood-brain barrier, and produces effects beginning within 20-60 minutes, peaking at 1-2 hours, and gradually subsiding over 4-6 hours.
