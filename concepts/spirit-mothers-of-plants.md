# Spirit Mothers of Plants

In Amazonian vegetalismo, every plant possesses a spirit or "mother"
(madre) -- a conscious being that guards, teaches, and sometimes
punishes those who interact with the plant. These spirit mothers are
the conservers and caretakers of the vegetation. As Pablo Amaringo
states: "They are the ones that have taken over these plants. They
are the conservers, they take care of the plants." The mothers do
not give visions to persons who fail to comply with the requirements
of the ciencia vegetalista.

## The Nature of Plant Spirits

Plant spirits appear in ayahuasca visions in anthropomorphic form --
often as women, kings, or animals. The sacha-huarmi (women of the
forest) appear in visions as seated women in conversation, their
true nature being plants found in certain Amazonian locations where
large rocks are present. These spirits are very hard to find and
recognise. They are the channels through which
[[amazonian-plant-teachers-doctores]] communicate their
knowledge to the vegetalista.

## Remocaspi and Its Wise King

The remocaspi tree (Aspidosperma excelsum) has as its spirit a wise
old Chinese king wearing a golden crown. He bestows sublime wisdom
upon those who have prepared themselves properly through diet. He
holds a sceptre of death and will bring down its power upon anyone
who has not followed the strict dietary regime that ingesting this
plant requires. This plant's cathartic medicine facilitates knowledge
of magical esoteric sciences.

## Puka-Lupuna and the Sorcery Princess

The puka-lupuna tree (Cavanillesia hylogeiton), also known as lupuna
cobrada (red lupuna), has a mother-spirit who is a woman of dark
complexion with cat eyes and a gold chain around her neck. She is
primarily useful to sorcerers. The knowledge she bestows is "almost
always turned towards sorcery and very seldom towards curanderos
that heal and save lives." This tree demands a strict and rigorous
diet; immediate death awaits the unprepared.

## Ajosquiro and the Ferocious Monkeys

The ajosquiro tree (Galizia corazema) has a spirit that appears as
a very small curly-haired man wearing a red cape and red clothes.
Few people ingest this plant because the diet it requires is very
rigid and severe, symbolised by the alicomachines -- a group of
ferocious monkeys who hang by their tails and jump wildly. This
medicine is used as a defence against enemies. Those who ingest it
become very hot and constantly want to bathe.

## Renaquilla and the Shipibo Healer

The renaquilla bush (Ficus sp.) wraps around trees and has a spirit
appearing as a Shipibo woman. If those who ingest this plant diet
correctly, the Shipibo woman comes to them in dreams and teaches
them to heal with this plant. It is unmatched in its curative power
for treating fractures and other lesions, applied either raw or
cooked. The queens or mothers of the renaco tree interweave their
dancing roots like ghostly threads during healing ceremonies.

## Bobinzana-Sisa: The Purifying Flower

The spirit of the bobinzana plant (Calliandra angustifolia) appears
as a woman called Bobinzana-sisa (bobinzana flower). She gives the
ayahuasca-preparation|ayahuasca purge more power so it can
cleanse all the impurity in the stomach. Her presence ensures the
effectiveness of the cleansing process during ceremony.

## Sacha-Coyas: Princesses of the Forest

The sacha-coyas (princesses of the forest) disguise themselves as
trees to fool sorcerers who come to meddle when evil spells are
being broken. Small white birds called tinganas live in their
branches, serving as guards. They are spiritual entities that protect
healing work from interference.

## The Great Gardener of Ayahuasca

A being called the great gardener, holding a golden stick and a
pipe shaped like a snake, has the rank of satrapa pitonico and
always cares for the ayahuasca plant. A cricket near him cries in
alarm when anyone cuts a piece of this plant without first making an
offering. If the offering is made, the cricket listens to the
prayers, and ayahuasca gives positive effects when ingested.

## Kapukiri and Defensive Trees

Trees associated with the kapukiri illness -- including puka-lupuna,
ajosquiro, catahua negra, and huairacaspi -- have a "sublime
function in esoteric shamanism." These trees are very rigid regarding
cleanliness and punish those who mock them. One should not urinate or
defecate on their leaves, use them for cleaning, cut them without
purpose, or use them as lumber. These trees generate substances used
as nourishment by invisible beings, and if contaminated, the trees
emit something harmful to those beings, triggering their defensive
response.

## Plants as Sources of Icaros

Every plant spirit teaches its own [[icaros-power-songs]] to
the practitioner. The tian camuri fairies bring medicinal flowers
so that the vegetalista will learn their icaros and heal with them.
The machashca cantaro (pitcher of vision) contains icaros that
vegetalistas sing to guard against enemy visions and to strengthen
their own. The spirits of plants explain to vegetalistas how the
plants are to be used in treatment.

## The Spirit of Papastrueno

The spirit of the papastrueno plant (Dioscorea sp.) appears as an
Inca called Yana-huya. This plant is very difficult to obtain, found
in high jungle hills looking like a golden heart. When ingested with
a special diet, it gives the power to control rain, wind, and
thunderbolt. Its person who ingests it is able to summon rain or
keep it from falling.

## The Mannaicunas and Tataicunas

The marnaicunas (mysterious women) and tataicunas (mysterious men)
are beings associated with the Ayamanchare spirit. The marnaicunas
tell mysterious tales and represent the five fingers of the right
hand, while the tataicunas represent the five fingers of the left
hand. They carry out mysterious functions in the cosmology of plant
spirits, connected to the fundamental elements of the earth.
