# Phytochrome Mobile Signal from Leaf to Flowering

In "What a Plant Knows" (2012), [[phototropism]], botanists

## The Leaf-Based Discovery

The actual discovery came through a simple but decisive experiment. If
researchers shone a beam of light on different parts of a plant in the
middle of the night, they found that:

- Illuminating **any single leaf** was sufficient to regulate flowering
  in the entire plant
- If **all leaves were pruned**, leaving only the stem and apex, the
  plant was completely blind to light flashes — even when the entire
  remaining plant was illuminated

This result was striking: the "eye" for photoperiodism was not in the tip
but distributed across the leaves. A single leaf could see for the whole
plant.

## The Mobile Signal

When phytochrome in a single leaf detects [[mollison-designers-day-length-and-flowering-response]] in the shoot apex,
even though the apex itself has no photoreceptors for this purpose.

The identity of this mobile signal was one of the great mysteries of
plant biology for decades. The signal is now known to involve the
FT (FLOWERING LOCUS T) protein, a "florigen" that is produced in
the leaves under inductive photoperiods and travels through the phloem
to the shoot apex, where it activates the genetic program for flowering.
