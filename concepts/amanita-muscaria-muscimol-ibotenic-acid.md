## Amanita muscaria — Muscimol and Ibotenic Acid

## Overview

Amanita muscaria ([[amanita-pantherina]] (panther
cap) are mushrooms containing the psychoactive compounds muscimol
and ibotenic acid. Unlike [[psilocybin-pharmacology-and-serotonin-receptors]], Amanita species produce intoxication through
GABA receptor agonism and glutamate receptor activity. These
mushrooms grow throughout much of the [[cho-fish-amino-acid-preparation]] that acts as a glutamate receptor
agonist, inducing agitated toxic delirium. Active dose estimated
at 30-60 mg. Ibotenic acid is a prodrug that decarboxylates to
form muscimol when the mushrooms are dried.

### Muscarine
Despite the name Amanita muscaria, muscarinic/anticholinergic
effects are actually minimal. The primary activity comes from
muscimol and ibotenic acid, not muscarine. The notable skin
reddening sometimes observed is the main anticholinergic effect.

### Concentration
100 grams of dried Amanita muscaria contains approximately
180 mg of combined muscimol and ibotenic acid, of which only
about 25 mg may be ibotenic acid.

## Intoxication Profile

### Effects
Intoxication is described by some as similar to alcohol
intoxication but can also induce:
- Powerful hallucinations and illusions
- Slurred incoherent speech
- Seizure
- Severe nausea and vomiting
- Transient deep sleep or coma
- Headache that may persist for weeks

### Time Course
Intoxication commences within 30 minutes of ingestion and
peaks over the next 2-3 hours (Waser, 1979).

### Variability
Reactions are quite dependent on the individual, and overdose
is sometimes lethal. This unpredictability makes Amanita muscaria
one of the more dangerous naturally occurring psychoactive
mushrooms.

## Unique Urine Recycling

A unique feature of Amanita intoxication is the excretion of
unmetabolized ibotenic acid into urine. This finding explains
the historical practice of consuming the urine of someone who
had initially ingested the mushrooms — the active compounds
pass through largely unmetabolized (Wasson, 1979).
