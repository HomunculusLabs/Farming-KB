# Psychedelic Therapy Set and Setting

The concept of "[[set-and-setting]]" — first articulated by [[ralph-metzner]], and [[leary-psychedelic-experience-bardo-mapping]]" (1964) — is the foundational principle for safe and productive psychedelic use. Set refers to the user's mindset; setting refers to the physical and social environment. Together, they are more important than the specific substance or dose in determining the quality of the experience.

## Set (Mindset)

### Pre-Session Preparation
- **Intention**: Clear, positive intention for the session (not vague wishes — specific questions or goals)
- **Emotional state**: Should be stable and positive. Not suitable during acute grief, crisis, or psychosis
- **Physical health**: Should be well-rested, well-fed (light meal 2-3 hours before), not ill
- **Knowledge**: Understanding what to expect reduces anxiety. Read about the substance and dosage
- **Meditation practice**: Regular meditation before a session improves ability to navigate the experience

### Psychological Readiness
- Willingness to let go of control
- Trust in the guide/sitter
- No pressing responsibilities (phone off, commitments cleared)
- Comfort with the idea of encountering difficult emotions or memories
- Stable mental health (no active psychosis, bipolar mania, or severe depression without professional support)

## Setting (Environment)

### Physical Environment
- **Comfortable**: Warm, clean, private space. Familiar is better than novel
- **Safe**: Doors locked, phones off or silenced, no unexpected interruptions
- **Aesthetics**: Beautiful, calming — flowers, candles, art, natural light or dim lighting
- **Music**: Carefully curated playlist (important influence on experience). Classical, ambient, world music. No lyrics initially (lyrics can impose narratives)
- **Props**: Eye mask (for inward focus), blanket, journal, art supplies, water
- **Nature**: Outdoor settings can be ideal (garden, forest, beach) — but must be private and safe

### Social Environment
- **Guide/sitter**: Experienced, calm, trustworthy person present. Their role is to hold space, not to direct the experience
- **Number of people**: Small groups (2-4) or alone with a sitter. Large groups reduce intimacy
- **Agreement**: All participants should know and agree on expectations (no one should be surprised)
- **Silence**: Minimal talking during the peak. Verbal processing during the come-down

## The Leary Model: The Psychedelic Session

### Phase 1: Preparation (Days to Weeks Before)
- Study the [[lsd-psychedelic-experience-guide]] framework
- Practice meditation or relaxation techniques
- Clarify intention
- Choose guide and setting
- Prepare music playlist
- Fast lightly (or eat light vegetarian meal) before the session

### Phase 2: Ingestion and Onset (0-30 minutes)
- Take substance in comfortable position
- Remain calm during come-up anxiety (normal)
- Guide offers reassurance but doesn't distract
- Music begins softly

### Phase 3: The Peak (30 minutes to several hours)
- Dissolution of ordinary reality
- May encounter ego death, mystical states, difficult memories
- Guide's role: minimal intervention, reassurance if needed ("you're safe," "let go," "breathe")
- Trust the process — resistance causes suffering
- Eye mask recommended for inward journey

### Phase 4: Return and Integration (Hours After)
- Gradual return to ordinary consciousness
- Verbal processing: talk about the experience with guide
- Journal: write or draw immediately while impressions are fresh
- Light meal, gentle activity (walk, stretch)
- Discuss next steps for integration

### Phase 5: Integration (Days to Weeks After)
- Daily reflection: meditation, journaling
- Implement insights: behavioral changes, creative projects
- Discuss with therapist or integration circle
- Allow time for processing — don't rush back to normal life
- Second session (if planned) after full integration of first

## The Guide's Role

### What a Guide Does
- Creates and holds safe physical space
- Manages practical concerns (food, water, comfort)
- Offers verbal reassurance during difficult moments
- Helps with navigation using specific techniques ("breathe," "open your eyes," "trust")
- Facilitates post-session integration discussion
