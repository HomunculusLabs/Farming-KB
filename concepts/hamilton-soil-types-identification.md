### Silt

Neither gritty nor sticky. Particles range from 0.002mm to 0.02mm,
making silt smooth and silky to the touch. When wet, it packs down,
becoming cold, heavy, and badly drained like clay. It can be improved
by applying liberal quantities of well-rotted compost or manure.

### Chalk

A pale, hungry-looking soil often containing rocks and flints. Large
particles make it free-draining and quick to lose nutrients and water.
The topsoil is often shallow. Chalk is very alkaline, containing a
great deal of lime, making it inhospitable to many plants.

### Peat

A distinctive dark brown or gray soil with a spongy texture. Rich in
decomposed organic matter, it requires little additional compost or
manure. Younger brown peat is easier to work and more fertile than the
heavier black type. All peaty soils tend to become waterlogged and are
usually acid, needing lime for a wider plant range.
