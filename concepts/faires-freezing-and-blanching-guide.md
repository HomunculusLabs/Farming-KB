# Freezing and Blanching Guide

Freezing is a quick and healthy preservation method. Before freezing vegetables, you must blanch them to slow or stop enzymes that cause loss of flavor and color.

## Preparation

- Day before: Turn freezer to -10°F (-23°C) for quick freezing
- Label every item with date and contents
- After everything is frozen, turn temperature back to 0°F (-17°C)
- Shelf life: fruits and vegetables ~1 year (except onions), baked foods ~6 months, animal products/meat ~3-6 months

## Boiling Method

1. Wash thoroughly, drain well
2. Some foods can be frozen whole; check the chart
3. Most must be trimmed and chopped
4. Use 1 gallon (3.7L) water per pound (0.5 kg) prepared vegetables; 2 gallons per pound of leafy greens
5. Bring water to boil, lower food with wire basket, mesh bag, or strainer
6. Water should return to boil in under 1 minute (if longer, too much water)
7. Submerge for the time specified in the chart
8. Quickly chill in ice water for the SAME length of time as boiled
9. Drain well, pack in container or freezer bag with minimal air, freeze

## Steaming Method

Same as boiling, but only ~2 inches (5 cm) boiling water in pot. Lower steamer basket, put lid on. Start timing when steam pushes out of the lid again.

## No Blanching Needed

Onions, peppers, herbs don't need blanching. Squash, pumpkins, sweet potatoes, and beets need to be fully cooked before freezing.
