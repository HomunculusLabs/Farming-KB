## Bicycle Day: April 19, 1943

Exercising extreme caution, Hofmann began with the smallest quantity that could
be expected to produce some effect, considering the activity of ergot alkaloids
known at the time: 0.25 mg of [[ergot-and-lysergic-acid-history]] diethylamide tartrate. His
laboratory journal entry for April 19, 1943, reads:

"4/19/43 16:20: 0.5 cc of 1/2 promil aqueous solution of diethylamide tartrate
orally = 0.25 mg tartrate. Taken diluted with about 10 cc water. Tasteless.
17:00: Beginning dizziness, feeling of anxiety, visual distortions, symptoms of
paralysis, desire to laugh. Supplement of 4/21: Home by bicycle. From 18:00-
ca. 20:00 most severe crisis."

The notes cease at this point. By 17:00, the altered perceptions were of the
same type as the April 16 experience, only much more intense. Hofmann struggled
to speak intelligibly and asked his laboratory assistant to escort him home.
They went by bicycle, as no automobile was available due to wartime restrictions.

On the way home, Hofmann's condition began to assume threatening forms.
Everything in his field of vision wavered and was distorted as if seen in a
curved mirror. He had the sensation of being unable to move, yet his assistant
later reported they had traveled very rapidly. Upon arriving home, Hofmann asked
his companion to summon the family doctor and request milk from the neighbors.

## The Crisis

Hofmann lay on a sofa as his surroundings transformed in terrifying ways.
Everything in the room spun around, and familiar objects assumed grotesque,
threatening forms. They were in continuous motion, animated, as if driven by an
inner restlessness. The neighbor who brought milk appeared as a malevolent witch
with a colored mask.
