# Cannabis Flowering Management
From [[greg-green]]'s *The Cannabis Grow Bible* (4th ed.), Chapter 7.
## End of Vegetative Growth
When your plant stops growing much taller and you notice small new
growths at most of the nodes between the stem and branch, your plant is
reaching the end of its vegetative stage. These new growths will produce
more leaf, branches, and flowers. The plant is entering its pre-flowering
stage.
Healthy plants at this point should have flat, stretched-out leaves. If
leaves are curled or drooping, consult the problem solver chapter.
## Pre-Flowering Stage
Pre-flowering lasts between 1 and 2 weeks. During this period the new
growth regions (calyx) begin to change shape depending on whether the
plant is male or female. This is when you can properly detect sex.
### Early Sexing Methods (Not 100% Accurate)
**Method 1 -- Height comparison:** If growing the same strain from seed
at the same time, smaller plants tend to be female and taller ones male.
Males generally start to pre-flower before females.
**Method 2 -- Calyx inspection:** Using a fine magnifying glass, check
the calyx. If raised on a small short stem, probably male. If not raised,
probably female.
**Method 3 -- Force-flowering cuttings:** Take a cutting, place in water
or cloning medium, give 12/12 light. The cutting will flower and reveal
sex. Clones carry the exact same genetics including sex as the mother.
## The All-Important 12/12 Photoperiod
Cannabis plants naturally flower when days shorten to 12 hours light
and 12 hours darkness (Sept/Oct/Nov outdoors). This 12/12 photoperiod
stimulates flowering. As long as 12/12 continues, flowers grow larger
and more plentiful.
### Implementation
- Manually turn lights on for 12 hours and off for 12 hours, OR
- Use an automatic timer.
- During the 12 hours of darkness, keep the grow area as dark as
  possible.
- A small desk light at the other side of the room will prevent proper
  flowering.
- The grow room must be completely light-proof.
- Study 'Photography Dark Rooms' techniques for light-proofing -- thick
  black screening around doorframes and any open light points.
## When to Flower
Pre-flowering indicates the plant is mature enough to start flowering.
Key facts to consider:
- Some cannabis plants can be kept alive for up to 10 years under
  continuous light, growing into bushes.
- Bud production is NOT equal to plant height. It depends on growing
  environment, strain genetics, and number of nodes.
- More bud is possible with many small plants flowered early than with
  fewer large plants grown long before flowering.
- When flowering, most strains can double (or more) in height and width.
- Prepare adequate space before switching to 12/12.
**Recommendation:** Wait until pre-flowers appear before switching to
12/12. Get the best from your plant by being patient.
## Problems with Early 12/12
Switching to 12/12 before pre-flowers show may cause:
1. **Hermaphrodites (stress-related sex problems):** The plant thinks
   its chances of reproduction are slim. It produces both male and
   female flowers (self-pollination). Male flowers then pollinate female
   flowers, producing seeds. This spoils a sinsemilla crop.
2. **Abnormal bud growth:** Male pollen sacks in with female flowers
   cause different-looking buds with decreased quantity.
### About Hermaphrodites
- Genetically, hermies only produce female seeds and hermaphrodite
  seeds -- never male seeds.
- "All-female seeds" advertised by seed banks come from stressed female
  plants producing male flowers, then self-pollinating.
- Growers try to keep away from hermie plants -- they spoil sinsemilla
  crops and can contaminate breeding projects with floating pollen.
- Check female buds every week for signs of hidden male flowers.
## Sexing Your Plants
### Male Plants
Produce little ball-like shapes clustered together like grapes at the
nodes. These are pollen sacks that burst when ripe to scatter pollen.
### Female Plants
Produce little white hairs (pistils) at the internodes and top cola.
These pistils begin to curl, grow longer and thicker, become sticky
(with THC), and begin to cover in resin.
The stickiness is used by the female to catch falling pollen. If not
pollinated, she tries to grow more sticky areas -- hence bigger and
better buds from sinsemilla crops.
## The Flowering Process
After removing males, the female enters the flowering stage. Under
strict 12/12:
1. The plant fills out more -- more leaves, branches, and flowers.
2. The plant takes a Christmas tree shape with lower fan leaves
   stretched out to receive light.
3. Running upward in a cone shape, she gets tighter with floral and
   leaf development.
4. During peak flowering, female pistils on the flower tips swell up.
5. Pistils change color: white to orange to red to brown.
6. When color change is significant, she is ready for harvest.
Each strain has its own flowering times and color tint at peak. Use
the breeder's recommended flowering times for harvest guidelines.
## Sinsemilla (Non-Pollinated Female) Flowering
Without males to pollinate, female plants:
- Continue to fill out with flowers
- Develop more resin glands
- Become very sticky with resin dropping onto leaves
- Pistils get thicker and cluster into balls
- Bud growth increases dramatically as the female tries to attract
  pollen
- Produce more quantities of THC than seeded females
