## Design Principles

Holzer makes the ditches long and wide with low banks, so that water can be
absorbed over a large area. The sides of the ditch should slope gently upward
and it should be set well into the hill. As with terraces, it is important not
to let water form channels, otherwise it will cause a great deal of damage.
The gentle sloping of the sides helps to prevent channelling and erosion.

If the hill already has any hollows or depressions, an excavator can be used
to make them into ditches very easily. A two-metre-wide "slope bucket" that
can be hydraulically operated in any direction is ideal. In this situation it
is not normally necessary to dig a ditch; you can just use the bottom of the
bucket to push the soil down, making the hollow deeper.

## Hydrological Benefits

Using ditches in this way has a very positive effect on the hydrology of the
land. The accumulated water evaporates slowly and brings significant
long-term benefits to the nearby vegetation. This reservoir of water is vital
for the survival of plants in dry areas and during hot summers, because they
do not receive any additional watering. This approach contrasts sharply with
conventional [[composting]]
