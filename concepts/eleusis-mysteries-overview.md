## The Pilgrimage and Ritual Sequence

Months of learning and rituals preceded the revelation on the Mystery night,
each action programming in further detail the meaning and substance of the
vision. Candidates for initiation first participated in the Lesser Mysteries
at Agrai in February, which was called the time of flowers. There they
experienced in some way the death of Persephone through ritual mimeses. The
Lesser Mystery was considered a preliminary for the Greater Mystery at the
autumn sowing in September.

At last the initiates would walk the [[powell-psilocybin-end-of-life-anxiety]] and known that they were one, something given
by god. The division between earth and sky melted into a pillar of light.

These are the symptomatic reactions not to a drama or ceremony, but to a
mystical vision. Since the sight could be offered to thousands of initiates
each year dependably upon schedule, an hallucinogen must have induced it.

## Secrecy and the Bond of Initiation

Silence as to what took place at Eleusis was obligatory. The laws of Athens
imposed extreme penalties on any who infringed. In 415 BCE there was a spate
of deliberate profanations by the Athenian elite, followed by a harsh
crackdown. But the secrecy ran far beyond the reach of Athenian law and was
kept spontaneously throughout the entire Greek world.

In the early centuries of the Christian era, with Eleusis breaking down,
we discover a few references that may permit some uncertain glimpses. We
find mention of a bond of alliance and friendship that sprang up among the
initiates, a feeling of cofradia, of brotherhood. The overwhelming effect
of the night under the influence of an hallucinogen gives natural birth to
a feeling of shared supernatural experience never to be forgotten. One who
has known the ineffable is loath to embark on explanations: words are
useless.
