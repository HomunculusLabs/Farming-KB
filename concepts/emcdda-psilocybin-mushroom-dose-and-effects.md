The hallucinogenic effects of psilocybin-containing mushrooms have been used for thousands of years in religious and spiritual contexts, but their increasing recreational use in Europe prompted the European Monitoring Centre for Drugs and Drug Addiction (EMCDDA) to conduct a comprehensive assessment of their pharmacology, dosing patterns, and health effects. The 2006 EMCDDA thematic paper provides detailed information on these aspects. ## Chemistry of [[forensic-identification-methods-psilocybin-mushrooms-tlc-gc-hplc]] Psilocybin and psilocin are the principal psychoactive compounds found in [[emcdda-hallucinogenic-mushrooms-emerging-trend-europe]]. They are chemically classified as indolealkylamines (tryptamines), belonging to the same chemical group as LSD. Psilocybin (4-phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) is the phosphate ester of psilocin (4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine). Psilocybin is more stable in air and is water soluble, but it is converted in the body into psilocin, which is the pharmacologically active compound. Psilocin appears to act on the serotonin system as a 5-HT2A post-synaptic agonist or partial agonist. Besides psilocybin and psilocin, two further tryptamines, baeocystin and norbaeocystin, may also be present but are thought to be less active.
