# [[cannabis-hashish-production]] Methods

Hashish (hash) is a concentrated form of cannabis made by separating and collecting the
resin glands (trichomes) from the plant material. Oner's guide frequently references
hash production suitability, resin characteristics, and traditional methods throughout
the strain reviews.

## Resin as the Foundation of Hash

All hash production depends on the quality and quantity of the plant's resin (trichome)
production. Oner evaluates strains extensively for their hash-making potential:

### Indicators of Hash Suitability

- **Heavy trichome production**: CookieWreck is described as having "heavy trichome
  production" making it "a good choice for hash production"
- **Resin density**: Harlequin was "bred specifically to produce some high-grade hash
  and oils for medical patients"
- **Crystal coverage**: Multiple strains are described as "caked in resin," "dripping
  with resin," or "covered in crystals"
- **Sticky texture**: Resinous buds that "catch you like Velcro" when brushed against
  indicate abundant trichomes

### Strains Noted for Hash Production

Oner specifically recommends the following strains for hash making:

- **CookieWreck**: Heavy trichome production makes it ideal for hash
- **Harlequin**: Bred specifically for high-grade hash and oil production for medical
  patients
- **Jack 47**: Described as "a fantastic choice for hash making" with its massive
  resin production
- **Psycho Killer Bubba Kush**: "Its buds are so resinous that you'd be a fool not to
  make hash from them"
- **Ganja White Night**: "Buds are so resinous that you'd be a fool not to make hash
  from them"
- **Lambsbread × Skunk**: "Only a fool wouldn't make hash from this incredibly
  resinous plant"
- **Nepalese Temple**: Described as producing "finer hash" from its resin

## Finger Hash

The simplest and most traditional form of hash is finger hash, produced during the
trimming process:

- When manicuring (trimming) resin-heavy buds, the trichomes accumulate on fingers and
  tools as a sticky, dark resin
- Harlequin plants produce so much resin that "finger hash made from after the manicure"
  is abundant
- NeoBlizzard buds are so large that the breeder recommends inviting friends to help
  with the harvest and "don't forget to roll yourselves some finger hash when you're
  done"
- This is the oldest method of hash production and requires no special equipment

## Dry Sift / Sieving

Dry sift hash is produced by passing dried cannabis flowers over screens of varying
micron sizes to separate trichome heads from plant matter:

- The "Polm Gold" strain name references traditional Moroccan dry-sift hash ("polm"
  being a term for Moroccan hashish)
- The method works best with well-dried, resinous plant material
- Multiple screen sizes (from coarse to fine) produce different grades of hash

## Traditional Hand-Rubbed Hash (Charas)

Charas is the traditional hand-rubbed hash of India and Nepal, made by gently
rubbing living cannabis plants between the hands to collect the resin:

- The Nepalese Temple strain references this tradition directly
- This method preserves the full spectrum of cannabinoids and terpenes since the resin
  is collected fresh from living plants
- The resulting product is typically soft, pliable, and extremely aromatic

## Hash Quality Factors

Based on Oner's descriptions, several factors determine hash quality:

1. **Starting material quality**: The more resinous the buds, the better the hash.
   Plants described as "absolutely caked in resin" or "dripping with resin" produce
   the highest quality concentrates.
2. **Strain genetics**: Sativa-dominant strains like Jack 47 (75% sativa) and
   Harlequin are noted for hash production, though resin-heavy indicas also produce
   excellent results.
3. **Growing method**: Organic soil grows are recommended for the best terpene
   preservation in the final hash product.
4. **Curing**: Proper curing of the starting material enhances the flavor profile of
   the resulting hash.

## Hash in Cannabis History

Oner's strain guide is rich with historical context about hash:

- The "Killer Green Bud" era of Northern California was dominated by "Afghanistan
  Hashplant Indicas" that were prized for hash production
- Landrace strains from hash-producing regions (Morocco, Lebanon, India, Nepal) carry
  genetics optimized for resin production
- The aroma of hash itself is noted in multiple strain descriptions (e.g., KillerSkunk
  produces aromas of "hash, lemon, and pine")

## Medical Applications

Oner notes that hash production serves medical purposes:

- Harlequin was specifically bred to "produce some high-grade hash and oils for
  medical patients"
- Concentrated forms allow patients to consume therapeutic doses with less plant
  material
- Hash and oil production is particularly important for strains with high CBD content
