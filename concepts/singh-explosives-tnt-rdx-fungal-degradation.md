# Explosives TNT and RDX Fungal Degradation

Military munitions contamination from manufacturing, testing, and disposal
activities has introduced persistent explosive compounds into soils and
groundwater worldwide. Trinitrotoluene (TNT) and royal demolition explosive
(RDX, hexahydro-1,3,5-trinitro-1,3,5-triazine) are the most widespread
contaminants, both classified as priority pollutants due to their toxicity,
mutagenicity, and environmental persistence. Singh's treatment highlights the
significant role fungi play in transforming and degrading these recalcitrant
nitroaromatic and nitramine compounds, complementing bacterial remediation
approaches.

## Fungal Degradation Pathways for RDX

RDX presents different challenges than TNT due to its nitramine (N-NO2) rather
than nitroaromatic (C-NO2) structure. Fungal degradation of RDX proceeds through:

### Direct Ring Cleavage

Certain fungi catalyze direct denitration and ring cleavage of RDX, producing
methylenedinitramine (MEDINA), formaldehyde, and nitrite. This pathway is
analogous to fungal degradation chlorinated compound metabolism in its
use of reductive elimination reactions.

### Xenobiotic Reductase Activity

Fungal xanthine oxidoreductase and cytochrome P450 systems catalyze sequential
N-nitroso reduction of RDX to hexahydro-1-nitroso-3,5-dinitro-1,3,5-triazine
and subsequently to triazine ring cleavage products. [[singh-fungal-consortia-synergistic-biodegradation]] combining [[biochemistry-of-ligninolytic-fungi-bioremediation]] with RDX-degrading
bacteria achieve more complete mineralization than either organism alone. The
synergy operates through fungal enzyme systems initiating RDX transformation
followed by bacterial mineralization of intermediates.
