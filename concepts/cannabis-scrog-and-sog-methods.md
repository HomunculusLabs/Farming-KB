# Cannabis SCROG and SOG Methods

[[cannabis-screen-of-green-scrog-guide]] (ScrOG) and [[comparison-sea-of-green-vs-screen-of-green]] (SOG) are two advanced [[comparison-cannabis-indoor-growing-basics-vs-cannabis-outdoor-growing]] techniques designed to maximize yield per [[comparison-biointensive-vs-square-foot-gardening]] by optimizing light distribution across a horizontal canopy. Both methods manipulate plant architecture but differ fundamentally in their approach: SOG uses many small plants flowered early, while ScrOG uses fewer plants trained through a horizontal screen.

See [[cannabis-vegetative-stage-management]] for timing considerations.

## Sea of Green (SOG)

### Concept

SOG is a method of growing that uses a large number of small plants packed closely together in the grow area. Each plant is flowered at a very small size, typically after only 2-3 weeks of [[cannabis-vegetative-growth-stage]]. The goal is to create a uniform "sea" of bud-covered colas across the entire grow surface. Because no individual plant is allowed to grow large, the method produces fast harvests with minimal vegetative time.

### How SOG Works

Plants are started from seed or clone and grown in small containers, often 1-2 gallon pots or even solo cups. After a short vegetative period of 10-21 days, the light cycle is switched to 12/12 to trigger flowering. The plants remain small and produce a single large central cola each. The tight spacing means the colas merge into an even canopy that captures light efficiently.

### SOG Plant Count

Typical SOG setups use 1-4 plants per square foot, depending on container size. A 3x3 foot area might hold 25-50 small plants. The exact number depends on the strain and pot size. Indica-dominant strains work best in SOG because of their naturally compact structure and short flowering time. Sativa strains can be used but may require more height management.

### SOG Advantages

- Fastest possible harvest turnaround from seed or clone to finished bud

- Reduced vegetative period saves time and electricity

- Uniform canopy simplifies light placement and environmental management

- High yield per square foot despite small individual plant size

- Ideal for growers with limited vertical space

### SOG Disadvantages

- Requires a large number of plants, which may be problematic in restricted-plant-count jurisdictions

- Small root systems mean each plant is more vulnerable to overwatering and nutrient stress

- If growing from seed, sexing must happen early and males must be removed quickly

- Clones are strongly recommended for consistency and guaranteed female plants

- Less opportunity for individual plant training compared to ScrOG

## Screen of Green (ScrOG)

### Concept

ScrOG uses a horizontal screen or net (commonly chicken wire or trellis netting) suspended above the plants. As plants grow through the screen, branches are woven and tied down to create an even, flat canopy of bud sites. The method maximizes light exposure to every bud site by keeping all growth at the same height beneath the light source.

### How ScrOG Works

A screen is positioned 8-20 inches above the growing medium, depending on available headroom and desired plant height. During the vegetative period, plants are allowed to grow up through the screen. As branches emerge above the screen, they are bent back under and woven through the openings. This process continues until the screen is roughly 70-80% filled, at which point flowering is initiated. During flowering, the branches continue to grow upward through the screen, producing an even canopy of colas.

### ScrOG Timing

The transition to flowering should begin when the screen is approximately 70% full. If you wait too long, the canopy will become too dense, restricting airflow and light penetration to lower sites. If you switch too early, the screen will not fill completely and yield potential is wasted. The exact timing varies by strain, with fast-growing Sativas filling the screen faster than compact Indicas.

### ScrOG Plant Count

Unlike SOG, ScrOG uses fewer plants per square foot, typically 1-2 plants per square foot in larger containers (3-5 gallons). Some growers use a single large plant trained across a substantial screen. This makes ScrOG more suitable for areas with plant count restrictions.

### ScrOG Advantages

- Dramatically increases yield per plant by creating many bud sites at equal light intensity

- Works well with limited plant counts

- Produces exceptionally even canopy for maximum light utilization

- Allows fine control over plant shape and height

- Effective for both Indica and Sativa varieties when properly trained

### ScrOG Disadvantages

- More labor-intensive than SOG due to ongoing training and weaving

- Longer vegetative period required to fill the screen

- Requires more skill and attention to detail than simple untrained growing

- Dense canopy can create airflow issues if not managed with adequate ventilation

- Difficult to move plants once trained through the screen

## SOG vs ScrOG Comparison

The choice between SOG and ScrOG depends on the grower's specific constraints and goals. SOG is faster from start to finish and simpler to execute, making it ideal for commercial operations or growers who prioritize speed. ScrOG produces more yield per plant and is better suited to restricted plant counts, but requires more hands-on management.

Both methods share a common principle: the creation of an even, flat canopy that maximizes light interception. In either method, plants that grow taller than the canopy should be bent down or cut back to maintain uniformity. Light should be positioned to cover the entire canopy evenly, with no hot spots or dark corners.

## Canopy Management Tips

Regardless of method, fan leaves that block light from reaching bud sites on neighboring plants should be removed selectively during the flowering period. In ScrOG setups, removing large fan leaves from the upper canopy can dramatically improve light penetration to lower bud sites on the screen. However, never remove more than 20-30% of foliage at once, as fan leaves continue to produce sugars essential for bud development.

## Combining Methods

Some growers combine elements of both methods, using moderate plant counts with some screen training. A "modified ScrOG" might use 2-3 plants per square foot with minimal training, simply using the screen to support heavy branches during flowering. This hybrid approach balances the simplicity of SOG with the yield advantages of ScrOG.
