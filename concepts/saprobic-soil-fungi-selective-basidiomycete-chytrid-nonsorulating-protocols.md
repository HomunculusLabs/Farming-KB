# Saprobic Soil Fungi — Selective Basidiomycete Isolation, Chytrid Baiting, and Nonsporulating Isolate Protocols

## Overview

Isolating [[saprobic-soil-fungi]] is challenging due to immense diversity and cryptic life forms. This page covers three major methodologies: selective isolation of basidiomycetes via the LGB method, chytrid baiting and enrichment, and characterization protocols for nonsporulating isolates.

## Selective Isolation of Saprobic Basidiomycetes (LGB Method)

The LGB method combines modified [[particle-filtration-dilution-plating-microfungal-isolation]] with lignin-guaiacol-benomyl (LGB) medium. Benomyl suppresses mitosporic ascomycete competitors, while lignin and guaiacol encourage oxidative enzyme activity characteristic of basidiomycetes, producing a diagnostic color change in the agar.

### Protocol Steps

1. **Soil [[bahay-kubo-imo-collection-and-preparation]]:** Collect soil samples and prepare for processing.
2. **Dispersion:** Weigh 5 g of soil into a 1 L jar with 500 mL sterile 0.1% sodium pyrophosphate. Shake 1 hour at 4°C to disperse aggregates and separate fungal propagules.
3. **Particle filtration:** Pour the suspension sequentially through 250 μm and 53 μm sieves. Rinse sieves with tap water and disinfect with 70% ethanol between samples. Wash particles retained on the 53 μm sieve for 5 min with a tap water spray. Tilt the sieve to separate organic (rising) from inorganic particles.
4. **Plating:** Pick up 1 mL of the dense organic suspension with a wide-mouth pipette; disperse in 9 mL sterile water. Plate 0.2–0.4 mL onto 5–10 LGB agar plates. Incubate up to 4 weeks.

### Identification and Transfer

Examine plates at 40–100× for colonies turning the agar bright red to reddish-brown (characteristic basidiomycete ligninolysis). Avoid undesired mitosporic fungi (*Alternaria*, *Trichoderma*). Transfer putative basidiomycetes to slant tubes.

### Controls and Disposal

Plate 0.2–0.4 mL of wash water from the 53 μm sieve after the last soil sample onto LGB agar as a contamination control. Autoclave all foreign soil wash water before disposal.

## Selective Baiting and Enrichment for Soil Chytrids

Chytrids are [[zoosporic-fungi]] requiring aquatic or water-saturated environments. Selective baiting exploits their motile zoospore stage by providing specific substrata in water while excluding competitors.

### General Method

Place ~1 teaspoon (3–5 g) of soil in a Petri dish, cover with double-distilled water, charcoal water (Couch 1939), sterilized pond water, or SEA (Hanson 1945). Float characteristic baits on the water surface, separated from soil. Lack of soil contact discourages hyphal fungal growth and forces zoosporic colonization. Larger baits may be submerged to prevent air-borne spore colonization. Minimize bait quantity to avoid over-enrichment (bacteria and hyphal fungi outcompete chytrids). As chytrid numbers increase, decrease soil per plate to ensure adequate substratum. Isolation periods range from 2 days to 6 weeks.

## Bait Preparation

### Cellulose Baits

- **Cellophane:** Boil dialysis tubing 5 min, split open, cut 5–10 mm squares, autoclave.
- **Filter/lens paper:** Cut 10 mm squares; use directly (less transparent but loose fibers accessible).
- **Grass:** Young *Paspalum* leaves cut diagonally to expose vascular bundles; clear in 100% ethanol, rinse, autoclave.
- **Onion epidermis:** Strip from storage bulbs, boil, autoclave; float with disrupted inner surface down.
- **Seeds:** Sterilized *Cannabis* most common (parasitic chytrids on oomycetes); also sesame, buckwheat, mung bean; cut or boil to expose cotyledons.

### Keratin Baits

- **Hair:** 15 mm lengths; remove oils with ether. Baby hair among the most effective (Karling 1977).
- **Horn, hoofs, nails:** Thinly sliced; no additional treatment.
- **Skin:** Molted snake skin or human skin (sunburn peel); use directly.

### Chitin Baits

- **Arthropod exoskeleton:** Shrimp cut into squares, treated with dilute HCl + 1% KOH, stored in 100% ethanol, washed in boiling water, autoclaved.
- **Fish scales and insect exuviae:** Pretreat with dilute HCl, autoclave.
- **Insect wings:** Termite, fly, or roach wings; autoclave.

### Pollen Baits

Pollen baits are particularly successful due to wide availability, refractory nature, and decay resistance. *Pinus* and *[[liquidambar-styraciflua]]* (sweet gum) pollen are commonly used; sweet gum lacks Pinus wings, offering better microscopic visibility. Collect from male cones/flowers on graduated-mesh sieves; air-dry or heat-dry at ~60°C. Sweet gum pollen sterilized with propylene oxide (carcinogenic — use under fume hood) or briefly autoclaved (121°C, 5 min). Sprinkle on water surface with a sterile cotton plug; remove by dragging a coverslip edge.

### Enrichment

After initial colonization (2 days to 6 weeks), remove the substratum, wash the surface, and transfer to fresh culture with new bait.

## Protocols for Nonsporulating Isolates

Many soil fungi fail to sporulate on standard media, requiring alternative identification approaches.

### Promoting Sporulation

Transfer isolates to specialized media: cornmeal agar ± autoclaved banana leaf, oatmeal agar, V8-juice agar, SNA, Alphacel agar, or Weitzman-Silva-Hutner agar. Incubation at 5–10°C for up to 1 year may be needed (Wang and Wilcox 1985; Petrini 1986). More labor-intensive physiological tests are used only if sporulation fails.

### Characterization Setup

1. Transfer isolate to PDA for fresh uniform inocula using sterile polypropylene transfer tubes or a flamed cork borer (4–5 mm).
2. Perform all tests with at least 3 replicates; ideally repeat the entire test series once.

### Metabolic Tests

- **Cellulose azure-dye test:** Dye bound to cellulose is released upon cellulolysis; disappearance correlates with ligninolysis (Thorn 1993). Incubate 2–8 weeks; quantify at 595 nm (Smith 1977).
- **Lipase-activity test:** Tween-20 medium; lipase hydrolysis releases fatty acids that combine with calcium to form visible precipitate (Hankin and Anagnostakis 1975).
- **Creatine test:** pH indicator medium with creatine (N-source) + sucrose (C-source) (Frisvad 1981). *Penicillium* species differentiated by pH shifts — yellow then purple over 2–3 weeks.
- **Milk solids test:** Differentiates *Trichophyton* species and basidiomycetes (Fischer and Kane 1971; Hutchison 1990a; Thorn 1991). Rapid clearers → purple; slow clearers → yellow.
- **Urea test:** Urea (N-source) + glucose (C-source); differentiates *Trichophyton* species and basidiomycetes (Kane and Fischer 1971; Hutchison 1991). Typically 2 weeks.

### Ligninolytic Enzyme Assays

**Spot tests on colonies:**
- **Laccase:** Syringaldazine (0.1% in 95% ethanol); positive = reddish-purple.
- **Tyrosinase:** *p*-Cresol (0.1 M in 95% ethanol); positive = brown to dark (Käärik 1965).
- **Peroxidases:** If laccase is absent, add dilute H₂O₂ (0.4%) to the syringaldazine spot (Harkin and Obst 1973). Peroxidases cannot be assayed when laccase is present.

**Broth assays:**

| Enzyme | Substrate | Reference |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| Laccase | ABTS | Niku-Paavola et al. 1990 |
| Tyrosinase | D-L-DOPA | Horowitz et al. 1960 |
| [[lignin-peroxidase]] | [[fungal-lignin-peroxidase-veratryl-alcohol]] + H₂O₂ | Tien and Kirk 1984 |
| Mn peroxidase | Phenol red or vanillylacetone + H₂O₂ + Mn(II) | — |

**Caution:** Enzyme substrates overlap and expression varies across media; interpret results carefully.

### Sensitivity Tests

Test growth on medium amended with defined concentrations of benomyl, cycloheximide, and NaCl. Express results as percentage growth relative to an unamended control (Harrington 1992; Hutchison 1990c; Thorn 1991). Incubate 1–2 weeks.

### DBB (Diazonium Blue B) Test — Ascomycetes vs. Basidiomycetes

Basidiomycete mycelia stain red-purple after 1 M KOH pretreatment; ascomycetes generally do not.

**Protocol:**
1. Grow isolate statically in standard liquid medium; harvest active mycelia.
2. Rinse 3× with distilled water.
3. Incubate 12 hours in 1 M KOH at 4°C.
4. Rinse 3× or more with distilled water.
5. Transfer to cold 0.1 M Tris-HCl buffer (pH 7.0).
6. Apply DBB solution (1 mg/mL in cold buffer).
7. Return to ice 1–2 min; basidiomycetes turn red-purple within 1–2 min.

**Caveats:** Longer incubation, elevated temperatures, or pH > 7.0 cause rust-red false positives. Heavily pigmented mycelia are difficult to interpret; some ascomycetes produce genuine red reactions. **DBB is extremely carcinogenic** — store desiccated at −20°C; prepare fresh solutions.

### Septal Structure Examination

Stain large-diameter thin-walled hyphae with trypan blue or ammoniacal Congo red. Ascomycetes show simple septal pores with Woronin bodies; holobasidiomycetes show dolipore septa ± parenthesomes. Microscope slide culture improves detail discernment by allowing hyphae to grow in a thin, optically favorable plane near the coverslip.
