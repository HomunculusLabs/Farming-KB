## Overview

Freshwater habitats host a remarkably diverse assemblage of fungi, including chytridiomycetes, hyphochytriomycetes, peronosporomycetes (formerly oomycetes), ascomycetes, and mitosporic fungi. These organisms play critical ecological roles as decomposers of plant and animal tissues, parasites of algae, aquatic macrophytes, fish, amphibians, and invertebrates, and as saprobes cycling nutrients through freshwater ecosystems. Freshwater habitats have disappeared at alarming rates, making documentation of their fungal [[fungal-taxonomy-and-kingdom-fungi]] and fungus-like straminipiles that disperse through water using motile, flagellated zoospores. They are treated together because they are outwardly similar, occupy the same habitats, and are studied with identical techniques.

- **Chytridiomycetes**: True Fungi with zoospores bearing a single posteriorly-directed flagellum. Five orders are recognised: Chytridiales (the most diverse, with ~80 genera and 500+ species), Spizellomycetales (primarily soil-dwelling), Blastocladiales (includes parasites of invertebrates and the model organism *Allomyces*), Monoblepharidales (four families, all saprotrophic), and Neocallimastigales (obligate anaerobes from herbivore guts).
- **Hyphochytriomycetes**: Allied with straminipiles, producing zoospores with a single anterior tinsellated flagellum. A single order with three families and four genera.

### Peronosporomycetes (Oomycetes)

These fungus-like organisms include important parasites of aquatic plants and animals. Many cause devastating diseases of fish and amphibians. They are studied alongside true fungi in freshwater surveys because of their ecological importance and similar collection [[biodiversity-fungi-freshwater-aquatic-habitats]].
