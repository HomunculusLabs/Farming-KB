# Flowering Phase and Harvest

Greg Green's Cannabis Grow Bible provides comprehensive guidance on the
flowering cycle, the critical 12/12 photoperiod, sexing plants, and
harvesting and curing techniques.

## The 12/12 Photoperiod

Cannabis naturally flowers when days shorten to 12 hours of light and
12 hours of darkness (outdoors, September through November). Indoors,
the grower triggers flowering by switching to a 12/12 light schedule
using a timer.

Critical requirements for successful 12/12:

- The flowering room must be completely light-tight -- even a small
  desk lamp across the room will prevent proper flowering
- Total darkness during the 12-hour dark period is essential
- A 100% light-tight room increases yields 30% over 99% light-tight
- During dark periods, temperature can drop as low as 55 degrees F

## Problems with Early 12/12

Switching to 12/12 before pre-flowers appear can cause:

- **Hermaphroditism**: The plant produces both male and female flowers
  in a self-pollination response, triggered by the stress of irregular
  photoperiod. This is not true "early flowering" -- it is a stress
  response. Hermie plants produce only female and hermaphrodite seeds,
  never male seeds. "Feminized" seeds sold commercially come from
  stressed females forced to produce male flowers.
- **Abnormal bud growth**: Pollinated flowers produce seeds, reducing
  bud quantity and quality.

Green's advice: wait for pre-flowers before switching to 12/12.

## Sexing Your Plants

Definitive identification comes from observing the flowers:

- **Female**: Produces little white hairs (pistils) at the internodes
  and top cola. Pistils are sticky (containing THC) and grow longer
  and thicker during flowering. An unpollinated female (sinsemilla)
  tries to grow more sticky areas, producing bigger, better buds.
- **Male**: Produces small ball-like shapes (pollen pods) in clusters.
  Males should be removed before pollen bursts to prevent pollination.

Switch to flowering feed (higher P than N and K) once sex is confirmed.

## The Flowering Process

Under strict 12/12, the female plant:

1. Fills out with more leaves, branches, and flowers, taking a
  Christmas tree shape
2. Fan leaves stretch outward to maximize light collection
3. Pistils at flower tips swell and change color: white to orange to
  red to brown
4. Resin (trichomes) covers the flowers; resin glands can burst and
  drop onto lower leaves
5. Flowering reaches a peak, then the plant is ready for harvest

Each strain has its own flowering times. Pure Indica can flower in 6
weeks. Indica/Sativa crosses typically flower in 2-4 months. Pure
Sativa can take 6-9 months.

## Harvest Timing

Signs that a plant is ready for harvest:

- 50-70% of pistils have changed color
- Plant has stopped producing crystals and resin
- Fan leaves and lower leaves are yellowing and dropping
- Smell has reached peak intensity
- Bud mass has not increased in the past few days

Harvest timing also influences the type of high. Later harvests
produce more couch-lock effect; harvesting just before peak induces a
more cerebral high.

## Harvesting Technique

### Indica Harvest
1. Cut the plant at the base (1-4 foot plants)
2. Hang upside down in a cool, dark room with fresh air
3. Remove fan leaves with clippers
4. Remove secondary leaves into a separate pile
5. Collect trim (small resin-covered leaves)
6. Wait until branches break easily between fingers (2-3 weeks)

### Sativa Harvest
Similar process but large outdoor plants (up to 12 feet, 20+ oz bud)
require a canvas for transport. Branches may need to be cut and hung
separately. Light degrades THC -- do not expose harvested material to
direct light.

## Curing

Once branches are brittle, clip bud from stems and place in airtight
cans:

- Open lids for ~6 hours daily for air exchange
- Shake the bud around every couple of days
- Allow 3-4 weeks minimum before sampling
- 8-week cured bud is the pinnacle; year-old bud may lose potency
- Do not speed up drying with ovens, microwaves, or heat

Fan leaves and trim are cured separately on a flat surface away from
direct light with fresh air, drying in about 3 weeks. Trim can also be
used for hash production.
