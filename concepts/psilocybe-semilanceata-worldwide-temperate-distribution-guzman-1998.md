## European Distribution

P. semilanceata is arguably most abundant and extensively documented in
Europe, where it has been recorded from virtually every country on the
continent. The Guzmán, Allen & Gartz survey lists confirmed reports from:

- **Great Britain and Ireland**: One of the earliest documented locations,
  recorded by Sowerby (1797-1809) and Cooke (1881-1906). Found throughout
  England, Scotland, Wales, and Ireland including the Hebrides and Shetland
  Islands. References include Benedict et al. (1962b), Heim et al. (1967),
  Dennis (1986), Watling & Gregory (1987), and Oldridge et al. (1989).
- **Scandinavia**: Documented in Norway (Høiland, 1978), Finland
  (Jokiranta et al., 1984), Sweden (Stijve, 1984), and the Faroe Islands
  (Möller, 1945). The species extends well into the boreal zone.
- **Central Europe**: Germany (Derbsch & Schmitt, 1984 & 1987), Austria,
  Switzerland (Samorini, 1992; Stijve & de Meijer, 1993), Czech Republic,
  Poland, Hungary, and the Netherlands.
- **Southern Europe**: Spain (Moreno et al., 1986; Becker, 1989; Samorini,
  1994), Italy (Gitti et al., 1983; Festi, 1985; Samorini, 1988, 1989,
  1992), Portugal, Greece (Pantidou, 1991), Bulgaria (Kutan & Kotlaba,
  1988), and the former Yugoslavia.
- **Eastern Europe**: Russia including Siberia (Guzmán, 1983; Samorini,
  1992), Estonia (Urbonas et al., 1986), Latvia, Lithuania, Ukraine
  (Minter & Dudka, 1996), and Romania.

The species is particularly associated with temperate grasslands, pastures,
and moorland habitats in Europe, fruiting in autumn. Its occurrence across
such a wide latitudinal range, from Mediterranean climates to subarctic
regions, demonstrates remarkable ecological plasticity.
