### Common Leaf-Spotting Genera

- **Tropical and subtropical regions**: *Alternaria*, *Ascochyta*,
  *Cercospora*, *Cladosporium*, *Corynespora*, *Phyllosticta*, *Pestalotia*,
  and *Pestalotiopsis*. Many produce lesions on stems, flowers, and fruits as
  well as leaves.

- **Temperate broadleaf [[nitrogen-fixing-trees-and-shrubs]]**: *Coccomyces*, *Discula*,
  *Gnomonia*, *Mycosphaerella*, *Rhytisma*, and *Venturia*.

- **Temperate grasses**: *Gaeumannomyces*, *Gibberella*, *Monographella*,
  *Phyllachora*, *Pleospora*, *Alternaria*, *Cladosporium*, *Drechslera*,
  *Fusarium*, *Pseudocercosporella*, *Rhynchosporium*, *Ulocladium*,
  *Ascochyta*, and *Colletotrichum*.

- **Tropical grasses**: *Cercospora*, *Drechslera*, *Magnaporthe*
  (*Pyricularia* anamorph), *Rhynchosporium*, and *Sphaerulina*. *Pyricularia
  grisea* (syn. *P. oryzae*; teleomorph *[[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]]. Foliage damage is most visible in
spring; collections not made at this time may overlook infections. The genus
appears almost exclusively in temperate zones, with over 100 species described
primarily from Betulaceae, Fagaceae, Juglandaceae, and Rosaceae hosts.

### Needle-Cast Fungi

In temperate conifer forests, needle-cast diseases are commonly caused by
facultative parasites in the **Rhytismataceae** (Ascomycota). This family
comprises 43 genera and 344 species. Common genera causing needle blights and
casts include *Lophodermium*, *Lophodermella*, *Elytroderma*, *Hypodermella*,
and *Lirula*. Symptoms include chlorotic, discolored foliage and repeated
defoliation. Fruiting bodies often do not sporulate until foliage is a year old
or older, so collecting both current and previous year's cast needles may be
necessary for identification.

## Cankers

### Types of Cankers

A canker is a sunken necrotic lesion on a stem or branch caused by localized
death of the cambium. Three general categories are recognized:

1. **Woody stem perennial (target) cankers**: Circular to lens-shaped with
   sunken centers, persisting for years and expanding at the same rate as
   radial tree growth. Concentric ridges of callus tissue create a target-like
   pattern. Associated genera include *Nectria* (Hypocreales) and *Ceratocystis*
   (Ophiostomatales), particularly on broadleaf trees.

2. **Woody stem diffuse cankers**: Expand faster than the host grows radially,
   with little or no callus formation. Discolored tissues and a raised margin
   mark the infection front. Often lethal within a few years. Commonly seen in
   shade-stressed understory trees. Associated with Diaporthales (*Valsa*,
   *Diaporthe*, *Cryptosporella*, *Cryptonectria*, *Endothia*), Diatrypales
   (*Eutypa*, *Leucostoma*), and Leotiales (*Cenangium*, *Tympanis*,
   *Encoelia*, *Lachnellula*). *Hypoxylon* (Xylariaceae) species are also
   associated with diffuse cankers.

3. **Twig and shoot blights**: Contain little or no callus, develop rapidly,
   and persist for only one season. May show discolored or sunken tissues with
   roughened appearances from fruiting bodies. Caused by *Melanconis*,
   *Sclerophoma*, *Cytospora*, *Phoma*, *Gelatinosporium*, *Diplodia*, and
associated with diffuse cankers.

## Fungi Associated with Galls

Gall-inducing fungi include several distinct groups:

- **Rust fungi**: *[[endocronartium-harknessii]]* on lodgepole pine and
  *Gymnosporangium* species on juniper cause prominent galls.

- **Apiosporina morbosa**: Causes galls on *Prunus* species in temperate
  climates (black knot of plum/cherry).

- **Synchytrium** (Chytridiales): A large genus of obligately biotrophic
  microfungi with ~120 species causing galls on algae, mosses, ferns, and
  flowering plants. Over 1,350 plant species in 800+ genera serve as hosts.
  *S. endobioticum* causes potato wart disease. Identification is based
  primarily on host determination. Galls range from scattered and inconspicuous
  to large and brightly colored.

- **Cyttaria**: A parasitic discomycete known only from galled branches of 12
  south-temperate *Nothofagus* species. Three groups are recognized based on
  geography, [[powdery-mildews-erysiphaceae-diversity-host-range]], and morphological features.

- **Plasmodiophoromycetes**: 15 genera of obligate endoparasites causing
  gall-like underground symptoms. Characterized by multinucleate, unwalled
  protoplasts developing into sporangia or cystosori within host cells.
  Identification requires [[biodiversity-of-fungi-endophytic-fungi]]
