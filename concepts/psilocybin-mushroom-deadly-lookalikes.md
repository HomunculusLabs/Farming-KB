# Psilocybin Mushroom Deadly Lookalikes

The [[forensic-identification-methods-psilocybin-mushrooms-tlc-gc-hplc]] states that if a gilled mushroom has purplish brown to black spores AND the flesh bruises bluish, it is very likely a psilocybin-producing species.

While there are few exceptions, the critical safety distinction is spore color: [[partial-veil-tropism-protection-before-spore-release]] that leaves a membranous ring or fibrillose annular zone.

### Galerina autumnalis (Deadly Galerina)

Cap: 1-6.5 cm broad, convex to broadly convex, dull yellowish brown to orangish brown to cinnamon.

Margin is translucent-striate when moist, surface slightly viscid to viscid.

Gills are golden yellowish brown to rusty brown, becoming dull cinnamon at maturity.

Stem: 20-60 mm long by 3-8 mm thick, grayish brown overall and darkening from the base upwards.

Partial veil leaves a small, fragile [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]].

Primarily found on decayed conifer and hardwood logs, woody debris, wood chips, bark mulch, or in newly laid lawns.

Other deadly Galerina species include G. cinnamomea, G. marginata, and many others that have not been tested.

### Confusion with Psilocybe stuntzii

The general shape of Galerina autumnalis can be very similar to Psilocybe stuntzii.

Both are small, squat mushrooms with brown caps and a ring on their stems.

Mycenoid Galerinas closely resemble [[field-guide-psilocybin-bluing-reaction-identification]].

Confusing a Gymnopilus with a Galerina could be deadly.
