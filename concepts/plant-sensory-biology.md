## Plant Sensory Biology

## Overview

Plant sensory biology is the study of how plants perceive and respond to
environmental stimuli. Daniel Chamovitz"s "What a Plant Knows" (2012)
systematically explores the [[plant-gravitropism-spatial-orientation]]
(gravitropism), and memory (priming and acquired responses). While
plants
lack nervous systems and brains, they possess sophisticated molecular
mechanisms for detecting and responding to their environment that
parallel
animal sensory systems in function, if not in mechanism.

## What a Plant Sees

Plants detect light across a broader spectral range than humans,
including
ultraviolet (280-400 nm), visible blue, green, red, and far-red (700-800
nm)
wavelengths. They use this information for phototropism (directional
growth
toward light), photomorphogenesis (developmental responses to light
quality),
photoperiodism (day-length measurement for flowering control), and shade
avoidance (detecting and responding to competing vegetation).

Plant photoreceptors include phytochromes (red/far-red), cryptochromes
(blue/UV-A), phototropins (blue, for phototropism), and UVR8 (UV-B).
Each
family mediates specific responses. Plants distinguish between direct
sunlight and shade by the red:far-red ratio, because chlorophyll in
neighboring leaves absorbs red light but reflects far-red light,
shifting
the ratio detected by phytochromes.
