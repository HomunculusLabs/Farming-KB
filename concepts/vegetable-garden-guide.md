## Vegetable Garden Guide

Comprehensive reference for home vegetable gardening based on Purdue University Extension guidelines (Lerner & Dana, HO-32-W). Covers planning, soil preparation, planting, care, and harvest.

## Site Selection

Vegetables grow best in open, level areas with loose, rich, well-drained soil. Minimum 6 hours direct sunlight daily. Improve poor soils by mixing in 2-3 inches of topsoil, peat moss, strawy manure, compost, or leaves plus fertilizer. Avoid heavy clays, sandy soils, and shady spots.

**Juglone warning**: Walnut trees produce juglone, toxic to many plants (especially tomatoes). Keep a minimum 50 feet between walnut trees and sensitive plants — roots remain active for years after tree removal.

### Layout Principles

- Tall crops (corn, tomatoes, pole beans) at the north end to avoid shading smaller plants
- Group early vegetables together (lettuce, radishes, green onions)
- Plant corn in blocks of several short rows (not one long row) for proper pollination
- Stagger plantings for extended harvest (e.g., weekly cabbage/bean plantings)
- Reference a 20x50 ft sample plan: alternating rows of root crops, leaf crops, and successional plantings

## Planting Technique

### Seed Planting
- Use hoe handle for fine seeds, hoe blade for larger seeds
- Barely cover small seed; 1 inch soil over large seed
- Most common error: planting too deep
- Mark rows with cord between stakes
- Moistening furrow before laying seed helps germination
- Light mulch (chopped straw, grass clippings, compost, vermiculite) prevents crusting

### Transplanting
- Buy sturdy, green transplants of known cultivars
- Tear off rim and bottom of peat pots before planting (edges wick moisture away)
- Plant in late afternoon/evening to reduce wilting
- "Harden off" by gradual outdoor exposure 1-2 weeks before planting
- Dig holes 2x width and depth of soil ball
- Set plant at same level it grew previously; water with starter solution
- Shade new transplants if sunny days expected

### Soil Readiness Test
If you can crumble a soil ball with your fingers, it is ready to plant. If it clings together, it is too wet. Working wet soil creates hard clods lasting weeks.

## Watering

- Apply 1-1.5 inches per week in a single watering (unless rain provides it)
- Light sprinklings do more harm than good — roots form near surface and become drought-susceptible
- Sandy soil requires more frequent watering than clay
- Water slowly for deep penetration; check that moisture reaches at least 6 inches
- Verify with straight-sided cans or rain gauges

## Weed Control

- Best time: when weeds are small
- Use sharp hoe with shallow shaving stroke (1/4-1/2 inch deep); never chop
- Deeper cultivation brings weed seeds to surface and damages plant roots
- Use wheel hoe for large plots
- Hand-pull weeds close to plants while small
- 2-4 inches organic mulch controls most weeds

### Mulching

**Black plastic**: Increases soil temperature; ideal for warm-season crops (tomatoes, eggplants, peppers, vine crops). Apply before planting.

**Organic mulches** (straw, corn cobs, bark chips, sawdust, compost): Keep soil cooler; ideal for moderating summer heat. Apply in late May/early June after soil warms. Use 4 inches for coarse material, 2 inches for fine material (grass clippings).

**Caution**: Any mulch may keep soil too damp during rainy seasons.

## Insect and Disease Control

1. Identify the problem first
2. Plant disease-resistant cultivars
3. Rotate crops yearly
4. Practice garden sanitation (remove debris, clean edges)
5. Hand-pick insect pests when possible
6. Use commercial sprays/dusts as directed, covering undersides of leaves

## Frost Protection

Cover tender crops (tomatoes, peppers, eggplants) with cardboard boxes, blankets, hot caps, fabric row covers, or newspaper tents. Do not let covers touch plants. Never use metal or plastic sheets (conduct cold). Remove covers in morning.
