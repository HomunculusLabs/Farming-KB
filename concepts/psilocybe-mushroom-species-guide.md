This guide covers the major psilocybin-containing mushroom species relevant to cultivation, foraging, and identification. Species are organized by cultivation difficulty and habitat.

## [[mushroom-cultivation-overview]] provides complete cultivation procedures
- Incubation: 70-80F; Fruiting: 65-85F
- Air changes: 2-3 per hour (Stamets)
- Fruiting cycle: 4-6 weeks from spore inoculation

### Potency
- Variable; up to ~1.0% psilocybin on brown rice (Gartz)
- Typically lower than P. semilanceata or P. azurescens
- Strongly dependent on substrate, harvest timing, and preservation
- Immature specimens significantly more potent than mature

### Dosage Reference (Stamets)
- 1-2g dried (P. semilanceata equivalent): moderate effects, 4-5 hours
- 2g: first colorful geometric patterns, auditory changes
- 3g+: visual waves, liquid-state visual distortions
- 4g+: intense experience, 4-5 hours
- 5g: nearly overwhelming, up to 6 hours
- Above 7g: not recommended even for experienced users

## [[panaeolus-cyanescens-entity]] (Copelandia cyanescens)

### Distribution and Habitat
- Tropical and subtropical regions worldwide
- Grows on cow dung in pastures
- Contains psilocybin and psilocin

### Cultivation (from source)
- Requires dung-based substrate (cow dung + straw + vermiculite)
- Does not colonize grain as densely as P. cubensis
- Very sensitive to CO2 -- insufficient FAE causes tall, spindly mushrooms with small caps and many aborts
- Prefers temperatures around 25C or slightly higher
- Casing layer (1-1.5 cm) required for fruiting
- Not all substrains from multispore germination are viable; cloning best performers is recommended
