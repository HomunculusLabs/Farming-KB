## Cannabis Genetics — Dominant and Recessive Traits, Test Crosses, and Breeding

## Overview

Cannabis breeding relies on understanding Mendelian genetics — how dominant and recessive genes are inherited and expressed. By controlling crosses, breeders can select for desirable traits like potency, bud colour, yield, and growth characteristics. This page covers the genetic principles underlying cannabis breeding.

## Dominant and Recessive Genes

- **Dominant genes**: Denoted by capital letters (e.g., **B**). Have a stronger effect; if present, they win over recessive genes at the same locus.
- **Recessive genes**: Denoted by lowercase letters (e.g., **b**). Only expressed when both alleles are recessive.

### Example: Bud Size

| Genotype | Phenotype | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| BB | [[big-bud]] | Homozygous dominant — both alleles dominant |
| Bb | Big Bud | Heterozygous — dominant allele wins |
| bb | Small Bud | Homozygous recessive — both alleles recessive |

Since B (big bud) is dominant, a plant with Bb genotype will still produce big bud. Only bb produces small bud.

## Modifying Genes

Through breeding, specific traits can be targeted:
- **Bud colour** (e.g., silver bud).
- **Potency**.
- **Yield**.
- **Growth characteristics**.
- **Aroma and flavour** (terpene profiles).

## The Test Cross

### Purpose

Determining the **genotype** (not just the visible phenotype) of a plant is critical for breeding programs. A plant"s phenotype can be observed directly, but its genotype is "hidden" — you cannot determine from appearance alone whether a plant showing a dominant trait is homozygous dominant (BB) or heterozygous (Bb).

### Method

1. Cross the plant in question with a plant known to be **homozygous recessive** (bb) for that trait.
2. Observe the offspring:
   - If **any offspring show the recessive phenotype**, the test plant is **heterozygous (Bb)**.
   - If **all offspring show the dominant phenotype**, the test plant is likely **homozygous dominant (BB)**.

### Breeding Implications

- If both parent plants are **homozygous dominant (BB)** for a desired trait, there is **no way** to produce a recessive genotype for that trait in the offspring.
- This is how breeders **stabilize** desirable traits in a strain — by selecting homozygous dominant parents over multiple generations.
- Test crosses ensure that the traits you value will actually be preserved in subsequent generations.

## Breeding Program Design

When creating seeds from a purchased strain:

1. **Identify desired traits** — potency, yield, flavour, colour, etc.
2. **Determine genotype** — use test crosses to identify homozygous dominant parents.
3. **Select homozygous parents** — ensures trait stability in offspring.
4. **Cross and evaluate** — grow out F1 generation and assess.
5. **Backcross if needed** — cross offspring back to parent to reinforce traits.
