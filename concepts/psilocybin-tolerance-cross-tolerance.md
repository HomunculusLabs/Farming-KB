# Psilocybin Tolerance and Cross-Tolerance

[[mushroom-cancer-clinical-trials-overview]] using psilocybin must space administrations at least one week apart. This is a critical methodological constraint that affects:

- **Study design:** Sessions cannot be scheduled more frequently than weekly
- **Study duration:** Research programs requiring multiple sessions take weeks or months
- **Cost:** Extended timelines increase research costs significantly
- **Subject retention:** Long study durations may affect participant retention

### European Psycholytic Therapy Practice

In European [[adverse-effects-and-harm-reduction-psilocybin]] advice is to wait at least one week between psychedelic experiences. This recommendation is based on:

- Tolerance dynamics (effects are significantly diminished if taken sooner)
- Integration needs (psychological processing of the experience takes time)
- Safety margins (reduced tolerance means users may be tempted to take dangerously large doses)

### The Escalation Trap

Tolerance creates a dangerous feedback loop:

1. User takes dose X on Day 1 and has a strong experience
2. On Day 3, user takes dose X again and has a weak experience (tolerance)
3. User increases to dose 1.5X on Day 5 and has a moderate experience
4. User increases to dose 2X on Day 7 -- but by now tolerance is partially recovered
5. User takes a much larger dose than intended and has an overwhelming experience

This pattern has been responsible for many difficult experiences, particularly among frequent users who do not account for tolerance recovery.

### Microdosing and Tolerance

The relationship between microdosing and tolerance is complex:

- Very low doses (sub-perceptual) may produce less receptor downregulation
- However, daily microdosing still produces measurable tolerance over time
- Most microdosing protocols recommend "off days" (e.g., 5 days on, 2 days off) specifically to manage tolerance
- Some practitioners recommend longer breaks (1-2 weeks every 1-2 months)

## Historical Context

### Hofmann's Observations

Albert Hofmann noted the rapid development of tolerance in his earliest self-experiments with psilocybin. This was one of the first pharmacological properties to be characterized, and it became a key tool in the investigation of psilocybin's mechanism of action.

### The Sandoz Research Program

During the [[timothy-leary-harvard-psilocybin-experiments]] initially used frequent dosing schedules before adjusting to account for tolerance. The Harvard group's experience with tolerance contributed to the development of more systematic dosing protocols.
