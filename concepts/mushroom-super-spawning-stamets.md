# Super Spawning and Active Mycelium Techniques

## Overview

Super spawning, also called "active mycelium spawning" or the Hunke-Till
process, is a method of inoculating [[comparison-grain-spawn-vs-grain-to-grain-transfer]]. This technique
allows cultivators to pyramid a small quantity of initial inoculum into a
considerable amount of fully colonized substrate, offering significant
efficiency advantages for certain cultivation scenarios.

## How Super Spawning Works

The process follows a straightforward principle: first, a set amount of
substrate is inoculated and colonized in the normal manner using grain spawn or
another method. Once fully colonized, this substrate is used as inoculum
("super spawn") to inoculate increased amounts of a similar substrate. The
newly inoculated substrate colonizes rapidly and becomes available for fruiting
or for yet another round of expansion.

Theoretically, a cultivator could pyramid a small quantity of inoculum into a
very large volume of fully colonized substrate through successive rounds of
super spawning. However, practical limits exist due to [[query-how-to-grow-oyster-mushrooms-at-home]]) and
Stropharia rugoso-annulata are excellent candidates.

The technique is less appropriate for species that require sterile substrates,
have slow colonization rates, or are being cultivated in indoor environments
where traditional grain spawn methods are already practical. Species requiring
supplemented substrates or specific nutritional profiles may not perform well
with the simple substrate-to-substrate transfer approach.

## Advantages vs Grain Spawn

Super spawning offers several advantages over traditional grain spawn methods.
It eliminates the need for jars, pressure cooking, and sterile laboratory work
for subsequent generations after the initial grain spawn step. This dramatically
reduces equipment costs and labor requirements. A small amount of colonized
material can inoculate large volumes of new substrate rapidly.
