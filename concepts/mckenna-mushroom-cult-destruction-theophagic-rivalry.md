# Mushroom Cult Destruction and Theophagic Rivalry in Colonial Mexico

## Overview

a literal embodiment of the divine that could be consumed to establish direct
communion with sacred realities.

This conception of theophagy—divine consumption—was structurally parallel to
the Christian Eucharist, in which bread and wine are transubstantiated into the
body and blood of Christ. But where the Christian theophagy required
institutional mediation (the priest, the Mass, the Church), the mushroom
theophagy was immediate and direct: the mushroom itself was the flesh of the
gods, and consuming it established unmediated contact with the divine.

## The Franciscan Response

The Franciscan missionaries who accompanied the Spanish conquest were, according
to McKenna, deeply threatened by the existence of the mushroom cults. Their
theological framework had no room for a rival theophagy that did not require
the institutional Church as intermediary. The mushroom represented a form of
divine access that bypassed the entire apparatus of priestly mediation,
confession, penance, and ecclesiastical authority.

McKenna's account emphasizes that "the Franciscans assumed they had an absolute
monopoly on theophagy, the eating of God; yet in the New World they came upon
people calling a mushroom teonanácatl, the flesh of the gods." The discovery
of a competing theophagy was not merely a theological inconvenience but an
existential threat to the missionary project.

## The Inquisition and Cultural Suppression

The response of the colonial authorities was the Inquisition. McKenna described
how the Inquisition "was able to push the old religion into the mountains of
Oaxaca so that it only survived in a few villages when Valentina and Gordon
Wasson found it there in the 1950s." The mushroom cult survived for four
hundred years in remote villages, preserved by indigenous practitioners who
maintained the ceremonies in secret despite the threat of persecution.

The suppression of the mushroom cult was part of a broader pattern of cultural
destruction that accompanied the Spanish conquest. McKenna connected this to
what he called "ignorance"—the Hellenistic-Christian-Judaic tradition that had
previously "burned the libraries of the Hellenistic world" and "dispersed the
ancient knowledge, shattering the stellar and astronomical machinery that had
been the work of centuries."

## The Wasson Rediscovery

The survival of the mushroom cult into the [[coleman-louis-savier-twentieth-century-maraicher]] was due to the
isolation of the Mazatec villages in the mountains of Oaxaca. In 1955, R.
Gordon Wasson, a vice president of J.P. Morgan and amateur mycologist, traveled
to Mexico and became the first Westerner to participate in a mushroom ceremony
guided by the Mazatec curandera María Sabina.
