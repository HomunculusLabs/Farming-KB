## Salvia divinorum

## Overview

Salvia divinorum is a psychoactive herb in the mint family (Lamiaceae), endemic to a small region of the Sierra Mazateca in Oaxaca, Mexico. It is the only known natural source of **salvinorin A**, a neoclerodane diterpene and the most potent naturally occurring psychedelic compound known. The Mazatec people have used S. divinorum in divination and healing rituals for generations, though the exact antiquity of its use is debated.

## Botany

- Family: Lamiaceae (mint family)
- Growth habit: perennial herb, 0.5-1.5 m tall
- Stems: square (characteristic of Lamiaceae), hollow, easily rooted from cuttings
- Leaves: large (up to 30 cm), ovate, hairy, dark green with lighter underside
- Flowers: white with purple calyces, rarely produced in cultivation
- Reproduction: almost exclusively vegetative; seeds are extremely rare and often non-viable
- Habitat: cloud forest edges, along streambanks, in shaded humid ravines at 300-1500 m elevation
- Cultivar diversity: several named strains exist (Blosser, Wasson-Hofmann, Luna, Appaloosa)

### Ecological Mystery

S. divinorum rarely sets viable seed, and its native range is remarkably restricted — possibly a clonal cultivar maintained by indigenous people for centuries. This has led to speculation that it may be a cultigen (a plant altered through long-term human selection) with no truly wild population. ## Salvinorin A — Pharmacology

Salvinorin A is pharmacologically unique among psychedelics:

- **Not a tryptamine, phenethylamine, or lysergamide** — it is a trans-neoclerodane diterpene
- **Potent kappa opioid receptor (KOR) agonist** — this is its primary mechanism of action
- Does not act on serotonin 5-HT2A receptors (the target of most classical psychedelics)
- Does not affect dopamine, norepinephrine, or serotonin receptors directly
- Active dose: 200-1000 micrograms when vaporized (comparable to LSD in potency by weight)
- Duration: typically 5-20 minutes when vaporized; 1-2 hours when held sublingually (quid method)
- Metabolized rapidly; salvinorin B is a minor inactive metabolite

### Effects Spectrum

The subjective effects of salvinorin A are qualitatively different from other psychedelics:

- **Mild doses** (sub-threshold to threshold): subtle visual shifts, laughter, bodily heaviness
- **Moderate doses**: strong visual transformations, sense of merging with objects, altered gravity, visitation by perceived entities
- **Strong doses**: complete dissociation, perceived reality dissolution, amnesia of the experience, encounters with "impossible" geometries and entities
- **Common themes**: cartoony or absurd imagery, mechanical or fabric-like reality textures, the sensation of being pulled, rotated, or folded into other dimensions

The KOR mechanism may explain the characteristic dysphoria, disorientation, and sometimes terrifying nature of the experience, as kappa agonism is associated with dissociative and aversive states in animal models.

## Traditional Mazatec Use

The Mazatec use S. divinorum primarily for:

- **Divination** (seeking respuestas — "seeking answers"): a healer (curandero/a) guides a patient through a session to diagnose illness, locate lost objects, or divine future events
- **Healing**: used to treat ailments including headache, rheumatism, and gastrointestinal complaints
- The leaves are traditionally chewed fresh in pairs (quid method), with the juice held in the mouth for absorption through the buccal mucosa
- Sessions typically occur at night, in darkness or dim candlelight, often accompanied by prayer

## Extraction and Preparation

### Traditional Methods

- **Quid method**: 6-32 large fresh leaves chewed and held in the mouth for 15-30 minutes without swallowing
- **Water infusion**: leaves steeped in water, though this is reportedly less effective due to poor water solubility of salvinorin A

### Modern Methods

- **Smoking/vaporization**: dried leaf enhanced with salvinorin A extract (5x, 10x, 20x, 40x "strengths")
- **Tinctures**: ethanol-based sublingual preparations, standardized to salvinorin A content
- **Pure salvinorin A extraction**: typically using acetone or ethanol solvent extraction followed by chromatographic purification
- **Changa-style blends**: salvinorin A combined with harmala alkaloids to extend duration (experimental)

### Extraction Protocol (Laboratory)

1. Dried leaf material extracted with acetone or chilled ethanol
2. Solvent filtered and evaporated to yield crude extract
3. Defatting with n-hexane removes chlorophyll and waxes
4. Final purification via column chromatography or recrystallization
5. Salvinorin A appears as white crystalline needles; melting point ~238-240 C

## Legal Status Worldwide

Salvinorin A and S. divinorum have been scheduled in an increasing number of jurisdictions:

- **United States**: Not federally scheduled (as of 2026), but regulated in many states (California, Illinois, Louisiana, and others have enacted restrictions)
- **Australia**: Schedule 9 (prohibited substance) since 2002 — one of the earliest countries to ban it
- **Canada**: Not scheduled under the CDSA, though sale may be restricted under food and drug regulations
- **United Kingdom**: Class B controlled drug since 2016
- **Germany**: Not controlled under the BtMG, but sale may be restricted under medical product regulations
- **Brazil**: Not scheduled; available commercially
- **Mexico**: Not scheduled; but traditional knowledge protections may apply
- **Japan**: Not controlled
- Many European and Asian countries have enacted various levels of restriction since ~2010

The rapid spread of online videos showing recreational use contributed significantly to legislative action in multiple countries.

## Safety Considerations

- S. divinorum is generally considered non-toxic with no known fatal overdose
- Primary risks are psychological: extreme fear, confusion, accidental injury during the experience
- A trip sitter is strongly recommended, especially at moderate-to-high doses
- Combining with other substances (particularly other dissociatives or alcohol) increases risk
- No established contraindications for specific psychiatric conditions, though caution is advised for those with psychosis history
- There is no evidence of physical dependence; no withdrawal syndrome
- Regular use may produce diminished effects (tolerance), though the mechanism and duration are not well studied
