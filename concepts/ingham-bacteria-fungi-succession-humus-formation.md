# Bacteria-Then-Fungi Succession and Humus Formation

## Overview

Dr. Elaine Ingham describes a fundamental [[plants-of-the-gods-chemical-structures-hallucinogens]]

During this stage, the easily-extracted nutrients are immobilized in bacterial biomass. The compounds bacteria process are the "easy-to-use sugars" and "simple, rapid growth food resources."

## The Fungal Stage

Fungi arrive after bacteria have processed the simplest compounds:

- Fungi produce enzymes capable of breaking down **lignin** and **cellulose** — complex structural polymers that bacteria alone cannot decompose efficiently
- Fungi begin making the complex "waste products" that become humus, specifically **humic acid**
- Fungal hyphae physically penetrate woody material, accessing nutrients that bacteria cannot reach
- Fungal growth is slower but more sustained than bacterial growth

Ingham states emphatically: "FUNGI finish the process of making humus, not bacteria."

## What Is Humus?

Ingham distinguishes several components within the broad term "humus":

**Humus (broad sense)** includes:
- All living organisms: bacteria, fungi, protozoa, nematodes, roots
- Simple organic matter: simple sugars, amino acids, proteins, simple carbohydrates, lipids
- Intermediate compounds: polysaccharides (multiple simple sugars in branched forms), amino-sugars, hormones, fats, lipopolysaccharides, fulvic acids
- All of the above bound together in intermediate molecular weight fractions

**Humic acid (specific)** is the most complex end-product:
- All of the above bound together in very complex tertiary or three-dimensional binding complexes
- This is the final, stable form that persists in soil for decades to centuries

## The Full Organism Community Required

Real humic acid production requires ALL organisms to be present:

- **Fungi** do the primary humic acid production work
- **Earthworms** help by grinding and mixing materials, increasing surface area for fungal colonization
- **Microarthropods** fragment [[query-how-to-build-a-compost-pile-that-works]] can favor fungi over bacteria
- This speeds up the entire decomposition process, particularly for woody, ligno-cellulosic materials
- Fungi can break down ligno-cellulosic waste without the extreme high temperatures that bacterial-dominated piles require
- The recommended rate is approximately 500 ml per 4 kg of dry waste material (about 2 pints per 10 pounds)
- Molasses also adds trace minerals and some nitrogen

Both cane and beet molasses can be used. This is significant because many compost operations are bacterial-dominated from the start, missing the fungal phase entirely. Favoring fungi from the beginning allows the system to reach humus production faster.

## The Composting Problem

Ingham identifies a common composting failure: most composting operations do not establish fungal communities initially. This means:

- The bacterial stage runs but the fungal stage never fully develops
- Woody materials break down slowly or incompletely
- True humic acid production is limited
