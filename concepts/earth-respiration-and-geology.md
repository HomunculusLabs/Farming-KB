# Earth Respiration and Geology

## Overview

The earth is not inert rock and dirt — it is a living, breathing system. All of the skin and organs of the earth participate in regular respiration, driven by tidal forces, weather patterns, and the activities of countless organisms. This concept is fundamental to [[permaculture]] understanding of soil as a living medium rather than a mere substrate for plant growth.

## The Respiration of Earth

### Mechanisms of Earth Breathing

The "diaphragm" or energy for earth's respiration comes from multiple sources:

- **Moon tides** in water, earth, and air drive rhythmic expansion and contraction
- **Rainwater filling soil** forces exhalation of air from pore spaces
- **Drying-out** of soil causes inhalation as air is drawn back in
- **Fast winds** disturb boundary layers, create low pressure, and trigger soil exhalation
- **Slow winds and high pressure** force inhalation
- **Millions of earth animals** open breathing tubes that force exchange between atmosphere and the waters, soils, and sea-sands in which they live

### Deeper Respiratory Processes

Beyond surface-level gas exchange, deeper processes occur:

- **Deeper flows and fissures** carry radon gas and methane seeping from the earth
- **Earth expansion** creates great inward and outward flows through multitudinous fissures in rigid sediments
- This respiration **transports and transforms fluids** and their associated loads — solutes, states, and ionic potential — from earth to atmosphere to ocean
- These transformations **set up the potentials that create thunderstorms and hurricanes**

### The Lung Analogy

The burrows of spiders, gophers, and worms are to the soil what the **alveoli of our lungs** are to our bodies. Without these countless breathing tubes, the soil suffocates. The implication is clear: we can assist this essential respiration by assisting life and natural processes in soils.

## The Navajo Warning and Its Vindication

The Navajo warning — *"If you kill off the prairie dogs, there will be no one to cry for rain"* — was dismissed by amused scientists in the 1950s who recommended exterminating all burrowing animals to protect sparse desert grass roots near Chilchinbito, Arizona. The area has since become a **virtual wasteland** from fierce run-off, soil compaction, and lack of fresh seedbed.

In contrast, the **Page ranch** used prairie dog burrows as water sinks, directing water run-off to flood down them and germinate stored underground seed — creating a productive dryland pasture. This demonstrates that burrowing animals are integral to the water cycle and soil health.

## Geology and Life Forms

### Biogenic Rocks and Minerals

Many rocks and strata on Earth arise directly from living organisms:

- **Manganese nodules** in oceanic depths
- **Diatomaceous earth** deposits
- **Coal** formations
- **Limestone** (from coral and shell deposits)
- **Opals and amber**
- **Coral sands** of oceanic islands

Much of the visible strata, except heavily altered granitic and volcanic deposits, were formed from or modified by life. **All soils are life-created.**

### Life as Earth Mover

Life actively transports and overturns earth materials:

- **Sea kelp drifts** float along coasts carrying hundreds of tons of volcanic boulders held in their roots — these streams can extend over 300 km
- Kelp strands boulders onto ancient basalt piles, all of which were carried there by living organisms
- **Round stones** dredged from great mid-Atlantic depths were likely carried there by drift kelp, not formed in place
- **Petrels** (Puffinus tenuirostris) fill their crops with Tasmanian pebbles, seeds, and charcoal before flying to Japan and Alaska, where they void the material somewhere in the Pacific
- **Life moderates every erosion process**, every river basin, every cliff and rock fall

### The Hydraulic Effect of Forests

The hydraulic weight of great forests — such as those once covering the Americas — would have exceeded any water catchment weight we can now afford to build, dispersing it over far greater areas. This greatly **moderates climate** and with it geological processes.

### The Consequences of Deforestation

Across Iran, Greece, North Africa, the USA, Mexico, Pakistan, and Australia, the consequences of life destruction are visible:

- The **hard bones of the earth** have been brought to the surface by stripping the life skin for ephemeral uses
- Continued destruction could create a **moon-landscape of the earth**
- Poor goatherds wander where lake-forests once stood
- Religions of resignation and fanaticism followed those of the nature gods
- Man-built temples replaced trees and tree spirits

## Root Systems as Geological Agents

### Root Diversity Underground

Roots create an underground topography that parallels aerial plant diversity:

- **Depth**: Agricultural crops exploit 0.6-4 m; some trees penetrate to **50 m** in deep desert sands
- **Patterns**: Horizontal spread varies enormously by species and conditions
- **Seasonal migrations**: Roots move to and from the surface on seasonal schedules
- **Underground storage**: Ligno-tubers, tubers, bulbs, and rhizomes are common
- **Root space sharing**: Scheduled so different species occupy root zones at different times

### Mineral Cycling by Roots

Around the roots of dune trees, calcium and other minerals are deposited as **stone-like secretions** by root-associated fungi and bacteria. This demonstrates that roots don't merely extract minerals — they actively transform and redeposit them.

### Root Interactions

- Spring bulbs feed, flower, and die before tree roots begin upward thrust
- Tap-rooted late starters (thistles, comfrey) reach deep for late summer moisture
- Some plants and fungi use autumn rains for flowering and dispersal
- Where no cold season exists (low latitudes), **aerial roots** develop or strangler figs send roots from tree crotches to earth, building great buttresses as they strangle host trees

### Allelopathy and Competition

Some species actively suppress competitors through root chemistry:

- **Phenol/creosote secreters**: Bracken, tamarisk, Juglandaceae, Brassicas
- **Physical encapsulators**: Eucalyptus, willows, tamarisks surround and smother competitors
- **Predatory plants**: Marigolds, fungi, and Crotalaria trap or poison nematodes

### Engineering Applications of Roots

- **Unstable slopes** are pegged with "tree nails" — chestnut, pine, oak, and walnut roots
- Slopes cleared of forest in Tasmania are only now starting to collapse after a hundred years as roots rot
- **Bamboo** not only holds landslides but provides earthquake-proof root mattresses for light structures
- **Swamp vegetation root mats** save bulldozers from watery graves
- **Prairie fibrous root webs** defeat wind erosion

## Key Principles

1. **The earth breathes** — [[soil-respiration]] is a real, measurable phenomenon driven by physical and biological forces
2. **All soils are created by life** — abiotic rock becomes soil only through biological activity
3. **Burrowing organisms are the lungs of the earth** — their tunnels are the alveoli of soil
4. **Life moves mountains** — kelp carries boulders, petrels transport pebbles, roots crack rock
5. **Deforestation exposes geology** — removing the "life skin" reveals bare earth that erodes rapidly
6. **Roots are geological agents** — they penetrate, decompose, redeposit, and stabilise earth materials
7. **Underground diversity mirrors aboveground diversity** — design for the whole system, surface and subsurface
