### Spawn Availability
There is no single spawn manufacturing company in Kenya. Farmers must import
spawn at KES600/kg (USD7.80) including airfreight, or produce their own from
culture collections. Self-produced spawn often suffers from quality loss after a
few months due to [[growing-gourmet-sawdust-spawn-production-use]].

### Information Secrecy
Commercial farms restrict access and keep growing procedures secret. Information
exchange between researchers and farmers is very poor. Technical expertise comes
from personnel trained in Japan, Britain, France, or Belgium. Local farmers
cannot afford such training.

### Employment Structure
Commercial farms employ a small number of well-paid managers/technicians and
many low-wage unskilled workers (up to 50 per farm). Over half the wage budget
goes to the management group. Low wages for workers limit the industry's
economic impact on local communities.

## Small-Scale and Home Cultivation

In August 2003, a project was initiated involving over 100 families growing
Pleurotus sajor-caju at home in small spaces like kitchens. The aim was to
provide alternative food sources to counter malnutrition. Several domesticated
Pleurotus species from Karura and Kakamega forests grow fast and fruit readily
in local tropical conditions. Some farmers have expanded beyond home consumption
to market surplus production.
