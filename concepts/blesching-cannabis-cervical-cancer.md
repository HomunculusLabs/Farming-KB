# Cannabis and Cervical Cancer

## Overview

Cervix is Latin for "neck." Cancer of the cervix uteri is the development of
cancerous cells at the narrow neck of the uterus. The uterus begins at the
interior end of the vagina and, together with the fallopian tubes and ovaries,
comprises the female reproductive system.

Initial symptoms of cervical cancer may include vaginal bleeding, discharge,
and painful intercourse. Symptoms of more advanced cancerous development may
include lower abdominal pains, pelvic pain, lower back pain, groin pain, and
persistent vaginal bleeding. General cancer signs may also be present, such as
loss of appetite, weight loss, and low energy.

## Risk Factors

Orthodox medicine hypothesizes numerous possible causes and co-factors for
cervical cancer development among higher-risk females, including:
- Family history of cervical cancer
- Poverty and substance abuse
- Human papillomavirus (HPV)
- HIV/AIDS and [[critical-haze-strain-profile-resin-seeds-oner]].

### THC and Tumor Invasiveness

By 2008, the Rostock experiment confirmed anandamide's protective abilities,
as well as those of THC, in significantly decreasing cervical tumor cell
invasiveness, even at very low dosages. This finding is notable because it
suggests that cannabinoids may reduce the metastatic potential of cervical
cancer cells.
