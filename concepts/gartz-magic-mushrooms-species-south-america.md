of the magic mushroom land." A large number of golden pendants in a

## Peru

Psychoactive mushroom species, primarily P. cubensis and Panaeolus
species, have been used in Peru. During the 1980s, roadside signs
proclaiming "No To San Ysidro Drugs!" referred to P. cubensis under an
old name, indicating familiarity with the species among the local
population.

## Brazil

Brazil is identified as one of two South American countries (along with
Argentina) where magic mushroom use was still common at the time of
Gartz's writing. In 1975, Pollock reported a Brazilian practice of mixing
a drink from milk, honey, bananas, and magic mushrooms.

## Amazonia

P. cubensis and Panaeolus species are common across Amazonian regions
where Colombia, Peru, and Brazil share common borders. The tropical
climate and abundance of cattle pastures provide ideal habitat for these
dung-inhabiting species.

## Argentina

Argentina is noted as a country where magic mushroom use is still common.
The country also served as the origin of feed grain imports that
introduced Gymnopilus purpuratus spores to Europe.

## Research Gaps

Gartz emphasizes that research into the taxonomy and chemistry of South
American psychotropic species has barely begun. The continent offers vast
opportunities for uncovering additional clues about ritual usage among
early cultures.
