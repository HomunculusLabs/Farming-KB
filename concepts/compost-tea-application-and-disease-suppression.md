# Compost Tea Application and Disease Suppression

## Overview

Compost tea is a liquid extract teeming with beneficial microorganisms that can
dramatically improve plant health and suppress a wide range of diseases. Unlike
chemical treatments, compost tea carries no risk of over-application—it will
never burn roots or leaves, and repeated use increases microbial diversity.

## General Principles

- There is no upper limit on compost tea application; more is never harmful.
- The tea does not chemically burn plant tissue, unlike synthetic fertilizers.
- Each successive application builds greater microbial diversity and complexity.
- A prophylactic (preventive) approach is far more effective than reactive
  treatment—healthy microbiomes prevent pathogens from gaining a foothold.

## Application Methods

### Soil Drench

Apply tea directly to the soil to deliver microbes to the root zone and
rhizosphere. Use a cup, bucket, or **plastic watering can**—never metal, as
bacteria can solubilize zinc from metal containers, altering the tea's
chemistry.

### Foliar Spray

Foliar application protects leaf surfaces by coating them with beneficial
organisms that occupy niche space and consume pathogen food sources:

- Must cover **at least 70% of the leaf surface**, including both top and bottom
  sides.
- Microbes need **15–30 minutes** to attach securely before environmental
  factors can dislodge them.

## Timing and Environmental Considerations

### UV Protection

UV radiation kills beneficial bacteria and fungi on contact:

- Apply **before 10:00 AM or after 3:00 PM** in southern latitudes.
- Alternatively, use spray droplets of **at least 1 mm** diameter so bacteria
  develop a protective slime layer before water evaporates.

### Sprayer Pressure

- Maximum pressure should not exceed **70 psi**.
- Application velocity should be slow—tea should parachute down, never splat.
- **Electrostatic sprayers** may destroy microbes with incorrect charge.

## Equipment and Straining

Bacteria are microscopic, but fungi and nematodes can be filtered out by
equipment that is too fine:

- **Straining:** Minimum **400-micrometer mesh**, or decant after 15 minutes.
- **Pump sprayers:** Use a concrete sprayer or any sprayer with wide orifices
  that won't shred fungal hyphae.
- **Large yards:** Gasoline-powered backpack mist sprayer.
- **Lawns:** Traveling sprinkler with fertilizer (siphon) dispenser.

## Shelf Life and Storage

Compost tea is a living product with limited viability:

- **Optimal:** Use within **4 hours** of manufacture.
- **Extended:** Refrigeration or continued aeration extends shelf life to
  **3–5 days**.
- **Warning signs:** Foul, putrid, or sulfurous odor = anaerobic. Discard
  immediately—anaerobic tea may contain ethanol, ammonia, or hydrogen sulfide
  toxic to plants.

## Application Rates

| Scenario | Rate |
|---|---|
| Soil drench only | 5 gallons per acre |
| Soil drench + foliar spray | 10 gallons per acre |
| Intensive treatment (¼-acre lot) | ~60 gallons per week |

Tea may be diluted with dechlorinated water for coverage, though full-strength
maximizes microbial delivery.

## Application Frequency

### Chemically Fertilized Lawns (Recovery Protocol)

1. **Every other week** for 3 months—rebuild depleted microbial populations.
2. **Monthly** for one growing season—establish stable communities.
3. **3 times per year** thereafter for maintenance.

### New Lawns (Establishment Protocol)

- Apply in **late autumn** after leaves fall and in **early spring** before new
  growth.
- Use 10 gallons/acre soil drench + 5 gallons/acre foliar spray.

## Disease Suppression

Compost tea suppresses disease through competitive exclusion, antibiosis,
induced systemic resistance, and direct predation of pathogens.

### Fungal-Dominated Teas

Effective against: powdery mildew, downy mildew, take-all, gray/pink snow mold,
red thread, crown/root rots, damping off, brown patch, summer patch, rusts,
fairy rings.

### Bacterial-Dominated Teas

Effective against: dollar spot, necrotic ring spot, yellow patch, leaf spots,
pink patch, stripe smut.

### Insect Pest Impacts

Beneficial microbes affect insect populations within **7 days**: chafers,
weevils, grubs, ants, scale insects, and whiteflies.

## Weed Management Through Microbial Balance

### Bacterial/Protozoal Teas (High Nitrogen Cycling)

Adding protozoa and nematodes increases nitrogen mineralization, suppressing
low-nitrogen weeds: **clover** and **quack grass**.

### Fungal-Dominated Teas (Low Nitrate Availability)

Fungal teas reduce soil nitrate levels, disadvantaging nitrate-loving weeds:
**plantains**, **chickweed**, **nut sedges** (disappear); **ivy** responds to
highly fungal teas.

## Key Takeaways

- Compost tea is fundamentally safe—over-application is impossible.
- Use plastic equipment, low pressure, wide orifices, and coarse straining.
- Time applications to avoid UV and ensure foliar microbial attachment.
- Match fungal vs. bacterial dominance to target pathogens and weeds.
- Consistent preventive application outperforms emergency reactive treatments.
