## End-Substrate versus Base Substrate Supplements

Stamets distinguishes between two supplementation strategies: end-substrate
supplementation and base substrate supplementation. End-substrate supplementation
involves mixing supplements throughout the entire substrate mass before
sterilization or pasteurization. This approach ensures uniform nutrient
distribution and supports rapid, even colonization. However, it also means that
every portion of the substrate is enriched, creating abundant food sources for
competing organisms throughout the entire mass.

Base substrate supplementation, by contrast, concentrates supplements in a
defined layer — typically at the bottom or center of the substrate block. The
unsupplemented portions act as a buffer zone that the mycelium must first
colonize before reaching enriched material. By the time the mycelium reaches the
supplemented zone, it has achieved sufficient biomass density to outcompete most
contaminants. This strategy reduces overall contamination risk while still
delivering much of the yield benefit associated with supplementation.

optimal levels, additional supplement provides diminishing yield returns while
dramatically increasing the probability of contamination by competing fungi and
bacteria. Enriched substrates are inherently more attractive to contaminants
than unenriched substrates, and the risk escalates sharply as supplement
concentrations rise.
