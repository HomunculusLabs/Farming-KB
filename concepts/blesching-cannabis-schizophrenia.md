---# Cannabis and Schizophrenia

## Overview

Schizophrenia is a severe psychiatric disorder characterized by distortions in
thinking, perception, emotions, language, sense of self, and behavior. Common
symptoms include hallucinations, delusions, disorganized thinking, and impaired
social functioning. The condition typically emerges in late adolescence or
early adulthood and can be profoundly debilitating.

The relationship between cannabis and schizophrenia has been the subject of
considerable scientific debate. Observational studies have concluded that
consuming cannabis as an adolescent may increase one's risk of developing
schizophrenia later in life. However, cannabis is not itself considered a
causal factor for schizophrenia; in some instances it may be a co-factor.

## Key Distinction: Risk Factor vs. Treatment

Blesching makes an important distinction between THC and CBD in the context
of schizophrenia:

- **THC** (the primary psychoactive component) may act as a risk co-factor
  for susceptible individuals, particularly adolescents with a family history
  of psychosis. Its psychotomimetic properties (ability to produce
  psychosis-like symptoms) are well documented.

- **CBD** (the non-psychoactive component) shows promise as an antipsychotic
  agent, with studies suggesting it may counteract some of THC's
  psychosis-inducing effects. CBD has greater affinity for CB2 receptors.
