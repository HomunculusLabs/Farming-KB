# Vertical Gardening Guide

Vertical gardening uses trellises, towers, walls, and other structures to grow plants upward, dramatically increasing growing space per square foot of ground. It is essential for [[edible-landscaping]] in small spaces and maximizes light exposure in [[food-forest-design|food forest]] understory layers.

## Trellises

The most common and versatile vertical gardening structure. Trellises support climbing vegetables and flowering vines.

### Types

**A-Frame Trellis**
- Two panels hinged at the top, forming an A shape
- Plant on both sides; lettuce or shade-tolerant crops grow underneath
- Good for cucumbers, peas, and small melons
- Fold flat for storage at season end

**Panel Trellis**
- Flat panel of lattice or wire mesh mounted to a wall, fence, or posts
- Simple construction, minimal footprint
- Best for beans, cucumbers, peas, indeterminate tomatoes
- Attach with zip ties or staples to wooden frame

**Obelisk or Pyramid Trellis**
- Tapered, freestanding structure — decorative and functional
- Ideal for pole beans, morning glories, climbing roses
- Creates strong visual focal point in garden beds

**Teepee Trellis**
- Bamboo or sapling poles lashed at the top, splayed at the base
- Traditional bean support, fast and cheap to build
- Space poles 6-8 inches apart at the base, 4-6 poles per teepee

### Trellis Materials
- Cedar or rot-resistant wood
- Galvanized wire mesh (concrete remesh is cheap and strong)
- Bamboo poles
- Metal livestock panels
- Twine or jute (biodegradable, good for annual crops)

## Tower Gardens

Vertical growing towers that hold multiple plants in a compact, upright format.

### DIY Bucket Tower
- Stack 5-gallon buckets with holes cut in the sides for planting
- Fill with soil mix; water from the top, drains through the chain
- Grow strawberries, herbs, lettuce in side pockets; larger crop on top
- Inexpensive, good for patios and balconies

### PVC Tower
- Vertical PVC pipe with holes drilled for planting pockets
- Internal perforated pipe for central watering
- Fill annular space with soil; cap at top and bottom
- Best for strawberries, herbs, and greens

### Commercial Options
- GreenStalk vertical garden (stackable tiers with built-in watering)
- Garden Tower (composting core in center, 50+ planting pockets)
- Foody towers (hydroponic vertical systems)

### Crops for Towers
- Strawberries (best performer)
- Lettuce, spinach, Swiss chard, Asian greens
- Herbs: basil, cilantro, parsley, thyme, oregano
- Compact peppers and cherry tomatoes (top tier only)

## Living Walls

Vertical gardens where plants grow in panels or pockets attached to a wall surface.

### Pocket Systems
- Felt or fabric pockets mounted on a frame against a wall
- Fill each pocket with lightweight soil mix
- Irrigate with drip line running across the top
- Best for herbs, greens, strawberries, succulents

### Modular Panel Systems
- Rigid panels with angled planting cells
- Automated irrigation built into many commercial systems
- Can cover entire walls for dramatic effect
- Higher cost but more professional appearance

### Construction Considerations
- **Weight**: Wet soil is heavy — 15-25 lbs per square foot; ensure wall can support load
- **Waterproofing**: Install waterproof membrane between living wall and building wall
- **Drainage**: Include drainage tray or channel at the bottom
- **Irrigation**: Drip irrigation essential; hand-watering is impractical
- **Sun exposure**: Most living walls need at least 6 hours of direct sun
- **Plant selection**: Shallow-rooted plants only — no deep-rooted vegetables
- **Maintenance**: Regular watering, occasional fertilizing, replanting as needed

### Best Plants for Living Walls
- Ferns (Boston fern, rabbit's foot fern) for shaded walls
- Succulents (sedum, echeveria) for sunny, low-water walls
- Herbs (thyme, oregano, mint) for kitchen-access walls
- Strawberries for edible walls
- Pothos, spider plant, and tradescantia for indoor walls

## Pallet Gardens

Upcycled shipping pallets converted into vertical planters.

### Safety First
- Use only heat-stamped pallets marked **HT** (heat-treated)
- Never use pallets marked **MB** (methyl bromide — toxic pesticide)
- Avoid pallets from unknown sources (may have chemical spills or contamination)
- Line with landscape fabric to contain soil and prevent leaching

### Construction Methods

**Standing Pallet Garden**
- Staple landscape fabric to the back and bottom of the pallet
- Fill horizontal slat openings with soil
- Slide plants into gaps between slats
- Lean against a wall at a slight angle; secure for safety
- Let soil settle for 2 weeks before standing upright

**Lying-Down Pallet Garden**
- Lay pallet flat, fill with soil between slats
- Plant in the gaps
- Once roots establish (4-6 weeks), stand upright against a wall
- Less risk of soil washout during establishment

## String Trellis

The simplest and cheapest vertical support system, ideal for lightweight climbers.

### Setup
- Anchor sturdy posts or mount eye hooks to a wall
- Run twine, jute string, or wire vertically from top anchor to ground
- Space strings 6-12 inches apart
- Train vines to climb by wrapping tendrils around string

### Best Uses
- Pole beans (train around strings, they self-climb)
- Cucumbers (may need help initially)
- Peas (naturally twine around string)
- Malabar spinach, climbing nasturtium

### Advantages
- Extremely low cost (jute twine is biodegradable)
