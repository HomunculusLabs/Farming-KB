## PF TEK Primordia Formation and Pinning Process

The transition from vegetative [[pf-tek-cultivation-method]] cycle. Understanding the sequence
of primordia formation, pinning, and fruit body development is essential for
optimal timing of cake birthing and harvest.

## Colonization to Fruiting Trigger

After the substrate turns completely white with mycelium (typically 2-3 weeks
after inoculation at 70F), the jars are left in indirect light. The mycelium
continues to infiltrate the substrate, accumulating nutrients and reaching the
biomass threshold needed to trigger the fruiting cycle. This post-colonization
waiting period can range from less than a week to a few weeks.

The cultivator should not rush this stage. Premature birthing of
under-colonized cakes results in poor or no fruiting, because the mycelium has
not yet accumulated sufficient energy reserves. The substrate must be fully
colonized, white throughout, with no visible uncolonized substrate, before the
shift to reproductive mode begins.

## Pinning Triggers

Pinning is triggered by a combination of [[cannabis-vegetative-growth-stage]] to reproductive development. Full
substrate colonization is the prerequisite; the mycelium must have completely
consumed the available nutrients before it will commit energy to fruiting.
This is an evolved response that ensures reproduction occurs only when
sufficient resources have been accumulated.

The primary environmental pinning triggers in the PF TEK system are: high
humidity (near 100% in the dual [[basidiocarp-four-stage-development-classification-psilocybe-cubensis-badham-1982]] fruits in the wild.

## Fresh Air Exchange Requirements

Fresh air exchange (FAE) serves two critical functions during pinning. First,
it reduces the concentration of carbon dioxide that accumulates around the
cake surface. High CO2 levels suppress pin formation and encourage continued
vegetative mycelial growth. Second, FAE introduces evaporative humidity that
creates the microclimate at the cake surface where primordia form.

In the PF TEK system, FAE is provided during the daily spraying procedure.
When the cultivator sprays water into the primary chamber, the act of removing
and replacing the lid allows stale air to escape and fresh air to enter. This
brief exchange, combined with the evaporative humidity from the spray shield
system, provides sufficient gas exchange for pinning without compromising the
high-humidity environment.

Over-ventilation is a risk: removing the lid for extended periods or using
fans near the terrarium can drop humidity below the critical threshold and
cause existing pins to abort. The goal is minimal but adequate air exchange,
not maximum airflow.

## Light Requirements

Light plays a directional rather than nutritional role in mushroom
development. Psilocybe cubensis does not photosynthesize; instead, it uses
light primarily for phototropic guidance, growing upward toward the light
source. A small low-wattage fluorescent plant light can be left on
continuously, but indirect room light (including indirect sunlight) is
sufficient for fruiting and poses no heating risk.

The light must be kept at a safe distance from the terrarium to avoid radiant
heat raising the internal temperature. A room with a window providing ambient
daylight is ideal. Light cycles are not critical; continuous low-level
illumination or a standard 12-hour on/off cycle both produce acceptable
results.

## Timeline from Inoculation to Pins

The complete timeline from [[growing-gourmet-spore-germination-strain-isolation]] begins within
3-5 days at 70F incubation temperature. Full colonization of the brown rice
flour substrate takes 14-21 days depending on temperature, [[dikaryon-formation-mushroom-strain-genetics]],
and inoculation quality. The post-colonization waiting period adds 3-14 days
before the mycelium is ready to fruit.
