# Holzer Natural Animal Husbandry

Animals are co-workers, not merchandise. This principle underpins Holzer's
approach to animal husbandry, where every animal has a purpose in the
ecosystem and is treated with respect and empathy.

## Critique of Industrial Animal Farming

### Confined Animal Operations

Global industrial animal husbandry is described as a disgrace and a disaster
for the earth. Animals are treated as merchandise and moneymakers, not as
living beings. The consequences include:
- Animals kept in cramped, confined spaces
- Cannibalism among stressed animals
- Routine mutilation: tail docking, ear clipping, beak cutting, horn
  burning
- Cattle conditioned with electric shocks to stand in place for automated
  dung removal
- Animals forced to live ankle-deep in their own waste

### The Horn Question

Cattle use their horns as antennae. Without them they lose their sense of
orientation and instincts. Holzer has observed that de-horned cattle do not
sense weather changes, whereas healthy Alpine cows sense storms and descend
to lower altitudes by themselves. Horns also serve as repositories where
toxins can be deposited in the body.

### Open-Land Industrial Farming

In Africa, Brazil, and Argentina, industrial [[holzer-earth-shelters-pigsties]] provide:
- Cooler temperatures in summer and warmth in winter
- Open access at all times
- A central position between several paddocks

### Natural Feeding

Animals in natural environments with free movement seek and find their own
food. They instinctively know which plants to eat for preventing and
treating illness. Animals have been observed eating toxic plants:
- Lupins and foxgloves treat worms and upset stomachs
- Animals must be free to decide what to eat and when

Important: feeding toxic plants in a bucket makes animals sick. The animal
must self-select. Biodiversity in the landscape is essential for natural
self-medication.
