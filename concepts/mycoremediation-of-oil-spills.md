## Overview

Mycoremediation of oil spills uses fungi -- particularly white-rot basidiomycetes -- to break down petroleum hydrocarbons into less harmful compounds. Fungi produce powerful extracellular enzymes (laccases, manganese peroxidases, lignin peroxidases) that can oxidize the complex aromatic structures found in crude oil, diesel, and other petroleum products.

## Why Fungi for Petroleum Cleanup

Petroleum is structurally similar to lignin -- the tough, complex polymer in wood that white-rot fungi evolved to decompose. This evolutionary capability makes them uniquely suited to breaking down aromatic hydrocarbons that bacteria struggle with.

Advantages of fungal remediation over bacterial approaches:
- Enzymes work extracellularly, degrading even poorly soluble compounds
- Hyphal networks penetrate soil aggregates and reach trapped oil
- Fungi tolerate higher hydrocarbon concentrations than many bacteria
- Mycelial mats physically contain and absorb spills

## Key Enzymatic Mechanisms

### Laccase (EC 1.10.3.2)
- Copper-containing oxidase that degrades phenolic compounds
- Works under mild conditions (pH 4-7, ambient temperature)
- Does not require hydrogen peroxide
- Effective against polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs)

### Manganese Peroxidase (MnP, EC 1.11.1.13)
- Oxidizes Mn(II) to Mn(III), creating a diffusible oxidant
- Mn(III) chelates penetrate soil and degrade non-phenolic compounds
- Key enzyme for breaking down high-molecular-weight PAHs

### Lignin Peroxidase (LiP, EC 1.11.1.14)
- Directly oxidizes non-phenolic aromatic compounds
- Particularly effective against benzo[a]pyrene and other carcinogenic PAHs
- Requires hydrogen peroxide as co-substrate

## Primary Fungal Species

### Pleurotus ostreatus (Oyster Mushroom)
The most widely studied species for petroleum remediation:
- Degrades 80-95% of aliphatic hydrocarbons within 8-12 weeks
- Effective against diesel, motor oil, and crude oil
- Edible fruiting bodies can be produced on contaminated substrate (DO NOT consume from contaminated sites)
- Robust growth on diverse substrates (straw, sawdust, wood chips)

### Trametes versicolor (Turkey Tail)
- Exceptional PAH degradation capabilities
- High laccase production under diverse conditions
- Used in both soil and water-based remediation
- Tolerates varying temperature and pH ranges

### Phanerochaete chrysosporium
- Model organism for [[lignin-degradation]] research
- Produces all three major [[singh-ligninolytic-enzymes-fungal-bioremediation]]
- Effective against both aliphatic and aromatic hydrocarbons
- Used extensively in laboratory studies; less common in field applications

### Other Effective Species
- **[[bjerkandera-adusta]]**: High [[manganese-peroxidase]] activity
- **[[ganoderma-lucidum]]** (reishi): Degrades PAHs; produces antimicrobial compounds that may suppress competing bacteria
- **Lentinula edodes** (shiitake): Effective against lighter petroleum fractions

## Field Applications

### Paul Stamets' Oil Spill Trials
Pioneering field demonstrations by mycologist Paul Stamets:
- 2007: [[oyster-mushroom]] mycelium placed on diesel-contaminated soil in Washington state
- Mycelial mats absorbed oil and produced fruiting bodies within weeks
- Bacterial communities shifted to hydrocarbon-degrading species in the mycosphere
- Insects attracted to fruiting bodies accelerated breakdown through soil aeration

### Bellingham Bay Pilot Study
- Oyster mushroom inoculated burlap sacks placed along contaminated shoreline
- Hydrocarbon levels reduced by over 50% in treated areas
- Native vegetation returned to previously barren zones

### Amazon Oil Contamination
- Trametes versicolor inoculum tested on petroleum-contaminated soils in Ecuadorian Amazon
- Combined fungal-bacterial consortia outperformed either alone
- Challenges with tropical climate adaptation noted

## Deployment Methods

### Mycelial Mat Placement
- Pre-grown mycelium on burlap, straw, or wood chip substrate
- Placed directly on contaminated soil or water surface
- Acts as both bioremediation agent and physical containment barrier

### Inoculated Substrate Amendments
- Mixing mushroom substrate (spent or fresh) into contaminated soil
- Provides both fungal inoculum and organic matter to support microbial activity
- Tilling improves contact between mycelium and contaminated soil

### Mycofiltration Barriers
- Mycelium-containing barriers installed down-gradient of contamination
- Filter petroleum-laden runoff before it reaches waterways
- See [[mycofiltration-of-water]] for details on constructed systems

### Enzyme Application
- Crude or purified fungal enzymes applied directly to contaminated surfaces
- Faster initial response but shorter activity period than living mycelium
- Useful for rapid response to fresh spills

## Limitations

### Environmental Factors
- Moisture must be maintained for fungal growth (60-80% field capacity)
- Temperature range: 15-30C for most species; tropical species for warmer climates
- UV exposure damages surface mycelium
- Nutrient supplementation (nitrogen, phosphorus) often needed for large spills

### Practical Constraints
- Remediation timelines: 2-6 months for significant hydrocarbon reduction
- Not suitable for all petroleum types (heavy crude more challenging than diesel)
- Heavy metal contamination co-occurring with oil complicates remediation
- Regulatory approval processes for fungal remediation are still evolving
