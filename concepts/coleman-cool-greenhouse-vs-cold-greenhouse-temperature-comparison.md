# Cool Greenhouse vs Cold Greenhouse — Temperature Management Comparison

## Overview

Eliot Coleman distinguishes between two types of protected [[mushroom-growing-environments]] for [[coleman-french-historical-winter-vegetable-production]]: the "cold greenhouse" (completely unheated) and the "cool greenhouse" (maintained at a minimal temperature just above freezing). Both systems have proven commercially viable at Four Season Farm in coastal Maine (USDA Zone 5), but they serve different purposes and involve different trade-offs in terms of cost, crop selection, and management intensity.

## Terminology

Coleman's definitions differ from British usage and should be understood clearly:

- **Cold greenhouse**: Completely unheated structure. May have a heater installed for occasional use (e.g., early-starting warm-season transplants), but no heat is applied during winter [[dry-gardening-unirrigated-vegetable-production-solomon]]. Also called a "cold house." This is the [[lichens-primary-production-soil-formation-dighton]] structure for the winter harvest system.

- **Cool greenhouse**: Maintained at a minimum temperature just above freezing, typically 32-34°F (0-1°C), using a supplemental heat source. Also called a "cool house." The thermostat is set low enough that heat runs only on the coldest nights.

- **Note**: Coleman uses "greenhouse," "high tunnel," "tunnel greenhouse," and "hoop house" interchangeably for pipe-frame, plastic-covered structures.

## The Cold Greenhouse System

### Temperature Profile

In a Zone 5 Maine winter, the cold greenhouse creates a single layer of [[coleman-double-layer-climate-modification-temperature-data]]. Typical temperature relationships:

- Outdoor temperature: -15°F (-26°C)
- Inside greenhouse: 10-15°F (-12 to -9°C) above outdoor temperature
- Under inner row cover: 15-18°F (-10 to -8°C) above outdoor temperature

The cold-hardy vegetables grown in this system tolerate these temperatures. Coleman has found that many cold-hardy crops easily survive temperatures down to 10°F (-12°C) or lower as long as they are protected from desiccating winter winds and direct outdoor exposure.

### Advantages

- **Zero heating costs**: No fuel consumption whatsoever for crop production
- **Simplicity**: No heating system to install, maintain, or manage
- **Sustainability**: Lowest possible carbon footprint for [[coleman-beet-greens-cold-house-winter-production]]
- **Reliability**: No risk of crop loss from heating system failure
- **Low capital cost**: Standard hoop [[low-cost-mushroom-growing-house-construction-developing-countries]] with no heating infrastructure

### Limitations

- **Slower growth**: Without heat, plant growth is entirely dependent on ambient conditions
- **Limited crop range**: Only cold-hardy species survive; no warm-season crops in winter
- **Fewer harvests per season**: Typically 3-4 crop cycles per winter vs 5-6 with minimal heat
- **Some crops excluded**: Baby turnips and crisp radishes are limited by freezing in unheated houses
- **Later spring harvest**: Spring carrots ready May 5 in cold houses vs April 1 in cool houses

### Crop Performance

Cold greenhouse crops from October through May in Zone 5 include:

| Crop | Harvest Period | Notes |
|------|---------------|-------|
| Spinach | Nov-Apr | Excellent performer; sweetens in cold |
| Claytonia | Nov-Apr | Prolific cut-and-come-again |
| Mache | Nov-Apr | Slowest grower; most cold-tolerant |
| Mizuna | Nov-Mar | Regrows well after cutting |
| Tatsoi | Nov-Mar | Hardy and reliable |
| Lettuce | Nov-Mar | Cold-tolerant varieties only |
| Scallions | Nov-Feb | Slow growth but reliable |
| Leeks | Dec-Mar | Can freeze solid; thaw and harvest |
| Carrots | Dec-Apr | Sweetest after frost exposure |

## The Cool Greenhouse System

### Temperature Management

Coleman's cool greenhouse at Four Season Farm uses a propane heater with the thermostat set just above 32°F (0°C). The heater runs only when the interior temperature threatens to drop below freezing. Key observations:

- The heater typically runs on the coldest nights (approximately 20-30 nights per winter in coastal Maine)
- Fuel consumption is minimal because the thermostat set point is so low
- The goal is frost prevention, not warmth creation
- Three-quarters of the house is used for growing; one-quarter serves as a wash/pack area

### Advantages Over Cold Houses

- **More crops per season**: 5-6 harvests per winter vs 3-4 in unheated houses
- **Broader crop range**: Baby turnips, radishes, and tender lettuces become viable
- **Earlier spring production**: Spring carrots available 6 weeks earlier (April 1 vs May 5)
- **Faster growth**: Even 2-3 degrees of [[cervantes-microclimate-frost-protection]] measurably accelerates growth
- **Insurance against extreme cold**: Protection during the most damaging cold events
