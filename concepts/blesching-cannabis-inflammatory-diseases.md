# Cannabis and Inflammatory Diseases

Inflammation is a fundamental [[medicinal-mushrooms-biological-response-modifiers]] with implications for
virtually every chronic disease. According to [[cannabis-health-endo-cannabinoid-system]] and Inflammation

The endocannabinoid system plays a central role in modulating inflammatory
responses throughout the body. CB2 receptors, concentrated in immune cells,
are particularly important for regulating inflammation. When activated, CB2
receptors can suppress excessive inflammatory responses while maintaining
necessary immune function.

The ECS initiates protective responses to injuries and inflammations
throughout life. It is involved in balancing and strengthening both the
nervous and immune systems, initiating pain control, and calming inflammation.

## Dietary Cannabinoid: (E)-Beta-Caryophyllene

Caryophyllene ((E)-BCP) is an FDA-approved dietary plant-cannabinoid that
activates CB2 receptor sites and initiates potent anti-inflammatory actions.
This food-based cannabinoid is found in numerous common spices and represents
a non-psychoactive way to harness the anti-inflammatory power of the
endocannabinoid system.

## Spices That May Aid Inflammatory Conditions

Blesching highlights several spices with anti-inflammatory properties that
may complement cannabinoid-based therapy:

### Cayenne

Cayenne stimulates peripheral circulation. In Cuba, topical tinctures and
creams are used to treat chronic aches and pains of lumbago, arthritis, and
rheumatism.

### Garlic

The garlic bulb is used in Cuba for the treatment of thrombophlebitis and
other inflammations, supporting cardiovascular health alongside
anti-inflammatory effects.

### Myrrh

A study from the University of Cincinnati confirmed myrrh's physiological
benefits for arthritis treatment, demonstrating that myrrh modulates
inflammatory responses.

### Nigella (Black Seed)

An experiment from Yuzuncu Yil University, Turkey, demonstrated that the
volatile oil of nigella can suppress artificially induced arthritis in rats.
Nigella has historically been used for arthritis and chronic inflammatory
conditions.
