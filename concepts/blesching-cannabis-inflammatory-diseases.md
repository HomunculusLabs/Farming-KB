# Cannabis and Inflammatory Diseases

Inflammation is a fundamental biological response with implications for
virtually every chronic disease. According to [[uwe-blesching]]'s Cannabis Health
Index, inflammatory diseases (in general) received a combined CHI value of 79
from 28 studies reviewed, making it one of the strongest evidence categories
for cannabinoid-based therapy.

## Understanding Inflammation

Inflamed tissue is a natural and necessary response of the healing process.
Without inflammation, injuries would not heal. Inflammation occurs in response
to the invasion of an organism, exposure to a toxin, or the presence of
impaired or injured cells. The inflammatory response involves:

- Swelling (accumulation of fluids)
- Heat (increased local temperature)
- Redness (increased micro-blood supply)
- Impairment of function at the affected site
- Pain, which serves as a constant reminder to guard the site until healing
  is complete

## Acute vs. Chronic Inflammation

Inflammation is classified as either acute or chronic:

### Acute Inflammation

A temporary reaction to an organism's injury that ends when the affected
tissue is healed. This is a normal, healthy response essential for recovery.

### Chronic Inflammation

A more persistent form that can result from several causes:
- The presence of a maintaining cause, such as a foreign object
- An invading organism or toxin that cannot be expelled or continuously
  reappears
- An injury that is not allowed to heal and is constantly agitated
- An over-reactive immune system that attacks itself, as in Crohn's Disease
  or rheumatoid arthritis

## The Endocannabinoid System and Inflammation

The endocannabinoid system plays a central role in modulating inflammatory
responses throughout the body. CB2 receptors, concentrated in immune cells,
are particularly important for regulating inflammation. When activated, CB2
receptors can suppress excessive inflammatory responses while maintaining
necessary immune function.

The ECS initiates protective responses to injuries and inflammations
throughout life. It is involved in balancing and strengthening both the
nervous and immune systems, initiating pain control, and calming inflammation.

## Dietary Cannabinoid: (E)-Beta-Caryophyllene

Caryophyllene ((E)-BCP) is an FDA-approved dietary plant-cannabinoid that
activates CB2 receptor sites and initiates potent anti-inflammatory actions.
This food-based cannabinoid is found in numerous common spices and represents
a non-psychoactive way to harness the anti-inflammatory power of the
endocannabinoid system.

## Spices That May Aid Inflammatory Conditions

Blesching highlights several spices with anti-inflammatory properties that
may complement cannabinoid-based therapy:

### Cayenne

Cayenne stimulates peripheral circulation. In Cuba, topical tinctures and
creams are used to treat chronic aches and pains of lumbago, arthritis, and
rheumatism.

### Garlic

The garlic bulb is used in Cuba for the treatment of thrombophlebitis and
other inflammations, supporting cardiovascular health alongside
anti-inflammatory effects.

### Myrrh

A study from the University of Cincinnati confirmed myrrh's physiological
benefits for arthritis treatment, demonstrating that myrrh modulates
inflammatory responses.

### Nigella (Black Seed)

An experiment from Yuzuncu Yil University, Turkey, demonstrated that the
volatile oil of nigella can suppress artificially induced arthritis in rats.
Nigella has historically been used for arthritis and chronic inflammatory
conditions.

### Turmeric

A meta-study found that turmeric's anti-inflammatory properties effectively
supported healing of wounds, arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease, ulcerative
colitis, atherosclerosis, pancreatitis, psoriasis, and chronic anterior
uveitis (inflammation of the middle eye layer).

## Evidence Across Specific Conditions

The inflammatory diseases category encompasses numerous specific conditions
with varying CHI values, including arthritis (CHI 6), rheumatoid arthritis
(CHI 10), inflammatory bowel disease (CHI 8), and many others. The combined
evidence suggests that cannabinoids work through multiple mechanisms to
modulate inflammatory responses across diverse tissue types.

## Key Takeaways

- Inflammatory diseases represent one of the strongest evidence categories
  for cannabis therapy, with a combined CHI of 79 from 28 studies.
- The ECS modulates inflammation through both CB1 and CB2 receptors, with
  CB2 playing a particularly important role in immune-mediated inflammation.
- (E)-Beta-caryophyllene, a dietary cannabinoid found in common spices,
  activates CB2 and provides non-psychoactive anti-inflammatory benefits.
- Complementary spices including turmeric, garlic, cayenne, myrrh, and
  nigella offer additional anti-inflammatory support.
- Chronic inflammation underlies many disease processes; modulating the ECS
  may address root causes rather than just symptoms.
- Both acute and chronic inflammation respond to cannabinoid-based
  interventions, though mechanisms and optimal approaches may differ.
