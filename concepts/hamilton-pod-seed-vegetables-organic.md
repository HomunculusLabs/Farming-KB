# Pod and Seed Vegetables - Organic Methods

Pod and seed vegetables are among the most productive and rewarding
crops in the organic garden. This group includes peas, broad beans,
runner beans, green beans, and sweet corn. Like sweet corn, peas taste
much better when eaten soon after picking, before the sugar changes
into starch. Legumes are especially valuable in organic systems because
they fix atmospheric nitrogen through bacteria in their root nodules,
enriching the soil for subsequent crops in the
[[hamilton-three-year-crop-rotation]].

## Peas

Two varieties are needed for a continuous supply: an early one and
a main crop type. Recommended early varieties are Douce Provence and
Meteor; Hurst Green Shaft and Onward are fine main crops. Snow peas,
also known as snap peas, have improved greatly and are worth trying.
Peas make their own nitrogen and require little extra feeding; too
rich a soil leads to abundant foliage but a small crop. They prefer
a soil pH of 6.5.

## Sowing Peas

The earliest peas can be sown in autumn and overwintered, though
this is risky in hard winters. A useful alternative is sowing in
plastic guttering in a greenhouse, then sliding the contents into
prepared trenches outdoors. Plant in open ground in early spring
under [[hamilton-cloches-and-frost-protection]]. Make a wide trench
about 2in (5cm) deep, scatter seeds roughly 2in apart, cover, and
firm down. Sow at two-week intervals until early summer for
successional harvesting.

## Broad Beans

A very rewarding vegetable, broad beans are high in protein and
serve as a good source of green manure. They should be picked young
and can be dried and stored for winter use. Sow in early spring or
autumn for overwintering in mild areas. Plant in double rows about
9in (23cm) apart with 8in (20cm) between plants. Pinch out the tops
when the first pods set to deter blackfly. See
[[hamilton-green-manure-crops-and-cover-cropping]] for their role
as a soil-improving crop.

## Runner Beans

Runner beans are prolific summer vegetables with attractive flowers
suitable for the ornamental border or training up wigwams of poles.
They need careful soil preparation and a water-retentive soil. Dig a
trench in autumn and fill with compost or manure. Sow seeds indoors
in mid-spring and harden off before planting out after frost danger
passes. Space plants 9in (23cm) apart along the base of supports.
Keep well watered during flowering and pod-set for maximum yields.

## Green Beans

Green beans, both bush and climbing varieties, are suitable for the
greenhouse and for outdoor growing in warm conditions. They require
warm soil and should not be planted until all danger of frost has
passed. Sow in mid-spring in 3in pots and transplant, or sow directly
outside in early summer. Space bush varieties 12in (30cm) apart and
climbing varieties 6in (15cm) apart at the base of supports. Pick
regularly to encourage continued production throughout the season.

## Sweet Corn

Homegrown sweet corn has an especially sweet flavor as it can be
eaten as soon as it is picked, before sugar turns to starch. Modern
fast-maturing varieties like Earlybird are easy to grow, but must
be grown away from other varieties since cross-pollination reduces
sugar content. Sweet corn likes sun and pH of about 6.5. Sow in
mid-spring in 3in pots at 65 degrees F (18 degrees C), harden off,
and plant outside in early summer in blocks with 2ft (60cm) between
plants.

## Sweet Corn Pollination

Sweet corn is wind-pollinated, so grow it in blocks rather than
rows to aid pollination. Plant seedlings in a series of short,
staggered rows to give the plants the best chance of wind
pollination. When plants flower, extra watering is beneficial.
Harvest when the tassels at the top of the cobs turn brown and
then black. Break off ripe cobs by pulling downward with one hand
while bracing the rest of the plant with the other.

## Pests and Diseases

Pod and seed vegetables are generally trouble free but can be
affected by aphids, pea moth, and bean-related fungal diseases.
Broad beans are susceptible to blackfly; pinching out growing tips
helps deter them. Mice may eat sown seeds, so protect with netting
or traps. See [[hamilton-organic-pest-control]] for management
strategies. Crop rotation within the
[[hamilton-crop-rotation-and-vegetable-planning]] system helps
prevent soil-borne diseases from building up.

## Summary

Pod and seed vegetables offer exceptional value in the organic
garden. Legumes enrich the soil through nitrogen fixation, while
sweet corn provides a uniquely sweet harvest eaten fresh. Success
with peas depends on successional sowing and support; beans need
rich, moisture-retentive soil and regular picking; sweet corn
requires block planting for pollination. All benefit from the
organic approach, which maximizes soil fertility and minimizes
pest problems through good cultural practices and rotation.

Provide sturdy supports for climbing beans at least 1.8 metres tall to
allow full development of the crop canopy during the growing season.
