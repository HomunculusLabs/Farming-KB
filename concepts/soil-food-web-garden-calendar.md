## Spring

Spring is the season of activation. [[the-soil-food-web]] awakens, and your job
is to feed and support the [[cannabis-rhizosphere-microbial-communities]] that will sustain plant
growth through the year.

### Composting

- **Check soils** for moisture, structure, and biological activity.
- **Crank up the compost pile** — turn last fall's pile to incorporate
  oxygen and restart decomposition.
- **Start a new [[fungal-compost]] pile** using winter debris and fall leaves.
  Fungal-dominated compost is ideal for perennials, trees, and shrubs.
- Collect **first grass clippings** for [[bacterial-compost]]. Grass clippings
  are high in nitrogen and favor bacterial growth — perfect for annual
  vegetables and lawns.

### Mulch Management

- **Pull back mulches** to allow the soil to warm in early spring, then
  **replace and supplement** once soil temperatures rise.
- **Tidy up brown mulch** under trees, shrubs, and perennials. Refresh
  with new fungal mulch as needed.

### Soil Microbiology Testing

- **Three weeks before leaves appear**: test soils and compost tea for
  microbiology. Use **Berlese funnel soil traps** for self-testing — a
  simple, low-cost method to assess microarthropod populations.
- Keep records of all test results year over year to track progress.

### Lawn Care

- **Two weeks before leaf-out**: aerate lawns. This is critical in the
  first year; thereafter, aerate only as needed.
- **After aeration**: apply organic microbe food — **soybean meal** is a
  solid choice (feeds bacteria and fungi).
- If there were **too many mushrooms** the previous year, apply
  **alfalfa meal** instead, which favors bacteria and helps rebalance
  the fungal-to-bacterial ratio.
- **Spray lawns** with slightly bacterial or balanced compost tea at
  a rate of **5 gallons per acre**.
- **Winter traffic paths** (compacted areas from foot traffic): cordoned
  off, sprayed with **fungal tea**, and amended with leftover compost.

### Trees, Shrubs & Perennials

- Apply **fungal foods**: humic/fulvic acids, cold-water kelp extracts,
  and phosphate rock dusts.
- Apply a **fungal compost tea soil drench** around the root zones of
  trees, perennials, and shrubs.
- After leaves appear, **spray fungal tea** on perennials as a foliar
  application.

### Vegetable Garden

- **Do not till.** Tilling destroys fungal networks and soil structure.
- Apply **soybean meal** at a rate of **4 pounds per 100 square feet**.
- **Spray with bacterial compost tea** across the bed.
- **Drill holes for seeds** or disturb only the planting row — minimize
  soil disruption.
- Once the soil warms, use **green mulch** (e.g., grass clippings,
  clover) to feed [[cannabis-endorhiza-bacterial-communities]] around annual vegetables.

### Seed & Transplant Inoculation

- **Inoculate all seeds** with appropriate **mycorrhizal fungi** before
  planting.
- **Soak transplants** in [[actively-aerated-compost-tea-introduction]] before placing in the ground.
- This ensures that young plants establish symbiotic fungal partnerships
  from day one.

## Summer

Summer is a season of **maintenance and monitoring**. The [[soil-food-web]] is
now in full operation, but it may need supplemental support, especially in
the first transition year.

### Ongoing Spray & Drench Program

- **Continue the compost tea spray and soil drench program** throughout
  summer. This is especially critical in the first year when microbial
  populations are still establishing.
- Apply compost tea as both a **foliar spray** (leaf surfaces) and a
  **soil drench** (root zone).

### Lawn Maintenance

- If grass clippings are accumulating or the lawn is not greening as
  expected, apply **protozoa soup** — a liquid extract rich in protozoa
  that helps cycle nutrients and stimulate bacterial activity.
- Continue monitoring lawn health; adjust bacterial vs. fungal tea ratios
  based on observations.

### Vegetable Garden

- Use **bacterial compost and green mulch** to suppress weeds and
  feed the bacterial communities that annual vegetables depend on.
- Apply **microbe food** (e.g., soybean meal, fish hydrolysate) every
  **two weeks** if plants show signs of [[cannabis-grow-nutrient-deficiency-problem-solving]] or slow
  growth.

### Trees, Shrubs & Perennials

- Maintain **fungal compost and mulch** around the base of trees, shrubs,
  and perennials. Replenish as mulch breaks down.
- Avoid disturbing root zones — fungal networks are active and fragile.

### Disease & Stress Response

- Any **diseased or stressed plants** should be treated **immediately**:
  - Spray with **compost tea** (foliar application).
  - Apply a **soil drench** of compost tea to the root zone.
  - The beneficial microbes in compost tea compete with pathogens and
    can suppress disease through competitive exclusion and induced
    [[endophytic-mycorrhizal-induced-systemic-resistance]].

### Monitoring & Record-Keeping

- Conduct **additional Berlese funnel tests** during summer to monitor
  soil food web health.
- **Keep detailed records** of all applications, test results, and plant
  observations. This data is invaluable for refining your approach in
  subsequent seasons.
