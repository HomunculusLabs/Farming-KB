# Herb Spiral Design

**Updated:** 2026-04-18

The herb spiral is a classic permaculture design element that combines a raised bed, a vertical garden, and a microclimate creation tool into a beautiful, space-efficient, and productive garden feature. It is often one of the first projects for new permaculture gardeners.

## What Is an Herb Spiral?

An herb spiral is a three-dimensional spiral-shaped raised bed, typically 5-6 feet in diameter at the base and rising 3-4 feet at the center. The spiral creates multiple microclimates in a small footprint:
- The top is dry, sunny, and well-drained
- The middle is moderately moist and sunny
- The bottom is moist, shady, and cool
- The north side (in northern hemisphere) is shadier and cooler
- The south side is sunnier and warmer

This range of microclimates allows herbs with different growing preferences to thrive in close proximity.

## Design and Construction

### Materials
- **Rocks, bricks, or blocks:** For the retaining wall (stacked in a spiral pattern)
- **Cardboard:** For weed suppression at the base
- **Soil/compost mix:** Fill material (good quality garden soil mixed with compost)
- **Optional:** Gravel or sand at the base center for drainage

### Dimensions
- **Diameter:** 5-6 feet (manageable; all plants within arm's reach)
- **Height:** 3-4 feet at center (creates significant microclimate variation)
- **Width of planting area:** 18-24 inches (enough for herbs to grow)

### Construction Steps
1. Mark a circle 5-6 feet in diameter on the ground
2. Lay cardboard over the circle to suppress weeds
3. Place rocks/bricks in a spiral pattern, starting from the outside and working inward and upward
4. Fill with soil/compost as you build the wall, creating a gradual slope
5. Top with finished compost for planting
6. Water thoroughly and allow to settle before planting

### The Spiral Pattern
- Begin at the outside edge and spiral inward (clockwise or counterclockwise)
- Each turn of the spiral rises approximately 6-8 inches
- The path between wall turns is 12-18 inches wide
- The top of the spiral is a small flat platform (2-3 feet diameter)

## Planting Guide

### Top (Dry, Sunny, Well-Drained)
Herbs that prefer dry conditions and good drainage:
- Rosemary
- Thyme
- Lavender
- Oregano
- Santolina

### Middle (Moderate Sun and Moisture)
Herbs that prefer average conditions:
- Sage
- Savory
- Tarragon
- Marjoram
- Chives

### Bottom (Moist, Shady, Cool)
Herbs that prefer moist, partly shaded conditions:
- Mint (plant here to contain its spread)
- Parsley
- Cilantro/coriander
- Lemon balm
- Watercress (in very wet areas)

### North Side (Cooler, Shadier)
- Mint
- Chervil
- Parsley
- Sweet woodruff

### South Side (Warmer, Sunnier)
- Basil (summer)
- Rosemary
- Thyme
- Oregano

## Functions

### Multiple Microclimates
The primary function: one structure creates the growing conditions of several different garden environments. Mediterranean herbs that need dry feet sit next to moisture-loving herbs that would rot in those conditions.

### Space Efficiency
A 5-foot diameter spiral provides 20-30 linear feet of growing edge in a compact footprint. Every plant is within arm's reach from the path.

### Water Harvesting
Water poured at the top of the spiral percolates down through the entire structure. The spiral naturally distributes moisture from wet (bottom) to dry (top).

### Aesthetic Beauty
Herb spirals are beautiful garden features that serve as focal points and conversation starters.

### Educational Value
The herb spiral demonstrates microclimate principles, water flow, and plant preference in a tangible, hands-on way.

### Edge Effect
The spiral shape maximizes edge between different microclimates, creating the most productive and diverse zone.

## Maintenance

- **Watering:** Water at the top during dry spells; water will distribute downward. Bottom plants may need less additional water
- **Pruning:** Regular harvesting serves as pruning; keeps plants compact and productive
- **Replanting:** Replace annual herbs (basil, cilantro, dill) each season
- **Weeding:** Minimal weeding needed due to dense planting and mulch
- **Winter protection:** In cold climates, the stone wall provides thermal mass that moderates temperature; mulch tender herbs

## Common Mistakes

1. **Building too large:** Keep it under 6 feet diameter or you can't reach the center
2. **Poor drainage at center:** Add gravel or broken pots at the bottom center
3. **No weed barrier:** Cardboard under the spiral prevents weeds from growing up through
4. **Inconsistent soil:** Use the same soil mix throughout; let the microclimate do the work
5. **Planting invasives outside:** Mint, lemon balm, and other runners should be at the bottom where moisture and containment keep them in check
