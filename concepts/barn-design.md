# Barn Design

A well-designed barn protects animals, stores feed and equipment, and serves as the central hub of a homestead livestock operation. This guide covers design principles, layout, flooring, and construction considerations for small to medium homesteads. Drawn from [[nicole-faires]]' comprehensive manual.

## Design Principles

### Site Selection

- Well-drained, elevated ground (never in a low spot or floodplain)
- South-facing exposure for maximum sunlight and warmth
- Protected from prevailing winds (natural windbreak or building placement)
- Close to the house for daily chores (but downwind of living spaces)
- Accessible by vehicle for feed delivery and animal transport
- Proximity to pasture and water sources
- Room for expansion

### Orientation

- Long axis running east-west (south-facing wall captures maximum sun)
- Open front of shelter facing south or southeast
- Doors positioned to avoid prevailing wind entering
- Manure storage area downwind and downhill from animal areas

### Sizing

| Animal | Indoor Space | Outdoor/Run Space | Notes |
|---------|-------------|-------------------|-------|
| Goat (per animal) | 15-20 sq ft | 30-50 sq ft | 8 ft ceiling |
| Sheep (per animal) | 10-15 sq ft | 25-50 sq ft | 8 ft ceiling |
| Cow (per animal) | 80-120 sq ft | 200+ sq ft (or pasture) | 10 ft ceiling |
| Horse (per animal) | 120-150 sq ft | Pasture | 10-12 ft ceiling |
| Pig (per animal) | 40-60 sq ft | 80-100 sq ft | 8 ft ceiling |
| Poultry (per bird) | 2-4 sq ft | 8-10 sq ft | Roosts + nest boxes |
| Rabbit (per animal) | 4-6 sq ft | N/A | Wire or solid floor |

## Layout

### Multi-Purpose Barn Floor Plan

A practical small homestead barn (24x36 feet) might include:

```
+------------------------------------------+
|  FEED STORAGE  |  TOOL  | MILK/PROCESS  |
|  (hay + grain) |  ROOM  |    ROOM       |
|                |        |               |
+----------------+--------+-------+-------+
|                        |       |       |
|    ANIMAL STALLS       | LAMB/ | FOAL/ |
|    (3-4 stalls)        |KID PEN|CALF   |
|                        |       | PEN   |
|                        |       |       |
+------------------------------------------+
|     MANURE STORAGE / COMPOST AREA        |
+------------------------------------------+
```

### Key Zones

1. **Feed storage**: largest single area; needs to be dry and rodent-proof
2. **Animal housing**: stalls, pens, bedding areas
3. **Milk/processing room**: clean area separate from animals (for dairy operations)
4. **Tool and equipment storage**: for fencing tools, medical supplies, halters
5. **Manure management**: covered area for composting or storage before spreading
6. **Handling/work area**: for vaccinations, hoof trimming, loading

### Stalls

- Minimum size for a cow/horse stall: 12x12 feet
- Goat/sheep stall: 6x6 feet (per 3-4 animals)
- Pig pen: 8x8 feet (per 2-3 pigs)
- Stall dividers: solid walls to 4 feet, then open bars or mesh above
- Stall doors: 4 feet wide (minimum), sliding or swinging
- Latches: secure but easy to open with one hand

### Feed Storage

- Hay: stack on pallets (never directly on concrete or earth)
- Store hay loose or in bales; 800-1000 lb round bales need strong floor support
- Grain: metal bins with tight lids (rodent-proof)
- Mineral feeders: covered, accessible to animals
- A 24x24 hay storage area holds approximately 6-8 tons of small square bales
- Calculate: one cow eats 30-40 lbs hay/day x 180 days = 5,400-7,200 lbs per cow per winter

## Flooring

### Options

| Type | Cost | Pros | Cons | Best For |
|------|------|------|------|----------|
| Dirt/Packed earth | Very low | Natural, warm, easy on hooves | Mud in wet weather, harder to clean | Goats, sheep |
| Concrete | High | Easy to clean, durable, rodent-proof | Cold, hard on joints, slippery when wet | Dairy cows, milk room |
| Gravel/Crushed rock | Low | Good drainage | Hard on hooves, difficult to clean | Outdoor runs, loafing areas |
| Wood (planks) | Medium | Warm, natural, easier on joints | Rot, slippery when wet, chewable | Horse stalls, goat pens |
| Rubber mats over concrete | Medium | Warm, cushioned, easy to clean | Expensive, can shift | Dairy stalls, horse stalls |
| Sand | Low | Good drainage, comfortable | Holds moisture, difficult to muck | Horse stalls, pig pens |

### Recommended Flooring by Area

- **Stalls**: Dirt or wood (with deep bedding) for ruminants; rubber mats over concrete for dairy cows and horses
- **Feed storage**: Concrete (rodent-proof, moisture barrier)
- **Milk room**: Concrete (easy to sanitize)
- **Manure area**: Concrete with slight slope toward drainage
- **Aisles**: Concrete or packed gravel

### Bedding

| Material | Absorbency | Cost | Notes |
|----------|-----------|------|-------|
| Straw (wheat/oat) | Good | Low | Most common, good for nesting |
| Hay (waste) | Moderate | Varies | Use older or moldy hay |
| Wood shavings | Excellent | Low-Medium | Very absorbent, avoid black walnut |
| Sawdust | Excellent | Low | Can cause respiratory issues (dust) |
| Pine needles | Good | Free (in some areas) | Natural pest repellent |
| Shredded paper | Good | Free | Avoid glossy/colored paper |
| Sand | Poor | Low | Use with rubber mats, not alone |

- Depth: 4-6 inches minimum for stalls, deeper in cold weather
- Remove wet spots daily, add fresh bedding weekly
- Full cleanout and replacement: every 4-8 weeks
- Compost soiled bedding (see [[composting-systems]])
