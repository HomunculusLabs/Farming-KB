# JADAM Universal Pesticide Approach

## Overview

JADAM's pesticide philosophy centers on a radical simplification: instead of developing separate pesticides for each of the 1,000+ pest species, JADAM uses a single formula that works universally by targeting the "size" of the organism rather than its species.

## The Key Insight: Size, Not Species

The difference between pathogens (bacteria, viruses, fungi) and pests (insects, aphids, mites) is simply size. This means:
- If you make germicide for germs, increase the dose, and it becomes a pesticide for pests
- One pesticide acts as germicide, fungicide, bactericide, insecticide, and pesticide simultaneously
- No need to develop separate pesticides for different organisms

## The Touchstone: Powdery Mildew and Aphids

Rather than studying all 1,000+ pest and pathogen species, JADAM identifies the two "nastiest" representatives:
1. Powdery mildew -- the hardest pathogen to control
2. Aphids -- the most persistent pest

If you can control powdery mildew, you can control most pathogens. If you can [[query-how-do-i-identify-and-control-aphids-on-cannabis]], you can control most pests. The pesticide that controls powdery mildew, when made stronger, controls aphids. This methodology solves approximately 90% of all pest and pathogen problems.

## The Cosmetics Analogy

JADAM compares pesticide product proliferation to the cosmetics industry:
- Initially one product, then diversified by season, gender, body part, function
- Now thousands of different products -- not from objective science, but from marketing pressure
- The same has happened in pesticide: 400+ products in Korea alone, each specified for a single pest

JADAM's inspiration: one type of anthelmintic (dewormer) kills roundworms, whipworms, threadworms, and hookworms. If one medicine works for many parasites, one pesticide can work for many pests.

## "I Am the Pesticide Expert"

JADAM's fundamental stance: all farmers can and should become pesticide experts. The knowledge should not belong exclusively to experts and corporations. Making pesticide is "as easy as cooking rice." Kitchen tools are sufficient for experiments.

If a couple farms together, JADAM suggests the wife should become the pesticide expert -- this is the path to breaking free from commercial pesticide dependency and achieving ultra-low-cost farming.

## The SESE Principle

JADAM's methods are SESE:
- Simple
- Easy
- Scientific
- Effective

The closer you get to the truth, the simpler it becomes. JADAM's vision: bring science down to earth, explain it in the language of common sense, let it be something everyone can enjoy.

## DIY Research: The Cup Method

JADAM developed a simple, affordable research method for farmers:

### Germicide Testing
1. Collect [[comparison-leaf-mold-composting-vs-hot-composting-method]] (holds millions of microorganisms)
2. Mix leaf mold in water
3. Pour equal amounts into many cups
4. Add same amount of molasses to feed microbes
5. Add germicide at increasing doses in each cup
6. Observe: less foam = inhibited microbial growth
7. Compare different germicide ingredients and dilution ratios
8. Results transfer accurately to field conditions

### Pest Testing
1. Use petri dishes or small containers
2. Drop in tens of target pests (aphids, etc.)
3. Spray with natural insecticide at different doses
4. Close lid, observe reactions (some drop heads, some overturn completely)
5. For larger pests (cabbage white butterfly larvae, tobacco moths, beet armyworm), use larger dishes

The simple lab costs approximately $50 to set up. Results are remarkably consistent between lab and field.
