## Peaceful Visions of the Second Bardo

The Second Bardo of the Tibetan Book of the Dead, as adapted by Leary, Metzner, and Alpert in *The Psychedelic Experience* (1964), presents a series of **Peaceful Visions** that arise after the ego-loss of the First Bardo. These visions represent progressively more structured encounters with the fundamental nature of reality. Each vision corresponds to a Dhyani Buddha, an element, and a specific mode of perception — and each carries both a **positive** (liberating) and **negative** (deluding) interpretation depending on the voyager"s preparation and mental set.

The central teaching is that these visions are not hallucinations to be grasped or fled from, but **radiant displays of consciousness itself**. The prepared voyager recognizes them as such and is liberated; the unprepared person freezes them into conceptual traps. The manual functions as a reality-map, orienting the voyager through terrains that the untrained mind cannot navigate.

## Vision 1: The Source — White Light, God the Creator

The first peaceful vision is the encounter with **brilliant white light**, described variously as God the Creator, the Central Sun, the Divine Mother, and the Radiant Father-Mother. This is the most fundamental and overwhelming of all bardo visions — the raw, undifferentiated ground of being from which all phenomena arise. The light is not metaphorical; it is experienced as a tangible, blinding radiance that fills all space and seems to emanate from the core of existence itself.

In the Tibetan system, this vision corresponds to **Bhagavan [[vairochana]]**, the Dhyani Buddha of the center, whose wisdom is the **Wisdom of the Dharmadhatu** — the recognition that all things are empty of inherent existence yet luminously manifest. Vairochana is depicted as white, radiating light in all directions, seated at the heart of the cosmic mandala. His element is ether — the space in which all other elements arise and dissolve.

### The Western Difficulty

Leary notes that this vision poses particular difficulty for those from
**Judaeo-Christian backgrounds**. The Western religious tradition has
conditioned people to experience God as an external authority figure — a
patriarchal judge — rather than as an immanent radiance identical with one's
own consciousness. When the white light appears, Western voyagers may feel
unworthy, terrified, or rebellious.

The problem is rooted in what Leary calls the "cosmological subject-object
distinction" — the deeply ingrained belief that divinity exists "up there"
while the self remains "down here," separated by an unbridgeable gulf.
Christian mystical claims of unity with divine radiance have always posed
problems for theologians committed to maintaining this separation.

Because of what Leary terms "bad karma" — usually religious beliefs of a
monotheistic or punitive nature — the glorious light of the seed wisdom can
produce awe and terror rather than bliss. The person may wish to flee from
the light and develop a fondness for a dull, lesser illumination symbolizing
stupidity or ignorance. The instruction is to **surrender to the light**,
recognizing it as the essence of one"s own awareness. Resistance produces
suffering; recognition produces liberation.

## Vision 2: Internal Flow of Archetypal Processes

The second vision shifts from the undifferentiated white light to a rich display of **colored forms** — microbiological shapes, cellular acrobatics, streaming patterns of energy that suggest the internal processes of creation itself. The voyager may perceive what appear to be biological forms: mitotic divisions, flagellar movements, viral geometries, and embryonic unfolding. These forms are archetypal in the Jungian sense — primordial images expressing fundamental patterns of the psyche and of nature.

### Internal Sounds

Alongside the visual display, the voyager may hear **internal sounds**: clicking, thudding, roaring, humming, and bell-like tones. These auditory phenomena are directly connected to the Tibetan tradition of **sacred music** — the instruments and chants designed to evoke specific visionary states. The texts suggest these sounds are intrinsic to consciousness itself, manifestations of the vibratory nature of reality.

### Cosmological Revelations

This vision often produces profound cosmological insights — the voyager may perceive the formation of galaxies, the structure of atoms, or the evolutionary unfolding of life as a single, unified process. The boundary between inner and outer dissolves as microcosm and macrocosm mirror one another.

### The Traps

The negative interpretations of this vision are numerous and seductive:

- **Labeling trap**: The intellectual voyager attempts to name and classify every form, reducing the living flow to a taxonomy and missing the direct experience.
- **Sexual interpretation**: Forms are interpreted through a sexual lens, reducing archetypal processes to erotic symbolism.
- **Physical symptom game**: The voyager becomes convinced that the visions are symptoms of bodily distress and falls into panic.

This vision corresponds to **Amoghasiddhi**, the Dhyani Buddha of the north, associated with the **element of air** and the **Wisdom of All-Accomplishing Action**.

## Vision 3: Fire-Flow of Internal Unity

The third vision is characterized by **intense feelings of unity and love** — an ecstatic merging with all life. The voyager may experience visions of the **circulatory system**, perceiving the flow of blood as the flow of life-force connecting all beings. The boundaries between self and other dissolve completely.

### The Ecstatic Merge

This is among the most blissful of all psychedelic experiences. The voyager
feels a warm, fiery embrace that encompasses all existence. Love is no longer
an emotion directed at objects but the **fundamental fabric of reality
itself**. Every particle vibrates with recognition and affection — a state
comparable to *agape* and *maitri* (loving-kindness) extended to all sentient
beings without exception.

Visions related to the **circulatory system** are common during this phase.
The subject tumbles down through their own arterial network. The motor of
the heart reverberates as one with the pulsing of all life. The heart then
breaks, and red fire bleeds out to merge with all living beings. One becomes
joyfully aware of what Leary calls the "two-billion-year-old electric sexual
dance," divested of robotic clothes and limbs, undulating in the endless chain
of living forms.

The memory of former delusions of self-hood and differentiation invokes
exultant laughter. All the harsh, dry, brittle angularity of game life is
melted. The voyager drifts off — soft, rounded, moist, warm — merged with
all life. Every cell in the body sings its song of freedom as the entire
biological universe achieves harmony, liberated from the censorship and
control of restricted ambitions.

### The Terror of Ego Dissolution

But this vision also carries the potential for acute terror. As the ego dissolves into the fire-flow, the voyager may experience a moment of absolute panic — the **"STOP" moment** — where consciousness confronts its own annihilation. This is the crucial juncture: surrender leads to liberation, resistance leads to the Wrathful Visions of the later Second Bardo.

### Methods to Return to Biological Flow

When the voyager becomes overwhelmed, the Tibetan manual offers specific methods to **return to biological flow**: shifting attention to the breath, opening the eyes, moving the body, or focusing on a simple physical sensation. These techniques are not escapes but ways to re-establish the bodily anchor that allows the experience to continue without panic.

This vision corresponds to **Amitabha**, the Dhyani Buddha of the west, associated with the **element of fire** and the **Wisdom of Discriminating Awareness**.

## Vision 4: Wave-Vibration Structure of External Forms

The fourth vision reveals the **wave-nature of all structure**. Rather than seeing solid objects, the voyager perceives patterns of light waves, interference patterns, and vibratory fields. This is the **Maya revelation**: the direct perception that the apparently solid world of objects is a constructed appearance overlaying a shimmering field of energy.

### Synesthesia and Auditory Phenomena

This vision frequently produces **synesthesia** — the blending of senses. Colors are heard, sounds are seen, and music is experienced not as organized sound but as **naked energy**, raw vibration without the filter of aesthetic interpretation. Musical structures may appear as visible geometries, and architectural forms may produce auditory harmonics. The senses, normally compartmentalized, reveal themselves as aspects of a single perceptual capacity.

### The Plastic Doll Trap

The negative interpretation of this vision is the experience of the world as artificial, hollow, or meaningless — the **"plastic doll"** perception. Objects appear as empty shells, people as mannequins, the entire world as a stage set devoid of authentic being. This nihilistic interpretation is a failure of recognition: the voyager sees the constructed nature of reality but misses the luminous awareness that constructs it.

This vision corresponds to **Akshobhya**, the Dhyani Buddha of the east, associated with **Mirror-like Wisdom** — the recognition that all phenomena are reflections of mind, appearing in consciousness like images in a mirror, without independent existence yet vividly real.

## General Themes

### Positive and Negative Interpretations

Every peaceful vision has two faces. The **positive interpretation** is one of liberation: the voyager recognizes the vision as a display of their own awareness and is freed from dualistic grasping. The **negative interpretation** is one of delusion: the voyager either flees from the vision in terror or freezes it into a fixed conceptual form, creating a karmic pattern that perpetuates suffering.

### The Role of Preparation and Guidance

Leary emphasizes that the difference between liberation and delusion depends largely on **preparation and guidance**. The voyager who has studied the manual, meditated on the nature of mind, and arranged for a knowledgeable guide is far more likely to recognize the visions for what they are. The unprepared voyager is far more likely to fall into the traps. The guide serves not as a director but as a steadying presence who can remind the voyager of the instructions at critical moments.

### The Dangers of Conceptual Freezing

The most pervasive danger throughout all four visions is **conceptual freezing**: the tendency of the mind to solidify fluid, radiant experience into fixed ideas, labels, and interpretations. The moment the voyager thinks "this is God" or "this is a hallucination" or "this is dangerous," the living vision is killed and replaced by a concept. The instruction throughout the Tibetan Book of the Dead is to remain in the state of **bare recognition**, seeing without naming, experiencing without grasping — not pushing away and not holding on, but resting in the luminous emptiness that underlies all appearances.
