# Anaerobic Sewage Digestion and Biogas Production

## Overview

Anaerobic digestion is the first and most critical stage in [[biological-sewage-treatment-plants]] treatment. Raw sewage — a mixture of nutrients, elements, heavy metals, and carbon compounds with dangerous levels of bacteria, viruses, and intestinal worm eggs — is processed in sealed, oxygen-free ponds (digesters) where it is converted into biogas (methane), a valuable fuel resource. This process simultaneously destroys pathogens and precipitates heavy metals from solution.

## Sewage Composition

A typical analysis of raw sewage (30% industrial, 60% domestic waste, from Werribee, Victoria, Australia) includes:

Of the total sewage input, 45–60% of the volume builds up as sludge in settling ponds.

## Scaling: Pond Area to Population

At Werribee (population 2,700,000), [[werribee-sewage-lagoons-mollison]] cover 1,500 hectares (3,700 acres), yielding approximately 1 hectare of pond per 1,800 inhabitants. Scaled down:

- **Total pond system**: ~1 hectare per 1,800 people
- **Primary settling ponds only**: ~1 hectare per 3,800 people
- Individual ponds rarely exceed 7 hectares

For 1,000 townspeople with associated industry, as little as 270 m² of settling pond at 1 metre depth is required. This can be configured as:
- A long pond 3 m wide × 90 m long
- Three side-by-side ponds 30 m × 3 m each
- A compact 3 m deep × 3 m wide × 30 m long digester pond

## The Three-Stage Pond System

### Stage 1: Anaerobic (Methane-Producing) Ponds

The first series of ponds is totally anaerobic. Sludge creates oxygen-free conditions, and biogas (a mixture of methane CH₄, [[carbon-dioxide-retention-jar-lid-mushroom-cultivation]] CO₂, and ammonia gas NH₃, with traces of nitrous sulphide) is produced. Biogas serves as useful fuel for engines or cooking, and should be captured rather than released to the atmosphere where it contributes to the greenhouse effect.

#### Design for Compact Digesters

Long, narrow ponds are easily made fully anaerobic by fitting water seals and a weighted cover (plastic, metal, butyl rubber, or fibreglass). For deeper digester ponds (2–3 m), artificial agitation is necessary using pumped biogas to stir the sludge — otherwise sludge settles and becomes inactive.

**Critical benefits of sealed and agitated digesters:**
- No scum forms on the surface (scum slows breakdown and causes acid conditions)
- Sludge remains "active" only in contact with semi-liquid inputs — stirring maximises breakdown

#### Retention Time and Temperature

- Retention: ~20 days
- Optimal temperature: 25–30°C (77–86°F)
- A high percentage of dissolved solids is transferred into methane
- Each cubic metre of methane generated removes approximately 2.89 kg of solids and reduces BOD proportionally

#### Pathogen Destruction

In the digester, 90–94% of worm eggs are destroyed, along with many harmful bacteria. Useful energy is generated and can be used locally to run motors for electricity or compress gas for cooking and machinery.

## Heavy Metal Removal

### Mechanism: Sulphide Precipitation

In the digester, marsh gas (hydrogen sulphide, H₂S) combines with soluble heavy metals to produce insoluble sulphides above pH 7. A small amount of lime can assist this result.

### Removal Efficiency (Hussainy Data)

| Metal | Total Removed | Anaerobic Portion |
|-------|--------------|-------------------|
| Copper | 97% | 78% |
| Cadmium | 70% | 100% |
| Zinc | 97% | 83% |
| Nickel | 65% | 18% (47% aerobic) |
| Lead | 95% | 90% |
| Chromium | 87% | 47% |
| Iron | 85% | 47% |

The results demonstrate that solids, metals, and disease organisms are very greatly reduced by the first (anaerobic) treatment alone.

## Sulphur-Loving Bacteria

In open anaerobic ponds, specialised sulphur bacteria of the genera *Thiosporallum*, *Chromatium*, and *Rhodopseudomonas* appear and may give ponds a pink colouration. These bacteria:
- Use hydrogen sulphide as a hydrogen source for carbon assimilation
- Produce elemental sulphur (S) as a by-product
- Sulphur binds to metals present — approximately 1.8–2.0 mg/L of heavy metals are precipitated as sulphides at 1.0 mg/L of elemental sulphur

## Self-Regulating Equilibrium

A key [[decomposition]] slows and sludge builds up; BOD processing drops to ~495 kg/ha/day
- **Summer** (18–22°C): Residues oxidised to nitrates; oxygen provided more by algae than wind; BOD processing rises to ~1,034 kg/ha/day; almost twice as much methane produced
- **Winter heating**: Cooling water from methane-powered engines can supply essential heat to digesters via closed-loop pipes

## Transition to Facultative Ponds

After the anaerobic stage, effluent passes to facultative ponds where conditions shift. Some sludge remains anaerobic at the pond base while surface water (driven by wind and algae) becomes aerobic. The final series of ponds is totally aerobic, completing the treatment progression.
