## Why Pressure Cookers Were Previously Required

### The Standard Approach

Traditional mushroom [[mushroom-substrate-sterilization-and-pasteurization]]. The reasoning is:

- Endospores of common contaminants (especially Bacillus species) survive boiling temperatures (100°C/212°F)
- Pressure canners achieve approximately 121°C (250°F), which destroys endospores
- Large substrate masses (grain bags, quart jars) require thorough penetration of high heat
- Without [[comparison-pressure-canning-vs-water-bath-canning]]
- **Small mass:** Half-pint jars reach internal temperatures close to boiling throughout the entire substrate
- **Moist heat:** Steam sterilization is more effective than dry heat at the same temperature

## Advantages Over Pressure Canning

### Accessibility

No expensive equipment required — any kitchen has a suitable pot. Beginners can start immediately.

### Simplicity

No pressure gauge monitoring, no weighted regulator, no special safety considerations for pressurized vessels.

### Adequacy for PF TEK

- The brown rice flour and vermiculite substrate is relatively contaminant-resistant compared to grain substrates
- The small jar size allows complete heat penetration
- Contamination rates with proper technique are acceptably low
- PF reported successful cultivation for thousands of growers using this method
