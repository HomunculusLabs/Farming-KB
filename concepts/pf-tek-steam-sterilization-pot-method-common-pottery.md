## Overview

A defining characteristic of the PF TEK is that it does not require a pressure cooker for sterilization. Instead, [[pf-tek-psilocybe-fanaticus-cultivation-method]] developed a steam sterilization method using common kitchen pots that achieves adequate sterility for the small half-pint substrate jars used in the technique. This eliminated one of the most expensive and intimidating barriers to home [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]], making the PF TEK accessible to virtually anyone with a kitchen.

## Why Pressure Cookers Were Previously Required

### The Standard Approach

Traditional mushroom [[synthetic-log-cultivation-methods-guide]] call for pressure canners operating at 15 PSI (pounds per square inch) for [[mushroom-substrate-sterilization-and-pasteurization]]. The reasoning is:

- Endospores of common contaminants (especially Bacillus species) survive boiling temperatures (100°C/212°F)
- Pressure canners achieve approximately 121°C (250°F), which destroys endospores
- Large substrate masses (grain bags, quart jars) require thorough penetration of high heat
- Without [[boil-a-bag-pressure-sterilization-technique]], contamination rates are unacceptably high

### The Cost Barrier

Pressure canners are expensive investments that deter many beginners. The pressure cooker became the primary barrier between curiosity and cultivation.

## The PF TEK Steam Sterilization Method

### Equipment Required

The steam sterilization setup requires only:
- A common kitchen cooking pot with a tight-fitting lid
- Metal jar lids, coins, or washers to elevate jars off the pot bottom
- A wash cloth or towel (optional, for jar separation)
- The filled half-pint substrate jars with modified lids

### Critical Design Principle: Small Jars

The entire steam sterilization method rests on one key factor — the use of half-pint (8 ounce/237 ml) wide-mouth canning jars. PF stated explicitly:

> "The reason the PF TEK uses small half pint canning jars is to keep the substrate mass small enough so that the internal temperature of the jar will reach near boiling temperatures and sterilize the substrate."

The small substrate mass allows heat to penetrate fully through steam alone, without requiring the elevated temperatures of pressure sterilization.

### Step-by-Step Procedure

1. **Prepare the pot bottom:** Place 3-4 metal jar lids (or coins or washers) in the bottom of the pot to create an elevated platform. This prevents the glass jars from directly touching the hot metal bottom, which could cause thermal shock and cracking.

2. **Arrange the jars:** Place the half-pint jars on top of the metal lid platform. Jars should not touch the pot bottom or each other. A wash cloth can be placed between jars to prevent contact.

3. **Add water:** Fill the pot with water to a level about one-third to halfway up the sides of the jars. The water should not be so high that it boils up into the jars (which would waterlog the substrate) nor so low that it boils dry.

4. **Cover the pot:** Place the pot lid on tightly to trap steam and heat.

5. **Bring to boil:** Heat on high until the water reaches a full rolling boil.

6. **Reduce heat and time:** Once boiling, reduce heat to maintain a gentle but steady boil. Steam for 1 to 1.5 hours. PF recommended a minimum of 1 hour, with longer times providing additional assurance.

7. **Cool slowly:** After the timer completes, turn off the heat and allow the pot to cool naturally. Do not remove the lid suddenly, as the temperature differential can crack the jars. Let the jars cool in the pot for several hours or overnight.

### Temperature Dynamics

While boiling water reaches 100°C (212°F) at sea level, the actual sterilization relies on:
- **Steam penetration:** Steam transfers heat more efficiently than boiling water alone
- **Extended time:** The 1+ hour exposure compensates for the lower temperature compared to [[comparison-pressure-canning-vs-water-bath-canning]]
- **Small mass:** Half-pint jars reach internal temperatures close to boiling throughout the entire substrate
- **Moist heat:** Steam sterilization is more effective than dry heat at the same temperature

## Advantages Over Pressure Canning

### Accessibility

No expensive equipment required — any kitchen has a suitable pot. Beginners can start immediately.

### Simplicity

No pressure gauge monitoring, no weighted regulator, no special safety considerations for pressurized vessels.

### Adequacy for PF TEK

- The brown rice flour and vermiculite substrate is relatively contaminant-resistant compared to grain substrates
- The small jar size allows complete heat penetration
- Contamination rates with proper technique are acceptably low
- PF reported successful cultivation for thousands of growers using this method
