# Pink Oyster Mushroom ([[pleurotus-djamor]]) Cultivation

## Overview and Description

The Pink Oyster mushroom encompasses a complex of brilliantly pink
[[oyster-mushrooms]], the most commonly occurring wild Pleurotus in
pan-tropical climatic zones of the world. Known for its speed to
fruiting, ability to flourish on a wide variety of base materials, and
high temperature tolerance, this species is so aggressive as to
colonize unpasteurized bulk substrates before competitors can flourish.
When growing en masse, albino clusters sometimes form.

Caps share the general shape and appearance of P. ostreatus except the
primordia are bright reddish ("salmon-egg") pink, becoming pinkish as
mushrooms develop, eventually a dull pink to light pinkish cinnamon
colored and often fading to straw-colored when over-mature. The color
transitions are age-dependent and vary between strains and light
conditions. The gills are particularly strongly pigmented with pinkish
tones when young, fading to a creamy beige in age. Commonly growing in
clusters of multiple mushrooms. Variants ranging from beige to cream to
white often appear from the same fruiting container as the pink forms.

## Common Names

The Pink Oyster Mushroom, The Salmon Oyster, The Strawberry Oyster,
The Flamingo Mushroom, Takiiro Hiratake (Japanese), Tabang ngungut
(Dasun-Northern Borneo).

## Taxonomy and Species Complex

This mushroom has a trail of synonyms representing a large complex of
pan-tropical varieties. Pleurotus flabellatus (Berk. & Br.) Saccardo,
P. ostreato-roseus Singer and P. salmoneo-stramineus Vasil. are
included within the P. djamor species complex. Although the mushrooms
are usually pink, this color is temporal, sometimes fading as the
mushrooms mature.

Originally published by Fries in 1838 as Lentinus djamor. Corner (1981)
proposed the pink gilled forms be called P. djamor var. roseus. The
color of the fruitbody directly influences the color of the spores:
pink mushrooms give pink spores, white to beige mushrooms from the same
dikaryon give off-white to light gray-beige spores. Redhead (1993)
suspects this pigment is present in the cytoplasm and not in the outer
spore coat.

This large group of Pink Oyster varieties represents a complex of
varieties in a state of rapid convergent and/or divergent evolution.
Until DNA studies are completed, the taxonomy is unlikely to be further
resolved by macroscopic or microscopic analyses.

## Distribution and Habitat

A tropical mushroom complex, widespread throughout the tropics and
subtropics. Collected in Thailand, Cambodia, Singapore, Vietnam, Ceylon,
Malaysia, New Guinea, North Borneo, Japan, Brazil, Mexico and the
Antilles. Preferring tropical and subtropical hardwoods including
palms, rubber trees, and also found on bamboo.

## Microscopic Features

Spores measure 6-10 x 4-5 micrometers, smooth, cylindrically shaped.
Clamp connections present. Cheilocystidia present. Pleurocystidia
absent. Hyphae arranged dimitically. Spores from the same fruiting
column vary: pink spores from pink mushrooms and light beige spores
from mushrooms that were originally pink but faded to cream-beige.

## Mycelial Characteristics

White at first, casting a longitudinally linear [[mycelium]], often
over-run with long, diverging rhizomorphs, eventually cottony with
maturity, and aerial. Most strains soon develop strong pinkish tones,
especially as the mycelium matures, at and around the sites of primordia
formation. Flaming pink primordia often form as cluster colonies along
the inside periphery of the petri dish. As grain spawn matures, pink
rhizomorphs and mycelia can predominate. A milky gray metabolic
exudate collects at the bottom of incubation containers.

## Cultivation Methods

### Natural Method
Cultivable on hardwood stumps and logs. Cultivators in Asia have found
that this species quickly colonizes unpasteurized cereal straws before
contaminants emerge, a unique advantage for growers without
pasteurization equipment.

### Spawn Expansion
Cultures in petri dishes are cut out, fragmented in a high-speed
blender, and used to generate grain spawn. Liquid-inoculated 1/2 gallon
grain jars are fully grown through in 4-5 days. Grain Masters can be
expanded two more orders of magnitude. Once colonized, grain spawn
should be implanted directly into the fruiting substrate.

### Agar Media
MYPA, PDYA, OMYA or DFA.

### Spawn Media
Grain spawn for all three generations.

### Fruiting Substrates
Hardwood sawdust, cereal straw, corn waste, coffee residue, cotton
waste, banana fronds, palm debris, and sugar cane bagasse. One Brazilian
formula: 100 lbs sugar cane bagasse, 8 lbs rice bran, 3 lbs rice straw,
2 lbs calcium carbonate, mixed, wetted, pasteurized at 140 degrees F
for 2-4 hours.

### Containers
Polyethylene bags or columns, trays, or racks.

## Growth Parameters

### Spawn Run
- Temperature: 75-85 degrees F (24 degrees C)
- Relative Humidity: 95-100%
- Duration: 7-10 days
- CO2: greater than 5000 ppm
- Fresh Air Exchanges: 0-1 per hour
- Light: not applicable

### Primordia Formation
- Temperature: 65-75 degrees F (18-25 degrees C)
- Relative Humidity: 95-100%
- Duration: 2-4 days
- CO2: 500-1000 ppm
- Fresh Air Exchanges: 5-8 per hour
- Light: 750-1500 lux

### Fruitbody Development
- Temperature: 70-85 degrees F (20-30 degrees C)
- Relative Humidity: 85-90%
