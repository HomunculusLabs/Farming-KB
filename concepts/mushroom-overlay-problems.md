# Overlay Problems and Solutions

## Overview

Overlay is one of the most common and frustrating problems encountered by
mushroom cultivators using casing layers. It is a dense, mat-like growth of
mycelium that covers the casing surface and shows little or no inclination
to form pinheads. Understanding the causes of overlay and implementing
corrective measures is essential for restoring productive fruiting.

## What Is Overlay

Overlay occurs when mycelium completely covers the casing surface, forming
an impervious mat. This dense mycelial growth makes the casing surface
resistant to water penetration and produces few or no pinheads. The
condition represents a failure of the normal transition from vegetative
growth to generative (fruiting) mode. The mycelium continues to grow
vegetatively instead of forming primordia.

## Primary Causes of Overlay

Overlay results from a combination of environmental factors that favor
continued vegetative growth over fruiting. The three principal causes are:

### 1. Dry Casing Layer

A casing that is too dry is the most common cause of overlay. In a dry
casing, the mycelium is characterized by a lack of rhizomorphs and an
abundance of fine capillary-type mycelia. This fine growth can totally
permeate the casing layer, which then becomes hard, compact, and
unreceptive to water. The arid microclimate rarely permits primordia
formation, and the mycelium continues spreading across the surface.

### 2. High CO2 Levels

Elevated carbon dioxide concentrations encourage vegetative mycelial
expansion. For Agaricus brunnescens, pinning does not proceed properly at
CO2 concentrations above 2000 ppm. When CO2 remains high, the mycelium
totally covers the casing surface rather than forming pinheads. During the
casing run, high CO2 (5000-10000 ppm) is actually beneficial for mycelial
colonization, but these levels must be reduced before pinning initiation.

### 3. Low Humidity

Low relative humidity in the growing room accelerates evaporation from the
casing surface. As the surface dries, the mycelial mat becomes dense and
impermeable. Room humidity should be maintained at 95% during casing
colonization and primordia formation. Values below this promote overlay.

### 4. High Temperature

Air temperatures remaining too high during the pinning initiation period
prevent the temperature shock needed to slow vegetative growth and trigger
fruiting. If the substrate stays at spawn-run temperatures, the mycelium
continues expanding rather than forming primordia.

## Additional Contributing Factors

- **Uneven casing depth**: Shallow areas colonize faster and break through
  the surface early, while deeper areas lag behind, creating irregular
  growth patterns.
- **Overly dense casing**: Fine peat or compacted casing materials can
  inhibit proper gas exchange and moisture movement.
- **Delayed initiation**: Waiting too long after the mycelium reaches the
  casing surface valleys before beginning the environmental transition to
  fruiting conditions.
- **Stroma formation**: In Agaricus, cottony mycelial sectors on agar
  correlate with later stroma formation on casing. Stroma is a dense
  mat-like growth that rarely produces mushrooms.

## Corrective Measures: Patching

Overlay can be counteracted by a technique called "patching." The procedure
involves lightly sprinkling moist casing material over areas where the
mycelium has broken through the casing surface prematurely or has formed a
dense mat. The patching material should be the same casing mix used
originally, brought to proper moisture content.

Patching serves to:
- Re-cover exposed mycelium with a protective moist layer
- Create new surface structure (mountains and valleys) for pin formation
- Allow mycelial growth to resume normal patterns in the casing zone
- Restore the humid microclimate necessary for primordia development

## Corrective Measures: Deep Scratching

For more severe overlay, deep scratching can be employed. When the mycelium
is midway through the casing or has formed a dense mat, the entire casing
layer is thoroughly ruffled down to the bulk substrate using a scratching
stick (a 1x2x24 inch board with parallel rows of nails). The agitated and
broken mycelium rapidly reestablishes itself, and within three to four days
completely recolonizes the casing. The result is early, even, and prolific
pinhead formation.

Important: before using deep scratching, the grower must be certain that
the substrate and casing are free of competitor molds and nematodes, as
scratching can spread these contaminants.

## Prevention Strategies

Prevention is far more effective than correction. Key preventive measures:

1. **Maintain optimum casing moisture** at 70-75% for peat-based casings
2. **Keep room humidity at 95%** during casing colonization
3. **Begin initiation promptly** when mycelium appears in the casing valleys
4. **Lower CO2 below 2000 ppm** by introducing fresh air during initiation
5. **Apply temperature shock** by lowering air temperature to fruiting range
6. **Use proper casing depth** (minimum 1 inch for bulk substrates)
7. **Maintain rough, open surface structure** for the casing layer
8. **Avoid panning** from heavy direct watering that seals the surface

## Impact on Yield

Few flushes will be as great from a casing with overlay as from a properly
managed casing. The dense mat redirects energy from fruitbody production to
continued vegetative expansion, permanently reducing yield potential. Early
detection and correction are critical for minimizing losses.
