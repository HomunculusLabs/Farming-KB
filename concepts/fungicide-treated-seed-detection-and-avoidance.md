### Visual Inspection

Treated seeds often have visible indicators that differ from natural seed appearance:

- **Colored dye coatings**: The most obvious indicator; commonly pink, blue, green, red, or purple. The dyes serve as a visual warning that the seed has been treated.
- **Powdery residue**: A dusty or powdery coating on seed surfaces that transfers to fingers when handled
- **Unusual sheen**: A glossy or waxy appearance not present on untreated seed
- **Chemical odor**: Some treatments produce a detectable chemical smell

However, visual inspection alone is not definitive. Some treatments use clear or colorless formulations that are invisible to the naked eye. Additionally, some untreated seeds may have natural coatings or discoloration that could be mistaken for treatment.

### Inquiring with the Supplier

When purchasing seed, directly asking the supplier about treatment status is advisable:

- "Has this seed been treated with any fungicides or pesticides?"
- "Is this seed suitable for sprouting or consumption?" (Treated seed cannot legally be sold for sprouting)
- "Can you provide the seed treatment label or specification sheet?"

Honest suppliers will be able to provide this information. Evasive answers or inability to confirm untreated status should be treated as a warning sign.
