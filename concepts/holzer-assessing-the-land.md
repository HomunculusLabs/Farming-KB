## Creating Suntraps

On north-facing slopes and areas with few hours of sunshine, I use many different techniques to capture heat. It is possible to make a niche in the hillside. You should place as many large stones on the hillside as possible. They store the heat like a masonry stove and release it slowly. I place plants that need a lot of heat next to the stones.

If possible, I put a pond or lake in front of the niche. The sun's rays are then reflected by the surface of the water and the overall effect of the niche is increased. This helps the niche to gather heat and therefore serve its purpose as a suntrap. Even plants that require very warm conditions can be cultivated at high altitudes and on north-facing slopes.

## Soil Conditions

It is particularly important to get a feel for the quality of the soil. The more I know about the properties of the soil, the better I can work with it. The best soil has a crumbly structure. A crumbly topsoil allows plants to establish their roots more easily. Its high pore volume means that it holds water and nutrients like a sponge.

'Light' soil is well aerated and heats up quickly but has low capacity to store water and nutrients. 'Heavy' soil retains water easily with higher nutrient content, but is poorly aerated and prone to compaction. [[holzer-terrace-systems]] | [[holzer-roundwood-shelters-earth-cellars]]
