### Pathogen Memory (Systemic Acquired Resistance)

When a plant survives a pathogen attack, it develops enhanced resistance to
subsequent infections throughout the rest of the plant, a phenomenon called
systemic acquired resistance (SAR). This immunological memory involves:

- Accumulation of [[comparison-jasmonate-vs-salicylic-acid-defense-signaling]] at the infection site
- Production of pathogenesis-related (PR) proteins
- Epigenetic priming of defense genes, making them faster and stronger to
  activate upon subsequent attack

SAR can persist for weeks to months, providing long-lasting protection
without the adaptive immune system that animals possess.

### Stress Priming

Plants exposed to moderate stress (drought, heat, cold, salinity) often
show improved tolerance to subsequent, more severe stress episodes. This
priming effect involves epigenetic marks at stress-responsive gene loci
that poise these genes for rapid reactivation.
