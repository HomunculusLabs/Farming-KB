# Holzer Irrigation Wisdom — When and How to Water

## Overview

Sepp Holzer's approach to irrigation contradicts many conventional practices. Based on decades of observation at the Krameterhof, he advocates for night watering, minimal irrigation of established crops, and understanding plant wilting as a natural protective mechanism rather than a distress signal.

## Night Watering

### The Principle

"Temperatures are lower at night, humidity is higher, and these are ideal conditions for the plants to absorb and use the water with maximum effect."

Watering at night rather than during the day ensures that water infiltrates the soil rather than evaporating from the surface. The combination of lower temperatures and higher humidity means that more water reaches the roots where it is needed.

### Day Watering is Wasteful

"Most water will evaporate straight away during the day and plants are unable to utilise enough." Daytime irrigation loses a large proportion of the applied water to evaporation before it can benefit the plants.

## Understanding Wilting

### A Protective Mechanism

"Do not panic when the plants wilt a little during the day, it is part of their protection mechanism to stop water from evaporating."

Wilting is the plant's natural response to water stress: by reducing the surface area of leaves, the plant minimizes water loss through transpiration. This is an adaptation, not a crisis.

### The Counterproductive Watering Response

"If I water them when they are wilting I will achieve the opposite to the desired effect as it takes a while for the plant to open and most of the initial watering will have run off unused."

Watering a wilted plant during the heat of the day is counterproductive for several reasons:
1. The plant needs time to recover from its protective wilted state
2. Most water runs off the soil surface before the plant can absorb it
3. Surface water evaporates quickly in the sun
4. The rapid temperature change can shock the plant

## Grain Does Not Need Watering

"Do not water grain, it is unnecessary, unless the region is extremely hot and dry."

Holzer challenges the common assumption that grain crops need supplemental irrigation. In most climates, grain's deep root system is sufficient to access soil moisture without irrigation.

### Undersowing with Clover

"Undersow with white clover that will protect and improve the soil." Instead of irrigating grain, Holzer recommends undersowing with nitrogen-fixing clover, which protects the soil surface, retains moisture, and improves soil fertility.

## Desertification Adaptations

### Buried Wood

"For regions affected by desertification: bury wood in trenches and plant on top of them." This is essentially the hugelkultur technique adapted for arid conditions: the buried wood serves as a moisture reservoir, reducing the need for irrigation.

### Mulching

Holzer consistently advocates for mulching—covering the soil surface with organic material—to reduce evaporation, suppress weeds, and build soil organic matter.
