## Why Lookalikes Matter

Misidentification of psilocybin mushrooms is a serious safety
concern. The Galerina genus, particularly Galerina marginata
(deadly galerina), shares habitat and general appearance with
several Psilocybe species and contains lethal amatoxins. The
image guide does not explicitly catalogue lookalikes but provides
photographic context for distinguishing features.

## Genera with Psilocybin Lookalikes

### Galerina marginata (Deadly Galerina)

The most dangerous lookalike. Shares woody-substrate habitat with
Psilocybe cyanescens and P. azurescens, both of which are
documented in the guide growing on wood chips. Key differences:

- Galerina has a brown spore print (rusty brown), unlike the dark
  purple-brown to black spore prints of Psilocybe species
- Galerina typically has a more prominent annulus (ring) on the
  stem
- Cap color tends more toward tawny brown rather than the
  caramel-golden tones of cyanescens
- Lacks the blue bruising reaction characteristic of psilocybin-
  containing species

### Panaeolina foenisecii (Haymaker's Mushroom)

Could be confused with [[gymnopilus-spectabilis-profile]] (Big Laughing Gym),
documented in the guide growing in clusters on wood. Key points:

- Hypholoma fasciculare (sulphur tuft) grows in similar clustered
  patterns on wood but has greenish-yellow gills and is bitter
- Psathyrella species are generally more fragile-stemmed
- G. spectabilis has an orange-to-rusty coloration that helps
  distinguish it

## Species in the Guide Requiring Particular Caution

Several species documented in the guide grow in habitats shared
with dangerous lookalikes:

- Psilocybe cyanescens on alder chips: shares substrate with
  Galerina marginata
- Panaeolus subbalteatus on dung: shares habitat with other
  Panaeolus species of uncertain toxicity
- Psilocybe semilanceata in grass: shares pasture habitat with
  multiple toxic small brown mushrooms
- Gymnopilus spectabilis on wood: similar clustered growth pattern
  to several toxic wood-inhabiting species

## Visual Discrimination Strategy

Based on the guide's photographic evidence, the most reliable
field discrimination combines:

1. Spore print color (the single most important macroscopic test)
2. Blue bruising reaction on stem and cap tissue
3. Habitat specificity (substrate preference)
4. Geographic range awareness
5. Growth season timing

The guide's inclusion of specimens from specific known locations
(Germany's Black Forest, northern California, Thailand, Hawaii)
provides geographic context that aids in narrowing possible
identifications.
