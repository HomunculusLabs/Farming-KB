# Earthworks Planning and Post-Earthworks Planting

Mollison provides detailed guidance on planning earthworks and
the critical follow-up of planting after soil disturbance. The
principle is that every time soil is moved, planting and
stabilisation must follow immediately to [[query-how-do-i-garden-on-a-slope-and-prevent-erosion]] and
weed invasion.

## Planning the Earthworks Process

It is best to plan all aspects of the earth-moving process
before machines or labourers arrive on site:

1. Make an initial decision where to place the road, dam,
   house site, drains, etc., using contour map and plan if
   necessary.
2. Test the soil by auger holes, soil samples, and soil pits
   to determine if the soil is good enough to suit plans (a
   good clay soil for dams is essential).
3. Peg out the site, using a level, measuring tape, and stakes
   with red or white cloth attached so the earth-moving machine
   can follow them.
4. Plan a place to store all topsoil removed during
   excavations. Never allow topsoil to be mixed with subsoil,
   but carefully remove it to be later returned to the site as
   a [[cervantes-green-algae-growing-medium]] and to stabilise subsoil erosion.

## Soil Volume Changes

When soil is moved, it becomes loose, so that soil air space
and total volume may increase to 145% of the original. Even
when compacted by machine, the fill may occupy a space 10%
larger than the cut it came from. Conscientiously compacted
clay in dam walls may settle as little as 1% over time, but
loose fill will eventually settle to 75% or less of its
uncompacted volume. This has great relevance to house
foundations and wall stability.

## Preparing a House Site

The process for preparing a house site with drainage:

- Careful survey. Place pegs outside the site. Call in the
  bulldozer.
- Strip off topsoil carefully and mound above and at either
  end of the site.
- Cut house/garden level; use subsoil fill for access roads
  only.
- Call in ditcher or backhoe to cut foundation and drainage
  trenches. Pour foundations and slab, paths; place drains and
  pipes.
- Call in small blade and bucket machine to replace topsoil
  and neaten the site. Some soil can be mounded to the windward
  side for hedges.
- Plant or seed all topsoil to prevent erosion.
- Fine-tune with barrow and rakes; check drainage.

## Planting After Earthworks

Every time soil is moved, planting or seeding must follow
immediately. There are two reasons: to prevent erosion, which
can be severe on bare slopes at only 2% slope, especially in
rains; and to prevent invasion by unwanted volunteer plants,
which may become difficult to displace later.

If a full set of [[permaculture-plants-food-forest-ground-covers-bamboos]], pioneers, and long-term plants
can be set out in new earth, a great deal of time and work is
saved. A broadscale scatter of mixed seed, raked in, will
prepare the way for permanent placements. Having planned the
planting and stabilisation, plant materials must be on standby
to implement the plan as soon after disturbance as possible.
