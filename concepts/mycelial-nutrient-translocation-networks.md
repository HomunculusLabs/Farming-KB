# Mycelial Nutrient Translocation Networks

## Overview

Basidiomycetes are the major agents of decomposition and nutrient [[pcsi-scintillation-imaging-mycelial-nutrient-transport-bebber]]
cycling in [[dighton-fungi-acidifying-pollutants-forest-ecosystems]], occurring as both saprotrophs and
mycorrhizal symbionts. Their mycelium can scavenge and sequester
nutrients from soil, concentrate nutrients from decomposing organic
matter, relocate nutrients between different organic resources, and
ultimately make nutrients available to plants to maintain primary
productivity. (see [[mycelial-network-nutrient-transport-pulsatile-dynamics]]).

## Network Architecture

### Cord Formation
Hyphae of both saprotrophic and ectomycorrhizal basidiomycetes that
ramify through soil often aggregate to form rapidly extending, persistent,
specialized high-conductivity channels called cords. These cords form
complex networks extending for meters or even hectares in natural
environments. (see [[mycelial-nutrient-translocation-networks-gadd]]).

### Dynamic Reconfiguration
The architecture of mycelial networks is not static. It is continuously
reconfigured in response to:

- Local nutritional cues
- Environmental conditions
- Physical damage or predation
- New resource discovery

This occurs through a combination of growth, branching, fusion, and
regression of hyphal elements. Whether specific global mechanisms exist
to couple local [[plant-sensory-perception]] over different length scales, or
whether collective behavior is an emergent property of local hyphal
interactions, remains an open question.

## Nutrient Transport Mechanisms

### Proposed Mechanisms
The precise mechanisms underlying nutrient translocation in fungi are
not yet fully known but are thought to include:

- **Mass flow**: Bulk movement of cytoplasm through hyphae
- **Diffusion**: Passive movement along concentration gradients
- **Cytoplasmic streaming**: Generalized flow of cytoplasmic contents
- **Vesicular transport**: Specific, directed movement of
  membrane-bound packages

### Vacuolar System
The highly dynamic pleiomorphic vacuolar system in [[environmental-sensing-filamentous-fungi-read]] may
play a key role in long-distance translocation. Vacuolar structure
develops from a complex reticulum of fine tubes interspersed with small
spherical vacuoles at the hyphal tip, to a series of larger adherent
vacuoles interconnected with fine tubes further back from the tip.

## Pulsatile Transport

### Discovery
Research using photon-counting scintillation imaging (PCSI) and
radiolabelled amino acid analogues (¹⁴C-AIB) has revealed that nutrient
transport through mycelial networks is pulsatile — occurring in rhythmic
oscillations rather than as steady flow.
