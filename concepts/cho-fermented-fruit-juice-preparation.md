Fermented Fruit Juice (FFJ) is described by Dr. Cho as "an artificial
honey" and a nutritional activation enzyme that is very effective in
natural farming. FFJ is a kind of [[master-cho-knf-fpj-fermented-plant-juice-guide]]
that only uses fruits as its main ingredients. It is used to revitalize
crops, livestock and humans.

## Suitable Fruits

As main fruit ingredients, use Banana, Papaya, Mango, Grape, Melon,
Apple, and other sweet fruits. The fruits must be sweet and should grow
in your locality. Important restrictions apply:

- Use grapes only for grapes and citrus for citrus fruits.
- These fruits are not good when used on other crops due to their cold
  and sour characteristics.
- If the quantity of fruits is not sufficient, add supplementary
  ingredients such as spinach roots, wild yam, cabbage, cucumber,
  zucchini and radish.

## Materials Needed

1. At least 3 fully ripened fruits (picked or fallen)
2. Jaggery / Brown sugar
3. Clay pot or glass jar container
4. Wooden stick (ladle)
5. Chopping board
6. Porous paper (paper towel)

## Preparation Steps

### Step 1: Sugar Ratio

For 1 kg of fruit ingredient, use 1.2 to 1.3 kg of brown sugar
(Jaggery) in summer and 1 kg in winter. One function of brown sugar
is to control moisture. During winter the temperature is lower, so
there is less need to control moisture.

### Step 2: Container Preparation

Wash and dry the jar to disinfect the container in the sun. This
step ensures no unwanted microorganisms interfere with fermentation.

### Step 3: Processing the Fruits

Spread sugar on the chopping board. Dice fruit ingredients starting
with the sweetest. After dicing, smear sugar on the fruits and put
them in the container. This step should be done quickly to prevent
the loss of essential substances. Fruits that are difficult to dice
like grapes and strawberries may be slightly crunched with clean
fingers.

### Step 4: Layering Sugar

Use half of the sugar while dicing and pour the remaining half after
all fruits have been diced and placed in the container. This layering
ensures even extraction.

### Step 5: Mixing

Slowly stir the mixture of diced fruits and sugar about 2 to 3 times
with a wooden stick. Temperature plays an important role in this
process:
- Stir with fewer strokes in summer (less agitation needed)
- Stir with more strokes in winter (more mixing needed)

### Step 6: Covering and Fermentation

Cover with porous paper and tie to the container. Porous paper allows
a good amount of air supply for fermentation. During summer,
fermentation completes in 4 to 5 days. In winter the process takes
7 to 8 days.

### Step 7: Storage

After fermentation, sprinkle some more sugar on the mixture and store
in a cool and shaded place. It is normal that some sugar can still be
found on the surface.

## Fermentation Quality Indicators

When big bubbles pop during the fermentation process, it means the
power of enzyme is weak. When tiny bubbles fiercely boil at once, it
means the power of enzyme is strong. Observing bubble behavior helps
determine the quality of the final product.

## When and How to Use FFJ

### Changeover Period

During the changeover period, crops require ample amounts of
phosphoric acid. Acid from FFJs of less ripe fruits is recommended,
such as those from Grape, Papaya, Mulberry or Raspberry.

### Reproductive Growth

FFJs made from fully ripened fruits of apple, banana, mango, papaya,
peach, and grape are useful to supply the calcium demand of crops at
this stage.

### General Application

- After the changeover period, apply FFJ diluted in 1:1000 ratio in
  water.
- Spray on chicken housing, vegetables, and orchards for protection
  against diseases.
- FFJ is an indispensable material in Natural Farming as a plant
  activation enzyme. It enables resources to be utilized in various
  applications such as enzyme feed and enzyme fertilizers.
- FFJ is an excellent output for re-energizing crops and livestock.

## Relationship to the Nutritive Cycle

FFJ fits into the [[cho-nutritive-cycle-theory]]
by providing appropriate nutrients at the changeover and reproductive
growth stages. The selection of fruit ripeness corresponds to the
plant's shifting nutritional demands.
