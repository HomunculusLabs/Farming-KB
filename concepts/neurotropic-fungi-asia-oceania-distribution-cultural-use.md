## Japan

Japan is the best-studied country in Asia for neurotropic fungi, with approximately 20 accepted species:

**Psilocybe species (approximately 10 taxa):**
- [[psilocybe-muliercula]], and Psilocybe pintonii, all belonging to Section Zapotecorum. This relationship represents a remarkable amphi-Pacific disjunction.
- [[psilocybe-zapotecorum]] complex
- Psilocybe farinacea — grows on rice straw, reflecting adaptation to agricultural substrates
- [[psilocybe-venenata]] — a Japanese species known to cause accidental poisonings when confused with edible mushrooms
- [[psilocybe-mexicana]] from Mexico, belonging to Section Mexicanae; this represents one of the most compelling trans-Pacific biogeographical connections in the genus
- Additional species include [[psilocybe-fimetaria]], Psilocybe pelliculosa, and [[amanita-muscaria-entity]]** and its close relatives are well documented in Japan. Imazeki et al. (1988) provide comprehensive treatments of Japanese [[panaeolus-subbalteatus]]** and **[[psilocybe-samuiensis]] — described from Koh Samui by Guzmán et al. and belonging to Section Mexicanae, closely related to [[psilocybe-makarorae]] from New Zealand
- [[copelandia-tropicalis]] and [[panaeolus-cyanescens-entity]] (syn. Copelandia cyanescens) — widespread in tropical grasslands
- [[psilocybe-zapotecorum]] from the Americas and [[copelandia-tropicalis]] — a pantropical species
- [[copelandia-cambodginiensis]] — named for its Cambodian type locality, this species has since been found across tropical Southeast Asia
- Other species remain poorly documented due to the region's turbulent political history limiting mycological exploration

**Borneo:**
- Borneo's neurotropic fungal diversity is almost completely unknown. The island's vast tropical rainforests and diverse ecosystems likely harbor many undocumented species. No specific neurotropic Psilocybe species have been confirmed from Borneo, though this almost certainly reflects collecting deficiency rather than genuine absence.

### New Zealand

New Zealand harbors some of the most biogeographically significant neurotropic fungi in the region:

- **[[psilocybe-zapotecorum]] from Mexico and South America. This relationship provides strong evidence for trans-Pacific dispersal or ancient Gondwanan distribution of the genus.
- **[[psilocybe-mexicana]] from Mexico and [[amanita-muscaria-entity]]** — introduced to New Zealand with pine plantations (Hongo and Yokoyama, 1978); now well-established and naturalized
