## Permaculture Food Forests (Design Course)
**Source:** [[bill-mollison]] (1981, Pamphlet X)
**Updated:** 2026-04-18
## Overview
Food forests are the highest expression of permaculture design —
perennial tree-based systems that provide staple foods, fuel,
forage, and structural materials while maintaining soil and water.
The forest is "a mighty great organism" — a complex,
interconnected system rather than a collection of trees.

## Modern Food Forest Pioneers
### Cliff Adam (Pacific Islands)
- UN grant of $136,000 to collect food plants
- Sent back 600 sorts of mango, 30-40 breadfruit varieties,
  all guavas
- Set out on 68 acres shoreline + 135 acres hillside
- Within 3-4 years: production from 200 acres would feed the
  entire island
- "I am in a very embarrassing position" — as agricultural
  research officer, already solely responsible for the island's
  food
### The Coconut Grenade Technique
- Run along ridges into grasslands, heaving coconuts down to
  creeks
- Approximately 4 of every 100 take root and start up
- One man assembled 600 tropical species, then added vines and
  epiphytes
- By year 2-3: "over his head in food," could feed the coastline
  for miles
### The Electronics Businessman
- Sold electronics business for millions
- Set up 12-month tree nutrition program — found varieties
  providing complete food every month of the year
- Read deeply into areas like avocado root temperature
- Proved year-round food self-sufficiency from trees is
  achievable
## Layered Forest Design
Food forests work best when designed in vertical layers:
- **Canopy:** Tall nut and fruit trees (oak, chestnut, walnut)
- **Understory:** Smaller fruit trees (apple, pear, plum)
