# Grain Processing and Milling Guide

## Grain Stages of Maturity

Grain progresses through: seed -> sprout -> seedling -> tiller -> booting -> heading -> flowering -> milk phase -> dough stage -> dead ripe. Harvest in late dough stage (far past milk, still dentable) and let dry to dead ripe.

## Harvesting

### Tools
- **Sickle**: for very small areas
- **Scythe**: larger areas -- large blade swung low with long wooden handle (snath). Hold nibs (handgrips), keep arms straight, twist at waist while stepping forward. Keep tip of blade from going into ground. A cradle attachment collects cut grain.
- **Horse-drawn mower**: fastest and easiest but requires horse care
- **Windrow**: long row of grain (from mower or scythe)
- **Pile**: small heap (from scythe if grain is dead ripe)

### Sheaving and Shocking
A sheave is a large handful of stalks tied near the top with barren tillers (secondary stalks from base) using a binder's knot. Several sheaves are leaned against each other with another on top, making a shock big enough for your hands to meet around.

### Drying
- Let dry in the field as sheaves/shocks
- Or bring into a large dry barn
- Ready when no green stems remain and corn stalks sound hollow when tapped

## Threshing

**Important**: Oats and barley cannot be hulled by flailing -- they must be steamed and ground in a mill.

For other grains, use a hand flail on the barn floor:
- Flail: wood handle with swingle (swinging piece) attached by a loose ring
- Swing back and forth quickly, hitting grain on the floor
- Flip grain with pitchforks and flail again
- Do your flailing in winter
- For oats use a heavy swingle; for beans a light swingle
- Pile straw separately

## Winnowing

Separate chaff from grain:
- Toss grain up with a basket and catch it on a windy day
- Or toss with a pitchfork
- Or use a winnowing tray (frame with screen)
- For beans: pour back and forth between two tubs on a windy day
- Save chaff for [[livestock-feed-formulation]]
- Save straw or pile in field for animals

## Seed Saving

- Save seed from your second crop (unless using a hybrid)
- Select the best seed heads: unbroken and healthy
- Let dry in the shock for at least one month until totally dry
- Thresh and ensure no leaves or twigs remain

## Storage

- All grains must be stored in rodent-proof containers in the house (not the shed)
- Grain must be very dry before packing
- Add bay leaves to help deter pests
- Keep in cool, dry place
- Grain keeps for a year or more until ground
- Check periodically for mold, bugs, or rodent signs
- Once ground, flour must be used immediately

### Bug Control in Stored Grain

- **Dry ice method**: 1 tablespoon dry ice per 5 gallons (19 liters) of grain. Put ice on bottom of airtight container, pour grain on top, wait 1 hour, then seal. The CO2 kills bugs.
- **Heat method** (not for seed grain): spread 1/4 inch (0.6 cm) on a pan, heat at 140F (60C) for 30 minutes.

### Ergot Fungus Warning
If grain gets damp it may develop ergot -- hard, black, purple kernels. Never eat moldy grain or feed it to animals (can be fatal). For desperate seed grain: soak in very salty water; sclerotia (ergot masses) float to the top for skimming.

## Processing Methods

- **Cracking**: breaking kernel in two or more pieces (usually for corn)
- **Crimping**: flattening kernel slightly (usually for oats)
- **Flaking**: treating with heat and/or moisture, then flattening
- **Grinding**: forcing through rollers and screens
- **Rolling**: smashing between rollers at different speeds, with or without steaming
