# Psychoactive Mushroom Species in Non-Psilocybe Genera

## Overview

While [[psilocybe-baeocystis]] dominates with 116 of the 216 known neurotropic
species, numerous other genera contain psychoactive fungi. These include
species with [[psilocybin-potency-dosage-guide]], those with entirely different chemistry (such
as [[amanita-muscaria-guide]]), and fungi used as sacred mushrooms by indigenous
peoples despite lacking confirmed chemical analysis.

### Amanita (Amanitaceae)

Three [[amanita-muscaria-guide]] species contain isoxazole derivatives rather than
indole alkaloids:
- **A. muscaria** (Fly Agaric): Contains ibotenic acid and muscimol;
  traditionally used by Siberian peoples (Chukchee, Koryak); Wasson
  (1968) proposed it as the Vedic Soma; worldwide distribution
- **A. pantherina** (Panther Cap): Contains ibotenic acid and muscimol;
  generally more toxic than A. muscaria; wide distribution
- **A. regalis**: Most potent of the three; more restricted distribution

### Panaeolus and Copelandia (Coprinaceae)

### Copelandia (12 species)
All confirmed Copelandia species contain psilocybin. Key species:
- **C. cyanescens**: Most widespread tropical species; found throughout
  the Caribbean, Americas, Africa, Asia, Australia, and Oceania
- **C. tropicalis**: Widespread in Africa, Asia, and Oceania
- **C. cambodginiensis**: Described from Cambodia; widespread in
  Southeast Asia and Hawaii
- Other species: C. anomala, C. bispora, C. mexicana, C. moelleri,
  C. tirunelveliensis, C. affinis, C. lentispora

### Panaeolus (13 species)
Several confirmed or suspected to contain psilocybin:
- **P. subbalteatus**: Widely distributed; confirmed by Gartz (1989b)
- **P. sphinctrinus**: Oldest known suspected neurotropic Panaeolus
- **P. retirugis**: Confirmed by Fiussello and Ceruti-Scurti (1972)
- **P. africanus**: Distinctly African species
- Other species: P. fimicola, P. castaneifolius, P. ater, P.
  papilionaceus, P. olivaceus, P. rubricaulis, P. microsporus

### Panaeolina (4 species)
- **P. foenisecii**: Most controversial neurotropic mushroom; ubiquitous
  in lawns worldwide. Mantle and Waight (1969), Ott and Guzman (1976),
  Beug and Bigwood (1982) found NO psilocybin, while others reported it.
  Gartz (1996) noted analytical errors may explain positive results.
- **P. rhombisperma**: Described from Java (Horak, 1980)
- **P. sagarae**: Described from Japan (Hongo, 1977)

## Gymnopilus (Cortinariaceae, 14 species)

One of the most controversial genera for neurotropic status:
- **G. validipes**: First confirmed to contain psilocybin (Hatfield et
  al., 1977)
- **G. purpuratus**: Confirmed by Gartz (1989c) to contain psilocybin,
  psilocin, and baeocystin; found in Germany, Netherlands, Chile,
  Australia, New Zealand
- **G. spectabilis**: "Big Laughing Gym"; widely reported but
  Stijve and Kuyper (1988) found NO psilocybin
- **G. aeruginosus** and **G. luteofolius**: Confirmed from the
  Pacific Northwest (Koike et al., 1981)
- Other species: G. sapineus, G. liquiritiae, G. penetrans, G.
  lateritius, G. subpurpuratus, G. longipes

### Inocybe (Cortinariaceae, 6 species)

Inocybe species are mycorrhizal associates of trees:
- **I. aeruginascens**: First found in Hungary (Babos, 1968);
  extensively studied by Gartz; contains psilocybin, psilocin,
  baeocystin, and the unique compound aeruginascin; also contains
  muscarine
- **I. corydalina** and **I. corydalina var. erinaceomorpha**: Confirmed
  by Stijve and Kuyper (1985)
- **I. haemacta**, **I. coelestium**, **I. tricolor**: Confirmed from
  Europe

## Pluteus, Conocybe, and Other Small Genera

### Pluteus (Pluteaceae, 6 species)
Pluteus species grow on dead wood:
- **P. salicinus**: Confirmed to contain psilocybin and baeocystin
  (Gartz, 1987c); widespread in Europe
- **P. atricapillus**, **P. cyanopus**, **P. villosus**: Confirmed
  from Europe and Morocco
- **P. nigroviridis**: Reported from Hungary

### Conocybe (Bolbitiaceae, 4 species)
Small, delicate coprophilous fungi:
- **C. cyanopus** and **C. smithii**: Confirmed (Christiansen et al.,
  1984); found in Europe and the Pacific Northwest
- **C. kuehneriana**: Confirmed from Europe
- **C. siliginoides**: Used by Mazatec Indians; chemistry requires
  modern re-examination

### Other Genera
- **Gerronema** (2 species): G. fibula and G. solipes reported but
  disputed (Stijve and Kuyper, 1988 found no indole compounds)
- **Hypholoma** (6 species): H. naematoliformis, H. guzmanii, H.
  gigaspora, H. neocaledonica, H. rhombispora reported
- **Galerina**: G. steglichii described as hallucinogenic by Besl
  (1994)
- **Mycena**: M. cyanorhiza reported from Italy and Switzerland
- **Hygrocybe**: H. psittacina reported but disputed
- **Psathyrella**: P. candolleana reported but Stijve and Kuyper
  (1988) found no psilocybin

## Claviceps and Cordyceps (Ascomycetes)

Two genera of Ascomycete fungi contain neurotropic compounds:
- **Claviceps purpurea**: The classic ergot fungus; parasitic on grasses;
  contains ergot alkaloids including ergotamine and lysergic acid
  derivatives; causes ergotism historically
- **C. paspali** and **C. nigricans**: Related ergot species
- **Cordyceps capitata** and **C. ophioglossoides**: Parasitic on
  truffle species; used as sacred fungi in Mexico; contain unknown
  compounds

## Sacred Fungi Without Confirmed Chemistry

Approximately 20 species used by indigenous peoples lack reliable
chemical confirmation. These include Boletus species (B. flammeus, B.
kumaeus, B. manicus, B. nigerrimus, B. nigroviolaceus, B. reayi),
Russula species (R. agglutina, R. kirinea, R. maenadum, R.
nondorbingi, R. pseudomaenadum, R. wahgiensis), Heimiella species,
Dictyophora indusiata, and several gasteromycetes (Vascellum spp.,
Lycoperdon spp.).
