# Small Forest Farm Cool Humid Layout

Mollison presents a detailed model for a 12-16 hectare (30-40 acre) family
forest farm adapted to [[mollison-designers-cool-humid-climate-design-overview]].
This design (referenced as Figure 12.10 in the Designers Manual) integrates
diverse yields including fungi, seed crops, small fruits, fur animals, honey,
timber poles, coppice products, and medicinals into a coherent whole.

## Farm Scale and Philosophy

The 12-16 ha scale is deliberately chosen as sufficient to support a family
while remaining manageable without heavy machinery. Mollison contrasts this
with conventional monoculture farms of similar size, arguing that polyculture
forest farms produce higher total caloric and nutritional output per hectare
while building soil and biodiversity rather than depleting them.

## Zonal Layout

The farm is organised in permaculture zones from the house outward:

**Zone 1 (0-0.5 ha):** Intensive garden, [[mollison-designers-cold-caves-root-cellar-storage]],
nursery, small livestock housing. Kitchen garden with annual and perennial
vegetables, herbs, and salad greens. Composting systems, worm farms, and
sprouting facilities for winter feed.

**Zone 2 (0.5-3 ha):** Dense food forest with fruit trees, berries, nut
hedges, and [[mollison-designers-vine-and-fruit-tree-integration]].
[[mollison-designers-poultry-forage-design-pastured-systems]] runs
integrated with orchard floor management. Beehives placed along garden edges.
Mushroom logs stacked in shade of fruit trees.

**Zone 3 (3-10 ha):** Mixed forest with timber trees, coppice rotations,
nut orchards, and [[mollison-designers-bee-forage-tree-planting]].
[[mollison-designers-free-range-forage-design-principles]] pigs
and poultry forage in forest understory. Small fruit plantings (blueberry,
elderberry, currant) in forest openings. Seed production plots for tree and
shrub species.

**Zone 4 (10-14 ha):** Managed woodland with selective harvesting, wildlife
corridors, and [[mollison-designers-winter-forest-foraging-wildlife-cooperation]].
Forest grazing for cattle, sheep, or goats. Aquaculture ponds in valley
bottoms with [[mollison-designers-dam-construction-and-water-storage]].
Fur animal housing (rabbits, potentially mink or fox in appropriate
contexts).

**Zone 5 (remaining):** Unmanaged reserve forest providing genetic
reservoirs, wildlife habitat, and reference ecosystem.

## Key Yield Streams

**Fungi:** Shiitake, oyster, and wine-cap mushrooms grown on logs and in
wood chip beds throughout Zones 2-4. Stumps from harvested trees inoculated
with edible species. Mollison notes that forest fungi can provide significant
income and nutrition with minimal labour.

**Seed production:** Zones 3-4 produce tree seeds, shrub seeds, and
herbaceous plant seeds for nursery sale or own planting. Seed orchards of
selected provenance planted at wide spacing for easy access.

**Small fruit:** Ribes (currants and gooseberries), blueberries, elderberries,
and bramble fruits planted in the understory and at forest edges. These
thrive in partial shade and produce reliably with minimal care.

**Fur animals:** Rabbits are the primary fur animal, housed in earth-sheltered
hutches and fed on garden waste and forage. Their manure cycles directly into
garden fertility. Mollison also discusses guinea pigs as a protein source
that requires minimal infrastructure.

**Honey:** [[mollison-designers-bee-forage-tree-planting]]
ensure continuous nectar flow from early spring (maple, willow) through late
autumn (ivy, asters). Hive placement throughout Zones 2-3 provides pollination
services and honey surplus.

**Poles and coppice:** Short-rotation coppice of willow, hazel, and ash
produces fencing material, garden stakes, basketry material, and fuelwood on
5-8 year cycles. coppice stools are harvested in winter when sap is down.

**Medicinals:** A wide range of medicinal herbs and tree products (willow
bark, elder flower, echinacea, valerian) are integrated throughout all zones.

## Water Systems

[[mollison-designers-dam-construction-and-water-storage]] are
placed at multiple elevations to store water for irrigation, stock, and
aquaculture. Swale systems on contour slow runoff and recharge groundwater.
