# Cannabis for Post-Surgery Wounds and Wound Care

updated: 2026-04-18

Cannabis has been used topically and internally for wound care throughout history. Modern research is now confirming what traditional healers have long known about the wound-healing properties of cannabinoids and cannabis-derived compounds.

## Cannabis and Post-Surgery Wounds

**CHI Value: 4** (1 study reviewed)

Research on wound healing with cannabis preparations has shown promising results for post-surgical wound care. The anti-inflammatory, analgesic, and antimicrobial properties of cannabis make it relevant to wound management.

## Anti-Inflammatory Action

A 2009 Worcester, Massachusetts meta-analysis reviewed all cannabinoid studies published between 2004-2009. Results revealed that all types of cannabinoids (endogenous, synthetic, and non-cannabinoid plant constituents) effectively reduce pain from inflammation. This anti-inflammatory action is critical to wound healing, as excessive inflammation impairs the healing cascade.

## Beta-Caryophyllene for Deep Wound Healing

Beta-caryophyllene, a dietary cannabinoid found in black pepper, cloves, cinnamon, and many other spices, signals CB2 receptors to initiate:
- Immune strengthening
- Inflammation reduction
- Deep wound healing
- Analgesic effects

### Traditional Bone Setter Evidence
Traditional bone setters in Accra, Ghana at the Aponchie clinic use topical poultices containing spices rich in beta-caryophyllene. Chief among these are:
- Black and white ashanti peppers (Piper guineense)
- Alligator pepper (Aframomum melegueta)

These poultices have been used for generations to accelerate wound and fracture healing.

## Cannabinoid Anti-Inflammatory Pathway

The cannabis plant contains over 200-250 constituents, many with anti-inflammatory properties:
- **CBD (cannabidiol)**: Potent anti-inflammatory, reduces cytokine production
- **THC**: Anti-inflammatory through CB2 receptor activation
- **Beta-caryophyllene**: Selective CB2 agonist, reduces inflammation without psychoactive effects
- **Beta-caryophyllene + alpha-humulene/isocaryophyllene**: Synergistic anticancer activity
- **Terpenes**: Myrcene, limonene, and pinene contribute anti-inflammatory effects

## Skin Conditions and Wound Healing

### Dermatitis/Eczema
**CHI Value: 4** (1 study reviewed). Cannabis topicals show promise for inflammatory skin conditions. The endocannabinoid system in skin regulates immune responses and inflammation.

### Psoriasis
**CHI Value: N/A** (1 study reviewed). Topical cannabis preparations have been studied for psoriasis management.

### Pruritus (Itching)
**CHI Value: 10** (3 studies reviewed, confidence level 3.33). Cannabis preparations effectively reduce itching associated with various conditions including dermatitis, kidney failure, and liver disease.

## Itching (Pruritus)

**CHI Value: 10** (3 studies reviewed)

Cannabis has shown significant anti-pruritic (anti-itch) effects across multiple conditions. The endocannabinoid system in the skin plays a role in regulating itch sensation through CB1 and CB2 receptors.

## Seborrhea

**CHI Value: N/A** (1 study reviewed). Cannabis-derived preparations have been studied for seborrheic dermatitis management.

## Standard Wound Care Treatments
Conventional wound care includes debridement, antiseptics, antibiotics, and dressings. The addition of cannabis-based topicals may complement standard care through pain relief, inflammation reduction, and potential antimicrobial effects.

## Strain-Specific Considerations
Indica strains tend to have higher CBD content, which is particularly relevant for topical wound healing applications. CBD-dominant topicals provide anti-inflammatory benefits without psychoactive effects.

## Mind-Body Perspective
Wounds represent a breach of our boundaries -- physical, emotional, or psychological. The skin is our interface with the world. Wound healing asks: What boundaries have been violated? What needs to be repaired at the deepest level? The healing of wounds parallels the healing of emotional wounds.

# Cannabis for Wound Care

Historically, cannabis has been applied topically in the form of poultices, plasters, salves, tinctures, and oils to treat slow-healing wounds and skin ulcers. Cannabinoids have shown potent abilities to destroy and inhibit the growth of numerous microbes. The involvement of the endocannabinoid system in modulating inflammatory responses to both acute and chronic injuries suggests a prominent and beneficial role in wound care.

## Evidence Base

The Cannabis Health Index (CHI) rates the evidence for cannabis in wound care at a combined CHI value of 32, based on 10 studies reviewed. This represents one of the stronger evidence bases among cannabis therapeutic applications, reflecting the long historical use and growing modern scientific validation.

## Cannabis-Infused Honey

Modern medicine has rediscovered honey's ancient use in wound care. Applied to slow-healing skin wounds such as ulcerations, burns, or infected wounds, honey provides antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, and circulation-improving properties. It stimulates formation of new capillaries and connective tissue and reduces pain.

While no current studies examine the combined and possibly synergistic properties of honey and cannabis in infused form, many of honey's therapeutic properties also exist in cannabis, most notably anti-inflammatory and pain-reducing properties. The Cuban Ministry of Health widely recommends honey as a skin-protective agent and antimicrobial.

## Cannabis-Infused Hempseed Oil

Oil made from hempseed is void of mind-altering cannabinoids and has historically been used for treating dry skin, age-related skin blemishes, and wounds. It is a rich and properly balanced source of omega-3 and omega-6 polyunsaturated fatty acids. Recent studies reveal that ingestion of hempseed oil positively changes fat profiles and significantly reduces symptoms of dryness, itching, and inflammation in atopic dermatitis.

Some patients have combined the skin-healing and anti-inflammatory properties of hempseed oil by infusing it with cannabinoid-rich cannabis to create a topical ointment. The skin is the largest organ of the body and provides an ideal route for gentle delivery of biologically active substances.

## Reported Benefits of Cannabis Topicals

Many cannabis-hemp oil users report a variety of benefits including shrinking of moles and age spots, localized and systemic pain relief, therapeutic relaxation of tight muscles and cramps, deeper and more restful sleep, soothing of inflammation, and a subtle increase in libido, all with a gentle uplift in mood.

As with all natural biologically active substances, effects are usually optimal within a specific therapeutic window, which may vary from person to person. Taking too much may aggravate symptoms. Taking too little may have a sub-optimal effect. Starting slowly and increasing application gradually is recommended.

## Antimicrobial Properties

Cannabinoids have demonstrated potent antimicrobial activity against numerous pathogens including methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA). This antimicrobial property is directly relevant to wound care, where infection is a primary concern.

The ability of cannabinoids to inhibit microbial growth while simultaneously reducing inflammation and pain makes them uniquely suited for wound care applications. These three properties (antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, analgesic) address the three primary challenges in wound management simultaneously.

## Endocannabinoid System in Skin

The skin has its own endocannabinoid system with CB1 and CB2 receptors present in various skin cell types. This local endocannabinoid system modulates cutaneous inflammation, pain, and cell growth, all of which are relevant to wound healing.

Activation of CB2 receptors in skin cells may promote anti-inflammatory effects and support the wound healing process. The local nature of this system means topical application can provide targeted effects without significant systemic absorption.

## Application Methods

Cannabis-infused oils, salves, and balves can be applied directly to wounds, though care should be taken with open wounds to avoid contamination. Cannabis-infused honey represents an especially promising approach given honey's own wound-healing properties.

For surgical wounds, cannabis topicals may be applied around (but not directly on) incision sites during the healing process. See [[cannabis-topical-applications]] for detailed preparation and application guidance.
