# Cannabis for Post-Surgery Wounds and Wound Care

updated: 2026-04-18

Cannabis has been used topically and internally for wound care throughout history. Modern research is now confirming what traditional healers have long known about the wound-healing properties of cannabinoids and cannabis-derived compounds.

## Cannabis and Post-Surgery Wounds

**CHI Value: 4** (1 study reviewed)

Research on wound healing with cannabis preparations has shown promising results for post-surgical wound care. The anti-inflammatory, analgesic, and antimicrobial properties of cannabis make it relevant to wound management.

## Anti-Inflammatory Action

A 2009 Worcester, Massachusetts meta-analysis reviewed all cannabinoid studies published between 2004-2009. Results revealed that all types of cannabinoids (endogenous, synthetic, and non-cannabinoid plant constituents) effectively reduce pain from inflammation. This anti-inflammatory action is critical to wound healing, as excessive inflammation impairs the healing cascade.

## Beta-Caryophyllene for Deep Wound Healing

Beta-caryophyllene, a dietary cannabinoid found in black pepper, cloves, cinnamon, and many other spices, signals CB2 receptors to initiate:
- Immune strengthening
- Inflammation reduction
- Deep wound healing
- Analgesic effects

### Traditional Bone Setter Evidence
Traditional bone setters in Accra, Ghana at the Aponchie clinic use topical poultices containing spices rich in beta-caryophyllene. Chief among these are:
- Black and white ashanti peppers (Piper guineense)
- Alligator pepper (Aframomum melegueta)

These poultices have been used for generations to accelerate wound and fracture healing.

## Cannabinoid Anti-Inflammatory Pathway

The cannabis plant contains over 200-250 constituents, many with anti-inflammatory properties:
- **CBD (cannabidiol)**: Potent anti-inflammatory, reduces cytokine production
- **THC**: Anti-inflammatory through CB2 receptor activation
- **Beta-caryophyllene**: Selective CB2 agonist, reduces inflammation without psychoactive effects
- **Beta-caryophyllene + alpha-humulene/isocaryophyllene**: Synergistic anticancer activity
- **Terpenes**: Myrcene, limonene, and pinene contribute anti-inflammatory effects

## Skin Conditions and Wound Healing

### Dermatitis/Eczema
**CHI Value: 4** (1 study reviewed). Cannabis topicals show promise for inflammatory skin conditions. The endocannabinoid system in skin regulates immune responses and inflammation.

### Psoriasis
**CHI Value: N/A** (1 study reviewed). Topical cannabis preparations have been studied for psoriasis management.

### Pruritus (Itching)
**CHI Value: 10** (3 studies reviewed, confidence level 3.33). Cannabis preparations effectively reduce itching associated with various conditions including dermatitis, kidney failure, and liver disease.

## Itching (Pruritus)

**CHI Value: 10** (3 studies reviewed)

Cannabis has shown significant anti-pruritic (anti-itch) effects across multiple conditions. The endocannabinoid system in the skin plays a role in regulating itch sensation through CB1 and CB2 receptors.

## Seborrhea

**CHI Value: N/A** (1 study reviewed). Cannabis-derived preparations have been studied for seborrheic dermatitis management.

## Standard Wound Care Treatments
Conventional wound care includes debridement, antiseptics, antibiotics, and dressings. The addition of cannabis-based topicals may complement standard care through pain relief, inflammation reduction, and potential antimicrobial effects.

## Strain-Specific Considerations
Indica strains tend to have higher CBD content, which is particularly relevant for topical wound healing applications. CBD-dominant topicals provide anti-inflammatory benefits without psychoactive effects.

## Mind-Body Perspective
Wounds represent a breach of our boundaries -- physical, emotional, or psychological. The skin is our interface with the world. Wound healing asks: What boundaries have been violated? What needs to be repaired at the deepest level? The healing of wounds parallels the healing of emotional wounds.
