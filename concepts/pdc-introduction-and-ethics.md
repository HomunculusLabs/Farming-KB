## What is Permaculture?

The word "Permaculture" combines Permanent + Agriculture, later re-envisioned as Permanent + Culture to emphasize that all cultures rely on their agricultural base, and all cultural activities affect their environment in turn. It is an organized system of design principles, terminology, and methods codified by **Bill Mollison** and **[[sepp-holzer-entity]]**, **Howard Odum**, and **Rudolph Steiner's Biodynamic Farming**. Mollison and Holmgren expanded the understanding beyond agriculture to encompass architecture, business practices, and social norms.

## Core Ethics

Permaculture rests on three core ethics:

1. **Earth Care** -- The health of the Earth must come first. The ultimate goal of any design is to ensure ecological health.
2. **People Care** -- After ensuring Earth's health, take care of personal needs.
3. **Fair Share** -- All things are entitled to a fair share of what the Earth has to offer, even humans. When you have enough, share your abundance with others through the gift economy.

These ethics are often expressed as: the ultimate goal of any Permaculture Design is first to ensure the health of the Earth, then take care of one's personal needs, then generate abundance. You can use abundance to create stores for lean times, and when you have enough stores, share with others.

## Sustainability and the Triple Bottom Line

Permaculture operates within the framework of the **Triple Bottom Line**, which identifies three factors necessary for any endeavor to be sustainable:

- **Financial** -- the economic viability
- **Social** -- the positive impact on people
- **Environmental** -- the ecological health

For an endeavor to be sustainable, it must be positive in all three. The manager must balance these factors, ensuring all three are roughly equally positive.

**Bruntland Definition**: Sustainability is meeting the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.

## Sustainable Land Goals

A Permaculture Design should produce a sustainable, ecologically sound plan for land use. The three ethics -- earth care, people care, and fair share (or return of surplus) -- provide the moral foundation. Every design decision should be evaluated against these three ethics.

## Common Challenges and Solutions

Several recurring challenges tend to arise in work related to this
topic. These include variability in environmental conditions, the
complexity of multi-variable interactions, and the difficulty of
predicting outcomes with certainty in dynamic systems. Anticipating
these challenges enables more proactive and effective management.

and adaptive capacity helps buffer against unpredictable events and
conditions. This approach recognizes that some degree of uncertainty is
inherent in working with natural systems and plans accordingly rather
than assuming perfect predictability or control over outcomes.

Documentation and record-keeping support continuous improvement by
creating a reference base of observations, interventions, and results.
This accumulated knowledge enables progressively better decision-making
and helps identify patterns that might otherwise be overlooked in the
complexity of day-to-day management and observation activities.
