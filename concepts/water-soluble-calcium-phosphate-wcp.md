## Water-Soluble Calcium Phosphate (WS-CaPo)

Water-Soluble Calcium Phosphate is a KNF input made by extracting calcium and phosphorus from animal bones using brown rice vinegar (BRV). It is the primary input for the **changeover period** — the critical transition from vegetative to reproductive growth. Calcium phosphate provides the phosphoric acid compounds that plants need to shift their metabolism from leaf production to flower/fruit production.

## Why Calcium Phosphate?

Phosphorus (P) is essential for:
- **Energy transfer** (ATP) — all cellular processes require phosphate
- **Root development** — especially during early growth and flowering
- **Flower and seed formation** — phosphorus demand peaks during reproductive stage
- **Genetic material** (DNA/RNA) — phosphate backbone
- **Membrane structure** — phospholipids in cell membranes

Combined with calcium (see [[water-soluble-calcium-wca]]), this input provides both the structural strength and energy transfer needed for the changeover transition.

## WS-CaPo Recipe

### Materials
- Animal bones (fish bones, chicken bones, beef bones, or any animal bones)
- [[brown-rice-vinegar-brv]] — natural acetic acid extraction solvent
- Glass jar with lid

### Bone Selection
- **Fish bones** — best option, especially from FAA production (see [[fish-amino-acid-faa]])
- **Chicken bones** — readily available, high calcium content
- **Beef/pork bones** — slower extraction but higher mineral content
- Avoid cooked bones with heavy seasoning (salt interferes with extraction)

### Ratios
- Bones to BRV: Fill jar 1/3 with bones, cover with BRV
- BRV should fully submerge bones (approximately 2:1 BRV to bone volume)

### Process
1. Collect bones from kitchen waste or fish processing
2. **Optionally char bones** (roast in fire until blackened but not ash) — charring increases porosity and surface area for faster extraction
3. Clean bones — remove meat, fat, and connective tissue (fat slows extraction)
4. Break or cut bones into small pieces (1-2 inches)
5. Fill glass jar 1/3 full with bone pieces
6. Pour BRV over bones until fully submerged
7. Bubbles will form as acetic acid reacts with calcium phosphate in bone matrix
8. Store in cool dark place
9. Ferment 7-10 days (charred bones extract faster, 5-7 days)
10. Strain liquid through cheesecloth — this is WS-CaPo
11. Bone residue can be added to [[fermented-mixed-compost-fmc]] or compost

### Signs of Quality
- Yellowish to amber liquid
- Slightly acidic smell
- No putrid odor (if meat/fat remained, may smell off — strain carefully)
- Bones appear softened and partially dissolved

### Storage
- Glass bottle, cool dark place
- Lasts 6-12 months
- Acidic pH preserves the solution

## Alternative: Eggshell + Fish Bone Method

From Cho's book — combining sources:
- Mix crushed eggshells and fish bones in the same jar with BRV
- Provides both pure calcium (from shells) and calcium phosphate (from bones)
- Creates a broader spectrum calcium input

## Application Rates

| Use Case | Dilution | Notes |
|----------|----------|-------|
| Changeover period | 1:500-1000 | Primary use — critical stage |
| Early flowering | 1:1000 | Support flower initiation |
| Root development | 1:1000 | Soil drench for seedlings |
| Stress recovery | 1:500 | Plants recovering from transplant or damage |
| Soil treatment (SOS) | 300 ml per acre | Part of full soil treatment solution |

Apply as foliar spray or soil drench. Foliar is faster for acute deficiency; soil drench provides longer-term availability.

## WS-CaPo in the Nutritive Cycle

According to [[nutritive-cycle-theory]], WS-CaPo is the star of the changeover period:

- **Vegetative stage**: Generally not needed (plants use nitrogen and carbohydrates)
- **Changeover period**: WS-CaPo at 1:500-1000 — THE key input for this stage
- **Reproductive stage**: Can continue at 1:1000 for first few weeks of flowering
- **Late reproductive**: Reduce or stop — plant has shifted metabolism

The changeover period is when the plant's phosphorus demand spikes as it prepares for reproductive growth. WS-CaPo provides immediately bioavailable phosphorus (unlike rock phosphate which takes months to break down).

## Combining with Other Inputs

WS-CaPo works synergistically with:
- [[brown-rice-vinegar-brv]] — the extraction solvent, also a sour nutrient
- WS-PA (sesame stem charcoal + water) at 1:30 — additional phosphorus source
- [[water-soluble-calcium-wca]] — complementary calcium input
- [[fermented-plant-extract-fpe]] — sour FFJ for additional changeover support
- [[indigenous-microorganisms-imo]] — IMO helps solubilize phosphorus in soil

## The Changeover Period Explained

The changeover period (morning sickness) is the 1-3 week window when plants transition from vegetative to reproductive growth. During this time:
- Vegetative growth slows dramatically
- The plant reconfigures its metabolism from nitrogen assimilation to phosphorus/carbon storage
- Older leaves may yellow as the plant mobilizes stored nutrients
- Flower buds begin forming but may appear stunted initially

WS-CaPo provides the phosphoric acid compounds that fuel this metabolic shift. Without adequate phosphorus during changeover:
- Flower initiation is delayed or incomplete
- Fruit set is poor
- Final yield and quality suffer significantly
- Plants may revert to vegetative growth (especially with excess nitrogen)

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Cause | Solution |
|---------|-------|----------|
| Slow extraction | Bones too large or not charred | Break bones smaller, char before extracting |
| Putrid smell | Meat/fat left on bones | Clean bones thoroughly, start over |
| Weak results | Dilution too high | Increase to 1:500 |
| No visible changeover effect | Applied too late | Apply when first flower buds appear |
| Precipitate in bottle | Calcium phosphate settling | Shake before use |
