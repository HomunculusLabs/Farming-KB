## Fukuoka Green Philosophy

## Overview

Masanobu Fukuoka"s "green philosophy" is not merely an agricultural method but a
comprehensive worldview that challenges the foundations of Western scientific
thought and industrial civilization. "The Natural Way of Farming: The Theory and
Practice of Green Philosophy" presents nature as a holistic, indivisible biosystem
that cannot be understood through reductionist analysis. His philosophy draws
heavily on Buddhist concepts, particularly "Mu" (nothingness), and offers a
radical alternative to both conventional [[coleman-deep-organic-farming-philosophy]].

### The Error of Cause and Effect

When man sees a brown spot on a leaf, he calls it abnormal and finds an unusual
bacteria, then calls the plant diseased. His confident solution is to kill the
pathogen with pesticides. But without a grasp of the true cause, his solution
cannot be real. Behind each cause lies another cause. What we view as a cause can
also be seen as the result of another cause. The rice plant itself may see blast
disease as a protective mechanism that halts excessive growth and restores
balance between the surface and underground portions of the plant.
