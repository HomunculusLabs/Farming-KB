## The Village of Benson

Benson sits on the north side of the River Thames, known locally as the Isis,
to whose flow it contributes a chalk stream or brook that bubbles through the
village along a stone-lined course. The brook water is clear as crystal and
flashes with trout and stickleback. It teems with crayfish, insect larvae,
and a profusion of other invertebrates, and here and there flourish patches
of the pepperiest watercress available to humanity. And invisibly, the water
carries fungal spores of incomparable beauty.

In the 1960s, Benson experienced a population explosion and new homes
gobbled up the surrounding wheat fields and dairy farms. Forty years later,
the community reverted to a sleepier existence. The orchard belonging to
Mr. Bloomfield, the village milkman, stood adjacent to the neighborhood where
both Ingold and, decades later, Money would spend portions of their lives.

## Ingold the Neighbor

In his teens, the author noticed an elderly man who had moved into the
cul-de-sac of twelve homes that formed the neighborhood. The author's mother
identified him as a retired professor from Birkbeck College in London, though
she did not know his area of expertise. The professor and his wife Nora were
in their seventies at the time. While the young Money was engaged in the
sundry responsibilities of a teenager, the professor walked the footpaths
around the village deep in contemplation and otherwise proceeded with his
retirement.

At Bristol University, studying biology under Mike Madelin — a mycologist
who had appeared in a British television documentary called *The Rotten
World About Us* — Money was converted to mycology. One morning in Bristol,
Madelin showed him a book called *The Nature of Toadstools*, written by
C. T. Ingold. At the end of the preface, Money read the words: "Benson,
Oxfordshire, 1978, C.T.I." His face flushed. The old man, the retiree,
his neighbor, was Ingold. He had decided to become a mycologist, and a year
later discovered that he had grown up next to England"s best-known fungal
biologist.
