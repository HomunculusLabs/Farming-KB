# Stinkhorn Fungi and Insect Spore Dispersal

Stinkhorn fungi are among the most conspicuous and bizarre members of the
[[basidiomycota-classification-major-clades]], defined by spores that form inside the fruiting body
rather than on exposed gills. Other gasteromycetes include puffballs,
earth-stars, bird's nest fungi, and *Sphaerobolus*, which shoots black
spore balls from a trampoline-like fruiting body. The gasteromycetes are
regarded as a ragbag of species rather than a natural group, having evolved
independently from mushroom-forming ancestors.

## Cultural History

Stinkhorns have been impossible to ignore throughout history. They appear in
Pliny the Elder's thirty-seven-volume *Natural History* from the first century
AD. John Gerard pictured them in his seventeenth-century herbal with the
description "Fungus virilis penis arecti forma, which wee English call Pricke
Mushrum." Charles Darwin's daughter Etty despised stinkhorns so intensely
that she mounted a personal campaign with gloves and a pointed stick,
burning specimens in secret to protect the moral purity of her servants.
