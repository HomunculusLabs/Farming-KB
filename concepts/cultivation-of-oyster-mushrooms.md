# Cultivation of Oyster Mushrooms

Oyster mushrooms (genus Pleurotus) are among the most widely cultivated
gourmet mushrooms worldwide, and for good reason. They are fast-growing,
aggressive colonizers that fruit prolifically on a wide range of
substrates. Their biological efficiency routinely exceeds 100%, and they
are forgiving enough for beginners while rewarding enough for commercial
growers. The genus includes dozens of species with diverse flavors,
textures, and growing requirements.

## Major Cultivated Species

**Pleurotus ostreatus** (pearl oyster): The standard oyster mushroom.
Gray to white caps, mild flavor, grows on straw and hardwood. Fruits at
12-20 C. The most common commercial variety and an excellent beginner
species.

**Pleurotus pulmonarius** (phoenix oyster / Indian oyster): Similar to
P. ostreatus but prefers warmer temperatures (18-28 C). Thinner flesh
but faster colonization and fruiting. Often preferred [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi-in-tropical-forest-restoration]] and
subtropical climates.

**Pleurotus eryngii** (king oyster): The largest oyster species, with
thick, meaty stems and small caps. Requires longer spawn run and lower
fruiting temperatures (10-18 C). Commands premium prices due to its
excellent texture and shelf life.

**Pleurotus citrinopileatus** (golden oyster): Bright yellow caps with
a delicate, slightly nutty flavor. Very fast grower but with a short
shelf life. Fruits at 18-26 C. Highly attractive at farmers markets.

**Pleurotus djamor** (pink oyster): Vibrant pink to salmon-colored caps.
Tropical species requiring warm temperatures (22-30 C). Intense, savory
flavor that some describe as bacon-like. Extremely fast colonizer.

## [[substrate-preparation]]

Oyster mushrooms are exceptionally versatile in their [[substrate-ph-management]]
preferences. They will grow on straw, sawdust, paper, coffee grounds,
[[staycare-fungal-agricultural-waste-conversion]], and many other cellulose-rich materials.

The simplest and most reliable method is straw-based cultivation. Wheat
or oat straw is chopped to 2-5 cm lengths, soaked in hot [[water-management]] (65-80 C)
for 1-2 hours, drained, and packed into autoclave bags or poly tubes.
The pasteurization process kills most competitors while preserving
beneficial bacteria. Supplementation with 10-20% wheat bran or soy hulls
dramatically increases yields but requires [[sterilization]] rather than
pasteurization.

For indoor commercial cultivation, supplemented hardwood sawdust blocks
are the standard. A typical formula is 80% hardwood sawdust, 18% wheat
bran, and 2% gypsum, hydrated to 63-65% moisture. Blocks of 2.5-5 kg
are sterilized at 121 C for 2 hours and inoculated with grain spawn at
5-10% [[allegro-star-of-the-morning-venus-and-the-sacred-mushroom]] dry substrate weight.

## Spawn Run

Oyster mycelium colonizes substrate rapidly. Straw logs typically reach
full colonization in 10-14 days at 22-25 C. Sawdust blocks may take
18-25 days. Higher incubation temperatures (up to 28 C) accelerate
colonization but increase the risk of thermophilic bacterial contamination
[[allegro-death-and-resurrection-in-the-mushroom-cult]] center of dense blocks.

During spawn run, CO2 levels should be maintained above 2,000 ppm to
discourage premature pinning. Blocks or logs are kept in sealed bags or
in a high-CO2 environment until colonization is complete, indicated by
a fully white surface with the substrate structure no longer visible.

## Fruiting

Oyster mushrooms respond strongly to fresh air exchange [[what-a-plant-knows-plant-vision-and-light-perception]] as
fruiting triggers. When the block is fully colonized, the bag is opened
or cut to expose the substrate surface. A sudden influx of fresh air and
light initiates pinning within 3-7 days.

Maintaining 85-95% relative humidity is critical during pinning [[allegro-dead-sea-scrolls-and-early-christian-sects]]
fruit development. Pins are extremely sensitive to drying and will abort
if humidity drops below 80%. Once mushrooms reach 2-3 cm in size, they
are more resilient and can tolerate slightly lower humidity.

Oyster mushrooms need generous fresh air exchange during fruiting. In
stagnant air, they develop long, stringy stems and small caps. Adequate
FAE produces well-formed, shelf-like clusters with thick stems and broad
caps. For many growers, the challenge is balancing high humidity with
sufficient air exchange, as these goals can conflict — humidifiers add
moisture while fresh air introduces drier ambient air.

## Harvesting

Oyster mushrooms should be harvested just before or as the cap margin
begins to curl upward and spore release begins. For most species, this
is when the cap is 5-15 cm in diameter depending on the variety.
Harvesting at the right stage ensures the best texture and flavor, and
prevents the heavy spore load that can cause respiratory irritation and
create a mess in the growing area.

Mushrooms are twisted or cut from the substrate in clusters. Removing
the entire cluster is generally preferred over individual mushroom
harvest, as it reduces the risk of leaving stipe material that can rot
and attract contaminants. A clean knife cut at the base of the cluster
is the standard commercial method.

## Yield Expectations

On unsupplemented straw, oyster mushrooms typically yield 50-75%
biological efficiency. With 10-20% bran supplementation on sawdust,
yields of 100-200% BE are common across 2-3 flushes. The first flush
produces 60-70% of total yield. Subsequent flushes are progressively
smaller. Most commercial growers cycle blocks after 2-3 flushes to
maximize throughput and minimize contamination risk.

## Pest and Disease Management

Oyster mushroom crops are susceptible to several pests. Fungus gnats and
sciarid flies lay eggs in moist substrate, and their larvae feed on
mycelium, reducing yield and creating entry points for bacterial
contamination. Sticky traps and careful moisture management are the
primary defenses. Slugs and snails can devour developing fruiting bodies
overnight in outdoor or poorly sealed growing areas. Biological
controls such as Hypoaspis miles predatory mites can be introduced
proactively in commercial operations.
