## Climatic Zones and Classification Systems

Climate is the primary determinant of species selection and technology choice in [[permaculture-plant-selection-guide]].

## Hardiness Zones

Many plant compendia attach "zones of hardiness" to plant listings, commonly used in the USA (as given in *Hortus Third*). These provide practical guidance on minimum winter temperatures that specific species can tolerate, though they do not account for summer heat, humidity, rainfall distribution, or soil factors.

## Measurement Limitations

Designers should be aware of several problems with climatic data:

1. **Instrument limitations**: Accurate measurement instruments are expensive and often specific to a narrow range of effects
2. **Incomplete spectral coverage**: Averages for precipitation and radiation often refer only to one part of the total spectrum; long-term records of fog precipitation, dew, long-wave radiation, ultraviolet incidence, or gaseous atmospheric composition are rare
3. **Dynamic interaction**: Rain, sun, and wind interact continuously and dynamically, so averages mean little to a plant or animal experiencing normally changeable effects across wide interactive ranges

## The Designer's Obligation

Because local climate is so site-specific and so critical to design success, it is **obligatory for any designer** to:
- Study regional long-term human and agricultural adaptations
- Observe and record local conditions over time
- Avoid introducing inappropriate temperate techniques to tropical and arid lands
- Respect and learn from native management practices
- Design for extremes, not averages
- Incorporate fail-safe diversity in all systems
