# Crops Grow of Their Own Accord: Independence from Fertilizer

## Overview

The second of Fukuoka's four [[four-principles-of-natural-farming]] farming is "no fertilizer." At the heart of this principle lies a radical reframing of the relationship between plants and soil: crops do not need to be raised because they grow of their own accord. This is not a semantic distinction but a fundamentally different understanding of plant nutrition, soil fertility, and the role of the farmer. Fukuoka's decades of experimentation demonstrated that trees and crops can produce yields equal to or better than fertilized cultivation when allowed to function within the natural soil ecosystem.

## The Scientific Fallacy of Cause and Effect

The conventional understanding of fertilizer is built on a causal chain that appears logical but is fundamentally flawed:

1. Scientists analyze a plant and identify its nutrient components (nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium)
2. They observe that these nutrients are absorbed from the soil by roots
3. They conclude that nutrient absorption causes plant growth
4. They apply these nutrients as fertilizer and observe growth
5. They conclude that fertilizer is necessary and effective

Fukuoka challenges this chain at its foundation. The error lies in the initial assumption that plants must "be grown." If we instead take as our starting point the view that a tree grows of its own accord, the uptake of nutrients by the tree's roots is no longer a cause but, in the eyes of nature, just a small effect.

The buds on a tree are made for budding and so this is what they do; the roots, with their powers of elongation, spread and extend throughout the earth. A tree has a shape perfectly adapted to the natural environment. With this, it guards the providence of nature and obeys nature's laws, growing neither too fast nor too slow, but in total harmony with the great cycles of nature.

## The Forest as Evidence

Fukuoka's most compelling evidence for the no-fertilizer principle comes from [[fukuoka-direct-observation-method]] of [[dighton-fungi-acidifying-pollutants-forest-ecosystems]]:

### Timber Stand Productivity
Reforested cedars in a favorable area generally grow about forty tons per quarter-acre over a period of twenty years, producing some two tons of new growth each year without fertilizer. Including small branches, leaves, and roots, annual production is probably closer to four tons. This would translate into two to four tons of fruit per year in an orchard without fertilizers, roughly equal to standard production levels.

### Nutrient Supply Mystery
Even though the entire surface portion of the tree, including branches, leaves, and trunk, is carried away at harvest, the soil continues to supply sufficient nutrients for decades of growth. Fertilizer components for this production volume are supplied each year to the growing trees through mechanisms that science cannot fully explain.

### Black Wattle Succession
When black wattle is planted in depleted soil on a mountainside, it is succeeded a number of years later by giant cedars many times its size. This demonstrates the great productive powers of the soil even after apparent exhaustion. The microbes present on the roots of the black wattle help subsequent cedar and cypress trees thrive.

### Natural Soil Building
When the forest is left to itself, the action of wind and snow over the years weathers rock. A layer of humus forms and deepens with the fall of leaves each year. Microorganisms multiply in the soil, turning it a rich black. The soil aggregates and softens, increasing water retention. There is no need for human intervention.

## The Destruction Caused by Conventional Orchard Establishment

Fukuoka contrasts the natural productivity of forests with the destructive process of conventional orchard establishment:

1. The farmer fells the trees in the forest and carries them off as logs, taking the branches and leaves as well
2. He digs deep into the earth, pulling up the roots of trees and grasses, which he burns
3. He turns the soil over and over again to loosen it, destroying the physical structure
4. He drives out air and the humus essential to microorganisms, reducing it to yellow mineral matter barren of life
5. He plants fruit saplings in the now lifeless soil, adds fertilizer, and attempts to grow [[comparison-grafting-vs-budding-fruit-trees]] entirely through human forces

The poor farmer has taken the long way around. He has destroyed the living soil ecosystem and then tried to replace it with chemical inputs. Fukuoka asks: if nature is not dead, if it lives and grows, then why not direct these vast hidden forces to the growth of fruit trees?

## Why No-Fertilizer Tests Are Rare

Fukuoka identifies a critical bias in [[fukuoka-inductive-deductive-intuitive-reasoning-agricultural-research]]: scientists hardly ever run experiments on no-fertilizer cultivation. The reason is all too clear. Scientists work from the basic premise that crops are to be grown with fertilizer. The very idea of no-fertilizer cultivation is dismissed as "idiotic and dangerous."

The few no-fertilizer tests that have been conducted suffer from fundamental flaws:

### Artificial Enclosures
Tests are conducted in small concrete enclosures or earthen pots where the soil is dead and the roots are boxed in. Growth under such conditions is highly unnatural. It is unreasonable to claim that because plants grown without fertilizer in an artificial enclosure grow poorly, they cannot be grown without fertilizers under natural conditions.

### Misleading Results
Scientists claim that a tree grows only about half as much without fertilizer, and yields are terrible, perhaps one-third of fertilized production. However, these conditions have little in common with true [[cho-loess-powder-in-natural-farming]]. No-fertilizer natural farming means cultivation under totally natural conditions, which scientists consider impossible to test.

### The Real Standard
The standard on which fertilizer experiments should be based is no-fertilizer tests, but three-element tests using nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium are the standard actually used. The entire edifice of fertilizer science is built on a biased foundation.

## The Hidden Costs of Chemical Fertilizer

Fukuoka documents numerous destructive effects of chemical fertilizer that are rarely acknowledged:

### Acidification
More than seventy percent of the "big three" fertilizers (ammonium sulfate, super-phosphate, potassium sulfate) is concentrated [[fukuoka-textdoc-fertilizer-soil-destruction-sulfuric-acid]]. Some 1.8 million tons of sulfuric acid are dumped onto Japanese farmlands annually in the form of fertilizer. This acidic fertilizer suppresses and kills soil microorganisms.
