# Tomato Growing - Organic Methods

Tomatoes are a very versatile vegetable, usually considered the most
important greenhouse crop. They come in bush and upright types, with
forms including elongated plum, large beefsteak, cherry, and yellow
varieties. Tomatoes can be grown outside in most climates, though
in cold areas early varieties should be chosen as late-maturing
plants may fail to ripen. Organic methods produce excellent results
with careful attention to feeding and [[cannabis-pest-management-guide]].

## Choosing Varieties

Bush types tend to produce smaller fruit. Red Alert is one of the
best bush varieties, giving very early crops of small, tasty fruits.
Incas is an excellent disease-resistant bush variety bearing plum
tomatoes. Of the upright varieties, Stupice is tried and tested in
cooler climates, while Golden Sunrise, a yellow variety, is
recommended for flavor in warmer climates. For greenhouse growing,
Herald and Shirley are early with good flavor, while cherry-fruited
Gardener's Delight and Sweet Million have superb flavor.

## Soil and Site

Tomatoes need sun and a well-manured soil that retains moisture and
plant nutrients. They prefer a pH of about 6.0. Incorporate plenty
of well-rotted compost or manure before planting. If you are using
a [[hamilton-deep-bed-system]];
plant them 20in (50cm) apart in deep beds for [[mollison-designers-market-garden-intensive-production-design]].

## Sowing and Planting

Seeds for outdoor varieties must be raised inside a greenhouse or
on a windowsill. Sow seeds in trays, spacing them about 1in (2.5cm)
apart, in mid-spring. Transfer seedlings into 3in (9cm) pots at the
seed-leaf stage. Harden off in the cold frame in late spring and
plant at the beginning of summer. Set the plants 2ft (60cm) square
and plant climbing varieties against poles. See
[[hamilton-cold-frames-and-hardening-off]] for detailed guidance.

## Greenhouse Cultivation

For [[coleman-cold-greenhouse-double-layer-protection]] planting, sow in late winter at 70 degrees F
(21 degrees C). Transplant to individual pots when seedlings are big
enough. Grow on at 50 to 55 degrees F (10-12 degrees C), spacing
progressively to produce short, bushy plants. Plant in the border
soil or growing bags 12in (30cm) apart in mid-spring. If planting in
the border, flood it with water about two weeks before planting to
flush out excess mineral salts, then dig in compost or manure.

## Training and Maintenance

Tie upright types to their poles regularly using soft string. Pinch
off the tops when they have made three clusters of fruit, or four in
warm climates. Remove the sideshoots arising from each leaf joint
when they are still small. Bush varieties need no staking or
side-shooting, but cover the soil underneath with straw or bark to
raise the fruit off the ground. Feed with liquid kelp or
animal-manure fertilizer at two-week intervals from midsummer to
early fall.

## Watering and Feeding

Water plants in initially, then leave them to search for water,
encouraging extensive root growth. Water again after about a week and
give each plant about 2pts (1 liter) per day. Smaller cherry tomatoes
need only about 3pts (1.5 liters) per week to retain sweetness. When
plants flower, spray with water daily to provide humid conditions
favoring good pollination. After about eight weeks, start feeding
with liquid kelp or animal-manure fertilizer every time you water.
