---# Hawaiian Neurotropic Mycoflora

### Copelandia Species (5 Species)

Hawaii supports an extraordinary five species of Copelandia, making it one
of the richest island locations for this genus worldwide:

- **C. anomala** (Murrill) Singer -- Reported by Pollock (1976), Stijve (1992),
  Merlin and Allen (1993), and Allen (1998)
- **C. bispora** (Malencon and Bertault) Singer and R.A. Weeks -- Documented
  by Merlin and Allen (1993) and Allen (1998)
- **C. cambodginiensis** (Olah and R. Heim) Singer and R.A. Weeks -- One of
  the most commonly encountered species in Hawaii; reported by Olah (1968,
  1970), Weeks et al. (1979), Merlin and Allen (1993), and Allen (1998)
- **C. cyanescens** (Berk. and Broome) Singer -- The most widespread species,
  first reported from Hawaii by Heim et al. (1966a, 1967); subsequently
  documented by Pollock (1976), Stamets (1978), Stijve (1992, 1995), Merlin
  and Allen (1993), and Allen (1998)
- **C. tropicalis** (Olah) Singer and R.A. Weeks -- Reported by Olah (1968,
  1970), Stamets (1978, 1996), Weeks et al. (1979), Merlin and Allen (1993),
  and Allen (1998)

### Panaeolus Species (3 Species)

Three species of Panaeolus have been documented from Hawaii:

- **P. subbalteatus** -- Reported by Beug and Bigwood (1982), Stijve and
  Kuyper (1985), Gartz (1989b), Merlin and Allen (1993), and Allen (1998)
- **P. sphinctrinus** -- Reported by Allen (1998)

### Altitudinal Range

Hawaiian neurotropic fungi exhibit a notable altitudinal range. Copelandia
cyanescens has been recorded growing at 3,000 m altitude on Maui (Merlin and
Allen, 1993), demonstrating the species' ability to colonize montane habitats
in tropical island environments.
