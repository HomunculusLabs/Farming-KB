## Electron Microscope Survival Experiments

Nick Read published a fascinating study in which ascospores were
subjected to the brutal vacuum and temperature conditions within
an electron microscope column. In these experiments, the spores
were dried in a vacuum and cooled to temperatures as low as
minus 180 degrees Celsius. They were then irradiated with an
intense beam of electrons used to form images in the transmission
electron microscope. After enduring this sequence of extreme
treatments, the ascospores were removed from the instrument and
placed on nutrient agar, where they germinated and grew into
healthy colonies.

The significance of these findings cannot be overstated. The
interior of an electron microscope represents one of the most
hostile environments that any biological specimen can encounter.
The combination of extreme desiccation, deep freezing, and
 bombardment by high-energy electrons would obliterate animal
cells, plant tissues, and even most bacterial spores. The fact
that fungal ascospores survived all of these insults and
retained their viability speaks to the extraordinary robustness
of their [[enzymatic-degradation-in-mycoremediation]].

This multilayered construction is analogous to the design of
a pressure vessel or a deep-sea submersible hull, where
multiple independent barriers provide redundancy against
failure. When one layer is compromised by mechanical stress,
chemical attack, or radiation damage, the remaining layers
continue to protect the spore's cytoplasm and genetic material.
The evolutionary pressure driving this investment in structural
fortification is clear: ascospores must survive extended periods
outside the parent fungus, often in environments that fluctuate
wildly in temperature, humidity, and exposure to damaging
radiation.

## The Fatal Exception: Alcohol Dehydration

Despite their near-indestructibility, Read discovered one
treatment that killed every ascospore without exception:
dehydration in alcohol. When spores were dried in the presence
of ethanol rather than in a plain vacuum, none of them
germinated afterward. This finding is significant because it
identifies a specific vulnerability in what is otherwise an
almost invulnerable structure.
