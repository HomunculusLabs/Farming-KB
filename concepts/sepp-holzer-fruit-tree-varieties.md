## Sepp Holzer Fruit Tree Varieties for Mountain Climates

Updated: 2026-04-18

Source: [[sepp-holzer]], *Sepp Holzer's Permaculture* (2010)

Sepp Holzer cultivates a large variety of [[holzer-fruit-growing-marginal-climates]] ends at 1,000m in the Lungau region.

## Altitude Adaptation Principle

Supposedly demanding varieties that experts say only thrive in warm climates and at low altitudes can adjust to high altitudes and give satisfactory yields. For example, Golden Delicious thrives at 1,400m and produces large fruit that stores well. Key requirements:
- Shelter from wind
- Climatically advantageous locations (microclimates)
- Stones or bodies of water to reflect heat
- Structures like hollows or windbreaks for weather protection
- No chemical fertilisers (these put trees out of balance and reduce frost resistance)
- Fertilised trees grow faster but do not lignify as well -- less frost resistant

## High-Altitude Ripening Pattern

Ripening times shift dramatically with altitude. At the Krameterhof:
- 1,000m: 'White Transparent' and 'Stark's Earliest' ripen mid-August
- 1,100m: 'White Transparent' floury by end of August, unusable for juice/cider
- 1,500m: 'White Transparent' still excellent juice and cider apple at end of September
- Higher altitude fruit is firmer, juicier, stores longer, and has improved flavour
- Harsh cold nights at altitude improve flavour and aroma
- Lower yields at altitude are compensated by higher quality and premium prices

## Recommended Old Pear Varieties

Pears should not be harvested late -- they quickly become overripe. Notable varieties:
- **Beurre Alexandre Lucas**: Undemanding, frost hardy, high altitude, espalier tree, stores until Dec
- **Coloree de Juillet**: Undemanding, high altitude, August, cannot be stored
- **Gellerts Butterbirne**: Undemanding, sunny position, October, stores until Jan
- **Mostberger**: Very undemanding, harsh conditions, late/long flowering, stores until Feb

## Stone Fruit

Holzer also cultivates cherries, plums, and other stone fruit:
- 'Kassin's Friihe' cherries ripen at 1,000m by end of June, but only by September at 1,500m
- Stone fruits benefit particularly from microclimates at high altitude

## Browse Protection

To protect young fruit trees from deer and other browsers, Holzer uses a salve made from:
- [[fukuoka-creating-natural-farm-site-selection-establishment]]: south-facing slopes with stone walls behind the trees to absorb and radiate heat. Apricots are particularly successful when planted against stone walls that create a warm microclimate. He recommends planting multiple varieties for cross-pollination and staggered harvest.
