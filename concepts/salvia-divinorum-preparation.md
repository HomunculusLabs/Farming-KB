# Salvia Divinorum Preparation

## Overview

Salvia divinorum is a psychoactive plant in the mint family (Lamiaceae), native to the Sierra Mazateca region of Oaxaca, Mexico. Its primary active compound, salvinorin A, is a neoclerodane diterpene — not an alkaloid — making it pharmacologically unique among classical psychedelics. Salvinorin A is a potent kappa opioid receptor agonist, producing intense but short-lived altered states of consciousness.

Salvia divinorum is one of the most powerful naturally occurring psychedelics known, with effective doses measured in micrograms. Yet its effects profile is radically different from other psychedelics: rather than the serotonin-receptor-mediated colorful visual patterns of psilocybin or DMT, salvinorin A produces a dissociative, reality-fragmenting experience that users often describe as "leaving this reality entirely."

## The Plant

- **Family**: Lamiaceae (mint family)
- **Appearance**: Large green leaves (up to 30 cm), hollow square stems, white flowers with purple calyces
- **Height**: 0.5–1.5 meters
- **Reproduction**: Primarily through vegetative cloning — rarely produces viable seeds in cultivation
- **Habitat**: Cloud forests of the Sierra Mazateca, 300–1500 m elevation, in shady, moist conditions
- **Cultivation**: Relatively easy to grow from cuttings; prefers humid, warm, shaded environments

## Mazatec Traditional Use

The Mazatec people have used S. divinorum for centuries under the name "ska María Pastora" (leaves of Mary the Shepherdess) or "hojas de la Pastora." Traditional use patterns:

- Consumed in quiet, dark, meditative settings
- Used for divination, healing, and spiritual exploration
- Typically taken as a quid of fresh leaves (sublingual absorption)
- Low doses are used for mild, contemplative states
- Higher doses for visionary experiences
- Always used with respect and intentionality
- The experience is considered a communion with the Virgin Mary or divine feminine principle

## Preparation Methods

### Method 1: Fresh Leaf Quid (Traditional)

This is the traditional Mazatec method and produces the most manageable experience:

1. Harvest 6–32 large, fresh, healthy leaves
2. Roll or ball the leaves into a compact wad (quid)
3. Place the quid in the mouth, between the gums and cheek
4. Chew slowly and gently for 15–30 minutes, keeping the juice in the mouth
5. Swallow sparingly — the active compound is absorbed through the oral mucosa, not the stomach
6. The bitter juice can be held under the tongue for maximum absorption
7. Effects begin within 10–15 minutes and last 30–60 minutes

This method requires fresh leaves and patience but provides a gentler, more controllable experience than smoking.

### Method 2: Salvia Tincture (Sublingual)

A tincture concentrates salvinorin A into an alcohol-based extract for sublingual use:

1. Crush or blend fresh leaves
2. Soak in high-proof ethanol (190 proof) for several days to weeks
3. Strain and, optionally, reduce the tincture by gentle evaporation
4. Place drops under the tongue and hold for 5–10 minutes before swallowing
5. Start with a very small amount — 1–2 drops — and increase gradually

This method offers more precise dosing than the quid method.

### Method 3: Dried Leaf (Smoking)

Smoking dried salvia leaf is the most common Western method, though it is far more intense than traditional use:

1. Dry leaves completely — they should be brittle
2. Crush to a medium consistency (not too fine)
3. Smoke in a pipe or water pipe
4. Use a torch lighter for best results — salvinorin A vaporizes at approximately 240°C (464°F)
5. Take a large, deep hit and hold for 20–30 seconds
6. Effects onset: within 30 seconds
7. Duration: 5–15 minutes

Dried leaf is relatively mild — approximately 2–4 mg salvinorin A per gram of leaf. Effective smoked dose: 0.25–0.75 g.

### Method 4: Enhanced / Fortified Leaf

Enhanced leaf is dried salvia leaf that has been infused with additional salvinorin A extract:

- **5x**: 5 times the potency of plain leaf (~10–20 mg/g salvinorin A)
- **10x**: 10 times potency (~20–40 mg/g)
- **20x, 40x, 80x**: increasingly concentrated — extreme potency

Preparation of enhanced leaf:
1. Extract salvinorin A from a large quantity of leaf using acetone or ethanol
2. Dissolve the extract in a small amount of solvent
3. Evenly distribute the solution over a smaller quantity of dried, crushed leaf
4. Allow the solvent to completely evaporate
5. The resulting leaf has a higher concentration of salvinorin A

Enhanced leaf is extremely potent and should only be used by experienced individuals. A single hit of 20x can produce a fully dissociative, ego-dissolving experience within seconds.

## Dosage Guidelines

### Smoked (Plain Dried Leaf)
- Threshold: 0.1–0.25 g
- Light: 0.25–0.5 g
- Moderate: 0.5–0.75 g
- Strong: 0.75–1.0+ g

### Smoked (Enhanced Leaf — 5x)
- Light: 0.05–0.1 g
- Moderate: 0.1–0.15 g
- Strong: 0.15–0.25 g

### Sublingual (Fresh Leaf Quid)
- Light: 6–8 leaves
- Moderate: 8–16 leaves
- Strong: 16–32 leaves

## Effects Profile

Salvia's effects are distinct from other psychedelics:

- Extremely rapid onset (seconds when smoked)
- Short duration (5–15 minutes smoked, 30–90 minutes sublingual)
- Dissociative — feeling of being pulled, twisted, or separated from one's body
- Reality fragmentation — the sense of consensus reality breaks down
- Vivid, often nonsensical visual landscapes
- Entity contact experiences are common
- Amnesia of the experience is frequent — people often forget what happened immediately after
- "Salvia gravity" — a sensation of being pushed, pulled, or folded by external forces
- Laughing fits are common
- Aftereffects: calm, sometimes confused, occasionally anxious

## Safety Considerations

- A sitter is absolutely essential — loss of physical coordination and awareness can lead to injury
- Use only in a safe, controlled environment (no standing, no open flames, no water nearby)
- Do not use while standing — lie down on a bed or soft surface before inhaling
- Avoid combining with other substances, especially other dissociatives or depressants
- Not recommended for people with a history of psychosis or severe anxiety
- Salvia can trigger intense dysphoria — it is not always a "pleasant" experience
