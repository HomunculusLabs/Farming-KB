# Maitake (Grifola frondosa)

Maitake, scientifically known as Grifola frondosa, is a large fleshy
polypore mushroom prized both as a gourmet delicacy and a potent medicinal
fungus. Its Japanese name means "Dancing Mushroom," derived from a legendary
tale (recorded in the 11th century Konjaku Monogatari) describing people who
found this mushroom in the wild and danced with joy. Also known as Hen-of-
the-Woods, Kumotake ("Cloud Mushroom"), and the Dancing Butterfly Mushroom,
Maitake has become one of the most commercially significant specialty
mushrooms.

## Description

Maitake forms large, compound [[polypore-fungi-eastern-north-america]]
America, Europe, and Japan. It is a weak parasitic to saprophytic fungus,
attacking the root systems of living trees and continuing to decompose the
wood after the tree dies. Wild Maitake is typically collected in autumn,
September through November, when temperatures drop to 50-65 F (10-18 C).
Japanese Maitake hunters keep their collection locations secret, passing
knowledge through generations.

## Taxonomy and Related Species

Grifola frondosa is synonymous with Polyporus frondosus Dicks.:Fr. It is
closely allied to [[grifola-umbellata]]
or Zhu Ling), which produces fruitbodies with multiple caps arising from a
common stem but has a lighter color, more fragile texture, and
fundamentally different life cycle. G. umbellata produces underground
sclerotia and has larger, more cylindrical spores. G. frondosa lacks a
sclerotial stage and arises from a multiple forking base.
