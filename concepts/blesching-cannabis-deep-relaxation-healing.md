# Cannabis, Deep Relaxation, and the Healing Response

The first section of Chapter V in The Cannabis Health Index explores the science and practice of deep relaxation as a healing modality, with particular attention to how the [[endocannabinoid system]] interfaces with what researchers call the "relaxation response" (RR). This concept, formalized by Dr. Herbert Benson at Harvard Medical School, represents the physiological opposite of the well-known stress response (fight, flight, or freeze).

## The Relaxation Response Defined

The relaxation response is not the same as ordinary relaxation. Going for a walk, taking a power nap, or watching television can be calming but generally do not induce the specific physiological state that researchers have termed a relaxation response. The RR is defined as a measurable, coordinated set of physiological changes that directly counteract the stress response.

During a relaxation response, the mind-body axis undergoes significant hormonal and signaling changes in the brain that calm the sympathetic nervous system. Specific measurable effects include:

- Reduction in stress molecules such as adrenaline and [[blesching-cannabis-cortisol-stress-endocannabinoid-system]]
- Decreased heart rate and resting blood pressure
- Lowered blood sugar levels
- Increased production of nitric oxide, which dilates major blood vessels
- Reduction in respiratory rate and oxygen consumption

These changes are particularly important for patients with cardiovascular illnesses and those suffering from adult-onset [[cannabis-and-diabetes]]. The relaxation response activates the parasympathetic nervous system, shifting the body from a state of physiological alarm to one of rest, repair, and regeneration.

## Stress Response vs. Relaxation Response

The stress response evolved as a survival mechanism, mobilizing the body's resources for immediate action. When chronically activated, however, the stress response becomes pathological:

- Chronic cortisol elevation promotes inflammation, insulin resistance, and immune suppression
- Sustained adrenaline production increases cardiovascular strain and anxiety
- The persistent sympathetic activation disrupts sleep, digestion, and tissue repair

The relaxation response reverses each of these pathological processes, restoring homeostatic balance. Regular induction of the RR has been shown to counteract the cumulative damage of chronic stress on virtually every organ system in the body. The key insight is that the RR is not merely the absence of stress but an active, measurable physiological state with its own distinct biochemical signature.

### The Nitric Oxide Connection

The relaxation response produces an increase in nitric oxide (NO), a gas molecule that dilates (widens) major blood vessels. This vasodilation is a primary mechanism through which the RR reduces blood pressure and improves cardiovascular function. Nitric oxide also has anti-inflammatory and anti-platelet properties, providing additional cardiovascular protection.

## Anandamide and the Relaxation Response

Researchers are beginning to understand how [[anandamide-aea]], the endogenous cannabinoid produced by nerve cells, contributes to the relaxation response. Anandamide has been shown to induce the widening of coronary arteries (which supply the heart), thus reducing hypertension. This vasodilatory effect potentially benefits numerous patient populations with cardiovascular conditions.

If these findings are confirmed in human trials, it would suggest that patients can learn to actively direct a relaxation response by engaging the [[endocannabinoid system]] through two complementary pathways:

1. **Physical signals**: Through the administration of cannabis or isolated cannabinoids that activate the same receptor systems involved in the RR
2. **Non-physical signals**: Through emotional states associated with meditation, mindfulness, or other contemplative practices that trigger endogenous cannabinoid release

This dual pathway model positions cannabis as a bridge between pharmacological intervention and self-directed healing practices. The key insight is that both exogenous cannabinoids and endogenous cannabinoid release (triggered by meditative states) can activate the same receptor systems that produce the relaxation response.

## Scientific Validation

A double-blind, randomized trial conducted at the Body Medicine Center at Massachusetts General Hospital demonstrated that the self-directed healing mechanism associated with the relaxation response is subject to conscious intervention and can be learned by almost anyone. The clinical significance of this finding cannot be overstated: it means that patients can be trained to activate their own physiological healing mechanisms at will, without ongoing external intervention.

This finding is particularly relevant to cannabis therapy because it suggests that the relaxation-promoting effects of cannabis can be gradually internalized, with patients eventually able to achieve similar states without the plant. Cannabis thus becomes a training tool for developing self-directed relaxation capacity rather than a permanent dependency.

## Techniques for Inducing the Relaxation Response

Researchers have tested numerous mind-body techniques and found that people can learn to use both ancient and modern practices to reliably induce a relaxation response. Documented effective approaches include:

### Ancient Practices

- **Tai Chi**: A Chinese martial art emphasizing slow, deliberate movements and breath awareness, practiced by millions worldwide for health and longevity
- **Qi Gong**: A system of coordinated body posture, movement, and breathing for health and meditation with roots in traditional Chinese medicine
- **Meditation**: Various contemplative traditions focused on sustained attention and present-moment awareness, including mindfulness-based stress reduction (MBSR)
- **Repetitive prayer**: Rhythmic spiritual practices across multiple religious traditions, including the Catholic Rosary, Islamic dhikr, and Buddhist mantra recitation
- **Yoga**: Physical, mental, and spiritual practices originating in ancient India combining postures, breathwork, and meditation

### Modern Practices

- **Awareness of emotional and behavioral stress responses**: Consciousness-based approaches to recognizing and modifying stress reactions in real time
- **Progressive muscle relaxation**: A systematic technique developed by Edmund Jacobson for reducing muscle tension through sequential tensing and releasing of muscle groups

## Clinical Applications

The use of self-induced relaxation response in hospital settings has gained popularity due to its effectiveness, low cost, and absence of adverse side effects. Documented clinical applications span a wide range of conditions:

### Cardiovascular Conditions

- **Hypertension**: Regular RR practice has been shown to produce clinically significant reductions in blood pressure
- **Cardiac arrhythmias**: The calming of the sympathetic nervous system can stabilize heart rhythms
- **Stroke prevention**: Through the combined effects of blood pressure reduction and improved vascular health via nitric oxide production

### Pain Management

- **Chronic pain**: The relaxation response reduces the muscle tension and psychological distress that amplify pain perception, making it a valuable adjunct to analgesic medication including cannabis

### Psychological Conditions

- **Anxiety**: RR directly counteracts the physiological basis of anxiety through cortisol and adrenaline reduction
- **Mild to moderate depression**: Regular practice has shown benefits comparable to some pharmaceutical interventions in clinical trials
- **Autism**: Some patients with autism spectrum disorders show improved emotional regulation with RR practices

### Hormonal Conditions

- **Premenstrual syndrome**: The hormonal modulation achieved through RR can alleviate PMS symptoms
- **Infertility**: Stress reduction through RR has been associated with improved fertility outcomes in several clinical studies

## Hospital Implementation

Many hospitals now engage patients' own capacity for self-healing by using in-room technology to teach relaxation response techniques. Patients learn these practices during their hospital stay and are encouraged to continue at home as part of their ongoing health care regimen.

The results of this integrated approach include shorter recovery times, fewer complications, improved immune responses against tumors and microbial infections, healthier cardiovascular function, and patients who feel like empowered members of their own healing team rather than passive recipients of treatment.

## The Cannabis Connection

Cannabis can serve as an adjunct to relaxation response practices in several ways:

- **Inducing the initial state**: Cannabis may help patients who struggle to achieve deep relaxation on their own enter a receptive state more easily
- **Lowering the barrier to practice**: The initial anxiolytic effects of certain cannabis strains can reduce resistance to meditation or relaxation exercises
- **Enhancing body awareness**: Some patients report that cannabis increases their somatic awareness, making it easier to notice and release muscle tension
- **Amplifying the endocannabinoid contribution**: Since anandamide appears to be involved in the relaxation response, supplementing the [[endocannabinoid system]] with plant cannabinoids may deepen the response

## Cannabis Strain Selection for Deep Relaxation

Different cannabis strains may be more or less suited to facilitating the relaxation response:

- **Indica-dominant strains**: Generally preferred for relaxation due to their sedating and body-focused effects
- **High-CBD strains**: CBD modulates THC's psychoactivity and may enhance anxiolytic effects without intoxication
- **Myrcene-rich strains**: The terpene myrcene has sedating properties that complement the relaxation response
- **Linalool-containing strains**: Linalool, also found in lavender, has calming properties that may deepen relaxation

Patients should experiment with different strains and preparations under medical guidance to find the combination that best supports their relaxation response practice.

## Summary

The relaxation response is a well-documented, scientifically validated physiological state that directly counteracts the harmful effects of chronic stress on the body. Its interface with the [[endocannabinoid system]], particularly through the vasodilatory actions of anandamide, positions cannabis as a potentially valuable tool for facilitating deep relaxation. When combined with mind-body practices such as meditation, yoga, or progressive muscle relaxation, cannabis may help patients activate their own self-directed healing mechanisms.
