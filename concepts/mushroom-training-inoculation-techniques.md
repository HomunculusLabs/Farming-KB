# Mushroom Bag Inoculation Techniques

Inoculation is the critical step where prepared mushroom-training-substrate-preparation.
Proper technique determines contamination rates and overall cultivation success. The FAO
[[mushroom-cultivation-disabilities-training-manual-overview]] for disabled mushroom farmers emphasizes that inoculation must be done under
strictly clean conditions, and that people with various disabilities can successfully perform
this step with appropriate adaptations.

## Clean Room Requirements

The inoculation room must be kept very clean and free of diseases to avoid contamination.
Sunshine should not be allowed to enter the area. Before inoculation begins:

- Clean the room thoroughly with a broom or dust vacuum
- Rub the floor with a disinfecting solution
- Bring all pasteurized substrate bags from the [[mushroom-training-incubation-management]]

## Key Principles for Success

- Speed is essential: minimize the time the bottle and bags are open
- Keep the bottle mouth near the flame at all times during the inoculation process
- The bottle mouth should remain higher than the bottom at all times
- Do not touch the mouth of the bottle with the PDA mycelium or substrate
- Disinfect hands between handling different materials

## Contamination Prevention

Contamination is the primary risk during inoculation. Sources of contamination include:

- Airborne spores from molds and bacteria
- Dirty hands or equipment
- Contaminated spawn material
- Opening the pasteurization chamber too quickly after pasteurization
- Poor hygiene in the inoculation room

The manual recommends that inoculation be done in hygiene conditions: clean and with no air
movement. For facilities with more resources, a [[comparison-laminar-flow-hood-vs-fruiting-chamber]] cabinet provides additional
protection against airborne contamination.

## Adaptations for People with Disabilities

Many trainees with disabilities can perform inoculation with adaptations. People with limited
use of one hand can use adapted tools or foot-operated devices. The key requirement is the
ability to work near the flame and maintain clean conditions. Some trainees may prefer to buy
pre-inoculated bags rather than performing inoculation themselves, which is also a viable
business model.

## Labeling and Record Keeping

After inoculation, all bags must be labeled with:
- Date of inoculation
- Type of mushroom
- Spawn reference number
- Inoculation time

Detailed records help track production timelines and identify any patterns in contamination
or yield issues. See [[mushroom-training-cost-profit-analysis]] for
how record keeping ties into business management.

## Relationship to the Cultivation Cycle

Inoculation is Step 7 in the full cultivation cycle described in the FAO manual. It follows
[[cotter-compost-substrate-preparation-manure-mushroom-cultivation]] and pasteurization, and precedes incubation. The entire sequence is:
substrate mixing, bagging, pasteurization, inoculation, incubation, bag opening, [[comparison-fruiting-body-vs-mycelium-extracts-medicinal-mushrooms]]
management, and harvesting.
