## Mesoamerican Mushroom Traditions

The use of [[allegro-the-sacred-mushroom-amanita-identification]] traditions:

- **Mazatec**: The most well-known mushroom-using group, inhabiting the
  Sierra Mazateca of Oaxaca. The Mazatec use [[shroom-cultural-history-maria-sabina-and-mazatec-tradition]], the famous Mazatec
  curandera who introduced Wasson to the mushroom ritual, is the most
  iconic figure in the history of sacred mushroom use.

- **Zapotec**: The Zapotec of Oaxaca use P. zapotecorum, P.
  muliercula, and other species in ceremonial contexts. The Sierra
  Juarez region has a particularly rich mushroom tradition.

- **Mixe**: The Mixe people of Oaxaca use P. zapotecorum, P.
  hoogshagenii, and P. moseri in healing ceremonies.

- **Nahua**: The Nahua (descendants of the Aztecs) of central Mexico
  use P. caerulescens, known locally as "derrumbes" or "piule."

- **Chinantec, Mixtec, Otomie, and Tzotzil**: These groups have also
  documented traditions of sacred mushroom use, though with varying
  levels of contemporary practice.
