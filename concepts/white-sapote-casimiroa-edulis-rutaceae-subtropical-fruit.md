# White Sapote (*Casimiroa edulis*)

## Overview

The white sapote (*Casimiroa edulis*, family Rutaceae) is a tall evergreen tree reaching 10–15 meters, originating in Mexico. Despite its common name, it is not related to the true sapotes (Sapotaceae) but belongs to the same family as citrus. It is valued for its sweet, creamy fruit and remarkable [[dighton-fungal-nutritional-composition-animal-diets]]** per 100 grams: Water 78.3%, Fat 0.03 g, Protein 0.143 g, Calcium 9.9 mg, Iron 0.33 mg, Phosphorus 20.4 mg, Carotene 0.053 mg, Ascorbic acid 30.3 mg. Digestion time: 3 hours.

Fruit production begins at approximately 3 years with grafted trees, or 3–8 years from seed. The plant is not always self-fertile, so planting several trees ensures cross-pollination. Fruit ripens mainly in early autumn.

## Medicinal Properties

The white sapote has a long history of medicinal use in Mexico and [[neurotropic-fungi-in-central-america]] for furniture.

## Propagation and Cultivation

White sapotes grow easily from seed and can also be produced by grafting. Seedlings should be pruned to encourage early branching, as they tend toward a straggly habit. Many named cultivars are available. Minimum spacing is 6 meters between trees.
