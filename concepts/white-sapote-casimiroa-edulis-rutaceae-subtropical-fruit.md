# White Sapote (*Casimiroa edulis*)

## Overview

The white sapote (*Casimiroa edulis*, family Rutaceae) is a tall evergreen tree reaching 10–15 meters, originating in Mexico. Despite its common name, it is not related to the true sapotes (Sapotaceae) but belongs to the same family as citrus. It is valued for its sweet, creamy fruit and remarkable [[ginseng-cultivation-and-medicinal-properties]], as well as its ability to grow in cooler subtropical regions where many tropical fruits fail. Extracts from the bark, leaves, and seeds have documented sedative, tranquillizing, and blood-pressure-lowering effects.

## Environmental Requirements

Although a subtropical tree, white sapote grows surprisingly well in cooler regions. It prefers good, well-drained soil and is moderately drought and wind tolerant. Mature trees tolerate frost to -4°C, though young trees are frost-sensitive.

## Fruit

Fruits are greenish-yellow when ripe, sweet in flavor but sometimes with a slightly resinous taste. Best eaten fresh but can also be made into jams, jellies, and drinks.

**[[dighton-fungal-nutritional-composition-animal-diets]]** per 100 grams: Water 78.3%, Fat 0.03 g, Protein 0.143 g, Calcium 9.9 mg, Iron 0.33 mg, Phosphorus 20.4 mg, Carotene 0.053 mg, Ascorbic acid 30.3 mg. Digestion time: 3 hours.

Fruit production begins at approximately 3 years with grafted trees, or 3–8 years from seed. The plant is not always self-fertile, so planting several trees ensures cross-pollination. Fruit ripens mainly in early autumn.

## Medicinal Properties

The white sapote has a long history of medicinal use in Mexico and [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-caribbean-central-america]]:

- **Fruit**: Claimed to induce sleep; eaten to relieve pain of rheumatism and arthritis
- **Bark, leaves, and seeds**: Employed as tranquillizers and sedatives; extracts found effective in lowering blood pressure
- **Leaf decoction**: Used in Costa Rica to treat diabetes
- **Bee forage**: The flowers provide moderate nectar for pollinators

## Timber

The wood is fine-grained, yellow, compact, moderately dense and heavy with medium strength. It is resistant to most rots and is sometimes used [[neurotropic-fungi-in-central-america]] for furniture.

## Propagation and Cultivation

White sapotes grow easily from seed and can also be produced by grafting. Seedlings should be pruned to encourage early branching, as they tend toward a straggly habit. Many named cultivars are available. Minimum spacing is 6 meters between trees.

## Permaculture Applications

1. **Cool subtropical fruit**: Extends fruit production into regions too cool for most tropical species
2. **Medicinal tree**: On-farm source of sedative and anti-hypertensive compounds
3. **[[fukuoka-mixed-grazing-pig-cow-chicken-orchard-integration]]**: Tolerant of moderate shade when young; provides canopy as it matures
4. **Bee forage**: Flowers support pollinator populations
5. **Timber**: Secondary yield of useful cabinet wood
