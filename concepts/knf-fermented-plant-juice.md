# KNF Fermented Plant Juice

Fermented Plant Juice (FPJ) is a cornerstone input of [[acacia]] flowers (Robinia pseudoacacia): promote flower bud
  formation and increase sugar content.

## Timing of Collection

Plants should be collected just before sunrise for maximum nutrient
content and vitality. At this time, catabolism is active and energy is
stored intensively in the plant tissues. Ingredients with dew on them
produce a higher volume of FPJ. Once the sun is up, photosynthesis
consumes energy and partially stores it in stems and leaves. Growing
points should be snapped quickly during collection.

Avoid picking during or after rainfall, as rain washes away lactic
acid bacteria and yeast on leaves. Wait two to three days after rain.
Also avoid excessive sunshine (drought conditions) and picking plants
near roads.

## Preparation Method

1. Shake off dirt from ingredients. Do not wash them in water; use
   with their own skin. Cut irregularly sized materials to broaden
   the surface area. Only one kind of plant per container.
2. Measure weight of ingredients and brown sugar. Use one-half to
   two-thirds the weight of the ingredient as brown sugar. For high-
   moisture fruits and flowers, use a 1:1 ratio.
3. Pile ingredient and brown sugar in layers or mix together. Fill
   the jar to the rim for green vegetables so air remains between
   materials. At the top, put enough brown sugar to cover materials.
4. Press materials with a stone weight to extract air and cover with
   porous paper and rubber bands.
5. After half a day, air will be extracted and materials should fill
   two-thirds of the jar. Remove the stone weight.
6. Keep in shade at 23 to 25 degrees Celsius for 7 days.
7. After 7 days, a fragrance should be present and clean juice
   visible when filtered. Small bubbles or fungi indicate insufficient
   brown sugar or unbalanced volume.

## Storage and Application

Add more brown sugar if FPJ is not used immediately. Store in clay
jars, glass bottles (brown glass to block sunlight), or containers made
from Japanese cedar. Bury the container or keep in a cool storehouse.
FPJ is typically diluted at a ratio of 1:500 for [[savory-growing-season-and-non-growing-season-management]].
