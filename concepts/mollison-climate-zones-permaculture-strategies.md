## The Climate Crisis

Mollison opens the course with a stark climate assessment: "It is
not that it is tending toward the greenhouse effect; it is not that
it is tending toward the ice age; it is starting now to fluctuate so
wildly that it is totally unpredictable as to which heat barrier
you will crack. But when you crack it, you will crack it at an
extreme and you will crack it very suddenly."

In his home town of Tasmania, buffered by ocean and forest, they
experienced in succession the windiest, driest, and wettest month in
200 years of recording. The implication is that permaculture designs
must buffer extremes, particularly drought extremes that seem to be
happening more and more.

## Climate and Forest Relationship

Forests are central to climate stability. They moderate excessive
cold and heat, excessive runoff, and excessive pollution. When
forests are removed, immoderate extremes arrive. Cutting forest from
ridges can reduce total precipitation by up to 86%, even when
measured rainfall drops only 10-30%. On quiet, clear nights with no
rainfall recorded on gauges, major precipitation occurs within forest
systems through condensation on leaf surfaces.

## Humid Landscapes (Pamphlet II)

Humid landscapes are characterised by reliable rainfall and active
hydrological cycles. Key strategies include:

- **Swale systems** to slow, spread, and sink water across slopes
- **Keyline planning** to store water high in the landscape for
  gravity-fed distribution
- **Forest integration** to maintain the precipitation cycle
- **Terrace and garden systems** on lower slopes with reliable
  moisture

Mollison emphasises that [[keypoint-and-keyline-in-humid-landscapes]], the main task is to
guide the abundant water through as many productive uses as possible
before it leaves the site. Forests store enormous quantities of water
both in soils and in the biomass of trees themselves.

## Island Systems (Pamphlets IV and V)

**Low islands** (atolls) present unique challenges: freshwater
lenses float on salt water beneath the island, and all water is
derived from rainfall. Strategies include maximising roof
catchment, protecting the freshwater lens from contamination, and
using salt-tolerant species at the perimeter.
