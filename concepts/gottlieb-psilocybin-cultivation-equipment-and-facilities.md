## Gottlieb Psilocybin Cultivation Equipment and Facilities

Adam Gottlieb's "The Psilocybin Producer's Guide" (1976) details the
equipment and facility requirements for both small-scale and large-scale
(5,000+ doses/week) psilocybin mycelium cultivation. The guide focuses on
liquid broth culture in mason jars rather than fruiting carpophores, which
simplifies the equipment list considerably.

## Core Equipment

### Containers

- One-quart mason jars serve as the primary cultivation vessels, filled
  halfway with broth medium (PDY -- Potato Dextrose Yeast broth).
- Petri dishes (15 cm) for stock cultures on [[agar-medium-preparation]] and kept at a
  17-degree angle. Sold in sets of 9-10 with steam sterilizers.

### Sterilization

- Canning-type [[gottlieb-psilocybin-production-extraction-methods]].
- Glassware boiled in water for 30 minutes before use.
- Metalware flame-sterilized until glowing, then allowed to cool before
  contacting cultures or specimens.
- Inoculating loops re-flame-sterilized after each individual transfer.

### Laboratory Supplies

- Inoculating loops for transferring mycelium fragments between dishes and
  jars.
- Pipettes or syringes for transferring spore water suspensions and mycelium
  liquid.
- Dissecting needles (3 per cap) for pinning [[challenge-organisms-nasa-microwave-surface-sterilization-testing]] of caps.
- Filter paper, Buchner funnel, and Celite for extraction filtration.
- Flasks for extraction and [[query-how-to-read-cannabis-trichomes-for-the-perfect-harvest-time]].

### Sources and Costs (1976)

- Mason jars: approximately $2.98 per dozen at retail stores (Sears, etc.);
  wholesale or discount outlets for large operations.
- Pipettes, loops, petri dishes, agar, pre-mixed media: available at many
  scientific supply houses or from Difco Laboratories, Inc., Detroit,
  Michigan 48201.

## Large-Scale Facility Design

A 10 x 15 foot room with standard 8-foot ceilings can support continuous
production of 5,000+ doses per week using Gottlieb's methods.

### Shelving

- Stock culture shelves: 1 foot deep, 5 feet long, spaced 6 inches apart
  (up to 16 levels stacked), holding up to twenty 15 cm petri dishes each
  (approximately 300 stock culture dishes total).
- Crop culture shelves: spaced 10 inches apart (10 levels), holding one
  quart mason jars. With the room dimensions given, this shelving can hold
  approximately 2,800 jars arranged 3 deep and 3 per running foot.

### Room Construction and Surfaces

- Walls, ceiling, and shelving painted with white glossy kitchen enamel.
  This serves dual purposes: important sanitary measure and improved
  efficiency and even distribution of light in the room.

  verathane or polyurethane. No cloth or carpeting permitted.
