## Dissolved Oxygen: The Critical Parameter

A general level of 5 parts per million (ppm) dissolved
oxygen is very satisfactory in ponds. At 1 ppm, many fish
species may die, except for a select adapted group of
air-breathers used in stagnant and weedy tropical still
ponds.

### Air-Breathing Fish Species
Several species have special adaptations for low-oxygen
conditions:

- Snakeheads (Ophiocephalus): possess air chambers near
  the gills
- Singhi (Heteropneustes): has labyrinth organs for air
  breathing
- Catfish Clarias: adapted to stagnant conditions
- Mahi (Notopterus) and Anabas: all have sacs or special
  air chambers near the gills

These air-breathers survive in the anaerobic conditions of
weedy tropical shallows where other fish die. Even derelict
swampy ponds can yield 2,400 kg protein/ha/year if a
polyculture of air-breathers is stocked as three or more
of the above species.

### Oxygen Dynamics in Ponds
Less oxygen can be dissolved in warm water than in cold
water. As the plants that produce oxygen by day consume it
at night, there is a danger that healthy, weed-filled
shallow ponds may develop severe oxygen deficiency on warm
summer nights. This is the most common cause of fish kills
in aquaculture ponds.

Oxygen supply becomes a limiting factor in fish stock
density at levels above 5,000 kg/ha or thereabouts, and
then oxygen as air bubbles needs to be supplied.

### Partial Pond Aeration
A considerable energy saving is achieved if only part of
the pond is aerated at critical periods. Fish will gather
(kettle) in the oxygenated area in times of need. For eels,
this aerated section is built with a narrow pass to the
main pond to prevent aerated water escaping.

### Cone Aerators (Trompe Systems)
A simple trompe or cone aerator draws air down into water
as it falls through a conical structure. Oxygenated water
is piped to refuges in ponds, preventing fish losses due
to anoxic conditions. These assist hatching of trout eggs
in gravels and provide emergency oxygenation during
critical warm periods.

## Acidity and Alkalinity (pH Management)

Pond pH is ideally 6.0-8.0 or even to 8.5. Lethal limits
for most fish are pH 3.7 (acid) and 10.5 (alkaline).
