# Capuli Cherry (*Prunus salicifolia*)

## Overview

The capuli, or capulin cherry (*Prunus salicifolia*, family Rosaceae), is a fast-growing semi-deciduous tree native to Mexico that was introduced to the Andes by the Spanish approximately 500 years ago. Over five centuries of cultivation, Andean people have selectively bred the capuli into a valuable backyard fruit tree that produces grape-like clusters of dark purple cherries without requiring winter chilling — a critical advantage over conventional cherries (*Prunus avium* and related species) that limits their cultivation to cooler climates. Its combination of rapid growth, low chilling requirement, quality timber, and multiple uses makes it an exceptional multi-purpose tree for tropical and subtropical permaculture systems.

## Growth Characteristics

The capuli is an extraordinarily fast-growing tree, reaching 3 meters in just 12–18 months and eventually attaining 10 meters at maturity. It is semi-deciduous and frost tolerant to approximately -7°C, with some damage possible at that threshold. Because it does not require a cold spell to set fruit, it can be grown in much warmer conditions than conventional cherries. In California, during exceptionally mild winters, it has produced two crops in a single year.

## Fruit

The fruit grows in grape-like clusters. Individual cherries resemble European cherries with dark purple skin and pale green, juicy flesh. The flavor is similar to wild cherries. In the Andes, the fruit is eaten fresh, stewed, preserved, made into jam and wine, and used as tamale fillings in Mexico.
