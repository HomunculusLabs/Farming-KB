## What Is a Ley?

The term "ley" comes from the British agricultural tradition of alternating arable (cultivated) crops with temporary grassland pasture. In the home garden context, a ley is a section of the garden that is taken out of vegetable production and sown to a mixture of grasses and clovers. During the ley period, typically three to five years, the area is not watered, fertilized, or cultivated for vegetables. Instead, it grows as rough pasture — mowed a few times each summer, with all clippings left in place to decompose.

The ley phase allows the soil to recover from the intensive demands of vegetable production. [[query-what-is-living-mulch-and-when-should-i-use-it]] and can be mowed regularly.

### Interplanting with Green Manures
Between vegetable crops, sow fast-growing green manures (buckwheat, mustard, clover) for 4-6 week periods. This provides some of the organic matter and biological benefits of a ley on a much shorter timescale, though it cannot match the deep soil improvement of a multi-year perennial grass ley.

### Container and Raised Bed Adaptations
For gardeners using raised beds or containers, a simplified version of ley rotation involves replacing the growing medium every 3-4 years or top-dressing with compost and allowing a cover crop to grow through the off-season.
