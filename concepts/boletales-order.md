## Order Boletales

## Overview

Boletales is an order of basidiomycete fungi within the class Agaricomycetes, best known for the boletes — fleshy pored mushrooms where the spore-producing surface consists of tubes rather than gills. The order is ecologically diverse, encompassing mycorrhizal, saprotrophic, and parasitic species. Boletales contains some of the most prized edible mushrooms (porcini, king bolete) as well as poisonous species. Molecular phylogenetics has dramatically expanded the order to include fungi with gills, teeth, and smooth hymenophores that were formerly classified in unrelated orders.

## Taxonomic History

The order Boletales was traditionally defined by the poroid (tube-bearing) hymenophore of its type genus, Boletus. Early systems by Singer (1986) and other classical mycologists grouped all pored mushrooms together, primarily within Boletales or the related Polyporales. The application of molecular systematics, beginning with the landmark multi-gene phylogenies of Binder and Hibbett (2006) and Nuhn et al. (2013), revealed that the poroid condition evolved independently multiple times and that the true Boletales includes a wide range of hymenophore morphologies.

The current concept of Boletales is based on monophyly from molecular data rather than shared morphology. This reclassification moved several genera with poroid fruiting bodies into other orders (e.g., some Polyporales) and incorporated numerous non-pored genera including the gilled Paxillus, the earthball Scleroderma, and the false truffle Rhizopogon.

## Morphological Diversity

### Poroid (Bolete) Morphology
The classic bolete form features a central stipe supporting a pileus, with the hymenophore composed of vertically oriented tubes opening as pores on the underside. Pore surface color ranges from white to yellow, olive, red, brown, and nearly black. Key characters include:
- **Pore shape**: round, angular, or radially elongated (daedaleoid)
- **Pore size**: fine (<1 mm) to large (2+ mm)
- **Pore color change**: many species bruise or stain blue, red, or brown when cut
- **Tube arrangement**: easily separable from the pileus context (as in Boletus) or adnate

### Gilled Morphology
Several genera within Boletales have true lamellae (gills), including Paxillus, Paxillina, and Phylloporus. The gilled condition in Boletales evolved independently from the gilled Agaricales, representing convergent evolution. Phylloporus is particularly notable as it has bolete-like morphology combined with gills, often causing field confusion.

### Secotioid and Gastroid Morphology
Secotioid (partially closed) and gastroid (fully enclosed, truffle-like) forms are common in Boletales, representing evolutionary transitions toward hypogeous (underground) fruiting. These include:
- **Rhizopogon**: false truffles, important ectomycorrhizal partners of conifers
- **Melanogaster**: dark-spored false truffles
- **Scleroderma**: earthballs, thick-skinned puffball-like fungi
- **Chamonixia**: rare hypogeous boletes

### Other Forms
Gomphidius and Chroogomphus have decurrent gills and slimy caps but belong firmly within Boletales based on molecular data. These "gilled boletes" are important associates of conifers, particularly in the Northern Hemisphere.

## Major Families

### Boletaceae
Boletaceae is the largest family and contains the typical boletes. Major genera include:

**Boletus (sensu stricto)**: A smaller genus after taxonomic revision, now containing primarily species with white tube context that easily separates from the pileus. *[[suillus-luteus]]* (slippery jack) and *S. granulatus* are widespread.

**Leccinum**: Scaber-stalk boletes, recognized by dark scales (scabers) on the stipe surface. Associated with birch, aspen, and poplar. *[[tylopilus-felleus]]* (bitter bolete) is a common lookalike of *B. edulis*.

**Xerocomus/Xerocomellus**: Distinguished by a dry, finely fibrillose tomentose cap surface that often cracks with age, revealing pinkish flesh beneath. Taxonomy has been substantially revised.

**Rubinoboletus**: Includes the striking *R. pulcherrimus* and allies with red pores.

**Aureoboletus**: Yellow to golden-pored boletes.

### Suillaceae
Suillaceae was elevated to family level and includes *Suillus* and related genera. Members are characterized by associations with conifers (especially Pinaceae), slimy pileipellis, and often glandular dots on the stipe. *Suillus* species are important pioneer ectomycorrhizal fungi, facilitating tree establishment in disturbed or burned areas.

### Paxillaceae
Paxillaceae includes *Paxillus* and *Paxillina*. *[[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]] [[fungal-serpula-lacrymans-dry-rot-buildings]]* is the causative agent of dry rot in buildings, one of the most economically important wood-decay fungi. Despite its common name, dry rot requires moisture to initiate colonization but can persist in wood with low moisture content through water-conducting mycelial strands (rhizomorphs).

## Ecological Roles

### Ectomycorrhizal Symbiosis
The majority of Boletales are ectomycorrhizal, forming mutualistic associations with forest trees. This symbiosis involves [[guzman-allen-gartz-africa-southern-hemisphere-underexplored-neurotropic-fungi]] boletes (Austroboletus, Fistulinella)

### Saprotrophy
Some Boletales are primarily saprotrophic, decaying wood and forest litter. [[brown-rot-fungi-cellulose-degradation-wood-decay]] in built environments.
