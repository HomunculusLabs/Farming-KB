# Fungal Spore Discharge Mechanics

Fungal spore discharge is the process by which mushrooms and other fungi eject
reproductive spores from their [[bloomfield-buller-drop-surface-tension-spore-catapult-basidiospore-discharge]] forces
and the evaporation of water. The study of spore discharge was pioneered by
A.H. Reginald Buller in his seven-volume *Researches on Fungi* (1909-1934) and
later elucidated in detail by John Webster.

## The Basidium and Spore Production

In mushroom-forming fungi (basidiomycetes), spores are produced on specialized
cells called basidia located on the gills, pores, or spines beneath the cap.
Each basidium is a four-pronged structure shaped like a miniature cow udder,
bearing a single basidiospore on each prong (or "teat"). When viewed under a
microscope, the basidia appear as tiny crowns projecting from the hymenium —
the spore-bearing surface of the mushroom. One by one, each spore in a quartet
is catapulted horizontally from its basidium.
