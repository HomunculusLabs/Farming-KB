## Chemical Structure and Activity Relationship

The basic beta-D-glucan is a repeating structure with the D-glucose units
joined together in linear chains by beta-bonds. These can extend from carbon 1
of one saccharide ring to carbon 3 of the next (beta-1,3), from carbon 1 to
carbon 4 (beta-1,4), or from carbon 1 to carbon 6 (beta-1,6). Mostly there
is a main chain which is either beta-1,3, beta-1,4 or mixed beta-1,3/beta-1,4
with beta-1,6 side chains. Key structural factors determining antitumour
activity include:

  - Molecular weight (higher weight generally more active)
  - Degree and pattern of branching
  - Water solubility
  - Triple-stranded right-winding helix conformation

Not all fungal beta-D-glucans exhibit antitumour activity. The extent of
antitumour activity seems to be influenced by solubility in water, size of
molecules, and the beta-(1-6)-bonding system in the beta-(1-3) major chain.
Water-insoluble beta-glucans soluble in dilute alkali can show marked antitumour
activity. The triple helical structure, stabilised by beta-D-glucopyranosyl
branch units, appears critical for immunomodulatory activity. It has been
suggested that immune response could be in part non-specific and determined
by size rather than by exact chemical structure.

## Mechanism of Immune Activation

Beta-glucans bind to complement receptor type 3 (CR3, CD11b/CD18) on immune
effector cells including macrophages, NK cells, and neutrophils. The binding
site has been mapped to CD11b at the C-terminus of the I-domain. Upon binding,
the ligand-receptor complex is internalised and NF-kappaB is activated.

Resulting immune cascade:

  - Cytokine production: IL-1, IL-2, IL-6, IL-10, TNF-alpha, IFN-gamma
  - Macrophages, NK cells, and cytotoxic T cells are activated
  - Complement system (classical and alternative pathways) activated
  - Acute phase proteins and colony-stimulating factors increase
  - B-cell antibody production enhanced
  - Helper T cell production and cytotoxic T cell differentiation promoted
