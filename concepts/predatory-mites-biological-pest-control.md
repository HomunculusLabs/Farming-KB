# Predatory Mites — Biological Pest Control Agents

Predatory mites (family Phytoseiidae) are among the most widely used biological
control agents in [[fukuoka-textdoc-pesticide-creates-pest-problem-citrus-orchard]].

**Phytoseiulus** — Specialist predators of tetranychid (spider) mites.
*P. persimilis* is the flagship species, used globally against two-spotted
spider mite (*Tetranychus urticae*). Its life cycle is roughly 5–7 days at
25°C, consuming 5–20 prey per day. Adults are orange-red, slightly larger
than their prey, and voraciously decimate spider mite colonies once
established.

**Amblyseius / Neoseiulus** — *Neoseiulus californicus* is a generalist
Type II predator tolerating higher temperatures and lower humidity than
*P. persimilis*. It survives on pollen and alternative foods when prey is
scarce, valued for preventive releases in greenhouse roses, strawberries,
and field crops where spider mite outbreaks are anticipated but not yet
present.

**Amblyseius swirskii** — Polyphagous predator effective against whiteflies,
thrips, and broad mites. It feeds on pollen as an alternative food, allowing
establishment before [[perennial-vegetables]]. *Galendromus
occidentalis* is the primary biocontrol agent for [[solomon-chemical-vs-organic-fertilizer-slow-release-trace-minerals]], and gel capsules for targeted placement.
[[ingham-aact-holding-time-shelf-life-decay]] varies from days to weeks depending on species and formulation.

## Release Methods

**Broadcast releases** distribute mites evenly over the canopy, common for
*P. persimilis* in curative treatments targeting spider mite hotspots. Rates
range from 50,000 to 200,000 predators per hectare depending on pest pressure.

**Controlled-release sachets** contain predatory mites with a food substrate,
continuously releasing predators over 4–6 weeks. Standard for *A. swirskii*
and *N. californicus* in greenhouse crops.

**Banker plants** support populations of alternative prey or pollen,
sustaining predators before target pests arrive. Castor bean plants supporting
*Ricinus* mites can maintain *A. swirskii* in pepper and cucumber greenhouses.
