# Resource Categories and Yield Dispersal in Permaculture

## Overview

### Category 4: Resources Reduced by Use

These resources diminish with every use and must be managed with conservation in mind. Examples include:

- **Soil minerals**: Every harvest exports nutrients that must eventually be replaced.
- **Fossil fuels**: Finite and non-renewable.
- **Old-growth timber**: Centuries to replace, minutes to harvest.
- **Topsoil**: Extremely slow to form, easily eroded.
- **Groundwater from fossil aquifers**: Withdrawn faster than it recharges.

The management implication: minimize use, maximize efficiency, and plan for replacement or substitution with Category 1 or 2 resources.

### Category 5: Resources That Pollute or Destroy Other Resources if Used

These resources cause systemic damage when employed. Their use is antithetical to [[cycles-in-nature]] are diversion routes away from entropic ends. Life itself cycles nutrients, creating opportunities for yield and thus opportunities for species to occupy time niches. The more cycles a designer can incorporate — nutrient cycles, water cycles, energy cycles, information cycles — the more yield the system produces.

### Types of Niches

1. **Niche in space** ("territory"): nest sites, forage areas, roosting locations.
2. **Niche in time** (cycles of opportunity): seasonal availability, diurnal patterns, successional stages.
3. **Niche in space-time** (schedules): specific locations at specific times — a pond edge in spring, a shaded understory in summer.

Designing for all three niche types maximizes the number of species and functions a system can support.

## The Principle of Order and Disorder

> Order and harmony produce energy for other uses. Disorder consumes energy to no useful end.

Neatness, tidiness, uniformity, and straightness signify an energy-maintained disorder in natural systems. A bare, weeded, perfectly straight row of a single crop is maintained only through constant energy input (weeding, pest control, fertilization). A polycultural [[food-forest]], by contrast, appears messy but produces more total yield with less energy input. The apparent disorder is actually a higher order of functional integration.
