# Maitake Environmental Parameters for Cultivation

*Grifola frondosa* (maitake/hen of the woods) cultivation requires precise management of environmental conditions across three main developmental stages: spawn run, primordia initiation, and fruiting body development. Each stage has distinct requirements for temperature, relative humidity, CO₂ concentration, light, and ventilation that must be carefully controlled to achieve successful mushroom production.

## Spawn Run (Growth and Maturation)

The spawn run is the vegetative mycelial phase during which the substrate is colonized. It typically lasts 30–60 days depending on strain and conditions.

### Temperature
- **Optimal range:** 20–25°C
- Mycelia grow well at room temperature; using the highest optimal temperature shortens the spawn run duration.
- North American strains may perform slightly differently than Chinese strains.

### Humidity
- **Relative humidity:** 95–98%
- In sealed cultivation bags, humidity is naturally maintained at high levels within the bag itself.
- External ambient humidity is less critical during this enclosed stage.

### CO₂ Concentration
- **Optimal range:** 20,000–40,000 ppm (2–4%)
- Elevated CO₂ is beneficial during spawn run and promotes vigorous mycelial colonization.
- This is significantly higher than atmospheric CO₂ (~400 ppm), necessitating sealed or semi-sealed bags with microfilters.

### Light
- **Light during spawn run:** Not specific for some protocols; low-level light (50 lux) recommended by others
- Mycelia can grow in complete darkness, but a low level of light throughout the spawn run period has been found to facilitate primordia initiation (Wu et al., 1997).
- Too much light during early and mid spawn run produces an undesirable grayish-brown surface mycelial coat.

### Ventilation
- **Ventilation:** 0–1 air changes per hour
- Minimal air exchange is sufficient; bags with microfilters provide passive gas exchange.
- Good substrate aeration (sawdust mix with coarse and fine particles) is more important than external ventilation during this stage.

### Duration
- **Time to colonization:** 4–6 weeks typical
- Fast-growing strains may complete spawn run in ~30 days
- Many strains require 60–90 days or more of mycelial growth and maturation

## Primordia Initiation

Primordia initiation is the critical transition from vegetative mycelial growth to reproductive fruiting body formation. This stage typically begins in enclosed bags.

### Temperature
- **Optimal range:** 10–15.6°C (Stamets, 1993)
- **Alternative range:** 20–25°C (Chalmers, 1994)
- Lower temperature is used in North American practice compared to Chinese methods.
- No cold shock is required to initiate maitake primordia.

### Humidity
- **Relative humidity:** 95% within sealed bags
- When using tented blocks, ambient RH of 60–70% with a clear bag over the substrate block maintains humidity if ambient RH drops below 75%.

### CO₂ Concentration
- **Optimal range:** 100–500 ppm
- Dramatically lower than spawn run — a massive reduction in CO₂ is required to trigger primordia formation.
- Upper limit is higher than for other mushroom species, giving some tolerance.

### Light
- **Light level:** 200 lux or more
- Increased light from spawn run levels is important for inducing primordia.
- Light-induced primordia in maitake are highly pigmented (dark grayish-black).

### Ventilation
- **Air changes:** 48 per hour
- Very high air exchange is needed to lower CO₂ and supply ample oxygen.
- Opening bag tops or cutting slits provides increased gas exchange.

### Duration
- **Time:** 5–10 days
- Primordia reaching 2.54–5.08 cm (1–2 inches) in diameter indicates readiness for bag opening.

## Fruiting Body Development

After primordia are established and bags are opened, fruiting bodies develop through several morphological stages (primordia → brain → cauliflower → cluster flower).

### Temperature
- **Optimal range:** 13–16°C, up to 18°C (Stamets, 1993)
- **Alternative range:** 8–15°C (Chalmers, 1994)
- *G. frondosa* is a mushroom of temperate climates with a relatively narrow fruiting temperature range.
- Reported ranges vary: 10–15.6°C, 16–18°C (Huang, 1997), 15–20°C (Wu et al., 1997).

### Humidity
- **Relative humidity:** 85–90%
- Slightly lower than during primordia initiation.
- Misting is used to maintain humidity but should be stopped one day before harvest to prevent bacterial intrusion.

### CO₂ Concentration
- **Optimal range:** 500–1,000 ppm
- Slightly higher than primordia initiation but still far below spawn run levels.
- Below 1,000 ppm (<0.1%) is the target.

### Light
- **Light level:** Same or slightly higher than primordia initiation
- Light at the upper limit of tolerance is used.
- Duration: 10–14 hours of light per day recommended.

### Ventilation
- **Air changes:** 4–8 per hour
- Moderate air exchange supports continued development.

### Duration
- **Time:** 14–21 days from primordia to harvest
- Total mushroom production cycle: approximately 3.5 months from inoculation to harvest.

| Parameter | Spawn Run | Primordia Initiation | Fruiting Body Development |
|---|---|---|---|
| Temperature | 20–25°C | 10–15.6°C | 13–16°C (up to 18°C) |
| Relative Humidity | 95–98% | 95% | 85–90% |
| CO₂ | 20,000–40,000 ppm | 100–500 ppm | 500–1,000 ppm |
| Light | 0–50 lux | 200+ lux | Upper limit |
| Ventilation (air changes/hr) | 0–1 | ~48 | 4–8 |
| Duration | 4–6 weeks | 5–10 days | 14–21 days |

## Key Considerations

- **CO₂ management is the single most critical parameter shift** between stages — dropping from ~30,000 ppm during spawn run to ~300 ppm for primordia initiation represents a 100-fold reduction.
- **Temperature drop** signals the transition from vegetative to reproductive growth.
- **Light plays a dual role:** facilitating primordia initiation during spawn run at low levels, while triggering differentiation at higher levels during primordia initiation.
- **North American vs. Chinese practices** differ in temperature regimes, with North American growers generally using lower temperatures for fruiting.
- **Sealed bag cultivation** simplifies humidity management during spawn run and primordia initiation, with bags opened only when primordia reach sufficient size.
