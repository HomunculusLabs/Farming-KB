# Vermicompost

Vermicompost is worm-produced compost — the castings (manure) of earthworms, primarily red wigglers (Eisenia fetida). It is one of the most biologically active and nutrient-rich soil amendments available, loaded with beneficial bacteria, enzymes, plant growth hormones, and humic acids.

## Why Vermicompost

- Contains 10-20x more microbial diversity than surrounding soil
- Bacteria-dominated — ideal for annual vegetables and cannabis
- Rich in plant-available nutrients (NPK ~1-1-1 with many trace minerals)
- Contains plant growth hormones (cytokinins, auxins) from worm gut processing
- Humic and fulvic acids improve nutrient uptake
- Improves soil structure, water retention, and cation exchange capacity
- Suppresses soil-borne diseases through competitive exclusion

## Worm Species

| Species | Common Name | Best For |
|---------|------------|----------|
| Eisenia fetida | Red wiggler | Standard bin composting (most common) |
| Eisenia andrei | Red tiger | Similar to red wiggler, processes faster |
| Perionyx excavatus | Indian blue | Tropical conditions |
| Lumbricus rubellus | European nightcrawler | Deeper bins, cooler temps |

**Red wigglers** (E. fetida) are the standard choice — they reproduce quickly, tolerate handling, and process organic waste efficiently.

## Setting Up a Worm Bin

### Bin Options
- **Plastic tote** (10-30 gal) with drainage holes — simplest
- **Stacking tray system** (e.g., Worm Factory) — continuous harvest
- **Flow-through system** (e.g., Urban Worm Bag) — easiest harvesting
- **Outdoor pit/trench** — in-ground, climate-protected

### Bedding Materials (carbon source)
- Shredded newspaper/cardboard (most accessible)
- Coconut coir
- Aged leaves
- Peat moss (mixed with lime to balance pH)
- Shredded brown paper bags

### Bedding Prep
1. Tear/shred bedding into 1-2 inch strips
2. Moisten to 70-80% (wrung-out sponge consistency)
3. Fill bin 6-8 inches deep with damp bedding
4. Let sit 24-48 hours before adding worms
5. pH should be 6.5-7.5

### Feeding
- **Feed**: Kitchen scraps (vegetable, fruit), coffee grounds, crushed eggshells
- **AVOID**: Meat, dairy, oils, citrus (small amounts OK), alliums (small amounts OK)
- **Bury food** 2-3 inches under bedding to prevent fruit flies
- **Feed rate**: 1/2 lb food per 1 lb worms per day (start slow, increase as population grows)
- **Pre-freeze** food scraps and thaw — speeds decomposition

### Stocking Density
- Start with 1 lb (approx 1000) worms per square foot of surface area
- Population will double every 2-3 months under good conditions
- A healthy bin can process 1/2 to 1 lb of food waste per day per sq ft

### Environmental Requirements
- **Temperature**: 55-77F (13-25C) ideal; below 40F they slow; above 85F they die
- **Moisture**: 70-80% — bedding should feel like a wrung-out sponge
- **pH**: 6.5-7.5 — add crushed eggshells or dolomite if too acidic
- **Ventilation**: Bin must breathe — drainage holes are essential
- **Light**: Worms are photophobic — keep bin in dark place

## Harvesting Castings

### Methods
1. **Light method**: Dump contents on tarp in bright light; worms move downward; scrape top layers off
2. **Migration method**: Push finished castings to one side, add fresh bedding/food to other; worms migrate over 2-4 weeks
3. **Screening**: Pass castings through 1/4 inch screen; worms and cocoons stay on top
4. **Continuous flow-through**: Harvest from bottom hatch without disturbing worms

### Using Castings
- **Top-dress**: Sprinkle 1/4 inch layer on soil surface (cannabis beds)
- **Mix into soil**: 10-20% by volume in potting mixes
- **Compost tea**: 2 cups castings per 5 gallons for [[compost-tea-aact]]
- **Seed starting**: Mix 10-20% into seedling mix
- **Transplant**: Add 1/4 cup to each planting hole

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Cause | Solution |
|---------|-------|----------|
| Fruit flies | Exposed food | Bury food deeper, add newspaper layer on top |
| Foul smell | Anaerobic/overfeeding | Add dry bedding, reduce feeding, improve drainage |
| Worms escaping | Bin too acidic/wet/hot | Check pH, moisture, temperature; add eggshells |
| Slow processing | Too cold / not enough worms | Move to warmer location; add more worms |
| Mold on food | Normal (fungi are part of the process) | Usually fine; reduce feeding amount |

## Worm Tea vs. Compost Tea

- **Worm tea** (leachate from bin): Drainage liquid, can contain pathogens, use diluted on soil only
- **[[compost-tea-aact]]** with castings**: Actively aerated extract — safe for foliar, biology multiplied

Always make AACT, don't just use raw bin drainage as a foliar spray.

## Solomon on Castings and Manure Quality

Steve Solomon ("Gardening When It Counts") raises an important caveat about manure and castings quality:

### The Manure Quality Problem
- Manure nutrient content varies enormously depending on what the animal ate and its bedding
- Horse manure N can range from 0.5% to 1.4%; poultry manure from 1.1% to 6.0%
- Animals fed nutrient-poor forage (depleted soils, poor hay) produce nutrient-poor manure
- Racehorse manure from animals fed high-protein diets with kelpmeal supplements is vastly superior to manure from pasture-fed horses on depleted land
- **You cannot assume manure will produce nutritious vegetables** — quality of input determines quality of output

### Implications for Vermicompost
- Worm castings inherit the nutrient profile of what the worms were fed
- If worm bin inputs are kitchen scraps from conventionally-grown vegetables (mineral-poor), castings will be biologically active but mineral-deficient
- Solomon recommends: don't rely solely on manure/compost for plant nutrition — use a balanced complete organic fertilizer (see [[soil-fertility-management]]) to ensure mineral completeness
- Castings remain valuable for their **biological inoculation** (microbes, enzymes, hormones) even when mineral content is uncertain
- The microbial diversity in castings is probably more important than their NPK numbers

### The COF Approach (Solomon)
Solomon's Complete Organic Fertilizer (COF) is designed to supplement the biological activity of compost/manure/castings with guaranteed mineral nutrition:
- Uses seedmeals (soybean, linseed, canola) for NPK (~6-4-2)
- Adds lime (agricultural, dolomitic, gypsum) for calcium and magnesium
- Includes kelpmeal for trace minerals and growth regulators
- See [[soil-fertility-management]] for the full COF recipe and application rates
