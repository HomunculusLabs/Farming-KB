## Shulgin -- Drugs of Abuse in the Future

[[morphine]]/heroin substitutes across multiple chemical families:

- **Oripavine derivatives** (from thebaine, ~1% of raw opium) -- exceed morphine potency by four [[event-classification-and-orders-of-magnitude]]. Ethorphine (M-99) used for immobilizing wild animals
- **Morphinans** -- phenethylamine analogs showed 20-40x morphine potency in primate evaluation; submilligram amounts could serve as heroin substitutes
- **Benzomorphans** -- pentazocine (Talwin) was marketed without narcotic controls but showed hallucinogenic side effects
- **Meperidine family** -- over 4,000 [[ephedrine]] analogs and pipradrol derivatives offer additional stimulant variants
- Cocaine synthesis from scratch is tedious but procedures exist in the literature for decades

## Depressants

- **Barbiturates** -- thousands of possible 5,5-disubstituted derivatives with sedative action
- **Non-barbiturate depressants** -- glutethimide (Doriden) and methyprylon established that piperidones outside the barbiturate class could be equally abusable
- **Methaqualone** (Quaalude) -- simple synthesis from acetylanthranilic acid and aniline; almost unlimited analog variety
- **Phencyclidine (PCP)** analogs -- easy synthesis, already appearing as N-ethyl and thiophene variants not covered by scheduling
- **Benzodiazepines** -- Librium and Valium abuse potential with severe withdrawal symptoms

## Hallucinogens -- Three Distinct Families

Shulgin identified three chemically and experientially distinct classes:

### Indoles (DMT, Harmaline, LSD family)
- Characterized by distortions and illusions arising from within the subject
- [[beta-carboline-chemistry]] relatively accessible through synthesis
- 5-methoxy-DMT orally active; alpha-methyl-5-methoxytryptamines largely unexplored
- 6-methoxy dihydroharman group (synthetic carbolines) more potent than natural 7-methoxy counterparts
- LSD structural modifications (methylation, acetylation of indolic nitrogen) produce equally potent compounds outside existing law -- yet these have never appeared on the illicit market

### Phenethylamines (Mescaline family)
- Characterized by sensory changes and perceptual synthesis involving the external world
- Extending the alkyl chain to three carbons with 2,5-dimethoxy substitution amplifies potency dramatically
- 4-methyl-2,5-dimethoxyphenylisopropylamine = DOM (STP), a street-abused compound
- 4-bromo-2,5-dimethoxy substitution yields the most potent phenethylamine yet studied
- Vast unexplored substitution space at the para-position

### Atropine Analogs (Deliriants)
- Lead to visual/auditory phenomena that are artificial in source, often accompanied by amnesia
- JB-318 and JB-336 are controlled, but hundreds of similar compounds exist in the literature
- Easily synthesized from commercially available benzilic acid and ethanolamines

## Regulatory Challenges

Shulgin identified the fundamental problem of drug scheduling:
- **Specific naming** requires continuous revision as new analogs emerge
- **Broad class scheduling** sweeps in non-problematic compounds
- The legal definition of "similar" is perpetually contested
- Illicit chemists need only uncontrolled starting materials and published procedures

## Nomenclature Problem

Shulgin critiqued the terminology itself:
- "Hallucinogen" -- a misnomer; most of these compounds don"t produce true hallucinations
- "Psychotomimetic" -- too broad; restricted to research settings
- "Psychedelic" -- connotation of approval; absent from scientific literature
- "Psychodysleptic" -- European usage; also artificial

## OTC and Prescription Drug Abuse

Shulgin warned about abuse potential in legal drugs:
- [[dextromethorphan]] (10-20x normal dose produces dissociative intoxication)
- Polypharmacy interactions are unpredictable and increasing
