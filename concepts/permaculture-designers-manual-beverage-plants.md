# Beverage Plants

updated: 2026-04-18

From: [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-pattern-understanding-in-permaculture]]

## Overview

Beverage plants form an important category of permaculture crops, providing teas, coffees, and fermented drinks from tree crops, shrubs, and herbaceous plants. While the Designers Manual does not dedicate a single section to beverage plants, references to coffee, cacao, tea plants, and fermentation systems appear throughout, particularly in discussions of tropical polycultures and specialist crop potential.

## Tropical Beverage Crops

### Coffee (Coffea spp.)
Coffee is listed among crops deliberately cultivated on large mounds in grasslands, alongside sisal, cotton, and tobacco. Palms and coffee can have much of their outer bark removed by termites in grassland environments. Coffee is a shade-loving understory tree crop suited to humid tropical and subtropical forests, where it can be integrated into food forest systems under canopy trees.

### Cacao/Cocoa (Theobroma cacao)
Cacao is listed as a trade and plantation crop in the humid tropics of Africa and South America, alongside spices, copra, palm oil, rubber, banana, manilla hemp, rare hardwoods, balsa, tropical nuts, chicle, and drug plants. Cacao is an understory tree that thrives in the shade of taller forest trees.

### Tea (Camellia sinensis)
Tea is referenced in the climate and species discussions. Tea plantations are a traditional feature of highland tropical and subtropical agriculture. In permaculture design, tea can be integrated as a shrub layer in forest garden systems.

### Yerba Mate (Ilex paraguariensis)
While not explicitly named in the Designers Manual, yerba mate fits the pattern of subtropical beverage trees suited to forest integration in South American climates.

## Palm Beverages

### Coconut Palm (Cocos nucifera)
Coconut water is a natural beverage from the immature nut. The coconut palm is a staple in tropical food systems, providing water, food, oil, and building materials.

### Sugar Palm and Other Palms
Palm sap (toddy) can be collected from several palm species and consumed fresh or fermented into palm wine or vinegar. The ideal palm circle in the permaculture garden design includes coconuts, oil palms, dates, and a sugar palm.

## Temperate Beverage Plants

### Herb Teas
Many garden herbs serve as beverage plants:
- **Mint** (Mentha spp.) - grown in pots under trellises in wet pond areas
- **Lemon balm** (Melissa officinalis) - common garden herb
- **Chamomile** (Matricaria chamomilla) - bee forage and tea
- **Rosemary** (Rosmarinus officinalis) - aromatic tea herb and hedge plant
- **Lavender** (Lavandula spp.) - aromatic tea and hedge plant
- **Thyme** (Thymus spp.) - bee forage hedgerow species
- **Lemongrass** (Cymbogon citratus) - tea and essential oil

### Fruit-Based Beverages
- **Grape** (Vitis spp.) - for wine production, grown on trellises including house roofs
- **Apple, pear, and other fruits** - for cider and juice
- **Berry fruits** - for wines, juices, and preserves

## Fermented Beverages

The Designers Manual references fermentation systems:
- **Palm wine** from palm sap
- **Ethanol from sugar crops** (sugarcane, sweet sorghum) for fuel or beverage
- **Biogas** from fermented organic wastes (see [[permaculture-designers-manual-biogas-digester]])

## Design Integration

Beverage plants integrate with other permaculture elements:
- Coffee and cacao as understory crops in food forests
- Tea shrubs in hedgerow systems
- Herbs in kitchen garden Zone 1
- Palms in mixed tropical polycultures
- Grape vines on house trellises for cooling
- Bee forage plants supporting honey production as a sweetener
