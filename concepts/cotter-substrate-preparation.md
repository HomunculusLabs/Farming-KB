### Sterilization
Heats substrate to 250°F (121°C) at 15 PSI for 60-120 minutes in a
[[savory-living-organisms-as-tools]], creating a
completely sterile environment. Sterilization is required for:
- [[cotter-grain-spawn-production]]
- Any substrate containing nitrogen supplements, which would otherwise
  be colonized rapidly by contaminant organisms
Sterilization demands more equipment and energy than pasteurization but is
essential for substrates enriched with supplements like bran or gypsum.
## Moisture Content Management
Proper moisture content is critical for successful substrate preparation:
- Ideal moisture content for most substrates: 55-65% by weight
- Squeeze test: substrate should hold together when squeezed firmly but
  not drip water; a few drops between fingers is acceptable
- Over-wet substrates promote bacterial contamination and anaerobic
  conditions that inhibit mycelial growth
- Under-wet substrates slow colonization and reduce yields
- Substrate should be cooled to below 90°F (32°C) before inoculation to
  avoid heat damage to the spawn mycelium
