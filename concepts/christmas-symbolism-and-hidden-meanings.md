# Christmas Symbolism and Hidden Meanings

## Overview

The traditions, icons, and symbols of Christmas contain encoded references to [[allegro-colour-and-consistency-of-the-amanita-muscaria]] mushroom that persist from ancient Winter Solstice celebrations. When decoded, these symbols reveal a system of meaning that predates and transcends the Christian holiday, connecting Christmas to a far older tradition of entheogenic spiritual practice rooted in [[siberian-shamanism-and-amanita]] earlier mythologies.

## The Philosophical Core of Christmas

The basic philosophy behind Christmas is elegantly simple: if you are good, you receive a present under the tree; if you are bad, you receive nothing (or punishment). [[fly-agaric-santa-claus-myth]] functions as an all-knowing icon who reads the hearts and intentions of everyone on the planet. The traditional story involves a round man in red and white, his associates (reindeer, little people, Mrs. Claus), a miraculous worldwide flight in a sleigh, and presents delivered under a tree.

The culturally prescribed moment when children learn "Santa Claus is not real" is identified as psychologically significant — a devastating blow to the young psyche that is immediately soothed by strong reinforcement of the parents' religious system as the "real" foundation. This pattern is argued to be a deliberate disinformation campaign that conditions young minds against later comprehending the encoded meanings within Christmas symbolism, creating an unconscious armor against concepts involving plants found under trees.

## The Pine Tree and the Mycorrhizal Connection

The central relic of Christmas — the pine tree brought into homes — directly mirrors the natural habitat of the [[allegro-amanita-muscaria-properties-and-pharmacology]]. These mushrooms grow in a **mycorrhizal relationship** with pine and other coniferous trees, meaning they are always found growing beneath them. The symbolic placing of gifts under the tree at Christmas is interpreted as a ritual proxy-action, mimicking the natural arrangement where the "Most Sacred Substance" appears in the wild.

The actual mushroom "plant" is the underground mycelium directly attached to tree roots; the mushroom itself is only the fruit-body. In this very literal sense, the mushroom is the **Fruit of the Tree** — a designation with profound implications when connected to the biblical Fruit of the [[stropharia-cubensis-tree-of-knowledge]].

Winter brings rain, rain brings mushrooms, and in the mountains at Christmas time, under the pine trees, this is where the mushrooms naturally appear. The tradition of placing brightly colored (red and white) packages under green tree boughs recapitulates this natural arrangement.

## The Paradise Tree Tradition

One tradition of ornamenting a tree comes from the German "Paradise Tree" — a fir tree decorated with apples representing the fruit of the [[tree-of-knowledge-and-forbidden-fruit-theory]] in the [[allegro-garden-of-eden-and-delight]]. This "fruit" is identified with the Amanita mushroom. During Germany's Middle Ages, a popular play symbolic of the paradise story was staged on December 24th (the religious feast day of Adam and Eve). Additional symbolic connections include:

- **Candles** on the tree = Christ (light of the world)
- **Cookies** hung on the tree = wafers of the Christian sacrament (the Eucharist)

## The Evolution of Santa Claus

Today's Santa Claus is described as a metamorphosis of many older mythologies:

### From Siberian Shaman
Siberian shamans commonly dressed in red and white. They would enter through the opening in the roof of a home where a ritual was to be performed — paralleling Santa's arrival on the roof and entry through the chimney. Shamans gathered mushrooms in bags they brought to ceremonies, just as Santa brings presents in a bag.

### From Germanic/Norse Gods
The German Weihnachtsmann (St. Nick) was an amalgamation of older Germanic/Norse gods:

- **Thor (Donar/Donner)**: Wore red and rode in a Golden Flying Chariot pulled by two goats (Cracker and Gnasher) — ancestors of the reindeer. Thor's mushroom-shaped hammer, thrown to the ground, causes thunder and lightning, which in mythology causes real mushrooms to appear
- **Odin/Wotan**: Rode through the sky in a chariot depicted by the Big Dipper, which circles the North Star in 24 hours — also known as "the sleigh of Santa Claus" circling his mythological home, the North Pole. Odin's over-exerted horses had blood-mingled spit that fell to earth and caused [[amanita]] to grow

### From Egyptian Mythology
**Osiris**, lord of the underworld (South), also rode the sky in a chariot. After his death, Isis found that an evergreen (Cedar) had grown overnight from a dead stump to full size — understood as a sign of rebirth and immortality (also relating to the Djed pillar). Osiris's traditional birth date: December 25th. The tradition of placing presents around the Cedar tree on that date is estimated to be at least five thousand years old.

## The Christmas Colors: Red, White, and Green

The canonical Christmas colors directly mirror the [[amanita-muscaria-in-eastern-religions]] its natural setting:

- **Red and white**: The colors of the mushroom itself
- **Green**: The pine tree beneath which it grows

## Reindeer and Flight

Reindeer are native to Siberia and are documented as being quite fond of eating Amanita muscaria mushrooms. The mythology of flying reindeer is interpreted as reflecting the pharmacological effects of reindeer consuming these mushrooms. Notably, Siberian hunters who are not quick enough in the hunt find only mushroom stubs, the rest having been greedily consumed by hungry reindeer.

## Drying Mushrooms and the Christmas Stocking

Drying was a necessary preparation step for Amanita muscaria, typically accomplished by stringing mushrooms up and hanging them above the hearth of the fireplace. The Christmas stocking — red and white, hung by the fireplace in a similar manner, and shaped similarly to the mushroom's white volva sack — is interpreted as another encoded reference to this practice.

## The Virgin Birth Symbolism

The "Virgin Birth" is connected to the **seedless growth/germination pattern** of the mushroom. To ancient peoples, who had no microscope to observe microscopic spores, the mushroom's seemingly spontaneous appearance from the ground appeared miraculous — a "birth without seed." This botanical reality provided the template for the virgin birth motif found in multiple religious traditions.

## The Etymology of "Christmas"

The word "Christmas" is composed of two elements:

- **Christ**: Meaning "one who is anointed with the Magical Substance" (from Greek *chrisma*, the anointing substance)
- **Mass**: A special religious service/ceremony involving sacramental ingestion of the Eucharist, the "Body of Christ"

In Catholic tradition, the original entheogenic sacrament was replaced by the **Doctrine of Transubstantiation**, whereby priests claim the ability to transform an ordinary cracker/wafer into the literal "Body of Christ" — described as a substitute or placebo for the original substance.

## The Pineal Gland Connection

The **pineal gland**, thought in some traditions to be the seat of the human soul, is shaped exactly like a pinecone (hence the name "Pine-al"). It is an autonomous part of the brain resting in the dead center, not attached to any other part — described as a "floating pinecone in the center of the human brain." Due to cultural phenomena (including the psychologically devastating revelation about Santa Claus), the pineal gland atrophies during youth and begins to calcify during puberty, corresponding to the culturally prescribed "day of reckoning" and causing a reduction in pineal endogenous secretions.

## The Winter Solstice and Solar Mythology

The Sun, reaching the winter solstice, appears to stop its course across the heavens for three days, then begins anew its trek toward the Northern hemisphere. This three-day "stoppage" was mythologized as the death of the Sun/Son of God on the cross of the celestial four points, and after the three-day waiting period, as it began another precession, it was thought to return to life. Later mythologies transferred this death on the cross to a hanging on wood, and the three-day death to the Son/Sun being locked inside a tomb. The requirement that mushrooms be dried before consumption serves as another parallel — the god must "die" or sacrifice himself to save mankind through atonement (at-one-ment).
