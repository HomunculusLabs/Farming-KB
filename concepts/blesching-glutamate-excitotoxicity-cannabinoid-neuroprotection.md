# Glutamate, Excitotoxicity, and Cannabinoid Neuroprotection

**Source:** The [[blesching-cannabis-health-index-autoimmune-conditions]] (Blesching, 2015)

## Overview

L-glutamic acid is the most abundant excitatory neurotransmitter in the human
brain, but exists in low concentrations under normal conditions. While the body
produces healthy L-glutamic acid naturally, all exogenous L-glutamic acid
(including MSG) contains unhealthy impurities and at high concentrations
becomes neurotoxic.

## Glutamate Biochemistry

- **Production:** Produced by mitochondria and released by brain cells called
  astroglia
- **Receptor sites:** Astroglia contain CB1 and possibly CB2 cannabinoid
  receptors
- **Precursor:** Glutamine (an amino acid) serves as the precursor
- **Emotional keyword:** "I am excited" — the body's natural "upper"

## Excitotoxicity: The Danger of Excess Glutamate

Excitotoxicity is defined as excessive stimulation of nerve cells by too much
glutamate, causing inflammation, damage, or death to nervous system cells.

### The Excitotoxic Cascade
1. Excess glutamate overstimulates NMDA and AMPA receptors on neurons
2. Calcium floods into cells through overactivated receptor channels
3. Calcium overload triggers inflammatory enzyme activation and free radical
   production
4. Inflammation further increases glutamate over-excitation and neurotoxicity
5. If unchecked, the neuron dies

### Conditions Linked to Excess Glutamate
- Anxiety [[cannabis-sleep-disorders-and-insomnia]]
- Autism spectrum disorders
- Parkinson's disease [[cannabis-and-multiple-sclerosis]]
- [[cannabis-for-epilepsy-and-seizure-disorders]] and increased stroke risk
- Elevated acetylcholine, amplifying anxiety, restlessness, and fear

## The Endocannabinoid System as Glutamate Regulator

### CB1-Mediated Modulation
Activation of CB1 receptors modulates synaptic glutamate transmission and
plasticity. The ECS has a dual capacity:

- **In healthy conditions:** CB1 modulates glutamate for normal synaptic
  plasticity, learning, and memory
- **In pathological conditions:** The ECS reduces excessive glutamate release,
  protecting neurons from excitotoxic damage

### The Stress-Glutamate-Cannabinoid Connection
Chronic stress reduces [[blesching-anandamide-exercise-natural-endocannabinoid-enhancement]] production and receptor
sensitivity, removing a critical biological mechanism for controlling
glutamate. This creates a vicious cycle: stress depletes the ECS, unregulated
glutamate increases anxiety, which further depletes the ECS.

### Dual Upper/Downer Capacity
The ECS's ability to balance excitatory (cortical glutamate) and inhibitory
(GABA) effects simultaneously may present a new approach to treating ADHD.
Unlike pharmaceutical stimulants, cannabinoids modulate both sides of the
excitation-inhibition balance.

## Dietary Glutamate Sources

- **MSG and hydrolyzed protein:** Processed foods, snack foods, restaurant
  dishes
- **Yeast extracts and autolyzed yeast:** Hidden in many ingredient lists
- **Aged and fermented foods:** Naturally contain higher glutamate levels
- **99% pure glutamate products:** Marketed as supplements or flavor enhancers

Gluten and synthetic glutamate are unrelated but both may contribute to
neurological illness for different reasons.

## Modulating Glutamate Levels

### Dietary Approaches
- Reduce or eliminate foods containing added glutamate
- Increase glutamine-rich foods to support natural regulation
- Support mitochondrial health with antioxidants (CoQ10, alpha-lipoic acid)

### Stress Reduction
Reducing fear, worry, and stress lowers glutamate through HPA axis modulation.
The adrenal [[blesching-ten-strategies-reduce-fear-anxiety-stress-hormones]] feed back to increase brain glutamate levels.

### Cannabinoid Therapy
CB1-activating cannabinoids regulate glutamate through:
1. Presynaptic CB1 activation reducing excessive glutamate release
2. Anti-inflammatory effects breaking the inflammation-glutamate feedback loop
