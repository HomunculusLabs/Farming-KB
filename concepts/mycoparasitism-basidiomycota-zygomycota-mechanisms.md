# Mycoparasitism Mechanisms in Basidiomycota and Zygomycota

Mycoparasitism — the parasitism of one fungus by another — is particularly widespread in various groups of [[basidiomycota]], including Urediniomycetes, Ustilaginomycetes, and phragmobasidial Hymenomycetes. These fungi have evolved diverse morphological and biochemical mechanisms for attacking, penetrating, and exploiting other fungi as nutrient sources. Complementing the basidiomycete mycoparasites are certain Zygomycota that also parasitize fungal hosts, often through highly specialized structures.

## Urediniomycetes — Platygloeomycetidae

The subclass Platygloeomycetidae (Sporidiales clade) comprises the majority of species formerly included in Auriculariales sensu lato, for the most part parasites of fungi, mosses, ferns, and flowering plants (Bandoni 1984). Besides saprotrophic yeastlike genera and dicot-inhabiting smuts (Microbotryum), this subclass includes four orders with mycoparasitic genera.

### Haustorial and Colacosomal Structures

Parasites and hosts in this group are generally in contact through haustoria. In some cases, parasites possess large numbers of a specialized organelle in the periplasmic region called **colacosomes** (or lenticular bodies). These vesicular structures have:

- An electron-opaque core
- An electron-transparent sheath
- Vesicular content that projects through the parasite cell wall and makes contact with the plasmalemma of the host (Bauer and Oberwinkler 1991)

First described by Kreger-van Rij and Veenhuis (1971) as "lenticular bodies" in Sporidiobolus species, these structures were later identified in Cryptomycocolax and Colacogloea (Oberwinkler and Bauer 1990). Their discovery in Bensingtonia and Rhodosporidium suggests those yeasts may also have mycoparasitic capabilities (Boekhout et al. 1992).

### Atractiellales

Species of Chionosphaera and Stilbum (Atractiellaceae) may be mycoparasites (Bandoni 1995; Roberts 1997). Two species of Chionosphaera grow on lichens (Diederich 1996).

### Platygloeales — Cystobasidiaceae

- **Platygloea fimetaria** and **Colacogloea peniophorae**: Parasitize corticiaceous hosts, but also Dacrymyces and Poria (Bauer and Oberwinkler 1991; Bandoni 1995)
- **Cystobasidium lasioboli**: Found on Lasiobolus; two other species elicit formation of conspicuous galls on lichens (Diederich 1996)
- **Naohidea**: Forms pustular basidiomes on old pyrenomycetes
- **Occultifur** and **Kryptastrina**: Intrahymenial mycoparasites found in basidiomata of Dacrymyces and on corticioid fungi, respectively (Oberwinkler 1990)

### Cryptomycocolacales

**Cryptomycocolax** is possibly one of the most primitive basidiomycetes, unusual in having simple septal pores and apparently some ascomycete features. It was found parasitizing an unidentified ascomycete in Cirsium culms in Costa Rica. The host fungus produces botryose outgrowths that are engulfed by the parasite (Oberwinkler and Bauer 1990).

### Heterogastridiales

**Heterogastridium pycnidioideum** (anamorph Hyalopycnis blepharistoma) parasitizes various fungi on rotting plant material, including decaying walnuts (Juglans ailanthifolia) in Japan (CBS, unpublished data), and various fungal sporocarps (Bandoni and Oberwinkler 1981). It can be grown axenically, but some strains require a host (such as Plectosphaerella cucumerina) for pycnidium formation in vitro. The presence of lenticular bodies has been noted in this genus.

## Ustilaginomycetes

### Tilletiopsis

Tilletiopsis (revised by Boekhout 1991) is an anamorph genus containing mycoparasites of powdery mildews — one of the most economically important groups of biotrophic plant-pathogenic fungi.

### Pseudozyma

The redefined genus Pseudozyma (Boekhout 1995) is characterized by dimorphic growth, variously shaped blastoconidia borne on sympodially proliferating conidiophores, and the absence of ballistoconidia. It comprises anamorphs of Ustilago and includes mycoparasites of powdery mildews formerly described as Stephanoascus species (Traquair et al. 1988).

## Zygomycete Mycoparasites

### Biotrophic Mycoparasites of Mucorales

Several groups of Zygomycota are specialized mycoparasites:

**Dimargaritales** — The Dimargaritales (Dimargaritaceae) include obligate biotrophic mycoparasites. Dispira simplex and D. parvispora grow on Chaetomium; the latter also grows on Monascus species. As far as known, Dimargaritales cannot utilize glucose; axenic growth requires glycerol, organic nitrogen, and some vitamins (Brunk and Barnett 1966; Kurtzman 1968; Barnett 1970). However, Singh (1975) found an exceptional strain of D. cornuta for which starch was the best carbon source.

**Piptocephalidaceae** — Species of Piptocephalis and Syncephalis are biotrophic mycoparasites of Mucorales. Piptocephalis lepidula forms minute branched haustoria that penetrate host hyphae. Syncephalis nodosa produces zygospores and contacts Mucor hosts through appressoria and extended haustoria (Benjamin 1959). These fungi can be grown axenically on media rich in proteins with addition of growth factors (Kurtzman 1968; Barker and Barnett 1973; Singh 1975).

### Nonspecific Mycoparasites Among Mucorales

- **[[parasitella-parasitica]]** (Mucoraceae): A weak parasite of various Mucoraceae, Thamnidiaceae, and Choanephoraceae, contacting hosts by means of a kind of capturing hypha (Burgeff 1924; Zycha et al. 1969)
- **Absidia parricida** (Mucoraceae): Another mycoparasitic species
- **Chaetocladium** (Thamnidiaceae): A weakly parasitic genus that grows easily on any culture medium (Benny and Benjamin 1976)
- **Spinellus, Syzygites, and Dicranophora** (all Mucoraceae): Parasitize agarics

### Spinellus and Related Genera

Axenic cultivation of Spinellus requires organic nitrogen and temperatures near or below 20°C. Its spores require chemical stimulants from host extracts or ascorbic or gluconic acid to germinate (Watson 1962, 1965; Jeffries 1985; Jeffries and Young 1994). In contrast, Syzygites grows easily on any culture medium (Hesseltine 1957).

### Mortierella

The genus Mortierella comprises highly chitinolytic fungi that often appear indiscriminately on old fungal sporocarps. Exceptions include:
- **M. bainieri**: Commonly found specifically on Amanita species
- **M. armillariicola**: Known only from Armillaria species

### Rozella

The placement of Rozella in the Blastocladiales is debated, with three additional species of Skirgiellia close to this genus (Batko 1975). Rozella species are obligate endoparasites of various fungi and oomycetes, representing one of the most basal lineages of fungicolous organisms.

## Hymenomycetes — Aphyllophorales as Mycoparasites

Destructive necrotrophic mycoparasites of some ascomycetes (e.g., Ceratocystis) have been found among diverse aphyllophorale genera:

- **Bjerkandera, Lenzites, Trichaptum, Trametes, Schizophyllum, and Pleurotus** (Griffith and Barnett 1967; Traquair and McKeen 1978)
- **[[schizophyllum-commune]]**: A potent mycoparasite of many plant-pathogenic fungi, coiling around and penetrating host hyphae, which it then destroys (Tzean and Estey 1978b). A suitable host is the nematophagous and fungicolous Arthrobotrys oligospora.
- **Geotrichopsis mycoparasitica**: An arthroconidial basidiomycete with characteristic spindle-shaped chlamydospores, attacking nematophagous fungi, Mucoraceae, Mortierella, Pythium, Chalara elegans, and Rhizoctonia solani — but not Aspergillus, Penicillium, Geotrichum, Drechslera, or Nematoctonus species (Tzean and Estey 1991, 1992)

Athelia rolfsii and Heterobasidion annosum are resistant to G. mycoparasitica. Athelia arachnoidea, however, is mainly lichenicolous.

### Boletales and Agaricales as Hosts

A few fungicolous boletes and agarics are known from diverse groups. Host-specific associations are found in:
- **Asterophora**: Parasitizes agarics
- **Squamanita**: Parasitizes agarics

## Comparative Mechanisms

| Group | Primary Mechanism | Host Specificity | Key Structures |
|-------|------------------|-----------------|----------------|
| Dimargaritales | Biotrophic haustoria | Mucorales | Haustoria |
| Piptocephalidaceae | Biotrophic penetration | Mucorales | Branched haustoria, appressoria |
| Tremellales | Haustorial penetration | Wide range | Bulbous haustoria, colacosomes |
| Platygloeales | Haustorial contact | Corticiaceous fungi | Colacosomes |
| Aphyllophorales | Necrotrophic, hyphal interference | Various | Coiling, penetration |
| Tilletiopsis/Pseudozyma | Surface parasitism | Powdery mildews | Blastoconidia |

The diversity of mycoparasitic mechanisms across Basidiomycota and Zygomycota reflects the ancient and evolutionarily labile nature of fungal-fungal antagonism, with multiple independent origins of parasitic strategies ranging from obligate biotrophy to aggressive necrotrophy.
