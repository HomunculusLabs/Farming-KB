## Alexander Shulgin and the Phenethylamine Discovery

Metzner credits chemist Alexander Shulgin with the discovery and
characterization of a variety of phenethylamine compounds. Shulgin and his
wife Ann documented these substances in their 1991 book *PIHKAL: A
Chemical Love Story*, which Metzner references. The phenethylamines differ
fundamentally from the classical hallucinogens such as LSD, psilocybin, and
mescaline, which are tryptamine or ergoline derivatives. The
phenethylamines' primary effect on emotional awareness rather than visual
or cognitive perception distinguishes them as a separate pharmacological
and therapeutic category.

## The Empathogen Classification

Because of their distinctive profile of effects, some researchers suggested
the name "empathogens," meaning substances that "generate a state of
empathy," for this class of compounds. This term distinguishes them from
the classical hallucinogens, which Metzner notes are also called
"psychedelic" (mind-manifesting) and "entheogenic" (releasing the divine
within). The empathogen classification reflects their primary mechanism of
action: rather than dissolving ego boundaries or producing visionary
experiences, these compounds enhance emotional openness, interpersonal
empathy, and the capacity for intimate communication.

The empathogen concept represents a conceptual innovation in the field of
