# Insectary Plants for Pest Control

Insectary plants are those that attract, shelter, and sustain [[query-how-do-parasitoid-wasps-control-pest-populations]] populations. In permaculture-companion-planting for full compatibility lists. Key insectary species include:

- **Marigold** (Tagetes spp) -- deters nematodes; companions to lettuce, potatoes, tomatoes, roses, beans
- **Nasturtiums** -- attract predatory insects; companions to apples, cabbages, cucumbers
- **Garlic** -- pest deterrent; beneficial to nearly all fruit trees
- **Thyme** -- companions to cabbage family
- **Borage** -- attracts bees; companion to strawberries
- **Dill** -- attracts predatory wasps; companions to carrots and tomatoes
- **Mint** -- companions to cabbages
- **Rosemary, sage, oregano** -- aromatic pest deterrents in cabbage family systems
- **Chamomile** -- companion to cabbages

## Design Principles

- Design for year-round flowering by selecting species that bloom in sequence
- Layer insectary plants through all vegetation strata: ground covers, herbs, shrubs, canopy trees
- Introduce a whole range of plants to pastures rather than relying on single species

## Beneficial Insects and Their Requirements

Understanding which beneficial insects you want to support helps guide plant selection. Different predators and parasitoids have different needs:

**Predatory insects:**
- Ladybirds (Coccinellidae): Both adults and larvae consume aphids, mealybugs, and scale insects. A single ladybird larva eats 200-500 aphids during its development. They require pollen and nectar as supplemental food, especially overwintering adults. Plants in the Umbelliferae (dill, fennel, coriander, Queen Anne's lace) are particularly attractive.
- Lacewings (Chrysopidae): Larvae are voracious predators of aphids, thrips, whiteflies, and small caterpillars. Adults feed on pollen and honeydew. Flowering dill, angelica, and golden marguerite are strong attractants.
- Ground beetles (Carabidae): Nocturnal predators of slugs, caterpillars, and soil-dwelling pests. They need permanent ground cover, undisturbed soil, and overwintering habitat in log piles or stone walls. White clover and perennial grasses in pathways support ground beetle populations.
- Hoverflies (Syrphidae): Larvae are among the most effective aphid predators, with each larva consuming 300-400 aphids. Adults are important pollinators that feed on pollen and nectar. They are strongly attracted to composite flowers (asters, yarrows, calendula) and umbellifers.
- Pirate bugs (Anthocoridae): Generalist predators of thrips, spider mites, and small caterpillars. Attracted to small flowers like alyssum, daisies, and marigolds.

**Parasitic wasps:**
- Trichogramma wasps: Parasitize eggs of Lepidoptera (moth and butterfly pests including cabbage loopers, codling moths, and borers).
