# Cannabis and Morning Sickness

## Overview

An estimated 50% of all pregnant women develop some kind of morning
sickness, usually starting in the middle of the first trimester (at about
six weeks). Normally these episodes of nausea and vomiting are
self-limiting and disappear toward the end of the first trimester (three
months). While women can experience nausea and vomiting at any time of the
day, symptoms more commonly occur in the morning hours.

One hypothesis for morning sickness and the associated heightened senses of
smell and taste is a fetal protection mechanism. Foods normally harmless to
the mother may contain substances that could easily harm a developing fetus.
When substances detected by smell or taste could harm the fetus, nausea
develops to prevent the mother from ingestion or to produce vomiting after
ingestion. Midwives often advise their clients to go with their desires for
certain food items and avoid those that produce revulsion.

## Cannabis and Morning Sickness

After an egg is fertilized, it stays in the fallopian tube for three days,
then moves into the uterus for implantation. Morning sickness generally
begins weeks after successful implantation. Levels of endogenous anandamide
via CB1 and CB2 play a deciding role in the process of implanting the
fertilized egg in the uterine wall. The cannabinoid THC binds to the same
receptors and therefore may also play a significant role in egg
implantation.

University researchers (2006) from Victoria, Canada, collected
self-assessment data from 51 pregnant women who inhaled cannabis to
alleviate their symptoms of morning sickness. Their analysis indicated that
the 40 women who chose to treat morning sickness with inhaled cannabis
found it to be either "extremely effective" or "effective."

### Strain-Specific Considerations

The study reviewed inhaled cannabis for morning sickness relief. THC binds
to both CB1 and CB2 receptors and may modulate nausea through CB1 pathways
in the brainstem's vomiting center. Both sativa and indica strains contain
cannabinoids that activate these receptors.

- Inhaled cannabis provides rapid onset for acute nausea episodes
- Lower THC doses may be preferable during pregnancy to minimize
  psychoactive effects
- Raw cannabis juice provides non-psychoactive CBD acid (CBDA), which has
  demonstrated anti-nausea properties in animal studies

### Key Takeaways

- An estimated 50% of pregnant women experience morning sickness, typically
  beginning around the sixth week of the first trimester
- One hypothesis frames morning sickness as a fetal protection mechanism
  through heightened smell and taste sensitivity
- In a Canadian study, women using inhaled cannabis rated it "effective"
  or "extremely effective" for morning sickness relief
- Endocannabinoid anandamide (via CB1 and CB2) plays a role in egg
  implantation, suggesting cannabinoids may interact with early pregnancy
  processes
- Ginger (650 mg three times daily) has shown effectiveness comparable to
  or exceeding vitamin B-6 for morning sickness
- Mind-body approaches addressing anxiety, overwhelm, and fear may
  complement cannabinoid-based treatments
