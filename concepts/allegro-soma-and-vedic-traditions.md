## The Soma of the Rig Veda

The Rig Veda (c. 1500-1200 BC) contains over 1,000 hymns, more than 120 of which are dedicated to Soma — a divine substance pressed from a plant, consumed by priests and gods, and credited with producing immortality, visionary insight, and poetic inspiration. Despite centuries of scholarship, the identity of the Soma plant remains one of the great unsolved mysteries of comparative religion.

Key Soma characteristics from the Rig Veda:
- Grows in the mountains (RV 9.46.1)
- Has no leaves, no blossoms, no seeds (RV 9.72.6)
- Is golden or tawny in colour (RV 9.42.1)
- Is pressed between stones to extract its juice (RV 9.61.7)
- Produces intoxication, visions, and a feeling of immortality (RV 8.48.3)
- Is called "the pillar of the sky" (RV 9.74.4)
- Is associated with eagles who bring it from the mountains (RV 4.26.4-6)
- Its juice is filtered through sheep"s wool (RV 9.79.4)

## Amanita Muscaria as Soma

R. Gordon Wasson proposed Amanita muscaria as Soma in his 1968 book "Soma: Divine Mushroom of Immortality." Allegro, working independently from Near Eastern evidence, reached the same conclusion. The Amanita fits the Vedic descriptions remarkably well:

- **Mountain habitat**: The Amanita grows in alpine and subalpine zones, particularly under birch and pine
