Updated: 2026-04-18 Quinoa (Chenopodium quinoa) is a protein-packed, drought-tolerant grain crop that grows well in cooler climates. Both leaves and seeds are highly nutritious. ## Uses - Edible seeds (grain substitute, high protein) - Edible leaves (nutritious greens) - Dryland tolerant ## Growing Conditions - Well-drained soil - Dryland tolerant — needs very little water - Prefers climates under 90°F (32°C) - Don't water until plant has 2-3 leaves - Cool weather crop ## Varieties Quinoa varieties are broadly categorized by their growing region and seed color. Quinoa from the Altiplano of Peru and Bolivia (high-altitude varieties) tend to be the most cold-hardy. 'Brightest Brilliant Rainbow' offers a mix of colorful seed heads for ornamental and edible use. 'Cherry Vanilla' produces pink seed heads with a mild flavor. 'Red Head' and 'Oro de Valle' are reliable producers in temperate climates. 'Kaslaa' is a drought-tolerant variety from the Andean highlands. Select varieties suited to your day length and altitude — most commercial varieties require short-day conditions to set seed. ## Propagation Quinoa is direct-seeded after the danger of frost has passed. Sow seeds 0.25-0.5 inches deep in rows 14-18 inches apart, thinning to 6-10 inches between plants. Germination occurs within 3-5 days in warm, moist soil. Quinoa can also be started indoors 3-4 weeks before last frost and transplanted, though [[comparison-seed-starting-indoors-vs-direct-seeding-techniques]] is preferred to avoid root disturbance. Each quinoa plant produces thousands of tiny seeds, so a small planting yields substantial harvests. ## [[comparison-companion-planting-reference-vs-jadam-monoculture-critique]] Quinoa fits well into Permaculture Cover Crops Guide rotations, particularly in cool, dry climates. It is a [[permaculture-farm-design-principles]], quinoa can be interplanted with legumes such as beans or peas for mutual benefit — legumes fix nitrogen while quinoa provides structural support. It also pairs well with root vegetables like potatoes and beets. Avoid planting near lamb's quarter (Chenopodium album), as cross-pollination can occur since they are the same genus.

## Culinary Uses

Quinoa is a complete protein and can be cooked the same as rice, used in salads, porridges, and pilafs. The leaves are edible raw or cooked, similar to spinach but with a slightly earthy flavor. Quinoa flour is gluten-free and can be used in baking, though it often needs to be combined with other flours for best results. Toasting the seeds briefly before cooking enhances their nutty flavor. Quinoa is also used in soups, stews, and as a breakfast cereal.

## Pests and Diseases

Quinoa is relatively pest-resistant due to its saponin coating, which deters most insects and birds. However, flea beetles can damage young seedlings — use floating row covers for protection during establishment. Aphids may attack flower heads in humid conditions. Downy mildew is the most common disease, particularly in cool, wet climates — ensure good [[stamets-growing-room-air-circulation-co2-management]] and avoid overhead watering. Leaf miners occasionally cause cosmetic damage. The main pest challenge is birds, who may strip seeds as they mature — netting may be necessary near harvest time.

## Harvesting Leaves

Young leaves can be harvested for salads throughout the [[savory-growing-season-and-non-growing-season-management]].

## Harvesting Seeds

1. Wait until leaves have fallen, or even after first frost

2. Seeds must be very dry (barely dentable with thumbnail)

3. Timing is critical — if it rains, seeds may germinate on the plant

4. Place on a screen, rub to remove debris

5. Blow away chaff with a fan

## Processing (Saponin Removal)

Quinoa seeds are coated in saponin, a bitter substance that must be removed:

- **Blender method**: Put quinoa in blender at lowest speed, blend until water gets soapy. Keep changing water until it no longer froths

- **Pillowcase method**: Put quinoa in a pillowcase, run through cold water cycle of [[cervantes-washing-machine-hash]]

Cook the same as rice.

## Nutritional Value

Quinoa is a complete protein (contains all essential amino acids), high in fiber, iron, magnesium, and B vitamins. The leaves are also among the most nutritious greens.
