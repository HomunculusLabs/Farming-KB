## Categories of Association

The categories of fungal-vertebrate association overlap to some
extent because a commensal or ordinarily harmless environmental fungus
may become pathogenic with changes in the immunological or ecological
status of the animal host. The principal categories include:

### True Commensals

Organisms that persist on body surfaces as part of the normal
microbiota without typically causing disease. Malassezia species and
certain Candida species are the primary examples.

### Opportunistic Pathogens

Ordinarily harmless fungi that can invade animal tissues or
extensively colonize body surfaces when a host becomes
immunocompromised or is exposed to a heavy load of potentially
immunodepressing [[cannabis-soil-and-nutrient-requirements]] met by the host environment. The Onygenalean
systemic pathogens (Blastomyces, Histoplasma, Paracoccidioides,
Coccidioides) exemplify this category.
