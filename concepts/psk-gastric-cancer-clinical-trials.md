# PSK Gastric Cancer Clinical Trials

## Overview

PSK (polysaccharide-K, trade name Krestin), a protein-bound polysaccharide derived from mycelial cultures of [[turkey-tail-trametes-versicolor]], has been used as a form of immunotherapy for more gastric cancer patients than any other cancer type. The clinical evidence spans over four decades of Japanese research, making it one of the most thoroughly studied mushroom-derived cancer adjuncts.

## PSK: The Compound

PSK is extracted from a mycelial strain CM-101 of Trametes versicolor and is approximately 62% polysaccharide and 38% protein. The glucan portion consists of a beta-1,4 main chain and beta-1,3 side chain, with beta-1,6 side chains that bond to a polypeptide moiety through O-N-glycosidic bonds. The polypeptide is rich in aspartic and glutamic acids. The molecular weight ranges from 94,000-100,000 daltons, and it is orally bioavailable.

PSK acts both directly on tumour cells (cytostatic and cytotoxic) and indirectly in the host to boost cellular immunity. It also shows antiviral activity through stimulation of interferon production. In 1987, Krestin accounted for 25% of the total expenditure of anti-cancer agents in Japan -- a remarkable figure for a single mushroom-derived compound.

## Early Trials: Kaibara and Colleagues

In the early 1970s, Kaibara's group began trialing PSK with existing chemotherapy regimens for stage IV disease. After surgical resection (partial or full gastrectomies), PSK at 3g per day was added to a chemotherapy regimen of Mitomycin C and 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) with 66 patients. When compared with a historical control group, the 2-year survival rate was more than double.

This finding was later confirmed by Fujimoto et al. (1979) in a larger prospective study of 230 patients, and further supported by Hattori et al. (1979) with 110 patients and Kodama et al. (1982) with 450 patients. These studies suggested that PSK gave some protection against the immunosuppression normally associated with surgery and long-term chemotherapy.

## The Kondo and Torisu Double-Blind Trial

One of the few double-blind randomised controlled trials (n=144) examining the role of single-agent PSK found a significant increase in disease-free and overall survival. PSK had significant effects on patients' immune systems as measured by:

- Increased delayed-type hypersensitivity on skin tests
- Enhanced chemotactic migration of neutrophils

The trial established that PSK modulates immune function in measurable ways that correlate with clinical outcomes.

## The Dendritic Cell Connection

Tsujitani et al. (1992) observed that dendritic cells could infiltrate gastric cancers in some patients, and biopsy examination correlated this dendritic infiltration with an increase in disease-free and overall survival post-surgery. They concluded that patients with gastric cancer who had limited dendritic cell infiltration prior to surgery, when given PSK immunotherapy, were more likely to have a significant response.

This finding was significant because it suggested that PSK could compensate for a specific immune deficiency -- the inability of dendritic cells to infiltrate tumours -- and restore immune surveillance capacity.

## Phase III Multi-Arm Trial

The most recent Phase III two-arm trial of PSK in the treatment of gastric cancer was carried out by the "Study Group of Immunochemotherapy with PSK for Gastric Cancer of Japan." This large-scale trial showed that combining PSK with conventional chemotherapy significantly improved both disease-free and overall survival (Nakazato et al., 1994).

## Important Caveats

Several important observations emerge from the body of PSK gastric cancer research:

- Individuals with very low immunity are less likely to benefit from PSK therapy than individuals with a reasonably competent immune system
- PSK functions primarily as an immune modulator, not as a direct cytotoxic agent
- The compound works best as an adjunct to conventional chemotherapy, not as monotherapy
- Non-randomised trials in Japan have consistently supported the findings of controlled studies

## Other Cancers Where PSK Has Been Studied

Beyond gastric cancer, PSK has shown promise in:

- Breast cancer (randomised adjuvant trial with tamoxifen and chemotherapy)
- Colorectal cancer
- Cervical cancer (adjuvant immunochemotherapy trials)
- Oesophageal cancer
- Non-small cell lung cancer

## Safety Profile

PSK has been used extensively in Japan for decades with minimal adverse effects. The compound is orally bioavailable and well-tolerated, making it suitable for long-term adjuvant therapy following surgery and chemotherapy.

## Immunological Mechanisms of Action

The immune-modulating effects of PSK are mediated through several distinct pathways. PSK activates natural killer (NK) cells, which are critical for immune surveillance against tumor cells. Studies have shown that PSK enhances NK cell cytotoxicity and increases the production of interferon-gamma, a cytokine that activates macrophages and enhances antigen presentation. PSK also stimulates the production of interleukin-2 (IL-2), which promotes the proliferation and activation of T-lymphocytes. Additionally, PSK has been shown to increase the number and activity of Langerhans cells in the skin, which are important antigen-presenting cells involved in initiating immune responses.

PSK binds to specific receptors on immune cells, most notably the TLR-2 (Toll-like receptor 2) and Dectin-1 receptors found on macrophages and dendritic cells. This binding triggers intracellular signaling cascades that result in the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines and the upregulation of co-stimulatory molecules necessary for effective T-cell activation. The beta-glucan component of PSK is primarily responsible for this receptor binding, while the protein moiety may contribute additional immunomodulatory effects through different receptor interactions.

## PSK Compared to PSP

PSK is often discussed alongside PSP (polysaccharide-peptide), another protein-bound polysaccharide derived from Trametes versicolor. While both compounds share structural similarities as beta-glucan-protein complexes, they differ in several important respects. PSK is derived from the CM-101 strain cultured in solid-state fermentation and has a higher protein content (approximately 38%). PSP is derived from the COV-1 strain cultured in submerged liquid fermentation and has a somewhat different polysaccharide structure. PSK has been more extensively studied in gastric cancer specifically, while PSP has a larger body of research in other cancer types. Both compounds appear to work through similar immunological mechanisms, but they cannot be considered interchangeable due to differences in molecular weight, protein binding, and clinical evidence profiles.

## Quality Control and Standardization

The clinical efficacy of PSK depends heavily on consistent quality and standardization of the extract. Krestin, the pharmaceutical-grade PSK product, is manufactured under strict quality control with standardized extraction protocols, defined molecular weight ranges (94,000-100,000 daltons), and specified protein-polysaccharide ratios. The strain of Trametes versicolor used, the fermentation conditions, and the extraction methodology all affect the final product's biological activity. This is an important consideration for researchers and clinicians evaluating PSK preparations from different manufacturers, as significant variability in product quality can affect clinical outcomes. The Japanese Ministry of Health and Welfare maintains specific standards for Krestin production that have been refined over decades of manufacturing experience.

## Dosage and Administration

In the Japanese clinical trials, PSK was typically administered at a dose of 3 grams per day, usually divided into three 1-gram doses taken orally. Treatment generally began within days to weeks following surgical resection and continued for extended periods, often one to two years or longer. The long duration of administration reflects the understanding that PSK works gradually through immune system modulation rather than through direct cytotoxic effects. Some trials used PSK in combination with specific chemotherapy regimens, with PSK administration beginning either concurrently with or immediately following the completion of chemotherapy cycles. The oral bioavailability of PSK is well established, with detectable immune-modulating effects measurable within hours of administration.

## Combination with Modern Immunotherapy

The immunological mechanisms of PSK complement modern cancer immunotherapy approaches in interesting ways. Immune checkpoint inhibitors such as anti-PD-1 and anti-CTLA-4 antibodies work by removing brakes on the immune system, allowing T-cells to attack tumor cells more effectively. PSK, by activating antigen-presenting cells and enhancing T-cell responses through cytokine production, may work synergistically with checkpoint inhibitors by providing a more robust immune activation signal. Research into combining PSK or similar mushroom-derived polysaccharides with modern immunotherapy drugs is an active area of investigation, though clinical trial data on such combinations remains limited. The long history of PSK use in Japan provides a valuable foundation for designing these combination studies.

## Statistical Considerations in Trial Interpretation

Interpreting the Japanese PSK clinical trial literature requires attention to methodological standards that differ between Japanese and Western clinical research traditions. Many of the earlier Japanese trials used historical controls rather than concurrent randomized controls, which introduces potential selection bias. Some trials had relatively small sample sizes by current standards. The end-points measured varied between studies, with some using overall survival and others using disease-free survival. Despite these methodological limitations, the consistency of positive findings across multiple independent research groups, study designs, and patient populations provides considerable evidence for PSK's clinical benefit. The Phase III randomized controlled trial by Nakazato et al., which met the highest methodological standards, confirmed the findings of the earlier less rigorous studies.

## Comparison with Other Mushroom-Derived Therapeutics

PSK is not the only mushroom-derived preparation with documented clinical anticancer activity. PSP (Polysaccharopeptide) from Coriolus versicolor is closely related to PSK but differs in its polysaccharide structure and binding protein. AHCC (Active Hexose Correlated Compound) from shiitake mushroom mycelia has shown immune-enhancing effects in clinical studies. Lentinan, a beta-glucan from shiitake, is approved as an adjuvant cancer treatment in Japan and is administered intravenously. Agaricus blazei preparations have shown immune-modulating activity in multiple studies. The common thread among these preparations is the presence of beta-glucan polysaccharides that activate innate immune responses, primarily through interaction with Dectin-1 receptors on macrophages and dendritic cells. Understanding how PSK compares to these other preparations helps clinicians select the most appropriate adjunctive therapy for individual patients.

## Safety Profile and Side Effects

PSK has an excellent safety profile documented across decades of clinical use in Japan. Side effects are generally mild and infrequent, with the most commonly reported being gastrointestinal symptoms including nausea, diarrhea, and abdominal discomfort in approximately 1-2% of patients. Allergic reactions are rare but have been reported. No significant drug interactions have been documented, though theoretical concerns exist about combining PSK with immunosuppressive medications, as PSK's mechanism of action involves immune activation rather than suppression. Unlike conventional chemotherapy agents, PSK does not cause bone marrow suppression, hair loss, or organ toxicity. This favorable safety profile is one of the reasons PSK has remained in clinical use for decades while many conventional adjuvant treatments have been replaced due to unacceptable toxicity.

## Future Research Directions

Ongoing research is exploring several promising directions for PSK in oncology. Combination studies with modern immune checkpoint inhibitors are investigating whether PSK can enhance the response rate to these drugs. Biomarker studies are attempting to identify which patients are most likely to benefit from PSK based on their tumor genetics or immune profile. New formulations including nanoparticle delivery systems may improve the bioavailability and targeting of PSK's active components. Research into PSK's effects on the gut microbiome is revealing that PSK may exert some of its immunological effects through modulation of intestinal microbial communities, opening new avenues for understanding and optimizing its therapeutic activity.

## Quality Control and Standardization

Ensuring consistent quality in PSK production presents challenges for both manufacturers and clinicians. The polysaccharide content and protein-binding characteristics of PSK can vary depending on the specific strain of Coriolus versicolor used, the cultivation conditions, the extraction process, and the degree of quality control during manufacturing. Japanese manufacturers have developed standardized production protocols that minimize this variability, but products from different manufacturers may not be bioequivalent. For researchers and clinicians outside Japan, obtaining PSK that meets Japanese quality standards can be difficult, and the growing market for Coriolus supplements means that products of uncertain quality are widely available. This variability underscores the importance of using products from reputable manufacturers with documented quality control processes.
