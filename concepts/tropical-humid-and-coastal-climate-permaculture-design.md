## Tropical Humid and Coastal Climate Permaculture Design

Tropical humid and coastal climates share many design challenges with arid lands—wind, salt, and exposure—while having unique advantages including year-round production, a vast range of useful fruits, and greater frequency of night dews and mists.

## The Humid Tropics

### The Critical Role of Trees
"It is in this climate that trees are most essential. Trees grow easily from seed and cuttings, divisions and roots. Some trees are necessary at all times, even over crop."

Paulsen asserts that **"more than 75% of the soluble plant nutrients that are present in a certain area are held within the biomass of the growing plant community."** These nutrients are not absorbed into soil as [[query-how-to-grow-and-use-oca-in-temperate-climates]], but caught in the web of roots and fungal symbionts below the soil surface. Only "transitory' fertility is released by clear-felling, then leaching of nutrients and sterility of soil results.

### The Nutrient Pump
Paulsen reinforces the "nutrient pump" role of trees: both leaves and roots trap minerals from air and weathered stone, and leaves recycle to topsoil. Examples:
- **Prosopis cineraria**: penetrates to 30 m depth
- **[[acacia-tortilis]]**: spreads a root net 40–50 m
- **[[acacia-albida]]**: unique species deciduous in the wet season—does not shade crops when rain falls, but protects from clear sunny skies

### Zero Tillage in Tropics
Branch mulch from Prosopis, Acacia, and Ailanthus excelsa serves as hedgerow, windbreak, and fodder crop for sheep. Farmers in Rajasthan "maintain as many as 40 Prosopis cineraria per hectare, and the nutrients are detoured through livestock as an ideal way of using branch mulch."

### Key Tropical Systems
- Cotton grows under shea butter trees
- Lab-lab (Dolichos) beans under Raphia palm
- Fukuoka"s no-dig system using poultry as manurial sources (ducks as pest controllers and recyclers)

### Soil Characteristics
- Almost all nutrients are mobile, contained in the web of life
- Soil is fragile, easily leached, often deeply rotted
- Converts quickly to laterite or erosion gullies if cleared

### Design Imperative
"We must maintain high biomass by mixed perennial/tree/crop species in wet tropics." Even outside rainy/monsoon seasons, winds are very arid and damaging, carrying "the breath of the desert into gardens."

## Coastal Climate Design

### The Coastal Challenge
"Across the great unmodifiable plain of water, winds arrive at gale force, carrying salt and abrasive sand grains." The similarities to desert and high plateau country are obvious—shared species distributions among birds, plants, and insects demonstrate this.

### Salt Death vs. Wind Pruning
"It is the salt death of leaves rather than wind-pruning which accounts for the streamlined shape of trees near the sea" (Lillian Callow).

### Coastal Benefits
- Low incidence of snow and frost
- Generally more temperate climate
- Greater frequency of night dews and mists than dry inland

### Plant Defenses for Coasts
Species useful in both desert and coastal environments:
- Agaves, yuccas, palms, cacti
- Tough, woolly, thick-leaved, waxy, shiny, and needle-leaved trees
- "Gorky" barks and fibrous-stemmed species resist sand blast
- Self-mulching species (tamarisk, casuarina)
- Plant-water reservoirs (baobab, bottle trees, ice-plants, saltbushes)
- New Zealand coprosmas and hardy coastal pines (Araucaria, Callitris)

### Damp Area Hedgerows
Coastal ti-tree (Melaleuca): M. pubescens, M. hypericifolia, Leptospermum laevigatum, M. flavescens. May be pruned or unpruned; provide useful stakes and poles.

### Follow-On Useful Species (Behind Windbreak)
- Olive (Olea europa)
- Carob Bean (Ceratonia siliqua)
- Kaffir Plum (Harpephyllum caffrum)
- Bamboo species
- Quinces
- Stone fruits and citrus
- Nectarines appreciate mild winters

### Ground Covers and Creepers
- **Kennedia prostrata**: Good leguminous mulch (Ruth Geneff, Perth)
- **Mesembryanthemum, Dolichos, Tecoma, Tetragonia**: Prevent sand drift
- **Lupins** (annual and perennial): Thrive as coastal scrub, add soil fertility, bind loose sand
- On rocky exposed headlands: Quercus ilex, [[casuarina-equisetifolia]], Callitris spp.

### Acid Mulch from Pines
Needle-leaved pines (P. pinea, P. pineaster, P. radiata) thrive on hillsides and provide acid mulch that offsets alkalinity of desert and coastal gardens, or provides mulch for blueberry crops.

## Trellis as Climate Modifier

### Vertical Trellis
- Built at near right-angles to E, W, and N walls
- Separates functional space into recreational, garden, or service areas
- Prevents flow of cold winds along walls (acts as sun trap)
- Presents basic structure for vine crop
- Built on earth banks, tyre or stone walls, at rockery bases even more effective
- Can curve out from house corners or break up building facades

### Applications
- Institutions with converging building surfaces creating wind tunnels
- Roads of no thoroughfare value designed as clear wind tunnels
- Large boulders, plants, and trellis convert driveways to sheltered, sinuous access
- Block dust, cold, and noise as side effect

### Horizontal Trellis
- Shade windows from full summer sun
- Create overhead vine trellis to shelter tender crop in extreme climates
- Increase solar radiation on crop

### The Shadehouse Effect
"The refreshing coolness of a shadehouse in the hot Australian summer has to be experienced to be believed." Even tiny pools, a few ferns, and spray or drip of water increase the effect physically (by evaporation) and psychologically.

### Microclimate Around Corners
"By just rounding a corner, the climate alters from one suited to the soft herbs such as celery, parsley, chives (and strawberries) to a dry, hot site suited to the aromatic herbs, and producing many more oils."

## Structures and Energy

"It is self-evident that a diverse garden becomes a source of a variety of foods that do not need to be cooked, so reducing the need for cooking fuels." Nuts, fruits, greens, and many root crops need no cooking and are of equal or superior food value.

Fuel conservation strategies:
- One-pot cooking of food needing only reheating
- Cooking for larger community
- Reducing dependence on grains and pulses that require cooking
- Using raw fruits, nuts, and greens wherever possible
