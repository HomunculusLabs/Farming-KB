## Understanding Anorexia and Cachexia

**Anorexia nervosa** is a psychological condition involving lack of interest in or refusal to eat sufficient food to maintain healthy body weight. It is distinct from bulimia nervosa (binge eating followed by purging).

**Cachexia** (wasting syndrome) is loss of body mass that cannot be replaced through nutrition, presenting as generalized weakness, poor health, malnutrition, and emaciation. Cachexia is typically a secondary condition in chronic diseases including:
- End-stage cancer
- AIDS
- Terminal tuberculosis
- Multiple sclerosis
- COPD/emphysema

Cancer-related anorexia-cachexia involves weight loss from limited appetite, nausea, and weakness. AIDS-related anorexia involves appetite loss leading to fat and muscle tissue loss.

## Cannabis and Anorexia

Cannabinoids modulate eating behavior primarily through CB1 receptors in the brain. Anandamide and THC stimulate appetite and ingestive behaviors. Most studies focus on cancer, AIDS, and [[blesching-cannabis-alzheimers-disease]] contexts.

| THC 2.5 mg TID | 18 cancer patients (Cleveland, 1994) | 13/18 reported improved appetite | 3 |

## Strain-Specific Considerations

Patients often prefer [[cannabis-health-strains-sativa-indica]] with higher THC relative to CBD and CBN, as CB1 receptor activation by THC drives appetite stimulation. [[cannabis-thc-therapeutic-profile]] increases appetite, reduces nausea, and promotes weight gain. Individual needs vary considerably.

## Mind-Body Medicine and Anorexia/Cachexia

Research reveals significant psychological factors in anorexia nervosa:
- 13.7% of AN patients have comorbid PTSD from abuse (mostly sexual)
- Personality traits include elevated need for order, perfectionism, reward dependence, and constant self-doubt
- AN patients may have difficulty recognizing facial emotional expressions (similar to autism spectrum patterns)
- Extreme self-loathing may cause rejection of nurturing influences

Helpful factors for recovery include:
- Healing prior traumatic events
- Addressing shame through therapy or meditation
- Developing self-acceptance and self-compassion
- Challenging false beliefs about body image

## Supporting Herbs

Traditional appetite-stimulating herbs include anise, basil, cardamom, cinnamon, fennel, garlic, ginger, rosemary, and turmeric. Cannabis-infused oils or [[cervantes-cannabis-tinctures]] can be added to recipes. See [[cannabis-for-nausea-and-appetite-stimulation]].
