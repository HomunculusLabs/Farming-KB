## Ecological Functions of Fiber Plants

### Soil Building
- Deep-rooted fiber plants (hemp, flax, kenaf) break compacted subsoil
- Perennial fiber plants add organic matter annually
- Cover crop fibers (flax, hemp) can be tilled in as green manure

### Erosion Control
- Dense root systems hold soil on slopes (sisal, vetiver, miscanthus)
- Living fiber plant hedges filter runoff and slow water flow
- Fiber mats (jute, coir) stabilize disturbed soil during revegetation

### Windbreaks
- Tall fiber plants (hemp, kenaf, miscanthus) provide seasonal wind protection
- Perennial fiber grasses (giant miscanthus, bamboo) provide permanent windbreaks

### Wildlife Habitat
- Fiber plant stands provide nesting sites, cover, and food for birds and insects
- Hemp flowers are excellent bee forage
- Mixed fiber plantings increase biodiversity

## Integration into Permaculture Systems

### In Food Forests
- Fiber plants in the herbaceous layer
- Hemp as a nitrogen accumulator between fruit trees
- Bamboo in the understory and shrub layers
- Fiber vines (sisal, rattan) on trellises

### In Polycultures
- Flax and hemp in annual polycultures with food crops
- Kenaf as a companion plant (tall, provides shade and wind protection)
- Cotton interplanted with food crops in tropical systems

### In Water Systems
- Cattails and bulrushes in constructed wetlands
- Water-retting of flax and hemp in ponds
- Fiber plant buffers along streams and rivers

### In Zone 4-5 (Broadscale)
- Large-scale hemp or kenaf plantings for fiber production
- Fiber plant hedgerows as field boundaries
- Mixed fiber and fuel wood plantations

## Processing Considerations

Most fiber plants require processing before use:
- **Retting:** Controlled microbial breakdown of plant material to separate fibers (done in water or dew-retted in fields)
- **Scutching:** Mechanical removal of woody material from fibers
- **Hackling:** Combing fibers to align and clean them
- **Spinning:** Twisting fibers into thread or yarn

Processing can be done at household scale with simple tools or at community scale with shared equipment.
