# Mescaline: Peyote and San Pedro Cactus

Mescaline is described by D.M. Turner as the favorite of the traditional psychedelics. Intensity ranges from 3 to 8 for most experiences.

## Material

Mescaline is a naturally occurring psychedelic found in several cactus species, most notably Peyote (Lophophora williamsii) and San Pedro (Trichocereus pachanoi). Mescaline belongs to the phenethylamine family, distinct from the indole family (LSD, psilocybin, harmaline, DMT). Many synthetic "designer" psychedelics like MDMA and 2C-B are related to mescaline's chemistry.

The cacti contain a large variety of related psychoactive compounds beyond mescaline, producing experiences qualitatively different from pure mescaline. Peyote produces quite different effects from Trichocereus species.

**Psychoactive Trichocereus species include:**
T. bridgesii, T. cuzcoensis, T. fulvinanus, T. macrogonus, T. pachanoi, T. peruvianus, T. taquimbalensis, T. tersheckii, T. validus, T. werdermannius.

T. peruvianus is reported to be several times as potent by weight as other species.

## History

Peyote was used throughout Mexico to as far north as Texas. San Pedro was used in the Andes mountain region of South America. The earliest known depiction of San Pedro is on a stone tablet from Peru dating to 1300 B.C. Ritual objects containing Peyote images date back to 500 B.C.

Mescaline was the first psychedelic compound extracted and isolated (1896) and the first synthesized (1919). Aldous Huxley brought it to public attention through The Doors of Perception (1953). The Native American Church was formed in 1918 to preserve the right to use Peyote.

## Dosage and Preparation

- **Pure mescaline sulfate:** ~500 mg for full experience (~350 mg hydrochloride). Take in two half-doses 30 minutes apart to minimize stomach disturbance.
- **Peyote:** 3-4 buttons for sensitive individuals; 12-15 typically; more than two dozen not unheard of.
- **San Pedro:** ~10 inches long, 3 inches diameter section.

Cactus is most potent when harvested during hotter times of the year. Each Trichocereus plant may produce one or more highs per year. When harvesting, the top 4 inches of growing tip is cut off for replanting. The tip should be laid on its side in sun until the flesh calluses, then placed in half soil/half sand mixture.

**San Pedro Preparation:**
1. Cut out spines
2. Cut skin off in "V" strips from each rib into the central section
3. Dark green flesh next to skin is most potent; eat first by flattening strips and scraping flesh from skin
4. Central part is eaten around like corn on the cob; core is woody and inedible
5. Onset begins ~45 minutes after ingesting

**Extraction:** Psychoactive alkaloids can be extracted using water (mash and boil several hours) or alcohol. One effective method: boil just the skin with 3/8 inch of flesh, evaporate liquid at ~140 degrees F, powderize residue and pack in gelatin capsules.

## The Mescaline Experience

### Signature Characteristics
Turner considers mescaline the favorite of the traditional psychedelics, combining advantages of acid (lucid, penetrating, focused mind) with total relaxation (calmer than mushrooms, no metallic edge).

**Compared to LSD and Mushrooms:**
- More visual than either LSD or mushrooms
- Links with collective evolutionary consciousness more than synthetics
- Feels more "significant" than acid, which is more analytical
- Acid magnifies the mind; cactus connects with something ancient, spiritual, and personal
- Unique feeling that "the Gods or protective allies are smiling down"
- Produces actual auditory hallucinations (not just heightened hearing)
- Sharpened olfactory sense far beyond LSD or psilocybin

**Duration:** 6 to 14 hours depending on amount consumed.

**Coming back:** Smoother than other traditional psychedelics. Never produces the "drained of energy" or "neural overload" feeling common after intense acid trips. Allows for a more conscious and therapeutic return to regular consciousness.

## Legal Status and Conservation

Peyote is classified as a Schedule I controlled substance in the United States, though the Native American Church has protected religious use rights through the American Indian Religious Freedom Act (1978, amended 1994). San Pedro and other Trichocereus species are legal to grow in most countries but become controlled when prepared for consumption.

Wild peyote populations in Texas and northern Mexico have declined significantly due to overharvesting and habitat loss. The cactus grows extremely slowly — a single button may take 5-10 years to reach maturity from seed. Several Trichocereus species grow much faster and can serve as sustainable alternatives for ceremonial use.

## Peyote vs. San Pedro

**Peyote vs. San Pedro:** Peyote's first couple hours are dream-like, drifting, almost delirious. An emetic element causes nausea ~2 hours in. San Pedro usually produces a very smooth, flowing experience.
