# Crop Rotation in the Vegetable Garden

All vegetables have specific soil and mineral requirements.
By grouping together crops with similar needs and planting
them in a different place each year, you make better use of
resources and allow the soil to replenish lost minerals.
This practice is known as crop rotation.

## Benefits of Crop Rotation

Crop rotation helps guard against [[holmgren-mineral-balance-soil-fertility]] of the soil to be
maintained and significantly delays the build-up of disease
spores in the soil. It also ensures the most efficient use
of fertilizer and [[comparison-double-digging-vs-no-till]] with manure
incorporated into upper and lower levels, plus two
handfuls of blood, fish, and bone meal per square
yard/meter.

**Suitable crops:** Potatoes, carrots, beets, parsnips,
onions, shallots, leeks, garlic, tomatoes, zucchini,
squash, pumpkins, celery, Florence fennel, eggplants,
peppers, cucumbers, melons, celeriac, Hamburg parsley,
salsify, and scorzonera.

### Plot B: Light Feeders with Nitrogen
Single digging with blood, fish, and bone meal applied
two to three weeks before sowing.

**Suitable crops:** Peas, green beans, runner beans,
broad beans, Lima beans, soybeans, peanuts, [[growing-globe-artichokes]].

### Plot C: Lime-Loving Crops
Single digging with blood, fish, and bone meal plus
lime to bring pH to 6.5 to 7.0.

**Suitable crops:** Cauliflower, cabbages, nappa
cabbages, Brussels sprouts, broccoli rabe and broccoli,
kale, rutabagas, turnips, radishes, and kohlrabi.

## Rotation Schedule

Each year, prepare the plots and move each group to the
next plot so that two years elapse before any crop returns
to its original site. In a four-plot system, space must
also be left for permanent crops that do not rotate.

### Permanent Crops (Plot D)
Asparagus, globe artichokes (perennial), Jerusalem
artichokes, rhubarb, sea kale, and herbs. These occupy
their own space and do not rotate. In small gardens,
some can be grown in ornamental borders.

## Practical Considerations

Do not be too rigid in your rotation. Serious problems are
unlikely if you deviate slightly. Runner beans may need to
stay at the same end of the plot for sunlight. Cabbages
need more room than peas and beans. The idea that root
vegetables fork in manured soil is a myth; well-rotted
manure produces better results in all soils.
