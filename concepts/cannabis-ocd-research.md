## Safety and Interactions

Cannabinoid interactions with OCD medications require careful consideration. CBD may inhibit the metabolism of SSRIs (fluoxetine, sertraline, fluvoxamine, escitalopram) and clomipramine through CYP2C19 and CYP3A4 inhibition, potentially increasing blood levels and side effects. Dose adjustments of concurrent medications may be necessary.

THC may interact with SSRIs to increase the risk of serotonin syndrome, though this risk is considered low at typical THC doses. The combination may also enhance sedative effects, impair cognitive function, and reduce the therapeutic benefit of exposure-based therapies by interfering with the emotional processing necessary for treatment.

CBD is generally well-tolerated and may actually reduce some SSRI side effects (sexual dysfunction, nausea, insomnia). However, any changes to medication regimens should be made gradually and under medical supervision to avoid destabilization.

Cannabis should not replace established OCD treatments, particularly cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT) with exposure and response prevention (ERP), which remains the gold standard. Cannabinoid therapy should be adjunctive and may be most helpful for patients who have not achieved adequate response to conventional treatments. See [[cannabis-health-index]]
