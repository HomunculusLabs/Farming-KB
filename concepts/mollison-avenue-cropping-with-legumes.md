## Avenue Cropping with Legumes in Permaculture

Avenue cropping is a deliberate fuelwood, mulch, and soil
improvement crop integration system of great use in the
tropics but adaptable to any climate with good species
selection. The system is adapted from Ray Wijewardene's 1981
work on conservation farming in Sri Lanka, and represents a
core permaculture strategy for integrating tree crops with
annual cultivation.

## Core Principles

The essentials of avenue cropping are simple:

- Correct spacing to shade the ground fully but to allow
  cropping between the tree legumes.
- Lopping to leave stems 0.5-1.0 m above ground. Suitable
  tree legumes include tagasaste and Leucaena.
- Sowing of crop just before rain or irrigation, to beat the
  growth of weeds.
- A possible two-crop sequence, with mulch also returned from
  crop wastes.

A very similar system is used in Africa with Gliricidia, and
more recently in New Zealand with tagasaste as the green crop.
A parallel system, whether regular or irregular in ground plan,
is successful in establishing small trees that otherwise perish
from frost or open grass competition.

## Non-Legume Mulch Under Legume Canopy

With permanent high legume cover, mulch can be obtained all
year from a variety of non-legume hedge and understory species.
Nicotiana, Echium fastuosum, Lantana, cinnamon, clumps of
daisies, wild ginger, lemongrass, [[comparison-vetiver-grass-vs-bamboo-for-erosion-control]], Pennisetum,
and crop wastes from maize, Sesbania, soft ground legumes, or
comfrey provide constant mulch under high legume cover. This
reduces or eliminates the need for coppicing susceptible
legumes themselves.
