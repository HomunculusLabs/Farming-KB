## Spore Dispersal Mechanism

### Olfactory Attraction

The gleba (spore mass) is embedded in slime containing a cocktail of
volatile chemicals including:

- **Hydrogen sulfide** (rotten egg smell)
- **Formaldehyde**
- **Methylmercaptan**
- Unique compounds called **phallic acids**

This blend impersonates the smell of rotting flesh, making the stinkhorn
irresistible to flies and slugs from considerable distance. The smell is
so pervasive that collectors must wipe steamed glasses every few minutes.

### Insect Vectors

Flies swarm on the head, feeding on the fetid spore paste. Slugs glide
20 or more feet for the reward of the "cadaverous confection." Within a
few hours, the head is cleaned down to the dimpled white receptacle
surface and the shaft begins to wilt. Flies carry spores on their bodies;
slugs defecate viable spores at distant sites. Despite the ephemeral
display, the stinkhorn succeeds — flies and slugs carry spores whose
genes contain the information needed to produce more stinkhorns.
