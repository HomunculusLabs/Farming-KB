# Sequestrate Fungi — Truffles, False Truffles, and Hypogeous Fungi Sequestrate fungi produce enclosed fruiting bodies in which spores are not forcibly discharged into the air. They include true truffles (Tuber, Ascomycota), false truffles (multiple Basidiomycota genera), and various Zygomycetes. This lifestyle has evolved independently many times across the fungal kingdom. ## Definition and Characteristics Sequestrate fungi have: - Spores not forcibly discharged — enclosed within the fruiting body (gleba) - Peridium (outer layer) surrounding the spore-bearing tissue - Reduced or absent stipe (stem) - Reduced or absent cap (pileus) - Typically hypogeous (below ground), though some are emergent ## Distribution and Habitat - Usually associated with trees or shrubs (mycorrhizal or saprobic) - Found in forests, woodlands, and shrublands worldwide - Ectomycorrhizal sequestrate fungi occur wherever ectomycorrhizal hosts occur (including many tropical forests) - Some species in deserts (special mycorrhizal types with annual/perennial hosts) - Diversity highest in subtropical to temperate forests - Declines toward polar regions and at high elevations - Absent from Antarctica ## Evolution — From Mushrooms to Truffles Sequestrate forms evolved independently along several phylogenetic lines: ### Boletales - **Suillus** (mushroom with forcibly discharged spores from tubes) - → **Gastrosuillus** (modified mushroom; tubes contorted and plugged) - → **Truncocolumella** (chambers enclosed in peridium; vestigial stipe) - → **Rhizopogon** (chambers, no columella) - → **Alpova/Melanogaster** (gelatinous-solid mass of pockets with basidia and spores) ### Cortinariaceae - **Cortinarius** (mushroom) → **Thaxterogaster** (stipitate sequestrate) → **Cortinomyces/Hymenogaster** (further reduced) ### Russulaceae - **Russula** → **Macowanites** → **Gymnomyces** - **Lactarius** → **Arcangeliella** → **Zelleromyces** ### Tricholomataceae - **Laccaria** → **Podohydnangium** → **Hydnangium** ### Pezizales (Ascomycota) - **Humaria** (epigeous cup fungus) → **Geopora** (sequestrate chambers) - → **Stephensia** (hymenia embedded in solid tissue) - → **Phaeangium** (asci packed in pockets, not layers) - **Peziza** → **Hydnoplicata** → **Hydnotryopsis** (further reduced) ### Zygomycetes - May represent evolutionary progression in opposite direction - Glomales fossil record suggests extremely early occurrence - Present-day forms range from simple spore masses to complex fruiting bodies ## Spore Dispersal — Animal Vectors Loss of forcible spore discharge required alternative dispersal: - **Mammals:** Primary dispersers; dig up and eat sporocarps - **Arthropods and gastropods:** Secondary dispersers - **Birds:** Minor role ### Attraction Mechanisms - Aromatic compounds including pheromones attract animals when spores are mature - Each species produces unique aroma combinations ### Dispersal Process 1. Animal digs up sporocarp, often moves to feeding spot 2. Peridium (leathery/carbonaceous) consumed 3. Sterile tissues digested; spores pass through gut unharmed 4. Spore-containing feces weather; spores wash into soil 5. Spores contact receptive host feeder roots ### Specialized Adaptations - Some genera (Gummiglobus, Mesophellia) have powdery gleba with central columella - Animals peel off peridium, releasing powdery spores to air/fur - Others (Scleroderma, Elaphomyces) have thick peridia consumed by animals - Desert species: some have delayed spore maturation, or produce during any season ## Nutritional Value Sequestrate fungi contain: - Carbohydrates - Nonprotein amino acids - Proteins - Elevated mineral concentrations Some animals eat almost nothing but sequestrate fungi, demonstrating their nutritional value. ## Sampling and Survey ### Key Challenges - Cryptic — fruit below litter layer, some within mineral soil - Need 2-4 years of data before concluding a species is absent - Species-area curves stabilize very slowly (32 months, 1536 m^2 — still not stable) ### Recommended Protocols - **Line transects:** 50-m transects with 4-m^2 plots every 6 m (25 plots per stand) - **Raking:** Carefully rake litter and duff layer to 5 cm depth - **Sample area:** 800-1400 m^2 minimum for biomass studies - **Stratification:** Upper, mid, and lower slope strata on slopes ### Biomass and Community Structure - Sporocarp biomass varies; does not always reflect mycorrhizal root colonization - Some species produce solitary sporocarps; others caespitose (clustered) - Sporocarp numbers poorly correlated with dry weight - Plot frequency (presence/absence) is the most useful abundance measure ## See Also - [[sequestrate-fungi-diversity]]
