# Candida albicans Molecular Epidemiology and MLST

## Overview

[[candida-albicans]] is the most common cause of human candidiasis, a range
of infections from superficial mucosal conditions to life-threatening
systemic disease in immunocompromised patients. Understanding the
population structure, transmission dynamics, and evolutionary history of
C. albicans requires robust molecular typing methods. Multilocus
[[gadd-fungal-multilocus-sequence-typing]] (MLST) has become the internationally agreed reference
standard for molecular epidemiology of this important pathogen.

## MLST Scheme for Candida albicans

The C. albicans MLST scheme was initially developed by Bougnoux et al.
(2002) using six gene fragments, then adapted by Tavanti et al. (2003)
into a standardized seven-gene scheme comprising AAT1a, ACC1, ADP1,
MPIb, SYA1, VPS13, and ZWF1b. This scheme is accessible at
http://calbicans.mlst.net/. The database has catalogued genotypes for
over 279 isolates, of which 244 (87%) are unique sequence types (STs),
reflecting the high [[biodiversity-fungal-genetic-diversity]] of this species.

Each ST is defined by the concatenated string of integer-coded alleles
at all seven loci. Because MLST is based on DNA sequence rather than
fragment size or banding patterns, results are 100% reproducible between
laboratories and directly comparable across studies.

## Population Structure

Analysis of the C. albicans MLST database reveals a predominantly
clonal population structure with evidence of limited recombination. The
high proportion of unique STs indicates substantial genetic diversity
maintained in natural populations. MLST data have been used to address
questions about nosocomial transmission, distinguishing whether
recurrent infections represent relapse of the same strain or
reinfection with a new strain.

The diploid nature of C. albicans presents additional complexity for
molecular typing. Heterozygous positions within sequenced loci must be
accounted for, as the organism carries two alleles at each locus that
may differ. MLST schemes for C. albicans handle this by treating
heterozygous sites as additional polymorphic characters.

## Candida glabrata MLST

A parallel six-gene MLST scheme for Candida glabrata (FKS, LEU2, NMT1,
TRP1, UGP1, URA3) identified five principal genetic clusters.
Comparison with a previous Southern blot hybridization fingerprinting
method (CG6/Cg12 probe) confirmed that both methods defined the same
five major clusters. Notably, a geographical component to the
[[aspergillus-fumigatus-population-genetics]] of C. glabrata was detected.

However, the C. glabrata MLST scheme suffers from low resolution compared
with the fingerprinting method. The CG6/Cg12 probe discriminated
between all isolates within groups, whereas MLST did not. For questions
about microevolution and nosocomial transmission in C. glabrata, either
sequencing more genes or focusing on more highly variable loci (such as
microsatellites) is necessary.

## Caenorhabditis elegans as a Pathogenesis Model

C. elegans has emerged as a powerful model host for studying C. albicans
virulence. The nematode is killed by C. albicans in a process that
mimics key aspects of mammalian infection. Because the C. elegans genome
is fully sequenced, transcripts expressed by the fungus and the host
during infection can be separated and analysed, even when both are
present in cDNA libraries. This system allows identification of both
fungal [[fungal-virulence-factors-host-immune-evasion]] and host defence pathways activated during
infection.

The C. elegans model has been particularly valuable for studying
[[bloomfield-cryptococcus-neoformans-capsule-melanin-brain]] pathogenesis as well. The tractability of the
worm system enables high-throughput screening of fungal mutants for
virulence defects and testing of antifungal compounds.
