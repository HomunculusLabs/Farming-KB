# Mead Making Guide

Mead is the world's oldest fermented beverage -- honey wine made from honey, water, and yeast. It is one of the simplest alcoholic beverages to make at home and is an excellent use for homestead honey. With patience and minimal equipment, you can produce meads ranging from dry and complex to sweet and fruity.

## Mead Varieties

### Traditional Mead (Show Mead)
Honey, water, and yeast only. The purest expression of mead. Quality depends entirely on the honey used. The best traditional meads showcase the floral and aromatic qualities of the honey. Ferments to dry or semi-sweet depending on honey quantity and yeast choice.

### Melomel (Fruit Mead)
Mead with fruit added. The most popular mead category. Fruit can be added as fresh, frozen, pureed, or juice.

- Cyser: apple mead (honey + apple juice/cider)
- Pyment: grape mead (honey + grape juice)
- Berry meads: blackberry, raspberry, strawberry, blueberry, cherry
- Stone fruit meads: peach, plum, cherry, apricot
- Tropical meads: mango, pineapple, [[comparison-dragon-fruit-vs-passion-fruit-cultivation]]

### Metheglin (Spiced Mead)
Mead with spices and herbs added. Historical meads often contained [[medicinal-herbs-for-livestock]].

- Common additions: cinnamon, cloves, ginger, nutmeg, allspice, cardamom, vanilla
- Herbal additions: rosemary, thyme, lavender, chamomile, hops (braggot if also includes malt)

### Braggot
Mead made with both honey and malted grain. Bridges the gap between mead and beer. Uses a partial mash of grain with honey as the primary fermentable.

### Session Mead (Short Mead)
Low-alcohol mead (3-7% ABV) that is ready to drink in 2-4 weeks. Uses less honey, ferments quickly, and is carbonated like a beer. Excellent introduction to mead making.

### Bochet
Mead made from caramelized or burned honey. The honey is heated until it darkens (or burns slightly), creating toffee, caramel, and sometimes smoky flavors. An ancient style with deep complexity.

## Ingredients

### Honey
The single most important ingredient. The character of the honey directly determines the character of the mead.

- Raw, unfiltered honey produces the best mead -- retains aromatic compounds destroyed by pasteurization
- Light honey (clover, wildflower, orange blossom): clean, mild flavor. Good for traditional meads.
- Dark honey (buckwheat, wildflower, avocado): bold, earthy, sometimes bitter. Good for bold, complex meads.
- Varietal honey (tupelo, sage, meadowfoam, basswood): each has distinct floral character
- Avoid supermarket honey (often adulterated or heated) when possible

### Water
Use clean, chlorine-free water. Spring water or well water is ideal. If using tap water, filter or let it sit uncovered for 24 hours.

### Yeast
Yeast choice determines alcohol tolerance, flavor profile, and fermentation speed.

- Lalvin 71B: versatile, fruity, good for melomels, tolerates 14% ABV
- Lalvin D47: clean fermentation, enhances floral aromas, good for traditional meads, tolerates 14% ABV
- Lalvin EC-1118 (Champagne yeast): very high alcohol tolerance (18%), clean, neutral. Good for strong meads.
- Lalvin K1-V1116: fruity, vigorous fermenter, good for difficult fermentations, tolerates 18% ABV
- Red Star Premier Blanc (Cote des Blancs): enhances floral and fruit character, tolerates 14% ABV
- Bread yeast: works in a pinch but produces off-flavors. Not recommended.

### Nutrients
Honey lacks nutrients that yeast needs for healthy fermentation. Add yeast nutrient to prevent stalled fermentations and off-flavors.

- Fermaid-O: organic nitrogen source (derived from yeast hulls). Add at the start.
- Fermaid-K: DAP-free nutrient with vitamins and minerals. Add in staggered doses.
- DAP (diammonium phosphate): inorganic nitrogen source. Add in first half of fermentation only.
- Staggered nutrient additions (SNA): add nutrients in 3-4 doses throughout the first third of fermentation for best results.

### Acid
Mead often benefits from acid adjustment to balance sweetness.

- Tartaric acid: standard for mead. Add after fermentation to taste.
- Citric acid: bright, fruity acidity. Can be used alone or blended with tartaric.
- Acid blend: commercial mix of tartaric, malic, and citric acids.

## Equipment

Basic mead-making requires the same equipment as [[beer-brewing-basics]]:

- Fermenter: food-grade bucket or glass carboy (1-gallon jugs work for small batches)
- Airlock and stopper
- Sanitizer (Star San)
- Siphon and tubing
- Hydrometer and test jar
- Long-handled spoon
- Pot for heating water/honey

Optional but helpful: pH meter or strips, [[hamilton-greenhouse-heating-and-temperature-control]], degassing tool.

## The Mead Making Process

### Basic Traditional Mead (1 gallon)

1. Sanitize all equipment.
2. Heat 1/2 gallon of water to 160F (not boiling). Remove from heat.
3. Add 2.5-3 pounds of honey. Stir until fully dissolved.
4. Top up to 1 gallon with room-temperature water. Target starting gravity: 1.080-1.120.
5. Cool to 70F. Add yeast nutrient per package instructions.
6. Pitch yeast. Seal with airlock.
7. Ferment at 65-70F for 4-6 weeks.
8. Rack (siphon to a clean vessel) to leave sediment behind.
9. Age for 3-12 months. Mead improves dramatically with time.
10. Stabilize with potassium sorbate and potassium metabisulfite if backsweetening.
11. Sweeten to taste with additional honey if desired.
12. Bottle. Age at least 1 month before drinking.

### Melomel Variation
Add 1-3 pounds of fruit per gallon. Fresh or frozen fruit works (freezing breaks down cell walls for better juice extraction). Add fruit in primary fermentation (more fruit flavor integration) or secondary fermentation (clearer mead, more fruit aroma). Berries are added whole or crushed; [[query-how-do-i-grow-stone-fruits-like-peaches-plums-and-cherries]] should be pitted and sliced.

### Metheglin Variation
Add spices during secondary fermentation. Start with small amounts -- spices intensify over time. Typical additions per gallon: 1 cinnamon stick, 3-4 whole cloves, 1 inch fresh ginger (sliced), 1 vanilla bean. Taste monthly and remove when flavor is where you want it.

## Aging

Mead is one of the most age-worthy beverages. Unlike beer, which peaks in weeks to months, mead continues to improve for years:

- Session mead: drink young (1-3 months)
- Standard mead: 6-12 months minimum, peaks at 1-3 years
- Big/high-gravity mead: 1-3 years minimum, can improve for 5-10+ years
- Store in a cool, dark, stable location (55-65F ideal)
- Bulk aging in a carboy is better than bottle aging for young mead

## Common Problems

- Stalled fermentation: usually caused by insufficient nutrients, low temperature, or exceeding yeast alcohol tolerance. Add more nutrient, warm the mead, or pitch a more tolerant yeast.
- Medicinal/band-aid off-flavors: caused by chlorine in water or wild yeast contamination
- Sulphur/rotten egg smell: stressed yeast from [[cannabis-grow-nutrient-deficiency-problem-solving]]. Add nutrient and aerate.
- Too sweet: fermentation stopped early. Check gravity. May need to repitch with a more tolerant yeast.
- Too dry: can backsweeten after stabilizing with potassium sorbate and sulfite.
