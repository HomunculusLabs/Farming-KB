### Vitality Checking Schedule
Ideally, check the vitality of stored cultures every six months by:
1. Removing fragments of mycelium
2. Inoculating petri dishes
3. Once mycelium colonizes two-thirds of the media dish, selecting
   for strandy rhizomorphic growth
4. Re-inoculating fresh slants
5. Labeling and storing until needed

Growing out minicultures is also a good way to check a stored strain's
vitality and fruiting ability.

## The Buddy System

An excellent method for preserving cultures is passing duplicates of
each species or strain to a cultivator friend. Mushroom strains are
more easily lost than one might expect. Once lost, they may never be
recovered. This simple practice provides insurance against total
culture loss from equipment failure, contamination events, or
neglect.

## Mineral Oil Preservation

For cultivators with large strain libraries, a simple method extends
preservation periods significantly:

1. Allow mycelium to fully establish in a test tube slant
2. Apply a thin layer of sterile mineral oil over the live mycelium
3. Store at 37-41 degrees Fahrenheit

The mineral oil is non-toxic to mycelium, greatly reduces metabolism,
and inhibits water evaporation from the agar base.

### Research Evidence
Perrin (1979) demonstrated that all 30 wood-inhabiting species stored
under mineral oil for 27 years produced viable cultures. To
reactivate, slants were inverted to drain oil, then incubated at 77
degrees Fahrenheit. Within three weeks each slant showed renewed
growth and yielded uncontaminated cultures when subcultured.
