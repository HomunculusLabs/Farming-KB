# Stock Culture Preservation Methods

Once a pure strain has been created and isolated, saving it as a
"stock culture" is essential for long-term cultivation success. Stock
cultures, commonly called "slants," are media-filled glass test tubes
sterilized and inoculated with mushroom [[mushroom-cultivator-agar-media-formulas]] discussed by Stamets and Chilton

### Procedure
1. Mix the chosen [[growing-gourmet-agar-media-formulas-preparation]] formula
2. Fill test tubes one-third of the way
3. Plug with cotton and cover with aluminum foil (or screw on cap
   if tubes are of this type)
4. Sterilize in a [[double-layer-tinfoil-jar-covering-pressure-cooker-method]] for 30 minutes at 15 psi
5. Allow the cooker to return to atmospheric pressure, then take
   it into the sterile room before opening
6. Remove slants, gently shake to distribute liquified media
7. Lay tubes at a 15-30 degree angle to cool and solidify

### Inoculation and Labeling
Inoculate slants with a fragment of [[antimicrobial-compounds-mushroom-mycelium]]. Label each
tube with:
- The date
- Type of [[agar-medium-preparation]]
- Species name
- Strain designation

Make at least three slants per strain to insure against loss.

## Incubation and Storage

Incubate slants for one week at 75 degrees Fahrenheit (24 degrees
Celsius). Once the mycelium has covered a major portion of the agar's
surface and appears free of contamination, store at 35-40 degrees
Fahrenheit (2-4 degrees Celsius). At these temperatures, the
[[arbuscule-isolation-metabolic-activity-assays]] of most mycelia is lowered to a level where growth
and nutrient absorption virtually stop.

### Vitality Checking Schedule
Ideally, check the vitality of stored cultures every six months by:
1. Removing fragments of mycelium
2. Inoculating petri dishes
3. Once mycelium colonizes two-thirds of the media dish, selecting
   for strandy rhizomorphic growth
4. Re-inoculating fresh slants
5. Labeling and storing until needed

Growing out minicultures is also a good way to check a stored strain's
vitality and fruiting ability.

## The Buddy System

An excellent method for preserving cultures is passing duplicates of
each species or strain to a cultivator friend. Mushroom strains are
more easily lost than one might expect. Once lost, they may never be
recovered. This simple practice provides insurance against total
culture loss from equipment failure, contamination events, or
neglect.

## Mineral Oil Preservation

For cultivators with large strain libraries, a simple method extends
preservation periods significantly:

1. Allow mycelium to fully establish in a test tube slant
2. Apply a thin layer of sterile mineral oil over the live mycelium
3. Store at 37-41 degrees Fahrenheit

The mineral oil is non-toxic to mycelium, greatly reduces metabolism,
and inhibits water evaporation from the agar base.

### Research Evidence
Perrin (1979) demonstrated that all 30 wood-inhabiting species stored
under mineral oil for 27 years produced viable cultures. To
reactivate, slants were inverted to drain oil, then incubated at 77
degrees Fahrenheit. Within three weeks each slant showed renewed
growth and yielded uncontaminated cultures when subcultured.
