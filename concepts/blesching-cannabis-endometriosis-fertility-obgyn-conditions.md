# Cannabis and OBGYN Conditions

## Overview

In *The [[herbal-materia-medica]] describe consistent use of cannabis for menstrual difficulties, [[blesching-cannabis-morning-sickness]], postpartum bleeding, and menopausal symptoms. Modern research is beginning to validate these traditional applications through the lens of the endocannabinoid system's role in female reproductive physiology.

## Historical Context

Cannabis was employed across a wide range of cultures to manage reproductive health:

- **Sumerian physicians**: Among the earliest recorded medical use of cannabis for gynecological complaints
- **Egyptian medicine**: Continued tradition of cannabis-based treatments for women's health
- **Nineteenth-century materia medica**: Described cannabis as a stimulant for uterine muscle fibers, used to treat:
  - **Subinvolution**: The uterus's inability to return to normal size after delivery
  - **Menorrhagia**: Excessively heavy menstrual bleeding
  - **Dysmenorrhea**: Painful menstruation
  - General uterine pain reduction

A nineteenth-century physician's reference also noted cannabis's ability to reduce pain during childbirth, increase uterine contractions, modulate lactation, and reduce inflammation associated with vaginal pains or mastitis.

## Fertility, Miscarriage, and the Endocannabinoid System

### Anandamide and Egg Implantation

The endocannabinoid system plays a direct role in the earliest processes of conception. Key findings include:

- **Dose-dependent effects on implantation**: Low levels of anandamide (the body's endogenous cannabinoid) **enhance** egg implantation in the uterine wall, while higher levels **diminish** implantation success
- **CB1 receptors on fertilized eggs**: CB1 receptors are present on the fertilized egg itself and work together with uterine anandamide levels toward successful implantation
- **Vanderbilt University study (2002)**: Examined the role of the endocannabinoid system during normal pregnancy in mice, revealing that anandamide levels in the uterus and CB1 receptors on the fertilized egg coordinate to regulate implantation

### The Dual Nature Problem

These dose-dependent and opposing properties of cannabis are not unique to reproduction—they appear throughout [[endogenous-opioids-pain-modulation-endocannabinoid-interaction]], mood regulation, and immune function. This biphasic response pattern helps explain the seemingly contradictory historical references:

- Cannabis used to **prevent miscarriage** (low-dose effect supporting implantation)
- Cannabis used as an **early abortifacient** (high-dose effect inhibiting implantation)

### First Trimester Vulnerability

Once the fetus is implanted, it is most vulnerable to the impact of environmental substances during the first trimester. Blesching cautions that few modern studies have adequately examined cannabinoid effects on the developing fetus, and the historical record suggests both caution and opportunity for those seeking to use cannabis in the context of pregnancy planning.

## Endometriosis

### Condition Overview

Endometriosis involves the proliferation of endometrial cells (the interior lining of the uterus) outside the uterus. These displaced cells continue responding to hormonal changes, growing and forming adhesions throughout the pelvic cavity. The condition affects the ovaries, bowels, and surrounding tissue, causing:

- Generalized pelvic pain
- Painful periods (dysmenorrhea)
- Painful sexual intercourse (dyspareunia)
- Abnormal bleeding
- Possible infertility

The exact causes remain unclear, but retrograde menstruation (menstrual blood containing endometrial cells flowing backward through the fallopian tubes) is a leading hypothesis. Definitive diagnosis requires laparoscopy.

### Cannabis Research Evidence

**Combined [[synthetic-cannabinoid-pharmaceuticals-sativex-dronabinol-marinol]] WIN55,212-2 **abrogated growth** of endometriotic tissue
   - Demonstrated an **anti-proliferative effect** on implanted endometrial tissue
   - Suggests the endocannabinoid system may offer novel treatment approaches

### Strain-Specific Considerations

- WIN55,212-2 binds with higher affinity to CB2 than CB1 receptors
- Indica and indica-dominant strains generally present a lower THC:CBD ratio, favoring CB2 expression
- For endometriosis, indica-dominant strains may offer advantages due to enhanced CB2-mediated anti-inflammatory and anti-proliferative effects
