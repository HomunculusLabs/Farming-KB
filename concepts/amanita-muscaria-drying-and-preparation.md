# Amanita Muscaria Drying and Preparation Guide

The preparation of [[amanita-muscaria-chemistry-and-pharmacology]] for sacramental use is a process of critical importance that determines whether the mushroom will produce its desired psychoactive and healing effects or cause nausea, vomiting, and stupor. The fundamental principle is simple: fresh Amanita muscaria must never be consumed. All psychoactive and therapeutic properties require thorough drying, which converts the raw mushroom's chemistry into its usable form.

## Why Drying is Essential

### Chemical Transformation: Ibotenic Acid to Muscimol

Fresh [[amanita-muscaria-chemistry-and-pharmacology]] contains mainly ibotenic acid, with only a trace of [[muscimol-and-amanita-muscaria]] possibly present on the outer surface. The active principles are:

- **Ibotenic acid** (pantherin, [[agar]]in): alpha-amino-3-hydroxy-5-isoxazoloacetic acid, C5H6N2O4, MW 158.11, water-soluble, mp 150-152 C with [[decomposition]]. Its primary effect is pupil dilation and sedation, accounting for the coma-like sleep and stupor reported from fresh ingestion. On drying, ibotenic acid loses CO2 (decarboxylation) and becomes muscimol.
- **Muscimol:** 5-(aminomethyl)-3-hydroxyisoxazole, C4H6N2O2, MW 114.10, water-soluble, mp 175 C with decomposition. This is the prime chemical responsible for the dried herb's psychoactive action. It is 4 to 10 times as active as ibotenic acid. Muscimol is a potent conformational analogue of GABA (gamma-aminobutyric acid) and acts as a GABA receptor agonist.
- **Muscazone:** A trace variant produced when ibotenic acid is exposed to ultraviolet light (sun drying). Less potent than muscimol but more than ibotenic acid, with different effects.

The conversion is dehydration/decarboxylation: ibotenic acid minus CO2 equals muscimol. This is the single most important chemical fact about Amanita muscaria preparation.

### Hydrazine Content

All species of mushrooms when fresh, including Amanita muscaria, contain a form of hydrazine that has been used by NASA as rocket fuel. The amount varies widely from species to species and among individual mushrooms. This hydrazine is not desirable for consumption. Drying Amanita muscaria completely and aging for a few months removes this compound.

### The Siberian Difference

A persistent myth holds that Siberian Amanita muscaria is chemically different from North American specimens. This is incorrect. Chemical analyses of specimens from Italy, Holland, South Africa, Japan, North America, and Siberia revealed no significant differences in the fungus's chemistry. The real difference is that Siberian users thoroughly dried their mushrooms before consumption, while Westerners made themselves sick by consuming raw ones.

## Drying Process

### Method

1. Remove the cap from the stem
2. Slice the stem into quarters lengthwise; dry the cap whole
3. Place on a screen or rack with good air movement
4. Dry completely -- pieces should snap when bent ("cracker dry")
5. Temperature: room temperature to about 120 F maximum
6. Above 130 F, the mushroom begins to cook and the active chemicals start breaking down

### Equipment Options

- Conventional food dehydrator with adjustable temperature control works very well
- Oven drying at low temperature (do not exceed 120 F)
- Screen racks in a warm, dry, well-ventilated area
- Placed over a heating vent (monitor temperature)

### Effects of Different Drying Methods

- **Shaded cool drying:** Produces more muscimol than sun drying
- **Sun drying:** Produces a trace of muscazone (UV-induced conversion)
- **Heated drying above 130 F:** Breaks down active chemicals
- **Room temperature to 120 F:** Considered optimal

The key principle is consistency: as long as drying practices are consistent from one mushroom to another, the result is fairly predictable.

### Aging and Curing

Dried Amanita muscaria should be aged for 2-3 months before using to allow proper curing. Proper drying and curing is essential for both good taste and potency. The dried herb develops a delectable fragrance when sealed in plastic bags -- no wonder the ancient Aryans called it "Andhas" (literally "the flower" in the Rig Veda).

### Storage

- Place in sealable plastic bags
- Store bags in airtight plastic or glass containers
- Keep in a dry, cool, dark area
- The herb degrades rapidly if over-exposed to the environment
- Grinding into powder allows chemicals to escape and oxygen in -- do not powder until immediately before use

## Critical Warning: Carbonated Beverages

Do not consume any carbonated beverage (beer, soda, etc.) before or after consuming dried Amanita muscaria. [[muscimol-and-amanita-muscaria]] can apparently be reconverted into ibotenic acid in the presence of carbonated water. This reverses the effects of drying where ibotenic acid loses CO2 and becomes muscimol. The resulting effect is similar to fresh Amanita muscaria ingestion: nausea, vomiting, stomach cramps, stupor, coma-like sleep, excess salivation, and no hallucinogenic effect.

## Dosage Guidelines

### Active Range

The most pleasurable range for most people is from 3.5 grams (1/8 oz) to 10 grams (3/8 oz), equal to approximately 3 to 10 mg muscimol. People familiar with dried Amanita muscaria can detect faint effects from less than 0.5 grams.

### Upper Limit

Maximum 14 grams (1/2 oz, about 3 specimens) per 200 lb person. Smaller people should use proportionally less: 1/8 oz per 50 lbs body weight maximum. All medicinal herbs should be kept out of the reach of children.

### Effect Variables

The effect of a drug plant is subject to several variables:

1. **Absorption rate** of active principles into the body
2. **Physical size of plant pieces** -- smaller pieces release active principles faster, leading to more intense but shorter effects; larger pieces produce lighter, longer-lasting experiences
3. **Individual variation** in potency between specimens -- averaging across multiple plants from a batch produces more predictable results
4. **Preservation** -- all herbs decline in potency over time due to oxygen, moisture, light, and heat

### Onset and Duration

Based on the detailed testimonial of Donald E. Teeter, the progression at increasing doses follows a consistent pattern:

- **1/16 oz (1.75 g):** Slight euphoria, slight increase in ambient light, ~3 hours duration
- **1/8 oz (3.5 g):** General calm, visual illusions of color (especially blue brilliance), waviness of visual field, slight walking impairment, illuminated closed-eye patterns, drowsiness after ~2 hours, extraordinary closed-eye visual illusions, inner peace. Total: ~4.5 hours
- **1/4 oz (7 g):** Much more pronounced effects, quicker onset, room appears brilliant with light, walking like drunk (lying down mandatory after ~1-2 hours), intense Persian carpet closed-eye patterns, great inner peace, auditory effects
- **1/2 oz (14 g):** Very quick onset (minutes), extreme inner calm, indescribable illuminated visions, extremely complex auditory effects including narrated visions and "teachings," spiritual journey to a "kingdom of light." Physical rejuvenation lasting ~14 days after ingestion

## Natural Science: Botanical Description

### Cap
- 1.5 to 8 inches diameter (sometimes much larger, up to 12 inches)
- Color ranges from straw yellow through yellow-orange, orange, orange-red to bright blood red
- Surface smooth with white or off-white warts
- Margin may have striated surface [[spore-print]]lls below, especially in older specimens

### Gills
- Crowded, white, broad, free from stem with extremely fine hairy edges

### Spores
- 8-11 x 6-8 micrometers, ellipsoid, thin-walled
- Spore print: white

### Stalk
- 1.5 to 10 inches long, cylindrical, pure white to off-white
- Interior pithy when young, hollow in older specimens
- Full skirt-shaped white persistent veil, sometimes with velvety yellow edge
- Bulbous base covered in concentric ring scales

### Flesh
- White to off-white, firm

### Season and Habitat
- North America: June to fall frost
- California and Florida: December-January
- Some areas: spring fruiting
- Associated trees: pine, spruce, larch, fir, hemlock, birch, beech, maple, hazel, olive, and rarely oak
- Worldwide distribution wherever suitable forest habitat exists

### Color Varieties

From yellow to orange to red, all varieties seem to have the same effect and identical taste when dried identically. Several named varieties exist:

- A. muscaria var. muscaria (red fly agaric)
- A. muscaria var. formosa (yellow-orange fly agaric)
- A. muscaria var. alba (white variety)

The experts are not finished classifying all variations. The author recommends gathering only the yellow, orange, or red ones.
