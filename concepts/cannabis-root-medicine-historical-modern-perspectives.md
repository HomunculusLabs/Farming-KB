# Cannabis Root Medicine: Historical and Modern Perspectives

## Introduction

While cannabis flowers and leaves have dominated both traditional use and modern research, the roots of the cannabis plant have a long — though often overlooked — history of medicinal use. Cannabis roots contain a distinct phytochemical profile from aerial parts, with significantly lower cannabinoid content but notable concentrations of terpenes (particularly β-caryophyllene), friedelin, pentacyclic triterpenes, alkaloids, and other [[metzner-ayahuasca-therapeutic-potential]].

## Historical Use

### Ancient China

The earliest documented medicinal use of cannabis roots comes from ancient Chinese texts. The *Shennong Ben Cao Jing* (circa 2700 BCE, compiled in Han dynasty) describes cannabis root preparations for treating gynecological disorders. The *Tang Ben Cao* (659 CE) specifically recommends cannabis root for "difficult parturition" (prolonged labor) and for expelling retained placenta.

The classical Chinese medical text *Ben Cao Gang Mu* (1596 CE) by Li Shizhen provides more detailed descriptions: cannabis root (大麻根, *da ma gen*) is characterized as sweet, neutral, and non-toxic. It is prescribed for:

- **Dysmenorrhea and amenorrhea**: To promote menstrual flow
- **Postpartum complications**: Retained placenta, postpartum hemorrhage
- **Joint pain and rheumatism**: Applied as a poultice
- **Inflammation**: Topical application for swelling and abscesses
- **Urinary obstruction**: Root decoction as a diuretic

### Ayurvedic Medicine (India)

In Ayurveda, cannabis root (*bhang ki jad*) is classified differently from the aerial parts (*bhang*). Root preparations are used in:

- **Joint diseases** (Vata disorders): As a paste or decoction applied topically
- **Gastrointestinal complaints**: Root bark decoction for diarrhea and dysentery
- **Skin conditions**: Topical application for wounds, burns, and inflammatory skin diseases
- **Fever**: Root preparations as antipyretic agents

## Phytochemistry of Cannabis Roots

### Terpenes

β-Caryophyllene (BCP) is the dominant [[cannabis-terpene-profile]] of roots is distinct from aerial parts: roots lack monoterpenes like myrcene and limonene that dominate the flower aroma.

### Triterpenes

Cannabis roots are rich in pentacyclic triterpenes:

- **Friedelin**: A friedelane-type triterpene ketone with anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties. Friedelin is found at concentrations of 0.3–0.8% dry weight in roots but is absent from aerial parts.
- **Epifriedelinol**: The alcohol derivative of friedelin, with reported hepatoprotective and anti-inflammatory activities.
- **Friedelin-3-one derivatives**: Several novel friedelin derivatives have been isolated from cannabis roots.

### Alkaloids

Low levels of alkaloids have been detected in cannabis roots, including:

- **Choline**: A quaternary ammonium compound with various physiological roles
- **Tryptamine derivatives**: At trace levels, distinct from the tryptamines found in [[aact-tannins-terpenes-phenolic-compounds-wood-composting-ingham]]**: Caffeic acid derivatives and other phenolics with antioxidant activity
- **Phytosterols**: β-sitosterol, stigmasterol, and campesterol
- **Sugars and polysaccharides**: Root extracts contain significant soluble sugars that may contribute to wound-healing properties through humectant and matrix-forming effects
- **Minerals**: High concentrations of potassium, magnesium, and calcium
