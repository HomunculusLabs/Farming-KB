# Ultra-Low-Cost Integrated Farming

## Traditional Integration: Animals and Crops Together

Before the introduction of factory farming, all traditional farming households kept cows, pigs, or chickens as integral parts of the farming system. Animal feed was completely self-sufficient, and animal manure was valued as essential fertilizer. Animals were treated as farmers' friends and family members, not as industrial production units. This integrated system created closed nutrient loops where crop residues fed animals, animal manure fertilized crops, and both contributed to household self-sufficiency.

JADAM advocates restoring this integrated approach while applying modern understanding of animal welfare and ecological principles.

## Organic Livestock Farming: The No-Feces-Removing Method

JADAM has developed a revolutionary approach to livestock farming that eliminates the need for manure removal — one of the most labor-intensive aspects of animal husbandry. The system works as follows:

**Housing design:**
- South-facing shelter for passive solar warming
- Natural ventilation through side openings and roof vents
- Galvanized steel roof promotes air circulation
- Sunlight reaches the floor through design features
- Rice husk, straw, and sawdust bedding on the floor

**The biological cycle:**
- Animal manure falls onto the bedding material
- JADAM Indigenous Microorganism Solution (JMS) is sprayed on the floor and fed to animals
- Microorganisms decompose the manure mixed with bedding
- Decomposed material becomes feed again — animals eat it after fermentation
- The process is almost free of smell and flies
- Manure only needs to be removed about once per year

**Benefits:**
- Eliminates daily manure management labor
- Produces valuable composted material annually
- Reduces feed costs through recycling
- Provides comfortable living conditions for animals
- JMS increases feed conversion ratio when diluted 20:1 and fed to animals
- Significantly reduces odor

This system originated from the Yamagishi movement in Japan and was further developed by Hankyu Cho. It solves most problems associated with modern livestock farming while maintaining animal welfare and producing high-quality organic fertilizer.

## Concerns About Livestock Manure

JADAM issues an important warning about imported livestock manure. In countries that are heavy users of antibiotics for livestock, manure often contains antibiotic residues that contaminate soil. JADAM absolutely advises against using livestock manure of unclear origin, as habitual application of contaminated manure inhibits soil microbial activity. This is one reason why the no-feces-removing method is superior — you know exactly what your animals have been fed and what is in their manure.

For farmers who do not keep their own animals, JADAM recommends:
- Using humus soil instead of livestock manure (mined from ancient bogs)
- Making fertilizer from crop residues and wild grass through anaerobic fermentation
- Collecting leaf mold from forests as a pure organic fertilizer
- Using food waste and human manure as fertilizer sources under controlled fermentation

## Aquaculture Integration

While JADAM's primary focus is on crop and livestock integration, the principles of low-cost, self-sufficient farming extend naturally to aquaculture. Small-scale aquaculture can be integrated into farming systems through:

- **Rice-fish culture**: Growing fish in flooded rice paddies, where fish eat pests, fertilize rice with their waste, and provide an additional protein crop
- **Pond fertilization**: Using JMS and organic materials to fertilize fish ponds, promoting natural food production
- **Duck-rice integration**: Ducks in rice paddies control pests and weeds while providing manure
- **Aquatic plant cultivation**: Water hyacinth and duckweed can serve as animal feed or compost material

## Multi-Function Systems

JADAM farming naturally creates multi-function systems where each element serves multiple purposes:

**Cover crops serve simultaneously as:**
- Green manure (organic fertilizer when cut and decomposed)
- Soil temperature regulators
- Mineral miners (deep roots pull up subsoil minerals)
- Compaction breakers (root penetration loosens compacted layers)
- Pest management (biodiversity support)
- Moisture retainers (living mulch effect)
- Weed suppression through competition

**Microorganism solutions serve simultaneously as:**
- Soil inoculants for crop production
- Livestock feed supplement (improves digestion)
- Livestock shelter treatment (odor control)
- Disease suppression agent (competitive exclusion)
- Decomposition accelerator for organic matter

**Crop residues serve simultaneously as:**
- Liquid fertilizer ingredient (when fermented)
- Mulch material (when left on soil surface)
- Carbon source for soil organisms
- Cover crop seed propagation medium
- Livestock feed supplement

## The Nutrient Cycle

JADAM's integrated farming aims to close nutrient cycles that modern agriculture has broken:

1. **Crops absorb nutrients** from soil and sea water amendments
2. **Crop residues return nutrients** to soil through surface application and mulching
3. **Animals consume crop residues** and convert them to manure
4. **Manure decomposes with microorganisms** into bioavailable nutrients
5. **Microorganisms mineralize organic matter** into plant-available forms
6. **Sea water replenishes leached minerals** from the broader geochemical cycle
7. **The cycle repeats** with minimal external inputs

## Economic Benefits of Integration

Integrated farming provides multiple income streams from the same land and labor:
- Primary crop sales
- Animal product sales (eggs, meat, milk)
- Value-added preserved products
- Seed sales from saved varieties
- Compost or fertilizer sales from excess production
- Reduced input costs through self-sufficiency

The diversification also reduces economic risk — if one crop fails or market prices drop, other farm products provide income stability.

## Scaling Integrated Systems

JADAM's integrated approach works at any scale:
- **Home gardens**: A small garden with a few chickens creates a complete micro-cycle
- **Small farms**: 1-5 hectares with mixed crops, chickens, and pigs can achieve full self-sufficiency
- **Larger operations**: Multiple integrated units can be managed while maintaining low input costs

The key principle at any scale is that each farm should produce as many of its own inputs as possible and sell products at competitive prices due to ultra-low production costs.
