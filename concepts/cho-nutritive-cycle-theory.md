# [[natural-farming]] system. Developed in the 1960s, this theory provides
a framework for understanding the nutritional needs of plants throughout
their entire growth cycle and guides farmers in applying the right inputs
at the right time in the right amounts. Rather than following a fixed
fertilizer schedule, the Nutritive Cycle Theory aligns nutrient
management with the natural physiological stages of plant development.

## Core Principles

The theory is built on the observation that plants have different
nutritional requirements at different stages of growth. Just as human
nutritional needs change from infancy through adulthood, a plant's
demand for specific nutrients shifts as it progresses from germination
through [[cannabis-vegetative-growth-stage]], flowering, fruiting, and maturation. By
matching inputs to these changing needs, farmers can maximize both yield
and quality while minimizing waste and potential harm to the plant.

## The Four Growth Stages

### Stage 1: Germination and Early Growth

During the initial phase of a plant's life, the seedling relies heavily
on energy reserves stored in the seed. As the first true leaves emerge,
the plant requires nitrogen to support rapid cell division and leaf
formation. In natural farming, this stage is supported primarily through
seed treatment with [[fermented-plant-juice]] (FPJ). The emphasis is on building a strong
root system and establishing beneficial microbial communities in the
rhizosphere. Too much nitrogen at this stage can produce leggy, weak
seedlings susceptible to disease.

### Stage 2: Vegetative Growth

The [[comparison-cannabis-vegetative-stage-vs-flowering-stage]] is characterized by rapid leaf and stem expansion.
Nitrogen demand peaks during this period as the plant builds its
photosynthetic machinery. Natural farming practitioners apply FPJ
liberally during this phase, along with LAB ([[fish-amino-acid]] (FAA) may also be
introduced to provide a balanced amino acid profile. The goal is to
produce sturdy, healthy foliage capable of supporting future fruit
production without becoming overly succulent, which would invite pest
and disease pressure.

### Stage 3: Flowering and Fruiting

As the plant transitions to reproductive mode, its nutritional needs
shift dramatically. Nitrogen demand decreases while demand for
phosphorus, potassium, and calcium increases. Phosphorus supports
energy transfer processes essential for flower development, while
potassium regulates water movement and fruit quality. Calcium is
critical for cell wall integrity in developing fruits. Natural farming
inputs applied during this stage include Water-Soluble Calcium (WCA),
Water-Soluble Phosphoric Acid, and [[chos-natural-farming-nutritive-cycle-theory]]

## Practical Considerations

Successful implementation of Cho's Nutritive Cycle Theory requires attention to
several practical factors including environmental conditions,
resource availability, and timing. Careful monitoring and
adaptive management help optimize outcomes across varying
conditions. Integration with other system elements enhances
overall effectiveness and creates beneficial synergies that
improve resilience and productivity over time.
