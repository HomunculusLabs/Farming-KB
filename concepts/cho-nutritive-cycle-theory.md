The Nutritive Cycle Theory is the foundational scientific framework of
Dr. Cho Han Kyu's Natural Farming system. It states that plants and
livestock need different nutrients during different stages of growth.
Like humans, plants require different kinds of food at different life
stages. Just as a baby can only eat baby food, young plants can only
be fed with nutrients that are appropriate for their age.

## Core Principle

Dr. Cho holds that providing nutrients in excess to plants is not
needed and will only bring disease. Natural Farming follows the
scientific approach of correctly using the right material at the right
amount and at the right stage, so that crops or livestock can reach
their optimum growth. The Nutritive Cycle guides farmers in obtaining
the best results from their crops and avoiding disease through proper
nutrient management.

The theory emphasizes intrinsic factors (the inner conditions of the
plant) over extrinsic factors (external conditions like weather and
soil). Current agricultural methods unilaterally emphasize external
conditions and underestimate the inner conditions intrinsic to plants.
The best harvest cannot be expected when only external conditions are
emphasized, as these constantly change every year.

## Three Growth Stages

All crops pass through three distinct growth stages, each with unique
nutritional requirements:

### 1. Vegetative Growth Stage

The vegetative growth stage is the period when plants develop their
roots and shoots through time until they mature. During this stage,
plants consume Carbohydrates (C) and convert them to Nitrogen (N),
which is their main growth requirement. The plant is building its
structural framework, focusing energy on leaf and stem development.

### 2. Cross-Over Period (Morning Sickness)

This is the transitional stage in which plants begin flowering in
preparation for reproduction. Dr. Cho uses the term "Morning Sickness"
to liken the state of plants in the cross-over period to pregnant women
who crave sour foods during pregnancy. Plants undergo a similar phase
in which they need "Sour Nutrients," which come in the form of
Phosphoric (P) compounds. This is a critical juncture where the
nutritional demand shifts from nitrogen to phosphorus.

### 3. Reproductive Growth Stage

Reproductive growth is the period from flowering to ripening of fruits
when the plant begins storing carbohydrates in fruits or other storage
organs (accumulative growth). For proper color development of fruits,
Potassium (K) is also needed. During this stage the plant shifts from
building structure to producing and filling its reproductive organs.

## Soil Fertility Dynamics

The same argument holds for the fertility of soils. Even though soils
may be proper in one growth stage, the degree of fertility may change
in the next growth stage. This means crops are generally deprived of
the same qualitative and physiological requirements across their growth
stages: Young, Adult, and Old. Understanding these shifting
requirements is key to the Nutritive Cycle approach.

## Application in Natural Farming

Natural Farming methodology is based entirely on the Nutritive Cycle
Theory, which guides the farmer on what inputs to apply, how much, and
how often. The system applies the principle of interdependence in which
people work with nature instead of surmounting or undermining it.

### Strengths of the Nutritive Cycle Approach

- **Higher Yield**: Strictly follows the Nutritive Cycle Theory, using
  accurate amounts of substances at the precise moment, nourishing the
  soil with the right nutrients when plants or animals need them.
- **Low Cost**: Lessens the farmer's financial burden with homemade
  materials and resources from the farm itself.
- **High Quality**: Better-quality crops and livestock result from
  non-usage of harmful chemicals.
- **Adaptability**: NF can be replicated in any region by substituting
  ingredients and products with natural resources distinct to each
  region or ecosystem.
- **Respect for Life**: Gives utmost respect and care to crops and
  animals following the natural cycle of life.

## Comparison with Conventional Farming

Natural Farming differs fundamentally from conventional agriculture:

| Conventional Farming | Natural Farming |
|---------------------|-----------------|
| Chemical fertilizers | Nutritive Cycle Theory |
| Mechanical tillage | Nature's tillers (earthworms, microbes) |
| Expensive technology | Simple microbe-based treatments |
| Pesticides to kill pests | Natural inputs to distract pests |
| Planting more crops per area | Proper spacing for formless nutrients |

## Key Insight

What is most important for farmers is to understand the exact growth
physiology of each stage and to encourage plants to fully take
advantage of the most proper conditions. The Nutritive Cycle Theory is
not merely a fertilization schedule but a complete philosophy of
working with nature's rhythms to achieve sustainable, high-quality
production.
