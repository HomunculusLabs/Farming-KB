# Cannabis and Prostate Cancer

**Source:** The Cannabis Health Index, Uwe Blesching, PhD (2017)
**Updated:** 2026-04-19

## Evidence-Based Therapeutic Potential

- **Total Studies Reviewed:** 3
- **CHI Value:** 3

## Overview

The prostate gland is part of the male reproductive system, located at the outlet of the urinary bladder, producing and storing seminal fluids. Prostate cancer is one of the most common cancers in elderly males yet is generally very slow-growing. Enlargement exerts pressure on the urethra, making urination difficult. Other symptoms include sexual dysfunction, bloody urine, painful urination, and regional pain. A significant eight-year study of nearly 30,000 males found that increased sexual activity (more than 21 ejaculations per month) correlated to a 30% decreased risk of developing prostate cancer.

Prostate cancer is the second most common cancer in men worldwide and the fifth leading cause of cancer death. While many prostate cancers are indolent (slow-growing) and may never cause symptoms, aggressive forms can metastasize to bone and other organs. Current screening methods (PSA testing, digital rectal examination) have limitations including overdiagnosis of indolent tumors. Treatment options include active surveillance, surgery, radiation therapy, hormone therapy (androgen deprivation therapy), and chemotherapy.

## Cannabis and Prostate Cancer

- **Naples, Italy (2000):** Anandamide inhibited the growth of both breast cancer and prostate cancer cells in the laboratory.

- **Wisconsin, USA (2004):** Prostate cancer cells contained significantly higher expressions of both CB1 and CB2 receptors, suggesting a possible novel approach to treating prostate cancer.

- **Debrecen, Hungary (2009):** For the first time, CB1 was identified in epithelial and smooth muscle cells of the healthy human prostate. Researchers strongly argued CB1 possesses a promising future role in prostate cancer treatment.

## Mechanisms of Action

Cannabinoids interact with prostate cancer biology through several pathways:

- **CB1/CB2 receptor upregulation in cancer cells:** The Wisconsin study demonstrated that prostate cancer cells express significantly higher levels of both CB1 and CB2 receptors compared to healthy prostate tissue. This upregulation is significant because it means that cannabinoids may selectively target cancer cells while having minimal effects on normal prostate cells. The reason for this receptor upregulation is not fully understood but may represent an endogenous protective mechanism.

- **Anandamide-mediated growth inhibition:** The 2000 Naples study showed that anandamide directly inhibits prostate cancer cell proliferation. Anandamide activates both CB1 and CB2 receptors, and its growth-inhibitory effects likely involve multiple downstream signaling pathways including p38 MAPK and p21-mediated cell cycle arrest.

- **CB1 in normal prostate function:** The Hungarian study was the first to identify CB1 receptors in healthy human prostate epithelial and smooth muscle cells. This finding suggests that the [[cannabis-endocannabinoid-system]] plays a normal physiological role in prostate function, which may be disrupted in cancer. Understanding this baseline function is important for predicting the effects of cannabinoid therapy.

- **Androgen receptor interaction:** Some research suggests that cannabinoids may modulate androgen receptor signaling, which is the primary driver of prostate cancer growth. Androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) is a mainstay of prostate cancer treatment, and cannabinoid effects on this pathway could complement existing therapies.

- **Anti-angiogenic effects:** Cannabinoids may inhibit the formation of new blood vessels that prostate tumors need for growth. This anti-angiogenic activity involves suppression of VEGF and other pro-angiogenic factors.

- **Epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) inhibition:** Some studies have suggested that cannabinoids may inhibit EMT, a process by which prostate cancer cells become more invasive and metastatic. Inhibition of EMT could slow disease progression and reduce metastatic spread.

## Additional Research

Research on cannabinoids and prostate cancer has expanded since the original review:

- Studies have shown that CBD inhibits prostate cancer cell proliferation and invasion in laboratory models, with effects mediated through multiple pathways including GPR55 (a non-classical cannabinoid receptor).

- Research has demonstrated that CB2 receptor activation reduces prostate cancer cell migration and invasion, suggesting a role in preventing metastasis.

- Pre-clinical studies have investigated combining cannabinoids with standard prostate cancer treatments (enzalutamide, docetaxel), with some showing synergistic effects.

- The role of GPR55 in prostate cancer has gained attention, as this receptor is overexpressed in prostate cancer tissue and may contribute to cancer cell proliferation.

## Dosage and Administration

For prostate cancer patients considering cannabinoid therapy, several factors are relevant:

- Oral preparations (capsules, tinctures) provide systemic cannabinoid delivery
- [[cannabis-cannabinoid-receptor-pharmacology]] considerations include the fact that prostate tissue penetration of cannabinoids has not been extensively studied
- [[cannabis-concentrates-and-extracts]] may provide standardized cannabinoid ratios
- Patients must be monitored for potential [[cannabis-drug-interactions-and-contraindications]] with androgen deprivation therapy (leuprolide, degarelix), anti-androgens (enzalutamide, bicalutamide), and chemotherapy (docetaxel)
- [[cannabis-dosing-guidelines]] should be followed with medical supervision

## Strain-Specific Considerations

Prostate cancer cells contain significantly higher CB1 and CB2 receptor expressions. Anandamide and THC bind relatively equally to both CB1 and CB2. Sativas and sativa-dominant strains with higher THC:CBD ratio provide increased similarity to anandamide's cannabinoid profile.

Strains rich in [[cannabis-minor-cannabinoids]] like CBG and THCV may provide additional anti-cancer effects. The [[cannabis-terpene-profile]] of selected strains can enhance therapeutic outcomes through entourage effects, with beta-caryophyllene (anti-inflammatory, CB2 agonist) and pinene being particularly relevant.

## Complementary Approaches

- **Lycopene:** Found in tomatoes, watermelon, and pink grapefruit; multiple studies show reduced prostate cancer risk with higher lycopene intake

- **Green tea (EGCG):** Has demonstrated anti-prostate cancer activity in epidemiological and pre-clinical studies

- **Pomegranate:** Pomegranate extract has shown potential for slowing PSA doubling time in prostate cancer patients

- **Saw palmetto:** May support prostate health, though evidence for cancer prevention is limited

- **Regular exercise:** Associated with reduced prostate cancer risk and improved outcomes in diagnosed patients

## Limitations of Current Research

All reviewed studies were pre-clinical (laboratory). No clinical trials have evaluated cannabis specifically for prostate cancer treatment. The relationship between cannabinoid therapy and androgen deprivation therapy (the backbone of advanced prostate cancer treatment) is not well understood. Cannabis should not replace conventional prostate cancer treatment, including active surveillance, surgery, radiation, hormone therapy, and chemotherapy.
