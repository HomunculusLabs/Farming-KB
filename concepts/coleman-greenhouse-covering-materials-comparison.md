# Greenhouse Covering Materials: A Comparative Analysis

## Overview

The choice of covering material is one of the most consequential decisions in
a [[coleman-aphid-management-winter-greenhouse-nitrogen-flushing]] is fundamentally a "sun-run" system
— its productivity depends on maximizing [[cervantes-wind-protection-outdoor-cannabis]]. This is the
  material Coleman recommends for the winter harvest system. It blocks enough
  wind to prevent desiccation without significantly reducing the solar energy
  that drives the daily freeze-thaw cycle.

- **Heavyweight row covers** (approximately 1.5 oz/sq yd or more) can block
  up to 50% of available light while providing greater thermal insulation.
  However, Coleman's trials have shown that the heavy covers provide minimal
  practical benefit in this system. The reason is straightforward: this is a
  sun-run system. The additional warmth retained at night by the heavier cover
  is more than offset by the reduced solar gain during the day. The soil
  stores less heat, and the net effect is negligible or even negative.

The insight is that insulation value, which dominates material selection in
heated greenhouses, is largely irrelevant in a system driven by daily solar
rewarming.

## PVA Row Covers from Japan

Among the more specialized materials Coleman has tested are PVA (polyvinyl
alcohol) row covers imported from Japan. These offer exceptional light
transmission — 90-92% — while providing approximately 1°F of additional
nighttime warmth compared to standard lightweight spun-bonded covers. However,
they cost roughly five times as much as conventional row covers, making their
cost-benefit ratio questionable for most operations. Coleman reports on them
as a point of thoroughness but does not recommend them as a standard choice.
