# Fungal Biofilms and Quorum Sensing

**Source:** Gadd, Watkinson & Dyer, *Fungi in the Environment* (2006), Chapters 3, 10 (Read; Liu et al.)

## Overview

Filamentous fungi form complex multicellular structures through hyphal aggregation, producing tissues analogous to biofilms in bacteria. These aggregated structures include fruit bodies, sclerotia, rhizomorphs, and mycelial cords. Quorum sensing (cell density-dependent signaling) regulates key developmental transitions in fungi, including dimorphism, [[neurospora-crassa]] has 28 morphologically distinct cell types, all derived from aggregated hyphae. Examples of hyphal aggregates include:
- **Fruit bodies:** Ascomata, basidiomata, conidiomata
- **Survival structures:** Sclerotia (resist adverse environments)
- **Transport structures:** Rhizomorphs and mycelial cords (long-distance transport of water and nutrients)
- **Reproductive structures:** Spore-producing aggregates

### Septal Pore Occlusion
Physical isolation of adjacent hyphal compartments is a prerequisite for cell specialization. Specialized septal pore plugs (e.g., basidiomycete dolipore septa) may play roles in selective cell-cell communication during multicellular development.

## Autoregulatory Signals and Quorum Sensing

### Extracellular Chemical Signals
The least understood biotic signals influencing filamentous fungi are the autoregulators -- extracellular chemical signals produced by the fungus itself. They play critical roles in regulating:

**Colony Organization:**
- Hyphal avoidance (preventing self-overgrowth)
- Growth directionality (away from colony center)
- Hyphal homing (prelude to hyphal fusion)

**Developmental Regulation:**
- Germination self-inhibitors
- Germ-tube emergence patterns from spores
- Regulation of asexual and [[fungal-life-cycles-sexual-reproduction-metamorphosis]]
- Choice between sexual and asexual reproduction
