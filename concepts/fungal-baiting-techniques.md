### Baiting for Predaceous Fungi

Fungi that capture nematodes, amoebae, or other microorganisms can be baited by adding the target prey organism to soil or water samples. Nematode-trapping fungi develop adhesive networks, constricting rings, or other capture structures in response to nematode presence.

### Baiting for Wood-Decay Fungi

Wood-decay fungi can be baited by placing small pieces of specific wood types (oak, pine, beech) into soil or forest floor samples. The wood type and decay state of the bait influence which species are attracted.

## Protocol for Soil Baiting

A general protocol for baiting fungi from soil samples:
1. Collect soil samples from the target site and horizon
2. Prepare selected bait materials (clean, cut to appropriate size, sterilize if needed)
3. Place baits on the soil surface or embed them within the soil in Petri dishes
4. Add sterile water to maintain moisture (or use water suspensions for aquatic baits)
5. Incubate at appropriate temperature (often room temperature or in situ temperature)
6. Examine baits daily to weekly for fungal colonization
7. Transfer fungal growth to isolation media when detected
8. Subculture to obtain pure isolates

## Selective Baiting with Antibiotics

When bacterial contamination is problematic, antibiotics can be added to the baiting medium:
- **Penicillin-streptomycin**: broad-spectrum antibacterial
- **Chloramphenicol**: effective against many bacteria without inhibiting most fungi
- **Acidification**: lowering pH can suppress bacterial growth without affecting many fungi

## Advantages of Baiting

Baiting offers several advantages over standard isolation methods:
- Targets specific ecological or functional groups
- Can isolate species missed by plating methods
- Exploits natural fungal behaviors (chemotaxis of zoospores)
- Can detect rare species by providing their preferred substrate
- Useful for inventory of particular fungal guilds
- Often simpler and less equipment-intensive than molecular methods

## Limitations

Baiting methods have constraints:
- Selective for specific groups; not comprehensive
- May miss species that do not respond to available baits
- Success depends on proper bait selection and preparation
- Quantitative interpretation is difficult
- Incubation time varies among target groups
- Some fungi may be overrepresented if their bait is too favorable
