Secretion occurs through a combination of mechanisms. Hydrophobic
cannabinoids and terpenes diffuse across the plasma membrane into the
subcuticular space. Vesicle-mediated transport may also contribute, with
lipid vesicles fusing with the cell wall to release their contents. The
cuticle acts as a semi-permeable barrier, retaining non-volatile
cannabinoids while allowing volatile terpenes to slowly diffuse through
to the atmosphere, creating the characteristic cannabis aroma.
The secretory cavity's hydrophobic environment is critical for proper
cannabinoid storage. Disruption of the cuticle (through mechanical
handling or solvent extraction) releases the resin and exposes
cannabinoids to oxygen, UV light, and moisture, accelerating
degradation.

  promotes trichome density and resin viscosity. High humidity reduces
  terpene volatility and may increase susceptibility to botrytis (gray
  mold) in resin-laden floral tissue.
- **Wind and physical stress**: Moderate air movement stimulates
  trichome development. Mechanical agitation of flowering plants
  increases capitate-stalked trichome density on adjacent tissues.
## Trichome Harvest Indicators
Experienced cultivators use trichome appearance as a primary harvest
timing indicator. The progression from clear to cloudy to amber correlates
with the ratio of THCA to CBN and the overall psychoactive profile:
- **70-80% cloudy, 10-20% clear**: Peak THC content with minimal
  degradation. Optimal for maximum psychoactive potency.
- **50% cloudy, 50% amber**: Balanced THC and CBN profile. Associated
  with more sedative, body-heavy effects. Preferred by medical patients
  seeking analgesic and sleep-promoting properties.
- **70%+ amber**: Significant THC degradation. Lower psychoactive
  potency but higher CBN content. CBN demonstrates independent sedative
  and anticonvulsant properties.
