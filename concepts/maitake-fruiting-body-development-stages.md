# Maitake Fruiting Body Development Stages

Grifola frondosa (maitake, hen of the woods) undergoes a distinctive series of morphological stages during fruiting body development. Understanding these stages is essential for successful cultivation, as each phase requires specific environmental conditions for optimal yields.

## Overview of the Growth Sequence

The complete growth cycle of G. frondosa consists of three overlapping phases:

1. **Spawn run** (vegetative mycelial growth and maturation)
2. **Primordia initiation** (formation of initial fruiting structures)
3. **Fruiting body development** (expansion into mature mushroom clusters)

Growth and differentiation is a continuous process where each stage merges with the next, with overlapping descriptions sometimes given in the literature.

## Fruiting Body Development Phases

### Primordia Stage

Primordia formation begins after the spawn run is complete. Key characteristics include:

- Dark gray amorphous masses develop on the mycelial surface within sealed bags that have ventilation
- These masses develop into dark grayish-black mounds with a globular, ball-like appearance
- The primordia are often dotted with exudates, sometimes appearing light yellow
- **Important**: Do not wipe off the exudates — they are a normal part of development
- Primordia then develop further, leading to formation of fruiting bodies

### The Brain Stage

As the dark grayish-black primordia continue to grow, convoluted folds begin to appear on the surface, creating a morphology that closely resembles a brain. This is a transitional stage between the compact primordia and the more elaborate cauliflower stage that follows.

### The Cauliflower Stage

Further growth involves unfolding of the convoluted surface folds into overlapping young pilei (caps) formed in a cluster. This stage is characterized by:

- Elongation of lateral stems, each bearing a young pileus (cap) on its upper portion
- Stems that are highly and repeatedly branched, sharing a short and chunky base
- The overall appearance closely resembles a cauliflower
- The color of the fruiting body becomes lighter, progressing toward almost white
- Overlapping petals (caps + lateral stems) become increasingly visible

### The Cluster Flower Stage

This is the final and most recognizable stage of maitake development:

- Overlapping fan-shaped caps develop along the elongated stems, creating the characteristic "cluster flower" appearance
- The color of the mushroom becomes progressively lighter throughout the intricate morphogenesis, evolving from dark grayish-black primordia
- Depending on the strain, mature maitake mushrooms may appear light gray, grayish white, or light brownish yellow
- The cluster resembles a flower in full bloom

## Environmental Conditions for Each Stage

### Primordia Initiation (in enclosed bags)

| Parameter | Condition |
|-----------|-----------|
| Temperature | 10–15.6°C (North American strains) |
| Relative humidity | 95% |
| CO₂ | 100–500 ppm |
| Light | 2000–5000 lux (upper limit, higher than other stages) |
| O₂ | 0.2–0.5% |
| Duration | 5–10 days |

### Fruiting Body Development (in opened bags)

| Parameter | Condition |
|-----------|-----------|
| Temperature | 13–16°C (up to 18°C) |
| Relative humidity | 85–90% |
| CO₂ | 500–1000 ppm |
| Light | <1000 lux |
| O₂ | <0.1% |
| Duration | 10–14 days |

Lower temperatures and higher light levels are used for primordia initiation compared to standard Chinese practice. North American strains tend to prefer slightly cooler conditions.

## Soil Casing as an Alternative Approach

G. frondosa can also be cultivated using soil casing, which provides an alternative pathway for fruiting:

- Mycelia begin to aggregate on the soil surface in preparation for primordia initiation
- Young maitake clusters thrive in the growth environment provided by soil casing
- The cauliflower and cluster flower stages proceed normally under soil casing conditions
- Prolific cluster flower production has been documented with this method

## Practical Cultivation Notes

- Bags should remain sealed during primordia initiation with microfilters for breathing
- When primordia reach 2.54–5.08 cm (1–2 inches) in diameter, the bags should be opened
- After opening, transfer to a fruiting facility and wait 2–3 days before opening the tops of the bags
- Total mushroom production time from inoculation is approximately 3½ months
- All morphological stages can be achieved through careful management of growth parameters specific to each developmental stage
