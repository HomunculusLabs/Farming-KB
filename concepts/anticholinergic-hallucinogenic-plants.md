# Anticholinergic Hallucinogenic Plants

## Overview

Atropine and scopolamine are tropane alkaloid drugs found in several
plant species, primarily within the Solanaceae (nightshade) family.
These compounds are competitive antagonists of muscarinic cholinergic
receptors and act as central nervous system depressants. Unlike
classical hallucinogens such as LSD or [[psilocybin]], anticholinergics
produce a distinct syndrome of toxic delirium often called "deliriant"
effects. The frighteningly adverse overdose profile usually precludes
common recreational abuse of these plants.

See also: [[amanita-muscaria-pharmacology]]

## Pharmacology

### Mechanism of Action

Atropine and scopolamine competitively block muscarinic acetylcholine
receptors throughout the body, affecting both the peripheral nervous
system (pupil dilation, dry mouth) and the central nervous system
(delirium, hallucinations, cognitive impairment).

### Approved Medical Uses

Despite toxicity at recreational doses, these are important medications:

- **Pupil dilation**: Used in ophthalmology for fundoscopy
- **Anesthesia**: Treating bradycardia, decreasing bronchial and
  salivary secretions during surgery
- **Organophosphate poisoning**: Atropine is a primary treatment for
  organophosphate pesticide poisoning and nerve agent exposure
- **[[blesching-cannabis-motion-sickness]]**: Scopolamine is a common treatment, available
  in transdermal patches

## Key Plant Species

### Datura stramonium (Jimson Weed)

[[atropa-belladonna]] contains both atropine and scopolamine. The name
"belladonna" (beautiful lady) comes from the historical use of berry
juice to dilate pupils for cosmetic purposes. It was introduced to
the US as an ornamental flower.

### Hyoscyamus niger (Henbane)

[[hyoscyamus-niger]] contains scopolamine without atropine. It has a long
history in European folk medicine and as a toxic additive to
beverages. Introduced to the US as an ornamental.

### Mandragora officinarum (Mandrake)

Mandrake contains both atropine and scopolamine. It has rich
mythological associations with magic and fertility rituals across
Europe and the Mediterranean. Introduced to the US as an ornamental.

## Deliriant Effects and Intoxication

### Acute Intoxication Symptoms

In overdose, anticholinergics induce a toxic delirium characterized by:

- **Anterograde amnesia**: Inability to form new memories
- **Confusion and disorientation**: Profound cognitive impairment
- **Dissociation**: Feeling disconnected from reality and self
- **Hallucinations and delusions**: Often frightening and
  indistinguishable from reality
- **Excited, giddy affect**: Paradoxical euphoria and restlessness
- **Impaired coordination**: Loss of motor control
- **Blurry vision**: With increasing pupillary dilation
- **Dry mucous membranes**: Difficulty talking or swallowing

### Overdose and Lethality

Overdose can be lethal with symptoms including fever, tachycardia,
cardiac arrhythmias, seizures, coma, and death. Reports of bizarre
self-injury are not uncommon because intoxication lasts for hours or
days, and the delirium induces dramatic disconnection from reality.
