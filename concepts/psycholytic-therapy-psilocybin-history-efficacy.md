# Psycholytic Therapy with Psilocybin — History and Efficacy

Psycholytic therapy is a form of psychotherapy in which low-to-moderate doses of hallucinogenic substances are administered as adjuncts to the therapeutic process. The term "psycholytic" literally means "mind-dissolving" — referring to the dissolution of psychological defenses that allows repressed traumatic material to enter conscious awareness. [[psilocybin]] was widely regarded by practitioners as the **most effective and safest** hallucinogen for this purpose.

## Origins (1950s)

Upon discovery of the unique properties of hallucinogenic agents during the 1950s, LSD gained widespread popularity as a therapeutic agent, while mescaline remained obscure. Patients under the influence of these substances confronted long-repressed traumatic events that began to surface and enter conscious awareness — in some cases, traumatic experiences repressed since early childhood. Medical records indicate that many patients not only remembered but **actually relived** traumatic experiences in therapeutic settings.

[[valentina-pavlovna-wasson]], R.G. Wasson's wife, wrote presciently after her 1957 self-experiment with [[psilocybin-mushrooms]] that the substance would benefit the treatment of alcoholism, drug addiction, mental problems, and ease the suffering of terminally ill patients — predictions that were validated by subsequent research.

## Why Psilocybin Was Preferred Over LSD

H. Leuner considered psilocybin the **most effective alkaloid** for psychotherapeutic use, even after the development of substituted phenethylamines like MDMA. Advantages of psilocybin:

1. **Extremely low toxicity** — LD₅₀ in mice: 280 mg/kg; human effective dose: 0.02 mg/kg. Safety margin of several hundred times
2. **Precise dosing** — dosages below 10 mg can be measured accurately; effects within this range are both therapeutically efficient and controllable
3. **Shorter duration** — effects last ~5 hours on average (vs. 8–12 hours for LSD), allowing more convenient use
4. **No hangover** — unlike other hallucinogens, psilocybin almost never induces hangovers the day after
5. **Gentler profile** — described as "friendlier" than LSD, not as fierce in exposing subconscious traumas
6. **Clean pharmacokinetics** — rapidly metabolized and eliminated from the body

Dosage equivalence: **10 mg psilocybin ≈ 100 µg LSD** (within a specific low dosage range, effects were largely similar except for shorter duration).

## Two Modes of Hallucinogen-Assisted Therapy

### Psycholytic Therapy (Europe)

- **Lower doses** administered over **multiple sessions**
- Focus on gradual dissolution of psychological defenses
- Exploration of psychodynamic level of behavior
- Patients progress through layers of "character armoring"
- Typical spacing: at least one week between sessions (tolerance develops quickly)

### Psychedelic Therapy (United States)

- **High doses** administered in **fewer sessions**
- Maximizes emergence of transpersonal states of consciousness
- Often resembles mystical experiences
- Particularly effective for terminal patients facing death anxiety
- Stereo music significantly deepens the intensity

Both modes share the principle that hallucinogens are **catalysts** — not substitutes for therapeutic skill. They shorten the duration of psychoanalytic treatment by surfacing problem areas faster and with greater clarity.

## Efficacy Data

### Statistical Outcomes

A comprehensive review of the literature found:

- **28 authors** described **1,600 therapy cases** across **42 investigations** (1953–1965)
- Course of illness judged **very grave and chronic** in 68% of cases
- **Up to 70%** success rate ("good and substantial improvement"), especially for:
  - Neurotic anxieties
  - Depressive-neurotic dysphorias
  - Neurotic character disorders
- **Follow-up study of 82 patients:** 65% showed **long-term, clinically significant improvement**

### Specific Programs

**M. Hausner (Czechoslovakia):**
- Used LSD with 350+ patients in ~3,000 sessions over many years (up to 1974)
- Also tested *[[psilocybe-bohemica]]* with outcomes comparable to LSD
- Found that LSD "flashbacks" between sessions were temporary manifestations of issues reaching consciousness — they disappeared as therapy progressed and did not recur after conclusion

**S. Roquet (Mexico, 1967–1974):**
- Developed "psychosynthesis" — hallucinogens as adjunct to therapy, influenced by shamanistic practices
- Groups of 10–20 patients received diverse substances (psilocybin mushrooms, peyote, LSD, ololiuqui)
- Stereo music + slide/film projectors presenting emotionally charged images (love to horror)
- Treatment duration: 18 months to 2 years, with many hours of drug-free therapy plus ~one hallucinogen-assisted session per month
- **85% success rate** treating neurotic character disorders

### Treatment of Terminal Patients

The most remarkable application involved treating terminal patients to ease death anxiety and reduce severe pain:

- LSD was the most widely studied substance for this purpose
- DPT (dipropyltryptamine) — structurally similar to psilocybin, active only by injection — was also used
- In many cases, symptoms **disappeared entirely**, with relief continuing after acute drug effects wore off
- Stereo music significantly enhanced the depth of transpersonal experiences
- S. Grof provided compassionate analysis of numerous successful treatments

### Fischer's "Letter Resynthesis" Experiments

R. Fischer demonstrated that under psilocybin influence, participants could **"re-synthesize" partially displayed words** — reading significantly more words when only letter stumps were visible. This confirmed that psilocybin's effects are not merely "psychotic" but rather **initiate new information processing mechanisms** between different interactive brain areas.

### Diagnostic Applications

In Czechoslovakia during the 1960s, psilocybin was used as a **diagnostic tool for brain damage**:

- Visual hallucinations were almost completely **suppressed** in patients with CNS lesions affecting visual-sensory functions
- Psilocybin **potentiated** a variety of neurological processes, effectively revealing latent paralyses and subtle CNS damage
- Preferred over LSD because dosing was simpler, effects were shorter, and patients experienced less fatigue

## The Grof Model: Stages of Psychedelic Experience

S. Grof analyzed 5,000 experimental LSD protocols and identified a consistent progression through types of experience with repeated administrations:

1. **Abstract** — geometric patterns, color fields
2. **Aesthetic** — nature imagery, artistic visions
3. **Psychodynamic** — emergence of repressed memories, emotional conflicts, perinatal material
4. **Perinatal** — experiences related to birth trauma
5. **Transpersonal** — mystical/religious experiences, archetypal imagery, experiences of unity and eternity

Grof concluded that hallucinogenic substances are **non-specific triggers** causing a sequence of altered states of consciousness. The individual's personality and the experimental setting ("[[set-and-setting]]") significantly shape the nature of the experience. Even "real" somatic symptoms like nausea or vomiting could often be controlled through psychological intervention.

## Ideal Treatment for Panic Reactions

Based on extensive British experience with *P. semilanceata* intoxications, J. Francis and V.S.G. Murray proposed:

1. Move the patient to a **darkened, beautiful room**
2. **Watch** them until effects subside
3. If necessary, administer **tranquilizers** such as diazepam
4. **Do NOT** pump the stomach (too drastic, often ineffective, can provoke aggressive behavior)

## Political Suppression and Revival

### The Crackdown

About 30 years ago, legislation severely restricted hallucinogen availability. The entire pharmacological class became subject to **more severe taboos** than truly addictive substances with legitimate medical uses (morphine-type drugs). The 1970s saw psycholytic therapy discontinued worldwide.

### Swiss Revival (1988–1993)

The Swiss Medical Society for Psycholytic Therapy succeeded in re-legitimizing hallucinogen-assisted therapy:

- 1988: Permission granted to selected psychotherapists
- Strictly defined conditions and limited time
- Legal psycholytic therapy continued until 1993
- Efforts to renew the permit were underway at time of publication

Gartz argued that psilocybin is likely to stand out as the **most valuable, beneficial, and least risky** substance for therapeutic applications as research resumes.

## Key Insight: Non-Addictive Nature

Comprehensive investigations have long proven that psilocybin, mescaline, and LSD:

- Are **not physically addictive**
- Cause **no withdrawal symptoms** of any kind
- **Tolerance develops rapidly** with daily use (users become unable to experience any effects)
- Most long-term users **reduce frequency** spontaneously due to the powerful transpersonal nature of the experiences
- Clinical trials are spaced at least one week apart to allow tolerance to reset
