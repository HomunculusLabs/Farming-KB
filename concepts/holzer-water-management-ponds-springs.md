# Holzer Water Management (Ponds and Springs)

Water management is a foundational principle of [[comparison-natural-swimming-pool-vs-conventional-pool]] pools

## Working with Different Soil Types

### Coarse, Stony Soil (Krameterhof)

- Separate coarse from fine material by shaking into tall mounds with excavator
- Fine material goes to terrace surfaces (fertile soil) or pond bases (watertight)
- Coarse material stabilises walls and banks

### Loam and Clay

- Material does not need separation (except humus layer, which must always be carefully removed, separated, and put back)
- Sealing ponds is easy due to high water retention
- Challenge: stabilising walls of deeper ponds (loam absorbs water quickly, becomes muddy, takes long to dry)
- Cannot be shaken into tall heaps -- will sink and crack under pressure as soil moisture is squeezed out
- Must dry and harden before bearing additional weight
- Separated material must have time to stabilise before further work

## Landscape Design for Water Retention

### Retaining Water

- [[comparison-raised-beds-vs-hugelkultur]] store water
- In dry areas, water must never be drained away -- land should be designed to retain it
- In wet areas, drainage prevents acidification of raised beds
- Ditches store surface water and runoff, letting moisture seep into surrounding soil

### Microclimate Effects

- Large water bodies balance temperature fluctuations on adjacent land
- Evaporation increases local moisture levels
- Ponds in front of niches reflect sunlight, creating warm suntraps
- Water gardens with fish and plants that prefer warm conditions profit from stones that absorb and release heat

## Avoiding Drainage

Holzer strongly advises against draining wetlands. Instead, he cultivates plants suited to damp conditions: orchids, aquatic plants, and marsh plants. A [[holzer-climate-zone-microclimate-creation]]
