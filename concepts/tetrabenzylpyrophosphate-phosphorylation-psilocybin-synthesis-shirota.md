## Overview

The synthesis of [[psilocybin]] from [[indole]] ring. This
phosphorylation step has historically been the most challenging aspect of
[[psilocybin-synthesis]] due to difficulties in regioselectivity, purification,
and yield. Shirota, Hakamata, and Goda (2003) identified the tetrabenzyl-
pyrophosphate method as the most practical approach for large-scale
synthesis, leading to the discovery of a novel [[shirota-glyoxalylamide-side-chain-construction-psilocin-synthesis]]. Several phosphorylation methodologies were evaluated by the
researchers:

### Methods That Failed

Most standard [[phosphorylation-methods-psilocybin-production-comparison]] did not adequately consume psilocin:
dibenzyl phosphite showed incomplete conversion, phosphoryl iodide suffered
from reproducibility issues, and conventional phosphate coupling reagents
gave poor yields with difficult purification.

### The Pyrophosphate Method Selected

The pyrophosphate method using tetrabenzylpyrophosphate and n-butyllithium
was selected for large-scale production due to two practical advantages:

1. **Easy handling**: The reagents are relatively stable and can be prepared
in advance
2. **Reagent stability**: Unlike some phosphorylation reagents that degrade
rapidly, tetrabenzylpyrophosphate maintains consistent reactivity

## Reaction Conditions

The phosphorylation proceeds as follows:

1. Psilocin is dissolved in anhydrous tetrahydrofuran (THF)
2. The solution is cooled to -78 degrees Celsius under inert atmosphere
3. 2.6 M n-butyllithium in n-hexane is added dropwise
4. After stirring for 5 minutes at -78 degrees C, tetrabenzylpyrophosphate
is added all at once
5. The reaction mixture is stirred for 1 hour while warming to 0 degrees C
6. The crude O,O-dibenzyl phosphate derivative (compound 7) is isolated

## The Zwitterionic Discovery

### Accidental Formation

During the standard aqueous workup to remove excess reagents, the researchers
observed an unexpected phenomenon. The crude reaction mixture, when dissolved
in dichloromethane (CH2Cl2) and allowed to stand, deposited a white
precipitate at the origin on TLC plates. This precipitate was insoluble in
CH2Cl2 but soluble in methanol.

Nichols and Frescas had previously reported a similar observation, concluding
that hydrolytic cleavage of one O-benzyl group was occurring. However, Shirota
and colleagues undertook a full structural characterization of this material.

### Structural Elucidation

The precipitate (compound 8) was isolated by preparative reversed-phase HPLC
and analyzed by comprehensive NMR spectroscopy:

- **1H NMR** (CD3OD): Two distinct benzyl groups with one showing a
characteristic high-field shift (delta H 4.56) indicating nitrogen
attachment
- **13C NMR** (CD3OD): Confirmed benzyl groups and psilocin core
- **31P NMR** (CD3OD): Confirmed phosphate moiety presence

### The Intramolecular Transfer

The key structural insight came from 2D NMR experiments (HMBC and NOESY):

The HMBC spectrum revealed that one benzyl group had migrated from an oxygen
to the nitrogen of the N,N-dimethylamine group, forming a quaternary
ammonium ion. The NOESY spectrum supported these linkages, confirming that
compound 8 was a **zwitterionic N,O-dibenzyl phosphate derivative** — an
intramolecular rearrangement product in which one benzyl group transfers from
the phosphate oxygen to the dimethylamine nitrogen.

### Simplified Conversion

The researchers discovered that the conversion from the O,O-dibenzyl
phosphate (7) to the zwitterionic N,O-dibenzyl derivative (8) could be
achieved simply by suspending the worked-up reaction mixture in CH2Cl2
