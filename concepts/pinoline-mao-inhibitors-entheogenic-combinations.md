## Pinoline, MAO Inhibitors, and Entheogenic Plant Combinations

## Overview

The intersection of endogenous neurochemistry and entheogenic plant pharmacology reveals a compelling framework for understanding both ancient sacramental brews and naturally occurring altered states of consciousness. At the center of this framework is **pinoline**, a naturally occurring monoamine oxidase inhibitor (MAOI) produced by the pineal gland, which acts in concert with endogenous DMT to facilitate visionary and psychic experiences.

This endogenous mechanism mirrors the pharmacological logic behind some of the world"s most powerful entheogenic preparations, particularly **Ayahuasca** and its analogues, and provides a biochemical key to one of the oldest mysteries in ethnobotany: the identity of the Vedic sacrament known as **Soma**.

The concept that visionary experiences can arise both from within the brain (through endogenous neurochemistry) and from without (through entheogenic plant combinations) bridges the gap between ancient spiritual wisdom and modern neuroscience, suggesting that the two traditions have been describing the same fundamental phenomenon from different vantage points.

## Pinoline as an Endogenous MAO Inhibitor

Pinoline (6-methoxy-tetrahydro-beta-carboline) is a beta-carboline compound synthesized naturally in the human pineal gland. As a monoamine oxidase inhibitor, pinoline functions by temporarily disabling the MAO enzymes—primarily MAO-A—that normally break down tryptamine compounds in the digestive tract and brain. This inhibition is pharmacologically significant because it allows otherwise orally inactive compounds, such as DMT, to reach the central nervous system in psychoactive concentrations.

Without MAO inhibition, DMT and similar tryptamines are metabolized almost immediately upon entering the body, rendered inactive before they can cross the blood-brain barrier. Pinoline effectively removes this metabolic barrier, allowing tryptamines to circulate and bind to serotonin receptors in the brain, producing their characteristic visionary effects.

The implications of pinoline extend beyond mere biochemistry. Research suggests that pinoline may be directly responsible for **naturally occurring psychic experiences**, including dream states, mystical visions, and even what some researchers have described as UFO contact visualizations. These experiences, rather than being purely pathological or hallucinatory, may represent the brain's own entheogenic capacity—moments when the pineal gland's natural chemistry replicates the effects of carefully constructed plant brews. The fact that the human body produces its own MAO inhibitor suggests that visionary states are not anomalies but rather an integral part of human neurochemistry, one that ancient cultures may have learned to amplify through botanical means.

## The DMT-Pinoline Connection to the Pineal Gland

The pineal gland, long revered as the "third eye" in esoteric and spiritual traditions, occupies a unique position in neurochemistry. Located deep within the brain, this small endocrine gland has been associated with [[papaver-somniferum]]*, the opium poppy. During somnambulistic episodes, DMT and pinoline excretion by the pineal gland is believed to be at its peak, reinforcing the hypothesis that the visionary states described in ancient religious texts may have been referring to chemically specific experiences that the human brain is capable of generating on its own.

The significance of this connection cannot be overstated: the very word "somnambulism" contains the root shared by both Soma and somniferum, suggesting that ancient cultures may have recognized a relationship between sleep-states, visionary experience, and their sacramental substances—a relationship that modern neurochemistry is only now beginning to validate.

This endogenous DMT-pinoline pairing serves as the biological template for understanding how entheogenic plant combinations achieve their effects. The ancient brewers of sacramental substances may have been, in essence, replicating and amplifying a natural brain process through botanical means—discovering plants that contained the same classes of compounds the pineal gland produces endogenously.

## The Five-Plant Soma Theory

A unified theory proposes that Soma was not a single plant but a **carefully calibrated combination of five plants** (four specific species and a fifth group of green plants), each contributing a specific pharmacological function to the overall experience. Five plants depicted in one picture, when understanding why each plant could play an important part in inducing the ultimate brew, may be the key to unlocking the mystery of Soma.

### 1. Syrian Rue (*Peganum harmala*)

Syrian Rue contains the beta-carboline alkaloids harmine and harmaline, which function as potent MAO inhibitors. In the Soma combination, Syrian Rue would serve the same role that *Banisteriopsis caapi* serves in Ayahuasca—enabling the oral activity of DMT and other tryptamine compounds. Flattery and Schwartz have made a strong case for Syrian Rue as a Soma candidate in its own right, noting the linguistic connection between Harmala and the Persian Haoma (a cognate of Soma). Without Syrian Rue or an equivalent MAOI, the other psychoactive components of the brew would be largely ineffective when consumed orally.

### 2. Psilocybin Mushrooms (*Psilocybe* species)

Terence McKenna and others have exhaustively documented the ecstatic visionary states produced by psilocybin-containing mushrooms. The Psilocybe mushrooms contribute potent serotonergic psychedelic effects, producing vivid visual imagery, altered cognition, and mystical-type experiences. Their validity as a Soma candidate cannot be discounted, nor should it be—McKenna speaks on this with authority. However, even McKenna, like many researchers, considered the question unresolved when evaluating single-plant theories. Within a multi-plant framework, Psilocybe mushrooms provide the primary visionary engine of the experience.
