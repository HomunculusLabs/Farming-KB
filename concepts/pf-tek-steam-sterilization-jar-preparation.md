# PF TEK Steam Sterilization and Jar Preparation

## Overview

The PF TEK steam sterilization method makes [[mushroom-substrate-sterilization-and-pasteurization]] accessible
using ordinary kitchen cookware. Unlike professional cultivation requiring a
[[double-layer-tinfoil-jar-covering-pressure-cooker-method]], this approach uses a standard vegetable steamer pot to achieve
adequate sterilization for shallow substrate jars.

This accessibility was central to the PF philosophy of making cultivation
available to anyone with basic kitchen supplies and minimal technical knowledge.

## Required Equipment

The basic setup requires only common household items:

- A 3-piece vegetable steamer pot with tight-fitting lid
- Tapered 1/2 pint canning jars
- Canning lids with holes punched through them
- Kitchen stove (gas preferred for [[hamilton-greenhouse-heating-and-temperature-control]])
- Professional grade masking tape
- Optional: tin foil as lid substitute for drinking glasses

The steamer pot provides the enclosed environment necessary to generate and
maintain steam at atmospheric pressure throughout the sterilization cycle.

## Jar Selection and Requirements

Jar selection is critical for success with the PF TEK method.

### Approved Jar Types

Only specific 1/2 pint jar styles work reliably:

- **KERR wide mouth half pint** — tapered sides, ideal for cake release
- **BALL regular half pint** — tapered, works well with the PF method
- **BALL jelly jar** — small and tapered, also suitable

All approved jars share tapered sides that allow the colonized substrate cake
to slide out intact when it is time for fruiting.

### Important Warning

The **regular mouth KERR half pint** jar is NOT tapered.

This jar has straight sides that grip the substrate cake, making removal nearly
impossible without damaging the mycelium. Many beginners make this mistake due
to the similar appearance of KERR jar styles.

Jars larger than 1/2 pint are unreliable for the PF TEK method. Larger volumes
increase contamination risk because atmospheric steam cannot reliably penetrate
deeper substrates, leaving the core unsterilized.

## Lid Preparation

Each canning lid requires modification before use:

- Punch 4 evenly spaced holes through the metal lid
- Holes allow inoculation with a spore syringe
- Rubber sealing edge faces UP toward the substrate

During steaming, lid bands should be left loose. This prevents pressure buildup
while maintaining a mostly sealed environment. After sterilization, bands are
tightened to seal the jars for cooling.

Professional grade masking tape is applied over the needle holes post-sterilization
to provide an additional barrier against airborne contamination.

## Steam Sterilization Procedure

The sterilization process requires attention to timing and temperature.

### Temperature Management

The pot should be brought to the lowest possible boil. A vigorous rolling boil
generates excessive heat that can damage the substrate. The goal is gentle steam
generation, not aggressive boiling.

Gas stoves are strongly preferred because they offer fine-grained temperature
control. Electric burner residual heat can cause temperature spikes that dry
out or overheat the substrate.
