Source: Paul Stamets, *The Mushroom Cultivator* (1983), Chapter XI

Each mushroom species fruits in response to unique conditions involving nutrition, temperature, pH, humidity, light, and CO2. Below are the key growing parameters for major cultivated species.

## Agaricus brunnescens (The Button Mushroom)

Most widely cultivated mushroom in the world. First cultivated in Europe in the late 1700s.

- Spawn: Rye grain buffered with CaCO3 and/or CaSO4
- Substrate: Nitrogen-enriched wheat straw and/or horse manure compost, 71-74% moisture
- Pasteurization: Live steam 2 hours at 140F, fill depth 6-12 inches
- Spawn Run: 90-100% RH, 76-78F substrate, 2 weeks, 5000-10,000 ppm CO2
- Casing: Required, 1-2 inches, pH 7.0-7.5
- Post-Casing: 76-80F, 8-12 days case run
- Primordia: 95-100% RH, compost 65-70F, air 62-65F, CO2 <1000 ppm, 4 air exchanges/hr
- Cropping: 85-92% RH, 62-65F air, CO2 <1000 ppm, 4 exchanges/hr
- Flush interval: 7-10 days
- Harvest: Before partial veil stretches
- Yield: 3 lbs/sq.ft. average (5 weeks), max 6 lbs/sq.ft.
- Nutrition: 24-44% protein (dry weight), 56mg niacin/100g dry
- Notes: Does not pin on agar without activated charcoal or select bacteria. Very CO2-sensitive. Secondary decomposer.

## Agaricus bitorquis (Rodman's Agaricus)

Warm-climate species with virus resistance and CO2 tolerance.

- Spawn Run: 84-86F substrate, thermal death at 93F
- Casing: pH 7.2-7.5, case run 10-12 days
- Primordia: Bed 77-80F, air 75-77F, CO2 <2000 ppm, no light required
- Cropping: 75-77F, CO2 <3000 ppm, 2-4 exchanges/hr
- Flush interval: 8-9 days
- Yield: 3 lbs/sq.ft. average, max 4 lbs/sq.ft.
- Notes: Coarser, firmer, more strongly flavored, longer shelf life than A. brunnescens. Higher disease incidence at its warmer temperature range.

## Coprinus comatus (Shaggy Mane)

Edible and choice but difficult to keep due to early deliquescence (gills liquify). Submerging in water slows deliquescence.

- Spawn: Rye grain
- Substrate: Composted wheat straw with horse/chicken manure, 70% moisture. Also fruits on pasteurized straw alone. Composts with ammonia scent after Phase II produce greatest fruitings.
- Spawn Run: 76-80F, 8-12 days, 5000-10,000 ppm CO2
- Casing: Optional, 1-2 inches, pH 7.0-7.5, case run 10-12 days
- Primordia: Bed 65-67F, air 62-65F, CO2 <1000 ppm, 4 exchanges/hr, 12hr light cycle
- Cropping: 62-65F, CO2 <1000 ppm, 4 exchanges/hr
- Yield: 2-3 lbs/sq.ft. average (4 weeks)
- Nutrition: 25.4% protein (dry weight)
- Notes: Thermotolerant mesophile. Antibiotic properties similar to Lentinus edodes.

## Flammulina velutipes (Enoke / Winter Mushroom)

Common cold-weather mushroom thriving on woody tissue. Japanese strains "Maruei" and "Ebios" lead commercial cultivation.

- Spawn: Sawdust/bran. 1L inoculates 50-160 800ml containers
- Substrate: 80-90% hardwood sawdust, 10-20% rice bran, 58-60% moisture. 4:1 volumetric ratio (10:1 mass). Japanese growers age sawdust several years before use.
- Sterilization: 1-2 hours at 250F (15 psi)
- Containers: Quart mason jars or 800ml plastic bottles
- Spawn Run: 72-77F, 20-30 days standard (12-13 days in vitro), 5000-10,000 ppm CO2
- Casing: Not required
- Primordia: 85% RH, 50-55F, CO2 <1000 ppm, 4 exchanges/hr
- Cropping: 85% RH, 50-55F, 2-3 weeks, 10-day flush interval
- Yield: 160-220g per 800ml bottle average, max nearly 600g
- Nutrition: 18-31% protein (dry weight), 107mg niacin/100g dry
- Notes: Forms mycelial "pellets" (add 5% corn starch + 2% malt to inhibit). Anti-tumor antibiotic "flammulin" isolated.

## Lentinus edodes (Shiitake)

Wood decomposer, typically saprophytic on deciduous trees (Fagaceae family -- oak, chestnut, shiia, beech). Indigenous to Japan, China, Indo-China temperate zone.

- Spawn: Pre-soaked wooden dowels or 4:1 sawdust/bran mixture
- Substrate: Oak or alder logs, 4-6 inches diameter, 3-foot lengths, cut in spring/fall for maximum sap. Can inoculate immediately or season 1 month in shaded stacks.
- Notes: One of the most commercially important cultivated mushrooms worldwide. Logs may produce for several years.

## Psilocybe cubensis

- Mycelium: Fast-growing, cottony to rhizomorphic
- Optimal spawn run temperature: 86F
- Photosensitive: requires blue and UV light for primordia formation (peaks at 370, 440, 460 nanometers; as little as 0.5ms/day effective)
- CO2 tolerant: 2-3 air exchanges/hour sufficient
- Red light recommended for checking cultures without premature pinning
- Tall stems characteristic and desirable (unlike Agaricus)
- Harvest: when veil begins to tear

## General Notes on All Species

- Most mushrooms are 90-92% water, 6-10% dry matter
- Protein content varies: 18-44% of dry weight depending on species
- Fresh air exchange rates are critical and species-specific
- Temperature directly controls rate of mushroom development
- Lower temperatures extend cropping period and improve quality
- Higher temperatures shorten cropping but increase risks (heat buildup, CO2 generation, pests, contaminants)
- 60-75% of total yield normally from first two flushes -- pinning period is the most critical phase
