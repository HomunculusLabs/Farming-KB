# Mollison on Flatland Design

**Source:** Bill Mollison, Permaculture Design Course, Pamphlet II (Permaculture in Humid Landscapes), 1981
**Last Updated:** 2026-04-18

## Overview

Mollison presents three detailed case studies for flatland design (300 acres with 2-foot drop), demonstrating how Earth banks, swales, excavated ponds, and creative house placement solve problems of water, privacy, noise, and heat.

## Core Principle

- "In flat lands, you have to make your slopes, throw up your roofs, and throw up your tanks, while you keep your house low"
- Alternative: high dwelling with bedrooms upstairs, service rooms below; roof becomes water collector
- "Don't be frightened to use Earth bank"

## Earth Banks

- Fastest way to establish windbreak: grade up 4-6 foot Earth bank
- Quick-growing plantings on top: pampas grass, bamboos
- Excellent radiation shields from fire
- Decrease noise (particularly traffic); vegetation alone needs 100+ meters to absorb street noise
- Accumulate leaf and silt from water flow = free mulch material
- Deflect water: crown of road to top of bank in straight line, clearing house roof

## Water Storage in Flat Lands

- Put all tanks up on slope above house roof level
- "You never fill a tank from a house roof, if you can avoid doing so"
- Use garages, barns, workshops (don't use water) for elevated tank platforms
- Their roofs supply cheap tank water
- Barns on level above house: wastes thrown out and rolled down to annual garden below
- Diversion drain falls to saddle; garden falls out from saddle

## Case Study 1: Privacy and Noise

- 300-acre flat site, eroded gully, noisy conditions
- Client had dam across gully with drain provision
- Backhoe dug cave under house site; house built on top
- Cave extended beyond house both ends, opened to pond at water level
- Underground glass house (alluvial plain = cheap to dig); integrated shower
- Cold cave end: root vegetable storage; hot end: bananas
- Heat rises through ducts anywhere in structure
- Result: private, cool summer retreat, low energy

## Case Study 2: Windmill Enthusiast

- 300-acre flat site, lots of water coming across, noisy
- Below-grade Earth tank (lake); excavated dirt formed 9-foot peninsula
- Two-story house on peninsula (wanted mountain views on shade side)
- Shaded veranda on second story for summer mountain viewing
- Two low Earth banks deflect flooding rains from garden
- All off-site water directed into dam; banks accumulate silt/mulch
- Result: sophisticated water control, mulch collection, fire protection

## Case Study 3: Landscape Architect

- Professional gardener/director of parks in Melbourne
- High fire frequency site (fire every 5-8 years)
- Client on peninsula in pond, three sides water = light reflection
- Earth banks for cooling wind circulation
- Windmill (aesthetic preference despite available water pressure)
- Result: "It is very private"

## Dam Biological Enhancement

- Make island in lake from excavation material = increased shoreline
- Barrier islands for wind protection on water
- Shelves at different levels for specific plants (18-36 inches for wild rice)
- Graded marshes from dam edge (low mud walls)
- Underwater stones for water bird nesting; fox-free islands
- Peninsula placement for fire-prone sites

## Slope Design Integration

- Water, roads, Earth walls, stone walls, short grazing = fire defense
- Fire travels uphill fast; downhill sector defenses are primary
- Barn and loads enter on level above house
- Gravity flow from barn tanks to house and garden below
- Tanks as structural elements: buried, built over, or trellised for shade

## Net and Pan (Steep/Rocky Slopes)

- Shallow guide lines for drainage; hand-cut
- Clear vegetation at junctions; flat planting platforms
- Dribbles of water gather in absorbing pans
- Effective in rocky country unusable for orchards
- 5-6 people can plant a large orchard in 3 days using this system
