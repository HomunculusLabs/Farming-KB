# Dopamine, Motivation, and Cannabinoid Modulation in ADHD and PTSD

**Source:** The [[blesching-cannabis-health-index-autoimmune-conditions]] (Blesching, 2015)

## Overview

Dopamine is a hormone and neurotransmitter primarily produced by the adrenal
glands, associated with emotional and behavioral motivation including reward,
emotional memory, and arousal. The molecule signals "pay attention, this is
worth remembering." The more intense, unpredictable, or novel the experience,
the greater the reward and associated dopamine release.

## Dopamine Biochemistry

- **Production site:** Primarily the [[blesching-endocannabinoid-autonomic-nervous-system-adrenal-glands]], located on top of each
  kidney. The adrenal glands contain CB1 [[cannabinoid-receptors-cb1-cb2-tissue-distribution-blesching]].
- **Brain region:** Dopamine modulates neurons in the substantia nigra
  (midbrain) via dopamine receptors and CB1 receptors
- **Precursor:** The [[cho-fish-amino-acid-preparation]] tyrosine is the natural precursor to dopamine
- **Emotional keyword:** "I am motivated" — encompassing arousal, emotional
  processing, memory, pleasure, love, and sensuality

## Dopamine Imbalance: Consequences

### Low Dopamine
- Fatigue and lack of energy
- Failure to finish tasks
- Low libido
- Burdensome emotional memory (difficulty releasing past hurts)
- Reduced motivation and productivity

### Excess Dopamine
Too much dopamine, whether from pharmaceuticals (L-dopa drugs) or stimulants
(methamphetamine), is associated with psychosis and aggression. This
illustrates the importance of modulation rather than simple elevation.

## Dopamine and Neurological Disorders

The loss of dopamine-producing nerve cells in the substantia nigra is
associated with multiple disorders:

1. **Parkinson's disease:** Direct result of dopamine cell loss; sufficient
   dopamine reduces risk of developing the disease
2. **Schizophrenia:** Characterized by excessive dopamine in certain brain
   regions
3. **ADHD:** Abnormal dopamine transmission in the striatum (the part of the
   forebrain that modulates the [[endocannabinoid-system]]) plays a pivotal role

## The Endocannabinoid-Dopamine Connection

### CB1 Receptors as Molecular Players in ADHD

Researchers have identified CB1 receptors as novel molecular players in ADHD.
Therapeutic strategies aimed at engaging the endocannabinoid system might prove
effective for this disorder. The pathway operates through:

1. **Striatal modulation:** The striatum modulates the endocannabinoid system,
   and abnormal dopamine transmission in this region is central to ADHD
2. **CB1 activation:** Cannabinoids acting on CB1 receptors can modulate
   dopamine release and reuptake in motivation-related brain circuits
3. **Adrenal pathway:** Since the adrenal glands produce dopamine and contain
   CB1 receptors, cannabinoids can influence dopamine production at its source

### PTSD and Emotional Memory Processing

Dopamine is involved in the processing of emotions, making it especially
relevant to patients with PTSD. The "burdensome emotional memory" associated
with low dopamine reflects the difficulty PTSD patients experience in
integrating traumatic memories. Cannabis may help by:

- Modulating dopamine release during memory reconsolidation
- Reducing the emotional intensity of traumatic memories through CB1-mediated
  pathways
- Supporting the brain's natural capacity for memory processing and
  emotional integration

## Evidence-Based Dopamine Enhancement

### Music and Emotional Engagement
Listening to music that accesses and moves deep emotions stimulates dopamine
release. Learning to play an instrument provides an even stronger dopaminergic
stimulus through the combination of emotional engagement and motor skill
development.

### Meditation and Altered States
Meditation-induced changes of consciousness have been shown to affect dopamine
levels. Extended Attention Span Training (EAST), a technique developed by NASA,
and its biofeedback-based video game offshoot called Self Mastery and Regulation
Training (SMART) directly target attentional circuits regulated by dopamine.
