Indigenous Microorganisms (IMOs) are the foundational input of Dr.
Cho's [[korean-natural-farming]] system. Rather than purchasing commercial
[[beneficial-indigenous-microorganisms-bim]], NF practitioners collect microorganisms that
have been living in the local area -- surviving and thriving in the
local climate. These locally adapted microbes are considered more
powerful and effective than artificially produced organisms cultured
in foreign or artificial environments.

## Why IMOs Matter

Microorganisms have two major functions in farming:

1. They decompose complex organic compounds (dead plant and animal
   matter, wastes) into nutrients that plants can absorb
2. They create antibiotic substances, enzymes, and [[bionutrients-lab-lactic-acid-bacteria-serum]] that
   suppress diseases and promote healthy soil conditions

In Non-Chemical agriculture, we do not feed the plant. We nurture the
soil and the soil nurtures the plant through the IMOs.

## Microbial Diversity

Dr. Cho advocates collecting microorganisms from different
environments to maximize diversity:

- Organisms from sunny areas differ from those in shaded areas (e.g.,
  under bamboo trees)
- Altitude affects microbial variety at every level
- Collect from all four directions of the field
- Include samples from mountains, summits, valleys, and trenches
- Include "tough guys" -- microbes from high mountains or
  uncontaminated regions that have survived extreme conditions

The best base material for culturing IMOs is steamed rice. The rice
should not be too soft or too sticky, since aerobic microorganisms
do not prefer sticky rice. Left-over rice works well. Plastic
containers should be avoided; wood or bamboo is recommended.

## Collection Method 1: From Native Soil

### Materials
- Wooden box (12" x 8" x 4", half-inch thickness, natural wood)
- Hard-cooked rice (low moisture for aerobic microbes)
- Porous paper (paper towel) and rubber band
- Jaggery or brown sugar
- Glass jar or clay pot

### Procedure
1. Fill the wooden box with steamed rice (do not press, leave fluffy
   for air supply; do not stuff higher than 3 inches)
2. Cover with white paper (not newspaper) secured with rubber band
3. Mark a 12" x 8" area in soil, excavate 2 inches
4. Place the box in a pit where IMOs abound: forest, field, or
   decomposed leaf mold area
5. Cover with leaves; protect from animals with a basket
6. Prevent rain (use plastic only during heavy rains)
7. At 20 degrees C, collection takes 5-6 days; at 30-35 degrees C,
   only 2-3 days
8. The rice covered with microorganisms is IMO-1
9. Mix IMO-1 with equal weight jaggery (1:1 ratio) to create IMO-2
10. Cover with paper and rubber band; 3 days in summer, 5 in winter

Black molds indicate the rice has been left too long.

## Collection Method 2: From Bamboo Stump

### Procedure
1. Choose a bamboo bush in the center of a bamboo grove
2. Cut a healthy shoot about 10 cm from the ground
3. Trim the inner edge to slant toward the hollow end (prevents
   bamboo juice from leaking)
4. Fill the cavity with boiled rice (low moisture), level above edge
5. Cover with the wooden box, then dry leaves, then plastic sheet
6. In 3-5 days, red, white, yellow, and black microorganisms
   will be collected along with bamboo juice
7. Cut the stump; transfer the rice to a jar. This is IMO-1
8. Mix with equal weight jaggery to create IMO-2

## Collection Method 3: From Paddy Field

### Procedure
1. Fill wooden box 3/4 with steamed rice
2. Immediately after paddy harvest, cover rice stumps with the box
   facing downward (rice touching stumps)
3. Protect with wire net (mice/rats) and plastic (rain)
4. In about one week, mostly anaerobic microorganisms are collected,
   including Bacillus licheniformis (breaks down protein, fat,
   carbohydrates) and [[cho-imo-collection-bamboo-stump-and-paddy-field]] (breaks strong fibers like
   straw and reeds)
5. The rice covered with fungal growth is IMO-1
6. Transfer to clay pot; mix with equal jaggery to create IMO-2

## IMO-3 Preparation (Mass Cultivation)

### Materials
- IMO-2, water (1:1000 dilution), rice bran or flour, paddy straw

### Procedure
1. Dilute IMO-2 with water; mix with rice bran to 65-70% moisture
   (lumps when pressed, loosens when shaken)
2. For better results, use diluted NF inputs ([[bionutrients-lab-fermented-plant-juice-fpj]], [[bionutrients-lab-fish-aminos]], [[bionutrients-lab-oriental-herbal-nutrients-ohn]]) in
   the water
3. Pile the mixture on soil floor (NOT concrete), 30-40 cm high
4. Cover with paddy straw; maintain 70% shade, 30% light
5. Temperature should reach 40-50 degrees C; turn when it does
6. Below 40 degrees C means anaerobic (too much moisture); above
   70 degrees C loses nutrients to thermophilic microbes
7. When the surface is covered with whitish spores and temperature
   stops rising (5-7 days), [[cho-fermented-mixed-compost-methods]] is complete
8. Properly fermented IMO-3 emits a pleasant fragrance
9. Store in ventilated jute/gunny/cloth bags in cool shade
10. As moisture drops to 20-30%, IMOs enter dormancy ("sleeping
    phase") -- normal for storage

Key organisms in IMO-3 include Aspergillus oryzae and Bacillus
subtilis.

## IMO-4 Preparation (Soil Integration)

### Materials
- IMO-3 (10 parts), field soil (5 parts), red fine soil from
  anthill/termite mound (5 parts)

### Procedure
1. Combine 1 part IMO-3 with 1 part soil: 50% field soil + 50%
   fresh mountain/anthill soil
2. Mix on soil floor, NOT concrete
3. Create heap, not more than 20 cm high
4. Cover for two days; control moisture with NF inputs (1:1000)
5. Add seawater for best results when inoculating soil
6. Adjust moisture to 65-75% before use

## Utilization Principles

1. Use IMOs continuously. They should be collected and prepared every
   year to maintain [[building-soil-fertility-long-term]].
2. Maintain diversity. Collect from different environments and mix.
3. Include "tough guys" from extreme environments.
4. Apply IMO-4 in small lumps (not smashed) -- this helps soil build
   aggregated structure rapidly.
