## Wave Pattern of Environmental Awareness
The emergence of environmental awareness and innovation in the last quarter of
the 20th century can be seen as clusters of intense activity followed by longer,
slower phases of consolidation. These phases of new activity tend to coincide
with recession in the mainstream economy. Environmental innovation in Australia,
for instance, has tended to cluster in periods of economic recession: the
1880s-1890s, the 1930s and 1940s, and since the 1970s, with very little of
enduring significance during economic booms, especially in the 1950s and 1960s.
## First Wave (1970s)
The first great wave of modern environmental awareness followed the Club of Rome
report in 1972 and the oil shocks of 1973 and 1975. Permaculture was one of the
environmental alternatives that emerged from this first wave. This period was
characterised by:
- Growing awareness of ecological limits to growth and resource depletion
- Development of alternative technologies and lifestyles
- Emergence of the environmental movement as a political force
- Foundational texts including The Limits to Growth, Small Is Beautiful, and
