# Wood-Based Substrates in The Mushroom Cultivator
Source: The Mushroom Cultivator (Stamets & Chilton, 1983), Chapter VI -- Non-Composted Substrates
Updated: 2026-04-18

## Overview

Chapter VI covers non-composted substrates used for cultivating wood-loving (lignicolous) mushroom species. These include sawdust/bran mixtures, [[pleurotus-natural-log-cultivation-methods]] culture, [[stamets-outdoor-wood-chip-bed-mycelium-transplantation]] chip beds, and [[amanita-preparation-methods]], and cultivation guidelines for each approach.

## Lignicolous Mushrooms

Mushrooms that grow on wood or wood wastes are termed lignicolous due to their ability to utilize lignin, a microbially resistant substance that constitutes the heartwood of trees. The main components of wood are cellulose and hemicellulose, which are also nutrients available to mushroom mycelium.

## Outdoor Wood Chip Bed Cultivation

### Virgin Spawn Preparation

1. Collect substrate pieces already colonized by mushroom mycelium
2. Use these as "virgin spawn" to inoculate similar materials
3. Freshly cut chips of alder, maple and fir support healthy [[brassinosteroid-psilocybe-cubensis-mycelial-growth-research]]
4. Alder preferred: high sugar content, no resins, abundant in stamets-fruiting-strategies-and-primordia-formation
- Place beds under rhododendrons, leafy ornamentals, fence rows, or in grass
- Never expose to direct sunlight, but must allow rainfall to reach
- Cover with plastic or cardboard during spring/summer colonization
- Water weekly to maintain moisture
- In fall, uncover and water heavily twice weekly (avoid flooding)

### Perennial Bed Management

- Once established, outdoor beds function like perennial plants
- Indoor spawn preparation becomes unnecessary
- Each year, chips drawn from original bed can inoculate new beds
- **Total bed area can be multiplied by five annually**
- Protect in winter with straw, cardboard, new chips topped with plastic
- Extended freezing weather ends outdoor cropping until following year
- For harsh climates: [[hamilton-cold-frames-and-hardening-off]] or plastic greenhouses
- Use local strains adapted to local conditions

## Growing on Pasteurized Straw

### Straw Preparation

- Wheat straw preferred (coarse fiber, good availability)
- Must be clean, mold-free, unspoiled
- Chop or shred into **1-3 inch pieces** using wood chipper, compost shredder, or power mower
- Shredding increases moisture absorption and substrate density
