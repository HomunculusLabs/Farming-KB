# Three Pillars of Mushroom Cultivation - Spawn, Substrate, Environment

Successful mushroom cultivation depends on three interdependent factors:
the quality of the spawn (inoculum), the [[query-what-is-the-difference-between-grain-spawn-and-liquid-culture-for-mushrooms|difference]] s. Spawn consists of mushroom mycelium growing on
a nutritious carrier medium such as sterilized grain (sorghum, barley,
rye, millet), sawdust, or wooden plugs. Unlike seeds, which are the
product of sexual reproduction and carry genetic diversity, commercial
spawn is clonally propagated from a selected parent culture to maintain
specific strain characteristics including growth rate, yield potential,
fruiting temperature range, and cap morphology.

The four main types of spawn are grain spawn (most common, made on
sterilized whole grains), sawdust spawn (preferred for log inoculation
and some bag systems), plug spawn (wooden dowels for log cultivation),
and liquid spawn (mycelium suspended in sterile nutrient broth for
rapid inoculation).

Spawn quality is one of the most decisive factors for successful
cultivation. Contaminated, aged, or weak spawn will fail to colonize
substrate efficiently, leading to reduced yields or total crop failure.
Commercial growers must source spawn from reputable laboratories that
maintain pure cultures and conduct regular strain testing.

The mushroom life cycle has two distinct phases with different
environmental requirements. During vegetative growth (spawn run),
mycelium colonizes the substrate optimally at 20-30 degrees Celsius,
in high humidity (80-90% relative humidity), in darkness or low light,
with minimal fresh air exchange.

During reproductive growth (fruiting), environmental conditions shift
dramatically to trigger the transition from mycelial growth to mushroom
formation. Lower temperatures (10-20 degrees Celsius, typically 10
degrees below the spawn run temperature), higher humidity (85-95%),
increased fresh air exchange (to supply oxygen and remove carbon
dioxide), and often light exposure are required to initiate pinning
and support fruit body development.
