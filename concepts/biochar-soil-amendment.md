## Biochar Soil Amendment

> Note: This page is compiled from general knowledge, not sourced from a specific file in the wiki's raw/ directory. See also [[biochar]] for the existing wiki page on biochar fundamentals.

Biochar as a soil amendment is the practice of incorporating pyrolyzed biomass into agricultural soils to improve fertility, water retention, nutrient cycling, and long-term carbon storage. The concept is inspired by terra preta (Amazonian dark earth), anthropogenic soils created by indigenous peoples in the Amazon basin that remain fertile thousands of years after their creation.

## Terra Preta

Terra preta do indio (Indian black earth) is found across the Amazon basin. These soils contain:
- 3-5x more organic carbon than surrounding soils
- High concentrations of biochar, pottery shards, bone, and compost residues
- Stable fertility maintained for 500-2,500+ years
- Significantly higher microbial activity and nutrient availability

The original inhabitants created these soils by incorporating charcoal from cooking fires, food waste, manure, and bone into the naturally poor tropical soils. The biochar acts as a persistent carbon skeleton that supports soil biology for millennia.

## How Biochar Benefits Soil

### Cation Exchange Capacity (CEC) Improvement

Raw biochar has relatively low CEC, but as it ages in soil (weeks to years), oxidation processes create carboxyl, phenolic, and other functional groups on its surfaces. These negatively charged sites attract and hold positively charged nutrient cations:

- Ammonium (NH4+)
- Potassium (K+)
- Calcium (Ca2+)
- Magnesium (Mg2+)

This "charging" process is why biochar is best inoculated with compost, compost tea, or liquid fertilizer before or during soil application. Uncharged biochar can initially draw nutrients away from plants.

### Water Retention

Biochar's porous structure (especially at higher pyrolysis temperatures of 500-700C) creates a vast internal surface area (150-400 m2/g). This acts like a sponge:

- Holds 3-5x its weight in water
- Reduces irrigation needs by 10-30% in sandy soils
- Improves drought resilience
- Reduces leaching of soluble nutrients

### Microbial Habitat

The porous structure of biochar provides refugia for beneficial soil microorganisms:
- Bacteria and fungi colonize the internal pore spaces
- Protection from predation by protozoa and nematodes
- Moisture retention supports microbial survival during dry periods
- Mycorrhizal hyphae associate with biochar surfaces
- Enhanced nitrogen fixation and phosphorus solubilization

### pH Buffering

Most biochar is alkaline (pH 8-10) due to ash content (calcium, magnesium, potassium carbonates). This can:
- Raise pH of acidic soils (liming effect)
- Buffer against pH swings
- Reduce aluminum toxicity in acidic tropical soils
- Be problematic in already alkaline soils — test before applying

### Carbon Sequestration

Biochar is resistant to decomposition, with half-lives ranging from hundreds to thousands of years depending on feedstock and production temperature. This makes it one of the most stable forms of carbon storage in soil. Application rates of 1-10 tonnes/hectare can sequester significant carbon while improving soil function.

## Application Rates

### General Guidelines

| Application | Rate |
|---|---|
| Light soil improvement | 0.5-1 kg/m2 (5-10 t/ha) |
| Standard garden bed amendment | 1-2 kg/m2 (10-20 t/ha) |
| Intensive vegetable beds | 2-5 kg/m2 (20-50 t/ha) |
| Potting mix addition | 5-10% by volume |
| Tree planting holes | 5-10% of backfill volume |

### Important Considerations

- Do NOT exceed 10% by volume in any soil mix — this can cause water repellency and nutrient issues
- Best results come from annual moderate applications rather than one massive dose
- Always combine with organic matter (compost, manure) for best results
- Fresh biochar should be "charged" before use — soak in compost tea, liquid manure, or nutrient solution for 2-4 weeks

## Pre-Charging Biochar

Raw biochar should be inoculated before soil application:

1. **Compost charging**: Mix biochar 50/50 with finished compost and let sit for 2-4 weeks
2. **Compost tea charging**: Soak biochar in aerated compost tea for 24-48 hours, drain, and apply
3. **Manure charging**: Mix with composted manure for 2-4 weeks
4. **Urine charging**: Dilute fresh urine 10:1 and soak biochar — high nitrogen charge
5. **In-pile charging**: Add biochar directly to an active compost pile — it absorbs nutrients and microbes during the composting process

## Feedstock Considerations

| Feedstock | Biochar Quality | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Hardwood | Excellent | High carbon stability, good porosity |
| Softwood/conifer | Good | Higher volatile content, may need longer aging |
| Bamboo | Very good | Excellent porosity, high surface area |
| Nut shells | Excellent | Dense, stable, high surface area |
| Rice husks | Good | High silica content |
| Corn stover | Moderate | Lower density, more ash |
| Manure | Variable | Nutrient-rich but less stable carbon |
| Paper/cardboard | Poor | High ash, contaminants possible |

## Limitations and Cautions

- Biochar is NOT a fertilizer — it improves soil function but does not supply significant NPK
- In alkaline soils, biochar can further raise pH beyond optimal ranges
- Contaminated feedstocks (treated wood, plastics) produce toxic biochar — use only clean biomass
- Excessive application can cause hydrophobicity (water repellency) in sandy soils
- Economic viability depends on local feedstock availability and production costs
