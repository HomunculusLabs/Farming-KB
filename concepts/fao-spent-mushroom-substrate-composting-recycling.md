## Step 2: Spent Substrate Reuse in New Bags

Spent substrate from harvested bags can potentially be re-used to make new substrate bags,
though this requires careful processing:

### Requirements for Reuse
- All residual mycelium must be removed from the spent substrate
- The substrate must be thoroughly re-pasteurized before reuse
- Nutrient supplementation may be needed since the first crop has partially depleted
  the substrate
- The substrate should be inspected for signs of contamination before processing

### Limitations
- Successive reuse cycles progressively reduce substrate quality and yield
- Risk of contamination accumulation increases with each reuse cycle
- The manual recommends limiting reuse to maintain production quality
- Fresh substrate should be mixed with spent substrate rather than relying on 100%
  reuse

## Step 3: Converting Spent Substrate to Compost

The most universally applicable approach is converting spent substrate into organic
compost. The FAO manual describes a straightforward process using Effective
Microorganisms (EM):

### The EM Composting Method

1. **Break bags open**: Remove the plastic and separate it for municipal collection
2. **Mix with EM and sugar**: Add Effective Microorganism (EM) culture and a sugar
   source to the spent substrate
3. **Maintain humidity**: Keep moisture content at 65-75% throughout the composting
   process
4. **Contain the compost**: Place the mixture in used rice bags or directly on the
   ground under shade
5. **Wait approximately 30 days**: The composting process transforms the spent
   substrate into stable, nutrient-rich compost

### Quality Indicators

Finished compost is dark brown to black with an earthy smell (no ammonia or sour odors),
crumbly texture, reduced volume, and is rich in organic matter, nitrogen, phosphorus,
and potassium.
