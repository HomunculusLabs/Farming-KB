---# Halotolerant and Halophilic Fungi

Halotolerant and halophilic fungi are capable of growing in
environments with elevated salt concentrations. These organisms
inhabit saline soils, salt marshes, hypersaline lakes, marine
environments, and salted food products. Understanding fungal
responses to salinity is important for food preservation,
agriculture in saline soils, and understanding the limits of
fungal adaptation to extreme environments.

## Definitions and Classification

Microbes are categorized as halophilic based on their requirements
for NaCl in the growth medium. Extreme halophiles grow best in
media containing 2.5-5.2 M NaCl. Borderline extreme halophiles
grow best in 1.5-4.0 M NaCl. Halotolerant microbes can tolerate
salt to the point of saturation. Nonhalophiles grow best in media
containing less than 0.2 M NaCl. No extreme halophiles have been
found among the eukaryotes.

## Halotolerant Fungi

Halotolerant fungi are commonly isolated from arid soils and
include species in the genera Alternaria, Aspergillus,
Penicillium, Myrothecium, Stachybotrys, and Trichoderma. These
fungi can grow at elevated salt concentrations but do not
require salt for growth. Cellulose decomposition by some
Aspergillus species increases with salt concentrations from
3-10%, suggesting a competitive advantage in saline
environments.

## Halophilic Fungi

True halophilic fungi include Basipetospora halophila, Hortaea
werneckii, and Aspergillus penicilloides, often isolated from
salted or dried fish. These species grow optimally at salinities
of 2.5 M NaCl. They are considered halophilic because they grow
at higher rates on NaCl media than on media with other osmotica
such as glucose or glycerol. Aspergillus halophilicus and
Scopulariopsis halophilica are halophilic species isolated from
various saline and arid-region soils.

## Research Gaps

Many saline environments remain unsampled for fungi, and the
[[fungal-salt-tolerance-and-ion-homeostasis]] are incompletely
understood for most species. The distinction between halophilic
and halotolerant strategies, and the evolutionary origins of
salt adaptation in fungi, require further investigation. The
recent discovery of black yeast fungi in hypersaline saltpans
has opened new avenues for understanding eukaryotic adaptation
to extreme salinity.
