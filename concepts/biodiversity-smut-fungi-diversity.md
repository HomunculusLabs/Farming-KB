# Smut Fungi Diversity

The smut fungi, class Ustilaginomycetes, phylum
[[biodiversity-basidiomycete-diversity-taxonomy]],
are a group of plant parasites occurring on angiosperms,
especially grasses and sedges. Modern treatments based on
ultrastructural, biochemical, and nucleotide-sequence analyses
have refined our understanding of their phylogenetic relationships
and revealed important patterns of diversity and distribution.

## Morphology and Life Cycle

The morphology of smut fungi is relatively simple, consisting of
parasitic intercellular mycelia and reproductive structures. The
reproductive structures are represented by teliospores and their
germination products. In most taxa, teliospores are formed in
sori. A sorus consists of a dark mass of powdery teliospores and
host tissue. Each sorus contains millions of one-celled, thick-
walled, and generally darkly pigmented teliospores ranging from
3.5 to 50 micrometers in diameter, depending on the species.

Teliospore wall ornamentation can be reticulate, cerebriform,
echinulate, or tuberculate, with variable pigmentation. About half
of the smut fungi produce teliospores in aggregates called "spore
balls," consisting of teliospores only or a combination of
teliospores and sterile cells. Many species are capable of limited
saprobic growth on nonliving substrata, but the predominant stage
is the intercellular mycelium within the plant host.

## Species Diversity

More than 1,300 species of smut fungi in approximately 80 genera
are known from 4,000 species of host plants, representing more
than 75 families of angiosperms worldwide. The number of known
species may account for only about half of the extant species.
Eighteen monotypic genera of smuts have been listed; 10 are based
on tropical or subtropical species, five on temperate species, and
three on species with widespread distributions.

## Host Specificity and Infection

Each smut species has a limited host range. Host specificity,
combined with morphological characters of the sorus and
teliospores, traditionally has been used to delimit species.
To identify a smut fungus, it is essential to know the identity
of the host plant. The site of sorus formation is generally
constant for a smut fungus and is critical for the delimitation
of different genera.

Infection by smut fungi is generally recognized only when the
sori are formed in stems, leaves, floral organs, and occasionally
the roots of the host plant. The sori of some inflorescence smuts
may assume the general shape of the infected host organ, making
infection difficult to detect. Conversely, gall-forming taxa may
be easy to detect because of gross distortion or hypertrophy of
the affected host organ.

## Geographic Distribution

Smut fungi are distributed worldwide and show roughly equal
distributions of species between tropical and temperate areas.
Most collections of Entyloma, Melanotaenium, and Urocystis are
from temperate regions, whereas Cintractia, Farysia, and
Sphacelotheca show predominantly tropical or subtropical
distributions.

Regional inventories include 280 species from the United States,
260 from Mexico and Central and South America, 100 from the
British Isles, 119 from Hungary, 194 from India, and 86 from
West Pakistan. Only about half of the smut fungi of Hungary had
been reported, suggesting significant undercollection even in
well-studied temperate regions.

## Economic Importance

Smut fungi are important pathogens of crop plants, including corn,
wheat, barley, oats, rice, and sugarcane. Infection by Ustilago
maydis (corn smut) causes large, tumorlike growths on the ears or
vegetative organs of corn plants. Tilletia species cause bunt or
stinking smut of wheat, reducing both yield and grain quality.
These diseases have driven extensive research into smut biology
and control measures.

## Exobasidiales

The order Exobasidiales (class Ustilaginomycetes) comprises four
families: Exobasidiaceae, Brachybasidiaceae, Cryptobasidiaceae,
and Graphiolaceae. Species of Exobasidium are parasitic on
angiosperms, with preferred hosts in the temperate Ericales.
Infected host tissues form misshapen galls or blisters, often
discolored white or pink. The Graphiolaceae includes smutlike
organisms pathogenic on palms, exclusively tropical and subtropical
in distribution.

## Collection and Study

Collection of smut fungi depends on the type of smut and the
phenology of the host. Presence of mature teliospores on the
infected host is important; for inflorescence smuts, it is
necessary to collect hosts that are flowering. Vánky and Durán
have provided excellent descriptions of morphological characters
and keys to the genera.
