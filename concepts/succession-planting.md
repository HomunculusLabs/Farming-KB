# Succession Planting

Succession planting is the practice of staggering plantings of the same or different crops throughout the growing season to ensure continuous harvests and maximize garden productivity. Rather than planting everything at once and harvesting all at once, succession planting spreads production across weeks and months.

## Types of Succession Planting

### Same Crop Succession

Planting the same crop multiple times at regular intervals for a continuous harvest. Most effective for crops that mature quickly and have a short harvest window.

**Best candidates for same-crop succession:**

| Crop | Days to Maturity | Succession Interval | Notes |
|------|-----------------|---------------------|-------|
| Lettuce | 30-60 | Every 2-3 weeks | Harvest outer leaves or cut entire head |
| Radishes | 25-35 | Every 2 weeks | Harvest promptly to avoid pithiness |
| Beans (bush) | 50-65 | Every 2-3 weeks | Stop 10 weeks before first frost |
| Beets | 50-65 | Every 3 weeks | Harvest small for tender roots |
| Carrots | 55-75 | Every 3-4 weeks | Later plantings store well |
| Cilantro | 40-50 | Every 2-3 weeks | Bolts quickly in heat |
| Spinach | 40-50 | Every 3 weeks | Plant in cool weather only |
| Arugula | 30-45 | Every 2-3 weeks | Bolt-resistant varieties extend season |
| Turnips | 40-60 | Every 2-3 weeks | Harvest at 2-3 inches for best flavor |
| Zucchini | 50-60 | Every 4 weeks | Later plantings avoid squash vine borer peak |

### Relay Cropping (Crop-to-Crop Succession)

Planting a new crop immediately after harvesting the previous one in the same space. See [[intercropping-systems]] for relay cropping where crops overlap.

**Classic relay sequences:**

| Spring Crop | Summer Crop | Fall Crop |
|------------|------------|-----------|
| Peas | Peppers or eggplant | Garlic |
| Lettuce | Bush beans | Spinach |
| Radishes | Summer squash | Cover crop |
| Spinach | Tomatoes | Kale |
| Beets | Sweet corn | Carrots |
| Onions (sets) | Cucumbers | Broccoli |
| Potatoes (early) | Green beans | Fall brassicas |

### Seasonal Succession (Year-Round Planting)

Planning plantings across all four seasons to maximize garden output year-round. This is the most comprehensive form of succession planting and integrates with [[winter-gardening-techniques]] and [[season-extension-techniques]].

## Sequential Planting Schedules

### Spring Plantings (4-8 weeks before last frost)

| Crop | Plant Date (relative to last frost) | Harvest | Notes |
|------|-------------------------------------|---------|-------|
| Peas | 6-8 weeks before | 60-70 days | Direct seed, cold-tolerant |
| Spinach | 4-6 weeks before | 40-50 days | Direct seed, bolts in heat |
| Lettuce | 4-6 weeks before | 40-60 days | Start indoors or direct seed |
| Onions (sets) | 4 weeks before | 90-120 days | Plant sets 1 inch deep |
| Radishes | 4 weeks before | 25-35 days | Direct seed, harvest promptly |
| Beets | 2-4 weeks before | 50-65 days | Direct seed |
| Carrots | 2-3 weeks before | 55-75 days | Direct seed, slow to germinate |
| Potatoes | 2 weeks before | 80-100 days | Plant seed pieces 4 inches deep |
| Broccoli | Start 6-8 weeks before | 60-80 days | Transplant 2 weeks before frost |
| Cabbage | Start 6-8 weeks before | 70-90 days | Transplant 2-4 weeks before frost |

### Summer Plantings (after last frost)

| Crop | Plant Date | Harvest | Notes |
|------|-----------|---------|-------|
| Bush beans | 1-2 weeks after last frost | 50-65 days | Direct seed, succession every 2-3 weeks |
| Corn | After last frost, soil 60°F+ | 70-100 days | Direct seed, block planting for pollination |
| Tomatoes | Transplant 2 weeks after last frost | 70-90 days | Start indoors 6-8 weeks before |
| Peppers | Transplant 2-3 weeks after last frost | 70-90 days | Start indoors 8-10 weeks before |
| Cucumbers | 1-2 weeks after last frost | 50-65 days | Direct seed or transplant |
| Squash | 1-2 weeks after last frost | 50-65 days | Direct seed |
| Eggplant | Transplant 2-3 weeks after last frost | 70-85 days | Start indoors 8-10 weeks before |
| Basil | 2 weeks after last frost | 30-60 days | Transplant, frost-sensitive |

### Fall Garden Plantings

The fall garden is often the most productive and least pest-affected season. Calculate planting dates by counting backward from the first fall frost date.

**Formula:**

    Planting Date = First Fall Frost Date - Days to Maturity - 14 days (fall factor)

The 14-day fall factor accounts for shorter days and cooler temperatures that slow growth in fall.

| Crop | Weeks Before First Frost | Notes |
|------|------------------------|-------|
| Broccoli | 12-14 | Transplant; tolerates light frost, improves flavor |
| Cabbage | 10-12 | Transplant; stores well after harvest |
| Cauliflower | 10-12 | Transplant; tie leaves over curds for blanching |
| Kale | 8-10 | Direct seed or transplant; survives to 10°F unprotected |
| Carrots | 8-10 | Direct seed; sweetens after frost |
| Beets | 8-10 | Direct seed; harvest small or overwinter |
| Turnips | 6-8 | Direct seed; very cold-tolerant |
| Radishes | 4-6 | Direct seed; matures quickly |
| Lettuce | 6-8 | Direct seed or transplant; use cold frames for extension |
| Spinach | 6-8 | Direct seed; overwinters in mild climates |
| Arugula | 4-6 | Direct seed; bolt-resistant varieties |
| Garlic | Plant 4-6 weeks before first frost | Separate cloves, plant 2 inches deep |
| Shallots | Plant 4-6 weeks before first frost | Plant sets 1 inch deep |

## Winter Crops

With protection, many crops can be harvested through winter. See [[winter-gardening-techniques]] and [[season-extension-techniques]].

### Crops That Survive Winter (with protection)

- **Hardy**: Kale, collards, Brussels sprouts, parsnips, leeks, spinach (survive to 0°F with row cover)
- **Moderately hardy**: Carrots, beets, turnips, cabbage, cauliflower, Swiss chard (survive to 20°F with protection)
- **Tender with protection**: Lettuce, arugula, radishes, Asian greens, scallions (survive to 25°F in cold frames)

### Overwintering Crops

Plant in fall for early spring harvest:

- **Garlic**: Plant fall, harvest early summer
- **Shallots**: Plant fall, harvest early summer
- **Fava beans**: Plant fall in mild climates, harvest spring
- **Overwintering onions**: Plant fall sets, harvest early summer
- **Spinach**: Plant fall, overwinter, harvest early spring

## Planning Your Succession Calendar

### Step-by-Step Process

1. Determine your first and last frost dates from local records or NOAA
2. List all crops you want to grow
3. For each crop, note days to maturity and preferred planting window
4. Mark spring, summer, and fall plantings on a calendar
5. Identify gaps in the calendar where beds will be empty
6. Fill gaps with cover crops, succession plantings, or season extension crops
7. Plan for overlap — have transplants ready when a crop is harvested

### Maximizing Bed Productivity

- Aim for every bed to produce 2-3 crops per year (spring, summer, fall)
- Use transplants to reduce the time a bed is occupied by a slowly establishing crop
- Interplant fast crops (radishes) between slow crops (carrots, parsnips)
- Use [[cover-cropping-guide]] for any period longer than 4 weeks between cash crops
- Keep a garden journal recording actual planting and harvest dates to refine future plans

## Regional Considerations

### Short-Season Climates (100-150 frost-free days)

- Focus on fast-maturing varieties (look for "early" in variety names)
- Use season extension structures to extend both ends of the season
- Maximize succession plantings of quick crops (radishes, lettuce, beans)
- Choose day-neutral varieties that do not require long days to mature

### Long-Season Climates (200+ frost-free days)

- Extended succession planting windows for most crops
- Multiple plantings of determinate tomatoes for staggered harvest
- Summer cover crops between spring and fall plantings
- Overwintering crops possible without heavy protection

### Mild Winter Climates (Zones 8-10)

- Year-round gardening possible with minimal protection
- Winter succession plantings of cool-season crops
- Summer may be too hot for some crops — use shade cloth or take a summer break
- Two distinct growing seasons: cool (fall-winter-spring) and warm (spring-summer)
