## Ice Cream Bean (Inga spp)

## Overview

The ice cream bean (Inga edulis and related species) is an evergreen tree native to the neotropics of Central and South America. Named for its edible seed pods, which contain a sweet, white, fluffy pulp with a texture and flavor reminiscent of vanilla ice cream, the ice cream bean is unusual among nurse crops in being directly edible by humans. With over 400 known species in the genus Inga, most of which produce edible pods, it represents one of the most versatile multipurpose trees available for [[query-how-does-the-nitrogen-cycle-work-in-a-garden]] helps sustain productive cropping without [[neurotropic-fungi-in-central-america]] and the West Indies, Inga species are widely used as shade trees for coffee and cacao plantations. The dappled shade provided by Inga's canopy is ideal for these understory crops, which require some protection from direct tropical sun while still receiving sufficient light for production. Benefits include:
- Temperature moderation in the understory
- Reduced evapotranspiration from coffee and cacao plants
- Leaf litter that contributes to [[leucaena-alley-cropping-systems]] where rows of trees alternate with crop alleys. The trees provide:
- Nitrogen fixation for companion crops
- Mulch from pruned branches and leaf litter
- Wind protection for alley crops
- Pod harvests as a supplementary food source
- Biomass for green manure when pruned

### Urban and Street Planting

Throughout South America, Inga edulis is commonly planted as a street tree due to its attractive evergreen foliage, manageable size, and dual-purpose production of shade and edible pods. Its use in urban landscapes demonstrates the potential for integrating food production into municipal planting schemes.

## Timber and Wood Products

The timber of Inga edulis is moderately heavy and has several applications:
- **Furniture**: Suitable for general furniture construction
- **Boxes and crates**: Used for packaging materials
- **Charcoal**: The wood produces good-quality charcoal
- **Limitation**: The wood is not suited to external use as it breaks down quickly when exposed to weather

## Species Diversity

The genus Inga is remarkably diverse with over 400 species, most of which produce edible pods. This diversity offers significant potential for:
- **Adaptation to different climates and soils**: Different species are suited to lowland tropical, premontane, and lower montane conditions.
- **Extended harvest seasons**: Different species fruit at different times of year.
- **Disease and pest resistance**: Genetic diversity within the genus provides resilience.
- **Varied growth habits**: Species range from small understory trees to tall canopy emergents.

Like most plants of the Andes, the ice cream bean appears able to withstand a large range of conditions, making it adaptable across much of the tropical world.

## Propagation

Inga species can be propagated by:
- **Seed**: Direct sowing of fresh seeds. Seeds have moderate viability and should be planted promptly after harvest.
- **Cuttings**: Some species can be propagated from stem cuttings, though this is less common.
- **Natural regeneration**: In suitable conditions, Inga will self-seed and establish naturally.
