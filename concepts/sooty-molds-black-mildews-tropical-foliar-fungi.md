# Sooty Molds and Black Mildews - Tropical Foliar Fungi
Sooty molds and black mildews are two groups of
[[fungal-biodiversity]]|Ascomycetes that form dark, conspicuous
colonies on the surfaces of [[fungal-parasites-living-plants-estimating-diversity]].
## Sooty Molds
### Biology
The sooty molds are saprobic ascomycetes belonging to several
different [[bloomfield-rust-germ-tube-tactile-sensing-leaf-surface-navigation]]
- A velvety growth
- A dark crust on leaves and smaller twigs
- More robust growth on trunks and larger branches
Some species of sooty molds produce as many as three
different asexual forms. The mycelium is composed of a weft
of dark hyphae, which may be several millimeters thick.
Conidiogenous cells and various types of conidia are formed
within the mycelium. In some species the mycelium is
unorganized, whereas in others it is highly organized with
a characteristic appearance.
### Growth Forms
Reynolds (1975) recognized two growth forms for sooty molds:
1. **Deciduous growth form** -- characterized by a thin
   hyphal layer that peels away from its substratum in
   drier, cooler months. Found predominantly in lowland
   tropical regions but absent from rain forests. Limacinula
   and Trichomerium are representative genera.
2. **Permanent growth form** -- occurs mainly on twigs and
   stems where it forms thick, cushion-like black hyphal
   mats. Occurs predominantly in neotropical highlands and
   temperate lowlands. Species of Limacinia are
   representative.
### Geographic Distribution
Sooty molds predominate along the continental margins of
North and stamets-mushrooms-world-mexico-central-america, and on islands
in the Western Hemisphere. They occur in some form in most
vegetation types worldwide but are prevalent around the
margins of rain forest communities. Hughes (1976) noted that
the trunks and branches of Nothofagus forests in New Zealand
can be covered up to 9 meters in height with sooty molds,
with growth so extensive that only small areas of bark are
visible.
### Specialized Families
- **Metacapnodiaceae** -- produce distinctive, tapering,
  moniliform hyphae with rigid smooth-walled cells. Colonies
  are dense and lustrous.
- **Seuratiaceae** -- produce dark, gelatinous, lobed
  thalli with reproductive structures quite different from
  other sooty molds.
### Collection Techniques
Sooty molds on leaves are easier to collect and identify
than those on tree trunks. The colloidal leaf peel technique
is the standard collection method: a drop of 2% collodion or
cellulose acetate solution is spread on the leaf colony,
allowed to dry to a transparent film, then peeled off with
the intact colony embedded. The film is placed on a
microscope slide, and the collodion is removed with acetone.
## Black Mildews
### Biology
Black mildews are obligate plant parasites especially abundant
in the tropics but also occurring from Chile to the southern
[[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]], from South Africa to central Europe and
Scotland, and from Japan to Tasmania. The name derives from
the scattered, dark, superficial, circular colonies produced
by the fungi on host plant surfaces. Black mildews belong to
the order Meliolales in the Ascomycota.
