# Oyster Mushroom Spawn Production: Grain and Sawdust Methods

## From the Mushroom Growers' Handbook 1

Spawn is the living [[antimicrobial-compounds-mushroom-mycelium]] propagated onto a carrier material for
inoculation of the production substrate. Spawn quality directly determines
cultivation success — poor spawn leads to contamination, slow colonization, and
reduced yields regardless of how well the [[cannabis-growing-environment-setup]] is managed.

## Types of Spawn

### Grain Spawn
The most common form for oyster [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]]. Grain kernels — typically
wheat, rye, millet, or sorghum — are cooked, sterilized, and inoculated with
mycelium. Each kernel becomes a discrete [[inoculation-point-taping-technique-sterile-bag-inoculation-jarrold]], allowing rapid and
even colonization of the production substrate.

Grain spawn advantages include fast colonization speed, high number of inoculation
points per unit weight, and relatively simple production. Disadvantages include
susceptibility to bacterial contamination during production, shorter shelf life
(2-3 months at proper storage), and vulnerability to insect pests attracted to grain.

### Sawdust Spawn
Produced by inoculating sterilized sawdust-bran mixtures with mycelium. Sawdust
spawn has lower nutritional value than grain, which actually provides an advantage
— it is less attractive to contaminants. Shelf life extends to 4-6 months.

Sawdust spawn is preferred for log cultivation and outdoor methods. It produces
fewer but larger inoculation points compared to grain, resulting in slower initial
colonization but potentially stronger [[fungal-mycelial-networks-nutrient-translocation]] in the final substrate.

### Liquid Spawn
An emerging technology where mycelium is grown in liquid nutrient medium and then
injected into substrate. Offers extremely fast colonization and easy automation.
However, it requires more sophisticated equipment and [[blesching-cannabis-contaminants-testing-quality-control]]. The handbook
notes liquid spawn is gaining popularity in commercial operations.

## Spawn Production Process

### Stock Culture Maintenance
The process begins with a pure culture of the desired strain, typically maintained
on agar slants or plates in a laboratory. [[blesching-cannabis-strain-selection-receptor-targeting]] is critical — different
strains of the same species can vary dramatically in yield, speed, temperature
tolerance, and fruiting quality. The handbook recommends periodic subculturing
every 3-6 months and cryopreservation for long-term storage.

### Mother Spawn (First Generation)
Pure culture is transferred to sterilized grain or sawdust in a sterile environment.
This first-generation spawn serves as the inoculum for producing larger quantities
of planting spawn. Mother spawn is typically produced in small quantities (500g-1kg)
under the most rigorous sterile conditions.

### Planting Spawn (Production Spawn)
Mother spawn is used to inoculate larger quantities of grain or sawdust for
distribution to growers. The handbook emphasizes that spawn should never be
propagated beyond the third generation to avoid genetic degeneration and
accumulation of contaminants. Each successive generation introduces risk.

### Sterilization Requirements
Grain spawn requires sterilization at 121°C (15 psi) for 60-90 minutes in an
autoclave or [[double-layer-tinfoil-jar-covering-pressure-cooker-method]]. Insufficient sterilization is the most common cause
of spawn contamination. Sawdust spawn may require 90-120 minutes due to the insulating
properties of sawdust. Cooling must occur in a clean environment to prevent
contamination during handling.

## Inoculation Technique

Inoculation must be performed in as sterile conditions as possible. A laminar flow
hood is ideal, but many small-scale operations use inoculation chambers — simple
boxes with filtered air supply. The handbook describes a low-cost inoculation box
made from locally available materials that reduces contamination rates dramatically.

Standard inoculation rate is 3-5% of substrate weight for grain spawn, or 5-10%
for sawdust spawn. Higher rates speed colonization but increase cost. The goal is
rapid, complete colonization before contaminants can establish.

## Spawn Quality Indicators

Good spawn appears uniformly white with vigorous [[brassinosteroid-psilocybe-cubensis-mycelial-growth-research]], has a pleasant
mushroomy smell, and shows no discoloration, sliminess, or off-odors. Contaminated
spawn may show green (Trichoderma), black, or pink patches. The handbook recommends
 growers always test a small batch before committing to full production runs.
