# Commercially Important Pleurotus Species for Cultivation

The genus *Pleurotus* encompasses a diverse group of edible mushrooms
commonly known as oyster mushrooms, valued worldwide for their culinary
qualities, nutritional benefits, medicinal properties, and relative ease of
cultivation. Of the approximately 40 described species in the genus, a
handful dominate commercial production. Each species has distinct growth
requirements, substrate preferences, fruiting temperatures, morphological
characteristics, and market qualities that make it more or less suitable
for different growing environments and market contexts. Understanding
these differences is essential for cultivators selecting species for their
specific conditions and target markets.

## Pleurotus ostreatus - The Classic Oyster

*Pleurotus ostreatus* is the type species of the genus and the most widely
cultivated oyster mushroom worldwide. It fruits in a temperature range of
10-20 degrees Celsius, making it well-suited to temperate and subtropical
climates. Its cap colour varies from grey to white to brown depending on
strain and growing conditions, and it typically grows in shelf-like clusters
on dead or dying hardwood trees in nature. *P. ostreatus* has a robust,
meaty texture and a mild, pleasant flavour that makes it popular in both
fresh and dried markets. It is one of the most cold-tolerant oyster
species and will fruit prolifically during winter months in temperate
regions, providing year-round production when combined with warm-season
species. Biological efficiency on straw substrates typically ranges from
50-100%, making it a reliable producer for commercial operations.

## Pleurotus pulmonarius - The Indian or Phoenix Oyster

*Pleurotus pulmonarius* is closely related to *P. ostreatus* but prefers
warmer fruiting temperatures of 15-25 degrees Celsius. It is often called
the phoenix oyster or Indian oyster and is the predominant species
cultivated in tropical and subtropical regions of South and Southeast
Asia. Compared to *P. ostreatus*, it tends to have a paler cap colour,
a finer stem, and slightly faster mycelial growth. *P. pulmonarius* is
favoured in many developing-country cultivation programs because of its
adaptability to a wide range of agricultural waste substrates including
rice straw, wheat straw, and various legume residues. Its protein content
ranges from 26-35% on a dry weight basis, making it nutritionally
comparable to or better than many other cultivated mushrooms.

## Pleurotus sajor-caju - The Grey Oyster

*Pleurotus sajor-caju* has been one of the most commercially important
oyster mushrooms in tropical Asia, though its taxonomic status has been
debated. Some authorities consider it a synonym or variety of *P.
pulmonarius* rather than a distinct species. It was among the first
oyster mushrooms introduced to farmers in Nepal (1984) and the
Philippines, where it proved easy to grow on chopped straw packets and
suitable to local climatic conditions. The species produces medium-sized,
grey-brown fruiting bodies with a robust texture suitable for both fresh
sale and drying. It has been widely used in poverty alleviation programs
throughout South and Southeast Asia because of its simple cultivation
requirements and reliable yields on locally available substrates.

## Pleurotus eryngii - The King Oyster

*Pleurotus eryngii*, known as the king oyster mushroom, is the largest
species in the genus and commands premium prices in international
markets. Its fruiting bodies can reach 15-20cm in diameter with a thick,
meaty stem that is entirely edible, unlike the tough stems of other
oyster species. King oysters prefer cooler temperatures (12-18 degrees
Celsius) and are typically grown on supplemented sawdust substrates
rather than straw. Their longer cultivation cycle and specific
environmental requirements make them more challenging to grow than
other oyster species, but the higher market value compensates for the
additional complexity. The thick, firm texture of *P. eryngii* makes
it particularly suitable for grilling, braising, and serving as a
meat substitute in vegetarian and vegan cuisine.

## Pleurotus cystidiosus - The Abalone Oyster

*Pleurotus cystidiosus*, the abalone oyster, is notable for producing
abundant chlamydospores (asexual spores) during mycelial growth, which
can complicate spawn production and identification. It fruits at warm
temperatures of 20-30 degrees Celsius and is popular in tropical and
subtropical Asian markets. The species produces attractive, shell-shaped
caps with a smooth surface and a texture reminiscent of abalone meat,
hence its common name. It is cultivated on straw and sawdust substrates
and has good biological efficiency, though the chlamydospore production
requires careful management during spawn preparation to maintain culture
purity and strain characteristics.

## Pleurotus djamor - The Pink Oyster

*Pleurotus djamor* (often cited as *P. flabellatus* or *P. salmoneo-
stramineus* in older literature) produces striking pink-to-salmon coloured
caps that make it one of the most visually distinctive cultivated
mushrooms. It requires warm fruiting temperatures of 22-30 degrees
Celsius and is well-suited to tropical cultivation. Pink oysters are
fast-growing and aggressive colonizers but have a shorter shelf life than
other oyster species, with the vivid pink colour fading to a pale tan
within days of harvest. Despite this limitation, they are popular in
specialty markets and farmer's markets where their unusual appearance
commands attention and premium pricing. The texture is more delicate than
other oyster species, making them best suited for quick cooking methods
such as sauteing.

## Pleurotus tuberregium - The Sclerotial Oyster

*Pleurotus tuberregium* is unique among cultivated oyster mushrooms for
its ability to produce underground sclerotia -- hard, dense masses of
mycelial tissue that serve as survival structures. These sclerotia can be
harvested, dried, and stored for extended periods, making *P. tuberregium*
an important food source in West Africa where it is collected from the
wild and increasingly cultivated. Sclerotia are prepared by soaking,
grinding, and cooking into soups and stews. The species also produces
conventional above-ground fruiting bodies, but these are smaller and
less economically important than the sclerotia. Cultivation typically
involves inoculating buried substrate and allowing sclerotia to form
over several months before harvesting.

## Species Selection Considerations

Choosing the right *Pleurotus* species depends on multiple factors:
local climate and seasonal temperature range, available substrate
materials, market preferences and pricing, cultivation infrastructure
(growing house temperature control capability), and grower experience
level. In tropical regions with warm temperatures year-round, *P.
pulmonarius*, *P. cystidiosus*, and *P. djamor* are natural choices.
In temperate regions with seasonal variation, *P. ostreatus* and *P.
eryngii* are preferred. Many commercial operations grow multiple
species in sequence throughout the year, selecting warm-season and
cool-season species to maintain continuous production and diversify
their product range.
