## Critical Safety Principles

1. **Never eat a mushroom in the field** -- always identify, compare to references, and dry first
2. **If uncertain, throw it out** -- no psychedelic experience is worth your life
3. **Learn the dangerous species in your area before hunting** -- know your lookalikes
4. **Multiple identification features must match** -- never rely on a single characteristic
5. **Spore prints are essential** -- the spore print color is a fundamental identification tool
6. **Consider consulting an expert** -- local mycological societies can help verify identifications

## Deadly Lookalike Genera

### Galerina (Death Caps of the LBMs)

**Galerina marginata** (and related species) is the most dangerous lookalike:

- Contains **amatoxins** -- the same lethal toxins found in Amanita phalloides (the death cap)
- Amatoxins destroy the liver and kidneys; ingestion can be fatal even with prompt medical treatment
- Symptoms may not appear for 6-24 hours after ingestion, by which time organ damage is underway
- **Resembles:** Psilocybe species that grow on wood (P. cyanescens, P. stuntzii, P. allenii)
- **Key differences from Psilocybe:**
  - Spore print: **rusty brown** (not dark purple-brown like Psilocybe)
  - Has a **partial veil** (ring on stem) that is more prominent and persistent
  - Cap color: typically more brown/orange-brown
  - Often grows on the same wood substrates as wood-loving Psilocybe species
  - The cap is generally less hygrophanous than Psilocybe
- This is the **single most important lookalike to learn**

### Conocybe

Several Conocybe species contain psilocybin, but many are toxic or deadly:

- **Conocybe filaris** -- contains amatoxins (same deadly toxins as Galerina and Amanita phalloides)
- **Resembles:** Small psilocybin Conocybe species and immature Psilocybe
- **Key features:** Small, fragile mushrooms with conical caps; brown spore print
- The genus is too difficult for amateur identification -- **Conocybe species should be avoided entirely**

### Inocybe

Many Inocybe species contain **muscarine**, a toxic alkaloid:

- Muscarine causes: excessive salivation, sweating, tearing, blurred vision, abdominal cramps, diarrhea, slowed heartbeat, and in severe cases, respiratory failure
- A few Inocybe species contain psilocybin (e.g., I. corydalina, I. aeruginascens)
- **Resembles:** Wood- and ground-inhabiting Psilocybe species
- **Key features:** Generally fibrous, dry caps; brown spore print; often has a distinctive "spermatic" or radish-like odor
- **Inocybe species should be avoided entirely** by amateur collectors

## Amanita Species

### Amanita muscaria (Fly Agaric)

- The famous red-and-white mushroom is **not** a psilocybin mushroom
- Contains **ibotenic acid** and **muscimol** (GABAergic compounds, not serotonergic)
- Can cause nausea, vomiting, dizziness, confusion, and at high doses, more serious effects
- The field guide warns that it is "highly toxic" and "ingestion of more than a few specimens can cause illness and possibly death"
- Very distinct appearance (red ball-shaped cap with white warts) -- unlikely to be confused with Psilocybe if properly observed
- Used traditionally in Siberia and some other cultures
- See [[psychedelic-ancient-history]] for more on its cultural role

### Amanita phalloides (Death Cap) and Amanita virosa (Destroying Angel)

- Contain **amatoxins** -- among the most deadly natural poisons known
- Responsible for the majority of fatal mushroom poisonings worldwide
- Not likely to be confused with small Psilocybe species, but beginners should know them
- White spore print, volva (cup at base of stem), and membranous skirt are distinctive features

## Non-Psychedelic Blue-Staining Mushrooms

Some non-psychedelic mushrooms also exhibit bluing:

- **Boletus species** -- bruise blue, but have **pores** (sponge-like underside) instead of gills, and are large, bulbous, with proportioned thick stems and smooth yellow-brown caps
- These do not grow on dung and are visually very different from psilocybin species

## Toxin Types Summary

| Toxin | Found In | Effects | Onset | Lethality |
|-------|----------|---------|-------|-----------|
| Amatoxins | Galerina, Amanita phalloides, Conocybe filaris | Liver/kidney destruction | 6-24 hours | Very high |
| Muscarine | Inocybe, some Clitocybe | Salivation, sweating, GI distress, bradycardia | 30 min-2 hours | Moderate |
| Ibotenic acid/muscimol | Amanita muscaria, A. pantherina | Nausea, confusion, delirium, sedation | 30 min-2 hours | Low-moderate |
| Gyromitrin | Gyromitra esculenta | Hemolysis, seizures, liver damage | 2-24 hours | Moderate |

## What To Do If Poisoning Is Suspected

1. **Call emergency services immediately** or contact a poison control center
2. **Save a sample** of the mushroom (whole, including stem base and any volva) for identification
3. **Do not wait for symptoms** -- if amatoxin-containing species were ingested, early treatment is critical
4. **Note the time of ingestion** and approximate quantity
5. Hospital treatment may include activated charcoal, IV fluids, and in amatoxin cases, specific therapies like silibinin or N-acetylcysteine
