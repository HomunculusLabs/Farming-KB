### Basic Formulation

The standard sawdust substrate for shiitake consists of:

- **Broad-leaf (hardwood) sawdust**: 77-80% by dry weight. Hardwood species
  such as oak, beech, and various tropical hardwoods provide the appropriate
  lignin and cellulose ratios. Softwood sawdust generally performs poorly
  due to resin content and unfavorable lignin structure
- **Rice bran or wheat bran**: 18-20% by dry weight. Bran provides supplemental
  nitrogen, vitamins, and growth factors that accelerate mycelial colonization
  and improve yields
- **Gypsum (calcium sulfate)**: 1-2% by dry weight. Gypsum buffers pH,
  provides calcium and sulfur, and improves substrate structure
- **Sucrose or molasses**: 0.5-1% by dry weight. An optional supplement that
  provides readily available carbohydrates to jump-start mycelial growth

### Stamets Formula with Woodchips

Paul Stamets has proposed a modified formulation incorporating hardwood
woodchips alongside sawdust. The woodchips provide longer-term lignin
availability, extending the productive life of the substrate through
multiple flushes. This formulation is particularly relevant for operations
seeking to maximize total yield per unit of substrate over an extended
cropping period.

### Moisture Content

The substrate should be hydrated to 60-65% moisture content before
sterilization. This is measured by weight: a 10kg dry mix should be hydrated
to approximately 25-28kg wet weight. Proper moisture content is critical —
too dry slows colonization, too wet promotes anaerobic conditions and
bacterial contamination.

## Substrate Preparation and Sterilization

1. **Mixing**: Dry ingredients are thoroughly mixed before water is added
   gradually. Uneven mixing produces pockets of uncolonized substrate
2. **Rest period**: Substrate rests 2-4 hours for complete water absorption
3. **Bag filling**: Packed firmly (not compacted) into polypropylene bags
   (1-3kg capacity) with air spaces for mycelial growth
