# Thrips Pest Control on Cannabis

Thrips are small, fast-moving, winged insects that are more
common in [[query-how-do-i-choose-between-indoor-outdoor-and-greenhouse-cannabis-growing]] environments
than strictly indoor grows. These tiny pests, measuring just
0.04-0.05 inch (1-1.5 mm) long, can be difficult to see but are
relatively easy to spot once you know what to look for. They
appear in various colors including white, gray, and darker shades,
often with fine stripes on their bodies.

## Identification

Thrips are most reliably detected by shaking parts of the plant
over a white surface and watching for movement. When numerous
thrips are present, they tend to jump and run rather than fly to
safety. You will often see them as a herd of tiny specks moving
across foliage. Female thrips make small holes in soft plant
tissue where they deposit eggs that are virtually invisible to
the naked eye. Winged adults easily migrate from infested plants
to spread throughout the entire garden.

## Damage Symptoms

Thrips cause two types of damage. First, they scrape tissue from
leaves and buds, then suck out the plant juices for food. This
feeding pattern produces characteristic symptoms:

- Stipples: whitish-yellowish specks appear on top of leaves
- Reduced [[cervantes-photosynthesis-chlorophyll-plant-science]] as leaf tissue is destroyed
- Leaves become brittle and discolored over time
- Black specks of thrip feces are visible on leaf surfaces
- Thrips often feed inside [[cervantes-cannabis-flower-anatomy]], causing internal damage
- They can also wrap up and distort emerging leaves

Thrips feeding inside flower buds is particularly problematic
because the damage is hidden and can compromise bud quality
before it becomes visible. Infested buds may have a speckled,
unhealthy appearance and reduced resin production.

## Cultural and Physical Control

Cleanliness is the first line of defense against thrips. Remove
dead plant material and debris that could harbor populations.
Several physical control methods are effective:

- Blue or pink [[cervantes-sticky-traps-pest-monitoring]] capture flying adults and reduce populations
- Misting plants with water impairs thrip travel and movement
- Manual removal works if only a few thrips are present, but
  they are extremely difficult to catch by hand
- Isolate new plants before introducing them to the garden
- Remove and destroy heavily infested foliage

Thrips can become very vexing to control once they establish
themselves in a garden. Early detection is critical for
successful eradication. Regular inspection of the undersides
of leaves, especially on newer growth, helps catch
infestations before they spread.

## Biological Control

Several natural predators and parasites effectively control
thrips populations:

- Predatory mites including Amblyseius cucumeris,
  Amblyseius barkeri, Neoseiulus cucumeris, Iphiseius
  degenerans, and Euseius hibisci
- Parasitic wasps including Thripobius semiluteus,
  Ceranisus menes, and Goetheana shakespearei
- Pirate bugs (Orius species) are generalist predators
  that consume thrips along with other small pests
- The fungus Verticillium lecanii is an effective biological
  control agent against thrips

Biological controls work best when introduced early, before
thrips populations explode. See [[cervantes-biological-pest-control-predators]] for detailed
information on using predators and parasites in cannabis
gardens.

## Chemical and Spray Control

Several spray options are available when cultural and biological
methods are insufficient:

- Homemade tobacco-nicotine-based sprays provide knockdown
- Commercial [[cervantes-pyrethrum-pyrethroid-insecticides]] formulations are effective against thrips
- Synthetic pyrethroids offer longer residual activity
- [[cervantes-neem-oil-organic-pest-control]]
  is a gentler option for minor infestations

Apply sprays two to four times at five to ten day intervals.
This schedule targets different life stages of the thrips,
since eggs, larvae, and adults may all be present
simultaneously. Thrips have a relatively short life cycle,
so multiple applications are necessary to break the
reproduction cycle completely.

## Environmental Considerations

Thrips thrive in warm, dry conditions. Maintaining proper
[[cervantes-humidity-control-dehumidification]] and
[[cervantes-reservoir-temperature-management]] can help
suppress populations. Avoid overly dry conditions in
[[query-how-do-i-choose-between-indoor-outdoor-and-greenhouse-cannabis-growing]] where thrips
are most problematic. Good [[cervantes-air-quality-ventilation]] also makes it harder for
thrips to establish themselves on plants.
