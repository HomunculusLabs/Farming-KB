## Acidophilic and Alkalophilic Fungi
Acidophilic and alkalophilic fungi are adapted to grow at pH
values outside the near-neutral range that supports most fungal
species. These extremophilic fungi colonize specialized habitats
ranging from [[mushroom-natural-habitats]] with pH values of 3-4 are
relatively common, including many lakes (increasing in number
with acid rain deposition), some pine soils, and acidic bogs.
More extreme acid environments with pH values of 3 or less are
rare and commonly associated with hot springs, coal mine refuse
piles, drainage waters, and mining effluents.
### Fungal Communities in Acid Soils
Many forest soils are naturally acidic due to organic acid
production during litter decomposition. Fungal communities in
acidic forest soils are often dominated by acid-tolerant species
that may be inhibited or absent from neutral to alkaline soils.
The low pH of some soils can act as a selective filter, favoring
particular fungal taxa over others.
### Acidophilic Adaptations
Acidophilic fungi maintain intracellular pH near neutrality
despite extremely acidic external conditions. Adaptations include:
- Proton-pumping ATPase systems that expel excess protons
- Modified membrane lipid composition
- Production of acid-stable enzymes
- Efficient metal ion sequestration systems to prevent toxicity
  at low pH
## Alkalophilic Fungi
### Natural Alkaline Habitats
Alkaline environments with high pH include:
- Alkaline soda lakes and soda deserts: pH values greater than
  10, distributed widely in tropical and subtropical regions
- Limestone caves: approximately 30% of fungi isolated are
  alkaline-tolerant or alkalophilic
- Concrete and cement surfaces: pH up to 11
- Anthropogenic sources: cement manufacturing, electroplating,
  paper manufacturing, lye treatment of potatoes and hides
- Animal excreta deposits: create transient patches of high
  alkalinity
### Known Alkalophilic Species
The early work of Johnson (1923) indicated that Fusarium
bullatum, F. oxysporum, and Penicillium variabile could grow
at pH values of 11. However, those fungi neutralize the alkaline
culture medium to approximately pH 7, so their characterization
as alkalophiles may be inappropriate (true alkalophiles grow
optimally at high pH without neutralizing the medium).
