# Acidophilic and Alkalophilic Fungi

Acidophilic and alkalophilic fungi are adapted to grow at pH values outside the near-neutral range that supports most fungal species. These extremophilic fungi colonize specialized habitats ranging from acid mine drainage to alkaline soda lakes and concrete surfaces.

## pH Tolerance Ranges

Most fungi germinate and grow well in weakly acidic to neutral pH ranges (approximately pH 4-9). Microorganisms able to grow or survive at pH values outside these limits are considered acid-tolerant or alkali(ne)-tolerant. The extremes of pH in natural environments include:
- **Acid environments**: acid mine drainage (pH 1-2), acid soils (pH 3-4), acidic bogs, volcanic spring effluents
- **Alkaline environments**: alkaline soils (pH 9), alkaline lakes (pH 10), concrete surfaces (pH up to 11)

## Acidophilic Fungi

### Natural Acid Habitats

Mildly acidic natural habitats with pH values of 3-4 are relatively common, including many lakes (increasing in number with acid rain deposition), some pine soils, and acidic bogs. More extreme acid environments with pH values of 3 or less are rare and commonly associated with hot springs, coal mine refuse piles, drainage waters, and mining effluents.

### Fungal Communities in Acid Soils

Many forest soils are naturally acidic due to organic acid production during litter decomposition. Fungal communities in acidic forest soils are often dominated by acid-tolerant species that may be inhibited or absent from neutral to alkaline soils. The low pH of some soils can act as a selective filter, favoring particular fungal taxa over others.

### Acidophilic Adaptations

Acidophilic fungi maintain intracellular pH near neutrality despite extremely acidic external conditions. Adaptations include:
- Proton-pumping ATPase systems that expel excess protons
- Modified membrane lipid composition
- Production of acid-stable enzymes
- Efficient metal ion sequestration systems to prevent toxicity at low pH

## Alkalophilic Fungi

### Natural Alkaline Habitats

Alkaline environments with high pH include:
- Alkaline soda lakes and soda deserts: pH values greater than 10, distributed widely in tropical and subtropical regions, characterized by high NaCO3 concentrations
- Limestone caves: approximately 30% of fungi isolated are alkaline-tolerant or alkalophilic
- Concrete and cement surfaces: pH up to 11
- Anthropogenic sources: cement manufacturing, electroplating, paper manufacturing, lye treatment of potatoes and animal hides
- Animal excreta deposits: create transient patches of high alkalinity

### Known Alkalophilic Species

The early work of Johnson (1923) indicated that Fusarium bullatum, F. oxysporum, and Penicillium variabile could grow at pH values of 11. However, those fungi neutralize the alkaline culture medium to approximately pH 7, so their characterization as alkalophiles may be inappropriate (true alkalophiles grow optimally at high pH without neutralizing the medium).

An alkalophilic strain of Aspergillus oryzae isolated from soil in Japan can grow well at pH values of 9 and 9.5 (Horikoshi 1991). Goto and colleagues (1981) identified Exophiala alcalophila, a pleomorphic black yeastlike member of the Herpotrichiellaceae, growing in soil at pH 10.4. Species of Acremonium and Fusarium are the fungi most frequently isolated from slightly alkaline forest soils and limestone caves.

## Fungi on Rock and Stone Surfaces

Rocks exposed to high solar radiation, high temperatures, low nutrient availability, high electrolyte concentration, and low relative humidity are extreme environments colonized by unique stress-tolerant fungi. Black yeasts and dematiaceous mycelial fungi have been isolated from various rock surfaces and crevices in stone monuments (Urzi et al. 1995; Sterflinger and Krumbein 1997). These fungi share high melanin production, resistance to high temperatures and UV radiation, and restricted meristematic growth morphology. Many rock-inhabiting fungi are both alkaline-tolerant (from the mineral substrate) and desiccation-tolerant.

## Ecological Significance

Acidophilic and alkalophilic fungi contribute to:
- Weathering of rocks and minerals
- Decomposition in extreme pH environments
- Bioremediation of contaminated sites (especially acid mine drainage)
- Deterioration of stone monuments and concrete structures
- Nutrient cycling in specialized ecosystems

## Sampling Considerations

When sampling fungi from extreme pH environments:
- Measure and record pH at each sampling location
- Use buffered media for isolation to maintain target pH
- Include control media at neutral pH to assess generalist species
- Consider that some apparent alkalophiles may be neutralizing the medium during growth
- Acidophiles may be overlooked if neutral extraction media are used
