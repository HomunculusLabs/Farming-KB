# Cannabis Subjective Therapeutic Window

## Overview

The subjective therapeutic window is the precise range of cannabis intake that produces the desired therapeutic effect without triggering [[blesching-cannabis-safety-adverse-effects-evidence]]. Evidence has shown that the vast majority of [[adverse-effects-and-harm-reduction-psilocybin]] are directly related to using cannabis in excess of this window. Taking too little can be sub-optimal, while too much can actually increase the very symptoms you are attempting to treat.

For example, cannabis has long been used to reduce anxiety and return to a state of relaxation. However, if too much cannabis is consumed too rapidly, feelings of apprehension and anxiety may actually multiply rather than diminish. This biphasic dose-response curve is fundamental to understanding cannabis therapeutics.

## Factors That Determine Your Therapeutic Window

Establishing one's therapeutic window is governed by three primary factors:

### 1. The Body's Present Endocannabinoid State
The current balance or deficiency in your endocannabinoid system directly affects how you respond to cannabis. This includes:
- Baseline levels of anandamide (the "[[anandamide-bliss-molecule]]")
- CB1 and CB2 receptor density and sensitivity
- Overall ECS tone — whether you are CB1-deficient or CB2-deficient

Patients dealing with neurological disorders or mental/emotional problems are most likely lacking CB1 activation. These patients would benefit from strains with a higher THC:[[cannabis-thc-cbd-ratio-guide-for-conditions]].

Patients who are immune-compromised or experience chronic inflammation are most likely CB2-deficient. These patients would benefit from strains with a lower THC:CBD ratio, favoring CBD.

### 2. The Cannabinoid Profile in the Medicine
Different [[cannabis-microbiome-otu-abundance-vs-presence-cannabis-strains]] and preparations contain different ratios of cannabinoids and terpenes. The ratio determines whether the whole plant's constituents are additive or cancel each other out.

**Sativa-dominant strains** (higher THC:CBD ratio):
- Generally stimulating, energizing, uplifting
- More mental/emotional effects
- Better for daytime use, depression
- Increases alertness

**Indica-dominant strains** (lower THC:CBD ratio):
- Generally sedating, relaxing, grounding
- More physical effects
- Better for evening use, anxiety, sleep
- Natural sleeping aid

### 3. The Form of Consumption
The delivery mechanism dramatically affects onset time, duration, and the therapeutic window width. Inhalation produces near-instant results; ingesting plant materials may not show effects until hours later, especially on a full stomach.

## Dosing Protocol: Inhalation (Smoke or Vapor)

Inhaling cannabis smoke or vapors produces rapid onset (typically within seconds to minutes), making it the most controllable method for establishing a therapeutic window.

### Step-by-Step Protocol
1. Take **one short to medium inhalation**
2. **Wait 5 to 10 minutes**
3. Assess how you feel — did this dose achieve your desired effect?
4. If not, inhale another vapor of about the same length
5. Wait again for 5 to 10 minutes
6. Continue until you find the "sweet spot"
7. **Stop. Do not inhale more.** More is not better.

### Critical Safety Points
- The line between an effective dose and an adverse effect is thin
- Stop immediately if you experience unwanted effects
- If adverse effects occur, wait a full day and start again at a lower dosage (shorter inhalation)
- If two medium inhalations produced the desired result, use two inhalations for future relief — this is your established window

## Dosing Protocol: Ingestion (Edibles, Oils, Tinctures)

Ingesting cannabis produces delayed onset (30 minutes to 2+ hours) and longer duration (4–8 hours), making precise dosing both more important and more challenging.

### Step-by-Step Protocol
1. Use an **exact and measured amount**
2. Take preferably **on an empty stomach** for most predictable results
3. **Wait at least one hour** (if on a full stomach, wait two hours)
4. Assess the effect
5. If needed, increase by the **same measured amount**
6. Repeat until the desired effect is achieved
7. **Do not be impatient** — the most common mistake is premature redosing

### The "I'm Not Feeling Anything" Trap
Many patients have made the mistake of thinking "I'm not feeling anything" and continuing to eat until it is too late and adverse effects occur. In the case of ingesting too much cannabis, the effect may last many hours and can be intense and very unpleasant.

### Establishing Future Doses
Once the effective amount is determined:
1. Remember the exact amount it took to achieve the effect
2. For subsequent uses, repeat the total amount minus about 20%
3. The 20% reduction allows for changes due to time and rate of digestion
4. Always follow the recommendations of your healthcare provider
