# PDC Trees and Forests

Pamphlet X of the 1981 Permaculture Design Course contains Bill Mollison's most detailed treatment of forests -- not as timber resources but as the primary organizing system of land, climate, and water. His analysis of forest ecology is one of the most original sections of the entire course.

## The Forest and Atmosphere

### Wind
"Wind completely disappears in an effective forest within a thousand meters. The forest is swallowing it. It is absorbing the total force of even gale force winds within a thousand meters, except at the crown."

"The forest forces 60% of the wind up." This creates a high pressure zone on the windward side with 15-20% more rainfall, and "belts of rain across the direction of the wind" at intervals of five tree lengths past the forest edge.

### Humidity
On a foggy night, air moving into the forest "will, within a hundred meters, reduce the humidity in the air by about 50%. This is called positive interception."

### Particles
"Particles carried by the wind -- they are reduced sometimes to about a quarter of their previous occurrences in the air mass." The forest "entraps much material" from the air, leading to the conclusion that "there is no shortage of any mineral or any element anywhere, because it is all on the move."

### Rain Nuclei
"The organic particles [pollen, bacteria, oil droplets from forests] are far more effective precipitation nuclei than the inorganic particles." About 60% of inland rain "falls from forest clouds, not sea clouds."

## The Forest as Water System

### Precipitation
In Tasmania, "up to 60% of our total precipitation is put down to this effect" of direct leaf interception of fog. "Only 14% of that water falls as rain -- trees catch 86%."

### Canopy Energy Absorption
"Even a violent thunderstorm doesn't come into the forest as anything but a fine mist." The canopy absorbs "thousands of pounds of kinetic energy." This water never hits the earth, so "any erosion from that pelting rain, which is an enormous force, just doesn't happen within forests."

### Water Storage
"Eighty-six percent of the mass of that forest is water. Ninety-six percent of its leaves and twigs are water." The forest is "a whole lot of vertical tanks."

The litter and humus of the forest floor absorb water before any percolation begins. With 30 inches of mineral soil below, "a 3-inch rainfall won't move at all."

### No Salting
"Water never travels upward again to the surface of the soil for evaporation. We therefore get no salting, no upward migration of salts."

### Slow Runoff
"On the floor of the forest, there is no such thing as a straight run-off system." Water persists much longer in the landscape. Runoff is "very, very slow in forests."

### Cutting Forests Diminishes Rainfall
Engineers say "'Let's cut the forest to increase the run-off.' They actually diminish the rainfall, drop the total water falling on the whole area to roughly 70% of what it was before."

## Forest Management Principles

### High Value Products First
"Our aims should be to leave as much of the biomass in the situation as possible, and to take out the smaller, highest value products." Seed is a good example.

### Dry Distillation
Load a brick-lined container, light a fire underneath. Products include methane, creosote, methanol, charcoal, and hot water -- all from the same fuel.

### Pioneer Species
"Trees that will stand alone in grassland or hostile areas" include acacias (hundreds of species, all nitrogen fixers), western and eastern red cedars. They "change the nature of the soil towards forest soils."

### The Nuclei Method
"Don't try to do it as a scatter of individual plants." Instead, "plant a small area densely and close it out." Two old women using only "two spoons and a couple of blunt table knives" re-established 1,500 acres of native Australian bush this way.

### Leave Some Forest Alone
"Maybe in every area we will find a patch of forest that should really be for itself." These undisturbed areas "can act as a control."

## Forest and Climate

### Snow Retention
"The forest probably delays melt at least a month" compared to open ground. Without forests, "that winter snow will melt extremely quickly, and cause sudden flooding."

### Temperature Regulation
"Even on very bitter nights in Tasmania, where we have thick forests above, we get a warm down-draft." Trees store heat as 86% water heat storage and release it slowly at night.

### Negative Ions
"The amount of negative ions increases sharply in the air stream" moving through forests. "Negative ions will take cigarette smoke out of the air very efficiently."

## The Critical Statement

"By the removal of ridge forests alone, we can produce deserts in any climate. By the removal of forests alone, we can remove soils."

"I am certain that the removal of the forest has been the main cause of the collapse of nations. Because when the forests go they just haven't the water, the soil, or the climate quality to sustain human life thereafter."

"So maybe we had better start to prize the forests a bit and to discover, not how to live without them, but how to live with them."
