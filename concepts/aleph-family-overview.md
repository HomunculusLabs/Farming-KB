# ALEPH Family of Psychedelic Amphetamines

## Overview

The ALEPH family is a series of thio-substituted (sulfur-containing) psychedelic amphetamines synthesized and evaluated by [[2c-family-compounds-overview]] of phenethylamines.

The original compound, [[shroom-scientific-study]] naming convention. ALEPH is specifically the para-isomer; positional isomers exist as ORTHO-DOT and META-DOT.

## Unfinished Compounds

Several ALEPH compounds were never completed:

- **ALEPH-3** (methallylthio): Attempted twice, years apart. The aldehyde synthesis produced impure products both times. The corresponding 2C-T-16 (allylthio) also reached only the nitrostyrene stage.
- **ALEPH-5** (cyclohexylthio): The nitrostyrene was obtained but "sits there on the shelf to be reduced... some inspired day."
- **ALEPH-16** (allylthio): The nitrostyrene (mp 114-115 deg C) was characterized but never reduced.

These unfinished compounds represent the practical limits of what could be achieved in a home laboratory setting, where [[md-family-structure-activity]]. They demonstrated that:

1. [[pihkal-sulfur-substitution-potency-sar]] at the 4-position is compatible with (and can enhance) psychedelic activity
2. The alkyl chain on sulfur significantly affects potency, duration, and character
3. Amphetamine and phenethylamine series can be systematically compared through shared substitution patterns
4. Some compound families show more inter-individual variability than others

The series ultimately gave way to the more thoroughly explored [[2c-family-compounds-overview]], but the ALEPH compounds remain important as early probes into sulfur-containing psychedelic chemistry.
