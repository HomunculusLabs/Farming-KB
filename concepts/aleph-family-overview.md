# ALEPH Family of Psychedelic Amphetamines

## Overview

The ALEPH family is a series of thio-substituted (sulfur-containing) psychedelic amphetamines synthesized and evaluated by [[alexander-shulgin]]. Named after the first letter of the Hebrew alphabet, the ALEPH compounds carry various alkylthio groups at the 4-position of the 2,5-dimethoxyamphetamine skeleton. They represent the amphetamine (3-carbon) counterparts of the [[2c-family-compounds-overview]] of phenethylamines.

The original compound, [[aleph-7-pihkal-entry-7]] (also known as DOT), was "probably the first sulfur-containing phenethylamine evaluated as a CNS stimulant or psychedelic." When first explored, no sulfur-containing phenethylamine had ever been tested for psychoactive properties, making the initial bioassay an exercise in extreme caution.

## Naming Convention

The ALEPH series follows a systematic numbering scheme:

- **ALEPH** (no number): 4-Methylthio (the original, PiHKAL #3)
- **ALEPH-2**: 4-Ethylthio (PiHKAL #4)
- **ALEPH-3**: 4-(beta-Methallylthio) -- never completed
- **ALEPH-4**: 4-Isopropylthio (PiHKAL #5)
- **ALEPH-5**: 4-Cyclohexylthio -- never completed (nitrostyrene sits on the shelf)
- **ALEPH-6**: 4-Phenylthio (PiHKAL #6)
- **ALEPH-7**: 4-Propylthio (PiHKAL #7)

The name DOT (desoxy- with thiomethyl) follows the [[shroom-scientific-study]] naming convention. ALEPH is specifically the para-isomer; positional isomers exist as ORTHO-DOT and META-DOT.

## Relationship to the 2C-T Series

Every ALEPH compound has a corresponding 2C-T phenethylamine (2-carbon chain) analogue:

| ALEPH Compound | Alkylthio Group | 2C-T Analogue |
|---------------|----------------|---------------|
| ALEPH | Methylthio | 2C-T |
| [[aleph-7-pihkal-entry-7]] | Ethylthio | [[2c-d-pihkal-entry-23]] |
| ALEPH-3 | Methallylthio | 2C-T-3/2C-T-20 (never made) |
| [[aleph-7-pihkal-entry-7]] | Isopropylthio | [[2c-d-pihkal-entry-23]] |
| ALEPH-5 | Cyclohexylthio | 2C-T-5 (never made) |
| ALEPH-6 | Phenylthio | -- |
| ALEPH-7 | Propylthio | [[2c-d-pihkal-entry-23]] |

The 2C-T series was "far more thoroughly explored" than the ALEPH series, likely because the phenethylamines are generally easier to synthesize and characterize.

## General Pharmacological Profile

The ALEPH compounds share several characteristics as a family:

- **Long duration:** 8-20+ hours depending on the substituent
- **Unpredictable dose-response:** A distinctive feature noted by Shulgin -- the same dose can produce dramatically different effects in different individuals
- **Potency:** Generally active in the 3-12 mg range, placing them among the more potent psychedelic amphetamines
- **Visual phenomena:** Visual effects are prominent, ranging from distortions to full hallucinations
- **Body effects:** Muscular tremor, physical discomfort, and long-lasting aftereffects are common

## The Unpredictability Problem

Shulgin identifies a hallmark of the ALEPH family: "there is no predictability of the dose/response relationship." One person at 4 mg of [[aleph-7-pihkal-entry-7]] maintained a fetal position for hours with substantial amnesia, while another at 8 mg experienced only a "bare threshold." This variability was not fully appreciated until ALEPH-7 had already been synthesized, making the entire series more exploratory than systematic.

## Unfinished Compounds

Several ALEPH compounds were never completed:

- **ALEPH-3** (methallylthio): Attempted twice, years apart. The aldehyde synthesis produced impure products both times. The corresponding 2C-T-16 (allylthio) also reached only the nitrostyrene stage.
- **ALEPH-5** (cyclohexylthio): The nitrostyrene was obtained but "sits there on the shelf to be reduced... some inspired day."
- **ALEPH-16** (allylthio): The nitrostyrene (mp 114-115 deg C) was characterized but never reduced.

These unfinished compounds represent the practical limits of what could be achieved in a home laboratory setting, where [[synthetic-tryptamine-methods]] shifted toward more promising directions.

## Significance in Shulgin's Research

The ALEPH family represents an important phase in Shulgin's systematic exploration of [[md-family-structure-activity]]. They demonstrated that:

1. Sulfur substitution at the 4-position is compatible with (and can enhance) psychedelic activity
2. The alkyl chain on sulfur significantly affects potency, duration, and character
3. Amphetamine and phenethylamine series can be systematically compared through shared substitution patterns
4. Some compound families show more inter-individual variability than others

The series ultimately gave way to the more thoroughly explored [[2c-family-compounds-overview]] and [[2c-family-compounds-overview]], but the ALEPH compounds remain important as early probes into sulfur-containing psychedelic chemistry.
