# Medicinal Mushroom Extraction and Preparation

Medicinal mushrooms contain bioactive compounds that require proper extraction to become bioavailable. Different compounds are extracted by different methods -- water for polysaccharides, alcohol for triterpenoids -- and understanding these differences is critical for effective preparation.

## Key Bioactive Compounds by Species

### Reishi (Ganoderma lucidum)
- Polysaccharides: immune-stimulating beta-glucans (water-soluble)
- Ganoderic acids: anti-coagulating, cholesterol-lowering (alcohol-soluble triterpenoids)
- Immuno-modulating proteins (Kino et al., 1989)
- Anti-inflammatory activity comparable to hydrocortisone (Stavinoha, 1990)
- Low molecular weight polysaccharide fraction prolongs HIV-exposed lymphocyte survival (Kim et al., 1994)

### Shiitake (Lentinula edodes)
- Lentinan: beta-1,3 glucan with beta-1,6 branchings (water-soluble), approved anti-cancer drug in Japan
- KS-2: anti-tumor polysaccharide active via oral administration
- Activates killer and helper T-cells

### Maitake (Grifola frondosa)
- D-fraction: beta-glucan extract with potent immune-modulating activity
- MD-fraction: approved adjunct cancer therapy in Japan
- Anti-HIV activity demonstrated by NIH drug screening
- Blood sugar regulation effects

### Lion's Mane (Hericium erinaceus)
- Erinacines and hericenones: stimulate nerve growth factor (NGF) synthesis
- Unique compounds found only in this genus
- Neuroprotective and potentially neurogenerative

### Enoki (Flammulina velutipes)
- FVP (Flammulina velutipes polysaccharide): anti-tumor
- 80-100% effective against Sarcoma 180 and Ehrlich carcinoma

### Nameko (Pholiota nameko)
- Water extracts: 60% effective against Sarcoma 180
- NaOH extracts: 90% effective against Sarcoma 180
- Improves resistance to Staphylococcus bacteria

## Extraction Methods

### Hot Water Extraction (Tea/Decoction)
Best for: polysaccharides, beta-glucans, water-soluble compounds
- Dried mushroom pieces boiled in water for 5-60 minutes
- Steeped for 30 minutes to several hours
- Strain and serve
- Traditional method for Reishi tea: 3-5 grams fresh per person daily
- Simmering at high heat develops stronger flavor in Nameko
- Multiple extractions from same material increase yield

### Alcohol Extraction (Tincture)
Best for: triterpenoids, ganoderic acids, alcohol-soluble compounds
- Dried mushroom material soaked in ethanol (40-95%) for weeks to months
- Glycerin tinctures as alcohol-free alternative
- Often combined with water extract for full-spectrum preparations

### Dual Extraction
Combines water and alcohol extraction to capture both polysaccharide and triterpenoid fractions. Process: water extraction first, then alcohol extraction of the residue, or combine both extracts. Considered the most comprehensive preparation method.

### Submerged Fermentation (Liquid Culture)
Mycelium grown in liquid media, then extracted. Considered "traditional" in China for Reishi. The mycelium itself contains bioactive compounds, though profiles may differ from fruitbody-derived extracts.

## Commercial Product Forms
- Dried whole mushrooms (traditional)
- Pills and capsules (powdered fruitbodies or extracts)
- Teas (dried pieces or powders)
- Tinctures (alcohol extracts)
- Syrups (cultured mycelium extracts)
- Combined formulations (e.g., Ginseng + Ling Chi liquid extracts, popular in China and Japan)
- Spore oil (Reishi spores cracked open for enhanced absorption)

## Quality Considerations
- Antler forms of Reishi have reduced hymenial surface, producing sweeter, less bitter tea
- Yellow Reishi strains are more often bitter than red and black strains
- Well-developed conk forms of Reishi have more bitter aftertaste (triterpenoid content)
- Post-dry autoclave treatment prevents insect larvae in long-grown specimens
- Dry at low temperatures (below 100 F) to preserve medicinal compounds
