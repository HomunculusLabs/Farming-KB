# Medicinal Mushroom Extraction and Preparation

Medicinal mushrooms contain bioactive compounds that require proper
extraction to become bioavailable. Different compounds are extracted by
different methods: water for polysaccharides, alcohol for triterpenoids.
Understanding these differences is critical for effective preparation.

## Key Bioactive Compounds by Species

The bioactive compounds discussed below — particularly [[beta-glucans]] and various polysaccharides — are the primary therapeutic agents in [[functional-foods-medicinal-mushrooms]]. Understanding their extraction requirements is essential for maximising health benefits. See also the individual species guides: [[medicinal-mushroom-guide-reishi]], [[medicinal-mushroom-guide-shiitake]], and [[medicinal-mushroom-guide-lions-mane]].

### Reishi (Ganoderma lucidum)
- Polysaccharides: immune-stimulating beta-glucans (water-soluble)
- Ganoderic acids: anti-coagulating, cholesterol-lowering
  (alcohol-soluble triterpenoids)
- Immuno-modulating proteins (Kino et al., 1989)
- Anti-inflammatory activity comparable to hydrocortisone
  (Stavinoha, 1990)
- Low molecular weight polysaccharide fraction prolongs HIV-exposed
  lymphocyte survival (Kim et al., 1994)

### Shiitake (Lentinula edodes)
- Lentinan: beta-1,3 glucan with beta-1,6 branchings (water-soluble),
  approved anti-cancer drug in Japan
- KS-2: anti-tumor polysaccharide active via oral administration
- Activates killer and helper T-cells
- Eritadenine: cholesterol-lowering compound unique to shiitake

### Maitake (Grifola frondosa)
- D-fraction: beta-glucan extract with potent immune-modulating
  activity
- MD-fraction: approved adjunct cancer therapy in Japan
- Anti-HIV activity demonstrated by NIH drug screening
- Blood sugar regulation effects for diabetes management

### Lion's Mane (Hericium erinaceus)
- Erinacines and hericenones: stimulate nerve growth factor (NGF)
  synthesis
- Unique compounds found only in this genus
- Neuroprotective and potentially neurogenerative
- Promising research for cognitive decline and peripheral nerve
  regeneration

### Turkey Tail (Trametes versicolor)
- PSK (polysaccharide-K): approved cancer drug in Japan since 1970s
- PSP: protein-bound polysaccharide approved as medicine in China
- Strong immune modulation through T-cell activation

### Enoki (Flammulina velutipes)
- FVP (Flammulina velutipes polysaccharide): anti-tumor
- 80-100% effective against Sarcoma 180 and Ehrlich carcinoma in
  laboratory studies

### Nameko (Pholiota nameko)
- Water extracts: 60% effective against Sarcoma 180
- NaOH extracts: 90% effective against Sarcoma 180
- Improves resistance to Staphylococcus bacteria

## Extraction Methods

### Hot Water Extraction (Tea/Decoction)
Best for: polysaccharides, beta-glucans, water-soluble compounds.

Process:
- Dried mushroom pieces boiled in water for 5 to 60 minutes
- Steeped for 30 minutes to several hours for milder extraction
- Strain and serve as tea or reduce to concentrate

Traditional method for Reishi tea: 3 to 5 grams fresh per person
daily. Simmering at high heat develops stronger flavour in Nameko.
Multiple extractions from the same material increase total yield.
A second extraction typically yields 30-50% of the first extraction.

### Alcohol Extraction (Tincture)
Best for: triterpenoids, ganoderic acids, alcohol-soluble compounds.

Process:
- Dried mushroom material soaked in ethanol (40-95%) for weeks
  to months
- Glycerin tinctures serve as alcohol-free alternatives
- Often combined with water extract for full-spectrum preparations
- Typical ratio: 1 part dried mushroom to 5 parts solvent (1:5)

### Dual Extraction
Combines water and alcohol extraction to capture both polysaccharide
and triterpenoid fractions. This is considered the most comprehensive
preparation method for mushrooms like Reishi that contain significant
amounts of both water-soluble and alcohol-soluble bioactives.

Process:
1. Perform water extraction first (decoction)
2. Separate water extract and retain the marc (residue)
3. Soak the marc in ethanol for 2 to 6 weeks
4. Combine both extracts or use separately
5. Optionally evaporate alcohol at low temperature

### Submerged Fermentation (Liquid Culture)
Mycelium grown in liquid media, then extracted. Considered
"traditional" in China for Reishi production. The mycelium itself
contains bioactive compounds, though profiles may differ from
fruitbody-derived extracts. Liquid culture allows rapid, controlled
production but may not replicate the full compound diversity found in
slowly-grown fruitbodies.

## Dosage Considerations

Proper dosing depends on the species, preparation method, and intended
effect:
- Reishi tea: 3 to 5 grams dried fruitbody per day
- Shiitake: 6 to 16 grams fresh or 1 to 3 grams dried daily
- Maitake D-fraction: 100 to 500 mg standardized extract daily
- Lion's Mane: 500 to 3000 mg extract daily for cognitive support
- Turkey Tail PSK: 3 grams daily as adjunct cancer therapy

Always start with low doses and increase gradually. Consult a
healthcare practitioner before using medicinal mushrooms alongside
conventional treatments, especially during chemotherapy or
immunosuppressive therapy.

## Commercial Product Forms

- Dried whole mushrooms (traditional, most versatile)
- Pills and capsules (powdered fruitbodies or standardised extracts)
- Teas (dried pieces or powders for decoction)
- Tinctures (alcohol extracts for concentrated dosing)
- Syrups (cultured mycelium extracts, popular in Asia)
- Combined formulations (e.g., Ginseng + Reishi liquid extracts)
- Spore oil (Reishi spores cracked open for enhanced absorption)

## Quality Considerations

- Antler forms of Reishi have reduced hymenial surface, producing
  sweeter, less bitter tea
- Yellow Reishi strains are more often bitter than red and black
  strains, indicating higher triterpenoid content
- Well-developed conk forms of Reishi have more bitter aftertaste
  (correlated with triterpenoid content)
- Post-dry autoclave treatment prevents insect larvae in long-grown
