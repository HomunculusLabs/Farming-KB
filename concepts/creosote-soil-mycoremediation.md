# Creosote-Contaminated Soil Mycoremediation

Creosote is a complex mixture of [[singh-fungal-biodegradation-of-polycyclic-aromatic-hydrocarbons]] hydrocarbons (PAHs) and
phenolic compounds that has been used extensively as a wood preservative.
Wood-preserving industries have historically relied heavily on creosote and
anthracene oil, with creosote containing approximately 85% PAHs by weight.
Contamination of soils at former wood treatment facilities represents a
significant environmental challenge, as creosote components include both low
and high molecular weight PAHs that vary widely in solubility, toxicity, and
recalcitrance. Creosote residues from wood-preserving industries are also a
major source of PAHs in water systems.

## Creosote Composition and Environmental Impact

Creosote consists primarily of two- and three-ring PAHs such as naphthalene,
acenaphthene, fluorene, phenanthrene, and anthracene, along with smaller
quantities of higher molecular weight compounds including fluoranthene, pyrene,
chrysene, and benzo[a]pyrene. The phenolic fraction includes cresols, xylenols,
and other alkylphenols that contribute to the acute toxicity of creosote.
These compounds persist in soil due to their hydrophobic nature and strong
sorption to [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]], making creosote-contaminated sites particularly
challenging to remediate using conventional approaches.

## Field-Scale Treatment Approaches

Field studies by Lamar and colleagues represent some of the most comprehensive
evaluations of fungal inoculation technology for creosote remediation. These
workers used P. chrysosporium, P. sordida, and T. hirsuta to detoxify
pentachlorophenol and creosote-contaminated soil on a field scale. The general
approach involved preparing [[ingham-fungal-inoculum-sourcing]] on lignocellulosic substrates such
as aspen wood chips, mixing the colonized substrate with contaminated soil, and
maintaining appropriate moisture, aeration, and nutrient conditions. Fungal
inoculation improved transformation levels of PAHs in field trials, though
concentrations of the more complex five- and six-ring PAHs remained resistant
to complete degradation.

## Soil Amendment Strategies

The effectiveness of fungal creosote remediation is significantly enhanced by
appropriate soil amendment. [[cervantes-organic-amendments-chart]] such as straw, wood chips, corn
cobs, and sawdust serve multiple functions: they provide a growth substrate for
the fungal inoculum, improve soil structure and aeration, increase water-
holding capacity, and may enhance bioavailability of PAHs through surfactant-
like effects. Several workers have observed that soil amendment with straw
improves degradation of PAHs, including benzo[a]pyrene, in soil microcosms.
The selection of amendment material requires careful consideration to avoid
introducing additional contamination.

## Molecular Weight-Dependent Degradation Patterns

A consistent finding across creosote remediation studies is that degradation
efficiency decreases with increasing PAH molecular weight. Two- and three-ring
PAHs in creosote-contaminated soil are generally degraded more readily than
four-ring compounds, while five- and six-ring PAHs are the most recalcitrant.
This pattern reflects both the decreasing water solubility and increasing
sorption of higher molecular weight PAHs, which limits their bioavailability
to fungal enzymes. The use of surfactants has been proposed as a strategy to
enhance bioavailability of these recalcitrant compounds, though results have
been mixed and surfactant selection must be carefully evaluated to avoid
toxicity to the fungal inoculant.

## Integration with Indigenous Microflora

Fungal creosote remediation in non-sterile soil involves complex interactions
between the introduced fungal inoculant and indigenous soil microorganisms.

fungi initially attack high molecular weight PAHs, transforming them into more
bioavailable intermediates that are subsequently mineralized by indigenous
bacteria. This synergistic approach leverages the complementary capabilities
