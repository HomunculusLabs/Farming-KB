# Fungi Around the World -- Species Distribution Fungi are among the most widespread and ecologically diverse organisms on Earth, found in virtually every terrestrial and aquatic habitat from polar ice caps to tropical rainforests. Estimated at 2.2 to 3.8 million species globally, only approximately 150,000 have been formally described, meaning the vast majority of fungal diversity remains undocumented. The geographic distribution of fungi is shaped by climate, vegetation type, soil conditions, and co-evolutionary relationships with specific plant hosts. ## Regions Covered ### Amazon The Amazon basin contains extraordinary fungal diversity due to its tropical climate, vast old-growth forests, and high humidity. The region is home to numerous psilocybin-containing species, bioluminescent fungi, and a vast unexplored mycological territory. The sheer volume of fungal biomass in tropical forests is staggering — mycorrhizal networks connect the majority of trees in old-growth tropical forests, creating an underground "wood wide web" of nutrient exchange and chemical signaling. The Amazon's fungal diversity is intimately linked to its plant diversity. Each tree species may host dozens of unique endophytic and mycorrhizal fungal species, meaning deforestation directly destroys unknown fungal taxa. The rate of fungal extinction in tropical regions is estimated to be far higher than the rate of discovery and description. ### Africa African fungal diversity spans from tropical West and Central Africa to the southern temperate zones. Notable features include edible species collected by indigenous peoples for centuries (including termite mushroom cultivation in parts of West and East Africa), bracket fungi of savanna woodlands, and dung- inhabiting species of grassland ecosystems. ## Climate Change and Shifting Distributions

Climate change is altering fungal distribution patterns worldwide. As
temperatures rise and precipitation regimes shift, fungal species are migrating
toward higher latitudes and elevations. In the Northern Hemisphere, ectomycorrhizal
fungi historically associated with boreal forests are appearing further north,
while tropical pathogens like *Cryptococcus gattii* have established populations
