# Gottlieb Psilocybin Cultivation Grain Spawn Production

Gottlieb's "The Psilocybin Producer's Guide" (1976) describes a liquid
broth-based cultivation system rather than traditional grain spawn. The
guide uses quart mason jars filled with Potato Dextrose Yeast (PDY) broth
as the [[lichens-primary-production-soil-formation-dighton]] medium, inoculated from agar stock cultures.
While not grain spawn in the conventional sense, the system serves the same
function: expanding mycelial mass from a small inoculum to a bulk
cultivation quantity.

## Media Preparation

### PDY Broth (Potato Dextrose Yeast)

1. Wash 250 grams of unpeeled potatoes, slice 1/8 inch thick.
2. Rinse slices several times in cool tap water until water runs clear.
3. Drain in a colander, rinse once with distilled water.
4. Cook potato slices in distilled water until tender.
5. Strain cooking liquid through flannel cloth or several layers of
   cheesecloth into a flask.
6. Rinse boiled potatoes several times with distilled water; add rinse
   waters to flask. Discard potatoes.
7. Add enough distilled water to make one liter total.
8. Bring to a boil, add 10 grams of dextrose and 1.5 grams of yeast
   extract. (Agar is omitted for broth; include 15 grams agar for PDA
   solid medium, added slowly to prevent boil-over.)
9. Fill mason jars halfway with hot or cool liquid.

### MEA (Malt Extract Agar) -- Alternative Stock Culture Medium

- To 1 liter gently boiling distilled water: add 20 g malt extract, 20 g
  agar (slowly to prevent boil-over), 100 mg potassium phosphate dibasic
  (K2HPO4), and 100 mg calcium carbonate.
- Pour hot liquid into culture dishes, filled halfway.
- Used for species that perform better on malt-based media and for
  alternating stock [[agar-culture-media]] to combat senescence.

## Sterilization (Autoclaving)

1. Place containers (no more than half full with medium) in a canning-type
   [[double-layer-tinfoil-jar-covering-pressure-cooker-method]].
2. Leave lids loose enough to allow escape of internal pressure (otherwise
   containers may crack).
3. Seal cooker, keep stopcock open. Heat on high until thick steam vents.
4. Close stopcock; bring pressure to 15-20 psi (250 degrees F) for 30
   minutes.
5. Do not exceed this temperature/duration -- sugars caramelize at higher
   temps, which inhibits [[brassinosteroid-psilocybe-cubensis-mycelial-growth-research]] and [[gottlieb-psilocybin-production-extraction-methods]].
6. Turn off heat; allow cooker to cool to room temperature before releasing
   stopcock (sudden pressure change causes containers to boil over).
7. Discard any containers that have cracked during sterilization.
8. Hold all containers at room temperature for three days to test
   sterilization effectiveness. Discard any showing foreign mold growth.
  Thoroughly clean and resterilize contaminated jars before reuse.

## Inoculation of Crop Jars

1. Select the most vigorous, fastest-growing, uncontaminated mycelia from
   stock culture dishes.
2. Healthy mycelia appear as pure white fibrous mats, sometimes with a
   light bluish tinge.
3. Contaminants appear as rapid-growing white circular spots with blue-green
  centers, or as surface scums or fuzzy clusters in gray, black, yellow,
  green, or blue. Discard any contaminated cultures.
4. Spray room and clean work area per pure [[agar-culture-technique]].
5. Spray outsides of stock dishes and culture jars with disinfectant.
6. Lift stock dish lid just enough; pick up a mycelium fragment with a
   flame-sterilized (and cooled) inoculation loop.
7. Lift jar cover, place fragment in broth, cover immediately.
8. Repeat for all jars. Refrigerate unused stock cultures.
9. Tighten jar covers, shake well to disperse inoculum and aerate the
   medium.
10. Loosen lids; place jars on growing shelf at 70-75 degrees F for
    [[brassinosteroid-morphological-effects-psilocybe-cubensis-fruiting]] (adjust for other species).

## Incubation and Maintenance

- Growth period: 10-12 days.
- Every 2-3 days: tighten covers, shake to aerate and disperse mycelium,
  reloosen covers, return to shelf.
