## Federal Legal Framework and Exemptions

Under federal law, peyote is a Schedule I controlled substance. However, NAC members with tribal
membership recognized by the Bureau of Indian Affairs are exempt from laws prohibiting peyote
possession and distribution for traditional ceremonial use. This exemption is rooted not in the
First Amendment's free exercise clause but in the federal government's treaty-based obligation to
uphold the traditions of recognized tribal peoples with limited sovereignty.

The legal framework contains notable ambiguities. Blood quantum requirements differ by jurisdiction:
in Texas, a minimum of 25% Native American blood is required, while elsewhere the requirement is not
clearly defined. The transportation of peyote by First Nation peoples of Canada through the United
States remains legally uncertain. The use of peyote by state-recognized tribes lacking federal
recognition, and by non-natives married to Native Americans, has been handled on a case-by-case
basis by the NAC for decades.
