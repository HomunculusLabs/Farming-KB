# Cannabis and Pain Management

For the last 5,000 years, cannabis has been used as an analgesic in
nearly all [[entheogens-in-ancient-cultures]] from Sumeria to China, Babylon, the Indus
Valley, and Judean, Greek, Roman, and Islamic civilizations. Modern
[[psilocybin-isolation-and-early-scientific-research]] confirms that cannabis is safe and effective when
used properly to treat both neuropathic pain and, in some cases,
nociceptive pain, while also functioning as an anti-inflammatory and
relaxing agent.

## How Cannabinoids Reduce Pain

The human body's endocannabinoid system plays a central role in pain
regulation. Naturally occurring cannabinoids or external cannabinoids
signal [[cannabinoid-receptors-cb1-and-cb2]] to modulate the sensation of pain through
two primary receptor types:

CB1 receptors are located in the central nervous system (brain and
[[blesching-cannabis-spinal-cord-injuries]]), controlling information received by the senses and
enabling response reactions. They are found in brain regions regulating
motor control, attention, emotion, thinking, habits, and memory.

CB2 receptors cluster in the body's periphery, especially in tissue
involved in immune responses. The spleen houses a high concentration of
CB2 receptors, making CB2 engagement important for [[cannabis-chronic-pain-management]]
associated with autoimmune diseases.

## Safety Advantage Over Opiates

CB1 receptors are absent in the brain's medulla oblongata, which
regulates heart and respiratory function. This makes cannabinoid-based
pain management inherently safer than opiates, which can cause death
from respiratory deficiencies or arrests. Cannabis carries a "unique
distinction of safety over four millennia of analgesic usage: no deaths
due to direct toxicity of cannabis have ever been documented in the
medical literature."

## Opiate-Sparing Effects

Cannabinoids cannot replace opiates' strong analgesic properties,
especially in cases of new trauma or injuries. However, in conjunction
with a reduced amount of opiates, cannabinoids produce a synergy of
effective pain control while reducing the risk of [[blesching-cannabis-safety-adverse-effects-evidence]] and
the habit-forming potential of opiates. Pre-clinical studies indicate
that delta-9-THC and morphine can be useful in low-dose combination as
an analgesic.

## Pain Conditions Treated

An overview of studies (2004-2009) determined that cannabinoids
effectively relieve pain involving inflammation, including:
- Post-surgical pain
- Rheumatism and [[blesching-cannabis-rheumatoid-arthritis]]
- Chronic neuropathic pain
- Fibromyalgia
- Migraine
- Pain due to [[blesching-cannabis-pain-advanced-cancer]]
- Chronic non-malignant pain

## Dose-Dependent Effects

Research from San Diego demonstrated the critical importance of dose
dependency in pain reduction. In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-
controlled trial using capsaicin-induced pain, the medium dose produced
significant pain reduction at 45 minutes, while the high dose
significantly increased pain. This illustrates that more is not better:
exceeding the [[cannabis-subjective-therapeutic-window-dosing]] window can actually increase pain.

## Inflammatory Pain and CB2

CB2 receptor engagement is particularly important for treating chronic
pain associated with autoimmune diseases. Numerous plant-based
cannabinoids, other plant constituents, [[blesching-cannabis-synthetic-cannabinoids]], and
endogenous cannabinoids engage significantly in reducing inflammation,
which often accompanies autoimmune disease. An overview of existing
studies confirmed cannabinoids effectively relieve inflammatory pain
across multiple conditions.
