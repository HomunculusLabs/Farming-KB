# Mycorrhizal Inoculation Guide

## Overview

Mycorrhizal inoculation involves introducing beneficial mycorrhizal fungi to plant root zones to improve nutrient uptake, disease resistance, and overall plant health. This guide covers commercial products, application methods, effectiveness data, and crop specificity. See [[glomeromycota-arbuscular-mycorrhizae]] for the science behind arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi, and [[fungal-symbiosis-types]] for the broader context of fungal symbiosis.

## Why Inoculate?

Benefits of mycorrhizal colonization:
- Increased phosphorus uptake (30-80% improvement in P acquisition)
- Enhanced micronutrient uptake (zinc, copper, manganese)
- Improved drought tolerance through extended hyphal networks
- Protection against soil-borne pathogens
- Improved soil structure through glomalin production
- Enhanced nitrogen fixation when paired with rhizobia

## Types of Inoculant

### Arbuscular Mycorrhizal Fungi (AMF)

- Used for most crops, vegetables, grasses, and many ornamentals
- Genera: Glomus (now Rhizoglomus), Rhizophagus, Funneliformis, Gigaspora, Scutellospora
- Most commercially available products are AMF-based

### Ectomycorrhizal Fungi (ECM)

- Used for tree seedlings, especially conifers, oaks, and other forest trees
- Genera: Pisolithus (common in commercial products), Laccaria, Suillus, Rhizopogon
- Applied primarily in forestry, reforestation, and orchard establishment

### Endomycorrhizal Products

Mixed products containing multiple AMF species are most common. Quality varies significantly between manufacturers. Key factors:
- Propagule count (CFU/g)
- Species diversity (more species = broader compatibility)
- Viability (proper storage is critical)
- Carrier material (peat, vermiculite, clay-based)

## Commercial Products

### Common Brands

- **Mycorrhizal Applications (MA)**: MycoApply line — granular, liquid, and seed-coat formulations
- **Premier Tech**: Myke products for gardens, trees, and agriculture
- **Plant Success**: Granular and soluble formulations for home gardeners
- **Root Naturally**: Endo/Ecto mix for diverse plantings
- **Xtreme Gardening**: Mykos WP (water-soluble) and granular

### Evaluating Product Quality

1. Check propagule count (minimum 100-300 propagules/g for granular products)
2. Verify species included and their relevance to your crop
3. Check expiration date — AMF viability declines over time
4. Look for independent testing data
5. Store cool and dry; never expose to extreme heat or direct sunlight

## Application Methods

### Seed Coating

Best for: row crops, grains, large-scale agriculture
- Inoculant is mixed with a sticking agent and applied to seeds before planting
- Ensures fungi are in immediate contact with emerging roots
- Rate: typically 5-10g product per kg of seed
- Apply just before planting — coated seeds should be planted within hours
- Avoid fungicide-treated seeds unless compatible with AMF

### Soil Drench

Best for: transplants, established plants, container-grown plants
- Mix soluble or suspendable inoculant in water
- Apply to root zone at transplanting or as a top-dressing
- Rate: follow product label (typically 1-5g per liter of water)
- Apply to moist soil for best colonization
- Can be repeated at 2-4 week intervals for stressed plants

### Granular Application

Best for: field planting, landscape installation, orchard establishment
- Mix granular inoculant into backfill soil or planting holes
- Rate: typically 1-2 teaspoons per planting hole, or 5-10kg/ha for field application
- Place inoculant in direct contact with roots
- Avoid applying too deep (within top 15cm of soil surface)

### Root Dip

Best for: bareroot transplants, tree seedlings
- Mix inoculant into a slurry with water and a clay/potting soil base
- Dip roots into slurry before planting
- Ensures thorough root coverage

## Effectiveness Data

### General Crop Response

- Row crops (corn, wheat, soy): 5-20% yield increase on average, higher on P-deficient soils
- Vegetables (tomato, pepper, lettuce): 10-30% yield increase in controlled conditions
- Tree seedlings: 20-100% biomass increase, especially on degraded sites
- Container-grown plants: variable — often less effective due to high fertility and limited soil volume

### When Inoculation Works Best

1. Low-phosphorus soils (AMF most beneficial when P is limiting)
2. Degraded or disturbed soils (restoration, mine reclamation)
3. Nursery production (especially for outplanting)
4. Organic systems (no soluble P fertilizers that suppress AMF)
5. Drought-prone conditions (hyphal water transport)
6. New construction sites with disturbed topsoil

### When Inoculation Is Less Effective

1. High-phosphorus soils (plants regulate AMF colonization based on P availability)
2. Recently fertilized with high-rate soluble phosphorus
3. Very sandy or waterlogged soils
4. Crops that are non-mycorrhizal (Brassicaceae: cabbage, broccoli, mustard; Amaranthaceae: spinach, beets; Chenopodiaceae: quinoa)
5. Soil that already has a rich, diverse AMF community

## Crop Specificity

### Highly Responsive Crops

- Allium (onion, garlic, leek)
- Legumes (beans, peas, clover, lentil)
- Grasses (corn, wheat, rye, most turf grasses)
- Solanaceae (tomato, pepper, potato, eggplant)
- Cucurbits (squash, cucumber, melon)
- Many fruit trees (apple, citrus, grape)

### Non-Host Crops (Do Not Form AMF)

- Brassica family (cabbage, broccoli, cauliflower, radish, kale)
- Chenopodium family (spinach, beet, quinoa)
- Polygonum (buckwheat)
- Some sedges (Cyperaceae)

### ECM-Dependent Crops

- Pines, spruces, firs (conifers)
- Oaks, beech, birch
- Eucalyptus
- Pecan, hazelnut
