## Using Spore Syringes

### Inoculating Agar

Flame-sterilize the needle. Open the Petri dish just enough to inject 1-2 drops of spore solution onto the agar surface. Close and gently tilt the plate to spread the suspension. When starting a new culture from spores, it is best to inoculate at least three media dishes to improve chances of successful germination. Alternatively, use a sterile loop to streak the spore solution across the surface for isolated colony formation.

### Inoculating Grain

Inject 1-2 cc of spore solution through the injection port of a grain spawn jar. The solution runs down the inside of the jar, distributing spores across multiple grain kernels. This method is less reliable than agar-to-grain transfer because the cultivator cannot verify mycelial purity before grain colonization.

## Multispore vs. Single Spore

Spore syringes produce multispore cultures, meaning thousands of spores germinate simultaneously, creating a genetically diverse population. The resulting mycelium is a mosaic of many different strains, some incompatible with others. This mixture can have a limiting effect on total yields, with less productive strains inhibiting more productive ones. In general, strains from spores have a high probability of resembling their parents, but cultures from wild specimens may fruit very poorly and must be selectively developed.
