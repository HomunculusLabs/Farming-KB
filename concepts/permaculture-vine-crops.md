# Permaculture Vine Crops

Vines occupy a unique vertical niche in [[permaculture-energy-systems]], climbing on trellises, fences, and trees to produce food without consuming ground-level growing space. In [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-forest-gardens-and-food-forests]], vines form the vertical layer between shrubs and canopy.

## Passionfruit (Passiflora spp)
Family: PASSIFLORACEAE. More than 40 edible species, mostly tropical to subtropical, woody, fruiting vines that climb by tendrils. Fruit falls when ripe. Named by the Spanish who saw the flowers as reminders of Christ's passion.

### Key Species

**Passiflora edulis** (purple passionfruit) -- Best known species, native to Brazil. Subtropical, fairly dense, evergreen. Prefers moist, well-drained, fertile, neutral to slightly alkaline soil. Withstands occasional light frosts. Two crops per year in warm conditions; sometimes year-round. Eaten raw, juiced, bottled, sauces, cakes, jellies. Seeds yield edible oil comparable to sunflower/soybean (12% protein, 50% fibre). Juice used medicinally as digestive stimulant and gastric cancer treatment.

**P. edulis var. flavicarpa** (yellow passionfruit) -- Less cold-tolerant but resistant to Fusarium wilt and nematodes. Does better climbing a tall tree rather than a trellis. Tall [[nitrogen-fixing-trees-and-shrubs]] are ideal, adding nitrogen fixation and long-term timber.

**P. mollissima** (banana passionfruit) -- Native to Andes (1800-3200 m). Cold tolerant to -2C. Aromatic, long, elliptical fruit with thicker, stronger juice than purple. Considered the best-tasting juice of all passionfruits in the Andes. Made into prized wine and liqueur (crema de curuba). Fruit eaten raw, in fruit salads, ice cream, jellies, sauces. Lime juice often added to enhance flavour.

**P. incarnata** (maypop) -- Most cold-tolerant species. Dies back in winter, reshoots in spring. Disturbed roots also send out new shoots. Native to southeastern USA. Leaves eaten raw in salads and cooked as greens. Flowers made into syrup and eaten as vegetable. Contains passiflorine -- pharmaceutical use as sedative and tranquilliser.

**P. quadrangularis** (giant granadilla) -- Fruit up to 15 cm wide, 30 cm long. Melon-like, pleasantly aromatic. Roots of old vines eaten roasted or baked like yams (see [[permaculture-tuber-crops]]). Requires deep, fertile, moist, well-drained soil. Unripe fruits used in soups, steamed and boiled with butter, nutmeg and pepper. Pulp contains passiflorine (sedative in large doses).

**P. ligularis** (sweet granadilla) -- Native to central Mexico, Central America and western South America. Can shade and kill all understorey. Orange-yellow fruit with soft, translucent, perfumed pulp comparable to purple passionfruit. Cold tolerant to -1C. Eaten fresh or in drinks and desserts.

**P. caerulea** (blue crown passionflower) -- Vigorous and hardy, native to south Brazil and Argentina. Small fruits edible and flavoursome but little juice. Used as rootstock for grafted purple passionfruit but root suckers often escape and become a nuisance.

### Propagation
Most can be grown from seed (fresh seed best). Passing seed through the digestive tract of a duck or human gives good germination results. Hydrochloric acid treatment also works. Many can be propagated from cuttings. Grafted cultivars available but rootstock can become a nuisance. See [[permaculture-plant-propagation-guide]] for techniques.

### Design Notes
- Provide a clump of trees so vines can spread without inhibiting any single support tree
- Use nitrogen-fixing trees as support -- adds timber yield to the system
- Mulberries are considered a good companion to grapes and can serve as living trellis
- See [[permaculture-companion-planting]] for grape companions: geraniums, mulberry, hyssop, basil, peas, beans, garlic

## Grape Vines (Vitis spp)
Grapes are classic permaculture vines suited to temperate and Mediterranean climates. In permaculture design, train grapes on:
- Trellis along the [[permaculture-climatic-zones-design]] for daily harvest access
- Mulberry trees as living support ([[permaculture-companion-planting]])
- Pergolas providing summer shade and winter sun access
- Fence lines as productive boundaries

Companion plants include geraniums, mulberry, hyssop, basil, peas, beans, and garlic. Avoid planting near radishes or hyssop. See [[permaculture-fruit-tree-guide]] for integrating grapes with tree crops.

## Kiwi (Actinidia spp)
Vigorous deciduous vines producing nutrient-dense fruit. Most species require male and female plants. Hardy kiwi (A. arguta) tolerates -25C. Excellent for covering arbors and producing high yields in vertical space. Fruit high in vitamin C and potassium. Position in [[zone-sector-analysis-guide]] for regular harvest access.

## Hops (Humulus lupulus)
Perennial vine in the Cannabaceae family. Grown commercially for beer brewing but also has medicinal properties (sedative, sleep aid). Rapidly climbing, dies back to ground in winter. Useful as a productive shade vine for pergolas and walkways. Dried female flowers (cones) are the brewing ingredient. Provides [[bee-forage-plant-system]] in summer.

## Choko / Chayote (Sechium edule)
Family: CUCURBITACEAE. Vigorous perennial vine producing pear-shaped fruits, young shoots, and edible tuberous roots. The entire plant is edible: fruit cooked like squash, young tendrils and shoots as vegetables, and the root like yam (see [[permaculture-tuber-crops]]). Extremely productive and easy to grow -- a single plant can cover a large trellis. Frost tender but will reshoot from roots. Excellent for covering fences, sheds, and pergolas with productive growth.

## Vanilla (Vanilla planifolia)
Family: ORCHIDACEAE. The only orchid grown commercially for food production. Climbing vine producing the world's most valuable spice after saffron. Requires a support tree (traditionally [[nitrogen-fixing-trees-and-shrubs]] or shade trees) and humid tropical conditions. Flowers must be hand-pollinated outside its native Mexico (where native melipona bees pollinate). The curing process takes months, developing the complex flavour profile. High-value crop for tropical permaculture enterprises.

## Akebia (Akebia quinata)
Family: LARDIZABALACEAE. Hardy deciduous vine from East Asia, tolerating -20C. Produces unusual purple sausage-shaped pods containing sweet pulp. Leaves used for tea. Vigorous climber for shade structures and fences. One of the few vine fruits that produces well in cold climates alongside hardy kiwi and maypop.

## Vanilla Growers' Companion Tree Practice

Vanilla growers in Brazil use Casuarina equisetifolia as a companion tree because the soil below it stays moist -- the dense root system creates a humid microclimate ideal for vanilla orchid growth. This illustrates the permaculture principle of [[multi-purpose-trees]] providing microclimate modification alongside other yields. See [[permaculture-companion-planting]] for companion tree selection.

## Vine Crop Yield Maximization

- Train vines on multiple support structures to maximize fruiting surface area
- Use deciduous support trees for summer shade and winter sun access
- Interplant [[nitrogen-fixing-trees-and-shrubs]] for dual timber + vine yields
- Maintain vine health through regular pruning and mulching-guide|mulching
- Combine early, mid, and late-season fruiting species for extended harvest
- Add [[insectary-plants-for-pest-control]] at vine bases for pest management
- Use [[permaculture-ground-cover-plants]] beneath vines to suppress weeds and retain moisture

## Design Integration

- Vines produce maximum food per square meter by utilizing vertical space
- Combine fruiting vines with [[permaculture-multipurpose-trees]] for dual yields
- Use deciduous vines on pergolas for summer shade, winter sun penetration
- Place vine crops along the multi-purpose walk for daily harvest access
- Avoid letting vigorous vines (especially P. mollissima) strangle support trees -- provide multiple climbing options
- Select cold-hardy species (maypop, hardy kiwi, hops, akebia) for cooler climates
- Integrate vines with [[insectary-plants-for-pest-control]] at the base for pest management
- Plan vine placement using [[zone-and-sector-analysis]]
- See [[permaculture-forest-garden-establishment]] for vine integration during food forest establishment
