# Tryptamine Hallucinogens — DMT, Psilocybin, and Molecular Pharmacology

## Overview

The tryptamine hallucinogens are a family of psychoactive compounds derived from the amino acid tryptophan, sharing a core indole ring structure. The principal members discussed in McKenna's 1983 Esalen lecture are dimethyltryptamine (DMT), psilocybin, and ayahuasca (an oral DMT preparation). These compounds are distinguished from other hallucinogens like LSD and mescaline by their unique phenomenological profile, their relationship to endogenous neurotransmitters, and their concentration of activity in the visual cortex and language centers of the brain.

## Structural Chemistry

### The Tryptamine Core

All tryptamine hallucinogens share a common structural backbone:

- **Indole ring**: A bicyclic structure consisting of a benzene ring fused to a pyrrole ring, providing the basic molecular scaffold
- **Ethylamine side chain**: A two-carbon chain with an amine group at the 3-position of the indole ring
- This core structure is identical to that of **serotonin** (5-hydroxytryptamine, 5-HT), the major neurotransmitter found in all animal life and most concentrated in the human brain

The structural similarity to serotonin is not coincidental — it is the basis of the compounds' pharmacological activity, as they act primarily as agonists at serotonin 5-HT2A receptors.

### DMT (N,N-Dimethyltryptamine)

- **Chemical formula**: C₁₂H₁₆N₂
- **IUPAC name**: N,N-Dimethyl-1H-indole-3-ethanamine
- **Molecular weight**: 188.27 g/mol
- **Structure**: Tryptamine with two methyl groups on the terminal amine nitrogen
- **Source**: Found endogenously in the human brain (in small amounts), in many plant species ([[ayahuasca-pharmacology-and-cultural-context]]**: Used for millennia by indigenous peoples of the Amazon basin in shamanic ceremonies
