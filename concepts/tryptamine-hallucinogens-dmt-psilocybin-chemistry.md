# Tryptamine Hallucinogens — DMT, Psilocybin, and Molecular Pharmacology

## Overview

The tryptamine hallucinogens are a family of psychoactive compounds derived from the amino acid tryptophan, sharing a core indole ring structure. The principal members discussed in McKenna's 1983 Esalen lecture are dimethyltryptamine (DMT), psilocybin, and ayahuasca (an oral DMT preparation). These compounds are distinguished from other hallucinogens like LSD and mescaline by their unique phenomenological profile, their relationship to endogenous neurotransmitters, and their concentration of activity in the visual cortex and language centers of the brain.

## Structural Chemistry

### The Tryptamine Core

All tryptamine hallucinogens share a common structural backbone:

- **Indole ring**: A bicyclic structure consisting of a benzene ring fused to a pyrrole ring, providing the basic molecular scaffold
- **Ethylamine side chain**: A two-carbon chain with an amine group at the 3-position of the indole ring
- This core structure is identical to that of **serotonin** (5-hydroxytryptamine, 5-HT), the major neurotransmitter found in all animal life and most concentrated in the human brain

The structural similarity to serotonin is not coincidental — it is the basis of the compounds' pharmacological activity, as they act primarily as agonists at serotonin 5-HT2A receptors.

### DMT (N,N-Dimethyltryptamine)

- **Chemical formula**: C₁₂H₁₆N₂
- **IUPAC name**: N,N-Dimethyl-1H-indole-3-ethanamine
- **Molecular weight**: 188.27 g/mol
- **Structure**: Tryptamine with two methyl groups on the terminal amine nitrogen
- **Source**: Found endogenously in the human brain (in small amounts), in many plant species ([[chacruna-psychotria-viridis]], Diplopterys cabrerana), and in certain animal tissues
- **Route**: Typically smoked or vaporized; active orally only with a monoamine oxidase inhibitor (MAOI)

### Psilocybin

- **Chemical formula**: C₁₂H₁₇N₂O₄P
- **IUPAC name**: 4-Phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine
- **Molecular weight**: 284.25 g/mol
- **Structure**: DMT with a phosphoryl group at the 4-position of the indole ring
- **Source**: Produced by over 200 species of mushrooms in the genus Psilocybe, Panaeolus, Gymnopilus, and others
- **Route**: Active orally; psilocybin is dephosphorylated in the body to **psilocin** (4-hydroxy-DMT), the active metabolite
- **Duration**: Onset in 20-60 minutes, peak at 60-90 minutes, total experience 4-6 hours

### Ayahuasca

- **Composition**: Oral combination of DMT-containing plants (usually *Psychotria viridis* or *Diplopterys cabrerana*) and MAOI-containing plants (usually *Banisteriopsis caapi*, which contains harmine, harmaline, and tetrahydroharmine — beta-carboline compounds)
- **Mechanism**: The MAOI prevents breakdown of DMT by monoamine oxidase enzymes in the gut and liver, allowing oral DMT to reach the brain
- **Duration**: Onset in 20-60 minutes, peak at 1-2 hours, total experience 3-6 hours
- **[[ayahuasca-pharmacology-and-cultural-context]]**: Used for millennia by indigenous peoples of the Amazon basin in shamanic ceremonies

## Pharmacology

### Receptor Activity

Tryptamine hallucinogens act primarily as agonists (activators) at the **5-HT2A [[serotonin-receptor-interference-psychedelic-mechanism]]**, though they have affinity for other receptor subtypes as well:

- **5-HT2A**: Primary site of hallucinogenic action; activation produces the characteristic visual and cognitive effects
- **5-HT1A**: May contribute to mood-modulating and anxiolytic effects
- **5-HT2C**: Involved in [[blesching-cannabis-appetite-regulation-ghrelin-leptin]] and may contribute to nausea
- **Sigma-1 receptor**: May modulate neuroprotective and neuroplastic effects
- **TAAR1 (Trace Amine-Associated Receptor)**: DMT is an endogenous ligand; this receptor system is still being characterized

### Quantum Mechanical Correlates

McKenna highlights a remarkable pharmacological observation: the difference between an inactive compound and a highly potent hallucinogen can be as small as moving a single atom on the molecular ring. John Smythies, [[alexander-shulgin]], and others have documented this sensitivity. McKenna interprets this as evidence of "dynamic linkage at the formative level between quantum mechanically described matter and mind" — the idea that consciousness-sensitive properties of these molecules operate at the quantum level of chemical bonding.

### Endogenous DMT

DMT is present naturally in the human brain, though its function remains debated:

- It is synthesized from tryptamine by the enzyme indolethylamine N-methyltransferase (INMT)
- Concentrations are low but detectable in cerebrospinal fluid and brain tissue
- Proposed roles include acting as a neurotransmitter/neuromodulator, a regulator of sigma-1 receptor activity, or a component of near-death experiences and dreaming
- The rapid onset (45 seconds when smoked) and short duration (5 minutes) suggest the brain is "absolutely at home with this compound" (McKenna), implying a natural neurological role

## Comparison with Other Hallucinogens

McKenna emphasizes that tryptamine hallucinogens are phenomenologically distinct from LSD and mescaline, despite being commonly grouped together:

| Property | Tryptamines (DMT/Psilocybin) | LSD | Mescaline |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core structure | Indole (tryptamine) | Ergoline (lysergamide) | Phenethylamine |
| Onset | 15-60 seconds (smoked DMT) / 20-60 min (oral) | 30-90 minutes | 45-120 minutes |
| Duration | 5 min (DMT) / 4-6 hr (psilocybin) | 8-12 hours | 8-14 hours |
| Visual intensity | Extremely high | High | Moderate |
| Entity encounters | Common (DMT) | Less common | Rare |
| Language center activation | Direct ("the Logos") | Indirect | Indirect |
| Endogenous | Yes (DMT) | No | No |

The most significant distinction McKenna draws is that tryptamines "invoke the Logos" — they work directly on the language centers of the brain, producing an interior dialogue that is a central feature of the experience. This linguistic dimension is less prominent with LSD and mescaline.

## Dosage and Safety

### DMT

- **Smoked/Vaporized**: 20-60 mg (threshold to strong); onset 15-45 seconds; duration 5-15 minutes
- **Oral (with MAOI)**: 30-100 mg DMT component; onset 20-60 minutes; duration 2-4 hours
- **LD-50**: Very high relative to active dose; the therapeutic index is wide

### Psilocybin

- **Oral**: 5-15 mg (moderate); 15-30 mg (strong); 30+ mg (very strong)
- **LD-50**: Estimated at 280 mg/kg in mice; no human fatalities have been documented from psilocybin alone

McKenna notes: "All the compounds are potentially dangerous, and all compounds, at sufficient doses or repeated over time, involve risks. The library is the first place to go when looking into taking a new compound."

## Legal Status

- DMT is Schedule I in the United States and internationally controlled under the UN Convention on Psychotropic Substances
- Psilocybin is Schedule I in the United States; several jurisdictions have decriminalized or are exploring regulated access
- Ayahuasca has complex legal status — the DMT component is controlled, but religious use of ayahuasca has been protected in some legal contexts (e.g., the UDV and Santo Daime churches in the United States and Brazil)
