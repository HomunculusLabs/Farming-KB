# Bulk Substrate Spawning and Spawn Running in The Mushroom Cultivator

Source: The Mushroom Cultivator (Stamets & Chilton, 1983), Chapter VII
Updated: 2026-04-18

## Overview

The inoculation of compost or [[antimicrobial-compounds-mushroom-mycelium]] is known as spawn running. The fundamental principle is that colonization must proceed as rapidly as possible to prevent competing organisms from becoming established. Once the mushroom mycelium becomes dominant, natural antibiotics secreted into the substrate inhibit competitors.

## Critical Factors for Spawning

### 1. Moisture Content

Mushroom mycelium does not grow in substrate that is either too dry or too wet:
- **Dry substrate**: Produces fine wispy [[bloomfield-mushroom-formation-fruiting-body-development-cultivation]] because water essential for [[agaricus-brunnescens]] showed yield depressions when moisture deviated more than 2% from optimum
- Deviations greater than 5% generally prevent fruitbody production entirely
- **Correction**: Dry compost should be lightly watered and mixed well; over-wet compost gets gypsum added to bind loose water

### 2. Substrate Temperature

The substrate temperature must be monitored closely with thermometers placed both in the center (hottest) and in the room atmosphere:
- Agaricus brunnescens grows fastest at 77°F
- [[cannabis-vegetative-growth-stage]]
- If cropping must be delayed, cool the substrate until a better time

## Spawning Methods

### Mixed Spawning

The standard modern technique. The principle is complete and thorough mixing of grain kernels throughout the substrate, ensuring all parts are equally inoculated for the most rapid and complete colonization.

**Standard Spawning Rate:**
- 7 liters per ton of compost (Agaricus standard)
- Or 1 quart per 8 square feet
- Higher spawning rates lead to more rapid colonization when spawn is available

**Procedure:**
1. Break up grain spawn into individual kernels the day before spawning
2. Fresh spawn breaks apart easily; if not used fresh, refrigerate at 38°F
3. Spawn under carefully controlled hygienic conditions -- exclude fungus gnats
4. Disinfect work area and tools one day prior with 10% bleach solution

### Application Methods

- **Shelves**: Broadcast spawn over surface and mix in with pitchfork or by hand
- **Trays**: Dump substrate onto clean surface, mix with spawn, replace in trays
- **Bulk room**: Remove substrate, mix with spawn, place into containers

After spawning, level and compress the substrate to avoid dehydration from excessive air penetration. Commercial growers compress compost into trays with hydraulic presses to create a table-top surface for even casing application.

## Environmental Conditions During Spawn Running

- **Substrate temperature**: Controlled by manipulation of surrounding air temperature
- **Humidity**: Must be held at 90-100% -- below this, water evaporates from substrate surface
- **CO2 levels**: 10,000-15,000 ppm considered beneficial and desirable; mushroom mycelium can fix CO2
- **Fresh air**: Generally not needed; being heavier than air, CO2 settles at the bottom of the room
- **Air circulation**: Steady or periodic recirculation prevents localized over-heating
- **Plastic covering**: Can be used to counteract drying, but remove during peak activity if temperatures rise
