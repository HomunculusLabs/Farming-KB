# Cannabis Lighting Systems — HID, LED, CFL Comparison

## Light Movers

Light movers are mechanical devices that slowly move HID lamps back and forth or in a circular pattern over the garden. They increase coverage area of each lamp by 20-30% and reduce hot spots directly beneath fixtures. A linear light mover on a track is the most common type, allowing a single 1000W HID to effectively cover a 6x6 foot area instead of 4x4.

Light movers eliminate shading problems because plants receive light from varying angles throughout the day. This promotes more even growth and allows lights to be positioned closer to the plant canopy without burning the uppermost foliage.

## Reflective Materials

Reflective walls maximize light utilization by bouncing photons back onto the plant canopy:

- **Mylar (90-95% reflectivity)**: Most effective reflective material. Available in various thicknesses (1-2 mil recommended). Must be kept clean and flat for maximum effectiveness.
- **Flat white paint (75-80% reflectivity)**: Cheapest and easiest option. Apply two coats for best results. Flat (not gloss) paint diffuses light more evenly.
- **Emergency blankets (50-70% reflectivity)**: Inexpensive but less effective than Mylar. Can create hot spots due to uneven surfaces.
- **White poly plastic (70-75% reflectivity)**: Durable and waterproof. Good option for lining walls.

## Light Output Degradation

HID lamp output decreases over time. HPS lamps typically lose 10-15% of their lumen output after 6 months of regular use, and 25-30% after one year. MH lamps degrade somewhat faster. Replacing lamps on a regular schedule (every 6-9 months for serious growers) ensures consistent light intensity. Running lamps on a proper ballast and avoiding frequent on/off cycling extends lamp life.

## Electricity Costs and Efficiency

Lighting accounts for the largest portion of [[cervantes-electricity-consumption-cost-management]] in indoor grows. A single 1000W HID lamp running 12 hours per day consumes approximately 365 kWh per month. Growers should factor electricity costs into their overall budget:

- **LED efficiency**: LEDs convert more electricity to photosynthetically active radiation (PAR) per watt than HID lamps. A 600W LED can produce comparable growth to a 1000W HPS while using 40% less electricity.
- **Heat management**: HID lamps produce significant heat that must be removed by ventilation, adding to electricity costs. LEDs produce much less heat, reducing cooling requirements.
- **Lamp disposal**: HID lamps contain small amounts of mercury and other metals and should be disposed of at hazardous waste facilities, not in regular trash.

## PAR (Photosynthetically Active Radiation)

PAR measures the [[cannabis-light-spectrum-and-color-bands]] that plants actually use for photosynthesis (400-700nm wavelength range). Unlike lumens, which measure light brightness as perceived by the human eye, PAR specifically quantifies the photons available for plant growth. PAR is measured in micromoles per square meter per second (μmol/m²/s).

Key PAR considerations:
- **[[comparison-cannabis-vegetative-stage-vs-flowering-stage]]**: Target 300-500 μmol/m²/s for healthy vegetative growth
- **[[cannabis-flowering-stage-management]]**: Target 600-1000 μmol/m²/s for optimal bud production
- **Light saturation point**: Cannabis light saturation occurs around 1500 μmol/m²/s; beyond this, additional light provides diminishing returns
- **Daily light integral (DLI)**: The total amount of PAR received in a 24-hour period. For cannabis, target 30-40 mol/m²/d during flowering

A quantum PAR meter is more useful for indoor growers than a lux or lumen meter, as it directly measures the light wavelengths that matter for plant growth. LED manufacturers typically report PAR values (PPF — photosynthetic photon flux) rather than lumens.
