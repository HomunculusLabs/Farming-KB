# Permaculture Designers Manual - Small Animals

updated: 2026-04-18

Small animals -- bees, chickens, ducks, geese, pigs, rabbits, and guineafowl -- are treated in Section 12.11 "Free-Range Forage Systems" as critical to domestic self-reliance. Mollison presents these species as integral components of integrated farm systems, not isolated production units. The emphasis is on free-range management, species-appropriate behaviour, and beneficial integration with crops, forests, and water systems.

## Principles of Range Management

To rear animals on range, three factors must be studied:
- The social behaviour of the animals: preferred sex ratios, ranging behaviour, preferred flock/herd size, and breed adaptations to soils, pH, site factors, and foraging
- Nutrition over a full year: adequate provision of energy foods, vitamins, and minerals essential to animal health
- Interactions of ranging animals with crops, forests, other animals, and people

Knowing the behaviour of the animal is intrinsic to good range management (lessening social stress), and knowing the characteristics of good range is critical for environmental factors including shelter, nutrition, and climate.

## Bee Range

Bees produce honey, wax, pollen, propolis, and royal jelly, and carry out essential pollination for food, oil, fruit, and seed crops (mustard, clovers, buckwheat, smallfruit, apples, grain legumes). Key design considerations:

- Range area: about 2.5 km radius per hive site; commercial sites placed 5 km apart to restrict disease transmission
- Site shelter: protected from cold, wet, wind, and sun, but not atypical of the climate (or bees are tempted out in adverse weather)
- Hive density: 100-150 hives commonly grouped at one site depending on range richness
- Forage distance: bees prefer to fly 100 m or more to forage; flight assists nectar evaporation
- Clumped plantings: bees more efficiently harvest clumped rather than scattered nectar sources (hedgerows, fields preferred to scattered individuals)
- Cold winds most restrict foraging, making hedgerow cover (even low 0.5-1 m) essential
- Water: clustered hives need very large quantities for cooling; provide soaked mats at pond edges to prevent drowning

### Bee Forage Design

Forage species are divided into early, mid, and late season, and into classical, bulk, and special honeys:
- Classical honeys: heather honeys (sage, thyme, catmint, rosemary); pine/basswood; marsh tupelo; leatherwood (Eucryphia); citrus
- Bulk honeys: clovers, eucalypts, Acacias, mustard, buckwheat, Rosaceae fruits, mixed garden honey
- Aiming for 35 main forage species over the whole season to provide insurance against annual variation

Crops within a mile of hives outyield bee-deficient areas by 3-10 times. The ideal bee farm is also a production farm for fruits and seeds.

## Poultry Range

### Breed Selection

Light breeds (Spanish ancestry): Anconas, Andalusians, Minorcas, Leghorns, Hamburghs. Flighty, poor mothers, white eggs in spring/summer, cold and wind sensitive, prefer sandy/light high-pH soils. Excellent foragers.

Heavy breeds (Chinese ancestry): Scots Dumpy, Orpington, Dorking, Wyandotte, Rhode Island Red, Plymouth Rock, Australorp. Non-fliers, excellent mothers, brown eggs in summer/autumn, hardy to cold and wind, tolerate clay and acid soils.

### Flock Behaviour

Natural flocks seldom exceed 20-30 individuals with 2-3 cockerels. Larger flocks of 40-60 break up of their own accord. Free-range flock averages: 130-150 eggs per hen/year for heavy breeds (rarely 180-200); 160-180 for light breeds. Unstressed flocks on range continue to lay 4-6 years versus 18-20 months for caged birds.

### Nutrition on Range

On correctly stocked range, poultry obtain 65% of food as insects, invertebrates, greens, and grains; 30-35% supplied as concentrated grain products. Key forage species include: Coprosma repens, Caragana, tagasaste, boxthorn, pigeon pea, Solanum berries, mulberry (5-6 varieties), Acacia seed, Leucaena, clovers, chicory, comfrey, dandelion.

Supplementary feeding: large acorns, chestnuts, and starchy seeds can be sprouted or rolled to crack. Table scraps supplement diets. Insect food is deliberately cultivated: manure piles for maggots, mulch for cockroaches, termites in sunken pits, mealworms in bran/root mixtures.

### Population Density

- Unstressed at 800-900/ha (housed as 20 small flocks in sheds about 2 m per side)
- At 800/ha, chickens forage so well that other livestock cannot find sufficient browse
- At 350-400/ha, sheep and cattle can also use the range
- 120-180/ha needed to clean up windfalls and supply fertiliser to orchards
- One flock of 20-30 hens is the typical household food supplement

### Chicken Tractor System

Flocks rotated around 12-16 pens on one hectare every 6-18 months. Chickens remove all weeds, surface bulbs, weed seeds, and grasses, functioning as a "chicken tractor" to clean ground of nutgrass, Kikuyu, onion weed, Oxalis, and almost every persistent weed. As pens are cleared, the ground is limed and vegetable or orchard crops placed. Forage crops of buckwheat, wheat, sunflower, millet, black nightshade, cleavers, chard, and mustard are sown before the chickens return.

### Health

Disease is rare on free range given: dry dust baths with diatomaceous earth or dried bracken and neem leaves for ectoparasite control; wormwoods for intestinal worms; shell grit or limestone gravel for eggshells; hard silica grit for food grinding; predator protection; clean water; good shade and night shelter.

## Duck and Geese

Ducks need marsh plants, snails, and shallow water for dabbling; up to 200 eggs each/year, relatively disease-free, good foragers. Geese are traditional small grazers of mixed orchards, replacing lawn-mowers to control grasses and groundcovers. The same principles for chickens apply to ducks, guineafowl, turkey, and wildlife such as pheasant and quail, differing only in food preferences.

## Pig Range

Pigs on range are healthier, cheaper to feed, and have less saturated fats than shed-kept pigs. Ideal sites are preferably wooded, well-drained, and dry in wet seasons. Mud and wet soils encourage worms and disease.

- Most economically kept where dairy, orchard, root crop, or meat wastes are available
- Farrowing pen used 3 months per litter, rested 6 months (two sows per pen per year)
- Shelter sheds should be thatched or insulated against cold
- Rubbing posts: strong diagonal posts wrapped in hessian dampened with oil plus neem or pyrethrum for ectoparasite control
- Dust baths with neem leaves or pyrethrum flowers; restricted wallows planted to cattails
- Stocking: 20 pigs per 4,000 sq m will plough by scratching and rooting over 3-4 weeks
- Forage: legumes (clover, lucerne), comfrey, chicory, young grasses plus seed, fruit, kernels
- Green feed halves the necessity for protein meal and grains
