# Oss & Oeric: Rice Cake Tek and Cultivation Methodology

The cultivation methodology described by Oss and Oeric centers on a
four-step process designed for reproducible indoor fruiting of
[[mckenna-stropharia-cubensis-extraterrestrial-hypothesis-spore-dispersal]] (Psilocybe cubensis). The method was adapted from
commercial [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]] techniques developed by San Antonio
(1971) for [[genetic-improvement-agaricus-bisporus-molecular-approaches]], with modifications suitable for home use.
The entire procedure could be completed in roughly sixty days from spore
isolation to first harvest.

## Step 1: Spore Collection and Germination

The process begins with locating wild specimens in cow pastures during
warm, rainy seasons. Fresh caps are placed gill-side down on clean white
paper or sterilized microscope slides for 24 hours to produce a dark
purplish spore print containing tens of millions of spores.

Spores are germinated on solid nutrient media, either Potato Dextrose
Agar (PDA) or Malt Extract Agar (MEA). Spores are scraped from the
print using a flame-sterilized inoculating loop and transferred to the
agar surface. After 3-5 days, monokaryotic mycelium grows radially from
inoculation points. When mycelia from different spores make contact,
somatogamy occurs, establishing dikaryotic mycelium suitable for
fruiting. See [[oss-oeric-spore-syringe-preparation]] for spore
handling techniques.

## Step 2: Growing Stock Inocula

Building a stock of agar-grown mycelial cultures provides insurance
against contamination losses. PDA is prepared from 250g shredded
potatoes boiled in 1L water, filtered, with 15g agar, 10g dextrose,
and 1.5g nutritional yeast added. MEA uses 20g malt extract and 20g
agar with optional cornsteep liquor and mineral supplements.

Media is poured into petri plates, baby food jars, or slant tubes and
sterilized at 15-20 lbs pressure for 45-60 minutes. The extended
sterilization time was found necessary to ensure complete sterilization
under non-laboratory conditions. The authors recommend alternating
between PDA and MEA to prevent mycelium from becoming accustomed to a
single medium, which could lead to senescence and vigor loss.

Inoculation is performed inside a homemade or commercial inoculation
hood pre-sprayed with Lysol or 25% Clorox solution. Cultures should
cover the plate surface within 7-20 days. See [[agar-culture-technique]]
for detailed agar work procedures.

## Step 3: Growing on Sterilized Rye

The core innovation involves expanding mycelium onto sterilized rye
grain. Each wide-mouth quart mason jar receives 112g whole rye grains,
2.0g calcium carbonate, optionally 0.2g potassium phosphate, and 180ml
water. Jars are sterilized at 15-20 lbs for one hour.

Inoculation uses agar squares (approximately 1-1.5 sq cm) cut from
stock plates with a flame-sterilized scalpel. An alternative syringe
method involves suspending mycelial fragments in sterile water and
injecting into jars. After inoculation, jars are maintained at 70-80
degrees F with 95% relative humidity, with periodic vigorous shaking
to redistribute the mycelium. Complete permeation occurs in 8-14 days,
evidenced by snow-white mycelium throughout the rye.

## Step 4: Casing and Fruiting

Casing involves covering the colonized rye with 0.5 to 0.75 inches of
sterilized soil mixture. The recommended casing recipe combines 2.5
liters peat moss, 3.5 liters fine vermiculite, 4 liters washed fine
sand, and 2 liters calcium carbonate (crushed oyster shell).

Cased jars are maintained in high-humidity styrofoam coolers with clear
plastic lids. Daily fine-mist spraying maintains moisture without
saturation. Mushroom primordia (pins) appear 14-20 days after casing,
with mature mushrooms ready for harvest 5-10 days later. A daily
10-hour Grow-lux cycle produces larger caps and shorter stems.

## Adaptability and Scale

The authors emphasize that the method can be adapted to any scale from
a few jars to thousands. No special training in mycology or microbiology
is necessary, only careful attention to sterile procedure. The method
is described as only slightly more complicated than canning or making
jelly.

## Relationship to Modern Cultivation Techniques

The four-step process described by Oss and Oeric became the conceptual
foundation for virtually all subsequent indoor [[synthetic-log-cultivation-methods-guide]]. The
PF Tek ([[pf-tek-psilocybe-fanaticus-cultivation-method]] Technique), developed in the 1990s,
simplified the process by eliminating the agar stage and using brown
rice flour and vermiculite as a substrate. However, the basic
principles of [[cotter-compost-substrate-preparation-manure-mushroom-cultivation]], inoculation, colonization, casing,
and controlled fruiting environment remain unchanged. See
[[pf-tek-cultivation-method]] for the modern simplified approach.
