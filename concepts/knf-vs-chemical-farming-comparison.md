## Pest and Disease Management
### Chemical Farming
- Synthetic pesticides kill target organisms
- Broad-spectrum chemicals also kill beneficial organisms
- Pest resistance develops, requiring stronger chemicals
- Natural predators eliminated, creating pest resurgence cycles
- Chemical residues remain on food and in soil
- Polluting pesticides accumulate in waterways and ecosystems
- No truly non-polluting pesticide exists
### Korean Natural Farming
- Healthy plants have natural resistance to [[knf-soap-water-and-hot-pepper]] provide physical pest control
- [[water-soluble-calcium-wca]] provide minerals in bioavailable forms
- [[coleman-winter-greens-nutritional-quality-cold-sweetening]] and better taste

## Cost Comparison
### Chemical Farming
- Continuous purchase of fertilizers, pesticides, herbicides
- Costs increase over time as soil degrades
- Machinery costs for tillage, spraying, harvesting
- Energy costs for fertilizer production and transport
- Fertilizers account for 30-50% of orchard operating costs
- Farmer becomes dependent on input suppliers
- Profit margins squeezed by rising input costs
### Korean Natural Farming
- Inputs made on-farm from locally available materials
- Primary cost is labor for input preparation
- No machinery needed for tillage or spraying (or minimal equipment)
- Costs decrease over time as soil improves
- Farmer becomes self-sufficient in inputs
- [[fukuoka-textdoc-fertilizer-acidification-trace-element-deficiency]] deficiencies multiply
- Water retention capacity declines
- Erosion increases
- Soil becomes "yellowish-white mineral matter" -- dead and inanimate
### Korean Natural Farming
- Soil organic matter increases
- Soil structure improves (more aggregated, porous)
- Microbial diversity and abundance increase
- Trace element availability improves
- Water retention capacity increases
- Erosion decreases
- Soil becomes "rich black" -- living and fertile

## Yield Comparison
### Chemical Farming
- Initial yield increases from fertilization
- Yields plateau or decline over time as soil degrades
- High yields require increasing inputs
- Yield stability depends on chemical applications
- Quality may decline even as quantity is maintained
### Korean Natural Farming
- Yields may be lower initially during transition
- Yields equal or exceed chemical farming within 2-3 years
- Yields are stable because they depend on soil health, not inputs
- Quality is consistently high
- Both quantity and quality improve over time
