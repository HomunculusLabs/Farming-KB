# Shikimate Pathway in Fungi

The Shikimate pathway is a seven-step metabolic route essential for the biosynthesis of aromatic amino acids (phenylalanine, tyrosine, and tryptophan) in bacteria, fungi, and plants. It is absent in animals, making it a key target for antibiotics and herbicides. In mycology, the pathway is of particular interest because tryptophan -- the product of one of its branches -- is the direct biochemical precursor to all [[tryptamine-family-overview]] compounds, including [[psilocybin-biosynthesis-pathway]], [[5-meo-dmt-compound-profile]], and the ergot alkaloids found in [[ergot-and-lysergic-acid-history]].

## Overview

The Shikimate pathway operates in the cytosol of fungal cells and represents the bridge between primary carbohydrate metabolism and the vast world of aromatic secondary metabolites. The pathway's products include:

- **Folate** (vitamin B9) -- essential for DNA synthesis
- **Ubiquinone** (coenzyme Q) -- central to the electron transport chain
- **Aromatic amino acids**: phenylalanine, tyrosine, tryptophan
- **Countless secondary metabolites**: antibiotics, pigments, [[fungal-secondary-metabolites]], alkaloids, and psychedelics

The pathway's absence in animals is biologically significant -- it explains why animals must obtain aromatic amino acids from their diet and why compounds targeting the Shikimate pathway (like the herbicide glyphosate, which inhibits EPSP synthase) are toxic to plants and fungi but not animals.

## Primary Metabolic Inputs

Both starting materials are ultimately derived from [[fungal-growth-dynamics]] metabolism:

### Phosphoenolpyruvate (PEP)
- Product of **glycolysis**
- Glucose is phosphorylated by ATP (becoming Glucose-6-P)
- Glucose-6-P enters glycolysis, eventually yielding PEP
- PEP serves as both a carbon source and an energy donor

### Erythrose 4-phosphate (E4P)
- Product of the **Pentose Phosphate Pathway** (PPP)
- E4P is produced via a transketolase enzyme acting on pentose phosphate intermediates
- The PPP runs parallel to glycolysis and serves as a source of NADPH and ribose sugars
- E4P acts as a phosphate donor in the initial Shikimate condensation

## The Seven-Step Enzyme Sequence

| Step | Product | Enzyme | Notes |
|------|---------|--------|-------|
| 1 | 7P-2-dehydro-3-deoxy-D-arabino-heptonate | DAHP Synthase | Condensation of PEP + E4P; rate-limiting step; feedback-regulated by aromatic amino acids |
| 2 | 3-dehydroquinate | 3-dehydroquinate synthase | Requires NAD+ and Co2+ cofactors |
| 3 | 3-dehydroshikimate | 3-dehydroquinate dehydratase | Removes water |
| 4 | Shikimate | Shikimate 5-dehydrogenase | Requires NADPH; reversible reaction |
| 5 | Shikimate-3-phosphate | Shikimate kinase | ATP-dependent phosphorylation |
| 6 | 5-O-(1-carboxyvinyl)-3-phosphoshikimate | EPSP synthase | Target of glyphosate herbicide; adds second PEP |
| 7 | **Chorismate** | Chorismate synthase | Final product; branch point to 5 pathways |

### Chorismate: The Central Branch Point

Chorismate is the gateway molecule from which five distinct metabolic pathways diverge:

1. **Tryptophan pathway** -- leading to tryptamine, psilocybin, DMT, and serotonin
2. **Phenylalanine pathway** -- leading to phenylpropanoids, flavonoids, and lignin precursors
3. **Tyrosine pathway** -- leading to melanin, catecholamines, and various alkaloids
4. **Folate pathway** -- essential for one-carbon metabolism and DNA synthesis
5. **Ubiquinone pathway** -- electron transport chain component

## The Tryptophan Branch (Tryptamine Precursor Pathway)

The branch from chorismate to tryptophan is the critical prelude to all tryptamine alkaloid biosynthesis:

| Step | Product | Enzyme |
|------|---------|--------|
| 1 | 2-amino-2-deoxychorismate | Anthranilate synthase (via 2-deoxyisochorismate intermediate) |
| 2 | Anthranilate | Anthranilate synthase |
| 3 | N-(5'-phosphoribosyl) anthranilate | Anthranylate-phosphoribosyl transferase |
| 4 | N-(5'-phosphoribosyl) anthranilate (isomerized) | Isomerase |
| 5 | Enol-1-O carboxyphenylamino-1-deoxyribulose phosphate | Indole-3-glycerolphosphate synthase |
| 6 | Indole-3-glycerolphosphate | -- |
| 7 | Indole | Tryptophan synthase (alpha subunit) |
| 8 | **Tryptophan** | Tryptophan synthase (beta subunit) |

The tryptophan synthase reaction is notable: the alpha subunit cleaves indole-3-glycerolphosphate, producing free indole, which is then channeled directly to the beta subunit where it condenses with serine to form tryptophan. This metabolic channeling prevents the loss of volatile indole and increases reaction efficiency.

## From Tryptophan to Tryptamine

The final step bridging the Shikimate pathway to tryptamine alkaloids is catalyzed by [[tryptophan-decarboxylase]]:

| Reaction | Enzyme |
|----------|--------|
| Tryptophan → **Tryptamine** + CO2 | Tryptophan decarboxylase |

This is the last well-characterized enzymatic step before the [[psilocybin-biosynthesis-pathway]] leading to psilocybin, baeocystin, norbaeocystin, and related compounds.

## Regulation and Feedback

The Shikimate pathway is tightly regulated at multiple levels:

- **DAHP Synthase** is feedback-inhibited by phenylalanine, tyrosine, and tryptophan (the three aromatic amino acids)
- Multiple isozymes of DAHP synthase exist, each preferentially regulated by a different aromatic amino acid
- [[tryptophan-decarboxylase]] is subject to product inhibition -- accumulation of tryptamine and related compounds downregulates its activity
- This feedback regulation is one reason why simply adding tryptamine to substrate can bypass the natural regulatory bottleneck and increase alkaloid production

## Significance in Mycology

### Antibiotic and Toxin Production
Many fungal antibiotics are derived from aromatic amino acids produced via the Shikimate pathway. Understanding this pathway has been crucial for [[fungal-antibiotics]] research and the discovery of new antimicrobial compounds.

### Pigment Biosynthesis
Fungal pigments including melanin (derived from tyrosine via DOPA) and various quinone pigments depend on Shikimate pathway intermediates. See [[fungal-chromogens-and-color-change]] for pigment-related topics.

### Psychedelic Alkaloid Evolution
The presence of the Shikimate pathway in fungi, combined with the existence of [[tryptophan-decarboxylase]], provides the biochemical foundation upon which [[psilocybin-biosynthesis-pathway]] evolved. The Gartz tryptamine supplementation experiments demonstrated that the pathway can be pushed to produce higher alkaloid levels when the regulatory bottleneck is bypassed, suggesting that psilocybin production is naturally downregulated and may serve an ecological function at lower concentrations.

### Biotechnological Applications
The Shikimate pathway is a target for industrial biotechnology. Engineering fungal strains for enhanced production of aromatic compounds -- including pharmaceuticals, food additives, and industrial chemicals -- is an active area of [[fungal-genetics]] research. [[mushroom-submerged-fermentation-pharmaceutical]] of fungi for shikimate-derived compounds has commercial applications.
