# Fruiting Initiation Fruiting initiation is the transition from vegetative [[growing-gourmet-global-environmental-shift-loss-fungal-diversity]] — known as
"shocking" or "triggering" — that signals the mycelium to form primordia
(pins) and develop into mature fruiting bodies.

## The Trigger: Why Fruiting Requires a Shift

During colonization, the mycelium grows in a CO₂-rich, dark, warm environment
that promotes vegetative expansion. Fruiting requires the opposite conditions:
fresh air exchange, light, and often a temperature drop. These changes mimic
the natural environmental cues that trigger reproduction in wild fungi —
typically the arrival of cooler, wetter conditions with more airflow.

## The Four Triggers

### 1. Fresh Air Exchange (FAE)

The most important trigger. CO₂ levels during colonization may reach 10,000-
20,000 ppm. Reducing CO₂ below 1,000 ppm (ideally 400-800 ppm) is the
primary signal that initiates pinning. In practice this means:

- Opening or unsealing grow containers
- Activating ventilation fans (typically 2-6 air changes per hour)
- Providing horizontal laminar airflow across the substrate surface

### 3. Temperature Drop

A 5-10°C reduction from colonization temperature signals seasonal change:

| Species | Colonization Temp | Fruiting Temp |
|---|---|---|
| *P. ostreatus* | 22-26°C | 12-18°C |
| *P. pulmonarius* | 25-28°C | 18-24°C |
| *P. eryngii* | 20-24°C | 10-16°C |
| *P. citrinopileatus* | 24-28°C | 20-26°C |

Cold shocking (submerging blocks in 4-10°C water for 6-12 hours) is
practiced by some growers, particularly for *P. ostreatus*, though research
shows the CO₂ reduction and humidity change may be more important than the
cold itself.

### 4. High Humidity

Maintaining 90-95% relative humidity during pinning prevents the tiny
primordia from drying out before they can develop. Once pins reach 1-2cm,
humidity can be gradually reduced to 85-90% to encourage stronger stems and
