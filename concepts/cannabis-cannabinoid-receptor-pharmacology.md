### Binding Affinity

- **CBD** has greater affinity for CB2 than CB1
- **THC** binds relatively equally to both CB1 and CB2
- **(E)-beta-caryophyllene** is a natural CB2-selective agonist found in many spice plants
- **Synthetic agonists:** JWH-133, AM1241, GW-405,833, HU308
- **Antagonists:** SR144528, AM630

## The CB2 Activation Advantage

CB2 activation offers therapeutic benefits without the psychoactive effects associated with CB1. This is particularly relevant for:

- Anti-inflammatory applications
- [[cannabis-pain-management-protocols]] in chronic inflammatory conditions
- Gastrointestinal disorders

## THC:CBD Ratios and Receptor Engagement

The ratio of THC to CBD in different cannabis strains determines the relative engagement of CB1 vs CB2:

| Strain Type | THC:CBD Ratio | Receptor Tendency |
|-------------|--------------|-------------------|
| Sativa | Higher THC:CBD | Favors CB1 activation |
| Indica | Lower THC:CBD | Favors CB2 activation |
| Indica-dominant hybrid | Moderate | Balanced with CB2 emphasis |

This explains why sativas tend to produce more cerebral, stimulating effects (CB1 in the brain) while indicas tend toward body-focused, relaxing, anti-inflammatory effects (CB2 in peripheral tissues).
