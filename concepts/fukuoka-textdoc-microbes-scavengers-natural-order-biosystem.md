## Pesticide Effects on Soil Microbiology

Fukuoka documents with precision how pesticides devastate soil microbial
communities. **PCP (pentachlorophenol)**, widely used as a wood
preservative and agricultural fungicide, acts as both a bactericide and
fungicide. When applied to orchard soil, it kills the putrefactive fungi
that decompose fallen leaves on the ground surface. This has cascading
consequences: without fungal decomposition, earthworms lose their food
source and decline; ground beetles that prey on pests disappear; and
microorganisms throughout the soil profile are destroyed. **Chloropicrin**
presents a different pattern of disruption. It halts bacterial soft rot
in crops, but simultaneously kills the bacteria that naturally moderate
and compete with the soft rot organism. The result is paradoxical: the
treatment suppresses the disease temporarily but eliminates its natural
competitors, leaving the field wide open for soft rot bacteria to
proliferate once the chemical dissipates.

## The Pine Rot Cascade

The pine rot epidemic devastating Japan's forests illustrates Fukuoka's
systems thinking about microbial ecology. Conventional forestry blamed
nematodes as the sole cause, but Fukuoka traced the true causal chain:
first, **soil acidification** kills the matsutake mycorrhizal fungi that
normally protect pine roots; second, with the mycorrhizal barrier gone,
**pathogenic molds** invade the weakened root systems; third, the
compromised trees become vulnerable to **nematode infestation**, which
delivers the final blow. The nematodes are the last symptom, not the
first cause. Each stage in this cascade involves microbial disruption,
and the epidemic can only be understood by tracing the chain backward to
its origin in degraded soil conditions.
