## The Signal Transmission Concept

The five-condition design established a fundamental principle of plant physiology: the
tip of the shoot perceives light direction and sends a signal to the lower stem,
which responds by differential cell elongation (cells on the shaded side elongate more
than cells on the illuminated side, causing the plant to bend toward the light). This
tip-to-bend signal transmission was later identified as the plant hormone auxin
(indole-3-acetic acid), which redistributes to the shaded side of the stem and promotes
cell elongation there.

The signal transmission concept was revolutionary because it demonstrated that plants
possess a decentralized but coordinated information processing system. The "eye" at the
tip and the "muscle" in the stem are separated spatially but connected by a mobile
chemical signal — an architecture that parallels, in an abstract sense, the
eye-to-brain-to-muscle pathway in animals, albeit without any neural tissue.
