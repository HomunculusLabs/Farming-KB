## Outdoor Wood Lover Cultivation

Detailed guide for cultivating wood-decomposing mushrooms (Psilocybe azurescens, P. cyanescens, P. ovoideocystidiata, P. allenii) in outdoor beds. See [[mushroom-cultivation-methods]] for method selection and [[mushroom-growing-reference]] for general parameters.

## Species Overview

| Species | Native Range | Fruiting Temp | Potency (psilocybin % dry) | Notes |
|---------|-------------|---------------|---------------------------|-------|
| P. azurescens | Oregon coast | 7-10C (45-50F) | 1.4-1.7% | Most potent psilocybin mushroom known |
| P. cyanescens | Pacific NW, worldwide | 10-15C (50-59F) | 0.5-1.2% | Wavy cap, aggressive colonizer |
| P. ovoideocystidiata | Eastern US, Ohio River valley | 10-15C | 0.3-0.6% | Ovoids, stream banks |
| P. allenii | Pacific NW | 10-15C | 0.6-1.0% | Allenii, wood chips |

All belong to section Cyanescens (wood-inhabiting, cold-fruiting). They require hardwood substrates and cool autumn conditions.

## Bed Preparation

### Wood Chip Spawn (Stage 1)
Prepare grain spawn first ([[mushroom-substrates]]), then expand to sterilized wood chips:

1. **Chip selection**: Beech (Fagus sylvatica), alder, or hazelnut. Use small chips for spawn, larger for beds
2. **Soak**: Submerge chips in water 24-48 hours (max 48h or fungi may start on wood). Drain thoroughly
3. **Supplement** (optional): Add 1/2 cup oatmeal or boiled grass seed per bag to accelerate colonization
4. **Sterilize**: Fill polypropylene bags, sterilize 3 hours at 121C. Cool in flow cabinet
5. **Inoculate**: Add colonized grain spawn to bags, seal with impulse sealer, shake to distribute
6. **Incubate**: 20C for 3-4 weeks until fully colonized

### Outdoor Bed (Stage 2)
1. **Site selection**: Half-shaded location under shrubs. Avoid full sun. Natural dune grass or garden edge locations work well
2. **Dig**: 15-20cm (6-8 inch) deep hole or raised bed
3. **Substrate**: Coarse hardwood chips soaked 24 hours and drained
4. **Spawn ratio**: Minimum 1:5 spawn to substrate by volume; 1:3 preferred if available. Azurescens guide recommends at least 1/5th spawn by volume
5. **Mix**: Combine colonized wood chip spawn with fresh chips, mix thoroughly
6. **Water**: Soak bed thoroughly. Do not overwater — repeat after 1 week rather than flooding
7. **Cover**: Wet cardboard + perforated plastic (or plain plastic) to retain moisture

## Seasonal Timeline

| Season | Action |
|--------|--------|
| Spring (Mar-May) | Prepare beds, inoculate. Ideal timing for same-year fruiting |
| Summer (Jun-Aug) | Keep covered. Check moisture weekly. Do not overwater (aggressive growth but no fruiting) |
| Early Autumn (Sep) | Remove plastic. Increase watering to 1-2x per week |
| Late Autumn (Oct-Nov) | First fruits appear at 7-10C (45-50F). Typically 2 flushes, ~1 month apart |
| Winter (Dec-Feb) | Growth stops below 0C. Add fresh wood chips on top for next year's nutrition |
| Year 2+ | Patches can produce for decades. Add fresh chips each spring |

Beds laid in autumn of the preceding year or early spring have excellent fruiting prospects. Later plantings require more spawn to secure autumn fruiting.

## Pinning Triggers

- **Temperature drop**: 7-10C (45-50F) is the primary trigger for azurescens
- **Moisture increase**: Increased rainfall and watering from September
- **Light**: Blue spectrum (370-460nm) initiates primordia (Badham 1980)
- **Cold shock**: Not required for outdoor beds — natural autumn temperature decline suffices

## Watering Management

Critical: overwatering during summer promotes aggressive mycelial growth but suppresses fruiting in autumn. Keep beds humid but not wet for first 2 weeks after establishment, then minimal water until September. From September, water 1-2x weekly unless rainfall is sufficient.

## Pest Protection

- **Slugs**: Most common pest. Use beer traps, diatomaceous earth barriers, or hand-pick at night
- **Rodents**: Grain spawn attracts rodents — this is why wood chip spawn is preferred over direct grain inoculation. Use wire mesh under beds if rodents are present
- **Insects**: Various insects under plastic covering are normal and generally harmless
- **Contamination**: Outdoor beds are resilient. If a contaminated jar is discarded in a garden bed, mushrooms may still appear in autumn

## Harvesting

- Pick fruits as caps expand, before full spore drop for best quality
- Expect 1-2 flushes per season, approximately 1 month apart
- Fruiting may continue until January in mild climates
- Prolong season by placing a tent over the bed

## Patch Maintenance and Overwintering

- After fruiting ceases (below 0C), work a layer of fresh wood chips into the top of the patch
- Cardboard used as bed cover becomes colonized and can inoculate new beds — place colonized cardboard at bottom of new bed, cover with chips
- Well-maintained patches can produce for decades
- Treated patches release enormous quantities of spores annually
