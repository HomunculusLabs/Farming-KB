# cannabis methods of consumption

## Overview

Cannabis may be eaten raw, cooked into other foods, drunk as juice, inhaled after vaporization, smoked in a pipe, wrapped in paper and smoked, or rubbed into the skin. The method of consumption significantly affects onset time, duration of effects, bioavailability, and therapeutic appropriateness for different conditions. The [[cannabis-health-index]] (Blesching) provides guidance on selecting the right administration method for specific health conditions, emphasizing that different modes of delivery produce noticeably different effects even with the same cannabis material.

It is not safe to drive or operate heavy machinery after taking any psychoactive form of cannabis.

## Inhalation

### Vaporization
Inhalation of cannabinoids via vaporization quickly reaches the bloodstream, and effects are often experienced in minutes or even seconds. However, effects are of a shorter duration than other modes of administration. Vaporization heats cannabis to a temperature that releases cannabinoids as vapor without combustion, avoiding the harmful byproducts of smoking (tar, carbon monoxide, carcinogens).

**Advantages:**
- Fastest onset -- seconds to minutes
- Precise dose control (especially with metered-dose vaporizers)
- Avoids combustion byproducts
- Full cannabinoid and terpene profile preserved
- Easy to titrate dose incrementally

**Disadvantages:**
- Shorter duration of effects (2-4 hours)
- Temporary heart rate increase may concern cardiovascular patients
- Lung irritation possible with prolonged use
- Requires vaporization equipment
- Not ideal for sustained, long-lasting therapeutic effects

**Best for:** Acute symptoms (nausea, breakthrough pain, anxiety attacks), situations requiring rapid relief

### Smoking
Smoking remains the most common traditional method but carries the greatest health risks due to combustion byproducts. The Cannabis Health Index generally recommends vaporization over smoking for therapeutic use. Smoking does provide rapid onset and full-spectrum cannabinoid delivery.

**Note:** Research from Vinciguerra et al. (1988) documented inhalation marijuana as an antiemetic for cancer patients, establishing the clinical foundation for inhaled cannabinoid therapy.

## Oral Consumption

### Edibles and Capsules
If eaten, it may take 45 minutes to an hour and a half for cannabis to be absorbed through the gastrointestinal tract. The effects of consumed cannabis thus tend to be delayed, last much longer (4-8 hours), and are noticeably different compared to the effects of inhalation. Oral consumption undergoes first-pass hepatic metabolism, which converts THC to 11-hydroxy-THC -- a more potent metabolite that produces stronger psychoactive effects.

Any favorite recipes can be fortified with cannabis-infused oil, or an alcohol-based tincture that supports specific needs. The oil or tincture should be added after the cooking process is completed, and at the right dose specific to the therapeutic window.

**Advantages:**
- Longest duration of effects (4-8 hours)
- No lung exposure
- Precise dosing with capsules
- Sustained therapeutic effects for chronic conditions
- Discreet consumption

**Disadvantages:**
- Delayed onset (45-90 minutes) makes titration difficult
- First-pass metabolism converts THC to more potent 11-hydroxy-THC
- Dose cannot be easily adjusted once consumed
- Effects are "noticeably different" from inhalation
- Variable absorption depending on food intake and individual metabolism

**Best for:** Chronic conditions requiring sustained effects (pain, inflammation, sleep), appetite stimulation, conditions where prolonged coverage is needed

### Raw Cannabis Juice
Raw cannabis juice is a unique preparation method highlighted in the Cannabis Health Index. Fresh, raw cannabis contains CBD-acid and THC-acid in their non-psychoactive acidic forms. Juicing raw cannabis provides:

- High doses of CB2-activating cannabinoids without psychoactive effects
- CBD-acid with selective COX-2 inhibition (anti-inflammatory without GI damage)
- Non-psychoactive alternative for patients who cannot tolerate THC
- Full-spectrum cannabinoid and terpene profile
- Enzyme-rich, nutrient-dense preparation

Raw cannabis, fresh leaf, and leaf juice contain CBD-acid and thus favor CB2 activation. This makes raw juice particularly appropriate for inflammatory conditions, autoimmune disorders, and patients with sensitivity to psychoactive effects.

## Sublingual and Tinctures

Infused oils or tinctures often come in dropper bottles, which allow for precise dosing. As always, start slowly with a few drops, and wait at least one hour to feel the effect. Then slowly increase the dosage by a couple of drops, repeating the process until the desired therapeutic effect is achieved. Many patients start with 3 or 4 drops and work from there. The oil or tinctures can easily be made at home or bought at most dispensaries. Since most products are not standardized, the titration process may need to be repeated each time a new bottle is made or purchased.

Sublingual administration places cannabinoids under the tongue for absorption through the mucous membranes directly into the bloodstream, partially bypassing first-pass hepatic metabolism.

**Advantages:**
- Faster onset than oral (15-45 minutes)
- Partially bypasses first-pass metabolism
- Precise dropper-based dosing
- Easy to titrate
- Longer duration than inhalation (3-6 hours)
- Available in both THC-dominant and CBD-dominant formulations

**Disadvantages:**
- Slower onset than inhalation
- Less precise dosing than capsules
- Products vary widely in concentration and quality
- Taste may be unpleasant for some patients

**Best for:** Conditions requiring intermediate onset and duration, patients who need precise dose control, anxiety and mood conditions, neurological conditions

### Sativex (Oromucosal Spray)
Sativex is a pharmaceutical cannabis-based medicine administered as a sublingual spray. It consists of roughly equal parts THC (2.7 mg per spray) and CBD (2.5 mg per spray). Sativex is not synthetic but rather made from cannabis, with formulation ratios very similar to actual [[cannabis-sativa]] ratios. It is approved in many countries for MS spasticity and neuropathic pain.

## Topical Application

Topical creams are used to deliver medicine to specific and isolated problem areas. Absorption rates may be similar to that of ingestion but tend to be less systemic and more local. Topicals act locally without producing psychoactive effects -- even THC-based topicals do not enter the bloodstream in significant amounts, because the cannabinoid molecules are too large to penetrate through the skin layers into systemic circulation.

Common topical preparations include:
- **Balms and salves**: Oil-based preparations for localized pain and inflammation
- **Creams and lotions**: Water-based preparations for skin conditions
- **Oils**: Direct application to affected areas
- **Patches**: Sustained-release topical delivery

**Advantages:**
- No psychoactive effects whatsoever
- Targeted delivery to specific problem areas
- Can be applied 2-3 times daily for chronic conditions
- Full-spectrum cannabinoid and terpene delivery
- Good for skin conditions, joint pain, muscle soreness

**Disadvantages:**
- Limited systemic absorption
- Variable absorption rates depending on skin condition
- May require consistent application for chronic conditions
- Quality and consistency of commercial products vary significantly

**Best for:** Dermatological conditions (psoriasis, eczema, acne), localized joint pain (arthritis), muscle soreness, post-surgical wound care, neuropathic pain in specific areas

## Rectal Suppositories

Some patients who want to deliver cannabinoids to the lower half of the intestinal tract use suction-bulbs filled with cannabis-infused oils inserted into the rectum similar to a suppository. This method provides direct delivery to the lower GI tract with minimal psychoactive effects.

**Advantages:**
- Direct delivery to lower GI tract
- Minimal psychoactive effects
- Useful for lower intestinal conditions (colitis, Crohn's)
- Bypasses much of first-pass metabolism

**Disadvantages:**
- Less familiar and socially awkward administration method
- Limited product availability
- Variable absorption
- Not well-studied in clinical trials

**Best for:** Lower GI inflammatory conditions (ulcerative colitis, Crohn's disease), pelvic pain conditions, patients who cannot tolerate oral or inhaled routes
