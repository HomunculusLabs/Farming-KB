# Hallucinogenic Mushroom User Terminology and Brand Names

## Overview

The language surrounding [[emcdda-eu-legislative-timeline-hallucinogenic-mushrooms-2001-2006]] is rich and varied, reflecting both the [[ayahuasca-pharmacology-and-cultural-context]] of use and the commercial strategies of retailers. In the case [[health-risks-of-hallucinogenic-mushrooms]], there is significant overlap between brand names created by retailers and organic users terms that have developed within drug using communities. These terms and names often reflect changing market strategies, new trends, and the evolving social groups who sell and use these substances.

## Common English Language Terms

Hallucinogenic mushrooms are most widely known by the colloquial name "magic mushrooms." However, a wide variety of other terms are used across English speaking communities:

- **Shrooms** - widely used in the United Kingdom and North America
- **Mush** and **Mushies** - informal shortened forms
- **Boomers** - a popular slang term, likely derived from the boom in popularity
- **Psilocybes** - referring to the predominant genus containing psychoactive species
- **Cubes** - short for [[brassinosteroid-morphological-effects-psilocybe-cubensis-fruiting]], the most commonly cultivated species
- **Liberty Caps** - referring specifically to [[guzman-allen-gartz-psilocybe-semilanceata-temperate-cosmopolitan-distribution]], the most common wild European species
- **Caps** - a general term referencing the cap of the mushroom
- **Philosopher's Stone** - used to describe sclerotia of certain species, notably Psilocybe mexicana
- **Sacred Mushrooms** - a term emphasizing the spiritual and ceremonial associations
- **Teonanacatl** - the Aztec name meaning "divine flesh" or "flesh of god," which has gained popularity with users interested in [[sacred-mushroom-use-mexico-teonanacatl-indigenous-traditions]]
- **Mexican Mushrooms** - referring to the cubensis varieties originating from Mexico
- **Moon Children** - a poetic term used among some user communities

## European Language Terminology

Across European countries, hallucinogenic mushrooms have distinct names in local languages that reflect both the pharmacological understanding and cultural perceptions of the substances:

- **Dutch:** "Paddos" is the most common term, used widely in smartshop culture
- **German:** "Zauberpilze" (magic mushrooms) and "Psychoaktive Pilze" ([[ceremonial-shamanic-use-psychoactive-mushrooms]])
- **French:** "Champignons hallucinogenes" or the abbreviated "Champis"
- **Spanish:** "Hongos alucinogenos," "Hongos lisergicos," and "Honguitos"
- **Italian:** "Funghi magici"
- **Hungarian:** "Varazsgombak" (magic mushrooms) and "Pszilocibingombak"
- **Czech:** "Magické houby"
- **Polish:** "Magiczne grzybki" and "Grzyby halucynogenne"
- **Swedish:** "Magiska svampar" and "Psykedeliska svampar"
- **Norwegian:** "Fleinsopp"
- **Portuguese:** "Cogumelos mágicos" and "Cogumelos psicadélicos"
- **Finnish:** Related terms used in the Taikasieniforum community
- **Danish:** "Psilocybinsvampe" and "Magiske svampe"
- **Slovenian:** "Cudne gobe"
- **Slovakian:** "Magické huby"

## The Hallucinogenic Versus Psychedelic Debate

The terminology used to describe these substances carries significant implications. At different times and by different groups, these drugs have been called:

- **Psychedelic** - meaning "mind opening" or "mind expanding," preferred by most users
- **Psychotomimetic** - meaning "resembling psychosis," used by some clinical researchers
- **Psychodysleptic** - meaning "mind disrupting," used in some regulatory and medical contexts
- **Hallucinogenic** - referring to hallucination producing properties, the term most widely adopted by the scientific community
- **Phantastica** - a less familiar historical term
- **Oneirogenic** - referring to dream inducing properties

All these names depend on the purposes and starting premises of those using them, and they bring different positive or negative connotations. The scientific community has largely adopted the term "hallucinogens," however inaccurate it might be, whereas most users naturally prefer the term "psychedelic." In practice, the two terms are used interchangeably.

## Commercial Brand Names and Smartshop Labels

With the commercialisation of hallucinogenic mushrooms by smartshops beginning in the late 1990s, new labels and brand names emerged reflecting changes in the groups who sell and use these drugs:

- **Philosopher's Stone** - marketed as a premium product, describing the sclerotia of Psilocybe mexicana sold by many smartshops as a truffle like product
- **Herbal Highs** - a general marketing label used by retailers following the trend in the late 1990s toward preference for organic and herbal products
- **Herbal XTC** - a marketing label for products containing stimulant herbs such as Ephedra alkaloids, Sida Cordifolia, Guarana, and Caffeine
- **Mexican, Thai, Colombian, Amazonian** - variety names for Psilocybe [[psilocybe-cubensis-strains-names-and-marketing]] marketed by smartshops, reflecting their [[sativa-landrace-strains-and-geographic-origins]]

## Aztec Terminology

The term **Teonanacatl** has gained particular popularity among users interested in the indigenous and ceremonial aspects of mushroom use. This Nahuatl word was used by the Aztecs and translates roughly to "divine flesh" or "the flesh of god." Its adoption by modern users reflects a broader trend of reconnecting psychoactive substance use with indigenous spiritual traditions.
