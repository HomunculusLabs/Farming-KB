# Permaculture Design Methods

## Description

[[permaculture-design]] Methods are a set of eight systematic approaches to land-use planning and system design outlined by [[bill-mollison]] in *Permaculture: A Designer's Manual* (Chapter 3). These methods provide structured frameworks for translating [[permaculture-principles]] into practical site-specific designs.

## Classification

- **Type:** Technique / Methodology
- **Domain:** Permaculture Design, Land-Use Planning, Systems Design
- **Origin:** Formalized by Bill Mollison, 1988
- **Scope:** Applicable to any scale from balcony garden to broadacre farm

## Key Facts

1. **Eight design methods:**
   - **Analysis (3.2):** Design by listing characteristics of components — cataloging inputs, outputs, and intrinsic properties.
   - **Observation (3.3):** Design by expanding on direct observation of a site.
   - **[[deduction-from-nature]] (3.4):** Design by adopting lessons learned from natural ecosystems.
   - **Options and Decisions (3.5):** Design as a selection of options based on decision-making criteria.
   - **Data Overlay (3.6):** Design by map overlays — stacking site factor maps to reveal patterns.
   - **Random Assembly (3.7):** Design by testing unexpected element pairings for beneficial interactions.
   - **Flow Diagrams (3.8):** Design for workplaces — mapping energy, materials, and people movement.
   - **[[zone-and-sector-analysis]] (3.9):** Design by master pattern — organizing by frequency of use and managing external energies.

2. **Technique vs. Strategy vs. Design:** Mollison distinguishes between technique (one-dimensional, how to do something), strategy (technique plus time), materials (physical resources), and assemblies (putting components together).

3. **Functional design principle:** Every component should function in many ways. Every essential function should be supported by many components.

4. **Continuous process:** Design is never finished — it is a continuous process guided by observations of that process. All designs involving life forms undergo long-term change.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Analysis method:** Listing inputs and outputs for each mushroom species mirrors Mollison's chicken analysis. Connecting these to other system elements creates integrated design.
- **Observation for site selection:** Watching sun patterns, moisture levels, and existing fungal activity before committing to a mushroom growing site.
- **Deduction from nature:** Studying how mushrooms grow in wild forests and replicating those conditions is deduction from nature applied to cultivation.
- **Zone planning:** High-maintenance indoor grow rooms in Zone 1; low-maintenance outdoor log cultivation in Zone 2-3.
- **Flow diagrams:** Mapping substrate flow from preparation through inoculation, incubation, fruiting, harvest, and disposal identifies bottlenecks.
