## Overview

**Carex bigelowii**, commonly known as Bigelow's sedge, stiff sedge, or Glatved sedge, is a perennial rhizomatous sedge in the family Cyperaceae. It is widely distributed across arctic and alpine regions of the Northern Hemisphere, occurring from the Canadian Arctic archipelago and Greenland south through the Rocky Mountains and European Alps.

The species is a dominant component of arctic tundra vegetation, where its extensive rhizome mats form a significant portion of the living ground cover. It is one of the most cold-tolerant graminoid species, capable of photosynthesis at near-freezing temperatures.

Photosynthetic measurements have shown that Carex bigelowii maintains positive net carbon assimilation at leaf temperatures as low as -2°C, a physiological adaptation shared by few other vascular plants. This capacity allows it to extend its [[psilocybe-cubensis-strain-potency-variability-controlled-conditions-bigwood-beug]]. Seedling establishment in the field is infrequent, with most population expansion occurring through clonal rhizome growth.

The species is closely related to Carex rigida of the European Alps and Carex scirpoidea of montane [[north-carolina-mycodiversity]].

In Europe, the species ranges from Svalbard and northern Scandinavia south through the Scottish Highlands, Alps, Carpathians, and Pyrenees. Asian populations occur across Siberia, Kamchatka, and mountain ranges of Japan.

The species occupies a broad range of arctic and alpine habitats including dry tundra, fell-field, snow-bed communities, alpine meadows, rocky slopes, moraines, and river gravel bars. It is particularly abundant on calcareous substrates.

Elevation ranges from sea level in the high Arctic to over 3,000 meters in the European Alps. Arctic populations typically occupy well-drained sites on gentle slopes, ridges, and patterned-ground features.

The species is intolerant of prolonged waterlogging and is generally absent from wet sedge meadows and fens. It prefers well-aerated mineral soils with low organic content and neutral to alkaline pH.

In the Rocky Mountains, Carex bigelowii occurs on exposed ridges and alpine tundra above treeline, often associated with fell-field communities dominated by cushion plants and lichens. It is less common in sheltered alpine meadows.

## Related Species and Taxonomy

The Carex bigelowii complex has been the subject of extensive taxonomic debate. Some European authors recognize Carex bigelowii s.l. as encompassing several morphologically similar taxa including C. rigida, C. scirpoidea, and C. parachorista.

Molecular phylogenetic studies support recognizing these as distinct species, though occasional hybridization occurs in contact zones. The tetraploid chromosome number (2n=52–56) distinguishes C. bigelowii from most of its close relatives.
