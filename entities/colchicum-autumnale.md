## Overview

**Colchicum autumnale** (meadow saffron, autumn crocus, naked ladies) is a
perennial herbaceous plant in the family Colchicaceae (formerly Liliaceae).
Native to Europe and the British Isles, it is one of the most pharmacologically
significant toxic plants in Western herbal tradition.

The plant produces [[crocus-sativus]]*) has historically caused
fatal poisonings, as the two plants share the common name "saffron" and bloom
at similar times in some regions.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Taxonomy:**

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Order:** Liliales
- **Family:** Colchicaceae
- **Genus:** Colchicum
- **Species:** *C. autumnale* L.

**Synonyms:** *Colchicum vernale* Ker Gawl., *Bulbocodium autumnale* (L.) Spreng.

**Morphology:**

- **Corm:** Ovoid, 3–5 cm diameter, dark brown, producing new cormlets annually
- **Leaves:** 4–6 basal, linear-lanceolate, 20–40 cm long, glossy dark green;
  emerge in spring and wither before flowers appear
- **Flowers:** Solitary or in groups of 2–6, arising directly from bare ground
  (hence "naked ladies"); perianth segments 6, lilac-pink to purple, 4–7 cm
  long; stamens 6; ovary inferior, underground
- **Fruit:** Capsule, 2–5 cm, containing numerous small dark brown seeds
- **Flowering:** August–October (autumn); leaves emerge February–May
- **Height:** 10–30 cm at flowering (leafless scape)

## Distribution and Habitat

Native to western and central Europe, from the British Isles eastward through
France, Germany, and the Alps to the Balkans and northern Italy. Introduced and
naturalized in Scandinavia and parts of eastern Europe.

Typical habitats include damp meadows, grazed pastures, river floodplains,
and open woodland margins. Prefers moist, calcareous to neutral soils at
elevations up to 1,800 meters. In Britain, the plant is an indicator species
of old unimproved meadows and has declined due to agricultural intensification.

## Bioactive Compounds

**Colchicine** (C₂₂H₂₅NO₆, MW 399.44) is the primary bioactive alkaloid.
It is a tricyclic tropolone alkaloid that binds tubulin, inhibiting microtubule
polymerization. This disrupts mitotic spindle formation, arresting cells in
metaphase.

Concentration varies by plant part: seeds contain 0.3–0.5% colchicine,
corms 0.2–0.4%, and flowers 0.1–0.2%. Additional related alkaloids include
colchiceine, demecolcine (colcemid), and N-formyl-N-deacetylcolchicine.

Demecolcine has similar antimitotic activity and was historically used in
cytogenetics as a karyotyping agent.

## Pharmacology and Therapeutic Use

Colchicine is FDA-approved for acute gout flares, familial Mediterranean fever
(FMF), and cardiovascular risk reduction (COLCOT trial, 2019).

**Mechanism:** By inhibiting microtubule assembly, colchicine blocks neutrophil
chemotaxis, adhesion, and inflammasome activation — key drivers of gouty
inflammation. Anti-inflammatory effects also suppress IL-1β release.

**Dosing:** Acute gout: 1.2 mg initially, then 0.6 mg one hour later.
FMF prophylaxis: 0.6–1.8 mg daily. Therapeutic range is extremely narrow.

**Toxicity:** LD₅₀ in humans estimated at 0.5–1.0 mg/kg (approximately
10–20 mg total, or 6–12 colchicine tablets). Symptoms include severe GI distress,
multi-organ failure, bone marrow suppression, and ascending paralysis.
Fatalities are common at doses above 0.8 mg/kg. No specific antidote exists;
treatment is supportive.

## Traditional and Historical Use

Use of *Colchicum* for joint pain dates to the 6th century CE in Byzantine
medicine. Ibn Sina (Avicenna, c. 980–1037 CE) described the plant for joint
disease. In 18th-century Europe, *C. autumnale* was standard for gout despite
its toxicity. Colchicine was first isolated in 1820 by Pelletier and Caventou.

## Ecological Role

*C. autumnale* is toxic to all livestock and most herbivores, providing
effective chemical defense. Sheep and cattle generally avoid it in mixed
pastures, though poisoned animals may consume it when other forage is scarce.

The underground corm and ovary attract specific pollinators despite the
autumn flowering period. Bees occasionally visit flowers, though pollination
ecology is understudied.

Seeds are dispersed by ants (myrmecochory) via an elaiosome-like structure,
promoting localized dispersal in meadow habitats.

## Cultivation and Caution

**Cultivation:** Corms planted 8–10 cm deep in late summer in well-drained soils.
Hardy to USDA zone 5. **All parts are extremely toxic** — wear gloves when
handling. Never grow near vegetable gardens or areas accessible to children.

The autumn crocus is not a true crocus (*Crocus* spp.). True autumn crocuses
(*C. speciosus*, *C. sativus*) belong to Iridaceae and do not contain
colchicine. Historical confusion with saffron has caused fatal poisonings.
