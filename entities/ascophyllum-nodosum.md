## Ascophyllum nodosum

## Description

*Ascophyllum nodosum* is a species of cold-water brown seaweed (kelp) widely used as a fungal food additive in [[actively-aerated-compost-tea]] teas and as a soil amendment. It is one of the most commercially available seaweed species in horticulture, sold through garden centers, Internet suppliers, and even animal feed stores (where it is often marketed as powdered algae).

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Chromista (Stramenopiles)
- **Phylum:** Ochrophyta
- **Class:** Phaeophyceae (brown algae)
- **Order:** Fucales
- **Family:** Fucaceae
- **Genus:** *Ascophyllum*
- **Species:** *A. nodosum*
- **Common names:** Knotted wrack, Norwegian kelp, rockweed

## Key Facts

- Cold-water kelp species harvested from North Atlantic coastlines.
- Rich in complex carbohydrates, trace minerals, cytokinins, auxins, and alginates that support fungal growth.
- Used as a fungal food source in [[compost-tea-recipes-and-ratios]] — promotes hyphal growth and mycelial development during brewing.
- Provides surfaces for fungi to attach to while growing in the tea brew.
- Available as powdered, meal, or liquid extract formulations.
- Contains natural growth hormones (cytokinins and auxins) that can directly benefit plant growth.
- Often sold in animal feed stores as powdered algae, making it accessible and inexpensive.
- Also valued for its content of humic and [[fulvic-acid]] precursors, alginic acid, and over 60 trace minerals.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

*Ascophyllum nodosum* is one of the most important fungal foods in the compost tea brewer's toolkit. In [[the-soil-food-web]] approach, teas are tailored to be either bacterially or fungally dominated depending on the target plant's needs. Kelp-based amendments like *A. nodosum* shift the microbial balance toward fungi, which is critical for perennials, trees, shrubs, and other plants that prefer fungally dominated soils.

Beyond compost tea, *A. nodosum* extracts are used as foliar sprays and soil drenches in their own right. The cytokinins they contain can delay leaf senescence, improve stress tolerance, and enhance root development. The alginic acid improves soil structure by binding soil particles into aggregates — a function that synergizes with [[fungal-hyphae]] and bacterial slime in building soil architecture.

For mycologists, kelp amendments are particularly relevant when culturing or promoting beneficial fungi in compost, mulch, or soil systems. The complex carbohydrates in kelp are preferentially consumed by fungi over bacteria, helping shift microbial [[core-endorhiza-bacterial-community-composition-cannabis]] in a fungal direction.
