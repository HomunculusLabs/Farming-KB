## Description

A spore syringe is a sterile medical-grade syringe filled with a suspension of mushroom spores in water, used to inoculate substrate jars in the PF TEK and other cultivation methods. In the context of the PF TEK, the spore syringe is the primary vector for introducing *Psilocybe cubensis* genetics to the substrate. Psylocybe Fanaticus distributed spore syringes commercially from Seattle, Washington, guaranteeing them as clean (uncontaminated) and viable (germinating), with a money-back replacement policy.

## Classification

- **Category:** Inoculation tool / cultivation supply
- **Type:** Sterile syringe with spore-water suspension
- **Volume:** Standard 10 cc capacity (enough for 10 jars at 1 cc per jar)
- **Contents:** Mushroom spores suspended in sterile water
- **Species (PF TEK):** *Psilocybe cubensis*, from "the finest available blueing strain"

## Key Facts

- Each spore syringe contains approximately 10 cc of spore solution, enough to inoculate 10 jars at the recommended rate of 1 cc per jar.
- More spore solution per jar speeds colonization but reduces the total number of jars that can be inoculated.
- Spore syringes must be shaken well before use to redistribute spores in the water, as they settle during storage.
- Spore syringes can survive freezing but extreme low temperatures are probably destructive to the spores.
- *Psilocybe cubensis* spores begin to degrade a few months after being taken. After approximately 1.5 years, germination is greatly reduced or absent.
- Fresh spore syringes produce "massive and quick" germination.
- Properly prepared spore syringes remain viable for several months and up to a year or more when stored in cool, dark conditions.

## Inoculation Technique (PF TEK)

1. Ensure jars are completely cool to the touch before inoculation.
2. Shake the syringe well to redistribute spores.
3. Remove tape from needle holes on the jar lid.
4. Insert the needle through a lid hole, tilting the syringe body back toward the center so the tip touches the glass.
5. Distribute a few drops of spore water down the side of the jar at each hole.
6. Release plunger pressure as soon as water appears around the needle tip.
7. Shake the syringe slightly between hole inoculations to keep spores distributed.
8. If the needle plugs, draw it back slightly to unplug.
9. Re-tape the holes after inoculation.

## Sterile Technique

- If the needle is touched, it must be flame-sterilized before continuing.
- **Alcohol flame technique:** Use denatured alcohol on an inverted tequila shot glass, ignited to produce a clean blue flame. Alcohol flames are preferred over butane lighters, which leave undesirable soot residue.
- Heat the needle for a few seconds; small "pops" of boiling water may spurt out but spores within the syringe remain safe.
- Resterilize the needle immediately before reuse.
- Store syringes in a dark, cool place when not in use.

## Spore Syringe Preparation

Spore syringes can be prepared from spore prints using the following materials and procedure:

**Materials:**
- Spore print in a jar (KERR 1/2 pint wide mouth)
- Sterile syringe with water for injecting
- Sterile syringe for loading spore solution
- Small Pyrex glass stirring rod
- Denatured alcohol, shot glass, and eye dropper
- Lid with two holes (one for stirring rod, one for syringe needle)

**Procedure:**
1. Boil syringes: draw boiling water in and out several times, refill with boiling water, replace needle guard, wrap in foil, and boil for one hour.
2. Inject sterile water into the spore print jar through the needle hole.
3. Flame-sterilize the glass stirring rod, let it cool, insert through the center lid hole, and scrape spores into the water.
4. Insert the sterile syringe needle through the small edge hole, tilt the jar to bring water to the needle tip, and draw spore water into the syringe.
5. Minimize exposure of the jar interior to room air throughout the procedure.
6. Store completed syringes at cool temperatures in the dark.

## Failure Modes

- Spore solution absorbed by the dry vermiculite layer instead of reaching the substrate (inoculate deep enough)
- Spores killed by hot jars after sterilization (wait until cool)
- Spores killed during shipping by extreme heat (overheated cargo holds, mail trucks, or mailboxes in direct sun)
- Contaminated syringes (identified by non-germination plus bacterial odor)

## Relevance to Cultivation

The spore syringe is central to the PF TEK for multiple reasons:

- **Mass spore inoculation:** The technique deliberately uses spore inoculation rather than tissue cloning, preserving the complete genotype and preventing senescence (mutating and ceased fruiting).
- **Genetic diversity:** Each spore syringe contains millions of spores representing the full genetic diversity of the parent organism.
- **Sustainability:** "The spores insure a never ending succession of fungus, with all the power of the spores reproductive ability intact."
- **Legal status (US):** While *Psilocybe cubensis* mushrooms contain the contraband compounds psilocybin and psilocin, there are no US federal laws prohibiting the spores themselves.
- **Commercial availability:** PF sold spore syringes for $10 each (postage paid) with guaranteed viability and discretion (no customer lists, hand-addressed mailers, "PF" return address only).
