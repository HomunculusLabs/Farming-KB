## Mentha aquatica

## Description

Mentha aquatica, commonly known as water mint, is a perennial aquatic and semi-aquatic herb in the Lamiaceae family. Mollison identifies it as one of the plant species active in pathogen removal from wastewater in [[comparison-constructed-wetland-vs-backyard-pond]] and reed bed treatment systems. It grows naturally along stream margins, pond edges, and in shallow water, where its roots and associated rhizosphere microorganisms help break down harmful bacteria and organic pollutants.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Division**: Magnoliophyta
- **Class**: Magnoliopsida
- **Order**: Lamiales
- **Family**: Lamiaceae
- **Genus**: Mentha
- **Species**: M. aquatica

## Key Facts

- Water mint grows 30-90 cm tall with characteristic mint-scented foliage and terminal flower heads bearing lilac-pink flowers.
- Native to Europe, North Africa, and parts of Asia; widely naturalised in temperate regions worldwide.
- Mollison lists Mentha aquatica alongside Alisma plantago-aquatica, [[juncus-effusus]], [[schoenoplectus-lacustris]], Spartina spp., and Iris pseudocorus as species active in pathogen removal from polluted water.
- After water passes through reed beds containing Mentha aquatica and associated species, low to zero populations of E. coli, coliform bacteria, Salmonella, and Enterococci are recorded.
- Virus and worm eggs are also eliminated through these biological treatment systems.
- Like other mints, Mentha aquatica spreads vigorously by creeping rhizomes and stolons, making it effective at colonizing treatment wetlands.
- The essential oils contain menthol and related compounds with documented [[medicinal-mushroom-antimicrobial-properties]], which may contribute to pathogen suppression.
- Tolerates a wide range of water depths from damp soil to 10 cm submerged.

## Role in Water Purification

Mollison describes a [[sand-filtration-and-biological-water-treatment]] system using series of plantings:

1. **Sand filter** (2 m deep) for initial filtration
2. **Rush reed beds** in gravel for biological treatment
3. **Holding tanks** with limestone bases for pH adjustment

Mentha aquatica functions within the reed bed stage, where its root system provides [[query-how-do-i-design-habitat-for-beneficial-insects]] microorganisms while the plant itself absorbs excess nutrients and its antimicrobial compounds help suppress pathogens.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Permaculture design**: Water mint is an excellent component of [[comparison-constructed-wetlands-vs-bioswales]] for greywater and blackwater treatment in permaculture systems.
- **Multi-function plant**: Provides water purification, aromatic herb harvest, pollinator support, and habitat for beneficial insects.
- **Companion planting**: Can be used around pond margins to deter pests while purifying water.
- **Culinary and medicinal**: Leaves are edible and used in teas, while the antimicrobial properties have traditional medicinal applications.
- **Integrated systems**: Combines effectively with Phragmites, Typha, Juncus, and Scirpus in multi-species treatment wetlands.

## Cultivation Notes

- Propagates easily from stem cuttings or division of established plants.
- Prefers full sun to partial shade in consistently moist or shallow water conditions.
- Hardy in USDA zones 5-11.
- Can become invasive in favorable conditions; containment in constructed wetlands is recommended.
