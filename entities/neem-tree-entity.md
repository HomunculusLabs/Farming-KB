## Neem Tree (Azadirachta indica)

## Description

The neem tree ([[bill-mollison]]
highlights neem in two distinct roles: as a source of dried leaves for
ectoparasite control in poultry dust baths, and as an insecticidal oil
applied to hessian rubbing posts for lice control on pigs. These dual
applications demonstrate the tree's extraordinary versatility in
integrated animal management.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Order:** Sapindales
- **Family:** Meliaceae
- **Genus:** Azadirachta
- **Species:** A. indica

## Key Facts

- Evergreen to semi-deciduous tree, typically 15-20 metres tall
- Spreading crown provides dense shade; trunk diameter up to 1 metre
- Compound leaves with 20-31 serrated leaflets per frond
- Small white, honey-scented flowers in axillary panicles
- Olive-like drupes containing a single seed with a hard endocarp
- Seed kernels contain 30-50% oil rich in azadirachtin and related limonoids
- Azadirachtin is one of the most potent natural insect growth regulators
- All parts contain bioactive compounds: leaves, bark, seeds, roots, flowers
- Extremely drought-tolerant once established
- Can live 150-200 years in suitable conditions

## Relevance to Cultivation

### Poultry Ectoparasite Control

Mollison recommends dried neem tree leaves as a component of poultry dust
baths for ectoparasite control. In this application:

- Dried neem leaves are provided in dust bathing areas or mixed into
  bedding material
- Chickens dust-bathe naturally, coating their feathers with the leaf dust
- Neem compounds repel and kill mites, lice, and other external parasites
- Combined with [[melia-azedarach-entity]] and pyrethrum) used in hessian rubbing posts:

- Hessian-wrapped posts placed in pig range areas
- Impregnated with neem oil (often combined with the other two oils)
- Pigs rub against posts voluntarily, self-applying the treatment
- Azadirachtin disrupts lice lifecycle, killing adults and preventing
  nymph development
- Treatment is ongoing and self-administering

### Insecticidal Properties

Neem's insecticidal action is multifaceted:

- **Antifeedant:** Insects stop feeding after exposure
- **Growth disruptor:** Azadirachtin interferes with moulting hormone
- **Reproduction inhibitor:** Reduces fecundity in treated populations
- **Repellent:** Many insects avoid treated surfaces
- Effective against 200+ insect species while sparing most beneficials

### Permaculture Design Roles

- **Poultry parasite control:** Dried leaves in dust baths
- **Pig parasite control:** Oil in rubbing posts
- **Crop protection:** Neem spray for vegetable and orchard pests
- **Soil amendment:** Neem cake (seed residue) is a nitrogen-rich fertiliser
  that also suppresses soil nematodes
- **Shade and windbreak:** Large canopy provides shelter for animals
- **Storage protection:** Dried leaves mixed with stored grain deter weevils

### Cultivation Notes

- Thrives in tropical and subtropical climates (USDA zones 10-12)
- Requires annual rainfall of 400-1200 mm; drought-tolerant once established
- Prefers well-drained, deep soils but tolerates poor and rocky sites
- Propagates from seed (viable for only 2-3 weeks) or vegetative cuttings
- Begins flowering and fruiting at 3-5 years
- Full oil production from 10 years onward; trees yield 10-50 kg fruit/year
- Extraction: seeds are depulped, dried, and cold-pressed or solvent-extracted

### Companion Integration

Neem works synergistically with Melia azedarach (a fellow Meliaceae member)
and pyrethrum in Mollison's integrated parasite management. Using three
different modes of action prevents resistance and provides broader-spectrum
protection than any single agent alone.

## Summary

The neem tree is perhaps the preeminent multi-purpose tree for tropical
permaculture systems. Mollison specifically integrates it into both poultry
and pig management for natural parasite control—dried leaves for chicken
dust baths and seed oil for pig rubbing posts. Its combination of
insecticidal potency, low vertebrate toxicity, and on-farm producibility
makes it indispensable for chemical-free animal husbandry.
