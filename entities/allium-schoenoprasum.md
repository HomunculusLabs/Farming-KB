## Overview

**Allium schoenoprasum**, commonly known as chives, is a perennial herbaceous plant in the
family Amaryllidaceae (formerly Alliaceae). Native to [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]] of Europe, Asia,
and [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]], it is one of the most widely cultivated [[holzer-medicinal-and-culinary-herbs-compendium]] globally.

Chives are distinguished from other *Allium* species by their hollow, tubular leaves and
delicate purple-pink flowers borne on spherical umbels. The leaves and flowers are both
edible, providing a mild onion flavor widely used in European, Asian, and [[growing-gourmet-north-american-reishi-species]]
cuisines. The species also has documented antimicrobial and antioxidant properties.

## Taxonomy and Classification

Division: Magnoliophyta → Class: Liliopsida → Order: Asparagales → Family:
Amaryllidaceae → Subfamily: Allioideae → Tribe: Allieae → Genus: *Allium* → Subgenus:
*Rhizirideum* → Section: *Schoenoprasum* → Species: *A. schoenoprasum*.

The species was described by Carl Linnaeus in 1753. The epithet *schoenoprasum* derives
from Greek *skhoínos* (rush) and *prason* (leek), referencing the rush-like foliage.
Synonyms include *Allium sibiricum* L. and *Allium raddeanum* Regel.

Chives belong to a section of approximately 150 [[datura-metel-old-world-ethnobotany]] species, with *A. schoenoprasum*
being the only widely cultivated member of section *Schoenoprasum*.

## Morphology

Plants grow 20-50 cm tall from slender, elongated bulbs 1-2 cm in diameter. Bulbs are
tunicate, often clustered, producing dense clumps from rhizomatous offsets.

Leaves are 2-3 per plant, hollow, tubular, terete in cross-section, 20-40 cm long and
2-4 mm in diameter, dark green. They arise directly from the bulb with no distinct stem.

Inflorescences are terminal, spherical umbels 2-4 cm diameter, bearing 10-50 pedicellate
flowers. Flowers are actinomorphic, 6-tepalled, purple-pink to lilac, each tepal
8-12 mm long. Flowering occurs May through August in temperate zones.

Seeds are small, black, trigonal, 2.5-3.5 mm, with a hard seed coat. Germination
requires stratification at 4°C for 2-4 weeks for optimal results.

## Distribution and Habitat

*A. schoenoprasum* is native across a broad circumboreal range, from Europe (Scandinavia
to the Mediterranean) through Central Asia to Siberia, and across [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]] from Alaska
to the Great Lakes region and Appalachian mountains.

Wild populations occur in meadows, rocky outcrops, riverbanks, and subalpine grasslands at
elevations up to 2500 m. The species prefers well-drained, moderately fertile soils with
pH 6.0-7.0 and full sun to partial shade.

The American wild chive (*A. schoenoprasum* var. *sibiricum*) is sometimes treated as a
distinct subspecies. Cultivated chives derive primarily from European germplasm.

## Bioactive Compounds

Chives contain organosulfur compounds similar to other *Allium* species, though at lower
concentrations. Alliin (S-allyl-L-cysteine sulfoxide) is the primary cysteine sulfoxide,
converted by alliinase upon tissue damage to allicin, which degrades to diallyl sulfides.

Key bioactive constituents include methyl allyl disulfide, dipropyl disulfide, and
diallyl trisulfide. Flavonoids include kaempferol and quercetin glycosides.
