# Ice Cream Bean

**Type:** Species (Plant)
**Category:** Nitrogen-fixing fruit tree
**Also known as:** [[inga-edulis]], inga, pacay, joaquiniquil

## Overview
The ice cream bean (*Inga edulis*) is an evergreen tree native to the
Neotropics (Central and South America), growing to 18 m tall. It is
notable for producing large pods containing a sweet, white, sugar-rich
pulp with a texture and flavour resembling vanilla ice cream. The tree
has root-associated nitrogen-fixing bacteria, making it valuable as a
nurse crop while simultaneously producing edible beans. There are over
400 species of Inga, most of which produce edible fruit.

## Key Details

### Botanical Profile
- **Family:** Mimosaceae
- **Height:** Up to 18 m; width to 4 m
- **Origin:** Neotropics (Central and South America)
- **Habit:** Evergreen tree
- **Nitrogen fixation:** Yes, via root associates

### Growth Characteristics
- Adaptable to a large range of conditions
- Like most Andean plants, tolerates varied conditions
- Not conditioned to cool extremes; most species prefer warm lowland
  and mid-elevation tropics
- Over 400 species in the genus, most producing edible fruit

### Fruit
- Large pods containing sweet, white pulp around the seeds
- Pulp is sugar-rich with a smooth, creamy texture
- Flavour described as similar to vanilla ice cream
- Eaten fresh by scooping pulp from opened pods

### Uses
- **Food:** Edible pulp eaten fresh; unusual among nurse crops for
  having edible beans
- **Fodder:** In Mexico, foliage is cut and fed to livestock
- **Shade tree:** Used as shade plant for coffee and cacao
  plantations in Central America and the West Indies
- **Alley cropping:** Used in alley cropping systems in Chile
- **Ornamental:** Used throughout South America as a street tree
- **Timber:** Moderately heavy wood used for furniture, boxes, crates
- **Charcoal:** Utilised for charcoal production

### Limitations
- Wood not suited to external use (breaks down quickly)
- Most species not tolerant of cool conditions
- Young trees require protection from frost during establishment
- Seed propagation is straightforward from fresh seed

## Relevance
The ice cream bean (*Inga edulis*) is a unique [[permaculture]] species that combines the
rare qualities of nitrogen fixation with edible fruit production. This
dual function makes it exceptionally valuable in tropical food forest
design, where it can serve as a nurse tree for coffee, cacao, or other
understorey crops while producing a valued food crop. Its use in alley
cropping and as a shade tree demonstrates excellent multi-strata
  integration potential. Inga alley farming systems in the Amazon basin
  have shown improved soil fertility and crop yields compared to
  conventional monoculture approaches.
represents one of the most diverse nitrogen-fixing tree genera
available for agroforestry systems.
