## Overview

Cyclamen repandum is a tuberous perennial herb in the family Primulaceae, commonly known as the spring cyclamen or eastern sowbread. It is native to the Mediterranean region, where it grows in shaded woodlands and rocky slopes.

The species is distinguished by its angular, coarsely-toothed leaves and its relatively large, fragrant flowers with reflexed petals. It typically blooms in spring, from March through May, filling the air with a sweet scent in its native woodlands.

Cyclamen repandum is one of the less commonly cultivated Cyclamen species, though it has gained popularity among enthusiasts of Mediterranean woodland plants. It is valued for its elegant flower form and its suitability for shaded garden conditions.

The species belongs to the Cyclamen repandum subgroup within the genus, which also includes Cyclamen balearicum and Cyclamen creticum. These species share a spring flowering period and a preference for shaded habitats.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

The genus Cyclamen was traditionally placed in Primulaceae, then moved to Myrsinaceae, and has been returned to Primulaceae under the APG IV classification. The genus comprises approximately 23 species distributed across the Mediterranean basin and surrounding regions.

Within Cyclamen, the repandum subgroup is one of several well-defined clades supported by molecular phylogenetic studies. The subgroup is characterized by spring flowering, relatively large flowers, and heart-shaped leaves with angular lobes.
