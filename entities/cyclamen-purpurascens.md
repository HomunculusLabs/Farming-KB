## Overview

**Cyclamen purpurascens** is a perennial flowering plant in the family Primulaceae, native to the Alps and Carpathian mountains of central and southeastern Europe.

It is commonly known as the **European cyclamen** or **alpine cyclamen**, and is one of the most cold-hardy species in the genus.

Unlike most Cyclamen species that flower in autumn or spring, *C. purpurascens* blooms from June through September, making it unique among cultivated cyclamens.

The species is valued in horticulture for its fragrant, rose-scented flowers and its ability to thrive in shaded woodland gardens.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

*Cyclamen purpurascens* was described by Philip Miller in 1768. It belongs to the subgenus *Cyclamen* (sometimes treated as *Eucosme*).

The species epithet *purpurascens* refers to the purplish coloration on the underside of the leaves, which is a distinctive [[palmer-mushroom-hunting-field-identification]] character.

The plant produces a subglobose tuber 3–6 cm in diameter, which grows at or near the soil surface.

Leaves are heart-shaped to reniform, 3–7 cm long and 3–8 cm wide, with a toothed or slightly lobed margin.

The upper [[bloomfield-rust-germ-tube-tactile-sensing-leaf-surface-navigation]] is dark green, often with a silvery or pale green marbled pattern, while the underside is purplish-red.

Flowers are borne on slender pedicels 5–10 cm tall, with reflexed petals 15–22 mm long.

Petals are typically deep rose-pink to carmine, occasionally pale pink or white, with a darker blotch at the base of each lobe.

The flowers are notably fragrant, producing a sweet rose-like scent that distinguishes this species from most other cyclamens.

Fruit is a globose capsule 1–1.5 cm in diameter that splits open at maturity to release sticky seeds.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Cyclamen purpurascens* is distributed across the Alps from eastern France through Switzerland, Austria, and into Slovenia, Croatia, and northern Italy.

It also occurs in the Carpathian Mountains of Romania, Slovakia, and western Ukraine, with disjunct populations in southern Poland.

The species typically grows at elevations of 250–1,300 meters, making it the most lowland of the European alpine cyclamens.

Its preferred habitat is deciduous or mixed woodland, particularly beech and fir forests with deep humus-rich soils.

It often colonizes rocky outcrops, limestone crevices, and shaded stream banks within forest understories.

The plant requires high humidity and dappled shade, and is sensitive to both full sun and prolonged drought.

It is one of the few cyclamens that retains its leaves year-round, remaining evergreen in mild climates and semi-evergreen in colder regions.

## Cultivation

*C. purpurascens* is considered one of the easiest cyclamens for garden cultivation, given adequate shade and moisture-retentive soil.

It is hardy to USDA Zone 5 (−23 °C), making it the most cold-tolerant species in the genus alongside *C. coum*.

The plant prefers a neutral to slightly alkaline soil pH (6.5–7.5) rich in [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]], similar to woodland [[comparison-leaf-mold-composting-vs-hot-composting-method]].

Propagation is primarily by seed, which should be sown fresh as viability declines rapidly with storage.

Seeds germinate in 4–8 weeks at 15–18 °C, often requiring a period of cold stratification for consistent results.

The tuber should be planted with the top surface at or slightly above soil level, as [[coleman-leek-deep-planting-technique]] promotes rot.

Division of established tubers is possible but risky, as the species resents root disturbance and may take 1–2 years to recover flowering vigor.

Major pests include vine weevil larvae (*Otiorhynchus* spp.) attacking the tuber, and cyclamen mite (*Phytonemus pallidus*) damaging new growth.

## Bioactive Compounds

Like other Cyclamen species, *C. purpurascens* contains triterpene saponins as its primary bioactive constituents.

The tubers are particularly rich in cyclamin and cyclamiretin derivatives, which contribute to the plant's historical medicinal and toxicological significance.

Saponins from Cyclamen species have demonstrated hemolytic, cytotoxic, and antimicrobial activities in laboratory studies.

The leaves contain flavonoids, including [[quercetin]] and [[kaempferol]] glycosides, which contribute to antioxidant activity.
