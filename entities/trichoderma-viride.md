# Trichoderma viride

**Type:** Species (Fungus)
**Category:** Mycoparasitic Filamentous Ascomycete
**Also known as:** [[growing-gourmet-trichoderma-green-mold-management]]

## Overview
Trichoderma viride is a cosmopolitan soil-dwelling fungus renowned for
its mycoparasitic activity against a wide range of plant-pathogenic
fungi. It is the anamorph of Hypocrea rufa and belongs to the
Hypocreales order, a lineage that includes many biologically important
fungicolous species. T. viride is extensively used as a biocontrol agent
in agriculture and is also valued for its production of cellulolytic
enzymes, which have industrial applications. The source text documents
T. viride across multiple contexts: as a source of cellulase for
protoplast isolation, as a mycoparasite inducing defense responses in
[[aspergillus-niger]], as an antagonist of numerous plant pathogens, and
as a suppressor of [[dactylium-cobweb-mold-mushroom-pathogens]] in commercial cultivation.

## Key Details

### Morphology
- Colonies are fast-growing, spreading rapidly on standard
  media, with a characteristic green to yellow-green
  sporulation
- Conidiophores are repeatedly branched (pyramidal),
  bearing phialides at the tips of branches
- Phialides are flask-shaped (ampulliform), producing conidia
  in slimy green heads
- Conidia are subglobose to ovoid, smooth or finely
  roughened, approximately 3 to 5 micrometers in diameter
- Teleomorph (Hypocrea rufa) produces perithecia with
  cylindrical asci containing eight bicellular ascospores
- Chlamydospores may be produced, globose to terminal,
  smooth-walled

### Ecology and Habitat
- Common soil fungus found worldwide in diverse habitats
- Frequently isolated from forest soils, agricultural fields,
  compost, and decaying wood
- Opportunistic colonizer of various [[brassinosteroid-fungal-fruiting-body-morphology]] bodies
  and substrates
- Found as an endophyte in some plant tissues
- Tolerant of a wide range of environmental conditions and
  substrate pH levels

### Mycoparasitic Activity
- Efficient antagonist of numerous plant-pathogenic fungi
  including [[fusarium-verticillioides]], Cochliobolus carbonum,
  and Colletotrichum graminicola (Vakili 1985)
- Induces vacuolation in hyphae of Aspergillus niger when
  grown in [[fukuoka-rice-barley-dual-culture]] (Park and Robinson 1964)
- Successfully used as a biocontrol agent against Verticillium
  malthousei in [[history-of-commercial-mushroom-cultivation]] production (de Trogoff
  and Ricard 1976)
- Demonstrated antagonism toward wood-inhabiting hymenomycetes
  including shiitake ([[lentinus-edodes-entity-the-mushroom-cultivator]]) on log-woods
  (Hashioka and Komatsu 1964; Komatsu and Hashioka 1964)
- Has been investigated for production of gliotoxin and
  viridin (Webster and Lomas 1964)

### Cellulolytic Enzyme Production
- Source of Trichoderma viride cellulase, commercially
  available from BDH Laboratory Supplies (per source text)
- Used in protoplast isolation protocols for molecular
  studies of fungi
- Combined with Penicillium funiculosum cellulase and
  Aspergillus niger hemicellulase to prevent cell clumping
  during protoplast formation
- Plays a key role in [[gadd-fungal-cell-structure]] wall degradation for DNA
