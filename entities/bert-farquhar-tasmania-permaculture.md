## Bert Farquhar

## Description

Bert Farquhar is a permaculture practitioner and farmer from Wyambi, Tasmania, who developed and documented techniques for coastal pasture establishment on degraded lands. Mollison credits Farquhar with providing practical data on establishing productive pasture on difficult coastal sites where conventional ploughing approaches fail. Farquhar's method demonstrates the permaculture principle of working with natural processes rather than against them — using minimal tillage ([[sod-seeding]]), controlled burning, and strategic amendments to convert degraded rush-infested coastal land into productive pasture.

## Classification

- **Category**: Permaculture practitioner / Farmer
- **Location**: Wyambi, Tasmania, Australia
- **Specialty**: Coastal pasture establishment and rehabilitation
- **Contribution**: Practical sod-seeding methodology for acid coastal soils

## Key Facts

- Based at Wyambi, Tasmania — a coastal location with challenging soil conditions
- Developed a specific coastal pasture establishment method using sod seeding
- His approach addressed lands where round-rush (Juncus) proliferates when land is conventionally ploughed
- Demonstrated that minimal tillage prevents the spread and growth of rushes and soil acidification
- His method: slash brush and rushes, autumn burn, sod-seed clover at 0.7 kg/ha in the first month of winter
- Added approximately one ton of dolomite per year to correct [[soil-ph]] on copper and cobalt deficient coastal soils
- Showed that conventional ploughing on marshy coasts produces pH so low that uneconomical amounts of dolomite are needed
- Mollison specifically credits Farquhar's data and practical experience, indicating direct collaboration or communication

## Method Summary (Farquhar Technique)

1. Slash existing brush and rushes on degraded coastal pasture
2. Conduct an autumn burn of slashed material
3. Sod-seed clover at 0.7 kg/ha (minimal tillage — no ploughing)
4. Complete sowing in the first month of winter (earlier if ground was previously fallow)
5. Add approximately 1 ton/ha dolomite annually to correct pH
6. Copper and cobalt supplements are also needed on many coastal sites
7. Cattle will browse off remaining rush growth when clover is present

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Bert Farquhar's coastal pasture method connects to [[fungal-ecology]] and cultivation:

1. **Mycorrhizal preservation**: By avoiding ploughing, Farquhar's method preserves existing [[mycorrhizal-networks]] in the soil — ploughing severs hyphal connections and destroys fungal communities
2. **Clover-mycorrhizal partnerships**: Clover establishment via sod seeding builds AM (arbuscular mycorrhizal) fungal partnerships that improve nutrient uptake on deficient coastal soils
3. **Rush suppression and fungal communities**: Juncus-dominated degraded lands support different fungal communities than diverse pastures — conversion shifts the [[soil-fungal-ecology]]
4. **Dolomite and soil biology**: The addition of dolomite (calcium magnesium carbonate) moderates soil pH, creating conditions more favorable for beneficial fungal activity than the extremely acid conditions created by ploughing
5. **Controlled burn effects**: The autumn burn in Farquhar's method may stimulate certain fire-adapted fungal species while the ash provides mineral nutrients
6. **Coastal mycology**: Coastal lands transitioning from rush beds to diverse pasture undergo fungal community succession that could be monitored and guided for ecological restoration

## Broader Significance

Farquhar's work exemplifies several permaculture principles:
- Work with nature, not against it (minimal tillage instead of ploughing)
- The problem is the solution (rush-infested land becomes productive pasture)
- Use small and slow solutions (gradual pH correction with dolomite)
- Integrate rather than segregate (cattle browsing rushes while grazing clover)
