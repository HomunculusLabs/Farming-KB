## Overview

Norpsilocybin (4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine, 4-OH-DMT) is a naturally occurring [[1960s-counterculture-and-magic-mushrooms]], though its individual pharmacological activity remains less characterized than that of [[hplc-analysis-of-psilocybin-and-psilocin]].

## Chemical Properties

The molecular formula of norpsilocybin is C₁₀H₁₄N₂O, with a molecular weight of 178.23 g/mol. It contains an indole ring system with a hydroxyl group at the 4-position and a dimethylaminoethyl side chain.

The compound exists as a free base that is soluble in organic solvents (ethanol, methanol, chloroform) and sparingly soluble in water. It forms crystalline solids when isolated in pure form.

The IUPAC name is 3-[2-(dimethylamino)ethyl]-1H-indol-4-ol. The hydroxyl group at the 4-position of the indole ring is the key pharmacophore, shared with psilocin but positioned differently than in bufotenin (5-hydroxy).

Norpsilocybin is structurally distinct from psilocybin in that it lacks the 4-phosphoryloxy group. Where psilocybin is a prodrug that requires dephosphorylation to the active psilocin, norpsilocybin is active in its native form.

The compound is sensitive to oxidation and light, requiring storage under inert atmosphere at low temperature for stability. Melting point data is limited due to scarcity of isolated material.

## Pharmacology

Norpsilocybin acts as a serotonergic psychedelic, primarily through agonist activity at the 5-HT₂A receptor. The 4-hydroxy indole substitution pattern confers affinity for multiple [[tihkal-receptor-binding-serotonin]] data is limited compared to psilocin, but the dimethylaminoethyl side chain with the 4-hydroxy indole is expected to produce moderate-to-high affinity at 5-HT₂A, 5-HT₂C, and 5-HT₁A receptors.

The compound is presumed to undergo first-pass metabolism in the liver, similar to other oral tryptamines. Monoamine oxidase (MAO) likely contributes to oxidative deamination of the side chain.

### Comparison with Psilocin

Norpsilocybin and psilocin share the same molecular formula (C₁₀H₁₄N₂O) and molecular weight but are positional isomers. Psilocin has the hydroxyl at the 4-position of the indole, while the "norphosphoryl" designation indicates the absence of the phosphate ester found in psilocybin.

Despite being sometimes confused in the literature, norpsilocybin and psilocin are the same compound when properly named. The term "norpsilocybin" most accurately refers to N-demethylpsilocybin (4-hydroxy-N-methyltryptamine), though usage varies in published sources.

This nomenclatural ambiguity has led to confusion in the mycochemical literature. Contemporary analytical studies use precise LC-MS identification to distinguish between these closely related alkaloids.

## Natural Occurrence

Norpsilocybin has been detected in multiple *Psilocybe* species, typically at concentrations of 0.01–0.1% dry weight — significantly lower than psilocybin (0.3–1.5%) and psilocin (0.1–0.5%).

Notable species containing norpsilocybin include *P. cubensis*, *P. semilanceata*, *P. cyanescens*, *P. azurescens*, and *P. baeocystis*. The compound appears to be a universal minor constituent of psilocybin-producing fungi.

The biosynthetic pathway likely involves hydroxylation of tryptamine to 4-hydroxytryptamine, followed by methylation steps. Norpsilocybin may represent a biosynthetic intermediate or shunt metabolite in the pathway to psilocybin.

Analytical detection typically employs LC-MS/MS with multiple reaction monitoring (MRM), using norpsilocybin reference standards. HPLC with UV or fluorescence detection can also separate and quantify the compound.

## Effects and Dosage

Subjective effects of isolated norpsilocybin are poorly documented, as the compound is rarely available in pure form separate from other [[beta-caryophyllene-entourage-effects-cannabis]] remain possible.

The presence of norpsilocybin alongside the major alkaloids may contribute to the variation in subjective effects reported between different mushroom species and even between individual specimens of the same species.
