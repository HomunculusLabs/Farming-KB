# Salvia divinorum (Diviner's Sage)

## Overview

**Salvia divinorum** is a perennial herbaceous plant in the mint family (*Lamiaceae*) endemic to the cloud forests of the Sierra Mazateca in Oaxaca, Mexico. It is the only known natural source of **salvinorin A**, an extraordinarily potent **κ-opioid receptor (KOR) agonist** and the most powerful naturally occurring hallucinogen by weight. Unlike classical psychedelics (psilocybin, LSD, DMT), salvinorin A has no activity at serotonin receptors, representing a unique pharmacological class.

Traditionally used by Mazatec shamans in divination rituals, *S. divinorum* has become widely known in global drug culture for its intense but short-lived dissociative experiences. It remains legal or unscheduled in many jurisdictions, though regulatory scrutiny has increased.

### Etymology

The specific epithet *divinorum* means "of the diviners," referring to its traditional use by Mazatec curanderos in spiritual and healing ceremonies. Common names include Diviner's Sage, Ska María Pastora (Mazatec: "leaves of the Virgin Mary"), Sally-D, and Magic Mint.

## Morphology

*Salvia divinorum* is a large herbaceous perennial growing 0.5–2.5 meters tall. Stems are square (characteristic of Lamiaceae), hollow, and often reclining, rooting at nodes that contact soil. Leaves are ovate to broadly lanceolate, 10–30 cm long, with serrated margins and a velvety surface. They are dark green above, lighter below, and emit a mild aromatic scent when crushed.

Flowers are white to violet-blue, arranged in whorls on a terminal inflorescence up to 60 cm long. The corolla is bilabiate, approximately 3 cm long, typical of the *Salvia* genus. Remarkably, *S. divinorum* almost never sets viable seed under natural or cultivated conditions — reproduction is almost exclusively vegetative, which has led to extremely low genetic diversity among cultivated clones worldwide.

## Chemical Constituents

### Salvinorin A

Salvinorin A is a trans-neoclerodane diterpenoid and the principal psychoactive compound. It is not an alkaloid — it contains no nitrogen atoms, distinguishing it from virtually all other psychoactive natural products.

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| **Molecular formula** | C₂₃H₂₈O₈ |
| **Molecular weight** | 432.47 g/mol |
| **Active dose (smoked)** | 200–500 μg (0.2–0.5 mg) |
| **Active dose (sublingual)** | 500–2000 μg |
| **Potency** | Comparable to LSD on a weight basis; ~10× more potent than psilocybin |

### Other Salvinorins

- **Salvinorin B** — inactive at KOR; potential prodrug
- **Salvinorin C–F** — minor neoclerodane diterpenoids; weak or inactive
- **Salvinicins A and B** — additional diterpenoids with unknown pharmacology

## Pharmacology

### Mechanism of Action

Salvinorin A is a **highly selective κ-opioid receptor (KOR) agonist** with:
- **KOR Ki:** ~1 nM (extremely high affinity)
- **No significant binding** to μ-opioid, δ-opioid, 5-HT₂A, 5-HT₁A, CB₁, CB₂, NMDA, or dopamine receptors
- **No activity** at monoamine transporters (SERT, DAT, NET)

This pharmacological profile is unique among hallucinogens. KOR activation produces effects fundamentally different from 5-HT₂A-mediated psychedelics:

### Subjective Effects

- **Intense spatial disorientation** — loss of connection to physical environment
- **Immersive altered realities** — vivid alternate worlds or dimensions
- **Ego dissolution** — complete loss of self-awareness at higher doses
- **Synesthesia** — cross-modal sensory blending
- **Amnesia** — partial or complete amnesia for the experience is common
- **Physical effects** — sweating, dizziness, heaviness, uncontrollable laughter

The experience is remarkably short-lived: 5–15 minutes when smoked (vaporized), or 30–90 minutes when held sublingually as a quid. The intensity of effects is disproportionate to duration — a single inhalation can produce a fully immersive experience comparable to high-dose DMT or ketamine.

## Ethnobotanical History

The Mazatec people of Oaxaca have used *S. divinorum* for centuries, though its use is less prominent than that of morning glory seeds (*Turbina corymbosa*) or psilocybin mushrooms in their tradition. Traditional preparation involves:

- **Quid method:** Fresh leaves are rolled into a ball and chewed, with the bitter saliva held in the mouth to allow sublingual absorption of salvinorin A. This is the traditional Mazatec method and produces a gentler, longer experience.
- **Water infusion:** Leaves are steeped in cold or lukewarm water (salvinorin A degrades at high temperatures, making hot tea ineffective).

Western knowledge dates to 1938 when anthropologist Jean B. Johnson collected specimens in Sierra Mazateca, and to R. Gordon Wasson's 1962 publication. The active compound was not isolated until 1982 by Alfredo Ortega and colleagues, with the structure fully elucidated in 1984.

## Cultivation

*Salvia divinorum* is easy to cultivate vegetatively. It requires:

| Parameter | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Light** | Partial shade to dappled sunlight; avoids full sun |
| **Temperature** | 15–27°C (59–80°F); sensitive to frost |
| **Humidity** | High (60–80% RH); native to cloud forest understory |
| **Soil** | Rich, well-draining; slightly acidic to neutral (pH 5.5–7.0) |
| **Water** | Regular watering; does not tolerate drought or waterlogging |
| **Propagation** | Stem cuttings root readily in water or moist medium within 2–4 weeks |

The plant is sensitive to temperature extremes and low humidity. In temperate climates it is grown as a houseplant. All cultivated plants are clones (the species is effectively sterile), with the most common strains being "Wasson-Hofmann" and "Blosser."

## Conservation Status

Wild populations are limited to a small area of the Sierra Mazateca in Oaxaca, Mexico, at elevations of 300–1500 m. Deforestation threatens native stands. The species' near-sterility makes it particularly vulnerable, though it is widely propagated in cultivation worldwide.
