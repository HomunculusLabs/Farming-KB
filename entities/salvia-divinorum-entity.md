# Salvia divinorum

Salvia divinorum is a psychoactive plant in the mint family (Lamiaceae), native to the cloud forests of Oaxaca, Mexico. Known as "diviner's sage" or simply "salvia," it is the most potent naturally occurring psychedelic substance known, producing intense, short-lived altered states mediated by the kappa-opioid receptor agonist salvinorin A. Unlike classical psychedelics (LSD, psilocybin, DMT), salvinorin A has no activity at serotonin receptors, making its pharmacological profile unique. See [[salvia-divinorum-profile]] for detailed compound information and [[psychedelic-plant-sources]] for broader ethnobotanical context.

## Taxonomy and Description

- **Order:** Lamiales
- **Family:** Lamiaceae (mint family)
- **Genus:** Salvia
- **Common names:** Diviner's sage, ska Maria Pastora (leaves of Mary the Shepherdess), ska Pastora, salvia, Sally-D, magic mint
- **Unique status:** S. divinorum is one of the few Salvia species known to be psychoactive, and it has no known wild population — it exists only in cultivation, propagated vegetatively by indigenous Mazatec peoples for centuries

### Morphology
- **Growth form:** Perennial herbaceous to semi-woody plant, 50-150 cm tall in cultivation (taller in ideal conditions)
- **Stem:** Square (characteristic of Lamiaceae), hollow, green to purplish-green, often leaning or sprawling
- **Leaves:** Large, ovate to triangular, 10-30 cm long, with serrated margins. Dark green, slightly fuzzy on both surfaces. The leaves are the primary plant part used. Leaves have a characteristic slightly bitter, aromatic scent when crushed.
- **Flowers:** White to blue-violet, approximately 25-35 mm long, produced in whorls on tall racemes (flower spikes) up to 80 cm long. Flowers have a distinctive curved upper lip. Blooming occurs in autumn/winter in its native habitat.
- **Root:** Extensive root system; plants propagate readily from stem cuttings placed in water or moist soil.
- **Reproduction:** Rarely produces viable seed; almost exclusively propagated by stem cuttings. This asexual reproduction means that all cultivated S. divinorum plants are likely clones of a small number of original Mazatec varieties.

## Distribution and Habitat

- **Native range:** Sierra Mazateca in Oaxaca, Mexico, between approximately 1,000 and 2,000 meters elevation
- **Habitat:** Cloud forest and tropical montane forest edges. Prefers shaded, humid, protected locations along stream banks, ravines, and forest clearings.
- **Climate:** Cool, humid, foggy mountain conditions. Temperatures of 15-25°C, high humidity, frequent cloud cover.
- **Current distribution:** Cultivated worldwide as an ornamental and psychoactive plant. No confirmed wild populations; all known plants are derived from cultivated clones from the Mazatec region.
- **Altitude in native range:** 300-1,800 meters, most commonly 1,000-1,800 meters

## Chemistry

### Salvinorin A
The primary psychoactive compound in S. divinorum:

- **Chemical class:** Neoclerodane diterpene (not an alkaloid — it contains no nitrogen)
- **Content:** 0.08-0.45% of dry leaf weight (varies significantly)
- **Mechanism:** Potent and selective kappa-opioid receptor (KOR) agonist. This is unique among psychedelic compounds — classical psychedelics act primarily as 5-HT2A serotonin receptor agonists
- **Potency:** One of the most potent naturally occurring psychoactive substances known; active at doses of 200-500 micrograms when vaporized
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Rapidly absorbed through mucous membranes in the mouth when chewed as a quid; rapidly absorbed through lungs when smoked/vaporized. Short duration due to rapid metabolism and elimination.
- **Other salvinorins:** Salvinorin B, C, D, E, F, and others are present but are either inactive or significantly less potent than salvinorin A

### Unique Pharmacological Properties
- **No serotonin receptor activity:** Unlike LSD, psilocybin, DMT, and mescaline, salvinorin A has no affinity for serotonin (5-HT) receptors
- **Kappa-opioid selectivity:** Highly selective for KOR, with minimal activity at mu- or delta-opioid receptors
- **Non-nitrogenous:** Salvinorin A is a diterpene, not an alkaloid — it contains no nitrogen atom, making it structurally unique among major psychedelics
- **No MAOI interaction:** Salvinorin A is not affected by MAO inhibitors

## Traditional Mazatec Use

S. divinorum has been used for centuries by the Mazatec people of Oaxaca:

- **Divination:** The primary traditional use is for divination — the Mazatec name "ska Maria Pastora" refers to its role in facilitating communication with the Virgin Mary and spiritual entities. Curanderos use it to diagnose illness, find lost objects, and receive guidance.
- **Healing:** Used by curanderos (healers) in healing ceremonies for physical and spiritual ailments
- **Method of administration:** Fresh leaves are chewed as a quid (ball of leaves held in the mouth) for 15-30 minutes, allowing salvinorin A to be absorbed through the buccal mucosa. Large quantities of fresh leaves (20-80+) may be chewed in a session.
- **Setting:** Always used in quiet, dark, secluded settings, often at night, with a guide present
- **Subordinate role:** S. divinorum is generally considered less important than psilocybin mushrooms in Mazatec healing practice; mushrooms are preferred for major healing work
- **Infusion:** Less commonly, a water infusion of fresh or dried leaves is prepared and drunk, though this is less effective than the quid method

## Effects

Salvia divinorum produces effects qualitatively different from classical psychedelics:

### Smoked/Vaporized (most common modern method)
- **Onset:** 10-30 seconds
- **Duration:** 5-20 minutes (peak effects last 2-5 minutes)
- **Effects:** Intense dissociation, loss of physical coordination, shifting perceptions of reality, experiences of being pulled, twisted, or merged with objects, contact with perceived entities, visual distortions, altered sense of self and body, laughter, fear, confusion
- **Afterglow:** 30-60 minutes of residual effects and gradual return to baseline

### Chewed (traditional quid method)
- **Onset:** 10-15 minutes
- **Duration:** 30-60 minutes
- **Effects:** Gentler, more gradual onset with less intense peak effects. More contemplative and meditative quality compared to smoking.

### Characteristic Experiences
- **Reality dissolution:** Sense of merging with surroundings or losing contact with consensus reality
- **Spatial disorientation:** Experiences of being rotated, pulled, or displaced in space
- **Entity contact:** Some users report encounters with beings or presences
- **Ego dissolution:** Temporary loss of sense of self, sometimes experienced as becoming an object or being absorbed into a landscape
- **Amnesia:** Many users have difficulty recalling the peak of the experience

## Cultivation

S. divinorum is relatively easy to cultivate:

- **Propagation:** Exclusively from stem cuttings. Cuttings 10-20 cm long root readily in water (1-3 weeks) or moist potting soil.
- **Soil:** Rich, well-draining potting mix. Prefers slightly acidic to neutral pH.
- **Light:** Bright indirect light or partial shade. Avoid direct afternoon sun which can scorch leaves. Tolerates lower light than many plants.
- **Water:** Keep consistently moist but not waterlogged. Does not tolerate drought.
- **Humidity:** Prefers high humidity (50-80%+). Dry air causes leaf curling and browning at edges.
- **Temperature:** Prefers 15-25°C. Can tolerate brief exposure to 35°C but growth suffers. Cannot tolerate frost; below 5°C will cause damage.
- **Growth rate:** Fast in ideal conditions; plants can grow 60-100 cm in a single growing season.
- **Pests:** Relatively pest-resistant but susceptible to aphids, whiteflies, and spider mites in dry indoor conditions.
- **Fertilizer:** Benefits from balanced liquid fertilizer during the growing season.
- **Indoor cultivation:** Adapts well to indoor growing with adequate humidity (humidifier or humidity tent recommended).

## Legal Status

The legal status of S. divinorum varies significantly by country and jurisdiction:

- **United States:** Not federally scheduled, though many states have enacted their own bans (California, Florida, Illinois, and numerous others). Check local regulations.
- **Controlled internationally:** Listed in some national controlled substance schedules. Several European countries have banned it (Australia, Denmark, Germany, Italy, Sweden, among others).
- **Mexico:** Not scheduled at the federal level; traditional Mazatec use continues.
- **Brazil:** Not scheduled; legal to possess and cultivate.
