## GROW BIOINTENSIVE

## Description

GROW BIOINTENSIVE is a sustainable, closed-system food-raising method developed by Ecology Action and based on the biointensive approach originated by Alan Chadwick. The method emphasizes deep soil preparation, compost-based fertility, close plant spacing, synergistic crop combinations, carbon-efficient crops, calorie-efficient crops, open-pollinated seeds, and integrated whole-system farming. When properly used, the method can build soil up to 60 times faster than in nature while achieving 200-400% increases in caloric production per unit area.

## Classification

- **Type**: Agricultural method / farming system
- **Originator**: Alan Chadwick (biointensive approach)
- **Developer**: Ecology Action / John Jeavons (GROW BIOINTENSIVE trademark)
- **Trademark registered**: 1999 by Ecology Action
- **Setting**: Small-scale sustainable mini-farming

## Key Facts

- Deep soil preparation develops good soil structure, maintained for years with 2-inch surface cultivation
- Compost (humus) used for soil fertility and nutrients instead of chemical fertilizers
- Close plant spacing mimics natural plant communities rather than row-based agriculture
- Synergistic planting of crop combinations for mutual plant enhancement
- 60% of growing area in carbon-efficient seed and grain crops for compost and calories
- 30% of growing area in calorie-efficient root crops (potatoes, parsnips, garlic, burdock)
- Open-pollinated seeds preserve genetic diversity
- Can reduce water consumption 67-88% per unit of production
- Can reduce purchased fertilizer 50+% per unit of production
- Can reduce energy use 99% per unit of production
- Can increase caloric production 200-400% per unit area

## The Eight Components

The GROW BIOINTENSIVE system comprises eight integrated components that must all be used together: (1) deep soil preparation, (2) compost use, (3) close plant spacing, (4) synergistic planting, (5) carbon-efficient crops, (6) calorie-efficient crops, (7) open-pollinated seeds, and (8) whole-system integration. Jeavons emphasized that using only some elements without the whole system can rapidly deplete soil due to the high yields achieved.

## The 60/30/10 Model

The GROW BIOINTENSIVE area allocation model recommends 60% of growing area for carbon-efficient crops (grains and seeds producing both calories and compost material), 30% for calorie-efficient root crops, and 10% for other vegetables and special crops. This model ensures that the farming system produces both adequate nutrition and sufficient compost material to maintain soil fertility in a closed-loop system.

## Historical Development

The method originated with Alan Chadwick, who developed the biointensive approach combining French intensive gardening and biodynamic principles. Ecology Action, under John Jeavons, refined and systematized the approach beginning in 1971. Research from 1972-1978 demonstrated the feasibility of an economic mini-farm earning $5,000 on as little as 1/4 acre, and a complete balanced diet for one person from as little as 2,800 square feet. The method was trademarked as GROW BIOINTENSIVE in 1999.

## International Impact

The method has been adopted in numerous countries worldwide. By 1980, the Shri A.M.M. Murugappa Chettier Research Centre in India reported successful use. A biointensive project was established in China following the Third International Conference on Small-Scale and Intensive Food Production. U.S. Secretary of Agriculture Bob Bergland noted in 1981 that Ecology Action's work was "10 years ahead of its time."

## Sustainability Claims

When properly used, GROW BIOINTENSIVE claims to build soil up to 60 times faster than natural processes. University of California soil science tests showed an "unexpected accelerated rate of humified carbon buildup, a process that normally takes hundreds of years." The method's emphasis on closed-loop nutrient cycling means that external inputs are minimized, and soil fertility increases over time rather than depleting.

## Relevance to Cultivation

The GROW BIOINTENSIVE method has direct relevance to mycology through its emphasis on compost production and soil building. The large quantities of carbonaceous material produced by the 60% carbon crop allocation provide abundant compost substrate. The deep soil preparation and compost-based fertility approach creates soil conditions rich in organic matter and beneficial microorganisms, including fungal networks. The method's whole-system approach parallels the integrated ecological perspective of mycology.

## Comparison with Conventional Agriculture

The GROW BIOINTENSIVE method stands in stark contrast to conventional chemical agriculture. Where conventional farming depletes soil, uses large land areas, and depends on chemical inputs, GROW BIOINTENSIVE builds soil, uses minimal land, and relies on biological processes. The method's 99% reduction in energy use per unit of production is particularly significant in an era of energy constraint. The method's ability to produce complete nutrition on 2,800 square feet makes it relevant for urban and suburban food production.

## The Whole-System Principle

Jeavons emphasizes that GROW BIOINTENSIVE is a whole system whose components must all be used together for optimum effect. If only some elements are used without the whole system, the method's high yields can rapidly deplete the soil. This is because each component addresses a different aspect of soil and plant health: deep preparation addresses structure, compost addresses fertility, close spacing addresses water efficiency, and carbon crops address sustainability. Only when all elements work together does the system become truly sustainable.

## Distinction from "Biointensive"

The text distinguishes between "GROW BIOINTENSIVE" (the trademarked system with all eight components) and "Biointensive" (used for projects and programs before the 1999 trademark registration or not using all features). This distinction reflects Ecology Action's concern that partial adoption of the method could lead to soil depletion and give the approach a bad reputation. The trademark serves as a quality assurance mechanism ensuring that anyone using the GROW BIOINTENSIVE name is committed to the complete system.
