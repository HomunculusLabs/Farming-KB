## Description

Vermicompost (also called worm castings or vermicast) is the product of processing organic materials through earthworms. Unlike thermal composting, vermicomposting does not involve heat -- the worms themselves (and the bacteria inside them) digest the materials and create nutrient-rich castings. Vermicompost is almost always bacterially dominated, as few if any fungi are involved in worm digestion. The resulting castings are coated with polysaccharides, carbohydrates, and simple proteins that make them ideal for supporting good bacterial populations. Vermicompost is particularly valued for use around plants that prefer bacterial dominance: annuals, vegetables, and lawns.

## Classification

- **Type:** Soil amendment / compost product
- **Production method:** Worm digestion (no thermal phase)
- **Microbial character:** Bacterially dominated
- **Key organisms:** Earthworms (Eisenia fetida, Eisenia andrei, and other composting worms), associated bacteria
- **Output:** Worm castings (vermicast)

## Key Facts

- **Bacterial dominance:** Vermicompost is almost always bacterially dominated. The worm digestive process favors bacterial proliferation while excluding fungi. This makes vermicompost ideal for plants that prefer ammonium nitrogen (produced by bacterial-dominated systems) -- primarily annuals, vegetables, herbs, and grasses.

- **No heat involved:** Unlike traditional composting, vermicomposting does not generate heat because high temperatures would kill the worms. Decomposition is entirely biological, driven by the worms and their gut bacteria.

- **Castings composition:** Fresh worm castings are coated with polysaccharides, carbohydrates, and simple proteins. This coating creates an ideal microenvironment for bacterial growth, making vermicompost an excellent bacterial inoculant for soils.

- **Worm species:** Special composting earthworms (typically red wigglers, Eisenia fetida) are used for vermicomposting. These are surface-dwelling worms adapted to consuming organic matter, unlike the deep-burrowing earthworms found in garden soil.

- **Nutrient density:** Vermicompost typically has higher concentrations of plant-available nutrients than thermal compost because the worm gut process is highly efficient at mineralizing nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium from organic matter.

## Vermicomposting Process

**Starting materials:**
- Food wastes (no fats or meats)
- Paper and cardboard
- Leaves and green grass
- Same materials as a normal compost pile
- Brown materials must be shredded or broken up for worms to ingest quickly
- Weeds should be thermally composted first to prevent seedling growth in the worm bin

**Setup:**
- Simple wooden or plastic box (bin system)
- Can be kept indoors or outdoors
- Outdoor placement encourages arthropod and insect activity that helps break down matter for the worms
- Microarthropods in starting materials help physically break down matter

**Process:**
1. Worms consume organic material
2. Bacteria inside the worms digest the material
3. Castings pass through the worm and are excreted
4. Castings are coated with polysaccharide mucus and enriched with minerals
5. Finished vermicompost has a bacterial dominance

## Comparison: Vermicompost vs. Thermal Compost

| Characteristic | Vermicompost | Thermal Compost |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature | Ambient (no heating) | 68-150F (20-65C) |
| Dominant organisms | Bacteria | Bacteria and/or fungi |
| Fungi present | Few or none | Can be fungal-dominated |
| Processing time | 2-6 months | 3 weeks to 1 year |
| Pathogen kill | Limited | Yes (thermophilic phase) |
| Best for | Annuals, vegetables, lawns | Depends on fungal/bacterial ratio |
| Weed seed kill | No | Yes (at 150F/65C) |
| Nutrient density | Very high | Moderate to high |
| Microbial diversity | Bacterial diversity | Full food web diversity |

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Bacterial inoculant for annuals:** Vermicompost is the ideal soil amendment for vegetable gardens, annual flower beds, and lawns because its bacterial dominance provides the nitrogen form (ammonium) preferred by these plants.

- **Not ideal for fungal crops:** Because vermicompost lacks significant fungal populations, it is less suitable as the primary amendment for trees, shrubs, perennials, and mushroom cultivation, which benefit from fungal dominance.

- **Nutrient supplement in mushroom cultivation:** While not a primary substrate, vermicompost can be used as a nutrient supplement in some mushroom growing systems where bacterial activity is desired (e.g., as a component of casing layers for Agaricus).

- **Compost tea base:** Vermicompost can be used as the compost source for making actively aerated compost tea (AACT), producing a bacterially dominated tea for foliar spraying or soil drenching.

- **Seed starting mix component:** The fine texture, high nutrient content, and bacterial dominance of vermicompost make it an excellent component of seed starting mixes, providing gentle nutrition and disease suppression for seedlings.

- **Worm-casting tea:** Castings steeped in aerated water produce a nutrient-rich, microbially active liquid fertilizer that can be applied as a soil drench or foliar spray.

## Soil Food Web Context

In the soil food web gardening framework:

- Vermicompost excels at **inoculating soils with bacteria** and providing bacterial foods
- It complements **fungal compost** (made with brown materials) for a complete soil food web approach
- Worm castings attract **protozoa and nematodes** (which eat bacteria), completing the nutrient cycling chain
- The polysaccharide coating on castings contributes to **soil aggregate formation**, improving soil structure

## Practical Considerations

- **Bin size:** Small bins (simple wooden or plastic boxes) are sufficient for home-scale vermicomposting
- **Feeding rate:** Worms consume approximately their body weight in food daily under optimal conditions
- **Moisture:**Bins should be kept moist but not waterlogged
- **Harvesting:** Castings can be harvested by moving finished material to one side and adding fresh food to the other; worms migrate toward the food
- **Outdoor bins:** Placing bins outdoors encourages beneficial arthropod colonization
