## Description

Microarthropods are small arthropod organisms (typically less than 2mm) that inhabit soil and leaf litter, including springtails (Collembola), mites (Acari), and other tiny invertebrates. They are higher-level predators in the soil food web, feeding on fungi, bacteria, nematodes, and each other. Their activity creates soil pores, distributes fungal spores and bacteria, and contributes to aggregate formation. Microarthropods are addressed in Step Twelve of the soil foodweb restoration protocol.

## Classification

- **Phylum**: Arthropoda
- **Size Class**: Microfauna (< 2mm)
- **Major Groups**: Collembola (springtails), Acari (mites), Protura, Diplura
- **Ecological Role**: Higher-level predators, decomposers, soil engineers
- **Trophic Level**: Secondary and tertiary consumers

## Key Facts

- Microarthropods include springtails (Collembola) and soil mites (Oribatida, Mesostigmata, Prostigmata) as the most abundant and ecologically important groups.
- They create larger soil pores through their movement and feeding activity, contributing to improved soil structure and water infiltration.
- Microarthropods distribute fungal spores and bacterial colonies throughout the soil profile, aiding colonization of new substrates.
- They are higher-level predators that consume nematodes, protozoa, and other small organisms, regulating populations throughout the food web.
- In tilled agricultural systems, microarthropod populations are often severely depleted due to habitat destruction.
- At least a few microarthropods per square foot are considered necessary for good soil health.
- Microarthropods often enter compost tea systems when worm compost is used as the inoculum source.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- Microarthropods are evaluated in Step Twelve of the foodweb restoration protocol — if adequate numbers cannot be observed in handfuls of soil, inoculation from worm compost or forest soil is recommended.
- [[contamination-prevention-in-mushroom-cultivation]], microarthropods can be both beneficial (distributing spores, decomposing spent substrate) and problematic (certain springtails and mites feed on mycelium or fruiting bodies).
- Springtails (Collembola) are known to feed on mushroom mycelium and can be pests in commercial mushroom production facilities.
- Predatory mites (Mesostigmata) can help control nuisance mite species and fungus gnat larvae in [[mushroom-growing-environments]].
- Forest soil is a rich source of microarthropod diversity for inoculating depleted agricultural soils.
- The presence of microarthropods indicates a mature, complex soil foodweb with multiple trophic levels functioning.
- Monitoring microarthropod populations provides insight into overall soil ecosystem maturity and health.
