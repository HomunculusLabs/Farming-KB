## Overview

**Gossypium hirsutum**, known as upland cotton or Mexican cotton, is the most widely planted cotton species in the world. It supplies approximately 90% of global cotton fiber production and is cultivated on every inhabited continent.

The species belongs to the tetraploid (2n = 4x = 52) New World cotton lineage, resulting from an ancient hybridization between an A-genome and D-genome diploid progenitor roughly 1–2 million years ago.

Gossypium hirsutum is native to [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-caribbean-central-america]] and southern Mexico. It was domesticated independently in the Yucatán Peninsula and the Gulf Coast region of present-day United States.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Kingdom:** Plantae — **Order:** Malvales — **Family:** Malvaceae

**Genus:** Gossypium — **Species:** G. hirsutum L.

Gossypium hirsutum was first described by Carl Linnaeus in 1753 in *Species Plantarum*.

Several subspecies and varieties are recognized, including the wild *G. hirsutum* var. *yucatanense* and the domesticated var. *latifolium*.

The plant is an upright to spreading perennial shrub grown as an annual in commercial production. Heights range from 0.6 to 1.5 meters depending on cultivar and growing conditions.

Leaves are broad, palmately lobed with 3–5 lobes, and 10–15 cm across. The upper surface is glabrous to sparsely pubescent, while the lower surface is typically more hairy.

Flowers are cream to yellow with a dark red or purple spot at the base of each petal. They emerge singly from leaf axils on pedicels 1–3 cm long.

The fruit is a 3–5 locular capsule (boll) that dehisces at maturity to release seeds covered in long white to off-white lint fibers. Fiber length (staple) ranges from 20 to 30 mm in upland cultivars.

Seeds are ovoid, approximately 8–10 mm long, and bear both long lint fibers (epidermal trichomes) and shorter fuzzy fibers (fuzz or linters).

## Distribution and Habitat

Gossypium hirsutum is native to tropical and subtropical regions of Central America, from [[psychoactive-cacti-of-central-mexico]] to northern Colombia. Wild populations persist in coastal dunes and dry thorn scrub of the Yucatán Peninsula.

Commercial cultivation extends between approximately 37°N and 32°S latitude. Major producing countries include China, India, the United States, Pakistan, and Brazil.

The species requires a frost-free [[extending-growing-season]] of 150–200 days with accumulated growing degree days above 15°C of 1800–2200. Annual rainfall requirements are 500–1200 mm, with adequate moisture most critical during flowering and boll development.

Optimal soils are deep, well-drained loams to clay loams with pH 5.5–8.0. Gossypium hirsutum is moderately tolerant of salinity but sensitive to waterlogging.

## Cultivation

Upland cotton is typically direct-seeded at rates of 8–15 plants per meter of row, with rows spaced 75–100 cm apart. Planting dates vary by latitude from February in the tropics to May in temperate zones.

Nitrogen fertilization rates range from 60–120 kg/ha depending on soil fertility and expected yield. Phosphorus and potassium applications are based on soil testing, typically 30–60 kg/ha each.

Irrigation is critical in arid production regions. Both furrow and center-pivot sprinkler systems are used, with total seasonal water requirements of 600–1000 mm.

[[query-what-is-biofumigation-and-how-does-it-work]] is a major production challenge. Key arthropod pests include the boll weevil (Anthonomus grandis), pink bollworm (Pectinophora gossypiella), and various aphid and whitefly species.

Major diseases include bacterial blight (Xanthomonas citri pv. malvacearum), [[query-how-do-i-identify-prevent-and-treat-fusarium-wilt]] ([[fusarium-oxysporum]] f. sp. vasinfectum), and Verticillium wilt. Resistant cultivars and crop rotation are primary management tools.

Harvest occurs 150–180 days after planting when 60–70% of bolls are open. Mechanical pickers and strippers are used in most production systems, with seed cotton ginned to separate fiber from seed.

Average lint yields range from 600–2500 kg/ha depending on region, cultivar, and management. World average lint yield is approximately 770 kg/ha.

## Chemistry and Uses

Cotton fiber is approximately 88–96% cellulose, with minor components including wax, pectin, protein, and natural pigments. The cellulose is highly crystalline, contributing to the fiber's strength and durability.

Gossypol, a toxic polyphenolic sesquiterpene, occurs in pigment glands throughout the plant but is concentrated in cottonseed. Gossypol is hepatotoxic and cardiotoxic to non-ruminant animals, limiting raw seed use in feed.

Cottonseed oil, extracted from delinted seeds, is rich in polyunsaturated fatty acids (primarily linoleic acid, ~55%). After refining to remove gossypol, it is used in cooking, salad dressings, and food processing.

Whole cottonseed and cottonseed meal (after oil extraction) are valuable livestock feeds. Ruminants can tolerate residual gossypol due to rumen microbial detoxification.

Linter cellulose is used in manufacturing cellulose derivatives (nitrocellulose, cellulose acetate), regenerated fibers (rayon), and high-purity cellulose products for pharmaceuticals and explosives.

Organic cotton production has expanded significantly, reaching approximately 1% of global production. Certification programs (GOTS, OCS) verify organic and sustainable practices throughout the supply chain.

## Ecological and Economic Role

Cotton is one of the world's most important fiber crops, with global production exceeding 25 million metric tons of lint annually. The cotton sector supports hundreds of millions of livelihoods across the production chain.
