## Overview

Campanula takesimana, commonly known as the Korean bellflower or Takeshima bellflower, is a rhizomatous herbaceous perennial in the family Campanulaceae.

It is endemic to the Korean Peninsula, where it grows on rocky slopes, forest edges, and coastal cliffs.

The species is valued in horticulture for its nodding, bell-shaped white flowers with pale pink veining, which bloom prolifically from mid-summer to early autumn. Its vigorous spreading habit makes it effective as a ground cover in partially shaded gardens.

Campanula takesimana was first described from material collected on Ulleungdo (Takeshima) Island, a volcanic island in the Sea of Japan.

It has gained popularity among gardeners in [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]] for its adaptability and long bloom period.

The species was described by Takenoshin Nakai in 1911 from specimens collected on Ulleungdo Island, South Korea. The specific epithet "takesimana" refers to Takeshima, the Japanese name for Ulleungdo.

Campanula takesimana belongs to the [[campanula-isophylla]] group, a cluster of East Asian bellflowers with nodding flowers.

It is closely related to [[campanula-punctata]], which shares a similar flower morphology but differs in coloration and leaf shape. While C. punctata has spotted pink-purple flowers, C. takesimana bears predominantly white flowers with subtle pink veining.

A white-flowered cultivar, C. takesimana 'Elizabeth', is widely cultivated and sometimes confused with the wild-type species in the nursery trade.

## Morphology

Campanula takesimana produces erect to ascending flowering stems reaching 30–60 cm in height.

The stems arise from a network of slender, creeping rhizomes that enable vigorous vegetative spread. These rhizomes can extend 15–30 cm per [[extending-growing-season]] under favorable conditions.

Basal leaves are cordate to reniform, 3–7 cm long, with crenate to doubly crenate margins. Leaf surfaces are sparsely pubescent with soft, appressed hairs.

Petioles are long, 5–15 cm in length, and slightly winged near the base. Stem leaves are smaller, ovate, and sessile or short-petiolate.

Flowers are nodding, broadly campanulate, 3–5 cm long, and predominantly white with delicate pink or lavender veining along the corolla lobes. Each flower has five lobes that reflex slightly at the tips.

The calyx lobes are linear-lanceolate and about one-third the length of the corolla. The style extends slightly beyond the corolla at anthesis.

The inflorescence is a terminal raceme or lax panicle bearing 5–15 flowers per stem. Blooming occurs from July through September in its native range.

The fruit is a nodding, ovoid capsule 8–12 mm long that dehisces by three basal pores. It releases numerous small brown seeds approximately 1 mm in length.

## Distribution and Habitat

Campanula takesimana is endemic to the Korean Peninsula and the adjacent volcanic island of Ulleungdo.

Its core populations occur in South Korea, particularly in montane regions of Gangwon Province and the Taebaek Mountains. Smaller populations exist in North Korea, though these are poorly documented.

The species inhabits forest margins, rocky outcrops, shaded cliff faces, and moist humus-rich soils at elevations of 100–1,500 m. It prefers partial shade and cool, humid microclimates.

On Ulleungdo, C. takesimana grows on north-facing volcanic slopes in association with Acer palmatum, Cornus controversa, and various fern species. These populations represent the most robust and genetically diverse stands.

Populations on the Korean mainland tend to occupy more disturbed sites including roadside banks and cleared forest edges. The species colonizes readily after disturbance due to its rhizomatous growth.

The species has been introduced to cultivation in Japan, Europe, and [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]], where it naturalizes readily in suitable [[fukuoka-natural-farming-temperate-climates]].

## Ecology and Conservation

Campanula takesimana flowers are pollinated primarily by bumblebees (Bombus spp.) and [[comparison-honey-bees-vs-native-solitary-bees]]. The nodding flower orientation protects pollen from rain while providing a landing platform for pollinators.

The species serves as a host plant for several oligophagic insects in its native range, including aphids specific to Campanula species.

On Ulleungdo, the species contributes to the unique island flora that includes numerous endemic taxa. The island has approximately 40 endemic plant species, making it a significant biodiversity hotspot.
