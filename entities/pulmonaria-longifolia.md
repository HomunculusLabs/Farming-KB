## Overview

Pulmonaria longifolia, the narrow-leaved lungwort, is a herbaceous perennial in the family Boraginaceae. Native to western Europe, it is valued as a shade garden ornamental for its early spring flowers and spotted foliage.

The plant produces nodding, funnel-shaped flowers that open pink and mature to blue, a color change driven by pH shifts in the corolla. Flowers appear in March through May, providing essential early nectar for emerging bumblebee queens.

## Ecological Role

The early spring flowers provide critical nectar and pollen for emerging bumblebee queens. Hairy-footed flower bees (Anthophora plumipes) are particularly frequent visitors.

The color change from pink to blue acts as a signal to pollinators, indicating which flowers have been recently opened and contain fresh nectar. This increases pollination efficiency.

Research has shown that blue-phase flowers contain less nectar than pink-phase flowers on the same plant. Bees learn to preferentially visit pink flowers, improving pollen transfer between plants.

The dense foliage provides ground cover that suppresses weed establishment in woodland gardens. Decaying leaves contribute organic matter to the soil surface layer.

Several species of leaf-mining insects feed on Pulmonaria foliage in the native range. The larvae of the moth Acrocercops brongniardella mine the leaves, creating characteristic blotch mines.
