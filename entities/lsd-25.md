# LSD-25 (Lysergic Acid Diethylamide)

## Description

LSD-25, or [[lysergic-acid-diethylamide]], is a powerful semi-synthetic psychedelic substance of the ergoline family. It is one of the most potent psychoactive compounds known, active at microgram-level doses. In [[timothy-leary]]'s *The [[leary-psychedelic-experience-bardo-mapping]]*, LSD-25 is treated as the primary chemical vehicle for inducing the Tibetan Book of the Dead-style transcendental states that form the core of the text's framework.

Originally synthesized by Swiss chemist [[albert-hofmann]] at the Sandoz Laboratories in Basel, Switzerland in 1938, its psychoactive properties were not discovered until April 19, 1943, when Hofmann accidentally absorbed a small quantity through his skin. This landmark discovery inaugurated the modern era of psychedelic research and exploration.

## Classification

- **Chemical class:** Ergoline (lysergamide) derivative
- **Pharmacological class:** Serotonin 5-HT2A receptor agonist (classical psychedelic / hallucinogen)
- **Legal status ([[ghouled-field-guide-historical-context]]):** Initially unregulated; later classified as Schedule I in the United States and similarly controlled worldwide
- **Type:** Semi-synthetic indole alkaloid

## Dosage Information (per Leary)

The Psychedelic Experience outlines specific dosage ranges calibrated to the intended depth of experience:

| Level | Dosage | Purpose |
|-------|--------|---------|
| Transcendental / solo high-dose | 200–500 mcg | Full ego-loss experience; deep [[mckenna-consciousness-expansion-and-drug-war]] |
| Experienced / group session | 100–200 mcg | Group work with experienced voyagers |
| Initial / exploratory | Lower ranges | First-time or cautious exploration |

These dosages assume pure, pharmaceutical-grade LSD-25 as was available from Sandoz during the period of Leary's research.

## Pharmacokinetics

- **[[mushroom-taste-route-of-administration-and-consumption-barriers]]:** Oral (sublingual absorption also effective)
- **Onset:** 20–30 minutes after ingestion
- **Peak effects:** 2–6 hours post-ingestion
- **Total duration:** 8–10 hours
- **After-effects:** Possible residual stimulation or introspection for several additional hours

## Key Facts

1. LSD-25 is active at extraordinarily low doses — measured in micrograms (millionths of a gram).
2. The compound acts primarily as an agonist at serotonin 5-HT2A receptors, though it interacts with numerous other receptor subtypes.
3. Hofmann's intentional self-experiment on April 19, 1943 (Bicycle Day) confirmed the compound's profound psychoactive effects at doses as low as 250 micrograms.
4. Sandoz distributed LSD under the trade name Delysid for psychiatric and research purposes through the 1950s and early 1960s.
5. The CIA's Project MKUltra investigated LSD as a potential tool for mind control and interrogation during the Cold War era.
6. Tolerance develops rapidly but also dissipates quickly — within 3–4 days of abstinence, sensitivity returns.

## Relevance to Psychedelic Studies

In *The Psychedelic Experience*, Leary and his colleagues ([[richard-alpert]] and Ralph Metzner) present LSD-25 as a sacramental tool for consciousness exploration. Drawing on the *Bardo Thodol* (Tibetan Book of the Dead), they map the LSD experience onto a sequence of psychedelic states or "bardos":

- **First Bardo:** The period of ego-loss or "clear light" experienced at peak onset.
- **Second Bardo:** The extended phase of hallucinatory and emotional phenomena as the ego attempts to reconstitute itself.
- **Third Bardo:** The return to ordinary waking consciousness, or "game-reality."

LSD-25 is treated not as a recreational drug but as a powerful psychopharmacological agent capable of dissolving the ego-structure and revealing deeper layers of consciousness. The text emphasizes the critical importance of **set** (mindset, intention) and **setting** (physical and social environment) in determining the quality and outcome of the experience.

The guide or "trip sitter" is instructed to help the voyager remain calm during ego-dissolution, avoiding the imposition of external stimuli or reassurances that would pull the subject back into ordinary consciousness prematurely. The goal is to allow full passage through the bardos toward liberation or insight.

## Historical Significance

LSD-25's discovery and subsequent popularization by figures including Leary, Alpert, Ken Kesey, and Aldous Huxley catalyzed the counterculture movement of the 1960s. Its prohibition in the late 1960s drove research underground for decades. Since the 2010s, a renaissance in psychedelic research has reopened clinical investigation of LSD for conditions including depression, anxiety, PTSD, and end-of-life distress.

## Safety Considerations

- Acute psychological distress (panic, paranoia) is the primary risk, particularly in uncontrolled settings.
- No established physical toxicity at psychoactive doses; no evidence of organ damage from typical use.
- May precipitate latent psychiatric conditions in vulnerable individuals.
- The Psychedelic Experience emphasizes careful preparation, trained guidance, and controlled environment to minimize adverse outcomes.

## Cultural Impact

The influence of LSD-25 on Western culture cannot be overstated. From the psychiatric research of the 1950s to the counterculture explosion of the 1960s, the compound served as both a scientific tool and a cultural catalyst. Its role in shaping music, art, literature, and spiritual practice continues to reverberate through contemporary society. Modern clinical trials at institutions including Johns Hopkins, Imperial College London, and the University of Zurich have renewed scientific interest in LSD's [[metzner-ayahuasca-therapeutic-potential]].

## Contraindications

- Individuals with personal or family history of schizophrenia or psychotic disorders should avoid LSD-25.
- Concurrent use with lithium may produce seizures.
- SSRI antidepressants may diminish LSD's effects due to [[serotonin-receptor-interference-psychedelic-mechanism]] downregulation.
- MAO inhibitors may potentiate effects unpredictably.
