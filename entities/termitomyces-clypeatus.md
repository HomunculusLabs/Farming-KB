T. clypeatus is one of the most widely consumed wild [[bloomfield-mushroom-formation-fruiting-body-development-cultivation]] originated within the termite comb.

Spores are smooth, ellipsoidal, 7–9 × 4.5–6 μm, hyaline, non-amyloid, with a prominent germ pore.

## Distribution and Habitat

T. clypeatus occurs in tropical and subtropical regions of Africa (Senegal, Ghana, Nigeria, Cameroon, Tanzania), and Asia (India, Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia).

The species is exclusively found in association with fungus-growing termites, primarily *Macrotermes bellicosus* and *M. subhyalinus* in Africa, and *Odontotermes* spp. in Asia.

Fruiting occurs from the termite combs, typically emerging through the mound surface during the rainy season after heavy rainfall.

The fungus is cultivated by termites within the mound on a substrate of partially digested plant material called the fungus comb.

## Termite Symbiosis

The mutualistic relationship between Termitomyces and fungus-growing termites is one of the most refined examples of insect-fungus agriculture.
