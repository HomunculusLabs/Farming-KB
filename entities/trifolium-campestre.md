The species is a common component of dry grasslands, agricultural fields, roadsides, and other disturbed habitats throughout its range.

Its small yellow flower heads turn brown and resemble dried hops after flowering, giving rise to the common name hop trefoil.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Trifolium campestre was described by Christian Friedrich Schreber in 1771 and is placed within the section Lotoidea of the genus Trifolium.

The specific epithet campestre means "of the fields," reflecting its common occurrence in agricultural and ruderal landscapes.

It is an annual or sometimes biennial herb growing 10 to 40 centimeters tall with procumbent to ascending stems.

The trifoliate leaves bear obovate to obcordate leaflets 5 to 15 millimeters long, each with faintly toothed margins and a pale chevron marking.

Flower heads are globose, 7 to 12 millimeters in diameter, composed of 20 to 40 small yellow florets arranged in a dense raceme.

After flowering, the heads turn brown and papery, with the persistent calyx giving the mature inflorescence a hop-like appearance.

Each flower produces a single small legume containing one seed, with seeds dispersed by animals, wind, and water.

Roots form effective nitrogen-fixing nodules in association with Rhizobium trifolii and related rhizobial strains.
