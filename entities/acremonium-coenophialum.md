# Acremonium coenophialum

## Description

Acremonium coenophialum (now reclassified as Epichloe coenophiala) is a fungal
endophyte systemically associated with tall fescue (Festuca arundinacea, now
Schedonorus arundinaceus). Along with Acremonium lolii in ryegrass, A.
coenophialum is one of the two most economically important grass endophytes
worldwide. It produces ergot alkaloids (particularly ergovaline) that cause
fescue toxicosis in grazing livestock, while simultaneously protecting host
plants from insect herbivory through multiple alkaloid classes.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Eukaryota
- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Sordariomycetes
- **Order:** Hypocreales
- **Family:** Clavicipitaceae
- **Genus:** Acremonium (now Epichloe)
- **Species:** A. coenophialum (E. coenophiala)

## Key Facts

- **Fescue toxicosis:** Produces ergovaline and other ergot alkaloids that cause
  fescue toxicosis in cattle, a syndrome including reduced weight gain, lowered
  milk production, elevated body temperature, rough hair coat, and reproductive
  problems. Economic losses in the US cattle industry exceed $1 billion annually.
- **Insect resistance:** Prestidge and Ball (1997) documented adverse effects of
  A. coenophialum-infected tall fescue on 12 beetle species, 2 fly species, 16
  Hemiptera/Homoptera species, and 8 lepidopteran larvae species.
- **Anti-herbivore spectrum:** Produces multiple alkaloid classes including
  ergot alkaloids, loline alkaloids, and peramine, providing broad-spectrum
  defense against insects and mammalian herbivores.
- **CO2 response stability:** Marks and Lincoln (1996) showed that the
  protective effect of A. coenophialum against Spodoptera frugiperda remained
  stable under elevated CO2 conditions, suggesting that endophyte benefits will
  persist under climate change scenarios.
- **Vertical transmission:** Seed-borne transmission ensures all progeny plants
  inherit the endophyte (Wilson, 1993), maintaining the association across
  generations.
- **Widespread pasture grass:** Tall fescue infects approximately 14 million
  hectares in the United States alone, making A. coenophialum one of the most
  abundant fungal endophytes in managed ecosystems globally.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Novel endophyte breeding:** US breeding programs have developed Jesup MaxQ
  and similar tall fescue cultivars containing non-toxic endophyte strains that
  retain insect resistance but eliminate ergovaline production.
- **Pasture management:** Understanding A. coenophialum ecology enables grazing
  management strategies that minimize livestock exposure to toxic alkaloids while
  maintaining the pasture productivity benefits of endophyte infection.
- **Seed industry:** Endophyte viability in commercial tall fescue seed is a
  major quality concern, as the fungus dies under improper storage conditions,
  converting valuable infected seed to less valuable endophyte-free seed.
- **Ecosystem management:** A. coenophialum-infected tall fescue has escaped
  cultivation and invaded natural grasslands, where it reduces plant diversity
  and alters soil ecosystem processes through allelopathy and competitive
  dominance.

## Ecological Interactions

- Systemically colonizes tall fescue (Festuca arundinacea) tissues without
  causing visible symptoms.
- Produces ergovaline, lolines, and peramine, affecting herbivores from insects
  to cattle.
- Reduces feeding, oviposition, and survival of diverse insect herbivores
  including S. frugiperda, Rhopalosiphum padi, and Blissus leucopterus.
- Competes with plant-pathogenic fungi on leaf surfaces.
- Seed-transmitted, creating stable multi-generational associations with host
  grass populations.
