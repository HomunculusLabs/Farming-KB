## Lakeland Plastics Boil Bag

## Description

The Lakeland Plastics Boil Bag is a kitchen roasting bag manufactured by Lakeland Plastics (Windermere, Cumbria, UK) that D.H. Jarrold identified as suitable for mushroom cultivation. These bags are designed to withstand pressure cooker and microwave temperatures without melting, making them ideal as low-cost, disposable substrate containers for amateur mushroom cultivation. Sold in packs of 50 at approximately 2.50 GBP, they represent one of the most affordable cultivation container options available.

## Physical Properties

The bags are made from a heat-resistant polymer film that maintains structural integrity at the temperatures encountered in a standard pressure cooker (approximately 121 degrees Celsius at 15 psi). The bag dimensions (20x30cm) provide sufficient volume for approximately one generous handful of straw substrate when firmly packed, with room left at the top for the filter can insertion. The plastic is thin enough to be penetrated by a syringe needle for inoculation while remaining thick enough to maintain a seal after needle removal.

## Role in the Boil-A-Bag Technique

In Jarrold"s Boil-A-Bag technique, these bags serve as the primary cultivation container. The workflow involves packing the bags with prepared straw substrate, fitting a filter can into the top, securing with masking tape, sterilizing in a pressure cooker, inoculating through the bag wall or filter can, allowing colonization in a warm location, and finally transferring to a humidity chamber for fruiting. After fruiting, the bags can be disposed of, eliminating cleaning and re-sterilization requirements.

## Advantages for Amateur Cultivation

The boil bags offer several advantages for amateur cultivators: extremely low cost per unit, no need for specialized mushroom cultivation supplies, disposability eliminates contamination carryover, transparency allows visual monitoring of mycelial colonization, and the pliable material conforms to the shape of the substrate for efficient use of space. Being available from a mainstream kitchen supplier also means they can be purchased without attracting attention.

## Sterilization Compatibility

Jarrold confirmed that the bags can be sterilized at 15 psi for 60 minutes in a standard pressure cooker without melting or failing. This is a critical requirement, as lower-quality plastic bags would melt or develop holes during sterilization, compromising the sterile environment needed for successful mushroom cultivation. The ability to withstand pressure cooking temperatures makes these bags a viable alternative to more expensive autoclave bags.

## Needle Penetration and Sealing

The bag material is designed to be penetrated by a syringe needle during inoculation. Jarrold's technique involves heating the needle until red to facilitate penetration. After the needle is withdrawn, the plastic naturally seals around the puncture, and a piece of masking tape is applied over the hole for additional security. This combination of self-sealing material and tape backup provides an effective contamination barrier.

## Comparison with Specialty Cultivation Bags

Commercial mycological supply companies sell autoclave bags with integrated filter patches at significantly higher prices (typically 1-3 USD per bag). The Lakeland boil bags, combined with Jarrold's separate filter can, achieve the same functionality at a fraction of the cost. The trade-off is slightly more assembly time and less integrated design, but for amateur cultivators, the cost savings are substantial.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

The repurposing of kitchen boil bags for mushroom cultivation illustrates an important principle in amateur mycology: that effective cultivation does not require expensive specialized equipment. By identifying a commercially available, low-cost product that meets the technical requirements of mushroom cultivation, Jarrold demonstrated that ingenuity and practical experimentation can substitute for capital investment.

## Manufacturer Background

Lakeland Plastics (now known simply as Lakeland) is a UK-based kitchenware company founded in 1964 in Windermere, Cumbria. The company is known for innovative kitchen products and has a long history of mail-order retail. The boil bags that Jarrold identified for mushroom cultivation were originally designed for oven roasting and boiling of food, where they must withstand high oven temperatures without melting or releasing harmful chemicals. This heat resistance is what made them suitable for pressure cooker sterilization.

## Availability and Accessibility

One of the key advantages of the Lakeland boil bags is their mainstream availability. Unlike specialty mycological supplies that must be ordered from dedicated vendors, these bags can be purchased from a household goods catalog or retail store. For cultivators who prefer discretion, this is a significant advantage. The product's innocuous kitchen purpose provides natural cover for mycological purchases. The low price point (approximately 2.50 GBP for 50 bags) also makes experimentation affordable.

## Historical Significance

The identification of kitchen boil bags as suitable cultivation containers represents a notable moment in the democratization of mushroom cultivation. Before such innovations, amateur cultivation required either expensive specialty equipment or improvised solutions with lower success rates. By bridging the gap between household products and mycological requirements, Jarrold helped establish the principle that effective mushroom cultivation is accessible to anyone willing to learn basic aseptic technique.
