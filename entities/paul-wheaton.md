# Paul Wheaton
Paul Wheaton is an American permaculture educator, homesteader, author,
and internet personality widely known as "The Duke of Permaculture." Based
in Montana, Wheaton has built one of the largest online permaculture
communities through his forums at Permies.com and his website RichSoil.com.
He is a vocal and often controversial advocate for radical sustainability,
natural building, organic gardening, and appropriate technology. His work
focuses on practical, low-cost implementations of permaculture principles,
particularly rocket mass heaters, hugelkultur, food forests, and
humanure composting. Wheaton has popularized several permaculture
techniques that have reached mainstream awareness through his prolific
online content and podcast appearances.

Before dedicating himself to permaculture full-time, Wheaton worked as a
software developer, an experience that informed his approach to building
large-scale online communities and his systems-thinking approach to
homesteading and ecological design. He transitioned to full-time
permaculture work in the late 2000s.
## Major Contributions
### Rocket Mass Heaters
Wheaton is the most prominent popularizer of rocket mass heaters —
hyper-efficient wood-burning heating systems using a J-tube combustion
chamber to achieve near-complete combustion at high temperatures, with
exhaust routed through a massive thermal bench for heat storage:
- Use 80–90% less wood than conventional wood stoves
- Produce significantly less smoke and particulate emissions
- Can be built from inexpensive, locally sourced materials (cob, brick,
  barrel, pipe)
- Provide comfortable radiant heat for 12–24 hours from a single firing
- His YouTube series and articles have been viewed millions of times
Wheaton sponsored rocket mass heater workshops and experiments at his
Montana property, documenting performance data and iterating on designs.
He has been a vocal critic of EPA regulations that he argues stifle
innovation in clean-burning heating technology.
### Hugelkultur Popularization
While hugelkultur (mound culture) originated in Eastern European and
German traditional gardening, Wheaton played a major role in introducing
the technique to the English-speaking permaculture world:
- Buried wood as long-term moisture reservoir and nutrient source
- Elimination of irrigation needs once established
- Creation of microclimates for extended growing seasons
- Transformation of waste wood into productive garden beds
- Sepp Holzer's work cited as primary inspiration
### Permies.com Community
Founded in 2009, Permies.com has grown into one of the largest online
permaculture forums in the world with over 100,000 registered members.
The community covers homesteading, natural building, organic gardening,
appropriate technology, animal husbandry, permaculture design, and
mushroom cultivation. Wheaton's community-building approach emphasized
free knowledge sharing, peer-to-peer support, and documentation of
real-world experiments over theoretical discussion alone.
### Wheaton Eco Scale
A provocative ranking system for sustainability practices:
1. **Level 0**: Average consumer lifestyle
2. **Level 1**: Basic recycling and awareness
3. **Level 3**: Organic gardening, composting
4. **Level 5**: Significant homesteading, food self-sufficiency
5. **Level 7**: Off-grid, natural building, humanure
6. **Level 9**: Full permaculture systems, zero waste
7. **Level 10**: "Beyond organic" — radical sustainability
The scale illustrates that sustainability is a spectrum rather than a
binary state, encouraging progression toward greater self-sufficiency.
## Key Projects and Properties
### Montana Basecamp
Wheaton's primary property in Montana serves as a living laboratory:
- Multiple rocket mass heater installations with performance data
- Large-scale hugelkultur beds (some over 100 feet long)
- Food forest plantings adapted to Montana's cold climate
- Humanure composting systems
- Natural building experiments (cob, wattle and daub)
- Solar dehydrators and root cellars
- Experimental animal systems (poultry, pigs)
### Digital Presence
- Over 100,000 forum members as of the mid-2020s

## Philosophy and Approach
- **Radical practicality**: Emphasis on techniques that save money and
  resources, not just environmental virtue
- **Anti-consumerism**: Advocacy for reducing consumption and dependency
  on commercial systems
- **Experimentation-first**: Preference for documented real-world
  experiments over theoretical frameworks
- **Provocative communication**: Willingness to use controversy and humor
  to challenge conventional thinking
- **Free knowledge**: Strong commitment to freely available online content
- **Appropriate technology**: Focus on low-tech, maintainable solutions
## Controversies and Criticisms
- **Wood stove regulation stance**: Opposition to EPA regulations on wood
  heating has drawn criticism from air quality advocates
- **Provocative rhetoric**: Communication style sometimes alienates
  mainstream environmental organizations
- **Anti-certification stance**: Critical of commercial permaculture
  certification programs, arguing they prioritize profit over knowledge
- **Community moderation**: Hands-off approach has at times allowed
  misinformation to circulate on Permies.com
## Published Works and Media

## Influence on Permaculture Movement
Paul Wheaton's significance lies not in theoretical innovation but in
popularization and accessibility. By building a massive online community
and producing free, practical content, he has introduced permaculture
concepts to hundreds of thousands of people who might never encounter them
through traditional channels. His emphasis on budget-friendly, DIY
implementations has made permaculture approachable for people without
significant land or financial resources. While academically trained
permaculturists sometimes distance themselves from his style, his impact
on mainstream awareness of rocket mass heaters and hugelkultur is
substantial and well-documented.
