## Overview

1-Butanoyl-d-[[lysergic-acid-diethylamide]] (1B-LSD) is a novel lysergamide psychedelic compound and functional analog of LSD. It was first identified as a new psychoactive substance in 2020, appearing on the European research chemical market.

The compound features a butanoyl group at the 1-position of the LSD molecule in place of LSD's dimethyl group, which serves as a metabolic prodrug that converts to LSD in vivo.

1B-LSD is one of several N¹-acylated LSD derivatives that have emerged as designer psychedelics following the scheduling of 1P-LSD and AL-LAD, exploiting [[hallucinogenic-mushroom-legal-loopholes-and-classification-challenges]] in many jurisdictions.

## Chemical Properties

**Molecular formula:** C₂₃H₃₀N₃O₃. **Molecular weight:** 396.52 g/mol.

**IUPAC name:** (6aR,9R)-7-butyl-7,9-dihydro-6H-indolo[4,3-fg][1,4]oxazino[3,4,6]decan-9-yl diethylcarbamate.

The structure consists of the classical tetracyclic lysergamide scaffold (ergoline ring system) with a butanoyl substituent on the indole nitrogen (N¹ position) and a diethylamide at C-8.

The N¹-butanoyl group increases molecular weight and lipophilicity compared to LSD, potentially affecting blood-brain barrier penetration and metabolic stability.

1B-LSD is typically encountered as a powder or deposited onto blotter paper. It is soluble in ethanol, methanol, and slightly soluble in water. It is sensitive to light, heat, and oxidation like other lysergamides.

## Pharmacology

1B-LSD acts primarily as a 5-HT₂A receptor agonist, the same mechanism responsible for the psychedelic effects of LSD, psilocybin, and other classical hallucinogens.

The compound functions as a prodrug: the N¹-butanoyl group is cleaved in vivo by hepatic and serum amidases, yielding 1B-LSD's active metabolite — [[lysergic-acid]] diethylamide (LSD) itself.

This prodrug mechanism is analogous to 1P-LSD (1-propionyl-LSD) and 1cP-LSD (1-cyclopropionyl-LSD), all of which convert to LSD after administration.

In vitro [[legal-responses-to-hallucinogenic-mushrooms-in-the-european-union]], 1B-LSD is controlled under generic lysergamide provisions in several member states (Germany, Sweden, Switzerland, Finland) but remains unscheduled in others.

The metabolic conversion rate from 1B-LSD to LSD has not been precisely quantified in humans. Based on animal data and user reports, conversion is estimated at 50–70% of the administered dose.

## Related Compounds

1B-LSD is part of the N¹-acyl lysergamide family, which includes 1P-LSD (1-propionyl), 1cP-LSD (1-cyclopropionyl), 1V-LSD (1-valeroyl), and AL-LAD (6-allyl-6-nor-LSD).

These compounds share the prodrug mechanism of LSD generation and are differentiated primarily by the size and lipophilicity of their N¹ acyl substituent, which affects onset time and conversion efficiency.
