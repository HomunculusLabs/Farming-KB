## Overview

1-Butanoyl-d-lysergic acid diethylamide (1B-LSD) is a novel lysergamide psychedelic compound and functional analog of LSD. It was first identified as a new psychoactive substance in 2020, appearing on the European research chemical market.

The compound features a butanoyl group at the 1-position of the LSD molecule in place of LSD's dimethyl group, which serves as a metabolic prodrug that converts to LSD in vivo.

1B-LSD is one of several N¹-acylated LSD derivatives that have emerged as designer psychedelics following the scheduling of 1P-LSD and AL-LAD, exploiting legal loopholes in many jurisdictions.

## Chemical Properties

**Molecular formula:** C₂₃H₃₀N₃O₃. **Molecular weight:** 396.52 g/mol.

**IUPAC name:** (6aR,9R)-7-butyl-7,9-dihydro-6H-indolo[4,3-fg][1,4]oxazino[3,4,6]decan-9-yl diethylcarbamate.

The structure consists of the classical tetracyclic lysergamide scaffold (ergoline ring system) with a butanoyl substituent on the indole nitrogen (N¹ position) and a diethylamide at C-8.

The N¹-butanoyl group increases molecular weight and lipophilicity compared to LSD, potentially affecting blood-brain barrier penetration and metabolic stability.

1B-LSD is typically encountered as a powder or deposited onto blotter paper. It is soluble in ethanol, methanol, and slightly soluble in water. It is sensitive to light, heat, and oxidation like other lysergamides.

## Pharmacology

1B-LSD acts primarily as a 5-HT₂A receptor agonist, the same mechanism responsible for the psychedelic effects of LSD, psilocybin, and other classical hallucinogens.

The compound functions as a prodrug: the N¹-butanoyl group is cleaved in vivo by hepatic and serum amidases, yielding 1B-LSD's active metabolite — lysergic acid diethylamide (LSD) itself.

This prodrug mechanism is analogous to 1P-LSD (1-propionyl-LSD) and 1cP-LSD (1-cyclopropionyl-LSD), all of which convert to LSD after administration.

In vitro receptor binding data are limited. By analogy with closely related N¹-acyl lysergamides, estimated 5-HT₂A Ki values are expected in the 2–20 nM range.

The onset of action is slightly delayed compared to LSD (estimated 90–120 minutes vs. 45–60 minutes for LSD), consistent with the additional metabolic deacylation step required for activation.

## Effects

Subjective effects are reported as essentially identical to LSD, given that the active species in the body is LSD. Users consistently describe visual hallucinations, altered cognition, synesthesia, and enhanced emotional introspection.

Reported dose range is approximately 80–150 µg (one blotter), roughly equivalent to 75–125 µg of LSD, accounting for the molecular weight difference and incomplete prodrug conversion.

Duration of effects is typically 8–12 hours, consistent with the pharmacokinetic profile of LSD generated from the prodrug. The comedown period may extend an additional 2–4 hours.

Common positive effects include enhanced color perception, pattern recognition, philosophical ideation, and feelings of unity or connectedness. Negative effects can include anxiety, confusion, and difficulty sleeping.

Physical effects are typical of classical psychedelics: mild pupillary dilation, increased heart rate and blood pressure, reduced appetite, and mild tremor. Hyperthermia is not typically observed at common doses.

## Legal Status

1B-LSD is not specifically listed in the UN Convention on Psychotropic Substances (1971) or its amendments. Its legal status varies by country depending on analog and generic scheduling provisions.

In the European Union, 1B-LSD is controlled under generic lysergamide provisions in several member states (Germany, Sweden, Switzerland, Finland) but remains unscheduled in others.

In the United Kingdom, the Psychoactive Substances Act (2016) criminalizes production and supply but not simple possession. However, the UK has also scheduled specific lysergamides under the Misuse of Drugs Act.

In the United States, 1B-LSD may fall under the Federal Analogue Act if intended for human consumption, as a structural analog of Schedule I LSD. State-level scheduling varies.

The rapid scheduling of precursor lysergamides (1P-LSD → 1cP-LSD → 1B-LSD) has created a cat-and-mouse dynamic between regulators and manufacturers, with new N¹-acyl variants appearing as each predecessor is controlled.

## Metabolism

In vivo metabolism studies (Grigoryev et al., 2021) using human liver microsomes and zebrafish models have confirmed that 1B-LSD undergoes rapid deacylation to LSD, detectable within 30 minutes of exposure.

Minor metabolites include N-deethyl-LSD (LAMPA), nor-LSD, and hydroxylated derivatives, consistent with the known metabolic pathway of LSD. No unique metabolites specific to the butanoyl moiety have been identified at significant levels.

The metabolic conversion rate from 1B-LSD to LSD has not been precisely quantified in humans. Based on animal data and user reports, conversion is estimated at 50–70% of the administered dose.

## Related Compounds

1B-LSD is part of the N¹-acyl lysergamide family, which includes 1P-LSD (1-propionyl), 1cP-LSD (1-cyclopropionyl), 1V-LSD (1-valeroyl), and AL-LAD (6-allyl-6-nor-LSD).

These compounds share the prodrug mechanism of LSD generation and are differentiated primarily by the size and lipophilicity of their N¹ acyl substituent, which affects onset time and conversion efficiency.

The emergence of increasingly bulky N¹-acyl groups (propionyl → butanoyl → valeroyl) represents a trend toward slower-onset, longer-lasting variants that may evade detection and scheduling.

## Harm Reduction

As a research chemical, 1B-LSD is sold without quality control or purity verification. Blotter doses may vary significantly between vendors and batches, with reported discrepancies of 20–40% from labeled amounts.

Reagent testing (Ehrlich's reagent turns purple for indole-containing compounds) can confirm lysergamide presence but cannot distinguish 1B-LSD from LSD or other N¹-acyl variants. TLC or GC-MS is required for specific identification.

Set and setting considerations are identical to LSD: a calm, familiar environment with a trusted trip sitter is recommended, especially for inexperienced users. Higher doses increase risk of anxiety and confusion.

There are no known fatal overdoses from 1B-LSD specifically, though the LD₅₀ is presumed to be similar to LSD (estimated >12,000 µg for an average adult). Serotonin syndrome risk is low but increases with concomitant serotonergic drugs.

No established clinical trials exist for 1B-LSD. All dosage and effect information derives from self-reported user experiences on harm reduction forums and anecdotal reports, which carry inherent reliability limitations.
