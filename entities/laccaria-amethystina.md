# Laccaria amethystina

**Laccaria amethystina**, commonly known as the amethyst deceiver, is a small
basidiomycete fungus in the family Hydnangiaceae (or Tricholomataceae in older
classifications). It is a widespread ectomycorrhizal species associated with a
broad range of trees in temperate and [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]]) and other taxa; some
populations in Asia may represent distinct lineages.

## Description

- **Cap:** 1–4 cm, convex to broadly convex, often with a slight umbo; surface
  dry, smooth or finely scurfy. Color vivid amethyst-purple when fresh and moist,
  fading to grayish-brown or buff with age or in dry conditions. This extreme
  color variability is the basis for the common name.
- **Gills:** Thick, widely spaced, adnate to slightly decurrent; same color as
  cap or slightly paler, purple to lilac fading with age. Distinctively waxy and
  thick-spaced compared to most agarics.
- **Stem:** 3–8 cm × 0.2–0.6 cm, equal or slightly tapered at base, fibrous,
  tough and flexible. Purple when young, fading to brownish. No ring.
- **Flesh:** Thin, watery, purplish when fresh; mild taste, no distinctive odor.
- **Spores:** White print; broadly ellipsoid, 7–10 × 6–8 µm, prominently spiny
  (echinulate) with spines up to 1 µm long. The spiny spores are diagnostic for
  the genus *Laccaria*.

## Distribution and Habitat

Highly generalist ectomycorrhizal associate found with conifers (pine, spruce, fir,
hemlock) and hardwoods (oak, beech, birch, eucalyptus). One of the most widely
distributed *Laccaria* species across Europe, Asia, North America, and Australasia.
Inhabits forest floors in mixed and coniferous woodlands, heathland edges, and
disturbed sites including plantations and reclaimed land. Fruits from July to
November in [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]], often in scattered groups or troops. Tolerant of
acidic soils and commonly found in pine plantations on nutrient-poor substrates.
