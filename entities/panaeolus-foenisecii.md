## Overview

*Panaeolus foenisecii* (Pers.:Fr.) Kühn., commonly known as the "haymaker"s mushroom" or "brown hay mushroom," is a small, dark-spored mushroom that grows in grassy areas, particularly lawns and fields after the hay harvest. It is one of the most commonly encountered mushrooms in the genus *Panaeolus* and has been the subject of considerable taxonomic and pharmacological controversy regarding its [[psilocybin]] content.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Phylum**: Basidiomycota
- **Class**: Agaricomycetes
- **Order**: [[agaricales]]
- **Family**: Bolbitiaceae (formerly placed in Coprinaceae)
- **Genus**: *Panaeolus* (sometimes classified as *Panaeolina*)
- **Species**: *Panaeolus foenisecii*

## Morphology

- **Cap**: Small, hygrophanous, color changing from whitish to grey or from reddish brown to very dark black-brown depending on moisture
- **Gills**: Dark, mottled appearance due to spores not ripening simultaneously
- **Spores**: Purplish-brown and abrasive (warty), distinguishing them from other *Panaeolus* species which have smooth, black spore prints
- **Stem**: Slender, fragile
- **Size**: Small — typically a few centimeters tall

## Habitat and Season

- **Substrate**: Grassy areas, lawns, meadows, pastures
- **Season**: Fruits primarily after the hay harvest
- **Distribution**: Widespread across Europe, North America, and Australia
- **Growth habit**: Does NOT grow on dung, unlike many other *Panaeolus* species
- **Fruiting pattern**: Often grows in the immediate vicinity of *[[panaeolus-subbalteatus]]*, creating opportunities for misidentification

## Taxonomic Controversy

*Panaeolus foenisecii* has been subject to extensive taxonomic reclassification:

- Previously classified in the genus *Psilocybe*
- Also placed in *Psathyrella*
- Sometimes separated into the genus *Panaeolina* to distinguish it from *Panaeolus* proper, based on its non-dung habitat preference, post-hay-harvest fruiting, and warty (abrasive) purplish-brown spores rather than smooth black ones

## Psilocybin Content — A Controversial Subject

The psychoactive status of *Panaeolus foenisecii* remains one of the most controversial topics in ethnomycology:

### Evidence Against Psilocybin Content
- Stijve collected and tested samples from eight countries across three continents (America, Europe, and Australia) and consistently failed to find psilocybin
- Gartz"s own analyses of 100 mushrooms from a wide variety of locations across Germany also failed to confirm the presence of psilocybin or its derivatives
- The species does produce 5-substituted indole compounds including serotonin and its biochemical precursor 5-hydroxytryptophan, which are completely inactive when taken orally but can easily be mistaken for psilocin during paper or thin-layer chromatography
- Ola'h's world monograph of *Panaeolus* (1960s) described many species as "latent psilocybin-producers," including *P. foenisecii*, but these findings have not been reliably replicated

### Evidence For Psilocybin Content
- Ola'h reported that *P. foenisecii* occasionally produces psilocybin
- Some early analyses may have detected psilocybin, but these results are confounded by the potential for misidentification with *P. subbalteatus*

### Current Understanding
The consensus among modern researchers is that *P. foenisecii* either does not contain psilocybin or contains it in such variable and trace amounts that it cannot be considered reliably psychoactive. The serotonin and 5-hydroxytryptophan present in the mushroom are orally inactive and account for many of the false positive identification results.

## Distinguishing from Psychoactive Species

*Panaeolus foenisecii* is easily confused with the psychoactive *Panaeolus subbalteatus*, which frequently grows in the same habitat. Key differences include:

| Feature | *P. foenisecii* | *P. subbalteatus* |
|---|---|---|
| [[spore-print]] | Purplish-brown, warty | Black, smooth |
| Habitat | Grassy areas, not on dung | Dung, manure, compost |
| Psilocybin | Not reliably present | 0.1–0.7% dry weight |
| Blue staining | No | Rarely |

## Chemical Composition

- **Serotonin**: Present, along with 5-hydroxytryptophan
- **Urea**: Present (shared with other *Panaeolus* species and *Pluteus*)
- **Psilocybin**: Controversial — not reliably detected in modern analyses
- **Psilocin**: Not confirmed

## Ecological Role

As a saprophytic species growing in grasslands, *P. foenisecii* plays a role in decomposing dead grass and organic matter in lawns and meadows. Its post-harvest fruiting pattern suggests an [[ecological-niche]] tied to the disturbance and deposition of dead plant material during haying operations.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Foraging

*Panaeolus foenisecii* is important to foragers primarily as a species to avoid confusing with the psychoactive *P. subbalteatus*. Its unreliable psilocybin content and the risk of misidentification make it unsuitable for psychoactive use. Accurate identification requires careful attention to spore characteristics and habitat preferences.
