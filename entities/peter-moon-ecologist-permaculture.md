# Peter Moon (Ecologist)

## Description

Peter Moon is a researcher cited by [[permaculture-design]] literature

## Key Facts

1. **Published in New Scientist (28 Feb 1985):** Moon's article addressed how ecologists measure and differentiate between types of species distribution in ecosystems.

2. **Three-part diversity framework:**
   - **Richness:** The number of species per unit area — a raw count of species present.
   - **Diversity:** The relative abundance of species — how evenly distributed individuals are across species.
   - **Evenness:** How species contribute to the total biomass — a measure of dominance versus equity.

3. **Key finding on succession:** Moon noted that richness may decrease in plant communities as systems age, because shade and competition reduce annuals and weaker species. However, richness may simultaneously increase in animal communities (especially decomposers), as mature systems develop a greater range of niches and microclimates.

4. **Implication for land management:** Ungrazed or uncut grasslands support more animal species but fewer plant species — a tradeoff that permaculture designers must navigate when deciding intervention intensity.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **[[fungal-ecology]]. A mature forest may have high fungal species richness in the decomposer community even as plant richness declines — meaning older forests may be prime sites for diverse fungal fruiting.
- **Guild design:** Permaculture guilds benefit from understanding that diversity is not just about species count (richness) but about balanced abundance (evenness). A mushroom bed dominated by one contaminant species has low evenness, even if many species are technically present.
- **Substrate ecology:** Moon's observation that older systems develop more niches suggests that aged, complex substrates (hardwood logs, mature compost) will support richer decomposer communities than simple, fresh substrates.
- **Disturbance management:** The stress mosaic concept Mollison derives from Moon — alternating disturbed and undisturbed zones — mirrors the strategy of alternating flush-inducing soaks with rest periods in mushroom cultivation.
