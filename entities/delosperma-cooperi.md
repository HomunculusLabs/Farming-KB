## Overview

Delosperma cooperi, commonly known as hardy ice plant or Cooper's ice plant, is a low-growing, mat-forming succulent perennial native to the highlands of eastern South Africa.

It is renowned for its vivid magenta-pink, daisy-like flowers that blanket the foliage from late spring through fall. The blooms open in full sun and close at night or on cloudy days.

The common name "ice plant" refers to the glistening, bladder-like epidermal cells on the leaves.

These specialized cells reflect sunlight and give the foliage an icy, sparkling appearance. Under magnification, they resemble tiny water droplets or crystals.

Delosperma cooperi is one of the most cold-hardy species in the genus, surviving temperatures to –23 °C.

This hardiness has made it a popular groundcover in temperate gardens. It thrives in regions where most succulents cannot survive winter conditions.

The species is valued [[hamilton-rock-garden-design]] edging, and [[aact-foliar-sticker-spreaders-leaf-surface-attachment-ingham]] is covered with transparent, bladder-like papillae that glisten in sunlight. These papillae are modified epidermal cells that store water and scatter light.

Flowers are solitary, terminal, daisy-like, and measure 3–5 cm across.

Petals are narrow, numerous, and bright magenta-pink with a white eye at the center. Flower color varies slightly with temperature and light exposure.

Stamens are numerous and form a conspicuous yellow ring around the central pistil.

Flowers open diurnally, responding to temperature and light intensity. Individual blooms last 2–3 days but are continuously replaced through the long blooming season.

Fruit is a hygrochastic capsule that opens when wetted, dispersing tiny seeds. This mechanism ensures seed release during rainy conditions favorable for germination.

## Distribution and Habitat

Delosperma cooperi is native to the Drakensberg Mountains and adjacent highlands of eastern South Africa and Lesotho.

It grows at elevations of 1,500–2,800 m in grassland, rocky outcrops, and alpine meadows. The high-altitude habitat experiences cold winters, regular frost, and summer rainfall.

Soils are typically thin, rocky, and well-drained, derived from basalt or sandstone parent material.

Plants colonize crevices, gravelly slopes, and exposed plateau surfaces. The ability to root at stem nodes helps stabilize loose substrate.

Summer rainfall averages 600–1,000 mm annually, concentrated in November–March.

Winter months are dry and cold, with temperatures frequently dropping below freezing. Snowfall occurs regularly at the highest elevations of its range.

The species shares its habitat with other high-altitude succulents and alpine plants. Companion species include Crassula, Sedum, and Helichrysum species.

## Cultivation

Delosperma cooperi is among the easiest and most rewarding succulent groundcovers for temperate gardens. It requires full sun, sharp drainage, and lean, gravelly soil.

The species thrives in USDA Zones 5–9, tolerating winter temperatures to –23 °C. Excellent winter drainage is critical; plants rot in heavy, water-retentive soils during cold dormancy.

Water moderately during the active [[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]] rejuvenates older plantings. Each division should include roots and several growing points for reliable establishment.

No serious pests or diseases affect Delosperma cooperi when grown in appropriate conditions. Overwatering and poor drainage are the primary causes of plant failure.
