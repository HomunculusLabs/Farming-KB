## Azolla caroliniana

## Overview

Azolla caroliniana, commonly known as Carolina azolla or mosquito fern, is a small free-floating aquatic fern native to [[livestock-feed-formulation]] supplement. Its ability to double its biomass in 2–5 days under optimal conditions makes it one of the fastest-growing plants on Earth.

A. caroliniana is one of seven recognized *Azolla* species and is distinguished by its relatively cold tolerance compared to tropical congeners like *A. pinnata* and *A. filiculoides*.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Kingdom: Plantae — Division: Polypodiopsida — Class: Polypodiopsida — Order: Salviniales — Family: Salviniaceae — Genus: Azolla — Species: A. caroliniana Willd.

The genus name *Azolla* derives from Greek *azo* ("to dry") and *ollyo* ("to kill"), referring to the plant's tendency to desiccate and die when removed from water. The specific epithet *caroliniana* references the Carolinas region of eastern North America where the species was first documented.

A. caroliniana is a small fern measuring 1–2.5 cm across individual fronds. The plant consists of overlapping bilobed fronds arranged in a mosaic pattern, creating a mat-like floating colony.

Each frond has an upper (dorsal) lobe that is green to reddish-green and contains the symbiotic *Anabaena* cavities, and a lower (ventral) lobe that is colorless or pale and functions as a float. The roots are simple, unbranched, and hang freely beneath the fronds.

The plant shifts color from green to deep red or burgundy under stress conditions including high light intensity, nitrogen deficiency, or cold temperatures. This color change is caused by accumulation of anthocyanin pigments and is often used as a visual indicator of nitrogen depletion in the water column.

## Symbiosis with Anabaena

The defining feature of *Azolla* is its obligate symbiosis with the nitrogen-fixing cyanobacterium *Anabaena azollae*. The cyanobacterium resides within specialized dorsal cavity cells of the fern, where it fixes atmospheric nitrogen into ammonium that the host plant can assimilate.

This symbiotic relationship is one of the most productive nitrogen-fixing systems in nature. Under favorable conditions, an *Azolla* mat can fix 3–7 kg of nitrogen per hectare per day, rivaling synthetic nitrogen fertilizers in agricultural efficiency.

The cyanobiont is vertically transmitted through the *Azolla* sporocarp, ensuring that each new generation of ferns carries the nitrogen-fixing partner. This co-evolutionary relationship is estimated to be at least 80 million years old, based on fossil evidence from the Late Cretaceous period.

The *Anabaena* filaments within the cavity differentiate into heterocysts, specialized cells that provide the anaerobic environment necessary for the nitrogenase enzyme to function. The fern provides carbon compounds to the cyanobacterium, while receiving fixed nitrogen in return.

## Distribution and Habitat

A. caroliniana is native to eastern North America, ranging from southern Ontario and the northeastern United States south to Florida and west to Texas. It has been widely introduced to other temperate and subtropical regions for agricultural use.

The species thrives in still or slow-moving freshwater habitats including ponds, ditches, marshes, rice paddies, and lake margins. It prefers full sun to partial shade and water temperatures between 15–30°C.

Unlike its tropical relatives, *A. caroliniana* can survive winter temperatures by producing dormant sporocarps that sink to the bottom and germinate when conditions improve in spring. This cold tolerance makes it the preferred species for [[coleman-winter-day-length-and-persephone-months]] cooler temperatures in autumn.

The plant is sensitive to high salinity, pH extremes (optimal range 5.5–7.0), and strong water currents. It cannot tolerate deep shade, which limits its growth beneath dense canopy cover.

## Permaculture and Agricultural Applications

A. caroliniana has been used as a green manure in rice paddies for centuries, particularly in East and [[query-how-to-protect-plants-from-frost-and-extend-the-growing-season]].

The fern's dense surface mat suppresses mosquito larvae by blocking oviposition sites, despite the common name "mosquito fern." It also reduces water evaporation from ponds and water features by up to 60%, a significant benefit in arid climate water management.

In aquaculture, *Azolla* is fed to fish, ducks, and livestock as a protein-rich supplement. Its dry matter contains 25–35% crude protein, making it nutritionally comparable to alfalfa meal.

## Bioactive Compounds

*A. caroliniana* contains several bioactive compounds of interest. [[aact-tannins-terpenes-phenolic-compounds-wood-composting-ingham]] including flavonoids and tannins exhibit antioxidant and antimicrobial activity in laboratory studies.

The plant accumulates significant quantities of carotenoids, including lutein and beta-carotene, contributing to its nutritional value as an animal feed supplement. Anthocyanin content increases under environmental stress, providing additional antioxidant capacity.

Triterpenoids isolated from *Azolla* species have shown anti-inflammatory and hepatoprotective effects in animal models. These findings are preliminary and specific to *A. caroliniana* have not been extensively studied.
