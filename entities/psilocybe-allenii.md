# Psilocybe allenii

## Overview

**Psilocybe allenii** is a psilocybin-containing mushroom in the family Hymenogastraceae, found along the Pacific coast of North America. First described scientifically in 2012 by mycologists Jan Borovička, Alan Rockefeller, and Peter G. Werner, it was named in honor of mycologist and ethnobotanist John W. Allen, who first recognized the species as distinct through decades of field work in the Pacific Northwest. The species was long conflated with [[psilocybe-cyanescens]] until molecular phylogenetic analysis of ITS rDNA confirmed its status as a separate species. It is commonly encountered in urban and suburban settings of the San Francisco Bay Area, Puget Sound, and Willamette Valley, where it fruits prolifically on landscaped wood chip mulch.

## Taxonomy & Etymology

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Hymenogastraceae
- **Genus:** *Psilocybe*
- **Species:** *P. allenii*

The specific epithet *allenii* honors **John W. Allen** (1931–2023), an American mycologist, author, and ethnomycological researcher. Allen was among the first to notice consistent morphological differences between West Coast collections and true *P. cyanescens*, accumulating specimens in the Seattle area during the 1970s and 1980s. He authored several books including *Mushrooms of the Pacific Northwest* and was a pioneering figure in documenting global psilocybin mushroom diversity. The species belongs to *Psilocybe* section *Cyanescens*, a clade of primarily wood-inhabiting, bluing species that includes [[psilocybe-cyanescens]], [[psilocybe-azurescens]], and [[psilocybe-subaeruginosa]].

## Morphology

### Cap (Pileus)
- 1.5–5 cm diameter
- Convex to broadly convex, becoming plane with age; sometimes with a slight umbo
- Hygrophanous: caramel-brown to ochraceous when moist, drying to pale buff or nearly white
- Surface smooth, slightly viscid when wet
- Margin typically **straight to slightly incurved**—not wavy or undulating (a key field mark distinguishing it from [[psilocybe-cyanescens]])
- Veil remnants may decorate the cap margin in young specimens

### Gills (Lamellae)
- Adnate to adnexed attachment
- Subdistant to close, with 2–3 tiers of lamellulae (short gills)
- Grayish at first, maturing to dark purplish-brown to nearly black
- Edges even, occasionally slightly fimbriate
- Bruise blue where damaged

### Stipe (Stem)
- 4–10 cm long × 2–5 mm thick
- Equal to slightly enlarged at the base
- Whitish to pale brown, bruising bluish where handled or cut
- Surface covered with fine white to silvery fibrils, especially in the upper portion
- Hollow at maturity; cottony white pith in younger specimens
- Partial veil evanescent, sometimes leaving a fibrillose annular zone

### Spores
- Basidiospores 11–14 × 7–9 µm
- Rhomboid to subrhomboid in face view, ellipsoid in side view
- Thick-walled with a distinct germ pore
- Spore print dark purplish-brown to nearly black

### Microscopic Features
- Cheilocystidia ventricose to lageniform, sometimes with a slight neck constriction
- Pleurocystidia absent or very rare
- Pileipellis a cutis of filamentous hyphae, with occasional erect terminal cells
- Clamp connections present at hyphal septa

## Distribution & Habitat

*Psilocybe allenii* is found along the Pacific coast of North America, from British Columbia south through Washington and Oregon to northern California. Its core distribution centers on the **San Francisco Bay Area**, the **Puget Sound region**, and the **Willamette Valley**.

The species is strongly associated with human-landscaped environments:
- Wood chip mulch in parks, gardens, and municipal plantings
- Especially beds containing alder (*Alnus*) or mixed hardwood chips
- Decorative landscaping bark and wood chip pathways
- Rarely encountered in undisturbed natural habitats

It grows gregariously to cespitose, often forming large, dense clusters. Fruiting season is **late autumn through winter**, typically October to January, with peak flushes in November and December following the first significant Pacific storms and sustained cool temperatures. It frequently co-occurs with [[psilocybe-cyanescens]] in the same wood chip beds.

## Chemistry

*P. allenii* contains the psychoactive indole alkaloids [[psilocybin]] and [[psilocin]], with baeocystin and norbaeocystin as minor constituents. Analytical studies report:

| Compound | Typical Range (% dry weight) |
|----------|------------------------------|
| Psilocybin | 0.6–1.3% |
| Psilocin | 0.05–0.25% |
| Baeocystin | 0.01–0.05% |

Total tryptamine content places *P. allenii* in the **moderate to high** potency range—comparable to but generally slightly lower than [[psilocybe-cyanescens]]. The psilocybin-to-psilocin ratio varies between collections and is influenced by drying method, storage duration, and environmental conditions during fruiting. Typical recreational doses are 1.0–2.5 g dried.

## Similar Species

Differentiating *P. allenii* from its closest relatives requires attention to several key characters:

| Feature | *P. allenii* | *P. cyanescens* | *P. azurescens* |
|---------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Cap margin | Straight, not wavy | Strongly wavy/undulate | Wavy, often more conic |
| Spore size | 11–14 × 7–9 µm | 12–15 × 7–9 µm | 13–17 × 7–9 µm |
| Habitat | Urban wood chips | Urban wood chips, dunes | Coastal dune grass only |
| Blueing | Moderate | Often intense | Very intense |
| Distribution | Pacific coast | Global (introduced) | Oregon coast only |

Additional lookalikes include *Galerina marginata* (deadly amatoxins, brown spore print, no blueing, persistent annulus), *Psilocybe subaeruginosa* (Australia/New Zealand, morphologically similar), and *Deconica* spp. (non-psychoactive wood-chip mushrooms, no blueing).

## Cultivation

*P. allenii* can be cultivated on sterilized hardwood chip substrates inoculated with grain spawn. Key requirements:
- Cool fruiting temperatures of **10–16°C** (similar to [[psilocybe-cyanescens]])
- Alder or mixed hardwood chips as primary substrate
- Outdoor beds colonized during warm months, fruiting triggered by autumn temperature drops
- Colonization takes 3–6 months before first fruiting
- Established beds may produce for several successive seasons

Indoor cultivation is challenging due to the cool temperature requirements and wood-based substrate preference. Colonized wood chip spawn is typically used to establish outdoor beds.

## Legal Status

As a psilocybin-containing species, *P. allenii* is illegal to possess, cultivate, or distribute in most jurisdictions:
- **United States:** Schedule I controlled substance under the Controlled Substances Act
- **Canada:** Controlled under the Controlled Drugs and Substances Act
- **Oregon (USA):** Legal for supervised therapeutic use under Measure 109 (2020)
- **Colorado (USA):** Decriminalized under Proposition 122 (2022)
- **International:** Psilocybin and psilocin are Schedule I under the UN Convention on Psychotropic Substances
