## Description

Soil pollution from biocides refers to the persistent contamination of agricultural soils by synthetic pesticides, herbicides, and other chemical agents applied to crops. Bill Mollison, in *Permaculture: A Designers' Manual*, documents the severe and long-lasting consequences of biocide use on orchards, sugarcane plantations, cotton, tobacco, pineapple, and banana crops. Residues of chemicals such as DDT, Aldrin, Dieldrin, BHC, arsenicals, copper salts, and dioxins can persist in soils for decades, entering the food chain through plants, animals, and groundwater.

## Classification

- **Category**: Environmental contamination -- persistent organic pollutants
- **Key contaminants**: Organochlorines (DDT, Aldrin, Dieldrin, BHC), arsenicals, copper salts, dioxins
- **Primary sources**: Orchard sprays, plantation agriculture, industrial waste dumps, roadside lead deposition
- **Persistence**: Years to decades for organochlorines; centuries for some heavy metals and radioactives

## Key Facts

- Apple orchards in Western Australia still produced unsafe levels of Aldrin and Dieldrin in free-range chicken eggs 18-20 years after chemical application ceased
- Attempts to grow prawns in ponds on former orchard land failed due to residual Dieldrin levels in soils
- Large quantities of lead, arsenic, copper, and persistent biocides were routinely applied to most apple orchards
- Copper in pasture plants under contaminated orchards can reach 50 ppm -- poisonous to sheep, causing toxaemic jaundice (liver poisoning) and blood in urine
- Arsenic stays near the soil surface in clay soils but can wash to subsoil (50-60 cm) in acid sands; phosphate application may dislodge arsenic to deeper levels
- Groundwater beneath farms can contain 80-90 biocidal substances from fertilisers, sprays, and fuels
- Molybdenum, zinc, and sulphur may buffer copper uptake in livestock
- Lead contamination from vehicle exhausts affects soils within 100 m of roadways with 800-1000 cars daily

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- Any site selected for food production (including mushroom cultivation) should be assessed for prior chemical use -- old orchards, plantations, and industrial sites may be contaminated
- Permaculture design recommends avoiding all biocides and instead using integrated pest management, companion planting, and biological controls
- For contaminated sites, Mollison recommends non-food production (fuel forests, structural timber) for decades until remediation occurs
- Bioremediation using fungi (mycoremediation) is a promising technique for breaking down persistent organic pollutants in contaminated soils
- Raised beds with imported clean soil (30 cm deep, 40%+ organic content) can allow safe gardening on lead-contaminated urban sites
- Testing vegetables for residual toxic materials is essential when growing on land with any history of chemical use
- Rainwater harvesting is recommended over groundwater use in areas where aquifers may be contaminated with biocide residues
