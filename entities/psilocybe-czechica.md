# Psilocybe czechica

**Psilocybe czechica** is a bluing [[psilocybin-compound]]-containing mushroom species from Central Europe, first described from the Czech Republic. It is a relatively obscure member of the Psilocybe genus, representing the psilocybian mycoflora of Central European forest and grassland ecosystems.

## Taxonomy

- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Hymenogastraceae
- **Genus:** Psilocybe
- **Authority:** Noordeloos (2009)

The specific epithet *czechica* honors the Czech Republic, where the species was first collected and formally described. The species was recognized during taxonomic revisions of European psilocybian fungi, as molecular tools allowed researchers to distinguish cryptic species within what were previously considered single, widespread taxa.

## Description

### Cap (Pileus)
- 5 to 18 mm in diameter
- Conic to campanulate, sometimes becoming broadly convex with age
- Umbo usually present
- Hygrophanous: dark reddish-brown to chestnut-brown when moist, drying to a pale ochraceous or straw color
- Surface smooth, matte when dry, slightly viscid when wet
- Margin translucent-striate at maturity
- Bluish bruising develops on handling

### Gills (Lamellae)
- Adnexed attachment
- Moderately crowded with lamellulae of varying lengths
- Color progresses from pale grey-brown to dark violet-brown as spores mature
- Edge paler than the face, sometimes whitish

### Stipe (Stem)
- 30 to 60 mm long, 1 to 2 mm thick
- Cylindrical, equal, sometimes slightly flexuous (wavy)
- Base may be slightly thickened or subbulbous
- Surface: pale ochraceous to light brown with fine whitish longitudinal fibrils
- Strong bluing reaction when bruised, particularly at the base
- Partial veil: absent in mature specimens

### Spores
- Spore print: dark purplish-brown
- Spores: ellipsoid to subamygdaliform (somewhat almond-shaped)
- 11 to 14.5 × 6.5 to 8.5 µm
- Thick-walled (approximately 0.8 to 1.0 µm)
- Prominent apical germ pore
- Q value: approximately 1.5 to 1.9

### Microscopic Features
- Cheilocystidia: ventricose to broadly lageniform, sometimes with a narrow neck
- Pleurocystidia: scattered, similar in shape to cheilocystidia
- Pileipellis: cutis composed of cylindrical hyphae 3 to 6 µm wide
- Clamp connections: absent

## Distribution and Habitat

**Psilocybe czechica** is known from Central Europe, with confirmed collections from the Czech Republic. Its true distribution may extend into neighboring countries such as Slovakia, Poland, Austria, and southern Germany, where similar habitats and climate conditions exist.

### Habitat
- Found in grasslands, pastures, and open meadows
- Occasionally in forest clearings and along woodland edges
- Prefers nutrient-rich soils, often in areas with livestock grazing
- Fruits in autumn (September to November), following periods of cool rain
- Elevation range: typically 200 to 800 meters

### Climate
- Temperate continental climate with warm summers and cold winters
- Requires consistent autumn moisture for fruiting
- Tolerates freezing temperatures during late-season fruiting

## Chemistry

**P. czechica** contains the standard psilocybian alkaloid profile, though detailed analytical data from multiple collections is limited.

### Alkaloid Content
- [[psilocybin-compound]]: 0.2 to 0.6% dry weight (estimated)
- [[psilocin]]: trace amounts
- [[baeocystin]]: present in small quantities

The psychoactive potency appears moderate, comparable to other European grassland Psilocybe species such as [[psilocybe-semilanceata]], though comprehensive quantitative analyses are needed.

## Similar Species

**P. czechica** can be confused with several other bluing psilocybes found in Central Europe:

- **[[psilocybe-semilanceata]]** — the most common European liberty cap; larger, more conical cap, different spore dimensions, typically more potent.
- **[[psilocybe-bohemica]]** — also from Central Europe (Czech Republic); differs in cap shape, stipe characteristics, and microscopic features.
- **[[psilocybe-liniformans]]** — distinguished by its gelatinous partial veil appendages on the gill edges.
- **[[psilocybe-serbica]]** — Balkan species found in forest habitats rather than grasslands.

## Identification Notes

Microscopic examination is essential for confident identification of **P. czechica**. The combination of spore size, cystidia morphology, and habitat preference helps separate it from similar species. Molecular analysis using ITS barcoding provides the most reliable identification.

Key field indicators:
- Central European grassland habitat
- Strong bluing reaction on the stipe base
- Small to medium-sized fruiting bodies
- Autumn fruiting period

## Cultural and Ethnobotanical Significance

There is no documented history of traditional or cultural use of **P. czechica**. The species was identified as psychoactive through modern mycochemical research. Its significance lies primarily in its contribution to understanding the diversity and distribution of psilocybian fungi in Central Europe.

The Czech Republic has a notable history of mycological research, and the discovery of **P. czechica** reflects the rich tradition of fungal taxonomy in the region.

## Conservation Status

**P. czechica** has not been formally assessed for the IUCN Red List. As a species with a relatively restricted known range, it may be vulnerable to habitat loss from agricultural intensification, urbanization, and changes in land management practices.

## Legal Status

As with all [[psilocybin-compound]]-containing mushrooms, **P. czechica** is controlled under drug legislation. In the Czech Republic, psilocybin and psilocin are scheduled substances, and the collection and possession of psychoactive mushrooms is restricted.
