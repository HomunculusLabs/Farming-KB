## Lepiota (Parasol Mushrooms)

## Description

Lepiota is a genus of mushrooms commonly known as parasol mushrooms, named for their distinctive large, umbrella-shaped fruitbodies. While some Lepiota species are prized edibles (notably [[macrolepiota-procera]], the true parasol), the genus also contains dangerously toxic species containing amatoxins. In [[mushroom-cultivation]], Lepiota species are noted for requiring or benefiting from microbial interactions in [[casing-soil]] to trigger fruitbody formation, placing them among the more challenging genera to cultivate.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Phylum**: Basidiomycota
- **Class**: Agaricomycetes
- **Order**: Agaricales
- **Family**: Agaricaceae
- **Genus**: Lepiota

## Key Facts

- Common name: parasol mushrooms, due to large umbrella-shaped caps
- Some species are prized edibles (Macrolepiota procera), while others are lethally toxic
- Lepiota species require or benefit from microbial casing interactions for fruiting
- The genus includes both Lepiota sensu stricto (smaller species, many toxic) and Macrolepiota (larger, often edible)
- Fruits terrestrially in grasslands, pastures, and woodland edges
- Known to benefit from microbial partners in casing soil, similar to Agaricus and Stropharia

## Relevance to Cultivation

Lepiota species are among the mushrooms that require or benefit from microbial interactions to trigger fruiting, making their cultivation more complex than wood-decomposing species. Successful cultivation typically involves casing soil treated with microbial slurries derived from native soil communities. The dilution-to-extinction method can be used to identify the specific microbial partners that promote fruiting.

## Relevance to Mycology

The Lepiota genus is significant in taxonomic mycology as the type genus for understanding parasol mushroom morphology and development. The presence of both edible and highly toxic species within the genus makes it important for mycological education and foraging safety. The microbial fruiting requirement provides another model system for studying fungal-microbial symbiosis.

## Safety Warning

Several Lepiota species contain lethal amatoxins. Positive identification to species level is essential before any consideration of consumption. Small Lepiota species (under 10 cm cap diameter) should generally be considered potentially toxic.
