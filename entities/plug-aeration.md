## Plug Aeration (Lawn)

## Description

Plug aeration is a mechanical lawn care procedure in which a specialized machine removes 2-inch-long cylindrical plugs (cores) of soil from the lawn, creating holes throughout the turf. In [[the-soil-food-web]] framework, plug aeration is a critical restorative practice for lawns that have compacted soil — a condition that particularly damages fungal populations. The holes allow water, air, and organic amendments to penetrate to the root zone, and the pulled plugs are left on the surface to decay and return biology to the soil.

## Classification

- **Type:** Mechanical lawn cultivation practice
- **Equipment:** Core aerator / plug aerator machine
- **Plug dimensions:** Approximately 2 inches (5 cm) long
- **Timing:** Early spring, every 3–4 years
- **Purpose:** Soil compaction relief, microbial habitat restoration

## Key Facts

- Removes 2-inch plugs of soil from the lawn at regular intervals
- Creates holes that allow water, air, and organic food to penetrate into the root zone
- Particularly beneficial for restoring fungal populations — fungi are the most fragile soil organisms and the first to suffer from compaction
- Should be performed in early spring every 3–4 years
- Helps repair compaction caused by:
  - Weight of snow and ice during winter
  - Foot traffic from pets, children, and vehicles
  - Natural settling over time
- Pulled plugs should be left on the lawn surface to decay, returning soil biology and organic matter
- After plug aeration, apply organic microbe food (e.g., [[soybean-meal]] at 3–4 lbs per 100 sq ft) so it falls into the plug holes and delivers nutrition directly to the root zone
- Works synergistically with compost tea application and organic microbe food to restore [[soil-food-web]] health

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Plug aeration connects to mycological practice in several ways:

- **Fungal fragility:** Soil fungi are the most sensitive organisms to compaction — their hyphal networks are physically crushed when soil pore space collapses
- **Habitat restoration:** Aeration restores the air-filled pore spaces that [[fungal-hyphae]] require for growth and colonization
- **Inoculation pathway:** The holes created by plug aeration serve as direct conduits for fungal inoculants (compost tea, mycorrhizal spores) to reach the root zone
- **Decomposition cycle:** Left on the surface, pulled plugs are colonized by [[saprophytic-fungi]] and bacteria, returning microbial biomass to the upper soil layers
- **Mycorrhizal recovery:** Compacted soils that have lost mycorrhizal fungi can be re-inoculated through the channels created by plug aeration
