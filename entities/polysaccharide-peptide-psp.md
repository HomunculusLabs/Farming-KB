# Polysaccharide-Peptide (PSP)

**Type:** Biochemical Compound (Fungal Polysaccharide)
**Category:** Mushroom-Derived Immunomodulator
**Also known as:** PSP, Coriolus versicolor polysaccharide,
Yun Zhi extract, I'm-Yunity

## Overview
Polysaccharide-peptide (PSP) is a protein-bound polysaccharide
isolated from the deep-layer mycelial culture of Trametes versicolor
(formerly Coriolus versicolor), commonly known as the turkey tail
mushroom. First isolated in 1983 from the COU-1 strain by Chinese
researchers, PSP is one of the most extensively studied mushroom-derived
compounds for cancer therapy. It has been recognized as an official drug
by the Chinese Ministry of Public Health and has undergone extensive
clinical trials, particularly in China.

## Key Details

### Chemical Properties
- **Classification:** Proteoglycan (polysaccharide-peptide complex)
- **Molecular weight:** Approximately 100,000 daltons
- **Composition:** May contain at least four discrete molecules, all
  true proteoglycans. Differs from PSK in saccharide makeup: lacks
  fucose, contains arabinose and rhamnose
- **Polysaccharide chains:** True beta-glucans with mainly 1-4, 1-2,
  and 1-3 glucose linkages, plus small amounts of galactose, mannose,
  and arabinose linkages
- **Bioavailability:** Orally bioavailable
- **Source organism:** Trametes versicolor (turkey tail mushroom),
  cultured deep-layer mycelium, strain COU-1

### Mechanism of Action
- **T-cell activation:** PSP is a potent immunostimulator with
  specific activity for T-cells and antigen-presenting cells such as
  monocytes and macrophages
- **White blood cell stimulation:** Increases white blood cell
  counts, interferon-gamma, and interleukin-2 production
- **Delayed-type hypersensitivity:** Enhances delayed-type
  hypersensitivity reactions, indicating strengthened cellular
  immune response
- **Immunomodulation:** PSP's biological activity is characterized
  by its ability to activate multiple arms of the immune system
  simultaneously

### Clinical Trial Results
- **Gastric cancer:** Demonstrated efficacy in clinical trials for
  stomach cancer
- **Esophageal cancer:** Shown effective in oesophageal cancer
  treatment
- **Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC):** Extensively studied and
  shown efficacy in NSCLC treatment
- **Safety profile:** Extensively studied by Chinese scientists and
  oncologists with little evidence of side effects
- **Adjunct therapy:** Like PSK, PSP is used as an adjunct to
  standard radiotherapy and chemotherapy, reducing side effects

### Distinction from PSK
While PSP and PSK are both derived from T. versicolor mycelial
cultures, they are distinct compounds:
- PSP lacks fucose; PSK contains it
- PSP contains arabinose and rhamnose; PSK lacks them
- PSP was developed by Chinese researchers; PSK by Japanese
- Both are approximately 100,000 daltons in molecular weight
- Both show oral bioavailability and similar immunostimulant activity
- At least 10 protein-bound polysaccharides have been isolated from
  T. versicolor, but only PSK and PSP have been used in clinical
  trials

## Relevance
PSP is one of the most important mushroom-derived therapeutic compounds
in both traditional Chinese medicine and modern oncology. Its approval
as a drug by the Chinese Ministry of Public Health, combined with
extensive clinical trial data showing efficacy against multiple cancer
types with minimal side effects, makes it a key example of how fungal
compounds can be integrated into conventional cancer treatment protocols.
For cultivators, T. versicolor is relatively easy to grow, making PSP
accessible for both commercial production and home cultivation.
