# Craterellus ignicolor (*Flame Chanterelle*)

## Overview

Craterellus ignicolor, commonly called the flame chanterelle or flame
trumpet, is an edible ectomycorrhizal fungus in the family
Cantharellaceae. It is widely distributed across [[fungal-ecology-forest-floor]] in association
with hardwoods.

The species is distinguished by its bright orange to yellow-orange
funnel-shaped [[fungal-endemism-host-specificity]].

## Taxonomy and Morphology

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Cantharellales
- **Family:** Cantharellaceae
- **Genus:** *Craterellus*
- **Species:** *C. ignicolor* (R.H. Petersen) T.W. Henkel, R.H. Petersen
  & A. Matheny (original basionym: *Cantharellus ignicolor* R.H. Petersen,
  1975)

**Cap (pileus):** 2–8 cm wide, convex to plane with age, developing a
central depression and eventually becoming funnel-shaped. Surface smooth
to slightly wrinkled, bright orange to orange-yellow, often paler toward
the margin. Margin wavy and irregular, sometimes incurved when young.

**Hymenium:** Located on the underside (inner surface of the funnel).
Smooth to shallowly wrinkled when young, developing low, blunt ridges
with age. Color pale yellow to buff, contrasting with the brighter
exterior.

**Stipe (stem):** 2–5 cm long, 0.5–1.5 cm thick, tapering downward,
solid, colored similarly to the cap or slightly paler. Often curved or
eccentric due to growth habit on the forest floor.

**Flesh:** Thin (2–5 mm), firm and waxy, pale orange-yellow. Odor
mildly fruity or apricot-like. Taste mild and pleasant.

**[[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]], from eastern Canada
(Québec, Ontario) south to Florida, and west to Minnesota and Texas.
It is most common in the [[fagus-grandifolia]]*),
hickory (*Carya* spp.), and other hardwoods. Found in mixed deciduous
and oak-hickory forests, often on well-drained sandy or loamy soils.

**Fruiting season:** June through October, peaking in July–September.
Fruiting is triggered by warm, wet conditions. It often fruits in the
same locations as other chanterelles (*Cantharellus* spp.) and
yellowfoots (*C. tubaeformis*).
