## Overview

Hypoxylon fuscum is a pyrenomycetous ascomycete fungus in the family Xylariaceae,
commonly found as a saprotrophic decomposer on dead hardwood branches and logs
in temperate and [[fungal-dead-wood-diversity]] —
is characteristic of many Xylariaceae and has significant ecological implications.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Kingdom:** Fungi — **Phylum:** Ascomycota — **Class:** Sordariomycetes**
— **Order:** Xylariales — **Family:** Xylariaceae — **Genus:** Hypoxylon

Hypoxylon fuscum (Pers.) Fr. was originally described by Christiaan Hendrik
Persoon and later sanctioned by Elias Magnus Fries. The genus Hypoxylon
contains approximately 130 species worldwide, with H. fuscum belonging to the
H. fuscum species complex within section Annulata.

Stromata are pulvinate to effused-reflexed, 1–5 cm broad and 2–5 mm thick,
superficial at first and becoming erumpent through the host bark. The surface
is dark brown to nearly black, roughened, with a granular to tuberculate
texture.

The stromatal tissue is carbonaceous, with a dark brown to black interior.
When sectioned, the tissue shows concentric zonation reflecting seasonal
growth layers, similar to tree rings.

Perithecia are immersed in the stromatal tissue, spherical to broadly
ovoid, 0.3–0.5 mm in diameter. Ostioles are papillate, dark, and slightly
protruding from the stromatal surface.

Asci are cylindrical to clavate, 120–180 µm long, with an apical ring that
stains blue in iodine (J+). Ascospores are unicellular, ellipsoid to
inequilateral, dark brown to olive-brown, 18–25 × 7–10 µm, with a straight
germ slit running the length of the spore on the convex side.

## Distribution and Habitat

H. fuscum is widely distributed across temperate and boreal regions of the
Northern Hemisphere, with confirmed records from Europe, [[antifungal-secondary-metabolites-coprophilous-fungi]] characteristic of the
Xylariaceae, including cytotoxic and [[staphylococcus-aureus]] and [[bacillus-subtilis]].
