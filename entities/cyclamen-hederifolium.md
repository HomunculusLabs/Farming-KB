## Cyclamen hederifolium

## Overview

Cyclamen hederifolium, commonly known as ivy-leaved cyclamen or neapolitan cyclamen, is a tuberous herbaceous perennial in the family Primulaceae. It is native to the Mediterranean region, where it grows in woodlands, rocky slopes, and scrubland.

The species is the most widely cultivated cyclamen and is considered the easiest to grow in gardens. Its autumn-blooming habit and attractive, ivy-patterned leaves make it a popular ornamental plant.

Cyclamen hederifolium is remarkable for flowering before or simultaneously with leaf emergence. The delicate flowers appear in late summer and autumn, while the beautifully marbled leaves develop afterward.

The plant is extremely cold-hardy and can survive temperatures below minus 15 degrees Celsius. This hardiness has made it one of the most successful Mediterranean geophytes in temperate gardens worldwide.

The genus Cyclamen contains approximately 23 species distributed around the Mediterranean basin. Cyclamen hederifolium has the largest natural range of any species in the genus.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Cyclamen hederifolium was first described by the Italian botanist Giovanni Amedeo Francesco Sebastiano Politanò in 1768. The specific epithet hederifolium refers to the ivy-like shape of the leaves.

The species was formerly known as Cyclamen neapolitanum, a name still encountered in older horticultural literature. The current name has nomenclatural priority and is universally accepted.

The plant grows from a flattened, disc-shaped tuber that can reach 15 centimeters in diameter. The tuber grows partially or fully above the soil surface, with roots arising from the upper surface and sides.

Leaves are 3 to 10 centimeters long and broadly cordate to hastate in outline. The leaf margin is coarsely toothed and the tip is typically acute or acuminate.

The leaf blade is dark green with a prominent silver or grey marbled pattern along the veins. This variegation varies considerably between individual plants and populations.

Leaf patterning includes forms with an all-silver central zone, all-silver margin, or irregular splashes. Horticultural selection has produced many named cultivars based on leaf pattern variation.

The flowering stem is 8 to 15 centimeters tall and arises from the tuber before or with the leaves. Each stem bears a single nodding flower.

Flowers are 1.5 to 2.5 centimeters long with five reflexed petals and a prominent basal cup. Petals are typically pink to light magenta with a darker zone at the base.

A white-flowered form occurs naturally and is common in cultivation. White forms are designated as forma albiflorum and are genetically stable.

The fruit is a five-chambered capsule that splits open at maturity to release the seeds. The capsule coiling is driven by differential drying of the placental tissues.

Seeds are 3 to 4 millimeters in diameter, brown, and equipped with a fleshy elaiosome. The elaiosome attracts ants, which serve as the primary [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]] [[coleman-cold-hardiness-testing-ratings]], [[nitrogen-fixing-trees-and-shrubs]]. It tolerates full sun in cooler maritime climates but requires shade in warmer regions.

Well-drained soil is essential, as the tuber is susceptible to rot in persistently waterlogged conditions. The species performs well in both alkaline and neutral soils.

Planting depth is critical: the tuber should be set with its upper surface at or slightly above soil level. Planting too deeply is the most common cause of cultivation failure.

Propagation from seed is straightforward but slow. Fresh seed germinates in late autumn or the following spring, with flowering plants produced in two to three years.

The species self-seeds prolifically in favorable garden conditions. Ants disperse seeds to new locations, leading to naturalized colonies in rock gardens and woodland settings.

Many named cultivars and selections exist, primarily distinguished by flower color and leaf pattern. Notable selections include Album, Silver Arrow, and Pewter Moon.

The plant is virtually pest-free and requires no regular maintenance. The deciduous habit of autumn flowering and winter foliage makes it an ideal component of seasonal shade gardens.
