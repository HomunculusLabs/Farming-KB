## Description

**Penicillium roseo-purpureum** is a mold species within the genus Penicillium (Ascomycota). It is notable for its demonstrated ability to colonize and degrade radioactive "hot particles" — carbon-based radioactive debris from nuclear reactor incidents. Alongside [[cladosporium-cladosporioides]], it was identified as one of the key fungal species capable of overgrowing and decomposing these highly radioactive particles at the Chernobyl exclusion zone.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Eurotiomycetes
- **Order:** Eurotiales
- **Family:** Aspergillaceae
- **Genus:** Penicillium
- **Species:** P. roseo-purpureum

## Key Facts

- **Hot particle degradation:** Shown to overgrow radioactive "hot particles" (carbon-based radioactive debris from the Chernobyl reactor) with activities below 1147 Bq of g-activity, destroying them within 50–150 days (Zhdanova et al., 1991).
- **Chernobyl isolation:** Isolated from soils around the Chernobyl Atomic Electric Station during 15 years of monitoring, as part of a collection of approximately 2000 strains spanning 180 species across 92 genera.
- **Radiation tolerance:** Capable of surviving and remaining metabolically active in areas of elevated radioactivity, demonstrating the broader resilience of fungal communities to nuclear contamination.

## Ecological Role

As a saprotrophic soil fungus, P. roseo-purpureum participates in organic matter decomposition. Its ability to degrade radioactive carbon-based debris places it among a select group of fungi with potential utility in the biological remediation of nuclear contamination sites.

## Relevance to Mycology and Cultivation

- **Nuclear bioremediation:** Its capacity to colonize and degrade hot particles suggests a potential role in biological cleanup strategies for nuclear accident sites.
- **Extreme environment mycology:** Represents an example of fungal adaptation to extreme anthropogenic environmental conditions.
- **Fungal community ecology at Chernobyl:** Part of the altered microfungal community structure documented around Chernobyl, where community complexity decreases with increasing radioactive dose while melanized and radiation-tolerant species become dominant.

## Distinguishing Features

- Rose to purplish colony pigmentation (as indicated by the species epithet)
- Penicillium-type conidiophores with brush-like spore-bearing structures
- Growth on and degradation of radioactive carbon-based substrates
- Tolerance to elevated ionizing radiation levels
