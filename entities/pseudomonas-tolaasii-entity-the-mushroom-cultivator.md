# Pseudomonas tolaasii

## Taxonomy

Pseudomonas tolaasii is a Gram-negative rod-shaped bacterium belonging to the class
Schizomycetes, order Pseudomonadales, family Pseudomonaceae. The genus name Pseudomonas
derives from the Greek "pseudes" meaning false or deceptive and "monas" meaning a single
unit, in reference to the variable forms of this single-celled bacterium. The genus
contains more than 140 identified species, though only a few are known to affect
mushrooms.

Pseudomonas tolaasii is one of the most significant bacterial pathogens affecting
[[fungi-cultivated-mushrooms-pathogens-competitors]]. It is the causative agent of bacterial blotch disease and is
commonly referred to by the common names Bacterial Blotch and Bacterial Pit (the latter
being a more severe form attributed to a related but unidentified Pseudomonas species).

## Morphology

Pseudomonas tolaasii is a cylindrical (bacilli) bacterium measuring approximately 0.4 to
0.5 by 1.0 to 1.7 microns. Like other members of the genus, it typically has one or
more flagella ("motile hairs") at one or both of its poles, which it uses for locomotion.
As a Gram-negative organism, it does not retain the crystal violet stain in Gram
staining procedures.

The genus Pseudomonas is characterized by variable cell shapes, including both
cylindrical (bacilli) and spherical (cocci) forms. Cells are extremely variable in
shape within the genus, which is reflected in the name's etymology.

## Habitat and Ecology

Pseudomonas tolaasii is ubiquitous in all soils and abounds in aqueous habitats. The
bacterium commonly parasitizes mushrooms that remain wet over a prolonged period of time.
Pseudomonas bacteria proliferate in standing water or anywhere there is moisture, making
the high-humidity environments of mushroom growing rooms ideal for propagation.

The organism is transmitted primarily through water and secondarily through grain, soils,
composts, flies, mites, nematodes, tools, and workers. Its ability to spread through
multiple vectors makes it a persistent challenge in [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]] facilities.

## Role in Mushroom Cultivation

Pseudomonas tolaasii is the cause of bacterial blotch that can devastate crops of
Agaricus and Psilocybe species. In a characteristic manner, Pseudomonas tolaasii causes
sunken grayish brown lesions on the mushroom cap in which a slimy fluid collects. These
lesions begin as yellowish spots or circular to irregular lesions that are superficial
in nature. They rapidly reproduce on wet mushrooms, becoming chocolate brown and slimy
with age. The bacterium produces a dull gray to mucus-like brownish slime and has a
mildly to strongly unpleasant odor.

Another Pseudomonas species, yet unidentified, has been implicated in the cause of a
more severe form of blotch known as Bacterial Pit. Additionally, Pseudomonas species
contaminate [[agaricus-brunnescens]]
yields. However, some researchers believe Pseudomonas fluorescens to be merely a variety
of Pseudomonas tolaasii and hesitate to recommend it as a control measure.

## Interactions

Pseudomonas tolaasii stands in contrast to its relative Pseudomonas putida, which is
beneficial to mushroom cultivation and stimulatory to [[growing-gourmet-primordia-formation-pinning]]. While P.
putida enhances fruiting in the casing layer, P. tolaasii is destructive to developing
fruitbodies.

The proposed [[fukuoka-textdoc-biological-control-fallacy-pheromone-critique]] using Pseudomonas fluorescens illustrates the complex
interactions within the Pseudomonas genus. The uncertainty about whether P. fluorescens
is truly a distinct species or merely a variety of P. tolaasii highlights the taxonomic
challenges within this genus.

Pseudomonas species are much more sensitive to heat sterilization than the
endospore-forming Bacillus species, which means standard [[query-difference-between-pasteurization-and-sterilization-mushroom-growing]]
procedures can be effective against them, though their ubiquitous presence in water and
soil means recontamination is a constant risk.

Some Pseudomonas species are pathogenic to humans. Of special note is Pseudomonas
aeruginosa (also known as Ps. pyocyanea), which causes blindness and other diseases.
This underscores the importance of maintaining hygienic conditions in mushroom growing
facilities, not only for crop protection but also for worker safety.
