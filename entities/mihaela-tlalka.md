## Description

Mihaela Tlalka is a researcher known for her contributions to understanding nutrient translocation dynamics in filamentous fungi, particularly the discovery and characterization of pulsatile amino acid transport in [[mycelial-networks]] of Phanerochaete velutina.

## Classification

- **Domain**: Mycological research
- **Specialty**: Nutrient transport physiology, fungal imaging
- **Affiliation**: University of Oxford (at time of publication)

## Key Facts

- First author of a landmark 2002 study demonstrating continuous imaging of amino acid translocation in intact mycelia of Phanerochaete velutina, revealing rapid pulsatile fluxes of 14C-labelled AIB
- Discovered that amino acid transport in fungal networks exhibits non-circadian oscillations, with complementary pulsation profiles in assimilatory versus foraging hyphae
- Showed that assimilatory mycelium on the inoculum and foraging mycelium both pulse but are out of phase with each other, suggesting coordinated but spatially differentiated transport regulation
- Demonstrated that pulsatile transport is particularly pronounced through corded systems, with phase domains showing locally synchronized behavior within the colony
- Contributed to the development of Fourier analysis techniques applied to PCSI data, enabling pixel-by-pixel mapping of transport frequency, amplitude, and phase across entire mycelial colonies
- Research used Phanerochaete velutina as a model organism, grown from both agar and wood-block inocula in microcosms with compressed sand or soil substrata
- Observed that pulsatile oscillations continued for 5-7 days in cord transport, superimposed on longer-term translocation trends
- Contributed to understanding route-switching behavior in cord networks, where pre-existing cords become transiently activated as transport routes

## Key Publications

- Tlalka, M., Watkinson, S. C., Darrah, P. R. & Fricker, M. D. (2002). Continuous imaging of amino acid translocation in intact mycelia of Phanerochaete velutina reveals rapid, pulsatile fluxes. New Phytologist 153, 173-84.
- Tlalka, M., Hensman, D., Darrah, P. R., Watkinson, S. C. & Fricker, M. D. (2003). Noncircadian oscillations in amino acid transport have complementary profiles in assimilatory and foraging hyphae of Phanerochaete velutina. New Phytologist 158, 325-35.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Tlalka's discovery of pulsatile nutrient transport fundamentally changed understanding of how fungi move resources through their mycelial networks. Rather than steady-state flow, nutrient translocation occurs in rhythmic pulses that may be coordinated across different regions of the colony. This has implications for understanding how cultivated fungi colonize substrates, respond to resource patches, and prioritize growth direction, all of which are relevant to optimizing spawn run conditions, substrate supplementation strategies, and [[fruiting-body-development]] in commercial mushroom cultivation.
