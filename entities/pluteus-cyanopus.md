## Pluteus Cyanopus

## Overview

*Pluteus cyanopus* is a psilocybin-containing mushroom in the family Pluteaceae, notable for being one of the few non-hymenogastraceous agarics known to produce indole alkaloids. It is commonly called the "blue-footed Pluteus" for the distinctive bluish staining that develops at the stipe base upon handling.

The species demonstrates that [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]].

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Pluteaceae
- **Genus:** *Pluteus* Fr.
- **Species:** *P. cyanopus* (Quél.) Kühner & Romagn.

The epithet *cyanopus* combines Greek *kyanos* (blue) and *pous* (foot), directly referencing the characteristic blue discoloration at the stipe base.

The species was originally described as *Agaricus cyanopus* by Quélet in 1872 and transferred to *Pluteus* by Kühner and Romagnesi in their 1953 revision of the genus.

## Morphology

### Pileus

Cap is 20–60 mm in diameter, hemispherical to convex, expanding to plano-convex with age. Surface is smooth and satiny, with a distinctive radial-fibrillose silvery sheen.

Color ranges from grayish-brown to dark brown at center, paler toward the margin. The margin is entire and slightly translucent-striate when moist. Surface is not hygrophanous, distinguishing it from many *Psilocybe* species.

### Lamellae

Gills are free from the stipe (a key diagnostic character of Pluteaceae), close to subdistant, with lamellulae in 2–3 tiers. They are initially white, maturing to pink and finally salmon-pink as spores develop.

This pink [[macrofungal-documentation-photography-spore-prints]].

### Stipe

Stipe measures 30–80 mm in length and 3–6 mm in diameter, central, cylindrical, and equal. Surface is white to pale gray, covered with fine longitudinal fibrils.

The base characteristically stains bluish-green to indigo when bruised or handled. This [[spore-print]] is pinkish-salmon. Spores are broadly ellipsoid to subglobose, smooth, and thin-walled.

Spore dimensions: 6–8 × 5–6.5 µm. Q range: 1.1–1.4. Cheilocystidia are numerous, cylindrical to ventricose with narrow necks, forming a sterile band on gill edges. Pleurocystidia are fusiform to lageniform.

## Distribution and Habitat

*P. cyanopus* has been documented across temperate Europe (Germany, France, Netherlands, Scandinavia) and in the Pacific Northwest [[fungal-diversity-leaf-litter-coarse-woody-debris]] debris of hardwoods including oak, beech, ash, and alder. Occasionally found on conifer substrates.

Fruiting occurs from late spring through autumn (May to October in the Northern Hemisphere), with peak abundance in late summer after sustained rainfall.

## Chemistry

### Bioactive Alkaloids

- **Psilocybin:** Present at 0.15–0.85% dry weight in European collections
- **Psilocin:** Present in fresh tissue; detectable in dried material at lower concentrations
- **Baeocystin:** Present as a minor constituent
- **Urea:** Present (non-psychoactive fungal metabolite)
