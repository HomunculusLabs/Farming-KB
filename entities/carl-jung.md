# Carl Jung

## Description

Carl Gustav Jung (1875–1961) was a Swiss psychiatrist and founder of analytical psychology.

He wrote the "Psychological Commentary" for W.Y. [[evans-wentz]]'s 1927 English edition of the Tibetan Book of the Dead.

Jung noted the convergence of Eastern and Western psychological understanding in the [[bardo-thodol]].

His concepts of archetypes, the collective unconscious, and individuation profoundly influenced psychedelic thought.

## Classification

- **Type**: Historical person — psychiatrist and depth psychologist
- **Nationality**: Swiss
- **Dates**: 1875–1961
- **Key Work**: "Psychological Commentary" on the Tibetan Book of the Dead
- **Role**: Bridge between Eastern contemplative traditions and Western psychology

## Key Facts

1. Carl Jung was born in 1875 in Kesswil, Switzerland.

2. He was initially a close collaborator of Sigmund Freud before their theoretical split in 1913.

3. Jung founded analytical psychology, emphasizing the exploration of the unconscious mind.

4. He wrote the "Psychological Commentary" for Evans-Wentz's 1927 edition of the Tibetan Book of the Dead.

5. In this commentary, Jung noted the remarkable convergence between Eastern and Western psychology.

6. He recognized that the Bardo Thodol described psychological processes that Western psychology was only beginning to understand.

7. Jung's concept of archetypes — universal patterns of imagery and experience — parallels the Bardo deities.

8. His concept of the collective unconscious suggests a shared psychic substrate underlying all human experience.

9. The process of individuation — integration of the total psyche — parallels the Bardo journey of liberation.

10. Jung's psychological framework provided a Western interpretive lens through which the Bardo Thodol could be understood.

## The Psychological Commentary

Jung's commentary on the Tibetan Book of the Dead was a landmark in East-West philosophical dialogue.

He argued that the Bardo Thodol was not merely a funeral text but a guide to the psychology of consciousness.

Jung recognized that the three Bardo states described psychological processes, not just post-mortem realms.

His reading opened the door to interpreting the Bardo Thodol as a map of altered states of consciousness.

This psychological interpretation was foundational for Leary's later psychedelic adaptation.

## Key Jungian Concepts

### Archetypes

Universal patterns of imagery and experience that emerge from the collective unconscious.

The Bardo deities can be understood as archetypal figures — universal psychic patterns.

### Collective Unconscious

A shared psychic substrate that contains the inherited potential for archetypal experience.

### Individuation

The lifelong process of integrating conscious and unconscious elements of the psyche.

Parallels the Bardo journey from ego-dissolution through visionary experience to reintegration.

## Relevance to Psychedelic Studies

Jung's psychological framework provided the conceptual bridge between Tibetan Buddhist practice and Western psychedelic use.

His commentary on the Bardo Thodol demonstrated that the text could be read as a psychological document.

This reading enabled Leary to map Bardo states onto psychedelic experience phases.

Jung's archetypes provide a framework for understanding the universal visionary content of psychedelic experiences.

The convergence Jung identified between East and West is exactly the synthesis *The Psychedelic Experience* attempts.

## Relation to Other Entities
