## Overview

**Lindera obtusiloba** (Japanese spicebush, blunt-lobed spicebush) is a deciduous shrub or small tree in the family Lauraceae, native to East Asia. The species is widely cultivated as an ornamental for its showy yellow spring flowers, attractive fall foliage, and winter branch structure.

In Korean traditional medicine (KKM), the bark and leaves of *L. obtusiloba* have been used to treat inflammatory conditions, digestive disorders, and circulatory ailments.

Autumn foliage color is excellent, ranging from bright yellow to orange and deep red.

Flowers appear in [[cervantes-selfing-single-seed-descent]] and is borne on a slender pedicel.

## Distribution and Habitat

*L. obtusiloba* is native to East Asia, with a distribution spanning Korea, Japan, and eastern China.

In Korea, it is found throughout the peninsula from lowlands to montane forests.

In Japan, the species occurs on Honshu, Shikoku, and Kyushu islands.

In China, it is distributed in provinces including Shandong, Anhui, Zhejiang, Jiangxi, and Hubei.

The species grows in deciduous and mixed forests, forest margins, and along mountain stream corridors.

Preferred elevations range from 100 to 2,000 meters, with optimal growth in mid-elevation montane forests.

It tolerates a wide range of soil types, from acidic forest soils to slightly calcareous substrates.

The plant prefers partial shade to full sun and moderate soil moisture.

## Chemistry and Pharmacology

The bark, leaves, and twigs of *L. obtusiloba* contain a diverse array of [[antifungal-secondary-metabolites-coprophilous-fungi]].

**Lignans** are the most extensively studied compounds, including (-)-syringaresinol, (+)-pinoresinol, lirioresinol B, and various furofuran-type lignans.

These lignans have demonstrated significant anti-inflammatory activity in both in vitro and in vivo models.

Flavonoids identified in the species include quercetin, kaempferol, and their glycoside derivatives.

The essential oil of leaves contains **linalool**, **geraniol**, **1,8-cineole**, and **alpha-terpineol** as major components.
