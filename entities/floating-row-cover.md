## Overview

Floating row cover is a lightweight, permeable fabric material used in agriculture and horticulture to protect crops from cold temperatures, wind, and pests while allowing light, air, and moisture to pass through. In the context of Eliot Coleman's winter-harvest system at [[four-season-farm]], floating row cover serves as the critical inner layer of protection inside unheated hoop houses, creating a "twice-tempered climate" that enables cold-hardy vegetable production through Zone 5 Maine winters.

## Physical Description

- **Material**: Lightweight spun-bonded or non-woven fabric (typically polypropylene)
- **Permeability**: Allows transmission of light, air, and moisture
- **Weight**: Light enough to rest directly on crops without damage (hence "floating")
- **Support System**: In Coleman's system, supported by flat-topped wire wickets at approximately 12 inches (30 cm) above the soil
- **Wicket Spacing**: Every 4 feet (120 cm) along the length of 30-inch-wide (75 cm) growing beds

## Function in the Winter-Harvest System

The floating row cover serves as the **inner layer** of a double-protection system:

### Outer Layer: Hoop House (Greenhouse)
- Standard 30×96-foot gothic-style hoop house
- Single-layer UV-resistant plastic covering
- Provides first level of wind protection and solar heat capture

### Inner Layer: Floating Row Cover
- Creates a microclimate within the greenhouse microclimate
- Traps radiant heat rising from the soil
- Dramatically reduces temperature extremes

### Temperature Moderation Effect

The combined effect is substantial. When outdoor temperatures drop to -15°F (-26°C):

| Location | Temperature |
|----------|-------------|
| Outdoors | -15°F (-26°C) |
| Inside hoop house | Approximately 0–5°F warmer than outdoors |
| Under inner row cover | 15–18°F (-10 to -8°C) |

This 30+ degree moderation transforms lethal cold into manageable conditions for cold-hardy crops.

### Additional Benefits

- **Increased humidity**: The inner layer raises relative humidity in the protected area, providing additional freeze protection
- **Wind exclusion**: Eliminates the desiccating effects of cold winter winds — often more damaging than cold itself
- **Pest exclusion**: Provides a physical barrier against flying insects when edges are sealed

## Crop Compatibility

The floating row cover inner layer is used with the full spectrum of cold-hardy vegetables in Coleman's system:

- **Leaf crops**: Spinach, lettuce, mache, claytonia, minutina, mizuna, tatsoi, arugula, sorrel, chard
- **Root crops**: Carrots, turnips, radishes
- **Brassicas**: Kale, pak choi, radicchio
- **Alliums**: Leeks, scallions, onion greens
- **Others**: Endive, escarole, chicory, parsley, watercress, collards, dandelion

Most of these crops tolerate temperatures down to 10°F (-12°C) or lower under protection.

## Types and Selection

Any lightweight floating row cover that allows light, air, and moisture transmission is suitable. Key selection criteria:

1. **Light transmission**: Must allow adequate photosynthetically active radiation (PAR)
2. **Air permeability**: Prevents excessive humidity buildup and disease
3. **Durability**: Must withstand repeated handling through the season
4. **Weight**: Light enough for easy deployment and removal during harvesting

## Historical Development

The use of row cover material as an inner protective layer in unheated greenhouses was pioneered by E. M. Emmert at the University of Kentucky in the 1950s. However, the concept lay dormant until Eliot Coleman independently developed and popularized it through his work at Four Season Farm beginning in the late 1970s.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

The floating row cover concept has direct applications beyond vegetable production:

- **Mushroom beds**: Row cover can moderate temperature and humidity for outdoor mushroom beds and tunnels
- **Spawn run protection**: Helps maintain stable conditions during mycelial colonization
- **Fruiting chamber modification**: Can be used to create microclimates within larger growing structures
- **Integrated systems**: Useful in combined vegetable-mushroom production setups where environmental control must be low-cost
