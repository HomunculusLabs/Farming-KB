## Description

Punga (*Bombax munguba*) is a large tropical tree recognized in [[amazonian-vegetalista-shamanism-degrees-and-hierarchy]] tradition for its ability to strengthen the *mann* (magical phlegm) — the primary spiritual substance used by healers and sorcerers for both curing and harming. In Amaringo's Vision 35 (Extracting Magical Darts from a Patient), the punga tree appears in the centre of the healing scene, its presence reinforcing the vegetalista's magical powers during the extraction of virotes (magical darts) from the patient's body.

The tree provides physical habitat for the *bufro-avispa* (dolphin-wasp), a species of wasp that nests in the punga's branches and whose venom causes prolonged fever lasting for days. Healers use the icaro of this wasp to cure sorcery, demonstrating the interconnected relationship between the tree, its insect inhabitants, and the vegetalista's therapeutic practice. The punga tree thus serves as both a source of spiritual power enhancement and a living pharmacy hosting multiple medicinal species that contribute to the healer's repertoire.

## Classification

- **Domain**: Plant entity; spiritual/medicinal tree
- **Scientific name**: *Bombax munguba* Mart. & Zucc.
- **Family**: Malvaceae (formerly Bombacaceae)
- **Common names**: Punga, pau-de-balsa, muxuiba, imbira-de-sapo
- **Cultural role**: Mann-strengthening tree; host of medicinal wasp species
- **Properties**: Spiritual empowerment; bark and wood with medicinal uses
- **Habitat**: Amazonian lowland forests, especially along rivers and floodplains (varzea)

## Key Facts

- The punga tree **makes the mann (magical phlegm) stronger**, enhancing the healer's primary spiritual tool for curing
- Appears in Vision 35 as the central tree during the extraction of magical darts from a patient's body
- The bufro-avispa (dolphin-wasp) nests in the punga's branches, creating a symbiotic ecological relationship
- The dolphin-wasp is described as very daring, following people even into the water without hesitation
- Its venom brings on fever for days, which is why healers use its icaro to cure sorcery through sympathetic magic
- The wasp's icaro is part of the vegetalista's repertoire of curing songs for treating magical afflictions
- As a member of the bombacoid group, it is related to other spiritually significant trees like the lupuna
- The tree grows in riverine and floodplain habitats, making it readily accessible to riverine communities
- Its lightweight wood is used traditionally for making floats, rafts, and other buoyant objects
- The tree produces large showy flowers with numerous stamens, characteristic of the *Bombax* genus
- Seed pods contain silky kapok-like fibers used traditionally for stuffing pillows and insulation
- The punga is a prominent component of varzea (flooded forest) ecosystems throughout the Amazon basin
- The healers are described as being on a "diamond platform" during the healing, with the punga nearby

## Botanical Characteristics

- Large deciduous tree reaching 20-35 meters in height with a spreading crown
- Trunk often slightly buttressed at the base, especially in floodplain specimens
- Bark is light gray and relatively smooth, sometimes with vertical fissures developing with age
- Leaves are palmately compound with 5-7 leaflets, each leaflet 10-20 cm long
- Flowers are large, showy, white to cream-colored, with numerous prominent stamens
- Fruit is an ellipsoid capsule containing seeds embedded in silky fibers (kapok)
- Common in seasonally flooded forests (varzea) along the Amazon River and its tributaries
- Drops leaves during the dry season, producing spectacular floral displays on bare branches
- Wood is very light and soft, traditionally used for floats, canoes, and rafts due to its buoyancy
