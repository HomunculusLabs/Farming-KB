The species forms extensive reddish-brown to dark green mats on the bark of trees, rocks, and occasionally brickwork. Its distinctive lobule shape makes it readily identifiable in the field.

Frullania is a large genus with approximately 350 described species, distributed primarily in tropical and subtropical montane forests. Frullania dilatata is among the most temperate-tolerant species in the genus.

The genus exhibits remarkable morphological diversity across its range, with tropical species often displaying larger and more ornate lobules than their temperate relatives.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Frullania dilatata was first validly described by Carl Linnaeus in 1753 as Jungermannia dilatata. It was later transferred to the genus Frullania by Raddi in 1818.

The genus name honors Leonardo Frullani, an 18th-century Italian patron of botany. The specific epithet dilatata refers to the dilated or expanded shape of the lobules.

Frullania dilatata is a leafy liverwort with prostrate, irregularly branched stems. Individual stems are 1–3 cm long, reddish-brown to almost black in color.

The leaves are succubous, overlapping in a shingle-like arrangement. Each leaf is divided into a larger upper lobe and a much smaller, helmet-shaped lobule.

The lobule is the most distinctive feature, appearing as a small water-sac or helmet attached to the base of each leaf. In Frullania dilatata, the lobule is inflated and roughly spherical.

The underleaves are small, bifid, and approximately half the width of the stem. They are present on all branches and help distinguish Frullania from morphologically similar leafy liverwort genera.

The leaf margins are entire or slightly toothed near the apex. The leaf cells are small, approximately 15–20 μm in diameter, with thin walls and distinct trigones.

Oil bodies are present in leaf cells, typically 2–4 per cell, appearing as granular or segmented structures. These oil bodies are important diagnostic characters for species identification within Frullania.

Male and female reproductive structures are produced on separate branches. The perianth is ovoid and prominently keeled, with a contracted mouth.

## Distribution and Habitat

Frullania dilatata has a widespread distribution across Europe, Asia, North Africa, and North America. It is one of the most commonly collected Frullania species in temperate regions.

In Europe, it is particularly abundant in western and southern regions with oceanic climates. It is less frequent in continental interiors and rare in the far north.

In North America, the species occurs primarily in the [[bolete-fungi-appalachian-region]], and along the Great Lakes. It is largely absent from the central plains and southeastern coastal plain.

The species is a obligate or facultative epiphyte, growing primarily on the bark of deciduous and coniferous trees. Preferred host trees include oak, ash, elm, maple, and various conifers.

It also colonizes stone walls, brickwork, and concrete in urban environments. The species shows a preference for base-rich substrates and is frequently found on mortared walls.

In urban settings, Frullania dilatata is one of the few bryophytes that persists on regularly maintained brick and stone surfaces. It tolerates periodic disturbance better than most other epiphytic liverworts.

Optimal growth occurs in sheltered, humid microsites with moderate light levels. It is most abundant in the lower trunk and root flare zone of host trees.

The species is relatively tolerant of desiccation compared to many other leafy liverworts. This tolerance allows it to persist on exposed bark surfaces during dry periods.

## Reproduction and Life Cycle

Sexual reproduction involves both antheridia and archegonia, produced on separate branches of the same or different plants. The androecia are short, spike-like branches with bract-like protective leaves.

Fertilization requires a water film for sperm transfer. Sporophytes are produced seasonally, most commonly in spring and early summer.

The sporophyte consists of a short seta and an ovoid capsule. The capsule dehisces into four valves when mature, releasing the spores.

Spores are approximately 12–16 μm in diameter with a finely papillose surface. They are dispersed by wind and can remain viable for several months.

Vegetative reproduction occurs through stem fragmentation and the production of caducous leaf lobules. Detached lobules can develop into new plants under favorable moisture conditions.

The species also reproduces through the formation of adventitious branches at stem tips and nodes. These branches can break off and establish new colonies.

Gemmae are not produced, unlike some other leafy liverwort genera. [[comparison-soil-protozoa-vs-nematodes-in-nutrient-cycling]] within the forest canopy.

As an epiphyte, Frullania does not parasitize its host trees but obtains nutrients from atmospheric deposition, rainwater, and canopy throughfall. It plays a role in intercepting and retaining nutrients within the forest ecosystem.
