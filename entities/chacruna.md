## Chacruna

## Description

Chacruna is a common name used in the Peruvian Amazon for plant species whose leaves are added to ayahuasca brews as admixture plants. In the context documented by Dobkin de Rios, chacruna is identified as Banisteriopsis rusbyana (now more commonly classified as Diplopterys cabrerana), the leaves of which contain dimethyltryptamine (DMT). Chacruna serves as the primary DMT source in many ayahuasca preparations, working synergistically with the beta-carboline-containing Banisteriopsis caapi vine.

## Classification

- **Common name:** Chacruna
- **Scientific identification:** Banisteriopsis rusbyana (per Dobkin de Rios / Schultes); modern classification: Diplopterys cabrerana
- **Family:** Malpighiaceae
- **Active compounds:** Dimethyltryptamine (DMT), 5-MeO-DMT
- **Role in ayahuasca:** DMT-containing admixture plant

## Key Facts

- Chacruna leaves contain dimethyltryptamine (DMT), a powerful visionary compound
- Identified by Schultes as Banisteriopsis rusbyana (personal communication cited by Dobkin de Rios)
- The leaves are boiled together with the ayahuasca vine (Banisteriopsis caapi) for several hours to produce the ayahuasca brew
- DMT is orally inactive without a monoamine oxidase inhibitor (MAOI), which is provided by the beta-carbolines in B. caapi — this synergistic combination is the pharmacological basis of ayahuasca
- Der Marderosian (1968) confirmed the presence of DMT in B. rusbyana leaves
- Chacruna is one of several admixture plants used in ayahuasca preparation; others include toé (Datura suaveolens)
- The use of chacruna as a named admixture reflects sophisticated ethnobotanical knowledge among Amazonian healers regarding synergistic plant combinations
- The pharmacological interaction between chacruna (DMT source) and the ayahuasca vine (MAOI source) represents one of the most remarkable examples of indigenous pharmacological knowledge in the world

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Chacruna is of central importance to entheogenic plant studies because:

1. **Ayahuasca pharmacology:** Understanding chacruna is essential for understanding how ayahuasca works — the DMT in chacruna leaves is the primary visionary compound, activated by beta-carbolines in the vine
2. **Ethnobotanical sophistication:** The indigenous discovery and use of this specific plant combination demonstrates advanced empirical plant knowledge
3. **Cultivation potential:** Diplopterys cabrerana is cultivated by healers and could be subject to conservation concerns as ayahuasca use expands globally
4. **Chemical ecology:** DMT occurs across multiple plant families, raising questions about its ecological function and the convergent evolution of psychoactive compounds
5. **Modern research:** DMT from chacruna and related species is a major focus of contemporary psychedelic research
