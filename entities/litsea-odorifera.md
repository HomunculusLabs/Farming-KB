## Overview

**Litsea odorifera** (syn. *Litsea cubeba* var. *odorifera*) is an evergreen shrub or small tree in the family Lauraceae, native to mountainous regions of southern and southwestern China.

It is valued for its intensely fragrant foliage and branches, which release a sweet, lemon-like aroma when crushed. The species is closely related to *Litsea cubeba* (May Chang), sharing many of the same aromatic compounds but distinguished by larger leaves and a more compact growth habit.

In traditional Chinese medicine, the leaves and twigs of *Litsea odorifera* are used to treat respiratory ailments, digestive disorders, and joint pain. Preparations typically involve dried leaf decoctions or topical compresses of bruised foliage.

Regional ethnobotanical records document use of the bark as a stimulant and the fruit as a treatment for indigestion. The aromatic wood is occasionally used in incense production, particularly in Guangxi province.

The essential oil is rich in citral and has attracted commercial interest as a natural fragrance ingredient and potential antimicrobial agent.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Taxonomy:** Kingdom Plantae → Order Laurales → Family Lauraceae → Genus *Litsea* → Species *L. odorifera*.

The species was first described by H. Liu and is sometimes treated as a variety of *Litsea cubeba* due to overlapping morphological characters. Modern molecular phylogenies place it within the *Litsea* core clade, closely allied with *Litsea cubeba* and *Litsea glutinosa*.

The plant is an evergreen shrub reaching two to four meters in height, occasionally forming a small tree up to six meters. Branchlets are slender, green when young, becoming brown and glabrous with age.

The bark is smooth and grayish-brown on young branches, developing shallow fissures with maturity. The wood is lightweight and pale yellow, with a faint aromatic scent when cut.

Leaves are alternate, elliptic to lanceolate, seven to fourteen centimeters long and two to four centimeters wide. The leaf blade is papery, dark green and glossy above, pale green below, with prominent lateral veins in six to nine pairs.

Inflorescences are umbellate, borne in leaf axils, usually solitary or in pairs. Flowers are small, pale yellow to white, with six tepals arranged in two whorls.

The fruit is a drupe, ellipsoid, approximately eight millimeters in diameter, ripening from green to dark purple-black at maturity. Fruiting occurs from August through October.

## Ecology and Cultivation

*Litsea odorifera* provides food for several Lepidoptera species whose larvae feed on Lauraceae foliage. The fruits are eaten by birds, facilitating seed dispersal across forest landscapes.

The species plays a modest role in secondary [[plants-without-mycorrhizal-associations]] with [[aact-microbial-foliar-nutrient-uptake-co2-stomata-ingham]] in nutrient-poor forest soils.

Propagation is primarily by seed, which requires cold stratification or gibberellic acid treatment to break dormancy. Semi-hardwood cuttings taken in summer root at moderate success rates under mist propagation.

Commercial cultivation for essential oil production is limited but growing in southern China. Yields of fresh leaf material range from two to four tonnes per hectare, with essential oil content of one to two percent by fresh weight.
