## Erysiphe graminis

## Description

*Erysiphe graminis* (now reclassified as *Blumeria graminis*) is an obligate biotrophic fungal pathogen that causes **powdery mildew** on grasses and cereals. It is one of the most economically significant diseases of turfgrass, wheat, barley, and other Poaceae family members worldwide. In *Teaming with Microbes*, it is listed on page 146 among the turfgrass diseases that can be managed through biological soil practices rather than chemical fungicides.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Eukarya
- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Leotiomycetes (Erysiphomycetes)
- **Order:** Erysiphales
- **Family:** Erysiphaceae
- **Genus:** *Blumeria* (formerly *Erysiphe*)
- **Species:** *B. graminis* (syn. *E. graminis*)

## Key Facts

- **Obligate biotroph:** *E. graminis* can only grow on living host tissue. It cannot be cultured on artificial media, which makes studying and controlling it particularly challenging.
- Produces characteristic **white to grayish-white powdery growth** on the leaf surfaces of infected grasses. The powder consists of masses of conidia (asexual spores) that are readily dispersed by wind.
- **Host-specialized formae speciales:** Different strains specialize on different grass hosts — *f. sp. tritici* on wheat, *f. sp. hordei* on barley, *f. sp. avenae* on oats, and *f. sp. poae* on turfgrasses such as Kentucky bluegrass and bentgrass.
- **Favors cool, humid conditions** with moderate temperatures (15-25 C) and high humidity but not free water on leaf surfaces. Overcrowded plantings, poor air circulation, and excessive nitrogen fertilization promote disease development.
- **Haustorial feeding:** The fungus penetrates epidermal cells with specialized feeding structures called haustoria, extracting nutrients without immediately killing the host cell.
- Severely infected plants show **reduced photosynthesis, stunted growth, leaf yellowing, and premature senescence**. On turf, powdery mildew causes thinning and unsightly white patches.
- Listed in *Teaming with Microbes* (p. 146) as one of the turfgrass diseases suppressible through proper soil food web management.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Turfgrass management:** Powdery mildew caused by *E. graminis* is a major problem on shaded, poorly ventilated lawns and golf course turf. Biological management through improved air circulation, reduced nitrogen inputs, and fungally dominated compost tea applications can suppress the disease without chemical fungicides.
- **Cereal crop losses:** In wheat and barley, powdery mildew can cause yield losses of 10-40% in susceptible varieties under favorable disease conditions.
- **Fungally dominated compost tea:** *Teaming with Microbes* recommends applying fungally dominated compost tea to the leaf surface as a biological control strategy. The diverse microorganisms in the tea compete with *E. graminis* for leaf surface space and nutrients.
- **Nitrogen management:** Excessive nitrogen fertilization promotes lush, succulent growth that is highly susceptible to powdery mildew. Organic nitrogen sources that release slowly produce tougher, more resistant plant tissue.
- **Resistance breeding:** Major gene resistance (R-genes) has been extensively deployed in cereal breeding, but *E. graminis* rapidly evolves to overcome single-gene resistance.

## Ecological Role

As an obligate biotroph, *E. graminis* has evolved an intimate relationship with its grass hosts. In natural grassland ecosystems, powdery mildew acts as a population regulator, reducing the competitive dominance of susceptible grass species and maintaining plant community diversity. In managed systems, its impact is purely negative and requires active management.
