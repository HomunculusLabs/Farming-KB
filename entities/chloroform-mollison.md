# Chloroform (Water Contaminant)

## Description

Chloroform (trichloromethane, CHCl3) is a carcinogenic compound that forms when chlorine used to disinfect drinking water, bathing water, and swimming pools reacts with organic matter. Mollison identifies this as a significant health hazard of conventional water chlorination and contrasts it with natural pool purification systems using pebble bed filtration and reed-bed treatment.

## Classification

- **Category**: Chemical / Water Contaminant
- **Chemical class**: Trihalomethane (THM)
- **Chemical formula**: CHCl3
- **Formation mechanism**: Reaction of chlorine with organic matter in water
- **Health classification**: Probable human carcinogen (Group 2B, IARC)
- **Occurrence**: Treated drinking water, swimming pools, bathing water

## Key Facts

- Forms when chlorine (added as disinfectant) reacts with natural organic matter in water
- Classified as carcinogenic; long-term exposure through drinking, bathing, and swimming increases health risks
- One of several disinfection byproducts (DBPs) including bromoform and dichlorobromomethane
- Conventional swimming pools using chlorine produce chloroform in measurable quantities
- Mollison contrasts chlorinated systems with natural pool designs that use biological filtration instead
- Natural pool alternative uses pebble bed filtration, reed-bed nutrient removal, and cascade oxygenation
- Natural pools also provide fire-fighting reserves, heat pump sources, and emergency water supplies

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Water quality**: Chloroform and other chlorine byproducts in irrigation water can affect soil microbial communities and mycorrhizal fungi
- **Natural pool design**: Permaculture water purification using reed beds and biological filters aligns with principles applicable to greywater treatment for garden use
- **Fungal sensitivity**: Many beneficial fungi are sensitive to chlorine and its byproducts; chlorine-free water supports better soil biology
- **Mushroom cultivation**: Chlorine-free water is preferred for mushroom substrate preparation and misting systems
- **Reed-bed systems**: Plants like Scirpus and Phragmites used in biological water purification also create habitat for edible and medicinal fungi
- **Compost tea**: Chlorine-free water is essential for brewing effective compost teas rich in beneficial microorganisms
