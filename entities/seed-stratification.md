## Description

Seed stratification (also known as vernalization when applied to flowering induction) is the process of subjecting seeds to a period of cold, moist conditions to break dormancy and trigger germination. The technique mimics the natural winter conditions that cold-climate seeds experience at the litter level in forests and marshes, typically at temperatures of 0-5 C (32-40 F).

## Classification

- **Category:** Seed treatment technique
- **Domain:** Horticulture, silviculture, [[plant-propagation]]
- **Also known as:** Cold stratification, vernalization, cold conditioning
- **Temperature range:** 0-5 C (32-40 F)
- **Duration:** Autumn to spring (weeks to months depending on species)

## Key Facts

### Natural Process

In nature, seeds from boreal, temperate, and cold-climate species fall to the forest floor in autumn and experience prolonged cold and moisture through winter. This period of chilling breaks internal chemical inhibitors (typically abscisic acid) and allows the embryo to prepare for germination when spring warmth arrives.

### Artificial Stratification Methods

Mollison describes several practical approaches:

1. **Refrigerator method:** Place seeds in sand, peat, or moist medium in a refrigerator at 0-5 C from autumn to spring. Apple seeds in sand and chestnuts in peat respond well. Pot out as shoots appear.
2. **Cold valley planting:** Sow seeds in sand or peat in cold, shaded valleys where natural winter temperatures achieve stratification.
3. **Open cool trellis:** In warm climates, suspend seed containers under open trellis structures to provide the coolest possible conditions.
4. **Aquatic stratification:** Wild rice and other soft aquatic seeds are stored in open ponds or under water in a refrigerator.

### Monitoring and Planting

Seeds under stratification should be checked in late winter and spring for signs of germination. As soon as radicles emerge, seeds must be potted or planted out promptly.

### Complementary Techniques

- **Heat treatment:** Many tree legume seeds respond to heating in an oven at 95 C (200 F) for 10-20 minutes, or pouring near-boiling water over them, or burning them in a light straw fire. This cracks hard seed coats.
- **Animal digestion:** Older gardeners feed seeds to chickens or cattle, collect the manure, make a slurry, and sow it. The digestive passage provides combined acid/alkali treatment, hot/cold cycling, mechanical cracking in teeth or bird crops, and manure packaging.

### Seed Germination and Temperature

Soil temperature profoundly affects germination speed:

- Between 0-38 C, increasing soil temperature can reduce germination time to one-tenth of that needed in cold soils.
- Most vegetable seeds germinate well at 15-20 C (59-68 F).
- Exceptions: Celery refuses to germinate above 24 C (74 F); cucurbits, beans, and subtropicals will not germinate below 10 C (50 F).

### Light Effects on Germination

Some seeds also require light exposure:

- Carrots need a definite quantity of light and are surface-planted.
- Pre-soaking seeds overnight followed by one day under light before covering can satisfy light requirements.
- Wild tobacco germinates from a mere flash of light (as when turning a clod of soil).

## Relevance to Cultivation

1. **Tree establishment:** Most temperate fruit and nut trees require stratification; understanding this prevents propagation failures.
2. **Climate adaptation:** Growers in warmer climates can grow cold-climate species by artificially providing the chill requirement.
3. **Local seed sourcing:** Choosing locally adapted varieties ensures correct day-length and chill-hour matching.
