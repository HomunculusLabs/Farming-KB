The species exhibits heterophylly, producing both submerged and floating leaf forms depending on water depth and growing conditions.

Callitriche palustris plays an important ecological role in freshwater ecosystems as habitat for invertebrates and as food for waterfowl.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Callitriche palustris was described by Linnaeus in 1753. The genus Callitriche was traditionally placed in its own family Callitrichaceae but is now classified within Plantaginaceae based on molecular phylogenetic studies.

The species is highly variable morphologically, and several former subspecies have been elevated to species rank.

The taxonomy of Callitriche remains complex, with ongoing debates about species boundaries and hybridization.

Stems are slender, rooting at the nodes, and can reach 30 to 60 centimeters in length.

Submerged leaves are linear to narrowly lanceolate, 5 to 15 millimeters long, arranged in opposite pairs along the stem.

Floating leaves, when produced, are broader and ovate, 6 to 12 millimeters long, forming rosettes at the water surface.

The transition between submerged and floating leaf forms occurs gradually along the stem as it approaches the surface.

Flowers are minute and unisexual, lacking petals and sepals, and are borne in the leaf axils.

Each flower consists of a single pistil or two stamens enclosed within a small bract.

The fruit is a schizocarp approximately 1.5 millimeters in diameter, splitting into four nutlets at maturity.

## Distribution and Habitat

Callitriche palustris has a circumpolar distribution in the Northern Hemisphere.

In Europe, it occurs from the British Isles and Scandinavia southward to the Mediterranean region.

In Asia, it ranges across Siberia, northern China, and Japan.

The species grows in a wide variety of freshwater habitats, including ponds, lake margins, slow streams, ditches, and spring-fed pools.

It prefers shallow water, typically 5 to 50 centimeters deep, with sandy or gravelly substrates.

Callitriche palustris is tolerant of a range of water chemistry conditions, from slightly acidic to moderately alkaline.

It often colonizes seasonally flooded habitats and can survive temporary desiccation.

## Ecology and Reproduction

Callitriche palustris flowers from April through September, depending on latitude and local conditions.

Pollination occurs underwater or at the water surface, making it one of the few truly aquatic insect-pollinated plants.

The pollen grains are elongated and released in strands that become entangled on the stigmas of female flowers.

This unique underwater pollination mechanism was among the first documented examples of hydrophily.

Seeds are dispersed by water currents and by adherence to the feet and feathers of waterfowl.

Vegetative reproduction through stem fragmentation is also important, especially in flowing-water habitats.

The dense growth form of Callitriche palustris provides microhabitat for aquatic invertebrates, including mayfly nymphs, water beetles, and snails.

Waterfowl, including mallards and teal, consume both the vegetative parts and seeds.

The plant serves as an indicator species for clean, well-oxygenated freshwater habitats.

## Traditional Uses

Callitriche palustris has limited recorded use in human traditions compared to more conspicuous aquatic plants.
