## Description

Ganoderma is a genus of polypore fungi belonging to the family Ganodermataceae (order Polyporales). Commonly known as "reishi" (Japanese) or "lingzhi" (Chinese), Ganoderma species are among the most important medicinal mushrooms in traditional East Asian medicine and modern nutraceutical science. The genus includes both lacquered, shelf-like bracket fungi and softer, stalked species. Ganoderma lucidum and Ganoderma lingzhi are the most widely cultivated and studied species.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Eukaryota
- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** [[basidiomycota|Basidiomycota]]
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Polyporales
- **Family:** Ganodermataceae
- **Genus:** Ganoderma

## Key Facts

- The name "Ganoderma" derives from Greek: "ganos" (brightness/shine) and "derma" (skin), referring to the lacquered appearance of many species.
- Ganoderma species are lignicolous (wood-inhabiting) fungi that decompose hardwood trees and stumps in nature.
- Unlike [[oyster-mushroom|Pleurotus]] (oyster mushrooms), a high percentage of Ganoderma strains fruit readily in cultivation, making strain selection somewhat less critical than for Grifola frondosa.
- Major bioactive compounds include triterpenoids (ganoderic acids), polysaccharides (β-D-glucans), and ganoderans, which have demonstrated immunomodulatory, anti-inflammatory, hepatoprotective, and antitumor properties.
- G. lucidum has been used in traditional Chinese medicine for over 2,000 years, documented in the earliest pharmacopoeia texts.

## Cultivation Notes

In the context of the [[maitake-cultivation-guide]] by [[alice-w-chen|Chen (1999)]], Ganoderma spp. (reishi) is mentioned for comparison:

1. **Strain reliability:** Ganoderma strains generally fruit more reliably than Grifola frondosa strains, whether isolated from natural habitats or obtained from culture collections.
2. **Cultivation method:** [[synthetic-log-cultivation|Synthetic-log cultivation]] methods similar to those used for G. frondosa are employed, using [[hardwood-sawdust-substrate|hardwood sawdust-based substrates]] in polypropylene bags.
3. **Growth form:** Ganoderma produces antler-like or conk-shaped fruiting bodies rather than the overlapping cap clusters characteristic of maitake.
4. **Market significance:** Reishi is one of the most commercially important medicinal mushrooms globally, widely sold as dried whole mushrooms, extracts, powders, and dietary supplements.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Ganoderma serves as an important reference point in medicinal mushroom cultivation:

1. **Benchmark species:** Its relative ease of fruiting compared to G. frondosa highlights the challenges specific to [[maitake-cultivation|maitake cultivation]].
2. **Substrate compatibility:** Both genera thrive on hardwood sawdust substrates, allowing shared cultivation infrastructure.
3. **Medicinal significance:** Alongside G. frondosa and Lentinula edodes (shiitake), Ganoderma is among the top three medicinal mushrooms by commercial value.
4. **Research model:** Ganoderma has been extensively studied for its bioactive compounds, providing a framework for investigating the medicinal properties of other cultivated fungi.
5. **Cultivation economics:** Global Ganoderma production exceeds several billion USD annually, with major production centers in China, Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan.

## Bioactive Compounds

Ganoderma produces a remarkable array of pharmacologically active compounds that have attracted significant scientific attention:

1. **Triterpenoids (ganoderic acids):** Over 150 distinct triterpenoids have been identified from Ganoderma species. These bitter-tasting compounds exhibit hepatoprotective, antihypertensive, and anti-inflammatory activities. The ganoderic acid profile varies between species and cultivation conditions.
2. **Polysaccharides (β-D-glucans):** Water-soluble polysaccharides, particularly β-D-glucans, are the primary immunomodulatory agents. They activate macrophages, natural killer cells, and T-lymphocytes.
3. **Ganoderans:** Glycoprotein compounds with demonstrated hypoglycemic (blood sugar-lowering) effects in animal studies.
4. **Sterols and sterol esters:** Including ergosterol and its derivatives, which contribute to the overall medicinal profile.

## Cultivation Methods

Commercial Ganoderma cultivation employs several distinct approaches:

1. **Synthetic-log method:** The most common commercial technique, using supplemented hardwood sawdust in bag culture. This method allows year-round production under controlled conditions.
2. **Wood-log cultivation:** Traditional outdoor method using hardwood logs (oak, plum, and other species). Logs are inoculated with spawn and buried partially in soil. This method produces higher-quality fruiting bodies but requires longer cultivation cycles (6-12 months).
3. **Spore production:** Some operations focus on collecting Ganoderma spores, which are marketed as a premium product. Spore oil extraction is a specialized sub-industry in Chinese herbal medicine.
4. **Mycelium fermentation:** Submerged liquid culture for biomass and polysaccharide extraction, used primarily in the nutraceutical industry.

## Notable Species

- **G. lucidum / G. lingzhi:** The classic "reishi" — lacquered, kidney-shaped conks
- **G. tsugae:** Found on hemlock trees in North America
- **G. applanatum:** "Artist's conk" — non-lacquered, woody bracket
- **G. curtisii:** Yellow-tinted, southeastern North America
- **G. oregonense:** Large species native to Pacific Northwest

## Taxonomic History

The taxonomy of Ganoderma has undergone significant revision. For many years, the name G. lucidum was applied broadly to lacquered Ganoderma species worldwide. Molecular studies have since shown that the European G. lucidum sensu stricto is distinct from the Asian species used in traditional medicine, which is now properly designated G. lingzhi. North American "G. lucidum" represents additional distinct species. This taxonomic complexity has implications for medicinal research, as different species produce different profiles of bioactive compounds.
