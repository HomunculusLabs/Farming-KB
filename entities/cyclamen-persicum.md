The leaf underside is typically purplish-red or green with reddish tones. Leaf petioles are 5 to 20 cm long, slightly grooved on the upper surface, and emerge from the center of the tuber.

The species is diploid with 2n = 48 chromosomes, one of the lower chromosome counts in the genus. Polyploid breeding lines have been developed for commercial use, including triploid and tetraploid cultivars with larger flowers.

Flowers are borne singly on long pedicels arising from the tuber. The corolla is 2 to 5 cm long in wild plants, with five reflexed petals that are typically white to pink or mauve, often with a darker pink or magenta zone at the base.

A distinct dark reddish-purple eye marks the base of each petal. The petals have prominent auricles at their base that form a small cup around the pistil. Flowering occurs from autumn to spring in native populations.

The fruit is a globose capsule 1.5 to 2 cm in diameter that splits open at maturity. Seeds are dispersed by ants, which are attracted to the fatty elaiosome attached to each seed.

Within the genus Cyclamen, C. persicum is placed in subgenus Cyclamen alongside species such as C. hederifolium and C. purpurascens. Molecular phylogenetic studies have confirmed its position in a well-supported clade of Mediterranean species.

## Distribution and Habitat

**Cyclamen persicum** is native to the eastern Mediterranean region, with a distribution extending from southern Turkey through Syria, Lebanon, Israel, the Palestinian territories, and western Jordan. It also occurs on several Aegean islands including Rhodes, Crete, and Cyprus.

The species grows in a range of habitats from sea level to approximately 1,200 meters elevation. Typical habitats include rocky hillsides, scrubland, olive groves, pine woodlands, and rocky outcrops in garrigue and phrygana vegetation.

In its native range, C. persicum is a plant of winter rainfall regions. Growth occurs during the cooler, wetter months from autumn through spring, with the plant entering dormancy during the hot, dry summer.

The species tolerates a wide range of soil types but prefers well-drained, rocky or sandy substrates. It is frequently found growing in rocky crevices, cliff faces, and among stone walls where drainage is excellent.

Calcareous soils are preferred in most of its native range, though the species also occurs on acidic substrates on some Greek islands.

Wild populations have declined in some areas due to habitat destruction from urbanization and agricultural expansion. Collection from the wild for the horticultural trade has also impacted some populations, though commercial cultivation has largely eliminated this pressure.

The species is remarkably tolerant of diverse microclimates within its range. Coastal populations experience mild, wet winters while inland populations at higher elevation endure colder temperatures and occasional frost.

## Cultivation and Uses

**Cyclamen persicum** is one of the most popular flowering houseplants in [[panax-ginseng]]. Pliny the Elder also referenced the plant in his Natural History, warning of its toxicity when consumed in excess.
