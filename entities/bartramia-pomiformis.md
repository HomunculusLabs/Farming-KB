# Bartramia pomiformis

## Overview

Bartramia pomiformis, commonly known as apple moss, is a distinctive acrocarpous moss
in the family Bartramiaceae. The species is immediately recognizable by its globose,
apple-shaped capsules that give it both its common name and its specific epithet.

The moss forms loose, silky-green tufts on acidic soil, rocks, tree bases, and
rotting wood in forests and moorlands. It is widely distributed across temperate
and boreal regions of the Northern Hemisphere.

Bartramia pomiformis was named in honor of John Bartram (1699-1777), the early
American botanist and explorer often called the father of American botany. The genus
Bartramia contains approximately 60 species distributed worldwide.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Bartramia pomiformis Hedw. is the type species of the genus Bartramia, family
Bartramiaceae, order Bartramiales, class Bryopsida. The family is characterized by
its distinctive globose capsules and elongate, narrowly lanceolate leaves.

Plants form loose, tufted cushions 3 to 8 centimeters tall. The stems are erect,
simple or sparingly branched, with a dense tomentum of reddish-brown rhizoids at
the base.

Leaves are lanceolate, 5 to 9 millimeters long, with a strongly serrulate margin
in the upper portion. The leaf nerve (costa) is strong, filling most of the leaf
width, and extends to or slightly below the leaf apex.

When dry, the leaves are crisped, contorted, and loosely appressed to the stem.
When moist, they spread widely, giving the plant an open, starry appearance.

Laminal cells are unipapillose, quadrate to short-rectangular in the upper leaf,
becoming elongate and pellucid toward the base. The alar cells are differentiated,
forming a well-defined group at the leaf base.

The capsule is the most diagnostic feature: globose to subglobose, 2 to 3
millimeters in diameter, borne on a yellowish seta 1 to 2 centimeters long.
The capsule appears apple-shaped when young and is borne erect. The operculum is
long-rostrate, giving the capsule a beaked appearance.

Spores are spherical, 14 to 20 micrometers in diameter, finely papillose.
Maturation occurs in spring to early summer. The peristome is double, with 16
teeth divided to the base.

## Distribution and Habitat

Bartramia pomiformis has a circumboreal distribution across the Northern Hemisphere.
It occurs throughout Europe, northern Asia, and [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]], extending southward
in mountainous regions.

In Europe, it is widespread from Scandinavia and the British Isles southward to
the Mediterranean mountains and eastward to the Caucasus. In North America, it
ranges from Alaska and northern Canada to the northeastern and Appalachian United
States, with disjunct populations in the Rocky Mountains.

The species grows on acidic substrates in shaded to partially shaded locations.
Typical habitats include forest floors, rotting [[query-how-to-grow-gourmet-mushrooms-outdoors-on-logs-and-stumps]], rock crevices,
tree bases, and banks along streams and roadsides.

It is most abundant in coniferous and mixed forests with acidic soils, but also
occurs in open moorlands and heathlands. It is found at elevations from sea level
to approximately 2000 meters.

Bartramia pomiformis shows a preference for humid microclimates but tolerates
periodic drying. It is often found in association with other acid-loving mosses
such as [[dicranum-scoparium]] and [[pleurozium-schreberi]].
