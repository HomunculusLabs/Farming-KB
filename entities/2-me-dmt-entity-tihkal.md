## [[indole]] MT (2,N,N-Trimethyltryptamine)

TIHKAL entry #34. 2-Me-[[dmt]], also known as 2,N,N-trimethyltryptamine (TMT) or desmethoxy-Indapex, is a synthetic tryptamine with a methyl group at the 2-position of the indole ring. It produces an unusual pharmacological profile characterized by tactile enhancement, sexual amplification, and subtle auditory distortion, rather than the classic visual psychedelic effects associated with most tryptamines.

## Synthesis

Two synthetic routes are documented. The primary route uses 2-methylindole in TBME treated with oxalyl chloride in dichloromethane to form the glyoxyl chloride. This intermediate is reacted with 40 percent aqueous dimethylamine in dichloromethane. The resulting 2,N,N-trimethylindoleglyoxamide is crystallized from warm isopropyl acetate/cyclohexane (mp 167-170 degrees C). Reduction with Red-Al in toluene under nitrogen gives the free base, which is converted to the fumarate salt (mp 205-208 degrees C).

An alternate synthesis starts from 3-methylindoleacetic acid, proceeding through the ethyl ester, reduction to the ethanol, conversion to the ethyl bromide with PBr3, and finally reaction with dimethylamine. The free base from this route has a reported melting point of 97-98 degrees C.

## Pharmacological Profile: A Tactile Stimulant

2-Me-DMT defies easy classification. It does not produce visual effects, cognitive clouding, or the classic psychedelic experience at any reported dose level. Instead, its primary effects are somatic: whole-body tingling, heightened skin sensitivity, enhanced sexual response, and a generalized activation of the tactile system.

At 75 mg, the sexual enhancement is particularly notable, with marked amplification of both pre-orgasmic and orgasmic phases persisting across multiple sessions over three hours. This effect, combined with mental clarity and absence of visual distortion, suggests a pharmacological profile quite different from typical 5-HT2A agonist psychedelics.

Shulgin characterized the compound as a potential "tactile stimulant" with applications for augmenting sexual pleasure rather than as a treatment for impotence. This distinguishes it from classical aphrodisiacs and places it in a rarely explored pharmacological category.

## Historical Context

2-Me-DMT was explored by Shulgin as part of the systematic investigation of 2-substituted tryptamines. The compound's unusual tactile-stimulant profile — characterized by whole-body tingling, heightened skin sensitivity, and sexual enhancement rather than visual psychedelic effects — represented a novel pharmacological category that did not fit neatly into existing classification schemes.

## Modern Significance

The tactile-stimulant and sexual-enhancement properties of 2-Me-DMT continue to generate interest in both research and recreational contexts. The compound demonstrates that tryptamine chemistry can produce effects far beyond the classic psychedelic profile, opening possibilities for therapeutic applications in sexual medicine and somatic therapy.

The auditory effects at moderate doses, paralleling those of 2-Me-DET and DIPT, support the hypothesis that 2-position substitution on the indole ring selectively modulates auditory processing pathways.
