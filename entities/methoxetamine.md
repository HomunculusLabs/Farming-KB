---# Methoxetamine

## Overview

Methoxetamine ([[mxe]]) is a synthetic dissociative compound of the arylcyclohexylamine
class, structurally related to [[blesching-cannabis-safety-adverse-effects-evidence]] including bladder toxicity, psychological dependence, and
fatal overdoses. The compound remains a significant case study in novel
psychoactive substance (NPS) [[mckenna-modest-proposal-drug-policy-reform]].

## Chemical Identity

- **IUPAC name:** 2-(3-Methoxyphenyl)-2-(ethylamino)cyclohexanone
- **CAS number:** 1239943-76-0
- **Molecular formula:** C₁₅H₂₁NO₂
- **Molecular weight:** 247.33 g/mol
- **SMILES:** COC1=CC=CC(=C1)C2(CCCCC2(=O)NCC)
- **Appearance:** White to off-white crystalline powder; sold as powder,
  capsules, or tablets
- **Solubility:** Soluble in water, [[ethanol]], and lipid membranes

## Pharmacology

- **NMDA receptor antagonism:** Primary mechanism — blocks the NMDA glutamate
  receptor channel, producing dissociative anesthesia, analgesia, and perceptual
  distortions; estimated potency 3–5× ketamine in vitro
- **DAT inhibition:** Blocks dopamine reuptake, contributing to stimulant-like
  effects and euphoria not typically seen with ketamine at equivalent doses
- **SERT inhibition:** Blocks serotonin reuptake, potentially adding to mood
  elevation but also increasing serotonergic risk in combination with other drugs
- **Sigma-1 receptor agonism:** May contribute to antidepressant and
  anti-anxiety effects; similar activity observed in ketamine metabolites
- **Opioid receptor activity:** No significant binding to mu, delta, or kappa
  opioid receptors at recreational doses

## Dosage and Administration

- **Common routes:** Oral, intranasal, sublingual, rectal, and intramuscular;
  oral bioavailability is notably higher than ketamine (~50–70% vs ~20%)
- **Threshold dose (oral):** 5–10 mg
- **Light dose (oral):** 10–20 mg
- **Common dose (oral):** 15–30 mg
- **Strong dose (oral):** 30–50 mg
- **Heavy dose (oral):** 50+ mg (high risk of adverse effects)
- **Intranasal:** Approximately 1.5–2× potency of oral route
- **Duration:** 3–5 hours oral, 1–3 hours intranasal; significantly longer than
  ketamine (45–90 min IV/IM, 1–2 hr oral)
- **After effects:** Residual dissociation, cognitive fog, or mood changes may
  persist 6–24 hours after primary effects subside

## Effects

- **Physical:** Analgesia, anesthesia at high doses, numbness, motor
  impairment, nausea, elevated heart rate and blood pressure, pupil dilation,
  nystagmus, loss of coordination
- **Cognitive:** Dissociation from body and environment, [[psilocybin-ego-dissolution-mechanisms]],
