# Methoxetamine

## Overview

Methoxetamine (MXE) is a synthetic dissociative compound of the arylcyclohexylamine
class, structurally related to [[ketamine]] and tiletamine with the addition of a
3-methoxy group and an N-ethyl group replacing the N-methyl of ketamine. First
synthesized and sold online around 2010, it was marketed as a "research chemical"
and gained popularity as a ketamine substitute with reportedly longer duration and
greater oral bioavailability. MXE acts primarily as an NMDA receptor antagonist but
additionally exhibits activity at the dopamine and serotonin transporters (SERT and
DAT), distinguishing its pharmacological profile from ketamine. Its recreational
use declined sharply after scheduling in multiple countries and increasing reports
of severe [[blesching-cannabis-safety-adverse-effects-evidence]] including bladder toxicity, psychological dependence, and
fatal overdoses. The compound remains a significant case study in novel
psychoactive substance (NPS) [[adverse-effects-and-harm-reduction-psilocybin]] and [[mckenna-modest-proposal-drug-policy-reform]].

## Chemical Identity

- **IUPAC name:** 2-(3-Methoxyphenyl)-2-(ethylamino)cyclohexanone
- **CAS number:** 1239943-76-0
- **Molecular formula:** C₁₅H₂₁NO₂
- **Molecular weight:** 247.33 g/mol
- **SMILES:** COC1=CC=CC(=C1)C2(CCCCC2(=O)NCC)
- **Appearance:** White to off-white crystalline powder; sold as powder,
  capsules, or tablets
- **Solubility:** Soluble in water, ethanol, and lipid membranes

## Pharmacology

- **NMDA receptor antagonism:** Primary mechanism — blocks the NMDA glutamate
  receptor channel, producing dissociative anesthesia, analgesia, and perceptual
  distortions; estimated potency 3–5× ketamine in vitro
- **DAT inhibition:** Blocks dopamine reuptake, contributing to stimulant-like
  effects and euphoria not typically seen with ketamine at equivalent doses
- **SERT inhibition:** Blocks serotonin reuptake, potentially adding to mood
  elevation but also increasing serotonergic risk in combination with other drugs
- **Sigma-1 receptor agonism:** May contribute to antidepressant and
  anti-anxiety effects; similar activity observed in ketamine metabolites
- **Opioid receptor activity:** No significant binding to mu, delta, or kappa
  opioid receptors at recreational doses

## Dosage and Administration

- **Common routes:** Oral, intranasal, sublingual, rectal, and intramuscular;
  oral bioavailability is notably higher than ketamine (~50–70% vs ~20%)
- **Threshold dose (oral):** 5–10 mg
- **Light dose (oral):** 10–20 mg
- **Common dose (oral):** 15–30 mg
- **Strong dose (oral):** 30–50 mg
- **Heavy dose (oral):** 50+ mg (high risk of adverse effects)
- **Intranasal:** Approximately 1.5–2× potency of oral route
- **Duration:** 3–5 hours oral, 1–3 hours intranasal; significantly longer than
  ketamine (45–90 min IV/IM, 1–2 hr oral)
- **After effects:** Residual dissociation, cognitive fog, or mood changes may
  persist 6–24 hours after primary effects subside

## Effects

- **Physical:** Analgesia, anesthesia at high doses, numbness, motor
  impairment, nausea, elevated heart rate and blood pressure, pupil dilation,
  nystagmus, loss of coordination
- **Cognitive:** Dissociation from body and environment, [[psilocybin-ego-dissolution-mechanisms]],
  conceptual thinking, enhanced introspection, memory disruption, difficulty
  forming new memories during acute effects
- **Emotional:** Euphoria, empathy, anxiety or paranoia (dose-dependent),
  mood elevation, sense of peace or connectedness
- **Perceptual:** Visual distortions, closed-eye imagery, altered sense of
  time and space, synesthesia-like experiences at higher doses, mania or
  psychosis in susceptible individuals

## Adverse Effects and Risks

- **Bladder toxicity:** Reports of cystitis, urinary frequency, urgency, and
  painful urination similar to ketamine-induced bladder damage; mechanism likely
  involves urothelial toxicity from NMDA antagonism and metabolites
- **Psychological dependence:** Users report compulsive redosing and difficulty
  stopping despite negative consequences; tolerance develops rapidly
- **Cognitive impairment:** Persistent memory, attention, and executive function
  deficits reported in heavy users
- **Psychosis:** High doses or prolonged use can precipitate psychotic episodes,
  particularly in individuals with personal or family history of psychiatric
  conditions
- **Overdose:** Fatalities documented; symptoms include respiratory depression,
  cardiovascular collapse, seizures, and hyperthermia
- **Serotonin syndrome:** Risk when combined with serotonergic drugs (SSRIs,
  MAOIs, MDMA, tryptamines) due to SERT inhibition
- **Injection risks:** Intravenous or intramuscular use carries additional risks
  of infection, abscess, and tissue damage

## Legal Status

- **International:** Not scheduled under the UN Convention on Psychotropic
  Substances; several countries have enacted national controls
- **UK:** Controlled under the Psychoactive [[controlled-substances-act-and-psychedelic-research]] 2016; Class B drug
  since 2015 under temporary class drug order
- **EU:** Scheduled in many member states; EMCDDA risk assessment published 2012
- **USA:** Not federally scheduled but may be prosecuted under the Federal
  Analog Act; several states have enacted specific bans
- **China:** Scheduled as a narcotic drug since 2015
