## Wilhelm Reich

## Description

Wilhelm Reich (1897–1957) was an Austrian psychoanalyst, a member of Sigmund Freud's inner circle, and a radical thinker who developed the concept of "orgone energy" and advocated for sexual liberation as essential to human freedom. In entheogenic discourse, Reich is cited as a key figure who recognized the connection between sexual repression and the suppression of indigenous spirituality.

## Classification

- **Domain**: Psychology / Social Philosophy
- **Category**: Person (psychoanalyst, social critic)
- **Born**: March 24, 1897, Dobzau, Austria-Hungary
- **Died**: November 3, 1957, Lewisburg, Pennsylvania, USA
- **Key Works**: "The Function of the Orgasm," "The Mass Psychology of Fascism"

## Key Facts

1. **Freud's Inner Circle**: Reich was a prominent member of Freud's psychoanalytic movement. He expanded on Freud's thesis that "cultural achievements result from sublimated sexual energy" but diverged by arguing that sexual suppression was not necessary for civilization — pointing to highly cultured societies where sexual suppression was non-existent.

2. **Sex-Political Criticism**: Reich developed a "sex-political criticism of culture" that identified the systematic repression of human sexuality as a tool of patriarchal control — the same mechanism used to suppress entheogenic plant use and indigenous spiritual practices.

3. **Indigenous Spirituality Advocate**: Reich recognized that the attack on individual freedom operated at the root level of sexuality and spiritual expression. He identified the repression of natural human instincts as an "atrocity against mankind."

4. **Government Persecution**: Reich was imprisoned by the U.S. government, died mysteriously in prison, and his books were confiscated and burned in incinerators by the FDA — an act of censorship paralleling the historical destruction of knowledge by religious and political authorities.

5. **Freud-Reich Split**: The divergence between Freud (who argued sexual repression was necessary for cultural advancement) and Reich (who argued it was the tool of patriarchal oppression) mirrors the larger conflict between authoritarian control and individual spiritual freedom.
