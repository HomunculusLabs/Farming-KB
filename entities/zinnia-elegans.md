# Zinnia elegans (*Zinnia elegans*)

## Overview

Zinnia elegans, commonly known as the common zinnia or elegant zinnia, is an
annual herbaceous plant in the family Asteraceae. Native to Mexico, it is one
of the most commercially important ornamental annuals grown worldwide, valued
for its vibrant, long-lasting flowers and exceptional heat tolerance.

The species has been extensively hybridized, producing hundreds of cultivars in
nearly every color except true blue. Flower forms range from simple single
daisies to fully double, cactus-flowered, and dahlia-flowered types. Zinnia
elegans blooms continuously from early summer until frost, making it a reliable
source of garden color and a major cut flower crop.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Clade:** Tracheophytes, Angiosperms, Eudicots, Asterids
- **Order:** Asterales
- **Family:** Asteraceae
- **Tribe:** Heliantheae
- **Genus:** Zinnia
- **Species:** Z. elegans

The species was first described by Jacquin in 1792 and later reclassified by
de Candolle. The genus name honors Johann Gottfried Zinn, an 18th-century
German anatomist and botanist. The specific epithet elegans means "elegant,"
referring to the showy inflorescences.

Zinnia elegans is a diploid species with 2n = 24 chromosomes. Molecular
phylogenetic analyses place the genus Zinnia within Heliantheae, closely
related to Heliopsis and Sanvitalia. Approximately 20 species comprise the
genus, distributed from the southwestern [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]] to [[herb-growing-guide]] 20–100 cm tall
depending on cultivar. The stems are stout, green to purplish, and typically
glabrous or sparsely hairy. The habit ranges from compact and bushy in dwarf
cultivars to tall and open in large-flowered types.

### Leaves

Leaves are opposite, ovate to lanceolate, 3–10 cm long and 1–4 cm wide, with
entire to slightly dentate margins. The [[aact-foliar-sticker-spreaders-leaf-surface-attachment-ingham]] is rough to the touch due
to short, stiff [[trichomes]], a trait that deters some herbivores. Leaf color is
medium to dark green, occasionally with a purplish tinge in certain cultivars.

### Flowers

Capitula are solitary at stem terminals, 4–15 cm in diameter depending on
cultivar. Wild-type flowers have 8–12 ray florets surrounding a prominent
central disc of yellow tubular florets. Cultivated double forms have ray
florets that are transformed into petaloid structures, sometimes completely
obscuring the disc.

Ray floret colors span white, yellow, orange, salmon, pink, red, crimson,
lavender, and purple. Some cultivars exhibit bicolor or striped patterns.
Individual flowers persist 7–14 days on the plant. The involucral bracts are
broadly ovate, 8–15 mm long, and united at the base.

### Fruit and Seed

Achenes are narrow, 7–12 mm long, compressed, and crowned with a persistent
pappus of two unequal awns. Seeds are brown to gray and weigh 3–5 mg each.
A single plant produces 500–3,000 seeds. Germination occurs in 5–7 days at
21–26°C, with seed viability lasting 3–5 years.
