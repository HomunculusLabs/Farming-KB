# Edgeworthia chrysantha

## Overview

Edgeworthia chrysantha, commonly called paper bush or oriental paperbush, is a deciduous shrub in the family Thymelaeaceae. It is native to China, where it grows in mountain forests and along stream banks at moderate elevations. The species is prized for its intensely fragrant yellow flowers that appear in late winter before the leaves emerge.

The plant is the source of *mitsumata* paper, a high-quality Japanese tissue paper made from its bark. This paper has been produced for centuries and is still used for banknotes, archival documents, and traditional artworks.

In cultivation, *Edgeworthia chrysantha* is valued as a winter-blooming [[hamilton-ornamental-shrub-care]] for woodland gardens. The species is hardy in USDA zones 7 through 9 and reaches 1.2 to 1.8 meters in height and spread.

The shrub has an open, spreading habit with flexible, reddish-brown branches. The bark is smooth and fibrous, a characteristic shared with other Thymelaeaceae members such as *Daphne* species.

Leaves are alternate, oblong-lanceolate, 8 to 20 cm long and 2 to 5 cm wide. They are dark green above and pale beneath, with prominent veins and entire margins.

The foliage is deciduous, dropping in late autumn to reveal the flower buds that have been forming since midsummer. These buds are covered in silky white hairs that provide winter protection.

Flowers are tubular, approximately 1.5 cm long, and bright yellow, arranged in dense terminal heads of 20 to 40 individual florets. Each flower head is approximately 4 to 5 cm in diameter.

The blooms are intensely fragrant with a sweet, spice-like scent detectable from several meters away. Flowering occurs from January through March, depending on climate.

Fruits are small dry drupes that are rarely produced in cultivation. The plant spreads primarily through vegetative means in garden settings.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Edgeworthia chrysantha* is native to central and southern China, including the provinces of Sichuan, Yunnan, Guizhou, Hubei, and Zhejiang. It occurs at elevations of 500 to 2,000 meters in mountainous regions.

The species grows in moist, well-drained soils along forest margins, stream banks, and in open woodlands. It prefers partial shade and is typically found in locations protected from cold, drying winds.

In Japan, the plant was introduced centuries ago for paper production and has become naturalized in some areas. It is particularly associated with the *washi* paper-producing regions of central Japan.

The species has been introduced to Western horticulture since the mid-19th century. It remains relatively uncommon in gardens outside of specialist collections and botanical institutions.

Wild populations in China have declined due to overharvesting of bark for paper production. Conservation efforts are focused on establishing cultivated supply chains to reduce pressure on natural stands.

## Cultivation

*Edgeworthia chrysantha* requires partial shade and consistently moist, humus-rich, well-drained soil. It performs best in locations protected from early morning sun, which can damage flowers after frost.

The shrub is hardy to approximately -12°C, though flower buds may be damaged below -8°C. [[query-how-do-i-grow-figs-in-cold-climates]], siting near a warm wall or in a courtyard provides necessary protection.

Soil should be acidic to neutral (pH 5.5 to 7.0) and enriched with [[comparison-leaf-mold-composting-vs-hot-composting-method]] or compost annually. The plant is intolerant of drought and requires regular irrigation during dry periods.

Propagation is primarily by softwood cuttings taken in early summer, which root readily under mist. Division of mature clumps in [[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]] is also effective.

[[green-cannabis-grow-bible-cloning-and-seed-propagation]] is possible but slow, requiring 12 to 18 months from sowing to flowering size. The cultivar 'Grandiflora' has larger flower heads and is the most commonly available form in the nursery trade.

The shrub requires minimal pruning, limited to removal of dead or damaged branches after flowering. It has no serious pest problems and is generally deer-resistant due to its fibrous, toxic bark.

In autumn, the foliage turns a clear yellow before dropping, adding seasonal interest. The silvery flower buds remain prominent throughout winter, providing garden structure during the dormant season.

## Uses and Ethnobotany

The primary non-ornamental use of *Edgeworthia chrysantha* is paper making. The inner bark yields long, strong fibers (2 to 4 mm) ideal for producing thin, durable paper sheets.

In Japan, this paper is called *mitsumata* (三椏) and has been used since the 16th century for banknote production. Japanese yen notes incorporate *mitsumata* fiber for its distinctive texture and durability.

The paper is also used for traditional *washi* crafts, shoji screens, and conservation-grade archival paper. Its insect-repellent properties make it especially suitable for document storage.

In traditional Chinese medicine, the flowers and roots are used to treat eye infections, inflammation, and skin disorders. The plant contains daphnoretin and other coumarin derivatives with documented antimicrobial activity.
