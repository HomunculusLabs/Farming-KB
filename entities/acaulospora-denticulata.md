## Acaulospora denticulata

## Description

*Acaulospora denticulata* is a species of arbuscular mycorrhizal fungus (AMF) in the family Acaulosporaceae, phylum Glomeromycota. It forms endomycorrhizal associations with a wide range of host plants, colonizing root cortical cells to produce characteristic arbuscules and vesicles. Research by Klironomos et al. (1997, 1998) demonstrated that this species responds differently to elevated atmospheric CO2 compared to other AMF genera, contributing to understanding of how mycorrhizal communities may shift under climate change scenarios.

## Key Facts

- *Acaulospora denticulata* produces characteristic asexual spores (azygospores) that are borne laterally on a hyphal stalk (suspensor) arising from the neck of a terminal or intercalary saccule, a diagnostic feature of the genus *Acaulospora*.
- In elevated CO2 experiments by Klironomos et al. (1997, 1998), *A. denticulata* showed different patterns of root colonization and extraradical hyphal growth compared to *Glomus* species and *Scutellospora calospora*. Unlike the two *Glomus* species tested, *A. denticulata* did not increase host plant growth under elevated CO2, but altered the form of the mycorrhizal association.
- The genus *Acaulospora* is distinguished from other AMF genera by spore ontogeny — spores develop laterally from the neck of a thin-walled saccule rather than terminally on a hyphal stalk.
- AMF in the genus *Acaulospora* are commonly found in tropical and temperate soils, often associated with acidic soil conditions and high organic matter content.
- These fungi are obligate symbionts that cannot complete their life cycle without a host plant, relying on carbon from photosynthesis to sustain their growth and spore production.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Climate change research:** *A. denticulata* is a model organism for studying how AMF communities may restructure under elevated CO2, with implications for predicting ecosystem responses to climate change.
- **Mycorrhizal community dynamics:** Species-specific responses to CO2 enrichment suggest that mycorrhizal community composition will shift in future atmospheres, potentially altering plant-fungal interaction networks and nutrient cycling.
- **Soil structure formation:** As with other AMF, *A. denticulata* contributes to soil aggregation through extraradical hyphal networks and glomalin production, enhancing soil stability and carbon storage.
- **Inoculum potential:** Understanding the growth responses of different AMF species to environmental variables is essential for developing effective mycorrhizal inoculum formulations for agriculture and restoration.
- **Biodiversity conservation:** The differential responses of *Acaulospora*, *Glomus*, and *Scutellospora* to CO2 enrichment highlight the importance of maintaining AMF diversity for ecosystem resilience.

## Ecological Role

*Acaulospora denticulata* forms arbuscular mycorrhizal associations with herbaceous plants, grasses, and some woody species. The arbuscules produced within root cortical cells serve as the primary sites of nutrient exchange, where the fungus transfers phosphorus and other nutrients to the plant in return for photosynthetic carbon. The extraradical mycelium extends into the soil matrix, exploring soil pores beyond the depletion zone around roots and contributing to soil aggregation.
