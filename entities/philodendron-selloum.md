# Philodendron selloum (Thermogenic Philodendron)

## Description

*Philodendron selloum* (now reclassified as *Thaumatophyllum bipinnatifidum*) is a large, evergreen perennial plant in the family Araceae, native to South America. It is notable among plants for its extraordinary thermogenic ability. During blooming, the inflorescence can reach temperatures up to 46 degrees C (115 degrees F) when ambient air is as low as 4 degrees C (39 degrees F).

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Family:** Araceae
- **Genus:** Thaumatophyllum (formerly Philodendron)
- **Species:** T. bipinnatifidum (formerly P. selloum)
- **Common names:** Tree philodendron, selloum, split-leaf philodendron, lacy tree philodendron

## Thermogenic Properties

P. selloum stores fats that are metabolically burnt to produce heat. During flowering:

- Inflorescence temperatures reach 46 degrees C (115 degrees F) against ambient air of 4 degrees C (39 degrees F)
- Temperature differential of approximately 42 degrees C above ambient
- Heat production serves to volatilize aromatic compounds that attract fly and beetle pollinators
- Among the highest recorded thermogenic outputs in the plant kingdom

## Habitat and Growth

Native to tropical and subtropical forests of South America, particularly Brazil, Paraguay, and Argentina. The plant grows as a large shrub or small tree-forming perennial with deeply lobed, spreading leaves up to 1.5 m long. It thrives in humid tropical and subtropical forests, partial shade to full sun, well-drained rich organic soils, and USDA zones 8b-11.
