# Glomerella cingulata

## Description

*Glomerella cingulata* is a species of [[ascomycota]] in the family
Phyllachoraceae and the teleomorphic (sexual) stage of the anamorphic fungus
*[[colletotrichum-gloeosporioides]]*. Together, this species pair represents one of
the most important causes of preharvest and postharvest diseases in both
temperate and tropical regions. The anamorph *C. gloeosporioides* is the most
common anthracnose pathogen in the tropics, causing disease on an enormous range
of wild and cultivated plants including avocado, citrus, mango, papaya, passion
fruit, guava, cocoa, and rubber (Holliday 1980; Dodd et al. 1992). Thirty-nine
species and numerous subspecies of *Colletotrichum* are recognized (Sutton
1992).

## Key Facts

- **Host Range:** Extraordinarily broad — causes anthracnose of avocado, citrus,
  mango, papaya, passion fruit, guava, cocoa, rubber, and numerous other wild
  and cultivated plants (Holliday 1980; Dodd et al. 1992). This wide host range
  makes it one of the most important plant pathogens in tropical agriculture.

- **Disease Symptoms:** Anthracnose symptoms are fairly conspicuous, typically
  consisting of sunken, irregularly shaped necrotic lesions on fruits, leaves,
  and stems (Holliday 1980). Lesions on fruits may be raised and cankerous,
  scablike, or wartlike in appearance. Branch die-back also may occur. The
  fungus can survive long periods on plant debris in the soil.

- **Field Recognition:** *Colletotrichum* species can be recognized
  macroscopically by the mucilaginous masses of often pink- or salmon-colored
  conidia oozing from acervuli associated with anthracnose lesions under humid
  conditions.

- **Microscopic Characters:** Acervuli frequently produce dark brown setae and
  hyaline, aseptate conidia that germinate to produce appressoria. Appressoria
  are specialized infection structures — melanized, dome-shaped cells that
  generate enormous turgor pressure to mechanically penetrate the host cuticle.

- **Appressoria:** A key diagnostic and functional feature. Appressorium
  production can be induced in the laboratory by placing conidia in a drop of
  water on a microscope slide in a moist chamber overnight. This simple
  technique aids in species identification.

- **Culture:** *Colletotrichum* species are grown readily in culture, but
  production of the *Glomerella* (teleomorphic) stage is less consistent. This
  is a common pattern in the Glomerellales — the sexual stage is often not
  observed in nature or culture.

- **Species Complex:** *C. gloeosporioides* is now recognized as a species
  complex containing multiple [[cryptic-species]] that differ in host range and
  geographic distribution. Modern taxonomy uses multilocus sequence analysis to
  resolve species boundaries.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Tropical Agriculture:** Anthracnose caused by *G. cingulata/C.
  gloeosporioides* is among the most damaging pre- and postharvest diseases of
  tropical fruits. Losses can be severe, especially during transport and
  storage. Postharvest dips and heat treatments are commonly used for control.

- **Host-Pathogen Interactions:** The *Colletotrichum* system has become an
  important model for studying hemibiotrophic fungal pathogenesis, including
  the transition from biotrophic to necrotrophic growth phases during infection.

- **Appressorium Biology:** *Colletotrichum* appressoria are among the
  best-studied fungal infection structures. Research on appressorial
  melanization and turgor generation has revealed fundamental mechanisms of
  fungal pathogenicity shared across diverse pathogen lineages.

- **Biocontrol Potential:** Some *Colletotrichum* species have been developed
  as mycoherbicides for biological weed control, exploiting the genus's host
  specificity for targeted weed management.
