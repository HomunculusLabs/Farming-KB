## Ergosterol

## Overview

Ergosterol (ergosta-5,7,22-trien-3β-ol) is the principal sterol in fungal cell membranes, functionally analogous to cholesterol in animal cells. It serves as a structural component of the lipid bilayer, modulating membrane fluidity, permeability, and the activity of membrane-associated enzymes.

Ergosterol is the biochemical precursor to vitamin D₂ (ergocalciferol), formed upon exposure to ultraviolet radiation. This conversion is the basis for the commercial production of vitamin D₂ supplements. Ergosterol content is also used as a biomarker for fungal biomass in environmental and soil samples.

All true fungi (Eumycota) contain ergosterol, while it is absent from bacteria, plants, and animals. This exclusivity makes ergosterol a definitive molecular marker for fungal presence in complex samples and the basis for selective antifungal drug toxicity.

## Chemical Identity

- **IUPAC name:** (3S,9S,10R,13R,14R,17R)-17-[(2R,5R)-5,6-Dimethylheptan-2-yl]-10,13-dimethyl-2,3,4,9,11,12,14,15,16,17-decahydro-1H-cyclopenta[a]phenanthren-3-ol
- **Molecular formula:** C₂₈H₄₄O
- **Molecular weight:** 396.65 g/mol
- **CAS number:** 57-87-4
- **Chemical class:** Sterol (mycosterol)
- **Structure:** Tetracyclic sterol nucleus with three double bonds (Δ⁵,⁷,²²) and a methyl group at C-24
- **Melting point:** 168 °C (decomposes)
- **Solubility:** Insoluble in water; soluble in chloroform, ether, benzene, and hot ethanol

## Biosynthesis and Distribution

Ergosterol is synthesized via the mevalonate pathway, shared with plant and animal sterols.

- **Pathway:** Acetyl-CoA → mevalonate → isopentenyl pyrophosphate (IPP) → squalene → lanosterol → ergosterol
- **Key enzymes:** HMG-CoA reductase (rate-limiting), squalene epoxidase, C-14 demethylase (CYP51), C-22/C-23 desaturase
- **Regulation:** Oxygen-dependent; anaerobic conditions block the pathway at C-5 and C-22 desaturation steps, leading to accumulation of intermediate sterols
- **Fungal specificity:** Found in all true fungi (Eumycota) but absent in bacteria, plants, and animals
- **Content by species:** [[agaricus-bisporus]] (~5.8 mg/g DW), [[lentinula-edodes]] (~8.4 mg/g DW), porcini (Boletus edulis, ~10.6 mg/g DW)
- **Mycelial vs. fruiting body:** Mycelium typically contains lower ergosterol concentrations than mature fruiting bodies
- **Environmental factors:** Light exposure, temperature, and substrate composition influence ergosterol accumulation; higher levels observed in sun-exposed specimens
- **Storage:** Concentrated in plasma membrane; small amounts in intracellular membranes (mitochondria, ER)

## Biological Function in Fungi

- **Membrane architecture:** Modulates membrane fluidity and permeability; maintains proper spacing between phospholipid fatty acid chains
- **Enzyme modulation:** Influences activity of membrane-bound enzymes including ATPases and transport proteins
- **Membrane domain organization:** Facilitates formation of lipid rafts involved in signal transduction
- **Stress response:** Ergosterol content increases in response to temperature stress, ethanol exposure, and antifungal agents
- **Sporulation:** Required for proper spore wall formation and germination competence
- **Hyphal growth:** Essential for tip growth and cell division; inhibition blocks apical extension
- **Membrane protein interaction:** Serves as a binding partner for many membrane-associated proteins; required for proper function of H⁺-ATPase and nutrient transporters

## Vitamin D₂ Formation

Ergosterol converts to vitamin D₂ (ergocalciferol) upon ultraviolet B (UVB) irradiation.

- **Mechanism:** UVB (280–315 nm) photolyzes the B-ring, opening it to form pre-vitamin D₂, which thermally isomerizes to vitamin D₂
- **Conversion rate:** 20–50% of ergosterol converts to vitamin D₂ under controlled UV exposure
- **Commercial production:** Large-scale irradiation of ergosterol extracted from yeast (*[[candida-albicans]]* and *[[aspergillus-fumigatus]]*

## Analytical Methods

- **Quantification:** HPLC with UV detection (282 nm) is the standard method; GC-MS after saponification also used
- **Biomarker applications:** Ergosterol concentration correlates with living fungal biomass in soil (µg ergosterol/g soil ≈ mg fungal C/g soil); used in mycorrhizal research and environmental monitoring
