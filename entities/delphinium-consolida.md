## Overview

**Delphinium consolida** (field larkspur or Consolida larkspur) is an annual herbaceous plant in the family Ranunculaceae, native to Europe and western Asia.

It was historically placed in the genus Consolida but has been reclassified within Delphinium by most modern taxonomic treatments.

The species is widely cultivated as an ornamental cut flower and has naturalized in parts [[berry-growing]] and Australia.

All plant parts are highly toxic, containing diterpenoid alkaloids similar to those found in [[aconitum-napellus]] and other Aconitum species.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Delphinium consolida** was originally described by Carl Linnaeus in 1753 as *Delphinium consolida*.

The genus was long treated as separate (Consolida) based on the single-spurred flower structure, but molecular phylogenetics places it firmly within Delphinium.

Plants are annual, growing 30 to 100 cm tall with slender, erect, branching stems.

Leaves are palmately divided into narrow linear lobes, giving a finely dissected appearance.

The inflorescence is an open raceme with flowers borne on long pedicels.

Each flower has a single backward-projecting spur measuring 12 to 20 mm, formed from the upper sepal.

The five petals include one modified into a nectar-storing spur and four small inner petals.

Flower color ranges from blue and violet to pink, purple, and occasionally white.

Fruit is a single follicle, 10 to 16 mm long, containing numerous small dark seeds.

## Distribution and Habitat

**Delphinium consolida** is native to Europe, the Mediterranean basin, and western Asia, extending from Portugal and the British Isles east to Iran and the Caucasus.

It has been widely introduced and naturalized in [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]] worldwide, including [[polypore-fungi-eastern-north-america]], parts of [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-south-america]], southern Australia, and New Zealand.

The species grows in cultivated fields, roadsides, disturbed ground, and dry grasslands.

It prefers well-drained, calcareous or neutral soils in full sun.

In its native range, it occurs from sea level to approximately 1,500 meters elevation.

The plant is considered a weed of agriculture in some regions, particularly in grain fields of southern Europe.

## Bioactive Compounds

**Delphinium consolida** contains diterpenoid alkaloids of the norditerpenoid type, with the principal toxins being methyllycaconitine and consolidine.

Methyllycaconitine is a potent antagonist of nicotinic acetylcholine receptors, particularly the α7 subtype.

This mechanism differs from the sodium-channel activation of Aconitum alkaloids, producing a different but equally dangerous toxicological profile.

Symptoms of poisoning include digestive distress, muscle weakness, respiratory paralysis, and in severe cases, death by asphyxiation.

Livestock poisoning is well documented, particularly in cattle grazing on contaminated hay or in pastures where larkspur is abundant.

The alkaloid concentration varies with plant age and growing conditions, with the highest levels occurring during [[cervantes-seedling-care-early-growth]] and in seeds.

## Traditional and Historical Use

Despite its toxicity, **Delphinium consolida** has a long history of use in European folk medicine, primarily as an external remedy.

The Latin name "consolida" reflects its traditional use in wound healing and tissue consolidation (from the Latin *consolidare*, to make firm).

Poultices of crushed seeds were applied to wounds, bruises, and insect bites in [[solanaceous-hallucinogens-of-medieval-european-witchcraft]] herbal practice.

The seeds were sometimes used as a parasiticide, particularly against head lice, though the efficacy is questionable and the risk of systemic absorption is significant.

In traditional Turkish medicine, aerial parts were used externally for hemorrhoids and skin inflammation.
