## Barry Commoner

**Classification:** Environmental scientist / Public intellectual  
**Category:** Person

## Biography

Barry Commoner (1917–2012) was an American biologist, environmental scientist, and public intellectual who became one of the most prominent figures in the environmental movement of the 20th century. He was a professor at Washington University in St. Louis and later at Queens College, City University of New York. Commoner combined rigorous scientific research with passionate public advocacy for environmental protection and sustainable agriculture.

## Major Contributions

### Organic Farming Comparison Study (1977)

In *The Winter Harvest Handbook*, [[eliot-coleman]] cites a landmark 1977 study from Barry Commoner's group at Washington University in Saint Louis. This study conducted side-by-side paired comparisons between Midwestern organic farmers and their chemical-using neighbors, demonstrating that organic farmers were "just as successful" as conventional operators. Coleman describes this as "the first major eye-opener of the world to come" and notes the establishment's shock at the results.

### The Closing Circle (1971)

Commoner's influential book *The Closing Circle: Nature, Man, and Technology* outlined four laws of ecology:
1. Everything is connected to everything else
2. Everything must go somewhere
3. Nature knows best
4. There is no such thing as a free lunch

### Environmental Activism

- Conducted pioneering research on nuclear fallout, linking strontium-90 in milk to above-ground nuclear testing
- Ran for President of the United States in 1980 as the Citizens Party candidate
- Founded the Center for the Biology of Natural Systems at Washington University
- Advocated for the connection between environmental degradation and economic systems

## Key Facts

- **Born:** May 28, 1917, Brooklyn, New York
- **Died:** September 30, 2012, New York City
- **Education:** Columbia University (BA, MA, PhD in biology)
- **Academic positions:** Washington University in St. Louis; Queens College, CUNY
- **Major publications:** *Science and Survival* (1966), *The Closing Circle* (1971), *The Poverty of Power* (1976)
- **Political career:** 1980 presidential candidate (Citizens Party)

## Relevance to Organic Farming

Commoner's 1977 organic farming study was significant because it provided scientific credibility to a movement that had been dismissed by the agricultural establishment. As Coleman recounts, the USDA and land-grant university system had consistently dismissed positive findings about organic farming. Commoner's rigorous, peer-reviewed comparison was one of the first major scientific validations that organic methods could match or exceed conventional agricultural productivity.

The study was particularly damaging to the chemical agriculture narrative because it came not from organic advocates but from an independent, respected scientific institution. Coleman notes this moment as pivotal in the ongoing struggle between organic farming and the "corporate/industrial" agricultural establishment.
