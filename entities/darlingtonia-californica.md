## Overview

**Darlingtonia californica**, commonly known as the cobra lily or California
pitcher plant, is a monotypic carnivorous plant species in the family
Sarraceniaceae. It is endemic to northern California and southern Oregon.

The plant produces distinctive tubular pitchers that resemble a rearing cobra
hood. A forked appendage resembling a serpent's tongue hangs from the opening,

## Carnivory Mechanism

The trapping mechanism of *Darlingtonia* is a passive pitfall trap augmented
by a unique translucent hood. Insects are attracted to nectar produced around
the pitcher opening and by the forked "tongue" appendage.

Upon entering the pitcher, insects encounter the translucent areolae (patches)
in the hood. These windows create the illusion of an exit, causing insects to
fly upward and strike the hood surface, where they fall into the pitcher tube.

Downward-pointing epidermal hairs lining the interior prevent climbing. A
smooth, waxy zone near the upper tube further reduces insect traction. The
bulbous base contains the digestive fluid where prey accumulates.

Unlike other Sarraceniaceae, the pitcher fluid contains no plant-produced
digestive enzymes. Instead, a complex community of bacteria (including
*Clostridium*, *Bacillus*, and *Pseudomonas* species) and insect larvae
(notably *Metriocnemus* midge larvae) [[query-can-fungi-break-down-plastic-pollution]] prey through saprophytic
decomposition.
