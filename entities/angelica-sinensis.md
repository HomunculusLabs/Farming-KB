## Bioactive Compounds

The root contains a complex mixture of bioactive constituents:

**Coumarins and their derivatives:**
- **Z-ligustilide** — the primary bioactive compound; anti-inflammatory,
  vasodilatory, and antispasmodic properties; responsible for the
  characteristic aroma
- **Ferulic acid** — antioxidant and anti-inflammatory phenolic acid
- **Osthole** — coumarin with antispasmodic and anti-osteoporotic activity
- **Angelolide** — antiplatelet aggregation activity

**Polysaccharides:**
- **Angelica sinensis polysaccharide (ASP)** — immunomodulatory, promotes
  hematopoiesis (blood cell formation), and has hepatoprotective effects

**Phthalides:**
- **Butylidenephthalide** — anti-inflammatory and neuroprotective
- **Sedanolide** — contributes to smooth muscle relaxation

**Volatile oils (0.4–0.7%):**
- Composed primarily of ligustilide, butylphthalide, and various terpenes

## Traditional Chinese Medicine Use

In TCM, dong quai is classified as sweet, acrid, and warm. It enters the
Liver, Heart, and Spleen meridians.

**Primary TCM functions:**
- Tonifies the blood (补血) — treats pale complexion, dizziness,
  palpitations, and brittle nails associated with blood deficiency
- Invigorates blood circulation (活血) — addresses [[bay-11-medical-sativa-granddaddy-purp-pain-relief-oner]] through prostaglandin inhibition

## Cultivation

Angelica sinensis is cultivated commercially in Gansu, Sichuan, Yunnan,
Hubei, and Shanxi provinces. It is typically grown as a biennial, with
roots harvested in the second year after autumn senescence.

**Growing requirements:**
- [[mushroom-growing-temperature-and-moisture]]
- High atmospheric humidity (70–80%)
- Partial shade to full sun at high elevation
