# Moringa

**Type:** Species (Plant)
**Category:** Multipurpose food and medicinal tree
**Also known as:** Horseradish tree, drumstick tree, [[permaculture]] systems. Its rapid
growth, ability to thrive in poor soils, and the extraordinary nutritional
density of its leaves make it invaluable for [[phosphate-accumulators-sustainable-agriculture]] in
developing regions. The tree's multiple harvest windows (leaves, pods, seeds)
provide year-round food production.

## Botanical Description

[[comparison-moringa-oleifera-vs-moringa-stenopetala]] is a fast-growing deciduous tree in the family
Moringaceae, native to the Indian subcontinent. It typically reaches
5-12 meters in height with a whitish-gray bark and drooping branches.
The compound leaves are tripinnate, with small leaflets approximately
1-2 cm in length. The tree produces fragrant white flowers in
panicles, followed by pendulous seed pods (drumsticks) 30-120 cm
long containing winged seeds.

## Nutritional Profile

Moringa leaves are exceptionally nutritious, containing significant
amounts of vitamin A, vitamin C, calcium, potassium, iron, and
protein. Dried leaf powder contains approximately 25% protein by
weight, making it a valuable nutritional supplement. The leaves
contain all essential amino acids, and the vitamin C content is
estimated at 7 times that of oranges on a dry weight basis.

## Cultivation and Growing Conditions

Moringa is remarkably drought-tolerant once established and grows
well in tropical and subtropical climates with annual rainfall of
250-1500 mm. It tolerates a wide range of soil types but prefers
well-drained sandy or loamy soils with a pH of 6.3-7.0. The tree
can be propagated from seed (germination in 1-2 weeks) or from
hardwood cuttings 1-1.5 meters in length. [[coleman-sunlight-and-winter-crop-growth-rates]] of 3-5
meters per year have been recorded in optimal conditions.

## Uses and Applications

Nearly every part of the moringa tree has practical applications.
Leaves are consumed fresh, dried as a powder, or cooked as a
vegetable. Young seed pods are eaten as a vegetable similar to
green beans. Seeds can be pressed for oil (ben oil) used in
cooking, cosmetics, and as a lubricant. Crushed seeds are used
as a natural coagulant for water purification, effectively
removing turbidity and bacteria from contaminated water.

## Traditional Medicine

In Ayurvedic medicine, moringa has been used for over 4,000 years
to treat more than 300 conditions. Traditional uses include
treatment of inflammation, infection, diabetes, cardiovascular
disease, and malnutrition. Modern research has identified numerous
[[cordyceps-cancer-bioactive-compounds]] including [[quercetin]], [[kaempferol]], and
isothiocyanates, supporting some traditional applications while
highlighting the need for rigorous clinical studies.
