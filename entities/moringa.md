# Moringa

**Type:** Species (Plant)
**Category:** Multipurpose food and medicinal tree
**Also known as:** Horseradish tree, drumstick tree, [[moringa-oleifera]],
ben oil tree, miracle tree

## Overview
Moringa is a small deciduous tree native to India, often called the miracle
tree due to its extraordinary range of edible and useful parts. Virtually
every part of the tree is usable: leaves as a nutrient-dense vegetable,
young pods as drumstick vegetables, seeds for oil extraction, and roots as a
horseradish substitute. The seeds produce ben oil, a stable oil that never
goes rancid and was historically used as watchmakers' precision oil.

## Key Details

### Botanical Classification
- **Family:** Moringaceae
- **Genus:** Moringa
- **Species:** M. oleifera

### Growth Characteristics
- **Habit:** Small deciduous tree, to 8 m tall and 3 m wide
- **Growth rate:** Very fast growing under favourable conditions
- **Lifespan:** Relatively short-lived but highly productive
- **Frost tolerance:** Frost tender; both drought and frost sensitive

### Environmental Requirements
- **Climate:** Tropical to subtropical; requires warm conditions
- **Rainfall:** Drought tolerant once established but grows best with
  regular water
- **Soil:** Adaptable to various soil types; prefers well-drained soils
- **Temperature:** Sensitive to frost; thrives in hot conditions

### Edible Parts and Uses
- **Leaves:** Young leaves and twigs have a mustard flavour; eaten in salads
  and cooked as vegetables; contain 7-10% protein; highly nutritious
- **Pods:** Long bean-like fruits used in soups and curries
- **Immature seeds:** Used like peas in cooking
- **Mature seeds:** Roasted or fried and eaten like peanuts
- **Root:** Grated and used as a horseradish substitute (hence the common
  name)
- **Seedlings:** Young tender seedlings cooked as a vegetable

### Ben Oil (Behen Oil)
- Extracted from moringa seeds
- Never goes rancid, making it exceptionally stable
- Used for salads and cooking
- Historically known as watchmakers' oil for precision machinery
- Valued in cosmetics and skin care formulations

### Nutritional Highlights
- Leaves contain 7-10% protein
- Rich in vitamins A, C, and minerals
- Considered one of the most nutrient-dense leafy greens available
- All parts of the tree have food or medicinal applications

## Relevance
Moringa is increasingly recognised as a critical species for food security
and nutrition in tropical and subtropical [[permaculture]] systems. Its rapid
growth, ability to thrive in poor soils, and the extraordinary nutritional
density of its leaves make it invaluable for sustainable agriculture in
developing regions. The tree's multiple harvest windows (leaves, pods, seeds)
provide year-round food production.
