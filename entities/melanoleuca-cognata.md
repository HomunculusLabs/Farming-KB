## Morphology

- **Cap:** 4–10 cm diameter, convex to broadly umbonate, sometimes
  irregular; surface smooth, slightly viscid when moist, hygrophanous;
  coloration variable — pale brown, ochre, tan, or grey-brown, often
  with a darker disc; margin initially inrolled, becoming straight
- **Gills:** Adnexed to sinuate, moderately crowded, white to pale
  cream when young, aging to pale grey-brown; edges sometimes
  finely fimbriate
- **Stipe:** 5–10 cm × 0.8–1.5 cm, cylindrical or slightly clavate,
  solid; surface smooth to finely fibrillose, white to pale brown,
  often with a mealy coating near the apex; no persistent annulus,
  though a faint cortina may be present in young specimens
- **Flesh:** White, firm, thick; odor mild and pleasant, sometimes
  faintly mealy or farinaceous; taste mild, slightly nutty

Europe and [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]], with scattered reports from Asia. It typically
fruits from April through July, making it one of the earlier agarics
to appear in the season. The species grows on soil in a variety of
habitats including mixed and coniferous woodlands, grassy clearings,
parkland, and along forest paths. It shows a preference for calcareous
or nutrient-enriched soils and is often found in disturbed ground
areas. Fruitings can be solitary, scattered, or in small trooping
groups.
