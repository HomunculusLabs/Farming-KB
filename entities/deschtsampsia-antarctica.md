# Deschampsia antarctica

## Description

*Deschampsia antarctica* (Antarctic hair grass) is one of only two flowering plant species native to Antarctica. It is a perennial grass in the family Poaceae, found along the Antarctic Peninsula and surrounding subantarctic islands. It is notable for its association with arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) fungi in one of the most extreme terrestrial environments on Earth, providing a model for studying the limits of [[mycorrhizal-symbiosis]].

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Family:** Poaceae
- **Genus:** *Deschampsia*
- **Species:** *D. antarctica*
- **Common name:** Antarctic hair grass

## Key Facts

- One of only two native vascular plant species in Antarctica (the other being *Colobanthus quitensis*)
- Cabello and colleagues (1994) described at least one arbuscular mycorrhizal species associated with *D. antarctica* along the Danko coast of the Antarctic Peninsula
- In contrast, no AM fungal associations were detected with the same plant species on Anvers Island (DeMars and Boerner 1995; Morton, unpublished data)
- This discrepancy illustrates the patchy distribution of AM fungi in extreme polar environments
- The plant grows in compact cushion-like tufts in ice-free areas along the Antarctic coast
- Associated AM fungal species appear to be limited in diversity, consistent with the harsh environmental conditions

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

*Deschampsia antarctica* is important for understanding the ecological limits of mycorrhizal symbiosis:

- Represents the extreme southern limit of AM fungal associations, testing hypotheses about global distribution patterns of [[glomales]]
- The inconsistent detection of AM fungi at different Antarctic sites raises questions about the environmental thresholds for mycorrhizal establishment
- Relevant to studies of plant–fungus coevolution in extreme environments, where both partners face severe cold, desiccation, and nutrient limitation
- Provides a natural model for understanding whether AM fungal distribution is limited by host plant availability, edaphic factors, or environmental extremes
- The species is a reference point in discussions of polar [[mycology]] and the global biodiversity inventory of AM fungi
