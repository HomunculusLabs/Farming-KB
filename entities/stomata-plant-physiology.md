## Description

Stomata are microscopic pore structures found primarily on leaf surfaces that regulate gas exchange between the plant and the surrounding atmosphere. In the context of [[compost]] tea and foliar applications, stomata serve as the primary entry points for nutrients and [[forest-and-bamboo-beneficial-microorganisms]] applied to leaf surfaces. Their opening and closing behavior is directly influenced by CO2 concentration, which is in turn modulated by microbial respiration on the leaf surface.

## Classification

- **Category**: Plant anatomical structure
- **Location**: Primarily on leaf surfaces (also present on stems in some species)
- **Function**: Gas exchange (CO2 intake, O2/water vapor release), nutrient uptake pathway
- **Regulation**: Controlled by guard cell turgor pressure, influenced by CO2, light, humidity, and microbial activity

## Key Facts

- Stomata opening is controlled by CO2 concentration in the leaf surface atmosphere
- Microbes on leaf surfaces respire, increasing local CO2 concentration and keeping stomata open longer
- Positively charged ions (cations) have difficulty passing through stomata due to the net negative charge of biological cells
- Chelation neutralizes charge on minerals, allowing more rapid passage through stomata
- Stomata are generally easier pathways for material entry than individual cell membranes
- As weather becomes drier, microbial respiration decreases, and stomata close — a self-regulating biological feedback system
- This mechanism allows plants to "pump nutrients in when it"s appropriate to do so"
- [[foliar-nutrient-uptake-plant-control]] depends heavily on stomatal function, which depends on beneficial microbial presence

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Understanding stomatal dynamics is critical for effective foliar application of compost tea and nutrients. The presence of beneficial microorganisms on leaf surfaces directly enhances nutrient uptake by maintaining stomatal opening through increased CO2 production. This creates a compelling argument for maintaining healthy [[cannabis-rhizosphere-microbial-communities]] on foliage: the microbes themselves facilitate the plant's ability to absorb foliar-applied nutrients. In [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]] and greenhouse operations, this principle applies to any [[foliar-feeding]] regimen. The interplay between microbial respiration, CO2 concentration, and stomatal aperture represents one of the key mechanisms by which [[aerobic-vs-anaerobic-compost-tea-benefits]] plants beyond simple disease suppression.

## Source Context

Ingham describes this mechanism in detail when explaining why compost tea improves foliar nutrient uptake: microbes respire CO2, which speeds stomatal opening and increases the duration stomata remain open, thereby enhancing nutrient absorption. She also notes that [[aact-microbial-chelation-of-minerals-ingham]] (performed by microbes) neutralizes charge, enabling minerals to pass through stomata more readily.
