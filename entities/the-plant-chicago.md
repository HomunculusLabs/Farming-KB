## Description

The Plant (plantchicago.com) is an urban agriculture facility in downtown Chicago that operates multiple demonstration farms within a repurposed old meatpacking plant. It exemplifies the concept of closed-loop urban food production by integrating three complementary farming systems — outdoor vegetable farming, indoor aquaponics, and indoor mushroom cultivation — within a single building. Tradd Cotter references The Plant as a model for how mushrooms can be incorporated into multilevel urban food factories.

## Key Facts

- Operates three demonstration farms in an old meatpacking plant
- Integrates outdoor vegetable farming, indoor aquaponics, and indoor mushroom cultivation
- Waste from the aquaponics system and spent mushroom substrate fertilize the vegetable garden
- Demonstrates closed-loop circular food system principles
- Mushrooms play a key role in biomass processing and nutrient cycling
- Mycelia can function as biological filters to supplement biofiltration in aquaponics systems

## Closed-Loop System Design

The facility demonstrates multiple pathways for nutrient and resource cycling:
- **CO₂ circulation**: Carbon dioxide from mushroom fruiting rooms circulated to rooftop greenhouses to benefit vegetable plants
- **Temperature management**: CO₂ circulation helps maintain temperatures and manage gas exchange
- **Waste-to-substrate**: Dried plant waste from vegetable operations used as mushroom growing substrate
- **Spent substrate to soil**: Mushroom compost cycled back into vegetable beds
- **Water hyacinth integration**: Water hyacinth from aquaponics biofiltration continuously harvested for oyster and paddy straw mushroom substrate
- **Composting**: Water hyacinth composted for almond portabella cultivation and vermicomposting
- **Algae integration**: Mushroom fruiting room exhaust air directed to high-fat algae tanks for biodiesel production

## Broader Implications

The Plant represents a paradigm for urban food production where:
- Multiple food production systems share resources and waste streams
- Buildings are repurposed rather than newly constructed
- Each system's waste becomes another system's input
- Mushrooms serve as biological converters of plant waste into protein and soil

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

The Plant demonstrates that mushrooms are not just a standalone crop but a critical component of integrated urban agriculture systems. Their ability to convert cellulosic waste into protein, produce CO₂ for plant growth, and generate compost for soil building makes them uniquely valuable in closed-loop systems. The facility serves as a working proof of concept for vertical urban farming that combines vegetables, fish, and fungi in a single building — a model that can be replicated in cities worldwide using abandoned industrial buildings.
