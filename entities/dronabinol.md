## Dronabinol

**Type:** Chemical / Pharmaceutical / [[nabilone]] and Sativex). Dronabinol is
chemically identical to natural THC in molecular formula but differs
in structural arrangement (isomer). It activates both CB1 and CB2
[[mushroom-cancer-clinical-trials-overview]] have demonstrated dronabinol's efficacy:

- **Appetite and weight gain (Saint Louis University, 2007):** Patients
  with HIV showed increased appetite and mood improvement with regular
  oral doses.
- **Anorexia treatment (Orlando Immunology Center, 2007):** 117 patients
  received dronabinol as treatment; results showed safety and efficacy
  for anorexia associated with weight loss.
- **Cancer-related symptoms (William Osler Health Center, 2006):**
  Nabilone (related synthetic cannabinoid) was used for cancer-related
  pain and symptoms over 53 days.
- **HIV patients (St. John's Hospital, 1995):** 139 patients received
  2.5 mg of dronabinol; dronabinol improved appetite and caused weight
  gain in seven of ten patients with symptomatic HIV infection.
- **Cancer and HIV (UNIMED, 1991):** Dronabinol improved appetite in
  both cancer and HIV patients.

### Limitations Compared to Whole-Plant Cannabis
Unlike Sativex (which is a plant-derived tincture containing multiple
cannabinoids), dronabinol is a single isolated compound. This means it
lacks the [[cannabis-terpene-entourage-effect-profiles]] provided by the full spectrum of cannabis
constituents (70+ cannabinoids, terpenes, flavonoids, etc.) that may
contribute to therapeutic outcomes. Patients using dronabinol do not
benefit from the modulating effects of CBD, CBG, CBC, CBN, and other
compounds found in whole-plant cannabis.

### Pharmaceutical Context
Dronabinol is part of a small family of FDA-approved cannabinoid
pharmaceuticals:
- Dronabinol (Marinol): synthetic THC isomer
- Nabilone (Cesamet): synthetic cannabinoid
- Sativex: plant-derived THC:CBD oromucosal spray
- Rimonabant: CB1 antagonist (withdrawn from market due to psychiatric
  side effects)

## Relevance
Dronabinol represents the pharmaceutical industry's approach to
cannabinoid medicine: isolate the active compound and deliver it in a
standardized dose. While effective for its approved indications,
dronabinol's limitations highlight the importance of the entourage
effect. Clinical evidence consistently shows that whole-plant cannabis
or broad-spectrum extracts often outperform isolated THC for many
conditions. Dronabinol's existence also demonstrates that cannabinoid
medicine gained mainstream medical acceptance decades before cannabis
