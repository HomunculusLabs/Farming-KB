# Gary Fisher

## Description

Gary Fisher was a psychedelic researcher active in the early 1960s.

He published dosage guidelines for psychedelic substances in the *Psychedelic Review* in 1963.

His work provided practical pharmacological data that informed the preparation instructions in *The Psychedelic Experience*.

Fisher operated within the research network that included [[timothy-leary]], [[richard-alpert]], and [[ralph-metzner]].

## The Psychedelic Review

The *Psychedelic Review* was founded in 1963 as a forum for serious psychedelic research.

It published articles on pharmacology, psychology, anthropology, and philosophy related to psychedelic substances.

Fisher's dosage guidelines appeared in this journal alongside contributions from Leary, Metzner, and others.

The journal represented the attempt to maintain scientific rigor in psychedelic research during a period of growing cultural controversy.

## Practical Contributions

Fisher's work addressed the practical question: how much of a given substance produces what kind of experience?

This seemingly simple question was critical for the systematic approach advocated in *The Psychedelic Experience*.

Without reliable dosage information, the manual's specific instructions for session preparation would lack a pharmacological foundation.

Fisher helped bridge the gap between chemical substances and the states of consciousness they produce.

## Relevance to Psychedelic Studies

Gary Fisher's dosage guidelines provided the empirical foundation for the practical instructions in *The Psychedelic Experience*.

The manual's session preparation section relies on accurate dosage information to ensure safety and effectiveness.

Fisher's work represents the scientific rigor that underlay the more publicly visible philosophical aspects of the psychedelic movement.

His contributions remind us that responsible psychedelic use requires precise knowledge of substance, dose, and effect.
