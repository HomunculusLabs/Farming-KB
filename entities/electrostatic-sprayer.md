# Electrostatic Sprayer (Compost Tea)

## Description

Electrostatic sprayers are application devices that impart an electrical charge to spray droplets, causing them to be attracted to and uniformly coat target surfaces (plant leaves, stems). While highly effective for chemical pesticide and fertilizer application, electrostatic sprayers pose a potential risk when used with compost tea: the electrical charge may destroy or damage living microorganisms in the tea by disrupting their cellular charge balance.

## Classification

- **Type:** Spray application equipment
- **Technology:** Electrostatic charging of spray droplets
- **Use case:** Uniform coverage of plant surfaces with minimal drift
- **Compatibility with compost tea:** Potentially problematic — requires testing

## Key Facts

- Imparts electrical charge to spray droplets, causing them to wrap around and uniformly coat plant surfaces
- Provides superior coverage compared to conventional sprayers — droplets are attracted to oppositely charged plant surfaces
- Widely used for chemical pesticide and fertilizer application in agriculture
- May destroy compost tea microbes by putting "the wrong charge" on them
- The electrical charge could potentially:
  - Disrupt cell membrane integrity of bacteria and fungi
  - Interfere with cellular ion gradients essential for microbial survival
  - Kill or inactivate the beneficial organisms that make compost tea effective
- Recommendation: test the tea after it passes through an electrostatic sprayer before relying on this application method
- Testing involves biological assay (microscopy or plate counts) to verify that microbial populations survive the electrostatic charging process
- Alternative application methods for compost tea:
  - Hand pump sprayers (under 70 psi)
  - Concrete sprayers (larger orifices, fewer bends)
  - Gasoline backpack mist sprayers (large areas)
  - Traveling sprinkler with inline fertilizer dispenser (lawns)
  - Simple watering can (soil drench)

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

The electrostatic sprayer caution highlights an important principle for biological application:

- **Living organisms vs. inert chemicals:** Compost tea contains living microbes that can be killed by physical and electrical forces that would not affect chemical inputs
- **Application pressure limits:** Sprayers must not exceed 70 psi; spray velocity should be slow
- **Parachute delivery:** Tea should "parachute" down to surfaces, not forcefully "splat" — impact force can kill microbes
- **UV sensitivity:** Microbes in compost tea are killed by UV light; application timing must account for solar exposure
- **Equipment selection:** Choosing the right application equipment is as important as brewing the tea correctly — the best tea is useless if application kills the biology
