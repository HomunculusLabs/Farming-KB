# Inga edulis

**Type:** Species
**Category:** Evergreen nitrogen-fixing fruit tree
**Also known as:** Ice cream bean, joaquiniquil, guaba

## Overview
Inga edulis is an evergreen tree native to the neotropics of Central
and South America. Growing to 18 meters tall, it produces large pods
containing seeds surrounded by sweet, white, sugar-rich pulp that
tastes similar to vanilla ice cream. It is unusual among nurse crops
in being both a nitrogen-fixing companion plant and an edible fruit
producer, with over 400 species in the genus.

## Key Details

### Botany
- **Family:** Fabaceae (Mimosoideae)
- **Form:** Evergreen tree to 18 m tall, 4 m spread
- **Root system:** Associates with nitrogen-fixing rhizobia
- **Genus:** Over 400 species; most produce edible pods

### Growth Conditions
- Native to neotropical lowlands and foothills
- Tolerates a large range of conditions, like most Andean plants
- Not conditioned to cool extremes of high Andes elevations
- Grows well in tropical and subtropical climates

### Fruit and Edible Parts
- Pods can grow to considerable size (up to 1 meter long in some
  species)
- White, sugar-rich pulp spooned from around seeds
- Eaten fresh; sweet flavour and smooth texture resembling ice cream
- Most of the 400+ Inga species produce edible fruit

### Agricultural Uses
- **Nurse crop:** Root associates fix atmospheric nitrogen
- **Shade tree:** Used as shade for coffee and cacao plantations in
  Central America and the West Indies
- **Alley cropping:** Used in alley cropping systems in Chile
- **Street tree:** Planted throughout South America as an ornamental
- **Fodder:** In Mexico, foliage is cut and fed to livestock

### Timber Properties
- Moderately heavy wood
- Used for furniture, boxes, and crates
- Not suited to external use (breaks down quickly)
- Utilized for charcoal production

### Propagation
- Propagated from seed
- Fast growth in tropical conditions
- Widely distributed across Central and South America
- Seed germination is relatively straightforward
- Seedlings establish quickly in suitable conditions
- Direct seeding in field conditions is common practice

### Environmental Adaptability
- Like most Andean plants, tolerates a large range of conditions
- Not conditioned to cool extremes of high Andes elevations
- Performs best in frost-free tropical lowlands
- Can serve as a pioneer species on disturbed sites

## Relevance
The ice cream bean is a uniquely valuable agroforestry species because
it combines the nitrogen-fixing nurse crop function with an edible
fruit product. In permaculture systems, Inga edulis serves as a
multipurpose support tree that provides shade, fixes nitrogen, improves
soil, and yields a sweet edible pulp, making it ideal for food forest
overstory layers in tropical climates.
