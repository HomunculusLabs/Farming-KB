# Trompe

## Description

A trompe is a hydraulic air-compression device that uses falling water to compress air underground without any moving parts or mechanical energy input. Mollison describes the trompe in the context of aquaculture systems as a method to aerate fish ponds and spawning beds using only the energy of flowing water. The device represents a classic [[permaculture]] technology: elegant, passive, energy-free, and multi-functional.

## Classification

- **Category:** Hydraulic pneumatic device
- **Function:** Air compression using water power
- **Energy source:** Gravity and flowing water (no external energy required)
- **Applications:** Workshop compressed air, fish pond aeration, spawning bed aeration

## Key Facts

- Operates with no moving parts — reliability is extremely high
- Uses the energy of falling water to entrain and compress air underground
- Compressed air can be used directly for fish pond aeration or routed to workshops
- Particularly valuable for remote aquaculture installations where electricity is unavailable or unreliable
- One of the oldest known pneumatic technologies, with historical use in mining and smelting
- Produces cool, clean compressed air as a byproduct of water flow

## How It Works

Water flows down a vertical or inclined pipe, entraining air through an intake. The water-air mixture descends to an underground chamber where the air separates from the water under pressure. The compressed air is collected from the top of the chamber while water exits at the bottom. The pressure achieved depends on the height of the water column (head).

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

The trompe exemplifies several core [[permaculture-principles]]:

1. **Multiple function:** A single water source provides both aeration for aquaculture and compressed air for workshop tools
2. **No external energy:** Uses only the gravity-driven water flow already present in the landscape
3. **Passive and low-maintenance:** No moving parts means no mechanical failures, no fuel costs, and minimal maintenance
4. **Integration with landscape design:** Can be incorporated into the natural fall of water from springs, streams, or dam outflows

For aquaculture specifically, dissolved oxygen is often the limiting factor in fish pond productivity. A trompe provides continuous aeration at zero operating cost, enabling higher stocking densities and better fish health without the energy costs of conventional aerators.

In integrated permaculture designs, a trompe positioned at a stream crossing or dam outfall can simultaneously serve:
- Fish pond and spawning bed aeration
- Workshop compressed air supply
- Water oxygenation for downstream irrigation benefits

## Design Considerations

- Requires reliable water flow and sufficient head (vertical drop)
- Underground chamber construction is the main capital investment
- Best sited where natural topography provides the necessary water fall
- Can be combined with [[chinampa]], terrace, or pond systems as described by Mollison
