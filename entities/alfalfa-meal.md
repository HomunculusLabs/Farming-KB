# Alfalfa Meal

## Description

Alfalfa meal is a finely ground [[complete-organic-fertilizer]] and soil amendment made from dried alfalfa (Medicago sativa) plants. At [[four-season-farm]], it serves as the standard extra-boost fertilizer applied between greenhouse crops in the [[no-tillage-rice-barley-succession-cropping]] cycle. Coleman values it not only for its effectiveness but also because it is an amendment farmers could theoretically produce themselves, aligning with his philosophy of locally based fertility programs.

## Classification

- **Category**: Organic soil amendment / fertilizer
- **Type**: Plant-based meal (non-synthetic nitrogen source)
- **NPK analysis**: Approximately 3-1-2 (low-analysis)
- **Source**: Dried and ground alfalfa (Medicago sativa) hay

## Chemical and Biological Properties

### Nutrient Content
- **Nitrogen (N)**: Approximately 2.5 to 3 percent, slow-release from protein decomposition
- **Phosphorus (P)**: Approximately 0.5 to 1 percent
- **Potassium (K)**: Approximately 1 to 2 percent
- **Trace minerals**: Iron, magnesium, zinc, and other micronutrients
- **Growth stimulant**: Contains triacontanol, a natural plant growth regulator found in alfalfa

### Biological Activity
- Feeds soil microorganisms as it decomposes
- Stimulates biological activity in the root zone
- Slow, steady [[solomon-cool-climate-compost-soil-temperature-nutrient-release]] over 4 to 6 weeks
- Improves soil structure through organic matter addition

## Role at Four Season Farm

### Primary Greenhouse Fertilizer
Alfalfa meal is the standard amendment applied between every greenhouse crop:

1. After removing the previous crop and broadforking
2. Alfalfa meal is spread on the bed surface
3. Incorporated into the top 2 inches using the Tilther
4. Compost is then applied on top

### Philosophy of Use
Coleman prefers alfalfa meal over higher-analysis fertilizers:

1. **Low test is ideal**: You can push crops too hard with high-analysis fertilizer like dried blood
2. **Self-sufficiency**: It is an amendment we could produce ourselves if necessary
3. **Local fertility**: Aligns with the goal of a truly locally based fertility program
4. **Consistent results**: Works reliably across a wide range of greenhouse crops

### When Alfalfa Meal Is Insufficient
For crops following heavy-feeding predecessors (spinach, claytonia), additional nitrogen may be needed:

- **Historical supplement**: Cottonseed meal (discontinued due to GMO concerns)
- **Current supplement**: Locally available dried crab waste fertilizer
- **Principle**: Supplement only when soil tests and crop performance indicate specific need

## Application Guidelines

| Parameter | Recommendation |
|-----------|---------------|
| Timing | Between crops, before replanting |
| Method | Surface broadcast, then incorporate 2 inches deep |
| Rate | Moderate, depends on [[interpreting-soil-test-results]] |
| Frequency | Every succession cycle in greenhouse beds |
| Complement | Always followed by compost application |

## Comparison to Other Organic Nitrogen Sources

| Amendment | N pct | Speed | Source | Notes |
|-----------|-------|-------|--------|-------|
| Alfalfa meal | 2.5-3 | Slow | Plant-based | Preferred; farm-producible |
| Cottonseed meal | 6-7 | Moderate | Plant-based | GMO concerns |
| Dried blood | 12-14 | Fast | Animal byproduct | Can push crops too hard |
| Crab waste | Variable | Moderate | Marine byproduct | Local alternative |
| Fish meal | 8-10 | Moderate-Fast | Marine byproduct | Higher analysis |
| Compost | 1-2 | Very slow | Various | Primary fertility source |

## Benefits for Intensive Greenhouse Systems

1. **Gentle nutrition**: Low-analysis means low risk of over-fertilization or salt buildup
2. **Soil biology friendly**: Feeds microbes rather than bypassing them
3. **Complements compost**: Combines with compost for a complete fertility program
4. **Economical**: Lower cost per application than concentrated fertilizers
5. **Sustainable**: Plant-derived, potentially self-produced on-farm

## Relevance to Cultivation

Alfalfa meal represents a key principle in Coleman's approach to soil fertility: the preference for modest, consistent, biologically active amendments over concentrated synthetic inputs. This is especially important in greenhouse systems where continuous cropping demands frequent fertility inputs, enclosed environments amplify risks of over-fertilization, and soil biological health must be maintained over years of [[mollison-designers-market-garden-intensive-production-design]].
