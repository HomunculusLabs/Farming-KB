# NET

N-Ethyltryptamine

## Chemical Identity

NET is N-ethyltryptamine, a monoalkyltryptamine in which a single ethyl group is attached to the primary amine nitrogen of the tryptamine molecule. The full chemical name is 3-[2-(ethylamino)ethyl]indole.

The free base spontaneously crystallizes to a white waxy solid with a melting point of 80-81 degrees Celsius. The hydrochloride salt has a melting point of 181-182 degrees Celsius (from isopropanol/ether).

Mass spectral data shows the C3H8N+ fragment at m/z 58 (100%), the indolemethylene+ fragments at m/z 131 and 130 (48%/33%), and the parent ion at m/z 188 (2%). Infrared spectroscopy shows characteristic absorptions at 751, 887, 940, 1021, 1051, and 1118 cm-1 for the free base, and at 750, 761, 825, 1020, 1108, and 1142 cm-1 for the hydrochloride salt.

NET is documented in [[tihkal-introduction-and-methodology]] as entry #49. The entry also includes the synthesis of NIPT (N-isopropyltryptamine) and extensive commentary on the broader family of monoalkyltryptamines. Neither NET nor NIPT has demonstrated clear psychedelic activity at tested doses, but the entry serves as a gateway to the systematic chemistry of mono-substituted tryptamines.

## Dosage and Administration

No active dosage has been established for NET. The compound has received only modest human trials without discovery of active levels.

NIPT (N-isopropyltryptamine), which is described in the same entry, similarly lacks established active dosage levels.

Duration is unknown for both compounds.

## Pharmacology and Activity

NET has not demonstrated clear psychoactive effects in the limited human trials that have been conducted. It belongs to the family of monoalkyltryptamines (NRTs, where R is a sizable alkyl group), most of which have not shown significant psychedelic activity.

However, the entry discusses two mono-substituted tryptamines that "just might have CNS activity":

NSBT (N-sec-butyltryptamine) has shown generalized and somewhat diffuse intoxication with several studies covering the 25 to 75 milligram range. The effects include short-lived intellectual excitement with some modest sensory enhancements and notable erotic enhancement, but no experiences have reached a +3 level.

NTBT (N-tert-butyltryptamine) produces a light-headed intoxication in the 5 to 20 milligram range described as a "totally pleasant buzz" but nothing more profound. Shulgin notes a rumor that NTBT is "extremely potent when smoked" but states he never received particulars and considers this a baseless rumor.

The overall pattern emerging from the monoalkyltryptamine series suggests these compounds may be GHB-like intoxicants rather than classical psychedelics. Shulgin poses the question of whether all mono-tryptamines (the NRTs) might be GHB-like intoxicants "totally devoid of psychedelic activity," which he describes as "a true challenge to the SAR crowd."

## Synthesis

Two complete synthetic routes to NET are provided in the TIHKAL entry, each demonstrating a different general approach to monoalkyltryptamine synthesis.

Route 1 (from indole via oxalylamide): Indole is dissolved in anhydrous diethyl ether and treated with oxalyl chloride in ether. The resulting indol-3-ylglyoxyl chloride crystallizes as a yellow solid and is collected by filtration. This intermediate is reacted with ethylamine in ether, and the resulting N-ethyl-3-ylglyoxamide is isolated with a melting point of 208-210 degrees Celsius in 67% yield from benzene.

The glyoxylamide is reduced with lithium aluminum hydride in tetrahydrofuran under reflux for 3 hours. After workup, the free base is converted to the hydrochloride salt with a melting point of 188-190 degrees Celsius in 35% overall yield from the glyoxylamide.

Route 2 (from tryptamine via acetylation): Tryptamine base is treated with acetic anhydride in triethylamine, heated on a steam bath overnight, and worked up to give N-acetyltryptamine as a viscous amber oil boiling at 185-200 degrees Celsius (Kugelrohr). The product crystallizes from toluene with a melting point of 73-74 degrees Celsius in 47% yield.

N-acetyltryptamine is reduced with lithium aluminum hydride in tetrahydrofuran under reflux for 12 hours. After workup and distillation at 0.1 mm/Hg, NET is obtained as a white oil boiling at 125-135 degrees Celsius, which spontaneously crystallizes with a melting point of 80-81 degrees Celsius in 73% yield.

NIPT (N-isopropyltryptamine) is synthesized from tryptamine by treatment with isopropyl iodide in isopropanol at reflux for 36 hours. The free base is distilled by Kugelrohr at 130-150 degrees Celsius and 0.08 mm/Hg, then converted to the hydrochloride salt with a melting point of 224-227 degrees Celsius.

## The Monoalkyltryptamine Family

The entry for NET serves as a comprehensive introduction to the chemistry of monoalkyltryptamines, which Shulgin describes as having "staggering potential power" for the systematic generation of new tryptamine compounds.

The oxalylamide route (used for NET) and the alkyl halide route (used for NIPT) together provide access to virtually any mono-substituted tryptamine. Using these methods, pure mono-ethyl, mono-propyl, mono-isopropyl, and various mono-butyl tryptamines can be readily prepared.

The true power of this chemistry lies in the ability to react these mono-substituted intermediates with additional alkyl halides, creating an enormous matrix of "this times that" possibilities for new disubstituted tryptamines, each potentially psychoactive and almost all unknown to the scientific literature. This approach circumvents the limitations of the oxalylamide process when unsymmetrical disubstituted amines are needed as intermediates.

Compounds in the monoalkyltryptamine series that have been synthesized include:

- NPT (N-propyltryptamine): hydrochloride mp 186-187 degrees Celsius, less toxic than DPT in mice, not assayed in man

- NBT (N-butyltryptamine): hydrochloride mp 203-205 degrees Celsius

- NIBT (N-isobutyltryptamine): hydrochloride mp 150-151 degrees Celsius

- NSBT (N-sec-butyltryptamine): hydrochloride mp 175-177 degrees Celsius, shows CNS activity at 25-75 mg

- NTBT (N-tert-butyltryptamine): active at 5-20 mg, rumored to be potent when smoked

- NAT (N-amyltryptamine) and NHT (N-hexyltryptamine): reported inactive by Stephen Szara at the 1967 Ethnopharmacologic Search for Psychoactive Drugs conference

## The 5-Methoxy Frontier

Shulgin emphasizes that every one of these monoalkyltryptamine adventures has "an exact counterpart with the inclusion of that magical 5-methoxy group." Whatever is found with the unsubstituted (5-H) archetype is certain to be more potent and correspondingly unpredictable with a 5-methoxy substituent. Some 5-methoxy monoalkyltryptamines have been made, but most have not. Shulgin characterizes this as "open territory" with the directive: "Go west, young man."
