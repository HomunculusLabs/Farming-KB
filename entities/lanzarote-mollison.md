# Lanzarote

## Description

Lanzarote is the **easternmost of the Canary Islands**, a Spanish archipelago
located off the northwest coast of Africa. It is cited by Mollison as a
remarkable example of **large-scale condensation trap agriculture**, where
cinder-covered pits are used to grow grape vines in an extremely arid
environment. Each pit grows a single grape vine, harvesting atmospheric
moisture through the condensation of cool night air on the cinder surfaces.
This system has sustained viticulture on the island for centuries.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Geography — Volcanic Island
- **Category:** Place / Agricultural innovation site
- **Location:** Canary Islands, Spain (Atlantic Ocean, ~125 km west of Morocco)
- **Climate type:** Arid/subtropical desert (Köppen BWh)
- **Annual rainfall:** Approximately 100-150 mm (4-6 inches)
- **Coordinates:** 29°N, 13.5°W

## Geography and Geology

Lanzarote is a **volcanic island** formed by hotspot activity, with the most
dramatic landscape features resulting from the **Timanfaya eruptions of
1730-1736** that buried much of the island under lava flows and cinder
deposits. The island's geology directly enabled the condensation trap
agriculture for which it is known:

- Extensive **picón** (volcanic lapilli/cinder) deposits cover large areas,
  providing the raw material for condensation pit construction.
- The porous, dark cinders have excellent thermal properties — they heat
  rapidly by day and cool rapidly by night, maximizing condensation.
- Low rainfall is partially compensated by consistent **trade wind humidity**
  and strong nocturnal radiative cooling under clear skies.

## Key Facts

- The island's unique condensation agriculture uses **cinder-covered pits**
  where cold air sinks and condenses moisture on the porous volcanic rock.
- Each pit typically supports **one grape vine**, demonstrating how minimal
  water harvesting can sustain productive agriculture.
- The system is entirely **passive and energy-free**, relying on natural
  temperature differentials between day and night.
- Lanzarote is renowned for its **Malvasía wines**, produced from grapes grown
  in these condensation trap systems — particularly the La Geria wine region.
- The technique represents one of the most extreme examples of dryland
  agriculture, working with mean annual rainfall of only 10-15 cm (4-6 inches).
- Pit construction involves excavating a depression, planting the vine at the
  bottom, and surrounding it with a low semicircular stone wall (zoco) for
  wind protection.

## The La Geria System

The La Geria viticulture region is the most famous example of Lanzarote's
condensation agriculture:

1. **Excavation**: Conical pits 2-4 meters deep are dug into the cinder layer.
2. **Planting**: A single grape vine is planted at the bottom of each pit.
3. **Wall construction**: A semicircular stone wall (zoco) is built on the
   windward side to protect the vine from desiccating trade winds.
4. **Condensation cycle**: At night, cold air sinks into the pit and
   condenses moisture on the cinder surfaces. The condensed water drips down
   to the vine's root zone.
5. **Mulching**: The cinder layer acts as a mulch, preventing evaporation of
   the condensed moisture during the heat of day.
