# Coprinellus micaceus

## Overview

**Coprinellus micaceus**, commonly known as the **Mica Cap** or **Glistening
Inkcap**, is a widely distributed saprotrophic basidiomycete mushroom. It is
one of the most recognizable coprinoid fungi, growing in dense clusters on
stumps, buried roots, and [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]]
worldwide. The species epithet *micaceus* ("mica-like") refers to the fine,
glistening granules on the young cap surface — a distinctive field mark.

Though edible when young, C. micaceus contains small amounts of coprine (the
compound causing disulfiram-like reactions in *[[spore-print]]**: Dark brown to black.

### Microscopic Features

- **Spores**: Ellipsoid to broadly ellipsoid, 7-10 × 5-6 µm, smooth,
  thick-walled, with central germ pore. Dark brown in deposit.
- **Basidia**: 4-spored, clavate.
- **Cheilocystidia**: Numerous, cylindrical to flask-shaped (lageniform),
  sometimes capitate. Important diagnostic feature.
- **Pileipellis**: Cutis of cylindrical hyphae; mica-like granules composed
  of clusters of globose to ellipsoid cells.

## Ecology and Distribution

### Habitat

Obligate saprotroph on [[fungal-dead-wood-diversity]] of broadleaf trees, particularly oak, beech,
elm, maple, and ash. Frequently fruits from buried roots or subsurface wood,
making it appear to grow on soil or grass. [[mycelium]] persists in buried wood
for years, producing repeated mushroom flushes.

### Growth Pattern

Strongly caespitose (dense clusters), often with dozens to hundreds of
[[query-how-to-grow-and-use-oca-in-temperate-climates]]. Mushrooms are extremely
short-lived, deliquescing completely within 24-48 hours.

### Geographic Range

Nearly worldwide in temperate regions. Common throughout Europe, [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]]
(Alaska to Mexico), Asia, and Australasia. One of the most widely distributed
coprinoid fungi.

## Edibility and Safety

- **Edible when young** — before gills darken. Mild, pleasant flavor, though
  thin flesh limits culinary value.
- **Must be cooked** before consumption.
- **Coprine content**: Low but present. Avoid alcohol within 48-72 hours of
  consumption due to risk of disulfiram-like reaction (flushing, nausea,
  palpitations).
- **Foraging caution**: Positive identification essential to distinguish from
  *C. atramentaria* (much higher coprine levels).
- **Rapid deterioration**: Must be collected and prepared within hours of
  emergence due to autodeliquescence.
