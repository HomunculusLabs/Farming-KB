# Psilocybe washingtonensis

**Type:** Species (Fungus)
**Category:** [[psilocybin]]-containing mushroom
**Also known as:** Washington Psilocybe

## Overview
Psilocybe washingtonensis is a small, conic-capped psilocybin mushroom
described by Smith, found growing on decaying conifer wood in the
Pacific Northwest of the United States. Named for the state of
Washington where it was first documented, this species remains
insufficiently studied regarding its psychoactive properties. It belongs
to a cluster of difficult-to-delineate taxa that require microscopic
examination for confident identification.

## Key Details

### Taxonomy
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Hymenogastraceae
- **Genus:** Psilocybe
- **Authority:** Smith

### Morphology
- **Cap:** 1-2 cm broad. Obtusely conic to convex. Margin incurved at
  first, then straightening. Deep walnut brown, lighter towards the
  margin, hygrophanous, fading to dull cinnamon or pale pecan in drying.
  Surface smooth, margin may be ornamented with faint veil remnants,
  viscid when moist from a gelatinous pellicle (separable only in
  shreds). Context thin, pliant, nearly concolorous with cap when moist.
- **Gills:** Adnate or subdecurrent, close to subdistant, with two or
  three tiers of intermediate gills. Color slightly darker than cap at
  maturity.
- **Stem:** 30-50 mm long by 1.5-2.5 mm thick. Equal, tubular, pliant.
  Concolorous with cap but becoming blackish brown from base upwards
  in age. Surface covered with grayish fibrils, adorned with grayish
  mat of mycelium around base. Flesh dark brown, fading to pallid tan.
- **Partial veil:** Thinly cortinate, leaving fibrillose patches on
  stem and remnants along cap margin.
- **Spores:** Purplish brown in deposit, ellipsoid to slightly ovoid,
  6-7.5 (8) by 4-4.5 microns.
- **Microscopic features:** Basidia 4-spored. Pleurocystidia 38-56 (64)
  by 9-12 microns, narrowly fusoid-ventricose to lageniform with
  expanded necks. Cheilocystidia 18-38 by 7-12 microns, variable,
  fusoid-ventricose, often mucronate, or clavate to capitate.

### Habitat and Distribution
Found scattered to gregariously in forests, directly on decaying
conifer wood in the fall. Reported from Mt. Angeles on the Olympic
Peninsula of Washington State and along the Salmon River near Welches,
Oregon. Probably more widely distributed throughout the Pacific
Northwest.

### Chemistry
Activity unknown. Could be weakly psilocybin-active. No formal
chemical analyses have been published.

## Relevance
Psilocybe washingtonensis is part of a taxonomically challenging group
of mycenoid mushrooms that resemble several Hypholoma species
(including H. udum and H. dispersum). Accurate identification requires
microscopic examination, particularly of the pleurocystidia and
cheilocystidia. The overall aspect of this mushroom places it into a
cluster of difficult-to-delineate taxa that can easily be confused.

Its presence on decaying conifer wood in the Pacific Northwest places
it in one of the world's richest regions for psilocybin mushroom
diversity. The Olympic Peninsula of Washington and the Cascade region
of Oregon are known hotspots for multiple Psilocybe species. Its
similarity to Psilocybe physaloides and Psilocybe crobula further
complicates field identification.

The unknown activity status of P. washingtonensis makes it a species
of interest for future chemical analysis. Given its taxonomic proximity
to confirmed active species and the bluing tendencies observed in
related taxa, it could potentially contain low levels of psilocybin.
Further collections and analyses are needed to determine its
psychoactive profile.
