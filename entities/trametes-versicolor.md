# Trametes versicolor

**Trametes versicolor**, commonly known as the **turkey tail** mushroom, is a cosmopolitan polypore basidiomycete fungus belonging to the family Polyporaceae.
It is one of the most extensively researched medicinal mushrooms in the world, recognized for its concentrically zonate cap displaying bands of brown, tan, white, blue, and rust colors that resemble the tail feathers of a wild turkey.
Found on every inhabited continent, *T. versicolor* is a prolific wood-decay fungus that plays a critical ecological role in forest nutrient cycling and has been used in traditional medicine systems across Asia, Europe, and the Americas for centuries.

The species was originally described by Carl Linnaeus in 1753 as *Boletus versicolor* and has undergone multiple taxonomic revisions.
The epithet *versicolor* (Latin for "changing color") refers to the distinctive multicolored zoning pattern on the upper cap surface.
Recent molecular phylogenetic analyses using ITS and LSU rDNA sequences confirm its placement within the core polyporoid clade of Polyporales, closely related to *Trametes hirsuta* and *Trametes pubescens*.

## Description and Morphology

The fruiting body is a sessile (stemless) to laterally stipitate bracket fungus, typically 2–8 cm wide and 1–5 cm deep, with a fan-shaped to semicircular profile.
The upper surface (pileus) is densely tomentose when young, becoming smooth with age, and displays conspicuous concentric zones of varying colors — typically alternating bands of brown, rust, tan, cream, and bluish-green zones caused by algal colonization.
The pore surface (hymenophore) is white to cream, with 3–8 pores per mm, and tubes 1–5 mm deep.
The context (flesh) is tough, leathery, corky, and 1–3 mm thick, white to cream-colored, and inedible due to its woody texture.
Spores are cylindrical to slightly curved, hyaline, smooth, and measure 5–7 × 1.5–2.5 µm.
The spore print is white. There is no distinctive odor, and the taste is mild to slightly astringent.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Trametes versicolor* has a truly cosmopolitan distribution, occurring on every continent except Antarctica.
It is among the most common wood-decay fungi in temperate and tropical forests worldwide.
The species is a white-rot saprotroph, primarily decomposing hardwoods including oak (*Quercus* spp.), beech (*Fagus* spp.), birch (*Betula* spp.), maple (*Acer* spp.), and various fruit trees, though it also colonizes some conifers.
It typically fruits year-round on dead logs, stumps, and fallen branches, and can also act as a wound parasite on living trees.
The fungus thrives in a broad temperature range (5–35°C) and is remarkably resilient, often being one of the first fungal colonizers of freshly dead wood.

## Chemistry and Active Compounds

The primary bioactive constituents of *Trametes versicolor* are polysaccharides, particularly:

- **Polysaccharide-K (PSK, krestin)** — a protein-bound β-(1→4)-glucan with α-(1→3) and α-(1→6) branching, molecular weight approximately 100 kDa. First isolated in Japan in the 1960s from CM-101 strain. PSK accounts for the immunomodulatory and antitumor activity. Commercially available as a prescription anticancer drug in Japan since 1977.
- **Polysaccharide-peptide (PSP)** — a protein-bound polysaccharide isolated from the COV-1 strain by Chinese researchers, with similar but not identical immunomodulatory properties to PSK. Molecular weight approximately 100 kDa. Approved as an over-the-counter drug in China.
- **β-Glucans** — various β-(1→3)/(1→6)-D-glucans that activate innate immune responses via Dectin-1 and complement receptor 3 (CR3) pathways.
- **Triterpenoids** — including trametenolic acid, with reported anti-inflammatory activity.
- **Phenolic compounds** — including p-hydroxybenzoic acid, protocatechuic acid, and caffeic acid, contributing to antioxidant activity.

## Medicinal Applications and Research

### Cancer Adjunct Therapy

*Trametes versicolor* preparations, particularly PSK and PSP, are among the most clinically studied mushroom-derived therapeutics globally.
PSK has been approved as a cancer adjuvant in Japan since 1977, prescribed alongside standard chemotherapy and radiation for gastric, colorectal, breast, and lung cancers.
Multiple randomized controlled trials and meta-analyses have demonstrated that PSK supplementation significantly improves overall survival and disease-free survival in colorectal cancer patients when combined with conventional chemotherapy.
A Cochrane systematic review (2015) found moderate-quality evidence supporting PSK as an adjuvant to chemotherapy for gastrointestinal cancers, with hazard ratios for overall survival ranging from 0.67 to 0.82 across major trials.

### Immunomodulation

PSK and PSP enhance immune function through multiple mechanisms: activation of natural killer (NK) cells, enhancement of dendritic cell maturation and antigen presentation, stimulation of T-cell proliferation and cytokine production (IL-2, IFN-γ, TNF-α), and modulation of gut-associated lymphoid tissue (GALT) immunity.
PSK has been shown to selectively enhance cytotoxic T-lymphocyte activity against tumor cells while reducing chemotherapy-induced immunosuppression.

### Antiviral Properties

In vitro studies have demonstrated that *T. versicolor* extracts inhibit replication of human immunodeficiency virus (HIV), herpes simplex virus (HSV), and hepatitis C virus (HCV).
The proposed mechanisms include inhibition of viral attachment and entry, enhancement of host antiviral immune responses, and direct virucidal activity of polysaccharide fractions.

### Gut Microbiome Support

PSP has been shown to modulate the composition of the gut microbiota, increasing populations of beneficial *Bifidobacterium* and *Lactobacillus* species while reducing potentially pathogenic taxa.
This prebiotic effect contributes to enhanced gut barrier function and systemic immune regulation.

## Cultivation and Production

*Trametes versicolor* can be cultivated on various lignocellulosic substrates including hardwood sawdust, straw, corncobs, and agricultural waste.
Commercial production of PSK involves submerged fermentation of specific *T. versicolor* strains (primarily CM-101) in large-scale bioreactors.
Optimal fermentation conditions for PSK production: temperature 25–28°C, pH 5.0–6.0, dissolved oxygen >30% saturation, with glucose or molasses as carbon source and soybean meal or yeast extract as nitrogen source.
The global market for *T. versicolor* extracts is estimated at over $500 million annually, with major production centered in Japan (PSK) and China (PSP).

## Safety and Toxicity

*Trametes versicolor* is generally regarded as safe with a long history of dietary and medicinal use.
Acute oral toxicity studies in rodents report LD₅₀ values exceeding 15 g/kg body weight, indicating very low toxicity.
Reported adverse effects are mild and infrequent, including occasional gastrointestinal discomfort (nausea, bloating, diarrhea), darkening of stools, and rare allergic skin reactions.
No significant drug interactions have been reported, though theoretical immunostimulatory effects warrant caution in patients on immunosuppressive therapy or with autoimmune conditions.
PSK should not be confused with the unrelated chemotherapy drug paclitaxel (Taxol), despite similar-sounding names.
