# Trametes versicolor

*[[staycare-trametes-versicolor-bioremediation-applications]]*, commonly known as [[turkey-tail-mushroom-cancer-research]], is one [[allegro-star-of-the-morning-venus-and-the-sacred-mushroom]] most extensively researched medicinal mushrooms [[allegro-death-and-resurrection-in-the-mushroom-cult]] world [[triptolemus-eumolpus-and-the-founding-families-of-eleusis]] source [[allegro-colour-and-consistency-of-the-amanita-muscaria]] best-studied mushroom-derived cancer therapy. Recognizable by its concentrically zoned, multicolored fan-shaped fruiting bodies that resemble the tail feathers of a wild turkey, this ubiquitous polypore fungus has been the subject of hundreds of clinical trials, particularly in Asia, where its extracts are approved as adjunct cancer treatments.








## Taxonomy and Nomenclature

The species has undergone considerable taxonomic revision. It was long known as *Coriolus versicolor* and *Polyporus versicolor* before being placed in its current genus *Trametes*. In the Japanese medical literature, the species is most commonly referred to by its basionym-associated name *Coriolus versicolor*. The common name "[[turkey-tail-trametes-versicolor]]" is used universally in English-speaking countries. The species epithet *versicolor* ("of various colors") refers to the distinctive multicolored zoning of the cap surface, which displays bands of brown, tan, white, blue, green, orange, and red.








## Morphology

*Trametes versicolor* produces sessile (stemless) to short-stipitate fruiting bodies that are fan-shaped to semicircular, typically 2–8 cm in diameter and 1–3 mm thick. The upper surface is densely hairy to velvety and displays striking concentric zones of varying colors. The pore surface (hymenium) is white to cream, with tiny, angular pores (3–5 per mm). The flesh is thin, tough, leathery, and inedible. The spore print is white. Spores are cylindrical, 5–7 × 1.5–2.5 µm. The fungus is a [[white-rot-fungi-in-pesticide-and-btex-bioremediation]] decomposer that secretes lignin-modifying enzymes including laccase and manganese peroxidase, enabling it to break down the lignin component of wood.








## Distribution and Habitat

*T. versicolor* is among the most widely distributed fungi on Earth, occurring on every inhabited continent. It is a ubiquitous [[wood-decay-fungi-buildings]] fungus found on dead stumps, logs, and branches of a vast range of hardwood and conifer species. It is particularly common on oak, beech, birch, and various conifers. The species fruits year-round in temperate regions and is one of the first fungi to colonize recently dead wood, playing a major role in forest nutrient cycling. Its abundance and conspicuous appearance make it one of the most commonly encountered mushrooms worldwide.








## Bioactive Compounds

The medicinal properties of *T. versicolor* are attributed primarily to its polysaccharide constituents, particularly the [[beta-glucan-receptor-binding]] polysaccharopeptide (PSP) and polysaccharide-K (PSK, also known as krestin). PSP and PSK are protein-bound polysaccharides with molecular weights of approximately 100 kDa, consisting of a beta-1,4-glucan backbone with beta-1,3 and beta-1,6 side chains bound to peptide residues. These compounds act primarily as immunomodulators, stimulating various components of the immune system including natural killer (NK) cells, macrophages, dendritic cells, and T-lymphocytes. Additional bioactive compounds include triterpenoids, sterols, and various phenolic compounds with antioxidant properties.








## Clinical Applications in Cancer

*T. versicolor* extracts have the most extensive clinical evidence base of any medicinal mushroom for cancer support. PSK has been approved as an adjuvant cancer therapy in Japan since 1977 and is prescribed alongside conventional chemotherapy and radiation for gastric, colorectal, breast, and lung cancers. PSP is approved for similar indications in China. Meta-analyses of randomized controlled trials have shown that PSK supplementation significantly improves overall survival, disease-free survival, and immune function markers in patients with various cancers, particularly gastrointestinal cancers. The typical mechanism involves enhancement of host anti-tumor immune responses rather than direct cytotoxic effects on tumor cells. PSK has demonstrated the ability to restore immunosuppression induced by chemotherapy, enhance the activity of NK cells and cytotoxic T-lymphocytes, and modulate cytokine production.








## Other Medicinal Applications

Beyond oncology, *T. versicolor* has been investigated for [[antiviral-properties-of-mushrooms]] (particularly against HPV and HIV), hepatoprotective effects, anti-inflammatory activity, and as a prebiotic for gut microbiome modulation. The mushroom's immunomodulatory effects make it relevant to a wide range of conditions involving immune dysregulation, including chronic fatigue syndrome, autoimmune conditions, and recurrent infections. It is one of the most commonly used medicinal mushrooms in traditional Chinese medicine, where it has been employed for centuries under various names.








## Preparation and Dosage

Medicinal preparations of *T. versicolor* include hot [[water-management]] extracts (decoctions), dual hot water/ethanol extracts, and refined polysaccharide isolates (PSK, PSP). The mushroom is too tough and woody for culinary use, so extraction is essential to access bioactive compounds. Typical doses of PSK in clinical studies range from 3 g/day, while whole mushroom extract doses are typically 2–3 g/day of dried extract. The product is widely available as a dietary supplement in capsule, tablet, tincture, and powder form in Western markets, though the standardization and quality of commercial preparations varies considerably.
