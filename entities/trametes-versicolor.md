# Trametes versicolor

Trametes versicolor, commonly known as turkey tail, is a bracket fungus in
the family Polyporaceae and one of the most extensively studied medicinal
mushrooms worldwide. It is recognized for its fan-shaped fruiting bodies
displaying concentric zones of brown, tan, white, and occasionally blue-green.

The common name derives from the resemblance of its colorful, overlapping
fruiting bodies to the fanned tail plumage of a wild turkey. In traditional
Chinese medicine it is known as yun zhi (cloud mushroom) and has been used
for centuries as a tonic for immune support and vitality.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Polyporales
- **Family:** Polyporaceae
- **Genus:** Trametes
- **Species:** T. versicolor (L.) Lloyd

### Synonyms

- Boletus versicolor L. (basionym, 1753)
- Coriolus versicolor (L.) Quél.
- Polyporus versicolor (L.) Fr.
- Bjerkandera versicolor (L.) P. Karst.

## Morphology

- **Cap:** 2–8 cm diameter, fan-shaped to semicircular, often overlapping
  in rosette formations. Surface distinctly zonate with concentric color bands.
- **Upper surface:** Velvety to smooth; grey, brown, rust, cream, occasionally
  blue-green where light-exposed. Zones sharply delineated.
- **Pore surface:** White to cream, small round pores (3–5 per mm).
- **Flesh:** Thin (1–2 mm), tough and leathery, white to cream.
- **Stipe:** Usually absent; when present, lateral, short, dark.
- **Spore print:** White.
- **Spores:** Cylindrical to ellipsoid, 5–7 × 1.5–2.5 µm, smooth, hyaline.
- **Hyphal system:** Trimitic (generative, skeletal, binding hyphae).

## Distribution and Habitat

Trametes versicolor has a nearly global distribution across temperate and
tropical forests, found throughout North America, Europe, Asia, Africa, and
Australia. It is a primary white-rot decomposer colonizing dead hardwood
stumps, logs, and branches from a very wide host range.

Common substrate species include oak (*Quercus*), beech (*Fagus*), birch
(*Betula*), and maple (*Acer*). Fruiting occurs year-round in temperate
regions, most prolifically in autumn and spring during sustained moisture.

## Cultivation

Commercial cultivation targets extraction of polysaccharide-K (PSK) and
polysaccharide-peptide (PSP) rather than whole fruiting body harvest.

- **Substrate:** Supplemented hardwood sawdust (oak/beech) with 10–20%
  wheat bran or rice bran.
- **Spawn run:** 20–30 days at 22–25°C, humidity >85%.
- **Fruiting induction:** Reduced temperature (18–22°C), increased FAE,
  ambient indirect light.
- **Biological efficiency:** 30–50% on supplemented sawdust.

## Bioactive Compounds

- **Polysaccharide-K (PSK, krestin):** β-(1→4)-glucan with β-(1→3)/(1→6)
  branching, protein-bound (~100 kDa). Approved as adjuvant cancer therapy
  in Japan since 1977. The most studied compound from this species.
- **Polysaccharide-peptide (PSP):** Similar β-glucan with peptide moieties,
  isolated from Chinese strain COV-1. Approved as adjuvant in China.
- **Triterpenoids:** Lanostane-type with anti-inflammatory activity.
- **Phenolic compounds:** p-Hydroxybenzoic acid, protocatechuic acid,
  caffeic acid — contribute antioxidant capacity.
- **FIP-tvc:** Fungal immunomodulatory protein (~12 kDa) from fruiting bodies.

## Pharmacology

- **Immunomodulation:** PSK and PSP activate dendritic cells, promote Th1
  cytokine response (IL-2, IFN-γ), and enhance NK cell cytotoxicity via
  TLR-2 and dectin-1 receptor pathways.
- **Antitumor activity:** Significant effects in vitro and in vivo against
  gastric, colorectal, breast, and lung cancers. Acts primarily as an
  immunotherapeutic adjuvant, enhancing chemotherapy and radiation efficacy
  while reducing side effects.
- **Antioxidant:** Methanolic extracts show strong DPPH scavenging (IC₅₀
  ~50 µg/mL), attributed to phenolic constituents.
- **Anti-inflammatory:** Triterpenoids inhibit NF-κB signaling, reducing
  TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β production in macrophage models.
- **Antiviral:** Preliminary activity against HIV, HPV, and HSV; clinical
  evidence remains limited.

## Ecological Role

As a white-rot fungus, T. versicolor degrades lignin preferentially over
cellulose using lignin peroxidase (LiP), manganese peroxidase (MnP), and
laccase. It is a model organism for studying bioremediation of lignocellulosic
biomass and environmental pollutants including PAHs and PCBs.

## Permaculture and Agroforestry Applications

- **Log inoculation:** Can be inoculated onto hardwood logs in food forests
  for medicinal harvest and accelerated woody debris decomposition.
- **Composting accelerator:** Inoculation of wood chip mulch piles speeds
  breakdown of coarse woody material and nutrient cycling.
- **Mycoremediation:** Demonstrated degradation of PAHs, PCBs, and
  pharmaceutical contaminants in soil and water.

## Related Species

- **Trametes hirsuta:** Similar zonate cap but hirsute surface; less
  studied medicinally.
- **Trametes ochracea:** Orange-brown zones, larger pores; European.
- **Stereum ostrea:** Similar bracket but lacks pores (smooth hymenium),
  thinner flesh.
- **Fomitopsis betulina:** Birch polypore, larger, brown pore surface;
  medicinal with different compound profile.
