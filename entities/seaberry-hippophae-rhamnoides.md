# Seaberry (Hippophae rhamnoides)

## Overview

Hippophae rhamnoides, commonly known as seaberry, sea buckthorn, or sandthorn, is a
deciduous shrub in the family Elaeagnaceae. Native to the temperate zones of Europe
and Asia, it ranges from the Atlantic coasts of Norway and Britain across Central
Europe and the Caucasus to the Altai Mountains, northern China, and the Himalayas.
The species is renowned for its bright orange berries, which contain exceptionally
high concentrations of vitamin C (400–1700 mg/100 g), vitamin E, carotenoids, and
rare omega-7 fatty acids (palmitoleic acid).

  Berries are consumed by thrushes, waxwings, and pheasants.
- **Pioneer species:** Tolerates nutrient-poor soils and full exposure, enabling
  establishment in disturbed sites where it prepares conditions for less hardy species.
- **Windbreak:** Rapid growth and dense branch structure make effective shelterbelts,
  reducing wind speed by 40–60% within 5× height distance.

## Nutritional and Medicinal Properties

Seaberry berries are among the most nutritionally dense fruits known:

- **Vitamin C:** 400–1700 mg/100 g fresh weight (10–40× orange), retained after
  freezing and moderate heating
- **Vitamin E (tocopherols):** 100–300 mg/100 g in seed oil, among the highest
  plant sources
- **Carotenoids:** β-carotene, lycopene, zeaxanthin — contributing to the orange
  pigmentation and provitamin A activity
- **Omega fatty acids:** Unique combination of omega-3 (α-linolenic), omega-6
  (linoleic), omega-9 (oleic), and omega-7 (palmitoleic acid) in both seed and
  pulp oils
- **[[aact-tannins-terpenes-phenolic-compounds-wood-composting-ingham]]:** Quercetin, kaempferol, isorhamnetin, and ellagic acid
  derivatives with documented antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activity

Clinical and preclinical studies report hepatoprotective effects (reducing ALT/AST
elevations), improvement in atopic dermatitis with topical seed oil, cardiovascular
benefit from improved lipid profiles, and gastroprotective activity against gastric
ulcers. Traditional Chinese Medicine prescribes seaberry for cough, digestive
disorders, and skin conditions.
