# Rolling Greenhouse

## Description
A rolling greenhouse is a [[coleman-mobile-greenhouse-design-and-operation]] placed on wheels that run on pipe rails, enabling the structure to be moved between plots with minimal effort. Developed by Eliot Coleman as an evolution of his earlier sled-based mobile greenhouses, the rolling design solved directional stability issues and allowed for wider end-wall openings, improved ventilation, and easier access. The rolling greenhouse is central to Coleman's multi-plot rotation system for year-round production.

## Classification
- **Category**: Protected agriculture / Mobile greenhouse
- **Type**: Wheeled hoop house on rail system
- **Scale**: Typically 22 x 48 feet (1,056 sq ft)
- **Mobility**: Moves on pipe rails between multiple plots

## Key Facts

### Structural Design
- Built on wheels that run on pipe rails (200 feet of pipe rail per side)
- Wheels provide directional stability — eliminates the crossbar needed on sled models
- The directional stability of the leading edge is assured by the rail system
- Diagonal bracing added for structural integrity during moves
- Secured at corners with metal fence posts driven into ground at slight angle
- Fence posts bolted to brackets extending from each corner

### End-Wall Design
- Wide 12-foot opening at both ends (compared to narrow doors on sled models)
- Narrow 2-foot door for winter access between large opening and sidewall
- Wide doorway covered with plastic sheet secured at top and sides with wiggle-wire channel
- Bottom edge held down with sandbags
- Wind brace added in winter to secure large plastic door sheet and prevent flapping

### Ventilation System
- **Winter**: Unclip wiggle wire across top and upper sides, drop plastic down to open large area. Snow and cold air cannot blow in across crops at soil level.
- **Early spring**: Roll up door from bottom during day, down at night. 2-foot-wide plastic strip stretched horizontally across bottom as air barrier.
- **Summer**: Roll up doors at both ends entirely, tie in place. Maximum ventilation and easy access.

### Movement and Anchoring
- Moves between four sites covering a 200-foot-long area
- Moved approximately once a month (five to six moves per year)
- T-post puller used to quickly extract fence-post anchors
- Fence-post anchors not quite as secure as screw-in ground anchors but very easy to use
- Coleman reports no problems with the fence-post system

### Rail System
- Initially purchased only 100 feet of pipe rail per side (required moving rails)
- Subsequently upgraded to full 200 feet per side to eliminate the "hassle factor"
- Coleman notes that saving small amounts on infrastructure often creates disproportionate extra work

### Rotation Integration
- Used in two-plot, three-plot, and four-plot rotation systems
- Four-plot, five-moves rotation example yields: onions/scallions, early carrots/beets, zucchini/beans, tomatoes, cucumbers, and lettuce — all from one greenhouse per year
- Crops harvestable five weeks earlier and/or five weeks later than unprotected crops
- Extends sales season by approximately 2.5 months

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

### Controlled Environment Mobility
- The concept of moving a controlled environment over different crops has potential [[grass-seed-substrate-for-mushroom-cultivation]]
- A mobile [[comparison-laminar-flow-hood-vs-fruiting-chamber]] could be moved over different mushroom bed plots on a rotation schedule
- The rail system concept could be scaled for moving mushroom grow tents between [[bulk-substrate-preparation]] areas

### Soil-Based Systems
- Rolling greenhouses maintain a connection to living soil — relevant for [[growing-gourmet-outdoor-mushroom-bed-establishment]] systems
- The movement cycle allows soil recovery between crops, similar to how mushroom beds need rest periods
- Open-air exposure between rotations provides natural sanitation (UV, weather, competing organisms)

### Season Extension
- Demonstrates that mobile protection can dramatically extend the productive season
- Same principle applicable to protecting outdoor mushroom beds during cold months
- The progressive hardiness rotation (tender → semi-hardy → hardy crops) could be adapted for different mushroom species with varying cold tolerance

## Comparison with Sled-Based Mobile Greenhouse
| Feature | Sled Model | Rolling Model |
|---------|-----------|---------------|
| Mobility | Skids on ground | Wheels on rails |
| Directional stability | Requires crossbar | Assured by rails |
| End-wall opening | Narrow doors | 12-foot wide |
| Ventilation | Limited | Excellent |
| Access | Awkward crossbar step-over | Wide open access |
| Complexity | Simpler construction | More infrastructure |
