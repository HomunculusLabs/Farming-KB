## Alpinia galanga

## Overview

Alpinia galanga, commonly known as greater galangal, Thai galangal, or siamese ginger, is a perennial herbaceous plant in the family Zingiberaceae. Native to [[comparison-essential-oils-vs-neem-oil-vs-insecticidal-soap]] differ — A. galanga is rich in 1'-acetoxychavicol acetate and ethyl cinnamate, while A. officinarum contains predominantly galangin and kaempferide.

## Morphology

### Vegetative Description

- **Growth habit:** Robust perennial herb, 1.5–3.5 m tall under optimal conditions. Forms dense clumps from an extensive rhizome network. Erect pseudostems composed of overlapping leaf sheaths.
- **Rhizome:** Thick, cylindrical, branched, 3–8 cm diameter, 10–30 cm per segment. External surface light brown to reddish-brown, rough and ringed. Internal white to pale yellow, sometimes pink-tinged, with strong aromatic, spicy, piney-camphoraceous odor.
- **Leaves:** Large, lanceolate to oblong-lanceolate, 30–60 cm × 5–15 cm, glossy dark green above, paler below. Leaf base cordate or rounded. Tip acuminate. Midrib prominent. Petiole 1–3 cm with 1–2 cm ligule.
- **Pseudostem:** Composed of tightly rolled leaf sheaths, green, sometimes purple-tinged at base, 2–4 cm diameter.

### Reproductive Structures

- **Inflorescence:** Terminal, erect, panicle-like raceme, 15–30 cm long. Bracts green, boat-shaped, 2–3 cm, each subtending 1–3 flowers.
- **Flowers:** Bisexual, zygomorphic, 3–4 cm long, fragrant. Calyx tubular, 3-toothed, 1–1.5 cm. Corolla tubular, 2.5–3.5 cm, white to pale yellow with prominent labellum — ovate, 2–3 cm, white with yellow central band and red-purple veins. One fertile stamen; staminodes reduced.
- **Fruit:** Capsule, globose to ovoid, 1–1.5 cm, smooth, green becoming brown, containing numerous small seeds.
- **Seeds:** Small, arillate, black, irregular, 3–4 mm.
- **Flowering:** Year-round in tropics, peaking May–September. Pollinated by bees and butterflies.

## Distribution and Habitat

- **Native range:** Uncertain, likely peninsular Malaysia, Indonesia, or southern Thailand. Widely naturalized in tropical Southeast Asia.
- **Current distribution:** Cultivated and naturalized across tropical Asia (India, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh, Myanmar, Thailand, Vietnam, Laos, Cambodia, Malaysia, Indonesia, Philippines, southern China), [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-south-america]].
- **Major production:** Thailand, Indonesia, Vietnam, China (Hainan, Guangxi, Yunnan), India (Assam, Kerala, Karnataka).
- **Habitat:** Tropical lowland and montane forests, forest margins, disturbed areas, and cultivated fields. Prefers warm, humid conditions with annual rainfall of 1,500–3,000 mm.
- **Altitude:** Sea level to 1,200 m. Optimal at 200–800 m.
