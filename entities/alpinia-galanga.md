# Alpinia galanga

## Overview

Alpinia galanga, commonly known as greater galangal, Thai galangal, or siamese ginger, is a perennial herbaceous plant in the family Zingiberaceae. Native to Southeast Asia, it is one of the most important aromatic spice plants in the region, essential in Thai, Indonesian, Malaysian, and Cambodian cuisines. Beyond culinary use, A. galanga has a long history in Ayurvedic, Thai, and Chinese traditional medicine, with modern research validating anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, antioxidant, and anticancer activities. The rhizome is the primary plant part used as both spice and medicine.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Division:** Angiospermae
- **Class:** Magnoliopsida
- **Order:** Zingiberales
- **Family:** Zingiberaceae
- **Subfamily:** Alpinioideae
- **Tribe:** Alpinieae
- **Genus:** Alpinia
- **Species:** A. galanga (L.) Willd. (1797)

- **Synonyms:**

- *Maranta galanga* L. (1753) — original basionym
- *Alpinia aromaticum* Nees (1836) — widely used synonym in older literature
- *Languas galanga* (L.) Stuntz — synonym in Southeast Asian botanical literature
- *Amomum galanga* (L.) L. — nomenclatural synonym

### Key Distinction from Alpinia officinarum

A. galanga (greater galangal) and A. officinarum (lesser galangal) are frequently confused but are distinct species. A. galanga has larger rhizomes with pinkish internal color, a ginger-pepper flavor, and is used in Southeast Asian cooking. A. officinarum has smaller, darker rhizomes with more pungent, resinous flavor and is used primarily in Chinese medicine. Their essential oils differ — A. galanga is rich in 1'-acetoxychavicol acetate and ethyl cinnamate, while A. officinarum contains predominantly galangin and kaempferide.

## Morphology

### Vegetative Description

- **Growth habit:** Robust perennial herb, 1.5–3.5 m tall under optimal conditions. Forms dense clumps from an extensive rhizome network. Erect pseudostems composed of overlapping leaf sheaths.
- **Rhizome:** Thick, cylindrical, branched, 3–8 cm diameter, 10–30 cm per segment. External surface light brown to reddish-brown, rough and ringed. Internal white to pale yellow, sometimes pink-tinged, with strong aromatic, spicy, piney-camphoraceous odor.
- **Leaves:** Large, lanceolate to oblong-lanceolate, 30–60 cm × 5–15 cm, glossy dark green above, paler below. Leaf base cordate or rounded. Tip acuminate. Midrib prominent. Petiole 1–3 cm with 1–2 cm ligule.
- **Pseudostem:** Composed of tightly rolled leaf sheaths, green, sometimes purple-tinged at base, 2–4 cm diameter.

### Reproductive Structures

- **Inflorescence:** Terminal, erect, panicle-like raceme, 15–30 cm long. Bracts green, boat-shaped, 2–3 cm, each subtending 1–3 flowers.
- **Flowers:** Bisexual, zygomorphic, 3–4 cm long, fragrant. Calyx tubular, 3-toothed, 1–1.5 cm. Corolla tubular, 2.5–3.5 cm, white to pale yellow with prominent labellum — ovate, 2–3 cm, white with yellow central band and red-purple veins. One fertile stamen; staminodes reduced.
- **Fruit:** Capsule, globose to ovoid, 1–1.5 cm, smooth, green becoming brown, containing numerous small seeds.
- **Seeds:** Small, arillate, black, irregular, 3–4 mm.
- **Flowering:** Year-round in tropics, peaking May–September. Pollinated by bees and butterflies.

## Distribution and Habitat

- **Native range:** Uncertain, likely peninsular Malaysia, Indonesia, or southern Thailand. Widely naturalized in tropical Southeast Asia.
- **Current distribution:** Cultivated and naturalized across tropical Asia (India, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh, Myanmar, Thailand, Vietnam, Laos, Cambodia, Malaysia, Indonesia, Philippines, southern China), Pacific Islands, tropical Africa, and South America.
- **Major production:** Thailand, Indonesia, Vietnam, China (Hainan, Guangxi, Yunnan), India (Assam, Kerala, Karnataka).
- **Habitat:** Tropical lowland and montane forests, forest margins, disturbed areas, and cultivated fields. Prefers warm, humid conditions with annual rainfall of 1,500–3,000 mm.
- **Altitude:** Sea level to 1,200 m. Optimal at 200–800 m.
- **Soil:** Rich, well-drained loamy soils with high organic matter, pH 5.0–7.5. Intolerant of waterlogging.
- **Climate:** Minimum 15°C; optimal 25–35°C. Killed by frost. Requires 60–80% humidity.

## Phytochemistry

### Essential Oil

- **Essential oil:** 1.0–3.0% yield by hydrodistillation. Primary contributor to A. galanga's aroma and bioactivity.

- **Major constituents:** 1'-Acetoxychavicol acetate (15–35%), ethyl cinnamate (5–20%), 1,8-cineole (eucalyptol, 5–15%), α-pinene (2–8%), β-pinene (2–6%), limonene (1–5%), farnesene (1–4%), p-cymene (1–3%).
- **Variability:** Essential oil composition varies with geographic origin, harvest time, and processing. Thai varieties tend to be higher in 1'-acetoxychavicol acetate; Indonesian varieties often have higher ethyl cinnamate.

### Non-volatile Compounds

- **Flavonoids:** Galangin (3,5,7-trihydroxyflavone), kaempferide, alpinin, and glycosides. Galangin is the most studied, with significant anti-inflammatory and anticancer activity.
- **Phenylpropanoids:** 1'-Acetoxychavicol acetate (ACA) and derivatives — major contributors to antimicrobial and anticancer activity.
- **Diterpenes:** Galanal A and B with notable anti-inflammatory and antiulcer properties.
- **Diarylheptanoids:** Including (E)-1,7-bis(4-hydroxy-3-methoxyphenyl)hept-4-en-3-one, with anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects.
- **Starch:** Rhizome contains 30–40% starch on dry weight basis.

## Pharmacology and Medicinal Uses

### Anti-inflammatory Activity

- **Mechanism:** Galangin inhibits COX-2 and 5-LOX, suppresses NF-κB activation, and reduces pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6).
- **1'-Acetoxychavicol acetate (ACA):** Potent NO inhibitor (IC50 2.5 µM). Also inhibits iNOS and COX-2 at transcriptional level.
- **Traditional use:** Applied topically for rheumatism, muscle pain, and skin inflammation in Thai medicine.

### Antimicrobial Activity

- **Antibacterial:** Active against Gram-positive (S. aureus, B. subtilis, S. mutans) and some Gram-negative species. MIC 62.5–500 µg/mL.
- **Antifungal:** Active against C. albicans, A. niger, and dermatophytes. ACA shows particularly strong antifungal activity.
- **Antiviral:** Activity against HIV-1 protease (ACA IC50 4 µg/mL) and influenza virus in vitro.
- **Antiparasitic:** In vitro activity against P. falciparum and L. donovani.

### Anticancer Activity

- **Anticancer:** ACA and galangin induce apoptosis in multiple cancer lines (HeLa, MCF-7, HL-60, A549) via caspase activation, mitochondrial depolarization, and G2/M arrest. Extract reduced tumor volume 40–60% in Ehrlich ascites and DMBA models.

### Gastrointestinal and Digestive

- **Carminative:** Rhizome chewed or consumed as tea for indigestion, flatulence, and bloating — supported by spasmolytic activity on intestinal smooth muscle.
- **Antiulcer:** Galanal A and B show gastroprotective effects, reducing ulcer index by 50–70% in rodent models.
- **Traditional use:** Key ingredient in Thai digestive tonics; consumed to stimulate appetite and relieve nausea.

### Neuropharmacological

- **Cognitive enhancement:** Improved memory in scopolamine-induced amnesia models (animal studies).
- **Anxiolytic:** Aqueous extract shows anxiolytic-like effects without sedation at effective doses.

## Culinary Uses

- **Thai cuisine:** Essential in tom yum, tom kha gai, green/red/massaman curry, and stir-fries. The sliced rhizome provides aromatic backbone and heat.
- **Indonesian cuisine:** Key component of rendang spice paste, sambal, and nasi goreng. Known as "laos" or "lengkuas."
- **Vietnamese cuisine:** Used in bún bò Huế and regional dishes.
- **Flavor profile:** Piney, camphoraceous, citrusy, with peppery heat and ginger-like pungency.

## Permaculture and Agroforestry Applications

- **Understory crop:** Excellent understory component in tropical food forests, growing well in partial shade beneath canopy trees.
- **Companion planting:** Aromatic rhizome may deter soil nematodes and insect pests when interplanted with vegetables.
- **Economic value:** High-value cash crop for smallholders. 2–3 harvests/year from established clumps. Market demand growing internationally.
- **Propagation:** Rhizome division or seed (2–3 weeks germination). Spacing 50–80 cm. 15–30 tonnes/ha/year fresh rhizome.
