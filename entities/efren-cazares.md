## Description

Dr. Efren Cazares is a mycology researcher who trained under [[jim-trappe]] at Oregon State University as a graduate student and post-doctoral researcher. He specializes in the identification and measurement of vesicular-arbuscular mycorrhizal (VAM) and ectomycorrhizal fungal colonization. Cazares plays a critical role in the [[soil-foodweb]] Inc. (SFI) ecosystem by performing mycorrhizal sample analyses and training SFI laboratory technicians in the identification and measurement of both VAM and [[ectomycorrhizal-fungi]].

## Classification

- **Category**: Scientist / Researcher
- **Training**: Graduate student and post-doc under Jim Trappe, Oregon State University
- **Specialty**: VAM and ectomycorrhizal fungi identification and measurement
- **Role**: Mycorrhizal sample analyst; SFI technician trainer

## Key Facts

- Trained directly under Jim Trappe, the leading ectomycorrhizal authority
- Performs VAM and ectomycorrhizal sample analyses
- Responsible for training SFI technicians in mycorrhizal identification and measurement techniques
- Expert in distinguishing VAM from ericoid and ectomycorrhizal colonization
- His work enables SFI to provide commercially available mycorrhizal colonization assessments
- Contributes to the dataset linking mycorrhizal colonization percentages to plant health outcomes

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Cazares represents the critical bridge between academic mycorrhizal research and commercial soil biology testing. By training SFI technicians, he enabled the scaling of mycorrhizal assessment beyond university labs to become accessible to everyday growers and farmers. His expertise in differentiating VAM from ericoid and ectomycorrhizal colonization is technically demanding — these distinctions matter because different plant communities associate with different [[mycorrhizal-types]], and the colonization thresholds for plant health vary by type. Cultivators who send soil samples to SFI or similar labs for mycorrhizal assessment are ultimately benefiting from the identification protocols Cazares established and transmitted to the technician workforce.

## Source Context

Ingham identifies Cazares as "one of Jim Trappe's grad students/post-docs" who "does many of the VAM and ecto samples, and works to train our SFI techs to do the ID and measurement of VAM and ectos." His role was essential in making mycorrhizal assessment a routine part of [[soil-food-web]] analysis.
