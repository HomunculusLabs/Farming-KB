 Glycyrrhiza glabra
Glycyrrhiza glabra, commonly known as licorice (or liquorice), is a
perennial herbaceous legume native to Southern Europe, the Middle East,
and parts of Asia. One of the most extensively used medicinal plants in
human history, licorice root has been employed in traditional medicine
systems across cultures for over 4,000 years. The root contains
glycyrrhizin (glycyrrhizic acid), a triterpenoid saponin 30–50 times
sweeter than sucrose, responsible for both the characteristic sweet taste
and the majority of the plant's pharmacological activity. Licorice remains
a critical component in modern phytotherapy, traditional Chinese medicine
(where it appears in roughly half of all herbal formulas), and the food
and pharmaceutical industries.

G. inflata (inflated licorice), and G. echinata (Hungarian licorice).
## Botanical Description
### Growth Habit
Herbaceous perennial growing 60–120 cm tall, forming extensive rhizome
and root systems that can extend 1–2 meters deep. Spreads via underground
stolons and can colonize large areas.
### Leaves and Stems
- **Stems**: Erect, branching, slightly woody at base, 60–120 cm
- **Leaves**: Pinnately compound, 10–20 cm long, with 9–17 leaflets
- **Leaflets**: Elliptic to ovate, 2–4 cm long, sticky glandular undersides
- **Stipules**: Small, lanceolate, caducous
### Flowers
- **Inflorescence**: Axillary racemes, 5–12 cm long
- **Flowers**: Papilionaceous, 8–12 mm long, pale purple to blue-violet
- **Calyx**: Bell-shaped, 5-toothed, 3–4 mm
- **Blooming period**: June–August (Northern Hemisphere)
### Fruit and Roots
- **Fruit**: Oblong legume pod, 2–3 cm long, brown when mature, 2–5 seeds
- **Roots**: Thick, cylindrical, yellow-brown exterior, bright yellow
  interior, extremely sweet — the commercially important part
## Distribution and Habitat
### Native Range
Mediterranean basin, Middle East (Turkey, Iran, Iraq), Central Asia,
and Western China.
### Major Cultivation Regions
China (largest global producer), Turkey, Iran, Afghanistan, Pakistan,
Spain, Italy, Greece, India (Rajasthan, Gujarat), and limited US cultivation.
