## Overview

Carex lupulina, commonly known as hop sedge, is a perennial sedge native to
eastern and central North America. It belongs to Carex section Lupulinae, a
group noted for thick, hop-like inflorescences that resemble the fruiting cones
of [[humulus-lupulus]].

The species is common in bottomland hardwood forests, floodplain swamps, and
riparian corridors throughout its range. It is a facultative wetland plant
(FACW) that tolerates both prolonged inundation and briefly drier conditions.

Hop sedge is one of the more recognizable Carex species due to its stout,
densely flowered spikes and broad leaves. It often co-occurs with Carex
crinita, Carex lupuliformis, and Carex tribuloides in lowland wetland
communities.

The species ranges from southern Maine and southern Ontario west to eastern
Nebraska and south to eastern Texas and northern Florida.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Carex lupulina was described by Gotthilf Heinrich Ernst Muhlenberg in 1803
and belongs to Carex section Lupulinae. The authority is C. lupulina Muhl. ex
Willd., published in Species Plantarum, edition 4, volume 2, page 321.

Section Lupulinae is characterized by thick, ovoid to ellipsoid perigynia that
give the spikes a hop-like appearance. This group includes approximately 15
species in North America.

The plant forms loose clumps from short, thick rhizomes. Culms are sharply
triangular, 50 to 120 centimeters tall, and 2 to 4 millimeters thick, with
smooth angles toward the base.

Leaf blades are 6 to 15 millimeters wide, firm in texture, and conspicuously
M-shaped in cross-section. The sheaths are green to brown, and the ligules are
5 to 10 millimeters long, much longer than in many other Carex species.

The inflorescence is androgynous, typically with 3 to 5 staminate spikes above
2 to 5 pistillate spikes. The pistillate spikes are thick and ovoid, 1 to 2.5
centimeters long, densely flowered, and nearly sessile.

Perigynia are broadly ovate to orbicular, 4 to 7 millimeters long, thick and
leathery, with a short, bidentate beak. The surface is smooth to finely
papillose, and the nerves are prominent.

## Distribution and Habitat

Carex lupulina ranges from southern Maine west through southern Ontario,
Michigan, and Wisconsin to eastern Nebraska and eastern Kansas, south to
eastern Texas, and east to northern Florida.

The species is most common in the Mississippi River valley, the Ohio River
valley, and the Atlantic coastal plain. It becomes less frequent in the
Appalachian highlands and is absent from the interior of the Great Plains.

It inhabits bottomland hardwood forests, floodplain swamps, alluvial
thickets, oxbow ponds, and slow-moving stream margins. Elevation ranges from
near sea level to approximately 500 meters.

Soils range from clay to silty loam with high organic content. The species
prefers neutral to slightly acidic conditions but tolerates a broad pH range
from approximately 4.5 to 7.5.

## Ecology

Carex lupulina is a common component of bottomland hardwood forest herb layers
and floodplain wetland communities. It is associated with [[quercus-palustris]],
[[acer-saccharinum]], and [[platanus-occidentalis]] canopy trees in many floodplain
systems.
