# Johanne Hanko

## Description

Johanne Hanko is an international consultant and technical specialist in disability matters who served as the author and primary developer of the FAO training manual "[[holzer-lebenshilfe-permaculture-project-for-disabled-people]]," implemented in [[ubon-ratchathani]] province, Thailand.

## Classification

- **Category**: Person / Author / Development specialist
- **Role**: International consultant for FAO TCP/THA/8821(A)
- **Specialization**: Technical specialist in disability matters
- **Publication**: Author of the FAO training manual on [[mushroom-cultivation]] for people with disabilities

## Contributions

### Training Manual Development
Hanko authored the comprehensive training manual that covers:
- The complete training methodology for people with disabilities
- Three-in-one training approach: rehabilitation + mushroom cultivation + enterprise development
- Selection criteria and procedures for trainees
- Motivational session design and delivery
- Hands-on mushroom cultivation curriculum covering 20 steps from tissue culture to business management
- Building and equipment adaptations for accessibility
- Monitoring and evaluation frameworks for follow-up support

### Training Methodology
Hanko developed the holistic "three-in-one" training approach that integrates:
1. **Rehabilitation**: Physical, mental, and emotional support for self-acceptance and confidence building
2. **Technical Skills**: Complete mushroom cultivation from laboratory tissue culture through production and processing
3. **Enterprise Development**: Business planning, basic bookkeeping, and marketing strategies

### Disability-Inclusive Design
The manual reflects Hanko's expertise in adapting training and work environments for diverse disabilities:
- Physical adaptations for wheelchair users and people with limited mobility
- Creative problem-solving approaches for task modification
- Recognition of individual differences even among people with the same disability
- Emphasis on capability rather than limitation
- Mental disability inclusion with appropriate support strategies

## Methodological Importance

Hanko's work is significant because it treats mushroom cultivation as a complete livelihood system rather than as a single technical skill. The manual moves from motivation and self-confidence to laboratory work, production, processing, bookkeeping, and follow-up. This sequence recognizes that a trainee may learn to produce mushrooms yet still fail without support in planning, marketing, or household-level enterprise management.

Her disability focus also changes how agricultural training is evaluated. Success is not measured only by yield; it includes whether tools, benches, buildings, lesson pacing, and peer support allow different bodies and learning styles to participate. That makes the manual relevant to broader [[ubon-ratchathani-mushroom-project]]. Together, these roles show that durable training programs require technical accuracy, institutional coordination, and learner-centered pedagogy.

## Training Philosophy

The manual's philosophy is participatory and confidence-building. Trainees are not treated as passive recipients of charity; they are potential producers who need appropriate instruction, adapted workspaces, and a realistic path to income. This framing matters because it shifts disability programming from care alone toward agency and enterprise.

Hanko also emphasizes follow-up after formal training. New growers need troubleshooting, encouragement, and market support when they return home, especially if family members or community expectations are uncertain. The training center is therefore only one stage in a longer transition toward self-managed production.

## Broader Use

The model can inform other livelihood programs that combine technical agriculture with rehabilitation. Its main lesson is that accessibility must be designed into the entire system: recruitment, motivation, classroom methods, tools, buildings, crop choice, finance, and market connection.
