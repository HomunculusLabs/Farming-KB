# Panax ginseng

## Overview

Panax ginseng, commonly known as Asian ginseng or Korean ginseng, is one of the most valued medicinal plants in traditional Chinese medicine (TCM), where it has been used for over 2,000 years as a tonic to restore vitality, strengthen the body, and promote longevity. The genus name Panax derives from the Greek word "panakos" meaning "all-healing," reflecting its reputation as a panacea. Its pharmacological activity is primarily attributed to a unique class of triterpene saponins called ginsenosides, of which over 40 have been identified. Modern research has validated adaptogenic, immunomodulatory, neuroprotective, anti-diabetic, and anti-cancer properties. Panax ginseng is distinguished from American ginseng (Panax quinquefolius) by its warming energetics in TCM and a different ginsenoside ratio favoring Rg1 and Rb1.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Clade:** Tracheophytes (vascular plants)
- **Clade:** Angiosperms (flowering plants)
- **Clade:** Eudicots
- **Clade:** Asterids
- **Order:** Apiales
- **Family:** Araliaceae (ginseng family)
- **Genus:** Panax
- **Species:** P. ginseng C.A. Mey., 1842
- **Common names:** Asian ginseng, Korean ginseng, Chinese ginseng, ren shen (人參, Chinese), insam (인삼, Korean), ninjin (ニンジン, Japanese)
- **Etymology:** "Panax" from Greek "pan" (all) + "akos" (healing). "Ginseng" from Chinese "rénshēn" meaning "person-root," referring to the root's frequent resemblance to a human figure.

## Description

- **Growth habit:** Slow-growing perennial herb, 30–60 cm tall at maturity. Wild plants may take 6–10 years to reach harvestable root size; cultivated plants are typically harvested at 4–6 years.
- **Leaves:** Compound, palmately divided into 5 leaflets (rarely 3–4 in younger plants). Each leaflet is elliptic to obovate, 5–15 cm long, serrate margins, dark green above and lighter below. Leaves arranged in a single whorl at the apex of the stem.
- **Flowers:** Small, greenish-white, arranged in a single terminal umbel. Each flower has 5 petals, 5 stamens, and a 2- or 3-carpellate ovary. Blooms June–July.
- **Fruit:** Bright red drupe, 5–7 mm diameter, containing 1–3 flattened seeds. Ripens August–September.
- **Roots:** Fleshy, fusiform taproot that often develops forked branches resembling human arms and legs. Outer surface pale yellow to light brown, inner tissue white to cream. The root is the primary medicinal organ, with ginsenoside content increasing with plant age.
- **Whole plant:** Dies back to ground level each winter, regenerating from the root crown in spring. Requires 3–5 years of vegetative growth before flowering.

## Habitat and Distribution

- **Native range:** Mountainous forests of the Korean Peninsula, northeastern China (Manchuria), and the Russian Far East (Primorsky Krai).
- **Naturalized range:** Limited naturalization outside native range due to specific cultivation requirements.
- **Habitat:** Understory of deciduous broadleaf forests at 400–1,000 m elevation. Prefers shaded conditions (10–30% full sunlight), well-drained loamy soils rich in organic matter, pH 5.5–6.5, and cool temperate climates with cold winters for dormancy.
- **Cultivation:** Major commercial cultivation in South Korea (Geumsan, Ganghwa), China (Jilin, Liaoning provinces), and increasingly in the United States and Canada. Requires 4–6 years from seed to harvestable root. Shade structures (often nylon netting) are used to simulate forest understory conditions.
- **Propagation:** By seed (requires 18–22 months stratification for germination) or by root division. Seed-grown plants produce higher quality roots.

## Chemistry and Bioactive Compounds

Panax ginseng contains a diverse array of bioactive compounds, with ginsenosides being the primary pharmacologically active constituents:

### Ginsenosides (triterpene saponins)

- **Classification:** Over 40 ginsenosides identified, broadly classified into two structural groups: protopanaxadiols (PPD; e.g., Rb1, Rb2, Rc, Rd, Rg3, Rh2) and protopanaxatriols (PPT; e.g., Rg1, Re, Rf, Rh1).
- **Rb1:** The most abundant ginsenoside. Neuroprotective, anti-inflammatory, promotes neurite outgrowth, enhances hippocampal-dependent learning and memory.
- **Rg1:** Potent neurotrophic factor, enhances synaptic plasticity, improves cognitive function, and demonstrates anti-fatigue activity. Central to the nootropic reputation of ginseng.
- **Rg3:** Formed during processing (steaming/red ginseng). Demonstrates significant anti-cancer activity through inhibition of angiogenesis and induction of apoptosis. Less abundant in fresh root but enriched in red ginseng.
- **Compound K:** A metabolite produced by intestinal microbiota from Rb1 and Rb2. More bioavailable than parent ginsenosides; exhibits potent anti-inflammatory and anti-tumor activity.
- **Total ginsenoside content:** 1.5–5% dry weight depending on age, cultivar, processing method, and growing conditions.

### Other constituents

- **Polysaccharides:** Ginseng polysaccharides exhibit immunomodulatory activity, enhancing macrophage function and natural killer cell activity.
- **Peptides and amino acids:** Including glutamine, arginine, and the bioactive peptide ginsentin.
- **Volatile oils:** Sesquiterpenes and monoterpenes contributing to the characteristic aroma.
- **Polyacetylenes:** Panaxynol and panaxydol with anti-platelet and anti-inflammatory properties.
- **Minerals:** Rich in germanium, calcium, magnesium, potassium, and phosphorus.
- **Vitamins:** B-group vitamins and vitamin C present in leaf and root tissues.

## Pharmacology and Traditional Uses

### Traditional Chinese Medicine classification

In TCM, Panax ginseng is classified as a qi tonic of the highest order, said to strongly tonify original qi (yuan qi), strengthen the spleen and lungs, generate fluids, and calm the spirit. It is considered warm in nature and sweet, slightly bitter in flavor. White ginseng (sun-dried) is milder, while red ginseng (steamed and dried) is warmer and more potent. It is one of the "superior" herbs in the Shennong Ben Cao Jing (Divine Farmer's Materia Medica), the earliest Chinese pharmacopoeia (c. 200 CE).

### Adaptogenic and anti-fatigue activity

- Modulates the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis, enhancing stress resilience and reducing cortisol dysregulation
- Improves physical endurance and reduces exercise-induced fatigue in human clinical trials
- Enhances oxygen utilization and mitochondrial function in skeletal muscle
- Reduces mental fatigue and improves cognitive performance under stress conditions

### Neuroprotective and nootropic effects

- Rg1 and Rb1 promote neurogenesis in the hippocampus and enhance synaptic plasticity through BDNF and CREB signaling
- Demonstrates protective effects against Alzheimer's disease pathology in animal models, reducing amyloid-beta accumulation and tau hyperphosphorylation
- Improves working memory, attention, and reaction time in controlled human studies
- Reduces age-related cognitive decline in epidemiological studies

### Immunomodulatory effects

- Enhances innate immune function through activation of macrophages, natural killer cells, and dendritic cells
- Modulates adaptive immunity by influencing T-cell and B-cell proliferation and cytokine production
- Ginseng polysaccharides are primarily responsible for immunostimulatory activity
- Shown to reduce the incidence and duration of upper respiratory infections in clinical trials

### Anti-diabetic effects

- Improves insulin sensitivity and glucose uptake in peripheral tissues
- Rb1 and Re enhance GLUT4 translocation and insulin signaling
- Demonstrates modest but consistent reductions in fasting blood glucose and HbA1c in meta-analyses of clinical trials

### Cancer chemoprevention

- Rg3, Rh2, and compound K demonstrate anti-cancer activity against multiple tumor types including breast, prostate, lung, liver, and colorectal cancers
- Mechanisms include inhibition of tumor angiogenesis, induction of apoptosis, cell cycle arrest, and suppression of metastasis
- Korean Red Ginseng extract (standardized to contain Rg3 and Rh2) has been investigated as an adjunctive cancer therapy in numerous clinical trials, showing improved quality of life and reduced fatigue in cancer patients

### Processing: White vs Red ginseng

- **White ginseng (baek-insam):** Fresh root dried in the sun or at low temperature. Milder, cooling energetics. Preserves original ginsenoside profile.
- **Red ginseng (hong-insam):** Fresh root steamed at 95–100°C for 2–3 hours, then dried. The steaming process converts malonyl-ginsenosides to their deglycosylated forms and generates rare ginsenosides (Rg3, Rh2, Rk1, Rg5) with enhanced bioactivity. Korean Red Ginseng is a government-certified product with strict quality standards.

## Legal Status and Safety

- **Regulatory status:** Approved as a dietary supplement in the United States, European Union, Japan, and most countries. Korean Red Ginseng has GRAS (Generally Recognized as Safe) status. Listed in the Chinese, Japanese, and Korean pharmacopoeias.
- **Contraindications:** Should be avoided during acute infections with fever (per TCM principle of not tonifying during acute illness). Caution in individuals with hypertension, bleeding disorders, or those taking anticoagulants/warfarin (ginsenosides may have anti-platelet effects). Avoid in autoimmune conditions with overactive immune response.
- **Drug interactions:** May interact with warfarin, antiplatelet drugs, MAO inhibitors, and stimulants (caffeine, amphetamines). May potentiate hypoglycemic effects of diabetes medications.
- **Side effects:** Generally well-tolerated at recommended doses (1–3 g dried root daily or 200–400 mg standardized extract). Insomnia, headache, gastrointestinal upset, and palpitations may occur at higher doses.
