# Panax ginseng

## Overview

*Panax ginseng*, commonly known as Korean ginseng or Asian ginseng, is a slow-growing perennial herb in the family Araliaceae. It is one of the most valued medicinal plants in East Asian traditional medicine systems, with a documented history of use spanning over 2,000 years. The genus name *Panax* derives from the Greek word "panakos" meaning "all-healing," reflecting its reputation as a panacea.

## Botanical Description

### Root
- The root is the primary medicinal organ, fleshy and forked, resembling a human figure
- Fresh roots are creamy-white to pale yellow; processed roots vary from white (air-dried) to reddish-brown (steamed/red ginseng)
- Root growth is extremely slow: 1-year-old roots weigh approximately 1–5 g, while 6-year-old roots reach 30–80 g
- Wild roots can be decades old and command extraordinary prices

### Aerial Parts
- Height: 30–60 cm at maturity
- Compound leaves with 5 leaflets, each 5–15 cm long with serrated margins
- Produces a single terminal umbel of small greenish-white flowers in summer
- Fruit is a bright red drupe, 4–5 mm diameter, containing 1–3 seeds

## Phytochemistry

The pharmacological activity of *P. ginseng* is primarily attributed to ginsenosides (also called ginseng saponins), a diverse group of triterpene saponins. Over 180 ginsenosides have been identified.

### Major Ginsenosides
- **Rb1**: Most abundant protopanaxadiol-type; neuroprotective, anti-inflammatory
- **Rg1**: Protopanaxatriol-type; enhances cognitive function, promotes neurogenesis
- **Re**: Protopanaxatriol-type; antioxidant, cardioprotective
- **Rd**: Protopanaxadiol-type; immunomodulatory, anti-apoptotic
- **Rg3**: Rare compound, enriched in red ginseng; anti-tumor properties
- **Compound K**: Gut metabolite of Rb1 and Rd; high bioavailability, potent bioactivity

### Non-Saponin Constituents
- **Polysaccharides**: Ginsan (immunomodulatory acidic polysaccharide)
- **Polyacetylenes**: Panaxynol, panaxydol (anti-inflammatory, anti-tumor)
- **Phenolic compounds**: Salicylic acid, caffeic acid derivatives
- **Alkaloids**: Trace amounts of beta-carboline alkaloids
- **Vitamins and minerals**: B-group vitamins, germanium (trace), magnesium

## Traditional Processing Methods

### White Ginseng (백삼)
- Fresh roots are peeled and air-dried in the sun for 2–3 weeks
- Retains a pale, creamy color
- Higher proportion of malonyl-ginsenosides
- Considered "cooling" in TCM energy theory (yin-nourishing)

### Red Ginseng (홍삼)
- Fresh roots are steamed at 95–100°C for 2–3 hours, then dried
- The Maillard reaction produces reddish-brown pigments
- Steam processing converts malonyl-ginsenosides to their deglycosylated forms (e.g., Rg3, Rh2)
- Considered "warming" in TCM (yang-strengthening)
- More stable for long-term storage

## Pharmacological Properties

### Adaptogenic Activity
*P. ginseng* is classified as an adaptogen — a substance that increases the body's resistance to stress. Mechanisms include:
- Modulation of the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis
- Regulation of cortisol levels under chronic stress
- Enhancement of cellular resistance to oxidative damage via Nrf2 pathway activation

### Neurological Effects
- Improves cognitive performance, particularly working memory and attention
- Neuroprotective effects in models of Alzheimer's and Parkinson's diseases
- Promotes hippocampal neurogenesis via BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor) upregulation
- Reduces neuroinflammation through inhibition of microglial activation

### Immunomodulation
- Enhances innate immune cell activity (NK cells, macrophages)
- Modulates adaptive immune responses (T-cell and B-cell regulation)
- Ginsan polysaccharide demonstrated anti-tumor immune enhancement in preclinical studies

### Metabolic Effects
- Improves insulin sensitivity and glucose metabolism
- Lipid-lowering effects: reduces triglycerides and LDL cholesterol
- Anti-diabetic properties demonstrated in multiple clinical trials

## Cultivation

### Growing Conditions
- Requires 70–80% shade (shade cloth structures are standard)
- Optimal soil: deep, well-drained loam, pH 5.5–6.5, high organic matter
- Temperature range: 15–25°C during the growing season; cold winters required for dormancy
- Cultivation cycle: typically 4–6 years before root harvest

### Cultivation Systems
1. **Mountain cultivation (산양삼)**: Semi-wild, seeds sown in forested mountain sites
2. **Field cultivation**: Intensive management with fertilization, pest control
3. **Hydroponic cultivation**: Emerging method for standardized year-round production

### Major Producing Regions
- South Korea (Geumsan, Chungcheongbuk-do) — world's largest producer of red ginseng
- China (Changbai Mountains, Jilin Province)
- North Korea (Kaesong region, historically significant)

## Dosage and Safety

### Typical Therapeutic Doses
- **Standardized extract (4–7% ginsenosides)**: 200–400 mg/day
- **Crude dried root powder**: 1–2 g/day
- **Fresh root**: 3–6 g/day (traditional decoction)

### Contraindications and Interactions
- May interact with anticoagulants (warfarin), antiplatelet agents, and MAO inhibitors
- Cautious use in patients with autoimmune conditions due to immunostimulatory effects
- Avoid during acute infections (TCM principle)
- Possible estrogenic effects — use with caution in hormone-sensitive conditions
