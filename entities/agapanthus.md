## Description

*Agapanthus* (lily of the Nile) is a clump-forming perennial bulb recommended
by Mollison as a **barrier plant** for garden edges in permaculture systems.
Its dense root mass and strap-like leaves form an effective barrier against
grass invasion when planted as part of a multi-layered garden border system.
The plant also produces attractive globe-shaped flower heads in blue or white.




## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Family**: Amaryllidaceae (formerly Alliaceae)
- **Genus**: *Agapanthus*
- **Species**: Various, commonly *A. africanus* and *A. praecox*
- **Common names**: Lily of the Nile, African lily
- **Growth form**: Clump-forming bulbous perennial, 0.5–1.5 m tall





## Key Facts

- Native to southern Africa; widely naturalised in temperate and subtropical
  regions worldwide.
- Forms dense clumps of strap-like leaves from a fleshy root system.
- Produces tall flower stems with spherical clusters of blue, purple, or white
  flowers in summer.
- Extremely hardy and drought-tolerant once established.
- Recommended by Mollison as one of five barrier plant types for garden edges:
  1. Deep-rooted broadleaf (e.g., comfrey)
  2. Clump grass (e.g., lemongrass, vetiver)
  3. Carpeting plant (e.g., sweet potato, nasturtium)
  4. Dense low shrub (e.g., *Oncoba*, *Coprosma*, *Echium*)
  5. **Bulb** (e.g., *Agapanthus*, *Canna*)





## Barrier Functions

- Dense root mass prevents **grass runners** (kikuyu, buffalo grass) from
  penetrating garden beds.
- Thick foliage forms a visual and physical barrier along garden edges.
- Combines effectively with other barrier types in a multi-species hedge.
- When first planted, needs mulching, manuring, and watering to establish.
- Once established, provides **fire barrier** function and ample mulch material.





## Relevance to Cultivation / Mycology

- Dense root mats create **mycorrhizal networks** that connect garden bed soil
  to border soil, facilitating nutrient exchange.
- Leaf litter from spent foliage provides **organic matter** for decomposer
  fungi in the garden border zone.
- The barrier function prevents grass roots from competing with **mycorrhizal
  fungi** associated with garden crop roots.
- Agapanthus roots can be divided and replanted — disturbed root zones
  temporarily release **fungal spores and organic compounds** into surrounding
  soil.
- As part of a multi-species border, contributes to **biodiverse soil ecology**
  supporting a wide range of fungal species.
