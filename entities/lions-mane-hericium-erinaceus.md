# Lion's Mane (Hericium erinaceus)

## Overview

Lion's Mane is a species of edible and medicinal mushroom belonging to the tooth fungus group, native to North America, Europe, and Asia. It is recognized by its distinctive cascading white spines that resemble a lion's mane, and has been used for centuries in traditional Chinese and Japanese medicine. Modern research has focused on its neuroprotective and cognitive-enhancing properties, making it one of the most studied medicinal mushrooms in contemporary neuroscience.

## Morphology

### Fruiting Body

The fruiting body of *Hericium erinaceus* is one of the most visually distinctive among edible mushrooms. It forms a single, unbranched mass of soft, dangling spines that can reach 2–25 cm in diameter. The spines (teeth) are typically 1–6 cm long, white when fresh, turning yellowish-brown with age. Unlike many mushrooms, it lacks a cap and gills entirely—the spines themselves are the spore-producing surface.

### Mycelium

The mycelium is white, robust, and grows aggressively on hardwood substrates. In culture, it forms dense, cottony networks that are relatively fast-colonizing compared to other medicinal mushrooms. This makes it commercially viable for both log-based and substrate-based cultivation.

### Spores

Spores are white, roughly spherical to broadly ellipsoid, measuring 5–7 × 4–6 μm. They are amyloid and smooth, produced in abundance from the surface of the spines.

## Habitat and Distribution

Lion's Mane is primarily found on dead or dying hardwood trees, especially oak (*Quercus* spp.), walnut (*Juglans* spp.), beech (*Fagus* spp.), and sycamore (*Platanus* spp.). It is a saprotrophic and occasionally parasitic white-rot fungus that decomposes lignin and cellulose in dead wood.

## Bioactive Compounds

### Hericenones and Erinacines

The most significant [[query-what-are-the-main-bioactive-compounds-in-medicinal-plants]] Lion's Mane are hericenones (found in the fruiting body) and erinacines (found in the mycelium). These are small, lipid-soluble molecules that can cross the blood-brain barrier and stimulate **Nerve Growth Factor (NGF)** synthesis in the brain.

- **Hericenones C–H:** Isolated from the fruiting body, these stimulate NGF production in astrocytes
- **Erinacines A–I:** Isolated from the mycelium, particularly erinacine A, which shows potent NGF-inducing activity

### Beta-Glucans

Lion's Mane contains significant levels of β-(1,3)/(1,6)-D-glucans, which contribute to its immunomodulatory effects. These polysaccharides activate macrophages, NK cells, and dendritic cells through Dectin-1 and complement receptor 3 [[plant-signaling-pathways]].

### Other Compounds

- **Hericerin:** A fatty acid derivative with anti-inflammatory properties
- **4-Hydroxy-2-ethyl-5-methyl-3(2H)-furanone:** Flavor compound responsible for seafood-like taste
- **Various [[ingham-tannins-terpenes-phenolic-compounds]]:** Antioxidant activity including hispidin and hericenols

## Medicinal Properties

### Neuroprotection and Neurogenesis

The most well-documented therapeutic application of Lion's Mane is in neurological health. Erinacines and hericenones promote NGF synthesis, which supports:

- **Neuronal survival and maintenance** of basal forebrain cholinergic neurons
- **Myelination** of neurons in the peripheral nervous system
- **Synaptic plasticity** in the hippocampus, associated with memory formation
- **Axonal regeneration** following nerve injury

Animal studies have demonstrated improved cognitive function in aged rats, with increased recognition memory and reduced age-related cognitive decline. Human clinical trials have shown promising results for mild cognitive impairment (MCI), with significant improvements in cognitive function after 16 weeks of supplementation.
