# Colin Tudge

## Description

Colin Tudge is a British biologist, science writer, and author known for his work on biodiversity, agriculture, and the relationship between science and society. Mentioned by Bill Mollison in *Permaculture: A Designers' Manual* for his influential 1986 article in *New Scientist* in which he estimated that perhaps 60% of the British Isles could be returned to nature while still meeting human food needs through intensive horticulture rather than extensive agriculture.

## Classification

- **Category**: Person / Scientist / Author
- **Nationality**: British
- **Fields**: Biology, agricultural science, science communication
- **Era**: Late 20th century to present

## Key Facts

- Published influential analysis in New Scientist (1986) on land use and rewilding potential of the British Isles
- Estimated that approximately 60% of the British Isles could be given back to nature
- This conservative estimate supported the permaculture argument that intensive food production on small areas can free vast tracts for ecological restoration
- Mollison contrasted this with John Jeavons' even more ambitious estimate of 94% based on biointensive gardening methods
- His work helped challenge the assumption that agriculture (rather than individual and market gardening) provides the food people actually eat
- Advocate for the idea that small-scale intensive horticulture is more productive per unit area than broadacre agriculture
- His 1986 article provided quantitative backing for permaculture land use principles

## Published Works and Contributions

- Articles in New Scientist on land use, biodiversity, and rewilding (notably 1986)
- Writings on the science of food production and ecological agriculture
- Analysis of the proportion of land needed for food versus potential for ecological restoration
- Contributions to the debate on agriculture versus horticulture as the future of food production
- Books on biodiversity, trees, and the philosophy of science

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

### Land Use Philosophy
- Tudge's estimates supported the permaculture vision of minimal footprint food production
- Demonstrated that the majority of land currently used for agriculture is not needed for food
- His analysis provided scientific backing for rewilding and ecological restoration movements
- Challenged the agricultural establishment's assumptions about land requirements
- Showed that [[bat-conservation-and-housing|conservation]] and food production need not be in conflict

### The Agriculture vs Horticulture Debate
- Tudge and others highlighted what Mollison calls a common fallacy: that broadacre agriculture (not horticulture) will provide future food
- Market gardening and intensive horticulture produce significantly more food per unit area
- This distinction is central to permaculture [[cervantes-breeding-program-design-goal-setting|design]] philosophy
- Supports the case that approximately 4% of land could be sufficient for food production through intensive methods
- John Jeavons estimated even higher: 94% could be returned based on biointensive gardens

### Conservation and Rewilding
- His work on returning land to nature aligns with permaculture ethics of earth care
- Demonstrated that conservation and food production need not be in conflict
- Provided quantitative estimates that made the case for rewilding credible and specific
- Influenced subsequent thinking about ecosystem restoration at landscape scale
- His ideas prefigured the modern rewilding movement in the UK and Europe

## Legacy

- Frequently cited in permaculture literature as supporting evidence for intensive land use
- His 1986 New Scientist article became a key reference point for the movement
- Contributed to the growing body of evidence that modern agriculture uses far more land than necessary
- His thinking helped bridge mainstream scientific discourse with [[permaculture-design-principles]]
- His conservation vision aligned with permaculture's goal of reducing the ecological footprint of food production
