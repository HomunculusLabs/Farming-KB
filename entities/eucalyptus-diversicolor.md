# Eucalyptus diversicolor

## Description

Eucalyptus diversicolor, commonly known as **karri**, is one of the tallest hardwood trees in the world, reaching heights of up to 90 metres. Native to the wet southwestern corner of Western Australia, it is a dominant canopy species in the karri [[fukuoka-pine-forest-ecosystem-collapse-cascade-matsutake]]. The species produces pale cream to light brown timber that is strong and moderately durable.

## Classification

- **Family:** Myrtaceae
- **Genus:** Eucalyptus
- **Species:** E. diversicolor
- **Common names:** Karri, Western Australian karri

## Key Facts

- Among the tallest flowering plants in the world, reaching 60–90 m in height
- Native to the high-rainfall zone (over 1000 mm annually) of southwestern Western Australia
- Smooth bark throughout, shedding in strips to reveal pale cream to white surfaces
- Timber is pale cream to light brown, strong, and moderately durable
- Fast-growing in suitable conditions
- Prefers deep, well-drained loamy or gravelly soils in high-rainfall areas
- Important commercial timber species in Western Australia

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

- **Timber:** Valued for structural applications including flooring, joinery, and building framing (pages 95, 116)
- **Firewood:** Good quality firewood, though not as dense as jarrah (page 95)
- **Honey:** Produces abundant nectar; karri honey is commercially significant (page 95)
- **Windbreaks:** Tall, fast growth makes it suitable for large-scale windbreak plantings
- **Pulp and paper:** Timber is used in pulp production (page 95)
- **Reforestation:** Used in reforestation programs in high-rainfall zones of Western Australia

## Cultivation Notes

- Requires high rainfall (minimum 900–1000 mm annually)
- Prefers deep, well-drained loamy or gravelly soils
- Fast [[soil-temperature-plant-growth-rate-doubling-rule]] in favourable conditions
- Intolerant of frost when young
- Best suited to temperate to [[query-how-to-grow-rare-tropical-fruits-in-marginal-and-subtropical-climates]] with reliable summer rainfall or Mediterranean winter-wet patterns
- Can be planted for timber in 30–50 year rotations
- Susceptible to Phytophthora dieback in poorly drained sites
