## Description

**Batrachochytridium dendrobatidis** (often abbreviated Bd) is a chytrid fungus and the causative agent of chytridiomycosis, an emerging infectious disease responsible for catastrophic amphibian population declines worldwide. It infects the keratinized skin of amphibians, disrupting osmoregulation and often leading to death.








## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Phylum**: Chytridiomycota
- **Class**: Chytridiomycetes
- **Order**: Rhizophydiales
- **Family**: Batrachochytriaceae
- **Genus**: Batrachochytridium
- **Species**: B. dendrobatidis








## Key Facts

- Identified as the major mortality factor in motorbike frogs in Australia (Morell, 1999)
- Reported as an emerging infectious disease of amphibians linked to climate change (Reed et al., 2000)
- Causes skin abnormalities in infected frogs, especially around the mouth
- Associated with widespread frog population declines in Panama (Lips, 1999; Kaiser, 1998)
- Has caused oral deformities in up to 41% of larval mountain yellow-legged frogs ([[rana-mucosa]]) in the Sierra Nevada of California (Fellers et al., 2001)
- Climate change believed to be one driver of increased incidence








## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Bd represents one of the most significant examples of a fungal pathogen causing population-level effects on vertebrate wildlife. For mycologists, it illustrates the ecological impact that chytrid fungi can have beyond their typical roles as saprotrophs and algal parasites. The species' dependence on moist conditions for transmission is relevant to understanding fungal disease dynamics in aquatic and riparian habitats. Bd research has driven advances in fungal disease surveillance, diagnostics, and host-pathogen coevolution studies.








## Ecological Role

Bd has emerged as one of the greatest threats to global amphibian biodiversity. It has been implicated in the decline or extinction of hundreds of amphibian species worldwide. The fungus disrupts amphibian skin function critical for osmoregulation and electrolyte balance. Its spread has been facilitated by global trade in amphibians and potentially by climate change altering environmental conditions to favor fungal growth.
