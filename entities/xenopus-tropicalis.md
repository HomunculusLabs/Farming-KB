## Description

**Xenopus tropicalis**, the western clawed frog, is a species of aquatic frog in the family Pipidae native to West Africa. It has been reported to suffer from Chlamydia species infections causing respiratory disease, with significant mortality in imported breeding colonies.









## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Animalia
- **Phylum**: Chordata
- **Class**: Amphibia
- **Order**: Anura
- **Family**: Pipidae
- **Genus**: Xenopus
- **Species**: X. tropicalis









## Key Facts

- Reed et al. (2000) reported Chlamydia species causing respiratory disease in up to 90% of imported frogs
- One breeding colony lost 90% of individuals within 4 months due to fungal infection
- Imported into the United States from West Africa
- Used as a model organism in developmental biology and genetics research









## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

X. tropicalis appears in the mycological literature as an example of how fungal-like pathogens can devastate amphibian populations, particularly in captive settings. The high mortality in breeding colonies illustrates the vulnerability of concentrated populations to fungal disease, a principle relevant to any dense cultivation system.









## Ecological Role

X. tropicalis is native to West African rainforests, where it inhabits temporary pools and flooded areas. It is an important model organism in biomedical research, particularly for studies of vertebrate development and genetics.
