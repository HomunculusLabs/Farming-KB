# Comfrey (*Symphytum officinale*)

## Overview

Comfrey is a perennial herbaceous plant in the Boraginaceae family, native to Europe and widely naturalized across temperate regions. Renowned in permaculture as a "dynamic accumulator" — a plant that mines minerals from deep subsoil layers and brings them to the surface — comfrey is valued equally as a medicinal herb (particularly for wound healing) and as an essential component of sustainable growing systems. Its common name derives from Latin *con firma* (with strength), referencing traditional use in healing broken bones.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Order:** Boraginales
- **Family:** Boraginaceae
- **Genus:** *Symphytum*
- **Species:** *S. officinale*

### Key Cultivars

- **'Bocking 14':** Sterile hybrid (*S. × uplandicum*), most recommended for permaculture — does not set seed
- **'Bocking 4':** Higher protein content, preferred for fodder
- **Russian Comfrey (*S. × uplandicum*):** Hybrid between *S. officinale* and *S. asperum*; larger and more vigorous

The Bocking cultivars were developed by Lawrence D. Hills at the Henry Doubleday Research Association (now Garden Organic) in the UK.

## Botanical Description

Comfrey forms large clumps 60–150 cm tall with thick, hairy stems and large lanceolate leaves 15–60 cm long. Bell-shaped flowers bloom late spring through summer: purple/violet/white (*S. officinale*), blue-violet (Bocking 14).

The defining feature is the deep taproot extending 1.8–3+ meters, mining potassium, phosphorus, calcium, magnesium, and trace minerals from subsoil layers. Root fragments as small as 1 cm can regenerate.

## Distribution

Native to temperate Europe, naturalized across North America, New Zealand, and parts of Asia. Prefers damp, fertile soils in partial shade to full sun. Hardy to USDA zones 3–9.

## Active Compounds

### Allantoin

The most significant medicinal compound — a cell-proliferant that stimulates tissue repair and cell growth. Accelerates wound healing by promoting mitosis in connective tissue. Widely used in dermatological products; GRAS.

### Mucilage

Polysaccharides forming a protective, soothing gel on mucous membranes and skin. Anti-inflammatory and demulcent properties.

### Rosmarinic Acid

Potent anti-inflammatory and antioxidant. Inhibits lipoxygenase and cyclooxygenase enzymes.

### Pyrrolizidine Alkaloids (PAs) — SAFETY WARNING

Comfrey contains hepatotoxic PAs (symphytine, echimidine, lycopsamine) that can cause veno-occlusive liver disease, pulmonary hypertension, and hepatic carcinoma.

- **Roots:** Highest PA content (up to 0.3%) — NOT for internal use
- **Leaves:** Lower PA content; Bocking 14 has among the lowest concentrations
- **PA-free cultivars:** Developed in New Zealand

The FDA warned against internal use in 2001. Banned as internal medicine in Australia, Canada, Germany, and others.

### Other Compounds

Tannins (astringent), silica, protein (up to 30% dry leaf weight), potassium (3–5× farmyard manure).

## Permaculture Applications

### Dynamic Accumulator

Deep taproot mines subsoil minerals concentrated in leaves: potassium (3–5× manure), calcium, and trace minerals (iron, magnesium, copper, zinc).

### Comfrey Tea (Liquid Fertilizer)

Pack leaves in a container, add water, steep 2–4 weeks. Dilute 1:10. NPK approximately 1.8–0.5–8.0. Best for fruiting and flowering plants (tomatoes, peppers, potatoes, berries).

### Mulch and Biomass

- 4–7 harvests per season; up to 20+ kg per plant per year
- Leaves break down rapidly (2–4 weeks), releasing potassium-rich nutrients
- Excellent mulch for tomatoes, potatoes, fruit trees, cucurbits
- Dense barrier planting suppresses weeds effectively

### Companion Planting and Soil Building

Benefits potatoes (reduces scab), tomatoes, fruit trees, currants. Attracts pollinators. Deep roots break compacted subsoil, improve drainage, and support mycorrhizal networks.

## Medicinal Uses

### External Use (Recommended)

Wound healing, bone healing, bruises, sprains, joint and muscle pain, eczema, psoriasis, minor burns, insect bites, varicose veins. Comfrey ointments (5–20% extract) approved in several European countries (4–6 week treatment limit).

### Internal Use (NOT Recommended)

Hepatotoxic PAs can cause veno-occlusive disease, liver fibrosis, cirrhosis, and death. Risks outweigh any potential benefits.

## Cultivation

- **Propagation:** Root cuttings (5–10 cm sections) preferred; crown divisions in spring/autumn
- **Spacing:** 60–90 cm apart
- **First cutting:** At ~60 cm tall; cut 5 cm above crown
- **Watering:** Drought-tolerant once established
- **Lifespan:** Productive for 20+ years
- **Containment:** Bocking 14 is sterile but root fragments spread; use root barriers if needed

## Ecological Value

Long bloom period attracts bees and bumblebees. Dense foliage shelters beneficial insects. Deep roots stabilize slopes and prevent erosion. Root exudates support soil microbial communities.
