## Overview

*Cladrastis lutea*, the American yellowwood, is a medium-sized deciduous tree in the family Fabaceae. It is native to the southeastern and central United States, occurring in scattered populations from North Carolina to Oklahoma.

The species is prized for its showy, fragrant white flower panicles, which cascade from the branches in late spring like wisteria blossoms. Its bright yellow heartwood, from which it takes its common name, was historically used for dye and specialized woodwork.

American yellowwood typically reaches 10–18 meters in height with a broad, rounded crown. In cultivation, it develops a spreading form with smooth grey bark reminiscent of American beech.
