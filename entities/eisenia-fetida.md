## Description

*Eisenia fetida*, commonly known as the red wiggler or red composting worm, is a species of earthworm widely used in vermicomposting systems worldwide. In mushroom cultivation contexts, this species plays a critical role in mycovermicomposting — the practice of introducing composting worms into spent mushroom substrate to convert it into nutrient-rich worm castings. Red wigglers are extremely fond of mushroom mycelium, making them ideally suited for processing spent growing media from mushroom operations.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Animalia
- **Phylum**: Annelida
- **Class**: Clitellata
- **Order**: Opisthopora
- **Family**: Lumbricidae
- **Genus**: *Eisenia*
- **Species**: *E. fetida*
- **Common names**: Red wiggler, red composting worm, tiger worm, brandling worm

## Key Facts

- Vertical migrators — they penetrate every cubic inch of substrate, making them far more effective than common earthworms for composting operations
- Earthworms (*Lumbricus* spp.) compost horizontally at the thin layer where fresh medium meets old, making them less effective in contained systems
- Can be purchased commercially from online suppliers or local worm farmers
- Can also be collected and raised from native populations, though they may be less efficient
- Require fine sand or grit in their substrate to grind food in their gizzard, similar to birds
- Benefit from the addition of native soil, which supplies beneficial microbes needed by their gut bacteria
- Attracted to spent mushroom substrate by sweet-smelling metabolites produced by the mycelium
- Worm castings are among the most effective natural fertilizers available, loaded with beneficial microbes

## Habitat and Behavior

- Thrive in decomposing organic matter, particularly mushroom mycelium-colonized substrates
- Prefer temperatures between 55–77°F (13–25°C)
- Require adequate moisture but not waterlogged conditions
- Respond negatively to bright light, which can be used to prevent escape from open bins
- Self-migrate upward toward fresh food sources in stacked systems

## Uses in Cultivation

- Primary biological agent in mycovermicomposting systems
- Can be introduced directly into holes of spent oyster mushroom columns
- Process spent substrate in situ, eliminating need to remove and separately compost material
- Produce castings that support symbiotic biofilms on developing plant root systems
- Can be harvested and sold for bait shops, chicken feed, and other poultry operations
- Their castings can be used to grow vegetables in subsequent planting cycles

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

*Eisenia fetida* is the cornerstone organism for closing the loop in mushroom cultivation. By converting spent mycelium-colonized substrate into premium worm castings, red wigglers transform what would be waste into high-value soil amendments and additional revenue streams. Their natural affinity for mushroom mycelium makes them uniquely suited to mushroom farm operations. Cotter notes that spent mushroom substrate is essentially a superfood for worms, making the integration nearly effortless.
