## Description

J. Sowerby was an English mycologist and botanical illustrator, author of *Coloured Figures of English Fungi or Mushrooms* (London, 1803). His work is of exceptional importance in the history of psychoactive mushroom taxonomy, as it provided the first visual rendition and description of the mushroom species responsible for the 1799 poisoning case documented by [[everard-brande]].

## Contributions to Mycology

### Identification of Psilocybe semilanceata

Sowerby's book included a detailed drawing and description of the mushroom that poisoned the Brande family, identifying it as **"[[agaricus]] glutinosus Curtis"**. His description is fully compatible with current knowledge about *[[psilocybe-semilanceata]]*:

- Stalks generally single, sometimes clustered, from two to four inches in height
- Thickness of a goose quill, thread-shaped, whitish, almost solid
- Cap from one to two inches broad, brown color, hemispherical, always convex, glutinous
- Gills numerous, single, brownish purple color, producing brownish purple [[spore]] powder
- No acrimonious or disagreeable taste

### Taxonomic History

Sowerby's identification was part of the complex taxonomic history of *P. semilanceata*:

1. **1818**: E. Fries referred to it as "Agaricus semilanceatus" in *Observationes Mycologicae*
2. **Later synonyms**: *Coprinarius semilanceatus* Fr., *Panaeolus semilanceatus* (Fr.) Lge.
3. **1870**: Kummer and Quelet classified it as a member of the genus *Psilocybe*
4. Valid designations: *Psilocybe semilanceata* (Fr.) Kumm. or *Psilocybe semilanceata* (Fr.) Quel.
