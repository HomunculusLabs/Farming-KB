# G13

## Overview

**G13** (also written **G-13** or **Government Indica Strain 13**) is a legendary indica-dominant cannabis strain purported to originate from a U.S. government [[hofmann-lsd-sandoz-research-program]] at the University of Mississippi during the late 1960s or 1970s.

The strain occupies a unique place in cannabis lore as one of the most storied and debated cultivars, with enthusiasts and breeders divided between those who regard it as a genuine government-bred cultivar and those who consider its origin story apocryphal.

Regardless of the truth of its provenance, G13 has become a foundational breeding parent that has contributed genetics to numerous award-winning hybrids.

## Lineage

According to the most widely circulated account, G13 was one of several numbered indica strains grown at the University of Mississippi under a DEA-funded research program authorized by NIDA.

The university's cannabis research facility, operated by Dr. Mahmoud ElSohly from 1980 onward, maintained a living collection of cannabis germplasm for pharmacological study.

The "13" in the name reportedly indicates its accession number within this collection, though no official documentation confirming this has ever been made public.

G13 is described as a pure indica or near-pure indica, likely derived from Afghan landrace genetics imported to the United States for research purposes.

An individual clone reportedly left the facility during the 1980s and entered the underground cannabis community, where it was propagated vegetatively and eventually used in breeding programs.

## Appearance

G13 buds are typically dense and compact, characteristic of indica morphology, with tightly coiled calyxes and moderate trichome coverage.

Coloration ranges from deep forest green to pale lime, occasionally displaying purple hues when exposed to cooler nighttime temperatures during late flowering.

The pistils are abundant and orange to rust-colored, contrasting sharply against the green calyx tissue.

[[cannabis-glandular-trichome-types-resin-production-clarke]] is heavy, with a thick coating of cloudy to amber trichomes that gives the buds a frosted appearance.

## Aroma and Flavor

The aroma profile is predominantly earthy and pungent, with deep notes of damp soil, pine, and a subtle skunky undertone.

Secondary notes include a faint citrus quality and a woody, hash-like character that reflects its Afghan heritage.

Flavor on inhalation is smooth and earthy with a slight sweetness, while the exhaled smoke carries more pronounced pine and hash flavors.

The overall flavor is less complex than many modern hybrids, reflecting its landrace-dominant genetics and lack of deliberate terpene-focused breeding.

## Terpene Profile

The dominant terpenes in G13 are **myrcene**, **caryophyllene**, and **pinene**, consistent with its Afghan indica lineage.

Myrcene contributes the sedative, musky, and earthy qualities, while caryophyllene adds peppery warmth and pinene provides pine notes.

Trace terpenes may include limonene and humulene, though at lower concentrations than in sativa-dominant hybrids.

The overall terpene profile is relatively simple compared to modern poly-hybrids, which is consistent with its older genetic background.

## Cannabinoid Profile

THC content typically ranges from 18–24% in well-grown specimens, placing it above average for classic indica strains.

CBD content is generally low, usually below 1%, as G13 predates the modern CBD-focused breeding movement.

Minor cannabinoids including CBG and CBC may be present in small quantities, contributing to the [[cannabis-terpene-entourage-effect-profiles]].

## Effects

Onset is relatively rapid, beginning within 5–10 minutes of inhalation, with effects progressing through several distinct phases.

The initial phase is cerebral and euphoric, producing a heady, uplifting sensation that gradually gives way to deep body relaxation.

The body stone is the defining characteristic — a heavy, enveloping physical sedation that can lock users to the couch at higher doses.

Duration of effects is typically 2–3 hours, with the sedative phase lasting longest.
