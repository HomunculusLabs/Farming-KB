## Overview

**Kadsura japonica**, commonly known as kadsura vine or Japanese kadsura, is an evergreen climbing vine native to Japan, Korea, and eastern China. It belongs to the family Schisandraceae and is closely related to the economically important genus Schisandra.

The species is valued in traditional East Asian medicine, where various plant parts have been used for centuries to treat respiratory ailments, digestive disorders, and [[query-how-do-i-prune-brambles-for-maximum-fruit-production]], which is an important consideration for garden cultivation.

The genus name Kadsura is derived from the Japanese common name "katsura" for the related Schisandra species. The specific epithet japonica refers to the species' native range in Japan.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Kadsura japonica** (L.) Dunal was first described by Linnaeus as Uvaria japonica in 1759 and later transferred to the genus Kadsura by Augustin Pyramus de Candolle. The genus Kadsura contains approximately 20 species distributed in tropical and subtropical East and Southeast Asia.

The species is an evergreen woody vine that climbs by twining stems. Mature vines can reach 3 to 6 meters in length under favorable conditions, attaching to supports by tightly coiling their stems around structures.

Leaves are simple, alternate, leathery, and obovate to elliptic, 5 to 12 centimeters long and 3 to 7 centimeters wide. They are dark glossy green above and slightly paler below, with entire margins and a short petiole. The foliage is aromatic when crushed.

Flowers are solitary or in small clusters, borne in the leaf axils from April to June. Male flowers have 12 to 20 creamy-white to pale yellow tepals arranged in several whorls, 5 to 10 millimeters long. Female flowers are similar but have a compact gynoecium at the center.

The flowers are mildly fragrant with a sweet, somewhat citrus-like scent. Male flowers are slightly more showy than female flowers, though neither is particularly ornamental in comparison to the fruit.

Flowering period lasts 3 to 4 weeks in mid-spring. Male flowers produce abundant pollen but no nectar, while female flowers produce small amounts of nectar that attract flies and small bees.

Fruit is an aggregate berry, 2 to 4 centimeters in diameter, composed of numerous fleshy carpels fused into a spherical mass. The fruit ripens from green to bright red or orange-red in September to October.

Individual berries within the aggregate contain one or two small seeds. The ripe fruit is visually striking and persists on the vine for several weeks, providing autumn and early-winter interest in the garden.

The fruit is edible but not particularly palatable, with a mildly sweet but insipid flavor. In some rural Japanese communities, the berries have been used to make preserves or sweetened beverages.

## Distribution and Habitat

Kadsura vine is native to warm-temperate and subtropical regions of East Asia. Its range extends through southern and central Japan (Honshu, Shikoku, Kyushu, and the Ryukyu Islands), the Korean peninsula, and eastern China.

The species grows as an understory vine in warm-temperate evergreen and mixed broadleaf forests. It is most commonly found in moist, shaded ravines, forest margins, and streamside thickets at elevations from 50 to 1,500 meters.
