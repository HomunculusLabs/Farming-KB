# Amanita velosa

## Overview

**Amanita velosa**, known as the springtime amanita, veiled
amanita, or California apricot amanita, is an [[bloomfield-boletus-satanus-edible-mushroom-hazards]]
species native to the western [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]], primarily California.
It fruits in spring, typically from March through May, coinciding
with the end of the rainy season.

*Amanita velosa* is considered one of the choice edible amanitas
of western [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]]. Despite belonging to the genus *Amanita*
— which includes deadly species like *A. phalloides* and
*A. virosa* — *A. velosa* is distinctive in appearance and
reliably identifiable when key features are observed.

The species was first described by mycologist William Alphonso
Murrill in 1912 from collections made in California.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:**
- Kingdom: Fungi
- Division: Basidiomycota
- Class: Agaricomycetes
- Order: Agaricales
- Family: Amanitaceae
- Genus: *Amanita*
- Section: *Vaginatae*
- Species: *A. velosa*
- Authority: (Peck) Lloyd (1898)

**Synonyms:** *Amanita calyptrata* Peck, *Vaginata calyptrata*
(Peck) Lloyd

**Cap:** 5–15 cm diameter, ovate to convex when young, expanding
to plano-convex with age. Surface smooth, dry, with a distinctive
pale apricot to buff coloration. Universal veil often leaves a
thin patch on the disc. Margin is smooth, non-striate.

**Gills:** Free, close to crowded, white to creamy white, with
frequent short gills. Edges are floccose when young.

**Stipe:** 8–20 cm × 1–3 cm, cylindrical, white to pale buff,
solid. Surface smooth above the annulus, fibrillose below. Volva
is saccate, thin, membranous, white to buff, typically buried
with only the upper rim visible at ground level.

**Annulus:** Thin, membranous skirt on the upper stipe, white
to cream, often collapsing with age.

**Flesh:** White, firm, with a mild odor sometimes described as
slightly farinaceous.

**Spores:** 8–11 × 6–8 μm, broadly elliptical, smooth,
non-amyloid. [[comparison-spore-print-collection-vs-spore-syringe-production]]: white.

**Chemical reactions:** KOH on cap surface: negative (no color
change). Spores: inamyloid.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Amanita velosa* is found primarily in California, with
additional records from Oregon and Baja California, Mexico.

It is an ectomycorrhizal species, forming associations with
oak trees, particularly *[[quercus-agrifolia]]* (coast live oak)
