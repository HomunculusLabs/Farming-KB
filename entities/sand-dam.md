## Sand Dam

## Description

Sand dams are water harvesting structures built across seasonal watercourses (wadis, washes) in arid foothills and semi-arid regions. They function by allowing sediment (sand, gravel, and rubble) to accumulate behind the dam wall, creating a massive underground reservoir that stores water within the pore spaces of the deposited sediment. Mollison describes them extensively in *[[mushroom-fruiting-chambers]] or outdoor mushroom beds.
- **Filtered water supply**: The natural filtration through sand and rubble produces clean water — ideal for mycological applications requiring non-chlorinated, sediment-free water.
- **Microclimate creation**: Sand dams create localized oases with higher humidity and lower temperature — beneficial for fungal fruiting in arid zones.
- **Organic matter accumulation**: Silt and organic debris trapped behind sand dams creates rich substrate for [[tagari-publications]]. Chapter 11, pp. 353–356.
- Lasage, R., & Verburg, P. H. (2015). "Evaluation of small-scale water harvesting techniques for semi-arid environments." *Journal of Arid Environments*.
