# Arctium lappa

## Overview

Greater burdock (*Arctium lappa*) is a tall biennial herb in the
Asteraceae family, native to Europe and Asia and naturalized across
North America. Renowned for its deep taproot — which can extend over
a meter into the soil — burdock is valued simultaneously as a food
crop, traditional medicine, and permaculture workhorse. The root is a
staple in East Asian cuisine (known as *gobo* in Japanese) and has
been used for centuries in European and Chinese herbal traditions for
its blood-purifying, hepatoprotective, and dermatological properties.

## Taxonomy and Botany

- **Family:** Asteraceae (daisy/aster family)
- **Genus:** *Arctium* (burdocks)
- **Order:** Asterales
- **Growth habit:** Biennial; large basal rosette in year one,
  bolted flowering stalk in year two reaching 1.5–3 m.
- **Leaves:** Large, cordate (heart-shaped), up to 50 cm long,
  with woolly undersides; alternate arrangement on flowering stem.
- **Flowers:** Purple, thistle-like composite heads, 2–3 cm diameter,
  blooming mid-summer to early autumn; grouped in corymbose clusters.
- **Fruit:** Burrs (burs) with hooked bracts that cling to animal
  fur and clothing — the plant's primary seed dispersal mechanism.

## Chemical Constituents

- **Inulin:** Fructan polysaccharide composing up to 45% of dry root
  weight; prebiotic fiber supporting gut microbiome health.
- **Arctiin and arctigenin:** Lignans with documented anti-inflammatory,
  antioxidant, and potential anti-cancer activities.
- **Polyacetylenes:** Compounds including falcarinol and falcarindiol
  with antibacterial and antifungal properties.
- **Sesquiterpene lactones:** Contributing bitter principles that
  stimulate digestion and bile production.
- **Flavonoids:** Quercetin, luteolin, and related glycosides.
- **Tannins and mucilage:** Contributing astringent and demulcent
  qualities respectively.

## Traditional and Modern Uses

- **Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM):** Root (*niú bàng zǐ* and
  the seed *niú bàng zǐ*) is used to clear heat, relieve toxicity,
  and disperse wind-heat; indicated for sore throat, fever, and
  skin eruptions including acne and eczema.
- **European herbalism:** Classified as an alterative or "blood
  purifier"; traditionally used in decoctions for skin conditions,
  joint pain, and urinary complaints.
- **Japanese cuisine:** Root (*gobo*) is peeled, julienned, and
  braised or stir-fried; rich source of dietary fiber and minerals.
- **Korean cuisine:** Root (*ueong*) is used in soups and banchan
  side dishes; valued for its earthy, slightly sweet flavor.
- **Beverage:** Burdock root tea is a popular caffeine-free tonic;
  combined with dandelion root in traditional "detox" blends.

## Permaculture and Ecological Role

- **Dynamic accumulator:** Deep taproot mines subsoil minerals
  (calcium, magnesium, potassium, iron) and brings them to the
  surface, making them available to neighboring plants via leaf
  litter and root decomposition.
- **Comfrey alternative:** Where comfrey (*Symphytum officinale*)
  is invasive or unsuitable, burdock serves a similar mineral-
  accumulating function in chop-and-drop mulch systems.
- **Biodiversity support:** Flowers attract bees, butterflies, and
  beneficial insects; seeds feed birds including goldfinches.
- **Soil structure:** Deep taproot breaks compacted subsoil layers,
  improving drainage and aeration for subsequent crops.
- **Pest management:** Burdock leaves can be used as a sacrificial
  trap crop for aphids, drawing them away from valuable vegetable
  crops.

## Cultivation

- **Hardiness:** USDA zones 3–9; tolerates a wide range of
  temperatures and soil conditions.
- **Soil:** Prefers deep, well-drained loam but tolerates clay and
  sandy soils; pH 6.0–7.5.
- **Propagation:** Direct seed in spring or autumn; stratification
  improves germination. Seeds remain viable for several years.
- **Spacing:** 45–60 cm apart; plants are large and need room.
- **Harvest:** Roots are dug in autumn of the first year or spring
  of the second year before flowering depletes root reserves.
- **Invasive potential:** Burs spread readily on clothing and animal
  fur; deadhead flowers before seed set to prevent unwanted spread.

## Wiki Relevance

Arctium lappa bridges medicinal herbalism, permaculture design, and
food forestry. Its role as a dynamic accumulator and soil builder makes
it a key species in regenerative agriculture discussions, while its
extensive phytochemistry provides rich content for ethnobotanical and
pharmacological cross-referencing with other Asteraceae members.
