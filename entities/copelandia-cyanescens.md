## Description

### Macroscopic Features

- **Cap:** 15–40 mm diameter, conic-campanulate to convex, expanding to broadly convex with age. Surface smooth, hygrophanous — dark chestnut-brown to olive-brown when moist, fading to pale grey or whitish when dry. Margin often translucent-striate when moist. Viscid when wet.
- **Gills:** Adnate to adnexed, close to subdistant, with 2–3 tiers of lamellulae. Greyish at first, becoming mottled black as spores mature. Edges white and fimbriate (fringed).
- **Stem:** 40–100 mm × 2–4 mm, cylindrical, equal, hollow, smooth to slightly fibrillose. White to pale grey, bruising intensely blue to blue-black upon handling. No annulus present.
- **Flesh:** Thin, white, bruising vividly blue throughout. Odor farinaceous (mealy).
- **[[spore-print]]:** Dark purplish-black to nearly black.

### Microscopic Features

| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| Spore shape | Ellipsoid to lemon-shaped (subamygdaliform) |
| Spore size | 11–17 × 7–11 µm |
| Spore wall | Thick, smooth, with distinct germ pore |
| Spore color | Dark brown to purplish-brown in KOH |
| Basidia | 2- and 4-spored; clavate, 20–30 µm |
| Cheilocystidia | Abundant, flask-shaped to ventricose, 15–35 µm |
| Pleurocystidia | Present (diagnostic for *Copelandia*), similar to cheilocystidia |
| Pileipellis | Cutis with embedded elements; hyaline, thin-walled |

The presence of pleurocystidia is the primary microscopic character distinguishing *Copelandia* from *Panaeolus* sensu stricto.

## Distribution and Habitat

### Geographic Range

*Copelandia cyanescens* has a pantropical and subtropical distribution:

| Region | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Hawaiian Islands** | Very common | Naturalized, likely introduced with cattle |
| **Southeast Asia** | Common | Thailand, Cambodia, Vietnam, Philippines |
| **Central America** | Common | Mexico, Costa Rica, Panama |
| **[[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-south-america]]** | Common | Colombia, Brazil, Ecuador, Venezuela |
| **Caribbean** | Common | Jamaica, Puerto Rico, Bahamas |
| **Australia** | Scattered | Northern territories, coastal Queensland |
| **Southern USA** | Rare/seasonal | Gulf Coast states, Florida |

### Habitat and Ecology

- **Substrate:** Dung of large herbivores — primarily cattle, water buffalo, horses
- **Habitat:** Tropical and subtropical pastures, meadows, grasslands; also enriched soils and compost
- **Season:** Warm, humid periods year-round in tropics; late spring through autumn in subtropics
- **Growth pattern:** Gregarious to cespitose; fruits prolifically after rainfall
- **Temperature preference:** Optimal fruiting at 24–30°C with >85% RH
