# Copelandia cyanescens

## Overview

*Copelandia cyanescens*, commonly known as the **blue meanies** mushroom, is a potent psilocybin-containing fungus belonging to the family Panaeolaceae. Originally described as *Panaeolus cyanescens* by Berkeley and Broome in 1871, it was reclassified into the genus *Copelandia* by Singer in 1951 based on its distinct microscopic features — notably the presence of pleurocystidia (cheilocystidia-like cells on the gill faces), a trait absent in typical *Panaeolus* species. Found primarily in tropical and subtropical regions worldwide, *C. cyanescens* is a coprophilous (dung-inhabiting) species that fruits on the dung of large herbivores. It is among the most potent psilocybin-containing mushrooms known, with psilocybin and psilocin concentrations rivaling or exceeding those of *Psilocybe azurescens*. The species is culturally significant in Hawaiian, Southeast Asian, and Central American contexts.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi → **Division:** Basidiomycota → **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales → **Family:** Panaeolaceae → **Genus:** *Copelandia*
- **Species:** *C. cyanescens* (Berk. & Broome) Singer
- **Synonyms:** *Panaeolus cyanescens* Berk. & Broome (1871), *Coprinus cyanescens* (Berk.) Sacc.
- **Etymology:** "Cyanescens" refers to the intense blue bruising; type locality: Sri Lanka

## Description

### Macroscopic Features

- **Cap:** 15–40 mm diameter, conic-campanulate to convex, expanding to broadly convex with age. Surface smooth, hygrophanous — dark chestnut-brown to olive-brown when moist, fading to pale grey or whitish when dry. Margin often translucent-striate when moist. Viscid when wet.
- **Gills:** Adnate to adnexed, close to subdistant, with 2–3 tiers of lamellulae. Greyish at first, becoming mottled black as spores mature. Edges white and fimbriate (fringed).
- **Stem:** 40–100 mm × 2–4 mm, cylindrical, equal, hollow, smooth to slightly fibrillose. White to pale grey, bruising intensely blue to blue-black upon handling. No annulus present.
- **Flesh:** Thin, white, bruising vividly blue throughout. Odor farinaceous (mealy).
- **Spore print:** Dark purplish-black to nearly black.

### Microscopic Features

| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| Spore shape | Ellipsoid to lemon-shaped (subamygdaliform) |
| Spore size | 11–17 × 7–11 µm |
| Spore wall | Thick, smooth, with distinct germ pore |
| Spore color | Dark brown to purplish-brown in KOH |
| Basidia | 2- and 4-spored; clavate, 20–30 µm |
| Cheilocystidia | Abundant, flask-shaped to ventricose, 15–35 µm |
| Pleurocystidia | Present (diagnostic for *Copelandia*), similar to cheilocystidia |
| Pileipellis | Cutis with embedded elements; hyaline, thin-walled |

The presence of pleurocystidia is the primary microscopic character distinguishing *Copelandia* from *Panaeolus* sensu stricto.

## Distribution and Habitat

### Geographic Range

*Copelandia cyanescens* has a pantropical and subtropical distribution:

| Region | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Hawaiian Islands** | Very common | Naturalized, likely introduced with cattle |
| **Southeast Asia** | Common | Thailand, Cambodia, Vietnam, Philippines |
| **Central America** | Common | Mexico, Costa Rica, Panama |
| **South America** | Common | Colombia, Brazil, Ecuador, Venezuela |
| **Caribbean** | Common | Jamaica, Puerto Rico, Bahamas |
| **Australia** | Scattered | Northern territories, coastal Queensland |
| **Southern USA** | Rare/seasonal | Gulf Coast states, Florida |

### Habitat and Ecology

- **Substrate:** Dung of large herbivores — primarily cattle, water buffalo, horses
- **Habitat:** Tropical and subtropical pastures, meadows, grasslands; also enriched soils and compost
- **Season:** Warm, humid periods year-round in tropics; late spring through autumn in subtropics
- **Growth pattern:** Gregarious to cespitose; fruits prolifically after rainfall
- **Temperature preference:** Optimal fruiting at 24–30°C with >85% RH

## Chemistry

### Active Compounds

| Compound | Concentration (% dry weight) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Psilocybin** | 0.30–1.20% | Among highest of any psilocybin mushroom |
| **Psilocin** | 0.10–0.60% | Free psilocin, often elevated |
| **Baeocystin** | 0.02–0.15% | Minor alkaloid |

Ranked among the 3–5 most potent psilocybin mushrooms by dry weight; Hawaiian specimens often cited as particularly strong.

## Psychoactive Effects

- **Onset:** 15–45 minutes (oral); notably rapid compared to *Psilocybe cubensis*
- **Duration:** 4–6 hours; peak at 1.5–3 hours
- **Visual:** Intense geometric patterns, color enhancement, tracers, synesthesia at moderate-to-high doses
- **Cognitive:** Deep introspection, philosophical insight, ego dissolution at higher doses
- **Emotional:** Euphoria, emotional catharsis, anxiety possible during onset
- **Body:** Strong body load, pupil dilation, temperature dysregulation, nausea (less common than with *Amanita*)

### Dosage Guidelines (dried grams)

| Level | Dose | Effects |
|---|---|---|
| Light | 0.25–0.75 | Enhanced colors, mild visual distortion |
| Moderate | 0.75–1.50 | Clear visuals, introspection, altered thought patterns |
| Heroic | 3.00+ | Complete mystical experience |

Due to elevated potency, doses are significantly lower than for *Psilocybe cubensis*.

## Cultivation

- **Substrate:** Pasteurized horse or cow dung; can also fruit on enriched straw or compost
- **Colonization temperature:** 27–32°C; slower than *P. cubensis* on standard grain
- **Fruiting temperature:** 24–30°C with high humidity (90–95% RH)
- **Fresh air exchange:** Critical — requires high FAE for proper development
- **Biological efficiency:** Moderate (50–80%); yields lower than *P. cubensis* per volume
- **Spawn:** Rye grain, millet, or brown rice; colonization typically 14–21 days
Widely cultivated in subtropical regions and naturalized in areas with suitable climate and dung substrate.

## Legal Status

- **United States:** Schedule I (controlled substance); psilocybin/psilocin content triggers federal scheduling
- **United Kingdom:** Class A under Misuse of Drugs Act 1971
- **Netherlands:** Illegal since 2008 (smart shop ban included *Panaeolus*)
- **Brazil:** Legal ambiguity; not explicitly scheduled
- **Thailand:** Illegal; enforcement varies
- **International:** Not under UN Convention, but most nations regulate under analogue scheduling
