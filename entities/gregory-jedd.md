## Description

Gregory Jedd is a fungal cell biologist based at the Temasek Life Sciences Laboratory, National University of Singapore. He is a leading researcher on the cell biology of fungal septal structures, particularly Woronin bodies and related septal-pore-associated organelles. His work has significantly advanced understanding of how multicellular organization is maintained in filamentous fungi through specialized organelles that regulate septal pore function.

## Classification

- **Category**: Person / Mycologist / Cell biologist
- **Affiliation**: Temasek Life Sciences Laboratory, National University of Singapore
- **Research area**: Fungal cell biology, septal pore organelles, Woronin bodies, multicellular organization in fungi

## Key Facts

- Jedd authored the chapter "Natural history of the fungal hypha: how Woronin bodies support a multicellular lifestyle" in Fungi in the Environment (Gadd, Watkinson and Dyer, 2006, Cambridge University Press).
- His research established that Woronin bodies are peroxisome-derived organelles, confirming earlier electron microscopy observations that linked Woronin bodies to microbodies (peroxisomes).
- Jedd has investigated the hex-1 gene, which encodes the major core protein of the Woronin body and is diagnostic of the Euascomycete lineage.
- He has contributed to understanding the phylogenetic distribution of septal-pore-associated organelles across the fungal kingdom, mapping the presence of Woronin bodies, septal pore caps, and Woronin-body-like organelles onto fungal phylogenies.
- His work draws connections between the evolution of complex septal pore organelles (in Hymenomycetes and Euascomycetes) and the production of large, complex multicellular fruiting bodies.

## Research Contributions

Jedd research programme addresses several fundamental questions in fungal biology:

1. **Woronin body biogenesis**: How peroxisomal pathways are co-opted to generate these specialized organelles
2. **Septal pore function**: How septal pores balance intercellular continuity with the need for rapid cellular isolation
3. **Evolution of multicellularity**: How organelle evolution in fungi supports increasingly complex multicellular organization
4. **Phylogenetic mapping**: Using septal pore ultrastructure as a phylogenetic character to trace evolutionary relationships

## Historical Context

Jedd work builds on a tradition stretching back to the original discovery of Woronin bodies by Mikhail Stepanovich Woronin in 1866 in [[ascobolus-pulcherrimus]], and the naming of these structures by A.H.R. Buller in 1933. He also cites the early cell biology work of Charlotte Ternetz (1900), who first defined basic features of Woronin body biology.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Jedd research on septal pore biology has direct implications for understanding fungal physiology in cultivation contexts:

- **Damage response**: Woronin bodies enable rapid septal pore closure, protecting colonies from catastrophic cytoplasmic loss when hyphae are damaged during substrate disturbance.
- **Intercellular transport**: Understanding septal pore regulation informs how nutrients, signals, and organelles move through the mycelial network.
- **Strain selection**: Differences in septal pore organelles between fungal groups may influence colony resilience and transport efficiency in cultivated species.
