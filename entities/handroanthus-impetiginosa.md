The species is valued both as a premium timber tree and as a traditional medicinal plant with a long history of indigenous use.

It was reclassified from the genus Tabebuia to Handroanthus in 2007 based on molecular phylogenetic evidence.

Its showy pink to magenta flowers make it a prominent ornamental tree in tropical urban landscapes.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Handroanthus impetiginosa** (Mart. ex DC.) Mattos was formerly classified as [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-central-america-caribbean]] to Bolivia, Paraguay, and northern Argentina.

It occurs in a wide variety of habitats including dry deciduous forests, cerrado, caatinga, and tropical seasonal forests.

Elevation range extends from sea level to approximately 1500 meters in Andean foothill valleys.

It is most abundant in well-drained soils on hillsides and ridges, where it establishes as a canopy or emergent tree.

The species is drought-deciduous, shedding its leaves during the dry season and flowering before new growth appears.

It tolerates a range of soil types including rocky, sandy, and clay soils but performs best in well-drained, fertile substrates.

## Cultivation and Timber

Pau d'arco wood is one of the most commercially valuable timbers in South America, traded internationally under various names.

Heartwood is extremely durable, resistant to decay and insect attack, with a density of 0.85–1.05 g/cm³ at 12% moisture content.

The wood color ranges from olive-brown to dark reddish-brown, often with attractive darker streaking.

It is used for exterior construction, decking, railroad ties, tool handles, and high-quality furniture.

Timber from natural forests is increasingly supplemented by plantation-grown stock, though [[coleman-sunlight-and-winter-crop-growth-rates]] in plantations are moderate.

The tree is also planted as an ornamental in tropical and subtropical parks, streetscapes, and botanical gardens.

Propagation is primarily by seed, which germinates within 3–6 weeks under warm, moist conditions.

Seedlings require full sun and moderate watering; established trees are highly drought-tolerant.

## Bioactive Compounds and Pharmacology

The inner bark is the primary plant part used medicinally, containing a complex mixture of bioactive compounds.

The principal constituents are naphthoquinones, including lapachol, β-lapachone, and α-lapachone.
