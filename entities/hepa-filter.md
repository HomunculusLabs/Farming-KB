## Description

HEPA (High Efficiency Particulate Air) filters are mechanical air filters that remove at least 99.97% of airborne particles measuring 0.3 micrometers in diameter. They are a critical component in laminar flow hoods used for sterile work in mycology, tissue culture, and laboratory settings.

## Classification

- **Category**: Air filtration device
- **Filter class**: H14 according to EN 1822 standard
- **Application**: Laboratory, cleanroom, and [[mushroom-cultivation-sterile-technique]] work

## Key Facts

- Removes 99.9% of airborne materials including fungi, bacteria, and dust
- Constructed from a mat of randomly arranged fiberglass fibers
- Operates through three mechanisms: interception, impaction, and diffusion
- Typical dimensions for DIY flow hoods: 30.5 cm width x 61 cm height x 7.8 cm depth
- Requires a pressure differential of approximately 150 Pa for rated airflow
- Must be paired with a correctly sized blower to achieve 0.5 m/s air velocity in the working area

## Technical Specifications

- **Efficiency**: 99.97% at 0.3 um particle size
- **Standard**: EN 1822 H14 class
- **Pressure drop**: ~150 Pa at 60% nominal airflow
- **Lifespan**: Varies with pre-filtration quality and environment

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

HEPA filters are essential for maintaining sterile conditions during mycological work. [[contamination-prevention-in-mushroom-cultivation]], they are the core component of laminar flow hoods used for:

- **Agar work**: Transferring mycelium cultures between plates
- **[[mushroom-spawn-production-grain-inoculation]]**: Transferring spawn to sterilized grain jars
- **Tissue culture**: Cloning mushroom tissue on agar media
- **Spore work**: Germinating spores and isolating strains

A properly functioning HEPA filter eliminates airborne contamination that would otherwise compete with or destroy cultivated fungi. It provides a continuous curtain of sterile air that protects open containers during [[cultivator-sterile-technique-and-agar-culture]] operations.
