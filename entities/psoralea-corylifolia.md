## Overview

*Psoralea corylifolia* is an annual herbaceous plant in the family Fabaceae, commonly known as babchi or Bu Gu Zhi (补骨脂). It is one of the most frequently prescribed herbs in traditional Chinese medicine for treating skin disorders, bone diseases, and kidney yang deficiency. The seeds are the primary medicinal part and contain a diverse array of [[dighton-fungal-drought-tolerance-plant-water-relations]] and ability to colonize nutrient-poor soils. [[antifungal-secondary-metabolites-coprophilous-fungi]]. The major compound classes are furanocoumarins (psoralen, isopsoralen, bergapten), flavonoids (bavachin, bavachalcone, isobavachalcone), meroterpenes (bakuchiol), and coumestans (psoralidin). These compounds collectively exhibit a wide range of pharmacological activities including antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and estrogenic effects.

Psoralen and isopsoralen are the primary compounds responsible for the species" traditional use in treating vitiligo and other pigmentary disorders. These furanocoumarins act as photosensitizing agents, stimulating melanocyte proliferation and melanin synthesis when activated by ultraviolet A radiation. This mechanism forms the basis of PUVA (psoralen plus UVA) therapy, a standard dermatological treatment for vitiligo and psoriasis that was derived directly from traditional *P. corylifolia* preparations. Modern PUVA therapy typically uses methoxsalen (8-methoxypsoralen) derived from other plant sources, but the therapeutic principle originated from observations of *P. corylifolia* use in traditional Indian medicine. The herb's efficacy in treating vitiligo has been validated in multiple clinical trials, with response rates of 50–70% in patients with active disease.

Beyond dermatological applications, preclinical studies have demonstrated that *P. corylifolia* extracts possess significant bone-protective activity. Psoralen and its derivatives stimulate osteoblast proliferation and differentiation while inhibiting osteoclast-mediated bone resorption. These dual actions support the traditional use of the herb for treating bone fractures, osteoporosis, and joint disorders. Animal models of postmenopausal osteoporosis have shown that *P. corylifolia* extracts can prevent bone mineral density loss comparable to standard bisphosphonate therapy.

Bakuchiol has emerged as the most commercially significant compound from the species. Clinical studies demonstrate that topical bakuchiol improves wrinkle depth, skin elasticity, and hyperpigmentation with efficacy comparable to retinol but significantly lower irritation potential. Unlike retinol, bakuchiol does not cause photosensitivity, making it suitable for daytime formulations. These properties have made bakuchiol a popular ingredient in premium anti-aging skincare products marketed as "clean" or "natural" alternatives to retinoids.

## Toxicity and Safety Concerns

Despite its therapeutic benefits, *P. corylifolia* poses significant safety concerns that limit its clinical use. Hepatotoxicity is the most serious adverse effect, with numerous case reports of liver injury associated with oral consumption of the seeds or extracts. The hepatotoxic constituents include both furanocoumarins and certain flavonoid glycosides, and the risk appears to be dose-dependent and influenced by individual susceptibility factors.

The mechanism of hepatotoxicity involves mitochondrial dysfunction, oxidative stress, and inhibition of bile acid transporters in hepatocytes. Histopathological examination of affected livers shows centrilobular necrosis and inflammatory infiltrates consistent with drug-induced liver injury. Risk factors include pre-existing liver conditions, concurrent use of other hepatotoxic medications, and prolonged high-dose consumption. Liver function monitoring is recommended for patients using *P. corylifolia* preparations for extended periods.

Photosensitivity reactions represent another important safety consideration. Oral or topical administration of psoralen-containing preparations followed by sun exposure can cause severe photodermatitis, characterized by erythema, edema, and blistering. Patients using *P. corylifolia* preparations must be carefully counseled about sun avoidance and ultraviolet protection. Drug interactions with cytochrome P450 substrates have also been reported, as furanocoumarins are potent inhibitors of CYP3A4 and CYP2C9.

Regulatory agencies in several countries have issued warnings about *P. corylifolia*-containing supplements. Health Canada has classified the herb as one requiring cautionary labeling for liver injury risk. The European Medicines Agency has highlighted the need for proper standardization and [[agar-culture-techniques-detailed]].
