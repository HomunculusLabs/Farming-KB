# Melliodora

## Description

Melliodora is the [[query-how-do-i-start-designing-a-permaculture-property-from-scratch]] of [[david-holmgren]] and Su Dennett, located
in [[holmgren-melliodora-permaculture-demonstration-site-hepburn-springs]], central Victoria, Australia. Named after *[[eucalyptus-melliodora]]*
(Yellow Box), the property is one of the most documented and influential permaculture
demonstration sites in the world. Established in the mid-1980s on approximately one
hectare (2.5 acres) of land, Melliodora integrates residential living with intensive
food production, animal husbandry, forestry, and [[water-management]] in a temperate
Australian climate.

The property serves as a living laboratory for [[permaculture-design-principles]],
demonstrating how a small suburban-rural property can provide a significant
proportion of a household's food, energy, and material needs through integrated
biological design. Melliodora is extensively documented in Holmgren's book
*Melliodora: A Case Study in Design* and is frequently referenced throughout
*Permaculture: Principles and [[permaculture-principles-pathways-beyond-sustainability]]* as a source of
practical examples.

## Classification

- **Category**: Permaculture Demonstration Property
- **Location**: Hepburn Springs, central Victoria, Australia
- **Owners**: David Holmgren and Su Dennett
- **Established**: Mid-1980s
- **Area**: Approximately 1 hectare (2.5 acres)
- **Climate**: Cool temperate (central Victorian highlands)

## Key Facts

- The property is approximately 1 hectare in size, demonstrating that significant
  self-reliance is achievable on a small suburban-rural holding.
- Black Australorp chickens are kept for egg production and soil cultivation,
  selected because the breed can generally be contained by standard-height fences.
- Goats are managed with movable electric mesh fencing for orchard grazing,
  with actual annual electricity use less than that of a regularly used light globe.
- Nitrogen-fixing tagasaste and wattle shrubs are hard-pruned in gardens and
  orchards for goat fodder and tree mulch, requiring management to prevent
  excessive moisture competition and shading.
- A motorised slasher is used to convert brambles, weeds, and rough pasture
  to mulch and worm food, with grazing animals gradually replacing the slasher.
- Cockatoos are a major pest for fruit and nut crops; deterrent strategies have
  limited effectiveness against these powerful, intelligent flock birds.
- Extensive container reuse systems are practised, including reusing bottles for
  preserves, plastic bags for storage, egg cartons, plant tubes, and repurposed
  drums for grain storage and liquid manure.
- Food waste is fed to fowls rather than composted directly — the fowls convert
  scraps to eggs and manure. Local restaurant waste is also collected as feed.
- Hot water is used in a cascading hierarchy: bathing, clothes washing, nappy
  rinsing, then irrigation of fruit trees.
- The property demonstrates multiple permaculture zones from intensive kitchen
  garden (Zone 1) through orchard (Zone 2) to managed native forest.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Soil Biological Management**: The integration of poultry, goats, and
  composting creates diverse organic matter inputs that feed soil microbial
  communities, including beneficial [[teaming-with-microbes-fungi-and-mycorrhizal-relationships]] networks.
- **Liquid Manure System**: 200-litre polyethylene barrels are used for liquid
  manure production, a process involving anaerobic and aerobic fermentation
  that supports diverse microbial communities including beneficial fungi.
- **Mulch and Fungal Habitat**: The slasher conversion of brambles and pasture
  to mulch creates woody organic matter that supports saprophytic fungal
  colonisation and gradual decomposition.
- **Worm Farming Integration**: Worm farms process wet, high-nitrogen wastes,
  with vermicompost introducing fungal spores and [[forest-and-bamboo-beneficial-microorganisms]]
  into garden soils.
- **Companion Planting**: Tagasaste and wattle (Acacia) species are nitrogen-
  fixers that also form [[plants-without-mycorrhizal-associations]], supporting underground fungal
  networks that benefit neighbouring fruit trees.
- **Water Cycling**: The cascading water use hierarchy maintains soil moisture
  levels and supports soil biological activity including fungal networks.
- **Zone Planning**: The property's zone design creates microclimates suitable
  for various cultivation approaches, including potential mushroom production
  in shaded, moist zones.
