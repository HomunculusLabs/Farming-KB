## Epichloe

## Description

*Epichloe* is a genus of [[fungal-endophytes]] in the family Clavicipitaceae that form systemic infections in cool-season grasses (Pooideae). *Epichloe* species are the sexual (teleomorphic) stage of the endophyte life cycle, producing external stromata on host grass stems that contain perithecia and ascospores for horizontal transmission. They are the progenitors of the asexual *Neotyphodium* endophytes, and the genus collectively represents one of the most important model systems for studying fungal–plant mutualism, coevolution, and the evolution of secondary metabolism.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Phylum**: [[ascomycota]]
- **Class**: Sordariomycetes
- **Order**: Hypocreales
- **Family**: Clavicipitaceae

## Key Facts

- **Sexual reproduction**: *Epichloe* species produce visible stromata (choke disease) on flowering tillers of host grasses, which prevents seed production on infected tillers. Perithecia within stromata produce ascospores that are ejected and dispersed to new hosts.
- **Dual transmission**: Unlike strictly asexual *Neotyphodium*, *Epichloe* employs both horizontal transmission (via ascospores from stromata) and vertical transmission (through seeds of unchokeed tillers). Some species are strictly horizontal transmitters, while others use both modes.
- **Host range**: Infects over 100 grass species across multiple genera including *Festuca*, *Lolium*, *Bromus*, *Poa*, *Agrostis*, *Elymus*, and *Brachypodium*.
- **Alkaloid production**: *Epichloe* species produce the same four major classes of alkaloids as their asexual derivatives: ergot alkaloids, indole-diterpenes (lolitrems), lolines, and peramine. These compounds confer anti-herbivore defense to the host plant.
- **Choke disease**: The production of stromata on flowering stems (choke) prevents seed set on infected tillers, representing a fitness cost to the plant. This is the primary "pathogenic" aspect of the otherwise mutualistic association.
- **Table 5.13 data**: Clay (1990) reported that *Epichloe* endophytes increase vegetative growth, suppress reproduction, and confer herbivore resistance across approximately 100 host species.

## Ecological Role

- The balance between mutualism (herbivore defense, growth enhancement) and parasitism (reproductive suppression via choke) determines the net fitness effect on host grasses.
- *Epichloe* stromata serve as mating platforms requiring cross-fertilization by flies of the genus *Botanophila*, adding another trophic level to the grass–endophyte interaction.
- The genus plays a central role in structuring grassland communities through differential effects on host-plant fitness.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Hybridization events**: *Neotyphodium* species (including those used in commercial grass cultivars) arose through interspecific hybridization between *Epichloe* lineages. Understanding *Epichloe* biology is essential for endophyte breeding programs.
- **Cultivation of stromata**: *Epichloe* can be maintained in culture, and stromata can be induced on host grasses in greenhouse settings for research on mating biology and spore production.
- **Evolutionary significance**: The *Epichloe–Neotyphodium* system is the premier model for studying the evolutionary transition from sexual to asexual reproduction in mutualistic fungi, and the consequences of this transition for genetic diversity and ecological function.
