## Overview

**Allium cepa** is a biennial bulb-forming plant in the family Amaryllidaceae, widely cultivated as one of the world's most important vegetable crops.

Commonly known as the bulb onion or common onion, it has been domesticated for over 5,000 years and is a staple ingredient in virtually every global cuisine.

The species is also cultivated for its edible leaves as green onions or scallions, and specialized forms produce pearl onions, boiler onions, and pickling onions.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

### Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Order:** Asparagales
- **Family:** Amaryllidaceae
- **Subfamily:** Allioideae
- **Genus:** *Allium*
- **Species:** *A. cepa* L.

The closest wild relative is *Allium vavilovii* Popov & Vved. from Central Asia, though the exact wild progenitor remains debated among taxonomists.

### Morphology

The bulb consists of swollen leaf bases (cataphylls) arranged in concentric layers around a compressed stem plate at the base.

Bulb diameter varies from 2–3 cm in pearl onion types to over 15 cm in large storage cultivars.

Leaves are hollow, tubular, and fistulose, typically 30–60 cm tall, arising from the stem plate in a fan-like arrangement.

The inflorescence is a terminal umbel containing numerous small flowers on pedicels, enclosed by a papery spathe before anthesis.

Flowers are actinomorphic with six tepals, six stamens, and a superior ovary, typically white, pink, or purple in color.

## Distribution and Habitat

The primary center of diversity is Central Asia, spanning from Turkmenistan and Iran through Afghanistan and Pakistan.

Secondary diversity centers exist in the Mediterranean region and in India, where distinct landrace groups have evolved over centuries of cultivation.

Cultivation has spread globally, with major production concentrated in China, India, the United States, Turkey, Pakistan, and Russia.

Onions are adapted to a wide range of temperate and subtropical climates, though bulb formation is strongly photoperiod-dependent.

Short-day cultivars require 10–12 hours of daylight and are grown at lower latitudes, while long-day types require 14–16 hours and dominate in northern temperate zones.

## Cultivation

Onions are propagated either from seed or from sets (small, dormant bulbs produced the previous season).

Direct seeding requires careful soil moisture management, as onion seeds are small and have low vigor during emergence.

Transplant production in nurseries or plug trays is common for large-scale commercial operations, allowing better establishment and earlier maturity.

Bulb initiation is triggered by photoperiod, with the critical daylength threshold varying by cultivar group.

Optimal growing temperatures range from 13–24°C, with temperatures above 27°C suppressing bulb development.

Harvest occurs when approximately 50% of the tops have fallen over naturally, indicating the onset of dormancy.

Curing at 25–30°C with adequate ventilation for 2–4 weeks develops the dry outer skin essential for long-term storage.

## Nutritional and Culinary Value

Raw onions are approximately 89% water, with significant amounts of vitamin C, vitamin B6, folate, and dietary fiber.

Quercetin, a flavonol concentrated in the outer skin layers, is one of the most abundant polyphenols in the human diet from onion consumption.

The characteristic pungency and lachrymatory (tear-inducing) properties are produced by sulfur-containing compounds released when cells are damaged.

When onion tissue is cut, the enzyme alliinase converts isoalliin (S-1-propenyl-L-cysteine sulfoxide) to 1-propenyl sulfenic acid, which rearranges to syn-propanethial-S-oxide.

Cooking reduces pungency through thermal degradation of these volatile sulfur compounds, while caramelization develops sweetness through Maillard reactions.

## Chemistry and Pharmacology

Allium cepa contains organosulfur compounds including thiosulfinates, cepaenes, and zwiebelanes that exhibit antimicrobial and antiplatelet activity.

Onion extracts have demonstrated hypoglycemic effects in animal models, attributed to sulfur compounds and flavonoid constituents.

Quercetin and its glycosides from onion have shown anti-inflammatory activity through inhibition of lipoxygenase and cyclooxygenase pathways.

The antioxidant capacity of onion peel extracts is among the highest of common food byproducts, supporting interest in industrial use as a natural preservative.

## Pests and Diseases

The onion thrips (*Thrips tabaci*) is the most damaging insect pest of onion worldwide, causing both direct feeding injury and vectoring iris yellow spot virus.

Onion maggot (*Delia antiqua*) larvae feed on the bulb and root system, often causing complete plant loss in heavily infested fields.

Downy mildew (*Peronospora destructor*) and purple blotch (*Alternaria porri*) are the two most important foliar diseases in humid production regions.

Basal rot, caused by *Fusarium oxysporum* f. sp. *cepae*, is a soil-bone pathogen that develops during storage and can destroy entire crops post-harvest.

Botrytis leaf blight (*Botrytis squamosa*) is problematic in regions with frequent leaf wetness, requiring predictive spray programs based on disease forecasting models.
