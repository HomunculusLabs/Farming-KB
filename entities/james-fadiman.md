## James Fadiman

James Fadiman (born 1939) is an American psychologist and author
known for his psychedelic research and his role in popularizing
microdosing. His 2011 book *The Psychedelic Explorer's Guide*
became a foundational text for the modern psychedelic renaissance.

## Early Life and Education

Fadiman earned his undergraduate degree from Harvard University,
where he studied social relations. He completed his doctorate in
psychology at Stanford University in 1965. His graduate training
coincided with the peak of academic psychedelic research in
America, exposing him to the field at a formative stage.

At Stanford, he studied under prominent psychologists including
researchers in humanistic and transpersonal psychology. These
influences shaped his interest in consciousness, creativity, and
the psychological dimensions of spiritual experience.

## Psychedelic Research Career

### International Foundation for Advanced Study

In the 1960s, Fadiman worked with the International Foundation for
Advanced Study (IFAS) in Menlo Park, California. IFAS conducted
some of the most rigorous early psychedelic research in the United
States. Researchers there administered [[mescaline]] to
volunteers in controlled therapeutic settings.

Fadiman's work at IFAS focused on psychedelic-assisted creative
problem-solving. He designed studies where professionals attempted
difficult technical or artistic challenges during LSD sessions.
Several participants reported breakthrough solutions that they later
implemented in their careers.

### Creative Problem-Solving Studies

Fadiman's creativity studies remain landmark contributions to
psychedelic research. In one study, engineers and scientists took
low doses of LSD before working on professional problems they had
been unable to solve. A significant percentage produced workable
solutions during or after the session.

These studies suggested that psychedelics could enhance divergent
thinking and pattern recognition. The research was cut short by
prohibition in the late 1960s, but the data preserved in
Fadiman's publications remains valuable for contemporary
researchers.
