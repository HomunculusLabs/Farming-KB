## Overview

**Litsea cubeba** is a small evergreen tree in the family Lauraceae, native to East and [[permaculture-designers-manual-essential-oil-plants]], which is rich in citral (a mixture of geranial and neral). The oil is widely used in perfumery, aromatherapy, food flavoring, and traditional medicine throughout Asia.

May Chang oil has a fresh, lemony fragrance and serves as a natural source of citral, an important precursor in the synthesis of vitamin A, ionones, and various aroma chemicals. Global production is centered in China, Vietnam, and Indonesia.

The tree is also used in traditional Chinese medicine for treating respiratory ailments, digestive complaints, and [[psilocybin-potency-variation-species-strain-growth-conditions]] include mean annual temperatures of 15–25 °C, annual rainfall of 1200–2000 mm, and well-drained, acidic to neutral soils (pH 5.0–6.5). The tree tolerates light frost but is damaged by temperatures below −5 °C.

In its native range, *L. cubeba* is a pioneer species that colonizes disturbed sites, forest clearings, and abandoned [[hamilton-vegetative-propagation]] by softwood cuttings under mist is also practiced, particularly for selecting high-yielding clones with superior essential oil profiles. Grafting onto seedling rootstocks is used in commercial orchards.

In commercial essential oil production, trees are planted at spacings of 2–3 m on center and managed through coppicing on a 3–5 year rotation. The entire above-ground biomass is harvested and steam-distilled for oil extraction.

Fruits are harvested when they begin turning from green to dark purple, typically from July to September. Fruit oil content ranges from 3–5% by fresh weight and contains up to 80% citral.

Leaf and branch distillation provides a lower-yielding but more sustainably harvested oil source. Leaf oil contains 60–70% citral with a different terpenoid profile than fruit oil.

## Essential Oil and Chemistry

The essential oil of *L. cubeba* is dominated by citral, a mixture of two isomeric monoterpene aldehydes: geranial (citral A, ~40–50%) and neral (citral B, ~25–35%). Total citral content ranges from 70–85% depending on plant part, [[staphylococcus-aureus]]* and *Escherichia coli*), fungi, and yeast. This activity is primarily attributed to the citral content.

Quality grading of commercial May Chang oil is based on citral content, with premium grades exceeding 80% total citral. Adulteration with synthetic citral or cheaper citral-rich oils (lemongrass) is a known quality concern.

Gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS) analysis is the standard method for verifying oil authenticity and profiling the complete volatile composition of commercial May Chang oil samples.

## Pharmacology and Uses

In traditional Chinese medicine, *L. cubeba* fruits are used to treat cough, asthma, bronchitis, and other [[cannabis-respiratory-conditions]]. The dried fruits are decocted or powdered for internal use.
