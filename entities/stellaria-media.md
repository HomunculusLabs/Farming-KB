# Stellaria media (Chickweed)

## Overview

Stellaria media, commonly known as common chickweed, is a low-growing annual or
overwintering herb in the Caryophyllaceae (pink) family. Native to Europe, it has
become one of the most widespread and recognizable weeds on Earth, found on every
continent including Antarctica. Despite its reputation as a garden weed, chickweed
is a highly nutritious edible plant with significant medicinal properties. Its mild
flavor, tender texture, and year-round availability in many climates make it a
staple for foragers. In permaculture systems, it serves as a living mulch,
nutrient cycler, and beneficial insect habitat.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae → **Clade:** Tracheophytes → **Clade:** Angiosperms →
  **Clade:** Eudicots → **Order:** Caryophyllales → **Family:** Caryophyllaceae →
  **Genus:** *Stellaria* → **Species:** *S. media*

## Botanical Description

- **Growth habit:** Prostrate to ascending annual or winter annual; forms dense
  mats 5–40 cm tall; one plant can spread over 50 cm
- **Stem:** Slender, trailing, rooting at nodes; a single line of hairs runs
  along one side of the stem between leaf nodes (key identification feature
  distinguishing it from similar species)
- **Leaves:** Opposite, ovate to elliptic, 5–25 mm long, pointed tip, entire
  margins, smooth to slightly hairy; upper leaves lack petioles
- **Flowers:** Small (6–8 mm), white, five-petaled; petals are deeply cleft
  (appearing as ten petals), giving a star-like appearance (hence *Stellaria*);
  borne on slender pedicels in terminal leafy cymes; flowers open in sunlight
- **Fruit:** Ovoid capsule, 4–6 mm, containing numerous tiny reddish-brown seeds
  (0.8–1.2 mm); a single plant can produce 2,500–15,000 seeds
- **Root:** Shallow, fibrous root system; readily roots from stem nodes

## Key Chemical Constituents

- **Saponins:** Including stellamerin A and B; contribute to anti-inflammatory
  and expectorant properties; give the plant a slightly soapy taste when chewed
- **Flavonoids:** Rutin, quercetin, apigenin, kaempferol derivatives; antioxidant
  and anti-inflammatory activity
- **Coumarins:** Umbelliferone; mild antispasmodic and anticoagulant activity
- **Vitamins:** Exceptionally high in vitamin C, beta-carotene (provitamin A),
  vitamin B complex, especially B6 and B12
- **Minerals:** Iron, calcium, magnesium, manganese, zinc, copper, phosphorus
- **Mucilage:** Polysaccharides providing demulcent and soothing properties
- **Gamma-linolenic acid (GLA):** Present in seeds; anti-inflammatory fatty acid
- **Silica:** Contributes to structural support of connective tissue

## Nutritional Profile (per 100 g fresh weight)

- **Calories:** ~25 kcal; **Protein:** ~2.3 g; **Fat:** ~0.5 g; **Carbs:** ~3.5 g
- **Vitamin C:** ~230 mg (250%+ DV); **Vitamin A:** ~2,300 IU (~46% DV)
- **Iron:** ~1.9 mg (~11% DV); **Calcium:** ~190 mg (~15% DV)

## Medicinal Properties

- **Anti-inflammatory and vulnerary:** Applied topically as a poultice, salve, or
  compress for wounds, burns, ulcers, eczema, psoriasis, rashes, and insect bites;
  one of the most effective topical wound-healing herbs in Western herbalism
- **Demulcent:** Internal use soothes inflamed mucous membranes in the
  gastrointestinal and respiratory tracts; useful for gastritis, peptic ulcers,
  bronchitis, and sore throats
- **Lymphatic support:** Traditional use as a lymphatic decongestant for swollen
  lymph nodes, cysts, and fibrocystic breast tissue
- **Diuretic:** Mild diuretic action supports kidney function and fluid retention
- **Expectorant:** Saponin content helps loosen and expel respiratory mucus
- **Antirheumatic:** Both internal and external applications for joint pain and
  inflammation
- **Weight management support:** Traditionally used to support metabolism; modern
  interest centers on its GLA content and nutrient density

## Edible Uses

- **Raw:** Tender leaves, stems, and flowers eaten fresh in salads, sandwiches,
  wraps, and as a garnish; mild, slightly sweet flavor reminiscent of corn silk
- **Cooked:** Excellent in soups, stews, stir-fries, pesto, and quiches; cooks
  quickly and retains texture; similar to spinach when wilted
- **Juice:** Fresh plant juice is a traditional spring tonic; can be added to
  smoothies and green drinks
- **Poultry forage:** Named for its popularity as chicken and game bird forage;
  one of the most nutritious greens for domestic poultry
- **Harvesting tips:** Best harvested before flowering for tenderest texture;
  the entire above-ground plant is edible; avoid areas treated with herbicides

## Permaculture and Ecological Role

- **Living mulch:** Dense mat-forming habit suppresses weed germination and
  retains soil moisture while allowing crop seedlings to push through
- **Nutrient cycling:** Shallow roots capture surface nutrients and draw them up;
  decomposing biomass returns minerals to the soil surface
- **Soil indicator:** Thrives in nitrogen-rich, cultivated soils; presence
  indicates fertile ground
- **Beneficial insect habitat:** Tiny flowers attract minute pirate bugs,
  parasitic wasps, syrphid flies, and other beneficial predators
- **Poultry integration:** Excellent ground cover in chicken forage systems;
  chickens actively seek it out and will help control spread while fertilizing
- **Self-seeding:** Reliable self-seeder that fills bare ground quickly;
  excellent pioneer species for disturbed soils and new garden beds
- **Cold tolerance:** Survives light frosts; available for foraging year-round
  in mild climates

## Safety and Contraindications

- Generally recognized as safe for food and moderate medicinal use
- Contains saponins; excessive consumption of raw plant may cause mild
  gastrointestinal irritation in sensitive individuals; cooking reduces
  saponin content
- Avoid large internal doses during pregnancy (limited safety data)
- Potential nitrate accumulation in nitrogen-fertilized agricultural soils;
  harvest from clean, organic sources when possible
- Not a concern for pets; chickens and other poultry consume it avidly
