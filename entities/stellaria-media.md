## Botanical Description

- **Growth habit:** Prostrate to ascending annual or winter annual; forms dense
  mats 5–40 cm tall; one plant can spread over 50 cm
- **Stem:** Slender, trailing, rooting at nodes; a single line of hairs runs
  along one side of the stem between leaf nodes (key identification feature
  distinguishing it from similar species)
- **Leaves:** Opposite, ovate to elliptic, 5–25 mm long, pointed tip, entire
  margins, smooth to slightly hairy; upper leaves lack petioles
- **Flowers:** Small (6–8 mm), white, five-petaled; petals are deeply cleft
  (appearing as ten petals), giving a star-like appearance (hence *Stellaria*);
  borne on slender pedicels in terminal leafy cymes; flowers open in sunlight
- **Fruit:** Ovoid capsule, 4–6 mm, containing numerous tiny reddish-brown seeds
  (0.8–1.2 mm); a single plant can produce 2,500–15,000 seeds
- **Root:** Shallow, fibrous root system; readily roots from stem nodes

## Key Chemical Constituents

- **Saponins:** Including stellamerin A and B; contribute to anti-inflammatory
  and expectorant properties; give the plant a slightly soapy taste when chewed
- **Flavonoids:** Rutin, [[apigenin]], [[comparison-silicon-vs-calcium-in-plant-defense-and-structural-support]] of connective tissue

## Nutritional Profile (per 100 g fresh weight)

- **Calories:** ~25 kcal; **Protein:** ~2.3 g; **Fat:** ~0.5 g; **Carbs:** ~3.5 g
- **Vitamin C:** ~230 mg (250%+ DV); **Vitamin A:** ~2,300 IU (~46% DV)
- **Iron:** ~1.9 mg (~11% DV); **Calcium:** ~190 mg (~15% DV)

## Medicinal Properties

- **Anti-inflammatory and vulnerary:** Applied topically as a poultice, salve, or
  compress for wounds, burns, ulcers, eczema, psoriasis, rashes, and insect bites;
  one of the most effective topical wound-healing herbs in Western herbalism
- **Demulcent:** Internal use soothes inflamed mucous membranes in the
  gastrointestinal and respiratory tracts; useful for gastritis, peptic ulcers,
