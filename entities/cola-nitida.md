## Overview

**Cola nitida** (Vent.) Schott & Endl. is an evergreen tree in the family Malvaceae, native to the tropical rainforests of West Africa.

It is the primary source of the **kola nut**, a culturally significant stimulant that has been consumed across West and Central Africa for centuries and was historically a key ingredient in Coca-Cola.

The seeds contain **caffeine (1.5–3.5%)** and [[cola-acuminata]]* (Schott & Endl.) produces the bitter kola, which has lower caffeine content.

## Morphology

**Tree:** Medium to large evergreen, reaching 12–20 m in height with a dense, spreading crown. Trunk diameter typically 30–60 cm at maturity.

**Bark:** Dark gray to brown, rough and fissured, exuding a reddish sap when cut.

**Leaves:** Simple, alternate, obovate to elliptical, 10–20 cm × 5–10 cm, leathery, glossy dark green above and paler beneath. Margins are entire to slightly wavy.

**Flowers:** Small, yellowish-white to cream, borne in dense axillary clusters. Flowers are unisexual, with male and female flowers typically on separate trees (dioecious) or occasionally monoecious.

**Fruit:** Large, woody, leathery follicles, 10–15 cm long, containing 4–10 seeds arranged in two rows within each pod.

**Seeds (kola nuts):** Approximately 2.5 cm long, ovoid, with a reddish-brown to white seed coat. Fresh seeds have a **bitter, astringent taste** followed by a sweet aftertaste.

## Distribution and Habitat

*C. nitida* is native to the **humid tropical lowland forests** of West Africa, from Sierra Leone eastward through Liberia, Côte d'Ivoire, Ghana, Togo, Benin, Nigeria, and into Cameroon.

It is widely cultivated throughout its native range and has been introduced to [[psilocybe-southeast-asia-pacific]], and the Indian Ocean islands.

The species grows at low elevations (0–500 m) in areas with **annual rainfall of 1,200–2,500 mm** and mean temperatures of 25–30°C.

It thrives in deep, well-drained, fertile forest soils but tolerates a range of tropical soil types.

## Bioactive Compounds

The primary bioactive constituents of kola nuts are **methylxanthine alkaloids**, principally caffeine and theobromine.

**Caffeine** content ranges from 1.5–3.5% of fresh seed weight, comparable to coffee beans (1–2%) and exceeding tea leaves (1–3% of dry weight).

**Theobromine** content is approximately 0.02–0.05%, similar to levels found in cacao.

The seeds also contain **kolatin** (a cardioglycoside), **kolanin** (a coloring compound), and significant amounts of **tannins** (4–8%) responsible for the characteristic astringent flavor.

**[[aact-tannins-terpenes-phenolic-compounds-wood-composting-ingham]]** including catechin, epicatechin, and procyanidins contribute antioxidant activity.

Vitamin C content is notably high in fresh seeds — approximately 100–200 mg per 100 g — making kola nuts a significant source in traditional diets.
