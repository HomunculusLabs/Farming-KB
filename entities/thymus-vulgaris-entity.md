# Thymus vulgaris
Thymus vulgaris, common thyme, is a low-growing evergreen subshrub in the
family Lamiaceae native to the Mediterranean basin. It is one of the most
important [[holzer-medicinal-and-culinary-herbs-compendium]] in European and Middle Eastern cuisines and has a
long history of medicinal use dating back to ancient Egypt, Greece, and Rome.
The plant's [[permaculture-designers-manual-essential-oil-plants]], dominated by thymol and carvacrol, possesses
potent antimicrobial, antiseptic, and antioxidant properties that have been
validated by modern pharmacological research. Thyme is also valued in
permaculture and [[hamilton-geoffhamilton-nick-organic-gardening-companion-planting]] as a [[fukuoka-citrus-orchard-ground-cover-cultivation]], pollinator attractant,
and companion plant that repels certain pest species. Its adaptability to
poor, well-drained soils and drought conditions makes it a resilient choice
for water-wise gardens and xeriscaping.
## Taxonomy and Naming
- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Division:** Magnoliophyta
- **Class:** Magnoliopsida
- **Order:** Lamiales
- **Family:** Lamiaceae
- **Genus:** Thymus
- **Species:** T. vulgaris L., 1753
- **Common names:** Common thyme, garden thyme, English thyme, French thyme
- **Chemotypes:** Six distinct chemotypes exist within T. vulgaris based on
  dominant essential oil constituents: thymol, carvacrol, linalool,
  geraniol, α-terpineol, and trans-thuyanol; chemotype is genetically
  determined and varies geographically across the native range
## Description
### Growth Habit and Morphology
- **Habit:** Low, bushy evergreen subshrub, 10–30 cm tall, spreading
  30–45 cm wide; woody base with herbaceous stems
- **Stems:** Numerous, slender, quadrangular, ascending; young stems
  green and pubescent, becoming woody and grey-brown at the base
- **Leaves:** Opposite, ovate to elliptic, 4–10 mm long, 2–5 mm wide;
  sessile or very short petiole; margins revolute (rolled under);
  dark green above, grey-green tomentose below; leathery texture;
  aromatic when crushed
- **Flowers:** Whorled in dense terminal capitula; calyx tubular,
  3–4 mm, two-lipped; corolla bilabiate, 4–6 mm, pale pink to
  lavender-white; flowering in late spring to midsummer (May–July)
- **Fruit:** Four nutlets (mericarps), 0.8–1 mm, brown, enclosed in
  persistent calyx
- **Roots:** Fibrous, shallow but extensive lateral spread; tolerant
  of rocky and shallow soils
- **Lifespan:** Typically 3–5 years as a productive culinary plant;
  woody bases can persist longer but become less productive

## Distribution and Habitat
- **Native range:** Southern Europe (Spain, Italy, Greece, France),
  North Africa, and western Turkey; grows wild on dry, rocky hillsides,
  limestone outcrops, and garigue (Mediterranean scrub)
- **Cultivated range:** Worldwide in temperate regions; commercially
  grown in Spain, France, Morocco, Albania, Turkey, and the USA
- **Naturalised:** Parts of [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]], Australia, and New Zealand
  where it has escaped cultivation
- **Habitat preference:** Dry, calcareous, well-drained soils; open,
  sunny positions; found in garrigue, phrygana, and steppe communities
## Chemistry and Active Compounds
### Essential Oil Composition
The essential oil (0.8–2.5% of dry weight) is the primary pharmacological
agent. Composition varies dramatically by chemotype:
- **Thymol (20–55%):** Dominant phenolic monoterpene; powerful antiseptic
  and antimicrobial; expectorant and antispasmodic; used in mouthwashes
  (Listerine) and cough medicines; the basis of thyme's medicinal
  reputation since antiquity
- **Carvacrol (1–15%):** Closely related to thymol; synergistic
  antimicrobial activity; effective against foodborne pathogens
  (Salmonella, E. coli, Listeria)
- **p-Cymene (10–25%):** Aromatic hydrocarbon; precursor in the
  biosynthetic pathway to thymol and carvacrol
- **Linalool (1–5%):** Floral-scented monoterpene alcohol; anxiolytic
  and sedative properties; dominant in the linalool chemotype
- **γ-Terpinene (5–15%):** Monoterpene precursor to thymol/carvacrol
- **Bornyl acetate, α-pinene, β-pinene, camphene, 1,8-cineole:**
  Minor constituents contributing to the complex aroma profile
### Non-Volatile Constituents
- **Flavonoids:** Apigenin, luteolin, thymonin, naringenin; antioxidant
  and anti-inflammatory activity
- **Phenolic acids:** Rosmarinic acid, caffeic acid; potent antioxidants
  with anti-inflammatory properties
- **Tannins:** 5–10% dry weight; astringent properties
- **Vitamins:** High in vitamin K, vitamin C, iron, manganese, and calcium
## Cultivation and Permaculture Role
### Cultivation
- **Propagation:** Seed (slow, 2–3 weeks germination), cuttings
  (preferred for cultivar preservation), or division
- **Spacing:** 20–30 cm apart in rows; intercropping with vegetables
  at 15–20 cm
- **Pruning:** Cut back by one-third after flowering to maintain
  compact shape and promote fresh growth; hard prune in [[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]]
- **Harvest:** Leaves can be harvested year-round; peak essential oil
  content occurs just before or at full flower
- **Drying:** Hang bunches in warm, dry, dark place; retains flavour
  well when dried
### Permaculture Functions
- **Pollinator plant:** Extremely attractive to bees, butterflies, and
  beneficial wasps; one of the top nectar-producing herbs; thyme honey
  is prized for its flavour and medicinal properties
- **Ground cover:** Dense, low growth suppresses weeds and protects
  soil from erosion on slopes
