# Escaline

Escaline (3,5-Dimethoxy-4-ethoxyphenethylamine) is a psychedelic phenethylamine closely related to mescaline-entity|mescaline.

It is documented as entry #168 in PiHKAL by [[proscaline]]|proscaline (4-propoxy), and buscaline-entity|buscaline (4-butoxy).

As the 4-position alkoxy chain lengthens, potency generally increases then decreases, with the ethoxy (escaline) and propoxy (proscaline-entity|proscaline) positions showing the most favorable profiles.

The ethoxy group provides an optimal balance between lipophilicity (for blood-brain barrier penetration) and receptor affinity.

## Synthesis

The synthesis begins with gallic acid or a suitable derivative.

One approach starts with eugenol or isoeugenol, converting it through a series of steps to the 3,5-dimethoxy-4-ethoxybenzaldehyde.

The aldehyde is condensed with nitromethane to form the beta-nitrostyrene.

Reduction with lithium aluminum hydride yields escaline as the free base.

The product is converted to the hydrochloride salt for isolation and characterization.

## Natural Occurrence Considerations

The relationship between escaline and natural products is worth noting.

While mescaline-entity|mescaline occurs naturally in peyote cactus and other species, escaline does not occur naturally.

However, the [[permaculture-designers-manual-essential-oil-plants]] precursors that could theoretically give rise to escaline-like compounds through metabolic amination do exist in nature.

This connects to Shulgin's broader concept of "Essential Amphetamines" that could arise from natural [[comparison-essential-oils-vs-neem-oil-vs-insecticidal-soap]].

## The Alkoxy Series

The 4-alkoxy mescaline analogues demonstrate the importance of the 4-position substituent in determining psychedelic character.

The series shows a consistent pattern: methoxy (mescaline-entity|mescaline) is the least potent, ethoxy (escaline) is more potent, propoxy (proscaline-entity|proscaline) may be equally or slightly more potent, and butoxy (buscaline-entity|buscaline) is less potent.

The sulfur analogues (TE, TB) provide additional comparison points, generally showing increased potency over their oxygen counterparts.

## Pharmacological Context

Escaline acts as a psychedelic through 5-HT2A receptor agonism, similar to mescaline-entity|mescaline.

The ethoxy group provides slightly greater lipophilicity than the methoxy group, which may enhance blood-brain barrier penetration and contribute to the increased potency.

The compound's pharmacokinetic profile (absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion) has not been formally studied.

## Legal Status

Escaline is controlled in many jurisdictions as a mescaline analogue.

In the [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]], it would likely fall under the Federal Analogue Act.

The compound has been specifically scheduled in some countries as part of broader phenethylamine scheduling.

## Harm Reduction

The 40-60 mg dose range requires accurate milligram measurement.

Nausea during onset is possible, consistent with the mescaline-like profile.

The 8-12 hour duration means users should plan for most of a day.

As with all psychedelic phenethylamines, escaline should not be combined with MAOIs or other serotonergic substances.

The compound should not be confused with 2c-e-entity|2C-E, which is an unrelated compound with a different substitution pattern.
