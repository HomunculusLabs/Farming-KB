## Overview

Harmalol is a β-carboline alkaloid found in several plant species, most notably
*Peganum harmala* ([[golden-guide-syrian-rue-and-maoi-plants]]) and *Banisteriopsis caapi* (ayahuasca). It is a
close structural analogue of harmine and harmaline, differing by the position
of a single hydroxyl group on the aromatic ring system.

As a reversible inhibitor of monoamine oxidase A (MAO-A), harmalol contributes
to the psychoactive effects of ayahuasca preparations by preventing the
metabolic breakdown of DMT in the gastrointestinal tract. However, it is
typically present in much lower concentrations than harmine or harmaline in
traditional preparations.

Harmalol was first isolated from *Peganum harmala* seeds in the mid-20th
century. Despite being less studied than its better-known relatives, it has
attracted research interest for its pharmacological profile and potential
neuroprotective properties.

## Chemical Properties

**Systematic name:** 1-Methyl-7-hydroxy-3,4-dihydro-β-carboline

**Molecular formula:** C₁₁H₁₂N₂O

**Molecular weight:** 188.23 g/mol

**CAS number:** 487-20-7

**Structure:** β-Carboline (pyrido[3,4-b]indole) skeleton with a methyl group
at the 1-position and a hydroxyl group at the 7-position. The 3,4-dihydro
form (partially saturated C-ring) distinguishes harmalol from the fully
aromatic compound harmol (7-hydroxy-harmine).

**Solubility:** Soluble in ethanol, methanol, and dilute acids. Poorly soluble
in water and nonpolar organic solvents. Forms water-soluble salts with
hydrochloric and sulfuric acids.

**Appearance:** Yellow crystalline solid in its pure form. Solutions exhibit
blue fluorescence under UV light (365 nm), a characteristic shared by many
β-carboline alkaloids due to the planar aromatic ring system.

## Pharmacology

**MAO inhibition:** Harmalol acts as a reversible competitive inhibitor of
monoamine oxidase A (MAO-A), the enzyme primarily responsible for oxidative
deamination of serotonin, norepinephrine, and other monoamine neurotransmitters
in the gut and central nervous system.

Its MAO-A inhibitory potency (IC₅₀) is reported in the range of 100–500 nM,
making it significantly less potent than harmine (IC₅₀ ≈ 5–50 nM) but still
pharmacologically relevant at the concentrations achieved in traditional
ayahuasca preparations.

**Serotonin receptor interactions:** Harmalol shows weak affinity for 5-HT₂A
receptors (Ki > 10 µM), insufficient to produce direct psychedelic effects at
typical oral doses. Its psychoactive contribution in ayahuasca is primarily
through MAO inhibition rather than direct serotonergic agonism.

**Neuroprotective effects:** In vitro studies have demonstrated that harmalol
exerts antioxidant and neuroprotective activity, reducing [[blesching-cannabis-oxidative-stress-and-antioxidant-defense]] in
neuronal cell models exposed to reactive oxygen species. The hydroxyl group at
the 7-position is thought to contribute to free radical scavenging capacity,
giving harmalol stronger antioxidant activity than harmine or harmaline.

**Other pharmacological targets:** Harmalol has been reported to interact with
DNA topoisomerases at high concentrations, suggesting potential cytotoxic
properties. It also shows moderate affinity for imidazoline I₂ receptors,
though the functional significance of this interaction is not well understood.

## Occurrence in Plants

Harmalol occurs naturally in several plant families:

- *Peganum harmala* (Zygophyllaceae): Seeds contain 0.03–0.2% harmalol as a
minor constituent alongside harmine (2–5%) and harmaline (1–3%).
- *Banisteriopsis caapi* (Malpighiaceae): Bark and leaves contain trace
amounts of harmalol among a complex mixture of β-carboline alkaloids.
- *Passiflora incarnata* (Passifloraceae): Present as a minor alkaloid in
the aerial parts of passionflower, contributing to its mild anxiolytic
effects.
- *Tribulus terrestris* (Zygophyllaceae): Detected in trace quantities in
plant extracts.

In all known sources, harmalol is a minor constituent relative to harmine and
harmaline. Its quantitative contribution to the pharmacological effects of
traditional preparations is therefore modest but additive.

## Effects and Dosage

Harmalol is rarely encountered in isolation. In the context of ayahuasca or
Syrian rue preparations, it contributes to the overall MAO-inhibitory effect
alongside more abundant β-carbolines.

**Estimated oral dose (pure compound):** No well-established human dose data.
Based on relative potency to harmaline, an active MAO-inhibitory dose would be
approximately 200–400 mg orally, though this is speculative and has not been
clinically validated.

**Duration:** Consistent with other reversible MAO-A inhibitors, harmalol's
effects persist for approximately 4–8 hours, depending on dose and individual
metabolism. The reversibility of MAO-A inhibition means dietary tyramine
restrictions are less critical than with irreversible MAOIs like phenelzine.

**Effects profile:** When consumed as part of ayahuasca, harmalol contributes
to nausea, vomiting, and the characteristic autonomic effects (pupillary
dilation, mild tachycardia). It does not produce significant visual
hallucinations or altered consciousness on its own at typical doses.

## Legal Status

Harmalol is not specifically scheduled in most jurisdictions. In the United
States, it is not listed as a controlled substance. It is not listed under
the UN Convention on Psychotropic Substances.

The plant sources (*Peganum harmala* seeds, *Banisteriopsis caapi* bark) are
generally legal to possess and sell, though extraction and concentration may be
restricted in some jurisdictions. France and Russia have specific restrictions
on *Peganum harmala* preparations.
