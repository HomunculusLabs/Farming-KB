## Overview

**Vaccinium pallidum**, the blue Ridge blueberry or hillside blueberry, is a deciduous low-growing shrub in the family Ericaceae.

It is native to the eastern [[aact-foliar-sticker-spreaders-leaf-surface-attachment-ingham]] is dark green and glossy; the lower surface is pale green with a distinctive whitish bloom.

Flowers appear in April to May, borne in short racemose clusters on the previous year's growth.

Individual flowers are urceolate, white to pinkish, approximately 5–7 mm long.

Fruits ripen from June through August, producing round berries 6–9 mm in diameter.

Berries are light blue with a waxy coating, containing numerous small seeds.

## Distribution and Habitat

**Vaccinium pallidum** is distributed across the eastern United States from Maine and southern Quebec to northern Georgia and Alabama.

Its range extends westward through the Appalachian Mountains into eastern Tennessee, Kentucky, and the Ozark Highlands of Missouri and Arkansas.

The species is most abundant on dry, acidic, well-drained soils derived from sandstone, granite, or shale parent material.

It is a characteristic component of oak-pine woodlands, dry rocky ridges, barrens, and glade margins.

Elevation typically ranges from 100 to 1500 m, with peak abundance in the 500–1200 m zone.

The species is fire-adapted and responds positively to periodic low-intensity burns that reduce canopy competition.

It also colonizes disturbed sites including roadsides, old fields, and utility rights-of-way.
