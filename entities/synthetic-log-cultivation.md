## Description

Synthetic-log cultivation is a widely used mushroom cultivation technique in which a sterilized substrate mixture — typically composed of hardwood sawdust supplemented with bran and other nutrients — is packed into special polypropylene bags with microfilter windows, inoculated with mushroom [[mushroom-spawn|spawn]], and managed through controlled environmental conditions to produce [[fungal-fruiting-body|fruiting bodies]]. The method simulates the natural log-based growth of wood-decomposing mushrooms but offers greater control, faster production cycles, and higher yields than traditional log cultivation.

## Classification

- **Category:** Cultivation Technique
- **Subtype:** Indoor Controlled Environment Agriculture
- **Applicable Species:** [[grifola-frondosa]], Pleurotus spp., Ganoderma spp., Lentinula edodes, and other lignicolous mushrooms

## Key Facts

- The substrate ("synthetic log") is typically 75–80% hardwood sawdust (fine + coarse blend, 3:1 ratio), supplemented with 18–23% coarse wheat bran, 1% sucrose, and 1% calcium compounds (lime or gypsum).
- Moisture content is maintained at 60–63% with pH 5.5–6.5.
- Special polypropylene bags with microfilter windows allow gas exchange while maintaining sterility (available from suppliers like Unicorn Imp. & Mfg. Corp.).
- The cultivation process involves three major stages: [[spawn-run-and-colonization|spawn run]] (mycelial colonization), primordia initiation, and fruiting body development.
- Each stage requires distinct environmental parameters for temperature, relative humidity, light, and CO₂/O₂ balance.

## Growth Stage Parameters

### Spawn Run
- **Temperature:** 20–25°C (China), 22–23°C (Japan), varies by region
- **Relative Humidity:** 60–70% (ambient)
- **Light:** Minimal (50 lux)
- **CO₂:** Mycelia tolerate elevated levels
- **Duration:** 30–40 days

### Primordia Initiation
- **Temperature:** 18–22°C (in enclosed bags)
- **Relative Humidity:** 80–90%
- **Light:** 200 lux
- **Ventilation:** 5–6 times/day
- **Duration:** 15–20 days (China), 40+7 days (Japan, two stages)

### Fruiting Body Development
- **Temperature:** 15–20°C (narrow range)
- **Relative Humidity:** 90–95% (avoid condensation)
- **Light:** 200–500 lux
- **CO₂:** Below 0.1% (1000 ppm); above 0.15% produces small/thin caps
- **Duration:** 14–21 days

## Substrate Variations

Three main formulas are documented for G. frondosa:

1. **Standard:** Hardwood sawdust 75%, wheat bran 23%, sucrose 1%, lime/gypsum 1%
2. **With forest soil:** Sawdust 80%, wheat bran 18%, lime 1%, sucrose 1%, plus 15% hardwood forest surface soil
3. **With recycled substrate:** Fine sawdust 40%, coarse sawdust 20%, spent substrate 20%, wheat bran 10%, additional supplements 10%

The [[hardwood-sawdust-substrate]] forms the base of all formulations, with [[substrate-preparation]] methods determining the final nutritional profile and physical structure of the synthetic log.

## Equipment and Infrastructure

### Cultivation Bags
- Polypropylene bags with microfilter patch windows are the standard container
- Bag dimensions vary by species and production scale
- The microfilter allows gas exchange while blocking contaminant spores
- [[mushroom-substrate-bags]] are available from specialized suppliers

### Environmental Control
- Dedicated rooms for spawn run and fruiting, each with independent climate control
- Temperature, humidity, CO₂, and lighting must be independently regulated
- The separation of spawn run and fruiting rooms is standard practice in China and Japan

## Species-Specific Applications

### Maitake ([[grifola-frondosa]])
- The dominant commercial method for maitake production worldwide
- Detailed in [[maitake-cultivation]] and [[maitake-fruiting-parameters]]
- Soil casing ([[soil-casing]]) can significantly increase yields
- Production cycle approximately 3 months from inoculation to harvest

### Other Species
- Shiitake (Lentinula edodes): Adapted from traditional log methods
- Oyster (Pleurotus spp.): Shorter cycles, higher yields on synthetic logs
- Reishi (Ganoderma spp.): Antler and conk forms both achievable

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Synthetic-log cultivation is the dominant commercial method for producing [[grifola-frondosa]] (maitake) and many other medicinal and edible mushrooms. Key advantages over traditional log cultivation include:

1. **Yield improvement:** 150–300 g/bag without soil casing; 300–500 g/bag with soil casing (using treated humus-rich forest or garden soil particles 0.5–0.8 cm diameter).
2. **Production cycle:** Approximately 3 months from inoculation to harvest, compared to 6–12 months for natural log methods.
3. **Environmental control:** Each growth parameter can be precisely regulated, enabling year-round production in climate-controlled facilities.
4. **Substrate flexibility:** Cottonseed hulls (25% substitution) and recycled spent substrate (20%) offer sustainable alternatives to fresh hardwood sawdust.
5. **Scalability:** The bag-based system is modular and scalable from small hobby operations to industrial production.

For detailed guidance on synthetic-log methods, see [[synthetic-log-cultivation-methods-guide]] and [[synthetic-log-cultivation-species-selection]].
