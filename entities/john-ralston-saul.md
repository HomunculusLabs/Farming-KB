# John Ralston Saul

## Description

John Ralston Saul is a Canadian philosopher, novelist, and public intellectual known for his critique of economic rationalism, corporatism, and the ideology of managerial efficiency that dominated Western public policy from the 1980s onward. David Holmgren cites Ralston Saul as a key critic of the economic rationality that has "failed to reinvigorate corporations or public institutions."

## Background and Classification

- **Type:** Person — philosopher, author, public intellectual
- **Nationality:** Canadian (born 1947)
- **Key work:** *Voltaire's Bastards: The Dictatorship of Reason in the West* (1992)
- **Domain:** Political philosophy, critique of economic rationalism
- **Recognition:** Winner of the Governor General's Literary Award; former president of PEN International

## Key Facts

- Argued that the dominance of technocratic rationalism has undermined democratic institutions and human welfare
- Critiqued the "dictatorship of reason" that privileges quantitative analysis over human judgement, ethics, and common sense
- Identified the neoliberal consensus of the 1980s–1990s as a period of consuming social capital rather than building it
- Advocated for pluralistic, humanistic approaches to governance and economics
- His critique aligns with Holmgren's observation that the productivity gains of the late 20th century were largely achieved by liquidating public and natural assets

## Relevance to Permaculture and Cultivation

- **Systems thinking over reductionism:** Both Ralston Saul and Permaculture reject the idea that complex living systems can be managed through simplistic metrics and top-down efficiency drives
- **Self-regulation:** His argument that corporate change driven solely by "efficiency" has failed mirrors the Permaculture insight that over-management of natural systems leads to brittleness
- **Energy descent context:** His analysis of how the neoliberal period consumed accumulated social capital parallels Holmgren's analysis of how industrial agriculture consumes accumulated natural capital
- **Feedback loops:** Both emphasise the importance of genuine feedback in systems — whether democratic accountability or ecological observation
