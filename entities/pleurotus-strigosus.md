# Pleurotus strigosus

## Description

*Pleurotus strigosus* is a species of oyster mushroom (genus *Pleurotus*) that was identified by Barron and Thorn (1987) as one of several *Pleurotus* species capable of killing nematodes using specialized secretory cells that produce toxin droplets. Along with *P. ostreatus*, *P. subareolatus*, and *P. cornucopiae*, *P. strigosus* produces minute spathulate (spoon-shaped) secretory cells on its mycelium that secrete droplets of a potent nematotoxic compound. Nematodes are killed within approximately 30 seconds of contact with these droplets, after which fungal hyphae penetrate the nematode body and consume it.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Phylum**: Basidiomycota
- **Class**: Agaricomycetes
- **Order**: Agaricales
- **Family**: Pleurotaceae
- **Genus**: *Pleurotus*
- **Species**: *P. strigosus*

## Key Facts

- **Nematode toxicity**: Barron and Thorn (1987) demonstrated that *P. strigosus* produces secretory cells that exude toxin droplets lethal to nematodes on contact. Death occurs within approximately 30 seconds — one of the fastest-acting natural nematocidal mechanisms known.
- **Spathulate secretory cells**: The toxin-producing structures are minute, spathulate (spoon or spatula-shaped) cells that project from the mycelium. These specialized cells are distinct from the sticky knobs, constricting rings, and adhesive networks used by other nematophagous fungi.
- **Post-kill colonization**: After killing the nematode, fungal hyphae penetrate through the nematode's orifices (mouth, anus, vulva) and digest the interior, using the nematode as a source of nitrogen and other nutrients.
- **Carnivorous basidiomycete**: The ability to trap and consume nematodes makes *Pleurotus* one of the few known carnivorous mushroom genera. This nematophagy is thought to supplement the fungus's nitrogen nutrition in nitrogen-poor wood substrates.
- **Related species**: The same nematode-killing ability was demonstrated in *P. ostreatus* (the common oyster mushroom), *P. subareolatus*, and *P. cornucopiae*, suggesting this is a genus-wide trait.

## Ecological Role

- Provides natural biological control of soil nematodes in wood decay habitats where *Pleurotus* species naturally occur.
- The carnivorous habit supplements nitrogen nutrition on dead wood substrates that are typically carbon-rich but nitrogen-poor.
- Demonstrates that nematophagy is not restricted to specialized predatory fungi (e.g., *Arthrobotrys*) but also occurs in common wood-decay mushrooms.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Oyster mushroom cultivation**: *Pleurotus* species are among the most widely cultivated mushrooms globally. Their nematophagy may contribute to the suppression of plant-parasitic nematodes in mushroom substrate and nearby soil.
- **Biocontrol potential**: The rapid-acting toxin produced by *Pleurotus* species has potential for development as a biological nematicide. The active compound has been identified as trans-2-decenedioic acid in some studies.
- **Nitrogen supplementation**: The carnivorous habit of *Pleurotus* has implications for substrate formulation in commercial cultivation — understanding the nitrogen economy of these fungi may improve yield optimization.
- **Substrate preparation**: Standard oyster mushroom cultivation on straw or supplemented sawdust substrates supports nematode-trapping activity in the colonized substrate.
