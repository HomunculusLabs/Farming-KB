# Tricholoma tigrinum

**Tricholoma tigrinum** is a mushroom species belonging to the genus Tricholoma, noted for its distinctive tiger-like cap pattern and its investigated medicinal properties. Found primarily in Asian forest ecosystems, it has been studied for its polysaccharide content and immunomodulatory potential, placing it among the growing number of Tricholoma species recognized for therapeutic value.

## Taxonomy and Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Tricholomataceae
- **Genus:** Tricholoma
- **Species:** T. tigrinum

The specific epithet "tigrinum" refers to the tiger-like (tigrine) pattern on the cap surface, which is a distinctive macroscopic feature of this species. It is related to other medicinally important Tricholoma species including [[tricholoma-matsutake]] and [[tricholoma-mongolicum]].

## Morphology

Tricholoma tigrinum is characterized by the following morphological features:

- **Cap:** Convex to plano-convex with an inrolled margin when young, becoming depressed at center with age. The cap surface is the most distinctive feature, displaying a pattern of darker scales or fibrils on a lighter background, giving a tiger-striped or mottled appearance. Cap color ranges from pale cream to tan with darker brown to reddish-brown fibrillose scales. Diameter ranges from 5 to 12 cm.
- **Gills:** Adnate to emarginate (notched), moderately crowded, white to cream. The gill edges may become slightly frayed or irregular with age.
- **Stipe:** Solid, stout, cylindrical, white to pale cream with a smooth or finely fibrillose surface. May develop a faint annular zone from a quickly disappearing partial veil. Length typically 5-10 cm, width 1-2.5 cm.
- **Flesh:** White, firm and thick, with a mild pleasant odor. The flesh may discolor slightly pinkish or brownish when cut or bruised.
- **Spore print:** White.
- **Spores:** Ellipsoidal to broadly ellipsoidal, smooth, thin-walled, non-amyloid. Spore dimensions approximately 5.5-7.5 × 4.5-6 μm.
- **Cystidia:** Clamp connections present at septa of hyphae.

The distinctive cap pattern makes T. tigrinum one of the more recognizable Tricholoma species in the field. However, some variability in cap ornamentation can occur depending on age and environmental conditions.

## Distribution and Habitat

T. tigrinum is distributed across forested regions of Asia, with documented occurrences in:

- China (particularly southwestern and central provinces)
- Japan
- Korean Peninsula
- Parts of the Himalayan region
- Possibly Southeast Asian montane forests

The species is typically found in coniferous or mixed forests, often in association with trees of the Pinaceae and Fagaceae families. It likely forms ectomycorrhizal associations with forest trees, a common ecological strategy within the genus Tricholoma. Fruiting occurs from late summer through autumn (August to November), with peak fruiting during the cool, moist conditions of early autumn.

## Chemical Composition

Chemical analysis of T. tigrinum has identified several bioactive constituents:

- **Polysaccharides:** The most significant bioactive fraction consists of water-soluble heteropolysaccharides containing predominantly glucose with smaller amounts of galactose, mannose, and arabinose. The polysaccharides feature beta-(1→3) and beta-(1→6) glucosidic linkages characteristic of immunomodulatory mushroom beta-glucans.
- **Proteins and glycoproteins:** Protein-bound polysaccharide complexes similar in nature to PSK from [[trametes-versicolor]] have been reported.
- **Triterpenoids:** Small quantities of triterpenoid compounds have been isolated, though at lower concentrations than in species like [[ganoderma-lucidum]].
- **Ergosterol and ergosterol peroxide:** These sterol compounds, common in basidiomycete fungi, have demonstrated anti-inflammatory and mild cytotoxic activities in vitro.
- **Phenolic compounds:** Various phenolic acids contribute to the mushroom's antioxidant capacity.
- **Organic acids:** Malic, fumaric, and other organic acids are present in the fruiting body.

## Medicinal Properties

### Immunomodulatory Activity

The polysaccharide fractions of T. tigrinum have been the primary focus of medicinal research. Reported immunomodulatory effects include:

- Activation of macrophages and enhancement of phagocytic activity
- Stimulation of T-lymphocyte proliferation and differentiation
- Modulation of cytokine profiles, including increased production of IL-2, IL-6, IL-12, and TNF-α
- Enhancement of NK cell-mediated cytotoxicity against target cells
- Activation of the complement system

These immunomodulatory effects are mediated through binding of beta-glucans to specific receptors on immune cells, including Dectin-1, complement receptor 3 (CR3), and toll-like receptors (TLRs). This mechanism is shared with other well-characterized mushroom immunomodulators such as [[lentinan]] and [[schizophyllan]].

### Anti-Tumor Effects

In experimental studies, T. tigrinum polysaccharide extracts have demonstrated anti-tumor activity:

- Significant inhibition of transplanted tumor growth in murine models (including S-180 sarcoma and Ehrlich ascites carcinoma)
- Prolongation of survival time in tumor-bearing animals
- Synergistic effects when combined with conventional chemotherapeutic agents
- Reduction of chemotherapy-induced immunosuppression when administered concurrently

The anti-tumor effects are primarily immune-mediated rather than resulting from direct cytotoxicity, consistent with the mechanism of action of other mushroom-derived polysaccharides.

### Anti-Inflammatory Activity

Extracts of T. tigrinum have shown anti-inflammatory effects in experimental models:

- Reduction of edema in carrageenan-induced inflammation models
- Inhibition of pro-inflammatory mediator release (NO, PGE2, TNF-α)
- Suppression of NF-κB signaling pathway activation
- These effects are attributed to both polysaccharide and triterpenoid components

### Antioxidant Activity

Methanolic and aqueous extracts demonstrate significant antioxidant capacity through multiple mechanisms:

- DPPH and ABTS radical scavenging
- Reducing power activity
- Metal chelating ability
- Inhibition of lipid peroxidation
- The antioxidant activity correlates with total phenolic content

## Traditional Use

T. tigrinum has been used in traditional medicine in parts of China and Japan, where it is valued as:

- A general health tonic and adaptogen
- An immune-supporting agent during illness or convalescence
- A nutritional food with perceived medicinal benefits
- An ingredient in traditional medicinal soups and decoctions

The traditional applications are consistent with the modern understanding of its immunomodulatory properties, though the extent of traditional documentation is less comprehensive than for better-known medicinal mushrooms in the Chinese pharmacopoeia.

## Cultivation

Commercial cultivation of T. tigrinum is not widely established, though laboratory-scale cultivation has been achieved. Key cultivation parameters include:

- Substrate: hardwood sawdust supplemented with wheat bran or rice bran (optimal supplementation 10-20%)
- Spawn: grain-based spawn (wheat, rye, or millet)
- Mycelial growth temperature: 22-28°C
- Fruiting temperature: 16-22°C with a temperature drop to initiate pinning
- Humidity: 85-95% relative humidity during fruiting
- Light: Indirect light sufficient for normal fruiting body development
- CO2: Adequate fresh air exchange during fruiting stage

The ectomycorrhizal nature of T. tigrinum may present challenges for axenic cultivation, as some mycorrhizal mushrooms are difficult to grow without their host plants. However, saprotrophic-like growth on supplemented substrates has been reported.

## Safety Profile

T. tigrinum is considered safe for consumption based on traditional use and preliminary toxicological data. Acute toxicity studies in animals have shown no significant adverse effects at therapeutic doses. However, caution is advised because the genus Tricholoma contains both edible and toxic species, and accurate identification by trained mycologists is essential before consumption.
