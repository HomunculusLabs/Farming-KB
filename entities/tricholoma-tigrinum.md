# Tricholoma tigrinum

**Tricholoma tigrinum** is a mushroom species belonging to the genus Tricholoma, noted for its distinctive tiger-like cap pattern and its investigated medicinal properties. Found primarily in Asian forest ecosystems, it has been studied for its polysaccharide content and immunomodulatory potential, placing it among the growing number of Tricholoma species recognized for therapeutic value.

## Taxonomy and Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Tricholomataceae
- **Genus:** Tricholoma
- **Species:** T. tigrinum

The specific epithet "tigrinum" refers to the tiger-like (tigrine) pattern on the cap surface, which is a distinctive macroscopic feature of this species. It is related to other medicinally important Tricholoma species including [[tricholoma-mongolicum]].

## Morphology

Tricholoma tigrinum is characterized by the following morphological features:

- **Cap:** Convex to plano-convex with an inrolled margin when young, becoming depressed at center with age. The cap surface is the most distinctive feature, displaying a pattern of darker scales or fibrils on a lighter background, giving a tiger-striped or mottled appearance. Cap color ranges from pale cream to tan with darker brown to reddish-brown fibrillose scales. Diameter ranges from 5 to 12 cm.
- **Gills:** Adnate to emarginate (notched), moderately crowded, white to cream. The gill edges may become slightly frayed or irregular with age.
- **Stipe:** Solid, stout, cylindrical, white to pale cream with a smooth or finely fibrillose surface. May develop a faint annular zone from a quickly disappearing partial veil. Length typically 5-10 cm, width 1-2.5 cm.
- **Flesh:** White, firm and thick, with a mild pleasant odor. The flesh may discolor slightly pinkish or brownish when cut or bruised.
- **Spore print:** White.
- **Spores:** Ellipsoidal to broadly ellipsoidal, smooth, thin-walled, non-amyloid. Spore dimensions approximately 5.5-7.5 × 4.5-6 μm.
- **Cystidia:** Clamp connections present at septa of hyphae.

The distinctive cap pattern makes T. tigrinum one of the more recognizable Tricholoma species in the field. However, some variability in cap ornamentation can occur depending on age and environmental conditions.

## Distribution and Habitat

T. tigrinum is distributed across forested regions of Asia, with documented occurrences in:

- China (particularly southwestern and central provinces)
- Japan
- Korean Peninsula
- Parts of the Himalayan region
- Possibly Southeast Asian montane forests

The species is typically found in coniferous or mixed forests, often in association with trees of the Pinaceae and Fagaceae families. It likely forms ectomycorrhizal associations with forest trees, a common ecological strategy within the genus Tricholoma. Fruiting occurs from late summer through autumn (August to November), with peak fruiting during the cool, moist conditions of early autumn.

## Chemical Composition

[[challenges-neurotropic-fungi-chemical-analysis-taxonomic-identification]] of T. tigrinum has identified several bioactive constituents:

- **Polysaccharides:** The most significant bioactive fraction consists of water-soluble heteropolysaccharides containing predominantly glucose with smaller amounts of galactose, mannose, and arabinose. The polysaccharides feature beta-(1→3) and beta-(1→6) glucosidic linkages characteristic of immunomodulatory mushroom beta-glucans.
- **Proteins and glycoproteins:** Protein-bound polysaccharide complexes similar in nature to PSK from [[ganoderma-lucidum]].
- **Ergosterol and ergosterol peroxide:** These sterol compounds, common in [[singh-basidiomycete-fungi-in-petroleum-hydrocarbon-cleanup]], have demonstrated anti-inflammatory and mild cytotoxic activities in vitro.
- **Phenolic compounds:** Various phenolic acids contribute to the mushroom's antioxidant capacity.
- **Organic acids:** Malic, fumaric, and other organic acids are present in the fruiting body.

## Medicinal Properties

### Immunomodulatory Activity

The polysaccharide fractions of T. tigrinum have been the primary focus of medicinal research. Reported immunomodulatory effects include:

- Activation of macrophages and enhancement of phagocytic activity
- Stimulation of T-lymphocyte proliferation and differentiation
- Modulation of cytokine profiles, including [[fukuoka-textdoc-increased-production-not-increased-yields]] of IL-2, IL-6, IL-12, and TNF-α
- Enhancement of NK cell-mediated cytotoxicity against target cells
- Activation of the complement system

These immunomodulatory effects are mediated through binding of beta-glucans to specific receptors on immune cells, including Dectin-1, complement receptor 3 (CR3), and toll-like receptors (TLRs). This mechanism is shared with other well-characterized mushroom immunomodulators such as [[schizophyllan]].

### Anti-Tumor Effects

In experimental studies, T. tigrinum polysaccharide extracts have demonstrated anti-tumor activity:

- Significant inhibition of transplanted tumor growth in murine models (including S-180 sarcoma and Ehrlich ascites carcinoma)
- Prolongation of survival time in tumor-bearing animals
- Synergistic effects when combined with conventional chemotherapeutic agents
- Reduction of chemotherapy-induced immunosuppression when administered concurrently

The anti-tumor effects are primarily immune-mediated rather than resulting from direct cytotoxicity, consistent with the mechanism of action of other mushroom-derived polysaccharides.
