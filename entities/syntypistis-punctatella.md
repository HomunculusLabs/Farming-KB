## Description

**Syntypistis punctatella**, the beech caterpillar, is a species of moth in the family Notodontidae whose larvae feed on Japanese beech (Fagus crenata). It is notable for its periodic population fluctuations regulated by fungal pathogens (particularly [[cordyceps-militaris]]), avian predators, parasitoids, and abiotic factors.

## Key Facts

- Kamata (2000) studied population regulation by fungal pathogens, predators, parasitoids, and abiotic factors
- Periodic population fluctuations driven by delayed density-dependent regulators
- The entomopathogenic fungus Cordyceps militaris identified as the prime suspect for initiating population decline
- Disease-induced decline starts before caterpillars reach outbreak densities
- Delayed induced defensive response (DIR) of beech trees was less closely related to population changes than fungal disease

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

S. punctatella is a model organism for understanding how [[entomopathogenic-fungi]] regulate insect herbivore populations in forest ecosystems. The interaction with C. militaris demonstrates the ecological significance of Cordyceps species as natural insect biocontrol agents. For cultivation, this system illustrates the potential for using entomopathogenic fungi in [[integrated-pest-management]] and the importance of density-dependent thresholds in triggering epizootic events.

## Ecological Role

As a periodic outbreak species in Japanese beech forests, S. punctatella drives temporal variation in herbivory pressure on beech trees. Its population cycles, regulated partly by fungal pathogens, create periodic pulses of defoliation that influence forest structure and nutrient cycling.
