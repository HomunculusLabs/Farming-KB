# MDEA

MDEA (3,4-methylenedioxy-N-ethylamphetamine), also known as Eve or MDE, is a synthetic entactogen and empathogen closely related to MDMA (Ecstasy). It is the N-ethyl homologue of MDMA, differing by a single ethyl group substitution on the amine nitrogen. Shulgin documented MDEA in PIHKAL and considered it a notable variant that offered a somewhat gentler, more relaxed experience compared to MDMA's energetic intensity.

## Chemistry

MDEA is an N-substituted amphetamine featuring the characteristic methylenedioxy bridge across positions 3 and 4 of the phenyl ring. The key structural difference from MDMA is the N-ethyl group (C₂H₅) replacing MDMA's N-methyl group (CH₃). Molecular formula: C₁₂H₁₇NO₂; molar mass: 207.27 g/mol.

This seemingly minor change produces meaningful pharmacological differences. The larger N-ethyl group reduces reuptake inhibition potency for serotonin while maintaining dopaminergic and noradrenergic activity, shifting the experiential profile away from MDMA's serotonergic-dominant character.

## Pharmacology

- **Typical dose**: 100–200 mg orally (threshold: ~50 mg)
- **Duration**: 3–6 hours
- **Onset**: 30–60 minutes
- **Mechanism**: Serotonin, dopamine, and norepinephrine release/reuptake inhibition; less serotonergic than MDMA

| Property | MDEA | MDMA |
|---|---|---|
| Serotonin release | Moderate | Strong |
| Dopamine release | Moderate | Moderate |
| Empathogenic quality | Gentle, warm | Intense, euphoric |
| Stimulation | Mild-moderate | Moderate-strong |
| Visual effects | More present | Less present |
| After-effects | Milder | More pronounced |

## Effects

MDEA produces effects that sit between MDMA and classical psychedelics:

- **Emotional**: Warmth, emotional openness, reduced defensiveness (less intense than MDMA)
- **Social**: Enhanced sociability, easier conversation, mild disinhibition
- **Sensory**: Increased tactile sensitivity, enhanced music appreciation
- **Visual**: More visual distortion than MDMA, including color enhancement and mild patterning
- **Physical**: Mild stimulation, reduced appetite, jaw tension (less than MDMA)

Users frequently describe MDEA as more "laid-back" and "stoning" compared to MDMA's energetic, emotionally intense character. It is often preferred by those who find MDMA overstimulating or who want a gentler entactogenic experience.

## History

MDEA first appeared in recreational contexts in the mid-1980s, shortly after MDMA began gaining popularity. It was often sold as MDMA or mixed with MDMA in pressed tablets — a practice that continues to this day. Shulgin was aware of the compound's recreational use and noted in PIHKAL that it represented a distinct pharmacological experience warranting its own characterization.

The compound saw significant use in European rave scenes in the 1990s and early 2000s, particularly in Germany and the Netherlands, where it was sometimes preferred over MDMA for its milder comedown.

## Risks & Harm Reduction

MDEA carries similar risks to other substituted amphetamines:
- Hyperthermia risk (lower than MDMA but still present)
- Cardiovascular strain, hypertension
- Serotonin depletion with frequent use
- Neurotoxicity potential (less studied than MDMA, assumed similar)
- Adulteration risk when purchased illicitly

The gentler comedown compared to MDMA does not eliminate the risk of serotonin syndrome when combined with other serotonergic substances (MAOIs, SSRIs, other entactogens).

## Legal Status

- **USA**: Schedule I (as a positional isomer of MDMA under the Federal Analogue Act)
- **UN**: Schedule I of the 1971 Convention
- **Germany**: Anlage I (controlled substance)
- **UK**: Class A
