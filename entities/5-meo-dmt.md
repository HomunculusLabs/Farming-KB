# 5-MeO-DMT

**5-Methoxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine** is a powerful serotonergic psychedelic tryptamine found naturally in numerous plant species, amphibian secretions (Bufo alvarius / Sonoran Desert toad), and trace amounts in mammalian tissues. It is among the most potent naturally occurring psychedelics, with active doses in the 5–20 mg range when vaporized.





## Chemistry

- **IUPAC**: 5-Methoxy-N,N-dimethyl-1H-indole-3-ethanamine
- **Molecular formula**: C₁₃H₁₈N₂O
- **Molecular weight**: 218.30 g/mol
- **CAS**: 1019-45-0
- Belongs to the [[dimethyltryptamine]] family; the 5-methoxy substitution dramatically increases potency and alters the pharmacological profile compared to DMT
- Typically encountered as a white crystalline freebase or as a water-soluble salt (fumarate, hydrochloride)





## Pharmacology

- Primarily acts as a **full agonist at 5-HT₂A receptors** with significant affinity for 5-HT₁A, 5-HT₂C, and other serotonin receptor subtypes
- Unlike DMT, 5-MeO-DMT produces a markedly less visual experience and is characterized by overwhelming somatic and ego-dissolution effects
- Rapid onset when vaporized (seconds), peak within 1–3 minutes, duration 15–30 minutes
- Subjective effects described as "the void," "white light," or complete dissolution of self-world boundaries with minimal hallucinatory imagery
- No significant MAO inhibition of its own; oral activity requires MAOI co-administration (ayahuasca analogs)





## Natural Occurrence

- **Plant sources**: Virola species (Amazon basin), Dictyoloma incanescens, [[anadenanthera-peregrina]] (trace), [[diplopterys-cabrerana]]
- **Animal source**: Bufotoxin secretions of Incilius alvarius (Sonoran Desert toad) — the primary natural reservoir, containing up to 15% 5-MeO-DMT by dry weight in dried venom
- Present in trace amounts in human cerebrospinal fluid, pineal gland, and urine, though the functional significance remains debated





## Cultural & Historical Use

- Central to the **Sébétán** snuff traditions of the western Amazon, prepared from Virola resin
- Traditionally combined with plant-derived MAOIs for oral activity
- Contemporary use includes vaporized toad secretion and synthetic preparations; has gained prominence in clinical research for treatment of depression, anxiety, and substance use disorders
- Retiros and ceremonial use by the **Río Napo** and **Putumayo** peoples documented since the 1950s





## Toxicology & Safety

- LD₅₀ in rodents is extremely high relative to active doses (wide therapeutic index)
- Primary risks are psychological: overwhelming ego dissolution, panic, hypertensive crisis (especially with MAOIs)
- Contraindicated with SSRIs, MAOIs, and serotonergic medications due to serotonin syndrome risk
- No evidence of physical dependence; tolerance builds rapidly and dissipates within days





## Legal Status

- Schedule I in the United States; controlled internationally under the UN Convention on Psychotropic Substances (1971)
- Legal status varies widely; decriminalized in some jurisdictions for religious/spiritual use
- Many countries have scheduled 5-MeO-DMT explicitly or through tryptamine analogue acts
