# Synchytrium

## Description

*Synchytrium* is a large genus of obligately biotrophic chytrid fungi
(Chytridiales) commonly associated with gall formation on a remarkably broad
range of host organisms, including algae, mosses, ferns, and flowering plants.
With approximately 120 recognized species, *Synchytrium* is one of the most
species-rich genera in the Chytridiomycota and is distributed worldwide across
tropical, temperate, and arctic regions. Species are best known for inducing
conspicuous galls on the leaves, petioles, flower buds, and stems of their
hosts. The most economically important species, *S. endobioticum*, causes
potato wart disease and is an internationally regulated quarantine pathogen.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Chytridiomycota
- **Class:** Chytridiomycetes
- **Order:** Chytridiales
- **Family:** Synchytriaceae
- **Genus:** *Synchytrium*

## Key Facts

- **Species Diversity:** Approximately 120 species are recognized worldwide.
  Geographic distribution includes 106 species from North America, 88 from
  Asia, 56 from Europe, 24 from South America, 16 from Africa, 8 from Australia
  and New Zealand, and 5 from Central America (Karling 1964). The genus occurs
  from arctic to tropical regions.

- **Host Range:** Extraordinarily broad. More than 1,350 plant species in over
  800 genera and 165 families have been reported as hosts. Some species occur
  on only one host genus; others are limited to a single host family. *S.
  macrosporum* has been shown to infect more than 1,300 host species under
  greenhouse conditions, making it one of the most polyphagous fungal pathogens
  known.

- **Gall Formation:** Infected flowering plants develop galls on leaves,
  petioles, flower buds, and stems. Two species (*S. fragariae* and *S.
  endobioticum*) infect roots and underground stems. Galls range from scattered
  and small to large and clustered or compound; some are brightly colored.
  Galls may form by enlargement of a single infected host cell or by
  proliferation of surrounding cells. The infected host organ may become
  noticeably hypertrophied and deformed.

- **Notable Species - *S. endobioticum*:** Causes potato wart disease, one of
  the most serious quarantine pests of potato worldwide. The disease produces
  dark, warty, cauliflower-like galls on potato tubers and stolons. The
  pathogen is internationally regulated and subject to strict phytosanitary
  measures in most potato-growing countries.

- **Life Cycle:** Resting sporangia (winter spores) are thick-walled and
  persistent in soil or plant debris. Upon germination, they release motile
  zoospores that swim to and infect host cells. Inside the host, the fungus
  develops into a multinucleate thallus that eventually produces new sporangia.
  Infected host cells enlarge dramatically.

- **Algal Hosts:** Species infecting algae cause enlargement and elongation of
  the infected host cell, rather than the multicellular galls typical of
  flowering plant infections. This demonstrates the range of host responses
  within a single fungal genus.

- **Identification:** Species identification is based primarily on host
  determination (Karling 1964). Voucher specimens should consist of dried plant
  material including galls or deformed host tissues containing resting
  sporangia. *Synchytrium* species cannot be grown in vitro, consistent with
  their obligately biotrophic lifestyle.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Quarantine Pathogen:** *S. endobioticum* (potato wart) is one of the most
  important regulated plant pathogens globally. Strict phytosanitary measures,
  including soil movement restrictions and resistant cultivar requirements, are
  enforced in many countries. Eradication of contaminated fields can take
  decades due to the extreme longevity of resting sporangia in soil.

- **Chytrid Biology:** *Synchytrium* represents one of the most morphologically
  complex and ecologically diverse genera of chytrid fungi. Unlike the
  better-known *Batrachochytrium* (amphibian chytrid), *Synchytrium* species
  are exclusively plant-associated. The genus provides insight into the
  evolutionary diversity of the earliest-diverging fungal lineages.

- **Gall-Forming Fungi:** The genus exemplifies the convergent evolution of gall
  formation across distantly related fungal groups (Chytridiomycota,
  Basidiomycota, Ascomycota, Oomycota). Comparative studies of *Synchytrium*
  galls with those of rust fungi and *Taphrina* provide insights into the
  mechanisms of host cell manipulation and the diverse strategies fungi use to
  modify plant growth.

- **Host Specificity Evolution:** The broad host range of some *Synchytrium*
  species contrasts with the narrow host specificity typical of most obligate
  plant parasites, raising interesting questions about the evolution and
  maintenance of host specificity in the Chytridiomycota.
