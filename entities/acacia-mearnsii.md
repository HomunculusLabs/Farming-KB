# Acacia mearnsii

Acacia mearnsii, commonly known as [[fukuoka-black-wattle-fertilizer-tree-method]], green wattle, or
Australian blackwood (not to be confused with A. melanoxylon), is a
fast-growing nitrogen-fixing tree in the family Fabaceae, subfamily
Mimosoideae.

Native to southeastern Australia, it has become one of the most
commercially significant wattle species worldwide, extensively planted
for tannin production, timber, and firewood.

Simultaneously, A. mearnsii is one of the most problematic invasive
species in several [[guzman-allen-gartz-africa-southern-hemisphere-underexplored-neurotropic-fungi]] countries, creating complex
management challenges that illustrate the dual nature of aggressive
nitrogen-fixing pioneer species.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Division:** Magnoliophyta
- **Class:** Magnoliopsida
- **Order:** Fabales
- **Family:** Fabaceae
- **Subfamily:** Caesalpinioideae (formerly Mimosoideae)
- **Genus:** Acacia (sensu lato; Vachellia in some classifications)
- **Species:** A. mearnsii De Wild.
- **Synonyms:**
  - Acacia mollissima Willd. (widely used historically)
  - Acacia decurrens var. mollis auct. non (Aiton) Benth.
  - Racosperma mearnsii (De Wild.) Pedley

## Morphology

- **Growth habit:** Evergreen tree, typically 8–15 m tall, occasionally
  to 20 m; crown spreading, open, with graceful weeping branchlets.
- **Bark:** Dark brown to nearly black, rough and deeply fissured on
  older trunks; inner bark rich in tannins (20–40% on dry weight basis).
- **Leaves:** Bipinnate, 8–15 cm long, with 8–20 pairs of pinnae, each
  pinna bearing 20–50 pairs of small, close-set leaflets (1.5–3 mm
  long, 0.5–1 mm wide) giving a fine, feathery appearance.
- **Phyllodes:** Absent in A. mearnsii (unlike many Australian Acacia
  spp. that transition from true leaves to phyllodes).
- **Flowers:** Pale yellow to creamy-white, in globular flower heads
  (3–5 mm diameter) arranged in axillary or terminal panicles; sweetly
  scented, blooming October–December in native range.
- **Pods:** Linear, flat, 5–10 cm long, 5–8 mm wide, dark brown to
  black at maturity, dehiscent along both sutures; seeds 3–5 per pod.
- **Seeds:** Oval, 3–4 mm long, black, with a hard seed coat and a
  conspicuous aril (strophiole) that attracts ants for myrmecochorous
  dispersal.
- **Root system:** Deep taproot (2–3 m) with extensive lateral roots
  and prolific nitrogen-fixing root nodules containing Bradyrhizobium
  spp.

## Distribution and Habitat

Acacia mearnsii is native to southeastern Australia, from southern
Queensland through New South Wales, Victoria, and eastern South
Australia, at 100–800 m elevation.

In its native range it grows in open forests on a variety of soil types,
often in areas with 600–1,200 mm annual rainfall.

The species has been widely planted outside its native range: South
Africa (largest commercial plantations, ~130,000 ha), Brazil, Kenya,
Tanzania, India, Sri Lanka, Indonesia, and parts of southern Europe.

It has become a severe invasive in South Africa, New Zealand, Indonesia,
Brazil, and parts of eastern Africa, where it colonizes riparian areas,
grasslands, and disturbed sites with alarming speed.
