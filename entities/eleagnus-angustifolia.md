# Elaeagnus angustifolia

**Type:** Species (Tree/Shrub)
**Category:** Nitrogen-fixing pioneer tree
**Family:** Elaeagnaceae
**Also known as:** Russian olive, Bohemian olive, oleaster, trebizond
date

## Overview
Elaeagnus angustifolia is a deciduous small tree or large shrub
reaching up to 12 m tall, native to the Mediterranean region through
southwest Asia. It is an exceptionally tough, cold-hardy plant that
forms a symbiotic relationship with Frankia spp. nitrogen-fixing
bacteria, making it a valuable pioneer species for land restoration
and [[permaculture]] systems in challenging environments. The species has
become naturalised well beyond its native range due to its
adaptability.

## Taxonomy
- **Order:** Rosales
- **Family:** Elaeagnaceae
- **Genus:** Elaeagnus
- **Species:** E. angustifolia
- **Common relatives:** E. umbellata (autumn olive), E. multiflora
  (cherry elaeagnus), E. pungens (silverberry), Hippophae
  rhamnoides (sea buckthorn)

## Key Details
- **Growth form:** Deciduous tree to 12 m, about 2 m wide; can be
  quite shrubby depending on conditions and management
- **Cold tolerance:** Extremely hardy to -34C, making it one of the
  most cold-tolerant nitrogen-fixing trees
- **Salt tolerance:** High; grows right to the sea in France and
  thrives in coastal exposure
- **Drought tolerance:** High; prefers dry, well-drained conditions
- **Nitrogen fixation:** Symbiotic relationship with Frankia spp.
  actinorhizal soil microbes, similar to alder (Alnus spp.)
- **Soil preference:** Dry, well-drained, alkaline soils in a
  sunny, open position
- **Fruit:** Yellow, sweet, mealy berries eaten fresh or made into
  pies, sauces, jams, jellies, sherbets, and wines; produced
  abundantly in late summer
- **Poultry forage:** Berries provide important forage in late
  summer when other food sources may be scarce
- **Windbreak function:** Excellent windbreak and hedge species due
  to dense branching and drought tolerance
- **Wildlife value:** Provides bird habitat and food; thorny
  branches offer nesting protection
- **Erosion control:** Used for erosion control on degraded and
  marginal land
- **Companion species:** Occurs naturally with Tamarix gallica in
  Mediterranean coastal zones
- **Drawback:** Has thorns, which can be problematic in some
  settings; can become invasive outside its native range

## Cultivation
Extremely easy to establish in poor, dry, alkaline soils where few
other trees will grow. Prefers full sun and open positions. Can be
grown as a hedge or windbreak with appropriate spacing. Propagation
is by seed, cuttings, or suckers. Once established, requires minimal
maintenance and no irrigation. One of the best trees for hostile
site conditions including saline, alkaline, and drought-prone soils.

## Relevance
Russian olive is one of the most versatile nitrogen-fixing trees for
cold, dry, and saline environments. Its extreme cold hardiness (-34C),
drought tolerance, and ability to fix nitrogen make it invaluable as
a pioneer species for land restoration, windbreak establishment, and
soil improvement in marginal landscapes. The edible berries add a
food-production dimension to its soil-building capabilities, making
it a multi-functional element for permaculture designs in continental
and steppe climates.
