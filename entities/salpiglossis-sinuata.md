## Overview

Salpiglossis sinuata is a species of flowering annual plant in the family Solanaceae, native to Chile. It is widely known as painted tongue, a reference to the vividly patterned, trumpet-shaped flowers that characterize the species.

The plant is cultivated as a summer-flowering ornamental for its showy, velvety blossoms that display intricate veining and color combinations. Flower colors range from deep violet and magenta to bronze, yellow, and white.

Salpiglossis sinuata is the most commonly grown species in the genus Salpiglossis, which contains approximately 2–3 species native to [[mckenna-club-des-hachischins-and-nineteenth-century-literary-hashish]].

The species was introduced to English gardens around 1820 and quickly became popular for its exotic appearance. Victorian gardeners prized it for bedding schemes and greenhouse displays.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Salpiglossis sinuata was first described by Hipólito Ruiz López and José Antonio Pavón Jiménez in 1794, based on specimens collected during their botanical expedition to Chile and Peru.

The genus name Salpiglossis derives from Greek words meaning "tube" and "tongue," referring to the funnel-shaped corolla. The specific epithet "sinuata" describes the deeply lobed or wavy leaf margins.

The combination of tubular corolla and sinuate leaf margin makes this species one of the more morphologically distinctive members of the Chilean Solanaceae flora.

Salpiglossis is placed in the tribe Cestreae of subfamily Cestroideae within Solanaceae. Molecular phylogenetic studies suggest a close relationship with the genera Cestrum and Vestia.

The plant is an annual or short-lived perennial growing 30–90 cm tall. The stems are erect, branching, and covered with sticky glandular trichomes that give the foliage a slightly clammy texture.

Plant height varies considerably among cultivars, with compact forms reaching only 30 cm and taller garden selections exceeding 90 cm. The branching pattern is typically open and airy.

Leaves are alternate, lanceolate to elliptic, measuring 5–12 cm long and 1–4 cm wide. The margins are irregularly sinuate or lobed, and the surface is glandular-pubescent with a characteristic sticky feel.

Flowers are borne in loose terminal racemes or panicles, each flower 4–6 cm long with a funnel-shaped corolla. The corolla tube is narrow at the base and expands into a broadly campanulate limb.

The flower coloration is exceptionally variable, often featuring a contrasting venation pattern on the petal surface. This veining creates a stained-glass effect that distinguishes Salpiglossis from other ornamental Solanaceae.

Anthocyanin pigments responsible for the flower colors include delphinidin, petunidin, and malvidin derivatives. The veining pattern results from differential pigment accumulation in specific cell layers of the corolla.

Each flower produces abundant nectar at the base of the corolla tube, attracting pollinating insects. The blooming period extends from mid-summer to autumn [[holzer-climate-zone-microclimate-creation]].

Naturalized populations have been reported in parts of Argentina, the Falkland Islands, and southeastern Australia. These populations are typically associated with agricultural or garden escape.

The plant prefers full sun and well-drained, moderately fertile soils. In cultivation, it tolerates a range of soil pH levels from slightly acidic to slightly alkaline.
