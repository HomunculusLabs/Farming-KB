## Overview

Salpiglossis sinuata is a species of flowering annual plant in the family Solanaceae, native to Chile. It is widely known as painted tongue, a reference to the vividly patterned, trumpet-shaped flowers that characterize the species.

The plant is cultivated as a summer-flowering ornamental for its showy, velvety blossoms that display intricate veining and color combinations. Flower colors range from deep violet and magenta to bronze, yellow, and white.

Salpiglossis sinuata is the most commonly grown species in the genus Salpiglossis, which contains approximately 2–3 species native to South America. It has been in cultivation in European gardens since the early nineteenth century.

The species was introduced to English gardens around 1820 and quickly became popular for its exotic appearance. Victorian gardeners prized it for bedding schemes and greenhouse displays.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Salpiglossis sinuata was first described by Hipólito Ruiz López and José Antonio Pavón Jiménez in 1794, based on specimens collected during their botanical expedition to Chile and Peru.

The genus name Salpiglossis derives from Greek words meaning "tube" and "tongue," referring to the funnel-shaped corolla. The specific epithet "sinuata" describes the deeply lobed or wavy leaf margins.

The combination of tubular corolla and sinuate leaf margin makes this species one of the more morphologically distinctive members of the Chilean Solanaceae flora.

Salpiglossis is placed in the tribe Cestreae of subfamily Cestroideae within Solanaceae. Molecular phylogenetic studies suggest a close relationship with the genera Cestrum and Vestia.

The plant is an annual or short-lived perennial growing 30–90 cm tall. The stems are erect, branching, and covered with sticky glandular trichomes that give the foliage a slightly clammy texture.

Plant height varies considerably among cultivars, with compact forms reaching only 30 cm and taller garden selections exceeding 90 cm. The branching pattern is typically open and airy.

Leaves are alternate, lanceolate to elliptic, measuring 5–12 cm long and 1–4 cm wide. The margins are irregularly sinuate or lobed, and the surface is glandular-pubescent with a characteristic sticky feel.

Flowers are borne in loose terminal racemes or panicles, each flower 4–6 cm long with a funnel-shaped corolla. The corolla tube is narrow at the base and expands into a broadly campanulate limb.

The flower coloration is exceptionally variable, often featuring a contrasting venation pattern on the petal surface. This veining creates a stained-glass effect that distinguishes Salpiglossis from other ornamental Solanaceae.

Anthocyanin pigments responsible for the flower colors include delphinidin, petunidin, and malvidin derivatives. The veining pattern results from differential pigment accumulation in specific cell layers of the corolla.

Each flower produces abundant nectar at the base of the corolla tube, attracting pollinating insects. The blooming period extends from mid-summer to autumn in temperate climates.

## Distribution and Habitat

Salpiglossis sinuata is native to central and southern Chile, where it occurs in the Valparaíso, Metropolitana, and Biobío regions. It grows in open, disturbed habitats including roadsides, fields, and rocky hillsides.

The native elevation range extends from near sea level to approximately 2,000 meters in the Andean foothills. The species is adapted to Mediterranean-type climate with dry summers and wet winters.

Mean annual temperature in native habitats ranges from 12–18°C, with summer maxima reaching 30°C. The plant is adapted to the cool, dry summers characteristic of central Chile's Mediterranean climate zone.

Naturalized populations have been reported in parts of Argentina, the Falkland Islands, and southeastern Australia. These populations are typically associated with agricultural or garden escape.

The plant prefers full sun and well-drained, moderately fertile soils. In cultivation, it tolerates a range of soil pH levels from slightly acidic to slightly alkaline.

## Cultivation

Salpiglossis sinuata is typically grown as a half-hardy annual in temperate climates. Seeds are sown indoors 8–10 weeks before the last expected frost, as the seedlings are sensitive to cold temperatures.

Germination occurs within 2–3 weeks at 18–21°C with light exposure. Seedlings are transplanted outdoors after all danger of frost has passed, spaced 25–30 cm apart.

Seeds are very fine and should be sown on the surface of the germination medium without covering, as they require light for germination. A thin layer of vermiculite helps maintain humidity around the seeds.

The plant performs best in full sun with consistent moisture during the growing season. It is moderately drought-tolerant once established but produces the best flowers with regular watering.

Deadheading spent flowers encourages continued blooming through the season. Pinching back young plants promotes bushier growth and more flower-bearing stems.

Pest problems include aphids, thrips, and spider mites. The sticky glandular foliage provides some natural resistance to herbivory by larger insects and mammalian browsers.

Botrytis gray mold can affect plants in humid conditions or when air circulation is poor. Fusarium wilt has been occasionally reported in commercial greenhouse production.

Fertilization with a liquid feed every 2–3 weeks during the growing season promotes vigorous flowering. Excessive nitrogen should be avoided as it promotes foliage at the expense of blooms.

## Ornamental Uses and Varieties

Salpiglossis sinuata is primarily valued as a bedding plant and container specimen for its vibrant, patterned flowers. It is used in mixed borders, cottage gardens, and cutting gardens.

In container plantings, the trailing habit of some cultivars makes them suitable for hanging baskets and window boxes. Combined with other warm-season annuals, they provide mid-summer color when spring-blooming plants have faded.

The flowers are moderately long-lasting as cut flowers, typically persisting 5–7 days in a vase. The unusual veining pattern makes them popular in floral arrangements and boutonnieres.

Stem length in cutting cultivars typically ranges from 40–60 cm, making them suitable for mid-height arrangements. Pre-treatment with floral preservatives extends vase life by 1–2 days.

Named cultivar series include the 'Kew Blue' strain with deep violet-blue flowers, and the 'Bolero' series featuring a broader color range. Many modern cultivars are F1 hybrids selected for uniformity and compact habit.

The species has contributed to ornamental breeding programs within Solanaceae. Its unique flower pigmentation patterns have been studied as a model for understanding anthocyanin transport and deposition in petal tissues.

Genetic studies have identified key transcription factors regulating the spatial distribution of anthocyanins in the corolla. These findings have implications for ornamental plant breeding across the family.

In its native Chile, Salpiglossis sinuata is occasionally found growing wild in agricultural margins and along roadsides. It is not considered threatened, though habitat fragmentation may affect local populations.
