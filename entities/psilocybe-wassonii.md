# Psilocybe wassonii

## Taxonomy

- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Hymenogastraceae (formerly Strophariaceae)
- **Genus:** Psilocybe
- **Species:** P. wassonii
- **Authority:** Heim (1958)
- **Synonyms:** Sometimes treated as a variety or synonym within the [[psilocybe-mexicana]] complex

## Description

### Cap (Pileus)
- 0.5 to 2.5 cm in diameter
- Conic to campanulate when young, expanding to convex or broadly convex with maturity
- Surface smooth, viscid when moist due to a distinct gelatinous pellicle
- Coloration deep brown to reddish brown when hydrated, fading to yellowish brown or pale ochre when dry
- Hygrophanous with pronounced color change between moist and dry states
- Often retains a small umbo or papilla even when fully expanded
- Margin sometimes slightly appendiculate with veil remnants

### Gills (Lamellae)
- Adnate to sinuate or subdecurrent
- Close to crowded
- Grayish brown when immature, becoming dark purple brown to nearly black with maturity
- Edges often paler, whitish to grayish

### Stipe (Stem)
- 3 to 8 cm long, 1 to 3 mm thick
- Slender, equal or slightly enlarged at the base
- Hollow
- Surface covered in fine silky fibrils, whitish to pale brown
- Prominent bluing reaction when bruised or cut, one of the more strongly bluing species in the mexicana complex
- Partial veil thin and ephemeral, sometimes leaving a faint annular zone

### Spores
- Spore print dark purple brown to blackish purple
- Spores ellipsoid to broadly ellipsoid
- 8 to 10 × 5.5 to 7 µm
- Wall moderately thick, with a visible germ pore
- Basidia predominantly 4 spored
- Pleurocystidia and cheilocystidia ventricose to flask shaped

## Distribution and Habitat

- Endemic to the highlands of central and southern Mexico
- Primarily found in the states of Oaxaca, Puebla, and surrounding regions
- Grows in subtropical and montane cloud forests
- Found in grassy areas, along trails, and in disturbed ground within forested regions
- Often associated with rich humic soils and decaying plant material
- Fruits during the rainy season, typically June through October
- Elevation range approximately 1500 to 2500 meters
- Prefers shaded, humid microenvironments

## Chemistry and Psychoactivity

- Contains [[psilocybin]] and [[psilocin]]
- Considered moderately potent
- Part of the [[psilocybe-mexicana]] species complex, sharing similar alkaloid profiles
- The strong bluing reaction suggests relatively high concentrations of psilocin
- Potency varies with altitude, substrate, and environmental conditions
- Traditional preparations involve fresh consumption

## Cultural Significance

- Named in honor of R. Gordon Wasson, the American banker and amateur mycologist who, along with his wife Valentina Pavlovna Wasson, brought the knowledge of psychoactive mushrooms to Western attention
- The Wassons participated in a velada (mushroom ceremony) with Mazatec curandera María Sabina in 1955, an event that launched the modern psychedelic movement
- Part of the traditional Mazatec mushroom pharmacopeia used in healing ceremonies and divination
- María Sabina used multiple species interchangeably in her ceremonies, including P. wassonii, [[psilocybe-caerulescens-entity]], and [[psilocybe-mexicana]]
- The Wasson expedition and subsequent publications by Roger Heim and Albert Hofmann led to the identification and synthesis of [[psilocybin]]

## Identification Notes

- Distinguished from [[psilocybe-mexicana]] by its more robust size, more strongly viscid cap, and more intense bluing reaction
- The gelatinous pellicle on the cap is more prominent than in related species
- Microscopic features including spore size and cystidia morphology are needed for definitive separation from closely related taxa
- The combination of highland Mexican distribution, strong bluing, and viscid cap aid field identification
- The species is part of a complex that has undergone taxonomic revision, and some authorities consider the distinctions between P. wassonii, P. mexicana, and related taxa to be subtle

## Similar Species

- [[psilocybe-mexicana]] — closely related and sympatric; generally smaller and less viscid
- [[psilocybe-caerulescens-entity]] — larger species found in similar habitat but with different cap coloration
- [[psilocybe-hoogshagenii]] — similar distribution but with different microscopic features

## Etymology

Named after R. Gordon Wasson (1898 to 1986), the vice president of J.P. Morgan and Co. whose fascination with mushrooms led to the 1957 Life magazine article "Seeking the Magic Mushroom" that introduced psilocybin mushrooms to the English speaking world.

## Ecology and Natural History

- Saprotrophic fungus of highland Mexican forests, decomposing organic matter in cloud forest soils
- Mycelium develops in humus rich substrates during the rainy season
- Contributes to fungal biodiversity in one of the world's most biodiverse mycological regions
- Fruitbody formation is triggered by seasonal rains and elevated humidity
- The species is part of a rich assemblage of Psilocybe species in the region, with multiple species often fruiting simultaneously

## Taxonomic Notes

- The taxonomic status of P. wassonii within the [[psilocybe-mexicana]] complex has been debated
- Some molecular phylogenetic studies suggest it may merit species rank based on genetic divergence
- The morphological characters used to separate it from [[psilocybe-mexicana]] are subtle and may reflect environmental variation
- Roger Heim originally described the species based on specimens collected during the Wasson expeditions
- Further study with modern genomic tools would help resolve its systematic position

## Conservation

- Wild populations may be threatened by habitat loss in Mexican highland forests
- Climate change affecting cloud forest ecosystems may impact this species
- Traditional knowledge of use is at risk as younger generations move away from indigenous practices

## Legal Status

- Protected under Mexican federal law alongside other psilocybin containing mushrooms
- Indigenous communities retain traditional use rights under certain circumstances
- Classified as a controlled substance in most other jurisdictions worldwide
