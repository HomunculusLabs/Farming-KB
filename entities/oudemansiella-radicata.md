# Oudemansiella radicata

## Overview

Oudemansiella radicata, the rootlet shank, is a saprotrophic basidiomycete
in the family Physalacriaceae, distinguished by its deeply rooting stipe,
viscid brown cap, and preference for decaying hardwood stumps and buried roots.
Found across temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere, it is edible when
thoroughly cooked and produces oudemansins — antifungal strobilurin-related
compounds that have served as lead structures for synthetic agricultural fungicides.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Physalacriaceae
- **Genus:** Oudemansiella
- **Species:** O. radicata (Relhan) Singer

**Synonyms:** Agaricus radicatus Relhan (1786) — basionym;
Collybia radicata (Relhan) Quél. (1872); Gymnopus radicatus (Relhan) Gray.

**Etymology:** The genus honors Dutch mycologist Cornelis Anton Jan Oudemans.
The epithet *radicata* refers to the characteristically long rooting stipe.

## Morphology

### Cap

- **Diameter:** 3–10 cm, convex to plano-convex with a low umbo
- **Surface:** Hygrophanous, viscid to glutinous when wet, silky-shiny when dry
- **Color:** Pale tan to cinnamon-brown, darker at center
- **Margin:** Inrolled when young, becoming straight; often irregular with age

### Gills

- **Attachment:** Adnate to sinuate, occasionally subdecurrent
- **Spacing:** Moderately crowded, with lamellulae of mixed lengths
- **Color:** White to cream throughout development

### Stipe

- **Length:** 5–15 cm total (often 5–10 cm below ground surface)
- **Thickness:** 4–10 mm, white to pale cream
- **Base:** Long tapering rooting stipe penetrating deeply into buried wood
- **Ring:** Thin, membranous, superior, often evanescent
- **Context:** Firm, white, unchanging when cut

### Spores

- **Shape:** Broadly ellipsoid to amygdaliform
- **Size:** 12–18 × 8–11 µm
- **Print:** White; smooth, non-amyloid, acyanophilous

## Distribution and Habitat

- **Range:** Widely distributed across Europe, [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]], [[neurotropic-fungi-japan-east-asia-species-cultural-context]],
  and parts of Australasia
- **Habitat:** Decaying hardwood stumps, buried roots, and [[fungal-diversity-leaf-litter-coarse-woody-debris]] debris
  in deciduous and mixed forests
- **Substrate:** Saprotrophic on Quercus, Fagus, Acer, Betula; occasionally conifers
- **Season:** Summer through autumn (June–November in Northern Hemisphere)
- **Frequency:** Common to fairly common in suitable habitat

## Bioactive Compounds

- **Oudemansin A and B:** Strobilurin-related antifungal agents that disrupt
  mitochondrial electron transport in competing fungi via inhibition of complex III
  (cytochrome bc1). These served as lead structures for synthetic agricultural
  fungicides including azoxystrobin and kresoxim-methyl.
- **Polysaccharides:** β-glucan fractions with reported immunomodulatory activity
  in preliminary in vitro studies.

## Edibility and Culinary Use

- **Edibility:** Edible when thoroughly cooked
- **Flavor:** Mild to slightly nutty; texture tender in caps, fibrous in stipe
- **Preparation:** Use caps of young specimens; remove tough rooting base
- **Rating:** Moderate — suitable for mixed mushroom dishes, soups, and stews
- ⚠️ **Caution:** Always positively identify before consumption

## Ecological Role

- **[[bloomfield-wood-decay-fungi-spalted-timber]] type:** [[comparison-brown-rot-vs-white-rot-bioremediation]] — degrades both cellulose and lignin via
  peroxidase and laccase enzyme systems
- **[[comparison-nutrient-cycling-vs-nutrient-dense-gardening]]:** Mobilizes carbon and nitrogen from woody substrates
- **Soil interactions:** Deep rooting stipe colonizes buried wood inaccessible
  to surface-decomposing fungi
