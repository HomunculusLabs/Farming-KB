# Casuarina (She-oak)

## Description

*Casuarina* is a genus of evergreen tree and shrub species in the family Casuarinaceae, native to Australasia, Southeast Asia, and the Pacific Islands. Commonly known as she-oaks, these plants are distinguished by their needle-like branchlets that resemble pine foliage. They are exceptionally wind-tolerant and are listed by Mollison as prime frontline windbreak species in exposed coastal and desert environments.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Family:** Casuarinaceae
- **Genus:** Casuarina
- **Notable species:** C. equisetifolia (coastal she-oak), C. cunninghamiana (river she-oak), C. glauca (swamp she-oak)
- **Common names:** She-oak, beefwood, ironwood, horsetail tree, coastal casuarina

## Key Characteristics

- **Growth habit:** Tall trees (10-35 m) or shrubs with weeping habit
- **Foliage:** Reduced to tiny scales; photosynthesis performed by grooved, needle-like branchlets (cladodes)
- **Bark:** Deeply fissured, dark grey-brown, fire-resistant
- **Nitrogen fixation:** Forms root nodules with actinomycete bacteria (Frankia), fixing atmospheric nitrogen
- **Wind tolerance:** Among the most wind-resistant trees, with hard needle-like branchlets that resist desiccation and sandblast
- **Salt tolerance:** Many species are highly tolerant of salt spray and saline soils
- **Growth rate:** Fast, especially in sandy coastal soils

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

### Frontline Windbreak Species

Mollison identifies Casuarina among the wind-resistant plants sharing key features: hard, needle-like leaves or stems that resist desiccation and sandblast. Their role in [[permaculture]]
- Fast-growing in sandy coastal soils
- Hard, dense timber valued for construction and fuel

## Cautionary Notes

- Can be invasive outside native range, particularly C. equisetifolia in Florida and the Caribbean
- Allelopathic effects may inhibit some understory species
- Dense litter layer can suppress germination of other plants
- May compete for water in dry environments

## Cultivation

- Full sun
- Well-drained sandy to loamy soils
- Highly drought and salt tolerant
- Propagation from seed
- USDA zones 9-12 (species-dependent)
- Minimal maintenance once established
