# Iquitos

## Description

Iquitos is the largest city in the Peruvian Amazon and serves as a significant center for ayahuasca shamanism. Located in the Loreto region of eastern Peru, it is accessible only by river or air. The city represents the urban continuation of Amazonian shamanic traditions in a modernizing context, where traditional ayahuasca healing practices adapt to the needs of a growing urban population.

## Classification

- **Type:** City / geographic location
- **Country:** Peru
- **Region:** Loreto, Amazon basin
- **Cultural significance:** Major center of urban ayahuasca practice

## Urban Shamanism

As documented by Dobkin de Rios, Iquitos represents a distinctive form of urban Amazonian shamanism:

- **Practitioners:** Ayahuasqueros — mestizo shamans who use ayahuasca as their primary diagnostic and therapeutic tool
- **Clientele:** Primarily slum-dwellers of mixed Indian-white ancestry (mestizos)
- **Context:** Urban poverty where modern Western medicine is either unavailable or unaffordable
- **Illness attribution:** Supernatural causation, drawing on both native American and European beliefs about witchcraft

## Ayahuasca Healing Practice

The ayahuasca curing sessions in Iquitos follow a distinctive pattern:

- **Group sessions:** The ayahuasquero and patients partake of the hallucinogenic drink together
- **Diagnostic trance:** The curer uses the trance state to determine the cause of each patient's illness
- **Blame attribution:** Illness is typically ascribed to a specific bewitcher in each case
- **Therapeutic mechanism:** Dobkin de Rios interprets this as transforming generalized, incapacitating anxiety into solid fear placed on a specific wrongdoer — a psychologically effective reframing
- **Reinforcement:** The hallucinations and shamanic guidance reinforce the patient's belief in the shaman's power and information

## Key Facts

- The inclination to employ an ayahuasquero is reinforced by the comparatively high cost of formal medical consultation
- Some ayahuasqueros go beyond diagnosis to actively punishing alleged bewitchers
- Practitioners maintain that while intoxicated they can leave their bodies and inflict harm upon their clients' enemies
- These beliefs resemble those reported for traditional European witchcraft
- The practice bridges indigenous Amazonian and European folk traditions

## Anthropological Significance

Iquitos is significant for understanding how traditional shamanic practices adapt to urban environments. The city's ayahuasqueros represent a living example of cultural syncretism, combining:

- Indigenous Amazonian plant knowledge ([[ayahuasca-preparation]])
- Native American concepts of supernatural illness and witchcraft
- European folk beliefs about malevolent magic
- Urban social dynamics of poverty and limited healthcare access

## Modern Context

In recent decades, Iquitos has also become a major destination for "[[ayahuasca-tourism]]," where foreigners travel to experience traditional ceremonies. This has further transformed the local shamanic economy, creating new dynamics between traditional practice and global demand.

## Relevance to Ethnobotany

The persistence of ayahuasca practice in Iquitos demonstrates the enduring importance of botanical knowledge in Amazonian cultures, even in urbanized settings. The city's ayahuasqueros maintain sophisticated knowledge of plant preparation, dosing, and therapeutic application that has been passed down through generations.
