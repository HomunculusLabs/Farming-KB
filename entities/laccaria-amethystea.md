The species is an ectomycorrhizal associate of broadleaf and coniferous trees and is one of the most commonly encountered purple fungi in temperate forests. While edible and widely consumed in some cultures, it is thin-fleshed and generally considered of limited culinary value.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Hydnangiaceae
- **Genus:** *Laccaria*
- **Species:** *L. amethystea*

The species was originally described as *Agaricus amethysteus* by Jean Baptiste François Pierre Bulliard in 1784 and later transferred to the genus *Laccaria* by Mordecai Cubitt Cooke in 1884. The epithet *amethystea* refers to the purple gemstone amethyst.

Many authorities continue to use the name *Laccaria amethystina*, which remains common in European mycological literature. Molecular studies have confirmed the species as distinct from other *Laccaria* species in the genus.

### Synonyms

- *Laccaria amethystina* (Bull.) Cooke
- *Agaricus amethysteus* Bull.
- *Clitocybe amethystina* (Bull.) Quél.

## Morphology

The cap is 1–4 cm in diameter, convex when young and becoming broadly convex to depressed with age. When fresh, the cap is a deep amethyst-purple that fades to pale lilac, buff, or nearly white as the mushroom matures — the source of the common name "Deceiver."

The gills are adnate to sinuate, thick and widely spaced, with the same purple coloration as the cap when young, fading similarly with age. A distinctive feature of the genus *Laccaria* is the warty or irregular spores, visible under magnification.

The stipe is 3–8 cm long and 2–5 mm thick, fibrous, and tough. It is solid when young, becoming hollow with age. Color matches the cap but may remain somewhat purple longer than the cap surface.

Flesh is thin, purple when cut in fresh specimens, and has a mild, indistinct taste and odor. Spore print is white. Spores are spherical to broadly ellipsoid, 7–10 µm in diameter, ornamented with conspicuous spines or warts — a key diagnostic feature for the genus.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Laccaria amethystea* has a wide distribution across Europe, Asia, and North America. In Europe, it is one of the most common woodland mushrooms, found from Scandinavia to the Mediterranean. In North America, it occurs primarily in the East and [[ectomycorrhizal-associations]] with a broad range of tree hosts, including oaks (*Quercus* spp.), beech (*Fagus* spp.), pines (*Pinus* spp.), spruces (*Picea* spp.), and birches (*Betula* spp.). ## Edibility

*Laccaria amethystea* is technically edible and is consumed in some European countries, particularly in Eastern Europe and Russia. However, the thin flesh and small size limit its culinary appeal.

When collected in quantity, the mushrooms can be used in soups, stews, or as a garnish. They are not generally considered worth collecting for the table in Western European and North American foraging traditions, where larger and more flavorful species are available.

There are no known toxic lookalikes in the *Laccaria* genus. The combination of purple coloration, widely spaced thick gills, and warty spores makes identification relatively straightforward. As always, positive identification should be confirmed before consumption.
