## Description

Green manure refers to crops grown specifically to be cut and incorporated into or placed on the soil surface to improve fertility, structure, and biological activity. Unlike animal manures, green manures are produced on-site from living plant material, making them a cornerstone of sustainable and permaculture soil management. Mollison advocates green manures as the safest and most reliable source of organic matter and nutrients, particularly from leguminous hedgerows, windbreaks, and intercrops that fix atmospheric nitrogen while providing additional yields.

## Classification

- **Category:** Soil fertility practice
- **Primary plant families used:** Fabaceae/Leguminosae (legumes), Poaceae (grasses), Brassicaceae (brassicas), Buckwheat (Polygonaceae)
- **Application methods:** Surface mulching, shallow incorporation, or slash-and-drop
- **Duration:** Annual cover crops, seasonal intercrops, or perennial hedgerow sources

## Key Facts

### Why Green Manures Are the Safest Nutrient Source
- Manures from pigs, poultry, cattle, and sewage sludges can add excessive copper, zinc, nickel, boron, lead, or cadmium to soils, especially acid sandy soils.
- Earthworm casts from contaminated wastes (pig, sheep, domestic) can build up excess zinc.
- Poultry mixes commonly show zinc contamination that inhibits plant growth and health.
- Copper and arsenic are commonly added to stock feeds, creating toxic residues.
- Green manures from plants grown on clean soil avoid all these contamination pathways.

### Nutrient Contributions
- **Leguminous green manures** fix atmospheric nitrogen through rhizobial root nodules, providing 50-200 kg N/ha depending on species and conditions.
- All green manures contribute organic matter, humus precursors, and mineral nutrients drawn from their root systems.
- Leaf material cut and mulched green contains more nutrients than fallen leaves, although both are valuable.
- Green manures cycle deep-soil minerals to the surface through deep-rooted species.

### Role in Soil Structure
- Root systems open and maintain soil pores and channels.
- Deep-rooted species such as lucerne (alfalfa) and tree roots re-open compacted soil structure.
- Root decay leaves organic matter channels that improve water infiltration and gas exchange.
- Living root exudates feed soil biota continuously — up to 30% of plant energy goes to soil via roots.

### Integration with Permaculture Systems
- **Leguminous hedgerows:** Provide green manure material, wind protection, habitat, and sometimes edible or fodder yields simultaneously.
- **Windbreak species:** When trimmed, provide nutrient-rich mulch material from nitrogen-fixing species.
- **Intercrops:** Green manure species grown between crop rows, providing living mulch that can be slashed and dropped.
- **Orchard floor:** Permanent green manure crops under fruit trees provide mulch, suppress weeds, and feed soil biology.
- Mollison recommends using legumes in proportion similar to natural plant associations in the area, at all stages of succession.

### Green Manures vs Imported Mulches
- Imported mulches (straw, hay, manure) can carry weed seeds, residual biocides, or toxic mineral residues.
- Green manures grown on-site are guaranteed free of external contamination.
- On-site production eliminates transport costs and energy inputs.
- Green manures can be grown specifically for their nutrient profile, e.g., high-phosphorus or high-potassium species.

### Composting with Green Manures
- Green manure material is excellent for both hot and cold composting.
- Chinese method: build aerated heap with bamboo poles as air tunnels, cover with mud, let heat to 55-60 degrees C for several days, then seal holes for anaerobic completion. This combines pathogen/seed kill with nitrogen conservation.
- 1% superphosphate added to hot compost prevents ammonia escape.

### Contribution to the Ethylene-Oxygen Cycle
- Surface-mulched green manure (not incorporated) serves as an ethylene precursor.
- Old leaves from green manure crops are particularly effective for this purpose.
- Maintaining continuous mulch from green manures supports the aerobic-anaerobic cycling that keeps nutrients available.

## Relevance to Cultivation

Green manures are Mollison's recommended primary nutrient source for permaculture systems after initial soil preparation. They address every major soil need simultaneously: organic matter, nutrient supply, structure improvement, biological feeding, moisture retention, and erosion prevention. The emphasis on leguminous species is strategic because nitrogen is typically the most limiting nutrient and the one most easily supplied through biological fixation rather than purchased inputs. Growing green manures on-site eliminates the contamination risks of animal manures and the dependency on external inputs, embodying the permaculture principles of self-reliance and closed-loop nutrient cycling.

## Common Green Manure Species

Popular green manure crops include crimson clover, hairy vetch, field peas,
and winter rye for temperate climates. Buckwheat excels as a summer cover
that suppresses weeds and accumulates phosphorus. Alfalfa provides deep
root penetration and nitrogen fixation in established plantings.
