# Kaligreen

## Description
Kaligreen is a commercially available fungicide based on potassium bicarbonate (KHCO3), used primarily for control of powdery mildew in vineyards, ornamental plants, and various food crops. It is classified as a minimum-risk pesticide and is approved for use in organic production systems. Elaine Ingham mentions Kaligreen in the context of vineyard disease management alongside copper, sulfur, and compost tea as part of an integrated approach to disease control.

## Classification
- **Active ingredient**: Potassium bicarbonate (KHCO3)
- **Product type**: Contact fungicide / bactericide
- **EPA classification**: Minimum-risk pesticide (exempt from tolerance)
- **OMRI listed**: Approved for organic production
- **Mode of action**: Disrupts fungal cell wall integrity and pH balance

## Key Facts

### Mechanism of Action
- Potassium bicarbonate creates an alkaline environment hostile to fungal spores
- Disrupts the cell walls of powdery mildew spores through osmotic shock
- Alters leaf surface pH, making it inhospitable for spore germination
- Contact-only activity — must directly cover the pathogen to be effective
- Does not have systemic or translaminar activity
- Short residual activity compared to synthetic fungicides

### Target Diseases
- **Primary target**: Powdery mildew (Uncinula necator on grapes, Podosphaera spp. on other crops)
- Also effective against:
  - Downy mildew
  - Black spot on roses
  - Anthracnose
  - Various foliar fungal diseases

### Use in Vineyards
- Elaine Ingham references copper, sulfur, and Kaligreen as chemical tools used in vineyard disease management
- Ingham's work showed that AACT could reduce sulfur applications from 10-14 per season down to one
- Kaligreen represents a lower-impact chemical option that can be integrated with biological approaches
- Can be used in rotation with compost tea applications to reduce total chemical inputs
- Does not accumulate in soil like copper-based products

### Application Guidelines
- Apply as foliar spray ensuring thorough coverage
- Best applied preventively or at first sign of disease
- Short pre-harvest interval (typically 0 days)
- Can be tank-mixed with some adjuvants for improved coverage
- Avoid application during extreme heat to prevent leaf burn
- Reapplication needed after heavy rain (not rainfast)

### Advantages Over Conventional Fungicides
- Approved for organic production
- Low toxicity to mammals, birds, and beneficial insects
- No soil accumulation concerns
- Short PHI (pre-harvest interval)
- Low risk of resistance development
- Potassium contributes to plant nutrition
- Compatible with IPM (Integrated Pest Management) programs

### Limitations
- Contact-only activity requires thorough spray coverage
- Short residual activity requires frequent applications
- Less effective than synthetic fungicides under high disease pressure
- Can cause phytotoxicity at high rates or in hot weather
- Must be used preventively rather than curatively

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology
- **Vineyard management**: Important tool for organic and sustainable grape production
- **Integrated approach**: Can be combined with AACT to reduce total chemical applications
- **Powdery mildew control**: One of the primary organic options for this ubiquitous vineyard disease
- **Soil biology preservation**: Unlike copper, does not harm soil organisms or accumulate to toxic levels
- **Transition tool**: Useful during transition from chemical to biological disease management
