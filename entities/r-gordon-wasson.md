# R. Gordon Wasson

Robert Gordon Wasson (September 22, 1898 – December 23, 1986) was an
American author, banker, and amateur mycologist who co-founded the field of
**ethnomycology** — the study of fungi in human culture, religion, and art.
His 1957 discovery of psychoactive mushroom ceremonies among the Mazatec
people of Mexico catapulted psilocybin mushrooms into global awareness and
catalyzed the modern psychedelic era. See also [[albert-hofmann]],
[[terence-mckenna]], [[paul-stamets]], and [[amanita-muscaria]].

## Early Life and Career

Born in Great Falls, Montana, Wasson served in the American Expeditionary
Forces during World War I (1917–1919). He earned a BA in English literature
(1920) and MA from Columbia's School of Journalism (1921). In 1928 he joined
J.P. Morgan & Co., rising to **Vice President of Public Relations** — held for
over three decades until early 1960s retirement. His banking career funded his
private mycological obsession from the late 1920s onward.

## Mycophilia and Mycophobia

Wasson's interest in mushrooms originated from a cultural divide with his wife,
**Valentina Pavlovna Guercken** (1896–1958), a Russian-born pediatrician. She
loved mushroom foraging; Wasson, an Anglo-American, inherited the widespread
English-speaking revulsion toward fungi. This led to their theory of human
cultural attitudes:

- **Mycophilia** — cultures that revere mushrooms (Slavic, French, Italian,
  Spanish, Mesoamerican, East Asian)
- **Myophobia** — cultures that fear mushrooms (Anglo-American, Germanic,
  Scandinavian)

This framework founded ethnomycology. Valentina was the Slavic-culture expert
and linguist; her contributions were central though sometimes underrecognized.
She died in January 1958.

## Mexican Mushroom Expeditions (1953–1957)

The Wassons made approximately seven trips to Mexico's Mazatec region of Oaxaca
around **Huautla de Jiménez**, investigating indigenous mushroom-based
divinatory rituals — *veladas* (night ceremonies) involving psychoactive
mushrooms the Mazatec called *nti xi tho* ("the little ones that spring forth").

### The 1955 Velada with María Sabina

On **June 29–30, 1955**, Wasson and photographer Allan Richardson became the
first known non-indigenous Westerners to participate in a Mazatec velada,
conducted by curandera **María Sabina** (c. 1894–1985). Wasson ingested
psilocybin mushrooms (*Psilocybe caerulescens* and/or *P. mexicana*) and
experienced profound visionary effects — one of the most consequential moments
in psychedelic history.

### Collaboration with Heim and Hofmann

French mycologist **Roger Heim** accompanied Wasson in 1956–1957, identifying
several new psychoactive mushroom species and cultivating specimens in Paris.
He provided them to **Albert Hofmann** at Sandoz, who isolated **psilocybin**
and **psilocin** in 1958, then synthesized psilocybin for clinical distribution
as "Indocybin." This was one of the most important interdisciplinary
partnerships in 20th-century ethnobotany.

## Major Publications

### *Mushrooms, Russia and History* (1957)

Co-authored with Valentina, published by Pantheon in two lavish volumes
(originally 512 copies). A founding text of ethnomycology documenting mushroom
cultural history through art, folklore, linguistics, and archaeology.

### "Seeking the Magic Mushroom" — LIFE (May 13, 1957)

A 17-page photo-essay with Allan Richardson — first mainstream exposure of
psilocybin mushrooms to the West. Coined "magic mushrooms." Impact:

- Sparked international interest in psilocybin mushrooms
- Brought tourists and counterculture figures to Huautla
- Inspired [[timothy-leary]] and Richard Alpert to travel to Mexico, leading
  directly to the Harvard Psilocybin Project (1960–1963)
- Catalyzed scientific investigation of psilocybin

### *Soma: Divine Mushroom of Immortality* (1968)

Wasson's magnum opus (Ethno-Mycological Studies Vol. 1). Argued that **Soma**
— the sacred plant/deity of the Rigveda — was **Amanita muscaria**. Drawing
on Vedic philology, Iranian *haoma* parallels, and Siberian shamanic use,
Wasson contended it was the only substance matching all Vedic descriptions.
Influential but controversial; alternatives include ephedra, Cannabis, and
*Peganum harmala*. See also [[amanita-muscaria]].

### Other Works

- *The Hallucinogenic Mushrooms of Mexico* (with Heim, 1975)
- *María Sabina and Her Mazatec Mushroom Velada* (1974)
- *The Wondrous Mushroom: Mycolatry in Mesoamerica* (1980)
- *Persephone's Quest* (with Ruck, [[jonathan-ott]], Bigwood, 1978) — argued
  psychoactive fungi played a role in the Eleusinian Mysteries

## Legacy

Wasson is the **founder of ethnomycology**. His work catalyzed the
popularization of psychedelic mushrooms worldwide, the counterculture's embrace
of natural psychedelics, and the entire field of psychedelic studies. His
papers are archived at Harvard's Botanical Museum; multiple species bear his
name. He died December 23, 1986, in Danbury, Connecticut, at age 88.

## Criticism and Controversies

### Impact on the Mazatec

The 1957 LIFE article devastated Huautla de Jiménez — publicity brought drug
tourists and police crackdowns. [[maria-sabina]] was harassed, arrested, and
ostracized. Critics argue Wasson extracted indigenous knowledge without
adequate consideration of consequences.

### CIA Connections

Evidence suggests Wasson's 1953 trip may have been partially funded through
CIA-connected channels (the **Society for the Investigation of Human Ecology**,
a CIA front). The degree of his awareness remains debated.
