# Prunella vulgaris

## Overview

**Prunella vulgaris**, commonly known as self-heal or heal-all, is a low-growing
perennial herb in the mint family Lamiaceae. It is one of the most widely
distributed medicinal plants in the world, found across temperate regions of
Europe, Asia, Africa, and [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]]. The plant has been used in traditional
medicine systems for centuries, particularly for wound healing, sore throats,
and inflammatory conditions.

The genus name *Prunella* likely derives from the German *Brunelle* (quinsy),
referring to its traditional use for throat ailments. The common name "self-heal"
reflects its reputation as a versatile folk remedy for minor injuries and
infections.

## Taxonomy and Classification

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Order:** Lamiales
- **Family:** Lamiaceae
- **Subfamily:** Nepetoideae
- **Tribe:** Prunelleae (monotypic tribe)
- **Genus:** Prunella
- **Species:** P. vulgaris L.

The genus Prunella is monogeneric within its tribe, making P. vulgaris the
sole representative. Several subspecies are recognized: subsp. *vulgaris*
(Eurasian), subsp. *lanceolata* ([[growing-gourmet-north-american-reishi-species]]), and subsp. *asiatica* (East
Asian), with some overlap in morphological characters.

## Morphology

- **Habit:** Low-growing perennial, 5–30 cm tall, procumbent to ascending,
  forming mats or loose tufts. Often roots at stem nodes.
- **Stems:** Square (characteristic of Lamiaceae), slender, often purplish,
  simple or branched at the base, pubescent.
- **Leaves:** Opposite, ovate to lanceolate, 1.5–5 cm long, serrate margins,
  dark green, often with purplish tinting. Petioles short, 2–8 mm.
- **Flowers:** Bilabiate, arranged in dense terminal spikes 1.5–4 cm long.
  Calyx tubular, bilabiate, with 5 teeth. Corolla violet-purple to
  pinkish-purple (rarely white), 8–14 mm long. Upper lip hooded, lower lip
  three-lobed with the middle lobe largest. Blooms June–September.
- **Fruit:** Four small nutlets (mericarps), brown, ~2 mm, enclosed in the
  persistent calyx.
- **Roots:** Fibrous, shallow, with creeping stolons enabling vegetative spread.

## Distribution and Habitat

- **Global range:** Circumboreal and cosmopolitan. Native to Europe, Asia,
  North Africa, and North America. Naturalized in South America and Australasia.
- **Habitat:** Grasslands, meadows, lawns, roadsides, forest edges, stream
  banks, and disturbed sites. Highly adaptable to a range of soil types and
  moisture conditions. Tolerates both full sun and partial shade.
- **Invasiveness:** Can be aggressive in maintained lawns and pastures; often
  considered a weed in turfgrass settings.

## Bioactive Compounds

Key constituents identified in P. vulgaris aerial parts include:

- **Rosmarinic acid:** A caffeic acid ester with significant antioxidant,
  anti-inflammatory, and antiviral properties. One of the most abundant
  phenolic compounds in the herb.
- **Ursolic acid and oleanolic acid:** Triterpenoid acids with demonstrated
  anti-inflammatory, hepatoprotective, and anticancer activities in vitro.
- **Prunellin:** A polysaccharide fraction with immunomodulatory properties.
- **Flavonoids:** Including hyperoside, rutin, and quercetin derivatives.
- **Tannins:** Condensed tannins contributing to astringent wound-healing
  activity.
- **Volatile oils:** Minor components including camphor and linalool derivatives.

## Medicinal Uses

Traditional and pharmacologically investigated applications include:

- **Wound healing:** Topical application of crushed leaves or poultices for
  cuts, abrasions, and minor burns. Astringent tannins promote tissue
  contraction and reduce bleeding.
- **Sore throat and oral inflammation:** Gargles or infusions for pharyngitis,
  stomatitis, and gingivitis, supported by anti-inflammatory activity.
- **Antiviral activity:** In vitro studies demonstrate activity against herpes
  simplex virus (HSV-1 and HSV-2) and HIV, attributed to rosmarinic acid and
  polysaccharide fractions.
- **Anti-inflammatory:** Used internally and topically for inflammatory
  conditions including dermatitis and minor joint pain.

## Permaculture Applications

P. vulgaris offers multiple functions in [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-permaculture-design-principles]]:

- **[[fukuoka-citrus-orchard-ground-cover-cultivation]]:** Dense, low growth habit provides excellent weed
  suppression and soil protection in garden beds and pathways.
- **Pollinator support:** Flowers attract bees, butterflies, and other
  pollinators over a long blooming season.
- **[[comparison-living-mulch-vs-cover-crops]]:** Compatible with orchard understories and food forests.
- **Edible:** Young leaves and flowers are edible raw or cooked, with a
  mildly bitter flavor. Used in salads, soups, and teas.
- **Soil stabilization:** Creeping stolons help bind soil on slopes and
  disturbed sites.
