## Dianthus superbus (Fringed Pink)

## Overview

Dianthus superbus L., commonly known as fringed pink or large pink, is a herbaceous perennial in the family Caryophyllaceae. It is celebrated for its deeply fringed, fragrant flowers in shades of pink, lavender, and white on stems reaching 30–60 cm.

The species is native to a vast area spanning Europe and temperate Asia, from France and Italy through central Asia to Japan and Korea. It has been cultivated in both European and East Asian gardens for centuries.

The species exhibits significant morphological [[cannabis-for-skin-conditions-detailed]]. The herb is typically prepared as a decoction or infused in wine.

Modern pharmacological studies have identified triterpenoid saponins, flavonoids, and anthocyanins as the primary bioactive constituents. The saponin dianthoside has been the subject of anti-inflammatory research.

In Korean traditional medicine, the plant is known as Gukmaek and is used similarly for urinary and [[cancer-medicinal-mushrooms-schizophyllan-spg-clinical-trials]].

The herb is generally considered safe at traditional dosage levels. However, excessive consumption may cause gastrointestinal discomfort due to the saponin content.
