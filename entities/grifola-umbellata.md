## Zhu Ling (*Grifola umbellata* (Pers.) Pilát)

## Overview

*Grifola umbellata*, known as Zhu Ling (猪苓) in Chinese pharmacopeia, is a
polypore fungus forming large, compound [[polyporus-umbellatus]]* Fr. (1821)
- **Common names:** Zhu Ling, Chuling, Umbrella polypore

The epithet *umbellata* refers to the umbel-like arrangement of multiple caps
radiating from a common base, resembling an umbrella cluster.

## Morphology

### Fruiting Body

- Compound, multi-branched structure, 10–40 cm across
- Multiple overlapping caps arising from a single thick base
- Individual caps 1–4 cm wide, kidney-shaped to fan-shaped
- Upper surface: tan to brown, finely velvety, radially wrinkled
- Pore surface: white to cream, pores angular, 2–4 per mm
- Context: white, soft when fresh, tough when dried

### Sclerotium

- Irregular, tuberous mass, 10–25 cm diameter
- Dark brown to black exterior, hard and woody; white marbled interior
- Develops underground at tree bases or root zones
- This is the primary harvested and medicinally used structure
- Weight: 200–500 g typically, up to 2 kg mature

### Microscopic Features

- Basidiospores: cylindrical to ellipsoid, 7–10 × 3–4 µm
- Hyphal system: dimitic (generative + skeletal hyphae); clamp connections absent

## Distribution and Habitat

- Temperate broadleaf forests of [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]] (less common)
- Found at bases of oak (*Quercus*), maple (*Acer*), elm (*Ulmus*), and beech
  (*Fagus*)
- Sclerotia develop underground in association with living or dead roots
- Fruiting bodies emerge in late summer to autumn (August–October)
- Prefers well-drained, humus-rich soils at 400–2000 m elevation
