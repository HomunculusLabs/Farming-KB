## Bill Mackently

## Description

Bill Mackently is an innovative practitioner cited by Mollison as a personal communication source for a creative harvesting strategy that uses grey squirrels to collect hickory nuts and acorns. This approach — providing animals with buried pipes as storage sites and taking approximately 85% of their cached harvest — exemplifies the [[permaculture]] principle of working with natural behaviors rather than against them.

## Classification

- **Role:** Innovative harvester, permaculture practitioner
- **Contribution:** Animal-assisted harvesting system
- **Source type:** Personal communication (pers. comm.) with [[bill-mollison]]
- **Geographic context:** North America (grey squirrel range)

## Key Facts

- Uses grey squirrels (Sciurus carolinensis) as harvesting agents for hickory nuts and acorns
- Provides squirrels with buried pipes as artificial storage sites for nuts
- Takes approximately 85% of the squirrels' stored harvest, leaving them about 15%
- This method produces cleaner, more efficiently gathered harvests than mechanical alternatives
- The approach demonstrates that understanding and exploiting natural animal behaviors can outperform technological solutions

## The Squirrel Harvesting Method

The technique operates on the principle that squirrels are far more efficient nut-gatherers than humans or machines:

1. Provide buried pipes or similar artificial storage chambers accessible to squirrels
2. Squirrels naturally collect and store nuts in these chambers during autumn foraging
3. Periodically harvest from the chambers, leaving approximately 15% for the squirrels' winter needs
4. The result is clean, sorted nuts gathered at zero human labor cost

## Connection to Packrat Harvesting

Mollison extends Mackently's squirrel method to wild rice (Zizania) harvesting, where packrats naturally carry large quantities of sound, cleaned grain into artificial shelters insulated with Typha (cattail) seed silk. The parallel principles are:

- Provide attractive artificial storage sites for the target animal
- Allow the animal to do the gathering work using its natural behaviors
- Harvest a sustainable portion of the cache (85/15 split)
- The method may be "the most efficient, as well as the cleanest, harvesting" available

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

Bill Mackently's approach represents a practical application of several [[permaculture-principles]]:

1. **Work with nature, not against it:** Instead of fighting squirrels as pests, harness their natural caching behavior as a productive force
2. **Multiple function:** Squirrels serve as both harvesters and sorters, selecting only sound nuts
3. **Fair share:** The 85/15 split ensures both human and animal needs are met sustainably
4. **[[appropriate-technology]]:** Buried pipes are simpler, cheaper, and more effective than mechanical harvesters
5. **Yield optimization:** Mollison notes that mechanical wild rice harvesters costing $300,000+ gather less than 10% of available grain — animals vastly outperform machines for certain crops

This technique challenges the assumption that mechanization is always superior and demonstrates that biological systems can achieve greater efficiency when properly designed.
