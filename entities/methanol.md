# Methanol (Methyl Alcohol)

## Description

Methanol (CH₃OH), also known as methyl alcohol, wood alcohol, or carbinol, is the simplest alcohol and a widely used industrial and laboratory solvent. With a boiling point of 64°C (147°F), it is one of the most volatile common solvents. In cannabis and natural product extraction, methanol is valued for its ability to dissolve cannabinoids efficiently without co-extracting excessive water-soluble tars. It is produced both industrially from synthesis gas and traditionally through destructive distillation of wood.

## Classification

- **Category**: Chemical / Solvent
- **Chemical type**: Primary alcohol
- **Molecular formula**: CH₃OH
- **Molecular weight**: 32.04 g/mol
- **Boiling point**: 64°C (147°F)
- **CAS number**: 67-56-1
- **Appearance**: Colorless liquid with characteristic odor

## Key Facts

- Commonly available at pharmacies in small quantities and at industrial chemical supply companies in larger volumes. Also sold as paint thinner (though often impure in this form).
- Produces cleaner cannabis oil extracts compared to [[isopropyl-alcohol]] because methanol does not extract significant amounts of water-soluble, non-psychoactive tars.
- Evaporates at a uniform temperature (approximately 190°F / 88°C in solution), facilitating controlled solvent removal.
- Highly toxic: ingestion of as little as 10 mL can cause permanent blindness through metabolism to formic acid, which damages the optic nerve. Inhalation causes body ache and illness.
- Fumes are both toxic and explosive, requiring careful ventilation during extraction operations.
- Any residual methanol remaining in the final product poses a serious health hazard to consumers, making complete solvent removal critical.
- Used in the isomerization process where cannabis oil is dissolved in absolute methanol (1g oil : 10g solvent) with sulfuric acid catalyst for CBD-to-THC conversion.
- Also used in laboratory settings for HPLC analysis, GC sample preparation, and as a general-purpose polar solvent.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Methanol has several important applications in mycology:

- **Mushroom alkaloid extraction**: Methanol is one of the most effective solvents for extracting [[psilocybin-and-psilocin-biosynthesis-variation]] from dried mushroom biomass. Acidified methanol (with dilute acetic or hydrochloric acid) improves extraction efficiency.
- **Fungal metabolite analysis**: Methanol is used in HPLC mobile phases and for preparing fungal extract samples for mass spectrometry.
- **Mycotoxin extraction**: Methanol/water mixtures are standard solvents for extracting mycotoxins (aflatoxins, ochratoxin, fumonisins) from grain substrates and fungal cultures.
- **Spore and tissue preservation**: Methanol can be used as a fixative for [[fungal-microscopic-examination-mounting-media-techniques]] of fungal structures.
- **[[ergot-alkaloid-chemistry-history]] extraction**: Methanol effectively extracts ergotamine and related alkaloids from *Claviceps* spp. sclerotia.

## Safety Considerations

- **Toxicity**: Methanol is metabolized to formaldehyde and then formic acid in the liver. Symptoms of poisoning include headache, nausea, abdominal pain, and visual disturbances progressing to blindness.
- **Flammability**: Flash point of 12°C (54°F) — extremely flammable. Use only with spark-free equipment and adequate ventilation.
- **Ventilation**: All methanol work must be performed in well-ventilated areas or under fume hoods. Never use in [[guzman-voluntary-hallucinations-psilocybin-vapor-enclosed-spaces]].
- **Complete removal**: When used for product extraction, the solvent must be completely removed through evaporation in a boiling water bath. A final water-wash step (adding water and evaporating at 220°F) ensures all traces of solvent are eliminated, since methanol boils well below water.
