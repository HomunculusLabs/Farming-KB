## Morphology

Red spruce typically reaches 18–23 m in height and 40–60 cm DBH, with
maximum heights of 30+ m on optimal sites.

The crown is narrow and conical, with dense horizontal to ascending branches.

Bark is thin (6–12 mm), gray-brown to reddish-brown, flaking in small
irregular scales.

Needles are single, 12–15 mm long, quadrangular in cross-section, yellow-
green to dark green with sharp tips.

They are arranged spirally on the twig, giving a bush-tufted appearance.

A distinguishing feature is the reddish-brown twig bark, from which the
species derives its common name.

Cones are cylindrical, 3–5 cm long and 1.5–2.5 cm wide, with thin,
flexible, irregularly toothed scales.

Cones are green to purplish when young, turning light brown at maturity.

Seeds are small (2–3 mm) with a 7–10 mm wing.

The root system is shallow and widespreading, with most fine roots in the
upper 30 cm of soil.

southern Ontario, and south through New England to the high Appalachians.

forming pure stands or mixed forests with Fraser fir at the highest sites.
