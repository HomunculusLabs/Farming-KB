## Overview

**Cyathea leichhardtiana** (F.Muell.) Copel. is a medium-sized tree fern in the family Cyatheaceae, endemic to the subtropical and tropical rainforests of eastern Australia. It is named after the explorer and naturalist Ludwig Leichhardt (1813–1848), who led several expeditions into the Australian interior.

The species occurs from south-eastern Queensland northwards to the Wet Tropics of far north Queensland. It forms part of the diverse tree fern assemblage that characterizes the wetter rainforest types of the Australian east coast.

Cyathea leichhardtiana is less commonly cultivated than the popular Cyathea cooperi but is valued by specialist fern growers for its compact crown and attractive scaly stipes. It can be distinguished from congeners by its combination of large indusiate sori and finely divided frond segments.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:**
- Kingdom: Plantae
- Division: Polypodiophyta
- Class: Polypodiopsida
- Order: Cyatheales
- Family: Cyatheaceae
- Genus: *Cyathea* J.E.Sm.
- Species: *C. leichhardtiana* (F.Muell.) Copel.

**Basionym:** *Alsophila leichhardtiana* F.Muell.

The trunk is erect, typically reaching 3–6 m in height and 6–10 cm in diameter. The crown supports a spreading rosette of bipinnate to tripinnate fronds measuring 2–3 m in length.

Stipes are dark brown, densely covered with glossy, dark brown to black scales that are broadly lanceolate with entire margins. The rachis is similarly dark, contrasting sharply with the bright green lamina beneath.

Pinnae are spaced moderately along the rachis, with pinnules that are deeply lobed. The ultimate segments are oblong with crenate margins and acute apices. The lamina is firm in texture, slightly glossy above, and paler below.

Sori are borne on the veins of the lower lamina surface, arranged in a single row on either side of the pinnule midrib. Each sorus has a globose, dark brown indusium that ruptures irregularly at maturity. Spores are trilete, brown, and measure approximately 40–55 µm in diameter.

The crozier is densely covered with dark brown scales and pale hairs. Young fronds unfurl in the typical crosier pattern, with the stipe bases forming a fibrous skirt on the upper trunk.

Adventitious roots form a dense mantle around the trunk base, extending up to 50 cm above ground level. This root mantle provides both structural support and supplementary water absorption during dry periods.

## Distribution and Habitat

Cyathea leichhardtiana occurs in eastern Australia, from the McPherson Range on the Queensland–New South Wales border northwards through the Sunshine Coast hinterland, Eungella National Park, and the Wet Tropics of far north Queensland.

The species grows in subtropical and tropical rainforests, preferring sheltered creek gullies, moist escarpment slopes, and the cool, humid microsites of upland notophyll vine forests. It is found at elevations of 100–900 m above sea level.

Annual rainfall across the species' range varies from 1200 mm in the southern part to over 3000 mm in the Wet Tropics. The species prefers sites with reliable moisture year-round and is rarely found in areas with extended dry seasons.
