## Overview

**Drosera burmannii** is an annual or short-lived perennial sundew native to tropical and subtropical regions of Asia and Australia. It is widely recognized for its remarkably fast tentacle movement, among the fastest in the genus.

The species was one of the first carnivorous plants to be studied by early botanists. It was named in honor of the Dutch botanist Johannes Burman by Carl Linnaeus.

*D. burmannii* is distinguished by its compact rosette of wedge-shaped leaves and its ability to fold leaves around captured prey within seconds. This rapid response makes it a popular subject for scientific research on plant movement.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:** Plantae → Caryophyllales → Droseraceae → *Drosera* → *D. burmannii*.

**Authority:** Vahl (1794).

The species belongs to *Drosera* section *Thelocalyx*, a group of tropical and subtropical species characterized by compact rosettes and rapid tentacle responses.

Leaves are arranged in a flat rosette 3–8 cm in diameter. Individual leaves are wedge-shaped (cuneate), 8–20 mm long and 5–10 mm wide at the broadest point.

The lamina is covered with glandular tentacles on both surfaces, an unusual trait among sundews. Most Drosera species have tentacles concentrated on the adaxial surface.

## Distribution and Habitat

*D. burmannii* has a wide distribution across tropical and subtropical Asia and northern Australia. It occurs from India and Sri Lanka eastward through Southeast Asia to southern China, Taiwan, and northern Australia.

The species inhabits open, wet habitats including seasonally flooded grasslands, rice paddies, damp sand, margins of ponds and streams, and disturbed sites. It is often found growing as a weed in agricultural fields.

In Australia, the species ranges from the Kimberley region of Western Australia across the Northern Territory and Queensland to northern New South Wales. It is one of the most common carnivorous plants in tropical Australia.

Elevation ranges from sea level to approximately 1,500 meters. The species is most common in lowland tropical climates with distinct wet and dry seasons.

As an annual or short-lived perennial, the species relies on a persistent seed bank for survival. Seeds germinate at the onset of the wet season, and plants complete their life cycle within months.

The seed bank can remain viable in soil for several years. This strategy allows the species to persist through unfavorable dry periods and rapidly colonize newly available wet habitats.

In rice paddy ecosystems, *D. burmannii* commonly appears as a volunteer plant after fields are flooded. Farmers generally tolerate its presence as it does not compete significantly with rice.

## Carnivory and Prey Capture

*D. burmannii* is notable for possessing one of the fastest tentacle responses in the plant kingdom. Marginal tentacles can bend toward prey items within seconds of contact, far faster than most other sundews.

The rapid response is mediated by an action-potential-like electrical signal that propagates through the leaf tissue. This mechanism is functionally analogous to animal nerve impulses.
