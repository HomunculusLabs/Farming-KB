## Overview

**Carex globularis** is a circumboreal perennial sedge of the family Cyperaceae, commonly known as globe sedge or round-fruited sedge. It is widespread across northern Europe, Asia, and [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]].

The species is named for its nearly spherical utricles, which are distinctive among Carex species in its range. It typically grows in wet peaty habitats including bogs, fens, and damp coniferous woodlands.

Carex globularis belongs to Carex section Vesicariae, a group characterized by inflated bladder-like utricles. This morphological trait gives the species its common name.

The species flowers from May through July across most of its range. It is considered an indicator of acidic, nutrient-poor mire conditions in boreal and subarctic regions.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Carex globularis** L. was described by Linnaeus in 1753 in *Species Plantarum*.

It is a rhizomatous perennial forming loose to dense tussocks, typically 20 to 50 cm tall. Culms are slender, trigonous, and smooth to slightly roughened at the apex.

Leaf blades are 1.5 to 3 mm wide, flat or slightly canaliculate, and shorter than the culms. The leaf sheaths are purplish-brown at the base, a useful field character.

The inflorescence consists of 2 to 5 lateral female spikes and a terminal male spike. Female spikes are approximately spherical to ovoid, 6 to 12 mm in diameter, giving the distinctive globular appearance.

Glumes are ovate, reddish-brown with a pale midrib, and shorter than the utricles. Utricles are 4 to 5.5 mm long, broadly ovoid to nearly spherical, inflated, and pale green to brownish.

The utricle surface is smooth or faintly nerved. The beak is short, conical, and bidentate. Stigmas are two per flower.

Nutlets are tightly enclosed within the inflated utricles and approximately 2 mm long. They are biconvex and dark brown at maturity.

The inflated utricles may aid in water dispersal by increasing buoyancy. This adaptation is particularly relevant in the waterlogged habitats where the species typically grows.

Plants growing in shaded coniferous woodlands tend to produce longer culms and more open inflorescences. Open-grown mire plants are more compact and produce denser spike clusters.

The species shows moderate phenotypic plasticity in response to water table depth. Seasonal fluctuations in moisture availability can influence tussock density and flowering effort.

## Distribution and Habitat

Carex globularis has a circumboreal distribution, occurring across northern Europe, Siberia, northern Japan, and Canada. In Europe, it is found from Scandinavia and the British Isles south to the Alps and Carpathians.

The species occurs in a range of wetland habitats including ombrotrophic bogs, poor fens, wet coniferous woodlands, and damp heath margins. It favors acidic, nutrient-poor substrates.

It is particularly abundant in boreal and subarctic mire systems, where it may form extensive stands in hollows and lawns within the bog complex.

In the British Isles, the species is most common in Scotland and northern England, occurring in blanket bogs and upland wet heaths. It is rare and declining in southern and eastern England.

Elevational range extends from sea level to approximately 2000 m. Alpine populations occur in damp snow-patch communities and flushes.

The species is tolerant of cold conditions and is a component of tundra vegetation in subarctic regions of Scandinavia and Siberia.

In North America, the species ranges from Alaska across Canada to the Great Lakes region and New England. It occurs in similar habitats as in Eurasia, primarily acidic peatlands and wet [[biodiversity-fungi-temperate-boreal-forests]].

Populations in the southern parts of its range, such as the Alps and Carpathians, are typically restricted to high-elevation mire habitats where conditions remain cool and moist.

## Ecology and Ecological Role

Carex globularis is a characteristic species of acidic mire communities across its circumboreal range. It typically occupies the wetter microtopographic positions within bog complexes.

Common associates include Sphagnum species, Eriophorum vaginatum, Carex nigra, [[carex-canescens]], Andromeda polifolia, and Vaccinium oxycoccos in boreal mire systems.

The inflated utricles float on water, facilitating hydrochory. Seeds can be dispersed across the mire surface by water movement during periods of high water table.

The species provides habitat structure in open mire landscapes. Its tussocks offer shelter for invertebrates and nesting material for birds.

Several sedge-feeding invertebrates use Carex globularis as a host plant, including curculionid weevils and gelechiid moths that specialize on Carex species.

In boreal forest mires, the species contributes to ground cover beneath open conifer canopies. It plays a role in maintaining the moss layer that underpins peat accumulation.

The root system is relatively shallow but extensive, forming a dense mat that contributes to peat formation. Slow decomposition of sedge roots is a key process in boreal peatland [[gadd-fungal-carbon-sequestration]].

Small mammals such as voles use the tussocks for cover and may cache seeds near the base of Carex globularis plants. Birds including snipe and curlew forage in mire areas where the species is abundant.

## Conservation and Threats

Carex globularis is assessed as Least Concern globally due to its wide circumboreal distribution. However, regional declines have been documented in intensively managed landscapes.
