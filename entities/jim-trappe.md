## Description

Jim Trappe is a renowned mycologist and leading authority on [[ectomycorrhizal-fungi]], based at Oregon State University (retired at time of writing). Ingham describes him as "the guru of ectomycorrhizal fungi" and credits his expertise as invaluable for understanding both ectomycorrhizal and vesicular-arbuscular mycorrhizal (VAM) fungal dynamics. His work has been instrumental in establishing the scientific foundation for [[mycorrhizal-colonization-thresholds]] that predict plant health outcomes.

## Key Facts

- Recognized as the preeminent expert on ectomycorrhizal fungi
- Based at Oregon State University
- Mentored Dr. Efren Cazares, who performs VAM and ectomycorrhizal sample analysis and trains SFI technicians
- Collaborated with Bob Linderman on mycorrhizal research questions
- Provided critical expertise to Soil Foodweb Inc. (SFI) on VAM, endomycorrhizal, and ectomycorrhizal identification
- His research contributed to establishing colonization thresholds: below 40% VAM colonization correlates with root disease; above 30-40% root problems begin to resolve
- Below 10-15% colonization, mycorrhizal inoculum application is recommended

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Trappe's expertise directly informs practical mycorrhizal management in cultivation. The colonization thresholds established through his research program give cultivators concrete benchmarks: if VAM colonization drops below 40%, root disease problems are likely; if it falls below 10-15%, inoculation is necessary. His student Efren Cazares trained SFI technicians in mycorrhizal identification, making this expertise commercially available to growers through soil biology testing. The recommendation to add compost, compost tea, or humic acids when colonization is between 15-40% comes from this research lineage. For any cultivation operation concerned with root health, understanding Trappe's colonization thresholds is essential for designing effective biological soil management programs.
