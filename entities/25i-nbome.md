# 25I-NBOMe

## Overview

**25I-NBOMe** (systematic name: **2-(4-iodo-2,5-dimethoxyphenyl)-N-[(2-methoxyphenyl)methyl]ethanamine**) is a potent synthetic psychedelic phenethylamine and member of the **NBOMe** (N-benzylmethoxy) family of compounds.
It acts as a highly selective and potent **5-HT₂A receptor agonist**, producing visual hallucinations, altered cognition, and intense sensory effects at extremely low doses (microgram range).
Originally developed as a research tool for mapping serotonin receptor pharmacology, 25I-NBOMe was never approved for human consumption. It appeared on the recreational drug market around 2010 and has been associated with numerous hospitalizations and deaths due to its extreme potency, narrow [[cannabis-therapeutic-index-overview]], and frequent misrepresentation as LSD.

## Chemical Properties

- **Chemical class:** Substituted phenethylamine → 2C family → NBOMe series
- **Molecular formula:** C₁₈H₂₂INO₃
- **Molecular weight:** 427.27 g/mol
- **IUPAC name:** 2-(4-iodo-2,5-dimethoxyphenyl)-N-[(2-methoxyphenyl)methyl]ethanamine
- **SMILES:** COc1ccc(cc1CNCCc2c(I)cc(OC)c(OC)c2)OC
- **Structure:** Core 2C-I (4-iodo-2,5-dimethoxyphenethylamine) with an N-(2-methoxybenzyl) substituent on the amine nitrogen. The N-benzyl group dramatically increases 5-HT₂A affinity and metabolic stability compared to the parent 2C-I.
- **Physical form:** White to off-white crystalline powder; insoluble in water, soluble in ethanol, methanol, and DMSO.
- **Stability:** Relatively stable at room temperature when protected from light and moisture. Degrays under UV exposure.

## Pharmacology

- **Primary mechanism:** Full or near-full agonist at the **5-HT₂A receptor** (Kᵢ ≈ 0.044 nM; EC₅₀ ≈ 0.1–0.5 nM), making it one of the most potent 5-HT₂A agonists known. For comparison, LSD has Kᵢ ≈ 1.7 nM at 5-HT₂A.
- **Receptor profile:** Highly selective for 5-HT₂A over 5-HT₂B and 5-HT₂C. Also shows significant affinity for 5-HT₁A (moderate), α₁-adrenergic (weak), and σ₁ receptors (weak). Minimal activity at dopaminergic and muscarinic receptors.
- **Pharmacokinetics:** Poor oral bioavailability (~10–20%) due to extensive first-pass metabolism. **Sublingual/buccal absorption** is the intended route and is significantly more efficient. Intranasal and intravenous routes deliver the full dose but carry substantially higher toxicity risk.
- **Metabolism:** Primary metabolism via **O-demethylation** (CYP2D6, CYP1A2) and N-dealkylation. The N-(2-methoxybenzyl) group is metabolically cleaved to produce 2C-I as a minor metabolite. Active metabolites may contribute to effects.
- **Onset:** 15–45 minutes sublingual; 5–15 minutes intranasal.
- **Duration:** 6–10 hours sublingual; 4–8 hours intranasal.
- **Half-life:** ~3 hours (parent compound); extended by active metabolites.

## Effects

- **Visual:** Intense and complex geometric patterns, color enhancement and shifting, patterning on surfaces, fractal visuals, synesthesia (particularly sound-to-color), and at higher doses, immersive visionary states comparable to high-dose LSD or DMT.
- **Cognitive:** Altered thought patterns, enhanced introspection, [[psilocybin-ego-dissolution-mechanisms]] at high doses, racing thoughts, time dilation, and heightened emotional sensitivity. Some users report profound mystical or religious experiences.
- **Physical:** Pupil dilation, vasoconstriction (peripheral cyanosis, cold extremities), tachycardia (100–160 bpm), hypertension, hyperthermia, jaw clenching, nausea (especially during onset), sweating, tremor, and restlessness.
- **Emotional:** Wide spectrum — from euphoria and connectedness to anxiety, paranoia, panic, and dysphoria. [[lsd-set-and-setting-framework]] are critical determinants.
- **Dose ranges (sublingual):** Threshold ~50–100 μg; light 100–250 μg; common 250–500 μg; strong 500–750 μg; heavy 750+ μg.
- **Lethal risk:** Deaths have been reported at doses estimated at 1–3 mg, though individual sensitivity varies enormously. The margin between a recreational dose and a dangerous dose is alarmingly narrow.

## Harm Reduction

- **Extreme potency:** Active in the 100–500 μg range. Accurate weighing requires analytical equipment (milligram scales are insufficient). Liquid volumetric dosing (dissolving in a known volume of alcohol or water and using a calibrated micropipette) is the only safe method.
- **Misrepresentation:** 25I-NBOMe is frequently sold on blotter paper misrepresented as "LSD." Unlike LSD (active at 50–150 μg), 25I-NBOMe at those doses is only threshold-active. Users expecting LSD who receive 25I-NBOMe may take dangerously high doses.
- **Reagent testing:** Ehrlich reagent turns **yellow-orange** with LSD but does **not** react with NBOMe compounds. The Marquis reagent shows **yellow → orange** for NBOMe. These tests can help distinguish the two but are not definitive.
- **Route risk:** Insufflation and injection dramatically increase toxicity risk and are strongly discouraged. Deaths are disproportionately associated with non-sublingual routes.
- **Hydration and temperature:** Monitor for hyperthermia and stay hydrated. Cool environment and physical rest during onset reduce complications.
- **Serotonin syndrome risk:** Dangerous interactions with MAOIs, SSRIs, tramadol, and other serotonergic drugs. Do not combine.
- **Aftereffects:** Residual anxiety, sleep disruption, and mild visual disturbances ("HPPD-like" symptoms) may persist for days to weeks after use.

## Legal Status

- **[[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]]:** Emergency scheduled as Schedule I under the DEA's temporary scheduling authority in November 2013; permanently placed in Schedule I in 2016.
- **United Nations:** Not specifically scheduled by the UN Convention on Psychotropic Substances, though it falls under general controlled substance analog provisions in many jurisdictions.
- **European Union:** Subject to EU-wide drug-related criminal offenses. Individually controlled in most member states (Germany, UK, Sweden, Poland, France, etc.).
- **China:** Scheduled as a narcotic drug; manufacturing and distribution carry severe penalties.
- **Australia:** Scheduled as a prohibited substance (Schedule 9).
- **Canada:** Controlled under the Controlled Drugs and [[controlled-substances-act-and-psychedelic-research]] (Schedule II).

## History

- **Discovery:** Synthesized by Ralf Heim at the Free University of Berlin in 2003 as part of a research program investigating 5-HT₂A receptor pharmacology. The NBOMe series was designed to improve the metabolic stability and receptor selectivity of the parent 2C compounds.
- **Research use:** Published in the pharmacological literature as a tool compound for studying 5-HT₂A receptor function and its role in consciousness, perception, and psychiatric disorders. [[neurochemical-probes-brain-imaging-pihkal]] studies using 25I-NBOMe radiolabeled derivatives helped map 5-HT₂A distribution in the human brain.
- **Recreational emergence:** First detected on the recreational market around 2010–2011, initially sold online as "research chemicals" and later on blotter paper. By 2012–2013, it was widely available internationally.
- **Deaths and toxicity:** First fatalities reported in 2012. Between 2012 and 2015, multiple deaths were attributed to 25I-NBOMe (and related NBOMe compounds), typically involving doses exceeding 1 mg, non-sublingual routes, or polysubstance use.
- **Media attention:** High-profile cases (including the 2012 death of a teenager in North Dakota) brought NBOMe compounds to public attention and accelerated scheduling actions worldwide.

## Related Compounds

- **2C-I** — the parent phenethylamine (without the N-benzyl group); ~100× less potent at 5-HT₂A; active at 10–25 mg oral. Shares the 4-iodo-2,5-dimethoxy substitution pattern.
- **25C-NBOMe** — 4-chloro analog; similar potency, slightly shorter duration (5–8 hours), often reported as more visual and less stimulating than 25I-NBOMe.
- **25B-NBOMe** — 4-bromo analog; comparable potency, associated with significant toxicity. Multiple fatalities reported. Slightly longer duration than 25I.
- **25D-NBOMe** — 2,5-dimethoxy unsubstituted analog; lower potency (~10× less than 25I), less studied, rarely encountered.
- **25H-NBOMe** — unsubstituted phenyl analog; very low potency, essentially inactive at typical NBOMe doses, rarely encountered.
- **25E-NBOMe** — 4-ethyl analog; moderate potency, less common.
- **25N-NBOMe** — 4-ethylthio analog; active at slightly higher doses than 25I.
