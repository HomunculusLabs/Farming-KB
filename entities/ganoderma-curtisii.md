# Ganoderma curtisii

## Description

Ganoderma curtisii, commonly known as golden reishi, is a lacquered polypore mushroom in the genus Ganoderma. It is closely related to the well-known reishi species ([[ganoderma-lucidum]]) and shares many of the same [[amanita-muscaria-medicinal-properties-heart-anxiety-dreams]] and cultivation characteristics. The species produces striking golden-colored antlers when grown in closed bags under elevated carbon dioxide, making it one of the most visually spectacular reishi-type mushrooms for cultivation. It is found primarily in the southeastern United States, where it fruits from hardwood stumps and buried roots.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Polyporales
- **Family:** Ganodermataceae
- **Genus:** Ganoderma
- **Species:** G. curtisii

## Key Facts

- One of several reishi species with documented medicinal properties
- Produces beautiful golden antlers when cultivated in closed bags with elevated CO2
- Mycelium is white and linear, producing a signature yellow metabolite that darkens to orange with age
- [[spore-print]] is cinnamon to chocolate brown
- Mycelium has an aggressive growth habit, capable of outcompeting most wild fungi and bacteria
- Spawn can transform into solid bricks at room temperature, demonstrating the [[mycelium-running-rhizomorphic-mycelium-structural-strength-erosion-control]] of the mycelium
- Some growers have used the solid mycelium bricks to construct living structures as demonstrations of fungal material strength
- Mycelial smell can be sour, similar to the fruitbody, and somewhat unpleasant

## Morphology

The fruitbody begins as an antler with a whitish tip, flattening to a cap positioned off-center, with white pores underneath. The growing edge is typically white or off-yellow when actively growing, then dulls and darkens at full maturity. Spores usually end up covering the entire cap surface due to convecting air currents sweeping spores up and around the cap edge. When grown in closed bags under elevated CO2, the antlers elongate dramatically, creating the characteristic golden reishi form prized by cultivators and medicinal mushroom enthusiasts.

## Cultivation Notes

### Difficulty Ratings
- **Outdoor cultivation:** 1 (easy)
- **Indoor cultivation:** 2 (moderate)

### Outdoor Cultivation
- **Substrates:** Buried hardwood logs and stumps; this mushroom will not fruit aboveground
- **Method:** Trenched log cultivation recommended; inoculate small-diameter logs, colonize 6-8 months, then completely bury in tight raft formation
- **Fruiting location:** Antlers typically form at the interface between ground and wood, on shadier sides
- **Inoculation to fruiting:** Ten to twelve months, depending on temperature and inoculation rates
- **Season:** Fruits in spring, continues producing all summer, persists into fall
- **Expected yields:** Good; reishi mushrooms are dense and prolific fruiters
- **Spawn types:** Sawdust, plug, or cardboard spawn

### Indoor Cultivation
- **Substrates:** [[oyster-mushroom-supplemented-sawdust-blocks]], sterilized in autoclave for two hours
- **Inoculation to fruiting:** Two to three weeks after complete colonization
- **Technique:** Antlers form inside closed bags and keep growing for weeks seeking exit; cutting the bag open allows mushrooms to flatten and sporulate
- **Expected yields:** Good
- **Spawn types:** Grain or supplemented sawdust spawn

## Medicinal Properties

Ganoderma curtisii is part of the reishi complex, one of the most important groups of medicinal fungi:

- Among the highest beta-glucan levels of any medicinal fungus
- Long history of use in traditional Chinese medicine as a "cure-all"
- Demonstrated ability to boost and modulate immune system function
- Regulates blood pressure, blood sugar, and cholesterol
- Provides cardiovascular and digestive support
- Contains antiviral and antibacterial properties
- Contains anti-inflammatory compounds
- Has been shown to suppress many cancer cell lines
- Best-known regulatory mechanism: blood sugar regulation, making it valuable for diabetes patients
- Hot water and alcohol extracts are the preferred method of consumption

## Mycoremediation Applications

Ganoderma curtisii mycelium shares the genus-wide properties valuable for environmental applications:

- Extremely high tensile strength suitable for mycofiltration
- Affinity for inhibiting and lysing bacterial cells
- Myceliated substrate forms an almost rubber-like material when dried
- Can be cut into custom shapes for manufactured filter modules
- Ability to bind substrate with great tenacity prevents biofilter disintegration

## Harvesting and Processing

- Can be harvested at any stage of development depending on appearance and intended use
- Antlers are believed (though not substantiated) to contain more beneficial medicinal properties
- Best harvested while still possessing growing edge, just [[partial-veil-tropism-protection-before-spore-release]]
- Retains energy that would have gone to spore production
- Low water content makes dehydration the preferred preservation method
- Can be dried and powdered or processed into hot water and alcohol extracts
