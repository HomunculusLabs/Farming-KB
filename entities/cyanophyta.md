## Cyanophyta

## Description

**Cyanophyta** (cyanobacteria, formerly called blue-green algae) is a phylum of photosynthetic bacteria that are among the most ancient and ecologically important organisms on Earth. Cyanobacteria are prokaryotes (not true algae) that were responsible for oxygenating Earth's atmosphere during the Great Oxidation Event approximately 2.4 billion years ago. In soil ecosystems, cyanobacteria are crucial primary producers and include many species capable of nitrogen fixation. They are key components of biological soil crusts in arid and semi-arid regions worldwide.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Bacteria
- **Phylum:** Cyanobacteria (Cyanophyta)
- **Classes:** Cyanophyceae (major class), others
- **Orders:** Includes Nostocales, Oscillatoriales, Chroococcales, Stigonematales, Pleurocapsales

## Key Facts

- Prokaryotic bacteria — not true algae despite the historical "blue-green algae" name.
- Among the oldest known organisms — fossil stromatolites date back 3.5 billion years.
- Responsible for Earth's oxygenated atmosphere via oxygenic photosynthesis.
- Contain chlorophyll a and phycobilins (phycocyanin gives blue-green color, phycoerythrin gives red).
- Many species fix atmospheric nitrogen (N₂ → NH₄⁺) via specialized cells called heterocysts.
- Form biological soil crusts (biocrusts) that stabilize soil surfaces in arid regions.
- Some form symbiotic relationships with fungi (lichens), plants (Azolla), and other organisms.
- Can form toxic blooms in nutrient-rich (eutrophic) water bodies.
- Cell walls contain peptidoglycan (bacterial characteristic), not cellulose.
- Include both unicellular and filamentous (chain-forming) species.

## Role in the Soil Food Web

In Lowenfels' soil food web framework, Cyanophyta play several key roles:

- **Nitrogen fixation:** Free-living cyanobacteria (e.g., *Nostoc*, *Anabaena*) convert atmospheric N₂ into ammonium, adding new nitrogen to the soil food web without chemical fertilizers.
- **Primary production:** Photosynthetic cyanobacteria on soil surfaces convert sunlight and CO₂ into organic matter, feeding the soil food web from the bottom up.
- **Soil crust formation:** Cyanobacterial filaments bind soil particles together, creating biological soil crusts that prevent erosion, improve water infiltration, and create micro-habitats for other organisms.
- **Soil moisture retention:** Biocrusts containing cyanobacteria help retain soil moisture, benefiting all soil food web organisms.
- **Organic matter contribution:** When cyanobacteria die and decompose, their biomass contributes carbon, nitrogen, and organic matter to soil.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Soil health in arid regions:** Cyanobacterial soil crusts are essential for maintaining soil fertility in dryland agriculture. Disruption of these crusts through overgrazing or tillage reduces nitrogen inputs and increases erosion.
- **Rice paddies:** Cyanobacteria (especially *Nostoc* and *Anabaena*) are natural nitrogen fixers in rice paddy ecosystems, contributing significantly to rice nutrition without synthetic fertilizers.
- **Azolla-cyanobacteria symbiosis:** *Azolla* ferns housing *Nostoc azollae* are used as green manure in rice cultivation, providing up to 40 kg N/ha/year.
- **Compost and compost tea:** Cyanobacteria may be present in compost and contribute nitrogen when compost tea is applied to soil surfaces.
- **Mushroom cultivation:** Generally not directly involved in mushroom substrate preparation, but cyanobacterial nitrogen fixation benefits outdoor mushroom beds and log cultivation in forest settings.
- **Bioremediation:** Cyanobacteria are used in wastewater treatment and soil rehabilitation projects.
- **Biofertilizers:** Commercial cyanobacterial biofertilizers are available for rice and other crops.

## Environmental Conditions

- **Light:** Photosynthetic — require adequate light for growth and nitrogen fixation.
- **Moisture:** Require moisture; most active during and after rainfall or irrigation.
- **Temperature:** Thermally tolerant — some species thrive in hot springs above 70°C; most soil species active 15–35°C.
- **pH:** Tolerant of wide pH range; some species prefer alkaline conditions.
- **Nutrients:** Can grow in extremely nutrient-poor environments due to nitrogen fixation capability.
