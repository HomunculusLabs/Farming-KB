## Ophiostoma ulmi

## Description

**Ophiostoma ulmi** is an ascomycete fungus responsible for Dutch elm disease, one of the most devastating tree diseases in history. The pathogen is vectored primarily by bark beetles of the genus *Scolytus* and *Hylurgopinus*, which introduce the fungus into the vascular system of elm trees (*Ulmus* species) during feeding. The fungus colonizes the xylem vessels, producing cerato-ulmin toxins and causing tylosis formation that blocks water transport, leading to wilting and eventual tree death. Dutch elm disease has killed hundreds of millions of elm trees across the Northern Hemisphere since its emergence in the early 20th century.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Eukaryota
- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** [[ascomycota]]
- **Class:** Sordariomycetes
- **Order:** Ophiostomatales
- **Family:** Ophiostomataceae
- **Genus:** *Ophiostoma*
- **Species:** *O. ulmi*

**Note:** Two other closely related species, *O. novo-ulmi* (more aggressive) and *O. himal-ulmi*, also cause Dutch elm disease. *O. novo-ulmi* has largely replaced *O. ulmi* in most regions due to its greater virulence.

## Key Facts

- First identified in the Netherlands in 1921, giving rise to the name "Dutch" elm disease
- Has killed over 100 million elm trees in Europe and [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]] since the 1920s
- Spread by elm bark beetles (*Scolytus scolytus*, *S. multistriatus*, *Hylurgopinus rufipes*)
- Produces cerato-ulmin, a hydrophobin toxin that contributes to pathogenicity
- Blocks xylem vessels through fungal growth and host tylosis response
- Can also spread between adjacent trees through root grafts
- *O. novo-ulmi* is now the dominant pathogenic species in most regions
- Resistant elm cultivars have been developed through breeding programs
- Mycoviruses (d-factors) have been explored as potential [[singh-entomopathogenic-fungi-biocontrol-agents]] against the pathogen

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

*Ophiostoma ulmi* is significant in [[mycology]] and plant pathology:

1. **Epidemic Disease Model:** Dutch elm disease serves as a classic case study in epidemic plant pathology, demonstrating how introduced pathogens can devastate ecologically and culturally important tree species.

2. **Vector-Mediated Transmission:** The fungus-beetle mutualism is a model system for understanding insect-vectored plant diseases. Bark beetles feed [[brassinosteroid-effects-on-fungal-fruiting-morphology]] structures and carry spores to new hosts.

3. **[[fukuoka-textdoc-arrowhead-scale-ladybug-biological-control]]:** Mycoviruses that reduce *Ophiostoma* virulence (d-factors) represent an early example of transmissible hypovirulence, a concept also explored in chestnut blight biocontrol.

4. **Tree Breeding:** Development of Dutch elm disease-resistant elm cultivars demonstrates the integration of pathology with horticulture and urban forestry.

5. **Ecosystem Impact:** The near-elimination of mature elms from European and [[growing-gourmet-north-american-reishi-species]] landscapes profoundly affected dependent organisms, including specialized mycorrhizal and epiphytic fungal communities.
