## Overview

*Campanula uniflora* L., the one-flowered bellflower or arctic bellflower, is a diminutive perennial herb with a circumboreal distribution. It occurs in arctic and alpine regions across North America, Europe, and Asia.

The species is one of the smallest members of the genus, rarely exceeding 10 cm in height. It produces a single (rarely two) nodding blue flower atop each slender stem, giving rise to both its common and scientific names.

It was described by Carl Linnaeus in his 1753 Species Plantarum and has remained taxonomically stable since. The species is widespread in suitable habitat across the Northern Hemisphere.

Despite its tiny stature, it plays an important role in arctic and alpine plant communities as a reliable nectar source. It is adapted to some of the harshest growing conditions on Earth.

The species is one of the few campanulas that successfully colonize the high Arctic, reaching 83°N in Greenland. At these latitudes, [[psilocybin-psilocin-variation-across-flushes]] its vast circumboreal range, suggesting recent post-glacial expansion from a single refugium.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Campanula uniflora* has a circumboreal distribution, occurring across the Arctic from Greenland and northern Canada through Scandinavia to Siberia. It also grows in alpine areas of the Rocky Mountains, the Alps, and the mountains of central Asia.

In North America, it ranges from Alaska across northern Canada to Labrador and Greenland, extending south in the Rocky Mountains to Colorado. In Europe, it occurs in Iceland, Scandinavia, and the Scottish Highlands.

It grows in alpine meadows, tundra, fellfields, gravelly moraines, and rocky ridges from sea level in the Arctic to 3,500 m in southern mountains. The species prefers moist but well-drained, slightly acidic to circumneutral substrates.

Populations at the southern edge of the range in the Rocky Mountains and Alps are restricted to high elevations. These disjunct populations represent Pleistocene relicts from more widespread glacial distributions.

Associated species vary across its range but commonly include *Dryas octopetala*, *Silene acaulis*, *Saxifraga oppositifolia*, and *Carex* spp. in arctic and alpine communities.

The species shows broad ecological amplitude compared to many alpine endemics. It tolerates both acidic and basic substrates and occurs from snowbed margins to exposed ridge crests.
