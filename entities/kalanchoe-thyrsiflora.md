## Overview

Kalanchoe thyrsiflora, commonly known as the paddle plant, flapjack plant, or desert cabbage, is a succulent perennial in the family Crassulaceae. It is native to South Africa, Eswatini, and Mozambique, where it grows on rocky outcrops and exposed slopes.

The species is recognized by its large, round, flat leaves arranged in dense rosettes that resemble stacked paddles or flapjacks. In strong light, leaf margins turn a vivid crimson to orange-red, creating a striking bicolor effect.

Kalanchoe thyrsiflora is widely cultivated as an ornamental succulent for its architectural form and dramatic coloration. It is frequently confused with the closely related [[kalanchoe-luciae]], which has similar paddle-shaped leaves but differs in flower structure and leaf orientation.

The species produces a tall, cylindrical inflorescence bearing densely packed yellow-green flowers with a distinctive sweet fragrance. After flowering, the main rosette typically dies, but the plant produces numerous offsets that continue growing.

In the horticultural trade, Kalanchoe thyrsiflora is frequently sold under the name "flapjacks" or "paddle plant." However, many plants sold under these names are actually the closely related Kalanchoe luciae, which is more commonly cultivated.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Kingdom:** Plantae · **Family:** Crassulaceae · **Subfamily:** Kalanchooideae · **Genus:** Kalanchoe · **Species:** K. thyrsiflora Harv.

Authority: Harvey, 1862. The species epithet "thyrsiflora" refers to the thyrse-shaped (cylindrical, branched) inflorescence.

Rosettes are stemless or short-stemmed, reaching 30–60 cm in diameter. Leaves are large, broadly obovate to nearly circular, 10–20 cm long and 8–15 cm wide, with a powdery white bloom (farina) on the surface.

Leaves are arranged in a dense, overlapping rosette that gives the appearance of stacked pancakes. The white farina rubs off easily and serves as a natural sunscreen protecting the photosynthetic tissue beneath.

In bright light, leaf margins and tips develop intense red to orange coloration due to anthocyanin accumulation. Shaded plants remain uniformly pale green to gray-green.

Inflorescences are erect, cylindrical thyrses reaching 60–120 cm tall. Flowers are tubular, 15–20 mm long, pale yellow-green, and produce a strong sweet scent that attracts bees and butterflies.

The species can be distinguished from Kalanchoe luciae by its more rounded leaves with heavier farina coating and its fragrant yellow-green flowers (K. luciae has scentless white to pale yellow flowers).

## Distribution and Habitat

Kalanchoe thyrsiflora occurs across a broad range in southern Africa, from the Eastern Cape through KwaZulu-Natal, Mpumalanga, and Limpopo provinces of South Africa into Eswatini and southern Mozambique.

It colonizes rocky outcrops, cliff faces, and well-drained slopes in grassland, thicket, and savanna vegetation types. Plants prefer north-facing exposures with full sun and excellent drainage.

The species is adapted to warm, seasonally dry conditions with summer rainfall. Annual precipitation in its native range varies from 500–900 mm, primarily falling as convective thunderstorms from October to March.

Plants grow at elevations from sea level to approximately 1,500 m. They are particularly abundant on quartzite and granite outcrops where thin soils and full sun create ideal conditions.

The species is not considered threatened in its native range due to its broad distribution and abundance. However, localized populations may be affected by overcollection for the horticultural trade.

In favorable conditions, plants can form large colonies covering entire rock outcrops. The species shows some weedy tendency in disturbed habitats within its native range, colonizing road cuts and eroded slopes.
