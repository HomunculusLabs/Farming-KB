## Soil Foodweb Institute (SFI)

## Description

[[soil-food-web]] Inc. (SFI) is the organization founded by Dr. [[elaine-ingham]] to provide commercial soil biology assessment services based on direct microscopy methods. SFI measures total and active bacteria, total and active fungi, protozoa (flagellates, amoebae, ciliates), nematodes (by functional group), and [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-colonization-patterns]] (VAM, ectomycorrhizal, ericoid) in soil, compost, and compost tea samples. The organization's methodology is grounded in the understanding that the biomass and activity of different organism groups, rather than simple plate count numbers, determine soil health and plant productivity.

## Classification

- **Type**: Organization / Laboratory
- **Founder**: Dr. Elaine Ingham
- **Field**: Soil biology assessment
- **Methodology**: Direct microscopy (not plate counts)
- **Services**: Soil, compost, and compost tea analysis

## Key Facts

- SFI uses direct microscopy to assess soil biology: diluting samples, staining subsamples, and measuring length, width, and number of individuals of each organism group.
- Based on morphology, SFI identifies morpho-species and uses activity stains to distinguish living, functioning organisms from the total set.
- SFI extracts active nematodes from soil and identifies them by morphology. Protozoa are also identified morphologically.
- Mycorrhizal colonization is assessed by type (VAM, ectomycorrhizal, ericoid), with disease fungi in roots also identified where possible.
- SFI maintains a database (partially published) showing how active and total organisms change through the year and with soil type, season, and plant communities.
- The original proof of concept was the Ingham et al. 1985 paper in Ecological Monograph.
- SFI data demonstrates that higher biomass of bacteria, fungi, or numbers of [[teaming-with-microbes-protozoa-and-nematodes]] improves disease protection, nutrient retention, [[aact-nitrogen-forms-microbial-nutrient-cycling-ingham]], and soil aggregation.
- SFI technicians were trained by Dr. Efren Cazares (Jim Trappe's student) for mycorrhizal identification.
- SFI is developing [[biodiversity-of-fungi-pcr-molecular-methods-fungal-diversity]] for species-level identification of bacteria and fungi, not yet ready for commercialization at the time of writing.
- Dr. Ingham envisions SFI directing clients to companies selling specific inocula of missing bacteria or fungi once [[biodiversity-fungal-molecular-identification-dna-barcoding]] is available.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Soil diagnostics**: SFI provides the primary commercial service for biological soil assessment based on direct microscopy, enabling evidence-based soil management decisions.
- **Compost tea quality**: SFI testing verifies that compost tea machines extract adequate organisms (not just bacteria but fungi, protozoa, and nematodes) from compost.
- **Threshold-based management**: SFI's colonization thresholds (mycorrhizal below 15% inoculate, 15-40% add compost, above 40% healthy) provide actionable guidance for growers.
- **Machine validation**: SFI tests compost tea machines for reliability (repeatability) and organism extraction capability, providing accountability for equipment manufacturers.
- **Bridge to practice**: SFI translates academic soil ecology research into practical recommendations that farmers and compost tea producers can implement immediately.
