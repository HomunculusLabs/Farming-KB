# Araucaria heterophylla

## Description

Araucaria heterophylla, commonly known as the [[norfolk-island-pine]], is a large evergreen conifer endemic to Norfolk Island in the Pacific Ocean. In permaculture literature, it is classified as a **wind-tolerant (wind-fast)** species, one of the frontline plants capable of withstanding severe coastal and maritime winds with minimal yield reduction. Mollison specifically recommends it for alternate (zig-zag) windbreak plantings on seafronts to reduce salt burn and provide shelter for denser plantings behind.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Division**: Pinophyta
- **Family**: Araucariaceae
- **Genus**: Araucaria
- **Species**: A. heterophylla
- **Common names**: Norfolk Island pine, star pine, triangle tree
- **Growth form**: Large evergreen conifer, 50-65 m in native habitat

## Key Facts

- Endemic to Norfolk Island (approximately 29 degrees S, 167 degrees E) in the southwestern Pacific Ocean.
- Grows to heights of 50-65 m in its native habitat, with a distinctive symmetrical, pyramidal crown that maintains its form even in exposed conditions.
- Classified as **wind-tolerant and wind-fast**: yields are little affected by strong winds, making it an ideal frontline species in exposed coastal windbreak systems.
- Used in **Configuration B windbreaks** (alternate zig-zag planting of very permeable trees), specifically for seafront systems to reduce salt burn and provide shelter for denser tree plantings on islands and coasts.
- Planted alongside Pinus and Casuarina in alternate rows, forming a permeable barrier that reduces wind speed without creating damaging turbulence on the leeward side.
- Salt-spray tolerant: the waxy, scale-like mature foliage resists salt deposition and desiccation from maritime winds.
- Wood is soft, light, and straight-grained; historically used for ship masts and construction on Norfolk Island.
- Widely planted as an ornamental in subtropical and tropical coastal cities worldwide, including Brisbane, Honolulu, San Diego, and the coastal Mediterranean.
- Juvenile foliage is needle-like and softer, while mature foliage consists of overlapping, scale-like leaves that are more wind-resistant.

## Role in Permaculture Windbreak Design

Mollison identifies Araucaria heterophylla as a key species in the **first line of defence** against maritime winds. Its role in Configuration B is critical:

- The zig-zag planting pattern allows 40-70% wind permeability, preventing turbulent overturn on the sheltered side.
- Protects more vulnerable, denser tree species planted behind it from salt burn and mechanical wind damage.
- Functions as a "first-line seafront system" to reduce salt burn before winds reach productive crop trees.
- Can serve dual purpose as windbreak and timber source in integrated agroforestry systems on tropical islands.
- The permeable crown design means the windbreak effect extends further downwind than a solid barrier would achieve.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Coastal agroforestry**: Essential for establishing productive agricultural zones on exposed tropical and subtropical islands where salt-laden winds would otherwise prevent crop cultivation.
- **Microclimate engineering**: The permeable crown allows airflow while reducing wind speed, creating conditions where tropical crops (coconut, banana, papaya) can be grown behind the windbreak.
- **Ectomycorrhizal associations**: As a member of the Araucariaceae, A. heterophylla forms ectomycorrhizal relationships with specific fungal partners, contributing to soil food web complexity in coastal plantings.
- **Soil stabilisation**: Deep root systems anchor sandy coastal soils, reducing erosion from both wind and wave action on exposed islands and coastlines.
- **Canopy habitat**: Mature trees provide roosting and nesting sites for birds and bats that contribute to pest control and pollination in adjacent crop areas.

## Cultivation Notes

- Hardy in USDA Zones 10-11; cannot tolerate prolonged frost (damaged below -2 degrees C).
- Prefers deep, well-drained sandy loam but tolerates poor coastal soils and limestone substrates.
- Full sun to partial shade; salt-wind exposure does not significantly reduce growth rate.
- Slow-growing initially, accelerating after establishment to 30-60 cm per year in favourable conditions.
- Propagated from seed only; cuttings rarely succeed. Fresh seed germinates in 10-30 days.
- Spacing in windbreak: 3-5 m apart in zig-zag rows for optimal wind permeability.
- Requires adequate space: root spread can extend well beyond the canopy drip line.
- Drought-tolerant once established but benefits from regular watering during the first 2-3 years after planting.
