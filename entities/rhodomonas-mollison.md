# Rhodomonas

## Description

Rhodomonas is a genus of cryptophyte algae found in freshwater and marine environments, named for their characteristic reddish coloration from phycoerythrin pigments. Mollison lists Rhodomonas as one of the typical organisms present in open sewage treatment ponds, contributing to the phytoplankton community in faculative and aerobic pond systems.

## Classification

- **Domain**: Eukaryota
- **Phylum**: Cryptophyta
- **Class**: Cryptophyceae
- **Order**: Cryptomonadales
- **Family**: Cryptomonadaceae
- **Genus**: *Rhodomonas*

## Key Facts

- Rhodomonas species are small flagellated algae (typically 8-20 um) with distinctive red or pinkish coloration from phycoerythrin.
- Cryptophytes are known for their nutritional quality as food for zooplankton, rich in polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) and amino acids.
- They are mixotrophic, capable of both photosynthesis and consuming bacteria, making them versatile in nutrient processing.
- Rhodomonas is widely used in aquaculture as a live feed for rotifers, copepods, and bivalve larvae due to its high nutritional value.
- In natural waters, cryptophytes often thrive in cooler seasons and deeper waters where other algae cannot compete effectively.
- Their cell covering (periplast) consists of proteinaceous plates, unlike the silica frustules of diatoms.

## Role in Wastewater Treatment

Mollison includes Rhodomonas among the typical organisms in faculative and aerobic sewage treatment ponds:

1. **Oxygen production**: Photosynthetic activity aerates pond water, supporting aerobic bacterial [[decomposition]].
2. **Nutrient uptake**: Efficiently absorbs dissolved nitrogen and phosphorus compounds.
3. **High-quality food source**: Exceptional nutritional profile supports robust zooplankton populations, especially Daphnia and copepods.
4. **Mixotrophic processing**: Can consume bacteria directly, providing an additional pathway for organic matter removal.
5. **Seasonal resilience**: Thrives in cooler conditions, helping maintain treatment efficiency during winter months.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Aquaculture feed**: Rhodomonas is commercially cultured as live feed in aquaculture hatcheries, directly relevant to integrated permaculture aquaculture systems.
- **Constructed wetlands**: Cryptophytes contribute to diverse phytoplankton communities that enhance biological water treatment.
- **Nutrient cycling**: Their mixotrophic nature makes them efficient at converting both dissolved nutrients and bacterial biomass into zooplankton-accessible food.
- **Compost tea systems**: Similar cryptophyte organisms may play roles in aerated compost tea production where water-based nutrient cycling occurs.

## Ecological Notes

Rhodomonas and other cryptophytes are considered highly edible algae for most zooplankton grazers, unlike some cyanobacteria and large colonial green algae that resist grazing. This makes them particularly valuable in treatment pond food webs, as nutrients absorbed by Rhodomonas are efficiently transferred to higher trophic levels.
