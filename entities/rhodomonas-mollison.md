# Rhodomonas

## Description

Rhodomonas is a genus of cryptophyte algae found in freshwater and marine environments, named for their characteristic reddish coloration from phycoerythrin pigments. Mollison lists Rhodomonas as one of the typical organisms present in open sewage treatment ponds, contributing to the phytoplankton community in faculative and aerobic pond systems.

## Classification

- **Domain**: Eukaryota
- **Phylum**: Cryptophyta
- **Class**: Cryptophyceae
- **Order**: Cryptomonadales
- **Family**: Cryptomonadaceae
- **Genus**: *Rhodomonas*

## Key Facts

- Rhodomonas species are small flagellated algae (typically 8-20 um) with distinctive red or pinkish coloration from phycoerythrin.
- Cryptophytes are known for their nutritional quality as food for zooplankton, rich in polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) and amino acids.
- They are mixotrophic, capable of both photosynthesis and consuming bacteria, making them versatile in nutrient processing.
- Rhodomonas is widely used in aquaculture as a live feed for rotifers, copepods, and bivalve larvae due to its high nutritional value.
- In natural waters, cryptophytes often thrive in cooler seasons and deeper waters where other algae cannot compete effectively.
- Their cell covering (periplast) consists of proteinaceous plates, unlike the silica frustules of diatoms.
