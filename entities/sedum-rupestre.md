## Overview

**Sedum rupestre** (syn. *Petrosedum rupestre*), commonly known as **reflexed stonecrop** or **Jenny's stonecrop**, is a mat-forming evergreen succulent in the family Crassulaceae. Native to Europe and western Asia, it is widely cultivated as a drought-tolerant groundcover and green-roof plant.

The species is notable for its bluish-gray, needle-like leaves arranged in dense, overlapping whorls along prostrate stems. Its fleshy foliage stores water efficiently, enabling survival in rocky, nutrient-poor substrates with minimal rainfall.

Yellow, star-shaped flowers appear in midsummer on upright inflorescences rising 15–25 cm above the foliage mat. The blooms attract pollinators including bees, butterflies, and hoverflies, making it valuable in wildlife gardens.

The plant typically forms mats 5–10 cm tall and spreads 30–60 cm wide. It is semi-evergreen in colder climates, retaining foliage in mild winters but dying back partially in harsh conditions.

It has gained the Royal Horticultural Society Award of Garden Merit for its reliability and ornamental value. The species is widely available from commercial nurseries and specialist succulent growers.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Family:** Crassulaceae
- **Genus:** *Sedum* (section *Sedum*; sometimes placed in *Petrosedum*)
- **Species:** *Sedum rupestre* L.
- **Synonyms:** *Petrosedum rupestre* (L.) Grulich, *Sedum reflexum* auct.

Stems are prostrate to ascending, reaching 10–30 cm long, and root at the nodes where they contact soil. Leaves are cylindrical to linear-lanceolate, 6–12 mm long, fleshy, and grey-green to blue-green with a glaucous bloom.

Inflorescences are terminal cymes with 3–6 branches, each bearing 5–12 flowers. Individual flowers are approximately 1.5 cm across with five bright yellow petals and five sepals.

Chromosomes are reported as 2n = 36 in most European populations. The species exhibits modest morphological [[berry-growing]], particularly in the northeastern United States and eastern Canada. There it can be found on old walls, roadsides, and disturbed stony ground.

In its introduced range, *Sedum rupestre* rarely becomes invasive due to its preference for dry, disturbed habitats. It coexists with other stress-tolerant species such as mosses, lichens, and annual herbs.

The species shows a strong affinity for calcium-rich substrates in the wild. Populations on limestone pavement and chalk downs are particularly vigorous.

## Cultivation

Reflexed stonecrop is among the easiest succulents to grow, thriving in full sun and sharply drained soil. It is hardy to USDA zones 4–9 and tolerates temperatures below −30 °C when established in well-drained sites.

Propagation is straightforward from stem cuttings, division, or seed. Cuttings root readily within two weeks when placed on moist gritty compost.

Division of established mats can be performed in spring or early autumn. Seeds require light for germination and should be surface-sown on sterile, gritty compost.

In cultivation, the cultivar **'Angelina'** is especially popular. It produces golden-yellow to chartreuse foliage that turns orange-red in cool weather, providing multi-season interest.

Another notable cultivar, **'Blue Spruce'**, features silvery-blue foliage reminiscent of conifer needles. It forms a dense, low mat and is widely used in trough gardens and green-roof installations.

The species is highly resistant to deer, rabbits, and drought. Overwatering and poor drainage are the primary causes of failure, promoting root rot and stem collapse.

No serious insect pests or diseases are commonly reported. Mealybugs may occasionally colonize plants grown indoors or in overly humid conditions.

Container-grown specimens benefit from a gritty, free-draining [[query-how-do-green-roofs-and-living-walls-benefit-urban-environments]] retaining walls.

Flowers are a nectar source for diverse pollinator species during midsummer when fewer plants are in bloom. The foliage serves as microhabitat for small invertebrates and overwintering [[fukuoka-natural-farming-temperate-climates]].
