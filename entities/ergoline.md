Ergoline alkaloids exhibit activity at serotonin, dopamine, and adrenergic

## Chemical Struct [[symbiotic-fungi-mycorrhizal-types-and-classification]] tion

The ergoline nucleus consists of four fused rings designated A through D:
a benzene ring (A), a pyrrole ring (B), a pyridine ring (C), and a
piperidine ring (D). The basic tetracyclic skeleton has the molecular
formula C₁₅H₁₈N₂.

**IUPAC name:** 6-methyl-4,6,6a,7,8,9,10,10a-octahydroindolo[4,3-fg]
quinoline. Molecular weight: 226.32 g/mol.

Ergolines are structurally related to tryptamines — the indole portion
(rings A and B) is identical to the tryptamine backbone, while rings C
and D form a quinoline-piperidine extension that confers receptor
specificity.

### Structural Classes

1. **Clavines** — Simple ergolines without the C-8 carboxyl group.
   Include [[agroclavine]], [[elymoclavine]], and chanoclavine.
   Biosynthetic precursors to all more complex ergolines.

2. **Lysergic acid amides (lysergamides)** — Ergolines with a carboxyl
   group at C-8, forming amide bonds with various amines.
   Include ergine (LSA), LSD, and ergometrine.

3. **Ergopeptines** — Complex ergolines where lysergic acid forms an
   amide with a tripeptide cyclol moiety.
   Include ergotamine, ergocristine, ergocornine, and ergocryptine.

4. **Modified ergolines** — Semi-synthetic derivatives with altered
   ring systems, developed for clinical specificity.
   Include pergolide, cabergoline, and lisuride.

## Pharmacology and Receptor Activity

Ergoline alkaloids are defined by their polypharmacology across multiple
monoamine receptor families. This promiscuity underlies both their
therapeutic utility and their toxicity profile.

**Serotonin receptors:** Most ergolines are partial agonists or antagonists
at 5-HT receptors, particularly 5-HT₁A, 5-HT₂A, 5-HT₂B, and 5-HT₂C.
Psychedelic activity is mediated primarily through 5-HT₂A agonism.
Chronic 5-HT₂B agonism causes cardiac valvulopathy — a dose-limiting
toxicity for clinical ergolines.

**Dopamine receptors:** Ergolines show variable activity at D₁, D₂, D₃,
and D₄ receptors. D₂ agonism produces antiparkinsonian and
prolactin-suppressing effects; D₁ agonism contributes to
psychedelic and cardiovascular effects.

**Adrenergic receptors:** Alpha-adrenergic agonism produces
vasoconstriction and uterotonic activity. This is the basis for
ergometrine's use in obstetrics and ergotamine's use in migraine.

The unique pharmacological profile of each ergoline derivative is
determined by the substituents on the amide nitrogen and the stereochemistry
at C-8 (R vs. S configuration of lysergic vs. isolysergic acid).

## Natural Occurrence and Biosynthesis

Ergoline alkaloids are produced by three groups of organisms:

- **Clavicipitaceous fungi** — *[[claviceps-purpurea]]* (ergot fungus) on
  cereal grasses; *Neotyphodium* and *Epichloë* endophytes in grasses.
- **Higher plants** — *Ipomoea* spp. (morning glories), *[[argyreia-nervosa]]*
  (Hawaiian baby woodrose), *[[rivea-corymbosa]]* (ololiuqui).
- **Ascomycete fungi** — *[[aspergillus-fumigatus]]* and related saprobes.

Biosynthesis proceeds from L-tryptophan via prenylation by dimethylallyl
tryptophan synthase (DMATS), followed by methylation, oxidation, and
ring closure to form the tetracyclic scaffold. Downstream modifications
produce the various structural classes.

The biosynthetic gene clusters are well-characterized in *Claviceps* and
*Epichloë*, containing enzymes for prenylation, methyltransferase,
oxidase, and peptide ligase activities.

## Historical Significance

Ergoline alkaloids have shaped human history through three distinct
pathways: ergotism epidemics, pharmaceutical development, and the
psychedelic era.

**Ergotism** — Consumption of ergot-contaminated grain caused mass
poisonings throughout medieval Europe. Two forms: gangrenous (St. Anthony's
Fire) and convulsive (hallucinations, spasms). Outbreaks killed thousands
and influenced the Salem witch trials hypothesis.

**Pharmaceuticals** — Isolation of ergot alkaloids in the early 20th
century led to drugs for migraine (ergotamine), postpartum hemorrhage
(ergometrine), and Parkinson's disease (bromocriptine, pergolide).

**Psychedelics** — [[albert-hofmann]]'s synthesis of LSD-25 in 1938 and
discovery of its psychoactive effects in 1943 launched psychedelic
research. LSD became central to 1960s counterculture and remains the most
studied psychedelic compound.
