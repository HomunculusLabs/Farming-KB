## Cistanche tubulosa

## Overview

**Cistanche tubulosa** (Schenk) Wight, known in Chinese as **肉苁蓉** (ròu cōng róng, "desert ginseng") and in Uyghur medicine as **ruo cong rong**, is a holoparasitic desert plant in the family Orobanchaceae.
It is one of the most important tonic herbs in Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) and Traditional Uyghur Medicine, where the dried fleshy stem has been used for over 1,800 years as a kidney yang tonic, anti-aging remedy, and treatment for sexual dysfunction and constipation.

Unlike most plants, *C. tubulosa* lacks chlorophyll entirely and obtains all nutrients and water by parasitizing the roots of desert shrubs, primarily **Haloxylon ammodendron** (saxaul) and **Tamarix** species.
It is native to the arid regions of Central Asia, including the Taklamakan Desert of Xinjiang, China, where it has been the subject of large-scale cultivation efforts due to severe overharvesting of wild populations.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Clade:** Tracheophytes → Angiosperms → Eudicots → Asterids
- **Order:** Lamiales
- **Family:** Orobanchaceae
- **Genus:** *Cistanche* Hoffmanns. & Link
- **Species:** *C. tubulosa* (Schenk) Wight (1850)
- **Synonyms:** *Cistanche ambigua* (Bunge) Beck; *C. salsa* (C.A. Mey.) Beck var. *tubulosa* (Schenk) Z.Y. Zhang
- **Note:** Often confused with *Cistanche deserticola* Y.C. Ma, the other pharmacopoeial species. The 2005 Chinese Pharmacopoeia added *C. tubulosa* as an official substitute due to declining wild *C. deserticola* stocks.

## Morphology

- **Growth habit:** Obligate holoparasite; no leaves, stems, or photosynthetic tissue above ground until flowering. The parasite exists entirely below ground, attached to host roots, for most of its [[plant-nutrient-uptake-mechanisms]].

## Distribution and Habitat

- **Native range:** Northwest China (Xinjiang — Hotan, Minfeng, Qiemo, Turpan), Inner Mongolia, Gansu; also found in Iran, Afghanistan, Pakistan, and Central Asian republics.
- **Habitat:** Sandy deserts, gravelly desert plains, and semi-fixed dunes at 225–1,200 m elevation.
