# Soil Erosion

## Description

Soil erosion is the loss of topsoil through the action of wind, water, and chemical degradation. Mollison identifies soil erosion as one of the central threats to permanent agriculture, noting that "soil creation is one of the central themes of [[erik-van-der-werf]] (Agomeda Agricultural Project, Ghana, 1985-86) showed that permanently mulched and cropped soils achieved bulk density of 0.92 g/cc versus 1.35 for annually burnt bush — even with active cropping.

## Relevance to Cultivation

- **Prevention strategies**: Windbreaks, tree crops, fast-spreading grasses for wind erosion; permanent crop, terracing, forestry for water erosion.
- **Gully repair**: Diversion and spreader drains plus gabions to heal scoured areas.
- **Tunnel erosion**: De-stocking, contour drainage, establishment of deep-rooted plants.
- **Salinity**: Reafforestation to lower groundwater tables, deep interceptor drains to cut off salt seepages.
- **Organic matter management**: Mulching, [[charles-arden-clarke-david-hodges-mollison]]
