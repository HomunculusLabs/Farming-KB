## Description

The Anasazi Spiral is a petroglyph (rock carving) created by the Anasazi culture of the North American Southwest. It is inscribed on a rock surface near the top of a mesa in desert country and functions as a sophisticated long-term solar and lunar calendar. The spiral features 19 intercepts along its horizontal axis, which correspond to the 18.6-year lunar cycle — the period of the moon's nodal precession caused by the sway of Earth's axis.

Three carefully balanced and shaped rock slabs serve as gnomons. These cast moon-shadows and direct vertical daggers of sunlight to specific points on the spiral. The shadow positions mark both solar events (equinoxes and solstices) and the 19-year lunar cycle, encoding a practical long-term calendar for regulating planting, ceremonies, and the prediction of drought and flood cycles.

## Classification

- **Type**: Archaeoastronomical artifact / petroglyph calendar
- **Culture**: Anasazi (Ancestral Puebloan), North American Southwest
- **Period**: Pre-Columbian, exact dating uncertain
- **Category**: Indigenous knowledge system, pattern-based information encoding

## Key Facts

- Features 19 intercept points on the horizontal axis matching the 18.6-year lunar precession cycle
- Three shaped rock slabs act as gnomons casting both lunar shadows and solar light daggers
- Records both solar (equinox, solstice) and lunar (nodal cycle) events
- Predicts the 19-year cycle of drought and flood — a phenomenon modern science only recently understood
- Demonstrates that pre-literate societies achieved sophisticated astronomical observation using pattern encoding
- The Anasazi culture is now extinct; the spiral's significance was decoded by artist Anna Soaer

## Design and Construction

The gnomon arrangement uses the natural curvature of the rock slabs to focus light into precise daggers of sunlight. Each gnomon serves a dual function:

1. **Lunar shadow casting**: The moon's shadow falls on different points of the spiral as the moon's elevation changes across the 18.6-year cycle
2. **Solar light direction**: Vertical daggers of sunlight mark solar events on the spiral face

The simplicity of the spiral form belies the extraordinary complexity of the information encoded — representing what would require a small library if expressed in alphabetical and numerical symbols.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

The Anasazi Spiral exemplifies the permaculture principle that pattern understanding is a primary tool for sustainable land management. Bill Mollison references it as evidence that:

- **Pattern encoding** can store complex environmental knowledge in compact, memorable forms accessible to entire communities
- **Long-term climatic cycles** (drought and flood) can be tracked and predicted without written records or instruments
- **Planting calendars** derived from astronomical observation enable agricultural planning across generations
- **Indigenous knowledge systems** often match or exceed the precision of modern scientific measurements for local conditions

The spiral demonstrates that permaculture design can draw on ancient pattern-based approaches to encode and transmit practical ecological knowledge, rather than relying solely on written or digital records.

## Cultural Significance

The Anasazi Spiral challenges the assumption that complex scientific understanding requires literacy or numerical systems. It demonstrates that pattern-based knowledge systems can:

- Encode multi-decadal environmental cycles
- Remain functional over centuries without maintenance of written records
- Serve entire communities as shared public knowledge
- Integrate astronomical, agricultural, and ceremonial functions into a single artifact

## Modern Discovery

The significance of the Anasazi Spiral was not recognised by mainstream archaeology until artist **Anna Soaer**, using intuitive observational skills, decoded the relationship between the gnomons, the spiral, and the lunar cycle. This discovery was featured on ABC TV Science programme (Australia, 20th January 1984), highlighting how pattern literacy — not just scientific training — can reveal encoded knowledge in ancient artifacts.
