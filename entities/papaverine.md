Papaverine is classified pharmacologically as a non-nitrogenous phosphodiesterase inhibitor with direct vasodilatory and antispasmodic properties.

It has been used clinically for over a century in the treatment of vascular spasms, gastrointestinal cramps, and erectile dysfunction.

## Chemical Properties

Papaverine has the molecular formula C20H21NO4 with a molar mass of 339.39 g/mol.

Its IUPAC name is 1-[(3,4-dimethoxyphenyl)methyl]-6,7-dimethoxyisoquinoline.

The compound features a benzylisoquinoline core with four methoxy substituents distributed across the isoquinoline and benzyl rings.

Papaverine forms white crystalline or powder-like aggregates with a melting point of 147 °C.

It is practically insoluble in water but soluble in ethanol, chloroform, and dilute acids as the hydrochloride salt.

The hydrochloride salt (papaverine HCl) is the standard pharmaceutical form, with improved aqueous solubility suitable for injection.

## Pharmacology

Papaverine acts primarily as a non-selective phosphodiesterase (PDE) inhibitor, increasing intracellular levels of cyclic AMP and cyclic GMP.

Elevated cyclic nucleotide levels lead to relaxation of vascular and visceral smooth muscle through activation of protein kinase A and protein kinase G pathways.

Papaverine inhibits multiple PDE isoforms including PDE3, PDE4, PDE5, and PDE10, with IC50 values in the low micromolar range.

In addition to PDE inhibition, papaverine directly blocks voltage-gated calcium channels and interferes with calcium uptake in smooth muscle cells.

The compound also demonstrates weak adenosine receptor antagonism, which may contribute to its vasodilatory effects.

When administered intravenously, papaverine produces rapid vasodilation with an onset of 1–2 minutes and a duration of 10–30 minutes.

[[papaver-rhoeas]] (corn poppy) and Argemone mexicana (prickly poppy) also contain papaverine in detectable quantities.

Commercial papaverine is obtained as a byproduct of morphine extraction from opium, separated during the purification process.

The alkaloid profile of different poppy cultivars varies; varieties bred for high [[thebaine]] content often have reduced papaverine levels.

Total synthesis of papaverine has been achieved but is not economically competitive with extraction from natural sources.

## Medical Applications

Papaverine has been used as a vasodilator for the treatment of cerebral vasospasm following subarachnoid hemorrhage, administered via intra-arterial infusion.

In interventional cardiology, papaverine is used as a coronary vasodilator to assess fractional flow reserve and to manage no-reflow phenomenon during angioplasty.

The drug is employed in the management of peripheral vascular disease, especially for vasospastic conditions such as Raynaud phenomenon.

Papaverine is a component of intracavernosal injection therapy for erectile dysfunction, often combined with phentolamine and alprostadil in triple-therapy formulations.

As a smooth muscle antispasmodic, papaverine has been used to treat biliary colic, renal colic, and gastrointestinal hypermotility.
