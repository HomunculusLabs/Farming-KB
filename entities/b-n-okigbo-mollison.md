## Description

B. N. Okigbo was a Nigerian agricultural scientist and researcher who conducted pioneering work on residue mulch systems and no-tillage agriculture in tropical soils. Along with R. Lal, Okigbo presented influential research at the International Conference on Ecological Agriculture in Montreal (1978) on the use of crop residue mulches to maintain and increase yields in tropical farming systems. Their work demonstrated that no-tillage systems combined with organic mulch applications could sustain or improve crop yields on fragile tropical soils where conventional tillage led to rapid soil degradation.

## Key Facts

- Co-authored "Residue Mulches and Agrislviculture" with R. Lal, presented at the International Conference on Ecological Agriculture, Montreal, 1978.
- Research demonstrated that no-tillage systems maintained or gained yields for maize in Nigeria.
- Documented yield increases from mulched crops for cowpea, soya bean, and cassava compared to bare-ground cultivation.
- Worked specifically with tropical Nigerian soils, addressing the challenges of nutrient leaching, erosion, and organic matter depletion in hot, wet climates.
- Research provided empirical evidence supporting mulch-based approaches over conventional bare-soil cultivation in tropical agriculture.
- The work was part of a broader effort to develop strategies for coping with increasing land pressures in tropical developing nations.
