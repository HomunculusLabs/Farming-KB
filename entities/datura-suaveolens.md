# Datura suaveolens

## Description

Datura suaveolens (now more commonly classified as Brugmansia suaveolens) is a species of the Solanaceae family identified by ethnobotanist Richard Evans Schultes as one of the admixture plants added to ayahuasca brews in the Peruvian Amazon. Known locally as toé, this plant is a powerful deliriant containing tropane alkaloids including scopolamine, hyoscyamine, and atropine. Its addition to ayahuasca is a rare and specialized practice, as the plant is considered far more dangerous than the primary ayahuasca components.

## Classification

- **Scientific name:** Datura suaveolens (historical); Brugmansia suaveolens (modern)
- **Family:** Solanaceae (nightshade family)
- **Common names:** Toe, toé, angel's trumpet, floripondio
- **Active compounds:** Tropane alkaloids — scopolamine, hyoscyamine, atropine
- **Drug class:** Deliriant / anticholinergic
- **Role in ayahuasca:** Occasional admixture plant (not a standard component)

## Key Facts

- Identified by Schultes as an admixture plant added to ayahuasca brews (personal communication cited by Dobkin de Rios, 1972)
- The plant is known locally as toé in the Peruvian Amazon
- Contains powerful tropane alkaloids that produce deliriant (not psychedelic) effects — fundamentally different from DMT or harmala alkaloids
- The addition of toé to ayahuasca is considered dangerous and is not standard practice — only certain healers use it
- Brugmansia species are woody trees or shrubs, distinguished from Datura species (which are herbaceous)
- All parts of the plant are toxic; overdose can be fatal
- In Amazonian folk medicine, toé is used independently for various purposes including divination and sorcery
- The plant's use as an admixture reflects the complex and sometimes dangerous experimentation that characterizes Amazonian ethnopharmacology
- Unlike DMT (which is a serotonin receptor agonist), tropane alkaloids are anticholinergic and produce fundamentally different altered states — true delirium rather than visionary experience

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Datura suaveolens is relevant to entheogenic plant studies because:

1. **Admixture complexity:** Toé represents the more dangerous end of the ayahuasca admixture spectrum, demonstrating that healers experiment with a wide range of psychoactive plants
2. **Deliriant pharmacology:** The plant provides a contrast to typical psychedelic compounds — its effects are deliriant rather than visionary, highlighting the pharmacological diversity of Amazonian plant use
3. **Risk assessment:** Understanding which admixtures are dangerous is critical for harm reduction in the context of expanding global ayahuasca use
4. **Ethnobotanical breadth:** The use of Solanaceae plants alongside Malpighiaceae vines in Amazonian medicine demonstrates cross-family pharmacological knowledge
5. **Taxonomic complexity:** The reclassification from Datura to Brugmansia illustrates the ongoing taxonomic refinement of psychoactive plants
