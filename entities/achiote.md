## Description

Achiote (*[[bixa-orellana]]*), also known as annatto or achote, is a shrub or small tree widely recognized in [[amazonian-vegetalista-shamanism-degrees-and-hierarchy]] medicine as indispensable in healing *mal aire* — an illness caused by contact with spirits of the dead (supay) or by exposure to harmful atmospheric influences. In Amaringo's Vision 35 (Extracting Magical Darts from a Patient), achiote leaves are used by a healer's apprentice to fan a woman who was shaken by a bad *tunchi* (spirit of a deceased person), demonstrating the plant's essential role in dispelling spiritual afflictions.

The plant also appears in Vision 45 (Vegetalistas Transforming Themselves into Wolves) as one of the key defensive plants used against evil sorcerers, alongside [[pinon-colorado]] (*Jatropha gossypifolia*), yuca verde (green manioc), patiquina (*Dieffenbachia* sp.), and sacha-ajos (*Mansoa alliacea*). The red pigment from its seed pods is one of the most widely used natural dyes in the Americas, employed both ceremonially and practically across dozens of [[ethnobotanical-use-of-sacred-mushrooms-in-indigenous-cultures]].

Achiote is also referred to as *achote* and appears in the context of the achote plant with its fruit visible in Vision 35. The woman being treated with achiote leaves stands still and straight, staring first at where the sun rises then turning to where it sets, following a specific ritual protocol for mal aire treatment.

## Classification

- **Domain**: Plant entity; medicinal/dye shrub-tree
- **Scientific name**: *Bixa orellana* L.
- **Family**: Bixaceae
- **Common names**: Achiote, achote, annatto, urucu, onoto, bija, roucou
- **Cultural role**: Essential mal aire remedy; defensive plant; ceremonial dye; food coloring
- **Properties**: Medicinal leaves and seeds; red-orange dye (bixin); antipyretic; astringent
- **Habitat**: Tropical lowlands, widely cultivated throughout the neotropics

## Key Facts

- Considered **indispensable** in healing any kind of *mal aire* — one of the most fundamental remedies
- Leaves used by apprentices to fan patients afflicted by bad *tunchi* (spirit of a deceased person)
- In Vision 35, the apprentice fans a woman shaken by a tunchi, restoring her through the achiote leaves
- The woman stands still and straight, staring at where the sun rises then turning to where the sun sets
- Listed in Vision 45 as a key defensive plant against evil sorcerers
- Red pigment from seed arils contains **bixin** and **norbixin**, carotenoid compounds with antioxidant properties
- Widely used as body paint and dye across indigenous Amazonian cultures for decoration and spiritual protection
- Brilliant red color associated with protection, vitality, and spiritual power in Amazonian cosmologies
- Leaves used in ritual baths (banos) for cleansing and spiritual protection
- Employed alongside pinon colorado, yuca verde, patiquina, and sacha-ajos in the defensive pharmacopoeia
- One of the oldest known natural food colorants in the Americas, used for centuries
- Modern commercial use includes E160b food coloring, cosmetics, and textile dyeing worldwide
- Seeds have documented antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory properties in pharmacological studies
- The plant is relatively easy to cultivate and begins producing within 1-2 years

## Botanical Characteristics

- Evergreen shrub or small tree reaching 3-8 meters in height with spreading branches
- Leaves are large, heart-shaped (cordate), 10-20 cm long, with prominent veins and thin texture
- Flowers are showy, pink to white, approximately 5 cm in diameter, with numerous prominent stamens
- Fruit is a distinctive ovoid capsule covered in soft bristles, 3-5 cm long, splitting when ripe
- Seeds are covered in a bright red-orange waxy aril containing the dye pigment bixin
- Thrives in [[fukuoka-natural-farming-tropical-climates]] with well-drained soil; highly adaptable to various growing conditions
- Begins [[query-why-are-my-plants-not-producing-fruit-and-how-do-i-hand-pollinate]] within 1-2 years of planting from seed
- Can produce two harvests per year under optimal growing conditions

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- Bixin and norbixin have demonstrated **antifungal activity** against several agricultural [[cordyceps-insect-pathogenic-fungi]]
- Leaves contain compounds with **[[medicinal-mushroom-antimicrobial-properties]]** that may inhibit fungal growth, relevant to the plant's role in dispelling mal aire
- Commonly grown in home gardens near settlements, readily available for **traditional [[herbal-medicine-preparation-methods]]**
- Seed aril's [[mycelium-running-mushroom-antimicrobial-properties-species-chart]] suggest potential applications in **natural food preservation** against fungal spoilage
- Traditional use against mal aire may encode observations of the plant's ability to address **respiratory infections** or environmental sensitivities
- Rapid growth and adaptability make it suitable for **agroforestry systems** and sustainable cultivation
- Leaf extracts show activity against **Candida species** and other [[malassezia-commensal-pathogenic-yeasts]] in laboratory studies
- The bright red dye has been used to protect skin from insects and UV radiation via **antimicrobial mechanisms**
- As a widely cultivated species, its [[plants-without-mycorrhizal-associations]] are relevant to **tropical soil health** management
- Priority species for **ethnopharmacological investigation** due to its dual healing and protective roles in vegetalista practice
