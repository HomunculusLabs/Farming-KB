## Overview

Thalictrum aquilegiifolium, commonly known as columbine meadow-rue, is a perennial herbaceous plant in the family Ranunculaceae. It is native to damp meadows and woodland margins across Europe and western Asia.

The species is valued in horticulture for its airy clusters of fluffy lilac-purple flowers that appear in early summer. Its Columbine-like blue-green foliage provides textural contrast in garden borders.

Unlike many Ranunculaceae, columbine meadow-rue is non-toxic and safe for garden planting near children and pets. It attracts numerous pollinator species, particularly small bees and hoverflies.

The genus Thalictrum contains approximately 200 species distributed across [[thalictrum-flavum]] and Thalictrum simplex, all European meadow-rue species.

The species has no widely accepted synonyms, though historical names include Thalictrum aquilegiifolium var. majus. No subspecies are currently recognized.

Thalictrum aquilegiifolium is tetraploid, which contributes to its vigor and adaptability in cultivation. Polyploidy is common in the genus and associated with larger flower size.

## Morphology

Columbine meadow-rue forms an upright clump reaching 60–120 cm in height and 45–60 cm in spread. Multiple branching stems arise from a stout, fibrous rootstock in spring.

Leaves are bi- to tri-ternate with rounded, lobed leaflets that resemble columbine foliage. Leaflets are glaucous blue-green above, 2–5 cm across, with conspicuous veining.

Flowers are borne in large, loose terminal panicles creating a cloud-like display. Each flower lacks true petals; the showy parts are numerous lavender-purple stamens with conspicuous yellow anthers.

Blooming occurs from May through July in the Northern Hemisphere. The airy flower heads remain attractive for three to four weeks.

Flowers open progressively from the base of the panicle upward, extending the display period. Individual flowers last only a few days but are produced in profusion.

Fruits are ribbed achenes, each containing a single seed. Seeds are small, dark brown, and require cold stratification for germination.

The plant produces a woody caudex at the base from which new shoots emerge annually. Rootstock is somewhat tuberous, allowing the plant to survive brief periods of drought.

Stems are glabrous, slightly glaucous, and branched in the upper portion. The overall form is elegant and airy, contributing a vertical accent to mixed plantings without dominating.

## Distribution and Habitat

The species is widespread across central and eastern Europe, extending from France and Germany to western Russia. Isolated populations occur in the Balkans, Turkey, and the Caucasus region.

It grows naturally in damp meadows, riverbanks, forest clearings, and marshy grasslands at low to montane elevations. The plant prefers moist, fertile soils but tolerates brief periods of dryness once established.

It thrives in full sun to partial shade and is indifferent to soil pH, growing in both acidic and calcareous conditions. In cultivation it adapts well to average [[query-how-to-protect-plants-from-frost-and-extend-the-growing-season]] and benefits from staking in exposed sites.

Established plants are long-lived and relatively low-maintenance. The clump slowly expands but is not aggressive or invasive in garden settings.

Thalictrum aquilegiifolium is wind-resistant despite its tall stature due to flexible stems that bend without breaking. This makes it suitable for exposed coastal and prairie gardens.

Propagation is by seed or division in [[cervantes-powdery-mildew-prevention-treatment]] may occur on foliage in hot, humid summers but rarely disfigures the plant significantly.

Thalictrum aquilegiifolium combines well with other moisture-loving perennials in border plantings. It flowers at the same time as early campanulas and geraniums, creating pleasing color combinations.
