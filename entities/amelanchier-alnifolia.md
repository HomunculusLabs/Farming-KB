## Overview

**Amelanchier alnifolia**, commonly known as saskatoon berry, western serviceberry,
or juneberry, is a deciduous shrub in the rose family (Rosaceae). Native to western
and central [[serviceberry-and-native-fruiting-shrubs]] succeed. It is cold-hardy
to USDA Zone 2, making it one of the most cold-tolerant [[backyard-food-forest-design]], *A. alnifolia* is valued as a productive,
low-maintenance fruiting shrub that supports pollinators, provides [[hemenway-backyard-wildlife-habitat]],
[[query-how-to-start-a-small-olive-grove-and-produce-olive-oil-at-home]] s nutrient-dense fruit with minimal input.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:** Magnoliophyta → Magnoliopsida → Rosales → Rosaceae → Amygdaloideae →
*Amelanchier* → *A. alnifolia*

The genus *Amelanchier* contains approximately 20–25 species across temperate North
America, Europe, and Asia. *A. alnifolia* is the most commercially significant,
particularly on the Canadian prairies.

*A. alnifolia* typically grows as a multi-stemmed shrub 1–5 m tall, though it can
reach 8 m in optimal conditions. Young stems are reddish-brown and smooth, maturing
to gray with longitudinal fissures.

Leaves are alternate, oval to elliptic, 2–5 cm long, with finely serrated margins.
Foliage emerges gray-green and woolly, maturing to dark green above and paler below.
Fall color is yellow to orange-red.

Flowers are white, five-petaled, 15–25 mm across, borne in erect racemes of 5–15
flowers in early to mid-spring (April–May). Fruit is a small pome, 6–12 mm diameter,
ripening from green through red to deep purple-black in June–July, with 5–10 small
seeds contributing a characteristic nutty flavor.

## Distribution and Habitat

*A. alnifolia* ranges from Alaska and Yukon south to California, New Mexico, and
Nebraska, and east across southern Canada to Ontario and the Great Lakes region.

It occupies a broad ecological range, from dry prairie hillsides and open woodlands
to streambanks and rocky outcrops. It is particularly abundant in the aspen parkland
belt of the Canadian prairies, where it forms extensive thickets.

The species tolerates a wide range [[biological-indicators-of-soil-conditions]], from sandy loams to clay
soils, and prefers slightly acidic to neutral pH (5.5–7.0). It is moderately
drought-tolerant once established and can persist in semi-[[fungal-diversity-arid-environments]] with
as little as 30 cm annual precipitation.

It tolerates full sun to partial shade and resprouts vigorously from the root crown
[[holzer-reforestation-after-fire]] or cutting.

## Nutritional Profile

Saskatoon berries are exceptionally nutritious, exceeding [[query-what-are-the-most-common-mushroom-contaminants-and-how-do-i-identify-them]] berries in
several key metrics.

- **Antioxidants:** 2–3× higher ORAC value than blueberries
- **Vitamin C:** 15–25 mg/100 g
- **Iron:** 1.2–2.0 mg/100 g (significantly higher than most berries)
