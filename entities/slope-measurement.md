## Slope Measurement in Permaculture

## Description

Slope measurement is a fundamental skill in [[permaculture-earthworks]] design, enabling precise planning of drains, terraces, roads, and water harvesting structures. Mollison describes three primary systems for expressing slope, each suited to different scales and applications. Understanding these measurement systems is essential for any earthworks project.

## Classification

- **Category:** Technical method / Surveying technique
- **Field:** Earthworks engineering / Landscape design
- **Complexity:** Basic to intermediate

## Three Systems of Slope Expression

### 1. Degrees from Horizontal
- Expressed as "a slope of 15 degrees"
- Commonly used by geologists to describe rock strata dip angles
- Intuitive for coarse slope description
- Range: 0° (flat) to 90° (vertical)

### 2. Percentages
- The 90° between horizontal and vertical divided into 100 parts
- Commonly used by engineers
- 100% = 45° slope (1:1 ratio)
- Road maximum typically 13% before concreting or sealing required
- Wheeled tractor safe maximum: approximately 18%

### 3. Proportions (Ratios)
- Expressed as base:height (e.g., 1:4 = 1 unit high with 4 unit base)
- Used by hydrologists for drains and pipes
- Irrigation drains typically 1:500 to 1:2000
- Gravels: stable at 1:15 (6.7%)
- Well-drained clay: stable at 1:2 (50%)
- Wet clay: stable at 1:1 (100%)

## Critical Slope Thresholds

| Slope | Description | Use |
|-------|------------|-----|
| 100% (90°) | Vertical | Only stable in rock or dry sediments |
| 80-90° | Precipitous | Safe only in stable rock or loess |
| 60% | Very steep | Bulldozer up/down only, no traverse |
| 50% (45°) | Very steep | Not for tree clearing, needs permanent forest |
| 40% | Steep | Maximum safe bulldozer traverse |
| 30% | Steep | Cautious firewood/coppice harvest, can be benched |
| 20% | Moderate | Maximum safe cultivation; tractors ~18% |
| 13% | Low | Maximum road surface before sealing needed |
| 10% | Low | May need contour banking; 4-6% ideal for railways |
| 0-10% | Low | Stable for crop agriculture, terraces, swales |

## Design Principles for Slopes

1. **Natural slopes are concave, not straight** — design to match natural slump profiles
2. **Cut concave curves into slopes** proactively rather than waiting for natural slump
3. **Sun side of roads should be cut further back** to allow sun in and dry the surface
4. **Shade side can be steeper** if shade is desired
5. **Fine sands need gentle drainage slope** (1:2000) and easy angles of rest (1:4.5)

## Relevance to Cultivation

Accurate slope measurement is the foundation of all [[water-management]] earthworks. A 1% error in drain slope over 500 meters can result in water flowing the wrong direction. Mollison emphasizes that levels need only be set once if done right, making proper slope measurement a critical investment of time that pays dividends for the lifetime of the earthworks.
