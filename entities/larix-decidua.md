## Larix decidua

## Overview

**Larix decidua**, the European larch, is a deciduous conifer in the
family Pinaceae native to the mountains of central and southern Europe.
It is one of the few conifer species that sheds its needles annually.

The deciduous habit produces a striking golden-yellow autumn display,
making European larch a popular ornamental tree. Larch wood is among the
hardest and most durable softwoods, with excellent natural decay resistance.

The species has been widely planted beyond its native range throughout the
British Isles, northern Europe, and northeastern [[psilocybe-bohemica-central-european]]) and var.
*polonica* (Polish larch), though some authorities treat them as subspecies.

European larch reaches 25–45 m in height with trunk diameters of 0.6–1.5 m.
The crown is conical when young, becoming irregular and open with age. Bark
is grey-brown, thick, and deeply fissured into irregular plates.

Needles are borne in clusters of 30–40 on short spur shoots, each 10–30 mm
long, light green and soft. They turn golden-yellow in autumn before
abscission, creating one of the most striking seasonal displays in European
montane forests.

Seed cones are ovoid, 2–4 cm long, with 40–50 scales. They remain on the
tree for several years after seed release. Seeds are 3–4 mm with a pale
wing of 8–12 mm.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Larix decidua* is native to the Alps, Carpathians, and Sudetes, extending
from southern France through Austria, northern Italy, Slovenia, and Poland.
Altitudinal range is approximately 600–2,500 meters, with optimal growth
between 1,000 and 2,000 meters.

The species prefers well-drained, slightly acidic to neutral soils on
mountain slopes. It grows on both calcareous and siliceous substrates,
showing broader soil tolerance than many associated conifers.

In the Alps, European larch forms characteristic mixed forests with
*[[ectomycorrhizal-associations]] with diverse fungi. *Suillus
grevillei* shows strong [[laccaria-laccata]]* and *[[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]] each autumn, supporting a distinct decomposer
community adapted to this seasonal input pattern.

Larch forests provide habitat for nutcracker, crossbill, and various
woodpeckers. The open canopy of mature larch supports a richer ground flora
than most evergreen conifer forests.
