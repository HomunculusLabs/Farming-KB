## Overview

Myriophyllum verticillatum, commonly known as the whorled water milfoil, is a submerged aquatic plant in the family Haloragaceae.

It is native to a broad range across Europe, Asia, and [[biodiversity-fungi-freshwater-aquatic-habitats]].

The species is distinguished from the invasive [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]], with scattered populations further south in mountainous areas.

The species grows in standing or slow-moving freshwater, including lakes, ponds, marshes, canals, and backwaters of rivers.

It prefers mesotrophic to eutrophic waters with moderate nutrient levels.

Water depths typically range from 0.5 to 3 meters, though the plant can grow in deeper water where light penetration is sufficient.

Myriophyllum verticillatum is tolerant of a range of substrate types, from sand and gravel to organic mud.

## Ecology and Reproduction

Myriophyllum verticillatum reproduces both sexually and vegetatively.

[[basidiomycete-mating-systems-sexual-reproduction]] produces seeds that germinate in spring when water temperatures reach approximately 15 degrees Celsius.

Vegetative reproduction through stem fragmentation and turion formation is the primary means of spread within and between water bodies.

Fragmented stems can develop roots at each node, enabling rapid colonization of new areas.

Turions form in late summer and autumn, sinking to the substrate where they remain dormant through winter.

The dense submerged foliage of Myriophyllum verticillatum provides important habitat structure for aquatic invertebrates, including mayfly larvae, damselfly nymphs, and amphipods.

Fish use the plant beds for spawning habitat and refuge from predators.

Waterfowl feed on both the vegetative parts and turions, and may facilitate dispersal between water bodies.

The plant is considered a moderate nutrient indicator, typically occurring in waters with moderate phosphorus and nitrogen levels.

## Invasive Potential and Management

Myriophyllum verticillatum is less aggressively invasive than its relative Myriophyllum spicatum, but can form dense beds that interfere with recreation and water flow.
