# Thor Heyerdahl

## Description

Thor Heyerdahl (1914-2002) was a Norwegian ethnographer and adventurer best known for his Kon-Tiki and Ra Expeditions. Coleman uses Heyerdahl's Ra Expeditions as a metaphor for organic farming: just as Heyerdahl's team failed by omitting a single seemingly unnecessary rope depicted in ancient Egyptian frescoes, chemical agriculture fails by omitting the "rope" of soil organic matter that traditional farming systems always included.

## Classification

- **Category**: Person
- **Domain**: Ethnography, experimental archaeology
- **Role**: Explorer, author, metaphorical figure in agricultural writing
- **Nationality**: Norwegian

## Key Facts

- Organized the Ra Expeditions (1969-1970) to prove ancient Egyptians could have crossed the Atlantic in papyrus reed boats
- Ra I collapsed mid-ocean because the team omitted one rope shown in 3,000-4,000-year-old tomb frescoes, believing it served no modern-engineering purpose
- Ra II, built with the previously omitted rope in place, completed the transatlantic voyage successfully
- The metaphor illustrates that traditional agricultural knowledge, like traditional boat-building knowledge, contains essential wisdom that modern science may not immediately understand but should not discard

## Relevance to Cultivation

Coleman draws a direct parallel between the omitted rope and the omission of soil organic matter in chemical agriculture. Just as Heyerdahl's team dismissed traditional knowledge encoded in ancient art -- leading to catastrophic failure -- modern agriculture dismissed centuries of [[composting]], green manuring, and crop rotation wisdom, leading to soil degradation and nutritional decline. The lesson for cultivators: respect traditional knowledge systems and understand their functional purpose before discarding them in favor of reductionist approaches.

## Source Context

Referenced in Eliot Coleman's "The Winter Harvest Handbook" in the chapter "Deep-Organic Farming and the Small Farm" as an extended metaphor for what was lost when agriculture transitioned from biological to chemical methods.
