# Jezo Spruce (*Picea jezoensis*)

## Overview

Jezo spruce is a large evergreen conifer native to northeast Asia, ranging from Japan through the Russian Far East, Sakhalin, and the Korean Peninsula.

It is one of the most important timber species in Hokkaido, northern Honshu, and the Russian Far East, producing strong, straight-grained wood used extensively in construction.

The species typically reaches 30–40 meters in height and is distinguished by its dark green, slightly glaucous needles and pendulous branch tips.

Jezo spruce forms extensive forests across subalpine and boreal zones, often co-dominating with Sakhalin fir (*Abies sachalinensis*) and various broadleaf species.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Division:** Pinophyta
- **Class:** Pinopsida
- **Order:** Pinales
- **Family:** Pinaceae
- **Genus:** *Picea*
- **Species:** *P. jezoensis* (Siebold & Zucc.) Carrière

The species was described by Siebold and Zuccarini as *Abies jezoensis* in 1842 and transferred to *Picea* by Carrière in 1855.

Two varieties are sometimes recognized: var. *jezoensis* (typical) and var. *microsperma* from northern Hokkaido and Sakhalin, the latter with smaller cones and seeds.

The epithet *jezoensis* refers to Ezo, the historical Japanese name for Hokkaido, where the species was first collected.

**Bark** is thin and scaly on young trees, becoming thick, fissured, and dark grayish-brown with age. The outer bark scales off in irregular plates.

**Needles** are 10–20 mm long, 1.5–2 mm wide, flattened but not as sharply quadrangular as many spruces, dark green above with two white stomatal bands beneath.

Needles are notably pungent when crushed, emitting a sharp resinous odor.

**Cones** are cylindrical, 4–8 cm long and 2–3 cm wide, with thin, flexible, slightly notched scales. They are green when immature, ripening to light brown.

Cones dehisce and release seeds in autumn, unlike the semi-serotinous cones of [[ectomycorrhizal-associations]] with a range of [[picea-mariana]] and is generally absent from peatland habitats.

The species is sensitive to [[bark-beetle-fungi-ophiostomatales-collection-isolation]] species can cause significant mortality during outbreak periods, particularly in drought-stressed stands.

Needle cast fungi, particularly *Lophodermium piceae*, cause premature needle drop and reduced growth, especially in dense, unthinned plantations.
