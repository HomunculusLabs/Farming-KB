## Distribution and Habitat

Welwitschia mirabilis is restricted to the Namib Desert, occurring from the Kuiseb River in central Namibia north to the Nicolau River in southern Angola.

The distribution range spans approximately 1,000 km along the coastal desert belt at elevations from near sea level to approximately 1,000 meters.

Plants grow on gravel plains, rocky outcrops, and dry riverbeds where subsurface moisture is accessible. The species is most abundant in the fog belt of the central Namib, where coastal fog provides regular moisture input.

The Namib Desert is one of the oldest and driest deserts on Earth, with annual precipitation below 25 mm in many parts of the range. Welwitschia derives most of its water from fog condensation on its leaves.

The Benguela Current, a cold ocean current flowing northward along the Namibian coast, generates the persistent fog that sustains Welwitschia populations. This coastal fog typically occurs on 150 to 300 days per year in the central Namib.

## Reproductive Biology

Welwitschia mirabilis is dioecious, with male and female reproductive structures on separate plants. Pollination is primarily wind-mediated, though insect pollination has also been observed.

Male strobili produce pollen in specialized structures called microsporangia. The pollen grains are monosulcate and similar in morphology to those of gymnosperms.

Female strobili contain ovules that develop into seeds after successful pollination. Seed set is highly variable and depends on favorable moisture conditions during the reproductive period.

Seeds have a peripheral wing that aids in wind dispersal. They remain viable for only a few months under natural conditions, making recruitment events dependent on rare episodes of adequate rainfall.

Natural regeneration is episodic and strongly linked to above-average rainfall years. Seedling survival is extremely low, with most germinated seeds failing to establish due to desiccation, herbivory, or unfavorable substrate conditions.

Established Welwitschia plants have few natural predators. However, desert-dwelling mammals such as gemsbok and springbok occasionally browse on the leaf tips and female strobili, particularly during periods of drought when other forage is scarce.

## Conservation Status

Welwitschia mirabilis is listed as Vulnerable on the IUCN Red List. The primary threats are habitat degradation from off-road vehicle traffic, mining activities, and overgrazing by domestic livestock.

Long-term monitoring data from the central Namib have documented declining fog frequencies since the 1970s.

The species is protected under Namibian law and occurs within the Namib-Naukluft Park, one of the largest conservation areas in Africa. Several populations in Angola also receive nominal protection.
