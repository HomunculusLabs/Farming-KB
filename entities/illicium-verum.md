## Overview

**Illicium verum** Hook.f., commonly known as star anise or Chinese star anise, is an evergreen medium-sized tree native to northeastern Vietnam and southern China. Its distinctive star-shaped fruits are one of the most important spices in Asian cuisine and a primary botanical source of shikimic acid.

Star anise is essential in the production of oseltamivir (Tamiflu), an antiviral medication used against influenza. The species has been cultivated in China for over a millennium and remains a major commercial crop in both China and Vietnam.

## Toxicity and Safety

The most significant safety concern with star anise is confusion or adulteration with Japanese star anise (Illicium anisatum). I. anisatum contains potent neurotoxins, primarily anisatin and neoanisatin, which are non-competitive GABA receptor antagonists.

Cases of neurological toxicity, including seizures and vomiting, have been reported in infants given star anise tea for colic. Investigation of these incidents consistently identified I. anisatum contamination rather than toxicity from true star anise.
