The species is edible when thoroughly cooked, though some individuals
report gastrointestinal upset — a pattern common across the genus
*Leccinum*. It is distinguished by its dark reddish-brown cap,
pale flesh that slowly stains pinkish or brownish, and scabrous stipe
surface typical of the genus.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** [[basidiomycota]]
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Boletales
- **Family:** Boletaceae
- **Genus:** *Leccinum*
- **Species:** *L. insigne* Snell & Dick (1965)

**Etymology:** The specific epithet *insigne* means "remarkable" or
"distinguished" in Latin, likely referring to the relatively large size
and striking cap coloration compared to other *Leccinum* species.

### Cap

Convex to broadly convex, sometimes nearly plane in age, 6–18 cm diameter.
Surface dry, finely velvety to nearly smooth, dark reddish-brown to
chocolate-brown or reddish-umber.
Does not stain when bruised. Cap flesh white, firm, slowly staining
pinkish to brownish-gray when cut.

### Pore Surface

Pores small, angular, 1–3 per mm, initially white to pale gray.
Becoming pale brown to olive-brown at maturity.
Staining dingy brown to pinkish-brown when bruised, sometimes slowly.
Tubes adnexed to slightly depressed around the stipe, 8–20 mm deep.

### Stipe

Central, solid, cylindrical to slightly clavate, 8–16 cm tall, 1.5–3.5 cm thick.
Surface white at apex, darkening to brownish below.
Covered with coarse, dark brown to black scabers (sterile hyphal tufts)
that darken with age — the defining character of the genus *Leccinum*.
Stipe flesh white, staining blue-gray to blackish in the base when cut.

### Spores and Microscopic Features

Spores 14–20 × 4.5–6.5 µm, elongate-fusoid, smooth, pale yellow-brown
in deposit, not amyloid.
Basidia clavate, 25–35 × 8–10 µm, 4-spored.
Pileipellis a trichopalisade (palisoderm) of narrow hyphae.
Scabers on stipe composed of bundles of septate, thick-walled hyphae.
No pleurocystidia or cheilocystidia observed.

## Distribution and Habitat

Widely distributed across [[populus-deltoides]]* (eastern [[cottonwood]])
