## Psilocybe brasiliensis

**Type:** Species (Fungus)
**Category:** Psilocybin-containing mushroom
**Also known as:** Brazilian Psilocybe

## Overview
Psilocybe brasiliensis is a psilocybin-containing mushroom described
by Guzman (1983) from Sao Paulo State, Brazil. Growing gregariously on
grassy soils in Araucaria forests at approximately 1500 meters
elevation, this species is known only from its type locality but is
considered likely to be more widely distributed. Its general shape
resembles temperate Psilocybe species like P. cyanofibrillosa.

## Key Details

### Taxonomy
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Hymenogastraceae
- **Genus:** Psilocybe
- **Authority:** Guzman (1983)

### Morphology
- **Cap:** 1-3 cm broad. Conic at first, soon convex and eventually
  campanulate. Surface smooth, viscid when moist, and translucent-
  striate along the cap margin. Reddish brown to brown when moist,
  hygrophanous, fading to light brown upon drying, becoming bluish in
  age or where damaged.
- **Gills:** Adnate attachment. Brownish to purplish brown with
  whitish margins.
- **Stem:** 35-80 mm long by 1-4 mm thick. Equal to slightly swelling
  at the base, which is usually adorned with whitish fibrils and/or
  rhizomorphs. Bruising bluish where injured.
- **Spores:** Dark grayish violet in deposit, subrhomboid to
  subellipsoid, 5.5-7.7 by 4.2-5.5 microns.
- **Microscopic features:** Basidia 4-spored. Pleurocystidia 22-29 by
  8.8-12 microns, fusoid-ventricose to sublageniform. Cheilocystidia
  9.6-13 by 5-7.2 microns, lageniform.

### Habitat and Distribution
Gregarious on grassy soils (Axonopus compressus) in forests of Araucaria
brasiliana and Podocarpus. Known only from Brazil, near Sao Paulo, in
March at approximately 1500 meters elevation. Not yet reported outside
the type locality, but as a grassland species it is considered likely
to be widespread.

### Chemistry
Apparently active. Listed by Guzman (1983) under psilocybin-producing
varieties because of its [[chiric-caspi-brunfelsia-south-american-hallucinogen]] Araucaria forests, a unique biome dominated by the
iconic Brazilian pine (Araucaria angustifolia). This forest type is
among the most threatened ecosystems in Brazil, giving P. brasiliensis
ecological significance as a potential indicator species.
