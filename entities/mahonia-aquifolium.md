## Overview

*Mahonia aquifolium* (Pursh) Nutt., commonly known as Oregon grape, is an
evergreen shrub in the family Berberidaceae native to western [[iboga-root-bark-preparation]], which contains
berberine — a potent isoquinoline alkaloid with broad-spectrum antimicrobial
and anti-inflammatory properties. Root extracts are approved in Germany for
topical treatment of psoriasis.

Oregon grape produces tart, dark blue-purple berries that are edible when
cooked and used in jams, jellies, and wines. Its tolerance of shade, drought,
and poor soils makes it useful in permaculture and restoration plantings.

## Taxonomy

Plantae → Tracheophytes → Angiosperms → Eudicots → **Ranunculales →
Berberidaceae → *Mahonia* → *M. aquifolium***

- **Authority:** (Pursh) Nutt. (1818)
- **Synonyms:** *Berberis aquifolium* Pursh, *Odostemon aquifolium*
  (Pursh) Rydb.
- **Common names:** Oregon grape, holly-leaved barberry, tall Oregon grape

Molecular phylogenetics places *Mahonia* nested within *Berberis*, but
horticultural and medicinal literature retains *Mahonia* due to distinct
morphology and tradition.

## Morphology

**Growth habit:** Evergreen shrub, 0.3–2 m tall, spreading by rhizomes to
form dense thickets. Stems erect to ascending, sharply ridged.

**Leaves:** Pinnately compound, 15–30 cm, with 5–9 leaflets. Leaflets
glossy, dark green, 4–8 × 2–4 cm, ovate to lanceolate with sharp spinose
teeth; new leaves bronzy-red. Evergreen in most of its range.

**Flowers:** Bright yellow, fragrant, borne in dense terminal racemes 3–8 cm
long; each flower 6-parted, 6–9 mm across. Blooms [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]] from southeast Alaska to northern California,
eastward into Idaho, Montana, and British Columbia. Occurs from sea level
to ~2000 m in coniferous forests, mixed woodlands, chaparral edges, and
rocky slopes.

Prefers partial shade to full sun and well-drained soils but tolerates clay,
sand, and serpentine substrates. Moderately drought-tolerant once established.
Naturalized in parts of Europe, New Zealand, and Australia.
