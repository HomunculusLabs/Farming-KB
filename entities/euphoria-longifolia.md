## Overview

**Euphoria longifolia** (syn. *Eurycoma longifolia* Jack) is a flowering plant in the family Simaroubaceae, native to [[psilocybe-southeast-asia-pacific]].

It is widely known by its Malay name **tongkat ali** (literally "Ali's walking stick") and has a long history of use as a traditional aphrodisiac, energy tonic, and antimalarial remedy.

The plant contains a diverse array of quassinoid compounds, particularly **eurycomanone**, which is considered the primary bioactive marker.

It is one of the most commercially significant medicinal plants in Malaysia and Indonesia, with a growing global market as a dietary supplement for testosterone support and male vitality.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Kingdom:** Plantae — **Family:** Simaroubaceae — **Genus:** Euphoria (syn. Eurycoma)

**Species:** *Euphoria longifolia* (Jack) — Authority: Jack (1822), commonly treated as *Eurycoma longifolia* Jack in most pharmacological literature.

The plant is a slender evergreen shrub or small tree reaching 5–15 meters in height.

Leaves are pinnately compound, 20–40 cm long, with 13–41 leaflets that are lanceolate to oblong-lanceolate.

Flowers are small, reddish, arranged in dense axillary panicles. The plant is dioecious, with separate male and female individuals.

Fruit is a drupe, approximately 1–2 cm long, turning reddish-orange at maturity.

Roots are the primary plant part harvested for medicinal use, typically from plants 4–5 years of age or older.

## Distribution and Habitat

Native to Malaysia, Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand, Laos, Cambodia, and Myanmar.

Found in lowland and hill dipterocarp forests up to approximately 700 meters elevation.

Prefers well-drained sandy or loamy soils with partial shade in primary or secondary forest understories.

The species has experienced significant population decline in the wild due to over-harvesting of roots, leading to increasing cultivation efforts.

## Bioactive Compounds

**Quassinoids** are the dominant chemical class. Eurycomanone (C20H22O9) is the major bioactive quassinoid, with a typical root content of 0.1–1.6% dry weight.

Additional quassinoids include eurycomanol, 14,15β-dihydroxyklaineanone, eurycomalactone, and 6α-hydroxyeurycomalactone.

**Canthine-6-one alkaloids** have been isolated from the roots, including 9-methoxycanthin-6-one.

The root also contains β-carboline alkaloids and various squalene-type triterpenes.

Root quality is standardized by eurycomanone content, with pharmacopoeial specifications requiring a minimum of 0.4–1.0% depending on the preparation.

## Pharmacology

Animal studies demonstrate that eurycomanone and related quassinoids increase serum testosterone levels through stimulation of the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis and partial inhibition of sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG).

Human [[mushroom-cancer-clinical-trials-overview]] have shown modest increases in free testosterone, improved sperm motility, and reduced cortisol levels in moderately stressed subjects.

Antimalarial activity has been documented against *Plasmodium yoelii* in rodent models, with quassinoids acting as antimalarial principles.

Anti-cancer properties have been investigated in vitro, with eurycomanone demonstrating cytotoxicity against breast, lung, and prostate [[blesching-cannabis-apoptosis-and-cancer-cell-death]] lines via apoptosis induction.

Anti-inflammatory and anti-oxidant activities have been confirmed through NF-κB pathway modulation and free radical scavenging assays.

## Cultivation

Propagation is primarily from seeds, which require scarification and warm stratification for germination.

Seedlings require 6–12 months in nursery conditions before field transplanting.

Root harvest typically begins at 4–5 years of age, with optimal bioactive compound accumulation reported at 5+ years.

The plant grows slowly in shade conditions (0.5–1 cm stem diameter per year) but responds to moderate light with accelerated growth.
