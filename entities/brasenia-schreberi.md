## Overview

**Brasenia schreberi**, commonly known as watershield, is a floating-leaved [[biodiversity-fungi-freshwater-aquatic-habitats]] plant in the family Cabombaceae.

It is the sole extant species in the genus *Brasenia*, making it a monotypic taxon with a distribution spanning [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]], East Asia, and Australia.

The species is distinguished by the thick mucilaginous coating covering its leaves and stems, which gives the foliage a distinctive gelatinous texture.

Watershield has a long history of human use as a food plant in East Asian cuisines, where it is valued for its nutritional properties.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Brasenia schreberi** J.F.Gmel. was described by Johann Friedrich Gmelin in 1791.

The species epithet honors Johann Christian Daniel von Schreber, an 18th-century German naturalist.

It belongs to the order Nymphaeales, family Cabombaceae, closely related to the water lily family Nymphaeaceae.

The family Cabombaceae contains two genera: *Brasenia* (monotypic) and *Cabomba* (approximately 5 species).

The plant is a perennial rhizomatous herb with a creeping rhizome anchored in bottom sediments.

Petioles are long and flexible, 30–100 cm, connecting submerged rhizomes to floating leaves at the water surface.

Floating leaves are elliptic to oblong, 5–12 cm long and 3–6 cm wide, with entire margins and no lobes.

The upper [[aact-foliar-sticker-spreaders-leaf-surface-attachment-ingham]] is glossy green, while the underside is reddish-purple and densely covered with a slippery mucilage.

This gelatinous coating, secreted by specialized trichomes, is a unique feature that protects leaves from herbivory and desiccation.

Submerged leaves are smaller, linear, and lack the mucilaginous coating of the floating leaves.

Flowers are solitary, small, 10–15 mm in diameter, with dark purple-red petals borne on long pedicels above the water surface.

Blooming occurs from June through September, with flowers opening above water and closing at night.

The fruit is a small leathery capsule containing 1–3 seeds, approximately 8 mm long.

## Distribution and Habitat

**Brasenia schreberi** has a broad disjunct distribution across the Northern and Southern Hemispheres.

In [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]] it occurs from eastern Canada and the northeastern [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]] south to Florida and Texas, and west to the Great Plains.

In East Asia it is found in China, Japan, Korea, and eastern Russia, where it is more widely distributed than in North America.

Isolated populations also occur in southeastern Australia and Tasmania.

The species grows in ponds, lakes, slow-moving rivers, and marshes in water 0.5–3.0 meters deep.

It prefers acidic to circumneutral waters with soft substrates rich in [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]].

Optimal conditions include clear water with full to partial sun exposure and stable water levels.

The plant is tolerant of moderate shade and can persist beneath a partial canopy of emergent vegetation.

## Ecology and Interactions

The mucilaginous coating on leaves and stems deters herbivory by aquatic insects, snails, and waterfowl.

Despite this defense, beavers, muskrats, and some waterfowl species do consume the rhizomes and seeds.

The floating leaf canopy provides shade and habitat for fish, amphibian larvae, and aquatic invertebrates.

Submerged foliage offers attachment surfaces for periphyton and shelter for small aquatic organisms.

The plant contributes [[fukuoka-textdoc-composting-critique-futility-prepared-organic-matter]] to aquatic food webs through [[leaf-senescence-autumn-colour-change-active-plant-process]] and rhizome decomposition.

Pollination occurs primarily through self-pollination, though some insect visitation has been documented.

Beetles and flies are occasional floral visitors, but the flowers are largely autogamous.
