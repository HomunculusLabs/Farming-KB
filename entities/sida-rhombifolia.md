# Sida rhombifolia

Sida rhombifolia, commonly known as picao preto, Queensland hemp,
arrowleaf sida, or broomweed, is an annual or perennial herb in
the family Malvaceae with a pantropical distribution. Native to
the Old World tropics, it has become naturalized throughout the
Americas, Africa, Asia, and the Pacific islands. The species has
a long history of use in traditional medicine across multiple
cultures and has attracted ethnobotanical interest for its mild
psychoactive and sedative properties.

In Brazilian folk medicine, S. rhombifolia is valued as a calming
and analgesic herb, often consumed as a tea for nervous
conditions, headaches, and pain relief. It is sometimes included
in traditional smoking mixtures in South America. The plant
contains a range of bioactive alkaloids, flavonoids, and
ephedrine-like compounds that contribute to its diverse
pharmacological activities, though its psychoactive effects are
mild compared to dedicated visionary plants.

## Taxonomy and Classification

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Phylum:** Tracheophyta
- **Class:** Magnoliopsida
- **Order:** Malvales
- **Family:** Malvaceae
- **Subfamily:** Malvoideae
- **Tribe:** Malveae
- **Genus:** Sida
- **Species:** S. rhombifolia

First described by Carl Linnaeus in 1753. The species epithet
'rhombifolia' refers to the rhombic (diamond-shaped) leaf blades,
a distinctive morphological character. Multiple subspecies and
varieties have been described, reflecting the species' wide
distribution and morphological variability. Related species in
the genus include S. acuta (common sida) and S. cordifolia
(bala), both of which share similar medicinal properties and
alkaloid profiles.

## Description

### Growth Habit and Morphology

- **Habit:** Erect annual or short-lived perennial, 30-150 cm tall
- **Stem:** Slender, cylindrical, green to reddish, covered in stellate and simple hairs
- **Leaves:** Alternate, simple; blade rhombic to ovate, 2-7 cm long and 1-4 cm wide, toothed margin, stellate-pubescent on both surfaces; petiole 0.5-3 cm
- **Flowers:** Solitary or in small clusters from leaf axils; calyx 5-lobed; corolla pale yellow, petals 6-8 mm long; flowering year-round in tropical climates
- **Fruit:** Schizocarp, disc-shaped, 5-8 mm diameter, breaking into 8-10 single-seeded mericarps
- **Seeds:** Reniform, dark brown, 1.5-2 mm long
- **Root:** Branched taproot with fibrous lateral roots

## Distribution and Habitat

- **Native range:** Old World tropics — possibly Africa or South Asia
- **Naturalized range:** Throughout tropical and subtropical regions worldwide
- **Habitat:** Disturbed sites, roadsides, pastures, cultivated fields, waste ground
- **Elevation:** 0-1500 m
- **Climate:** Tropical and subtropical; tolerates moderate drought
- **Soil:** Grows in a wide range of soils from sandy to clay; common in agricultural areas

S. rhombifolia is considered a weed in many agricultural systems,
particularly in tropical pastures and row crops. Its prolific
seed production and ability to persist in disturbed soils make it
a successful colonizer. Despite its weedy status, it is
deliberately cultivated in some regions for its medicinal
properties and as a source of fiber.

## Chemistry and Active Compounds

| Compound | Class | Notes |
|----------|-------|-------|
| Ephedrine | Alkaloid | Sympathomimetic amine, mild stimulant |
| Pseudoephedrine | Alkaloid | Decongestant properties |
| Sida alkaloids | Alkaloid | Including vasicine (peganine) |
| Vasicine (peganine) | Quinazoline alkaloid | Bronchodilator, uterotonic |
| Vasicinone | Quinazoline alkaloid | Related to vasicine |
| Flavonoids | Flavonoid | Quercetin, kaempferol derivatives |
| Phenolic acids | Phenolic | Chlorogenic, caffeic acids |

The presence of ephedrine and pseudoephedrine in S. rhombifolia
is pharmacologically significant, as these compounds are
adrenergic agonists that stimulate the sympathetic nervous
system. However, concentrations are generally much lower than in
Ephedra species. The quinazoline alkaloid vasicine contributes
bronchodilatory and anti-inflammatory effects, explaining the
traditional use for respiratory conditions.

## Traditional Medicinal Uses

- **Brazil (picao preto):** Tea for anxiety, insomnia, pain, and inflammation; topical applications for skin wounds and infections
- **India (Ayurveda):** Used as a febrifuge, diuretic, and anti-inflammatory; root preparations for nervous disorders
- **West Africa:** Decoctions for malaria, diarrhea, and respiratory infections
- **China:** Whole plant used as an anti-inflammatory and analgesic
- **Southeast Asia:** Leaf paste applied to headaches and wounds

## Ethnobotanical and Psychoactive Notes

While S. rhombifolia is not a major visionary or entheogenic
plant, it has been included in traditional smoking mixtures in
parts of South America. The ephedrine-like compounds may
contribute mild stimulant effects. In Brazilian
ethnopharmacology, it is sometimes grouped with other calming
herbs (such as Leonurus sibiricus) in teas or smoking blends
intended to produce relaxation and mild sedation. Its
psychoactive profile is best described as subtly calming rather
than hallucinogenic.

## Cultivation Notes

S. rhombifolia is not typically cultivated commercially but grows
readily from seed. Seeds germinate without pretreatment in 1-2
weeks. The plant prefers full sun and tolerates a wide range of
soil conditions. It is moderately drought-tolerant. Harvest
aerial parts during flowering for maximum bioactive compound
content. The plant can become invasive in tropical agricultural
areas.

## Legal Status

- **Uncontrolled worldwide** — not listed on any international drug convention
- Widely available as a weed; sold in some herbal markets in Brazil, India, and Southeast Asia
- Ephedrine content is minimal and does not trigger ephedrine-related regulations in most jurisdictions
