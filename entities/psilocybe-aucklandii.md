# Psilocybe aucklandii

*Psilocybe aucklandii* Guzman, King and Bandala is a psilocybin-containing mushroom endemic to New Zealand's North Island. Named for the Auckland region, it was described by the collaborative work of Mexican mycologist Gaston Guzman, C.J. King of the University of Auckland, and Bandala. John W. Allen includes it among the confirmed hallucinogenic fungi of Australasia, with photographs credited to C.J. King.

## Taxonomy

The species was described by Guzman, King and Bandala as part of their systematic study of New Zealand Psilocybe species. The specific epithet *aucklandii* refers to the Auckland region of New Zealand's North Island where the species was first documented. It is one of the more recently described psychoactive Psilocybe species and contributes to the growing recognition of New Zealand's distinctive native psychoactive mycoflora.

The collaborative nature of the description — involving a Mexican specialist in Psilocybe taxonomy (Guzman) and New Zealand-based researchers (King and Bandala) — exemplifies the international effort required to document Australasian fungal diversity.

## Distribution

*Psilocybe aucklandii* is documented from:

- **Auckland region**, North Island, New Zealand
- **North Island** — broader distribution confirmed

The species appears to be restricted to the North Island of New Zealand, with no confirmed records from the South Island or from Australia. This geographic restriction is shared with some other New Zealand psychoactive fungi but contrasts with the wider distribution of species like [[psilocybe-subaeruginosa]] and [[psilocybe-semilanceata]].

## Habitat

Allen's guide documents the species primarily from the Auckland region of the North Island. The specific habitat details are limited in the available source material, but given its classification among wood-inhabiting Psilocybe species, it likely grows on woody debris, forest litter, or in similar forest floor environments rather than on dung.

The Auckland region supports a mix of native podocarp-broadleaf forest and introduced plantation forestry. If *P. aucklandii* is associated with native forest ecosystems, it may be restricted to remnant bush patches in the Auckland isthmus, such as the Waitakere Ranges and Hunua Ranges. If it can utilize exotic substrates, it may have a wider distribution in parks, gardens, and forestry plantations.

## Description

While detailed morphological descriptions are limited in the available source material, *P. aucklandii* is expected to share the general features of the *Psilocybe* section to which it belongs. As a wood-inhabiting species, it likely has:

- A conic to convex cap, brown to caramel in color, hygrophanous and often paler when dry
- Adnate to adnexed gills, darkening from grey-brown to purplish-brown with spore maturity
- A slender, cylindrical stipe that bruises blue when handled
- A distinct pellicle on the cap surface, removable when moist
- Spores that are dark brown to purple-brown in deposit, ellipsoid to subellipsoid

Photographs by C.J. King accompanying Allen's guide provide visual documentation but have not been formally described in taxonomic literature.

## Potency

Specific dosage information is not provided in Allen's guide. As with other New Zealand Psilocybe species including [[psilocybe-makarorae]] and [[global-psychedelic-mushroom-species-diversity]], bioassays have reportedly indicated high potency, though these reports remain anecdotal. The species presumably contains psilocybine and likely psilocine, as evidenced by blue bruising reactions typical of the genus.

## Chemical Analysis

No published chemical analyses of *P. aucklandii* exist in the peer-reviewed literature. The blue bruising reaction strongly suggests the presence of psilocybin and/or psilocin. Given its relationship to the *Cyanescens* group of Psilocybe species, baeocystin and norbaeocystin may also be present as minor alkaloids. Quantitative analysis using HPLC or GC-MS of authenticated voucher specimens would be needed to establish the complete alkaloid profile and determine relative potency.

## Cultivation Notes

No documented cultivation of *P. aucklandii* has been reported. Its wood-inhabiting lifestyle suggests it may respond to similar cultivation techniques used for other temperate wood-decomposing Psilocybe species, such as sterilized hardwood sawdust or wood chip substrates. Cold-shocking to induce fruiting would be appropriate given the temperate North Island climate. However, the species has never been cultured in vitro, and its specific nutritional requirements remain unknown.

## New Zealand's Native Psychoactive Mycoflora

*Psilocybe aucklandii* is part of a group of psychoactive fungi that may represent New Zealand's endemic fungal fauna:

- **P. aucklandii** — Auckland/North Island endemic
- **P. makarorae** — South Island species associated with Nothofagus forests
- **P. subaeruginosa** — Present in NZ since 1995, possibly introduced
- **P. semilanceata** — Widespread, likely introduced with livestock

The distinction between endemic and introduced psychoactive species in New Zealand is an active area of investigation. While dung-inhabiting species like [[panaeolus-cyanescens-cultivation-guide]] and [[psilocybe-semilanceata]] are almost certainly introduced (see [[cattle-dispersal-of-psychoactive-fungi]]), forest-inhabiting species like *P. aucklandii* and *P. makarorae* may have evolved in situ in New Zealand's native ecosystems.

## Related Species

- **Psilocybe makarorae** Johnston and Buchanan — South Island counterpart; Nothofagus-associated
- **Psilocybe australiana** Guzman and Watling — Australian species also found in NZ's North Island
- **Psilocybe eucalypta** Guzman and Watling — Australian/NZ species
- **Psilocybe tasmaniana** Guzman and Watling — Australian species found in NZ's North Island
- **Psilocybe subaeruginosa** Cleland — Widespread in NZ since 1995

## Research Significance

*Psilocybe aucklandii* represents the underexplored mycological diversity of New Zealand's native forests. The country's geographic isolation and unique flora (dominated by Nothofagus, Podocarpaceae, and other Gondwanan lineages) suggest that its fungal fauna may include many undescribed and poorly known species. Systematic surveys combining morphological and molecular approaches would likely reveal additional psychoactive and non-psychoactive species.

The Auckland region, despite being New Zealand's most urbanized area, still contains significant native forest remnants. These fragmented habitats serve as refugia for native fungal species and deserve systematic mycological survey.

## Legal Status

Controlled under the New Zealand Misuse of Drugs Act 1975 as a psilocybin-containing species. Collection, possession, cultivation, and sale are prohibited.
