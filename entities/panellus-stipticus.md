## Panellus stipticus

Panellus stipticus is a saprotrophic basidiomycete in the family
Mycenaceae, widely known as one of the brightest bioluminescent
mushrooms in the Northern Hemisphere. Found across temperate and boreal
forests of [[canary-grass-phototropism-model-organism]] for studying fungal luciferin-luciferase
systems. Unlike many [[bloomfield-asterophora-and-mycoparasites-of-fruiting-bodies]] were used to staunch bleeding wounds
in European folk medicine. The genus was transferred from Pleurotus to
Panellus by Petter Karsten in 1879 based on spore and gill differences.

## Bioluminescence

Panellus stipticus is one of ~120 known bioluminescent fungal species
and among the most intensely luminous. Bioluminescence is produced
primarily in gill tissue, appearing as a steady green glow (~525 nm)
visible in complete darkness with dark-adapted eyes. Under ideal
conditions with multiple fruiting bodies, the glow has been reported
bright enough to read text by.
