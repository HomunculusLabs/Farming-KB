# C.E.L.T. (Community Enterprise Loans Trust)

## Description

C.E.L.T. (Community Enterprise Loans Trust) is a New Zealand-wide charitable trust
established to promote and support small businesses and cooperatives through education,
training, and low-interest lending. Documented by [[bill-mollison]] in *[[permaculture]]: A
Designer's Manual*, C.E.L.T. represents a national-scale approach to community economic
development that combines financial services with hands-on business mentoring. It has
since achieved formal banking status, expanding its capacity to serve community enterprises.

## Classification

- **Category:** Charitable trust / Community development finance institution
- **Domain:** Permaculture -- [[informal-and-formal-community-economics]], cooperative support
- **Location:** Nationwide in New Zealand
- **[[mollison-pdc-community-legal-structure]]:** Charitable trust, later achieved bank status
- **Function:** Small business and cooperative financing, education, and training

## How It Works

1. **Funding Sources:** C.E.L.T. is funded by public subscriptions ($5), donations, and
   government special schemes
2. **Education:** Runs training sessions teaching cooperative business skills to members
3. **Advisory Services:** Provides hands-on advice to people forming and running cooperatives
   and other enterprises
4. **Deposits:** Accepts cash deposits from the public at variable interest rates
5. **Lending:** Provides loans to community enterprises, working closely with borrowers
   until they are established
6. **Interest Rates:** Depositors receive 0-12% per year depending on deposit duration and
   whether they want interest paid
7. **Borrowing Criteria:** Entrepreneurs must be willing to work closely and regularly with
   C.E.L.T. staff during the loan period
8. **Banking Status:** Achieved formal bank status, enabling full banking services

## Core Services

- **Formation Support:** Helps people structure and legally establish cooperatives
- **Business Training:** Teaches cooperative governance, financial management, and marketing
- **Advisory:** Ongoing mentorship and problem-solving for funded enterprises
- **Financing:** Low-interest loans with flexible terms for community-beneficial businesses
- **Banking:** Full banking services including deposits, transfers, and account management

## Key Principles

- **Close Partnership:** Borrowers work closely with C.E.L.T. throughout the loan period,
  not just at application
- **Education First:** Business training is provided alongside financing to ensure success
- **Cooperative Model:** Prioritizes cooperative and community-owned enterprise structures
- **Accessible Entry:** $5 subscription makes membership available to anyone
- **Patience:** Variable interest rates allow depositors to choose lower returns to support
  lower lending rates

## Key Facts

- Operates nationwide across New Zealand
- Accepts both small subscriptions ($5) and larger deposits
- Interest rates on deposits range from 0-12% depending on terms
- Mandatory close working relationship between borrower and C.E.L.T. staff
- Achieved bank status, providing full financial services to communities
- Combines charitable, educational, and financial functions in one organization
- Funded by public subscriptions, donations, and government schemes

## Advantages Over Conventional Banking

- **Holistic Support:** Combines financing with education and ongoing mentorship
- **Community Focus:** Prioritizes community benefit over profit maximization
- **Low Barrier to Entry:** $5 subscription enables broad participation
- **Flexible Terms:** Variable interest rates accommodate diverse needs
- **Cooperative Expertise:** Specialized knowledge in cooperative governance and management
- **Patient Capital:** Works with borrowers through difficulties rather than calling in loans

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Cooperative Farming Support:** C.E.L.T.-style organizations can finance and mentor
  cooperative [[duggar-mushroom-growing-history-and-methods]] operations, [[gaias-garden-creating-community-gardens]], and shared cultivation
  facilities
- **Value-Chain Development:** Helps build complete local food value chains from cultivation
  through processing to retail, keeping all value within the community
- **Training Integration:** Combines financing with [[mushroom-cultivation-training-program-design]], ensuring new growers
  have both capital and knowledge to succeed
- **Processing Infrastructure:** Can fund shared-use facilities like community kitchens,
  drying rooms, and [[substrate-preparation]] areas used by multiple cultivators
- **Market Development:** Supports establishment of farmers markets, food cooperatives,
  and direct-trade systems that create reliable outlets for cultivated products
- **Research Funding:** Can finance cultivar trials, substrate experiments, and growing
  technique development that benefits the entire local growing community
- **Land Access Trusts:** C.E.L.T.-style trusts can hold land in trust for community
  cultivation, removing land cost barriers for new growers

## Implementation Lessons

- The combination of education and financing dramatically improves success rates
- Government partnership (special schemes) provides additional capital and legitimacy
- Achieving bank status unlocks regulatory advantages and public confidence
- The $5 subscription model builds broad community ownership and participation
- Close borrower-lender relationships reduce default rates below conventional banking
- Training in cooperative governance prevents the internal conflicts that destroy many
  community enterprises
