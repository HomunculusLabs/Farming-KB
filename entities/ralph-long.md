# Ralph Long

## Description

Ralph Long is an Australian inventor who devised the Long Inclinometer, a tractor-mounted level used for cutting drains with the tractor itself serving as a check on levels. His innovation was documented in Permaculture Journal #22 (1985). The Long Inclinometer represents a practical fusion of farm machinery and surveying capability, allowing farmers to lay out drains and earthworks without separate surveying equipment.

## Classification

- **Category:** Person / Inventor / Farmer
- **Nationality:** Australian
- **Field:** Agricultural engineering / Farm surveying
- **Era:** Active in 1980s

## Key Facts

- Inventor of the Long Inclinometer
- Based in Australia
- Published in Permaculture Journal #22, 1985
- His device is a bubble level that can be fitted to any vehicle on farms
- Successfully used a tractor-mounted level to cut drains
- The tractor itself serves as a verification tool for levels during drain cutting

## The Long Inclinometer

- **Type:** Bubble level adapted for vehicle mounting
- **Mounting:** Tractor-mounted (also adaptable to other farm vehicles)
- **Function:** Enables drain cutting while simultaneously checking levels
- **Advantage:** Eliminates the need for separate pre-surveying of drain routes
- **Availability:** Documented in Permaculture Journal #22, 1985

## Relevance to Cultivation

Long's invention exemplifies the permaculture principle of integrating functions. By combining the drain-cutting tool (tractor) with the surveying tool (level), a single machine operation achieves what would otherwise require two passes and separate equipment. This approach is particularly valuable for broadacre permaculture where kilometers of contour drains may need to be cut across a landscape.

The Long Inclinometer joins the Bunyip level, A-frame, and plane table as low-cost surveying tools that democratize precision earthworks for farmers and smallholders.
