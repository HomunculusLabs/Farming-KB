# Ready-to-Till Test

## Description

The "ready-to-till test" is a simple field method for determining whether soil is at the correct moisture content for digging or tilling without causing structural damage. Working soil that is too wet creates rock-hard clods; working soil that is too dry is impossibly difficult or creates dust. This test is essential when working clay soils, which have a very narrow window of workable moisture, but is valuable for all soil types.

## Classification

- **Category:** Soil assessment technique
- **Type:** Field test / tactile assessment
- **Purpose:** Determine workable soil moisture
- **Essential for:** Clay soils, new garden preparation

## Key Facts

- **Method:** Take a small handful of soil from 3-4 inches below the surface and squeeze it into a ball about the size of a golf ball
- **Interpretation — Ready to work:** Cradling the ball in your palm, press firmly with your thumb. If it comes apart easily and crumbles, the soil is at the right moisture.
- **Interpretation — Too wet:** If the ball is gooey and your thumb merely makes a dent in the ball sticky side, the soil is too wet. Digging now will create rock-hard clods.
- **Interpretation — Too dry / no clay:** If the soil would not form a ball that sticks together, it is either too dry or contains no clay. A clayless soil can be worked at any moisture level but will not hold water well.
- **Critical for clay:** Clay soils have a very brief window of correct moisture; missing this window delays spring planting significantly
- **Consequences of ignoring test:**
  - Too wet: Forms instant rock-hard clods nearly impossible to break up
  - Too dry: Forms dust that slumps into airless goo when first rained on or irrigated

## Test Procedure

1. Dig a small sample from 3-4 inches (7.5-10 cm) below the soil surface
2. Compress firmly in your hand to form a ball about golf-ball sized
3. If the soil will not form a ball at all — either too dry or clayless (proceed with caution)
4. Cradle the ball in your palm
5. Press firmly on the ball with your thumb from your other hand
6. **Ball crumbles easily** — Perfect moisture for working
7. **Ball stays intact, thumb dents it** — Too wet; wait for drying
8. **Ball is rock-hard** — Too dry; wait for rain or irrigate

## Soil Type Considerations

| Soil Type | Window Size | Consequence of Error |
|---|---|---|
| Clay | Very narrow | Severe clod formation; delayed planting |
| Loam | Moderate | Manageable; some clodding possible |
| Sandy loam | Wide | Rarely a problem |
| Pure sand | Always workable | No clods possible; drought risk instead |
