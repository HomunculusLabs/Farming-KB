# Amanita gemmata

*Amanita gemmata*, commonly known as the gemmed amanita or jeweled deathcap, is a species of basidiomycete fungus in the family Amanitaceae. It is closely related to the iconic fly agaric (*[[amanita-muscaria-muscimol-ibotenic-acid-pharmacology]] and muscimol. While its reputation and bioactive profile are less extensively documented than those of *A. muscaria*, *A. gemmata* represents an important member of the psychoactive Amanita group and has been the subject of increasing phytochemical and pharmacological interest.

## Taxonomy and Description

*Amanita gemmata* is a medium-sized mushroom with a cap typically 3–8 cm in diameter. The cap is initially hemispherical, becoming convex to plano-convex with age, and has a smooth, slightly sticky surface when moist. The cap color is variable, ranging from pale yellow and golden-yellow to pale ochre, sometimes with a slight orange tint. The cap margin typically bears short, radial grooves (striations) that correspond to the position of the gills beneath.

The universal veil remnants appear as small, white to cream-colored warts or patches on the cap surface—hence the common name "gemmed" amanita. These patches are often sparse and may be washed away by rain, making field identification more challenging. The volva at the base of the stem forms a short, membranous sac or collar.

The gills are free from the stem, crowded, and white to cream-colored. The stem is 5–10 cm tall and 0.8–1.5 cm thick, white, cylindrical or slightly tapering upward, and bears a membranous, pendant annulus (ring) near the apex. The base of the stem is typically bulbous and enclosed in a sac-like volva.

The [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]]. The species is associated with both coniferous and deciduous forests, forming ectomycorrhizal associations with various tree species including pines, spruces, oaks, and beeches.

Fruiting occurs from late summer through autumn, typically from August through October. The mushroom is found in a variety of forest types, from lowland woodlands to montane and subalpine forests.

## Taxonomic Considerations

The taxonomy of the *Amanita gemmata* complex has been the subject of considerable debate. Some mycologists have treated *A. gemmata* as part of a variable species complex that includes *A. muscaria* and *A. pantherina*, while others recognize it as a distinct species. Molecular phylogenetic studies have generally supported the recognition of *A. gemmata* as a separate species, though some populations previously assigned to *A. gemmata* may represent distinct taxa.

## Bioactive Compounds

Like other members of section *Amanita* of the genus *Amanita*, *A. gemmata* contains the characteristic psychoactive compounds of this group:

### Ibotenic Acid

Ibotenic acid (α-amino-3-hydroxy-5-isoxazoleacetic acid) is a glutamate receptor agonist that acts primarily on ionotropic glutamate receptors, particularly AMPA and metabotropic Group I receptors. It is the predominant psychoactive compound in fresh *A. gemmata* fruiting bodies and is responsible for many of the mushroom's pharmacological effects.

Ibotenic acid is a polar, water-soluble compound that is structurally related to glutamic acid. Its agonist activity at glutamate receptors can lead to neuronal excitation, and at high doses it exhibits neurotoxic properties. This excitotoxic potential has made ibotenic acid a useful research tool in neuroscience, where it is employed as a selective neurotoxin for creating experimental lesions.

### Muscimol

Muscimol (5-aminomethyl-3-hydroxyisoxazole) is the decarboxylated derivative of ibotenic acid and is formed through dehydration of ibotenic acid during drying, heating, or extraction. Muscimol is the primary psychoactive compound in dried *A. gemmata* preparations and is significantly more potent than ibotenic acid.

Unlike ibotenic acid, muscimol acts as a potent and selective GABA_A receptor agonist. This mechanism of action is shared with benzodiazepines, barbiturates, and alcohol, though muscimol's [[growing-gourmet-north-american-reishi-species]] hardwood forests.
