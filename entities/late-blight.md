## Late Blight

## Description
Late blight is a devastating plant disease caused by the oomycete pathogen *Phytophthora infestans*, infamous for causing the Irish Potato Famine (1845-1852). It affects primarily potatoes and tomatoes but can infect other members of the Solanaceae family. The disease spreads rapidly in cool, moist conditions and can destroy entire crops within days. Elaine Ingham reports extensive experience managing late blight using compost tea approaches, noting significant success when adequate fungal biomass is maintained on leaf surfaces.

## Classification
- **Causal organism**: Phytophthora infestans
- **Kingdom**: Chromista (formerly classified as fungus)
- **Phylum**: Oomycota
- **Class**: Oomycetes
- **Order**: Peronosporales
- **Family**: Peronosporaceae
- **Genus**: Phytophthora
- **Species**: P. infestans
- **Note**: Oomycetes are not true fungi — they are water molds with distinct biology

## Key Facts

### Disease Development
- Spreads rapidly in cool (60-70F, 15-21C), wet, humid conditions
- Produces sporangia on leaf undersides that are spread by wind and rain splash
- Can infect and kill foliage within 7-10 days under optimal conditions
- Zoospores swim in free water on leaf surfaces to initiate infection
- Tubers can be infected through contact with sporangia washed into soil
- Overwinters in infected potato tubers (culls, volunteers)

### Symptoms
- **Foliage**: Water-soaked, irregularly shaped lesions that expand rapidly, turning brown-black
- **Lesion border**: White fuzzy growth (sporangia) visible on lesion undersides in moist conditions
- **Stems**: Dark brown-black lesions that can girdle and kill stems
- **Tubers**: Reddish-brown, granular rot that extends into flesh; firm at first, then soft rot may follow
- **Tomato fruit**: Dark, greasy, firm lesions that can encompass entire fruit

### Historical Significance
- Caused the Irish Potato Famine (1845-1852), resulting in approximately 1 million deaths and mass emigration
- Led to the founding of plant pathology as a scientific discipline
- Remains one of the most economically destructive plant diseases globally
- Annual global losses estimated in billions of dollars

### Biological Management via AACT
- Elaine Ingham reports extensive experience with late blight management using compost tea
- Fungal component in AACT is critical — beneficial fungi compete with P. infestans for resources and space on leaf surfaces
- Preventive applications are essential — once established, late blight progresses too rapidly for remedial action
- Strategy involves maintaining continuous beneficial organism coverage on foliage
- Ingham notes they have suppressed many kinds of blight using compost tea approaches
- The mechanism is competitive exclusion: beneficial organisms occupy the ecological niches that late blight would exploit

### Integrated Management Approaches
- Destroy potato culls and volunteers to reduce overwintering inoculum
- Use certified disease-free seed potatoes
- Monitor weather conditions and use forecasting models
- Ensure good air circulation through proper plant spacing
- Avoid overhead irrigation that creates wet foliage conditions
- Hill potatoes well to protect tubers from spore wash-down
- Destroy infected plants immediately to prevent spread

### Challenges
- Extremely rapid disease cycle — can produce new spores within 4-5 days
- Long-distance spore dispersal via wind makes regional management important
- Multiple mating types (A1 and A2) can produce resistant oospores in some regions
- Fungicide resistance is an increasing concern (metalaxyl/mefenoxam resistance widespread)
- Climate change may expand the geographic range and disease pressure

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology
- **Potato and tomato production**: Most destructive disease of these crops globally
- **Biological control research**: Important test case for compost tea efficacy against aggressive oomycete pathogens
- **Food security**: Historically and currently a threat to global food security
- **Disease forecasting**: Pioneer in weather-based disease prediction models
- **Sustainable agriculture**: Reducing fungicide dependence for late blight is a major research priority
- **Compost tea validation**: More replicated field trials needed to document efficacy of AACT against late blight
