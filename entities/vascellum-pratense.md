## Vascellum pratense

## Description

*Vascellum pratense* (formerly *Lycoperdon pratense*) is a small puffball fungus in the family Agaricaceae, order Agaricales. It is found in grasslands, meadows, pastures, and open woodland across Europe and North America. The fruiting body is roughly spherical to depressed-globose, typically 2–5 cm in diameter, with a white outer surface that yellows with age. Like other puffballs, it releases spores through an apical pore when mature. This species is notable for its demonstrated ability to accumulate **methylmercury** at high concentration factors.

## Key Facts

- Recorded as having one of the highest **methylmercury concentration factors** (38.0) among fungi studied by Fischer et al. (1995).
- Methylmercury content in fruiting bodies reached 1.56 µg Hg/g dry weight, while total mercury was 8.2 µg Hg/g dry weight.
- The concentration factor for methylmercury (38.0) was dramatically higher than the concentration factor for total mercury, demonstrating selective accumulation of the more toxic methylated form.
- Inhabits open grassland habitats, often appearing in groups or scattered on nutrient-poor soils.
- The genus *Vascellum* is separated from *Lycoperdon* based on structural differences in the peridium (outer wall) and capillitium (sterile threads among spores).

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

*Vascellum pratense* is significant for understanding fungal mercury dynamics:

- **Mercury biomagnification**: The high methylmercury concentration factor (38.0) demonstrates that this species selectively bioaccumulates the most toxic form of mercury, with implications for food chain transfer to herbivores and decomposers.
- **Environmental monitoring**: The species could serve as a bioindicator for mercury contamination in grassland ecosystems.
- **Comparative toxicology**: Alongside *[[coprinus-comatus]]* (concentration factor of 198.5 for MeHg), *Vascellum pratense* demonstrates that puffballs and their relatives can be significant vectors for methylmercury in terrestrial food webs.
- **Bioremediation research**: Understanding the mechanisms of methylmercury accumulation could inform strategies for using fungi in mercury-contaminated site remediation.
- **Food safety**: As a member of grassland fungal communities, its mercury accumulation properties are relevant to grazing livestock and human foragers.

The data on *V. pratense* contributes to the broader understanding that fungal mercury methylation and accumulation are widespread phenomena across diverse fungal lineages, not limited to specific taxonomic groups.
