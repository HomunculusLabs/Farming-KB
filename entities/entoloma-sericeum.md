## Entoloma sericeum

## Description

**Entoloma sericeum** is an ectomycorrhizal basidiomycete fungus distinguished by its exceptionally high phosphatase and phytase enzyme activity. Research by Antibus et al. (1992) demonstrated that this species shows remarkably high phosphorus uptake from organic sources, making it one of the most enzyme-competent ectomycorrhizal species studied.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Phylum**: Basidiomycota
- **Class**: Agaricomycetes
- **Order**: Agaricales
- **Family**: Entolomataceae
- **Genus**: Entoloma
- **Species**: E. sericeum

## Key Facts

- Exhibits the highest phosphorus uptake from organic sources among [[ectomycorrhizal-fungi]] studied by Antibus et al. (1992)
- Phosphorus uptake from organic (Po) sources measured at 4000 CPM mg dm⁻¹ h⁻¹ — vastly higher than from inorganic (Pi) sources at 136.5 CPM
- Produces high levels of both mycelial surface acid phosphatase (pNPPase: 18.3 mg dm⁻¹ h⁻¹ from Po) and soluble acid phosphatase (41.4 mg dm⁻¹ h⁻¹ from Po)
- Phytase enzyme activity measured at 60.0–83.8 nmol P released mg protein⁻¹ h⁻¹
- The dramatic difference between organic and inorganic phosphorus uptake indicates strong adaptation for accessing complex organic P sources
- This enzyme competency suggests a role in late-successional forest communities where organic P dominates

## Ecological Role

E. sericeum is ecologically significant because:

- It is one of the most effective ectomycorrhizal species at mobilizing phosphorus from organic sources
- Likely plays a crucial role in phosphorus cycling in mature forest ecosystems with organic-rich soils
- Its high phytase activity allows it to decompose phytate ([[inositol-hexaphosphate]]), a major organic P pool in soils
- Demonstrates that some ectomycorrhizal species are better adapted to organic nutrient acquisition than inorganic uptake
- Contributes to understanding of functional diversity within ectomycorrhizal communities

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- Important model for studying phosphatase enzyme systems in [[mycorrhizal-fungi]]
- Potentially valuable for inoculation of tree seedlings on phosphorus-poor, organic-rich soils
- Demonstrates the range of functional capabilities within ectomycorrhizal fungal communities
- Relevant to forestry practices on sites with high organic matter and low available phosphorus
- Contributes to understanding enzyme-mediated nutrient acquisition strategies
