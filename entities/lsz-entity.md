
# LSZ

## Overview

LSZ, also known as N6-(2,4-dimethylazetidin-1-yl)-N6-propionylnor-
lysergamide or [[lysergic-acid]] 2,4-dimethylazetidide, is a synthetic
psychedelic of the lysergamide class. Structurally related to LSD,
LSZ features a dimethylazetidine ring in place of the diethylamide
moiety, along with an N6-propionyl group. First synthesized by Albert
Hofmann and documented in TiHKAL, LSZ produces effects closely
resembling LSD — visual hallucinations, altered cognition, and
enhanced emotional sensitivity — with reports of a slightly shorter
duration and subtly different experiential character.

## Chemical Identity

- **IUPAC name:** (6aR,9R)-N-propionyl-7-propyl-6,6a,8,9,10,10a-
  hexahydroindolo[4,3-fg]quinoline-9-carboxamide
- **Molecular formula:** C23H29N3O2
- **Molecular weight:** 375.50 g/mol
- **CAS number:** Unregistered in most public databases; primarily
  known through TiHKAL documentation.

The core modification from LSD is replacement of the diethylamide
group with a 2,4-dimethylazetidinyl amide. This four-membered
azetidine ring constrains amide substituent geometry, potentially
affecting [[1p-lsd]], [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]]:** Prosecutable under the Federal Analogue Act
