# DOEF

**Chemical name:** 2,5-Dimethoxy-4-(2-fluoroethyl)-amphetamine
**PiHKAL entry:** #65

## Overview

DOEF is a member of the [[2c-family-compounds-overview]] carrying a 2-fluoroethyl group at the 4-position. The introduction of a fluorine atom into the alkyl chain is a significant structural modification, as fluorine's unique properties (high electronegativity, small size, metabolic stability) can dramatically alter a compound's pharmacological profile.

[[alexander-shulgin]] describes DOEF as a compound where "all systems [are] intact, body, mind and emotion" -- a notably positive assessment that places it among the more benign members of the psychedelic amphetamines.

## Dosage

| Level | Amount (mg) |
|-------|-------------|
| Threshold | ~2 |
| Light | 2 - 2.5 |
| Common | 2.5 - 3.5 |

## Duration

12 - 16 hours. Consistent with the extended duration typical of DOx compounds.

## Effects

The qualitative reports from [[pihkal-qualitative-effects-themes]] are remarkably positive:

- **At 2.2 mg:** A gradual growth into a "slightly unworldly" state with no appreciable visual component. Time moved slowly. Music became magical. Comfortable driving was possible at 10 hours. Good sleep followed, though dreams were described as "pointless."
- **At 3.0 mg (first report):** Very slow onset (~3 hours to full activity). Absolutely no body discomfort. A "general humorousness about my state of mind which is always a good sign." Notable erotic response -- "far from anti-erotic" with full, "exceptionally delicious" orgasm at the 5th-6th hour. Good for writing. "No dark corners apparent at all." Would take again at higher dose.
- **At 3.0 mg (second report):** No body threat at any time. Good eyes-closed imagery with complex visualizations to music, but not much with eyes open. Short attention span, easily diverted. "Quite reminiscent of doi-entity|DOI both as to dosage and effect." Still too alert to sleep at 13 hours.

## Character Assessment

DOEF appears to be one of the "gentler" members of the DOx series. Key characteristics include:

- **Smooth onset:** Gradual, without "discernible jumps"
- **Comfortable body feel:** No physical discomfort reported
- **Erogenic properties:** Noted as potentially enhancing
- **Cognitive clarity:** Good for writing and conversation
- **Visual profile:** More eyes-closed than eyes-open imagery
- **Comparable to:** doi-entity|DOI in both dosage range and general effect profile

## Synthesis Notes

DOEF's synthesis is notably more complex than most PiHKAL entries, involving several sophisticated steps:

1. **Silyl protection of DOB:** Free base dob-entity|DOB is protected with a disilylethylene group.
2. **Lithium-halogen exchange:** Treatment with t-butyllithium followed by ethylene oxide introduces the 2-hydroxyethyl group, yielding DOEH (mp 102-104 deg C).
3. **Fluorination:** DOEH is converted to the fluoroethyl derivative using DAST (diethylaminosulfur trifluoride), a specialized fluorinating agent.
4. **Deprotection:** The final steps involve hydrolysis and isolation as the hydrochloride salt (mp 205-208 deg C).

The multi-step synthesis reflects the challenge of introducing fluorine at a specific position on an existing molecule.

## Pharmacology

The fluorine substitution may confer metabolic stability (fluorine-carbon bonds are resistant to oxidative metabolism), potentially contributing to the long duration. The 2-fluoroethyl group occupies a similar spatial volume to the ethyl group of [[doet-compound-profile]] but with different electronic properties, which may explain the subtle differences in character.

## Shulgin's Assessment

Shulgin's description of DOEF -- "all systems intact, body, mind and emotion. Gentle. Good for writing. No dark corners apparent at all" -- places it among the more favorable compound evaluations in PiHKAL. This contrasts sharply with compounds like dobu-entity|DOBU (described as potentially dangerous due to slow onset) or don-entity|DON (noted for anxiety and physical discomfort). The favorable profile likely reflects both the specific pharmacological properties of the fluoroethyl group and the dose range at which it was tested.

DOEF's profile suggests that fluorine substitution may be an underexplored avenue for optimizing psychedelic phenethylamines, though the complex synthesis required to introduce the fluorine atom limits practical exploration.
