# Clitocybe vibecina

## Description

*[[clitocybe]] vibecina* (now reclassified as *Infundibulicybe vibecina* in some modern taxonomies) is a small to medium-sized saprotrophic basidiomycete fungus in the family Tricholomataceae, order Agaricales. It produces funnel-shaped (infundibuliform) fruiting bodies with pale grey-brown to tan caps (2–6 cm), decurrent gills that are white to cream, and a slender, flexible stem. The flesh is thin and has a characteristic strong, mealy to farinaceous (flour-like) odor. It fruits in troops or rings on the forest floor, typically among leaf litter in deciduous and mixed woods.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Phylum**: Basidiomycota
- **Class**: Agaricomycetes
- **Order**: Agaricales

- **Genus**: *Clitocybe* (reclassified to *Infundibulicybe* by some authors)

## Key Facts

- Recorded in Belgian forest ecosystems with **radiocesium** levels of **630 Bq/kg** following the Chernobyl disaster (Fraiture et al., 1990).
- This radiocesium level was among the **lowest** of all fungi measured in the Belgian survey, reflecting its saprotrophic lifestyle (vs. higher accumulation in ectomycorrhizal species).
- The low radiocesium accumulation in *C. vibecina* supports the broader observation that **[[saprotrophic-fungi]] generally accumulate less radiocesium** than ectomycorrhizal species.
- As a saprotroph, it decomposes leaf litter and organic matter on the forest floor, contributing to nutrient cycling.
- Often confused with other *Clitocybe* species, some of which contain muscarine and are toxic.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

*Clitocybe vibecina* provides important comparative data in radioecological studies:

- **Saprotroph vs. mycorrhizal comparison**: With only 630 Bq/kg compared to 8,000–27,000 Bq/kg in mycorrhizal species like *Cortinarius* and *Xercomus*, *C. vibecina* exemplifies the consistent pattern of lower radiocesium accumulation in saprotrophic fungi.
- **Ecosystem-level contamination mapping**: The contrast between saprotrophic and mycorrhizal accumulation levels provides insight into the different pathways through which radionuclides move through forest food webs.
- **Taxonomic reclassification**: The move from *Clitocybe* to *Infundibulicybe* reflects ongoing molecular phylogenetic revisions in the Agaricales, illustrating the dynamic nature of [[fungal-taxonomy]].
- **Toxicology caution**: Its similarity to toxic *Clitocybe* species (particularly *C. dealbata* and *C. rivulosa*, which contain dangerous levels of muscarine) underscores the risks of misidentification in foraging.
- **Litter decomposition ecology**: As a leaf litter decomposer, *C. vibecina* plays a role in the forest floor nutrient cycle, processing organic matter in a different soil horizon than the [[mycorrhizal-fungi]] that accumulate more radiocesium.

This species demonstrates how [[fungal-ecology]] (saprotrophic vs. mycorrhizal lifestyle) strongly influences radionuclide accumulation patterns and has implications for food safety in radiation-affected regions.
