## Description

Tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) is the primary psychoactive constituent of Cannabis. Specifically, delta-1-tetrahydrocannabinol (also called delta-9-THC) is responsible for the main narcotic effects of marihuana and hasheesh. THC is a non-nitrogenous terpenophenolic compound classified as a dibenzopyran, distinct from the alkaloid class that contains most other hallucinogens.

## Classification

- **Category**: Psychoactive cannabinoid
- **Chemical class**: Terpenophenolic compound / Dibenzopyran
- **Molecular formula**: C21H30O2
- **Notable**: Non-nitrogenous (unlike most hallucinogens which are alkaloids)
- **Source**: Cannabis species (C. sativa, C. indica, C. ruderalis)

## Key Facts

- Main effects are attributable to delta-1-tetrahydrocannabinol (delta-9-THC)
- Non-nitrogenous [[vioxx-scandal-evidence-based-medicine-gaps-lessons-cannabis-research-blesching-2015]] was the synthesis of pure THC for controlled studies

## Chemistry and Degradation

- Fresh plant contains mainly cannabidiolic acid (precursor)
- Over time, inactive cannabidiolic acid converts to active THC
- THC eventually degrades to inactive cannabinol
- These conversions happen faster in tropical than cooler climates
- Material from plants of different ages may vary in narcotic effect
- The unstable character of constituents explains high variability between strains

## Pharmacological Effects

- **Primary effect**: Euphoria
- **Psychological**: Dreamy state, altered consciousness, distorted time perception
- **Perceptual**: Exaggeration of sound, visual hallucinations (occasionally vivid)
- **Physical**: Increased pulse rate and blood pressure, tremor, vertigo
- **Motor**: Difficulty in muscular coordination, dilation of pupils
- **Not addictive** but psychological dependence may result from continual use
- Highly variable from person to person and between plant strains

## Historical Context

- Cannabis is one of the oldest cultivated plants
- Chinese tradition puts use back 4,800 years
- Indian medical writing before 1000 B.C. reports therapeutic uses
- Known as "liberator of sin" and "delight giver" in Chinese tradition
- Early Hindus called it "heavenly guide" and "soother of grief"
- The term "assassin" may derive from "hashishins" (13th century Asia Minor)
