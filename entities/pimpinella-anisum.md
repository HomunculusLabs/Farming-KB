# Pimpinella anisum

## Overview

Pimpinella anisum, commonly known as anise or aniseed, is an annual herb
in Apiaceae native to the eastern Mediterranean and Southwest Asia. It is
cultivated worldwide for its aromatic seeds, which are among the oldest and
most widely used spices in human history, dating back over 4,000 years.

The seeds contain 2–6% [[medicinal-herb-growing]], and fragrance ingredient
across Greek, Roman, Egyptian, Indian, and Chinese traditions.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Taxonomy:** Angiosperms → Eudicots → Apiales → Apiaceae → Pimpinella
anisum L.

**Growth habit:** Annual herb, 30–80 cm tall. Erect, branching stem,
finely grooved, with short hairs at nodes.

**Leaves:** Basal leaves petiolate, cordate to orbicular, coarsely
toothed, 2–5 cm long. Upper leaves pinnately divided, increasingly
filiform toward the inflorescence.

**Flowers:** Small, white, in compound umbels 2–5 cm across. Terminal
umbel larger than lateral ones. Petals 5, ovate, slightly notched.

**Fruit:** Schizocarp, 3–5 mm long, ovoid to oblong, grayish-brown with
five prominent ridges. These are the commercial "aniseed."

## Distribution and Cultivation

Native to the eastern Mediterranean (Egypt, Turkey, Levant) and Southwest
Asia. Naturalized in southern Europe, North Africa, and [[quercetin]], [[apigenin]], luteolin
glycosides), coumarins, phenylpropanoids, sterols ([[stigmasterol]],
β-sitosterol), and fixed oil (16–23% of seed weight, rich in petroselinic
acid).

## Pharmacology

Traditional uses span Greek, Roman, Egyptian, Ayurvedic, and Chinese
medicine as carminative, expectorant, galactagogue, and antispasmodic.

**Modern evidence:**
- **Carminative/antispasmodic:** Anethole relaxes intestinal smooth muscle
  in vitro. EMA-approved for dyspepsia and flatulence.
- **Expectorant:** Increases respiratory mucus secretion. EMA-approved for
  upper respiratory catarrh.
- **Galactagogue:** Anethole has estrogenic activity; clinical evidence
