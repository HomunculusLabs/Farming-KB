## ICRAF (International Centre for Agroforestry)

## Description

The International Centre for Research in Agroforestry (ICRAF), now known as the
World Agroforestry Centre, is an international research organisation headquartered
in Nairobi, Kenya. Established in 1978, ICRAF is one of the 15 research centres
supported by the Consultative Group on International Agricultural Research (CGIAR).
The centre is dedicated to generating science-based knowledge about the complex
role of trees in agricultural landscapes and using this research to improve the
lives of smallholder farmers and the environment.

In the context of Holmgren"s permaculture text, ICRAF is highlighted for its
research during the 1990s on the use of fast-growing leguminous shrubs and trees
to help the poorest African farmers rebuild soil fertility and provide fodder for
dairy cows. This work represents one of the most dramatic and well-documented
examples of biological services replacing industrial inputs in agriculture,
producing corn yield increases of up to tenfold in subsistence farming systems.

## Key Facts

- Founded in 1978 to address the role of trees in sustainable agricultural
  development, particularly in tropical developing countries.
- Headquartered in Nairobi, Kenya, with research programmes across sub-Saharan
  Africa, Southeast Asia, South Asia, and Latin America.
- During the 1990s, ICRAF researched fast-growing leguminous shrubs and trees
  for integration into African subsistence farming systems.
- Their research demonstrated that biological nitrogen fixation through leguminous
  tree intercropping could increase corn yields by up to tenfold for the poorest
  farmers dependent on natural fertility alone.
- The leguminous trees and shrubs provided multiple benefits: soil nitrogen
  fixation, fodder for dairy cows, subsequent manure production, and milk
  for protein and cash income.
- Some legumes used in African agroforestry originated from Mexico, creating an
  ironic connection: Mexico is the original home of corn (maize), which was
  brought to Africa centuries ago.
- ICRAF's work has led to adoption of agroforestry practices across Kenya and
  beyond, representing a "classic Permaculture solution" using plants as
  substitutes for unavailable conventional fertilisers.
- Now merged with CIFOR (Centre for International Forestry Research) to form
  CIFOR-ICRAF, expanding the scope to include forest landscape research.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Mycorrhizal Enhancement**: Leguminous trees used in ICRAF"s agroforestry
  systems form mycorrhizal associations that enhance [[phosphorus-availability]]
  and support soil fungal networks critical for crop nutrition.
- **Soil Food Web**: The integration of trees, crops, and livestock creates
  diverse root systems and organic matter inputs that support complex soil
  biological communities, including fungal decomposers and mycorrhizal partners.
- **Nitrogen Fixation Symbiosis**: The legume-rhizobium symbiosis at the heart
  of ICRAF's approach parallels other fungal-plant symbioses, demonstrating how
  biological partnerships can replace external inputs.
- **[[rhizosphere-ecology]]**: Tree-crop [[intercropping-systems]] create diverse
  rhizosphere environments that support a wider range of soil microorganisms
  than monoculture systems, including beneficial fungi.
- **Organic Matter Cycling**: The leaf litter and root turnover from
  fast-growing leguminous trees provide abundant organic matter that feeds
  [[saprophytic-fungi]] and builds humus-rich soils.
- **Scaling Biological Solutions**: ICRAF demonstrates that biological approaches
  to soil fertility can be scaled from garden to landscape, relevant to anyone
  designing integrated cultivation systems that incorporate fungal elements.
- **Modelling for Permaculture**: The ICRAF approach is cited as a "classic
  Permaculture solution," providing a research-validated model for biological
  services in agriculture that can inform [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]] system design.
