# Peter Henderson

## Description

Peter Henderson (1822–1890) was a Scottish-born American horticulturist and market gardener, widely regarded as the father of American market gardening. His 1867 book *Gardening for Profit* was the first American text devoted entirely to commercial vegetable production and became one of the most influential agricultural works of the 19th century.

## Classification

- **Category**: Person — horticulturist, author, market gardener
- **Era**: 19th century (1822–1890)
- **Nationality**: Scottish-American
- **Field**: Commercial vegetable production, market gardening

## Key Facts

- Authored *Gardening for Profit*, first published in 1867 by Orange Judd Company, with multiple editions through 1907
- The book was described as "the classic of classics" in American market gardening literature
- It inspired a generation of commercial growers to take up market gardening as a profession
- Henderson operated extensive market gardens in New Jersey, supplying New York City
- He was among the first to systematize vegetable production methods for American conditions
- His work emphasized intensive cultivation, efficient use of space, and sound business practices
- He also wrote *Gardening for Pleasure* and *Hand Book of Plants*
- His methods formed a bridge between European intensive traditions and American commercial agriculture

## Relevance to Cultivation

Henderson's work is foundational to understanding the development of intensive vegetable production in North America. His emphasis on:

- **Intensive spacing**: Maximizing yield per unit area through careful plant arrangement
- **[[succession-planting]]**: Continuous harvesting through staggered sowing schedules
- **[[soil-fertility-management]]**: Maintaining productive capacity through systematic amendment
- **Business acumen**: Treating market gardening as a profitable enterprise requiring planning and record-keeping

These principles directly influenced the systems later refined by [[eliot-coleman]] and other organic growers. Henderson's practical orientation toward profitability alongside quality production remains relevant to small-scale commercial vegetable operations today.

## Historical Context

Henderson's work appeared during a period when American agriculture was transitioning from subsistence to commercial models. His books provided practical guidance at a time when most agricultural literature was geared toward field crops rather than intensive vegetable production. The Parisian *maraicher* system was contemporaneous with Henderson's career, and parallels between his methods and French intensive techniques are notable.

## Legacy

- *Gardening for Profit* remained in print for over 40 years through multiple editions
- The book is still cited as a valuable historical reference for vegetable production methods
- Henderson's emphasis on systematic, business-like market gardening helped establish commercial vegetable growing as a respected profession in America
- His writing style — clear, practical, and experience-based — set a standard for agricultural writing that influenced later authors including Coleman
