## Overview

**Canna edulis** Ker Gawl., commonly known as **achira**, **canna**, or **Queensland arrowroot**, is a perennial rhizomatous plant in the family Cannaceae, native to the Andean region of South America. It is cultivated primarily for its edible starchy rhizomes, which have been a food source for indigenous Andean peoples for thousands of years.

The species is both an important traditional food crop and a widely grown ornamental plant. It produces large, banana-like leaves and showy flowers in shades of red, orange, and yellow, making it a dual-purpose species for food production and landscape design.

## Nutritional Value and Culinary Use

The rhizomes are composed primarily of **starch (approximately 70–80% dry weight)**, making them one of the most starch-dense root crops available. The starch granules are notably large (40–100 µm), which gives achira starch unique functional properties.

**Macronutrients (per 100 g fresh weight):**

- Energy: ~80–100 kcal
- Carbohydrates: 18–23 g (predominantly starch)
- Protein: 1–2 g
- Fat: <0.5 g
- Dietary fiber: 2–3 g

**Key minerals:** Potassium, phosphorus, magnesium, and calcium in moderate amounts. Vitamin C content is low compared to other [[comparison-cassava-vs-taro-vs-sweet-potato-vs-yam]] il. Its height and leaf area make it a useful windbreak for smaller crops.
