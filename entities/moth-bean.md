# Moth Bean (Vigna aconitifolia)

## Description

The moth bean, also known as mat bean or dew bean, is a small-seeded, low-growing legume native to the arid regions of the Indian subcontinent. It is one of the most heat- and drought-tolerant pulse crops cultivated anywhere in the world. Mollison recommends moth bean alongside [[permaculture]] principle of working with nature rather than against it. Where most legumes succumb to desert heat and drought, moth bean thrives - producing food, fixing nitrogen, and protecting soil simultaneously. Its inclusion alongside the Indigenous American tepary bean in Mollison's desert garden succession demonstrates the global synthesis of arid-adapted crops that [[dolichos]] - another drought-tolerant leguminous vine for desert systems

## Varieties and Genetic Resources

- Rajasthan holds the greatest diversity of moth bean landraces
- Cultivars range from prostrate mat-forming types to semi-erect bush types
- Seed colors include yellow, brown, greenish, and mottled forms
- India's National Bureau of Plant Genetic Resources (NBPGR) maintains extensive germplasm collections
- Limited breeding work has been done compared to other pulse crops
- Cross-compatibility with other Vigna species offers breeding potential

## Agronomic Data

- Days to maturity: 60-90 days (one of the fastest maturing pulses)
- Seed rate: 10-15 kg/ha under dryland conditions
- Yield potential: 300-800 kg/ha under rainfed arid conditions
- Row spacing: 30-45 cm between rows, 5-10 cm within rows
- No inoculation typically needed; native rhizobia usually effective
- Minimal pest and disease pressure under arid conditions
