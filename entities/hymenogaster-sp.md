# Hymenogaster sp.

## Description

*Hymenogaster* is a genus of sequestrate (truffle-like) fungi in the family Hymenogastraceae. These hypogeous fungi produce closed, underground fruiting bodies that do not open to release spores, relying instead on mycophagous animals for dispersal. Species in this genus are ectomycorrhizal associates of various tree species.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Hymenogastraceae
- **Genus:** *Hymenogaster*

## Key Facts

- Exhibits high tolerance to multiple heavy metals including Al (200 mg/L), Fe (200 mg/L), Cu (10 mg/L), and Zn (100 mg/L) at 50% inhibition concentrations.
- Among the most heavy-metal-tolerant ectomycorrhizal genera tested, comparable only to *[[pisolithus-tinctorius]]* and *Scleroderma* sp.
- Demonstrated significantly higher tolerance to aluminum and iron than genera such as *Thelephora* and *Cenococcum*.
- Like other sequestrate fungi, produces subterranean fruiting bodies that contribute to soil structure and nutrient cycling.
- Ectomycorrhizal lifestyle enables mutualistic associations with host trees, exchanging soil nutrients for plant-derived carbon.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

*Hymenogaster* species are of interest in mine spoil reclamation and contaminated site restoration due to their exceptional heavy metal tolerance. Their ability to form functional ectomycorrhizal associations under high metal stress makes them candidates for inoculation programs in degraded ecosystems. The sequestrate habit also contributes to soil food webs and mycorrhizal inoculum potential in forest soils.

## Ecological Role

- Ectomycorrhizal symbiont contributing to host plant nutrient acquisition
- Potential bioremediation agent in heavy metal-contaminated soils
- Subterranean fruiting bodies serve as food source for small mammals, aiding spore dispersal
- Contributes to below-ground fungal biomass and soil aggregation
