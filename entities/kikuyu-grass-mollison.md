## Description

Kikuyu grass (Pennisetum clandestinum, syn. Cenchrus clandestinus) is a vigorous, rhizomatous and stoloniferous perennial grass native to the highland regions of East Africa (Kenya). It has become one of the most widespread and persistent pasture and weed grasses in tropical and subtropical regions worldwide, particularly in Australia, Hawaii, and the Pacific Islands. In permaculture systems, kikuyu is both valued as a productive pasture species and regarded as one of the most challenging weeds to manage in food gardens and orchards.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Family**: Poaceae
- **Genus**: Pennisetum (sometimes classified as Cenchrus)
- **Species**: P. clandestinum
- **Common names**: Kikuyu grass, kikuyu
- **Growth form**: Perennial, stoloniferous and rhizomatous grass, forming dense mats 10-45 cm tall
- **Native range**: East African highlands (Kenya, Ethiopia, Tanzania), 1950-3000 m elevation
- **Introduced range**: Australia, New Zealand, Hawaii, Pacific Islands, South Africa, Mediterranean, California

## Key Facts

- Extremely aggressive spreader via both above-ground stolons and below-ground rhizomes, forming dense mats that exclude most other plant species.
- Highly palatable and nutritious as a pasture grass, supporting high stocking rates for cattle and other grazing animals.
- Tolerant of close grazing, mowing, and a wide range of soil types and moisture conditions.
- Difficult to eradicate once established, as rhizome fragments as small as 2-3 cm can regenerate into new plants.
- In tropical home gardens, kikuyu is one of the primary weed species that must be suppressed for successful food production.
- Thick mulch application (20-25 cm / 8-10 inches) is one of the few effective non-chemical methods for kikuyu suppression.
- Produces seed heads that should be excluded from mulch to prevent garden re-infestation.
