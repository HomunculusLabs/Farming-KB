# Rimol Greenhouse Systems

## Description

Rimol Greenhouse Systems is a greenhouse manufacturing company that collaborated with [[eliot-coleman]] on the design and development of a mobile greenhouse on wheels, marketed under the name "Rolling Thunder." Located at Northpoint Industrial Park, 40 Londonderry Turnpike, Hooksett, NH 03106. Contact: (603) 629-9004, www.rimolgreenhouses.com.

## Classification

- **Entity Type:** Company / Greenhouse Manufacturer
- **Industry:** Agricultural Structures, Protected Cultivation
- **Location:** Hooksett, New Hampshire, USA
- **Notable Product:** Rolling Thunder mobile greenhouse

## The Rolling Thunder Mobile Greenhouse

The Rolling Thunder is the flagship mobile greenhouse design resulting from the collaboration between Rimol Greenhouse Systems and Eliot Coleman. Its design addresses the specific needs of intensive, small-scale organic farming through mobility and structural flexibility.

### Design Features

1. **Heavy-duty wheels with bearings** — Mounted at each set of hoops, attached to specially designed "base posts"
2. **Wheel/base-post combination** — Seated on a rail system that allows the greenhouse to move along desired growing areas
3. **Rail system** — Laid out flat according to desired greenhouse width; does not require pinning or staking but should be set on a solid base to prevent sinking
4. **Tractor mobility** — Larger greenhouses (such as 30-by-96 feet) can be moved with an ordinary tractor
5. **Manual mobility** — Smaller greenhouses require only two individuals to move
6. **Adjustable height** — Various height options available through different length base posts

### Construction Process

- The rail is laid out flat according to the desired width
- The first few hoops are the most complex, requiring several sets of hands because wheels, base posts, and hoops are erected simultaneously
- Once a few hoops are up and diagonally braced, remaining construction proceeds quickly
- Significant bracing is installed to prevent wind-induced movement
- Temporary bracing is used on end walls for stability during construction

## Role in Four-Season Farming

The mobile greenhouse concept is central to Coleman's [[winter-harvest-system]] because it addresses fundamental challenges of intensive organic farming:

1. **Crop Rotation** — The greenhouse can be moved between growing areas, allowing the soil underneath to recover and preventing the buildup of soil-borne pathogens and pests
2. **Soil Health** — Mobile structures avoid the permanent soil coverage that leads to soil degradation in stationary greenhouse systems
3. **Field Integration** — The greenhouse can be positioned over field-planted crops for winter protection, then moved to expose them to spring conditions
4. **Succession Management** — Multiple growing areas can be served by a single mobile structure

## Key Facts

- Resulted from direct collaboration between Eliot Coleman and Rimol engineers
- Designed specifically for the needs of small-scale commercial organic farming
- The rail system does not require permanent foundation work
- Bracing design addresses wind loads without compromising mobility
- Available in both small and large configurations to suit different farm scales

## Relevance to Cultivation

Rimol Greenhouse Systems and the Rolling Thunder design represent a critical innovation in protected-culture agriculture:

- **Soil Management:** Mobile greenhouses prevent the soil degradation common in permanent greenhouse installations, maintaining the deep-organic principles of soil-first farming
- **Pest and Disease Management:** By rotating the greenhouse position, growers break pest and disease cycles without chemical interventions
- **Economic Efficiency:** A single mobile structure can serve multiple field areas, reducing capital investment per protected acre
- **Organic Compliance:** The system eliminates the need for soil fumigation or sterilization sometimes used in permanent greenhouse operations
- **Adaptability:** The adjustable height and modular design allow farms to customize the structure to their specific crops and climate conditions
