## Overview

*Auricularia delicata* is a species of jelly fungus in the family Auriculariaceae, order Auriculariales. It is widely distributed across tropical and subtropical regions and is commercially cultivated as an [[bloomfield-boletus-satanus-edible-mushroom-hazards]] in parts of [[psilocybe-southeast-asia-pacific]].

The species is closely related to the more commercially prominent *Auricularia polytricha* (wood ear mushroom) but can be distinguished by its paler coloration, finer surface hairs, and slightly thinner gelatinous [[bloomfield-mushroom-formation-fruiting-body-development-cultivation]].

## Taxonomy

Basidiomycota → Agaricomycetes → Auriculariales → Auriculariaceae → ***Auricularia* → *A. delicata***

- **Authority:** (Fr.) P. Karst. (1879)
- **Basionym:** *Tremella delicata* Fr. (1822)
- **Synonyms:** *Auricularia fibrillosa* (Berk. & M.A. Curtis) Farl., *Auricularia tenuis* (Berk. & M.A. Curtis) Underw.
- **Common names:** white wood ear, snow ear mushroom, pale jelly fungus

## Morphology

Fruiting body is gelatinous to rubbery, ear-shaped or cup-shaped, 2–8 cm in diameter.

- **Upper surface:** Light tan to pinkish-brown, covered in fine short hairs (setae) visible under magnification; surface is smooth to slightly wrinkled.
- **Hymenial surface (underside):** Smooth, lighter in color (whitish to pale tan), often with a frost-like or pruinose appearance; strongly gelatinous when fresh.
- **Flesh:** Translucent, gelatinous, 1–3 mm thick; rubbery and elastic when fresh, becoming brittle and horny when dried.
- **[[boil-a-bag-spore-print-technique-jarrold]]:** White to cream.
- **Spores:** Allantoid (sausage-shaped), 12–16 × 5–6 μm, smooth, thin-walled, hyaline, inamyloid.
- **Basidia:** Cylindrical to clavate, transversely septate (phragmobasidia), 50–65 × 4–5 μm, with four sterigmata.

## Distribution and Habitat

*A. delicata* has a [[copelandia-cyanescens-pantropical-distribution-and-ecology]], found throughout Southeast Asia, Oceania, sub-Saharan Africa, and tropical regions of the Americas.

The species fruits year-round in warm, humid climates, with peak fruiting during the rainy season.

- **Substrate:** Saprotrophic on dead hardwood, especially broadleaf trees including *Acacia*, *Mangifera*, *Hevea*, *Durio*, and *Eucalyptus* species.
- **Habitat:** Lowland and montane [[fungal-biodiversity-tropical-forests]], plantations, orchards, and agroforestry systems; commonly found on fallen branches, stumps, and logs.
- **Altitude:** Sea level to approximately 1,500 m elevation.

## Cultivation

*A. delicata* is cultivated on a modest commercial scale, primarily in Thailand, Vietnam, and southern China, often alongside the more common *A. polytricha*.

- **Substrate:** [[oyster-mushroom-supplemented-sawdust-blocks]] (rubber wood, mango wood, or mixed hardwood), rice straw, or bagasse in polypropylene bags.
- **Spawn:** Grain spawn (wheat or sorghum) inoculated from [[comparison-seed-saving-techniques-vs-tissue-culture-cloning]] or spore print.
- **Fruiting temperature:** 25–32 °C; unlike temperate species, it requires consistently warm conditions.
- **Relative humidity:** 85–95% during fruiting.
- **CO₂ tolerance:** Tolerates moderately elevated CO₂ levels, making it suitable for enclosed growing structures.
- **Crop cycle:** 25–35 days from inoculation to first flush; total yield 200–350 g fresh per kg dry substrate.
- **Harvest:** Fruit bodies are twisted off by hand when fully expanded but still soft and gelatinous.

## Edibility and Culinary Use

*A. delicata* is edible and widely consumed across its native range. Its culinary properties closely resemble those of *A. polytricha*.

- **Texture:** Crisp and crunchy when cooked, with a mild gelatinous snap; retains some firmness in soups.
- **Flavor:** Very mild, almost neutral, absorbing flavors of accompanying ingredients.
- **Preparation:** Typically rehydrated if dried, then added to stir-fries, soups, hot pots, and salads; often combined with garlic, ginger, soy sauce, and chili.
- **Nutrition:** Low in calories; contains dietary fiber, polysaccharides, small amounts of protein, iron, and B vitamins.
- **Dried shelf life:** Excellent — dried [[bloomfield-asterophora-and-mycoparasites-of-fruiting-bodies]] store for 12+ months at room temperature in sealed containers.
