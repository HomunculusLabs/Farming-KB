## Overview

**Thin Mint Cookies** (often shortened to "Thin Mint" or "Thin Mints") is a phenotype of Girl Scout Cookies (GSC), one of the most celebrated cannabis hybrids of the 2010s. It emerged from the San Francisco Bay Area cannabis scene and quickly became one of the most sought-after phenotypes within the GSC genetic line.

The strain is known for its complex mint-chocolate aroma, deep purple-green coloring, and potent psychoactive effects.

Thin Mint consistently ranks among the highest-THC GSC phenotypes, with laboratory-tested samples regularly exceeding 25% THC.

It won the 2014 High Times Cannabis Cup in Amsterdam in the best U.S. strain category.

The award cemented Thin Mint's reputation as the definitive GSC expression and propelled it into mainstream cannabis culture. It remains one of the most recognizable strain names in the modern market.

## Lineage

Thin Mint is a specific phenotype of Girl Scout Cookies, itself a cross between **OG Kush** and **Durban Poison**. The "Thin Mint" designation refers to one of three primary GSC phenotypes, alongside the Forum Cut and the Platinum Cookies.

The OG Kush parent contributes the heavy, sedating indica influence, dense bud structure, and fuel-pine terpene character.

Durban Poison adds uplifting sativa effects, mental clarity, and the sweet, anise-like notes that characterize the Cookies line. The combination of these two legendary parents is what makes the GSC line so genetically potent.

Within GSC populations, Thin Mint is distinguished by its particularly pronounced minty terpene profile, darker coloration, and more indica-dominant growth pattern. It is considered approximately 60% indica / 40% sativa, though individual expressions vary.

## Appearance

Buds are dense, chunky, and medium to large in size. The calyxes are tightly packed with a compact structure typical of OG Kush-influenced varieties. Colors range from deep forest green to dark purple, often with striking purple-blue undertones.

A heavy coating of milky-white trichomes gives the buds a frosted, silver-green appearance. Orange pistils are abundant, curling through the resinous calyxes. The overall impression is dark, moody, and heavily crystallized.

Leaf structure shows broad indica-dominant leaflets with serrated edges. Some phenotypes exhibit dark red to purple stems and petioles, particularly when exposed to cooler night temperatures during late flowering.

## Aroma and Flavor

The aroma is dominated by a fresh mint character layered over rich chocolate and earthy undertones. A distinct cookie-dough sweetness emerges when buds are ground, with subtle notes of pepper, pine, and diesel in the background.

On inhalation, the flavor delivers cool mint and sweet earth.

The exhalation reveals the chocolate-cookie foundation, with a lingering minty freshness on the palate. The flavor is complex and dessert-like, living up to its name with remarkable fidelity.

The terpene profile is one of the most recognizable in modern cannabis. The combination of limonene, caryophyllene, and linalool creates the signature sweet-mint aroma, while myrcene contributes to the earthy, herbal depth.

## Terpene and Cannabinoid Profile

**Dominant terpenes:** Limonene (0.5–1.2%), Caryophyllene (0.4–0.9%), Linalool (0.3–0.7%), Myrcene (0.3–0.6%).

Limonene provides the bright, citrus-mint top notes and contributes mood-elevating effects.

Caryophyllene adds peppery warmth and functions as a CB2 receptor agonist, contributing anti-inflammatory properties. Linalool contributes the sweet floral character and anxiolytic effects.

**Cannabinoid profile:** THC typically ranges from 20–28%, with some tested samples reaching 30%. CBD is very low, usually below 0.1%. The strain produces significant THCV in some phenotypes (0.1–0.3%), which may contribute to the clear-headed aspects of its effects.

Minor cannabinoids detected include CBG (0.1–0.3%) and CBC (trace amounts). The entourage effect of high THC with limonene and linalool is considered key to the strain's distinctive psychoactive character.

## Effects

Onset is rapid, with cerebral effects felt within minutes of inhalation. The initial experience is euphoric and uplifting, with enhanced mood, creativity, and sociability. This head high is attributed to the Durban Poison sativa influence.

Within 30–45 minutes, the OG Kush-influenced body effects emerge. A deep physical relaxation spreads from the neck and shoulders through the body, often described as a warm, enveloping sensation. Despite this relaxation, most users maintain mental clarity.

The strain is popular for evening and nighttime use.

At higher doses, the body effects can become quite sedating, leading to couch-lock and sleep. The transition from cerebral stimulation to physical relaxation makes Thin Mint versatile for different tolerance levels.

Common therapeutic applications include stress relief, anxiety management, mild pain, and insomnia.

The balanced onset allows patients to address mood symptoms while the later body effects help with physical tension and sleep. Medical cannabis patients frequently cite Thin Mint for its effectiveness against chronic stress and PTSD-related symptoms.

## Cultivation

Thin Mint is considered moderately difficult to cultivate, suited for growers with some experience.

It can be grown indoors or outdoors, though indoor cultivation produces the highest quality and most consistent phenotype expression. The strain requires attention to humidity and nutrient management.

Indoor flowering time is 9–10 weeks. Plants reach medium height (80–120 cm) with a bushy, lateral branching structure.

SCROG (Screen of Green) training is recommended to maximize light exposure and yield. LST (low-stress training) in early vegetative stage promotes even canopy development.

Expected yields are 350–450 g/m² indoors under optimal conditions.

Outdoor harvest occurs in mid to late October (Northern Hemisphere), with individual plants capable of producing 400–600 g each. Outdoor plants benefit from dry autumn conditions to prevent bud rot.

The strain responds well to moderate nutrient levels and benefits from cal-mag supplementation during flowering.

It is moderately resistant to mold and pests but can be sensitive to high humidity during late flowering, which may affect trichome preservation. Proper air circulation and dehumidification are recommended in the final weeks.

Temperature reduction during the final 2–3 weeks of flowering (dropping to 15–18°C at night) enhances purple coloration and can boost terpene production.

This cold-weather technique is commonly used to bring out the signature dark appearance. Harvest timing should be based on trichome maturity, with 70–80% cloudy/amber being optimal for maximum potency.
