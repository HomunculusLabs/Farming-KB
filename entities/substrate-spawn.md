# Substrate Spawn

## Description

Substrate spawn (also called sawdust spawn) is the third-generation expansion of mushroom mycelium, typically grown on hydrated sawdust supplemented with grain-based nutrients. It bridges the gap between [[grain-spawn]] and the final fruiting substrate by acclimating the mycelium to a wood-based medium similar to what it will colonize during fruiting. Substrate spawn is the most economical form of spawn for most cultivators, as it multiplies the inoculation potential of grain spawn across a much larger volume.

## Classification

- **Category**: Cultivation intermediate
- **Type**: Spawn / inoculum (generation 3)
- **Substrate**: Sawdust-based with grain supplements

## Key Facts

- Typically generation 3 in the spawn expansion pipeline
- Made from soaked and drained hardwood sawdust supplemented with wheat bran (~1/2 cup per 5 lb bag)
- Inoculated with grain spawn at approximately 1 cup per 5-pound bag of sterilized substrate
- Mycelium prefers to go from like to like — wood-decomposing species transition best through sawdust
- Aged sawdust (up to 3 months) holds moisture better than extremely fresh sawdust
- Sterilization: 15 PSI (~250°F / 121°C) for 1.5-2 hours
- Supplementation increases risk of contamination and thermogenesis; err on the lower side
- Sawdust should be moist but not wet (squeeze test: no water stream, sticks to hands)

## Relevance to Cultivation

Substrate spawn is the most cost-effective inoculum for most [[mushroom-cultivation]] operations. While grain spawn can be used directly for fruiting (yielding slightly higher yields), it is more expensive per unit volume. Substrate spawn allows a small amount of grain spawn to inoculate a much larger volume of fruiting substrate, reducing production costs significantly. The acclimation from grain to wood-based media through substrate spawn improves colonization performance on final fruiting substrates.

## Relevance to Mycology

[[substrate-spawn-production]] demonstrates the principle of gradual media transition in fungal cultivation. The concept that mycelium performs best when moved from like-to-like substrates reflects the adaptational plasticity of [[fungal-enzyme-systems]]. This principle has implications for understanding how fungi colonize new substrates in nature and how their metabolic machinery adjusts to changing nutrient environments.

## Recommended Recipe

Per 5-pound bag:
- 5 pounds (2.36 kg) soaked and drained hardwood sawdust
- 1/2 cup (~200 g) hydrated wheat bran
- Soak sawdust up to 12 hours; drain for about 1 hour
- Supplement with grains (corn, wheat, rye, millet, milo) for additional nutrition
