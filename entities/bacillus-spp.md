## Description

*Bacillus* spp. are a genus of rod-shaped, Gram-positive bacteria that are among the most important and versatile microorganisms found in soil, compost, and the plant rhizosphere. They are known for their ability to form endospores—highly resistant structures that allow them to survive extreme conditions including heat, desiccation, UV radiation, and chemical exposure. This resilience makes them key players in composting, soil fertility, and plant disease suppression.

## Classification

- **Domain**: Bacteria
- **Phylum**: Firmicutes
- **Class**: Bacilli
- **Order**: Bacillales
- **Family**: Bacillaceae
- **Genus**: *Bacillus*

## Key Facts

### Role in Composting
- Active during the **mesophilic phase** (68–104°F / 20–40°C) of composting, where they begin breaking down cellulose and other complex organic compounds through **depolymerization**.
- Produce **endospores** that survive the thermophilic phase (104–150°F / 40–65°C) and reactivate when temperatures cool, allowing them to participate in the later maturation stage of compost.
- Generate metabolic heat that contributes to raising compost pile temperatures.

### Role in Soil and Plant Health
- Many *Bacillus* species are **plant growth-promoting rhizobacteria (PGPR)**, enhancing plant growth through nitrogen fixation, phosphate solubilization, and production of phytohormones.
- Produce **antibiotics** and **lipopeptide compounds** (e.g., surfactin, iturin, fengycin) that suppress fungal and bacterial plant pathogens.
- Important members of the soil food web, serving as food for protozoa and nematodes, which in turn release plant-available nitrogen through excretion.

### Common Species in Soil
- *Bacillus subtilis* — widely used biocontrol agent and compost inoculant
- *Bacillus thuringiensis* (Bt) — produces insecticidal proteins
- *Bacillus megaterium* — phosphate solubilizer
- *Bacillus amyloliquefaciens* — produces antifungal compounds

### Survival Characteristics
- Endospores resist temperatures exceeding 100°C, allowing survival through thermophilic composting.
- Spore-forming ability enables long-term persistence in dry or nutrient-poor soils.
- Facultative anaerobes — can function in both aerobic and low-oxygen environments.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

In organic cultivation and mycology, *Bacillus* spp. serve multiple critical functions:

1. **Compost production**: Primary decomposers during early composting stages, initiating breakdown of cellulose and generating heat for thermophilic phase transition.
2. **Disease suppression**: Producing antibiotics that suppress pathogenic fungi (e.g., *Fusarium*, *Rhizoctonia*, *Pythium*), making them valuable in Integrated Pest Management.
3. **Nutrient cycling**: When consumed by protozoa and nematodes in the soil food web, their stored nitrogen is mineralized into plant-available ammonium (NH₄⁺).
4. **Compost tea**: *Bacillus* populations thrive in actively aerated compost teas (AACTs), where they contribute to foliar disease suppression when sprayed on leaf surfaces.
5. **Mushroom cultivation hygiene**: Some *Bacillus* species can be competitors on mushroom substrates, while others are used as biological controls against green mold (*Trichoderma* spp.) in commercial production.

## Environmental Preferences

- **Temperature range**: Mesophilic (20–45°C), with spores surviving much higher temperatures
- **pH range**: 5.5–8.5, with optimum near neutral (6.5–7.5)
- **Moisture**: Require adequate moisture for vegetative growth; spores survive dry conditions
- **Oxygen**: Facultative aerobes — prefer oxygen but can function anaerobically
