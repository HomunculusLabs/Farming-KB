## Overview

Hydrangea macrophylla, commonly known as bigleaf hydrangea or French hydrangea, is a deciduous shrub in the family Hydrangeaceae native to Japan and Korea.

It is the most widely cultivated hydrangea species, prized for its large showy inflorescences that change color depending on soil chemistry.

The species encompasses two primary floral forms: the mophead form with rounded flower clusters and the lacecap form with flat clusters.

Bigleaf hydrangea has become one of the most commercially important ornamental shrubs worldwide, with hundreds of named cultivars in cultivation.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Hydrangea macrophylla (Thunb.) Ser., family Hydrangeaceae, tribe Hydrangeeae. The species was first described by Carl Peter Thunberg in 1784 from Japanese collections.

The genus Hydrangea contains approximately 70 to 75 species distributed across Asia and the Americas.

Bigleaf hydrangea is a deciduous shrub growing 1 to 2 meters tall and wide, with a rounded, spreading habit.

## Flower Color Chemistry

The most distinctive feature of Hydrangea macrophylla is its ability to produce flowers ranging from blue to pink depending on [[citric-acid-cycle-tca-krebs-cycle]] in its roots, which chelates aluminum ions and facilitates their transport into aboveground tissues.

Sepal cell vacuoles serve as the primary aluminum storage site, where concentrations can reach several thousand parts per million.

Gardeners exploit this chemistry by applying aluminum sulfate or elemental sulfur to acidify soil and produce blue flowers.

Conversely, adding lime raises soil pH and shifts flower color toward pink or red tones.
