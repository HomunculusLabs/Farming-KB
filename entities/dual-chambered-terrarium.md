## Description

The Dual Chambered Terrarium is a fruiting chamber design developed by Psylocybe Fanaticus for the [[pf-tek-mushroom-cultivation-method]]. It is constructed from a standard 10-gallon aquarium divided into two sections by a slanted Plexiglas (acrylic) spray shield. The design achieves and maintains near-100% humidity while protecting the fruiting mycelium cakes from direct water droplet contact, which can damage delicate fungal structures and promote contamination.

## Classification

- **Category:** Fruiting chamber / terrarium design
- **Type:** Humidification system with physical barrier
- **Construction:** 10-gallon aquarium + Plexiglas partition + airtight lid
- **Inventor:** Psylocybe Fanaticus
- **Purpose:** High-humidity environment for mushroom fruiting

## Key Facts

- The terrarium is based on a standard 10-gallon aquarium with outside dimensions of 24" × 14 1/2" and inside dimensions of approximately 21 1/4" × 12".
- The **spray shield** (chamber partition) is made from 1/8" thick clear acrylic (Plexiglas) window insulation, cut to approximately 15" × 18". A tight fit is desirable.
- The aquarium is divided into a **primary chamber** (receives direct spray) and a **secondary chamber** (houses the fungus cakes).
- The **airtight lid** is constructed from a wooden frame (1/4" thick board or 4 wood strips) with clear polyethylene plastic film tacked to the underside, creating a seal against the aquarium top.
- Simpler lid alternatives include saran wrap or similar plastic film.
- The system achieves 100% humidity through a molecularization process: spray droplets strike the shield, are broken into finer mist, and flow around the partition into the secondary chamber.

## Operating Principle

The terrarium's effectiveness relies on a two-stage humidification process:

1. **Primary chamber:** Water is sprayed vigorously downward into the middle of the shield area. The spray strikes the Plexiglas barrier and is broken down into a finer mist.

2. **Secondary chamber:** The molecularized water vapor flows around the sides of the spray shield into the fungus chamber, where it provides humidity without exposing the mycelium to damaging water droplets.

3. **Condensation cycle:** Over time, humidity condenses on terrarium surfaces and drips down. The slanted spray shield acts as a drip shield and roof, directing condensation away from the cakes.

## Spraying Procedure

- Before placing cakes, spray all interior surfaces including the shield and lid.
- Slightly lift the lid, insert the spray bottle nozzle, and spray vigorously downward into the primary chamber for about 5 seconds.
- Immediately withdraw the nozzle and seal the lid to trap the swirling mist.
- Repeat 2–3 times per session, with at least 2 sessions daily.
- Compensate for missed daytime spraying with extra evening sessions.
- Interior surfaces should appear foggy or dripping with water after spraying.
- Mushrooms can grow with as little as one good spraying per day in a properly set up terrarium.

## Ventilation and Maintenance

- **Ventilation:** Remove the lid and fan the chamber with cardboard while holding the spray shield vertically, then respray. Perform with each spraying session.
- **Water removal:** Collected water at the bottom must be siphoned out to prevent bacteria buildup, using a rubber bulb battery filler or enema bottle.
- **Lighting:** Expose to normal room light (indirect sunlight). A small low-wattage fluorescent plant light encourages upward phototropic growth and can be left on continuously.
- **Heating:** Do NOT directly heat the terrarium. Direct heating works against humidification. No heating cables, pads, or blankets. Keep grow lights at a safe distance. Optimal range: 65–80°F+; they grow well even at 60°F. Excessive heat causes mycelial spreading without fruiting.

## Symptoms of Low Humidity

- Fuzzy white mycelium growing on top of caps (crown-like white hair) — not contamination, but indicates humidity is slightly low
- Deformed, convoluted, and withering mushrooms and primordia
- For best growth, humidity must be very high (near 100%)

## Relevance to Cultivation

The [[dual-chambered-terrarium-design]] is significant for several reasons:

- **No complex equipment:** Achieves professional-grade humidity control without humidifiers, foggers, or electronic controllers.
- **Gentle humidification:** The spray shield system prevents the damage that direct water droplets cause to fine mycelial networks, which would inhibit growth or promote contamination.
- **Low maintenance:** With proper setup, the system requires only manual spraying 1–2 times per day.
- **Scalable:** Multiple cakes can be fruited simultaneously in a single 10-gallon aquarium.
- **Cost effective:** Built from inexpensive, readily available materials (aquarium, Plexiglas, spray bottle, plastic film).
- **Alternative humidification:** A perlite-based passive humidification technique was later developed as a complementary method, described by PF as "proving once again, that less is more."
