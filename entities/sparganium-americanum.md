## Taxonomy and Morphology

Sparganium americanum belongs to the family Sparganiaceae within the order Poales.

Some modern classifications place Sparganium within the Typhaceae, but the Sparganiaceae is still widely recognized.

The species was described by Thomas Nuttall in 1818 and has been the subject of taxonomic revision involving several closely related taxa.

Distinction from Sparganium eurycarpum and Sparganium emersum requires careful examination of fruit characteristics and bract morphology.

The plant is a perennial emergent growing 50 to 150 centimeters tall from a thick, creeping rhizome.

Leaves are basal and cauline, linear, 5 to 15 millimeters wide, and up to 1 meter long, with a prominent midrib and keeled back.

Leaf margins are entire and scabrous near the tip.

Stems are erect, unbranched, and bear inflorescences at or near the apex.

The inflorescence is composed of 1 to 3 staminate heads above 1 to 4 pistillate heads on a branched or simple axis.

Staminate heads are globose, 8 to 15 millimeters in diameter, with numerous stamens.

Pistillate heads are globose and bur-like, 10 to 20 millimeters in diameter, composed of numerous densely packed pistils.

Each pistil has a single sessile ovary and a persistent style that forms a beak on the mature fruit.

Mature achenes are obovoid to ellipsoid, 4 to 7 millimeters long, brown, with a persistent style base.

Muskrats and other aquatic mammals consume the rhizomes, and the dense stems provide cover for fish and invertebrates.

It often co-occurs with cattails, sedges, rushes, and other emergent aquatic plants.
