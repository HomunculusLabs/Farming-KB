## Overview

Sparganium americanum is a perennial emergent aquatic plant commonly known as American bur-reed or simple bur-reed.

It is native to eastern and central [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]], where it grows in shallow marshes, pond margins, and slow-moving streams.

The species is recognized by its narrow, ribbon-like leaves and distinctive spherical bur-like fruiting heads.

American bur-reed provides food and habitat for waterfowl and contributes to the ecological structure of freshwater wetland communities.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Sparganium americanum belongs to the family Sparganiaceae within the order Poales.

Some modern classifications place Sparganium within the Typhaceae, but the Sparganiaceae is still widely recognized.

The species was described by Thomas Nuttall in 1818 and has been the subject of taxonomic revision involving several closely related taxa.

Distinction from Sparganium eurycarpum and Sparganium emersum requires careful examination of fruit characteristics and bract morphology.

The plant is a perennial emergent growing 50 to 150 centimeters tall from a thick, creeping rhizome.

Leaves are basal and cauline, linear, 5 to 15 millimeters wide, and up to 1 meter long, with a prominent midrib and keeled back.

Leaf margins are entire and scabrous near the tip.

Stems are erect, unbranched, and bear inflorescences at or near the apex.

The inflorescence is composed of 1 to 3 staminate heads above 1 to 4 pistillate heads on a branched or simple axis.

Staminate heads are globose, 8 to 15 millimeters in diameter, with numerous stamens.

Pistillate heads are globose and bur-like, 10 to 20 millimeters in diameter, composed of numerous densely packed pistils.

Each pistil has a single sessile ovary and a persistent style that forms a beak on the mature fruit.

Mature achenes are obovoid to ellipsoid, 4 to 7 millimeters long, brown, with a persistent style base.

## Distribution and Habitat

Sparganium americanum is native to eastern and central [[huckleberries-and-wild-berries-of-north-america]], ranging from New Brunswick to Minnesota, south to Florida and eastern Texas.

It is absent from most of the Great Plains and western North America, where other Sparganium species predominate.

The species grows in shallow freshwater wetlands including marshes, swamps, pond margins, ditches, and slow-moving streams.

It typically occurs in water depths of 0 to 50 centimeters on mucky or sandy substrates.

American bur-reed tolerates both permanent and seasonal hydroperiods, often persisting in marshes that dry down in late summer.

It is common in the littoral zones of lakes and ponds across its range.

The species prefers full sun but can tolerate partial shading from adjacent tall emergents.

Elevation ranges from sea level to approximately 600 meters in the Appalachian Piedmont.

## Ecology

Sparganium americanum spreads through both rhizomatous growth and [[cannabis-controlled-pollination-seed-production]].

Rhizomatous spread allows the formation of dense clones that contribute to emergent marsh structure.

The spherical bur-like fruiting heads are an important food source for waterfowl, particularly ducks and swans.

Muskrats and other aquatic mammals consume the rhizomes, and the dense stems provide cover for fish and invertebrates.

American bur-reed is a common component of emergent marsh and shallow-water plant communities.

It often co-occurs with cattails, sedges, rushes, and other emergent aquatic plants.

The species contributes to [[nutrient-cycling-in-soil]] wetland ecosystems through [[decomposition]] of annual biomass.

Stems and leaves provide substrate for periphyton and habitat for aquatic invertebrates.
