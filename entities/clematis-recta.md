## Overview

**Clematis recta** L., commonly called ground clematis or upright virgin's bower, is a herbaceous perennial in the family Ranunculaceae. Unlike most Clematis species, it grows as an erect, non-climbing subshrub reaching 60–150 cm tall.

The species is native to central and southern Europe, extending eastward through the Balkans to the Caucasus and Turkey. It produces masses of fragrant white flowers in terminal panicles during midsummer, making it valued in ornamental horticulture.

All parts contain ranunculin-derived protoanemonin, rendering the plant toxic if ingested and capable of causing skin irritation on contact. Despite this, C. recta has a recorded history of use in traditional European folk medicine as a topical remedy.

The species epithet *recta* refers to the upright, non-climbing habit that distinguishes it from the vast majority of Clematis species. It is sometimes placed in section *Meclatis* within the genus.

**Authority:** Carl Linnaeus, Species Plantarum (1753).

**Synonyms:** *Clematis lathyrifolia* Besser, *Clematis erecta* Tausch (nom. illeg.).

**Morphological description:**

Stems are erect, angular, and glabrous to sparsely pubescent, typically unbranched below the inflorescence. Leaves are pinnate with 5–7 leaflets, each leaflet ovate to lanceolate, 3–8 cm long, entire or sparingly toothed.

Flowers are white, 2–3 cm in diameter, with four oblong sepals (petals absent), borne in large, branching terminal cymes. Each flower bears numerous stamens with creamy-white filaments and multiple free carpels.

The fruit is an achene with a persistent, feathery style forming a silky tail 2–4 cm long, aiding wind dispersal. Root system features a deep, woody taproot with fibrous lateral roots.

Flowering occurs from June through August in the northern hemisphere. The blooming period lasts 3–4 weeks per plant, with individual flowers persisting 5–7 days.

## Distribution and Habitat

Clematis recta occurs across central and southern Europe from eastern France and northern Italy through Austria, Hungary, Romania, and the Balkan Peninsula. Its range extends east to Ukraine, the Caucasus, and northern Turkey.

It grows in dry, warm, calcareous habitats including woodland edges, scrub, rocky slopes, and disturbed embankments. The species prefers full sun to partial shade and well-drained, alkaline to neutral soils.

Populations are most abundant in sub-Mediterranean oak-hornbeam zones at elevations of 100–1200 m. It tolerates drought better than most Clematis species thanks to a deep taproot system.

The species is locally common throughout its range and not considered threatened. In some eastern populations it forms large, spreading colonies through root suckering.

## Cultivation

Hardy in USDA zones 4–9, Clematis recta is one of the easiest Clematis species to grow in temperate gardens. Unlike climbing species, it requires no support structure.

**Growth requirements:**

Plant in full sun to light shade in fertile, well-drained soil. Space plants 45–60 cm apart. The species benefits from a neutral to alkaline pH (6.5–7.5).

Water regularly during the first [[extending-growing-season]] to establish roots. Once established, C. recta shows good [[dighton-fungal-drought-tolerance-plant-water-relations]]. Apply a balanced fertilizer in early spring.

**Pruning and maintenance:**

Cut all stems to 15–20 cm above ground in late winter or early spring before new growth emerges. This hard pruning promotes vigorous, upright stems with abundant flowers.

The species may benefit from discrete staking or growing through supporting perennials, as heavy rain or wind can flatten the tall stems. Several compact cultivars (e.g. 'Savannah' at 60 cm) address this issue.

**[[mushroom-pests-and-diseases]]:** Generally trouble-free. Occasional [[cervantes-powdery-mildew-prevention-treatment]] in humid conditions. Slugs may damage emerging shoots in spring. Clematis wilt (caused by *Phoma clematidina*) is rare in this species but possible.

**Propagation:**

Sow seeds in autumn in [[query-how-do-i-choose-between-a-cold-frame-row-cover-and-low-tunnel]] for germination the following spring. Division of established clumps in early spring is reliable. Softwood cuttings taken in late spring root well under mist.
