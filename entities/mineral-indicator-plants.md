# Mineral Indicator Plants

## Description

Mineral indicator plants are species that accumulate or are associated with specific mineral deposits in the underlying soil or rock. Their presence, vigour, or chemical composition can reveal subsurface ore bodies, trace element concentrations, or soil mineral status. Mollison draws on research from Davidov (published in *Sputnik*, 12 December 1979) documenting plant-ore associations in Russia, and extends the concept to practical applications in [[permaculture]], mine rehabilitation, and biological mineral concentration.

## Classification

- **Category**: Biological indicators / geobotanical prospecting
- **Discipline**: Biogeology, geobotany, phytogechemistry
- **Application**: Mineral exploration, soil assessment, bioremediation, [[compost]] amendment

## Key Facts

- Plant ash analysis reveals mineral concentrations from underlying ore bodies
- Some plants can concentrate minerals to extraordinary levels in their tissues
- Patents have been granted for mining gold deposits using banana or citrus plants deprived of common elements (potash, phosphate) — their leaves then concentrate sparse gold deposits
- Oysters concentrate zinc to 11% dry weight (an emetic dose)
- Abalone concentrate cadmium
- Large fish concentrate mercury and biological poisons from corals to inedible levels
- The concept has implications for both mineral exploration and public health monitoring

## Applications in Permaculture

1. **Site Assessment**: Indicator plants reveal subsurface mineral conditions during site analysis
2. **Compost Amendment**: Concentrator plants can be grown and harvested to add trace minerals to compost
3. **Bioremediation**: Plants that concentrate toxic metals (lead, uranium, cadmium) can be used to clean contaminated soils through repeated harvesting
4. **[[phytomining]]**: Growing plants to extract valuable minerals from low-grade ores or mine tailings
5. **Health Monitoring**: Testing fish and shellfish for accumulated heavy metals as a public health indicator

## Relevance to Cultivation

Mineral indicator plants represent a sophisticated layer of site analysis that goes beyond basic [[soil-testing]]. In permaculture, they offer a low-cost, long-term method for understanding subsurface mineral conditions. The concept of biological concentration — using plants to accumulate trace minerals for compost — is particularly valuable for organic growers seeking to address micronutrient deficiencies without synthetic inputs. Conversely, understanding which plants concentrate toxic metals (lead, cadmium, mercury) is essential for food safety on potentially contaminated sites.

## Practical Applications

Understanding mineral indicator plants is essential for optimizing plant health and crop yields.
Proper diagnosis and treatment of mineral imbalances can significantly improve agricultural
productivity while reducing unnecessary fertilizer applications.

## Diagnostic Methods

Visual symptom identification, soil testing, and tissue analysis are the primary methods
for assessing plant nutrient status. Early detection prevents yield loss and allows for
timely corrective action through soil amendments or foliar sprays.
