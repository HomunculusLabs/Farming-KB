Beug's collaboration with Bigwood brought rigorous analytical chemistry methods to
psychoactive fungi research.

Beug developed educational materials and identification keys emphasizing critical
diagnostic features: spore print color, volva presence, annulus characteristics,
and microscopic features.
His identification guides have been credited with preventing numerous potential
poisoning incidents.

### Poison Case Consultation

Beug served as a consultant for mushroom poisoning cases, providing identification
services to hospitals and poison control centers.
His expertise in both taxonomy and chemistry of toxic mushrooms made him valuable
in emergencies where rapid species identification determined treatment protocols.

He contributed to clinical guidelines for managing amatoxin poisoning and other
mushroom toxicities.
His ability to rapidly identify specimens from photographs and physical descriptions
saved lives on multiple occasions.

## Photography and Identification

Beug was an accomplished mushroom photographer whose images appeared in numerous
field guides and mycological publications.
His photographic documentation of Psilocybe species, particularly Pacific
Northwest taxa like [[stamets-psi-psilocybe-cyanescens-species-guide]], provided essential reference
material for identification by amateur and professional mycologists.

His photographs were notable for attention to diagnostic features including cap
surface details, gill attachment, stem characteristics, and habitat context.
Many images have become standard reference photographs for these species in
field guides and online identification resources.

## NAMA Involvement

Beug was actively involved with the North American Mycological Association,
contributing to educational programs, identification workshops, and the
association's toxicology reporting system.
Beug trained hundreds of amateur mycologists in safe mushroom identification
through workshops, forays, and published guides. He mentored many who went on to make their own contributions,
creating a network of knowledgeable identifiers across the Pacific Northwest.
His impact on amateur mycology in North America is significant, as his students
continue to spread his approach to careful, evidence-based mushroom identification.

## Publications

In addition to psilocybin research, Beug authored numerous articles for NAMA
publications and mycological journals covering species identification keys,
chemical analysis methods, and poisonous mushroom recognition.
His writing was characterized by clear, accessible language making complex
mycological and chemical concepts understandable to non-specialist audiences.
