# Erinacines

**Erinacines** are a family of cyathane-type diterpenoids isolated primarily from the **mycelium** of *Hericium erinaceus* (lion's mane mushroom). First discovered by Kawagishi and colleagues in 1993, erinacines are among the most potent natural stimulators of **nerve growth factor (NGF)** synthesis known, with erinacine A showing activity at picomolar concentrations. Unlike the related hericenones found in fruiting bodies, erinacines are predominantly produced in the mycelial stage of the fungus, making them accessible through liquid fermentation for large-scale production.

## Chemical Classification

Erinacines are **cyathane-type diterpenoids** built on the characteristic 5/6/7-fused tricyclic carbon skeleton shared with hericenones, but with two additional isoprene units giving them a C₂₀ backbone (vs C₁₅ for sesquiterpenoid hericenones). The larger carbon framework provides more sites for oxidative diversification, resulting in a larger family of known congeners.

The erinacine series includes at least fifteen identified congeners designated **erinacines A through S**, each differing in oxidation patterns, ring modifications, and side-chain configurations. Erinacines A, B, C, and S are the most pharmacologically studied members of the family.

## Chemical Properties

- **Class:** Cyathane-type diterpenoids
- **Core skeleton:** Tricyclo[6.3.1.0²,⁷]dodecane (cyathane) with C₂₀ framework
- **Molecular weight range:** ~400–500 g/mol (varies by congener)
- **Solubility:** Moderately lipophilic; more water-soluble than hericenones due to additional polar functional groups; soluble in methanol, ethanol, and DMSO
- **Location in fungus:** Predominantly in mycelium [[query-what-is-the-difference-between-grain-spawn-and-liquid-culture-for-mushrooms]] filtrate; low concentrations in fruiting bodies
- **Stability:** More heat-stable than hericenones; retain activity after moderate processing

## Key Congeners

### Erinacine A
The most potent and extensively studied erinacine. Erinacine A stimulates NGF synthesis in vitro at concentrations as low as 10 pg/mL, making it one of the most powerful natural NGF inducers discovered. It acts through activation of the JNK and ERK1/2 [[plant-signaling-pathways]] in astroglial cells. Erinacine A also promotes neuronal differentiation through TrkB-dependent signaling and has shown neuroprotective effects in animal models of Parkinson's disease.

### Erinacine B and C
Co-isolated with erinacine A from mycelial extracts, erinacines B and C share the core cyathane structure but differ in their side-chain oxidation. Both show NGF-stimulating activity, though approximately 10–100× less potent than erinacine A. They contribute to the overall neurotrophic activity of lion's mane mycelial extracts.

### Erinacine S
A more recently characterized congener with a unique oxidized side chain. Erinacine S has shown notable anti-inflammatory activity in addition to neurotrophic effects. It inhibits NF-κB activation and reduces pro-inflammatory cytokine production in microglial cells, suggesting potential for addressing neuroinflammation associated with [[blesching-cannabis-neurodegenerative-diseases]].

## Mechanism of Action

### NGF Synthesis Stimulation
Erinacines stimulate the synthesis and secretion of **nerve growth factor** from astrocytes and Schwann cells through multiple converging signaling pathways. Key mechanisms include activation of **MAPK/ERK**, **JNK**, and **PI3K/Akt** cascades, which converge on the transcriptional regulation of the NGF gene. The potency of erinacine A (active at picomolar concentrations) far exceeds that of most other natural NGF inducers.

In vivo studies have confirmed that oral administration of erinacine-containing mycelial extracts increases NGF levels in the hippocampus and cortex of rodents. This central NGF elevation is associated with improved [[chamovitz-plant-maze-navigation-spatial-memory]], enhanced neurogenesis, and protection against experimentally induced neuronal injury.

### Anti-Inflammatory Effects
Erinacines modulate neuroinflammation through several mechanisms: inhibition of microglial activation, suppression of TNF-α and IL-6 production, and downregulation of iNOS and COX-2 expression. Erinacine S in particular shows strong anti-neuroinflammatory activity, reducing microglial activation in models of neurodegeneration.

### Amyloid-β Modulation
Emerging evidence suggests erinacines may influence amyloid-β metabolism. In cellular models, erinacine A has been shown to reduce amyloid-β-induced neurotoxicity and promote the clearance of amyloid-β aggregates through enhanced phagocytic activity of microglia. This mechanism is of particular interest for Alzheimer's [[cannabis-huntingtons-disease-research]].

## Production and Extraction

Erinacines are produced during the **mycelial growth phase** of *Hericium erinaceus*, making liquid [[cancer-medicinal-mushrooms-submerged-fermentation-production]] the preferred [[clay-seed-pellet-production-method-concrete-mixer]]. Key [[mushroom-species-cultivation-parameters]] that influence erinacine yield include:

- **Carbon source:** Glucose and maltose support higher erinacine production than sucrose or fructose
- **Nitrogen source:** [[marmite-yeast-extract-straw-substrate]] and peptone combinations optimize biomass and erinacine accumulation
- **pH:** Optimal range 5.0–6.0 for maximum erinacine A production
- **Temperature:** 25–28°C for optimal growth; lower temperatures (20–22°C) during stationary phase enhance erinacine yield
- **Stirring speed:** Moderate aeration (150–200 rpm) balances [[mycelial-growth-and-colonization]] compound secretion
