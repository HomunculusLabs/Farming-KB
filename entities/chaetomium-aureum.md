## Description

**Chaetomium aureum** is a species of filamentous ascomycete fungus in the family Chaetomiaceae. Members of the genus Chaetomium are well-known cellulolytic decomposers found in soil, dung, and decaying plant material. C. aureum was specifically identified as a **bioindicator species for high levels of radionuclide contamination** in woodland soil ecosystems around the Chernobyl nuclear disaster site.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Sordariomycetes
- **Order:** Sordariales
- **Family:** Chaetomiaceae
- **Genus:** Chaetomium
- **Species:** C. aureum

## Key Facts

- **Bioindicator for radionuclide contamination:** Alongside [[paeciliomyces-lilacinus]], C. aureum was identified by Zhdanova et al. (1995) as an indicator of high-level radionuclide contamination in woodland soils around Chernobyl.
- **Ecological survey tool:** Used in ecological studies to characterize the degree of radioactive contamination of soil by its presence and abundance within the fungal community.
- **Part of the Chernobyl monitoring effort:** Included in the 15-year fungal community monitoring program around the Chernobyl Atomic Electric Station.
- **Cellulolytic activity:** Like other Chaetomium species, C. aureum is a strong cellulose decomposer, contributing to the breakdown of plant cell wall material in soil ecosystems.

## Ecological Role

Chaetomium species are important saprotrophic decomposers in soil, specializing in cellulose degradation. They produce perithecia (flask-shaped fruiting bodies) covered with characteristic hair-like appendages (setae). Their strong cellulolytic enzyme systems make them important contributors to carbon cycling in terrestrial ecosystems. C. aureum additionally serves as an ecological marker for environmental stress from radioactive contamination.

## Relevance to Mycology and Cultivation

- **Bioindicator for environmental monitoring:** Its association with high-level radionuclide contamination makes it useful for ecological assessment of radioactive sites.
- **Cellulose decomposition research:** As a member of the Chaetomium genus, it contributes to our understanding of fungal [[cellulase-enzyme-systems-wood-rotting-fungi]].
- **Fungal community ecology:** Demonstrates how specific fungal species can serve as indicators of environmental perturbation and contamination levels.

## Distinguishing Features

- Perithecia with distinctive coiled or wavy terminal hairs (setae) characteristic of the genus
- Golden-yellow coloration suggested by the species epithet "aureum"
- Strong cellulose-degrading capability
- Association with highly radioactive soils as an indicator species

## Related Species at Chernobyl

- [[chaetomium-globosum]] was also isolated from the Chernobyl reactor walls (20.1% frequency at low radiation, 22.2% at high radiation), indicating the genus as a whole shows radiation tolerance.
