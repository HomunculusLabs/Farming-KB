## Description

**Arthrobotrys dactyloides** is a nematode-trapping fungus in the family Orbiliaceae that captures nematodes using specialized constricting ring structures. It is one of the most well-studied predatory fungi and has been investigated as a potential biocontrol agent against plant-parasitic nematodes.









## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Phylum**: Ascomycota
- **Class**: Orbiliomycetes
- **Order**: Orbiliales
- **Family**: Orbiliaceae
- **Genus**: Arthrobotrys
- **Species**: A. dactyloides









## Key Facts

- Produces specialized constricting ring traps that close around nematodes within fractions of a second upon contact
- Mankau (1981) identified it as occurring in the rhizosphere of plants, positioning it to predate plant-parasitic nematodes
- Researched as a potential biocontrol agent for plant-pathogenic nematodes
- Limited success in practical biocontrol applications despite laboratory efficacy
- Other related trapping fungi include [[dactylaria-brochopaga]] and [[monacrosporium-ellipsosporum]]









## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

[[nematophagous-fungi]] like A. dactyloides have potential applications in [[integrated-pest-management]] for mushroom cultivation and agriculture. Plant-parasitic nematodes can damage mycorrhizal associations and reduce crop yields. In mushroom cultivation, where substrate nematode populations can become problematic, predatory fungi offer a potential non-chemical management approach. The challenge of translating laboratory efficacy to field conditions remains a key research area.









## Ecological Role

A. dactyloides occupies a unique trophic niche as a fungal predator of nematodes. It helps regulate nematode populations in soil ecosystems, particularly in the rhizosphere where plant-parasitic nematodes concentrate. The fungus produces adhesive networks and constricting rings in response to chemical cues from nematodes, demonstrating sophisticated prey-detection mechanisms.
