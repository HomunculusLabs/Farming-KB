# Psilocybin

## Overview

**Psilocybin** (C₁₂H₁₇N₂O₄P, MW 284.25) is a naturally occurring **psychedelic prodrug** found in over **200 species of mushrooms**. A **serotonin 2A receptor (5-HT₂A) agonist**, it produces profound alterations in consciousness, perception, and emotional processing. Synthesized by **Albert Hofmann** in 1958, psilocybin has undergone a remarkable clinical renaissance, demonstrating therapeutic potential for treatment-resistant depression, end-of-life anxiety, PTSD, and substance use disorders. As of 2026, it has been **decriminalized or legalized** in multiple jurisdictions.

## Chemical Identity

- **Systematic name**: 3-[2-(dimethylamino)ethyl]-1H-indol-4-yl dihydrogen phosphate
- **CAS**: 520-52-5 | **Appearance**: White crystalline powder
- **Solubility**: Water-soluble; slightly soluble in ethanol
- **pKa**: 1.3 (phosphate), 6.5 (amine), 9.9 (indole NH)
- **Structure**: A **4-phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine** — tryptamine derivative related to serotonin, DMT, and LSD

### Biosynthesis

Characterized enzymatic pathway in fungi (genes: *psiD*, *psiH*, *psiK*, *psiM*):
L-tryptophan → tryptamine → 4-hydroxytryptamine → **psilocin** → **psilocybin**

## Pharmacology

### Pharmacokinetics

- **Absorption**: Rapid oral; peak plasma 40–90 min
- **Prodrug**: Rapidly dephosphorylated to **psilocin** (active metabolite) by alkaline phosphatase
- **Metabolism**: Hepatic via MAO-A and CYP2D6; major metabolite: 4-hydroxyindole-3-acetic acid
- **Half-life**: Psilocin ~2–3 hours; ~65% excreted in urine within 24 hours

### Receptor Binding (Psilocin)

| Receptor | Ki (nM) | Activity |
|----------|---------|----------|
| **5-HT₂A** | 6–12 | Partial agonist — **primary psychedelic mechanism** |
| **5-HT₁A** | 20–50 | Partial agonist — anxiolytic/antidepressant |
| **5-HT₂C** | 20–50 | Partial agonist — mood/anxiety modulation |
| **5-HT₂B** | 100–200 | Agonist — cardiac valvulopathy concern at chronic high doses |

### Mechanism

5-HT₂A activation on layer V pyramidal neurons → increased cortical excitability → **DMN connectivity disruption** → reduced self-referential rigidity → increased global brain connectivity → enhanced neural entropy. The **entropic brain hypothesis** (Carhart-Harris, 2014) frames this as a shift toward criticality.

## Subjective Effects

### Dose-Response

| Dose (mg psilocybin) | Level | Experience |
|-----------------------|-------|------------|
| 1–2 | Microdose | Sub-perceptual; enhanced focus, mood |
| 2–5 | Mini | Mild perceptual changes, relaxation |
| 5–10 | Moderate | Pronounced visuals, introspection |
| 10–20 | Strong | Immersive psychedelic, ego dissolution |
| 20+ | Heroic | Complete ego dissolution, transpersonal |
**Perceptual**: Geometric patterns, color enhancement, synesthesia, time dilation
**Cognitive**: Enhanced pattern recognition, reduced rigidity, novel perspectives, vivid memory recall
**Emotional**: Awe, wonder, catharsis, enhanced empathy; possible anxiety/paranoia (10–30% of users)
**Transpersonal**: Ego dissolution, mystical experiences, sense of unity, noetic quality

## Clinical Research

### Depression
- **Treatment-resistant**: Imperial College trials (2016, 2021) showed rapid, sustained antidepressant effects from single 25 mg dose; effects lasting 3–6 months
- **COMPASS Phase III**: Significant MADRS reduction; FDA **Breakthrough Therapy Designation** granted

### End-of-Life Anxiety
NYU and Johns Hopkins studies (2016): Single dose produced significant, sustained reductions in death-related anxiety in cancer patients; 80% rated among top 5 most meaningful life experiences

### Substance Use Disorders
- **Tobacco**: 80% abstinence at 6 months (Johns Hopkins pilot)
- **Alcohol**: Significantly reduced heavy drinking days (NYU pilot)
- Mechanism: Perspective shifts, increased self-efficacy, mystical experiences reframing substance relationship

## Safety

### Physiological Safety
**No known lethal dose** in humans; no organ toxicity, addictive potential, or neurotoxicity. Minimal cardiovascular effects at standard doses.

### Psychological Risks
Challenging experiences (10–30%); **HPPD** rare (<1/50,000); theoretical psychosis risk in vulnerable individuals; significantly reduced with therapeutic support and proper **set and setting**.

### Contraindications
Personal/family history of psychotic disorders, mania, uncontrolled cardiovascular conditions, pregnancy, concurrent MAOIs. SSRIs/SNRIs may attenuate effects; washout recommended.

## Legal Status (2026)

US federal Schedule I; legal in Oregon and Colorado; decriminalized in 20+ cities. Authorized for clinical use in Australia. Decriminalized in Netherlands (fresh truffles), Portugal, Spain. Generally scheduled in EU; clinical trial frameworks developing.

## Natural Occurrence

Psilocybin is produced by over 200 species across multiple genera:
| Genus | Notable Species | Typical Psilocybin (% dry weight) |
|-------|----------------|----------------------------------|
| **Psilocybe** | *P. cubensis*, *P. semilanceata*, *P. cyanescens* | 0.5–1.5% |
| **Panaeolus** | *P. cyanescens* | 0.3–1.0% |
| **Gymnopilus** | *G. spectabilis* | 0.1–0.4% |
| **Inocybe** | *I. corydalina* | 0.01–0.1% |
| **Pluteus** | *P. salicinus* | 0.1–0.3% |

## Microdosing

Sub-perceptual doses (0.1–0.3 g dried mushroom, ~0.5–1 mg psilocybin) taken every 3–4 days. Anecdotal reports suggest improved focus, creativity, mood, and reduced anxiety. Clinical evidence remains **mixed** — some controlled trials show benefit, others show no effect above placebo. The **Fadiman protocol** (1 day on, 2 days off) is the most widely followed schedule.
