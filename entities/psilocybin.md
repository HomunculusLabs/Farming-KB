# Psilocybin

**Psilocybin** (systematic name: 3-[2-(dimethylamino)ethyl]-1H-indol-4-yl dihydrogen phosphate) is a naturally occurring psychedelic prodrug of the tryptamine class. It is the principal psychoactive compound found in over 200 species of mushrooms, predominantly in the genus *Psilocybe*.




## Chemical Properties

- **Molecular formula:** C₁₂H₁₇N₂O₄P
- **Molar mass:** 284.25 g/mol
- **IUPAC name:** [3-(2-Dimethylaminoethyl)-1H-indol-4-yl] dihydrogen phosphate
- **CAS number:** 520-53-6
- **Appearance:** White crystalline solid

Psilocybin is a zwitterionic compound at physiological pH. It is soluble in water and moderately soluble in methanol and ethanol. The molecule is an O-phosphorylated derivative of psilocin, with the phosphate group increasing its stability and bioavailability compared to the free phenol.




## Pharmacology

Psilocybin is itself pharmacologically inactive at serotonin receptors. After oral ingestion, it is rapidly dephosphorylated by alkaline phosphatase enzymes, primarily in the intestinal mucosa and liver, to yield **psilocin** (4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine, 4-HO-DMT), the active metabolite.

### Mechanism of Action

Psilocin acts primarily as an agonist at the **serotonin 5-HT2A receptor**, with additional affinity for 5-HT1A, 5-HT2C, and 5-HT2B receptors. The psychedelic effects are mediated primarily through 5-HT2A receptor activation in the prefrontal cortex, leading to:

- Increased cortical entropy and disorganized functional connectivity
- Reduced activity in the default mode network (DMN)
- Enhanced cross-talk between brain regions that normally do not communicate

### Pharmacokinetics

- **Onset:** 20–60 minutes after oral ingestion
- **Peak effects:** 60–120 minutes
- **Duration:** 4–6 hours
- **Half-life:** ~160 minutes (psilocin)
- **Metabolism:** Deamination by monoamine oxidase (MAO-A), followed by glucuronidation




## Effects

The subjective effects of psilocybin are dose-dependent and highly variable between individuals. Common effects include:

- Visual and auditory hallucinations, synesthesia
- Altered perception of time and space
- Ego dissolution or mystical-type experiences
- Enhanced emotional range and introspection
- Changes in pattern recognition and creative thinking

At higher doses (15–25 mg psilocybin), experiences can include profound mystical or transcendent states. The effects are significantly influenced by **set** (mindset, expectations, personality) and **setting** (physical and social environment).




## Therapeutic Research

Contemporary clinical research has demonstrated promising results for psilocybin-assisted therapy in:

- **Treatment-resistant depression:** Phase II trials at Imperial College London and Johns Hopkins showed rapid and sustained antidepressant effects lasting 6+ months after 1–2 sessions.
- **End-of-life anxiety:** Studies with terminal cancer patients demonstrated significant reductions in depression and anxiety with a single guided session.
- **Alcohol and nicotine dependence:** Pilot studies suggest psilocybin may reduce addictive behaviors through increased psychological flexibility and self-efficacy.
- **Obsessive-compulsive disorder:** Early open-label studies showed symptom reduction in treatment-resistant OCD.

As of 2026, psilocybin is in Phase III clinical trials for major depressive disorder, with regulatory approval pathways advancing in the US (FDA Breakthrough Therapy designation granted 2018), EU, and Australia.




## Biosynthesis

Psilocybin is biosynthesized in fungal mycelium via a pathway involving four key enzymes: PsiD (L-tryptophan decarboxylase), PsiH (monooxygenase), PsiK (kinase), and PsiM (methyltransferase). The pathway proceeds: L-tryptophan → tryptamine → 4-hydroxytryptamine (norbaeocystin) → 4-hydroxy-N-methyltryptamine (baeocystin) → 4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine (psilocin) → psilocybin (phosphorylated psilocin). The *psi* gene cluster was independently identified in 2017 by research groups at the Friedrich Schiller University Jena and Ohio State University.




## History

Psilocybin was first isolated and characterized in 1958 by Swiss chemist **Albert Hofmann** at Sandoz Laboratories, following his identification of LSD in 1943. Hofmann identified the compound from specimens of *Psilocybe mexicana* collected by R. Gordon Wasson in Mexico. Sandoz marketed it under the brand name **Indocybin** for psychiatric research until it was banned in the late 1960s amid the broader prohibition of psychedelic substances.
