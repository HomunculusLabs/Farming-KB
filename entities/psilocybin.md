# Psilocybin

**Psilocybin** (INN: psilocybine) is a naturally occurring psychedelic prodrug and tryptamine alkaloid. It is the principal psychoactive compound found in over 200 species of mushrooms, predominantly in the genus *Psilocybe*. Upon ingestion, psilocybin is rapidly dephosphorylated to its active metabolite **psilocin**, which acts primarily as a serotonin 5-HT₂A receptor agonist, producing profound alterations in perception, cognition, and consciousness.

## Chemical Identity

- **IUPAC name:** 3-[2-(Dimethylamino)ethyl]-1H-indol-4-yl dihydrogen phosphate
- **CAS:** 520-53-6 | **Formula:** C₁₂H₁₇N₂O₄P | **MW:** 284.25 g/mol
- **Class:** 4-Phosphoryloxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine (4-PO-DMT)
- **Appearance:** White to off-white crystalline powder (synthetic)

Structurally related to **DMT** (4-position phosphoryloxy vs. hydrogen), **serotonin** (same receptor targets), and **LSD** (shared 5-HT₂A agonism, different structural class — tryptamine vs. ergoline). Bufotenin (5-HO-DMT) is the 5-hydroxy positional isomer.

## History

- 1939: R. Gordon Wasson participates in Mazatec mushroom ceremony in Oaxaca, Mexico
- 1957: Wasson's "Seeking the Magic Mushroom" in *LIFE* introduces psilocybin mushrooms to the West
- 1958: **Albert Hofmann** isolates psilocybin and psilocin from *P. mexicana*; synthesizes it at Sandoz
- 1960s: Sandoz markets as **Indocybin** for psychiatric research; Harvard Psilocybin Project (Leary & Alpert)
- 1970: US Schedule I; 1971: UN Convention Schedule I
- 1990s–2000s: Research resumes (Moreno, Vollenweider, Griffiths)
- 2018: FDA **Breakthrough Therapy Designation** for TRD (COMPASS Pathways)
- 2019–present: Phase II/III trials for depression, PTSD, substance use disorders, end-of-life anxiety

## Pharmacokinetics

- **Bioavailability:** ~50% oral (variable, first-pass); **onset:** 20–60 min (peak 60–120 min); **duration:** 4–6 hours
- **Active metabolite:** Psilocin (4-HO-DMT) — psilocybin is a prodrug with minimal direct activity
- **Metabolism:** Rapid dephosphorylation by alkaline phosphatase (intestinal mucosa, liver, blood), then hepatic MAO-A oxidation to 4-hydroxyindole-3-acetic acid (4-HIAA); glucuronide conjugates
- **Half-life (psilocin):** 2–3 hours; ~65% excreted in urine within 24 hours
- **Detection:** Urine 1–3 days; hair up to 90 days; not included in standard drug panels

## Pharmacodynamics

### Primary Receptor Targets

| Receptor | Action | Significance |
|----------|--------|-------------|
| **5-HT₂A** | Full/partial agonist | Primary mediator of psychedelic effects |
| **5-HT₂C** | Agonist | Modulates anxiety, mood |
| **5-HT₁A** | Partial agonist | Contributes to anxiolytic effects |
| **5-HT₁B** | Agonist | Vasoconstriction, modulatory |
| **5-HT₅A** | Agonist | Unknown significance |
| **5-HT₆** | Agonist | Cognitive modulation |
| **5-HT₇** | Agonist | Circadian rhythm, cognition |
| **D₂ (dopamine)** | Low affinity | Minimal direct dopaminergic activity |

### Neural Mechanisms
- **DMN suppression:** Decreased activity and connectivity in the default mode network (self-referential thought, ego, rumination)
- **Global entropy increase:** Brain networks become more diverse, flexible, and disorganized; enhanced cross-network communication between normally segregated regions
- **Thalamic gating disruption:** Reduced thalamic sensory filtering; glutamate release amplifies downstream signaling
- **Neuroplasticity:** Upregulates BDNF, promotes dendritic spine growth and synaptogenesis in prefrontal cortex — effects persisting weeks after dosing, hypothesized to underlie sustained therapeutic benefits

### *Psilocybe cubensis* — Most Cultivated Species
Subtropical; grows on bovine/equine dung in pastures. Widely distributed in tropical/subtropical regions worldwide. Over 100 named cultivars (Golden Teacher, B+, Penis Envy, Cambodian, etc.). Easy to cultivate — primary species in home cultivation and clinical research. Typical potency: 0.5–1.2% psilocybin + psilocin by dry weight. Environmental factors (substrate, genetics, harvest timing) account for more variation than strain selection.

## Clinical Research

### Treatment-Resistant Depression (TRD)
- **COMPASS Pathways Phase IIb (2021):** 233 patients; single 25 mg dose produced rapid antidepressant effect. Response at 3 weeks: 29% (1 mg), 37% (10 mg), 57% (25 mg). Remission: 7%, 19%, 29%
- **Imperial College (Carhart-Harris, 2016):** Open-label, 12 TRD patients; all improved at 1 week, 5/12 in remission at 3 months
- **Phase III trials** ongoing (2023–2026)

### End-of-Life Anxiety & Depression
- **Johns Hopkins (Griffiths, 2016):** 51 cancer patients; high-dose psilocybin produced 82% depression reduction and 78% anxiety reduction at 6 months
- **NYU Langone (Ross, 2016):** 29 patients; single dose produced rapid, sustained decreases in depression, anxiety, and demoralization

### Alcohol Use Disorder
- **NYU Langone (Bogenschutz, 2022):** 96 AUD patients; 2 psilocybin sessions + psychotherapy vs. placebo; **PHDD at 32 weeks: 9.7% vs. 23.6%** — 59% greater reduction

### Other Indications
Ongoing trials: PTSD (Usona), OCD (Yale), anorexia (UCSD), cluster headache (Yale/MIND), smoking cessation (Johns Hopkins). Effect sizes consistently large (Cohen's d > 1.0).

|----------|----------------------|--------------------|---------|
| **Microdose** | 0.1–0.3 g | 0.1–0.3 g | Sub-perceptual; debated efficacy |
| **Low** | 0.5–1.0 g | 0.5–1.0 g | Mild perceptual changes, mood lift |
| **Moderate** | 1.0–2.5 g | 1.0–2.5 g | Clear psychedelic effects, visual distortion |
| **Strong** | 2.5–5.0 g | 2.5–5.0 g | Intense experience, ego dissolution possible |
| **Heroic/Mystical** | 5.0+ g | 5.0+ g | Complete ego dissolution, mystical-type experience |

Clinical trial doses: 10 mg (moderate), 25 mg (high), 30 mg (very high). A clinical 25 mg dose ≈ 2.5–3.5 g dried *P. cubensis*.

## Microdosing

Sub-perceptual doses (0.1–0.3 g dried mushrooms) taken every 3 days ("Fadiman protocol"). Reported benefits include improved focus, mood, and creativity, but rigorous double-blind studies (Family et al., 2020; Szigeti et al., 2021) fail to show significant differences from placebo on most measures — expectancy effects account for much of the reported benefit. Generally well-tolerated; no tolerance develops at sub-perceptual doses.

## Synthesis & Biosynthesis

Hofmann first synthesized psilocybin in 1959 from protected indole intermediates. Modern methods include total synthesis from tryptophan, enzymatic biosynthesis using genetically modified *E. coli* expressing *psiD*, *psiK*, and *psiM* genes, and GMP production by COMPASS Pathways. Fungal biosynthetic pathway: L-Tryptophan → Tryptamine → (psiD: 4-monooxygenase) → 4-Hydroxytryptamine → (psiK: kinase) → Norpsilocybin → (psiM: methyltransferase) → **Psilocybin**.
