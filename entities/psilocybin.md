# Psilocybin (4-PO-DMT)

## Chemical Profile

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| **IUPAC name** | [3-(2-Dimethylaminoethyl)-1H-indol-4-yl] dihydrogen phosphate |
| **CAS number** | 520-52-5 |
| **Molecular formula** | C₁₂H₁₇N₂O₄P |
| **Molecular weight** | 284.25 g/mol |
| **Chemical class** | Tryptamine; Indolealkylamine; Phosphoryloxy-tryptamine |
| **SMILES** | CN(C)CCc1c[nH]c2cc(O=P(O)(O)O)ccc12 |
| **Melting point** | 220–228 °C (decomposes) |
| **Solubility** | Soluble in water; slightly soluble in ethanol |

Psilocybin is a naturally occurring **prodrug** — it is dephosphorylated in the body by the enzyme **alkaline phosphatase** to its pharmacologically active form, **psilocin** (4-HO-DMT). This conversion occurs primarily in the liver and gastrointestinal tract, though some may occur in the brain.

## History

### Indigenous Use

Psilocybin-containing mushrooms have been used in Mesoamerican spiritual and healing practices for at least 3,000 years. The **Mazatec** people of Oaxaca, Mexico, refer to them as *nti xhi ntio* ("divine mushroom") or *teonanácatl* ("flesh of the gods"). They were used in divination, healing ceremonies, and communal rituals overseen by *curanderas* (healers), most famously María Sabina.

Archaeological evidence includes mushroom-shaped stone figurines from Guatemala (c. 500 BCE) and the "mushroom stones" of the Maya. The **Codex Vindobonensis Mexicanus** (c. 13th century) depicts the Aztec deity Xochipilli surrounded by psychoactive fungi.

### Modern Discovery and Research

- **1939:** Richard Schultes identifies *Psilocybe caerulescens* in Oaxaca and links it to Aztec mushroom worship.
- **1957:** R. Gordon Wasson publishes "Seeking the Magic Mushroom" in *Life* magazine, introducing psilocybin mushrooms to Western audiences.
- **1958:** Albert Hofmann isolates and synthesizes psilocybin and psilocin from *Psilocybe mexicana* at Sandoz Laboratories.
- **1959–1965:** Sandoz markets psilocybin as **Indocybin** for psychiatric research. Over 1,000 clinical papers published during this period.
- **1966:** US Controlled Substances Act classifies psilocybin as Schedule I. Sandoz ceases production.
- **1971:** UN Convention on Psychotropic Substances restricts psilocybin internationally.
- **2006:** Roland Griffiths publishes landmark Johns Hopkins study confirming mystical-type experiences with psilocybin.
- **2018:** FDA grants **Breakthrough Therapy** designation for psilocybin in treatment-resistant depression.
- **2020s:** Multiple Phase II and III clinical trials underway. Oregon and Colorado legalize therapeutic use.

## Pharmacology

### Mechanism of Action

Psilocybin (as psilocin) is a **serotonin 5-HT₂A receptor agonist** with additional affinity for:

| Receptor | Affinity (Ki) | Functional Effect |
|----------|--------------|-------------------|
| 5-HT₂A | ~6 nM | Full agonist — primary psychedelic mechanism |
| 5-HT₂C | ~12 nM | Agonist |
| 5-HT₁A | ~190 nM | Partial agonist |
| 5-HT₂B | ~5 nM | Agonist |

The psychedelic effects are primarily mediated by 5-HT₂A receptor activation in the **prefrontal cortex**. This leads to:

1. **Cortical desynchronization:** Disruption of default mode network (DMN) activity, reducing ego-boundary enforcement
2. **Increased entropy:** Elevated neural signal diversity and cross-regional connectivity
3. **Glutamate release:** 5-HT₂A activation on cortical pyramidal neurons triggers glutamate cascades
4. **Neuroplasticity induction:** Upregulation of BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor), activation of mTOR signaling, and stimulation of dendritic spine growth in the prefrontal cortex within 24 hours of administration

### Pharmacokinetics

- **Onset:** 20–60 minutes after oral ingestion
- **Peak effects:** 60–120 minutes
- **Duration:** 4–6 hours (oral)
- **Half-life of psilocin:** ~2.5 hours
- **Elimination:** Renal, primarily as unchanged psilocin glucuronide

## Clinical Applications

### Treatment-Resistant Depression

The most advanced clinical application. Carhart-Harris et al. (2021) published a Phase IIb trial (n=233) showing:
- **Single 25 mg dose** of synthetic psilocybin produced significant reduction in depressive symptoms at 3 weeks (MADRS score reduction of 12.0 vs. 4.4 for 1 mg control)
- Response rate: 36.7% (25 mg) vs. 8.5% (1 mg)
- Remission rate: 29.1% (25 mg) vs. 7.7% (1 mg)
- Effects sustained at 12-week follow-up

### End-of-Life Anxiety and Depression

Two landmark trials (Gros et al., 2011; Ross et al., 2016) demonstrated that a single moderate dose of psilocybin (0.3–0.4 mg/kg) significantly reduced anxiety and depression in patients with life-threatening cancer diagnoses, with effects persisting at 6-month follow-up.

### Substance Use Disorders

- **Tobacco addiction:** Johnson et al. (2014): 80% abstinence rate at 6 months (n=15) following 2–3 psilocybin sessions with cognitive behavioral therapy
- **Alcohol dependence:** Bogenschutz et al. (2022): Phase II trial showed significant reduction in heavy drinking days at 32-week follow-up

### Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder

A small open-label study (Moreno et al., 2006) found that psilocybin reduced OCD symptoms in all 9 participants, with effects lasting days to weeks after a single session.

## Species Containing Psilocybin

Over 200 species of mushrooms across multiple genera contain psilocybin and/or psilocin:

| Genus | Notable Species | Approximate Psilocybin % |
|-------|----------------|--------------------------|
| *Psilocybe* | *P. cubensis*, *P. semilanceata*, *P. azurescens* | 0.1–1.8% |
| *Panaeolus* | *P. cyanescens* (blue meanies) | 0.3–1.5% |
| *Gymnopilus* | *G. spectabilis* (big laughing gym) | 0.1–0.3% |
| *Inocybe* | *I. aeruginascens* | 0.01–0.1% |
| *Pluteus* | *P. salicinus* | 0.01–0.1% |

*Psilocybe azurescens*, found in the Pacific Northwest USA, contains the highest known concentrations (up to 1.8% psilocybin, 0.5% psilocin, 0.4% baeocystin).

## Legal Status

Psilocybin is classified as a **Schedule I** controlled substance under the UN Convention on Psychotropic Substances and in many countries worldwide. However, legal reform is accelerating:

- **Oregon (USA):** Legalized for therapeutic use under Measure 109 (2020)
- **Colorado (USA):** Decriminalized and established regulated access (Proposition 122, 2022)
- **Australia:** Approved for clinical use in treatment-resistant depression and PTSD (2023)
- **Netherlands:** Fresh mushrooms legal (truffles sold commercially in "smart shops")
- **Brazil:** Non-criminal; not scheduled
- **Canada:** Clinical trials authorized; special access program available

## Harm Reduction and Safety

Psilocybin has a remarkably low toxicity profile:
- **LD₅₀ (IV, mice):** 285 mg/kg — approximately 1,000× a typical recreational dose
- **No known lethal overdoses** from psilocybin alone in humans
- **No evidence of physical dependence** or significant withdrawal syndrome
- **Low abuse potential:** Tolerance develops rapidly (within days) and diminishes use frequency

**Psychological risks** include:
- Acute anxiety or panic ("bad trip"), especially in unprepared users or high doses
- HPPD (Hallucinogen Persisting Perception Disorder) — rare, estimated <1 in 50,000
- Precipitation of psychotic episodes in individuals with personal or family history of schizophrenia or bipolar disorder

**Harm reduction:** Set (mindset), setting (environment), dose awareness, trip sitter, integration, screening for contraindications.
