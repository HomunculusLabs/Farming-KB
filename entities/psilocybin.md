# Psilocybin

## Overview

Psilocybin (4-PO-DMT) is a naturally occurring psychedelic prodrug and tryptamine alkaloid produced by more than 200 species of fungi, primarily in the genus *Psilocybe*. Upon ingestion, psilocybin is dephosphorylated to psilocin (4-HO-DMT), the pharmacologically active compound. Used for millennia in Mesoamerican spiritual ceremonies, psilocybin is now the subject of a major research renaissance exploring its therapeutic potential for depression, anxiety, addiction, and end-of-life distress.

## Chemical Properties

- **Molecular formula:** C₁₂H₁₇N₂O₄P
- **Molecular weight:** 284.25 g/mol
- **Appearance:** White crystalline powder
- **Solubility:** Soluble in water; slightly soluble in ethanol
- **Melting point:** 220–228°C (decomposition)
- **Stability:** Stable under acidic conditions, degrades in alkaline environments

## Pharmacology

### Mechanism of Action

Psilocin acts primarily as a partial agonist at serotonin 5-HT₂A receptors (Ki ≈ 6 nM), with additional activity at 5-HT₂C, 5-HT₁A, 5-HT₂B, and weak dopamine receptor binding. Activation of cortical 5-HT₂A receptors on layer V pyramidal neurons is the key mechanism for psychedelic effects.

### Pharmacokinetics

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Onset | 20–60 minutes |
| Peak | 60–90 minutes |
| Duration | 4–6 hours |
| Half-life | ~160 minutes (psilocin) |
| Bioavailability | ~50% |
| Metabolism | Hepatic dephosphorylation, MAO-A oxidation, glucuronidation |

## Dosing

- **Microdose:** 0.1–0.3 g dried mushrooms (0.5–2 mg psilocybin)
- **Light:** 0.5–1.0 g dried (5–10 mg)
- **Moderate:** 1.0–2.5 g dried (10–25 mg)
- **Strong:** 2.5–5.0 g dried (25–50 mg)
- **Heroic:** 5.0+ g dried (50+ mg)

Potency varies significantly between species and growing conditions. Clinical trials use 10–30 mg synthetic psilocybin with psychological support.

## Subjective Effects

- **Perceptual:** Visual hallucinations, synesthesia, enhanced music/art appreciation, altered time perception
- **Cognitive:** Ego dissolution at higher doses, enhanced introspection, increased cognitive flexibility, mystical experiences
- **Emotional:** Euphoria, profound peace, emotional catharsis, enhanced empathy — or anxiety and fear in challenging experiences

## Natural Sources

### Major Genera

- ***Psilocybe:*** *P. cubensis*, *P. semilanceata*, *P. cyanescens*, *P. azurescens*
- ***Panaeolus:*** *P. cyanescens* (Blue Meanies)
- ***Gymnopilus:*** *G. spectabilis* (Big Laughing Gym)
- ***Copelandia:*** Tropical species, very potent

### Potency

- *P. azurescens:* ~1.78% (most potent)
- *P. semilanceata:* ~1.0%
- *P. cubensis:* ~0.63% (most commonly cultivated)

## History

### Mesoamerican Origins

Ritual use dates back at least 3,000 years. The Aztecs called them *teonanácatl* ("flesh of the gods"). Mazatec healers in Oaxaca continue ceremonial use today.

### Western Discovery and Prohibition

- 1799: First Western report (British family consumed *P. semilanceata*)
- 1957: Wasson's *Life* magazine article on Mazatec ceremonies
- 1958: Albert Hofmann isolated psilocybin at Sandoz
- 1970: Schedule I classification halted research; 1999: Johns Hopkins resumed
- 2018: FDA Breakthrough Therapy Designation for treatment-resistant depression
- 2020s: Oregon and Colorado decriminalized natural psychedelics

## Therapeutic Research

### Treatment-Resistant Depression

A 2021 RCT (Carhart-Harris et al., NEJM) found a single 25 mg dose produced rapid antidepressant effects comparable to escitalopram. Brain imaging shows increased global connectivity and reduced default mode network activity.

### End-of-Life Distress

Multiple studies demonstrate significant, sustained reductions in depression and anxiety in cancer patients (6 months to 5 years). Griffiths et al. (2016): 80% showed clinically significant improvement at 6 months.

### Substance Use Disorders

Pilot studies show promise for alcohol use disorder, nicotine addiction (80% abstinence at 6 months in one open-label study), and cocaine use disorder.

## Neuroscience

### Default Mode Network

Psilocybin reduces DMN activity and connectivity, correlated with ego dissolution and mystical-type experiences — underlying therapeutic outcomes.

### Neuroplasticity

Psilocybin promotes structural neuroplasticity: increased dendritic spine density, upregulated BDNF expression, and activated mTOR pathways. These effects may explain sustained benefits weeks to months after a single dose.

### Entropic Brain Theory

Psychedelics increase brain entropy, returning the brain to a more flexible state allowing revision of entrenched cognitive patterns (Carhart-Harris et al., 2014).

## Microdosing

Common protocols: Fadiman (1 day on, 2 off) and Stamets (4 on, 3 off). A 2021 double-blind study (Szigeti et al.) found no significant difference from placebo, suggesting strong expectancy effects. Science remains inconclusive.

## Legal Status

- **US:** Schedule I federally; decriminalized in Oregon, Colorado, numerous municipalities
- **Netherlands:** Truffles legal in smart shops
- **Canada:** Special access for therapeutic use
- **Australia:** Therapeutic use approved (2023)
- **Brazil, Jamaica:** Legal or unregulated
- **Portugal:** Decriminalized

## Harm Reduction

### Risks
- Psychological: Challenging experiences, transient psychosis in susceptible individuals
- HPPD: Hallucinogen Persisting Perception Disorder (rare)
- Psychosis risk: Increased with personal/family history of schizophrenia or bipolar disorder
- Physical: Very low toxicity; no known lethal dose

### Best Practices
Screen for psychosis history; prepare set and setting; use a sober trip sitter; test substances with Ehrlich's reagent; practice post-experience integration.
