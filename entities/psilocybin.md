# Psilocybin

**Psilocybin** (4-PO-DMT; O-phosphoryl-4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) is a naturally occurring **psychedelic prodrug** of the tryptamine class. It is the primary psychoactive compound found in over 200 species of mushrooms in the genus *Psilocybe* and related genera (*Panaeolus*, *Gymnopilus*, *Inocybe*, *Pluteus*). Upon ingestion, psilocybin is rapidly dephosphorylated by alkaline phosphatase enzymes to **psilocin** (4-HO-DMT), which is the pharmacologically active form responsible for the psychedelic experience. Psilocybin is currently the most extensively studied classic psychedelic in modern clinical research.

## Chemical Properties

### Structural Classification

Psilocybin is a **4-substituted tryptamine** — a structural analog of serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine, 5-HT) with a phosphate ester group at the 4-position and dimethyl substitution on the terminal amine nitrogen.

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| **IUPAC name** | [3-(2-Dimethylaminoethyl)-1H-indol-4-yl] dihydrogen phosphate |
| **Molecular formula** | C₁₂H₁₇N₂O₄P |
| **Molecular weight** | 284.25 g/mol |
| **CAS number** | 520-53-6 |
| **Appearance** | White to off-white crystalline powder |
| **Solubility** | Soluble in water; slightly soluble in ethanol |
| **Melting point** | 220–228°C (decomposes) |
| **pKa** | 1.3 (phosphate), 6.5 (amine), 9.9 (phenol) |

### Biosynthesis

Psilocybin is biosynthesized in fungal mycelium and fruiting bodies via a pathway first fully elucidated in 2017 by researchers at Friedrich Schiller University Jena. The pathway involves four key enzymes:

1. **PsiD** (L-tryptophan decarboxylase): Converts L-tryptophan to tryptamine
2. **PsiH** (monooxygenase): Hydroxylates tryptamine at the 4-position to produce 4-hydroxytryptamine
3. **PsiK** (kinase): Phosphorylates 4-hydroxytryptamine to 4-hydroxytryptamine phosphate (norbaeocystin)
4. **PsiM** (methyltransferase): Sequentially dimethylates the amine to produce baeocystin, norpsilocybin, and finally psilocybin

This biosynthetic gene cluster (*psi* cluster) has been identified in multiple *Psilocybe* species and successfully expressed heterologously in *E. coli* and yeast (*Saccharomyces cerevisiae*), enabling biotechnological production of psilocybin without fungal cultivation.

## Pharmacology

### Mechanism of Action

Psilocybin (via its active metabolite psilocin) is a **serotonin 5-HT₂A receptor agonist** — the primary mechanism underlying its psychedelic effects. The receptor binding profile includes:

| Receptor | Action | Significance |
|----------|--------|-------------|
| **5-HT₂A** | Full/partial agonist | Primary mediator of psychedelic effects |
| **5-HT₂C** | Agonist | Contributes to anxiogenic effects; modulates dopamine |
| **5-HT₁A** | Partial agonist | May contribute to anxiolytic and anti-depressant effects |
| **5-HT₂B** | Agonist | Cardiac valve concern at high chronic doses |
| **Sigma-1** | Agonist | Neuroprotective and neuromodulatory effects |

### Pharmacokinetics

- **Absorption:** Psilocybin is rapidly absorbed after oral administration. Peak plasma concentrations of psilocin occur within 40–90 minutes.
- **Metabolism:** Complete dephosphorylation to psilocin occurs primarily in the gastrointestinal tract and liver. Psilocin is further metabolized by monoamine oxidase (MAO-A) to 4-hydroxyindoleacetic acid (4-HIAA) and by glucuronidation.
- **Half-life:** Psilocin elimination half-life is approximately 2–3 hours.
- **Excretion:** Primarily renal, with ~65% of dose excreted in urine within 24 hours (mostly as psilocin glucuronide).
- **Duration of action:** The psychedelic experience typically lasts 4–6 hours, depending on dose, set, and setting.

## Clinical Research

### Depression

Psilocybin has shown remarkable efficacy in treatment-resistant depression (TRD) in multiple clinical trials:

- **Imperial College London (2016):** Open-label study of 12 patients with TRD showed significant reduction in depressive symptoms at 1 week and 3 months post-treatment, with effect sizes comparable to or exceeding those of conventional antidepressants.
- **COMPASS Pathways Phase II (2021):** Randomized controlled trial of 233 patients with TRD. Single 25 mg dose of COMP360 psilocybin produced rapid and sustained antidepressant effects at 3 weeks, with ~29% achieving remission vs. 8% for 1 mg control.
- **Yale University (2023):** fMRI studies revealed that psilocybin's antidepressant mechanism involves increased global brain connectivity, particularly in networks that are typically rigidified in depression.

### End-of-Life Anxiety

A landmark study at Johns Hopkins (2016) demonstrated that a single moderate-to-high dose of psilocybin significantly reduced depression and anxiety in patients with life-threatening cancer diagnoses, with effects sustained at 6-month follow-up. These findings have been replicated at NYU Langone and other centers.

### Substance Use Disorders

- **Tobacco cessation:** A pilot study at Johns Hopkins achieved an 80% abstinence rate at 6 months, dramatically exceeding standard therapies (~35% at 6 months).
- **Alcohol dependence:** A randomized controlled trial at NYU (2022) showed significant reductions in heavy drinking days following psilocybin-assisted psychotherapy.
- **Other addictions:** Preliminary research suggests potential for treating cocaine, opioid, and cannabis use disorders.

### Neuroplasticity

Psilocybin promotes structural and functional neuroplasticity:
- Increased dendritic spine density and arborization in cortical neurons
- Upregulation of BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor)
- Enhanced functional connectivity between brain networks (default mode, salience, executive control)
- Disruption of rigid patterns in the default mode network (DMN), associated with the "ego dissolution" experience

## Therapeutic Framework: Set and Setting

The psychedelic therapeutic model, first articulated by Timothy Leary and refined by modern researchers, emphasizes two critical variables:

- **Set:** The psychological state, expectations, personality, and preparation of the individual. Extensive preparation (often 8–12 hours of preparatory sessions) is standard in clinical protocols.
- **Setting:** The physical and social environment in which the experience occurs. Clinical settings typically involve a comfortable room, calming music, eyeshades, and the continuous presence of one or two trained facilitators/therapists.

Post-experience **integration sessions** (typically 2–4 sessions) help patients process and apply insights from the psychedelic experience to their daily lives and therapeutic goals.

## Safety and Toxicity

### Toxicity Profile

Psilocybin has an exceptionally favorable safety profile for a psychoactive substance:

- **LD50 (IV, rats):** 285 mg/kg — approximately 100× a typical recreational dose
- **No known lethal overdose** in humans has been documented
- **No physical dependence** or withdrawal syndrome
- **No significant organ toxicity** at recreational or clinical doses
- **Self-limiting effects:** Even at very high doses, the experience resolves within 8–12 hours

### Psychological Risks

- **Acute anxiety/panic ("bad trip"):** Can occur, especially in uncontrolled settings. Managed with reassurance, change of music/environment, or in clinical settings, benzodiazepines.
- **Hallucinogen Persisting Perception Disorder (HPPD):** Rare condition involving persistent visual disturbances. Prevalence estimated at 1–4.2 per 10,000 lifetime psychedelic users.
- **Precipitation of psychosis:** Extremely rare but possible in individuals with personal or family history of schizophrenia or bipolar disorder. Rigorous screening protocols are used in clinical trials.
- **Flashbacks:** Transient re-experiencing of psychedelic effects, distinct from HPPD.

## Occurrence in Nature

Psilocybin occurs naturally in over 200 species across multiple genera:

- ***Psilocybe* (most common):** *P. cubensis*, *P. semilanceata*, *P. cyanescens*, *P. azurescens*, *P. mexicana*, *P. tampanensis*
- ***Panaeolus*:** *P. cyanescens* (blue meanies)
- ***Gymnopilus*:** *G. spectabilis* (big laughing gym)
- ***Inocybe*:** Several species (difficult to identify; not recommended)
- ***Pluteus*:** *P. salicinus*, *P. salicinus*

Psilocybin-producing species are found on every inhabited continent, growing primarily on herbivore dung, decaying wood, and rich organic soils in grasslands and forests.
