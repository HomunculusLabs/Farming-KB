    21|# Psilocybin
    22|
    23|## Overview
    24|
    25|**Psilocybin** (O-phosphoryl-4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) is a naturally occurring prodrug psychedelic compound of the tryptamine class. It is produced by over 200 species of fungi, primarily in the genera *Psilocybe*, *Panaeolus*, *Copelandia*, *Inocybe*, and *Gymnopilus*. Upon ingestion, psilocybin is rapidly dephosphorylated by alkaline phosphatase into **psilocin** (4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine, 4-HO-DMT), the pharmacologically active form responsible for its psychoactive effects.

    39|### Structure-Activity Relationships
    40|
    41|Psilocybin is a 4-substituted tryptamine closely related to:
    42|
    43|- **DMT** (N,N-dimethyltryptamine) — lacks the 4-hydroxyl group; active parent compound
    44|- **Psilocin** (4-HO-DMT) — active metabolite; 4-hydroxy analog of DMT
    45|- **Bufotenin** (5-HO-DMT) — 5-hydroxy isomer; less psychoactive, more toxic
    46|- **LSD** — shares serotonergic mechanism but different structure
    47|
    48|The 4-hydroxyl group increases polarity and reduces blood-brain barrier penetration relative to DMT, contributing to psilocybin's longer duration and smoother onset compared to inhaled DMT.
    49|
    50|## Mechanism of Action
    51|
    52|### 5-HT2A Receptor Agonism
    53|
    54|Psilocybin's primary mechanism is as a partial agonist at the **serotonin 5-HT2A receptor**. This receptor is densely expressed in cortical layer V pyramidal neurons and is the key mediator of the psychedelic experience:
    55|
    56|- **Glutamate release**: 5-HT2A activation stimulates glutamate release from thalamocortical projections, amplifying cortical signaling
    57|- **Default Mode Network (DMN) suppression**: Psilocybin reduces activity and connectivity within the DMN — the brain network associated with self-referential thought, rumination, and the ego. This "DMN disintegration" correlates with the subjective experience of ego dissolution
    58|- **Global connectivity increase**: Simultaneously, psilocybin increases functional connectivity between brain regions that normally do not communicate, which may underlie novel insights and cognitive flexibility
    59|
    60|### Other Receptor Interactions
    61|
    62|| Receptor | Affinity | Functional Effect |

    70|### Neuroplasticity
    71|
    72|Research from 2018 onward has demonstrated that psilocybin promotes rapid and sustained structural neuroplasticity:
    73|
    74|- **Dendritic spine growth**: In animal models, a single dose increases dendritic spine density in the prefrontal cortex within 24 hours
    75|- **BDNF upregulation**: Brain-derived neurotrophic factor expression increases through TrkB signaling
    76|- **mTOR activation**: The mammalian target of rapamycin pathway is activated, supporting synaptic protein synthesis
    77|- **Duration**: Structural changes persist for at least 30 days post-dose in animal studies
    78|
    79|These neuroplastic effects are believed to be central to psilocybin's therapeutic potential — essentially "resetting" maladaptive neural circuits.

    88|| Half-life | ~50 minutes (psilocybin); ~3 hours (psilocin) |
    89|| Active metabolite | Psilocin |
    90|| Excretion | Renal (urine), ~80% within 24 hours |
    91|
    92|Psilocybin is typically administered orally, often in capsule form in clinical settings. The effective dose ranges from 10–30 mg (0.14–0.42 mg/kg for a 70 kg person), with higher doses producing more intense experiences.

    94|## Clinical Research
    95|
    96|### Treatment-Resistant Depression
    97|
    98|The most advanced clinical application is for treatment-resistant depression (TRD):
    99|
   100|- **COMPASS Pathways Phase IIb (2021)**: 233 patients with TRD received a single 25 mg dose of synthetic psilocybin. At week 3, 37% achieved remission (vs. 7% placebo). Effects sustained at 12 weeks for many participants
   101|- **Imperial College London studies**: Repeated-dose protocols showed sustained antidepressant effects at 6-month follow-up
   102|- **Yale (2022)**: Found psilocybin produces rapid and large antidepressant effects in mouse models, mediated by structural neuroplasticity
   103|
   104|### End-of-Life Anxiety
   105|
   106|- **NYU Langone (2016)**: Single dose of psilocybin (0.3 mg/kg) produced significant reductions in death-related anxiety and depression in cancer patients, sustained at 6-month and 4.5-year follow-ups
   107|- **Johns Hopkins (2016)**: Similar results — 83% of psilocybin group rated it among the top 5 most meaningful experiences of their lives
   108|
   109|### Substance Use Disorders
   110|
   111|- **Tobacco cessation (Johns Hopkins, 2014)**: Open-label study showed 80% abstinence at 6 months (15/17 participants), remarkably higher than standard treatments (~35%)
   112|- **Alcohol dependence (NYU, 2022)**: Double-blind study showed 50% reduction in heavy drinking days at 32 weeks post-treatment
   113|
   114|### Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder
   115|
   116|Early open-label studies (Moreno et al., 2006) showed acute reductions in OCD symptoms, though larger controlled trials are still needed.

   118|## The Psychedelic Experience
   119|
   120|### Subjective Effects
   121|
   122|The psilocybin experience is highly variable, influenced by set (mindset), setting (environment), and dose:
   123|
   124|- **Visual**: Enhanced colors, geometric patterns (form constants), synesthesia, altered perception of space and time
   125|- **Emotional**: Intensified emotions, profound awe, grief processing, emotional catharsis
   126|- **Cognitive**: Altered thought patterns, philosophical insights, dissolution of ordinary conceptual frameworks
   127|- **Mystical-type experiences**: At higher doses, experiences meeting criteria for "mystical experiences" (as measured by the Mystical Experience Questionnaire) are common and predict better therapeutic outcomes
   128|
   129|### Dosage Categories
   130|
   131|| Category | Dose Range | Character |
   132||----------|-----------|-----------|
   133|| Mini-dose | 0.1–0.3 g (dried) | Sub-perceptual; mood enhancement |
   134|| Low | 0.5–1.5 g | Mild sensory enhancement; relaxed |
   135|| Moderate | 1.5–3.0 g | Full psychedelic; visual effects, introspection |
   136|| High | 3.0–5.0 g | Intense experience; ego dissolution common |
   137|| Heroic | 5.0 g+ | Complete ego dissolution; profound experiences |
   138|
   139|### Risks and Contraindications
   140|
   141|- **Psychological**: Acute anxiety, paranoia (more likely in uncontrolled settings), HPPD (Hallucinogen Persisting Perception Disorder — rare)
   142|- **Schizophrenia risk**: Contraindicated for individuals with personal or family history of psychotic disorders
   143|- **Serotonin syndrome risk**: Theoretical interaction with SSRIs/MAOIs, though clinical incidence is low
   144|- **Cardiovascular**: Mild increases in blood pressure and heart rate; caution in those with cardiovascular disease
   145|- **No significant toxicity**: Psilocybin has no known lethal dose in humans (LD₅₀ estimated > 1000× typical active dose)
