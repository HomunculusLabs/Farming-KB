# Psilocybin

## Overview

Psilocybin (4-PO-DMT, O-phosphoryl-4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) is a naturally occurring psychedelic prodrug and the primary psychoactive compound found in over 200 species of psilocybin mushrooms (*Psilocybe* genus and others). Upon ingestion, psilocybin is rapidly dephosphorylated by alkaline phosphatase enzymes into psilocin (4-HO-DMT), the pharmacologically active form that acts primarily as a serotonin 5-HT2A receptor agonist. After decades of suppression, psilocybin has emerged as one of the most promising compounds in modern psychiatry, showing remarkable efficacy in treating depression, anxiety, addiction, and end-of-life distress.

## Pharmacology

### Pharmacokinetics

- **Onset of action:** 20–60 minutes after oral ingestion
- **Peak plasma concentration:** 80–120 minutes post-ingestion
- **Duration of effect:** 4–6 hours (oral)
- **Half-life:** ~50 minutes (psilocybin); ~160 minutes (psilocin)
- **Bioavailability:** ~50% (oral, first-pass metabolism)
- **Metabolism:** Hepatic dephosphorylation to psilocin, then monoamine oxidase (MAO-A) oxidation to 4-hydroxyindoleacetic acid (4-HIAA)
- **Excretion:** Primarily renal (~65% unchanged psilocin and metabolites)

### Receptor Binding Profile

Psilocin is a moderately selective serotonergic psychedelic:

| Receptor | Ki (nM) | Functional effect |
|----------|---------|-------------------|
| 5-HT2A | 6–12 | Full agonist (primary psychedelic mechanism) |
| 5-HT2C | 12–25 | Full agonist (contributes to anxiety/thought loops) |
| 5-HT1A | 50–100 | Partial agonist (anxiolytic contribution) |
| 5-HT1B | 100–200 | Agonist |
| 5-HT1D | 100–200 | Agonist |
| 5-HT6 | 150–300 | Moderate affinity |
| 5-HT7 | 200–500 | Low affinity |
| Dopamine D2 | 10,000+ | Negligible |

### Mechanism of Action

The psychedelic experience induced by psilocybin arises primarily from 5-HT2A receptor activation in the cerebral cortex, particularly in layer V pyramidal neurons. This leads to:

1. **Cortical hyperconnectivity:** Increased cross-talk between brain regions that normally don't communicate, correlating with synesthesia, ego dissolution, and novel insights
2. **Default Mode Network (DMN) suppression:** Reduced activity in the DMN (medial prefrontal cortex, posterior cingulate cortex), associated with decreased self-referential thinking and ego boundaries
3. **Entropy increase:** Elevated brain signal complexity and unpredictability, measured via fMRI and EEG
4. **Glutamate release:** 5-HT2A activation triggers glutamatergic signaling, promoting neuroplasticity

### Neuroplasticity

A critical discovery in recent years is psilocybin's ability to promote structural and functional neuroplasticity:

- Stimulates dendritic spine growth in the prefrontal cortex (observed in animal models within 24 hours)
- Upregulates Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor (BDNF) and mammalian Target of Rapamycin (mTOR) signaling
- Increases synaptic protein synthesis (PSD-95, GluA1, Synapsin-1)
- Promotes neurogenesis in the hippocampus
- These plasticity effects persist for weeks to months after a single dose, potentially explaining the sustained clinical benefits

## Clinical Applications

### Treatment-Resistant Depression

The most advanced clinical application. In a Phase IIb trial (Carhart-Harris et al., 2021), 25 mg of psilocybin in a single session produced:

- **17-point reduction** in MADRS depression scores at 3 weeks
- **Remission rate of 29%** at 3 weeks (vs. 8% for escitalopram control)
- **Sustained improvement** at 12-week follow-up
- **Rapid onset:** Significant improvement within 1 week (vs. 4–6 weeks for SSRIs)

In 2024, COMPASS Pathways reported Phase III trial results showing 25 mg psilocybin achieved a statistically significant reduction in depressive symptoms compared to 1 mg (active placebo) at week 12.

### End-of-Life Anxiety and Depression

In landmark studies by Griffiths and Ross at Johns Hopkins and NYU:

- Single high-dose session (0.3–0.4 mg/kg) produced significant reductions in depression and anxiety in patients with life-threatening cancer diagnoses
- Benefits sustained at 6-month and 4.5-year follow-ups
- ~80% of patients reported clinically significant improvement
- Increased acceptance of death and quality of life

### Substance Use Disorders

Preliminary but promising results:
- **Tobacco cessation:** 80% abstinence rate at 6 months in an open-label study (vs. ~35% for varenicline)
- **Alcohol use disorder:** 48% reduction in heavy drinking days at 8 weeks in a randomized controlled trial (Bogenschutz et al., 2022)
- **Cocaine/methamphetamine:** Early-phase trials underway

### Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder

Open-label studies have shown significant Y-BOCS score reductions following psilocybin administration, with some patients experiencing months of symptom relief from a single session.

## Therapeutic Model: Psychological Support

Clinical outcomes are heavily dependent on the therapeutic context. The established model includes:

1. **Preparation (1–3 sessions):** Building trust, setting intentions, explaining the experience, managing expectations
2. **Dosing session (6–8 hours):** Administered in a comfortable, controlled setting with 1–2 trained therapists present; eyeshades and curated music playlist; non-directive psychological support
3. **Integration (1–3 sessions):** Processing the experience, translating insights into behavioral change, maintaining therapeutic gains

## Natural Occurrence

Psilocybin is produced by over 200 species of fungi across multiple genera:

- ***Psilocybe*** (most common): P. cubensis, P. semilanceata, P. cyanescens, P. azurescens, P. mexicana
- ***Panaeolus***: P. cyanescens, P. subbalteatus
- ***Gymnopilus***: G. spectabilis, G. junonius
- ***Inocybe***: Various species
- ***Pluteus***: P. salicinus

## Legal Status

Psilocybin is classified as a Schedule I substance under the UN Convention on Psychotropic Substances (1971). However, regulatory reform is accelerating:

- **United States:** Schedule I federally; decriminalized in Denver (2019), Oregon (2020, Measure 109), Santa Cruz, Oakland, Washington DC, and numerous other municipalities. Oregon's licensed psilocybin therapy program began operations in 2023. Colorado decriminalized in 2022 (Proposition 122).
- **Canada:** Granted "Section 56" exemptions for palliative care use; Health Canada considering rescheduling
- **Netherlands:** Fresh "truffles" (sclerotia) are legally sold in smart shops
- **Australia:** TGA approved psilocybin as a Schedule 8 controlled drug for treatment-resistant depression (2023, psychiatrist-prescribed)
- **EU:** Clinical trials ongoing in UK, Germany, Netherlands, Switzerland

## Safety and Risks

### Physiological Safety

Psilocybin has an exceptionally high physiological safety profile:

- **LD50:** ~280 mg/kg in mice (estimated human LD50 >6 g, far above therapeutic doses of 10–30 mg)
- **No demonstrated organ toxicity** at any studied dose
- **No physical dependence** or withdrawal syndrome
- **Minimal cardiovascular effect:** Transient increase in blood pressure and heart rate (generally <20% increase)
- **No evidence of teratogenicity** (animal studies)

### Psychological Risks

The primary risks are psychological rather than physiological:

- **Challenging experiences ("bad trips"):** Occur in ~10–20% of sessions even in clinical settings; can involve extreme anxiety, paranoia, confusion, or fear
- **Hallucinogen Persisting Perception Disorder (HPPD):** Rare (<1 in 50,000 users); persistent visual disturbances
- **Precipitation of psychotic episodes:** Significant risk in individuals with personal or family history of schizophrenia or bipolar disorder
- **Trauma reactivation:** Unprocessed traumatic memories may surface during sessions

### Absolute Contraindications

- Schizophrenia spectrum disorders
- Bipolar I disorder (manic phase)
- Current psychosis
- Severe cardiovascular disease
- Pregnancy (precautionary)
- Use on SSRIs/SNRIs (may significantly attenuate effects due to 5-HT2A receptor downregulation; requires 2–4 week washout)
