# Psilocybin

**Psilocybin** (systematic name: **3-[2-(dimethylamino)ethyl]-1H-indol-4-yl dihydrogen phosphate**)
is a naturally occurring **prodrug psychedelic compound** and the principal psychoactive constituent
of "magic mushrooms" (psilocybin-containing fungi of the genus *Psilocybe* and related genera).
Upon ingestion, psilocybin is rapidly dephosphorylated by alkaline phosphatase enzymes into its
active metabolite **psilocin** (4-hydroxy-DMT), which is primarily responsible for the
characteristic psychedelic effects.

## Discovery and History

- **1957:** R. Gordon Wasson and his wife Valentina Pavlovna Wasson published a photo-essay in
  *LIFE* magazine titled "Seeking the Magic Mushroom," describing their experiences with
  *Psilocybe* mushrooms in Mexico with Mazatec curandera María Sabina. This brought psilocybin
  mushrooms to Western attention.
- **1958:** Albert Hofmann — the Swiss chemist who first synthesized LSD — isolated and identified
  psilocybin and psilocin from *Psilocybe mexicana* specimens collected by Wasson. Hofmann
  subsequently synthesized both compounds.
- **1959–1968:** Sandoz Laboratories marketed psilocybin as **Indocybin** for psychiatric research.
  Over 1,000 clinical papers were published during this period exploring its therapeutic potential.
- **1970:** The U.S. Controlled Substances Act placed psilocybin in Schedule I, effectively halting
  all legal research for over two decades.
- **2000–present:** A renaissance in psychedelic research has produced landmark clinical trials
  demonstrating efficacy in treatment-resistant depression, anxiety, substance use disorders,
  and end-of-life distress.

## Pharmacology

### Mechanism of Action

Psilocybin is a **serotonin 5-HT₂A receptor agonist** — the same receptor subtype through which
LSD, DMT, and mescaline exert their primary psychedelic effects. The pharmacological cascade:

1. **Psilocybin → Psilocin:** Alkaline phosphatase in the gut wall and liver rapidly dephosphorylates
   psilocybin to psilocin (half-life ~30 minutes for conversion)
2. **Psilocin → 5-HT₂A activation:** Psilocin acts as a partial agonist at the 5-HT₂A receptor
   (intrinsic activity ~80% relative to serotonin), with additional affinity for 5-HT₁A, 5-HT₂B,
   5-HT₂C, and 5-HT₇ receptors
3. **Downstream effects:** 5-HT₂A activation on cortical pyramidal neurons (Layer V) leads to
   increased cortical excitability, disrupted default mode network (DMN) connectivity, and
   enhanced cross-modal neural communication

### Pharmacokinetics

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Onset of action | 20–60 minutes (oral) |
| Time to peak plasma | 80–100 minutes |
| Active metabolite | Psilocin (t½ ~3 hours) |
| Elimination half-life | ~50 minutes (psilocybin), ~3 hours (psilocin) |
| Duration of effects | 4–6 hours (10–30 mg oral dose) |
| Route of metabolism | Hepatic (CYP450, primarily dephosphorylation) |
| Excretion | Renal (as psilocin glucuronide) |
| Bioavailability | ~50% (first-pass metabolism) |

### Tolerance

Tolerance to psilocybin develops rapidly — significant reduction in effects occurs after just
2–3 consecutive daily doses. Full tolerance reversal requires 5–14 days of abstinence.
Cross-tolerance exists with other serotonergic psychedelics (LSD, DMT, mescaline).

## Clinical Applications

### Treatment-Resistant Depression (TRD)

The most extensively studied clinical application. Key findings:

- **Carhart-Harris et al. (2016, 2021):** Phase II trials at Imperial College London demonstrated
  rapid and sustained antidepressant effects. A single 25 mg dose produced significant reduction
  in PHQ-9 scores at 3 weeks, maintained at 6-month follow-up in many patients.
- **COMPASS Pathways Phase IIb (2021):** 25 mg psilocybin + psychological support produced
  significant reduction in MADRS scores vs. 1 mg control (difference of -6.6 points at week 3).
- **Mechanism:** Believed to involve DMN "reset," increased neural plasticity (BDNF upregulation),
  and enhanced emotional processing. Functional MRI shows decreased DMN connectivity correlates
  with therapeutic outcome.

### End-of-Life Anxiety and Depression

- **Griffiths et al. (2016):** Single moderate-to-high dose of psilocybin produced significant
  decreases in depression and anxiety in patients with life-threatening cancer diagnoses.
  Effects sustained at 6-month follow-up. Rated by 80% of participants as among the most
  personally meaningful experiences of their lives.

### Substance Use Disorders

- **Alcohol use disorder:** Bogenschutz et al. (2022) demonstrated that two psilocybin sessions
  (25 mg and 10–25 mg) plus psychotherapy significantly reduced heavy drinking days compared to
  placebo (44% reduction vs. 28% for placebo) over 32 weeks.
- **Tobacco cessation:** A 2014 pilot study (Johnson et al.) found 80% abstinence at 6-month
  follow-up from a single psilocybin session — dramatically outperforming standard therapies
  (~35% at 6 months with varenicline).
- **Cocaine dependence:** Ongoing trials (NIDA-funded) exploring psilocybin for stimulant use
  disorder, an area with no FDA-approved pharmacotherapy.

### Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder

- A 2006 pilot study (Moreno et al.) found significant reductions in Y-BOCS scores at 24 hours
  post-dose. Full-scale trials are underway.

## Psychological Effects

### Subjective Experience

The psilocybin experience is highly variable depending on dose, set (mindset), setting (environment),
and individual factors:

- **Low dose (sub-perceptual to mild, 0.5–2.5 mg):** Enhanced mood, increased sociability,
  heightened sensory appreciation, subtle visual changes
- **Moderate dose (2.5–10 mg):** Noticeable visual distortions, altered time perception,
  enhanced introspection, emotional openness, synesthesia-like experiences
- **High dose (10–25+ mg):** Ego dissolution, mystical-type experiences, vivid closed-eye
  visuals, profound emotional catharsis, philosophical insights, encounters with archetypal
  imagery, altered sense of self and reality

### Mystical Experience Scale (MEQ30)

High-dose psilocybin reliably produces experiences scoring in the "complete mystical experience"
range on the Mystical Experience Questionnaire. Key components:
- Unity — sense of oneness with all things
- Sacredness — encounter with something holy or divine
- Noetic quality — sense of gaining genuine knowledge or insight
- Deep positive mood — peace, joy, love
- Transcendence of time/space
- Ineffability — difficulty describing the experience in words

## Natural Occurrence

Psilocybin and psilocin are produced by over 200 species of fungi across multiple genera:

- ***Psilocybe*** — the primary genus (~150+ species); includes *P. cubensis*, *P. semilanceata*,
  *P. cyanescens*, *P. azurescens*
- ***Gymnopilus*** — ~14 species including *G. spectabilis* (Big Laughing Gym)
- ***Panaeolus*** — ~13 species including *P. cyanescens* (Blue Meanies)
- ***Copelandia*** — ~12 species; very potent
- ***Inocybe*** — ~10 species; easily confused with toxic species
- ***Pluteus*** — ~6 species
- ***Conocybe*** — ~4 species

## Safety and Risk Profile

Psilocybin has a remarkably favorable safety profile for a psychoactive substance:

- **Lethal dose:** Estimated LD50 > 280 mg/kg in rats; human fatal overdose has never been
  documented from psilocybin alone. Physiologically, psilocybin is one of the least toxic
  recreational substances known.
- **Physical risks:** Minimal. May cause transient increases in blood pressure and heart rate.
  Nausea is common during onset.
- **Psychological risks:** The primary risks are psychological — anxiety, confusion, paranoia,
  and challenging ("bad trip") experiences, especially at high doses, in unprepared individuals,
  or in unsupportive settings. HPPD (Hallucinogen Persisting Perception Disorder) is extremely
  rare (<1 in 50,000).
- **No addiction potential:** No evidence of physical dependence or addiction. Reinforcing
  effects are low; most users report decreased desire for frequent use after a meaningful
  psychedelic experience.
- **Contraindications:** Schizophrenia spectrum disorders, bipolar I disorder (active manic
  episode), personal or family history of psychosis, cardiovascular disease (relative),
  pregnancy.
