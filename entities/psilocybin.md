# Psilocybin

**4-PO-DMT** | **O-Phosphoryl-4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine**

Psilocybin is a naturally occurring psychedelic prodrug and the principal psychoactive compound found in over 200 species of mushrooms, primarily in the genera *Psilocybe*, *Panaeolus*, *Gymnopilus*, and *Inocybe*. Originally isolated by Swiss chemist Albert Hofmann in 1958 from *Psilocybe mexicana*, psilocybin is a serotonergic psychedelic that has experienced a remarkable renaissance in psychiatric research since the 2010s, showing clinical efficacy in treatment-resistant depression, end-of-life anxiety, substance use disorders, and other psychiatric conditions.

## Chemical Properties

- **IUPAC:** [3-(2-Dimethylaminoethyl)-1H-indol-4-yl] dihydrogen phosphate
- **Formula:** C₁₂H₁₇N₂O₄P | **MW:** 284.25 g/mol | **CAS:** 520-52-5
- **Appearance:** White crystalline solid
- **Solubility:** Water (soluble), ethanol (slight), nonpolar solvents (insoluble)
- **Melting point:** 220–228°C (decomposes) | **pKa:** ~6.5, ~9.4
- **Class:** Prodrug (converted to psilocin)

## Pharmacology

## Metabolism

Psilocybin is a prodrug rapidly dephosphorylated by alkaline phosphatase to **psilocin** (4-HO-DMT), the active compound.

```
Psilocybin → alkaline phosphatase → Psilocin → CYP2D6, MAO-A → 4-HO-IAA → glucuronidation → urine (65% in 24h)
```

### Pharmacokinetics

- **Onset:** 20–60 min | **Peak:** 60–90 min | **Duration:** 4–6 hours | **t½:** 2–5 hours
- **Bioavailability:** ~50% oral (first-pass metabolism) | **Protein binding:** ~43%

### Mechanism of Action

Psilocin is a **partial agonist at 5-HT₂A** (Ki ≈ 6 nM), with additional activity at 5-HT₂C, 5-HT₁A:

- **5-HT₂A agonism:** Increases cortical excitability and brain entropy in layer V pyramidal neurons
- **DMN suppression:** Reduces default mode network connectivity, enabling "ego dissolution" correlated with therapeutic outcomes
- **Brain entropy:** Increases complexity of spontaneous activity, facilitating novel associative thinking
- **Glutamate release:** 5-HT₂A activation triggers downstream cortical glutamate modulation
- **BDNF upregulation:** Promotes neuroplasticity, enabling lasting change from limited sessions

## Natural Occurrence

Psilocybin and analogs (psilocin, baeocystin, norbaeocystin, aeruginascin) are biosynthesized via the tryptamine pathway. Key enzymes: PsiD (Trp decarboxylation), PsiH (4-hydroxylation), PsiK (phosphorylation), PsiM (N-methylation). This pathway has been engineered into yeast and *E. coli* for biomanufacturing.

### Major Psilocybin-Containing Genera

| Genus | Notable Species | Content |
|-------|----------------|---------|
| *Psilocybe* | *P. cubensis, P. semilanceata, P. cyanescens, P. azurescens* | 0.1–1.8% dry wt |
| *Panaeolus* | *P. cyanescens, P. subbalteatus* | 0.1–0.8% |
| *Gymnopilus* | *G. spectabilis, G. junonius* | 0.01–0.3% |
| *Inocybe* | *I. corydalina, I. aeruginascens* | 0.01–0.2% |

*P. azurescens* is the most potent known species (up to 1.8% psilocybin, 0.5% psilocin).

## Clinical Research and Therapeutic Applications

### Treatment-Resistant Depression

- **Carhart-Harris et al. (2016, Lancet Psychiatry):** Open-label, 12 TRD patients. Single 25 mg dose produced rapid antidepressant effects; all showed symptom reduction, 8 in remission at 3 months.
- **COMPASS Pathways Phase IIb (2021):** RCT of 233 patients. 25 mg dose significantly reduced MADRS scores vs. 1 mg control (p<0.001); 37% remission at 3 weeks.

### End-of-Life Anxiety

- **Ross et al. (2016):** RCT in advanced cancer patients showed rapid reductions in anxiety/depression persisting at 6 months.
- **NYU Langone:** 80% showed clinically significant reductions in cancer-related depression/anxiety.

### Substance Use Disorders

- **Tobacco (Johns Hopkins, 2014):** 80% abstinence at 6 months; 67% at 12 months
- **Alcohol (Bogenschutz et al., 2022):** Phase II RCT showed significant reductions in heavy drinking days
- Ongoing trials for cocaine, opioid use, and gambling disorder

### OCD

- Pilot (Moreno et al., 2006): acute OCD symptom reductions with 0.25–4.5 mg/70 kg; larger trials planned

## Dose-Response Relationship

| Dose (oral) | Category | Effects |
|------------|----------|---------|
| 0.5–1.0 g dried | Microdose | Sub-perceptual; reported cognitive/mood benefits (not robustly validated) |
| 1.0–2.5 g dried | Low | Mild perceptual changes, enhanced mood |
| 2.5–5.0 g dried | Moderate | Significant visuals, altered thought, mystical experiences possible |
| 5.0+ g dried | High | Intense effects, ego dissolution, challenging states possible |

## Safety Profile

### Acute Toxicity

- **LD₅₀ (mice, IV):** 280 mg/kg | **LD₅₀ (rats, oral):** 12.5 mg/kg
- **No confirmed human fatalities** from psilocybin overdose alone
- **Non-addictive:** No physical dependence; tolerance develops rapidly (3–7 days)

### Psychological Risks

- **Challenging experiences:** Anxiety, paranoia, confusion (10–20% incidence); mitigated by set, setting, and preparation
- **HPPD:** Hallucinogen Persisting Perception Disorder; rare (<1:50,000)
- **Psychosis risk:** Elevated with personal/family history of schizophrenia or bipolar disorder
- **Contraindicated with:** MAOIs, lithium, other serotonergic drugs (serotonin syndrome risk)

### Legal Status

- **UN Convention (1971):** Schedule I
- **United States:** Schedule I; DEA-approved clinical research
- **Netherlands:** Fresh mushrooms banned 2008; truffles (sclerotia) remain legal
- **Oregon:** Measure 109 (2020) — therapeutic use under licensed facilitation
- **Colorado:** Proposition 122 (2022) — decriminalized, regulated access
- **Australia (2023):** TGA rescheduled to Schedule 8 for treatment-resistant depression
- **Canada:** Special Access Programme for serious conditions
- **Portugal, Brazil, Jamaica:** Various decriminalization levels

## Research Chemical Analogs

- **4-AcO-DMT (Psilacetin):** Synthetic prodrug converting to psilocin; nearly identical subjective effects
- **4-HO-MET (Metocin):** Shorter-acting, more colorful visual effects, less cognitive depth
- **5-MeO-DMT:** Distinct compound from *Bufo alvarius* toad venom; not a psilocybin analog but grouped in research context

## Cultural and Historical Context

- **Aztec/Nahua:** *Teonanácatl* ("flesh of the gods") — used in religious ceremonies
- **Mazatec tradition:** Continued ceremonial use by healers like María Sabina, who introduced psilocybin to Western researchers (R. Gordon Wasson, 1955)
- **Western counterculture (1960s):** Promoted by Timothy Leary, Terence McKenna, and others
- **Psychedelic renaissance (2010s–present):** Renewed scientific interest; decriminalization movements
