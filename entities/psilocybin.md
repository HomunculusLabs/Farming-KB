# Psilocybin

## Overview

Psilocybin (4-PO-DMT; O-phosphoryl-4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) is a naturally occurring prodrug [[psychedelic-research-legal-history-united-states|Psychedelic]] compound produced by over 200 species of [[neurotropic-fungi-classification|Fungi]] in the genus *[[psilocybe-cubensis|Psilocybe]]* and related genera. Upon ingestion, psilocybin is rapidly dephosphorylated by alkaline phosphatase enzymes into psilocin (4-HO-DMT), the pharmacologically active form. Psilocybin is classified as a classic psychedelic (serotonergic hallucinogen) and has been used for millennia in indigenous Mesoamerican healing and divinatory ceremonies.

## Pharmacology

### Pharmacokinetics

- **Absorption:** Rapid oral absorption; onset 20–60 minutes
- **Bioavailability:** ~50% oral (first-pass metabolism to psilocin)
- **Conversion:** Psilocybin → psilocin via alkaline phosphatase (primarily hepatic and intestinal)
- **Peak plasma concentration:** 60–90 minutes post-ingestion
- **Half-life:** ~2.5–3 hours (psilocin); total experience 4–6 hours
- **Metabolism:** Psilocin is glucuronidated by UGT1A10 (intestinal) and UGT1A9 (hepatic) into psilocin-O-glucuronide (inactive)
- **Excretion:** Primarily renal (~80% as psilocin-O-glucuronide)
- **Detection:** Detectable in urine 1–3 days; hair up to 90 days

### Mechanism of Action

Psilocin is a serotonergic psychedelic acting primarily as a **partial agonist at 5-HT₂A receptors**:

- **5-HT₂A (primary):** Ki ≈ 6 nM — partial agonist; mediates hallucinogenic effects
- **5-HT₂C:** Ki ≈ 20 nM — modulates mood, anxiety, and appetite
- **5-HT₁A:** Ki ≈ 50 nM — partial agonist; contributes to anxiolytic effects
- **5-HT₂B:** Agonist; theoretical cardiac valvulopathy risk at chronic high doses (not observed with intermittent use)
- **Sigma-1 receptor:** Binds with moderate affinity; neuromodulatory effects
- **Dopamine D₂:** Weak binding; minimal dopaminergic contribution
- **TAAR1:** Trace amine-associated receptor 1 activation (emerging research)

### Neuroplasticity

Psilocybin has been shown to promote structural and functional neuroplasticity:

- **Spinogenesis:** Rapid increase in dendritic spine density in cortical neurons (within 24 hours)
- **BDNF upregulation:** Increases brain-derived neurotrophic factor expression via TrkB signaling
- **mTOR activation:** Mammalian target of rapamycin pathway activation drives synaptogenesis
- **5-HT₂A-mediated:** Effects are blocked by 5-HT₂A antagonists (ketanserin), confirming receptor specificity
- **Persistent effects:** Neuroplastic changes may last weeks to months after a single dose

## Clinical Research

### Treatment-Resistant Depression

Landmark trials have demonstrated significant antidepressant effects:

- **Carhart-Harris et al. (2016):** Open-label study showing rapid and sustained antidepressant effects at 6-month follow-up
- **Carhart-Harris et al. (2021):** Phase 2 RCT — single 25 mg dose of psilocybin vs. escitaloprame; psilocybin non-inferior at 6 weeks with faster onset
- **[[compass-pathways]] Phase 2b (2022):** 25 mg psilocybin showed significant reduction in MADRS scores vs. 1 mg control; ~30% achieved remission
- **Effects:** Rapid onset (often within days), sustained response (months), and effective in patients unresponsive to conventional antidepressants

### End-of-Life Anxiety and Depression

- **Grob et al. (2011):** Pilot study in cancer patients showing reduced anxiety and improved mood
- **Ross et al. (2016):** Single-dose psilocybin produced significant, rapid, and sustained reductions in depression, anxiety, and demoralization in cancer patients
- **Griffiths et al. (2016):** Similar results with high-dose psilocybin; 80% of patients showed clinically significant improvement at 6 months

### Substance Use Disorders

- **Tobacco:** Pilot study (Johnson et al., 2014) — 80% abstinence rate at 6 months (vs. ~35% for standard therapy)
- **Alcohol:** Bogenschutz et al. (2015, 2022) — Significant reduction in drinking days and heavy drinking; Phase 2 trials ongoing
- **Mechanism:** May disrupt maladaptive patterns through enhanced psychological flexibility and self-transcendence

### Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder

- **Moreno et al. (2006):** Open-label pilot showing reduction in Yale-Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale scores
- Phase 2 trials planned by multiple research groups

## Dosing

| Dose Range (oral) | Effect Level | Description |
|-------------------|-------------|-------------|
| 0.5–1.0 g dried | Microdose | Sub-perceptual; enhanced focus, mood (ongoing research) |
| 1.0–2.5 g dried | Low | Mild perceptual changes, enhanced colors, light mood elevation |
| 2.5–5.0 g dried | Moderate | Classic psychedelic experience; visual hallucinations, introspection |
| 5.0+ g dried | High (heroic) | Intense ego dissolution, mystical experiences, complete sensory alteration |

*Note: Potency varies significantly between species, growing conditions, and individual mushrooms.*

## Natural Sources

Psilocybin is produced by fungi in the order Agaricales, primarily:

- ***Psilocybe cubensis*** — Most widely distributed; cultivated globally; moderate potency
- ***Psilocybe semilanceata*** — Liberty cap; European and North American; potent
- ***Psilocybe azurescens*** — Astoria Oyster; [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]]; most potent known species (up to 1.8% dry weight)
- ***Psilocybe cyanescens*** — Wavy-cap; widely distributed; potent
- ***Psilocybe mexicana*** — Original ceremonial species used by Mazatec healers
- ***Psilocybe tampanensis*** — "Magic truffles" (sclerotia); cultivated in the Netherlands
- ***[[panaeolus-cyanescens-bali-omelette]]*** — Copelandia; tropical/subtropical; very potent

## Legal Status

Psilocybin is classified as a Schedule I substance under the UN Convention on Psychotropic Substances (1971). However, regulatory landscapes are evolving:

- **United States:** Schedule I federally; decriminalized in several cities and states (Oregon, Colorado); FDA breakthrough therapy designation for treatment-resistant depression
- **Canada:** Schedule III; special access program for medical use; exemptions granted for clinical trials
- **Netherlands:** Psilocybin mushrooms illegal since 2008; "truffles" (sclerotia) remain legally sold
- **Brazil:** Not specifically scheduled; legal gray area
- **Portugal:** Decriminalized for personal use (along with all drugs since 2001)
- **Australia:** Therapeutic Goods Administration approved psilocybin for treatment-resistant depression (2023); Schedule VIII for clinical use
- **Jamaica:** No specific scheduling; retreat centers operate openly

## Risks and Precautions

- **Psychological risk:** Hallucinogen Persisting Perception Disorder (HPPD) is rare (~1 in 50,000 users)
- **Precipitation of psychosis:** Contraindicated for individuals with personal or family history of schizophrenia or [[blesching-cannabis-bipolar-disorder]]
- **Cardiovascular:** Increases blood pressure and heart rate; caution with cardiovascular conditions
- **Serotonin syndrome risk:** Theoretical risk when combined with SSRIs, MAOIs, or other serotonergic drugs (extremely rare in practice)
- **Set and setting:** Psychological outcomes are highly dependent on mindset, expectations, and environment; therapeutic contexts emphasize preparation, integration, and trained facilitator support
