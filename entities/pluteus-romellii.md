# Pluteus romellii

**Pluteus romellii** is a wood-inhabiting mushroom in the family Pluteaceae and one of
several Pluteus species reported to contain [[psilocybin-compound]]. The genus Pluteus is
distinctive for its free gills and pink spore print, characteristics that separate it from
most other gilled mushrooms. While the majority of Pluteus species are non-psychoactive,
a subset of the genus produces indole alkaloids, making them of interest to mycologists
and those studying psychoactive fungi.

## Taxonomy and Classification

Pluteus romellii belongs to the genus Pluteus, a group of saprotrophic mushrooms
within the order Agaricales. The genus is the type genus of the family Pluteaceae,
which also includes the edible Volvariella species. The family is characterized by
free gills (not attached to the stem) and pink spore prints, features that are relatively
uncommon among agarics and serve as key identification markers.

Within Pluteus, species are divided into several sections based on cap surface
characteristics and microscopic features. P. romellii is placed among species with smooth,
non-scutate (non-scaly) caps and dark pigmentation. The species was named in honor of the
Swedish mycologist Lars Romell, who made significant contributions to the study of
wood-inhabiting fungi in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

Molecular phylogenetic studies have confirmed the placement of P. romellii within
Pluteus and have helped clarify species boundaries within the genus, which has
historically been taxonomically challenging due to morphological conservatism and wide
intraspecific variation. The genus appears to be monophyletic, and the psychoactive
species are distributed across multiple clades.

## Description

**Cap:** Convex to broadly convex, sometimes becoming nearly plane with age. Surface is
smooth and silky, typically dark brown to grayish-brown or blackish-brown when young,
often with a silvery or metallic sheen that is distinctive. The surface may become
slightly radially fibrillose with age but does not develop the pronounced scales seen in
some other Pluteus species. The cap is hygrophanous in some collections. Cap diameter
ranges from 20 to 60 mm. The margin is typically smooth and even.

**Gills:** Free (not attached to the stem), which is the defining feature of the genus
Pluteus. The gills are close to subdistant, broad, and white at first, gradually
becoming pink to salmon-pink as spores mature. This pink spore coloration is a critical
identification feature that distinguishes Pluteus from other wood-inhabiting genera with
white or brown spore prints. The gill edges are entire and white.

**Stem:** Central, cylindrical, equal or slightly enlarged at the base. Surface is smooth
to longitudinally striate, white to pale gray or pale brownish. The stem is typically
solid to stuffed, becoming hollow with age. It is smooth and lacks a ring or annulus,
which distinguishes it from some Cortinarius and Gymnopilus species. Length ranges
from 30 to 70 mm, with a thickness of 3 to 6 mm.

**Spore print:** Pink to salmon-pink, characteristic of the Pluteaceae.

**Spores:** Broadly ellipsoid to subglobose, smooth, with a prominent germ pore. Spore
dimensions are approximately 6 to 8 μm by 5 to 7 μm. The spores are relatively
thick-walled and non-amyloid.

**Taste and odor:** Mild, not distinctive, or slightly mealy (farinaceous).

**Bruising:** The context of the cap and stem may develop a bluish or greenish
discoloration when cut or bruised, indicating the presence of psilocybin and/or psilocin.
However, the bluing reaction in Pluteus species is often less dramatic and less reliable
than in Psilocybe species, sometimes requiring several minutes to become visible.

**Microscopic features:** The cheilocystidia are numerous and conspicuous, thin-walled,
ranging from clavate to lageniform or occasionally with diverticulate (branched) apices.
These large, distinctive cheilocystidia are an important microscopic identification feature.
The pileipellis is a cutis of narrow, cylindrical hyphae. Clamp connections are absent,
characteristic of the genus Pluteus.

## Distribution and Habitat

Pluteus romellii has a primarily temperate distribution:

- **Europe:** Widespread across the continent, particularly in western and central
  Europe including the british isles, France, Germany, and Scandinavia
- **North America:** Reported from various regions, though less commonly documented
- **Asia:** Scattered reports from temperate regions including Japan and China

The species is strictly lignicolous, growing on dead wood of deciduous trees:

- Fallen logs and branches of hardwood species, particularly oak, beech, elm,
  and ash
- Stumps of broadleaf trees in various stages of decomposition
- Decomposing timber in various stages of decay
- Occasionally on wood chips or mulch derived from hardwood
- Rarely on conifer wood, though this is atypical

The species shows a preference for well-decayed wood in humid forest environments. Fruiting
occurs from summer through autumn, typically from June to October in temperate regions,
though the season may extend in areas with mild winters.

## Chemistry

Chemical analysis has confirmed the presence of [[psilocybin-compound]] in Pluteus romellii, placing
it among the psychoactive members of the genus. The psychoactive Pluteus species share
several characteristics: they all produce psilocybin and/or psilocin, they typically bruise
bluish when damaged (though subtly), and they are all lignicolous.

The psilocybin content is generally reported as low to moderate. Quantitative data are
limited, but available analyses suggest concentrations are lower than those found in most
Psilocybe species. Other tryptamine derivatives may also be present, including
[[baeocystin]] and [[norbaeocystin]].

## Similar Species

Accurate identification of Pluteus romellii requires attention to several key features:

- [[pluteus-salicinus]]: Another psychoactive species with bluish reaction, more
  grayish-blue cap, preference for willow wood. Generally considered the most reliably
  psychoactive Pluteus species.
- [[pluteus-cervinus]]: The deer shield mushroom is common and widespread, non-psychoactive,
  typically larger with brown fibrillose cap that develops small scales.
- [[pluteus-americanus]]: North American species reported to contain psilocybin, similar
  to P. salicinus in appearance.
- [[pluteus-glaucotinctus]]: Another psilocybin-containing species with distinctive
  blue-green tint to the cap and gills.
- [[pluteus-nigroviridis]]: Dark cap with greenish tones, reported psychoactive.
- Volvariella species: Share free gills and pink spore print but have a volva at the
  stem base, which Pluteus lacks.
- Entoloma species: Also have pinkish spores but sinuate gills and angular spores.

The combination of smooth dark cap with silvery sheen, free pink gills, growth on hardwood,
and bluing reaction distinguishes P. romellii from most similar species.

## Cultivation

P. romellii has not been widely cultivated. Its requirement for hardwood substrates and
relatively low psilocybin content have limited interest. The species could potentially be
grown on sterilized hardwood sawdust using standard agaric cultivation techniques. As a
lignicolous saprotroph, it contributes to the decomposition of dead hardwood in forest
ecosystems.
