# Yakuruna

## Description
Yakuruna (from Quechua *yaku* = water, *runa* = person/people) are aquatic humanoid beings in Amazonian mythology, often described as water people or underwater dwellers. They inhabit submerged cities and palaces beneath the rivers and lakes of the Amazon basin, possessing their own culture, hierarchy, and magical abilities.

## Classification
- **Type**: Mythological being / aquatic humanoid
- **Origin**: Amazonian mestizo and indigenous cosmology (Quechua language roots)
- **Domain**: Subaquatic / riverine
- **Tradition**: Vegetalismo and general Amazonian folklore

## Key Facts
- Multiple types of yakuruna exist, classified by their abilities and moral alignment.
- The *masha yakuruna* is an ally of murayas, teaching them how to rescue people lost in the watery depths. He smokes a pipe made from an encanto (magical stone) with a stem made from the bone of the tuyuyo (*Jabiru mycteria*).
- The *taksha yakuruna* (little yakuruna) has green hair, carries a boa, and comes out to feed between midnight and dawn. If captured by a vegetalista, he must stay on earth and teach hunting in the waters.
- The *yana-sacraruna* (black evil person) practices black magic, lives in the deepest darkest underwater caves, and uses a red conch as a fan to cast evil spells. His sole purpose is killing, and he takes pleasure in causing riverbanks to collapse.
- The *puka-ninaruna* (people of the red flame) lives in the largest underwater cities and is invoked by murayas to control electric eels, sea monsters, thunder, and storms.
- White yakurunas inhabit the first subaquatic region alongside fair-haired mermaids and pink and black dolphins.
- Red yakurunas inhabit the second region alongside blue and red mermaids.
- Black yakurunas inhabit the third and deepest region alongside black mermaids and pato-bufeos (duck-dolphins).
- Yakurunas are depicted eating fish, surrounded by fierce dogs with piranha heads and cats with canero heads.
- Their underwater realms include forests of golden trees with serpent-hammocks, enchanted forests of aquatic plants, and cities of great splendour.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology
- The yakuruna mythology reflects the Amazonian understanding of rivers as inhabited, intelligent ecosystems deserving of respect and caution.
- Stories of yakuruna encounters serve as cultural guidelines for safe behavior near water, relevant to any cultivation or harvesting near riverine environments.
- The hierarchical classification of yakurunas mirrors the indigenous understanding that natural forces can be benevolent, neutral, or malevolent depending on context.

## Cultural Context
Yakurunas appear throughout [[ayahuasca-preparation-pablo-amaringo-ucayali-shamanism]]'s [[amaringo-ayahuasca-visions-shipibo-iconography]], most prominently in Visions 19 (The [[world-of-the-yakuruna]]) and 21. They represent one of the most richly detailed aspects of Amazonian underwater cosmology, reflecting the cultural importance of rivers in Amazonian life.
