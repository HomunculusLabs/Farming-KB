---# Mazatec people

The **Mazatec people** are an Indigenous people of Mexico associated especially with the Sierra Mazateca in northern Oaxaca.
They also live in neighboring communities of Puebla and Veracruz, reflecting migration, trade, and regional continuity around the Papaloapan basin.
In the wiki, they are most important as the living cultural context behind the twentieth-century Western encounter with [[maria-sabina]], and [[metzner-mazatec-mushroom-veladas-darkness-singing-and-christian-syncretism]] are nocturnal healing or divinatory sessions conducted with prayer, chanting, candles, and disciplined attention.
The purpose is not recreational intoxication; it is consultation, diagnosis, healing, or guidance.
The mushrooms are treated as powerful beings or mediators rather than inert chemical delivery systems.
This distinction is essential for interpreting the later laboratory isolation of psilocybin without erasing the ritual frame that led researchers to the material.
## Other Entheogens
Mazatec plant knowledge also includes **Salvia divinorum**, known in Mazatec contexts by names translated through Catholic imagery such as ska María Pastora.
Morning glory seeds from species such as *Ipomoea tricolor* and *[[plants-of-the-gods-turbina-corymbosa-ololiuqui]]* also appear in regional ritual pharmacology.
These materials show that Mazatec ethnobotany is not reducible to mushrooms alone.
The shared feature is a disciplined ceremonial use of plants and fungi to seek knowledge, healing, and communication.
## Twentieth-Century Encounter
In the 1930s, researchers such as Robert J. Weitlaner and Richard Evans Schultes documented continuing mushroom use in southern Mexico.
The major public turning point came when R. Gordon Wasson visited Huautla de Jiménez in the 1950s.
Wasson's reports, and especially his 1957 Life magazine article, introduced a mass English-speaking audience to Mazatec mushroom ceremonies.
