## Take-All Disease

## Description

Take-all is a serious root and crown rot disease of turfgrass and cereal crops caused by fungi in the genus *[[gaeumannomyces]]*. It is one of the most destructive diseases of wheat worldwide and a significant turfgrass problem. In [[the-soil-food-web]] framework, take-all can be suppressed by fungally dominated [[compost]] teas that establish competitive fungal communities in the root zone, preventing *Gaeumannomyces* from colonizing roots.

## Classification

- **Causal organisms:** *Gaeumannomyces* spp.
- **Primary pathogen:** *G. graminis* var. *tritici* (wheat), *G. graminis* var. *avenae* (oats/turf)
- **Type:** Root and crown rot disease
- **Host plants:** Turfgrasses (especially creeping bentgrass), wheat, barley, oats, other cereals
- **Symptoms:** Yellowing, thinning, root rot, darkened crown tissues, patchy dieback

## Key Facts

- Caused by *Gaeumannomyces* species — root-infecting ascomycete fungi
- One of the most important diseases of wheat globally and a major turfgrass pathogen
- Attacks roots and crown (lower stem) tissues, severing the plant"s connection to water and nutrients
- Characterized by darkened, rotted roots and yellowing, stunted aboveground growth
- Suppressed by fungally dominated compost teas through competitive exclusion in the root zone
- Competitive fungi occupy root surfaces and rhizosphere niches that *Gaeumannomyces* would colonize
- Disease severity increases in:
  - Alkaline soils (high pH)
  - Nutrient-poor soils
  - Soils with low manganese availability
  - Recently tilled or disturbed sites
- "Take-all decline" phenomenon: after several years of severe disease, antagonistic microbes build up and naturally suppress the pathogen — compost tea accelerates this process

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Take-all disease demonstrates several important mycological and [[soil-food-web]] principles:

- **Root pathogen ecology:** *Gaeumannomyces* is a root-infecting fungus — suppression requires establishing competitive biology at the root surface, not on leaves
- **Fungal competition:** Fungal teas introduce saprophytic and [[mycorrhizal-fungi]] that compete with *Gaeumannomyces* for root colonization sites
- **Take-all decline:** Natural biological suppression develops over time as antagonistic organisms accumulate — compost tea shortcuts this process by introducing competitors immediately
- **Manganese connection:** Take-all severity is linked to manganese availability; soil biology influences manganese cycling and availability
- **Integrated approach:** Combining fungal compost tea with pH management, proper drainage, and organic amendments provides the most effective control
