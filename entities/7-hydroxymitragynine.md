## Chemical Identity

- **IUPAC Name**: (4E,6S,12bS)-6-ethyl-7-hydroxy-3,4,6,7,8,12b-hexahydro-2H-1,6-methanoindolo[2,3-a]quinolizin-9-carboxylic acid methyl ester
- **CAS Number**: 1390252-55-5
- **Molecular Formula**: C₂₃H₃₀N₂O₅
- **Molecular Weight**: 414.52 g/mol
- **Structure**: 7-Hydroxymitragynine is an oxidized derivative of mitragynine, differing by the addition of a hydroxyl group at the C7 position of the indole core. This single structural modification significantly enhances its affinity for opioid receptors compared to the parent compound. Like mitragynine, it features a complex pentacyclic skeleton incorporating a corynanthe-type indole framework with a methoxy-carbonyl substitution at C16 and an ethyl side chain at C6. The C7 hydroxyl group introduces an additional hydrogen bond donor that is critical for high-affinity binding to the mu-opioid receptor ligand pocket.
- **Solubility**: Lipophilic; soluble in organic solvents (methanol, ethanol, DMSO). Poor water solubility.

## Pharmacology

7-Hydroxymitragynine acts primarily as a **mu-opioid receptor (MOR) partial agonist** with remarkably high binding affinity. It shows selectivity for MOR over other opioid receptor subtypes, although it maintains measurable affinity at the delta-opioid receptor.

- **MOR Ki**: ~5.1 nM (compared to mitragynine at ~234 nM morphine hine at ~20 nM)
- **Delta-opioid receptor (DOR) Ki**: ~51 nM
- **Kappa-opioid receptor (KOR) Ki**: >10,000 nM (negligible affinity)

7-OH is approximately **46 times more potent than mitragynine** and **5–10 times more potent than morphine** in producing analgesic effects in rodent models (tail-flick and hot-plate assays). Despite its high potency, it retains partial agonist activity at the MOR, meaning its maximal efficacy is lower than that of full agonists such as fentanyl or heroin.

In functional assays, 7-OH achieves approximately 60–70% of the maximal response produced by the benchmark full agonist DAMGO. This partial efficacy ceiling has important implications for both therapeutic utility and safety.

## Mechanism of Action

7-Hydroxymitragynine exhibits **G-protein biased signaling** at the mu-opioid receptor. This means it preferentially activates Gᵢ/o-protein-mediated [[blesching-cannabis-safety-adverse-effects-evidence]] of classical opioids including respiratory depression, constipation, and tolerance development.

This biased signaling profile is of considerable therapeutic interest, as it may explain why kratom-derived alkaloids produce analgesia with a comparatively lower incidence of life-threatening respiratory depression compared to conventional opioid drugs.

The G-protein bias of 7-OH has been quantified in β-arrestin recruitment assays, where it shows substantially lower efficacy than morphine or fentanyl at driving arrestin-mediated internalization of the MOR.

However, the degree of bias and its clinical relevance in humans remain under active investigation. Some researchers caution that biased signaling in vitro does not always translate to improved safety profiles in vivo, and that 7-OH's bias ratio may be insufficient to prevent respiratory depression entirely at high doses.

## Occurrence in Nature

7-Hydroxymitragynine is a **trace constituent** of the raw kratom leaf, typically comprising only **0.01–0.02%** of the total alkaloid content. By comparison, mitragynine accounts for approximately 66% of total alkaloids, with paynan  iogynine, and other minor alkaloids making up the remainder. Despite its minimal presence in the plant material, 7-OH is the **primary active metabolite** formed in the body after kratom ingestion.

Hepatic **CYP3A4** enzymes hydroxylate mitragynine at the C7 position to produce 7-OH, making it a major contributor to the overall pharmacological effect of kratom consumption even though it is barely present in the raw leaf. The ratio of 7-OH to mitragynine in vivo varies significantly between individuals depending on metabolic enzyme activity, liver function, and concurrent use of CYP3A4 inhibitors or inducers.

## Pharmacokinetics

- **Oral Bioavailability**: Limited data in humans; animal studies suggest moderate oral bioavailability with significant first-pass metabolism. Sublingual and insufflated routes may bypass some first-pass loss.
- **Half-life**: Estimated at 1–3 hours based on rodent studies; human pharmacokinetic data are sparse and confounded by the fact that 7-OH is simultaneously produced and eliminated.
- **Onset of Action**: Rapid onset due to efficient blood-brain barrier penetration, contributing to the relatively fast-acting nature of kratom's analgesic effects.
- **Metabolic Pathways**: Primarily formed via CYP3A4-mediated hydroxylation of mitragynine. 7-OH itself undergoes further phase I (oxidation, demethylation) an [[glucuronidation]] curonidation) metabolism before elimination.
- **Distribution**: Lipophilic nature (log P estimated ~3.5) permits efficient crossing of the blood-brain barrier, consistent with its central opioid effects.
- **Excretion**: Primarily via renal elimination of glucuronide conjugates, with a small fraction excreted unchanged in feces.

## Therapeutic Potential

7-Hydroxymitragynine has been investigated for several potential [[blesching-cannabis-therapeutic-window-dosing]]**, with the effective analgesic dose being close to doses that produce [[adverse-effects-and-harm-reduction-psilocybin]]. This narrow margin limits its direct therapeutic viability compared to mitragynine, which has a wider safety profile at typical kratom consumption levels. Synthetic derivatives based on the 7-OH scaffold have been explored as potential drug candidates, including the pseudoindoxyl derivatives developed by Váradi and colleagues, which show improved bias profiles and wider therapeutic windows.

## Safety and Toxicology

- **Respiratory Depression**: 7-OH can produce respiratory depression at high doses, though studies suggest it is **less severe** than that caused by classical full MOR agonists such as morphine or fentanyl, consistent with its partial agonist and G-protein biased profile. However, the safety margin is narrower than that of mitragynine.
- **Dependence Potential**: Chronic administration in animal models produces physical dependence and withdrawal symptoms, indicating meaningful addiction liability. Withdrawal severity appears dose-dependent and comparable to mild-to-moderate opioid withdrawal. Tolerance develops with repeated dosing over a period of days to weeks.
- **Toxicity**: LD₅₀ values in rodents suggest moderate toxicity. Overdose risk in humans is elevated compared to mitragynine due to the higher potency of 7-OH.
- **Adulteration Concerns**: Some commercial kratom products have been found adulterated with synthetic 7-OH, significantly increasing overdose risk. This is a growing public health concern as synthetic 7-OH is more potent than anything naturally occurring in the plant.
- **Seizure Risk**: High doses have been associated with proconvulsant activity in animal models, though the mechanism is not fully understood.

## Legal Status

The legal status of 7-hydroxymitragynine varies significantly by jurisdiction:

- **United States**: Not currently federally scheduled, but the DEA has made multiple attempts to place kratom alkaloids including 7-OH on the Schedule I list. The Kratom Consumer Protection Act has been adopted in numerous states, regulating product labeling and adulteration. Several states have enacted independent bans on kratom and its constituents.
- **Thailand**: Kratom was decriminalized in 2021, but synthetic 7-OH and enriched extracts remain controlled under Thailand's narcotics law.
