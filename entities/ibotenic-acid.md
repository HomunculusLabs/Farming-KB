# Ibotenic Acid

Ibotenic acid (pantherin, agarin) is the primary psychoactive and toxic
compound found in fresh specimens of **Amanita muscaria** (fly agaric) and
related Amanita species including *A. pantherina* and *A. gemmata*. It is a
conformationally restricted derivative of glutamic acid that acts as a potent
agonist at ionotropic glutamate receptors (iGluRs) in the central nervous
system.

## Chemical Identity

- **IUPAC name**: α-amino-3-hydroxy-5-isoxazoleacetic acid
- **Molecular formula**: C₅H₆N₂O₄
- **Molecular weight**: 158.11 g/mol
- **Melting point**: 150–152 °C (decomposition)
- **Appearance**: Colorless crystalline solid
- **Solubility**: Readily soluble in cold water; very water-soluble

Ibotenic acid belongs to the isoxazole class of compounds, featuring a
five-membered isoxazole ring bearing an amino group and a carboxylic acid
side chain that closely mimics the excitatory neurotransmitter glutamate.

## Biosynthesis and Natural Occurrence

In fresh Amanita muscaria fruiting bodies, ibotenic acid is the dominant active
compound while **muscimol** is present only in trace amounts. The two
compounds are interconverted through a simple decarboxylation reaction: upon
dehydration or heating, ibotenic acid quantitatively yields muscimol.

When specimens are dried — particularly through sun-drying or gentle heating —
ibotenic acid undergoes decarboxylation to produce muscimol, which is 4 to 10
times more pharmacologically active. UV light exposure converts ibotenic acid
into muscazone, a less potent variant that nonetheless exceeds ibotenic acid's
activity. This transformation explains why dried Amanita specimens are
significantly more potent than fresh ones.

## Pharmacology

Ibotenic acid is a **conformationally restricted derivative of glutamic acid**,
the major excitatory neurotransmitter in the mammalian CNS. It acts primarily
as an agonist at **AMPA receptors** (a subtype of ionotropic glutamate
receptor) and at metabotropic glutamate receptors. The synthetic compound
AMPA was designed as an analogue of ibotenic acid and proved more effective
and specific than the parent compound (Watkins, Krogsgaard-Larsen & Honore
1990).

Glutamate receptors are implicated in epilepsy, Huntington's disease, learning,
and memory. Ibotenic acid has served as an important research tool for studying
excitatory neurotransmission and is used to create animal models of
neurodegenerative diseases.

## Relationship to Muscimol and GABA

While ibotenic acid acts on glutamate (excitatory) receptors, its
decarboxylation product muscimol acts on **GABA receptors** — the primary
inhibitory neurotransmitter system. Muscimol is a potent bioisostere that
tightly binds the gamma-aminobutyrate receptor, inhibits neuronal and glial
GABA uptake, and serves as a substrate for GABA transaminase. The dual
glutamatergic/GABAergic pharmacology of this pair makes Amanita species unique
among psychoactive mushrooms, which typically contain tryptamine-based
compounds acting on serotonin receptors.

## Historical Significance

Ibotenic acid-containing Amanita species have a deep history across Northern
Europe and Asia. Indigenous Siberian and Arctic peoples reportedly consumed
dried Amanita specimens for psychoactive effects in shamanic rituals. In fresh
form, ibotenic acid causes primarily pupil dilation with minimal mental effects.
The more profound visionary experiences result from the conversion to muscimol
through drying — a process likely discovered empirically by ancient peoples.

## Toxicity and Effects

Effects of fresh mushroom ingestion (ibotenic acid predominant):
- Dilated pupils (mydriasis)
- Mild nausea and gastrointestinal discomfort
- Dizziness and ataxia

At high doses, ibotenic acid can cause excitotoxicity through overactivation of
glutamate receptors, potentially leading to neuronal damage. The compound is
classified as a neurotoxin in many jurisdictions.

After drying (muscimol predominant): sedation, visual distortions, color
enhancement, auditory phenomena, altered perception of space, and a sense of
inner peace or dissociation.

## Medicinal and Research Applications

1. **Epilepsy research**: Both ibotenic acid (glutamate receptors) and muscimol
   (GABA receptors) show beneficial effects on seizure models. The drug
   tiagabine (Gabitril®) was developed from bioisostere research on muscimol.
2. **Neurological disease models**: Ibotenic acid lesions create animal models
   of Alzheimer's and Huntington's diseases.
3. **Receptor pharmacology**: A standard tool for studying ionotropic glutamate
   receptor subtypes.

## Stability

Ibotenic acid is relatively unstable. It spontaneously decarboxylates to
muscimol upon heating, converts to muscazone under UV light, is very
water-soluble, and can be reconverted from muscimol in carbonated water.

## Legal Status

In Japan, fungi containing ibotenic acid and muscimol have been controlled
under the Narcotics and Psychotropic Control Law since June 6, 2002.
