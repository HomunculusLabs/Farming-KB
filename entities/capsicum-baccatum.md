## Capsicum baccatum

## Overview

**Capsicum baccatum** is a species of chili pepper native to [[capsicum-annuum]]* in South [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-central-america-caribbean]], Africa, and [[query-how-to-grow-rare-tropical-fruits-in-marginal-and-subtropical-climates]] with well-drained soils and moderate rainfall.

## Culinary Use and Varieties

*C. baccatum* is the dominant pepper species in traditional [[chiric-caspi-brunfelsia-south-american-hallucinogen]] cuisine, particularly in Peru, Bolivia, and Ecuador.

**Ají amarillo** is the most commercially important cultivar, forming the flavor base of Peruvian cuisine including dishes like ají de gallina and causa limeña.

Ají amarillo is prized for its unique fruity, citrus-like flavor profile with moderate heat (30,000–50,000 SHU).

**Ají limo** is a smaller, extremely pungent cultivar (80,000–100,000 SHU) widely used in Peruvian ceviche preparations.

**Ají panca** is a dried, mild, deep-red pepper with smoky, berry-like notes, essential in Peruvian stews and adobos.

**Bishop's crown** (or Christmas bell) is an ornamental cultivar with a distinctive three-lobed, flattened fruit shape.

The species has gained popularity in international markets for its complex flavor, which differs markedly from the grassy, earthy notes of *C. annuum*.

Several cultivars are pickled whole for use as condiments, particularly in Italian and Mediterranean cuisine.
