# Carpobrotus spp. (Pigface, Karkalla)

## Description

Carpobrotus is a genus of succulent creeping ground-cover plants native to southern Australia. The leaves are thick and fleshy, triangular in cross section, and often reddish. The flowers are purple and the fruits are purplish-red. They are common creepers of sand dunes, beaches, and headlands. Carpobrotus rossii and Carpobrotus modestus extend well inland from the coast.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Family**: Aizoaceae
- **Genus**: Carpobrotus
- **Common names**: Pigface, karkalla, beach bananas, sea fig
- **Type**: Succulent creeping perennial ground cover

## Key Facts

- Growth habit: Prostrate, creeping succulent
- Leaf morphology: Thick, fleshy, triangular in cross section, often reddish
- Flowers: Purple; fruits: Purplish-red
- Habitat: Sand dunes, beaches, headlands; some species extend inland
- Notable species: C. rossii, C. modestus (inland), C. edulis (South African)
- Native range: Southern Australia; related species in South Africa

## Uses

### Culinary

- Fruit described as one of Australia's tastiest wild fruits, tasting like salty apples
- Leaves eaten cooked by Aboriginal Australians
- Dried fruit also consumed

### Medicinal

- Mucilaginous leaf juice used to treat midge bites in Queensland
- Effective against Portuguese man-of-war and blue bottle jellyfish stings
- Related South African species contains mesembrine (weaker than cocaine in action)
- Juice traditionally used to soothe burns and scalds

### Environmental

- Excellent sand dune stabiliser
- Ground cover for coastal [[comparison-vetiver-grass-vs-bamboo-for-erosion-control]]
- Habitat restoration in coastal zones

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

Carpobrotus is a multifunctional coastal plant providing food, medicine, and soil stabilisation. Its tolerance of salt spray, drought, and poor sandy soils makes it ideal for coastal food forests, dune restoration, and saline sites. Requires virtually no maintenance once established.

## Cultivation Notes

- Extremely drought and salt tolerant
- Propagates easily from stem cuttings
- Prefers full sun and well-drained sandy soil
- Can become invasive outside native range (particularly C. edulis)
- Fruit ripens in summer to autumn
