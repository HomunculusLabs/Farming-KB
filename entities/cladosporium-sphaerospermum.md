## Description

**Cladosporium sphaerospermum** is a melanized dematiaceous (dark-pigmented) fungus belonging to the Ascomycota phylum. It is a ubiquitous cosmopolitan mold found in soil, air, and on plant surfaces. It has gained significant scientific attention for its remarkable ability to not only survive but actively grow toward sources of ionizing radiation — a behavior termed **radiotropism**.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Dothideomycetes
- **Order:** Capnodiales
- **Family:** Cladosporiaceae
- **Genus:** Cladosporium
- **Species:** C. sphaerospermum

## Key Facts

- **Dominant species at Chernobyl:** Isolated from the walls of the Chernobyl nuclear reactor room at frequencies of 73.7% at low radiation (1.5–25 mR/h) and 99.9% at high radiation (40–220 mR/h), making it the most frequently isolated microfungus in the reactor room (Zhdanova et al., 2000).
- **Radiotropism:** Strains isolated from high-radiation zones actively direct their growth toward collimated sources of radioactivity in culture, a behavior not seen in conspecific isolates from low-radiation environments (Zhdanova, 1994; Vember et al., 1999).
- **Hot particle degradation:** Demonstrated the ability to overgrow and decompose carbon-based radioactive debris ("hot particles") from the Chernobyl reactor with activities below 1147 Bq, destroying them within 50–150 days (Zhdanova et al., 1991).
- **Melanin-mediated radiation resistance:** Contains melanin pigment in its cell walls, which is believed to provide protection against ionizing radiation damage in a manner analogous to UV protection seen in melanized lichen fungi.
- **Bioindicator:** Used as a bioindicator species for characterizing high, middle, and low levels of radioactive contamination in woodland soil ecosystems.

## Ecological Role

C. sphaerospermum functions as a saprotroph in soil ecosystems, participating in the decomposition of organic matter. Its melanized cell walls provide resilience against environmental stressors including UV radiation and ionizing radiation. In radioactively contaminated environments, it plays a unique role in the decomposition of radioactive carbon-based debris.

## Relevance to Mycology and Cultivation

- **Bioremediation potential:** Its ability to colonize and degrade radioactive particles makes it a candidate organism for [[bioremediation-strategies]] in radioactively contaminated environments.
- **Radiation ecology:** Serves as a model organism for studying fungal adaptations to extreme radiation environments and the mechanisms of radiotropism.
- **Community dynamics:** Its dominance in high-radiation fungal communities demonstrates how radiation stress simplifies fungal community structure while favoring melanized species.

## Distinguishing Features

- Spherical conidia (spores), distinguishing it from other Cladosporium species
- Dark olive-green to brown colonies on culture media
- Melanized hyphal cell walls providing characteristic dark pigmentation
- Exceptionally high frequency of isolation from radioactive environments compared to other microfungi
