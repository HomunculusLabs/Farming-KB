# Allylescaline

## Overview

Allylescaline, also known as 3,5-dimethoxy-4-allyloxyphenethylamine or 4-allyloxy-3,5-dimethoxyphenethylamine, is a synthetic psychedelic compound of the phenethylamine class. It is an analogue of mescaline where the 4-methoxy group is replaced by an allyloxy substituent.

The compound is one of the lesser-known members of the phenylisopropylamine family described by [[alexander-shulgin]] in PIHKAL (Phenethylamines I Have Known and Loved). It is designated as entry number 112 in Shulgin's numbering system. Allylescaline is not widely used outside of research and psychonautic communities.

## Chemical Properties

Allylescaline has the molecular formula C13H19NO3 and a molecular weight of 237.29 g/mol. Its IUPAC name is 2-(4-allyloxy-3,5-dimethoxyphenyl)ethan-1-amine. It is structurally related to mescaline (3,4,5-trimethoxyphenethylamine) with an allyloxy group replacing the 4-position methoxy.

The allyl (CH2=CH-CH2-O-) side chain at the 4-position distinguishes allylescaline from other mescaline analogues. This modification increases lipophilicity compared to mescaline, which may influence blood-brain barrier penetration and [[leary-psychedelic-experience-bardo-mapping]] with less visual intensity than mescaline at equivalent doses. He noted a relatively clear headspace with minimal confusion or disorientation, which distinguishes it from more potent phenethylamines such as 2C-B.

## Synthesis

Allylescaline can be synthesized from 3,5-dimethoxy-4-hydroxybenzaldehyde (syringaldehyde) through a sequence of allylation, nitroolefin formation via Henry reaction, and subsequent reduction of the nitro group to the primary amine.

The allylation step typically uses allyl bromide with a base such as potassium carbonate in a polar aprotic solvent. The nitrostyrene intermediate is then reduced using lithium aluminum hydride or catalytic hydrogenation to yield the final phenethylamine product.

This synthetic route is analogous to the preparation of other 4-substituted mescaline analogues. Purity and characterization are confirmed through gas chromatography-mass spectrometry, nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy, and melting point determination of the hydrochloride salt.

## Legal Status

Allylescaline is unscheduled in most countries, though its structural similarity to scheduled phenethylamines may subject it to analogue acts in some jurisdictions. Several European countries have scheduled specific phenethylamines, though allylescaline is not explicitly listed in most national scheduling tables.

The compound is not approved for human consumption and is available only through specialty chemical suppliers for research purposes. Its legal status should be verified for any specific jurisdiction before acquisition or handling.

## History

Allylescaline was first synthesized and characterized by Alexander and [[ann-shulgin]] during their systematic exploration of phenethylamine psychedelics. It appears in PIHKAL (1991) with Shulgin's qualitative commentary on its effects.

The compound represents one of many mescaline analogues investigated by the Shulgins in their effort to understand structure-activity relationships within the phenethylamine class. The allyloxy substitution was chosen to explore how unsaturated alkoxy groups at the 4-position influenced psychoactivity compared to the simpler methoxy group.

Since its publication in PIHKAL, allylescaline has received minimal scientific attention. There are no published [[mushroom-cancer-clinical-trials-overview]] or formal pharmacological studies beyond Shulgin's initial reports. It remains a research chemical of primarily historical and pharmacological interest.

The compound's rarity in both research and recreational contexts reflects a broader pattern among PIHKAL phenethylamines: only a small subset achieved widespread use, while the majority remained obscure chemicals known only to dedicated researchers and enthusiasts.
