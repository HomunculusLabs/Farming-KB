# Allylescaline

## Overview

Allylescaline, also known as 3,5-dimethoxy-4-allyloxyphenethylamine or 4-allyloxy-3,5-dimethoxyphenethylamine, is a synthetic psychedelic compound of the phenethylamine class. It is an analogue of mescaline where the 4-methoxy group is replaced by an allyloxy substituent.

The compound is one of the lesser-known members of the phenylisopropylamine family described by Alexander Shulgin in PIHKAL (Phenethylamines I Have Known and Loved). It is designated as entry number 112 in Shulgin's numbering system. Allylescaline is not widely used outside of research and psychonautic communities.

## Chemical Properties

Allylescaline has the molecular formula C13H19NO3 and a molecular weight of 237.29 g/mol. Its IUPAC name is 2-(4-allyloxy-3,5-dimethoxyphenyl)ethan-1-amine. It is structurally related to mescaline (3,4,5-trimethoxyphenethylamine) with an allyloxy group replacing the 4-position methoxy.

The allyl (CH2=CH-CH2-O-) side chain at the 4-position distinguishes allylescaline from other mescaline analogues. This modification increases lipophilicity compared to mescaline, which may influence blood-brain barrier penetration and receptor binding affinity.

The allyl group introduces an unsaturated carbon-carbon double bond not present in the saturated propoxy chain of proscaline. This structural feature may affect metabolic stability, as the double bond is a potential site for epoxidation by hepatic cytochrome P450 enzymes.

The compound exists as a freebase oil or crystalline hydrochloride salt. The hydrochloride form is stable at room temperature when stored in a cool, dry environment away from light. Shulgin reported the hydrochloride salt as a white crystalline solid with a characteristic melting point.

## Pharmacology

Allylescaline acts primarily as a serotonin 5-HT2A receptor agonist, the mechanism shared by most classical psychedelic phenethylamines. Affinity for the 5-HT2A receptor is believed to be responsible for its psychoactive effects, though full receptor binding profiles have not been published.

Like mescaline and related phenethylamines, allylescaline likely exhibits partial agonist activity at 5-HT2C and may interact with trace amine-associated receptors. The allyloxy substitution at the 4-position may slightly alter receptor selectivity compared to the parent mescaline molecule.

The compound is metabolized by monoamine oxidase (MAO) enzymes, primarily MAO-A, which deaminates the primary amine. The allyl ether linkage may undergo oxidative metabolism by cytochrome P450 enzymes, potentially producing active or inactive metabolites.

The N-dealkylated metabolite is unlikely to be psychoactive at physiological concentrations. However, oxidative cleavage of the allyl ether could theoretically produce phenolic metabolites with altered receptor binding profiles. Detailed pharmacokinetic studies have not been conducted.

Concurrent use of MAO inhibitors would be expected to significantly increase blood levels and prolong the duration of effects, similar to the well-documented MAOI interactions with other phenethylamine psychedelics.

## Effects

Shulgin reported the active dose range of allylescaline as 20 to 40 milligrams. Effects are described as mescaline-like but generally milder and shorter in duration. Total duration is approximately 8 to 12 hours, consistent with other phenethylamine psychedelics.

The onset occurs within 30 to 60 minutes following oral administration. Peak effects are reached at 2 to 3 hours, with a gradual decline over the subsequent 6 to 9 hours. The experience is characterized by open and closed-eye visuals, enhanced color perception, and altered thought patterns.

Common effects include geometric visual patterning, enhanced appreciation of music and art, and a sense of emotional openness. Compared to mescaline, the visual component is often described as less intense while the cognitive and emotional effects are relatively more prominent.

Physical side effects may include mild nausea, pupil dilation, increased heart rate, and elevated body temperature. These effects are generally consistent with other phenethylamine psychedelics and tend to diminish as the experience progresses.

The nausea, sometimes referred to as "body load," is typically most pronounced during the onset phase and usually resolves within the first two hours. Administration with a light meal or antacids may reduce gastrointestinal discomfort in sensitive individuals.

Dose-dependent increases in blood pressure and heart rate have been reported for related phenethylamines. Individuals with cardiovascular conditions should exercise caution. There are no published reports of serious adverse events specifically attributed to allylescaline.

Shulgin's qualitative assessment in PIHKAL rates the compound as producing a mild to moderate psychedelic experience with less visual intensity than mescaline at equivalent doses. He noted a relatively clear headspace with minimal confusion or disorientation, which distinguishes it from more potent phenethylamines such as 2C-B.

## Synthesis

Allylescaline can be synthesized from 3,5-dimethoxy-4-hydroxybenzaldehyde (syringaldehyde) through a sequence of allylation, nitroolefin formation via Henry reaction, and subsequent reduction of the nitro group to the primary amine.

The allylation step typically uses allyl bromide with a base such as potassium carbonate in a polar aprotic solvent. The nitrostyrene intermediate is then reduced using lithium aluminum hydride or catalytic hydrogenation to yield the final phenethylamine product.

This synthetic route is analogous to the preparation of other 4-substituted mescaline analogues. Purity and characterization are confirmed through gas chromatography-mass spectrometry, nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy, and melting point determination of the hydrochloride salt.

## Legal Status

Allylescaline is unscheduled in most countries, though its structural similarity to scheduled phenethylamines may subject it to analogue acts in some jurisdictions. In the United States, the Federal Analogue Act potentially covers allylescaline as a controlled substance analogue of mescaline.

In the United Kingdom, the Psychoactive Substances Act 2016 may apply to allylescaline. Several European countries have scheduled specific phenethylamines, though allylescaline is not explicitly listed in most national scheduling tables.

The compound is not approved for human consumption and is available only through specialty chemical suppliers for research purposes. Its legal status should be verified for any specific jurisdiction before acquisition or handling.

## History

Allylescaline was first synthesized and characterized by Alexander and Ann Shulgin during their systematic exploration of phenethylamine psychedelics. It appears in PIHKAL (1991) with Shulgin's qualitative commentary on its effects.

The compound represents one of many mescaline analogues investigated by the Shulgins in their effort to understand structure-activity relationships within the phenethylamine class. The allyloxy substitution was chosen to explore how unsaturated alkoxy groups at the 4-position influenced psychoactivity compared to the simpler methoxy group.

Since its publication in PIHKAL, allylescaline has received minimal scientific attention. There are no published clinical trials or formal pharmacological studies beyond Shulgin's initial reports. It remains a research chemical of primarily historical and pharmacological interest.

The compound's rarity in both research and recreational contexts reflects a broader pattern among PIHKAL phenethylamines: only a small subset achieved widespread use, while the majority remained obscure chemicals known only to dedicated researchers and enthusiasts.

Contemporary interest in allylescaline is primarily pharmacological, as part of ongoing efforts to map structure-activity relationships within the phenethylamine class. Modern computational methods including molecular docking studies may eventually provide quantitative binding data that was unavailable during Shulgin's era.

## Related Compounds

Allylescaline belongs to the broader family of 4-substituted mescaline analogues. Related compounds include escaline (4-ethoxy-3,5-dimethoxyphenethylamine) and proscaline (4-propoxy-3,5-dimethoxyphenethylamine), which differ in the length of the 4-position alkoxy chain.

Mescaline itself (3,4,5-trimethoxyphenethylamine) is the parent compound. Other notable analogues include the 2C family (ring-substituted phenethylamines with a methoxy group at the 2-position), which share the 5-HT2A agonist mechanism but have different pharmacokinetic profiles.

Cross-tolerance between allylescaline and other serotonergic psychedelics (LSD, psilocybin, mescaline) develops rapidly and persists for approximately 3 to 7 days. This tolerance is mediated by 5-HT2A receptor downregulation and is consistent across the classical psychedelic class.
