## Overview

**Cyathea woollsiana** (F.Muell.) Domin is a tall tree fern in the family Cyatheaceae, endemic to the wet tropical rainforests of north-eastern Queensland, Australia. It is one of several Cyathea species that form part of the characteristic canopy-understory fern layer in the Australian Wet Tropics bioregion.

The species is named after William Woolls (1814–1893), a prominent Australian botanist and clergyman who made extensive collections of the New South Wales and Queensland floras.

It was first described by Ferdinand von Mueller and later transferred to Cyathea by Karel Domin in his 1929 revision of the Australian pteridophyte flora. The species is closely allied to Cyathea cunninghamii and several other Victorian/New South Wales tree ferns.

Cyathea woollsiana can be distinguished from the more common Cyathea cooperi by its darker stipe scales, more openly divided fronds, and the presence of a well-developed indusium around each sorus. Its trunk is also typically more slender than that of Cyathea cooperi at comparable heights.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:**
- Kingdom: Plantae
- Division: Polypodiophyta
- Class: Polypodiopsida
- Order: Cyatheales
- Family: Cyatheaceae
- Genus: *Cyathea* J.E.Sm.
- Species: *C. woollsiana* (F.Muell.) Domin

**Basionym:** *Alsophila woollsiana* F.Muell.

**Synonyms:** *Hemitelia woollsiana* (F.Muell.) F.Muell.

**Morphology:** The trunk is erect and slender, typically reaching 4–8 m in height and 8–12 cm in diameter. The crown bears a spreading rosette of large fronds up to 2.5–3.5 m long.

Fronds are bipinnate-pinnatifid to tripinnate at the base, with a dark brown to blackish stipe. The stipe is densely covered with dark scales that are narrow, lanceolate, and often bicolored with a darker central stripe and paler margins.

The rachis is dark brown to nearly black, contrasting with the green lamina. Stipe scales are an important diagnostic character for distinguishing Cyathea woollsiana from co-occurring congeners in the field.

Pinnae are arranged in a relatively open pattern, with pinnules that are sessile or short-stalked, dissected into oblong to lanceolate segments. The lamina surface is dull green above and paler beneath.

Sori are positioned on the veins of the lower pinnae surface. Each sorus is protected by a small, globose indusium that opens at maturity to release the spores. Spores are trilete, with a finely granulate perispore surface.

The crozier (fiddlehead) is densely scaly and covered in dark brown to blackish hairs. Unfolding fronds emerge in a distinctive crosier pattern typical of Cyatheaceae, with the stipe bases persisting on the upper trunk as a fibrous skirt.

Trunk cross-sections show a central vascular cylinder surrounded by a persistent mantle of adventitious roots. This root mantle provides structural support and water absorption capacity, a characteristic feature of tree ferns in the Cyatheaceae.

## Distribution and Habitat

Cyathea woollsiana is restricted to the Wet Tropics of north-eastern Queensland, Australia. Its range extends from the Daintree region in the north to the Paluma Range in the south, spanning approximately 350 km of coastal and near-coastal mountain ranges.

The species grows in tropical raingreen mesophyll vine forests and complex notophyll vine forests. It occupies sheltered creek gullies, moist ravines, and the mid-montane slopes of the coastal ranges.

Altitudinal range is approximately 200–1000 m above sea level. Populations are concentrated in areas receiving annual rainfall exceeding 2000 mm, often in cloud-forest transitions on the western escarpment of the ranges.

The species is generally found on basalt-derived soils and metamorphic substrates, avoiding the acidic granite-derived soils that characterize parts of the southern Wet Tropics. Soil moisture retention appears to be a critical factor in determining local abundance.
