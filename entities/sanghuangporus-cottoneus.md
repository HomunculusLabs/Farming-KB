## Overview

**Sanghuangporus cottoneus** is a perennial polyporoid fungus in the family
Hymenochaetaceae, one of several species collectively known as *sanghuang* in
traditional Chinese medicine.

It forms woody, hoof-shaped basidiocarps on the dead branches of broadleaf
trees, particularly species of *Quercus* and *Castanea*.

Like other *Sanghuangporus* species, it produces a diverse array of
triterpenoids and polysaccharides with reported immunomodulatory,
anti-inflammatory, and antitumor bioactivities.

The species was historically confused with *S. linteus* and *S. baumii* before
molecular phylogenetics clarified species boundaries within the genus.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:** Fungi → Basidiomycota → Agaricomycetes → Hymenochaetales →
Hymenochaetaceae → *Sanghuangporus cottoneus* (Lloyd) L.W. Zhou & Y.C. Dai

**Synonyms:** *Fomes cottoneus* Lloyd, *Phellinus cottoneus* (Lloyd) Imazeki,
*Inonotus cottoneus* (Lloyd) T. Wagner & M. Fisch.

The basidiocarp is perennial, sessile to effused-reflexed, and woody-corky in
consistency. The upper surface is dark brown to nearly black with concentric
zonation and occasional radial cracks.

The pore surface is cinnamon-brown to rust-colored, with circular to angular
pores numbering 5–7 per millimeter. The context is duplex, with a dark
cinnamon-brown upper layer and a lighter tan lower layer.

Basidiospores are broadly ellipsoid, hyaline, thick-walled, and measure
4.5–6.0 × 3.5–4.5 µm. The hyphal system is dimitic, with generative hyphae
bearing simple septa and skeletal hyphai dominant in the trama.

Setae are present in the hymenium, ventricose to subulate, and dark
brown-colored, measuring 20–40 × 5–8 µm. This is a key diagnostic feature
separating *Sanghuangporus* from morphologically similar genera.

## Distribution and Habitat

*S. cottoneus* is primarily distributed in subtropical and warm temperate
forests of East and Southeast Asia, with confirmed records from southern China,
Taiwan, Japan, and Korea.

It is a white-rot saprotroph found on dead or moribund branches and trunks of
broadleaf trees. Preferred hosts include oaks (*Quercus* spp.), chestnuts
(*Castanea* spp.), and occasionally other Fagaceae.

The species fruits predominantly in warm, humid conditions from late spring
through early autumn. Specimens are most commonly collected at elevations
between 200 and 1200 meters in mixed deciduous forests.

Habitat overlap with other *Sanghuangporus* species is common, and
morphological identification in the field is unreliable without microscopic
examination or DNA barcoding.

## Bioactive Compounds and Pharmacology

Chemical investigations of *S. cottoneus* have identified triterpenoids,
polysaccharides, and hispidin derivatives as the primary bioactive constituents.

Triterpenoids isolated from the fruiting body include inotodiol,
trametenolic acid, and several lanostane-type compounds with reported
antitumor activity against various cancer cell lines in vitro.

Beta-glucan polysaccharides from the mycelial culture exhibit significant
immunomodulatory effects, stimulating macrophage activation and enhancing
natural killer cell cytotoxicity in murine models.

Hispidin and its analogs show potent antioxidant and anti-inflammatory
activity through inhibition of NF-κB and MAPK signaling pathways.

Water extracts of *S. cottoneus* demonstrate hepatoprotective effects in
animal models of acetaminophen-induced liver injury, comparable to those
reported for *S. linteus* and *S. vaninii*.

Clinical evidence remains limited, as most pharmacological studies have been
conducted in vitro or in animal models. Formal human trials are needed to
validate traditional medicinal applications.

## Cultivation

[[fukuoka-matsutake-artificial-cultivation-attempt]] of *S. cottoneus* has been achieved using both
[[cancer-medicinal-mushrooms-submerged-fermentation-production]] and solid-state substrate methods, though commercial
production remains less developed than for *S. vaninii* or *S. linteus*.

Optimal mycelial growth occurs at 25–30°C on malt extract agar, with
colonization rates comparable to related Sanghuangporus species.

Solid-state cultivation typically employs a substrate blend of hardwood sawdust
(oak or beech) supplemented with wheat bran (10–20%) and calcium carbonate
(1–2%). Fruiting induction requires high humidity (85–95%) and moderate
lighting.

The [[stamets-biological-efficiency-yield]] for fruiting body production is generally lower than
for more commonly cultivated Sanghuangporus species, which has limited
commercial interest in this particular species.

Submerged fermentation is preferred for polysaccharide production, as it
offers faster biomass accumulation and easier extraction of bioactive
compounds from mycelial culture.

## Ecological Role

*S. cottoneus* functions as a primary decomposer of hardwood in forest
ecosystems, contributing to [[comparison-nutrient-cycling-vs-nutrient-dense-gardening]] through its white-rot decay
mechanism.

The species produces lignin-degrading enzymes including laccase,
[[fungal-manganese-peroxidase-remediation]], and lignin peroxidase, which break down lignin and
hemicellulose in dead wood.

As a component of the saproxylic fungal community, it competes with other
wood-decay fungi for substrate resources. Its perennial fruiting body
provides microhabitat for various invertebrates and small organisms.

The [[king-stropharia-ecological-interactions-permaculture]] of *S. cottoneus* with host trees and associated
fungal communities remain understudied compared to its medicinal chemistry.
