In field-collected ergot sclerotia, ergocristine typically constitutes
30–40% of the total ergopeptine fraction, making it the single most
abundant ergopeptine in many *C. purpurea* strains.

## Pharmacology

Ergocristine acts primarily through interactions with monoamine
receptors and adrenergic receptors:

- **5-HT2A receptor:** Moderate partial agonist activity; contributes
  to the mild vasoconstrictive and uterotonic properties shared by
  ergopeptines.
- **5-HT1A receptor:** Weak agonist activity.
- **α-adrenergic receptors:** Partial agonist activity at α1 and α2
  subtypes, producing vasoconstriction at higher doses.
- **D2 dopamine receptor:** Weak partial agonist; this pharmacological
  activity was the basis for developing semisynthetic derivatives
  (bromocriptine, cabergoline) with enhanced D2 selectivity.
- **Uterine smooth muscle:** Direct agonist action via 5-HT2A and
  α-adrenergic receptors, producing sustained uterine contractions.

**⚠️ Toxicity:** Like all ergopeptines, ergocristine causes ergotism
(St. Anthony's Fire) at excessive doses — symptoms include severe
vasoconstriction, gangrene, hallucinations, and convulsions.
