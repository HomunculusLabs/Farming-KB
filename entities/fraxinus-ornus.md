*Fraxinus ornus* is native to southern and central Europe, from Italy and the Balkan Peninsula eastward to Turkey, Lebanon, and the Caucasus. It also occurs in southern France and Switzerland.

The species is characteristic of thermophilous deciduous forests and scrubland. It grows on calcareous, well-drained soils in warm, sunny positions at elevations from sea level to 1,200 metres.

It is a component of sub-Mediterranean oak-hornbeam forests and is particularly abundant in southern Italy, Sicily, and the southern Balkans. In these regions it can form nearly pure stands on south-facing slopes.

## Bioactive Compounds and Traditional Uses

Manna is the dried sap exuded from incisions made in the bark of *F. ornus* during summer. The principal constituent is mannitol (40–60%), a sugar alcohol with mild osmotic laxative properties.

Manna harvesting is a traditional practice in the Madonie Mountains of Sicily and in the Pollino region of Calabria. The product is collected in granular or flake form and classified by quality.

Manna was used in European medicine from at least the first century CE, when Dioscorides described it in *De [[ingham-blackstrap-molasses-compost-tea-concentration-osmotic-effects]].

Manna was also used as a sweetener before the widespread availability of cane sugar. In southern Italian cuisine, manna was dissolved in water as a refreshing summer drink or added to desserts.
