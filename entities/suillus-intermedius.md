## Suillus intermedius

## Description

Suillus intermedius is an ectomycorrhizal basidiomycete fungus belonging to the suilloid clade within the Boletales. Like other members of the genus Suillus, it forms obligate mutualistic associations with coniferous trees, particularly pines (Pinus spp.). The species is notable for its ecological interactions with plant-derived phenolic compounds and its role in nutrient cycling within coniferous forest ecosystems.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Boletales
- **Family:** Suillaceae
- **Genus:** Suillus
- **Species:** S. intermedius

The genus Suillus is part of the suilloid clade, a group of [[ectomycorrhizal-fungi]] that show strong host specificity toward members of the Pinaceae. Molecular phylogenetic studies have refined species boundaries within this group, though S. intermedius remains a recognized species in the ecological literature.

## Morphology

- **Cap:** Convex to plane, viscid when fresh, yellowish-brown to cinnamon
- **Pores:** Yellowish, angular, staining brownish when bruised
- **Stem:** Cylindrical, with glandular dots near the apex, pale yellow to brownish
- **Flesh:** Yellowish, soft, sometimes slightly bluing on exposure
- **Spores:** Fusoid to elliptical, smooth, pale brown in mass
- **Mycorrhizae:** Forms typical ectomycorrhizal mantles with a well-developed [[hartig-net]] on fine roots of host trees

## Ecology and Habitat

S. intermedius is found in coniferous forest ecosystems, where it associates primarily with pine species. Its [[ecological-niche]] includes:

- Pine-dominated forests and woodlands
- Acidic, sandy, or well-drained soils
- Forest floor environments with accumulations of coniferous leaf litter
- [[fungal-mycelial-networks-nutrient-translocation]] extending through organic and mineral soil horizons

As a member of the suilloid ectomycorrhizal community, S. intermedius plays a critical role in mobilizing phosphorus and nitrogen from organic matter and transferring these nutrients to host trees.

## Key Facts

- **Phenolic stimulation:** Koide et al. (1998) demonstrated that the polyphenols catechin and epicatechin gallate, found in pine leaf litter water extracts, stimulate the growth of S. intermedius in culture. This is significant because the same compounds inhibit the growth of other ectomycorrhizal fungi such as [[amanita-rubescens]].
- **Litter chemistry interactions:** The differential response of S. intermedius to leaf litter phenolics compared to co-occurring fungi suggests that litter chemistry plays a major role in structuring ectomycorrhizal communities, with S. intermedius being favored in pine litter-rich microenvironments.
- **Volatile compound effects:** Volatile terpenes such as α-pinene and β-pinene, common constituents of coniferous leaf litter, have differential effects on a range of ectomycorrhizal fungi, including S. intermedius.
- **Nutrient acquisition:** Like other Suillus species, S. intermedius produces extracellular enzymes (phosphatases, proteases) that enable it to access organic nutrient pools in forest soils.
- **Suilloid dominance:** Suilloid species, including S. intermedius, have been observed to dominate ectomycorrhizal communities on defoliated trees, suggesting tolerance to reduced carbon allocation from hosts.

## Interactions with Other Organisms

- **Host trees:** Primarily associates with Pinus species, consistent with the strong host specificity characteristic of the suilloid clade
- **Competitive dynamics:** Stimulated by pine litter phenolics (catechin, epicatechin gallate), giving it a competitive advantage over Amanita rubescens in pine-dominated environments
- **Saprotroph interactions:** Competes with saprotrophic fungi for organic nutrient resources in the forest floor
- **Bacterial associates:** May interact with mycorrhiza helper bacteria (MHB) that facilitate root colonization

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

S. intermedius contributes to several areas of mycological research and practice:

- **Chemical ecology:** Its contrasting responses to phenolic compounds compared to other ectomycorrhizal species make it a model for studying how plant secondary metabolites structure fungal communities
- **Forestry inoculation:** Suillus species are important in pine seedling inoculation programs, and S. intermedius may contribute to successful seedling establishment in reforestation
- **Community dynamics:** Understanding how S. intermedius responds to environmental cues (litter chemistry, host carbon supply) helps predict mycorrhizal community shifts under environmental change
- **Nutrient cycling:** As an active decomposer of organic matter, S. intermedius plays a role in phosphorus and nitrogen mineralization in coniferous forest soils
