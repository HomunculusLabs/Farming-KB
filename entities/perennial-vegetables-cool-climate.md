## Description

[[green-manure]] crop (oats, tares, fava beans) sown before winter, slashed or dug-in during spring

## Techniques for Perennialising Annuals

### Carrots
Cut a disc off the top of a mature carrot, sprout it on soft wet paper in a shallow tray, and replant. Mollison reports one gardener eating from the same carrots for 9 years using this method.

### Leeks
Let one in four plants go to seed and dry off, then remove small bulbils from the base and replant. When harvesting, cut the leek 2 cm below ground level — the central shoot quickly regrows. A perennial bunching leek variety known as "pearl onions" also exists.

### Lettuce
Pick single leaves rather than harvesting the whole head, allow plants to go to seed, and scatter seed. Mignonette variety is noted as a good self-seeder, along with some Italian varieties.

### Cabbage
Cut the head 8 cm above the ground, then deeply crosscut the remaining stem. Each quarter of the stem regrows a small cabbage.

### Self-Seeding Species
Garden fennel, chard, parsley, and parsnip will self-seed if allowed to go to seed, requiring only judicious thinning, weeding, and transplanting.

## Key Perennial and Over-Winter Crops

- **Fava bean** — field crop left to over-winter
- **Kale** — hardy, persists through winter
- **Chard (silver beet)** — important all-year food; multiple successions sown to reseed at irregular intervals
- **Parsley** — important all-year food, self-seeds readily
- **Cabbage, broccoli, cauliflower, sprouts** — grown under straw or shelters for winter harvest
