# Baccharis

## Description

*Baccharis* is a large genus of flowering plants in the family Asteraceae, comprising approximately 400 species of shrubs and small trees native primarily to the Americas. Several *Baccharis* species are notable for their production of macrocyclic trichothecene mycotoxins — powerful secondary metabolites that are highly toxic to mammals, particularly cattle. Jarvis et al. (1987) demonstrated that *Baccharis* species produce high concentrations of these trichothecenes, which were later recognized as being synthesized by [[fungal-endophytes]] associated with the plant rather than by the plant itself. The *Baccharis*–endophyte system is a classic example of how fungal symbionts can render otherwise palatable plants toxic to herbivores.

## Key Facts

- **Toxic trichothecenes**: *Baccharis megapotamica* (Rio Grande do Sul in Brazil) produces baccharinoids — macrocyclic trichothecenes that are among the most potent naturally occurring toxins. These compounds were initially attributed to the plant but are now known to be produced by [[endophytic-fungi]], likely belonging to the genus *Myrothecium* or related taxa.
- **Cattle toxicity**: Cattle grazing on *Baccharis* species can suffer acute poisoning characterized by gastrointestinal hemorrhage, dehydration, and death. The trichothecene compounds inhibit protein synthesis in eukaryotic cells.
- **Endophyte origin**: The production of macrocyclic trichothecenes in *Baccharis* demonstrates the broader principle that many plant-derived toxins are actually of fungal origin, produced by endophytic symbionts as part of a defensive mutualism.
- **Species diversity**: Notable species include *B. halimifolia* (groundsel bush, eastern U.S. coastal plain), *B. pilularis* (coyote brush, California chaparral), and *B. genistelloides* (Carqueja, used in traditional medicine).
- **Weed status**: Several *Baccharis* species are aggressive colonizers of disturbed sites and are considered invasive weeds in parts of Australia and the United States.

## Ecological Role

- Produces secondary metabolites (via endophytic fungi) that provide strong defense against both vertebrate and invertebrate herbivory.
- Functions as a pioneer species in disturbed habitats, rapidly colonizing open ground.
- The plant–endophyte–herbivore interaction illustrates the cascading effects of fungal secondary metabolism on ecosystem trophic structure.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Toxin sourcing**: Macrocyclic trichothecenes from *Baccharis*-associated endophytes have been investigated for anticancer properties due to their potent biological activity.
- **Weed management**: Understanding the endophytic basis of *Baccharis* toxicity may inform strategies for managing invasive populations.
- **Model system**: The *Baccharis* case is frequently cited as evidence for the widespread role of fungal endophytes in plant chemical ecology, extending the concept of endophyte-mediated defense beyond grasses.
