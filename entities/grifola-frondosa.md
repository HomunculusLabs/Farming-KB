# Grifola frondosa

## Overview

*Grifola frondosa*, commonly known as maitake (舞茸, "dancing mushroom") or hen-of-the-woods,
is a polyporoid bracket fungus in the family Meripilaceae. It forms large, overlapping clusters
of fan-shaped caps at the base of hardwood trees, particularly oaks. Native to temperate forests
of East Asia, Europe, and eastern North America, it is both a prized culinary mushroom and a
significant medicinal fungus in traditional Chinese and Japanese medicine.

The Japanese name refers to the dancing for joy its discoverers performed upon finding it,
reflecting its historical rarity and value. Modern cultivation has made it widely available,
and it is extensively researched for immunomodulatory and antitumor polysaccharides,
notably the beta-D-glucan fraction designated D-fraction.

## Taxonomy

Basidiomycota → Agaricomycetes → Polyporales → Meripilaceae → **Grifola → G. frondosa** (Dicks.) Gray (1821).

**Synonyms:** *Boletus frondosus* Dicks. (1785), *Polyporus frondosus* (Dicks.) Fr. (1821),
*Grifola umbellata* (Pers.) Pilát.

## Morphology

- **Fruiting body:** Large compound cluster of overlapping fan- to spoon-shaped caps arising
  from a single thick branching base. Clusters reach 30–100 cm across, weighing up to 20 kg.
- **Caps:** 2–10 cm wide, grayish-brown to dark brown, smooth to finely velvety; margins
  lighter, cream-colored and wavy.
- **Pore surface:** White to cream, small round pores (1–3 per mm), running decurrent down
  short stalks. Does not bruise noticeably.
- **Stipe:** Multiple short tough branching stems from a common base, each supporting 1–3 caps.
- **Flesh:** White, firm, fibrous when young, becoming tough and woody with age. Nutty aroma.
- **Spore print:** White. **Spores:** Cylindrical to ellipsoidal, smooth, 5–7.5 × 3–4 µm.

## Distribution and Habitat

Found across temperate Northern Hemisphere: northeastern China, Japan, Korea, eastern Russia,
Europe (Scandinavia to Mediterranean), and eastern North America. Primarily a root and butt rot
pathogen of hardwoods, especially oaks (*Quercus* spp.), also elm, maple, and beech. Fruits
in late summer to autumn (September–November) at the base of living trees or from subterranean
roots. Prefers warm, moist autumns with cool nights.

## Cultivation

- **Substrate:** Supplemented hardwood sawdust (oak, beech) with wheat or rice bran (20–30%).
- **Sterilization:** 121°C for 90–120 minutes; inoculate with grain spawn.
- **Incubation:** 20–25°C, 60–75% humidity, dark, 30–45 days until full colonization.
- **Fruiting:** Induce via fresh air exchange, reduced temperature (15–18°C), high humidity
  (85–95%), indirect light. Primordia in 7–14 days; maturation in 14–21 days.
- **Yield:** 100–200 g per 1 kg dry substrate (BE 10–20%).
- **Difficulty:** Moderate — sensitive to contamination and requires precise environmental control.

## Bioactive Compounds

- **β-D-glucans (D-fraction):** Heteropolysaccharides (β-1,6-glucan with β-1,3 side chains)
  from fruiting body and mycelium. Potent immunomodulators activating macrophages, NK cells,
  and T-cells via Dectin-1 and complement receptor 3.
- **MD-fraction:** Purified protein-bound β-glucan (~20% protein) with enhanced bioactivity.
  Demonstrated tumor growth inhibition synergistic with chemotherapy.
- **Grifolan:** β-1,3-glucan with β-1,6 branches; activates complement pathway and enhances
  macrophage cytotoxicity.
- **Ergosterol and ergosterol peroxide:** Antioxidant and anti-inflammatory sterols.
- **Phenolic compounds and flavonoids:** Free radical scavenging activity.

## Pharmacology

- **Immunomodulation:** D-fraction increases macrophage activity, NK cell cytotoxicity, and
  IL-1, IL-6, TNF-α production. Human pilot studies report increased CD4+/CD8+ counts.
- **Antitumor:** β-glucans activate macrophages and NK cells via Dectin-1/CR3, inducing
  apoptosis and inhibiting angiogenesis. Japanese Phase I/II studies showed tumor regression
  combined with chemotherapy in breast, lung, and liver cancers.
- **Hypoglycemic:** Reduces blood glucose and insulin resistance in diabetic models via AMPK
  activation and inhibition of hepatic gluconeogenesis.
- **Hypolipidemic:** Reduces serum cholesterol and triglycerides via HMG-CoA reductase
  inhibition and increased fecal bile acid excretion.
- **Hepatoprotective:** Protects against CCl4-induced liver damage in rodent models.

## Culinary and Nutritional Use

Prized in Japanese cuisine (*yaki maitake*, tempura, soups). Flavor is rich, earthy, and
peppery with firm, meaty texture. **Per 100 g fresh:** ~34 kcal, 1.9 g protein, 0.3 g fat,
6.6 g carbohydrate, 2.7 g fiber. Rich in potassium (310 mg), phosphorus, niacin, and
riboflavin. Contains ergothioneine, a unique antioxidant amino acid.

## Ecological Role

White-rot saprotroph and root pathogen decomposing lignin and cellulose in hardwood heartwood
and roots. Contributes to nutrient cycling by breaking down coarse woody debris. Valued in
permaculture as an indicator species for healthy, mature oak ecosystems.

## Related Species

- *Grifola umbellata* — sometimes considered a synonym; whiter, more delicate fronds.
- *Meripilus giganteus* (giant polypore) — much larger, bruises black; not edible.
- *Polyporus umbellatus* (zhuling) — Chinese medicinal mushroom from underground sclerotia.
