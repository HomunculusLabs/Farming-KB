# Grifola frondosa (Maitake, Hen of the Woods)

## Overview

**Grifola frondosa** is a polypore mushroom in the family Meripilaceae, widely known as maitake (舞茸, "dancing mushroom" in Japanese) or hen of the woods. It is a choice edible fungus and one of the most commercially significant medicinal mushrooms, with a multi-billion dollar market primarily in Japan, China, and increasingly in Western countries.

The species forms large, overlapping clusters of grayish-brown caps at the base of hardwood trees, particularly oaks. It has been used in traditional Chinese and Japanese medicine for centuries as an adaptogen and immune tonic.

Modern research has focused on its D-fraction, a protein-bound β-glucan with demonstrated immunomodulatory, anti-tumor, and antidiabetic activities. Maitake is cultivated commercially on supplemented sawdust substrates and is among the top three medicinal mushrooms by global market value.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Polyporales
- **Family:** Meripilaceae
- **Genus:** Grifola
- **Species:** G. frondosa (Dicks.) Gray

**Synonyms:** *Boletus frondosus* Dicks. (basionym), *Polyporus frondosus* (Dicks.) Fr., *Coriolus frondosus* (Dicks.) Pat.

**Etymology:** *Grifola* derives from *grifo-* (a mythical creature) + *-folia* (leaf), referring to the frond-like appearance of the fruiting body. The Japanese name *maitake* (舞茸) means "dancing mushroom" — legend holds that foragers danced with joy upon finding it due to its rarity and value.

## Morphology

**Fruiting body:** Large, compound, and composed of multiple overlapping, fan-shaped caps arising from a single branched base. Entire clusters can reach 20–100 cm in diameter and weigh up to 20 kg, though 1–5 kg specimens are more typical.

**Individual caps:** 2–10 cm broad, spatulate to fan-shaped, with wavy margins. Upper surface is grayish-brown to dark brown, smooth to finely velvety. Surface develops radial fibrils with age.

**Hymenophore:** White to cream pore surface on the underside of each cap. Pores are small, angular, 1–3 per mm, and do not stain when bruised — a key identification feature distinguishing it from similar species.

**Stipe/stem structure:** Multiple short, tough, branching stems arising from a common base. Interior is white and marbled, firm but becoming tough with age.

**Spores:** Broadly ellipsoid, smooth, hyaline, 5–7 × 3.5–5 µm. Spore print is white.

**Flesh:** White, firm, and aromatic with a distinctive spicy, earthy fragrance. Taste is described as rich, nutty, and peppery.

## Distribution and Habitat

**Geographic range:** Native to temperate hardwood forests of eastern North America, Europe, Japan, and China. In North America, found from eastern Canada to the Appalachian Mountains and west to the Great Lakes region.

**Substrate:** A root and butt rot pathogen of hardwoods, especially oaks (*Quercus* spp.) but also elm, maple, beech, and walnut. The mycelium colonizes the root system and lower trunk, often fruiting at the base of living or recently dead trees.

**Fruiting season:** Late summer to early autumn (August–November in the Northern Hemisphere). Prefers cool, wet conditions following warm summer months. Initiated by dropping temperatures and increased moisture.

The species is ecologically significant as a decomposer of large root systems, recycling substantial quantities of woody biomass in forest ecosystems. It can persist on infected root systems for years, producing annual fruitings.

## Bioactive Compounds and Pharmacology

**β-Glucans (D-Fraction):** The most studied bioactive component. D-Fraction is a protein-bound β-1,6-glucan with β-1,3 branches, isolated from the fruiting body. It activates macrophages, dendritic cells, and NK cells via the Dectin-1 and complement receptor 3 (CR3) pathways.

Documented immunomodulatory activities include:

- Enhanced production of interleukin-1, IL-6, IL-12, and TNF-α by activated macrophages
- Increased NK cell cytotoxicity against tumor cell lines (2–3 fold increase in vitro)
- Activation of T-helper cell and cytotoxic T-cell responses
- Synergistic enhancement of chemotherapy efficacy in murine tumor models

**MD-Fraction:** A refined, acid-soluble β-glucan preparation with higher molecular weight and enhanced bioactivity compared to D-Fraction. Used in commercial maitake supplements.

**Antidiabetic compounds:** Maitake SX-fraction, isolated from the mycelium, demonstrates insulin-sensitizing effects. Clinical studies in Japan (2001–2009) reported improved glycemic control in type 2 diabetic patients at doses of 500–1500 mg/day, with HbA1c reductions of 0.3–0.8% over 12-week periods.

**Other compounds:**

- **Grifolin** — an isoprenoid phenol with antimicrobial and cytotoxic activity
- **Ergosterol derivatives** — anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties
- **Phenolic acids** — chlorogenic, ferulic, and p-coumaric acids with free radical scavenging activity
- **Nucleotides and nucleosides** — adenosine, guanosine, and uridine contributing to umami flavor

⚠️ **Safety:** Generally recognized as safe (GRAS) in the US. Rare cases of allergic contact dermatitis and mild gastrointestinal upset reported. Immunocompromised individuals should consult a healthcare provider before use.

## Cultivation

**Commercial methods:** Primarily cultivated on supplemented hardwood sawdust (oak, beech) in autoclavable bags. Substrate formula typically includes 75% sawdust, 20% bran, 3% rice bran, 2% gypsum. Sterilization at 121°C for 90 minutes.

**Spawn run:** 25–30 days at 20–25°C in darkness. Full colonization produces a firm white mycelial block.

**Fruiting induction:** Requires a cold shock (5–10°C for 72 hours), followed by maintenance at 15–18°C, 90–95% humidity, and 500–1000 lux light (12 hours/day). Fresh air exchange is critical — CO₂ levels above 1500 ppm suppress normal cap development and promote abnormal morphology.

**Yield:** Biological efficiency of 40–60% on supplemented sawdust. First flush appears 7–14 days after induction. Two to three flushes total, with the first flush comprising 60–70% of total yield.

**Outdoor cultivation:** Log inoculation is possible using oak bolts (15–20 cm diameter) with dowel or sawdust spawn, but is less common due to longer production cycles (1–2 years to first fruiting) and lower yields.

## Culinary and Nutritional Value

Maitake is considered one of the premier edible mushrooms, prized for its complex, rich flavor described as earthy, peppery, and slightly sweet. The texture is firm and meaty when young, becoming tough with age.

**Nutritional profile (per 100 g fresh):**

- Calories: ~30 kcal
- Protein: 3.5 g
- Carbohydrates: 7 g (including 2.5 g dietary fiber)
- Fat: 0.2 g
- Potassium: 340 mg
- Vitamin D₂: Variable, increases significantly with UV exposure post-harvest

The mushroom is used in a wide range of Japanese, Chinese, and Western cuisines — sautéed, grilled, in soups and tempura. Dried maitake is used as a base for dashi-style broths.
