# Cercospora

## Description

*Cercospora* is a large genus of fungal plant pathogens in the family Mycosphaerellaceae (order Capnodiales) that causes leaf spot diseases on a wide range of economically important crops. The genus is notable for its production of cercosporin, a perylenequinone photosensitizing toxin that generates reactive oxygen species (singlet oxygen and superoxide) in the presence of light. Cercosporin kills host plant cells, making nutrients available to the invading fungus. Richardson (2000) cited *Cercospora* as an example of how fungal secondary metabolites can influence host plant physiology, drawing parallels between pathogenic and endophytic fungal strategies.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Phylum**: Ascomycota
- **Class**: Dothideomycetes
- **Order**: Capnodiales
- **Family**: Mycosphaerellaceae
- **Genus**: *Cercospora*
- **Teleomorph**: *Mycosphaerella* (in part) / *Pseudocercospora*

## Key Facts

- **Cercosporin toxin**: The hallmark of the genus is the production of cercosporin, a non-host-specific photosensitizing toxin activated by light. Upon illumination, cercosporin generates singlet oxygen (^1O_2) and superoxide radicals (O_2^−) that peroxidize membrane lipids, causing rapid host cell death.
- **Self-protection**: *Cercospora* species have evolved sophisticated mechanisms to resist their own toxin, including active cercosporin efflux (via ABC transporters), transient reduction of cercosporin to a less toxic form, and enhanced antioxidant defenses.
- **Economic importance**: Major diseases include Cercospora leaf spot of sugarbeet (*C. beticola*), soybean (*C. kikuchii* and *C. sojina*), banana (*C. musae* = *Pseudocercospora musae*), and many vegetable and field crops. Sugarbeet leaf spot alone causes millions of dollars in crop losses annually.
- **Nutrient acquisition strategy**: By killing host cells with cercosporin, the fungus liberates intracellular nutrients (sugars, amino acids) that support its growth and sporulation — a necrotrophic strategy facilitated by toxin production.
- **Parallels with endophytes**: Richardson (2000) drew attention to the fact that both endophytic fungi (which produce alkaloids) and pathogenic fungi (like *Cercospora*, which produces cercosporin) use secondary metabolites to manipulate host plant physiology — the difference lying in the degree of harm caused to the host.

## Ecological Role

- Causes leaf spot diseases that reduce host photosynthetic capacity, growth, and yield.
- Cercosporin-mediated host cell killing represents a form of necrotrophic nutrient acquisition.
- The genus demonstrates that fungal secondary metabolites serve as chemical tools for manipulating plant physiology across the pathogen–mutualist continuum.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Crop disease management**: Cercospora leaf spots are among the most common and economically damaging fungal diseases in agriculture. Management relies on fungicide application, resistant cultivars, and cultural practices (crop rotation, residue management).
- **Fungicide resistance**: *C. beticola* has developed resistance to multiple fungicide classes (benzimidazoles, DMIs, QoIs), making it a model system for studying fungicide resistance evolution.
- **Toxin research**: Cercosporin is studied as a model photosensitizing compound with potential applications in photodynamic therapy and as a tool for understanding oxidative stress biology.
- **Secondary metabolism**: The *Cercospora* cercosporin pathway is a model for understanding fungal polyketide biosynthesis and self-protection mechanisms.
