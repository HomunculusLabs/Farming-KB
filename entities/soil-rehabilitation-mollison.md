# Soil Rehabilitation

## Description

Soil rehabilitation is the process of restoring degraded, compacted, eroded, or lifeless soils to productive health. Mollison presents a comprehensive framework for soil rehabilitation encompassing four stages: water control, soil conditioning, fertilisation, and crop/plant selection. The approach integrates mechanical, biological, and management strategies, always prioritising permanent solutions over temporary fixes.

## Classification

- **Type**: Agricultural practice / land management methodology
- **Category**: Permaculture, soil science, [[regenerative-agriculture]]
- **Sub-approaches**: Mechanical (chisel plough), biological (root penetration, earthworms), management (grazing, cropping systems)

## Key Facts

### Four-Stage Rehabilitation Framework

1. **Water Control**: Drainage and sophisticated irrigation for salted areas; soil mounding/shaping for gardening in salted lands. Reliance on natural rainfall and water harvest rather than groundwaters. Three drainage techniques:
   - Raised garden beds with paths as drains
   - Deep open drains every 10-80 m
   - Underground tile drains (fluted plastic pipes) at 1.5 m depth, 10-80 m apart

2. **Soil Conditioning**: Compacted, collapsed, and eroded soils need rehabilitative aeration and a change in land use.

3. **Fertilisation**: Light trace element adjustment via foliar sprays on undisturbed permanent crop systems. [[seed-pelleting]] with key elements. Utilising phosphate locked in clays and nitrogen from legume interplants and green manures.

4. **Crop and Plant Species Selection**: Older varieties yield with less fertiliser and water; many have superior flavour, pest resistance, and nutritive value. Diverse food crops stabilise soils.

### Soil Building Methods
- [[building-soil]] at garden scale: raise/lower beds, mix compost/humus/clay/sand/nutrients, mulch to reduce water loss.
- Mechanical conditioning: chisel ploughing in sequential depth passes.
- Life form management: plants (daikon, comfrey, lucerne, Acacias, legumes) and macro/micro-fauna (earthworms, rhizobia).

### Key Results of Rehabilitation
- Living soil with earthworms adding alkaline manure and nitrogen
- Friable, open soil with easy water penetration
- Aerated soil (warmer in winter, cooler in summer)
- Absorbent soil retaining water and preventing runoff
- Dead roots creating air spaces, tunnels, and fixing nitrogen
- Easy root penetration for new plantings
- Permanent change if not re-compacted or chemically damaged

## Relevance to Cultivation

- **Garden scale**: Raised beds, [[sheet-mulching]], compost addition, and careful observation of plant health.
- **Farm scale**: Chisel ploughing, grazing management, deep-rooted species, and minimum tillage.
- **Landscape scale**: Forestry, reafforestation, contour earthworks, and [[water-management]] infrastructure.
- **Economic imperative**: Mollison argues that society does not give farmers time or money to create or preserve soil, yet expects them to serve commodity markets that care little for soil or nutrition.
- **Permanence principle**: Mechanical soil rehabilitation should ideally be a one-time process; it is the land use practices that follow that determine long-term success or failure.

## Biological Chisel Ploughs

Different cultures use different species for biological soil penetration:
- **Japan (Fukuoka)**: Radish and Acacia
- **Africa**: Acacia albida, Glyricidia
- **New Guinea**: Casuarina
- **Mediterranean**: Tamarix
- **Universal**: Daikon radish, comfrey, earthworms, rhizobia

## Practical Applications

Soil Rehabilitation Mollison plays a critical role in sustainable land management and regenerative agriculture.
Understanding these processes enables practitioners to design systems that work with natural
soil biology rather than against it, reducing inputs while improving long-term productivity.

## Management Considerations

Effective management requires monitoring soil indicators and responding to changes in
soil structure, organic matter content, and biological activity. Key strategies include
minimizing soil disturbance, maintaining ground cover, and incorporating diverse plantings.
