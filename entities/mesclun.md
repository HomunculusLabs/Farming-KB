# Mesclun

## Description

Mesclun is a traditional French concept referring to a mixture of young, tender salad greens harvested at baby-leaf stage. Originating in the Provence region of southern France, the term describes both the mixture of greens and the growing method that produces them. In modern market gardening, mesclun has become a high-value crop category encompassing diverse blends of lettuces, brassicas, herbs, and specialty greens sold as ready-to-eat salad mix.

## Classification

- **Category**: Agricultural product and growing technique
- **Origin**: Provence, France (traditional)
- **Type**: Mixed baby-leaf salad greens
- **Harvest method**: [[cut-and-come-again]] or single-cut

## Key Facts

- Traditional Provencal mesclun included chervil, [[arugula]], lettuce, and endive in specific proportions
- The word "mesclun" derives from the Provencal "mesclumo" meaning "mixture"
- Modern mesclun blends may contain 5–15 different greens including lettuces, brassicas, and herbs
- Harvested at 3–6 inches tall, typically 21–35 days after sowing depending on season
- Can be sold as whole leaves for customers to mix, or pre-mixed and washed
- Premium product commanding significantly higher prices per pound than individual greens
- Greens are typically sown in dense beds and harvested with knives or specialized greens harvesters
- Regrows after cutting for multiple harvests in the cut-and-come-again system
- Well-suited to winter production under protection due to cold tolerance of many component greens

## Relevance to Cultivation

Mesclun production is central to profitable winter harvest systems:

- **High revenue per bed foot**: Dense planting and premium pricing make mesclun one of the most profitable crops per unit area
- **Quick turnover**: 21–35 day cycles allow multiple harvests from the same bed space per season
- **Winter production**: Many mesclun components ([[mizuna]], [[tatsoi]], [[claytonia]], arugula, spinach) are cold-hardy and produce well under row covers or in unheated greenhouses
- **Customer appeal**: The visual diversity and variety of flavors and textures attract market customers
- **Labor efficiency**: Mechanical harvesting with greens harvesters can significantly reduce hand-labor
- **Flexibility**: Compositions can be adjusted seasonally to match available greens and customer preferences

## Common Components

**Lettuces**: Various leaf types for color, texture, and mild flavor
**Brassicas**: Arugula, mizuna, tatsoi, mustard greens for peppery bite
**Specialty greens**: Claytonia, mache, minutina, sorrel for unique textures and flavors
**Herbs**: Occasional additions of parsley, chervil, or basil depending on season

## Production Notes

- Sow seeds densely in beds at approximately 1–2 inch spacing
- Use pinpoint seeders for efficient, even sowing
- Harvest with sharp knives or mechanical greens harvesters
- Wash and pack immediately in perforated bags for market
- Maintain cold chain from harvest to sale for maximum shelf life
- Price by weight or by bag — pre-portioned bags reduce customer handling
