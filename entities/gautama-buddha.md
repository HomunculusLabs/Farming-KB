# Gautama Buddha

## Description

Gautama Buddha (c. 563–483 BCE) was the historical founder of Buddhism.

He attained enlightenment under the Bodhi tree after confronting the Hosts of Mara.

He is listed among the devas and saints in the [[bardo-thodol]]'s litany of awakened beings.

His struggle with Mara provides the archetypal model for all subsequent confrontations with ego-delusion.

## Classification

- **Type**: Historical person — spiritual teacher and founder of Buddhism
- **Tradition**: Buddhism (founder)
- **Dates**: c. 563–483 BCE
- **Birthplace**: Lumbini, present-day Nepal
- **Key Event**: Enlightenment under the Bodhi tree at Bodh Gaya

## Key Facts

1. Gautama Buddha was born Siddhartha Gautama, a prince of the Shakya clan in present-day Nepal.

2. He lived c. 563–483 BCE, though scholarly estimates of his dates vary.

3. He renounced his royal life at age 29 after encountering suffering, old age, and death.

4. After six years of ascetic practice, he attained enlightenment at Bodh Gaya under the Bodhi tree.

5. During his enlightenment, he struggled with the Hosts of Mara — the forces of delusion and ego.

6. His victory over Mara established the model for all Buddhist practice: seeing through illusion to attain liberation.

7. He is listed among the devas and saints who appear in the Bardo Thodol as witnesses to the dying person's experience.

8. His first teaching (the Dharmachakra Sutra) established the Four Noble Truths and the Eightfold Path.

9. He taught for approximately 45 years after his enlightenment, establishing the monastic Sangha.

10. His parinirvana (final passing) occurred at Kushinagar, where he entered the deathless state beyond Sangsara.

## The Enlightenment Under the Bodhi Tree

The confrontation with Mara is the central event of the Buddha's enlightenment narrative.

Mara sent armies of demons, temptations, and illusions to break the Buddha's meditation.

The Buddha remained unmoved, touching the earth as witness to his attainment.

This event is the prototype for every practitioner's confrontation with ego-resistance.

In the psychedelic context, it models the correct response to fear and delusion during ego-dissolution.

## Relevance to Psychedelic Studies

In *The Psychedelic Experience*, the Buddha's confrontation with Mara is the model for the psychedelic voyager's journey.

The voyager, like the Buddha, must face the terrifying dissolution of ego and remain present.

The Buddha's victory demonstrates that the [[clear-light]] can be recognized and liberation attained.

His presence among the Bardo Thodol's devas and saints provides a guiding archetype for the dying person.

The Buddha's path — from privilege to renunciation to enlightenment — parallels the psychedelic journey from ego to ego-loss to liberation.

## Relation to Other Entities

- **Mara**: The demon of delusion whom the Buddha conquered under the Bodhi tree.
- **Sangsara**: The cycle of suffering from which the Buddha discovered the path of liberation.
- **Clear Light**: The awakened state the Buddha embodied and taught others to recognize.
- **[[dharma-kaya]]**: The truth body the Buddha realized and manifested.
- **[[tri-kaya]]**: The three-fold doctrine describing the Buddha's three modes of being.
