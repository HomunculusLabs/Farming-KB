# Tylopilus felleus

## Overview

*Tylopilus felleus*, commonly known as the bitter bolete or gall bolete, is a
pored mushroom in the family Boletaceae. It is among the most frequently
confused boletes for beginning foragers due to its superficial resemblance to
the prized *[[comparison-spore-print-collection-vs-spore-syringe-production]] and bitter flesh,
characters that distinguish the genus from the *Boletus edulis* group.

## Morphology

**Cap:** 5–20 cm broad, convex to plano-convex, sometimes shallowly depressed
at maturity. Surface dry, finely tomentose when young, becoming smooth or
slightly fibrillose with age. Color varies from pale brown to dark brown,
tannish, or grayish-brown; occasionally with a pinkish or vinaceous tint.

**Pore surface:** Adnate to slightly depressed around the stipe. Pores are
white when young, becoming pinkish then distinctly pink to reddish-pink at
maturity — a key diagnostic feature distinguishing it from *Boletus edulis*,
which retains white pores.

** Tubes:** 1–2 cm deep, white when young, becoming pinkish with age.

**Stipe:** 5–15 cm tall, 1.5–4 cm thick, clavate to subequal, solid.
Surface reticulate (netted) at least in the upper portion, a feature that
contributes to confusion with porcini. Color is pale brown above the
reticulation, darker below. Base often shows white mycelium.

**Flesh:** White to pale cream, unchanging or very slowly becoming pinkish
when cut. Extremely bitter taste — even a tiny piece on the tongue produces
an intense and lingering bitterness. Odor is mild, pleasant, and mushroomy,
offering no warning of the taste.

## Distribution and Habitat

*T. felleus* is widely distributed across [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]] (from eastern
Canada to the southeastern and [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]]), and parts
of Asia. It has also been reported from North Africa and Australia.
