# Eric Duffy

## Description

Eric Duffy is an author and commentator on grassland ecology and wildlife management, quoted by Mollison from his 1979 work. His statement — "Grasslands unsuited for farming exist in every continent, and there is surely no better way to use it than to preserve its wildlife" — encapsulates a core principle of permaculture rangeland design: that marginal lands are best managed through wildlife preservation rather than forced agricultural conversion.

- **Type**: Person — author and grassland/wildlife advocate

## Key Facts

- Advocated for wildlife preservation on unsuitable agricultural land as the highest and best use
- His 1979 work is cited by Mollison in the context of global grassland types and their management
- Argued against converting natural grasslands to domestic livestock pasture or cropping
- His position aligns with Mollison's broader critique of cattle-based rangeland destruction

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

Duffy's observation serves as a foundational principle for permaculture approaches to marginal grasslands:

1. **Appropriate land use**: Not all grasslands should be farmed. Those with poor soils, insufficient rainfall, or fragile ecosystems are better suited to wildlife management.
2. **Wildlife as superior production**: On semi-arid rangelands, indigenous wildlife (kangaroo in Australia, bison and pronghorn in North America, antelope in Africa) produce more protein per hectare with lower environmental damage than domestic livestock.
3. **Low-input sustainability**: Wildlife-based systems require no supplemental feed, fencing, veterinary costs, or infrastructure beyond harvesting capacity.

Mollison extends Duffy's argument by noting that dry savannah veldt areas of Africa and Australia, though "unsuited for farming," are "rich in natural protein yields" that can never be matched by "a propped-up, energy-consuming and essentially retrograde pastoral system of a few species of domestic cattle and goat flocks."

## Broader Context

Duffy's advocacy for wildlife preservation on rangelands anticipates modern conservation grazing and rewilding movements. His principle — that the best use of unsuitable farmland is to preserve its natural inhabitants — has been validated by decades of rangeland degradation caused by attempting to impose domestic livestock systems on fragile semi-arid ecosystems.
