## Overview

**Drosera anglica**, commonly known as the English sundew or great sundew, is a perennial carnivorous plant in the family Droseraceae.

Flowers are borne on a single, erect scape that rises 10–30 cm above the rosette. The inflorescence is a one-sided cyme, with flowers opening sequentially from the base upward.

Individual flowers are small (8–12 mm diameter), white to pink, with five petals and five stamens. Flowering occurs from June to August in northern hemisphere populations.

Seed capsules are small, dehiscent, and release numerous fine seeds that are dispersed by wind and water.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Drosera anglica* has a broad circumboreal distribution. In North America, it ranges from Alaska across Canada to the northeastern [[ph-and-nutrient-availability-garden-soils]]. Optimal pH ranges from 4.0 to 6.0.

The species tolerates cold winters and is frequently found at higher latitudes and elevations than most other *Drosera* species. It can survive being submerged for extended periods.

## Trapping Mechanism

Prey capture in *Drosera anglica* follows the classic sundew mechanism described by Charles Darwin. The sticky mucilage on the tentacle tips acts as a flypaper trap.

When an insect contacts the mucilage, it becomes ensnared. The initial contact stimulates a slow tentacle-bending response (thigmonasty) that takes several minutes to hours.

Adjacent tentacles bend toward the captured prey, increasing the adhesive surface area and ensuring secure entrapment. The leaf blade itself may also curl around the prey.

The leaf secretes additional digestive enzymes from sessile glands once prey is secured. These enzymes dissolve the soft tissues of the insect over 3–7 days.

Nutrient absorption occurs through the leaf surface. The plant derives nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium from the digested prey, supplementing the nutrient-poor substrate.

After digestion is complete, the leaf unfurls and the tentacles reset. The undigested exoskeleton is typically washed away by rain or blown off by wind.

## Cultivation

*Drosera anglica* is considered one of the easier temperate sundews to cultivate. It requires full sun to partial shade, distilled or rainwater, and a peat-based growing medium.

A standard mix of one part sphagnum peat moss to one part silica sand works well. The plants should be kept in a tray of water year-round to maintain soil saturation.

Outdoor cultivation is feasible in USDA zones 5–8. The species requires a winter dormancy period of 3–4 months, during which above-ground growth dies back to the rootstock.

Indoor growers can provide dormancy by placing plants in a cool location (2–5°C) with reduced light for the winter months. A refrigerator dormancy method is also effective.

Propagation is most commonly achieved through leaf cuttings, which root readily in moist peat. Gemmae (vegetative buds produced in winter) offer another reliable propagation method.
