## Distribution and Habitat

Ceroxylon quindiuense is endemic to the Andes of Colombia, with disjunct populations extending into northern Peru. In Colombia, the species occurs in the departments of Quindío, Valle del Cauca, Cauca, Tolima, and Huila.

The species is restricted to montane cloud forests between 2000 and 3000 meters elevation. These high-altitude forests are characterized by persistent fog, high humidity, frequent cloud cover, and moderate temperatures ranging from 8 to 18 degrees Celsius.

Soils are typically volcanic-derived, well-drained, and rich in [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-south-america]]. The valley receives approximately 300,000 visitors annually.

## Ecology and Conservation

Ceroxylon quindiuense is a keystone species of Andean cloud [[dighton-fungi-tropical-forest-ecosystems]].

The species is classified as Endangered on the IUCN Red List. Principal threats include deforestation for agriculture and cattle ranching, urban expansion, and the harvesting of young leaves for Palm Sunday processions.

Palm Sunday leaf harvesting is particularly damaging because removing young leaves kills the apical meristem of immature palms. An estimated 500,000 palm leaves are harvested annually for religious observances in Colombia alone.

Growth is extremely slow, with seedlings taking 10 to 15 years to develop a visible trunk above ground. Mature palms are estimated to be 80 to 150 years old, making population recovery from harvesting or habitat loss a century-scale process.

Rising temperatures and shifting cloud bases may reduce the extent of suitable habitat at the upper elevations where the species is concentrated.
