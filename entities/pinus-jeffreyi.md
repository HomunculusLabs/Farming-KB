Mature trees typically reach 25 to 40 meters in height, with exceptional specimens exceeding 50 meters.

Trunk diameter at breast height ranges from 60 to 120 centimeters on good sites.

The bark is thick, deeply furrowed, and distinctly reddish-brown to cinnamon-colored, often with a vanilla or butterscotch scent.

Needles occur in bundles of three, measuring 14 to 23 centimeters in length.

They are stiff, bluish-green, and persist on the tree for 4 to 9 years.

Cones are large and heavy, measuring 15 to 30 centimeters in length.

They are sessile or nearly so, lacking the pronounced stalk characteristic of ponderosa pine cones.

Cone scales are armed with stout, curved prickles that point inward or are reflexed.

## Distribution and Habitat

Jeffrey pine occurs across a discontinuous range in the western [[dighton-fungi-acidifying-pollutants-forest-ecosystems]].

Mature trees provide nesting cavities for woodpeckers, owls, and flying squirrels.

The species is moderately fire-adapted.

Thick bark insulates the cambium from low to moderate severity fire, and mature individuals commonly survive surface fires.

Seedlings establish best on mineral soil exposed by fire, making Jeffrey pine a post-fire successional species.

Stand-replacing crown fires can kill even mature trees, but the species recovers through serotinous cone openings and [[ectomycorrhizal-associations]] with numerous fungal genera, including Suillus, Rhizopogon, and Amanita.
