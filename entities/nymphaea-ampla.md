# Nymphaea ampla

Nymphaea ampla is an aquatic plant in the family Nymphaeaceae, commonly
known as the white water lily or dotleaf waterlily. Native to tropical
and subtropical regions of the Americas, it has a long history of
ethnobotanical use among indigenous Mesoamerican and Caribbean peoples,
who consumed the flowers and rhizomes for their mild psychoactive and
sedative properties. The plant contains aporphine alkaloids, most
notably [[nymphaea-caerulea]] | Blue flowers, toothed leaf margins |
| Nymphaea odorata | White flowers, fragrant, northern distribution |
| Nymphaea lotus | White to pink flowers, tropical African/Asian |
| [[serotonin]] receptors.
- **Apomorphine:** Present in trace amounts; a potent dopamine agonist
  used medically for Parkinson's disease.
- **Aporphine alkaloids:** Including anonaine, asimilobine, and
  pronuciferine, contributing to the pharmacological profile.
- **Flavonoids:** [[kaempferol]], and glycosides with antioxidant
  and anti-inflammatory properties.
- **Tannins:** Present in rhizomes, contributing to astringent
  traditional applications.
- **Alkaloid content:** Varies by population and growing conditions.
  Flowers typically contain 0.01–0.05% total alkaloids by dry weight.

## Ethnobotanical and Cultural Significance

- **Maya and Aztec traditions:** N. ampla was consumed by indigenous
  Mesoamerican peoples, possibly as a sacramental or medicinal plant.
  Archaeological evidence from water-associated ceremonial contexts
  suggests ritual significance.
- **Caribbean ethnobotany:** Used in folk medicine as a sedative,
  analgesic, and aphrodisiac. Flower infusions are traditional remedies
  for anxiety and insomnia.
- **Modern use:** Dried flowers sold as "white lotus" in herbal smoking
  blends and teas, though psychoactive effects are mild compared to
  classical psychedelics.
- **Brujo practice:** Among some Central American indigenous groups,
  associated with divination and healing ceremonies.

## Pharmacology and Effects

- **Effects:** Mild euphoria, relaxation, visual hypersensitivity,
  drowsiness, and dreamlike state. Subtle and dose-dependent.
- **Mechanism:** Primarily mediated by aporphine alkaloids acting as
  dopamine D1/D2 receptor modulators with weak 5-HT2A interaction.
  Nuciferine shows biased agonism at dopamine receptors.
- **Duration:** 2–4 hours when consumed as tea or smoked.
- **Toxicity:** [[fukuoka-low-toxicity-pesticide-deception]] profile. No deaths attributed to N. ampla.
  High doses may cause nausea and dizziness.

## Cultivation

- **Propagation:** Division of rhizomes in spring. Seeds require
  stratification and germinate in warm, shallow water.
- **Growing conditions:** Full sun to partial shade, 20–30°C water,
  pH 6.0–7.5. Nutrient-rich substrate promotes flowering.
- **Hardiness:** USDA zones 9–12. Cannot tolerate freezing.
- **Uses:** Suitable for ornamental water gardens and constructed
  wetlands for water purification.

## Legal Status

Not scheduled or controlled in most jurisdictions. Sold legally as an
ornamental aquatic plant and dried botanical specimen.
