## Nymphaea ampla

Nymphaea ampla is an aquatic plant in the family Nymphaeaceae, commonly
known as the white water lily or dotleaf waterlily. Native to tropical
and subtropical regions of the Americas, it has a long history of
ethnobotanical use among indigenous Mesoamerican and Caribbean peoples,
who consumed the flowers and rhizomes for their mild psychoactive and
sedative properties. The plant contains aporphine alkaloids, most
notably [[serotonin]] receptors.
- **Apomorphine:** Present in trace amounts; a potent dopamine agonist
  used medically for Parkinson's disease.
- **Aporphine alkaloids:** Including anonaine, asimilobine, and
  pronuciferine, contributing to the pharmacological profile.
- **Flavonoids:** [[fukuoka-low-toxicity-pesticide-deception]] profile. No deaths attributed to N. ampla.
  High doses may cause nausea and dizziness.

## Cultivation

- **Propagation:** Division of rhizomes in spring. Seeds require
  stratification and germinate in warm, shallow water.
- **Growing conditions:** Full sun to partial shade, 20–30°C water,
  pH 6.0–7.5. Nutrient-rich substrate promotes flowering.
- **Hardiness:** USDA zones 9–12. Cannot tolerate freezing.
- **Uses:** Suitable for ornamental water gardens and constructed
  wetlands for water purification.

## Legal Status

Not scheduled or controlled in most jurisdictions. Sold legally as an
ornamental aquatic plant and dried botanical specimen.
