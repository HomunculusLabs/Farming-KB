## Overview

*Laccaria proxima* is a mycorrhizal agaric mushroom in the family Hydnangiaceae,
widely distributed across temperate and boreal regions of the Northern Hemisphere.
The species forms [[ectomycorrhizal-associations]] primarily with willows (*Salix* spp.)
and other pioneer trees in disturbed, nitrogen-poor, or sandy habitats including
coastal dunes, riverbanks, and reclaimed mine sites. Its role as an early-stage

## Taxonomy

Basidiomycota → Agaricomycetes → Agaricales → **Hydnangiaceae → *Laccaria → *L.
proxima***

Described by Jean-Baptiste Émile Boudier in 1887 from French collections. The genus
*Laccaria* contains approximately 75 species worldwide, characterized by
long-spined basidia and amyloid [[spore]] ornamentation. *L. proxima* belongs to the
*Laccaria laccata* species complex, a group of morphologically similar species
differentiated by microscopic features and molecular phylogeny. Within this
complex, *L. proxima* is distinguished by its preference for willow associates
and coastal habitats.

### Synonyms

- *Laccaria laccata* var. *proxima* (Boud.) Kühner & Romagn. (1953)
- *Laccaria maritima* (Theodorow) Sing. (1943) — a coastal form sometimes
  treated as a separate species

## Morphology

- **Cap**: 1.5–6 cm diameter, convex becoming plano-convex with a depressed
  center. Surface dry, finely scaly to fibrillose, pinkish-brown to
  reddish-brown with paler margin. Hygrophanous — fading to ochraceous or
  tan when dry.
- **Gills**: Adnate to subdecurrent, moderately spaced, with frequent short
  gills (lamellulae). Color similar to cap or paler pinkish, sometimes with
  a lilac tinge when fresh.
- **Stipe**: 4–10 cm long, 3–8 mm thick, cylindrical to slightly clavate,
  fibrous, solid to hollow. Same color as cap or paler, often with a
  whitish mycelial base.
- **Flesh**: Thin, fibrous, pale pinkish-brown. Mild taste and odor.
- **Spore print**: White. **Spores**: Subglobose, prominently echinulate
  (spiny), 7–9.5 × 6.5–8.5 µm including spines. Spines 1–1.5 µm long.
  Spores are strongly amyloid (turning blue-black in Melzer's reagent).
- **Basidia**: Long and slender (40–60 µm), 4-spored, with prominent sterigmata.

## Distribution and Habitat

Widespread in temperate and boreal zones: Europe (especially common in coastal
areas of Scandinavia, the British Isles, and Atlantic France), North America
(both coasts), and parts of Asia. Found in sandy soils along coastlines,
dune systems, river gravel bars, alluvial plains, and reclaimed or disturbed
sites. The species is an obligate ectomycorrhizal associate of willows
(*Salix* spp.) and occasionally poplars (*Populus* spp.), birches (*Betula* spp.),
and alders (*Alnus* spp.). Fruiting from late summer through autumn (August–November).

## Mycorrhizal Biology

*Laccaria proxima* forms ectomycorrhizal associations that are ecologically
significant in several contexts:

- **Pioneer species**: As one of the first [[ectomycorrhizal-fungi]] to colonize
  disturbed or primary successional sites, *L. proxima* facilitates
  establishment of willow and other pioneer trees on nutrient-poor substrates.
- **Nutrient exchange**: The mycorrhizal mantle enhances host plant uptake of
  phosphorus, nitrogen, and micronutrients from poor soils in exchange for
  photosynthetically-derived carbon. This is particularly important in sand
  dune ecosystems where available phosphorus is limiting.
- **Stress tolerance**: *L. proxima* mycorrhizae confer enhanced drought
  tolerance and resistance to heavy metals (zinc, copper, lead) on host
  plants, making the association valuable in phytoremediation contexts.
- **Host specificity**: While primarily associated with *Salix* species,
  *L. proxima* shows moderate host breadth among early-successional trees,
  particularly in wet or sandy environments.

## Ecological Restoration and Permaculture

*Laccaria proxima* has direct applications in ecological restoration and
[[permaculture-design]]:

- **Mine site reclamation**: Inoculation of willow seedlings with *L. proxima*
  significantly improves survival and growth on heavy-metal-contaminated mine
  tailings and spoil heaps. The fungal mantle binds metals, reducing
  phytotoxicity while enabling [[plant-nutrient-uptake]].
- **Dune stabilization**: Willows mycorrhized with *L. proxima* establish
  more rapidly on coastal dunes, contributing to sand stabilization and
  facilitating succession to more diverse plant communities.
- **Riparian restoration**: The species naturally colonizes willow plantings
  along degraded waterways, accelerating root system development and nutrient
  cycling.
- **Food forest edges**: In permaculture systems, willow edges can be
  inoculated with *L. proxima* to improve willow vigor for coppice, basketry,
  or biomass production in marginal sandy soils.

## Edibility

*Laccaria proxima* is technically edible but rarely collected for food due to
its small size and tough, fibrous texture. The flavor is mild and not
particularly distinctive. Young specimens can be added to mixed mushroom
dishes, but more palatable species are usually preferred. As with all
wild-collected mushrooms, positive identification is essential. No toxic
lookalikes within the *Laccaria* genus exist, but confusion with small brown
mushrooms from other genera is possible for inexperienced foragers.

## Related Species

- ***Laccaria laccata***: Very similar; found under conifers and hardwoods in
  forest soils rather than sandy/willow habitats. Slightly larger spores.
- ***Laccaria tortilis***: Smaller species (cap 1–3 cm) found in similar
  habitats; more delicate stipe and more pronounced lilac tones.
- ***Laccaria bicolor***: North American species with distinctive violet-blue
  tones on cap and stipe; associated with conifers and hardwoods.
- ***[[laccaria-amethystina]]***: Larger, with distinctive deep violet color
  throughout; found in forest settings, not typically in sandy dunes.
