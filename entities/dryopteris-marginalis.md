Marginal wood fern is widely cultivated as a shade-garden ornamental, prized

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Dryopteris marginalis (L.) A. Gray was described by Asa Gray in 1848. The
basionym is Polypodium marginale L. (1753). It belongs to the family
Dryopteridaceae within the order Polypodiales.

Recent phylogenetic studies place D. marginalis in the "marginalis clade"
within Dryopteris subgenus Dryopteris. This clade includes several eastern
[[dmt-maoi-plants-united-states]].

The species occurs most abundantly in rich, rocky woodlands, especially on
north-facing slopes and in ravines with moist, well-drained soils. It is a
characteristic component of the herbaceous layer in mixed mesophytic forests
and oak-hickory associations.

It frequently colonizes rock crevices, cliff bases, talus slopes, and the
margins of limestone outcrops. Its preference for calcareous substrates
distinguishes it from several co-occurring Dryopteris species that favor
acidic soils.

Elevation ranges from near sea level to approximately 1,200 m in the southern
Appalachians. It is most abundant between 200 and 800 m elevation in regions
with moderate to high annual precipitation.
