## 4-HO-MPT — TIHKAL #23

- Synonyms: 3-[2-(methylpropylamino)ethyl]-4-indolol

- Class: Tryptamine, 4-hydroxy substituted, N-methyl-N-propyl

## Pharmacological Notes

Only a single trial exists, making it impossible to give a fair estimate
of dose or duration. Shulgin extrapolates that this material is likely active
in the psilocin range of approximately 10-20 mg orally.

A useful generalization emerges from the 4-hydroxy tryptamine series: anything
with up to six carbons attached to the tryptamine nitrogen atom (and bearing
a 4-hydroxy group) appears active in the 10-20 mg range orally. This holds
for methyl-methyl ([[det-entity-tihkal]]), dipropyl ([[psilocybin-tryptamine-chemistry-fungi]]. It states
that 4-hydroxytryptamines with six or fewer total carbons on the nitrogen
atom are active in the 10-20 mg range, while compounds exceeding this threshold
show dramatically reduced activity. 4-HO-MPT, with its four total nitrogen
carbons, falls comfortably within this range.

The compound's mixed N-alkyl pattern (one methyl, one propyl) versus symmetric
substitution (two identical groups) provides data on whether the specific
identity of alkyl groups matters beyond their total carbon count. The
available evidence suggests that the total carbon count is more important
than the specific arrangement, though subtle qualitative differences exist.

## Detailed Pharmacological Discussion

4-HO-MPT's single trial at 8 mg showed mild visual distortion and prominent
vertigo, with relatively rapid subsidence and residual insomnia. The vertigo
component is an interesting feature not typically prominent with psilocin,
and may reflect differential activity at vestibular-related serotonin
receptors or at 5-HT1A receptors (which can produce dizziness).

The residual insomnia lasting eight hours after a mild 8 mg experience is
noteworthy. In standard psilocin experiences, sleep is typically possible
4-6 hours after dosing. The extended sleep disruption could reflect slower
metabolic clearance of the propyl-containing compound compared to the
dimethyl (psilocin) or diethyl (4-HO-DET) variants.

4-HO-MPT presumably acts primarily as a 5-HT2A receptor agonist, though
the prominence of vertigo in the single trial may indicate additional
contributions from other receptor systems. The compound's pharmacological
profile remains largely uncharacterized beyond this single data point.
