# Backswimmer Mosquito Control

## Description

Backswimmers (family Notonectidae) are aquatic insects that swim upside-down near the water surface and are voracious predators of mosquito larvae. [[bill-mollison]] recommends them as one of two primary biological mosquito control methods in permaculture pond systems, alongside garlic oil treatment. Backswimmers are distinguished from assassin bugs and should be selectively introduced into garden ponds.

## Classification

- **Category**: Biological Pest Control Agent
- **Order**: Hemiptera
- **Family**: Notonectidae
- **Type**: Aquatic predatory insect
- **Permaculture Application**: Mosquito control in garden ponds

## Key Facts

- Backswimmers are flying insects that naturally colonize water bodies; if they do not arrive naturally, they can be collected and introduced manually.
- They prey on mosquito larvae but do not harm tadpoles, making them compatible with frog-based pest management.
- Mollison specifies using small backswimmers, not large ones, and specifically not assassin bugs.
- Part of an integrated mosquito control strategy: garlic oil kills larvae, backswimmers consume survivors, and frogs (from tadpoles in the same pond) provide additional predation.
- A standard permaculture pond design (4 feet across, 18 inches deep) can produce 200-300 frogs twice per summer, with backswimmers controlling mosquitoes between frog generations.
- Mollison references a mosquito control standard design written by a Ph.D. in mosquito control that was never formally published.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Pond Ecosystems**: Healthy pond ecosystems support fungal and microbial diversity in surrounding soils through water infiltration and organic matter cycling.
- **Integrated Design**: Mosquito-free pond systems make water features practical near mushroom cultivation areas and composting operations.
- **Biodiversity**: Backswimmers are part of a complex food web that supports overall ecosystem health, including soil biota.
- **Outdoor Cultivation**: Outdoor mushroom beds and log cultures benefit from proximity to well-managed water features without pest problems.
