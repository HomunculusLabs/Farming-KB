## Gentiana purpurea

## Overview

Gentiana purpurea L. is a tall, robust perennial gentian native to the mountains of central and southern Europe. It is notable for its dark purple to reddish-brown flowers, which stand in contrast to the more typical blue gentians.

Flowering occurs late in the season, from July to September, making it one of the last gentians to bloom in Alpine meadows. The species can reach 30–60 cm in height, far exceeding most alpine gentians.

It is sometimes called the "purple gentian" or "autumn gentian" due to its unusual flower color and late bloom time. The species has a long history of use as a bittering agent in traditional herbal liqueurs.

The specific epithet "purpurea" refers to the distinctive purple coloration of the flowers. Linnaeus described the species in 1753 in his Species Plantarum.
