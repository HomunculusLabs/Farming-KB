## Description

Tessellation in [[edge-effect]]

## Key Facts

- Originates from geometry: the tiling of a plane with repeating shapes (triangles, hexagons, squares) without gaps
- Applied to landscape design as the formation or arranging of mosaic patterns of units, functions, and species
- Can apply to:
  - **Spatial tessellation:** Arranging garden beds, paddocks, or plantings in repeating patterns
  - **Functional tessellation:** Distributing functions (water points, compost, access) in repeating patterns across a site
  - **Cyclic tessellation:** Arranging successions, rotations, and temporal cycles in repeating sequences
- Maximizes edge between different units — and edge is where biological activity is greatest
- Reduces distance between complementary elements, improving efficiency of energy and material flows
- Creates redundancy and resilience through repeating functional units

## Geometric Basis

Common tessellation patterns applicable to landscape design:

- **Hexagonal:** Most efficient packing (honeycomb pattern); maximizes area-to-perimeter ratio; ideal for orchard and [[keyline-design]]:** Keyline geometry is a form of contour-based tessellation
