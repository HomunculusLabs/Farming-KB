## Elsholtzia ciliata

## Overview

**Elsholtzia ciliata** (Thunb.) Hyl., commonly known as **Vietnamese balm**,
**Chinese mosla**, or **creeping mint**, is an aromatic annual herb in the
family Lamiaceae. Native to East and [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]], and [[aact-foliar-sticker-spreaders-leaf-surface-attachment-ingham]] is green and
sparsely hairy; the lower surface is grayish-green with denser pubescence.

Inflorescences are terminal, spike-like thyrses, 2–8 cm long, with densely
crowded whorls of small flowers. Calyx is campanulate, 5-toothed, ciliate.
Corolla is bilabiate, 4–5 mm long, pale purple to pinkish-white.

The fruit is a small nutlet (mericarp), approximately 1 mm long, ovoid,
smooth, and dark brown at maturity.

## Distribution and Habitat

*E. ciliata* is native to China, Japan, Korea, Mongolia, eastern Siberia, and
the Himalayan region. It is naturalized across much of temperate Europe,

## Traditional and Culinary Uses

In East Asian cuisines, the fresh leaves and young shoots are used as a
flavoring herb similar to mint or basil. They are added to soups, stir-fries,
salads, and pickled dishes. The lemon-mint flavor pairs well with fish,
poultry, and vegetable preparations.

In traditional Chinese medicine, *E. ciliata* is classified as pungent and
warm, associated with the Lung and Bladder meridians. It is used to dispel
wind-cold, promote sweating, and relieve summer-heat conditions. The herb is
a common ingredient in cold and flu remedies.

In Korean traditional medicine, the plant is used to treat headaches,
abdominal pain, and diarrhea. The dried aerial parts are brewed as a tea or
prepared as a medicinal decoction.
