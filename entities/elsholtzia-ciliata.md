# Elsholtzia ciliata

## Overview

**Elsholtzia ciliata** (Thunb.) Hyl., commonly known as **Vietnamese balm**,
**Chinese mosla**, or **creeping mint**, is an aromatic annual herb in the
family Lamiaceae. Native to East and [[psilocybe-southeast-asia-pacific]], it is widely used as a
culinary herb, traditional medicine, and insect-repellent companion plant.

The plant has a distinctive lemony-mint aroma when crushed and is naturalized
in parts of Europe, [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]], and [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-south-america]] following intentional
introduction for its medicinal and aromatic properties.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:** Kingdom Plantae → Division Tracheophyta → Class
Magnoliopsida → Order Lamiales → Family Lamiaceae → Subfamily Nepetoideae →
Genus *Elsholtzia* → *E. ciliata*.

**Synonyms:** *Elsholtzia patrini* (Lepech.) Garcke, *Aphanochilus ciliatus*
(Thunb.) Kudô, *Perilla elsholtzioides* Benth.

The plant is an erect annual herb, 20–60 cm tall, with square stems typical
of the mint family. Stems are green to purplish, densely pubescent with
ciliate hairs, and branched in the upper portions.

Leaves are opposite, ovate to lanceolate, 2–8 cm long and 1–4 cm wide, with
serrate margins and a ciliate leaf edge. The upper [[aact-foliar-sticker-spreaders-leaf-surface-attachment-ingham]] is green and
sparsely hairy; the lower surface is grayish-green with denser pubescence.

Inflorescences are terminal, spike-like thyrses, 2–8 cm long, with densely
crowded whorls of small flowers. Calyx is campanulate, 5-toothed, ciliate.
Corolla is bilabiate, 4–5 mm long, pale purple to pinkish-white.

The fruit is a small nutlet (mericarp), approximately 1 mm long, ovoid,
smooth, and dark brown at maturity.

## Distribution and Habitat

*E. ciliata* is native to China, Japan, Korea, Mongolia, eastern Siberia, and
the Himalayan region. It is naturalized across much of temperate Europe,
[[polypore-fungi-eastern-north-america]] America, and parts of South America and New Zealand.

The species colonizes disturbed habitats including roadsides, riverbanks,
field margins, waste ground, and forest clearings. It prefers moist,
well-drained soils in partial shade to full sun, and is tolerant of a wide
range of soil types including sandy loams and clay soils.

Elevation range extends from sea level to approximately 2,500 m in its native
Himalayan distribution. The plant is frost-sensitive and completes its life
cycle within a single [[extending-growing-season]] [[query-how-to-grow-and-use-oca-in-temperate-climates]].

## Traditional and Culinary Uses

In East Asian cuisines, the fresh leaves and young shoots are used as a
flavoring herb similar to mint or basil. They are added to soups, stir-fries,
salads, and pickled dishes. The lemon-mint flavor pairs well with fish,
poultry, and vegetable preparations.

In traditional Chinese medicine, *E. ciliata* is classified as pungent and
warm, associated with the Lung and Bladder meridians. It is used to dispel
wind-cold, promote sweating, and relieve summer-heat conditions. The herb is
a common ingredient in cold and flu remedies.

In Korean traditional medicine, the plant is used to treat headaches,
abdominal pain, and diarrhea. The dried aerial parts are brewed as a tea or
prepared as a medicinal decoction.
