## Description

**Paeciliomyces lilacinus** (also reclassified as *[[purpureocillium-lilacinum]]*) is a filamentous fungus in the phylum Ascomycota. It is a cosmopolitan soil fungus known primarily as an entomopathogenic species and nematode egg parasite. In the context of [[fungal-ecology]] around the Chernobyl nuclear disaster, P. lilacinus was identified as a **bioindicator species for high levels of radionuclide contamination** in woodland soil ecosystems.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Sordariomycetes
- **Order:** Hypocreales
- **Family:** Ophiocordycipitaceae
- **Genus:** Purpureocillium (formerly Paeciliomyces)
- **Species:** P. lilacinum (syn. P. lilacinus)

## Key Facts

- **Bioindicator for radionuclide contamination:** Identified by Zhdanova et al. (1995) as an indicator species of high-level radionuclide contamination in woodland soil ecosystems around Chernobyl.
- **Co-indicator species:** Alongside Chaetomium aureum, P. lilacinus serves as a marker for the most heavily contaminated soils, enabling ecological characterization of radioactive contamination gradients.
- **Part of the Chernobyl monitoring effort:** Included in the broader survey of approximately 2000 strains of 180 species across 92 genera isolated and cultured during 15 years of monitoring fungal communities around Chernobyl.
- **Nematode biological control:** Beyond its role in radiation ecology, the species is widely studied and commercially used as a biological control agent against plant-parasitic nematodes, attacking their eggs and juveniles.

## Ecological Role

P. lilacinus is a common soil saprotroph with significant entomopathogenic and nematophagous capabilities. It colonizes soil organic matter and prey upon nematode eggs, making it an important component of soil biological control. Its presence in elevated numbers at highly radioactive sites demonstrates adaptation to or tolerance of ionizing radiation.

## Relevance to Mycology and Cultivation

- **Bioindicator utility:** Can be used in ecological surveys to identify zones of high radioactive contamination based on fungal community composition.
- **Nematode control in cultivation:** Widely used as a biocontrol agent against root-knot nematodes (Meloidogyne spp.) and cyst nematodes in agricultural and horticultural settings.
- **Radiation ecology research:** Serves as a model for understanding fungal community responses to anthropogenic radioactive stress.

## Distinguishing Features

- Lilac to purple colony coloration on culture media
- Verticilliate conidiophores producing chains of ellipsoidal conidia
- Strong nematode egg parasitism
- Indicator of high-level radioactive soil contamination
