## Lime Sulphur (Agricultural Use)

## Description

Lime sulphur (calcium polysulfide) is a traditional agricultural fungicide and insecticide made by boiling calcium hydroxide (lime) with elemental sulfur. Elaine Ingham's field guide addresses lime sulphur as a chemical that is biologically detrimental to beneficial soil organisms, with significant implications for biological farming and compost tea programs.

## Classification

- **Category**: Agricultural chemical - fungicide/insecticide
- **Chemical name**: Calcium polysulfide (CaSx)
- **Type**: Inorganic compound
- **Application**: Foliar spray and dormant season application
- **Impact**: Biologically detrimental to soil food web organisms

## Key Facts

- **Composition**: Made from calcium hydroxide (lime) and sulfur, producing calcium polysulfide solution
- **Broad toxicity**: Toxic to both fungal pathogens and beneficial fungi, including mycorrhizal species
- **Alkaline pH**: Highly alkaline solution that can significantly alter leaf surface and soil pH
- **Traditional use**: One of the oldest fungicides still in use, particularly in orchard and vineyard management
- **Dormant application**: Commonly applied during dormancy to control overwintering pests and diseases
- **Growing season**: Also applied during growing season at lower concentrations, but with greater risk to beneficial organisms

## Impact on Soil and Plant Biology

### Negative Effects
- **Fungal damage**: Kills beneficial saprophytic and mycorrhizal fungi alongside pathogenic species
- **Bacterial disruption**: High pH and sulfide toxicity affect bacterial communities
- **Protozoan reduction**: Protozoan populations are reduced, impairing nutrient cycling
- **Leaf surface biology**: Destroys the phyllosphere community of beneficial organisms on leaf surfaces
- **Soil food web**: Repeated applications degrade the overall [[soil-food-web-structure]]

### Specific Concerns in Vineyards
- Vineyard applications are particularly problematic because vines rely heavily on mycorrhizal partnerships for nutrient and water uptake
- Sulfur and copper combinations (Bordeaux mixture + lime sulfur) create compounding toxicity
- Fermentation quality in vineyards can be affected by residual biological damage

## Bio Lime Sulphur Alternative

Ingham references a "Bio Lime Sulphur" approach - a modified formulation or application strategy that attempts to reduce harm to beneficial organisms while maintaining disease control efficacy. Key principles include:

- **Reduced rates**: Using the minimum effective concentration
- **Timing**: Applying when beneficial organism populations can recover most quickly
- **Biological follow-up**: Applying compost tea after lime sulphur to restore beneficial biology
- **Integrated approach**: Combining reduced chemical use with biological disease suppression

## Relevance to Compost Tea and Biological Farming

### The Conflict
Lime sulphur represents a fundamental conflict in biological farming: it is effective against certain diseases but destructive to the beneficial organisms that provide natural disease suppression.

### Integration Strategy
1. **Minimize use**: Use lime sulphur only when absolutely necessary
2. **Time applications**: Apply during dormancy when impact on active biology is reduced
3. **Follow with biology**: Apply AACT 1-2 weeks after lime sulphur to restore beneficial organisms
4. **Monitor biology**: Use soil and foliar biology testing to assess recovery
5. **Transition**: Gradually reduce lime sulphur reliance as biological disease suppression improves

### Comparison with Other Vineyard Chemicals

| Chemical | Fungal Impact | Bacterial Impact | Persistence |
|----------|--------------|------------------|-------------|
| Lime sulphur | High toxicity | Moderate toxicity | Low-moderate |
| Copper compounds | High toxicity | Moderate toxicity | High (accumulates) |
| Sulfur (alone) | Moderate toxicity | Low toxicity | Low |
| Kaligreen (potassium bicarbonate) | Low-moderate | Low | Very low |

## Application Considerations

- **No spraying during blossom**: Critical to avoid applications during flowering to protect pollinators and young tissue
- **Temperature sensitivity**: Apply when temperatures are moderate; high temperatures increase phytotoxicity risk
- **Coverage**: Thorough coverage is needed for disease control but increases biological impact
- **Re-entry intervals**: Observe proper re-entry intervals after application

## Safety and Environmental Notes

- **PPE required**: Protective equipment essential during mixing and application
- **Corrosive**: Highly corrosive to metal equipment
- **Storage**: Must be stored in sealed containers; degrades when exposed to air
- **Waterways**: Toxic to aquatic organisms; prevent runoff into waterways
