# Leonotis nepetifolia

## Overview

**Leonotis nepetifolia**, commonly known as **klip dagga** or **lion's ear**,
is an annual or short-lived perennial herbaceous plant in the family
Lamiaceae. It is closely related to the better-known *L. leonurus* (wild
dagga) but is more widely distributed across tropical and subtropical
regions globally.

The plant is recognized in African traditional medicine for its

## Taxonomy [[trichome-development-and-morphology]]

### Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Division:** Angiosperms
- **Class:** Eudicots
- **Order:** Lamiales
- **Family:** Lamiaceae
- **Subfamily:** Nepetoideae
- **Tribe:** Mentheae
- **Genus:** *Leonotis*
- **Species:** *L. nepetifolia* (L.) R.Br. (1810)

The basionym is *Phlomis nepetifolia* L., published by Linnaeus in 1753.
Robert Brown transferred it to *Leonotis* in 1810.

The genus name derives from Greek *leon* (lion) and *otis* (ear),
referring to the resemblance of the whorled flower heads to a lion's
ear. The specific epithet *nepetifolia* means "with leaves like catnip"
(*Nepeta*), referencing the similar leaf morphology.

### Morphology

**Growth Habit:** Erect annual or short-lived perennial, 0.5–3 m tall.
Stems are square (typical of Lamiaceae), branching, covered in fine
pubescence.

**Leaves:** Opposite, ovate to broadly lanceolate, 5–15 cm long and
3–8 cm wide, with serrate margins. Surface rough-textured with
glandular trichomes that release an aromatic scent when crushed.

**Flowers:** Produced in dense whorls in the axils of reduced
bracteal leaves, forming conspicuous spherical clusters along the
stem. Individual flowers are 25–35 mm long, tubular, bright orange
to reddish-orange, bilabiate with a prominent curved upper lip.

**Fruit:** Schizocarp splitting into four small nutlets at maturity,
each approximately 2 mm long, dark brown to black.

**Root:** Fibrous taproot system, relatively shallow.

## Distribution and Habitat

*L. nepetifolia* is native to tropical Africa but has naturalized
widely across [[mollison-pasture-legumes-and-forage-legumes-of-the-tropics]] and subtropics, including Central and South
America, South and Southeast Asia, and the Pacific Islands.

It grows as a weed in disturbed areas, roadsides, cultivated fields,
pastures, and forest margins, typically at elevations below 1,500 m.

The plant thrives in full sun to partial shade and tolerates a wide
range of soil types, from sandy loams to heavy clays, provided
drainage is adequate. It is moderately drought-tolerant once
established but performs best with regular moisture.

In temperate regions it is grown as a warm-season annual, often
self-seeding prolifically in suitable conditions.

## Traditional and Medicinal Uses

In African traditional medicine, *L. nepetifolia* has a broad range
of reported applications:

- **Respiratory conditions:** Leaf and flower preparations used as
  expectorants for coughs, bronchitis, and asthma.
- **Pain and inflammation:** Topical application of leaf poultices
  for headaches, arthritis, and muscle pain.
- **Antimicrobial:** Leaf extracts used topically for wound healing
  and skin infections.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Leaf infusions consumed for stomach aches,
  diarrhea, and dysentery.
- **Psychoactive:** Dried flowers smoked as a mild cannabis substitute
  in parts of southern Africa and the Caribbean. Effects are subtle,
  described as mildly relaxing rather than intoxicating.

The smoking of *Leonotis* flowers is documented in Zulu, Xhosa, and
Khoisan ethnobotanical traditions, where it was sometimes mixed with
other herbs.

## Bioactive Compounds

Phytochemical analysis has identified several notable constituents:

- **Labiatae diterpenoids:** Including leonotin, leonotinin, and
  related compounds with reported anti-inflammatory activity.
- **Iridoid glycosides:** Including leonuride, which may contribute
  to the plant's mild relaxant effects.
- **Flavonoids:** Including luteolin, apigenin, and their
  glycosides, with documented antioxidant activity.
- **Essential oil:** Contains α-pinene, β-pinene, limonene,
  and humulene; contributes to the aromatic character of the
  foliage and flowers.

No significant cannabinoid-like compounds have been isolated from
*L. nepetifolia*. The mild psychoactive effects reported from smoking
the flowers are attributed to the combined action of iridoid
glycosides and terpenoids on GABAergic pathways.

## Cultivation and Permaculture Applications

*L. nepetifolia* is easily grown from seed, which germinates readily
in 1–2 weeks at temperatures above 20°C. Direct sowing after the
last frost is recommended.
