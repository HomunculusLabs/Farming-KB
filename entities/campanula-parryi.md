# Campanula parryi

## Overview

Campanula parryi, commonly known as Parry's bellflower, is a perennial herbaceous plant in the family Campanulaceae. It is native to the Rocky Mountains and Intermountain West [[extending-growing-season]] length.

## Cultivation

Campanula parryi is cultivated in native plant and rock gardens, primarily in western North America. It requires full sun and sharply drained, gravelly soil with a slightly acidic to neutral pH.

Propagation is by seed, which benefits from 6–8 weeks of cold stratification at 0–4°C. Germination rates are moderate (40–60%) and seedlings develop slowly in their first year.

Plants are hardy to USDA zones 3–6 and are well adapted to the cold, dry winters of continental climates. In cultivation, they are relatively short-lived, typically persisting 3–4 years.

Division of mature clumps in early spring can propagate established plants. The species has no serious pest or disease problems when grown in appropriate conditions with good drainage and [[growing-gourmet-north-american-reishi-species]] gardens. It is best suited to xeric rock gardens and alpine troughs.

The species has not been widely commercialized and remains primarily a collector's plant. A few native plant nurseries in Colorado and Utah offer seed-grown plants.

## Ecological Role

The flowers are pollinated by native bumblebees (Bombus spp.) and small [[clay-pellet-seed-dispersal]] occurs through passive release from poricidal capsules. Wind and water are the primary dispersal vectors across alpine scree and meadow habitats.
