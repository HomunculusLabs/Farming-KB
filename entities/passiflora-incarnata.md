# Passiflora incarnata

**Type:** Species (Vine)
**Category:** Medicinal fruit vine
**Family:** Passifloraceae
**Also known as:** Maypop, apricot vine, wild passionflower

## Overview
Passiflora incarnata is a perennial climbing vine native to the
southeastern United States. It is the most cold-tolerant of all
passionfruit species, dying back to the ground in winter and shooting
new growth from the roots in spring. Beyond its edible fruit, the
plant is widely valued for its medicinal properties, particularly as
a mild sedative and tranquilliser. The name "maypop" comes from the
loud popping sound the ripe fruits make when stepped on.

## Taxonomy
- **Order:** Malpighiales
- **Family:** Passifloraceae
- **Genus:** Passiflora
- **Species:** P. incarnata
- **Common relatives:** P. edulis (purple passionfruit), P.
  caerulea (blue crown passionflower), P. quadrangularis (giant
  granadilla)

## Key Details
- **Growth habit:** Rampant perennial vine; dies back in winter,
  regrows from roots in spring; can cover large areas quickly
- **Cold tolerance:** Most cold-hardy passionfruit; dormant roots
  survive freezing winters while aerial parts die back completely
- **Root propagation:** Disturbed roots readily send out new
  shoots, making the plant easy to propagate vegetatively and
  also potentially weedy in garden settings
- **Fruit:** Edible, eaten raw or made into jellies, jams, fruit
  salads, and wine; juice makes a refreshing drink
- **Leaves:** Eaten raw in salads and cooked as a green vegetable
- **Flowers:** Strikingly ornamental; made into syrup and eaten as
  a vegetable
- **Medicinal (primary value):** Contains passiflorine, a glycoside
  with sedative and tranquilliser properties used in
  pharmaceutical preparations; widely prescribed for anxiety,
  insomnia, and nervous tension in [[herbal-medicine]]
- **Landscape function:** Vigorous ground cover; useful for erosion
  control on banks and slopes; ornamental flowers attract
  butterflies (especially Gulf fritillary)

## Cultivation
Extremely easy to grow from root cuttings or division; disturbed
roots readily produce new plants. Requires support for climbing
(such as trellises, fences, or other plants). Grows well in a wide
range of soils and conditions, from sandy to clay. In colder
climates the aerial parts die back each winter but the root system
is fully hardy. Can become aggressive in favourable conditions and
may need management to prevent spreading beyond desired areas.

## Related Passiflora Species
- **P. edulis:** Purple passionfruit, subtropical, commercial
  fruit crop; less cold hardy than P. incarnata
- **P. caerulea:** Blue crown passionflower, vigorous and hardy,
  used as rootstock for P. edulis but can become weedy
- **P. quadrangularis:** Giant granadilla, largest passionfruit
  (up to 30 cm long), tropical, roots eaten like yams
- **P. ligularis:** Sweet granadilla, orange-yellow fruit,
  subtropical
- **P. moliissima:** Banana passionfruit, native to Andes, best
  tasting juice of all passionfruit

## Relevance
Maypop is a dual-purpose plant valuable in permaculture for both
food and medicine. Its exceptional cold tolerance extends the range
of passionfruit cultivation well beyond tropical and subtropical
zones into temperate areas. The sedative properties of passiflorine
make it an important medicinal herb for home gardens and herbal
medicine systems. As a native North American species, it supports
local ecology and pollinator populations.
