## Overview

Biscotti is a potent indica-dominant hybrid cannabis strain created by the Cookies Fam breeding collective, credited to Berner and the Cookies team in the San Francisco Bay Area. It emerged in the early 2010s as part of the wave of Cookies-family crosses that defined the modern West Coast cannabis market.

The strain has earned a reputation for its exceptional resin production, dense bud structure, and complex dessert-like aroma. With THC content routinely testing between 25–30%, Biscotti ranks among the heaviest hitters in the contemporary cannabis landscape.

Biscotti has won or placed in multiple cannabis competitions, including the Emerald Cup, and has become a highly sought-after parent strain for further crosses. Its genetics have been used to create popular derivatives such as Biscotti Sherbet and Jetlato.

The name derives from the Italian twice-baked cookie, referencing the strain's sweet, nutty, and slightly earthy flavor profile that evokes baked goods. The Cookies branding and limited-release drops have elevated Biscotti to a boutique status in licensed dispensaries.

As a multi-generational Cookies cross, Biscotti represents the culmination of decades of West Coast cannabis breeding. It embodies the shift from older landrace-based genetics toward purpose-built boutique varieties optimized for both potency and sensory appeal.

## Lineage

Biscotti is a three-way cross of Girl Scout Cookies (GSC), Gelato #25, and South Florida OG. This combination brings together the Cookie family's signature sweet terpene profile with the OG lineage's potency and gas-like undertones.

Girl Scout Cookies contributes the complex Cookie terpene foundation — sweet, earthy, and slightly pungent notes that define the family. Gelato #25 adds creamy, dessert-like sweetness and a purple-leaning bud coloration, while South Florida OG provides the heavy sedative body effects and elevated THC production.

The exact ratios of each parent in the cross are not publicly disclosed by the Cookies breeding program. Phenotypic variation among Biscotti seeds is moderate, with some expressions leaning more Cookie-dominant and others showing stronger OG influence in both morphology and effect.

Biscotti itself has been used as a parent in numerous second-generation crosses. Notable derivatives include Biscotti Sherbet (Biscotti × Sherbet), Biscotti Sundae (Biscotti × Ice Cream Cake), and multiple Gelato backcrosses that incorporate Biscotti traits.

The South Florida OG parent is a relatively rare cut within the broader OG family, distinguished by its tropical climate adaptation and particularly pungent fuel aroma. Its inclusion in the Biscotti cross distinguishes it from other Cookies-family hybrids that rely more heavily on Northern California OG cuts.

Some breeders have speculated that the South Florida OG parent may carry Afghani landrace genetics introduced to Florida during the 1970s and 1980s. This would explain its unusual heat tolerance and the intensity of its sedative effects relative to other OG phenotypes.

## Appearance

Biscotti produces medium to large-sized buds that are exceptionally dense and tightly packed, characteristic of its indica-dominant genetics. The calyxes are small and tightly clustered, giving the buds a rock-hard consistency that requires a quality grinder.

Coloration ranges from deep forest green to dark purple, with the purple hues intensifying in cooler growing conditions. Bright orange pistils stand out sharply against the dark foliage, often curling and receding into the resinous bud structure as maturity approaches.

Trichome coverage is outstanding — the buds appear frosted or sugar-coated, with a thick layer of cloudy white to amber glandular trichomes. This heavy resin production makes Biscotti a popular choice for solvent-based and solventless concentrate extraction, yielding high returns of hash, rosin, and BHO.

### Visual Distinction from Similar Cookies Strains

Biscotti can be visually differentiated from its parent Gelato #25 by its darker overall coloration and tighter bud structure. While Gelato tends toward lighter green with lavender accents, Biscotti displays deeper purples and more saturated forest green tones.

Compared to Girl Scout Cookies, Biscotti buds are generally larger and more elongated, with a more pronounced trichome frosting. The pistil density is also higher, giving mature Biscotti buds a more textured, speckled appearance.

## Aroma and Flavor

The aroma is complex and dessert-like, dominated by sweet cookie and nutty notes with undertones of diesel fuel and earthy spice. On the initial sniff, users typically detect a bakery sweetness reminiscent of almond cookies or vanilla, followed by a pungent, gassy backend.

Breaking apart the buds releases a more pronounced diesel and funk aroma, particularly in phenotypes with stronger OG influence. The terpene bouquet is layered: sweet on top, pungent underneath, with an earthy, slightly herbal finish.

The flavor closely mirrors the aroma but tends toward the sweeter end of the spectrum. On inhalation, Biscotti delivers a smooth, cookie-like sweetness with vanilla and almond notes. The exhale brings out earthy, spicy, and slightly diesel-like flavors that linger on the palate.

When consumed as a concentrate (rosin or hash), the flavor profile concentrates and sweetens further. Live resin extractions in particular preserve the volatile terpene compounds responsible for the cookie-like top notes, producing a more vivid and aromatic experience than flower alone.

## Terpene and Cannabinoid Profile

The dominant terpenes in Biscotti are caryophyllene, limonene, and myrcene, with secondary contributions from linalool and humulene. Caryophyllene contributes the spicy, peppery undertones and also acts as a CB2 receptor agonist, adding to the strain's anti-inflammatory potential.

Limonene provides the citrus-sweet top notes and is associated with mood elevation and stress relief. Myrcene, the most abundant terpene in many cannabis varieties, contributes sedative body effects and enhances the overall potency through the entourage effect.

THC content typically ranges from 25–30%, with some premium phenotypes testing above 30%. CBD is present in trace amounts only (below 0.1%), making Biscotti a THC-dominant strain with minimal CBD-mediated effects.

Minor cannabinoids including CBG and THCV may be present in small quantities, depending on cultivation conditions and harvest timing. The overall cannabinoid-terpene synergy produces a powerful, multifaceted psychoactive experience.

### Terpene Synergy and Entourage Effects

The combination of caryophyllene and linalool in Biscotti is particularly noteworthy, as these two terpenes have demonstrated anxiolytic and analgesic properties in preclinical studies. Their co-occurrence may partially explain the strain's reported therapeutic efficacy for anxiety and pain.

The high limonene content also modulates the absorption and activity of other terpenes and cannabinoids. This terpene-terpene interaction is a key component of the entourage effect and may contribute to Biscotti's uniquely balanced cerebral and somatic effects.

## Effects and Cultivation

Effects onset is relatively rapid, beginning with a cerebral euphoria that elevates mood and can produce mild visual amplification. The initial head high transitions into a deeply relaxing body stone that can be heavily sedative at higher doses.

Biscotti is well-suited for evening or nighttime use due to its strong sedative properties. Medical patients commonly use it for chronic pain management, insomnia, anxiety, and appetite stimulation. Novice consumers should approach with caution due to the high THC content.

Flowering time indoors is approximately 8–9 weeks, with yields of 12–16 oz per square meter under optimal conditions. The strain performs best in controlled indoor environments with consistent temperatures (68–80 °F) and moderate humidity (45–55% RH) during flowering.

Biscotti is considered moderately difficult to cultivate, requiring attention to nutrient management and environmental control. The dense bud structure makes it susceptible to bud rot in high-humidity environments, so adequate airflow and dehumidification are essential during late flowering.

Training techniques such as low-stress training (LST) and scrogging are recommended to maximize light penetration and yield. The strain responds well to defoliation during the early flowering stretch, which helps manage humidity and exposes bud sites to more light.

### Side Effects

Common side effects include dry mouth and dry eyes, typical of high-THC cannabis varieties. Some users report mild dizziness or paranoia at higher doses, particularly those with low THC tolerance or predisposition to anxiety.

The sedative effects can be pronounced enough to cause couch-lock in less experienced consumers. Operating vehicles or machinery after consuming Biscotti is strongly discouraged. As with any high-potency cannabis strain, starting with a small dose and waiting 15–30 minutes before re-dosing is advisable.
