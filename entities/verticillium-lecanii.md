# Verticillium lecanii

**Type:** Species (Fungus)
**Category:** Entomopathogenic and Mycoparasitic Hyphomycete
**Also known as:** Lecanicillium lecanii (current classification)

## Overview
Verticillium lecanii is a fungus with dual ecological roles as an
entomopathogen (insect pathogen) and a mycoparasite of fungal plant
pathogens. It has been reclassified taxonomically and is now placed in
the genus Lecanicillium within the Cordycipitaceae family. The species
is widely used as a [[bloomfield-fungal-plant-pathogens]] (particularly [[singh-entomopathogenic-fungi-biocontrol-agents]], occurrence as a fungal
endophyte in living bark, association with aphid galls, and its
mycoparasitic activity against powdery mildews.

## Taxonomy and Classification
- **Kingdom:** Fungi

- **Class:** Sordariomycetes

## Key Details

### Morphology
- Colonies are slow to moderately growing, white to pale
  yellow, with a velvety to cottony texture
- Conidiophores are erect, bearing whorls (verticils) of
  3 to 5 phialides at each node
- Phialides are slender, acuminate (tapering to a point),
  producing conidia in slimy heads
- Conidia are hyaline, ellipsoidal to cylindrical,
  approximately 2.5 to 6.0 by 1.0 to 2.0 micrometers
- Conidial masses may appear pinkish or salmon-colored in
  mass
- Produces characteristic verticillate (whorled) branching
  pattern on conidiophores
- Some strains produce chlamydospores or [[fukuoka-natural-farming-in-tropical-regions]]

### Entomopathogenic Activity
- Infects and kills aphids, whiteflies, thrips, scale insects,
  and other soft-bodied arthropod pests
- Conidia adhere to insect cuticle, germinate, and penetrate
  using [[dry-gardening-unirrigated-vegetable-production-solomon]]
- Effective against greenhouse whitefly (Trialeurodes
  vaporariorum) and various aphid species

### Mycoparasitic Activity
- The nonspecific mycoparasite [[lecanicillium-muscarium]],
  formerly identified as V. lecanii, commonly inhabits
  powdery mildews and kills conidia (Heintz and Blaich 1990)
- Interacts with hyphae of Rhizoctonia solani through
  appressed growth, coiling, and penetration (Kuter 1984)
- Belongs to a complex of Verticillium and Lecanicillium
  species that interact with other fungi via diverse
  mechanisms
- May act as either insect parasite or mycoparasite
  depending on environmental context
