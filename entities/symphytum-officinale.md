# Symphytum officinale (Comfrey)

## Overview

**Comfrey** (*Symphytum officinale*) is a perennial herb in the borage family (Boraginaceae), widely regarded as one of the most valuable plants in **permaculture design** and organic gardening. Renowned for its deep taproot that mines minerals from deep subsoil layers, comfrey serves as nutrient accumulator, mulch crop, compost activator, medicinal herb, livestock forage, and bee plant. It has been called the **"wonder plant"** of permaculture.

**Note**: The commonly cultivated **Bocking 14** (*S. × uplandicum*, a sterile hybrid of *S. officinale* × *S. asperum*) is preferred for permaculture — it won't self-seed invasively and produces superior biomass.

## Description

Clump-forming perennial, 60–150 cm tall. Large, broadly lanceolate leaves (15–40 cm), hairy and rough. Bell-shaped purple-blue flowers in cymose clusters (May–July). Extremely deep taproot reaching **2–3 meters**, with thick black branching roots that penetrate compacted subsoil. Long-lived (10+ years); USDA zones 3–9.

## Dynamic Accumulation

Comfrey concentrates nutrients from deep subsoil layers inaccessible to most plants, storing them in bioavailable form in its leaves:

| Nutrient | Content (per 100g dry weight) | Significance |
|----------|------------------------------|--------------|
| **Potassium (K)** | 3.0–4.5% | Exceptionally high; comparable to commercial K fertilizers |
| **Nitrogen (N)** | 1.5–3.0% | High protein; comparable to legume leaves |
| **Calcium (Ca)** | 1.5–2.0% | Cell wall strength and soil pH buffering |
| **Phosphorus (P)** | 0.3–0.8% | Supports root development |
| **Magnesium (Mg)** | 0.3–0.5% | Chlorophyll and enzyme function |

**Comparison**: Comfrey leaves (~3% N, 0.8% P, 4% K) concentrate potassium at ~8× the rate of farmyard manure (~0.6% N, 0.3% P, 0.5% K).

## Permaculture Functions

### 1. Mulch Crop ("Chop and Drop")
Cut 3–5 times per growing season and laid as mulch. Rapidly decomposing leaves release nutrients and suppress weeds. Typical yields: 2–5 kg fresh leaves per plant per cut = **8–25 kg/plant/year**.

### 2. Compost Activator
Added to compost piles, comfrey provides nitrogen and moisture to accelerate microbial decomposition. Layer with carbon-rich materials for optimal C:N ratios.

### 3. Liquid Feed
- **Aerobic tea**: Steep leaves in water 2–4 weeks. Dilute 1:10.
- **Anaerobic (NOG)**: Pack leaves in container with weight, no water. Ferment 4–6 weeks. Dilute 1:20. Potent potassium-rich feed — excellent for tomatoes, peppers, fruit trees.

### 4. Soil Remediation
- Deep taproot breaks compacted soil, improves drainage and aeration
- Brings minerals from 2–3m depth to topsoil via leaf drop and decomposition
- Some evidence of heavy metal accumulation (useful for phytoremediation, concern for medicinal use)

### 5. Pollinator & Beneficial Insect Support
Long flowering period provides nectar for bumblebees; attracts predatory insects (hoverflies, parasitic wasps).

### 6. Livestock Forage & Erosion Control
High protein content (20–30% dry matter) — fed to poultry, rabbits, goats, and pigs. Dense root network stabilizes banks and waterways.

## Medicinal Use & Safety

### Traditional Uses
- **Wound healing**: Poultice for cuts, burns, sprains, fractures ("knitbone")
- **Anti-inflammatory**: Arthritis, joint pain, bruises
- **Skin conditions**: Eczema, psoriasis, acne

### Active Compounds
- **Allantoin** (0.3–1.0%): Stimulates cell proliferation, wound healing
- **Rosmarinic acid**: Anti-inflammatory, antioxidant
- **Mucilage**: Soothing barrier for skin and mucous membranes

### ⚠️ Pyrrolizidine Alkaloids (PAs)
Comfrey contains hepatotoxic PAs (symphytine, lycopsamine) that can cause **veno-occlusive liver disease**:
- **Do NOT consume internally** — oral use banned in many countries
- **Topical use only**: On intact skin for short periods
- **Avoid** during pregnancy, breastfeeding, or in children
- EU limits topical products to <0.35 µg PA/day (PA-free extracts)

## Polyculture Integration

```
[Apple/Plum tree — canopy]
    ├── Comfrey (nutrient accumulator) — drip line
    ├── Nitrogen-fixer (e.g., Elaeagnus umbellata)
    ├── Allium spp. (pest deterrent)
    └── Ground cover (white clover, thyme)
```

Also planted in banana circle systems and keyhole garden beds for continuous nutrient cycling.
