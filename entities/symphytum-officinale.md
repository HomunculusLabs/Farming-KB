    19|# Symphytum officinale
    20|
    21|**Common Comfrey** — a vigorous perennial herb in the borage family (*Boraginaceae*), valued in permaculture as a dynamic mineral accumulator, compost activator, mulch crop, and livestock fodder. Its deep taproot mines subsoil nutrients and concentrates them in abundant leaf biomass.

    35|### Nomenclature and Related Species
    36|
    37|The genus name derives from Greek *symphyo* (to unite), referencing its traditional wound-healing use. The common name "comfrey" descends from Latin *con firma* (with firmness), alluding to bone-knitting properties.
    38|
    39|| Species | Common Name | Notes |
    40||---------|-------------|-------|
    41|| *S. officinale* | Common Comfrey | True medicinal comfrey; variable flower color |
    42|| *S. × uplandicum* | Russian Comfrey | Hybrid (*S. officinale* × *S. asperum*); sterile, higher biomass |
    43|| *S. asperum* | Prickly Comfrey | Wild parent of Russian Comfrey; rough-haired |
    44|| *S. tuberosum* | Tuberous Comfrey | Smaller, shade-tolerant; rhizomatous spread |
    45|
    46|**Russian Comfrey ('Bocking 14')** is the most widely cultivated variety in permaculture. Developed by Lawrence D. Hills at the Henry Doubleday Research Association (HDRA), Bocking 14 is a **sterile triploid hybrid** that produces no viable seed, preventing unwanted spread while generating enormous biomass.

    48|## Botanical Description
    49|
    50|Comfrey is a robust, clump-forming perennial producing multiple growth flushes per season from a deep, fleshy root crown.
    51|
    52|- **Roots:** The defining feature. A thick, black, branching taproot extends 1.5–3+ meters deep, with a massive root mass that can occupy 1–2 m³ of soil volume. The root system acts as a **biological subsoiler**, penetrating compacted layers and cycling minerals from deep soil horizons to the surface.
    53|- **Stems:** Erect, hollow, angular, 60–120 cm tall, covered with stiff bristly hairs. Multiple stems arise from each crown.
    54|- **Leaves:** Large, broadly lanceolate to ovate, 15–40 cm long, 5–15 cm wide. Surfaces covered in coarse, irritant trichomes. Lower leaves petiolate; upper leaves sessile, clasping the stem. Dark green, sometimes slightly wrinkled.
    55|- **Flowers:** Pendulous, bell-shaped, 12–18 mm long, in scorpioid cymes (characteristic of Boraginaceae). *S. officinale* flowers range from **purple-violet to creamy-white**; *S. × uplandicum* is typically violet-blue. Blooms May–July, with intermittent flowering into autumn.
    56|- **Seeds:** Four nutlets per flower, each ~4 mm, black, glossy. *S. × uplandicum* (Bocking 14) is sterile and produces no viable seed.
    57|- **Lifecycle:** Perennial; individual plants persist 20+ years. Crown division every 4–5 years maintains vigor.

    59|## Nutrient Composition
    60|
    61|Comfrey's reputation as a **dynamic accumulator** is well-supported by tissue analysis. Its deep roots mine potassium, calcium, and other minerals from subsoil layers unavailable to shallow-rooted crops.

    65|| Nutrient | Content | Comparison |
    66||----------|---------|------------|
    67|| **Nitrogen (N)** | 3.0–7.0% | Comparable to farmyard manure |
    68|| **Potassium (K)** | 3.0–8.0% | Exceptional; exceeds most green manures |
    69|| **Calcium (Ca)** | 1.5–3.5% | Very high; comparable to lime application |
    70|| **Phosphorus (P)** | 0.3–0.8% | Moderate |
    71|| **Magnesium (Mg)** | 0.2–0.5% | Adequate |
    72|| **Iron (Fe)** | 200–500 ppm | High |
    73|| **Trace minerals** | Variable | Includes Cu, Zn, Mn, B |
    74|
    75|The exceptionally high **potassium content** makes comfrey particularly valuable for potassium-hungry crops (tomatoes, potatoes, fruit trees, berries).

    87|## Permaculture Applications
    88|
    89|### 1. Compost Activator
    90|
    91|Comfrey leaves added to compost piles act as a natural **nitrogen and potassium accelerator**. The high nitrogen content stimulates thermophilic bacterial activity, raising compost temperatures rapidly. Add alternating layers of comfrey (10–15 cm) with carbon-rich materials (straw, cardboard, dry leaves).
    92|
    93|### 2. Liquid Fertilizer (Comfrey Tea)
    94|
    95|One of the most valuable comfrey preparations:
    96|
    97|**Method:**
    98|1. Harvest comfrey leaves (wear gloves — trichomes irritate skin)
    99|2. Pack leaves into a container (bucket or barrel), filling ¾ full
   100|3. Weight down with a stone or brick
   101|4. Cover with water (1:1 ratio leaves:water by weight)
   102|5. Seal and ferment for 3–6 weeks in warm weather
   103|6. Strain; dilute 1:10 (comfrey tea:water) for foliar spray or soil drench
   104|
   105|**Result:** A dark, foul-smelling liquid rich in soluble potassium, nitrogen, and trace minerals. Analysis shows ~NPK of approximately 0.3-0.1-0.3 (diluted). Apply every 2–3 weeks to fruiting crops.
   106|
   107|**Anaerobic vs. Aerobic:**
   108|- **Anaerobic** (sealed bucket): Higher nutrient extraction; very odorous; faster (3–4 weeks)
   109|- **Aerobic** (open barrel, stirred): Less odorous; retains more nitrogen; takes 4–6 weeks
   110|
   111|### 3. Mulch
   112|
   113|Fresh comfrey leaves make an excellent **nutrient-rich mulch** around fruit trees, berries, and vegetables. As leaves decompose, they release potassium, calcium, and nitrogen directly to the soil surface. Apply 5–10 cm layer around drip line of fruit trees.
   114|
   115|### 4. Chop-and-Drop Green Manure
   116|
   117|In food forests and guild plantings, comfrey serves as a living mulch:
   118|
   119|- **Spring cut:** First harvest feeds emerging annuals
   120|- **Summer cuts (2–4):** Ongoing mulch and nutrient cycling
   121|- **Autumn cut:** Final mulch before winter dormancy
   122|
   123|### 5. Fodder Crop
   124|
   125|Comfrey has a long history as livestock fodder, particularly for poultry, pigs, and rabbits:
   126|
   127|- **Protein content:** 20–30% of dry matter
   128|- **Digestibility:** Moderate; palatability increases with wilted or ensiled material
   129|- **Yield:** 3–5 cuts per year; 5–10 kg fresh matter per plant per cut
   130|- **Safety note:** Pyrrolizidine alkaloid (PA) content requires caution for ruminant livestock (see toxicity section)
   131|
   132|### 6. Bee Forage
   133|
   134|Comfrey flowers are excellent **nectar and pollen sources** for bumblebees and long-tongued bees. The tubular flowers require proboscis length that excludes short-tongued species, making comfrey a specialist bee plant.
   135|
   136|### 7. Bioremediation
   137|
   138|Comfrey's deep root system has been investigated for **phytoremediation** of contaminated soils:
   139|- Accumulates heavy metals (Zn, Cd, Pb) from contaminated sites
   140|- Useful as a "clean-up crop" on brownfield sites (plants must be properly disposed of, not composted)
   141|- Breaks compacted soil layers with penetrating taproots
