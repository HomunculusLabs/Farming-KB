## Galbulimima belgraveana

**Galbulimima belgraveana** (agara, pink silkwood) is a large evergreen rainforest tree in the family Himantandraceae, native to Papua New Guinea, northeastern Australia, and possibly Borneo. It is renowned in ethnopharmacology for its use by indigenous peoples of Papua New Guinea, who prepare a psychoactive drink from its bark and leaves, combined with an admixture plant (*Homalomena* spp., called "ereriba"), producing violent intoxication followed by deep visionary sleep. Over 30 alkaloids have been isolated from the tree, including the hexacyclic lactone himbacine, a selective M2 muscarinic receptor antagonist investigated for Alzheimer's disease, yet the specific psychoactive principle responsible for the traditional visionary effects remains unidentified, making it one of the most significant unresolved questions in ethnopharmacology.

## Taxonomy and Nomenclature

- **Class:** Magnoliopsida (Dicotyledons, Subclass Magnoliidae)
- **Order:** Magnoliales
- **Family:** Himantandraceae, a small primitive family of 2 genera, closely allied with Magnoliaceae, Annonaceae, Myristicaceae, and Degeneriaceae within the order Magnoliales
- **Genus:** *Galbulimima* (2-4 species, taxonomy debated)
- **Species:** *G. belgraveana* (F.Muell.) Sprague, 1915
- **Original basionym:** *Belgravea belgraveana* F.Muell., 1887 (described by Ferdinand von Mueller from Australian specimens)
- **Synonym:** *Himantandra belgraveana* (F.Muell.) Diels
- **Common names:** Agara (PNG), pink silkwood (Australia, timber name), brown pine (local Australian)
- **Etymology:** *Galbulimima* likely from Greek *galbos* (pale/wan) + *mimos* (mimic), referring to the pale flowers; *belgraveana* honors an individual connected to the Belgrave family

Himantandraceae is one of the most primitive angiosperm families. Molecular phylogenetics places it as sister to or within a clade with Degeneriaceae and Magnoliaceae. The family shares morphological features with Magnoliaceae (spiral floral parts, apocarpous gynoecium, aromatic oils) and shares [[equatorial-photoperiod-early-flowering]] plant evolution.

**Fruit** is an aggregate of ovoid to ellipsoid drupes, 2-4 cm long overall, with individual drupes 1-2 cm long, green ripening to orange-red or brownish. Each drupe contains a single hard-coated, slightly oily seed.

**Wood** is commercially known as pink silkwood: pink to pale brown heartwood with fine, even texture and straight grain, used locally for cabinet work, carving, and light construction.

## Geographic Distribution

Galbulimima belgraveana has a disjunct distribution across the Australasian region. It is most widespread and common in **Papua New Guinea**, occurring throughout lowland and montane rainforests from sea level to approximately 2,400 m elevation, recorded from Morobe, Eastern Highlands, Southern Highlands, Western Highlands, Milne Bay, Central, and Gulf Provinces.

In **Australia**, it is restricted to the Wet Tropics of northeastern Queensland, from the Bloomfield River south to the Tully River area and Atherton Tablelands, at altitudes of approximately 100-1,200 m. Populations are scattered within mature rainforest and considered uncommon.

Reports from **Malaysia and Indonesia** (Borneo: Sabah, Sarawak, Kalimantan) may refer to *G. belgraveana* or to the closely related *G. baccata*, and the two species have not been consistently distinguished in older literature. The Queensland populations represent the southernmost extent of a primarily Papuasian/Malesian genus. The wide geographic distribution across multiple countries and indigenous cultural areas suggests that its psychoactive properties may have been discovered independently by different peoples, though detailed documentation of traditional use outside Papua New Guinea remains limited.

## Chemical Constituents

Galbulimima belgraveana is extraordinarily rich in structurally diverse alkaloids, particularly hexacyclic lactone alkaloids unique to the genus. Over 30 alkaloids have been isolated, with total alkaloid content highest in bark (0.5-2.0% dry weight) and moderate in leaves (0.1-0.5%). Considerable variation exists between Papua New Guinean and Australian populations.

**Himbacine-type alkaloids (signature chemistry):** Himbacine (C22H29NO3, MW ~351) is the most abundant and extensively studied alkaloid, a hexacyclic lactone with a pavine-type core bearing additional lactone and piperidine rings. First isolated by CSIRO (Australia) researchers in the 1960s-1970s, it is a relatively selective M2 muscarinic acetylcholine receptor antagonist (Ki in low nM range), with M2 > M1 >> M3 affinity, some histamine H1 receptor affinity, and minimal CNS penetration due to its polar nature. Related compounds include himbine (demethylated himbacine), himbeline, himboline, himgravine, and systematically numbered alkaloids GB1 through GB4.

**Himandrine-type alkaloids:** Himandrine is a dimeric bisbenzylisoquinoline alkaloid reported to have CNS activity and considered a candidate psychoactive principle. Himandridine is a related compound. These more complex structures may contribute to the hallucinogenic effects described in traditional use.

**Simple benzylisoquinoline and pavine alkaloids:** Himbadine (pavine-type), galbuline, homogalbuline, and various aporphine, proaporphine, and simple isoquinoline bases contribute to the overall pharmacological profile. Several unnamed alkaloids have been detected in chromatographic surveys, suggesting the phytochemistry is not yet fully characterized.
