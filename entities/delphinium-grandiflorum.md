The species typically flowers from early to mid-summer (June–July in the northern hemisphere), with individual flowers lasting 7–10 days. A well-grown plant produces 3–5 flowering stems simultaneously, creating a substantial floral display despite the plant's modest size.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Delphinium grandiflorum was described by Carl Linnaeus in 1753. The species has a complex taxonomic history with numerous synonymized names including D. chinense and D. grandiflorum var. chinense.

The plant forms a basal rosette of finely divided, palmate leaves with narrow, linear lobes. Leaves are 5–10 cm across, deeply cut into thread-like segments that give the foliage a soft, feathery appearance.

The feathery foliage distinguishes D. grandiflorum from other garden delphiniums at a glance, even when not in flower. This fine texture makes the species useful for providing foliage contrast in mixed plantings with bolder-leaved plants.

Flowering stems bear racemes of 5–20 flowers, each 2–4 cm across with five sepals (the uppermost forming a spur) and four petals. The upper petals form the characteristic "bee" or eye at the center of the flower.

The long nectar spur (15–25 mm) is a key diagnostic feature, adapted for pollination by long-tongued bumblebees. Spur length varies among populations and is under strong selective pressure from local pollinator communities.

Roots are fibrous and somewhat fleshy, forming a compact crown at the soil surface. The species does not produce the tuberous roots found in some Delphinium sections.

The root crown is sensitive to crown rot in poorly drained soils, particularly during winter dormancy. Good drainage is essential for long-term survival, and gravel mulch around the crown helps prevent moisture accumulation.

Chromosome number is 2n = 16, which is the base number for the genus Delphinium section Delphinium. The species is diploid, unlike many cultivated delphiniums that are tetraploid or hexaploid hybrids.

## Distribution and Habitat

The species is native to a broad swathe of northern Asia, from central Siberia eastward through Mongolia to northeastern China. It occurs in the Russian regions of Buryatia, Irkutsk, and the Russian Far East.

In China, D. grandiflorum is found in Heilongjiang, Jilin, Inner Mongolia, and Hebei provinces. Populations in northern China represent the southern edge of the species' natural range.

The plant grows in dry to mesic meadows, forest clearings, rocky slopes, and steppic grasslands at elevations of 200–2,000 meters. It tolerates continental climate extremes with cold winters (-30°C) and hot, dry summers.

Naturalized populations have established in parts of eastern Europe and [[growing-gourmet-north-american-reishi-species]] gardens where both co-occur. The intense blue color is particularly visible to birds, which can see into the ultraviolet spectrum reflected by delphinium petals.

The finely divided foliage provides food for the larvae of several moth species, including the dot moth (Melanchra persicariae) in Eurasia. Caterpillar feeding damage is usually cosmetic and does not threaten plant health.
