# Soil Compaction

## Description

Soil compaction is the compression of soil particles that reduces pore space, increases bulk density, and severely impairs the soil's ability to support biological life, water infiltration, gas exchange, and root growth. In the context of [[soil-food-web]] science, compaction is one of the most destructive forces affecting garden and landscape soils, devastating [[the-soil-food-web]] at every trophic level from bacteria to earthworms.

## Classification

- **Category**: Soil physical condition
- **Type**: Degraded soil structure
- **Severity**: Ranges from surface crusting to deep profile compaction

## Key Facts

- Compaction destroys the tunnels, burrows, and pore spaces created by soil organisms
- Larger soil organisms (nematodes, earthworms, microarthropods) cannot move through compacted soil
- [[fungal-hyphae]] networks are physically crushed and destroyed
- [[mycorrhizal-fungi]] associations with plant roots are severed or drowned
- Oxygen levels drop as pore spaces close, creating anaerobic conditions
- Anaerobic bacteria produce metabolic products toxic to plant roots
- Water infiltration decreases dramatically, increasing runoff and erosion
- Root growth is physically impeded, reducing plant access to water and nutrients
- The soil food web simplifies to only the smallest organisms (bacteria, opportunistic fungi, small protozoa)
- Beneficial fungi that compete with pathogens (Pythium, Rhizoctonia) are eliminated, opening the door to disease

## Causes in Gardens

- Foot traffic on wet soils, especially in lawn areas around trees and shrubs
- Heavy equipment or wheeled tools operated on garden soils
- Rototilling and excessive soil disturbance that destroys soil structure
- Rainfall on bare, unprotected soil surfaces
- Lack of organic matter and soil biology needed to maintain soil aggregates
- Construction activities that compress soil with heavy machinery

## Impact on the Soil Food Web

Compaction creates a cascading collapse of the soil food web:

1. **Macrofauna eliminated**: Earthworms, beetles, and other large organisms cannot move or survive
2. **Fungal networks destroyed**: Miles of hyphae are crushed, eliminating fungal dominance
3. **Mycorrhizae lost**: The critical plant-fungal partnerships that deliver phosphorus and water are severed
4. **Nutrient cycling halts**: Without predators (nematodes, microarthropods) to graze on bacteria and fungi, nutrients remain locked in microbial biomass
5. **Anaerobic takeover**: Oxygen-poor conditions favor anaerobic bacteria that produce root-toxic compounds
6. **Pathogen dominance**: Without beneficial fungal competitors, root rot pathogens (Pythium, Rhizoctonia) flourish

## Remediation

Soil food web approaches to reversing compaction:

- **[[plug-aeration]]**: Mechanical first step to open channels in severely compacted soil
- **Compost application**: Apply 1–2 inches of appropriate compost to introduce soil organisms that rebuild structure
- **Mulching**: Brown mulches for fungal dominance (trees/shrubs), green mulches for bacterial dominance (vegetables)
- **Compost tea**: Soil drenches of compost tea rapidly introduce microbiology that begins rebuilding soil aggregates
- **Eliminate traffic**: Restrict foot and equipment traffic; create designated pathways
- **Patience**: Biological remediation takes time; the soil food web must re-establish and rebuild pore space through its normal activities

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Soil compaction is the enemy of mycorrhizal fungi and healthy root systems. Trees, shrubs, and perennials planted in compacted soils (particularly those in lawn settings) are especially vulnerable because they depend on extensive mycorrhizal networks. For mycologists, compacted soils represent the complete absence of the fungal-dominated conditions that most mushrooms and mycorrhizal partnerships require. Remediation through biological means—compost, mulch, compost tea—restores the fungal habitat and the full soil food web.

## Relationship to Other Entities

- **Mycorrhizal fungi**: Compaction directly destroys mycorrhizal associations
- **Pythium and Rhizoctonia**: Pathogenic fungi that thrive in compacted, anaerobic soils after beneficial fungi are eliminated
- **Plug aeration**: Mechanical remediation technique for severely compacted soils
- **Compost tea**: Biological tool for restoring soil structure after compaction
- **Rototilling**: A practice that creates temporary looseness but ultimately contributes to compaction by destroying soil biology
