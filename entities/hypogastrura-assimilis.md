## Description

**Hypogastrura assimilis** is a species of collembolan (springtail) in the family Hypogastruridae. It was studied by Setala (2000) as part of an investigation into the effects of [[ectomycorrhizal-fungi]] and soil mesofauna community complexity on Scots pine growth.









## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Animalia
- **Phylum**: Arthropoda
- **Class**: [[collembola]]
- **Order**: Poduromorpha
- **Family**: Hypogastruridae
- **Genus**: Hypogastrura
- **Species**: H. assimilis









## Key Facts

- Used in Setala (2000) experiment on ectomycorrhizal effects on soil mesofauna
- Represented the Collembola component in a soil faunal community of increasing complexity
- Studied alongside the enchytraeid [[cognettia-sphagnetorum]] and four species of oribatid mites
- Pine biomass was 1.43x higher in mycorrhizal than nonmycorrhizal systems after 60 weeks
- Neither numbers nor biomasses of mesofauna differed significantly between mycorrhizal treatments









## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

H. assimilis was part of a landmark study showing that despite significantly increased plant and fungal biomass in mycorrhizal systems, soil mesofauna populations did not increase correspondingly. This suggests that the short-term role of ectomycorrhizal fungi in sustaining the detrital food web may be less significant than generally assumed.









## Ecological Role

H. assimilis is a common soil collembolan that participates in fungal grazing and organic matter decomposition. As part of the soil mesofauna, it contributes to [[nutrient-mineralization]] and serves as prey for larger soil predators.
