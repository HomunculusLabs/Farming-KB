## Heracleum sphondylium

## Overview

Heracleum sphondylium, commonly known as cow parsnip or hogweed, is a perennial herbaceous plant in the family Apiaceae. It is widely distributed across Europe and temperate Asia, where it grows in meadows, woodland edges, and along streams.

The plant is notable for its large compound leaves and broad umbels of white flowers. It has a long history of use as both a wild edible and a traditional medicinal plant in European folk practices.

Cow parsnip should not be confused with the invasive giant hogweed (Heracleum mantegazzianum), which is far larger and causes severe phytophotodermatitis. Heracleum sphondylium can also cause mild skin reactions in sensitive individuals.

The common name "hogweed" refers to its historical use as fodder for pigs and other livestock. The genus name honors Heracles, reflecting the imposing stature of many species in the group.

Several subspecies are recognized across the species range. Heracleum sphondylium subsp. sphondylium is the most widespread European form, while subsp. sibiricum occupies northern and eastern ranges.
