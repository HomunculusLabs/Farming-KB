## Overview

**Adonis amurensis**, commonly known as Amur adonis or Far East adonis, is a perennial herb in the buttercup family (Ranunculaceae). It is native to northeastern Asia, including Japan, Korea, northeastern China, and the Russian Far East.

The plant is celebrated as one of the earliest spring flowers in East Asia, often blooming while snow still covers the ground. Its bright golden-yellow flowers emerge in February and March, preceding almost all other wildflowers.

In Japan, the plant is known as *fukujusō* (福寿草), meaning "fortune-longevity herb." It is a traditional New Year's decorative plant symbolizing happiness and longevity.

The species is named after the Amur River region, which forms part of the border between Russia and China. It was first described by Regel and Radde in 1861.

The genus *Adonis* is named after the Greek mythological figure Adonis, associated with beauty and rebirth. The golden-yellow flowers evoke this connection to radiant beauty.

In traditional Japanese calendar systems, the blooming of fukujusō marked the transition from winter to spring. The plant was referenced in many haiku and waka poems.

## Ecology

As one of the earliest spring ephemerals, Adonis amurensis exploits the brief window of high light before forest canopy closure. The plant completes its flowering before most deciduous trees have leafed out.

The flowers produce both nectar and pollen at a time when few other floral resources are available. Early-emerging solitary bees and flies are the primary pollinators.

The plant is thermogenic, capable of generating metabolic heat that melts surrounding snow. This allows flowers to open and attract pollinators even in sub-freezing conditions.

All parts contain cardenolide glycosides, including adonitoxin and cymarin, which are toxic to livestock and humans. The bitter taste deters most herbivores from grazing the foliage.

The plant prefers partial shade, humus-rich soil, and consistent moisture during the growing season. It tolerates a range of soil pH from slightly acidic to neutral.

Propagation is by seed sown fresh in summer or by division of the rhizome in autumn. Seeds require a period of warm stratification followed by cold stratification to germinate.

Division is the preferred method for maintaining specific forms and cultivars. Clumps should be divided every 4–5 years to maintain vigor.

Plants resent root disturbance and should be left undisturbed once established. Choose a permanent position carefully when planting.

Several cultivars are available, including 'Fukujukai' with semi-double flowers and 'Chichibu-benikane' with orange-tinged petals. Variegated forms are rare and highly prized in Japanese horticulture.
