## Overview

**Termitomyces umkowaani** is a species of agaric fungus in the family Lyophyllaceae,
obligately associated with fungus-growing termites of the genus *Macrotermes*.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Termitomyces umkowaani* is found across sub-Saharan Africa, from Tanzania and
Kenya southward through Zambia, Zimbabwe, Mozambique, and into South Africa.

It grows exclusively in association with *Macrotermes* species, particularly
*Macrotermes natalensis* and *Macrotermes falciger*. The fungus is cultivated
within the termite mound on specialized fungal combs constructed from plant
material brought in by worker termites.

Fruiting occurs during the warm rainy season, typically November through March
in southern Africa, triggered by sustained soil moisture and high humidity.
Sporocarps emerge singly or in small clusters at the base or on the sides of
active termite mounds.

## Ethnomycology and Culinary Use

This species is one of the most commercially important edible-wild-mushrooms-by-region in
southern Africa. It is widely collected and sold in rural and urban markets
throughout Zimbabwe, Zambia, and Tanzania.

In Zimbabwe, *Termitomyces umkowaani* is known locally as "nhovi" (Shona) and
commands premium prices during the fruiting season, often exceeding the value
of cultivated vegetables per kilogram.

The flesh is firm, white, and mild-flavored with a pleasant nutty aroma. It is
prepared by grilling, frying, or drying for preservation. Dried specimens
store well for several months and are reconstituted for use in stews and relishes.

Traditional knowledge of mound location, seasonal timing, and sustainable
harvesting practices is passed matrilineally in many communities, with women
serving as the primary collectors and traders.
