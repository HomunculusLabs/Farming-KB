**Inflorescences** are racemose, rising on erect peduncles 20–80 cm above the water. Each peduncle bears 3–12 whorls of flowers, with female flowers in the lower whorls and male flowers in the upper whorls.

Flowers are 1.5–2.5 cm in diameter with three white petals and three green sepals. Male flowers have numerous yellow stamens while female flowers have a dense cluster of free carpels.

**Tubers** are produced at the ends of stolons in late summer and autumn. They are globular to ovoid, measuring 1–4 cm in diameter, with a pale brown skin and white, starchy interior.

Each plant can produce 10–20 tubers depending on growing conditions. The tubers serve as perennating organs that allow the plant to survive winter dormancy.

## Distribution and Habitat

Sagittaria sagittifolia is native to Europe, western and central Asia, and North Africa. Its range extends from the British Isles and Scandinavia eastward through Russia to Siberia and Central Asia.

In East Asia, closely related Sagittaria species (particularly Sagittaria trifolia) are cultivated commercially for their edible tubers. The taxonomy of Asian Sagittaria populations is complex, and some cultivated forms may represent hybrids.

The species grows in shallow, standing or slow-flowing freshwater at elevations from sea level to approximately 1500 meters. Typical habitats include marshes, ditches, pond margins, slow river backwaters, and seasonally flooded meadows.

Sagittaria sagittifolia prefers full sun but tolerates partial shade. It grows in a range of substrates from clay to sandy loam, provided there is adequate moisture.

Water depths of 10–40 cm are optimal for tuber production. In deeper water, the plant tends to produce more submerged leaves and fewer tubers.
