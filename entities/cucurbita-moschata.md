## Overview

Cucurbita moschata is a species of squash in the family Cucurbitaceae, native to
Central and South America. It produces vining annual plants with large lobed
leaves and round to elongated fruits ranging from 0.5 to 20 kg.

The species encompasses many commercially important cultivars including butternut
squash, cheese pumpkin, and calabaza. It is one of the most widely cultivated
squash species globally, valued for its sweet orange flesh and long storage life.

Unlike Cucurbita pepo, C. moschata vines tolerate heat, humidity, and many
pest pressures better. The fruits develop a hard rind at maturity that enables
months of storage at ambient temperatures.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Cucurbita moschata (Duchesne) Poir. belongs to the genus Cucurbita, family
Cucurbitaceae, order Cucurbitales. The species epithet refers to the musky
aroma characteristic of ripe fruits.

Vines are annual, trailing or climbing, reaching 3–10 meters in length. Stems
are cylindrical, heavily pubescent, and lack the prominent pentagonal ribs seen
in C. pepo.

Leaves are large (15–30 cm diameter), broadly cordate, usually unlobed or with
shallow lobes. The leaf surface is often silvery-pubescent on the underside,
a distinctive field character compared to the smoother leaves of C. pepo.

Tendrils are simple, coiling, and robust. Flowers are monoecious with separate
male and female flowers on the same plant.

Male flowers are borne on long peduncles with a campanulate corolla 8–12 cm
long. Female flowers have a thick, cylindrical ovary at the base and are
borne on shorter peduncles.

Fruits are pepo-type berries with a hard, corky or woody rind at maturity.
Shape varies widely among cultivars: cylindrical (butternut), oblate
(cheese), globular (calabaza), or turban-shaped.

The flesh is typically deep orange, dense, and sweet, containing a central
cavity filled with seeds and fibrous placental tissue.

Seeds are oval to elliptic, 15–25 mm long, cream-colored to tan, with a
distinctive margin. Seed coats are relatively thin compared to C. maxima.

## Cultivation

Cucurbita moschata is grown as a warm-season annual in USDA zones 3–12. It
requires a frost-free [[savory-growing-season-and-non-growing-season-management]] of 90–120 days depending on cultivar.

Plants perform best in full sun with well-drained, fertile soils pH 6.0–7.0.
They are more tolerant of hot, humid conditions and poor soils than C. pepo,
making them the dominant squash species in tropical and subtropical regions.

[[comparison-seed-starting-indoors-vs-direct-seeding-techniques]] is preferred when soil temperatures exceed 18°C. [[wide-row-spacing-intensive-gardening-comparison]]
ranges from 1.5 to 3 meters with 0.5–1 meter in-row spacing depending on
cultivar bushiness.

The species exhibits strong vining vigor and produces large root systems that
improve [[dighton-fungal-drought-tolerance-plant-water-relations]] compared to other cultivated Cucurbita species.

Disease resistance is generally superior to C. pepo. Notable resistances
include [[cervantes-powdery-mildew-prevention-treatment]] (Podosphaera xanthii), squash vine borer, and some
strains of watermelon mosaic virus.

Key pests include squash bugs (Anasa tristis), cucumber beetles
(Acalymma vittatum), and pickleworms (Diaphania nitidalis). Squash vine
borer causes less damage to C. moschata than to C. pepo.

Average yields range from 15 to 40 tonnes per hectare under commercial
production. Market maturity occurs 80–110 days after planting depending
on the intended use and cultivar.
