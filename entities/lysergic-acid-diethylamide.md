## Description

Lysergic acid diethylamide (LSD) is a powerful semisynthetic hallucinogenic compound derived from ergot alkaloids. While LSD itself is not found in nature, it contains an indole nucleus shared with many natural hallucinogens and is structurally related to the [[morning-glory-ergoline-alkaloids-chemistry]] found in [[sacred-mexican-morning-glories-ololiuqui-and-tlitliltzin]] and ergot fungus ([[bloomfield-ergot-claviceps-purpurea-ergotism]]). The source text notes LSD as having an indole nucleus, placing it in the same chemical family as DMT, psilocybin, and serotonin.

## Classification

- **Category**: Semisynthetic hallucinogen
- **Chemical class**: Ergoline / Indole alkaloid derivative
- **Molecular formula**: C20H25N3O
- **Derivation**: Semisynthetic from lysergic acid (ergot alkaloids)
- **Activity**: One of the most potent psychoactive substances known

## Key Facts

- Contains an indole nucleus, shared with serotonin and many natural hallucinogens
- Derived from lysergic acid, which occurs naturally in ergot fungus [[schultes-ololiuqui-and-morning-glory]] seeds
- Active in microgram doses (one of the most potent psychoactive compounds known)
- Structurally related to the ergoline alkaloids: ergine and others found in plants
- Part of the indolic hallucinogen family alongside DMT, psilocybin, ibogaine, and beta-carbolines
- The [[serotonin-psilocin-structural-similarity-hallucinogenic-mechanism]] to serotonin may explain its profound psychoactive effects

## Chemical Context

LSD occupies an important position in the spectrum of indolic hallucinogens:

- **Tryptamines**: DMT, psilocybin, psilocin (simple indole structures)
- **Ergoline alkaloids**: LSD, ergine (complex indole with additional ring systems)
- **Beta-carbolines**: Harmine, harmaline (fused indole structures)
- **Ibogaine alkaloids**: Complex indole structures

All share the indole ring that is also the core of serotonin, the neurotransmitter they primarily interact with.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

While LSD itself is semisynthetic, its connection to mycology is significant:

- Derived from ergot fungus (Claviceps purpurea) alkaloids
- Morning glory seeds contain related ergoline alkaloids (ergine, etc.)
- Part of the indole pharmacology that includes [[hplc-analysis-of-psilocybin-and-psilocin]] from mushrooms
- Understanding LSD helps contextualize the broader family of indole hallucinogens
- Ergot is a fungal parasite of grasses with profound historical significance
