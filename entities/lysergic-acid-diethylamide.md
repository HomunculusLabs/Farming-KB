# Lysergic Acid Diethylamide (LSD)
**Lysergic acid diethylamide**, commonly known as **LSD** or **acid**, is a potent semi-synthetic psychedelic substance of the lysergamide class. It is one of the most potent psychoactive substances known, with active doses measured in micrograms. LSD is widely regarded as both the prototypical psychedelic compound and one of the most influential psychoactive substances in human history, having profoundly shaped neuroscience, psychiatry, counterculture, and popular culture.

### Structure and Classification

LSD belongs to the **lysergamide** family, which are amide derivatives of lysergic acid. Lysergic acid is an ergoline alkaloid found naturally in the **ergot fungus** (*Claviceps purpurea*), a parasitic fungus of cereal grasses.
The core pharmacophore consists of:
- **Tetracyclic ergoline ring system** (indole + two additional rings)
- **Diethylamide substituent** at position C-8 (critical for psychoactivity)
- **Methyl group** at position N-6

## History

### Discovery (1938–1943)

- **1938:** Albert Hofmann, a chemist at Sandoz Laboratories in Basel, Switzerland, first synthesized LSD-25 (the 25th in a series of lysergic acid derivatives) while investigating circulatory stimulants and respiratory analeptics derived from ergot alkaloids. The initial pharmacological testing on animals showed no remarkable effects, and the project was shelved.
- **April 16, 1943:** Hofmann resynthesized LSD-25 and accidentally absorbed a small amount through his fingertips, becoming the first person to experience its effects. He described "an uninterrupted stream of fantastic pictures, extraordinary shapes with intense, kaleidoscopic play of colors."
- **April 19, 1943 — Bicycle Day:** Hofmann deliberately ingested 250 µg (a dose he believed to be a threshold amount, but which is now known to be a moderately strong dose) and cycled home from the laboratory, experiencing the world's first intentional LSD trip. April 19 is now celebrated annually as "Bicycle Day."

### CIA and MK-Ultra (1950s–1960s)

- The CIA investigated LSD as a potential truth serum, mind-control agent, and chemical weapon under projects **Bluebird**, **Artichoke**, and **MK-Ultra**.
- Unwitting subjects were dosed in Operation Midnight Climax, a San Francisco-based program using prostitutes to administer LSD to clients.
- The program was exposed by the Church Committee in 1975 and formally ended in 1973.

### Psychiatric Research Era (1950s–1960s)

- Over 1,000 clinical papers and 6 international conferences explored LSD's therapeutic potential.
- Humphry Osmond coined the term "psychedelic" ("mind-manifesting") in 1957.
- LSD was investigated for treatment of alcoholism, schizophrenia, autism, anxiety, and terminal illness.
- By 1965, over 40,000 patients had been treated with LSD in psychiatric settings.
- Abram Hoffer and Humphry Osmond reported significant improvements in alcoholism recovery rates.

### Counterculture (1960s)

- LSD became central to the 1960s counterculture movement, championed by figures like Timothy Leary, Ken Kesey (Merry Pranksters), and Aldous Huxley.
- Leary founded the International Federation for Internal Freedom (IFIF) and popularized the slogan "Turn on, tune in, drop out."
- The widespread recreational use of LSD — and growing public concern — led to its criminalization.
- **1966:** California banned LSD. **1968:** The U.S. federal government classified LSD as a Schedule I controlled substance under the Controlled Substances Act.

### Research Revival (2000s–Present)

- Regulatory barriers began easing in the 2000s, leading to a resurgence of clinical research.
- Modern studies focus on LSD-assisted psychotherapy for anxiety, depression, cluster headaches, and end-of-life distress.
- As of 2026, several organizations (MAPS, MIND Foundation, University Hospital Basel) are conducting clinical trials.

## Pharmacology

### Mechanism of Action

LSD acts primarily as a **potent agonist at 5-HT2A serotonin receptors**:
- **Primary target:** 5-HT2A receptor (Ki ≈ 2.5 nM) — mediates psychedelic effects
- **Partial agonist at:** 5-HT2B, 5-HT2C, 5-HT1A, 5-HT5A, 5-HT6, 5-HT7
- **D2 dopamine receptor:** Moderate affinity (Ki ≈ 6–10 nM)
- **TAAR1 receptor:** Agonist activity with high affinity (Ki ≈ 6–14 nM)

A pivotal 2017 study (Wacker et al., *Cell*) using X-ray crystallography revealed that LSD binds to the 5-HT2A receptor with an unusual **"lid" mechanism** — part of the receptor folds over the LSD molecule, trapping it in the binding pocket. This explains LSD's extraordinarily long duration of action (8–12 hours) despite its low dose.

### Pharmacokinetics

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Onset** | 20–90 minutes (oral) |
| **Duration** | 8–12 hours |
| **Half-life** | ~3–5 hours |
| **Active dose** | 25–200 µg (oral) |
| **LD50 (intravenous, rat)** | ~16.5 mg/kg |
LSD is absorbed rapidly and reaches peak plasma concentrations within 1.5–3 hours. It is metabolized primarily in the liver by CYP450 enzymes and excreted in feces and urine. The active metabolite **nor-LSD** (N-desethyl-LSD) may contribute to the overall effect profile.

## Dosage and Effects

### Dosage Categories

| Category | Dose (µg) | Effects |
|----------|-----------|---------|
| **Threshold** | 10–25 | Subtle mood lift, mild sensory enhancement |
| **Light** | 25–75 | Noticeable perceptual shifts, enhanced colors, mood elevation |
| **Common** | 75–150 | Strong visual hallucinations, altered thought patterns, synesthesia |
| **Strong** | 150–300 | Intense hallucinations, ego dissolution, mystical experiences |
| **Heavy** | 300–500+ | Complete ego dissolution, extreme altered states; high risk of psychological distress |

### Subjective Effects

**Sensory:**
- Visual hallucinations (geometric patterns, color enhancement, morphing surfaces, tracers)
- Auditory distortions and synesthesia
- Altered perception of time (time dilation or compression)
- Enhanced tactile sensitivity

**Cognitive:**
- Altered thought patterns and cognitive flexibility
- Enhanced creativity and divergent thinking
- Ego dissolution or "ego death"
- Mystical or transcendent experiences
- Enhanced introspection and emotional processing

**Emotional:**
- Intensified emotions (both positive and negative)
- Sense of interconnectedness and unity
- Anxiety or paranoia (especially at higher doses or in uncontrolled settings)

**Physical:**
- Pupil dilation (mydriasis)
- Increased heart rate and blood pressure
- Elevated body temperature
- Loss of appetite
- Sleeplessness (during experience)
- Mild tremors or muscle tension

## Therapeutic Applications

### Current Clinical Research (as of 2026)

- **Anxiety associated with life-threatening illness:** A 2014 Swiss study (Gasser et al.) found significant, sustained anxiety reduction with LSD-assisted psychotherapy. Follow-up showed benefits persisting at 12 months.
- **Major depressive disorder:** Ongoing trials exploring microdose and therapeutic-dose protocols.
- **Cluster headaches:** Anecdotal reports and preliminary studies suggest LSD and related ergolines may abort cluster headache cycles.
- **Alcoholism:** Modern replication of early studies (e.g., the Norwegian study by Krebs & Johansen, 2012) showed significant associations between psychedelic use and reduced alcohol misuse.
- **End-of-life anxiety:** Continuing research at University Hospital Basel and other centers.

### Microdosing

Sub-perceptual doses (5–20 µg, typically every 3 days) have become popular for purported cognitive enhancement, though clinical evidence remains inconclusive:
- Self-reported benefits: improved focus, creativity, mood, and energy
- Placebo-controlled studies (e.g., Szigeti et al., 2021) have found limited evidence for objective cognitive benefits, with many effects attributable to expectancy
- A 2024 double-blind study found modest improvements in well-being and mindfulness but no significant cognitive performance changes

## Safety Profile

### Physical Safety

- LSD has **no known lethal dose in humans**; the LD50 is estimated at >12,000 µg (12 mg), more than 100× a typical recreational dose
- No organ toxicity has been demonstrated
- No evidence of neurotoxicity
- **Physiological dependence:** None; LSD is not considered addictive

### Psychological Risks

The primary risks are psychological:
- **Acute anxiety ("bad trip"):** Can occur at any dose, particularly in uncontrolled settings
- **Hallucinogen Persisting Perception Disorder (HPPD):** Rare condition involving persistent visual disturbances after psychedelic use; estimated prevalence < 1 in 50,000 users
- **Precipitation of psychosis:** Possible in individuals with personal or family history of schizophrenia or bipolar disorder
- **Trauma from difficult experiences:** Mitigated by proper set, setting, preparation, and integration

### Harm Reduction Principles

- Test substances (reagent testing) — LSD is frequently adulterated with NBOMe compounds, which are more dangerous
- Use with trusted individuals in a safe, comfortable environment
- Start with low doses; wait 2–3 hours before redosing (due to delayed onset)
- Avoid mixing with other substances, particularly alcohol, stimulants, and other psychedelics
- Allow adequate integration time after the experience
- Maintain at least 1–2 weeks between experiences to prevent tolerance
