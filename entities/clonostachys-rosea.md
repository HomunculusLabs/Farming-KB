# Clonostachys rosea

**Type:** Species (Ascomycete)
**Category:** Polyphagous Mycoparasite / Biocontrol Agent
**Also known as:** Gliocladium roseum (synonym)

## Overview
Clonostachys rosea is a ubiquitous polyphagous mycoparasitic
ascomycete, also known by the widely used synonym Gliocladium
roseum. The species has a recognized form, C. rosea f.
catenulata, which was isolated from sunflower field soil and
exhibits particularly aggressive mycoparasitic activity. C. rosea
is remarkable for its ability to overgrow and destroy a wide
range of fungal hosts without being inhibited by their chemical
defenses.

C. rosea belongs to the Ascomycota, with the name Gliocladium
roseum representing an older classification. The consolidation
under Clonostachys reflects modern phylogenetic understanding.
Keinath et al. (1991) studied its biocontrol potential against
Verticillium dahliae under the name Gliocladium roseum, and much
of the older literature on biocontrol by Gliocladium species
actually refers to Clonostachys.
