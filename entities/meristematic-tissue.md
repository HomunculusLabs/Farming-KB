# Meristematic Tissue

## Description

Meristematic tissue is the source of all new cells for plant growth and expansion. Composed of small, undifferentiated cells that retain the ability to divide, meristematic tissue is found only in specific growth zones of the plant. The word "meristem" comes from a Greek word meaning "division." These remarkable cells can become any type of cell in the plant, and each individual meristematic cell is theoretically capable of cloning the entire organism.

## Classification

- **Type**: Plant tissue
- **Category**: Undifferentiated / embryonic tissue
- **Cell characteristics**: Small, thin-walled, large nuclei, small or absent vacuoles
- **Function**: Generation of all new cells for plant growth
- **Locations**: Shoot tips, root tips, and lateral zones

## Types of Meristematic Tissue

### Apical Meristems (Primary Growth)

Located at the growing tips of the plant:
- **Shoot apical meristem**: Found at stem tips, produces cells that grow upward
- **Root apical meristem**: Found just above the root cap, grows both downward and outward
- Responsible for primary growth — increasing plant height and root length
- Hormones signal apical meristem tissue in the stem to produce cells in only one direction (upward)

### Lateral Meristems (Secondary Growth)

- Located in a ring surrounding trunks and limbs
- Responsible for secondary growth — increasing plant diameter
- Produces the growth rings seen in woody plants
- Not all plants have lateral meristems — monocots (maize, grasses) lack them entirely
- Includes the vascular cambium, which produces new xylem and phloem cells

## Cell Characteristics

Meristematic cells are distinct from mature plant cells:
- Very large nuclei relative to cell size
- Very small vacuoles or none at all
- Extremely thin cell walls
- Closely packed together
- Cytoplasm is undeveloped — contains only precursors to organelles like plastids
- Rich in protoplasm — a concentrated stew of building blocks ready for construction

## Differentiation Process

When a meristematic cell receives a signal to differentiate:

1. An unbelievable number of specialized proteins (many as enzymes) flow into the cell
2. Hormones and compounds like auxins activate processes leading to cell specialization
3. Transport proteins in the plasmalemma become very active
4. The large nucleus responds to chemical signals and directs ribosome construction
5. RNA copies DNA instructions; mRNA directs protein synthesis in ribosomes
6. Proteins are processed through ER → Golgi apparatus for finishing
7. The vacuole grows to hold nutrients and regulate pH
8. Mitochondria provide energy; microtubules and actin filaments build the transport system
9. Once differentiated, the cell permanently loses the ability to divide

## Key Facts

- All ground, vascular, and dermal tissues originate from meristematic tissue
- Every meristematic cell is capable of cloning the entire plant (totipotency)
- Meristematic cells at the tips are pushed outward/upward as new cells divide behind them
- The differentiation signaling system is extremely complex and not yet fully understood
- Monocots lack lateral meristems, which is why grasses and corn don't develop woody trunks
- A shoot meristem cell can be moved to a growing tip and differentiate into a bud, leaf, or flower cell depending on signals received

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- All plant growth originates from meristematic tissue — anything that damages meristems (pests, disease, nutrient deficiency, pruning errors) directly reduces growth potential
- Apical dominance (controlled by auxins from the shoot apical meristem) determines plant branching pattern — tip pruning removes apical dominance and promotes lateral branching
- Root meristem health determines root system expansion and thus nutrient uptake capacity
- In tissue culture and micropropagation, meristematic tissue is used to clone plants because of its totipotency
- Understanding meristem function explains why plants respond to training techniques (topping, fimming, LST) — these methods manipulate meristem signaling
- Mycorrhizal fungi often colonize near root apical meristems, forming symbiotic relationships in the zone of active root growth
