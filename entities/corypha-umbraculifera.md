## Overview

**Corypha umbraculifera** (talipot palm) is one of the largest palms in the world, native to South India and Sri Lanka.

It is famous for its enormous fan-shaped leaves, which can reach up to 5 meters in diameter.

The palm is monocarpic, flowering once after 40–80 years and then dying in a spectacular terminal display.

Historically, its leaves were the primary writing material in South and [[psilocybe-southeast-asia-pacific]] before paper became widespread.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Corypha umbraculifera** L. belongs to the family Arecaceae, subfamily Coryphoideae, tribe Corypheae.

The genus Corypha comprises only 2–3 species, making it one of the smallest palm genera.

The trunk is massive, cylindrical, and smooth, reaching 20–30 meters in height and 60–100 centimeters in diameter.

It is gray to brown and bears prominent, close-set leaf scars that encircle the stem.

The crown consists of 30–40 large, costapalmate leaves arranged in a graceful, hemispherical pattern.

Leaves are the largest of any palm, reaching 4–5 meters in diameter with a sturdy petiole up to 3 meters long.

Each leaf is divided into 80–120 stiff, pointed segments radiating from a central point.

The leaf segments are dark green above and paler beneath, with fibrous margins.

The inflorescence is terminal and massive, rising 6–8 meters above the crown.

It bears millions of small, cream-colored flowers on many-branched pendulous spikes.

The palm is monocarpic, producing this single enormous inflorescence after decades of [[cannabis-vegetative-growth-stage]].

Fruits are globular drupes approximately 2–3 centimeters in diameter, greenish-brown when ripe.

## Distribution and Habitat

Corypha umbraculifera is native to the Malabar Coast of South India and the lowland dry zone of Sri Lanka.

It has been widely planted as an ornamental throughout tropical South and [[guzman-thailand-southeast-asia-psychoactive-fungi-distribution]].

The species grows in well-drained soils in tropical monsoon climates with distinct wet and dry seasons.

It occurs naturally in open woodlands, forest margins, and grasslands at low elevations.

Elevation range in native habitats is from sea level to approximately 300 meters.

Annual rainfall in native habitats ranges from 1,000 to 2,500 millimeters.

The palm prefers well-drained loamy or sandy soils but tolerates a range of substrates.

Natural populations have declined significantly due to habitat loss and over-exploitation.

## Cultivation

Propagation is from seed, which is large and germinates slowly over 3–12 months.

Seedlings require partial shade and consistent moisture during the first several years.

Growth is relatively slow during the juvenile phase but accelerates as the palm establishes.

The species is widely cultivated as an ornamental in tropical botanical gardens and landscapes.

Its massive size at maturity limits its use to large parks, avenues, and estate grounds.

The palm requires full sun and ample space, with a mature crown spread of 8–12 meters.
