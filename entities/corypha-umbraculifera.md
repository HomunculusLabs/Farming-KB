## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Corypha umbraculifera** L. belongs to the family Arecaceae, subfamily Coryphoideae, tribe Corypheae.

The genus Corypha comprises only 2–3 species, making it one of the smallest palm genera.

The trunk is massive, cylindrical, and smooth, reaching 20–30 meters in height and 60–100 centimeters in diameter.

It is gray to brown and bears prominent, close-set leaf scars that encircle the stem.

The crown consists of 30–40 large, costapalmate leaves arranged in a graceful, hemispherical pattern.

Leaves are the largest of any palm, reaching 4–5 meters in diameter with a sturdy petiole up to 3 meters long.

Each leaf is divided into 80–120 stiff, pointed segments radiating from a central point.

The leaf segments are dark green above and paler beneath, with fibrous margins.

Elevation range in native habitats is from sea level to approximately 300 meters.

## Cultivation

Propagation is from seed, which is large and germinates slowly over 3–12 months.

Seedlings require partial shade and consistent moisture during the first several years.

Growth is relatively slow during the juvenile phase but accelerates as the palm establishes.

The species is widely cultivated as an ornamental in tropical botanical gardens and landscapes.

Its massive size at maturity limits its use to large parks, avenues, and estate grounds.

The palm requires full sun and ample space, with a mature crown spread of 8–12 meters.
