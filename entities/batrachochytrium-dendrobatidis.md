# Batrachochytrium dendrobatidis

**Batrachochytrium dendrobatidis** (Bd) is a chytrid fungus and the causative agent of chytridiomycosis, an infectious disease devastating to amphibian populations worldwide.

It is considered one of the most destructive pathogens ever described.

It has been implicated in the decline or extinction of hundreds of amphibian species and is recognized as a key driver of the global amphibian biodiversity crisis.

## Taxonomy and Classification

B. dendrobatidis belongs to the phylum Chytridiomycota.

The Chytridiomycota are the only group of true fungi that produce motile zoospores with flagella.

Chytrids were among the earliest-diverging fungal lineages.

B. dendrobatidis represents a notable example of a basal fungal lineage evolving to become a highly virulent vertebrate pathogen.

Its phylogenetic position was elucidated through [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-central-america-caribbean]], South America, and Australia.

In these regions it has driven dozens of species to extinction.

Some affected species experienced population crashes of over 90 percent within months of the pathogen's arrival.

The global scale of B. dendrobatidis impact has drawn comparisons to the most destructive wildlife diseases in history.
