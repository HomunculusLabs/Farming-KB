## Overview

Campanula latifolia, the giant bellflower or wide-leaved bellflower, is a robust herbaceous perennial in the family Campanulaceae. It is one of the tallest European bellflower species, reaching heights of 90–150 cm in flower. Native to temperate woodlands across Europe and western Asia, it produces showy violet-blue, nodding campanulate flowers in terminal racemes during mid to late summer.

The species is valued in ornamental horticulture for its architectural stature and shade tolerance. It serves as an important nectar source for long-tongued bumblebees.

In its native range, Campanula latifolia is one of the most characteristic species of ancient woodland ground flora. Its presence often indicates long-established, undisturbed woodland habitat with healthy soil ecology.

The species hybridizes naturally with [[glechoma-hederacea]], and Dryopteris filix-mas in ancient woodland communities. In the British Isles, it is a component of the Fraxinus–Acer–Mercurialis woodland type (W8 National Vegetation Classification).
