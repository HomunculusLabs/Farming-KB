## Overview

**Amaranthus cruentus** is an annual herbaceous plant in the family Amaranthaceae,
cultivated as both a grain pseudocereal and a leafy green vegetable.

Commonly known as purple amaranth, red amaranth, or Mexican grain amaranth, it is
one of three principal grain amaranth species alongside *A. hypochondriacus* and
*A. caudatus*.

The species originated in the highlands of [[dighton-fungal-drought-tolerance-plant-water-relations]] and nutrient scavenging in poor soils.

**Leaves** are alternate, ovate to lanceolate, 5–15 cm long and 3–8 cm wide,
with entire margins and long petioles.

Leaf color ranges from green to deep purple-red, with the most pigmented
cultivars used both ornamentally and as a source of betacyanin pigments.

**Inflorescences** are terminal and axillary panicles, 15–60 cm long, densely
packed with smallglomerulate flowers.

Bracts and bracteoles are prominently colored red, orange, or green, giving
the panicle its characteristic striking appearance.

Flowers are unisexual, pentamerous, with the pistillate flowers bearing
superior ovaries and the staminate flowers producing abundant pollen.

**Seeds** are lenticular, 1–1.5 mm in diameter, with a smooth glossy surface.
Seed color ranges from cream and gold to brown and black depending on cultivar.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Amaranthus cruentus* is believed to have been domesticated in the valleys of
[[psilocybe-southeast-asia-pacific]].

Today it is widely grown in Nigeria, Benin, Togo, India, Nepal, China, and
throughout [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-caribbean-central-america]] as both a grain and a leaf vegetable.

## Cultivation

Purple amaranth is adapted to a wide range of tropical and subtropical
conditions, from sea level to 2,400 m elevation.

It performs best in well-drained sandy loam soils with pH 5.5–7.5 and
requires moderate fertility, though it can produce acceptable yields on
marginal soils.
