# 4-D

4-D (3,5-dimethoxy-4-trideuteromethoxy-phenethylamine) is a deuterium-substituted analogue of mescaline described in [[2c-b]].

Open-eye visuals include movement and flow of objects with dilated pupils.

The subject described a state of "alert lassitude," "interested detachment," or "vibrating equanimity."

Upon reflection, several important mental shifts had occurred despite the experience feeling uneventful in the moment: "It was a day that allowed some peaceful gear-shifting in the mind."

At 400 milligrams, nausea peaks at about one hour and continues at a low level throughout.

Color shows were enjoyed considerably: trees changed color in wave-like patterns, book covers became distorted, brightly lit items underwent color changes.

Concentrating on objects easily triggered visual changes.

An element of confusion lasted approximately 4-5 hours.

Sleep was achievable that evening without difficulty.

## Scientific Context

4-D was designed to test the hypothesis that the ability to remove methyl groups from aromatic ethers might correlate with endogenous schizophrenia.

Two reports exist describing metabolic products of mescaline that have lost the methyl group on the 4-position oxygen.

It was hypothesized that these metabolites might be produced in abnormal quantities in mentally ill subjects, and that deuterium substitution (which forms a stronger carbon-deuterium bond) might alter this [[gluconeogenesis-biochemistry-metabolic-pathway]].

Some studies have supported the idea that the ability to remove methyl groups from aromatic ethers might be correlated to endogenous schizophrenia.

It is possible that in such individuals, the effects of substituting trideuteromethyl groups for normal methyl groups might result in psychopharmacological differences of action.

Similar deuterium substitution patterns are feasible with TMA and related 3,4,5-trimethoxy-substituted analogues.

The compound 3,5-D (3,5-bis-trideuteromethoxy-4-methoxyphenethylamine) might reveal quantitative response differences relevant to this theory.
