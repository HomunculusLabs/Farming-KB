## Devonport Recycling System

## Description

The Devonport Recycling System is a municipal solid waste recycling programme
operated by the borough of Devonport in Auckland, New Zealand. Cited by Bill
Mollison as an exemplary model of community waste management, this system
achieves something rare: it returns a cash benefit to households rather than
charging rates for waste disposal. The system operates on the principle that
waste is a resource, and that careful separation at source enables high-value
[[solomon-humanure-recycling-and-composting]].

## Classification

- **Type:** Municipal waste management system
- **Category:** [[community-recycling-and-waste-recovery-systems]], composting, resource recovery
- **Location:** Devonport, Auckland, New Zealand
- **Domain:** [[permaculture-waste-management]], community design
- **Key Innovation:** Colour-coded calendar-based waste separation

## Key Facts

- Operated by the borough of Devonport in Auckland, New Zealand
- Returns a cash benefit to households (versus rates payment in non-recycling areas)
- System has two key features that make it work:
  1. **Colour-coded calendar system:** The borough issues an annual calendar
     that is colour-coded, with only one category of waste collected per day
     (clean glass, metal, tyres, paper, organic waste, oils, etc.). No mixed
     loads are collected — separation begins with the consumer.
  2. **Supervised waste disposal site:** Loads must arrive in a single category.
     Wood is sorted into useful wood (community woodwork centre), firewood
     (distributed to elderly residents), and chipping/mulch wood.

- All organic waste is composted using a small tractor windrow system
- All oils are collected, filtered, and resold as lubrication oils
- Compost and mulch are sold, with surplus used to build up a "ziggurat"
- The ziggurat is an ascending spiral ramp made of broken pipe, brick, clay,
  concrete, and clean fill — community organic gardens follow the ascending fill
- Mixed waste is sorted at the disposal site; saleable and recyclable items are
  grouped in clearly marked areas

## The Ziggurat Concept

The Devonport waste site features an innovative "ziggurat" — an ascending spiral
ramp built from broken pipe, brick, clay, concrete, and clean fill. As the
ziggurat ascends, compost and mulch from the waste processing carpet the surface,
and community organic gardens follow the fill upward. This creates productive
growing space from what would otherwise be a landfill, and demonstrates the
[[permaculture-principle-apply-self-regulation]] of turning problems into solutions.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

1. **Compost Production Model:** The windrow composting of all organic waste
   provides a direct template for municipal-scale compost production — the same
   compost can be used as substrate material for [[mushroom-cultivation]] or as
   garden amendments.

2. **Mushroom Substrate from Waste:** The chipping/mulch wood stream and organic
   waste stream are ideal feedstocks for [[mushroom-substrate-preparation]]. Wood
   chips from the sorting process can be used directly for wood-loving species.

3. **Closed-Loop Nutrient Cycling:** This system embodies the permaculture
   principle of "[[holmgren-produce-no-waste-waste-hierarchy-refuse-reduce-reuse-repair-recycle]]" — every waste stream becomes an input to
   another process. [[spent-mushroom-substrate]] becomes compost, which becomes
   garden soil, which produces food waste, which becomes compost again.

4. **Community-Scale Composting:** The small tractor windrow system is directly
   applicable to community-scale [[fungicide-treated-seed-hazard-mushroom-substrate-preparation]] and composting.

5. **Oil Recycling:** The filtration and reuse of waste oils demonstrates
   resource recovery relevant to small-scale permaculture operations where
   equipment maintenance generates waste oils.

6. **Urban Cultivation Integration:** The ziggurat concept — building productive
   garden space on reclaimed "waste" land — can incorporate [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]]
   in the cooler, moister lower levels of the structure.

## Design Principles for Permaculture Application

- Source separation is the foundation — waste must be sorted at point of generation
- Calendar-based collection enforces discipline without being burdensome
- Community woodwork centres add value to waste wood before it becomes fuel or mulch
- Prioritise elderly and vulnerable community members for direct benefits
- Supervised disposal sites prevent contamination of separated waste streams
- Sell products (compost, mulch, filtered oil) to generate revenue offsetting costs
