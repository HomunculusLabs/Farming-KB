# Psilocin

**Psilocin** (4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine, abbreviated as 4-HO-[[dmt]]) is the primary pharmacologically active metabolite of psilocybin and a naturally occurring psychedelic tryptamine alkaloid. It is found alongside its phosphorylated prodrug psilocybin in psychedelic mushrooms and is directly responsible for the hallucinogenic effects associated with their consumption.

## Chemical Properties

- **IUPAC Name:** 3-[2-(Dimethylamino)ethyl]-1H-indol-4-ol
- **Molecular Formula:** C₁₂H₁₆N₂O
- **Molecular Weight:** 204.27 g/mol
- **CAS Number:** 520-53-6 (historically conflated with psilocybin); 520-53-6 for psilocybin, 521-61-9 for psilocin
- **Appearance:** White to off-white crystalline solid
- **Solubility:** Moderately soluble in water; soluble in alcohols and dilute acids
- **Melting Point:** Approximately 173 to 176°C

Psilocin is structurally analogous to the neurotransmitter serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine, 5-HT), differing by the substitution of two methyl groups on the terminal amine nitrogen (N,N-dimethyl versus primary amine). This structural similarity underlies its affinity for serotonin receptors and is a shared feature among the classical psychedelic tryptamines, which also include DMT, 5-MeO-DMT, and LSD.

## Relationship to Psilocybin

Psilocin exists in fresh and dried mushroom tissue alongside its phosphorylated precursor, psilocybin (4-PO-DMT). The interconversion between the two compounds is a critical factor in the preparation and storage of psilocybin mushrooms:

- **In vivo:** Alkaline phosphatase enzymes in the liver, intestinal mucosa, and blood plasma rapidly dephosphorylate psilocybin to psilocin following ingestion. This conversion is essential for psychoactivity, as psilocybin itself has negligible receptor affinity.
- **In dried mushrooms:** Psilocybin slowly dephosphorylates to psilocin over time, particularly under conditions of heat, moisture, and light exposure. Improperly stored dried mushrooms may show elevated psilocin-to-psilocybin ratios.
- **In fresh mushrooms:** The ratio of psilocybin to psilocin is typically between 2:1 and 10:1, depending on the species and growing conditions. Bigwood and Beug found that psilocybin generally predominates, though some species and individual specimens contain higher psilocin proportions.

## Pharmacology

Psilocin acts primarily as a partial agonist at the 5-HT₂A serotonin receptor, the receptor subtype most strongly associated with classical psychedelic effects. Additional receptor interactions include:

- **5-HT₁A:** Partial agonist activity contributes to anxiolytic and mood-modulating effects
- **5-HT₂C:** Agonist activity may influence anxiety and perceptual changes
- **5-HT₂B:** Agonist activity at higher concentrations
- **Dopamine receptors:** Weak affinity, not considered clinically significant

The 5-HT₂A receptor activation in layer V pyramidal neurons of the prefrontal cortex leads to increased cortical excitability and altered thalamocortical signaling, which is believed to underlie the characteristic visual hallucinations, altered sense of self, and changes in emotional processing experienced under psilocin's influence.

Functional neuroimaging studies have shown that psilocin decreases activity in the default mode network (DMN), a brain network associated with self-referential thinking and autobiographical memory. This DMN desynchronization correlates with the subjective experience of ego dissolution and is thought to be a key mechanism underlying the therapeutic potential of psychedelic-assisted psychotherapy.

## Pharmacokinetics

- **Onset:** 20 to 60 minutes after oral administration (faster than psilocybin due to bypassing dephosphorylation)
- **Peak plasma levels:** 60 to 100 minutes post-ingestion
- **Duration of subjective effects:** 3 to 6 hours
- **Elimination half-life:** Approximately 1.5 to 3 hours
- **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic, via monoamine oxidase (MAO-A) oxidation to [[4-hydroxyindole]]-3-acetaldehyde, which is further oxidized to 4-hydroxyindole-3-acetic acid (4-HIAA) and conjugated with glucuronic acid for renal excretion.

## Stability and Storage

Psilocin is considerably less stable than psilocybin. It is sensitive to oxidation, particularly in solution and under alkaline conditions, degrading to blue-colored oxidation products (the same compounds responsible for the characteristic blue bruising of psilocybin mushrooms). For analytical purposes, mushroom extracts containing psilocin are typically acidified and stored at low temperature to minimize degradation.

## Detection Methods

Psilocin can be detected in biological samples including blood, urine, and hair. Standard detection methods include:
- **Immunoassay screening:** Cross-reacts with some psilocin antibodies, though commercial availability is limited
- **LC-MS/MS:** The gold standard for quantitative analysis with detection limits in the low ng/mL range
- **GC-MS:** Requires derivatization due to the thermal instability of underivatized psilocin

Detection windows vary by matrix: typically 12 to 24 hours in blood, 1 to 3 days in urine, and potentially weeks to months in hair depending on hair length and cosmetic treatments.
## Synthesis

The first total synthesis of psilocin was reported by Albert Hofmann and colleagues at Sandoz Laboratories in 1959, following the isolation of psilocybin from [[psilocybe-cubensis]] in 1958. Several synthetic routes have since been developed, with the most notable contributions from Shirota, Hakamata, and Goda, who published a concise large-scale synthesis. Common synthetic strategies include:
- **Indole-based routes**: Starting from 4-hydroxyindole or protected derivatives, with introduction of the dimethylaminoethyl side chain through various coupling methods
- **Tryptamine-based routes**: Hydroxylation of DMT or N,N-dimethyltryptamine at the 4-position, though direct hydroxylation of the indole ring presents regiochemical challenges
- **Reductive routes**: Reduction of psilocybin (dephosphorylation) provides psilocin directly, though this is not practical for large-scale synthesis since psilocybin itself must first be produced

Modern synthetic approaches have focused on improving yields, reducing the number of steps, and avoiding the use of highly toxic or regulated reagents. The synthesis of psilocin and its phosphate ester (psilocybin) has become increasingly relevant in the context of clinical research into psychedelic-assisted therapy, where Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP)-grade material is required for human trials.
## Receptor Binding Profile

Detailed receptor binding studies have characterized psilocin's affinity for various serotonin receptor subtypes (expressed as Ki values):
- **5-HT₂A**: 6–12 nM (primary site of psychedelic action)
- **5-HT₁A**: 56–112 nM (may contribute to anxiolytic and mood effects)
- **5-HT₂C**: 18–25 nM (modulatory role in anxiety and perception)
- **5-HT₂B**: 70–100 nM (potential cardiac safety concern at high doses)
- **5-HT₅A**: >10,000 nM (negligible affinity)
- **5-HT₆**: >10,000 nM (negligible affinity)
- **5-HT₇**: >1,000 nM (weak affinity)

The high affinity for 5-HT₂A, combined with functional partial agonist activity, is the primary pharmacological characteristic that distinguishes psilocin from other serotonergic compounds. The 5-HT₁A activity may contribute to the anxiolytic and prosocial effects reported in therapeutic settings. The affinity for 5-HT₂B receptors has raised theoretical concerns about cardiac valve effects at very high or chronic doses, though no clinically significant cardiac toxicity has been documented at typical recreational or therapeutic doses of psilocybin mushrooms.
## Structure-Activity Relationships

The structure-activity relationships (SAR) of psilocin and related tryptamines have been extensively studied, establishing several key pharmacophore requirements:
- The 4-hydroxyl group on the indole ring is essential for high-affinity 5-HT₂A binding; its removal (as in DMT) shifts receptor selectivity
- The N,N-dimethyl substitution on the terminal amine is optimal for 5-HT₂A affinity; N-monomethyl (norpsilocin) and N-diethyl substitutions retain significant but reduced activity
- The ethyl bridge between the indole nitrogen and the terminal amine is optimal at two carbons; homologation or shortening reduces potency
- Addition of a 5-methoxy group (as in 5-MeO-DMT) dramatically shifts the pharmacological profile toward non-visual, primarily somatic effects
- Phosphorylation of the 4-hydroxyl group (as in psilocybin) eliminates receptor affinity but creates a prodrug that is dephosphorylated in vivo

These SAR principles have guided the development of novel tryptamine derivatives for research purposes and have informed understanding of the molecular basis of psychedelic action.
## Therapeutic Applications

Psilocin (administered as its prodrug psilocybin) has shown significant therapeutic potential in clinical trials for several psychiatric conditions:
- **Treatment-resistant depression**: Phase II trials have demonstrated rapid and sustained antidepressant effects following single or limited-dose administration, with effects persisting for months in some patients
- **End-of-life anxiety**: Studies in patients with terminal cancer diagnoses have shown significant reductions in death-related anxiety and depression
- **Substance use disorders**: Preliminary trials for alcohol use disorder and tobacco smoking cessation have shown promising results
- **Obsessive-compulsive disorder**: Early studies suggested potential benefit, though larger trials are needed

The mechanism of therapeutic action is thought to involve both acute psychological experiences (such as mystical-type experiences) and longer-term neuroplastic changes, including increased neural connectivity and synaptic density in cortical regions. Functional neuroimaging has shown that psilocin-induced 5-HT₂A activation leads to global increases in brain network integration, breaking down the normally segregated patterns of default mode network activity that are associated with rigid, maladaptive thought patterns in depression and anxiety disorders.
## Comparison with Other Classical Psychedelics

Psilocin occupies a distinctive position among the classical psychedelic compounds:
- Compared to LSD, psilocin has a shorter duration (3–6 hours versus 8–12 hours), lower potency per unit weight, and a somewhat different subjective quality often described as more "organic" or "natural" by experienced users
- Compared to DMT, psilocin is orally active without the need for monoamine oxidase inhibition, has a longer duration, and produces a less intensely overwhelming experience at standard doses
- Compared to mescaline, psilocin is significantly more potent and has a shorter duration, with more pronounced visual effects relative to somatic effects
- The subjective effects profile of psilocin is often described as intermediate between the visual intensity of DMT and the extended, contemplative quality of LSD

These differences reflect the distinct receptor binding profiles and pharmacokinetic properties of each compound, which in turn influence the quality, intensity, and duration of the subjective experience.
