# Psilocin (4-HO-DMT)

Psilocin (4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine, 4-HO-DMT) is a
classical serotonergic psychedelic and the primary
pharmacologically active metabolite of psilocybin. It acts as a
partial agonist at the 5-HT2A receptor, producing profound
alterations in perception, mood, and cognition characteristic of
the [[leary-psychedelic-experience-bardo-mapping]].

First isolated from Psilocybe mushrooms in 1959 by Albert Hofmann
at Sandoz, psilocin was identified as the active principle after
psilocybin was found largely inactive until enzymatically
dephosphorylated. In humans, psilocybin is rapidly converted to
psilocin by alkaline phosphatase enzymes before entering systemic
circulation.

## Chemical Structure and Properties

- **IUPAC name:** 3-[2-(Dimethylamino)ethyl]-1H-indol-4-ol
- **Molecular formula:** C₁₂H₁₆N₂O
- **Molecular weight:** 204.27 g/mol
- **CAS number:** 520-53-6
- **Appearance:** White to off-white crystalline powder; darkens
  on air/light exposure due to oxidation to quinones
- **Solubility:** Moderate in water, ethanol, methanol; poor in
  nonpolar solvents. Free base is more lipid-soluble than
  psilocybin, facilitating BBB penetration.
- **Melting point:** 173-176°C (decomposes)
- **pKa:** ~9.5 (amine), ~9.9 (phenol)

The phenolic 4-hydroxyl is the key feature distinguishing
psilocin from DMT, dramatically altering [[tihkal-receptor-binding-serotonin]] —
converting a short-acting tryptamine into a classical psychedelic
with enhanced 5-HT2A affinity and longer duration (4-6 hr vs
30-90 min for DMT). The 4-hydroxyl also makes psilocin
susceptible to glucuronidation.

## Pharmacology

### Receptor Binding Profile

| Receptor | Ki (nM) | Functional Activity |
|----------|---------|-------------------|
| 5-HT2A | 6-12 | Partial agonist |
| 5-HT2C | 12-25 | Partial agonist |
| 5-HT1A | 30-50 | Partial agonist |
| 5-HT1B | 40-80 | Weak agonist |
| 5-HT7 | 20-40 | Agonist |
| 5-HT2B | 50-100 | Agonist |
| D2 | >10,000 | Negligible |

Effects are mediated through 5-HT2A activation in cortical layer
V pyramidal neurons, promoting entropic neural activity. The
partial agonist profile (lower efficacy than serotonin) may
explain favorable safety. 5-HT1A activation contributes
anxiolytic and antidepressant effects. fMRI shows decreased
[[psilocybin-default-mode-network-ego-dissolution]] network connectivity with magnitude correlating to
mystical experience intensity and therapeutic outcomes.

### Comparison with Related Tryptamines

| Compound | MW | 5-HT2A Ki | Duration |
|----------|------|-----------|----------|
| DMT | 188 | ~200 nM | 30-90 min |
| Psilocin (4-HO-DMT) | 204 | 6-12 nM | 4-6 hr |
| Psilocybin (4-PO-DMT) | 284 | Inactive | 4-6 hr |
| [[5-meo-dmt-entity]] | 218 | ~10 nM | 15-45 min |

The 4-substituted tryptamine series shares common pharmacological
profiles. 4-AcO-DMT is widely regarded as a psilocin prodrug
producing nearly identical effects. Differences are subtle —
primarily onset time, body load, and visual character.

## Biosynthesis in Mushrooms

Psilocin is produced through a four-enzyme pathway from
L-tryptophan, fully elucidated in 2017 through genomic studies of
P. cubensis and P. cyanescens:

1. **L-Tryptophan → Tryptamine:** Decarboxylation by psiD.
2. **Tryptamine → 4-Hydroxytryptamine:** C-4 hydroxylation by
   cytochrome P450 monooxygenase psiH.
3. **4-Hydroxytryptamine → Psilocybin:** N-methylation by
   SAM-dependent methyltransferase psiK.
4. **Psilocybin → Psilocin:** Non-enzymatic dephosphorylation
   during drying or by alkaline phosphatase.

The gene cluster (psiD, psiH, psiK, psiM) is conserved across
phylogenetically distant species, suggesting horizontal gene
transfer. Total alkaloid content ranges 0.1-3.3% dry weight.

## Natural Occurrence

{w("[[tryptamine-chemistry-psilocin-and-psilocybin-synthesis]] occur in 200+ species across 15+ genera:")}

- **Psilocybe:** P. cubensis, P. semilanceata, P. cyanescens,
  P. azurescens, P. mexicana. Potency 0.2-3.3%.
- **Panaeolus:** P. cyanescens, P. cinctulus. Generally lower
  potency than Psilocybe.
- **Gymnopilus:** G. spectabilis, G. junonius. Wood-inhabiting
  species with variable potency.
- **Inocybe:** I. corydalina, I. aeruginascens. Not recommended
  for foraging due to toxic lookalikes.
- **Pluteus:** P. salicinus, P. americanus. Hardwood logs.

Within fruiting bodies, psilocybin is typically 1.5-3x higher
than psilocin. Peak alkaloid levels occur just before veil tearing.

## Metabolism and Pharmacokinetics

Psilocybin is rapidly dephosphorylated by alkaline phosphatase in
gut and liver. Conversion is essentially complete. After oral
psilocybin (10-25 mg), psilocin appears in plasma within 20-40
min, peaking at 60-100 min. Primary elimination via
glucuronidation by UGT1A9/1A10 to psilocin-O-glucuronide.

Elimination half-life is 2-3 hours with effects resolving over
4-6 hours. ~50-65% excreted as glucuronide in urine within 24
hours. Minor pathways include N-demethylation to norpsilocin and
MAO-A oxidation.

## Analytical Detection

- **HPLC-UV:** Standard pharmacopeial method; ~0.1 μg/mL at 267 nm.
- **LC-MS/MS:** Gold standard; detection 0.01-0.1 ng/mL. MRM: m/z 205→160.
- **GC-MS:** Requires BSTFA derivatization.

Scheduled in most jurisdictions. US Schedule I (separately from
psilocybin). UN Convention Schedule I. Recent changes: Oregon
2020, Colorado 2022, Australia 2023.
