# Spodoptera frugiperda

## Description

Spodoptera frugiperda, commonly known as the fall armyworm, is a moth species
whose larvae are major agricultural pests. Native to the tropical and
subtropical regions of the Americas, S. frugiperda feeds on a wide range of
host plants including grasses, corn, sorghum, and many vegetable crops. It has
become a globally significant invasive pest, spreading to Africa, Asia, and
Oceania since 2016. In [[fungal-ecology]], S. frugiperda serves as a model
herbivore for studying endophyte-mediated plant defense.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Eukaryota
- **Kingdom:** Animalia
- **Phylum:** Arthropoda
- **Class:** Insecta
- **Order:** Lepidoptera

## Key Facts

- **Polyphagous pest:** Larvae feed on over 80 plant species, with corn (maize),
  sorghum, rice, and pasture grasses being the most economically important
  hosts.
- **Endophyte-mediated deterrence:** Clay (1990) documented that fungal
  endophytes in grasses reduce S. frugiperda survival and development rate in
  Cenchrus (sandbur) host plants.
- **Elevated CO2 effects:** Marks and Lincoln (1996) studied the effect of
  elevated carbon dioxide on grazing of tall fescue by S. frugiperda. While
  total consumption increased under elevated CO2, the proportion of plant
  consumed relative to plant size remained similar between endophyte-infected
  and uninfected plants.
- **Endophyte consistency:** The degree of protection afforded to host plants
  by endophytes (Acremonium coenophialum) did not change under elevated CO2
  conditions, suggesting stable defensive benefits regardless of atmospheric
  conditions.
- **Global invasive species:** Since 2016, S. frugiperda has spread to over 70
  countries across Africa, Asia, and Oceania, causing billions of dollars in
  crop losses.
- **Multiple host strains:** Two strains exist - the corn strain (preferred
  host: corn, sorghum, cotton) and the rice strain (preferred host: rice,
  pasture grasses, millet), with different pesticide resistance profiles.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Endophyte bioassay species:** S. frugiperda is commonly used as a test
  organism in laboratory bioassays to quantify the antiherbivore effects of
  fungal endophytes in pasture and crop grasses.
- **Climate change interactions:** Research on S. frugiperda feeding under
  elevated CO2 informs predictions of how plant-endophyte-herbivore
  interactions will respond to climate change.
- **Biological control target:** Endophyte-infected grass varieties that deter
  S. frugiperda offer a component of [[integrated-pest-management]] for this
  destructive pest.
- **Resistance management:** The species has developed resistance to multiple
  insecticide classes and Bt toxins, increasing the value of endophyte-mediated
  resistance as a complementary control strategy.

## Ecological Interactions

- Larvae deterred by endophyte-infected grasses (Festuca, Cenchrus) producing
  alkaloid secondary metabolites.
- Feeding behavior influenced by plant nutritional quality, which changes under
  elevated atmospheric CO2.
- Serves as prey for numerous natural enemies including parasitoid wasps,
  predatory bugs, and entomopathogenic fungi.
- Fall armyworm populations show density-dependent regulation by natural
  enemies including Cordyceps militaris and other fungal pathogens.
