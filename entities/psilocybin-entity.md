# Psilocybin
**O-phosphoryl-4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine**
- **Class:** Indolealkylamine tryptamine / serotonergic psychedelic
- **Empirical formula:** C₁₂H₁₇N₂O₄P
- **Molecular weight:** 284.25 g/mol
- **CAS:** 520-52-5
- **Legal status:** Schedule I (US), Class A (UK), Schedule III (Canada),
  Table I (UN Convention on Psychotropic Substances)

## Overview
Psilocybin is the primary psychoactive prodrug found in psilocybin-producing
mushrooms across the genera Psilocybe, Panaeolus, Copelandia, Gymnopilus,
Pluteus, Inocybe, and Conocybe. First isolated by Albert Hofmann in 1958
from Psilocybe mexicana, it is dephosphorylated in vivo by alkaline
phosphatase to psilocin (4-HO-DMT), the pharmacologically active metabolite
that acts as a partial agonist at 5-HT₂A serotonin receptors. Psilocybin
has been used for millennia in Mesoamerican indigenous ceremonies and is
currently under clinical investigation for treatment-resistant depression,
end-of-life anxiety, PTSD, and substance use disorders.

## Chemical Identity
### Structure and Properties
- **IUPAC name:** [3-(2-Dimethylaminoethyl)-1H-indol-4-yl] dihydrogen
  phosphate
- **SMILES:** CN(C)CCc1c[nH]c2ccc(OP(=O)(O)O)cc12
- **Appearance:** White crystalline powder when pure
- **Solubility:** Soluble in water; moderately soluble in methanol/ethanol
- **Melting point:** 220–228 °C (decomposes)
- **Stability:** Degrades to psilocin upon heat, moisture, or alkaline pH

### Chemical Relationships
- **Prodrug of:** Psilocin (4-HO-DMT), the active serotonergic metabolite
- **Structural analogs:** DMT, 5-MeO-DMT, bufotenin (5-HO-DMT),
  baeocystin (4-HO-NMT), norbaeocystin (4-HO-T)
- **Biosynthetic precursor:** L-Tryptophan → Tryptamine →
  4-Hydroxytryptamine → 4-PO-HO-tryptamine → Psilocybin

## Biosynthesis
The psilocybin biosynthetic pathway proceeds through four enzymatic steps
from L-tryptophan:
1. **Decarboxylation:** L-tryptophan → tryptamine via aromatic L-amino
   acid decarboxylase (AADC)
2. **N-methylation:** Tryptamine → N,N-dimethyltryptamine via PsiM
   (monoamine oxidase)
3. **Hydroxylation:** DMT → 4-hydroxy-DMT (psilocin) via PsiH
   (cytochrome P450)
4. **Phosphorylation:** Psilocin → psilocybin via PsiK (4-hydroxytryptamine
   kinase)
The psilocybin gene cluster (psiD, psiH, psiK, psiM + regulatory factor)
was characterized independently by Fricke et al. and Reynolds et al. in
2017, revealing conservation across distantly related fungal lineages and
suggesting possible horizontal gene transfer events.

## Pharmacology
### Receptor Binding Profile
| Receptor | Affinity (Ki) | Activity |
|----------|--------------|----------|
| 5-HT₂A | ~20–40 nM | Partial agonist |
| 5-HT₂C | ~30–50 nM | Partial agonist |
| 5-HT₁A | ~1–5 µM | Agonist (weak) |
| 5-HT₂B | ~100 nM | Agonist |
| Dopamine D₂ | >10 µM | Negligible |

### Mechanism of Action
Psilocybin is rapidly dephosphorylated to psilocin, which crosses the
blood-brain barrier and binds primarily to 5-HT₂A receptors in the
prefrontal cortex. Activation increases glutamate release, enhances
thalamocortical connectivity, and disrupts the default mode network (DMN).
The resulting decrease in DMN connectivity correlates with ego dissolution
and is hypothesized to drive therapeutic effects by increasing cognitive
flexibility and enabling emotional reprocessing.

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Onset:** 20–60 minutes (oral)
- **Peak plasma:** 80–120 minutes
- **Duration:** 4–6 hours
- **Half-life:** ~2.5–3 hours (psilocin); ~50 minutes (parent)
- **Excretion:** ~65% renal within 24 hours as psilocin metabolites

## Natural Occurrence
Psilocybin is confirmed in 200+ fungal species. Highest concentrations
are found in Psilocybe section Psilocybe and Copelandia:
- **Psilocybe azurescens:** Up to 1.78% total alkaloids (most potent)
- **Psilocybe cubensis:** 0.5–1.2% (most widely cultivated)
- **Psilocybe semilanceata:** 0.2–2.4% (variable by geography)
- **Psilocybe cyanescens:** 0.3–1.0%
- **Psilocybe bohemica (serbica):** 0.5–1.5%
- **Copelandia cyanescens:** 0.5–1.9%

## Historical Context
### Indigenous Use
Rock paintings in the Sahara (~7000–9000 BCE) and Mesoamerican mushroom
stone effigies (~1000 BCE–500 CE) indicate millennia of ritual use. The
best-documented traditions come from Mazatec communities in Oaxaca,
Mexico, where Psilocybe mexicana and P. caerulescens are used by
curanderos including María Sabina for divination and healing.

### Modern History
- **1957:** Wasson published "Seeking the Magic Mushroom" in LIFE
- **1958:** Hofmann isolated psilocybin and psilocin from P. mexicana
- **1959:** Sandoz marketed psilocybin as "Indocybin" for research
- **1966:** Schedule I placement halted research for ~25 years
- **2000s–present:** Clinical resurgence at Johns Hopkins, Imperial
  College London, and Usona Institute

## Therapeutic Research
- **Treatment-resistant depression:** Phase II/III trials (COMPASS
  Pathways) showing ~30% response at 3 weeks from single 25 mg dose
- **End-of-life anxiety:** Griffiths et al. (2016) demonstrated
  sustained depression/anxiety reduction in cancer patients
- **Alcohol use disorder:** NYU trial showing ~80% reduction in heavy
  drinking days at 8-month follow-up

## Legal Status
- **United States:** Schedule I federally; decriminalized in Oregon
  (licensed use), Colorado, and multiple cities
- **Netherlands:** Truffles (sclerotia) legal; fresh mushrooms banned 2008
- **Brazil:** Legal to possess and cultivate for personal use
- **Jamaica:** Fully legal; retreat centers operate openly
- **Canada:** Schedule III (reduced from I in 2022); expanded-access ongoing
