# Dichloran-Glycerol Medium (DG18)

## Description

Dichloran-glycerol medium (commonly designated DG18) is a selective culture medium used for the isolation and enumeration of xerophilic fungi from low-moisture foods and from arid environments with low water availabilities. The medium was recommended by Hocking and Pitt (1980) as superior to other standard media for isolating xerophilic fungi. It combines the antifungal agent dichloran (2,6-dichloro-4-nitroaniline) to suppress rapidly growing mucoraceous molds with glycerol as a solute to reduce water activity (aw) to levels that favor xerophilic and osmophilic fungi.

## Classification

- **Type:** Selective mycological culture medium
- **Category:** Low water activity medium
- **Primary use:** Isolation and enumeration of xerophilic fungi
- **Reference:** Hocking and Pitt 1980

## Key Facts

### Composition and Properties
- Contains glycerol as the primary solute to lower water activity (aw) of the medium
- Glycerol is preferred over sodium chloride (NaCl) for lowering aw because NaCl at high concentrations may be toxic to some fungi (Cooke and Whipps 1993; Pitt and Hocking 1997)
- Contains dichloran to suppress fast-growing molds, allowing slower-growing xerophiles to be detected
- Referenced as a standard medium in Appendix II of the source text

### Water Activity and Solute Concentrations
- Glycerol solutions at different molalities produce specific water activities:
  - 0.277 molal glycerol → aw 0.995
  - 2.21 molal glycerol → aw 0.960
  - 5.57 molal glycerol → aw 0.900
  - 11.5 molal glycerol → aw 0.800
- DG18 typically targets aw values around 0.95 or below
- The relationship between water activity and water potential at 25°C is described by the equation: Ψ = 8.314T ln(aw)/Vw

### Comparison with Other Solute Systems
- Glycerol is the preferred solute for general xerophile isolation
- Sodium chloride (NaCl) can be toxic to some fungi at high concentrations
- Sucrose is an alternative solute but has lower solubility limits
- The preferred solute depends on growth conditions and target fungal species

### Applications
- **Food mycology:** Isolation of xerophilic spoilage fungi from dried foods, grains, and stored products
- **Environmental mycology:** Isolation of fungi from arid and semi-arid soils with low water availability
- **Biodiversity surveys:** Detection of xerophilic fungal species in desert ecosystems and other water-limited environments
- **Ecological studies:** Assessment of fungal community composition in xeric environments

### Selective Properties
- The reduced water activity selectively inhibits mesophilic fungi that require higher aw values
- Dichloran suppresses rapidly growing mucoraceous fungi (Rhizopus, Mucor) that might otherwise overgrow the plate
- Enables isolation of slow-growing xerophilic species from Aspergillus, Penicillium, Eurotium, and Wallemia

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

### Fungal Biodiversity Studies
- Essential for comprehensive fungal diversity surveys in arid environments
- Without selective media adjusted to appropriate aw levels, xerophilic fungi may not be isolated at all
- Hocking and Pitt (1980) demonstrated that DG18 outperformed other standard media for xerophile enumeration
- Critical for understanding fungal community dynamics in desert, semi-arid, and stored-product ecosystems

### Methodological Considerations
- Growth rates of isolated fungi may change at selected aw values depending on incubation temperature, pH, and nutrient availability (Wheeler et al. 1988; Hocking 1993)
- If a selective medium is not used, osmophiles may not be isolated from mixed fungal communities
- Diluents for plating should contain 20–30% glucose or fructose in peptone water to prevent osmotic shock (Corry 1987)

### Ecological Research
- Used to study fungal communities in xeric environments such as the Chihuahuan Desert
- Enables investigation of how fungal community composition varies with seasonal changes in organic matter distribution and moisture
- Supports research on wood-decomposer fungal communities in desert shrublands and woodrat middens

### Related Media
- Horikoshi I and II media for alkalophilic fungi
- Alkaline cornmeal agar (ACMA) for alkalophilic and alkali-tolerant fungi
- Czapek-Dox agar (diluted 1:50) for oligotrophic and rock-inhabiting fungi
- DRBC (dichloran-rose bengal-chloramphenicol) medium for general fungal enumeration
