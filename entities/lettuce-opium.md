# Lettuce Opium

## Description

"Lettuce opium" (also called lettuce extract or lactucarium) is a dried, tarry extract obtained from lettuce (Lactuca sativa) through [[cannabis-alchemy-overview]], lettuce opium was used as a binding agent for Iso hash — helping powdered cannabis material cohere into solid hash blocks. The name "opium" is colloquial and refers to the tar-like appearance of the extract, not to any opioid content.

## Classification

- **Category**: Botanical extract / hash binder
- **Source plant**: Lactuca sativa (common lettuce)
- **Active constituents**: Lactucin, lactucopicrin (sesquiterpene lactones)
- **Physical form**: Dark, tarry resin
- **Application domain**: Hash production, cannabis processing

## Key Facts

- A single head of lettuce can yield up to half an ounce (14 gm) of extract.
- Was retailed commercially for up to $5.00 per gram (1970s pricing).
- Can be produced cheaply using the Isomerizer or ISO-2 with [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]] Pharmacopeia until the early 20th century
- Used as a mild sedative and cough suppressant
- Sometimes called "lettuce opium" due to superficial resemblance to [[amanita-muscaria-medicinal-properties-heart-anxiety-dreams]]
- The binding principle applies to any herbal extract production requiring solidification
- Understanding natural binders is relevant to artisanal hash and charas production

## Safety Notes

- Lactucin and lactucopicrin can cause mild sedation
- Not a controlled substance — lettuce is legal worldwide
- The "opium" designation is purely descriptive of appearance and form, not chemical content
- No addiction potential or opioid activity
