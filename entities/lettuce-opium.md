# Lettuce Opium

## Description

"Lettuce opium" (also called lettuce extract or lactucarium) is a dried, tarry extract obtained from lettuce (Lactuca sativa) through [[cannabis-solvent-extraction]] and evaporation. In the context of [[cannabis-alchemy-overview]], lettuce opium was used as a binding agent for Iso hash — helping powdered cannabis material cohere into solid hash blocks. The name "opium" is colloquial and refers to the tar-like appearance of the extract, not to any opioid content.

## Classification

- **Category**: Botanical extract / hash binder
- **Source plant**: Lactuca sativa (common lettuce)
- **Active constituents**: Lactucin, lactucopicrin (sesquiterpene lactones)
- **Physical form**: Dark, tarry resin
- **Application domain**: Hash production, cannabis processing

## Key Facts

- A single head of lettuce can yield up to half an ounce (14 gm) of extract.
- Was retailed commercially for up to $5.00 per gram (1970s pricing).
- Can be produced cheaply using the Isomerizer or ISO-2 with [[isopropyl-alcohol]] extraction.
- Used in small amounts as a binder — overuse affects taste and smoking quality.
- Reported to smoke nicely and may contribute mildly to the psychoactive experience.
- Added to Iso hash during the kneading and pressing stage.
- The extract is produced by drying and pulverizing lettuce, then extracting with isopropyl alcohol.

## Preparation Method (per Cannabis Alchemy)

1. Chop lettuce as if making a salad
2. Dry thoroughly by spreading in the sun for several days or in a 250°F (120°C) oven
3. Pulverize the dried lettuce
4. Extract using the Isomerizer or ISO-2 with standard isopropyl alcohol method
5. Remove and collect the solvent
6. The tarry lettuce extract remains in the bottom of the reaction vessel
7. Scrape out and use as needed

## Historical Context

Lettuce opium has a long history in folk medicine and herbalism:

- Ancient Egyptians and Romans used wild lettuce (Lactuca virosa) extracts medicinally
- Lactucarium was an official drug in the [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]] Pharmacopeia until the early 20th century
- Used as a mild sedative and cough suppressant
- Sometimes called "lettuce opium" due to superficial resemblance to [[papaver-somniferum]] opium
- The psychoactive effects are mild compared to true opium

## As a Hash Binder

In Iso hash production:

- Added in small quantity during the kneading process
- Works by thumb in palm — kneading the hash with a small amount of lettuce extract and a few drops of water
- Causes the cannabis powder to bind into a cohesive solid mass
- Particularly useful when the oil-to-powder ratio is below 20% (where hash won't naturally bind)
- An alternative method involves placing powder in a heavy plastic bag with water and lettuce extract, then squeezing and pressing

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

While lettuce opium is primarily relevant to cannabis processing rather than cultivation:

- Demonstrates the use of botanical extracts as functional processing aids
- Lactuca virosa (wild lettuce) is sometimes cultivated for its [[amanita-muscaria-medicinal-properties-heart-anxiety-dreams]]
- The binding principle applies to any herbal extract production requiring solidification
- Understanding natural binders is relevant to artisanal hash and charas production

## Safety Notes

- Lactucin and lactucopicrin can cause mild sedation
- Not a controlled substance — lettuce is legal worldwide
- The "opium" designation is purely descriptive of appearance and form, not chemical content
- No addiction potential or opioid activity
