## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Taxonomy:**

- Kingdom: Plantae
- Clade: Tracheophytes
- Clade: Angiosperms
- Clade: Monocots
- Order: Asparagales
- Family: Orchidaceae
- Subfamily: Cypripedioideae
- Genus: *Paphiopedilum*
- Species: *Paphiopedilum insigne* (Wall. ex Lindl.) Pfitzer

The species was first collected by Nathaniel Wallich in northeastern India and described by Lindley in 1821. Pfitzer transferred it to *Paphiopedilum* in 1894.

**Morphology:**

Plants are stemless, producing fans of 4–8 linear-oblong leaves 15–30 cm long. Leaves are dark green above with paler green mottling beneath, thick and leathery in texture.

The inflorescence is a single-flowered scape 20–30 cm tall, bearing one flower 8–12 cm across. The dorsal sepal is large, rounded, white to pale green with green and brown spotting.

The synsepalum (fused lateral sepals) is smaller, greenish-brown, and positioned behind the pouch. Petals are spreading, wavy-edged, yellow-green to tan with brown spotting.

The labellum forms a deep pouch or "slipper" 4–6 cm long, brownish-green with darker veining. This pouch serves as a trapping mechanism for pollinators.

Roots are thick, fleshy, and hairy, adapted for growing in [[humus]] and moss on rock surfaces. Unlike many epiphytic orchids, *Paphiopedilum* roots lack the typical white velamen layer.

Plants lack pseudobulbs, storing water in their thick fleshy leaves and roots instead. This growth habit requires more consistent moisture than pseudobulb-bearing orchids.

The chromosome number is 2n = 26, consistent with most *Paphiopedilum* species. The species hybridizes readily with related species in the section *Paphiopedilum*, producing fertile offspring.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Paphiopedilum insigne* is native to northeastern India (Assam, Meghalaya), Bangladesh, and possibly northern Myanmar. Its range is restricted to the Khasi and Jaintia Hills of Meghalaya.

The species grows at elevations of 1,000–2,000 m on moss-covered rocks and cliff faces in humid montane forests. It is a lithophyte, rooted in accumulated humus and moss on rock surfaces.

*Paphiopedilum insigne* often grows on vertical rock faces in deeply shaded gorges, where it receives constant moisture from mist and seepage. Associated vegetation includes mosses, ferns, and other shade-tolerant lithophytes.

The habitat experiences a monsoon climate with heavy summer rainfall and a cooler, drier winter period. Temperatures range from 5–10°C in winter to 20–30°C in summer with high humidity year-round.

Wild populations are critically depleted due to intensive commercial collection over the past two centuries. The species is considered endangered in the wild, though it is abundant in cultivation worldwide.

## Ecology and Cultivation

**Ecology:**

The slipper-shaped labellum functions as a pollinator trap. Insects enter the pouch attracted by scent and color but cannot exit the same way, forcing them to pass the stigma and anthers while escaping.

Pollination is likely performed by ground-dwelling bees or wasps, though specific pollinators for *Paphiopedilum insigne* have not been conclusively documented in the wild.
