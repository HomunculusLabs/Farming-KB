# (E)-beta-Caryophyllene (BCP)

**Type:** Chemical / Dietary cannabinoid / Sesquiterpene
**Category:** CB2-selective cannabinoid agonist
**Also known as:** (E)-BCP, beta-caryophyllene, BCP,
(E)-beta-caryophyllene

## Overview
(E)-beta-Caryophyllene ((E)-BCP) is a non-psychoactive dietary
cannabinoid and sesquiterpene found in many spice plants. Discovered by
an international research team (Switzerland, Germany, Italy, U.S.) in
2008 to function as a selective CB2 receptor agonist, it represents the
first FDA-approved dietary plant-based cannabinoid. While some chemists
classify it as a terpene or terpenoid, its functional role as a CB2
activator makes it pharmacologically relevant as a cannabinoid.

## Key Details

### Chemical Properties
- Molecular formula: C15H24
- FDA-approved as a food additive (GRAS status)
- Selective CB2 receptor agonist (does not activate CB1)
- Non-psychoactive (CB1 is responsible for intoxication)
- Found in [[herbal-medicine]] systems. Their
therapeutic effects may be partly attributable to CB2 receptor
activation via (E)-BCP, a mechanism only scientifically confirmed in
2008 by the research groups led by K.H. Altmann, M. Karsak, and
A. Zimmer.

## Relevance
(E)-beta-Caryophyllene is significant because it provides a legal,
widely available, FDA-approved dietary pathway to CB2 receptor
activation. Patients can access cannabinoid therapy through common
kitchen spices without any legal restrictions or psychoactive effects.
This discovery (2008) shifted understanding of how traditional spice-
based medicine works at the molecular level. The fact that Ashanti
peppers contain 52-58% BCP compared to 12-35% in cannabis suggests
spice-based CB2 activation can exceed what cannabis provides. This
molecule bridges the gap between culinary traditions and cannabinoid
pharmacology.
