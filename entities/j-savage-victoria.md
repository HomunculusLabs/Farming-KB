# J. Savage (Victoria, Australia)

## Description

J. Savage is an Australian farmer from Victoria who successfully implemented a [[mixed-livestock-rotation]] system using multiple animal species in succession over mixed forb/grass/legume/forage plantings. Mollison cites Mr. Savage as a practical example of the "pig tractor" system and multi-species grazing, demonstrating that sequential animal grazing can reproduce the high yields of natural wildlife systems on domesticated farmland.

## Classification

- **Type**: Person — innovative farmer
- **Location**: Victoria, Australia
- **Field**: Mixed livestock management, rotational grazing, permaculture farming
- **Era**: Late 20th century (contemporary with 1988 publication of the Manual)

## Key Facts

- Operated a successful mixed livestock rotation system in Victoria, Australia
- Used horse/cow/sheep/goose/pig succession (or similar combinations) over mixed plantings
- Employed pigs as "ground preparation" — the "pig tractor" method of ploughing ground for new sowings and root sets
- Identified comfrey as a key element in the system, along with lucerne and grasses
- Achieved results that attempted to reproduce the high yields of natural wildlife systems

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

Savage's system exemplifies several core permaculture principles:

1. **Multi-species integration**: Rather than single-species grazing, Savage's rotation uses each animal species to prepare the sward or browse for its successor. Each animal selects different forage, controls different weeds, and deposits different manure patterns.

2. **The pig tractor**: Pigs are used well in advance of other species to "plough" the ground through their natural rooting behaviour. This eliminates the need for mechanical tractors, improves soil structure, and prepares seedbeds for comfrey, lucerne, and other forage plantings. The system "obviates the use of mechanised tractors and improves soils."

3. **Comfrey as keystone forage**: Comfrey (*Symphytum officinale*) serves as a deep-rooted, high-protein forage that persists through the rooting activity of pigs and provides reliable browse for subsequent grazing species. Its deep root system mines minerals from subsoil layers.

4. **Biomimicry of natural systems**: By rotating multiple animal species with different grazing habits and forage preferences, the system attempts to replicate the diverse browsing pressure of natural wildlife ecosystems, where herbivore guilds of varying body size and diet maintain plant diversity.

## Practical Implications

Mollison recommends trials of this system in any area, "choosing a succession of animal species following each other at optimum densities and intervals." The key variables are:

- Selection of 4-8 animal species with complementary grazing habits
- Optimal stocking density per species for each paddock
- Timing of rotation based on observed forage regeneration
- Integration of permanent forage plantings (comfrey, lucerne) with seasonal growth
