## Overview

Lychnis coronaria, commonly known as rose campion or [[lychnis-chalcedonica]] (Maltese cross) and Lychnis flos-cuculi (ragged robin).

These three Lychnis species share the characteristic of showy flowers in shades of red or pink, adapted to butterfly pollination. They are frequently grown together in cottage garden schemes.

## Morphology

Plants grow 60–90 cm tall in flower, with a basal rosette 20–30 cm across. The entire plant is densely covered in white, woolly trichomes that give it a striking silvery appearance.

Basal leaves are oblanceolate to elliptic, 8–15 cm long, with entire margins. Stem leaves are smaller, lanceolate, and sessile, clasping the upright flowering stems.

Flowers are produced in loose cymes at the top of each stem from June through August. Each flower is 2.5–3.5 cm in diameter with five notched petals in vivid magenta-pink.

The calyx is tubular, 15–18 mm long, with five teeth and prominent veins. It is also covered in woolly hairs, giving the flower buds a silvery appearance before opening.

A white-flowered form (f. albiflora) and a double-flowered form are known in cultivation. The white form is sometimes sold as 'Alba' or 'Angel's Blush.'

The fruit is an ovoid capsule containing numerous small, dark brown, kidney-shaped seeds. Each plant can produce several thousand seeds over a single [[berry-growing]]. In some regions, it is considered a casual weed of waste ground and old walls.

The species is not considered invasive in any jurisdiction and poses minimal ecological risk. It is listed as Least Concern by the IUCN throughout its native range.

In cultivation, the species has spread beyond its native range primarily through deliberate planting rather than aggressive naturalization. It is a familiar sight in old European churchyards and castle ruins.

## Cultivation

Rose campion is among the easiest perennials to grow in full sun and well-drained soil. It tolerates poor, sandy, or gravelly soils and is an excellent choice for xeriscaping and Mediterranean-style gardens.

Propagation is primarily by seed, which germinates readily in 10–15 days at 18–22°C. Self-seeding is prolific, and volunteer seedlings are easy to transplant.

Plants are short-lived perennials, typically persisting 3–5 years in the garden. However, they maintain themselves through abundant self-seeding, effectively behaving as permanent garden residents.

The cultivar 'Alba' (white flowers) and 'Gardeners' World' (compact habit) are commonly available. The species type with magenta flowers is the most widely grown form.

Hardy to USDA zones 4–9, Lychnis coronaria requires no special winter protection. It is pest-free and disease-resistant, with no significant insect or pathogen problems.
