# Carrizo Indians

## Description

The Carrizo Indians are a Native American group from northern Mexico identified as the original source from which the [[lipan-apache]] obtained peyote. They served as a critical transmission vector for peyote ceremonialism from Mesoamerican traditions into the Apache cultural sphere of the American Southwest.

## Classification

- **Type:** Indigenous people / ethnic group
- **Geographic region:** Northern Mexico
- **Cultural role:** Transmitter of peyote ceremonialism
- **Historical significance:** Link in the northward diffusion of peyote use

## Role in Peyote Transmission

According to the aged informant [[antonio-apache]] (as reported by Opler, 1938), the Carrizo Indians were the original source of peyote for the Lipan Apache. This established a transmission chain:

1. **Carrizo Indians** (northern Mexico) — cultivators/practitioners of peyote ceremonialism
2. **Lipan Apache** — obtained peyote from the Carrizo, used it in shamanistic contexts
3. **[[mescalero-apache]]** — learned peyote rites from the Lipans not long before 1870

The Carrizo thus represent a crucial link connecting the ancient peyote traditions of central Mexico with the northern Apache peoples.

## Cultural Context

The Carrizo inhabited regions where peyote ([[lophophora-williamsii]]) grows naturally, giving them direct access to the cactus and familiarity with its preparation and ceremonial use. Their willingness to share peyote knowledge with the Lipan Apache reflects the complex patterns of intertribal exchange that characterized the frontier zone between Mesoamerica and North America.

## Key Facts

- Identified by Antonio Apache as the source of Lipan peyote knowledge (Opler, 1938)
- Located in northern Mexico, within the natural range of Lophophora williamsii
- Part of the broader network of peyote-using cultures in the Mexico-Texas borderlands
- Their transmission of peyote to the Lipans occurred before 1870
- Represent one of several possible transmission routes (alternatives include Tonkawa, Yaqui sources)

## Historical Significance

The Carrizo's role in peyote transmission illustrates:

- The extensive trade and exchange networks connecting indigenous groups across the Mexico-US borderlands
- The northward diffusion of psychoactive plant knowledge from Mesoamerican centers
- The role of intermediary groups in adapting and transmitting botanical knowledge across cultural boundaries
- The importance of intertribal relationships in the spread of ceremonial plant use

## Relevance to Ethnobotany

The Carrizo represent an important node in the ethnobotanical diffusion of peyote across North America. Understanding their role helps map the pathways through which knowledge of Lophophora williamsii — its identification, harvesting, preparation, and ceremonial application — spread from its Mesoamerican center of origin to dozens of tribes across the United States and Canada.

## References in Source

The Carrizo Indians are mentioned in the chapter by Boyer, Boyer, and Basehart on Mescalero Apache shamanism, citing Opler's 1938 ethnographic work based on the testimony of Antonio Apache.
