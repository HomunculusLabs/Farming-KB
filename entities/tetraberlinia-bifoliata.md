# Tetraberlinia bifoliata

## Description

Tetraberlinia bifoliata is a tropical tree species in the family Fabaceae
(subfamily Detarioideae) native to the rainforests of Central Africa,
particularly Cameroon. It is an ectomycorrhizal tree species that has been
studied in the context of Janzen-Connell effects, where seedling survival under
parent trees is influenced by the interaction between mycorrhizal fungi and
soil-borne pathogens. Newbery et al. (2000) investigated its seedling dynamics
under conspecific adult trees at varying stem densities.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Eukaryota
- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Clade:** Angiosperms, Eudicots, Rosids
- **Order:** Fabales
- **Family:** Fabaceae
- **Subfamily:** Detarioideae
- **Genus:** Tetraberlinia
- **Species:** T. bifoliata

## Key Facts

- **Ectomycorrhizal tropical tree:** Unlike most tropical trees which form
  arbuscular mycorrhizal associations, T. bifoliata is ectomycorrhizal, forming
  associations that facilitate nutrient exchange between trees in African
  rainforest ecosystems.
- **Density-dependent seedling survival:** Newbery et al. (2000) found that
  seedling survival of T. bifoliata after 16 months was 33% at low adult stem
  density but increased to 65% at high adult stem density.
- **Mycorrhizal network effects:** The enhanced survival at high adult density
  suggests that denser ectomycorrhizal networks provide greater access to
  shared nutrient and carbon pools, benefiting establishing seedlings.
- **Janzen-Connell dynamics:** Unlike some tropical trees where seedling
  survival decreases near parent trees due to pathogen accumulation, T.
  bifoliata showed a positive relationship between adult density and seedling
  survival, suggesting mycorrhizal benefits may override pathogen effects.
- **Cameroon rainforest habitat:** Native to the lowland and submontane
  rainforests of Cameroon, where it is a canopy or subcanopy tree species.
- **Conservation significance:** As an ectomycorrhizal tree in African
  rainforests, T. bifoliata plays an important role in maintaining forest
  structure and nutrient cycling in one of the world's most biodiverse
  ecosystems.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Tropical ectomycorrhizal ecology:** T. bifoliata is a model species for
  studying how ectomycorrhizal networks influence tropical tree seedling
  establishment and forest community dynamics.
- **Forest regeneration:** Understanding density-dependent seedling survival
  informs reforestation and conservation strategies for Central African
  rainforests.
- **Mycorrhizal network research:** The species demonstrates how mycorrhizal
  connectivity between adult trees and seedlings can override negative
  density-dependent effects of pathogens in tropical forests.
- **Nutrient transfer studies:** Related research by Onguene and Kuyper (2002)
  on Paraberlinia bifoliolata (same forest type) showed direct mycorrhizal
  carbon and nutrient transfer between tropical trees.

## Ecological Interactions

- Forms ectomycorrhizal associations that connect seedlings to established adult
  tree root systems through shared fungal networks.
- Seedling survival enhanced at high conspecific adult density, suggesting
  mycorrhizal benefits override pathogen accumulation effects.
- Shares forest habitat with other ectomycorrhizal legumes including
  Microberlinia bisulcata, Tetraberlinia moreliana, and Paraberlinia
  bifoliolata.
- Seedlings near adult trees benefit from enhanced access to nutrients and
  carbon through mycorrhizal bridges.
