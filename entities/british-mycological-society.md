## Description

The British Mycological Society (BMS) is one of the oldest and most prestigious mycological societies in the world, dedicated to promoting all aspects of mycological science. Founded in 1896, the BMS serves as an international organization with members throughout the world, facilitating research, education, and collaboration across all subdisciplines of mycology including taxonomy, ecology, genetics, physiology, and applied mycology. The society organizes symposia, publishes scientific journals, and supports mycological research through grants and awards.

## Key Facts

- The BMS was founded in 1896, making it one of the world's oldest dedicated mycological societies.
- The society promotes all aspects of mycology including [[environmental-mycology]], medical mycology, plant pathology, [[fungal-genetics]], and fungal systematics.
- It has international membership spanning researchers, educators, students, and amateur mycologists from around the world.
- The BMS publishes peer-reviewed scientific journals that are leading outlets for mycological research, including Mycological Research (now Fungal Biology) and other publications.
- The society organizes regular scientific symposia and conferences, including the 2004 symposium at the University of Nottingham that resulted in the publication "Fungi in the Environment" (Cambridge University Press, 2007).
- BMS awards include the Berkeley Award, which recognizes outstanding contributions to mycology. Fordyce A. Davidson's chapter on mathematical modelling of fungal mycelia is presented as the Berkeley Award Lecture in the volume.
- The society provides research grants, student bursaries, and travel funds to support the next generation of mycologists.
- Educational outreach includes resources for schools, public engagement events, and support for fungal recording and conservation.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- The BMS serves as a major hub for dissemination of mycological knowledge directly relevant to fungal cultivation, including mushroom biology, fungal ecology, and applied mycology.
- Society symposia frequently feature research on edible fungi, mycorrhizal associations relevant to agriculture, and biocontrol applications of fungi.
- Publications arising from BMS symposia (such as "Fungi in the Environment") compile cutting-edge research that informs best practices in cultivation and fungal biotechnology.
- The society's support for young researchers helps build the scientific workforce that advances fungal cultivation science and technology.
- BMS resources and networks connect academic mycologists with commercial cultivators and industry, facilitating knowledge transfer between research and practice.
- Conservation initiatives supported by the BMS contribute to understanding and preserving fungal biodiversity, including species with potential cultivation value.
