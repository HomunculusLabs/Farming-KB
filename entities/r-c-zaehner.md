## R. C. Zaehner

## Description
Robert Charles Zaehner (1913–1974) was a British academic and intelligence officer who served as Spalding Professor of Eastern Religions and Ethics at Oxford University. In *The Psychedelic Experience*, Leary cites Zaehner as a cautionary example — an Oriental scholar and "expert" on mysticism who, despite his credentials, failed to navigate the psychedelic experience successfully, illustrating the gap between intellectual knowledge of mysticism and direct experiential understanding.

## Classification
- **Category:** Person — Scholar / Orientalist
- **Domain:** Comparative religion, mysticism studies, Eastern philosophy
- **Era:** 20th century (1913–1974)

## Key Facts
- Served as Spalding Professor of Eastern Religions and Ethics at Oxford University
- Authored *Mysticism, Sacred and Profane* (1957), a key text in the academic study of mysticism
- Argued against the idea that all mystical experiences are essentially the same (the "perennial philosophy" position)
- Personally experimented with mescaline and published a negative account of his experience
- His mescaline experience produced a "curious sensation" at the base of the spine that he compared to mania
- Leary quotes Zaehner"s own admission: "I did not like it at all"
- Served in British intelligence (MI6) in Iran during WWII

## The Zaehner Episode in Context
Leary uses Zaehner as an example of someone who had extensive intellectual knowledge of mysticism but was unable to handle the actual experience of consciousness expansion. Zaehner felt tingling at the base of the spine (the beginning of kundalini-type energy movement) but interpreted it through a pathological lens as a precursor to mania rather than recognizing it as a sign of consciousness expansion. This illustrates Leary's central thesis that preparation, set, and setting determine whether a psychedelic experience is liberating or frightening.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology
Zaehner's failed experience illustrates a critical point for psychedelic facilitation: intellectual knowledge alone is insufficient preparation. Even world-class experts in mystical literature can have negative experiences if they lack proper set and setting. This underscores the importance of preparation protocols for psilocybin sessions, regardless of the participant's academic background.
