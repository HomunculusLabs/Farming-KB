## Geophagy

## Description

Geophagy is the practice of eating earth, specifically clay and mineral-rich soils. It is a widespread and seemingly natural habit observed in children, tribal peoples, and numerous animal species including deer, kangaroo, cattle, and antelope. The practice has deep evolutionary roots and serves multiple nutritional and medicinal functions.

## Classification

- **Category:** Ethnobiology / Soil Science / Nutritional Anthropology
- **Domain:** Cross-cultural practice spanning human and animal behavior
- **Related Fields:** Pedology, ethnobotany, traditional food processing

## Key Facts

- Geophagy is practiced by both humans and animals across all inhabited continents
- Animals migrate to specific salt licks and dusting sites to consume mineral-rich earths
- Birds and mammals roll or bathe in specific dusting sites to rid themselves of parasites
- White, mineral-rich ash of trees is widely used as a food dip at campfires by Australian Aborigines
- Cooking food in clay is prevalent throughout the world
- Clay-salt licks are commonly visited by deer, kangaroo, cattle, and antelope

## Clay Types Used in Geophagy

- **Illite:** A common clay mineral used in traditional practices
- **Smectite (Smeltite):** Highly absorbent clay with medicinal properties
- **Kaolin:** Used to relieve feelings of nausea and reduce digestive upsets
- **Ferrous oxide "red" clays:** Rich in iron, used traditionally by tribal peoples

## Medicinal Applications

Specific clays have been traditionally used to:
- Absorb poisons from foods (e.g., from Solanum spp. and yams)
- Reduce diarrhea and digestive upsets
- Relieve feelings of nausea (kaolin clay specifically)
- Supply missing trace elements in the diet
- Fix bitter tannins as insoluble compounds (e.g., iron oxide clays mixed with acorn meals to form ferric tannates, producing "black" breads)

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

Understanding geophagy is relevant to permaculture design because:
1. **Soil mineral content** directly affects the health of plants and the animals that consume them, including humans
2. **Trace element availability** in soils can inform composting and amendment strategies
3. **Traditional food processing** using clays offers low-tech methods for detoxifying wild foods
4. **Animal behavior** around mineral licks can inform placement of mineral supplements in holistic grazing systems
5. **Specific clay identification** and their absorptive properties can aid in designing water filtration and soil remediation systems

## Research Notes

Bill Mollison notes that although little research has been carried out on geophagy's therapeutic mechanisms, all uses should be recorded when specific clays are combined with specific foods to reduce or eliminate poisonous or unpalatable toxins. The systematic study of traditional geophagy practices could yield valuable insights for modern nutrition and detoxification strategies.
