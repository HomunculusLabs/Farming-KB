# 4-HO-PYR-T — TIHKAL #24

## Compound Identity

- Full name: 4-Hydroxy-N,N-tetramethylenetryptamine

- Synonyms: 4-Hydroxy-pyrrolidinylindole, 3-[2-(1-pyrrolidyl)ethyl]-4-indolol,
1-[2-[3-(4-hydroxy)indolyl]ethyl]pyrrolidine

- Class: Tryptamine, 4-hydroxy substituted, pyrrolidine ring

- Source: [[tihkal-introduction-and-methodology]]

## Dosage and Duration

- Dosage: >20 mg orally

- Duration: Unknown

- Active routes: oral

## Qualitative Effects

At 20 mg (oral): "This substance proved to be quite unlike psilocin and bordered
on the bizarre." There was a latency period of about three hours — remarkably
long — followed by a cascade of effects. The experience was described as
"the most completely toilet experience I have ever had" with both physical
urgency and intense sensory effects. A distinctly "not friendly" character
was noted, with an unfamiliar quality unlike other tryptamines.

## Synthesis

From 4-acetoxyindole via oxalyl chloride to the glyoxyl chloride, then treatment
with pyrrolidine. The resulting glyoxylamide (mp 174-176°C) is reduced with
LAH in THF. The product 4-HO-pyr-T (mp 193-195°C) is obtained in 50% yield.

## Pharmacological Notes

The pyrrolidine ring (N,N-tetramethylene substitution) ties the two nitrogen
substituents together into a single cyclic structure. This is structurally
analogous to taking the two propyl groups of [[4-ho-dpt-entity-tihkal]] and
joining them. The resulting compound is pharmacologically distinct from all
other 4-hydroxy tryptamines.

The extremely long three-hour latency is unprecedented among oral tryptamines
and suggests unusual pharmacokinetics. The "bizarre" character may relate
to the constrained geometry of the pyrrolidine ring affecting receptor binding
in unexpected ways.

The 5-methoxy counterpart 5-MeO-pyr-T is also described
as having a very negative character with amnesia at low doses (0.5-2 mg),
reinforcing the observation that pyrrolidine-ring tryptamines produce atypical
effects.

## Chemical Family Context

4-HO-pyr-T is a member of the 4-hydroxytryptamine family but is distinguished
by its cyclic nitrogen substitution pattern. Most 4-hydroxytryptamines have
two freely rotating alkyl groups on the nitrogen, which can adopt multiple
conformations. The pyrrolidine ring in 4-HO-pyr-T locks these substituents
into a single conformational family, dramatically altering the compound's
interaction with receptor sites.

The pyrrolidine tryptamine series provides a striking demonstration of how
conformational constraint affects psychedelic activity:

- pyr-T (unsubstituted): 200-250 mg, gentle, warm, "inner sun"

- 4-HO-pyr-T (4-hydroxy): >20 mg, bizarre, "not friendly," 3-hour latency

- 5-MeO-pyr-T (5-methoxy): 0.5-2 mg, very negative, amnestic

The dramatic variation in dose range (0.5-250 mg) and qualitative character
across this series is remarkable and suggests that the pyrrolidine ring
engages receptor systems differently depending on the indole ring substitution
pattern.

## Detailed Pharmacological Discussion

The three-hour latency of 4-HO-pyr-T is one of its most unusual features and
deserves careful consideration. Standard oral tryptamines typically show onset
within 15-60 minutes, with psilocin and its close analogues beginning effects
within 30-45 minutes. A three-hour delay suggests either:

- Extremely slow absorption from the gastrointestinal tract

- The need for extensive metabolic conversion to an active species

- Unusual distribution kinetics, possibly involving slow brain penetration

- A pharmacokinetic interaction that delays receptor engagement

The "bizarre" and "not friendly" character of the experience may reflect
activity at receptor systems beyond the typical 5-HT2A target. The pyrrolidine
ring's constrained geometry may prevent optimal 5-HT2A binding while creating
a novel pharmacophore that engages other receptor systems with unfamiliar
subjective effects.

The compound's receptor binding profile has not been formally characterized,
but its effects suggest contributions from multiple receptor systems. The
combination of long latency, unfamiliar subjective character, and physical
urgency (the "toilet experience") suggests a complex pharmacology that differs
fundamentally from classical tryptamine psychedelics.

## Comparison to Related Compounds

- 4-HO-DMT (psilocin): 10-20 mg, onset 30-45 min, classic psychedelic

- 4-HO-DET: 10-25 mg, onset ~45 min, slightly longer duration

- 4-HO-DIPT: 10-25 mg, onset ~30 min, distinct qualitative profile

- 4-HO-pyr-T: >20 mg, onset ~3 hours, bizarre character

The three-hour latency alone distinguishes 4-HO-pyr-T from every other
4-hydroxytryptamine, suggesting fundamentally different pharmacokinetics.

## Harm Reduction and Safety Considerations

- The three-hour latency is a significant hazard: users may conclude the
compound is inactive and take additional doses, potentially leading to
dangerous overdose when effects finally emerge

- The "not friendly" and "bizarre" character suggests difficult experiences
are likely even at moderate doses

- The compound should NOT be combined with other psychoactive substances,
especially other tryptamines or serotonergic agents

- Given the unusual pharmacology, standard tryptamine dose expectations
should not be applied

- This compound is primarily of academic interest and is not recommended
for recreational or exploratory use

- Individuals prone to anxiety or gastrointestinal sensitivity should
exercise particular caution
