# Psilocybe maiorchina

**Psilocybe maiorchina** is a psilocybin-containing mushroom in the family Hymenogastraceae.
The species epithet "maiorchina" refers to Mallorca (Majorca), one of the balearic islands
in the western mediterranean sea, where the species was originally documented and from which
its distribution is primarily known.

## Taxonomy and Classification

Psilocybe maiorchina belongs to section Semilanceatae within the genus Psilocybe,
placing it alongside other bluing, psilocybin-producing species such as [[psilocybe-semilanceata]]
and [[psilocybe-galindoi]]. The taxonomy of Mediterranean Psilocybe species has been
historically complex, with several taxa showing significant morphological overlap. Some
mycologists have suggested that P. maiorchina may represent a regional variant or synonym
of other semilanceata-group species, though it retains distinct status in some treatments.

The species was first described from collections made in the Balearic Islands, where it
fruits in grassland and pastured habitats characteristic of the Mediterranean climate zone.
Its placement in the semilanceatae section is supported by its habitat preferences, spore
morphology, and the presence of [[psilocybin]] and [[psilocin]] as the primary active
compounds.

Modern molecular phylogenetic studies have begun to clarify the relationships within the
Semilanceatae section, revealing that morphological similarity does not always reflect
genetic relatedness. The Mediterranean Psilocybe species, including P. maiorchina,
P. galindoi, and P. hispanica, appear to form a distinct clade within the section that
is geographically and ecologically cohesive. Further molecular work with additional
collections from across the Mediterranean will be necessary to resolve the species
boundaries within this group definitively.

The genus Psilocybe itself has undergone significant taxonomic revision in recent years.
Molecular phylogenetics has revealed that the traditional broad concept of Psilocybe
was polyphyletic, leading to the segregation of many non-bluing species into other genera
such as Deconica. The remaining bluing, psilocybin-containing species form a
monophyletic group that retains the name Psilocybe.

## Description

**Cap:** Conic to broadly convex, sometimes with a slight umbo. Surface is hygrophanous,
typically brownish when moist, fading to a lighter tan or straw color as it dries. The cap
margin is often translucent-striate when wet, similar to other members of its section. Cap
size is generally small, typically 5 to 20 mm in diameter. The cap surface may be slightly
viscid when very fresh but soon becomes dry and matte. The color change during drying can
be quite dramatic, from dark chocolate brown to almost white in extreme cases.

**Gills:** Adnate to adnexed, subdistant. Color is grayish at first, darkening to
purplish-brown with maturity as spores mature. The gill edges are typically whitish and
fringed, with the fringes formed by sterile (cheilocystidia) cells that extend beyond
the gill edge. This sterile margin is a useful microscopic character for confirming
identification.

**Stem:** Cylindrical, equal or slightly enlarged at the base. Surface is smooth to
slightly silky, often whitish with a pale bluish or greenish base. The stem is relatively
fragile and hollow. Length typically ranges from 30 to 70 mm, with a thickness of 1 to
2 mm. A thin, fibrillose partial veil may be present in very young specimens but is
typically evanescent and does not leave a persistent ring.

**Spore print:** Dark purplish-brown to nearly black, typical of the genus Psilocybe.

**Spores:** Ellipsoid to somewhat amygdaliform in face view, with a thick wall and a
distinct germ pore. Spore dimensions are approximately 10 to 13 μm in length by 6 to 8
μm in width. The spore surface is smooth to very slightly roughened under scanning
electron microscopy. In Melzer's reagent, the spores are non-amyloid, which is
consistent with the genus.

**Taste and odor:** Farinaceous (mealy or flour-like), similar to [[psilocybe-semilanceata]].
The taste is typically mild to slightly bitter.

**Bruising:** The species bruises bluish to bluish-green when handled or damaged,
indicating the presence of psilocybin and/or psilocin. The bluing is most pronounced on
the stem base and can be slow to develop compared to more potent species.

**Microscopic features:** The cheilocystidia are ventricose (swollen) to lageniform
(flask-shaped), with narrow necks and sometimes capitate (head-like) apices. The
pleurocystidia are similar but less abundant. The pileipellis (cap cuticle) is a cutis
composed of narrow, hyaline hyphae. Clamp connections are present on the hyphae.

## Distribution and Habitat

Psilocybe maiorchina is found in the mediterranean basin, with confirmed collections
from Mallorca and potentially other Balearic Islands. It has also been reported from
other parts of the western Mediterranean, including coastal regions of Spain. The
full extent of its distribution remains uncertain due to confusion with morphologically
similar species.

The species grows in grassy habitats, particularly pastures, meadows, and open grasslands.
It is typically found in association with rich organic soils, often in areas grazed by
livestock or in otherwise disturbed grassland. Fruiting occurs in autumn, generally from
September through November, coinciding with the Mediterranean autumn rainfall pattern. The
species may also fruit in spring following heavy winter rains in areas with sufficient
soil moisture.

Elevation preferences are not well documented, but Mediterranean grassland Psilocybe
species generally fruit across a range of elevations from sea level to approximately
1500 meters. The species is likely adapted to the characteristic Mediterranean climate
pattern of warm, dry summers and cool, wet winters. It may be sensitive to prolonged
drought and may not fruit in years with below-average autumn precipitation.

## Chemistry

Chemical analysis of P. maiorchina collections has confirmed the presence of [[psilocybin]]
and [[psilocin]] as the primary psychoactive compounds. The concentrations are generally
moderate, comparable to other semilanceata-group species, though precise quantitative
data from controlled studies remain limited.

The species also contains the typical tryptamine profile found in psilocybin mushrooms,
with baeocystin and norbaeocystin likely present as minor constituents, though
comprehensive alkaloid profiling has not been published. The relative proportions of
psilocybin to psilocin in dried material may shift depending on drying method and storage
conditions, as psilocybin can dephosphorylate to psilocin over time, especially with
exposure to heat or light.

The biosynthetic pathway for psilocybin in P. maiorchina is presumed to be identical to
that characterized in [[psilocybe-cubensis]], involving the enzymes PsiD, PsiK, PsiM,
and PsiH that convert [[tryptophan]] through a series of steps to 4-hydroxytryptamine,
then to psilocybin via 4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine. This pathway is conserved
across all psilocybin-producing fungi that have been studied.

## Similar Species

Psilocybe maiorchina can be confused with several other small, brown, grassland
Psilocybe species:

- [[psilocybe-semilanceata]]: The [[1799-liberty-cap-discovery]] is morphologically very similar and
  occupies a comparable ecological niche. P. semilanceata typically has a more pronounced
  and persistent papilla (nipple-like umbo) and a more slender stem.
- [[psilocybe-galindoi]]: Another Mediterranean semilanceata-group species with which
  P. maiorchina shares significant morphological similarity. Definitive identification
  often requires microscopic examination of spore size and cheilocystidia morphology.
- [[psilocybe-hispanica]]: Known from the Iberian Peninsula, this species also grows in
  grassland habitats and can be difficult to distinguish without microscopic analysis.
