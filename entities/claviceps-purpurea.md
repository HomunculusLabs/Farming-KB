    10|# Claviceps purpurea
    11|
    12|**Claviceps purpurea** is a parasitic fungus in the family Clavicipitaceae that infects the
    13|flowers of grasses and cereals, particularly rye (secale cereale), wheat, barley, and oats.
    14|It is the causative agent of ergot disease and produces a complex mixture of [[claviceps-purpurea-chemistry-and-water-soluble-ergot-alkaloids]],
    15|making it one of the most historically significant fungi in medicine, agriculture, and the history
    16|of psychoactive substances. It is classified in the third group of neurotropic fungi alongside
    17|other Claviceps and Cordyceps species in the system of Guzmán, Allen, and Gartz (2000).
    18|
    19|## Taxonomy and Classification
    20|
    21|*C. purpurea* belongs to the order Hypocreales and family Clavicipitaceae. The genus
    22|Claviceps contains approximately 50 described species, all of which are obligate parasites
    23|of grasses, sedges, or related monocots. The species epithet "purpurea" refers to the dark
    24|purple to black sclerotia (ergot bodies) produced in place of host grain kernels.
    25|Recent molecular phylogenetic studies have revealed cryptic diversity within what was traditionally
    26|considered *C. purpurea* sensu lato, leading to the recognition of several cryptic species:
    27|*C. purpurea* sensu stricto (infecting open-field grasses), *C. humidiphila* (in humid habitats),
    28|*C. spartinae* (on saltmarsh grasses), and *C. arundinis* (on reed grasses).
    29|
    30|## Life Cycle
    31|
    32|The life cycle of *C. purpurea* involves three distinct phases spanning two growing seasons:
    33|
    34|### Sphacelial Phase (Summer)
    35|
    36|Infection begins when ascospores (or, in some cases, conidia from honeydew) land on the receptive
    37|stigmas of host grass florets during flowering. The spores germinate and the fungus grows
    38|intercellularly within the ovary, eventually replacing the developing grain with a soft, sugary
    39|fungal tissue called the sphacelium. The sphacelium produces copious quantities of a sticky,
    40|sweet-tasting honeydew containing millions of conidia. This honeydew exudes from the infected
    41|florets and attracts insects (particularly flies, beetles, and other pollinators), which then
    42|mechanically transfer the conidia to other florets, spreading the infection within and between
    43|grass stands. This insect-mediated dispersal is highly efficient and can result in significant
    44|infection rates within a single growing season.
    45|
    46|### Sclerotial Phase (Autumn/Winter)
    47|
    48|As the infection progresses, the soft sphacelial tissue gradually hardens and darkens, forming
    49|a hard, dense sclerotium — the ergot body. This sclerotium replaces the host grain entirely
    50|and protrudes slightly from the spikelet. It is elongated, cylindrical to slightly curved,
    51|typically 10 to 30 mm long and 2 to 5 mm wide, with a dark purple to black exterior and a
    52|white to purple-tinged interior when cut open. The sclerotium is the overwintering structure
    53|of the fungus. In autumn, infected grain heads are harvested along with the crop, or the
    54|sclerotia fall to the ground where they overwinter among crop residue and soil.
    55|
    56|### Ascospore Phase (Spring)
    57|
    58|In spring, overwintered sclerotia germinate and produce stalked, club-shaped stromata
    59|bearing perithecia with thread-like ascospores (approximately 50 to 80 μm long), which
    60|are forcibly discharged into the air and carried by wind to infect new host florets.
    61|
    62|## Distribution
    63|
    64|*C. purpurea* has a nearly cosmopolitan distribution, found on every inhabited continent.
    65|It is most prevalent in temperate regions of Europe, Asia, and North America where susceptible
    66|cereals (particularly rye) are cultivated. The species is particularly associated with cool,
    67|moist growing conditions that favor infection and sclerotial development. Historical ergotism
    68|epidemics were concentrated in central and eastern Europe, Scandinavia, and Russia, where rye
    69|was a dietary staple and climatic conditions favored the fungus. In modern times, improved grain
    70|cleaning and screening methods have greatly reduced the incidence of ergot-contaminated grain
    71|in commercial food supplies, though the fungus remains common in wild grass populations.
    72|
    73|## Chemistry and Ergot Alkaloids
    74|
    75|The sclerotia of *C. purpurea* contain over 40 different [[claviceps-purpurea-chemistry-and-water-soluble-ergot-alkaloids]], which represent
    76|one of the most pharmacologically important classes of natural products. These alkaloids are
    77|classified into three main structural groups based on their chemistry:
    78|
    79|### Clavine Alkaloids
    80|
    81|These are the simplest ergot alkaloids, consisting of the tetracyclic ergoline ring system
    82|without additional peptide or amide substituents. They include [[ergot-and-lysergic-acid-history]]
    83|derivatives and simple clavines such as agroclavine, elymoclavine, and penniclavine.
    84|[[ergot-and-lysergic-acid-history]] is the core structure from which all more complex ergot alkaloids are
    85|derived and is of enormous pharmaceutical importance.
    86|
    87|### Ergoline Alkaloids (Water-Soluble Amides)
    88|
    89|These are amide derivatives of [[ergot-and-lysergic-acid-history]] with simple amino alcohols. The most
    90|important member is ergometrine (also called ergonovine), which is paired with its
    91|C-8 epimer ergometrinine. These compounds are water-soluble and are medically important
    92|as uterotonic agents.
    93|
    94|### Ergopeptine Alkaloids (Lipid-Soluble Peptide Alkaloids)
    95|
    96|These are cyclic tripeptide derivatives of [[ergot-and-lysergic-acid-history]] and are the most pharmacologically
    97|active and complex group. They include ergotamine, ergocristine, ergocryptine,
    98|ergocornine, and ergosine, each paired with its corresponding C-8 epimer (-inine form).
    99|These compounds are lipid-soluble and have potent vasoconstrictive, uterotonic, and
   100|neuroreceptor activity.
   101|
   102|The total alkaloid content of ergot sclerotia typically ranges from 0.15% to 0.5% by dry
   103|weight, depending on the host plant, fungal strain, and growing conditions. The ratio of
   104|individual alkaloids varies considerably among strains.
   105|
   106|## Historical Significance: Ergotism
   107|
   108|*C. purpurea* is responsible for ergotism, a devastating disease caused by consuming
   109|ergot-contaminated grain. Two major clinical forms of ergotism have been documented
   110|throughout history:
   111|
   112|### Gangrenous Ergotism (St. Anthony's Fire, Ignis Sacer)
   113|
   114|Caused primarily by the vasoconstrictive effects of ergopeptine alkaloids (especially
   115|ergotamine and ergocristine), which cause sustained constriction of peripheral
   116|blood vessels. Symptoms begin with intense burning sensations in the extremities (hence
   117|"fire"), followed by progressive tissue ischemia, gangrene, and frequently amputation
   118|or death. The condition was widespread in medieval Europe, particularly during periods of
   119|famine when grain of poor quality was consumed. The Order of St. Anthony (founded in
   120|France circa 1095) specialized in caring for ergotism sufferers, which gave rise to the
   121|common name "St. Anthony's Fire."
   122|
   123|### Convulsive Ergotism
   124|
   125|Caused by neurotoxic effects on the central nervous system. Symptoms include hallucinations,
   126|convulsions, muscle spasms, mania, delirium, and occasionally death. Some historians have
   127|speculated that outbreaks may have contributed to events such as the Salem witch trials
   128|of 1692, though this hypothesis remains debated.
   129|
   130|## Medical and Pharmaceutical Applications
   131|
   132|Ergot alkaloids from *C. purpurea* have been used in medicine for centuries and remain
   133|important pharmacological agents:
   134|
   135|- **Ergometrine** is used in obstetrics to treat and prevent postpartum hemorrhage by
   136|inducing sustained uterine contractions.
   137|- **Ergotamine** is used for the acute treatment of migraine and cluster headaches through
   138|its vasoconstrictive effects on cranial blood vessels.
   139|- **Semisynthetic derivatives** include bromocriptine (for Parkinson's disease and
   140|hyperprolactinemia), cabergoline, pergolide, and lisuride.
   141|- The [[ergot-and-lysergic-acid-history]] obtained from ergot was used by Albert Hofmann at Sandoz
   142|Laboratories to synthesize LSD-25 (lysergic acid diethylamide) in 1938.
   143|Hofmann inadvertently discovered its psychoactive properties in 1943.
