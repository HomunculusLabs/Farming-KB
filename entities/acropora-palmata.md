# Elkhorn Coral (*Acropora palmata*)

## Overview

*Acropora palmata* is a prominent branching coral endemic to the Caribbean
Sea and western Atlantic. It is one of the primary reef-building corals of
the region, contributing substantially to peat calcium carbonate perlite casing [[psilocybe-cubensis]] miniculture [[psilocybe-cubensis-miniculture-method]] reef framework
construction. The species suffered catastrophic declines exceeding 95% since
the 1970s from white-band disease, bleaching, and hurricane damage.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Animalia
- **Phylum:** Cnidaria
- **Class:** Anthozoa
- **Order:** Scleractinia
- **Family:** Acroporidae
- **Genus:** *Acropora*
- **Species:** *A. palmata* (Lamarck, 1816)
- **Common names:** Elkhorn coral, antler coral
- **Etymology:** The epithet *palmata* derives from Latin for "palmate,"
  describing the broad, flattened branch morphology reminiscent of elk
  antlers or open hands.

## Morphology

Elkhorn coral forms large arborescent colonies reaching 2+ meters in
diameter. Branches are thick and flattened, growing predominantly
horizontally from a central base. Branch tips appear pale or white as
concentrated zooxanthellae-free growth zones.

Individual polyps are small (1–2 mm) embedded within a porous aragonite
skeleton. The skeleton is lightweight with a highly porous structure
facilitating rapid upward calcification of 5–10 mm per year. Living
colonies range from golden-brown to tan, colored by endosymbiotic
dinoflagellate algae in the family Symbiodiniaceae.

*Acropora palmata* is distinguished from the closely related staghorn coral
(*Acropora cervicornis*) by its flattened, palmate branches versus the
cylindrical stalks of staghorn. Elkhorn branches grow more horizontally and
form broader, more plate-like structures.

## Distribution and Habitat

The species occurs throughout the Caribbean basin, from southern Florida
and the Bahamas through the Antilles to northern [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-caribbean-central-america]] from Belize to Panama and into the Gulf of
Mexico. Elkhorn coral occupies shallow reef environments from 0.5 to 5
meters depth, with optimal growth at 1–3 meters.

It requires hard substrate and is most common on reef crests, shallow
fore-reef slopes, and patch reef margins with high water flow. The species
thrives in clear tropical waters at 25–29°C and 33–37 ppt salinity. High
light intensity is essential for zooxanthellae photosynthesis, providing up
to 90% of daily energy requirements.

## Ecology

*Acropora palmata* is a major architectural component of Caribbean reef
crest ecosystems. Its dense branching provides complex three-dimensional
habitat for hundreds of associated species including reef fish, crustaceans,
mollusks, and polychaete worms. The species contributes disproportionately
to reef accretion relative to its abundance.

Like most reef-building corals, elkhorn maintains an obligate mutualism
with dinoflagellate algae in the family Symbiodiniaceae. The primary
symbiont is typically *Symbiodinium fitti* (clade A3), which confers
relatively high thermal tolerance. This symbiosis drives calcification and
supplies the majority of fixed carbon to the host.
