# Straw Box Potato System (Permaculture)

## Description

A permanent, no-dig potato growing system in which potatoes are grown in a straw-filled wooden frame without soil. Bill Mollison describes this as a highly productive, long-lasting system that can produce potatoes continuously for over a decade. Potatoes are simply picked from the straw as needed, with the system operating year-round.

## Classification

- **Category**: Horticultural technique / growing system
- **Type**: Permanent raised bed / straw mulch culture
- **Primary crop**: Potato (Solanum tuberosum)

## Key Facts

- **Permanent system**: A straw box once established can produce potatoes for 12+ years without rebuilding.
- **No soil required**: Potatoes grow in straw alone — no bottom, no soil needed. Works even on concrete.
- **Dimensions**: Board up an area approximately 8 feet by 5 feet, lined with straw or seaweed.
- **Planting density**: Set potatoes at 9-inch spacing.
- **Continuous harvest**: Pick potatoes from the straw as needed; push any green ones back under the straw.
- **Companion planting**: Grow comfrey nearby — wrap seed potatoes in comfrey leaves before planting for added nutrients and potash.

## System Design

1. Board up a frame approximately 8x5 feet (poles make a good frame).
2. Fill with straw or seaweed (no soil, no bottom).
3. Scatter wood ashes lightly over the base.
4. Set seed potatoes at 9x9 inch spacing.
5. Fill the box with straw.
6. Pick potatoes from straw as needed.
7. Push green potatoes back under the straw.
8. Keep the system "ticking all the time."

## Companion Plants

- **Comfrey**: Wrap seed potatoes in comfrey leaves before planting for nutrient boost. Comfrey supplies potash to the root zone.
- **Mint**: Grow a pot of mint nearby to cook with potatoes — pick mint and potatoes simultaneously. Keep mint in a pot to prevent spreading.
- **Horseradish**: Good companion for potatoes, grown in sunken earthenware pots nearby.

## Longevity

Mollison reports: "We have had potatoes growing for 12 years in straw boxes. Some of the people I know, for as long as I can remember, have had their straw beds of potatoes."
