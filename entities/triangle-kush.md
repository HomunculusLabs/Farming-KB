---# Overview

Triangle Kush is a pure indica cannabis strain that originated in Florida during the 1990s and is widely regarded as one of the most influential Kush varieties in modern [[cannabis-genetic-stability]] and distinct phenotype have made it one of the most sought-after parent strains in the modern cannabis industry, contributing its robust Kush genetics to a vast family of progeny strains. The cultivar's enduring popularity speaks to the quality of the original Florida selection and the skill of the growers who maintained it through years of prohibition.

## Lineage and Genetics

Triangle Kush is believed to descend from a Hindu Kush landrace brought to the United States during the 1970s and 1980s, though the exact genetic path remains debated among cannabis geneticists. Most sources describe it as a pure indica, though some analyses suggest possible Afghani and Pakistani Kush influences in its ancestry. The strain was never commercially bred through seed — instead, it was maintained for decades as a clone-only cultivar within Florida's underground cannabis community, which contributes to both its genetic stability and the mystery surrounding its precise lineage.

Phylos Bioscience's genetic testing has placed Triangle Kush within the broader "Kush" cluster of [[fungal-life-cycles-sexual-reproduction-metamorphosis]] in its history means there is no seed line, and all authentic Triangle Kush plants are genetically identical clones tracing back to the original Florida mother plant.

## Appearance

Triangle Kush produces medium to large-sized buds that are dense, chunky, and tightly packed in a classic indica structure. The flowers are predominantly forest green with occasional hints of deep purple, especially when exposed to cooler temperatures during the late flowering stage. A thick, generous coating of milky-white trichomes covers the calyxes, giving the buds a frosted, almost crystalline appearance that signals their high potency and resin-rich composition.

Bright orange pistils wind through the bud structure in moderate density, contrasting sharply against the dark green foliage. The leaves surrounding the calyxes are typically broad-fingered, consistent with indica morphology, and may display dark undertones. Well-cured Triangle Kush buds feel exceptionally sticky and resinous to the touch, with a substantial weight that reflects their density. The overall visual presentation is one of classic, old-school cannabis quality — compact, resinous, and heavily frosted. When properly cultivated and cured, Triangle Kush buds rank among the most visually appealing of the traditional Kush family.

## Aroma and Flavor

Triangle Kush is renowned for its pungent, complex aroma that combines earthy and woody notes with sharp citrus undertones. The scent is often described as reminiscent of fresh hashish blended with lemon and damp earth, producing a fragrance that is immediately recognizable to experienced cannabis consumers. On the exhale, flavors of spicy pepper, pine, and subtle sweetness emerge, lingering on the palate with a distinct Kush character that is both heavy and smooth.

The flavor profile is dominated by its Kush terpene backbone, with myrcene providing the earthy, musky foundation, limonene contributing bright citrus top notes, and caryophyllene adding peppery warmth. Secondary notes of fuel and skunk may also be present, particularly in phenotypes that lean toward the strain's more pungent expressions. The smoke is typically thick and smooth, with an expansive quality that fills the palate and coats the throat. A distinct hash-like aftertaste often lingers, reflecting the strain's landrace Kush ancestry.

Lab results consistently place Triangle Kush in the high-THC category, with most tested samples falling between 20% and 26% THC by dry weight. CBD is virtually absent, which is typical of pure Kush varieties. The strain's cannabinoid profile is heavily dominated by THC, with negligible amounts of minor cannabinoids, resulting in pronounced psychoactive effects. Some exceptional phenotypes have been documented testing as high as 28% THC, though these represent upper outliers rather than typical expression.

The terpene profile of Triangle Kush is strongly myrcene-dominant, which contributes significantly to its sedative, body-heavy effects. The interplay between myrcene and limonene creates the strain's signature earthy-citrus duality, while caryophyllene adds depth and complexity to both the aroma and the [[oner-sativa-medical-applications]] include relief from chronic pain conditions, insomnia, muscle spasms, anxiety, and stress-related disorders. The strain's sedative properties make it particularly effective for patients dealing with sleep disturbances and severe physical tension. Duration of effects typically ranges from two to four hours, with the sedative phase extending longest.

Due to its potency, novice consumers are advised to approach Triangle Kush with caution and start with lower doses. The strain is best reserved for evening or nighttime use due to its sedative properties, though lower doses in experienced consumers may be suitable for late afternoon relaxation. Negative effects are generally limited to dry mouth and dry eyes, with occasional dizziness or mild paranoia reported at very high doses.

## Growing Information

Triangle Kush is considered a moderately difficult strain to cultivate, primarily because it is available only as a clone and requires attentive environmental management. Flowering time indoors is approximately 8 to 9 weeks, with outdoor harvests typically ready by mid to late October. The plants are relatively compact and bushy, making them suitable for indoor grows with adequate vertical space.

Indoor yields range from 10 to 14 ounces per square meter, while outdoor plants can produce 12 to 16 ounces per plant under optimal conditions. The strain prefers warm, humid climates similar to its native Florida environment and responds well to [[cannabis-soil-and-nutrient-requirements]] are moderate, with careful attention needed during the mid-flowering phase to prevent nutrient burn. Triangle Kush is resistant to most common molds but can be susceptible to spider mites in dry conditions.

Growers should maintain daytime temperatures between 70–80°F (21–27°C) and relative humidity of 50–60% during vegetative growth, reducing humidity to 40–50% during flowering to prevent bud rot and promote resin production. The strain responds well to organic soil cultivation and also performs admirably in hydroponic systems when pH and nutrient levels are carefully monitored.
