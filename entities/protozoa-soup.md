# Protozoa Soup (Homemade Inoculant)

## Description

Protozoa soup is a simple, homemade microbial inoculant used in [[soil-food-web]] gardening to introduce protozoa populations to mulches and soils. It is made by soaking fresh grass clippings, alfalfa, hay, or straw in dechlorinated water for 3–4 days with active aeration. The resulting liquid teems with protozoa ([[flagellates]], [[amoebae]], and [[ciliates]]) that serve as critical nutrient cyclers — their primary ecological role is consuming bacteria and releasing plant-available nitrogen in the process.

## Classification

- **Type:** Homemade microbial inoculant
- **Key organisms:** Protozoa (flagellates, amoebae, ciliates)
- **Substrate:** Fresh grass clippings, alfalfa, hay, or straw
- **Preparation time:** 3–4 days
- **Aeration:** Required (aquarium air pump and air stone recommended)

## Key Facts

- Made by soaking fresh grass clippings, alfalfa, hay, or straw in dechlorinated water for 3–4 days
- Requires active aeration using an aquarium air pump and air stone to maintain aerobic conditions
- After 3–4 days, protozoa populations become visible to the naked eye (enhanced with a hand lens)
- Can be poured directly onto mulches to increase nutrient cycling power
- Protozoa are essential nutrient cyclers — they consume bacteria and release nitrogen in plant-available forms (ammonium)
- Without protozoa and nematodes, bacteria and fungi alone cannot efficiently cycle nutrients to plants
- The soup also contains bacteria that grow on the decomposing plant material, which serve as food for the multiplying protozoa
- Cost-effective alternative or supplement to commercial [[compost]] tea for protozoa inoculation
- Particularly useful for increasing nitrogen cycling in soils with low protozoan populations

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Protozoa soup illustrates several key soil food web concepts:

- **Nutrient cycling bottleneck:** Bacteria and fungi immobilize nutrients in their biomass; protozoa release those nutrients by consuming the microbes — without protozoa, nutrients remain locked up
- **The grazing food chain:** Bacteria → Protozoa → Plant-available nitrogen is the primary nitrogen mineralization pathway in soil
- **Simple production:** The technique demonstrates that effective biological inoculants can be produced with minimal equipment and cost
- **Mulch integration:** Protozoa soup applied to mulches accelerates decomposition and nutrient release, completing the cycle from organic matter to plant-available nutrients
- **Complement to fungal inoculation:** While compost tea introduces bacteria, fungi, protozoa, and nematodes, protozoa soup specifically targets the protozoan component for soils that need enhanced nutrient cycling
