## Overview
Paracoccidioides brasiliensis is a thermally dimorphic fungal pathogen
and the causative agent of paracoccidioidomycosis (PCM), also known as
[[mushroom-spawn-making-and-pure-culture-methods]], representing a significant
practical impediment to its study.

## Key Details

### Morphology
- Mold form (environmental, 25 to 28 degrees Celsius): produces
  slow-growing, white to cream-colored filamentous colonies
- Yeast form (host tissue, 37 degrees Celsius): large, thick-
  walled yeast cells (8 to 40 micrometers) with multiple
  budding daughter cells surrounding the parent cell,
  creating a characteristic "pilot"s wheel" or "Mariner"s
  compass" appearance
- Conidia produced by the mold form are presumed infectious
  propagules
- No known teleomorph has been identified, though molecular
  evidence suggests affinity with other Onygenales
- Transition between mold and yeast forms is reversible and
  temperature-dependent

### Ecology and Habitat
- Environmental reservoir has not been definitively identified
- Associated with moist soils in humid, forested areas of
  southern Mexico, [[singh-detection-methods-degrading-fungi-environment]]
- Armadillos have been proposed as potential environmental
  reservoirs
- Infection occurs primarily through inhalation of airborne
  conidia from disturbed soil

### Geographic Distribution and Epidemiology
- Endemic to Latin America, with highest incidence in Brazil,
  Colombia, Venezuela, and Argentina
- Predominantly affects adult males in agricultural occupations
  (sex bias of 10:1 to 15:1 male to female ratio)
- Hormonal factors, particularly estrogen, may inhibit the
  yeast-to-mold transition, explaining the male predominance
- Co-infection with HIV has altered the epidemiological
  landscape in some regions
- Disease manifestations range from subclinical to acute
  juvenile and chronic adult forms

### Clinical Significance
- Causes paracoccidioidomycosis, affecting lungs, mucous
  membranes, skin, lymph nodes, and [[aact-plate-counts-vs-direct-microscopy-molecular-dna-testing-ingham]]
  of clinical specimens
