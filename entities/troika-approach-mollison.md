## Description

The Troika Approach is a management and organisational technique described by [[bill-mollison]] for permaculture communities and [[effective-working-groups]]. It limits responsibility for any one area of action or task to 1-3 people who manage independently but work to a general plan and schedule. Named after the Russian three-horse sled team, it replaces consensus-based decision-making with small autonomous work groups.

## Classification

- **Category**: Organisational technique / Community governance
- **Domain**: Social permaculture and [[holmgren-community-design-and-intentional-communities]]
- **Proponent**: Bill Mollison

## Core Principles

1. **Small groups**: Assign 1-3 people per function or task area
2. **Independent management**: Each troika operates autonomously within its domain
3. **General plan alignment**: Groups coordinate through shared schedules and completion dates
4. **Public reporting**: Each group produces reports, diaries, or plans that are made public or minuted
5. **Understudy system**: Attach occasional understudies to ensure continuity if key people leave

## Why It Works

Mollison argues that large group decision-making is inherently flawed:

- **Groups of 7+**: Require chairpersons, agendas, proposals, votes — time-consuming and frustrating
- **Consensus**: Leads to "endless and pointless" processes with coercion of silent or incoherent abstainers by vociferous minorities
- **Voting**: Creates tyranny of the majority
- **Veto/consensus blocking**: Creates tyranny of the minority

The troika approach eliminates these by keeping decision-making in small, functional groups where fast agreement is natural.

## Group Size Dynamics

Mollison provides specific guidance on group sizes:

- **1-3 people**: Executive decision, least meeting time, fast changes, fast replacement of key people
- **4-6 people**: Good volunteer/cooperative group work; ideal for work exchange systems
- **7-20 people**: Function well only in social conditions; decision-making becomes slow and frustrating
- **30-40 people**: Minimum group covering most human functions
- **200-300 people**: Minimum for genetic variability and hamlet-level sustainability
- **300-600 people**: Upper limit where people still know every other person by name

## How to Implement

1. **List all functions**: Identify every task the community needs performed
2. **Classify tasks**: Separate creative/productive tasks from maintenance tasks
3. **Volunteer for creative tasks**: Seek volunteers and allot tasks with agreed timetables
4. **Roster maintenance tasks**: Lay out worksheets with weekly or monthly rotation
5. **Contract unclaimed tasks**: If no volunteers appear, the group funds contracted work
6. **No meetings for business**: All meetings become social and convivial information exchanges

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

The troika approach applies directly to farm and garden management:

- **[[nursery-management]]**: 1-3 people responsible for propagation schedules
- **Compost operations**: Small team managing all composting infrastructure
- **[[holzer-natural-animal-husbandry]]**: Dedicated small group for livestock care
- **Water systems**: Troika managing irrigation, dams, and water quality
- **Harvesting and processing**: Coordinated small teams for seasonal work
- **[[homestead-tool-maintenance]]**: One person responsible for sharpening, repair, and inventory

For larger seasonal tasks (harvest, building projects), the troika coordinates broader community work days using a calendar system where conveners notify everyone when and where personpower is needed.

## The Work-Netting Concept

Beyond the local troika, Mollison describes "work-netting" (as distinct from "pointless networking") — a system where cells of 1-3 people run very large networks. Every 2 years, small autonomous groups meet in affinity or work integration groups to share strategies. No person can force another group to cooperate; cooperation must be offered as reasonable, rewarding, and fair.

## Failure Prevention

Mollison identifies common community failures the troika avoids:

- Group consensus on all decisions (tyranny of the dissenter)
- Group leadership by one or a few (tyranny of the leader)
- Rules about lifestyle (tyranny of proscription)
- Attempts to live in large group households (lack of privacy)
- Overscheduling of meetings
- Neglect of income-producing activities
- Poor financial accounting
