## Description

Eiichi Kurosawa was a Japanese plant pathologist and research scientist who made one of the most significant discoveries in plant science: the isolation of **[[gibberellin]]**, a plant growth hormone, from the fungus that causes bakanae disease in rice. He worked at the research laboratory of the Yokohama Customs Bureau Plant Inspection Division, where a young **[[masanobu-fukuoka]]** studied under his guidance.

## Scientific Contributions

### Discovery of Gibberellin

Kurosawa isolated and cultured the fungus responsible for **bakanae disease** ("foolish [[cervantes-damping-off-seedling-disease-prevention]]") in rice. He was the first to:

1. Extract the plant growth hormone from the fungal culture
2. Demonstrate that the hormone, when absorbed in small amounts by young rice plants, causes abnormally tall growth
3. Show that in excess, the hormone retards plant growth — the opposite reaction

### Legacy

Despite remaining largely unknown in the academic world, Kurosawa's discovery became internationally significant:

- Initially disregarded in Japan
- Became a topic of active research overseas
- Led an American researcher to develop the **seedless grape** using gibberellin
- Now recognized as one of the most important discoveries in plant physiology

## Relationship to Masanobu Fukuoka

Fukuoka regarded Kurosawa as a father figure and studied plant pathology under his guidance at the Yokohama Customs Bureau laboratory. Under Kurosawa's mentorship, Fukuoka:

- Built a dissection microscope
- Devoted himself to research on resin diseases in citrus trees
- Observed fungus cultures and crossbred fungi
- Developed the deep observational skills that later informed his [[natural-farming-philosophy]]

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Kurosawa's work demonstrates the profound interconnection between fungal metabolism and plant growth. His discovery that a fungal metabolite (gibberellin) can fundamentally alter plant development laid the groundwork for understanding how fungal enzymes and metabolites interact with plant substrates — knowledge directly applicable to [[mushroom-cultivation]] on agricultural substrates.
