## Overview

**5-MeO-DALT** (N,N-diallyl-5-methoxytryptamine) is a synthetic psychedelic tryptamine first synthesized by Alexander Shulgin and documented in TiHKAL (entry #38). It is classified as a serotonergic hallucinogen with primary activity at 5-HT₂A receptors.

The compound gained popularity as a research chemical in the late 2000s following its appearance on the online vendor market. Its short duration and relatively mild visual effects distinguish it from classical psychedelics like psilocybin and LSD.

## Chemical Properties

**IUPAC name:** N,N-diallyl-5-methoxy-1H-indole-3-ethanamine.

**Molecular formula:** C₁₇H₂₂N₂O. **Molecular weight:** 270.37 g/mol. **CAS number:** 928792-43-2.

The freebase form is an oily liquid at room temperature. The hydrochloride salt is a white crystalline powder with moderate solubility in water and good solubility in ethanol.

The structure features a 5-methoxy substitution on the indole ring and two allyl groups on the terminal amine nitrogen. The allyl substituents are distinctive among psychedelic tryptamines, most of which carry dimethyl, diethyl, or dipropyl groups.

## Pharmacology

5-MeO-DALT acts as a partial agonist at the 5-HT₂A receptor, the primary molecular target responsible for classical psychedelic effects. Binding affinity (Kᵢ) at human 5-HT₂A is approximately 240–450 nM, making it less potent than 5-MeO-DMT (Kᵢ ≈ 5 nM) or DMT (Kᵢ ≈ 50 nM).

The compound also shows moderate affinity for 5-HT₁A receptors (Kᵢ ≈ 300 nM) and 5-HT₂C receptors (Kᵢ ≈ 500 nM). Activity at 5-HT₁A may contribute to the anxiolytic and calming effects reported by some users.

Metabolism proceeds via oxidative dealkylation of the allyl groups and O-demethylation, likely mediated by CYP2D6 and CYP1A2. The metabolite 5-OH-DALT has been detected in urine but has not been pharmacologically characterized.

The pharmacokinetic profile includes rapid onset (10–20 minutes), peak effects at 45–90 minutes, and total duration of 2–4 hours. The short duration relative to other oral tryptamines is likely due to rapid hepatic metabolism.

## Effects

The subjective effects of 5-MeO-DALT are described as less visual and more cognitive compared to classical psychedelics. Common effects include enhanced tactile sensitivity, altered thought patterns, mild visual distortions, and a sense of mental clarity.

Recreational doses range from 12–25 mg orally. Vapourization produces more rapid onset (1–5 minutes) with shorter duration (1–2 hours) at lower doses of 5–15 mg.

Unlike 5-MeO-DMT, which frequently produces overwhelming ego-dissolution experiences, 5-MeO-DALT is generally described as more gentle and manageable. However, the dose-response curve is steep — doses above 30 mg can produce intense anxiety and confusion.

Common side effects include nausea, pupil dilation, increased heart rate, and transient insomnia. There are no confirmed fatalities attributable to 5-MeO-DALT alone in the medical literature.

## Synthesis

The Shulgin synthesis starts from 5-methoxyindole, which is reacted with oxalyl chloride to form the glyoxylamide, then reduced to the tryptamine via LiAlH₄. The final step is N-alkylation with allyl bromide in the presence of base.

Alternative routes use 5-methoxytryptamine as the starting material, with direct N,N-diallylation using allyl bromide and potassium carbonate in DMF. This route is shorter but requires protection of the indole nitrogen to prevent over-alkylation.

## Legal Status

5-MeO-DALT is unscheduled in most jurisdictions due to its relative novelty. It is controlled as an analogue of 5-MeO-DMT under the Federal Analogue Act in the United States, though prosecution under this provision is rare.

The compound is specifically controlled (Class B) in the United Kingdom under the Psychoactive Substances Act 2016. In Japan, it is designated as a designated narcotic. Several other countries have issued blanket bans on 5-substituted tryptamines.

## Dose and Duration Summary

**Oral route:** Light dose 12–16 mg, common dose 18–22 mg, strong dose 24–30 mg. Onset 15–30 minutes, peak 60–90 minutes, total duration 2–4 hours, aftereffects up to 2 hours.

**Vaporized route:** Light dose 5–8 mg, common dose 10–15 mg, strong dose 16–25 mg. Onset 1–5 minutes, peak 15–30 minutes, total duration 1–2 hours. Vaporization is not recommended above 25 mg due to steep dose-response.

**Insufflation** has been reported but is poorly characterized. Estimated doses are lower than oral (8–15 mg), with faster onset but increased nasal irritation and discomfort.

Tolerance to 5-MeO-DALT develops rapidly and persists for approximately 3–7 days. Cross-tolerance with other serotonergic psychedelics (LSD, psilocybin) is expected due to shared 5-HT₂A receptor mechanism.

## History

Alexander Shulgin first synthesized 5-MeO-DALT and assigned it TiHKAL entry #38, with a footnote suggesting it was one of the later compounds in his research series. The lab notes date the initial synthesis to the mid-1990s.

The compound remained obscure until approximately 2004, when it appeared on online research chemical vendor websites marketed as a "legal alternative" to controlled psychedelics. By 2007–2008, it was among the most widely available novel tryptamines.

The European Monitoring Centre for Drugs and Drug Addiction (EMCDDA) issued its first risk assessment in 2009, noting limited pharmacological data but identifying the compound as a potential public health concern due to its widespread online availability.

Academic interest increased in the 2010s with the first published pharmacological characterizations. Reckweg et al. (2021) provided the most comprehensive analysis of receptor binding, metabolism, and toxicology.

## Toxicology and Safety

No fatal overdoses of 5-MeO-DALT alone have been documented. The therapeutic index appears relatively wide, though formal LD₅₀ data are not available from controlled animal studies.

In vitro cytotoxicity assays (HEK293 and HepG2 cell lines) show no significant toxicity at concentrations up to 100 µM. However, chronic toxicity data are entirely absent, and the long-term effects of repeated use are unknown.

The allyl groups raise theoretical concerns about potential metabolic activation to reactive intermediates. Allylic oxidation could produce epoxides or aldehydes that form protein adducts, though this has not been demonstrated in vivo.

Drug interactions are expected with MAO inhibitors, which would potentiate effects similarly to other tryptamines. Concomitant use with serotonergic drugs (SSRIs, tramadol) carries theoretical serotonin syndrome risk, though no cases have been published.

## Related Compounds

5-MeO-DALT occupies a middle ground between the more potent 5-MeO-DMT and the longer-lasting 5-MeO-DIPT ("foxy methoxy"). The allyl substitution pattern is unique among the major psychedelic tryptamines.

Structural analogues include 5-MeO-MIPT, 5-MeO-DET, and 5-MeO-DPT, all of which share the 5-methoxyindole core but differ in the N,N-dialkyl substituents. Among these, 5-MeO-DALT is notable for its relatively mild effects and short duration.
