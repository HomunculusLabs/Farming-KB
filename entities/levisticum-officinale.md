## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae → **Division:** Magnoliophyta
- **Class:** Magnoliopsida → **Order:** Apiales
- **Family:** Apiaceae → **Genus:** *Levisticum*
- **Species:** *L. officinale* W.D.J. Koch (1824)
- **Common names:** Lovage, garden lovage, love parsley
- **Monotypic genus** — single species in *Levisticum*

## Morphology

- **Habit:** Robust perennial, 1.5–2.5 m; thick fleshy
  taproot
- **Stems:** Hollow, ridged, 1–2.5 m; solid at base
- **Leaves:** Tri-pinnate, 40–70 cm; leaflets rhombic to
  ovate, 2–5 cm, serrate, dark glossy green
- **Flowers:** Yellow-green, 2–3 mm, compound umbels
  4–8 cm; 8–16 rays; involucre bracts present
- **Fruit:** Schizocarp, 5–7 mm, oval, ribbed, aromatic
- **Root:** Fleshy taproot to 30 × 5 cm; pale yellow;
  aromatic; most potent medicinal part
- **Lifespan:** 10–20+ years; dies back to crown in winter

## Distribution and Habitat

- **Native:** Mountains of S. Europe (Italy, Balkans, Greece)
  and W. Asia (Iran, Caucasus)
- **Naturalized:** Widely across Europe, N. America, temperate
  Asia from cultivation
- **Habitat:** Mountain meadows, forest margins, stream banks
- **Hardiness:** USDA zone 3 (-40°C); cool-temperate;
  struggles in hot humid climates above zone 7
- **Soil:** Deep, moisture-retentive, fertile loams; pH
  6.0–7.5; tolerates clay, not waterlogging
- **Light:** Full sun to partial shade

## Bioactive Compounds

- **[[psoralen]] —
  photosensitizing compounds in roots
- **Furanocoumarins:** Root and seed; can cause phototoxicity
- **Flavonoids:** [[kaempferol]], [[luteolin]] glycosides
- **Vitamins:** High vitamin C (leaves), carotene (roots),
  B complex
- **Minerals:** Rich in potassium, calcium, magnesium, iron
