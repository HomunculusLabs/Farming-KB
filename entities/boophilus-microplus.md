## Description

**Boophilus microplus** (now reclassified as Rhipicephalus microplus), the cattle tick, is a parasitic ixodid tick species and one of the most economically important ectoparasites of cattle worldwide. It has been the target of entomopathogenic fungal biocontrol research.









## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Animalia
- **Phylum**: Arthropoda
- **Class**: Arachnida
- **Order**: Ixodida
- **Family**: Ixodidae
- **Genus**: Boophilus (now Rhipicephalus)
- **Species**: B. microplus









## Key Facts

- Onofre et al. (2001) tested [[entomopathogenic-fungi]] [[metarhizium-flavoviride]] and M. anisopliae against engorged adult ticks
- M. flavoviride proved more effective, reducing both larval emergence from eggs and reproductive efficiency
- LD50 for M. flavoviride isolates ranged from 3.4 x 10^5 to 4.2 x 10^5 conidia/ml
- LD50 for M. anisopliae isolates ranged from 2.3 x 10^7 to 1.4 x 10^7 conidia/ml (approximately 100x higher)
- At highest concentration (10^8 conidia/ml), M. flavoviride achieved 63-78% control









## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

The research on fungal biocontrol of B. microplus demonstrates the practical application of entomopathogenic fungi for veterinary pest management. For mycologists, this illustrates the importance of fungal isolate selection, as significant differences in virulence exist between species and isolates. The conidial concentration-response data provide benchmarks for developing fungal biocontrol products.









## Ecological Role

B. microplus is a major constraint on cattle production in tropical and subtropical regions. It transmits bovine babesiosis (cattle fever), causing significant economic losses. Natural regulation by entomopathogenic fungi may contribute to tick population control, particularly under warm, humid conditions favoring fungal growth.
