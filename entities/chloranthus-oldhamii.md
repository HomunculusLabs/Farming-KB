## Overview

**Chloranthus oldhamii** is a perennial herbaceous plant in the family Chloranthaceae, native to East Asia. It is commonly known as Oldham's chloranthus or black spike flower, referring to the distinctive dark inflorescence spikes.

The species was described by Hermann Solms-Laubach in 1868, named in honor of Richard Oldham, a British botanical collector who gathered specimens in Taiwan during the 19th century.

Chloranthus oldhamii typically grows 30 to 60 centimeters tall with erect, unbranched stems arising from a creeping rhizome. The plant forms loose clumps in shaded forest understories.

The genus Chloranthus represents one of the oldest extant angiosperm lineages, with fossil records dating to the Early Cretaceous period. Molecular phylogenetic studies place Chloranthaceae among the earliest-diverging angiosperm families.

The species is distributed across southern Japan, Taiwan, and southeastern China. It occurs in warm-temperate to subtropical forests at elevations from 200 to 1,500 meters.

## Cultivation

Chloranthus oldhamii is cultivated as a shade garden ornamental for its architectural form and unusual dark flower spikes. It is considered somewhat challenging in cultivation compared to more common shade perennials.

The plant requires partial to full shade with protection from direct afternoon sun. Morning sun or dappled light throughout the day is ideal for robust growth and flowering.

Soil should be consistently moist, well-drained, and rich in organic matter. A loose, open mix incorporating leaf mold, compost, and perlite provides excellent growing conditions.

Hardiness is rated at USDA Zone 8, tolerating temperatures to approximately -10 degrees Celsius. In colder zones, the plant requires heavy winter mulching and may not survive prolonged freezes.
