## Overview

**Dioscorea alata** L., commonly known as winged yam, water yam, or greater yam, is a species of yam in the family Dioscoreaceae.

It is one of the most widely cultivated yam species, grown extensively across tropical and subtropical regions of Africa, Asia, Oceania, and the Caribbean.

The species is named for the characteristic winged or angular cross-section of its stem, which distinguishes it from other cultivated yams.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

### Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Order:** Dioscoreales
- **Family:** Dioscoreaceae
- **Genus:** Dioscorea L.
- **Species:** D. alata L.
- **Authority:** Carl Linnaeus (1753)

The species epithet *alata* refers to the winged stems. The genus comprises over 600 species of twining vines with tuberous roots.

### Morphology

*D. alata* is a twining vine that can climb 3–12 meters on supporting vegetation. The stems are quadrangular with four prominent wings (ridges), which are green to purplish.

Leaves are opposite, cordate to ovate, 8–20 cm long and 5–15 cm wide, with 5–7 prominent basal veins. Leaf surfaces are smooth to slightly pubescent.

Tubers are variable in shape — cylindrical, clavate, or irregular — and can weigh from 1 to 25 kg. The skin color ranges from white to brown, while flesh may be white, purple, or yellow.

Purple-fleshed varieties are particularly valued for their high anthocyanin content. The tuber surface may be smooth or rough with shallow root scars.
