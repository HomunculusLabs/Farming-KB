## Overview

Blackstonia acuminata is an annual herb in the family Gentianaceae, native to the western Mediterranean basin. It is commonly known as acccuminate yellow-wort and is one of four species in the small genus Blackstonia.

The species is closely related to the more widespread [[blackstonia-perfoliata]] but is distinguished by its more narrowly acute calyx lobes and preference for drier, more open habitats. It ranges from the Iberian Peninsula through southern France to northwestern Italy.

Like all Blackstonia species, it produces bright yellow, four-merous flowers that stand out against the glaucous blue-green foliage. The entire plant has a noticeably bitter taste characteristic of the Gentianaceae.

Blackstonia acuminata is less studied than Blackstonia perfoliata, with limited pharmacological and phytochemical data available. It shares the secoiridoid glycoside chemistry typical of the family but in distinct proportions.

The species is of conservation interest in parts of its range where Mediterranean coastal habitats are under pressure from urbanization and agricultural intensification.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Blackstonia acuminata (W.D.J. Koch) Domin belongs to the genus Blackstonia, family Gentianaceae. The basionym is Chlora perfoliata var. acuminata W.D.J. Koch.

The genus Blackstonia is distinguished from Centaurium by its yellow (not pink) corollas and its calyx lobes that are longer than or equal to the corolla tube. Some authors historically placed Blackstonia within Centaurium as a subgenus.

Blackstonia acuminata is differentiated from Blackstonia perfoliata by its more narrowly lanceolate, long-acuminate calyx lobes and its generally smaller stature. Intermediate forms occur where the two species co-occur, suggesting occasional hybridization.

Plants are erect annual herbs, 8–30 cm tall, with glabrous, glaucous stems that are simple or sparingly branched from the base. The stem is four-angled in cross-section, with the angles bearing narrow wings.

Leaves are opposite, sessile, ovate to broadly lanceolate, and perfoliate — the basal leaves being united at the base to form a disc through which the stem passes. This perfoliate character gives the genus its most distinctive vegetative feature.

Upper stem leaves are more narrowly lanceolate and less obviously perfoliate. All leaves are glaucous with a conspicuous whitish bloom, thicker than those of Centaurium species.

Flowers are solitary or in small cymose clusters at stem tips and upper leaf axils. The corolla is bright yellow, rotate-campanulate, 10–18 mm in diameter, divided into four ovate lobes.

Calyx lobes are narrowly lanceolate, 8–14 mm long, acute to long-acuminate, equaling or exceeding the corolla tube. This proportion is diagnostic: in Blackstonia perfoliata, calyx lobes are typically shorter than the tube.

Flowering occurs from April to July, earlier than Blackstonia perfoliata at overlapping sites. Capsules are ovoid, 6–10 mm long, enclosed within the persistent calyx.

## Distribution and Habitat

Blackstonia acuminata is a western Mediterranean species with a range extending from Portugal and Spain through southern France to Liguria in northwestern Italy. Isolated populations occur in the Balearic Islands and possibly northern Morocco.

It inhabits dry, open habitats on calcareous substrates including limestone pavements, rocky grasslands, garrigue, and abandoned agricultural terraces. The species is a strict calcicole, rarely found on acidic soils.

Elevational range extends from sea level to approximately 1,200 meters in the Pyrenees foothills. Most populations occur below 800 meters in coastal and sub-coastal Mediterranean zones.

Associated vegetation includes Brachypodium retusum grasslands, [[thymus-vulgaris-entity]] garrigue, and open [[quercus-ilex]] woodland margins. Companion annuals include species of Linum, Trifolium, and Medicago.

The species favors sites with full sun exposure and competes poorly in tall, dense vegetation. Disturbance from grazing or fire maintains the open habitat it requires, making it a stress-tolerant ruderal in Mediterranean succession.

Population sizes fluctuate markedly between years depending on autumn and spring rainfall patterns. Dense stands may develop in favorable years while the species may be nearly absent in drought years.

## Chemistry and Bioactive Compounds

Blackstonia acuminata contains the secoiridoid glycosides characteristic of Gentianaceae, with gentiopicroside as the dominant compound. Concentrations in dried aerial parts typically range from 1.0–2.5%.

Swertiamarin is present as a secondary iridoid, occurring at lower concentrations than in Blackstonia perfoliata. The gentiopicroside-to-swertiamarin ratio is a useful chemotaxonomic marker distinguishing the two species.

Secoiridoid aglycones including gentiogenin and swertianogenin have been detected after acid hydrolysis. These are breakdown products rather than native constituents but are useful for chemotaxonomic profiling.

Xanthones detected in Blackstonia acuminata include bellidifolin and demethoxybellidifolin. The xanthone profile is simpler than that of Gentiana or Swertia species, consistent with the reduced xanthone diversity in the Chironieae tribe.

Flavonoid constituents include [[apigenin]] and [[luteolin]] C-glycosides in [[leaf-tissue-analysis-crop-nutrient-status]]. These compounds contribute to the UV-protective properties of the glaucous [[aact-foliar-sticker-spreaders-leaf-surface-attachment-ingham]].

The bitter principle is primarily attributable to gentiopicroside, though the overall bitterness is less intense than in Swertia or Gentiana species. Traditional use reflects this milder bitter profile.

## Traditional and Medicinal Use

Blackstonia acuminata has limited documented use in traditional medicine compared to Blackstonia perfoliata. Where the two species co-occur, they have been used interchangeably as bitter tonics.

In Provençal folk medicine, the aerial parts of yellow-wort species were infused as a digestive bitter and appetite stimulant. The bitter secoiridoid glycosides stimulate gastric acid secretion via vagal reflex pathways.

Traditional use in Catalan herbalism includes preparations for liver complaints and mild fevers. The species is listed in some regional herbals as "Herba de la febre" (fever herb), reflecting its febrifuge reputation.

Modern pharmacological investigation of Blackstonia acuminata is scarce. Extrapolation from Blackstonia perfoliata suggests anti-inflammatory and antioxidant activities attributable to shared secoiridoid glycosides.

The species has potential as a source of gentiopicroside for pharmaceutical applications. However, the low biomass yield of wild populations makes commercial harvesting impractical without cultivation development.
