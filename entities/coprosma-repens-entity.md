## Coprosma repens

## Description

coprosma repens entity, commonly known as the looking-glass plant or mirror plant,
is an evergreen shrub or small tree native to New Zealand and coastal areas
of the southern hemisphere. It is valued in [[bill-mollison]] specifically lists Coprosma repens as a seed-bearing tree or
shrub suitable for poultry range in permaculture designs. The fleshy fruits
and seeds are consumed readily by chickens and other poultry, providing
nutritional variety and supplemental protein. When planted in orchards or
pasture ranges, Coprosma serves as a perennial food source that reduces the
need for purchased poultry feed.

### Permaculture Design Roles

- **Poultry forage tree:** Integrated into chicken ranges and orchard systems
  as a self-seeding, low-maintenance food source for fowl
- **Hedgerow and windbreak:** Dense growth habit makes it ideal for shelter
  planting around poultry yards and garden zones
- **Edge species:** Tolerant of exposed coastal margins, useful for stabilising
  zone 3-4 boundaries
- **Wildlife habitat:** Berries attract native birds, supporting biodiversity

### Companion Planting

Coprosma repens fits well into polyculture designs alongside other
seed-bearing poultry forage species such as Caragana, tagasaste, boxthorn
(Lycium spp.), pigeon pea, and mulberry. In mixed hedgerows it provides
structural diversity and year-round foliage cover.

### Cultivation Notes

- Prefers well-drained soils but tolerates clay and sandy substrates
- Full sun to partial shade; flowering and fruiting best in full sun
- Requires both male and female plants for fruit production
- Drought-tolerant once established, making it suitable for low-water
  permaculture sites
- Responds well to hedging and can be kept at 1-2 metres for easy poultry
  access to berries
- Minimal pest and disease problems in most growing conditions

### Ecological Considerations

In some regions outside its native range, Coprosma repens has demonstrated
invasive tendencies. Permaculture designers should check local weed
regulations and monitor spread. In its native New Zealand, it plays an
important role in coastal ecosystems and provides food for native bird
species including tui and kereru.

## Summary

Coprosma repens is a versatile, hardy shrub that earns its place in
permaculture poultry systems as a reliable seed-bearing forage plant. Its
dual function as windbreak and food source, combined with low maintenance
requirements, makes it a practical choice for integrated orchard-poultry
designs in temperate and subtropical climates.
