# Bunyip Level (Hose Level)

## Description

The Bunyip level (also called a hose level or water level) is a simple, accurate surveying tool made from clear plastic hose filled with water. Used by two people working approximately 15 meters apart, it exploits the principle that water seeks its own level to determine precise elevation differences across a landscape. It is one of the most useful and inexpensive tools for permaculture earthworks.

## Classification

- **Category:** Surveying instrument / Earthworks tool
- **Type:** Water-based leveling device
- **Complexity:** Simple (easily purchased materials)
- **Accuracy:** High — capable of surveying kilometers of diversion drain

## Materials and Construction

Components:
- **Clear plastic hose:** Approximately 20 m (65 feet) in length, approximately 10 mm (1/2 inch) diameter
- **Corks or stoppers (2):** Fitted to hose ends, each with a breather hole
- **Cork balls (2, optional):** Small floats that fit freely inside the clear tube below the corks
- **Stakes (2):** Approximately 2 m (6.5 feet) tall, marked with measurement increments (dressmakers' tapes work well)
- **Clamps or tape (4):** To connect the clear hose to the stakes

## Calibration

1. Uncork one end and fill the entire hose with water
2. Remove all air bubbles (only certain with completely clear hose)
3. Place both stakes together, tops level, on a level surface
4. Keeping stakes vertical, adjust the measuring strips until the water level (or both cork floats) read precisely the same measurement on both stakes
5. Recork the tubes

## Field Usage

1. Two people walk past each other alternately
2. Drive a stake every 6–15 m (20–50 feet) to guide diggers or machine operators
3. Allow for desired rise or fall by adjusting one cork ball relative to the other
4. Both stakes must be kept vertical during measurement

## Precision Capabilities

- A 1 cm difference in 5 m equals a 1:500 fall — ample for most diversion drains
- Dead level lines can be run across landscape
- Dam walls can be checked for level
- Gentle rises for roads can be set

## Applications

- **Diversion drain layout:** Surveying drains to carry runoff to dams at slopes of 1:500 to 1:2000
- **House site leveling:** Checking level from corner to corner
- **Wall and spillway setting:** Pre-determining levels for construction
- **Long-distance surveying:** Mollison reports a 12 km drain laid in sand at 1:2000 by two women trained in 30 minutes

## Relevance to Cultivation

The Bunyip level democratizes precision surveying. Its remarkable accuracy at minimal cost makes it ideal for smallholders and permaculture designers who need to lay out water management earthworks. The fact that a 12 km drain was successfully surveyed with this tool by novice users demonstrates its accessibility and reliability. For any water-harvesting or earthworks project, the Bunyip level provides professional-grade results without professional-grade costs.

## Construction and Use

Bunyip Level is a fundamental surveying tool in permaculture design, used to
establish contour lines for earthworks, swales, and terraces. Simple construction
from locally available materials makes it accessible for small-scale applications.
