## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Mycenaceae
- **Genus:** Mycena
- **Species:** M. interrupta

**Basionym:** Agaricus interruptus Berk. (1843)

**Synonyms:**
- Agaricus interruptus Berk.
- Mycena interrupta (Berk.) Sacc.

The epithet *interrupta* refers to the interrupted or broken appearance
of the gill edges when viewed under magnification.

## Morphology

**Pileus (cap):** 5–20 mm diameter, hemispherical to campanulate when young,
expanding to broadly convex or nearly plane with age. Surface smooth, glabrous,
hygrophanous, pale blue to brilliant azure when moist, fading to whitish or
very pale blue as it dries. Margin striate, translucent-striate when wet.
Flesh thin, membranous, blue, odor mild.

**Lamellae (gills):** Adnate to slightly decurrent, moderately crowded, 16–24
lamellae with 2–4 tiers of lamellulae. Color white to pale blue, edges
concolorous or slightly darker, sometimes appearing flecked.

**Stipe (stem):** 15–50 × 1–2 mm, central, equal or slightly tapered toward the
base. Hollow, fragile, smooth, pale blue to whitish. Base sometimes covered
with fine white tomentum. No [[spore-print]] white.

**Microscopic features:** Cheilocystidia clavate to ventricose, sometimes
with apical projections. Pleurocystidia absent. Pileipellis a cutis of
radially arranged cylindrical hyphae.

## Distribution and Habitat

**Geographic range:** Eastern Australia (New South Wales, Queensland,
Victoria, Tasmania), New Zealand (North Island and South Island), and
reported from New Caledonia. The species is primarily Australasian
with no confirmed records outside this region.

**Habitat:** Found in cool temperate rainforests, wet sclerophyll forests,
and mixed native forests. Fruits solitary to scattered on decaying hardwood
logs, branches, and [[acacia]]* species. Prefers moist, shaded microsites with high humidity.
Typically fruits in autumn through early winter (March–July) in Australia,
and May–August in New Zealand.
