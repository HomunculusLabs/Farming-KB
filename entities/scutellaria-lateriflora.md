## Description

Scutellaria lateriflora is a perennial herbaceous plant in the mint family (Lamiaceae) native to [[growing-gourmet-north-american-reishi-species]] medicinal plants:

### Native American Use

- **Cherokee**: Infusions of the root and leaf used for menstrual disorders, nervous conditions, and as a digestive aid
- **Iroquois**: Used the plant for treating diarrhea, kidney problems, and as a general tonic
- **Delaware**: Employed the plant for promoting menstruation and treating female reproductive complaints

### Thomsonian and Eclectic Medicine

American skullcap was a central nervine remedy in 19th-century American botanical medicine. Samuel Thomson (1769–1843) popularized its use as a nerve tonic and antispasmodic. The Eclectic physicians, particularly John King and John Uri Lloyd, extensively documented its clinical applications in *King's American Dispensatory* (1898), where it was described as "one of the best nervine and antispasmodic vegetables."

Eclectic physicians used skullcap for:

- Nervous exhaustion and "nervous prostration"
- [[valeriana-officinalis]]*, *[[herbal-tinctures-guide]]
- [[scutellaria-baicalensis]].
