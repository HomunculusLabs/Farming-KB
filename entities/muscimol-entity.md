# Muscimol

## Overview

**Muscimol** (5-aminomethyl-3-hydroxyisoxazole) is a naturally occurring psychoactive compound and the principal pharmacologically active constituent of **Amanita muscaria** (fly agaric) and related *Amanita* species. It is a **potent and selective GABA_A receptor agonist**, making it pharmacologically distinct from all classical psychedelics, which act primarily on serotonin receptors. Muscimol produces sedative, hypnotic, dissociative, and oneirogenic (dream-enhancing) effects, and has recently attracted significant commercial interest as an ingredient in psychoactive edibles and wellness products.

Muscimol was first isolated in 1964 by Japanese chemists and independently by Swiss researchers. It is formed biosynthetically from **ibotenic acid** via decarboxylation, a reaction that occurs spontaneously upon heating or drying of mushroom tissue.

### Structural Features

Muscimol is an **isoxazole** derivative — a five-membered ring containing both nitrogen and oxygen adjacent to each other. The molecule is a conformational analog of **GABA** (γ-aminobutyric acid), the brain's primary inhibitory neurotransmitter. This structural similarity underlies its high affinity for GABA_A receptors. The 3-hydroxy group on the isoxazole ring and the aminomethyl side chain at the 5-position are both essential for receptor binding.

### Biosynthesis

In *Amanita* species, muscimol is produced as the decarboxylation product of ibotenic acid (α-amino-3-hydroxy-5-isoxazoleacetic acid). This conversion occurs via:
1. **Enzymatic decarboxylation** in living fungal tissue (catalyzed by glutamate decarboxylase-like enzymes)
2. **Thermal decarboxylation** during drying, cooking, or smoking of mushroom material
3. **Spontaneous decarboxylation** at physiological pH and temperature

This is why traditional preparation methods (parboiling and drying) increase the muscimol:ibotenic acid ratio and improve the subjective experience.

## Pharmacology

### Receptor Binding Profile

| Receptor | Affinity (Ki) | Activity |
|----------|--------------|----------|
| **GABA_A** | ~100–300 nM | Full agonist |
| **GABA_A (ρ subunits / GABA_C)** | ~1 μM | Partial agonist |
| **GABA_B** | No significant binding | — |
| **5-HT₂A** | No binding | — |
| **NMDA** | No binding | — |
| **Dopamine receptors** | No binding | — |

### Mechanism of Action

As a GABA_A receptor agonist, muscimol binds to the same site as GABA and activates the receptor's chloride ion channel. This produces:

1. **Increased chloride conductance** — neuronal membrane hyperpolarization
2. **Reduced neuronal excitability** — generalized CNS depression
3. **Altered thalamocortical signaling** — disruption of sensory processing contributing to dissociative effects
4. **Modulation of sleep architecture** — enhanced slow-wave sleep and REM sleep intensity

Unlike benzodiazepines (which are positive allosteric modulators), muscimol is a **direct orthosteric agonist** — it activates the receptor directly. This produces a qualitatively different experience: deeper, more immersive, and less controllable than benzodiazepine sedation.

### Subjective Effects

Dose-dependent effects (approximate, based on dried *A. muscaria* equivalent):
- **Low dose (5–10 mg muscimol):** relaxation, mild sedation, body warmth, slight perceptual changes
- **Moderate dose (10–15 mg):** pronounced dissociation, dream-like visual states, altered body image (Alice-in-Wonderland effect), introspective calm
- **High dose (15+ mg):** intense oneirogenic (dream-like) immersion, ego dissolution, amnesia, out-of-body experiences, deep sleep with vivid dreams

The experience is characterized by:
- **Onset:** 30–90 minutes (oral), 5–15 minutes (smoked/vaporized)
- **Duration:** 4–8 hours (oral), 1–3 hours (smoked)
- **Afterglow:** calm, relaxed state persisting several hours after primary effects subside
- **Come-up:** often preceded by nausea, sweating, and salivation (the "purge phase")

## Pharmacokinetics

- **Absorption:** Oral bioavailability is moderate (~40–50%); enhanced by combining with lipids
- **Distribution:** Crosses the blood-brain barrier readily; volume of distribution ~1.5–2.5 L/kg
- **Metabolism:** Minimal hepatic metabolism; primarily excreted unchanged
- **Half-life:** 2–3 hours
- **Excretion:** Renal, largely as unchanged muscimol; this is the basis for the Siberian urine-recycling practice
- **Blood-brain barrier:** Readily crosses due to small molecular size and partial lipophilicity

## Toxicology

### Acute Toxicity

- **LD₅₀ (mice, IP):** ~45 mg/kg
- **LD₅₀ (rats, oral):** ~125 mg/kg
- **Therapeutic index:** Moderate — the effective dose is relatively close to the toxic dose
- **No confirmed human fatalities** from muscimol alone

### Risks and Adverse Effects

- **Nausea and vomiting** — common with crude preparations containing residual ibotenic acid
- **Confusion and delirium** — at high doses, particularly in elderly individuals
- **Ataxia and incoordination** — falls and injury risk
- **Respiratory depression** — theoretical risk at very high doses
- **CNS depressant interaction** — alcohol and benzodiazepines potentiate sedation
- **Coma** — reported in massive overdose, typically resolving within 24 hours

## Emerging Research and Therapeutic Potential

Muscimol is being investigated for several therapeutic applications:

- **Insomnia and sleep disorders** — GABA_A agonism promotes sleep onset
- **Anxiety disorders** — anxiolytic effects without benzodiazepine dependence liability
- **Alcohol use disorder** — modulation of GABAergic dysregulation
- **Dream therapy** — oneirogenic properties explored for nightmare disorder

## Legal Status

Muscimol itself is not scheduled under the UN Convention on Psychotropic Substances or the U.S. Controlled Substances Act. Regulatory status is evolving:

| Jurisdiction | Status |
|-------------|--------|
| United States | Unscheduled federally; some states regulating Amanita products |
| European Union | Generally unscheduled; under review |
| Australia | Unscheduled |
