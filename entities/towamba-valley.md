## Towamba Valley

## Description

The Towamba Valley is a valley located on the far south coast of New South Wales, Australia, situated at the centre of one of the most significant ecotones on the Australian continent. It represents a three-way edge between the warm temperate east coast, the cool temperate south coast, and the cold dry plateaus of the Monaro region. This convergence of three major bioregions creates extraordinary biodiversity, including an exceptional concentration of eucalypt species.

## Classification

- **Type:** Place — valley, [[ecotone]]
- **Location:** Far south coast, New South Wales, Australia
- **Biogeographic significance:** Three-way ecotone between major Australian bioregions

## Key Facts

- **Exceptional eucalypt diversity:** During development of a bush property in the Towamba Valley in 1980, Holmgren documented 13 species of eucalypts from all three bioregions on less than 75 hectares. This is remarkable compared to typical properties of similar size in southern Australia, where 2 to 8 eucalypt species would be expected.

- **Three-way bioregional convergence:** The valley sits at the intersection of:
  - **Warm temperate east coast** bioregion
  - **Cool temperate south coast** bioregion
  - **Cold dry Monaro plateau** bioregion

- **Complex microclimate:** The convergence of three bioregional influences produces bizarre combinations of weather that are of critical importance to what can be grown in gardens and on properties in the area.

- **Management complexity:** The diversity of species from multiple bioregions creates complexities for forest management and timber value assessment, as species have different growth habits, timber qualities, and management requirements.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

The Towamba Valley ecotone is a textbook example for permaculture designers:

1. **[[edge-effect]] at landscape scale (Principle 11):** The valley demonstrates that edges between bioregions — not just between garden beds or paddocks — are zones of amplified diversity and productivity. This principle scales from centimetres to hundreds of kilometres.

2. **Site selection insight:** Properties located at ecological edges may offer extraordinary natural diversity and productive potential. Understanding bioregional ecotones can inform property purchase and design decisions.

3. **Climate uncertainty and adaptability:** The bizarre weather combinations at this ecotone mean that a wider range of species may find suitable conditions than in more climatically homogeneous areas — both a challenge and an opportunity for cultivation.

4. **[[design-from-patterns-to-details]] (Principle 7):** The ecotone pattern visible at Towamba — overlapping species distributions creating zones of heightened diversity — is a pattern that permaculture designers can replicate at smaller scales through strategic placement of different microclimates, soil treatments, and plant communities.

5. **Venn diagram analogy:** The Towamba ecotone functions like a Venn diagram where three circles overlap, and the central zone contains elements from all three — a conceptual tool that designers can use to plan for maximum beneficial overlap between different system elements.
