## Overview

**Lotus corniculatus** (bird's-foot trefoil, common bird's-foot trefoil,
butter-and-eggs) is a perennial herbaceous legume in the family Fabaceae.
Native to Eurasia and North Africa, it is widely naturalized across temperate
regions worldwide and valued as a forage crop, ground cover, and
biodiversity-supporting plant.

The species forms symbiotic relationships with rhizobia to fix atmospheric
nitrogen, making it valuable in sustainable agriculture and permaculture
systems. Its bright yellow-and-orange flowers are an important nectar source
for pollinators.

Unlike many legumes, *L. corniculatus* does not cause bloat in ruminants
due to its condensed tannin content, giving it advantages over alfalfa and
white clover as a forage.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Taxonomy:**

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Order:** Fabales
- **Family:** Fabaceae (subfam. Faboideae, tribe Loteae)
- **Genus:** *Lotus*
- **Species:** *L. corniculatus* L.

**Morphology:**

- **Root:** Deep taproot (to 1 m+), extensively branched, with rhizobial
  nodules on lateral roots
- **Stems:** 10–40 cm, prostrate to ascending, branching from base, green
  to reddish-green, glabrous
- **Leaves:** Compound trifoliate, 5–15 mm leaflets, obovate; distinctive
  stipules at base of petiole adnate to stem
- **Flowers:** Umbel-like racemes of 5–12 flowers, 10–15 mm; pea-type,
  yellow with red or orange veins (hence "butter-and-eggs"); calyx
  tubular, 5-toothed; standard petal 8–12 mm
- **Fruit:** Cylindrical pod, 15–30 mm × 2–3 mm, dark brown when ripe,
  spreading at maturity (resembling a bird's foot)
- **Seeds:** 6–10 per pod, 1–1.5 mm, olive-green to brown

## Distribution and Habitat

Native to Europe, western Asia, and North Africa. Naturalized across
North America, South America, Australia, and New Zealand. In North America,
it occurs in all contiguous US states and most Canadian provinces.

Grows in a wide range of habitats: grasslands, meadows, roadsides,
pastures, and disturbed sites. Tolerates poor, acidic, and shallow soils
better than most forage legumes. Found from sea level to 2,500 m.

## Cultivation and Agronomic Use

*L. corniculatus* is a widely used forage legume, particularly on marginal
or acidic soils where alfalfa (*Medicago sativa*) performs poorly.

- **Establishment:** Seed at 4–8 kg/ha; seed requires scarification or
  inoculation with compatible *Lotus*-specific rhizobia (e.g., *Mesorhizobium
  loti*). Slow to establish compared to clovers
- **Persistence:** Long-lived perennial; persists 5–10+ years in pastures
- **Yield:** Dry matter yield 4–8 t/ha depending on fertility and moisture
- **Nutrition:** Crude protein 18–22% DMD; moderate digestibility; high
  condensed tannins (2–4% DM) prevent bloat and improve protein utilization

## Nitrogen Fixation

*L. corniculatus* fixes atmospheric nitrogen through symbiosis with
*Mesorhizobium loti* (formerly *Rhizobium loti*). Effective nodules are
pink due to leghemoglobin.

Fixation rates: 50–200 kg N/ha/year depending on stand density, soil
conditions, and climate. This makes the species valuable in low-input
pasture systems and organic farming.

The deep taproot accesses subsoil nutrients and moisture, cycling them to
the surface through leaf litter.

## Ecological Value

*L. corniculatus* is one of the most important nectar plants for
temperate pollinators. Long flowering (May–September) provides continuous
forage for bees, butterflies, and hoverflies.

It is the primary larval host for several butterflies, including the common
blue (*Polyommatus icarus*) and the dingy skipper (*Erynnis tages*).

Condensed tannins provide chemical defense against herbivory while
maintaining palatability for ruminants — an unusual combination.

## Bioactive Compounds

Condensed tannins (proanthocyanidins) are the primary secondary metabolites:

- **Cyanogenic glycosides:** Lotusin and linamarin (trace amounts; not
  hazardous at normal forage concentrations)
- **Flavonoids:** Quercetin, kaempferol, and isorhamnetin glycosides
- **Tannins:** Prodelphinidin-type condensed tannins, responsible for
  the non-bloating properties and anthelmintic activity

Traditional medicinal uses include treatment of heart palpitations and
respiratory ailments, though clinical evidence is limited.
## Permaculture Applications

*L. corniculatus* serves multiple functions in designed ecosystems:
- **Nitrogen fixation:** Permanent N input in food forests and pastures
  without annual reseeding
- **Ground cover:** Dense mat suppresses weeds and protects soil from erosion
- **Pollinator support:** Long bloom supports beneficial insects throughout
  the growing season
- **Forage integration:** Requires no nitrogen fertilizer and prevents bloat
- **Soil improvement:** Deep taproot breaks compacted subsoil; leaf litter
  adds organic matter
