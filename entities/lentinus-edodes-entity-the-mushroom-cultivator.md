# Lentinus edodes

## Taxonomy

Lentinus edodes (Berk.) Sing. is a basidiomycete fungus in the family Polyporaceae (or
Lentinaceae, depending on classification system). The genus name Lentinus comes from
"lentis" meaning lens-shaped, referring to the form of the cap, while "edodes" signifies
the edibility of this species. Commonly known as the Shiitake Mushroom, the Japanese
Black Mushroom, and the Chinese Black Mushroom. The name "shiitake" derives from the
mushroom's association with the shii tree (Pasania), a member of the Fagaceae family.

Numerous strains of Lentinus edodes are available from commercial and private stocks.
The American Type Culture Collection maintains several wild and domesticated strains.
Strains are often distinguished by their preferences for fruiting in colder or warmer
temperature zones.

Genetic characteristics include basidia that are tetrapolar, forming four haploid spores.
The species is heterothallic. Dikaryons feature clamp connections.

## Morphology

The cap is pale to dark reddish brown, convex when young, becoming broadly convex to
nearly plane with age. The cap margin is typically inrolled when young and covered with
whitish veil remnants, especially along the margin. The flesh is firm, pliant, and easily
drying and reconstituting.

The gills are whitish, close to crowded, and often feature serrated edges. The stem is
centrally attached to the cap, short, very tough, and adorned with scattered fibrillose
remnants of the partial veil. Spores are whitish in mass deposit.

Mycelial types range from rhizomorphic to linear depending on strain and substrate
conditions.

## Habitat and Ecology

Lentinus edodes is a wood decomposer, typically saprophytic. Lentinus species are common
on the dead tissue of deciduous trees, mainly from the family Fagaceae, including oak,
chestnut, shii (Pasania), and beech. In nature, they particularly prefer oaks.

Fruiting occurs in the fall, early winter, and spring. This species is indigenous to
Japan, China, and other countries in the temperate zone of the Indo-China region. The
mushroom has been cultivated in Japan for approximately two thousand years.

## Role in Mushroom Cultivation

Lentinus edodes is the most notable commercial species grown on wood. The traditional
method of cultivation involves oak logs 3 to 6 inches in diameter and three feet long,
cut between fall and spring when sap content is highest. The bark is critical for fruiting
and is one of the key factors considered by commercial growers when selecting tree
species.

Logs are drilled with four longitudinal rows of one-inch deep holes spaced eight inches
apart, plugged with spawn, and covered with wax. After 9 to 15 months of incubation the
logs begin to fruit. Alternatively, sawdust and bran are the preferred spawn materials for
indoor cultivation, with a standard ratio of 4 parts sawdust to 1 part bran.

Sawdust based substrates have been developed in Japan for growing Lentinus edodes and are
increasingly used by commercial shiitake growers in the United States. These substrates
are sterilized at 15 psi for 60 to 90 minutes. Fortified rye grass straw has also been
used as a sterile fruiting medium.

## Cultivation Parameters

### Spawn Run
- **Spawn Medium:** Pre-soaked wooden dowels or a 4:1 sawdust/bran mixture
- **Relative Humidity:** 60 to 75% for logs; 90% for sawdust blocks
- **Substrate Temperature:** Fast growth at 77 degrees F; growth stops above 95 degrees F and below 41 degrees F
- **Duration:** 6 to 12 months for cut logs; 30 to 60 days for sawdust blocks
- **CO2:** No controls needed using standard methods
- **pH Optima:** 5 to 6
- **Light:** None required

### Pinhead Initiation
- **Initiation Technique:** Submerge logs and blocks in cold water for 24 to 72 hours
- **Relative Humidity:** 95%
- **Air Temperature:** 59 to 68 degrees F
- **Duration:** 7 to 14 days after soaking
- **Fresh Air Exchanges:** 2 to 4 per hour in greenhouse settings
- **Light:** Ambient natural light or optimally 10 lux in the 370 to 420 nanometer range

### Cropping
- **Relative Humidity:** 85 to 90%
- **Air Temperature:** 59 to 68 degrees F
- **CO2:** Less than 1000 ppm
- **Fresh Air Exchanges:** 2 to 4 per hour
- **Duration:** 3 to 5 years on oak logs; 2 to 3 years on alder
- **Harvest Stage:** Directly before the incurved margin straightens and the cap expands to plane
- **Flushing Interval:** 2 flushes per year outdoors (fall and spring); up to 4 flushes indoors

### Yield and Nutrition
- **Yield Potential:** 2 to 3 lbs fresh weight per log (commercial average)
- **Moisture Content:** 85% water; 15% dry matter
- **Nutritional Content:** 10.0 to 17.5% crude protein (dry weight); 55 mg niacin per 100 g dry weight

## Interactions

Compounds in this mushroom have anti-cholesterol effects. Chihara (1979) reported that
lentinan, a water soluble polysaccharide in L. edodes, was found to almost completely
regress solid type tumors of sarcoma-180 and several kinds of tumors. Other researchers
(Cochran, 1978; Tokita et al., 1972; Tokuda and Kaneda, 1979) have similarly described
beneficial properties.

Individuals can significantly reduce serum cholesterol levels by eating these mushrooms
for as short a period as one week (Suzuki and Ohshima, 1974). Extracts from shiitake
spores and the isolation of mushroom RNA from them have proved effective against influenza
(Suzuki et al., 1974). Health conscious individuals beginning a daily regimen of eating
shiitake have been shown to be less susceptible to virus-induced diseases.

Bacillus thermomucicolous, if introduced at spawning, inhibits the growth of competitor
molds in rice bran/sawdust spawn prepared for shiitake cultivation (Steineck, 1973).

Supplements that increased mycelial growth more than rice bran include yeast powder (2.0%),
soybean meal (5.0%), milk powder (2.0%), and molasses (1.5%). Water bath periods of 48 to
72 hours increased moisture content by 5 to 15% and yields by 50%.
