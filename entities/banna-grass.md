## Banna Grass

## Description

Fast-growing tall grass ([[permaculture]]. Provides rapid wind protection and abundant
biomass while slower permanent hedges develop. Furrow-planted on manure and
requires irrigation to remain productive.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Family**: Poaceae
- **Genus**: [[pennisetum]]
- **Common names**: Banna grass, Napier grass, [[acacia]].

### Parallel Planting System
The recommended dryland garden boundary uses parallel plantings:
- **Inner**: Banna grass on manure furrows (fast, temporary, irrigated)
- **Outer**: Columnar cactus or Euphorbia (slow, permanent, drought-hardy)
- **Additional**: Thorny Acacia species for animal exclusion

### Mulch Production
Regular cutting provides abundant material for:
- Garden bed mulching
- [[compost]] pit filling
- Potato box mulching
- Soil improvement through [[decomposition]]

### Animal Fodder
Highly nutritious as livestock fodder for cattle, goats, and other domestic
animals in integrated permaculture systems.

## Cultivation Requirements

- Furrow-planted on manure for rapid establishment
- Requires irrigation to maintain productivity
- Cannot survive without water — not for unirrigated sites
- Regular cutting prevents lodging and invasiveness

## Key Facts

- Fastest-establishing hedge species in dryland gardens
- Requires irrigation to remain productive
- Furrow-planted on manure for best results
- Temporary — replaced by permanent species
- Excellent mulch source when cut
- Also serves as animal fodder
- 2-4m height provides effective windbreak

## Advantages

- Immediate wind protection from rapid growth
- Abundant mulch material
- Additional animal fodder
- Low establishment cost
- Can be removed once permanent hedges mature

## Limitations

- Cannot survive without irrigation
- Temporary — dies back without water
- Can become invasive if unmanaged
- Does not provide permanent boundary

## Relevance to Cultivation

Serves as nurse crop and biomass engine in permaculture systems. Rapid growth
provides immediate protection while permanent species establish. Succession-based
approach fundamental to permaculture — using short-lived productive species to
create conditions for long-lived productive ones.
