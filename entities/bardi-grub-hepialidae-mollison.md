# Bardi Grub (Hepialidae)

## Description

Bardi grubs are the larval stage of swift moths (family Hepialidae), large subterranean larvae that spend 7-8 years underground feeding on root sheaths and soil organic matter. Mollison describes them as a significant component of soil ecosystem engineering in Australia, where they open thousands of shafts to the air and cycle tons of nutrient underground. They are also a traditional food source for Indigenous Australians.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Animalia
- **Phylum**: Arthropoda
- **Class**: Insecta
- **Order**: Lepidoptera
- **Family**: Hepialidae (swift moths, ghost moths)
- **Common names**: Bardi grub, witchetty grub (related species), bardy grub
- **Related**: Ghost moths, witchetty grubs (different but closely related larvae)

## Key Facts

- Bardi grubs spend 7-8 years underground as large larvae, with only a few days of nocturnal, foodless life in air for mating and egg-laying before returning to root sheaths and soil.
- During their extended subterranean life, they open thousands of shafts to the air, providing essential soil aeration channels.
- They cycle tons of nutrient underground, processing root material and organic matter at depth.
- Bardi grubs are described as "a succulent treat for Australian diggers" — a high-protein traditional food source for Aboriginal peoples.
- Their near cousins the ghost moths and witchetty grubs perform similar ecological roles in aerial stems and root zones.
