# Psilocybe martinezii

**Psilocybe martinezii** is a psilocybin-containing mushroom in the family Hymenogastraceae, originally described from the state of Oaxaca in southern Mexico. The species was first collected by the Mexican mycologist gastón-guzmán and named in honor of a local collector who assisted with fieldwork in the region. It belongs to [[psilocybe-tampanensis-entity]], and psilocybe atlantis, all of which share a preference for subtropical to tropical environments and produce sclerotia (hardened masses of mycelium) under certain conditions. The taxonomic position within this section is supported by both morphological characters (spore shape, cystidia morphology, habitat) and molecular phylogenetic data. Some molecular studies have suggested that section Mexicanae may be polyphyletic, but psilocybe martinezii consistently clusters with other Mexican members of the group.

## Morphology

The pileus is 8–20 mm in diameter, hemispherical to convex when young, expanding to broadly convex or nearly plane with age. The surface is smooth and slightly viscid when moist, hygrophanous, dark brown to reddish brown at the disc fading to a paler cinnamon or tan color toward the margin. The margin is slightly translucent-striate when moist, and the surface is often adorned with fine whitish fibrils or remnants of the partial veil when young. The flesh is thin and fragile, whitish, and develops a moderate blue bruising when damaged.

The lamellae are adnexed to adnate, subdistant to close, narrow, and initially grayish with a vinaceous or purplish tint. They darken to purplish brown or nearly black as the spores mature. The stipe is 30–60 mm long and 1.5–2.5 mm thick, cylindrical, equal, and hollow. It is covered with fine white to silvery appressed fibrils that create a silky appearance, more conspicuous near the apex. A rudimentary annular zone may be present from the evanescent partial veil. The stipe base is slightly thickened and sometimes develops a whitish mycelial tomentum. Bluing on the stipe is moderate to strong, especially near the base.

## Microscopic Features

Basidiospores are ellipsoid to slightly ovoid in face view, occasionally slightly rhomboid, measuring 7–9 × 4.5–5.5 μm. The spore wall is approximately 0.5 μm thick, smooth, with a distinct apical germ pore 1–1.5 μm in diameter. The spore print is dark purplish brown to nearly black. Basidia are two- or four-spored, clavate, 14–18 × 4–5 μm. Cheilocystidia are numerous, forming a conspicuous sterile band on the gill edges, varying from ventricose to lageniform or subcapitate, 12–22 × 4–7 μm. Pleurocystidia are rare or absent. The pileipellis is a cutis of narrow (2–3.5 μm), hyaline, thin-walled, gelatinized hyphae.

## Distribution and Habitat

The species is known primarily from Oaxaca state in southern Mexico, where it occurs at moderate elevations (approximately 1,000–2,000 meters) in subtropical and tropical montane forests. It has been collected in cloud forest margins, along trails in disturbed areas, and in pastures enriched with animal dung. Fruiting occurs during the rainy season from June to October. The species shows a preference for well-decomposed herbivore dung (primarily bovine) and enriched soil, a characteristic it shares with other members of section Mexicanae. It has also been reported from the states of Puebla and Veracruz, suggesting a broader distribution in southern Mexico than initially documented.
