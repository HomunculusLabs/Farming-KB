## Cultivation and Permaculture

Lesser yam is typically propagated vegetatively using small tuber pieces (setts) of 50–150 g, each containing at least one bud or eye. Setts are planted at the onset of rains, 5–10 cm deep in mounds or ridges spaced 80–100 cm apart.

Support structures (trellises, stakes, or companion trees) are essential for optimal vine climbing and tuber development. Plants trained on supports consistently outperform ground-trailing plants by 30–50% in tuber yield.

The crop has a growing period of 7–11 months, with tuber maturation typically occurring 8–9 months after planting. Yields range from 8–25 tonnes per hectare depending on variety, soil fertility, and management practices. The tubers store well under ambient tropical conditions for 3–5 months, making them an important famine-reserve crop.

**Permaculture functions:**

- **Vertical production:** The climbing habit enables high-yield production in minimal ground area, making it suitable for smallholder and [[pdc-urban-permaculture-design-principles]] systems.
- **Agroforestry integration:** Grows effectively beneath fruit trees (coconut, breadfruit, mango) and between annual crops, utilizing vertical space without competing for ground-level light.
