## Ecology

As a large pleurocarpous-feathermoss mimic, *D. polysetum* contributes significantly to the moss layer that covers 60–80% of the boreal forest floor in many regions.

The species intercepts and retains precipitation, reducing surface runoff and maintaining soil moisture levels critical for conifer seedling establishment.

Its dense mats create favorable microsites for the germination of spruce and fir seeds, serving as a primary seedbed in boreal forest regeneration.

The moss layer moderates soil temperature fluctuations, insulating permafrost and maintaining cool root-zone conditions during summer.

*D. polysetum* accumulates atmospheric nitrogen and phosphorus from precipitation and throughfall, releasing nutrients slowly upon decomposition.

Decomposition rates of dicranoid mosses are slow, contributing to the build-up of organic soil horizons characteristic of boreal podzols.

The species provides habitat for microarthropods including oribatid mites, springtails, and nematodes that form the base of boreal soil food webs.

## Uses and Significance

*Dicranum polysetum* has limited direct economic use but is ecologically important as a dominant component of the boreal forest ground layer.

Historically, large *Dicranum* species were collected in Scandinavia and Russia for use as mattress stuffing and packing material, though this practice has largely ceased.

The species is used as a bioindicator of old-growth boreal forest conditions, declining in abundance under intensive [[mycelium-running-forestry-practices-and-mushrooms]] that remove the moss layer.

It is sensitive to sulfur dioxide and other air pollutants, making it useful for monitoring air quality in boreal and sub-boreal regions.
