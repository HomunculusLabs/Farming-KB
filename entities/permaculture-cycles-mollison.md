## Permaculture Cycles

## Description

Cycles are a foundational concept in [[permaculture]]: A Designers' Manual*, presented in section 2.6 as "A Niche in Time." Mollison defines cycles as recurring events or phenomena that represent "diversion routes away from entropic ends" — the mechanism by which life itself maintains and builds complexity. Cycles are presented as the temporal complement to spatial niches.

## Classification

- **Concept type**: Core permaculture principle
- **Category**: Systems theory / Ecological design
- **Related principles**: Niche theory, nutrient cycling, succession, entropy

## Key Facts

- Mollison's Principle: "Every cyclic event increases the opportunity for yield. To increase cycling is to increase yield."
- Cycles are defined as "interruptions or eddies in the straight-line progression towards entropy" — it is "the special provenance of life to cycle materials."
- In tropical forests, "almost all material nutrients are in cycle in life forms" — an extremely efficient system.
- "If NICHES are opportunities in space, CYCLES are opportunities in time (a time-slot) and both together give harbour to many events and species."
- Each cycle is unique: "diet, choice, selection, season, weather, digestion, decomposition, and regeneration differ each time it happens."
- The number of cycles determines the potential for diversity.
- Mollison connects cycles to deep ecology: "Just by living we have achieved immortality — as grass, grasshoppers, gulls, geese, and other people."

## Types of Cycles Described

1. **Nutrient cycles**: Geese eat grass, digest it, defecate; fungi, bacteria, and roots decompose waste; grass regrows. Each cycle is unique.
2. **Degenerative-regenerative cycles**: Continuous breakdown and rebuilding that creates opportunities for new species and yields.
3. **Climatic cycles**: Day/night, seasons, sunspot cycles — all create time-niches.
4. **Life cycles**: Organisms cycling materials through themselves — "People are built up molecule by molecule, cycling through themselves the materials of their environment."

## Design Applications

- Build as many cycles as possible into every permaculture system
- Close loops: return all wastes to productive use
- Use animals as cycling agents (pigeons as phosphate factories)
- Design for both spatial niches AND temporal cycles
- Recognise that cycling rate directly determines system productivity

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Cycles are the fundamental process in mycology. [[permaculture-design]] that maximises cycling — through composting, mulching, polyculture, animal integration, and soil food web support — directly amplifies the mycological engine that drives fertility.
