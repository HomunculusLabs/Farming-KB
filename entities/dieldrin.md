# Dieldrin

## Description

Dieldrin is a persistent organochlorine insecticide and the primary environmental breakdown product of Aldrin. It was widely used in agriculture and termite control from the 1950s onward and is now banned globally under the Stockholm Convention due to its extreme persistence, bioaccumulation through food chains, and toxicity to humans and wildlife. Mollison highlights Dieldrin as a critical contamination concern on former agricultural land, noting that even 18-20 years after cessation of use, residual levels remain dangerous.

## Classification

- **Category**: Chemical / Organochlorine pesticide
- **Chemical formula**: C12H8Cl6O
- **CAS number**: 60-57-1
- **Parent compound**: Aldrin (converts to Dieldrin in environment and organisms)
- **Regulatory status**: Banned globally under Stockholm Convention (2001)

## Key Facts

- Aldrin rapidly oxidizes to Dieldrin in the environment and in living organisms
- Dieldrin is more toxic and more persistent than its parent compound Aldrin
- Apple orchards in West Australia still produce unsafe Dieldrin levels in free-range chicken eggs 18-20 years after chemical use ceased
- Attempts to grow prawns in ponds on former agricultural land failed due to residual Dieldrin in soils
- Banned in the USA in 1987 (all uses except termite control); fully banned by EPA in 2000
- Extremely bioaccumulative with a half-life of 2-7 years in soil
- Mollison lists it alongside DDT, Aldrin, and BHC as persistent biocides requiring special management

## Environmental Fate

- Strongly adsorbed to soil particles, particularly clays [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]]
- Resists degradation by sunlight, heat, or microbial action
- Volatilizes slowly from soil surface over many years
- Enters water bodies through soil erosion and runoff
- Accumulates in aquatic sediments where it persists indefinitely
- Biomagnifies through food chains: soil to plants to insects to birds and fish to mammals
- Arsenic may stay at the surface in clay soils or wash to subsoil (50-60 cm) in acid sands

## Health Effects

- Potent neurotoxin affecting the central nervous system
- Probable human carcinogen (EPA Group B2)
- Causes liver damage and toxaemic jaundice (liver poisoning)
- Disrupts endocrine and reproductive function
- Chronic low-level exposure through contaminated food is the primary risk pathway
- Children and developing fetuses are most vulnerable
- Excess copper in the diet causes toxaemic jaundice and blood in the urine

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

### Land Assessment Critical
- Former orchards, cane fields, cotton fields, banana plantations, and pineapple farms are highest risk
