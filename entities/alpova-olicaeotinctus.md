## Description

**Alpova olicaeotinctus** is a species of sequestrate (truffle-like) ectomycorrhizal fungus in the family Boletaceae, related to the genus Melanogaster. It was studied by Schultz (1991) for its palatability to [[collembola]] in pairwise feeding choice experiments demonstrating temporal changes in fungal food preference.









## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Phylum**: Basidiomycota
- **Class**: Agaricomycetes
- **Order**: Boletales
- **Family**: Boletaceae (or Melanogastraceae)
- **Genus**: Alpova
- **Species**: A. olicaeotinctus









## Key Facts

- Schultz (1991) used it in pairwise choice experiments with the collembolan Proistoma minuta
- Offered alongside [[melanogaster-tuberiformis]], unknown isolate M-40, and PDA agar
- Collembolan feeding preferences changed over time when fungal food items were combined
- A sequestrate (hypogeous) fungus producing below-ground fruiting bodies
- Demonstrates that fungal palatability to collembola is not taxonomically determined









## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

A. olicaeotinctus is relevant to understanding feeding preferences of soil microarthropods for different [[ectomycorrhizal-fungi]]. The temporal changes in collembolan preference suggest that fungal palatability may change as fungi grow, possibly due to changes in secondary metabolite production or mycelial age. For cultivation, this highlights the dynamic nature of fungal-faunal interactions.









## Ecological Role

As a sequestrate ectomycorrhizal fungus, A. olicaeotinctus forms hypogeous fruiting bodies and relies on animals (particularly small mammals) for [[spore-dispersal]]. Its below-ground fruiting habit makes it a component of forest soil biodiversity and a food resource for soil-dwelling invertebrates and small mammals.
