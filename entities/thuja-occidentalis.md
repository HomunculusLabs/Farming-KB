# Thuja occidentalis

## Overview

**Thuja occidentalis**, northern white cedar or arborvitae ("tree of life"), is an evergreen
coniferous tree in the family Cupressaceae, native to eastern Canada and the northeastern
and north-central [[ethnomycology-cultural-significance-sacred-fungi]] among Indigenous peoples of northeastern
North America, who used it as medicine, [[sabinene]], terpinene-4-ol, bornyl acetate:** Minor constituents with synergistic effects.
- **Flavonoids:** [[permaculture-designers-manual-essential-oil-plants]] active against *C. albicans*, *Trichophyton* spp., and
  dermatophytes.

**Safety:** Internal use of thujone-rich essential oil contraindicated in pregnancy, epilepsy,
and children. Concentrated oil can cause seizures and hepatotoxicity. Tinctures (1:5, 45%
ethanol) at 1–3 mL/day are considered safe; the oil itself should not be taken internally.

## Permaculture and Agroforestry Applications

- **Windbreak and hedging:** Dense, columnar habit; growth of 30–60 cm/year; tolerates
  heavy shearing. One of the best coniferous windbreaks for northern sites.
- **Wildlife habitat:** Year-round cover for songbirds, grouse, deer, and snowshoe hare.
  Seeds eaten by finches, siskins, and crossbills. White-tailed deer browse heavily in winter.
- **Swale and riparian stabilization:** Shallow, fibrous roots stabilize wetland edges and
  stream banks.
- **Wood products:** Lightweight, naturally rot-resistant; lasts 15–20+ years in ground
  contact. Valued for fence posts, shingles, cabin logs, and boat building.
- **Guild placement:** Understory component in conifer-hardwood food forests; compatible
  with sugar maple, beech, hemlock, and serviceberry associations.

## Cultivars

- **'Smaragd' (Emerald Green):** Most popular hedging cultivar; narrow, bright green;
  retains color through winter; Zone 2 hardy.
- **'Techny':** Broader pyramidal form; darker green; excellent for larger screens.
- **'Nigra':** Deep green winter color; does not bronze.
- **'Pyramidalis':** Very narrow, fastigiate; formal accent tree.
- **'Tiny Tim':** Dwarf globe form, 60–90 cm at maturity; rock garden specimen.
