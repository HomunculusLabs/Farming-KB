# Bamboo Microorganisms

## Description

Bamboo microorganisms are beneficial indigenous microorganisms collected from the root zone and litter of bamboo plants using a cooked-rice trapping method. In Asian natural farming traditions, bamboo is observed to attract particularly powerful beneficial microorganisms because the roots of bamboo exude sugary substances that attract beneficial microbial communities. These microorganisms are cultured using the same carbohydrate-based fermentation approach used for forest microorganisms, and are valued as a component of the Beneficial Indigenous Microorganism (BIM) consortium used in Korean Natural Farming.

## Classification

- **Category**: Beneficial microorganism culture
- **Collection source**: Bamboo root zone and litter
- **Method**: Cooked-rice trapping technique
- **Field**: Natural farming, Korean Natural Farming, soil microbiology

## Key Facts

- Bamboo roots exude sugary substances that attract beneficial microorganisms, making the bamboo root zone a particularly rich source of desirable microbial diversity.
- Collection method: bury a container with approximately 1 inch of cooked rice in the litter/humus around bamboo plants, cover with metal netting to protect from animals, and allow 2-10 days for microbial colonization (timing depends on temperature).
- After the rice is contaminated with visible white and colored molds (indicating successful microbial trapping), add 1/3 volume of crude sugar or molasses and allow to ferment for one week.
- The resulting sticky, liquidy rice mixture is then preserved by adding equal amount of crude sugar or molasses to arrest microbial activity for storage.
- For use, dilute the serum with 20 parts water (1:20 ratio) to create the basic bamboo microorganism spray.
- Bamboo microorganisms are used as one component of the BIM (Beneficial Indigenous Microorganisms) formulation, typically comprising one part of a diverse blend alongside lactic acid bacteria and other cultured organisms.
- Used specifically for fermenting animal feeds in natural farming systems.
- The recommended BIM formulation is 50% lactic acid bacteria and 50% other microorganisms (forest, bamboo, and plant-specific in roughly equal proportions).

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Bamboo microorganisms are directly relevant to both plant cultivation and mushroom growing as a component of the beneficial microorganism toolkit used to improve soil fertility, plant health, and disease resistance. In natural farming systems, bamboo microorganisms contribute to the microbial biodiversity that is considered essential for balanced soil ecology. For mushroom cultivators, the concept of trapping indigenous microorganisms from specific plant environments is relevant to understanding the ecological relationships between fungi, bacteria, and plant root systems in natural settings. The technique demonstrates how specific plants (like bamboo) cultivate distinct microbial communities that can be harvested and applied agriculturally.

## Collection and Culture Protocol

1. Select a healthy bamboo grove with deep litter/humus accumulation.
2. Place 1 inch of cooked rice in a flat container with a lid.
3. Bury the container in the bamboo litter, leaving air space.
4. Cover with metal netting to protect from animals.
5. Wait 2-10 days (temperature dependent) for microbial colonization.
6. When rice shows visible mold growth (white and colored), add 1/3 volume crude sugar or molasses.
7. Ferment for 7 days until sticky and liquidy.
8. Add equal amount crude sugar or molasses for storage.
9. Dilute 1:20 with non-chlorinated water for use.

## Role in BIM Formulation

Bamboo microorganisms serve as a key component of the diverse BIM consortium:

- **50% Lactic acid bacteria** (primary component for odor control and decomposition)
- **~16.7% Forest microorganisms** (diverse microbial community from aged forest humus)
- **~16.7% Bamboo microorganisms** (powerful beneficial organisms from bamboo root zone)
- **~16.7% Plant-specific microorganisms** (organisms associated with the target crop)

The emphasis on diversity reflects the natural farming principle that a biodiverse microbial community provides more resilient and effective soil biology than a monoculture of any single organism.

## Historical and Cultural Context

The collection of bamboo microorganisms is part of the Korean Natural Farming tradition developed by Dr. Han Kyu Cho and promoted by practitioners like Gil Carandang of Herbana Farms in the Philippines. The approach reflects the Asian natural farming philosophy of working with locally available biological resources rather than purchasing commercial microbial inoculants. The observation that bamboo attracts particularly beneficial microorganisms is based on centuries of empirical observation by East Asian farmers.
