## Overview

**Chloranthus japonicus** Sieb. is a perennial herb in the family Chloranthaceae, one of the most ancient lineages of flowering plants (angiosperms). It is native to cool-temperate forests of East Asia and is valued as an ornamental plant for its unusual inflorescences.

The species is characterized by its opposite leaves with serrate margins and distinctive spike-like inflorescences bearing small, petal-less flowers. It has a long history of cultivation in Japanese gardens, where it is known as *Hitoemusa* (一重茉).

## Taxonomy and Morphology

*Chloranthus japonicus* was described by Philipp Franz von Siebold and is the type species of the section *Fructus* within the genus. The genus *Chloranthus* contains approximately 15–20 species distributed in tropical and temperate Asia.

The plant grows from a slender, creeping rhizome, producing erect aerial stems 15–40 cm tall. Stems are unbranched, four-angled, and slightly pubescent at the nodes.

The rhizome is shallow, typically found within the top 5 cm of the soil surface. It grows slowly, extending 2–5 cm per year, with new aerial shoots arising from nodes along the rhizome.

Leaves are opposite, ovate to elliptic, 5–12 cm long and 3–7 cm wide. Leaf margins are sharply serrate with 8–15 teeth per side, and the leaf base is cuneate to rounded. The upper surface is dark green and glossy.

Leaf venation is prominent, with 3–5 pairs of lateral veins arising from the midrib. Petioles are 1–3 cm long and slightly winged near the base.

Inflorescences are terminal, spike-like panicles 2–5 cm long, composed of numerous tiny flowers arranged along a central axis. Each flower is approximately 2–3 mm long and lacks petals and sepals entirely.

Flowering occurs in April to May, before or coinciding with canopy leaf-out in deciduous forests. This early flowering phenology allows pollination to occur under relatively high light conditions before the forest canopy closes.

The unusual floral structure of Chloranthaceae — with a highly reduced perianth and distinctive stamen morphology — has made the family a key group for understanding floral evolution in early angiosperms.

Male flowers possess a single stamen with a thick, fleshy filament and a short anther. Female flowers have a unilocular ovary with a single ovule and a short style.

The fruit is a small, globose drupe approximately 3–4 mm in diameter, ripening from green to white or pale yellow. Each drupe contains a single seed with a fleshy endosperm and a small straight embryo.

Fruiting occurs from August to September across most of the species' range. The white drupes are conspicuously displayed against the dark green foliage, enhancing their visibility to dispersal agents.

Seed viability in soil seed banks is relatively short, typically less than one year. This limits the species' capacity for long-term dormancy and makes it dependent on annual fruit production for population maintenance.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Chloranthus japonicus* occurs in Japan (Honshu, Shikoku, Kyushu), the Korean Peninsula, and eastern China. In Japan, it is widespread in mountainous regions from the Kanto area northward.

The species grows in deciduous and mixed forests at elevations from 200 to 2000 meters. It is most common in cool, moist forests on north-facing slopes and in valley bottoms.

Preferred habitats include the understory of *Fagus crenata* (Japanese beech) forests, *Quercus crispula* (Mongolian oak) woodlands, and mixed conifer-broadleaved forests. It thrives in deep shade to partial shade conditions.
