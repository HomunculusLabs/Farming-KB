## Overview

*Polytrichum juniperinum* Hedw., the juniper haircap moss, is a widespread
acrocarpous moss in the family Polytrichaceae. It is easily recognized by its
tall, reddish stems topped with a star-like rosette of narrow, lanceolate
leaves resembling juniper foliage.

The species is a common pioneer colonist of disturbed soils, including burned
areas, roadcuts, mine tailings, and recently exposed substrates. Its tolerance
of desiccation and full sun allows it to establish in harsh, open habitats
where few other bryophytes persist.

*P. juniperinum* is circumboreal in distribution and one of the most
frequently encountered Polytrichaceae species in temperate and boreal regions.
It is closely related to the larger [[bloomfield-rust-germ-tube-tactile-sensing-leaf-surface-navigation]], and a calyptra that covers
the developing spore capsule. Molecular studies confirm the monophyly of
Polytrichaceae and place *Polytrichum* as sister to *Polytrichastrum*.

Plants form loose tufts or cushions 3–12 cm tall. Stems are erect, reddish to
brownish, and typically unbranched. The upper stem is surrounded by a sheath of
withered leaf bases.

Leaves are 5–8 mm long, lanceolate to linear-lanceolate, with incurved margins
and a sharp, serrulate apex. The leaf margins are strongly recurved in the
lower third, a distinctive feature separating it from *P. commune*.

## Distribution and Habitat

*P. juniperinum* has a circumboreal distribution across Europe, Asia, and
[[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-south-america]], Australasia, and
mountainous [[fukuoka-textdoc-agricultural-land-law-obstruction-return-farming]]. It is among the first bryophytes to establish on volcanic
lava flows and glacial moraines.

Preferred substrates include acidic to neutral sandy or loamy soils with good
drainage. It tolerates higher pH and nutrient levels than many Sphagnum
species, allowing it to coexist with vascular plants in early successional
communities.

The species grows in full sun to moderate shade. It is more drought-tolerant
than most mosses due to its thickened leaf margins, dense lamellae, and
conductive tissue. However, prolonged desiccation reduces growth rates
significantly.
