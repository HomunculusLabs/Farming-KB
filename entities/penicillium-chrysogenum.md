# Penicillium chrysogenum

**Type:** Species (Fungus)
**Category:** Filamentous Ascomycete
**Also known as:** Penicillium notatum (historical synonym)

## Overview
Penicillium chrysogenum is one of the most historically significant
fungal species in the history of science and medicine. It is the
original source of penicillin, the first widely used antibiotic, whose
discovery by Alexander Fleming in 1928 revolutionized the treatment of
[[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]], and
indoor environments. The source text specifically cites P. chrysogenum
as an example of a fungus that has had a major impact on human
civilization as a source of medicine.

## Taxonomy and Classification
- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** [[psilocybe-semilanceata]] in soil, decaying vegetation, and
  indoor environments
- Commonly isolated from household dust, air samples, and food
  products
- Found in temperate and subtropical regions worldwide
- Frequently encountered as a contaminant in laboratory settings
- Salt-tolerant strains have been isolated from marine
  environments

### Historical Significance
- Original Fleming isolate (P. notatum, later reclassified as
  P. chrysogenum) produced the first penicillin
- A strain from a moldy cantaloupe in Peoria, Illinois (NRRL
  1951) became the basis for industrial penicillin production
- This strain was subjected to extensive mutagenesis and
  selection programs, increasing penicillin yields more than
  1000-fold
- The development of deep-tank fermentation for penicillin
  production laid the foundation for the modern biotechnology
  industry

### Penicillin Biosynthesis
- Penicillin is synthesized via the non-ribosomal peptide
  synthetase (NRPS) pathway
- Key intermediates include LLD-ACV, isopenicillin N, and
  phenylacetyl-CoA
- The penicillin biosynthetic gene cluster contains pcbAB,
  pcbC, and penDE
- Industrial strains produce penicillin G (benzylpenicillin) and
  penicillin V (phenoxymethylpenicillin)
- [[mushroom-strain-improvement-and-breeding-methods]] continues through classical mutagenesis
  and modern genome engineering approaches

### Biotechnological Applications
- Penicillium chrysogenum remains the primary industrial source
