## Overview

**stephania tetrandra ra ra** S. Moore, known as **fang ji** (防己) or **han fang ji**
(汉防己) in Chinese medicine, is a perennial climbing vine in the family
Menispermaceae. The dried root (Radix Stephaniae Tetrandrae) has been used
in traditional Chinese medicine for over 2,000 years as a diuretic,
anti-inflammatory, and antirheumatic agent.

The root's primary bioactive constituents are **bisbenzylisoquinoline
alkaloids**, principally tetrandrine (C₃₈H₄₂N₂O₆) and fangchinoline.
Tetrandrine has been extensively studied for its calcium channel blocking,
anti-inflammatory, antifibrotic, and potential anticancer properties,
making it one of the most pharmacologically characterized traditional
Chinese medicine compounds.

## Taxonomy [[trichome-development-and-morphology]]

- **Kingdom:** Plantae → **Order:** Ranunculales
- **Family:** Menispermaceae → **Genus:** *Stephania* Lour.
- **Species:** *S. tetrandra* S. Moore (published 1875)

The species is distinct from *Stephania cepharantha* Hayata (Japanese fang
ji), which produces cepharanthine [[permaculture-integrate-rather-than-segregate]] tetrandrine. This distinction
has clinical importance due to a 1990s adulteration incident (see below).

**Growth habit:** Perennial deciduous vine, climbing 2–5 meters on shrubs
and trees in forest understory.

**Root:** The medicinal organ — a large tuberous root reaching 30 cm length
and 10 cm diameter. Cross-section shows a distinctive pale yellow-white
[[xylem]] ring pattern.

**Leaves:** Peltate (petiole attached to blade interior), broadly ovate to
reniform, 4–10 × 4–9 cm, with 5–9 palmate veins radiating from the point
of attachment. This peltate morphology is diagnostic for *Stephania*.

**[[fukuoka-road-flowers-and-beauty]] fruit:** Dioecious. Male flowers in umbellate cymes; female
flowers in clustered heads. Fruit is a red globular drupe, ~6 mm diameter.

## [[seasonal-distribution-and-habitat-of-wild-mushrooms]]

*S. tetrandra* is native to central and southern China (Hubei, Anhui,
Zhejiang, Jiangxi, Fujian, Guangdong) and Taiwan.

**Habitat:** Forest margins, shaded ravines, and stream banks at 200–1,000 m
[[slope-aspect-and-elevation-in-design]] subtropical broadleaf forests. Prefers rich, well-drained
soils with high organic content and partial shade, often climbing on
supporting vegetation. Annual precipitation range: 1,000–1,800 mm.

Wild populations are declining due to overcollection, though commercial
cultivation is established in Hubei and Zhejiang provinces.

## Phytochemistry and Pharmacology

The root contains 1–3% total alkaloids, dominated by bisbenzylisoquinolines:

**Tetrandrine** (C₃₈H₄₂N₂O₆, 0.8–1.5% dry weight) is the major bioactive
compound. It is a non-selective voltage-gated **calcium channel blocker**
(inhibiting L-type and T-type channels), which underlies vasodilatory,
antiarrhythmic, and anti-hypertensive effects.

**Fangchinoline** (C₃₇H₄₀N₂O₆, 0.2–0.5%) is a demethylated tetrandrine
analogue with similar but weaker biological activity. Minor alkaloids include
cyclanoline and stephanthrine in trace amounts.

Key pharmacological actions of tetrandrine:
- **Anti-inflammatory:** Inhibits NF-κB, TNF-α, and COX-2 expression;
  suppresses T-cell proliferation and Th17 differentiation
- **Antifibrotic:** Suppresses hepatic stellate cells and TGF-β/Smad
