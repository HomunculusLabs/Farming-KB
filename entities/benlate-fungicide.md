## Description

Benlate is the brand name for benomyl, a systemic benzimidazole fungicide used in mycological laboratories as a selective agent in antibiotic agar formulations. In [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]], it is included in specialized agar recipes to suppress unwanted mold growth while allowing target mushroom mycelium to colonize the medium. Its ability to selectively inhibit filamentous fungi without harming basidiomycete mycelium makes it valuable for culture purification protocols.

## Classification

- **Category**: Fungicide / Laboratory reagent
- **Active ingredient**: Benomyl (methyl 1-(butylcarbamoyl)-2-benzimidazolecarbamate)
- **Form**: 50% wettable powder (commercially available formulation)
- **Chemical class**: Benzimidazole
- **Mechanism of action**: Inhibits microtubule assembly during fungal cell division by binding to beta-tubulin

## Key Facts

- Used at 0.06 gram per 1,000 ml in the antibiotic agar formula recommended by Dr. Bob Blanchette from the University of Minnesota
- Combined with malt extract, agar, yeast extract, [[streptomycin-sulfate]], and lactic acid in the complete formula
- Added to the base medium before autoclaving (unlike streptomycin, which is heat-sensitive and must be added post-autoclaving)
- Effective against a wide range of filamentous fungi and molds including *Aspergillus*, *Trichoderma*, and *Penicillium* species
- Works by inhibiting microtubule assembly during fungal cell division
- Particularly useful in the "[[antibiotic-sandwich]]" technique for rescuing contaminated cultures
- The most common mold contaminants in grain culture appear as light green, dark green, or mustard yellow colonies

## Application in Antibiotic Agar

The complete antibiotic agar formula includes Benlate as a mold suppressant alongside streptomycin sulfate (antibacterial) and lactic acid (pH adjustment). This combination creates a selective medium that favors mushroom mycelium over both bacterial and fungal contaminants, giving cultivators a window to purify valuable isolates.

## Mold Contamination Context

Molds are among the most aggressive competitors in mushroom cultivation. They can be green, black, yellow, or brown circular colonies that keep popping up all over culture plates. *Aspergillus* contamination appears as green and powdery circular colonies. The common bread mold (*Rhizopus* spp.) looks like white or clear fur with little black pinheads on the upper surface where spores discharge. Benlate in the agar formula helps suppress these competitors during critical culture purification steps.

## Relevance to Cultivation

Mold contamination is one of the most persistent challenges in mushroom tissue culturing and [[spawn-production]]. Benlate-supplemented agar provides cultivators with a chemical tool to suppress these competitors during critical culture purification steps. The antibiotic agar allows screening cultures through one or two transfers before returning them to standard antibiotic-free media for normal growth and expansion.

## Safety and Regulatory Notes

- Benomyl has been withdrawn from many commercial agricultural markets due to health concerns (including potential teratogenic effects) but remains available as a laboratory reagent
- Handle with appropriate PPE (gloves, mask, eye protection) when weighing powder
- Dispose of used agar plates containing Benlate according to local hazardous waste regulations
- Do not use in food production or composting applications; strictly for laboratory culture purification
