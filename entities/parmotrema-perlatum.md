Elevation range extends from sea level to approximately 1500 m in montane habitats.

## Chemistry and Bioactivity

**Parmotrema perlatum** produces a characteristic medullary chemistry including atranorin in the cortex and salazinic acid as the primary medullary compound.

Other [[antifungal-secondary-metabolites-coprophilous-fungi]] detected include consalazinic acid and protocetraric acid in some populations.

Spot tests yield K+ yellow turning red (atranorin in cortex) and KC+ red (salazinic acid in medulla).

C+ red and PD+ orange-red reactions further confirm the salazinic acid chemotype.

The salazinic acid chemotype distinguishes *P. perlatum* from morphologically similar species like *P. chinense*.

Extracts of *P. perlatum* have demonstrated moderate antioxidant and antimicrobial activities in vitro.

Antibacterial effects have been observed against both Gram-positive and Gram-negative pathogens in laboratory studies.

Anti-inflammatory properties have been documented in cell culture assays using ethyl acetate extracts.

## Culinary and Ethnobotanical Use

**Parmotrema perlatum** is one of the most commercially important edible lichens, sold extensively in Indian spice markets.

In Indian cuisine it is a key ingredient in *garam masala* blends, *biryani* spices, and certain *chettinad* curry preparations.

It contributes a distinctive earthy, umami flavor and acts as a thickening agent in slow-cooked dishes.

The lichen is typically sun-dried, cleaned of debris, and lightly toasted before use.

Commercial trade volume is significant, with dried material sourced from forests in central and southern India.

Beyond culinary use, traditional medicine systems have employed *P. perlatum* for treating digestive ailments.
