## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Rubus occidentalis** L. was described by Linnaeus in 1753. It belongs
to the subgenus *Idaeobatus* (raspberries), within the genus *Rubus* of
the family Rosaceae.

The plant grows as a biennial cane-producing shrub with a perennial
root system. First-year canes (primocanes) are greenish-purple and
pubescent, producing leaves but no flowers.

Second-year canes (floricanes) bear fruit and die after harvest.
Canes typically reach 0.9–1.8 m in height with a distinctive
bluish-white bloom on the stems.

Leaves are pinnately compound with 3–5 leaflets. Leaflets are ovate,
5–10 cm long, with serrate margins and whitish pubescence on the
abaxial surface.

Flowers are white, approximately 1.5 cm in diameter, with five petals.
They are borne in clusters of 2–5 at the tips of floricanes.

Fruit is an aggregate of drupelets, 1–1.5 cm in diameter, dark purple
to black at maturity. Unlike blackberries, the fruit separates from
the receptacle at harvest, leaving a hollow center.
