# Psilocybe argentipes

**Type:** Species (Fungus)
**Category:** Basidiomycota, Agaricales, Strophariaceae, Psilocybe
**Also known as:** None widely used

## Overview
Psilocybe argentipes is a moderately to highly active [[psilocybin-compound]]
mushroom known only from Japan. The species name ("silver-footed")
refers to the silvery patches of fibrils adorning the stem. It grows
gregariously on soil rich in woody debris near Cryptomeria japonica,
Quercus glauca, and Pinus taeda. Active according to Koike et al.
(1981), it is likely comparable in potency to Psilocybe cyanescens.
The species remains poorly known outside of Japanese mycology.

## Key Details
- **Cap:** 2.5-5 (6) cm broad. Conic to conic-campanulate, expanding
  to convex to plane, variably with sharp umbo. Chestnut brown when
  wet, hygrophanous, fading from disc to golden brown, honey yellow
  or mustard brown. Surface smooth, not viscid when moist. Bruises
  bluish. Margin incurved when young, often wavy, with partial veil
  remnants.
- **Gills:** Adnate or adnexed, soon seceding. Grayish orange at
  first, eventually purple-brown with whitish edges.
- **Stem:** 60-80 mm long by 2-4 mm thick. Equal, enlarging towards
  base with radiating whitish rhizomorphs. Silky white at first,
  then yellow toned, soon brownish to reddish brown. Adorned with
  white fibrillose veil remnants in lower two-thirds. Cortinate
  partial veil, sometimes leaving fragile annular zone.
- **Spores:** Dark violet brown in deposit, subellipsoid, 6.5-7.5 x
  9.5 x 1.44 um. Basidia 4-spored. Pleurocystidia absent.
  Cheilocystidia 13-25 x 5-8 um.
- **Habitat:** Gregarious to clustered on soil rich in woody debris,
  along trails, near Cryptomeria japonica, Quercus glauca, or Pinus
  taeda. Known only from Japan.
- **Chemistry:** Moderately to highly active (Koike et al. 1981).
  Likely comparable in potency to Psilocybe cyanescens.

## Distribution and Ecology
Psilocybe argentipes is currently known only from Japan, where it
grows on forest soils rich in woody debris along trails. Its
association with Cryptomeria japonica (Japanese cedar), Quercus glauca
(blue oak), and Pinus taeda (loblolly pine) places it in mixed
conifer-hardwood forests typical of Japan's mountainous regions. The
species fruits gregariously to in clusters, suggesting competitive
advantage in colonizing decomposing wood substrates. Its restriction
to Japan may reflect genuine endemism or simply insufficient
mycofloral surveys in similar habitats across East Asia. The
rhizomorphs radiating from the stem base indicate a well-developed
subterranean mycelial network for nutrient acquisition.

## Relevance
Psilocybe argentipes is one of the few psilocybin mushrooms endemic
to Japan, representing the east Asian distribution of the genus. Its
moderate to high potency makes it significant for understanding the
geographic distribution of psilocybin content in fungi. The species
may be more widely distributed than currently known, as Japanese
mycoflora remains incompletely documented. Its association with
Cryptomeria (Japanese cedar) and Quercus (oak) forest soils
parallels the habitat preferences of other wood-loving Psilocybe
species in North America and Europe. Its discovery could indicate
the presence of additional undescribed psychoactive fungi in East
Asian forests.
