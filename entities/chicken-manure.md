# Chicken Manure

## Description

Chicken manure is a potent organic fertilizer derived from poultry waste. When sold in sacks for garden use, it has been somewhat composted but is not labeled as compost. It is significantly richer in nutrients than steer manure and is considered one of the best animal manure fertilizers for vegetable gardening. Its high nitrogen content makes it particularly valuable for heavy-feeding crops.

## Classification

- **Category**: Organic fertilizer / soil amendment
- **Type**: Animal manure (poultry)
- **Form**: Sold bagged, partially composted
- **NPK value**: Approximately 4-3-2 (potent)
- **Availability**: Garden centers, farm supply stores

## Key Facts

- Far more nutrient-dense than steer manure for fertilizing vegetable gardens.
- Typical commercial product NPK of 4-3-2 — classified as potent by Solomon.
- Available as chicken manure (somewhat composted) or chicken manure compost.
- Must be used carefully — overapplication can burn plants or unbalance soil chemistry.
- Was Solomon's primary commercial fertilizer before he switched exclusively to COF.
- Contains significant nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium — the three primary macronutrients.

## Application Rates (from Solomon)

For medium-demand vegetables:
- 1/4 inch (3 mm) layer composted chicken manure per 1,000 sq ft

For high-demand vegetables:
- 1/4 inch (6 mm) layer composted chicken manure per 1,000 sq ft

These amounts are intentionally conservative. Take care not to overuse due to potency.

## Relevance to Cultivation

Chicken manure is a key tool in the gradient soil fertility system described by Solomon. It occupies the middle tier between weak steer manure and concentrated COF. For gardeners who cannot make COF, chicken manure provides the nutrient density needed for medium- and high-demand vegetables. In self-sufficiency contexts, poultry can be raised on-site, creating a closed-loop fertility system.

## Comparison with Other Amendments

| Amendment | NPK | Cost | Best Use |
|-----------|-----|------|----------|
| Steer manure | ~0.5-0.5-0.5 | Low | Soil structure, microbes |
| Chicken manure | ~4-3-2 | Moderate | Nutrient boost |
| COF | ~5-5-5+ | Higher | Complete nutrition |

## Cautions

- Overuse can burn plants due to high nitrogen.
- May introduce excess phosphorus if used heavily over many years.
- Must be composted or aged before direct application.

## Nutrient Cycling
Organic amendments play a vital role in closing nutrient loops
on farms and in gardens. Manure and compost return nutrients
to the soil that were removed through harvest. The slow release
of nutrients from organic sources provides sustained feeding
for crops throughout the growing season.

## Application Methods
Proper application ensures maximum benefit and minimizes
nutrient loss. Incorporate amendments into the soil surface
or apply as side-dressing during the growing season. Avoid
applying fresh manure directly to edible crops. Composting
or aging manure before application reduces pathogen risks.

## Safety and Best Practices
Always wear gloves when handling animal manures. Age or
compost manure for at least six months before garden use.
Keep amendments covered during storage to prevent nutrient
runoff. Follow local regulations regarding manure application
rates and timing near waterways.
