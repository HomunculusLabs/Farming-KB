# Gynostemma pentaphyllum

## Overview

Gynostemma pentaphyllum, commonly known as jiaogulan, sweet tea vine, or "immortality herb" (Chinese: 绞股蓝), is a climbing vine in the family Cucurbitaceae. Native to southern China and Southeast Asia, it has been used for centuries in traditional Chinese medicine and is now recognized globally as an adaptogenic herb.

The species gained widespread attention in the 1970s when Japanese researchers discovered that it contains dammarane-type saponins called gypenosides, which are structurally identical to the ginsenosides found in *Panax ginseng* (ginseng). Some gypenosides have demonstrated even broader pharmacological activity than their ginseng counterparts.

The genus name *Gynostemma* derives from Greek *gyne* (woman) and *stemma* (garland), referring to the flower structure. The species epithet *pentaphyllum* means "five-leaved," though the plant typically produces 3–9 leaflets per leaf.

## Morphology

### Growth Habit

A fast-growing, dioecious climbing vine reaching 2–8 m in length. In cultivation it is typically maintained as a low groundcover or managed on trellises. Growth is perennial in frost-free climates and annual in temperate zones.

The plant produces slender, branching stems that root at the nodes when in contact with moist soil, enabling rapid vegetative spread. This makes it both an effective groundcover and potentially invasive in warm, moist environments.

### Leaves

Palmately compound with typically 5 leaflets (range 3–9), each leaflet 3–8 cm long, ovate with serrate margins and a slightly hairy surface. The leaves are bright green and have a pleasant, slightly sweet taste when fresh.

The leaf morphology can vary considerably based on growing conditions and genetics, with some populations producing predominantly 3-leaflet leaves and others 7-leaflet forms.

### Flowers and Fruit

Small, greenish-white flowers, approximately 4 mm in diameter, produced in axillary racemes. Male and female flowers are borne on separate plants (dioecious).

Fruit is a small, globular berry, 5–8 mm in diameter, green ripening to black. Each fruit contains 1–3 seeds. Fruiting requires both male and female plants.

## Distribution and Habitat

Native to the mountainous regions of southern China, northern Vietnam, Korea, and Japan. It grows wild in forests, thickets, and along stream banks at elevations of 300–3,200 m.

The species is most abundant in the karst limestone regions of Guizhou, Guangxi, and Yunnan provinces in China, where it has been wild-harvested and cultivated for centuries.

Adaptable to a wide range of conditions, from tropical lowlands to cool mountain forests. Prefers partial shade, rich loamy soil, and consistent moisture but tolerates full sun and moderate drought once established.

## Phytochemistry

### Gypenosides

Over 170 dammarane-type saponins (gypenosides) have been identified, making *G. pentaphyllum* one of the richest botanical sources of these compounds. At least four gypenosides are identical to ginseng ginsenosides (Rb1, Rb3, Rd, and F2).

Gypenoside XLIX, gypenoside LII, and gypenoside LXXVII are unique to *G. pentaphyllum* and have shown notable anti-cancer and cardioprotective activity in animal studies.

### Flavonoids and Polysaccharides

Contains quercetin, kaempferol, and rutin as major flavonoids, along with water-soluble polysaccharides with immunomodulatory properties.

### Amino Acids and Minerals

Rich in glutamine, arginine, and gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA). Mineral content includes significant levels of zinc, selenium, and germanium.

## Pharmacology and Medicinal Uses

### Adaptogenic Activity

Like ginseng, jiaogulan functions as an adaptogen, helping the body resist physiological and psychological stress. Studies have demonstrated normalization of cortisol levels, improved endurance, and reduced fatigue in both animal and human trials.

### Cardiovascular Effects

Clinical and preclinical evidence supports benefits for cardiovascular health, including reduction of blood pressure, lowering of LDL cholesterol, and improvement in lipid profiles. Gypenosides inhibit platelet aggregation and improve endothelial function.

### Antidiabetic Properties

Animal studies have shown that jiaogulan extracts improve insulin sensitivity, reduce blood glucose levels, and protect pancreatic beta cells. Human clinical trials have reported improved glycemic control in type 2 diabetes patients.

### Antioxidant and Anti-inflammatory

Gypenosides and flavonoids from the plant demonstrate significant antioxidant activity, reducing lipid peroxidation and scavenging free radicals. Anti-inflammatory effects are mediated through inhibition of NF-κB signaling pathways.

**Safety:** Generally well-tolerated at therapeutic doses. Mild gastrointestinal upset and rare cases of nausea have been reported. Not recommended during pregnancy due to limited safety data.

## Permaculture and Cultivation

Jiaogulan is an excellent groundcover for food forests and permaculture gardens. Its rapid growth, shade tolerance, and ability to root at nodes make it effective for erosion control and weed suppression.

Cultivation is straightforward from seed (stratify at 4°C for 2 weeks) or stem cuttings. Prefers rich, well-drained soil with pH 6.0–7.0 and partial shade. Harvesting of leaves can begin 3–4 months after planting.

In temperate climates, the vine dies back in winter but regrows from the root crown in spring. In subtropical and tropical areas it can be evergreen and may require management to prevent excessive spread.
