## Stephanoma

## Description

Stephanoma is a genus of biotrophic contact mycoparasitic fungi in the [[ascomycota]]. The genus is notable for its specialized mycoparasitic lifestyle, in which it contacts host fungi through direct physical interaction rather than producing penetrating haustoria. Stephanoma species are among the best-studied biotrophic contact parasites, alongside Gonatobotrys and Nematogonum. The genus has teleomorph connections to the Hypocreales.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Eukaryota
- **[[kingdom-fungi]]
- **Division:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Sordariomycetes
- **Order:** Hypocreales
- **Family:** Uncertain (likely Ceratostomataceae or related family)
- **Genus:** Stephanoma

## Key Species

- **S. phaeosporum** — A biotrophic contact mycoparasite of Fusarium species and at least 14 other fungi (Butler and McCain 1968; Rakvidhyasastra and Butler 1973; Hoch 1978; Hawksworth 1981a). The most thoroughly studied species in the genus.
- **S. tetracoccum** — Parasitic on Geoglossaceae (earth tongue fungi). Listed as a characteristic fungicolous species on discomycete sporocarps.
- **S. strigosum** — The anamorph of Hypomyces stephanomatis, which parasitizes Helvella species.

## Morphological Characteristics

- **Conidiophores:** Produce distinctive darkly pigmented, echinulate (spiny) conidia characteristic of the genus
- **Conidia:** Large, dark, muriform (multicelled) conidia with ornate wall ornamentation
- **Contact cells:** Specialized hyphal cells that make direct contact with host hyphae without penetration
- **Growth habit:** Colonies grow over the surface of host [[mycelium]], forming a dense network of contact cells

## Mycoparasitic Behavior

Stephanoma employs a biotrophic contact strategy:

1. **Contact phase:** Hyphae grow along and make direct contact with host hyphae through specialized contact cells
2. **Nutrient acquisition:** Nutrients are extracted from the host through the contact interface without physical penetration of the host cell wall
3. **Host range:** S. phaeosporum can parasitize Fusarium species and at least 14 other fungal genera, making it relatively polyphagous for a biotrophic contact parasite
4. **Cultural requirements:** Can be grown axenically on media supplemented with fungal extract, but dependence on mycotrophein is uncertain

## Ecological and Nutritional Requirements

- **Host association:** Primarily found on Fusarium species in nature; also on other soil fungi
- **Axenic culture:** Can be maintained on artificial media with fungal extract supplements
- **Mycotrophein:** The requirement for this growth factor (a mixture of tetraethyleneglycol and pentaethyleneglycol mono(nonylphenyl) ethers found in fungal culture extracts) is uncertain for Stephanoma, unlike some other biotrophic contact parasites

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Biocontrol potential:** S. phaeosporum's ability to parasitize Fusarium species—a major group of plant pathogens—makes it a candidate for biological control research
- **Mycoparasite biology:** Serves as a model system for understanding biotrophic contact mycoparasitism, a distinct strategy from necrotrophic mycoparasitism
- **Nutritional studies:** The genus has been important in characterizing mycotrophein and other fungal growth factors (Gain and Barnett 1970; Calderone and Barnett 1972)
- **Taxonomic connections:** The teleomorph connections to Hypomyces link the genus to the broader Hypocreales mycoparasite complex

## Key References

## Notes

Stephanoma is one of several genera of biotrophic contact mycoparasites that share similar nutritional requirements, including Gonatobotrys, Nematogonum, and Hansfordia. The biotrophic contact strategy represents an intermediate level of parasitic specialization between purely necrotrophic and fully haustorial biotrophic parasites. The genus illustrates the diversity of mycoparasitic strategies within the Hypocreales.
