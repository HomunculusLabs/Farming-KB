# Negev Desert

## Description

The Negev Desert is a large **arid region** in southern Israel covering
approximately 13,000 km² (5,000 sq mi), representing about 60% of Israel's
land area. It is cited by Mollison as an important example of **dew
condensation techniques** used for plant cultivation in extreme arid
environments. The Negev demonstrates how simple physical structures —
specifically loose piles of stones — can harvest atmospheric moisture to
sustain plant growth in areas with minimal rainfall, providing a model for
arid-land [[stone-mulch]] piles.
- The Negev's approach to desert agriculture represents an ancient and
  effective strategy for water harvesting without mechanical irrigation.
- Dew in deserts accumulates most effectively 3-100 cm above ground level,
  with highest deposition over dry ground (which cools faster than wet soil).

## Stone Mulch Technique

The condensation trap method used in the Negev works through several
mechanisms:

1. **Radiative cooling**: Stone surfaces lose heat rapidly on clear nights,
   cooling below the dew point of passing air.
2. **Night air movement**: Slight wind speeds (1-5 km/h) assist moisture
   deposition; both still air and strong winds reduce dewfall effectiveness.
3. **Capillary storage**: Condensed moisture drips from stone surfaces to the
  soil beneath the mound, where it is retained by the stone mulch layer.
4. **Root zone delivery**: Trees planted at the center of each mound receive
   a steady drip of condensed moisture directly to their root zone.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

The Negev Desert exemplifies several key [[appropriate-technology]]**: The simplicity of stone piles as condensation
  traps aligns with permaculture's preference for low-energy, locally-
  available solutions.
- **Pattern understanding**: The Negev demonstrates that **scattered
  individual shrubs** (1-2 m height) are more efficient dew collectors than
  continuous plant stands, explaining the natural spacing pattern of desert
  vegetation where perhaps 40% more dew is trapped on scattered shrubs.
- **Arid-land design**: Permaculture designers working in arid zones can
  adapt the Negev's stone mulch technique as a water strategy, especially
  during plant establishment phases.
- **Scale applicability**: The technique scales from individual tree
  establishment to orchard-level application.

Mollison presents the Negev example alongside the **Lanzarote condensation
traps** in the Canary Islands as two different approaches to harvesting
atmospheric moisture in extremely dry climates.
