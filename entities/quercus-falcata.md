The species is valued for its timber quality, wildlife value, and role as a dominant component of upland hardwood forests across the southeastern Piedmont and Coastal Plain. Southern red oak wood is marketed interchangeably with other red oaks for flooring, furniture, and construction lumber.

Southern red oak typically reaches 20–28 meters in height with a trunk diameter of 60–100 cm. The crown is broad and rounded with a straight, clear bole on forest-grown trees, while open-grown specimens develop a shorter trunk with wide-spreading branches.

On the best sites, champion trees can exceed 35 meters in height with trunk diameters over 120 cm. [[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]], pendant and 7–12 cm long. Female flowers are borne singly or in pairs on short peduncles in the axils of new leaves. Wind pollination occurs during a 1–2 week period in March or April.

The upper [[dighton-fungal-decomposition-leaf-litter-dynamics]] decomposes slowly and contributes to the development of mor humus on forest floors.

Bark on mature trees is dark brown to nearly black, divided into broad, scaly ridges separated by deep fissures. The bark surface is rough and platy, with outer scales that are easily dislodged. Inner bark is reddish, typical of the red oak group.

Acorns are 10–16 mm long, broadly ovoid, and enclosed about one-third within a shallow, reddish-brown cap. The cap scales are appressed and pubescent. Acorns mature in two [[dmt-maoi-plants-united-states]]. It is less common in the Mississippi Alluvial Valley, where cherrybark oak replaces it on bottomland sites.

Southern red oak is primarily an upland species, occurring on dry to mesic slopes, ridges, and well-drained terraces. It is also found on sandy soils of the coastal plain and on rocky slopes in the Ozark and Ouachita Highlands.

The root system develops a deep taproot on well-drained upland sites, providing excellent [[cannabis-leaf-morphology-and-phyllotaxy]].

## Ecology and Applications

Southern red oak acorns are an important food source for wildlife including white-tailed deer, wild turkeys, squirrels, raccoons, and numerous bird species. Acorns are relatively high in tannins compared to white oaks but are consumed heavily during years of abundant production.

Mast production begins at approximately 25–30 years of age, with heavy crops occurring at irregular 3–5 year intervals. Individual trees produce 1,000–5,000 acorns during peak mast years, supporting wildlife populations through fall and winter.

Acorn predation by weevils (Curculio spp.) can reduce viable seed significantly, with infestation rates exceeding 50% in some years. The species compensates for predation losses through relatively consistent annual production across the population.
