# Juglans nigra

**Juglans nigra**, the black walnut, is a large deciduous tree in
the family Juglandaceae native to [[polypore-fungi-eastern-north-america]] America. It is one
of the most valuable timber trees and a significant species in
permaculture and agroforestry systems.

The tree is known for producing premium dark wood prized in
furniture-making, and for the allelopathic compound juglone
(5-hydroxy-1,4-naphthoquinone), which inhibits growth of many
competing plant species. It also produces edible nuts with a
distinctive rich flavor commanding premium prices.

## Taxonomy

- **Division:** Magnoliophyta → **Class:** Magnoliopsida
- **Order:** Fagales → **Family:** Juglandaceae
- **Genus:** Juglans → **Species:** J. nigra
- **Authority:** L. (1753)
- **Synonyms:** *Juglans oblonga* (Poir.) Dode; *Wallia nigra*
  (L.) Alef.

The genus *Juglans* comprises approximately 21 species across
temperate and subtropical regions of the Northern Hemisphere.

## Morphology

Black walnut typically reaches 20-30 m in height with trunk diameter
of 60-90 cm. Exceptional specimens exceed 40 m and 150 cm diameter.

The crown is open and rounded with a straight trunk free of branches
for 6-10 m. Bark is dark grey-brown to black, deeply fissured into
diamond-shaped ridges on mature trees.

Leaves are pinnately compound, 30-60 cm long, with 15-23 lanceolate
leaflets 6-12 cm long. Fruit is a round drupe 3-6 cm in diameter
with a thick green husk enclosing a hard ridged shell.

## Distribution and Habitat

Native to eastern [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]], from southern Ontario and Minnesota
south to northern Florida and Texas. Introduced and naturalized in
Europe and parts of Asia.

Grows best on deep, fertile, well-drained alluvial soils along
floodplains and stream terraces. Optimal growth at USDA zones 4-9
with 75-125 cm mean annual precipitation. Shade-intolerant in youth,
developing moderate shade tolerance with age.

## Juglone and Allelopathy

All plant parts contain juglone, released into soil primarily through
root exudation. Sensitive species include tomatoes, potatoes, eggplant,
peppers, lilacs, apples, and pines. Symptoms include wilting,
yellowing, and stunted growth.

Tolerant species include black raspberry, elderberry, [[pawpaw]],
persimmon, redbud, and most grasses. Juglone concentration decreases
with distance from the tree and with soil depth. The allelopathic
zone extends approximately to the drip line.

## Permaculture and Agroforestry Applications

- **Timber:** Premium hardwood for furniture, gunstocks, veneer;
  harvestable in 30-50 years
- **Food:** Nut production begins at 8-10 years, full bearing at
  20-30 years; yields 50-100 kg per mature tree
