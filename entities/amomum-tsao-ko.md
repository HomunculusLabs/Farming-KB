## Distribution and Habitat

The species is native to southern China (Yunnan, Guizhou, Guangxi, and southern Sichuan provinces) and northern Vietnam.

It grows naturally in subtropical broadleaf forests and forest margins at elevations of 1,100–2,300 meters.

The plant prefers cool, moist, shaded environments with well-drained, humus-rich forest soils.

Annual rainfall in its native range typically exceeds 1,000 mm, with a pronounced dry season.

The species is cold-tolerant compared to many other Zingiberaceae, surviving light frost at higher elevations.

Wild populations have been reduced by over-collection and habitat loss, though commercial [[short-day-and-long-day-plant-classification]]-simmered preparations where their robust flavor develops slowly.

Tsao-ko is a key ingredient in **master stock** (lǔshuǐ), the concentrated braising liquid used repeatedly in Chinese restaurant kitchens.

It is an essential component of **five-spice powder**, contributing a smoky, camphoraceous note that balances the sweetness of star anise and fennel.

In Vietnamese cuisine, tsao-ko (known as **thảo quả**) is used in phở broth and beef stew (bò kho).
