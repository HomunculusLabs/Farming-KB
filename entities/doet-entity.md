# DOET

DOET (2,5-Dimethoxy-4-ethylamphetamine) is a psychedelic amphetamine and one of Shulgin's "Classic Ladies."

It carries the additional name HECATE, placing it among the select group of compounds that Shulgin honored with mythological names.

DOET is documented as entry #66 in PiHKAL.

## Chemical Identity

Full chemical name: 2,5-Dimethoxy-4-ethylamphetamine.

Molecular formula: C13H22ClNO2 (as hydrochloride).

DOET was originally coded as DOE, following the naming convention where DO represents the removal of the oxygen from the DOM name and the final letter indicates the 4-position substituent.

However, DOE was already an established abbreviation for desoxyephedrine (methamphetamine), so the T was added to create DOET, reflecting the three-carbon ethyl group.

The hydrochloride salt melts at 190-191 degrees Celsius, with recrystallization raising this to 194-195 degrees Celsius.

## Dosage and Duration

Effective oral dosage: 2 - 6 mg.

Duration of effects: 14 - 20 hours.

The effective dose range is notably broad, with considerable individual variation in sensitivity.

At lower doses (1-4 mg), effects are subtle and primarily mood-enhancing, with minimal psychedelic character.

At higher doses (6-7 mg), full psychedelic effects emerge including visual enhancement, rich eyes-closed imagery, and sensory amplification.

## Qualitative Effects

At 1 mg, effects are described as a gentle, relaxing state with no overt psychedelic properties.

One subject reported complete relief from menstrual cramps at this dose, though this effect was not reproduced in all trials.

At 2.5 mg, users report visual movement, reality distortion, and difficulty finding a centering point of reference.

Body effects including leg twitching and crawling sensations may occur at this dose level.

At 4 mg, the compound functions more as a "mood energizer" with a plus-two effect, described as lacking the seriousness of lsd or [[dob-entity-pihkal]]|DOB: the 4-bromo analogue
