## Mark Shepard

## Overview
Mark Shepard is an American farmer, author, and pioneering figure in the restoration agriculture movement. Based in southwestern Wisconsin, he founded New Forest Farm in 1994 on 106 acres of degraded former corn/soybean cropland, which he converted into one of the most documented large-scale perennial polyculture systems in [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-permaculture-design-principles]] with commercial-scale farming, demonstrating that perennial polycultures can be economically viable at scale.

## Biographical Details
- **Born:** 1965, United States
- **Location:** Viola, Wisconsin (New Forest Farm, Richland County)
- **Education:** Studied permaculture with Bill Mollison; agricultural background in conventional farming before conversion
- **Occupation:** Farmer, consultant, author, founder of Restoration Agriculture Development (RAD)
- **Organization:** Restoration Agriculture Development LLC — consulting firm designing and installing large-scale perennial crop systems
- **Farm founded:** New Forest Farm, 1994, on 106 acres
- **Website:** restorationag.com (Restoration Agriculture Development)

## Key Contributions and Philosophy

### Restoration Agriculture Framework
- Core thesis: agricultural systems should mimic the structure and function of pre-settlement native ecosystems (specifically midwestern oak savanna)
- Replaces annual grain monocultures with perennial polycultures that produce human food calories, animal fodder, and [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]] before European settlement, supporting both wildlife and indigenous peoples through mast crops (acorns, chestnuts, hickory nuts)

### New Forest Farm Design
- **Total acreage:** 106 acres (43 hectares); ~90 acres in active perennial polyculture production
- **Overstory species:** Hybrid chestnut (*Castanea* spp.), hickory, oak, walnut — spaced at 40–50 foot intervals
- **Midstory species:** Apple, pear, plum, cherry, pawpaw, persimmon — between overstory trees
- **Shrub layer:** Hazelnut, elderberry, aronia, currant, gooseberry, honeyberry
- **Groundcover:** Managed [[teaming-with-nutrients-natural-vs-synthetic-fertilizers]] or pesticides; grazing animals provide fertility cycling
- **Soil health:** [[comparison-rotational-grazing-vs-set-stocking]]: cattle, sheep, pigs, chickens, turkeys
- Pigs used for site preparation (rooting behavior clears ground for tree planting)
- Chickens follow cattle through paddocks — dung beetle larvae and fly larvae provide protein
- Grazing replaces mowing, fertilization, [[swale-construction-guide]], contour planting, and passive water harvesting
- Case studies from RAD consulting installations across North America

### The Restoration Agriculture Development Handbook (2021)
- Technical manual for designing and installing restoration agriculture systems
- Includes species selection matrices, planting plans, and establishment protocols
- Economic modeling and enterprise budgets for perennial crop systems
