## Cinnamomum cassia

## Overview

Cinnamomum cassia, known as Chinese cassia, Chinese cinnamon, or simply cassia, is an evergreen tree in the family Lauraceae. It is the source of the most widely consumed cinnamon product in the world — the thick, rough bark quills sold as "cinnamon" in most [[commercially-important-pleurotus-species-cultivation-guide]] cinnamon species:

- **Bark:** Cassia — thick (2–5 mm), hard, rough, single-layer quills; Ceylon — thin (0.5–1 mm), papery, multi-layered cigar-like quills.
- **Flavor:** Cassia — bold, pungent, strongly sweet with harsh heat; Ceylon — delicate, warm, complex with citrus notes.
- **Chemistry:** Cassia — 2–5% cinnamaldehyde, high coumarin (0.3–1.0%); Ceylon — 50–65% cinnamaldehyde, trace coumarin (<0.004%).
- **⚠️ Coumarin safety:** Cassia's high coumarin is a health concern. EFSA TDI: 0.1 mg/kg body weight — 1–2 teaspoons of cassia can exceed this.

## Morphology

### Vegetative Description

- **Growth habit:** Medium-sized evergreen tree, 10–15 m tall gartz-magic-mushrooms-species-south-america (Brazil, Ecuador), and Caribbean.
- **Elevation:** 100–500 m native; cultivated from sea level to 1,000 m.
- **Climate:** Requires warm, humid tropical to subtropical conditions. Optimal temperature 20–30°C. Tolerates brief light frost but not sustained freezing. Annual rainfall requirement: 1,250–2,500 mm, with a distinct dry season preferred for bark harvesting.
- **Soil:** Prefers deep, fertile, well-drained sandy loam to loam soils, slightly acidic (pH 5.5–6.5). Tolerates poor soils but bark quality suffers. Does not tolerate waterlogging.
- **Habitat:** Subtropical evergreen and deciduous broadleaf forests, forest margins, river valleys, and cultivated plantations.

## Phytochemistry

### Bark Chemistry

- **[[mushroom-cancer-clinical-trials-overview]].

- **Mechanism:** Cinnamaldehyde and procyanidins enhance insulin receptor kinase activity, increase GLUT4-mediated glucose uptake, inhibit hepatic gluconeogenesis (AMPK activation), and improve β-cell function.
- **Clinical evidence:** Meta-analysis of 18 RCTs (n=1,284) found cassia cinnamon (1–6 g/day) reduced fasting glucose (−11.2 mg/dL), HbA1c (−0.31%), and total cholesterol (−12.6 mg/dL).
- **[[microwave-sterilization-dose-response-microbial-kill-curves]]:** Optimal dose 2–4 g/day; benefits plateau above 6 g/day.

### Anti-inflammatory Activity

- **Mechanism:** Cinnamaldehyde inhibits NF-κB and MAPK pathways, suppresses COX-2 and iNOS, and reduces TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6.
- **In vivo evidence:** Reduces carrageenan-induced edema, adjuvant-induced arthritis, and LPS-induced inflammation at 50–200 mg/kg.

### Antimicrobial Activity

- **Antibacterial:** Strongest against Gram-positive organisms: S. aureus (MIC 125–500 µg/mL), S. mutans, B. cereus. Moderate against Gram-negative species.
- **Antifungal:** Active against C. albicans (MIC 250–500 µg/mL), A. flavus, dermatophytes. Cinnamaldehyde disrupts cell wall integrity.
- **Antiviral:** In vitro activity against influenza A, HIV-1, and dengue virus via entry and replication inhibition.

### Cardiovascular Effects

- **Reduces blood pressure** in hypertensive models (vasodilation via calcium channel blockade and NO pathway).
- **Improves lipid profile:** Reduces total cholesterol, LDL, and triglycerides in clinical trials.
- **Antiplatelet:** Inhibits platelet aggregation via thromboxane A2 synthesis suppression.
