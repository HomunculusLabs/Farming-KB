# Psilocybe moravica

**Psilocybe moravica** is a bluing [[guzman-allen-gartz-psilocybe-section-relationships-trans-pacific-disjunctions]] psilocybe-sect.-psilocybe, which contains the majority of known bluing, psilocybin-containing species.
Molecular phylogenetic analyses place *P. moravica* within a European clade that also includes [[fukuoka-european-distribution-system-critique]] and similar ecological preferences.
The circumscription of *P. moravica* was part of a broader effort to resolve the taxonomic complexity of the *P. cyanescens* complex in Europe, where many bluing Psilocybe species had been lumped under a few variable names. Borovička's integrative approach, combining morphological, ecological, and molecular data, revealed several distinct lineages previously unrecognized as separate species.
ITS rDNA sequence data support the recognition of *P. moravica* as a phylogenetically distinct lineage sister to *P. serbica*, from which it can be distinguished by a combination of microscopic characters and ecological preferences.

## Description

The basidiocarps are small to medium-sized. The pileus is convex to broadly convex, 10 to 30 mm in diameter, hygrophanous, and ochraceous to brownish when moist, becoming lighter as it dries.
The pileus surface is smooth to slightly viscid when wet, with a strongly striate margin at maturity. The lamellae are adnate to adnexed, moderately crowded, initially pale grayish, darkening to purplish brown with spore maturity.
The stipe is central, cylindrical, 30 to 60 mm long, 1 to 2 mm thick, whitish to pale brown, and bruises distinctly blue where damaged. The veil is ephemeral, sometimes leaving a barely perceptible annular zone.
Spore prints are dark purplish brown. The overall stature of *P. moravica* is similar to that of other European grassland Psilocybe species, with a relatively slender stipe and broadly expanding pileus that becomes strongly hygrophanous as it matures.
The blue bruising is most pronounced on the stipe base and on damaged areas of the pileus, and may develop slowly, becoming more intense over several minutes after handling.

### Microscopic Features
Basidiospores measure 9 to 12 µm long by 5.5 to 7 µm wide, ellipsoid in face view, with a distinct apical germ pore and moderately thickened walls. The spore surface is smooth to slightly roughened in mature specimens. Spore print color is dark purplish brown.
Cheilocystidia are ventricose to lageniform, measuring 18 to 28 µm in total length, with a narrow, sometimes slightly capitate neck. They are abundant on the lamellar edges, forming a conspicuous sterile band.
Basidia are 4-spored, clavate, measuring 16 to 22 µm long. The pileipellis is a cutis composed of hyaline, filamentous, interwoven hyphae that are 2 to 4 µm wide, with occasional short, erect terminal elements.
Clamp connections are present at the septa of hyphae. The lamellar trama is regular, composed of parallel hyphae. These microscopic features collectively distinguish *P. moravica* from morphologically similar European taxa.

## Distribution and Habitat

### Geographic Range
*Psilocybe moravica* is known primarily from Moravia in the eastern Czech Republic, with additional suspected occurrences in surrounding Central European countries including Slovakia, Austria, and southern Poland.
The type locality is in the Moravian lowlands, and subsequent collections have expanded the known range within the Czech Republic. Molecular confirmation of specimens from neighboring countries is still needed to determine the full extent of its distribution.

### Ecology
The species is associated with temperate grasslands, particularly floodplain meadows along river systems such as the Morava and Dyje. It grows gregariously or scattered in nutrient-rich soils, favoring rich loamy alluvial deposits.
These meadows are often periodically inundated, which contributes to the high organic content of the substrate. Fruiting occurs from September through November, typically triggered by the first sustained autumn rains when daytime temperatures range from 8 to 15°C.
The species has been collected at elevations from approximately 200 to 600 meters above sea level. Its association with floodplain habitats distinguishes it ecologically from several closely related species that prefer forest margins or higher elevations.
