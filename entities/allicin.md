## Allicin

Allicin (diallyl thiosulfinate) is an organosulfur compound responsible for the characteristic pungent aroma
and many of the bioactive properties of freshly crushed garlic (*[[cryptococcus-neoformans]]*), and parasites
  (*Giardia lamblia*, *Entamoeba histolytica*)
- Demonstrates synergistic antimicrobial effects with conventional antibiotics, partially reversing
  methicillin resistance in MRSA by disrupting efflux pump function

### Cardiovascular Effects
- Inhibits HMG-CoA reductase (the rate-limiting enzyme in cholesterol biosynthesis), contributing to
  LDL cholesterol reduction of 10–15% with regular garlic consumption (meta-analysis evidence)
- Suppresses hepatic cholesterol synthesis by 30–40% in animal models
- Exhibits anti-platelet activity by inhibiting cyclooxygenase and thromboxane synthesis, reducing
  platelet aggregation by 20–40%
- Promotes vasodilation through hydrogen sulfide (H₂S) signaling and nitric oxide (NO) pathway activation
- Reduces oxidative modification of LDL, a key step in atherogenesis

### Anticancer Properties
- Induces apoptosis in cancer cells through mitochondrial membrane depolarization, cytochrome c release,
  and caspase-3 activation
- Inhibits proliferation in colon, breast, lung, and prostate cancer cell lines at IC₅₀ values of
  10–50 µM
- Suppresses angiogenesis via downregulation of VEGF and MMP-9
- Enhances glutathione S-transferase activity, promoting detoxification of carcinogens

### Immunomodulation
- Stimulates macrophage activity, NK cell cytotoxicity, and T-lymphocyte proliferation
- Low concentrations stimulate pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β) while higher doses suppress
  excessive inflammation via NF-κB modulation

## Pharmacokinetics

Allicin itself is too reactive to survive intact through the digestive tract; oral bioavailability is
essentially zero. The pharmacological effects of garlic consumption are mediated primarily through its
degradation products and metabolites:

- **Diallyl disulfide (DADS):** Major stable metabolite; detectable in plasma, liver, and adipose tissue
  after oral garlic ingestion; contributes to cholesterol-lowering and antiplatelet effects
- **Diallyl sulfide (DAS):** Volatile metabolite excreted via lungs (responsible for garlic breath);
  also possesses antimutagenic activity
- **S-allyl cysteine (SAC):** Water-soluble, stable compound predominant in aged garlic extract;
  excellent oral bioavailability; primary active component in most clinical trials
- **Ajoene:** Formed from allicin degradation; potent antithrombotic agent that inhibits fibrinogen
  binding to platelet receptors

Peak plasma concentrations of DADS occur 1–3 hours after garlic consumption, with a half-life of
approximately 4–10 hours depending on the metabolite.
