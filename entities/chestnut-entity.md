# Chestnut

**Type:** Species (Plant)
**Category:** Nut-producing deciduous tree
**Also known as:** Castanea spp, sweet chestnut, Spanish chestnut,
marron

## Overview
Chestnuts are long-lived, deciduous, drought-resistant trees closely
related to oaks, thriving on well-drained, preferably light soils with
fair lime tolerance. The genus includes several major species valued
for both nut production and timber. Mature groves can yield up to 5,000
kg of nuts per hectare, and the European chestnut has been a staple
carbohydrate source across southern Europe for millennia. The trees are
also coppiced for timber production.

## Key Details

### Botanical Profile
- **Family:** Fagaceae (closely related to oaks)
- **Height:** Up to 35 m (C. sativa)
- **Habit:** Long-lived deciduous tree
- **Salinity tolerance:** Does not withstand salinity
- **Spacing:** 56-70 trees/ha recommended

### Key Species
- **C. sativa** (Spanish/sweet chestnut): Large spreading tree to 35
  m; prized timber in Europe; coppiced; yields up to 5,000 kg/ha of
  nuts; nuts tastier roasted, boiled, or preserved
- **C. crenata** (Japanese chestnut): Smaller tree; coppices well;
  excellent nut crop strains developed
- **C. mollissima** (Chinese chestnut): To 20 m; sweeter than European;
  resistant to Phytophthora root fungus
- **C. dentata** (American chestnut): Once a major North American
  deciduous tree; nearly destroyed by bark fungus in early 1900s;
  nuts said to be the sweetest of any species
- **C. alnifolia** (downy chinquapin): Small clumping tree from
  southeastern USA; small but good quality nuts

### Nutritional Composition
Fresh (per 100 g): Energy 194 cal, Carbohydrate 42.1 g, Protein 2.9 g,
Fat 1.5 g, Potassium 454 mg, Phosphorus 88 mg, Thiamin 0.22 mg
Dried (per 100 g): Energy 377 cal, Carbohydrate 78.6 g, Protein 6.7 g,
Fat 4.1 g, Potassium 875 mg, Phosphorus 162 mg

### Yields
- Year 10 at 56-70 trees/ha: 0.77 t/ha (minimal irrigation), 1.7
  t/ha (full irrigation)
- Mature C. sativa: Up to 5,000 kg/ha

### Uses
- **Food:** Eaten raw, roasted, boiled, dried, pickled; traditional
  European staple carbohydrate
- **Timber:** Young timber tough and durable; coppiced for sustainable
  harvest; old growth mainly useful as firewood
- **Ornamental:** Large spreading canopy provides excellent shade

### Cautions
- Phytophthora root fungus can kill trees (Chinese chestnut is
  resistant)
- American chestnut virtually wiped out by blight; seed available from
  surviving populations
