# Chestnut

**Type:** Species (Plant)
**Category:** Nut-producing deciduous tree
**Also known as:** Castanea spp, [[permplant-chestnuts-castanea-nut-crop-coppice]] strains developed
- **C. mollissima** (Chinese chestnut): To 20 m; sweeter than European;
  resistant to [[growing-gourmet-north-american-reishi-species]]
  deciduous tree; nearly destroyed by bark fungus in early 1900s;
  nuts said to be the sweetest of any species
- **C. alnifolia** (downy chinquapin): Small clumping tree from
  southeastern USA; small but good quality nuts

### Nutritional Composition
Fresh (per 100 g): Energy 194 cal, Carbohydrate 42.1 g, Protein 2.9 g,
Fat 1.5 g, Potassium 454 mg, Phosphorus 88 mg, Thiamin 0.22 mg
Dried (per 100 g): Energy 377 cal, Carbohydrate 78.6 g, Protein 6.7 g,
Fat 4.1 g, Potassium 875 mg, Phosphorus 162 mg

### Yields
- Year 10 at 56-70 trees/ha: 0.77 t/ha (minimal irrigation), 1.7
  t/ha (full irrigation)
- Mature C. sativa: Up to 5,000 kg/ha

### Uses
- **Food:** Eaten raw, roasted, boiled, dried, pickled; traditional
  European staple carbohydrate
- **Timber:** Young timber tough and durable; coppiced for sustainable
  harvest; [[comparison-nut-trees-vs-grain-farming-for-caloric-staple-production]] for temperate
[[macadamia-entity]]
- Oak
- Walnut
- Hazelnut
