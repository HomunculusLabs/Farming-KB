## Overview

Aeonium haworthii, commonly called "pinwheel desert rose" or "Haworth's aeonium," is a branched succulent shrub in the family Crassulaceae.

It is native to the Canary Islands, specifically Tenerife, where it grows on coastal cliffs and rocky hillsides.

The species is valued for its compact rosettes of blue-green leaves edged with pink margins in full sun.

It is one of the most widely cultivated Aeonium species, frequently used in Mediterranean-climate gardens worldwide.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Aeonium haworthii was described by George Claridge Druce, honoring the English botanist Adrian Hardy Haworth.

The genus Aeonium contains approximately 35–40 species, almost all endemic to the Macaronesian archipelagos.

Plants form freely branching shrubs 30–60 cm tall with many rosettes per stem.

Each rosette measures 5–8 cm in diameter with 30–60 tightly packed leaves.

Leaves are obovate to spatulate, 2–5 cm long, glaucous blue-green with pink to reddish margins under stress.

The leaves are slightly pubescent, with fine hairs visible along the margins and lower surface.

Flowering occurs in late spring to early summer, producing conical inflorescences 10–20 cm tall.

Flowers are pale yellow to greenish-white, 7–10 mm across, with 6–8 petals.

## Distribution and Habitat

Aeonium haworthii is endemic to the island of Tenerife in the Canary Islands, Spain.

It grows on north-facing coastal cliffs, rocky slopes, and walls at elevations from sea level to 500 meters.

The species prefers partially shaded rock crevices where it receives morning sun and afternoon shade.

Annual rainfall in its habitat is low (200–400 mm), supplemented by frequent fog and oceanic mist.

Temperatures remain mild year-round, rarely dropping below 10 °C or exceeding 30 °C.

It grows alongside other Canary Island succulents such as Aeonium urbicum and various Kleinia species.

The volcanic substrate provides mineral-rich, well-drained conditions that the species requires.

## Cultivation

Aeonium haworthii thrives in bright, filtered light with some direct morning sun.

It prefers a well-draining soil mix with moderate organic content, unlike the pure mineral mixes preferred by desert succulents.

Water regularly during [[query-how-to-protect-plants-from-frost-and-extend-the-growing-season]] (autumn to spring) and reduce in summer when the plant enters dormancy.

Unlike many succulents, Aeonium species are winter-growers that are active during cooler months.

Hardy to approximately −2 °C (USDA zone 10) for brief periods.

Propagation is easy from stem cuttings, which root readily in moist, gritty soil.

The species tolerates coastal conditions including salt spray and wind exposure.

Older stems become woody and may shed lower leaves, requiring pruning to maintain appearance.

## Varieties and Related Species

Aeonium haworthii var. haworthii is the typical form with blue-green leaves and pink margins.

A cultivar 'Variegatum' with cream-striped leaves exists but is uncommon in cultivation.

[[aeonium-arboreum]] is a larger, more robust relative with deeper green or purple-black rosettes.

Aeonium decorum has more open rosettes and white to pink flowers, also native to the Canary Islands.
