# Lanzarote Condenser Pits

## Description

An agricultural technique from Lanzarote in the Canary Islands where large pits
(8-10m across, 1-3m deep) are excavated with a single vine or tree planted in
each. Covered with cinder (volcanic ash) that acts as a condensation surface
harvesting moisture from nighttime sea winds. Enables rain-fed agriculture in
one of the driest inhabited islands on Earth.

## Classification

- **Category**: Water harvesting technique, agricultural earthwork
- **Domain**: Desert agriculture, volcanic terrain cultivation, arid viticulture
- **Technology level**: Low-tech, traditional
- **Geographic origin**: Lanzarote, Canary Islands, Spain

## System Design

### Pit Specifications
- Diameter: 8-10 meters across
- Depth: 1-3 meters
- Planting: One vine or tree per pit
- Cover: Cinder (volcanic ash) carefully raked over the surface

### How It Works
1. Pits act as night condensers, trapping cold night air
2. Cold air from sea winds sinks into the pits
3. Moisture condenses on cinder surfaces
4. Cinder's air pockets and gas bubbles retain moisture from dews
5. Condensation wets root areas below the surface
6. Daytime temperatures moderated by pit depth

### Cinder Properties
- Contains many air pockets and gas bubbles that trap moisture
- Retains moisture from dews and condensation
- Allows rain infiltration through to roots
- Prevents soil erosion on the pit surface
- Prevents soil overheating in direct sun
- Works effectively over clay, loam, and volcanic soils

## Applications

- Vine cultivation (primary use on Lanzarote)
- Tree planting in arid volcanic terrain
- Potato ridges on sloping country
- Orchards in sloping terrain
- Home gardens wherever cinder is plentiful

## Advantages

- No irrigation infrastructure required
- Uses freely available volcanic material
- Enables agriculture without rainfall dependence
- Low maintenance after initial construction
- Protects from wind at ground level
- Provides thermal mass for temperature moderation

## Key Facts

- Traditional technique from Canary Islands
- Pits 8-10m diameter, 1-3m depth
- Single vine or tree per pit
- Cinder cover essential for condensation harvesting
- Enables rain-fed agriculture in extremely arid conditions
- Sea winds provide nighttime moisture source
- Works over clay, loam, and volcanic soils

## Relevance to Cultivation

Demonstrates how traditional knowledge enables agriculture in seemingly impossible
conditions. Particularly relevant for volcanic islands and arid regions with
access to volcanic material. Works with natural daily temperature cycles and sea
wind patterns to harvest water passively without any energy input.
