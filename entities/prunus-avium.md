## Overview

**Prunus avium**, the sweet cherry, is a deciduous tree in the family
Rosaceae native to Europe, western Asia, and northwestern Africa. It is
one of the most commercially important temperate [[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]] before or with leaf emergence.

Fruit is a drupe ([[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]],
Italy, Spain, and Chile.

## Cultivation

Modern sweet cherry orchards are typically planted at densities of
300–1,200 trees per hectare using dwarfing rootstocks such as
*Gisela 5* and *Gisela 6*. These rootstocks reduce tree size and
encourage earlier bearing.

Flowering occurs early in spring, making the crop vulnerable to late
frost damage. [[fukuoka-australia-fruit-fly-incident]] (*Rhagoletis cerasi*), cherry
aphid (*Myzus cerasi*), [[query-difference-between-white-rot-and-brown-rot-fungi]] (*Monilinia fructicola*).
Bacterial canker (*Pseudomonas syringae*) is the most serious disease
concern in humid growing regions.

Harvest is labor-intensive, as sweet cherries bruise easily and must
be picked by hand. Postharvest handling requires rapid cooling to
maintain fruit quality.

Yields range from 10–25 tonnes per hectare depending on cultivar,
rootstock, and growing conditions. Orchard lifespan is typically
15–25 years.
