# Mushroom Substrate Bags

## Description

Mushroom substrate bags are sealed containers filled with a prepared growing medium (substrate) in which mushroom mycelium colonizes and eventually produces fruiting bodies. This bag-based cultivation system is the predominant method for growing oyster mushrooms and other species in tropical and subtropical regions, particularly in Thailand and Southeast Asia. The system is well-suited to small-scale and commercial operations alike, offering flexibility, scalability, and relatively low startup costs.

## Classification

- **Category**: Mushroom cultivation system
- **Type**: Bag culture method
- **Container**: Polypropylene or polyethylene bags (typically 15×30 cm)
- **Application**: Fruiting [[plant-secondary-macronutrients-calcium-magnesium-and-sulfur]] for enzymatic processes
- **Sugar**: Readily available carbon energy source to jump-start mycelial colonization

### Preparation Process
1. Mix all dry ingredients thoroughly
2. Add water gradually while mixing to achieve proper moisture content (60-70%)
3. Fill substrate into plastic bags (approximately 1-1.5 kg wet weight per bag)
4. Compact the substrate moderately and form a central cavity for spawn placement
5. Install a plastic ring (PVC neck) at the bag opening
6. Plug with cotton or filter material for gas exchange
7. Label bags with date and batch information

## Pasteurization

After filling, substrate bags must be pasteurized to eliminate competing organisms:

### Country-Style Pasteurization
- Uses simple drums or chambers heated over fire
- Suitable for small-scale operations
- Temperature maintained at 60-80°C for 4-8 hours
- Lowest cost option, appropriate for resource-limited farmers

### Commercial Pasteurization
- Uses purpose-built steam chambers
- Higher throughput and more consistent results
- Temperature controlled precisely
- Requires greater initial investment

### Solar Heating Pasteurization
- Uses solar energy to heat substrate
- Environmentally friendly and low operating cost
- Weather-dependent, requires sunny conditions
- [[mushroom-cultivation-disabilities-substrate-bag-production]] is the core production activity in the FAO training program. Every trainee, regardless of disability, must master the bag-making process. The manual emphasizes creative adaptation:

- Trainees unable to use both hands can use their feet to fill bags, place rings, and tie closures
- The repetitive nature of bag production makes it accessible to people with mental disabilities
- The process can be adapted to various physical capabilities through creative problem-solving

The bag system's modular nature means that production can start very small (as few as 100 bags) and scale up as the farmer gains experience and capital. A mushroom house for 1,000 bags can be built for as little as 500 Baht (approximately US$12) using locally available materials.
