# [[cervantes-dolomite-lime-ph-buffering|Dolomite]]

## Description

Dolomite is a naturally occurring carbonate mineral composed of calcium magnesium carbonate. It is widely used in agriculture and permaculture as a soil amendment to raise pH (liming), supply calcium and magnesium, and improve soil structure. Dolomite is one of the first minerals to precipitate during seawater evaporation, making it abundant in marine sedimentary deposits worldwide.

## Classification

- **Category**: Mineral / Carbonate
- **Chemical formula**: CaMg(CO3)2
- **Crystal system**: Trigonal (rhombohedral)
- **Mohs hardness**: 3.5-4
- **Specific gravity**: 2.85

## Formation and Occurrence

Dolomite forms in sedimentary environments, typically through the alteration of limestone by magnesium-rich fluids. It is also one of the early evaporite minerals that precipitate from seawater during evaporation, preceding [[comparison-gypsum-vs-dolomite|gypsum]] and rock salt formation. Major deposits occur worldwide in ancient marine sedimentary basins. [[mollison-designers-macadamia-nut-in-tropical-polyculture|Mollison]] notes that as seawater evaporates, calcite and dolomite separate out first, then gypsum and anhydrite.

## Key Facts

- Composed of approximately 30% calcium, 21% magnesium, and 48% carbonate by weight
- The dual calcium-magnesium content distinguishes it from calcite (pure calcium carbonate)
- Widely quarried and sold as agricultural lime (dolomitic limestone)
- Raises [[soil-ph]] while simultaneously supplying both calcium and magnesium
- Second most needed mineral after calcium in non-calcareous soils, per Mollison
- Effective as a soil conditioner for improving [[crumb-structure]] in clay soils
- One of the first evaporite minerals to precipitate from seawater

## Properties

- **Color**: White, gray, pink, or brown
- **Luster**: Vitreous to pearly
- **Solubility**: Slowly soluble in dilute acids
- **Reaction with acid**: Fizzes only in powdered form with dilute HCl (distinguishes from calcite)
- **Neutralizing value**: Approximately 95-108% relative to pure calcium carbonate

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

### Soil pH Amendment
- Primary agricultural use is raising soil pH (liming) in acidic soils
- Dual nutrient supply (Ca and Mg) makes it preferable to calcite on magnesium-deficient soils
- Should not be used where magnesium levels are already high (can cause soil compaction)
- Typical application rates: 1-5 tonnes per hectare depending on soil test results

### Soil Structure Improvement
- Calcium from dolomite promotes soil crumb structure through clay flocculation
- Improved structure enhances water infiltration and root penetration
- Aids in reducing soil crusting and surface sealing
- Particularly effective on heavy clay soils

### Nutrient Availability
- Raising pH with dolomite increases availability of phosphorus, molybdenum, and other nutrients
- Supplies magnesium essential for chlorophyll production
- Calcium supports cell wall development and root growth
- Correct pH range maximizes microbial activity and nutrient cycling

### Evaporite and Marine Connections
- First mineral to precipitate from seawater during evaporation (alongside calcite)
- Links marine and terrestrial mineral cycles
- Marine-derived dolomite used as lime in coastal agriculture
- Understanding evaporite sequence helps locate mineral resources for soil amendment

### Considerations for Use
- Avoid over-liming which can create alkalinity problems and micronutrient deficiencies
- Not recommended where soil magnesium is already in high ratio to calcium
- Apply well in advance of planting (3-6 months) for maximum pH effect
- Incorporate into soil rather than surface spreading for faster action
- Monitor soil pH and nutrient levels regularly after application
