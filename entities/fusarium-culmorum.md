# Fusarium culmorum

## Overview

fusarium culmorum is an important soil-borne fungal plant pathogen belonging to the phylum [[soil-foodweb]] dynamics as a food resource for soil fauna.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Sordariomycetes
- **Order:** Hypocreales
- **Family:** Nectriaceae
- **Genus:** Fusarium
- **Species:** F. culmorum

The genus Fusarium is one of the most economically important groups of filamentous fungi, containing numerous plant pathogens, mycotoxin producers, and opportunistic human pathogens. fusarium culmorum belongs to the Fusarium sambucinum species complex and is closely related to F. graminearum, with which it shares many ecological and pathological characteristics. The teleomorph of F. culmorum has been reported but is rarely observed in nature.

## Morphology and Identification

fusarium culmorum is a filamentous fungus with distinctive morphological features:

- **Colony:** On potato dextrose [[blumeria-graminis]] and [[mycorrhizal-fungi]]. These antagonists may suppress the pathogen through competition, antibiosis, or parasitism.

## Economic and Practical Significance

- **Crop losses:** fusarium culmorum causes significant yield losses in cereal crops worldwide, particularly in cooler climates where it replaces F. graminearum as the dominant cause of ear blight.
- **Mycotoxin contamination:** The mycotoxins produced by F. culmorum contaminate grain, reducing grain quality and marketability. Deoxynivalenol contamination can render grain unsuitable for human consumption or animal feed.
- **Food safety:** Mycotoxin exposure through contaminated grain poses risks to human and animal health, including acute toxicity, chronic health effects, and immune system suppression.
- **[[fukuoka-textdoc-biological-control-fallacy-pheromone-critique]]:** The feeding preference of collembola for F. culmorum suggests opportunities for enhancing natural biological control through conservation or augmentation of soil fauna populations.

## Management

Management strategies for Fusarium diseases caused by F. culmorum include:

- [[comparison-crop-rotation-vs-cover-cropping]] with non-host crops
- Tillage practices to bury infected crop residues
- Use of resistant or tolerant cereal cultivars
- Application of fungicides at flowering (for ear blight control)
- Biological control using microbial antagonists
- Conservation of soil fauna that contribute to natural pathogen regulation

## Research Notes

Dighton (2003) references fusarium culmorum in the context of collembola feeding preferences on soil-borne plant pathogenic fungi. The finding that all tested collembola species preferred F. culmorum mycelium highlights the importance of soil fauna in regulating plant pathogen populations and adds to the understanding of multitrophic interactions in soil food webs. This research has implications for designing farming systems that promote natural biological control through soil faunal conservation.
