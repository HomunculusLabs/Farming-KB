# Morus alba

**Type:** Species (Tree)
**Category:** Multipurpose tree (fodder, fruit, timber)
**Family:** Moraceae
**Also known as:** White mulberry, silkworm mulberry

## Overview
Morus alba is a fast-growing deciduous tree native to China, most
famous as the primary food source for silkworms (Bombyx mori) in the
sericulture industry that shaped global trade for millennia. Growing
up to 25 m tall, it is exceptionally hardy, drought-resistant,
shade-tolerant, and adaptable to most soils. Beyond sericulture, it
serves as a high-quality fodder tree, fruit producer, and timber
source, making it a highly versatile element in permaculture systems.

## Taxonomy
- **Order:** Rosales
- **Family:** Moraceae
- **Genus:** Morus
- **Species:** M. alba
- **Common relatives:** M. nigra (black mulberry), M. rubra (red
  mulberry)

## Key Details
- **Growth rate:** Fast, up to 25 m
- **Hardiness:** Frost and drought tolerant; well suited to cool
  areas
- **Shade tolerance:** Tolerant of shade and suited to most soils
  and sites
- **Fruit yield:** Heavy and regular bearer; 19-24 tonnes per
  hectare annually
- **Fruit:** White fruit; can be dried and ground into flour for
  cooking; dried mulberry analysis: total solids 94.81%, invert
  sugar 70.01%, protein 2.59%, crude fibre 2.65%
- **Fodder value (leaves, dry basis):** Crude protein 15.1%, Fat
  5.7%, Fibre 13.7%, Nitrogen-free extract 50.3%, Calcium 1.95%,
  Phosphorus 0.4%
- **Livestock forage:** Supports 2-3 pigs per tree at 86 trees/ha
  for the 60-day fruiting season (Hicks variety); excellent
  self-forage for poultry; leaves very palatable to all livestock
- **Silkworm food:** Primary food source for Bombyx mori in global
  sericulture; leaves are optimally nutritious for silkworm
  production
- **Timber:** Used for sporting goods, furniture, tool handles,
  house building, turnery, carriage and cart parts; sapwood
  especially valued commercially
- **Bark fibre:** Used in China and Europe for paper-making and the
  textile industry; bark yields fibre suitable for textiles
- **Companion planting:** Considered a good companion plant for
  grapes; can be pruned to form a natural trellis for grapevines

## Cultivation
Easily propagated and transplanted. Prefers deep soil, good drainage
and full sun, but tolerates shade and a wide range of conditions.
Annual pruning maintains productive foliage for fodder; on
well-drained soils it is one of the best trees for cultivation. The
tree coppices readily and can be managed for sustained biomass
production. Pruned young trees can be shaped into tool handles.

## Relevance
White mulberry is one of the most productive multipurpose trees for
temperate permaculture systems. Its combination of high-quality
fodder (15% protein leaves), edible fruit, useful timber, and
companion planting benefits with grapes makes it a multi-functional
element well-suited to food forest design, integrated livestock
systems, and agroforestry. The sericulture connection adds cultural
and economic depth to its permaculture value.
