# Arctium minus (*Lesser Burdock*)

## Overview

Arctium minus, commonly known as lesser burdock, is a biennial herbaceous
plant in the family Asteraceae. Native to Eurasia, it has become widely
naturalized across [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]] and [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]] worldwide. The
species is valued both as [[query-how-do-i-build-a-medicinal-herb-drying-rack-and-optimize-drying-conditions]] (its root is used in
traditional European and Asian medicine) and as a practical permaculture
plant with deep taproots for soil improvement and bioremediation.

The plant is perhaps best known for its burrs — hooked involucral bracts
that cling tenaciously to animal fur and clothing, a trait that inspired
the invention of Velcro. Lesser burdock is closely related to and often
confused with greater burdock (*A. lappa*), which is the primary species
used commercially.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Division:** Magnoliophyta
- **Class:** Magnoliopsida
- **Order:** Asterales
- **Family:** Asteraceae
- **Genus:** *Arctium*
- **Species:** *A. minus* (Hill) Bernh.
- **Synonyms:** *Lappa minor* Hill, *Arctium vulgare* (Lam.) Léveille

**Growth habit:** Biennial, forming a rosette in the first year and
a flowering stem (1–2.5 m tall) in the second year.

**Leaves:** First-year rosette leaves are large (up to 50 cm long),
ovate to cordate, with finely toothed margins and a woolly gray-green
undersurface. Petioles are solid (not hollow, distinguishing from
*A. lappa* which has hollow petioles). Second-year stem leaves are
smaller and alternate.

**Stem:** Erect, stout, branching above, green to purplish,
covered with short glandular hairs.

**Flowers:** Purple to pink, composed of numerous tubular florets
packed into globular flower heads (1.5–3 cm diameter). Heads are
subtended by hooked bracts (the burrs). Blooms July–October.

**Root:** Thick, fleshy taproot extending 0.5–1.5 m deep, creamy
white when cut, with a sweet, earthy flavor.

**Fruit (achene):** Cylindrical, 5–6 mm long, brown, with short
pappus. Enclosed within the persistent burr of hooked bracts.

## Distribution and Habitat

**Native range:** Europe and temperate Asia. Widely naturalized in
[[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]], Australia, New Zealand, and [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-south-america]].

**Habitat:** Disturbed areas, roadsides, fence lines, waste ground,
forest edges, stream banks, and pastures. Tolerates a wide range of
soil types including compacted, clay-rich, and nutrient-poor soils.
Prefers full sun to partial shade and moderate moisture.

**Invasiveness:** Considered a noxious weed in some jurisdictions due
to its vigorous growth and burr dispersal. However, it is less
aggressive than some thistle species and can be managed through
grazing and cultivation.
