## Overview

**Orchis mascula** (L.) L., the early purple orchid, is a perennial tuberous herb in the family Orchidaceae. It is one of the most widespread and recognizable wild orchids in Europe, flowering from April to June.

The species produces showy purple-pink flower spikes on tall stems above a rosette of unspotted lanceolate leaves. Its common names include early purple orchid, male orchid, and long purple.

The tubers have a long history of use in traditional medicine and as the source of salep, a starchy flour used in beverages and desserts across the eastern Mediterranean and Middle East.

*Orchis mascula* is distinguished from similar species by its unspotted leaves and early flowering period. It is a characteristic species of ancient woodlands and species-rich calcareous grasslands across its range.

The species name "mascula" refers to the paired testis-shaped tubers, which were historically associated with male virility in the Doctrine of Signatures. This association contributed to its extensive use in folk medicine.

Synonyms include *Orchis mascula* var. *acutiflora* W.D.J.Koch and *Orchis mascula* subsp. *olivetorum* (Gren.) E.G.Camus. The species was originally described by Carl Linnaeus in 1753.

**Morphology:**

Plants grow 20–60 cm tall from two subterranean ovoid tubers. The leaves are broadly lanceolate, glossy green, typically unspotted, and form a basal rosette of 4–8 leaves.

The inflorescence is a dense cylindrical spike of 10–40 flowers, each 1.5–2.5 cm across. Petals and sepals are purple to pinkish-purple, forming a hood over the column.

The labellum is three-lobed, spotted with darker purple markings, and bears a short spur 5–8 mm long. Flowers produce a faint sweet scent and secrete nectar from the spur.

The fruit is an erect elliptical capsule 10–20 mm long containing numerous dust-like seeds. Each seed is approximately 0.3–0.5 mm in length, typical of orchid species relying on wind dispersal.

The root system consists of two palmate tubers replaced annually. One tuber provides the current season's growth while a second develops as a replacement for the following year.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Orchis mascula* is native to most of Europe, extending from the British Isles and Iberian Peninsula eastward to the Caucasus and northern Iran. Its range spans approximately 40–65°N latitude.

It also occurs in northern Africa (Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia) and the Canary Islands. The species is absent from the far north of Scandinavia and most Mediterranean islands.

The preferred habitats include broadleaved woodland, scrub, grassland, calcareous meadows, and rocky hillsides. It favors lightly shaded to open situations on alkaline to neutral soils.

Populations occur from sea level to approximately 2,500 m elevation in mountainous regions. Plants typically grow in association with *[[corylus-avellana]]* woodlands.

In the northern parts of its range, it is predominantly a woodland species, while in southern Europe it occupies more open habitats including garigue and montane grassland. [[soil-ph]] preferences range from neutral to strongly calcareous.
