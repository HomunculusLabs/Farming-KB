## Description

Oxalic acid (C₂H₂O₄) is a dicarboxylic organic acid produced by a wide range of fungi. It is one of the most significant fungal metabolites in terms of ecosystem impact, playing critical roles in mineral weathering, nutrient mobilization, metal detoxification, and pathogenicity. Fungi secrete oxalic acid into their environment as part of their extracellular biochemical toolkit.

## Classification

- **Type**: Organic acid (dicarboxylic acid)
- **Chemical formula**: C₂H₂O₄ (also written as HOOC-COOH)
- **Molecular weight**: 90.03 g/mol
- **pKa values**: 1.27 (pKa1), 4.27 (pKa2)
- **Appearance**: White crystalline solid
- **Solubility**: Highly soluble in water

## Key Facts

- Secreted by fungi to assist in the weathering of calcareous (calcium carbonate) bedrock — the acid dissolves mineral substrates, releasing bound nutrients (calcium, phosphorus, trace metals) into forms available for biological uptake
- Acts as a powerful chelating agent, forming complexes with metal ions including calcium, iron, magnesium, and heavy metals (lead, copper, zinc, cadmium)
- The calcium oxalate crystals formed when oxalic acid reacts with calcium are among the most common biominerals in soils and are frequently observed associated with fungal hyphae
- Plays a central role in the "rock-eating" behavior of ectomycorrhizal fungi, which use oxalic acid to bore micropores into mineral grains, accessing phosphorus and other nutrients locked within rock matrices
- Functions as a pH-lowering agent, creating acidic microenvironments around fungal hyphae that favor the activity of certain extracellular enzymes
- Involved in fungal pathogenicity: oxalic acid produced by pathogens like *Sclerotinia sclerotiorum* suppresses host plant defenses by acidifying infected tissues and generating toxic levels of reactive oxygen species
- Contributes to the formation of protosoils by lichens through synergistic action with lichenic acids
- Production is particularly notable in basidiomycete fungi, especially brown-rot wood decay fungi, which use oxalic acid in conjunction with Fenton chemistry to degrade cellulose in wood

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- In mushroom cultivation, oxalic acid production affects substrate pH — understanding this helps cultivators manage substrate chemistry and anticipate pH changes during colonization
- The chelating properties of oxalic acid are relevant to heavy metal accumulation in fruiting bodies, which has food safety implications for mushrooms grown on contaminated substrates
- Brown-rot fungi (which produce oxalic acid as a key metabolic tool) represent a category of wood-decay organisms distinct from the white-rot fungi commonly cultivated for food — understanding the chemical differences informs substrate preparation
- In mycorrhizal inoculation programs, the capacity of fungal partners to produce oxalic acid and access mineral nutrients is a key selection criterion for improving plant nutrient uptake
- Oxalic acid's role in fungal pathogenicity mechanisms is relevant to understanding and combating fungal diseases in both mushroom crops and host plants

## Ecological Significance

Oxalic acid represents one of the primary biochemical mechanisms by which fungi reshape their physical environment. Through its production, fungi actively participate in pedogenesis (soil formation), mineral nutrient cycling, and geochemical transformations at scales ranging from individual mineral grains to landscape-level weathering processes.
