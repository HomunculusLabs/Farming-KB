## LOPHOPHINE

## Extensions and Commentary

Shulgin provides an excellent argument for why LOPHOPHINE should be active, making its inactivity "completely unexpected." [[tihkal-receptor-binding-serotonin]].

## Comparison to Related Compounds

LOPHOPHINE can be compared to several structurally related compounds:

- Mescaline (3,4,5-trimethoxy): Active at 200-500 mg orally

- MMDA (3-methoxy-4,5-methylenedioxyamphetamine): Active at 100-250 mg

- MDA (3,4-methylenedioxyamphetamine): Active at 75-150 mg

The fact that MMDA, the amphetamine homologue of LOPHOPHINE, is active while LOPHOPHINE itself is not reinforces the general pattern that three-carbon amphetamines tend to be more potent than their two-carbon phenethylamine counterparts. However, the degree of difference in this case is unusual, as many phenethylamines retain significant activity at moderate doses.

## Harm Reduction and Safety Considerations

While LOPHOPHINE appears to be largely inactive even at 250 mg, general precautions still apply:

- The compound has not been extensively characterized pharmacologically; idiosyncratic reactions cannot be ruled out

- The euphoric mood elevation reported at 150 mg, while mild, suggests some central activity that could interact unpredictably with other substances

- Individuals taking MAO inhibitors, SSRIs, or other serotonergic medications should exercise caution

- As with all research chemicals, purity and identity should be verified before any handling

## Legal Status

LOPHOPHINE is not specifically scheduled in most jurisdictions. As a methylenedioxy-substituted phenethylamine, it could potentially fall under analogue legislation in countries with broad controlled substance analogue acts. In the [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]], the Federal Analogue Act could potentially apply given its structural similarity to MDA (a Schedule I substance). However, its lack of demonstrated psychoactivity makes prosecution under such provisions unlikely.

## Cultural and Historical Significance

LOPHOPHINE represents one of the most compelling examples of Shulgin"s approach to chemical exploration, which was deeply informed by natural products chemistry and ethnobotany. Rather than randomly synthesizing novel compounds, Shulgin often began with observations from nature and sought to understand the chemical logic underlying traditional psychoactive preparations.

The story of LOPHOPHINE is a reminder of how much remains unknown about the chemistry of even well-studied psychoactive plants. The Peyote cactus has been used ceremonially for thousands of years, and its chemistry has been investigated for over a century, yet the absence of a compound that "should be there" remains unexplained.
