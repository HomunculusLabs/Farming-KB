# Reverse Osmosis Water for Compost Tea

## Description

Reverse osmosis (RO) water treatment is discussed in Elaine Ingham's field guide as a method for preparing high-quality water for brewing [[actively-aerated-compost-tea]] (AACT). Water quality is critical for successful AACT production, as contaminants in source water can harm [[forest-and-bamboo-beneficial-microorganisms]] or introduce unwanted compounds.

## Classification

- **Category**: Water treatment technology
- **Type**: Membrane filtration process
- **Application**: Pre-treatment of brewing water for compost tea production
- **Purpose**: Remove chlorine, chloramine, dissolved minerals, and potential contaminants

## Key Facts

- **Principle**: RO forces water through a semi-permeable membrane that removes >95% of dissolved solids, including chlorine, chloramine, heavy metals, and minerals
- **[[aact-water-quality-chlorine-removal-brewing-standards-ingham]]**: Critical for AACT - chlorine in municipal water kills beneficial microorganisms
- **Chloramine removal**: Unlike chlorine, chloramine does not dissipate by simply leaving water to stand; RO effectively removes it
- **Mineral control**: RO removes dissolved minerals, allowing the brewer to precisely control the nutrient profile through food resources added to the tea
- **pH effect**: RO water tends to be slightly acidic (pH 5.5-6.5) due to dissolved CO2, which may need adjustment

## Water Quality Requirements for AACT

| Parameter | Acceptable Range | Concern |
|-----------|-----------------|---------|
| Chlorine | 0 ppm | Kills microorganisms |
| Chloramine | 0 ppm | Persistent biocide |
| pH | 6.0-7.5 | Affects organism growth |
| Hardness | Low-moderate | Can affect brewing |
| Heavy metals | Below toxic levels | Harmful to biology |
| Temperature | 15-25C | Affects growth rate |

## Water Treatment Options

### Reverse Osmosis
- **Pros**: Removes virtually all contaminants; consistent water quality
- **Cons**: Expensive; produces waste water; removes beneficial minerals too
- **Best for**: Municipal water with chlorine/chloramine; areas with poor water quality

### Aeration (Simple Method)
- **Pros**: Free; simple - bubble air through water or let stand 12-24 hours
- **Cons**: Only removes free chlorine, not chloramine; slow
- **Best for**: Water with chlorine only; budget operations

### Citric Acid Treatment
- **Pros**: Inexpensive; also adjusts pH downward; removes chlorine
- **Cons**: Does not remove all contaminants; adds organic carbon
- **Best for**: Quick chlorine neutralization in the field

### Carbon Filtration
- **Pros**: Removes chlorine and organic contaminants; relatively inexpensive
- **Cons**: Does not remove minerals or chloramine reliably
- **Best for**: Chlorine removal in continuous-flow systems

## RO in Practice

### Pre-Brewing
1. **Produce RO water**: Run sufficient water through RO system for the brew volume
2. **Check pH**: RO water may need pH adjustment to optimal range (6.2-6.8)
3. **Add minerals back**: If using RO, may need to add trace minerals for optimal organism growth
4. **Temperature**: Bring water to [[compost-tea-brewing-temperature-ingredients]] before starting

### Cost Considerations
- **Residential RO systems**: $150-500 for under-sink units
- **Commercial RO systems**: $2,000-20,000+ depending on volume
- **Operating cost**: Filter replacement + waste water; typically $0.01-0.05 per gallon
- **Waste ratio**: Most systems produce 2-4 gallons of waste water per gallon of RO water

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Water quality directly impacts the success of [[compost-tea-brewing]]:

- **Organism survival**: Chlorine and chloramine in untreated water kill the [[ingham-aact-beauveria-fruit-fly-beneficial-bacteria-inoculant]], fungi, and other organisms the tea is meant to cultivate
- **Fungal growth**: Fungi are particularly sensitive to water quality; chlorinated water severely inhibits fungal development in AACT
- **Consistency**: RO water provides a consistent base, eliminating variability from source water quality changes
- **Scale**: For commercial operations, RO may be necessary to produce consistent, high-quality compost tea at scale

## Common Mistakes

- **Not treating water**: Using chlorinated tap water directly kills organisms before brewing begins
- **Assuming standing removes chloramine**: Only free chlorine dissipates; chloramine requires active treatment
- **Over-purifying**: Extremely pure RO water may lack trace minerals that support organism growth
- **Ignoring temperature**: Cold RO water from the system may need warming before brewing
