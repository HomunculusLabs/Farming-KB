## Description

Anaerobic brews are [[compost]] tea or liquid amendments produced without adequate aeration, resulting in oxygen-depleted conditions that favor the production of toxic metabolites. Ingham strongly warns against using anaerobic brews, noting that while they may show inhibition of disease organisms in laboratory plate cultures, they introduce harmful toxins to soil, leaf surfaces, and blossoms. These toxins are analogous to pesticides and antibiotics in their potential for harm to plants, humans, and animals, but unlike regulated chemicals, their concentrations in anaerobic brews are neither controlled nor monitored.

## Classification

- **Category**: Compost tea [[clay-seed-pellet-production-method-concrete-mixer]] (undesirable)
- **Process**: Fermentation without adequate oxygen supply
- **Byproducts**: Toxic organic acids, alcohols, and other anaerobic metabolites
- **Contrast**: [[actively-aerated-compost-tea]] (AACT) — the recommended alternative

## Key Facts

- [[aact-ph-and-anaerobic-conditions]] produce toxic metabolites harmful to plants, humans, and animals
- May show pathogen inhibition in lab plate cultures but this does not translate to real-world effectiveness
- Toxins include short-chain organic acids characteristic of anaerobic [[decomposition]]
- No careful studies have been performed on the specific toxins produced in anaerobic brews
- Toxin concentrations are not controlled or monitored
- Toxins function similarly to pesticides and antibiotics — broad-spectrum, non-selective killing
- Lab results showing disease inhibition are misleading because plate culture conditions differ radically from real soil environments
- Use of anaerobic brews "should be discouraged" per Ingham
- The apparent disease inhibition in labs occurs because toxins kill organisms on plates, but in soil the toxins harm beneficials too

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

This is a critical safety and efficacy warning for anyone producing compost tea. The temptation to skip aeration equipment is understandable, but the consequences are severe. Anaerobic brews not only fail to provide the benefits of AACT but actively introduce harmful substances to the [[cannabis-growing-environment-setup]]. For mushroom cultivators, this is especially relevant because [[brassinosteroids-in-fungal-cultures]] are highly sensitive to toxic metabolites — anaerobic byproducts could damage or kill mycelium. The principle extends beyond tea brewing to any liquid amendment preparation: maintaining aerobic conditions throughout the process is non-negotiable. The distinction between lab plate results (where anaerobic toxins kill pathogens on inert media) and field results (where the same toxins damage the entire biological community) illustrates why controlled studies matter and testimonials about "[[aact-foliar-disease-control]]" from anaerobic products should be viewed with extreme skepticism.

## Source Context

Ingham devotes significant attention to warning against anaerobic brews in her field guide, noting that while plate count labs may show pathogen inhibition from anaerobic products, these results do not translate to real-world benefit and the toxins produced are dangerous and uncharacterized.
