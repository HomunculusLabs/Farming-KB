# Noladin Ether

**Type:** Chemical Compound (Endocannabinoid)
**Category:** Endogenous Cannabinoid
**Also known as:** 2-Arachidonoyl-glyceryl-ether, 2-AGE,
O-arachidonoylethanolamine glycerol ether

## Overview
Noladin ether (2-arachidonoyl-glyceryl-ether, or 2-AGE) is one of
five identified endocannabinoids produced naturally by the human body.
First isolated and characterized in 2001 by researchers Hanus et al.,
noladin ether was the third endogenous cannabinoid discovered after
anandamide (1992) and 2-AG (1995). It acts primarily as a CB1 receptor
agonist and contributes to the body's endocannabinoid signaling
network, though it is found at much lower concentrations than either
anandamide or 2-AG.

## Key Details

### Chemical Properties
- **Molecular formula:** C23H38O3
- **Classification:** Endocannabinoid (glyceryl ether)
- **Molecular weight:** 362.5 g/mol
- **Structure:** Similar to 2-AG but with an ether linkage instead of
  an ester bond at the sn-1 position of the glycerol backbone
- **Stability:** The ether linkage makes noladin ether more resistant
  to enzymatic degradation compared to ester-linked endocannabinoids

### Pharmacology
- **CB1 receptor activity:** Noladin ether acts as an agonist at CB1
  cannabinoid receptors, though its potency and efficacy differ from
  both anandamide and 2-AG
- **CB2 receptor activity:** Limited binding to CB2 receptors, making
  it primarily a CB1-mediated signaling molecule
- **Enzymatic stability:** The ether bond is not a substrate for the
  primary endocannabinoid-degrading enzymes (FAAH for anandamide,
  MAGL for 2-AG), which may result in a longer duration of action
  in vivo

## Relevance
Noladin ether is significant as part of the expanding understanding of
the [[endocannabinoid-system]]'s complexity. While it is not the most
abundant or well-studied endocannabinoid, its discovery demonstrated
that the body produces multiple distinct lipid mediators capable of
activating cannabinoid receptors. This has implications for cannabis
medicine, as plant cannabinoids may modulate not just anandamide and
2-AG signaling but the entire family of endocannabinoid pathways.
