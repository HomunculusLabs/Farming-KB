## Overview

**Cumbungi** refers to several species of **bullrush/cattail** in the genus *Typha*, native to Australia. Referenced on page 108 of Holmgren's *Permaculture: Principles and Pathways Beyond Sustainability*, cumbungi is a multifunctional wetland plant valuable for permaculture design, water treatment, and traditional indigenous use.

## Species

- *Typha domingensis* (narrow-leaved cumbungi) — Most widespread, across mainland Australia
- *Typha orientalis* (broad-leaved cumbungi) — Eastern Australia, Queensland to Tasmania
- *Typha latifolia* (common cattail) — Introduced Northern Hemisphere species

## Description

Robust rhizomatous perennial, 1.5-3m tall, with long sword-like leaves, characteristic brown cylindrical flower spikes, and extensive rhizome system forming dense monospecific stands in shallow water and wet soils.

## Ecological Functions

### Water Purification
- Absorbs excess nitrogen and phosphorus
- Traps sediment by slowing water flow
- Accumulates heavy metals from contaminated water
- Root zone supports beneficial bacteria processing pollutants

### Habitat
- Nesting habitat for waterbirds, shelter for aquatic organisms
- Food source for waterbirds, fish, and wildlife
- Amphibian breeding habitat

### Waterway Stability
- Extensive roots stabilize banks against erosion
- Regulates water flow through channels

## Traditional Uses

Indigenous Australians used cumbungi for millennia. **Food**: starchy rhizomes (roasted or ground into flour), young shoots (like asparagus), pollen (nutritious flour rich in protein), seeds. **Materials**: leaf fibers for weaving mats, baskets, shelters; seed fluff for pillow stuffing and wound dressing; stems for rafts and fish traps.

## Permaculture Applications

Holmgren references cumbungi (page 108) in permaculture water management:
- **Greywater treatment** in reed beds
- **Constructed wetlands** for wastewater treatment
- **Dam and pond edge** stabilization while improving water quality
- **Edible wetland** integration with water chestnut, taro, and other aquatic crops
- **Biomass production** for mulch material

## Cultivation

Propagated from seed, rhizome division, or transplanting. Requires shallow water (0-50cm) or consistently damp soil, full sun to partial shade. Rapid growth; may require containment. Hardy across most Australian climates.

## Relevance to Mycology

- Roots support arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi (AMF)
- Dead leaves and stems provide substrate for saprophytic fungi
- Wetland fungal communities important for nutrient cycling
- Biomass usable in compost formulations as fungal food source
- Rhizosphere in treatment wetlands supports complex fungal food webs

## Caution

Can become invasive in artificial water bodies. Rapid rhizome spread requires management to prevent blockage of waterways.
