## Overview

Sansevieria trifasciata, commonly called snake plant or mother-in-law's tongue, is an evergreen perennial succulent in the family Asparagaceae. It is native to tropical West Africa from Nigeria east to the Democratic Republic of Congo.

The species is among the most popular houseplants worldwide, valued for its architectural form, extreme tolerance of neglect, and reputed air-purifying properties. Its stiff, upright leaves create bold vertical accents in interior landscapes.

The genus Sansevieria has been reclassified into Dracaena based on molecular phylogenetic evidence, though the name Sansevieria remains in widespread horticultural use. The correct nomenclature is Dracaena trifasciata (Prain) Mabb.

Hundreds of cultivars have been developed, ranging from dwarf forms under 20 cm tall to large varieties exceeding 1.2 m. The species received the Royal Horticultural Society Award of Garden Merit in 1993.

Popular cultivars include "Laurentii" with golden-yellow leaf margins, "Zeylanica" with dark green cross-banding, and "Hahnii" (bird's nest sansevieria) forming compact rosettes.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:**
- Kingdom: Plantae
- Clade: Tracheophytes
- Clade: Angiosperms
- Clade: Monocots
- Order: Asparagales
- Family: Asparagaceae
- Subfamily: Nolinoideae
- Genus: Dracaena (formerly Sansevieria)
- Species: Dracaena trifasciata (Prain) Mabb.

Plants produce stiff, fleshy, sword-shaped leaves arranged in rosettes. Leaves are 30-120 cm tall and 3-7 cm wide, dark green with lighter green cross-banding patterns.

The leaves are succulent and store water in thick-walled cells, enabling prolonged [[dighton-fungal-drought-tolerance-plant-water-relations]]. A waxy cuticle covers the leaf surface, further reducing water loss.

Inflorescences are paniculate racemes bearing small, greenish-white, tubular flowers. Flowers are fragrant, producing a strong sweet scent especially in the evening hours.

The root system consists of fleshy rhizomes that spread horizontally, producing new rosettes at intervals. This rhizomatous growth enables rapid vegetative spread.

The leaf cross-section reveals a central water-storage parenchyma surrounded by photosynthetic chlorenchyma tissue and a thick fibrous epidermis providing [[comparison-silicon-vs-calcium-in-plant-defense-and-structural-support]].

## Distribution and Habitat

Native to tropical West Africa, from Nigeria eastward through Cameroon to the Democratic Republic of Congo. The species occurs in dry, open forests and savanna margins.

It grows on well-drained, often rocky substrates in areas with pronounced wet and dry seasons. Annual rainfall in native habitat ranges from 600-1,200 mm.

The species has been introduced globally as an ornamental and has naturalized in parts of Florida, the Caribbean, tropical Asia, and Australia. It thrives in disturbed habitats.

Sansevieria trifasciata is hardy in USDA zones 9b-11 and tolerates brief exposure to -4 C. In cooler climates it is grown exclusively as an indoor plant.

In its native range, the species occasionally grows epiphytically on tree crotches where organic debris accumulates, demonstrating adaptability to diverse substrates.

Populations show considerable morphological [[psilocybin-psilocin-variation-across-flushes]] the native range, with eastern populations tending toward taller, narrower leaves than western forms.

## Cultivation

Sansevieria trifasciata is exceptionally easy to grow and tolerates a wide range of conditions. It is frequently recommended as a beginner houseplant due to its indestructible nature.

Plants adapt to light conditions from deep shade to full sun, though growth is fastest in bright indirect light. Variegated cultivars require more light to maintain their yellow or white margins.

Water thoroughly but infrequently, allowing soil to dry completely between waterings. Overwatering is the primary cause of decline, leading to root rot and basal collapse.

Any well-draining [[solomon-potting-mix-and-seedling-soil-recipe]] is acceptable. A standard cactus and succulent mix works well. Plants are remarkably tolerant of poor soils and prolonged neglect.

Propagation is straightforward via division of rhizome offsets, leaf cuttings, or seed. Variegated cultivars must be propagated by division to maintain leaf patterning.

Pest problems are rare but include mealybugs and spider mites under stress. Plants are generally pest-resistant when grown in appropriate conditions.

Repotting is rarely needed as the plant prefers being slightly rootbound. Overpotting in large containers increases the risk of root rot from excess soil moisture.

Fertilizer requirements are minimal. A diluted balanced fertilizer applied two to three times during [[query-how-to-protect-plants-from-frost-and-extend-the-growing-season]] is sufficient for healthy growth.

## Air Purification Research

Sansevieria trifasciata gained widespread attention following NASA's Clean Air Study (1989), which found it effective at removing indoor air pollutants including formaldehyde, benzene, and trichloroethylene.
