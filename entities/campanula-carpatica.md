## Overview

**Campanula carpatica** Jacq., the Carpathian bellflower or tussock bellflower, is a low-growing herbaceous perennial in the family Campanulaceae. It is native to the Carpathian Mountains of Central Europe, where it inhabits subalpine meadows, rocky outcrops, and limestone scree slopes.

The species is one of the most widely cultivated bellflowers in temperate gardens worldwide. Numerous cultivars have been selected for flower color, size, and growth habit, making it a staple of rock gardens and border fronts.

Campanula carpatica was first described by Nikolaus Joseph von Jacquin in 1774 from material collected during his botanical expeditions through the Austrian Empire. The specific epithet *carpatica* directly references the Carpathian mountain range where the type specimen was discovered.

It has become one of the most commercially important Campanula species in the horticultural trade. [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]].

## Ecological Role

The upright, open bell flowers are accessible to a wide range of pollinators. Bumblebees (Bombus spp.) are the primary visitors, using the broad lower petal surface as a landing platform while reaching nectar at the flower base.
