# Psilocybe caerulescens

## Overview

**Psilocybe caerulescens** is a psilocybin-containing mushroom in the family Hymenogastraceae, notable for its historical significance in Mesoamerican indigenous ceremonies and its natural distribution spanning Mexico through Central America.

The species was one of the mushrooms identified by R. Gordon Wasson and Roger Heim during their famous 1950s expeditions to Mexico, where it was documented as part of the Mazatec mushroom complex used in healing and divination rituals.

It is closely related to **Psilocybe mexicana** and **Psilocybe weilii**, sharing similar habitat preferences for disturbed grassy areas and forest margins, and is distinguished by its blue-staining reaction upon bruising and its preference for subtropical montane environments.

The epithet *caerulescens* (Latin: "becoming blue") refers to the distinctive blue bruising reaction caused by oxidation of psilocybin and psilocin.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi — **Division:** Basidiomycota — **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales — **Family:** Hymenogastraceae
- **Genus:** Psilocybe — **Species:** P. caerulescens Murrill

**Authority:** William Alphonso Murrill (1923)

**Synonyms:** Psilocybe caerulescens var. mazatecorum Heim, Psilocybe mazatecorum Heim, Psilocybe zapotecorum Heim (in part), Deconica caerulescens (Murrill) Noordel.

**Etymology:** *Caerulescens* from Latin *caeruleus* (blue), referring to the characteristic blue bruising. This bluing reaction is a hallmark of psilocybin/psilocin presence across the genus.

**Common names:** Landslide mushroom, derrumbe (Mexican Spanish, "landslide"), Mazatec mushroom, San Isidro mushroom.

## Morphology

**Cap (pileus):** 15-50 mm diameter, convex to broadly convex, sometimes with a slight umbo in maturity. Surface smooth to slightly viscid when moist, hygrophanous, strongly striate at the margin when wet. Color: ochraceous brown to chestnut brown when fresh, fading to pale tan or straw as it dries.

**Gills (lamellae):** Adnate to adnexed, subdistant to close, narrow. Color: grayish to dark purplish brown with age, with white edges.

**Stipe (stem):** 40-80 mm × 3-6 mm, equal or slightly thicker at base. Hollow, fragile. Surface pruinose near the apex, smooth below. Color: whitish to pale brown, bruising intensely blue where handled. Partial veil evanescent, rarely leaving a fragile annular zone.

**Flesh:** Thin, watery. Color whitish to pale brownish. Bruises blue rapidly, especially at the base of the stipe.

**Odor and taste:** Farinaceous (mealy, flour-like). Taste slightly bitter.

**Spores:** Ellipsoidal to subrhomboidal in face view, slightly thick-walled. Size: 9-12 × 6-8 μm. Color: dark purplish brown in deposit. Basidia 4-spored. Cheilocystidia ventricose to lageniform. Pleurocystidia absent or rare.

**Microscopic features:** Presence of a germ pore on spores; absence of pleurocystidia distinguishes it from some close relatives.

## Distribution and Habitat

**Geographic range:** Southern Mexico (Oaxaca, Puebla, Veracruz, Chiapas) through Guatemala, Honduras, and northern South America (Venezuela, Colombia). Also reported in the southeastern US (Georgia, Alabama) and subtropical Brazil.

**Habitat:** Primarily disturbed areas, especially recent landslides, road cuts, and eroded banks. Also grassy fields, forest margins, cloud forests, and coffee plantations. Gregarious or in small groups, often on clay-rich or volcanic soils.

**Substrate:** Terrestrial, directly on soil. Associated with decomposing grass roots and organic matter. Occasionally on dung-enriched soils, though not strictly coprophilous.

**Elevation:** 800-2,000 m in Mexico and Guatemala.

**Season:** Rainy season, June-October in Mexico. Late summer to autumn (August-November) in the southeastern US.

## Phytochemistry

Psilocybe caerulescens contains the classic psilocybin mushroom alkaloid profile:

**Major alkaloids:**

- **Psilocybin:** 0.15-0.60% dry weight (varies by collection location and maturity)
- **Psilocin:** 0.01-0.15% dry weight
- **Baeocystin:** 0.01-0.10% dry weight (present in most collections)

**Total tryptamine content:** Approximately 0.2-0.8% dry weight, placing it in the moderate potency range among Psilocybe species — less potent than [[psilocybe-azurescens]] or [[psilocybe-cyanescens]], comparable to [[psilocybe-cubensis]].

**Chemical stability:** Like all psilocybin-containing species, alkaloid content degrades with heat, light, and prolonged storage. Drying at temperatures below 40°C preserves maximum potency.

## Effects and Dosage

**Threshold dose:** 0.5-1.0 g dried
**Light dose:** 1.0-2.5 g dried
**Moderate dose:** 2.5-5.0 g dried
**Strong dose:** 5.0+ g dried

**Duration:** 4-6 hours after oral ingestion. Onset within 20-60 minutes. Peak effects at 1.5-2.5 hours.

**Effects profile:** Visual hallucinations (geometric patterns, color enhancement, morphing objects), altered perception of time, synesthesia, emotional lability, mystical or spiritual experiences. At higher doses: ego dissolution, profound introspection.

The subjective effects are consistent with the 5-HT2A receptor agonist mechanism shared by all psilocybin-containing mushrooms.

⚠️ **Safety note:** Psilocybin mushrooms are illegal in most jurisdictions. Risk of anxiety, paranoia, and challenging psychological experiences, especially at high doses or in unprepared set and setting. Contraindicated with MAOIs, lithium, and in individuals with personal or family history of psychosis.

## Ethnobotanical and Cultural Significance

**Mazatec tradition:** P. caerulescens is part of the complex of entheogenic mushrooms used by Mazatec healers (*curanderos*) in the Sierra Mazateca of Oaxaca, Mexico. The Mazatec call mushrooms *nti-ši-tao* ("little things that spring forth") or refer to them collectively with [[psilocybe-mexicana]] under the name *derrumbe* (landslide), referencing their growth habitat.

**Wasson expedition:** Roger Heim identified collections from Wasson's 1955-1956 expeditions as P. zapotecorum and P. caerulescens var. mazatecorum. These identifications contributed to the scientific validation of Mesoamerican mushroom cults and ultimately led to Albert Hofmann's isolation of psilocybin in 1958.

**Traditional use context:** Consumed in nocturnal healing ceremonies (*veladas*) led by a curandero. The mushrooms are regarded as sentient spiritual beings rather than mere drugs. Ceremonies involve prayer, chanting, and diagnostic divination for illness.

## Cultivation

Cultivation of P. caerulescens is less documented than [[psilocybe-cubensis]], as it has not been widely domesticated. However, it is amenable to standard Psilocybe cultivation techniques:

**Substrate:** Pasteurized straw or supplemented hardwood sawdust. Has been successfully fruited on enriched soil and compost-based substrates mimicking its natural landslide habitat.

**Environmental parameters:**
- Temperature: 22-26°C for colonization, 18-22°C for fruiting
- Humidity: 90-95% relative humidity for primordia formation
- Light: Ambient indirect light sufficient; 12 hours on/off cycle

**Difficulty:** Moderate. More demanding than P. cubensis regarding substrate preparation and environmental control. Less reliable fruiting and lower yields than domesticated species.

**Note:** Cultivation of psilocybin-containing mushrooms is illegal in most countries.

## Ecological Role

As a saprotrophic basidiomycete, P. caerulescens decomposes organic matter in soil, contributing to nutrient cycling in subtropical forest ecosystems. Its preference for landslide and disturbed areas suggests a role as an early-successional decomposer on freshly exposed soil surfaces.

The blue-staining reaction (oxidation of psilocybin to blue quinoid compounds) may serve an ecological defensive function against invertebrate predation, though this hypothesis remains unproven.
