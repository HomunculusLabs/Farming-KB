## Psilocybe caerulescens

## Overview

**Psilocybe caerulescens** is a psilocybin-containing mushroom in the family Hymenogastraceae, notable for its historical significance in Mesoamerican [[cordyceps-neurotropic-fungi-mexican-indigenous-ceremonies]] and its natural distribution spanning Mexico through [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-caribbean-central-america]].

The species was one of the mushrooms identified by R. Gordon Wasson and Roger Heim during their famous 1950s expeditions to Mexico, where it was documented as part of the [[metzner-shamanic-healing-and-divination-paradigm]] rituals.

It is closely related to **[[field-guide-psilocybin-bluing-reaction-identification]] is a hallmark of psilocybin/psilocin presence across the genus.

**Common names:** Landslide mushroom, derrumbe (Mexican Spanish, "landslide"), Mazatec mushroom, San Isidro mushroom.

## Morphology

**Cap (pileus):** 15-50 mm diameter, convex to broadly convex, sometimes with a slight umbo in maturity. Surface smooth to slightly viscid when moist, hygrophanous, strongly striate at the margin when wet. Color: ochraceous brown to chestnut brown when fresh, fading to pale tan or straw as it dries.

**Gills (lamellae):** Adnate to adnexed, subdistant to close, narrow. Color: grayish to dark purplish brown with age, with white edges.

**Stipe (stem):** 40-80 mm × 3-6 mm, equal or slightly thicker at base. Hollow, fragile. Surface pruinose near the apex, smooth below. Color: whitish to pale brown, bruising intensely blue where handled. Partial veil evanescent, rarely leaving a fragile annular zone.

**Flesh:** Thin, watery. Color whitish to pale brownish. Bruises blue rapidly, especially at the base of the stipe.

**Odor and taste:** Farinaceous (mealy, flour-like). Taste slightly bitter.

**Spores:** Ellipsoidal to subrhomboidal in face view, slightly thick-walled. Size: 9-12 × 6-8 μm. Color: dark purplish brown in deposit. Basidia 4-spored. Cheilocystidia ventricose to lageniform. Pleurocystidia absent or rare.

**Microscopic features:** Presence of a germ pore on spores; absence of pleurocystidia distinguishes it from some close relatives.

## Distribution and Habitat

**Geographic range:** Southern Mexico (Oaxaca, Puebla, Veracruz, Chiapas) through Guatemala, Honduras, and northern South America (Venezuela, Colombia). Also reported in the southeastern US (Georgia, Alabama) and subtropical Brazil.

**Habitat:** Primarily disturbed areas, especially recent landslides, road cuts, and eroded banks. Also grassy fields, forest margins, cloud forests, and coffee plantations. Gregarious or in small groups, often on clay-rich or volcanic soils.

**Substrate:** Terrestrial, directly on soil. Associated with decomposing grass roots and organic matter. Occasionally on dung-enriched soils, though not strictly coprophilous.

**Elevation:** 800-2,000 m in Mexico and Guatemala.

**Season:** Rainy season, June-October in Mexico. Late summer to autumn (August-November) in the southeastern US.

## Phytochemistry

Psilocybe caerulescens contains the classic psilocybin mushroom alkaloid profile:

**Major alkaloids:**

- **Psilocybin:** 0.15-0.60% dry weight (varies by collection location and maturity)
- **Psilocin:** 0.01-0.15% dry weight
- **Baeocystin:** 0.01-0.10% dry weight (present in most collections)

**Total tryptamine content:** Approximately 0.2-0.8% dry weight, placing it in the moderate potency range among Psilocybe species — less potent than [[psilocybe-mexicana]] under the name *derrumbe* (landslide), referencing their growth habitat.
