Lavender essential oil is among the top-selling [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]], and parts of Australia.

It grows naturally on dry, stony, calcareous soils at elevations of 300–1500 m.

Commercial cultivation is concentrated in Provence (France), Bulgaria, England, and Tasmania.

Optimal conditions include full sun, well-drained alkaline soils (pH 6.5–8.0), and moderate annual precipitation (500–800 mm).

Hardiness ranges from USDA zones 5–9, though winter wetness is more limiting than cold temperatures.

## Chemistry and Pharmacology

The essential oil (1.0–3.0% v/w in dried flowers) has a complex profile of over 150 identified constituents.

Linalool demonstrates anxiolytic-like effects via modulation of GABA-A receptors in animal models.

Inhalation of lavender essential oil reduces cortisol levels and improves sleep quality in clinical studies.

Topical application shows analgesic and anti-inflammatory activity in models of inflammatory pain.

Antimicrobial activity is moderate, with greater efficacy against Gram-positive bacteria than Gram-negative species.

The essential oil demonstrates spasmolytic effects on smooth muscle preparations, supporting traditional use for digestive and respiratory complaints.

The European Medicines Agency recognizes lavender flower preparations as traditional herbal medicines for relief of mild anxiety and sleep disturbances.
