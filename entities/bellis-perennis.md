The species has a long history of use in European folk medicine, where the flowers and leaves have been employed to treat wounds, bruises, and [[fukuoka-ideal-rice-plant-morphology-compact-growth-high-yield]], and varied flower colors. Its ability to tolerate close mowing has made it a persistent component of temperate lawns worldwide.

### Leaf morphology

Leaves are basal, arranged in a compact rosette, and spathulate to obovate. They measure 1–5 cm long and 0.5–2 cm wide, with rounded tips and finely toothed margins.

The dried flowers were steeped in wine or vinegar to create topical liniments for joint pain.

The species was included in the medieval Doctrine of Signatures, where the flower's resemblance to an eye led to its use as a treatment for eye complaints. Culpeper's Complete Herbal (1653) recommends daisy preparations for migraines and visual disturbances.

In modern herbalism, daisy flower tinctures and infusions are used as expectorants, anti-inflammatory agents, and mild diuretics. The young leaves are edible raw in salads or cooked as a potherb, with a slightly bitter, peppery flavor.

The flowers contain triterpenoid saponins, flavonoids (apigenin and luteolin derivatives), and polyacetylenes. Extracts have demonstrated in vitro activity against several bacterial pathogens, supporting traditional wound-healing applications.

Flower buds can be pickled and used as a caper substitute in Mediterranean cuisine. The petals add color to salads and can be infused into vinegars and oils for culinary use.

## Cultivation

B. perennis is readily cultivated from seed or by division of established clumps. Seeds germinate in 10–25 days at 15–20°C without special pretreatment. Established plants spread by rhizomes and can form dense mats in favorable conditions.

Numerous ornamental cultivars are available, including double-flowered forms (e.g., 'Bellissima' series), red-tipped ray varieties (e.g., 'Pomponette'), and dwarf types suitable for rock gardens and containers. Flower colors range from pure white through pink to deep rose.

The 'Habanera' series offers large, semi-double flowers in mixed colors, while 'Tasso' types produce fully double, pom-pom-like blooms on compact plants. These cultivars are widely sold as bedding plants for spring and autumn displays.

The species is hardy to USDA Zone 4 and tolerates both full sun and partial shade. It prefers evenly moist, moderately fertile soils but adapts to a range of conditions. Over-fertilization produces lush foliage at the expense of flowering.
