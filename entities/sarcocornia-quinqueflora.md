## Sarcocornia quinqueflora (Beaded Glasswort, Chicken Claws)

## Description

Sarcocornia quinqueflora, commonly known as chicken claws or beaded glasswort, is a succulent, jointed, leafless halophyte forming extensive colonies on tidal mudflats. The fleshy stems rarely exceed 50 cm in height. It is a characteristic species of salt marsh and mangrove margin communities across southern Australia and New Zealand. The plant's jointed, beaded appearance gives rise to its common names. The related English native Sarcocornia stricta (formerly Salicornia stricta) is a preferred plant for pickling in European cuisine.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Family**: Amaranthaceae (formerly Chenopodiaceae)
- **Genus**: Sarcocornia
- **Species**: S. quinqueflora
- **Common names**: Beaded glasswort, chicken claws, samphire
- **Type**: Succulent perennial herb

## Key Facts

- Growth habit: Prostrate to low-growing succulent, forming extensive colonies
- Height: Rarely exceeds 50 cm
- Stem morphology: Fleshy, jointed, leafless stems with a beaded appearance
- Habitat: Tidal mudflats, salt marshes, mangrove margins
- Distribution: Coastal southern Australia, New Zealand
- Salt tolerance: True halophyte requiring or tolerating high salinity
- Fleshy stems absorb pickle flavours well
- Woody centre within each stem is a nuisance when eating
- Related S. stricta is preferred for pickling in England

## Uses

### Culinary

- Fleshy stems are pickled (samphire-style delicacy)
- Absorbs pickle flavours well due to succulent texture
- The woody inner core must be removed before eating
- Related S. stricta is the preferred English species for pickling
- Can be lightly steamed or blanched as a sea vegetable

### Ecological

- Colonises and stabilises tidal mudflats
- Provides habitat for salt-marsh invertebrates and shorebirds
- Pioneer species in coastal wetland succession
- Bioindicator of healthy salt marsh ecosystems
- Helps trap sediment and build marsh elevation over time

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

Glasswort occupies the most saline zone of coastal properties - areas too salty for any conventional crop. Its significance in permaculture includes:
1. **Zero freshwater requirement**: Grows in seawater-inundated zones
2. **Premium food crop**: Samphire-type product commands high prices
3. **Ecological services**: Stabilises mudflats, supports biodiversity
4. **Living mulch**: Dense colonies suppress weeds in saline zones
5. **Niche production**: Fills the most saline niche in a coastal zonation design

Pairs well with mangroves and other halophytes in a saline food production system.

## Cultivation Notes

- Requires regular tidal inundation with saline water
- Propagates from stem fragments and seed
- Very low maintenance once established
- Harvest young, tender stems for best eating quality
- Remove woody central core before pickling
- Cannot be grown in freshwater or non-saline conditions
- Suitable for constructed saline wetlands [[salicornia-europaea]] (common glasswort, marsh samphire) - widely harvested in Europe
- Suaeda australis (sea blight) - companion halophyte on Australian salt flats
- Sesuvium portulacastrum (sea purslane) - fleshy coastal companion plant
