## Overview

Epipactis helleborine, commonly known as the broad-leaved helleborine, is a perennial terrestrial orchid in the family Orchidaceae.

It is native to Europe and temperate Asia but has become widely naturalized in [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]].

The species is notable for its broad ecological amplitude, colonizing diverse habitats from deep woodlands to urban sidewalks.

Unlike many orchids, Epipactis helleborine thrives in disturbed and anthropogenic environments.

It is considered invasive in parts [[berry-growing]], particularly in the northeastern [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]] and eastern Canada.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Epipactis helleborine was originally described as Serapias helleborine by Linnaeus in 1753.

Crantz transferred the species to the genus Epipactis in 1769.

The genus name derives from the Greek epipaktis, a name used by Theophrastus for a plant possibly in this genus.

The specific epithet helleborine refers to a resemblance to Helleborus, the hellebore genus.

The species belongs to the tribe Neottieae within the subfamily Epidendroideae.

Plants typically grow 30–80 cm tall from a rhizomatous rootstock.

The stem is stout, green to purplish, and bears 2–8 leaves along its length.

Leaves are ovate to elliptic, 5–15 cm long, with prominent parallel venation.

The inflorescence is a one-sided raceme bearing 10–50 small flowers.

Each flower is 10–15 mm across, with greenish sepals and a distinctive purplish-brown labellum.

The labellum is divided into two parts: a basally concave hypochile and a forward-pointing epichile.

The hypochile contains nectar, while the epichile serves as a landing platform for pollinators.

The ovary is inferior and densely glandular-pubescent.

## Distribution and Habitat

Epipactis helleborine is native to Europe, North Africa, and temperate Asia.

In Europe, it occurs from the British Isles and Scandinavia southward to the Mediterranean.

Its native range extends eastward through Russia to Japan and the Korean Peninsula.

The species was first recorded in North America near Syracuse, New York, in 1879.

It has since spread across much of the northeastern [[dmt-maoi-plants-united-states]] and southeastern Canada.

The broad-leaved helleborine colonizes an exceptionally wide range of habitats.

In its native range, it grows in deciduous and coniferous woodlands, shaded ravines, and damp meadows.

In North America, it is frequently found in urban and suburban environments.

Populations occur in parks, gardens, roadsides, railroad embankments, and lawn margins.

The species tolerates both acidic and calcareous soils and grows in full shade to partial sun.

## Ecology

Epipactis helleborine exhibits remarkable pollinator flexibility.

In Europe, primary pollinators include social wasps such as Vespula vulgaris and Vespula germanica.

In Japan, the Asian hornet Vespa crabro serves as the principal pollinator.

In North America, the species is pollinated by a variety of generalist insects including bees and wasps.
