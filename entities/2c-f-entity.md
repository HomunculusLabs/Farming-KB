# 2C-F

**[[2c-b-pihkal-entry-20]] #26** -- 2,5-Dimethoxy-4-fluorophenethylamine

## Chemical Name

2,5-Dimethoxy-4-fluorophenethylamine

## Dosage and Duration

- **Dosage:** greater than 250 mg

- **Duration:** unknown

## Qualitative Effects

2C-F is one of the least active members of the 2C family. Even at 250 mg, effects were described as "slight and uncertain" with possible mild eyes-closed imagery above normal but "certainly not profound." At several hours there was a pleasant lethargy and sleep was completely normal that night.

A number of graded acute dosages were tried, and it was only with amounts in excess of 100 mg that any baseline disturbances were noted. At no dose attempted was there any convincing indication of believable central effects.

## Synthesis

Synthesized from 2,5-dimethoxyaniline via fluoroborate salt formation (Schiemann reaction), pyrolysis to give 2,5-dimethoxyfluorobenzene, formylation via Vilsmeier-Haack reaction to yield 2,5-dimethoxy-4-fluorobenzaldehyde (mp 99-100 degrees C), nitrostyrene formation, and LAH reduction. The hydrochloride salt had a mp of 182-185 degrees C.

## Extensions and Commentary

The three-carbon amphetamine analogue of 2C-F would be called DOF (2,5-dimethoxy-4-fluoroamphetamine). It was prepared via the nitroethane route, with the nitropropene intermediate having a mp of 128-129 degrees C and the final hydrochloride salt a mp of 166-167 degrees C. Animal studies comparing DOF to the highly potent DOI and DOB implied human activity would be some four to six times less than these heavier halide analogues. As of the time of writing, no human trials of DOF had been made.

The extreme inactivity of 2C-F contrasts sharply with its bromine, iodine, and sulfur-containing cousins, highlighting the dramatic effect that the 4-position substituent has on psychedelic potency in the 2,5-dimethoxyphenethylamine framework.

## Chemical Family Context

2C-F belongs to the 2C family of phenethylamines, characterized by the 2,5-dimethoxy-4-substituted pattern on the phenethylamine backbone. The 4-position substituent is the primary variable within this family, and its identity has a profound impact on both potency and qualitative effects.

The 2C [[2c-family-compounds-overview]] ordered by 4-position substituent potency demonstrate a clear trend:

- 2C-H (hydrogen): Inactive (not in PIHKAL as active)

- 2C-F (fluorine): Essentially inactive (>250 mg)

- 2C-D (methyl): Active at 60-100 mg

- 2C-E (ethyl): Active at 10-25 mg

- 2C-B (bromine): Active at 12-24 mg

- 2C-I (iodine): Active at 12-25 mg

The trend roughly follows both atomic size and lipophilicity, with larger, more polarizable substituents generally producing more potent compounds. Fluorine, despite being the most electronegative element, is too small and lacks the polarizability needed for optimal receptor interaction in this context.

## Pharmacological Discussion

The inactivity of 2C-F provides important evidence about the pharmacophore requirements for [[optical-isomers-psychedelic-phenethylamines]]. The 2,5-dimethoxyphenethylamine scaffold requires a sufficiently large and polarizable substituent at the 4-position to achieve significant 5-HT2A receptor agonism.

Fluorine's small atomic radius (approximately 1.35 angstroms, similar to hydrogen) and its extreme electronegativity create unique electronic effects that are not replicated by larger halogens. While fluorine substitution is widely used in medicinal chemistry to improve metabolic stability and membrane permeability, in the specific context of 2,5-dimethoxyphenethylamines, it fails to provide the necessary steric bulk for receptor activation.

This finding has been confirmed by radioligand binding studies showing that 2C-F has very low affinity for 5-HT2A receptors compared to 2C-B, 2C-I, and 2C-E. The compound may retain some affinity for other [[serotonin-receptor-interference-psychedelic-mechanism]] subtypes or for trace amine-associated receptors (TAARs), which could explain the mild subjective effects reported at very high doses.

## Comparison to Related Compounds

- 2C-B (bromine): Dramatically more potent at 12-24 mg, with vivid visual effects

- 2C-I (iodine): Similar potency to 2C-B with a slightly longer duration

- 2C-E (ethyl): Active at 10-25 mg, more cognitive/intellectual character

- 2C-D (methyl): Active at 60-100 mg, milder effects

- 2C-F (fluorine): Essentially inactive at doses up to 250 mg

The progression from fluorine through methyl, ethyl, bromine, and iodine at the 4-position provides one of the clearest demonstrations of steric effects in psychedelic [[phenethylamine-pharmacology]].

## Harm Reduction and Safety Considerations

- 2C-F is not considered a recreational substance due to its lack of significant activity

- The "pleasant lethargy" reported at high doses could be cause for concern if it reflects CNS depression

- Any experimentation should use standard phenethylamine precautions

- The compound has not been evaluated for chronic toxicity
