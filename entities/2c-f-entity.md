# 2C-F

**[[2c-family-compounds-overview]] ordered by 4-position substituent potency demonstrate a clear trend:

- 2C-H (hydrogen): Inactive (not in PIHKAL as active)

- 2C-F (fluorine): Essentially inactive (>250 mg)

- 2C-D (methyl): Active at 60-100 mg

- 2C-E (ethyl): Active at 10-25 mg

- 2C-B (bromine): Active at 12-24 mg

- 2C-I (iodine): Active at 12-25 mg

The trend roughly follows both atomic size and lipophilicity, with larger, more polarizable substituents generally producing more potent compounds. Fluorine, despite being the most electronegative element, is too small and lacks the polarizability needed for optimal receptor interaction in this context.

## Pharmacological Discussion

The inactivity of 2C-F provides important evidence about the pharmacophore requirements for [[serotonin-receptor-interference-psychedelic-mechanism]] subtypes or for trace amine-associated receptors (TAARs), which could explain the mild subjective effects reported at very high doses.

## Comparison to Related Compounds

- 2C-B (bromine): Dramatically more potent at 12-24 mg, with vivid visual effects

- 2C-I (iodine): Similar potency to 2C-B with a slightly longer duration

- 2C-E (ethyl): Active at 10-25 mg, more cognitive/intellectual character

- 2C-D (methyl): Active at 60-100 mg, milder effects

- 2C-F (fluorine): Essentially inactive at doses up to 250 mg

The progression from fluorine through methyl, ethyl, bromine, and iodine at the 4-position provides one of the clearest demonstrations of steric effects in psychedelic [[phenethylamine-pharmacology]].

## Harm Reduction and Safety Considerations

- 2C-F is not considered a recreational substance due to its lack of significant activity

- The "pleasant lethargy" reported at high doses could be cause for concern if it reflects CNS depression

- Any experimentation should use standard phenethylamine precautions

- The compound has not been evaluated for chronic toxicity
