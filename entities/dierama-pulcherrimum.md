## Overview

Dierama pulcherrimum, commonly known as angel's fishing rod or wandflower, is a graceful cormous perennial from southern Africa. It is celebrated for its arching wands of pendulous, bell-shaped pink to magenta flowers.

The long, slender flowering stems bend under the weight of the blooms, creating a fountain-like display. This distinctive growth habit makes it one of the most recognizable ornamental grassland plants.

The species is a staple of perennial borders and waterside plantings in temperate gardens worldwide. It has received the Royal Horticultural Society's Award of Garden Merit for its garden performance.

Several cultivars have been selected for flower color, including 'Blackbird' with deep purple flowers and 'Guinevere' with silvery-pink blooms.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Clade:** Tracheophytes
- **Clade:** Angiosperms
- **Clade:** Monocots
- **Order:** Asparagales
- **Family:** Iridaceae
- **Genus:** Dierama
- **Species:** D. pulcherrimum (Hook.f.) Baker

The species was first described by Joseph Dalton Hooker in 1864 as Sparaxis pulcherrima. John Gilbert Baker transferred it to Dierama in 1876.

The genus name Dierama derives from the Greek word for "funnel," referring to the flower shape. The species epithet "pulcherrimum" means "most beautiful."

Dierama comprises approximately 44 species, all native to sub-Saharan Africa. The genus is placed in the tribe Irideae alongside Iris and Moraea.

## Morphology

Dierama pulcherrimum grows from a small, ovoid corm 15–25 mm in diameter. The corm is covered in brown, fibrous tunics.

Leaves are evergreen to semi-evergreen, narrow, and grass-like. They grow in dense tufts reaching 30–60 cm long and 3–6 mm wide.

Flowering stems are slender, wiry, and arching. They emerge from the leaf tuft and extend 80–150 cm in length.

The inflorescence bears 6–15 pendulous flowers spaced along the upper portion of the stem. Each flower hangs from a slender, curved pedicel.

Individual flowers are bell-shaped, 25–40 mm long, and range from pale pink through deep magenta. The tepals are equal in size and spread slightly at the tips.

The perianth tube is narrow and cylindrical at the base, flaring into the campanulate flower. Stamens are three, with versatile anthers.

The style is slender with three short, recurved stigmatic branches. Capsules are ovoid, 8–12 mm long, and contain numerous small, brown seeds.

Flower color varies from pale pink to deep magenta across the species' range. Some populations produce nearly white flowers, while others are distinctly purple.

## Distribution and Habitat

Dierama pulcherrimum is native to the eastern regions of South Africa. Its range extends from the Eastern Cape through KwaZulu-Natal to the Drakensberg Mountains.

It grows in moist grasslands, along stream banks, and in rocky seeps. Elevations range from 600 to 2400 m, with most populations above 1000 m.

The species prefers areas with high summer rainfall and cold, dry winters. It experiences frost and occasional snow at higher elevations.

Populations are often found in seasonally waterlogged soils. The corms sit at or just below the soil surface in grassland turf.

Flowering occurs from November to February in the southern hemisphere. In the northern hemisphere, bloom time is June through August.

The species is summer-growing, unlike many Cape Iridaceae that follow a winter-rainfall cycle. This difference reflects its occurrence in summer-rainfall regions of eastern South Africa.

## Cultivation

Dierama pulcherrimum requires a sunny position with moist but well-drained soil. It thrives in humus-rich loam that does not dry out completely in summer.

The species is hardier than many South African Iridaceae, tolerating temperatures to about −10 °C. Good drainage is essential in winter to prevent corm rot.

Established clumps resent disturbance. Transplanting mature plants often results in a setback of 1–2 years before flowering resumes.

Propagation is most reliable from fresh seed sown in autumn. Seedlings flower in their third or fourth year.

Division of clumps is possible but should be done in spring. Each division should include at least one growing point and a portion of the root system.

The species benefits from a mulch of leaf mold or compost in spring. Avoid high-nitrogen fertilizers, which promote lush foliage at the expense of flowers.

Staking is generally not needed as the stems are self-supporting. Wind protection prevents tangling of the long flowering wands.

Plants pair beautifully with ornamental grasses and other moisture-loving perennials. They are particularly effective when planted near water features where their reflection doubles the visual impact.

## Ecological Role

The pendulous flowers are pollinated by long-proboscid flies and bees that hover beneath the bells. The flower orientation protects pollen from rain washout at high elevations.

Dierama pulcherrimum is a component of montane grassland ecosystems in the Drakensberg. These grasslands support exceptional plant diversity within a structurally simple vegetation type.

The species provides nectar for mountain-endemic insects during the midsummer flowering period. Its presence indicates intact, moist grassland habitat.

Grassland burning stimulates flowering in many Dierama species. Controlled burns in managed grasslands promote population health and flowering density.

The species is not considered threatened due to its wide range across mountainous terrain. However, overgrazing and grassland degradation affect some lower-elevation populations.

Conservation of montane grasslands is important for the genus as a whole. Several Dierama species have extremely restricted ranges and are vulnerable to habitat change.
