# Cichorium intybus
Cichorium intybus (chicory) is a perennial herb in Asteraceae
native to Europe, North Africa, and Western Asia, now
naturalized worldwide. Valued for edible leaves and roots,
use as a coffee substitute, [[panax-ginseng]] as a bitter
digestive tonic, and its role in [[phosphate-accumulators-sustainable-agriculture]] as
a deep-rooted [[nutrient-dynamic-accumulator-reference]]. Cultivated forms include
radicchio, Belgian endive, and sugarloaf chicory.

## Taxonomy and Naming
- **Kingdom:** Plantae | **Division:** Magnoliophyta
- **Class:** Magnoliopsida | **Order:** Asterales
- **Family:** Asteraceae | **Genus:** Cichorium
- **Species:** C. intybus | **Authority:** L. (1753)
- **Common names:** Chicory, succory, blue daisy, coffeeweed,
  wild endive
- **Etymology:** Greek kichorion; "intybus" from Egyptian
  tybi (January harvest)
- **Cultivars:** Radicchio (Chioggia, Treviso), Belgian
  endive (Witloof), Sugarloaf, Puntarelle

## Botanical Description
### Growth Habit
- **[[bloomfield-fungal-life-cycle-spiral-versus-circle]]:** Perennial (USDA zones 3-9)
- **Height:** 30-120 cm flowering | **Spread:** 30-60 cm
- **Root:** Deep taproot 1-2 m; thick, fleshy, penetrates
  compacted soils
- **Lifespan:** 5-10+ years

### Leaves
- **Basal rosette:** Dandelion-like, 10-25 cm, pinnatifid
  to deeply lobed
- **Stem leaves:** Smaller, lanceolate, clasping
- **Color:** Green to blue-green; radicchio forms develop
  red-purple anthocyanins
- **Taste:** Bitter; milder in spring, stronger after
  flowering

### Flowers
- **Heads:** Solitary or clusters of 2-5; 8-20 blue to
  lavender ray florets; 2.5-4 cm
- **Blooming:** June-October; opens morning, closes midday
  in heat
- **Pollination:** Insect-pollinated; attractive to bees
  and butterflies

### Fruit and Seed
- **Achene:** 2-3 mm, brown, ridged, short scale pappus
- **Production:** 500-2000 seeds/plant; viability 3-5
  years; best germination 20-25°C

## Distribution and Habitat
- **Native:** Europe, North Africa, W/Central Asia
- **Naturalized:** Americas, Australia, NZ, South Africa
- **Habitat:** Roadsides, meadows, disturbed ground
- **Soil:** Wide tolerance; prefers well-drained, fertile;
  tolerates alkaline soils
- **Climate:** Temperate; drought-tolerant, frost-hardy
- **Invasiveness:** Noxious weed in some jurisdictions
