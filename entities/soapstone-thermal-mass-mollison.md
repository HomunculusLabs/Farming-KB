# Soapstone Thermal Mass (Mollison's Energy System)

## Description

Soapstone (steatite) is a naturally occurring metamorphic rock with extraordinary thermal capacity, used historically by Indigenous peoples for cooking and heat storage. Bill Mollison proposes it as a key component of passive solar heating systems, where soapstone absorbs concentrated solar energy and releases it slowly for space heating and cooking.

## Classification

- **Category**: Natural building material / thermal energy storage
- **Mineral type**: Metamorphic rock (steatite / talc schist)
- **Key property**: Exceptional specific heat capacity and thermal mass

## Key Facts

- **Thermal capacity**: One of the highest heat capacities of any natural stone.
- **Indigenous precedent**: American Indians heated soapstone on communal fires, then carried it on green branches to cook for hours.
- **Non-polluting**: Completely passive energy storage with no emissions.
- **Mollison's proposal**: Heat soapstone using fold-out focusing mirrors, bring inside for cooking and heating. Plug-in system allows periodic recharging via truck-mounted solar concentrator.

## Role in Permaculture Energy Systems

- **Solar energy storage**: Paired with focusing mirrors for efficient solar capture.
- **House heating**: Slow radiant heat release over many hours.
- **Cooking**: Retains heat for extended cooking without continuous fuel.
- **Integration**: Combined with methane, ammonia, CO2 systems for comprehensive energy.

## Comparison with Conventional Systems

Mollison contrasts soapstone thermal mass with expensive metal solar collectors ($3,000+), arguing the old Indian trick is both cheaper and more effective. Permaculture finds simple material-based solutions where technological society seeks complex ones.
