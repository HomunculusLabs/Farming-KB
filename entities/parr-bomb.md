## Parr Bomb

## Description

The Parr bomb (Parr pressure reactor) is a sealed, mechanically stirred pressure vessel used in chemical synthesis reactions that require elevated pressure and controlled temperature. In the context of cannabis alchemy, a Parr bomb was specifically employed in the purification of cannabidiol (CBD) from its bis-3,5-dinitrobenzoate derivative using liquid ammonia as a reagent.

## Classification

- **Category**: Laboratory equipment / pressure reactor
- **Type**: Sealed high-pressure reaction vessel
- **Manufacturer**: Parr Instrument Company (Moline, Illinois)
- **Application domain**: High-pressure chemical synthesis, hydrogenation, hydrolysis

## Key Facts

- In the documented CBD purification, liquid ammonia was added to a solution of cannabidiol-bis-3,5-dinitrobenzoate in anhydrous toluene at -70°C inside a Parr bomb.
- The sealed apparatus was mechanically stirred for 5 hours, during which pressure built to 110 psi and temperature rose from -70°C to 20°C.
- This reaction cleaved the dinitrobenzoate protecting groups from cannabidiol, regenerating the free cannabinoid.
- After the reaction, ammonia fumes were released overnight and the product was dissolved in heptane for further purification.
- The reaction produced insoluble 3,5-dinitrobenzamide as a byproduct, removed by filtration.

## Role in Cannabinoid Purification

The Parr bomb step was part of a multi-stage process to obtain ultra-pure cannabidiol:

1. Hashish extracted with ethyl alcohol
2. Chromatographed on Florisil
3. CBD converted to cannabidiol-bis-3,5-dinitrobenzoate for purification
4. **Parr bomb reaction with liquid ammonia** to regenerate pure CBD
5. Additional Florisil chromatography
6. Crystallization from petroleum ether → pure white CBD crystals
7. Isomerization of pure CBD to pure THC using sulfuric acid

## Technical Specifications

- Standard Parr bombs range from 25 mL to several liters
- Rated for pressures up to 2000 psi or higher depending on model
- Equipped with pressure gauges, safety rupture discs, and mechanical stirring
- Constructed from stainless steel or Hastelloy for chemical resistance
- Can be heated externally or with internal electric heaters

## Safety Considerations

- Must be operated within rated pressure limits
- Proper venting procedures required before opening
- Liquid ammonia is extremely hazardous — causes severe burns and is toxic
- All reactions should be performed in a fume hood with appropriate PPE
- Safety rupture discs must be inspected and replaced per manufacturer guidelines
