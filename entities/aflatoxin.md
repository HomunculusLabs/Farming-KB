## Overview

Aflatoxins are a group of structurally related toxic [[cytochrome-p450-enzymes-structure-function-pharmacology]] enzymes (particularly CYP1A2 and CYP3A4):

- **Bioactivation:** Epoxidation at the 8,9-double bond forms aflatoxin B₁-8,9-epoxide (AFBO), the ultimate toxic metabolite
- **DNA adduct formation:** AFBO binds covalently to guanine residues, forming the N⁷-guanine adduct (AFB₁-N⁷-Gua), which can lead to G→T transversion mutations
- **TP53 mutation hotspot:** Aflatoxin exposure is strongly associated with a characteristic AGG→AGT (Arg→Ser) mutation at codon 249 of the TP53 tumor suppressor gene, found in ~50% of hepatocellular carcinomas in aflatoxin-endemic regions
- **Synergism with hepatitis B:** Co-infection with hepatitis B virus (HBV) multiplies the risk of hepatocellular carcinoma by 30-fold or more compared to either risk factor alone
- **Immunosuppression:** Aflatoxins impair both humoral and cell-mediated immune responses, reducing resistance to infectious diseases

Acute aflatoxicosis presents with abdominal pain, vomiting, hepatitis, and in severe cases fulminant liver failure and death. Chronic low-level exposure is the primary concern for carcinogenesis and growth impairment in children.

## Agricultural Impact and Prevention

Aflatoxin contamination can occur at every stage of the food supply chain — pre-harvest in the field, during harvest, and in post-harvest storage:

- **Pre-harvest factors:** Drought stress, insect damage, and high temperatures increase plant susceptibility to Aspergillus colonization
- **Post-harvest factors:** Improper drying, high moisture content (>12%), and poor storage ventilation promote fungal growth
- **Regulatory limits:** FDA action levels for aflatoxin B₁ in human food range from 20 ppb (most foods) to 0.5 ppb in milk (due to the carcinogenic Aflatoxin M₁ metabolite)
- **EU limits:** Stricter standards at 2–12 ppb depending on the commodity

Prevention and mitigation strategies include:

- **[[mushroom-pests-insect-control-stamets]]
- **Nixtamalization:** Traditional Mesoamerican alkaline cooking of maize reduces aflatoxin levels by 80–95%
- **Binders and adsorbents:** Clay minerals (e.g., [[bentonite]], NovaSil™) added to animal feed can sequester aflatoxins in the gastrointestinal tract
- **Breeding for resistance:** Development of with reduced susceptibility through marker-assisted selection

## Health Effects in Humans

Human exposure to aflatoxins spans a continuum from acute poisoning to chronic disease:

- **Acute aflatoxicosis:** Rare but devastating outbreaks have been documented in Kenya (2004, 2005), India (1974), and Uganda, with case fatality rates exceeding 60% in severe outbreaks involving contaminated grain
- **Childhood stunting:** Chronic aflatoxin exposure in children is associated with impaired growth, underweight, and kwashiorkor independent of caloric intake, likely through immune dysfunction and nutrient malabsorption
- **Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC):** Aflatoxin B₁ is the leading dietary cause of liver cancer in developing nations, responsible for an estimated 4.6–28.2% of HCC cases globally
- **Reproductive toxicity:** Animal studies demonstrate embryotoxicity and teratogenicity at high doses; human data are limited but suggest associations with low birth weight
- **Aflatoxin M₁:** The hydroxylated metabolite found in milk of lactating animals (and humans) that consumed contaminated feed, retaining significant carcinogenic potency

## Detection Methods

Accurate detection of aflatoxins requires sensitive analytical techniques:

- **HPLC with fluorescence detection (HPLC-FLD):** Reference method with limits of detection at 0.1–1 ppb, often preceded by immunoaffinity column cleanup
- **ELISA-based rapid tests:** Lateral flow devices and microplate assays for field screening at 1–20 ppb; widely used in developing nations for rapid surveillance
- **LC-MS/MS:** Gold standard for simultaneous multi-mycotoxin quantification and confirmation; enables detection of all four major aflatoxins plus M₁, M₂, and masked metabolites
- **Thin-layer chromatography (TLC):** Low-cost visual screening method using UV fluorescence; still used in resource-limited settings
- **Biosensor approaches:** Emerging technologies including aptamer-based sensors and surface plasmon resonance platforms for rapid, on-site detection
