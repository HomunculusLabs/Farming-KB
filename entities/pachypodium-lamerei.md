## Overview

Pachypodium lamerei, commonly known as Madagascar palm, is a striking, spiny-stemmed succulent tree native to southern Madagascar.

Despite its common name, it is not a palm at all but a member of the dogbane family (Apocynaceae).

The species is notable for its thick, gray, bottle-shaped trunk covered in sharp spines. This swollen trunk is an adaptation to the arid conditions of southern Madagascar.

The trunk serves as a [[desert-water-storage-evaporation]] organ, allowing the plant to survive extended dry periods.

Mature specimens develop a swollen caudex base and a crown of glossy, dark green leaves. The contrast between the armored trunk and soft foliage is visually dramatic.

Large, white, fragrant flowers appear in summer on mature plants, adding considerable ornamental value.

The species is one of the most commonly cultivated Pachypodium species worldwide. It is more cold-tolerant and faster-growing than most other species in the genus.

Pachypodium lamerei is prized by succulent collectors for its dramatic sculptural form.

It adapts well to container culture and makes a distinctive houseplant. Young plants are particularly popular in the nursery trade due to their compact size.

## Taxonomy [[trichome-development-and-morphology]]

**Kingdom:** Plantae
**Family:** Apocynaceae
**Genus:** Pachypodium
**Species:** Pachypodium lamerei Drake

The species was described by Emmanuel Drake del Castillo in 1899. The epithet honors the French naturalist Lamere.

Plants develop an unbranched or sparsely branched trunk reaching 3–8 m tall in habitat. In cultivation, heights of 1–3 m are typical.

The trunk is thick, gray-green to silvery, and covered with prominent paired spines 2–6 cm long. Spines are conical, sharp, and arranged in spiral rows along the stem.

Leaves are simple, glossy, dark green, and oblanceolate, 15–30 cm long.

They cluster at the stem apex in a rosette and are deciduous during the dry season. Leaf drop occurs [[query-how-do-i-manage-mosquitoes-naturally-in-my-permaculture-pond]] response to drought and cool temperatures.

Flowers are large, white, salverform, and fragrant, measuring 8–12 cm across.

The corolla tube is yellowish-white with five spreading lobes. Blooming typically occurs in late spring to midsummer on mature plants 5+ years old.

All parts of the plant contain toxic cardiac glycosides.

The milky sap is poisonous if ingested and can cause skin irritation. Handle with care and keep away from [[children-and-mushroom-discrimination]] pets.

## [[seasonal-distribution-and-habitat-of-wild-mushrooms]]

Pachypodium lamerei is endemic to southern and southwestern Madagascar. It grows in the spiny forest and dry deciduous forest ecoregions.

The species occurs on limestone plateaus, rocky outcrops, and well-drained sandy soils. Elevations range from sea level to approximately 750 m.

The native climate is strongly seasonal, with a hot, wet season (November–March) and a long, dry season (April–October). Annual rainfall is typically 300–600 mm.

Temperatures in the habitat range from 15–35 °C year-round, with minimal frost risk. The species grows in full sun in open woodland and exposed rock faces.

Pachypodium lamerei shares its habitat with other Madagascan endemics including Alluaudia species and Didieraceae shrubs. The vegetation is adapted to extreme drought and poor soils.

## Cultivation

Pachypodium lamerei is a popular container plant grown for its architectural form and relatively easy care. It requires full sun, excellent drainage, and a well-draining cactus or succulent mix.
