# Sesbania grandiflora
Sesbania grandiflora (agati, hummingbird tree) is a fast-
growing nitrogen-fixing legume tree in Fabaceae native to
Southeast Asia and Northern Australia. Widely cultivated in
the tropics for edible flowers, leaves, and pods, fodder,
medicine, and agroforestry. Notable for extremely rapid
growth (up to 8 m/year), waterlogging tolerance, and prolific
large showy flowers popular in South/Southeast Asian cuisine.
One of the most productive multipurpose tropical trees.

## Taxonomy and Naming
- **Kingdom:** Plantae | **Division:** Magnoliophyta
- **Class:** Magnoliopsida | **Order:** Fabales
- **Family:** Fabaceae | **Subfamily:** Faboideae
- **Genus:** Sesbania | **Species:** S. grandiflora
- **Authority:** (L.) Pers. (1807)
- **Basionym:** Aeschynomene grandiflora L.
- **Common names:** Agati, hummingbird tree, vegetable
  hummingbird, West Indian pea, katurai, corkwood tree
- **Varieties:** White-flowered (common), Red-flowered
  (var. coccinea, less common)

## Botanical Description
### Growth Habit
- **Life cycle:** Perennial (often biennial in cultivation)
- **Height:** 5-15 m; typically 8-12 m at maturity
- **Growth rate:** 3-5 m in 6 months, up to 8 m in year one
- **Trunk:** 10-25 cm diameter | **Crown:** Open, umbrella
- **Lifespan:** 5-20 years
- **Roots:** Extensive Rhizobium nodules; deep taproot +
  surface roots adapted to waterlogging

### Leaves
- **Type:** Pinnately compound, alternate, 15-30 cm rachis
- **Leaflets:** 10-25 pairs, oblong, 1-2.5 cm, bright green
- **Movement:** Nyctinastic (fold at night); mild thigmonasty
- **Nitrogen content:** 3-4% N on dry weight basis

### Flowers
- **Inflorescence:** Axillary racemes, 2-4 pendulous
  flowers per cluster
- **Size:** 7-10 cm; papilionaceous with reflexed standard
- **Color:** White (common) or deep red/salmon (var.
  coccinea)
- **Blooming:** Year-round in tropics; heaviest in wet
  season
- **Pollination:** Bird-pollinated (sunbirds); also bees
- **Production:** Hundreds daily on mature trees

### Fruit and Seed
- **Pod:** Linear, pendulous, 20-60 cm × 6-8 mm; green to
  brown/woody at maturity
- **Seeds:** 15-50 per pod, 3-5 mm, olive-brown
- **Viability:** 2-3 years; scarification improves
  germination
- **Dehiscence:** Explosive when dry

## Distribution and Habitat
- **Native:** Malaysia, Indonesia, Philippines, N. Australia
- **Naturalized:** Tropical Africa, Americas, Caribbean,
  Pacific Islands, South Asia
- **Climate:** Tropical/subtropical; optimal 22-35°C;
  killed by frost below 0°C
- **Rainfall:** 1000-3000 mm; tolerates seasonal drought
  with irrigation
- **Soil:** Saline, alkaline, and waterlogged soils tolerated
- **Altitude:** Sea level to 800 m
- **Key adaptation:** Thrives in prolonged waterlogging where
  few productive trees survive

## Phytochemicals
- **Saponins:** Triterpenoid saponins in leaves and seeds;
  mild raw-seed toxicity reduced by cooking
- **Flavonoids:** Kaempferol, quercetin, isorhamnetin
  glycosides; antioxidant
- **Tannins:** Condensed tannins protect protein in
  ruminant digestion; reduce bloat
- **Alkaloids:** Sesbanine (trace in seeds)
- **Mucilage:** Polysaccharide gums in bark and seeds

## Medicinal Uses
- **Headaches/colds:** Flower decoction for headache and
  nasal congestion across SE Asia
- **Anti-inflammatory:** Leaf poultices for swollen joints;
  extracts active in animal models
- **Diabetes:** Leaf extracts show hypoglycemic effects
- **Antimicrobial:** Active against gram-positive bacteria
  and some fungi
- **Eye health:** Flower juice for eye infections
- **Fever:** Bark decoction as antipyretic in Ayurveda
- **GI:** Bark mucilage for dysentery and diarrhea

## Permaculture and Agroforestry
- **N fixation:** 40-100 kg N/ha/year; among the highest-
  fixing fast-growing legume trees
- **Windbreak:** Rapid establishment as temporary windbreak
  while slower trees mature
- **Shade:** Light canopy for understory crops, coffee, cacao
- **Living fence:** Sprouts from fence posts; provides
  barrier and fodder simultaneously
- **Paddy bunds:** Widely planted on rice bunds in SE Asia;
  no competition with rice crop
- **Waterlogged sites:** Best species for seasonally flooded
  areas where few trees will grow
- **Bee forage:** Nectar during scarce periods
- **Soil improvement:** Litter adds N and organic matter;
  roots break compacted soil layers
## Fodder and Livestock
- **Palatability:** Highly palatable to cattle, goats, sheep
- **Protein:** 20-28% crude protein (DM basis), comparable
  to high-quality alfalfa
- **Digestibility:** 60-70% in vitro DMD; improved with
  wilting or ensiling
- **Tannins:** Reduce bloat and improve protein utilization
- **Systems:** Cut-and-carry, zero-grazing, rice straw
  supplement; up to 10-15% of rabbit/poultry rations
- **Seeds:** Avoid in feed due to higher saponin content
## Cultivation
- **Seed:** Direct or nursery; scarify 80°C water 3-5 min;
  Rhizobium inoculation recommended
- **Spacing:** 1-2 m within rows, 2-3 m between
- **Cuttings:** Stem cuttings root readily in moist conditions
- **Pruning:** Cut at 0.5-1 m; regrows rapidly; prune
  every 2-3 months in fodder systems
- **Pests:** Relatively pest-free; pod-borers on seed
