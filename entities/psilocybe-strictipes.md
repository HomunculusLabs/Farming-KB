# Psilocybe strictipes

## Overview

*Psilocybe strictipes* is a bluing psilocybin-containing mushroom in the
family Hymenogastraceae, closely related to *Psilocybe semilanceata*
(liberty cap) and frequently confused with it in the field.

Found across temperate Europe in nutrient-poor grasslands and pastures,
the species is named for its characteristically straight, rigid stipe
(Latin *strictipes*, "straight foot").

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Hymenogastraceae
- **Genus:** Psilocybe
- **Species:** P. strictipes

Described by R. Singer and A.H. Smith in 1958.

Some authorities have treated it as a synonym of *P. semilanceata*, but
molecular phylogenetic data support its status as a distinct species.

## Morphology

### Cap (Pileus)

- 5–25 mm diameter, conic to campanulate, sometimes broadly convex
with age.
- Hygrophanous: ochraceous brown when moist, fading to straw-yellow
or pale buff when dry.
- Surface viscid when wet from a separable gelatinous pellicle.
- Margin often translucent-striate at maturity; umbo small or absent.

### Gills (Lamellae)

- Adnate to adnexed, subclose to close.
- Grayish at first, developing dark purplish-brown color at maturity.

### Stipe (Stem)

- 40–90 mm long, 1.5–3 mm thick, characteristically straight
and rigid — the key field mark separating it from *P. semilanceata*.
- Equal to slightly thickened at the base, pale yellowish.
- Bruises blue to blue-green where handled.
- Thin, fragile partial veil sometimes leaving a fugacious annular zone.

### Spores

- Spore print: dark purplish-brown.
- Spores: ellipsoid, 10–13 × 6.5–8.5 µm.
- Basidia: 4-spored, clavate.

## Distribution and Habitat

- **Geographic range:** Temperate Europe (British Isles, France,
Germany, Netherlands, Scandinavia, Central Europe); also reported
from the Pacific Northwest of North America.
- **Habitat:** Grasslands, pastures, moorland edges, ruderal grassy
areas.
- **Substrate:** Terrestrial, on soil with decaying organic matter,
often in dung-enriched or fertilized grassland.
- **Season:** Late summer to early winter (August–November).
- **Fruiting:** Solitary to scattered, occasionally in small clusters.

## Bioactive Compounds

- **Psilocybin:** 0.2–0.6% dry weight (variable by specimen).
- **Psilocin:** Trace to 0.1% dry weight.
- **Baeocystin:** Present in minor amounts.

## Pharmacology

Psilocybin acts primarily as a 5-HT2A receptor agonist, with additional
affinity for 5-HT1A and 5-HT2C receptors.

Subjective effects are comparable to *P. semilanceata* but generally
milder at equivalent doses, reflecting lower average psilocybin content.

## Effects and Dosage

- **Threshold:** 0.5–1.0 g dried
- **Light:** 1.0–2.0 g dried
- **Moderate:** 2.0–3.5 g dried
- **Strong:** 3.5–5.0 g dried

Effects include visual distortion, altered time perception, euphoria,
and introspective thought. Duration: 4–6 hours.

## Comparison with *Psilocybe semilanceata*

| Feature | *P. strictipes* | *P. semilanceata* |
|---------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Stipe | Straight, rigid | Often curved |
| Umbo | Small or absent | Distinct papilla |
| Cap shape | Conic-campanulate | Conic with prominent nipple |
| Psilocybin | Lower average | Higher average |

## Ecological Role

As a saprotroph, *P. strictipes* decomposes organic matter in grassland
soils, contributing to nutrient cycling.

## Legal Status

Psilocybin and psilocin are Schedule I under the 1971 UN Convention on
Psychotropic Substances.

- **United States:** Schedule I (Controlled Substances Act).
- **Netherlands:** Prohibited under the Opium Act since 2008.
- **UK:** Class A (Misuse of Drugs Act 1971).
- **Germany:** Controlled under BtMG.

## Related Species

- *Psilocybe semilanceata* — most commonly confused; curved stipe,
prominent umbo.
- *Psilocybe pelliculosa* — North American, smaller stature.
- *Psilocybe liniformans* — gelatinous gill edge distinguishes it.
- *Psilocybe fimetaria* — found on dung, more robust.
