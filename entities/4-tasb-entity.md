# 4-TASB

4-TASB (4-thioasymbescaline; 3-ethoxy-4-ethylthio-5-methoxyphenethylamine) is a phenethylamine described in [[2c-b-pihkal-entry-20]] #147.

It carries both ethoxy and ethylthio substituents on the benzene ring, making it a member of the thioasymbescaline (TASB) family.

4-TASB is notable for producing reports of significant physical discomfort and neurological overstimulation, earning a "DO NOT REPEAT" assessment from an experienced subject.

## Dosage and Duration

Dosage range: 60 to 100 milligrams.

Duration: 10 to 15 hours.

## Qualitative Effects

At 60 milligrams, the compound has a "petroleum-refinery type taste."

Loose bowels develop during onset.

The experience is described as one of these "What is it" or "What isn't it" compounds where the subject had to push erotic, visual, and psychedelic effects to document that the material was indeed effective.

The overall assessment: "I am not impressed."

At 100 milligrams, trivial physical problems occur during early stages.

Fantasy content develops with music and some "jumpy" responses to music are noted.

Neurological hyperreflexia is suspected.

Sleep is possible at the 12-hour point but the subject feels quite irritable, agitated, and twitchy.

The experience is described as "very intense" with incomplete comfort even after the nominal endpoint.

A second 100-milligram report is more alarming.

Music is lovely but pictures are not exciting.

"Feelings that my nerve-endings were raw and active" develop, along with water retention, heartbeat irregularity, and respiration irregularity.

During attempts to sleep, eyes-closed fantasies become "extremely negative."

The subject could "actually feel the continuous electrical impulses travelling between my nerve endings."

Continuous erotic arousability occurs as part of "the same over-sensitivity of the nervous system," but orgasm does not soothe the feeling of vulnerability.

The conclusion: "This is a very threatening material. DO NOT REPEAT."

## Chemical Context

4-TASB is the 4-thio derivative of asymbescaline, with an ethylthio group replacing the ethoxy group at the 4-position.

The asym-bescaline substitution pattern (3-ethoxy-5-methoxy with the third substituent at position 4) is less common than the symmetrical mescaline pattern.

The synthesis was notably challenging, involving benzyne chemistry, lithium-halogen exchange, and careful purification through distillation and recrystallization.

Gas chromatographic analysis confirmed isomeric purity of the final product.

The [[four-hydroxyindole-psilocin-synthesis-route-shirota]] passed through 3-ethoxy-2-(ethylthio)anisole, bromination, benzyne-mediated cyanation with acetonitrile, and final reduction with lithium aluminum hydride.
