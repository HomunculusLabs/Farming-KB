## Description

Marpa Chökyi Lodrö (1012–1097), known as "Marpa the Translator," was a Tibetan Buddhist teacher who rendered Indian Buddhist texts into Tibetan, thereby preserving them from extinction. He is mentioned in *The [[leary-psychedelic-experience-bardo-mapping]]* as a historical parallel to W.Y. Evans-Wentz — both served as bridges transmitting esoteric teachings from one culture to another. Marpa translated Sanskrit Buddhist texts from India into Tibetan, while Evans-Wentz translated Tibetan texts into English.

## Classification

- **Category:** Person — Buddhist teacher / Translator
- **Tradition:** Tibetan Buddhism (Kagyu school)
- **Historical period:** 11th century
- **Nationality:** Tibetan

## Key Facts

- Lived 1012–1097 CE
- Made three journeys to India to study with Indian Buddhist masters, particularly Naropa
- Translated numerous Sanskrit Buddhist texts into Tibetan, preserving teachings that were later lost in India
- Founded the Kagyu lineage of Tibetan Buddhism
- Student of Naropa; teacher of Milarepa, one of Tibet's most famous yogis
- Leary compared Evans-Wentz's role to Marpa's: "The Oxford scholar, like his great predecessor of the eleventh century, Marpa ('The Translator'), who rendered Indian Buddhist texts into Tibetan, thereby preserving them from extinction, saw the vital importance of these doctrines and made them accessible to many"
- This comparison frames the transmission of consciousness-expanding knowledge as a recurring historical pattern

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Marpa's role in preserving and transmitting esoteric knowledge about consciousness states is historically connected to the chain of textual transmission that eventually produced modern psychedelic frameworks. The Kagyu lineage he founded emphasizes direct experiential practices (particularly the Six Yogas of Naropa) that include techniques for navigating altered [[mckenna-food-gods-designer-states-of-consciousness]]. These practices share phenomenological features with [[psilocybin-mushroom]] experiences, including [[default-mode-network-and-ego-dissolution]], visionary states, and the importance of guided preparation. Understanding this lineage helps contextualize [[archaeology-of-psychedelic-mushroom-use]] within a longer history of structured consciousness exploration.

## Historical Context

Marpa was a householder (not a monk) who combined scholarly translation work with tantric Buddhist practice. His translation work was instrumental in establishing the Kagyu school as one of the major traditions of Tibetan Buddhism. His most famous student, Milarepa, is one of the most revered figures in Tibetan religious history. The Marpa-Naropa-Milarepa lineage represents one of the most important transmission lines of esoteric Buddhist practice.
