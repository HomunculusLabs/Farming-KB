---# Spilanthes acmella

**Spilanthes acmella** (L.) Murr., commonly known as the **toothache plant**, **paracress**, **buzz button**, or **Szechuan buttons**, is an annual herbaceous plant in the family Asteraceae. Native to the tropics of Brazil and Peru, it is widely naturalized across tropical and subtropical regions worldwide. The plant is prized for its potent tingling, numbing sensation caused by the alkylamide spilanthol, making it both a culinary curiosity and a traditional remedy for toothaches and oral inflammation.

## Botanical Description

- **Growth habit:** Erect, branching annual herb, 30–60 cm tall
- **Stem:** Herbaceous, green to purplish, slightly hairy
- **Leaves:** Opposite, ovate to triangular, 3–7 cm long, serrate margins, dark green
- **Flowers:** Distinctive globular flower heads, 1–2 cm diameter, yellow-orange central cone surrounded by a ring of pale yellow ray florets; the cone is the most bioactive part
- **Seeds:** Small dark achenes, 2–3 mm
- **Roots:** Fibrous, shallow, suitable for container cultivation
- **Pollination:** Insect-pollinated, attractive to butterflies and [[psilocybe-southeast-asia-pacific]], the Caribbean, and parts of the southern [[permaculture-designers-manual-essential-oil-plants]]:** Rich in (E)-β-caryophyllene, limonene, and germacrene D
- **Tannins:** Astringent polyphenols supporting traditional use for gum health
- **Phytosterols:** Including β-sitosterol and stigmasterol

## Traditional and Medicinal Uses

- **Toothache relief:** The primary traditional use globally — chewing fresh flower heads produces rapid localized numbing of oral tissues; spilanthol acts as a topical anesthetic by blocking voltage-gated sodium channels
- **Oral health:** Used as a mouthwash or chewed for gingivitis, periodontal discomfort, and halitosis; antimicrobial activity against oral pathogens including Streptococcus mutans
- **Sialagogue:** Stimulates saliva production, useful for dry mouth (xerostomia)
- **Anti-inflammatory:** Alkylamides inhibit NF-κB signaling and COX-2 expression, comparable in some assays to NSAIDs at equivalent concentrations
- **Immunomodulatory:** In vitro studies show enhanced phagocytic activity and lymphocyte proliferation
- **Insecticidal:** Spilanthol exhibits contact toxicity against mosquitoes (Aedes aegypti) and agricultural pests; used as a botanical insecticide in traditional farming
- **Stomatitis and aphthous ulcers:** Traditional gargles for mouth ulcers in Indian (Ayurvedic) and Brazilian folk medicine

## Culinary Applications

- **Buzz buttons:** Raw flower heads used in high-end cuisine and molecular gastronomy for their tingling, electric sensation on the tongue
- **Salads and garnishes:** Leaves and flowers added to salads for a peppery, numbing kick
- **Chutneys and condiments:** Used in Brazilian and Indian regional cuisine
- **Beverages:** Infused as a tea or tincture; used in cocktails for sensory novelty
- **Spice substitute:** Dried and ground flower heads used as a pepper substitute in some regions

## Cultivation and Permaculture Role

- **Propagation:** Easy from seed; direct sow after last frost, or start indoors 4–6 weeks before transplant; seeds germinate in 7–14 days at 20–25°C
- **Spacing:** 20–30 cm apart in rows or broadcast as a ground cover
- **Watering:** Moderate; drought-tolerant once established but performs best with consistent moisture
- **Harvest:** Flower heads harvested 60–90 days from sowing; continuous harvest through the season encourages further flowering
- **[[candida-albicans]] and dermatophytes, supporting potential use in oral thrush management
- **Neuroprotective potential:** Preliminary in vitro studies suggest alkylamides may modulate TRP channels (TRPA1, TRPV1) involved in pain signaling, opening avenues for neuropathic pain research
