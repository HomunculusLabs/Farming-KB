## Overview

*Campanula cervicaria* is a perennial herbaceous bellflower native to central and eastern Europe. Its common name, the bristly bellflower, refers to the stiff, white hairs that cover the stems and leaves.

The species produces dense, cylindrical inflorescences of pale blue to nearly white flowers in mid to late summer. It occupies dry meadows, forest clearings, and rocky slopes across its European range.

*Campanula cervicaria* is less commonly cultivated than other European bellflowers but is valued by native plant enthusiasts for its distinctive upright habit. It is considered declining in several western European countries due to grassland improvement and abandonment.

The species has a diploid chromosome count of 2n = 34. It is closely related to *[[campanula-glomerata]]* and shares the character of clustered, sessile flowers in dense terminal heads.

Hybridization with *Campanula glomerata* has been reported where the two species co-occur in central Europe. The hybrids show intermediate hair density and flower head morphology but are generally sterile.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:**
- Kingdom: Plantae
- Family: Campanulaceae
- Genus: *Campanula*
- Species: *C. cervicaria* L.
- Section: *Campanula* sect. *Campanula*

The species was described by Linnaeus in 1753. The specific epithet *cervicaria* derives from the Latin *cervus* (deer), possibly referring to the rough, bristly hairiness of the stems. No widely accepted synonyms exist, though *C. cervicaria* f. *albiflora* has been described for white-flowered variants.

The plant grows 30–80 cm tall with an erect, unbranched stem covered in stiff, spreading white hairs. The stem is robust and solid, giving the plant a rigid, upright appearance even in strong wind.

Basal leaves are broadly lanceolate to ovate, 5–12 cm long, with crenate to dentate margins and long petioles. Stem leaves are smaller, sessile, and progressively narrower toward the apex.

The inflorescence is a dense, cylindrical to ovoid head, 3–8 cm long, composed of numerous sessile flowers. The head is subtended by leafy bracts that are longer than the individual flowers.

Corollas are campanulate, 12–16 mm long, pale blue to nearly white, with five shallow lobes. The calyx lobes are linear-lanceolate, hairy, and about half the length of the corolla.

Capsules are ovoid, 4–6 mm long, opening by three pores near the base. Seeds are light brown, ellipsoid, and approximately 0.6 mm long.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Campanula cervicaria* is distributed across central and eastern Europe, from eastern France and southern Germany eastward through Poland, the Czech Republic, Slovakia, Hungary, Romania, and the Baltic states. Isolated populations occur in southern Scandinavia and western Russia.

The species reaches its northern limit in southern Sweden and its eastern limit in the Urals. It is absent from the Mediterranean region and the British Isles, where it is listed as an extinct casual.

It grows at elevations from 50 to 1,400 meters. Preferred habitats include dry to mesic meadows, forest margins, clearings, and rocky slopes on calcareous or neutral soils.

*Campanula cervicaria* is a weak competitor in tall, rank vegetation and relies on moderate disturbance to maintain open conditions. Historically, traditional mowing and light grazing created suitable habitat throughout its range.

The species prefers full sun to partial shade and tolerates seasonal drought once established. It is winter-hardy to USDA zone 4.

Soil pH preferences range from neutral to mildly alkaline (pH 6.5–8.0). The root system is fibrous and relatively shallow, concentrating in the top 15 cm of soil.

## Ecology

Flowers are pollinated primarily by bees and hoverflies (Syrphidae). The dense flower head presents a concentrated nectar resource that attracts a range of generalist pollinators over an extended bloom period.

Vegetative spread is limited, with plants forming tight clumps that slowly expand through short rhizome growth. Clonal patches rarely exceed 15–20 cm in diameter.

In native meadow communities, *Campanula cervicaria* associates with *[[briza-media]]*, *Salvia pratensis*, *[[knautia-arvensis]]*, and *Betonica officinalis*. It is characteristic of species-rich mesic grasslands classified under the alliance *Cynosurion cristati*.

The species shows no tendency toward weediness or invasion outside its native range. Herbivory by insects is minimal, though occasional damage from leaf-mining flies (Agromyzidae) has been observed.

## Cultivation

*Campanula cervicaria* is cultivated primarily in native plant gardens and wildflower meadows. It requires full sun to light shade and well-drained, moderately fertile soil.

Established plants are drought-tolerant but benefit from occasional irrigation during prolonged dry periods. Heavy, waterlogged soils cause root rot and should be avoided.

Propagation from seed is straightforward when cold-stratified. Seeds are best sown in autumn in [[query-how-do-i-choose-between-a-cold-frame-row-cover-and-low-tunnel]], with germination occurring the following spring.

Division of mature clumps is possible in early spring or autumn. Each division should include several growth buds and a portion of the rhizome system.

Plants flower in their second or third year from seed. The flowering period extends from June through August, with individual heads remaining in bloom for 3–4 weeks.
