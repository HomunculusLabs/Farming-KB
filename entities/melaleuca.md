# Melaleuca

## Description

*Melaleuca* is a genus of trees and shrubs in the family Myrtaceae, commonly known as paperbarks or honey myrtles. Mollison mentions *Melaleuca* as part of the natural tea-tree vegetation found in swampy valleys of Australia, noting that these plants are killed by salt but can be re-established if uphill salinity effects are controlled through [[water-management]].

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Family:** Myrtaceae
- **Genus:** *Melaleuca*
- **Common names:** Paperbark, honey myrtle, tea tree (some species)
- **Growth habit:** Trees and shrubs, 2–25 m tall
- **Species diversity:** Approximately 300+ species, predominantly Australian

## Ecological Role

Mollison groups *Melaleuca* with other natural valley vegetation genera:

- **[[dryland-salting-mollison]] — death of natural *Melaleuca* stands signals rising water tables
- Can be **re-established** once uphill effects are controlled through interceptor banks
- Naturally occupies freshwater swamp and wetland habitats

## Habitat and Distribution

- Almost entirely Australian, with a few species in Malesia and New Caledonia
- Dominates freshwater swamps, melaleuca woodlands, and seasonally inundated areas
- Characteristic papery bark provides fire protection and habitat for wildlife
- Often the dominant canopy species in coastal and inland wetland communities
- Plays critical hydrological role — absorbing and transpiring large volumes of water

## Key Species

| Species | Common Name | Habitat |
|---|---|---|
| *M. quinquenervia* | Broad-leaved paperbark | Coastal swamps, eastern Australia |

| *M. linariifolia* | Snow-in-summer | Rocky sites, eastern Australia |

## Hydrological Functions

- **High transpiration rates:** Capable of moving large volumes of water from saturated soils to atmosphere
- **Flood mitigation:** Swamp melaleuca stands absorb and slow floodwaters
- **Water filtration:** Root systems filter nutrients and sediments from slow-moving water
- **Groundwater regulation:** Deep roots access and help regulate water table levels

## Role in Salinity Diagnosis

In Mollison's dryland management framework, *Melaleuca* serves as a diagnostic tool:

- **Healthy stands** indicate valley floors with fresh groundwater
- **Dying stands** signal salt mobilization from deforested upslope areas
- **Recovery after treatment** (interceptor banks) confirms effective salinity control
- Loss of paperbark woodlands is a clear indicator of advancing dryland salinity

## Permaculture Applications

- **Bioindicator:** Monitor stand health as early warning of salinity problems
- **Essential oil production:** *M. alternifolia* produces commercially valuable tea tree oil
- **Honey production:** Nectar-rich flowers support apiary operations
- **Wetland restoration:** Pioneer species for freshwater wetland rehabilitation
- **Erosion control:** Dense root mats stabilize wet soils
- **Windbreak:** Tall species provide wind protection in lowland areas

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

*Melaleuca* occupies a critical niche in permaculture dryland design as both a productive species and a salinity bioindicator. Its natural role as the dominant tree of freshwater swamps makes it ideally suited to valley floor plantings in permaculture systems where interceptor banks have restored fresh water conditions. Its death provides early warning of salinity problems; its recovery confirms successful remediation. Designers should retain existing *Melaleuca* stands and use them as living monitors of landscape health.
