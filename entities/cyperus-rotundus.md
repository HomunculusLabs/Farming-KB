## Overview

Cyperus rotundus, commonly known as purple nut sedge or nut grass, is a perennial sedge in the family Cyperaceae native to Africa, southern Europe, and southern Asia.

It is widely regarded as one of the world's most problematic agricultural weeds, infesting over 50 crop species across more than 90 countries.

The plant produces an extensive network of underground tubers that enable it to persist and spread aggressively in cultivated fields.

Despite its status as a weed, C. rotundus has a long history of use in traditional medicine systems including Ayurveda, Traditional Chinese Medicine, and Unani.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Cyperus rotundus was described by Linnaeus in 1753 and is placed in the genus Cyperus within the tribe Cypereae.

The species epithet "rotundus" refers to the rounded shape of the underground tubers, which are the plant's most distinctive feature.

Culms are erect, slender, and triangular in cross-section, typically reaching 15 to 70 centimeters in height.

Leaves are basal, grass-like, and shorter than the culms, measuring 2 to 5 millimeters in width.

The inflorescence is an irregular umbel consisting of 3 to 8 unequal rays bearing reddish-brown to purplish spikelets.

The root system consists of fibrous roots, rhizomes, and chains of tubers that can extend 30 centimeters or more into the soil.

Individual tubers are oblong to spherical, approximately 1 to 2 centimeters in diameter, with a dark brown outer surface and white interior.

A single mature plant can produce hundreds of tubers per season, with each tuber capable of generating a new plant.

## Distribution and Habitat

Cyperus rotundus has achieved a nearly [[copelandia-cyanescens-pantropical-distribution-and-ecology]] is found in tropical, subtropical, and warm [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]] worldwide.

The species is believed to have originated in Africa or India and spread globally through human agricultural activity.

It occurs as a weed in row crops, orchards, pastures, gardens, and disturbed sites across Asia, Africa, the Americas, and the [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-hawaii-pacific-islands]].

C. rotundus tolerates a wide range of soil types including sandy, loamy, and clay soils, from acidic to moderately alkaline pH.

It prefers warm, moist conditions with temperatures between 25 and 35 degrees Celsius but can survive in drier environments using tuber reserves.

The plant is particularly problematic in irrigated agricultural systems where frequent watering promotes tuber germination and rhizome growth.

It is found from sea level to approximately 1,800 meters elevation in tropical mountain regions.

## Invasive Biology

Cyperus rotundus is consistently ranked among the top ten worst weeds globally by [[fukuoka-inductive-deductive-intuitive-reasoning-agricultural-research]] institutions.

The species spreads through both seed production and vegetative reproduction via tubers and rhizomes.

Tubers can remain viable in the soil for up to 6 years, making eradication extremely difficult once a population is established.

New plants emerge from tuber fragments as small as 5 millimeters, meaning even thorough tilling can spread rather than control the weed.

Chemical control is complicated by the plant's waxy leaf surface, which limits herbicide absorption, and the protective effect of the soil on tubers.

C. rotundus competes aggressively with crops for water, nutrients, and light, with yield reductions of 30 to 70 percent reported in heavily infested fields.

The plant's phenotypic plasticity allows it to adapt to diverse environmental conditions and management practices.

Integrated weed management combining cultivation, mulching, crop rotation, and targeted herbicide application is the most effective control strategy.

## Traditional and Medicinal Uses

In Ayurvedic medicine, C. rotundus tubers (known as "musta") are used to treat digestive disorders, fever, and menstrual irregularities.

The tubers contain sesquiterpenes, flavonoids, and alkaloids with demonstrated anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and [[medicinal-mushroom-antimicrobial-properties]].

Traditional Chinese Medicine uses the tuber to regulate qi, alleviate pain, and treat conditions of the digestive and reproductive systems.
