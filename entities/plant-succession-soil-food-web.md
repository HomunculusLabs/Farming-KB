## Plant Succession (Soil Food Web)

## Description

**Plant succession** in the context of [[soil-food-web]] science refers to the progressive series of changes in [[leake-mycorrhizal-carbon-sequestration-plant-community-composition]] that occurs from bare soil to mature forest, and the parallel changes in [[endorhiza-rhizosphere-bulk-soil-microbial-communities]] — particularly the shift from bacterial to fungal dominance. Understanding this continuum is essential for soil food web gardening because each successional stage favors different F:B ratios and therefore supports different plant types.

## Classification

- **Category:** Ecological concept / soil food web principle
- **Related rules:** Soil Food Web Rule #2 (vegetables/annuals/grasses prefer bacterially dominated soils), Rule #3 (trees/shrubs/perennials prefer fungally dominated soils)

## Key Facts

- **Continuum progression:** Pioneer weeds to grasses to small shrubs to soft hardwoods to expanding saplings to mature hardwoods to old-growth conifer forest
- **Driving mechanism:** Bacterial dominance in early succession gives way to fungal dominance as organic litter accumulates, providing nutrients for fungal spore germination
- **Bacterial populations remain constant:** ~100 million to 1 billion per teaspoon across all successional stages
- **Fungal biomass increases** progressively from garden soils (several yards of hyphae per teaspoon) to coniferous forests (1-40 miles of hyphae per teaspoon)
- **Plant lifespan predicts preference:** Plants living one season prefer bacterially dominated soils; plants living a year or more prefer increasingly fungally dominated soils

## The Succession Continuum

### Stage 1: Pioneer Weeds (Bare Soil)
- **F:B ratio:** Strongly bacterially dominated
- **Soil characteristics:** Low organic matter, minimal litter layer
- **Dominant microbes:** Bacteria thriving on simple sugars from early plant exudates
- **Typical plants:** Fast-growing annual weeds, pioneer species

### Stage 2: Grasses and Prairie
- **F:B ratio:** Slightly bacterial to balanced (~0.5:1 to 1:1)
- **Soil characteristics:** Accumulating organic matter, initial litter layer
- **Transition point:** Fungal spores begin finding sufficient nutrients to germinate
- **Typical plants:** Perennial grasses, lawn grasses, prairie plants

### Stage 3: Small Shrubs
- **F:B ratio:** Transitional to fungal dominance
- **Soil characteristics:** Significant organic litter, woody material entering soil
- **Fungal growth:** More lignin and cellulose from woody plants provides fungal food
- **Typical plants:** Berry bushes, small ornamental shrubs

### Stage 4: Soft Hardwoods / Expanding Saplings
- **F:B ratio:** Moderately fungally dominated
- **Soil characteristics:** Deep litter layer, substantial woody debris
- **[[teaming-with-microbes-fungi-and-mycorrhizal-relationships]]:** Longer-lived root systems support mycorrhizal fungal partnerships
- **Typical plants:** Early successional trees (aspen, birch, alder)

### Stage 5: Mature Hardwoods
- **F:B ratio:** Strongly fungally dominated (10:1 to 100:1)
- **Soil characteristics:** Deep organic horizons, complex fungal networks
- **Typical plants:** Maples, oaks, poplars, beech

### Stage 6: Old-Growth Conifer Forest
- **F:B ratio:** Maximally fungally dominated (50:1 to 1000:1)
- **Soil characteristics:** Massive fungal biomass, deep organic layers, acidic pH
- **Typical plants:** Conifers (pine, spruce, fir, hemlock, cedar)

## Mechanisms Driving Succession

### Why Bacteria Dominate Early
- Pioneer plants produce simple exudates (sugars, carbohydrates) that feed bacteria
- Short plant lifespans prevent establishment of mycorrhizal relationships
- Limited organic matter provides insufficient nutrients for fungal spore germination
- Plant litter is primarily cellulose (bacterial food), not lignin (fungal food)

### Why Fungi Dominate Later
- Accumulating organic waste provides nutrients for fungal spore germination
- Longer-lived plants support mycorrhizal partnerships
- Woody plant litter contains lignin that only fungi can efficiently decompose
- Fungi produce organic acids that lower [[soil-ph]], inhibiting nitrifying bacteria and maintaining ammonium dominance
- [[fungal-hyphae]] are fragile and require stable environments to develop

### The Role of Nitrogen Form
- **Early succession (bacterial):** Nitrifying bacteria convert ammonium to nitrate; high pH supports this process; plants receive nitrate
- **Late succession (fungal):** Organic acids lower pH below 7; nitrifying bacteria are inhibited; ammonium remains as ammonium; plants receive ammonium

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

1. **Matching soil to crop:** Understanding succession allows gardeners to create appropriate F:B ratios for their target crops — bacterial for vegetables, fungal for perennials and trees
2. **[[mushroom-cultivation]]:** Fungal-dominant soils (late successional stages) are natural environments for many [[classification-of-cultivated-mushroom-species]]; substrate preparation mimics late-successional soil conditions
3. **Transition management:** Converting a lawn (grass stage) to a perennial garden (shrub stage) or orchard (hardwood stage) requires intentional shifts in soil biology
4. **Restoration ecology:** Rebuilding soil food webs on degraded land follows the successional progression
5. **Forest management:** Understanding why conifer forests are maximally fungally dominated informs [[sustainable-forestry-coppicing-and-woodlot-management]] practices
6. **Planting strategy:** Plant lifespan is a simple heuristic for determining soil biology needs — annuals need bacterial soils, perennials need fungal soils

## Practical Application: Rules of Thumb

- **Short-lived plants (1 season):** Prefer bacterially dominated soils (nitrate nitrogen)
- **Medium-lived plants (1+ years):** Prefer fungally dominated soils (ammonium nitrogen)
- **Long-lived woody plants:** Require strongly fungally dominated soils
- **Conifers:** Need the highest fungal dominance of all cultivated plants
