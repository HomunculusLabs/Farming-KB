## Description

Rhizomorphs are thick, rope-like bundles of parallel hyphae that form during the vegetative growth phase of fungi. In the context of the PF TEK and *Psilocybe cubensis* cultivation, rhizomorphs appear as "strandy" growth patterns during spore germination and substrate colonization. They are a visible indicator of healthy, vigorous mycelial development and are among the first signs of successful spore germination alongside cottony white growth.

## Classification

- **Category:** Mycelial morphological structure
- **Type:** Aggregated hyphal bundle (cord-like)
- **Organism:** Present in *Psilocybe cubensis* and many other fungi
- **Growth Phase:** Vegetative colonization stage
- **Appearance:** White, strand-like, rope-forming

## Key Facts

- Rhizomorphs appear during spore germination in PF TEK jars within 3–5 days at approximately 70°F, alongside cottony white fuzzy growth.
- They are described as "strandy" structures that spread quickly through the vermiculite-based substrate.
- The PF TEK substrate formula specifically promotes rhizomorphic growth because vermiculite allows "mycelial threads [to] stretch across space," unlike grain-only substrates that produce dense mycelial masses.
- Rhizomorphic growth is generally considered an indicator of strong, healthy mycelium with good fruiting potential.
- After 2–3 weeks of colonization, the entire substrate cake turns white as the mycelium fully infiltrates the substrate.
- Rhizomorphs are distinct from tomentose (cottony) growth; both forms are normal but rhizomorphic is often associated with more vigorous strains.

## Formation and Development

In the PF TEK cultivation cycle:

1. **Germination (days 3–5):** Small white fuzzy spots appear at inoculation points, quickly growing and spreading.
2. **Colonization (weeks 2–3):** Cottony white growth and strandy rhizomorphs spread throughout the substrate. The mycelium infiltrates the vermiculite-brown rice flour matrix.
3. **Maturation (post-colonization):** The mycelium continues to consolidate until it has enough food to trigger the fruiting cycle. The cake appears solid white.
4. **Pinning (within 2 weeks post-colonization):** Tiny white pin-like structures appear, followed by small round fungus growths that turn yellow.
5. **Primordia formation:** Tiny worm-like structures with reddish heads — the first mushrooms — emerge from the rhizomorphic network.

## Relationship to Substrate

The PF TEK's use of vermiculite is directly connected to rhizomorph development:

- Vermiculite creates air spaces between particles that allow rhizomorphic strands to extend and branch freely.
- The airy substrate structure mimics natural growing conditions where mycelium must traverse gaps between organic matter.
- In contrast, grain-only substrates produce dense mycelial masses with little air space, limiting rhizomorphic development.
- The "secret" of the PF TEK is that "when grown with vermiculite, the mycelial threads stretch across space."

## Relevance to Cultivation

Understanding rhizomorphs is important for cultivators for several reasons:

- **Growth assessment:** The presence and vigor of rhizomorphic growth is a visual indicator of successful colonization and substrate health.
- **Strain vigor:** Highly rhizomorphic growth typically indicates a vigorous, potentially high-yielding genetic line.
- **Contamination detection:** Any colored growths (pastels to black) appearing alongside or instead of white rhizomorphic mycelium indicate contamination.
- **Senescence indicator:** Declining rhizomorphic vigor across successive generations may indicate genetic senescence, which the PF TEK avoids by using spore inoculation rather than cloning.
- **Bruising:** When handling colonized cakes, bruising results in bluish marks — the mycelium is "resilient and can tolerate a certain amount of handling, but handle it as least as possible."

## Distinction from Related Terms

- **Hyphae:** Individual fungal filaments; the microscopic building blocks
- **Mycelium:** The collective mass of hyphae forming the vegetative body of the fungus
- **Rhizomorphs:** Aggregated bundles of hyphae appearing as visible strands or cords
- **Tomentose growth:** Cottony, fluffy aerial mycelium (complementary growth form)
- **Primordia:** Tiny worm-like early fruiting bodies that develop after full colonization
