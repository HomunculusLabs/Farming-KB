## A.H.R. Buller

Arthur Henry Reginald Buller (1874–1944) was a British-Canadian mycologist considered the greatest experimental mycologist in history. His seven-volume *Researches on Fungi* (1909–1934) established the foundations of fungal spore biology, including the mechanics of spore discharge, spore velocity, and the role of surface tension in ballistospore launch.

## Biography

- Born in Birmingham, England; obtained Ph.D. from University of Leipzig (1899) under William Pfeffer
- Conducted research in Munich, Naples, and Birmingham before emigrating to Canada in 1904
- First professor of botany at the University of Manitoba, Winnipeg — lived in a two-room suite at the McLaren Hotel for 40 years
- Nicknamed "Uncle Regie" by students; renowned as a superb, humorous teacher
- Saturday morning lectures at the farmers' market on cereal crops and fungal diseases
- Laboratory work began at 6:00 AM before classes

## Key Discoveries

### Spore Discharge Mechanics
- Discovered "Buller's drop" — the fluid droplet that forms at the base of basidiospores seconds before discharge
- Demonstrated that the drop is carried with the spore, resolving discrepancies with Stokes' law of falling objects
- Hypothesized surface tension as the driving force in the catapult mechanism — confirmed 70+ years later by modern researchers
- Measured spore fall velocity using an ingenious apparatus: glass-sided chamber, horizontally tilted microscope with calibrated silk threads, and an electric tapper recording on a spinning drum

### Spore Physics
- Applied Stokes' law to microscopic spores, measuring spore size, density, and fall speed
- Studied effects of humidity on spore sedimentation, spore trajectories, and electrostatic charge
- Published in *Nature* and seven volumes of *Researches on Fungi* (1909–1934)

### Personal Life and Eccentricities

- Lifelong bachelor who lived in a two-room suite at the McLaren Hotel for 40 years (monthly bill >$100 CAD — far beyond most professors)
- Never married; possibly gay though no evidence survives in correspondence or folklore; may have decided against marriage during early religious struggles
- Scrapbooks preserved in the Buller Library show early religious themes (chapels, clergymen, poems) later replaced by Great War articles, politics, and even eugenics; continued attending church for hymn singing
- Played piano, memorized Shakespeare and Milton, wrote poems (some about fungi); his relativity limerick published by Punch (1923) appears in The Oxford Dictionary of Quotations
- Polished billiards player who enjoyed sharking young men in Winnipeg bars; housed his own table at the McLaren
- Foraged in a moth-eaten raccoon coat with a four-button black jacket and striped pants cut by a Birmingham tailor — fashionable on arrival in 1904 but a peculiar costume 40 years later
- Crossed the Atlantic 65 times; used voyage time for writing, transcribing lab notes into prose
- Friends with Curtis Gates Lloyd (Cincinnati millionaire mycologist); visited Lloyd's library in 1920 and corresponded until Lloyd's death in 1926

### Fungal Luminescence Research

- Systematically studied bioluminescence in fungi, particularly *Panellus stypticus* (a bracket fungus on tree stumps)
- Rose at 4:00 AM and walked to lab before sunrise for dark-adapted eyes; muffled head in horse blinders during commute to avoid street lamp light
- Photographed using light from fungal mycelia and fruiting bodies; studied dependence on oxygen and temperature
- Found North American strains luminous but English strain non-luminous; when paired on agar, produced hybrid mycelium with luminous and non-luminous sectors

### Rust Fungi and Genetics

- Made major contributions to understanding rust fungi (*Puccinia*) that parasitize cereal crops — life cycles involving four spore types and two host species
- Hypothesized that flies transfer sex cells between rust strains (like insect pollinators); advised plant pathologist John Craigie to study insects on infected leaves — the idea was correct and led to solving the puzzle of rust sexuality
- Investigated sexuality of ink-caps (*Coprinus*) and processes of hyphal fusion and nuclear migration preceding mushroom formation
- Contemporaneous molecular research on mating types and compatibility built on his experiments

### Honors and Retirement

- Elected to the Royal Society of Canada and later the Royal Society of London
- Full title: "A. H. Reginald Buller, F.R.S.; B.Sc. (Lond.); D.Sc. (Birm.); Ph.D. (Leip.); LL.D. (Man., Sask., and Calcutta); D.Sc. (Penn.); F.R.S.C."
- Delivered six evening lectures at Northwestern University (1927): "Recent advances in our knowledge of the fungi, or the romance of the fungi life"
- Retired 1936; university comptroller turned him out of his office, confined him to a single desk, dumped his leather furniture in a rainstorm; faculty club refused his billiard table
- Moved to England in 1939 intending retirement in Birmingham; stranded in New York by outbreak of WWII; returned to Winnipeg and continued writing
- Died January 1944 of a glioblastoma (brain tumor); bequeathed manuscripts to Kew, botanical library to Dominion Rust Research Laboratory
- Cremated in Minneapolis (Winnipeg lacked a crematorium); ashes returned in copper urn; faculty kept urn open at social occasions so colleagues could add cigarette ashes to his remains
- In 1963, the science building was renamed the Buller Biological Laboratories

## Legacy

Buller's work on spore discharge laid the foundation for all subsequent research on basidiomycete spore liberation. The surface tension catapult mechanism he hypothesized was confirmed decades later using high-speed photography, validating his experimental genius. His seven-volume magnum opus remains one of the most comprehensive bodies of experimental mycological work ever produced. His approach complemented [[ingold-cecil-t]]'s work on aquatic spore morphology — together they established the field of [[fungal-spore-biology]].
