# Hysterangium gardneri

## Description

*Hysterangium gardneri* is a sequestrate (false truffle) fungus in the family Hysterangiaceae, order Hysterangiales. It produces hypogeous (underground) fruiting bodies that are roughly globose to irregular in shape, typically 1–4 cm in diameter. The outer peridium is whitish to pale brown, while the gelatinous gleba (internal spore-bearing tissue) is olive-green to dark greenish-brown. As an ectomycorrhizal fungus, it forms symbiotic associations with coniferous trees, particularly [[douglas-fir]] (*[[pseudotsuga-menziesii]]*) and other members of the Pinaceae.

## Classification

## Key Facts

- Demonstrated the ability to degrade **80% of 2,2'-dichlorobiphenyl (PCB)**, tying with *Radiigera atrogleba* for the highest degradation rate among 21 mycorrhizal fungi screened (Donnelley and Fletcher, 1994).
- Outperformed ericoid mycorrhizal species (*Hymenoscyphus ericae*, *Oidiodendron griseum*) in PCB degradation capability.
- Like the related *H. setchellii*, it forms dense mycelial mats in forest soils that can account for a significant fraction of total soil organic biomass.
- Ectomycorrhizal associate primarily of Douglas-fir and other western North American conifers.
- Produces extracellular enzymes capable of breaking recalcitrant chlorinated aromatic compounds.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

*Hysterangium gardneri* is notable for several reasons in mycological research:

- **PCB bioremediation**: Its exceptional PCB degradation ability (80% of 2,2'-dichlorobiphenyl) positions it as a candidate organism for bioremediation of contaminated forest soils and industrial sites.
- **Ecosystem engineering**: Related *Hysterangium* species (notably *H. setchellii*) can comprise 45–55% of total soil organic biomass in some forests (Cromack et al., 1979), suggesting *H. gardneri* may similarly dominate soil fungal communities.
- **Hydrocarbon degradation**: The ability to metabolize aromatic compounds suggests potential for degradation of other environmental contaminants including petroleum hydrocarbons and atrazine.
- **Forest ecology**: As an ectomycorrhizal partner of economically important timber species (Douglas-fir), understanding its physiology and ecology has practical forestry applications.
- **Enzyme chemistry**: The ligninolytic and aromatic-degrading enzyme systems of *Hysterangium* species are of interest for industrial biotechnology applications.

The genus *Hysterangium* represents an important but often overlooked group of ectomycorrhizal fungi that play disproportionate roles in forest soil ecology and have significant potential for bioremediation applications.
