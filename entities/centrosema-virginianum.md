## Overview

**Centrosema virginianum** is a perennial herbaceous vine in the family Fabaceae, commonly known as spurred butterfly pea, Virginia centrosema, or wild blue pea.

It is native to the southeastern United States, the Caribbean, and parts of Central and South America, occurring from Virginia and Missouri south to Florida and west to Texas.

The species is recognized by its twining stems, trifoliolate leaves, and distinctive asymmetric flowers with a prominently curved keel petal.

This unique floral architecture requires large-bodied bee pollinators to trip the flower mechanism, making it an excellent indicator species for native bee populations.

Spurred butterfly pea is valued in native plantings for its attractive flowers, [[query-how-does-rock-dust-improve-soil-fertility]] through [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-caribbean-central-america]], and northern South America, making it one of the more widely distributed Centrosema species.

The species occurs in open woods, thickets, pine flatwoods, disturbed sites, and along fence rows and forest edges.

It tolerates a range of soil types from sandy to clay loams and prefers slightly acidic conditions with pH 5.5–6.5.

Optimal growth occurs in partial shade to full sun with moderate moisture. It is less common in very dry or waterlogged habitats.

Spurred butterfly pea is frequently found climbing through shrubs and low trees in open woodland settings, rarely forming dense ground covers.

## Ecology and Wildlife Value

**Centrosema virginianum** fixes atmospheric nitrogen through root nodules formed by Bradyrhizobium bacteria, contributing to soil fertility in woodland and successional communities.

The flowers are pollinated primarily by large bees, including bumblebees and carpenter bees, which are strong enough to depress the keel and trigger pollen release.

Butterfly pea serves as a larval host for the long-tailed skipper (Urbanus proteus) and the gray hairstreak (Strymon melinus).

The foliage is browsed sparingly by white-tailed deer and rabbits, while the seeds are consumed by quail, wild turkey, and other ground-dwelling birds.

In forest ecosystems, the vine contributes to vertical structural diversity by connecting ground-level vegetation to the shrub layer.

Its late-season flowering provides a nectar source for pollinators after many other native legumes have finished blooming.

## Cultivation and Uses

Spurred butterfly pea is used in native wildflower plantings, pollinator gardens, and [[query-how-to-protect-plants-from-frost-and-extend-the-growing-season]]. Palatability is moderate for cattle and good for wildlife.
