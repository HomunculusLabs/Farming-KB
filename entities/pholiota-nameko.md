# Pholiota nameko

**Type:** Species
**Category:** Basidiomycete
**Also known as:** Nameko, Slimy Mushroom

## Overview

Pholiota nameko, commonly known as Nameko (Japanese for "Slimy
Mushroom"), is a popular edible mushroom native to the cool temperate
highlands of northern Japan, China, and Taiwan. It ranks among the most
widely cultivated mushrooms in Japan, trailing only Shiitake and
Enokitake. Despite its unappealing slimy appearance when raw, it
develops a distinctive, appealing flavor when cooked, with crunchy and
nutty characteristics that make it a staple in miso soups and stir fries.

## Key Details

### Taxonomy and Classification

Kingdom: Fungi
Division: Basidiomycota
Class: [[agar]]icomycetes
Order: Agaricales
Family: Strophariaceae
Genus: Pholiota

Synonyms include Pholiota glutinosa Kawamura. The species was formerly
placed in the genera Collybia and Kuehneromyces. Uniquely among species
described in the source, Nameko possesses a conidial (asexual) stage
that allows single-spore homokaryotic mycelium to produce mushrooms.

### Description

Cap measures 3 to 8 cm, hemispheric to convex becoming plane with age.
The surface is covered with a thick, orangish glutinous slime that is
its most distinctive feature. Gills are white to yellowish, becoming
brown. The stem is 5 to 8 cm, equal, fibrillose, and swollen at the
base. Spores are cinnamon brown, ellipsoid, 4 to 7 x 2.5 to 3.0
micrometers, smooth with a distinct germ pore. Pleurocystidia are
absent. Clamp connections are present.

### Distribution and Habitat

Found in the cool temperate highlands of northern Japan, China, and
Taiwan. It is not known from Europe or North America. Natural habitat
is broad-leaf hardwood stumps and logs in temperate forests, especially
deciduous oaks and beech (Fagus crenata). It is one of the few gourmet
mushrooms known to fruit on conifer wood.

### Cultivation

Cultivated on sterilized supplemented sawdust and/or hardwood logs,
particularly beech, poplar, and oaks using methods similar to Shiitake
production. Logs are partially buried in high peat moss soil. Standard
supplementation uses 20% rice bran on alder sawdust.

Growth parameters:
- Spawn: 75 to 85 degrees F, 95 to 100% RH, 2 weeks
- Primordia: 50 to 60 degrees F, 98 to 100% RH, 7 to 10 days
- Fruitbody: 55 to 65 degrees F, 90 to 95% RH, 5 to 8 days

Produces 2 crops within 60 days, spaced 10 to 14 days apart. First
flush yield is approximately 1 pound from 5-pound sawdust blocks.
Mycelium is whitish, longitudinally radial, becoming light orangish or
tawny. On grain it appears densely cottony white with yellowish to
orangish speckling at maturity.

### Culinary and Nutritional

The glutinous slime disappears upon cooking, revealing a distinct and
appealing flavor profile. Well-cooked Nameko is crunchy and nutty,
making it excellent for stir fries and miso soups. Nutritional profile
per dry weight includes 20.8% protein, 4.2% fat, 66.7% carbohydrate,
8.3% ash, and 6.3% fiber. Vitamins per 100g dry weight: thiamine 18.8
mg, riboflavin 14.6 mg, niacin 72.9 mg. Minerals per 100g dry weight:
calcium 42 mg, potassium 2083 mg, iron 22.9 mg, sodium 63 mg.

### Medicinal Properties

Water and sodium hydroxide extracts demonstrated 60% and 90%
effectiveness against Sarcoma 180 in mice (Ying 1987). The species also
improves resistance to Staphylococcus infections. These properties
contribute to its valued status in traditional Japanese cuisine and
medicine.

## Relevance

Nameko represents a major commercial cultivated mushroom in Asian
markets. Its ability to fruit on conifer wood expands substrate options
for growers. The unique asexual reproduction capability (conidial stage)
makes it scientifically interesting for genetics research. Its dual
culinary and medicinal value exemplifies the functional food concept
central to gourmet and medicinal mycology.
