# Giannozzo Pucci

## Description

Giannozzo Pucci is an Italian environmentalist cited by [[david-holmgren]] in the context of discussing the relationship between tradition and sustainability. In a personal conversation with Holmgren, Pucci offered a nuanced defence of traditional knowledge, distinguishing between "true tradition" — where practitioners understand the functional basis of their practices — and what he called the "shadow of tradition" — practices that persist without understanding of their purpose.

## Classification

- **Type:** Person — environmentalist
- **Nationality:** Italian
- **Context:** Traditional agriculture, sustainability, knowledge systems

## Key Facts

- **True tradition vs. shadow of tradition:** When Holmgren asserted that people within a tradition rarely understand the functional basis of traditional practices, Pucci responded that this was merely the "shadow of tradition," and that in true tradition, people understood exactly why they did things. This distinction is crucial for understanding how traditional agricultural knowledge is transmitted and when it becomes vulnerable to erosion.

- **The Chianina share farmer example:** Pucci illustrated his point with the example of a share farmer in his seventies who ploughed with rare white Chianina bullocks, appeared to dance while forking hay, and used complex seasonal patterns and natural signs to determine sowing times. This practitioner embodied true tradition — integrated, embodied knowledge that connected observation, timing, skill, and productivity.

- **Loss of embodied knowledge:** Holmgren acknowledged Pucci's point while noting the practical difficulty: today it is hard to find people like that share farmer, let alone learn what they know. The implication is that the shadow of tradition has largely replaced true tradition in most agricultural communities.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

Giannozzo Pucci's distinction between true and shadow tradition is directly relevant to [[permaculture]]:

1. **Knowledge preservation:** Permaculture seeks to preserve and transmit functional understanding of land management practices — not just the practices themselves. This aligns with Pucci's "true tradition" concept where practitioners understand why they do what they do.

2. **Observation and timing:** The Chianina share farmer's use of complex seasonal patterns and natural signs to determine sowing times parallels permaculture's emphasis on careful observation and working with natural cycles rather than imposed schedules.

3. **Embodied skill:** The farmer who appeared to dance while forking hay represents the level of embodied, integrated skill that permaculture practitioners aspire to develop — where technique becomes so internalised that it appears effortless.

4. **Bridge between traditional and modern:** Pucci's framework suggests that the goal is not simply to preserve old practices or to replace them with modern methods, but to maintain or recover the functional understanding that made traditional practices effective.

5. **Cultural knowledge as system understanding:** The conversation highlights that sustainable agriculture requires not just technical knowledge but cultural embeddedness — understanding the context, timing, and interconnectedness of practices that reductionist approaches tend to lose.
