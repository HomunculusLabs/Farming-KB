## Mycoforestry

Mycoforestry is the use of fungi to sustain forest communities. Coined and
popularized by **Paul Stamets** in *[[cervantes-trichoderma-beneficial-fungi]] in forest health, regeneration, and sustainability. The
foundational principle is simple: **without fungi, there are no forests.**

## Core Philosophy

Mycoforestry recognizes that fungi are the primary architects of forest
ecosystems. [[mollison-pdc-forest-management-products]] and restoration.

Mycoforestry aims to accomplish the following goals:
- Preservation of native forests
- Recovery and recycling of woodland debris
- Enhancement of replanted trees
- Strengthening sustainability of ecosystems
- Economic diversity beyond timber extraction

## The Role of Fungi in Forests

### Nutrient Cycling
Mycelium absorbs phosphorus and minerals from the environment, transporting
nutrients over distances through hyphal networks and releasing them when
mushrooms decompose. Mushrooms serve as launching platforms for explosive
bacterial population growth. Fungi have a preselecting influence on bacteria
sharing their habitat, and beneficial bacteria regulate nitrogen inputs and
outputs.

### Animal Interactions
Bears actively spread mushroom spores while foraging. Migrating fish carry
