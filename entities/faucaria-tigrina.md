## Overview

**Faucaria tigrina** (Haw.) Schwantes, commonly known as **tiger jaws**, is a compact succulent in the family Aizoaceae. It is native to the Eastern Cape province of South Africa, where it grows in semi-arid scrubland.

The genus name *Faucaria* derives from the Latin *faux* (throat), referring to the jaw-like appearance of the leaf pairs. The species epithet *tigrina* means "tiger-like," alluding to the toothed leaf margins that resemble open jaws.

The distinctive triangular leaves are arranged in opposite pairs, with soft, white, recurved teeth along the margins. Despite their fierce appearance, the teeth are soft and flexible. New leaves emerge from between the old pair, creating a stacked appearance.

Faucaria tigrina produces large, showy yellow flowers in autumn that open in the afternoon and close at night. The flowers are disproportionately large relative to the plant body, often exceeding 5 cm in diameter on plants only 5–8 cm tall.

The species hybridizes readily with other *Faucaria* species in cultivation, producing intermediate forms with varying leaf markings and tooth patterns. Several named hybrids exist in the horticultural trade.

## Ecological Role

In its native Eastern Cape habitat, Faucaria tigrina is a component of the valley bushveld (now termed Albany thicket) plant community. The low-growing succulent occupies microsites between larger shrubs.
