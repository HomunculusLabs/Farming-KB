## Overview

**Umbilicaria phaea** (Ach.) Tuck., commonly known as the **smoky rock tripe**, is a large foliose lichen in the family Umbilicariaceae.

It grows as a circular, leathery thallus attached to rock surfaces by a single central holdfast.

The species is widespread across western [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]], where it colonizes exposed granitic and volcanic rock faces.

It has a long history of use as a starvation food by Indigenous peoples and early European explorers in western North America.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Umbilicaria phaea** was originally described as **Lichen phaeus** by Erik Acharius and later transferred to *Umbilicaria* by Edward Tuckerman.

It is classified in the order Umbilicariales, family Umbilicariaceae, alongside other rock-tripe lichens such as *U. mammulata* and *U. arctica*.

The thallus is foliose, reaching 5–15 cm in diameter, with a dark brown to nearly black upper surface when dry.

When wet, the upper surface becomes glossy and dark olive-brown in coloration.

The lower surface is pale tan to light brown with a dense covering of rhizinose threads or small papillae.

A single central umbilicus (holdfast) attaches the thallus firmly to the substrate rock.

The margins are often undulate and may curl slightly upward, especially in drier conditions.

Apothecia are rare; when present they are black, lecanorine discs up to 3 mm in diameter, raised on short stipes.

The photobiont is a trebouxioid green alga, typical of many foliose lichens.

Spores are ellipsoid, colorless, and 8-spored per ascus, measuring approximately 12–18 × 6–9 μm.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Umbilicaria phaea* occurs widely across western North America, from southern Alaska through the Cascade Range, Sierra Nevada, and Rocky Mountains.

Its range extends southward into the mountains of southern California, Arizona, and New Mexico.

Disjunct populations are reported from the Black Hills of South Dakota and the Lake Superior region.

The species is particularly abundant on exposed granitic outcrops in montane and subalpine zones.

It is found at elevations ranging from approximately 500 to 3500 meters above sea level.

Optimal habitats include dry, well-lit rock faces with minimal competing vegetation.

It prefers siliceous rock substrates, especially granite, gneiss, and volcanic basalt.

The lichen is absent from calcareous substrates and rarely found on shaded or moss-covered surfaces.

## Ecology

*Umbilicaria phaea* is an obligate epilithic species, colonizing bare rock surfaces in exposed, high-light environments.

It contributes to [[dighton-fungi-volcanic-glacier-primary-succession]] by facilitating the breakdown of rock surfaces through physical and chemical weathering.

The lichen is slow-growing, with annual radial [[coleman-sunlight-and-winter-crop-growth-rates]] typically measured in millimeters per year.

It serves as a substrate for microscopic invertebrates including tardigrades and nematodes.

Lichenivorous gastropods and some insects may feed on the thallus in areas of high moisture.

The species is sensitive to [[bloomfield-stachybotrys-indoor-mold-trichothecenes-air-quality]] and serves as a passive bioindicator of atmospheric deposition.

It accumulates trace metals and radionuclides from atmospheric fallout, making it useful in pollution monitoring studies.

*Umbilicaria phaea* can survive extreme desiccation and rapid temperature fluctuations typical of its high-elevation habitat.

Rehydration after prolonged dry periods occurs within minutes when moisture becomes available.
