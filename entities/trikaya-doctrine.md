## Trikaya Doctrine

## Description

The Trikaya (Sanskrit: "Three Bodies") doctrine is a fundamental teaching of Mahayana and Vajrayana Buddhism describing the three modes or dimensions of Buddhahood. In *The Psychedelic Experience*, Leary presents the Trikaya as corresponding to three levels of consciousness attainable during psychedelic sessions: the Dharma-Kaya (Void/Truth Body), the Sambhoga-Kaya (Bliss/Enjoyment Body), and the Nirmana-Kaya (Emanation/Incarnation Body). A fourth term, Adi-Kaya, is used synonymously with Dharma-Kaya to refer to the primordial, unmodified state of consciousness.

## Classification

- **Tradition**: Mahayana / Vajrayana Buddhism
- **Category**: Buddhist Doctrine / Cosmological Framework
- **Related Texts**: Bardo Thodol, Avatamsaka Sutra

## Key Facts

### The Three Bodies

1. **Dharma-Kaya** ("Divine Body of Truth")
   - The primordial, formless essential wisdom
   - True experience freed from all error or obscuration
   - Includes both Nirvana and Sangsara as polar but identical states of consciousness
   - Attained in the First Bardo through recognition of the Clear Light

2. **Sambhoga-Kaya** ("Divine Body of Perfect Endowment")
   - Reflected or Modified Wisdom embodied in the five Dhyani Buddhas
   - The state experienced when consciousness shines with unbearable intensity
   - Voidness and Brightness experienced as inseparable
   - Attained during positive Second Bardo visions

3. **Nirmana-Kaya** ("Divine Body of Incarnation")
   - Practical or Incarnate Wisdom embodied in human Buddhas
   - The radiant power of enlightenment shining unobstructedly in all directions
   - Attained through wise re-entry from the Third Bardo
   - Enlightened beings reborn to work for the benefit of others

### Adi-Kaya
- Synonymous with Dharma-Kaya
- The primordial state of unmodified consciousness
- Described as the state where "Voidness bright by nature and Brightness inseparable from Voidness"

### Esoteric Correspondences
- The Tri-Kaya corresponds to the exoteric trinity of: Buddha, Scriptures, and Priesthood
- In Leary"s psychedelic context: your own divinity, this manual, and your companions
- Lama Kazi Dawa-Samdup held that the Adi-Buddha and all Dharma-Kaya deities are not personal deities but personifications of primordial universal forces and spiritual influences

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- The Trikaya framework provides a sophisticated model for understanding the three phases of a psychedelic experience: peak ego-dissolution (Dharma-Kaya), visionary hallucination (Sambhoga-Kaya), and conscious reintegration (Nirmana-Kaya)
- This maps remarkably well onto the modern understanding of psilocybin's three-phase experience: onset/peak, plateau with visions, and comedown/integration
- The concept of returning to ordinary consciousness as a "Nirmana-Kaya incarnation" — bringing back insights for the benefit of others — parallels the therapeutic integration model used in modern psychedelic-assisted therapy
- The idea that all three bodies exist simultaneously in every moment of consciousness offers a framework for understanding how psilocybin can produce simultaneous experiences of unity, visionary content, and embodied awareness
