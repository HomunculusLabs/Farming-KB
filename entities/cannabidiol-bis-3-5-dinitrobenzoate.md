## Cannabidiol-bis-3,5-dinitrobenzoate

## Description

Cannabidiol-bis-3,5-dinitrobenzoate is a chemical derivative of cannabidiol (CBD) formed by reacting CBD with 3,5-dinitrobenzoyl chloride in dry pyridine. This compound serves as a purification intermediate in the isolation of pure cannabidiol from cannabis extracts. The dinitrobenzoate derivative crystallizes readily (unlike free CBD, which is an oily liquid), enabling purification by recrystallization. The pure crystalline derivative is then converted back to pure cannabidiol by treatment with liquid ammonia. This two-step purification route was documented in the Lloydia Journal of Natural Products (vol. 33, no. 4, pp. 456-457).

## Key Facts

- The derivative is formed by rapidly adding 300 gm fresh 3,5-dinitrobenzoyl chloride (melting point 68-69 degrees C) to a mechanically stirred solution of chromatographed hashish extract in dry pyridine at 0 degrees C under nitrogen atmosphere.
- After stirring for 15 minutes, the mixture is warmed in a 60 degree C water bath for 30 minutes, then poured into a mixture of 200 gm ice and 300 ml concentrated hydrochloric acid.
- The product is extracted with ethyl acetate (750 ml), washed with aqueous sodium bicarbonate (2 x 200 ml) and 300 ml distilled water, dried over CaSO4, and the solvent removed in vacuum.
- Purification by crystallization from 1800 ml ethyl ether yields off-white powdered cannabidiol-bis-3,5-dinitrobenzoate with a melting point of 97-101 degrees C.
- From 340 gm dark oil intermediate, 194 gm of the crystalline dinitrobenzoate derivative was obtained, demonstrating good yield for a two-step derivatization.
- Conversion back to pure cannabidiol is achieved by adding 220 ml liquid ammonia to 288 gm of the dinitrobenzoate in 400 ml anhydrous toluene at -70 degrees C in a Parr bomb. During five hours, pressure builds to 110 psi and temperature rises to 20 degrees C.
- The byproduct 3,5-dinitrobenzamide is removed by filtration after dissolving in heptane and washing.
- Final chromatography on Florisil (180 gm product on 3400 gm Florisil, eluted with 30% chloroform in hexane) and crystallization from [[cbd-to-thc-isomerization-chemistry]]. While modern chromatographic and spectroscopic methods (HPLC, GC-MS) have largely supplanted derivative-based purification, the cannabidiol-bis-3,5-dinitrobenzoate route remains a classic example of purification through crystallizable derivative formation in natural products chemistry. It also illustrates the sophisticated chemical methodology that was applied to [[greg-green-thc-and-cannabinoid-science]].

## Chemical Significance

The dinitrobenzoate derivatization strategy exploits two key principles: (1) the introduction of aromatic nitro groups dramatically increases the crystallinity of the derivative compared to the oily parent compound, and (2) the ester linkage is readily cleaved under strongly basic conditions (liquid ammonia) to regenerate the parent alcohol without racemization or degradation. This approach was commonly used in steroid and terpenoid chemistry for the purification of compounds that resisted crystallization in their free form.

## Safety Considerations

- 3,5-Dinitrobenzoyl chloride is a strong acylating agent and lachrymator; handle in a fume hood with appropriate PPE.
- Dry pyridine is toxic and has a strong odor; use under nitrogen with proper ventilation.
- Liquid ammonia at -70 degrees C requires specialized equipment (Parr bomb) and extreme caution due to cryogenic hazards and pressure buildup (110 psi at 20 degrees C).
- Concentrated hydrochloric acid and the overall strongly acidic workup require acid-resistant PPE.
- Ethyl ether is extremely flammable with a low flash point; exclude all ignition sources.
