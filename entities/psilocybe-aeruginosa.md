# Psilocybe aeruginosa

**Type:** Species (Fungus)
**Category:** Basidiomycota, Agaricales, Strophariaceae, Psilocybe
**Also known as:** Stropharia aeruginosa (Curtis: Fries) Quelet

## Overview
Psilocybe aeruginosa is a strikingly beautiful mushroom with
distinctive dark bluish-green coloration, historically placed in the
genus Stropharia. Noordeloos (1995) reclassified it into Psilocybe
following the suggestions of Alexander Smith (1979). Activity is
suspected but unconfirmed, as analyses of Washington specimens found
no psilocybin or psilocin. The species has been historically reported
as poisonous, possibly due to its psychoactive potential.

## Key Details
- **Cap:** 2-8 cm broad, convex to campanulate, expanding to broadly
  convex with low, broad umbo. Dark bluish green, sometimes fading
  with maturity. Surface viscid when wet with bluish green separable
  gelatinous pellicle. Margin even with whitish flecks (veil
  remnants).
- **Gills:** Adnately attached, broad, fawn to clay brown, sometimes
  tinged purplish, with white edges.
- **Stem:** 30-80 mm long by 3-12 mm thick. Nearly concolorous with
  cap, equal, flared towards apex, swollen at base. Covered with
  whitish patches. Partial veil membranous, leaving fragile annulus,
  whitish above and bluish green below.
- **Spores:** Dark vinaceous purple-brown to purple-black in deposit,
  7.5-9 x 4.5-5 um, ellipsoid, thick-walled with central germ pore.
  Cheilocystidia lageniform-capitate to bluntly capitate, 40-55 x
  10-12.5 um. Pleurocystidia mucronate, clavate, 40-60 x 10-15 um.
- **Habitat:** Widespread in British Isles, northern Europe, western
  [[microfungal-biodiversity-ecology-wood-debris-sampling]], gardens, parks, grassy woodland
  edges. Beneath conifers (Pacific NW) and aspens (SW). Under oaks
  in southern California.
- **Chemistry:** Activity suspected but not confirmed. Beug and
  Bigwood (1982b) found no psilocybin or psilocin in Washington
  specimens. Some report no effects from consumption. European
  sources consider it edible. Biochemistry requires further study.

## Distribution and Ecology
Psilocybe aeruginosa is widely distributed across the British Isles,
northern Europe, and western North America. It shows broad habitat
tolerance, fruiting on wood debris, in gardens and parks, and along
grassy woodland margins. Regional preferences include beneath
conifers in the Pacific Northwest, under aspens in the Southwest,
and under oaks in southern California. This ecological versatility
across multiple tree associations and soil types suggests the species
is adapted to a wide range of forest and garden environments. The
gelatinous pellicle may aid in moisture retention during the
variable conditions of its diverse habitats.
