## Overview

**Keyline Catchment Design** is a comprehensive land management and water harvesting system developed by Australian farmer and engineer **P.A. Yeomans** in the 1950s. Referenced on page 146 of Holmgren's *Permaculture: Principles and Pathways Beyond Sustainability*, it is one of the most influential landscape-scale design methodologies incorporated into permaculture.

## Historical Development

P.A. Yeomans (1904-1984) developed Keyline after inheriting a degraded Hunter Valley farm. His books include *The Keyline Plan* (1954), *The Challenge of Landscape* (1958), *Water for Every Farm* (1964), and *The City Forest* (1971).

## Core Principles

### The Keypoint
The point in a primary valley where slope changes from steeper to flatter. This is the optimal location for water storage. Keyline cultivation patterns radiate from this point.

### The Keyline
A contour line through the keypoint extended across adjacent ridges. Above and below this line, cultivation runs slightly off-contour to distribute water toward ridges evenly.

### Scale of Permanence
Design decisions ordered from most to least permanent: Climate > Land shape > Water supply > Farm roads > Trees > Buildings > Fences > Soil.

## Key Techniques

- **Keyline Plowing** — Subsoiling with angled shanks along keyline patterns, creating below-ground water channels without inverting soil
- **Keypoint dams** — Water storage at the optimal valley point
- **Contour channels** — Gravity-fed water distribution across landscape
- **Landscape reading** — Identifying valleys, ridges, keypoints, and water movement patterns

## Integration with Permaculture

Keyline was a major influence on Mollison and Holmgren. Holmgren credits Yeomans for soil management (page 39), landscape patterns (pages 146, 148), and tree belt systems (page 232). Keyline principles are foundational to permaculture water harvesting and earthworks design.

## Relevance to Mycology

- Keyline plowing preserves fungal networks and mycorrhizal associations by not inverting soil layers
- Even water distribution supports consistent soil moisture for fungal activity
- Keyline tree belts create diverse fungal habitats including mycorrhizal associations
- Soil carbon building from Keyline management feeds saprophytic fungi
- Catchment-scale water and nutrient flow understanding is essential for ecological mushroom cultivation

## Legacy

Keyline Design remains one of the most sophisticated landscape design methodologies ever developed. Its integration of water management, soil building, forestry, and farm layout directly influenced permaculture and continues to inform regenerative agriculture and carbon farming worldwide.
