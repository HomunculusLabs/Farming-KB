## Pigeon (Desert Livestock)

## Description

Pigeons are identified by Mollison as the premier domestic livestock species for desert settlements. Both homing pigeons and domesticated pigeons descend from desert rock pigeons (Columba livia) that naturally nest in eroded rock cavities across arid regions from India to Iran. In the desert permaculture system, pigeons convert the enormous but scattered seed production of desert plants into concentrated protein and fertilizer, while requiring almost no water or supplemental feed.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Animalia
- **Class**: Aves
- **Order**: Columbiformes
- **Family**: Columbidae
- **Species**: Columba livia (rock pigeon / domestic pigeon)
- **Wild ancestor**: Desert rock pigeon of dry India, Iran, and Mediterranean coasts
- **Role**: Primary desert domestic livestock

## Key Facts

- Rock pigeons naturally inhabit eroded rock holes in dry regions of India, Iran, sea coasts, and desert scarps — the same locations humans settle in arid landscapes.
- Increasing pigeon numbers requires only chiseling additional rock holes in cliff faces — a simple, zero-cost expansion strategy.
- Desert environments produce huge quantities of seed that pigeons harvest at no cost to the settlement.
- Pigeon water requirements are extremely light — the scarce desert water supply easily supports large flocks.
- Pigeon manure is described as "the best desert fertilizer" and "the highest market value manure we know."
- Egyptian pigeon habitats are "grandiose things, like little castles, all penetrated with thousands of holes" housing enormous pigeon populations.
- The nesting hole is sized for two eggs but only one young — as squabs grow, the weaker is pushed out and falls. This creates a self-cleaning system where waste and surplus pigeons accumulate below the nesting structure.
- Nearly predator-free in the desert, except for occasional hawks, which are not plentiful in arid regions.
- Eggs can also be harvested as a food resource.
- The Egyptian model demonstrates industrial-scale pigeon production integrated with desert agriculture.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

Pigeons serve multiple critical functions in the desert permaculture system:

1. **Protein production**: Convert inedible (to humans) desert seeds into high-quality animal protein at zero feed cost.

2. **Fertilizer factory**: Pigeon manure is the most valuable organic fertilizer for desert gardens, providing concentrated nutrients in a form that buffers [[soil-ph]] when combined with mulch.

3. **Self-managing system**: The natural nesting behavior of pushing out weaker squabs and letting waste fall below means the system is largely self-cleaning and self-maintaining.

4. **Scalable**: Population scales with the number of rock cavities available — adding capacity is as simple as chiseling more holes.

5. **Water efficiency**: Minimal water requirements mean pigeons don't compete with crops for the scarcest resource.

6. **[[integrated-pest-management]]**: Some pigeon species consume significant quantities of weed seeds, reducing competition in garden areas.

## Egyptian Pigeon Tower System

The Egyptian pigeon towers represent the most developed form of desert pigeon husbandry:

- **Structure**: Multi-story towers resembling small castles, with thousands of nesting holes
- **Construction**: Stone or mud-brick, integrated into settlement architecture
- **Capacity**: Thousands of pigeons per tower
- **Harvest**: Squabs (young pigeons) collected from below the nesting structure
- **Manure collection**: Accumulates at the base of the tower for garden application
- **Cultural significance**: Ancient technology dating back thousands of years in Egypt

## Management Notes

- **Housing**: Natural cliff cavities or constructed pigeon towers with appropriately sized nesting holes
- **Feed**: No supplemental feed required — desert seed production is sufficient
- **Water**: Small drinkers near nesting areas; consumption is minimal
- **Predators**: Hawks are the primary threat; generally not a major problem in desert environments
- **Harvest**: Collect surplus squabs that fall from nests; gather eggs; collect manure from below roosts

## Integration with Other Desert Systems

- Pigeon manure fertilizes the mulch layer in drip-irrigated desert gardens
- Pigeons can be kept near food drying rooms on the sun-facing scarp
- Pigeon towers can double as windbreak structures on earth banks
- Manure tea from pigeon droppings can be applied through [[drip-irrigation]] lines
- The pigeon system integrates with the broader seed-eating bird ecology of the desert
