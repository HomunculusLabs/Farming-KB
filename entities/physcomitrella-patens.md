The species has a subcosmopolitan distribution, occurring across Europe, Asia, Africa, and the Americas. It prefers temperate to [[funaria-hygrometrica]], [[bryum-argenteum]].

## Genome and Molecular Biology

The Physcomitrella patens genome was published in 2008 by an international consortium led by Stefan Rensing. At approximately 480 megabase pairs, it contains around 35,000 predicted protein-coding genes — comparable to the gene count of [[fungal-lichen-stress-tolerance-and-poikilohydry]] DNA repair. Gene families encoding LEA proteins, heat shock proteins, and DNA repair enzymes are significantly expanded relative to vascular plants.

Homologous recombination occurs at frequencies 100–1000 times higher than in flowering plants. This enables precise gene replacement using simple DNA constructs, a capability that has attracted broad interest from the synthetic biology community.

The species possesses functional RNA interference (RNAi) machinery and a full complement of small RNA pathways. MicroRNAs, siRNAs, and trans-acting siRNAs have all been characterized and play roles in developmental regulation.

Gene expression is regulated through promoter elements broadly conserved with vascular plants. However, Physcomitrella also employs unique regulatory mechanisms, including extensive alternative splicing and transcriptional read-through events.

## Research Applications

Physcomitrella patens is the only land plant in which gene targeting by homologous recombination is routine. Over 1,000 genes have been knocked out in systematic reverse-genetics campaigns, creating an unparalleled resource for [[reactive-oxygen-species-and-oxidative-stress]] and defense-related metabolites. This chitin response represents the earliest known form of fungal pathogen detection in the plant lineage.
