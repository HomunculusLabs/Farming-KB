# Acetobacter

## Description

Acetobacter is a genus of acetic acid bacteria known for their ability to oxidize ethanol into acetic acid (vinegar). These aerobic, gram-negative bacteria are ubiquitous in nature—found on fruits, in soil, in alcoholic beverages, and on mushroom surfaces. In mycology and mushroom product development, Acetobacter species are primarily relevant as spoilage organisms that can contaminate mushroom-infused beverages, particularly wines and low-alcohol fermented drinks. Their presence on dried mushrooms poses a risk of undesirable fermentation in products where they are not the intended culture organism.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Bacteria
- **Phylum:** Proteobacteria
- **Class:** Alphaproteobacteria
- **Order:** Rhodospirillales
- **Family:** Acetobacteraceae
- **Genus:** Acetobacter

## Key Facts

### Ethanol Oxidation

The defining metabolic capability of Acetobacter is the oxidation of ethanol to acetic acid:

**C₂H₅OH + O₂ → CH₃COOH + H₂O**

This reaction is the basis of vinegar production and, undesirably, wine spoilage. The process requires oxygen, making Acetobacter primarily a surface-dwelling organism in liquid environments.

### Ecological Niche

Acetobacter species are naturally present on:

- Fruit surfaces (especially damaged or overripe fruit)
- Mushroom fruitbodies (both fresh and dried)
- Flowers and plant tissues with sugar residues
- Soil and organic matter
- Fermenting liquids exposed to air

### Spoilage in Mushroom Products

Cotter specifically warns that dried mushrooms can carry Acetobacter species that may spoil mushroom-infused wines. The bacteria remain dormant in dried material but can reactivate when introduced to the moist, sugar-rich environment of wine fermentation. If not eliminated, they convert the beverage's ethanol to acetic acid, creating an unwanted vinegar flavor and potentially ruining the batch.

### Prevention and Control

To prevent Acetobacter contamination in mushroom-infused beverages:

1. **Autoclave mushrooms** before adding to fermentation: 1 hour at 250°F (121°C) kills Acetobacter and other contaminants
2. **Use high-proof spirits** for infusions: The high alcohol concentration in vodka and other spirits (typically 40%+ ABV) naturally inhibits Acetobacter, making pre-sterilization unnecessary for spirit infusions
3. **Minimize oxygen exposure** during fermentation: Since Acetobacter is aerobic, limiting headspace and using airlocks reduces growth
4. **Use clean, sanitized equipment:** Standard brewing sanitation practices

### Beneficial Applications

While problematic as a spoiler, Acetobacter has important beneficial uses:

- **Vinegar production:** Intentional fermentation of wines, ciders, and other alcoholic beverages
- **Kombucha production:** Acetobacter is one of the primary bacteria in kombucha SCOBY (along with yeast)
- **Biotechnology:** Used in industrial production of acetic acid and cellulose
- **Bacterial cellulose:** Some Acetobacter strains produce pure cellulose mats with material science applications

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

### Dried Mushroom Quality

Acetobacter presence on dried mushrooms highlights the importance of proper drying and storage:

- Dry mushrooms completely to halt microbial activity
- Store in airtight containers to prevent rehydration and bacterial reactivation
- Consider autoclaving before use in fermentation to ensure product safety
- Inspect dried material for off-odors that may indicate bacterial activity

### Mushroom Wine Production

For mushroom-infused wine specifically, Cotter recommends:

- Sterilize all [[mushroom-powder]] by autoclaving for 1 hour at 250°F (121°C)
- Add sterilized powder to fermentation tanks or aging barrels
- Start with 5 grams of powder per liter of wine for basic medicinal dose
- Increase concentration for stronger flavor or higher therapeutic dose

### Contamination Awareness

Understanding Acetobacter helps cultivators and product developers distinguish between:

- **Desired fermentation:** Brewing yeast (Saccharomyces) converting sugars to ethanol
- **Spoilage fermentation:** Acetobacter converting ethanol to acetic acid
- **Mold contamination:** Unwanted fungal growth from unsterilized substrates

### Laboratory Hygiene

Acetobacter species can also contaminate laboratory cultures and [[mushroom-spawn]] if sterile technique is compromised. While less aggressive than bacterial contaminants like Bacillus or Pseudomonas, Acetobacter can still overrun agar plates and [[grain-spawn]] if present in sufficient numbers.

## Safety Considerations

- Acetobacter is generally non-pathogenic to humans
- The primary concern is product spoilage, not health risk
- Standard food safety and sanitation practices are sufficient
- Vinegar produced by Acetobacter is itself a food-safe preservative
