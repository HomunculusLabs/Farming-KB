## Overview

Dryopteris affinis, commonly known as the scaly male fern or golden shield fern, is a robust evergreen fern in the family Dryopteridaceae. It is native to western and central Europe, the British Isles, and parts of western Asia.

The species is one of the largest European ferns, producing imposing fronds that can exceed 1.5 meters in length under favorable conditions. It is named for the dense covering of golden-brown scales on the young fronds and stipe, which give the emerging croziers a distinctive golden appearance.

Dryopteris affinis has a long history of use in European traditional medicine as a vermifuge, similar to the related Dryopteris filix-mas. It is also widely cultivated as an ornamental fern, valued for its architectural form and evergreen habit.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Dryopteris affinis was described by Carl Ludwig von Willdenow in 1810. The specific epithet affinis means "related to," reflecting its close similarity to Dryopteris filix-mas, with which it was long confused.

The species is part of the Dryopteris filix-mas complex, a group of closely related taxa that includes Dryopteris filix-mas, Dryopteris borreri, and Dryopteris cambrensis. Taxonomic treatment varies among authorities, with some treating these as subspecies of a single variable species.

Fronds are 60 to 150 centimeters long, forming a large shuttlecock-shaped rosette from a short, ascending rhizome. The stipe is stout, one-third to one-half the frond length, densely covered in pale golden-brown scales.

The lamina is lanceolate, bipinnate, with 20 to 35 pairs of pinnae. Each pinna bears 15 to 25 pairs of pinnules that are oblong to lanceolate, with serrate margins tipped with small bristles. The texture is coarser than that of Dryopteris filix-mas.

Sori are circular, borne in two rows on the undersurface of pinnules. Each sorus is covered by a kidney-shaped indusium attached at its center, a characteristic shared with other members of the genus.

The rhizome is thick, ascending, and covered with persistent old stipe bases and golden-brown scales. Mature specimens develop an elevated crown that gives the plant a distinctive trunk-like base in old age.

In cultivation, the species responds well to annual applications of leaf mold or composted bark. A spring mulch of organic material helps maintain soil moisture and suppresses weed competition around the crown.

## Distribution and Habitat

Dryopteris affinis is native to western and central Europe, from Ireland and Scotland southward through France, the Iberian Peninsula, and Italy. Its range extends eastward through central Europe to the Carpathians and western Anatolia.

The species is most abundant in oceanic and suboceanic climates with mild, wet winters and moderate summers. It is particularly common in the British Isles, where it is one of the most frequently encountered woodland ferns in western and upland areas.

Typical habitats include deciduous and coniferous woodlands, stream banks, rocky ravines, hedge banks, and damp pastures. It prefers acidic to neutral soils and is tolerant of both [[blechnum-spicant]] in mixed fern communities. These assemblages form important structural components of the woodland ground flora, particularly in western oceanic woodlands.

The species is tolerant of moderate waterlogging and is frequently found in areas with seasonal soil saturation, such as flush lines on hillslopes and the margins of upland streams and springs.
