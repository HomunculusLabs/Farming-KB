## Overview

1cP-LSD (1-cyclopropionyl-D-lysergic acid diethylamide) is a serotonergic psychedelic compound of the lysergamide class. It is a semi-synthetic derivative of LSD in which a cyclopropionyl group is attached to the indole nitrogen of the lysergamide core.

First detected in the European drug market around 2019, 1cP-LSD is marketed as a "legal high" and research chemical in jurisdictions where LSD itself is controlled but its N-acylated analogues remain unscheduled. It functions as a prodrug of LSD, converting to the active parent compound through metabolic hydrolysis in vivo.

## Chemical Properties

**Molecular formula:** C₂₃H₂₉N₃O₃

**Molecular weight:** 395.50 g/mol

**IUPAC name:** (6aR,9R)-N-[(2S)-1-cyclopropylethyl]-7-methyl-4,6,6a,7,8,9-hexahydroindolo[4,3-fg]quinoline-9-carboxamide

**SMILES:** C1CC1C[C@H](C(=O)N2[C@@H](C[C@H]3[C@H]2CN(C)C4=C3C=CC5=C4C=CN=C5)C)N

The compound is a white crystalline solid at room temperature, soluble in ethanol and DMSO, and poorly soluble in water. The cyclopropionyl group on the indole nitrogen is a common structural modification that increases metabolic stability and delays onset compared to the parent compound.

## Pharmacology

1cP-LSD acts as a **prodrug of LSD**. After oral administration, the cyclopropionyl group is cleaved by hepatic and serum esterases/amidases to yield LSD, which is the primary active species responsible for the compound's psychoactive effects.

The resulting LSD acts as a **potent 5-HT₂A receptor agonist** (Kᵢ ≈ 1.7 nM for LSD at human 5-HT₂A), with additional agonist activity at 5-HT₂B, 5-HT₂C, 5-HT₁A, and dopamine D₂ receptors. The psychedelic effects are primarily mediated through cortical 5-HT₂A receptor activation, leading to increased glutamate release, thalamic gating disruption, and altered default mode network connectivity.

The cyclopropionyl moiety does not confer significant pharmacological activity at serotonin receptors in its own right. In vitro receptor binding assays show that 1cP-LSD has reduced affinity compared to LSD at 5-HT₂A, consistent with its prodrug mechanism.

## Effects

The subjective effect profile of 1cP-LSD is nearly indistinguishable from LSD. Users report visual and auditory hallucinations, synesthesia, altered perception of time and space, enhanced emotional openness, ego dissolution at higher doses, and increased cognitive flexibility.

**Onset:** 40–90 minutes after oral administration, slightly delayed compared to LSD due to the prodrug conversion step.

**Duration:** 8–12 hours total experience, comparable to LSD.

**Dosage range:** Approximately 50–200 µg orally. Due to the prodrug conversion efficiency, doses are roughly equivalent to LSD by weight, though some users report slightly reduced potency (estimated 70–90% of LSD by molar equivalent).

At sub-perceptual "microdose" levels (5–20 µg), users report enhanced focus, mood elevation, and creative thinking, consistent with the broader microdosing literature for lysergamides.

## Metabolism

In vitro and in vivo studies using human liver microsomes and controlled administration have identified the primary metabolic pathway as hydrolysis of the cyclopropionyl amide bond to yield LSD. This conversion occurs both hepatically and in blood plasma.

Secondary metabolic pathways parallel those of LSD: N-demethylation to nor-LSD, hydroxylation at various positions on the ergoline ring system, and glucuronidation. The detection window in urine is approximately 24–48 hours for the parent compound and 48–72 hours for LSD metabolites using LC-MS/MS methods.

## Legal Status

1cP-LSD occupies a complex legal position globally. It is **controlled as an analogue of LSD** in many jurisdictions through generic scheduling provisions:

- **Germany:** Controlled under the NpSG (New Psychoactive Substances Act) since 2021.
- **United Kingdom:** Controlled as a Class A drug under the Psychoactive Substances Act amendments.
- **United States:** Potentially covered by the Federal Analogue Act; not explicitly scheduled at the federal level.
- **Switzerland:** Controlled under the Betäubungsmittelverzeichnis since 2022.
- **Japan:** Controlled as a designated substance since 2020.

## History and Context

1cP-LSD first appeared on the European research chemical market in 2019, sold on blotter paper alongside 1P-LSD and 1V-LSD as an alternative to LSD in countries where the parent compound was controlled. It was first formally characterized by Brandt et al. (2019) in *Drug Testing and Analysis*.

The compound represents part of a broader trend of N-acylated lysergamide analogues (1P-LSD, 1V-LSD, 1B-LSD, ETH-LAD) that emerged following the scheduling of earlier research chemicals. Each analogue exploits a regulatory gap by modifying the indole nitrogen while retaining the pharmacophore responsible for psychedelic activity.

Analytical detection presents challenges because standard drug screening immunoassays target LSD metabolites. LC-MS/MS methods are required for definitive identification, and many routine forensic laboratories do not include 1cP-LSD in standard screening panels.

## Comparison with Related Lysergamides

1cP-LSD belongs to a family of N-acylated LSD prodrugs that share a common pharmacological profile. **1P-LSD** (propionyl) is the most widely studied and serves as the reference compound for this class. **1V-LSD** (valeryl) has a longer acyl chain, producing a slower onset and slightly extended duration.

**ETH-LAD** differs by substitution on the amide nitrogen rather than the indole, and has distinct receptor binding properties with reportedly higher visual potency. **1B-LSD** (butyryl) occupies an intermediate position between 1P-LSD and 1V-LSD in terms of onset speed.

All N-acylated lysergamides share the common limitation that analytical differentiation from LSD is difficult in routine drug testing. Their primary practical difference lies in onset kinetics and shelf stability — longer acyl chains generally confer greater stability on blotter paper.
