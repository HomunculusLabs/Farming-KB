## Fatsia japonica

## Overview

Fatsia japonica, commonly called Japanese aralia or glossy-leaf paper plant, is an evergreen shrub in the family Araliaceae. It is native to southern Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan, where it grows in the understory of moist, shaded forests.

The species is widely cultivated as an ornamental landscape plant for its dramatic, deeply lobed leaves and tolerance of deep shade. It also has a history of use in traditional East Asian medicine, particularly for anti-inflammatory purposes.

The plant typically reaches 1.5 to 3 meters in height with an upright, branching habit. Its large, glossy, palmately lobed leaves can exceed 30 cm in diameter, making it one of the most visually striking broadleaved evergreens for shade gardens.

Small white flowers appear in late autumn as terminal panicles, followed by small black drupes. The combination of bold foliage, late-season bloom, and winter fruit gives the species multi-season ornamental value in mild climates.

## Cultivation

*Fatsia japonica* is hardy in USDA zones 7 through 10, tolerating winter temperatures to approximately -15°C when established. It prefers partial to full shade and sheltered positions, as cold winds can scorch the foliage.

Strong direct sunlight causes leaf yellowing and marginal burn, particularly in hot summer climates. The shrub thrives in fertile, humus-rich, moist but well-drained soil with a pH range of 5.5 to 7.5.

It tolerates a wide range of soil types including clay, loam, and sandy soils provided adequate moisture is available. Mulching annually with compost or leaf mold benefits growth significantly and conserves soil moisture.
