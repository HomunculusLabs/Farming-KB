## Beauveria bassiana

**Type:** Species (Ascomycete)
**Category:** Entomopathogenic Fungus / Biocontrol Agent
**Also known as:** B. bassiana (Bals.-Criv.) Vuill.

## Overview
Beauveria bassiana is a cosmopolitan ascomycete fungus and one of
the most extensively studied [[cervantes-biological-pest-control-predators]] control in agriculture,
forestry, and public health. Named after the Italian entomologist
Agostino Bassi, who first demonstrated that a microorganism could
cause disease in animals, B. bassiana has become a model system for
understanding fungal pathogenicity against insects.

B. bassiana is a soil-dwelling fungus found in diverse geographic
regions, from temperate zones such as Alberta (Canada) and Georgia
(USA) to tropical and subtropical areas worldwide. Isolates have
been obtained from soil samples as well as from infected insect
hosts, including the spruce budworm and the greenbug aphid
Schizaphis graminum.

Genotypic analysis has revealed that isolates from tropical and
subtropical regions show considerably more genetic variation than
those from temperate regions, suggesting tropical centers of
diversification. This greater diversity may translate to a wider
array of [[fungal-biodiversity]], taxonomy, and
evolutionary genetics within the genus.

### Pathogenicity Mechanism
B. bassiana infects insects through direct penetration of the
cuticle. The infection process involves: attachment of conidia to
the cuticle surface, germination, formation of an appressorium or
penetration peg, penetration through the cuticle, proliferation
within the hemocoel, and eventual death of the host. The fungus
produces cuticle-degrading enzymes including proteases, chitinases,
and lipases.

Once inside the hemocoel, B. bassiana produces blastospores that
circulate in the hemolymph, overwhelming the insect's immune
defenses. After host death, the fungus emerges from the cadaver and
produces conidia on the surface, which are then dispersed by wind,
rain, or contact to infect new hosts.

### Biocontrol Applications
B. bassiana is widely used as a biological insecticide in both
[[jadam-conventional-and-organic-pesticide-integration]] agriculture. Commercial formulations are
available for controlling whiteflies, thrips, termites, and beetles.
The fungus can be applied as a foliar spray, soil drench, or bait
depending on the target pest and cropping system.
