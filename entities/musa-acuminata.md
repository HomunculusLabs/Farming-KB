## Overview

*Musa acuminata* Colla is the primary wild progenitor of the majority of cultivated bananas and plantains consumed worldwide.

It is a large herbaceous monocot in the family Musaceae, native to [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]] content.

Production cycle from planting to harvest is 9–15 months depending on cultivar and environmental conditions.

Each pseudostem produces a single bunch before dying, with suckers arising from the corm to produce the next generation.

Major producing countries include India, China, Indonesia, Brazil, Ecuador, and the Philippines.

Key diseases include black Sigatoka (*Mycosphaerella fijiensis*), banana Xanthomonas wilt, and the devastating Panama disease TR4.

Nematodes (*Radopholus similis*, *Pratylenchus coffeae*) and weevils (*Cosmopolites sordidus*) are the most important pests.

## Nutritional Value

Banana fruit is approximately 75% water, 23% carbohydrate, and 1% protein by weight.

A medium banana (118 g) provides approximately 105 kcal, 3 g fiber, 422 mg potassium, 17 mg vitamin C, and 0.4 mg vitamin B6.

Bananas are a significant dietary source of resistant starch when unripe, which functions as a prebiotic fiber supporting gut microbiome health.

The fruit contains bioactive amines including dopamine and serotonin, though these do not cross the blood-brain barrier.

Cooking bananas (plantains) are starchier and less sweet than dessert bananas, with approximately 30% starch content at the ripe stage.
