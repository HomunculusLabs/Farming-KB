# Panaeolus cyanescens

**Panaeolus cyanescens**, commonly known as **Blue Meanies**, **Copelandia cyanescens**, or the **Bali mushroom**, is a potent psilocybin-containing mushroom in the family **Bolbitiaceae**. It is a coprophilous (dung-inhabiting) species distributed across tropical and subtropical regions worldwide. Despite its smaller stature compared to *Psilocybe cubensis*, *P. cyanescens* is notably more potent, containing up to 2–3× the psilocybin and psilocin concentrations by dry weight.

## Taxonomy and Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Bolbitiaceae
- **Genus:** *Panaeolus* (sometimes placed in *Copelandia*)

The taxonomic history is complex. Originally described by Berkeley and Broome in 1871 from specimens in Sri Lanka, the species was later moved to the genus *Copelandia* by Singer in 1951 based on its distinct microscopic features — particularly the presence of **cheilocystidia** and a **bilocellular** (two-celled) spore morphology. Many mycologists still use *Copelandia cyanescens* as a synonym. Modern molecular phylogenetics places it firmly within *Panaeolus* section *Copelandia*, alongside approximately 12 other blueing *Panaeolus* species.

## Morphology

### Cap (Pileus)
- 1.5–4 cm diameter
- Hemispherical to convex, expanding to broadly convex or plane with age
- Color: Pale grayish to brownish when young, becoming grayish-white to off-white with a yellowish or brownish center
- Surface: Smooth, hygrophanous — notably lighter when dry, darker when moist
- Margin: Often slightly appendiculate with veil remnants in young specimens

### Gills (Lamellae)
- Adnexed to adnate, crowded, narrow
- Grayish at first, developing mottled black and white — the hallmark two-tone pattern of *Panaeolus*
- Edges: Whitish

### Stipe (Stem)
- 4–12 cm tall, 2–4 mm thick, white to pale gray
- Often bluish at the base; hollow, fragile
- Spore Print: **Black**

### Microscopic Features
- Spores: 11–15 × 7–9 µm, lemon-shaped, thick-walled, **bilocellular** with germ pore
- Basidia: 2- or 4-spored
- Cheilocystidia: Present, flask-shaped — distinguishing from other *Panaeolus* species

## Distribution and Habitat

*P. cyanescens* has a **pantropical** distribution: Central/South America, Southeast Asia, Sub-Saharan Africa, Oceania, the southern US Gulf states, and occasionally Mediterranean Europe.

The species is strictly **coprophilous**, fruiting on herbivore dung — primarily cattle and horse, but also elephant and water buffalo. Found in open pastures, tropical grasslands, and subtropical coastal plains. Fruits during warm, rainy seasons at 24–35°C with high humidity.

## Chemical Composition

*Panaeolus cyanescens* is among the most potent naturally occurring psilocybin mushrooms:

| Compound | Concentration (% dry weight) |
|----------|---------------------------|
| Psilocybin | 0.17–0.95% |
| Psilocin | 0.02–0.50% |
| Baeocystin | 0.02–0.36% |
| Norbaeocystin | Trace |

The total tryptamine content can reach approximately **1.5%** of dry weight in potent specimens, compared to approximately 0.6–1.0% for typical *Psilocybe cubensis*. This elevated potency is attributed to the species' efficient biosynthetic pathway and the stability of its alkaloid profile.

### Bluing Reaction

Like all psilocybin mushrooms, *P. cyanescens* exhibits characteristic **blue bruising** from enzymatic oxidation of psilocin to quinone polymers. Bluing is often more intense and rapid than in *P. cubensis*.

## Cultivation

Cultivating *P. cyanescens* is considered **intermediate to advanced** in difficulty:

### Substrate
- The natural substrate is herbivore dung, making **pasteurized horse or cow manure** the preferred cultivation substrate
- Supplemented straw-manure blends (50/50) work well
- Some cultivators use composted dung or enriched horse manure with vermiculite
- The species is less adaptable to grain-based substrates alone compared to *P. cubensis*

### Environmental Parameters
| Parameter | Range |
|-----------|-------|
| Temperature (colonization) | 26–30°C (79–86°F) |
| Temperature (fruiting) | 24–28°C (75–82°F) |
| Relative humidity (fruiting) | 90–95% |
| Fresh air exchange | High — the species demands significant FAE |
| Light | Indirect ambient or 12/12 cycle |

### Cultivation Challenges
- Slower colonization than *Psilocybe cubensis*
- More sensitive to contamination, particularly bacterial infections
- Thin mycelium can be difficult to spot on substrate

## Related Species

The *Panaeolus* section *Copelandia* contains approximately 12 blueing species:

- *Panaeolus tropicalis* — Very similar, tropical distribution, slightly smaller
- *Panaeolus bisporus* — Rare 2-spored species, high potency
- *Panaeolus cambodginiensis* — Southeast Asian, often confused with *P. cyanescens*
- *Panaeolus cinctulus* — More temperate, less potent, found on grass and dung

Differentiating *P. cyanescens* typically requires microscopic examination of spore size and cheilocystidia shape.

## Ethnobotanical Significance

- In **Bali and other Indonesian islands**, *P. cyanescens* has a long history of use as an entheogen. The term "Blue Meanies" is thought to originate from the Bali tourist scene
- In **Hawaii**, the species is among the most commonly encountered psychoactive mushrooms
- The species gained wider recognition through the work of Paul Stamets and Gastón Guzmán

## Legal Status

*P. cyanescens* is illegal to possess, cultivate, or distribute in most jurisdictions due to its psilocybin content. It is scheduled in many countries alongside other psilocybin-containing mushrooms. In an increasing number of jurisdictions (notably parts of the United States, Netherlands, and Brazil), psilocybin mushrooms are being decriminalized or legalized for therapeutic use.
