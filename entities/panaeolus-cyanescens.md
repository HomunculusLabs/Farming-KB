# Panaeolus cyanescens

*Panaeolus cyanescens*, commonly known as "Blue Meanies" or "Copelandia cyanescens," is a potent psilocybin-containing mushroom native to tropical and subtropical regions worldwide. Belonging to the family Bolbitiaceae (unlike *Psilocybe* species in Hymenogastraceae), it is one of the most powerful naturally occurring psilocybin mushrooms, with documented psilocybin concentrations exceeding those of most *Psilocybe* species.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Bolbitiaceae
- **Genus:** *Panaeolus* (section *Copelandia*)
- **Species:** *P. cyanescens* (Berk. & Broome) Sacc., 1887
- **Synonyms:** *Agaricus cyanescens* Berk. & Broome (1871), *Copelandia cyanescens* (Berk. & Broome) Singer (1951)

The taxonomic history reflects ongoing debate about generic boundaries. Rolf Singer erected the genus *Copelandia* in 1951 to accommodate bluing *Panaeolus* species, a classification still used by many mycologists and popular literature. Molecular phylogenetics has shown the *Copelandia* clade to be monophyletic within *Panaeolus*, supporting Singer's original distinction while maintaining it as a section or subgenus. Other species in section *Copelandia* include *P. tropicalis*, *P. bisporus*, *P. cambodginiensis*, and *P. alienus*.

## Description

### Macroscopic Features
- **Cap (Pileus):** 1.5–5 cm diameter, hemispherical when young, expanding to convex or broadly convex, often with a slight papilla. Surface smooth and hygrophanous, dark chestnut-brown when moist, fading to pale grey or almost white as it dries. Not viscid. Bruises intensely blue to blue-black.
- **Gills (Lamellae):** Adnexed to adnate, crowded. Color varies with maturity: grey to mottled black and white at first (characteristic two-toned appearance from spores maturing asynchronously on the same gill), becoming entirely black at maturity. Gill edges remain whitish.
- **Stipe (Stem):** 4–12 cm long, 2–4 mm thick, equal to slightly enlarged at base. White to pale cream, smooth, very fragile and hollow. Bruises vividly blue when handled.
- **Veil:** Evanescent partial veil, leaving no distinct annulus on the stipe. Occasionally faint fibrillose traces.
- **Flesh:** Very thin, white to pallid. Bruises immediately and intensely blue upon exposure.
- **Spore print:** Jet black.
- **Odor:** Mildly farinaceous to earthy.
- **Taste:** Farinaceous, mild.

### Microscopic Features
- **Basidiospores:** 11–14 × 7–9 µm, ellipsoid to lemon-shaped, thick-walled, smooth, with a distinct germ pore. Black in mass.
- **Basidia:** 2-spored (characteristic of the section *Copelandia*; most other *Panaeolus* are 4-spored).
- **Cheilocystidia:** Numerous, ventricose to cylindrical, 20–40 × 8–14 µm.
- **Pleurocystidia:** Similar to cheilocystidia, occasionally subcapitate.

## Distribution and Habitat

*P. cyanescens* has a pantropical and subtropical distribution:
- Hawaii (extremely common, one of the most frequently encountered psychoactive fungi)
- Southeast Asia (Thailand, Indonesia, Philippines, Cambodia)
- Central and South America (Mexico, Colombia, Brazil, Bolivia, Costa Rica)
- Sub-Saharan Africa (Nigeria, Tanzania, Madagascar, Congo)
- Northern Australia (Queensland, New South Wales)
- Southern United States (Florida, Gulf Coast, occasionally California)
- Caribbean islands
- Parts of southern Europe and the Mediterranean

The species is strictly coprophilous, fruiting on the dung of large herbivores, particularly cattle, horses, and water buffalo. It also occasionally fruits on very nitrogen-rich soils. Unlike *Psilocybe cubensis*, *P. cyanescens* strongly prefers dung in open, sun-exposed pastures and grasslands, often appearing in the same habitat as *P. cubensis* but sometimes in greater abundance. Fruiting is triggered by warm temperatures (above 24°C/75°F) combined with high humidity and recent rainfall.

## Chemistry

### Active Compounds

| Compound | Typical Range (% dry weight) |
|----------|------------------------------|
| Psilocybin | 0.85–1.6% |
| Psilocin | 0.15–0.6% |
| Baeocystin | 0.02–0.36% |
| Norbaeocystin | Trace – 0.05% |

*P. cyanescens* is notably more potent than *Psilocybe cubensis* on a dry-weight basis, with some analyses reporting up to 2.5× the psilocybin content. The 2-spored basidial condition (compared to 4-spored in *P. cubensis*) results in larger spores with proportionally greater cytoplasmic content, though this does not directly correlate with potency — the elevated psilocybin is genetically determined.

## Cultivation

*P. cyanescens* is considered moderately difficult to cultivate compared to *P. cubensis*, presenting several challenges:

- **Substrate specificity:** Requires dung-based or very nitrogen-rich substrates. Does not fruit on standard grain-to-CVG bulk substrate without manure supplementation.
- **Slower colonization:** Grain spawn colonization is notably slower than *P. cubensis*, taking 14–21 days.
- **Environmental sensitivity:** More sensitive to contamination, particularly *Trichoderma* and bacterial bloom on manure substrates.
- **Smaller yields:** Individual fruits are smaller and less dense than *P. cubensis*, though higher potency compensates somewhat.

### Recommended Approach
1. Spawn to sterilized rye grain or millet
2. Transfer to pasteurized horse or cow manure, optionally mixed with straw (70/30 manure/straw ratio)
3. Maintain colonization at 26–28°C
4. Initiate fruiting with temperature drop to 24–26°C, high humidity (95%+), and intense fresh air exchange
5. Casing with peat-based casing layer can improve pinning consistency

## Ethnobotanical Use

*P. cyanescens* has a documented history of traditional use in several cultures:
- **Hawaii:** The species was used by indigenous Hawaiians in ceremonial contexts, though specific details are limited by historical suppression. It remains one of the most abundant psilocybin mushrooms in the islands.
- **Samoa and Tonga:** Ethnographic accounts suggest traditional use of *Panaeolus* species in ritual contexts.
- **Southeast Asia:** Used traditionally in parts of Thailand, Bali, and the Philippines.
- **Africa:** Some Nigerian and East African communities have documented traditional use of bluing *Panaeolus* species.

The common name "Blue Meanies" is shared with a different Australian *Psilocybe* species (*P. aeruginosa*), causing significant taxonomic confusion in popular literature. The *P. cyanescens* usage is more common in the Americas and Hawaii.

## Toxicological Notes

- **Confusion risk:** *Panaeolus* contains some toxic species, notably *P. foenisecii* (the "mower's mushroom"), which is non-psychoactive but can cause gastrointestinal distress. *P. cyanescens* is reliably identified by its intense blue bruising, jet-black spore print, and 2-spored basidia.
- **Panaeolina foenisecii:** Frequently confused with *P. cyanescens* by inexperienced foragers. Distinguished by its brown spore print, lack of blue bruising, and habitat preference for grass rather than dung.
- **Contraindications:** As with all 5-HT2A agonists, avoid with MAOIs, SSRIs, and in individuals with personal or family history of psychotic disorders.
