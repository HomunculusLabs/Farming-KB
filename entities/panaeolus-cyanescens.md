# Panaeolus cyanescens

**Panaeolus cyanescens** is a potent psilocybin-containing mushroom in the family Bolbitiaceae, distinct from the genus *Psilocybe*. It is commonly known as "blue meanies" (not to be confused with the *Psilocybe cubensis* strain of the same name), "Copelandia cyanescens," or simply "Pans." It is a pantropical, coprophilous species known for its high potency relative to its small size.

## Taxonomy and Classification

Kingdom: Fungi > Division: Basidiomycota > Class: Agaricomycetes > Order: Agaricales > Family: Bolbitiaceae > Genus: Panaeolus

The species was first described by Berkeley and Broome in 1871 from specimens in Sri Lanka (then Ceylon). The taxonomic history is complex: many mycologists placed this species in the genus *Copelandia*, named in honor of mycologist Edwin Bingham Copeland. While some authorities still recognize *Copelandia* as a separate genus, modern molecular phylogenetics generally places these species within *Panaeolus*. The epithet "cyanescens" refers to the blue bruising characteristic.

Section *Copelandia* within *Panaeolus* includes approximately 12 species of bluing, psilocybin-containing mushrooms found in tropical and subtropical regions worldwide.

## Morphology

- **Cap**: 1.5 to 4 cm in diameter, hemispheric to campanulate when young, becoming broadly convex to nearly plane with age. Surface is smooth, slightly viscid when moist, and distinctly hygrophanous, changing from dark brown to pale gray or almost white as it dries. The cap margin is often translucent-striate.
- **Gills**: Adnate to broadly adnate, close to subdistant. The hallmark of the genus *Panaeolus* is **mottled gills** — the spores mature unevenly, creating a distinctive spotted or patchy pattern of dark and light areas. Mature gills are dark gray to black.
- **Stipe**: 4 to 12 cm tall, 2 to 4 mm thick, equal or slightly thickened at the base, hollow. Color is white to pale cream, smooth or finely pruinose. Bruises intensely blue to indigo when damaged, especially at the base. No annulus (partial veil is absent or rudimentary).
- **Spore Print**: Black. Spores are ellipsoid to lemon-shaped, 11 to 15 by 7 to 9 μm, thick-walled with a distinct germ pore.
- **Odor and Taste**: Farinaceous (mealy). Taste is mild.

The combination of mottled gills, black spore print, coprophilous habit, and intense blue bruising is diagnostic. The black spore print distinguishes it from *Psilocybe* species, which have dark purplish-brown spore prints.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Panaeolus cyanescens* is a pantropical species with one of the widest distributions of any psilocybin mushroom:

- **Central and South America**: Mexico, Caribbean islands, Colombia, Brazil, Ecuador, Peru, Bolivia, Argentina
- **North America**: Gulf Coast states (Florida, Texas, Louisiana, Georgia), Hawaii
- **Africa**: Throughout sub-Saharan Africa, Madagascar
- **Asia**: India, Southeast Asia (Thailand, Indonesia, Philippines, Cambodia), southern China
- **Oceania**: Northern Australia, Pacific Islands
- **Europe**: Rare, occasionally found in the Mediterranean region and southern Spain

**Habitat**: Strictly coprophilous — it grows on herbivore dung, particularly cattle, horse, and elephant dung. It also occurs on enriched soils and decaying grass in pastures. It favors warm, humid tropical and subtropical environments. Fruiting occurs from spring through autumn, and year-round in tropical zones, typically 1 to 3 days after significant rainfall.

## Chemistry and Psychoactive Properties

*Panaeolus cyanescens* is notably potent for its size, consistently ranking among the more potent psilocybin mushrooms:

| Compound | Concentration (dry weight) |
|----------|---------------------------|
| Psilocybin | 0.3% to 1.3% |
| Psilocin | 0.2% to 0.9% |
| Baeocystin | trace to 0.2% |

Average total alkaloid content is comparable to or slightly exceeds that of *Psilocybe cubensis*. The high psilocin-to-psilocybin ratio contributes to a faster onset and more intense initial effects. Some collections from Hawaii and Thailand have tested among the most potent of any psilocybin mushrooms.

## Cultivation

*P. cyanescens* is more challenging to cultivate than *P. cubensis* but is actively grown by enthusiasts:

- **Substrate**: Horse or cow manure is the preferred natural substrate. It can also be grown on pasteurized straw, composted manure, or enriched grain substrates. It is more sensitive to contamination than *P. cubensis*.
- **Spawn Preparation**: Grain spawn (rye, millet, or bird seed) works well. Colonization is relatively fast.
- **Fruiting Parameters**:
  - Temperature: 24 to 30°C (75 to 86°F) — warmer than most psilocybin mushrooms
  - Humidity: 90% to 95%
  - Light: Moderate indirect light
  - Fresh air exchange: Important for preventing cobweb mold contamination
- **Yield**: Lower than *P. cubensis* per flush, but multiple flushes are possible.

The species' sensitivity to competing organisms requires careful sterile technique and pasteurization of substrates.

## Other Section Copelandia Species

Several other *Panaeolus* species in Section *Copelandia* also contain psilocybin:

- **Panaeolus tropicalis** — Similar distribution and potency, found throughout the tropics
- **Panaeolus bisporus** — Found in subtropical regions, produces only two spores per basidium
- **Panaeolus cambodginiensis** — Southeast Asian species, moderate potency
- **Panaeolus africanus** — African species
- **Panaeolus cinctulus** — Wider temperate distribution, lower potency

## Cultural Significance

*P. cyanescens* is one of the most commonly foraged psilocybin mushrooms in tropical regions worldwide. In Southeast Asia, particularly Thailand, it has been used both traditionally and recreationally, contributing to the region's reputation in psychedelic tourism. The "blue meanies" name has become iconic in psychedelic culture.

In Hawaii, where it grows abundantly in pastures, it has been used both by indigenous Hawaiians and by settlers. The species was among the first tropical psilocybin mushrooms to gain widespread recognition outside of Mesoamerica.

## Lookalikes and Safety

The black spore print and mottled gills are key identification features. However, several non-psychoactive *Panaeolus* species grow in identical habitats and look very similar:

- **Panaeolus foenisecii** (mower's mushroom) — Common in lawns, does not blue, lower potency or inactive
- **Panaeolus acuminatus** — Does not bruise blue, found on dung
- **[[panaeolus-sphinctrinus]]** — Similar appearance, generally considered inactive or very weakly active

The critical diagnostic feature is the blue bruising reaction. If a suspected *P. cyanescens* specimen does not blue prominently when handled, it should not be consumed.

## Legal Status

Controlled in most jurisdictions alongside other psilocybin-containing fungi. In some tropical countries where it grows abundantly, enforcement varies considerably. The species is included in Schedule I of the UN Convention on Psychotropic Substances.
