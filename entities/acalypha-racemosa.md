# Acalypha racemosa

## Overview

*Acalypha racemosa* is a perennial herbaceous plant in the family Euphorbiaceae, widely distributed across tropical and subtropical Africa. It is an important component of traditional pharmacopoeias across West and Central Africa, where it is used to treat a broad spectrum of ailments including [[blesching-cannabis-gastrointestinal-disorders]], and [[ethnomycology-cultural-significance-sacred-fungi]] in traditional medicine.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Clade:** Tracheophytes
- **Clade:** Angiosperms
- **Clade:** Eudicots
- **Clade:** Rosids
- **Order:** Malpighiales
- **Family:** Euphorbiaceae
- **Genus:** *Acalypha*
- **Species:** *A. racemosa*

The genus *Acalypha* comprises approximately 450–570 species, making it one of the largest genera in Euphorbiaceae. *A. racemosa* was first described by Otto Wilhelm Sonder and is closely related to other medicinally important African species such as *A. hispida* and *A. wilkesiana*.

## Morphology

### Growth Habit
- **Life form:** Perennial herb or subshrub
- **Height:** 0.3–1.5 m
- **Habit:** Erect, branching from the base; stems often pubescent

### Leaves
- **Arrangement:** Alternate
- **Blade:** Ovate to broadly ovate, 5–15 cm long, 3–10 cm wide
- **Margin:** Crenate to serrate, occasionally dentate
- **Texture:** Rough to the touch due to stellate [[trichomes]] on both surfaces
- **Petiole:** 2–6 cm, pubescent
- **Venation:** Prominent lateral veins, palmately arranged

### Inflorescence
- **Type:** Axillary or terminal racemes, 5–20 cm long
- **Flowers:** Unisexual; plants are monoecious
- **Male flowers:** Small, greenish, in elongated spikes above; calyx with 4 lobes, 8–12 stamens
- **Female flowers:** Below male flowers in the raceme; subtended by deeply lobed bracts
- **Blooming:** Primarily during the rainy season

### Fruit and Seed
- **Capsule:** Trilocular, approximately 3 mm in diameter
- **Seeds:** Small, ovoid, roughly 1.5 mm
- **Dispersal:** Autochorous (explosive dehiscence) and zoochorous

## Distribution and Ecology

*A. racemosa* is native to tropical Africa, with a range extending from Senegal and Guinea eastward through Nigeria, Cameroon, the Democratic Republic of Congo, Uganda, Tanzania, and southward to Angola and Mozambique. It has also been recorded in Madagascar.

### Habitat Preferences
- Forest margins and clearings
- Savanna woodland
- Secondary growth and disturbed sites
- Roadside thickets
- Cultivated fields (as a weed)
- Altitude: 0–1,800 m above sea level

The species is adaptable to a range of soil types but shows a preference for well-drained, sandy-loam soils in areas receiving 800–2,000 mm of annual rainfall.
