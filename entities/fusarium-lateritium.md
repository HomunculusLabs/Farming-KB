Fusarium lateritium is an ascomycete fungus within the
Nectriaceae family, belonging to the order Hypocreales. The
genus Fusarium is one of the most economically important
groups of [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]] in soil
ecosystems. The species has attracted particular research
attention for its interactions with soil fauna, especially
earthworms. Studies have revealed a complex and somewhat
paradoxical relationship between F. lateritium and
earthworms: while earthworms readily consume fungal-colonized
substrates containing this species, passage through the
earthworm gut completely destroys the viability of its spores.

fungus-fauna interactions in soil food webs and has

during the [[chaetomium-globosum]] exhibited even
more remarkable gut tolerance, with 33 out of 41 spores
remaining viable after passage through the earthworm
digestive system. These interspecific differences in spore
survival rates demonstrate that the impact of earthworm gut
passage on fungal propagules is highly species-dependent,
with F. lateritium being particularly vulnerable to the
conditions encountered during transit through the earthworm
alimentary canal.

The researchers determined that spore destruction in
F. lateritium was caused by the chemical action of
intestinal fluids rather than by the abrasive physical
effects of soil particles within the gut. This finding
suggests that specific enzymatic or chemical properties of
earthworm digestive fluids are selectively lethal to F.
lateritium spores while permitting the survival of spores
from other fungal species.
