## Lycoperdon mixtecorum

**Lycoperdon mixtecorum** Heim is a small puffball fungus known only from Oaxaca, Mexico, where it is used by the Mixtec people for its psychoactive properties. It is one of the more obscure [[stamets-psi-mazatec-mushroom-ceremonies-ethnobotany]] of the Mazatec people.

The specific epithet "mixtecorum" honors the Mixtec people who use the fungus. Some modern taxonomic treatments place Lycoperdon within the family Agaricaceae. The relationship between L. mixtecorum and other Lycoperdon species remains a subject of mycological investigation.

## Morphology

Lycoperdon mixtecorum is a small puffball, attaining a diameter of no more than 1.25 inches (3 cm). It is subglobose, somewhat flattened, and abruptly constricted into a peduncle (stem) scarcely 0.125 inches (3 mm) long.

The exterior surface is densely cobbled-pustuliform and light tan in color. This distinctive surface texture, described as resembling a cobblestone pattern, is an important taxonomic character that helps distinguish L. mixtecorum from other Lycoperdon species. The exterior may develop fine cracks as the puffball dries.

The interior substance (gleba) is straw colored when young, turning olive-brown and eventually dark brown as the spores mature. The gleba is initially firm and compact but becomes powdery as the basidiospores mature. The subgleba (sterile base) is well-developed and chambered.

The spherical spores are brownish tawny with a subtle tinge of olive. Spore morphology, including size, shape, and surface ornamentation, is critical for the microscopic identification of Lycoperdon species. The basidia are typically club-shaped and produce two to four spores each. Capillitial threads are present and help structure the gleba.

## Distribution

L. mixtecorum is known only from Oaxaca in southern Mexico, making it one of the most geographically restricted psychoactive fungi known. Its limited range suggests a highly specialized ecological niche or possible endemism. The fungus grows in the mountainous terrain of the Mixteca region, where it fruits during the rainy season in grasslands and forest margins.

The Mixteca region of Oaxaca is also home to other psychoactive fungi used by indigenous peoples, including various species of Psilocybe. The biodiversity of this region in terms of psychoactive fungi is remarkable and reflects the complex ecological and cultural factors that have shaped indigenous mushroom knowledge in southern Mexico.
