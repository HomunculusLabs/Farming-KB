# Saccharomyces cerevisiae

**Saccharomyces cerevisiae** is a species of budding yeast that is arguably the most extensively studied and commercially important single-celled eukaryote. Commonly known as **baker's yeast** or **brewer's yeast**, it has been used by humans for thousands of years in the production of bread, beer, wine, and other fermented foods and beverages. Beyond its traditional roles, *S. cerevisiae* serves as a premier model organism in molecular and cell biology, and its genome was the first eukaryotic genome to be completely sequenced.

## Taxonomy and Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Saccharomycetes
- **Order:** Saccharomycetales
- **Family:** Saccharomycetaceae
- **Genus:** Saccharomyces

The genus name *Saccharomyces* derives from Greek words meaning "sugar fungus," reflecting its central role in sugar fermentation. The species epithet *cerevisiae* comes from the Latin word for beer (*cerevisia*). Molecular phylogenetic studies have revealed that *S. cerevisiae* is part of the *Saccharomyces sensu stricto* complex, which includes several closely related species including *S. paradoxus*, *S. bayanus*, *S. pastorianus*, *S. kudriavzevii*, and *S. mikatae*.

## Cell Biology and Morphology

*Saccharomyces cerevisiae* is a unicellular fungus with the following characteristics:

- **Cell Shape:** Ovoid to ellipsoidal, typically 5 to 10 μm in diameter
- **Reproduction:** Reproduces asexually by budding, in which a small daughter cell emerges from the surface of the mother cell. Under nutrient limitation, it can also undergo [[fungal-life-cycles-sexual-reproduction-metamorphosis]], forming diploid cells that undergo meiosis to produce four haploid ascospores contained within an ascus
- **[[staycare-degradation-of-plant-cell-wall-polymers-by-fungi]]:** Composed of glucans, mannoproteins, and chitin. Beta-1,3-glucan is the major structural component, while beta-1,6-glucan provides cross-linking. The cell wall also contains alpha-1,3-glucan, though this is a minor component compared to pathogenic fungi
- **Genome:** Approximately 12 Mb in size, distributed across 16 chromosomes, containing approximately 6,275 protein-coding genes. The genome is highly compact, with relatively small intergenic regions compared to higher eukaryotes
- **Subcellular Organization:** Contains a nucleus, mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, vacuoles, and peroxisomes. Unlike filamentous fungi, it does not produce [[fungal-woronin-bodies-and-septal-pore-organization]]

## Historical Significance

The relationship between humans and *S. cerevisiae* is one of the oldest examples of biotechnology:

- **Ancient Origins:** Archaeological evidence suggests that humans were using yeast for bread and alcoholic fermentation by at least 4,000 BCE in ancient Egypt and Mesopotamia, and possibly much earlier
- **Discovery of Microorganisms:** In the 19th century, Louis Pasteur's work on alcoholic fermentation established the role of yeast as a living organism responsible for the process, disproving the spontaneous generation theory
- **Modern Genetics:** *S. cerevisiae* was instrumental in the development of modern genetics. George Beadle and Edward Tatum used *[[neurospora-crassa]]* to establish the one gene-one enzyme hypothesis, but *S. cerevisiae* became the dominant model for studying eukaryotic molecular biology

## Metabolism and Fermentation

*Saccharomyces cerevisiae* is a facultative anaerobe with versatile metabolic capabilities:

- **Fermentation:** In the absence of oxygen, *S. cerevisiae* ferments sugars to produce ethanol and carbon dioxide through the glycolytic pathway. This is the basis of its use in brewing, winemaking, and bread-making
- **Crabtree Effect:** Unlike many microorganisms, *S. cerevisiae* preferentially ferments glucose to ethanol even in the presence of oxygen (aerobic fermentation), a phenomenon known as the Crabtree effect or Custer effect
- **Carbon Source Utilization:** Can ferment glucose, fructose, sucrose, maltose, and maltotriose. It cannot ferment lactose, cellobiose, or xylose natively (though metabolic engineering has enabled xylose fermentation)
- **Nitrogen Metabolism:** Can utilize a wide range of nitrogen sources, including ammonium, amino acids, and peptides. Glycerol production is an important osmoprotectant strategy under stress conditions

## Industrial Applications

*Saccharomyces cerevisiae* is one of the most important industrial microorganisms:

### Brewing
- **Ale Yeast:** Top-fermenting strains of *S. cerevisiae* are used to produce ales, stouts, porters, and wheat beers
- **Distilling:** Used in the production of whiskey, vodka, rum, and other distilled spirits
- **Lager Yeast:** *Saccharomyces pastorianus* (a natural hybrid of *S. cerevisiae* and *S. eubayanus*) is used for lager production

### Winemaking
- Both natural wine strains and selected commercial strains are used for grape must fermentation
- Different strains produce distinct flavor profiles through differential production of esters, higher alcohols, and other [[query-how-do-fungal-volatile-compounds-affect-plant-growth-and-other-microorganisms]]

### Baking
- Baker's yeast is produced industrially as fresh (compressed), active dry, or instant dry yeast
- Carbon dioxide production during fermentation causes dough to rise, while ethanol and other metabolites contribute to bread flavor

### Modern Biotechnology
- **Recombinant Protein Production:** Used as a cell factory for producing therapeutic proteins (human insulin, hepatitis B vaccine, human serum albumin), industrial enzymes, and other bioproducts
- **Biofuel Production:** Engineered strains capable of fermenting pentose sugars from lignocellulosic biomass are being developed for cellulosic ethanol production
- **Synthetic Biology:** A chassis organism for synthetic biology applications, including the Sc2.0 project to synthesize a redesigned yeast genome

## Research as a Model Organism

*Saccharomyces cerevisiae* has contributed more fundamental discoveries to biology than any other single-celled eukaryote:

- **Cell Cycle:** Discovery of cyclins and cyclin-dependent kinases (CDKs), fundamental regulators of the eukaryotic cell cycle (Leland Hartwell, Paul Nurse, and Tim Hunt; Nobel Prize 2001)
- **Autophagy:** The molecular machinery of autophagy was first elucidated in yeast (Yoshinori Ohsumi; Nobel Prize 2016)
- **Protein Targeting and Secretion:** Key components of the secretory pathway, including the signal recognition particle and vesicular transport machinery, were discovered in yeast (Randy Schekman; Nobel Prize 2013)
- **Gene Silencing:** Position-effect variegation and telomeric silencing were first characterized in yeast
- **Prions:** The first mammalian prion protein (PrP) was modeled using the [PSI+] and [URE3] prions of yeast
- **Saccharomyces Genome Database (SGD):** A comprehensive, curated database of yeast genetic and genomic information, serving as a model for other organism databases

## Ecological and Environmental Role

In natural environments, *S. cerevisiae* is found primarily in association with sugar-rich substrates:

- **Fruit Surfaces:** Commonly isolated from the surfaces of grapes, apples, berries, and other fruits, particularly during ripening when sugars become available
- **Tree Exudates:** Found in association with tree bark and sap fluxes
- **Soil:** Occasionally isolated from soil, though it is not considered a major soil fungus
- **Insect Associations:** Can be found in the guts of insects, particularly those feeding on fruit or nectar

## Related Species

- **Saccharomyces paradoxus:** The closest wild relative of *S. cerevisiae*, found in association with oak trees and soil. Multilocus typing studies have revealed significant population structure and geographic variation
- **Saccharomyces pastorianus:** A natural interspecific hybrid used in lager brewing
- **Saccharomyces boulardii:** A strain used as a probiotic, particularly for preventing antibiotic-associated diarrhea

## Notable Strains and Genetic Tools

*S. cerevisiae* has an unparalleled collection of laboratory strains and genetic tools:

- **S288C:** The reference strain whose genome was completed in 1996. It is the basis for the Saccharomyces Genome Database (SGD) and is the most widely used laboratory strain worldwide
- **BY4741/BY4742:** Haploid derivatives of S288C used extensively in [[magnaporthe-grisea-functional-genomics-rice-blast]]. The BY4741 deletion collection (knockout of each non-essential gene) is a foundational resource for systematic phenotyping
- **W303:** A widely used laboratory strain with good sporulation efficiency and transformation competence, commonly employed in cell cycle and DNA repair studies
- **Sc2.0 Project:** An international synthetic biology effort to design and construct a fully synthetic yeast genome with redesigned features, including the removal of transposable elements, introduction of loxPsym sites for SCRaMbLE (Synthetic Chromosome Rearrangement and Modification by LoxP-mediated Evolution), and recoding of TAG stop codons
- **Yeast Two-Hybrid (Y2H):** A powerful technique for detecting protein-protein interactions, first developed in *S. cerevisiae* and now widely applied across eukaryotes

## Economic Impact

The global economic significance of *S. cerevisiae* spans multiple industries:

- **Beverage Alcohol:** The global beer market alone exceeds $600 billion USD annually, with *S. cerevisiae* and *S. pastorianus* as the essential fermentation organisms. Wine production, valued at approximately $300 billion USD globally, is entirely dependent on yeast fermentation
- **Baking Industry:** Industrial baker's yeast production exceeds 2 million metric tons per year worldwide, with a market value estimated at $3 to $4 billion USD
- **Bioethanol:** *S. cerevisiae* is the primary organism for first-generation bioethanol production from corn and sugarcane, with global bioethanol production exceeding 100 billion liters per year
- **Biopharmaceuticals:** Recombinant insulin produced in *S. cerevisiae* (and later in *Pichia pastoris*) was the first commercially successful recombinant therapeutic protein. Yeast-produced hepatitis B vaccine remains a major global health product
- **Research Reagents:** Commercial yeast media, strains, and kits constitute a market exceeding $500 million USD, underpinning basic and applied research worldwide
