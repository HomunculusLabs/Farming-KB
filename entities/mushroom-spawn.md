# Mushroom Spawn

## Description

Mushroom spawn is the vegetative mycelial growth of a fungus used to seed a compost substrate for mushroom production. In commercial Agaricus cultivation, spawn consists of living mycelium that has colonized a carrier material, traditionally manure-based substrates but later grain and other media. Spawn is the fungal equivalent of seed in plant agriculture and is the critical input that determines the variety and productivity of the mushroom crop.

## Classification

- **Type**: Cultivation input / propagation material
- **Organism**: Fungal mycelium (primarily Agaricus campestris and relatives)
- **Traditional carrier**: Composted manure bricks
- **Modern carrier**: Grain (rye, millet, sorghum), supplemented substrates

## Key Facts

- Spawn may be produced from spores or from fragments of living tissue when pure culture methods are employed
- Fresh undried spawn of Agaricus campestris has a pleasing aroma combining fresh mushrooms with a slight powdered almond note
- Spawn of Agaricus fabaceus has a far more pronounced almond or amygdaline odor
- Spawn odors are largely lost upon drying
- Before pure culture techniques, spawn was sold generically as "mushroom spawn" with no variety indication
- For centuries after cultivation began, it was not possible to select or propagate specific varieties
- Pure culture methods developed around 1900 in both France and the United States revolutionized [[mushroom-cultivation]]. The transition from uncontrolled, unreliable spawn to pure-culture spawn around 1900 marked a watershed moment in the industry. As one large grower stated: "If we had to depend upon foreign spawn, as in the past, mushrooms would still be scarce and high." Pure culture techniques allowed propagators to maintain named varieties, ensure spawn viability, and produce consistent crops. The development also broke the secrecy that had surrounded mushroom growing for centuries, as reliable spawn made the craft accessible to a broader range of cultivators.

## Spawn Development Stages

1. **Mycelium colonizes** the substratum in all directions, absorbing water and nutrients
2. **Food accumulation** occurs as surplus materials build up in the mycelial network
3. **Pin heads** appear as minute cushion-like growths on larger threads
4. **Buttons** form as pin heads grow and become recognizable mushroom primordia
5. **Mature mushrooms** develop from buttons in approximately 7–10 days under optimal conditions
