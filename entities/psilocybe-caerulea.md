# Psilocybe caerulea

**Type:** Species (Fungus)
**Category:** Basidiomycota, Agaricales, Strophariaceae, Psilocybe
**Also known as:** [[stropharia-caerulea]] (Krieger) Kreisel

## Overview
Psilocybe caerulea is a litter mushroom with distinctive bluish-green
coloration that fades to yellow-green with age. Originally described
as Stropharia caerulea, it was transferred to Psilocybe by Noordeloos.
Found in manure-enriched soils and garden habitats across Europe and
northwestern [[guzman-allen-gartz-conflicting-chemical-analyses-neurotropic-fungi]] have been published. The name "caerulea" refers to
the azure blue color of fresh specimens. In Europe, P. caerulea is more
common than its close relative [[psilocybe-aeruginosa]].

## Key Details
- **Cap:** 3-7.5 cm broad, convex, soon broadly convex with obtuse
  umbo. Bluish, fading to azure, then yellow to yellowish green,
  margin remaining tinged bluish green. Viscid when wet from
  separable gelatinous pellicle. Partial veil fragments sometimes on
  margin. Flesh thin, bluish at first, fading to straw color.
- **Gills:** Adnate to subdecurrent, close, fawn brown to chocolate
  brown to dark brown with spore maturity, paler edges.
- **Stem:** 40-110 mm long by 3-12 mm thick, equal to slightly
  enlarged near base. Bluish green to azure, becoming pale buff.
  Pruinose above annulus, fibrillose-floccose scaly below. Partial
  veil membranous, leaving fragile annulus or annular zone.
- **Spores:** Light purplish brown in deposit, ellipsoid, 7-10 x
  4.5-5.5 um. Basidia 4-spored. Cheilocystidia and pleurocystidia
  28-55 x 10-16 um, mucronate to fusiform with acute apices.
- **Habitat:** Litter mushroom in garden-like habitats or soils
  enriched with manure. Widely distributed: British Isles, Europe,
  northwestern North America.
- **Chemistry:** Activity possibly mild. No chemical analyses known.
  Differs from P. aeruginosa in rapidly discoloring cap, paler gills,
  lack of veil-formed scales on cap surface, and generally smaller
  overall stature. The lighter spore deposit color is also diagnostic.

## Distribution and Ecology
Psilocybe caerulea is widely distributed across the British Isles,
Europe, and northwestern North America. It thrives in garden-like
habitats and manure-enriched soils, making it one of the more
accessible Psilocybe species for observation. The species is
particularly associated with anthropogenic habitats: compost piles,

## Seasonality

Psilocybe caerulea fruits from late summer through autumn (August to
November in the Northern Hemisphere), with peak fruiting during cool,
moist conditions in September and October. In northwestern North
America, the season may begin as early as July in coastal Pacific
Northwest climates. The species requires sustained moisture and
moderate temperatures (10-18°C), and is most commonly found after
periods of heavy rain followed by cooler weather.
