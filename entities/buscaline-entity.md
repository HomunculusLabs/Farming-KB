# B (Buscaline)

**Chemical name:** 4-(n)-Butoxy-3,5-dimethoxyphenethylamine
**PiHKAL entry:** #10

## Overview

Buscaline (B) is a mescaline-entity|mescaline analogue where the 4-position methoxy group is replaced by a butoxy (four-carbon) chain. It represents the upper limit of practical alkoxy substitution on the mescaline skeleton for psychoactive purposes. Originally patented in England in 1930 without any pharmacological description, it was later evaluated by [[alexander-shulgin]] and found to be essentially inactive as a psychedelic.

The compound exemplifies a key principle in [[md-family-structure-activity]]: beyond a certain chain length, the 4-position alkoxy substituent no longer contributes to psychedelic activity and instead produces predominantly unpleasant physical effects.

## Dosage and Activity

| Dose Tested | Result |
|-------------|--------|
| 120 mg | Slight baseline change, gone by 8th hour. No visual or auditory changes. |
| 150 mg | Body load without mental reward. Heart arrhythmia, GI discomfort, cold extremities. |

**Effective psychedelic dose:** Greater than 150 mg (essentially inactive in the psychedelic sense)

## Duration

Several hours at the tested doses, but no meaningful psychoactive effects were observed.

## Qualitative Comments

The experience reports from [[pihkal-qualitative-effects-themes]] are uniformly unenthusiastic:

- **At 120 mg:** A strange, non-bitter taste that doesn't linger. The slight change of baseline disappears by the eighth hour. No noticeable changes in visual or auditory areas.
- **At 150 mg:** Effects felt more in body than mind. Body load never mellows out (unlike mescaline). Awareness of brief heart arrhythmia. Gastrointestinal discomfort with light diarrhea. Cold feet persisting to the fifth hour. The overall feeling: "Why did I bother?"

## Shulgin's Commentary

Shulgin quotes a chemical adage: "There's ethyl and propyl, but butyl is futile." This proved largely true for the 4-position homologues of mescaline:

- mescaline-entity|Mescaline (methoxy): Active at 200-400 mg
- escaline-entity|Escaline (ethoxy): Active at 40-60 mg
- proscaline-entity|Proscaline (propoxy): Active at 30-60 mg
- **B / Buscaline** (butoxy): Essentially inactive at 150 mg

Shulgin speculates that higher homologues (amylescaline, hexescaline, heptescaline) would likely show even less activity, with toxic symptoms discouraging the high doses that might be needed to affect the brain.

## Synthesis Notes

The synthesis starts from homosyringonitrile (see escaline-entity|escaline for its preparation), which is alkylated with n-butyl bromide in the presence of decyltriethylammonium iodide (phase-transfer catalyst) and anhydrous K2CO3 in acetone. The resulting nitrile is reduced using aluminum hydride (AH/LAH in THF with H2SO4) to provide the amine as the hydrochloride salt (mp 155-157 deg C).

## Shulgin's Naming Humor

Shulgin extends the naming game with humor. Following B (butoxy), the higher homologues would be:

- A (amyloxy/pentoxy) -- P avoided to prevent confusion with pentoxy/propoxy
- H (hexyloxy) -- though H has also been used for DMPEA (Homopiperonylamine)
- S (heptyloxy) -- shifting from Greek "hepta" to Latin "septem" for seven

Shulgin dryly notes: "It seems most likely that the toxic symptoms that might well come along with these phenethylamines would discourage the use of the dosage needed to affect the higher centers of the brain."

The same principle applies to the amphetamine counterparts 3C-B, 3C-A, 3C-H, and beyond.

## Significance

Buscaline is important as a **negative data point** in the [[phenethylamine-chemistry-basics]] studies of phenethylamines. It helps define the boundary conditions for psychoactive alkoxy substitution and supports the understanding that there is an optimal size range for the 4-position substituent (methoxy through propoxy) beyond which activity drops off sharply. In [[synthetic-tryptamine-methods]], negative results are as informative as positive ones -- they map the boundaries of pharmacological activity.

The compound also illustrates the difference between lipophilicity (which increases with chain length) and brain penetration/activity (which depends on many factors beyond simple fat solubility). While Buscaline is more lipophilic than mescaline, it is less psychoactive, demonstrating that "more lipid-soluble" does not automatically mean "more potent."
