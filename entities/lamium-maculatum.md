## Overview

**Lamium maculatum**, commonly known as spotted dead-nettle, is a low-growing
perennial herb in the family Lamiaceae. It is native to Europe and temperate
Asia, widely cultivated for its attractive variegated foliage and prolonged
flowering season.

The common name "dead-nettle" refers to the resemblance of its leaves to those
of stinging nettles (*[[permaculture-plants-food-forest-ground-covers-bamboos]] in
temperate horticulture. Numerous cultivars have been selected for leaf color,
flower color, and growth habit, making it a staple of woodland gardens.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

*Lamium maculatum* L. was described by Linnaeus in *Species Plantarum* (1753).
The genus name *Lamium* is derived from the Greek *lamia*, meaning a devouring
monster, possibly referencing the superficial resemblance to nettles.

The plant forms creeping stems that root at the nodes, producing a dense mat
15–30 cm tall. The square stems are ascending to trailing, densely pubescent,
and often purplish-tinged.

Leaves are opposite, ovate to cordate, 2–5 cm long, with dentate margins. The
upper surface is typically green with a prominent silver-white medial stripe,
though the extent and intensity of variegation varies by cultivar. The
underside is paler with scattered hairs.

Flowers are borne in whorls (verticillasters) in the upper leaf axils, with
2–6 flowers per whorl. The corolla is two-lipped, 15–25 mm long, with the
upper lip hooded and the lower lip spreading. Flower color ranges from pink to
purple to white.

Flowering occurs over a long season from April through November in favorable
conditions. The calyx is 5-toothed, 6–8 mm long, with a campanulate tube.

## Distribution and Habitat

Spotted dead-nettle is native to much of Europe, from the British Isles and
France eastward through central Europe to the Caucasus, northern Turkey, and
western Iran. It has been widely introduced and naturalized in [[query-best-kiwifruit-varieties-for-cold-climates]]. Full sun is
tolerated in cooler regions but may cause leaf scorch in hotter areas.

'White Nancy' (silver leaves with white flowers), 'Chequers' (pink flowers),
and 'Purple Dragon' (large purple flowers with silver foliage).
