## Russula fellea (Bitter Russula)

## Description

Russula fellea is an entirely pale ochraceous (yellowish) species of Russula recognised by its very acrid and bitter taste, association with beech woods, and the watery drops exuded from the gills in early stages. It is considered poisonous when raw but the irritant substance is dispersed by cooking.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Russulales
- **Family:** Russulaceae
- **Genus:** Russula
- **Species:** R. fellea

## Morphology

The cap is convex then flattened, viscid, with a darker centre and thin, striate margin. Average cap width is approximately 4 inches; stem height about 2 inches; stem width about 0.75 inch. The entire fruiting body is pale ochraceous in colour.

The gills are adnate (broadly attached to the stem), thin and somewhat crowded. They are white at first and exude watery drops — an unusual feature. The stem is cylindrical, white, then becoming the colour of the cap but paler. The flesh is firm, whitish then pale ochraceous. The taste is very acrid and bitter; the smell is pleasant.

## Key Facts

- Entirely pale ochraceous (yellowish) coloration — unusual among the acrid Russula species
- Gills exude watery drops in early stages
- Very acrid AND bitter taste (most Russula are acrid but not necessarily bitter)
- Associated specifically with beech woods
- Thin, striate cap margin
- Gills are adnate (broadly attached)
- Flesh transitions from whitish to pale ochraceous
- Grows in beech woods in late summer and autumn

## Toxicity

Considered poisonous when raw due to the very acrid and bitter substances. As with other acrid Russula species, the irritant principle is dispersed during cooking, but the species is best avoided. The bitterness makes it thoroughly unpalatable in any case.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Habitat specificity:** Strong association with beech woods suggests a mycorrhizal relationship with Fagus sylvatica
- **Guttulation:** The exudation of watery drops from young gills is a notable physiological feature rarely seen in Russula
- **Colour as diagnostic:** The uniform pale ochraceous colour distinguishes it from the more commonly red/purple acrid Russula species
- **Taste dualism:** The combination of both acrid and bitter tastes is distinctive — many Russula species are acrid without being bitter
- **Seasonal timing:** Late summer to autumn fruiting in beech woods provides temporal as well as spatial habitat specificity
- **Taxonomic importance:** The species epithet "fellea" (gall-like, bitter) directly references its most distinctive taste character

## Habitat and Ecology

Grows specifically in beech woods in late summer and autumn. The strong association with beech suggests an ectomycorrhizal relationship. The viscid cap and guttulate gills indicate adaptation to humid conditions within the beech woodland microclimate.

## Historical Notes

Described and illustrated by Ramsbottom (1945) on Plate 8 alongside R. emetica, R. fragilis, and R. drimeia. The species name "fellea" derives from the Latin "fel" (gall, bile), referring to the intensely bitter taste.
