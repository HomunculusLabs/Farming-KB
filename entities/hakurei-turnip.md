# Hakurei Turnip

## Description

The Hakurei turnip (*[[brassica-rapa]]* subsp. *rapa*) is a Japanese-type salad turnip known for its smooth, pure white skin, crisp texture, and remarkably sweet, mild flavor. Unlike traditional storage turnips, Hakurei is best eaten raw or lightly cooked, and has become a staple of winter harvest systems and farm-to-table cuisine due to its excellent cold tolerance and rapid growth.

## Classification

- **Category**: Vegetable cultivar
- **Species**: *Brassica rapa* subsp. *rapa*
- **Family**: Brassicaceae (mustard family)
- **Type**: Japanese salad turnip (kabu-type)
- **Growth habit**: Globe-shaped root with smooth white skin and tender greens

## Key Facts

- Matures in approximately 38–40 days from sowing
- Roots are 2–3 inches in diameter at harvest maturity
- Pure white, smooth skin with no fibrous exterior
- Crisp, apple-like texture when raw — sweet and mildly peppery
- Greens are also edible and highly nutritious, suitable for sauteing or salad
- Excellent cold tolerance — can be grown as a fall and winter crop under protection
- Does not require peeling — the entire root is tender
- Best harvested promptly at maturity to avoid becoming woody or pithy
- Popular in Stir-Fry Paks and salad mixes at market stands
- Suitable for [[succession-planting]] every 1–2 weeks for continuous harvest

## Relevance to Cultivation

Hakurei turnips are particularly well-suited to winter harvest and season extension systems:

- **Quick maturity**: At 38–40 days, they fit easily into fall planting windows
- **Cold tolerance**: Can withstand light freezes and continue growing under row covers or in unheated greenhouses
- **Space efficiency**: Compact growth habit allows close spacing (typically 2–4 inches between plants)
- **Dual crop**: Both roots and greens are marketable, increasing revenue per bed foot
- **Succession planting**: Rapid growth cycle enables multiple successions through fall and winter
- **Market appeal**: The sweet, mild flavor and attractive appearance command premium prices at farmers markets
- **Versatility in crop rotation**: Fits well in brassica rotations following legumes or alliums

## Growing Conditions

- **Soil**: Prefers loose, fertile, well-drained soil with consistent moisture
- **Sowing depth**: 1/4–1/2 inch
- **Spacing**: 2–4 inches between plants in rows 6–12 inches apart
- **Temperature**: Germinates at soil temperatures of 50–85F; grows well at 50–65F
- **Daylight**: Tolerates short days of late fall and winter
- **Protection**: Benefits from floating row covers or [[quick-hoops]] in cold climates

## Culinary Notes

- Excellent raw in salads — thinly sliced or grated
- Light cooking (saute, roast, or braise) brings out natural sweetness
- Pairs well with butter, miso, citrus, and herbs
- Greens can be prepared like any braising green
