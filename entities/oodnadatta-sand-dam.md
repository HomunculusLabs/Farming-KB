# Oodnadatta Sand Dam

## Description

The Oodnadatta [[permaculture]]: A Designers' Manual* as a prime example of a simple but effective sand dam system that was still functioning well over 60 years after construction. It demonstrates the long-term durability and self-sustaining nature of properly designed sand dam systems.

## Classification

- **Type**: Sand dam / historical water infrastructure
- **Location**: Oodnadatta, South Australia, Australia
- **Construction date**: 1920s
- **Builder**: Railway company (for railway and settlement water supply)
- **Status**: Still functioning well in the 1980s (60+ years of service)

## Key Facts

- Built in the 1920s by a railway company to serve the remote settlement of Oodnadatta on the edge of the Simpson Desert.
- The dam function is based on allowing the reservoir behind the barrier wall to fill with sand and sediment from flood events.
- Water is stored within the pore spaces of the accumulated sand — effectively a buried underground reservoir.
- One documented sand dam of this type dropped only 8 cm in level over a 7-year period, demonstrating the extremely low evaporation losses from sand-filled reservoirs.
- The downstream effects include forest extension 1–2 km in the river sands for a wall only 50 m wide.
- The Oodnadatta dam exemplifies Mollison's principle that "this simple but effective system creates oases where there were none before."
- There are hundreds or thousands of similar potential sites a few kilometers from foothills in deserts worldwide.

## Design Principles Demonstrated

The Oodnadatta dam validates several key sand dam design principles:
- **Stage construction**: Large dams are built in 1.5–2 m height stages, raised as earlier stages silt up.
- **Gabion filtration**: Behind each wall stage, gabions allow water to filter to a base pipe.
- **Deep and narrow**: Clearwater storage should be deep (5–9 m) with reduced surface area rather than shallow and wide.
- **Self-maintaining**: Once established, the sand reservoir requires minimal maintenance.
- **Multi-benefit**: Provides water supply, supports downstream vegetation, and creates habitat.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Long-term water security**: The 60+ year lifespan demonstrates that sand dam water supplies can support permanent agricultural and mycological installations in arid regions.
- **Cool underground storage**: Water stored in sand reservoirs remains cool — ideal for supplying [[mushroom-substrates]].
- **Scalable model**: The Oodnadatta example proves the concept works at village scale, encouraging replication at smaller scales for individual farm or cultivation facility water supply.
