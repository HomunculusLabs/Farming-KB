# Fedge System (Fence-Hedge)

## Description

The fedge (fence-hedge) is a combined boundary system for dryland gardens consisting
of a strong rail and wire fence carrying vigorous productive vines. This integrated
design provides wind protection, mulch production, bee fodder, food production, and
animal exclusion simultaneously. At 1.5-2m high, a fedge protects 9-10m widths of
garden behind it.

## Classification

- **Category**: Garden boundary system
- **Domain**: Dryland agriculture, windbreak design, integrated food production
- **Technology level**: Low to medium
- **Protection zone**: 9-10m of garden

## System Design

### Fence Structure
- Strong rail and wire fence as primary support
- Posts must be termite-resistant or creosoted
- Height: 1.5-2 meters for effective wind protection

### Vine Species

**[[dolichos]] Beans (Lablab)** — edible bean production, nitrogen fixation for soil
improvement, vigorous vine growth for coverage, animal fodder from leaf material.

**Lima Beans** — food production, additional vine coverage, nitrogen fixation,
harvestable as dried beans for storage.

**Passionfruit** — fruit production, dense attractive foliage, drought-tolerant
once established, economic value from fruit sales.

### Outer Windbreak Layer
Outside the fedge, taller slow-growing species provide high-level protection:
- Roman cypress (tall, narrow, evergreen)
- [[acacia]] (nitrogen-fixing, mulch-producing)
- Established on [[drip-irrigation]] in 25-50m grids

## Seven Functions

1. **Wind protection** — primary function, reduces desiccation
2. **Food production** — beans, passionfruit
3. **Mulch production** — vine trimmings for [[compost]]
4. **Animal exclusion** — physical barrier
5. **Bee habitat** — pollen and nectar from flowering vines
6. **Nitrogen fixation** — from legume vines
7. **Aesthetic value** — flowering vines beautify boundary

## Key Facts

- Combined fence + hedge in one system
- 1.5-2m height protects 9-10m of garden
- Posts must be termite-resistant
- Multiple productive vine species
- Soft mulch production from trimming
- Bee fodder from flowering vines
- Outer layer of tall windbreak species
- 25-50m windbreak grids for large areas

## Relevance to Cultivation

Exemplifies the permaculture principle that every element should serve multiple
functions. Rather than building a fence that only excludes animals, the fedge
simultaneously produces food, mulch, bee fodder, and wind protection. Essential
in desert gardens where space is limited and every plant must justify its water.
