## Overview

**Blackstonia perfoliata** (L.) Huds., commonly known as yellow-wort, is an annual herb in the family Gentianaceae. It is native to Europe, western Asia, and North Africa.

The plant is distinguished by its glaucous, perfoliate leaves and bright yellow, four-petaled flowers that bloom from June to September. It is one of the few gentians with yellow rather than blue or purple flowers.

Yellow-wort thrives on calcareous soils including chalk grasslands, limestone pavements, and dunes. It is a key indicator species for base-rich substrates across its range.

The species was formerly placed in the genus *Chlora* before reclassification into *Blackstonia* by William Hudson in 1762. The genus name honors the English botanist and apothecary John Blackstone (1703–1753). Despite its common name "yellow-wort," the plant is not closely related to the true worts (Stellaria, Hypericum).

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:**

- Kingdom: Plantae
- Clade: Tracheophytes
- Clade: Angiosperms
- Clade: Eudicots
- Clade: Asterids
- Order: Gentianales
- Family: Gentianaceae
- Genus: *Blackstonia* 
- Species: *B. perfoliata* (L.) Huds.

**Synonyms:** *Chlora perfoliata* L., *Gentiana perfoliata* (L.) E.H.L.Krause.

The plant grows 10–40 cm tall with erect, glabrous stems. Leaves are opposite, ovate to lanceolate, glaucous blue-green, and stem-clasping (perfoliate). The upper stem leaves unite at the base, forming a disc around the stem.

Flowers are solitary at stem tips, bright yellow, 15–25 mm across, with four ovate petals. The calyx is divided nearly to the base into four narrow linear lobes. Flowering occurs from June through September. The fruit is an ovoid capsule containing numerous small seeds.

The root system consists of a slender taproot with few lateral branches. This is typical of annual species adapted to dry, open habitats. Plants occasionally exhibit a reddish tint at the stem base, especially under drought stress.

**Variation:** Two subspecies are recognized in some treatments. Subsp. *perfoliata* is the widespread tetraploid (2n = 40). Subsp. *imperfoliata* (L.) Franco & Rocha Afonso, found in Iberia and North Africa, has non-perfoliate lower leaves and a diploid chromosome count (2n = 20). The two taxa intergrade where ranges overlap.

## Distribution and Habitat

Blackstonia perfoliata is widespread across southern and central Europe, extending from the Iberian Peninsula eastward to Turkey and the Caucasus. Its northern range reaches southern Scandinavia, the Baltic states, and the British Isles.

In North Africa, it occurs in Morocco, Algeria, and Tunisia. It is also found in the eastern Mediterranean including Greece, Cyprus, and the Levant.

The species has been introduced to parts [[extending-growing-season]]. Seeds germinate in autumn or early spring, and plants flower in the first summer.

The species relies on open, disturbed microsites for establishment. It functions as a pioneer species on bare calcareous substrates, often appearing in the first year after soil disturbance.

The flowers are visited by various [[plants-without-mycorrhizal-associations]], which is unusual for Gentianaceae. Most members of the family form [[gentiana-lutea]]*.

The yellow flowers were historically used as a source of yellow dye in parts of rural southern Europe. The species is primarily valued today as an ecological indicator.

Botanists use its presence to identify calcareous grassland communities and assess habitat quality. In phytosociological classification, *B. perfoliata* is a characteristic species of the Festuco-Brometalia alliance. Its absence from seemingly suitable sites may indicate recent eutrophication or acidic deposition.

No significant modern pharmacological studies exist for *B. perfoliata*. Phytochemical analyses have confirmed the presence of gentiopicrin and other secoiridoid bitter compounds at low concentrations.
