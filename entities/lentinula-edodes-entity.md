# Lentinula edodes (*Shiitake*)

## Overview

Lentinula edodes, commonly known as shiitake, is the second most cultivated
mushroom species worldwide after *Agaricus bisporus*.

Native to East Asia, it has been used as both food and medicine for over two
thousand years in China and Japan. Shiitake accounts for roughly 25% of global
mushroom production by weight.

The species is valued for its umami-rich flavor, nutritional profile, and
well-documented immunomodulatory and antitumor properties attributed to its
beta-glucan polysaccharides.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Omphalotaceae
- **Genus:** *Lentinula*
- **Species:** *L. edodes* (Berk.) Pegler (1975)

**Synonyms:** *Armillaria edodes* Berk. (1878), *Cortinellus edodes* (Berk.)
S. Ito & S. Imai, *Lentinus edodes* (Berk.) Singer (1941)

The genus name derives from Latin *lentis* (lens-shaped), referring to the
pileus shape. *Edodes* means "edible."

### Macroscopic Features

- **Cap:** 5–12 cm diameter, convex to plano-convex with age, margin often
  inrolled when young. Surface dry, covered with fine white fibrillose scales
  that break apart, revealing brown to tan background.
- **Gills:** White to cream, adnate to sinuate, crowded. Edge entire or
  slightly serrate.
- **Stipe:** 3–8 cm long, 0.8–1.5 cm thick, central to eccentric, fibrous,
  tough. Surface white above the annulus, brown below.
- **Flesh:** White, firm, pleasant odor, meaty texture when cooked.
- **Spore print:** White to cream.
- **Spores:** Cylindrical to elliptical, 6.5–7.5 × 3–4 µm, smooth,
  inamyloid.

## Distribution and Habitat

Shiitake is native to the deciduous and mixed forests of East Asia, occurring
naturally on dead or dying hardwood logs.

Primary geographic range spans China, Japan, Korea, and eastern Russia. It has
been introduced to cultivation worldwide but rarely naturalizes outside its
native range.

Preferred host trees include *Quercus* spp. (oaks), *Castanea* spp. (chestnut),
*Fagus* spp. (beech), *Betula* spp. (birch), and *Shorea* spp. in tropical
Southeast Asia. The fungus is a white-rot decomposer, breaking down lignin and
cellulose in hardwood.

## Cultivation

Shiitake cultivation dates to at least the Song Dynasty in China (~1000 CE),
where it was grown on "shii" logs (*Castanopsis cuspidata*).

### Traditional Log Cultivation

Hardwood logs (10–15 cm diameter, ~1 m length) are inoculated by drilling
holes and inserting spawn. Logs are stacked in shaded, humid environments.
Fruiting begins 6–18 months after inoculation and continues for 3–6 years
with periodic cold-water soaking to stimulate flushes.

Biological efficiency on logs: 10–20%.

### Modern Sawdust Cultivation

Supplemented sawdust blocks sterilized at 121°C for 2–3 hours, inoculated,
and incubated at 20–25°C for 60–90 days. Biological efficiency reaches
80–120%. China produces ~90% of the global supply.

## Phytochemistry and Bioactive Compounds

- **Lentinan (LNT):** β-(1→3)-D-glucan with β-(1→6) branches. Primary
  immunomodulatory polysaccharide. Molecular weight ~400–800 kDa.
- **Lentinula edodes mycelium extract (LEM):** Water-soluble fraction
  containing polysaccharides, glycoproteins, and peptides with antiviral
  activity.
- **KS-2:** Acid-soluble polysaccharide fraction with immunostimulant
  properties.
- **Ergothioneine:** 2.0–3.5 mg/g dry weight, a potent antioxidant
  amino acid derivative.
- **Ergosterol:** Provitamin D2 precursor; UV-B irradiation of fresh
  mushrooms increases vitamin D2 content to 10–20 µg/g dry weight.
- **Eritadenine:** Cholesterol-lowering nucleotide derivative.
- **Lovastatin:** Naturally occurring statin compound at 0.5–1.0% dry
  weight.

## Pharmacology

### Immunomodulation

Lentinan acts primarily as a biological response modifier (BRM). It activates
macrophages, NK cells, and T-lymphocytes through binding to specific
β-glucan receptors (Dectin-1, CR3) on immune cells.

In Japan, lentinan is approved as an injectable adjuvant for gastric cancer
treatment, extending survival when combined with chemotherapy.

### Antitumor Activity

Tumor-inhibitory effects demonstrated in sarcoma 180, Lewis lung carcinoma,
and MH-134 hepatoma models. Mechanism involves enhanced host immune response
rather than direct cytotoxicity.

### Cardiovascular Effects

Eritadenine lowers serum cholesterol by accelerating excretion and inhibiting
absorption. Clinical studies show 10–15% reduction in total cholesterol at
doses of 9 g dried mushroom per day.
