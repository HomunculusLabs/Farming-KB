## Description

**Rana mucosa** (now reclassified as Rana muscosa), the mountain yellow-legged frog, is a species of true frog in the family Ranidae endemic to the Sierra Nevada mountains of California. It has suffered severe population declines due to chytrid fungal infection caused by [[batrachochytridium-dendrobatidis]].









## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Animalia
- **Phylum**: Chordata
- **Class**: Amphibia
- **Order**: Anura
- **Family**: Ranidae
- **Genus**: Rana
- **Species**: R. muscosa (mucosa)









## Key Facts

- Fellers et al. (2001) documented chytrid infection causing oral deformities in up to 41% of larval frogs
- Inhabits high-elevation lakes and streams in the Sierra Nevada of California
- Populations have experienced dramatic declines attributed to chytridiomycosis
- The chytrid Batrachochytridium dendrobatidis is the primary fungal pathogen
- Larval oral deformities compromise feeding ability and reduce survival to metamorphosis









## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

The impact of chytrid fungi on R. mucosa demonstrates the ecological significance of chytrid pathogens beyond their traditional roles as saprotrophs and algal parasites. For mycologists, this illustrates how environmental change can trigger fungal disease emergence. The high-elevation aquatic habitat parallels conditions where chytrid contamination can be problematic in cultivation settings.









## Ecological Role

R. muscosa was once among the most abundant vertebrates in Sierra Nevada aquatic ecosystems. Its decline has had cascading effects on alpine lake food webs, including increased algal populations and reduced prey availability for garter snakes and birds.
