## Gastropods (Soil Food Web)

## Description

Gastropods (from Greek "gastros" meaning stomach and "podos" meaning foot) are a class within the phylum Mollusca that includes **slugs** and **snails**. With approximately 40,000 species, gastropods are the largest group in the order Mollusca. In [[the-soil-food-web]] context, terrestrial [[jadam-pesticide-for-slugs-and-snails]] serve as important shredders and decomposers that accelerate the breakdown of organic matter, create soil pathways, and serve as prey for numerous organisms. While often viewed as garden pests, they play an essential role in balanced soil ecosystems.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Animalia
- **Phylum:** Mollusca
- **Class:** Gastropoda
- **Major terrestrial groups:** Pulmonata (air-breathing land snails and slugs)

## Key Facts

- **Number of species:** ~40,000 terrestrial and aquatic species; the largest class in Mollusca
- **Evolutionary history:** Land snails emerged from the sea approximately 350 million years ago; slugs later evolved from snails, losing their shells either entirely or mostly
- **Size range:** Most garden slugs are fingernail-sized; some species reach 18 inches
- **Nocturnal behavior:** Primarily active at night when moisture is highest and predation risk is lowest
- **Hermaphroditic:** Capable of self-fertilization; most cross-fertilize; each mate can lay 100-200 eggs up to 6 times per year
- **Egg longevity:** Eggs deposited just below soil surface can remain viable for years until moisture conditions are right
- **Underground activity:** Slugs spend only 5-10% of their time above ground; for every slug seen above ground, 3-4 are underground foraging

## Anatomy and Physiology

### Radula
Gastropods possess a **radula**, a series of chitinous teeth resembling a wood rasp, which grinds food into very tiny particles. Many species are capable of digesting [[cellulose]], making them effective decomposers of plant material.

### Slime Production
- Glycoprotein slime (sugars and proteins) is manufactured in cells of the muscular foot
- Slime serves as a lubricant for locomotion, allowing gastropods to stretch up to 20x their body length
- Hardened slime trails serve as navigation paths recognizable by other gastropods
- Contains chemicals obnoxious to potential predators

### Shell vs. No Shell
- **Snails:** Retain calcium shells; can seal themselves inside using an operculum (hardened slime) and survive up to 4 years in dormancy
- **Slugs:** Evolved to lose shells, gaining greater mobility, shape control, and the ability to squeeze into tight spaces; foraging range reported up to a mile per night; not limited by calcium requirements

### Sensory and Movement
- Move by gliding on a single muscular foot
- Foraging range: up to a mile per night
- Juveniles are tiny but feed like adults within 1-2 days of birth
- Sexual maturity reached at ~6 months; fully grown at ~2 years

## Ecological Role in the Soil Food Web

### Decomposition and Shredding
Gastropods speed decomposition by shredding food before consuming it, opening up organic matter for fungal and bacterial colonization. Their underground travels create pathways for air, water, and root penetration. Their slime helps bind soil particles.

### Diet
- Primary: Living plant material (lettuce, kale, and other crops)
- Secondary: Fungi, algae, lichens, and rotting organic matter
- Both above-ground and below-ground foraging

### Prey Species
Gastropods are food sources for:
- Ground beetles and rove beetles (especially larval stages)
- Spiders
- Garden snakes, salamanders, lizards
- Birds
- **[[comparison-free-living-vs-parasitic-nematodes]]:** Some commercially available nematode species parasitize slugs, "heat-seeking" into the host

### Population Control
In a healthy, balanced [[soil-food-web]], gastropod populations are controlled by their [[fukuoka-fruit-tree-disease-pest-control-natural-predators]]. They become serious pests only when chemical use and other damaging practices disrupt the food web balance, removing predator populations.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

1. **Decomposition acceleration:** Gastropods contribute to organic matter breakdown in both garden and forest settings
2. **Fungal grazing:** Slugs and snails consume fungi, including mycelium, which can impact fungal populations in cultivation systems
3. **[[integrated-pest-management]]:** [[fukuoka-textdoc-arrowhead-scale-ladybug-biological-control]] using parasitic nematodes offers an organic approach to slug management
4. **Soil structure contribution:** Slime production and underground movement improve soil aggregation and porosity
5. **Indicator species:** Balanced gastropod populations indicate a healthy predator-prey relationship in the soil food web

## Management Considerations

- Chemical control disrupts the entire soil food web, removing slug predators and worsening the problem
- Maintaining habitat for ground beetles, birds, and other natural predators keeps gastropod populations in check
- Organic mulches provide gastropod habitat but also support their predators
- In [[mushroom-cultivation]], slugs can be significant pests [[bloomfield-asterophora-and-mycoparasites-of-fruiting-bodies]]
