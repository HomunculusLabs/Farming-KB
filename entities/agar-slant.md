## Description

An agar slant is a culture storage technique in which nutrient agar is gelled at an angle inside a test tube, creating an elongated slope of growing medium with maximum surface area. Agar slants are a standard short-term to medium-term storage method for maintaining purified mushroom mycelial cultures in refrigeration for up to a year or longer. They are one of the most widely used preservation methods in mycological laboratories worldwide.

## Classification

- **Category**: Cultivation technique / Culture storage
- **Equipment**: Test tubes, test tube rack or small jar, autoclave or pressure cooker, [[laminar-flow-hood]]
- **Storage duration**: Up to 1 year under refrigeration, sometimes longer
- **Difficulty**: Beginner-friendly; requires basic laboratory equipment

## Key Facts

- Test tubes are filled one-third full with warm agar medium, then sterilized for 20-30 minutes
- After autoclaving, tubes are cooled at an angle under sterile laminar airflow to create the characteristic slanted surface
- The slant orientation maximizes the surface area available for [[mycelial-colonization]] compared to a flat gel
- A small wedge of mycelium from a purified plate is transferred to the gelled agar surface
- Once colonized, tubes are sealed with Parafilm or caps and stored in refrigeration
- Slants can be prepared in bulk and stored blank, ready for instant backup use without needing to fire up the autoclave
- Stored in refrigeration, slants keep cultures viable for up to a year, sometimes longer
- Cotter recommends storing blank or extra poured slants ready for instant backup

## Preparation Steps

1. Mix agar formula with warm water (standard [[malt-extract-agar]] or preferred formula)
2. Fill test tubes one-third full with the liquid agar
3. Place tubes upright in a test tube rack or small jar to keep them upright
4. Sterilize for 20-30 minutes in an autoclave or pressure cooker
5. Transfer to laminar flow station for cooling under sterile airflow
6. Tip the test tube rack or jar at an angle to create the slanted agar surface
7. Allow tubes to cool completely until the agar is firmly gelled
8. Transfer a small mycelial wedge from a purified culture to the slant surface
9. Allow colonization at appropriate temperature
10. Seal tubes and store in refrigerator

## Advantages

- **Compact storage**: Many cultures fit in a small refrigerator space compared to petri plates
- **Easy organization**: Simple to label and organize by species, origin code, and generation
- **Reduced contamination**: Provides a clean, sealed environment less prone to contamination than plates
- **Quick retrieval**: Simple to retrieve and transfer to fresh media when needed
- **Advance preparation**: Blank slants can be prepared in bulk for quick backups
- **Cost effective**: Test tubes are inexpensive and reusable

## Role in Culture Preservation Strategy

Agar slants are one component of a comprehensive culture preservation strategy. Cotter recommends maintaining cultures in multiple forms at multiple locations to protect against catastrophic loss from power outages, fire, or natural disaster. Other methods include:

- **Submerged agar cubes**: Over 1 year in water, longer in mineral oil
- **Colonized grain**: Submerged in water or mineral oil, up to 1 year refrigerated
- **Freezing**: Near-indefinite with glycerol cryoprotectant
- **Dehydration**: Up to 1 year for some species

## Relevance to Cultivation

Agar slants are one of the foundational [[culture-storage-methods]] in [[mushroom-cultivation]]. They provide a reliable, space-efficient way to maintain purified isolates for medium-term storage. Combined with other storage methods, slants form part of a comprehensive culture preservation strategy that protects valuable genetic material from loss and ensures cultivators always have access to pure, vigorous cultures for spawn production.

## Practical Applications

Agar slant has several important applications in sustainable agriculture and ecological design. Practitioners integrate these methods into holistic management systems that enhance productivity while maintaining ecological balance.
