## Acremonium lolii

## Description

Acremonium lolii (now reclassified as [[epichloe-festucae]] var. lolii) is a fungal
endophyte intimately associated with perennial ryegrass ([[lolium-perenne]]). This
fungus is inseparable from its host in nature, having co-evolved to the point
where the two partners function as a single ecological unit. A. lolii produces
the neurotoxic alkaloid lolitrem B, which causes ryegrass staggers in grazing
livestock, along with peramine, which deters insect herbivores.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Eukaryota
- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Sordariomycetes
- **Order:** Hypocreales
- **Family:** Clavicipitaceae
- **Genus:** Acremonium (now Epichloe)
- **Species:** A. lolii (E. festucae var. lolii)

## Key Facts

- **Inseparable association:** Clay (1990) cites A. lolii and its ryegrass host
  as an example of an endophyte association so closely evolved that the two
  partners are inseparable, representing one end of the mutualism continuum.
- **Lolitrem B production:** Produces lolitrem B, an indole diterpene alkaloid
  that causes ryegrass staggers, a neurological disorder in sheep and cattle
  characterized by tremors, ataxia, and in severe cases, collapse.
- **Peramine production:** Also produces peramine, a pyrrolopyrazine alkaloid
  that deters Argentine stem weevil (Listronotus bonariensis), a major pest of
  ryegrass pastures in New Zealand and Australia.
- **Vertical transmission:** Transmitted from plant to plant via the seed,
  ensuring all new plants inherit the endophyte (Wilson, 1993). This vertical
  transmission maintains the symbiosis across plant generations.
- **Seed protection:** A. lolii infection of seeds protects against insect
  damage during storage and germination. Clay (1990) documented cases where
  endophyte-free seeds suffered reduced germination due to insect damage while
  infected seeds germinated normally.
- **New Zealand significance:** The A. lolii-ryegrass association is of major
  economic importance in New Zealand pastoral agriculture, driving extensive
  research and breeding programs for endophyte-enhanced ryegrass cultivars.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Pasture grass breeding:** New Zealand and Australian breeding programs have
  developed AR1 and AR37 endophyte strains that provide insect resistance
  without causing ryegrass staggers, directly building on A. lolii research.
- **Livestock health management:** Understanding the toxicology of lolitrem B
  has enabled development of grazing management strategies to minimize livestock
  exposure during periods of high alkaloid concentration.
- **Seed production:** Endophyte viability in stored seed is a critical concern
  for the seed industry, as lolitrem B-producing strains lose viability faster
  than novel endophyte strains under warm, humid storage conditions.
- **Model system for co-evolution:** The inseparable nature of the A.
  lolii-ryegrass association provides a model for studying long-term
  co-evolutionary dynamics between fungi and their plant hosts.

## Ecological Interactions

- Systemically colonizes perennial ryegrass (Lolium perenne) above-ground
  tissues.
- Produces lolitrem B (toxic to mammals) and peramine (deterrent to insects).
- Reduces feeding and oviposition by Argentine stem weevil (Listronotus
  bonariensis) and other insect herbivores.
- Competes with pathogenic fungi on leaf surfaces, providing additional disease
  protection.
- Influences nitrogen allocation within the host plant, redirecting resources
  toward alkaloid synthesis.
