## Description

Thermophilic organisms are microorganisms that thrive at **elevated temperatures**, specifically between 104°F and 150°F (40°C and 65°C) and above. In the composting cycle, thermophiles dominate the **second stage** (thermophilic phase) after mesophilic activity raises the pile temperature above 104°F (40°C). Their metabolic activity drives the most intensive period of organic matter decomposition, pathogen destruction, and weed seed elimination in the composting process. Thermophilic bacteria and fungi break down the most complex carbohydrates, proteins, and hemicelluloses that resist mesophilic degradation.

## Classification

- **Type**: Temperature-based ecological classification
- **Temperature range**: 104–150°F+ (40–65°C+)
- **Includes**: Thermophilic bacteria, thermotolerant fungi, actinomycetes
- **Phase in composting**: Second stage (between mesophilic initiation and maturation)

## Key Facts

### Role in the Composting Cycle

#### Transition to Thermophilic Phase
- Mesophilic metabolic activity raises compost temperature to 104°F (40°C), triggering the transition.
- Mesophilic organisms that cannot tolerate higher temperatures either die, go dormant, or survive as **heat-resistant endospores**.
- Thermophilic organisms, adapted to higher temperatures, **take over** all major decomposition functions.

#### Thermophilic Decomposition Activity
- **Complex carbohydrates** are fully broken down during this phase.
- **Proteins** undergo decomposition, releasing nitrogen compounds.
- **Hemicelluloses** — more resistant structural polysaccharides — are decayed.
- Many additional bacteria join or assume more prominent roles: *Arthrobacter* spp., *Pseudomonas* spp., *Streptomyces* and other **actinomycetes**.
- Thermotolerant fungi increase their activity on recalcitrant materials.

#### Pathogen and Weed Seed Destruction
- This is the **critical sanitation phase** of composting.
- At **140–150°F (60–65°C)**, pathogenic microbes (including *E. coli*, *Salmonella*, and plant pathogens) are killed.
- At **150°F (65°C)**, weed seeds are destroyed, preventing them from germinating when compost is applied.
- Piles should be maintained at 140–150°F (60–65°C) for **at least several days** for effective sanitation.
- **Never exceed 155°F (68°C)** — this begins to burn off carbon and reduces compost quality.

### Key Thermophilic Compost Organisms

| Organism | Role in Thermophilic Phase |
|----------|---------------------------|
| *Arthrobacter* spp. | Complex carbohydrate breakdown |
| *Pseudomonas* spp. | Protein decomposition, diverse substrate utilization |
| *Streptomyces* spp. | Cellulose and hemicellulose decay |
| Thermophilic actinomycetes | Degradation of resistant compounds |
| Thermotolerant fungi | Continued lignocellulose processing |

### Temperature Management

#### Monitoring
- A properly made pile should reach **135°F (57°C) in 24–72 hours**.
- Center of pile typically reaches **135°F in one day** and **150°F in three days**.
- Monitor with a soil thermometer, oven thermometer, or by inserting a metal rebar/gutter nail (heat conducts through metal).

#### Corrective Actions
- **Pile not heating**: Turn to add oxygen; add fresh green material (simple sugars feed bacteria); add newspaper, fruit pulps, or commercial compost inoculant.
- **Pile too hot (>155°F/68°C)**: Turn to cool temporarily; add water; add brown (carbon) materials to shift ratio away from bacterial dominance.
- **Pile cooling prematurely**: Add more green, high-nitrogen material.
- **Pile staying too hot**: Add more brown, carbon-containing material; aerate by turning.

### Transition to Maturation
- As complex substrates diminish, metabolic activity decreases and temperature drops below 104°F (40°C).
- Mesophilic organisms re-emerge from protected spores and resume dominance.
- Compost enters the **maturation stage** where the final resistant materials (especially lignin) are decomposed.
- Compost is considered mature when turning no longer causes reheating.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

1. **Compost sanitation**: The thermophilic phase is the primary mechanism for producing pathogen-free, weed-seed-free compost — essential for safe application to food crops and mushroom substrates.
2. **Hot composting speed**: Thermophilic activity enables compost production in as little as **2–3 weeks** (vs. 1+ year for cold composting), by dramatically accelerating decomposition rates.
3. **Substrate pasteurization**: The same thermophilic principles apply to mushroom substrate pasteurization, where temperatures of 140–160°F (60–71°C) eliminate competitors and pathogens.
4. **Carbon management**: Excessive thermophilic activity (>155°F) can "burn" carbon, reducing organic matter content and compost quality — temperature monitoring is critical.
5. **Soil food web reset**: The thermophilic phase kills most mesophilic organisms (except spore-formers), creating a biological reset. Diversity is re-established during maturation as mesophiles return.

## Temperature Profile Summary

| Temperature | Significance |
|------------|-------------|
| 32°F (0°C) | Psychrophilic bacteria still active |
| 68°F (20°C) | Mesophilic range begins |
| 104°F (40°C) | Thermophilic range begins |
| 135°F (57°C) | Target temperature within 24–72 hours |
| 140–150°F (60–65°C) | Pathogen destruction zone |
| 150°F (65°C) | Weed seed destruction temperature |
| 155°F (68°C) | Carbon burn-off begins — do not exceed |
