## Overview

**Hericium novae-zealandiae** is a species of tooth fungus in the family
Hericiaceae, endemic to New Zealand. Commonly referred to as the New Zealand
lion's mane, it is closely related to the well-known *Hericium erinaceus*
and shares many of the same culinary and medicinal properties. The species
produces distinctive cascading spines rather than gills or pores, forming
white to cream-colored fruiting bodies that resemble a waterfall or icicles.

Like other *Hericium* species, *H. novae-zealandiae* is both edible and
medicinal, with growing interest in its neuroprotective and
neuroregenerative compounds, particularly hericenones and erinacines.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:** Basidiomycota → Agaricomycetes → Russulales →
**Hericiaceae → *Hericium → *H. novae-zealandiae***

**Authority:** (G. Stev.) Segedin, 1991. Originally described as *Hydnum
novae-zealandiae* by G.H. Cunningham (later revised by Segedin).

The species was long considered conspecific with *Hericium coralloides* but
was elevated to species rank based on distinct morphological and ecological
characteristics particular to New Zealand populations.

### Macroscopic Features

- **Fruiting body:** 5–20 cm broad, forming irregular, branched masses
  of soft, hanging spines
- **Spines:** 1–3 cm long, white aging to pale cream or yellowish, soft
  and easily compressed, growing in cascading clusters
- **Stipe:** Rudimentary or absent; attaches directly to substrate
- **Flesh:** White, soft when fresh, becoming rubbery as it dries
- **Spore print:** White to very pale cream

### Microscopic Features

- **Spores:** Subglobose to broadly ellipsoid, 5.5–7.5 × 5–6.5 µm,
  smooth, hyaline, amyloid
- **Basidia:** Clavate, 25–40 × 5–7 µm, four-spored
- **Hyphal system:** Monomitic; generative hyphae thin-walled with
  clamp connections

## Distribution and Habitat

- **Substrate:** Saprotrophic on dead and dying hardwood timber, especially
  *Nothofagus* (southern beech), *Agathis australis* (kauri), and various
  *Podocarpus* species
- **Ecology:** A white-rot decomposer that breaks down lignin and cellulose
  in dead wood, playing an important role in forest [[comparison-nutrient-cycling-vs-nutrient-dense-gardening]]
- **Season:** Autumn to early winter (March–July in the [[guzman-allen-gartz-africa-southern-hemisphere-underexplored-neurotropic-fungi]])
- **Frequency:** Moderately common in suitable habitat but easily overlooked
  due to its preference for high canopy positions on standing dead wood

## Bioactive Compounds

Like other *Hericium* species, *H. novae-zealandiae* produces a range of
bioactive metabolites. While research specific to this species is limited,
phytochemical studies of New Zealand populations confirm the presence of:

- **Hericenones C–H:** Cyathane-type terpenoids isolated from the fruiting
  body, associated with stimulation of nerve growth factor (NGF) synthesis
- **Erinacines A–I:** Compounds found in the mycelium, particularly
  potent stimulators of NGF and brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF)
- **β-Glucans:** Polysaccharides with immunomodulatory and
  anti-inflammatory properties (primarily (1→3)- and (1→6)-linked)
- **Hericium polysaccharides:** Water-soluble fractions demonstrating
  antioxidant and hepatoprotective activity in vitro

## Pharmacology and Medicinal Potential

Research on *H. novae-zealandiae* specifically is limited but growing,
with most pharmacological inferences drawn from the well-studied
*H. erinaceus*. Key areas of investigation include:

- **Neuroprotection:** Hericenones and erinacines cross the blood-brain
  barrier and stimulate NGF synthesis, showing promise in models of
  Alzheimer's disease and peripheral nerve injury
- **Cognitive enhancement:** Animal studies demonstrate improved spatial
  memory and reduced cognitive decline with oral administration
- **Anti-inflammatory:** β-glucans modulate macrophage and NK cell activity,
  reducing pro-inflammatory cytokine production
- **Gastric protection:** Traditional use and preliminary studies suggest
  protective effects against gastric mucosal damage

Traditional Māori use of this species is not well-documented, though
other native fungi were used medicinally. Modern interest centers on
its potential as a nootropic and neuroprotective supplement.

## Cultivation

Cultivation of *H. novae-zealandiae* follows protocols similar to those
for *H. erinaceus*, though the species is less commercially developed.

- **Substrate:** Supplemented hardwood sawdust (beech, oak, or mixed
  hardwoods) with wheat bran or rice bran at 10–20% supplementation
- **Spawn:** Grain spawn (rye or millet) colonized at 20–25°C for 14–21
  days; full substrate colonization in 21–35 days
- **Fruiting trigger:** Temperature drop to 15–18°C, high humidity
  (90–95%), ambient light, and adequate fresh air exchange
- **Yield:** [[stamets-biological-efficiency-yield]] of 50–80% over 2–3 flushes
- **Primary challenges:** Slow colonization compared to *H. erinaceus*;
  susceptibility to Trichoderma contamination; limited commercial strain
  availability outside New Zealand
