## Echinacea angustifolia

## Overview

**Echinacea angustifolia** DC. (narrow-leaved purple coneflower) is a perennial herbaceous flowering plant in the family Asteraceae, native to the prairies and plains of central [[childrens-herbal-medicine]] in the late 19th century and became one of the best-selling herbal supplements in [[query-what-are-the-main-bioactive-compounds-in-medicinal-plants]] responsible for immunomodulatory activity. *E. angustifolia* root typically contains higher alkylamide concentrations than *E. purpurea*, supporting its reputation for greater potency.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Division:** Magnoliophyta
- **Class:** Magnoliopsida
- **Order:** Asterales
- **Family:** Asteraceae
- **Tribe:** Heliantheae
- **Genus:** *Echinacea* Moench
- **Species:** *E. angustifolia* DC. (1836)
- **Synonyms:** *Brauneria angustifolia* (DC.) Heller, *Echinacea angustifolia* var. *angustifolia*

The species epithet *angustifolia* (Latin: "narrow-leaved") distinguishes it from the broader-leaved *E. purpurea*. Two varieties are sometimes recognized: var. *angustifolia* (typical) and var. *strigosa* McGregor, the latter with strigose (stiff-hairy) stems and leaves.

The genus *Echinacea* comprises 10 species, all endemic to North America east of the Rocky Mountains.
*E. angustifolia* is the most western species in the genus, adapted to the drier conditions of the Great Plains compared to the more eastern *E. purpurea* and *E. pallida*.

*E. angustifolia* is a drought-tolerant perennial, 30–60 cm tall at maturity. The stems are erect, unbranched, and covered with coarse hairs (hirsute). The taproot is thick, fleshy, and aromatic, reaching depths of 60–120 cm — this is the primary medicinal plant part.

Leaves are lanceolate to narrowly elliptic, 5–20 cm long and 1–4 cm wide, with entire margins. They are alternate, progressively reduced upward on the stem. Basal leaves form a rosette; cauline leaves are sessile.

The inflorescence is a single terminal capitulum (flower head) 4–8 cm in diameter. Ray florets are 2–3 cm long, reflexed, pink to purple (rarely white). The receptacle is conical to hemispherical, covered with sharp, stiff chaff (paleae) — the source of the genus name (*echinos*, Greek for hedgehog). Flowering occurs June to August, with seed maturation in September and October.

## Distribution and Habitat

*E. angustifolia* is native to the Great Plains of North America, ranging from southern Saskatchewan and Manitoba (Canada) through Montana, the Dakotas, Nebraska, Kansas, Oklahoma, and Texas, with scattered populations extending into Colorado, Wyoming, and New Mexico.

It inhabits **prairies, open woodlands, barrens, and rocky hillsides** at elevations of 300–1800 m. The species prefers well-drained loamy or sandy soils and full sun. It is highly drought-tolerant due to its deep taproot system.

Wild populations have declined significantly due to overharvesting for the herbal supplement trade and habitat conversion to agriculture.
The species is listed as threatened or endangered in several U.S. states, including Montana, Kansas, and Tennessee.
