The species forms extensive, dark green to purplish-green mats on damp soil, rocks, and decaying wood along stream banks and in moist woodlands. Its thallus is relatively large for a simple thalloid liverwort.

Its common names include overleaf liverwort and umbrella liverwort, referring to the appearance of its thallus margins. It is the type species of the genus Pellia.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Pellia epiphylla was described by Carl Linnaeus in 1753 as Jungermannia epiphylla. The species was later transferred to Pellia by August Johann Georg Karl Batsch in 1789.

The genus Pellia contains approximately 6–10 accepted species worldwide, with Pellia epiphylla being by far the most common and widespread member.

The thallus is broadly ovate to lanceolate, 1–3 cm wide and 3–10 cm long. It is irregularly branched and forms overlapping rosettes or extensive creeping mats.

Individual thalli can persist for several years, with the growing apex producing new tissue while older portions gradually decay. This creates a characteristic zonation of age classes within colonies.

The upper surface is dull green to purplish-green, often with a glossy sheen when wet. A conspicuous midrib runs the length of the thallus, visible as a raised central vein.

Unlike Marchantia and Lunularia, Pellia lacks air pores and air chambers on the thallus surface. This anatomical simplicity places it in the simple thalloid group rather than the complex thalloid group.

The lower surface bears numerous colorless rhizoids and scattered purple scales. The rhizoids are both smooth and pegged, with internal wall projections.

The thallus margins are distinctly undulate and often overlap neighboring thalli. The margin lacks gemma cups entirely, distinguishing Pellia from Lunularia.

Male and female reproductive structures are borne on the same thallus. The archegonia are enclosed in a tubular perianth that projects slightly above the thallus surface.
