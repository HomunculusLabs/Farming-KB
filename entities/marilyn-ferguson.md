## Marilyn Ferguson

Marilyn Ferguson (1938–2008) was an American author and journalist
best known for *The Aquarian Conspiracy* (1980). The book
documented and named the emerging consciousness movement of the
1970s, selling over two million copies worldwide.

## Early Life and Career

Ferguson grew up in Colorado and studied English at the University
of Colorado. She worked as a newspaper reporter and editor before
turning to freelance writing. Her early journalism covered
education, psychology, and social trends.

A transformative personal experience with consciousness expansion
in the early 1970s redirected her focus. She became fascinated by
the intersection of neuroscience, psychology, and spiritual
practice. This curiosity led her to interview hundreds of
researchers, therapists, and spiritual teachers across disciplines.

## Brain/Mind Bulletin

In 1975, Ferguson launched the *Brain/Mind Bulletin*, a newsletter
covering research in neuroscience, consciousness, and human
potential. The bulletin synthesized findings from fields that
rarely communicated with each other. Topics ranged from split-brain
research to meditation studies to psychedelic therapy.

The *Brain/Mind Bulletin* became required reading for researchers
and practitioners across multiple fields. Ferguson's editorial
approach emphasized accessible summaries of complex research. The
publication ran for over two decades and built a network of
thousands of subscribers interested in consciousness studies.

The newsletter connected researchers working in isolation.
Neuroscientists learned about meditation research, psychologists
discovered brain hemisphere studies, and spiritual teachers
encountered clinical findings. Ferguson functioned as a hub and
translator between communities.

## The Aquarian Conspiracy

Ferguson published *The Aquarian Conspiracy: Personal and Social
Transformation in the 1980s* in 1980. The book argued that a
loosely networked movement of scientists, therapists, educators,
and spiritual seekers was creating a paradigm shift in Western
culture. She named this network the Aquarian Conspiracy.
