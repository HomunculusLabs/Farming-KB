# Yucca filamentosa

## Overview

- **Common names:** Adam's [[mushroom-bag-inoculation-short-needle-vs-long-needle]], Beargrass, Needle Palm,
  Spoonleaf Yucca
- **[[soil-temperature-plant-growth-rate-doubling-rule]] form:** Ever[[green-cannabis-grow-bible-seed-selection-and-seed-banks]] perennial shrub, acaulescent
  (stemless or nearly so)
- **Lifespan:** Long-lived; individual rosettes persist 20+
  years; clonal colonies are indefinite
- **Native range:** Southeastern [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]], Virginia to
  Florida, west to Louisiana
- **Key trait:** Classic obligate pollination mutualism with
  yucca moths (Tegeticula spp.)
- **Multi-use:** [[comparison-scented-geraniums-vs-edible-flowers]] and stalks, fiber crop,
  soap-[[query-why-are-my-plants-not-producing-fruit-and-how-do-i-hand-pollinate]] [[nutrient-movement-through-soil-to-plant-roots]]
- **Hardiness:** One of the most cold-hardy yuccas; USDA
  Zones 5-10

## Taxonomy

- **Binomial:** Yucca filamentosa L.
- **Family:** Asparagaceae (subfamily Agavoideae)
- **Order:** Asparagales
- **Authority:** Carl Linnaeus, 1753 ([[golden-guide-hallucinogenic-plants-dmt-containing-species]] Plantarum)
- **Genus:** Yucca L. (approximately 40-50 species in the
  Americas and Caribbean)
- **Synonyms:**
  - Yucca filamentosa var. concava (Haw.) Engelm.
  - Yucca filamentosa var. smalliana (Fernald) H.E. Ahles
  - Yucca concava Haw.
  - Yucca smalliana Fernald (sometimes treated as separate
    species)
  - Yucca flaccida Haw. (often merged with Y. filamentosa)
- **Etymology:** "filamentosa" refers to the conspicuous curly
  white filaments along the leaf margins

## Description and Morphology

- **Habit:** Stemless evergreen shrub; dense basal rosette
  60-120 cm in diameter
- **Leaves:** 30-75 cm long, 2.5-5 cm wide, rigid,
  lanceolate, sword-shaped, dark green
- **Leaf margins:** Entire with numerous thin, curly white
  filaments (peeling leaf fibers) -- the namesake feature
- **Leaf apex:** Sharp terminal spine; handle with care
- **Trunk:** Usually absent; very old specimens may develop
  a short procumbent stem to 30 cm
- **Inflorescence:** Tall paniculate flower spike 1.5-3 m
  rising from the center of the rosette
- **Flowers:** Pendulous, bell-shaped, creamy-white, 5-7 cm
  long, 6 tepals, waxy texture; highly fragrant at night
- **Flowering period:** June to July; blooms once per rosette,
  then the flowering rosette dies (monocarpic)
- **Fruit:** Erect, woody, loculicidal capsule 5-8 cm long,
  2.5-4 cm broad
- **Seeds:** Numerous, black, flat, winged, approximately
  6-8 mm; 20-30% consumed by moth larvae
- **Root [[plant-immune-system-pti-eti]]:** Deep taproot with fibrous laterals;
  extremely drought-resistant
- **Monocarpic flowering:** The flowering rosette dies after
  seed set, but surrounding offsets (pups) persist and
  continue the colony

## Distribution and Habitat

- **Native range:** Southeastern US coastal plain from
  Virginia [[query-how-do-i-use-knf-inputs-through-the-plant-growth-cycle]] Carolinas, Georgia, northern Florida
- **Inland range:** Extending west to Alabama, Mississippi,
  Louisiana, and into the Piedmont region
- **Disjunct populations:** North to New Jersey along the
  Atlantic coast
- **Naturalized:** Widely beyond native range in eastern US
  and parts of the Midwest; also naturalized in Europe
- **Habitat:** Dry sandy coastal plains and sandhills; rocky
  barrens and glades; oak-pine woodlands, forest edges,
  roadsides, disturbed areas, and dune systems
- **Elevation:** Sea level to approximately 300 meters
- **Soil:** Sandy, gravelly, or rocky; tolerates poor
