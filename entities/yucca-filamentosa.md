## Description and Morphology

- **Habit:** Stemless evergreen shrub; dense basal rosette
  60-120 cm in diameter
- **Leaves:** 30-75 cm long, 2.5-5 cm wide, rigid,
  lanceolate, sword-shaped, dark green
- **Leaf margins:** Entire with numerous thin, curly white
  filaments (peeling leaf fibers) -- the namesake feature
- **Leaf apex:** Sharp terminal spine; handle with care
- **Trunk:** Usually absent; very old specimens may develop
  a short procumbent stem to 30 cm
- **Inflorescence:** Tall paniculate flower spike 1.5-3 m
  rising from the center of the rosette
- **Flowers:** Pendulous, bell-shaped, creamy-white, 5-7 cm
  long, 6 tepals, waxy texture; highly fragrant at night
- **Flowering period:** June to July; blooms once per rosette,
  then the flowering rosette dies (monocarpic)
- **Fruit:** Erect, woody, loculicidal capsule 5-8 cm long,
  2.5-4 cm broad
- **Seeds:** Numerous, black, flat, winged, approximately
  6-8 mm; 20-30% consumed by moth larvae
- **Root [[query-how-do-i-use-knf-inputs-through-the-plant-growth-cycle]] Carolinas, Georgia, northern Florida
- **Inland range:** Extending west to Alabama, Mississippi,
  Louisiana, and into the Piedmont region
- **Disjunct populations:** North to New Jersey along the
  Atlantic coast
- **Naturalized:** Widely beyond native range in eastern US
  and parts of the Midwest; also naturalized in Europe
- **Habitat:** Dry sandy coastal plains and sandhills; rocky
  barrens and glades; oak-pine woodlands, forest edges,
  roadsides, disturbed areas, and dune systems
- **Elevation:** Sea level to approximately 300 meters
