# Zanthoxylum piperitum

## Overview

**Zanthoxylum piperitum** is a deciduous shrub or small tree in the Rutaceae family, native to Japan and Korea.

It is the source of **sanshō** (山椒), one of the most important traditional spices in Japanese cuisine.

The plant produces small, reddish-brown pericarps (husks) that deliver a unique tingling, numbing sensation on the tongue caused by the alkylamide sanshool.

Sanshō is considered one of Japan's essential seasonings alongside wasabi, shiso, and yuzu, and has been used in [[fukuoka-japanese-cooking-culture-disappearing]] for over a millennium.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:**

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Family:** Rutaceae
- **Genus:** Zanthoxylum
- **Species:** *Z. piperitum* DC.

**Botanical authority:** Augustin Pyramus de Candolle, 1824.

The genus *Zanthoxylum* contains approximately 250 species distributed across temperate and [[fukuoka-natural-farming-in-tropical-regions]] worldwide.

Several related species produce similar spices, including Chinese *Z. bungeanum* (huājiāo) and Nepalese *Z. armatum* (timur).

**Morphology:**

Plants are dioecious shrubs or small trees reaching 2–5 meters in height with a spreading, multi-stemmed habit.

Branches are armed with sharp, paired prickles at the nodes — a characteristic feature of the genus.

Leaves are pinnately compound, 10–20 cm long, with 5–11 ovate to lanceolate leaflets per leaf.

Leaflets are 2–5 cm long, serrate, and aromatic when crushed, emitting a citrus-like fragrance.

Flowers are small, yellowish-green, borne in axillary cymes, with male and female flowers on separate plants.

The fruit is a small follicle, 3–5 mm in diameter, splitting open when ripe to reveal a single glossy black seed.

The aromatic pericarp (husk) is reddish-brown at maturity and is the commercially harvested part of the plant.

## Distribution and Habitat

The species is native to Japan, where it occurs from Hokkaido southward through Honshu, Shikoku, and Kyushu.

It also grows naturally in the Korean Peninsula and has been reported in parts of northeastern China.

Natural populations are found in mountainous forests, forest margins, and stream banks at elevations of 200–1,800 meters.

The plant prefers partial shade to full sun in well-drained, slightly acidic to neutral forest soils.

It is cold-hardy to approximately -15°C, making it suitable for cultivation in [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]].

Wild populations have declined in some areas due to habitat loss, though the species is not currently considered threatened.

## Culinary Use

The dried pericarp, known as **sanshō** or **Japanese pepper**, is the primary culinary product derived from this species.

Sanshō has a distinctive flavor combining citrus notes with a numbing, tingling mouthfeel produced by hydroxy-α-sanshool.

It is a key ingredient in **shichimi tōgarashi** (seven-flavor chili pepper), Japan's most popular table spice blend.

Ground sanshō is commonly sprinkled over grilled eel (unagi), yakitori, and noodle dishes.

The young spring shoots (kinome) are used as a garnish and flavoring in traditional Japanese dishes.

Kinome has a fresh, minty-citrus flavor and is typically lightly bruised or slapped against the hand to release its aroma before use.

Sanshō pairs particularly well with fatty meats and fish, as the numbing sensation enhances the perception of richness.
