## Overview

A mobile greenhouse is a greenhouse structure mounted on rails or wheels that can be physically moved between two or more field positions. The concept dates to 1898 in England, where a large glasshouse was moved on railroad wheels and steel rails. Eliot Coleman adapted the concept at [[four-season-farm]] using inexpensive pipe-frame hoop houses that are manually pulled between summer-crop and winter-crop positions each October and back again the following year.

## Historical Origins

The first documented mobile greenhouse was built in England in 1898. It was a large glasshouse supported by an iron framework on railroad wheels running on steel rails. Coleman copied the concept but at a far less expensive scale, using lightweight pipe-frame plastic-covered structures.

## Design at Four Season Farm

### Structure
- Standard 30-by-96-foot gothic-style hoop houses
- Pipe-frame construction with plastic covering
- Mounted for manual movement along the field
- Two positions: summer-crop site and winter-crop site

### Movement Cycle
1. **August**: Winter crops sown outdoors in the field where the greenhouse will move
2. **Summer (August-October)**: Greenhouse remains over summer crops (tomatoes, peppers, cucumbers, eggplants, melons, [[growing-sweet-potatoes]])
3. **Mid-late October**: Greenhouse moved to cover the winter-sown crops
4. **Winter**: Greenhouse protects winter vegetables
5. **Following October**: Process reverses — greenhouse moves back to the summer position

## Advantages

### 1. Avoidance of August Cooling Costs
By sowing winter crops outdoors and moving the greenhouse over them later, Coleman avoids the need to cool a greenhouse during August planting. The greenhouse stays over heat-loving summer crops during the hottest month.

### 2. Pest and Disease Break
For one year out of every two, each growing position is uncovered and exposed to:
- Sun (solarization of soil pathogens)
- Rain (leaching of accumulated salts)
- Wind (physical removal of debris and pests)
- Snow (freeze-thaw cycling)
- Natural biological competition

This rotation dramatically reduces the buildup of greenhouse-specific pests, diseases, and excess soil nutrients.

### 3. Green Manure Opportunity
During the uncovered year, the exposed position can be planted with a long-term, deep-rooting, leguminous green manure crop:
- Up to 13 months (June through following July) if summer vegetable crop is forgone
- Up to 10 months (September through July) if sown toward end of summer-crop season
- Green manures should be turned under 3-4 weeks before the next planting date

## Economics

The mobile greenhouse concept reduces costs by:
- Eliminating the need for separate summer and winter greenhouse structures
- Reducing pest management costs through biological rotation
- Eliminating cooling energy costs for August planting
- [[building-soil-fertility-long-term]] through green manure years
- Avoiding permanent greenhouse soil degradation

## Technical Considerations

- Greenhouse alignment: East-west axis for optimal solar gain
- Rail or track system must be level and stable
- Moving operation requires adequate labor (typically crew of 4-6)
- Pipe anchors at each position to secure the structure against wind
- Planning must account for crop timing relative to move date

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

The mobile greenhouse concept has direct applications for integrated mushroom-vegetable systems:

- **Substrate rotation**: Moving the structure between mushroom and vegetable positions allows [[fukuoka-microbes-scavengers-natural-decomposition-biosystem]] of spent substrate
- **Pest break**: Uncovered periods break pest cycles for both mushroom and vegetable crops
- **Soil building**: Green manure years improve soil for subsequent mushroom bed production
- **Cost efficiency**: One structure serves multiple production purposes across seasons
- **Solarization**: Uncovered periods use solar energy to sterilize soil, reducing pathogen load for subsequent [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]]
