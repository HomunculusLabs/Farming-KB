## Overview

**Voyria rosea** is a fully mycoheterotrophic herb in the family Gentianaceae, meaning it obtains all its nutrition from soil fungi rather than through photosynthesis.

The species is commonly called the ghost gentian or pink saprophytic gentian due to its pale pink to white, completely chlorophyll-free appearance.

It is one of approximately 20 species in the genus Voyria, all of which are leafless and non-photosynthetic.

Voyria rosea is found in [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-south-america]] where it grows on the forest floor in deep shade.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Voyria rosea was first described by Jean-Baptiste Lamarck and later transferred to its current placement by several authors.

The genus Voyria is unique within Gentianaceae for its complete loss of chlorophyll and leaves.

Plants are small, 5–25 cm tall, with a simple unbranched stem that is pale pink to whitish and somewhat translucent.

True leaves are absent entirely. Instead, the stem bears small scale-like bracts 2–4 mm long that lack chlorophyll.

Flowers are terminal, solitary or in small clusters, 1–2 cm across, with 4–5 pale pink to white petals.

The corolla is tubular to campanulate, delicate in texture, and persists briefly before withering.

Roots are highly modified, forming a dense coral-like mass that serves as the interface with [[mycorrhizal-fungi]].

The root system lacks root hairs and instead features dense fungal hyphae colonizing the cortical cells.

The stem contains no chloroplasts and the vascular tissue is reduced compared to photosynthetic gentians.

Capsules are ovoid, 4–6 mm long, dehiscing irregularly to release thousands of minute seeds.

Each seed is less than 0.3 mm long, among the smallest in the Gentianaceae family.

## Distribution and Habitat

Voyria rosea occurs in tropical lowland and premontane forests from southern Mexico through [[dighton-ericoid-mycorrhizal-nutrient-acquisition]].

The fungal partners are saprotrophic basidiomycetes that decompose leaf litter and wood on the forest floor.

This distinguishes Voyria from most mycoheterotrophic orchids, which typically associate with [[arbuscule-isolation-and-nutrient-exchange-mechanisms]].

This nutritional strategy allows Voyria to thrive in deep forest shade where photosynthesis is impossible.

## Ecology

Voyria rosea appears only briefly above the leaf litter, typically during the wet season when conditions favor fungal activity.

The above-ground portion may persist for only a few weeks before flowering, setting seed, and senescing.

Below ground, the root-fungus association persists year-round, allowing the plant to survive extended dry periods subterraneanly.

Pollination is poorly documented but likely involves small flies or bees attracted to the pale flowers.
