# Osmotic Shock (Chemical Fertilizer Damage)

## Description

Osmotic shock is the cellular damage and death that occurs when soil organisms are exposed to the high salt concentrations in synthetic chemical fertilizers. When nitrogen-based lawn fertilizers (which are salts) contact soil microbes, water flows out of microbial cells toward the higher salt concentration outside, literally bursting cell walls and killing the organisms. This is the primary mechanism by which chemical fertilizers destroy soil food web biology, creating the vicious cycle of increasing fertilizer dependence.

## Classification

- **Type:** Cellular damage mechanism
- **Cause:** High salt concentrations from synthetic chemical fertilizers
- **Effect:** Water loss from cells, cell wall rupture, organism death
- **Scope:** Affects bacteria, fungi, protozoa, nematodes — all soil food web organisms

## Key Facts

- Chemical lawn fertilizers are salts — high concentrations of nitrogen compounds (nitrates, ammonium salts)
- When these salts contact soil microbes, they create an osmotic gradient
- Water flows from inside microbial cells (lower salt concentration) to the surrounding soil (higher salt concentration)
- This water loss causes cells to literally burst through their own cell walls
- The result is massive die-off of soil food web organisms: bacteria, fungi, protozoa, and nematodes
- Rule of thumb: 100 pounds of nitrogen fertilizer per acre will wipe out a healthy soil food web
- Lesser quantities still damage the food web proportionally
- Organisms not killed outright are driven away by lack of food resources or by the odor of chemical fertilizers
- This sets off a vicious cycle:
  1. Fertilizer kills soil biology
  2. Without biology, natural nutrient cycling stops
  3. More fertilizer must be applied to feed plants directly
  4. More biology is killed
  5. Soil structure collapses, disease increases, more fertilizer needed
- Earthworms also leave the area — salts are irritants, and gut microbes responsible for worm digestion die if fertilizers are ingested

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Osmotic shock from fertilizers is the central problem that soil food web gardening addresses:

- **Why organic matters:** Organic fertilizers (compost, meals, meals) release nutrients slowly at low concentrations that do not cause osmotic shock
- **NPK limits:** Rule #14 advises nothing higher than 10-10-10 to avoid osmotic damage; soybean meal (6-1-1) is preferred
- **Recovery through biology:** Compost tea applications after chemical use help repopulate soils and detoxify chemical residues
- **Soil structure loss:** Fungal hyphae that bind soil aggregates are destroyed by osmotic shock, leading to compaction
- **Mycorrhizal damage:** High phosphorus (>10) not only prevents mycorrhizal growth but kills existing mycorrhizal fungi through osmotic effects
