## Description

Tagasaste (*Chamaecytisus palmensis*, formerly *Cytisus palmensis*), also known as tree lucerne or escabon, is a fast-growing, nitrogen-fixing evergreen tree native to the Canary Islands. It is highly valued in permaculture and agroforestry as a multi-purpose windbreak species that produces abundant edible foliage for livestock fodder. Mollison specifically recommends tagasaste alongside Leucaena as a prime example of a windbreak tree that serves multiple functions simultaneously.








## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Family:** Fabaceae (Leguminosae)
- **Genus:** Chamaecytisus
- **Species:** C. palmensis
- **Synonyms:** *Cytisus palmensis*, *Cytisus proliferus*
- **Common names:** Tagasaste, tree lucerne, escabon, al-escobon
- **Growth form:** Small to medium evergreen tree or large shrub
- **Native range:** Canary Islands (La Palma)








## Key Facts

- Nitrogen-fixing legume that enriches soil through symbiotic root nodule bacteria, making it a living fertilizer factory.
- Produces highly palatable, protein-rich edible leaves that are comparable to lucerne (alfalfa) in nutritional value, hence the common name "tree lucerne."
- Grows rapidly, typically reaching 4-6 meters in height within a few years under favorable conditions.
- Drought-tolerant once established, making it suitable for dryland and Mediterranean-climate permaculture systems.
- Responds well to coppicing and pollarding, regrowing vigorously after cutting to provide repeated fodder harvests.
- White flowers produced in profusion are attractive to bees, providing excellent bee forage and nectar source.








## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

- **Multi-functional windbreaks:** Mollison specifically cites tagasaste as a windbreak planting providing edible leaves, demonstrating the permaculture principle of stacking functions.
- **Fodder production:** Trees can be lopped or grazed directly by livestock; the foliage has crude protein levels of 20-30%, making it an excellent supplementary feed, especially during dry seasons when grass is scarce.
- **Soil improvement:** As a nitrogen fixer, tagasaste improves soil fertility for neighboring crops in alley cropping or agroforestry layouts.
- **Erosion control and land rehabilitation:** Fast growth and deep root systems make it effective for stabilizing degraded hillsides and eroded land.
- **Wildlife habitat:** Dense foliage provides shelter for birds and beneficial insects; flowers support pollinator populations.
- **Fire resilience:** While the foliage is flammable, the plant recovers quickly from fire due to strong regrowth from the root crown.








## Cultivation Notes

Tagasaste prefers well-drained soils and full sun. It tolerates poor, shallow, and alkaline soils but performs poorly in waterlogged conditions. Direct seeding is the most common establishment method. Seed should be scarified or hot-water treated before sowing to break hard seed coat dormancy. Once established, it requires minimal maintenance and persists for 15-20 years under regular coppicing.
