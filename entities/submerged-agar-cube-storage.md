## Description

Submerged agar cube storage is a medium-term culture preservation technique in which colonized agar from a fully grown petri plate is cut into small cubes and submerged in sterile water (or mineral oil) inside a test tube under refrigeration. This method significantly extends the viable storage life of mycelial cultures compared to standard agar plate storage, maintaining viability for over a year.

## Classification

- **Category**: Cultivation technique / Culture storage
- **Storage duration**: Over 1 year (water), potentially longer (mineral oil)
- **Equipment**: Test tubes, sterile water or mineral oil, scalpel, refrigerator

## Key Facts

- Addresses the primary problem with long-term agar plate storage: cultures losing viability and aging into flat, discolored mats
- The water (or oil) protects and hydrates the [[mycelium]], preventing desiccation
- Individual cubes can be retrieved with a sterilized tool as needed without disturbing the remaining culture
- Mineral oil provides even higher success rates than water, though cultures must be rinsed in sterile water before use
- Test tubes are capped and stored refrigerated with cubes fully submerged at all times
- Particularly useful for maintaining backup copies of purified isolates

## Procedure

1. Allow a petri plate culture to fully colonize with mycelium
2. Using a sterile scalpel, slice the colonized agar into small cubes
3. Fill a test tube approximately half full with sterilized water (or mineral oil)
4. Transfer the agar cubes into the test tube, ensuring they are fully submerged
5. Cap the test tube and refrigerate
6. To retrieve culture, use a sterile tool to fish out one cube
7. Transfer the cube to fresh agar and allow colonization
8. Recap the test tube and return to cold storage

## Advantages

- Significantly longer storage life than standard agar plates
- Individual cubes can be retrieved without disturbing the rest of the culture
- Simple technique requiring minimal specialized equipment
- Water provides both hydration and protection from desiccation
- Compatible with both temperate and cold-sensitive species (when stored at appropriate temperatures)

## Variations

- **Water submersion**: Standard method, over 1 year viability
- **Mineral oil submersion**: Higher success rate, requires rinsing before use
- **[[glycerol]] solution**: For frozen storage, uses 5% glycerol as cryoprotectant

## Relevance to Cultivation

Submerged agar cube storage is one of the most practical and accessible medium-term culture preservation methods available to mushroom cultivators. It requires minimal equipment (test tubes, water, refrigerator) and provides reliable preservation of valuable isolates. As part of a comprehensive culture management strategy that includes agar slants, colonized grain storage, freezing, and dehydration, submerged agar cubes offer a balance of simplicity, reliability, and storage duration.
