## Overview

*Boletus rex-veris* is a large edible bolete fungus native to western North America, closely related to the European porcini (*palmer-edible-mushrooms-north-america in western North America, with a rich, nutty flavor comparable to Italian porcini.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Boletales
- **Family:** Boletaceae
- **Genus:** *Boletus*
- **Species:** *B. rex-veris*

The epithet *rex-veris* means "spring king" in Latin, referencing its early-season fruiting habit. It was originally described by W.A. Murrill in 1938 as *Ceriomyces rex-veris* and later transferred to *Boletus*.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Boletus rex-veris* is found along the Pacific Coast of North America from southern California to British Columbia, with inland populations in the Sierra Nevada and Cascade ranges. It forms pseudotsuga-menziesii*), white fir (*Abies concolor*), and various pines.

It fruits in spring and early summer, typically March through June, often appearing shortly after seasonal rains cease and temperatures warm. Unlike the fall-fruiting *Boletus edulis* sensu stricto, *B. rex-veris* fills the spring porcini niche in western North American forests.

Elevation ranges from near sea level in coastal forests to approximately 2,000 m in montane coniferous forests. It prefers well-drained soils in mature or old-growth forest stands.

## Ecological Role

As an ectomycorrhizal fungus, *B. rex-veris* forms symbiotic associations with the roots of forest trees, exchanging mineral nutrients and water for photosynthetic carbon from its host. This relationship is critical for forest health, particularly in nutrient-poor soils.

The fruiting bodies serve as food for numerous forest animals including deer, squirrels, and various insect larvae. Fly larvae (Diptera) commonly infest mature specimens, making early collection important for foragers.

In permaculture and forest gardening contexts, *B. rex-veris* represents a valuable late-winter/spring food resource that can be encouraged through appropriate stand management and minimal soil disturbance in conifer-dominated plantings.
