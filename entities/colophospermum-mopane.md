# Colophospermum mopane (Mopane)

## Description

Colophospermum mopane, commonly known as mopane, is a moderately tall leguminous tree growing to 23 m. It is native to central and southern Africa and is renowned for its ability to thrive on extremely poor, badly drained soils where even useful grasses fail to grow. The species is a cornerstone of the African savanna ecosystem, forming extensive woodlands known as mopane veld. The bilobed leaves resemble butterfly wings, giving it the alternate name butterfly tree.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Family**: Fabaceae (Leguminosae)
- **Genus**: Colophospermum
- **Species**: C. mopane
- **Common names**: Mopane, mopane tree, butterfly tree
- **Type**: Leguminous tree, often with a spreading crown

## Key Facts

- Height: Up to 23 m under favourable conditions
- Rainfall range: 125-800 mm annually
- Altitude: Best growth below 900 m
- Frost tolerance: Withstands high summer temperatures but only mild frosts; heavy frosts may cause crown dieback
- Soil preferences: Shallow compacted clay, alkaline and badly drained soils; best growth on rich deep alluvial soils
- Reproduction: Young plants produce root suckers; seedlings may be suppressed by grasses
- Salinity: Amazing ability to withstand dry, saline sites
- Leaf morphology: Distinctive bilobed leaves resembling butterfly wings

## Uses

### Fodder

The leaves are readily eaten by stock and retain nutrient values even after they have fallen to the ground, making them an important dry-season browse resource in African pastoral systems.

### Timber and Fuel

The durable wood has multiple applications:
- Pylons in bridge construction
- Mine timbers
- Railroad ties and fence posts
- Parquet flooring
- Carving wood
- Requires heavy saws due to extreme hardness; should be cut while green

### Firewood

Reputed to be the best firewood in Africa. The dark wood has a specific gravity greater than 1.0 and burns slowly and quietly while giving off great heat. The trees coppice vigorously after fire, frost, or felling, making them ideal for sustainable fuelwood rotations.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

Mopane is an exceptional pioneer species for harsh, degraded sites with poor drainage and saline soils in arid and semi-arid regions. Its nitrogen-fixing ability (as a legume) improves soil fertility. The coppicing habit makes it suitable for sustainable fuelwood production on a rotation basis. - Colophospermum is monotypic - no other species in the genus
