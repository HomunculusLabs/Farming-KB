# Sporidesmium sclerotivorum

**Type:** Species (Ascomycete)
**Category:** Sclerotial Mycoparasite / Biocontrol Agent
**Also known as:** None

## Overview
Sporidesmium sclerotivorum is a soil-dwelling ascomycete and an
aggressive mycoparasite specialized on the sclerotia of
Sclerotinia species. It is a true sclerotial parasite with an
exceptionally narrow host range, attacking primarily Sclerotinia
minor and S. sclerotiorum. The fungus is widely distributed in
soil environments worldwide and has been extensively studied as a
biological control agent for Sclerotinia diseases in lettuce and
other susceptible crops.

The species is remarkable for its ability to detect sclerotia
from a distance, grow toward them through soil, and initiate
infection upon contact. This active host-seeking behavior
distinguishes S. sclerotivorum from passive soil saprotrophs and
reflects a highly evolved parasitic relationship with its
Sclerotinia hosts. Its population dynamics are directly linked to
the availability of sclerotia in soil.

S. sclerotivorum can reduce sclerotial populations in soil by up
to 90% under favorable conditions (Adams 1990), making it one of
the most effective natural regulators of Sclerotinia inoculum.
This level of control rivals or exceeds that achieved by chemical
treatments, while avoiding the environmental and human health
risks associated with fungicide application.

## Taxonomy and Classification
- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Sordariomycetes
- **Order:** Pleosporales
- **Genus:** Sporidesmium
- **Species:** S. sclerotivorum

Sporidesmium sclerotivorum is an anamorphic ascomycete placed in
the order Pleosporales. The genus Sporidesmium contains numerous
species, many of which are saprotrophs on decaying plant
material. S. sclerotivorum is unusual within the genus for its
specialized parasitic lifestyle targeting fungal sclerotia rather
than plant debris.

## Key Details

### Host Detection and Infection
S. sclerotivorum detects sclerotia from a distance and grows
toward them through soil (Adams 1986; Adams and Fravel 1990; Del
Rio et al. 2002), an active host-seeking behavior unusual among
mycoparasites. Upon contacting a sclerotium, the fungus produces
appressoria or infection structures and penetrates the melanized
rind, colonizing the medullary tissue within. This directed
growth suggests chemotropic sensing of sclerotial exudates.

### Host Range
S. sclerotivorum has an extremely narrow host range, specializing
almost exclusively on Sclerotinia species. The primary hosts are
S. minor and S. sclerotiorum, though slight differences in host
preference have been documented among isolates. This narrow host
range is both a strength (ensuring targeted action with minimal
non-target effects) and a limitation (restricting its biocontrol
utility to Sclerotinia management).

### Biocontrol Efficacy
Field and greenhouse studies have demonstrated that S.
sclerotivorum can reduce sclerotial populations in soil by up to
90% (Adams 1990). In lettuce production systems, the fungus
provided effective control of Sclerotinia drop caused by S.
minor, reducing disease incidence and increasing marketable
yield. The degree of control is influenced by soil temperature,
moisture, and the initial density of both the parasite and its
host.

### Population Dynamics
S. sclerotivorum population levels in soil are directly
correlated with sclerotial density, as the fungus depends on
sclerotia for nutrition and reproduction. When sclerotia are
abundant, S. sclerotivorum populations increase; when sclerotia
are depleted, the parasite population declines. This density-
dependent regulation creates a natural feedback mechanism that
can maintain sclerotial populations below economic damage
thresholds.

### Mass Production
Mass production of S. sclerotivorum for field application relies
on culturing the fungus on autoclaved sclerotia as a substrate.
This approach provides the specific nutritional environment the
fungus requires for sporulation. Conidia produced on colonized
sclerotia serve as the primary inoculum for field application,
either incorporated into soil or applied to the soil surface at
planting.

## Relevance
S. sclerotivorum represents a highly specialized and effective
natural enemy of Sclerotinia species. Its active host-seeking
behavior and density-dependent population dynamics make it a
self-regulating biocontrol agent that can maintain Sclerotinia
inoculum below economic thresholds over multiple growing seasons
without repeated applications.
