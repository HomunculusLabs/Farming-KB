## Overview

Thalictrum minus L., commonly known as lesser meadow-rue, is a perennial herb in the family Ranunculaceae. It is native to Europe, western Asia, and northern Africa, where it inhabits dry grasslands, scrub, and rocky slopes.

The species is one of the smaller European Thalictrum species, typically reaching 30–80 cm in height. Its delicate, finely divided foliage gives it a lacy appearance that is valued in ornamental horticulture.

T. minus is notable for its bispecific alkaloid profile, producing both benzylisoquinoline and diterpenoid alkaloids. These compounds have attracted pharmacological interest for their antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory properties.

The flowers lack true petals, displaying instead a ring of showy yellow stamens that serve as the visual attractant for pollinators. This floral architecture is characteristic of the genus.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:** Kingdom Plantae → Class Magnoliopsida → Order Ranunculales → Family Ranunculaceae → Tribe Ranunculeae → Genus *Thalictrum* → Species *T. minus* L.

Linnaeus described the species in 1753. The epithet *minus* means "smaller," distinguishing it from the larger T. flavum (common meadow-rue).

The species exhibits considerable morphological variability across its range. Several subspecies and varieties have been described, though their taxonomic status remains debated.

The stem is erect, slender, and usually unbranched below the inflorescence. It is glabrous or sparsely pubescent, often with a glaucous bloom.

Leaves are 2–3 times pinnately compound with small, cuneate to obovate leaflets. Each leaflet is 5–15 mm long, with three rounded lobes at the apex.

The foliage is bluish-green, providing textural contrast in garden settings. Leaves are concentrated in the lower two-thirds of the stem.

The inflorescence is a loose, spreading panicle with nodding flower buds. Each flower is 8–12 mm across with 4–5 early-deciduous sepals and no petals.

Stamens are numerous, 3–5 mm long, with conspicuous yellow anthers on slender filaments. The anthers are the showy part of the flower.

Achenes are 3–4 mm long, ellipsoid, strongly ribbed, and pendulous on short stalks. They mature from August through October.

Chromosome number is 2n = 28. Polyploid populations have been reported from southeastern Europe.

## Distribution and Habitat

Thalictrum minus occurs across Europe from southern Scandinavia to the Mediterranean, extending east to the Caucasus, Turkey, and Iran. Northern African populations exist in Morocco and Algeria.

In its northern range, it grows in dry, calcareous grasslands and on limestone pavements. Further south, it colonizes open scrub, woodland margins, and montane slopes.

The species is strongly calcicolous, rarely occurring on acid substrates. It prefers well-drained, shallow soils over limestone or chalk.

## Cultivation

Thalictrum minus is cultivated as an ornamental perennial for its fine-textured foliage and airy flower panicles. It is hardy to USDA zones 5–8.

Propagation is by seed sown in autumn or by division of established clumps in spring. Seeds benefit from a period of cold stratification.

The species prefers full sun to light shade and well-drained, alkaline to neutral soils. It tolerates dry conditions once established.
