# Ficus carica

**Type:** Species (Tree)
**Category:** Fruit tree
**Family:** Moraceae
**Also known as:** Common fig, edible fig

## Overview
Ficus carica is one of the most ancient cultivated plants in human
civilisation, with as many as 700 known varieties. It is a deciduous
tree growing 5-10 m tall with a spread up to 25 m. Native to the
Mediterranean and western Asia, figs have been cultivated for food
since at least 5,000 BCE, making them among the first domesticated
fruit trees. The species is exceptionally well-suited to small-scale
and permaculture food production due to its minimal care requirements
and high productivity.

## Taxonomy
- **Order:** Rosales
- **Family:** Moraceae
- **Genus:** Ficus
- **Species:** F. carica
- **Common relatives:** F. macrophylla (Moreton Bay fig), F.
  benghalensis (banyan), F. sycomorus (sycamore fig)

## Key Details
- **Growth rate:** Moderate
- **Height:** 5-10 m, spread up to 25 m
- **Lifespan:** Up to 100 years
- **Bearing age:** 2-3 years from planting
- **Frost tolerance:** Hardy to -10C; young trees need protection
  from early or late frosts
- **Soil tolerance:** Wide range but dislikes alkaline or salty
  soils; periodic lime applications beneficial
- **Drought tolerance:** Tolerant of drought but produces better
  fruit with adequate water
- **Fruit production:** Two crops per year in some climates (early
  summer crop larger, late summer/autumn crop smaller but more
  numerous)
- **Pollination:** Wild types require tiny wasp (Blastophaga
  psenes); many cultivated varieties are parthenocarpic (produce
  fruit without pollination)
- **Nutritional value (fresh, per 100g):** Energy 80 cal,
  Carbohydrate 20.3 g, Protein 1.2 g, Calcium 35 mg, Potassium
  194 mg, Vitamin A 80 IU, Niacin 0.4 mg
- **Nutritional value (dried, per 100g):** Energy 274 cal,
  Carbohydrate 69.1 g (about 50g dextrose), Protein 4.3 g,
  Calcium 126 mg, Potassium 640 mg, Iron 3.0 mg
- **Drying:** Sugar content rises from 12% to 50% on drying; best
  method is partial sun-drying on tree then trays
- **Medicinal:** Mild laxative effect; mixed with senna, rhubarb,
  and other laxatives; demulcent decoctions for catarrhal
  infections of nose and throat; roasted split figs as poultice
  for dental abscesses and boils; milky sap applied to warts

## Cultivation
Tolerates a wide range of soils but dislikes alkaline or salty
conditions. Performs better with restricted roots, making it ideal
for container growing in small gardens. Propagation is by layering
or hardwood cuttings from short-jointed shoots of previous year's
growth. Requires minimal pruning; fruit only matures on new or
recently ripened wood. Can be trained against walls or fences in
cooler climates.

## Notable Cultivars
- **Brown Turkey:** Medium fruit, mahogany-brown skin, rich
  flavour, cold hardy
- **Mission (Black Mission):** Dependable, large purplish-black
  fruit, excellent flavour
- **Kadota:** Vigorous, hot climate, yellowish-green skin, popular
  for canning
- **Desert King:** Large dark green fruit, best in cooler climates
- **Genoa (White Genoa):** Best for cooler areas, hardiest variety

## Relevance
The fig is one of the most versatile and easy-to-grow fruit trees
for permaculture systems in warm temperate and subtropical regions.
Its ability to crop with minimal irrigation, produce two harvests
annually, and thrive in container culture makes it invaluable for
urban permaculture and small-space food production. The combination
of fresh and dried fruit, medicinal uses, and ornamental value make
it a multi-functional garden element.
