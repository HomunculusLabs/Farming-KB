# IRIS (5-Ethoxy-2-methoxy-4-methylamphetamine)

IRIS is a substituted amphetamine compound and one of the ten "Classic Ladies," the possible alpha-homologues of [[dom-entity]]. The compound is listed as PIHKAL entry #93.

## Chemical Classification

IRIS belongs to the amphetamine class of chemical compounds.

It is formally named 5-ethoxy-2-methoxy-4-methylamphetamine.

IRIS is one of the ten possible positional isomers and homologues of [[dom-entity]] that Shulgin called the Classic Ladies.

The compound was named IRIS as part of the classical naming tradition for this series of DOM-related compounds.

## Pharmacological Profile

IRIS has shown minimal activity at the doses explored.

At up to 9 mg, no significant psychoactive effects were observed.

The active level is unknown but is higher than 9 milligrams.

Since [[dom-entity]] itself would have been "smashingly active" at this level, IRIS is clearly a homologue with substantially decreased potency.

## Qualitative Effects

At 7.5 mg, subjects felt possibly at threshold at about three hours, but an hour later there was nothing.

At 9 mg, there might have been slight lightheadedness, but the effects were ambiguous at best.

The compound appears to be largely inactive at practical dosage levels.

## The Classic Ladies Series

IRIS is one of the ten Classic Ladies, the ten possible positional isomers and homologues of DOM.

The Ladies were discussed beginning with the [[ariadne-entity]] entry, which was the first of the series.

Each Lady represents a different arrangement of substituents on the phenethylamine or amphetamine skeleton related to the DOM pattern.

The exploration of these compounds was systematic, with each isomer synthesized and tested to map the structure-activity relationships of psychedelic amphetamines.

## Complexity in Drug-Mind Interactions

The discussion of IRIS prompted Shulgin to reflect on the nature of "complex" drug-mind interactions.

The term "reasonably complex" does not simply mean a drug shows many facets.

There is intrinsic importance implied by the term "complex" in the context of psychoactive compounds.

Simple things can have simple explanations that are both satisfactory and complete.

But some systems, like the interaction between psychoactive drugs and human consciousness, may involve irreducible complexity.

Shulgin referenced Godel's incompleteness theorem, suggesting that some aspects of drug-mind interaction may be fundamentally beyond complete explanation.

This philosophical digression in the PIHKAL commentary highlights the inherent difficulty in fully characterizing or predicting the effects of psychedelic compounds.

## Synthesis

The synthesis begins with 4-methoxy-2-methylphenol (prepared as described under [[2c-d-entity]]).

This is ethoxylated with ethyl iodide and potassium hydroxide in ethanol to give 2-ethoxy-5-methoxytoluene.

Formylation using phosphorus oxychloride and N-methylformanilide produces 5-ethoxy-2-methoxy-4-methylbenzaldehyde as pale cream-colored solids with a melting point of 81-82 degrees C.

The aldehyde is condensed with nitroethane and ammonium acetate in glacial acetic acid to give the nitrostyrene with a melting point of 121-122 degrees C.

Reduction with LAH in ether gives the amine, which is converted to the hydrochloride salt with a melting point of 192-193 degrees C.

Recrystallization from acetonitrile gives lustrous crystals melting at 196-197 degrees C with decomposition.

## Structural Comparison with DOM

[[dom-entity]] has the 2,5-dimethoxy-4-methyl substitution pattern and is active at 3-10 mg.

IRIS has the 5-ethoxy-2-methoxy-4-methyl pattern, with the 5-methoxy replaced by a 5-ethoxy.

The substitution pattern change from 2,5-dimethoxy to 2-methoxy-5-ethoxy dramatically reduces potency.

This illustrates the sensitivity of psychedelic activity to the precise arrangement and nature of substituents on the aromatic ring.

## Dosage and Duration

Dosage: Greater than 9 mg (no significant effects observed).

Duration: Unknown.

The compound is considered to have substantially decreased potency compared to [[dom-entity]].
