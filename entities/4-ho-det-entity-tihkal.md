# 4-HO-DET (Ethocin) — TIHKAL #16

## Compound Identity

- Full name: N,N-Diethyl-4-hydroxytryptamine
- Synonyms: CZ-74 (free base/acetate), CEY-19 (phosphate ester), Ethocin,
3-[2-(diethylamino)ethyl]-4-indolol
- Molecular weight: 234 (free base), 276 (acetate), 314 (phosphate)
- Class: Tryptamine, 4-hydroxy substituted, N,N-diethyl
- Source: [[tihkal-introduction-and-methodology]]

## Dosage and Duration

- Dosage: 10-25 mg orally (as indolol, acetate ester, or phosphate ester)
- Duration: 4-6 hours
- Active routes: oral

## Qualitative Effects

At 15 mg (indolol, oral), effects arrive rapidly within 30-45 minutes with
intensity described as "gang-busters." Time distortion, sparkling visual
effects, and a compulsion to interact and share are prominent, though erotic
elements are absent. Some users report an uncomfortable quality at a deep
level.

The 15 mg phosphate ester experience was described in transcendent terms
— complete dissolution of self into "the ground of Being, in Brahman, in
God, in nothingness, in Ultimate Reality." Feelings of cosmic tenderness,
infinite love, penetrating peace, and boundless devotion were reported. The
peak lasted at least an hour with no duality between self and experience.

At 20 mg (acetate), mild stomach discomfort precedes a mix of sedation, jaw-tightening,
and body tremor. Firelight produces bursts of color and music enables drifting
thoughts. Anorexia and diuretic effects are notable.

## Synthesis

Synthesis begins with protection of 4-hydroxyindole as the acetate ester
(4-acetoxyindole, mp 98-100°C). This intermediate is reacted with oxalyl
chloride to form the acid chloride, then treated with diethylamine. The resulting
glyoxylamide is reduced with LAH in THF to give 4-HO-DET (mp 103-104°C).
The product discolors quickly in air and should be stored under inert atmosphere
at -30°C.

Conversion to the phosphate ester follows the same route as psilocybin —
reaction of the sodium salt with dibenzylchlorophosphonate, followed by reductive
removal of benzyl groups with catalytic hydrogenation.

## Mushroom Bioconversion Studies

Fascinating German studies demonstrated that the metabolically active mycelium
of psilocybe-cubensis|Psilocybe species can bioconvert synthetic tryptamines.
Normally, the mushroom converts DMT to psilocin by introducing a 4-hydroxyl
group via indole 4-hydroxylase. When DET (not found in nature) was administered
to the growing mycelium, the mushroom dutifully hydroxylated it to 4-HO-DET
— a potent drug also not known in nature. This creates an interesting philosophical
question about the "natural versus synthetic" distinction.

## Pharmacological Notes

Shulgin hypothesizes that the phosphate, acetate, and free phenol forms of
4-HO-DET are all interchangeable in potency, as ubiquitous esterases would
split them to the same end product. The molecular weight ratios (4 to 3 across
the three forms) support this. See also [[alpha-n-dmt-entity-tihkal]] for discussion of ester conversion in the body.
