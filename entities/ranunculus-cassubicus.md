## Overview

**Ranunculus cassubicus** L., commonly called the Caspian buttercup or Kaszub buttercup, is a perennial herbaceous plant in the family Ranunculaceae. It is native to central and eastern Europe, where it grows in deciduous woodlands, forest margins, and damp meadows.

The species is notable for its large, rounded basal leaves and bright yellow flowers that appear in early spring. It is part of the *Ranunculus auricomus* complex, a taxonomically difficult group.

Ranunculus cassubicus is sometimes treated as an aggregate species encompassing several microspecies. Its relationships within the *R. auricomus* group continue to be studied using molecular methods.

The plant is sometimes confused with *Ranunculus auricomus* in the field. However, *R. cassubicus* typically has larger basal leaves and a more robust habit.

Despite its common name, the species has no particular connection to the Caspian Sea. The name derives from the historical Cassubian region of Pomerania.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Clade:** Tracheophytes
- **Clade:** Angiosperms
- **Clade:** Eudicots
- **Order:** Ranunculales
- **Family:** Ranunculaceae
- **Genus:** Ranunculus
- **Species:** R. cassubicus
- **Authority:** Linnaeus (1753)

The species epithet refers to the Kaszuby region of northern Poland. Linnaeus described the species from material collected in the Baltic region.

It belongs to *Ranunculus* sect. *Ranunculus* and is placed within the *R. auricomus* aggregate. This group is characterized by apomictic reproduction and numerous microspecies.

Chromosome number is 2n = 32 (tetraploid). The polyploid nature contributes to the taxonomic complexity of the group, with many intermediates reported.

## Morphology

Plants grow 20–50 cm tall from a short, ascending rootstock. The stem is erect, solitary or sparingly branched, and sparsely appressed-pubescent.

Basal leaves are long-petiolate with orbicular to reniform blades, 3–8 cm across. The blade is crenate to shallowly lobed with rounded teeth.

Stem leaves are few, smaller than the basal leaves, and deeply divided into 3–5 linear to lanceolate segments. The uppermost leaves are bract-like.

Flowers are 2–3 cm in diameter with 5 glossy yellow petals, 8–14 mm long. Sepals are spreading, hairy, and 4–6 mm long.

Stamens are numerous. Achenes are approximately 3 mm, with a short, curved beak, and arranged in a globose head.

The root system consists of a vertical rootstock with slender fibrous roots. The rootstock produces a basal rosette in late autumn that overwinters.

Plants often form small clumps through rootstock branching. The overwintering rosette is resistant to moderate frost and snow cover.

## Distribution and Habitat

Ranunculus cassubicus occurs across central and eastern Europe from eastern Germany and Poland eastward to the Baltic states, Belarus, and western Russia.

Its range extends southward through the Czech Republic, Slovakia, and into the northern Balkans. Northern populations reach southern Scandinavia and Finland.

The species is absent from western Europe, the Mediterranean, and the Atlantic coast. It is replaced by *Ranunculus auricomus* sensu stricto in more western areas.

It grows at low to moderate elevations, typically below 1,000 m. Habitats include damp deciduous woodlands, forest clearings, hedgerows, and moist meadows.

The species prefers nutrient-rich, moderately moist loam soils. It is tolerant of seasonal shade from the deciduous canopy, flowering before full leaf-out.

It is considered an indicator species of ancient woodland in parts of its range. Its presence often signals long-established, relatively undisturbed forest habitat.

## Ecology

Flowering occurs from April to May, making it one of the earlier buttercup species in its range. Flowers produce pollen and nectar accessible to a range of early spring insects.

The species is partially apomictic, producing seeds asexually through agamospermy. This reproductive strategy allows rapid colonization of suitable habitats.

Like all buttercups, the plant contains ranunculin, which converts to the irritant protoanemonin upon tissue damage. Grazing animals generally avoid the fresh foliage.

The foliage dies back by midsummer as the forest canopy closes. The plant persists as a rootstock through the remainder of the growing season.

Seedling establishment is uncommon in stable populations. Most reproduction occurs vegetatively through rootstock branching.

The species is classified as Least Concern across its range. However, habitat loss from intensive forestry and agricultural intensification threatens some populations.

## Cultivation

Ranunculus cassubicus is occasionally grown in woodland gardens and native plant collections. It is suitable for naturalistic planting in damp, shaded borders.

Plants thrive in partial to full shade in humus-rich, moist soil. They are fully hardy and require minimal maintenance once established.

Propagation is by seed sown fresh or by division in autumn. Seeds benefit from cold stratification and may germinate irregularly over several months.

The species is hardy to USDA zones 4–7. It has no serious pest or disease problems and is resistant to deer browsing due to its toxic foliage.

It is rarely available commercially outside specialist native plant nurseries. The plant has not been widely bred or selected for horticultural traits.
