The species is widely distributed across temperate Europe and is generally considered an [[bloomfield-fairy-rings-armillaria-giant-fungi]], often with dozens of individuals in a single cluster.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

The species was originally described as *Agaricus geotropus* by Jean Bulliard in 1792 and transferred to *Clitocybe* by Lucien Quélet.

Recent molecular phylogenetic studies place it in a clade with other large clitocyboid fungi, though its generic placement remains debated.

The epithet *geotropa* derives from the Greek *geo* (earth) and *tropos* (turning), referring to the downward-curving cap margin.

### Cap

The cap is initially convex with an inrolled margin, expanding to a broad funnel shape, 8–25 cm in diameter.

The surface is smooth, dry, and pale tan to ochre-buff, often with a slightly darker center disc.

Cap color fades to nearly white in dry weather and may develop slightly concentric zones of coloration.

The margin is thin and wavy in mature specimens, often irregularly shaped from overlapping with neighboring fruiting bodies.

### Gills and Stipe

Gills are strongly decurrent, extending well down the stipe, white to pale cream, crowded, and often forking near the stipe.

Spores are white in deposit, broadly ellipsoid to subglobose, 5.5–7.5 × 4.5–5.5 µm, smooth and inamyloid.

The stipe is 8–15 cm tall and 1.5–3 cm thick, solid, pale cream, and often slightly curved at the base.

The stipe surface is smooth with a mealy texture near the apex, and the base may be slightly rooting.

### Flesh

The flesh is thick, firm, and white, with a mild pleasant mealy or slightly farinaceous odor when cut.

The taste is mild and nutty, becoming slightly bitter in older specimens or those growing under conifers.

## Distribution and Habitat

Clitocybe geotropa is widely distributed across temperate and Mediterranean Europe, from Scandinavia to the Iberian Peninsula.

It also occurs in parts of North Africa, western Asia, and has been reported from [[fungi-in-the-environment-symbiotic-relationships]] with root systems.
