## Overview

Diplazium esculentum (Retz.) Sw., commonly known as the vegetable fern, is an edible fern species in the family Athyriaceae.

It is widely consumed as a vegetable across South and [[psilocybe-southeast-asia-pacific]], where it is known by numerous local names including pako (Philippines), ningro (India), and pucuk pakis (Indonesia).

The young fronds are harvested as a [[seasonal-vegetable-planting]] prized for their crisp texture and mild, slightly astringent flavor.

It is one of the most economically important edible ferns in tropical and subtropical Asia.

The species epithet "esculentum" means "edible" in Latin, directly referencing its culinary importance.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:**

- Kingdom: Plantae (Polypodiopsida)
- Order: Polypodiales
- Family: Athyriaceae
- Genus: Diplazium
- Species: D. esculentum (Retz.) Sw.

**Synonyms:** Asplenium esculentum Retz., Athyrium esculentum (Retz.) Copel., Dictyodroma esculenta (Retz.) Ching.

The species has been placed in several genera over its taxonomic history, reflecting the complex relationships within the athyrioid ferns.

**Morphology:**

The fern forms an erect rhizome covered with narrow, brown scales.

Fronds are bipinnate, 60–150 cm long, with a stout, grooved stipe (stalk) 20–50 cm in length.

Pinnae are lanceolate, 10–25 cm long, with serrate margins and pinnate lobing.

Sori are linear, running along the veins on the underside of the pinnae, protected by a hooked indusium.

The young, tightly coiled fiddleheads (croziers) are the primary harvested part, appearing pale green with a covering of brown scales.

Mature fronds are dark green and somewhat leathery in texture.

## Distribution and Habitat

Diplazium esculentum is distributed across tropical and subtropical regions of South Asia, Southeast Asia, East Asia, and Oceania.

Its range extends from northeastern India and southern China through Thailand, Vietnam, the Philippines, Indonesia, and Papua New Guinea.

It also occurs in northern Australia, Hawaii, and parts of tropical Africa and Madagascar.

The species typically grows in moist, shaded environments along stream banks, in forest clearings, and on damp slopes.

It thrives in humid tropical lowlands from sea level to approximately 1,500 meters elevation.

The fern prefers acidic to neutral, well-drained loamy soils rich in [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]].

It is commonly found in secondary forests, disturbed areas, and alongside cultivated fields.

## Nutritional Value

The young fronds of D. esculentum are a nutritious vegetable with a favorable micronutrient profile.

Analysis shows high levels of beta-carotene (provitamin A), vitamin C, and several B vitamins.

Mineral content is notably rich in iron, calcium, potassium, and phosphorus.

Protein content ranges from 3–5 g per 100 g fresh weight, which is relatively high for a leafy vegetable.

The fronds contain moderate amounts of dietary fiber and are low in fat.

[[aact-tannins-terpenes-phenolic-compounds-wood-composting-ingham]] with antioxidant activity have been identified in the leaves.

Raw ferns contain small quantities of thiaminase and trace amounts of fern-specific toxins (pteridotoxins), though these are destroyed by cooking.

Boiling or blanching is recommended before consumption to neutralize any potentially harmful constituents.
