# Entrophospora

## Description

Entrophospora is a small genus of arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) fungi in the order Glomales, suborder Glomineae. It contains only four described species, making it one of the least speciose genera in the Glomales. The genus is notable for the unique positioning of its spores relative to the sporiferous saccule—a character that distinguishes it from its likely sister genus, Acaulospora.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Eukarya
- **Phylum:** Glomeromycota (formerly Zygomycota)
- **Order:** Glomales
- **Suborder:** Glomineae
- **Genus:** Entrophospora

## Key Facts

- Contains only four described species—the low species count is difficult to explain definitively.
- Spore morphologies of Entrophospora species are nearly identical to those of "sister" species in Acaulospora (Morton and Benny 1990), differing primarily in the position of spore formation relative to the sporiferous saccule.
- The genus is hypothesized to be polyphyletic, with each species potentially having evolved independently from a different Acaulospora ancestor through repeated shifts in the position of spore formation.
- Spore walls typically consist of a bilayered structure (an outer layer plus a laminate layer), with two layers that co-vary in ornamentation.
- Entrophospora species are found in diverse habitats, including arid environments where they are among the small-spored species that characterize fungal communities.
- The genus appears in trap cultures from habitats including desert scrub sites (Namibia, Arizona), prairies (Minnesota), sand dunes (Virginia), and acid mine soils (West Virginia).

## Ecological Role

As arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi, Entrophospora species form mutualistic associations with plant roots, producing arbuscules and vesicles within root cortical cells. They contribute to the broader community of AM fungi that enhance plant phosphorus uptake, particularly in nutrient-poor soils. Their presence in arid habitats suggests tolerance to low moisture conditions.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Taxonomic challenge:** The close morphological similarity to Acaulospora species makes identification difficult, particularly with degraded field-collected spores.
- **Arid habitat studies:** Entrophospora is among the genera commonly recovered from arid and semi-arid environments, where small-spored species tend to dominate.
- **Phylogenetic significance:** The probable polyphyletic nature of the genus makes it an interesting case study for understanding evolutionary convergence in AM fungi.
- **Trap cultures:** Entrophospora species are recovered in multi-cycle trap cultures, often appearing in second or third propagation cycles alongside Glomus and Acaulospora species.
