## Overview

**Hibiscus rosa-sinensis**, commonly known as Chinese hibiscus, shoeblackplant, or
simply hibiscus, is an evergreen flowering shrub in the family Malvaceae.

Native to tropical and subtropical [[neurotropic-fungi-japan-east-asia-species-cultural-context]], it has been widely cultivated for
centuries as an ornamental plant and has significant applications in traditional
medicine across multiple cultures. It is the national flower of Malaysia and is
deeply embedded in Hindu religious traditions.

The species is distinct from *Hibiscus sabdariffa* (roselle), which is the
primary species used for commercial hibiscus tea production, though *H. rosa-
sinensis* shares many pharmacological properties.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:** Plantae > Angiosperms > Eudicots > Rosids > Malvales >
Malvaceae > Malvoideae > *Hibiscus*.

**Authority:** L.

**Growth habit:** Evergreen shrub, typically 2–5 m tall in cultivation but can
reach 8 m in ideal tropical conditions. Branching is dense and upright.

**Leaves:** Alternate, simple, ovate to lanceolate, 5–15 cm long, with a
serrate margin and acute apex. Surface is glossy dark green with prominent
palmate venation.

**Flowers:** Solitary, axillary, showy, 6–12 cm diameter. Calyx is campanulate
with 5 linear bracteoles. Corolla has 5 petals that may be single or double,
ranging from white through pink, red, orange, yellow, and purple depending on
cultivar.

**Fruit:** Capsule, ovoid, 2–3 cm long, dehiscing at maturity to release
numerous reniform seeds approximately 3 mm long.

## Distribution and Habitat

The exact native range of *H. rosa-sinensis* is debated due to centuries of
cultivation, but it is generally accepted to originate from tropical and
subtropical regions of East and [[psilocybe-southeast-asia-pacific]], possibly Vanuatu or China.

It has been introduced pantropically and is now naturalized in many tropical and
warm [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]] worldwide, including the Caribbean, [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-hawaii-pacific-islands]],
sub-Saharan Africa, and [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-south-america]]. The species is frost-sensitive and does
not survive temperatures below 5°C without protection.

It thrives in full sun to partial shade in well-drained, fertile soils with a
pH range of 6.0–7.5. Established plants are moderately drought-tolerant but
perform best with consistent moisture.

## Traditional and Medicinal Uses

In Ayurvedic medicine, *H. rosa-sinensis* is used as a hair tonic, with flowers
and leaves applied to promote hair growth and prevent premature graying. The
flowers are also used to treat [[cannabis-for-skin-conditions-detailed]], menstrual disorders, and
respiratory complaints.

In Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM), the flower is used to regulate
menstruation, treat coughs, and reduce inflammation. The roots are used as an
emmenagogue and antifertility agent in some traditions.

Across Southeast Asia, a decoction of the flowers is consumed as a cooling
beverage, and the petals are used in salads and as a natural food colorant. In
Hawaiian culture, the flower (known as pua aloalo) is used in lei making and
has ceremonial significance.
