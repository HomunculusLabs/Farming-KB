## Appearance and Aroma

Thin Mint buds are medium to large in size, with a dense, tightly packed
structure characteristic of indica-dominant hybrids.

The flowers display deep forest-green coloration interspersed with violet
and indigo hues, particularly when exposed to cooler nighttime temperatures
during late flowering.

A thick coating of milky-white trichomes gives the buds a frosted
appearance, with prominent amber pistils threading throughout.

The aroma is where Thin Mint earns its name — a sharp, cool mint scent
dominates on the first inhalation, underpinned by earthy, musky notes from
the OG Kush lineage and a subtle sweet cookie undertone.

When ground, the bud releases additional notes of damp earth, pine, and
a faint chemical pungency that rounds out the complex bouquet.

## Terpene and Cannabinoid Profile

### Dominant Terpenes

The terpene profile of Thin Mint is led by **[[limonene]]**, which contributes
citrus and mint-adjacent brightness, followed by **caryophyllene**, which
adds peppery warmth and potential anti-inflammatory activity.

**Linalool** is present in moderate quantities, lending floral sweetness
and contributing to the strain's relaxing qualities.

**Myrcene**, the most common cannabis terpene overall, appears at elevated
levels in Thin Mint compared to other GSC phenotypes, reinforcing the
indica-dominant sedative effect.

**Pinene** is detectable but secondary, providing subtle pine notes.

### Cannabinoid Content

THC levels in well-grown Thin Mint typically range from 18% to 25%, with
some phenotypic selections testing above 27%.

CBD content is very low, generally below 0.1%, which is typical for
modern dessert-strain genetics.

Minor cannabinoids including CBC and CBG have been detected at trace
levels, though they do not contribute significantly to the overall effect
profile.

## Effects and Medical Applications

Thin Mint delivers a dual-phase effect that begins with a cerebral rush
— euphoria, creative energy, and mood elevation — before transitioning
into deep physical relaxation over the course of 60–90 minutes.

The onset is relatively fast, typically felt within 5–10 minutes of
inhalation, with peak effects lasting 2–3 hours.

Commonly reported recreational effects include uplifted mood, heightened
sensory perception, giggly euphoria, and progressive body stone.

In medical contexts, Thin Mint has been used to manage chronic pain,
nausea, appetite loss, insomnia, and stress-related disorders.

The strain's mood-elevating properties make it particularly well-suited
for patients dealing with treatment-resistant depression and anxiety.

Potential side effects include dry mouth, dry eyes, dizziness at higher
doses, and in rare cases, heightened anxiety or paranoia — particularly
in users sensitive to THC-dominant strains.

## Cultivation

Thin Mint is considered a moderately difficult strain to cultivate,
requiring attention to training and environmental control for optimal
results.

The clone-only genetics mean that growers must obtain verified cuts from
trusted sources — seeds sold as "Thin Mint" are often GSC crosses or
imitations rather than the true phenotype.

Indoor flowering time ranges from 9 to 10 weeks, with yields of
approximately 12–16 ounces per square meter under optimal conditions.

The plants respond well to SCROG ([[screen-of-green]]) and low-stress
training techniques, which help manage lateral branching and maximize
light penetration.

Thin Mint is susceptible to powdery mildew in high-humidity environments,
requiring adequate airflow and dehumidification during dense flowering.

Nutrient requirements are moderate to heavy during weeks 4–8 of flowering,
with a preference for phosphorus and potassium supplementation in the
final weeks to support trichome production.
