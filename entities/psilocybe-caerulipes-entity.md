# Psilocybe caerulipes

**Type:** Species (Fungi, Basidiomycota, Agaricales)
**Category:** [[psilocybin]]-containing mushroom
**Also known as:** blue foot, blue-footed Psilocybe

## Overview
Psilocybe caerulipes is a small psychedelic mushroom found in the
deciduous forests of eastern North America. Named for its characteristic
bluing stem base ("caerulipes" meaning "blue foot"), it is one of the
few lignicolous (wood-inhabiting) bluing Psilocybes native to eastern
North America. Despite its wide distribution, it is considered uncommon
to rare in most of its range.

## Key Details

### Taxonomy
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Hymenogastraceae
- **Genus:** Psilocybe
- **Authority:** (Peck) Saccardo

### Morphology
- **Cap:** 1-3.5 cm broad, obtusely conic to broadly convex to plane with
  age, may retain a slight umbo. Margin incurved at first, often tinged
  greenish, closely translucent-striate, decorated with fibrillose veil
  remnants. Cinnamon brown to dingy brown, hygrophanous, fading to pale
  ochraceous buff. Surface viscid when moist from gelatinous pellicle.
- **Gills:** Adnate to subdecurrent, close, grayish brown with whitish
  edges.
- **Stem:** 30-70 mm long by 1-3 mm thick. Characteristically bruises
  bluish at the base (hence "blue foot"). Whitish to brownish, covered
  with fine fibrils.
- **Spores:** Dark purplish brown in deposit, ellipsoid.
- **Partial veil:** Cortinate, soon disappearing.

### Habitat and Distribution
- Grows on decaying hardwood logs, sticks, and woody debris, particularly
  favoring oak, beech, maple, and birch.
- Found in deciduous and mixed forests, often in deep leaf litter.
- Fruiting from June through September.
- Range: Eastern North America, from Florida north to Ontario and Nova
  Scotia, west to Michigan and Ohio. Also reported from Mexico and
  parts of Central America.

### Chemistry
- Contains psilocybin and [[psilocin]] (moderately active).
- Potency is variable; specimens are typically less potent than
  P. cyanescens or P. azurescens but still produce noticeable effects.
- Strong bluing reaction, especially at the stem base, the feature
  that gives the species its common name "blue foot."
- The bluing is most pronounced when the stem base is disturbed or
  cut, serving as a key field identification marker.

## Relevance
P. caerulipes occupies an important ecological niche as a wood-decomposing
psilocybin mushroom in eastern North American forests, contrasting with
the more dung-associated species common in the southeastern United States.
Its preference for hardwood debris and leaf litter connects it to forest
ecology and decomposition cycles involving oak, beech, and maple. The
species is relatively rare compared to other Psilocybes, making it a
sought-after find for foragers. Its distribution in eastern hardwood
forests complements that of P. caerulescens in the southeastern states
and P. cyanescens in the Pacific Northwest, together representing the
major regional lignicolous psychedelic fungi of North America.
