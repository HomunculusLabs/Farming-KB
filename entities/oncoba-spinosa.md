## Description

*Oncoba spinosa* is a thorny shrub or small tree used in tropical and subtropical
permaculture systems as a **living barrier plant** and hedgerow species. It is
valued for its dense, spiny growth habit which effectively restrains livestock
and marks boundaries. The plant produces snuff-box-like fruits and attractive
white flowers, making it both functional and ornamental.




## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Family**: Salicaceae (formerly Flacourtiaceae)
- **Genus**: *Oncoba*
- **Species**: *O. spinosa*
- **Common names**: Snuff-box tree, Fried egg tree
- **Growth form**: Thorny shrub / small tree




## Key Facts

- Native to tropical and southern Africa.
- Featured by Mollison as one of the **ideal species for bomas** (thorn fences)
  used to corral livestock including cattle, goats, and sheep.
- Can be planted as a living hedge or used as coppice material cut and built
  into a thorn fence (boma).
- May not require barbed wire reinforcement, unlike some other barrier species.
- Produces hard, spherical fruit used traditionally as containers (snuff boxes).
- Flowers are large, white with yellow centres — attractive to pollinators.




## Barrier Functions

- **Animal corrals**: Dense, thorny growth restrains cattle, goats, and sheep.
- **Boundary marking**: Defines compound edges and garden perimeters.
- **Living fence**: Can be maintained as a permanent hedgerow.
- **Boma construction**: Cut branches can be woven into thorn fences for
  temporary or semi-permanent enclosures.




## Relevance to Cultivation / Mycology

- Woody biomass from pruning can be chipped for **mushroom substrate**,
  particularly for species tolerant of harder hardwood substrates.
- The dense root system supports **mycorrhizal associations** that contribute
  to soil structure in barrier hedgerow systems.
- As part of a mixed hedgerow, contributes to **biodiverse edge habitat**
  supporting decomposer organisms including fungi.
- Leaf litter and woody debris from hedge maintenance add organic matter
  to soil, feeding fungal decomposer communities.




## Companion Species

Mollison recommends combining *Oncoba spinosa* with other barrier plants:
- *[[lycium-ferocissimum]]* (thorny shrub)
- *[[acacia-tortilis]]* (umbrella thorn)
- *Euphorbia tirucalli* (pencil cactus — requires eye protection when handling)
