## Description

*Pseudomonas* spp. are a genus of Gram-negative, rod-shaped bacteria that are among the most ecologically significant and metabolically diverse microorganisms in soil and compost systems. In composting, they become prominent during the **thermophilic phase** (104–150°F / 40–65°C), where they contribute to the breakdown of complex organic compounds. Many *Pseudomonas* species are also key plant growth-promoting rhizobacteria (PGPR) and biocontrol agents, producing compounds that suppress plant pathogens.

## Classification

- **Domain**: Bacteria
- **Phylum**: Pseudomonadota (Proteobacteria)
- **Class**: Gammaproteobacteria
- **Order**: Pseudomonadales
- **Family**: Pseudomonadaceae
- **Genus**: *Pseudomonas*

## Key Facts

### Role in Composting
- Active during the **thermophilic phase** (104–150°F / 40–65°C), joining other heat-tolerant bacteria in accelerating decomposition.
- Participate in the breakdown of complex carbohydrates and proteins that resist mesophilic degradation.
- Contribute metabolic heat that sustains the high temperatures necessary to kill pathogens and weed seeds.
- Their metabolic versatility allows them to degrade a wide spectrum of organic substrates.

### Role in Soil and Plant Health
- Many species are **fluorescent pseudomonads**, producing siderophores (iron-chelating compounds) that deprive pathogenic fungi of iron, suppressing diseases like damping-off and root rot.
- Produce **antibiotics** including pyoluteorin, 2,4-diacetylphloroglucinol (DAPG), and pyrrolnitrin that directly inhibit fungal pathogens.
- Some species promote plant growth through **indole-3-acetic acid (IAA)** production and phosphate solubilization.
- Important members of the rhizosphere community, colonizing plant roots and forming protective biofilms.

### Metabolic Versatility
- Aerobic metabolism with some species capable of **denitrification** under low-oxygen conditions.
- Can degrade hundreds of organic compounds including hydrocarbons, pesticides, and aromatic compounds.
- Fluorescent species produce characteristic yellow-green **pyoverdine** pigments visible under UV light.
- Rapid growth rates allow quick colonization of organic matter and root surfaces.

### Notable Species
- ***Pseudomonas fluorescens*** — biocontrol agent, produces multiple antifungal compounds; widely used in commercial biological control products.
- ***Pseudomonas putida*** — soil dweller, bioremediation agent, degrades toluene and other contaminants.
- ***Pseudomonas aeruginosa*** — opportunistic human pathogen (not plant-associated in normal circumstances).
- ***Pseudomonas syringae*** — plant pathogen causing various leaf spot and canker diseases.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

1. **Compost thermophily**: Their activity during the thermophilic phase ensures thorough organic matter decomposition and pathogen destruction in compost piles.
2. **Disease suppression**: Fluorescent *Pseudomonas* species are among the most effective bacterial biocontrol agents against *Fusarium*, *Pythium*, *Rhizoctonia*, and other fungal pathogens affecting crops and mushroom substrates.
3. **Compost tea efficacy**: *Pseudomonas* populations thrive in AACTs, contributing to foliar disease suppression when teas are sprayed on leaf surfaces.
4. **Nutrient cycling**: Serve as prey for protozoa and nematodes in [[the-soil-food-web]], facilitating nitrogen mineralization and nutrient availability.
5. **Mushroom cultivation**: Some *Pseudomonas* species (e.g., *P. putida*) can compete with mushroom mycelium on substrates, while others may help suppress competitor molds.

## Environmental Preferences

- **Temperature**: Broad range; thermotolerant species active at 40–65°C in compost; mesophilic species active at 20–35°C in soil
- **pH**: 5.5–8.5, optimum near neutral
- **Moisture**: Require adequate moisture; sensitive to desiccation
- **Oxygen**: Predominantly aerobic; some species capable of anaerobic respiration using nitrate
