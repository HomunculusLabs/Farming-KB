Curcuma longa, commonly known as turmeric, is a perennial herbaceous
plant in the family Zingiberaceae. Native to the Indian subcontinent
and [[query-what-are-the-main-bioactive-compounds-in-medicinal-plants]] are curcuminoids, with
curcumin being the most studied.

The species was first described by Carl Linnaeus in 1753. The genus
name derives from the Arabic "kurkum" meaning saffron or yellow, a
reference to the intense color of the rhizome. The specific epithet
"longa" means long, referring to the elongated rhizome shape. Common
names include turmeric (English), haldi (Hindi), haridra (Sanskrit),
jiang huang (Chinese), and ukon (Japanese). The plant is sometimes
referred to as "Indian saffron" due to its use as a saffron substitute
in some culinary traditions.

The taxonomy of Curcuma has been revised multiple times, and several
synonyms exist including Curcuma domestica Valeton. The species is
distinguished from related Curcuma species by its large rhizome size,
the orange-yellow internal color, and the presence of a prominent
central "mother" rhizome with lateral "fingers."
