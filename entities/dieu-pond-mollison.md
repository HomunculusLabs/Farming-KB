## Description

A dieu-pond (from the French "dieu," meaning god) is a small, hand-dug water catchment traditionally constructed on upland hillsides and ridges. These semi-mystical small catchments dot the British landscape, mainly constructed by monasteries in historical times. They are said to be "fed by dieu" — the god Himself sends down the rain — because they appear to fill without any visible inflow, spring, or stream.

## Classification

- **Category**: Water storage / Earthworks
- **Scale**: Micro-scale (3 to 20 feet diameter)
- **Terrain**: Upland hills, mini-catchments, cup-shaped depressions
- **Historical origin**: Monastic construction, British Isles
- **Labor requirement**: 2–3 people can dig one in a day

## Construction

Dieu-ponds are hand-dug, and therefore not machine-compacted. They are often clay-tamped but need not be. Key construction features:

1. **Tapered design**: The walls are tapered at a ratio of approximately 3:1, matching the resting angle of normally strong soil. This is the critical secret — as the pond evaporates, the surface area decreases geometrically, so the rate of evaporation slows progressively.
2. **Mini-catchment siting**: Located where a small cup-shaped area in the hill provides natural drainage into the pond.
3. **Clay tamping**: The material removed is laid out on the catchment area, minimizing vegetation and maximizing runoff into the pond.
4. **Stone access**: Animals are given a stone or low-side access point to water, which also causes some edge collapse that is acceptable.

## Key Facts

- **Never dry up**: Due to the tapered geometry, as water level drops, surface area decreases, reducing evaporation rate. The pond always retains some water.
- **No visible water source**: No streams, springs, or run-in channels are needed. They fill entirely from direct rainfall on their mini-catchment.
- **Low maintenance**: Occasional cleaning is needed — silt and leaves accumulate at the narrow bottom point. In very low summer periods, one hops in and drags leaves out.
- **Stock watering**: These are traditional high-country watering points for livestock in Britain.
- **Modern adaptation**: Can be excavated with a backhoe if manual digging is impractical.
- **Secret knowledge**: Old dieu-pond builders passed construction secrets from one to another and would never tell anyone how to build them. Mollison deduced the physics after studying them.

## Physical Properties

- **Diameter range**: 3 feet to 20 feet maximum
- **Depth**: Variable, tapering from edges to center
- **Wall angle**: 3:1 slope (resting angle of normal strong soil)
- **Evaporation resistance**: Self-regulating — reduced surface area at lower water levels slows evaporation

## Relevance to Cultivation

Dieu-ponds provide permanent upland water sources critical for:
- **Livestock watering** in areas with no natural springs or streams
- **Wildlife habitat** — important watering points for seed-eating birds and wild animals
- **Fire protection** — water stored at high elevations for emergency access
- **Permaculture design** — demonstration of low-energy, permanent water storage without pumps, pipes, or complex infrastructure

## Design Principles

The dieu-pond illustrates a key permaculture principle: working with natural geometry rather than against it. The self-regulating evaporation characteristics emerge purely from the tapered shape, requiring no active management or mechanical systems. This makes them ideal models for low-maintenance water storage in remote or off-grid situations.
