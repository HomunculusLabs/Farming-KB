## Charles H. Peck

## Description

Charles Horton Peck (1833–1917) was an American mycologist who served as the State Botanist of
New York from 1867 to 1915. He is regarded as one of the most prolific describers of North
American fungal species, having described over 2,700 species during his career. His annual reports
issued through the Board of Regents of the University at Albany are considered foundational
documents in American [[mycology]].

## Classification

- **Field:** Mycology / Botany
- **Role:** State Botanist of New York
- **Institution:** New York State Museum, Albany
- **Active Years:** 1867–1915
- **Nationality:** American

## Key Facts

- Served as State Botanist of New York for nearly 48 years, one of the longest tenures in
  American botanical history.
- Published annual Reports of the State Botanist containing descriptions of new species and
  observations on fungal distribution.
- Compiled the influential *Catalogue of [[boleti]]* (1889), referenced by Palmer in his work.
- Described approximately 2,700 new species of fungi, a record unmatched by any other American
  mycologist of his era.
- Based at State Hall, Albany, N.Y., where his herbarium collection became a major resource.
- Did not hold advanced academic degrees; largely self-taught in mycological taxonomy.
- Palmer credited Peck with "the most thorough work in the classification or identification of
  mushrooms and toadstools" in America.

## Contributions to Mycology

- Standardized nomenclature for many [[growing-gourmet-north-american-reishi-species]] boletes, agarics, and polypores.
- His catalogue system provided a reference framework for amateur and professional mycologists
  alike.
- Reports included habitat descriptions, seasonal data, and distribution records across New York
  State and the northeastern [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]].
- Palmer relied on Peck's nomenclature for species like Boletus sub-tomentosus ("the olive-red
  Boletus of Mr. Peck's catalogue").
- Planned (as of Palmer's 1894 writing) to publish an illustrated work on American fungi, which
  Palmer enthusiastically anticipated.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- Peck's taxonomic work laid the groundwork for reliable identification of [[classification-of-edible-and-poisonous-mushroom-families]]
  species in North America.
- His *Catalogue of Boleti* was a primary reference for distinguishing edible from harmful bolete
  species.
- Annual reports served as continuing education for the mycological community, updating
  nomenclature and adding newly described species.
- His collections at the New York State Museum remain valuable reference specimens.
- Palmer noted that government-issued pamphlets (partly based on Peck's work) had limited utility
  because they relied heavily on earlier British classifications that did not distinguish between
  different types of "poisonous" — e.g., the fatal [[amanita-vernus]] versus the merely acrid
  [[lactarius-piperatus]].

## Historical Context

Peck worked during a period when [[early-american-mycology-and-palmer]] was heavily dependent on European authorities
such as Berkeley, Cooke, and Fries. His work helped establish an independent American taxonomic
tradition. Palmer regarded Peck's contributions as the most thorough classification work done on
American soil, despite the limitations of government-issued pamphlets that drew on earlier British
sources.

## Legacy

- Over 2,700 species described, many still valid today.
- The New York State Museum herbarium houses his extensive collections.
- His reports are digitized and remain accessible through biodiversity heritage libraries.
- Numerous fungal species bear the epithet *peckii* in his honor.
