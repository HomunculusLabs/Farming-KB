# Tetragoniomyces uliginosus

## Description

*Tetragoniomyces uliginosus* is a tremelloid basidiomycete fungus that parasitizes *[[rhizoctonia-solani]]*, a major plant pathogen. The species is notable for producing micropores in its haustoria — a specialized structure that creates a direct cytoplasmic connection between parasite and host. This fusion-type interface is one of the rarest categories of mycoparasite-host interaction and places *T. uliginosus* among a small group of fungi that use this unusual mechanism for nutrient acquisition from living fungal hosts.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Eukarya
- **[[kingdom-fungi]]
- **Phylum:** [[basidiomycota]]
- **Class:** Tremellomycetes
- **Order:** Tremellales
- **Genus:** *Tetragoniomyces*
- **Species:** *T. uliginosus*

## Key Facts

- *T. uliginosus* parasitizes *Rhizoctonia solani*, one of the most important and widespread soil-borne plant pathogens, causing damping-off, root rot, and stem canker in hundreds of plant species.
- The species produces micropores in its haustoria — small openings that connect the cytoplasm of the parasite with that of the host, allowing direct nutrient exchange.
- This micropore-mediated fusion interface is shared with *Syzygospora pallida* (on *Phanerochaete*) and represents a rare biotrophic interaction type.
- The fusion interface is distinct from the more common haustorial interface (short branches without micropores) and invasive necrotrophic interfaces.
- As a member of the Tremellales, *T. uliginosus* is related to other mycoparasitic tremelloid fungi, many of which parasitize macrofungi (mushrooms, polypores).
- The biotrophic nature of the interaction means the host (*R. solani*) remains alive during the parasitic relationship, potentially making *T. uliginosus* less aggressive but more sustainable as a biocontrol agent.
- The Tremellales contain approximately 170 fungicolous species, making them one of the most important orders of mycoparasitic fungi.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

*Tetragoniomyces uliginosus* has significant relevance to agriculture and cultivation:
- **Biocontrol of *Rhizoctonia*:** As a natural parasite of *R. solani*, *T. uliginosus* represents a potential biocontrol agent for one of the most damaging soil-borne pathogens. Its biotrophic nature means it could provide sustained suppression rather than temporary elimination.
- **[[mushroom-cultivation]]:** *R. solani* (thanatephorus stage) is a common contaminant in mushroom cultivation substrate, causing competitive mold problems. Understanding its natural enemies, including *T. uliginosus*, could inform biological control strategies.
- **Tremellalean mycoparasitism:** The Tremellales are the most diverse group of mycoparasitic fungi. *T. uliginosus* contributes to understanding how this order has radiated to exploit diverse fungal hosts.
- **Fusion interface biology:** The micropore-mediated fusion interface is poorly understood at the molecular level and represents a frontier in mycoparasitism research.
