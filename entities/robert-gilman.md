## Overview

Robert Gilman is an American sustainability researcher and editor best known as the founder of **In Context magazine**, referenced on page xxiii of David Holmgren's *Permaculture: Principles and Pathways Beyond Sustainability*. Gilman was pivotal in articulating the intellectual foundations of the ecovillage movement.

## In Context Magazine

Gilman founded **In Context: A Quarterly of Humane Sustainable Culture** in 1983 from Seattle. The magazine became one of the most influential publications in the sustainability movement, covering ecological design, community economics, ecovillage development, social innovation, and technology assessment. Holmgren cites it as an important influence on permaculture thinking.

## Ecovillage Movement

Robert and Diane Gilman played a central role in defining ecovillages. Their 1991 report *"Ecovillages and Sustainable Communities"* defined ecovillages as "human-scale, full-featured settlements in which human activities are harmlessly integrated into the natural world." This influenced the **Global Eco-village Network (GEN)**, referenced by Holmgren on page 175.

## Contributions to Sustainability Thinking

Gilman's work bridged systems thinking, sustainable economics, community governance, technology assessment, and cultural change. His emphasis on "humane sustainable culture" complemented permaculture's design focus.

## Connection to Permaculture

- In Context provided a forum for discussing permaculture alongside other sustainability approaches
- His ecovillage framework helped permaculture practitioners think about social design
- Gilman's work helped legitimize permaculture within the broader sustainability movement

## Legacy

Gilman's contributions continue through the Global Eco-village Network, the ecovillage movement, and regenerative design. His insistence that sustainability encompasses social, cultural, and economic dimensions alongside ecological ones remains influential.
