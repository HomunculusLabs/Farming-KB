## Overview

**Gigartina pistillata** is a species of red marine algae in the family Gigartinaceae, commonly found along temperate Atlantic coastlines. It is harvested commercially as a source of carrageenan, a polysaccharide used widely in food processing and cosmetics.

The species forms dense bushy thalli up to 15 cm tall, with cylindrical to compressed branches arising from a small discoid holdfast. Its color ranges from dark red to purplish-brown, often bleaching to yellowish at branch tips.

G. pistillata is one of several Gigartina species collected for phycocolloid extraction in Europe and North Africa.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Gigartina pistillata** (S.G.Gmelin) Stackhouse belongs to the order Gigartinales, family Gigartinaceae. The species was originally described by Gmelin in 1768 and later transferred to Gigartina by Stackhouse in 1809.

The thallus is perennial and cartilaginous, consisting of repeatedly dichotomously branched axes that are 2–5 mm in diameter. Branches are cylindrical near the base, becoming increasingly flattened toward the tips.

The holdfast is a small crustose disc that anchors the alga to rock substrates in the lower intertidal and shallow subtidal zones. Cystocarps are borne on short stalked branchlets and appear as dark-colored, urn-shaped structures.

Male reproductive structures (spermatangial sori) appear as pale patches on branch surfaces. The tetrasporangia are cruciately divided and embedded in nemathecia on the branch cortex.

## Distribution and Habitat

G. pistillata has a broad distribution across the northeastern Atlantic, from Norway and the British Isles south to Morocco and the Canary Islands. It also occurs in the Mediterranean Sea and along parts of the western African coast.

The species typically inhabits the lower intertidal and upper subtidal zones, growing on exposed to semi-exposed rocky shores at depths of 0–20 m. It prefers areas with moderate to strong water movement.

It is frequently found growing alongside other carrageenan-producing red algae such as *[[mastocarpus-stellatus]]*. Seasonal biomass peaks occur during late spring and summer months.

## Chemistry and Nutrition

G. pistillata produces kappa and lambda-type carrageenans in its cell walls. The carrageenan yield varies seasonally, typically ranging from 30–55% of dry weight, with highest extraction rates during summer.

The primary carrageenan fraction consists of D-galactose and 3,6-anhydro-D-galactose residues linked by alternating alpha-1,3 and beta-1,4 glycosidic bonds. Sulfation levels influence the gel strength and solubility of the extracted polysaccharide.

Mineral content includes significant concentrations of iodine, potassium, calcium, and magnesium. The alga also contains trace amounts of B-group vitamins and polyphenolic compounds with antioxidant activity.

## Cultivation and Harvesting

Wild harvesting of G. pistillata remains the primary source of raw material, with hand-picking conducted during low tide along accessible shorelines. Mechanical harvesting has been tested but risks damaging holdfasts and reducing regrowth rates.

## Related Species

G. pistillata is closely related to *Gigartina chamissoi* and *Gigartina radula*, both of which are also harvested for carrageenan in [[guzman-allen-gartz-africa-southern-hemisphere-underexplored-neurotropic-fungi]]. G. pistillata is one of the few species with a primarily Northern Hemisphere distribution.

Molecular phylogenetic studies have revealed that the traditional morphological classification within Gigartinaceae does not fully reflect evolutionary relationships. Several species previously placed in Gigartina have been transferred to other genera based on DNA sequence data.
