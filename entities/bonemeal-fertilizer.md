## Description

Bonemeal is an organic phosphorus fertilizer made from ground animal bones, typically from slaughterhouse processing. It is one of three phosphorus source options in Steve Solomon's Complete Organic Fertilizer (COF) formula, used at 1 part by volume as a substitute for rock phosphate or guano. Bonemeal provides slow-release phosphorus along with some calcium and trace minerals.

## Classification

- **Type**: Organic fertilizer / Phosphorus source
- **Source**: Ground animal bones (slaughterhouse byproduct)
- **Primary nutrients**: Phosphorus (P), Calcium (Ca)
- **Form**: Fine powder / meal

## Key Facts

- Bonemeal is produced by steaming and grinding animal bones from slaughterhouse operations
- It is a concentrated phosphorus source suitable for use in organic fertilizer blends
- In the COF formula, bonemeal is one of three interchangeable phosphorus sources (the others being rock phosphate and high-phosphate guano)
- Application rate in COF: 1 part by volume
- Bonemeal releases phosphorus slowly as soil microorganisms break down the organic material
- It also contributes calcium to the soil, complementing the lime components of COF
- The phosphorus in bonemeal is less immediately available than chemical phosphate fertilizers but provides longer-lasting soil enrichment

## Relevance to Cultivation

- Phosphorus is critical for root development, flowering, and fruiting in all vegetable crops
- Bonemeal is particularly valuable for transplant establishment and root crop production
- As an organic phosphorus source, it feeds soil biology while providing nutrients, unlike chemical phosphate fertilizers
- Bonemeal contributes to the balanced nutrition approach of COF, where the goal is highly nutritious vegetables rather than maximum bulk yield
- It is widely available at garden centers and feed stores, making it accessible to most gardeners

## Phosphorus Source Comparison in COF

| Source | Availability | Cost | Nutrient Profile |
|--------|-------------|------|-----------------|
| Bonemeal | Garden centers | Moderate | P + Ca |
| Rock phosphate | Feed stores | Low | P + Ca + trace minerals |
| High-phosphate guano | Specialty suppliers | High | P + N + trace minerals |

## Application Notes

- In COF, use 1 part by volume as the phosphorus component
- Can also be applied directly to planting holes for transplants
- Mix well with other COF ingredients for uniform distribution
- Store in a dry, sealed container

## Types of Bone Meal

Steamed bone meal (NPK 3-15-0) is the most common form, processed
at high temperatures to make nutrients more available. Raw bone meal
has lower nutrient availability but releases over a longer period.

## Application Best Practices

Mix bone meal into planting holes at 1-2 tablespoons per plant for
heavy-feeding crops like tomatoes and brassicas. It is most effective
in soils with pH below 7.0 where phosphorus solubility is higher.

## Environmental Considerations

Bone meal is a byproduct of the meat industry, diverting waste from
landfills. However, excessive application can contribute to phosphorus
runoff in waterways. Apply only at recommended rates.
