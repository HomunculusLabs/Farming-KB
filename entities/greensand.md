## Greensand

## Description

Greensand is a natural mineral soil amendment derived from glauconite, a green-colored potassium-iron silicate mineral found in ancient marine deposits. In organic farming, it is valued as a slow-release source of potassium and trace minerals that does not leach readily from soil. Referenced in *The Winter Harvest Handbook* at p. 132 as a key component of Coleman's mineral soil fertility program at [[four-season-farm]].

## Classification

- **Category**: Mineral amendment / Rock powder
- **Domain**: Organic [[soil-fertility-management]]
- **Chemical composition**: Primarily glauconite — a hydrous potassium-iron-magnesium-aluminum silicate
- **Primary nutrients**: Potassium (K2O approximately 5-7%), iron, magnesium, silica
- **Trace minerals**: Over 30 trace elements including boron, manganese, zinc, copper, and cobalt
- **Appearance**: Greenish-gray granular powder or sand-sized particles

## Key Facts

- Mined from ancient seabed deposits, primarily from the Cretaceous-era Hornerstown Formation in New Jersey, USA
- Provides slow-release potassium available over 3-5 years after application
- Contains iron, magnesium, silica, and over 30 trace minerals essential for plant nutrition
- Improves soil structure and moisture-holding capacity due to its clay-like mineral nature
- OMRI-listed (Organic Materials Review Institute) for use in certified organic production
- Applied as part of Coleman's mineral amendment program alongside phosphate rock and limestone (p. 132)
- Typical application rate: 5-10 pounds per 100 square feet, depending on soil test results
- Works synergistically with compost and biological soil activity to release nutrients

## Mineral Composition

Greensand's nutrient profile makes it uniquely valuable in organic systems:

| Nutrient | Approximate Content | Role in Plant Growth |
|---|---|---|
| Potassium (K2O) | 5-7% | Disease resistance, fruit quality, cold hardiness |
| Iron (Fe) | 10-20% | Chlorophyll formation, enzyme function |
| Magnesium (Mg) | 2-3% | Central atom in chlorophyll, enzyme activator |
| Silica (SiO2) | 40-50% | Cell wall strength, pest resistance |
| Trace minerals | Variable | Micronutrient functions across all metabolic pathways |

## Application in Coleman's Fertility System

Greensand is one component of Coleman's three-part mineral amendment program for building baseline soil fertility:

1. **Limestone** (p. 102, 132) — raises pH to 6.5-7.0 range and provides calcium and magnesium for cell wall and chlorophyll formation
2. **Phosphate rock** (p. 132) — supplies slow-release phosphorus for root development and energy transfer
3. **Greensand** (p. 132) — supplies slow-release potassium and trace minerals for overall plant vigor

These rock powders establish the mineral foundation, which is then supplemented by:

- **Compost** (pp. 137-139) for organic matter, biological activity, and [[cation-exchange-capacity]]
- **Alfalfa meal** (p. 132, 134, 137) for nitrogen, triacontanol growth stimulant, and organic matter
- **[[green-manure-crops]]** (pp. 8, 31, 93, 123-124) for soil building, nitrogen fixation, and organic matter
- **Seaweed amendments** (p. 101, 132) for trace minerals, cytokinins, and plant growth hormones

## Properties for Soil Health

Beyond its nutrient content, greensand contributes to soil physical properties:

- **Cation exchange capacity (CEC)**: The clay-like particles increase the soil's ability to hold and exchange nutrients
- **Moisture retention**: Absorbs and holds water, improving drought resistance
- **Soil structure**: Helps bind soil particles into stable aggregates
- **Microbial habitat**: Porous structure provides habitat for beneficial soil microorganisms

## Relevance to Cultivation

Greensand represents the "deep organic" approach to soil fertility (as contrasted with "shallow organic" at pp. 205-207), where the focus is on building long-term mineral reserves in the soil rather than applying quick-release soluble fertilizers. This philosophy aligns with the broader organic principle that healthy soil biology, fed by mineral diversity, produces healthier crops with superior nutritional quality (pp. 170-173).

At Four Season Farm, the native soil is a sandy acid podzol (pp. 131-132) — naturally infertile, acidic, and low in cation exchange capacity. The greensand-phosphate rock-limestone triad, combined with compost and cover crops, transforms this challenging soil into a highly productive growing medium. This approach demonstrates that even marginal soils can be made productive through patient mineral and biological management.
