## Overview

Agroclavine is an ergoline alkaloid of the clavine class, produced by
fungi in the Clavicipitaceae family including *Claviceps* species,
*[[gadd-aspergillus-fumigatus-glucan-virulence]]*, and several [[comparison-endophytic-fungi-vs-mycorrhizal-fungi]] of grasses.

It is a tetracyclic ergoline lacking the characteristic C-8 carboxyl
group of the [[ergot-and-lysergic-acid-history]] amides, placing it in the "simple clavine"
subclass alongside lysergol, setoclavine, and elymoclavine.

Agroclavine exhibits uterotonic, vasoconstrictive, and mild psychoactive
properties, though its pharmacological potency is significantly lower
than that of the peptide ergot alkaloids (ergotamine, ergocristine).

It serves as a key biosynthetic intermediate in the ergot alkaloid
pathway, preceding the formation of [[ergot-fungi-lysergic-acid-and-the-history-of-ergotism]] and subsequently
the ergopeptines.

## Chemical Properties

**Chemical name:** 8-Methylergoline
**Molecular formula:** C₁₆H₁₈N₂
**Molecular weight:** 238.33 g/mol
**CAS number:** 478-74-4
**IUPAC name:** (8R)-8-methyl-6-methylidene-9,10-didehydroergoline

Agroclavine is a tetracyclic compound consisting of the classic
ergoline ring system: an indole fused to a quinoline with a pyrrolidine
ring closure at C-8.

The C-8 position bears a methyl substituent rather than the
carboxamide found in lysergic acid derivatives.

The compound is optically active, with the natural isomer having
the 8R configuration.

It is a colorless crystalline solid at room temperature, soluble
in organic solvents (chloroform, ethanol, acetone) and poorly
soluble in water.

Agroclavine is a weak base (pKa ~8.5) and forms salts with mineral
acids.

## Pharmacology

Agroclavine acts as a partial agonist at serotonergic 5-HT₂A
receptors and adrenergic α-receptors, consistent with the broader
pharmacological profile of [[morning-glory-lsa-and-ergoline-alkaloids]].

Its affinity for 5-HT₂A receptors is moderate (estimated Ki in the
low micromolar range), substantially weaker than LSD (nanomolar) or
lysergic acid amides.

Uterotonic activity has been demonstrated in isolated uterine tissue
preparations, though it is less potent than ergometrine or ergotamine.

Vasoconstrictive effects are present but clinically insignificant at
doses achievable through natural exposure.

No well-documented psychoactive effects in humans at typical exposure
levels; high doses may produce mild sedation or dysphoria.

The compound has not been scheduled or controlled internationally,
likely due to its low potency and rarity as a recreational substance.
