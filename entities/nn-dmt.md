# N,N-Dimethyltryptamine (DMT)

**N,N-Dimethyltryptamine** (abbreviated **DMT** or **N,N-DMT**) is a substituted tryptamine and endogenous psychoactive compound that serves as the primary psychoactive constituent of ayahuasca and numerous other traditional entheogenic preparations. It is one of the most potent classical psychedelics known, producing profound alterations in consciousness, perception, and cognition at remarkably low doses. DMT is also produced endogenously in mammals, including humans, though its natural physiological role remains a subject of active investigation.

## Chemical Identity

- **IUPAC Name:** N,N-Dimethyl-1H-indole-3-ethanamine
- **Molecular Formula:** C₁₂H₁₆N₂
- **Molecular Weight:** 188.27 g/mol
- **CAS Number:** 61-50-7
- **SMILES:** CNC(C)CCC1=CNC2=C1C=CC=C2
- **Chemical Class:** Indolealkylamine (tryptamine derivative)
- **Appearance:** White to off-white crystalline powder (freebase); white crystalline solid (fumarate salt)

DMT is a structural analog of serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine, 5-HT) and melatonin, sharing the indole ring structure that characterizes this class of compounds. The N,N-dimethyl substitution at the terminal amine distinguishes it from psilocin (4-HO-DMT) and 5-MeO-DMT (5-methoxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine).

## Pharmacology

### Mechanism of Action
DMT is a high-affinity agonist at the 5-HT₂A serotonin receptor, which is considered the primary site responsible for its psychedelic effects. Additional receptor interactions include:

- **5-HT₂A:** Full agonist (primary psychedelic effects) — Ki ≈ 0.1 µM
- **5-HT₁A:** Agonist (contributing to anxiolytic and mystical-type experiences)
- **5-HT₂C:** Agonist (may contribute to perceptual alterations)
- **Sigma-1 receptor:** Agonist (neuroprotective, modulatory role)
- **TAAR1:** Agonist (trace amine-associated receptor)
- **SERT:** Substrate (serotonin transporter — DMT is taken up and may act as a releaser)

DMT does not bind significantly to dopamine D₂ receptors, opioid receptors, or NMDA receptors, which distinguishes it from other hallucinogenic classes (dissociatives, deliriants).

### Pharmacokinetics

**Route-dependent characteristics:**

| Parameter | Intravenous | Intramuscular | Inhalation (Vaporized) | Oral (with MAOI) |
|-----------|-------------|---------------|------------------------|-------------------|
| Onset | 10-30 sec | 2-5 min | 10-30 sec | 20-60 min |
| Peak | 2-5 min | 5-15 min | 1-3 min | 60-120 min |
| Duration | 15-30 min | 30-60 min | 5-20 min | 2-6 hours |
| Bioavailability | ~100% | High | ~50% | ~3% (without MAOI) |

Oral DMT is rapidly metabolized by peripheral monoamine oxidase A (MAO-A) in the gut and liver, rendering it inactive when consumed alone. This is the pharmacological basis for ayahuasca, which combines DMT-containing plants with MAO-inhibiting plants (typically *Banisteriopsis caapi*) to enable oral activity. When vaporized or injected, DMT bypasses first-pass metabolism and produces effects within seconds.

DMT is primarily metabolized by MAO-A to indole-3-acetic acid (IAA) and by CYP2D6 and CYP1A2 to N,N-dimethyltryptamine N-oxide (DMT-N-oxide). The elimination half-life is approximately 15-60 minutes depending on route of administration.

## Natural Occurrence

### Plant Sources
DMT has been identified in over 65 plant species across multiple families, including:

- **Fabaceae:** *Mimosa hostilis/tenuiflora* (root bark, 0.3-1.0%), *Anadenanthera peregrina* (seeds), *Anadenanthera colubrina* (seeds)
- **Malpighiaceae:** *Banisteriopsis caapi* (trace amounts; primarily MAOIs), *Diplopterys cabrerana* (leaves, ~0.2%)
- **Rubiaceae:** *Psychotria viridis* (leaves, 0.1-0.6%)
- **Euphorbiaceae:** Various species
- **Leguminosae:** *Desmanthus illinoensis*, *Acacia* spp. (bark and phyllodes)

### Animal Sources
DMT has been detected in mammalian tissues and bodily fluids, including human:

- **Brain:** Detected in pineal gland, cerebral cortex, and other brain regions
- **Blood:** Endogenous levels typically 0.01-0.1 ng/mL
- **Cerebrospinal fluid:** Present at trace levels
- **Urine:** Excreted in detectable quantities

The biosynthetic pathway involves two enzymatic steps from tryptamine: tryptamine → N-methyltryptamine (NMT) via indolethylamine N-methyltransferase (INMT), then NMT → DMT via a second INMT methylation. The enzyme INMT is found in human brain, lung, liver, and adrenal gland tissue.

### Proposed Endogenous Roles
Several hypotheses exist for DMT's endogenous function, though none is conclusively established:

- **Neurotransmitter/neuromodulator:** Acting as a trace amine neurotransmitter
- **Pineal function:** Possible role in dream states, near-death experiences, or mystical experiences (Strassman hypothesis, controversial)
- **Neuroprotectant:** Sigma-1 receptor agonism suggests neuroprotective and anti-inflammatory roles
- **Peripheral signaling:** Potential immunomodulatory role in peripheral tissues

## Traditional and Cultural Use

### Ayahuasca (South America)
The most well-documented traditional use of DMT is in the context of ayahuasca, a complex entheogenic brew used by indigenous peoples of the Amazon basin for at least several centuries (possibly millennia). Ayahuasca typically combines:

- **DMT source:** *Psychotria viridis* (Chacruna) or *Diplopterys cabrerana* (Chaliponga)
- **MAOI source:** *Banisteriopsis caapi* (Ayahuasca vine), containing harmine, harmaline, and tetrahydroharmine (β-carboline alkaloids)

Ayahuasca is used in ceremonial contexts for healing, divination, spiritual guidance, and communal bonding. The practice has spread globally through syncretic religious movements, particularly the União do Vegetal (UDV), Santo Daime, and Barquinha, which use ayahuasca as a central sacrament.

### Yopo and Vilca (South America)
*Anadenanthera peregrina* (Yopo/Cohoba) and *Anadenanthera colubrina* (Vilca/Cebil) seeds contain DMT, 5-MeO-DMT, and bufotenin (5-HO-DMT). Indigenous peoples of the Orinoco basin and Andean highlands traditionally grind the seeds into a powder that is insufflated (snuffed) through tubes, producing rapid-onset visionary experiences.

### Epená (Amazon)
The Yanomami and other Amazonian peoples prepare snuffs from *Virola* species bark resin, which contains DMT and 5-MeO-DMT, used in shamanic healing and warfare-related rituals.

## Subjective Effects

DMT experiences are typically described in terms of distinct phases:

### Onset (0-2 minutes, inhaled)
Rapid onset characterized by a "rush" or "loading" sensation — a feeling of immense energy or vibration, often accompanied by auditory phenomena (buzzing, humming, roaring). Visual effects begin with geometric patterns, color intensification, and wavering of visual field edges.

### Peak (2-10 minutes, inhaled)
The most intense phase, often described as:

- **Breakthrough experiences:** Complete dissolution of the sense of self (ego dissolution). Encounters with apparently autonomous entities ("beings," "machines," "clowns," "insectoids") in hypercomplex, often geometrically structured environments
- **Hyperspace:** A commonly reported domain described as vast, multidimensional space filled with complex geometric architecture, impossible colors, and a sense of immense scale
- **Ineffability:** The extreme difficulty of conveying the experience through language, a near-universal feature of high-dose DMT experiences
- **Unity/convergence:** Experiences of merging with the environment, other beings, or a fundamental ground of reality

### comedown and Integration (10-30+ minutes)
Gradual return to baseline consciousness. Residual visual phenomena (trailing, color enhancement) may persist for 30-60 minutes. Emotional processing and cognitive reflection are common in the aftermath. Many users report lasting changes in perspective, values, or sense of self following DMT experiences.

## Modern Clinical Research

### Psychedelic-Assisted Therapy
DMT (particularly in the form of ayahuasca) is being investigated as a potential therapeutic agent for:

- **Treatment-resistant depression:** Several open-label studies and one randomized controlled trial have demonstrated rapid and sustained antidepressant effects
- **PTSD:** Preliminary evidence suggests efficacy in reducing PTSD symptom severity
- **Substance use disorders:** Particularly alcoholism and drug dependence
- **Anxiety and end-of-life distress:** Reductions in existential anxiety in terminal illness patients
- **Eating disorders:** Emerging evidence for treatment of anorexia nervosa and binge eating disorder

### Neuroimaging
Functional MRI and EEG studies have revealed that DMT produces:

- Decreased activity in the default mode network (DMN), correlating with ego dissolution
- Increased global brain connectivity and entropy
- Disruption of alpha and increased theta/gamma oscillations
- Altered functional connectivity between the visual cortex and other brain regions

## Legal Status

DMT is classified as a Schedule I controlled substance under the United Nations Convention on Psychotropic Substances (1971). It is scheduled or controlled in most countries worldwide. Notable exceptions and special legal frameworks exist for traditional religious use (ayahuasca ceremonies have received legal protections in several jurisdictions, including Brazil, Peru, the United States (UDV v. DEA, 2006), and the Netherlands).

## Safety Profile

DMT is considered physically safe at common doses, with no documented cases of fatal overdose from DMT alone. The primary risks are psychological:

- **Acute anxiety/panic:** Particularly at high doses or in unprepared users
- **HPT (Hyperventilation-induced panic trigger):** Inhaled DMT can trigger rapid breathing
- **Cardiovascular effects:** Transient increases in heart rate and blood pressure
- **Serotonin syndrome risk:** Theoretical when combined with serotonergic medications (SSRIs, MAOIs, tramadol), though documented cases are extremely rare
- **Psychiatric contraindications:** Persons with personal or family history of psychosis or schizophrenia are generally advised against use
