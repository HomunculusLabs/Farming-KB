## Morphology

### Growth Habit

Linaria vulgaris is an erect, rhizomatous perennial growing 30–90 cm tall.
The stems are slender, unbranched or sparingly branched, smooth or slightly
hairy, and often tinged with purple or red at the base. The root system
consists of a deep, creeping rhizome that enables aggressive vegetative
spread. Mature plants form dense colonies from both rhizomatous growth and
abundant seed production.

### Leaves

Leaves are alternate, linear to narrowly lanceolate, 2–6 cm long and 1–4 mm
wide, with entire margins and a single prominent midrib. The leaves are
glabrous to sparsely hairy, pale green to blue-green, and sessile or with a
very short petiole. The narrow, grass-like foliage distinguishes the species
from most other garden plants.

### Flowers

The zygomorphic flowers are borne in dense, terminal racemes 10–30 cm long,
each containing 10–30 flowers opening sequentially from bottom to top.
Individual flowers are 20–35 mm long with a prominent spurred corolla (spur
8–12 mm long). The upper lip is pale yellow while the lower lip is bright
yellow-orange, creating the "butter-and-eggs" bicolor pattern.
