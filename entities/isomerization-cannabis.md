## Description

Isomerization in the context of cannabis processing refers to the chemical conversion of cannabidiol (CBD) into tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) and the simultaneous conversion of lower-potency "low-rotating" THC isomers into higher-potency "high-rotating" THC isomers. This process is a core technique described in Ben Lemon's *Cannabis Alchemy* (Chapter Two) and can dramatically increase the potency of cannabis oil — doubling it at minimum, and in some cases increasing it five to six times.

## Classification

- **Category:** Chemical isomerization (acid-catalyzed cyclization and stereochemical conversion)
- **Field:** Organic chemistry / cannabinoid chemistry
- **Reaction type:** Acid-catalyzed rearrangement

## Key Facts

1. **CBD-to-THC conversion:** Cannabidiol (non-psychoactive) is cyclized to THC (psychoactive) via acid catalysis, effectively converting inactive precursor material into active cannabinoid.
2. **Rotation enhancement:** Low-rotating (less potent) THC isomers are simultaneously converted to high-rotating (more potent) isomers, improving both the strength and the subjective quality of the effects.
3. **Potency increase:** Oil potency can at least double, and may increase 5–6x depending on the CBD content of the starting material.
4. **CBD abundance:** Experience indicates that CBD quantity in typical cannabis oil is usually at least equal to the THC quantity, meaning isomerization has significant potential upside.
5. **Reagent:** Concentrated sulfuric acid (100% pure) is used as the catalyst — one drop per gram of oil, added to the oil/alcohol solution.
6. **Solvent:** The oil is dissolved in absolute ethanol or pure methanol (anhydrous) at a ratio of 1g oil to 10g solvent.
7. **Reaction conditions:** Refluxed in a Pyrex vessel for 2 hours in a boiling water bath.
8. **Workup:** After reaction, the mixture is poured into water, extracted with petroleum ether, washed with 5% sodium bicarbonate solution, and washed with pure water (twice).

## Process Steps

1. Dissolve ether-purified cannabis oil in absolute ethanol (1g oil : 10g ethanol).
2. Add one drop of 100% sulfuric acid per gram of oil, slowly, with continuous stirring.
3. Place in Pyrex vessel within the refluxing apparatus (boiling water bath).
4. Reflux for 2 hours.
5. Cool in ice-water bath.
6. Pour into equal volume of water.
7. Extract with petroleum ether.
8. Wash ether layer with 5% bicarbonate of soda solution.
9. Wash ether layer with pure water (repeat twice).
10. Evaporate ether to collect isomerized oil.

## Safety Considerations

- Concentrated sulfuric acid causes severe burns — requires safety glasses, rubber gloves, and full-body coverage.
- Sulfuric acid must be stored in a safety bottle (glass in styrofoam-lined metal can).
- Burns are treated by immediate washing with water and bicarbonate of soda.
- Denatured ethanol may be used but must not contain water.
- All solvent handling carries fire/explosion risk.

## Chemical Theory

The isomerization exploits the structural similarity between CBD and THC — CBD lacks the closed-ring structure of THC. Under acid catalysis, the open ring of CBD closes (cyclization) to form THC. Simultaneously, the double-bond geometry of existing THC molecules shifts from the less active configuration to the more active ("high-rotating") configuration, enhancing potency. The "rotation" refers to optical rotation — the spatial arrangement of the molecule affects how it binds to cannabinoid receptors.

## Relevance to Cultivation / Mycology

Isomerization is a post-harvest processing technique with no direct relevance to cultivation. However, it is highly relevant to cannabis chemists and processors seeking to maximize the yield of psychoactive compounds from plant material. The principle that CBD-rich strains can be converted to potent THC products has significant implications for cannabis breeding strategy — breeders can select for high total cannabinoid content rather than THC specifically, knowing that CBD can be chemically converted.
