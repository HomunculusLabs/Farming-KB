## Description

The kickdown system is a permaculture technique for managing chickens on sloped terrain to produce and distribute mulch for terraced gardens. Chickens are housed at the top of a slope above a series of garden terraces. As they scratch and forage, they kick mulch material downhill where it accumulates against a fence at the bottom. This collected mulch is then used to enrich the garden terraces. The system leverages the natural behavior of chickens to convert chicken forage and organic material into rich, garden-ready compost distributed by gravity.

## Classification

- **Category**: Animal integration / Soil building
- **Type**: Gravity-fed mulch production system
- **Components**: Chicken house, slope, terraces, collection fence, chicken forage trees
- **Labor requirement**: Minimal — chickens do the work

## How It Works

1. **Chicken placement**: The chicken house is positioned at the top of the slope, above the garden terraces.
2. **Natural behavior**: Chickens scratch, forage, and kick material downhill as part of their normal activity.
3. **Mulch accumulation**: Organic material (leaves, bedding, forage, manure-mixed material) accumulates against a lower fence.
4. **Collection point**: The bottom fence serves as the collection point where enriched mulch gathers.
5. **Distribution**: The collected mulch is taken from the bottom fence and applied to the garden terraces.
6. **Slope stabilization**: The area is planted with chicken forage trees to hold the slope and provide additional organic input.

## Key Facts

- **Gravity-assisted**: The slope does the work of moving material from chickens to collection point — no carrying required.
- **Integrated production**: Chickens simultaneously produce fertilizer (manure), pest control (foraging), and mulch (kicked material).
- **Slope management**: Chicken forage trees stabilize the slope while providing food and shade for the birds.
- **Alternative placement**: Chickens can also be placed at the bottom near the terraces, with mulch carried up manually — but the kickdown arrangement is preferred for efficiency.
- **Complementary to terracing**: Works with the mini-terrace system Mollison describes for sloped residential sites.

## Design Configuration

```
[Chicken House at TOP of slope]
        |
        | (chickens kick mulch downhill)
        v
[Chicken Forage Trees on slope]
        |
        v
[Garden Terraces (3-4 tiers)]
        |
        v
[Collection Fence at BOTTOM]
  -> Rich mulch collected here
```

## Variations

Mollison describes two chicken management options for terraced slopes:

1. **Kickdown system (preferred)**: Chicken house at top, mulch flows downhill to collection fence. Gravity does the work.
2. **Bottom placement**: Chicken house at bottom near terraces. Chickens kick mulch around the immediate garden area. Requires more manual handling.

## Relevance to Cultivation

- **Soil building**: Converts raw organic material and chicken manure into rich garden-ready compost.
- **Terrace maintenance**: Provides ongoing fertility input for terraced growing systems on difficult slopes.
- **Reduced labor**: Eliminates the need to carry mulch uphill or across the garden.
- **Animal integration**: Demonstrates the permaculture principle of stacking functions — chickens produce food, pest control, fertilizer, and mulch simultaneously.
- **Slope utilization**: Makes productive use of steep land that would otherwise be difficult to farm.

## Design Principles

The kickdown system exemplifies several permaculture principles:
- **Work with nature, not against it**: Use the chickens' natural scratching behavior rather than fighting it.
- **Each element serves multiple functions**: Chickens produce eggs, pest control, fertilizer, and distributed mulch.
- **Use gravity as a resource**: The slope that makes the land challenging becomes the mechanism that distributes the mulch.
- **Produce no waste**: All organic material is captured and redirected to productive use.
