## Reed Bed Filtration

## Description

Reed bed filtration is a biological water treatment technique that uses constructed wetlands planted with reeds (Phragmites, Scirpus, Typha) and other aquatic plants to filter and purify water through natural processes. Mollison describes this as a key component of natural swimming pool design, where water is filtered below a base pebble bed using the pebbles as algal and bacterial cleaners, then cycled through a reed-bed to remove excess nutrients before returning freshly oxygenated water to the pool. The technique also applies broadly to greywater treatment, effluent management, and stormwater purification.

## Classification

- **Category**: Technique / Water treatment
- **Type**: Constructed wetland / Biofiltration
- **Principle**: Biological, physical, and chemical treatment using plants and microorganisms
- **Scale**: Residential to municipal

## Key Facts

- Natural pools use pebble-bed filtration followed by reed-beds for complete water purification
- Reed root zones host dense populations of bacteria that break down organic pollutants
- Plants absorb excess nitrogen and phosphorus from nutrient-rich water
- The process removes contaminants without chemicals or energy-intensive mechanical systems
- Can be integrated with greywater treatment, effluent schemes, and common sewage management
- Reed beds handle varying water quality and flow rates effectively
- Provide wildlife habitat while simultaneously treating water
- Low maintenance once established (3-5 years to full maturity)

## How It Works

### Physical Filtration
- Substrate (gravel, sand, pebbles) physically traps suspended solids and particles
- Root networks of reeds and aquatic plants create additional filtration pathways
- Sedimentation occurs in the slow-moving water passing through the bed

### Biological Treatment
- Aerobic bacteria in root zones oxidize organic matter and convert ammonia to nitrate
- Anaerobic zones deeper in the substrate support denitrification (nitrate to nitrogen gas)
- Fungi and protozoa contribute to pathogen removal and organic matter processing
- Biofilms on root and substrate surfaces are the primary treatment engines

### Chemical Processes
- Plants actively uptake dissolved nutrients (nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium) from water
- Precipitation of phosphorus with calcium, iron, and aluminum minerals
- pH buffering through carbonate chemistry in the substrate
- Adsorption of heavy metals to organic matter and clay minerals in the bed

## Design Components

### Subsurface Flow Systems
- Water flows horizontally through gravel substrate below the surface
- No standing water visible - reduces mosquito habitat and odor
- Most common design for residential greywater treatment
