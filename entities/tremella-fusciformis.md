## Overview

*Tremella fuciformis* is a species of jelly fungus in the family Tremellaceae, widely recognized for its gelatinous, translucent fruiting bodies that resemble white coral or sea anemones.

It is one of the most commercially important edible mushrooms in East and Southeast Asia, with a cultivation history spanning over a thousand years in China.

The fungus is prized both as a culinary delicacy in Chinese cuisine and as a medicinal mushroom in traditional Chinese medicine, where it is valued for its purported anti-aging, moisturizing, and immune-enhancing properties.

Modern pharmacological research has focused on its polysaccharide fractions, particularly tremellan, which demonstrates significant immunomodulatory, antioxidant, and neuroprotective activities in vitro and in animal models.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

*Tremella fuciformis* was first described by Miles Joseph Berkeley in 1856. The basionym is *Tremella fuciformis* Berk.

Classification: Fungi → Basidiomycota → Tremellomycetes → Tremellales → Tremellaceae → *Tremella* → *T. fuciformis*.

The fruiting body is gelatinous, soft, and translucent white to pale yellow, typically 2–10 cm in diameter. It forms irregular, lobed, or brain-like masses that are highly water-retentive.

When fresh, the texture is crisp and rubbery, becoming more gelatinous after rehydration from the dried state. The surface is smooth and slightly wrinkled.

Microscopically, basidia are cruciate-septate, producing ellipsoid to broadly ellipsoid basidiospores measuring 7–10 × 6–8.5 µm.

Hyphae are hyaline, clamped, and form a loose network within the gelatinous matrix. The species is a parasite of *Stereum hirsutum* and other *Stereum* species in nature.

### Distinctive Features

The translucent, jelly-like fruiting body with white to ivory coloration and brain-like lobing is diagnostic. No other commonly encountered jelly fungus matches its combination of size, color, and substrate association.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Tremella fuciformis* has a broad distribution across tropical and subtropical regions worldwide, occurring in Asia, Australia, the Pacific Islands, South America, Central America, and parts of Africa.

In nature, it is an obligate parasite that fruits on dead or dying branches of broadleaf trees, especially species of *Quercus*, *Castanopsis*, and *Schima*.

The fungus requires a host fungus — typically *Stereum hirsutum* or *S. rugosum* — to complete its life cycle. It does not directly decompose wood but obtains nutrients through its parasitic relationship with the host Stereum mycelium.

Natural fruiting occurs during warm, humid conditions, typically after sustained rainfall in late summer through autumn. Wild collections remain commercially significant in some regions of China and Southeast Asia.

## Cultivation

Commercial cultivation of *T. fuciformis* is well-established, particularly in China, which produces over 100,000 metric tons annually. The primary method involves a dual-culture technique using a host fungus.

Substrates commonly include sawdust of broadleaf trees (especially oak), supplemented with wheat bran, rice bran, and calcium carbonate. Bottles or bags are sterilized, inoculated with both the host *Stereum* culture and the *Tremella* culture, and incubated at 24–28°C.

Fruiting is induced by reducing temperature to 18–22°C and maintaining high humidity (85–95%). Air exchange and diffuse light are critical for proper fruiting body development.

Yields vary by strain and substrate but typically range from 80–150 g fresh weight per kg of dry substrate. Harvest occurs 30–45 days after inoculation.

## Bioactive Compounds and Pharmacology

The primary bioactive constituents are water-soluble polysaccharides, particularly acidic heteropolysaccharides composed of mannose, xylose, glucose, and glucuronic acid residues.

Tremellan, the major polysaccharide fraction, has a backbone of (1→3)-linked mannose with (1→2)-linked xylose and glucuronic acid side chains. Molecular weight ranges from 500–1,500 kDa depending on extraction method.

### Immunomodulatory Effects

Tremellan polysaccharides activate macrophages, enhance NK cell activity, and stimulate cytokine production (IL-2, TNF-α, IFN-γ) in vitro and in murine models. These effects are mediated through TLR-2 and TLR-4 receptor pathways.

### Antioxidant and Neuroprotective Effects

The polysaccharides demonstrate significant free-radical scavenging activity against DPPH, hydroxyl, and superoxide radicals. Animal studies show protection against oxidative neuronal damage and potential benefits for cognitive function.

### Skin and Connective Tissue

Topical application of tremella extracts increases skin hydration and collagen synthesis. The polysaccharides' water-holding capacity exceeds that of hyaluronic acid by weight, making them popular in cosmetic formulations.

## Culinary and Nutritional Use

In Chinese cuisine, *T. fuciformis* is a key ingredient in desserts, sweet soups, and congees. It is commonly paired with goji berries, jujubes, lotus seeds, and rock sugar.

The dried fungus is reconstituted in water before cooking, expanding dramatically in volume. The texture is smooth, slippery, and slightly crunchy, valued for its mouthfeel rather than strong flavor.

Nutritionally, dried tremella is composed of approximately 65–70% carbohydrates (mostly polysaccharides), 10–15% protein, 2–3% fat, and significant amounts of dietary fiber, iron, and vitamin D.

It is considered a "cooling" food in traditional Chinese dietary therapy, recommended for reducing internal heat, nourishing the lungs, and promoting longevity.

## Related Species

*Tremella fuciformis* belongs to a genus of approximately 100+ described species of jelly fungi. Several relatives are also commercially or medicinally significant.

*Tremella mesenterica* (witch's butter) is the most widespread species in the genus, forming bright yellow-orange jelly-like fruiting bodies on dead hardwood branches.

*Tremella aurantialba*, known as golden ear, is a closely related species parasitic on *Stereum hirsutum* that produces amber-colored fruiting bodies and is also cultivated in China for food and medicine.

*Tremella fuciformis* var. *aurantia* is a yellow-pigmented variant found in tropical regions, sometimes considered a separate species by some taxonomists.
