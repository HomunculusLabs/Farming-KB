## Chemical Properties

**Chemical name:** 3-[2-[ethyl(propyl)amino]ethyl]-1H-indol-4-ol

**Molecular formula:** C₁₃H₂₀N₂O

**Molecular weight:** 220.31 g/mol

**SMILES:** CCN(CCC)CCc1c[nH]c2ccc(O)cc12

**Structure type:** 4-substituted tryptamine with an unsymmetrical N,N-dialkyl substitution pattern (ethyl and propyl groups). The 4-hydroxy group on the [[serotonin-receptor-interference-psychedelic-mechanism]], the primary receptor responsible for psychedelic effects in the classical hallucinogen family.

The unsymmetrical N-ethyl-N-propyl substitution pattern reduces binding affinity at 5-HT₂A compared to the N,N-dimethyl substitution of psilocin. This reduction in affinity translates to higher threshold doses and generally milder subjective effects.

The compound likely shows cross-reactivity with other serotonin receptor subtypes including 5-HT₁A, 5-HT₂B, and 5-HT₂C, consistent with the broader tryptamine pharmacological profile.

**Receptor affinity data** for 4-HO-EPT specifically is extremely limited in the published literature. Estimates are derived from structure-activity relationship (SAR) analysis of related 4-substituted tryptamines.

The 4-hydroxyl group on the indole ring is essential for psychedelic activity. Dehydroxylation (removing the 4-OH group) produces EPT, which is reported to be significantly less potent and may be inactive at typical oral doses.

## Effects

The subjective effects of 4-HO-EPT are broadly similar to those of other 4-substituted tryptamines but are generally described as milder and less visually intense than psilocin.

**Onset:** 20–60 minutes when taken orally. The onset is somewhat slower than psilocin, likely due to reduced lipid solubility from the larger N-alkyl groups.

**Duration:** 3–5 hours total experience. The comedown is described as relatively smooth and gradual.

**Visual effects:** Moderate closed-eye visuals including geometric patterns, color enhancement, and mild visual distortion. Open-eye visuals are typically less pronounced than with psilocin or 4-HO-MET at equivalent subjective intensity levels.

**Cognitive effects:** Mild to moderate alteration of thought patterns. Users report a contemplative, introspective headspace with less cognitive chaos than stronger tryptamines. Some users describe a "clean" quality to the experience.

**Physical effects:** Typical tryptamine somatic effects including mild nausea (more common on the come-up), pupil dilation, increased body temperature, and mild tactile enhancement. Body load is generally reported as light.

## Dosage

Due to limited human experience data, dosage ranges are approximate and derived from a small number of self-reports in the research chemical community.

**Threshold dose:** 5–10 mg orally

**Light dose:** 10–15 mg orally

**Common dose:** 15–25 mg orally

**Strong dose:** 25–40 mg orally

The compound is approximately 2–4 times less potent than psilocin by weight, consistent with the reduced receptor affinity expected from the N-ethyl-N-propyl substitution pattern.
