## Overview

Cerinthe major, commonly known as honeywort, is an annual herbaceous plant in the family Boraginaceae. Native to the Mediterranean region, it is widely cultivated for its distinctive blue-purple bracts and tubular yellow flowers.

The plant attracts bees and other pollinators in abundance, making it a valued addition to wildlife gardens. It produces fleshy, blue-green foliage topped with pendant, tubular flowers enclosed by showy bracts.

These bracts deepen from blue-green to rich purple as they mature through the blooming period. Plants reach 30–60 cm in height and bloom prolifically from late spring through early summer.

The species is occasionally called "wax flower" or "blue shrimp plant" in the horticultural trade. It has gained significant popularity in contemporary garden design for its architectural form and unique coloration.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:** Kingdom Plantae → Clade Angiosperms → Clade Eudicots → Clade Asterids → Order Boraginales → Family Boraginaceae → Genus Cerinthe → Species C. major.

The genus name derives from the Greek "kerinos" (wax-like), referencing the waxy texture of the bracts and the plant's historical association with beekeeping. Cerinthe major is the type species of its genus.

The genus contains approximately 6 species distributed across the Mediterranean basin and southern Europe. Cerinthe major is the most widespread and horticulturally significant member.

Cerinthe minor is a related species with smaller flowers and bracts, occasionally found in cultivation. Hybridization between Cerinthe species is rare in the wild but has been reported in garden settings.

Leaves are fleshy, glaucous blue-green, and ovate to oblong in shape. They clasp the stem at the base and have a distinctive succulent texture.

The glaucous bloom on the leaves rubs off easily with handling, revealing a brighter green beneath. This waxy coating is an adaptation to reduce water loss in Mediterranean conditions.

Upper leaves transition gradually into the showy bracts that subtend the flowers. The tubular flowers are approximately 2 cm long and yellow to orange internally.

They are enclosed by the prominent purple-blue bracts that give the plant its ornamental appeal. The plant has a branching, somewhat sprawling habit with multiple flowering stems.

Each stem produces a terminal cluster of 3–8 flowers that open sequentially over several weeks. The fruit is a four-lobed schizocarp containing small, dark brown nutlets.

## Distribution and Habitat

Cerinthe major is native to southern Europe, North Africa, and the eastern Mediterranean basin. Its range extends from Portugal and Spain eastward through Italy, Greece, and Turkey to the Levant.

Populations in North Africa occur across Morocco, Algeria, and Tunisia in coastal and submontane habitats. The species shows a strong Mediterranean distribution pattern linked to winter-rainfall climates.

It grows naturally in rocky slopes, scrubland, field margins, and disturbed ground at low to moderate elevations up to 1200 m. The species thrives in full sun to light shade and prefers well-drained soils.

It tolerates poor, dry conditions and is well-adapted to Mediterranean climates with hot, dry summers. Naturalized populations occur in parts of western Europe, California, and southeastern Australia.

In these regions it has escaped cultivation and established along roadsides and waste ground. The species shows a preference for alkaline to neutral soils but adapts readily to most garden conditions.

It is not considered invasive in any jurisdiction despite its self-seeding capacity. The scattered naturalized populations remain localized and do not form dense monocultures.
