# Caryophyllene Oxide

## Overview

Caryophyllene oxide is a naturally occurring oxygenated bicyclic sesquiterpene and the primary oxidation product of β-caryophyllene. Found abundantly in the [[comparison-steam-distillation-vs-co2-extraction-for-essential-oils]] of numerous aromatic plants — including cannabis (*[[cannabis-sativa]]*), black pepper (*[[piper-nigrum]]*), cloves (*Syzygocimum-basilicum]], cinnamon (*Cinnamomum* spp.), basil (*[[ocimum-basilicum]]*), and rosemary (*[[rosmarinus-officinalis]]*) — caryophyllene oxide plays a significant role in plant defense chemistry and has attracted considerable research interest for its pharmacological activities. Notably, it is the **first terpene approved by the FDA as a food additive**, reflecting its established safety profile. It is also the compound responsible for the distinctive peppery "tingle" experienced when smoking cannabis, and it serves as a marker compound for detecting synthetic cannabinoid ("Spice") products in forensic drug testing.

## Chemical Identity

- **IUPAC name:** (1S,4R,6R,7S,10S)-4,12,12-Trimethyl-10-prop-1-en-2-yltricyclo[5.4.0.0⁴,⁶]undecan-8-one
- **Molecular formula:** C₁₅H₂₄O
- **Molecular weight:** 220.35 g/mol
- **CAS number:** 1139-30-6
- **Appearance:** Colorless to pale yellow viscous liquid
- **Odor:** Warm, woody, spicy, slightly balsamic and peppery
- **Solubility:** Insoluble in water; soluble in ethanol, ether, chloroform, and fixed oils
- **Boiling point:** 267–269°C at 760 mmHg
- **Density:** 1.00 g/cm³ at 20°C
- **Optical rotation:** [α]D²⁰ = −50° (in chloroform)

### Chemical Structure

Caryophyllene oxide is the epoxidized derivative of β-caryophyllene, featuring a characteristic 9-membered carbocyclic ring fused to a cyclobutane ring, with an epoxide bridge at the 4,5-position of the caryophyllene skeleton. The epoxide functional group is responsible for many of the compound's biological activities, including its reactivity with biological nucleophiles and its ability to modulate enzyme systems.

### Biosynthesis

Caryophyllene oxide is biosynthesized in plants via the following pathway:
1. Farnesyl pyrophosphate (FPP) is cyclized by **β-caryophyllene synthase** to form β-caryophyllene
2. β-Caryophyllene is subsequently oxidized by **cytochrome P450 monooxygenases** (specifically CYP71 family enzymes) at the 4,5-double bond to form the epoxide
3. The oxidation step is oxygen-dependent and occurs primarily in the glandular trichomes of cannabis and the secretory ducts of spice plants

The epoxidation can also occur non-enzymatically through oxidation during drying, curing, or storage of plant material, which explains why caryophyllene oxide levels often increase in cured cannabis relative to fresh material.

## Pharmacology

### CB2 Receptor Agonism

Caryophyllene oxide inherits the CB2 receptor binding activity of its parent compound β-caryophyllene, though with reduced affinity. β-Caryophyllene was identified by Gertsch et al. (2008) as a **dietary cannabinoid** — the first phytocompound outside the cannabis plant shown to selectively activate the CB2 receptor. The CB2 receptor is primarily expressed in immune cells (peripheral tissues) and mediates anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory effects without the psychoactive effects associated with CB1 receptor activation.

- **CB2 binding affinity (β-caryophyllene):** Kᵢ ≈ 155 nM; full agonist at CB2; no activity at CB1
- **Caryophyllene oxide:** Retains partial CB2 activity; contributes to the entourage effect in cannabis

### Anti-inflammatory Activity
Caryophyllene oxide has demonstrated significant anti-inflammatory effects across multiple preclinical models:

- **NF-κB pathway inhibition:** Suppresses the nuclear translocation of NF-κB, reducing the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines including TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, and COX-2
- **COX-2 inhibition:** Direct inhibition of cyclooxygenase-2 enzyme activity, comparable in vitro to some NSAIDs at appropriate concentrations
- **iNOS suppression:** Reduces inducible nitric oxide synthase expression in activated macrophages
- **TLR4 modulation:** Interferes with toll-like receptor 4 signaling, attenuating inflammatory cascade initiation

### Anticancer Properties

Preclinical studies have demonstrated promising anticancer activity:

- **Apoptosis induction:** Induces programmed cell death in multiple cancer cell lines including MCF-7 (breast), A549 (lung), and PC-3 (prostate) through mitochondrial-dependent pathways
- **Anti-proliferative:** Inhibits cancer cell proliferation via cell cycle arrest (G0/G1 and G2/M checkpoints)
- **Synergistic effects:** Legault and Pichette (2007) demonstrated that caryophyllene oxide potentiates the anticancer activity of α-humulene, its co-occurring sesquiterpene, suggesting enhanced efficacy through natural co-administration
- **Metastasis inhibition:** Reduces migration and invasion of cancer cells by downregulating matrix metalloproteinases (MMP-2, MMP-9)
- **Chemopreventive:** Phase II enzyme induction in hepatic tissue suggests potential chemopreventive activity through enhanced xenobiotic metabolism

### Antimicrobial Activity

- **Antifungal:** Active against *[[candida-albicans]]*, *Aspergillus niger*, and *Dermatophytes*; disrupts fungal cell membrane integrity
- **Antibacterial:** Demonstrated activity against *Staphylococcus aureus*, *Escherichia coli*, and *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* in vitro; mechanism involves membrane disruption and efflux pump inhibition
- **Anti-biofilm:** Inhibits biofilm formation and disrupts pre-formed biofilms in *P. aeruginosa* and *S. aureus*, suggesting potential applications in treating device-associated infections

### Neuroprotective Effects

- Reduces neuronal oxidative stress by scavenging reactive oxygen species (ROS) and upregulating endogenous antioxidant enzymes (SOD, catalase, GPx)
- Attenuates neuroinflammation in microglial cells via CB2-mediated pathways
- Demonstrated neuroprotective effects in rodent models of ischemic stroke and traumatic brain injury

### Analgesic Properties

Produces analgesic effects in preclinical pain models, mediated through both CB2 receptor activation and direct modulation of TRP channels (particularly TRPA1). The peppery "tingle" associated with caryophyllene oxide in cannabis smoke is likely mediated by TRPA1 activation on trigeminal nerve endings.

## Role in Cannabis

### Entourage Effect Contributor

Caryophyllene oxide contributes to the **entourage effect** — the synergistic interaction between cannabinoids, terpenes, and other cannabis constituents that modulates the overall pharmacological profile beyond the activity of any single compound:

- **Anti-inflammatory synergy:** Enhances the anti-inflammatory effects of CBD and THC through CB2 receptor co-activation and complementary pathway modulation
- **Bioavailability modulation:** May increase the transdermal permeability of cannabinoids, acting as a penetration enhancer
- **Flavor and sensory contribution:** Provides the characteristic peppery, woody, and spicy finish in cannabis smoke and vapor; the "peppery tickle" in the throat is a hallmark of high-caryophyllene strains

### Forensic Marker

Caryophyllene oxide serves as a critical **forensic marker** for distinguishing natural cannabis from synthetic cannabinoid products:

- Natural cannabis contains caryophyllene oxide as a normal component of its essential oil profile
- Synthetic cannabinoid products ("Spice," "K2") are typically plant material sprayed with synthetic cannabinoid agonists; the presence and ratio of caryophyllene oxide relative to other terpenes can help identify adulterated products
- Gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS) analysis of caryophyllene oxide is a standard procedure in forensic drug testing laboratories

## Industrial and Commercial Applications

- **Food additive:** FDA-approved as a flavoring agent (GRAS status); used in baked goods, beverages, candies, and chewing gum for its spicy, woody flavor
- **Fragrance industry:** Used as a base note in perfumery; provides warm, spicy, woody undertones in oriental and fougère fragrance families
- **Cosmetics:** Included in skincare formulations for its anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties
- **Insect repellent:** Demonstrated repellent activity against mosquitoes and agricultural pests; used in some natural insecticide formulations
- **Preservative:** Antimicrobial properties make it a candidate for natural food and cosmetic preservation systems

## Safety Profile

- **Acute toxicity:** Low acute toxicity (LD₅₀ > 5 g/kg in rodents); classified as essentially non-toxic
- **GRAS status:** Generally Recognized as Safe by the US FDA for use as a food flavoring agent
- **Skin sensitization:** Generally well-tolerated topically; rare cases of contact dermatitis reported at high concentrations
- **Drug interactions:** Theoretical potential for CYP450 enzyme interactions at high doses; no clinically significant interactions documented at normal dietary exposure levels
- **Pregnancy:** No specific contraindications at dietary exposure levels; insufficient data for therapeutic doses
