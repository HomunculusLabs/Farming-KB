## Overview

**Aristolochia clematitis** (birthwort) is a herbaceous perennial vine in the family Aristolochiaceae, native to southern and central Europe. The species has been used medicinally since antiquity but is now recognized as a significant health hazard due to high concentrations of carcinogenic aristolochic acids.

The plant is responsible for **aristolochic acid nephropathy (AAN)** and is implicated in **Balkan endemic nephropathy (BEN)**, a chronic [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]] and Australia, spreading aggressively in disturbed habitats.

Despite its toxicity, the species remains an important [[bloomfield-rust-germ-tube-tactile-sensing-leaf-surface-navigation]] is glabrous to slightly pubescent, bright green above and paler beneath.

Flowers are borne in the leaf axils on short pedicels from May to July.

The perianth forms a distinctive tubular structure, 1.5–3 cm long, with an inflated base (utricle) and a pointed, curved lip.

Flower color is yellow-green to pale yellow, sometimes with darker veining or brownish markings near the opening.

The gynostemium is a complex columnar structure uniting the stigma, style, and stamens.

Fruit is a dehiscent, spherical capsule approximately 1–1.5 cm in diameter, containing numerous triangular, winged seeds.

## Distribution and Habitat

*A. clematitis* is native to central and southern Europe, the Mediterranean region, and western Asia.

Its native range extends from France and Germany eastward to the Balkans, Turkey, and the Caucasus.

The species has been widely introduced beyond its native range as a medicinal and ornamental plant.

In [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]] and parts of Canada.

In Australia, naturalized populations occur in several eastern states.

The plant colonizes disturbed sites, waste ground, railway embankments, field margins, and riparian areas.

It prefers fertile, moist, well-drained soils in full sun to partial shade.

Agricultural fields in endemic nephropathy regions of the Balkans are a particularly significant habitat, as flour contamination from *A. clematitis* seeds is the proposed mechanism of BEN.

## Chemistry and Pharmacology

The primary bioactive constituents of *A. clematitis* are **aristolochic acid I** (AAI) and **aristolochic acid II** (AAII), nitrophenanthrene carboxylic acid derivatives.

AAI and AAII are potent nephrotoxins, causing progressive renal tubular atrophy and interstitial fibrosis.

These compounds are also mutagenic and carcinogenic, classified as Group 1 carcinogens by the IARC.

Metabolic activation of aristolochic acids forms aristolactam-DNA adducts, which are detectable in renal tissue decades after exposure.

Aristolactam adducts induce characteristic A-to-T transversion mutations in the tumor suppressor gene TP53.

The species also contains aristolochic acid III, IV, and aristolactam derivatives.

Additional constituents include aristolochic acid aristolactam nitro derivatives, various phenolic acids, and trace volatile oils.

The aristolochic acid content varies by plant part: roots and fruits contain the highest concentrations, leaves contain moderate amounts.
