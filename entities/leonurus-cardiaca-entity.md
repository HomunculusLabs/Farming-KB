## Leonurus cardiaca

**Leonurus cardiaca** L., commonly known as motherwort, is a perennial herbaceous
plant in the family Lamiaceae. Native to Eurasia, it has become naturalized
across [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]] worldwide and is valued both as a traditional medicinal
herb and as a functional plant in permaculture systems. The genus name
*Leonurus* derives from the Greek for "lion's tail," referring to the shape of
the calyx lobes, while *cardiaca* reflects its historical use as a
cardiotonic remedy.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

L. cardiaca is an erect, branching perennial reaching 60-120 cm in height.
Stems are square (characteristic of Lamiaceae), ribbed, and covered with
short hairs. Leaves are opposite, palmately lobed with 3-7 pointed lobes,

Modern pharmacological studies support anti-inflammatory (COX inhibition),
antioxidant (DPPH and ABTS scavenging), and mild antimicrobial activities.
Uterotonic effects of leonurin have been confirmed in isolated uterine
smooth muscle preparations. Clinical evidence remains limited, with most
data from animal models and in vitro studies.
