## Kaolin Clay

## Description

Kaolin (kaolinite) is a hydrated aluminium silicate clay mineral formed through the weathering of feldspar minerals such as orthoclase. Mollison describes the process by which water combines with feldspars in rock to swell and fracture them, then hydrates the feldspars into kaolin, clay, and potassium carbonate. Kaolin is a fundamental component of many soils and has significant applications in permaculture design.

## Classification

- **Category**: Mineral / Clay
- **Mineral group**: Kaolinite-serpentine group
- **Chemical formula**: Al2Si2O5(OH)4
- **Clay type**: 1:1 layer silicate (non-expanding)
- **Formation process**: Weathering of feldspar minerals (especially orthoclase) in granite

## Key Facts

- Forms through hydration and chemical breakdown of feldspar minerals in the presence of water
- One of the most common clay minerals in soils worldwide
- Non-expanding clay with lower cation exchange capacity than montmorillonite or bentonite clays
- Contributes to soil plasticity and workability
- Released carbonates from feldspar-to-kaolin weathering make soil water a stronger carbonic acid than rain alone
- Used industrially in ceramics, paper coating, and as a carrier for pesticides and fertilizers

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Soil structure**: Kaolin contributes to crumb structure in soils, maintaining pore space for air and water movement essential for fungal hyphae and root growth
- **Dam and pond sealing**: Kaolin-bearing soils (40%+ clay content) are suited to earth dam construction and natural pond sealing
- **Mud brick construction**: Soils with appropriate kaolin content can be pressed into building materials
- **Fungal habitat**: Clay minerals provide micro-habitats and exchange sites that support soil microbial and fungal communities
- **Cation exchange**: While lower than expanding clays, kaolin still contributes to nutrient retention in soils
- **Soil classification**: Kaolin content is a key factor in determining soil suitability for various permaculture earthworks
