## Quick Hoops

## Description

Quick hoops are low-cost, low-profile temporary tunnel structures used for overwintering hardy crops in the field. Designed by [[eliot-coleman]] at [[four-season-farm]] in Harborside, Maine, they provide a fraction of the protection of a full greenhouse at roughly 5% of the cost. They represent a key innovation in the "hidden farm" concept — the idea that any piece of land sitting empty through winter represents unrealized production potential.

## Classification

- **Category**: [[cervantes-season-extension-cloches-row-covers]] infrastructure
- **Type**: Low tunnel / temporary field structure
- **Scale**: Small-scale market farm
- **Climate suitability**: Northern temperate (zone 4–6)

## Physical Specifications

- **Hoops**: 10-foot lengths of ½-inch electrical conduit (plastic or metal)
- **Height**: ~30 inches at the apex when bowed into a half-circle
- **Coverage**: Two 30-inch beds side by side with a path between them
- **Spacing**: One hoop every 5 feet along the bed length
- **Bed length**: Typically 50 or 100 feet
- **Covering layers**:
  - Inner layer: 10-foot-wide spun-bonded row cover (agrofabric)
  - Outer layer: 10-foot-wide clear plastic (added in late November/early December)
- **Anchoring**: Sandbags every 5 feet along both edges

## Construction Method

1. Insert each end of the conduit ~10 inches into the soil on either side of the two beds
2. Plastic conduit can be bent as placed; metal conduit must be pre-bent with a tubing bender
3. Cover with spun-bonded row cover first, secured with sandbags
4. In late fall, add clear plastic over the row cover for additional [[cervantes-microclimate-frost-protection]]
5. Tighten plastic by driving stakes 4 feet beyond each end and tying rope to pull taut lengthwise
6. Adjust sandbags to tighten side-to-side
7. In heavy snow areas using plastic conduit, reduce spacing to 2½ feet between hoops

## Management Through the Seasons

### Fall
- Plant overwintering onions last week of August
- Place hoops and row cover mid-October
- Spinach and lettuce: direct sow or seed into soil blocks around October 1, transplant two weeks later
- Add plastic layer late November/early December
- Sowing too late risks seedling failure to establish before winter

### Winter
- Double-covered structures can maintain temperatures up to 55°F on sunny February days
- Everything freezes to ground level, locking covers in place naturally
- Minimal intervention required during deepest cold

### Spring
- Begin ventilating on sunny days by removing select sandbags on the southern edge
- Insert notched props to hold up edges for airflow
- Remove plastic layer by late March when fabric alone is sufficient
- Remove row cover entirely as outdoor temperatures moderate
- Begin harvesting overwintered scallions first, then successive crops through spring

## Suitable Crops

- **Onions / Scallions**: Plant 5 rows per bed; harvest 2 intermediate rows as scallions in spring, leaving 3 rows to develop bulbs by end of June — 5+ weeks before spring-planted crop
- **Spinach**: Direct sow or transplant; harvest up to a month earlier than spring transplants
- **Lettuce**: Overwintered lettuce gives outdoor crops far earlier than spring starts
- **Experimental possibilities**: Late-fall sowing of peas, carrots, beets, arugula for mid-February to mid-March germination under double cover

## Economics

- Cost to cover 1,000 sq ft (equivalent to one 22×48 ft greenhouse): approximately **1/20th (5%)** of greenhouse cost
- Quick hoops can be disassembled and redeployed to cover other crops in spring
- Have completely replaced curved wire wickets for supporting fabric covers over field crops

## Key Design Principles

1. Crops must be hardy enough to survive overwintering stress — [[coleman-winter-greens-variety-selection]] is critical
2. Sowing date must allow establishment before deep freeze but not trigger premature bolting
3. The system exploits "progressive hardiness" — matching crop cold tolerance to level of protection
4. Northern growers can experiment with late-fall sowing of seeds that remain dormant until February

## Relevance to Cultivation

Quick hoops are a practical, scalable solution for small farms seeking year-round production without the capital outlay of permanent greenhouse infrastructure. They are particularly relevant to:
- Market gardeners extending their sales season by 2+ months
- Farms with limited capital budgets
- Growers in northern climates (zones 4–6) protecting hardy crops through winter
- Any producer exploring the "hidden farm" — maximizing productive use of every square foot year-round
