## Description

**Ulorchestia spartinophila** is a species of amphipod crustacean found in salt marsh ecosystems where it feeds on decaying cordgrass ([[spartina]]) litter. It has complex dietary requirements and depends on fungal-colonized detritus for optimal growth and reproduction.









## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Animalia
- **Phylum**: Arthropoda
- **Subphylum**: Crustacea
- **Class**: Malacostraca
- **Order**: Amphipoda
- **Family**: Talitridae
- **Genus**: Ulorchestia
- **Species**: U. spartinophila









## Key Facts

- Inhabits salt marsh ecosystems dominated by cordgrass (Spartina spp.)
- Has complex dietary requirements that cannot be met by leaf litter alone
- Grows best and produces the most offspring when fed on decaying leaves containing high fungal biomass
- Demonstrates the importance of fungal conditioning in marine/estuarine detrital food webs









## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

U. spartinophila illustrates the principle that fungal biomass is often essential for invertebrate nutrition in detritus-based food webs. This has implications for mycological applications in aquaculture feed development, where fungal pre-treatment of agricultural wastes could enhance their nutritional value for crustacean and fish production.









## Ecological Role

In salt marsh ecosystems, U. spartinophila functions as a detritivore that processes cordgrass litter, making nutrients available to higher trophic levels. Its dependence on fungal-colonized detritus demonstrates the critical role of fungi as intermediaries in energy flow through estuarine ecosystems.
