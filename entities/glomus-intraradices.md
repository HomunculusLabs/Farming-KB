## Glomus intraradices

**Type:** Species (Fungus)
**Category:** [[rhizophagus-irregularis]] (current name), Glomus
  intraradices Schenck and Smith

## Overview
Glomus intraradices (now reclassified as Rhizophagus irregularis) is one of
the most widespread and well-studied arbuscular [[aact-microbial-foliar-nutrient-uptake-co2-stomata-ingham]] and providing protection against pathogens. It is
widely used in commercial mycorrhizal inoculant products.

## Taxonomy and Classification
- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Glomeromycota (formerly Zygomycota)
- **Order:** Glomerales
- **Family:** Glomeraceae
- **Genus:** Glomus (current: Rhizophagus)
- **Species:** G. intraradices (current: R. irregularis)

Note: Taxonomic revision has moved this species from Glomus to
Rhizophagus, but the name G. intraradices remains widely used in
the ecological and agricultural literature.

## Key Details

### Morphology
- Does not produce large [[arbuscule-isolation-and-nutrient-exchange-mechanisms]]
- May also form vesicles (swollen hyphal storage structures) in some
  cortical cells
- Produces an extensive network of extraradical hyphae in soil

### Symbiotic Mechanism
- Penetrates root cortical cells, forming arbuscules that optimize the
  surface area for nutrient and carbohydrate exchange
- The [[verticillium-dahliae]] (Lui, 1995)
- Reduced incidence of root [[bloomfield-fungal-pathogens-and-parasites]] when used as inoculant
- Mechanism involves both enhanced plant nutrition and direct
  antagonism against pathogens

### Ecological Distribution
- Found globally in agricultural soils, grasslands, and forests
- Associates with most crop plants, making it the most commercially
  relevant AMF species
- A biomass of 10 to 20% of root biomass is maintained by the fungus
- Daily maintenance cost is 0.1 to 1% of root biomass per day
  (Fitter, 1991)

### Soil Interactions
- Promotes formation of water-stable soil aggregates (0.5 to 2 mm
  diameter class)
- Hyphal networks contribute to soil structure stabilization
- Interacts with other soil microorganisms in the mycorrhizosphere
