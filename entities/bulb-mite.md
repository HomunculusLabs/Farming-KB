# Bulb Mite

## Description

Bulb mites are tiny arachnid pests belonging primarily to the genus Rhizoglyphus. They infest bulbs, corms, tubers, and roots of many ornamental and crop plants. They are particularly damaging during storage and during the early stages of plant growth. [[molasses-and-sugars-in-compost-tea-brewing]] research, bulb mites are notable because their actual feeding habits may differ from common assumptions — they may primarily be fungal feeders that switch to plant feeding only when fungal food sources are depleted.

## Classification

- **Class**: Arachnida
- **Order**: Sarcoptiformes
- **Family**: Acaridae
- **Genus**: Rhizoglyphus (most common)
- **Common Species**: Rhizoglyphus echinopus, Rhizoglyphus robini

## Key Facts

- Bulb mites are often assumed to be plant feeders, but may actually be primarily fungal feeders
- When their fungal food source is depleted, they attack seedlings, roots, and bulbs as secondary food
- Damage is most significant during [[seed-germination-and-stratification]] early seedling growth
- Once plants are larger and established, bulb mite damage becomes less critical
- The distinction between primary fungal feeding and secondary plant feeding has important management implications

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

### Feeding Behavior Insights
- Many insects and mites assumed to eat plants actually eat fungi as their primary food source
- When fungal food is available, these organisms prefer it over plant tissue
- Only when fungi are depleted do they switch to feeding on seedlings and roots
- This behavioral insight suggests that maintaining adequate [[bioremediation-fungal-biomass-biosorbent-material]] may redirect mite feeding away from plants

### Management Implications
- Understanding the actual food preferences of bulb mites is critical for effective management
- If bulb mites prefer fungi, then maintaining a healthy fungal population in soil may reduce plant damage
- Conversely, eliminating all fungi (as with fungicide applications) could force mites to feed on plants
- Compost tea with good fungal content may provide alternative food sources that keep mites away from plant tissue

### Biological Control Approach
- Local microbiologists or SFI advisors can help assess the food web relationships in affected soils
- Field-level biology assessment is more informative than laboratory plate [[mushroom-spawn-making-and-pure-culture-methods]]
- The goal is to maintain a balanced food web where mites have fungal food available
- Beneficial [[query-what-are-predatory-mites-and-how-can-i-use-them-for-pest-control]] insects that feed on bulb mites should be encouraged

### Research Needs
- Detailed studies on what bulb mites actually eat in field conditions are needed
- The conditions that trigger the switch from fungal feeding to plant feeding need documentation
- Whether compost tea applications can provide sufficient alternative fungal food needs testing
- The specific fungal species preferred by bulb mites could inform targeted [[forest-management-strategies]]
