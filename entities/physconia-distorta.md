## Overview

Physconia distorta is a foliose lichen in the family Physciaceae, commonly known as the twisted rosette lichen. It forms distinctive rosette-shaped thalli with lobes that curl upward at the margins, giving the species its name.

The lichen is widely distributed across [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]] of Europe and is particularly common on nutrient-enriched bark. It is considered an indicator of moderately eutrophic conditions and is often associated with agricultural landscapes.

P. distorta is morphologically variable, which has historically led to confusion with related Physconia species. Modern taxonomic treatments recognize it as a distinct species with characteristic chemical and morphological features.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Physconia distorta was originally described by August Heinrich Rudolf Grisebach and later placed in its current genus. The species epithet *distorta* refers to the characteristically upturned, twisted lobe margins.

The thallus forms rosettes 3–8 cm in diameter, closely appressed to the substrate at the center and becoming loosely attached at the lobe tips. The upper surface is gray to brownish-gray, typically pruinose near the center.

Lobes are 1–3 mm wide, irregularly branched, with margins that curl upward to expose the white medullary undersurface. This characteristic marginal twisting is the most reliable field identification feature.

The lower surface is white to pale buff with simple to rhizinate holdfasts. Rhizines are sparse, white, and primarily concentrated near the thallus center.

Apothecia are common, laminal to submarginal, 1–3 mm in diameter, with a brown disc and a thalline margin. The exciple contains physodic acid, which reacts P+ orange in spot tests.

Spores are brown, two-celled (Dictyospores), ellipsoid, measuring 18–28 × 9–14 μm. Pycnidia produce bacillar conidia approximately 5 × 1 μm.

## Distribution and Habitat

Physconia distorta is distributed across much of temperate Europe, from the British Isles and Scandinavia to the Mediterranean region. It is less common in eastern Europe and extends into western Asia.

In the British Isles, P. distorta is widespread and often locally abundant, particularly in lowland agricultural areas. It has declined in some heavily urbanized regions due to [[stamets-growing-room-air-circulation-co2-management]]. The species is absent from extremely xeric, exposed rock faces and from deeply shaded forest interiors where direct moisture retention is prolonged.

## Ecology and Conservation

Physconia distorta is considered an early-successional epiphyte that colonizes young bark and nutrient-enriched substrates. Its abundance is positively correlated with bark pH and the availability of dust-derived nutrients.

The species has shown significant recovery in parts of western Europe following reductions in sulfur dioxide emissions since the 1980s. It has recolonized many urban and peri-urban areas where it was previously extirpated.

However, P. distorta remains sensitive to ammonia pollution from intensive livestock operations, which can cause thallus bleaching and reduced fertility.
