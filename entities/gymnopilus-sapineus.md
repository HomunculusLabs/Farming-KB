# Gymnopilus sapineus

**Flammulina sapinea** | *Gymnopilus sapineus* (Fr.) Maire ex Singer

Gymnopilus sapineus is a wood-inhabiting mushroom in the family cortinariaceae (or hymenogastraceae under modern classification) that has been reported to contain [[comparison-spore-print-collection-vs-spore-syringe-production]] is rusty brown to ferruginous. Basidiospores are ellipsoid to amygdaliform, 7 to 10 by 5 to 6.5 µm, with a rough to warty ornamentation, and are dextrinoid.

Gymnopilus sapineus has a wide distribution across temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere. The bitter taste characteristic of the genus may be related to sesquiterpene compounds rather than the tryptamine alkaloids.

The psychoactive effects reported from Gymnopilus species are broadly similar to those of psilocybe species, including visual hallucinations, altered perception of time and space, and changes in mood and cognition. However, the experience is often described as less predictable and sometimes more dysphoric, possibly due to the presence of additional [[comparison-nutrient-cycling-vs-nutrient-dense-gardening]]. The species is primarily a white-rot decomposer, capable of degrading both cellulose and lignin in wood. This process releases nutrients bound in dead wood back into the soil, making them available for uptake by living plants.

The preference of G. sapineus for conifer substrates means it is particularly important in coniferous forest ecosystems where dead wood accumulation can be significant. It is one of the earlier successional saprotrophs on freshly fallen conifer logs, often appearing within a year or two of tree death.

## Similar Species

Several Gymnopilus species are morphologically similar and can be difficult to distinguish in the field:

- **gymnopilus penetrans**: Very similar, growing on conifer wood; some authorities consider it synonymous with G. sapineus
- **gymnopilus liquiritiae**: Grows primarily on broadleaf wood and has a darker cap; also contains psilocybin
