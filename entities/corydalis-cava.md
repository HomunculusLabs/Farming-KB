## Overview

*Corydalis cava* (formerly *Bulbocapnos cavus*), commonly known as hollowroot or bird-in-a-bush, is a perennial herbaceous plant in the family Fumariaceae. It is native to central and southern Europe and is notable for its tuberous root system and rich isoquinoline alkaloid profile.

The species has a long history of use in European folk medicine, particularly as a mild sedative, analgesic, and antispasmodic agent.

*Corydalis cava* is closely related to the well-studied Chinese [[query-how-do-i-build-a-medicinal-herb-drying-rack-and-optimize-drying-conditions]] [[corydalis-yanhusuo]] species.

The alkaloid content varies seasonally, with highest concentrations in the tuber during [[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]] emergence and lowest during the dormant period.

Minor constituents include several aporphine and protoberberine-type alkaloids.

## Pharmacology

Bulbocapnine acts primarily as a dopamine receptor antagonist with particular activity at D1 and D2 receptor subtypes.

This dopaminergic antagonism underlies the plant's traditional use as a sedative and tranquilizer in European folk medicine.

Corydaline exhibits smooth muscle relaxant and antispasmodic properties, supporting traditional use for menstrual cramps and gastrointestinal spasms.

Protopine shows mild analgesic and anti-inflammatory activity in animal models.

The combination of dopaminergic antagonism and spasmolytic effects produces the characteristic calming and pain-relieving profile of the whole tuber extract.

Modern pharmacological studies have confirmed mild anxiolytic effects in rodent models, though clinical human data remains limited.

## Traditional Uses

In European folk medicine, *Corydalis cava* tubers were prepared as decoctions or tinctures for nervous disorders and insomnia.

The plant was used in German and Austrian traditional medicine (noted in the BGA Commission E monographs) as a mild sedative.

Traditional applications included treatment of menstrual pain, headaches, and mild anxiety states.

The tubers were also applied externally as poultices for rheumatic complaints and skin inflammations.

Corydalis preparations were common in 19th-century European pharmacy before being largely replaced by synthetic sedatives and analgesics.

## Cultivation

*Corydalis cava* is cultivated as an ornamental garden plant for its early spring flowers and shade tolerance.

It prefers partial to full shade in humus-rich, well-drained soils with neutral to slightly alkaline pH.

Propagation is primarily by division of the tuberous corms in late summer during the dormant period.

[[jeavons-seed-propagation-techniques]] is possible but requires cold stratification for germination, and plants may take 2-3 years to flower from seed.

The species is generally pest-free and requires minimal maintenance once established in suitable conditions.
