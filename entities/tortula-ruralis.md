## Tortula ruralis

## Overview

Tortula ruralis, commonly known as star moss or twisted moss, is a widely distributed
desiccation-tolerant moss in the family Pottiaceae. It is among the most extensively
studied bryophytes in plant stress physiology due to its extraordinary ability to
survive nearly complete dehydration and rapidly rehydrate with full metabolic recovery.

The species is recognized by its star-shaped rosette growth form and the twisted
appearance of its leaves when dry. It colonizes exposed soil, rock surfaces, walls,
and disturbed ground across temperate and boreal regions worldwide.

Tortula ruralis has served as a [[syntrichia-ruralis]] (Hedw.) F.Weber & D.Mohr is
increasingly used in floristic literature.

Plants form loose to dense turfs or cushions, typically 1 to 3 centimeters tall.
The stems are erect and sparsely branched, arising from a creeping protonema.

Leaves are lanceolate to lingulate, 2.5 to 4.5 millimeters long, with recurved
margins. When dry, the leaves twist and contort tightly around the stem.

The leaf nerve (costa) is strong and excurrent, extending beyond the leaf apex as
a short, colorless hair point. Laminal cells are quadrate to rounded-rectangular in
the upper leaf, becoming elongate toward the base.

The capsule is cylindrical to ellipsoidal, erect and symmetric, with 16 peristome
teeth that are spirally twisted when dry. Spores are spherical, 9 to 14 micrometers
in diameter, papillose.

## Distribution and Habitat

Tortula ruralis has a nearly [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]], Asia, and parts of Africa, [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-south-america]],
and Australasia.

In North America, the species ranges from Alaska southward to California and Texas.
It is absent only from the most humid tropical lowlands and the driest deserts.

The moss colonizes exposed soil, rock outcrops, concrete walls, rooftops, disturbed
ground, and mine tailings. It is particularly characteristic of dry, mineral-rich
surfaces at elevations from sea level to over 3000 meters.

Tortula ruralis is a pioneer species on disturbed substrates and is among the
first bryophytes to colonize newly exposed soil or rock.

## Desiccation Tolerance and Physiology

Tortula ruralis is one of the most desiccation-tolerant organisms known. It can
lose over 95 percent of its cellular water content and survive in an air-dried
state for months or even years.

Recovery upon rehydration is remarkably rapid. [[photosynthetic-efficiency]] returns
to near-normal levels within minutes to hours, and protein synthesis resumes within
30 minutes of rehydration. Full physiological recovery typically occurs within 24
hours.
