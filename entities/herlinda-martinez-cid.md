## Herlinda Martinez Cid

## Description

Dona Herlinda Martinez Cid was a teacher from Huautla de Jimenez who served as the interpreter and cultural intermediary for the 1962 Hofmann-Wasson expedition to the Mazatec country in search of the magic plant ska Maria Pastora (Salvia divinorum). She played a crucial role in the expedition by providing translation between Mazatec and Spanish, facilitating contacts with curanderos and curanderas through her extensive network of relatives scattered throughout the Sierra Mazateca, and helping navigate the complex social dynamics of the remote mountain communities.

## Classification

- **Type**: Person, interpreter and cultural intermediary
- **Occupation**: Teacher
- **Location**: Huautla de Jimenez, Oaxaca, Mexico
- **Languages**: Mazatec and Spanish
- **Role**: Interpreter and cultural facilitator for the 1962 expedition

## Key Facts

- Joined the expedition at Rio Santiago, having ridden there on invitation from Gordon Wasson who had known her since his earlier mushroom expeditions
- Served as the critical linguistic bridge between the German-speaking/Spanish-speaking visitors and the Mazatec-speaking curanderas and curanderos
- Had numerous relatives scattered throughout the Sierra Mazateca who helped establish contacts with local healers
- Was acquainted with the dangers of the region and had been apprehensive about the expedition party when they were delayed
- Translated Consuela Garcia"s instructions and ceremony from Mazatec into Spanish during the hojas de la Pastora ceremony at San Jose Tenango
- Translated Maria Sabina's words during the mushroom ceremony with synthetic psilocybin pills at Huautla de Jimenez
- Hosted the Hofmann-Wasson party in her house in Huautla de Jimenez for the mushroom ceremony with Maria Sabina
- Her home served as the venue for the historic ceremony where Maria Sabina confirmed that synthetic psilocybin pills had the same power as the mushrooms

## Role in the Expedition

Herlinda's role extended far beyond simple translation. She served as the cultural navigator who understood both the Western scientific perspective of the visitors and the indigenous healing traditions of her people. Her network of family connections throughout the Sierra Mazateca was essential for gaining access to the closely guarded secret practices of the curanderos and curanderas. Without her mediation, the expedition would have been unable to establish the trust necessary to witness and participate in the sacred ceremonies that provided crucial scientific and ethnographic data.

## The Mushroom Ceremony at Her Home

The ceremony with Maria Sabina took place in Herlinda's house in Huautla de Jimenez. Herlinda translated Hofmann's explanation that one pill contained the spirit of two pairs of mushrooms. When Maria Sabina initially declared that the pills lacked the spirit of the mushrooms (because absorption from pills was slower than from chewed mushrooms), Herlinda communicated this concern. After additional pills were distributed and the effects became apparent, Maria Sabina confirmed through Herlinda that the pills had the same power as the mushrooms, a historic validation of synthetic psilocybin.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Herlinda Martinez Cid exemplifies the essential role of cultural intermediaries in ethnobotanical research. Without bilingual individuals who can navigate both indigenous and Western cultural frameworks, the documentation and understanding of traditional plant medicine practices would be impossible. Her contribution to the 1962 expedition was indispensable for the botanical identification of Salvia divinorum, the chemical investigation of its active principles, and the ethnographic documentation of Mazatec healing practices. Her work demonstrates that successful ethnopharmacological research depends not only on scientific expertise but on the trust, translation, and cultural navigation provided by local intermediaries.

## The Historic Psilocybin Validation

Perhaps the most historically significant moment in which Herlinda played a role was the validation of synthetic psilocybin by Maria Sabina. When Sabina initially declared the pills lacked the spirit of the mushrooms, Hofmann and Wasson distributed additional pills, bringing the total dose to approximately 30 mg psilocybin each for the curanderas and curandero. After about fifteen more minutes, the effects became apparent and the ceremony proceeded through the night until dawn. As the party took leave, Maria Sabina said through Herlinda that "the pills had the same power as the mushrooms, that there was no difference." She was also delighted to receive a vial of psilocybin pills as a parting gift, explaining through Herlinda that she could now give consultations even in the season when no mushrooms grow — a moment that symbolically bridged the ancient indigenous tradition and modern pharmaceutical science.

## The Return Journey

After the expedition, Hofmann noted that he had lost several kilograms in body weight but was "overwhelmingly compensated in enchanting experiences." The herbarium samples of hojas de la Pastora that the expedition collected were subsequently identified by Carl Epling and Carlos D. Jativa-M at the Botanical Institute of Harvard University as a previously undescribed species of Salvia, which they named Salvia divinorum. This botanical identification was the direct scientific outcome of the expedition that Herlinda's mediation had made possible, and it confirmed the existence of a new psychoactive Salvia species used in traditional Mazatec healing.
