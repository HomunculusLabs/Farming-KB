## Description

Mibuna (*[[brassica-rapa]]* subsp. *nipposinica*) is a Japanese leafy green vegetable in the brassica family. It produces slender, elongated leaves with a mild mustard flavor and is grown as a component of both salad and braising mixes in winter greenhouse production. Closely related to mizuna, it is one of the hardy Asian greens that thrive in unheated winter structures.

## Classification

- **Type**: Species / Vegetable crop
- **Species**: *Brassica rapa* subsp. *nipposinica*
- **Family**: Brassicaceae (mustard family)
- **Category**: Asian green, salad/braising green
- **Origin**: Japan

## Key Facts

- Produces narrow, elongated spoon-shaped leaves on slender white stems
- Mild mustard flavor, less pungent than many other Asian greens
- Extremely cold-hardy — thrives in unheated cold houses through Zone 5 winters
- Included in [[eliot-coleman]]'s braising mix alongside mizuna, tatsoi, pak choi, tokyo bekana, spinach, and chard
- Leaves are harvested at 4 inches or less for braising mix
- Can also be used in baby-leaf salad mixes at smaller sizes
- Quick-growing and suitable for succession planting

## Cultivation Notes

- One of the Oriental greens excluded from the salad mix because customers prefer salads without the mustard tang
- Better suited to braising mix or Asian salad applications where its flavor is desirable
- Responds well to the protected microclimate of cold houses — the combination of wind protection and moderated temperatures is ideal
- Like other Asian greens, should be harvested promptly when ready, as it will bolt to seed as days lengthen in late winter

## Relevance to Cultivation

Mibuna is a core crop in the diversified winter greenhouse system, representing the category of hardy Asian brassicas that make unheated winter production viable. Its inclusion in the braising mix alongside mizuna, tatsoi, and pak choi demonstrates how a suite of cold-hardy Asian greens can provide continuous harvests through the coldest months with no heat input. These crops fill the niche between the extremely hardy European winter vegetables (mache, spinach) and the less hardy salad components (lettuce, endive) that require some heat protection.
