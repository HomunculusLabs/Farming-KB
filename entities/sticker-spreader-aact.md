# Sticker-Spreader (AACT Application)

## Description

Sticker-spreaders are adjuvant materials added to [[actively-aerated-compost-tea]] (AACT) before [[aact-foliar-application-and-sticker-spreaders]] to improve the attachment and distribution of [[forest-and-bamboo-beneficial-microorganisms]] on leaf surfaces. [[elaine-ingham]] discusses several sticker-spreader options, emphasizing natural and biology-compatible choices while warning against products that harm beneficial organisms.

## Classification

- **Category**: Agricultural adjuvant
- **Type**: Surfactant + adhesive
- **Function**: Improves coverage and retention of foliar-applied organisms
- **Application**: Added to AACT just before spraying

## Key Facts

- **Dual function**: "Sticker" improves adhesion of organisms to leaf surfaces; "spreader" improves uniform coverage
- **Critical for foliar success**: Without sticker-spreaders, beneficial organisms from compost tea may fail to colonize leaf surfaces
- **Rain resistance**: Proper sticker-spreaders help organisms survive light to moderate rainfall
- **Compatibility essential**: Must not harm the beneficial organisms being applied

## Recommended Sticker-Spreaders (Ingham)

### 1. Aloe Vera
- Natural plant-derived product
- Provides both sticking and spreading properties
- Compatible with beneficial microorganisms
- May provide additional nutrients and growth compounds
- Gentle on biology; does not harm bacteria or fungi

### 2. Saponin-based Products (e.g., Nu-Film)
- Derived from plant saponins
- Effective at reducing [[bloomfield-buller-drop-surface-tension-spore-catapult-basidiospore-discharge]]
- Good film-forming properties
- Widely available commercially

### 3. Fish Hydrolysate (Used Carefully)
- Provides nutrients while improving adhesion
- Must be used at low rates to avoid attracting pests
- Protein content helps organisms adhere to surfaces
- Also feeds organisms during the attachment phase

### 4. Unsulphured Molasses
- Provides food for organisms while improving stickiness
- Sugar content helps with adhesion
- Also provides minerals (calcium, magnesium, potassium, iron)
- Must be unsulphured to avoid harming biology

### 5. Natural Bacterial and Fungal Stick (Good Growth)
- When organisms have grown well in the tea, they naturally produce extracellular polymeric substances (EPS) that help them stick
- Well-brewed tea with good fungal biomass may need less additional sticker
- This is a quality indicator - if organisms do not stick, the tea may not have adequate biology

## Not Recommended: Dormant Oils

- **Harmful to beneficial organisms**: Petroleum-based oils can suffocate bacteria and fungi
- **Incompatible with biology**: Defeats the purpose of applying living organisms
- **Leaf surface damage**: Can interfere with natural leaf processes

## Application Protocol

1. **Brew AACT** to completion (18-24 hours with proper aeration)
2. **Dilute** to application strength if needed
3. **Add sticker-spreader** at recommended rate
4. **Mix gently** - avoid high shear that could damage organisms
5. **Apply immediately** - do not store tea with sticker-spreader added
6. **Spray for coverage** - aim for thorough coverage including leaf undersides

## Leaf Coverage Targets

Ingham provides specific coverage targets for foliar AACT applications:
- **Minimum coverage**: At least 60-70% of leaf surface should be colonized by beneficial organisms
- **Leaf underside**: Critical to cover undersides where stomata and many pathogens are located
- **No high magnification needed**: Effectiveness can be assessed with standard microscopy
- **Reapplication**: If coverage is insufficient, reapply within 3-7 days

## Rain Considerations

- **Light rain (1-5mm)**: Organisms with sticker-spreader should persist; minimal impact
- **Moderate rain (5-15mm)**: Some loss of organisms; monitor and reapply if needed
- **Heavy rain (>15mm)**: Significant loss likely; reapplication recommended
- **Timing**: Apply when at least 4-6 hours of dry weather follows application

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

The sticker-spreader concept is central to successful biological [[clubroot-disease-management]]:

- **Phyllosphere colonization**: Beneficial organisms must establish on leaf surfaces to compete with pathogens for space and resources
- **Biofilm formation**: Sticker-spreaders help initiate the formation of protective biofilms by [[ingham-aact-beauveria-fruit-fly-beneficial-bacteria-inoculant]]
- **Fungal attachment**: Beneficial fungi need to establish hyphal contact with leaf surfaces; sticker-spreaders improve initial attachment
- **Economic efficiency**: Better coverage and retention mean fewer applications are needed
