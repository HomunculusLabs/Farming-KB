# European Squid (*Loligo vulgaris*)

## Overview

*Loligo vulgaris* is a medium-sized neritic squid distributed throughout
the eastern Atlantic, Mediterranean Sea, and adjacent waters. It is one of
the most commercially important cephalopods in European fisheries, forming
the basis of significant trawl and artisanal catches. The species is a key
pelagic predator in continental shelf ecosystems and serves as prey for
numerous marine vertebrates.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Animalia
- **Phylum:** Mollusca
- **Class:** Cephalopoda
- **Order:** Myopsida
- **Family:** Loliginidae
- **Genus:** *Loligo*
- **Species:** *L. vulgaris* (Lamarck, 1798)
- **Common names:** European squid, common squid
- **Etymology:** *Loligo* is a Latinization of a vernacular name for squid;
  *vulgaris* means "common" in Latin.

## Morphology

Adult mantle lengths range from 15 to 40 cm, with total body lengths
reaching 50 cm in large females. Females are larger than males, a
consistent sexual dimorphism in the species. The mantle is elongated and
cylindrical, tapering to a pointed posterior. The head bears eight short
arms and two longer tentacles with expanded clubs bearing suckers and
hook-like structures.

The fins are rhomboid and extend along approximately two-thirds of the
mantle length, providing primary locomotion through undulatory swimming.
Jet propulsion via mantle contraction provides rapid escape bursts. The
internal shell (pen or gladius) is a chitinous, feather-shaped structure
providing rigid support along the dorsal mantle.

Chromatophore cells in the skin enable rapid color changes for camouflage
and intraspecific signaling. The eye is camera-type with a lens projecting
an image onto a single-layered retina, adapted for low-light conditions
in deeper waters. Hemocyanin-based blue blood transports oxygen at lower
efficiency than vertebrate hemoglobin but functions effectively in cold,
low-oxygen marine environments.

## Distribution and Habitat

The species ranges from the North Sea and British Isles south along the
European coast to Senegal and the Canary Islands. It is abundant throughout
the Mediterranean Sea, the Adriatic, and the Aegean. Populations also
occur around the Azores, Madeira, and along the northwest African coast.

*Loligo vulgaris* inhabits continental shelf waters from 10 to approximately
500 meters depth, with peak abundance at 50–200 meters. It prefers sandy
or muddy substrates near the seafloor during the day, migrating vertically
into the water column at night to feed. Seasonal migrations follow water
temperature gradients, with populations moving to deeper, warmer waters in
winter.

## Ecology

European squid are opportunistic predators feeding primarily on small pelagic
fish (sardines, anchovies, herring), crustaceans, and other cephalopods.
Prey capture involves rapid tentacle extension followed by transfer to the
arm crown for manipulation. Feeding rates are highest at dawn and dusk,
coinciding with vertical migration periods.
