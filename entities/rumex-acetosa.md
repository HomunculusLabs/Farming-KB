# Rumex acetosa

Rumex acetosa, commonly known as common sorrel, garden sorrel, or
narrow-leaved dock, is a perennial herbaceous plant in the family
Polygonaceae. Native to Europe and temperate Asia, it has been
naturalized across much of [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]] and is widely cultivated
as a culinary herb. Sorrel is prized for its distinctive sharp,
lemon-sour flavor, which comes from high concentrations of oxalic
acid in the leaves. The plant has a long history of culinary and
medicinal use dating back to [[psychedelic-use-in-ancient-greece]] and Rome, where it was
valued both as a spring tonic and as a treatment for scurvy due
to its high vitamin C content. In permaculture and [[food-forest]]
designs, sorrel serves as a productive ground-cover perennial
that provides [[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]] greens before many annual vegetables
are harvestable.

## Taxonomy and Classification

- **Order:** Caryophyllales
- **Family:** Polygonaceae (buckwheat family)
- **Genus:** Rumex
- **Species:** R. acetosa
- **Authority:** Carl Linnaeus, 1753
- **Common names:** Common sorrel, garden sorrel, spinach dock,
  narrow-leaved dock, sour dock
- **Common relatives:** R. scutatus (French sorrel), R. patientia
  (patience dock), R. crispus (curly dock), R. obtusifolius
  (broad-leaved dock), Rheum rhabarbarum (rhubarb, same family)

## Description

Common sorrel forms a basal rosette of elongated, arrow-shaped
leaves with distinctive pointed basal lobes (hastate leaf shape).
The leaves are 6-15 cm long and 2-5 cm wide, with a smooth to
slightly wavy margin. The plant produces flowering stems (bolts)
in late spring to early summer, reaching 30-100 cm in height.
The inflorescence is a slender, densely packed raceme of small,
reddish-green flowers. As a dioecious species, individual plants
are either male or female, with female plants typically producing
more leaf biomass. The root system consists of a deep, branching
taproot that allows the plant to persist through drought and
winter dormancy. Established plants are extremely cold-hardy,
with new growth emerging as soon as soil temperatures rise
above freezing in early spring.

## Nutritional Value

Sorrel leaves are nutritionally dense, particularly in vitamin C,
vitamin A (as beta-carotene), and potassium. The following values
are approximate per 100 g of fresh leaves:

| Nutrient | Amount per 100g |
|----------|----------------|
| Energy | 22 kcal |
| Protein | 2.0 g |
| Fat | 0.7 g |
| Carbohydrate | 3.2 g |
| Fiber | 2.9 g |
| Vitamin C | 48 mg (80% DV) |
| Vitamin A | 4000 IU (80% DV) |
| Iron | 2.4 mg (13% DV) |
| Potassium | 390 mg |
| Calcium | 44 mg |
| Magnesium | 103 mg |
| Oxalic acid | 300-700 mg |

The high oxalic acid content is both sorrel's defining flavor
characteristic and its primary nutritional concern. Individuals
with kidney stones, gout, or [[blesching-cannabis-rheumatoid-arthritis]] should consume
sorrel in moderation due to oxalate's potential to aggravate
these conditions. Cooking reduces oxalic acid content by 30-50%.
