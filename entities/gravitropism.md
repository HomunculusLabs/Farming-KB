# Gravitropism

## Description

Gravitropism (also called geotropism) is the directional growth response of organisms to the force of gravity. In plants, roots exhibit positive gravitropism (growing toward gravity) while stems exhibit negative gravitropism (growing away from gravity). Mushrooms also display gravitropic responses, which influence the direction of fruiting body development and the overall morphology of the mushroom.

## Classification

- **Category:** Biological phenomenon
- **Domain:** Mycology / Plant physiology
- **Related concepts:** Tropism, phototropism, hydrotropism, thigmotropism

## Key Facts

- Most plants exhibit gravitropism: roots grow toward gravitational pull (positive gravitropism) while stems grow in the opposite direction (negative gravitropism).
- Mushrooms respond to gravity during fruiting body development. The stipe (stem) grows upward (negative gravitropism) while the cap or fruiting structure develops to orient spore-producing surfaces downward for effective spore dispersal.
- Reishi mushrooms (Ganoderma lucidum) are recommended for gravitropism experiments because they form antlers—long, thin, sturdy pins that are slow growing, making observations very easy.
- Gravitropism in mushrooms can be disrupted by centrifugal force, demonstrated using a rotating turntable setup that creates a sideways gravitational pull.
- The experiment involves attaching colonized fruiting cups to a record player turntable and comparing their growth patterns to a stationary control group over one to two weeks.
- Gravitropic responses help ensure that spore-bearing surfaces face downward, optimizing spore release and dispersal in natural environments.
- Understanding gravitropism is important for mushroom cultivators, as it affects fruiting body morphology and can influence how mushrooms develop in different orientations.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Gravitropism has practical implications for mushroom cultivation. When mushrooms are grown in unusual orientations or in environments with altered gravity vectors, their fruiting body morphology changes. This knowledge helps cultivators understand why mushrooms sometimes grow in unexpected directions when fruiting containers are moved or rotated. In educational settings, gravitropism experiments using reishi mushrooms on turntables provide compelling visual demonstrations of fungal responses to physical forces. The phenomenon is also relevant to space-based mushroom cultivation, where microgravity conditions would fundamentally alter fruiting body development patterns.

## Experimental Protocol

The gravitropism experiment involves cultivating reishi mushrooms in cups, using half as a control group in a standard plastic bin and attaching the other half to a record player turntable in an identical bin. When primordia appear, the turntable is activated continuously. The centrifugal force creates a sideways gravitational pull, causing the developing fruiting bodies to grow at angles rather than straight up. Students observe and compare growth patterns between the experimental and control groups over one to two weeks, documenting how altered gravitational vectors influence mushroom morphology.
