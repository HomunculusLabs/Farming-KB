# Bryoria trichodes (Pale Horsehair Lichen)

## Overview

*Bryoria trichodes* is a fruticose, hair-like lichen in the family Parmeliaceae, forming pale grey to brownish pendulous tufts on the branches and trunks of coniferous trees in boreal and montane forests. It is one of the more widespread species in the *Bryoria* section *Implexae*.

The species is characterized by its slender, cylindrical thallus branches, absence of soralia, and the presence of norstictic acid as its primary secondary compound. It is easily confused with other dark *Bryoria* species but can be distinguished by its lighter coloration and chemistry.

*B. trichodes* occurs predominantly in subalpine and boreal conifer forests across the Northern Hemisphere. It is particularly abundant on spruce and fir in humid, old-growth forest stands.

The species is ecologically important as a winter food source for caribou and other boreal herbivores in some regions. It also contributes significantly to epiphytic biomass and moisture retention in forest canopies.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Lecanoromycetes
- **Order:** Lecanorales
- **Family:** Parmeliaceae
- **Genus:** *Bryoria* Brodo & D. Hawksw.
- **Species:** *B. trichodes* (Michx.) Brodo & D. Hawksw.

**Synonyms:**
- *Usnea trichodes* Michx. (basionym)
- *Alectoria trichodes* (Michx.) Ach.
- *Bryoria trichodes* subsp. *americana* (Gyeln.) Brodo & D. Hawksw.
- *Bryoria lanestris* auct. sensu Degel. (misapplied in part)

The thallus is fruticose, forming pendent to suberect tufts 5–20 cm long. Main branches are cylindrical, 0.15–0.35 mm in diameter, dark grey to pale brown, and intricately branched in a dichotomous to anisotomic pattern.

Branching is generally denser and more tangled than in *B. fuscescens*, with frequent anastomoses where branches fuse together. The surface is matte to weakly shiny, lacking the glossy sheen seen in some congeners.

Pseudocyphellae are absent or extremely rare and inconspicuous, distinguishing *B. trichodes* from species like *B. caperata* which has prominent pseudocyphellae. Soralia are absent; reproduction is primarily by fragmentation and ascospores.

Apothecia are occasional, lateral, 1–3 mm in diameter, with a brown disc and a thin thalline margin. Spores are broadly ellipsoid, colorless, 8 per ascus, approximately 7–11 × 4–6 μm.

Pycnidia are common, immersed to slightly protruding, producing bacilliform conidia approximately 5–7 × 1 μm. The medulla is white to pale yellow, loosely woven, and the axis is relatively thin and elastic.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Bryoria trichodes* has a circumpolar boreal and montane distribution. In [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]], extending southward along the Rocky and Appalachian mountain chains.

In Europe, the species occurs in Fennoscandia, the Alps, the Carpathians, and scattered mountain ranges through Central and Eastern Europe. It is more frequent in continental boreal regions than in oceanic areas.

The species is strongly associated with coniferous forests. Preferred phorophytes include *Picea abies*, *[[abies-lasiocarpa]]*, *[[dighton-nitrogen-deposition-mycorrhizal-community-shifts]]. It has declined in [[cervantes-air-quality-ventilation]] but persists in more remote boreal and alpine regions.

*B. trichodes* contributes to canopy epiphyte biomass and intercepts moisture from fog and precipitation. This intercepted water drips to the forest floor, contributing to local hydrological cycling.

In parts of North America, *Bryoria* species including *B. trichodes* are consumed as winter forage by caribou (*Rangifer tarandus*) and other cervids. The lichens provide digestible carbohydrates during periods of snow cover.

The tangled thallus structure provides microhabitat for tardigrades, mites, and other microarthropods. Several [[lichenicolous-fungi]] have been recorded on *Bryoria* species, though specific associates of *B. trichodes* are poorly documented.

## Chemistry and Secondary Metabolites

The primary secondary metabolite of *Bryoria trichodes* is norstictic acid, a depsidone that produces a characteristic K+ yellow to red spot test reaction. This distinguishes it from the physodic acid chemotype of *B. fuscescens*.

Some populations additionally contain connorstictic acid, stictic acid, or traces of atranorin. The norstictic acid chemotype is the most widespread and is considered the defining chemical profile for the species.

Spot test reactions: K+ yellow turning red (norstictic acid), C−, KC+ yellow, Pd+ yellow. The PD test is positive, distinguishing *B. trichodes* from some *Bryoria* species that lack norstictic acid.

No significant toxic compounds have been reported from *B. trichodes*. However, some *Bryoria* species contain vulpinic acid, which is toxic to mammals. *B. trichodes* does not produce this compound.
