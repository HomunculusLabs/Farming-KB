## Overview

**Conophytum bilobum** (Marloth) N.E.Br., commonly known as the **two-lobed conophytum** or **living pebble**, is a dwarf succulent in the family Aizoaceae. It is native to the arid winter-rainfall regions of the Northern Cape and Western Cape provinces of South [[guzman-allen-gartz-africa-underexplored-neurotropic-fungi]].

The genus name *Conophytum* means "cone-shaped plant," referring to the conical body shape common in the genus. The species epithet *bilobum* describes the distinctive bilobed body, which resembles a small pair of fused green or gray-green cones.

Conophytum bilobum is one of the most variable and widespread species in the genus. It displays extraordinary morphological [[guzman-allen-gartz-india-neurotropic-fungi-diversity-psychoactive-species]] across its range, with forms varying in body shape, color, markings, and flower color from white to yellow to deep magenta.

The species is popular among succulent collectors for its compact size, unusual morphology, and showy autumn flowers. It requires a strict winter-growing, summer-dormant cultivation cycle that differs from most common succulents.

The genus *Conophytum* contains approximately 100 species, all native to South Africa and southern Namibia. It is the second-largest genus in the Aizoaceae after *Lithops* in terms of horticultural popularity. Many species are extremely rare in cultivation.

## Distribution and Habitat

Conophytum bilobum is endemic to South Africa, occurring across a broad swath of the Northern Cape and Western Cape. Populations extend from the Richtersveld in the north to the southern edges of Namaqualand.

The species grows on quartzite gravel plains, rocky hillside crevices, and shale slopes. It is frequently found growing in the partial shade of larger rocks or shrubs, which provide protection from intense afternoon sun.

The climate is arid, with winter rainfall of 100–250 mm annually. Summers are extremely hot and dry. Plants survive the summer dormant period by re[[query-how-to-prevent-and-treat-damping-off-in-cannabis-seedlings]]ing into their dried, papery sheaths, which protect the living tissue from desiccation.

Populations at different localities show marked morphological [[query-difference-between-white-rot-and-brown-rot-fungi]]s. Plants from coastal areas tend to be larger with greener bodies, while inland populations are more compact with gray or purple-tinged bodies. This variability has led to the description of numerous subspecies and varieties.

Taxonomists have described over 20 infraspecific taxa within Conophytum bilobum, though many of these are not universally accepted. The species complex is actively studied, and taxonomic revisions are ongoing as molecular data clarifies relationships within the genus.

## Cultivation

Conophytum bilobum requires specific cultural conditions that differ from most commonly grown succulents.

**Growing requirements:**
- Light: Bright indirect light to morning sun; protect from intense afternoon sun
- Water: Winter-growing; water regularly in autumn/winter, keep dry in summer
- Soil: Very well-draining mineral mix (pumice, perlite, coarse sand)
- Temperature: Hardy to approximately −2 °C brief; prefers cool winter temperatures
- Propagation: Seeds (primary method); division of multi-headed clumps

The most common cultivation error is watering during the summer dormant period. This [[query-what-causes-cannabis-seedlings-to-die-and-how-to-save-them]] the plant to burst or rot, as the old sheath must dry completely before new growth begins. Resume watering only when the new body pair has fully emerged from the fissure in autumn.

Under ideal conditions, Conophytum bilobum can form large clumps of 20–50+ heads over many years. These mature specimens are highly prized by collectors and can reach 10–15 cm across. Each body pair produces one flower annually, so large clumps create a spectacular autumn display.
