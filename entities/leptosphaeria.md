## Leptosphaeria

## Description

*Leptosphaeria* is a genus of ascomycete fungi that includes several significant plant pathogens. In the context of turf management, *Leptosphaeria* species are associated with necrotic ring spot, a destructive disease of turf grasses, particularly Kentucky bluegrass. Bacterially dominated [[compost]] teas have been shown to help suppress this pathogen.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** [[ascomycota]]
- **Class:** Dothideomycetes
- **Order:** Pleosporales
- **Family:** Leptosphaeriaceae
- **Genus:** *Leptosphaeria*
- **Notable species:** *L. korrae* (necrotic ring spot of turf), *L. maculans* (blackleg of canola/rapeseed)

## Key Facts

- *L. korrae* causes necrotic ring spot, a root and crown disease of turf grasses.
- Necrotic ring spot produces characteristic circular or arc-shaped patches of dead grass with green centers (frog-eye pattern).
- Most damaging to Kentucky bluegrass (*[[poa-pratensis]]*) lawns and sports turf.
- Disease develops during warm summer months but infection begins in spring.
- Favored by excess thatch, compacted soils, and drought stress.
- Can be suppressed by bacterially dominated compost teas applied as soil drenches.
- *L. maculans* is one of the most important pathogens of canola/rapeseed worldwide (blackleg disease).
- The genus includes both plant pathogens and saprophytic species involved in organic matter [[decomposition]].

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

*Leptosphaeria* demonstrates the specificity of compost tea approaches — while fungally dominated teas suppress some turf pathogens, bacterially dominated teas are more effective against *Leptosphaeria*-caused necrotic ring spot. This reflects the different ecological niches occupied by different pathogens and underscores the importance of matching tea biology to the specific disease challenge.

The genus is also of major agricultural importance beyond turf: *L. maculans* causes blackleg disease in brassica crops and is one of the most studied plant-pathogenic fungi, serving as a model system for understanding gene-for-gene interactions in plant immunity.

For cultivators, understanding *Leptosphaeria* highlights the need for targeted biological approaches rather than one-size-fits-all compost tea applications. [[soil-testing]] and pathogen identification should guide the choice of bacterial vs. fungal dominance in tea formulations.
