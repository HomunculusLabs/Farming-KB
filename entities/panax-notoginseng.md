# Panax notoginseng
## Overview
- **Common names**: Tienchi ginseng, San Qi, Sanchi, Notoginseng, Tianqi
- **Family/Genus**: Araliaceae / *Panax*
- **Primary medicinal part**: Root (tuber)
- **Region of origin**: Yunnan and Guangxi provinces, China
- **Key traditional action**: Invigorates blood, resolves stasis, stops bleeding
- **Major commercial product**: Yunnan Baiyao (primary ingredient)
- Also known as *Panax pseudoginseng* (synonym); closely related to *P. ginseng* and *P. quinquefolius*.
- "San Qi" (三七) refers to the plant's characteristic three compound leaflets per leaf.
- One of the most commercially valuable medicinal plants in Chinese pharmacopeia.
- Distinguished from other *Panax* species by higher total saponin content and unique notoginsenoside profile.
- Total saponin content in dried root ranges from 6–10%, significantly higher than *P. ginseng* (2–4%).
## Taxonomy & Classification
- **Kingdom**: Plantae → **Order**: Apiales → **Family**: Araliaceae → **Genus**: *Panax* → **Species**: *P. notoginseng*
- **Authority**: (Burkill) F.H.Chen (1948)
- **Basionym**: *Aralia pseudoginseng* Burkill (1902)
- Genus *Panax* contains ~12 species of slow-growing perennials with fleshy roots.
- Phylogenetically closest to *P. vietnamensis* and *P. stipuleanatus*.
- The name *Panax* derives from Greek "pan" (all) + "akos" (cure), reflecting its reputation as a panacea.
## Chemical Constituents
### Major Saponins (Dammarane-type Triterpenoids)
- **Ginsenoside Rg1** (protopanaxatriol): Cardioprotective, neuroprotective
- **Ginsenoside Rb1** (protopanaxadiol): Anti-inflammatory, antioxidant
- **Ginsenoside Rd** (protopanaxadiol): Neuroprotective, anti-cancer research
- **Ginsenoside Re** (protopanaxatriol): Antioxidant, vasodilatory
- **Ginsenoside Rg3** (rare): Anti-angiogenic; formed during steaming
- **Ginsenoside Rh1/Rh2** (rare): Immunomodulatory / anti-tumor research
- **Notoginsenoside R1** (unique marker): Distinctive to *P. notoginseng*; vasoprotective
- **Notoginsenoside R2–R6** (unique): Minor constituents
### Other Constituents
- **Polysaccharides**: Immunomodulatory; water-soluble fraction with significant bioactivity
- **Flavonoids**: Quercetin, kaempferol derivatives — antioxidant activity
- **Volatile oils**: Nerolidol, linalool, and other sesquiterpenes
- **Amino acids**: Rich in GABA and arginine
- **[[teaming-with-nutrients-micronutrients-and-trace-elements]]**: Germanium, selenium, zinc, iron
- **Dencichine** (β-N-oxalyl-L-α,β-diaminopropionic acid): Hemostatic amino acid; promotes platelet aggregation
### Processing Effects
- **Raw (sheng) San Qi**: Stronger hemostatic and blood-stasis-resolving action
- **Steamed (shu) San Qi**: Ginsenosides partially convert; enhanced tonifying/nourishing properties
- Steaming promotes conversion of Rb1/Rg1 to rare ginsenosides (Rg3, Rh1, Rh2), shifting pharmacological activity.
## Traditional & Medicinal Uses
### TCM Properties
- **Nature**: Warm | **Taste**: Sweet, slightly bitter | **Meridians**: Liver, Heart, Stomach
- **Functions**: Invigorate blood, resolve stasis, stop bleeding, reduce swelling, relieve pain
- **Contraindications**: Pregnancy; caution with anticoagulant therapy
### Key Indications
- Traumatic injury, bruises, and swelling
- Internal and external bleeding (hemoptysis, hematuria, metrorrhagia)
- Coronary [[blesching-cannabis-cardiovascular-heart-disease-evidence-review]] and angina pectoris
- Cerebrovascular disease and ischemic [[cannabis-for-stroke-recovery]]
- Hyperlipidemia and atherosclerosis
### Key Formulations
- **Yunnan Baiyao**: Proprietary hemostatic powder; *P. notoginseng* is primary active ingredient
- **Xuesaitong Injection**: Standardized extract for cardiovascular/cerebrovascular conditions (approved in China)
- **Fufang Danshen Diwan**: Combination with *[[cylindrocarpon-destructans]]*
- Wenshan Prefecture, Yunnan — [[lichens-primary-production-soil-formation-dighton]] region (~90% of global supply)
- Harvest in autumn (September–November); post-harvest: cleaning, drying, grading
### Ecological Status
- Wild populations rare and declining due to overharvesting and habitat loss
- Listed as endangered in some regional assessments; commercial cultivation reduces wild harvest pressure
- Requires specific [[plants-without-mycorrhizal-associations]]; [[root-exudates]] exhibit allelopathic effects, contributing to soil sickness in monoculture.
