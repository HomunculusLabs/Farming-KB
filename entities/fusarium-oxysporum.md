# Fusarium oxysporum

**Type:** Species (Fungus)
**Category:** Plant Pathogenic Ascomycete
**Also known as:** Fusarium wilt pathogen

## Overview
Fusarium oxysporum is a widespread soil-borne [[cervantes-fusarium-vascular-wilt-diseases]] diseases in over 100 plant species. The species is divided
into numerous formae speciales (f. sp.), each specialized to infect a
particular host plant. It is one of the most economically damaging plant
pathogens worldwide, affecting crops from banana to tomato to cotton.

## Taxonomy and Classification
- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Sordariomycetes
- **Order:** Hypocreales
- **Family:** Nectriaceae
- **Genus:** Fusarium
- **Species:** F. oxysporum

Note: The F. oxysporum species complex includes both pathogenic and
non-pathogenic strains. Formae speciales are defined by host
specificity and include f. sp. cubense (banana), f. sp. lycopersici
(tomato), f. sp. vasinfectum (cotton), and many others.

## Key Details

### Disease Mechanism
- Infects through roots, entering the vascular system (xylem)
- Colonizes water-conducting vessels, blocking water transport
- Causes wilting, yellowing, and eventual death of the plant
- Produces three types of asexual spores: macroconidia,
  microconidia, and chlamydospores
- Chlamydospores persist in soil for years without a host

### Formae Speciales (Host-Specific Groups)
- f. sp. cubense: Panama disease of banana (Tropical Race 4 is
  currently devastating global banana production)
- f. sp. lycopersici: Fusarium wilt of tomato
- f. sp. vasinfectum: Fusarium wilt of cotton
- f. sp. melonis: Fusarium wilt of melon
- f. sp. niveum: Fusarium wilt of watermelon
- f. sp. basilici: Fusarium wilt of basil
- Over 120 formae speciales have been described

### Cell Biology
- Woronin [[fungal-woronin-bodies-and-septal-pore-organization]] plug septal pores to maintain cellular integrity
  after damage
- HEX-1 homologs are conserved across Fusarium species

### Genetic Diversity
- Capable of both clonal and recombining reproduction
  (O'Donnell et al., 1998)
- Mating populations have been used to study biological species
  recognition
- Phylogenetic analysis has revealed nine phylogenetically distinct
  species within the Fusarium grisea complex

### Interactions with Mycorrhizae
- [[mycorrhizal-inoculation]] and biological diversity is
a key approach to managing Fusarium wilt without chemical inputs.
