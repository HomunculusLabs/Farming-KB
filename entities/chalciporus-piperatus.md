## Chalciporus piperatus

## Overview

*Chalciporus piperatus*, the peppery bolete, is a small to medium-sized pored
mushroom in the family Boletaceae. It is one of the most commonly encountered
boletes in coniferous forests across the Northern Hemisphere. The species is
readily identified by its dull cinnamon-brown cap, reddish pores that stain
blue when bruised, and a conspicuously peppery taste. Unlike many boletes,
it is a mycoparasite or weak ectomycorrhizal associate rather than a strongly
mycorrhizal species, often found fruiting near or on the decaying remains of
other boletes.

## Taxonomy and Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi → **Division:** Basidiomycota → **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Boletales → **Family:** Boletaceae → **Genus:** *Chalciporus*
- **Species:** *C. piperatus* (Bull.) Bataille (1908)

**Basionym:** *Boletus piperatus* Bull. (1790)

The genus *Chalciporus* was formerly placed in *Boletus* but was segregated
based on its unique combination of features: reddish pore surface, blue
staining, peppery taste, and the presence of bundle-forming hyphae rather
than the typical boletoid monomitic hyphal system. Molecular phylogenetics
places *Chalciporus* within the *Suillineae* subfamily, closely related to
*Suillus* and *Rubroboletus*, reflecting its evolutionary history as an
early-diverging bolet lineage.

## Morphology

**Cap:** 2–8 cm broad, convex becoming broadly convex to nearly flat. Surface
is dry, finely velvety to smooth, often appearing slightly wrinkled or
rivulose at maturity. Color is cinnamon-brown to reddish-brown or
orange-brown, sometimes paler at the margin. The cap cuticle does not peel
easily.

**Pore surface:** Tubes are adnate to slightly decurrent. Pores are angular,
1–2 per mm, initially bright red to reddish-orange, darkening with age.
The pore surface and tubes stain distinctly blue when cut or bruised — a
key diagnostic character.
