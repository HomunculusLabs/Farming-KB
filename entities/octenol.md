## Description

Octenol (1-octen-3-ol) is a volatile organic compound produced by mushrooms and mycelium that serves as a powerful attractant for mosquitoes and other insects. It mimics the chemical composition of human breath and sweat, making it an effective lure in mosquito trapping systems. Octenol is commercially used in mosquito control products and is notably the active ingredient in "octenol lures" found in many commercial mosquito killers. [[tradd-cotter]] discovered its natural production by oyster mushrooms and leveraged this property to create biological mosquito traps integrated with mushroom rescue modules.

## Classification

- **Category**: Chemical compound / Volatile organic compound (VOC)
- **Chemical name**: 1-octen-3-ol
- **Also known as**: Mushroom alcohol, octenol
- **Molecular formula**: C₈H₁₆O
- **Natural source**: Produced by fungi, particularly oyster mushrooms and other fruiting bodies

## Key Facts

- Produced naturally by mushrooms as a metabolic byproduct alongside heat, carbon dioxide, and water
- Chemically mimics compounds found in human breath and sweat, making it irresistible to mosquitoes
- Commercially available mosquito killers use synthetic octenol lures as their primary attractant
- Many commercial octenol products are actually mushroom extracts rather than fully synthetic compounds
- Oyster mushrooms produce octenol during fruiting, making mushroom cultivation operations natural mosquito attractants
- Mosquitoes feed voraciously on fungi, likely because they mistake mushrooms for animals (both produce heat, CO₂, and water)
- The compound is also responsible for the characteristic "mushroom smell" of fresh fungi

## Applications in Cultivation

### Mosquito Trapping
- Mycelium chunks placed in 5-gallon buckets with side holes produce CO₂ and octenol
- Mosquitoes are drawn to the chemical plume and enter the bucket
- Females lay eggs in Bti-laced water at the bottom of the trap
- Larvae ingest *Bacillus thuringiensis* var. *israelensis* and die
- Creates a self-sustaining mosquito population reduction system powered by mushroom biology

### Alternative Trap Method
- Diluted apple cider vinegar + liquid soap (1 tsp soap per cup vinegar)
- Vinegar scent attracts insects; soap breaks [[bloomfield-buller-drop-surface-tension-spore-catapult-basidiospore-discharge]], drowning them
- Can be used when Bti is unavailable

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Octenol bridges the fields of mycology and public health by connecting mushroom biology with vector-borne disease prevention. For mushroom cultivators, understanding octenol production is important for two reasons: first, it explains why mushroom operations attract mosquitoes and fungus gnats, requiring [[forest-management-strategies]]; second, it provides a biological tool for mosquito control that can be integrated into cultivation systems, particularly in tropical regions where malaria, dengue fever, and yellow fever are endemic. Cotter's insight that oyster mushrooms naturally produce this commercially valuable attractant transforms a cultivation "pest problem" into a public health intervention.

## Environmental Significance

As a naturally occurring compound, octenol represents an environmentally benign alternative to synthetic chemical insect attractants. The use of mushroom-derived octenol in [[integrated-pest-management]] aligns with [[phosphate-accumulators-sustainable-agriculture]] principles and avoids the environmental contamination associated with chemical pesticides.
