# Nicolas Léonard Sadi Carnot

## Overview
- Nicolas Léonard Sadi Carnot was a French engineer and physicist.
- He is best known for the 1824 book on the motive power of fire.
- That work introduced the ideal heat-engine reasoning behind the [[carnot-cycle-and-heat-engines]] concept page depends directly on this entity.

## Later interpretation
- Émile Clapeyron revived Carnot's work in 1834.
- Clapeyron represented the cycle graphically on pressure-volume diagrams.
- William Thomson, later Lord Kelvin, studied Carnot's analysis.
- Kelvin used Carnot reasoning to help define an absolute temperature scale.
- Rudolf Clausius used Carnot reasoning while formulating entropy and the second law.
- The later thermodynamic tradition preserved Carnot's ideal reversible engine.
- It discarded caloric as substance but retained heat-engine reversibility as a limiting principle.

## Private notes
- Carnot's private notes were discovered after his death.
- Those notes suggest he had begun to move beyond strict caloric theory.
- He anticipated aspects of heat-work equivalence before Joule's famous experiments.
- The notes strengthen the impression that his thinking was more advanced than his publication allowed.
- They also show the incomplete and transitional state of early nineteenth-century heat theory.
- Carnot stood between old caloric mechanics and modern energy physics.

## Death
- Carnot died in Paris on 24 August 1832.
- He was only thirty-six years old.
- His death occurred during a cholera epidemic.
- Because of disease concerns, some of his papers and possessions were destroyed.
- The loss likely removed material that could have clarified his scientific development.
- He died in relative obscurity and did not see the later influence of his work.

## Legacy
- Carnot supplied the theoretical upper bound for all heat engines.
- His work helped turn practical engine improvement into thermodynamic science.
- The Carnot limit remains central in power generation, refrigeration, and heat-pump analysis.
- His name anchors Carnot efficiency, Carnot theorem, and the Carnot cycle.
- The importance of his work increased through Clapeyron, Kelvin, and Clausius.
- He remains a central person entity for any wiki treatment of thermodynamics.
