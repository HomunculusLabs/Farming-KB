## Description

The Beaufort Scale is an empirical measure for estimating wind speed based on observed conditions at sea or on land. Developed by Sir Francis Beaufort in 1805, it remains the standard method for reporting wind intensity in meteorological and agricultural contexts.

## Classification

- **Category:** Meteorological measurement scale
- **Domain:** Atmospheric science, agriculture, [[permaculture-design]]
- **Range:** 0 (calm) to 12+ (hurricane force)
- **Units:** Knots, km/h, MPH equivalents

## Key Facts

### Scale Ranges and Effects on Crops

The scale correlates wind speed with observable effects critical for [[permaculture]] design:

- **Beaufort 0-2 (calm to light airs):** Negligible wind. No damage to crops or structures. Smoke rises vertically.
- **Beaufort 3-4 (gentle to moderate breeze):** Useful wind energy. Some damage to very susceptible species. Leaves and small twigs in constant motion.
- **Beaufort 5-6 (fresh to strong breeze):** Mechanical damage to crops begins. Some structural damage. Crop production reduced; animals lose weight.
- **Beaufort 7-12 (near gale to hurricane):** Severe structural and crop damage. Trees uprooted. Windmill damage.

### Critical Thresholds for Agriculture

- **8 km/h:** Winds are harmless to crops.
- **24 km/h:** Crop production measurably reduced; animal weight loss begins.
- **32-40 km/h:** Sheer mechanical damage to plants exceeds all other effects. Plants may uproot entirely.

### Wind Penetration in Forests

Forests dramatically reduce wind velocity. The degree of wind reduction is measured by penetration distance into the stand, with velocity decreasing as a function of depth into the canopy.

## Relevance to Cultivation

Understanding the Beaufort Scale is essential for site design in permaculture:

1. **Site assessment:** Observing wind-flagging on existing trees reveals prevailing wind directions and intensities over time.
2. **Windbreak design:** Knowing typical wind speeds determines the height, density, and number of rows needed for effective shelter.
3. **Crop protection:** Wind-sensitive crops require shelter when local conditions regularly exceed Beaufort 3-4.
4. **Structural planning:** Buildings and infrastructure must be designed to withstand the maximum expected Beaufort number for the region.

In permaculture design, Mollison emphasizes that wind is the factor over which we have the least control in terms of storage or generation, but the most control in terms of on-site behavior modification through windbreaks, earth banks, and strategic planting.
