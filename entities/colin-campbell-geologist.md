## Colin Campbell (Petroleum Geologist)

## Description

Colin J. Campbell (1931–2022) was a British petroleum geologist who became one of the most authoritative voices on global oil peak (peak oil) theory. His book *The Coming Oil Crisis* (1997) provided a rigorous geological analysis of the finite nature of petroleum resources and the implications of reaching global production maximum. His work directly informed David Holmgren's permaculture framework around energy descent.

## Classification

- **Category**: Person — petroleum geologist, energy analyst
- **Domain**: Geology, energy economics, sustainability
- **Era**: 20th–21st century

## Key Facts

- Born 1931 in Berlin, Germany; died 2022
- Worked as a petroleum geologist for major oil companies including Shell, BP, and Amoco
- Served as vice-president of Fina and explored for oil on every continent
- Authored *The Coming Oil Crisis* (Multi-Science Publishing, 1997), considered one of the most authoritative books on the global energy peak phenomenon
- Co-founded the Association for the Study of Peak Oil and Gas (ASPO) in 2000
- Drew on the work of M. King Hubbert, who correctly predicted the 1970 US oil production peak
- Argued that conventional oil production would peak globally around 2010, with significant consequences for industrial civilization
- His analysis emphasized that the largest oil fields were discovered decades ago and that discovery rates had been declining since the 1960s

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

- **Energy descent foundation**: Campbell's peak oil analysis provides the geological and economic basis for Holmgren's energy descent scenario, which underpins the entire permaculture design philosophy
- **Agricultural vulnerability**: Modern industrial agriculture is deeply dependent on fossil fuels for fertilizers, pesticides, machinery, and transport; Campbell's work implies these systems are inherently unsustainable
- **Design for constraint**: Understanding that energy will become scarcer and more expensive gives urgency to permaculture's emphasis on low-energy, biologically intensive food production systems
- **Community resilience**: The energy descent future Campbell described validates permaculture's focus on local food systems, energy capture, and community self-reliance
