# T-22 (Trichoderma harzianum strain)

## Description

T-22 is a proprietary strain of *[[trichoderma-harzianum]]* (Rifai) developed through protoplast fusion by researchers at Cornell University. It is one of the most commercially successful and widely studied biocontrol agents in agriculture. Marketed under various brand names including T-22 Planter Box and RootShield, this strain provides broad-spectrum disease suppression while simultaneously promoting plant growth.

## Classification

- **Domain**: Eukarya
- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Phylum**: Ascomycota
- **Class**: Sordariomycetes
- **Order**: Hypocreales
- **Family**: Hypocreaceae
- **Genus**: *Trichoderma*
- **Species**: *T. harzianum*
- **Strain**: T-22

## Key Facts

- T-22 was developed by Gary Harman and colleagues at Cornell University through inter-strain protoplast fusion, combining the best traits of two parent strains for enhanced biocontrol efficacy.
- The strain colonizes plant roots aggressively and forms a physical barrier against soil-borne pathogens, establishing a symbiotic relationship with the host plant.
- It exhibits mycoparasitism — directly attacking and lysing pathogenic fungi such as *Rhizoctonia*, *Fusarium*, *Pythium*, and *Thielaviopsis* through the production of cell wall-degrading enzymes.
- T-22 produces volatile and non-volatile antibiotics that suppress pathogen growth in the rhizosphere.
- The strain induces systemic resistance in plants, priming their natural defense mechanisms against foliar and root pathogens.
- Unlike many *Trichoderma* isolates, T-22 is effective across a wide temperature range (50–95°F / 10–35°C) and tolerates a broad pH range, making it suitable for diverse agricultural conditions.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

### Biocontrol Applications

- Widely used as a seed treatment, soil drench, and foliar spray in commercial agriculture, horticulture, and turf management.
- In compost tea (AACT) formulations, *Trichoderma harzianum* T-22 can be added as an inoculant to enhance fungal diversity and disease suppression capacity.
- Effective against damping-off diseases caused by *Pythium* and *Rhizoctonia*, which are common problems in [[mushroom-cultivation]] substrate preparation.

### Integration with Compost Tea

- Elaine Ingham's field guide discusses T-22 in the context of beneficial fungal inoculants for AACT.
- The strain can be preactivated before addition to compost tea by soaking in a nutrient solution for 12–24 hours to stimulate spore germination.
- Combining T-22 with humic acid and kelp-based nutrients in AACT formulations enhances both fungal growth and disease suppression efficacy.

### Mushroom Cultivation Relevance

- *Trichoderma* species are generally considered competitor molds (green mold) in mushroom cultivation, but understanding their biology is critical for contamination management.
- T-22 specifically is less aggressive as a competitor compared to species like *T. aggressivum* (formerly *T. harzianum* Th2/Th4), which devastates button mushroom crops.
- Knowledge of *Trichoderma* ecology helps cultivators design substrate treatment protocols that suppress unwanted *Trichoderma* while maintaining beneficial microbial communities.

### Biomass Increase

- Studies documented in Ingham's guide show T-22 can increase total fungal biomass in the rhizosphere by 30–100% compared to untreated controls.
- The strain persists in soil for entire growing seasons when organic matter is adequate, providing season-long protection.

### Application Rates and Methods

- As a soil drench: typical application rates of 1–5 lbs per acre depending on crop and target pathogen.
- In AACT: can be added as a supplemental inoculant at rates determined by compost tea volume and desired fungal density.
- Seed treatment: applied as a coating at 2–8 oz per 100 lbs of seed for many row crops.
