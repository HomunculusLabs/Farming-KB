## Taxonomy

Sapindaceae → **Hippocastanoideae → *Aesculus* → *A. hippocastanum***

Described by Linnaeus in 1753. The genus *Aesculus* contains 13–19 species
distributed across [[holzer-sweet-chestnut-and-nut-trees]] (*Castanea
sativa*) and a historical (erroneous) belief that the seeds cured coughs
in horses, or alternatively from the horseshoe-shaped scar on the hilum.

## Morphology

- **Habit:** Large deciduous tree, 20–35 m tall, 15–25 m spread; crown
  broadly ovate to rounded, dense.
- **Bark:** Dark grey-brown, developing scaly plates with age; young
  twigs brown, with prominent lenticels and opposite, thick bud scales
  that are very sticky (resinous).
- **Leaves:** Opposite, palmately compound with 5–7 leaflets (rarely 9);
  each leaflet 10–25 cm long, ovate-oblong, serrate, dark green above,
  paler below; petiole 10–20 cm. Foliage turns yellow-brown in autumn.
- **Flowers:** Showy, in erect terminal panicles ("candles") 15–30 cm tall;
  individual flowers 1–2 cm, white with yellow to red blotch; strongly
  scented; bloom May–June.
- **Fruit:** Globular capsule (3–5 cm), green and spiny, dehiscent at
  maturity; typically contains 1–3 glossy brown seeds ("conkers"),
  2–4 cm diameter, with a large pale hilum scar.
- **Root system:** Deep taproot when young, developing a broad, dense
  lateral root network with age; moderately sensitive to soil compaction.
