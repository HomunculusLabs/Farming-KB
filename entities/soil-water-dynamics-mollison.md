## Description

Soil water is a complex solution of dissolved gases, salts, minerals, organic gels, and wash-off from tree throughfall (waxes, frass, tree body wastes). The water-holding capacity of soil varies enormously based on composition and structure, from 2% in surface sands to over 40% of soil volume in clay-rich or organic soils. Managing soil water availability is fundamental to all cultivation.

## Classification

- **Category**: Soil hydrology / soil physics
- **Key parameters**: Water-holding capacity, molecular bonding, osmotic pressure
- **Range**: 2% to over 40% of soil volume

## Key Facts

### Water-Holding Capacity by Soil Type

- Sands absorb and retain water more quickly than clays
- Clays hold more water per unit volume than sands
- Available water ranges from 2% (surface sands) to over 40% of soil volume

### Methods to Increase Water Retention

- Swaling (shallow contour ditches)
- Contouring or terracing
- Loosening compacted soils
- Adding flocculants (gypsum)
- Introducing artificial or natural gels (seaweeds or polymer absorbers)
- Placing clay or plastic sheet layer 30 cm below garden surface
- Maintaining soil organic matter and [[soil-moisture-management-and-root-growth]]
