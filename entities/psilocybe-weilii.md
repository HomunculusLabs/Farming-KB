# Psilocybe weilii

**Psilocybe weilii** (nom. prov.) is a moderately potent psilocybin mushroom first reported from northern Georgia in the southeastern United States. It is a bluing species in the family Hymenogastraceae found fruiting in association with loblolly pine and sweetgum in red-clay soils. The species remains provisionally named and has not been formally validly published under the rules of fungal nomenclature.

The species is of interest to mycologists both for its psychoactive properties and for its ecological role in the piedmont forest ecosystem, where it contributes to the decomposition of organic matter and nutrient cycling within the pine-sweetgum association. Its restricted known range and specific habitat requirements make it one of the more geographically limited psilocybin mushrooms documented in North America. Its distinctive combination of habitat preference, microscopic characters, and geographic range set it apart from other members of the genus.

## Description

### Cap (Pileus)

The cap measures 2 to 6 cm broad. When young it is campanulate to bluntly conic with an inrolled margin, soon expanding to broadly convex to nearly plane, becoming uplifted in age. The margin is often irregular and translucent-striate near the edge, which can split with age and become tough and opaque upon drying.

Coloration ranges from dark chestnut brown to deep olivaceous brown, typically with a distinctive blackish brown zone around the disc. The flesh is 3 to 4 mm thick at the center. The cap surface is viscid when moist, owing to a separable gelatinous pellicle that is a characteristic feature of the section *Psilocybe*. It is strongly hygrophanous, fading in drying to pallid brown or light brown. The flesh is whitish and bruises a conspicuous bluish color where damaged, an indicator of the presence of psilocybin and related compounds.

### Gills (Lamellae)

Gill attachment is adnate to sinuate, with two tiers of intermediate gills (lamellulae). They are close, even, and broad. When young they are light brown overall with pallid, floccose edges. At maturity the gills become dark chocolate brown as spores mature and deposit their pigment. The two-tiered lamellulae arrangement is a notable feature that aids in identification.

### Stem (Stipe)

The stem measures 25 to 70 mm long by 4 to 8 mm thick. It is equal or slightly swelling towards the base, which projects white rhizomorphs into the surrounding substrate. The stem surface is white, becoming dingy brown with age, and bruises bluish overall in age or from drying. It is covered with a well-developed sheath of whitish fibrillose patches below the partial veil zone and is pruinose (frosted with fine particles) above. The stem is cartilaginous and strigose, hollow and stuffed with a whitish pith.

### Partial Veil

The partial veil is cortinate in nature, forming a spider-web-like connection between the cap margin and the stem in young specimens. As the cap expands, the veil ruptures and leaves behind a fibrillose annular zone on the stem that is sometimes dusted with purplish violet brown spore deposits. This annular zone can serve as an aid in field identification.

## Similar Species

Several *Psilocybe* species may be confused with *P. weilii* due to overlapping morphological features and habitat preferences. Careful attention to macroscopic and microscopic characters is essential for accurate identification. Field foragers in the southeastern United States should exercise particular caution, as several toxic species of the genera Galerina and Inocybe may share similar forest-floor habitats and could be confused with small, brownish Psilocybe species.

- [[psilocybe-cyanescens]]: A more robust species with a distinctly wavy cap margin at maturity, typically associated with woody debris in landscaped areas rather than pine needle litter over clay.

The combination of its northern Georgia distribution, association with loblolly pine needles over red-clay soil beneath sweetgum, and its specific microscopic features together distinguish *P. weilii* from its close relatives. Careful microscopic examination is recommended for definitive identification.
