## Description

**Dioryctria albovitella** is a species of moth in the family Pyralidae whose larvae feed on pinyon pine ([[pinus-edulis]]). It is notable for its indirect effects on mycorrhizal fungal communities through its herbivory on host trees, which reduces photosynthate allocation to roots and subsequently decreases ectomycorrhizal colonization.

## Classification

## Key Facts

- Larvae damage the photosynthetic apparatus of pinyon pine trees
- Gehring and Whitham (1991, 1994) demonstrated that larval grazing on susceptible trees significantly reduced the number of ectomycorrhizal root tips compared to herbivore-resistant trees
- When herbivore grazing pressure was artificially removed, mycorrhizal status of susceptible trees recovered to match resistant trees
- Effects are more pronounced in stressed environments (oligotrophic cinder soil) than in nutrient-rich soils (sandy loam)
- Pinyon pine trees show both susceptible and resistant phenotypes to moth attack

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

This moth species demonstrates the important concept of above-belowground linkages in ecosystem ecology. Herbivory on above-ground plant parts can cascade to affect mycorrhizal associations underground. For mycologists and cultivators, this illustrates how insect pest management can indirectly affect fungal symbionts. The differential impact on [[mycorrhizae]] depending on soil nutrient status suggests that fertilization practices could mitigate herbivore-induced mycorrhizal decline in cultivation systems.

## Ecological Role

D. albovitella acts as a driver of plant-fungal community dynamics in pinyon-juniper woodlands of North America. Its selective herbivory creates heterogeneity in mycorrhizal colonization across the landscape, influencing nutrient cycling and plant community structure. The moth's impact demonstrates how canopy herbivores can serve as indirect regulators of soil fungal communities.
