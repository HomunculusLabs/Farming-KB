## Quanat (Qanat / Kariz)

## Description

Quanats (also spelled qanat, kariz, or falaj) are ancient underground water galleries that tap into aquifers and deliver water to the surface by gravity flow. They have been used for centuries across the Middle East, North Africa, and Central Asia, extending for thousands of kilometers across many desert regions. Mollison describes them in *[[mushroom-fruiting-chambers]] in arid climates.
- **Aquifer awareness**: Quanats remind us that water extraction must be balanced with aquifer recharge. Mollison emphasizes that deforestation and overgrazing reduce aquifer intake effectiveness, ultimately drying up quanats.
- **Modern adaptation**: The principle of gravity-fed underground water delivery can be adapted using modern PVC pipes and filtration systems for small-scale fungal cultivation water supply in arid regions.

## Construction Principles

Traditional quanat construction follows these steps:
1. Identify a potential aquifer at the base of alluvial fans or in fold sediments
2. Sink a mother well to confirm water table depth and yield
3. Plan a gently sloping tunnel from the discharge point to the mother well
4. Sink vertical shafts at 20–30 m intervals along the planned route
5. Connect shafts with a hand-dug tunnel, maintaining a gradient of 1:1000 to 1:1500
6. The tunnel emerges at the surface at the village or cultivation area
