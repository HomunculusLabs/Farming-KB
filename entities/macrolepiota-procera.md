# Macrolepiota procera

## Overview

**Macrolepiota procera** (Scop.) Singer, the parasol mushroom, is a large agaric found across temperate and subtropical regions worldwide. One of the most prized wild [[palmer-edible-mushrooms-north-america]] in Europe, it is recognized by its tall stature, prominent movable ring, and large scaly cap that expands from egg-shaped to broadly parasol-shaped. The flesh has a nutty, mild flavor well suited to sautéing, and caps are commonly prepared like Wiener schnitzel.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Agaricaceae
- **Genus:** Macrolepiota
- **Species:** M. procera

**Basionym:** Agaricus procerus Scop. (1772)
**Authority:** (Scop.) Singer (1948)

Synonyms include Agaricus procerus Scop. and Lepiota procera (Scop.) P. Kumm.
Singer segregated Macrolepiota from Lepiota in 1948 based on size and microscopic characters. Molecular phylogenetics confirm M. procera as the type species of Macrolepiota sensu stricto.

## Morphology

**Cap:** 8–30 cm diameter. Egg-shaped when young, expanding to broadly convex with a central umbo.
Surface covered with concentric brown to tawny scales on a cream-white background. Margin strongly striate, extending beyond the gills.

**Gills:** Free, crowded, white to cream, darkening slightly with age. Edge entire.

**Stipe:** 15–35 cm tall, 0.8–2 cm diameter. Cylindrical with a slightly bulbous base. Hollow at maturity. Smooth above the ring, finely scaly below. Color cream to pale brown.

**Ring:** Large, double-movable (slides freely on the stipe). Upper surface white and smooth; lower surface with brownish scales matching the cap. This movable ring is the single most diagnostic field character.

**Spores:** Print white to cream. Spores 14–20 × 9–13 μm, broadly elliptical to amygdaliform, thick-walled with a distinct germ pore, smooth and hyaline in KOH.

**Microscopic:** Cheilocystidia clavate to cylindrical, pleurocystidia absent, clamp connections absent. Pileipellis a cutis with erect elements forming the cap scales.

## Distribution and Habitat

Widespread across Europe (Scandinavia to Mediterranean), temperate Asia (China, Japan, Korea), North Africa, parts of Australia, and [[polypore-fungi-eastern-north-america]] America. Introduced in New Zealand and southern South America.

Prefers open grasslands, meadows, pastures, and woodland edges. Common along hedgerows and forest paths. Favors well-drained, nutrient-rich soils. Solitary to scattered. Fruits from late summer to late autumn (July–November in the Northern Hemisphere).

Considered primarily saprotrophic but may form weak [[spore-print]] is green, not white. Common in subtropical regions.

## Cultivation

Cultivation has been attempted but remains challenging. Key difficulties include the long growth cycle (6–8 weeks), sensitivity to competing organisms, and variable yields.

Composted straw and [[oyster-mushroom-supplemented-sawdust-blocks]] substrates have achieved moderate success. Outdoor beds of composted [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]] show promise in Mediterranean climates.

## Related Species

- **Macrolepiota excoriata** — smaller, more delicate, similar habitat
