## Overview

*Hygrophorus chlorophanus* is a species of agaric fungus in the family
Hygrophoraceae, commonly known as the olive waxy cap or verdant waxy cap.
It is a small to medium-sized mushroom recognized by its viscid olive-green
to yellowish cap and slimy stipe under wet conditions.

The species is mycorrhizal, forming associations with coniferous trees,
particularly spruce (*Picea*) and pine (*Pinus*) in boreal and montane
forests. It is considered inedible due to its unremarkable taste and
rubbery texture, though it is not known to be toxic.

First described by Elias Magnus Fries, the species has undergone several
taxonomic revisions. Modern molecular phylogenetics places it within the
*Hygrophorus* sensu stricto clade, closely related to other conifer-associated
waxy caps.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:**

- Kingdom: Fungi
- Division: Basidiomycota
- Class: Agaricomycetes
- Order: Agaricales
- Family: Hygrophoraceae
- Genus: *Hygrophorus*
- Species: *H. chlorophanus* (Fr.) Fr.

**Synonyms:**

- *Agaricus chlorophanus* Fr. (basionym)
- *Limacium chlorophanum* (Fr.) P. Kumm.
- *Hygrophorus psittacinus* var. *chlorophanus* (Fr.) Maire

**Cap:** 2–6 cm diameter, convex to plano-convex, sometimes with a low
umbo. Surface smooth and viscid when moist, often translucent-striate at
the margin. Color variable: olive-green, yellow-green, or sometimes
pale yellow with greenish tones, fading to buff with age.

**Gills:** Adnexed to subdecurrent, moderately spaced, thick and waxy
in texture. Color white to pale cream, sometimes with a faint greenish
tinge near the cap edge.

**Stipe:** 3–7 cm long, 4–10 mm thick, cylindrical or slightly tapering
upward. Surface viscid to glutinous, especially in wet weather.
White to pale yellow above the ring zone, greenish below. Partial veil
often leaving a fleeting ring or fibrillose zone.

**Flesh:** Thin, white, rubbery and pliant. Odor mild or slightly
farinaceous. Taste not distinctive.

**Spores:** 6.5–9 × 4.5–6 μm, ellipsoid, smooth, inamyloid.
Spore print white.

## Distribution and Habitat

*H. chlorophanus* is found across northern and central Europe, with
scattered reports from North America and Asia. It is more common in
boreal and montane regions than in lowland areas.

The species fruits from late summer through autumn (August to November
in the Northern Hemisphere), typically appearing during wet periods.
It grows on soil under coniferous trees, especially Norway spruce
(*Picea abies*) and Scots pine (*Pinus sylvestris*).

Elevation range is typically 400–1800 m in mountainous regions,
though it can occur at lower altitudes in northern latitudes.
It prefers acidic to slightly acidic soils rich in organic matter.

## Edibility

*H. chlorophanus* is generally regarded as inedible. The flesh is thin,
rubbery, and lacks notable flavor, making it unappealing for culinary
use. No toxic compounds have been identified in the species.

Some older field guides list it as "suspect" or "to be avoided,"
but this caution likely stems from confusion with other green-capped
species rather than confirmed toxicity. The waxy cap genus (*Hygrophorus*)
contains several excellent edibles, and no poisonous members are known.

## Ecological Role

As an ectomycorrhizal species, *H. chlorophanus* forms mutualistic
associations with conifer root systems. The fungal mycelium extends
the effective root surface area, improving water and mineral nutrient
uptake for the host tree. In exchange, the fungus receives
photosynthetically derived carbohydrates.

This relationship is particularly important in nutrient-poor boreal
forest soils, where mycorrhizal networks can connect multiple trees
and facilitate nutrient sharing between individuals. Waxy caps in
general are considered indicator species of relatively undisturbed
forest habitats with established mycorrhizal networks.

## Related Species

- ***Hygrophorus psittacinus*** — Parrot waxy cap; similar size but
  more vividly green, often with pink or purple tones; typically
  associated with broadleaf trees.
- ***Hygrophorus hypothejus*** — Olive-brown waxy cap; larger, with
  a distinctly darker cap and stronger odor; common under pines.
- ***Hygrophorus lucorum*** — Yellow waxy cap; lacks green tones,
  associated with broadleaf trees; more common in lowland forests.
- ***Hygrophorus arbustivus*** — Similar habitat under conifers;
  cap is entirely white to cream with no green pigmentation.
