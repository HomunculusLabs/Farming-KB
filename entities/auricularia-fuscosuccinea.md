## Auricularia fuscosuccinea

## Description

Auricularia fuscosuccinea is a species of wood ear mushroom in the family Auriculariaceae. It is one of three primary Auricularia species commonly cultivated, alongside A. auricula and A. polytricha. Like other wood ears, it produces thin, gelatinous, rubbery fruiting bodies that are prized for their crunchy texture in Asian and tropical cuisines. The species is relatively easy to cultivate both indoors and outdoors, earning a difficulty rating of outdoor 1 / indoor 2.

## Key Facts

- **Cultivation difficulty**: Outdoor 1 (easy), Indoor 2 (intermediate)
- **Fruiting [[query-what-is-the-ideal-temperature-range-for-growing-cannabis]]**: 60 to 80 degrees F (16 to 27 degrees C), temperate to tropical
- **Preferred substrate**: Hardwood logs, stumps, and [[oyster-mushroom-supplemented-sawdust-blocks]]; omnivorous on non-coniferous wood
- **Fruiting body form**: Jelly fungus, cup-shaped or lobed, gelatinous and rubbery
- **[[spore-print]]**: White; spores released from the incurved cup side of the fruitbody
- **Yield (indoor)**: Up to 1 pound per 5 pounds of supplemented [[stamets-supplemented-sawdust-substrate-preparation]]
- **Yield (outdoor)**: Very low; slow to mature compared to fleshy gilled mushrooms
- **Inoculation to fruiting**: 10 to 14 months (outdoor), 50 to 60 days (indoor)
- **Storage**: Fresh for 3 to 4 weeks at 38 to 42 degrees F (3 to 6 degrees C); dries well for long-term storage
- **[[fukuoka-vegetable-disease-insect-resistance]]**: Extremely low insect pressure, making it an excellent candidate for outdoor transfer from indoor incubation

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

### Cultivation Characteristics

A. fuscosuccinea shares the general [[auricularia-cultivation]] profile: it thrives on hardwood substrates in warm conditions and produces multiple flushes indoors (three or more, 2 to 3 weeks apart). The species can be grown on sterilized supplemented sawdust with wheat or rice bran at 5 percent supplementation by dry weight.

### Strain Rotation Strategy

Cotter recommends rotating among Auricularia strains with different temperature fruiting windows throughout the year in [[fukuoka-natural-farming-temperate-climates]]. This maximizes harvest periods and yields without requiring significant energy inputs for heating or cooling fruiting rooms.

### Fruiting Initiation

For indoor cultivation, make 3 to 4 inch slices in the bag — either on opposite sides or as an X pattern. This is especially effective when bags are stacked to create fruiting walls for efficient harvesting.

### Outdoor Cultivation

The mushroom forms on the open face of stumps, squeezing out from under the bark surface. Half-buried rounds are an excellent outdoor cultivation format. Because insect pressure is extremely low, colonized bags can be transferred from indoor incubation to outdoor fruiting structures without significant losses.

### Wild Isolation Technique

When collecting wild specimens for cultivation:
1. Peel back the bark near the mushroom attachment point
2. Insert a small piece of wet cardboard
3. Replace the bark firmly
4. Wait a few days for mycelium to leap onto the cardboard
5. Transfer to agar or make layers of inoculated cardboard

For direct isolation, use a sterilized dental pick to pull mycelium from attachment points, soak in 3 percent [[cervantes-hydrogen-peroxide-sterilization]] for several minutes, then transfer to antibiotic agar.

### Culinary and Market Value

While described as flavorless on its own, the crunchy texture is highly valued in Asian cuisine. The species ships well and can be reconstituted from a dried state in minutes by soaking in warm water. A creative marketing approach involves soaking dried wood ears in flavored broth (teriyaki, etc.), then re-drying to produce crunchy, flavored crackers suitable as trail food.

### Mycoremediation Applications

Like other Auricularia species, A. fuscosuccinea may be best deployed for habitat renewal in devastated ecosystems rich in lignicolous debris, such as logged forests. The genus tolerates more sun exposure than other wood-decomposing mushrooms, making it useful for [[building-soil]] in arid, sunny environments.

## Nutritional Profile

- **Moisture content**: Approximately 90 percent
- **Protein (dry weight)**: 8 to 10 percent
- **Notable properties**: Anticoagulant (blood-thinning) and anti-inflammatory actions
- **Traditional use**: Used in homeopathy to treat pancreatic imbalances
