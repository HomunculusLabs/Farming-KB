The species is now cultivated across the global tropics between approximately 30°N and 30°S latitude.

- **Major producers:** Nigeria (world's largest), Democratic Republic of Congo, Thailand, Indonesia, Brazil, Ghana, Mozambique, Cambodia, Vietnam, Angola.
- **Climate:** Grows best at 25–29 °C with 1,000–1,500 mm annual rainfall; tolerates prolonged drought (3–6 months) due to deep root system and stomatal control.
- **Soils:** Highly adaptable — grows in sandy loams to heavy clays, acidic to neutral pH (4.5–8.0); tolerates low fertility better than most [[query-how-do-i-use-knf-inputs-through-the-plant-growth-cycle]]:** 8–24 months depending on variety and conditions; sweet varieties mature in 8–12 months, bitter varieties in 12–24 months.
- **Yield:** 10–40 tonnes fresh roots per hectare; average world yield ~12 t/ha; improved varieties can exceed 50 t/ha.
- **[[mollison-shade-systems-and-canopy-architecture]].
- **[[cannabis-disease-pest-resistance-breeding-clarke]]:** Generally resistant to most pests; the cassava mealybug (*Phenacoccus manihoti*) and cassava green mite (*Mononychellus tanajoa*) are the most significant pests.
- **Diseases:** Cassava mosaic disease (CMD, caused by begomoviruses), cassava brown streak disease (CBSD), and bacterial blight (*Xanthomonas axonopodis* pv. *manihotis*) are the most damaging.

## Nutritional Profile

Cassava roots are primarily composed of starch and water, with relatively low protein and micronutrient content compared to cereals.
