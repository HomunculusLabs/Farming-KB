## Distribution and Habitat

Calamus manan is native to Peninsular Malaysia, Sumatra, and Borneo. It occurs in lowland and hill dipterocarp forests from sea level to approximately 800 meters elevation.

The species is most abundant in the lowland mixed dipterocarp forests of Peninsular Malaysia and the Riau Archipelago of Sumatra. It is less common in Borneo, where other Calamus species dominate the rattan flora.

Manau rattan requires tall, mature forest canopy for support, as its stems must climb to reach the light conditions needed for optimal growth. It is typically absent from degraded or secondary forests with insufficient canopy height.

The species thrives in well-drained, loamy soils of river valleys and foothills. It prefers areas with consistent rainfall throughout the year, typically 2,000 to 4,000 mm annually.

Optimal growth occurs at ambient temperatures between 25 and 32 °C with high humidity. The species is sensitive to prolonged drought and does not occur in seasonally dry forests.

## Commercial Uses

Manau rattan is the premier cane species for the furniture industry due to its large diameter, exceptional strength, and uniform quality. It is the preferred material for structural furniture frames, particularly in high-end cane furniture manufacturing.

The cane is harvested from natural forests and plantations by cutting stems near the base and stripping the leaf sheaths. Processing involves boiling, drying, and grading the canes by diameter and length.

After processing, manau rattan is used to produce a wide range of products including furniture, walking sticks, umbrella handles, basketry, and wickerwork. The inner core, known as rattan reed, is used for weaving and binding.

The global rattan furniture industry is valued at several billion dollars annually, with manau rattan commanding premium prices. ## Cultivation

Calamus manan has been successfully cultivated in plantations since the 1980s in response to declining wild populations. Malaysia and Indonesia have established the largest commercial rattan plantation estates.

Silvicultural management includes control of competing vegetation, maintenance of support trees, and protection from fire and pest damage. Rattan beetles and stem-boring larvae are the primary pest concerns in plantation settings.
