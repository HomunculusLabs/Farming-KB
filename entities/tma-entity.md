# TMA

**PIHKAL Entry #157** -- 3,4,5-Trimethoxyamphetamine

## Chemical Name

3,4,5-Trimethoxyamphetamine

## Dosage and Duration

- **Dosage:** 100 - 250 mg

- **Duration:** 6 - 8 h

## Qualitative Effects

TMA is a potent, mood-variable psychedelic that was the first totally synthetic phenethylamine found active in man:

- At 135 mg: no nausea (unlike [[mescaline-entity]]). Personality divided and exposed, allowing clearer understanding of psychic structure. Not completely pleasant due to this personal intimacy.

- At 140 mg: not the color changes of [[mescaline-entity]] but good humor and over-appreciation of jokes. Remarkable eyes-closed imagery tied to music. Irritated by other people's conversations. Equated to 300-350 mg [[mescaline-entity]]; would prefer the latter. Out in 8 hours.

- At 225 mg: quite a bit of nausea in first hour. Emotionally volatile -- sometimes gentle and peaceful, sometimes irritable and pugnacious. Connected to music. Bernstein's "Joy of Music" was fully audible. Rachmaninoff's 2nd piano concerto produced total involvement. Commercials were irritating. Easily crushed a rose.

## Synthesis

Synthesized from 3,4,5-trimethoxybenzaldehyde via nitropropene formation with nitroethane (two methods: n-butylamine catalysis at 40 degrees C for 7 days, or ammonium acetate in nitroethane on steam bath), followed by LAH reduction. The crude product contained 10-15% 3,5-dimethoxy-4-hydroxyamphetamine as impurity. Pure material had mp 220-221 degrees C; the phenolic impurity could be separated by base extraction.

## Extensions and Commentary

TMA was the very first totally synthetic psychedelic phenethylamine found active in man, reported in 1955 from Canadian research, six years before Shulgin's own report. The Canadian studies used 50-150 mg with Dramamine for nausea prevention, employing a stroboscope to induce visual phenomena.

Military interest was maintained with the code name EA-1319. A publication emphasizing possible antisocial nature discouraged further medical exploration. The presence of the phenolic impurity (3,5-dimethoxy-4-hydroxyamphetamine) raises an intriguing unanswered question about its contribution to observed effects.

TMA is one of the "Essential Amphetamines" -- compounds corresponding to essential oils that lack only a molecule of ammonia to become amphetamines. TMA corresponds to elemicin. The pattern "if one is good, then two is better" led to exploring higher homologues (AEM, etc.).

## Chemical Family and Historical Significance

TMA holds a unique place in psychedelic history as the first fully synthetic phenethylamine demonstrated to have psychedelic properties in humans. Discovered independently by Canadian researchers in the mid-1950s, TMA preceded Shulgin's more famous syntheses and helped establish the foundation for the systematic exploration that would eventually produce PIHKAL.

The compound is classified as one of the "Essential Amphetamines" -- a concept central to Shulgin's approach to psychedelic chemistry. Essential oils are naturally occurring isoprenoid compounds found in many plants. Shulgin recognized that many of these molecules could be conceptually "completed" by the addition of an amine group to form amphetamine derivatives:

- Elemicin (from nutmeg) plus ammonia yields TMA

- Asarone (from calamus) plus ammonia yields TMA-2

- Myristicin (from parsley, nutmeg) plus ammonia yields MMDA

This connection between naturally occurring essential oils and synthetic amphetamines provided a theoretical framework for understanding why certain plants have psychoactive properties and suggested systematic avenues for exploration.

## Pharmacological Discussion

TMA acts primarily as a 5-HT2A receptor agonist, with the 3,4,5-trimethoxyphenyl group providing the classic pharmacophore for psychedelic amphetamines. The compound's pharmacological profile also includes some dopaminergic activity, consistent with its amphetamine backbone and the stimulant-like qualities reported in some trials.

The question of the phenolic impurity's contribution to TMA's effects remains unresolved. 3,5-Dimethoxy-4-hydroxyamphetamine is a catecholamine-like compound that could have its own pharmacological activity, potentially contributing to the emotional volatility and irritability reported at higher doses. Modern analytical techniques could resolve this question by comparing pure TMA with historically prepared material containing the impurity.

Receptor binding studies have shown that TMA has moderate affinity for 5-HT2A receptors (Ki approximately 300-500 nM), with weaker affinity for 5-HT2C and trace amine-associated receptors. This places it in the moderate potency range for psychedelic amphetamines.

## Comparison to Related Compounds

- Mescaline (3,4,5-trimethoxyphenethylamine): TMA is the amphetamine homologue, roughly 2-3x more potent by weight

- TMA-2 (2,4,5-trimethoxyamphetamine): More potent at 20-40 mg, different substitution pattern

- TMA-6 (2,4,6-trimethoxyamphetamine): Less well characterized

- MDA (3,4-methylenedioxyamphetamine): Replaces one methoxy with methylenedioxy, more empathogenic

- DOM (2,5-dimethoxy-4-methylamphetamine): Much more potent at 3-10 mg

The comparison between TMA and mescaline illustrates the general pattern that amphetamine homologues are more potent than their phenethylamine counterparts, though this is not always the case (seeset-and-settingHA discussion).

## Harm Reduction and Safety Considerations

- The emotional volatility reported at higher doses (225 mg) suggests careful attention to set and setting

- Nausea, while less than with mescaline, can still occur particularly at higher doses

- The military code name EA-1319 and reports of "antisocial" effects should not be overinterpreted but do suggest potential for unpredictable behavior

- TMA is a phenethylamine amphetamine with both serotonergic and adrenergic properties; cardiovascular monitoring is advisable

- The compound's history as a military research subject does not imply any unique danger, but does suggest it has been studied in contexts where safety was not the primary concern

- As with all amphetamines, the risk of anxiety, paranoia, and sleep disruption increases with dose

## Legal Status

TMA is specifically scheduled as a controlled substance in many jurisdictions. In the United States, it is listed as a Schedule I controlled substance under the Controlled Substances Act. It is also controlled under international drug conventions in many countries. Its status as one of the earlier synthetic psychedelics and its association with military research led to early regulatory attention.

## Cultural and Historical Significance

TMA's discovery in 1955 represents a watershed moment in the history of psychedelic chemistry. It demonstrated that synthetic modifications of natural product scaffolds could produce genuinely novel psychedelic compounds, opening the door to the systematic exploration that the Shulgins would pursue over the following decades.

The military interest in TMA (under the code name EA-1319) reflects the broader Cold War-era investigation of psychoactive compounds by intelligence agencies, most famously documented in the CIA's MK-ULTRA program. The compound's classification as a potential "incapacitating agent" or "truth drug" was shared by many psychedelics of the era.

The "Essential Amphetamine" concept that TMA exemplifies has proven to be an enduring and productive framework in psychedelic chemistry, linking ethnobotanical knowledge with synthetic chemistry in a way that continues to inform modern drug discovery efforts.
