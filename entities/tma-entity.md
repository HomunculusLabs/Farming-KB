# TMA

**[[2c-b-pihkal-entry-20]] #157** -- 3,4,5-Trimethoxyamphetamine

## Chemical Name

3,4,5-Trimethoxyamphetamine

## Dosage and Duration

- **Dosage:** 100 - 250 mg

- **Duration:** 6 - 8 h

## Qualitative Effects

TMA is a potent, mood-variable psychedelic that was the first totally synthetic phenethylamine found active in man:

- At 135 mg: no nausea (unlike [[mescaline-entity]] but good humor and over-appreciation of jokes. Remarkable eyes-closed imagery tied to music. Irritated by other people's conversations. Equated to 300-350 mg [[set-and-setting]]

- Nausea, while less than with mescaline, can still occur particularly at higher doses

- The military code name EA-1319 and reports of "antisocial" effects should not be overinterpreted but do suggest potential for unpredictable behavior

- TMA is a [[shulgin-dom-stp-phenethylamine-amphetamine-relationship]] with both serotonergic and adrenergic properties; cardiovascular monitoring is advisable

- The compound's history as a military research subject does not imply any unique danger, but does suggest it has been studied in contexts where safety was not the primary concern

- As with all amphetamines, the risk of anxiety, paranoia, and sleep disruption increases with dose

## Legal Status

TMA is specifically scheduled as a controlled substance in many jurisdictions. In the [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]], it is listed as a Schedule I controlled substance under the Controlled [[controlled-substances-act-and-psychedelic-research]]. It is also controlled under international drug conventions in many countries. Its status as one of the earlier synthetic psychedelics and its association with military research led to early regulatory attention.

## Cultural and Historical Significance

TMA's discovery in 1955 represents a watershed moment in the history of psychedelic chemistry. It demonstrated that synthetic modifications of natural product scaffolds could produce genuinely novel psychedelic compounds, opening the door to the systematic exploration that the Shulgins would pursue over the following decades.

The military interest in TMA (under the code name EA-1319) reflects the broader Cold War-era investigation of psychoactive compounds by intelligence agencies, most famously documented in the CIA's MK-ULTRA program. The compound's classification as a potential "incapacitating agent" or "truth drug" was shared by many psychedelics of the era.

The "Essential Amphetamine" concept that TMA exemplifies has proven to be an enduring and productive framework in psychedelic chemistry, linking ethnobotanical knowledge with synthetic chemistry in a way that continues to inform modern drug discovery efforts.
