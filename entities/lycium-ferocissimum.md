## Description

*Lycium ferocissimum* is an extremely thorny shrub native to southern Africa,
used in permaculture systems as a **living barrier plant** and hedgerow species.
It is one of the primary species recommended by Bill Mollison for constructing
**bomas** (thorn fences) to corral livestock and protect garden compounds from
grazing animals. Its dense, viciously spiny branches create impenetrable barriers
without the need for wire fencing.





## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Family**: Solanaceae (nightshade family)
- **Genus**: *Lycium*
- **Species**: *L. ferocissimum*
- **Common names**: African boxthorn
- **Growth form**: Dense, thorny shrub, 2–5 m tall





## Key Facts

- Native to southern Africa (South Africa, Namibia, Botswana).
- Member of the Solanaceae family, related to tomatoes, peppers, and goji berry
  (*Lycium barbarum*).
- Produces small red or orange berries, though edibility varies by preparation.
- Extremely dense, intertwined branches with long, sharp thorns make it an
  effective **living fence** against livestock and wildlife.
- Listed by Mollison as one of four ideal boma species, alongside *Oncoba
  spinosa*, *Acacia tortilis*, and *Euphorbia tirucalli*.
- May not require supplementary barbed wire, unlike some other barrier species.
- Hardy and drought-tolerant once established.





## Barrier Functions

- **Animal corrals**: restrains cattle, goats, sheep, and wild herbivores.
- **Compound protection**: keeps grazing animals out of food gardens.
- **Living fence**: maintained as permanent hedgerow with minimal pruning.
- **Boma material**: cut branches woven into thorn fences for flexible,
  movable enclosures.





## Relevance to Cultivation / Mycology

- Woody prunings from hedge maintenance can serve as **chip substrate** for
  hardy wood-decomposer mushroom species.
- As a Solanaceae member, root exudates may influence soil microbial community
  composition, including fungal populations.
- Dense hedgerows create **windbreak microclimates** that reduce evaporation
  and maintain humidity — beneficial for adjacent mushroom cultivation areas.
- The root system associates with **mycorrhizal fungi**, contributing to soil
  health in mixed hedgerow plantings.
- Berry production may attract birds whose guano enriches soil with nutrients
  and introduces additional organic matter for fungal decomposition.
