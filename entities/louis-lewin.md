## Louis Lewin

Louis Lewin (1850–1929) was a German pharmacologist, physician, and pioneer of
psychopharmacology. He is widely regarded as the father of modern psychoactive
substance research for his systematic approach to studying mind-altering plants and
chemicals. His 1924 book "Phantastica" remains a landmark in the field, cataloguing
psychoactive substances from [[roger-heim-scientific-study-psilocybin]] of
[[forensic-identification-methods-psilocybin-mushrooms-tlc-gc-hplc]], ayahuasca, cannabis, and various other plant preparations used
in indigenous ceremonial contexts worldwide. His research on peyote was particularly
influential, as he was among the first European scientists to study it seriously.

### Inebriantia (Intoxicating)

Substances that reduce inhibitions and produce disinhibition. This category included
alcohol, ether, and various fermented preparations. Lewin examined the social and
cultural roles of intoxicants across different societies, noting that virtually
every human civilization had developed some form of inebriant.

### Hypnotica (Sleep-Inducing)

Substances that produce drowsiness and sleep. This included various sedative plants
and early pharmaceutical sedatives. Lewin documented both the therapeutic
applications and the dangers of these substances.

### Stimulantia (Stimulating)

Substances that increase alertness and energy. This category included coca and its
alkaloid cocaine, coffee (caffeine), khat, and betel nut. Lewin studied the
indigenous use of coca leaves in South America and documented the transition from
traditional use to pharmaceutical extraction of cocaine.

## Phantastica: Die betäubenden und erregenden Genussmittel (1924)

Lewin"s magnum opus, "Phantastica," was published in Berlin in 1924. The book
provided a comprehensive survey of psychoactive substances from around the world,
organized by his classification system. It covered opium, cannabis, coca, peyote,
psilocybin mushrooms, ayahuasca, kava, betel nut, and many other substances.

"Phantastica" was notable for its objective, scientific approach at a time when most
writing about psychoactive substances was either moralistic prohibition literature or
sensationalist accounts. Lewin treated indigenous use of psychoactive plants with
respect and scientific curiosity, documenting [[ethnomycology-cultural-significance-sacred-fungi]] alongside pharmacological data.

The book was translated into English in 1998 by Michael Ripinski-Naxon and has since
become a foundational text in the field of [[psilocybin-toxicity-and-safety-profile]]
- ethnobotanical history
