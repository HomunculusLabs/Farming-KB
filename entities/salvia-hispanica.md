Native to central and southern Mexico and Guatemala, chia was a staple crop of pre-Columbian Mesoamerican civilizations, valued by the Aztecs and Maya as a food, medicine, and religious offering.

The species experienced a dramatic resurgence in global popularity after the early 2000s superfood movement, becoming one of the most commercially significant functional food ingredients worldwide.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Division:** Tracheophyta
- **Class:** Magnoliopsida
- **Order:** Lamiales
- **Family:** Lamiaceae
- **Genus:** Salvia
- **Species:** S. hispanica L. (1753)

Salvia hispanica is an erect, branched annual herb, typically 0.5–1.75 m tall at maturity, with a square stem characteristic of the mint family.

Leaves are opposite, ovate to lanceolate, 4–8 cm long and 2–4 cm wide, with serrated margins and a slightly rough texture.

The inflorescence is a terminal spike, 10–20 cm long, bearing whorls of small bilateral flowers with blue to purple corollas, approximately 6–8 mm long.

Seeds are small, oval, 1.5–2 mm long, with a glossy surface. The most common commercial varieties produce either white, grey, or black seeds, with color influenced by flower pigmentation.

Each seed can absorb 10–12 times its weight in water, forming a distinctive mucilaginous gel that is the basis for chia's unique culinary properties.

## Distribution and Habitat

The species is native to the semi-arid highlands of central and southern Mexico (Jalisco to Chiapas) and Guatemala, typically found at elevations of 1000–2500 m.

Chia grows naturally in open, disturbed sites, grasslands, and on rocky hillsides in areas with well-defined wet and dry seasons.

Optimal growing conditions include 500–1500 mm annual rainfall, mean temperatures of 18–25°C, and well-drained soils ranging from sandy loams to clay loams.

The species is drought-tolerant once established but is sensitive to frost and waterlogging. Daylength sensitivity (short-day photoperiod response) limits cultivation to tropical and subtropical latitudes.

## Cultivation

Chia is direct-seeded at 5–8 kg/ha in rows 40–60 cm apart, with germination occurring within 7–14 days under adequate moisture.

The crop requires minimal fertilization due to its adaptation to nutrient-poor soils, though moderate nitrogen application (20–40 kg/ha) can improve yields.
