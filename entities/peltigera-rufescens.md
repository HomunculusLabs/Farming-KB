Apothecia are common, developing along the lobe margins. They are saddle-shaped, reddish-brown, and measure 2 to 8 millimeters in diameter with slightly raised margins.

Spores are hyaline, fusiform to ellipsoid, 3 to 5 septate, measuring 28 to 50 micrometers in length. Pycnidia produce hyaline, bacilliform conidia approximately 5 to 7 micrometers long.

The cephalodia, if present, contain Nostoc and are embedded in the upper thallus surface. These structures are the primary sites of nitrogen fixation.

## Distribution and Habitat

Peltigera rufescens has a circumpolar distribution across the Northern Hemisphere. It occurs throughout Europe, northern Asia, and North America, extending into mountainous regions of temperate zones.

In Europe, it is widespread from Scandinavia to the Mediterranean basin. It is particularly common in the British Isles, where it occurs in coastal heathlands and upland moors.

The species reaches its southern distribution limit in mountainous areas of the Mediterranean region. Isolated populations have been recorded in the Caucasus and the Altai Mountains of Central Asia.

The species prefers open, well-lit habitats with moderate moisture availability. Typical substrates include acidic to neutral soils, moss mats, decaying wood, and occasionally damp rock faces.

It is frequently found in heathlands, moorlands, grasslands, and open woodlands. It also colonizes disturbed sites including roadside banks and abandoned fields.

Peltigera rufescens shows some tolerance for nutrient-enriched conditions. It is occasionally found in areas with moderate nitrogen deposition, though excessive nitrogen reduces thallus vitality.

The species is absent from extremely dry or waterlogged environments. Optimal moisture conditions are provided by high humidity, regular fog, or consistent precipitation throughout the growing season.

In North America, Peltigera rufescens occurs from Alaska and northern Canada south to the Appalachian Mountains. It is also found in the western mountains from British Columbia to California.

## Ecology

As a cyanolichen, Peltigera rufescens fixes atmospheric nitrogen through its Nostoc photobiont. Nitrogen fixation rates can reach 5 to 15 kilograms per hectare per year in favorable conditions.

This nitrogen input benefits surrounding plant communities, particularly in nutrient-poor heathland and moorland ecosystems. The lichen effectively serves as a slow-release fertilizer for associated vegetation.

The species provides habitat and shelter for various soil invertebrates, including mites, springtails, and nematodes. These organisms contribute to decomposition [[fungal-elicitors-enhanced-secondary-metabolite-production]]. It also produces methyl tenuiorin and smaller quantities of gyrophoric acid in some populations.

Spot tests show the medulla KC+ yellow to orange, confirming the presence of tenuiorin. The cortex is typically C-negative and K-negative.
