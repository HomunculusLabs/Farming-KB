## Check Dam

## Description

A check dam is a small barrier constructed across a stream channel, gully, or intermittent watercourse to reduce flow velocity, trap sediment, and promote water infiltration. Check dams are among the oldest and most versatile water management structures used in permaculture and land rehabilitation. They range from simple rock piles to engineered concrete structures, and serve multiple functions depending on their design and placement.

## Classification

- **Domain**: Water management / Earthworks
- **Category**: Erosion control structure
- **Subtypes**: Silt check dam, Diversion check dam, Concrete flood check dam, Gabion check dam, Ram pump check dam
- **Scale**: Small to medium (farm and landscape scale)

## Key Facts

### Silt Check Dams
Earth or concrete walls or gabions placed across eroding gullies to hold silt fields, spread water across the landscape, and reduce silt loading in streams. They are particularly effective in dryland valleys where erosion is active. The base opening allows silt scour, keeping the area free of excessive siltation while still trapping beneficial sediment upstream.

### Diversion Check Dams
These divert intermittent flow to ridges, storages, or canals on contour. They redirect water from natural watercourses into storage systems or irrigation channels, making them essential components of rainwater harvesting systems in semi-arid regions.

### Concrete Flood Check Dams
Engineered structures that allow normal flow to pass while retarding floodwaters and preventing rapid flood discharge. These are typically used where flash flooding poses a risk to downstream infrastructure or where water needs to be slowed to allow infiltration.

### Gabion Check Dams
Permeable barriers made of rock-filled wire mesh baskets (gabions) placed across eroding valleys in drylands. They create silt fields and spread water across the landscape. Farm-scale versions are typically 2–2.65 metres high. Their purpose is not to store free surface water but to create flat areas where silt loads deposit and form absorption beds during flood conditions.

### Ram Pump and Water Wheel Check Dams
Designed to provide a tripping head that enables modest energy use for mechanical power, lift pumps, or diversion to canals. These combine water storage with energy generation potential.

## Construction Principles

- Materials include earth, concrete, gabions (rock-filled wire baskets), or combinations
- Gabion dams use strong wire baskets containing stones or shingle to resist floods
- Eroding gullies are best stabilised using gabion systems
- Base design must allow for silt scour to prevent excessive accumulation
- Height typically ranges from 2–3 metres for farm-scale structures

## Relevance to Cultivation and Land Management

Check dams are fundamental tools in permaculture water management because they:

1. **Control erosion** in gullies and intermittent streams, preventing soil loss
2. **Harvest water** by slowing overland flow and promoting infiltration into soils
3. **Build soil** by trapping silt and organic matter upstream of the structure
4. **Enable water diversion** into storage systems, swales, or irrigation channels
5. **Rehabilitate degraded land** by creating conditions for vegetation establishment
6. **Generate energy** when designed with sufficient head for ram pumps or water wheels

In cultivation systems, check dams create moist, fertile zones upstream where productive trees and crops can be established. They are especially valuable in dryland permaculture where every drop of water must be captured and used efficiently. Combined with swales and diversion drains, they form an integrated water harvesting network that can drought-proof a landscape.
