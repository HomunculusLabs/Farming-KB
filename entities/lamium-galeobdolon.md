## Overview

**Lamium galeobdolon**, commonly known as yellow archangel, is a perennial herb
in the family Lamiaceae. It is native to Europe and western Asia, where it
grows in woodlands and shaded habitats. The species is notable for its bright
yellow flowers and attractive silver-variegated leaves.

The common name "yellow archangel" likely refers to the bright yellow hooded
flowers, which were thought to resemble the golden aura of an archangel. The
species has become an invasive plant in several regions outside its native
range, particularly in the Pacific Northwest of [[hamilton-shade-garden-plants]] plant, though its aggressive spreading
behavior has led to restrictions in some jurisdictions.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

*Lamium galeobdolon* (L.) Crantz was originally described as *Galeobdolon
luteum* by Hudson and later transferred to *Lamium* by Crantz. Some
taxonomists treat the variegated horticultural form as a separate subspecies,
*Lamium galeobdolon* subsp. *argentatum* (Smit) Mennema.

The plant forms trailing stems 20–60 cm long that root at the nodes, creating
dense mats of foliage. Stems are square, ascending to prostrate, and
pubescent with retrorse hairs.
