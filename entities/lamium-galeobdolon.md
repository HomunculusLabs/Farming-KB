## Overview

**Lamium galeobdolon**, commonly known as yellow archangel, is a perennial herb
in the family Lamiaceae. It is native to Europe and western Asia, where it
grows in woodlands and shaded habitats. The species is notable for its bright
yellow flowers and attractive silver-variegated leaves.

The common name "yellow archangel" likely refers to the bright yellow hooded
flowers, which were thought to resemble the golden aura of an archangel. The
species has become an invasive plant in several regions outside its native
range, particularly in the Pacific Northwest of [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]].

Yellow archangel is closely related to the spotted dead-nettle (*Lamium
maculatum*) but differs in flower color, leaf shape, and growth habit. It is
widely cultivated as a [[hamilton-shade-garden-plants]] plant, though its aggressive spreading
behavior has led to restrictions in some jurisdictions.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

*Lamium galeobdolon* (L.) Crantz was originally described as *Galeobdolon
luteum* by Hudson and later transferred to *Lamium* by Crantz. Some
taxonomists treat the variegated horticultural form as a separate subspecies,
*Lamium galeobdolon* subsp. *argentatum* (Smit) Mennema.

The plant forms trailing stems 20–60 cm long that root at the nodes, creating
dense mats of foliage. Stems are square, ascending to prostrate, and
pubescent with retrorse hairs.

Leaves are opposite, broadly ovate to cordate, 3–8 cm long, with coarsely
serrate margins. The upper [[bloomfield-rust-germ-tube-tactile-sensing-leaf-surface-navigation]] is dark green with a prominent
silver-white medial stripe and lateral markings. The underside is
grayish-green and pubescent.

Flowers are borne in whorls of 6–10 in the upper leaf axils, with conspicuous
yellow bracts. The corolla is 18–25 mm long, bright yellow, with a prominent
hooded upper lip and a spreading three-lobed lower lip. Flowering occurs from
April through June.

The calyx is tubular-campanulate, approximately 10 mm long, with five
triangular teeth. The fruit consists of four brown, ovoid nutlets, each
approximately 2.5 mm long with a white elaiosome.

## Distribution and Habitat

Yellow archangel is native to much of Europe, from the British Isles and
France eastward to the Caucasus and northern Iran. It is absent from the
Mediterranean region and most of Scandinavia.

The species grows in deciduous and mixed woodlands, woodland margins,
hedgerows, and shady stream banks. It prefers moist, nutrient-rich, loamy
soils in partial to deep shade, though it tolerates some sun.

In [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]], *L. galeobdolon* subsp. *argentatum* (the variegated
cultivar) has escaped cultivation and become invasive in the Pacific
Northwest, particularly in western Washington and British Columbia. It forms
dense mats that suppress native [[fungal-ecology-forest-floor]] vegetation.

The invasive subspecies spreads primarily vegetatively through stem
fragmentation. Even small stem segments can root and establish new colonies,
making eradication difficult once established.

## Cultivation

Yellow archangel has been cultivated as an ornamental shade plant for
centuries. The variegated form is particularly popular for its silvery
foliage and bright yellow flowers, which provide contrast in shaded borders.

The plant is easily propagated from stem cuttings or by division. It prefers
partial to full shade with moist, well-drained soil. It is hardy to USDA
zone 4 and is tolerant of a range of soil pH levels.
