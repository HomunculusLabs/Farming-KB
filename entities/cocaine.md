## Cocaine

## Overview

**Cocaine** (benzoylmethylecgonine) is a tropane alkaloid and powerful central nervous system stimulant derived from the leaves of *[[serotonin]], and norepinephrine, producing intense euphoria, increased energy, and local anesthesia.

Cocaine is one of the most widely used illicit substances globally. It has a long history of both medicinal and recreational use dating back thousands of years in [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-south-america]].

## Chemical Properties

- **Molecular formula:** C₁₇H₂₁NO₄
- **Molecular weight:** 303.35 g/mol
- **IUPAC name:** methyl (1R,2R,3S,5S)-3-(benzoyloxy)-8-methyl-8-azabicyclo[3.2.1]octane-2-carboxylate
- **Structure type:** Tropane alkaloid (benzoate ester of ecgonine)
- **Appearance:** White crystalline powder (hydrochloride salt); opaque, flaky crystals (freebase/crack)
- **Melting point:** 195 °C (hydrochloride); 98 °C (freebase)
- **Solubility:** Freely soluble in water as HCl salt; insoluble in water as freebase
- **pKa:** 8.6 (tertiary amine)
- **CAS number:** 50-36-2

Cocaine is biosynthesized in *[[first-pass-metabolism-and-oral-bioavailability]] is low (~20%) due to hepatic first-pass metabolism.
- **Half-life:** 0.7–1.5 hours (varies by route and individual metabolism)
- **Volume of distribution:** 1.6–2.7 L/kg
- **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic via plasma and liver cholinesterases to benzoylecgonine (major inactive metabolite), ecgonine methyl ester, and norcocaine
- **Excretion:** Renal (1–10% unchanged as cocaine; benzoylecgonine detectable in urine for 2–4 days)

## Effects

### Subjective Effects

- Intense euphoria and well-being (peak 5–15 minutes post-administration)
- Increased energy, alertness, and confidence
- Heightened sensory awareness and sociability
- Decreased appetite and fatigue
- Anxiety, paranoia, and restlessness at higher doses or with chronic use

### Physiological Effects

- Acute hypertension and tachycardia
- Vasoconstriction and hyperthermia
- Mydriasis (pupil dilation)
- Bronchodilation
- Increased cardiac output and myocardial oxygen demand

### Toxicity and Adverse Effects

- Cardiotoxicity: myocardial infarction, arrhythmias, aortic dissection
- Cerebrovascular events: hemorrhagic and ischemic stroke
- Seizures (dose-dependent)
- Hyperthermia and rhabdomyolysis
- Nasal septal perforation (chronic insufflation)
- Severe psychological dependence
