## Description

A lock-pipe (also called a sluice gate or sliding gate) is a water release mechanism built into the wall of an earth dam. It consists of a square or rectangular pipe set through the dam wall, typically about 18 inches square for large dams, with a sliding gate or valve that controls water outflow. Lock-pipes allow controlled release of stored water for irrigation, domestic supply, or energy generation without relying on siphons or pumps.

## Classification

- **Category**: Water infrastructure / Dam component
- **Scale**: Small garden dams (2-inch) to large agricultural dams (18-inch square)
- **Material**: Typically metal pipe with sliding gate mechanism
- **Function**: Controlled water release from earth dams

## Construction and Installation

Lock-pipes are installed during dam wall construction:

1. **Placement**: Set into the dam wall at the desired release height, generally near the base.
2. **Size selection**: Determined by dam volume — a 5,000-gallon garden dam needs a small pipe, while a 5–6 million gallon dam requires an 18-inch square lock-pipe with a large wheel valve.
3. **Sealing**: The pipe must be carefully sealed with compacted clay around it during wall construction. Most lock-pipes leak slightly, which is considered normal.
4. **Flow control**: A sliding gate or wheel valve allows precise regulation of outflow rate.

## Key Facts

- **Flow rate**: Water exits a large lock-pipe about as fast as a person can walk at a moderate pace.
- **Gravity-fed**: Requires no pumps — water flows under hydrostatic head pressure from the dam.
- **Commercial availability**: Lock-pipes can be purchased as manufactured units.
- **Leakage tolerance**: Small leakage is expected and acceptable in earth dam construction.
- **Scale range**: From 2-inch pipes for garden tanks to 18-inch square conduits for large agricultural storages.
- **Alternative**: Siphon pipes over the top of the dam wall serve a similar function where lock-pipes are impractical.

## Applications in Permaculture Design

- **Irrigation**: Release water into contour ditches and diversion drains for gravity-fed irrigation across hundreds of acres.
- **Domestic water supply**: Provide pressurized water to households situated below the dam.
- **Mechanical power**: Feed water to turbines for generating electricity or running mechanical equipment (sawmills, washing machines, grain mills).
- **Fire suppression**: Rapid release of stored water for fire fighting, especially from high saddle dams.
- **Flood management**: Controlled release prevents downstream flooding during heavy rain events.

## Design Considerations

The outflow from a lock-pipe feeds directly into diversion drains or irrigation channels. The size of the receiving ditch must be proportional to the maximum flow rate of the lock-pipe. For a dam releasing 2.5 million gallons per day, a very large ditch is required. For a 2,000-gallon earth tank, only a trickle through the garden is needed.

## Relationship to Other Dam Components

A complete dam typically includes four elements:
1. **Diversion channel** leading water in
2. **Irrigation channel** leading water away
3. **Water release device** (lock-pipe or siphon)
4. **Spillway** for overflow management
