## Overview

**Cladonia fimbriata** is a fruticose lichen in the family Cladoniaceae, commonly known as
the fringed cup lichen. It belongs to the *Cladonia* pyxidata group and is characterized
by its small, distinctly fimbriate (fringed) podetia.

disjunct populations extending into montane fukuoka-natural-farming-in-tropical-regions.

The species is a versatile colonizer found on diverse substrates including soil, rotting
stumps, tree bases, peat, sand, and acid rock. It prefers well-drained, nutrient-poor
substrates in open or partially shaded sites.

It is particularly abundant in coniferous and mixed woodlands, heathlands, and disturbed
open areas. The species thrives on road cuts, quarries, and recently burned sites where
competition from vascular plants is minimal.

Elevation ranges from lowlands to approximately 2,500 meters in mountainous areas. It
reaches maximum abundance in subalpine and boreal coniferous forests with acidic soils.

## Chemistry

The secondary chemistry of *C. fimbriata* is relatively simple compared to many
*Cladonia* species. Fumarprotocetraric acid dominates the medullary chemistry, with
protocetraric acid as a minor constituent.
