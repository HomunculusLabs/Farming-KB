## Microscopic Features

Spores are purplish brown in deposit and characteristically mango-shaped to ellipsoid,
measuring 10 to 13 by 6 to 7.5 micrometers, with thick walls and a distinct germ pore
at the apical end. Basidia are 4-spored, clavate, measuring 18 to 28 by 5 to 8
micrometers. Pleurocystidia are absent. Cheilocystidia are lageniform to fusoid with a
narrow neck, measuring 20 to 40 by 4.5 to 9 micrometers. The pileipellis is a cutis
composed of repent, interwoven hyphae, with a gelatinous overlay that gives the cap its
viscid texture when moist. Clamp connections are present.

## Distribution and Habitat

P. baeocystis grows gregariously to subcespitose on decaying conifer mulch, in wood
chips, or in lawns with high lignin content. It occasionally fruits from fallen seed
cones of [[psilocybe-cyanofibrillosa]]. The species has not been reported outside the Pacific
Northwest region.

## Chemistry and Potency

P. baeocystis is moderately active but loses significant potency during drying or from
physical damage. Reported alkaloid concentrations range from 0.15% to 0.85% psilocybin,
up to 0.59% psilocin, and up to 0.10% baeocystin (Repke et al. 1977; Beug and Bigwood
1981, 1982b). McCawley et al. (1962) reported 0.63% psilocybin and less than 0.1%
psilocin from specimens analyzed following an intoxication incident. Total psilocybin
content is typically moderate compared to other section Cyanescens species, generally
ranging from 0.2% to 0.6% per dry weight.
