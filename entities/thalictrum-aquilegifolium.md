## Morphology

Thalictrum aquilegifolium forms sturdy, upright stems 80–120 cm tall from a compact, fibrous rootstock. The stems are branched above and often tinted purple at the base.

Leaves are 2–3 times ternately compound with rounded, lobed leaflets resembling columbine foliage. Each leaflet is 1–3 cm wide, blue-green above, and paler beneath.

The leaflets are sessile and clasp the stem at the base. The foliage texture is soft and creates a delicate, misty effect in the garden.

Flowers are small, lacking true petals, but showy due to numerous long, pendulous stamens. The stamens are lilac-pink to mauve, 8–15 mm long, and far more conspicuous than the tiny sepals.

Flowering occurs from June to August. The flower heads are compound corymbs 10–20 cm across, creating a cloud-like display above the foliage.

Fruit consists of small, ribbed achenes 4–6 mm long, each with a short, curved beak. The achenes are initially green, maturing to brown in late summer.

## Distribution and Habitat

Thalictrum aquilegifolium is native to Europe from the Pyrenees to the Urals, extending eastward through Siberia to northern China, Korea, and Japan. It is absent from the Mediterranean region and the far north.

The species grows in damp meadows, open deciduous woodlands, stream banks, and montane grasslands. It prefers moist, fertile, humus-rich soils in partial shade.

In the Alps it occurs up to 1800 m elevation. It thrives in areas with reliable summer rainfall and does not tolerate prolonged drought.

It is locally common in central Europe but scattered and uncommon in western Europe. In Britain it is a rare native confined to a few sites in southern England.

British populations are protected under the Wildlife and Countryside Act. Habitat loss and agricultural intensification have contributed to its decline in western Europe.

In eastern Asia, the species is more widespread and occupies a broader range of habitats. Japanese populations show slightly larger flower heads on average compared to European material.

## Ecology

The pendulous, nectar-rich flowers are adapted for insect pollination. Short-tongued bees and hoverflies are the primary visitors, attracted by the exposed pollen.

The tall flower stems provide vertical structure in meadow plantings. Seed heads remain ornamental after flowering, providing autumn interest.

Thalictrum aquilegifolium is the larval food plant for several moth species. The foliage contains thalicarpine and other alkaloids that deter most mammalian herbivores.
