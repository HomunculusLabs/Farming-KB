## Overview

Rubus ursinus is a deciduous trailing bramble native to western [[salix-alba]], [[betula-pendula]].

The plant prefers partial shade to full sun and well-drained, acidic to neutral soils.

It tolerates a wide range of soil types including sandy loams, clay loams, and rocky substrates.

Optimal soil pH ranges from 5.5 to 7.0.

## Cultivation and Uses

Rubus ursinus is the foundation species for the trailing blackberry industry of the [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]].

Major commercial cultivars including 'Marion', 'Boysenberry', 'Loganberry', and 'Tayberry' all derive from it.

'Marionberry', released in 1956 by Oregon State University, is the most economically important.

It is estimated to contribute over 50% of the parentage in modern trailing blackberry cultivars.

Commercial production requires a trellis system to support the trailing canes.
