## Overview

**Khaya senegalensis**, known as African mahogany or dry-zone mahogany, is a large evergreen to semi-deciduous tree in the family Meliaceae. It is native to the Sahelian and Sudanian savanna zones of sub-Saharan Africa.

The species is valued for its high-quality timber, which is traded as a substitute for true mahogany (*Swietenia* spp.). It also serves important roles in traditional medicine, agroforestry, and urban landscaping.

African mahogany has been widely planted across tropical Africa and beyond for timber, shade, and windbreak purposes. Its [[fukuoka-natural-farming-in-tropical-regions]] including Australia, Southeast Asia, and the Caribbean. Plantations in northern Australia have shown promising growth rates.

## Timber Properties and Uses

Heartwood is pinkish-brown to reddish-brown, moderately durable, and resistant to termites and borers. The wood is less durable than true mahogany when exposed to weather.

Timber density ranges from 560–680 kg/m³ at 12% moisture content. The grain is interlocked, producing a distinctive ribbon figure on quarter-sawn surfaces.

Wood is moderately easy to work, taking a good finish with proper techniques. The interlocked grain can cause tearing during planing if cutting angles are not adjusted.

Primary uses include furniture, cabinetry, flooring, boat building, and general construction. The timber is widely used in West Africa for door and window frames.

The bark is harvested for traditional medicine across its range. Decoctions treat fever, malaria, digestive ailments, and skin conditions.

Seed oil has been investigated for soap making and as a lubricant. The bitter bark extract shows demonstrated antimalarial and anti-inflammatory properties in laboratory studies.

## Cultivation and Silviculture

Propagation is primarily from seed, which requires no pretreatment. Germination occurs within 2–3 weeks at rates of 70–90%.

Seedlings are raised in nursery beds or polybags for 4–6 months before outplanting. Planting at the onset of rains significantly improves establishment success.

Spacing in plantations ranges from 3 × 3 to 4 × 4 meters depending on management objectives. Thinning begins at 8–10 years, targeting final stockings of 150–200 trees per hectare.

## Ecology and Conservation

In its native range, Khaya senegalensis provides food and habitat for numerous wildlife species. The seeds are eaten by birds and small mammals, facilitating natural regeneration.

The species is a significant component of riparian forest ecosystems, contributing to streambank stabilization and water cycle regulation. Its deep root system helps maintain groundwater levels.

Natural regeneration is often poor under heavy [[gadd-mycelial-network-resilience-grazing-pressure]]. Seedlings are palatable to livestock and frequently browsed before they can establish.
