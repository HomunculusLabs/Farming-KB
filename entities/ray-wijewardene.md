## Description

Ray Wijewardene was a Sri Lankan agricultural scientist, engineer, and inventor
known for developing the concept of **conservation farming** and **[[avenue-cropping]]**
in tropical regions. His work on integrating legume trees with food crops provided
a foundational technique for sustainable tropical agriculture, later adopted and
popularised by the permaculture movement.








## Classification

- **Category**: Person — Agricultural scientist / Inventor
- **Domain**: Tropical agriculture, Conservation farming, Agroforestry
- **Region**: Sri Lanka, South and Southeast Asia, tropical systems worldwide








## Key Facts

- Authored *Conservation Farming* (1981) published through the ITTA–Sri Lanka Program,
  Dharmapala Mawatha, Colombo 7, Sri Lanka.
- Developed the **avenue cropping** system: integrating regularly coppiced legume
  trees (such as *Leucaena*, *Gliricidia*, *Cassia*, *Acacia*) with intercrops of
  maize, sweet potato, ginger, pineapple, and other food crops.
- His avenue cropping technique provides simultaneous yields of fuelwood, mulch,
  nitrogen fixation, and food — all from the same land area.
- The system is specifically designed for tropical climates but adaptable to any
  climate with appropriate species selection.
- Recognised that coppicing legume trees must be timed to warm wet periods, and
  that constant coppicing weakens trees over 5–8 years, requiring replanting.








## Relevance to Cultivation / Mycology

Wijewardene's avenue cropping system is directly relevant to **integrated
permaculture design** and **mycological cultivation**:

- The deep mulch layers generated by coppiced legume prunings create ideal
  substrate conditions for fungal growth and soil food web development.
- Nitrogen-rich leaf litter from legume trees supports decomposer communities,
  including beneficial fungi, which accelerate nutrient cycling.
- The layered canopy structure (legume overstorey, food crop understorey) creates
  microclimatic niches suitable for cultivating shade-tolerant mushrooms such as
  *Pleurotus* ([[oyster-mushrooms]]) on spent legume prunings.
- His emphasis on zero-tillage and constant ground cover preserves mycorrhizal
  networks essential for crop health.
