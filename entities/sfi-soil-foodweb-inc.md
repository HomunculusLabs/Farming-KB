## Description

[[soil-foodweb]] Inc. (SFI) is a soil biology testing and consulting organization founded by Dr. [[elaine-ingham]]. SFI developed and commercialized direct microscopy methods for assessing soil, compost, and compost tea biology. The organization provides quantitative measurements of bacteria, fungi, protozoa, nematodes, and mycorrhizal colonization to guide biological soil management.

## Classification

- **Type**: Laboratory services and consulting organization
- **Founder**: Dr. Elaine Ingham
- **Field**: Soil biology assessment, compost tea quality control
- **Methods**: Direct microscopy, activity assays, molecular identification

## Key Facts

- Founded by Dr. Elaine Ingham based on research published in her 1985 Ecological Monograph.
- Uses direct microscopy methods including staining, dilution, and morphological identification to assess soil biology.
- Measures active and total biomass of bacteria, fungi, protozoa, and nematodes.
- Identifies mycorrhizal colonization levels, differentiating VAM from ericoid and ectomycorrhizal types.
- Maintains a database showing how organism populations change through seasons, soil types, and plant communities.
- Published scientific papers demonstrating relationships between biological measurements and disease protection, nutrient retention, nutrient cycling, and soil aggregation.
- Developing molecular methods for species-level identification of bacteria and fungi (not yet commercialized at time of writing).

## Testing Methods

### Direct Microscopy
- Dilution and staining of soil/compost/tea samples
- Measurement of length, width, and number of individuals per group
- Morphological identification of morpho-species
- Activity staining to distinguish living/functional organisms from total organisms

### Nematode Assessment
- Active nematode extraction and morphological identification
- Classification into bacterial-feeding, fungal-feeding, predatory, and root-feeding groups

### Mycorrhizal Assessment
- Root colonization measurement
- Differentiation of VAM, ericoid, and ectomycorrhizal types
- Trained by Dr. [[efren-cazares]] ([[jim-trappe]]'s lab)

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- Provides the data foundation for biological soil management decisions.
- SFI testing enables growers to assess whether compost tea applications are achieving desired organism levels.
- The colonizer percentage thresholds (<15%, 15-40%, >40% for VAM) guide mycorrhizal management decisions.
- Data supports the development of compost tea standards and machine performance benchmarks.
- Bridges the gap between academic soil ecology and practical agricultural decision-making.

## Controversy and Advocacy

- SFI advocates direct microscopy over plate count methods, citing that plate counts miss ~99.99% of soil organisms.
- Dr. Ingham positions SFI as willing to recommend other labs' methods when appropriate, rather than exclusively promoting their own.
- The organization supports development of AACT ([[actively-aerated-compost-tea]]) standards using scientific approaches.
