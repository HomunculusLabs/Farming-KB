## Overview

**Inocybe rimosa** (Bull.) P. Kumm., commonly known as the torn fibercap or
split fibrecap, is a terrestrial agaric fungus in the family Inocybaceae.
It is widely distributed across temperate hardwood and conifer forests of
Europe and [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]]. The species is inedible and suspected of containing
[[muscarine]], making it potentially toxic if consumed.

The epithet *rimosa* refers to the prominently radially fibrillose and often
cracked or split cap surface, which is the species' most diagnostic field
character. Inocybe rimosa is one of the more frequently encountered members
of a notoriously difficult genus, many species of which contain dangerous
levels of muscarine alkaloids.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

### Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Inocybaceae
- **Genus:** Inocybe (Fr.) Fr.
- **Species:** I. rimosa (Bull.) P. Kumm. (1871)

**Basionym:** Agaricus rimosus Bull. (1789).

Synonyms include *Inocybe fastigiata* (Schaeff.) Quél. and *Agaricus
fastigiatus* Schaeff. The nomenclatural history of I. rimosa has been
complicated by its frequent confusion with I. fastigiata sensu various
authors. Modern treatments generally treat I. fastigiata as a separate
species with smoother spores.

### Cap

The pileus is 2–7 cm broad, conico-convex when young, expanding to broadly
umbonate or plano-convex with age. The surface is covered with conspicuous
radial fibrils that often split or crack into small scales toward the margin,
giving the cap a ragged or torn appearance.

Color ranges from pale ochre-buff to yellowish-brown or tan, often darkening
with age. The margin is incurved when young, often splitting radially as it
expands. The context is thin, whitish to buff, and has no distinctive odor
when fresh.

### Gills

Gills are adnexed to sinuate, moderately crowded, with lamellulae of three
lengths. Color is pale clay-buff at first, developing a snuff-brown to
ferruginous-brown tone as spores mature. Edges are fibrillose and paler than
the gill face.

### Stipe

The stipe measures 4–9 cm long × 4–10 mm thick, equal or slightly enlarged
at the base. Surface is white to pale buff, covered with fine white to
pale fibrils above, becoming more scaly toward the base. A pruinose zone
is often visible near the apex. No ring or volva is present.

### Spores

Basidiospores are smooth, bean-shaped (phaseoliform) in face view, with a
distinct apical germ pore. Dimensions are 9–12 × 5–6.5 μm. [[comparison-spore-print-collection-vs-spore-syringe-production]] is
snuff-brown to tobacco-brown. Pleurocystidia are thin-walled, cylindrical
to lageniform, often with a crystalline incrustation at the tip. Cheilocystidia
are similar but more abundant.
