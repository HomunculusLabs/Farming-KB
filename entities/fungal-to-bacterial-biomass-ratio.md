## Fungal to Bacterial Biomass Ratio (F:B Ratio)

## Description

The **fungal to bacterial biomass ratio** (F:B ratio) is a key metric in [[soil-food-web]] science that describes the relative proportion of fungal biomass to bacterial biomass in a given soil. This ratio is one of the most important indicators of soil ecosystem type and directly determines which plant species will thrive in a particular soil. Matching the F:B ratio to plant preferences is a fundamental principle of soil food web gardening.

## Classification

- **Category:** Soil biology metric / soil food web parameter
- **Units:** Ratio of fungal biomass to bacterial biomass (by weight)
- **Measurement:** Determined by agricultural testing laboratories through direct microscopy or biochemical methods

## Key Facts

- **Bacterial populations remain relatively constant:** Approximately 100 million to 1 billion bacteria per teaspoon of soil across garden, prairie, and forest environments
- **Fungal biomass varies dramatically:** It is the increase in fungal biomass (not decrease in bacteria) that drives changes in the F:B ratio across ecosystem types
- **[[fungal-hyphae]] length:** Ranges from several yards in garden soils to 1-40 miles in coniferous forest soils (per teaspoon)
- **Protozoa counts:** Range from hundreds of thousands per teaspoon across all soil types
- **Succession correlation:** F:B ratio increases along the plant succession continuum from pioneer weeds to old-growth forest

## F:B Ratios by Plant Type

### Vegetables and Annuals (Bacterially Dominated)
| Plant Type | Preferred F:B Ratio |
|---|---|
| Carrots, lettuce, broccoli, cole crops | 0.3:1 to 0.8:1 |
| Tomatoes, corn, wheat | 0.8:1 to 1:1 |
| Lawn grasses | 0.5:1 to 1:1 |

### Trees and Shrubs (Fungally Dominated)
| Plant Type | Preferred F:B Ratio |
|---|---|
| Conifers | 50:1 to 1000:1 |
| Maples, oaks, poplars | 10:1 to 100:1 |
| Orchard specimens | 10:1 to 50:1 |
| Alder, beech, aspen, cottonwood (young) | Bacterially dominated |
| Alder, beech, aspen, cottonwood (mature) | 5:1 to 100:1 |

### Flowers
| Plant Type | Preferred F:B Ratio |
|---|---|
| Most annuals | Bacterially dominated |
| Most perennials | Fungally dominated |

### Shrubs
| Plant Type | Preferred F:B Ratio |
|---|---|
| Rhododendrons (conifer forest natives) | Very high fungal dominance |
| Cotoneaster, lilac (deciduous natives) | Moderate fungal dominance |

## The Science Behind the Ratio

### Why F:B Ratio Determines Plant Success
The F:B ratio directly controls two critical soil properties:

1. **Nitrogen form availability:**
   - **Bacterially dominated soils (high pH >7):** Bacterial slime raises pH, favoring nitrifying bacteria that convert ammonium to nitrate (NO3-). Plants preferring bacterially dominated soils receive nitrogen primarily as nitrate.
   - **Fungally dominated soils (low pH <7):** Fungi produce organic acids that lower [[soil-ph]], inhibiting nitrifying bacteria. Ammonium (NH4+) remains as ammonium. Plants preferring fungally dominated soils receive nitrogen primarily as ammonium.

2. **Soil [[root-anatomy-structure-and-nutrient-uptake]] cycling:**
   - Bacterial dominance produces different soil aggregate structures than fungal dominance
   - Mycorrhizal fungi require longer-lived root systems, explaining why perennials and trees prefer fungally dominated soils

### The Succession Continuum
Plant succession from bare soil to old-growth forest correlates with increasing F:B ratio:
1. **Pioneer weeds** - bacterially dominated
2. **Grasses/small shrubs** - transitional
3. **Soft hardwoods/saplings** - increasing fungal dominance
4. **Mature hardwoods** - strongly fungally dominated
5. **Old-growth conifer forest** - maximum fungal dominance (100:1 to 1000:1)

## Modifying the F:B Ratio

### To Increase Fungi (Raise F:B)
- Apply fungal foods: woody materials, lignin-rich organic matter, humic acids
- Reduce or eliminate tillage (tillage breaks fungal hyphae)
- Apply compost with higher fungal content
- Use mulches (wood chips, leaves)
- Plant perennials and maintain living root systems

### To Increase Bacteria (Lower F:B)
- Apply bacterial foods: simple sugars, fresh green plant material, molasses
- Incorporate organic matter into soil (light tillage)
- Apply compost tea with bacterial dominance
- Use [[green-manures-and-cover-crops]]
- Plant annuals and vegetables

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

1. **[[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]]:** Understanding F:B ratios helps in preparing substrates; fungal-dominant soils are natural environments for many [[edible-mushroom-species-identification]]
2. **Mycorrhizal management:** Mycorrhizal fungi require fungally dominated soils; adjusting F:B ratio supports [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-colonization-patterns]]
3. **Crop-specific soil preparation:** Different crops require different F:B ratios; soil management should target the appropriate ratio for the intended crop
4. **Soil testing:** Agricultural testing labs can measure F:B ratio, providing actionable data for soil management decisions
5. **Transition management:** When converting lawn (bacterial) to perennial beds (fungal) or vice versa, intentional F:B ratio adjustment is necessary
