# Jane Jacobs (Holmgren Reference)

## Description

Jane Jacobs (1916–2006) was an American-Canadian journalist, author, and urban theorist whose work revolutionised thinking about cities and community vitality. David Holmgren cites her landmark *The Death and Life of Great American Cities* (Vintage Press, 1961) as a key reference in understanding urban systems and change.

## Background and Classification

- **Type:** Person — urban theorist, author, activist
- **Nationality:** American-born, later Canadian citizen
- **Lifespan:** 1916–2006
- **Key work:** *The Death and Life of Great American Cities* (1961)
- **Domain:** Urban planning, community sociology, economics

## Key Facts

- Challenged dominant mid-20th century urban planning orthodoxies of slum clearance and highway construction
- Advocated for mixed-use neighbourhoods, short blocks, dense but human-scale streetscapes
- Argued vibrant cities emerge from bottom-up organic processes rather than top-down master planning
- Successfully organised opposition to Robert Moses' proposed expressways through Greenwich Village
- Later expanded analysis to economics (*Cities and the Wealth of Nations*, 1984)
- Influenced New Urbanism and walkable cities movement worldwide

## Relevance to Permaculture and Cultivation

- **Bottom-up design:** Her critique of top-down urban planning mirrors Permaculture's preference for working with natural systems
- **Diversity and resilience:** Diverse mixed-use neighbourhoods are more resilient than monoculture zones, paralleling Permaculture Principle 10 (Use and Value Diversity)
- **Edge effect:** Street-level interaction and mixed uses map onto Permaculture Principle 11 (Use Edges and Value the Marginal)
- **Self-organisation:** Healthy cities self-organise through many participants, like healthy ecosystems
- **Observation first:** Jacobs' methodology was grounded in careful observation of how cities actually function
- **Social succession:** Holmgren's discussion of gentrification and urban succession draws on dynamics Jacobs documented
