# Hypsizygus ulmarius

**Elm [[paul-stamets]] ranks hypsizygus ulmarius above all other oyster-like
mushrooms in terms of flavor and texture, making it one of the most desirable
species for home cultivators and [[cannabis-harvest-timing-visual-cues]] critical.

## Distribution

hypsizygus ulmarius is found throughout temperate forests of [[hypsizygus-tessulatus]] but is distinguished by its larger size, lighter
coloration, and wavy cap margin. In contrast, *H. tessulatus* is smaller,
stouter, with a thicker stem and cap speckled with dark "water" markings.
*H. ulmarius* looks more like *Pleurotus*; *H. tessulatus* is closer to
*Lyophyllum* or *Tricholoma* in overall appearance.

**Microscopic features:**

Spores are white, spherical to egg-shaped, measuring 3 to 5 um. Clamp
connections are numerous. The hyphal system is monomitic.

**Mycelium:**

White, cottony, closely resembling agar media.

**Fragrance:**

Sweetly oyster-esque with a floury overtone, not anise-like, and generally
pleasant.

## Cultivation

hypsizygus ulmarius benefits from supplemented sterilized sawdust with rye,
rice, or wheat bran. Oak, alder, or cottonwood sawdust are preferred substrates.
Yields range from one-half to one pound of fresh mushrooms from five pounds of
moist sawdust, chips, and bran mixture.

superior texture and flavor, and the ability to become quite large.
