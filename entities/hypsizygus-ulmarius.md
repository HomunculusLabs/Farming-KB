# Hypsizygus ulmarius

**Elm Oyster Mushroom**

*Author: (Bulliard:Fries) Redhead*

## Introduction

hypsizygus ulmarius, commonly known as the Elm Oyster Mushroom or
Shirotamogitake (Japanese "White Elm Mushroom"), is a large and highly prized
edible mushroom found throughout temperate forests of the Northern Hemisphere.
Despite its common name, it is not a true oyster mushroom of the genus
*Pleurotus* but rather belongs to the genus *Hypsizygus*.

The taxonomic history of this species is complex. It was formerly classified as
*Pleurotus ulmarius*, then as *Lyophyllum ulmarius*, before being placed in its
current position as *Hypsizygus ulmarius* by Redhead. It is closely related to
[[hypsizygus-tessulatus]], occupying the same ecological niche on hardwood trees.

[[paul-stamets]] ranks hypsizygus ulmarius above all other oyster-like
mushrooms in terms of flavor and texture, making it one of the most desirable
species for home cultivators and gourmet mushroom enthusiasts.

## Description

Mushrooms are hemispheric to plane, sometimes umbelicate, uniformly tan, beige,
grayish brown to gray, sometimes with faint streaks. Caps measure 4 to 15 cm in
diameter. The cap margin is inrolled when young, expanding to slightly
undulating at maturity with a flared thin, uneven, wavy margin.

Gills are decurrent, close, running down the stem. The stem is eccentrically
attached, thick, tapering and curved at the base. Fruiting bodies are usually
found singly, sometimes in groups of 2 to 3, and rarely in larger clusters.

Unlike most mushrooms where biomass increase ceases when caps reach broadly
convex stage, hypsizygus ulmarius continues lateral growth with appreciable
mass increase. Picking too early results in substantial yield loss, making
harvest timing critical.

## Distribution

hypsizygus ulmarius is found throughout temperate forests of eastern North
America, Europe, and Japan. Though less common than [[hypsizygus-tessulatus]] in
the wild, it occupies a similar broad geographic range across temperate regions.

## Natural Habitat

A saprophyte on elms, cottonwoods, beech, maple, willow, oak, and occasionally
other hardwoods. hypsizygus ulmarius shares its ecological niche with
[[hypsizygus-tessulatus]] but is distinguished by its larger size, lighter
coloration, and wavy cap margin. In contrast, *H. tessulatus* is smaller,
stouter, with a thicker stem and cap speckled with dark "water" markings.
*H. ulmarius* looks more like *Pleurotus*; *H. tessulatus* is closer to
*Lyophyllum* or *Tricholoma* in overall appearance.

**Microscopic features:**

Spores are white, spherical to egg-shaped, measuring 3 to 5 um. Clamp
connections are numerous. The hyphal system is monomitic.

**Mycelium:**

White, cottony, closely resembling [[pleurotus-ostreatus]] mycelium. A few
strains produce primordia directly on 2% malt yeast [[agar]] media.

**Fragrance:**

Sweetly oyster-esque with a floury overtone, not anise-like, and generally
pleasant.

## Cultivation

hypsizygus ulmarius benefits from supplemented sterilized sawdust with rye,
rice, or wheat bran. Oak, alder, or cottonwood sawdust are preferred substrates.
Yields range from one-half to one pound of fresh mushrooms from five pounds of
moist sawdust, chips, and bran mixture.

Advantages over *Pleurotus* species include substantially less spore load,
superior texture and flavor, and the ability to become quite large.

A notable patent controversy exists: a Japanese group was awarded a patent for
a strain producing a "convex cap." [[paul-stamets]] notes this is hardly unusual
since most gilled mushrooms progress through hemispheric, convex, to plane
stages during normal development.

| Fruitbody Development | 55 to 65 F (13 to 18 C) | 94 to 98% | 4 to 7 days | 600 to 1500 ppm | 500 to 1000 lux |

Cropping cycle produces two flushes, 7 to 10 days apart.

## Medicinal Properties

Anecdotal, unpublished reports suggest hypsizygus ulmarius may be highly
anti-carcinogenic. Traditional Chinese medicine recommends mushrooms in this
group for treating stomach and intestinal diseases. While formal clinical
studies are lacking, the close relationship to [[hypsizygus-tessulatus]], which
has demonstrated remarkable anti-cancer activity, suggests potential
pharmacological merit worthy of further investigation.

## Flavor and Preparation

hypsizygus ulmarius is considered one of the finest edible mushrooms, with
a firm meaty texture and rich, nutty flavor that surpasses most other oyster-like
species. The large cap size makes it versatile in the kitchen, suitable for
grilling, sauteing, roasting, and soups. It pairs well with butter, garlic,
herbs, cream sauces, and hearty meat dishes.
