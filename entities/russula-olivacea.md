## Russula olivacea

## Overview

*Russula olivacea* is a basidiomycete fungus in the family Russulaceae, noted for its large olive-green to purplish cap and its status as one of the more robust European Russula species.

It is an ectomycorrhizal associate of deciduous and coniferous trees, commonly found in mature [[comparison-spore-print-collection-vs-spore-syringe-production]]:** Cream to pale ochre (IIb–IIc on the Romagnesi color scale).

**Spores:** Globose to broadly ellipsoid, 7–9 × 6–8 µm, ornamented with isolated warts and short ridges, partially connected by fine lines forming a partial reticulum. Amyloid reaction (dextrinoid to amyloid).

**Taste:** Mild to slightly acrid, depending on age and specimen.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Russula olivacea* is found across temperate Europe, from Scandinavia to the Mediterranean, with scattered records from parts of western Asia.

It fruits from late summer through autumn (July to October in the Northern Hemisphere), typically in association with mature forest trees.

**Mycorrhizal partners include** oaks (*Quercus* spp.), beech (*[[carpinus-betulus]]*), and occasionally spruce (*[[picea-abies]]*).

It prefers well-drained, slightly acidic to neutral soils in old-growth or semi-natural forests with rich humus layers. The species is less common in recently disturbed or plantation forests.

## Edibility

*Russula olivacea* is generally regarded as edible and is consumed in several European countries, particularly in France, Italy, and Spain.

The flesh is firm and holds its texture well when cooked, making it suitable for stews, sautés, and preserves. The mild fruity aroma is considered agreeable.

It should always be cooked thoroughly before consumption. Raw consumption is not recommended due to the presence of thermolabile irritants common to many *Russula* species.

As with all *Russula*, positive identification is essential before eating, as several green-capped congeners are toxic or disagreeable.
