# Gigaspora gigantea

## Description

*Gigaspora gigantea* is a species of arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) fungus in the genus *Gigaspora*, suborder Gigasporineae, order Glomales. It produces some of the largest spores among AM fungi, reaching up to 300 µm or more in diameter. The species is globally distributed and serves as an important reference organism for understanding spore biology and functional morphology within the Gigasporaceae.

## Classification

- **[[kingdom-fungi]]
- **Phylum:** Zygomycota / Glomeromycota
- **Order:** Glomales
- **Suborder:** Gigasporineae
- **Family:** Gigasporaceae
- **Genus:** *Gigaspora*
- **Species:** *G. gigantea*

## Key Facts

- Spores can exceed 300 µm in diameter, with a volume of approximately 1.4 × 10⁷ µm³ (substantially larger than most *Glomus* species)
- Distributed widely across most continents
- *Gigaspora* contains only 5 described species—the low number attributed to developmental constraints on spore wall variation associated with germination
- Unique among AM fungal genera in that the terminal event in spore wall differentiation is germination itself (Bentivenga and Morton 1995)
- Members of Gigasporineae produce auxiliary cells (rather than vesicles) and arbuscules within host roots
- Auxiliary cells and detached hyphae of Gigasporineae are generally not infective, unlike roots and spores (Biermann and Linderman 1983)

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

*Gigaspora gigantea* is significant in AM fungal research for several reasons:

- Its large spore size makes it relatively easy to extract, identify, and manipulate in laboratory settings
- Used in studies of spore volume as a proxy for carbon allocation from host plants to fungal symbionts
- The genus *Gigaspora* serves as a model for understanding the Gigasporineae suborder, which differs fundamentally from Glomineae in its reproductive biology and infection structures
- Relevant to studies of spore viability, as spores in high-organic-matter tropical soils are often degraded or parasitized
- Illustrates the relationship between spore biovolume and importance values in community ecology studies
