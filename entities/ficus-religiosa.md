## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Taxonomy:**

- Kingdom: Plantae
- Order: Rosales
- Family: Moraceae
- Subfamily: Moroideae
- Tribe: Ficeae
- Genus: Ficus
- Species: F. religiosa L.

The species was described by Carl Linnaeus in 1753 in *Species Plantarum*. The specific epithet *religiosa* reflects the sacred status of the tree in the religious traditions of the Indian subcontinent.

Ficus religiosa belongs to the subgenus Urostigma, the group of "strangler figs" that begin life as epiphytes and may eventually engulf their host trees. It is most closely related to Ficus microcarpa and Ficus benjamina within the section Urostigma.

**Morphology:**

Ficus religiosa is a large, semi-evergreen to deciduous tree with a broad, spreading crown. Mature trees develop massive trunks up to 3 meters in diameter, often with prominent buttress roots at the base.

The bark is smooth, pale gray to whitish, and flakes off in irregular patches. All plant parts exude a milky latex when cut, which is characteristic of the Moraceae family.

Leaves are alternate, simple, cordate (heart-shaped), 10 to 17 centimeters long and 8 to 12 centimeters wide. The most distinctive feature is the long, slender drip tip extending 2 to 5 centimeters beyond the leaf blade, which facilitates rapid water drainage in tropical rainfall.

The leaf surface is smooth and leathery, with 5 to 7 pairs of lateral veins. New leaves are bronzy-red to pinkish and may be produced in flushes several times per year. The petiole is 5 to 10 centimeters long, with a pair of small, rounded basal stipules.

The figs (syconia) are small, globose, 1 to 1.5 centimeters in diameter, borne in pairs in the leaf axils. They ripen from green to dark purple-black and contain numerous tiny seeds. The figs are pollinated by a species-specific wasp, Pleistodontes froggatti.

Aerial roots develop abundantly from the branches of mature trees. These roots grow downward toward the ground and, upon contact, thicken into pillar-like prop roots that can provide additional [[query-what-is-the-ideal-temperature-range-for-growing-cannabis]] from approximately 5°C to 45°C and annual rainfall from 500 to 3,000 millimeters. It is somewhat drought-tolerant but performs best with reliable moisture during [[entheogenic-folk-religious-ceremonies]], and meditation.

## Cultivation and Uses

**Propagation and Growth:**

Propagation is primarily by seed or by cuttings from mature branches. Seeds germinate readily in moist, well-drained soil but require exposure to the species-specific pollinating wasp for fig production.
