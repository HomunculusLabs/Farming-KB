## Gymnopilus brunneus

**Gymnopilus brunneus** is a wood-inhabiting mushroom in the family Cortinariaceae
and one of several Gymnopilus species reported to contain [[psilocybin]]. The distribution of psychoactive compounds within the genus does not
follow clear phylogenetic patterns, suggesting either multiple independent evolutionary
origins of psilocybin biosynthesis or repeated evolutionary loss of the trait.

The genus name Gymnopilus derives from Greek "gymnos" (naked) and Latin "pilos" (cap),
referring to the typically smooth or bare cap surface of many species in the genus.

## Description

**Cap:** Convex to broadly convex, sometimes becoming plano-convex or slightly depressed
with age. Surface is dry, smooth to finely fibrillose, typically brown to reddish-brown
or dark brown. The cap may develop slight cracking or scaling in dry conditions. Cap
diameter ranges from 30 to 80 mm, sometimes larger in favorable conditions. The margin
is typically inrolled when young, becoming straight or slightly uplifted with age. The
cap surface is non-viscid even when wet.

**Gills:** Adnate to sinuate or slightly subdecurrent, close to subdistant. Color is
initially pale yellowish or cream, darkening to a distinctive rusty orange or rusty brown
as spores mature. This rust-colored spore deposit is a key diagnostic feature of the genus
Gymnopilus and the family Cortinariaceae. The gills may be somewhat mottled in
appearance due to uneven spore maturation.

**Stem:** Central, cylindrical, often curved or eccentric. Surface is smooth to
fibrillose, colored similar to the cap or somewhat paler. The stem is typically solid
and fibrous, difficult to break cleanly. A partial veil is present in young specimens,
sometimes forming a fragile annular ring or leaving a fibrillose ring zone on the upper
stem. The ring is often ephemeral and may disappear entirely in mature specimens. Length
ranges from 40 to 100 mm, with a thickness of 5 to 15 mm.

G. brunneus has been reported from [[psilocybin]],
placing it among the psychoactive members of the genus. The Gymnopilus genus is
unusual among mushroom genera in that psychoactive and non-psychoactive species coexist
within the same group.
