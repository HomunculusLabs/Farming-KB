## Steer Manure

## Description

Steer manure is a commonly available organic soil amendment produced from cattle (bovine) waste. It is widely sold in sacked form at supermarkets and garden centers, particularly during spring planting season. In gardening contexts, it serves as a source of [[savory-soil-organic-matter-and-decomposition-dynamics]] modest soil fertility improvement rather than a potent fertilizer.

## Classification

- **Category:** Organic soil amendment / fertilizer
- **Type:** Animal manure (ruminant)
- **Form:** Typically sold sacked and partially composted; may contain semi-decomposed sawdust as bedding material
- **NPK profile:** Low — considerably weaker than chicken manure

## Key Facts

- **Availability:** Sold bagged at supermarkets and garden centers in spring at relatively low price per bag
- **Composition:** Often contains semi-decomposed sawdust bedding material mixed with manure
- **Fertilizing value:** Modest; has "little fertilizing value" as a nutrient source compared to alternatives like chicken manure or COF ([[complete-organic-fertilizer-cof]])
- **Primary benefit:** Feeds soil microbes and improves soil structure (tilth), which helps roots breathe and access nutrients
- **Processing:** At least partially composted before sale; not raw manure
- **Application depth:** Recommended at 1/4 inch (6 mm) layer for low- and medium-demand vegetables; 1/2 inch (12 mm) for high-demand vegetables per 1,000 square feet
- **Safety:** Useful when applied appropriately but not hugely beneficial when overapplied
- **Comparison:** Significantly weaker than chicken manure (NPK ~4-3-2) for fertilizing purposes

## Application Rates (Solomon Method)

| Crop Demand | Layer Depth | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Low-demand veggies | 1/4 inch (6 mm) | Minimum application |
| Medium-demand veggies | 1/4 inch (6 mm) | With additional amendments |
| High-demand veggies | 1/2 inch (12 mm) | Or substitute 1/4 inch finished compost |

Can be replaced by finished compost at roughly equivalent depths for better results.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Soil building:** Contributes organic matter that supports [[cannabis-rhizosphere-microbial-communities]], including [[ingham-aact-beauveria-fruit-fly-beneficial-bacteria-inoculant]] and fungi relevant to both plant and fungal cultivation
- **Compost ingredient:** Can be used as a nitrogen source in compost piles intended for [[fungicide-treated-seed-hazard-mushroom-substrate-preparation]]
- **Soil structure:** The fibrous organic matter content improves aeration and water retention in heavy soils
- **Caveat:** May contain residual antibiotics or hormones from conventional livestock operations; organic sources preferred for mycological applications
- **Microbial feeding:** Supports soil food web organisms that contribute to nutrient cycling, beneficial in both garden [[amanita-identification-and-mushroom-cultivation]] contexts

## Warnings

- Do not exceed double the recommended application rates to avoid unbalancing soil mineral content
- If vegetables are underperforming, add more manure or compost rather than additional concentrated fertilizer
- Semi-decomposed sawdust content can temporarily tie up nitrogen as it breaks down

## Nutrient Cycling
Organic amendments play a vital role in closing nutrient loops
on farms and in gardens. Manure and compost return nutrients
to the soil that were removed through harvest. The slow release
of nutrients from organic sources provides sustained feeding
for crops throughout the growing season.

## Application Methods
Proper application ensures maximum benefit and minimizes
nutrient loss. Incorporate amendments into the soil surface
or apply as side-dressing during the growing season. Avoid
applying fresh manure directly to edible crops. Composting
or aging manure before application reduces pathogen risks.

## Safety and Best Practices
Always wear gloves when handling animal manures. Age or
compost manure for at least six months before garden use.
Keep amendments covered during storage to prevent nutrient
runoff. Follow local regulations regarding manure application
rates and timing near waterways.
