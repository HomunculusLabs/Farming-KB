## Overview

**Calamintha grandiflora** (L.) Schult., commonly known as large-flowered calamint or showy calamint, is a perennial herbaceous plant in the family Lamiaceae.

It is native to southern and central Europe, where it grows in woodland clearings, rocky slopes, and mountain meadows. The species is valued for its showy pink flowers and pleasant minty fragrance.

Large-flowered calamint is distinguished from related species by its relatively large, showy flowers and preference for shaded or semi-shaded habitats.

The plant has been used in traditional European [[calamintha-nepeta]]*.

In mountainous regions, it is a characteristic component of subalpine meadow communities and forest-edge ecotones.

## Cultivation

Large-flowered calamint is increasingly popular as a garden ornamental, valued for its long bloom period, attractive flowers, and aromatic foliage.

Deadheading extends the flowering period. Cutting back after the first flush of bloom encourages a second flowering in autumn.

## Chemistry and Pharmacology

The essential oil of *Calamintha grandiflora* is chemically distinct from that of *C. nepeta*, with different dominant compounds reflecting genetic differentiation.

**Principal essential oil constituents:**

- **Pulegone:** 20–45% in most studied populations, the dominant compound.
- **Menthone:** 10–25%, contributing minty character.
- **Piperitone:** 5–15%, varies with geographic origin.
- **Isomenthone:** 3–10%, stereoisomer of menthone.
- **Limonene, β-Caryophyllene:** 2–5% each.

Tunisian and Sicilian populations show distinct chemical profiles, with piperitone oxide as a significant component in some samples.

**Reported pharmacological activities:**

Essential oil demonstrates significant antibacterial activity against *[[staphylococcus-aureus]]*, *Bacillus cereus*, and *Listeria monocytogenes* in vitro.
