# Guduchi (*Tinospora cordifolia*)

## Overview
*Tinospora cordifolia*, known as Guduchi or Giloy in Ayurvedic medicine, is a large deciduous climbing shrub in the family Menispermaceae. Native to tropical India, Myanmar, and Sri Lanka, it is one of the most important Rasayana (rejuvenating) herbs in Ayurveda, used for millennia as an immunomodulator, anti-arthritic, and anti-diabetic agent. Modern pharmacological research has validated its immunostimulant, hepatoprotective, and adaptogenic properties, leading to widespread commercial cultivation and incorporation into dozens of polyherbal formulations across South Asia.

## Taxonomy
- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Order:** Ranunculales
- **Family:** Menispermaceae
- **Genus:** Tinospora
- **Species:** *T. cordifolia*
- **Author citation:** (Willd.) Hook.f. & Thomson, 1855
- **Synonyms:** *Cocculus cordifolius* DC., *Menispermum cordifolium* Willd.
- **Etymology:** *Tinospora* from Greek "tinos" (fibrous) + "spora" (seed); *cordifolia* Latin for "heart-leaved"
- **Sanskrit name:** Guduchi ("that which protects the body"); Amrita ("divine nectar")
- **Related species:** *T. sinensis* (Chinese Guduchi), *T. crispa* (Hao Pa Kao, Southeast Asia)

## Morphology
- **Habit:** Large, glabrous, perennial climbing shrub; stems twine around support trees up to 15 m
- **Stems:** Succulent, green when young, becoming grey-brown and corky with age; prominently lenticulate; exude watery latex when cut; distinctive aerial roots form at nodes
- **Leaves:** Simple, alternate, cordate (heart-shaped), 8–15 cm long, 7–13 cm broad; acute apex; palmately veined with 5–7 prominent veins; glabrous on both surfaces; petiole 5–10 cm
- **Flowers:** Unisexual (dioecious); greenish-yellow; arranged in axillary or terminal racemes; male flowers in clustered fascicles, female flowers solitary; 6 sepals, 6 petals (small); stamens 6, connate
- **Fruit:** Drupes, red when ripe, 6–10 mm diameter, produced in clusters; 1–3 seeded
- **Root:** Fibrous, tuberous; pale yellow interior
- **Bark:** Creamy-white to grey, smooth, thin; exfoliates in papery strips

## Phytochemistry

### Alkaloids
- **Berberine:** Major isoquinoline alkaloid (0.5–1.2% in stem); antibacterial, anti-diabetic, hepatoprotective
- **Palmatine:** Protoberberine alkaloid; anti-inflammatory, neuroprotective
- **Jatrorrhizine:** Antibacterial, anti-inflammatory
- **Magnoflorine:** Aporphine alkaloid; immunomodulatory activity
- **Tinosporine, tinosporidine:** Characteristic alkaloids of *Tinospora* genus

### Terpenoids and Diterpenoid Lactones
- **Tinosporide, tinosporic acid, tinosporol:** Clerodane-type furanoditerpenes; immunomodulatory
- **Cordifolide A, cordifolide B:** Diterpenoid lactones; cytotoxic activity (in vitro)
- **Tinocordiside:** Sesquiterpene glycoside; adaptogenic activity

### Polysaccharides
- **Guduchi polysaccharide fraction (G1-4):** Water-soluble arabinogalactan; primary immunostimulant; activates macrophages and NK cells
- **α-D-glucan:** Anticomplementary and immunopotentiating
- **Crude polysaccharide yield:** 5–8% of stem dry weight

### Other Compounds
- **Syringin:** Phenylpropanoid glycoside; immunomodulatory, anti-arthritic
- **Columbin:** Nor-diterpenoid furanolactone; bitter principle, anti-inflammatory
- **Gossypetin:** Flavonoid; antioxidant
- **β-Sitosterol, daucosterol:** Phytosterols; hypocholesterolemic
- **Fatty acids:** Palmitic, stearic, oleic, linoleic acids in seeds

### Compound Distribution by Plant Part
| Part | Key Compounds | Primary Traditional Use |
|------|--------------|------------------------|
| Stem | Berberine, polysaccharides, syringin, columbin | Immunomodulator, fever |
| Root | Berberine, palmatine, jatrorrhizine | Antidiabetic, anti-diarrheal |

## Pharmacology and Medicinal Uses

### Immunomodulation (Best-Evidenced)
- Stem aqueous extract significantly increases neutrophil phagocytosis and macrophage activation in clinical trials
- G1-4 polysaccharide fraction enhances antibody production and delayed-type hypersensitivity in animal models
- Used as adjunct therapy in tuberculosis, HIV, and post-chemotherapy immune recovery
- **Evidence level:** Multiple human clinical trials (Phase II-III), Cochrane review on allergic rhinitis

### Anti-Diabetic Activity
- Stem extract reduces fasting blood glucose 15–25% in human trials (500 mg 3×/day, 8 weeks)
- Berberine activates AMPK pathway, improves insulin sensitivity
- Comparable glycemic control to metformin in mild-to-moderate Type 2 diabetes (small trials)
- **⚠️ Monitor blood glucose closely when combining with oral hypoglycemics**

### Hepatoprotection
- Stem extract protects against CCl₄, paracetamol, and alcohol-induced hepatotoxicity in rodents
- Reduces elevated ALT, AST, and bilirubin; restores glutathione levels
- Used in Ayurvedic liver formulations (Liv.52 contains *T. cordifolia*)

### Anti-Arthritis and Anti-Inflammatory
- Rodent models: reduces carrageenan-induced paw edema 40–60% at 400 mg/kg
- Clinical improvement in rheumatoid arthritis (reduced ESR, joint pain, morning stiffness)
- Syringin identified as primary anti-inflammatory constituent

### Respiratory and Allergic Conditions
- Cochrane systematic review (2014): significant improvement in allergic rhinitis symptoms vs. placebo
- Reduces nasal discharge, sneezing, and nasal obstruction scores
- Immunomodulatory mechanism: shifts Th2-dominant response toward Th1 balance

### Traditional Indications (Ayurveda)
- **Jvara (fever):** Primary herb for all types; used in malaria, dengue, and chronic low-grade fever
- **Prameha (diabetes):** Root decoction; one of the most important anti-diabetic herbs
- **Vatarakta (gout):** Anti-inflammatory and uricosuric
- **Kustha (skin diseases):** Blood-purifying, anti-dermatitic
- **Agnimandya (digestive weakness):** Bitter tonic stimulates digestive fire (Agni)
- **Rasayana (rejuvenation):** Daily use as general vitality tonic

## Cultivation

### Growing Conditions
- **Climate:** Tropical to subtropical; 15–40°C; frost-sensitive; annual rainfall 750–2500 mm
- **Soil:** Well-drained loamy to sandy loam; pH 6.0–8.0; tolerates poor soils
- **Support:** Requires trellis, trees, or pergola for climbing; stems trail on ground without support
- **Propagation:** Stem cuttings (15–20 cm, 2–3 nodes), planted during monsoon season
- **Spacing:** 1.5–2 m between plants along support structures

### Growth and Harvest
- **Growth rate:** Rapid; 3–5 m of vine growth per year under favorable conditions
- **First harvest:** 12–18 months after planting
- **Stem yield:** 2–3 tonnes fresh per hectare per year; 400–600 kg dry
- **Harvest method:** Cut mature stems at ground level; plant regenerates from base
- **Processing:** Stems cut into 5–10 cm pieces, shade-dried, powdered
- **Storage:** Dried stem powder stable 12–18 months in airtight containers

### Permaculture Applications
- Excellent multipurpose climber for food forest canopy edges
- Provides fodder (leaves palatable to goats and cattle)
- Nitrogen contribution moderate; leaf litter improves soil organic matter
- Medicinal crop with high market value in Ayurvedic herb trade
- Can be intercropped with mango, neem, or moringa as support trees
