## Overview

Tinospora cordifolia, known as Giloy or Guduchi in Ayurveda, is a large
deciduous climbing shrub in the family Menispermaceae. It is one of the most
important rasayana (rejuvenating) herbs in traditional Indian medicine.

The species is native to the Indian subcontinent and [[fukuoka-natural-farming-in-tropical-regions]] of Asia
and Africa. In classical Ayurvedic texts, it is classified as *medhya rasayana*
(brain tonic) and is used to treat a wide range of conditions including fever,
diabetes, and immune dysfunction.

Modern pharmacological research has validated several traditional uses,
particularly its immunomodulatory, anti-diabetic, and hepatoprotective
activities. The stem is the primary plant part used medicinally.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:** Kingdom Plantae → Division Magnoliophyta → Class
Magnoliopsida → Order Ranunculales → Family Menispermaceae → Genus
*Tinospora* → Species *T. cordifolia*.

**Authority:** (Willd.) Miers (1851). Basionym: *Menispermum cordifolium*
Willd. (1794). Synonyms include *Cocculus cordifolius* DC. and *Tinospora
tinosporoides* DC.

The plant is a glabrous, succulent, perennial climber that can grow up to
15 meters. Stems are fleshy, green or grayish-green, with prominent nodes
giving them a jointed, chain-like appearance. The bark exudes a milky latex
when cut.

Leaves are simple, alternate, cordate (heart-shaped) at the base, 8–15 cm
long and 5–12 cm broad. The upper surface is smooth and dark green; the lower
surface is paler with distinct venation. Petioles are 5–10 cm long.

Flowers are unisexual, small, greenish-yellow, arranged in axillary racemes.
Male flowers have 6 sepals, 6 stamens, and no petals. Female flowers have 6
sepals, 6 staminodes, and a single-celled ovary. The fruit is a drupe, red
when ripe, containing a single curved seed.

## Distribution and Habitat

*T. cordifolia* is native to India, Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Bangladesh, and
southern China. It has been introduced to tropical Africa and parts of
[[psilocybe-southeast-asia-pacific]]. In India, it is found from the Himalayan foothills to the
southern peninsula.

The species grows in a wide range of habitats from tropical semi-evergreen to
deciduous forests, typically at elevations up to 1200 meters. It is commonly
found climbing on trees along forest edges, in disturbed areas, and near
watercourses.

Propagation is primarily through stem cuttings, which root readily in moist
soil during the monsoon season. The plant is drought-tolerant once established
but grows most vigorously with consistent moisture and partial shade.

## Bioactive Compounds

The stem contains a diverse array of bioactive constituents. Major chemical
classes include diterpenoid lactones, sesquiterpenoids, alkaloids,
polysaccharides, and [[aact-tannins-terpenes-phenolic-compounds-wood-composting-ingham]].

Key diterpenoid lactones include tinosporide, tinosporic acid, columbin, and
tinosporon. The clerodane furano-diterpenes are considered the primary
bioactive compounds. Sesquiterpenoids include tinosporone and syringin.

magnoflorine, and tembetarine. Berberine is notable for its well-documented
anti-diabetic and [[mycelium-running-mushroom-antimicrobial-properties-species-chart]].
