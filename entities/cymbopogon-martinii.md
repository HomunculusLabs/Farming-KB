## Cymbopogon martinii

## Overview

**Cymbopogon martinii** (Roxb.) Wats., known as **palmarosa** or **Indian geranium**, is a perennial aromatic grass in the family Poaceae.

Native to the Indian subcontinent, it is cultivated commercially for its essential oil, which is rich in **[[savory-perennial-grass-and-desertification]] growing 1.5–3.0 m tall with a short, stout rhizome.

Leaves are linear-lanceolate, 30–80 cm long and 1–1.5 cm wide, glaucous-green, with a strong aromatic scent when crushed.

Inflorescence is a large, nodding panicle, 30–60 cm long, composed of paired spikelets. The panicle is purplish when young, becoming straw-colored at maturity.

Root system is fibrous and superficial, concentrated in the top 30 cm of soil.

## Distribution and Habitat

Native to India, Nepal, and Pakistan, growing wild in grasslands and open forests.

Currently cultivated across tropical and subtropical regions worldwide, including India (Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, Karnataka), Brazil, Indonesia, Madagascar, and the Comoros Islands.

Thrives in warm, humid conditions with 1,000–2,500 mm annual rainfall and temperatures of 20–35°C.

Adaptable to a range of soil types from sandy loams to heavy clays, but performs best on well-drained, fertile loams with pH 6.0–8.0.

## Essential Oil Composition

Palmarosa oil is steam-distilled from the aerial parts (primarily flowering tops). Typical yield is 0.5–1.5% fresh weight.

The principal constituent of var. **motia** oil is **geraniol** (75–90%), with supporting compounds including:

- **Geranyl acetate** (5–15%)
- **limonene**, **farnesol**, **cis-β-ocimene** (trace amounts)

Var. **sofia** oil is dominated by **geranyl acetate** (70–80%) rather than free geraniol.

Oil quality varies with harvest stage — geraniol content peaks just before full flowering and declines as the plant matures.

GC-MS analysis typically identifies 30–50 minor components that contribute to the oil's characteristic sweet, rosy-herbaceous aroma.

## Pharmacology and Traditional Uses

### Antimicrobial Activity

Palmarosa oil demonstrates broad-spectrum antimicrobial activity in vitro:

- Effective against Gram-positive bacteria (*[[candida-albicans]]* at MIC values of 0.25–1.0 mg/mL

### Skin and Wound Healing

Geraniol accelerates wound contraction and collagen deposition in rat excision wound models.

The oil has been used traditionally for acne, dermatitis, and minor skin infections. It is classified as generally recognized as safe (GRAS) for topical use.
