## Channel Catfish (Ictalurus punctatus)

## Description

The channel catfish is a North American freshwater fish species widely regarded as one of the most important aquaculture species in temperate regions. An omnivorous bottom-feeder, it is central to permaculture pond polyculture systems due to its efficient food conversion, tolerance of dense stocking, and high market value.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Animalia
- **Phylum**: Chordata
- **Class**: Actinopterygii (ray-finned fishes)
- **Order**: Siluriformes (catfishes)
- **Family**: Ictaluridae (North American catfishes)
- **Genus**: Ictalurus
- **Species**: I. punctatus

## Key Facts

- Native to North America, found throughout the central and eastern United States and northern Mexico
- Omnivorous diet: consumes insects, crustaceans, plant material, and commercial pelleted feeds
- Food conversion efficiency of approximately 1.7:1 on pelleted diets, making it highly efficient
- Stocking rates typically range from 2,500-4,400 fish per hectare in pond culture
- Grows well in warm water conditions above 16 degrees C
- Can reach market size (0.5-1.5 kg) in 12-18 months depending on climate and feeding regime
- Tolerant of low oxygen conditions compared to many other cultured species

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

Mollison highlights channel catfish as a cornerstone species in freshwater aquaculture polycultures. Key permaculture applications include:

### Polyculture Integration
- **With Tilapia**: Channel catfish (4,400/ha) stocked with Tilapia (1,250/ha) achieve yields of 1,834 kg/ha, compared to 1,400 kg/ha for catfish alone -- a 31% increase. Critically, catfish food conversion (1.7:1) is unaffected by the presence of Tilapia, demonstrating true polyculture complementarity
- **Food reversal effect**: In fertilised ponds without supplementary feeding, bluegill outproduce catfish; however, when pelleted food is provided, catfish dramatically outperform bluegill, reversing the yield order

### Yield Performance
- Alone at 4,400/ha: 1,400 kg/ha
- With supplementary Tilapia: 1,834 kg/ha
- In fertilised ponds, yields depend heavily on the food management strategy employed

### Stock Management
- Can be bred in ponds or obtained as fingerlings from hatcheries
- Compatible with predator species for population control
- [[brown-bullhead]], a related species, ceases breeding above 7,500 fish/ha -- a useful crowding-based population control mechanism applicable to catfish management

## Cultivation Notes

- Optimal water temperature: 24-30 degrees C for growth; feeding ceases below 16 degrees C
- Requires clay-based or sealed ponds for reliable production
- Susceptible to overcrowding stress and waste accumulation at very high densities
- Commonly raised in the "ruled-up" rectilinear pond systems of the southern United States

## Selection and Breeding

Mollison emphasises that selection within species can produce efficiency gains greater than the differences between species. Fast-growing catfish strains selected from high-density fingerling populations show dramatically improved food conversion ratios. This selective breeding approach is critical for optimising permaculture pond productivity.

In traditional flatland systems, rice farmers converting to catfish production often develop the "ruled-up" rectilinear pond system that Mollison describes as visible from airliners as networks of precise regularity. While easy to understand and manage, these monocultural systems depend heavily on external food inputs and lack the resilience and interest of more diverse polyculture arrangements.

## Traditional Integration

In the American South, channel catfish have been raised in combination with rice paddies, where the flooded fields provide both fish habitat and rice cultivation. The fish contribute to pest control and nutrient cycling within the rice crop, while the rice provides shade and forage habitat.
