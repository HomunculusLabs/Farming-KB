Boletales). It is easily recognized by its viscid, wine-red to orange-brown
cap and its decurrent, grayish-purple gills that develop a distinctive
cinnamon-brown color at maturity. The species is widely distributed across
coniferous forests of the Northern Hemisphere and is commercially collected
in parts of China and eastern Europe.

Despite its colloquial grouping with "chanterelles" in some regional
foraging traditions, *C. rutilus* is not closely related to true chanterelles
(Cantharellaceae) but is instead allied with boletes and gomphus-like fungi.
Its edibility is well-established, though it is considered a secondary
culinary species owing to its soft, watery texture when cooked.

### Macroscopic Features

- **Cap:** 3–10 cm diameter, broadly convex to plano-convex, often with a
  shallow central depression; surface smooth and viscid when wet, deep
  wine-red to brick-red fading to orange-brown with age; margin incurved
  in young specimens
- **Gills:** Decurrent, waxy, subdistant; initially pallid gray-violet
  darkening through smoky gray to cinnamon-brown as spores mature; gill
  edges sometimes slightly ragged
- **Stipe:** 4–10 cm × 0.8–2 cm, cylindrical or slightly clavate, solid;
  surface dry, fibrillose, pale yellow at apex grading to wine-red below;
  often with a whitish basal mycelium
- **Flesh:** Soft, watery, pale yellowish-orange; odor mild or slightly
  fruity; taste mild
