## Overview

*Chroogomphus rutilus*, commonly known as the pine spike or rusty spike, is
an edible ectomycorrhizal mushroom in the family Gomphidiaceae (order
Boletales). It is easily recognized by its viscid, wine-red to orange-brown
cap and its decurrent, grayish-purple gills that develop a distinctive
cinnamon-brown color at maturity. The species is widely distributed across
coniferous forests of the Northern Hemisphere and is commercially collected
in parts of China and eastern Europe.

Despite its colloquial grouping with "chanterelles" in some regional
foraging traditions, *C. rutilus* is not closely related to true chanterelles
(Cantharellaceae) but is instead allied with boletes and gomphus-like fungi.
Its edibility is well-established, though it is considered a secondary
culinary species owing to its soft, watery texture when cooked.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Boletales
- **Family:** Gomphidiaceae
- **Genus:** *Chroogomphus*
- **Species:** *C. rutilus* (Schaeff.) O.K. Mill.

**Synonyms:** *Gomphidius rutilus* (Schaeff.) Lundell & Nannf.;
*Gomphidius viscidus* (L.) Fr.; *Agaricus rutilus* Schaeff.

### Macroscopic Features

- **Cap:** 3–10 cm diameter, broadly convex to plano-convex, often with a
  shallow central depression; surface smooth and viscid when wet, deep
  wine-red to brick-red fading to orange-brown with age; margin incurved
  in young specimens
- **Gills:** Decurrent, waxy, subdistant; initially pallid gray-violet
  darkening through smoky gray to cinnamon-brown as spores mature; gill
  edges sometimes slightly ragged
- **Stipe:** 4–10 cm × 0.8–2 cm, cylindrical or slightly clavate, solid;
  surface dry, fibrillose, pale yellow at apex grading to wine-red below;
  often with a whitish basal mycelium
- **Flesh:** Soft, watery, pale yellowish-orange; odor mild or slightly
  fruity; taste mild
- **Spore print:** Cinnamon-brown to dark umber

### Microscopic Features

- **Basidiospores:** 17–22 × 5.5–7 µm, fusoid, elongated, smooth,
  thick-walled, brownish in KOH
- **Basidia:** 4-spored, clavate
- **Cystidia:** Cheilocystidia present as cylindrical to ventricose cells;
  pleurocystidia absent or sparse

The exceptionally long, narrow spores are diagnostic for the genus and
readily distinguish *Chroogomphus* from all gilled mushrooms outside
Gomphidiaceae.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Chroogomphus rutilus* is found across temperate and boreal coniferous
forests of the Northern Hemisphere, with documented collections from:

- Europe (Scandinavia to the Mediterranean)
- Asia (China, Japan, Korea, Siberia)
- North America (primarily northeastern and western regions)

It forms obligate ectomycorrhizal associations with conifers, particularly
two-needle pines (*Pinus sylvestris*, *P. densiflora*, *P. resinosa*,
*P. ponderosa*). Fruiting occurs from late summer through autumn,
typically in acidic soils under mature pine stands. The species fruits
solitarily or in scattered groups and is relatively common in its
preferred habitat.

## Edibility and Culinary Use

*Chroogomphus rutilus* is edible and widely consumed in China, where it
is commercially harvested and sold in markets under the name "松蘑"
(song mo, "pine mushroom"). It is also collected for food in parts of
eastern Europe and Russia.

The flesh is soft and can become watery when cooked, which limits its
culinary appeal compared to firmer mushroom species. It is typically
prepared by sautéing, drying, or inclusion in soups and stews. Drying
concentrates its flavor and improves texture. The species has no known
toxic look-alikes within its range, though beginning foragers should
note that some toxic species in the genus *Chroogomphus* exist in
tropical regions.

## Nutritional and Bioactive Properties

Analysis of *C. rutilus* fruiting bodies reveals:

- **Protein:** 15–20% dry weight (relatively high for a wild mushroom)
- **Carbohydrates:** 50–60% dry weight
- **Fat:** 3–5% dry weight
- **Minerals:** Rich in potassium, phosphorus, and magnesium; contains
  notable amounts of zinc and selenium
- **Vitamins:** Contains B-group vitamins including thiamine, riboflavin,
  and niacin

Extracts of *C. rutilus* have demonstrated moderate antioxidant activity
in DPPH and ABTS radical scavenging assays, attributed to phenolic
compounds and carotenoid pigments. Polysaccharide fractions isolated from
the species show immunomodulatory activity in murine macrophage models.

## Ecological Role

As an obligate ectomycorrhizal symbiont, *C. rutilus* plays a role in
[[comparison-nutrient-cycling-vs-nutrient-dense-gardening]] within pine forest ecosystems. The mycorrhizal
association enhances host tree uptake of phosphorus and nitrogen,
while the fungus receives photosynthetic carbon from the host.

The species may serve as a bridging host for [[bloomfield-fungal-parasites-of-insects]]. Specimens
are frequently parasitized by *Chlorophyllum rhacodes* or by the bolete
parasite *Asterophora lycoperdoides*, though this is more commonly
observed in related *Chroogomphus* species.
