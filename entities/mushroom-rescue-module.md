## Description

The Mushroom Rescue Module is a low-cost, transportable emergency food production system designed by Tradd Cotter to provide rapid, sustainable protein generation in disaster-affected areas. For an estimated cost of $50, a single module contains everything needed to cultivate edible [[cultivation-of-oyster-mushrooms]], with the potential to scale to thousands of pounds of mushrooms within eleven weeks. Beyond food production, the modules can also filter contaminated water and reduce mosquito populations (and thereby malaria transmission) using integrated biological traps.

## Classification

- **Category**: Integrated cultivation system / Humanitarian technology
- **Designed by**: Tradd Cotter, Mushroom Mountain
- **Cost**: ~$50 per module
- **Yield potential**: 7,560 lbs of mushrooms in 11 weeks (with 20% contamination losses)
- **Primary species**: Oyster mushrooms (*Pleurotus* spp.)

## Module Contents

1. **Fruiting containers**: 20 plastic 5-gallon pots (~$1 each from nurseries)
2. **Growing medium**: 15 lbs dried, chopped straw
3. **Spawn**: 5 lbs oyster mushroom sawdust spawn
   - Tropical: pink, golden, phoenix oysters
   - Temperate/cold: cold blue, elm, brown oyster
4. **Fuel stick**: Compressed fuel log for heat-treating straw (4–6 hour burn)
5. **Waterproof matches**: Self-striking type
6. **Clear plastic sheeting**: 12 ft × 12 ft for humidity tents
7. **Rope**: 50 ft for suspending plastic sheeting
8. **5-gallon bucket**: Side-holed, for mosquito trapping (optional)
9. **Bti culture**: Dried *[[cervantes-bacillus-thuringiensis-bt-control]]* var. *israelensis* for larvicide

## Week-by-Week Deployment

- **Week 1**: Inoculate ~5 gallons of substrate with 1 lb spawn; create mother culture at 65–85°F (18–29.5°C)
- **Week 2**: Expand 10× by mixing colonized substrate with fresh soaked medium to fill 10 additional pots
- **Week 3**: Create 10 master spawn pots from each colonized pot; allow some to fruit for immediate food
- **Week 4**: 100 expansion pots; save best pot as new master spawn; use rest to inoculate fruiting containers
- **Weeks 8–11**: Mushrooms emerge ~1 month after fruiting containers prepared; average 3 lbs per fruiting pot

## Scaling Mathematics

Assuming 20% contamination losses at each stage:
- 8 master spawn pots (from 10 attempted)
- Each produces 8 expansion pots → 63 total expansion pots
- Each expansion pot inoculates 30 fruiting containers → 24 successful each (80%)
- 63 × 24 × 5 gallons = 7,560 lbs of [[palmer-edible-mushrooms-north-america]] in 11 weeks
- Held-back master spawn perpetuates the cycle indefinitely

## Secondary Benefits

### Mosquito Control
- Oyster mushrooms produce heat, CO₂, and octenol — chemicals mimicking human breath and sweat
- Mosquito traps built from 5-gallon buckets with side holes, Bti-laced water, and mycelium chunks
- CO₂ from mycelium attracts mosquitoes; females lay eggs in Bti water; larvae die from larvicide
- Alternative: diluted apple cider vinegar + liquid soap (1 tsp soap per cup vinegar)

### Water Purification
- Contaminated water percolated through mushroom biomass can filter biological and chemical pollutants
- Potential to remove *E. coli*, coliforms, and cholera-causing bacteria
- Chemical pollutants require multiple passes through mushroom media
- Requires testing by qualified microbiologist to verify filter efficiency

## Food Security Applications

- Mushrooms are 20–30% protein (dry weight)
- Over 14 weeks, oyster mushrooms produce almost double the protein of free-range chickens
- Excess can be sun-dried or solar-dehydrated for indefinite storage
- Powdered mushrooms become protein-rich flour for breads and pastries
- Module can be illustrated in pictures for non-literate users

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

The Mushroom Rescue Module represents one of the most compelling applications of [[mushroom-cultivation-technology-and-world-production]] for humanitarian purposes. It demonstrates that mushroom cultivation can be a genuine solution to food insecurity in crisis situations, with cascading benefits for water purification [[query-how-to-make-and-use-plant-based-foliar-sprays-for-pest-and-disease-prevention]]. The module's scalability from a single $50 kit to thousands of pounds of food production within weeks makes it an extraordinary example of biological leverage. Cotter tested these modules at his farm and proposed them for deployment in post-disaster contexts such as post-earthquake Haiti (2010).
