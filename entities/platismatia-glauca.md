## Platismatia glauca

## Description

*Platismatia glauca* is a **foliose (leaf-like) lichen** in the family Parmeliaceae, order Lecanorales. It forms large, flattened, grey-green to bluish-grey rosettes (5–15 cm) on tree bark and occasionally rock surfaces in moist forests. The thallus is broadly lobed with a distinctive whitish, glaucous (waxy bloom) surface. The lower surface is black with simple rhizines for attachment. This species is widely distributed in temperate and boreal forests of the Northern Hemisphere, particularly in coastal and montane regions with high humidity.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Phylum**: Ascomycota
- **Class**: Lecanoromycetes

## Key Facts

- Identified as one of the **less tolerant** epiphytic lichen species to heavy metal exposure near a copper and nickel smelter in Finland (Fritze et al., 1989).
- Showed greater sensitivity to heavy metals than *[[hypogymnia-physodes]]* and *[[pseudevernia-furfuracea]]* in the same study.
- Alongside *[[usnea-hirta]]* and *[[bryoria-fuscescens]]*, *P. glauca* was among the first lichens to disappear near the pollution source (within 500 m of the smelter).
- Epiphytic lichens were completely absent within 500 m of the smelter, replaced by free-living algae.
- Fungal hyphal length and [[soil-respiration]] increased significantly only beyond 10 km from the smelter, demonstrating the extensive zone of ecological impact.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

*Platismatia glauca* is ecologically important in forest canopy ecosystems:

- **Pollution sensitivity**: Its high sensitivity to heavy metals and industrial emissions makes it a valuable **bioindicator** for environmental monitoring, particularly for detecting early-stage ecosystem degradation.
- **Forest health assessment**: As a canopy-dwelling lichen, *P. glauca* reflects atmospheric conditions and can be used to assess the impact of industrial activities on forest health.
- **Biodiversity indicator**: The presence of sensitive species like *P. glauca* indicates relatively pristine air quality, while their absence in otherwise suitable habitat signals environmental stress.
- **Community ecology**: The differential tolerance of lichen species creates predictable community zonation patterns around pollution sources, useful for mapping contamination gradients.
- **Lichen conservation**: Understanding species-specific pollution tolerances aids in setting air quality standards for lichen conservation in protected areas.

The contrasting tolerance levels among epiphytic lichens — from highly tolerant (*Hypogymnia physodes*) to highly sensitive (*Platismatia glauca*, *Usnea hirta*) — provides a powerful tool for assessing the degree of environmental contamination and tracking ecosystem recovery following pollution abatement.
