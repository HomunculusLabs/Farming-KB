# DOBU

**Chemical name:** 2,5-Dimethoxy-4-(n)-butylamphetamine
**PiHKAL entry:** #63

## Overview

DOBU is a member of the [[2c-family-compounds-overview]] of psychedelic amphetamines, carrying a four-carbon butyl chain at the 4-position. It is one of the longer-chain members of the series and exhibits markedly different properties from shorter-chain analogues like dom-entity|DOM and [[doet-compound-profile]], with very slow onset, extremely long duration, and primarily stimulant-like effects at the doses tested.

## Dosage

| Level | Amount (mg) |
|-------|-------------|
| Threshold | ~2.2 |
| Tested range | 2.2 - 2.8 |

**Note:** An exact dosage range cannot be given due to the compound's unusual pharmacokinetics. Shulgin advises: "Go up slowly."

## Duration

Very long. At 2.2 mg, onset occurred around the 4th hour and sleep was impossible until some 18 hours after administration. At 2.8 mg, effects persisted into the following day.

## Effects

The qualitative reports describe an experience markedly different from typical DOx psychedelics:

- **At 2.2 mg:** Almost nothing for nearly 4 hours, then an "increasing manic intoxication, winding up tighter and tighter." Paresthesia was present but no mydriasis (pupil dilation). Assessed as "might be a stimulant, but not a psychedelic, at least at this level."
- **At 2.8 mg:** Nothing for over 7 hours, then irritability and shortness of temper. Mentally clear but no more alert than usual. No sleep that evening. The next day brought "a feeling of overall depression" -- possibly due to sleep deprivation rather than direct pharmacological effects.

## Safety Concerns

The extremely slow onset (4-7+ hours before noticeable effects) creates a significant dosing hazard. Users might reasonably conclude the compound is inactive and take additional doses before the first dose has taken effect, potentially leading to dangerous overdose situations. The very long duration (18+ hours) compounds this risk.

## Synthesis Notes

The synthesis follows the standard DOx pattern:

1. **Friedel-Crafts acylation:** p-Dimethoxybenzene is acylated with butyryl chloride using AlCl3 in CH2Cl2 to give 2,5-dimethoxybutyrophenone (~85% pure by GC). An alternative route using polyphosphoric acid gave lower yields.
2. **Clemmensen reduction:** Amalgamated zinc in HCl reduces the ketone to 2,5-dimethoxy-(n)-butylbenzene (~90% pure).
3. **Vilsmeier-Haack formylation:** POCl3 + N-methylformanilide gives 2,5-dimethoxy-4-(n)-butylbenzaldehyde (mp 47-48 deg C).
4. **Nitrostyrene formation:** Condensation with nitroethane in acetic acid yields the nitropropene (mp 54-56 deg C).
5. **LAH reduction:** Using a Soxhlet condenser provides the hydrochloride salt (mp 151-152 deg C from acetonitrile).

## Significance

DOBU illustrates a critical principle in [[shulgin-dosage-guidelines]]: slow-onset compounds are among the most dangerous because they tempt re-dosing before the first dose has taken effect. Shulgin's warning to "go up slowly" with DOBU reflects this fundamental safety concern. The compound also demonstrates that extending the alkyl chain progressively shifts the character from psychedelic toward stimulant.

## The "Ten Classics" Research Program

DOBU was part of what Shulgin called his "Ten Classics" -- a systematic series of compounds exploring the 4-position alkyl chain on the 2,5-dimethoxyamphetamine skeleton. The series included dom-entity|DOM (methyl), [[doet-compound-profile]] (ethyl), dopr-entity|DOPR (propyl), DOBU (butyl), and doam-entity|DOAM (amyl), along with the halogenated members dob-entity|DOB (bromo), [[doc-compound-profile]] (chloro), doi-entity|DOI (iodo), and don-entity|DON (nitro).

This systematic approach, varying one substituent while keeping the rest of the molecule constant, exemplifies the [[permaculture-design-course-methodology]] that Shulgin applied throughout his career. The resulting data set provides one of the most complete structure-activity relationship maps for any class of psychoactive compounds. The slow-onset, long-duration character of the higher homologues like DOBU was not predicted in advance but emerged clearly from the systematic data, demonstrating the value of this approach.
