## Entoloma lividum

## Description

*Entoloma lividum* (syn. *Rhodocybe trabalis*; also known as *[[entoloma-sinuatum]]*, the livid pinkgill or lead poisoner) is a large agaricoid fungus in the family Entolomataceae. It is one of the most poisonous mushrooms in Europe, responsible for numerous poisonings due to its superficial resemblance to edible species. The fruiting bodies appear in autumn in deciduous forests, particularly under oaks and beeches.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Entolomataceae
- **Genus:** *Entoloma*
- **Species:** *E. lividum*

## Key Facts

- Found to contain inorganic arsenic, the most toxic form of arsenic, in its fruiting bodies (Byrne et al., 1995).
- Unlike some other arsenic-accumulating fungi that transform arsenic through methylation, *E. lividum* does not appear to methylate arsenic, retaining it in the highly toxic inorganic form.
- This lack of arsenic methylation contrasts with species like *[[sarcosphaera-coronaria]]* (which produces methylarsonic acid) and *[[laccaria-amethystina]]* (which produces dimethylarsenic acid).
- Despite its toxicity to humans, the species is widespread in European deciduous forests.
- Responsible for gastrointestinal poisoning that can be severe, earning it the common name "lead poisoner."

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

*Entoloma lividum* is primarily of concern as a poisonous species that is frequently confused with edible mushrooms. Its arsenic profile (containing only inorganic arsenic without methylation products) provides insight into the diversity of fungal arsenic metabolism. This species is an important cautionary example in mycological education and foraging guides.

## Ecological Role

- Saprotrophic decomposer in deciduous forest soils
- Participates in arsenic cycling, retaining inorganic forms without transformation
- Poisonous species contributing to forest ecosystem dynamics through mycophagy avoidance by some animals
- Indicator species for mature deciduous forest habitats
