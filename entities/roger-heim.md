# Roger Heim

**Roger Jean Heim** (1900–1979) was a French mycologist and botanist who served as director of the **Muséum national d'Histoire naturelle** in Paris from 1951 to 1965. He is best known for his taxonomic work on mushrooms and his pivotal role in identifying the [[psilocybe-southeast-asia-pacific]]
- **Psychoactive Agaricales** — his most famous contribution

## Role in Psilocybin Discovery

Heim's collaboration with **R. Gordon Wasson** was transformative. In 1956, Wasson participated in a **velada** (night ceremony) with Mazatec curandera **[[medicinal-mushroom-guide-agaricus-blazei]]
- **"Les Champignons toxiques et hallucinogènes"** (1963)
- **"Champignons toxiques et hallucinogènes du Mexique"** — with Wasson (1958)
- Numerous papers in the *Revue de Mycologie* and *Comptes Rendus de l'Académie des Sciences*

## Biography and Career
Roger Jean Heim was born on February 12, 1900, in Paris, France. He
developed an interest in natural history early in life and pursued
botanical and mycological studies at the Sorbonne. He earned his
doctorate and quickly established himself as one of France's leading
mycologists. During World War II, Heim served as director of the
Muséum national d'Histoire naturelle under the difficult conditions
of the German occupation, maintaining the institution's scientific
integrity. After the war, he continued as director from 1951 to 1965.
Heim was elected to the French Academy of Sciences in 1964, one of
the highest scientific honors in France. He continued active research
until his death on September 17, 1979, in Paris.

## Influence and Legacy
Heim's taxonomic work established the classification framework for
psychoactive mushrooms that remains the basis for modern Psilocybe
taxonomy. His specimens at the Muséum national d'Histoire naturelle
in Paris are irreplaceable reference material. The species Psilocybe
heimii, named in his honor by Gaston Guzmán, recognizes his
foundational contributions. His collaboration with Wasson and Hofmann
created the chain of events — from Mazatec velada to Parisian
laboratory to Swiss pharmaceutical synthesis — that brought psilocybin
to the attention of Western science and, ultimately, the world.

## Key Ideas and Philosophy
- **Taxonomy as foundation:** Heim believed that accurate species
  identification must precede any chemical or pharmacological
  investigation — a principle that guided his meticulous descriptive
  work on psychoactive Agaricales
- **Interdisciplinary collaboration:** His partnership with Wasson
  (ethnomycologist) and Hofmann (chemist) exemplified his belief that
  understanding required bridging taxonomy, cultural anthropology,
  and chemistry
- **Institutional science as stewardship:** As museum director, Heim
  saw scientific collections as humanity's shared heritage, maintaining
  specimens and documentation for future generations of researchers
