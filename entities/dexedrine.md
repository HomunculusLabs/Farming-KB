# Dexedrine

## Description

Dexedrine (dextroamphetamine) is a central nervous system stimulant of the amphetamine class. In the context of Leary's "The Psychedelic Experience," Dexedrine is mentioned as an optional adjunct that can be used to "break the set" if a person becomes trapped in a repetitive game-routine during a psychedelic session. A dose of 25 mg is cited as potentially sufficient to interrupt a stuck pattern, though Leary emphasizes such additional dosages should only be given with the person's own knowledge and consent.

## Classification

- **Category**: Chemical — Pharmaceutical Stimulant
- **Drug Class**: Amphetamine / CNS Stimulant
- **Chemical Name**: Dextroamphetamine sulfate
- **DEA Schedule**: Schedule II (US)

## Key Facts

- Brand name for dextroamphetamine, the more pharmacologically active enantiomer of amphetamine
- Used medically for ADHD, narcolepsy, and occasionally for treatment-resistant depression
- In Leary's framework: 25 mg can be used to break a repetitive stuck pattern during a psychedelic session
- Also used (as [[methedrine]]/methamphetamine) to induce a pleasant, euphoric mood prior to a session
- Leary emphasizes all adjunct substances should only be given with the person's knowledge and consent

## Role in Psychedelic Sessions

Leary describes two potential uses for stimulants during psychedelic sessions:

1. **Pre-session mood induction**: Methedrine (methamphetamine) used to induce a pleasant, euphoric mood prior to the session, especially for anxious participants
2. **Mid-session set-breaking**: 25 mg of Dexedrine (or 50 mg of [[dmt]]) can "break the set" if a person becomes trapped in a repetitive game-routine during the session
3. **Staggered dosing support**: For excessively nervous persons, stimulants may complement a staggered dosing protocol

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Dexedrine is relevant to the broader pharmacological context of psychedelic practice:

- Represents the historical use of pharmaceutical adjuncts in psychedelic sessions — a practice that continues in modern clinical settings with different medications
- The concept of "breaking the set" during a stuck psychedelic experience has modern parallels in psychedelic-assisted therapy approaches
- Understanding the full pharmacological toolkit referenced in historical psychedelic literature provides context for modern [[psilocybin]] session protocols
- Modern clinical psilocybin research generally avoids stimulant adjuncts, preferring proper [[set-and-setting]] preparation instead
