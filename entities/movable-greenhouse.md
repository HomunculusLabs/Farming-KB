# Movable Greenhouse

## Description

A movable greenhouse is a greenhouse structure designed to be physically relocated between two or more field sites, enabling crop rotation without permanent greenhouse infrastructure on any single piece of land. Eliot Coleman developed and refined movable greenhouse designs as a core component of his [[winter-harvest-system]] at [[four-season-farm]] in Harborside, Maine. The concept addresses soil health, crop rotation, and economic efficiency for small-scale commercial growers.

## Classification

- **Category**: Agricultural infrastructure / greenhouse design
- **Type**: Mobile protected-culture structure
- **Scale**: Typically 30-foot-wide by 96-foot-long houses
- **Origin**: Historical European practice, modernized by Coleman

## Key Facts

### Design Evolution

Coleman experimented with four distinct systems for moving hoop houses between alternate sites:

1. **Greased wooden skids**: The first design relied on greased wooden skids sliding on wooden rails
2. **Ball casters on rails**: Rails on the bottom of the structure rolled along ball casters set in the top of support posts
3. **Ground runners**: Runners slid directly along the ground like a sled
4. **Flanged wheels on pipes**: Flanged wheels on the bottom of each hoop rolled along pipes lying on the ground

### Commercial-Scale Design

For serious commercial production, Coleman settled on 30-foot-wide by 96-foot-long houses as the most efficient choice. These large structures are moved using the metal sled-runner design, pulled by a tractor like a large sled. Once in position, the greenhouses are bolted securely to ground anchors.

### Historical Context

The pioneers of movable greenhouses recognized that it was far less work to move the greenhouse to fresh soil than to replace the soil inside a stationary greenhouse. Historically, it was traditional practice to dig soil out down to 16 inches deep and replace it with new field soil when problems arose in stationary greenhouses. Movable greenhouses eliminated this laborious process.

### Soil Health Benefits

Movable greenhouses allow the covered soil to be exposed to the elements when the structure is moved, preventing the salt buildup, pest accumulation, and soil fatigue common in permanent greenhouse structures. Coleman's original cool (non-mobile) house required meticulous attention to soil preparation, compost quality, crop rotation, and irrigation to maintain productivity for fourteen years.

## Relevance to Cultivation

### Crop Rotation Integration

The movable greenhouse enables a rotation where each square foot produces at least three crops per year — two in winter and one or more in summer. A typical rotation might include:
- Winter-harvested carrot crop
- Followed by baby new potatoes planted March 15
- Followed by summer crop of Charantais melons transplanted in late May
- Followed by fall-harvested spinach
- Then moved to cover later-planted spinach for spring harvest

### Overwintered Spinach Cycle

The signature rotation involves mid-September-sown outdoor spinach that is hardy enough to not need protection until summer crops finish in the greenhouse. The house is then moved over the spinach during the second half of October, providing protection through winter and enabling monthly harvests until the spinach bolts in late March. After the final harvest, beds are prepared for early-April transplanting of tomatoes.

### Four-Season Growing

Movable greenhouses make year-round production economically viable for small farms by allowing the same structure to serve multiple functions across seasons — winter cold-house production, summer heat-loving crops, and transitional periods for green manures.

## Design Considerations

- Structures must be light enough to move but sturdy enough to withstand winter weather when anchored
- Ground anchors must be robust and easy to engage/release
- Moving is typically done with a tractor and requires coordination with the crop schedule
- The path between alternate sites must be relatively level and clear

## Relationship to Other Concepts

- **Crop rotation**: Movable greenhouses enable field-scale rotation under cover
- **Cold house / cool house**: The structures function as unheated or minimally heated growing environments
- **Double coverage**: Inner row covers used inside the greenhouse for additional frost protection
- **Green manure**: Summer periods when the greenhouse is moved allow cover cropping of the exposed site
