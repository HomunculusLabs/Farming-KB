## Overview

**Cortinarius violaceus** (L.) Gray, commonly known as the violet webcap, is a striking basidiomycete fungus in the family Cortinariaceae. It is one of the most easily recognized mushrooms in temperate forests due to its deep violet to purple coloration, which persists throughout all stages of development — from the cap and stipe to the gills and even the flesh.

*C. violaceus* is an ectomycorrhizal species found across the Northern Hemisphere, primarily associated with coniferous and broadleaf trees in old-growth and mature forests. It is considered edible, though it is not widely collected for food due to its rarity, slow growth, and the general caution applied to the genus *Cortinarius*, which contains several deadly toxic species.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

### Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Cortinariaceae
- **Genus:** Cortinarius
- **Species:** C. violaceus

The species was originally described by Linnaeus in 1753 as *Agaricus violaceus* and transferred to *Cortinarius* by Samuel Frederick Gray in 1821. It is the type species of the genus *Cortinarius* and is placed in subgenus *Cortinarius*, section *Violacei*. The genus name derives from the Latin *cortina* (curtain), referring to the cobweb-like partial veil (cortina) characteristic of the group.

### Macroscopic Features

**Cap:** 5–15 cm diameter, hemispherical to convex when young, becoming broadly umbonate with age. Surface is covered with dense, fine, matted scales or fibrils that give it a velvety texture. Color is deep violet to dark purple, often darkening with age but never losing its purple hue entirely.

**Gills:** Adnate to slightly sinuate, moderately spaced. Color is deep violet when young, becoming brownish-violet to rust-brown as the spores mature. The cortina (partial veil) is prominent in young specimens, appearing as a cobwebby membrane connecting the cap margin to the stipe.

**Stipe:** 6–12 cm tall, 1–2.5 cm thick, cylindrical to slightly clavate. Surface is covered with the same velvety, fibrillose scales as the cap, colored deep violet. The base may be slightly bulbous. The cortina leaves a faint ring zone on the upper stipe in young specimens, which quickly disappears.

**Flesh:** Violet throughout, more intensely colored near the surface. The flesh is firm and has a mild, pleasant odor variously described as reminiscent of cedar, wet leaves, or iodine. The taste is mild to slightly bitter.

**[[ectomycorrhizal-associations]] primarily with:

- **Conifers:** Spruce (*Picea* spp.), fir (*Abies* spp.), pine (*Pinus* spp.)
- **Broadleaf trees:** Oak (*Quercus* spp.), beech (*Fagus* spp.), birch (*Betula* spp.)

Fruiting occurs from late summer to late autumn (August–November). It prefers moist, acidic soils rich in [[beber-mycelial-network-graph-theory-cord-routing]], enhancing nutrient and water uptake in exchange for photosynthates.

The species is sensitive to habitat disturbance, [[fungi-cultivated-mushrooms-pathogens-competitors]].
