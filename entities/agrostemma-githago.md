# Agrostemma githago (Corncockle)

## Overview

Agrostemma githago L., commonly known as corncockle, is an annual herbaceous plant in the family Caryophyllaceae. It produces large, showy pink to purplish-red flowers with five petals on upright stems reaching 30–100 cm in height.

Once a widespread weed of cereal crops throughout Europe and western Asia, corncockle has declined dramatically due to improved seed cleaning techniques. It is now rare in the wild and is considered a conservation priority in several European countries.

All parts of the plant contain toxic saponins, particularly the seeds, which can contaminate grain harvests. Despite its toxicity, corncockle is valued as an ornamental annual and features prominently in wildflower seed mixtures.

The species is sometimes confused with the unrelated corncockle of the genus Vaccaria (Vaccaria hispanica, formerly Saponaria vaccaria), which shares a similar agricultural habitat and common name. The two can be distinguished by flower size and calyx morphology.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Agrostemma githago is the sole widely cultivated species in the genus Agrostemma, which contains only two species within the tribe Sileneae. The name derives from the Greek "agros" (field) and "stemma" (crown), referring to its crown-like flowers in agricultural fields.

The plant has erect, branched stems covered with long, soft, white hairs. Leaves are linear-lanceolate, 3–8 cm long, arranged in opposite pairs along the stem.

Flowers are solitary and terminal, 3–5 cm in diameter, with five broad petals in shades of pink to purplish-red. Each petal is notched at the tip and marked with a pattern of darker lines radiating from the center.

The calyx is a prominent, elongated tube with five long, narrow lobes that exceed the petals in length. These lobes are green with purple veining and form a striking architectural feature of the flower.

The fruit is an ovoid capsule enclosed within the persistent calyx. Seeds are dull black, reniform, 2–3 mm in diameter, with a distinctive rough, tuberculate surface.

The root system consists of a slender taproot with limited lateral branching. As an annual species, the root system is relatively shallow compared to perennial Caryophyllaceae.

The entire plant is covered with dense, soft, white pubescence. This hairiness gives the foliage a grey-green appearance and may serve as a deterrent to herbivorous insects.

## Distribution and Habitat

Agrostemma githago is native to the Mediterranean region and western Asia. Its historical range expanded dramatically with the spread of cereal agriculture across Europe during the Neolithic period.

The species became a ubiquitous associate of wheat and other cereal crops throughout temperate Europe. It was introduced to [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]] with grain shipments.

Corncockle thrives in disturbed, open habitats with well-drained, neutral to alkaline soils. It was historically confined to arable fields, germinating in spring alongside cereal crops.

The species shows a particular affinity for winter wheat and rye fields. Its germination timing closely matches that of autumn-sown cereals, an adaptation resulting from millennia of coevolution with agriculture.

Livestock are also susceptible to poisoning from contaminated feed. Poultry are particularly sensitive, with seed consumption at levels above 2% of diet causing mortality.

The plant also contains the lectin agrostin, which has been investigated for its mitogenic and potential antitumor properties. This lectin is unrelated to the toxic saponins and does not contribute to the plant's toxicity.
