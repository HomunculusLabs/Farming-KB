# Blighia sapida

## Overview

Blighia sapida, commonly known as ackee, is an evergreen tree in the family Sapindaceae. It is native to West Africa and is the national fruit of Jamaica, where it forms an integral part of the national cuisine.

The fruit is notable for its unusual ripening behavior and toxicity profile. Only the fully opened, properly prepared arils are edible — unripe or improperly processed fruit contains hypoglycin A and B, potent toxins that cause Jamaican vomiting sickness.

Ackee was introduced to the Caribbean in the 18th century, reportedly aboard a slave ship from West Africa. Captain William Bligh brought the species to England in 1793, and the genus was named in his honor.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:** Kingdom Plantae → Order Sapindales → Family Sapindaceae → Genus *Blighia* → Species *B. sapida*.

The species was described by Karl Friedrich von Gaertner. The genus *Blighia* contains three species, with *B. sapida* being the most economically important.

The tree is medium-sized, reaching 10–15 meters in height with a dense, spreading crown. Bark is smooth and grey, becoming slightly fissured with age.

Leaves are pinnately compound, with 4–6 pairs of leaflets per leaf. Each leaflet is elliptic to oblong, 8–15 cm long, dark green and glossy above, paler beneath.

Flowers are unisexual, creamy-white to pinkish, borne in terminal panicles. Male and female flowers are typically on separate trees (dioecious), though some trees produce bisexual flowers.

The fruit is a pear-shaped capsule, 7–10 cm long, turning from green to yellow-orange to bright red upon ripening. The fruit splits open naturally when mature, revealing three glossy black seeds, each surrounded by a white to yellow aril.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Blighia sapida* is native to the Guinean forest-savanna mosaic of West Africa, occurring from Ivory Coast eastward to Ghana, Togo, Benin, and Nigeria. It has been widely introduced throughout the Caribbean and tropical Americas.

In the Caribbean, Jamaica is the primary center of cultivation and consumption. The tree has naturalized in parts of [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-south-america]], and southern Florida.

The species grows at elevations from sea level to approximately 900 meters. It prefers tropical lowland climates with annual rainfall of 900–1,800 mm and mean temperatures of 18–30 degrees Celsius.

Soil requirements are moderately flexible, with trees growing on sandy loams, clay loams, and limestone-derived soils. Optimal growth occurs on deep, well-drained, fertile soils with pH 5.5–7.5.
