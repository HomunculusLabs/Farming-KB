## Description

Lagochiline is a crystalline diterpene compound isolated from [[syrian-rue-turkestan-mint-and-lesser-old-world-hallucinogens]]). It is the best-characterized chemical constituent of this traditionally intoxicating Central Asian plant, though its role as the primary psychoactive principle remains unconfirmed.

## Classification

- **Category**: Diterpene
- **Chemical class**: Diterpenoid
- **Source organism**: [[lagochilus-inebrians]]
- **Physical form**: Crystalline compound

## Key Facts

- Isolated as a crystalline compound from Lagochilus inebrians
- Classified as a diterpene (a type of terpenoid with 20 carbon atoms)
- Its psychoactive properties have not been definitively established
- May or may not be responsible for the intoxicating effects of the whole plant
- The full pharmacological profile has not been extensively studied

## Chemistry

Diterpenes are a class of terpenoid compounds composed of four isoprene units. They are widely distributed in the plant kingdom and include many biologically active compounds. Lagochiline is notable as one of the few diterpenes potentially associated with psychoactive effects in humans, though most hallucinogenic compounds are alkaloids rather than terpenes.

## Relationship to Turkestan Mint

- Found in the leaves of Lagochilus inebrians
- The plant is prepared by toasting leaves gathered in October
- Honey and sugar are added to counteract the intense bitterness
- Whether lagochiline alone accounts for the psychoactivity or if other compounds contribute is unknown
