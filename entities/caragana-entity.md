# Caragana (Siberian Pea Shrub)

## Description

Caragana is a genus of deciduous shrubs and small trees in the pea family
(Fabaceae), most notably represented by [[bill-mollison]]
in his [[permaculture-design-framework]].

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Order:** Fabales
- **Family:** Fabaceae
- **Subfamily:** Faboideae
- **Genus:** Caragana
- **Notable Species:** C. arborescens (Siberian pea shrub)

## Key Facts

- Deciduous shrub or small tree, typically 3-6 metres tall
- Nitrogen-fixing legume, enriching soil through symbiotic rhizobium bacteria
- Produces numerous small yellow pea-like flowers in spring
- Seed pods contain protein-rich seeds (up to 36% protein by weight)
- Extremely cold-hardy, surviving temperatures below -40°C
- Drought-tolerant once established with deep taproot system
- Fast-growing in youth, reaching useful size within 3-5 years
- Thorny branches provide protection for bird nesting sites
- Long-lived, with specimens surviving 50+ years
- Adaptable to a wide pH range (alkaline to neutral soils preferred)

## Relevance to Cultivation

### Poultry Forage

Mollison identifies Caragana as a seed-bearing tree and shrrub suitable for
poultry range. The pea-like seeds fall from pods as they mature and dry,
creating a self-harvesting feed system. Chickens and other poultry eagerly
consume the seeds, which provide a high-protein supplement to their diet.
This makes Caragana an ideal component of orchard-based poultry systems
where birds forage beneath and around the shrubs.

### Nitrogen Fixation

As a leguminous plant, Caragana fixes atmospheric nitrogen through root
nodules, improving soil fertility for neighbouring plants. In permaculture
design, it functions as a "support species" planted among fruit trees and
other crop plants, contributing nitrogen without competing aggressively for
nutrients or water.

### Permaculture Design Roles

- **Poultry forage:** Protein-rich seed drop for free-range chickens
- **Nurse plant:** Provides wind protection and partial shade for young trees
- **Hedgerow species:** Forms dense, thorny barriers useful for livestock
  fencing and windbreaks
- **Soil builder:** Nitrogen fixation benefits adjacent crop plants
- **Erosion control:** Deep root system stabilises slopes and banks

### Companion Planting

Caragana integrates well with other poultry forage species including
Coprosma repens, tagasaste, boxthorn (Lycium spp.), pigeon pea, and
mulberry. In orchard systems it can be interplanted with fruit trees,
providing nitrogen and wind protection while the seed drop feeds poultry
working the orchard floor.

### Cultivation Notes

- Propagates readily from seed; scarification improves germination rates
- Prefers full sun but tolerates light shade
- Tolerates poor, sandy, and saline soils
- Little to no fertiliser required due to nitrogen fixation
- Can be coppiced or pollarded to manage size and encourage dense growth
- Prune in late winter to maintain shape and accessibility for poultry

### Cautions

In some regions, particularly North America, Caragana arborescens has
escaped cultivation and is considered invasive in certain states and
provinces. Check local regulations before planting and manage seed
dispersal in sensitive ecological areas.

## Summary

Caragana, particularly the Siberian pea shrub, is a multifunctional
permaculture plant valued for its nitrogen fixation, extreme hardiness,
and protein-rich seeds that feed poultry. Its role as a self-sustaining
poultry forage species in cold-climate designs makes it indispensable for
integrated orchard-livestock systems in temperate and boreal regions.
