# Arthrobotrys musiformis

**Arthrobotrys musiformis** is a nematophagous ascomycete fungus that captures nematodes using adhesive trapping structures.

It belongs to the monophyletic clade of nematode-trapping fungi within the Orbiliomycetes.

The species has been studied for its potential as a biological control agent against plant-parasitic and animal-parasitic nematodes.

## Taxonomy and Classification

A. musiformis belongs to the order Orbiliales within the class Orbiliomycetes.

Key taxonomic details include:

- **Phylum:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Orbiliomycetes
- **Order:** Orbiliales
- **Family:** Orbiliaceae

The genus name *Arthrobotrys* derives from Greek, meaning "jointed brush," referring to the branching pattern of the trapping structures.

## Phylogenetic Position

Based on 18S rDNA sequences, A. musiformis is firmly placed within the monophyletic clade of nematode-trapping fungi.

This clade forms a distinct lineage among an unresolved cluster of apothecial ascomycetes.

The phylogenetic pattern within the clade is concordant with the morphology of the traps.

Species with similar trapping structures tend to be more closely related.

## Genome and Molecular Tools

Genomic resources for A. musiformis are being developed as part of broader efforts to sequence nematophagous fungi.

Transcriptomic studies have identified genes specifically upregulated during trap formation and nematode infection.

Secreted proteases and lectins are among the key effector molecules involved in the nematode-fungus interaction.

Comparative genomics between trapping and non-trapping Orbiliomycetes is revealing the genetic basis of predatory behaviour.
