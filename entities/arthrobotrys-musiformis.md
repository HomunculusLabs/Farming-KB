## Arthrobotrys musiformis

**Arthrobotrys musiformis** is a nematophagous ascomycete fungus that captures nematodes using adhesive trapping structures.

It belongs to the monophyletic clade of nematode-trapping fungi within the Orbiliomycetes.

The species has been studied for its potential as a [[fungal-ecology]]

[[magnaporthe-grisea]].

Shared upregulated genes include:

- Serine proteases
- Peptidyl-prolyl cis-trans isomerases

These parallels suggest that predatory and [[cordyceps-insect-pathogenic-fungi]] have co-opted similar molecular toolkits for host interaction.

## Genome and Molecular Tools

Genomic resources for A. musiformis are being developed as part of broader efforts to sequence [[nematophagous-fungi]].

Transcriptomic studies have identified genes specifically upregulated during trap formation and nematode infection.

Secreted proteases and lectins are among the key effector molecules involved in the nematode-fungus interaction.

Comparative genomics between trapping and non-trapping Orbiliomycetes is revealing the genetic basis of predatory behaviour.
