## Overview

**Nepenthes thorelii** is a tropical pitcher plant in the family
Nepenthaceae, native to Indochina. It was historically recorded from
Vietnam, Laos, and Cambodia, though wild populations have not been
confirmed since the early 20th century.

The species produces robust climbing stems with two distinct types of
pitcher: lower terrestrial traps and upper aerial pitchers. The pitchers
are narrowly funnel-shaped with a pronounced peristome and conspicuous
wings on the lower traps.

N. thorelii is one of the most enigmatic Nepenthes species due to the
uncertainty surrounding its status in the wild. Cultivated material
purporting to represent the species has circulated widely, though the
provenance of these plants has been debated.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Nepenthes thorelii was described by Marcel G. Lecomte in 1909 based on
specimens collected in southern Vietnam. The specific epithet honors French
botanist Clovis Thorel, who collected extensively in Indochina during the
19th century.

The species belongs to the "N. thorelii aggregate," a group of Indochinese
species that also includes N. anamensis and N. smilesii. Some authorities
have treated N. anamensis as a synonym of N. thorelii, though Cheek and
Jebb (2001) maintained them as distinct.

**Stem:** The climbing stem can reach 3–4 m in length, with internodes
3–8 cm long. The stem is cylindrical, 3–5 mm in diameter, and produces
leaves at regular intervals along its length.

**Leaves:** Leaves are coriaceous, lanceolate to oblong, 15–25 cm long and
3–6 cm wide. The petiole is winged, clasping the stem, and bears an
elongate tendril at its apex that develops into the pitcher trap.

**Lower pitchers:** Terrestrial pitchers are 8–15 cm tall and 3–5 cm wide.
The peristome is cylindrical, 5–8 mm wide, with prominent ribs. The inner
surface is glandular, with a waxy zone preventing insect escape.

**Upper pitchers:** Aerial pitchers are smaller, 6–12 cm tall, and more
infundibular. Wings are reduced to ridges. The lid is ovate and held at an
angle over the pitcher mouth.

## Distribution and Habitat

Nepenthes thorelii was originally collected from several locations in
southern Vietnam, including the vicinity of Saigon (now Ho Chi Minh City)
and the provinces of Bà Rịa-Vũng Tàu and Đồng Nai. Historical records
also exist from eastern Cambodia and southern Laos.

The species grew in open, disturbed habitats including roadside ditches,
savanna grasslands, and secondary forest edges at low elevations
(0–200 m). It was reported from both sandy and lateritic soils.

No confirmed wild populations have been documented since the early 1900s.
The apparent disappearance from the wild is attributed to extensive habitat
destruction from urbanization, agriculture, and military activity during
the Vietnam War.

## Carnivorous Adaptations

Nepenthes thorelii captures prey using pitfall traps formed by its
modified leaf tips. The pitcher contains a pool of digestive fluid at the
base, with the inner wall divided into a waxy zone above and a glandular
zone below.

The waxy zone is covered with microscopic platelets that cause insects to
lose traction and fall into the digestive fluid. The peristome, when wet,
becomes extremely slippery, directing prey toward the pitcher interior.
