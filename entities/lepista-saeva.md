## Overview

*Lepista saeva* is a species of agaric fungus in the family Tricholomataceae, commonly known as the field blewit or tricolor blewit.

It is a widely distributed saprotrophic mushroom found in grasslands, pastures, and woodland edges across Europe and parts of [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]].

The species is highly regarded as an [[bloomfield-boletus-satanus-edible-mushroom-hazards]], valued for its robust flavor and firm texture.

Its name "blewit" derives from the Old French "blewit" meaning blue mushroom, referring to the distinctive bluish tones of the stipe and gills.

## Taxonomy and Classification

Basidiomycota → Agaricomycetes → Agaricales → **Tricholomataceae → *Lepista* → *L. saeva***

The species was originally described as *Agaricus personatus* by Augustin Pyramus de Candolle in 1815.

It was later transferred to *Tricholoma* by Paul Kummer in 1871, then to *Lepista* by Mordecai Cubitt Cooke in 1878.

The epithet *saeva* was applied by Petter Adolf Karsten, and this name has nomenclatural priority in modern treatments.

*L. saeva* is closely related to *[[lepista-nuda]]* (the wood blewit), the two forming a distinct clade within the genus.

Some authorities still use *Clitocybe saeva*, but molecular phylogenetics supports placement in *Lepista*.

## Morphology

**Cap:** 6–12 cm diameter, convex to plano-convex, often with a broad low umbo. Surface is smooth, dry, pale buff to grayish-brown, sometimes with a faint lilac flush when young.

The cap margin is inrolled when young, becoming straight and slightly undulating with age.

**Gills:** Adnexed to sinuate, crowded, pale pinkish-buff to lilac-gray when young. As spores mature, gills become buff to pale salmon-pink.

The gills are fairly thick and have a characteristic decurrent tooth.

**Stipe:** 4–8 cm tall × 1–2.5 cm thick, cylindrical or slightly clavate. Surface is covered in fine longitudinal fibrils, pale lilac-blue at the base, fading to whitish above.

The stipe is solid and firm, with a pale bluish base that is a key identification feature.

**Flesh:** Pale buff to whitish, thick and firm. Odor is strongly fragrant and perfumed, reminiscent of orange peel or violet.

The taste is mild to slightly peppery, with a pleasant aromatic quality.

**Spores:** Pinkish-buff [[boil-a-bag-spore-print-technique-jarrold]]. Basidiospores are broadly ellipsoid, 6–8.5 × 4–5 μm, with ornamented warty surface, inamyloid.

## Distribution and Habitat

*L. saeva* is widespread across temperate Europe, from Scandinavia to the Mediterranean, and extends into western Russia.

In [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]], it is found primarily in the Pacific Northwest and northeastern regions.

The species fruits in autumn, typically from September through November, sometimes extending into December in mild years.

It is a grassland species, commonly found in pastures, meadows, lawns, parkland, and woodland edges.

Unlike the closely related *L. nuda*, which prefers [[dighton-fungal-decomposition-leaf-litter-dynamics]] and compost, *L. saeva* grows in open grassy areas.

It often forms [[bloomfield-fairy-rings-armillaria-giant-fungi]] in permanent pastures and can appear in the same locations year after year.
