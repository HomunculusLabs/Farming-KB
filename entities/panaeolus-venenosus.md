## Panaeolus venenosus

**Panaeolus venenosus** is a psychoactive mushroom species in the genus Panaeolus, reported from Australia. The species epithet "venenosus" (meaning poisonous or venomous) reflects its bioactive properties. It has historical significance in the study of Australian [[emcdda-psilocybin-non-addictive-profile-tolerance-dynamics]] effects.

## Taxonomy and Classification

*Panaeolus venenosus* belongs to the family Bolbitiaceae, order Agaricales. The genus Panaeolus is characterized by its predominantly dung-inhabiting lifestyle, black spore print, and mottled gill appearance caused by the asynchronous maturation of spores (some basidia mature and release spores while others remain immature, creating a spotted pattern).

The species has been referenced in the scientific literature on Australian psychoactive fungi, notably in the work of Dr. Sam Stein, who conducted clinical studies using mushroom extracts obtained from dried specimens of *Panaeolus venenosus* in the late 1950s. These studies were among the earliest investigations of the subjective effects of Australian psychoactive mushrooms.

## Morphology

*Panaeolus venenosus* shares the general morphological features of the Panaeolus genus. The pileus (cap) is small to medium, typically 15-40 mm in diameter, conic to broadly convex or bell-shaped (campanulate) when young, expanding to hemispheric with age. The cap surface is smooth and often slightly viscid when moist, with a color ranging from grayish-brown to dark brown.

The lamellae (gills) are adnate to adnexed, close, and display the characteristic mottled or spotted pattern of the genus due to uneven spore maturation. The gills are initially pale gray, becoming black as spores mature. The stipe is slender, cylindrical, equal, and typically 40-80 mm long and 2-4 mm thick. It is generally whitish to pale gray and may exhibit slight bluing at the base when handled.

Spores are ellipsoid, smooth, and black in deposit, approximately 12-16 x 7-9 micrometers.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Panaeolus venenosus* has been reported from Australia, where it grows on herbivore dung, particularly in pastures and grasslands. Like other Panaeolus species, it is a coprophilous (dung-loving) fungus that fruits directly on or near cow, horse, and other livestock manure.

The species favors warm, humid conditions and is most commonly found during the warmer months in tropical and subtropical regions of Australia, particularly in Queensland and northern New South Wales. It may also occur in temperate regions during periods of warm, wet weather.
