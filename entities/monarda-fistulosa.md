## Overview

Monarda fistulosa, commonly known as wild bergamot or bee balm, is a perennial
herbaceous plant in the mint family (Lamiaceae). Native to [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]], it ranges
from Canada to Mexico across prairies, open woodlands, and meadows.

The plant is valued for its showy lavender-to-pink flower heads, aromatic foliage,
and exceptional attractiveness to pollinators. Despite the common name "wild
bergamot," it is unrelated to the bergamot orange ([[citrus-bergamia]]) used to flavor
Earl Grey tea, though the leaf scent is reminiscent.

M. fistulosa has a long history of use by Indigenous peoples [[berry-growing]] for
medicinal, culinary, and ceremonial purposes. It remains widely used in herbal
medicine and is a staple in native plant gardens and [[tropical-rainforest-seedling-mycorrhiza-ecological-restoration]].

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:**

- Kingdom: Plantae
- Order: Lamiales
- Family: Lamiaceae
- Genus: Monarda
- Species: M. fistulosa L. (1753)

**Synonyms:** Monarda fistulosa var. fistulosa, Monarda fistulosa var. menthifolia.

M. fistulosa grows 60–120 cm tall with square stems typical of the mint family.
Leaves are lanceolate to ovate, 5–12 cm long, oppositely arranged, with serrate
margins and a grayish-green coloration.

The inflorescence is a terminal head of tubular flowers, 3–7 cm across, surrounded
by pinkish to lavender bracts. Individual flowers are bilabiate, two-lipped, and
produced in whorls. Bloom period extends from June to September.

Root systems are fibrous with shallow rhizomes, enabling clonal spread. A single
plant can form a colony 60–90 cm in diameter within several years.

## Distribution and Habitat

M. fistulosa is native to virtually all of North America, from Quebec to Georgia
and west to British Columbia and Arizona. It occupies prairies, savannas, open
woodlands, meadows, and disturbed sites, preferring full sun and well-drained soils.

Several varieties are recognized: var. menthifolia (western), var. fistulosa
(eastern), and var. rubra (southern, with redder flowers).

## Bioactive Compounds

The [[permaculture-designers-manual-essential-oil-plants]] of M. fistulosa contains a complex mixture of monoterpenes and
[[aact-tannins-terpenes-phenolic-compounds-wood-composting-ingham]]:

- **Thymol:** Primary antimicrobial constituent (20–60% of essential oil depending
  on chemotype).
- **Carvacrol:** Closely related phenolic monoterpene with antimicrobial activity.
- **p-Cymene:** Aromatic monoterpene hydrocarbon.
- **Linalool:** Monoterpene alcohol with anxiolytic and sedative properties.
- **Geraniol:** Floral-scented monoterpene alcohol.
- **Rosmarinic acid:** Phenolic acid with anti-inflammatory and antioxidant
  properties.

Chemotype variation is significant; populations from different geographic regions
produce oils dominated by different constituents (thymol, carvacrol, or geraniol
chemotypes).
