---he compound is valued for its sedative, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial
properties. In cannabis, it contributes to the characteristic woody, floral
aroma and is believed to enhance transdermal absorption of co-occurring
cannabinoids and terpenes, making it a functional component of the entourage
effect.

## Chemical Identity

- **IUPAC name:** (1S,4R)-1-methyl-4-[(E)-prop-1-en-1-yl]cyclohex-1-en-1-yl]methanol (trans)
- **Molecular formula:** C₁₅H₂₆O
- **Molecular weight:** 222.37 g/mol
- **CAS number:** 40716-66-3 (cis), 37877-14-0 (trans), 7212-44-4 (mixed)
- **Structure:** Acyclic sesquiterpene alcohol with a double bond in both the
  ring and side chain
- **Boiling point:** 276 °C (trans isomer)
- **Density:** 0.89 g/mL
- **Solubility:** Insoluble in water; soluble in ethanol, oils, and organic
  solvents
- **Odor:** Fresh, woody, floral, slightly citrus; apple and rose notes

## Natural Sources

### Cannabis (*Cannabis sativa* L.)

- Present in the trichome head resin of many cultivars
- Typically 0.01–0.3% of total [[bisabolol]]) but
  contributes to overall antioxidant capacity of essential oil blends

## Biosynthesis

Nerolidol is biosynthesized from farnesyl pyrophosphate (FPP) via nerolidyl
diphosphate intermediate, catalyzed by nerolidol synthase (TPS enzymes). In
cannabis, the pathway originates from the MEP (methylerythritol phosphate)
pathway in glandular trichomes. The sesquiterpene skeleton (15 carbons) derives
from three isoprene units, distinguishing it from monoterpenes (10 carbons)
like [[myrcene]] — Monoterpene with overlapping sedative properties in cannabis
