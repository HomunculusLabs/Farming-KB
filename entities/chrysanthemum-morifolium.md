## Overview

**Chrysanthemum morifolium** Ramat. (Asteraceae) is a perennial herbaceous plant native to East Asia. Its dried flower heads, known as *Ju Hua* (菊花), are among the most important medicinal and beverage materials in Chinese culture and traditional medicine.

The species is the parent of most modern ornamental chrysanthemum cultivars, which rank among the top commercially produced cut flowers worldwide. Beyond ornamental use, the flowers are consumed as herbal tea, culinary garnish, and medicinal preparation across East Asia.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Chrysanthemum morifolium** Ramat., Dict. Hist. Nat. 5: 145 (1789). Family Asteraceae, tribe Anthemideae. The genus Chrysanthemum sensu stricto contains approximately 40 species centered in East Asia.

The plant is an erect or slightly spreading perennial, 30–150 cm tall, with aromatic, deeply lobed leaves. Stems are branched, green to purplish, and slightly woody at the base. The root system is fibrous with rhizomatous spread.

Leaves are alternate, ovate to oblong, 5–15 cm long, pinnately lobed with coarsely toothed margins. The upper surface is green and sparsely hairy; the lower surface is grayish-green with denser pubescence. Leaf texture varies among cultivars.

The inflorescence is a capitulum (flower head) 2–10 cm diameter depending on cultivar. The involucre consists of multiple rows of bracts. Ray florets (petals) are white, yellow, or purple; disc florets are yellow. The fruit is an indehiscent achene.

## Distribution and Habitat

C. morifolium is native to China, where it has been cultivated for over 3,000 years. Wild populations are found in mountainous regions of central and eastern China. The species has been naturalized in Japan, Korea, and parts of [[permaculture-designers-manual-temperate-climate-design]]. Cultivated forms are grown commercially across China, Japan, Korea, and increasingly in other countries.

Major production regions in China include Tongxiang (Zhejiang), Bozhou (Anhui), Jiaozuo (Henan), and Huanggang (Hubei). Each region produces distinct medicinal varieties with characteristic chemical profiles.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine

In TCM, *Ju Hua* (菊花) is classified as sweet, bitter, and slightly cold. It enters the lung and liver meridians. Its primary actions are dispelling wind-heat, calming the liver, clearing heat, and improving vision.

It is prescribed for headaches, dizziness, red and painful eyes, and sore throat associated with wind-heat invasion. For liver yang hyperactivity manifesting as headache and vertigo, it is often combined with Gou Teng (Uncaria) and Shi Jue Ming (Concha Haliotidis).

Several regionally distinct varieties are recognized. *Hang Ju* from Zhejiang (white flowers) is used for calming the liver and improving vision. *Bo Ju* from Anhui (yellow flowers) is preferred for dispelling wind-heat. *Gong Ju* from Henan is used as both medicine and beverage.

Daily dosage is 5–10 g in decoction. The flowers are also consumed as a daily health tea throughout China, often combined with [[query-how-do-i-grow-goji-berries-at-home]], wolfberry, or rock sugar. Excessive consumption may cause digestive upset in cold-pattern constitutions.

## Culinary Use

Chrysanthemum tea is one of the most popular herbal beverages in China, consumed both for its pleasant floral aroma and its perceived health benefits. The dried flowers are steeped in hot water, often with additions of rock sugar, goji berries, or dried longan.

In Chinese cuisine, chrysanthemum petals are used as a garnish for soups, hotpots, and stir-fried dishes. The petals add a subtle bitter-sweet flavor and visual appeal. Chrysanthemum-infused liquors and wines are produced commercially.

In Japan, the petals of edible chrysanthemum cultivars (*shungiku*, C. coronarium) are used in nabemono (hot pot) dishes and tempura. The leaves are also eaten as a leafy vegetable with a distinctive herbal flavor.

Chrysanthemum extract is used as a natural food coloring agent, providing yellow to green hues. It is also employed as a flavoring agent in beverages, confections, and ice cream across East Asian markets.

## Cultivar Groups

Chinese medicinal chrysanthemums are classified into several major cultivar groups based on geographic origin and processing. *Hang Ju* (杭菊) from Zhejiang is the most prestigious, featuring large white flower heads with a sweet flavor.

*Bo Ju* (亳菊) from Anhui produces smaller, bright yellow flowers with a more bitter profile. It is preferred in formulas targeting wind-heat conditions due to its stronger cold nature.
