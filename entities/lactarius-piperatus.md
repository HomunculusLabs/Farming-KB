## Overview

**Lactarius piperatus** (L.) Pers., commonly known as the peppery milk cap, white pepper milk cap, or simply the pepper milkcap, is a basidiomycete fungus in the family Russulaceae. It is one of the most widely recognized members of the genus *Lactarius*, easily identified by its pure white coloration, crowded white gills, and the intensely peppery, acrid latex (milk) that exudes from all parts of the fruiting body when cut or damaged.

The species is found across temperate forests of Europe and [[spore-print]]:** White to cream.

### Microscopic Features

- **Basidia:** Clavate, 4-spored, 40–55 × 8–11 µm.
- **Spores:** Broadly ellipsoid, 7–9 × 5.5–7 µm, ornamented with a prominent reticulum (network-like pattern) of ridges and warts connected by fine lines. Spore ornamentation is amyloid.
- **Cheilocystidia:** Numerous, cylindrical to fusiform, 30–60 × 4–7 µm, thin-walled.
- **Pileipellis:** An ixocutis of repent to interwoven hyphae, with scattered cylindrical to clavate pileocystidia.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Lactarius piperatus* is distributed across the temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere:

- **Europe:** Widespread and common throughout the continent, from the British Isles and Scandinavia to the Mediterranean and eastward to the Ural Mountains. Particularly abundant in oak and beech forests of Central and Eastern Europe.
- **North America:** Occurs in [[corylus-avellana]]*)
- Occasionally with birch (*Betula* spp.) and hornbeam (*Carpinus betulus*)

Fruiting occurs from midsummer to late autumn (July–November), with peak abundance in August and September. It prefers warm, dry summers and is often one of the more prolific milk caps in oak-dominated woodlands during favorable years.

## Edibility and Culinary Use

*Lactarius piperatus* is one of the most important edible milk caps in Eastern European and Russian culinary traditions, but requires careful preparation:

### Preparation Method

The intense acridity of the latex is heat-labile and can be removed through prolonged cooking:

1. **Cleaning:** Brush or wipe clean; avoid soaking in water, as the flesh absorbs moisture readily.
2. **Pre-boiling:** Slice the mushrooms and boil in abundant water for 15–20 minutes, discarding the cooking water. The water often turns slightly yellow.
3. **Rinsing:** Drain and rinse thoroughly.
4. **Final cooking:** The blanched mushrooms can then be used in various dishes — sautéed, added to soups, or pickled.

In Polish and Russian traditions, *L. piperatus* is commonly pickled with spices and served as a zakuska (appetizer). The pickling process further mellows the remaining pungency and produces a firm, crunchy texture.

The species is low in calories and provides modest amounts of protein, fiber, B vitamins, and minerals (particularly potassium and phosphorus). Analysis has also revealed the presence of [[chalciporus-piperatus]]
