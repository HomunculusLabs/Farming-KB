# Erysiphaceae

## Description

The Erysiphaceae, commonly known as powdery mildews, are a family of obligate parasitic Ascomycetes that infect the above-ground parts of higher plants. They are nourished by haustoria and develop a characteristic white powdery conidial stage on the surface of the host. The family includes 19-22 accepted genera and an estimated 400 species, with the majority in the genus Erysiphe.

## Key Facts

- 19-22 currently accepted genera with approximately 400 species; majority in the genus Erysiphe.
- Worldwide distribution, with 9,838 angiosperm host species in 44 orders, 169 families (roughly half of known angiosperm families), and 1,617 genera.
- 93% of hosts are dicotyledons. Of 662 known monocot hosts, 634 are in the Gramineae.
- No powdery mildews occur on gymnosperms, pteridophytes, or lower plants (mosses, liverworts).
- Recognized in the field by white, dusty conidial state coating leaf and small stem surfaces.
- Nonostiolate perithecia (cleistothecia) are ornamented with hyphal appendages (simple, hooked, branched) used for generic delimitation.
- Hirata (1986) calculated the ratio of [[powdery-mildew]] species to angiosperm species at 4.5%.
- North-temperate regions have far larger numbers of host species than tropical, subtropical, arctic, or subarctic regions.
- Number of herbaceous species hosting powdery mildews (8,250) far exceeds number of tree hosts (2,645).
- Areas of greatest abundance and damage: California and Israel, characterized by warm, rain-free summers and intensive agriculture.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- Powdery mildews are among the most commonly encountered and economically important plant diseases worldwide.
- Major pathogens of cereals (Blumeria graminis on wheat/barley), grapes (Erysiphe necator), cucurbits (Podosphaera xanthii), and ornamentals.
- The family is a model system for studying obligate biotrophy, haustorium function, and host-parasite coevolution.
- Many species are host-specific, making host identification essential for species determination.
- Conidial states alone can separate four form genera, but perithecia generally are needed for species-level identification using current taxonomic treatments.

## Ecological Context

Powdery mildews reach their greatest diversity in temperate regions rather than the tropics, an unusual pattern for plant pathogenic fungi. Hirata (1986) listed close to 4,000 publications on powdery mildews including regional monographs. The growth habit of the fungus is generally limited to leaves and succulent shoots, explaining the higher number of herbaceous hosts compared to woody species. Future studies in the tropics may reveal whether powdery mildews truly are more species-rich in temperate areas.
