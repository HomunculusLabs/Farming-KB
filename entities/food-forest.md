## Food Forest (Forest Garden)

## Overview

A **Food Forest** (also called a **Forest Garden**) is a designed, multi-layered polyculture system modeled on the structure and ecology of a natural woodland ecosystem, optimized for the production of food, medicine, fiber, and other useful products. Unlike conventional agriculture, which relies on annual monoculture and external inputs, food forests are **perennial polycultures** that become increasingly productive and self-maintaining over time.

The concept represents one of the oldest human land-use strategies — tropical home gardens in Southeast Asia, Africa, and the Amazon have functioned as proto-food-forests for millennia. The modern permaculture food forest movement draws from these traditions, formalized through the work of **Robert Hart** (Shropshire, UK, 1960s–1990s), **Bill Mollison** and **David Holmgren** (permaculture design), and **Dave Jacke** and **Eric Toensmeier** (comprehensive temperate design methodology).

## The Seven Layers

### 1. Canopy Layer (Large Trees)
- **Height**: 10–30+ meters at maturity
- **Role**: Overstory structure, microclimate creation, large yields, nitrogen cycling
- **Examples**: Chestnut (*Castanea*), walnut (*Juglans*), pecan (*Carya illinoinensis*), oak (*Quercus*), mulberry (*Morus*), persimmon (*Diospyros*)
- **Yield timeline**: 5–15 years to production; productive for 50–300+ years

### 2. Understory / Low Tree Layer
- **Height**: 3–10 meters
- **Role**: Sub-canopy fruit and nut production, shade-tolerant crops, wildlife habitat
- **Examples**: Apple (*Malus domestica*), pear (*Pyrus*), plum (*Prunus*), pawpaw (*[[hippophae-rhamnoides]]*), Siberian pea shrub (*[[mycorrhizal-networks]]
- **Examples**: [[humulus-lupulus]]*)

### Eighth Layer (Fungal)
Many designers now recognize an **eighth fungal layer**: cultivated mushrooms on logs or stumps, mycorrhizal inoculation of root zones, and decomposition of woody material → humus building. Fungal networks connect plants, facilitating nutrient and information exchange (the **"wood wide web"**).

## Ecological Principles

### Succession and Evolution

Food forests mimic ecological succession:
1. **Year 0–2**: Pioneer species (nitrogen fixers, fast growers) establish; soil building
2. **Year 2–5**: Shrubs and herbaceous perennials fill in; first berry and herb yields
3. **Year 5–15**: Understory trees begin fruiting; system becomes self-regulating
4. **Year 15–50**: Full canopy closure; mature polyculture; minimal external inputs
5. **Year 50+**: Climax community; self-sustaining; managed through selective harvesting

### Guild Design

A **guild** is a group of plants around a central element providing mutual support:

| Function | Example Plants |
|---|---|
| Nitrogen fixation | Siberian pea shrub, goumi, clover, groundnut |
| Dynamic accumulation | Comfrey, dandelion, yarrow |
