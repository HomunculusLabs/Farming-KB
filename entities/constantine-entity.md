## Description

Constantine the Great (Flavius Valerius Constantinus, c. 272–337 CE) was Roman
Emperor from 306 to 337 CE and is traditionally credited with the Christianization
of the Roman Empire. In ethnomycological analysis, Constantine's role is
recontextualized as a political architect who consolidated diverse entheogenic
religious traditions into a single state-controlled religion, deliberately
obscuring the mushroom-based sacramental practices at their core.

## Classification

- **Category:** Historical personage / Political figure
- **Role:** Roman Emperor, founder of Christian state religion
- **Ethnomycological Significance:** Key figure in the suppression and
  reinterpretation of entheogenic sacramental traditions

## Historical Background

Constantine ruled during a pivotal period when the Roman Empire encompassed
hundreds of diverse religious traditions, many of which incorporated entheogenic
sacraments. Rather than allow this diversity to continue, Constantine recognized
that governmental control of the population becomes much easier and more
effective when the state can also claim divine authority.

His approach was systematic: create a single "One World Religion" that would
unify the empire under both political and spiritual authority. The result was
the Catholic (meaning "universal" or "one from all") Church.

## The Council of Nicaea and Biblical Compilation

According to the source text, Christianity was created through a governmental
council that:

1. **Acquired and examined** as many of the world's religious doctrines as could
   be found across the empire and beyond
2. **Selected elements** from Egyptology, Mithraism, Hinduism, Buddhism, the
   Torah of the Jews, and many other traditions
3. **Altered** the selected doctrines as desired to serve political purposes
4. **Discarded** elements that did not fit the agenda, including direct
   references to entheogenic practices
5. **Compiled** the plagiarized and altered writings into the new Catholic Bible

This process created a document that, despite heavy alteration, retained enough
original symbolic content that the mushroom references could still be decoded
by knowledgeable readers.

## Political Motivation

Constantine understood a fundamental principle of governance: combining political
and divine authority creates an unbeatable control mechanism. By creating a state
religion that:

- **Claimed exclusive access** to God (removing individual direct experience)
- **Replaced entheogenic sacraments** with symbolic substitutes (bread/wine)
- **Established priestly intermediaries** between the individual and the divine
- **Condemned the original practices** as heresy or witchcraft
- **Enforced compliance** through both religious and secular power

The state gained unprecedented control over the spiritual lives of its subjects.

## Impact on Entheogenic Traditions

### Suppression of Direct Experience

The most significant impact was the systematic removal of the individual's
ability to experience divine consciousness directly through entheogenic
sacraments. This served multiple political purposes:

- Created dependency on institutional religion for spiritual access
- Eliminated independent spiritual practitioners (shamans, mystics) as power
  rivals
- Enabled the "doctrine of [[transubstantiation]]" — priests claiming to
  magically transform bread into the body of God — as a control mechanism
- Made the population easier to govern by removing transformative consciousness-
  expanding experiences

### Preservation of Symbolism

Despite the suppression, Constantine's compilation process inadvertently
preserved extensive [[stropharia-cubensis-tree-of-knowledge]]/Life
- Sacramental ingestion language (body and blood)
- Visionary experiences (Ezekiel, Revelation)
- Parables about seeds, growth, and harvesting
- Cross symbolism and anointing references

These elements survived because the compilers either did not recognize them as
mushroom references or could not remove them without destroying narrative
coherence.

## Connection to Other Suppression Patterns

Constantine's approach set a template repeated throughout history:

- **Spanish Inquisition:** Murdered an estimated 8 million people as "witches"
  (traditional plant knowledge practitioners)
- **Central American conquest:** An estimated 20 million indigenous people
  killed, nearly exterminating Aztec/Inca/Mayan entheogenic traditions
- **[[cervantes-war-on-drugs-statistics]]" continues the same pattern of
  suppressing entheogenic plant knowledge

## The Bible as Historical Document

The source text argues the Bible "should not be regarded as a historical
document" in its current form, as it represents:

- A re-translation of a re-translation of a re-translation
- An initially altered plagiarism from multiple older sources
- A text that pales in comparison to Hindu and Buddhist writings in complexity
- A document whose power to convince derives partly from readers' lack of
  exposure to other religious texts

However, the very complexity that makes the Bible impressive also ensured that
sufficient encoded entheogenic material survived for later scholars to decode.

mushroom knowledge is not merely forgotten but actively suppressed for political
gain. Understanding this pattern is essential for:

- Recognizing why entheogenic traditions appear "hidden" in religious texts
- Appreciating the political forces that shaped modern religious practice
- Understanding the [[john-allegro]] — Scholar who decoded the surviving mushroom references
