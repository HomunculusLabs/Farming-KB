## Pawpaw

**Type:** Species (Plant)
**Category:** Deciduous understory fruit tree
**Also known as:** Asimina triloba, custard banana, Michigan banana,
American pawpaw, poor man's banana

## Overview
Pawpaw (Asimina triloba) is a deciduous shrub or small tree native to eastern
[[fukuoka-agriculture-tomorrow-quarter-acre-vision]] in ideal
  conditions, forming thickets
- **Branches:** Brittle; very prone to wind damage
- **Frost tolerance:** Copes with snow conditions; severe spring frosts can
  destroy flowers and cause crop loss

### Environmental Requirements
- **Soil:** Prefers well-drained, fertile soil rich in [[polypore-fungi-eastern-north-america]] America

### Fruit Characteristics
- **Size:** Largest native fruit of North America; up to 300 grams
- **Skin:** Dark brown to almost black when fully ripe
- **Flesh:** Soft, creamy, banana-custard flavour; delicious raw
- **Culinary uses:** Eaten fresh, dried, preserves, pies, puddings, desserts
- **Varieties:** Yellow-fruited varieties said to be superior to white
- **Caution:** Handling fruit can cause skin rashes on some people; pigs and
  poultry reportedly avoid the fruit

### Nutritional Composition
- **Water:** 76.6%
- **Carbohydrate:** 16.8% (16% sucrose, 35% reducing sugars)
- **Protein:** 5.2%
- **Fat:** 0.9%
- **Ash:** 0.5%

### Alkaloids and Medicinal Properties
- **Asimine:** Colourless, tasteless alkaloid in seeds; acts on the brain
  causing somnolence, stupor, then unconsciousness; also has emetic
  properties
- **Analobine:** Alkaloid in bark; once used in medicine
- **Traditional use:** Inner bark stripped in [[monarda-fistulosa]]
