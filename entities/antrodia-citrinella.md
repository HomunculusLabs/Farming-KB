# Antrodiella citrinella

## Description

Antrodiella citrinella is a small, polypore-like wood-decaying fungus in the family Steccherinaceae (Polyporales, Basidiomycota). The species is one of the best-known examples of a "successor" fungus—one that characteristically fruits on the decaying sporocarps of a "predecessor" species, in this case Fomitopsis pinicola. This association is so consistent that finding A. citrinella can be used to locate old F. pinicola brackets. The species is a white-rot fungus and produces bright yellowish (citrine) fruiting bodies.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Eukaryota
- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Polyporales
- **Family:** Steccherinaceae (or Meruliaceae, classification evolving)
- **Genus:** Antrodiella
- **Species:** A. citrinella

## Morphological Characteristics

- **Fruiting body:** Small, resupinate to pileate, with a bright citrine (yellowish) color that gives the species its name
- **Pore surface:** Fine, round to angular pores; white to cream when fresh
- **Hyphal system:** Dimitic, with both generative and skeletal hyphae
- **Spores:** Small, cylindrical to allantoid, hyaline, negative in Melzer's reagent
- **Texture:** Tough to corky when fresh, becoming brittle when dry

## Ecology and Succession

Antrodiella citrinella is a textbook example of fungal succession on wood:

1. **Predecessor stage:** Fomitopsis pinicola colonizes and decays conifer wood, producing large perennial brackets
2. **Decay phase:** As F. pinicola sporocarps age and begin to decay, the underlying wood is modified by brown-rot decomposition
3. **Successor colonization:** A. citrinella colonizes the decayed wood and old sporocarps of F. pinicola
4. **Fruiting:** A. citrinella produces its own sporocarps directly on the dead F. pinicola brackets or on the adjacent decayed wood

The great majority of successor species like Antrodiella are white-rot fungi, which follow the brown-rot predecessors in the decomposition sequence.

## Host Association

- **Primary predecessor:** Fomitopsis pinicola (red-belted bracket)
- **Related pattern:** A. (Trametes) hoehnelii grows mainly on sporocarps of Inonotus radiatus
- The consistent association suggests either a parasitic, competitive, or successional relationship

## Nature of the Relationship

The relationship between A. citrinella and F. pinicola could represent:

- **True mycoparasitism:** Direct parasitism of the predecessor's mycelium
- **Competitive replacement:** Outcompeting the predecessor for resources
- **Opportunistic saprotrophy:** Exploiting wood pre-conditioned by the predecessor's decay
- **Canal colonization:** Channels left by dead predecessor mycelial strands may serve as pathways for successor hyphae (Niemelä et al. 1995)

Observed succession in fruiting does not indicate whether a successor is a selective parasite or merely profiting from substrate pre-conditioning by its predecessor.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Succession model:** One of the clearest examples of fungal succession in wood decay, widely cited in mycological literature
- **Forest ecology:** Plays a role in the transition from brown-rot to white-rot decay phases in coniferous forests
- **Indicator species:** Its presence indicates the stage of wood decomposition and previous F. pinicola activity
- **Taxonomic significance:** Represents the genus Antrodiella, which contains many successor species associated with specific predecessor fungi

## Notes

Antrodiella citrinella illustrates the complexity of interpreting fungal associations on wood. While it consistently fruits on F. pinicola, it remains unclear whether this reflects true mycoparasitism, competitive ability, or simply opportunistic colonization of pre-conditioned substrate. The genus Antrodiella as a whole is rich in species that show similar predecessor-successor patterns with various wood-decaying fungi, making it a key genus for understanding fungal succession.
