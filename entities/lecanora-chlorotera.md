## Overview

**Lecanora chlorotera** is a common crustose lichen in the family Lecanoraceae, widely distributed across temperate regions of Europe and North America. It forms greyish-green to yellowish-white crusts on the bark of deciduous trees.

The species is one of the most frequently encountered epiphytic Lecanora species in lowland temperate forests. It is easily recognized by its bright yellow-green soralia and its preference for nutrient-enriched bark surfaces.

Lecanora chlorotera belongs to the Lecanora subfusca group, a complex of morphologically similar species that has been the subject of extensive taxonomic revision. The species epithet "chlorotera" refers to its characteristic greenish colouration.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Lecanora chlorotera was described by Erik Acharius in 1799. The species has undergone several nomenclatural changes and was for many years treated as a synonym or variety of L. chlarotera.

The thallus is crustose, forming continuous to areolate crusts 2 to 8 cm in diameter. The surface is greyish-white to pale green, often with a distinct yellowish or greenish tinge that is visible to the naked eye.

In shaded conditions, the thallus tends to be thinner and more evenly grey. On exposed, well-lit bark, it develops a more pronounced yellowish-green colouration.

The thallus margin is usually well-defined, forming a thin prothallus of black hyphae at the edges. The cortex is approximately 15 to 25 μm thick with a densely packed paraplectenchymatous structure.

The photobiont is a chlorococcoid green alga, typically Trebouxia species. Algal cells are 8 to 14 μm in diameter and arranged in a roughly continuous layer beneath the cortex.

Soralia are prominent, farinose to granular, and typically bright yellow-green to lemon yellow in colour. They develop initially as small pustules that expand and coalesce into irregular soral patches covering much of the thallus surface.

Apothecia are common, lecanorine, 0.5 to 1.5 mm in diameter, with a brown to dark brown disc. The thalline margin is concolorous with the thallus or slightly paler, and the proper exciple is indistinct.

Ascospores are ellipsoid to broadly ellipsoid, simple, hyaline, measuring 10 to 16 by 6 to 9 μm. Pycnidia are immersed, producing bacilliform conidia approximately 5 to 8 μm long.

Chemical spot tests on the thallus show K+ yellow to orange (atrnaorin), C−, KC−, Pd+ yellow. Soralia test K+ yellow due to atranorin, and UV+ bright yellow-white due to usnic acid content in the soredia.

## Distribution and Habitat

Lecanora chlorotera is widespread across Europe from the Mediterranean to southern Scandinavia and the British Isles. It is also found in [[fungal-elicitors-enhanced-secondary-metabolite-production]]. The soredia contain both atranorin and usnic acid, the latter responsible for the bright UV+ fluorescence.

The presence of usnic acid in the soredia but not in the thallus cortex is a characteristic feature. This differential chemistry has taxonomic significance and helps distinguish L. chlorotera from similar species in the L. subfusca group.

Atranorin provides antimicrobial protection to the thallus surface. Usnic acid in the soredia may serve as a propagule defense compound, protecting the dispersed soredia from microbial degradation during dispersal and establishment.

The dual-chemistry system of L. chlorotera is unusual among Lecanora species. Most members of the L. subfusca group contain atranorin in both the thallus and the soredia.

Extracts of L. chlorotera have shown moderate antimicrobial activity in screening programs. However, the concentrations of bioactive compounds are relatively low compared to usnic acid-rich species such as Cladonia or Usnea.

Research has investigated the potential of Lecanora extracts as natural preservatives. The [[dighton-nitrogen-deposition-mycorrhizal-community-shifts]].

As a sorediate species, L. chlorotera contributes to lichen dispersal and colonization of new substrates. Soredia are readily dispersed by wind and can establish new colonies considerable distances from parent thalli.

Establishment success of soredia is highest on nutrient-rich, relatively smooth bark with moderate moisture availability. The species colonizes both young and mature bark surfaces but is most abundant on intermediate-aged substrates.

The species provides microhabitat for various lichenicolous fungi, including species of Arthonia and Marchandiomyces. These [[escovopsis-parasitic-fungi-attine-ant-gardens]] are often found on thalli of L. chlorotera in mature lichen communities.

The bright yellow soralia of L. chlorotera make it one of the most visually conspicuous crustose lichens on bark. This conspicuousness has made it a popular introductory species in lichen identification workshops and field courses.
