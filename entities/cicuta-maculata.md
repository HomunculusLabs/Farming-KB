# Spotted Water Hemlock (*Cicuta maculata*)

## Overview

*Cicuta maculata* is the most widely distributed of four *Cicuta* species and is widely regarded as [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]]'s most violently toxic native plant. All parts of the plant contain cicutoxin, a potent polyacetylene neurotoxin that causes fatal seizures, often within hours of ingestion.

Despite its lethal toxicity, *C. maculata* is frequently confused with edible Apiaceae such as wild parsnip, wild carrot, and water celery — a dangerous misidentification that causes livestock and human fatalities nearly every year.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Order:** Apiales
- **Family:** Apiaceae
- **Genus:** *Cicuta*
- **Species:** *C. maculata* L.

**Etymology:** Greek *kikuta* (a kind of plant); Latin *maculata* (spotted),
referring to the purple streaking on the stem.

**Varieties (4 recognized):**
- *C. m.* var. *maculata* — [[polypore-fungi-eastern-north-america]] America
- *C. m.* var. *angustifolia* — Western, narrow-leaved form
- *C. m.* var. *bolanderi* — Sierra Nevada and Pacific ranges
- *C. m.* var. *picta* — Great Plains and Rocky Mountains

## Morphology

- **Habit:** Perennial herb, 0.5–2.5 m tall; dies back to tuberous rootstock in winter
- **Root:** Thick, chambered tuber with horizontal internal partitions (diagnostic
  feature distinguishing *Cicuta* from all other Apiaceae)
- **Stem:** Hollow, purplish-spotted or streaked, 1–3 cm diameter at base;
  branching in upper portion
- **Leaves:** 2–3× pinnately compound, 15–40 cm long; leaflets lanceolate to
  ovate, 3–10 cm, serrate margins, pointed tips
- **Inflorescence:** Compound umbels, 5–15 cm diameter; white flowers, each
  umbel with 12–25 rays; blooming June–September
- **Fruit:** Ovoid schizocarp, 3–4 mm, with prominent winged ribs

The chambered rootstock with horizontal cross-partitions is the single most
reliable diagnostic character for *Cicuta* identification.

## Distribution and Habitat

- **Range:** Alaska to Newfoundland, south to California, Texas, and Florida;
  the most broadly distributed *Cicuta* species
- **Habitat:** Wet meadows, marshes, stream banks, pond margins, ditches, and
  seepage areas; requires saturated or seasonally flooded soils
- **Elevation:** Sea level to 3,000 m in mountainous areas
- **Community type:** Common in freshwater wetlands, riparian corridors, and
  disturbed wet areas such as roadside ditches

Plants are most toxic in [[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]] during active growth, but all parts remain
dangerous year-round. The rootstock retains toxicity even when dried.

## Chemistry: Cicutoxin

Cicutoxin is the principal toxic principle:

- **Cicutoxin (C₁₇H₂₂O₂):** A polyacetylene alcohol; highly unsaturated
  linear molecule with a terminal enyne-diol
- **Mechanism:** Non-competitive antagonist of GABA_A receptors in the central
  nervous system, causing loss of chloride channel inhibition
- **Potency:** Estimated lethal dose in humans is 2–5 mg/kg (approximately
  one bite of root); the root contains 1–4% cicutoxin by dry weight
- **Additional compounds:** Cicutoxin isomers (isocicutoxin), related
  polyacetylenes, and minor flavonoid constituents
