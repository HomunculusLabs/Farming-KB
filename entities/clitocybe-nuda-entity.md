# Clitocybe nuda
Clitocybe nuda, commonly known as the blewit or wood blewit, is an edible
mushroom in the family Tricholomataceae found across temperate regions of the
Northern Hemisphere. It is recognised by its distinctive violet-to-lilac cap
colour that fades with age, its robust stature, and its pleasant fruity odour.
Though widely collected for food in Europe and North America, it requires
careful identification because several toxic Cortinarius species share similar
habitat and colouration. The blewit is one of the few commercially cultivated
wild mushrooms, though indoor cultivation remains uncommon compared to
collected wild harvests. Its name derives from the Old English "blewit,"
meaning "blue hat," referring to the characteristic cap colour.
## Taxonomy and Naming
- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Tricholomataceae (historically); some modern treatments place it
  in Tricholomataceae s.s. or propose the genus Lepista
- **Genus:** Clitocybe (or Lepista in some classifications)
- **Species:** C. nuda
- **Authority:** (Bull.) P. Kumm., 1871
- **Basionym:** Agaricus nudus Bull., 1790
- **Synonyms:** Lepista nuda (Bull.) Cooke, Tricholoma nudum (Bull.) Quél.,
  Rhodopaxillus nudus (Bull.) Maire
- **Common names:** Wood blewit, blewit, blueleg
The taxonomic placement has been debated. Many mycologists prefer Lepista
based on spore ornamentation and pinkish spore print, distinguishing it from
true Clitocybe s.s. which has smooth spores and white spore prints. Molecular
phylogenetic studies have not fully resolved the position, and both names
remain in active use.
## Description
### Cap
- **Size:** 5–15 cm diameter, occasionally up to 20 cm
- **Shape:** Convex when young, expanding to broadly convex with an inrolled
  margin, eventually becoming nearly flat or slightly depressed in age
- **Colour:** Distinctive violet-blue to lilac when young, fading to
  tan-buff or greyish-brown with age; often retains violet tints at the
  margin longer than the disc
- **Surface:** Smooth to slightly silky, often with a waxy sheen when
  moist; not hygrophanous
- **Flesh:** Thick, firm, pale lilac to whitish; odour fruity, sometimes
  described as reminiscent of orange peel or frozen juice; taste mild
  to slightly peppery
### Gills
- **Attachment:** Adnexed to slightly sinuate, sometimes notched
- **Spacing:** Crowded to subdistant
- **Colour:** Pale pinkish to buff, becoming darker pinkish-brown with
  age
- **Spore print:** Pinkish-buff to pale salmon (distinguishing from
  white-spored Clitocybe s.s.)
### Stem
- **Size:** 4–8 cm long, 1–3 cm thick
- **Shape:** Equal or slightly clavate at the base
- **Colour:** Similar to cap, violet-blue when young fading to
  pale tan or buff
- **Surface:** Smooth to finely fibrillose, solid and firm
- **Partial veil:** Absent; no annulus
### Microscopic Features
| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Spores | Ellipsoidal, 6–8 × 3.5–5 µm |
| Spore ornamentation | Finely roughened to warty (distinctive vs smooth Clitocybe s.s.) |
| Basidia | 4-spored, clavate, 20–28 × 6–8 µm |
| Cheilocystidia | Club-shaped to cylindrical, thin-walled |
| Pleurocystidia | Rare or absent |
| Pileipellis | Cutis of interwoven hyphae |
## Distribution and Habitat
- **Geographic range:** Widely distributed across Europe, North America,
  and parts of Asia; introduced and naturalised in Australia and New Zealand
- **Habitat:** Terrestrial, growing in leaf litter, compost heaps, wood
  chips, and disturbed ground; often in mixed forests, parks, gardens,
  and along trails
- **Substrate:** Saprotrophic on leaf litter, grass debris, and woody
  material; frequently associated with conifer and hardwood duff
- **Season:** Late autumn to early winter (October–January in the Northern
  Hemisphere); occasionally fruits in spring in mild climates
- **Ecology:** A secondary decomposer that thrives in disturbed habitats;
  often appears in compost piles and along roadsides where organic matter
  accumulates
- **Frequency:** Common to locally abundant in suitable habitats
## Chemistry and Active Compounds
C. nuda is not psychoactive and contains no significant toxic compounds.
However, it has been investigated for nutritional and bioactive constituents:
- **Ergosterol:** The primary sterol, precursor to vitamin D2 upon UV
  exposure
- **Beta-glucans:** Polysaccharides with immunomodulatory properties found
  in the cell walls, similar to other edible Basidiomycota (see [[beta-glucans]])
- **Phenolic compounds:** Antioxidant activity attributed to phenolic
  content, though less studied than in medicinal mushrooms
- **Nutritional profile:** Good source of protein, fibre, B vitamins
  (especially riboflavin and niacin), and minerals including selenium
  and potassium
- **Pigments:** The violet colouration is due to bioactive anthraquinone
  and related polyketide pigments; these are not toxic but are responsible
  for the distinctive appearance

| Cortinarius violaceus | Cortina (cobwebby veil), rusty-brown spore print, darker violet | Edible but easily confused with toxic Cortinarius |
| Cortinarius iodes | Slimy cap, cobweb veil remnants, rusty spore print | Suspect; best avoided |
| Clitocybe odora | Anise-scented, grey-blue, never fades to tan | Edible |
| Lepista sordida | Smaller, thinner-fleshed, more intensely violet | Edible |
| Entoloma rhodopolium | Pink spores, angular under microscope | Toxic; causes GI distress |
**Critical identification warning:** Toxic Cortinarius species (subgenus
Leprocybe and others) can resemble young blewits. The key distinguishing
features are the pinkish spore print of C. nuda versus the rusty-brown print
of Cortinarius, and the absence of a cobwebby partial veil (cortina). Always
check the spore print before consumption.
## Cultivation Notes
C. nuda is one of the few wild edible mushrooms that can be cultivated
indoors, though it is not commercially significant compared to button
mushrooms or shiitake:
- **Substrate:** Composted straw, leaf litter, or enriched garden soil;
  performs well on pasteurised straw with supplements
- **Inoculation:** Grain spawn at 5–10% rate; colonisation takes 2–4
  weeks at 18–22°C
  (85–95%); casing with peat-based mix improves yields
- **Yield:** Moderate; biological efficiency typically 30–50%
  easier than many wild species
  hobbyists and specialty cultivators

## Culinary Significance
C. nuda is considered a good edible mushroom with a robust, earthy flavour
that intensifies with cooking. It is widely collected in Europe, particularly
in France and the British Isles. The mushroom must be thoroughly cooked before
consumption as raw blewits can cause gastrointestinal upset in some
individuals. It is suitable for sautéing, braising, soups, and preserves
well when pickled or dried. The violet colour fades entirely upon cooking,
yielding a greyish-brown appearance.
