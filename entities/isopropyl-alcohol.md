# Isopropyl Alcohol

## Description

Isopropyl alcohol (isopropanol, 2-propanol, rubbing alcohol) is a secondary alcohol widely used as an extraction solvent for cannabis. Commercially available as "rubbing alcohol" (typically 70% isopropyl alcohol and 30% water), it is one of the most accessible solvents for [[cannabis-extraction-basics]]. While convenient and inexpensive, its water content introduces significant drawbacks in terms of extract purity and potency.

## Classification

- **Category**: Secondary alcohol solvent
- **Chemical formula**: C₃H₇OH (CH₃CHOHCH₃)
- **Molecular weight**: 60.10 g/mol
- **Boiling point**: 82°C (180°F)
- **CAS Number**: 67-63-0
- **Appearance**: Colorless liquid

## Key Facts

- Available at many retail stores at [[coleman-quick-hoops-low-cost-season-extension-structures-design]] — the most accessible extraction solvent for home processing.
- **Much less toxic and explosive** than methanol, making it safer to handle.
- Contains approximately 30% water in standard rubbing alcohol formulations, which causes extraction of **water-soluble, non-psychoactive substances** alongside cannabinoids.
- Oil yield using isopropyl alcohol is approximately **twice that of methanol**, but the product is **proportionally less potent** due to the co-extraction of inactive material.
- Water-soluble tars extracted alongside cannabinoids may give the oil **undesirable taste and burning qualities**.
- Higher boiling point than methanol (due to water content) means longer evaporation times and higher temperatures needed for solvent removal.
- Best suited as a **first-stage extraction solvent** when the crude oil will be subsequently re-extracted with a more selective solvent like petroleum ether.
- The water component causes the solvent to evaporate at a much higher temperature; once alcohol evaporates, residual water must be driven off in an oil bath — a process that can cause dangerous spattering.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Processing

Isopropyl alcohol is the practical entry-point solvent for cannabis extraction. Its wide availability and lower toxicity make it attractive for small-scale processors. However, the water content fundamentally limits the quality of the extract: the resulting oil contains significant amounts of tars, waxes, and other non-psychoactive water-soluble compounds. For this reason, isopropyl [[pf-tek-alcohol-extraction-method]] is best viewed as a preliminary step. The crude oil can subsequently be purified through liquid-liquid partitioning with petroleum ether, which selectively extracts the cannabinoid fraction while leaving the water-soluble impurities behind.

## Safety Considerations

- Less flammable than methanol but still requires careful handling near heat sources.
- Water content causes spattering when heated in an oil bath — use caution.
- Adequate ventilation is essential; fumes can cause dizziness and headache.
- All traces of solvent must be removed from the final product before consumption.
