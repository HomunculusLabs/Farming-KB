# Isopropyl Alcohol

## Description

Isopropyl alcohol (isopropanol, 2-propanol, rubbing alcohol) is a secondary alcohol widely used as an extraction solvent for cannabis. Commercially available as "rubbing alcohol" (typically 70% isopropyl alcohol and 30% water), it is one of the most accessible solvents for [[coleman-quick-hoops-low-cost-season-extension-structures-design]] — the most accessible extraction solvent for home processing.
- **Much less toxic and explosive** than methanol, making it safer to handle.
- Contains approximately 30% water in standard rubbing alcohol formulations, which causes extraction of **water-soluble, non-psychoactive substances** alongside cannabinoids.
- Oil yield using isopropyl alcohol is approximately **twice that of methanol**, but the product is **proportionally less potent** due to the co-extraction of inactive material.
- Water-soluble tars extracted alongside cannabinoids may give the oil **undesirable taste and burning qualities**.
- Higher boiling point than methanol (due to water content) means longer evaporation times and higher temperatures needed for solvent removal.
- Best suited as a **first-stage extraction solvent** when the crude oil will be subsequently re-extracted with a more selective solvent like petroleum ether.
- The water component causes the solvent to evaporate at a much higher temperature; once alcohol evaporates, residual water must be driven off in an oil bath — a process that can cause dangerous spattering.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Processing

Isopropyl alcohol is the practical entry-point solvent for cannabis extraction. Its wide availability and lower toxicity make it attractive for small-scale processors. However, the water content fundamentally limits the quality of the extract: the resulting oil contains significant amounts of tars, waxes, and other non-psychoactive water-soluble compounds.
