# LSD (Lysergic Acid Diethylamide)

**LSD** (N,N-diethyllysergamide) is a semi-synthetic psychedelic of the
lysergamide class and one of the most potent psychoactive substances known.
First synthesized by gordon-wasson at Sandoz in 1938, its effects were
discovered in 1943. LSD profoundly shaped 20th-century psychiatry, culture,
and neuroscience through **5-HT2A receptor agonism**. See also [[dmt]], [[timothy-leary]].

## Chemical Identity

- **Systematic name:** (6aR,9R)-N,N-diethyl-7-methyl-4,6,6a,7,8,9-hexahydroindolo[4,3-fg]quinoline-9-carboxamide
- **Formula:** C₂₀H₂₅N₃O | **MW:** 323.43 g/mol | **CAS:** 50-37-3
- **Appearance:** Colorless, odorless crystalline solid; blotter paper, liquid, or microdots
- **Classification:** Lysergamide; semi-synthetic [[kykeon-composition-and-ergot-alkaloid-hypothesis]] derivative
- **Stereospecificity:** Only the (+)-D-isomer is psychoactive; the (-)-L-isomer is inactive — one of the most stereospecific drugs known.

## Discovery

### Sandoz Synthesis (1938)
LSD was first synthesized **November 16, 1938** by Albert Hofmann at Sandoz
Laboratories in Basel — the 25th compound in a series of lysergic acid
derivatives from ergotamine (from the ergot fungus *[[bloomfield-ergot-claviceps-purpurea-ergotism]]*).
Sandoz initially deemed LSD-25 pharmacologically uninteresting.

### Bicycle Day — April 19, 1943
On April 16, 1943, Hofmann re-synthesized LSD-25 and accidentally absorbed a
small amount through his fingertips. Three days later, on **April 19, 1943**
("Bicycle Day"), he deliberately ingested 250 µg (believed threshold — actually
very strong) and experienced profound effects during his famous bicycle ride
home. This date is celebrated annually by the psychedelic community.

### Sandoz Distribution (1947–1966)
Sandoz marketed LSD as **Delysid** from 1947 for psychiatric research. Over
1,000 clinical papers and 40,000 patients were involved between 1950 and 1965.
Distribution was voluntarily halted April 13, 1966 amid public concern.

## Pharmacology

LSD is a **partial agonist** at the **5-HT2A serotonin receptor** (Ki ≈ 1.9–3.8
nM) with broad serotonergic affinity: 5-HT2A (primary psychedelic effects),
5-HT2B, 5-HT2C, 5-HT1A (anxiolytic), D2/D3 dopamine receptors, and TAAR1.
Research (Nichols, Roth 2017+) shows LSD acts as a **biased agonist** at 5-HT2A,
preferentially activating β-arrestin over G-protein signaling — possibly
explaining its long duration and unique profile. **PK:** Onset 30–90 min; peak
3–5 h; duration 8–12 h; half-life ~3–5 h. Metabolized by hepatic CYP450
(CYP2D6, CYP1A2, CYP3A4). Renal excretion; detection 1–3 days urine.

## Dosage

| Level | Dose (µg) | Effects |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| Threshold | 15–25 | Minimal perceptible changes |
| Light | 25–75 | Enhanced colors, mild visual distortions |
| Common | 75–150 | Pronounced visuals, synesthesia, altered thinking |
| Strong | 150–300 | Intense hallucinations, [[psilocybin-ego-dissolution-mechanisms]], mystical states |
| Heavy | 300–500+ | Complete ego dissolution, overwhelming experience |

Active at microgram doses. **[[adverse-effects-and-harm-reduction-psilocybin]]:** Ehrlich's reagent turns purple;
NBOMe compounds are sometimes sold as LSD and are more dangerous.

## Effects

**Sensory:** Geometric patterns, color intensification, trailing, morphing
surfaces, closed-eye visuals, synesthesia, time dilation, enhanced music
appreciation. **Cognitive/Emotional:** Enhanced pattern recognition, ego
dissolution, emotional intensification, mystical-type experiences (unity,
transcendence, noetic quality). **Physiological:** Mydriasis, mild tachycardia,
nausea, insomnia, tremors, jaw clenching, hyperreflexia. All effects are
dose-dependent and influenced by [[lsd-set-and-setting-framework]].

## History of Use

### Early Medical Research (1950s–1960s)
**Humphry Osmond** and **Abram Hoffer** pioneered LSD therapy for alcoholism in
Saskatchewan (40–50% sobriety after single session). Osmond coined "psychedelic"
in 1956. **Ronald Sandison** established the first LSD psychotherapy clinic at
Powick Hospital, England (1952). **Stanislav Grof** conducted extensive research
in Czechoslovakia and Maryland. Applications: alcoholism, neurosis, OCD, autism,
terminal illness anxiety, criminal rehabilitation.

### CIA MK-Ultra
**Project MK-Ultra** (1953–1973), directed by **Sidney Gottlieb**, tested LSD on
unwitting subjects — soldiers, mental patients, civilians. **Operation Midnight
Climax** dosed patrons at CIA safehouses. **Frank Olson** (1953), an Army
scientist secretly dosed by Gottlieb, died under suspicious circumstances. The
Army tested LSD on ~7,000 soldiers at Edgewood Arsenal (1955–1975).

### 1960s Counterculture
**Ken Kesey** distributed LSD at "Acid Tests" with the Merry Pranksters.
**Timothy Leary** coined "Turn on, tune in, drop out." **Aldous Huxley**
requested LSD on his deathbed (November 22, 1963). LSD became synonymous with
the cultural revolution — psychedelic music (Beatles, Grateful Dead, Pink
Floyd), art, anti-war movement. Underground chemist **Owsley Stanley** produced
~5 million doses (1965–1967). The **Controlled [[controlled-substances-act-and-psychedelic-research]] (1970)** placed
LSD in **Schedule I**; the 1971 UN Convention established international control.

## Modern Research

LSD re-entered clinical investigation in the 2010s. **MAPS** (Rick Doblin)
funds LSD-assisted psychotherapy. **University Hospital Basel** (Matthias
Liechti) leads pharmacology: dose-response, LSD-music interactions, cluster
headache treatment. **MindMed** completed Phase 2 anxiety trials (2022–2024)
with positive results. **Imperial College London** researches brain mechanisms
via fMRI. Targets: depression, anxiety, end-of-life distress, cluster
headaches, alcoholism, PTSD. Microdosing (5–20 µg) is widely practiced but
evidence remains mixed.

## Safety

- **Lethal dose:** ~12,000–14,000 µg (~100× strong dose). No confirmed deaths
  solely from LSD overdose
- **Toxicity:** Not physiologically toxic; no neurotoxicity; no physical
  dependence or withdrawal
- **HPPD:** Hallucinogen Persisting Perception Disorder in ~4% — rare,
  generally manageable
- **Risks:** Bad trips (~5–15%), psychosis precipitation in vulnerable
  individuals, mania in bipolar disorder
- **Interactions:** Lithium strongly potentiates effects — avoid combination

## Legal Status

Schedule I in the US, Class A in the UK, controlled under the 1971 UN
