## Armillaria spp.

## Description

*Armillaria* species (honey fungi) are basidiomycete fungi belonging to the family Physalacriaceae. They are among the most significant root rot pathogens of woody plants worldwide. The genus is notable for its ability to form extensive rhizomorphs — root-like structures of aggregated hyphae with a melanized outer cortex — that enable long-distance spread through soil and infection of new hosts.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Physalacriaceae
- **Genus:** *Armillaria*

## Key Facts

- Rhizomorphs of *Armillaria* spp. are capable of accumulating heavy metals from the environment at concentrations 50–100 times greater than surrounding soil.
- Concentrations of Al, Zn, Fe, Cu, and Pb in rhizomorphs have been measured up to 3,440, 1,930, 1,890, 15, and 680 mg/g, respectively (Rizzo et al., 1992).
- X-ray dispersal electron microscopy (EDAX) showed that accumulated metal ions are concentrated in the outer portion of the [[rhizomorph]], not the interior.
- The melanized outer cortex of rhizomorphs may contribute to both heavy metal binding and [[ectomycorrhizal-pathogen-resistance-ecosystem-research]].
- Metal ion coating of the outer rhizomorph layers may play a key role in longevity and survival, acting as antagonists against other microorganisms and grazing fauna.
- Some *Armillaria* individuals are among the largest and oldest living organisms on Earth, with genets spanning hundreds of hectares.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

*Armillaria* is a major concern in orchard, vineyard, and plantation management as a destructive root rot pathogen. Understanding its [[dighton-fungal-heavy-metal-accumulation-bioremediation]] capacity is relevant to both [[plant-disease-ecology-and-organic-management]] bioremediation research. The rhizomorph's ability to concentrate metals in its outer layers without apparent toxicity suggests specialized detoxification mechanisms. In cultivation settings, *Armillaria* root rot is one of the most difficult diseases to manage due to the pathogen's persistence in soil through rhizomorphs and stumps.

## Ecological Role

- Primary root rot pathogen of hardwoods and conifers in forests worldwide
- Important decomposer of coarse woody debris as a saprotrophic alternative lifestyle
- Rhizomorphs serve as significant metal sinks in contaminated forest soils
- Formation of bioluminescent mycelia in some species (foxfire)
- Produces edible fruiting bodies (honey mushrooms), though proper identification is critical
