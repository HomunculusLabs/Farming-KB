## Overview

**Rubus idaeus** (red raspberry) is a deciduous perennial shrub in the family
Rosaceae, native to Europe and northern Asia. It produces aggregate fruits
composed of numerous drupelets, prized for their sweet-tart flavor and
aroma.

The species is among the most commercially important berry crops worldwide.
Global production exceeds 800,000 metric tons annually, with major growing
regions in Serbia, Russia, the [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]], and Poland.

Red raspberry is also valued in traditional [[childrens-herbal-medicine]] for its leaves,
which contain tannins and flavonoids with documented astringent and
anti-inflammatory properties.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Taxonomy:** Rosaceae, subfamily Rosoideae, genus Rubus, subgenus Idaeobatus.

- **Authority:** L. (1753)
- **Synonyms:** *Rubus idaeus* subsp. *vulgaris*, *Rubus idaeus* var.
  *strigosus* ([[growing-gourmet-north-american-reishi-species]] subspecies)
- **Chromosomes:** 2n = 14 (diploid)

The plant forms biennial canes from a perennial root system. Floricane
(first-year) canes produce leaves only; primocane (second-year) canes bear
fruit and then die back.

Canes grow 1.5–2.5 m tall with arching stems armed with sparse prickles.
Leaves are pinnate with 3–7 serrated leaflets, pale green to whitish
beneath (tomentose abaxial surface).

Flowers are white, 10–15 mm diameter, with five petals arranged in
cymose clusters. Each flower produces 60–120 drupelets that mature into
the characteristic aggregate fruit.

Fruits are 10–20 mm in diameter, red to deep crimson at maturity. Each
drupelet contains a single small seed (pyrene), giving the fruit its
characteristic texture.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Rubus idaeus* is native across temperate Europe, northern Asia, and
western [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]]. The [[north-american-dmt-containing-plants-phalaris-desmanthus-peganum]] form is sometimes treated as
subspecies *strigosus*.

The species colonizes disturbed sites, forest clearings, stream banks,
and mountain meadows. It spreads aggressively by rhizomes and root
suckers, forming dense thickets that exclude competing vegetation.

Optimal growth occurs in USDA hardiness zones 3–8. The plant requires
cold winter chilling (800–1600 hours below 7°C) for proper bud break and
fruit set.

It thrives in well-drained, acidic to neutral soils (pH 5.5–6.5) rich in
[[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]]. Natural populations are common along woodland edges and
in post-fire successional communities.

## Cultivation

Red raspberry cultivation centers on two fruiting types: **floricane-fruiting**
(summer-bearing) and **primocane-fruiting** (everbearing or fall-bearing).

Floricane types produce one large crop per season on second-year canes.
Primocane types fruit on first-year canes in late summer through autumn,
allowing double-cropping in mild climates.

Planting is typically done in [[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]] at 0.6–0.9 m spacing within
rows 2.5–3 m apart. Support trellises (V-shaped or T-bar wire systems)
are standard for commercial production to improve [[stamets-growing-room-air-circulation-co2-management]] and
harvest efficiency.
