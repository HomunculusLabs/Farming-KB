## Overview

**Thuja plicata**, commonly known as western redcedar, is a large evergreen conifer in the family Cupressaceae. It is native to the Pacific Northwest coast of North America, ranging from southern Alaska to northern California.

Despite its common name, western redcedar is not a true cedar (*Cedrus* spp.) but a member of the cypress family. The species is among the most culturally significant trees to Indigenous peoples of the Pacific Northwest.

Western redcedar is renowned for its exceptionally durable, decay-resistant heartwood, which has made it a premier timber species for outdoor construction applications for centuries.

Mature specimens can exceed 60 meters in height and live for over 1,000 years. The largest known individual, the Cheewhat Lake cedar on Vancouver Island, has a trunk volume exceeding 750 cubic meters.
