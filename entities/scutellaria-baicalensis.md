# Scutellaria baicalensis

## Overview

Scutellaria baicalensis, commonly known as Chinese skullcap or Huang Qin (黄芩), is one of the most important medicinal herbs in traditional Chinese medicine, with over 2,000 years of recorded use. The root of the plant, known as Scutellariae Radix, is a primary herb in numerous classical formulas and is valued for its ability to clear heat, dry dampness, purge fire, and resolve toxicity. Modern pharmacological research has identified over 50 flavonoid compounds, with baicalin, baicalein, and wogonin being the most pharmacologically significant. These compounds demonstrate potent anti-inflammatory, antiviral, antioxidant, neuroprotective, and anticancer activities through multiple molecular pathways. Scutellaria baicalensis is one of the most extensively researched Chinese herbs in modern biomedical science.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Clade:** Tracheophytes (vascular plants)
- **Clade:** Angiosperms (flowering plants)
- **Clade:** Eudicots
- **Clade:** Asterids
- **Order:** Lamiales
- **Family:** Lamiaceae (mint family)
- **Genus:** Scutellaria
- **Species:** S. baicalensis Georgi, 1802
- **Common names:** Chinese skullcap, Huang Qin (黄芩), Baikal skullcap, Scutellaria root
- **Etymology:** "Scutellaria" from Latin "scutella" meaning "small dish" or "bowl," referring to the calyx shape. "Baicalensis" refers to Lake Baikal in Siberia, near part of its native range. "Huang Qin" translates as "yellow root" in Chinese, describing the distinctive yellow color of the dried root.

## Description

- **Growth habit:** Perennial herb, 20–60 cm tall. Upright, branching stems arising from a woody rootstock. Dies back to ground level in winter and regrows each spring.
- **Leaves:** Opposite, simple, lanceolate to ovate-lanceolate, 2–5 cm long and 0.5–2 cm wide. Margins entire or shallowly crenate. Dark green above, grayish-green below with short pubescence. Petiolate with a pubescent petiole.
- **Flowers:** Bilabiate (two-lipped), typical of the mint family. Blue-violet to purple, 1.5–2.5 cm long, borne in one-sided racemes in the axils of upper leaves. Upper lip hooded, lower lip three-lobed. Blooms July–August.
- **Fruit:** Small nutlets (schizocarps), approximately 2 mm, dark brown to black, borne on a flattened, concave persistent calyx (the "skullcap" that gives the genus its name).
- **Roots:** The primary medicinal organ. Long, cylindrical taproot, 10–30 cm long and 1–3 cm in diameter. Outer surface dark brown, inner tissue bright yellow — the yellow pigment is a hallmark of the flavonoid-rich root. When broken, the root shows a characteristic yellow cross-section.
- **Whole plant:** Aromatic when crushed, though less strongly scented than many Lamiaceae members. The distinctive yellow root color distinguishes it from the American skullcap (Scutellaria lateriflora).

## Habitat and Distribution

- **Native range:** Mongolia, northern China (Inner Mongolia, Hebei, Shanxi, Gansu, Shaanxi), eastern Siberia, and the Korean Peninsula. Found in the Lake Baikal region of Russia.
- **Naturalized range:** Limited naturalization; primarily a cultivated species outside its native range.
- **Habitat:** Dry, sunny slopes, grassy steppes, open woodlands, and rocky hillsides at 600–2,000 m elevation. Prefers well-drained sandy or loamy soils, full sun, and moderate moisture.
- **Cultivation:** Major commercial production in China (Hebei, Shanxi, Inner Mongolia, Gansu, and Shandong provinces). Cultivation has expanded to meet growing pharmaceutical demand. Roots are typically harvested after 2–3 years of growth.
- **Propagation:** By seed (sown in spring after stratification) or by root division. Seed germination rates improve with cold stratification at 4°C for 2–4 weeks.

## Chemistry and Bioactive Compounds

Scutellaria baicalensis root contains a rich profile of flavonoids and other bioactive compounds:

### Major flavonoids

- **Baicalin (baicalein-7-O-glucuronide):** The most abundant flavonoid, constituting 10–20% of dry root weight. Hydrolyzed by intestinal β-glucuronidase to the aglycone baicalein. Demonstrates potent anti-inflammatory, antiviral, and antioxidant activity. Inhibits COX-2, iNOS, and NF-κB signaling.
- **Baicalein (aglycone of baicalin):** Potent lipoxygenase (12-LOX) inhibitor with significant anti-inflammatory and neuroprotective activity. Scavenges reactive oxygen species and inhibits lipid peroxidation. Demonstrates antitumor activity through multiple mechanisms including apoptosis induction and cell cycle arrest.
- **Wogonin (8-methoxybaicalein):** A unique methoxylated flavone with selective anti-inflammatory activity. Induces apoptosis in cancer cells while sparing normal cells. Demonstrates anxiolytic (anti-anxiety) activity through modulation of GABA_A receptors without the sedative effects of benzodiazepines.
- **Wogonoside:** The glucuronide of wogonin, similar pharmacological profile to its aglycone but with different pharmacokinetic properties.
- **Oroxylin A:** A methoxylated flavone with neuroprotective and anti-inflammatory activity. Enhances memory consolidation in animal models.

### Other constituents

- **Scutellarin:** A flavone glycoside with cerebrovascular protective effects, used clinically in China for ischemic stroke.
- **Skullcapflavone I and II:** Minor flavonoids with antibacterial and antifungal activity.
- **β-Sitosterol and stigmasterol:** Phytosterols with cholesterol-lowering and anti-inflammatory properties.
- **Volatile oils:** Including caryophyllene and germacrene D.
- **Polysaccharides:** Immunomodulatory activity through activation of macrophages.

## Pharmacology and Traditional Uses

### Traditional Chinese Medicine classification

In TCM, Scutellaria baicalensis root (Huang Qin) is classified as bitter, cold, and entering the Lung, Gallbladder, Stomach, and Large Intestine meridians. Its primary actions are clearing heat and drying dampness (especially in the upper and middle jiao), purging fire and resolving toxicity, stopping bleeding, and preventing miscarriage. It is one of the most frequently used herbs in the Chinese pharmacopoeia, appearing in hundreds of classical formulas.

### Anti-inflammatory activity

- Baicalin and baicalein inhibit NF-κB, MAPK, and AP-1 inflammatory signaling cascades
- Suppresses pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8) in multiple cell types
- Inhibits COX-2 and 5-LOX arachidonic acid metabolic pathways simultaneously
- Demonstrates efficacy in models of rheumatoid arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease, and allergic asthma

### Antiviral activity

- Inhibits replication of influenza A and B viruses, including oseltamivir-resistant strains
- Active against hepatitis B and C viruses through multiple mechanisms
- Demonstrates inhibitory effects against HIV, SARS-CoV, and SARS-CoV-2 in vitro
- Baicalin interferes with viral neuraminidase activity and viral entry processes

### Neuroprotective and anxiolytic effects

- Baicalein protects neurons from glutamate-induced excitotoxicity and oxidative stress
- Wogonin acts as a positive allosteric modulator of GABA_A receptors, producing anxiolytic effects without sedation, motor impairment, or tolerance development
- Improves cognitive function in animal models of Alzheimer's disease by reducing amyloid-beta aggregation and tau phosphorylation
- Scutellarin improves cerebral blood flow and protects against ischemic stroke damage

### Hepatoprotective effects

- Protects hepatocytes from damage induced by acetaminophen, carbon tetrachloride, and alcohol
- Reduces hepatic fibrosis by inhibiting hepatic stellate cell activation and TGF-β signaling
- Reduces elevated liver enzymes (ALT, AST) in clinical trials of patients with chronic hepatitis

### Anticancer activity

- Baicalein, wogonin, and baicalin demonstrate anticancer activity against multiple cancer cell lines including breast, prostate, lung, colon, liver, and ovarian cancers
- Mechanisms include induction of apoptosis, cell cycle arrest, inhibition of angiogenesis, and suppression of metastasis
- Wogonin selectively induces apoptosis in tumor cells through ROS-mediated pathways while protecting normal cells

### Antibacterial and antifungal activity

- Baicalein and baicalin demonstrate broad-spectrum antibacterial activity against Gram-positive bacteria (Staphylococcus aureus, Streptococcus pneumoniae, Bacillus subtilis) and moderate activity against Gram-negative species
- Baicalin disrupts bacterial biofilm formation, a key virulence factor in chronic infections
- Skullcapflavone II and other minor flavonoids exhibit antifungal activity against Candida species
- Synergistic effects with conventional antibiotics have been observed, suggesting potential for overcoming antimicrobial resistance

## Legal Status and Safety

- **Regulatory status:** Approved as a dietary supplement in the United States and European Union. Listed in the Chinese Pharmacopoeia (2020 edition) with quality standards for baicalin content (not less than 9.0%). Included in the Japanese Pharmacopoeia. Used as an official ingredient in approved pharmaceutical formulations in China and Japan.
- **Contraindications:** Contraindicated in TCM for patients with cold-dampness or spleen/stomach deficiency due to its cold, bitter nature. Caution during pregnancy (though traditionally used to prevent miscarriage, modern safety data is limited). Use with caution in patients on anticoagulant therapy.
- **Drug interactions:** May potentiate the effects of sedatives, antihypertensives, and anticoagulants. May interact with cytochrome P450 enzymes (particularly CYP1A2, CYP2C9, and CYP3A4), potentially affecting the metabolism of concurrent medications.
- **Side effects:** Generally well-tolerated at recommended doses (3–10 g dried root daily in decoctions, or 200–500 mg standardized extract). Rare reports of gastrointestinal upset, dizziness, or mild allergic reactions.
