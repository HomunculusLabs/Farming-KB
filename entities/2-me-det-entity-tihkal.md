## 2-Me-DET (N,N-Diethyl-2-Methyltryptamine)

TIHKAL entry #33. 2-Me-DET is a [[synthetic-tryptamine-methods]] combining the diethyl substitution pattern of DET with a methyl group at the 2-position of the indole ring. It is notable for producing auditory distortion effects reminiscent of [[4-ho-dipt-entity-tihkal]], making it a compound of particular interest for understanding how structural modifications selectively alter sensory processing.

- **70 mg, orally**: "A very subtle onset characterized most notably as a mild stomach ache that lasted a short while. There was a sort of vague unreal feeling at an hour, but my thought pattern seemed to be quite clear. In another hour I noticed that higher pitches of the music on the radio were being muffled and the tones seemed to be shifting to lower frequency. I phoned a friend, and first the dial tone, and then her voice sounded wrong. The sense of touch (the phone receiver) was normal and my conversation flowed easily. Reading didn"t seem to connect, and I had to turn the radio off as it was lousy. Texture, not content. I could type OK, and did, so my body was OK, but things came to me slowly. I wasn't very hungry but food tasted OK. The 'cloudy' was pretty much gone after six hours. I don't particularly want to repeat this, as there isn't much here that I enjoy."

## Historical Context

2-Me-DET represents one of the more pharmacologically interesting entries in TIHKAL due to its unexpected auditory distortion effects. The discovery that a 2-methyl substitution on the indole ring could selectively enhance auditory effects — a property previously associated primarily with DIPT — opened a new line of inquiry into how structural modifications can produce sensory modality-specific alterations.

## Modern Significance

The auditory selectivity of 2-Me-DET remains relevant to contemporary neuroscience research into auditory processing pathways. The compound's ability to produce pitch-shifting effects without visual alterations suggests a degree of receptor or neural pathway specificity that could be exploited in basic research on the neurobiology of hearing.

Shulgin's proposal to synthesize 2-Me-DIPT, combining the diisopropyl and 2-methyl modifications, has been taken up by subsequent researchers and the compound is now available as a research chemical, with reports generally confirming the predicted enhancement of auditory effects.
