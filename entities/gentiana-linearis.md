## Overview

Gentiana linearis, commonly called the narrowleaf gentian, is a perennial herb native to northern [[jeavons-climate-adaptation-growing-seasons]]. It is one of the hardiest gentians in [[gentiana-nivalis]] and the Asian Gentiana aquatica. These species share the narrow [[psilocybe-semilanceata-transcontinental-distribution-patterns]] across northern North America. It occurs from Alaska across the Canadian Arctic and subarctic to Labrador and Newfoundland.

The southern extensions of its range reach the northern Rocky Mountains of Alberta and British Columbia. Disjunct populations occur in the White Mountains of New Hampshire and mountains of Maine.

It occupies moist to wet habitats including alpine meadows, stream margins, seepage slopes, and wet tundra. Elevational range extends from sea level in the Arctic to 2,500 m in the Rockies.

The species favors acidic to neutral substrates, often growing on peat or organic soils. It is commonly associated with mosses and sedges in wetland communities.

Populations are generally stable across the core boreal range. Southern populations are small, isolated, and vulnerable to warming temperatures.

Climate envelope models project significant northward range contraction. Southern Appalachian populations are at particular risk of extirpation by mid-century.

## Ecology

Flowering occurs from July to September, depending on latitude and elevation. Arctic populations flower in a compressed 3–4 week window during the brief summer.

The small flowers are pollinated by a variety of small bees and flies. Bumblebees also visit but are less specialized on the small corollas.

Gentiana linearis is a long-lived perennial with a deep, fibrous root system. It reproduces primarily through seed, with limited vegetative spread.

Plants often grow in dense clusters resulting from local seed dispersal. These clusters can persist for decades in stable alpine and boreal habitats.

The species shows adaptations to cold stress, including a low growth form and dark pigmentation that enhances heat absorption. Leaf anatomy includes thickened cuticles and sunken stomata.

Winter survival depends on persistent snow cover that insulates the crown from extreme cold. Shallow snow or exposed sites can result in winterkill.

## Cultivation

Gentiana linearis is rarely cultivated but can be grown with attention to its specific requirements. It needs moist, acidic, organic soil and cool temperatures.

Seeds require 90–120 days of cold stratification for germination. Surface sow on peat-based mix in fall for natural stratification.

Germination rates are typically low (10–30%). Seedlings are tiny and require careful attention to moisture during their first growing season.
