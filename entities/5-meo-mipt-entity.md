# 5-MeO-MiPT (*5-Methoxy-N-methyl-N-isopropyltryptamine*)

## Overview

5-MeO-MiPT (5-methoxy-N-methyl-N-isopropyltryptamine) is a synthetic psychedelic tryptamine and entactogen, classified as a member of the MiPT family described by Alexander Shulgin in TiHKAL.

Known colloquially as "Moxy," it produces a distinctive profile combining mild visual effects with pronounced tactile and emotional sensitivity, distinguishing it from closely related compounds like 5-MeO-DiPT ("Foxy").

The compound occupies an unusual pharmacological niche: psychedelic at moderate doses but with serotonergic entactogen qualities more commonly associated with phenethylamines than tryptamines.

## Chemical Properties

- **IUPAC name:** 5-methoxy-N-methyl-N-propan-2-yl-1H-indole-3-ethanamine
- **Molecular formula:** C₁₅H₂₂N₂O
- **Molecular weight:** 246.35 g/mol
- **CAS number:** 860622-50-2
- **SMILES:** COc1ccc2[nH]c(CCN(C)C(C)C)cc2c1
- **Appearance:** White to off-white crystalline powder
- **Solubility:** Freely soluble in ethanol, slightly soluble in water

The 5-methoxy substitution on the indole ring increases lipophilicity and CNS penetration relative to unsubstituted MiPT.

The N-isopropyl group provides metabolic stability compared to N-methyl analogs, contributing to a longer duration of action than 5-MeO-DMT.

## Pharmacology

5-MeO-MiPT acts primarily as a serotonin 5-HT₂A receptor agonist, the canonical mechanism underlying classical psychedelic effects.

Additional receptor interactions include partial agonism at 5-HT₁A receptors, which may contribute to the compound's anxiolytic and entactogenic qualities.

Limited evidence suggests weak activity at 5-HT₂C and trace binding to serotonin transporter (SERT), potentially accounting for mild empathogenic effects reported by some users.

The compound shows negligible affinity for dopamine D₂ and norepinephrine transporters at typical doses, distinguishing it from classical entactogens like MDMA.

## Effects

Onset occurs within 15–30 minutes when taken orally. Peak effects manifest at 60–90 minutes, with total duration of 4–6 hours.

The experience is characterized by a body-dominant profile with moderate visual alterations, rather than the intense visual hallucinations of psilocybin or DMT.

**Common effects:**

- Enhanced tactile sensitivity — often described as the most prominent effect, with skin becoming unusually sensitive to touch and texture
- Mild to moderate visual distortions — color enhancement, patterning, and light sensitivity without full hallucination
- Emotional openness and empathic feelings — reduced social anxiety and enhanced interpersonal connection
- Mild euphoria with a calm, relaxed mental state
- Increased music appreciation and sensory pleasure

**Uncommon effects:**

- Nausea during onset (relatively common at higher doses)
- Mild visual geometry and patterning
- Enhanced introspection without the intensity of 5-MeO-DMT
- Muscle tension or jaw clenching at higher doses

## Dosage

| Route | Threshold | Light | Common | Strong |
|-------|-----------|-------|--------|--------|
| Oral | 2–4 mg | 4–6 mg | 6–10 mg | 10–15 mg |
| Insufflated | 1–2 mg | 2–3 mg | 3–5 mg | 5–8 mg |

⚠️ Doses above 15 mg (oral) or 8 mg (insufflated) significantly increase risk of anxiety, nausea, and overwhelming somatic sensations. This compound has a steep dose-response curve in the upper range.

## Legal Status

5-MeO-MiPT is unscheduled in most jurisdictions but is controlled under analogue acts in several countries.

In the United States, it may be prosecuted under the Federal Analog Act as an analogue of 5-MeO-DiPT (a Schedule I substance).

The United Kingdom classifies it as a Class A drug under the Psychoactive Substances Act amendments.

Japan, Sweden, and Australia have specifically scheduled 5-MeO-MiPT as a controlled substance.

Legal status varies widely across jurisdictions and changes frequently; users should verify current local regulations.

## History

5-MeO-MiPT was first synthesized and characterized by Alexander Shulgin, with findings published in TiHKAL (1997).

Shulgin assigned it a +++ rating on his subjective scale and noted its unusual entactogen-psychedelic character for a tryptamine.

The compound gained popularity in the research chemical market during the 2000s–2010s as an alternative to scheduled psychedelics.

It remains available through online vendors in jurisdictions where it is unscheduled, though its availability has declined since the mid-2010s due to increasing regulatory pressure on tryptamine analogues.

Analytical detection methods (LC-MS, GC-MS) have been developed for forensic identification in seized materials.
