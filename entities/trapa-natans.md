# Trapa natans (*Water Caltrop, Water Chestnut, Singhara*)

## Overview

Trapa natans is an annual aquatic plant of the family Lythraceae,
floating on still or slow-moving freshwater bodies across Eurasia.
It produces distinctive horned fruits containing a single starchy,
edible seed that has been harvested as food for over 3,000 years in
China, India, and Europe. The plant forms dense floating mats that
provide habitat complexity but can become invasive outside its native
range. Its edible seeds (singhara) are a seasonal food crop and
traditional medicine ingredient in South Asia.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae → **Order:** Myrtales → **Family:** Lythraceae
- **Genus:** Trapa → **Species:** T. natans L.
- **Synonyms:** T. bispinosa Roxb., T. quadrispinosa, T. japonica
- **Common names:** Water caltrop (English), Singhara (Hindi/Urdu),
  Ling (Chinese), Hishi (Japanese), Water chestnut (English, not to
  be confused with Eleocharis dulcis)
- **Etymology:** From Latin *trapa*, a type of caltrop (four-pointed
  iron spike); refers to the fruit's horned appearance

Taxonomic complexity: The genus has been placed in Trapaceae,
Onagraceae, and now Lythraceae based on molecular phylogeny. Multiple
species are recognized but often treated as T. natans sensu lato.

## Morphology

### Vegetative Structures

- **Growth habit:** Annual, free-floating aquatic herb; anchored to
  substrate by submerged stems in shallow water
- **Submerged stem:** Slender, 1–5 m long, bearing feathery submerged
  leaves and functioning as anchor
- **Floating leaves:** Rosette of triangular to rhombic leaves, 2–5 cm,
  with inflated spongy petiole (float) up to 15 cm
- **Root system:** Fibrous roots from submerged stem nodes

### Reproductive Structures

- **Flowers:** Small, white, 4-petaled, 6–12 mm, borne in leaf axils;
  short-lived (one day); insect-pollinated
- **Fruit:** Woody nut, 2.5–5 cm, bearing 2–4 sharp backward-pointing
  horns; matures underwater; [[cervantes-selfing-single-seed-descent]] per fruit
- **Seeds:** 1–2 cm diameter; starchy white kernel inside hard shell
- **[[query-how-do-i-create-a-seed-bank-and-start-seed-saving]]:** Viable 2–12 years in sediment

## Distribution and Habitat

Native to warm temperate and subtropical Eurasia: Europe (native
west/central, naturalized east), China (primary cultivation), India,
Japan, Korea, [[psilocybe-southeast-asia-pacific]]. Invasive in northeastern [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]],
parts of Australia and Africa.

Habitat: still or slow freshwater (ponds, lakes, backwaters, rice
paddies), 0.5–3 m depth, nutrient-rich substrate, water temperature
20–30°C. Tolerates moderate turbidity.

## Nutritional Composition

Fresh kernels (100 g): carbohydrates 18–22% (starch-dominant), protein
3–5%, fat 0.1–0.2%, fiber 2–4%, ~97 kcal. Minerals: potassium (450 mg),
phosphorus (80 mg), calcium (20 mg), iron (1.5 mg/100 g). Vitamins C, B6,
riboflavin, thiamine. Starch granule size 3–8 μm, low amylose, highly
digestible. Singhara flour is used in Indian fasting foods.
