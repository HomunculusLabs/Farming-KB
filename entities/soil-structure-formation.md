## Description

Soil structure refers to the arrangement of soil particles into aggregates (clusters of sand, silt, clay, and organic matter bound together). Unlike soil texture (which is determined by particle size and cannot be changed), soil structure is dynamic and is created and maintained primarily by the activities of soil food web organisms. Good soil structure provides balanced air and water movement, root penetration pathways, and habitat for soil biology.

## Classification

- **Domain**: Soil science (pedology)
- **Category**: Soil physical property
- **Distinguished from**: Soil texture (particle size — inherent and unchangeable)
- **Key feature**: Dynamic and manageable through biological activity

## Mechanisms of Structure Formation

### Bacterial Contribution
- Bacteria produce sticky slime (biofilm/exopolysaccharides) to attach themselves to soil particles.
- This slime acts as a binding agent, gluing individual soil particles together into microaggregates.
- The analogy used by Lowenfels: plaque produced overnight in the mouth enables bacteria to stick to teeth — soil bacterial slime works similarly on mineral particles.

### Fungal Contribution
- Fungal hyphae grow through soil, physically threading between and around particles.
- Hyphae stick to soil particles and bind them together into larger aggregates.
- Mycorrhizal fungal networks are particularly important for aggregate formation in the root zone.
- Fungal-produced glomalin (a glycoprotein) is one of the most important binding agents for soil aggregates.

### Faunal Contribution
- Earthworms, insect larvae, and moles create burrows and tunnels as they move through soil searching for food.
- These pathways allow air and water to enter and leave the soil, improving porosity.
- Earthworm casts (vermicast) are highly structured micro-aggregates rich in nutrients and microbial life.
- Arthropods shred organic matter, opening it up for further bacterial and fungal colonization.

### Root Contribution
- Plant roots push through soil, creating channels.
- Root exudates feed the bacteria and fungi that bind particles around root channels.
- As roots grow and die back, they leave behind organic channels that maintain porosity.

## Key Facts

- Soil structure and the soil food web are inseparable: the biology creates the structure, and the structure provides habitat for the biology.
- Without a functioning soil food web, soil structure deteriorates: compaction increases, drainage decreases, and air exchange fails.
- An acre of good garden soil contains 900 lbs each of earthworms and arthropods — all constantly engineering soil structure.
- Aggregates range in size from microaggregates (<0.25mm, bound by bacterial slime and fungal hyphae) to macroaggregates (>0.25mm, bound by roots and fungal networks).

## Impacts of Poor Soil Structure

- Compacted soil has reduced pore space, limiting both water infiltration and air exchange.
- Without adequate air, aerobic organisms die and anaerobic conditions develop, producing alcohols and other compounds toxic to plant roots.
- Water runs off compacted soil rather than infiltrating, increasing erosion and decreasing plant-available moisture.
- Root penetration becomes physically difficult, limiting plant access to water and nutrients.

## What Destroys Soil Structure

- **Rototilling**: Breaks fungal hyphae networks, decimates worms, crushes arthropods, destroys existing aggregates. Initially fluffs soil but degrades structure long-term.
- **Chemical fertilizers**: Salt-based fertilizers kill soil organisms through osmotic dehydration, removing the biological engineers that maintain structure.
- **Pesticides, fungicides, herbicides**: Kill beneficial organisms directly or chase them away.
- **Compaction**: Foot traffic, heavy equipment, and working wet soil crush aggregates.
- **Bare soil**: Without living roots and surface cover, aggregates break down under rain impact and UV exposure.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- Healthy soil structure is the primary reason why no-till and reduced-till methods produce superior long-term results.
- Mycorrhizal fungal networks are major structural engineers — soils with active mycorrhizal associations have significantly better aggregation and porosity.
- In mushroom bed preparation, creating a well-structured substrate with adequate pore space for air exchange while maintaining moisture is directly analogous to maintaining good soil structure.
- Compost applications improve soil structure by adding organic matter (binding agent) and introducing diverse microbial communities (structural engineers).
- Cover crops maintain soil structure by keeping living roots in the ground year-round, feeding the microbial community that binds aggregates.
- The concept that "gardening with the soil food web is easy" is fundamentally about maintaining biological soil structure: let the organisms do the work of building and maintaining the physical environment.

## Practical Assessment

- **Ribbon test**: Squeeze moist soil between thumb and finger. Long ribbons indicate high clay and potential structure issues; crumbly texture indicates good granular structure.
- **Drainage test**: Dig a hole, fill with water. If it drains within hours, structure is good. Days indicates poor structure.
- **Earthworm count**: Dig a cubic foot of soil. More than 10 earthworms generally indicates good soil structure and biology.
