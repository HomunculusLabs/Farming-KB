## Description

A.B. Watt is an ecologist cited by Mollison as one of the formulators of "the guiding principles and laws of natural systems" alongside Odum and Birch. Mollison specifically credits Watt with restating the basic law of thermodynamics in ecological terms: "All energy entering an organism, population or ecosystem can be accounted for as energy which is stored or leaves. Energy can be transferred from one form to another, but it cannot disappear, or be destroyed, or created. No energy conversion system is ever completely efficient."

This restatement of the first law of thermodynamics in ecological language provides the scientific foundation for permaculture's emphasis on energy accounting — tracking where energy enters a system, how it is stored or transformed, and where it leaves.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Watt's ecological restatement of thermodynamic principles is foundational to cultivation system design:

1. **Energy accounting**: Every cultivation system receives energy (sunlight, organic inputs, water flow, human labor). Watt's principle requires designers to account for all energy — what is stored in biomass, what is exported as product, and what is lost as waste.
2. **No perfect efficiency**: The recognition that "no energy conversion system is ever completely efficient" means that every stage of a cultivation process loses some energy. Designing multiple stages of energy capture (e.g., sunlight to plants to compost to fungi to soil) reduces total losses.
3. **Storage optimization**: If energy entering an organism or ecosystem is either stored or leaves, the designer's job is to maximize storage and minimize unproductive losses. In fungal cultivation, this means capturing the heat, CO2, and spent substrate energy rather than letting them escape.
4. **System boundaries**: Watt's principle requires defining clear system boundaries to track energy flows, which is essential for designing and evaluating cultivation systems of any scale.
