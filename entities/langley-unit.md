## Description

The Langley (Ly) is a unit of solar radiation measurement defined as one gram calorie per square centimeter (1 cal/cm2). It is used to quantify the total energy received from the sun at the Earth's surface, accounting for atmospheric attenuation, latitude, slope angle, and cloud cover.

## Classification

- **Category:** Unit of measurement
- **Domain:** Meteorology, solar energy, climatology
- **Unit symbol:** Ly
- **SI equivalent:** 1 Langley = 41,840 J/m2
- **Named after:** Samuel Pierpont Langley (1834-1906), American astronomer and solar radiation researcher

## Key Facts

### Solar Radiation Intensity

- The sun delivers approximately **2 Langleys per minute** to the outer atmosphere.
- At ground level, the actual Langleys received vary enormously due to:
  - **Latitude and season:** Which determines the depth of atmosphere the radiation must pass through.
  - **Slope angle:** Which affects reflection and absorption on the ground surface.
  - **Atmospheric content:** Ice, water vapour, dust, and cloud all attenuate incoming radiation.

### Atmospheric Attenuation

The atmosphere dramatically reduces incoming solar radiation:

- **Sun directly overhead (clear day):** Only 22% of radiant energy (1 atmosphere depth) penetrates to ground level.
- **Polar regions (sun at 5 degrees elevation):** As little as 1% of incoming energy reaches the surface, as radiation passes through 11 atmospheres of oblique path length.
- **Slope effects:** Pole-facing slopes receive even less energy than flat ground at the same latitude.

### Practical Significance

Despite these losses, Mollison notes that most homes receive enough sunlight on their sun-facing areas to heat both water and interior space, provided systems are designed to capture and store this heat.

### Related Measurements

The Langley has been largely superseded by W/m2 (watts per square meter) in modern scientific use, but remains referenced in [[permaculture]] and agricultural literature. Conversion: 1 Ly/min = approximately 697.8 W/m2.

## Relevance to Cultivation

1. **[[greenhouse-design]]:** Calculating the solar energy available for passive heating of growing structures.
2. **Site selection:** Choosing sun-facing slopes that maximize Langleys received for both house siting and crop production.
3. **Solar thermal systems:** Sizing solar hot water and space heating collectors based on local radiation data.
4. **Season extension:** Estimating available energy for growing crops outside their normal range by concentrating radiation.
5. **Photosynthetic planning:** Different crops require different light intensities; radiation data helps match crops to sites.
