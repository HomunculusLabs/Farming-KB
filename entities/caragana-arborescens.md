## Edible Uses

The young pods are edible when harvested green and tender, before the seeds harden, typically 2 to 3 weeks after flowering.
They can be eaten raw, steamed, boiled, or added to stir-fries, with a mild pea-like flavor.
The mature seeds can be cooked as a pulse similar to lentils or chickpeas, though they require boiling in two changes of water to remove mild bitterness.
Seeds contain approximately 30 percent protein, 10 percent fat, and 35 percent carbohydrate on a dry weight basis.
Flowers are edible and can be added to salads. Caragana was historically an important food plant in Siberia and Mongolia.

## Conservation Status

Caragana arborescens is not considered threatened and is assessed as Least
Concern by conservation authorities. It is widely naturalized across
northern North America, Europe, and parts of Central Asia beyond its
native range in Siberia, Mongolia, and northern China. It is considered
mildly invasive in some regions, particularly the northern Great Plains
of the United States and the Canadian Prairies, where it can spread from
shelterbelt plantings into adjacent grasslands. No active conservation
programs exist for the species, and it is readily available from nurseries
and seed suppliers worldwide.
