## Description

Basalt rock dust is a finely ground material produced from basalt, a dark, dense volcanic rock rich in iron, magnesium, calcium, and a broad spectrum of trace elements. It is used as a soil amendment to remineralise leached and depleted soils, particularly in tropical regions where heavy rainfall has stripped soluble nutrients from the soil profile. Basalt dust is often available cheaply as a byproduct from quarries and gravel pits.

## Classification

- **Category**: Volcanic igneous rock dust
- **Rock type**: Mafic extrusive igneous rock (basalt)
- **Key mineral constituents**: Plagioclase feldspar, pyroxene, olivine, magnetite
- **Rich in**: Iron, magnesium, calcium, silicon, manganese, zinc, copper, cobalt

## Key Facts

- Basalt is one of the most common rock types on Earth, forming the ocean floor and large continental flood basalt provinces
- When finely ground, basalt dust slowly releases a wide spectrum of minerals into the soil through weathering
- Particularly effective on leached tropical soils (oxisols and ultisols) where intense rainfall has depleted calcium, magnesium, and trace elements
- Field trials have shown that cheap, finely ground ores are as effective as more refined sulphates or oxides for soil remineralisation
- Available as a byproduct from quarries and gravel pits, making it one of the most economical mineral amendments
- Rock dust as an unselective category can do harm on some soils -- adding excessive or toxic nutrients, or excessive micronutrients if applied indiscriminately
- Basalt weathering also contributes to long-term carbon sequestration through enhanced rock weathering (ERW), absorbing CO2 from the atmosphere

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- Restores calcium and magnesium levels in acidic, leached soils, improving pH buffering and nutrient availability
- The silicon released from basalt weathering strengthens plant cell walls and can improve resistance to fungal pathogens and insect pests
- In mushroom cultivation, trace minerals from basalt may support enzyme production in mycelium
- Permaculture systems use basalt dust as part of broad-scale soil rehabilitation strategies on degraded land
- The slow-release nature of rock dust minerals means benefits accumulate over years rather than providing an immediate nutrient spike
- Basalt-derived soils (Andisols) are among the most naturally fertile agricultural soils globally
- Must be applied according to soil test results -- indiscriminate application can create imbalances or toxicity
