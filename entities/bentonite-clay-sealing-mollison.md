## Description

Bentonite is a slippery clay powder derived from volcanic ash that swells dramatically when wetted. In permaculture and earthworks, it is used as a sealing agent for porous soils in dam and pond construction. When rototilled into clay-loam soils at shallow depth and rolled down, bentonite forms an impervious layer that prevents water seepage. It is one of five sealing methods described by Mollison for leaky dams and water storages.

## Classification

- **Category**: Mineral / Construction material
- **Domain**: Geology, civil engineering, permaculture water storage
- **Origin**: Derived from volcanic ash deposits
- **Physical form**: Fine clay powder

## Key Facts

### Properties

- Naturally derived from weathered volcanic ash
- Exhibits extreme swelling behavior when hydrated
- Forms an impervious barrier when compacted
- Slippery, plastic consistency when wet

### Application Method

1. Rototill bentonite powder into the soil at 5–7 cm (2–3 inches) depth
2. Roll down firmly to compact the treated layer
3. The bentonite swells on contact with water, sealing pores in the soil matrix

### Performance Characteristics

- Effective in clay-loam soils
- Expensive relative to other sealing methods
- Does not always work reliably
- Performance varies with soil composition and application quality

### Alternative Approaches

- Cement and tamping plus sprinkling may be preferable in some conditions
- Bituminous spray rolled in after tilling can substitute
- In naturally clay-rich soils, salt or sodium carbonate can achieve similar sealing effects



### Cost Considerations

Bentonite is noted as expensive compared to biological sealing methods like gley. Its use is most justified when local clay sources are unavailable, gley materials are scarce, rapid predictable sealing is needed, or the storage is high-value (house water supply).



## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Water storage sealed with bentonite enables reliable water supply in porous-soil regions where cultivation would otherwise be impractical. For mushroom cultivation, sealed ponds ensure consistent water availability for substrate moisture management, misting systems, and climate control in growing chambers. The inert nature of bentonite means it does not introduce chemicals that could affect fungal substrates.



## Comparison with Other Sealing Methods

| Method | Cost | Reliability | Biological Compatibility |
|--------|------|-------------|------------------------|
| **Gley** | Very low | Good | Excellent |
| **Bentonite** | High | Variable | Good |
| **Clay** | Moderate | Good | Good |
| **Explosives** | Moderate | Unpredictable | Poor |
| **Membranes** | Very high | High | Poor (without soil overlay) |
