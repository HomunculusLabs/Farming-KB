# Limanim (Israeli Run-Off Traps)

## Description

Limanim (singular: liman) are dry dams developed in Israel for harvesting runoff in gently-sloping uplands with well-developed valley runoff during rainy periods. They are essentially impoundment structures ranging from 0.1 to 0.5 hectares in size, designed to capture and infiltrate runoff into deep [[loess-soils]] for tree establishment. The system was developed to create tree-covered areas in arid upland regions without irrigation infrastructure.

## Classification

- **Type**: Water harvesting structure / dry dam system
- **Region of origin**: Israel (Negev Desert and semi-arid uplands)
- **Soil requirement**: Loess soils enabling deep infiltration
- **Scale**: 0.1–0.5 hectare impoundment area per unit

## Key Facts

- Each liman consists of a flat-graded basin enclosed by earth walls 1–2 m high and 2–5 m wide.
- The inside base of the bank is often supported with stone to prevent erosion.
- Sill spillways at the ends of the walls allow water to spill at 40–60 cm depth, flowing to the next limanim downstream.
- The runoff catchment area is 30–500 times the area of the impoundment — an enormous ratio reflecting the aridity of the landscape.
- Trees are established directly within these impoundments, surviving on the stored soil moisture from periodic flood events.
- Catch-cropping is possible in wet years when excess water accumulates.
- Soil must be kept open by tillage, and livestock compaction must be prevented to maintain infiltration capacity.
- Water is led in gently via low-slope diversion drains or sheet flow to minimize silting and sill erosion.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Dryland afforestation**: Limanim provide a proven model for establishing trees in arid regions without piped irrigation. The principle can be applied to establishing mycorrhizal tree-fungal networks in dryland permaculture.
- **Soil moisture banking**: The system effectively "banks" water in deep loess soil profiles, maintaining moisture availability for months after a single flood event — relevant for fungal substrate moisture management.
- **Fungal habitat**: The periodic inundation and slow drying cycle of limanim soils creates unique habitat conditions. Fungi adapted to wet-dry cycles (e.g., some arbuscular mycorrhizal species) may thrive in these systems.
- **Organic matter accumulation**: Tree leaf litter within limanim basins accumulates over years, building humus and creating substrate for [[mushroom-cultivation]] sites where periodic watering is the only option.
