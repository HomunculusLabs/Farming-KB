---# Cortinarius orellanus
Cortinarius orellanus, the deadly webcap, is a highly
[[orellanine]] toxin causes
irreversible kidney damage with a long latency period of
2-14 days. Notoriously difficult to distinguish from edible
Cortinarius species, it is among the most dangerous fungi for
temperate forest foragers.

## Taxonomy and Naming
- **Kingdom:** Fungi | **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes | **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Cortinariaceae | **Genus:** Cortinarius
- **Species:** C. orellanus | **Authority:** Fries (1838)
- **Synonyms:** Agaricus orellanus (1821), Dermocybe
  orellana (M.M. Moser, 1953)
- **Common names:** Deadly webcap, fool's webcap, orange
  webcap
- The epithet "orellanus" refers to the type locality near
  Orléans, France. Some place it in subgenus Leprocybe.

## Description
### Cap
- **Diameter:** 3-8 cm | **Shape:** Convex to broadly
  convex, shallow umbo; margin inrolled when young
- **Surface:** Dry, silky-fibrillose to finely scaly
- **Color:** Orange-brown to rusty tan, fading to ochraceous
- **Context:** Thin, pale buff, mild or radish-like odor

### Gills
- **Attachment:** Adnate to slightly emarginate
- **Spacing:** Moderately crowded | **Color:** Rusty brown
  to cinnamon at maturity | **Edge:** Even, lighter

### Stem
- **Dimensions:** 4-10 cm × 0.8-1.5 cm
- **Shape:** Equal or slightly clavate at base
- **Surface:** Silky, fibrillose, subtle scaly zone near
  base | **Color:** Light ochre to orange-brown
- **[[comparison-spore-print-collection-vs-spore-syringe-production]] | Rusty brown (Cinnamomeus) |
| Spore size | 7.5-9.5 × 5-6.5 µm |
| Spore shape | Ellipsoid, finely warty |
| Basidia | 4-spored, clavate |
| Cheilocystidia | Ventricose to cylindrical |
| Pleurocystidia | Absent or very rare |
| Clamp connections | Present |

## Distribution and Habitat
- **Range:** Temperate Europe; Scandinavia to Eastern Europe
- **Habitat:** Mycorrhizal with broadleaf trees (oak, beech,
  hazel) on acidic to neutral soils
- **Season:** Late summer to autumn (August-November)
- **Frequency:** Uncommon to locally common; found singly
  or scattered, never in large troops

## Chemistry and Toxicity
### Orellanine
Primary toxin: orellanine (3,3',4,4'-tetrahydroxy-2,2'-
bipyridine-N,N'-dioxide), a bipyridyl with potent
nephrotoxic properties.
- **Formula:** C10H8N2O6 | **MW:** 252.18 g/mol
- **Mechanism:** Uptake by proximal tubule cells generates
  ROS, causing lipid peroxidation and irreversible damage
- **Latency:** 2-14 days before symptoms appear
- **Lethal dose:** ~100-200 g fresh tissue
- **Thermal stability:** NOT destroyed by cooking, drying,
  or freezing

### Symptom Progression
- **Phase 1 (hours):** Nausea, vomiting, headache; often
  mild, leading to false recovery
- **Phase 2 (days 2-5):** Asymptomatic; kidney damage
