## Sarcosphaera coronaria

## Description

*Sarcosphaera coronaria* (syn. *S. crassa*, *Peziza coronaria*) is an ascomycete fungus in the family Pezizaceae, commonly known as the pink crown fungus or violet crown-cup. It produces large, cup-shaped to crown-like fruiting bodies that emerge from the soil, often in coniferous forests. This species is notable for its ability to accumulate and chemically transform arsenic compounds within its tissues.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** [[ascomycota]]
- **Class:** Pezizomycetes
- **Order:** Pezizales
- **Family:** Pezizaceae
- **Genus:** *Sarcosphaera*
- **Species:** *S. coronaria*

## Key Facts

- Found to contain methylarsonic acid (MA), a methylated form of arsenic, indicating active arsenic transformation within fungal tissues (Byrne et al., 1995).
- This methylation represents a detoxification pathway where inorganic arsenic is converted to less toxic organic forms.
- One of several fungal species identified as capable of arsenic biotransformation, alongside *[[laccaria-amethystina]]*, *[[sarcodon-imbricatus]]*, *[[agaricus-placomyc]]*, and *A. haemorrhoides*.
- The discovery of methylarsonic acid in this species contributed to understanding that fungi play active roles in arsenic cycling in ecosystems, not merely passive accumulation.
- Occurs in forest soils where arsenic may be naturally present or anthropogenically introduced.
- Previously classified among the puffballs but now recognized as a cup fungus (Pezizales).

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

*Sarcosphaera coronaria* is significant for understanding fungal metalloid transformation biochemistry. Its ability to methylate arsenic has implications for environmental toxicology, as the methylation process can both detoxify arsenic for the fungus and alter the mobility and toxicity of arsenic in the broader ecosystem. This species demonstrates that fungi are active participants in arsenic biogeochemical cycling.

## Ecological Role

- Decomposer in coniferous forest soils
- Active agent in arsenic biotransformation and cycling
- Contributes to understanding of fungal roles in metalloid detoxification
- Produces relatively large fruiting bodies that contribute to soil organic matter upon decomposition
