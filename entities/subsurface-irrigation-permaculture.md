## Description

Subsurface irrigation encompasses a family of techniques that deliver water below the soil surface (typically 12-50 cm deep), making it the most conservative method of irrigation. Best suited to arid sites where solar evaporation is a major factor, these systems are critical for establishing windbreaks of desert-hardy plants around orchards and crops.

## Classification

- **Category**: Irrigation technique
- **Domain**: [[humus]] pits to water circles of vegetables.

### Home Slot-Pipe System

- Top of pipe (6-5 cm diameter) has slots cut to one level.
- Nylon mesh or old stockings bound around slots.
- Wastewater led in from handbasins or sinks past grease trap or crude sieve.
- Excellent for small-fruit beds with slots at correct spacing for plants.
- Pipes should be laid to one side of plants for periodic checks as they eventually block up.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- Subsurface moisture delivery creates consistent humidity zones ideal for mycorrhizal fungal colonization of plant roots.
- Buried organic matter combined with subsurface moisture creates habitats for [[bill-mollison]], *Permaculture: A Designers' Manual*, Chapter 11.9 (Subsurface [[irrigation-systems]])
