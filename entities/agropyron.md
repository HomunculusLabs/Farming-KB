# Agropyron (Tall Wheat Grass)

## Description

*Agropyron*, as referenced by Mollison in the context of tall wheat grass, is a genus of perennial grasses in the family Poaceae identified as salt-tolerant plants capable of thriving in soils with more than 0.4% sodium chloride. It is listed among species that can supplant conventional crops on salt-affected agricultural land in dryland regions.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Family:** Poaceae (grass family)
- **Genus:** *Agropyron* (note: tall wheat grass is now often classified as *Thinopyrum ponticum* or *Elytrigia elongata*)
- **Common name:** Tall wheat grass
- **Growth habit:** Tall perennial bunchgrass, 1–2 m height

## Salt Tolerance

Tall wheat grass is among the most salt-tolerant forage grasses:

- Thrives in soils with **>0.4% sodium chloride**
- Tolerates moderate to high soil salinity (EC 10–20 dS/m)
- Establishes on saline discharge areas where conventional pasture grasses fail
- Persists where annual rainfall is as low as 300 mm

## Agricultural Value

### Forage Production

- **High biomass:** Produces substantial dry matter even on saline soils
- **Palatability:** Moderate — best grazed when young before stems become coarse
- **Persistence:** Long-lived perennial once established
- **Seasonal growth:** Productive through spring and summer
- **Grazing tolerance:** Withstands rotational grazing

### Soil Stabilization

- **Deep fibrous roots:** Stabilize saline soils against wind and water erosion
- **Organic matter input:** Builds soil structure on degraded sites
- **Ground cover:** Reduces evaporation from saline soil surface, slowing salt concentration

## Role in Dryland Salinity Management

Mollison includes *Agropyron* (tall wheat grass) alongside other salt-tolerant species:

- **Saltbushes (*Atriplex*)**
- **Acacias**
- **[[lolium-rigidum]]*)**

All are noted for thriving at >0.4% NaCl, but Mollison cautions that relying on salt tolerance alone delays addressing underlying causes.

## Establishment

- Sown from seed in autumn or early winter
- Benefits from minimal soil preparation on saline sites
- May require nurse crop in first year on severely degraded land
- Responds to phosphorus application on deficient soils
- Once established, requires minimal ongoing management
