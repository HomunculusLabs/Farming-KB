### 2. Cinnamon (Cinnamomum cassia / 계피)
* **Plant family:** Lauraceae
* **Active constituents:** Cinnamaldehyde, cinnamic acid, eugenol, coumarin, proanthocyanidins
* **Functions:** Potent broad-spectrum antimicrobial and antifungal agent; disrupts microbial cell membranes; insect repellent properties; antioxidant
* **Plant part used:** Dried bark
### 3. Garlic (Allium sativum / 마늘)
* **Plant family:** Amaryllidaceae
* **Active constituents:** Allicin (formed from alliin by alliinase upon crushing), diallyl disulfide, S-allyl cysteine, ajoene
* **Functions:** Broad-spectrum antimicrobial (bacterial, fungal, viral); insect repellent and insecticidal; stimulates plant defense responses; enhances systemic acquired resistance (SAR)
* **Plant part used:** Fresh cloves
### 4. Licorice (Glycyrrhiza uralensis / 감초)
* **Plant family:** Fabaceae
* **Active constituents:** Glycyrrhizin (glycyrrhizic acid), glycyrrhetinic acid, liquiritin, isoliquiritigenin, glabridin
* **Functions:** Anti-inflammatory; harmonizes and potentiates the effects of other herbs (traditional "harmonizer" role); saponin content enhances bioavailability of other compounds; mild antifungal activity
* **Plant part used:** Dried roots
### 5. Ginger (Zingiber officinale / 생강)
* **Plant family:** Zingiberaceae
* **Active constituents:** Gingerol, shogaol, zingerone, paradol, β-bisabolene
* **Functions:** Antimicrobial and antifungal; anti-nauseant (used traditionally for digestive support); promotes plant metabolism and enzyme activity; insect repellent
* **Plant part used:** Fresh rhizome
## Preparation Protocol
### Stage 1: Individual Herb Extracts
Each herb is prepared separately before combining:
**For Angelica, Cinnamon, and Licorice (dried herbs):**
1. Coarsely crush or chop the dried herb material
2. Soak in rice wine (cheongju or makgeolli) at approximately 1:2 ratio (herb to wine) in a glass or ceramic container
3. Cover and ferment for 7–10 days at room temperature
4. Add brown sugar at 1:1 ratio with the original herb weight
5. Continue fermenting for another 7–10 days
6. Strain the liquid — this is the individual herb extract
7. The alcohol from rice wine serves as a solvent for fat-soluble compounds (coumarins, essential oils, terpenoids) that are poorly extracted by water alone
**For Garlic and Ginger (fresh herbs):**
1. Crush or finely chop the fresh herb material
2. Mix with brown sugar at 1:1 ratio by weight
3. The sugar draws out the juices through osmotic extraction, and natural [[cervantes-pest-disease-prevention-overview]]:** Apply every 7–14 days during periods of disease pressure, especially in humid conditions
* **Growth stimulation:** Apply during key growth transitions (transplanting, flowering initiation, fruit set)
* **Pest management:** Apply as a preventive spray before pest populations establish; less effective as a curative treatment for established infestations
* **Timing:** Apply in the early morning or late afternoon. OHN can cause leaf burn if applied during hot midday conditions.

## Mechanisms of Action
### Direct Antimicrobial Effects
* **Allicin** from garlic disrupts thiol-containing enzymes in microbial cells, inhibiting growth of bacteria, fungi, and viruses
* **Cinnamaldehyde** from cinnamon damages microbial cell membranes through interaction with lipid bilayers, causing leakage of cellular contents
* **Decursin** from Angelica gigas inhibits fungal spore germination and mycelial growth
* **Gingerol** from ginger disrupts bacterial membrane integrity and inhibits biofilm formation
