## Description

**Liquid spawn** (also called liquid culture or LC) is a type of mushroom inoculum consisting of vigorously growing mycelium suspended in a liquid nutrient medium. It is one of the four primary spawn types used in mushroom cultivation alongside [[sawdust-spawn]], grain spawn, and wooden stick spawn. Liquid spawn can be produced via liquid-surface culture or submerged fermentation, with the latter scalable to industrial production levels. Liquid spawn is increasingly important in commercial [[maitake-cultivation]] and is used across a wide range of cultivated mushroom species.

## Classification

- **Category:** Spawn type / Cultivation material
- **Type:** Liquid mycelial inoculum
- **Production methods:** Liquid-surface culture, submerged fermentation
- **Use case:** Rapid, large-scale inoculation of mushroom substrates

## Key Facts

- Liquid spawn can be labor-saving during the spawning (inoculation) process through the use of specially designed inoculators that distribute the liquid evenly.
- Two primary production methods exist: liquid-surface culture (simpler, smaller scale) and submerged fermentation (industrial scale).
- Submerged fermentation can produce liquid spawn on an industrial scale, enabling rapid production of large volumes of inoculum (Fang et al., 1998).
- Liquid spawn distributes mycelium more evenly throughout the substrate compared to solid spawn types, as it can percolate through the substrate matrix.
- Colonization is often faster with liquid spawn because the mycelium is already in an actively growing state and distributed throughout the substrate.
- Requires careful quality control to detect contamination, as bacteria and yeasts can proliferate rapidly in liquid media.
- Storage life is shorter than grain or [[sawdust-spawn]]; liquid spawn should be used soon after production for best results.

## Production Methods

### Liquid-Surface Culture
The simpler method suitable for small-scale and hobbyist operations:
1. **Medium preparation:** Nutrient broth (typically malt extract, dextrose, or potato dextrose) is prepared in jars or flasks.
2. **Sterilization:** The medium is sterilized by autoclaving at 121°C for 15-30 minutes.
3. **Inoculation:** A small piece of mycelium from agar culture is introduced under sterile conditions.
4. **Incubation:** Mycelium grows as a mat on the liquid surface or as submerged pellets, depending on agitation. Static cultures produce a surface mat; gently agitated cultures produce floating mycelial balls.
5. **Harvest:** After full colonization (7-21 days), the liquid culture is ready for use as spawn.

### Submerged Fermentation (Industrial Scale)
The industrial method for large-scale spawn production:
1. **Fermenter setup:** A bioreactor (5L to 5000L+) is equipped with agitation, aeration, temperature control, and sterile inlet/outlet ports.
2. **Medium optimization:** Industrial media may include glucose, maltose, yeast extract, peptone, and mineral salts optimized for the target species.
3. **Inoculation:** A seed culture (typically from a smaller fermentation or agar) is introduced into the sterilized fermenter.
4. **Fermentation parameters:** Temperature 22-28°C, agitation 100-300 rpm, aeration 0.5-2.0 vvm (volumes of air per volume of medium per minute), duration 3-7 days.
5. **Quality control:** Samples are checked microscopically and plated on agar to verify purity before use.

## Liquid Media Formulations

Common liquid culture media used for spawn production:
- **Malt extract broth (MEB):** 2% malt extract in water — simple and effective for many species
- **Potato dextrose broth (PDB):** Standard laboratory medium, widely available
- **Glucose-yeast extract broth:** 2% glucose + 0.5% yeast extract — supports rapid growth
- **Optimized industrial media:** May include defined carbon sources, amino acids, vitamins, and mineral salts for maximum biomass production

## Advantages and Disadvantages

### Advantages
- **Rapid inoculation:** Liquid spawn can be injected or poured through filter patches, inoculating entire substrate blocks in seconds.
- **Uniform distribution:** Unlike [[sawdust-spawn]] that must be broken up and mixed, liquid spawn percolates throughout the substrate, distributing inoculation points evenly.
- **Faster colonization:** Multiple inoculation points throughout the substrate reduce the distance mycelium must grow, shortening the spawn run by 30-50% in some cases.
- **Scalability:** Submerged fermentation enables production of thousands of liters of spawn from a single agar plate.
- **Automation compatibility:** Liquid spawn works well with automated inoculation systems in commercial facilities.

### Disadvantages
- **Contamination risk:** Liquid media is equally hospitable to bacteria and yeasts. A single contaminated batch can ruin thousands of substrate blocks. See [[mushroom-contamination-identification-and-troubleshooting]] for contamination management.
- **Shorter shelf life:** Liquid spawn must be used within days to weeks of production, unlike solid spawn types that can be refrigerated for months.
- **Equipment cost:** Bioreactors, peristaltic pumps, and sterile filtration equipment require significant capital investment.
- **Skill requirement:** Producing clean liquid spawn consistently requires advanced sterile technique and laboratory infrastructure.

## Application in Maitake Cultivation

In [[maitake-cultivation]], liquid spawn is one of the four spawn types listed as suitable for inoculating [[hardwood-sawdust-substrate]] production blocks. The rapid colonization is particularly advantageous for maitake because:
- Maitake's long spawn run (30-45 days with [[sawdust-spawn]]) can be shortened to 20-30 days with liquid spawn.
- The highly aerobic nature of maitake benefits from the multiple inoculation points that liquid spawn provides, allowing more rapid establishment throughout the substrate.
- Commercial maitake operations can use specialized inoculation equipment to rapidly inoculate hundreds of bags per hour.

## Quality Control

Maintaining liquid spawn quality requires rigorous protocols:
- **Microscopic examination:** Check for bacterial contamination (rod-shaped or spherical organisms moving rapidly) and yeast contamination (budding cells).
- **Plate testing:** Streak a sample onto nutrient agar and incubate to detect hidden contamination.
- **Smell test:** Healthy liquid spawn has a mild mushroom odor; foul or sour smells indicate bacterial contamination.
- **Visual inspection:** Cloudy liquid (other than mycelium) or unusual colors indicate contamination.
- **Viability testing:** Inoculate a test substrate to confirm the spawn will colonize before committing to full-scale inoculation.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Inoculation efficiency:** Liquid spawn enables rapid, uniform inoculation of synthetic logs, bags, or other substrates, reducing labor costs in commercial operations.
- **Industrial scalability:** Submerged fermentation allows spawn production at volumes impractical with solid spawn methods, supporting large-scale commercial mushroom farms.
- **Maitake cultivation:** Listed as one of the four spawn types suitable for *G. frondosa* cultivation, offering speed advantages for growers with appropriate equipment.
- **Contamination risk:** The liquid medium is equally hospitable to unwanted microorganisms. Rigorous sterile technique and quality testing (microscopy, plate cultures) are essential before use.
- **Equipment requirements:** Effective use requires inoculation tools (inoculators, peristaltic pumps) and, for production, fermentation vessels with agitation and aeration.
