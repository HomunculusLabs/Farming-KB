## Description

Bonism (or Bon) is the traditional pre-Buddhist religion of Tibet. According to *The [[leary-psychedelic-experience-bardo-mapping]]*, the language and symbolism of Bonist death rituals were "skillfully blended with Buddhist conceptions" in the [[bardo-thodol]] ([[psychedelic-experience-tibetan-book-of-the-dead]] the Dead). This syncretism is significant because it means the text that Leary adapted as a psychedelic guide already contained layers of pre-Buddhist shamanic death-and-rebirth symbolism overlaid with Buddhist philosophy.

## Classification

- **Category:** Religion / Spiritual tradition
- **Geographic origin:** Tibet
- **Historical period:** Pre-Buddhist (before 7th century CE)
- **Status:** Survives as Bon tradition (reformed and Buddhist-influenced)

## Key Facts

- Pre-Buddhist indigenous religion of Tibet
- Death rituals and symbolism from Bonism were incorporated into the Tibetan Book of the Dead
- The Bardo Thodol represents a syncretic blend of Bonist and Buddhist traditions
- Bon practices historically included shamanic elements: spirit journeying, death-and-rebirth symbolism, and direct engagement with altered [[mckenna-food-gods-designer-states-of-consciousness]]
- This shamanic substrate may partially explain why the Tibetan text mapped so effectively onto psychedelic experiences
- The blending of Bonist and Buddhist elements created a text that was simultaneously a funerary ritual and a consciousness-guide

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

The Bonist shamanic tradition that underlies the Bardo Thodol shares features with indigenous mushroom-using cultures worldwide, including death-and-rebirth symbolism, spirit journeying, and the use of ritual frameworks for navigating altered states. The fact that Leary's psychedelic guide drew on a text with deep shamanic roots is not coincidental — both traditions address the same fundamental human experiences of ego dissolution and expanded consciousness that are produced by [[psilocybin-mushrooms]]. Understanding the Bonist roots of the psychedelic framework connects [[modern-mushroom-festival-culture]] use to ancient [[jivaro-bewitching-and-curing-shamanic-practices]].

## Historical Context

Bon predates the arrival of Buddhism in Tibet (7th century CE). The original Bon tradition (often called "black Bon" or unreformed Bon) included shamanic practices, animal sacrifice, and elaborate death rituals. After Buddhism became dominant in Tibet, Bon evolved and incorporated many Buddhist elements while retaining its distinct identity. Modern Bon (Yungdrung Bon) is recognized as one of Tibet's major religious traditions. Scholars debate the extent to which Bonist [[shamanism]] may have involved [[cultural-evolution-and-psychoactive-substances]], though no direct evidence connects Bon practices to mushroom use specifically.
