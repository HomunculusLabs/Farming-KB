# Daikon Radish

## Description

[[raphanus-sativus]] var. longipinnatus) is a large, deep-rooted radish variety used in permaculture and [[green-manure]]

## Key Facts

- Daikon radish produces a large, thick taproot capable of penetrating compacted and hardened soils to considerable depth, creating channels for water infiltration and air exchange.
- Masanobu Fukuoka used daikon radish and Acacia species as biological alternatives to mechanical ploughing in Japan, following a "graze or cut and let lie" method.
- When the radish root dies and decomposes, it leaves behind organic matter and a vertical channel in the soil, improving aeration, water percolation, and root penetration for subsequent plantings.
- Mollison recommends daikon as part of a suite of deep-rooted plants (alongside comfrey, lucerne, Acacias, and tree legumes) for rehabilitating compacted and degraded soils.
- As a member of the Brassicaceae family, daikon produces glucosinolates which may act as biofumigants, suppressing soil-borne pests and pathogens when incorporated into the soil.
- The plant grows rapidly, producing substantial biomass both above and below ground within a single growing season.
- Daikon can be overseeded into existing pasture; its rapid growth allows it to compete with established grasses before the sward recovers from grazing or mowing shock.

## Relevance to Cultivation

- **Soil rehabilitation**: Acts as a biological alternative to mechanical subsoiling, penetrating hardpans and compacted layers that prevent water infiltration and root growth.
- **No-till systems**: Fits perfectly into minimum-tillage and no-dig gardening approaches championed by Fukuoka and Mollison.
- **Green manure**: When cut and left to decompose, returns substantial organic matter to the soil profile.
- **Water management**: Root channels created by daikon improve soil's capacity to absorb and retain rainfall, reducing runoff and erosion.
- **Companion planting**: Can be interplanted with legumes and other cover crops for a multi-functional soil improvement strategy.
- **Seed pelleting**: Fukuoka developed methods of pressing seed-clay mixes through coarse sieves to create seed pellets, with daikon being a key species in this approach.

## Cultivation Notes

- Direct sow into moist soil; no transplanting needed.
- Tolerates a wide range of soil conditions including compacted and poor soils.
- Best used as a winter cover crop in temperate climates or as a pioneer species in soil rehabilitation projects.
- Allow plants to grow large and penetrate deeply before terminating (by cutting, grazing, or frost-kill).
- Seed is inexpensive and readily available, making daikon an accessible tool for broadacre soil improvement.
