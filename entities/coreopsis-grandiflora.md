## Overview

**Coreopsis grandiflora** (large-flowered tickseed, Atlantic coreopsis) is a clump-forming perennial herb in the family Asteraceae.

It is native to central and [[dighton-fungal-drought-tolerance-plant-water-relations]], and ease of cultivation have made it a standard component of perennial borders and meadow plantings.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Coreopsis grandiflora** (Hogg ex Hook.) S.F. Blake was originally described by William Jackson Hooker and later revised by Sidney Fay Blake.

The genus name *Coreopsis* derives from Greek *koris* (bug) and *opsis* (appearance), referring to the tick-like appearance of the achenes.

Plants form compact clumps 30–60 cm tall and 30–45 cm wide with upright, branching stems.

Leaves are primarily basal, lanceolate to oblanceolate, 8–15 cm long, with entire to slightly toothed margins.

Cauline leaves are smaller, opposite, and sessile, reduced toward the inflorescence.

Flower heads are solitary or in loose corymbs, 4–6 cm in diameter.

Ray florets number 6–10, are bright yellow, deeply notched at the tips, and 15–25 mm long.

The disk is small, dome-shaped, 6–10 mm wide, with yellow to dark brown disk florets.

Achenes are flat, dark brown, winged, and approximately 3–4 mm long.

## Distribution and Habitat

The native range extends from Ontario and Minnesota south to Missouri, Arkansas, and Georgia.

It is most common in prairies, open woodlands, glades, and roadsides of the central [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]] beyond its historical range.

## Cultivation

**Coreopsis grandiflora** is propagated readily from seed, division, and softwood cuttings.

Seed germinates in 14–30 days at 18–22°C and does not require cold stratification.

Plants bloom reliably in the second year from seed, though some cultivars flower in the first year.

Division every 2–3 years maintains plant vigor and prevents center die-out in mature clumps.

The species performs best in full sun with average, well-drained soil of moderate fertility.

Deadheading spent flowers extends the bloom period by several weeks and reduces unwanted self-seeding.

Excessive fertility or overly rich soils promote floppy growth and reduce flowering intensity.

Notable cultivars include 'Early Sunrise' (double flowers), 'Solar Flash', and 'Baby Sun' (compact habit).

The species is relatively pest-free, though aphids and leafhoppers may occasionally infest new growth.

Its compact form and reliable bloom make it suitable for borders, containers, mass plantings, and cut-flower production.

## Ecological Role

The flowers provide nectar and pollen for a wide range of pollinators, including [[comparison-honey-bees-vs-native-solitary-bees]], butterflies, and syrphid flies.

Long-tongued bees such as bumble bees (*Bombus* spp.) are especially effective pollinators of Coreopsis species.
