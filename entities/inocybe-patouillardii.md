## Description

*Inocybe patouillardii* (also known as *Inocybe erubescens*), the Red-Staining Inocybe, is one of the most dangerous species in the genus *Inocybe*. It is classified among the fungi containing substances that paralyse the nervous system, alongside *Amanita muscaria* and *Amanita pantherina*. The species is notable for containing high concentrations of muscarine, making it genuinely dangerous despite its relatively unremarkable appearance.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Inocybaceae
- **Genus:** *Inocybe*
- **Species:** *I. patouillardii* (syn. *I. erubescens*)

## Key Facts

- Contains substantial quantities of muscarine, a toxin that stimulates the parasympathetic nervous system.
- Placed in Ramsbottom"s Category 2 of poisonous fungi: "Those containing substances which paralyse the nervous system."
- Shares this category with *Amanita muscaria* and *Amanita pantherina*.
- The fruiting body stains or bruises reddish, which is the origin of both its common name and its synonym *erubescens* (meaning "becoming red").
- Species of *Inocybe* are generally difficult to identify in the field, making the entire genus suspect for foragers.
- Unlike the deadly *Amanita* species whose toxins (amatoxins) have a long incubation period, muscarine poisoning typically manifests within 15–30 minutes of ingestion.

## Symptoms of Muscarine Poisoning

- Profuse sweating (diaphoresis)
- Salivation and lacrimation
- Abdominal cramps, nausea, vomiting, and diarrhoea
- Slowed heart rate (bradycardia)
- Constricted pupils (miosis)
- In severe cases, breathing difficulties due to bronchoconstriction

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

*Inocybe patouillardii* represents a significant hazard for mushroom foragers because *Inocybe* species as a whole are difficult to distinguish from one another and from various little brown mushrooms (LBMs) in other genera. Many *Inocybe* species contain muscarine, making the entire genus one that should be avoided by all but expert mycologists. The species is mycorrhizal, typically found in deciduous and mixed woodlands during summer and autumn. Its inclusion in Ramsbottom's classification of poisonous fungi underscores the importance of understanding toxin groups rather than just individual species when studying mycotoxicology.
