# OG Kush

**OG Kush** is one of the most iconic and influential cannabis strains in modern history. An indica-dominant hybrid, it has served as the genetic backbone for countless West Coast cannabis varieties and remains a staple in dispensaries worldwide. Its name is widely believed to stand for "Ocean Grown," a reference to its cultivation origins along the coast of Southern California, though alternative theories attribute "OG" to "Original Gangster," referencing the strain's prominence in hip-hop culture.

## Lineage and Genetics

The exact genetic origins of OG Kush are the subject of considerable debate and are likely lost to undocumented breeding history. The most widely accepted theory posits that OG Kush emerged in Florida during the early 1990s as a cross between a Chemdawg cut and a Hindu Kush or Lemon Thai phenotype. The strain was later brought to Southern California by breeder and cultivator Matt "Bubba" Berger, where it was propagated and popularized throughout the Los Angeles area. Over subsequent decades, OG Kush was selectively bred into numerous phenotypic variants, including Tahoe OG, SFV OG, Ghost OG, and XXX OG, each retaining the core genetic signature while expressing slightly different characteristics. Despite its indica-dominant classification, OG Kush's genetic composition is typically reported as roughly 55% indica and 45% sativa.

## Appearance

OG Kush plants produce dense, medium-to-large buds with a chunky, rounded structure characteristic of indica varieties. The flowers are a vibrant forest green, often accented by subtle yellowish and golden hues. The calyxes are tightly packed and heavily coated in a thick layer of milky-white trichomes, giving the buds a frosty, almost silver sheen. Rust-orange pistils wind through the resinous colas, becoming more pronounced as the plant approaches full maturity. The overall visual impression is one of exceptional resin production and density.

## Aroma and Flavor Profile

OG Kush is renowned for its complex and pungent terpene profile. The aroma is immediately striking, dominated by an earthy, pine-forward scent with sharp notes of lemon and citrus. A distinct fuel-like or skunky undertone is often present, attributed to its Chemdawg lineage. Upon breaking apart the buds, deeper notes of wood, spice, and a faintly sour quality become apparent.

The flavor mirrors the aromatic profile closely. On inhalation, users typically notice a citrus and pine character that coats the palate. The exhale brings forward earthy, woody notes with lingering hints of lemon zest and a subtle peppery spice. The smoke is generally considered smooth, though it can be expansive and occasionally induce coughing in less experienced consumers.

## Cannabinoid Content

OG Kush is classified as a high-THC strain. Laboratory-tested samples typically report THC concentrations ranging from **19% to 26%**, with some exceptionally cultivated phenotypes reaching up to 28%. CBD content is characteristically low, generally measuring below **0.3%**, which is standard for recreational-oriented strains of this lineage. The strain's potency has remained consistently high over decades of cultivation, contributing to its enduring popularity among experienced consumers.

## Terpene Profile

The defining terpenes in OG Kush are:

- **Myrcene** — The dominant terpene, contributing earthy, herbal, and musky notes. Myrcene is associated with sedative and relaxing effects and is believed to enhance the permeability of the blood-brain barrier to other cannabinoids.
- **Limonene** — Imparts the strain's characteristic citrus and lemon aromas. Limonene is associated with mood elevation, stress relief, and anti-inflammatory properties.
- **Caryophyllene** — Adds peppery, spicy undertones. Notable for its ability to bind directly to CB2 receptors, caryophyllene contributes anti-inflammatory and analgesic effects without psychoactive activity.

Secondary terpenes may include pinene, humulene, and linalool in varying concentrations depending on the specific phenotype and growing conditions.

## Effects

OG Kush delivers a well-balanced experience that begins with a pronounced cerebral onset before transitioning into deep physical relaxation. The initial effects are typically described as euphoric and uplifting, with enhanced mood, heightened sensory perception, and a gentle cerebral stimulation. This head high is generally clear-headed rather than racy or disorienting.

As the experience progresses, a powerful body buzz emerges, spreading from the neck and shoulders downward. This physical relaxation can become quite heavy, often leading to couch-lock in higher doses. The comedown is gradual and typically culminates in drowsiness, making OG Kush best suited for evening or nighttime use.

Commonly reported effects include:
- Euphoria
- Deep relaxation
- Happiness
- Increased appetite (munchies)
- Sedation (at higher doses)

Potential negative effects include dry mouth, dry eyes, occasional paranoia at high doses, and dizziness, particularly in novice consumers.

## Medical Applications

OG Kush has a long history of use in medical cannabis circles. Its therapeutic profile makes it a versatile option for a range of conditions:

- **Stress and Anxiety** — The euphoric and mood-elevating onset can provide significant relief from acute stress and generalized anxiety, though high doses may exacerbate anxiety in predisposed individuals.
- **Pain Management** — The combined analgesic properties of high THC and caryophyllene make it effective for chronic pain, migraines, arthritis, and muscle spasms.
- **Insomnia** — The sedative body effects are beneficial for individuals struggling with sleep onset or maintenance.
- **Appetite Stimulation** — OG Kush is widely used to combat appetite loss associated with chemotherapy, HIV/AIDS, and eating disorders.
- **Depression** — The uplifting cerebral effects may temporarily alleviate symptoms of mild to moderate depression.
- **PTSD** — Anecdotal and preliminary clinical evidence suggests potential benefits for trauma-related symptoms, particularly the sleep-disruptive component, though formal research remains limited.
- **Nausea** — Antiemetic properties linked to THC activation of CB1 receptors in the brainstem's vomiting center make OG Kush useful for patients undergoing chemotherapy or dealing with gastrointestinal conditions.
- **Headaches and Migraines** — The combination of myrcene (muscle relaxant) and caryophyllene (anti-inflammatory) may help reduce the frequency and severity of tension headaches and migraines.

## Growing Information

OG Kush is considered a moderately challenging strain to cultivate, best suited for growers with some prior experience. It is available in both regular and feminized seed forms, though clone cuttings from verified mother plants are the preferred propagation method for maintaining genetic consistency.

| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| **Flowering Time** | 8–9 weeks indoors |
| **Harvest Time** | Early to mid-October (outdoors) |
| **Indoor Yield** | 14–18 oz per square meter |
| **Outdoor Yield** | 16–21 oz per plant |
| **Height** | Medium (3–5 ft indoor) |
| **Difficulty** | Moderate |
| **Preferred Climate** | Warm, dry Mediterranean |

OG Kush expresses moderate resistance to common pests, including spider mites and aphids, though its dense bud structure makes it particularly vulnerable to botrytis (bud rot) in humid conditions. Regular inspection during late flowering is essential. The strain is relatively sensitive to nutrient lockout caused by pH fluctuations; maintaining soil or hydroponic pH between 6.0 and 6.8 is recommended. Some phenotypes exhibit significant stretch during the first two weeks of flowering, which should be anticipated when managing indoor canopy height.

## Pests, Diseases, and Environmental Sensitivity

Beyond the common pest pressures mentioned above, OG Kush requires attention to several environmental factors:

- **Botrytis cinerea (bud rot).** The dense, resinous bud structure creates microclimates where moisture can accumulate, making OG Kush especially susceptible to gray mold. Greenhouse and outdoor growers in humid regions (Pacific Northwest, tropical climates) should ensure robust air circulation and consider planting in areas with good prevailing wind exposure. Bud washing with hydrogen peroxide solutions prior to harvest can reduce mold spore load.
- **Powdery mildew.** While less common than bud rot, powdery mildew can affect foliage during vegetative growth, particularly in crowded indoor grows with poor ventilation. Sulfur-based foliar sprays during the vegetative stage are an effective preventive measure.
- **Nutrient sensitivity.** OG Kush is known to be sensitive to over-fertilization, particularly nitrogen during the flowering phase, which can produce overly vegetative growth, reduce bud density, and delay maturation. A controlled nutrient taper during weeks 5–8 of flowering is recommended. CalMag supplementation is beneficial in coco or hydroponic media to prevent calcium and magnesium deficiency symptoms (leaf tip burn, interveinal chlorosis).
- **Light stress.** Some OG Kush phenotypes are sensitive to excessive light intensity, particularly during late flowering. Light bleaching of calyxes can occur with high-wattage LED or HID fixtures positioned too close to the canopy.
- **Temperature and humidity.** Optimal daytime temperatures range from 70–80°F (21–27°C) with nighttime temperatures 10°F lower to encourage terpene production. Relative humidity should be maintained at 45–55% during flowering to minimize mold risk while supporting resin development.

OG Kush thrives in warm, dry environments and is particularly well-suited to Southern California's Mediterranean climate. It is susceptible to mold and powdery mildew in high-humidity conditions, necessitating proper airflow management. The strain responds well to techniques such as topping, low-stress training (LST), and the Screen of Green (ScrOG) method. Nutrient requirements are moderate, though the plants can be sensitive to over-fertilization, particularly with nitrogen during the flowering phase. The plants emit a strong odor throughout the growth cycle, making odor control measures essential for discreet indoor operations.

## Cultural Significance

OG Kush occupies a foundational place in contemporary cannabis culture. Emerging from the Southern California medical cannabis scene of the mid-1990s, it became synonymous with West Coast cannabis and played a pivotal role in establishing Los Angeles as a global capital of cannabis cultivation. The strain has been referenced extensively in hip-hop music, with artists including Snoop Dogg, Cypress Hill, and Wiz Khalifa name-dropping OG Kush in lyrics, further cementing its status as a cultural icon.

## Notable Phenotypes and Derivatives

The genetic stability and desirability of OG Kush have given rise to numerous distinct phenotypes and crossbreeds, many of which have achieved fame in their own right:

- **Tahoe OG** — A phenotype known for particularly sedative effects and high resin production, originating from the Lake Tahoe region.
- **SFV OG (San Fernando Valley OG)** — A slightly more sativa-leaning phenotype with a pronounced lemon-fuel aroma and energetic cerebral effects.
- **Ghost OG** — A phenotype characterized by its pale, ghostly trichome coverage and balanced hybrid effects.
- **XXX OG** — A heavy indica phenotype with intense sedation, popular among medical patients for pain and insomnia.
- **Girl Scout Cookies (GSC)** — A cross of OG Kush with Durban Poison, one of the most awarded strains of the 2010s, combining OG potency with Cookie family sweetness.
- **Headband** — OG Kush × Sour Diesel, named for the pressure-like sensation around the temples reportedly experienced by users.
- **Thin Mint Cookies** — Another OG Kush descendant through the GSC lineage, known for its minty aroma and dense, dark buds.
- **Larry OG** — An OG Kush phenotype popularized in Orange County, noted for its balanced effects and complex terpene profile.

## Terpene Entourage Effects

The interaction between OG Kush's dominant terpenes and its cannabinoid profile illustrates the entourage effect — the theory that cannabis compounds produce synergistic therapeutic outcomes when consumed together rather than in isolation. Myrcene's sedative properties amplify THC's psychoactive effects while potentially reducing anxiety, limonene enhances mood elevation and provides anti-anxiety benefits, and caryophyllene contributes analgesic effects via CB2 receptor activation without adding to psychoactive intensity. This specific terpene-cannabinoid combination is thought to underlie OG Kush's characteristic experience: a euphoric, cerebral onset that smoothly transitions into deep physical relaxation.

OG Kush is frequently cited as one of the most important cannabis strains ever developed, alongside foundational varieties like Skunk #1, Northern Lights, and Haze.
