# Psilocybe graecia

**Psilocybe graecia** is a bluing [[psilocybe-semilanceata]] being recognized as distinct species.

## Description

### Cap (Pileus)
- 8 to 25 mm in diameter
- Conic to broadly campanulate when young, expanding slightly with age
- Umbo typically present and persistent
- Hygrophanous: dark brown to reddish-brown when moist, fading to ochre or pale tan when dry
- Surface smooth, sometimes slightly viscid when wet
- Margin translucent-striate when moist
- Exhibits blue-green bruising when damaged

### Gills (Lamellae)
- Adnexed to broadly adnexed
- Crowded, with 2 to 3 tiers of lamellulae (short gills)
- Color develops from pale grey to dark purple-brown as spores mature
- Edge often whitish and slightly eroded in age

### Stipe (Stem)
- 35 to 70 mm long, 1.5 to 2.5 mm thick
- Cylindrical, equal or slightly thicker at the base
- Color: pale yellowish to brownish
- Surface covered with fine whitish fibrils
- Strong [[field-guide-psilocybin-bluing-reaction-identification]] when handled or bruised, especially at the base
- No annulus (ring) present

### Spores
- [[comparison-spore-print-collection-vs-spore-syringe-production]]: dark purplish-brown to nearly black
- Spores: ellipsoid to slightly oblong
- 12 to 15 × 7 to 9 µm
- Thick-walled with a distinct apical germ pore
- Q value: approximately 1.6 to 1.9
- Wall thickness: approximately 1 µm

### Microscopic Features
- Cheilocystidia: ventricose (flask-shaped) to lageniform (narrow-necked)
- Pleurocystidia: similar to cheilocystidia but less common
- Pileipellis: a cutis of filamentous hyphae
- Clamp connections: absent

## Distribution and Habitat

**Psilocybe graecia** is found in the eastern Mediterranean, with confirmed records from Greece. It may also occur in neighboring Mediterranean countries including Turkey, Albania, and Italy, though these records require molecular confirmation.

### Habitat
- Grows in grasslands, pastures, and open meadows
- Prefers well-drained, calcareous or neutral soils
- Often found in mountainous and sub-mountainous grasslands
- Fruits in late autumn to early winter (November to January)
- Elevation range: typically 500 to 1,500 meters above sea level

### Climate
- Associated with Mediterranean [[mollison-climate-zones-permaculture-strategies]] characterized by warm, dry summers and cool, wet winters
- Requires prolonged autumn rainfall to initiate fruiting

## Chemistry

**P. graecia** contains the classic psilocybian alkaloid profile shared with other members of the genus.

### Alkaloid Content
- [[psilocin]]: trace to 0.15% dry weight
- [[psilocybe-semilanceata]], though analytical data is limited due to the rarity of collected specimens.

## Similar Species

Several European Psilocybe species could be confused with **P. graecia**:

- **[[psilocybe-galindoi]]** — reported from Spain; differs in spore morphology and cheilocystidia shape.
- **[[psilocybe-gallaeciae]]** — northwestern Iberian species; smaller overall and found in Atlantic rather than Mediterranean climates.

## Identification Notes

Definitive identification of **P. graecia** requires [[fungal-microscopic-examination-mounting-media-techniques]] of spore size and shape, cystidia morphology, and preferably molecular analysis (ITS sequencing). Field identification based on macroscopic features alone is unreliable given the similarity to other bluing psilocybes.

Key diagnostic features include:
- Mediterranean distribution
- Spore dimensions larger than [[psilocybin]]-containing species, **P. graecia** is subject to drug control legislation in most countries. In Greece, the collection and possession of psilocybian mushrooms is restricted under national drug laws.
