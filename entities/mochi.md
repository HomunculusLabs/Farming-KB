## Overview

**Mochi**, also known as **Gelato 33**, is a hybrid cannabis strain created by the San Francisco-based breeder **Cookie Fam Genetics**. A cross between **Thin Mint Cookies** (Girl Scout Cookies phenotype) and **Sunset Sherbet**, Mochi inherits the cookie lineage's potency and dessert-like flavor profile.

The strain was named for its dense, trichome-rich buds that resemble mochi rice cakes in texture. It has won no major cannabis cups but maintains strong popularity on the West Coast and in legal markets for its balanced effects and sweet aroma.

Mochi is classified as an indica-dominant hybrid, typically reported as approximately 60/40 indica to sativa. THC content ranges from 21–29%, with CBD consistently below 0.1%.

## Lineage

- **Female parent:** Thin Mint Cookies (a phenotype of Girl Scout Cookies, itself OG Kush × Durban Poison)
- **Male parent:** Sunset Sherbet (Girl Scout Cookies × Pink Panties)

This breeding makes Mochi a heavily cookie-lineage-dominant strain, with both parents carrying Girl Scout Cookies genetics. The Thin Mint parent contributes a minty, cookie-dough aroma, while Sunset Sherbet adds creamy, fruity sweetness.

## Appearance

Mochi produces medium-to-large, densely packed buds with a rounded, chunky structure. Calyxes are tightly curled and heavily coated in trichomes, giving the flowers a frosted, almost white appearance at peak maturity.

Colors range from deep forest green with occasional purple hues in the calyxes, particularly when exposed to cooler nighttime temperatures during late flowering. Pistils are sparse, thin, and orange-amber.

The bud density and resin production are notably high — a characteristic inherited from the Thin Mint Cookies lineage. Properly cured Mochi buds feel sticky and dense, often described as having a marshmallow-like compression.

## Aroma and Flavor

Mochi's aroma is dominated by sweet, creamy notes with prominent cookie and vanilla undertones. Secondary notes include fresh mint, earthy pine, and a subtle berry sweetness from the Sherbet parent.

The flavor closely mirrors the aroma, presenting a smooth, sweet smoke or vapor with a cookie-dough and vanilla cream character on the inhale. The exhale reveals earthy, slightly herbal undertones with a lingering minty freshness.

The dessert-like terpene profile places Mochi firmly in the "cookie" flavor category alongside [[gelato]], [[girl-scout-cookies]], and [[runtz]].

## Terpene Profile

- **Limonene** (dominant): 0.8–1.2% — citrus sweetness, mood elevation, stress relief
- **Caryophyllene** (secondary): 0.4–0.7% — peppery spice, anti-inflammatory, binds CB2 receptors
- **Myrcene** (secondary): 0.3–0.6% — earthy, sedative body effects, muscle relaxation
- **Linalool** (minor): 0.1–0.3% — floral, anxiolytic, enhances calming effects
- **Humulene** (trace): <0.1% — woody, appetite suppressant

The high limonene content differentiates Mochi from many cookie-family strains that lean heavier on caryophyllene.

## Effects

Onset is relatively rapid (5–10 minutes), beginning with a cerebral euphoria and uplifted mood. Users report enhanced creativity and sociability during the initial phase, with a gentle, happy mental stimulation.

Within 30–45 minutes, the indica effects emerge as a warm, full-body relaxation that spreads from the shoulders downward. Body effects are described as calming rather than sedating at moderate doses, making Mochi suitable for late afternoon or early evening use.

Common therapeutic applications include stress relief, mild anxiety management, and chronic pain. The balanced onset makes it versatile for both recreational and medical users, though high-THC phenotypes may be intense for novice consumers.

## Cultivation

Mochi has moderate cultivation difficulty. It performs well in both indoor and outdoor environments, with indoor cultivation preferred for terpene expression. Flowering time is 8–10 weeks indoors, with outdoor harvest in early to mid-October.

Indoor yields average 300–400 g/m². The plants grow to medium height (90–130 cm) with a bushy structure requiring some training (LST, ScROG) for even canopy development.

Mochi is moderately resistant to mold and pests but can be sensitive to nutrient overfeeding, particularly nitrogen during early flowering. Cooler nighttime temperatures (18–20 °C) during weeks 5–8 enhance purple coloration and terpene production.

## Cannabinoid Profile

Mochi is a high-THC, low-CBD strain. Typical laboratory analyses report THC concentrations between 21% and 29%, depending on cultivation conditions and phenotype. CBD is consistently below 0.1%, with trace amounts of CBG (0.05–0.15%).

Minor cannabinoids detected include CBC (0.1–0.3%) and THCV in some phenotypes (0.05–0.1%). The THCV presence may contribute to the strain's clear-headed effects at moderate doses, as THCV is an antagonist at CB1 receptors at low concentrations.

Total cannabinoid content typically ranges 22–30%, placing Mochi among the more potent cookie-family strains. Extractors favor Mochi for its high resin output and diverse terpene profile, which produces flavorful concentrates.

## Notable Phenotypes

Several named phenotypes and crosses have emerged from Mochi genetics. **Mochi Gelato** (a backcross to Gelato #33) emphasizes the creamy dessert terpenes. **Mochi Breath** crosses Mochi with Mendo Breath for heavier sedative effects.

**Mochi 41** is a selected phenotype with higher THC (up to 29%) and a more pronounced mint aroma. **Runtz Mochi**, a cross with Runtz, has gained traction in California dispensaries for its extreme sweetness and potency.

Mochi has also been used as a parent in breeding programs aimed at combining cookie-family flavors with novel terpene expressions. Its stable genetics and reliable resin production make it a popular donor in polyhybrid crosses.

## Side Effects

Common side effects include dry mouth and dry eyes, typical of high-THC strains with significant myrcene content. Some users report mild dizziness or paranoia at higher doses, particularly with phenotypes exceeding 26% THC.

Due to the indica-dominant body effects, Mochi may cause couch-lock or sedation in sensitive individuals, especially when consumed in the evening. Novice consumers should start with low doses (1–2 puffs) and wait 15–20 minutes before re-dosing.

Mochi has no documented contraindications with medications, but its potential to elevate heart rate means users with cardiovascular conditions should exercise caution. The anxiolytic linalool content may offset THC-induced anxiety in some users, though individual responses vary significantly.
