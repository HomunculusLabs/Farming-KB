## Description

Coppice forestry is a sustainable woodland management method where trees are cut to ground level (or stool height) and allowed to regrow from the stump, producing a continuous supply of wood products on a rotation cycle. In permaculture dryland design, coppiced fuel forests are a critical settlement strategy, providing cooking fuel, power supply, essential oils, mulch, honey, and other tree products using recycled wastewater for irrigation.

## Classification

- **Category**: Forestry management technique
- **Domain**: Sustainable forestry / Fuel production / Dryland settlement
- **Rotation**: 4-6 years for coppice; perpetual forest matrix
- **Scale**: Settlement to regional

## Key Facts

### Dual-Grid System Design

1. **Perpetual long-term forest matrix**: A grid of permanent forest no more than 300 m apart, nor less than 8 trees wide. Initially established by trickle irrigation but selected to be very hardy on rainfall alone once established.

2. **Coppice harvest grid**: In the sheltered spaces, a grid of close-spaced trees (2-3 m) on trickle irrigation pipes (preferably automatic), harvested in a 4-6 year rotation as coppice.

### Wastewater Integration

- Settlement greywater and sewage is first ponded in deep (to 3 m), narrow, preferably roofed or trellised, and well-sealed collection ponds.
- Water is then led by drip line to a carefully selected and designed fuel forest.
- Example system in operation at Yulara, near Uluru (Ayres Rock) in Central Australia using *[[underground-fungal-networks]] (mycorrhizal associations) that persist through harvest cycles.
- Cut wood and slash from coppice operations provide ideal substrate for [[oyster-mushrooms]] on logs).
- Shredded organic waste from coppice operations feeds biogas digesters; spent digester material becomes mushroom substrate.
- The perpetual forest matrix creates continuous fungal habitat supporting diverse mycological communities.
- Wood chip mulch from coppice operations builds soil organic matter and fungal-dominated soil food webs.
- Anaerobic settling ponds support unique fungal communities involved in decomposition and methane production.

## Design Principles

- Coppice forests must be integrated into settlement layout as primary infrastructure.
- Wastewater-to-forest systems close nutrient cycles and prevent pollution.
- Fuel self-sufficiency reduces pressure on surrounding native vegetation.
- Combined with methane and alcohol production from organic waste for complete energy independence.
