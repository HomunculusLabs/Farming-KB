# Taraxacum officinale

The common dandelion is a perennial herb in the Asteraceae
family, native to Eurasia and naturalized on every continent
except Antarctica. One of the most universally recognized plants,
it has a pharmacological history spanning over 1,000 years in
European, Middle Eastern, and Asian herbal traditions. Every part
— root, leaves, flowers, stems — has documented food or medicinal
use.

Young leaves contain more vitamin A than carrots, more vitamin C
than tomatoes, more potassium than bananas, and significant
vitamins E, K, B-complex, calcium, iron, and zinc. Increasing
scientific interest has focused on hepatoprotective, diuretic,
anti-inflammatory, and potential anti-cancer properties.

## Taxonomy and Classification

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Clade:** Tracheophytes, Angiosperms, Eudicots, Asterids
- **Order:** Asterales
- **Family:** Asteraceae
- **Tribe:** Cichorieae
- **Genus:** Taraxacum
- **Species:** T. officinale

The genus contains ~2,500 species with complex taxonomy from
widespread apomixis (asexual [[cervantes-seed-production-storage]]). Many 'T.
officinale' populations are actually microspecies within section
Ruderalia. 'Dandelion' derives from French 'dent de lion' (lion's
tooth), referring to deeply toothed leaf margins.

## Morphology

Forms a basal rosette of deeply pinnatifid to pinnatisect leaves
5-45 cm long with irregular backward-pointing lobes. Leaves are
glabrous to sparsely pubescent, bright green. The scape is
hollow, leafless, 5-40 cm tall, exuding milky latex when broken.

Each scape bears a single capitulum 2-5 cm diameter of bright
yellow ray florets — lack of disk florets is diagnostic of
Cichorieae. Fruit is an achene 2-3 mm long with pappus of fine
white hairs (5-8 mm). Single plants produce 2,000-5,000 seeds
annually. Taproot is thick, fleshy, deep (15-30 cm, occasionally
>1 m), exuding bitter latex when cut.

## Active Compounds

### Root Constituents

- **Taraxasterol and taraxerol:** Triterpenes with anti-inflammatory
  and anti-edematous properties. Inhibit neutrophil migration and
  TNF-α/IL-6 production.
- **Inulin:** 12-40% dry weight, among richest plant sources.
  Prebiotic stimulating bifidobacteria and lactobacilli; improves
  calcium absorption and modulates blood glucose.
- **Chicoric acid:** Predominant phenolic with potent antioxidant
  activity. Also: caffeic acid, chlorogenic acid, p-coumaric acid.
- **Sesquiterpene lactones:** Dihydrolactucin, ixerin D, taraxinic
  acid derivatives. Contribute bitter taste and stimulate
  digestive enzyme secretion.
- **Taraxacum polysaccharides (TPS):** Immunomodulatory
  heteropolysaccharides stimulating macrophage phagocytosis.

### Leaf Constituents

- **Luteolin and luteolin-7-glucoside:** Flavones with antioxidant
  and diuretic-enhancing activities.
- **Apigenin:** Anxiolytic (GABA_A binding) and anti-inflammatory.
- **Vitamins:** Exceptional beta-carotene (7,700-13,500 μg/100g,
  2-3× carrots), vitamin C (18-35 mg/100g), vitamin K (250-780
  μg/100g), vitamins E, B-complex.
- **Minerals:** Potassium (397 mg/100g), calcium (187 mg/100g),
  iron (3.1 mg/100g), magnesium, zinc, manganese.

### Flower Constituents

- **Flavonoids:** Luteolin, apigenin, isorhamnetin, quercetin
  glycosides in yellow ray florets.
- **Carotenoids:** Lutein, zeaxanthin, beta-carotene — flower color
  and ocular health benefits.
- **Polyphenols:** High total content with ORAC values comparable
  to many berry fruits.

## Pharmacological Properties

1. **Hepatoprotective:** Root extracts protect against CCl₄-induced
   hepatotoxicity, reducing ALT, AST, MDA while restoring GSH
   and SOD. Involves antioxidant protection and NF-κB inhibition.
2. **Diuretic:** Unique potassium-sparing profile — high potassium
   (397 mg/100g) prevents hypokalemia unlike pharmaceutical
   diuretics. Trials confirm increased urine volume at 8 mL
   tincture TID.
3. **Anti-inflammatory:** Potent free radical scavenging and
   pro-inflammatory cytokine reduction (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6,
   COX-2). Chicoric acid is primary contributor.
4. **Anti-diabetic:** Root inulin acts as prebiotic improving
   insulin sensitivity. Animal studies show reduced fasting
   glucose and HbA1c.
5. **Anti-cancer:** Selective cytotoxicity against leukemia,
   melanoma, pancreatic, and colorectal lines while sparing
   normal cells via caspase activation and apoptosis.

## Permaculture and Ecological Value

Deep taproot (up to 1+ m) functions as biological subsoiler,
breaking compacted layers, creating water infiltration channels,
and mining minerals from deep subsoil. Classified as a dynamic
accumulator of potassium, calcium, copper, iron, and silicon.

Flowers are among earliest spring nectar sources, providing
critical forage for honeybees, bumblebees, and native pollinators
over extended bloom periods. Leaves serve as nutrient-rich mulch
or compost. Roots dried and roasted as caffeine-free coffee
substitute retaining prebiotic inulin. Self-seeding, persistent
[[fukuoka-citrus-orchard-ground-cover-cultivation]] requiring zero maintenance.

## Distribution

Native to temperate Eurasia, introduced to every continent.
Thrives in all temperate/subtropical climates and soil types.
Wind-dispersed seeds, deep taproot, self-compatibility, apomixis,
and broad tolerance make it one of Earth's most successful
colonizers. Considered a noxious agricultural weed but not
ecologically damaging invasive in most jurisdictions.
