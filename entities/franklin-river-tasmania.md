## Franklin River (Tasmania)

## Description

The Franklin River is a major river in the Wilderness World Heritage Area of western Tasmania, Australia, renowned as one of Australia's last truly wild rivers and the centre of one of Australia's most significant environmental battles in the early 1980s. David Holmgren references it as an example of how wilderness areas at the "dynamic margin" become catalysts for environmental conflict and cultural transformation.

## Background and Classification

- **Type:** Place — river, wilderness area, environmental battleground
- **Location:** Western Tasmania, Australia
- **Catchment:** Tasmanian Wilderness World Heritage Area (UNESCO listed 1982)
- **Ecological significance:** Temperate rainforest, pristine river system, endemic species habitat
- **Cultural significance:** Indigenous heritage, environmental movement landmark

## Key Facts

- Flows through remote, rugged terrain in western Tasmania largely untouched by European development
- Late 1970s/early 1980s: Tasmanian government proposed damming the Franklin (Gordon-below-Franklin dam) for hydroelectric power
- Sparked one of Australia's largest environmental campaigns (1979–1983)
- Franklin River blockades of 1982–1983: over 1,200 arrests
- Election of Hawke Federal Government in 1983, partly on stopping the dam platform
- Area declared UNESCO World Heritage site; dam never built
- Campaign credited with mobilising a generation of environmental activists

## Relevance to Permaculture and Cultivation

- **Dynamic margins:** The Franklin exemplifies how edges and margins — ecological and social — are where the most important change happens (Principle 11: Use Edges and Value the Marginal)
- **Wilderness as teacher:** Undisturbed ecosystems provide essential reference points for understanding natural system function
- **Energy descent context:** The dam represented industrial-growth extraction; the campaign represented valuing natural capital over energy development
- **Counterculture and change:** The campaign was driven by many of the same people and values that informed the Permaculture movement
- **Resilience through diversity:** The diverse coalition demonstrated the power of diversity in achieving systemic change
