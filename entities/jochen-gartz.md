# Jochen Gartz

German mycologist and chemist known for extensive research on the global distribution, chemistry, and cultural use of psilocybin-containing mushrooms.

## Overview

Jochen Gartz is a German scientist whose work spans the taxonomy, chemistry, and ethnomycology of psychoactive fungi. He has published extensively on the worldwide occurrence of magic mushrooms and conducted pioneering [[guzman-allen-gartz-conflicting-chemical-analyses-neurotropic-fungi]] of numerous species. Based at the Institut fur Biotechnologie der AdW der DDR, Leipzig.

## Key Contributions

- **Global distribution mapping:** Co-authored the landmark paper "A Worldwide Geographical Distribution of the Neurotropic Fungi" (1998) with [[gaston-guzman]] and [[john-w-allen]], cataloguing 216 known species across four groups (psilocybin-containing, ibotenic acid, ergot fungi, and sacred fungi used without chemical confirmation).
- **Chemical analysis:** Performed HPLC analyses on psilocybin and psilocin content across dozens of species, documenting significant inter- and intra-species variation in alkaloid levels.
- **Discovery of Psilocybe natalensis:** Discovered and described the first psychotropic Psilocybe species in South Africa's Natal Province (1994) with Reid, Ecker, and Smith.
- **Tryptamine biotransformation (1989):** Demonstrated that adding 25 mM tryptamine HCl to P. cubensis substrate caused the fungus to convert it to psilocin at unprecedented levels (up to 3.3% dry mass), while psilocybin dropped to near-zero. This showed the fungal enzyme systems have extraordinary hydroxylation and methylation capacity, and that excess tryptamine downregulates the phosphorylation step (Planta Medica 55:249-250).
- **Brassinosteroid research (1990):** With G. Adam and H.-M. Vorbrodt, demonstrated that 22S,23S-homobrassinolide (a plant steroidal growth regulator) dramatically accelerates mycelial growth (2-3x) and advances fruiting by 1-2 weeks in P. cubensis. First study to show brassinosteroid activity on fungi (Naturwissenschaften 77:388-389).
- **European mycophilia documentation:** Compiled the most detailed accounts of casual [[guzman-allen-gartz-psilocybe-semilanceata-temperate-cosmopolitan-distribution]] use by laypersons anywhere in the world, documenting usage patterns in Great Britain, Norway, Germany, and other European countries.
- **African mushroom cult research:** With Giorgio Samorini and Terence McKenna, contributed to documentation of the oldest known mushroom cult in Africa (Tassili rock art, 7000-5000 BCE).
- **Japanese mushroom research:** Documented psychoactive species in Japan including Psilocybe subcaerulipes, P. argentipes, and P. venenata (formerly Stropharia venenata Imai).
- **[[stamets-psi-psilocybe-azurescens-species-guide]] description:** Co-described P. azurescens with [[paul-stamets]] (1995), the most potent [[emcdda-psilocybin-mushroom-dose-and-effects]] known (up to 1.78% total alkaloids), from the Columbia River basin near Astoria, Oregon.

## Major Works

- "Magic Mushrooms Around the World" — Comprehensive survey of global psilocybin mushroom species, distribution, chemistry, and cultural contexts.
- "A Worldwide Geographical Distribution of the Neurotropic Fungi" (1998, with Guzman and Allen) — Published in Ann. Mus. civ. Rovereto, the definitive academic treatment of neurotropic fungal distribution.
- "Biotransformation of Tryptamine in Fruiting Mycelia of [[brassinosteroid-morphological-effects-psilocybe-cubensis-fruiting]]" (1989, Planta Medica) — Key paper on enzymatic alkaloid conversion.
- "Growth-Promoting Effect of a Brassinosteroid in Mycelial Cultures of the Fungus Psilocybe cubensis" (1990, Naturwissenschaften) — First demonstration of brassinosteroid effects on fungi.
- Numerous chemical analysis papers on psilocybin/psilocin content in mushroom species.

## Legacy and Influence

Gartz's work on the global distribution of [[forensic-identification-methods-psilocybin-mushrooms-tlc-gc-hplc]] remains
among the most frequently cited references in psychedelic mycology. His
1998 paper with [[gaston-guzman]] established the foundational
biogeographical framework still used by researchers today. The tryptamine
biotransformation technique he demonstrated in 1989 continues to influence
both academic research and cultivation practices worldwide. His discovery
of Psilocybe natalensis expanded the known range of psychoactive fungi
into southern Africa. His books have been translated into multiple
languages and serve as standard references for mycologists globally.
His contributions to documenting European Psilocybe semilanceata use
remain the most detailed accounts of casual foraging practices ever
published, providing valuable ethnographic data for researchers.

## Later Career

Gartz has continued to publish on fungal chemistry and distribution
throughout his career, contributing to European mycological journals and
encyclopedias. His body of work bridges academic mycology and
ethnopharmacology, making complex chemical and taxonomic data accessible
to a broad audience of researchers and enthusiasts. He remains one of
the most prolific researchers in the field of psychedelic mycology, with
a publication record spanning more than three decades of active research
on the chemistry and ecology of psychoactive fungi worldwide.
