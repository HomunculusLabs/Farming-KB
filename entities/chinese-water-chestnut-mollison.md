## Description

Chinese Water Chestnut (*Eleocharis dulcis*) is an aquatic sedge grown in shallow ponds for its edible corms. It is one of the most valuable aquatic food crops mentioned in Mollison's permaculture manual, thriving in the shallow margins of fish ponds where it also serves habitat functions for forage organisms and contributes to pond ecosystem health.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Phylum**: Tracheophyta
- **Class**: Liliopsida (monocots)
- **Order**: Poales
- **Family**: Cyperaceae (sedge family)
- **Genus**: *Eleocharis*
- **Species**: *E. dulcis*

## Key Facts

- Grows in shallow ponds and wetland margins, typically in water 2-30 cm deep.
- Produces crisp, sweet corms that are a staple food in Chinese and Southeast Asian cuisine.
- Can be integrated into fish pond margins where it provides cover for small fish, shrimp, and forage organisms.
- Contributes to pond health by oxygenating water and absorbing excess nutrients from fish waste.
- Mollison notes that shallow pond zones suitable for Chinese water chestnut also support kangkong (*Ipomoea aquatica*), watercress, taro, frogs, and small fish.
- Related species in the *Eleocharis* genus serve similar functions in constructed wetlands and water purification systems.
- Can produce yields of approximately 600 kg per hectare when integrated into pond polyculture systems alongside fish and mussels.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Chinese Water Chestnut is relevant to integrated permaculture-aquaculture systems that can include fungal components:

- **Substrate production**: The leaf and stem biomass of *E. dulcis* can be composted to create mushroom substrate material.
- **Pond margin integration**: Shallow pond zones planted with water chestnut can be designed to also support outdoor mushroom cultivation on logs or beds at the pond edge.
- **Nutrient cycling**: Water chestnut absorbs nitrogen and phosphorus from fish pond water, producing biomass that can feed fungal decomposition pathways.
- **Wetland mycology**: The rhizosphere of *Eleocharis* species supports diverse fungal communities including mycorrhizal associates that improve nutrient uptake.
- **Polyculture design**: Water chestnut exemplifies the permaculture principle of stacking functions — food production, water purification, habitat creation, and biomass generation in a single plant.
