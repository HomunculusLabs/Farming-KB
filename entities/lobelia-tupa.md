## Lobelia tupa

**Lobelia tupa** L., commonly known as **Tabaco del Diablo** ("Devil"s Tobacco"), is a large herbaceous plant in the family Campanulaceae native to [[oner-sativa-medical-applications]]. The psychoactive and toxic effects of L. tupa are attributed to these piperidine alkaloids. Lobeline was once used in commercial smoking cessation preparations before being largely replaced by nicotine replacement therapies.

## Pharmacology

The piperidine alkaloids in L. tupa primarily act on nicotinic acetylcholine receptors, producing effects that are qualitatively similar to but more intense than those of nicotine. Lobeline is a partial agonist at neuronal nicotinic receptors, meaning it activates these receptors but to a lesser degree than nicotine itself. At higher doses, lobeline acts as an antagonist, blocking the effects of acetylcholine at nicotinic receptor sites.

The pharmacological profile of lobeline explains both the stimulant-like and relaxant effects reported by users, as well as the nausea and vomiting that can occur at higher doses.
