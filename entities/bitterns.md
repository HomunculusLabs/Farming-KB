# Bitterns

## Description

Bitterns are the concentrated, bitter oily liquid residue that remains after common salt (sodium chloride) has been crystallized out of evaporating seawater. This byproduct of salt production is exceptionally rich in potassium, magnesium, bromine, and a wide spectrum of trace minerals and rare elements. In permaculture and sustainable agriculture, bitterns serve as a potent, low-cost source of micronutrients and trace elements for soil amendment and foliar feeding.

## Classification

- **Category**: Mineral extract / Agricultural amendment
- **Type**: Evaporite residue (sea salt production byproduct)
- **Primary components**: Magnesium chloride, potassium chloride, magnesium sulfate, bromine, trace elements

## Composition and Properties

Bitterns contain a concentrated suite of elements from the periodic table, including:

- **Magnesium (Mg)**: Major component, essential for chlorophyll
- **Potassium (K)**: Primary macronutrient for plant growth
- **Bromine (Br)**: Present in significant quantities
- **Trace elements**: Many minor and rare minerals from the early periodic table
- **Sulfate (SO₄)**: Important for protein synthesis
- **Chloride (Cl)**: Various chloride salts

## Key Facts

- The liquid remaining after common salt deposition during seawater evaporation
- Sold commercially as "bitterns" for agricultural use
- Highly corrosive — must be handled in non-corrosive vessels (polyethylene pipes and drums)
- Very economical and easily transported to leached or mineral-deficient areas
- Diluted 100-500:1 for use as foliar spray
- Applied at rates of 1-20 liters per hectare when diluted
- Effects must be established by local trials on specific crops due to variability in composition

## Formation and Production

1. Seawater is drawn into shallow evaporation ponds
2. As water evaporates, calcite and dolomite precipitate first
3. Gypsum and anhydrite crystallize next
4. Rock salt (halite) deposits form
5. The remaining concentrated liquid is bitterns — rich in the most soluble minerals

This sequential precipitation means bitterns contain exactly those minerals most likely to be leached from soils.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

### Soil Amendment
- Restores trace minerals to leached and depleted soils
- Particularly valuable for heavily weathered tropical soils deficient in micronutrients
- Provides a broad spectrum of elements rather than isolated single nutrients
- Economical for large-scale application in extensive agriculture

### Foliar Spray Applications
- Diluted bitterns (100-500:1) applied as foliar spray for rapid nutrient uptake
- Effective at correcting micronutrient deficiencies visible in plant foliage
- Foliar application bypasses soil fixation problems common with trace elements

### Fungal Cultivation Context
- Trace minerals from bitterns support [[fungal-enzyme-systems]]
- Magnesium is critical for fungal metabolism and spore production
- Broad-spectrum mineral content supports diverse microbial communities

### Permaculture Design Principles
- Embodies the principle of using all outputs from a process (waste = resource)
- Connects marine and terrestrial nutrient cycles
- Provides a renewable resource from ongoing salt production
- Local salt works can supply bitterns for nearby agricultural land

## Handling and Safety

- **Corrosive**: Use only polyethylene or other corrosion-resistant containers and pipes
- **Dilution essential**: Always dilute before application
- **Test first**: Conduct local trials on specific crops before large-scale application
- **Storage**: Keep in sealed, corrosion-resistant containers away from metal surfaces
