## Description

**Bob Kingwell** is a permaculture practitioner from **Malanda, Queensland,
Australia**, recognised by Bill Mollison for devising a simple **manifold
irrigation system** that directs water in multiple directions from a head drain.
His low-cost, practical approach to flood irrigation uses concrete plugs with
rubber O-rings and carefully levelled pipes — demonstrating that effective
irrigation infrastructure does not require expensive manufactured components.







## Classification

- **Category**: Person — Permaculture practitioner / Irrigation innovator
- **Domain**: Tropical irrigation, Water management
- **Location**: Malanda, Queensland, Australia (Atherton Tablelands region)
- **Specialty**: Low-cost gravity-fed irrigation design







## Key Facts

- Based in the **Atherton Tablelands** of Far North Queensland — a high-rainfall
  tropical agricultural region known for dairy, fruit, and nut production.
- Devised a **simple manifold** to lead water in 3 directions from a head drain
  using basic pipe infrastructure.
- Pipes are **plugged or opened** to control flow direction, using simple
  concrete plugs with rubber O-ring seals.
- Fitted head drains with **cross-stops** to allow water to back up and flood
  fields to one side, again using simply levelled pipes.
- Design emphasises **low cost, simplicity, and repairability** — essential
  for smallholder and community-scale agriculture.
- Demonstrates that gravity-fed flood irrigation can be managed with minimal
  infrastructure when careful levelling and basic materials are used.







## Relevance to Cultivation / Mycology

- Controlled **flood irrigation** of raised beds (as shown with papaya on slight
  ridges) maintains consistent soil moisture that supports **mycorrhizal
  colonisation** of crop roots.
- Flood irrigation cycles create **alternating wet-dry soil conditions** that
  can stimulate fungal fruiting in interplanted mushroom beds.
- The Atherton Tablelands climate (warm, wet tropics) is ideal for **tropical
  mushroom cultivation** — species such as *Pleurotus pulmonarius* and
  *Volvariella volvacea* thrive in these conditions.
- Irrigated papaya systems interplanted with **Desmodium** ground cover and
  **comfrey** mulch interplants create multi-layered fungal habitat in the
  root zone.
