## Overview

Euphorbia tirucalli, commonly known as pencil cactus, milk bush, or firestick plant, is a spineless, succulent shrub or small tree native to tropical Africa and the Indian subcontinent.

Despite its common name, it is not a true cactus but a member of the spurge family (Euphorbiaceae).

The plant is characterized by its pencil-thin, cylindrical green branches that photosynthesize in the absence of leaves. Branches are leafless for most of the year, an adaptation to arid conditions.

The toxic, milky latex sap is a defining feature of the species.

This sap causes severe skin irritation and temporary blindness upon contact with eyes. Medical literature documents cases of corneal abrasion and keratitis from sap exposure.

Euphorbia tirucalli has gained popularity as a modern ornamental houseplant and landscape specimen.

Its architectural form and vivid coloration make it striking in container gardens. The plant is particularly popular in contemporary minimalist and desert-themed landscapes.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Kingdom:** Plantae
**Family:** Euphorbiaceae
**Genus:** Euphorbia
**Species:** Euphorbia tirucalli L.

The species was described by Carl Linnaeus in 1753. Common synonyms include Euphorbia laro and Tirucalia virgata.

The plant grows as a multi-branched shrub or small tree reaching 4–12 m tall. Mature specimens develop a woody trunk with smooth, gray bark.

Branches are cylindrical, 5–8 mm in diameter, and segmented at joints. They are green and smooth, functioning as photosynthetic organs since true leaves are tiny and ephemeral.

New growth tips emerge bright green, turning yellow-green with age.

In cultivar 'Firestick,' tips turn vivid orange-red during cooler months. This color change intensifies with brighter light exposure and temperature drops.

The latex is white, copious, and extremely irritating.

It contains diterpene esters including 4-deoxyphorbol and ingenol compounds. These compounds are potent tumor promoters and inflammatory agents.

Cyathia (specialized Euphorbia inflorescences) are small, yellow-green, and appear at branch tips.

Male and female flowers are borne on the same plant. Fruit is a three-lobed capsule containing smooth, ovoid seeds.

## Distribution and Habitat

Euphorbia tirucalli is native to tropical eastern and southern Africa, from Kenya and Tanzania southward to Mozambique, Zimbabwe, and South Africa.

It also occurs naturally in India and Sri Lanka, though its nativity there is debated. The species has been widely introduced across the tropics and subtropics as an ornamental and hedge plant.

It has naturalized in many regions including [[psilocybe-southeast-asia-pacific]], Australia, [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-caribbean-central-america]], and the southern [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]].

In some areas it is considered invasive. The plant spreads readily from stem fragments that root upon contact with soil.

The species thrives in dry, open habitats including coastal scrub, savanna, and disturbed ground.

It tolerates poor soils, drought, and salt spray. This adaptability contributes to its success as a colonizer of disturbed sites.

Growth is most vigorous in full sun with temperatures above 10 °C.

The plant is intolerant of frost and waterlogged conditions. Hardiness zones are 10b–12, making it suitable only for frost-free climates or [[cannabis-sativa-indoor-cultivation-techniques]].

## Cultivation

Euphorbia tirucalli is among the easiest succulents to grow, tolerating neglect and poor conditions. It requires well-drained soil, full sun to partial shade, and minimal watering.

Propagation is straightforward from stem cuttings. Cuttings should be allowed to callus for several days before planting to prevent rot from the flowing latex.

Rooting occurs within 2–3 weeks in warm conditions. Plants grow rapidly, adding 30–60 cm of growth per year under optimal conditions.

Container specimens should be grown in a coarse, well-draining cactus mix. Pots must have drainage holes; the plant is highly susceptible to [[query-what-causes-root-rot-in-cannabis-and-how-to-prevent-it]] wet conditions.

The 'Firestick' cultivar is the most widely grown ornamental form. It produces brilliant red-orange coloration on new growth during fall and winter.

Pruning is rarely necessary but can shape plants and control size. Always wear gloves and eye protection when handling due to the toxic sap.
