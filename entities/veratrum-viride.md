## Overview

**Veratrum viride**, the **American white hellebore** or **green false
hellebore**, is a large perennial herbaceous plant in the family
Melanthiaceae, native to moist meadows and swamps across [[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]], but the two are not closely related.

## Taxonomy

- **Family:** Melanthiaceae (formerly Liliaceae)
- **Genus:** *Veratrum* L.
- **Species:** *V. viride* Aiton (1789)

The species was described by William Aiton from material cultivated at
Kew Gardens, originally collected in [[polypore-fungi-eastern-north-america]] America. It is closely
related to *V. album* (European white hellebore) and *V. californicum*
(California false hellebore), all containing similar steroidal alkaloid
profiles.

## Morphology

**Growth habit:** Robust perennial, 0.5–2 m tall, from a thick, short
rhizome with numerous fleshy roots. Leaves and flowering stem emerge
simultaneously in early spring.

**Leaves:** Broadly elliptic to ovate, 15–30 cm long × 8–15 cm wide,
strongly pleated with prominent parallel veins, clasping at base and
reduced upward. Leaves are alternate, sheathing, giving the stem a
distinctive pleated appearance. Bright green, hairless.

**Inflorescence:** Panicle, 20–60 cm long, much-branched, with
numerous flowers on ascending pedicels.

**Flowers:** 1–2 cm across, perianth segments 6, greenish-yellow,
spreading. Flowers are imperfect — the species is functionally
dioecious or polygamous, with male and female flowers on separate or
mixed plants. Stamens 6, opposite the tepals; ovary superior, 3-lobed.

**Fruit:** Capsule, 1.5–3 cm, ovoid, 3-valved, containing numerous
flattened, winged seeds.

## Distribution and Habitat

V. viride ranges from Labrador and Quebec west to Minnesota and south
through the Appalachian Mountains to Georgia and Alabama. Disjunct
populations occur in the Black Hills of South Dakota and in the Pacific
Northwest.

The species inhabits wet meadows, swampy thickets, streambanks, spring
seeps, and moist montane forests. It tolerates both acidic and neutral
soils, typically in partial shade to full sun. Elevation: 0–1,800 m.
