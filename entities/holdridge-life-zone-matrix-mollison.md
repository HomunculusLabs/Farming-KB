# Holdridge Life Zone Matrix

## Description

The Holdridge Life Zone Matrix is a **bioclimatic classification system** that
[[maps]] world vegetation patterns based on three intersecting axes: biotemperature,
annual precipitation, and potential evapotranspiration ratio. Developed by
American botanist **Leslie R. Holdridge**, it provides a more finely resolved
framework for plant list classification than the broader Köppen system, enabling
botanists and [[permaculture]] designers to annotate plant lists with concise
climatic keys. The system is presented in Figure 5.2 of Mollison's manual as
a key reference for matching plant species to specific climatic conditions.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Science — Bioclimatology and Ecology
- **Category:** Classification system / Analytical tool
- **Scope:** Global — all terrestrial biomes
- **Variables:** Biotemperature, annual precipitation, potential evapotranspiration
- **Output:** Named "life zones" with associated vegetation types

## Key Facts

- Uses a **triangular coordinate system** where three variables intersect to
  define distinct "life zones" — each zone characterized by specific vegetation
  assemblages.
- The system identifies major biomes including: Tundra, Boreal Forest,
  Chaparral, Temperate Grassland, Temperate Forest, Desert, Tropical Shrub
  and Woodland, Tropical Savannah, Tropical Deciduous Forest, and Tropical
  Evergreen Forest.
- Biome coverage by land area and plant mass (from Mollison's Figure 5.2):
  - Tropical evergreen forest: 10.5% land area, 34% of plant mass
  - Boreal forest: 8% land area, 16% of plant mass
  - Temperate forest: 7% land area, 19% of plant mass
  - Tropical savannah and grassland: 15% land area, 5% plant mass
  - Chaparral: 25% land area, 1% plant mass
  - Desert: 29% land area, 1% plant mass
- **James Duke** and other botanists used the Holdridge system to annotate
  plant lists with climatic keys, making it a practical tool for species
  selection in [[permaculture-design]].
- The matrix accounts for **latitudinal regions and altitudinal belts**,
  recognizing that similar life zones occur at different latitudes and
  altitudes (e.g., a tropical alpine zone shares characteristics with boreal
  zones at lower altitude).

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

The Holdridge system is directly useful for permaculture practitioners:

- **Plant selection**: More precise than hardiness zones alone, allowing
  designers to match species to the specific combination of temperature,
  rainfall, and evapotranspiration at a site.
- **Analogue design**: Similar life zones in different parts of the world can
  inform species substitution — a plant thriving in one Holdridge zone may
  succeed in the same zone elsewhere.
- **Biome understanding**: The matrix reveals the relationship between climate
  and vegetation biomass, helping designers set realistic yield expectations.
- **Data-driven planting**: Using James Duke's annotated plant lists, designers
  can select species with confidence based on quantitative climatic matching.
