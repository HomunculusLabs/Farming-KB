---# Russula virescens

**Russula virescens**, the green cracking russula or quilted green russula, is a
basidiomycete fungus in the family Russulaceae. Widely regarded as one of the
finest edible [[neurotropic-fungi-japan-east-asia-species-cultural-context]], it is an ectomycorrhizal
species associated primarily with oak (*Quercus* spp.) and other hardwoods. The
species is easily recognized by its pale green cap with a distinctive cracked
(mosaic) surface pattern.

## Taxonomy

Basidiomycota → Agaricomycetes → Russulales → **Russulaceae → *Russula → *R. virescens***

First described by Jacob Christian Schäffer in 1774. The epithet *virescens* means
"becoming green" in Latin. Sometimes confused in literature with *R. aeruginea*
or *R. virescens* var. *cervina*; modern molecular studies confirm *R. virescens*
as a well-defined species. Chinese mycological texts recognize it as *lu jun*
(绿菌), a highly prized culinary mushroom with a long history of collection.

## Description

- **Cap:** 5–15 cm, convex becoming flat with a shallow depression; margin smooth
  and slightly incurved when young. Surface pale green to grayish-green, developing
  a characteristic cracked or quilted pattern (areolate) as it matures — this
  mosaic cracking is the most reliable [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]]. Fruits from June to October, peaking in late summer.
In China it is commercially harvested from oak forests in Yunnan, Sichuan, and
Guizhou provinces.

## Culinary Value

Considered one of the premier edible *Russula* species, often ranked alongside
chanterelles and porcini in European foraging traditions. In Italy it is known as
*fungo verde* and commands premium prices at markets. The flesh is firm and crunchy
even after cooking, with a mild nutty flavor. Common preparations include sautéing
in butter or [[edible-wild-mushrooms-by-region]] in southwestern China, with annual harvests exceeding
several thousand tonnes in peak years. The firm flesh holds up well to canning and
drying without becoming mushy.
