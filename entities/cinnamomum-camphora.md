# Cinnamomum camphora

## Overview

Cinnamomum camphora is a large evergreen tree in the family Lauraceae, native to [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]], and some [[dighton-fungal-decomposition-leaf-litter-dynamics]] and dense shading.

## Taxonomy and Classification

Kingdom: Plantae → Clade: Tracheophytes → Clade: Angiosperms → Clade: Magnoliids → Order: Laurales → Family: Lauraceae → **Genus: *Cinnamomum* → Species: *C. camphora***.

The genus *Cinnamomum* contains approximately 250 species of aromatic evergreen [[cervantes-selfing-single-seed-descent]]. The fleshy pericarp is attractive to birds, which serve as primary dispersal agents.

## Distribution and Habitat

Native range spans southeastern China (Fujian, Jiangxi, Hunan provinces), southern Japan (Kyushu, Shikoku, Ryukyu Islands), Taiwan, and northern Vietnam.

It grows in warm-temperate to subtropical broadleaf forests at elevations from sea level to 2000 m. The species prefers well-drained, acidic to neutral soils with good moisture retention.

Outside its native range, C. camphora is widely naturalized in eastern Australia (declared a noxious weed in NSW and Queensland), the southeastern [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-south-america]].

## Chemical Composition

Camphor content varies significantly among chemotypes. Five main chemotypes have been identified based on dominant leaf oil constituents: camphor, linalool, cineole, nerolidol, and borneol.

The camphor chemotype (the most commercially important) contains 60–80% (+)-camphor in its [[permaculture-designers-manual-essential-oil-plants]]. Linalool chemotypes yield oil with 70–90% linalool, valued in perfumery and aromatherapy.

Other constituents include safrole (in some chemotypes), eucalyptol (1,8-cineole), α-terpineol, α-pinene, limonene, and various sesquiterpenes. Bark oil differs from leaf oil in composition, generally containing higher camphor concentrations.

## Pharmacology and Traditional Medicine

In Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM), camphor wood and oil are classified as acrid, warm, and toxic. They are used externally to relieve pain, kill parasites, and reduce swelling.

Camphor exerts its pharmacological effects primarily through TRPV1 and TRPA1 receptor modulation, producing a cooling/warming sensation followed by local analgesia. It also demonstrates antimicrobial activity against Gram-positive bacteria and some fungi.

Linalool-rich chemotypes show anxiolytic, sedative, and anti-inflammatory properties in preclinical studies. Linalool modulates GABAergic neurotransmission and suppresses pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-6).

**Safety note:** Camphor is neurotoxic in high doses. Oral ingestion of as little as 2 g can cause seizures in children. Topical use should avoid application to broken skin or near the faces of infants.

## Cultivation

C. camphora is hardy to approximately -10°C when mature, though young trees are more frost-sensitive. It grows best in full sun to partial shade with consistent moisture.

Propagation is primarily by seed, which requires cold stratification for 2–3 months. Semi-hardwood cuttings under mist also succeed but with lower strike rates than seed.
