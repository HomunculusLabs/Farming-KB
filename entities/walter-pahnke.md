# Walter Pahnke

**Walter Norman Pahnke** (1931 -- 10 July 1971) was an American physician,
psychiatrist, and theologian whose pioneering research on the relationship
between psychedelic drugs [[cannabis-for-cancer-patients]] at Spring Grove Hospital laid
the groundwork for modern psychedelic-assisted [[mckenna-suppression-psychedelic-research-lsd-therapy]] program of **[[richard-alpert]]** at
the Center for Research in Personality. Concurrently, he studied at
**Harvard Divinity School**, reflecting his interest in the intersection of
religion, mysticism, and psychopharmacology. The comparative religion
scholar **Huston Smith** served on his dissertation committee. This
combination of medical, psychiatric, and theological training was unique in
the field and gave Pahnke an unparalleled perspective on psychedelic
experiences.

## The Good Friday Experiment (1962)

Pahnke's most celebrated contribution was the Good Friday Experiment,
formally "Drugs and Mysticism: An Analysis of the Relationship between
Psychedelic Drugs and the Mystical Consciousness," his doctoral
dissertation and one of the most famous experiments in the history of
psychology of religion.

### Design

On **Good Friday, 20 April 1962**, Pahnke conducted a double-blind,
placebo-controlled experiment at **Boston University's Marsh Chapel** during
a service led by Reverend **Howard Thurman**. Twenty Protestant divinity
students were randomly assigned to receive either **30 mg of purified
psilocybin** or an **active placebo** (nicotinic acid, which produces
flushing without psychoactive effects). Subjects listened to a broadcast
of the service from a basement room below the chapel.

### Results

Pahnke assessed experiences using a modified version of philosopher
**Walter T. Stace's** typology of mystical consciousness, measuring seven
dimensions: unity, transcendence of time and space, deep positive mood,
sense of sacredness, paradoxicality, ineffability, and transiency. **9 of
10** psilocybin subjects reported a genuine mystical experience versus only
**1 of 10** controls -- a statistically significant result. Several
subjects described the experience as among the most meaningful of their
lives.

### Significance and Follow-Up

The experiment was the first to demonstrate under rigorous conditions that
a psychedelic could reliably produce experiences meeting philosophical
criteria for mystical consciousness. In 1989, **Rick Doblin** (founder of
MAPS) published a 25-year follow-up in the *Journal of Transpersonal
Psychology* confirming lasting positive effects on participants' lives.
