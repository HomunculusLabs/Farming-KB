# Sphaerostilbella

## Description

Sphaerostilbella is a genus of fungicolous ascomycetes in the Hypocreaceae (Hypocreales) whose members grow primarily on basidiomata of wood-decaying aphyllophorales, particularly Stereum species. The genus is characterized by its Gliocladium sensu stricto anamorphs, which produce penicilliate conidiophores. Sphaerostilbella species share morphological characters with Hypocrea and Hypomyces but form a distinct lineage of specialized mycoparasites.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Eukaryota
- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Sordariomycetes
- **Order:** Hypocreales
- **Family:** Hypocreaceae
- **Genus:** Sphaerostilbella

## Key Facts

- Anamorphs belong to Gliocladium sensu stricto
- Most species grow mainly on Stereum species (Seifert 1985)
- S. aureonitens (anamorph Gliocladium penicillioides) common on Stereum
- S. lutea (anamorph G. aurifilum) also found on Stereum basidiomata
- S. novaezelandiae described from New Zealand
- S. berkeleyana grows on polypores (Samuels 1976; Helfer 1991)
- S. broomeana (formerly Hypomyces broomeana) found on Heterobasidion annosum
- Revised by Seifert (1985) and Põldmaa (1999)

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Sphaerostilbella species are ecologically important mycoparasites of Stereum and other corticioid fungi, which are among the most common wood-decay organisms in temperate forests. Because Stereum species can be competitors of cultivated mushrooms on wood substrates (particularly shiitake and oyster mushrooms on logs), understanding natural parasites of Stereum may inform biological control strategies. The genus also illustrates the taxonomic complexity within the Hypocreales, where anamorph-teleomorph connections and generic boundaries continue to be revised.

## Notable Interactions

The species S. broomeana is particularly notable for its association with Heterobasidion annosum — one of the most destructive root-rot pathogens of conifers in the Northern Hemisphere. This association has been studied in the context of biological control of this important forest pathogen.
