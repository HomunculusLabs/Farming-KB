## Overview

**Woodsia obtusa** is a small deciduous fern commonly known as the blunt-lobed woodsia or common woodsia. It is native to eastern and central North America, where it grows on rocky substrates.

The species is a member of the rock fern group, typically found on limestone cliffs, dolomite outcrops, and calcareous talus slopes. Its preference for basic rock substrates distinguishes it from many other woodland ferns.

Blunt-lobed woodsia is one of the most widely distributed Woodsia species in North America. Its range extends from New England and Ontario west to the Great Plains and south to Texas and Georgia.

The genus Woodsia was named in honor of the English botanist Joseph Woods (1776–1864). The specific epithet obtusa refers to the blunt or rounded tips of the pinna lobes.

The genus comprises approximately 30 species distributed across temperate and arctic regions of the Northern Hemisphere. In calcareous cliff communities, blunt-lobed woodsia grows alongside other calcicolous ferns including Pellaea atropurpurea and Asplenium ruta-muraria. These assemblages represent some of the most botanically diverse rock outcrop communities in eastern North America.

## Ethnobotany and Uses

No significant ethnobotanical applications are recorded for Woodsia obtusa. The small size and limited distribution of the species likely precluded widespread use by indigenous peoples.

In modern horticulture, blunt-lobed woodsia is cultivated by specialized rock garden enthusiasts. Its compact size and calcicolous preference make it suitable for trough gardens and limestone rockeries.

The fern is not commercially available through mainstream nurseries. Propagation is primarily through specialized fern societies and native plant exchanges.

Conservation concern is low for the species as a whole, though local populations on heavily quarried limestone cliffs may be vulnerable to habitat destruction. Monitoring of populations near active quarry operations is recommended.
