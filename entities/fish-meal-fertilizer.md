# Fish Meal (Fertilizer)

## Description

Fish meal is a dry organic fertilizer produced by heating and drying ground fish parts. With an N–P–K of approximately 10–6–2, it stands out among fish-based fertilizers as a dual-source of both nitrogen and phosphorus. Unlike fish emulsion, fish meal is not readily soluble and acts as a moderate-release fertilizer that relies on soil microbial activity to break down its nutrients.

## Classification

- **Category:** Organic nitrogen and phosphorus fertilizer (dry)
- **N–P–K:** 10–6–2
- **Origin:** Animal by-product (ground fish parts, heated and dried)
- **Availability:** Moderate; requires microbial decomposition; not immediately soluble

## Key Facts

- One of the few organic fertilizers that provides significant amounts of both nitrogen (10%) and phosphorus (6%).
- Not readily soluble in water, meaning nutrients release gradually through microbial activity rather than dissolving immediately.
- Slower to start than fish emulsion or fish powder, but provides sustained nutrition for 1 to 4 months.
- Heat processing during manufacturing destroys some enzymes, proteins, vitamins, and micronutrients that remain in non-heat-processed fish products like hydrolyzed fish.
- A solid, dry product that can be stored easily and applied with broadcast spreaders or by hand.
- Odor is present but less intense than fish emulsion, dissipating after a few days.

## Application Guidelines

- Incorporate into soil before planting for best phosphorus placement, since phosphorus is relatively immobile in soil.
- Use when both nitrogen and phosphorus are needed, such as during transplanting or early vegetative growth.
- Apply as a side dressing to established plants during active growth periods.
- Suitable for mixing into planting holes for transplants, providing slow-release nutrition at the root zone.
- Store in a dry, sealed container to prevent moisture absorption and premature decomposition.

## Cautions

- **Odor:** Smells for a few days after application, though less intensely than fish emulsion.
- **Wildlife attraction:** Can attract flies, dogs, and bears — use caution in wildlife-prone areas.
- **Nutrient loss from processing:** Heat treatment reduces some of the enzymes and vitamins present in raw fish, making it less biologically active than hydrolyzed fish products.
- **Not quick-acting:** Should not be relied upon for correcting acute nutrient deficiencies.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- The combination of nitrogen and phosphorus makes fish meal valuable for supporting both vegetative growth (N) and root development/flowering (P).
- Soil microbes are required to mineralize the organic nitrogen and phosphorus, directly feeding and stimulating the soil food web.
- In organic mushroom cultivation, fish meal has been used as a nitrogen supplement in substrate formulations.
- The moderate release profile supports sustained biological activity in compost and soil systems.
- Heat-processed fish meal loses some bioactive compounds, illustrating the trade-off between shelf stability and biological activity in fertilizer processing.
