## Spartina cynosuroides

## Description

Spartina cynosuroides, commonly known as big cordgrass or giant cordgrass, is a large perennial rhizomatous grass native to the Atlantic and Gulf coasts [[huckleberries-and-wild-berries-of-north-america]]. It is the tallest species in the genus Spartina, reaching heights of up to 3 meters. The species is ecologically significant as a dominant plant in brackish marshes and as one of the Spartina species that consistently forms arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) associations, in contrast to the closely related S. alterniflora, which is typically non-mycorrhizal.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Division:** Magnoliophyta (Angiosperms)
- **Class:** Liliopsida (Monocots)
- **Order:** Poales
- **Family:** Poaceae
- **Genus:** Spartina (also classified in Sporobolus)
- **Species:** S. cynosuroides

Recent molecular phylogenetic work has proposed merging Spartina into an expanded genus Sporobolus. Under this classification, S. cynosuroides becomes Sporobolus cynosuroides. The name Spartina remains in widespread use in the ecological literature.

## Morphology

- **Height:** 100–300 cm, among the tallest grasses in North American coastal marshes
- **Stems:** Robust, erect, 5–15 mm diameter, often solitary or in loose clumps from rhizomes
- **Leaves:** Long, flat, 8–20 mm wide, 30–80 cm long, scabrous margins, gray-green to dark green
- **Inflorescence:** Erect, narrow panicle, 20–50 cm long, with appressed branches
- **Spikelets:** Strongly flattened, 12–18 mm long, single-flowered
- **Roots:** Extensive fibrous and rhizomatous root system, colonized by AMF

## Ecology and Habitat

S. cynosuroides occupies a distinctive niche in [[mycorrhizae-aquatic-estuarine-ecosystems]]:

- **Habitat:** Brackish marshes, tidal riverbanks, upper estuarine zones
- **Salinity range:** Brackish water (5–18 ppt salinity), less tolerant of full seawater salinity than S. alterniflora
- **Tidal regime:** Regularly to irregularly flooded by tides; occupies the high marsh and streamside zones
- **Geographic range:** Atlantic coast from Massachusetts to Florida, Gulf coast to Texas
- **Community role:** Forms dense, tall stands that dominate brackish marsh zones

## Key Facts

- **Mycorrhizal contrast with S. alterniflora:** S. cynosuroides is frequently mycorrhizal while S. alterniflora is consistently reported as non-mycorrhizal, despite both growing in intertidal marsh habitats. This difference has made them a model system for studying controls on mycorrhizal formation in saline environments.
- **Salinity and AMF:** McHugh (2001) showed that hyphal colonization of S. cynosuroides roots was not reduced by higher salinity conditions, unlike S. alterniflora which showed reduced colonization under high salinity.
- **Nitrogen enhancement:** [[mycorrhizal-colonization-thresholds]] of S. cynosuroides roots increased total nitrogen assimilation by the plant, demonstrating a functional benefit of the AM association.
- **Increased tillering:** [[mycorrhizal-inoculation]] resulted in more shoots per pot (increased tillering), a response also observed in dune grass and wetland rice, suggesting mycorrhizae promote vegetative spread.
- **Soil stabilization:** The dense root-rhizome network and enhanced tillering from mycorrhizal colonization could significantly enhance rates of lateral spread in restoration plantings.

## Mycorrhizal Associations

S. cynosuroides forms functional arbuscular mycorrhizal associations with specific characteristics:

- **Colonization type:** [[arbuscular-mycorrhizae]] with hyphae, arbuscules, and vesicles
- **Salinity tolerance:** AMF colonization persists under brackish conditions; arbuscule abundance is not significantly reduced by salinity
- **Depth distribution:** Vesicles and arbuscules found to considerable depth in sediments (25+ cm)
- **Seasonal variation:** Colonization increases during periods of maximal plant growth, linked to nutrient demand
- **Functional benefit:** Enhanced nitrogen uptake is the primary documented benefit, rather than [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-phosphorus-acquisition]]

## Interactions with Other Organisms

- **[[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi]]:** Functional AMF associations that enhance nitrogen uptake
- **[[spartina-alterniflora]]:** Closely related but typically non-mycorrhizal; the two species overlap in brackish marsh habitats
- **Salt marsh fauna:** Dense stands provide nesting habitat for marsh birds and refuge for fish and crustaceans
- **Sediment processes:** Rhizome network stabilizes sediments and influences marsh accretion rates

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

S. cynosuroides has significance across several applied and research domains:

- **Comparative mycorrhizal ecology:** The mycorrhizal vs. non-mycorrhizal contrast with S. alterniflora provides a natural experiment for studying controls on AMF formation in saline environments
- **Marsh restoration:** [[mycorrhizal-inoculation-and-inoculum-production]] could enhance establishment and spread of S. cynosuroides in restoration projects
- **Soil stabilization:** Enhanced tillering from AMF colonization accelerates lateral spread and soil stabilization
- **Nitrogen cycling:** Mycorrhizal enhancement of nitrogen uptake links fungal symbiosis to marsh nutrient cycling
