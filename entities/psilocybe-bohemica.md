# Psilocybe bohemica

A psychoactive mushroom species found primarily in Central Europe, particularly the Czech Republic (Bohemia). Known for having the most intense [[field-guide-psilocybin-bluing-reaction-identification]] of any psilocybin-containing species despite relatively low psilocin content. Named for the Bohemian region where it was first documented. See [[european-psilocybin-species]] for comparison with other European species and [[psilocybin-bluing-reaction]] for its unique biochemical properties.

## Taxonomy and Discovery

Originally described from specimens collected near Sázava, Bohemia (Czech Republic). The species was confirmed as distinct from *[[growing-gourmet-caramel-capped-psilocybe-cyanescens-cultivation]]* through cross-breeding experiments — mycelia from *P. bohemica* and *P. cyanescens* (US collections) cannot be fused together, proving they are separate species.

## Identification

- **Cap**: Discolors very rapidly and intensely blue when touched or handled — the most dramatic bluing reaction among all known psilocybin species
- **Habitat**: Grows in grassy areas and parks in Central Europe
- **Season**: Fruits during autumn months
- **Spore color**: Dark purple-brown (typical of Psilocybe genus)

## Cultivation

Successfully cultivated by [[jochen-gartz]] on damp rice substrate. Key findings:

- Fruiting occurs after 2-3 months of mycelial growth
- Cultivated specimens appeared **much hardier** than wild-collected fruiting bodies
- Some cultivated specimens developed **two annular rings** — unusual for the species
- Fruitng required exposure to **cold temperature shock** to trigger
- Mycelial growth rate is faster than *[[guzman-allen-gartz-psilocybe-semilanceata-temperate-cosmopolitan-distribution]]* but comparable to *[[gartz-gymnopilus-purpuratus-south-american-mushroom-in-europe]]*

## Psychoactive Properties

- **Bluing reaction**: Exceptionally strong and rapid despite low or non-existent psilocin levels
- **Biochemistry**: The rapid bluing is due to extremely fast enzymatic removal of the phosphate group from psilocybin, rapidly producing psilocin which then oxidizes to blue compounds
- **Potency**: Described as producing experiences that "bypass" initial phases, propelling directly into transpersonal consciousness (Gartz)
- **Alkaloid profile**: Low psilocin, but psilocybin present

## Distribution

Primarily known from the Czech Republic (Bohemia region). Usage was reported beginning in the late 1980s in the former Czechoslovakia, around the Brno region. The species is quite common in that area and appears to be used more frequently than *Psilocybe semilanceata* in the Czech Republic.

## Habitat and Ecology

Detailed observations from the type locality near Poricko v Pozavi, Sázava River (Czech Republic):

- **Substrate**: Fruiting on wood chips of *Carpinus* (hornbeam), *Alnus* (alder), and *Salix* (willow), plus raw compost mixtures of *Picea* (spruce), *Pinus* (pine), and *Larix* (larch) needles
- **Associated vegetation**: Commonly found growing among nettles along creek banks
- **Mycelial habits**: Mycelia colonize diverse plant debris and even grow on wet cardboard, developing rhizomorphs (thick mycelial strands for nutrient/water transport) identical to those formed in nature
- **Fruiting density**: 440 fruiting bodies (550g total) collected along a nearly two-mile stretch of creek bank during a single foray
- **Fruiting season**: Primarily September through January (autumn through early winter), as documented in the Czech Republic

## Clinical Observations

### Prague Experiments (Gartz, 1980s)

Controlled [[mushroom-cancer-clinical-trials-overview]] in Prague documented the species' psychoactive effects:

- **Standard dose**: ~30 mg psilocybin in mushroom tissue prepared in hot water, onset within 10 minutes
- **Effects profile**: Initial somatic effects (leg tingling, underarm tingling), deeper breathing, fits of laughter from unusual cognitive associations, growing hyperacuity interfering with music perception (Vivaldi's "Springtime" caused "painful stabs inside the brain")
- **Visual effects**: Existing bodily characteristics stimulated illusionary transformation (thinning hair → monk with tonsure); voices appeared reverent
- **High-dose incident**: In a separate session, analytical error led to four participants ingesting 72 mg psilocybin plus psilocin (intended: 30 mg). One participant sank into an "autistic state devoid of visions" lasting three hours, while another female subject experienced "fantastic images of moving colors" and saw "visions of her whole life unfolding behind closed eyes." A third reported feeling "as if legs were increasingly merging into the wall" progressing to "the experience of a pure soul."

## Discovery History

- **1942**: First collected by Kubicka on December 6 and 13 in the Kresicky Creek Valley village of Poricko v Pozavi near Sázava, Czech Republic
- **1950**: Mycologist Herink provided detailed description; proposed that Fries had classified specimens of the *Psilocybe cyanescens* complex as *P. callosa* during the 19th century
- **1986 (November 11)**: [[jochen-gartz]] and Herink conducted joint mycological field research at the type locality, confirming extensive natural fruiting
