# Dollar Spot (Lawn Disease)

## Description

Dollar spot is one of the two most troublesome diseases of golf course greens and a significant turfgrass pathology. It is caused by fungi in the genus *Sclerotinia* (formerly *Lanzia* or *Moellerodiscus*). In [[the-soil-food-web]] framework, dollar spot is directly linked to excessive nitrate levels in soils — a condition created by synthetic chemical fertilizers. Mild cases can be suppressed by bacterially dominated [[compost]] teas, which outcompete the pathogen for resources and space.

## Classification

- **Causal organisms:** *Sclerotinia* spp. (also classified as *Clarireedia* spp. in modern taxonomy)
- **Host plants:** Turfgrass species (bentgrass, bermudagrass, fescues, ryegrasses)
- **Symptoms:** Small, circular, bleached or straw-colored patches approximately the size of a silver dollar (2–6 inches in diameter)
- **Indicator status:** Excessive nitrates from chemical fertilizers

## Key Facts

- One of the two most prevalent and costly diseases on golf course greens worldwide
- Caused by *Sclerotinia* species that thrive in high-nitrate soil conditions
- Excessive nitrates from chemical lawn fertilizers directly promote dollar spot development
- Presents as small, round, sunken patches of bleached or tan grass
- Bacterially dominated compost teas have been effective in suppressing mild cases by:
  - Outcompeting the pathogen for leaf surface space and nutrients
  - Establishing protective bacterial biofilms on grass blades
  - Cycling excess nitrates into microbial biomass
- Severe infestations require not only competitor organisms but also reduction of nitrate inputs
- In the [[soil-food-web]] model, the disease is a symptom of chemical fertilizer misuse — addressing root cause (excess nitrates, killed soil biology) eliminates the conditions that favor the pathogen

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Dollar spot management through compost tea exemplifies biological disease control:

1. **Pathogen mechanism:** *Sclerotinia* exploits high-nitrate conditions created by chemical fertilizers
2. **Biological suppression:** Bacterial teas introduce competitors that consume available nitrogen and physically occupy leaf surfaces
3. **Root cause approach:** Eliminating synthetic fertilizers restores the soil food web, which naturally keeps pathogens in check
4. **Fungal connection:** While bacterial teas suppress mild dollar spot, the broader strategy involves restoring full soil food web diversity including fungal, protozoan, and nematode populations

This case study demonstrates how understanding soil food web ecology enables disease management without chemical fungicides.
