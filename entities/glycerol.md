## Glycerol (Culture Preservation)

## Description
Glycerol (also known as glycerin) is a cryoprotectant chemical used in [[mycology]] to protect mushroom [[mycelium]] cells from rupture due to ice crystal formation during freezing. It is an essential component of long-term culture preservation protocols, enabling cultivators to maintain living libraries of mushroom strains almost indefinitely when properly applied.

## Classification
- **Category**: Chemical / Cryoprotectant
- **Domain**: Mycology, laboratory science, cryobiology
- **Chemical formula**: C3H8O3
- **Function**: Preventing cellular damage during freezing

## Key Facts

### Mechanism of Action
- Prevents ice crystals from rupturing mycelial cell walls during freezing
- Acts as an antifreeze protecting cellular structures
- Without glycerol, freezing damages and kills mycelium

### Application Protocol
1. Prepare a 5% glycerol solution (e.g., 95 ml culture slurry + 5 ml glycerol)
2. Pre-chill the [[liquid-culture]] before adding glycerol
3. Adding glycerol to warm culture can damage mycelium of some species
4. Combine colonized agar cubes or grain with the sterile water-glycerol solution
5. Fill test tubes half-full; keep caps loose during initial freezing for ice expansion
6. Place tubes in sterile autoclavable bag with filter patch, freeze
7. Once frozen, tighten caps, seal with Parafilm, store in ziplock bags

### Two Main Preservation Methods
**Method 1: Agar/Grain Cubes** — Colonized material submerged in 5% glycerol, frozen directly
**Method 2: Liquid Slurry with Broth** — Slurry in weak nutrient broth, agitate 1-3 days for recovery, pre-chill, add glycerol to 5%, freeze

### Safety Considerations
- Glycerol itself can be toxic to mycelium if the procedure is not exacting
- Temperature management (pre-chilling) is critical
- Follow guidelines precisely — this is chemistry not home cooking
- Test viability before committing entire collection to frozen storage

### Viability Testing
- Prepare multiple identical samples of each strain
- Remove one tube after one month, thaw in warm water bath (77-86F/25-30C) for 2-5 minutes
- Transfer to fresh agar plate and evaluate recovery
- Look for uniform mycelial growth resembling the original culture
- Repeat at increasing intervals to determine maximum storage duration

## Relevance to Cultivation
Glycerol-based freezing is the gold standard for long-term preservation of mushroom culture collections. When properly executed, frozen glycerol stocks can preserve cultures almost indefinitely, making them essential for commercial operations protecting proprietary strains and researchers maintaining biodiversity collections.
