## Cenococcum geophilum

**Type:** Species (Fungus)
**Category:** Ectomycorrhizal Ascomycete
**Also known as:** Cenococcum geophilum Fr.

## Overview
Cenococcum geophilum is a cosmopolitan ectomycorrhizal fungus notable for
being one of the few ascomycetes in a group dominated by basidiomycetes.
It is one of the most widely distributed and abundant ectomycorrhizal
fungi globally, found in diverse habitats from [[fungal-lichen-stress-tolerance-and-poikilohydry]] and
dark, melanized mycelium.

## Taxonomy and Classification
- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Dothideomycetes
- **Order:** Mytilinidiales
- **Genus:** Cenococcum
- **Species:** C. geophilum (monotypic genus)

## Key Details

### Morphology
- Does not produce a typical [[query-what-are-ectomycorrhizal-fungi-and-which-plants-do-they-associate-with]] in
  global forest surveys

### Nutrient Solubilization
- Shows strong capacity for solubilizing complex inorganic phosphate
  forms, with activity up to 250 units against calcium phytate
  (Lapeyrie et al., 1991)
- Maintains phosphate solubilization even in the presence of
  available soluble phosphorus, unlike many other species
- Capable of accessing organic nitrogen sources

### Stress Tolerance
- Melanized hyphae provide protection against desiccation,
  UV radiation, and microbial attack
- Shows moderate tolerance to aluminum, with growth suppression at
  146 mM (Thompson and Medve, 1984)
- 50% inhibition concentration for heavy metals: Al 10 mg/L, Fe 200,
  Cu 10, Zn 10, Ni 1, Cd 1, Pb 200 mg/L (Tam, 1995)
- Particularly sensitive to nickel and cadmium compared to metals
  like lead and iron

### Ecological Role
- Functions as both an ectomycorrhizal partner and a soil saprotroph
- Important for tree seedling establishment in [[mycorrhizal-fungi-soil-carbon-sequestration-by-type]] through
  its dark, recalcitrant melanized hyphae
- Persists in disturbed sites and is often a pioneer ectomycorrhizal
  colonizer

### Hyphal Development
- Produces extensive extraradical mycelium (28.1 mm2 hyphal area
  per gram of soil with pine seedlings)
- Does not produce rhizomorphs (differentiating it from species like
  Pisolithus)
- Total fungal area of 28.1 mm2/g soil (Rousseau et al., 1994)
