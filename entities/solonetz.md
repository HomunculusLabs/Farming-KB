# Solonetz

## Description

Solonetz (also referred to as "solonetic soils") are a group of soils characterized by a dense, strongly structured natric (sodium-rich) B horizon. They are noted in Mollison's *[[gypsum]] (calcium sulfate) application is a standard reclamation strategy — calcium displaces sodium on clay particles, improving structure and permeability.
- Solonetz soils often occur in semi-arid to arid regions and are associated with desertification.
- They can be spread over alkaline clay pans with 0.5 m or more of neutral or acidic sand to create a garden or tree site that first infiltrates and then holds rainwater.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Water harvesting challenge**: Solonetz soils are effectively impervious, making them both a curse (for crop growth) and a potential asset (as natural catchment surfaces for runoff harvesting into adjacent permeable areas).
- **Reclamation**: Deep ripping combined with gypsum incorporation can fracture the natric layer. Swale construction over ripped gypsum-treated solonetz allows water infiltration.
- **Fungal limitations**: The high pH and sodium toxicity of untreated solonetz severely limits fungal activity. Reclamation through gypsum and organic matter addition is a prerequisite for establishing [[permaculture-design]], solonetz areas are best managed as water harvesting catchments rather than cropped areas, directing runoff to adjacent improved soils.
- **Bioremediation potential**: Salt-tolerant plants (halophytes) can be used as pioneer species to begin organic matter accumulation on solonetz sites.

## Design Strategies

Mollison recommends several approaches for solonetz landscapes:
- Ripping swale bases and applying gypsum in clay soils to aid infiltration
- Covering alkaline clay pans with 0.5 m+ of neutral or acidic sand for garden/tree sites
- Using solonetz areas as high-runoff catchments to feed swales and dams on better soils
- Establishing salt-tolerant tree species as pioneer vegetation
