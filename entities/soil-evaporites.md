## Description

Evaporites are minerals formed by the evaporation of saline water bodies, including ancient seas, salt lakes, and desert saltpans. In the context of soil science and permaculture, evaporites represent both a natural source of mineral nutrients and a challenge in arid land management where salt accumulation degrades soils. The sequential crystallisation of evaporites from seawater produces a spectrum of useful mineral amendments, from calcite and gypsum to rock salt, potash, and concentrated bitterns.

## Classification

- **Category**: Sedimentary mineral deposits
- **Formation process**: Sequential crystallisation from evaporating saline water
- **Common evaporite minerals**: Calcite, Dolomite, Gypsum, Anhydrite, Halite (rock salt), Potash salts, Bitterns (final liquid residue)
- **Occurrence**: Ancient seabed deposits, salt flats, playas, sabkhas, inland saltpans

## Key Facts

- As seawater evaporates, minerals precipitate in a predictable sequence based on solubility -- least soluble first (calcite, dolomite), most soluble last (potash, magnesium salts)
- Gypsum and anhydrite from evaporite sequences are widely used for soil conditioning, pH adjustment, and restoring soil crumb structure
- Rock salt (halite) is a common evaporite providing sodium chloride; desert salts typically contain 20-25% potash from associated evaporites
- Potash and magnesium salts are among the last evaporites to crystallise, concentrating in the final stages of evaporation
- Evaporites are the most easily dissolved minerals, making them the first to be leached from soils by rainfall and carried to the sea
- Major evaporite deposits exist in the Permian Basin (USA), Stassfurt (Germany), the Dead Sea region, and numerous ancient lake beds worldwide

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- Gypsum from evaporite deposits is used to remediate sodic soils, improving soil structure and drainage for crop production
- Potash from evaporite minerals is one of the three primary macronutrients (NPK) essential for all plant growth
- In arid regions, evaporite salt accumulation in soils creates salinity problems that can render land unproductive unless managed through leaching, drainage, and salt-tolerant plant selection
- Bitterns (the liquid residue after salt crystallisation) provide trace element supplements for leached soils when diluted and applied as foliar sprays
- Permaculture strategies in arid areas focus on infiltrating water into soil storages before it evaporates and leaves salt deposits at the surface
- Understanding evaporite sequences helps designers select appropriate mineral amendments -- gypsum for structure, dolomite for pH, rock dust for trace elements
- In mushroom substrate preparation, gypsum (an evaporite product) is commonly added at 1-5% to improve substrate structure, reduce greasing, and provide calcium
