## Overview

**Pinguicula moranensis** is a carnivorous perennial plant in the family Lentibulariaceae, commonly known as a butterwort. It is native to the mountainous regions of Mexico and Guatemala.

The species is among the most widely cultivated butterworts due to its attractive flowers and compact rosette form. It produces sticky glandular leaves that capture small flying insects.

P. moranensis undergoes a distinct seasonal heterophylly, producing different leaf types in summer and winter. This adaptation allows survival through both wet and dry seasons.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:** Plantae → Lamiales → Lentibulariaceae → Pinguicula → P. moranensis.

Authority: Kunth (1817). The epithet refers to the Morán mining region in Hidalgo, Mexico, where the species was first collected.

**Summer rosette:** The carnivorous summer form produces a flat rosette of succulent, ovate to elliptical leaves 4–10 cm in diameter. Leaf margins are slightly incurved.

**Winter rosette:** The winter non-carnivorous form produces a tight compact rosette of smaller, thick, succulent leaves. These protect the plant through the dry season.

**Glandular trichomes:** The upper [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-vegetation-types-mesophytic-cloud-forest]] zones. It frequently colonizes vertical or near-vertical substrates.

Habitats are characterized by high humidity and frequent moisture from cloud immersion or rainfall. Soils are typically thin, mineral-poor, and well-drained.

The species tolerates considerable temperature variation due to its montane distribution. Daytime temperatures may reach 25°C while nighttime temperatures regularly drop below 10°C.

## Trapping Mechanism

P. moranensis employs a flypaper trapping mechanism using glandular trichomes on the [[query-how-to-control-fungus-gnats-organically]], midges, and fruit flies are attracted to the glistening droplets and become trapped on contact. Struggling insects contact additional glands and become further immobilized.

The leaf margins slowly roll inward over 30 minutes to several hours, enveloping the prey. This thigmonastic response brings sessile digestive glands into direct contact with the captured organism.

The flowers attract pollinators including bees, butterflies, and hoverflies. The nectar guides on the lower lip direct pollinators toward the reproductive structures.

The species serves as a microhabitat for specialized invertebrates. Some small arthropods live on the leaf surface without being captured, feeding on the remains of trapped prey.

P. moranensis is an indicator species for humid montane forest conditions. Its presence signals consistent atmospheric moisture and minimal habitat disturbance.

## Related Species and Variation

P. moranensis is part of the P. moranensis species complex, which includes several closely related Mexican taxa. Taxonomic boundaries within this complex have been historically contentious.

The species exhibits considerable natural variation in flower color, leaf size, and rosette diameter across its range. Populations from higher elevations tend to produce more compact rosettes.
