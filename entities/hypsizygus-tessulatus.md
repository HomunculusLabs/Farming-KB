# Hypsizygus tessulatus

**Hypsizygus tessulatus**, commonly known as the elm oyster, buna-shimeji, or hon-shimeji, is a highly prized edible mushroom in the family Lyophyllaceae. Native to temperate hardwood forests ooyster-mushroomsAmerica, and East Asia, it has become a commercially important cultivated mushroom, particularly in Japan where it ranks among the top cultivated species by market value. Despite the common name "elm oyster," it is not closely related to true [[oyster-mushrooms]] (*Pleurotus* spp.) and belongs to a different family and order.

* **Harvest:** 14–21 days after pin initiation. Entire clusters are harvested by cutting at the base. Commercial yields range from 80–150% biological efficiency.

### Key Cultivation Challenges

* **Slow growth rate:** Requires longer production cycles than oyster mushrooms (60–90 days total vs. 30–45 days)
* **Strand formation:** Inadequate temperature control during primordia formation causes elongated, stringy mushrooms with poor market quality
* **Contamination sensitivity:** Higher supplementation levels (needed for good yield) increase vulnerability to *Trichoderma* and bacterial contamination
* **Genetic degeneration:** Repeated subculturing of commercial strains leads to yield decline; maintaining strain vitality through periodic spore isolation or cryopreservation is essential

## Nutritional and Medicinal Value

### Nutritional Profile

Buna-shimeji is nutritionally comparable to other premium cultivated mushrooms:

* 18–22% protein (dry weight) with all essential amino acids, particularly rich in glutamic acid (contributing to umami flavor)
* Low in calories and fat
* Rich in B vitamins: niacin (3.5 mg/100g fresh), riboflavin (0.4 mg/100g), pantothenic acid (1.5 mg/100g), thiamine (0.1 mg/100g), folate (30 μg/100g)
* Good mineral content: potassium (350 mg/100g), phosphorus (100 mg/100g), zinc (1 mg/100g)
* Dietary fiber: 2–3 g/100g fresh weight, including bioactive β-glucans
* Vitamin D2: modest content, increasing significantly with UV exposure post-harvest

### Bioactive Compounds and Health Effects

* **β-glucans:** *Hypsizygus* polysaccharides (designated HT-F2, HT-F3 fractions) demonstrate immunomodulatory activity, enhancing macrophage function and NK cell activity. Oral administration in mice enhanced tumor rejection in sarcoma-180 models.
* **Hypsiziprenol A₉:** A polyisoprenoid compound isolated from *H. marmoreus* with reported anti-inflammatory activity through suppression of COX-2 and iNOS expression in macrophages.
* **Ergothibacillus-subtiliscant content of this antioxidant amino acid (1–3 mg/100g fresh), comparable to oyster mushrooms.
* **Antimicrobial peptides:** Low-molecular-weight proteins with activity against Gram-positive bacteria, including *[[staphylococcus-aureus]]* and *[[bacillus-subtilis]]*.
* **Anti-obesity effects:** Animal studies suggest *H. marmoreus* extracts reduce visceral fat accumulation, improve lipid metabolism, and enhance insulin sensitivity, possibly through activation of AMPK pathway and modulation of adipokine secretion.

## Culinary Uses

Buna-shimeji is one of the most versatile culinary mushrooms:

* **Flavor:** Mild, sweet, nutty with pronounced umami character. The flavor intensifies and develops caramelized notes when cooked.
* **Texture:** Firm, crisp, and slightly crunchy when lightly cooked; becomes tender but retains some bite. Does not become slimy even with extended cooking.
* **Preparation:** The mushroom should always be cooked before eating (raw consumption causes gastrointestinal discomfort in some individuals). Best prepared by sautéing in butter or oil at medium-high heat until lightly browned, which develops sweetness and nutty aromas.
* **Applications:** Stir-fries, soups, risotto, pasta dishes, tempura, sukiyaki, nabemono (Japanese hot pot), and as a garnish for grilled meats and fish. The cluster form makes attractive plate presentations.
* **Storage:** Refrigerated at 2–4°C, fresh buna-shimeji keeps for 10–14 days. Freezing is possible but affects texture; blanched mushrooms freeze better than raw.
