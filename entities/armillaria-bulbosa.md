# Armillaria bulbosa

## Overview

armillaria bulbosa is a species of Basidiomycota fungus in the family Physalacriaceae, order Agaricales. It is one of the most ecologically significant root-rot pathogens of woody plants worldwide and has gained considerable fame as one of the largest and oldest [[armillaria-ostoyae]] species are capable of significant [[armillaria-ostoyae]] can cover enormous areas. The Michigan individual described by Smith et al. (1992) occupied approximately 15 hectares, while an individual of A. ostoyae in Oregon was reported to cover over 900 hectares, making it potentially the largest organism on Earth by area.

The mating system of armillaria bulbosa is bipolar (unifactorial), meaning that compatibility between mating types is controlled by a single genetic locus. This mating system influences the genetic diversity of populations and the potential for adaptation to different host species and environmental conditions.

## Economic and Practical Significance

armillaria bulbosa is one of the most economically damaging forest pathogens worldwide. It causes significant losses in timber production, orchard systems, and ornamental plantings. Economic impacts include:

- Increased tree mortality requiring costly salvage operations
