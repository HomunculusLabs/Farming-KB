## Description

**Michel and Jude Fanton** are permaculture practitioners based in **Nimbin, NSW,
Australia**, recognised by Mollison for their innovative use of **lemongrass
planted on contour** to hold steep slope soils, provide mulch, and create
upslope planting sites. The Fantons are also widely known as founders of **The
Seed Savers' Network** in Australia, dedicated to preserving heirloom and
locally adapted plant varieties.

## Key Facts

- Based in the **Nimbin** area of the Northern Rivers region of NSW — a
  subtropical high-rainfall zone with steep terrain.
- Used **lemongrass** (*[[vetiver-grass]]** is used similarly in Africa wherever slope stability is
  needed — the Fanton system parallels this African technique.
- Lemongrass also provides culinary and medicinal products (beverages, cooking,
  essential oils).

## Relevance to Cultivation / Mycology

- Contour-planted grass barriers create **terraced microsites** with improved
  moisture retention — ideal locations for outdoor **mushroom beds** or log
  cultivation sites on steep land.
- Lemongrass root masses and accumulated mulch provide **organic matter** that
  supports decomposer fungal communities on slopes.
- The retained moisture upslope of contour plantings creates **fungal-friendly
  microclimates** in what would otherwise be fast-draining hillside soils.
- Harvested lemongrass biomass can be **composted or used directly as substrate
  supplement** for [[mushroom-cultivation]], particularly for species that tolerate
  grass-based substrates.
- The Northern Rivers region's warm, wet climate is excellent for **tropical
  and subtropical mushroom cultivation** including *Pleurotus*, *Ganoderma*,
  and *Lentinula* species.
