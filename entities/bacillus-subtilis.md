# Bacillus subtilis

*Bacillus subtilis* is a Gram-positive, rod-shaped bacterium and one of the
most extensively studied microorganisms in biology. Known commonly as the hay
bacillus or grass bacillus, it serves as the premier model organism for
Gram-positive bacteria and for research on sporulation, competence, biofilm
formation, and cell differentiation. Its ubiquity in soil, combined with its
GRAS (Generally Recognized As Safe) status, has made it a cornerstone of
industrial microbiology, agriculture, and probiotic medicine.

## Discovery and Nomenclature

First described by Christian Gottfried Ehrenberg in 1835 as *Vibrio subtilis*
after observation in hay infusions. In 1872, Ferdinand Cohn reclassified it
into the genus *Bacillus* and established it as the type species. The epithet
"subtilis" is Latin for "thin" or "fine," referring to the slender rod-shaped
cells. Cohn demonstrated endospore formation surviving boiling temperatures,
providing crucial evidence against spontaneous generation. The phylum was
reclassified from Firmicutes to Bacillota in 2021.

## Morphology and Cell Structure

*B. subtilis* is a Gram-positive rod, 2–6 μm long and 0.7–0.8 μm in
diameter, forming chains during active growth. The cell wall contains thick
peptidoglycan with teichoic acids. Motile via peritrichous flagella. Under
nutrient deprivation, it undergoes sporulation forming a highly resistant
endospore containing dipicolinic acid (~10% dry weight), surrounded by
proteinaceous cortex and coat layers conferring extreme resistance to heat,
radiation, desiccation, and chemical disinfectants. A mucoid capsule of
poly-γ-glutamic acid may develop under certain conditions.

## Genome

The reference strain 168 has a 4.2 Mbp genome (4,214,810 bp) with ~4,100
protein-coding genes and 43.5% GC content. Sequenced in 1997, it was among
the first Gram-positive genomes completed. Encodes genes for central
metabolism, sporulation (100+ *spo* genes), natural competence (*com* genes),
chemotaxis, biofilm formation, and motility. Key regulators: Spo0A
(sporulation phosphorelay), ComK (competence), SinR (biofilm repression),
CodY (nutrient response). Natural competence makes it highly amenable to
genetic manipulation.

## Metabolism and Physiology

Primarily an obligate aerobe but grows anaerobically via nitrate respiration
or mixed-acid fermentation. Optimal growth: 30–37°C, pH 6.5–7.5, with
tolerance from pH 4.5–9.0 and 5–50°C. Catalase- and oxidase-positive.
Utilizes diverse carbon sources including glucose, glycerol, starch, and amino
acids. Produces extracellular amylases, proteases, cellulases, lipases, and
xylanases. Colonies on agar are large, irregular, matte, becoming wrinkled
and leathery with sporulation.

## Ecological Role

Ubiquitous in soil, decaying organic matter, and the rhizosphere. Promotes
plant growth directly through IAA production, phosphate solubilization, and
siderophore-mediated iron acquisition. Indirect mechanisms include pathogen
biocontrol via antibiosis and induced systemic resistance (ISR). Also found
in marine environments, the phyllosphere, and animal gastrointestinal tracts.
Endospore formation allows persistence through hostile conditions and rapid
recolonization when conditions improve.

## Industrial Applications

Holding FDA GRAS status, *B. subtilis* is a major industrial microorganism.
Large-scale enzyme production includes amylases, proteases (detergents),
lipases, cellulases, and xylanases. Excellent heterologous protein expression
host due to high secretion capacity and lack of endotoxins (unlike *E. coli*).
Engineered for vitamins (riboflavin, B12), amino acids (L-lysine), and
specialty chemicals. Premier model organism for Gram-positive cell biology.

## Agricultural Applications

Widely used as biofertilizer and biopesticide. Strain QST 713 (Serenade® by
Bayer) suppresses pathogens (*Botrytis*, [[biodiversity-powdery-mildews]]) via lipopeptide
antibiotics (iturin, surfactin, fengycin), competitive exclusion, and ISR.
Solubilizes insoluble phosphate and produces IAA for root growth stimulation.
Biofertilizer formulations used globally for cereals, vegetables, fruits, and
ornamentals, often combined with [[mycorrhizal-fungi]] for synergistic effects.

## Medical and Pharmaceutical Significance

Produces bacitracin (topical antibiotic against Gram-positives, in triple
antibiotic ointment), subtilin (lantibiotic against *C. difficile*), bacilysin
(broad-spectrum), and antifungal lipopeptides (mycosubtilin, fengycin). Active
ingredient in Enterogermina® (Sanofi), a spore-based probiotic for treating
diarrhea. Spores survive gastric passage (pH 2.0), germinate intestinally,
and provide competitive exclusion of pathogens plus immune modulation.

## Sporulation Cycle

Nutrient starvation triggers a 7–8 hour irreversible program. Stage 0: Spo0A
activated via phosphorelay (KinA/B → Spo0F → Spo0B → Spo0A~P). Stages I–II:
asymmetric division producing mother cell and forespore, regulated by σF/σE.
Stage III: forespore engulfment. Stages IV–V: cortex and coat formation.
Stages VI–VII: DPA accumulation, SASP DNA protection, mother cell lysis.
Spores survive decades, withstanding 100°C, radiation, desiccation, and
disinfectants. Germination triggered within minutes by L-alanine or AGFK.

## Food Fermentation

*B. subtilis* var. *natto* produces natto, a Japanese fermented soybean food
over 1,000 years old. Fermentation yields sticky poly-γ-glutamic acid,
vitamin K2 (menaquinone-7), nattokinase (fibrinolytic enzyme with
cardiovascular benefits), and bioactive peptides. Related Asian fermentations
include thua nao (Thailand), kinema (Nepal), and chungkookjang (Korea) —
independent domestications of *B. subtilis* across diverse cultures.

## Key Strains

- **Strain 168:** Reference lab strain; trpC2 auxotroph. Genome sequenced
  1997.
- **Strain NCIB 3610:** Wild-type ancestral strain with natural competence,
  robust biofilm, swarming motility, and surfactin production.
- **Strain QST 713:** Commercial biocontrol strain (Serenade®).
- **Strain FZB42:** PGPR with sequenced genome; produces bacillomycin D,
  fengycin, and bacillaene.
- ***B. subtilis* var. *natto*:** Natto strains producing γ-PGA, nattokinase,
  and vitamin K2.
