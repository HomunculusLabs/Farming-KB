## N-P-K Fertilizer Rating System

## Description

The **N-P-K rating system** is the standard labeling convention for fertilizers worldwide, displaying three numbers that represent the guaranteed percentages of nitrogen (N), phosphorus (as P₂O₅), and potassium (as K₂O) by weight in the fertilizer product. This trilogy appears on every commercial fertilizer package and is mandated by law in most jurisdictions. The system was developed to give growers a quick reference for comparing fertilizer formulations, though the numbers do not directly represent the elemental percentages of phosphorus and potassium.

## Classification

- **Category:** Agricultural standard / Fertilizer labeling convention
- **Components:** Nitrogen (N), Phosphorus pentoxide (P₂O₅), Potassium oxide (K₂O)
- **Domain:** Agronomy, horticulture, soil science, agricultural regulation

## Key Facts

- The three numbers represent percentages by weight: e.g., 10-3-1 means 10% N, 3% P₂O₅, 1% K₂O.
- **The phosphorus number is NOT elemental phosphorus** — it represents phosphorus pentoxide (P₂O₅). To get actual phosphorus content, multiply by **0.44**.
- **The potassium number is NOT elemental potassium** — it represents potassium oxide (K₂O). To get actual potassium content, multiply by **0.83**.
- This discrepancy is a historical artifact of how chemists originally measured these elements.
- Laws require N-P-K disclosure on fertilizer labels, but fillers and other ingredients often do not need to be identified (varies by jurisdiction).
- In Australia, sulfur (S) is also required on labels alongside N-P-K.
- Compost and manures are technically **soil amendments**, not fertilizers, because their N-P-K values vary from batch to batch and are not guaranteed.

## Natural vs. Synthetic N-P-K Values

- Synthetic fertilizers may contain up to **60% nitrogen**, whereas most organic/natural fertilizers max out around **12% nitrogen**.
- Natural fertilizers compensate with broader nutrient profiles (micronutrients, trace elements) and organic matter that feeds the soil food web.
- The N-P-K system can be misleading for organic gardeners because it ignores the biological value of organic matter, microbial habitat, and slow-release nutrient cycling.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- Understanding the N-P-K system is essential for interpreting soil test recommendations and selecting appropriate organic amendments.
- High-N-P-K synthetic fertilizers can suppress mycorrhizal fungi formation and nitrogen-fixing bacteria symbiosis when nitrogen and phosphorus levels are too high.
- Organic fertilizers with moderate N-P-K values support a diverse soil food web, including mycorrhizal fungi, which are the largest single source of carbon in soils.
- The narrow focus on N-P-K has historically led to over-application of these three macronutrients at the expense of the other eleven essential mineral nutrients, degrading soil biology over time.
- Mushroom substrates have their own nutritional profiles that parallel N-P-K concepts — carbon-to-nitrogen ratio (C:N) is often more relevant in fungal cultivation than N-P-K.
