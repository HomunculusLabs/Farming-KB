# Six Worlds of Rebirth

## Description

The Six Worlds (Sanskrit: Sad-gati; also called Six Realms of Existence) are the six possible modes of existence in Buddhist cosmology into which consciousness can be reborn. In *The Psychedelic Experience*, Leary interprets these as six personality types or levels of re-entry from a psychedelic session — the type of ego one reconstructs after ego-dissolution. Two are higher than normal human existence, three are lower. Each world has characteristic colors, symbols, and forms of psychological bondage.

## Classification

- **Tradition**: Tibetan Buddhism
- **Category**: Buddhist Cosmology / Personality Typology
- **Related Concepts**: Karma, Sangsara, Bardo Thodol

## Key Facts

### The Six Worlds (Highest to Lowest)

1. **Devas** (Saints / Sages / Divine Teachers)
   - **Light**: White
   - **Symbols**: Delightful temples, jewelled mansions
   - **Bondage**: Ever-recurring round of pleasure, free game ecstasy
   - **Examples**: Gautama Buddha, Lao Tse, Christ
   - **Character**: Most enlightened people walking the earth

2. **Asuras** (Titans / Heroes)
   - **Light**: Green
   - **Symbols**: Magical forests, fire images
   - **Bondage**: Incessant heroic warfare
   - **Character**: More than human degree of power and vision

3. **Humans** (Normal human beings)
   - **Light**: Yellow
   - **Bondage**: Inertia, smug ignorance, physical/psychological handicaps
   - **Character**: Struggling through game-networks, occasionally breaking free

4. **Animals** (Primitive incarnations)
   - **Light**: Blue
   - **Symbols**: Caves, deep holes in the earth
   - **Bondage**: Helplessness and slavery
   - **Animal symbols**: Dog/cock (hypersexuality + jealousy), Pig (lustful stupidity), Ant (industrous hoarding), Worm (grovelling disposition), Snake (anger), Ape (rampaging primitive power), Wolf of the steppes, Bird (soaring freedom)

5. **Pretas** (Neurotics / Unsatisfied Spirits)
   - **Light**: Red
   - **Symbols**: Desolate plains, forest wastes
   - **Bondage**: Torments of unsatisfied needs and wants
   - **Character**: Frustrated, lifeless spirits forever pursuing unsatisfied desires

6. **Hell / Psychosis**
   - **Light**: Smoke-colored
   - **Symbols**: Sounds of wailing, gloomy lands, black and white houses, black roads
   - **Bondage**: Self-created torment
   - **Character**: The lowest possible re-entry

### Key Principles

- Less than 1% of ego-transcendent experiences end in sainthood or psychosis; most return to the normal human level
- The level one is destined for is determined by karma (psychological momentum from past actions)
- Premonitory signs and visions appear during the Third Bardo, with the destined level appearing most clearly
- The chances of favorable re-entry increase if the process is allowed to take its natural course without effort or struggle
- One should follow signs of the three higher types and shun those of the three lower
- Complete impartiality during re-entry ensures the wisest choice, since appearances can be deceptive

### Re-Entry Navigation

- Use foresight to choose a good post-session "robot" (ego structure)
- Do not be attracted to the old ego
- Choose impartially, without attraction or repulsion
- Enter game existence with good grace, voluntarily and freely
- Visualize re-entry as entering a celestial mansion — an opportunity for game-ecstasy

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- The Six Worlds framework provides a typology for understanding the range of post-psilocybin integration outcomes, from transformative mystical insight to worsening of pathological patterns
- The animal symbolism (dog, cock, pig, snake, ape) maps onto recognizable post-session behaviors: compulsive sexuality, greed, anger, chaotic impulsivity
- The instruction to "choose impartially" during re-entry parallels modern integration practices where patients are encouraged to reflect on their session without grasping or aversion
- The observation that "less than 1%" end in either sainthood or psychosis is consistent with modern research showing that psilocybin rarely produces either full mystical transformation or lasting psychosis in screened populations
- The concept that each world has characteristic "bondage" helps therapists identify which patterns a patient is likely to fall back into after a session
