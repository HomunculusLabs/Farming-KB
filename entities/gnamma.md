# Gnamma (Rockhole)

## Description

Gnammas (also called rockholes) are natural rock basins that collect and retain water, found extensively in arid and semi-arid Australia and other dryland regions. They form through several geological processes and serve as critical water sources for wildlife, Aboriginal peoples, and desert travelers. Mollison discusses them in *[[water-conservation]] in gnammas:
- **Pebble filling**: Fill the hole with pebbles to lower evaporation (some water is lost to pebble volume)
- **Thatch or cover**: Cover the hole to prevent direct evaporation
- **Gutter construction**: Build rock gutters directing additional surface water to the hole
- **Dew fencing**: Erect metal mesh fences to direct dew condensation into the hole
- **Deepening and sealing**: Deepen the basin and plaster with cement if it leaks through fractures

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Desert water harvesting**: Gnammas represent a model for passive, zero-energy water capture that can inform the design of small-scale water collection systems for [[mushroom-growing-environments]].
- **Natural design inspiration**: The geometry of gnammas — deep, narrow basins with reduced surface area — minimizes evaporation. This principle can be applied to water storage design for fungal cultivation.
- **Bioregional adaptation**: Understanding local gnamma formation processes helps designers identify sites where enhanced rock catchments can support mycological or agricultural projects.
