## Overview

Myosotis sylvatica, commonly known as wood forget-me-not, is a short-lived perennial or biennial herb in the family Boraginaceae. It is native to Europe and western Asia and is widely cultivated as an ornamental spring-flowering plant.

The species produces dense clusters of small, flat, five-petaled flowers in shades of blue, pink, or white with a distinctive yellow eye. It is valued for woodland gardens, borders, and naturalized plantings.

It naturalizes freely from self-sown seed, forming spreading colonies in moist, partially shaded sites. The common name "forget-me-not" derives from a [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]] worldwide. Myosotis sylvatica belongs to the M. sylvatica species group, which is primarily European in distribution.

A naturally occurring hybrid with Myosotis alpestris is sometimes reported in cultivation. Synonyms include Myosotis sylvatica var. caespitosa and Myosotis ruprechtii.

The species is closely related to Myosotis alpestris (alpine forget-me-not) and [[myosotis-scorpioides]] (true forget-me-not). These three species are often confused in the horticultural trade.

Chromosome number is 2n = 48 for Myosotis sylvatica, which helps distinguish it cytologically from the diploid Myosotis alpestris (2n = 24). Molecular phylogenetic studies place it firmly within the European clade of the genus.

## Morphology

Plants grow 15–40 cm tall with erect to ascending, branched stems covered in spreading hairs. The basal leaves are lanceolate to oblanceolate, forming a low rosette in the first year.

Stem leaves are smaller, sessile, and alternate, with soft pubescence on both surfaces. Leaf margins are entire, and the foliage is mid-green in color and softly hairy to the touch.

Flowers are 6–10 mm in diameter, typically bright blue with a yellow or white central eye. They are borne in scorpioid cymes that uncurl as they open, a characteristic inflorescence pattern of the Boraginaceae.

Each flower has five rounded petals, a five-lobed calyx with hooked hairs, and five stamens attached near the corolla tube base. The corolla tube is short, approximately 2–3 mm long.

The fruit is a small, shiny, dark brown to black nutlet approximately 1.5–2 mm long. Nutlets are produced in groups of four per flower and can persist on the plant for several weeks.

The root system is fibrous and relatively shallow, typical of short-lived perennials. Plants form a compact mound 15–25 cm wide when in full bloom.

Flower color in wild populations is predominantly blue, but cultivated strains exhibit white, pink, and pale lavender variants. The blue pigment is delphinidin-based anthocyanin, common across Boraginaceae.

## Distribution and Habitat

Myosotis sylvatica is native to most of Europe, from the British Isles and Iberian Peninsula eastward to the Caucasus and Turkey. It occurs in damp deciduous woodlands, forest margins, stream banks, and shaded meadows at elevations from sea level to 2,000 m.

The species prefers moist, humus-rich soils in partial shade but tolerates full sun in cooler climates. It thrives in neutral to slightly acidic soils with good drainage and consistent moisture.

It has become naturalized in parts [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-south-america]] through garden escape. In its native range, it is not considered threatened and is often locally abundant.

The species is classified as Least Concern on the IUCN Red List due to its wide distribution and stable populations. It is common throughout central and western Europe, becoming less frequent at the northern and southern extremes of its range.

In the Alps and Carpathians, populations occur up to 2,000 m elevation in subalpine meadows. These montane populations show slightly more [[fukuoka-ideal-rice-plant-morphology-compact-growth-high-yield]] and deeper flower coloration than lowland forms.

## Cultivation

Widely grown as a spring bedding plant, Myosotis sylvatica is typically treated as a biennial in cultivation. Sow seed in midsummer for flowering the following spring, or purchase young plants in autumn for overwintering.

Germination occurs in 14–21 days at temperatures of 15–20°C. Seeds require light for germination and should be surface-sown onto moist compost without covering.

Plants prefer consistently moist but not waterlogged soil in partial shade. They perform well under deciduous trees where they receive spring sun and summer shade.

Popular cultivar series include 'Victoria' (compact, large flowers), 'Blue Ball' (deep blue, globe-shaped habit), 'Rosea' (pink-flowered), and 'Snowball' (white-flowered). The 'Victoria' series is the most widely available in commercial trade.

Myosotis sylvatica self-seeds prolifically, and volunteer seedlings can be transplanted in late summer. Deadheading after flowering reduces self-seeding if naturalization is not desired.
