## Description

**Charles Frost** of Vermont is identified by Palmer as "a living authority" in the science of [[mycology]] at the time of writing. He is listed among the few English-language authorities on the [[neglected-science-of-mycology]] fungi in America.

## Biography

- **Residence:** Vermont
- **Expertise:** Mycology, specifically the study of fungi
- **Era:** Late 19th century (active during Palmer"s writing period)
- **Status:** Described as a "living authority" — still actively practicing at time of publication

## Context Among American Mycologists

Palmer names Charles Frost as one of a very small number of American authorities on mycology, alongside:

- **Dr. Curtis** of South Carolina
- **Mr. Ravenal** of South Carolina
- **Mr. [[charles-james-sprague]]** of Boston

Palmer notes that "with an occasional exception, American literature shows the ingenuity of professional writers in weaving the web of other men"s thoughts and researches on this subject into diverse forms for the magazines" — suggesting Frost was among the rare genuine original researchers rather than a compiler of others" work.

## Contributions

While specific published works by Frost are not detailed in Palmer's text, his inclusion among the named authorities implies:

- Original field research and observation of fungi in Vermont and New England
- Expertise in species identification and classification
- Recognition by peers as a reliable authority on [[classification-of-edible-and-poisonous-mushroom-families]] fungi

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Charles Frost represents the small community of self-taught or independently motivated American mycologists who advanced the science through dedicated personal study during a period when formal institutional support for mycology was minimal in the [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]]. His recognition as a "living authority" by Palmer suggests his contributions were substantial enough to warrant citation despite the absence of major American institutional publications on edible fungi.
