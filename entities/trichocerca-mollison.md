# Trichocerca

## Description

Trichocerca is a genus of rotifers (wheel animals) commonly found in freshwater habitats worldwide. Mollison identifies Trichocerca as one of the zooplankton species that thrive in sewage treatment lagoons, where they feed on the rich algal populations and contribute to the aquatic food web that processes wastewater.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Animalia
- **Phylum**: Rotifera
- **Class**: Monogononta
- **Order**: Ploima
- **Family**: Trichocercidae
- **Genus**: *Trichocerca*

## Key Facts

- Trichocerca species are loricate rotifers with a protective outer casing (lorica), often with distinctive spines or projections.
- They are typically 100-500 um in length, making them visible under low-power microscopy.
- Like all rotifers, they use a crown of cilia (corona) to create water currents that draw in food particles, primarily algae, bacteria, and detritus.
- Trichocerca species are found in freshwater ponds, lakes, marshes, and sewage treatment lagoons globally.
- They reproduce primarily through parthenogenesis, with populations capable of rapid increase when food is abundant.
- Rotifers are among the most important primary consumers in freshwater ecosystems, bridging the microbial and macroscopic food webs.

## Role in Wastewater Treatment

Mollison lists Trichocerca among the zooplankton thriving in sewage lagoons with rich algal food supplies:

1. **Algal grazing**: Consumes phytoplankton including Euglena, Chlamydomonas, and Chlorella, controlling algal bloom densities.
2. **Nutrient transfer**: Converts algal biomass into zooplankton biomass, concentrating nutrients in larger particles.
3. **Food web support**: Serves as prey for larger zooplankton (copepods), insect larvae, and small fish.
4. **Water clarification**: Grazing activity reduces turbidity caused by dense algal populations.
5. **Metal concentration**: Zooplankton accumulate heavy metals from their food, potentially concentrating metals for removal through harvesting.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Aquaculture systems**: Rotifers including Trichocerca are essential food for fish larvae in aquaculture, directly relevant to integrated permaculture fish farming.
- **Biological water treatment**: Rotifer grazing on bacteria and algae enhances the efficiency of constructed wetlands and treatment ponds.
- **Compost tea**: Zooplankton in compost tea systems may contribute to biological processing of organic matter.
- **Ecosystem health indicator**: Rotifer diversity and abundance are widely used bioindicators for freshwater ecosystem health and water quality.

## Ecological Notes

Trichocerca and other rotifers can produce resting eggs (ephippia) that survive drying and freezing, allowing them to colonize new water bodies rapidly. This resilience makes them reliable components of treatment pond ecosystems even after seasonal disruptions. Their short generation time (1-3 days) allows rapid population responses to changes in food availability.
