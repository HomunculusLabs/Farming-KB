## Description

Xochipilli is the Aztec god of flowers, dance, and song, also known as the "Prince of Flowers." A statue of Xochipilli was unearthed in Tlalmanalco on the slopes of the volcano Popocatepetl and is now on display in the Museo Nacional in Mexico City. The statue is of particular ethnobotanical significance because its surface bears stylized glyphs that have been interpreted as representations of known hallucinogenic plants.

## Key Facts

- Aztec god of flowers, dance, song, and possibly games and beauty
- Statue unearthed at Tlalmanalco on the slopes of Popocatepetl
- Surface features stylized botanical glyphs representing hallucinogenic plants
- Labels have been attached indicating probable botanical interpretations of the glyphs
- Represents the central role of hallucinogenic plants in Aztec religious and cultural life
- Demonstrates that the Aztecs codified their knowledge of psychoactive plants in sacred art

## Ethnobotanical Significance

The statue of Xochipilli is one of the most important artifacts in the study of Mesoamerican hallucinogen use:

- The carved glyphs have been identified as probable representations of specific hallucinogenic species
- Plants depicted likely include psychoactive species used in Aztec ceremonies
- The statue demonstrates the formalization and deification of hallucinogenic plant knowledge
- Shows that hallucinogens were not merely used but were objects of worship
- The artifact predates and confirms Spanish colonial descriptions of Aztec psychoactive plant use
