## Belen (Iquitos)

## Description

Belen is an urban slum district of Iquitos, Peru, located on the Amazon River. With a population of approximately 12,000 people (as of the late 1960s), Belen served as the primary fieldwork site for anthropologist Marlene Dobkin de Rios during her groundbreaking study of ayahuasca healing in 1968-1969. The community is notable for its high concentration of both folk healers (ayahuasqueros) and patients seeking magical healing, making it a living laboratory for the study of plant hallucinogen use in therapeutic contexts.

## Classification

- **Type:** Urban slum district / neighborhood
- **City:** Iquitos, Loreto Region, Peru
- **Geographic features:** Located on the Amazon River; floods at least four months per year
- **Population:** ~12,000 (late 1960s)
- **Significance:** Major center for ayahuasca healing and folk medicine in the Peruvian Amazon

## Key Facts

- Belen serves as a receiving area for vast numbers of forest migrants who come to Iquitos seeking work and better education for their children
- Houses are built on balsa log supports or balsa rafts to rise with seasonal flood waters; residents use canoes during flood periods
- The community suffers from exceedingly high unemployment, malnutrition, family breakdown, prostitution, vandalism, and chronic illness
- Belen is reputed to have large numbers of both ayahuasqueros (healers) and patients
- Formal medical consultation in Iquitos is prohibitively expensive for most Belen residents — a single visit costs the equivalent of two days' food budget
- The city hospital has the reputation as a place where poor people go to die
- Poorly trained medical aides called sanitarios dispense penicillin and antibiotics for small fees
- Most residents suffer from magical illnesses — conditions attributed to witchcraft, envy, or spirit punishment
- The community lacks shared community tradition, composed of diverse forest migrants
- Occupational categories include rematistas (small-scale resellers of produce) and regatones (boat-owning entrepreneurs)

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Belen is of exceptional importance to entheogenic plant studies because:

1. **Ayahuasca hub:** Belen is one of the most documented urban centers of ayahuasca healing in the anthropological literature
2. **Living tradition:** The community demonstrates how traditional plant medicine persists and adapts in an urban, economically marginalized setting
3. **Magical illness framework:** Belen provides the primary ethnographic context for understanding how ayahuasca is used to diagnose and treat magical illnesses (susto, dano, pulsario, mal de ojo)
4. **Cultural change:** The community exemplifies the intersection of traditional plant medicine with Western pharmaceutical use in a rapidly changing social environment
5. **Fieldwork methodology:** Dobkin de Rios's innovative use of fortunetelling cards (naipes) to gather data on illness beliefs demonstrated new anthropological methods for studying hallucinogenic plant use
