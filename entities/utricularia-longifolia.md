## Overview

**Utricularia longifolia** is a large terrestrial and occasionally epiphytic bladderwort in the family Lentibulariaceae, native to Brazil. It produces showy violet to purple flowers that are among the largest in the genus, with corollas reaching 3–4 cm in length.

The species is highly valued in carnivorous plant horticulture for its robust growth and impressive floral display. Unlike many bladderworts that are inconspicuous, U. longifolia forms substantial rosettes of foliage and produces tall, multi-flowered scapes.

U. longifolia was among the earlier Utricularia species to enter cultivation and remains widely available in the specialty plant trade. Its horticultural popularity has led to the selection of several notable forms with varying flower colors and sizes.

The species name *longifolia* refers to the elongated, grass-like leaves that distinguish it from many other bladderworts with reduced or absent foliage. It is grouped with the tropical terrestrial and epiphytic species in section Orchidioides.

U. longifolia is not considered difficult to maintain in cultivation, making it an excellent introductory species for growers interested in expanding beyond the more common aquatic bladderworts.

The bladder traps of U. longifolia function identically to those of other Utricularia species. Each trap maintains negative internal pressure through active ion pumping, creating a vacuum that drives the suction capture mechanism.

When trigger hairs on the trapdoor are contacted by prey, the door buckles inward within milliseconds. Water and prey are swept into the trap interior, and the door seals shut within approximately 10–15 milliseconds.
