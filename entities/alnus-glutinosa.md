- **Root system:** Shallow, spreading lateral roots with prominent nitrogen-fixing root nodules; often develops adventitious roots in waterlogged conditions

### Aerial Parts

- **Bark:** Dark grey-brown, rough and fissured with age; young bark is smooth and greenish-brown
- **Twigs:** Purplish-brown to olive-green, slightly sticky, with prominent lenticels
- **Buds:** Purplish, stalked, 6–8 mm long; glutinous with sticky resin
- **Leaves:** Alternate, simple, broadly ovate to orbicular, 5–10 cm long, 4–8 cm wide; doubly serrate margin; 5–8 pairs of lateral veins; dark green and glossy above, paler below; sticky when young, becoming glabrous; leaf base rounded to slightly cordate; petiole 1–3 cm
- **Flowers:** Monoecious; catkins appear before leaves in February–March
  - Male catkins: Pendulous, 5–10 cm long, purplish-brown, in groups of 2–4
  - Female catkins: Erect, ovoid, 1–1.5 cm long, dark red to purple, developing into woody cones
- **Fruit:** Small woody cone-like strobile, 1.5–2.5 cm long, composed of persistent bract scales; each scale bears a small winged nutlet; cones persist through winter

## Distribution and Habitat

- **Native range:** Europe (from Ireland and northern Spain to the Urals), western Siberia, North Africa (Morocco, Tunisia), western Asia Minor
- **Introduced range:** Widely naturalized in [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-south-america]]
- **Altitude:** 0–1,500 m; most common in lowland and riparian zones
- **Habitat:** Riparian corridors, wet woodlands, marsh edges, floodplains; also colonizes disturbed sites, gravel bars, and nutrient-poor soils
- **Soil tolerance:** Extremely broad; thrives in waterlogged, poorly drained soils that kill most other tree species; also tolerates acidic, nutrient-poor, and compacted soils
- **Hardiness:** USDA Zones 3–7; tolerates winter temperatures to −35°C
- **Light:** Full sun to partial shade; shade-intolerant as seedling, becoming moderately shade-tolerant

## Nitrogen Fixation Symbiosis

### *Frankia alni* Association

- **Symbiont:** *Frankia alni* (actinomycete bacterium), forming actinorhizal root nodules
- **Nodule structure:** Coralloid, multi-lobed, 1–3 cm diameter, appearing on lateral roots; beige to reddish-brown when active
- **Nitrogen fixation rate:** 40–100 kg N/ha/year, depending on site conditions and tree density
- **Mechanism:** Oxygen-limited compartment within nodules protects nitrogenase; hemoglobin (similar to leghemoglobin in legumes) regulates oxygen diffusion
- **Seasonality:** Active during [[mycorrhizal-networks]], leaf [[root-exudates]]
- **Comparison to legumes:** Fixes less nitrogen per unit area than soybeans (~200 kg N/ha) but provides year-round fixation over a much longer lifespan and across a broader range of soil conditions
