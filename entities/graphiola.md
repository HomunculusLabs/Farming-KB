# Graphiola

## Description

*Graphiola* is a genus of smutlike fungi in the family Graphiolaceae (order
[[exobasidiales]], class Ustilaginomycetes, phylum [[basidiomycota]]) that are
pathogenic on palms (Arecaceae). The genus includes nine species and is the type
genus of the Graphiolaceae, a family that is exclusively tropical and
subtropical in distribution (Oberwinkler et al. 1982; Oberwinkler 1993).
*Graphiola* species cause false smut or leaf spot diseases on a variety of palm
hosts, with the most economically significant being date palm false smut caused
by *G. phoenicis*. The genus illustrates the convergent evolution of smutlike
morphology across multiple basidiomycete lineages.

## Classification

- **[[kingdom-fungi]]
- **Phylum:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Ustilaginomycetes
- **Order:** Exobasidiales
- **Family:** Graphiolaceae
- **Genus:** *Graphiola*

## Key Facts

- **Species Diversity:** The genus includes nine described species, all
  parasitic on palms. The Graphiolaceae is one of four families in the
  Exobasidiales, alongside Exobasidiaceae, Brachybasidiaceae, and
  Cryptobasidiaceae (Bauer et al. 2001).

- **Host Plants:** All *Graphiola* species occur on palm hosts (Arecaceae).
  Species on date palms (*Phoenix dactylifera*) are restricted mainly to
  coastal areas. Species found on cultivated host species occur wherever the
  palms are grown under humid conditions, extending beyond the native range of
  their palm hosts.

- **Symptoms and Signs:** Early stages appear on palm leaves as dark, 1-3 mm
  diameter, subepidermal pustules surrounded by pale yellow halos. The pustules
  rupture the epidermis as the fungus matures. Mature fruiting bodies often are
  opposed to each other on abaxial (lower) and adaxial (upper) sides of leaves,
  a distinctive diagnostic character.

- **Fruiting Structure:** A mature pustule is composed of a hard, dark outer
  peridium and an inner, thin hyaline peridium that surrounds masses of smooth,
  yellow, thick-walled spores (Cook 1978). The spores are released when the
  peridium ruptures, and are dispersed by wind and rain.

- **Disease Impact:** *Graphiola* species cause premature shedding of leaves by
  date palms. In severe infections, premature defoliation can reduce fruit yield
  and weaken the tree. The disease is particularly problematic in humid
  date-growing regions, especially in coastal areas.

- **Taxonomic Placement:** The Graphiolaceae belongs within the Exobasidiales,
  an order that molecular phylogenetic analyses have shown to be closely related
  to *Tilletia* and allied taxa (Bauer et al. 2001). This placement reflects the
  modern understanding that traditional morphological classification of smut
  fungi does not always reflect evolutionary relationships.

- **Geographic Distribution:** Exclusively tropical and subtropical,
  distributed in the natural range of palm hosts. Species on cultivated palms
  occur wherever palms are grown under humid conditions.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Date Palm Production:** *Graphiola* leaf spot is a significant disease of
  date palms in humid growing regions. Management involves cultural practices to
  reduce humidity around the canopy and, where warranted, fungicide
  applications. The disease constrains date production in coastal and irrigated
  areas with high humidity.

- **Smut Fungi Phylogeny:** The placement of *Graphiola* within the
  Exobasidiales rather than with the true smuts (Ustilaginales) illustrates the
  convergent evolution of smutlike morphology across multiple basidiomycete
  lineages. Molecular phylogenetics has been essential for resolving these
  relationships.

- **Tropical Mycology:** As an exclusively tropical/subtropical group,
  *Graphiola* exemplifies the many fungal lineages poorly known from tropical
  regions. The Graphiolaceae warrant further study, particularly regarding
  species diversity on wild palm hosts in tropical forests.

- **Palm Pathology:** Palms are hosts to a diverse assemblage of fungal
  pathogens. *Graphiola* represents one component of this pathogen complex,
  alongside other important diseases such as Fusarium wilt, bud rot, and lethal
  yellowing caused by diverse fungal and bacterial agents.
