## Description

**Atropia** ([[atropine]]) is a toxic alkaloid derived from plants of the nightshade family (Solanaceae) that Palmer identifies as the only known treatment for [[amanita]] mushroom poisoning. In the text, it is mentioned as an experimental antidotal measure delivered via hypodermic injection, described as being "yet in its infancy" at the time of writing.

## Classification

- **Chemical class:** Tropane alkaloid
- **Source plants:** [[atropa-belladonna]] (deadly nightshade), Datura species, and other Solanaceae
- **Pharmacological category:** Anticholinergic / antimuscarinic

## Historical Use as Antidote

Palmer references atropia in the context of treating Amanita poisoning:

> "Excepting the hypodermic injection of atropia, or some other alkaloid from the nightshade family (an antidotal measure yet in its infancy), physicians are absolutely powerless to save life, however prompt and energetic the treatment."

### Context of Amanita Poisoning

- Amanita species (particularly *[[amanita-phalloides]]* and *[[amanita-virosa]]*) contain amatoxins that cause fatal liver and kidney failure
- The poisoning is described as surer than arsenic in its fatal consequences
- No antidote had been discovered at the time of Palmer's writing
- Atropia was the only treatment even tentatively suggested

## Pharmacological Considerations

The use of atropia against Amanita poisoning reflects 19th-century medical practice, though modern toxicology does not support atropine as an effective treatment for amatoxin poisoning. The connection between the "nightshade family" alkaloids and mushroom toxin treatment may have stemmed from the broader 19th-century pharmacological principle of using alkaloids against alkaloids, or from observations of symptom overlap between anticholinergic and cholinergic poisoning syndromes.

## The Nightshade Connection

Palmer specifically notes that atropia comes from "the nightshade family," drawing a botanical-pharmacological link between two groups of organisms notorious for containing lethal alkaloids — poisonous mushrooms and deadly nightshade plants.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Atropia's mention highlights the historical desperation surrounding Amanita poisoning treatment and underscores the critical importance of prevention through proper identification rather than reliance on post-exposure treatment. The fact that 19th-century physicians were "absolutely powerless" without even this questionable treatment reinforces Palmer's central argument that learning to identify individual species is the only reliable approach to safe mushroom foraging.
