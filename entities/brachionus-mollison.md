# Brachionus

## Description

Brachionus is a genus of rotifer zooplankton that plays a critical role in the biological treatment of wastewater and sewage lagoons. These microscopic wheel-animalcules are among the most common and abundant zooplankton found in facultative and aerobic sewage treatment ponds, where they serve as primary consumers of phytoplankton (algae) and bacteria.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Animalia
- **Phylum**: Rotifera
- **Class**: Monogononta
- **Order**: Ploima
- **Family**: Brachionidae
- **Genus**: Brachionus

## Key Facts

- Brachionus species are filter-feeding rotifers that consume bacteria, phytoplankton, and detritus suspended in water columns.
- In sewage treatment lagoons, they appear in the facultative and aerobic pond stages, thriving on the rich algal food supply produced by phytoplankton such as Euglena, Chlamydomonas, Chlorella, and Scenedesmus.
- Along with other zooplankton genera (Trichocerca, Filinia, Hexarthra), Brachionus helps convert algal biomass into animal protein, effectively concentrating nutrients and trace metals from the water column.
- Zooplankton including Brachionus accumulate heavy metals in their tissues: approximately 1,200 mg/kg iron, 152 mg/kg zinc, 37 mg/kg copper, 28 mg/kg chromium, 12.2 mg/kg nickel, 103 mg/kg lead, and 1.7 mg/kg cadmium (dry weight).
- Bloom densities of zooplankton can reach 100 mg/l in productive sewage lagoons.
- When harvested, Brachionus and other zooplankton can be added to poultry feedstuffs as a high-protein supplement, or pumped into forests and fields as manure and trace element amendments.
- They serve as a food source for fish, waterfowl, and larger invertebrates in subsequent aerobic pond systems.

## Role in Sewage Treatment

The sewage treatment process described by Mollison follows a three-stage lagoon system:

1. **Anaerobic ponds**: Methane-producing digesters where sludge breaks down into biogas.
2. **Facultative ponds**: Where Brachionus and other zooplankton begin to thrive alongside algal blooms.
3. **Aerobic ponds**: Where zooplankton populations peak, consuming algae and bacteria, further cleaning the water.

Brachionus contributes to the reduction of biological oxygen demand (BOD) by consuming organic particles and converting them into animal biomass.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Nutrient cycling**: Brachionus plays a role in converting waste nutrients into harvestable biomass, relevant to integrated aquaculture and permaculture systems.
- **Water quality**: Their presence indicates improving water quality in treatment systems, as they are sensitive to toxic pollutants.
- **Feed source**: Harvested zooplankton containing Brachionus can supplement animal feed, closing nutrient loops in sustainable agriculture.
- **Integrated systems**: In permaculture designs, sewage treatment lagoons incorporating natural zooplankton populations can simultaneously produce clean water, biogas, and protein-rich feed.

## Ecological Notes

Brachionus populations are self-regulating, as they consume their own algal food supply. This natural population control prevents overgrowth while maintaining continuous water purification. Their sensitivity to biocides and low pH makes them useful biological indicators of water quality.
