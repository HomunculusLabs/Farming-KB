## Description

The antibiotic sandwich technique is a culture purification method used to rescue mushroom [[mycelium]] from bacterial contamination. By sandwiching contaminated mycelium between layers of antibiotic-supplemented agar, the technique forces mycelium to drill through the antibiotic-laced medium while bacteria are unable to follow, resulting in purified mycelium emerging on the outside of the agar layers. It is particularly effective against "hitchhiking bacteria" that fungi naturally cultivate on their hyphae.

## Classification

- **Category**: Laboratory technique / Culture rescue
- **Purpose**: Purifying bacteria-contaminated mycelial cultures
- **Equipment**: Antibiotic agar plates, [[laminar-flow-hood]], sterile scalpel, alcohol lamp
- **Difficulty**: Intermediate; requires familiarity with aseptic technique and antibiotic agar preparation

## Key Facts

- Specifically designed to address "hitchhiking bacteria" that fungi intentionally harbor on their hyphae for metabolic collaboration
- In natural soil or wood ecosystems, fungi and bacteria collaborate, and fungi "collect" favorable strains of bacteria to help metabolize their waste by-products, like a septic tank
- The technique exploits the ability of [[fungal-hyphae]] to grow through solid agar, while bacteria cannot penetrate the antibiotic-laced medium
- Uses the Blanchette antibiotic agar formula containing [[streptomycin-sulfate]] (antibacterial), Benlate (antifungal), and lactic acid (pH adjustment)
- After the mycelium emerges purified on the outside of the agar layers, it can be transferred to a fresh antibiotic-free plate
- The formula and technique are credited to Dr. Bob Blanchette from the University of Minnesota

## Procedure

1. Pour antibiotic-supplemented agar plates using the Blanchette formula (see below)
2. Place the contaminated mycelium on one side of the plate
3. Cut a large wedge of antibiotic agar from another area of the plate
4. Flip the agar wedge over on top of the contaminated culture, sandwiching it between two agar layers
5. Incubate the plate and monitor growth over several days
6. The mycelium will grow through the agar to reach the surface, but bacteria will be unable to penetrate the antibiotic-laced medium and will be left behind
7. Once purified mycelium "fuzzes up" on the outside of the agar layers, transfer it to a fresh antibiotic-free plate

## Antibiotic Agar Formula (Blanchette)

Base ingredients (autoclaved 20 minutes):
- 1,000 ml distilled/deionized water
- 15 grams malt extract
- 15 grams agar
- 2 grams yeast extract
- 0.06 gram Benlate (50% wettable powder; a fungicide)

Combine, autoclave 20 minutes, cool to 113-122 F (45-50 C), then add:
- 0.01 gram [[streptomycin]] sulfate
- 2 ml lactic acid

## When to Use

- When bacteria-contaminated mycelium cannot be easily replaced (rare or wild-collected specimens)
- When standard re-streaking or transfer techniques have failed to separate bacteria from mycelium
- When "hitchhiking bacteria" are physically adhering to hyphae and cannot be left behind by simple transfer
- As an alternative to discarding valuable but contaminated cultures

## Important Considerations

- Use for one or two screening transfers, then return cultures to antibiotic-free medium
- Never open contaminated plates near the laminar flow hood; perform transfers in a "still-air" glove box or separate area
- Bacteria are less of a contamination risk than molds for spreading in the lab; bacterial plates can be opened in the lab with lower risk
- Monitor purified cultures closely after transfer; repeat the sandwich technique if any bacteria persist

## Relevance to Cultivation

The antibiotic sandwich technique is a specialized rescue method for valuable cultures that have become contaminated with bacteria. It is particularly important when working with rare or wild-collected specimens that cannot be easily replaced. Rather than discarding months of work spent collecting and culturing a fungus, cultivators can use this technique to purify the mycelium and restore it to a usable state, preserving the unique genetic characteristics of the original isolate.

## Practical Applications

Antibiotic sandwich has several important applications in sustainable agriculture and ecological design. Practitioners integrate these methods into holistic management systems that enhance productivity while maintaining ecological balance.
