## Overview

**Polystichum munitum** (Kaulf.) C. Presl is an evergreen fern in the family
Dryopteridaceae, commonly known as the western sword fern. It is among the
most abundant and recognizable ferns of the Pacific Northwest coast of North
America.

The species forms large, arching clumps of leathery fronds up to 1.5 meters
long. Each frond bears numerous sharply toothed pinnae arranged in a herringbone
pattern, giving the plant its "sword" epithet.

Western sword fern is a dominant understory species in coniferous forests from
southeastern Alaska to central California. It plays a critical role in forest
ecology by providing year-round cover for wildlife and contributing to soil
development through persistent litter.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Polystichum munitum** was described by John Smith and placed in its current
combination by Carl Presl in 1836. The epithet *munitum* means "armed,"
referring to the spiny margins of the pinnae.

It belongs to the genus *Polystichum*, which contains over 260 species of
mostly terrestrial ferns distributed worldwide. The genus is characterized by
scaly rhizomes and fronds with the lowest pinnae reduced and often deflexed.

Fronds are pinnately compound, evergreen, and typically 50–150 cm long. The
stipe (stalk) is densely covered with brown scales, especially near the base.

Pinnae are lanceolate, 5–15 cm long, with sharply serrate margins and a
conspicuous basal auricle overlapping the rachis. Each pinna bears a small,
bristle-tipped tooth at its apex.

Sori are circular, arranged in two rows on the underside of each pinna, and
covered by a kidney-shaped indusium. Spores are produced from summer through
winter on mature fronds.

The rhizome is short-creeping to erect, forming a dense crown that supports
a tuft of fronds. Individual plants can live for over 100 years.

## Distribution and Habitat

Western sword fern ranges from southeastern Alaska along the Pacific coast to
Santa Barbara County, California. Inland populations occur in the Rocky
Mountains of Idaho and western Montana.

Its range extends only a short distance east of the Cascade Range crest. It is
most abundant in the hypermaritime to maritime [[mollison-climate-zones-permaculture-strategies]] with cool, wet
winters and mild, dry summers.

The species occupies a wide elevational gradient, from sea level to
approximately 1,800 meters. It is most luxuriant in low-elevation riparian
areas and old-growth coniferous forests.

Optimal habitats include moist, shaded coniferous and mixed forests dominated
by *[[pseudotsuga-menziesii]]*, *[[tsuga-heterophylla]]*, and *[[picea-sitchensis]]*. It
also thrives along streambanks, in seepage areas, and on shaded cliff faces.

Soil preference is for well-drained to moderately moist loams rich in organic
matter, with pH ranging from acidic to neutral. It tolerates seasonal flooding
but not prolonged waterlogging.

## Ecological Role

*Polystichum munitum* is a foundation species in Pacific Northwest forest
understories. Its dense frond canopy modifies microclimate by reducing light
intensity and maintaining higher humidity at ground level.
