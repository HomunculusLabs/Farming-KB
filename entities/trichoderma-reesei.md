# Trichoderma reesei

Trichoderma reesei is a filamentous ascomycete fungus renowned for its
exceptional cellulolytic [[mushroom-strain-improvement-and-breeding-methods]]
programs that have increased cellulase production by several orders of
magnitude over the wild-type strain, making industrial-scale enzyme
production economically feasible for diverse applications.

## Taxonomy and Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Euascomycetes (Sordariomycetes)
- **Order:** Hypocreales
- **Family:** Hypocreaceae
- **Genus:** Trichoderma
- **Species:** T. reesei

T. reesei is classified among the Euascomycetes and possesses hex-1,
the gene encoding the structural protein of [[fungi-in-the-environment-rice-blast-magnaporthe-grisea]], and Penicillium
chrysogenum. This contrasts with the large hexagonal Woronin bodies
produced by [[hex1-protein-woronin-body-crystal-structure-euascomycetes]]
morphology across the Euascomycetes.

Proteomic analysis of the T. reesei cell envelope has identified
numerous proteins associated with cell wall biosynthesis, protein
secretion pathways, surface adhesion mechanisms, and cell envelope
architecture, revealing the complexity of protein-carbohydrate
interactions at the [[staycare-fungal-treatment-of-textile-industry-wastewater]] (biostoning and
biopolishing), paper and pulp industry (enzymatic deinking), food and
feed industry (improving digestibility), and detergent industry
(cellulase additives).

### Cellulase System

T. reesei produces a complete cellulase system: cellobiohydrolases CBH
I and CBH II (approximately 80% of total cellulase protein)
processively hydrolyze cellulose chains from reducing and non-reducing
ends, endoglucanases randomly cleave internal beta-1,4 bonds, beta-
glucosidases hydrolyze cellobiose to glucose, and xylanases degrade
hemicellulose components.

The synergistic action of these enzymes enables efficient degradation
of crystalline cellulose, a substrate that is highly resistant to
enzymatic hydrolysis. This enzyme system is the foundation of the
multi-billion dollar industrial enzyme market for biomass conversion.

## Genome and Genetics

T. reesei has a fully sequenced genome of approximately 34 megabases
encoding approximately 9,100 protein-coding genes. The genome encodes
more glycoside hydrolase genes than most other [[environmental-sensing-filamentous-fungi-read]],
reflecting its specialization for plant biomass degradation and
cellulose utilization.

Comparative genomics with other Trichoderma species including the
biocontrol agent T. atroviride and the mycoparasitic T. virens has
revealed both shared and species-specific gene family expansions
related to their different ecological strategies.
