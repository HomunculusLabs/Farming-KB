## Clitocybe (Blewits)

## Description

Clitocybe is a large genus of mushrooms that includes several edible species commonly known as blewits. The most notable cultivated species include [[clitocybe-nuda]] (syn. [[lepista-nuda]], wood blewit) and Clitocybe saeva (field blewit). These mushrooms are prized for their distinctive lilac to purple coloration and fruity aroma. In [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]], Clitocybe species are among those that require or benefit from microbial interactions in casing soil to trigger fruitbody formation.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Phylum**: Basidiomycota
- **Class**: Agaricomycetes
- **Order**: Agaricales
- **Family**: Tricholomataceae
- **Genus**: Clitocybe

## Key Facts

- Common name: blewits, named for their distinctive lilac to purple coloration
- Clitocybe nuda (wood blewit) and C. saeva (field blewit) are the primary edible species
- Fruits terrestrially in grasslands, compost, and woodland areas, often in rings
- Requires or benefits from microbial interactions in casing soil for reliable fruiting
- Known for a distinctive fruity, floral aroma
- Some Clitocybe species contain muscarine and are toxic; accurate identification is critical

## Relevance to Cultivation

Clitocybe cultivation is challenging due to the microbial interaction requirement for fruiting. Like Agaricus, Stropharia, and Morchella, blewits need specific microbial communities present in casing soil to trigger the transition from vegetative colonization to reproductive fruiting. This makes them more difficult to cultivate than wood-decomposing species that fruit readily on sterile substrates.

## Relevance to Mycology

Clitocybe species are important for understanding fungal ecology in grassland and woodland ecosystems. The genus has undergone significant taxonomic revision, with many species reclassified into Lepista, Paralepistopsis, and other genera. The microbial fruiting requirement makes blewits a useful model for studying the diversity of fungal-microbial symbioses across different mushroom lineages.
