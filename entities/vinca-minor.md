The species is of particular pharmacological interest due to its production of over 70 indole alkaloids, most notably vincamine. Vincamine is a vasodilatory alkaloid used as a cognitive enhancer and in the treatment of cerebrovascular insufficiency.

In European traditional medicine, periwinkle has been used for centuries to treat headaches, dizziness, and memory impairment. Its reputation as a brain tonic is substantiated by modern pharmacological research into its alkaloid constituents.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:** Plantae > Tracheophyta > Magnoliopsida > Gentianales > Apocynaceae > Rauvolfioideae > *Vinca* > *V. minor*.

The species was described by Carl Linnaeus in 1753. It is closely related to the larger *[[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]], the British Isles, Scandinavia, Australia, and New Zealand. In parts of the eastern United States and the Pacific Northwest, it is considered an invasive species that displaces native forest floor vegetation.

The species thrives in partial to full shade, preferring moist, well-drained soils rich in [[ursolic-acid]], oleanolic acid). The total alkaloid content of dried leaves ranges from 0.3–1.0% depending on genetic and [[mushroom-cancer-clinical-trials-overview]] show modest but statistically significant improvements in cognitive test scores.

Typical therapeutic doses of vincamine range from 10–30 mg per day. The compound is available as a prescription pharmaceutical in many European countries under brand names such as Cavit, Oxybral, and Intelectol. It is administered orally with good bioavailability.

Vincamine also exhibits mild antihypertensive activity and has been investigated for tinnitus and retinal vascular disorders. Its effects on cerebral microcirculation may also benefit patients with chronic cerebral hypoperfusion.

The related compound vinpocetine (ethyl apovincaminate), a semisynthetic derivative of vincamine, is more widely used clinically. Vinpocetine has improved pharmacokinetics and greater selectivity for cerebral vasculature compared to vincamine.

Antitumor research has focused on the related species *[[catharanthus-roseus]]* (Madagascar periwinkle), which produces the powerful antitumor alkaloids vincristine and vinblastine. *V. minor* does not produce these bisindole alkaloids in significant quantities.
