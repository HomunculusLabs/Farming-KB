# Crotalaria juncea

## Overview

*Crotalaria juncea*, commonly known as sunn hemp, is a fast-growing tropical legume
in the family Fabaceae, widely cultivated as a green manure, cover crop, and fiber
crop. Native to the Indian subcontinent, it has become one of the most important
cover crop species in tropical and subtropical agriculture due to its rapid biomass
production, nitrogen fixation capacity, and weed suppression. Under optimal
conditions, sunn hemp produces 5–8 tonnes of dry matter per hectare within 60–90
days, fixing 60–120 kg N/ha.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae → **Division:** Angiospermae → **Class:** Eudicotyledones
- **Order:** Fabales → **Family:** Fabaceae → **Subfamily:** Faboideae
- **Genus:** *Crotalaria* L. → **Species:** *C. juncea* L. (1753)
- **Etymology:** *Crotalaria* from Greek *krotalon* (rattle), referring to
  seeds rattling in mature pods. *Juncea* means "rush-like," describing the
  slender stems.
- **Common names:** Sunn hemp (English), san (Hindi), crotalaria (Spanish),
  chanvre de soleil (French)

## Morphology

### Growth Habit

- Erect, branching annual herb, 1.5–3.0 m tall; up to 4 m in tropical conditions
- Fast growth rate: 2–4 cm/day during peak vegetative phase
- Taproot system with moderate lateral root development

### Vegetative Structures

- **Stems:** Slender, cylindrical, 0.5–2.0 cm diameter, green to
  greenish-purple; unbranched at base, branching in upper portions
- **Leaves:** Trifoliate, alternate; leaflets 5–10 cm long, oblong-lanceolate,
  entire margin; petiole 1–3 cm
- **Stipules:** Small, linear, 5–8 mm, caducous

### Reproductive Structures

- **Inflorescence:** Terminal or axillary racemes, 10–30 cm long
- **Flowers:** Papilionaceous, 2–3 cm long; calyx deeply lobed;
  corolla bright yellow with purple markings on standard petal
- **Pods:** Oblong, 3–5 cm, inflated, blackish at maturity; 10–25 seeds
- **Seeds:** Reniform, 3–4 mm, mottled brown to dark gray; ~30–40 g/1000 seeds

## Distribution and Habitat

- **Origin:** Indian subcontinent, possibly tropical Africa
- **Current range:** Pantropical; South and Southeast Asia, Africa, Central
  and South America, southern US
- **Climate:** Tropical and subtropical; optimal temperature 25–35°C; frost
  sensitive
- **Rainfall:** 500–2,000 mm annually; drought-tolerant once established
- **Soil:** Wide range from sandy loams to heavy clays; pH 5.0–8.0;
  moderately salt-tolerant
- **Photoperiod:** Short-day flowering response; day-neutral cultivars available

## Agronomic Properties

### Nitrogen Fixation

- Forms effective symbiosis with *Bradyrhizobium* spp. (cowpea-type rhizobia)
- Fixes 60–120 kg N/ha over 60–90 days
- Fixed nitrogen available to subsequent crops within 4–6 weeks of incorporation
- Green manure value equivalent to 30–60 kg N/ha of synthetic fertilizer

### Biomass Production

- Dry matter yield: 5–8 t/ha in 60–90 days; up to 12 t/ha with irrigation
- C:N ratio: 20–25 at early flowering (ideal for green manure)
- Increases soil organic matter by 0.1–0.3% per incorporation season

### Weed Suppression

- Rapid canopy closure within 30–45 days shades competing weeds
- Allelopathic compounds inhibit germination of pigweed, crabgrass, nutsedge
- Residue mulch reduces weed emergence by 60–80% in no-till systems

### Biofumigation

- Decomposing residues release isothiocyanate-like compounds
- Suppresses *Fusarium*, *Rhizoctonia*, *Pythium*, and root-knot nematodes
- Nematode population reduction of 40–80% documented in infested fields
- Most effective when incorporated into moist soil at early flowering

## Cultivation

- **Seed rate:** 20–30 kg/ha for green manure; 10–15 kg/ha for fiber
- **Inoculation:** Cowpea-type rhizobial inoculant recommended for new plantings
- **Sowing:** 1–3 cm depth; drilled in rows 20–30 cm apart
- **Germination:** 3–7 days at >20°C soil temperature
- **Fertilizer:** 20–40 kg P₂O₅/ha and 30–40 kg K₂O/ha if soil-deficient
- **Termination:** Mow or roll at early flowering (50–60 days); allow 2–3
  weeks decomposition before next crop

## Fiber Production

- Stem fibers among strongest natural bast fibers
- Fiber length: 2–6 mm; tensile strength comparable to jute
- Used for rope, twine, sacking, paper pulp, and geotextile composites
- Fiber yield: 800–1,200 kg/ha under rainfed conditions
- Renewed interest as sustainable alternative to synthetic geotextiles

## Toxicity Warning

⚠️ Seeds contain pyrrolizidine alkaloids (PAs), primarily monocrotaline.
- Seeds toxic to livestock, poultry, and humans if ingested
- Leaves and stems contain much lower PA concentrations
- Terminating before pod set eliminates most toxicity concerns
- Use day-neutral, low-seeding cultivars for forage applications

## Permaculture Applications

- **Chop-and-drop mulch:** Exceptional biomass producer for food forest mulching
- **Nitrogen contributor:** Fixes significant nitrogen for intercropped trees
- **Soil builder:** Deep taproot breaks compacted subsoil and brings up nutrients
- **Pest management:** Biofumigant properties reduce nematode pressure
- **Erosion control:** Rapid establishment protects bare soil on slopes
- **Nurse crop:** Shade and wind protection for establishing perennials
