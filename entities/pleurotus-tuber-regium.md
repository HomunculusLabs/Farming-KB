The species is native to tropical and subtropical regions of Africa and Asia, where it has been used for centuries as both food and traditional medicine. The sclerotium is the primary part harvested in traditional practice, valued for its nutritional content and purported [[tropical-mushroom-cultivation-agricultural-waste-substrates-poverty-alleviation]].

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Pleurotaceae
- **Genus:** *Pleurotus*
- **Species:** *P. tuber-regium* (Fr.) Singer (1951)
- **Basionym:** *Agaricus tuber-regium* Fr. (1821)
- **Synonyms:** *Lentinus tuber-regium* (Fr.) Fr., *Panus tuber-regium* (Fr.) Corner

The species was originally described by Fries in 1821 as *Agaricus tuber-regium*. Singer transferred it to *Pleurotus* in 1951. Some mycological treatments retain it in *Lentinus* based on the dimitic hyphal system, but molecular phylogenetics supports its placement within *Pleurotus*.

The epithet *tuber-regium* (Latin: "royal tuber") refers to the large, potato-like sclerotium that was historically regarded as a prized food item in West African kingdoms.

## Morphology

The fruiting body is an oyster-shaped pileus, 5–15 cm in diameter, with a short lateral or eccentric stipe. The cap surface is smooth to slightly velvety, white to cream-colored, sometimes developing brownish tinges with age. Gills are decurrent, white, and crowded.

The stipe is 1–3 cm long and 1–2 cm thick, solid and fibrous. Spore print is white to pale lilac. Basidiospores are cylindrical to ellipsoid, 7–10 × 3–4 µm, smooth, and hyaline.

### Sclerotium

The sclerotium is the most distinctive feature of *P. tuber-regium*. It is an underground, compact, hard mass of densely interwoven mycelium, typically 5–30 cm in diameter and weighing 0.5–3 kg, though specimens exceeding 5 kg have been recorded.

The exterior is dark brown to black and rough-textured. The interior is firm and white to pale cream. Sclerotia serve as survival structures during adverse conditions and as nutrient reserves for fruiting. They are rich in carbohydrates (50–60% dry weight), protein (10–15%), and contain significant levels of β-glucans.

## Distribution and Habitat

*P. tuber-regium* is pantropical, found across sub-Saharan Africa (Nigeria, Cameroon, Ghana, Tanzania), South and Southeast Asia (India, Thailand, Malaysia), and parts of tropical [[mollison-designers-pioneer-species-for-exhausted-tropical-soils]].
Formation is triggered by nutrient depletion and environmental stress, particularly drought conditions.

## Bioactive Compounds

The primary bioactive constituents are high-molecular-weight **polysaccharides**, particularly β-(1→3)-glucans and β-(1→6)-glucans, extracted from both fruiting bodies and sclerotia. These polysaccharides are the main compounds responsible for the species' immunomodulatory activity.

Additional constituents include:
- **Ergosterol** — provitamin D2 precursor, present at 2–4 mg/g dry weight
- **[[aact-tannins-terpenes-phenolic-compounds-wood-composting-ingham]]** — including flavonoids and phenolic acids with antioxidant activity
- **Proteins and peptides** — sclerotia contain lectins with immunostimulatory properties
- **Dietary fiber** — sclerotia are 30–40% fiber by dry weight
- **Minerals** — significant potassium, phosphorus, calcium, and magnesium content

## Pharmacology

In vitro and animal studies demonstrate several pharmacological activities:

- **Immunomodulation:** Polysaccharide extracts activate macrophages, enhance natural killer (NK) cell activity, and stimulate cytokine production (TNF-α, IL-2, IFN-γ). The β-glucan fraction is primarily responsible.
- **Anti-tumor activity:** Polysaccharide extracts inhibit growth of Sarcoma 180 and Ehrlich ascites tumors in murine models, with inhibition rates of 40–60%. Mechanisms include immune-mediated tumor cell lysis rather than direct cytotoxicity.
- **Antioxidant activity:** Methanolic extracts show DPPH and ABTS radical scavenging activity, attributed to [[ingham-tannins-terpenes-phenolic-compounds]].
