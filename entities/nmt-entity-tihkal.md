## NMT (N-Methyltryptamine) — TIHKAL #50

## Compound Identity

- Full name: N-Methyltryptamine (monomethyltryptamine)

- Synonyms: 3-[2-(methylamino)ethyl]indole

- Class: Tryptamine, N-monomethyl substituted

- Source: [[amt-entity-tihkal]]).
The convention used in TIHKAL reserves the two letters before "T" for the
two alkyl groups. N-methyl (mono-substituted) becomes NMT, while dimethyl
stays as DMT. This avoids confusion.

## Chemical Family and Botanical Context

NMT occupies a central position in [[5-meo-dmt-entity]] in a variety of plant
species, particularly in the Virola genus (used in [[tihkal-oral-activity-mao-inhibition]]. Smoking bypasses first-pass metabolism and delivers the
compound directly to the bloodstream, though the very brief duration of
effects (approximately 15 seconds) suggests rapid CNS metabolism or clearance
even when administered by this route.

NMT has moderate affinity for serotonin receptors in vitro, though with lower
potency than DMT at 5-HT2A receptors. Its affinity for trace amine-associated
receptors (TAARs) may be relevant to any endogenous signaling function.

## Comparison to Related Compounds

- Tryptamine: Unsubstituted parent, minimal psychoactivity

- NMT: N-monomethyl, brief activity when smoked, inactive orally

- DMT: N,N-dimethyl, highly active, 3-5 min (smoked), inactive orally (without MAOI)

- 5-MeO-NMT: 5-methoxy-N-methyl, activity unknown

- a-MT (AMT): alpha-methyl tryptamine, orally active, 8-14 h, much longer acting

The progression from tryptamine through NMT to DMT shows increasing psychoactive
potency with each additional methyl group, suggesting that N-alkylation enhances
affinity for psychedelic receptors while the mono-methyl compound represents
an intermediate state.
