## Description

Swingle (referenced as Swingle, 1966 throughout Mollison's aquaculture chapter) is a foundational fisheries researcher whose work on fish stocking rates, polyculture yields, food conversion efficiency, and pond management formed a primary scientific basis for the aquaculture recommendations in *Permaculture: A Designers' Manual*. Swingle's experimental data on fish yields under various stocking configurations, species combinations, and feeding regimes is extensively cited across multiple sections.

## Classification

- **Type**: Researcher / Published authority
- **Field**: Fisheries science, aquaculture, pond management
- **Key publication**: Swingle (1966) — referenced extensively for pond yield data
- **Context**: Auburn University tradition of warmwater aquaculture research (H.S. Swingle)

## Key Facts

- Swingle's data demonstrates that fish species low on the trophic scale (plankton feeders) produce higher yields than predators in fertilised ponds.
- Documented food conversion ratios: selected bass fingerlings achieved 3.4:1 conversion versus 7.3:1 average and 24.2:1 worst case for unselected stock — proving that within-species variation can exceed between-species variation.
- Demonstrated that Bluegill yields increase 18-fold when Gambusia are added as forage (186.4 kg to 3,449.4 kg/ha).
- Polyculture data: buffalo fish plus common carp yielded 925 kg/ha versus 314 kg/ha for carp alone — a 300% increase.
- Established that approximately 50% of pond area can be devoted to forage cover without reducing pan fish yield, analogous to 30% tree shelter benefit for livestock.
- Documented stocking rate thresholds: Tilapia at 5,000/ha produces 97% marketable fish, while 10,000/ha drops to approximately 50% marketable.
- Showed that bluegill plus bass polyculture yields 484.4 kg/ha versus 316.6 kg/ha for bluegill alone — a modest 30-50% increase typical of predator-prey systems.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Swingle's research provides quantitative validation for polyculture principles that parallel fungal cultivation strategies:

- **Trophic efficiency**: Just as lower trophic-level fish convert energy more efficiently, primary decomposer fungi outproduce secondary decomposers on fresh substrate.
- **Polyculture synergy**: Multi-species fish ponds mirror multi-species mushroom beds where complementary decomposer niches increase total yield.
- **Stocking density optimisation**: The concept of optimal stocking rate with diminishing returns parallels inoculation rate experiments in mushroom cultivation.
- **Selection for efficiency**: Swingle's demonstration that intra-species metabolic variation exceeds inter-species variation has direct implications for strain selection in mycology.
