## Overview

**Vigna subterranea**, commonly known as the bambara groundnut, is a drought-tolerant legume native to West Africa.

It is the third most important legume in semi-arid Africa after groundnut and cowpea, though it remains largely underutilized globally.

The plant produces its seeds underground, similar to the peanut, after the flowers burrow into the soil following pollination.

It is valued for its high protein content, resilience to poor soils, and ability to produce yields where other legumes fail.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Order:** Fabales
- **Family:** Fabaceae
- **Subfamily:** Faboideae
- **Genus:** Vigna
- **Species:** V. subterranea (L.) Verdc.

The species was originally placed in the genus *Voandzeia* by Thouars and reclassified to *Vigna* by Bernard Verdcourt in 1980.

The synonym *Voandzeia subterranea* still appears in older literature.

**Growth habit:** An annual herb with a creeping to semi-erect growth form, 20–40 cm tall.

The plant forms a shallow taproot system with abundant lateral roots and nitrogen-fixing nodules.

**Leaves:** Trifoliolate with ovate leaflets 3–6 cm long, similar in appearance to other Vigna species.

**Flowers:** Small, yellow, papilionaceous flowers borne on short peduncles near the base of the plant.

After fertilization, the peduncle elongates and pushes the developing pod 2–7 cm underground.

**Pods:** Underground pods 1.5–3 cm long, each containing 1–2 seeds that are hard and round when dry.

Seed coat colors range from cream, white, red, brown, to black, often with mottled patterns.

Seeds are 8–14 mm in diameter and contain approximately 18–25% protein and 55–65% carbohydrate.

## Distribution and Cultivation

Bambara groundnut is believed to have been domesticated in West Africa, likely in present-day Nigeria or Cameroon.

Today it is cultivated across sub-Saharan Africa, from Senegal to Ethiopia and south to Mozambique and Madagascar.

It is also grown in parts of [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-south-america]], and northern Australia, though primarily as a subsistence crop.

The crop is well-adapted to sandy, infertile soils with low [[tryptophan]], making it a valuable complement to cereal-based diets.

It is considered to have a more balanced amino acid profile than most other [[carbon-isotope-labelling-mycorrhizal-fatty-acids]], including linoleic and oleic acid.

[[kava-traditional-preparation-ceremonial-context]] methods include boiling, roasting, and milling into flour for making porridge and flatbreads.

A fermented product called "okpa" is popular in southeastern Nigeria, made from bambara groundnut flour wrapped in banana leaves.
