## Description

A spinner drain is a low-impact earthwork technique for creating gentle drainage channels across flat landscapes. It uses a large wheel with small cups mounted on it, towed behind a tractor. As the tractor drives across the landscape, the wheel revolves and chews out a gentle channel while throwing the excavated dirt well away from the channel. The result is a shallow drain through which water runs, barely visible on the landscape except in low-lying conditions. Vehicles and tractors can drive across spinner drains without difficulty.

## Classification

- **Category**: Water infrastructure / Earthwork technique
- **Type**: Machine-dug shallow drain
- **Equipment**: Spinner wheel attachment for tractor
- **Terrain**: Very low slopes, flat lands, desert margins
- **Visibility**: Minimal — integrates into the landscape

## Construction

The spinner drain is created by:

1. **Equipment setup**: A large wheel with small cups is mounted on and towed behind a tractor.
2. **Traverse**: The tractor drives across the landscape on the desired line.
3. **Excavation**: The revolving wheel chews out a gentle channel approximately 4 feet wide and 1 foot deep.
4. **Spoil disposal**: Dirt is thrown well away from the channel, leaving no banks or ridges.
5. **Grassing**: The drain is normally grassed as part of the field, making it nearly invisible.

## Key Facts

- **Dimensions**: Approximately 4 feet wide and 1 foot deep.
- **No banks**: Unlike conventional drains, there are no raised edges. The spoil is distributed far from the channel.
- **Trafficable**: Vehicles and tractors can enter and leave the drain without difficulty — no embankments to cross.
- **Landscape integration**: Barely visible on the landscape except in low-lying wet conditions.
- **Application**: Primarily used on very low slopes and flat lands where conventional tilted-blade drains are unnecessary.
- **Cost**: Very low — single pass with tractor creates the complete drain.
- **Feeds earth tanks**: Spinner drains can direct gentle flow into excavated earth tanks below ground level.

## Applications

- **Low-slope drainage**: Moves water across nearly flat terrain where even slight grading is needed.
- **Desert water harvesting**: Creates subtle collection channels in arid landscapes.
- **Earth tank supply**: Feeds water into excavated tanks for storage below ground level.
- **Pasture drainage**: Removes excess water from flat pastures without creating obstacles for livestock or machinery.

## Related Equipment

- **Delvers**: Joined double plows towed behind bulldozers, with two wings that throw out a V-shaped drain while spreading soil to the sides. Used for low-slope systems.
- **Tilted blade**: For steeper slopes, a tractor blade set on tilt creates a drain with an outer earth wall. Can double as a contour road.
- **Chisel plow / Soil conditioner**: For soil conditioning rather than drainage, creating subsurface water channels.

## Relevance to Cultivation

- **Flat-land farming**: Enables productive agriculture on very low-grade land by managing water movement without significant earthworks.
- **Low-impact design**: Preserves the visual and physical character of the landscape while providing essential drainage function.
- **Cost-effective water management**: Single-pass construction makes extensive drainage networks affordable.
- **Desert reclamation**: Subtle water harvesting in arid regions where visible infrastructure would be inappropriate or impractical.
