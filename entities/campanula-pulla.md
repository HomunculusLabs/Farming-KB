## Campanula pulla

## Overview

**Campanula pulla** (syn. *C. pulloides*), commonly called the **dark bellflower** or **dusky bellflower**, is a low-growing perennial species in the family Campanulaceae. It is native to the Eastern Alps and adjacent mountain ranges of Central Europe.

The species is prized in alpine and rock-garden horticulture for its nodding, deep violet-purple campanulate flowers and compact cushion-forming habit. Its specific epithet *pulla* means "dusky" or "dark-colored," referring to the unusually dark corolla pigmentation.

It is one of the darkest-flowered species in the genus *Campanula*, making it a sought-after plant for collectors of alpine species. The plant has received the RHS Award of Garden Merit in recognition of its ornamental value under suitable conditions.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:**
- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Family:** Campanulaceae
- **Genus:** *Campanula*
- **Species:** *C. pulla* L.
- **Synonyms:** *C. pulloides* Witasek

The species forms dense, moss-like cushions typically 5–10 cm tall. Basal leaves are small, ovate to lanceolate, and crenate-serrate along the margins, measuring 8–15 mm long.

The cushion growth form is an adaptation to the harsh alpine environment, reducing wind exposure and trapping heat within the plant canopy. Individual cushions can reach 15–20 cm in diameter over many years.

Stem leaves are narrower and sparsely arranged along the short flowering stems. ## Ecological Role and Conservation

In its native alpine habitats, Campanula pulla contributes to the biodiversity of limestone scree and crevice communities. Its flowers are visited by small alpine bees and hoverflies.

The deep purple corolla pigmentation is produced by delphinidin-based anthocyanins, which may serve a dual function of attracting pollinators and providing UV protection at high elevations.
