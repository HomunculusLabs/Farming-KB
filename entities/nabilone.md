# Nabilone

**Type:** Chemical / Pharmaceutical / Synthetic Cannabinoid
**Category:** FDA-approved synthetic cannabinoid
**Also known as:** Cesamet

## Overview
Nabilone is a synthetic cannabinoid approved by the FDA and marketed
under the brand name Cesamet. It is structurally related to THC but is
a chemically distinct compound designed to mimic THC's therapeutic
effects. Nabilone is one of three major FDA-approved cannabinoid-based
pharmaceuticals, alongside dronabinol (Marinol) and Sativex. It is
primarily prescribed for nausea and vomiting associated with
chemotherapy.

## Key Details

### Chemical Properties
- Chemical class: Synthetic cannabinoid (carbazole derivative)
- Structurally related to but distinct from delta-9-THC
- Activates cannabinoid receptors (CB1/CB2)
- Available in capsule form for oral administration
- FDA-approved pharmaceutical product
- Longer half-life than natural THC

### FDA-Approved Indications
- **Chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting:** Primary approved use;
  prescribed for patients who have not responded adequately to
  conventional anti-emetic therapies
- **Off-label uses:** Investigated and used for various conditions
  including chronic pain, fibromyalgia, multiple sclerosis spasticity,
  and PTSD

### Clinical Evidence
- **Cancer-related pain and symptoms (William Osler Health Center,
  Toronto, 2006):** 82 cancer patients received nabilone for 53 days.
  Patients showed improvement in pain management and other cancer-
  related symptoms during the treatment period.
- **Hepatitis C treatment (referenced in [[cannabis-health-index]]):**
  Nabilone was used to reduce nausea and vomiting while improving
  appetite in hepatitis C patients.
- **Appetite stimulation:** Nabilone demonstrates appetite-promoting
  effects similar to dronabinol, making it useful for cachexia and
  wasting syndromes.

### Comparison with Dronabinol
While both nabilone and dronabinol are FDA-approved synthetic
cannabinoids, they differ in several respects:
- Nabilone is a carbazole derivative, not a THC isomer
- Nabilone has a different chemical structure than natural THC
- Nabilone may have a longer duration of action
- Both lack the full spectrum of cannabis plant constituents
- Neither provides the entourage effect of whole-plant cannabis

### Comparison with Sativex
- Sativex is plant-derived (contains both THC and CBD from cannabis)
- Nabilone is purely synthetic
- Sativex provides a more natural cannabinoid profile
- Nabilone offers standardized, consistent dosing
- Sativex is administered as an oromucosal spray; nabilone as oral
  capsules

### Limitations
- Like dronabinol, nabilone is a single isolated compound lacking
  the synergistic effects of whole-plant cannabis
- Does not contain CBD, CBG, CBC, CBN, terpenes, or flavonoids found
  in natural cannabis
- Potential side effects include drowsiness, dizziness, and
  psychoactive effects
- Limited to specific FDA-approved indications

## Relevance
Nabilone represents the pharmaceutical approach to harnessing
cannabinoid therapeutics through synthetic chemistry. Its FDA approval
for chemotherapy-induced nausea validates the anti-emetic properties
of cannabinoids. However, nabilone's limitations compared to whole-
plant cannabis highlight the ongoing tension between pharmaceutical
standardization and botanical complexity in cannabinoid medicine. The
growing body of evidence supporting the entourage effect suggests that
single-compound synthetic cannabinoids may be inherently limited
compared to full-spectrum cannabis preparations for many conditions.
