## Overview

Vincetoxicum hirundinaria Medik., commonly known as white swallowwort, is a perennial herbaceous climbing vine in the family Apocynaceae (formerly Asclepiadaceae).

The species is native to Europe and western Asia, where it grows in woodland margins, hedgerows, and rocky slopes.

It has a long history of use in European traditional medicine as an anti-inflammatory and wound-healing remedy.

The genus name Vincetoxicum derives from Latin "vincere" (to conquer) and "toxicum" (poison), reflecting its historical reputation as an antidote to poisons.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:**

- Kingdom: Plantae
- Order: Gentianales
- Family: Apocynaceae
- Subfamily: Asclepiadoideae
- Genus: Vincetoxicum
- Species: V. hirundinaria Medik.

**Synonyms:** Cynanchum vincetoxicum (L.) Pers., Asclepias vincetoxicum L.

The species epithet "hirundinaria" refers to the swallow-like (hirundo) shape of the follicular fruits.

**Morphology:**

The plant forms a twining vine reaching 1–2 meters in length, with opposite, cordate-ovate leaves 5–10 cm long.

Flowers are small, creamy-white, borne in axillary umbels from June to August.

Each flower has a deeply five-lobed corolla with a gynostegial corona characteristic of the Asclepiadoideae.

The fruit is a slender follicle 6–10 cm long, containing numerous seeds with a tuft of silky coma (pappus) for wind dispersal.

Roots are thick and creeping, forming a rhizomatous crown that enables vegetative spread.

## Distribution and Habitat

Vincetoxicum hirundinaria is widely distributed across central and southern Europe, extending eastward through the Balkans into Turkey, the Caucasus, and parts of western Asia.

In northern Europe, its range reaches southern Scandinavia and the Baltic states.

The species is absent from the British Isles, though it has been recorded as a rare casual introduction.

It prefers calcareous soils and is frequently found on limestone outcrops, dry stone walls, and rocky escarpments.

Woodland edges, scrubland, and abandoned vineyards are also common habitats.

The plant tolerates partial shade but flowers most profusely in open, sunny positions.

It occurs from lowland elevations up to approximately 1,500 meters in montane regions of the Alps and Carpathians.
