# Titanium Oxide Nitrogen Fixation (Mollison's System)

## Description

A catalytic nitrogen fixation system described by Bill Mollison in which titanium oxide (TiO2), a common white paint pigment, catalyzes the conversion of atmospheric nitrogen into ammonia in the presence of sunlight. Mollison presents this as "Mollison's third world endless nitrogen fertilizer supply system" — a low-cost, low-technology method for producing nitrogen fertilizer without industrial processes.

## Classification

- **Category**: Chemical catalysis / soil amendment technique
- **Type**: Photocatalytic nitrogen fixation
- **Materials**: White sand, titanium oxide (TiO2), water

## Key Facts

- **Catalytic reaction**: Titanium oxide catalyzes atmospheric N2 into NH3 (ammonia) when exposed to sunlight. Neither the sand nor the TiO2 is consumed in the reaction.
- **Water solubility**: The resulting ammonia is highly water-soluble and can be flushed from the system with water to fertilize gardens directly.
- **Scale**: Three acres of desert under this system would supply as much nitrogen as a commercial fertilizer plant. Practically, three square feet in a small garden suffices.
- **Desert relevance**: In desert environments, the nitrogen naturally evaporates into the atmosphere, which is why deserts can support plant growth after rain — rain carries the ammonia back down.
- **Scientific basis**: Noted in Chemical Abstracts circa 1977, with implications for atmospheric circulation. The atmosphere's excess of ammonia was partly accounted for by this catalytic process.

## System Design (Mollison's Instructions)

1. Build a sand box with a trickle-in water system and subsurface barriers to slow water flow.
2. Fill the box with white sand.
3. Add approximately 0.25 ounce of titanium oxide.
4. Expose to sunlight — the catalytic reaction produces ammonia continuously.
5. Periodically flush the system with water (not continuous, to prevent algae buildup).
6. Use the ammonia solution to water gardens.

## Applications

- **Small-scale gardening**: A tiny box provides enough nitrogen for household food production.
- **Sand dune rehabilitation**: Adding 1-2 pounds of TiO2 to sand dunes enables clover and other plants to establish by providing continuous nitrogen after rainfall.
- **Desert agriculture**: Explains why plants can establish in deserts without nitrogen-fixing pioneer species.
- **Third world agriculture**: Requires only sand, TiO2 (available as paint filler), and sunlight.

## Ecological Significance

Mollison frames this as an example of natural "factories" working everywhere — black sand beaches cook and dehydrate materials through solar energy, and titanium oxide catalysis occurs naturally in desert sands worldwide. The technique represents permaculture's emphasis on understanding and harnessing natural processes rather than relying on industrial agriculture.
