## Taxonomy and Morphology

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Order:** Poales
- **Family:** Poaceae
- **Subfamily:** Panicoideae
- **Genus:** Zea
- **Species:** Z. mays L.

The genus Zea contains only this domesticated species and its wild progenitor, Zea mays ssp. parviglumis (teosinte).

Teosinte, native to southwestern Mexico, bears little resemblance to modern maize but shares the same chromosome number (2n = 20).

**Stems:** Stout, solid culms 1–4 meters tall, depending on variety. Some tropical landraces exceed 6 meters.

**Leaves:** Broad lanceolate blades 50–100 cm long and 5–10 cm wide, arranged alternately along the stem.

**Inflorescence:** Maize is monoecious, bearing separate male and female flowers on the same plant.

The male tassel is a terminal panicle that produces enormous quantities of pollen, dispersed by wind.

The female ear is a modified lateral spike enclosed in husks, with silk (styles) protruding from the tip.

Each kernel develops from a single fertilized ovule, with a typical ear bearing 400–600 kernels arranged in 12–20 rows.

Kernels vary widely in color, texture, and composition across different racial groups and cultivars.

**Roots:** A fibrous root system supplemented by brace roots (prop roots) from the lower stem nodes.

## Distribution and Cultivation

Maize was domesticated from teosinte in the Balsas River valley of southwestern Mexico, as confirmed by archaeological and genetic evidence.

It spread throughout the Americas before European contact and subsequently to all inhabited continents.

## Nutritional Value

Yellow maize kernels contain approximately 9–10% protein, 72–74% starch, and 4–5% fat per 100 g dry weight.

The protein is predominantly zein, a prolamin deficient in the essential amino acids lysine and [[tryptophan]].

Quality Protein Maize (QPM) varieties were developed to address this deficiency, containing opaque-2 gene modifiers that double the lysine content.

Maize is a significant source of dietary energy in many [[low-cost-mushroom-growing-house-construction-developing-countries]], providing over 20% of caloric intake in sub-Saharan Africa and Latin America.

Yellow maize contains carotenoids, including lutein and zeaxanthin, which are important for eye health.

White maize, preferred in many African and Latin American countries, lacks significant carotenoid content.

Whole-grain maize provides dietary fiber, B vitamins (thiamine, niacin, folate), iron, and magnesium.
