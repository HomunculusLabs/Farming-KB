# BEG (Bank of European Glomales)

## Description

BEG is a European culture collection and repository dedicated to the conservation, maintenance, and distribution of arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) fungi in the order Glomales. Alongside INVAM (International Culture Collection of Vesicular Arbuscular Mycorrhizal Fungi) in the United States, BEG serves as one of the two major international reference collections for these agriculturally and ecologically vital symbiotic fungi. Researchers and cultivators can deposit authenticated isolates, request reference material for comparison, and access diagnosed species for experimental work.

## Classification

- **Type:** Microbial culture collection / Reference repository
- **Taxonomic focus:** Arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi (Glomales/Glomeromycota)
- **Geographic scope:** Europe (international access)
- **Complementary collections:** INVAM (USA), other regional AM fungal collections

## Key Facts

- BEG maintains living cultures of authenticated AM fungal species, providing a critical resource for taxonomic verification and comparative morphology studies.
- When a mycorrhizal fungus is established in culture, researchers are recommended to register it with either BEG or INVAM so that inoculum can be archived, maintained, and made available to other investigators.
- The collection serves as a safeguard against loss of unique germplasm — important given that many AM fungal species cannot be cultured independently of a host plant.
- BEG supports the standardization of species descriptions by providing reference accessions with documented morphological and molecular characters.
- As a central repository, BEG accepts mailed inoculum from researchers worldwide, maintaining and increasing cultures for distribution.
- The collection plays a key role in resolving taxonomic confusion in Glomales, where 90% or more of published species descriptions are considered vague or incomplete.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

For cultivators working with mycorrhizal systems, BEG provides verified inoculum of known species identity — essential for reproducible experiments and commercial mycorrhizal products. Misidentified cultures are a persistent problem in AM fungal research and commerce; reference to BEG-authenticated material helps ensure quality control. Researchers developing mycorrhizal inocula for agriculture, horticulture, or restoration ecology should consider depositing novel isolates with BEG to ensure long-term preservation and availability.

## Practical Notes

- Contact BEG before shipping live cultures to arrange appropriate permits and handling procedures.
- Deposit voucher specimens (spores mounted in PVLG and PVLG + Melzer's) alongside live cultures for complete documentation.
- When species identification is uncertain, BEG staff may assist with verification by comparison to reference accessions.
