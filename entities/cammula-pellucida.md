## Description

**Cammula pellucida** (also spelled Camnula pellucida), the clearwinged grasshopper, is a species of grasshopper in the family Acrididae native to North America. It experienced near-complete eradication by the entomopathogenic fungus [[entomophthora-grylli]] during a 1963 outbreak in Saskatchewan, Canada.









## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Animalia
- **Phylum**: Arthropoda
- **Class**: Insecta
- **Order**: Orthoptera
- **Family**: Acrididae
- **Genus**: Cammula (Camnula)
- **Species**: C. pellucida









## Key Facts

- Pickford and Riegert (1964) documented near-complete eradication by Entomophthora grylli in Saskatchewan in 1963
- The fungal epizootic was correlated with unusually high precipitation and humidity in June and July of 1963
- Serves as a classic case study of density-dependent fungal population regulation









## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

The C. pellucida - E. grylli interaction is a textbook example of fungal biocontrol at the population level. The near-eradication by a single fungal pathogen demonstrates the potential power of [[entomopathogenic-fungi]] as biocontrol agents. For mycologists, this illustrates how fungal pathogens can act as keystone regulators of insect populations.









## Ecological Role

C. pellucida is a grassland grasshopper that can reach outbreak densities causing significant damage to cereal crops and rangeland forage. Its population dynamics are strongly influenced by fungal pathogens, predators, and weather conditions.
