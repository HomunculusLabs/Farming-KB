# Cannabinol (CBN)

**Type:** Chemical / Phytocannabinoid
**Category:** Degradation product cannabinoid
**Also known as:** CBN, cannabinol

## Overview
Cannabinol (CBN) is a phytocannabinoid found in [[cannabis-sativa]] and
[[cannabis-indica]], notable primarily as an oxidative degradation product of
tetrahydrocannabinol (THC). As cannabis ages and is exposed to oxygen,
light, and heat, THC gradually converts to CBN. This makes CBN content
a useful indicator of cannabis freshness and storage conditions. CBN
is one of the five major cannabinoids studied in medical cannabis
research.

## Key Details

### Chemical Properties
- Molecular formula: C21H26O2
- Weakly psychoactive (significantly less than THC)
- Higher affinity for CB2 receptors than CB1
- Primary degradation product of THC oxidation
- Forms naturally as cannabis ages and cures
- Present in higher concentrations in older, improperly stored cannabis

### Relationship to THC
CBN is produced when THC is exposed to oxygen over time. Fresh cannabis
typically contains very little CBN, while aged or poorly stored material
may have elevated CBN levels at the expense of THC content. The ratio
of THC:CBD:CBN is used to characterize cannabis strains:
- Sativa: higher THC to lower CBD/CBN ratio
- Indica: lower THC to higher CBD/CBN ratio

This ratio determines whether the plant's effects are more stimulating
(sativa) or sedating (indica), and whether the balancing properties of
whole-plant constituents are additive or cancel each other out.

### Therapeutic Applications
- **Antibacterial:** CBN demonstrated potent activity against
  methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) in the 2008
  multi-center international study, alongside THC, CBD, CBG, and CBC.
- **Anti-cancer:** CBN and THC (but not CBD) retarded lung cancer cell
  growth and reduced tumor size in laboratory studies conducted in
  Richmond, VA.
- **Sedative effects:** CBN is often associated with sedative properties
  and is believed to contribute to the "couch-lock" effect of aged
  cannabis. Indica strains with higher CBN content may be preferred
  for nighttime use.
- **Cardiovascular:** CBN has potentially potent cardioprotective
  properties alongside THC, as referenced in cardiovascular research
  sections of the [[cannabis-health-index]].
- **Appetite stimulation:** CBN may contribute to appetite stimulation
  effects observed with whole-plant cannabis use.

### Receptor Pharmacology
CBN has a higher affinity for CB2 receptors than CB1 receptors, placing
it in the same general category as CBD in terms of receptor preference.
However, CBN's weak psychoactivity suggests some CB1 interaction,
though at a much lower efficacy than THC. Its pharmacological profile
is distinct from both THC and CBD.

### Strain and Form Considerations
- Indica strains tend to have higher CBN relative to sativa strains
- CBN content increases with improper storage (heat, light, oxygen)
- Higher CBN ratios associated with more sedating, relaxing effects
- CBN-rich cannabis may be preferred for insomnia and pain management
- Proper storage (cool, dark, airtight) preserves THC and minimizes CBN

## Relevance
CBN is important both as a therapeutic compound and as a marker of
cannabis quality. Its role as a THC degradation product makes it
relevant to patients and cultivators concerned with cannabinoid profile
stability. The discovery that CBN and THC (but not CBD) retarded lung
cancer growth suggests CBN may have unique anti-cancer mechanisms. CBN
is gaining attention in the "minor cannabinoid" research space,
particularly for its sedative and sleep-promoting properties, with some
companies now producing CBN isolate products.
