## Overview

Carex stipata, commonly known as awned sedge or common fox sedge, is a
widespread perennial sedge native to North America. It is one of the most
common and ecologically important sedges in the northern [[guzman-magic-mushrooms-temperate-zone-species]].

The species is recognized by its sharply pointed, awn-tipped perigynia and
stout, densely flowered spikes. It is highly adaptable and occurs in a wide
range of wetland and moist habitats.

Carex stipata has a circumboreal distribution, occurring across North America
and extending into Eurasia. The [[growing-gourmet-north-american-reishi-species]] populations are assigned to
varieties that differ in perigynia morphology and habitat preference.

The species is classified as a facultative wetland plant (FACW) and is widely
used in wetland restoration [[mollison-designers-cloudburst-and-stormwater-management]] projects throughout
eastern North America.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Carex stipata was described by Henry Ernest Muhlenberg in 1803. The authority
is C. stipata Muhl. ex Willd., published in Species Plantarum, edition 4,
volume 2, page 318. The species belongs to Carex section Paludosae.

Two varieties are generally recognized in North America: var. stipata, which
has narrower perigynia and occurs in northeastern North America, and var.
maxima, which has broader perigynia and ranges across the continent.

The plant forms dense tussocks from short, stout rhizomes. Culms are sharply
triangular, 30 to 100 centimeters tall, and 1.5 to 3.5 millimeters thick.

Leaf blades are 3 to 8 millimeters wide, green to dark green, and typically
shorter than the culms. Sheaths are green to brown, and ligules are
approximately 3 to 6 millimeters long.

The inflorescence is androgynous with 1 to 3 staminate spikes above 2 to 5
pistillate spikes. Pistillate spikes are ovoid to cylindric, 0.8 to 2
centimeters long, and densely flowered on short peduncles.

Perigynia are lanceolate to broadly ovate, 3.5 to 6 millimeters long, with a
distinctive awn at the tip that extends 1 to 3 millimeters beyond the beak.
This awn is the diagnostic feature distinguishing C. stipata from similar
species.

## Distribution and Habitat

Carex stipata ranges across most of North America from Alaska and the Yukon
east to Newfoundland, south to California, New Mexico, Georgia, and the
Carolinas. It also occurs across northern Europe and Asia.

The species is absent from the southeastern coastal plain, the Gulf Coast, and
the arid interior of the western United States. It is most abundant in the
northern Great Lakes region and New England.

It occupies a broad range of wetland and moist habitats including marshes,
sedge meadows, fens, streambanks, pond margins, roadside ditches, and
depressional wetlands. Elevation ranges from sea level to over 2,000 meters.

Carex stipata tolerates both mineral and organic substrates. It grows in full
sun to partial shade and is notably tolerant of fluctuating water levels.

## Ecology

Carex stipata is a dominant species in many sedge meadow and marsh
communities across northern North America. It is a characteristic species of
the [[carex-stricta]] sedge meadow alliance and the Calamagrostis–Carex wet
meadow type.

The dense root systems and rhizome mats stabilize soil in riparian and
palustrine wetlands. Plants spread clonally, forming extensive patches that
resist erosion and sediment movement.
