# Lepiota americana

## Description

Lepiota americana, commonly known as the reddening lepiota, is an edible mushroom species in the Lepiota complex that is notable for its flesh turning reddish when bruised or cut. It is related to the well-known parasol mushroom ([[macrolepiota-procera]]) and shares similar cultivation parameters. The species has demonstrated potent antibiotic properties in laboratory studies, making it interesting for both culinary and medicinal research. Found in temperate to tropical regions, it is part of the larger Lepiota group common throughout many areas of the world.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Agaricaceae
- **Genus:** Lepiota
- **Species:** L. americana

## Key Facts

- Edible species commonly known as the reddening lepiota
- Flesh turns reddish when bruised or cut — a key identification feature
- Related to the parasol mushroom (Macrolepiota procera) with similar cultivation parameters
- Shows potent antibiotic properties in laboratory studies
- Found in temperate to tropical regions
- Part of the larger Lepiota complex that includes both edible and poisonous species
- Proper identification is critical — some Lepiota species are poisonous
- Difficulty level: Outdoor cultivation 2/5, Indoor cultivation 4/5

## Identification

- Reddening of flesh when bruised or cut is the most distinctive feature
- Similar in overall form to the parasol mushroom but smaller
- Must be distinguished from poisonous Lepiota species — expert verification recommended
- [[spore-print]] characteristics should be checked to rule out toxic lookalikes
- When collecting for cultivation, proper identification is essential to avoid culturing poisonous specimens

## Cultivation Notes

### Substrate Preferences

- Prefers composted mixtures of grasses, cereal straws, wood mulch, spent coffee grounds
- Adaptable to agricultural by-products: bean hulls, cornstalks, cotton waste, beet pulp, sugarcane bagasse
- Prefers complex growing media over basic formulations
- Terrestrial habitat — grows in compost and decomposing organic matter

### Cultivation Methods

Cultivation parameters are similar to those of the parasol mushroom (Macrolepiota procera):

- Shredded agricultural waste pasteurized in hot water bath
- Fill beds or containers 8–10 inches deep with growing medium
- Diamond-pattern spawn holes every 6–8 inches across surface
- Cover with wet compost or perforated plastic to retain moisture
- Microbial [[casing-soil]] required when substrate is fully colonized
- Casing stimulates primordia formation

### Outdoor Cultivation

- Spawn can be scattered using sawdust or grass seed spawn
- Ants and other terrestrial insects may assist in distributing spawn underground
- Expect fruitings in late summer or fall following spring inoculation
- Inoculation to fruiting time: four to eight months

### Indoor Cultivation

- Pasteurize substrate in hot water bath for one to two hours
- Maintain high humidity or fog-like environment until primordia form
- Reduce humidity and watering as mushrooms mature
- Inoculation to fruiting time: six to eight weeks
- Expected yields: Average

### Spawn Types

- [[grain-spawn]] (fruits sooner, larger yields)
- Supplemented sawdust spawn

### Casing Soil

- Microbial casing soil is required for fruiting
- Dress beds with 2–3 inches of casing when substrate is fully colonized
- Water generously after applying casing

## Wild Collection and Cloning

- Can be found and cloned from wild specimens
- Proper identification essential to avoid confusing with poisonous Lepiota species
- Consult expert mycologists for verification
- NAMA-sponsored mushroom hunts provide expert identification services
- Tissue cloning from interior of stem base or cap

## Nutritional and Medicinal Properties

- Protein content comparable to other Lepiota species (12–22 percent dry weight estimated)
- Potent antibiotic properties demonstrated in laboratory studies
- The Lepiota family has shown antiviral, antibacterial, and anti-inflammatory compounds
- Shown to suppress many cancer cell lines in related species

## Caution — Poisonous Relatives

- Many Lepiota species are poisonous, some deadly
- L. americana must be positively identified before any cultivation or consumption
- Never collect or culture Lepiota specimens without expert verification
- The reddening reaction is helpful but not sufficient alone for identification
- Always perform spore prints and consult multiple identification resources

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

L. americana represents an interesting species for expanding the range of cultivated Lepiota mushrooms. Its similarity to the well-known parasol mushroom means cultivation techniques are already established. The potent antibiotic properties make it valuable for medicinal research and potentially for [[mycoremediation-applications]]. However, the critical importance of proper identification within the Lepiota complex — which contains both edible and deadly poisonous species — cannot be overstated. Cultivators should work with verified cultures from reputable suppliers and exercise extreme caution with wild-collected specimens.
