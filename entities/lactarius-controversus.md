## Lactarius controversus

## Description

**Lactarius controversus** (the poplar milkcap) is an ectomycorrhizal basidiomycete fungus that associates primarily with poplar (Populus) and willow (Salix) species. It has been studied in the context of enzyme competency and organic nitrogen utilization, where it showed limited ability to access nitrogen from protein sources.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Phylum**: Basidiomycota
- **Class**: Agaricomycetes
- **Order**: Russulales
- **Family**: Russulaceae
- **Genus**: Lactarius
- **Species**: L. controversus

## Key Facts

- Forms [[ectomycorrhizal-associations]] primarily with Populus and Salix species
- Tested for organic nitrogen utilization from bovine serum albumin (BSA) alongside other [[ectomycorrhizal-fungi]]
- Accumulated no more nitrogen from BSA than from basal medium, indicating limited protease enzyme activity
- This contrasts with [[suillus-bovinus]], which showed significantly greater access to nitrogen in BSA
- Belongs to the genus Lactarius, which is known for producing a milky latex when fruiting bodies are cut or broken
- Part of the functional diversity of ectomycorrhizal fungi in forest ecosystems

## Ecological Role

L. controversus is ecologically important because:

- It represents a moderate to low enzyme-competency ectomycorrhizal species
- Associates with riparian and floodplain tree species, playing a role in nutrient cycling in wetland and riverine forests
- Its limited protease activity suggests reliance on inorganic nitrogen sources
- Contributes to functional diversity within the ectomycorrhizal community of poplar-willow woodlands
- May be more dependent on saprotrophic mineralization to access nutrients

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- Relevant to poplar and willow forestry and short-rotation coppice systems
- Demonstrates functional variation within the Lactarius genus regarding enzyme competency
- Important for understanding nitrogen acquisition strategies in riparian mycorrhizal communities
- Contributes to matching fungal partners with appropriate soil conditions and management objectives
- May be best suited to sites where inorganic nitrogen is readily available
