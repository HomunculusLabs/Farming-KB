## Overview

**Panaeolus cyanescens** (blue meanies, Hawaiian copelandia) is a
coprophilous (dung-inhabiting) psilocybin mushroom in the family
Bolbitiaceae. Native to tropical and subtropical regions worldwide, it is
one of the most potent psilocybin-containing mushrooms known.

The species is widely used as an entheogen in traditional and recreational
contexts. It belongs to the *Panaeolus* subgenus *Copelandia*, a group
characterized by blue-staining reactions and potent psilocybin content.

"Panaeolus" and "Panaolina" are orthographic variants; recent molecular work
places the species in *Panaeolus* s.l.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Taxonomy:**

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Bolbitiaceae
- **Genus:** *Panaeolus* (subg. *Copelandia*)
- **Species:** *P. cyanescens* (Berkeley & Broome) Sacc.

**Synonyms:** *Copelandia cyanescens* (Berk. & Broome) Singer,
*Agaricus cyanescens* Berk. & Broome.

**Morphology:**

- **Cap:** 1.5–4 cm, hemispherical to convex, expanding to plano-convex;
  hygrophanous, pale brown to gray when moist, whitish when dry; surface
  smooth, often wrinkled with age
- **Gills:** Adnexed to adnate, close, mottled gray to black (two-spored)
- **Stipe:** 4–12 cm × 1–3 mm, equal, hollow, whitish, finely pruinose
  above; bruises intensely blue where handled
- **Spores:** 11–14 × 7–9 μm, elliptical to lemon-shaped, thick-walled,
  dark brown to black in deposit, two-spored basidia
- **Odor:** Farinaceous (mealy); taste mealy to slightly bitter
- **Key feature:** Intense blue bruising on all parts when damaged,
  a reliable field indicator of psychoactive content

## Distribution and Habitat

Pantropical distribution. Found throughout Central and South America,
the Caribbean, Southeast Asia, Oceania (Australia, Hawaii, Fiji),
sub-Saharan Africa, and coastal regions of the southeastern United States
(Florida, Gulf Coast).

Grows exclusively on herbivore dung, particularly cattle and horse dung in
open pastures and grasslands. Fruits in warm, humid conditions, typically
after rainfall. Also reported on enriched soils and compost.

In Hawaii, *P. cyanescens* fruits year-round in pastures below 1,000 m
elevation and is one of the most commonly encountered psychoactive fungi.

## Bioactive Compounds

Psilocybin and psilocin are the primary psychoactive alkaloids. *P.
cyanescens* consistently ranks among the most potent psilocybin mushrooms:

- **Psilocybin:** 0.5–2.95% dry weight (average ~1.5%)
- **Psilocin:** 0.15–0.9% dry weight
- **Baeocystin:** 0.05–0.3% dry weight
- **Norbaeocystin:** trace amounts

Potency varies significantly by specimen, substrate, geographic origin,
and drying/storage conditions. Hawaiian specimens historically reported at
the higher end of the range.

## Pharmacology and Effects

Psilocybin is a prodrug dephosphorylated to psilocin, which acts as a
partial agonist at serotonin 5-HT₂A receptors. This mechanism produces
altered perception, synesthesia, and mystical-type experiences.

**Typical dose (dried):** 0.5–1.5 g for mild effects, 1.5–3 g for
moderate effects, 3–5 g for strong effects. Lower than *Psilocybe cubensis*
due to higher potency.

**Onset:** 15–45 minutes. **Peak:** 1–3 hours. **Duration:** 4–6 hours.

Common effects include visual distortion, enhanced color perception,
altered time sense, introspective ideation, and emotional lability.
At higher doses: ego dissolution, complex hallucinations, synesthesia.

## Cultivation

*P. cyanescens* is more challenging to cultivate than *P. cubensis*
due to specific requirements and slower colonization.

- **Substrate:** Sterilized horse or cow manure; can be supplemented with
  straw or vermiculite. Pasteurization is preferred over full sterilization.
- **Temperature:** Colonization at 24–27°C; fruiting at 23–27°C with
  high humidity (90–95% RH)
- **Light:** Indirect ambient light initiates pinning
- **Yield:** Generally lower than *P. cubensis*; flushes are smaller
  but potency compensates
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate to advanced; contamination-prone

Outdoor cultivation on enriched manure beds in tropical/subtropical
climates is the most reliable approach. Indoor cultivation is possible
but requires careful humidity and gas exchange management.

## Traditional and Cultural Use

In traditional Thai and Balinese contexts, *P. cyanescens* (known as
"hed keequai" — "mushroom that grows after water buffalo") has been used
ceremonially for centuries. Western popularization began in the 1970s
through ethnobotanical documentation of psychoactive fungi use.
