# Hypocreopsis

## Description

Hypocreopsis is a genus of [[fungicolous-fungi]] in the order Hypocreales ([[ascomycota]]). Species of Hypocreopsis are obligate mycoparasites that grow on other fungi, particularly aphyllophorales and [[lichen]]-associated fungi. The genus is notable because its species cannot be cultured in vitro, making their physiology poorly understood. Hypocreopsis species produce distinctive stromatic fruiting bodies on or near their host fungi and are considered rare or threatened in many parts of their range.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Eukaryota
- **[[kingdom-fungi]]
- **Division:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Sordariomycetes
- **Order:** Hypocreales
- **Family:** Hypocreaceae (placement uncertain)
- **Genus:** Hypocreopsis

## Key Species

- **H. lichenoides** — Found on tree bark in association with species of Hymenochaete, especially H. tabacina. Recorded from central and northern Europe (Cauchon and Ouellette 1964; Niemelä and Nordin 1985). The species occurs with its Hymenochaete host on bark of living trees.
- **H. xylariicola** — Found on old perithecia of Xylaria species (Samuels 1988). Grows on carbonized stromatic tissue of dead Xylaria fruiting bodies.
- **H. rhododendri** — Associated with Hymenochaete corrugata on hazel and rhododendron.
- Several additional undescribed species are known from various hosts.

## Morphological Characteristics

- **Stromata:** Distinctive, often fan-shaped or irregular stromatic fruiting bodies that develop on the surface of host fungi or adjacent bark
- **Ascomata:** Perithecia embedded in the stroma, with ostioles opening to the surface
- **Ascospores:** Typically hyaline, septate, filiform to fusiform
- **Anamorph:** Not known for most species; the genus may lack a conidial stage entirely
- **Coloration:** Stromata range from brownish to dark colored, sometimes with zonate patterns

## Ecology and Habitat

- **Host association:** Obligately associated with specific host fungi:
  - Hymenochaete species (especially H. tabacina) for H. lichenoides
  - Xylaria species for H. xylariicola
  - Hymenochaete corrugata for H. rhododendri
- **Substrate:** Tree bark where host aphyllophorales grow; old stromatic tissue of pyrenomycetes
- **Distribution:** Primarily temperate regions; central and northern Europe, with some species in North America
- **Conservation status:** Considered rare or threatened in many areas due to dependence on old-growth forest habitats

## Mycoparasitic Behavior

- **Unculturable:** Hypocreopsis species cannot be grown axenically, making their physiology entirely unknown
- **Host specificity:** Strong host specificity is suggested by consistent associations with particular host genera
- **Parasitic strategy:** The exact nature of the interaction is unclear due to the inability to culture the fungus; may range from biotrophic parasitism to specialized saprotrophy on host tissue
- **Pleurocatena acicularis:** Can be found in association with Hypocreopsis and probably belongs to the same family (G. Arnold, personal communication)

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Conservation mycology:** Hypocreopsis species are indicator organisms for old-growth forest habitats and [[fungal-biodiversity]]
- **Biodiversity assessment:** Their presence signals healthy, undisturbed fungal communities
- **Taxonomic interest:** The genus occupies an uncertain position within the Hypocreales and may represent a distinct lineage
- **Physiological mystery:** The inability to culture Hypocreopsis makes it one of the more enigmatic genera among fungicolous fungi

## Key References

- Samuels (1988) — revision of fungicolous Hypocreales including H. xylariicola

- Læssøe (1989) — additional distribution data

## Notes

Hypocreopsis represents one of the more challenging genera for mycological study due to its unculturable nature and rarity. The genus is sometimes placed near the Hypocreaceae based on morphological similarity to Hypomyces, but molecular data are needed to confirm its phylogenetic position. Conservation efforts for Hypocreopsis species focus on preserving old-growth forest habitats where their host Hymenochaete and Xylaria species persist.
