## Pashaquilla Sensitiva (*Macrolobium acaciaefolium*)

## Description

Pashaquilla sensitiva is the Amazonian name for *Macrolobium acaciaefolium*, a leguminous plant in the Fabaceae family. In Amazonian vegetalismo, this plant is associated with amarres amorosos (love magic/love binding). When a vegetalista uses pashaquilla sensitiva to unite two lovers, they are said to remain faithful to each other for years. The plant's branches are inhabited by sylphs — spiritual beings who are experts in love magic. The plant is mentioned in Vision 41 (Pulsations) of Pablo Amaringo's *[[cordyceps-cancer-bioactive-compounds]] including alkaloids and flavonoids
- *Macrolobium* species are important components of Amazonian floodplain forests

## Ritual Logic

Pashaquilla sensitiva is significant because its medicinal action is described in relational rather than individual terms.

The plant is used to bind two people, stabilize affection, and preserve fidelity over time.

That makes it a plant of social medicine: the patient is a relationship, not a single body.

The presence of sylphs in the branches gives the plant an airy and communicative quality.

Love magic depends on messages, attraction, mood, memory, and subtle influence, all qualities associated with airy spirits.

The plant's name also suggests responsiveness.

Whether or not the species shows dramatic touch movement, the cultural label frames it as sensitive to contact and intention.

This is why the note belongs beside [[entheogenic-plants-and-psychoactive-flora]], and [[ayahuasca-preparation-pablo-amaringo-ucayali-shamanism]].

## Floodplain Ecology

The identification with *Macrolobium acaciaefolium* places pashaquilla sensitiva in Amazonian floodplain forest ecology.

Seasonal inundation shapes root structure, microbial associations, seed dispersal, and access for harvest.

Plants from igapó and várzea habitats often participate in nutrient pulses created by rising and falling water.

As a legume, the plant may contribute to nitrogen cycling in these wet forest margins.

Its ecological role therefore complements its magical role: both involve binding, exchange, and continuity.

In cultivation, any attempt to grow the plant outside its range would need to account for water rhythm, heat, and microbial partners.

A dry upland garden would not reproduce the conditions implied by the source context.
