# Psilocybe gallaeciae

**Psilocybe gallaeciae** is a bluing [[psilocybin]]-containing mushroom species in the family Hymenogastraceae. It was described from northwestern Spain (Galicia) and is one of the lesser-known European psilocybian fungi. The species is closely related to [[psilocybe-semilanceata]] and was for many years confused with it before being recognized as distinct.

## Taxonomy

- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Hymenogastraceae
- **Genus:** Psilocybe
- **Section:** Semilanceatae
- **Authority:** Castilla, Alonso & Garcia (2003)

The species epithet *gallaeciae* refers to Gallaecia, the ancient Roman name for the region of northwestern Iberia corresponding to modern Galicia in Spain. It was segregated from the [[psilocybe-semilanceata]] complex based on morphological and molecular evidence.

## Description

### Cap (Pileus)
- 5 to 20 mm in diameter
- Conic to campanulate (bell-shaped), often with a pronounced umbo
- Hygrophanous: ochraceous to brown when moist, drying to a pale straw color
- Surface smooth to slightly translucent-striate near the margin
- Often with a slight bluish tint at the base of the stipe where bruised

### Gills (Lamellae)
- Adnexed to sinuate attachment
- Color ranges from greyish to dark purplish-brown with maturity
- Subdecurrent, moderately crowded
- Edge typically whitish and fringed

### Stipe (Stem)
- 30 to 80 mm long, 1 to 2 mm thick
- Cylindrical, equal, sometimes slightly bulbous at the base
- Pale brown to whitish, darkening with age or handling
- Exhibits bluing reaction when bruised, especially near the base
- Partial veil absent or leaving only a faint fibrillose zone

### Spores
- Spore print: dark purplish-brown
- Spores: ellipsoid to slightly amygdaliform (almond-shaped)
- 11 to 14 × 6.5 to 8 µm
- Thick-walled with a distinct germ pore
- Q value (length/width ratio): approximately 1.5 to 1.8

## Distribution and Habitat

**Psilocybe gallaeciae** is endemic to northwestern Spain, primarily found in the autonomous community of Galicia. It has also been reported from northern Portugal, within the same biogeographic region.

### Habitat
- Grows in grasslands, pastures, and meadows
- Often found in association with rich, acidic soils in Atlantic climate zones
- Fruits in autumn (October to December) following cool, wet periods
- Typically found at elevations between 200 and 800 meters

### Associated Vegetation
- Frequently occurs in coastal and sub-coastal grasslands
- Found in areas dominated by heath (*Erica* spp.) and gorse (*Ulex* spp.)
- Sometimes associated with pastoral grasses in cattle-grazed fields

## Chemistry

Like other members of section Semilanceatae, **P. gallaeciae** contains the psychoactive indole alkaloids [[psilocybin]] and [[psilocin]], along with [[baeocystin]] as a minor constituent.

### Reported Alkaloid Levels
- [[psilocybin]]: approximately 0.2 to 0.8% dry weight
- [[psilocin]]: trace to 0.1% dry weight
- [[baeocystin]]: present in small amounts

The total tryptamine content is generally lower than that of [[psilocybe-semilanceata]], but sufficient to produce noticeable psychoactive effects.

## Similar Species

**Psilocybe gallaeciae** is most easily confused with the following species:

- **[[psilocybe-semilanceata]]** — the liberty cap; larger, more conical cap, larger spores, more potent. Found across a broader geographic range.
- **[[psilocybe-hispanica]]** — another Iberian bluing species; differs in microscopic features and distribution.
- **[[psilocybe-liniformans]]** — has a distinctive gelatinous partial veil; different spore morphology.
- **Galerina species** — potentially lethal look-alikes that lack bluing reactions; critical to distinguish in the field.

## Identification Notes

The key distinguishing features of **P. gallaeciae** from [[psilocybe-semilanceata]] include:
- Smaller overall size
- Slightly different spore dimensions and Q values
- Preference for the acidic Atlantic soils of Galicia
- Less pronounced umbo on the cap

Microscopic examination is typically required for definitive identification. DNA barcoding using the ITS region has proven useful for distinguishing members of the Semilanceatae section.

## Cultural and Ethnobotanical Significance

**P. gallaeciae** has no well-documented history of traditional use. Its psychoactive properties were discovered through modern chemical analysis rather than indigenous knowledge. The species is of primarily academic interest to mycologists studying the [[psilocybin]]-containing fungi of the Iberian Peninsula.

It is sometimes encountered inadvertently by foragers targeting [[psilocybe-semilanceata]] in northwestern Spain.

## Conservation Status

The species has not been formally evaluated for the IUCN Red List. Its limited known range in northwestern Iberia suggests it may be vulnerable to habitat loss from agricultural intensification and urbanization of coastal grasslands.

## Legal Status

Like all [[psilocybin]]-containing mushrooms, **P. gallaeciae** is controlled under drug legislation in most jurisdictions. In Spain, the collection and possession of psilocybian mushrooms is restricted.

## Ecological Role

As a saprotrophic grassland fungus, **P. gallaeciae** contributes to the decomposition of organic matter in pastoral ecosystems. It forms part of the diverse fungal communities associated with Atlantic grasslands, alongside numerous non-psychoactive agarics and other basidiomycetes.

The bluing reaction that characterizes psilocybian fungi is caused by the oxidation of psilocin, which forms when the enzyme phosphatase cleaves the phosphate group from psilocybin upon tissue damage.
