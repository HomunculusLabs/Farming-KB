# Peltigera leucophlebia

## Overview

*Peltigera leucophlebia* is a large foliose cyanolichen commonly known as the freckle pelt or speckled pelt lichen. It is a tripartite lichen associating with both a green algal photobiont and a cyanobacterial partner, *Nostoc*.

The species is recognized by its broad, pale grey to brown thallus with distinctive white veins on the lower surface and characteristic dark spots or "freckles" on the upper surface near the lobe tips. It is widespread across boreal and montane regions of the Northern Hemisphere.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Lecanoromycetes
- **Order:** Peltigerales
- **Family:** Peltigeraceae
- **Genus:** *Peltigera*
- **Species:** *P. leucophlebia* (Nyl.) Gyeln.

**Etymology:** The specific epithet *leucophlebia* derives from Greek *leuko-* (white) and *phlebos* (vein), referring to the prominent white veining on the thallus underside.

The thallus is large, reaching 10–30 cm in diameter, with broadly rounded lobes 1–3 cm wide. The upper surface is grey to greyish-brown, often with a powdery coating when dry, and develops distinctive dark spots near lobe margins.

The lower surface is white with a prominent network of raised veins (rhizines) that are pale to white. These veins distinguish it from the dark-veined *Peltigera canina* and related species.

Apothecia are common, borne on the upper surface near lobe margins, with red-brown discs 2–8 mm in diameter. The margins of the apothecia are covered with short, pale tomentum that matches the thallus surface color.

The cyanobacterial partner *Nostoc* is localized in dark cephalodia on the upper surface, appearing as dark rounded bumps 1–3 mm across. This tripartite association with both green algae and cyanobacteria is a key taxonomic feature.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Peltigera leucophlebia* has a circumboreal distribution across the Northern Hemisphere. It occurs throughout Canada, Alaska, the northern continental [[comparison-biological-nitrogen-fixation-vs-synthetic-fertilizer]], converting atmospheric N₂ into bioavailable ammonia. Nitrogen fixation rates in *Peltigera* species can reach 1–3 kg N per hectare per year in productive stands.

Nitrogen fixation activity is strongly temperature-dependent, with optimal rates occurring between 15–25°C. Activity ceases below 5°C and declines sharply above 30°C due to cyanobacterial thermal sensitivity.

Moisture is critical for [[antifungal-secondary-metabolites-coprophilous-fungi]]. These depsidones are detectable by thin-layer chromatography and serve as chemotaxonomic markers.

Spot test reactions are typically K−, C−, KC−, Pd+ yellow to orange. The chemistry is relatively simple compared to other *Peltigera* species that may contain peltigerin or other complex metabolites.

The species has documented use in traditional Chinese medicine as a treatment for digestive ailments and hemorrhoids. Preparations typically involve boiling dried thalli to extract [[query-what-are-the-main-bioactive-compounds-in-medicinal-plants]].

Indigenous peoples of northern Europe historically used *Peltigera* species as food during famines, though the lichens require extensive leaching to remove bitter triterpenes and lichen acids. They were sometimes mixed with flour in bread preparations.

## Similar Species

- **Peltigera canina** — dark veins on lower surface, lacks cephalodia, generally smaller thallus with more erect lobes
- **Peltigera membranacea** — larger thallus, dark rhizines, lacks the characteristic dark freckles near lobe tips
- **Peltigera apthosa** — similar tripartite morphology but with larger, more prominent cephalodia and a generally smoother upper surface
- **Peltigera horizontalis** — smaller, more closely appressed to substrate, with distinctive concentric ridges on the upper surface

The combination of white lower-surface veining, dark freckles on the upper surface, and the presence of cephalodia reliably distinguishes *P. leucophlebia* in the field.
