## Description

The Halles Centrales (Central Markets) of Paris served as the primary wholesale distribution hub for cultivated mushrooms in France during the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Market data collected from this venue provides the most comprehensive historical record of French mushroom production volume and pricing during the formative period of the commercial mushroom industry. These markets were the central point through which the enormous output of the Parisian cave mushroom gardens flowed to consumers and exporters.

The data collected from the Halles Centrales represents an extraordinary level of market transparency for an agricultural product of this era, providing researchers and historians with detailed monthly production figures, price data, and annual trend information spanning the critical period of 1899 through 1906 and beyond.

## Classification

- **Type**: Market / distribution center
- **Location**: Paris, France
- **Period of significance**: 1899-1911 (documented data)
- **Primary product**: Cultivated Agaricus campestris and related species
- **Distribution**: Fresh and canned mushrooms

## Key Facts

- Annual production passing through the Halles Centrales ranged from approximately 700,000 to 1,000,000 kilograms during 1899-1901, with an additional estimated equal volume not reaching the central markets.
- Wholesale prices averaged approximately 1.30-1.35 francs per kilogram during this period, with seasonal variation between 1.20 and 2.20 francs per kilogram.
- By 1911, the estimated total value of mushrooms passing through Paris markets reached approximately 7,500,000 francs wholesale (12,500,000 francs retail, approximately $2,500,000 USD at contemporary exchange rates).
- Monthly production data showed seasonal variation, with production ranging from roughly 270,000 kg to 400,000 kg per month, demonstrating year-round production capability.
- The amount passing through the central markets was believed to equal roughly half of total French mushroom output, with significant production consumed locally or exported without entering Paris markets.
- From 1901 to 1906, there was no material advance in production, suggesting the market had reached a plateau in its traditional form.
- The canned product was very largely exported, with approximately 3,000,000 pounds of canned mushrooms consumed in the US by 1900, rising to 4-5 million pounds by 1906.
- By 1907, the average wholesale price had risen to approximately two francs per kilogram, suggesting either increased demand or reduced supply.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Industry scale indicator**: The Halles Centrales data demonstrates the enormous scale of French mushroom production, which dwarfed all other national outputs during this period and set the template for industrial [[mushroom-cultivation]] worldwide.
- **Market dynamics**: The price and volume data reveal seasonal production patterns and the economic viability of mushroom cultivation as a commercial enterprise, providing benchmarks that guided investment decisions.
- **Export trade significance**: The substantial export of canned mushrooms to America both demonstrates the quality of French production and created the market awareness that eventually stimulated domestic US cultivation.
- **Year-round production**: The monthly data confirms that Paris-area cave facilities enabled nearly continuous production throughout the year, a key competitive advantage of subterranean cultivation that no other country could match at the time.
- **Historical benchmark**: These figures serve as a baseline for understanding the growth and development of the global mushroom industry from its French origins to its modern worldwide distribution.
