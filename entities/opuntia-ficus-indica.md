## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae → **Division:** Angiosperms → **Class:** Eudicots
- **Order:** Caryophyllales → **Family:** Cactaceae → **Subfamily:**
  Opuntioideae
- **Genus:** *Opuntia* → **Species:** *O. ficus-indica*
- The genus *Opuntia* contains 150–200 species; *O. ficus-indica* is the
  most economically important and widely cultivated.
- Long domesticated — exact wild progenitor debated but likely *O.
  streptacantha* or a similar central Mexican species; domestication dates to
  at least 6,500–9,000 years ago.
- Two main cultivar groups: pad-producing (e.g., 'Mojave', 'Carlitos') and
  fruit-producing (e.g., 'Gialla', 'Rossa', 'Bianca' in Sicily).

## Morphology

- **Growth habit:** Shrub-like or small tree-forming cactus, 1–5 m tall,
  with a defined trunk in older plants (up to 25 cm diameter).
- **Cladodes (pads):** Flat, oval to oblong, 30–60 cm long × 15–25 cm wide,
  1–3 cm thick; green to blue-green; covered in areoles with glochids (fine
  hair-like spines) and sometimes 1–3 larger spines per areole; pads persist
  1–3 years before senescing.
- **Spines:** Variable — cultivated varieties range from nearly spineless to
  heavily armed; glochids always present, causing dermatitis lasting 1–3
  days.
- **Flowers:** Large, 7–10 cm diameter, yellow, orange, or red, appearing in
  late spring to early summer; pericarpel bears areoles with glochids.
- **Fruits (tunas):** Ovoid to ellipsoid, 5–10 cm long, green, yellow,
  orange, or reddish-purple; sweet, juicy pulp with 100–300 seeds per fruit.
- **Roots:** Shallow but extensive lateral root system (95% within top 30
  cm); also develops adventitious roots from fallen cladodes.

## Distribution and Habitat

- **Native:** Central Mexico, cultivated since pre-Columbian times; depicted
  in [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-south-america]], and southwestern US.
- Thrives in arid and semi-arid climates with 200–800 mm annual rainfall;
  tolerates temperatures from -5°C to 50°C.
- Grows on rocky, shallow, saline, and alkaline substrates; pH tolerance
  5.0–8.5.
- Hardy to USDA zones 8b–11; brief freezes tolerated but sustained cold
  below -5°C causes tissue damage.

## Uses

- **Food — pads (nopales):** Young cladodes used raw in salads, cooked in
  stews (nopalitos), scrambled with eggs, or grilled; mucilaginous texture
