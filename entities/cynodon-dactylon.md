## Overview

**Cynodon dactylon**, commonly known as **bermudagrass**, **couch grass**, or **devil's grass**, is a [[savory-perennial-grass-and-desertification]] in the family Poaceae, subfamily Chloridoideae.

It is one of the most widely distributed and ecologically important grass species in tropical and subtropical regions worldwide.

The species is valued as a turfgrass, forage crop, and soil-stabilization plant, but is also regarded as one of the world's most troublesome agricultural weeds.

Its aggressive stoloniferous and rhizomatous growth habit allows it to form dense mats that can dominate diverse habitats.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

*Cynodon dactylon* was described by Carl Linnaeus in 1753 as *Panicum dactylon* and later transferred to the genus *Cynodon* by Louis Claude Marie Richard in 1806.

The genus name derives from the Greek *kyon* (dog) and *odous* (tooth), referring to the sharp-toothed inflorescence.

The species is a C4 perennial grass with prostrate to ascending stems 5–40 cm tall, though stolons may extend several meters.

Stems are slender, freely branching, and produce roots at the nodes, enabling rapid vegetative spread.

Leaves are linear, 2–15 cm long and 1–4 mm wide, with a prominent midrib and scabrous margins.

The ligule is a short ciliate membrane 0.5–1 mm long, a key character distinguishing it from related species.

Inflorescences are digitate, composed of 3–7 slender racemes 2–7 cm long arranged at the tip of the culm.

Spikelets are laterally compressed, 2–3 mm long, borne in two rows along one side of the rachis.

Each spikelet contains one fertile floret; the glumes are unequal, with the lower glume much shorter than the upper.

The extensive root system includes both fibrous roots from nodes and thickened rhizomes 5–15 cm below the soil surface.

## Distribution and Habitat

*C. dactylon* has a [[copelandia-cyanescens-pantropical-distribution-and-ecology]], occurring naturally across Africa, southern Asia, and southern Europe.

It has been introduced to the Americas, Australia, and [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-hawaii-pacific-islands]], and is now present on every continent except Antarctica.

The species occupies an extraordinarily wide ecological range, from sea level to over 2,000 meters in elevation.

It grows in sandy soils, heavy clays, saline soils, and disturbed sites including roadsides, cultivated fields, and urban areas.

Optimal growth occurs at temperatures of 25–35 °C with annual rainfall of 625–1,750 mm, but the species tolerates both drought and periodic flooding.

Bermudagrass is sensitive to frost and cold temperatures, with top growth killed at −2 °C and rhizomes damaged below −5 °C.

It is considered one of the world's ten worst weeds in tropical agriculture due to its ability to infest and suppress [[coleman-sunlight-and-winter-crop-growth-rates]].

## Cultivation

As a turfgrass, bermudagrass is established vegetatively using sprigs, plugs, or sod, as seed of common types has low and erratic germination.

Improved seeded cultivars have been developed with [[query-how-do-i-stratify-and-scarify-seeds-for-better-germination]] and more uniform turf quality for lawns and sports fields.

The species requires full sun for optimal growth and density; it thins significantly under shade of 30% or more.

Nitrogen fertilization at 150–300 kg/ha annually is recommended for maintained turf, with lower rates sufficient for general pasture.

Mowing height for turf applications ranges from 1.3–5 cm depending on use, with putting greens maintained at 3–6 mm using dwarf cultivars.

Irrigation requirements are moderate once established, as the deep root system provides good [[dighton-fungal-drought-tolerance-plant-water-relations]].

For forage production, the grass is typically rotationally grazed or harvested as hay with cutting intervals of 4–6 weeks.

Major turf pests include the bermudagrass mite (*Eriophyes cynodoniensis*), armyworms (*Spodoptera* spp.), and various [[duggar-mushroom-insect-pests-and-fungal-diseases]] such as dollar spot and spring dead spot.

## Uses and Applications

Bermudagrass is the most widely used warm-season turfgrass, valued for its drought tolerance, wear resistance, and rapid recovery from damage.

It is the primary turf species for golf courses, athletic fields, and lawns throughout the southern [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]] and tropical regions.
