## Curvularia

## Description

**Curvularia** is a genus of dematiaceous (darkly pigmented) fungi in the family Pleosporaceae. Species are widely distributed in soil, on plant debris, and as plant pathogens, endophytes, and opportunistic human pathogens. The genus is characterized by curved (geniculate) conidia, which give it its name. *Curvularia* species are of interest in agriculture as pathogens of cereal crops (particularly rice, wheat, and maize), in medicine as agents of phaeohyphomycosis and allergic fungal sinusitis, and in biotechnology for production of various enzymes and metabolites.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Eukaryota
- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** [[ascomycota]]
- **Class:** Dothideomycetes
- **Order:** Pleosporales
- **Family:** Pleosporaceae
- **Genus:** *Curvularia*

**Note:** The teleomorph (sexual) stage of many *Curvularia* species belongs to the genus *Cochliobolus*, and taxonomic revisions continue to reclassify species between these genera.

## Key Facts

- Over 80 recognized species worldwide
- Common in tropical and subtropical soils, especially warm, moist environments
- Produce characteristic curved, multi-celled conidia from geniculate (zigzag) conidiophores
- Important plant pathogens: *C. lunata* causes leaf spot on rice; *C. geniculata* affects various grasses
- Some species are endophytes that can benefit host plants by producing anti-herbivore compounds
- Opportunistic human pathogens causing sinusitis, keratitis (eye infections), and systemic mycoses
- *C. lunata* produces the mycotoxin cytochalasin B
- Melanin pigmentation contributes to environmental resilience and pathogenicity
- Some species show resistance to common agricultural fungicides

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

*Curvularia* is relevant in several mycological and cultivation contexts:

1. **Cereal Crop Pathology:** *Curvularia* species cause leaf spots, grain discoloration, and seed rot in rice, wheat, sorghum, and maize, resulting in significant yield losses in tropical and subtropical agriculture.

2. **Endophytic Interactions:** Some *Curvularia* species form endophytic associations with grasses, producing alkaloids and other secondary metabolites that can deter insect herbivores. This mirrors the endophyte-plant mutualism seen in *[[epichloe]]* systems.

3. **Mycotoxin Production:** *C. lunata* produces cytochalasin B and other toxic metabolites that contaminate grain, posing food safety concerns.

4. **Medical [[mycology]]:** As emerging human pathogens, *Curvularia* species are important in clinical mycology, particularly for immunocompromised patients. Melanized fungi (dematiaceous) present unique diagnostic and treatment challenges.

5. **Biocontrol Target:** Management of *Curvularia* diseases in agriculture involves seed treatment, crop rotation, and fungicide application, with ongoing research into biological control approaches.
