# Scorzonera

## Description

Scorzonera (*Scorzonera hispanica*), also known as black salsify or Spanish salsify, is a root vegetable cultivated for its black-skinned, white-fleshed taproot. In Eliot Coleman's [[winter-harvest-system]], scorzonera is listed among the storage crops that provide winter food alongside fresh harvest from cold houses and cool houses. It is a traditional European vegetable valued for its nutty, oyster-like flavor.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Family**: Asteraceae (daisy family)
- **Genus**: Scorzonera
- **Species**: *Scorzonera hispanica*
- **Common names**: Black salsify, Spanish salsify, viper's grass
- **Category**: Root vegetable / storage crop

## Key Facts

### Botanical Characteristics

Scorzonera is a perennial plant typically grown as an annual for its roots. The plant produces long, narrow, dark green leaves and a thick, black-skinned taproot with creamy white interior flesh. The roots can reach 12 to 18 inches in length at maturity. Yellow composite flowers are produced in the second year if roots are left in the ground.

### Culinary Properties

- **Flavor**: Often described as nutty, earthy, and reminiscent of oysters or artichoke hearts
- **Preparation**: Roots are peeled (or rubbed clean after cooking) and can be boiled, roasted, mashed, or added to soups and stews
- **Nutritional value**: Rich in inulin (a prebiotic fiber), iron, calcium, and vitamins B and E
- **Traditional use**: Long history in European cuisine, particularly in France, Italy, and Eastern Europe

### Growing Requirements

- **Soil**: Prefers deep, loose, well-drained soil for long, straight root development
- **Sowing**: Direct-seeded in spring; slow germinator (10-20 days)
- **Growing season**: Long season crop requiring 120-180 days to maturity
- **Hardiness**: Very cold-hardy; roots can overwinter in the ground in many climates
- **Spacing**: Thin to 3-4 inches apart in rows 12-18 inches apart

### Storage Quality

Scorzonera is included in Coleman's list of storage crops that sustain winter food supply:
- Stores well in root cellar conditions (cool, humid)
- Can be left in the ground through winter and harvested as needed
- Among the storage crops listed alongside onions, garlic, shallots, winter squash, celery root, parsley root, and storage beets

## Relevance to Cultivation

### Winter Food Security

As a storage crop, scorzonera contributes to year-round food availability during the [[persephone-period]] (days under 10 hours of daylight) when fresh greenhouse production slows. Combined with other storage crops, it helps bridge the gap between fresh harvest seasons.

### Market Garden Potential

Scorzonera is an underutilized crop in North American markets but has strong potential for direct market sales, restaurant sales, CSA shares, and winter sales when fresh local produce is scarce.

### Companion in Winter Harvest Systems

In Coleman's system, scorzonera represents one of several categories of winter food:
1. **Fresh from cold houses**: Spinach, mache, claytonia, carrots, leeks
2. **Fresh from cool houses**: Radishes, turnips, chard, watercress, arugula
3. **From storage**: Scorzonera, onions, garlic, shallots, squash, celery root, beets

### Cultural Heritage

Scorzonera connects to the broader tradition of European root vegetable cultivation that informed the [[french-maraicher-system]] and the Dutch [[cold-frame]] tradition. It exemplifies the diversity of crops that were once staples but have been largely forgotten in modern industrial food systems.
