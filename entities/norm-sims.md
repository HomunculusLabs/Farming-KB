## Description

Norm Sims is an Australian farmer cited by Bill Mollison in *Permaculture: A Designers' Manual* as a real-world example of dramatic yield improvement through integrated site design strategies. His farm, featured in the *Weekly Times* (5 January 1983), demonstrated how water management, land forming, and rotational grazing could multiply production while reducing inputs. Sims achieved a fourfold benefit by combining laser levelling, water reticulation, storage, recycling, and intensive rotational grazing.

## Classification

- **Category:** Person — Innovative farmer
- **Nationality:** Australian
- **Role:** Dairy farmer, land management innovator
- **Era:** Early 1980s (documented), Victoria, Australia

## Key Facts

- Operated a dairy farm in Victoria, Australia
- Expected to double production using half the irrigation water — a 4× benefit factor
- Achieved salinity reduction through improved water management
- Maintained pasture production even during severe drought conditions
- Reduced irrigation labor from six days to two days per cycle
- Managed 185 milking cows on just 24 hectares (approximately 7.7 cows/ha) — an exceptionally high stocking rate
- Implemented 26 paddocks grazed in a 21-day rotation (average 1.6 ha per paddock)
- Each paddock equipped with a trough water-point for cattle
- Featured in the *Weekly Times*, 5 January 1983

## Integrated Strategies Used

Sims combined the following strategies simultaneously to achieve his results:

1. **Laser levelling of fields** — Ensured even irrigation distribution, eliminating dry spots and waterlogging
2. **Water reticulation** — Piped water distribution system replacing flood irrigation
3. **Water storage and recycling** — Captured and reused runoff water
4. **Grazing rotation** — 21-day rotation across 26 paddocks for optimal pasture recovery
5. **Central access road** — Minimized soil compaction and improved farm logistics
6. **Concentrated rations grown on-farm** — Reduced external feed dependence
7. **Pasture area reduction** — Counterintuitively reduced grazing area to optimize the watering regime on remaining land

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

Sims' farm is cited by Mollison as proof that the primary increases in crop yield do not come from varietal selection alone (as promoted by seed companies and agricultural extension), but from **attention to site design and development** followed by wise enterprise selection. His integrated approach demonstrates:

- Water management is foundational — storing, recycling, and distributing water efficiently underpins all other strategies
- Small paddock sizes with intensive rotational grazing outperform extensive continuous grazing
- Multiple strategies interact synergistically — the total benefit exceeds the sum of individual improvements
- Site design is a permanent investment that pays dividends indefinitely, unlike annual inputs
- Counterintuitive strategies (reducing pasture area) can improve total system performance

## Design Lessons

- Laser levelling + water recycling = 4× benefit (double production, half the water)
- Stocking density can be dramatically increased with proper rotation and water infrastructure
- Drought resilience comes from system design, not from larger water allocations
- Labor efficiency improves as a direct consequence of good design
