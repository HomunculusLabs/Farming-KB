# Trichoderma harzianum

**Type:** Species (Fungus)
**Category:** Biocontrol Agent / Saprotrophic Ascomycete
**Also known as:** T. harzianum Rifai

## Overview
Trichoderma harzianum is a filamentous saprotrophic fungus widely used as a
biological control agent against plant pathogenic fungi. It is one of the
most studied and commercially applied biocontrol fungi in agriculture,
horticulture, and forestry. Species within the Trichoderma genus are
characterized by rapid growth, prolific spore production, and strong
antagonistic activity against a broad spectrum of plant pathogens.

## Taxonomy and Classification
- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Sordariomycetes
- **Order:** Hypocreales
- **Family:** Hypocreaceae
- **Genus:** Trichoderma
- **Species:** T. harzianum (alongside T. viride, T. koningii,
  T. reesei, T. atroviride, and others)

## Key Details

### Morphology
- Fast-growing, green-spored mold forming dense white colonies that
  turn green as conidia mature
- Conidiophores are branched, bearing phialides that produce
  ellipsoidal conidia (asexual spores)
- T. viride and T. atroviride are closely related species with
  similar colony morphology
- Grows rapidly on a wide range of substrates

### Biocontrol Mechanisms
- **Mycoparasitism:** Directly attacks and parasitizes pathogenic
  fungi through hyphal coiling, penetration, and lysis of host cells
- **Antibiosis:** Produces secondary metabolites and enzymes
  (chitinases, glucanases, proteases) that degrade fungal cell walls
- **Competition:** Outcompetes pathogens for space and nutrients on
  root surfaces and in soil
- **Induced resistance:** Triggers systemic defense responses in host
  plants (priming)

### Species in the Trichoderma Complex
- T. koningii is a good biological control agent for damping-off
  diseases (Dighton and Mason, 1985)
- T. viride is effective against soil-borne pathogens and has been
  studied for radiocesium accumulation (Witkamp, 1968)
- T. reesei is an industrial cellulase producer with a fully
  sequenced genome
- T. album and T. favovirens have documented interactions with soil
  microarthropods

### Ecological Interactions
- Interacts with soil microarthropods: collembola selectively feed
  on Trichoderma, Pythium, and Cladosporium (Dighton, 1985)
- Nematodes show increased reproduction when Trichoderma is
  available as food, suggesting trophic cascade effects
- In mixed soil communities, diffusion is the primary mechanism for
  nutrient acquisition (Olsson and Jennings, 1991)

### Applications in Agriculture
- Used as seed treatment, soil drench, and foliar spray
- Effective against Rhizoctonia, Fusarium, Pythium, Sclerotinia, and
  other soil-borne pathogens
- Compatible with many [[mycorrhizal-fungi]] in [[integrated-pest-management]]
- Available commercially as formulations of conidia or chlamydospores

### Soil and Environmental Role
- Contributes to organic matter decomposition and nutrient cycling
- Produces cellulases and other hydrolytic enzymes for plant litter
  breakdown
- Can accumulate radiocesium, relevant to radioactive contamination
  scenarios

## Relevance
Trichoderma harzianum represents the gold standard for biological disease
control in sustainable agriculture. In permaculture and organic farming,
it offers a natural alternative to synthetic fungicides, protecting crops
while maintaining soil health. Its compatibility with mycorrhizal fungi
makes it suitable for integrated soil management strategies that leverage
multiple beneficial fungal groups simultaneously.
