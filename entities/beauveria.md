# Beauveria

**Beauveria** is a genus of [[singh-entomopathogenic-fungi-biocontrol-agents]] in the family
Cordycipitaceae (order Hypocreales) that infect and kill insects across a
remarkably broad [[powdery-mildews-erysiphaceae-diversity-host-range]]. The genus was erected by Vuillemin in 1912 and
named in honor of the French physician and naturalist Jean Beauverie. The type
species, *B. bassiana*, is the most commercially successful entomopathogenic
fungus worldwide and has been documented against over 700 insect species. A
2011 molecular phylogenetic revision by Rehner & Humber resolved the *B.
bassiana* species complex into multiple [[cryptic-species]].

The original basionym was *Botrytis bassiana* described by Balsamo-Crivelli in
1888, itself based on Agostino Bassi's 1835 work identifying the fungus as the
cause of muscardine disease in silkworms — one of the earliest demonstrations
of germ theory, predating Pasteur and Koch. The teleomorph has been linked to
*Cordyceps* species but [[fungal-life-cycles-sexual-reproduction-metamorphosis]] is rare; most propagation is clonal
via hydrophobic conidia.

## Major Species

The modern taxonomy recognizes approximately 10 species following the 2011
multilocus phylogenetic revision:

- ***B. bassiana*** (Bals.-Criv.) Vuill. — the type species; broadest host
  range (700+ insect species), most widely used in commercial biopesticides;
  strain GHA is the active ingredient in BotaniGard and Mycotrol
- ***B. brongniartii*** (Sacc.) Petch — originally described from the
  European cockchafer (*Melolontha melolontha*); more specialized on soil-
  dwelling coleopteran pests; commercial product Boverol
- ***B. pseudobassiana*** Rehner & Humber — cryptic species separated from
  *B. bassiana* by molecular phylogeny; common in temperate soils
- ***B. caledonica***, ***B. malawiensis***, ***B. asiatica***, ***B. australis***,
  ***B. varroae*** (associated with varroa mites), and ***B. sungii*** —
  additional species with geographic or host-specific distributions

## Morphology

On potato dextrose agar, colonies are initially white, becoming cream to pale
yellow with a woolly to powdery surface. Growth is moderate (15-25 mm at 25C
over 10 days). Conidia are the key diagnostic feature: globose to subglobose
in *B. bassiana* (2-3 um diameter), produced singly on a zigzag (geniculate)
rachis. *B. brongniartii* has smaller, ellipsoidal conidia (2-3 x 1-1.5 um).
Conidia are hyaline, unicellular, and hydrophobic — adapted for dispersal and
cuticle adhesion. Some species form synnemata (erect bundled conidiophore
columns) under certain conditions. The characteristic symptom of infection is
"white muscardine" — dead insects covered in a white layer of sporulating
[[mycelium]].

## Infection Mechanism

Infection follows six stages: (1) **Attachment** — hydrophobic conidia contact
the insect cuticle via hydrophobin-mediated adhesion; (2) **Germination** —
conidia germinate within 12-24h at >85% RH, forming appressoria; (3)
**Penetration** — appressoria secrete proteases (Pr1, Pr2 subtilisins),
chitinases, and lipases that degrade the cuticle, aided by mechanical
pressure; (4) **Blastospore proliferation** — yeast-like cells multiply in the
hemocoel, evading immunity via antioxidant production; (5) **Death** — toxins
(beauvericin, bassianolide, oosporein) and nutrient depletion kill the insect
in 3-7 days; (6) **Sporulation** — white mycelium erupts through the cadaver
and produces conidia that disperse to infect new hosts.
