It grows at elevations from sea level to approximately 1,600 meters.

Disturbed ground is frequently colonized, making it a pioneer in secondary successional habitats.

Plants often appear in urban and semi-urban settings, including lawns, parks, and railway embankments.

## Pollination Biology

Ophrys apifera is a textbook example of sexual deception in plant-pollinator interactions.

The labellum produces [[bloomfield-fungal-sex-pheromones-mating-communication]] of female bees, primarily Andrena nigroaenea.

Male bees are attracted to the flower and attempt pseudocopulation with the labellum.

During this process, pollinia attach to the bee's head and are transferred to the next flower visited.

However, the primary pollinator Andrena nigroaenea is absent from much of northern Europe.

In these regions, Ophrys apifera relies almost exclusively on autonomous self-pollination.

The pollinia gradually bend forward under their own weight and contact the stigma without any external agent.
