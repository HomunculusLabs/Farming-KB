# Picea abies

## Overview

**Picea abies**, the Norway spruce, is a large evergreen conifer in the
family Pinaceae native to northern, central, and eastern Europe. It is one
of the most economically important tree species in European forestry.

Norway spruce dominates the boreal and montane forests of Scandinavia, the
Alps, and the Carpathians. Its rapid [[soil-temperature-plant-growth-rate-doubling-rule]] and versatile timber have
made it the principal plantation species across much of Europe.

The species typically reaches 35–55 meters in height, with exceptional
specimens exceeding 60 meters. It is the tallest native European tree.

Its conical crown, drooping branchlets, and reddish-brown bark make it a
distinctive and widely recognized conifer, particularly as a Christmas
tree species.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:** Plantae → Pinophyta → Pinopsida → Pinales → Pinaceae →
*Picea* → *Picea abies* (L.) H.Karst.

The species was originally described as *Pinus abies* by Linnaeus in 1753
and later transferred to the genus *Picea* by Karsten in 1881.

Norway spruce is an evergreen reaching 35–55 m, with a trunk diameter of
0.6–1.5 m. The crown is conical and densely branched, with characteristically
pendulous or weeping branch tips.

Bark is thin, scaly, and reddish-brown to grey-brown, often flaking in
small round plates. Young bark is smooth and greyish, becoming rough and
fissured with age.

Needles are solitary (not in fascicles), 12–25 mm long, quadrangular in
cross-section, and dark green. Each needle persists for 6–9 years, longer
than most pines. They are sharply pointed and radiate from all sides of
the twig.

Seed cones are cylindrical, 10–18 cm long, hanging pendulously from upper
branches. They are green when young and turn brown at maturity. Seeds are
dark brown, 4–5 mm, with a pale brown wing of 10–14 mm.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Picea abies* is native to a broad band across Europe, from the Scandinavian
Peninsula and the British Isles eastward through the Baltics, Poland, the
Czech Republic, and the Balkans to the Ural Mountains.

The southern limit follows the mountains of central and southern Europe,
including the Alps, Carpathians, Apennines, and Balkan ranges. Altitudinal
range spans from near sea level in Scandinavia to approximately 2,100 meters
in the central Alps.

It has been widely planted for forestry since the 16th century and is
now the most planted tree species in Germany and many [[psilocybe-bohemica-central-european]]
countries.

The species prefers deep, moist, well-drained acidic soils. It is less
drought-tolerant than Scots pine and sensitive to late spring frosts.

## Ecology and Mycorrhizal Relationships

Norway spruce forms [[ectomycorrhizal-associations]] with a diverse fungal
community. Over 100 species have been documented, including *Laccaria
laccata*, *[[paxillus-involutus]]*, *[[cenococcum-geophilum]]*, and numerous
Cortinarius species.

The genus *Cortinarius* is particularly species-rich in spruce forests,
with many species showing strong host preference for *Picea*. These
associations are critical for [[comparison-nutrient-cycling-vs-nutrient-dense-gardening]] in boreal forest soils.
