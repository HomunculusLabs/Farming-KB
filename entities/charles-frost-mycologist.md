# Charles Frost

## Description

Charles Frost was an American mycologist based in Vermont, active during the 1870s. He was described by Julius Auboineau Palmer as 'a living authority in this science,' placing him among the small number of recognized American experts on fungi during the late 19th century.

Frost represents the tradition of gentleman-naturalists who contributed to American mycological knowledge through dedicated field study and observation, despite the relative scarcity of institutional support for fungal science in the United States at that time.

## Classification

- **Nationality:** American
- **Location:** Vermont, New England
- **Era:** 1870s
- **Field:** Mycology
- **Role:** Field mycologist, authority on New England fungi

## Key Facts

- Recognized by Palmer as a 'living authority' in mycological science
- Based in Vermont, contributing to study of New England fungi alongside Palmer's Boston-area work
- One of few American authorities cited by Palmer, alongside Dr. Curtis and Mr. Ravenal of South Carolina
- His recognition indicates extensive field experience and respected observations
- Represented the Vermont and northern New England tradition of mycological study
- His work likely documented species found in Vermont's higher elevations and colder climates

## Role in American Mycology

Frost's inclusion in Palmer's list of American authorities highlights the decentralized nature of American mycology in the 19th century. Unlike England with Kew Gardens, American mycologists worked independently, connected through correspondence and publications. Palmer's recognition of a Vermont-based mycologist suggests Frost's work was known through published observations or shared collections.

## Regional Context

Vermont's forests and pastures provided rich territory for fungal study. The state's mix of deciduous and coniferous forests, combined with agricultural landscapes, supported diverse fungal flora. Vermont's colder climate and higher elevations host species adapted to those conditions, potentially different from coastal Massachusetts.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Frost's work contributes to the historical record of New England fungal distribution. Modern mycologists studying changes in fungal populations may find value in comparing Frost's observations with current species distributions in Vermont. His recognition as an authority illustrates the importance of regional expertise in mycology that remains relevant today.
