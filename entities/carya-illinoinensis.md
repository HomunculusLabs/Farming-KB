It is the most commercially important hickory species, cultivated
primarily for its edible nuts, which are rich in monounsaturated fats,
antioxidants, and minerals.

Pecan holds significant cultural importance for Indigenous peoples of
the southeastern US, who cultivated and traded the nuts for millennia
before European contact. Today it is the state tree of Texas and a
multibillion-dollar crop in the southern US, with major plantings in
Georgia, New Mexico, Texas, and Arizona.

## Taxonomy

Magnoliophyta → Magnoliopsida → **Fagales** → **Juglandaceae** → **Juglandoideae** → **Caryeae** → ***Carya*** → ***C. illinoinensis***.

Authority: Friedrich von Wangenheim (1787). The species epithet references
the Illinois River region where the species was first scientifically
described. The common name "pecan" derives from the Algonquin word
*paccan*, meaning a nut requiring a stone to crack.

The genus *Carya* contains approximately 17–19 species of hickories
native to [[neurotropic-fungi-japan-east-asia-species-cultural-context]]. *C. illinoinensis* is the only
commercially cultivated species on a large scale.

## Morphology

- **Size**: Large deciduous tree, 20–40 m tall with a trunk diameter
  of 0.6–2.0 m. Crown is broad and spreading, 15–25 m wide at maturity.

- **Bark**: Gray-brown, developing deep longitudinal fissures and
  vertical ridges with age. Young bark is smooth and brown.

- **Leaves**: Pinnately compound, 30–50 cm long, with 9–17 leaflets.
  Leaflets are lanceolate, 5–12 cm long, serrate margins, dark green
  above and paler below. Yellow fall color.

- **Flowers**: Monoecious; male catkins 8–15 cm, pendulous, in clusters
  of 3. Female flowers are terminal spikes, 2–4 per cluster. Wind-
  pollinated; pollen is shed before or during leaf emergence.

- **Fruit**: Drupe-like nut (technically a nutlet within a husk),
  oblong-ellipsoid, 3–5 cm long. Husk splits at maturity to release
  the thin-shelled nut. Kernel is golden-brown, sweet, and oily.

## Distribution and Habitat

Coast of Texas, and from western Kentucky to northern Mexico (Coahuila,
Chihuahua). The native range roughly follows the Mississippi River
