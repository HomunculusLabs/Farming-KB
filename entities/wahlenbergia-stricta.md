The plant grows 20–60 cm tall from a thickened, often tuberous rootstock. Stems are slender, erect, branching above, and typically glabrous or sparsely hairy.

Basal leaves are obovate to lanceolate, 1–5 cm long, forming a rosette that may persist or wither at flowering. Stem leaves are smaller, narrower, and alternate.

Flowers are solitary or in loose terminal racemes. Each flower is 12–20 mm in diameter with five broadly spreading petals forming a star shape, typically blue to pale mauve, occasionally white.

The fruit is an ovoid capsule, 5–8 mm long, opening by three valves near the apex. Seeds are numerous, small (0.3–0.5 mm), ellipsoid, and light brown with a smooth surface.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Wahlenbergia stricta* occurs throughout eastern Australia, from northern Queensland through New South Wales, the Australian Capital Territory, Victoria, and into southeastern South Australia. It also extends to Tasmania.

The species occupies a wide range of habitats including grasslands, open woodlands, forest margins, roadsides, and disturbed areas. It is particularly common in moist, fertile sites such as creek banks and drainage lines.

Elevational range extends from sea level to approximately 1,500 meters in the southern tablelands and alpine foothills. Populations in the Australian Alps are often more robust and taller than lowland forms.

The species tolerates a range of soil types from sandy loams to heavy clays. It prefers moderately fertile, moist but well-drained soils and benefits from occasional inundation.

*Wahlenbergia stricta* is considered a weed of lawns and pastures in some regions. However, it is not listed as a declared weed in any Australian state and is generally regarded as a benign component of native grasslands.
