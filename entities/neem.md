# Neem

**Type:** Species (Plant)
**Category:** Multipurpose arid-zone medicinal tree
**Also known as:** [[azadirachta-indica]], Indian lilac, nimtree, margosa tree

## Overview
Neem is a deep-rooted, broad-leaved evergreen tree native to the dry forests
of India through to Burma, considered potentially among the most valuable of
all arid land trees. Every part of the neem tree has been used in traditional
medicine, agriculture, and daily life for thousands of years. Its seeds and
leaves contain azadirachtin, a powerful natural insecticide that has become
globally important in organic pest management. The wood resembles Cuban
mahogany and resists both decay and insect attack.

## Key Details

### Botanical Classification
- **Family:** Meliaceae
- **Genus:** Azadirachta
- **Species:** A. indica

### Growth Characteristics
- **Habit:** Usually evergreen; may lose leaves during severe drought
- **Root system:** Deep taproot; can extract nutrients from heavily leached
  sandy soils
- **Coppicing:** Coppices readily; produces straight, strong poles

### Environmental Requirements
- **Rainfall:** Optimal 450-1150 mm annual; tolerates as low as 130 mm
- **Temperature:** Tolerates great heat; not frost hardy
- **Soil:** Grows on most soils including dry, stony, clay, and shallow
  soils; not tolerant of seasonally waterlogged soils (taproot rots) or
  deep dry sands below 18 m water table
- **Soil pH:** Optimum 6.2; will grow at pH 5 and actually brings soil
  toward neutral through leaf litter
- **Salinity:** Not tolerant of saline soils

### Key Compounds
- **Azadirachtin:** Potent natural insecticide found in seeds and leaves;
  disrupts insect hormone systems, feeding, and reproduction without harming
  beneficial insects
- Used similarly to compounds from the related Melia azedarach

### Wood Properties
- Similar appearance and properties to Cuban mahogany
- Resists decay and insect attack; tougher than teak
- Termite attack is rare
- Excellent for construction and furniture making
- High calorific value as firewood, approaching that of coal

### Traditional Uses (India)
- **Insect repellent:** Dried leaves placed in books, grain, and clothes
- **Dental hygiene:** Twigs used as toothbrushes
- **Medicinal bark:** Decoction for fevers, nausea, liver complaints, and
  jaundice; poultice for wounds and skin diseases
- **Bark oil:** Rub for rheumatism
- **Fruit:** Steeped or crushed for urinary complaints, piles, and worms;
  considered a purgative and emollient
- **Seed oil:** Applied as cure for skin diseases
- **Sacred groves:** One of five trees planted in traditional Indian sacred
  groves alongside mango and Ficus religiosa

### Propagation
- By seed; seed is very short-lived (2-3 weeks viability)
- Seedlings killed by frost and fire; require weed-free establishment

## Relevance
Neem is perhaps the single most important tree species for integrated pest
management in tropical and subtropical [[permaculture]] systems. Azadirachtin
provides effective, biodegradable insect control without the ecological
damage of synthetic pesticides. Its extreme drought tolerance, soil-improving
qualities, and valuable timber make it a cornerstone species for sustainable
dryland agriculture.
