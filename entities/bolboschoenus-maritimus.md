## Overview

**Bolboschoenus maritimus**, commonly known as sea clubrush or saltmarsh clubrush, is a perennial sedge with a [[guzman-allen-gartz-psilocybe-semilanceata-temperate-cosmopolitan-distribution]].

It inhabits coastal salt marshes, inland saline wetlands, and freshwater margins across much of the globe.

The species was formerly classified in the genus **Scirpus** and is still widely referred to as *Scirpus maritimus* in older literature.

It is ecologically significant as a primary producer in estuarine and lacustrine wetland systems.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Bolboschoenus maritimus (L.) Palla was transferred from Scirpus by Karl Palla in 1888.

The genus Bolboschoenus is distinguished by tuber-producing rhizomes and pedunculate spikelet clusters.

Plants grow 50–150 cm tall from a network of horizontal rhizomes bearing chains of tubers.

Culms are trigonous, stiff, and 3–7 mm thick at the base.

Leaf blades are 4–10 mm wide, flat, and typically shorter than the culms.

The inflorescence is a compound anthela with 1–3 primary rays bearing dense head-like clusters of spikelets.

Spikelets are ovoid to ellipsoid, 8–15 mm long, brown or reddish-brown.

Glumes are 3.5–5 mm long, broadly ovate, with a green midrib and hyaline margins.

Achenes are obovate, biconvex, 2.5–3 mm long, brown, and smooth to finely reticulate.

Tubers are ovoid, 1–3 cm in diameter, white to yellowish, and rich in starch.

## Distribution and Habitat

Bolboschoenus maritimus has a nearly cosmopolitan distribution, occurring in Europe, Asia, Africa, Australia, and the Americas.

In [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]], it ranges from coastal Alaska to California and across the continent to the Atlantic seaboard.

It is common in coastal salt marshes, brackish ditches, and estuarine margins.

Inland populations occupy alkaline and saline wetlands, including playas and prairie potholes.

The species also occurs in freshwater margins of lakes, rivers, and irrigation canals.

It tolerates a wide range of salinities, from fresh to hypersaline conditions.

Elevation ranges from sea level to approximately 2,000 m in interior basins of western North America.

## Ecology

Bolboschoenus maritimus is a clonal species that spreads aggressively through rhizomatous growth and tuber production.

Tubers serve as both storage organs and vegetative propagules, enabling rapid colonization of disturbed sites.

The species forms dense monospecific stands that can exclude other wetland vegetation.

It provides habitat and food for waterfowl, especially during autumn migration when tubers are an important food source.

Muskrats and other semi-aquatic mammals consume both tubers and culm bases.

The dense root network stabilizes sediments and reduces erosion in coastal and riparian zones.

Bolboschoenus maritimus is an indicator species for moderately saline wetland conditions.

## Ethnobotany and Uses

The starchy tubers of Bolboschoenus maritimus have been consumed by Indigenous peoples in several regions.

[[halpern-sacramental-peyote-and-native-american-church]] groups in the Great Basin and California harvested and roasted tubers as a carbohydrate source.

In parts of Asia, the tubers are collected as a famine food and occasionally sold in local markets.
