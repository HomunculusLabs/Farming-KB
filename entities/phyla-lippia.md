# Phyla (Lippia)

## Description

Phyla (formerly classified as Lippia) is a genus of low-growing, mat-forming perennial plants in the Verbenaceae family. In [[integrated-pest-management]] within windbreak-protected crop systems.
- **Soil building**: As a dense ground cover, it adds organic matter to sandy soils through leaf turnover and [[root-exudates]], gradually improving soil structure and water-holding capacity.
- **Foot traffic tolerance**: Can withstand moderate foot traffic, making it suitable for pathways and living mulch in orchard alleys.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

### Cultivation Relevance

Phyla serves multiple functions in permaculture and agricultural systems:

- **Erosion control**: The primary permaculture application is as a ground cover that prevents soil and sand loss from wind erosion, which can reach 100 tonnes per hectare per day in exposed coastal sites.
- **Windbreak foundation**: As the lowest tier in compound windbreak systems, it reduces wind speed at ground level and prevents undercutting of taller shrub and tree plantings.
- **Living mulch**: Dense mats suppress weeds and moderate soil temperature, reducing evaporation from the soil surface.
- **Nitrogen contribution**: While not a nitrogen fixer itself, Phyla supports the soil microbial community that enhances nitrogen cycling when planted in association with leguminous windbreak species (Acacia, Leucaena, Prosopis).
- **Integrated design**: Works synergistically with taller windbreak species — the ground cover prevents soil loss while shrubs ([[echium-fastuosum]], Coprosma repens) and trees (Lycium, Cedrus, Cupressus) provide vertical wind filtering.

### Mycological Relevance

- The dense, moist microclimate created by Phyla ground cover supports arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) fungal networks in the rhizosphere, benefiting adjacent crop plants and trees.
- Organic matter accumulation under Phyla mats provides substrate for saprophytic soil fungi, contributing to nutrient cycling in otherwise sandy, low-fertility coastal soils.
- In integrated systems, Phyla ground cover helps maintain soil moisture levels that favor beneficial fungal populations while protecting mushroom beds or inoculated woodchip areas from wind desiccation.

## Design Integration

- Plant as the ground layer in Mollison's Type C compound windbreak for eroding beaches.
- Combine with Convolvulus and Mesembryanthemum for diverse ground cover; mid-layer with Echium fastuosum, Coprosma repens; canopy with Lycium, Cedrus, Cupressus.
- Spacing: Plant at 30–50 cm intervals for rapid coverage; spreads to fill gaps within one season.
- Tolerates mowing and can be maintained as a low lawn substitute between crop rows.
- Useful as a living mulch in subtropical orchards where wind protection and weed suppression are both priorities.
