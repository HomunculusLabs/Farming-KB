## Lepiota cristata (Crested Lepiota)

## Description

Lepiota cristata is a small, common species of Lepiota recognised by its distinctive concentric rings of small brownish scales on a white satiny cap surface, giving it a crested or coronet-like appearance. Despite being commonly considered suspect due to its disagreeable smell and unpleasant taste, it is not actually poisonous.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Agaricaceae
- **Genus:** Lepiota
- **Species:** L. cristata

## Morphology

The cap is at first campanulate (bell-shaped) and covered with a brownish or reddish-brown cuticle. As the cap becomes convex, the cuticle breaks up except in the centre where there is usually a boss (small raised area), and shows as concentric circles of small scales that appear encrusted in a white satiny surface. Average cap width is approximately 1.5 inches; stem height about 5.5 inches; stem width about 3/16 inch.

The gills are white or whitish, crowded, thin and attached to a sort of collarette which separates them from the stem. The slender hollow stem is white and silky, often tinged with pink or yellow especially towards the base, from which white branching mycelial fibres spread into the soil. The ring is membranous, white and fugaceous (soon disappearing).

## Key Facts

- Cap has distinctive concentric circles of small brownish scales on white background
- Centre of cap usually has a prominent boss
- Gills are separated from the stem by a collarette structure
- Ring is fugaceous (quickly disappearing)
- Strong, disagreeable smell and unpleasant taste
- White branching mycelial fibres spread from the base into soil
- Grows from spring to autumn in grassy places

## Toxicity

Despite long being considered suspect, Lepiota cristata is **not poisonous**. The disagreeable smell and unpleasant taste that led to its suspect status are sensory qualities rather than indicators of toxicity. Ramsbottom explicitly states: "It is not poisonous." However, it should still be avoided due to its unpalatable qualities and because of the difficulty of distinguishing it from related Lepiota species that may be genuinely toxic.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Taxonomic marker species:** Common and widespread, serving as a reference species for the genus Lepiota
- **Identification education:** The distinctive concentric scale pattern makes it an excellent teaching species for fungal morphology
- **Cautionary example:** Demonstrates that smell and taste, while useful identification characters, are not reliable indicators of toxicity
- **Lookalike hazard:** Closely resembles Lepiota helveola and related species that do contain amatoxins — illustrates the critical importance of precise identification
- **Ecological role:** Found in grassy places across multiple seasons, suggesting a saprobic ecological strategy
- **Collarette structure:** The unusual gill attachment via a collarette is a diagnostic feature

## Habitat and Ecology

Grows from spring to autumn in grassy places. Common and widespread. White branching mycelial fibres at the base suggest an active relationship with the soil substrate. The extended fruiting season from spring through autumn indicates environmental flexibility.

## Historical Notes

Illustrated on Plate 6 of Ramsbottom's "Poisonous Fungi" (1945) alongside Lepiota helveola. The species epithet "cristata" refers to the crested or coronet-like appearance of the concentric scale rings on the cap.
