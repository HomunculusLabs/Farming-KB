## Fish Meal

## Description
Fish meal is a dry natural fertilizer made from ground fish parts that have been heated and dried. It carries an N-P-K ratio of approximately 10-6-2 and is notable for being one of the best natural sources of **phosphorus** among fish-based fertilizers, while also providing substantial nitrogen. Unlike fish emulsion, fish meal is not readily soluble and acts as a moderate-release fertilizer, with nutrients available for 1 to 4 months. It serves as an important phosphorus source [[query-what-is-the-role-of-trichoderma-in-organic-growing]] systems where rock phosphate and bone meal are the primary alternatives.

## Classification
- **Category**: Natural/Organic Fertilizer (Dry)
- **[[plant-primary-nutrients-and-mineral-sources]]**: Nitrogen (N) and Phosphorus (P)
- **N-P-K**: 10-6-2
- **Source**: Animal by-product (fish parts)
- **Form**: Dry meal
- **Availability**: Moderate-release (not readily soluble)
- **Duration**: 1 to 4 months

## Key Facts

### Nutrient Profile
Fish meal provides a dual benefit of high nitrogen (10%) and significant phosphorus (6%), with some potassium (2%):
- **Nitrogen (10%)**: Supports vegetative growth, protein synthesis, and chlorophyll production
- **Phosphorus (6%)**: Supports root development, flowering, fruiting, and energy transfer (ATP/ADP)
- **Potassium (2%)**: Supports water regulation and enzyme activation

### Phosphorus Significance
The 6% phosphorus content distinguishes fish meal from other fish-based fertilizers:
- **Fish emulsion** (5-2-2): Lower phosphorus relative to nitrogen
- **Fish powder** (12-0.25-1): Very low phosphorus
- **Hydrolyzed fish** (4-2-2): Moderate phosphorus
- **Fish meal** (10-6-2): Highest phosphorus among fish products

This makes fish meal particularly valuable when both nitrogen and phosphorus supplementation are needed.

### Processing Method
Fish meal is produced by grinding fish parts (frames, heads, and by-products), then heating and drying them. The heat processing:
- Concentrates nutrients by removing water
- Denatures proteins, making them partially available
- Unfortunately destroys some heat-sensitive enzymes, proteins, vitamins, and micronutrients

### Application
- Mixed into soil at planting time
- Applied as a side dressing during the growing season
- Worked into the root zone of established plants

### Warnings and Practical Considerations
1. **Odor**: Fish meal smells for a few days after application, though not as strongly as fish emulsion
2. **Attracts wildlife**: Can attract flies, bears, and dogs
3. **Heat processing losses**: Some nutritional value is lost during manufacturing compared to [[fish-hydrolysate]] products

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

### Phosphorus for Root Development
Phosphorus is critical for root development, energy transfer, and reproductive growth. Fish meal provides organic phosphorus that is released gradually through microbial decomposition, supporting sustained root growth throughout the season.

### Mycorrhizal Support
[[phosphorus-availability]] influences [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-colonization-patterns]]. When phosphorus is readily available, plants invest less in mycorrhizal partnerships. The moderate, slow-release phosphorus from fish meal supports a balanced relationship where mycorrhizal fungi remain active while providing adequate phosphorus to the host plant.

### Microbial Decomposition
Fish meal is decomposed by soil bacteria and fungi, which:
- Release nutrients progressively over 1-4 months
- Contribute to [[humus-and-soil-organic-matter]] and humus formation
- Support the soil food web as a protein-rich food source

### Vegetable and Fruit Production
The combination of nitrogen and phosphorus makes fish meal particularly valuable for:
- Fruiting vegetables (tomatoes, peppers, squash) that need both nutrients
- Root crops that benefit from phosphorus for root development
- Transplants establishing new root systems

## Application Guidelines
- Mix into soil at planting time for best phosphorus placement near roots
- Apply as side dressing during active growth phases
- Particularly effective for phosphorus-demanding crops
- Not suitable for quick nutrient correction due to slow solubility
- Pair with a faster-acting nitrogen source if immediate nitrogen is needed
