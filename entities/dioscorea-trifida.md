**Dioscorea trifida**, commonly known as cush-cush yam, ají yam, or yampee,
is a species of yam in the family Dioscoreaceae native to the Caribbean and
[[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-south-america]].

It is one of [[permaculture-plants-root-crops-and-tubers]] in the Caribbean basin,
particularly in Trinidad, Tobago, Dominica, and the Lesser Antilles, where it
is valued for its early maturity and palatable flavor.

The species produces clusters of small to medium tubers with [[hamilton-vegetative-propagation]], which has preserved its genetic
diversity in traditional farming systems.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:** Plantae → Tracheophyta → Liliopsida → Dioscoreales →
Dioscoreaceae → *Dioscorea trifida* L.f.

**Synonyms:** *Dioscorea triphylla* Lam., *Dioscorea nummularia* Kunth,
*Dioscorea hastiformis* R. Knuth.

The plant is a twining, herbaceous vine producing annual stems from perennial
tubers. Stems are angular to winged, reaching 2–5 meters in length, and
rotate clockwise (dextrorse) around supporting vegetation.

Leaves are alternate, broadly ovate to cordate, 8–20 cm long and 6–15 cm
wide, with 5–7 prominent basal veins. The [[gadd-fungal-wood-decay-brown-rot-white-rot-soft-rot-mechanisms]] and roughened, while the
flesh is white, cream, or pale yellow, firm, and smooth-textured when cooked.
