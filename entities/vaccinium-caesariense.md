## Overview

**Vaccinium caesariense** Mackenz., commonly known as the New Jersey blueberry or sandy-ridge blueberry, is a deciduous shrub in the family Ericaceae.

It is native to the eastern United States, primarily the Atlantic Coastal Plain from New Jersey south to the Carolinas and Georgia.

The species is a diploid member of the Cyanococcus section and contributed genetic material to the early development of cultivated highbush blueberry (*V. corymbosum*) through natural hybridization.

Its berries are smaller than commercial cultivars but are valued for their intense flavor and high anthocyanin content.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Vaccinium caesariense** was described by William Mackenzie in 1932.

It belongs to section *Cyanococcus* within genus *Vaccinium*, characterized by berries with a persistent calyx and a ten-loculed ovary.

The species is diploid (2n = 24), distinguishing it from the tetraploid *V. corymbosum* (2n = 48) and hexaploid *V. corymbosum* var. *angustifolium* complex.

**Growth habit:** The shrub typically reaches 0.5–1.5 meters in height with a spreading, multi-stemmed form.

**Leaves:** Elliptic to obovate, 2–5 cm long, with finely serrate margins and a glaucous underside.

**Flowers:** Urceolate, white to pinkish, borne in axillary racemes of 4–10 flowers in early to mid-spring (April–May).

**Fruit:** Round berries, 5–9 mm in diameter, blue to black with a waxy bloom, ripening mid-summer (June–July).

The calyx is prominently persistent on the ripe fruit, a key distinguishing feature from some co-occurring *Vaccinium* species.
