## Overview

**Mephedrone**, chemically known as **4-methylmethcathinone (4-MMC)**, is a synthetic stimulant of the cathinone class. It is a beta-keto analogue of **mdma** and **dopamine-releasing** amphetamines, with significant activity at serotonin, dopamine, and norepinephrine transporters.

## Chemical Properties

Mephedrone has the molecular formula **C₁₁H₁₅NO** and a molecular weight of **177.24 g/mol**. Its IUPAC name is **2-(methylamino)-1-(4-methylphenyl)propan-1-one**.

The compound is a white or off-white crystalline powder at room temperature, soluble in water, ethanol, and organic solvents. It is typically administered via insufflation (nasal), oral ingestion, or intravenous injection.

The chemical structure consists of a cathinone core (beta-keto phenethylamine) with a methyl group at the para position of the phenyl ring and a methyl group on the amino nitrogen. The beta-keto group is the distinguishing structural feature from amphetamines, reducing lipophilicity and altering pharmacokinetic properties.

Mephedrone exists as two enantiomers due to the chiral center at the alpha carbon. The (S)-enantiomer is generally more potent at monoamine transporters, analogous to the enantioselectivity observed in amphetamine and cathinone derivatives.

Synthesis is relatively straightforward from 4-methylpropiophenone via bromination followed by amination with methylamine, making it accessible to clandestine laboratory production. This synthetic accessibility contributed to its rapid emergence as a widely available designer drug.

## Pharmacology

Mephedrone acts primarily as a **non-selective monoamine releasing agent** and **reuptake inhibitor** at the three major monoamine transporters: serotonin transporter (SERT), dopamine transporter (DAT), and norepinephrine transporter (NET).

In vitro studies using rat brain synaptosomes demonstrate that mephedrone is a potent substrate-type releaser at DAT and SERT, with EC₅₀ values in the low micromolar range. It is approximately equipotent at DAT and SERT, similar to the profile of **mdma**. Users report enhanced empathy, talkativeness, and sensory enhancement.

Stimulant effects include elevated heart rate, increased blood pressure, pupillary dilation, hyperthermia, bruxism, and reduced appetite. These effects are dose-dependent and more pronounced with intravenous or insufflated administration compared to oral dosing.

Common oral doses range from 100–250 mg. Intranasal doses are typically lower at 50–150 mg due to higher bioavailability. Effects onset within 10–20 minutes (nasal) to 15–45 minutes (oral), peak at 30–60 minutes, and last 2–4 hours.

In many countries, mephedrone is controlled through generic cathinone analogues or broad "designer drug" legislation that covers structural classes rather than individual compounds. These catch-all provisions aim to prevent the rapid market cycling of new analogues.

## History and Epidemiology

Mephedrone was first synthesized in 1929 but remained an obscure laboratory compound until its rediscovery by underground chemists around 2003–2004. It appeared on online research chemical markets in 2007 and rapidly gained popularity.

The drug's peak prevalence occurred in the UK between 2009 and 2010, where it was reportedly the fourth most popular recreational drug after cannabis, **thc** products, and cocaine. Surveys from this period estimated that over 300,000 people in England and Wales had used mephedrone in the preceding year.

Following the UK ban in April 2010, prevalence declined sharply but did not disappear. The drug continued to circulate in illicit markets alongside newer synthetic cathinones that filled the market gap left by scheduling.
