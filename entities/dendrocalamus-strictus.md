# Dendrocalamus strictus

## Description

Dendrocalamus strictus, commonly known as **male bamboo** or **solid bamboo**, is a large, densely clumping bamboo species native to tropical and subtropical regions of South and [[mollison-designers-earthworks-for-erosion-control]]

## Key Facts

- Large clumping bamboo, typically 10–20 m tall with culms 5–10 cm in diameter
- Culms are thick-walled and often nearly solid, especially in drier conditions
- Native to India, Bangladesh, Myanmar, Thailand, and Indonesia
- One of the most important bamboo species for commercial use in India
- Dense, clumping habit (non-invasive) makes it suitable for managed plantings
- Young shoots are edible
- Tolerates drier conditions better than most large bamboo species
- Important source of poles for rural construction and agriculture
- Culms become stronger and more useful when harvested at the proper age
- Useful as a perennial biomass crop in warm climates

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

- **Construction:** Primary bamboo for structural applications in South Asia — houses, scaffolding, bridges (page 97)
- **Paper and pulp:** Major raw material for paper and rayon production in India
- **Fuel:** Dried culms used as fuel in rural areas
- **Agricultural uses:** Used for fence posts, garden stakes, and trellises (page 97)
- **Craft:** Used for baskets, mats, and household items
- **[[agroforestry]]:** Clumps can occupy boundaries, gullies, and marginal corners without spreading through rhizomes
- **Material resilience:** Provides local replacement for imported poles, stakes, and lightweight construction materials
