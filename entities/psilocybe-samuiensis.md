# Psilocybe samuiensis

*Psilocybe samuiensis* Guzmán, Allen & Merlin is a bluing, psychoactive mushroom species
in the family Hymenogastraceae. First reported from the island of Koh Samui, Thailand,
the species is noted for its strong morphological resemblance to the temperate liberty
cap, [[psilocybe-semilanceata]], despite its tropical origin. It is one of several
psilocybin-containing mushrooms documented from Southeast Asia.

## Taxonomy

*Psilocybe samuiensis* was described by Guzmán, Allen, and Merlin based on specimens
collected by mycologist John Allen from the island of Koh Samui, located off the
southeastern coast of Thailand. The species epithet "samuiensis" directly references
the type locality, following standard mycological nomenclature.

The species belongs to the genus *Psilocybe* and is placed among the bluing,
psilocybin-containing taxa. Its overall morphology is strikingly similar to that
of [[psilocybe-semilanceata]], despite the two species being separated by thousands
of kilometers and vastly different climatic regimes. This resemblance suggests a
close phylogenetic relationship between geographically distant members of the genus.

Additional species reported from the same general Southeast Asian region include
[[psilocybe-southeast-asia-pacific]], which shares some ecological characteristics and may be
encountered in overlapping habitats. The taxonomic relationship between *P. samuiensis*
and other tropical Asian *Psilocybe* species warrants further study.

## Description

### Cap

The cap measures 0.7 to 1.5 cm in diameter. Its shape is variable, ranging from
convex to conic-convex to campanulate, and it is often umbonate with a small papilla
at the center. The surface is translucent-striate near the gill edge. When moist,
the cap becomes distinctly viscid, owing to a separable gelatinous pellicle that can
be peeled away. In young specimens the coloration is chestnut to reddish brown to
straw yellow. The cap is strongly hygrophanous, fading to straw or brownish tones
as it dries.

### Gills

Gill attachment is adnate. In young fruiting bodies the gills are clay colored. As
the mushroom matures the gill color deepens to violaceous brown or chocolate violet,
with whitish margins that contrast against the darker gill faces.

### Stem

The stem ranges from 40 to 65 mm in length and 1 to 2 mm in thickness. It is
whitish to yellowish and is covered with a fibrillose sheath consisting of remnants
of the partial veil. The stem is equal to slightly enlarged towards the base and is
concolorous with the cap. It bruises bluish where injured, a hallmark indicator of
the presence of psilocybin and related tryptamine alkaloids.

### Partial Veil

The partial veil is cortinate in nature, meaning it is webby rather than
membrane-like. It leaves a superior, fibrillose annular zone on the stem that soon
disappears as the mushroom matures. This ephemeral nature is an important diagnostic
feature distinguishing it from species with more persistent veils, such as
[[psilocybe-cubensis]].

### Microscopic Features

Microscopic examination is essential for definitive identification:

- **Spores:** Purplish brown in deposit, rhomboid to subrhomboid in face view,
  measuring 10 to 13 µm in length by 6.5 to 8 µm in width.
- **Basidia:** 4-spored, typical of the genus.
- **Pleurocystidia:** Scattered across the gill face, ventricose towards the base
  and sublageniform towards the apex, measuring 16 to 20 by 5 to 6.4 µm.
- **Cheilocystidia:** Ventricose at the base, lageniform, narrowing to a thinner
  neck, and often forked at the tip, measuring 18.5 to 28 (30) by 5 to 7 (8) µm.

The cheilocystidia are particularly distinctive, as their frequently forked
morphology provides a reliable microscopic identification character.

## Habitat and Distribution

*Psilocybe samuiensis* grows in well-manured, claylike soils in pastures, meadows,
and amongst rice paddies. The species was first discovered by John Allen on Koh Samui,
Thailand, with the initial collection made in early August. The full extent of its
fruiting season remains unknown.

*P. samuiensis* may be widely distributed throughout the broader Southeast Asian
tropical and subtropical region, wherever suitable manured, warm-climate habitats
exist. The species frequently shares its habitat with [[psilocybe-cubensis]] and
[[panaeolus-cyanescens]], both common tropical psychoactive species found in similar
pasture and agricultural environments. These three species can often be found
fruiting in close proximity during the rainy season.

## Chemistry and Potency

*Psilocybe samuiensis* is confirmed to be psychoactive. According to Gartz et al.
(1994), the tryptamine alkaloid content of dried material is as follows:

| Compound     | Concentration (% dry weight) |
|--------------|-----------------------------|
| psilocybin | 0.73%                       |
| [[psilocin-and-psilocybin-chemistry]]   | 0.52%                       |
| [[baeocystin-and-norbaeocystin-in-psilocybin-biosynthesis]] | 0.05%                       |

These concentrations place *P. samuiensis* among the more potent tropical *Psilocybe*
species. The relatively high psilocybin and [[psilocin-and-psilocybin-chemistry]] levels, combined with
the presence of [[baeocystin-and-norbaeocystin-in-psilocybin-biosynthesis]], produce a strong psychoactive effect with a chemical
profile comparable to that of [[psilocybe-semilanceata]]. The combined psilocybin
and [[psilocin-and-psilocybin-chemistry]] content of approximately 1.25% dry weight is significant.

## Identification Notes

Several key features are useful when identifying *P. samuiensis* in the field:

- **Size:** The small cap (0.7–1.5 cm) and slender stem (1–2 mm thick) are
  characteristic, contributing to its resemblance to [[psilocybe-semilanceata]].
- **Coloration:** The strongly hygrophanous cap, transitioning from chestnut or
  reddish brown when moist to straw-colored when dry, is a helpful field mark.
- **Bluing reaction:** Bluish bruising on the stem upon injury confirms the
  presence of psilocybin and distinguishes the species from non-psychoactive
  lookalikes.
- **Habitat:** Occurrence in manured, claylike soils of tropical pastures and rice
  paddies in Southeast Asia is a strong ecological indicator.
- **Microscopy:** Rhomboid to subrhomboid spores in the 10–13 by 6.5–8 µm range,
  together with the distinctive forked cheilocystidia, provide definitive
  identification.
- **Comparison with similar species:** [[psilocybe-cubensis]] is considerably larger
  with a more persistent annulus. [[psilocybe-southeast-asia-pacific]] overlaps in range but
  differs microscopically. [[panaeolus-cyanescens]] belongs to a different genus
  and has black spore deposits.
