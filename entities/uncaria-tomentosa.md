# Uncaria tomentosa

Uncaria tomentosa, widely known as cat's claw (una de gato in
Spanish), is a large woody vine in the family Rubiaceae native to
the Amazon rainforest and other tropical areas of Central and
South America. It is one of the most commercially important
medicinal plants of the Amazon, with a long history of indigenous
use and a growing body of modern pharmacological research
supporting its immunomodulatory, anti-inflammatory, and
antioxidant properties.

The common name 'cat's claw' derives from the distinctive curved
thorns on the vine that resemble a cat's claws. Indigenous
Ashaninka, Shipibo, and other Amazonian peoples have used the
vine's inner bark for centuries to treat a wide range of
conditions including arthritis, digestive disorders, wounds,
infections, and as a general tonic. Modern herbal medicine has
adopted cat's claw as one of the primary antiviral and immune-
supporting botanicals.

## Taxonomy and Classification

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Phylum:** Tracheophyta
- **Class:** Magnoliopsida
- **Order:** Gentianales
- **Family:** Rubiaceae
- **Subfamily:** Cinchonoideae
- **Tribe:** Naucleeae
- **Genus:** Uncaria
- **Species:** U. tomentosa

First described by Augustin Pyramus de Candolle in 1818. The
genus Uncaria includes approximately 40 species of climbing vines
found throughout tropical regions. The most closely related
medicinally used species is Uncaria guianensis, which shares the
common name cat's claw but has different alkaloid profiles and is
sometimes confused in commercial products. U. tomentosa is
distinguished by its larger thorns and different leaf morphology.

## Description

### Growth Habit and Morphology

- **Habit:** Large woody liana (climbing vine), climbing 10-30 m into the forest canopy
- **Stem:** Cylindrical, up to 20 cm diameter; young stems green, mature stems brown and woody
- **Thorns:** Curved, claw-like hooks derived from modified branch tips, 5-12 mm long, arranged in pairs at nodes — the source of the common name
- **Leaves:** Opposite, simple; blade elliptic to oblong, 8-18 cm long and 3-8 cm wide, glossy green above, paler beneath; stipules interpetiolar, ovate
- **Flowers:** In terminal or axillary heads, sessile; corolla tubular, 5-lobed, yellowish-white to pale yellow; stamens exserted; flowering during wet season
- **Fruit:** Small capsule, 10-15 mm long, containing numerous winged seeds
- **Root bark:** Inner bark is the primary medicinal part, reddish-brown

### Distinguishing from U. guianensis

| Feature | U. tomentosa | U. guianensis |
|---------|-------------|---------------|
| Thorns | Large, curved, paired | Smaller, straighter |
| Leaf base | Rounded to cuneate | Acute to cuneate |
| Alkaloid type | Pentacyclic oxindole | Tetracyclic oxindole |
| Vine diameter | Thicker, 5-20 cm | Thinner, 2-8 cm |
| Altitude range | Lowland to premontane | Primarily lowland |

## Distribution and Habitat

- **Native range:** Amazon basin: Peru, Ecuador, Colombia, Bolivia, Brazil; also Central America (Panama, Costa Rica, Guatemala, Belize)
- **Habitat:** Tropical moist lowland and premontane forests; also secondary growth and forest edges
- **Elevation:** 0-800 m above sea level (occasionally to 1200 m)
- **Climate:** Warm, humid; mean annual temperature 24-28°C; rainfall 1500-4000 mm
- **Soil:** Well-drained forest soils; tolerates a range of tropical soil types

U. tomentosa is an abundant component of the Amazonian forest
canopy. Its climbing habit allows it to access full sunlight in
the upper canopy while rooting in the shaded understory. The
species is relatively tolerant of habitat disturbance and can
persist in secondary forests and forest fragments, though
overharvesting of wild populations for the medicinal trade is a
conservation concern in some areas.

## Chemistry and Active Compounds

| Compound | Class | Concentration | Activity |
|----------|-------|---------------|----------|
| Mitraphylline | Oxindole alkaloid | 0.1-0.3% | Immunomodulatory, antitumor |
| Pteropodine (uncarine C) | Oxindole alkaloid | 0.05-0.2% | Immune stimulation |
| Isomitraphylline | Oxindole alkaloid | 0.03-0.15% | Anti-inflammatory |
| Rhynchophylline | Oxindole alkaloid | 0.02-0.1% | Calcium channel blocker |
| Speciophylline | Oxindole alkaloid | 0.02-0.1% | Immunomodulatory |
| Quinovic acid glycosides | Triterpene | 2-5% | Anti-inflammatory |
| Proanthocyanidins | Polyphenol | 1-3% | Antioxidant |

Two distinct chemotypes of U. tomentosa exist based on their
oxindole alkaloid profiles. One chemotype contains predominantly
pentacyclic oxindole alkaloids (POAs) such as mitraphylline and
pteropodine, while the other contains predominantly tetracyclic
oxindole alkaloids (TOAs) such as rhynchophylline. The POA
chemotype is considered therapeutically superior because TOAs may
antagonize the immune-enhancing effects of POAs. Reputable
commercial preparations are standardized to the POA chemotype and
tested to ensure TOA levels below 0.05%.

## Traditional Medicinal Uses

- **Ashaninka people (Peru):** Primary medicine for arthritis, rheumatism, bone pain, and as a contraceptive; used as a general tonic and purgative
- **Shipibo-Conibo:** Inner bark decoction for digestive disorders, wounds, and fever
- **Campa people:** Used for cancer-like symptoms and as a blood purifier
- **Modern herbalism:** Immune support, adjunct therapy for cancer, arthritis, chronic fatigue, viral infections, and inflammatory bowel conditions
- **European phytotherapy:** Approved in Germany as a supportive treatment for chemotherapy side effects

## Cultivation and Sustainability

Cat's claw is cultivated from seed or cuttings. Seeds require
fresh collection and sowing within weeks, as viability drops
rapidly. Cuttings from semi-hardwood stems root in 4-8 weeks
under mist. The vine requires support structures or a trellis
system in cultivation. Harvesting of inner bark typically begins
after 3-5 years of growth. Sustainable harvesting practices are
critical — stripping bark from the entire circumference of the
vine kills the plant. Recommended practice is to harvest
longitudinal strips from one side only, allowing regeneration.

## Conservation Status

- **IUCN:** Not globally threatened, but regional concerns exist
- **CITES:** Not listed, but some countries have export restrictions on wild-harvested material
- **Primary threat:** Overharvesting of wild populations for the international herb trade
- **Sustainable sourcing:** Peru has established cultivation programs; certified organic cat's claw is increasingly available from cultivated sources

## Legal Status

- **Uncontrolled worldwide** — not listed on any international drug convention
- Widely available as a dietary supplement in the United States, Europe, and Japan
- Regulated as a food supplement under FDA and EFSA frameworks; health claims are restricted
- Some countries require documentation of sustainable sourcing for import
