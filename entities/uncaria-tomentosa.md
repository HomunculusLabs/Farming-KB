## Uncaria tomentosa

Uncaria tomentosa, widely known as cat's claw (una de gato in
Spanish), is a large woody vine in the family Rubiaceae native to
the Amazon rainforest and other tropical areas of Central and
South America. It is one of the most [[mollison-designers-sea-level-change-coastal-zones]]- **Elevation:** 0-800 m above sea level (occasionally to 1200 m)
- **Climate:** Warm, humid; mean annual temperature 24-28°C; rainfall 1500-4000 mm
- **Soil:** Well-drained forest soils; tolerates a range of tropical soil types

U. tomentosa is an abundant component of the Amazonian forest
canopy. Its climbing habit allows it to access full sunlight in
the upper canopy while rooting in the shaded understory. The
species is relatively tolerant of habitat disturbance and can
persist in secondary forests and forest fragments, though
overharvesting of wild populations for the medicinal trade is a
conservation concern in some areas.

## Chemistry and Active Compounds

| Isomitraphylline | Oxindole alkaloid | 0.03-0.15% | Anti-inflammatory |
| Rhynchophylline | Oxindole alkaloid | 0.02-0.1% | Calcium channel blocker |
| Speciophylline | Oxindole alkaloid | 0.02-0.1% | Immunomodulatory |
| Quinovic acid glycosides | Triterpene | 2-5% | Anti-inflammatory |
| Proanthocyanidins | Polyphenol | 1-3% | Antioxidant |

Two distinct chemotypes of U. tomentosa exist based on their
oxindole alkaloid profiles. One chemotype contains predominantly
pentacyclic oxindole alkaloids (POAs) such as mitraphylline and
pteropodine, while the other contains predominantly tetracyclic
oxindole alkaloids (TOAs) such as rhynchophylline. The POA
chemotype is considered therapeutically superior because TOAs may
antagonize the immune-enhancing effects of POAs. Reputable
commercial preparations are standardized to the POA chemotype and
tested to ensure TOA levels below 0.05%.

## Traditional Medicinal Uses

- **Ashaninka people (Peru):** Primary medicine for arthritis, rheumatism, bone pain, and as a contraceptive; used as a general tonic and purgative
- **Shipibo-Conibo:** Inner bark decoction for digestive disorders, wounds, and fever
- **Campa people:** Used for cancer-like symptoms and as a blood purifier
- **Modern herbalism:** Immune support, adjunct therapy for cancer, arthritis, [[blesching-cannabis-inflammatory-bowel-disease]] conditions
- **European phytotherapy:** Approved in Germany as a supportive treatment for chemotherapy side effects

## Cultivation and Sustainability

Cat's claw is cultivated from seed or cuttings. Seeds require
fresh collection and sowing within weeks, as viability drops
rapidly. Cuttings from semi-hardwood stems root in 4-8 weeks
under mist. The vine requires support structures or a trellis
system in cultivation. Harvesting of inner bark typically begins
after 3-5 years of growth. [[banisteriopsis-caapi]]
