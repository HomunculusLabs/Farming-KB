# Beneficial Indigenous Microorganisms (BIM)

## Description

Beneficial Indigenous Microorganisms (BIM) is a natural farming concept and preparation consisting of locally-sourced, cultured beneficial microbes used to enhance soil fertility, plant health, animal health, and waste decomposition. BIM is central to Korean Natural Farming (KNF) and Asian natural farming traditions. Rather than importing or purchasing commercial microbial inoculants, BIM emphasizes trapping and culturing microorganisms that are already adapted to local environmental conditions from nearby forests, bamboo groves, and specific crop plants.

## Classification

- **Category:** Microbial inoculant / Soil biology system
- **Origin:** Korean Natural Farming / Japanese Natural Farming
- **Key Components:** Lactic acid bacteria (50%), plus forest microorganisms, bamboo microorganisms, plant-specific microorganisms

## Key Facts

- The standard BIM recipe consists of 50% lactic acid bacteria and 50% other cultured microorganisms (forest, bamboo, and plant-specific).
- A typical BIM blend: 1 part forest microorganism, 1 part bamboo microorganism, 1 part plant-specific microorganism, mixed with 3 parts lactic acid bacteria.
- Lactic acid bacteria is the primary component and serves as the "workhorse" organism — arresting foul odors, aiding decomposition, and producing natural antibiotics.
- Forest microorganisms are collected by burying cooked rice in aged forest humus near old robust trees with deep litter layers (2 feet to 1 yard deep).
- Bamboo microorganisms are gathered by burying cooked rice containers in bamboo plant litter, as bamboo roots exude sugary substances that attract powerful beneficial microbes.
- Plant-specific microorganisms are trapped by placing cooked rice containers over cut stems of healthy target plants, capturing microbes specific to that plant species.
- BIM is mixed with bionutrients (fermented plant extracts, ginger-garlic extract, brown rice vinegar, fish amino acid) for enhanced effectiveness.
- Applications include: soil and compost inoculation, foliar spraying, animal drinking water supplementation, waste decomposition, and odor control.
- The principle of "more diverse microbes = better" guides formulation — biodiversity is considered essential for balanced ecosystems.

## Collection Methods

1. **Rice wash method (LAB):** Rice wash water fermented 5-7 days, then saturated with milk to isolate pure lactic acid bacteria.
2. **Forest humus method:** Cooked rice buried in forest litter, retrieved after 2-10 days when colonized by molds.
3. **Bamboo litter method:** Same as forest method but placed in bamboo grove litter.
4. **Plant-specific method:** Cooked rice container inverted over cut target plant to capture plant-associated microbes.

## Relevance to Cultivation

BIM is foundational to natural farming because it addresses the root cause of plant and animal health — the soil microbiome. By culturing locally-adapted microorganisms, farmers create inputs that are more effective than generic commercial products because the microbes are already acclimated to local climate, soil chemistry, and flora. In mushroom cultivation, the principles of BIM — maintaining microbial diversity, using carbohydrate substrates, and promoting balanced decomposition — directly apply to substrate preparation and contamination management.
