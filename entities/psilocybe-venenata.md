# Psilocybe venenata

**Psilocybe venenata** is a psilocybin-containing mushroom species originally described from Japan. Despite its specific epithet, which means "poisonous" in Latin, the species is classified among the psychoactive *Psilocybe* mushrooms and is not considered lethally toxic. It is a relatively obscure species that has received limited attention in the mycological and ethnopharmacological literature.

## Taxonomy

The species was first described by Japanese mycologist Tsuguo Hongo. The epithet *venenata* (Latin for "poisoned" or "venomous") reflects the historical classification of psychoactive mushrooms as poisonous substances rather than entheogens. This naming convention was common in early mycology, when the psychoactive effects of certain mushrooms were documented primarily in the context of accidental poisonings.

Some authorities have suggested that *P. venenata* may be conspecific with or closely related to *Psilocybe subaeruginascens*, a species described from Java, based on morphological similarities. The taxonomy of Asian *Psilocybe* species remains an area of active study, and molecular phylogenetic analyses may clarify relationships in this group.

## Description

- **Cap**: Small to medium, typically 1 to 3 cm in diameter. Convex to plane in age, hygrophanous. Color ranges from brownish to reddish brown when moist, fading to pale brownish or buff when dry. Surface is smooth to slightly viscid when wet.
- **Gills**: Adnate to slightly adnexed, close. Color is grayish initially, darkening to purplish brown with spore maturity.
- **Stipe**: Slender, typically 4 to 8 cm long and 1 to 2 mm thick. Equal, sometimes slightly enlarged at the base. Pale brownish, often bruising bluish, particularly near the base. Surface is smooth to finely pruinose near the apex.
- **[[comparison-spore-print-collection-vs-spore-syringe-production]]**: Dark purplish brown.
- **Spores**: Ellipsoidal to slightly rhomboidal in face view, with a prominent germ pore.
- **Partial veil**: May form a transient fibrillose annular zone.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Psilocybe venenata* is primarily known from Japan, where it has been collected in various temperate forest environments. It has also been reported from other parts of [[neurotropic-fungi-japan-east-asia-species-cultural-context]]. The species grows on decomposing plant material, particularly grasses, herbaceous debris, and enriched soils in forested areas and grasslands. It fruits in the autumn months, typically during periods of warm, wet weather.

## Psychoactive Properties

[[challenges-neurotropic-fungi-chemical-analysis-taxonomic-identification]] of *P. venenata* collections has confirmed the presence of [[psilocin]]. Some analyses have also reported [[norbaeocystin]]. The chemical profile includes:
- **Psilocybin**: 0.15% to 0.85% of dry weight (varies by collection)
- **Psilocin**: 0.02% to 0.25% of dry weight
- **Baeocystin**: trace to 0.1% of dry weight

The exact potency varies between collections, but the species is considered moderately psychoactive. The blue bruising reaction, while not always pronounced, is a reliable indicator of psychoactive alkaloid content. The variability in reported potency may reflect genuine differences between populations, differences in substrate and growing conditions, or the possibility that collections identified as *P. venenata* actually represent multiple closely related species within the Asian *Psilocybe* flora.

## Identification Notes

*Psilocybe venenata* is a relatively inconspicuous mushroom that can be overlooked in the field due to its small size and muted coloration. Key identification features include the hygrophanous cap that fades from brownish to buff as it dries, the dark purplish-brown spore print, and the tendency to bruise bluish when handled, particularly at the stipe base. The partial veil, when present, leaves only a faint fibrillose annular zone that is easily missed. [[fungal-microscopic-examination-mounting-media-techniques]] reveals ellipsoidal spores with a prominent germ pore, and lageniform cheilocystidia — features consistent with the genus Psilocybe.

The species should be distinguished from non-psychoactive lookalikes including various inactive Psilocybe, Psathyrella, and Stropharia species found in similar Japanese forest and grassland habitats. As with all Psilocybe species, confusion with [[psilocybe-argentipes]].
- **Psilocybe coprophila**: Small coprophilous species that may superficially resemble *P. venenata* but typically grows directly on dung. See [[psilocybe-yungensis]].
- **Galerina species**: Potentially toxic lookalikes with rusty-brown spore prints.

## Legal Status

*Psilocybe venenata* is controlled under Japanese drug laws as a Schedule I substance. Japan has some of the strictest laws regarding [[forensic-identification-methods-psilocybin-mushrooms-tlc-gc-hplc]] in Asia, with the 2002 revision of the Stimulant [[japan-narcotics-control-law-psilocybin-fungi-2002]] explicitly banning psilocybin and psilocin-containing mushrooms. Prior to 2002, fresh psilocybin mushrooms were sold openly in Japanese stores and markets, particularly in Tokyo's Shibuya district and in tourist areas. The law was changed after several high-profile incidents involving tourists and the recognition that psilocybin-containing mushrooms were being widely sold despite being psychoactive substances.
