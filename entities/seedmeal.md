## Description

Seedmeal (also called oilseed meal) is the protein-rich byproduct remaining after oil is pressed from oilseeds. It is the primary nitrogen source in Steve Solomon's Complete Organic Fertilizer (COF) formula. Most seedmeals have a typical NPK analysis of approximately 6-4-2 when chemically analyzed. Seedmeal is widely available from feed stores and grain dealers in agricultural areas.

## Classification

- **Type**: Organic fertilizer / Nitrogen source
- **Source**: Pressed byproduct of oilseed processing
- **Primary nutrients**: Nitrogen (N), Phosphorus (P), Potassium (K)
- **Typical analysis**: ~6-4-2 NPK
- **Form**: Dry meal / cake

## Key Facts

- Seedmeal is the residue left after vegetable oil is extracted from oilseeds such as canola, cottonseed, linseed (flax), sunflower, and soy
- When chemically analyzed, most seedmeals test at approximately NPK 6-4-2
- Coprameal (coconut oil byproduct) is about one-third weaker in NPK than other seedmeals
- Coprameal has one advantage: coconuts are never grown with chemical fertilizers, so coprameal is inherently organic
- Seedmeal is purchased in bulk sacks from feed stores and grain dealers
- The general rule for buying seedmeal: choose whichever type provides the most protein (and thus nitrogen) for the least cost
- Seedmeal must be stored dry and protected from moisture, ideally in a container with a tight lid
- In COF, seedmeal is used at 4 parts by volume (or 3 parts when supplemented with tankage)

## Common Types

| Type | Notes |
|------|-------|
| Canola (rapeseed) meal | Widely available, good NPK |
| Cottonseed meal | Common in southern US |
| Linseed (flax) meal | Excellent quality |
| Soybean meal | High protein, widely available |
| Sunflower meal | Good availability in some regions |
| Coprameal | ~1/3 weaker NPK, but guaranteed organic |

## Relevance to Cultivation

- Seedmeal is the most cost-effective organic nitrogen source available to home gardeners
- Unlike chemical nitrogen fertilizers, seedmeal releases nitrogen slowly through decomposition by soil organisms
- The rate of nutrient release depends on soil temperature: approximately doubling for each 10F (5C) increase
- Seedmeal provides a balanced nutrient profile rather than pure nitrogen, contributing phosphorus and potassium as well
- Its slow-release nature matches nutrient availability to plant uptake, avoiding the rapid boost and subsequent sag characteristic of chemical fertilizers
- When combined with lime, gypsum, dolomite, and a phosphorus source in COF, seedmeal provides the complete nitrogen foundation for vegetable production

## Purchasing Notes

- Feed stores and grain dealers are the proper sources for seedmeal
- Urban gardeners may need more effort to find bulk suppliers
- Buying in bulk sacks is far more economical than purchasing small quantities from garden shops
- Store in a dry, protected location in a sealed container

## Common Types and NPK Values

Seed meals vary by source: cottonseed meal (6-2-1), soybean meal
(7-2-1), canola meal (5-2-1), and sunflower meal (5-2-1). Each
type has slightly different nutrient profiles and availability rates.

## Application Guidelines

Apply seed meal at 1-2 kg per 10 square meters, mixed into the
soil before planting. Avoid direct contact with seeds as the
initial nitrogen release can inhibit germination.

## Soil pH Effects

Most seed meals are slightly acidic, making them ideal for use in
alkaline soils. In already acidic soils, combine with agricultural
lime to maintain balanced pH levels.
