# Stropharia ambigua

## Overview

Stropharia ambigua is a saprotrophic basidiomycete fungus in the family
Strophariaceae, commonly found in the Pacific Northwest of North America.
It fruits in scattered to gregarious clusters on decaying wood chips,
mulch, and woody debris in landscaped and forested habitats.

The species is of particular interest for its morphological similarity
to several psilocybin-containing taxa and its potential role in
mycoremediation and permaculture mulch management systems.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Strophariaceae
- **Genus:** Stropharia
- **Species:** S. ambigua

- **Authority:** (Peck) Kreisel (1984)
- **Synonyms:** Agaricus ambiguus Peck (1872),
  Psilocybe ambigua (Peck) Sacc. (1887),
  Hypholoma ambiguum (Peck) A.H. Sm. (1934)
- **Etymology:** The epithet *ambigua* reflects the ambiguous
  taxonomic placement that historically confused this species with
  both Stropharia and Psilocybe genera.

## Morphology

### Cap

- **Diameter:** 30–80 mm
- **Shape:** Convex to broadly convex, becoming plano-convex with age
- **Color:** Hygrophanous, chestnut-brown to reddish-brown when moist,
  fading to yellowish-buff or pale tan as it dries
- **Surface:** Smooth to slightly viscid when wet, becoming silky-dry
- **Margin:** Incurved when young, often appendiculate with partial veil
  remnants in young specimens

### Gills

- **Attachment:** Adnate to adnexed, sometimes subdecurrent
- **Color:** Grayish to pale brown when young, darkening to purplish-brown
  with spore maturity
- **Spacing:** Close to subdistant
- **Edges:** Slightly fringed, whitish from cheilocystidia

### Stipe

- **Length:** 50–120 mm
- **Thickness:** 4–10 mm
- **Color:** Whitish to pale brown, often staining yellowish where handled
- **Context:** Solid, whitish
- **Ring:** Membranous, superior, often evanescent in age

### Spores

- **Shape:** Ellipsoid to slightly amygdaliform
- **Size:** 9.5–13 × 6–8 µm
- **Color:** Dark purplish-brown in deposit
- **Wall:** Thick-walled, smooth
- **Germ pore:** Present, broad

### Microscopic Features

- **Cheilocystidia:** Ventricose to lageniform, 20–35 × 6–12 µm
- **Pleurocystidia:** Similar to cheilocystidia but less abundant
- **Pileipellis:** Cutis of repent cylindrical hyphae, 3–6 µm wide

## Distribution and Habitat

- **Geographic range:** Pacific Northwest of North America, from
  British Columbia south to northern California
- **Habitat:** Primarily on wood chips, landscaped mulch, sawdust,
  and decaying hardwood logs
- **Season:** Late summer through early winter (September–January)
- **Associations:** Often found in urban and suburban settings where
  woody mulch is applied, also in second-growth coniferous forests
  along trails and disturbed areas

## Chemistry

Stropharia ambigua has been screened for psilocybin and psilocin
with inconsistent results across collections. Some Pacific Northwest
collections have shown trace amounts of psilocybin while others have
tested negative.

This variability may reflect genetic differences between populations,
environmental factors, or misidentification with morphologically
similar species such as Stropharia aeruginosa and certain Psilocybe
taxa.

## Ecological Role

S. ambigua is a primary decomposer of woody substrates, playing a
role in nutrient cycling in forest floor ecosystems. Its preference
for disturbed wood-chip habitats makes it a common early colonizer
of freshly applied landscape mulch.

The species may form associations with common garden and ornamental
plants through incidental mycorrhizal-like interactions, though
this has not been formally documented.

## Similar Species

- **Stropharia aeruginosa:** Similar size and habitat but with a
  distinctive greenish-blue cap color and more persistent ring
- **Stropharia hornemannii:** Larger species with a more robust stipe
  and different cheilocystidia morphology
- **Psilocybe stuntzii:** Shares habitat preference for wood chips;
  distinguished by blueing reaction and smaller spores (8–10 µm)
- **Psilocybe cyanescens:** More strongly hygrophanous with prominent
  blueing; wavy cap margin at maturity

## Cultivation

S. ambigua has not been deliberately cultivated but will readily
colonize pasteurized hardwood chip substrates. Its appearance in
landscaped mulch beds suggests tolerance of a range of woody
substrates and moderate temperature ranges.
