## Overview

**Asarum sieboldii** is a perennial herbaceous plant in the family Aristolochiaceae, native to East Asia. It is closely related to [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]] in shaded understory conditions. It is typically found at elevations of 100–1500 m, often on north-facing slopes and along stream banks.

In Korea, *A. sieboldii* f. *seoulense* is endemic and considered a distinct taxon by some authorities. It occupies similar forest understory habitats on the Korean Peninsula.

Populations have declined across much of their range due to overharvesting for medicinal trade and habitat loss from deforestation. The species is classified as **Vulnerable** in several Chinese provincial red lists.

Methyleugenol is the compound most associated with the herb's pharmacological activity.
- **Aristolochic acids:** Present at significantly lower concentrations than in *Aristolochia* species (typically < 0.01% dry weight in properly processed material). However, detectable levels warrant caution in long-term use.
- **Flavonoids and phenolic acids:** Including asarinin, sesamin, and various kaempferol glycosides with antioxidant activity.

**Pharmacological activities documented in vitro and in vivo:**

The essential oil demonstrates **analgesic and anti-inflammatory** effects in animal models, supporting its traditional use for pain relief. Methyleugenol has been shown to possess **local anesthetic** properties, contributing to the herb's topical applications.

**Antitussive and expectorant** activity has been documented, validating the traditional use of saishin in respiratory formulations. The volatile oil inhibits cough reflex sensitivity in guinea pig models.

**Antimicrobial activity** against *Staphylococcus aureus*, *Bacillus subtilis*, and *[[bloomfield-candida-albicans-yeast-hyphal-dimorphism]]* has been demonstrated for the essential oil, though clinical relevance remains unestablished.

**Safety concerns:**

Methyleugenol and safrole are classified as **possible carcinogens** (IARC Group 2B) based on rodent studies. Long-term oral administration at high doses produced hepatocellular tumors in rats, though typical Kampo doses deliver far lower amounts. Aristolochic acid, even at trace levels, carries **nephrotoxic and carcinogenic** risk.

## Ethnobotany and Traditional Use

In **Kampo medicine**, *A. sieboldii* rhizome (saishin) is a standard component in several classical formulations. It is prescribed for respiratory conditions including cough, bronchitis, and allergic rhinitis, often combined with other herbs in multi-herb formulas.

Saishin is considered to have a **warming, acrid** nature in traditional East Asian energetics. It is used to dispel cold, alleviate pain, and open the nasal passages. Typical daily doses in Kampo practice range from 1–3 g of dried rhizome.
