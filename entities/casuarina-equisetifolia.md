# Casuarina equisetifolia

**Type:** Species
**Category:** Evergreen nitrogen-fixing tree
**Also known as:** Horsetail tree, Australian pine, coast sheoak,
  whistling pine

## Overview
Casuarina equisetifolia is a small evergreen tree, 8-16 meters tall,
typically growing as wide as it is tall with a characteristic bent
stem. Native to coastal sandy environments across the Indo-Pacific, it
is one of the world's most important coastal protection and
sand-stabilization species. It forms a unique symbiosis with
Frankia bacteria for atmospheric nitrogen fixation.

## Key Details

### Botany
- **Family:** Casuarinaceae
- **Form:** Small tree, 8-16 m; often bent stem; as wide as tall
- **Habit:** Usually long-lived evergreen
- **Foliage:** Needle-like branchlets resembling pine or horsetail
- **Nitrogen fixation:** Symbiosis with Frankia spp (actinorhizal)

### Growth Conditions
- **Rainfall:** 175-1,500 mm
- **Soils:** At home in sand dunes; coastal sandy soils
- **Hardiness:** Hardy to drought and frost
- **Fire:** Will not survive fire
- **Coppice:** Will not tolerate coppicing
- **Salinity:** Salt-tolerant; excellent for coastal plantings

### Coastal Protection Value
- **Windbreaks:** Capable of absorbing heavy winds with belts two or
  three trees deep
- **Erosion control:** Planted along estuaries, river banks, waterways,
  and coastal areas
- **Sand stabilization:** Primary species for dune fixation worldwide

### Timber Properties
- Heartwood: dark brown, very hard, very heavy (1,000 kg/cu m)
- Strong and durable in ground or submerged in saltwater
- Used for house posts, rafters, electric poles, mine timbers,
  roofing shingles, tool handles, wagon wheels, and oars
- Considered one of the best firewoods (4,950 kcal/kg)
- Burns even when green; ashes retain heat for extended periods
- Makes exceptionally fine charcoal

### Bark Uses
- Contains 6-18% tannin
- Used extensively in Madagascar for leather tanning
- Produces plump, pliant, soft leather in pale reddish-brown colour
- Penetrates hide quickly
