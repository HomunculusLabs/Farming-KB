## Description

Amanita wellsii is a visually striking mushroom species in the genus Amanita, noted for its stunning appearance. It is documented in Andrus Voitk"s *Common Mushrooms of Newfoundland and Labrador* (2007) as a species found in Newfoundland that is rarely covered in standard North American field guides.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Phylum**: Basidiomycota
- **Class**: Agaricomycetes
- **Order**: Agaricales
- **Family**: Amanitaceae
- **Genus**: Amanita
- **Species**: A. wellsii

## Key Facts

- Described as a visually stunning species — reviewer Britt Bunyard called it "what a stunner!"
- Occurs in Newfoundland and Labrador, Canada.
- Not commonly featured in standard North American field guides.
- Belongs to the genus Amanita, one of the most important mushroom genera containing both choice edibles and deadly poisonous species.
- Documented in Voitk's comprehensive Newfoundland guide, which covers nearly 400 species with color illustrations.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

As a member of the genus Amanita, A. wellsii is part of an ecologically critical group of ectomycorrhizal fungi. Amanita species form essential symbiotic relationships with trees and are integral to forest ecosystem health. While Amanita species are generally not cultivated due to their obligate mycorrhizal relationships, studying their distribution and ecology contributes to understanding forest mycology and the diversity of mycorrhizal networks. The species also illustrates the richness of underdocumented North American mycoflora.

## Cautions

The genus Amanita contains some of the most poisonous mushrooms known (e.g., A. phalloides, the death cap). Positive identification is essential before any consideration of edibility.
