# Psilocybe mairei

**Type:** Species (Fungus)
**Category:** Basidiomycota, [[agar]]icales, Strophariaceae, Psilocybe
**Also known as:** Hypholoma cyanescens Maire,
  Psilocybe maire sensu Guzman

## Overview
Psilocybe mairei is a bluing, likely active Psilocybe species known
only from North Africa (Morocco and Algeria). Originally described
as Hypholoma cyanescens by Maire, it was reclassified by Singer.
Found from October through December in mixed forests of pine, fir,
and oak, this species has a distinctive orangish brown viscid cap
that fades to yellowish white, bruising bluish where injured. Reports
of this species from Europe are considered doubtful.

## Key Details
- **Cap:** 1.5-3.5 cm broad. Convex to campanulate to conic-campanulate,
  expanding with age, not umbonate. Viscid when moist from separable
  gelatinous pellicle. Orangish brown, becoming olive toned,
  hygrophanous, fading to yellowish white. Flesh amber, bruising
  bluish where injured.
- **Gills:** Adnate, pallid at first, soon darkening to purplish
  brown with whitish fringed edges.
- **Stem:** 25-75 mm long by 2-5 mm thick. Equal to slightly enlarged
  towards base. Whitish to yellowish white, bruising bluish. Surface
  pruinose above, finely fibrillose below. Stuffed with silky pith.
  Base adorned with thick, radiating white rhizomorphs. Partial
  veil cortinate, leaving fibrillose remnants on stem.
- **Spores:** Dark purplish brown in deposit, elongate-ellipsoid,
  10-12 (13.5) x 5.5-7 um. Basidia 4-spored. Pleurocystidia absent
  or near gill edge. Cheilocystidia 30-40 x 6-8 um, variable:
  lageniform, fusiform, or ampullaceous.
- **Habitat:** Known only from North Africa (Morocco and Algeria),
  October through December. Gregarious on soil rich in woody debris
  in forests mixed with Pinus pinaster, Abies pinsapo, Quercus ilicis,
  and Quercus pyrenaica. European collections considered doubtful.
- **Chemistry:** Likely active based on bluing reaction and morphology.
  Exact chemical composition not documented.

## Distribution and Ecology
Psilocybe mairei is known only from North Africa, specifically
Morocco and Algeria, where it fruits from October through December.
It grows gregariously in mixed conifer-hardwood forests dominated by
Pinus pinaster (maritime pine), Abies pinsapo (Spanish fir), Quercus
ilicis (holm oak), and Quercus pyrenaica (Pyrenean oak). Reports of
this species from European collections are considered doubtful by
Guzman, highlighting the importance of verifiable geographic records.
The North African climate, with its Mediterranean-influenced
seasonality of wet winters and dry summers, likely governs the
species' autumn fruiting pattern. The thick radiating rhizomorphs
at the stem base suggest adaptation to the well-drained, rocky
forest soils of the Atlas and Rif mountain ranges.

## Relevance
Psilocybe mairei is significant as the primary documented
psilocybin mushroom from the North African region, suggesting
that psychoactive fungi may be more widely distributed across the
Mediterranean basin than commonly recognized. Its original
classification as Hypholoma cyanescens reflects the historical
taxonomic confusion between Psilocybe, Hypholoma, and Stropharia.
The species' restriction to North African conifer-hardwood forests
may indicate a specific climatic requirement or simply reflect
under-sampling of mycoflora in the region. The thick radiating
rhizomorphs at the stem base suggest a well-developed underground
mycelial network, potentially aiding in colonization of woody
debris.
