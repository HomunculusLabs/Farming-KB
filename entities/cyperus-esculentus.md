## Overview

Cyperus esculentus L., commonly known as tigernut, chufa, earth almond, or yellow nutsedge, is a perennial sedge in the family Cyperaceae.

The species produces small, spherical tubers underground that are edible and have been cultivated as a food crop for millennia.

Tigernut tubers are particularly famous as the primary ingredient in horchata de chufa, a traditional sweet beverage from Valencia, Spain.

The plant is also one of the world's most troublesome agricultural weeds in its wild form, competing aggressively with cultivated crops.

Archaeological evidence indicates that C. esculentus was cultivated in ancient Egypt and Nubia as early as 6,000 years ago.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:**

- Kingdom: Plantae
- Order: Poales
- Family: Cyperaceae
- Genus: Cyperus
- Species: C. esculentus L.

The genus Cyperus is one of the largest genera of monocots, with over 600 species worldwide.

C. esculentus is part of the [[psilocybin-psilocin-variation-across-flushes]] its range.

Several infraspecific taxa and cultivated varieties have been described, particularly in West Africa and the Mediterranean.

**Morphology:**

The plant grows 30–90 cm tall with triangular, solid stems typical of sedges.

Leaves are grass-like, narrow, 3–10 mm wide, arising from the base of the plant.

The inflorescence consists of spikelets arranged in umbellate clusters, yellowish-brown in color.

Each tuber is approximately 1–2 cm in diameter, roughly spherical to oval, with a smooth, light-brown skin.

The tubers develop along rhizomes at depths of 10–25 cm, with a single plant capable of producing hundreds of tubers per season.

Tuber color varies from pale yellow to dark brown depending on cultivar and soil conditions.

The flesh is white, firm, and sweet, with a distinctive nutty flavor.

## Distribution and Habitat

Cyperus esculentus has a nearly [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-hawaii-pacific-islands]].

As a weed, it is particularly problematic in maize, cotton, peanut, and vegetable crops in warm climates.

The plant thrives in a wide range of soil types, from sandy to clay, and tolerates both drought and waterlogged conditions.

Cultivated varieties are grown commercially in Spain (Valencia region), West Africa (especially Nigeria), and parts of the Middle East.

In Spain, chufa is grown as a specialized crop for horchata production under regulated geographic indication.

## Nutritional Value

Tigernut tubers are nutritionally dense, containing approximately 25–35% fat, 20–30% carbohydrate, and 5–8% protein.
