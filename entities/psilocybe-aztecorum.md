## Overview

*Psilocybe aztecorum*, known as teonanácatl ("flesh of the gods") in Nahuatl, is a psychedelic mushroom species native to the high-elevation pine forests of central Mexico. It was one of the principal ceremonial entheogens used by the Aztec civilization and continues to be used by indigenous Mazatec and Nahua communities today.

The species was first described scientifically by Roger Heim and R. Gordon Wasson in 1958 following their groundbreaking expeditions to identify the mushrooms used in Mesoamerican ritual practices.

*Psilocybe aztecorum* is distinguished from other Mexican psilocybin mushrooms by its preference for alpine and subalpine elevations above 3,000 meters.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Kingdom:** Fungi — **Division:** Basidiomycota — **Class:** Agaricomycetes — **Order:** Agaricales.

**Family:** Hymenogastraceae — **Genus:** Psilocybe — **Species:** P. aztecorum Heim.

**Authority:** R. Heim, 1958.

The cap is 1.5-3 cm in diameter, convex to broadly convex, sometimes with a slight umbo in age.

Cap color is chestnut-brown to ochraceous when moist, fading to straw-yellow or nearly white as it dries.

The cap surface is hygrophanous, smooth, and often develops a silky sheen when dry.

The gills are adnate to adnexed, grayish with whitish edges, becoming dark purplish-brown as spores mature.

The stipe is 50-75 mm long and 2-3 mm thick, equal, hollow, and whitish, bruising prominently blue when handled.

A partial veil is present in young specimens, sometimes leaving a fibrillose annular zone on the upper stipe.

Spores are dark purple-brown in deposit, ellipsoid to subellipsoid, 10-13 × 6.5-8 µm in size.

Cheilocystidia are ventricose with elongated necks, a characteristic feature of the species.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Psilocybe aztecorum* is endemic to the high mountains of central Mexico, particularly the Sierra Nevada and Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt.

Known localities include the slopes of Popocatépetl and Iztaccíhuatl volcanoes at elevations of 3,000-4,000 meters.

The species grows gregariously or scattered in montane and subalpine coniferous forests dominated by *Pinus hartwegii* and *Pinus montezumae*.

It fruits during the rainy season from July through October, often appearing in mossy areas along forest trails and meadow edges.

The high-elevation habitat is unusual among psilocybin mushrooms, most of which prefer lower-altitude subtropical environments.

## Bioactive Compounds

*Psilocybe aztecorum* contains the classic tryptamine alkaloids [[psilocin]] as its primary psychoactive constituents.

Psilocybin concentrations range from approximately 0.2-0.6% of dried mushroom weight, with the highest levels found in the cap.
