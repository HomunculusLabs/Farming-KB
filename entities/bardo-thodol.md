## Bardo Thodol

## Description

The Bardo Thodol (literally "Liberation by Hearing on the After-Death Plane") is a Tibetan Buddhist text known in the West as *The Tibetan Book of the Dead*. It is a guide for the consciousness of a dying person, describing the experiences expected at the moment of death, during a 49-day intermediate phase (bardo), and during rebirth into another bodily frame. The text was skillfully composed by blending the death ritual language and symbolism of Bonism (the pre-Buddhist Tibetan religion) with Buddhist mystical conceptions.

## Classification

- **Category:** Religious / mystical text
- **Tradition:** Tibetan Buddhism (Nyingma school)
- **Language of origin:** Tibetan
- **Historical period:** Composed approximately 8th–14th century CE

## Key Facts

- The text describes three successive bardo states: [[chikhai-bardo]] (moment of ego-loss/transcendence), [[chonyid-bardo]] (visions and hallucinatory experiences), and [[sidpa-bardo]] (the return to ordinary consciousness and rebirth impulse).
- It was not intended for general reading; it was designed to be understood only by those initiated personally by a guru into Buddhist mystical doctrines and the pre-mortem death-rebirth experience.
- The text stresses repeatedly that the free consciousness has only to hear and remember the teachings in order to be liberated.
- The exoteric framework uses [[shamanism-death-and-rebirth]] as metaphors; the esoteric meaning, as interpreted by [[lama-govinda]], is that it describes the death and rebirth of consciousness, not of the body — "It is a book for the living as well as the dying."
- The Bardo Thodol was translated into English by W.Y. Evans-Wentz (1927), with a psychological commentary by Carl Jung.

## Relevance to Psychedelic Studies

The Bardo Thodol served as the structural model for Timothy Leary, Ralph Metzner, and Richard Alpert's *The [[leary-psychedelic-experience-bardo-mapping]]* (1964). The three bardo phases were mapped directly onto the phases of an LSD session: initial transcendence, hallucinatory visions, and the struggle to return to ordinary ego-consciousness. This cross-cultural application was one of the most influential frameworks in the history of [[psychedelic-therapy-and-research]] guided session design.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

While not directly related to [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]], the Bardo Thodol is foundational to the understanding [[archaeology-of-psychedelic-mushroom-use]] experiences within a structured, intentional framework. Cultivators and facilitators working with [[amanita-muscaria-legal-alternative-psilocybin-mushrooms-europe]] frequently draw upon bardo-phase models for session preparation, guiding, and integration work.
