# Leptogium

## Description

*Leptogium* is a large genus of gelatinous cyanolichens in the family Collemataceae. The genus is characterized by foliose to subfruticose thalli with a gelatinous texture due to the abundance of cyanobacterial (*Nostoc*) cells distributed throughout the fungal medulla. *Leptogium* species are found in diverse habitats from arctic tundra to tropical rainforests, but their ecology in tropical environments has been particularly well studied. In the tropics, *Leptogium* species exhibit remarkably high rates of respiratory carbon loss, which severely limits their net primary productivity.

## Key Facts

- Lange et al. (2000) studied four *Leptogium* species in the Panamanian tropics and found that between 47% and 88.7% of photosynthetically fixed carbon is lost through respiration.
- *Leptogium azureum* showed the highest carbon loss at 88.7%, while *Leptogium phyllocarpon* juveniles showed the lowest at 47.1%.
- Net photosynthetic gain ranged from 6.2 mg C (gC)-1 d-1 (*L. azureum*) to 15.7 mg C (gC)-1 d-1 (*L. phyllocarpon* juvenile).
- High dark respiration rates in tropical environments are a primary factor limiting [[lichen]] biomass production in lowland tropical forests.
- The gelatinous nature of the thallus provides excellent water-holding capacity but also creates conditions for elevated metabolic rates when hydrated.
- Contains cyanobacterial photobionts (*Nostoc* spp.) that enable nitrogen fixation.

## Ecological Role

- **Tropical nutrient cycling**: Despite low net productivity, *Leptogium* species contribute to nitrogen input through cyanobacterial fixation in tropical ecosystems.
- **Epiphytic communities**: Many species grow on tree bark and rock surfaces in humid tropical and temperate forests.
- **Moisture indicators**: The rapid response of gelatinous thalli to hydration makes *Leptogium* species useful bioindicators of microclimatic moisture regimes.
- **Biodiversity hotspots**: The genus is species-rich in tropical montane cloud forests, where persistent moisture supports diverse lichen communities.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Carbon economy**: The dramatic carbon loss through respiration in tropical *Leptogium* species illustrates fundamental constraints on fungal-algal symbiosis at high temperatures.
- **Nitrogen fixation**: As cyanolichens, *Leptogium* species provide a model for biological nitrogen fixation in fungal symbioses.
- **Climate response**: The sensitivity of *Leptogium* carbon balance to temperature and moisture regimes makes the genus a valuable indicator for studying climate change effects on fungal symbioses.
- **Gelatinous lichen cultivation**: The water-holding properties of gelatinous [[lichens]] offer lessons for maintaining hydration in cultivated fungal systems.
