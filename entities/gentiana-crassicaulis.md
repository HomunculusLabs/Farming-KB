## Overview

Gentiana crassicaulis is a perennial herbaceous plant in the family Gentianaceae, native to the high-altitude meadows and shrublands of the eastern Himalayas and the Hengduan Mountains. It is commonly known as thick-stemmed gentian and is valued in traditional Chinese medicine under the name "Qin Jiao" alongside several related Gentiana species.

The species is distinguished by its robust, thickened stems and clusters of deep blue to violet trumpet-shaped flowers that bloom in late summer. It typically grows at elevations between 2,800 and 4,500 meters.

Gentiana crassicaulis is one of the official source species of the Qin Jiao drug in the Chinese Pharmacopoeia, alongside [[coleman-louis-savier-twentieth-century-maraicher]].

Key synonyms include Gentiana macrophylla var. crassicaulis and Gentiana wutaiensis. It is closely allied to Gentiana macrophylla but differs in its thicker stems and broader cauline leaves.

The plant reaches 30–60 cm in height with stout, unbranched stems that arise from a short, thick rhizome. The root system consists of a slender taproot with lateral branching, typically yellowish-brown externally.

Basal leaves are lanceolate to ovate-lanceolate, 6–15 cm long, with prominent parallel veins. They form a rosette that persists through the early [[clay-pellet-seed-dispersal]] occurs in October through wind and gravity.

## Distribution and Habitat

Gentiana crassicaulis is endemic to the eastern Himalayan region and the Hengduan Mountain range. Its range extends from Nepal and Bhutan through southern Tibet to western Sichuan, northwestern Yunnan, and southeastern Qinghai in China.

It grows in alpine meadows, shrub-grassland ecotones, and open forest margins at elevations of 2,800–4,500 meters. The species prefers moist, well-drained soils on slopes with partial shade.

Associated vegetation includes alpine Rhododendron thickets, Kobresia meadows, and scattered Betula and Salix shrubs. It often co-occurs with other high-altitude gentians such as [[gentiana-veitchiorum]].

The species is adapted to cold, semi-arid continental climates with intense UV radiation, short [[fukuoka-textdoc-composting-critique-futility-prepared-organic-matter]] content and slightly acidic to neutral pH (5.5–7.0). Populations at the upper elevational limit show stunted growth and earlier flowering phenology.

The species exhibits limited gene flow between isolated populations due to the fragmented nature of alpine habitat. Genetic studies reveal moderate population differentiation across the Hengduan Mountains, with higher diversity in central Sichuan populations.

## Chemistry and Bioactive Compounds

The roots of Gentiana crassicaulis are rich in iridoid glycosides, the primary class of [[gentiana-lutea]].

Phenolic acids including ferulic acid and sinapic acid are present in trace amounts. The overall phytochemical profile is consistent with the Qin Jiao group of gentians used in Chinese medicine.
