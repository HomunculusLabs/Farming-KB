# Monacrosporium

## Description

*Monacrosporium* is a genus of nematophagous fungi that capture and kill soil nematodes using adhesive hyphal structures (adhesive networks, adhesive knobs, and constricting rings). Viaene and Abawi (1998) identified *Monacrosporium* species as important natural enemies of plant-parasitic nematodes of the genus *Meloidogyne* (root-knot nematodes), trapping and killing second-stage juveniles in organic soils. The genus is closely related to *Arthrobotrys* and shares many morphological and ecological features with other nematode-trapping fungi.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Phylum**: Ascomycota
- **Class**: Sordariomycetes
- **Order**: Orbiliales
- **Family**: Orbiliaceae
- **Genus**: *Monacrosporium* (some species now reclassified to *Dactylellina* or *Gamsylella*)
- **Teleomorph**: *Orbilia*

## Key Facts

- **Trapping structures**: *Monacrosporium* species produce various nematode-trapping devices including adhesive three-dimensional networks, non-constricting rings, and constricting rings that close around nematodes via rapid turgor pressure changes.
- **Predatory mechanism**: When a nematode contacts an adhesive trap, it becomes firmly attached. The fungus then produces penetration pegs that breach the nematode cuticle, grows inside the nematode body, secretes digestive enzymes, and absorbs nutrients from the decomposing prey.
- **Nematode targets**: *Monacrosporium* species trap a wide range of soil nematodes, including plant-parasitic species (*Meloidogyne*, *Pratylenchus*, *Radopholus*) and free-living bacterivorous nematodes.
- **Organic soil prevalence**: Viaene and Abawi (1998) found that 5–24% of *Meloidogyne hapla* J2 juveniles in New York organic soils were attacked by nematophagous fungi including *Monacrosporium* and *Arthrobotrys*.
- **Taxonomic revision**: Modern molecular phylogenetics has led to the reclassification of many *Monacrosporium* species into genera such as *Dactylellina* (constricting ring producers) and *Gamsylella* (adhesive knob producers), though the traditional name persists in the literature.

## Ecological Role

- Contributes to natural regulation of plant-parasitic nematode populations in soil, providing a biological check on nematode abundance.
- Functions as both a predator (active trapping) and a saprotroph in the soil food web, with trapping structures induced by the presence of nematode prey or nematode-derived signaling compounds.
- Part of the broader community of soil fungi that maintain nematode populations below damaging thresholds in natural ecosystems.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Biocontrol potential**: *Monacrosporium* species have been investigated as biological control agents for plant-parasitic nematodes, particularly in organic farming systems where chemical nematicides are prohibited.
- **Inoculum challenges**: As with other nematophagous fungi, successful biocontrol requires adequate conidial density in soil. Kerry and Jafee (1997) identified density-dependent parasitism as a key factor — trapping efficiency is closely correlated with the density of fungal trapping structures.
- **Culture and application**: Can be grown on artificial media but formulation and delivery to field soils remains a practical challenge.
- **Integrated management**: Most effective when combined with other nematode management strategies (resistant cultivars, organic amendments, crop rotation) rather than used alone.
