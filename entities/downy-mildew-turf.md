# Downy Mildew (Turf)

## Description

[[downy-mildew]] of turf grasses is a disease caused by oomycete pathogens in the genus *Sclerophthora* spp. Unlike true fungi, the causal organisms are oomycetes (water molds), which are more closely related to brown algae. Downy mildew on turf produces yellowish streaks or patches on leaves and is favored by wet, poorly drained conditions. It is among the turf diseases that can be suppressed using fungally dominated compost teas as part of an integrated [[soil-food-web]] [[troika-management-approach-mollison]].

## Classification

- **Causal organism:** *Sclerophthora* spp. (Oomycota)
- **Kingdom:** Chromista (Stramenopiles)
- **Phylum:** Oomycota
- **Order:** Peronosporales
- **Family:** Peronosporaceae
- **Disease type:** Oomycete turf disease
- **Common name:** Downy mildew, yellow tuft

## Key Facts

- Caused by oomycete pathogens (not true fungi), which are related to brown algae and require free water for infection and spread
- *Sclerophthora* spp. produce zoospores that swim through water films on leaf surfaces to reach infection sites
- Favored by prolonged leaf wetness, poor drainage, and excessive irrigation
- Symptoms include yellowish to pale green streaks or patches, stunted growth, and in severe cases, tufted growth patterns (yellow tuft)
- Can be confused with nutrient [[knf-calcium-deficiency-symptoms]] (particularly nitrogen or iron)
- Affects cool-season turf grasses, especially under wet conditions
- Listed among the diseases suppressible by fungally dominated compost teas

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

### Soil Food Web Suppression

Downy mildew is included in the list of turf diseases that respond to **fungally dominated compost tea** applications. The mechanism of suppression involves:

- Beneficial fungi in the tea outcompete the oomycete pathogen for space and resources on leaf surfaces (phyllosphere) and in the root zone (rhizosphere)
- [[fungal-hyphae]] create physical barriers that prevent oomycete zoospores from reaching infection sites
- The diverse microbial community in compost tea produces antibiotic compounds and lytic enzymes that inhibit oomycete growth
- Improved soil drainage and structure (resulting from [[fungi-in-the-environment-fungal-soil-aggregation-structure]]) reduces the standing water that oomycetes require

### Oomycete Biology and Control Implications

Because oomycetes are not true fungi, some fungicides effective against true fungi may have limited efficacy against *Sclerophthora*. However, the ecological competition approach — using beneficial microbes to outcompete the pathogen — works regardless of [[neurotropic-fungi-taxonomic-classification]]. The key principle is that a diverse, healthy soil food web leaves no niche for pathogenic organisms to establish.

### Integrated Management

1. Improve drainage to reduce standing water and leaf wetness duration
2. Apply fungally dominated AACT as both soil drench and foliar spray
3. Reduce irrigation frequency and duration
4. Aerate compacted areas to improve water infiltration
5. Maintain a balanced soil food web with appropriate fungal:bacterial ratios
6. Apply compost top-dressing to introduce additional beneficial fungal diversity

## Practical Notes

- Downy mildew's requirement for free water makes [[hamilton-soil-drainage-improvement]] essential
- Fungally dominated compost tea should be applied preventatively during wet seasons
- The disease's oomycete nature means it may respond differently to conventional fungicides than true fungal diseases
- Biological soil testing can identify whether [[the-soil-food-web]] is sufficiently diverse to suppress oomycete pathogens
- Avoid overwatering as a primary preventive measure
