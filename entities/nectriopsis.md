# Nectriopsis

## Description

Nectriopsis is a genus of [[fungicolous-fungi]] in the family Bionectriaceae (Hypocreales, [[ascomycota]]) comprising approximately 39 described species. The genus is characterized by small, usually white to yellow perithecial ascomata that develop on or near fungal hosts. Most species are obligate or facultative mycoparasites, colonizing sporocarps of other fungi—particularly [[myxomycetes-slime-molds]]), discomycetes, agarics, and aphyllophorales.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Eukaryota
- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Sordariomycetes
- **Order:** Hypocreales
- **Family:** Bionectriaceae
- **Genus:** Nectriopsis

## Key Species

- **N. violacea** — Found exclusively on Fuligo species (myxomycetes). One of the more host-specific members of the genus. Belongs to the group that parasitizes calcium-rich fructifications of the Physarales.
- **N. exigua** (anamorph Verticillium rexianum) — One of the most ubiquitous myxomyceticolous species, recorded from all major groups of slime molds. Found exclusively on sporocarps of myxomycetes.
- **N. tubariicola** — Found on agarics, probably also a member of the Bionectriaceae.
- **N. discophila** — Found on Lachnum species (discomycetes).
- **N. albidopilosa** — Found on inoperculate discomycetes.

## Morphological Characteristics

- Perithecia small, typically white to yellow in color—a common feature among fungicolous taxa in the Hypocreales (Samuels 1988)
- Asci unitunicate, containing ascospores that are typically hyaline and septate
- Anamorphs include Verticillium-like phialidic conidiophores (e.g., V. rexianum for N. exigua)
- Mycelium grows over the surface of host fructifications, penetrating spore masses

## Ecology and Habitat

Nectriopsis species are found exclusively in association with other fungi, particularly:

1. **Myxomycetes (slime molds):** Several species are obligate colonizers of slime mold sporangia. They smother sporangia, usually turning them white, and penetrate the spore mass killing spores and preventing release. In most collections, only a single colonizing species is present (Rogerson and Stephenson 1993).

2. **Discomycetes:** Species like N. discophila parasitize cup fungi.

3. **Agarics:** N. tubariicola and related species colonize mushroom fruiting bodies.

## Mycoparasitic Behavior

Nectriopsis species function primarily as biotrophic or necrotrophic mycoparasites:

- Hyphae grow over the entire surface of host fructifications
- Penetration of host spore masses kills spores and prevents their release
- Whether most myxomyceticolous species should be viewed as true parasites is debated, as they colonize terminal phases of sporocarps after many spores have already been liberated (Hawksworth 1981b)
- The relationship is considered parasitic because the mycelium kills host spores and prevents release (Rogerson and Stephenson 1993)

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Ecological significance:** Nectriopsis species play roles in regulating slime mold populations in natural ecosystems
- **Biocontrol potential:** Species parasitizing pathogenic fungi could have applications in biological control
- **Taxonomic importance:** The genus illustrates the high degree of host specialization among fungicolous Hypocreales
- **Research interest:** Host specificity patterns (e.g., N. violacea on Fuligo, N. exigua on diverse hosts) provide insights into mycoparasite evolution

## Notes

The genus Nectriopsis overlaps morphologically with Nectria and related genera. Taxonomic revisions continue to refine species boundaries based on host specificity, cultural characteristics, and molecular data. The white to yellow perithecial coloration shared with other fungicolous Hypocreales may reflect convergent evolution in the mycoparasitic lifestyle.
