# Rubus idaeus
Rubus idaeus, the red raspberry, is a deciduous perennial shrub in the family
Rosaceae native to Europe and northern Asia. It is one of the most commercially
important berry crops worldwide, prized for its sweet-tart fruit that is consumed
fresh, frozen, and in processed products including jams, juices, and dietary
supplements. The species has exceptional permaculture value as a pioneer plant,
living hedge component, pollinator resource, and soil stabiliser on disturbed
ground. Raspberry leaves have a long history of medicinal use, particularly in
women's health, and the fruit is nutritionally dense with high concentrations of
ellagitannins, anthocyanins, and vitamin C. The plant's vigorous suckering
growth habit allows it to spread rapidly, forming dense thickets that provide
wildlife habitat and windbreaks.
## Taxonomy and Naming
- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Division:** Magnoliophyta
- **Class:** Magnoliopsida
- **Order:** Rosales
- **Family:** Rosaceae
- **Genus:** Rubus (subgenus Idaeobatus)
- **Species:** R. idaeus L., 1753
- **Common names:** Red raspberry, European raspberry, wild raspberry
- **Subspecies:** R. idaeus subsp. idaeus (European), R. idaeus subsp.
  strigosus (Michx.) Focke (North American); commercially interbred
- **Related species:** Black raspberry (R. occidentalis), blackberry
  (R. fruticosus agg.), arctic raspberry (R. arcticus), wineberry
  (R. phoenicolasius)
## Description
### Growth Habit and Morphology
- **Habit:** Deciduous shrub forming dense thickets via basal suckers;
  biennial canes (floricanes fruit in second year, then die); typically
  1–2 m tall in cultivation, up to 2.5 m in the wild
- **Canes (stems):** Round, glabrous to finely hairy, often with sparse
  weak prickles (much less thorny than blackberry); pale green the first
  year (primocanes), becoming brown and woody in the second year
  (floricanes); bark peeling on older canes
- **Leaves:** Pinnately compound, 5–7 leaflets per leaf; leaflets
  ovate, 3–8 cm long, serrate margin, white-tomentose beneath;
  terminal leaflet often larger; autumn colour yellow to red
- **Flowers:** White, 1–1.5 cm diameter, with 5 petals; arranged in
  axillary and terminal racemes or panicles; numerous stamens; flowering
  in late spring to early summer (May–July); self-fertile but bee
  pollination increases fruit set and size
- **Fruit:** Aggregate of drupelets (like all Rubus), 1–2 cm diameter,
  conical to hemispherical; red when ripe (some cultivars yellow, amber,
  or purple); easily separates from the receptacle (unlike blackberry);
  sweet-tart flavour; composed of 50–120 drupelets
- **Roots:** Fibrous, extensive; shallow but spreading; forms dense
  networks with abundant adventitious buds producing suckers at 30–60 cm
  from the parent plant
- **Lifespan:** Individual canes are biennial; the root system is
  perennial and can persist indefinitely, producing new canes annually

## Distribution and Habitat
- **Native range:** Europe (from Iceland and Norway to the Mediterranean),
  northern and temperate Asia (Turkey, Caucasus, Siberia, northern China,
  Japan)
- **Naturalised:** North America, parts of South America (Chile),
  New Zealand, and southeastern Australia
- **Habitat:** Forest margins, clearings, stream banks, roadsides,
  disturbed ground, mountain slopes, and subalpine meadows; a pioneer
  species that colonises disturbed sites rapidly
- **Current commercial production:** Major producers include Russia,
  Serbia, Poland, USA (Washington, Oregon, California), Mexico, Chile,
  Spain, and the UK; Serbia is the world's largest exporter of frozen
  raspberries
## Chemistry and Active Compounds
### Fruit Composition
- **Anthocyanins:** 20–60 mg/100g fresh weight; primarily cyanidin-3-
  O-sophoroside, cyanidin-3-O-glucoside, and cyanidin-3-O-rutinoside;
  responsible for red colour; potent antioxidants with anti-inflammatory
  and neuroprotective properties
- **Ellagitannins:** Raspberries are the richest dietary source of
  ellagitannins (sanguiin H-6, lambertianin C); metabolised by gut
  microbiota to urolithins, which have anti-cancer and anti-inflammatory
  activity; ellagitannin content 250–600 mg/100g
- **Vitamin C:** 25–30 mg/100g (40–50% RDI); heat-sensitive, reduced
  in processed products
- **Dietary fibre:** 6–7 g/100g fresh weight; one of the highest-fibre
  fruits; primarily insoluble cellulose and lignin from drupelet walls
  sucrose; moderate glycaemic index (approximately 40)
- **Minerals:** Manganese (exceptionally high at 0.7 mg/100g), magnesium,
  potassium, calcium, iron
### Leaf Constituents (Medicinal)
Raspberry leaves have been used medicinally for centuries, particularly
for women's reproductive health:
- **Tannins:** 8–10% dry weight; astringent properties; responsible for
  traditional use in treating diarrhoea and dysentery
- **Flavonoids:** Quercetin, kaempferol, tiliroside; anti-inflammatory
  and antioxidant
- **Fragarine:** Historically cited as a uterine tonic compound; modern
  analysis has not isolated a specific compound by this name; the
  uterine-relaxant effect is attributed to the synergistic action of
  tannins, flavonoids, and polypeptides
- **Vitamins and minerals:** Vitamin C, A, B-complex; calcium, iron,
  magnesium, phosphorus; high nutrient density in dried leaf
## Cultivation and Permaculture Role
### Commercial Cultivation
- **Types:** Summer-bearing (floricane) fruit on 2-year-old canes;
  everbearing/fall-bearing (primocane) fruit on first-year canes
  in late summer and again on lower portions the following summer
- **Support:** Trellis systems (T-bar, V-trellis, or single wire)
  are essential for commercial production; keeps canes upright and
  improves air circulation
- **Pruning:** Floricane types: remove fruited canes after harvest,
  thin primocanes to 4–6 per metre of row; primocane types: mow to
  ground level after fall harvest for simplified management
- **Yield:** 3–8 tonnes per hectare depending on variety, management,
  and climate; everbearing types can yield 10+ t/ha under optimal
  conditions
  tissue culture for certified virus-free planting stock; softwood
  cuttings under mist
### Permaculture Functions
- **Pioneer species:** Rapidly colonises disturbed ground; fixes
  atmospheric carbon and builds soil organic matter; prepares sites
  for succession by larger perennials and trees
- **Living hedge and windbreak:** Dense, thorny thickets provide
  wind protection, stock fencing, and security barriers; reduces wind
  speed by 40–60%
- **Pollinator resource:** Extended bloom period (4–6 weeks) provides
  critical nectar and pollen for bees, bumblebees, and hoverflies
  during a period when few other food sources are available
- **Wildlife habitat:** Dense thickets provide nesting sites and
  cover for birds and small mammals; fruit is a food source for
  numerous bird and mammal species
- **Erosion control:** Extensive fibrous root systems and rapid
  colonisation stabilise slopes, stream banks, and disturbed soil
- **Multi-strata integration:** Excellent understorey plant in
  food forests; tolerates partial shade from canopy trees; combines
  well with fruit trees, nitrogen fixers, and ground covers
- **Income generation:** High-value fruit commands premium prices
  at farmers' markets; low input costs after establishment; suitable
  for pick-your-own operations
