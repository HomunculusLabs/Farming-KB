# Armillaria gallica

## Overview

*Armillaria gallica* (syn. *A. bulbosa*, *A. lutea*) is a honey fungus in the family
Physalacriaceae, notable for forming some of the largest and longest-lived organisms
on Earth. A single clone in Michigan's Upper Peninsula was estimated at over 1,500
years old and spanning approximately 37 hectares. The species is primarily a root
rot pathogen of hardwood trees but also forms associations with living roots,
blurring the line between pathogen and symbiont.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi → **Division:** Basidiomycota → **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales → **Family:** Physalacriaceae
- **Genus:** *Armillaria* (Fr.) Staude → **Species:** *A. gallica* Marxm. & Romagn. (1987)
- **Synonyms:** *A. bulbosa* (Barla) Kile & Watling; *A. lutea* Gillet
- **Etymology:** *gallica* refers to France (Gaul), where the species was first
  described near Paris. The synonym *bulbosa* references the bulbous stipe base.

## Morphology

### Macroscopic Features

- **Cap:** 3–10 cm, convex to plano-convex with slight umbo; surface dry,
  smooth to finely scaly, honey-yellow to brownish-orange; margin lighter
- **Flesh:** White, firm, mild to slightly bitter taste
- **Gills:** Adnate to slightly decurrent, crowded, white to cream becoming
  pinkish-brown with age
- **Stipe:** 4–12 cm × 0.5–1.5 cm, central; white to pale brown with
  conspicuous ring zone; base distinctly bulbous in young specimens
- **Ring:** Membranous, thick, persistent, white with yellowish edges
- **Rhizomorphs:** Dark brown to black, 1–3 mm diameter, melanized outer cortex

### Microscopic Features

- **Basidia:** Clavate, 4-spored, 25–35 × 6–9 µm
- **Basidiospores:** Elliptical, smooth, hyaline, non-amyloid, 7–9 × 5–6.5 µm
- **Cheilocystidia:** Abundant, clavate to ventricose, 15–30 µm long
- **Pileipellis:** Cutis of repent hyphae, 3–6 µm wide

## Distribution and Habitat

- Widespread across temperate Northern Hemisphere: Europe, eastern North America,
  Pacific Northwest, East Asia, northern Africa
- Found in hardwood and mixed forests, preferring oak, beech, and maple hosts
- Fruits late summer to autumn (September–November in Northern Hemisphere)
- Grows in clusters at the base of living trees, stumps, or from buried roots
- Rhizomorphs spread through soil connecting root systems across large areas

## Pathogenicity

⚠️ Considered one of the most damaging forest pathogens in temperate regions.

- Primary cause of armillaria root rot in hardwood forests and orchards
- Infections begin at wound sites or through root contact via rhizomorphs
- Infected trees show thinning crowns, yellowing foliage, and eventual mortality
- Can persist in stumps and root systems for decades after host death
- Economic impact significant in vineyards, orchards, and timber plantations

**Disease cycle:** Rhizomorphs contact healthy roots → colonize cambium →
girdle root → spread upward through root collar → basidiocarps form →
spores initiate new infections on freshly cut stumps.

## Ecological Role

- **Wood decomposition:** White rot strategy; degrades lignin, cellulose, and
  hemicellulose; critical role in temperate forest nutrient cycling
- **Symbiotic phases:** Recent genomic evidence suggests dual
  saprotrophic-biotrophic lifestyle; may function as mycorrhizal partner
  in certain tree species during non-pathogenic phases
- **Ecosystem engineering:** Genet networks spanning hectares influence soil
  structure, water retention, and microbial communities; dead genet areas
  create canopy gaps driving forest succession

## Bioactive Compounds

- **Armillarin:** Sesquiterpene with antifungal properties
- **Polysaccharides:** Beta-glucans with immunomodulatory activity (in vitro)
- **Protocatechuic acid:** Phenolic antioxidant
- **Armillaquinone:** Naphthoquinone with cytotoxic properties
- Limited pharmacological research compared to other *Armillaria* species

## Differentiation from Related Species

| Feature | *A. gallica* | *A. mellea* | *A. ostoyae* |
|---------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| Cap color | Honey-yellow | Amber-brown | Dark brown |
| Stipe base | Bulbous | Clavate-rooting | No true bulb |
| Ring | Thick, white | Thin, fragile | Thick, brown |
| Spore size | 7–9 × 5–6.5 µm | 8–10 × 5.5–7 µm | 7.5–9.5 × 5.5–6.5 µm |
| Host preference | Hardwoods | Mixed/hardwood | Conifers |

## Genomic Significance

- Genome sequenced (2022) reveals extensive heterozygosity (~73 Mb)
- Large genome with numerous transposable elements
- Genome stability maintained despite clonal reproduction over millennia
- One of the best-studied organisms for clonal longevity in eukaryotes
