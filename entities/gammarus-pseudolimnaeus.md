## Description

**Gammarus pseudolimnaeus** is a species of freshwater amphipod crustacean in the family Gammaridae. It is an important leaf-shredding detritivore in stream ecosystems, playing a critical role in processing fungal-colonized leaf litter and serving as a key link in aquatic detrital food webs.








## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Animalia
- **Phylum**: Arthropoda
- **Subphylum**: Crustacea
- **Class**: Malacostraca
- **Order**: Amphipoda
- **Family**: Gammaridae
- **Genus**: Gammarus
- **Species**: G. pseudolimnaeus








## Key Facts

- Demonstrates selective feeding preferences among fungal species colonizing leaf litter
- Shown to have greater growth on leaves colonized by four preferred fungal species compared to six other species
- Functions as a leaf-shredder in aquatic ecosystems, processing fungal-conditioned leaf material
- Growth is enhanced on fungal-colonized leaves compared to uncolonized leaves
- Suberkropp (1992) suggests synergistic benefit from combined fungus-plus-leaf diet rather than either alone
- Fungal biomass accounts for only 1-5% of detrital biomass but makes up 90-95% of microbial biomass on decaying leaves








## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

G. pseudolimnaeus demonstrates the important concept of fungal conditioning, where fungal colonization improves the food value of leaf litter for invertebrate consumers. This principle is relevant to mycological applications in aquaculture and [[composting]], where fungal pre-treatment of substrates enhances their nutritional value. The species' selective feeding on specific fungal colonizers illustrates the importance of fungal species identity in determining ecological interactions.








## Ecological Role

As a dominant shredder in temperate stream ecosystems, G. pseudolimnaeus processes coarse particulate organic matter into fine particles, making nutrients available to other organisms. Its feeding preference for fungal-colonized leaves drives the coupling between [[fungal-decomposition]] and invertebrate secondary production in aquatic ecosystems.
