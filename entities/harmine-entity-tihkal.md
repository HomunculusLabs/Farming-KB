# Harmine (7-Methoxy-beta-Carboline)

TIHKAL entry #14. A beta-carboline alkaloid and monoamine oxidase inhibitor found in [[mckenna-food-gods-peganum-harmala-soma]] ([[golden-guide-syrian-rue-and-maoi-plants]]) and Banisteriopsis caapi (ayahuasca). Once known as "banisterine" or "telepathine," harmine was briefly celebrated as a wonder drug for Parkinson's disease in the late 1920s before falling out of favor. It is a more effective facilitator of oral DMT activity than harmaline, as it is substantially without action at effective MAOI doses.

## Chemical Names

- beta-Carboline, 7-methoxy
- 7-Methoxy-beta-carboline
- Banisterine
- Yageine
- Telepathine
- Leucoharmine

## Dosage

| Route | Dose Range |
|-------|-----------|
| Oral | >300 mg (for own psychoactive effects) |
| Oral (as DMT facilitator) | 140–190 mg with 35–40 mg DMT |
| Intramuscular | 25–75 mg |
| Intravenous | 35–150 mg |

## Duration

Unknown for own effects (reported effects brief). DMT potentiation lasts 1–3 hours.

## Qualitative Comments

- **35 mg, orally and intranasally**: "No notable psychoactive or somatic effect. Harmine not detected in plasma samples."
- **35-40 mg, i.v.** (Pennes and Hoch): "Bradycardia, trouble focusing eyes, tingling, hypotension, cold extremities, light-headedness. All symptoms disappeared within 45 minutes except bradycardia in two subjects and drowsiness in three."
- **40 mg, orally**: "Immediate sensation of excitement with difficulty remaining in one place. Restlessness was predominant. All activity performed as if with greater ease. Consciousness felt packed with ether."
- **>40 mg, orally**: "The excitement increased even in a belligerent way. I started a fight with a man in the street."
- **140 mg, orally**: "No stimulation, no suggestion of entheogenic response, perhaps a little bit of sedation."
- **150-200 mg, i.v.** (Pennes and Hoch): "5 of 11 subjects reported visual hallucinations of varying complexity. Bradycardia and hypotension occurred with all doses despite 20-30 min injection time."
- **300 mg, sublingually**: "Pleasantly relaxed and withdrawn from environment. Slightly diminished capacity to concentrate."
- **750 mg, sublingually**: "Dizziness, nausea and ataxia. I do not choose to go any higher — there must be other substances responsible for the hallucinogenic effects of Ayahuasca."

## DMT Combinations

- **140–190 mg harmine + 35–40 mg DMT**: Produced unmistakable effects lasting 1–3 hours.
- **120–140 mg harmine + 30 mg DMT**: No signs of central activity at all.

The threshold for effective DMT potentiation is narrow: 140-190 mg works, but 120-140 mg does not. This suggests that MAO inhibition must reach a critical threshold before oral DMT becomes bioavailable.

## The Banisterine Story — Wonder Drug of 1928

The medical history of harmine is one of the most dramatic in psychopharmacology:

**Discovery**: Louis Lewin (of Phantastica fame) first suggested banisterine for nervous system diseases.

**Clinical success**: Kurt Beringer ran the first clinical study in 1928 on 15 postencephalitic Parkinson's patients. Initial doses of 20-40 mg i.m. produced less motor rigidity and easier movement within 15 minutes. Even oral doses of 10 mg thrice daily showed remarkable responses — tremor diminished in some, exaggerated in others, but mental status brightened without producing "psychic" effects. Banisterine became the "wonder drug of the year."

**Rapid fall from favor**: Three factors contributed to its decline:
1. A large withdrawal study failed — patients controlled on scopolamine deteriorated and did not respond to banisterine
2. Dr. Halpern self-experimented and published findings of belligerent aggression
3. The pharmaceutical industry was moving toward patentable synthetic compounds, losing interest in natural products

## The MPTP Connection

A remarkable chemical parallel exists between harmine and MPTP, the neurotoxin that causes irreversible Parkinsonism. During illicit synthesis of a meperidine analogue, dehydration produced MPTP, which is aromatized in the brain to MPP+, a toxic quaternary salt. If a N-methyl bridge were placed between the two rings of MPP+, the result would be a carboline — specifically 2,N-dimethylharmine, which has been synthesized and vies with MPP+ as a neurotoxin. Thus harmine can serve as a treatment for Parkinson's disease, while a dimethylated harmine can be a potential causative agent — a striking pharmacological paradox.

## Harmine vs Harmaline as DMT Facilitators

Harmine is more closely related to the basic structure of ayahuasca than harmaline — Banisteriopsis caapi contains much more harmine than harmaline. Unlike harmaline, harmine can serve as a true facilitator at doses where it is itself substantially without action (140-190 mg produces no subjective effects but enables oral DMT), making it more suitable as a potentiator.

## Physical Properties

- Free base: off-white powder
- HCl hydrate IR fingerprint (cm-1): 737, 800, 821, 1021, 1076, 1110, 1138, 1162
- MS (m/z): parent ion 212 (100%); 169 (67%); 197 (24%)

## Synthesis

Harmine is prepared by oxidative dehydrogenation of harmaline: harmaline HCl is treated with concentrated HNO3 in ethanol, producing an exothermic reaction. The resulting harmine HCl monohydrate is neutralized with NH4OH to give the free base (89% yield from HCl, 67% overall from harmaline).
