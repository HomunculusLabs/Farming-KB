# Vole Trapping (Coleman Method)

## Description

A highly effective, bait-free vole trapping system developed on Eliot Coleman's farm that exploits vole behavior -- specifically their tendency to dart into small dark holes -- rather than relying on food bait. The method uses simple wooden box traps placed at ground level with mouse-sized entrance holes, capitalizing on natural vole behavior patterns.

## Classification

- **Category**: Technique / Pest management
- **Domain**: Market gardening, small farm management
- **Target pest**: Voles (field mice, genus Microtus)

## Key Facts

- Eliminates the need for bait entirely by exploiting vole hole-entering behavior
- Box dimensions: approximately 12 inches long by 8 inches wide by 6 inches deep
- Construction: wooden box with fixed bottom, removable top, mouse-sized holes drilled at floor level in each 8-inch end
- Standard mouse traps placed inside each entrance hole -- un-baited
- Once the first vole is caught, the box smells like vole, which attracts more voles (they have no aversion)
- Daily maintenance: lift the top each morning, empty traps, reset
- Described as "impressively effective" and continues to work long-term

## Design Rationale

Traditional baited trapping requires maintaining attractive baits and suffers from bait avoidance. By removing bait and relying purely on the vole's instinct to investigate dark holes, the system becomes self-sustaining. The "scent feedback loop" -- where captured voles leave scent that attracts more voles -- makes the traps more effective over time rather than less.

## Relevance to Cultivation

Voles can cause significant damage to root crops, young transplants, and direct-seeded crops in both field and greenhouse settings. This method provides a low-cost, low-maintenance solution that avoids poisons and does not require purchasing ongoing bait supplies. The principle of designing pest management around animal behavior rather than chemical attractants aligns with the deep-organic farming philosophy.

## Construction and Use

1. Build wooden boxes to specified dimensions with removable lids
2. Drill rough mouse-sized holes at floor level in both short ends
3. Place one standard mouse trap inside each entrance hole -- no bait needed
4. Position boxes in areas of vole activity
5. Check and empty traps each morning
6. Reset traps; the accumulated vole scent improves effectiveness over time
