# Cambarus clarkii (Red Swamp Crayfish)

## Description
*Cambarus clarkii* (also referred to as *Procambarus clarkii* in modern taxonomy), the red swamp crayfish, is a freshwater crustacean native to the southeastern United States. It is one of the most commercially important crayfish species worldwide and is widely cultured in rice paddy systems, particularly in Asia. In [[permaculture-design]], crayfish serve as both a protein crop and a benthic detritivore in integrated pond systems.

## Classification

## Key Facts

### Habitat and Biology
- Prefers slow-moving or standing freshwater: ponds, swamps, rice paddies, ditches
- Burrows into mud during dry periods, surviving desiccation
- Omnivorous detritivore -- consumes decaying plant matter, small invertebrates, and detritus
- Tolerates a wide range of water quality conditions

### Role in Rice Paddy Systems
- Stocked in rice paddies as a dual crop alongside rice
- Paddy systems are described as difficult and specialised but yield significant additional protein
- Fish yields in crayfish-stocked rice paddies can add a critical nutritional element
- Benefits from shelter refuges: pots, pipes, bundles of rope, reeds, or brush
- Yield increases of 10-30% recorded when refuges are provided for crustaceans

### Production
- Grown extensively in Louisiana (USA), China, Spain, and other countries
- Compatible with rice-fish polyculture systems
- Benefits from liming and moderate fertilisation
- Harvested by trapping or draining

### Integration with Other Species
- Can coexist with fish polycultures in deeper pond systems
- Requires shelter from predatory fish
- Mud-burrowing behaviour aerates pond sediments and improves nutrient cycling

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology
- Crayfish pond sediments and waste contribute to organic matter accumulation useful for composting
- The rice-crayfish dual-crop model parallels the integrated cultivation approach used in mushroom-crop systems
