# Simmondsia chinensis

**Type:** Species
**Category:** Evergreen drought-tolerant shrub
**Also known as:** Jojoba, goat nut, pig nut, coffee bush, deer nut

## Overview
Simmondsia chinensis (jojoba) is a drought-tolerant evergreen shrub
native to the Sonoran Desert and Californian Gulf region. It produces
a unique liquid wax from its seeds that is highly prized in cosmetics,
lubricants, and industry. The plant lives over 100 years and represents
one of the most commercially significant arid-zone crop species.

## Key Details

### Botany
- **Family:** Simmondsiaceae (monotypic family)
- **Form:** Evergreen shrub to 5 m tall, 6 m spread
- **Stems:** Usually multi-stemmed; trunks to 400 mm diameter
- **Lifespan:** Over 100 years

### Growth Conditions
- **Rainfall:** Establishes at 200 mm; best production above 400 mm;
  tolerates up to 1,300 mm with good drainage
- **Temperature:** Established plants withstand -9 degrees C;
  flower buds damaged at -2 degrees C
- **Heat tolerance:** Soil temperature to 65 degrees C; ambient air
  to 48 degrees C
- **Soils:** Tolerant of most soil types; needs reasonable porosity;
  will not tolerate waterlogging
- **Salinity:** Very resistant; trials show no production loss at
  7,000 ppm salinity

### Seed and Oil
- **Oil type:** Liquid wax (not a true oil); chemically unique
- **Uses:** Cosmetics, shampoos, high-grade lubricant for precision
  machinery
- **Historical use:** Replaced sperm whale oil in industrial
  applications
- **Oil cake:** 30% protein; considered good stock feed after
  extraction
- **Edible nuts:** Eaten raw or roasted by indigenous peoples

### Traditional and Indigenous Uses
- Name "jojoba" from the [[dighton-fungal-drought-tolerance-plant-water-relations]], longevity, and salinity resistance
make it ideal for desert agroforestry, while the protein-rich seed
cake provides a valuable livestock feed byproduct.
