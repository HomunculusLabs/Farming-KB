## Overview

**Dicranum scoparium** (broom moss, windswept moss, dicranum moss) is a widespread acrocarpous moss in the family Dicranaceae. It forms dense, cushion-like tufts that are characteristically curved and swept to one side, giving it a windswept appearance.

The species is among the most recognizable forest-floor mosses in temperate and boreal regions of the Northern Hemisphere. It commonly colonizes decaying logs, tree bases, stumps, and rocky substrates in coniferous and mixed woodlands.

Dicranum scoparium is an important contributor to forest floor moisture retention and provides microhabitat for invertebrates. Its distinctive growth form and wide distribution make it a frequent subject of bryological field study.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

- **Division:** Bryophyta
- **Class:** Bryopsida
- **Order:** Dicranales
- **Family:** Dicranaceae
- **Genus:** *Dicranum* Hedw.
- **Species:** *D. scoparium* Hedw.
- **Authority:** Johann Hedwig, 1801
- **Synonyms:** *Dicranum scoparium* var. *scoparium*; *Dicranum undulatum* Ehrh. ex Weber

Gametophytes form dense turfs 3 to 10 cm tall. Stems are erect, simple or sparingly branched, and tomentose at the base with reddish-brown rhizoids.

Leaves are lanceolate, 5 to 8 mm long, falcate-secund (curved to one side) when dry. The leaf margin is recurved from near the base to near the apex, and the costa is strong, extending to the leaf tip or shortly excurrent.

Leaf cells are smooth, quadrate to rectangular in the alar regions, and elongate and sinuose in the upper lamina. The combination of falcate-secund leaves and recurved margins distinguishes this species from congeners.

When moist, the leaves spread outward and the turf appears softer and more open. Upon drying, leaves contract and curve tightly, giving the cushion its characteristic swept or broom-like appearance. This hygroscopic movement aids water channeling toward the stem base.

In North America, it ranges from Alaska to Newfoundland and southward in the Appalachian Mountains to Georgia. In Europe, it is found from Scandinavia to the Mediterranean and eastward through Russia. Disjunct populations occur in montane North Africa and the Caucasus.

The species colonizes a wide range of substrates including rotting logs, tree bases, humus-covered rocks, cliff ledges, and forest floor litter. It shows a strong preference for acidic substrates in shaded to partially shaded locations.

It is particularly abundant on decaying conifer stumps and logs in old-growth forests, where it forms extensive pure stands. It also occurs as an epiphyte on tree trunks and roots, especially in humid microsites.

Dicranum scoparium tolerates moderate light levels and can persist in forest openings and edges. However, it achieves maximum vigor under closed canopy conditions where humidity remains consistently high and direct sun exposure is limited.
