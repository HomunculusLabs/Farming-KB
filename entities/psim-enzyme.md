# PsiM (Norbaeocystin N-Methyltransferase)

**PsiM** is an S-adenosyl-L-methionine (SAM)-dependent N-methyltransferase and the final enzyme in the psilocybin biosynthetic pathway. It catalyzes the iterative N-methylation of norbaeocystin through baeocystin to psilocybin, making it the primary determinant of the ratio of psilocybin-related alkaloids found in a given fungal species or strain.






## Biochemical Function

PsiM transfers methyl groups from SAM to the amine nitrogen of its phosphoryloxytryptamine substrates in a stepwise fashion:

1. **Norbaeocystin** (desmethyl) + SAM → **Baeocystin** (monomethyl) + S-adenosyl-L-homocysteine (SAH)
2. **Baeocystin** (monomethyl) + SAM → **Psilocybin** (dimethyl) + SAH

This iterative mechanism means PsiM controls the relative proportions of norbaeocystin, baeocystin, and psilocybin in the final alkaloid profile. Species or strains with less efficient PsiM activity accumulate more baeocystin and norbaeocystin relative to psilocybin.






## Position in the Psilocybin Pathway

PsiM occupies the terminal position in the core biosynthetic pathway:

1. L-Tryptophan → (PsiD) → Tryptamine → (PsiH) → 4-HT → (PsiK) → Norbaeocystin → **(PsiM)** → Baeocystin → **(PsiM)** → Psilocybin

The dual-step activity of PsiM means it acts twice on the pathway intermediate, progressively building the N,N-dimethyl substitution pattern that characterizes psilocybin.






## Enzymatic Properties

- **Cofactor:** S-adenosyl-L-methionine (SAM)
- **Substrates:** Norbaeocystin (primary), baeocystin (secondary)
- **Products:** Baeocystin (from norbaeocystin), psilocybin (from baeocystin)
- **Reaction type:** N-methylation (methyltransferase)
- **Kinetics:** The second methylation step (baeocystin → psilocybin) is typically slower than the first (norbaeocystin → baeocystin), which explains the common observation that baeocystin accumulates to higher levels than norbaeocystin in most species






## Role in Chemotaxonomic Variation

The expression level and catalytic efficiency of PsiM vary significantly between species and even between strains of the same species. This variation accounts for much of the observed diversity in alkaloid profiles across the genus *Psilocybe*:

- High PsiM expression → high psilocybin:baeocystin ratio (e.g., *P. azurescens*, *P. bohemica*)
- Moderate PsiM expression → mixed alkaloid profile with notable baeocystin (e.g., *P. cubensis*)
- Low PsiM expression → elevated baeocystin and norbaeocystin relative to psilocybin






## Biotechnological Applications

PsiM is a target for metabolic engineering in synthetic biology platforms. Overexpression of PsiM in engineered microbial systems shifts production toward psilocybin rather than the intermediate alkaloids. Protein engineering efforts have explored altering PsiM's substrate preference to produce novel N-alkylated derivatives.
