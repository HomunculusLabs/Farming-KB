# DOC

DOC (2,5-Dimethoxy-4-chloroamphetamine) is a potent, long-acting psychedelic amphetamine and a member of the DOX family.

It is the 4-chloro substituted analogue of the classic 2,5-dimethoxyamphetamine series, documented as entry #64 in PiHKAL by Alexander Shulgin.

## Chemical Identity

Full chemical name: 2,5-Dimethoxy-4-chloroamphetamine.

Molecular formula: C11H16ClNO2.

DOC is one of the four classic "DOX" compounds alongside dob-entity|DOB (bromo), doi-entity|DOI (iodo), and doef-entity|DOEF (2-fluoroethyl).

These four haloamphetamines share a nearly identical substitution pattern, differing only in the halogen or halogen-containing group at the 4-position of the aromatic ring.

The hydrochloride salt melts at 187-188 degrees Celsius (from acetone/ethanol) or 193-194.5 degrees Celsius (from ethanol/ether), depending on the synthetic route.

## Dosage and Duration

Effective oral dosage: 1.5 - 3.0 mg.

Duration of effects: 12 - 24 hours.

DOC is extremely potent, requiring only milligram doses for full psychedelic effects.

The onset is gradual, with effects building slowly over the first 2-3 hours.

The remarkably long duration is characteristic of all DOX compounds, and users are advised to plan for a full day of altered consciousness.

Sleep may be difficult for up to 24 hours after ingestion.

## Qualitative Effects

At 1.6 mg, users report a pleasant "spacey" disconnection that is described as favorable and fun, with residual effects still noticeable 12 hours post-dose.

At 2.4 mg, the experience is described as "archetypal psychedelic" with full sensory immersion.

Visual effects, interpretive challenges, and the characteristic sense of being in a place "where nothing makes sense, and yet everything makes sense" are prominent at this dose.

Eyes-closed fantasy is described as a "completely dark screen, lovely and seductive, subtle, and yet light must be deliberately brought in."

Body comfort is generally good despite the intensity of the mental effects.

The compound is neither consistently anti-erotic nor consistently anorexic.

Sleep architecture may be disturbed, with reports of tight dreams and defensive dreaming patterns even 24 hours post-dose.

## Synthesis

The synthesis starts from 2c-d-entity|2,5-DMA (2,5-dimethoxyamphetamine), which serves as the precursor for all DOX compounds.

The free base of 2,5-DMA is dissolved in glacial acetic acid and cooled to 0 degrees Celsius.

Liquid chlorine is added dropwise, introducing the chloro group at the activated 4-position of the aromatic ring.

The reaction proceeds via electrophilic aromatic substitution, with the strongly activated 2,5-dimethoxy pattern directing substitution exclusively to the 4-position.

The product is isolated as the hydrochloride salt in approximately 20% yield after recrystallization.

## Pharmacological Context

DOC acts as a potent agonist at the 5-HT2A receptor, the primary receptor believed responsible for psychedelic effects.

Its potency and duration are comparable to dob-entity|DOB and doi-entity|DOI, with all three requiring similar milligram doses and producing 12-24 hour experiences.

The long duration is attributed to the metabolic stability of the 4-haloamphetamines, which resist rapid deamination.

DOC has been studied in the context of structure-activity relationships within the DOX family, helping to establish that the 4-position halogen is critical for psychedelic activity in this class.

## Comparison with Other DOX Compounds

dob-entity|DOB (4-bromo) and doi-entity|DOI (4-iodo) share nearly identical potency and duration with DOC.

doet-entity|DOET (4-ethyl) is a separate compound with a different character, originally studied for its cognitive-enhancing properties at low doses.

doef-entity|DOEF (4-2-fluoroethyl) extends the side chain and alters the pharmacological profile.

Shulgin noted that the differences between the three haloamphetamines (DOI, DOB, and DOC) are subtle, with all being "pretty much of the same potency" and "all of them very long lived."

## Intermediates and Derivatives

Two intermediates in the synthesis of DOC are noted as potential future research targets.

DON (2,5-dimethoxy-4-nitroamphetamine) can be reduced to DOA (2,5-dimethoxy-4-aminoamphetamine), melting at 248-250 degrees Celsius.

The acetamido analogue, DOAA (2,5-dimethoxy-4-acetamidoamphetamine), melts at 249-250 degrees Celsius.

Neither DOA nor DOAA has been bioassayed in humans as of the PiHKAL publication.

## Legal Status

DOC is classified as a Schedule I controlled substance in the United States.

It is also controlled in many other jurisdictions due to its classification as a positional isomer of scheduled DOX compounds.

The compound is specifically named in many analogue drug laws worldwide.

## Harm Reduction

The extremely low active dose (1.5-3 mg) makes accurate measurement critical.

A milligram scale is essential for safe dosing.

The 12-24 hour duration means users should have no responsibilities requiring normal cognitive function for at least 24 hours after dosing.

DOC should never be confused with mdma-entity|MDMA or other substances sold on the unregulated market, as the dose difference could be fatal.

The compound should not be combined with other serotonergic substances.

Individual sensitivity varies, and users are advised to start at the lower end of the dose range.
