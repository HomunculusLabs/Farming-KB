# Rick Strassman

## Overview

Rick Strassman (born 1952) is an American clinical psychiatrist and
psychopharmacologist best known for conducting the first federally
approved human research with psychedelic drugs in the United States in
over two decades. Between 1990 and 1995, Strassman administered over
400 doses of DMT to approximately 60 human volunteers at the University
of New Mexico, generating foundational data on DMT pharmacology,
psychology, and phenomenology. His 2001 book *DMT: The Spirit Molecule*
brought [[mckenna-suppression-psychedelic-research-lsd-therapy]] to mainstream attention and helped catalyze
the modern psychedelic renaissance.

## Education and Training

- **Stanford University** (BA): Biological sciences, early 1970s.
- **Albert Einstein College of Medicine** (MD): 1977.
- **UC Davis Medical Center**: Psychiatric residency, completed 1981.
- Additional psychopharmacology research fellowship at UCSF.

## Early Career

Strassman joined the University of New Mexico (UNM) psychiatry faculty.
His early research focused on the pineal gland and melatonin, studying
circadian rhythms and neuroendocrinology of altered consciousness. This
led to his interest in DMT, hypothesized by Julius Axelrod in the 1960s
to be an endogenous brain compound.

Strassman proposed DMT might be synthesized in the pineal gland and
released during extraordinary states — near-death experiences, mystical
states, meditation, and dreaming. This "spirit molecule" hypothesis
became his central thesis.

## The DMT Research Project (1990-1995)

### Regulatory Approval

Strassman obtained approvals from UNM IRB, the FDA (IND application),
and DEA Schedule I research license #RS001 — the first such license
issued in over two decades. This made his the first FDA-approved human
psychedelic study since the mid-1970s.

### Study Design

Research was conducted at UNM Hospital with healthy adults who had
prior [[leary-psychedelic-experience-bardo-mapping]], screened extensively. Studies progressed
through escalating IV DMT doses:

- **Dose-finding**: Established safe parameters for IV DMT — threshold,
  breakthrough, and maximum tolerated doses.
- **Pilot studies**: Explored subjective effects, physiological
  responses, and psychological integration.
- **Full studies**: Systematically investigated repeated DMT sessions.

Approximately 400 doses administered to ~60 volunteers. Doses ranged
from 0.05 mg/kg to 0.4 mg/kg IV; effects lasted 15-30 minutes.

### Key Clinical Findings

- DMT produced rapid-onset, intensely immersive psychedelic experiences
  with complete same-day tolerance.
- At higher doses, volunteers consistently reported contact with
  apparently autonomous entities ("beings," "aliens," "teaching entities").
- Near-death and mystical-type experiences were common, often rated
  among volunteers most significant life experiences.
- Physiological effects: transient heart rate, blood pressure, pupil
  dilation, and temperature increases — all within safe ranges.
- No serious adverse events or lasting psychopathology in controlled
  setting.
- Hormone measurements (melatonin, beta-endorphin, cortisol, DHEA)
  showed complex interactions with DMT.

## *DMT: The Spirit Molecule* (2001)

Strassman popular science book synthesized [[hofmann-lsd-clinical-research-early-studies]] with
speculative hypotheses about [[mckenna-endogenous-dmt-brain-familiarity]]. Published by Park Street
Press, it became a cult classic introducing millions to psychedelic
science. A 2010 documentary (Mitch Schultz) further amplified reach.

The pineal gland DMT hypothesis was presented as speculative, but this
nuance was often lost, leading to the widespread unsupported claim that
DMT is definitively produced in the pineal gland or released at death.

## Later Work

- *DMT: The Spirit Molecule* (2001) — Clinical research and hypotheses.
- *Inner Paths to Outer Space* (2008, co-edited with Wojtowicz, Luna,
  Frecska) — [[dmt-entity-encounters-and-the-logos]] and parallels with UFO contact and
  religious revelation.
- *The Psychedelic Census* — Ongoing survey project on psychedelic
  experiences.

Strassman has lectured widely and served on advisory boards for several
psychedelic research organizations.

## Buddhist Practice

Strassman is a longtime Zen Buddhism practitioner in Soto and Rinzai
traditions. His practice informed his research, particularly the
phenomenological parallels between psychedelic states and meditative
states (jhana, samadhi). He maintains the two are distinct paths with
different goals.

## Criticisms

- **Pineal hypothesis overstated**: DMT production in pineal remains
  unproven; INMT is present but definitive in vivo evidence lacking.
- **Entity encounters**: Skeptics argued these reflected expectancy
  effects and cultural priming.
- **Study limitations**: Small samples, no adequate active placebo,
  experienced users limited generalizability.
- **Spiritual framing**: Some felt religious language undermined
  scientific credibility.

## Legacy

Strassman DMT research is a landmark in psychopharmacology. It proved
rigorous, federally approved human psychedelic research was possible
after decades of prohibition, and established the [[cannabis-safety-profile-and-overdose-risk]] and
phenomenological map of IV DMT that informed all subsequent research.
