## Downstream Signaling Cascade

1. **BRI1/BAK1 activation**: Ligand binding and reciprocal trans-
   phosphorylation at the plasma membrane.
2. **RLCK phosphorylation**: BSK1, CDG1, and related kinases are activated.
3. **BSU1 phosphatase activation**: Activated RLCKs promote BSU1 activation.
4. **BIN2 kinase inactivation**: BSU1 dephosphorylates and inactivates
   BIN2, a GSK3-like kinase that normally phosphorylates and inhibits
   brassinosteroid-responsive transcription factors.
5. **BZR1/BES1 release**: When BIN2 is inactive, BZR1 and BES1
   transcription factors are dephosphorylated, allowing nuclear
   translocation, DNA binding, and regulation of thousands of genes.

## BRI1 Mutants and Phenotypes

- **bri1 null mutants**: Extreme dwarfism (~10% of wild-type height),
  dark green leaves, reduced apical dominance, male sterility.
- **bri1 weak alleles**: Moderate dwarfism, useful for dose-response
  studies and agricultural applications where reduced height is desired.
- **bri1 overexpressors**: Enhanced brassinosteroid sensitivity and
  larger organ size.

The severity of bri1 phenotypes established that brassinosteroids are
essential hormones, as mutants cannot be rescued by any other hormone
class. This genetic evidence was the final proof of brassinosteroid
essentiality.

## Cross-Kingdom Considerations

The discovery that [[psilocybe-cubensis]] (demonstrated by [[adam-vorbrodt]]) raises questions about fungal brassinosteroid-binding
proteins. No fungal BRI1 homologs have been identified, as fungi lack
the LRR-RLK family entirely. Possible explanations include convergent
evolution of brassinosteroid-responsive mechanisms, interaction with
conserved sterol-binding domains in fungal proteins, or indirect effects
through membrane modulation. The shared sterol biochemistry of plants and
fungi may provide a basis for the observed cross-kingdom activity.
