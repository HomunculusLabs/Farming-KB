# Peganum harmala L.

## Common Names

[[golden-guide-syrian-rue-and-maoi-plants]], Harmal, Harmel, Aspand, Esfand, African Rue, Wild Rue,
Harmala.

## Taxonomy

**Family:** Zygophyllaceae (Caltrop Family)
**Order:** Zygophyllales
**Genus:** Peganum
**Species:** P. harmala L.

Peganum harmala is the type and most widely known species of the genus
Peganum, which contains a small number of species distributed across
arid and semi-arid regions of the [[datura-metel-old-world-ethnobotany]]. The genus is placed in
the family Zygophyllaceae, though some modern classifications have
proposed moving it to its own monogeneric family, Nitrariaceae, based
on molecular phylogenetic evidence.

## Description

Peganum harmala is a perennial, herbaceous to suffruticose plant
growing 1 to 2 ft (30 to 60 cm) tall, though robust specimens may
reach 3 ft (90 cm). The plant forms a dense, bushy clump from a
woody rootstock with a deep and extensive taproot system that enables
it to survive in [[fungal-diversity-arid-environments]].

Stems are erect, branched from the base, glabrous or finely
pubescent, and pale green to grayish-green in color. The leaves are
alternate, sessile, deeply two to three times pinnatisect, with
narrow, linear segments 0.4 to 0.8 in (1 to 2 cm) long. The foliage
has a distinctive, somewhat acrid odor.

Flowers are solitary or borne in few-flowered terminal cymes. Each
flower has five pale greenish-white to yellowish petals, 0.5 to
0.75 in (1.2 to 2 cm) long, and is subtended by five lanceolate
sepals slightly shorter than the petals. Flowers are fragrant and
open in the morning.

The fruit is a globose, three-lobed capsule approximately 0.5 to
0.75 in (1.2 to 2 cm) in diameter, brown when mature, splitting
open to release numerous small, angular, brownish-black seeds. Each
capsule may contain 30 to 50 or more seeds.

## Distribution and Habitat

Peganum harmala is native to a broad region extending from western
Asia (Asia Minor, the Levant, Iran) across Central Asia to northern
India, with disjunct populations in Mongolia and Manchuria. The plant
has been widely naturalized in other regions, including the
Mediterranean basin, North Africa, the southwestern [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]],
and parts of [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-south-america]].

It grows in dry, sandy or stony soils, steppe and desert margins,
saline flats, abandoned fields, and along roadsides. The plant is
remarkably drought-tolerant and thrives in conditions that would be
hostile to most vegetation.

## Chemistry

The seeds of Peganum harmala are rich in beta-carboline alkaloids,
which are potent inhibitors of monoamine oxidase (MAO). The principal
alkaloids include:

- **Harmine** — the major alkaloid, a reversible inhibitor of MAO-A,
  with hallucinogenic properties at sufficient doses.
- **[[harmaline]]** — closely related to harmine, also an MAO-A inhibitor,
  with additional tremorigenic effects at higher doses.
- **Harmalol** — a minor constituent, an O-demethylated derivative
  of harmine.
- **Other beta-carbolines** — including [[tetrahydroharmine]] and trace
  amounts of various related compounds.
