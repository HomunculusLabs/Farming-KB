# Pseudomonas putida

## Taxonomy

Pseudomonas putida is a Gram-negative rod-shaped bacterium belonging to the class
Schizomycetes, order Pseudomonadales, family Pseudomonaceae. The genus name Pseudomonas
derives from the Greek "pseudes" meaning false or deceptive and "monas" meaning a single
unit, referring to the variable forms of this single-celled bacterium. More than 140
species have been identified in the genus Pseudomonas, though only a few have been
identified as affecting mushrooms.

Two proven stimulative strains of Pseudomonas putida are deposited with the American Type
Culture Collection: ATCC number 12633 and ATCC number 17419.

## Morphology

Pseudomonas putida is a cylindrical (bacilli) bacterium measuring approximately 0.4 to
0.5 by 1.0 to 1.7 microns. Cells in this genus typically have one or more flagella
("motile hairs") at one or both poles, distinguishing them from Bacillus species which
have flagella along their entire outer periphery. These flagella are used for locomotion
through aqueous environments.
