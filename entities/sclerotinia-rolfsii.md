## Sclerotinia rolfsii

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Phylum**: Basidiomycota
- **Class**: Agaricomycetes
- **Order**: Amylocorticiales
- **Family**: Amylocorticiaceae
- **Anamorph**: *Sclerotium rolfsii* (synonym: *Sclerotinia rolfsii*)
- **Teleomorph**: *Athelia rolfsii*

> **Taxonomic note**: Originally placed in Ascomycota due to morphological
> similarities with *Sclerotinia* species. Molecular systematics later confirmed
> its placement in Basidiomycota. The correct teleomorph is *Athelia rolfsii*.

## Description

*Sclerotinia rolfsii* is a soil-borne basidiomycete and the causal agent of
**Southern blight** (southern stem rot, crown rot). The disease is identified
by a conspicuous **white mycelial mat** at the base of infected stems near the
soil line. This cottony, fan-shaped mycelium spreads rapidly under warm, humid
conditions, girdling stems and causing rapid wilting and plant death.

The most diagnostic feature is the production of **small, spherical sclerotia**
(0.5–2 mm), initially white but maturing to tan-to-dark-brown. These hardened
survival structures are composed of a dense rind of thick-walled cells
surrounding a medulla of aggregated hyphae, persist viable in soil for
**3–5 years or longer**, and serve as primary inoculum for new infections.

## Pathogenicity and Host Range

*S. rolfsii* attacks over **500 plant species** across ~100 families, including
tomato, pepper, peanut, soybean, cotton, carrot, bean, and numerous ornamentals.

### Disease Cycle

1. **Germination**: Sclerotia germinate in response to [[root-exudates]]
2. **Colonization**: Mycelium grows on organic debris, then infects stems at
   ground level
3. **Expansion**: Mycelial mat encircles the stem, secreting enzymes and
   [[oxalic-acid]]
4. **Plant death**: Stem girdling causes wilting and collapse
5. **[[sclerotia-formation]]**: New sclerotia form on dead tissue, returning to soil

## Virulence Factors

### Oxalic Acid

The primary virulence factor is **oxalic acid** secretion, which lowers pH to
optimal levels for fungal enzymes, **chelates Ca²⁺** from the cell wall middle
lamella weakening cell adhesion, suppresses plant defenses by inhibiting the
oxidative burst, and is directly toxic at high concentrations. Mutants with
reduced oxalic acid production show diminished virulence.

### Cell-Wall-Degrading Enzymes

*S. rolfsii* produces polygalacturonase, cellulase, pectin methylesterase,
and hemicellulases that collectively macerate host tissue.

## Environmental Conditions

Southern blight is favored by **warm, moist conditions**: optimal growth at
25–35°C, high soil moisture, sandy soils with surface organic matter, and
disease pressure peaks during hot, wet summer months.

## Survival Mechanisms

- **Sclerotial persistence**: Resistant to desiccation, UV, and microbial
  degradation; survive 3–5+ years in soil
- **Saprophytic growth**: Colonizes crop residues to maintain populations
- **Shallow distribution**: Sclerotia concentrated in top 5–10 cm where they
  contact stem bases

## Management and Biological Control

Cultural and chemical approaches include deep plowing, soil solarization,
fungicides (PCNB, tebuconazole), and debris removal. Crop rotation has limited
efficacy due to the broad host range and long sclerotial survival.

### Biological Control with *Trichoderma*

*Trichoderma* species (*T. harzianum*, *T. viride*, *T. virens*) are the most
effective biocontrol agents:

- **Mycoparasitism**: Degrades sclerotia and hyphae via chitinases and
  β-1,3-glucanases
- **Antibiosis**: Produces antibiotics inhibiting *S. rolfsii* growth
- **Competition**: Rapidly colonizes rhizosphere, depriving pathogen of
  nutrients and infection sites
- **Induced resistance**: Root colonization primes plant defenses

This system is a model for fungal antagonism and [[integrated-pest-management]].

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Major production constraint**: One of the most destructive soil-borne
  diseases of vegetable and legume crops in warm climates worldwide
- **Sclerotia biology model**: Important model for fungal survival structure
  development, dormancy, and germination cues
- **Biocontrol research model**: The *Trichoderma*–*S. rolfsii* interaction is
  foundational for mycoparasitism and biocontrol deployment research
- **Oxalic acid paradigm**: Informed understanding of virulence in related
  pathogens (*S. sclerotiorum*, *[[botrytis-cinerea]]*)
- **Soil ecology**: Bridges decomposer and pathogenic guilds, illustrating
  ecological flexibility of soil fungi
