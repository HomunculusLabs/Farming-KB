# Mortierella alpina

## Overview

mortierella alpina is a filamentous soil fungus belonging to the phylum Zygomycota (subphylum Mucoromycotina). It is a widespread saprotrophic species found in forest soils, grasslands, and various other terrestrial habitats. The species is ecologically notable as an indicator of forest disturbance and management intensity, and it has gained considerable biotechnological importance as a prolific producer of polyunsaturated fatty acids, particularly arachidonic acid.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Zygomycota (or Mucoromycota under revised classification)
- **Class:** Mortierellomycetes
- **Order:** Mortierellales
- **Family:** Mortierellaceae
- **Genus:** Mortierella
- **Species:** M. alpina

The genus Mortierella contains numerous species that are common inhabitants of soil environments. Molecular phylogenetic studies have refined the classification of the genus, and M. alpina is now placed within a well-supported clade within the Mortierellaceae.

## Morphology and Identification

mortierella alpina displays the characteristic morphology of the Mortierellales:

- **Colony:** On potato dextrose [[agar]] or malt extract agar, colonies grow rapidly, reaching several centimeters in diameter within days. Colonies are typically white to pale cream or pale yellow, with a cottony to woolly texture. The colony surface may become wrinkled or folded with age.
- **Mycelium:** The vegetative mycelium is coenocytic (non-septate) in young cultures, developing septa with age. Hyphae are broad, hyaline, and branched.
- **Sporangia:** The species produces sporangiophores that bear sporangia at their tips. The sporangiophores may be simple or branched, arising singly or in clusters from the substrate mycelium.
- **Spores:** Sporangiospores are globose to subglobose, smooth-walled, and hyaline. The columella (sterile structure within the sporangium) is well-developed.
- **Zygospores:** When compatible mating types encounter each other, the species can produce zygospores as part of its sexual cycle. Zygospores are thick-walled, ornamented resting structures.
- **Chlamydospores:** The species may also produce chlamydospores as asexual resting structures, allowing survival during adverse conditions.
- **Distinctive feature:** Unlike many other Mucoromycotina, species of Mortierella typically lack a well-developed columella that protrudes prominently into the sporangium.

## Ecology and Habitat

mortierella alpina is a cosmopolitan soil fungus with a wide geographic distribution across temperate and boreal regions. It is commonly isolated from:

- Temperate and boreal forest soils
- Grassland soils and pastures
- Alpine and subalpine soils
- Agricultural soils
- Peat and organic-rich substrates

The species is particularly associated with cool, moist soil conditions and is frequently encountered in forest ecosystems of the Northern Hemisphere. Its ecological amplitude allows it to persist in a range of soil types, from acidic forest soils to more neutral agricultural soils.

## Role in Ecosystem Processes

### Indicator of Forest Management Intensity

A particularly significant ecological observation regarding mortierella alpina comes from studies of soil microfungal communities in relation to forest management practices. As cited in Dighton (2003), research on soil microfungal species in the Mikhailovsky Reserve revealed that mortierella alpina is a characteristic species of disturbed forest ecosystems:

- In virgin (undisturbed) forest ecosystems, the dominant Mortierella species was M. stylospora
- In periodically cut forests, mortierella alpina appeared alongside [[aspergillus-flavus]] and A. nidulans
- In frequently cut forests, mortierella alpina persisted as a dominant species, alongside Absidia corymbifera and Rhizopus oryzae

This pattern suggests that mortierella alpina is an indicator of moderate to high disturbance intensity in forest ecosystems. Its increased abundance with cutting frequency reflects changes in soil conditions, organic matter inputs, and microbial community structure that accompany forest management activities.

### Nutrient Cycling

As a soil saprotroph, mortierella alpina contributes to the decomposition of organic matter and the cycling of nutrients in soil ecosystems. The species is capable of degrading complex organic compounds, including cellulose and chitin, releasing mineral nutrients that become available for plant uptake.

### Lipid Metabolism

mortierella alpina is renowned for its ability to produce long-chain polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs), particularly arachidonic acid (ARA, 20:4 n-6) and eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA, 20:5 n-3). These fatty acids are important components of cell membranes and serve as precursors for a range of bioactive signaling molecules. The production of PUFAs by soil fungi like M. alpina may influence [[soil-food-web]] dynamics, as these fatty acids are essential nutrients for many soil organisms.

## Interactions with Other Organisms

### Plant Interactions

While mortierella alpina is primarily a saprotroph, some Mortierella species have been reported to form associations with plant roots. Recent research has suggested that certain Mortierella species may function as endophytes or form associations analogous to mycorrhizae, enhancing [[plant-nutrient-uptake]]. However, the specific nature of M. alpina interactions with living plant roots requires further investigation.

### Microbial Competition

In soil environments, mortierella alpina competes with bacteria, other fungi, and actinomycetes for space and resources. Its rapid growth rate and ability to utilize diverse organic substrates give it a competitive advantage in disturbed soils where organic matter is freshly available.

### Soil Fauna Interactions

The mycelium of mortierella alpina may serve as a food source for soil microarthropods such as [[teaming-with-microbes-springtails-and-collembola]] and mites. Grazing by soil fauna can influence the growth and distribution of the fungus in soil, creating complex trophic interactions.

### Arachidonic Acid Production

The most commercially significant feature of mortierella alpina is its ability to produce large quantities of arachidonic acid (ARA, 20:4 n-6), a long-chain polyunsaturated fatty acid. Key aspects of this production include:

- ARA is an essential component of cell membranes in mammals and a precursor to eicosanoid signaling molecules (prostaglandins, leukotrienes, thromboxanes)
- Industrial fermentation processes using M. alpina can achieve ARA yields exceeding 10 grams per liter of culture broth
- The fatty acid profile can be modulated by manipulating culture conditions, including carbon source, nitrogen source, temperature, and aeration
- M. alpina strains also produce eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA, 20:5 n-3) under certain fermentation conditions, making the species valuable for omega-3 fatty acid production as well

## Physiological Characteristics

mortierella alpina displays several noteworthy physiological traits:

- **Temperature range:** Optimal growth occurs at 20 to 28 degrees Celsius, with the species capable of growth at temperatures as low as 5 degrees Celsius
- **pH tolerance:** The fungus grows over a broad pH range, from approximately pH 3 to pH 9, with optimal growth near neutrality
- **Oxygen requirement:** M. alpina is an obligate aerobe, requiring adequate oxygen supply for growth and lipid production
- **Nutrient utilization:** The species can utilize a wide range of carbon sources, including glucose, sucrose, starch, and glycerol, as well as diverse nitrogen sources

## Economic and Practical Significance

mortierella alpina has substantial commercial and practical importance:

- **Industrial lipid production:** The species is used commercially for the production of arachidonic acid (ARA), which is an important dietary supplement for infants and is added to infant formula to support brain and eye development. M. alpina strains are among the most efficient biological producers of ARA known.
- **Biofuel research:** The high lipid content of M. alpina biomass has attracted interest for [[biodiesel-production]] research.
- **Ecological monitoring:** The species serves as a bioindicator of forest disturbance and soil quality, helping forest managers assess the impacts of harvesting and other management activities on soil microbial communities.
- **Enzyme production:** Some Mortierella species produce industrially useful enzymes, including lipases and proteases.

## Research Notes

Dighton (2003) references mortierella alpina in the context of soil microfungal community responses to forest management intensity. Its presence as a dominant species in periodically and frequently cut forest ecosystems, replacing M. stylospora of virgin forests, illustrates how human activities can alter [[soil-fungal-community-composition]] and the implications of such changes for ecosystem functioning.
