# Sarcodon scorodonium (*Sarcodon scorodonium* (Fr.) P. Karst.)

## Overview

*Sarcodon scorodonium* is a species of tooth fungus in the family
Bankeraceae, widely distributed across coniferous forests of Europe and
North America. It is recognized by its brown, felty cap, pale stipe, and
characteristic teeth-like spines on the hymenial surface that produce a
distinctive garlic-like odor when bruised.

The species is notable as an edible fungus with a long history of
culinary use in Central and Eastern Europe. The epithet *scorodonium*
derives from Greek *skorodon* meaning "garlic," directly referencing the
characteristic alliaceous fragrance of fresh basidiocarps.

*S. scorodonium* is an ectomycorrhizal associate, forming mutualistic
relationships with coniferous trees, particularly spruces and pines.
It contributes to forest health through nutrient exchange with host
trees and plays a role in soil ecology.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Thelephorales
- **Family:** Bankeraceae
- **Genus:** *Sarcodon*
- **Species:** *S. scorodonium* (Fr.) P. Karst.
- **Synonyms:** *Hydnum scorodonium* Fr., *Bankera scorodonium* (Fr.)
  Coker & Beers

The pileus is convex to plano-convex, 5–15 cm in diameter, with an
irregular to slightly depressed center. The cap surface is dry and
felty-tomentose, ranging from light brown to dark reddish-brown, often
developing cracks with age. The margin is inrolled when young, becoming
wavy and irregular at maturity.

The stipe is central to eccentric, 3–8 cm long and 1–3 cm thick,
solid, and colored similarly to the cap or slightly paler. The context
is white to cream, firm, and does not change color on exposure.

### Microscopic Features

- **Basidia:** Clavate, 25–40 × 5–7 µm, four-spored.
- **Basidiospores:** Subglobose to broadly ellipsoid, 5.5–7.5 ×
  4.5–6 µm, tuberculate-echinulate, brownish in deposit.
- **Teeth:** Decurrent, 3–8 mm long, brittle, cream-colored when young
  turning brownish with age.
- **Cystidia:** Present as setae-like elements on the teeth surfaces.

The most distinctive macroscopic feature is the strong garlic-like odor
of fresh basidiocarps, especially pronounced when the teeth or flesh are
bruised or cut. The taste is mild to slightly peppery.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Sarcodon scorodonium* is found across much of temperate Europe, from
Scandinavia and the British Isles southward through the Mediterranean
region. In North America, it occurs primarily in the Pacific Northwest
and in montane coniferous forests of the Rocky Mountains.

The species is an obligate ectomycorrhizal partner of coniferous trees,
most commonly associated with Norway spruce (*Picea abies*), Scots pine
(*Pinus sylvestris*), and Douglas-fir (*Pseudotsuga menziesii*). It
prefers acidic soils in mature forests with well-developed humus layers.

Fruiting occurs from late summer to late autumn (August to November),
typically on the forest floor among needle litter and moss. It can
appear singly or in scattered groups, often recurring in the same
locations year after year.

## Edibility and Culinary Use

*S. scorodonium* is widely regarded as an excellent edible fungus,
particularly valued in Central European and Slavic culinary traditions.
The garlic-like aroma makes it a natural flavoring agent for dishes
where a mild alliaceous note is desired.

In traditional preparations, young specimens are collected and used as
a garlic substitute in soups, stews, and egg dishes. The teeth are the
primary part consumed, as the cap flesh can become tough with age.
Drying concentrates the flavor and allows long-term storage.

Specimens should be collected when young, as older basidiocarps become
bitter and tough. The garlic odor must be confirmed at collection, as
several superficially similar *Sarcodon* species lack this
characteristic and are unpalatable.

## Bioactive Compounds

*Sarcodon scorodonium* has been investigated for its bioactive
metabolites, particularly its anti-inflammatory and antimicrobial
properties. Several secondary metabolites have been isolated from the
basidiocarps.

The species produces scodonin and related cyathane-type diterpenes,
which have demonstrated anti-inflammatory activity in vitro. These
compounds are of interest for potential pharmaceutical development due
to their ability to modulate inflammatory signaling pathways.

Extracts have also shown antimicrobial activity against Gram-positive
bacteria, including *Staphylococcus aureus* and *Bacillus subtilis*.
The antimicrobial properties are attributed to a combination of
terpenoid and phenolic compounds present in the fruiting body tissue.

## Similar Species

Several *Sarcodon* species share similar habitat and morphology,
requiring careful attention to odor and spore ornamentation for
identification.

- **[[sarcodon-imbricatus]]** — Larger species with darker, more
  prominently imbricate cap scales and mild (non-garlic) odor.
- **[[boletus-edulis]]** — Shares coniferous forest habitat but has
  poroid hymenium and completely different morphology.
- **[[trametes-versicolor]]** — Bracket fungus on wood, not
  ectomycorrhizal, but can occur in similar forest environments.
