# Sarcodon scorodonium (*Sarcodon scorodonium* (Fr.) P. Karst.)

## Overview

*Sarcodon scorodonium* is a species of tooth fungus in the family
Bankeraceae, widely distributed across coniferous forests of Europe and
[[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]]. It is recognized by its brown, felty cap, pale stipe, and
characteristic teeth-like spines on the hymenial surface that produce a
distinctive garlic-like odor when bruised.

The species is notable as an edible fungus with a long history of
culinary use in Central and Eastern Europe. The epithet *scorodonium*
derives from Greek *skorodon* meaning "garlic," directly referencing the
characteristic alliaceous fragrance of fresh basidiocarps.

*S. scorodonium* is an ectomycorrhizal associate, forming mutualistic
relationships with coniferous trees, particularly spruces and pines.
It contributes to forest health through [[arbuscule-isolation-and-nutrient-exchange-mechanisms]] with host
trees and plays a role in soil ecology.

- **Genus:** *Sarcodon*

- **Synonyms:** *Hydnum scorodonium* Fr., *Bankera scorodonium* (Fr.)
  Coker & Beers

The pileus is convex to plano-convex, 5–15 cm in diameter, with an
irregular to slightly depressed center. The cap surface is dry and
felty-tomentose, ranging from light brown to dark reddish-brown, often
developing cracks with age. The margin is inrolled when young, becoming
wavy and irregular at maturity.

The stipe is central to eccentric, 3–8 cm long and 1–3 cm thick,
solid, and colored similarly to the cap or slightly paler. The context
is white to cream, firm, and does not change color on exposure.

### Microscopic Features

- **Basidia:** Clavate, 25–40 × 5–7 µm, four-spored.
- **Basidiospores:** Subglobose to broadly ellipsoid, 5.5–7.5 ×
  4.5–6 µm, tuberculate-echinulate, brownish in deposit.
- **Teeth:** Decurrent, 3–8 mm long, brittle, cream-colored when young
  turning brownish with age.
- **Cystidia:** Present as setae-like elements on the teeth surfaces.

The most distinctive macroscopic feature is the strong garlic-like odor
of fresh basidiocarps, especially pronounced when the teeth or flesh are
bruised or cut. The taste is mild to slightly peppery.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Sarcodon scorodonium* is found across much of temperate Europe, from
Scandinavia and the British Isles southward through the Mediterranean
region. In [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]], it occurs primarily in the Pacific Northwest
and in montane coniferous forests of the Rocky Mountains.

The species is an obligate ectomycorrhizal partner of coniferous trees,
most commonly associated with Norway spruce (*[[picea-abies]]*), Scots pine
(*[[pinus-sylvestris]]*), and Douglas-fir (*[[pseudotsuga-menziesii]]*). It
prefers acidic soils in mature forests with well-developed humus layers.
