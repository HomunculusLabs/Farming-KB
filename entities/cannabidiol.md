## Description

Cannabidiol (CBD) is a major non-psychoactive cannabinoid found in Cannabis. It occurs as one of the related constituents alongside tetrahydrocannabinols and cannabinol in the complex chemical mixture of [[blesching-cannabis-resin-preparations-kief-hashish-concentrates]]. CBD and its acidic precursor ([[cannabidiolic-acid-cbda]]) represent the primary form in which cannabinoids exist in fresh Cannabis plants.

## Classification

- **Category**: Cannabinoid
- **Chemical class**: Terpenophenolic compound / Dibenzopyran
- **Molecular formula**: C21H30O2
- **Source**: Cannabis species
- **Activity**: Non-psychoactive (does not produce intoxication)

## Key Facts

- One of the most abundant cannabinoids in fresh Cannabis plants
- Present primarily as cannabidiolic acid in living plant tissue
- Precursor to tetrahydrocannabinols (THC) through decarboxylation
- Non-psychoactive; does not produce the euphoria or hallucinations of THC
- Present alongside THC, cannabinol, cannabichromene, and related compounds
- The unstable nature of CBD acid explains the variable potency of Cannabis preparations

## Chemistry

In the fresh plant, CBD exists primarily as cannabidiolic acid. Through drying, heating, or aging, this acid converts through decarboxylation to become CBD, which can then further convert to THC under certain conditions. This chemical instability is why the potency of Cannabis preparations varies so greatly.

## Role in Cannabis Chemistry

The conversion pathway is:
1. Cannabidiolic acid (fresh plant) -> Cannabidiol (dried)
2. Cannabidiol -> Tetrahydrocannabinol (under certain conditions)
3. Tetrahydrocannabinol -> Cannabinol (degradation)

This explains why plant material of different ages and from different climates varies in narcotic effect.
