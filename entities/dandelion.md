 Dandelion

The dandelion (*[[cervantes-seed-production-storage]]**: A single plant produces 2,000–5,000+ seeds per year; seeds remain viable for 5–8 years in soil
- **Dispersal**: Wind-dispersed; pappus enables long-distance travel (up to 100+ km under optimal conditions)
- **Flowering**: Nearly year-round in temperate climates, with peak flowering in spring and autumn

## Nutritional Composition

All parts of the dandelion are edible and nutritionally dense:

### Leaves (per 100 g fresh weight)
- Calories: 45 kcal
- Protein: 2.7 g
- Vitamin A: 10,161 IU (203% DV) — one of the highest vegetable sources
- Vitamin C: 35 mg (58% DV)
- Vitamin K: 778 µg (775% DV) — among the highest dietary sources
- Calcium: 187 mg (19% DV)
- Iron: 3.1 mg (17% DV)
- Potassium: 397 mg (11% DV)

### Root
- High in **inulin** (up to 40% dry weight) — a prebiotic fructan polysaccharide that supports beneficial gut bacteria
- Contains taraxasterol, taraxerol, and other triterpenes

## Ecological Value

### Pollinator Support

Dandelions are among the most important early-season nectar and pollen sources for:

- **Honey bees** (*Apis mellifera*): Dandelion pollen is moderate in protein (15–20%) and is readily collected during spring dearth periods
- **Bumble bees**: Critical spring resource for emerging queens establishing nests
- **Solitary bees**: Mining bees (*Andrena* spp.) and other early-emerging species depend on dandelion pollen
- **Hoverflies**: Adults feed on dandelion nectar; larvae are important aphid predators
