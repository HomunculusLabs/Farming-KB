## Overview

Gentiana tibetica is a robust perennial gentian native to the high-altitude regions of the Tibetan Plateau and adjacent Himalayan areas. It produces large, cream-white to pale yellowish flowers streaked with blue or purple, making it one of the more striking species in the genus.

The species has a long history of use in Tibetan and traditional Chinese medicine as a bitter tonic and digestive aid. Its roots contain iridoid glycosides, particularly gentiopicroside, which are responsible for the characteristic bitter taste.

Gentiana tibetica is one of several species traded under the commercial name "Qin Jiao" in Chinese herbal markets. It commands significant economic value in parts of western China and the Himalayan region.

The species has received increased research attention due to its medicinal iridoid glycosides. Chemotaxonomic studies help distinguish it from other Qin Jiao species in the herbal trade.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Scientific classification:**
- Kingdom: Plantae
- Family: Gentianaceae
- Genus: Gentiana
- Section: Cruciatа
- Species: G. tibetica (King ex Hook.f.) T.N.Ho

Gentiana tibetica was first collected during the Himalayan botanical expeditions of the 19th century. The species was formally described by J.D. Hooker and later transferred to Gentiana by T.N. Ho.

**Morphological details:**
- Height: 30–80 cm, erect, stout stems from a thick rootstock
- Leaves: opposite, lanceolate to ovate-lanceolate, 5–15 cm long, prominently 3–5 nerved
- Inflorescence: axillary and terminal clusters of 2–8 flowers
- Corolla: tubular-funnelform, 25–40 mm long, cream-white to pale yellow with blue-purple streaks
- Calyx: tubular, 10–15 mm, lobes unequal
- Roots: thick, yellowish-brown, aromatic when fresh

The species can be distinguished from the closely related G. macrophylla by its broader leaves and shorter corolla tube. G. tibetica also tends to grow at higher elevations than most G. macrophylla populations.

## Distribution and Habitat

Gentiana tibetica is native to the Tibetan Plateau and surrounding high-altitude regions. Its range extends across central Tibet, Qinghai, western Sichuan, and into Bhutan, Nepal, and northern India.

The species grows between 3,000 and 5,000 meters elevation, occupying alpine meadows, shrubby slopes, and grassland-steppe interfaces. It favors moist but well-drained soils, often near streams or on north-facing slopes.

Populations are widely distributed across the Tibetan Plateau but population densities vary considerably. Some valleys support dense stands while adjacent areas may have scattered individuals.

The species tolerates the extreme temperature fluctuations of the continental Tibetan climate. Winter temperatures regularly reach −30°C, while summer daytime temperatures can exceed 25°C at ground level.

## Ecology

## Cultivation

Gentiana tibetica is uncommon in cultivation but can be grown with attention to its specific requirements. It is more forgiving than many alpine gentians due to its robust perennial habit.

Seeds require 4–6 weeks of cold stratification for reliable germination. Sow in autumn in a cold frame, covering lightly with grit. Germination typically occurs the following spring.

Plants prefer a deep, humus-rich soil that retains moisture without becoming waterlogged. Full sun to partial shade is acceptable, with afternoon shade beneficial in warmer climates.

Established plants are drought-tolerant once the deep taproot develops. However, consistent moisture during [[query-how-to-protect-plants-from-frost-and-extend-the-growing-season]] produces more vigorous growth and better flowering.

Hardiness extends to USDA zone 4, making it suitable for cold-climate gardens. In cultivation, plants reach flowering size in 3–4 years from seed.

## Uses

Gentiana tibetica is an important species in Tibetan medicine, where it is known as "Chi-ka" or "Tibetan gentian." The dried roots are used as a bitter tonic to stimulate digestion and appetite.

In traditional Chinese medicine, the species is one of several substituted for G. macrophylla under the drug name "Qin Jiao." It is prescribed for rheumatic conditions, fever, and jaundice.

Pharmacological studies have confirmed the presence of gentiopicroside, swertiamarin, and amarogentin in the roots. These iridoid glycosides exhibit hepatoprotective, anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant activities.
