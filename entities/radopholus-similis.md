# Radopholus similis

## Description

Radopholus similis, commonly known as the burrowing nematode, is a migratory
endoparasitic nematode that attacks the roots of a wide range of economically
important crops. It is one of the most destructive plant-parasitic nematodes in
tropical and subtropical agriculture, causing significant yield losses in banana,
citrus, and other crops. Elsen et al. (2001) demonstrated that arbuscular
mycorrhizal fungi can significantly reduce R. similis populations.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Eukaryota
- **Kingdom:** Animalia
- **Phylum:** Nematoda
- **Class:** Secernentea
- **Order:** Tylenchida
- **Family:** Pratylenchidae
- **Genus:** Radopholus
- **Species:** R. similis

## Key Facts

- **Migratory endoparasite:** Burrows into root cortex tissue, creating tunnels and
  destroying root cells as it feeds and moves through the root system.
- **Broad host range:** Attacks over 250 plant species, with banana and citrus being
  the most economically important hosts worldwide.
- **Mycorrhizal suppression:** Elsen et al. (2001) found that Glomus intraradices
  inoculation significantly reduced R. similis populations in dixenic culture.
- **Female-biased impact:** The major suppressive effect of mycorrhizae was seen in
  female nematodes, suggesting long-term population regulation through reduced
  fecundity.
- **Spreading decline:** In citrus, causes spreading decline disease characterized by
  progressive decline of tree vigor and eventual tree death.
- **Banana toppling disease:** Most important nematode pest of banana globally,
  causing toppling of fruit-bearing plants due to destroyed root systems.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Banana production:** Most important nematode pest of banana worldwide.
- **Mycorrhizal biocontrol:** Glomus intraradices reduces populations, suggesting
  mycorrhizal inoculation as a biological control strategy.
- **Integrated management:** Combining mycorrhizal inoculation with resistant
  cultivars offers sustainable nematode management.
- **Population dynamics research:** Female-specific suppression by mycorrhizae
  provides insights into targeted biocontrol of reproductively important pest
  segments.

## Ecological Interactions

- Migratory endoparasite of banana, citrus, and 250+ other plant species.
- Populations significantly reduced by arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi (Glomus
  intraradices), particularly affecting female nematodes.
- Interacts with soil microbial communities influencing survival and infectivity.
- Movement through soil facilitated by moisture films between soil particles.

## Pitfalls and Research Gaps

- Mycorrhizal suppression effects demonstrated in dixenic culture may not
  translate directly to field conditions with complex soil microbial communities.
- The mechanism of female-biased suppression by mycorrhizae is not understood.
- R. similis has shown ability to overcome some host plant resistance genes,
  raising concerns about durability of resistant cultivars.
- Economic thresholds for mycorrhizal-based biocontrol in banana plantations
  have not been established.
