# Blood Meal

## Description

**Blood meal** is a dry, powdered fertilizer made from the blood collected during livestock processing. It is one of the highest-nitrogen natural fertilizers available (N-P-K 12-0-0) and serves as a fast-acting nitrogen source that directly counters the criticism that organic fertilizers release nutrients too slowly. Its nitrogen is readily available upon application, making it one of the few organic fertilizers that performs comparably to synthetic nitrogen products in terms of speed.

## Classification

- **Category:** Natural organic fertilizer / Animal by-product
- **N-P-K:** 12-0-0
- **Nutrient Availability:** Fast-acting (1–4 months)
- **Application Form:** Dry powder, broadcast or mixed into soil
- **Domain:** Organic gardening, horticulture, livestock by-product utilization

## Key Facts

- At 12% nitrogen by weight, blood meal is among the highest-nitrogen organic fertilizers, approaching synthetic fertilizer concentrations.
- The nitrogen is **readily available** — not slow-release — making blood meal an exception to the general rule that natural fertilizers act slowly.
- Nutrients are released over 1 to 4 months depending on soil conditions (temperature, moisture, microbial activity).
- Functions as a rapid-response fertilizer for nitrogen-deficient plants or early-season applications.

## Cautions and Considerations

- **Can burn plants** if over-applied due to high nitrogen concentration; use recommended rates carefully.
- **Unpleasant odor** that can persist for several days after application.
- **Attracts animals** — dogs and cats may be drawn to the scent; conversely, deer and moose are repelled by the blood smell (they associate it with predator kills).
- The deer-repelling property makes blood meal a dual-purpose product: fertilizer and animal deterrent.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- Blood meal is sometimes used in mushroom substrate formulation to boost nitrogen content and lower the carbon-to-nitrogen (C:N) ratio, particularly in compost for Agaricus species.
- The rapid nitrogen availability of blood meal can stimulate bacterial activity in compost piles, accelerating the decomposition process.
- In soil applications, blood meal feeds the soil food web (bacteria and fungi) while also providing directly available nitrogen — bridging the gap between synthetic and slow-release organic fertilizers.
- Blood meal represents an example of industrial waste stream valorization: converting a slaughterhouse by-product into a valuable agricultural input.
