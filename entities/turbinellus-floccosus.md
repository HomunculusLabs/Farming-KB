# Turbinellus floccosus (*Gomphus floccosus*)

## Overview

*Turbinellus floccosus* is a large, vase-shaped ectomycorrhizal fungus in the
family Gomphaceae. Known commonly as the scaly chanterelle, woolly chanterelle,
or pine cone mushroom, it is found in coniferous forests across [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]],
[[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-central-america-caribbean]], Europe, and Asia. The species forms [[plants-without-mycorrhizal-associations]]
primarily with *Pinus* (pine) and occasionally with *Pseudotsuga* (Douglas-fir).

Historically classified in *Gomphus* or *Cantharellus*, molecular phylogenetics
placed it in *Turbinellus* in 2011. Despite its visual similarity to true
chanterelles (*Cantharellus*), it is only moderately edible and causes GI upset
in some individuals. It is commercially collected in parts of Mexico and
southern China.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Taxonomic classification:**
- Kingdom: Fungi
- Division: Basidiomycota
- Class: Agaricomycetes
- Order: Gomphales
- Family: Gomphaceae
- Genus: *Turbinellus* ( Lodge, 2011)
- Species: *T. floccosus* (Schwein.) Lodge, Aime & T.W. Henkel

**Synonyms:**
- *Cantharellus floccosus* Schwein. (1822) — original basionym
- *Gomphus floccosus* (Schwein.) Singer (1945) — widely used mid-20th century name
- *Craterellus floccosus* (Schwein.) Quel. (1888)

**Morphology — [[comparison-fruiting-body-vs-mycelium-extracts-medicinal-mushrooms]]:**
- **Cap:** 5–25 cm broad when mature, funnel-shaped to deeply vase-shaped;
  margin inrolled when young, becoming irregularly wavy with age
- **Upper surface:** Dry, finely tomentose when young; developing coarse,
  erect, fibrous scales (2–8 mm) with age — the namesake "floccose" character
- **Color:** Orange-brown to reddish-brown, paler toward the margin; scales
  darker than the background surface
- **Hymenium (fertile surface):** False gills — shallow, forking ridges running
  down the stipe, often anastomosing; pale yellowish to buff
- **Stipe:** 3–10 cm long, 1–3 cm thick, central to slightly eccentric;
  solid when young, becoming hollow; scaly like the cap
- **Context:** Pale yellowish, firm; up to 1.5 cm thick at the stipe
- **Spore print:** Pale ochraceous to yellowish-brown
- **Spores:** Ellipsoid, 8–12 × 5–7 µm, smooth, thin-walled, inamyloid
- **Odor:** Mild, pleasant, faintly fruity
- **Taste:** Mild, slightly peppery

## Distribution and Habitat

**Geographic range:**
- Western North America: British Columbia to California and Rocky Mountains
- [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-caribbean-central-america]]: high-elevation pine forests of Mexico and Guatemala;
  commercially harvested in Oaxaca and Puebla
- Europe and Asia: mountainous central/southern Europe; Himalayan foothills;
  Yunnan and Sichuan (China); Japan

**[[psilocybin-mushroom-habitat-and-ecology]]:**
- Ectomycorrhizal, forming obligate associations with conifers
- Primary hosts: *[[pinus-ponderosa]]*, *P. lambertiana*, *P. jeffreyi* (western
  NA); *Pinus hartwegii* (Mexico); *[[pinus-sylvestris]]* (Europe); *P. yunnanensis*
- Fruiting singly or scattered on forest floor, often in [[dighton-pine-needle-litter-decomposition-fungal-succession]]
- Season: July–October (northern hemisphere); post-monsoon in Himalayas
- Elevation: 1,500–3,500 m (Asia); 500–2,500 m (North America)
