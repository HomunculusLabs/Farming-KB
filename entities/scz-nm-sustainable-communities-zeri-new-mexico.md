# Sustainable Communities ZERI-New Mexico (SCZ-NM)

## Description

Sustainable Communities/ZERI-New Mexico (SCZ-NM) is a creative sustainability enterprise based near Santa Fe, New Mexico, and one of the first ZERI (Zero Emissions Research and Initiatives) projects in the United States. SCZ-NM operates under the auspices of the U.S. Forest Service Collaborative Forest Restoration Program, with a primary mission of reducing fire hazard in overgrown National Forests through innovative mycological approaches. The organization uses woody waste from forest thinning operations as substrate for mushroom cultivation, converts spent substrate into animal feed and vermicompost, and maintains a native fungal culture bank for long-term ecological restoration research.

## Key Facts

- One of the first ZERI projects established in the United States, implementing Gunter Pauli's zero-emissions design principles in a North American context.
- Operates under the U.S. Forest Service Collaborative Forest Restoration Program in New Mexico.
- Primary mission: reducing fire hazard in overgrown National Forests by utilizing woody waste from thinning operations.
- Uses woody refuse from forest thinning as substrate for oyster and shiitake mushroom cultivation, converting a fire hazard into a valuable food product.
- Discovered that even wood digested by mushrooms can be incorporated into digestible feed for cows, sheep, bison, and earthworms.
- Co-founded by Lynda Taylor, who initiated the establishment of a long-term New Mexico native fungal culture bank.
- The fungal culture bank collects and maintains native fungi on agar plates, testing them for growth on various local tree species including ponderosa pine, pinon, and juniper.
- Also tests fungi on invasive species such as salt cedar and Russian olive, exploring biological control of invasive plants through fungal decomposition.
- Employs mycological consultants including Carmenza Jaramillo Lopez from Colombia, who brings expertise from coffee waste mushroom cultivation projects.
- Implements mycofiltration and mycoremediation techniques: filling roadside depressions and ditches with mushroom-inoculated wood chips to create mycelial mats that reduce erosion and silt flow.
- These mycoforestry practices align with Paul Stamets's principles from *Mycelium Running*, particularly the concept of matching native fungi to native tree species.
- The organization's projects demonstrate practical applications of ZERI principles: forest waste becomes mushroom cultivation substrate, then animal feed, then vermicompost, then soil enrichment, completing a zero-waste cycle.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

SCZ-NM is directly relevant to mushroom cultivation and mycology:

1. **Mycoforestry model** — SCZ-NM provides a working model for integrating mushroom cultivation with forest management, showing how cultivators can partner with forestry operations to access low-cost substrate while providing ecological services.

2. **Native fungal culture bank** — The establishment of a native fungal culture bank preserves local fungal genetic diversity and provides a resource for cultivators seeking regionally adapted strains for outdoor cultivation and ecological restoration.

3. **Invasive species substrate** — Research into growing mushrooms on invasive species (salt cedar, Russian olive) opens possibilities for cultivators to simultaneously produce food and contribute to invasive species management.

4. **Spent substrate utilization** — SCZ-NM's finding that myceliated wood can be used as animal feed provides cultivators with a value-added disposal pathway for spent substrate.

5. **Mycofiltration applications** — The organization's erosion control work demonstrates how mushroom cultivation infrastructure can provide additional environmental services, potentially creating new revenue streams for cultivators through ecological restoration contracts.

6. **Practical mycoforestry techniques** — SCZ-NM's implementation of Stamets's recommendation to mix spores into chainsaw oil and wood chipper oil provides cultivators with an innovative, low-labor method for inoculating forest substrate.

## Core Philosophy and Approach

The Sustainable Communities ZERI-New Mexico (SCZ-NM) embodies a philosophy of zero-waste industrial
ecology where outputs from one process become inputs for
another. This cascading resource model challenges the
conventional assumption that economic growth requires
environmental degradation.

## Implementation and Case Studies

Practical implementation has demonstrated viability across
diverse sectors including agriculture, brewing, and
forestry. Each application adapts the core principle to
local conditions and available biological resources.

## Network and Global Reach

The initiative has spawned research centers and pilot
projects across multiple continents. Collaboration between
scientists, entrepreneurs, and communities drives ongoing
innovation in closed-loop production systems.
