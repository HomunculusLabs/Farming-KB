## Overview

**Peltigera membranacea** (Ach.) Nyl., commonly known as the **membranous pelt lichen**, is a large foliose lichen in the family Peltigeraceae. It forms broad, semi-erect thalli up to 20 cm across, typically found on mossy ground, soil, and decaying wood in humid forest ecosystems.

The species is notable for its association with the cyanobacterium *Nostoc* as its primary photobiont, making it an important contributor to [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]], and parts of Asia.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:** Kingdom Fungi → Phylum Ascomycota → Class Lecanoromycetes → Order Peltigerales → Family Peltigeraceae → Genus *Peltigera* → Section *Peltidea*.

The species was originally described by Erik Acharius in 1803 as *Lichen membranaceus* and later transferred to *Peltigera* by William Nylander in 1858. The epithet *membranacea* refers to the thin, papery texture of the thallus lobes.

The thallus is large, typically 10–20 cm in diameter, with broadly rounded to irregular lobes that are semi-erect or prostrate. The upper surface is grayish to brownish-gray, smooth to slightly wrinkled, and often pruinose near the lobe tips.

The lower surface is densely tomentose with pale veins that contrast against a darker brown to black background. Rhizines are simple to branched, dark brown, and concentrated along the veins.

Apothecia are common, arising from the upper surface near lobe margins. They are lecanorine, 1–5 mm in diameter, with a brown disc and a well-developed thalline margin.

The photobiont is *Nostoc*, visible as dark green to bluish-green cells in the medulla. This cyanobiont gives the lichen its nitrogen-fixing capability.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Peltigera membranacea* has a circumpolar distribution in the Northern Hemisphere. In Europe, it ranges from Scandinavia and the British Isles south to the Alps and Carpathians. In North America, it occurs from Alaska to Newfoundland and south along the Pacific Coast to California.

The species is most common in cool, humid forest environments. It grows on mossy soil, rotting logs, tree bases, and disturbed ground in coniferous and mixed forests.

Elevation ranges from sea level to approximately 1,800 meters in mountainous regions. It favors areas with high atmospheric humidity and consistent moisture, often near streams or in ravines.

It is less tolerant of prolonged desiccation than some *Peltigera* species, which restricts it to relatively moist microhabitats within its range.

## Ecology and Symbiosis

As a cyanolichen, *P. membranacea* fixes atmospheric nitrogen through its *Nostoc* photobiont. Fixed nitrogen is leached into surrounding soil, enriching the substrate and benefiting adjacent vascular plants.

The species is an indicator of old-growth forest conditions in some regions, particularly in the [[antifungal-secondary-metabolites-coprophilous-fungi]] in its cortex.

The thallus provides microhabitat for various invertebrates, including tardigrades, nematodes, and collembolans. These communities contribute to [[biodiversity-fungal-secondary-metabolites]] of *P. membranacea* include **tenuiorin** and **peltigerin**. These are depsidones found in the medulla and contribute to the lichen's chemical defense against herbivory and microbial colonization.

Spot tests are characteristically negative for the cortex (K−, C−, KC−) and positive for the medulla with potassium hydroxide (K+ yellowish to reddish). The PD reaction is negative in the medulla.

Tenuiorin has been studied for its anti-inflammatory and cytotoxic properties in vitro, though concentrations in the lichen thallus are low relative to pharmaceutical standards.

No toxicologically significant concentrations of secondary compounds have been reported from this species. The chemical profile distinguishes it from the morphologically similar *Peltigera neckeri* (which contains gyrophoric acid).

## Uses and Conservation

*Peltigera membranacea* has limited direct economic use. It has been used historically as a source of brown dye in Scandinavian textile traditions, alongside other *Peltigera* species.
