## Cleve Backster

Cleve Baxter Backster (February 27, 1924 – June 24, 2013) was an American
polygraph examiner and interrogation specialist best known for his controversial
claims about "[[plant-communication-volatile-organic-compounds]] of Plants* (1973) and remains a
cultural reference point in discussions about plant intelligence and the
boundaries [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]]. In the 1960s, he founded the
Backster School of Lie Detection in San Diego, California, which became one of
the most recognized polygraph training institutions in the country. The school
trained generations of polygraph examiners and produced standard training
materials still used in the field. Backster served as president of the American
Polygraph Association and chaired its ethics committee, testifying extensively
as an expert witness in criminal and civil court cases.

## The 1966 Dracaena Experiment

On February 2, 1966, Backster was working late in his polygraph school in Times
Square, New York City. On impulse, he attached galvanic skin response (GSR)
electrodes from one of his polygraph instruments to a leaf of a *Dracaena
massangeana* (corn plant) in his office. His initial intention was to measure
how quickly water would travel from the roots to the leaf. When he poured water
on the root system, the polygraph tracing showed a gradual upward sweep rather
than the flat or declining line he expected — a pattern resembling an emotional
excitation response in a human subject. Intrigued, Backster abandoned his
original plan and began a systematic series of experiments exploring plant
responsiveness to human presence and intention.

## The Threat Experiment

Backster"s most famous experiment followed the initial observation. He decided
to threaten the plant by burning one of its leaves with a match. According to
his account, the polygraph recording showed a massive spike at the precise
moment he formed the conscious intention to burn the leaf — before any physical
movement toward the plant. When he merely pretended to go for matches without
genuine intent to harm, the plant showed no reaction. On subsequent actual burn
attempts, the plant's response diminished, which Backster interpreted as
habituation. He coined the term "[[tompkins-cleve-backster-primary-perception]]" to describe his theory
that all living cells communicate at a fundamental biocommunicative level,
responding to threats and emotional states across distances.
