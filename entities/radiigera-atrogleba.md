# Radiigera atrogleba

## Description

*Radiigera atrogleba* is a sequestrate (truffle-like) ectomycorrhizal fungus in the family Mesophelliaceae. Like other members of this family, it produces below-ground fruiting bodies and relies on mycophagous animals for [[spore-dispersal]]. This species has been specifically identified as one of the most effective [[mycorrhizal-fungi]] for degrading polychlorinated biphenyls (PCBs).

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Hysterangiales
- **Family:** Mesophelliaceae
- **Genus:** *Radiigera*
- **Species:** *R. atrogleba*

## Key Facts

- Demonstrated the ability to degrade 80% of 2,2'-dichlorobiphenyl, a specific PCB congener (Donnelley and Fletcher, 1994).
- Among the most effective mycorrhizal fungi for PCB biodegradation, tied with *[[hysterangium-gardneri]]* at 80% degradation.
- Significantly outperformed ericoid mycorrhizal species (*[[hymenoscyphus-ericae]]*, *[[oidiodendron-griseum]]*) in PCB degradation.
- Ectomycorrhizal lifestyle enables dual function: supporting host plant growth while remediating contaminated soils.
- One of 14 mycorrhizal fungal species (out of 21 screened) capable of metabolizing at least 20% of PCBs.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

*Radiigera atrogleba* is a prime candidate for mycoremediation of PCB-contaminated soils. Its 80% degradation efficiency for 2,2'-dichlorobiphenyl places it among the most effective fungal PCB degraders known. The combination of ectomycorrhizal function with high enzymatic PCB degradation capacity makes it particularly valuable for restoration of contaminated sites where revegetation and pollutant removal are simultaneous goals.

## Ecological Role

- Ectomycorrhizal symbiont supporting tree establishment in degraded habitats
- Highly effective PCB-degrading fungus for bioremediation
- Subterranean fruiting bodies consumed by mammals, dispersing spores
- Persistent mycelial networks provide sustained enzymatic activity in soil
