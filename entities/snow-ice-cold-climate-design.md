## Snow and Ice in Cold Climate Design

## Description

Snow and ice are not merely challenges to be overcome in cold climate design — they represent a suite of design opportunities when their unique physical properties are understood and harnessed. Mollison devotes extensive coverage to the constructive use of cold phenomena in [[permaculture]], treating snow and ice as design elements that can provide heating, cooling, water storage, structural materials, and food preservation.

## Classification

- **Category**: Design element / Environmental resource
- **Domain**: Cold [[tropical-humid-and-coastal-climate-permaculture-design]]
- **Related concepts**: Albedo, ice lenses, snow insulation, frost heave, ice construction

## Key Facts

### Snow as a Heating Device

Snow"s high albedo (reflectivity) makes it an effective [[fao-solar-heating-pasteurization-mushroom-cultivation]] amplifier:

- Fresh dry snow reflects close to 100% of incoming light
- Muddy slush reflects about 20%
- Snow is most effective at low sun angles (dawn, evening, winter)
- Low sun angles bounced off snow surfaces can add 60-70% more heat to house walls
- Earth-formed reflectors covered in snow can concentrate heat to buildings
- Radiation penetrates snow 10-15 cm (most absorbed by water in snow mass)
- In dry snow and hoarfrost, light penetrates 30-60 cm

### Snow as Insulation

- Snow thinner than 15 cm can chill soil to 35 degrees C below air temperature (net radiative loss)
- Snow thicker than 15 cm acts as an effective insulator for soils, buildings, and [[permafrost]]
- Snow is almost a "black body" for radiation, with values of 0.962-0.986, losing heat rapidly at night
- Snow is completely non-transparent to incoming long-wave radiation
- Melting more commonly occurs from below due to earth (soil) heat — this basal melting causes poor snow cohesion and can trigger avalanches

### Ice as a Design Material

Ice can be moulded in any shape that water can be poured into:

- **Ice lenses**: Water poured into moulds creates cheap solar energy concentrators; components of complex lenses can be glued with water
- **Reinforced ice**: Bars or fibres can be added to moulds for [[mycelium-running-rhizomorphic-mycelium-structural-strength-erosion-control]]
- **Ice buggies**: Moulded ice around axles can be towed to summer storages
- **Underground ice pits**: Uninsulated pits packed with winter snow and earthed over provided chilled storage before 1830
- **Insulated ice**: Frozen inside insulated boxes in winter, stored insulated from ground, lasts through summer in shaded places

### Frost Heave as a Soil Preparation Technique

Frost heave on bare, part-eroded, or exposed soils creates ideal seedbed conditions during the thaw period:

- Only bare soils exhibit frost heave — these are most in need of seed to stabilise erosion potential
- Frosted and late snow areas present ideal surface for spreading manures in late winter
- Meltdown deposits seed and manure on the ground surface for spring growth
- Sprouted seeds have 10-100 times higher vitamin and sugar content than unsprouted seeds

### Snow Melt Patterns as Design Guides

The pattern of snow melt reveals microclimate information:

- Black objects, soot, and leaves sink into snow (absorbing heat)
- Snow melts rapidly 4 metres from bare-leaf, white-stemmed birch stands
- Rock walls backed by birch create early warm planting sites
- Chinese slanted bamboo and straw lean-tos extend growing season
- Stone walls in embayment form warm early sites
- Semicircles of tyres facing low sun trap heat

### Ice and Water Storage

Cold climate water storage design must account for:

- Freeze depth: Ponds may freeze 15-90 cm (6-36 inches), making that water unavailable
- Expansion: Freezing expands water; storages must be left unfilled, slope-sided, or open-topped
- Pipes buried to 1 metre depth or allowed to trickle at 450 litres/day
- Best solution: water storage within house cellar structure as well or cistern below floor level

### Wildlife Integration with Snow

- Branch piles and loose [[compost]] mounds provide winter wildlife refuge
- Field and pack rats collect wild rice, acorns, and seeds in provided storage containers
- 85% of collected seed can be harvested for human use if 15% is left for the animals
- Winter acorns sprout in spring, providing high-value forage for pigs and poultry

## Relevance to Cultivation

Understanding snow and ice dynamics is essential for cold-climate [[permaculture-design]]. Rather than fighting cold, these techniques use it constructively — for solar concentration, food preservation, soil preparation, water storage, [[tree-temperature-effects-and-microclimate-modification]]. The key insight is that cold phenomena offer a complementary set of design opportunities to those available in warm climates, each exploited by appropriate [[solomon-harvesting-techniques-and-timing]].
