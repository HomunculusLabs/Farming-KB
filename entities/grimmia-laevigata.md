The species forms distinctive dark green to blackish cushions on rocks, walls, and occasionally on tree bark. It is characterized by its smooth capsule when mature and its strongly incurved leaf margins.

Grimmia laevigata is remarkably tolerant of desiccation and full sun exposure, making it a primary colonizer of exposed stone surfaces in a wide range of climatic conditions.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Grimmia laevigata was described by Johann Hedwig and is placed in the genus Grimmia, which contains over 200 species worldwide. The species epithet laevigata refers to the smooth capsule surface, a key diagnostic character.

The species forms dense, dark green to nearly black tufts or cushions 1–3 cm tall. Stems are erect, irregularly branched, and arise from extensive brown to blackish rhizoidal mats.

Leaves are lanceolate, 3–5 mm long when moist, with margins strongly incurved and usually concealing the lamina. The leaf apex is acute to acuminate, often with a colorless hair point that can exceed the leaf length.

The costa is stout and percurrent, filling much of the leaf cross-section. Leaf cells are quadrate to short-rectangular in the upper lamina with thickened, sinuose walls, becoming elongated toward the base.

The capsule is erect, ellipsoidal, and smooth when dry and mature, lacking the ribbed or furrowed surface common in many other Grimmia species. Capsules are approximately 1.5–2.0 mm long and brown to dark brown.

The peristome consists of 16 lanceolate teeth that are split into two filiform segments at the tips. The operculum is long-rostrate, approximately half the length of the capsule.

Spores are 10–14 μm in diameter, finely papillose, and mature from late winter through spring. The seta is elongated, typically 3–8 mm, and is twisted clockwise when dry.
