# Inonotus hispidus

Inonotus hispidus, commonly known as the shaggy bracket or silky rottenwood
fungus, is a perennial polypore in the family Hymenochaetaceae. It is a
significant pathogen of hardwood trees, particularly ash, walnut, apple,
and elm, causing progressive white-rot heartwood decay that ultimately
weakens [[ectomycorrhizal-host-tree-partnerships]] structural integrity. Widely distributed across temperate
Northern Hemisphere regions, the species has attracted medicinal interest
for its [[query-what-are-the-main-bioactive-compounds-in-medicinal-plants]] — hispidin, [[hispolon]], and immunomodulatory
polysaccharides — with demonstrated antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and
antitumor activities in preclinical studies.

## Taxonomy and Naming

- **Order:** Hymenochaetales
- **Family:** Hymenochaetaceae
- **Genus:** Inonotus
- **Species:** I. hispidus (Bull.) P. Karst. (1879)
- **Basionym:** Boletus hispidus Bull. (1789)
- **Synonyms:** Polyporus hispidus (Bull.) Fr., Mucronoporus hispidus
  (Bull.) Imazeki, Xanthochrous hispidus (Bull.) Pat.
- **Common names:** Shaggy bracket, silky rottenwood, ash bracket

The species epithet *hispidus* refers to the rough, bristly upper surface.
Historically placed in Polyporus and Xanthochrous, molecular phylogenetics
confirmed its placement in Inonotus within the Hymenochaetaceae.

reddish-brown liquid when cut — a diagnostic field character. The stiff
upper surface hairs can cause skin irritation in some individuals.

### Microscopic Features

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Spores** | Subglobose to broadly ellipsoid, 6–9 × 5–7 µm, smooth, hyaline |
| **Basidia** | Clavate, 4-sterigmate, 15–22 × 6–8 µm, with basal clamp connections |
| **Setal hyphae** | Abundant in context and trama, thick-walled, 15–30 µm long |
| **Hyphal system** | Monomitic, generative hyphae with clamp connections |
| **Hymenial setae** | Subulate, dark brown, 25–50 × 5–9 µm, projecting from hymenium |

The presence of setal hyphae (chlamydospore-like thick-walled cells) in the
context is diagnostic for the Hymenochaetaceae, distinguishing Inonotus
from morphologically similar polypore genera.

## Distribution and Habitat

- **Geographic range:** Widely distributed across Europe, [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]],
  temperate Asia, and parts of North Africa. Common throughout the British
  Isles, central Europe, and [[polypore-fungi-eastern-north-america]] America.
- **Substrate:** Saprotrophic and parasitic on living and dead hardwoods.
  Primary hosts: Fraxinus excelsior (ash), [[juglans-regia]] (walnut), Malus
  domestica (apple), Ulmus spp. (elms), Acer spp. (maples), Quercus spp.
- **Habitat:** Deciduous woodlands, orchards, parkland, hedgerows.
- **Season:** Late summer through autumn (July–November), occasionally
  persisting into winter.
- **Ecological role:** Causes aggressive white-rot heartwood decay,
  significantly reducing host tree stability. Serious pathogen in managed
  orchards and ornamental plantings.
