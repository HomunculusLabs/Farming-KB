## Double Digging

## Description

Double digging is a soil preparation technique used in Biointensive mini-farming where the soil is loosened to a depth of 24 inches (approximately 60 cm), compared to the conventional agricultural standard of 8-10 inches achieved with plows or tractors. This deep loosening allows significantly more air to penetrate the soil profile, which is vital for root development, microbial activity, and overall plant health. The technique is a foundational practice in the Biointensive growing method and exemplifies the [[coleman-british-adoption-of-french-intensive-gardening]]
- **Method:** Manual deep cultivation
- **Depth:** 24 inches (double the conventional 8-12 inches)

## Key Facts

- Double digging loosens soil to 24 inches versus conventional agriculture's 8-10 inches.
- The deeper soil preparation allows significantly more air to pass through the soil profile.
- Air is a vital element for plant growth that is often taken for granted — plants grow notably better in loosened versus compacted soil.
- The technique is part of the Biointensive mini-farming system that emphasizes maximizing output from minimal land area.
