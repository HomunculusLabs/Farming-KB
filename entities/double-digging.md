# Double Digging

## Description

Double digging is a soil preparation technique used in Biointensive mini-farming where the soil is loosened to a depth of 24 inches (approximately 60 cm), compared to the conventional agricultural standard of 8-10 inches achieved with plows or tractors. This deep loosening allows significantly more air to penetrate the soil profile, which is vital for root development, microbial activity, and overall plant health. The technique is a foundational practice in the Biointensive growing method and exemplifies the [[natural-farming]] principle of maximizing the utilization of air, water, and sunlight.

## Classification

- **Category:** Soil preparation technique
- **Origin:** Biointensive mini-farming / [[coleman-british-adoption-of-french-intensive-gardening]]
- **Method:** Manual deep cultivation
- **Depth:** 24 inches (double the conventional 8-12 inches)

## Key Facts

- Double digging loosens soil to 24 inches versus conventional agriculture's 8-10 inches.
- The deeper soil preparation allows significantly more air to pass through the soil profile.
- Air is a vital element for plant growth that is often taken for granted — plants grow notably better in loosened versus compacted soil.
- The technique is part of the Biointensive mini-farming system that emphasizes maximizing output from minimal land area.
- Double digging works synergistically with the [[beneficial-indigenous-microorganisms-natural-farming]] principle of "[[farming-with-air-water-and-sunlight]], water and sunlight."
- The practice facilitates deeper root penetration, better water infiltration, and improved microbial habitat.
- Combined with beneficial [[indigenous-microorganisms]], double-dug beds create an optimal environment for soil biology.
- The technique requires significant initial labor but produces long-term benefits as soil structure improves over successive seasons.

## Relationship to Natural Farming Principles

Double digging supports the "farming with air, water and sunlight" philosophy by:
- **Air:** Creating deep pore spaces for gas exchange and aerobic microbial activity.
- **Water:** Improving infiltration and retention in the deeper root zone.
- **Sunlight:** When combined with proper bed orientation (east-west or north-south), maximizing light exposure.

## Relevance to Cultivation

Deep soil preparation is relevant to any root crop or deep-rooted plant cultivation. [[contamination-prevention-in-mushroom-cultivation]], the principle of providing adequate aeration in substrate translates directly — well-aerated substrates support healthier mycelial-growth-and-colonization reduce anaerobic contamination. The broader lesson is that physical soil/substrate structure matters as much as [[chemical-composition-of-hallucinogens]]: porosity, aeration, and depth directly influence the health of any biological growing system.
