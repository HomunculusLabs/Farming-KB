## Danthonia

## Description

Danthonia is a genus of tussock-forming perennial grasses in the family Poaceae, commonly known as oat grasses or wallaby grasses. In permaculture and dryland management, Danthonia species are significant as native bunch grasses that produce substantial biomass for mulch harvesting in semi-arid environments, while also providing forage and contributing to soil stability.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Family**: Poaceae (Gramineae)
- **Subfamily**: Danthonioideae
- **Genus**: Danthonia
- **Number of species**: Approximately 20-30 species worldwide
- **Growth habit**: Perennial tussock/bunch grass
- **Common names**: Oat grass, wallaby grass (Australian species), heath grass

## Key Facts

- **Mulch production**: In unburnt semi-desert environments, Danthonia produces "very large quantities of grass mulch after rains," growing to chest height when conditions are favorable
- **Fire risk**: When dried to tinder after rains cease, dense stands of Danthonia alongside Stipa, Themeda, and Agrostis present significant fire hazard — Mollison describes standing in such stands as "quite frightening" when contemplating fire implications
- **Forage harvest**: Can be forage-harvested in strips after rains, supplying abundant mulch for garden use while reducing fire risk
- **Soil building**: When protected from fire and managed through rolling/cutting, contributes to development of humus soils in semi-arid zones
- **Native grass ecology**: As a native bunch grass, Danthonia supports indigenous soil ecosystems and provides habitat for beneficial insects and wildlife
- **[[root-exudates]] and decaying organic matter support diverse soil microbial communities
- **Water infiltration**: Tussock bases trap water and organic debris, improving infiltration in semi-arid soils

## Notable Species

- **Danthonia caespitosa** (common wallaby grass): Australian native, important in semi-arid rangelands
- **[[danthonia-californica]]** (California oat grass): Western North America, tolerant of seasonal drought
- **Danthonia linkii** (knotted wallaby grass): Australian species used in native pasture restoration

## Management Notes

- Requires protection from fire to accumulate useful biomass
- Best harvested after seed set to allow natural regeneration
- Strip harvesting maintains wildlife habitat while providing mulch
- Can be combined with other native grasses (Stipa, Themeda, Agrostis) in diverse native grassland management
- Rolling is preferred over slashing for roll-down mulching — leaves biomass in contact with soil for decomposition
- In areas with 15 cm or more annual rainfall, unburnt grassland can develop into productive humus soil if fire is excluded
