# INVAM (International Culture Collection of Vesicular Arbuscular Mycorrhizal Fungi)

## Description

INVAM is the oldest and largest centralized living germ plasm collection of arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) fungi in the world. It is located at West Virginia University and serves as the primary repository for live cultures and voucher specimens of fungi in the order Glomales. As of 2001, the collection consisted of almost 1,100 accessions of more than 86 species.

## Classification

- **Type:** Specimen repository / culture collection
- **Domain:** Mycological infrastructure
- **Location:** West Virginia University, Morgantown, West Virginia, USA
- **Associated website:** Extensive online database and resources

## Key Facts

- Contains more than 1,000 isolates representing 98 species across all recognized genera of Glomales
- The routine culture host at INVAM is [[sudangrass]] (*Sorghum sudanense*), which supports colonization by as many as 10 fungal species simultaneously
- Accepts and maintains living germ plasm deposited by researchers worldwide
- Supervised by individuals experienced in AM [[fungal-taxonomy]] and culture management
- Provides accessions for research on taxonomy, ecology, and applied mycorrhizal science
- Pot cultures at INVAM use a sand–soil mix adjusted to pH 6.2 as the standard growth medium
- Maintains an extensive biogeography database tracking global AM fungal distributions

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

INVAM is indispensable for cultivation and mycology research because it provides authenticated, viable inoculum of [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi]][[mycorrhizal-fungi]]. Researchers use INVAM accessions to:

- Establish monospecific cultures for controlled experiments on plant–fungus interactions
- Study the taxonomic diversity and systematic relationships of Glomales
- Develop inoculum for agricultural and horticultural applications of [[mycorrhizal-symbiosis]]
- Compare functional diversity of fungal isolates under varying environmental conditions
- Archive type specimens and voucher material for newly described species

The collection plays a central role in standardizing AM fungal identification and ensuring reproducibility in mycorrhizal research globally.

## Culture Methodology at INVAM

The standard pot-culture protocol involves overseeding Sudangrass in pots of 1,000–1,500 cm³ volume with sterile sand–soil mix at pH 6.2. Plants are grown for a minimum of 4 months with low-phosphorus fertilizer. Peak sporulation occurs approximately 30–40 days after shoot growth ceases. Contamination is controlled by excluding field soils and other potted plants from culture rooms, regular surface disinfection, and vigilant monitoring for fungal gnats and Collembola.
