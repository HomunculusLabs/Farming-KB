# 2C-T-17

2C-T-17 (2,5-dimethoxy-4-(s)-butylthiophenethylamine), also known as NIMITZ, is a psychedelic phenethylamine and a member of the 2C-T family described in PiHKAL entry #48.

It was nicknamed after the Nimitz Freeway (State Highway 17) connecting Oakland to San Jose, paralleling the "17" in its systematic designation.

The freeway has since been renamed Interstate 880, making the nickname a historical artifact.

## Dosage and Duration

Dosage range: 60 to 100 milligrams.

Duration: 10 to 15 hours.

The onset is characteristically slow, requiring up to three hours to reach maximum effect.

This gradual development, combined with the high dosage requirement relative to other 2C-T compounds, makes dose titration challenging.

## Qualitative Effects

At 60 milligrams, the material produces a clear but subtle experience that took fully three hours to reach maximum.

No interference with concentration or mental coordination occurs, though driving would be inadvisable.

Appetite remains intact and there are no "Tomso effects" (rekindling of psychedelic state with alcohol consumption).

The overall character was described as a "good and instructive ++" with no visual phenomena.

The experience is "totally benign" and the subject expressed no hesitation about repeating it.

At 100 milligrams, the experience becomes a "truly heavy psychedelic" despite having "very few explicit sensual changes or unusual perceptions to justify that comment."

The plateau stretches from the third to the seventh hour before tapering relatively quickly.

Sleep is fitful with hints of nervous sensitivity, and residual effects may persist into the following morning.

The quality of heaviness is difficult to articulate but unmistakable to the experiencer: "Why is it heavy? It just is."

A burning sensation from undissolved material contacting oral mucosa was noted as a practical concern when preparing solutions.

## Chemical Properties and Stereochemistry

2C-T-17 is one of the first active psychedelic compounds to carry a potential optically active center on the side of the benzene ring away from the nitrogen atom.

In most substituted amphetamines, the asymmetric carbon is adjacent to the amine group, and the "R" isomer consistently carries the psychedelic effects.

This probably says something about the nitrogen end of the receptor site, suggesting intrinsic asymmetry in the area that binds near the basic nitrogen atom.

2C-T-17's secondary butyl group introduces a new chiral center across the ring from the nitrogen, potentially probing a previously unexplored region of the receptor site, the so-called "south end."

Both "R" and "S" secondary butyl alcohols were obtained and used to synthesize separate optical isomers of 2C-T-17.

As of the time of writing, these isomers had been carried through to the nitrostyrene stage but had not yet been completed or tested.

It would be fascinating if one of the two optically active 2C-T-17 isomers carried all of the central activity and the other none.

What is more likely is that the spectrum of effects would be teased apart, with one isomer responsible for some effects and the other for the remainder.

## Status

Active psychedelic with high dosage requirement.

The nickname NIMITZ has been retired since the freeway was renumbered to Interstate 880.

The compound's theoretical importance lies in its potential to illuminate receptor geometry at the "south end" of the binding site, complementing what is known about the "north end" from alpha-methyl amphetamine stereochemistry studies.

The slow onset and long duration require patience but are manageable for experienced users.
