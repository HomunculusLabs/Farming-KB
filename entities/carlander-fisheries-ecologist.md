# Carlander (Fisheries Ecologist)

## Description

[[carlander]] is a fisheries ecologist cited by [[bill-mollison]] in *[[permaculture]]: A Designer's Manual* (Chapter 2) for research demonstrating that the standing crop of fish in reservoirs is directly correlated with the number of species present. This finding supports the broader permaculture principle that species diversity drives system productivity — a key argument for polyculture over monoculture.

## Classification

- **Type:** Person — Fisheries Ecologist / Researcher
- **Domain:** Fisheries Science, Aquatic Ecology
- **Context:** Cited in [[permaculture-design]] literature
- **Key Contribution:** Diversity-productivity relationship in aquatic systems

## Key Facts

1. **Standing crop correlation:** Carlander demonstrated that fish standing crop (total biomass) in reservoirs increases as a function of the number of fish species present. More species means more total biomass, not just more kinds of fish.

2. **Mechanism:** The diversity-productivity relationship works because different species occupy different ecological niches — feeding at different depths, on different food sources, and at different times. This niche partitioning means total available energy is utilized more completely.

3. **Support for polyculture:** Mollison uses Carlander's finding to argue against replacing complex natural systems with simplified monocultures. Clearing diverse veld or savannah to plant single-species pasture grossly decreases total yields.

4. **Energy economics:** The research supports the permaculture position that future economics must be based on energy return rather than monetary return — diverse systems capture more energy per unit area than simplified ones.

5. **Natural vs. cultivated systems:** Carlander's work shows that wild systems with complexity and high standing crop are much more productive than the simple cultivated ecologies that replace them.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Polyculture mushroom growing:** The diversity-productivity principle supports growing multiple mushroom species together or in sequence on the same substrate — different species extract different nutrients, increasing total utilization efficiency.
- **Integrated aquaculture:** Carlander's fisheries research directly supports permaculture aquaculture designs where fish ponds are integrated with mushroom production.
- **Substrate diversity:** Diverse fungal and microbial species in a mushroom substrate can exploit different nutrient niches, potentially increasing total biological conversion efficiency.
- **Pond design for permaculture:** Carlander's work validates the practice of stocking ponds with multiple fish species at multiple trophic levels to maximize energy capture and protein production.
