# Lysergic Acid Amide

**Lysergic acid amide** (LSA), also known as **D-lysergamide** or **ergine**, is a naturally occurring ergoline alkaloid and psychedelic compound. It is found in several species of morning glories ([[ipomoea-violacea]], [[turbina-corymbosa]]) and other Convolvulaceae, as well as in the fungal genus Claviceps (ergot fungi). LSA is a direct chemical precursor to LSD and shares a similar ergoline backbone, though it is significantly less potent and produces a qualitatively different experience.

## Chemical Profile

- **IUPAC name:** (6aR,9R)-N,6-diethyl-7-methyl-4,6,6a,7,8,9-hexahydroindolo[4,3-fg]quinoline-9-carboxamide
- **Molecular formula:** C₁₆H₁₇N₃O
- **Molecular weight:** 267.33 g/mol
- **CAS number:** 478-94-4
- **Structure:** Tetracyclic ergoline skeleton with an amide substituent at C-8
- **Relationship to LSD:** LSA is the amide of lysergic acid; LSD is the diethylamide (N,N-diethyllysergamide)

## Pharmacology

LSA acts primarily as a partial agonist at serotonin 5-HT receptors, with significant affinity for 5-HT1A, 5-HT2A, and 5-HT2C subtypes. Its pharmacological profile is notably different from LSD:

| Property | LSA | LSD |
|----------|-----|-----|
| 5-HT2A potency | Moderate | Very high |
| 5-HT1A activity | Significant | Moderate |
| Active dose (oral) | 0.5–3 mg | 50–200 μg |
| Duration | 6–10 hours | 8–12 hours |
| Ergotism risk | Moderate | None (synthetic) |

The additional 5-HT1A agonism of LSA contributes to its sedative, dreamy quality and the physical discomfort (nausea, vasoconstriction) commonly reported. This serotonergic profile more closely resembles that of other natural ergolines than the purely synthetic LSD.

## Natural Sources

LSA occurs in several plant families:

### Convolvulaceae (Morning Glory Family)
- **Ipomoea tricolor** (Heavenly Blue morning glory): ~0.05% LSA in seeds
- **Ipomoea violacea**: Similar alkaloid profile to I. tricolor
- **Turbina corymbosa** (Ololiuqui): ~0.02% LSA in seeds; sacred to Aztec/Mazatec peoples
- **Argyreia nervosa** (Hawaiian Baby Woodrose): ~0.3% LSA in seeds — highest known natural concentration
- **Rivea corymbosa**: Synonymous with T. corymbosa in older literature

### Fungal Sources
- **Claviceps purpurea** (ergot): LSA is a minor alkaloid in ergot sclerotia
- **Claviceps paspali**: Contains LSA and related ergolines

### Other Compounds Present
Natural LSA-containing seeds rarely contain pure LSA. They typically also contain:
- **Lysergic acid hydroxyethylamide (LSH):** More ergot-like, potentially contributing to vasoconstrictive effects
- **Ergometrine (ergonovine):** Uterotonic alkaloid
- **Isoergine:** Inactive stereoisomer
- **Chanoclavine, elymoclavine:** Minor ergoline precursors

## Historical and Cultural Use

The use of LSA-containing seeds in Mesoamerica dates back at least to the Aztec civilization:
- **Ololiuqui** (Turbina corymbosa seeds): One of the most important Aztec entheogens, described by Spanish chroniclers including Sahagún
- **Tlitliltzin** (Ipomoea violacea seeds): Black-seeded morning glory used in divination and healing
- Albert Hofmann's 1960 identification of LSA and LSH in ololiuqui seeds resolved centuries of botanical debate about the identity of the Aztec "magic seeds"

The Mazatec of Oaxaca continue to use ololiuqui seeds in divinatory rituals, though this practice is less common than the use of psilocybin mushrooms ([[psilocybe-cubensis]]) or Salvia divinorum ([[salvia-divinorum]]).

## Effects

Typical effects of LSA (oral, 2–10 seeds depending on species):
- **Onset:** 30–90 minutes
- **Peak:** 2–5 hours
- **Duration:** 6–10 hours
- **Visual:** Mild color enhancement, pattern recognition, closed-eye imagery (less intense than LSD or psilocybin)
- **Cognitive:** Dreamy, contemplative state; introspective thought patterns
- **Physical:** Nausea (common and often significant), sedation, mydriasis, mild vasoconstriction, lethargy

The experience is frequently described as more sedating and less visually spectacular than LSD or psilocybin, with a quality resembling a lucid dream.

## Safety Considerations

- **Nausea:** One of the most consistently reported adverse effects, attributed to LSH and other non-LSA ergolines in crude seed preparations
- **Vasoconstriction:** Ergoline compounds can cause peripheral vasoconstriction; concern for prolonged or high-dose use
- **Uterotonic effects:** Ergometrine content contraindicates use during pregnancy
- **Purity:** Commercial morning glory seeds may be coated with pesticides or anti-fungal treatments; seeds should be sourced as untreated horticultural stock
- **Legality:** LSA is scheduled in many jurisdictions as a controlled substance analogue or precursor; legal status varies by country

## Synthesis and Derivatives

LSA is a controlled precursor to LSD. Key relationships:
- LSA → LSD via N,N-diethylation (the Hofmann synthesis, 1938)
- LSA serves as starting material for numerous ergoline pharmaceuticals (methylergometrine, nicergoline, pergolide)
- The distinction between natural LSA and synthetic LSD is both chemical (diethylamide vs. simple amide) and experiential (potency, duration, character)

## Analytical Detection

LSA can be detected and quantified using several analytical methods:
- **HPLC with fluorescence detection:** Most common method for quantitative analysis of ergoline alkaloids in plant material
- **GC-MS:** Requires derivatization due to thermal lability; useful for forensic identification
- **LC-MS/MS:** Most sensitive and specific method; capable of detecting LSA and metabolites in biological fluids
- **TLC:** Thin-layer chromatography with UV and Dragendorff's reagent for rapid screening of plant extracts
- **Ehrlich's reagent:** Colorimetric test that produces a blue/purple reaction with indole-containing compounds including LSA

LSA is metabolized in the body through oxidative dealkylation and conjugation. The elimination half-life is approximately 3–4 hours. It does not appear on standard drug screening panels, though specialized ergoline assays can detect it.

## Contemporary Research

Modern research on LSA and related ergolines encompasses several areas:
- **Serotonin receptor pharmacology:** LSA serves as a tool compound for studying 5-HT1A/5-HT2A receptor interactions
- **Ergoline chemistry:** The ergoline scaffold remains important in medicinal chemistry; numerous pharmaceuticals (ergotamine, pergolide, cabergoline, nicergoline) are derived from or structurally related to lysergic acid
- **Analytical standard:** LSA is used as a reference standard in forensic and analytical chemistry
- **Ethnopharmacology:** Continuing documentation of traditional use practices among Mazatec and other indigenous communities
- **Biosynthesis:** Research into the fungal biosynthetic pathway of ergoline alkaloids has identified key enzymes and intermediates
