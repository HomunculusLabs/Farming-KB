## Description

Tokyo Bekana (*[[brassica-rapa]]* subsp. *chinensis*) is a Japanese loose-leaf cabbage cultivar producing light green, ruffled leaves with a mild, sweet flavor. It is grown as a component of braising and Asian salad mixes in winter greenhouse production, valued for its cold hardiness and tender texture.

## Classification

- **Type**: Cultivar (Leafy vegetable)
- **Species**: *Brassica rapa* subsp. *chinensis*
- **Family**: Brassicaceae (mustard family)
- **Category**: Asian green, braising/salad green
- **Origin**: Japan

## Key Facts

- Produces loose, ruffled light green leaves on thin white stems — visually similar to lettuce but botanically a cabbage
- Very mild flavor with almost no mustard pungency, making it suitable for raw salad use
- Cold-hardy — successfully grown in unheated cold houses through Zone 5 winters
- Included in Eliot Coleman's braising mix alongside mizuna, mibuna, tatsoi, and pak choi
- Harvested at 4 inches or less for braising mix applications
- Leaves are tender enough for salad use when young — some customers prefer the braising mix as a raw salad
- Quick-maturing and suitable for succession planting through the winter season

## Cultivation Notes

- Classified among the hardy Asian greens that do not require minimal heat protection
- Like other brassicas in the winter system, benefits from wind protection and moderated temperatures under row covers
- Should be harvested promptly at peak quality as it will bolt with lengthening days in late winter
- The mild flavor profile makes it versatile — usable raw in salads or cooked in stir-fries and braises

## Relevance to Cultivation

Tokyo Bekana represents the diversity of Asian brassica cultivars available for winter production. Its extremely mild flavor bridges the gap between assertive mustard greens and delicate European salad crops. In the [[four-season-farm]] system, it contributes to a braising mix that doubles as an "Asian salad" — demonstrating how crop selection can create products with multiple market applications. Its hardiness in unheated structures makes it a reliable producer during the months when conventional outdoor growing is impossible.
