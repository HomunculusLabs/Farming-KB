## Description

Earthworms are segmented annelid worms (phylum Annelida, class Oligochaeta) that
constitute one of the most important groups of soil macrofauna. They are primary
agents of soil turnover, organic matter decomposition, aeration, and nutrient cycling
in temperate and tropical soils. Their biomass per hectare can vastly exceed that of
livestock grazing the same land, yet they remain largely invisible to the casual observer.

## Classification

- **Phylum:** Annelida
- **Class:** Oligochaeta
- **[[crumb-structure]]:** Casts and burrowing promote soil aggregation.
- **pH modification:** Bring alkaline materials to the surface, creating vertical pH gradients.
- **Microbial dispersal:** Carry bacteria and fungi throughout the soil profile.
- **Gas exchange:** Burrows act as pump pistons drawing air through soil (like trains in tunnels).

## Population Dynamics

- Worm numbers increase dramatically under mulch.
- Acid soils (coniferous/oak forests) support few earthworms.
- Humic peats support even fewer.
- Climate strongly affects balance: tropical soils may have more insect-driven turnover.
- About 30% of plant energy may be lost as sugars released to soil via roots — feeding worms.

## Contamination Risks

- Wormcasts from pig, sheep, or domestic wastes on galvanized surfaces accumulate excess zinc.
- Excess zinc inhibits plant growth and health.
- Handreck (1987) recommends maximum 10% worm casts in potting mixes.
- Poultry manures are particularly problematic for copper and zinc contamination.
- Always test urban worm castings before heavy use.

## Numbers in Temperate Grassland (per square metre)

- Nematodes: 120 million
- Mites: 100,000
- Springtails: 45,000
- Enchytraeid worms: 20,000
- Molluscs: 10,000
- Fungi: up to 50% of total living biomass

## Relevance to Cultivation

- Best single indicator of soil health.
- Mulching, minimal tillage, and avoiding biocides encourage them.
- Their presence signals good crumb structure, adequate organic matter, balanced pH.
- In tropical soils, ants and termites partially fill the earthworm role.

## Relevance to Mycology

- Disperse fungal spores throughout soil profile.
- Create channels colonised by [[fungal-hyphae]].
- Increase anaerobic microsites supporting ethylene production.
- Their casts are rich in microbial life including beneficial fungi.
- Support the oxygen-ethylene cycle essential for nutrient availability.
