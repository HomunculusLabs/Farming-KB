# Jeremy Rifkin

## Description

Jeremy Rifkin is an American economic and social theorist, writer, and public speaker known for his critical analyses of technology, economics, and environmental policy. He is referenced by David Holmgren in *Permaculture: Principles and Pathways Beyond Sustainability* in the context of genetic engineering and its potential to transform humanity. Rifkin suggested that genetic engineering could lead to the transformation of humanity into a new recombinant species, a prospect Holmgren discusses within the broader framework of evolutionary change and the Maximum Power Law.

## Classification

- **Type:** Person / Author / Social Theorist
- **Domain:** Environmental Policy, Economics, Technology Critique
- **Era:** Late 20th – Early 21st Century

## Key Facts

- Author of *Entropy: A New World View* (Viking, 1980), which Holmgren cites in the context of thermodynamic limits to growth and energy descent futures.
- Rifkin was an early and prominent critic of genetic engineering, arguing that recombinant DNA technology poses fundamental risks to the integrity of species, including humanity.
- He suggested that genetic engineering combined with computer technology could create a massive acceleration in evolutionary change, potentially transforming humanity into a new species — a scenario consistent with the Maximum Power Law's prediction of rapid reorganisation around new energy sources.
- His work on entropy applied the second law of thermodynamics to economic and social systems, arguing that all energy transformations increase disorder and that economic growth based on energy consumption is ultimately unsustainable.
- Rifkin served as advisor to the Foundation on Economic Trends and was involved in legal actions to regulate biotechnology.

## Key Works

- **Entropy: A New World View** (1980) — Applied thermodynamic principles to economics and society; argues that energy-intensive civilisation accelerates entropic decline.
- **Algeny** (1983) — Critical examination of genetic engineering and its philosophical implications.
- **The End of Work** (1995) — Predicted technological displacement of labour across the global economy.
- **The Hydrogen Economy** (2002) — Proposed hydrogen as an energy carrier for a post-fossil-fuel world.
- **The Third Industrial Revolution** (2011) — Argued for distributed energy networks as the basis of a new economic paradigm.

## Relevance to Permaculture and Ecological Design

Rifkin's work is relevant to permaculture in several ways:

1. **Thermodynamic awareness:** His entropy framework supports the permaculture principle of catching and storing energy, recognising that energy quality degrades through use.
2. **Energy descent context:** Rifkin's critique of infinite growth aligns with Holmgren's energy descent scenarios and the permaculture focus on designing for reduced energy availability.
3. **Genetic engineering critique:** His warnings about recombinant DNA technology parallel permaculture's preference for working with natural biological processes rather than overriding them through high-energy technological intervention.
4. **Systems thinking:** Rifkin's holistic analysis of technology-economy-society interactions mirrors the permaculture design approach of understanding system connections.

Holmgren uses Rifkin's perspective to argue that the grand illusion of genetic engineering is the faith in an ability to manipulate our environment using high energy and technology — an approach that becomes untenable in an energy descent future where competition for capital from established fossil-fuel industries intensifies.
