## Vandana Shiva

## Description

Vandana Shiva is an Indian scholar, environmental activist, food sovereignty advocate, and author whose work has been highly influential in critiques of industrial agriculture, genetic engineering, and the Green Revolution. She is cited by [[david-holmgren]] as a key source of evidence documenting the destructive effects of the Green Revolution, particularly in India, and its erosion of agricultural biodiversity.

## Classification

- **Type:** Person — scholar, activist, author
- **Nationality:** Indian
- **Fields:** Environmental philosophy, food sovereignty, biodiversity conservation, anti-globalization activism
- **Born:** 1952, Dehradun, India

## Key Facts

- **Author of Monocultures of the Mind:** This influential work is cited twice by Holmgren as providing detailed evidence of the negative effects of the Green Revolution in India. The book argues that industrial monoculture thinking — the reduction of diverse agricultural systems to single-crop production — represents not just a farming method but a worldview that eliminates biological and cultural diversity.

- **Green Revolution critique:** Shiva has documented how Green Revolution grain varieties, while producing higher yields under ideal conditions, required more fertilisers and pesticides and produced less straw for feeding animals and soil organisms. This reduced the integrated productivity of traditional farming systems and created dependency on external inputs.

- **Navdanya movement:** Shiva founded Navdanya, a network of seed keepers and organic producers across India, dedicated to conserving indigenous seed varieties and promoting organic farming methods as alternatives to industrial agriculture.

- **Intellectual property and seed sovereignty:** She has been a leading voice against patenting of seeds and genetic resources by multinational corporations, arguing that these practices constitute biopiracy and threaten the livelihoods of small farmers.

- **Biodiversity as wealth:** Shiva's framework treats biological and cultural diversity as forms of abundance that industrial agriculture systematically destroys, a perspective that aligns closely with [[permaculture-ethics-and-principles]].

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

Vandana Shiva's work is directly relevant to [[permaculture]] in several dimensions:

1. **Critique of monoculture:** Her documentation of how Green Revolution monocultures reduce total system productivity (including animal feed, soil organic matter, and diverse food production) provides empirical support for permaculture's emphasis on polyculture and integrated systems.

2. **Seed saving and biodiversity:** Her advocacy for seed sovereignty and conservation of traditional crop varieties parallels permaculture's emphasis on maintaining genetic diversity in cultivated systems as insurance against an uncertain future.

3. **Third ethic alignment:** Shiva's work embodies the permaculture ethic of setting limits to consumption and redistributing surplus, particularly in her critique of how industrial agriculture concentrates wealth while impoverishing small farmers.

4. **Systems thinking:** Her analysis of how monoculture thinking extends beyond farming into economics, science, and culture supports the permaculture principle of designing from integrated patterns rather than segregated elements.

## Key Works

- *The Violence of the Green Revolution* (1991)
- *Stolen Harvest: The Hijacking of the Global Food Supply* (2000)
- *Soil Not Oil: Environmental Justice in an Age of Climate Crisis* (2008)
