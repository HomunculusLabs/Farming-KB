## Overview

Hypericin is a red-colored naphthodianthrone compound found in *Hypericum*
species, most abundantly in *[[singh-fungal-biodegradation-of-polycyclic-aromatic-hydrocarbons]]
quinone with potent photodynamic activity. When exposed to [[reactive-oxygen-species-and-oxidative-stress]]
(ROS) that can damage cellular membranes, proteins, and nucleic acids.

This photosensitizing property underlies both its [[metzner-ayahuasca-therapeutic-potential]] in
photodynamic therapy and its risk of causing severe photosensitivity
reactions in humans and livestock consuming the compound.

## Chemical Properties

- **Chemical class:** Naphthodianthrone (dianthraquinone derivative)
- **Molecular formula:** C₃₀H₁₆O₈
- **Molecular weight:** 504.45 g/mol
- **IUPAC name:** 1,3,4,6,8,13-hexahydroxy-10,11-dimethyl-
  phenanthro[1,10,9,8-opqra]perylene-7,14-dione
- **CAS number:** 548-04-9
- **Appearance:** Dark blue-black crystalline solid, intensely red
  fluorescence in solution
- **Solubility:** Practically insoluble in water; soluble in DMSO, DMF,
  pyridine, and alkaline aqueous solutions; poorly soluble in ethanol

Hypericin exists predominantly in its keto form under physiological
conditions. The extended planar polycyclic chromophore absorbs strongly in
the 540–600 nm range, giving solutions a deep red color and intense
fluorescence at approximately 590–650 nm.

## Pharmacology and Mechanism of Action

**Antidepressant activity:** Hypericin was initially believed to be the
primary antidepressant compound in St. John's wort through inhibition of
monoamine oxidase (MAO-A and MAO-B). However, subsequent research revealed
that hypericin concentrations in standard extracts are too low for
significant [[serotonin]], and GABA receptor systems.

**Photodynamic activity:** Upon absorption of visible light, hypericin
undergoes type I and type II photosensitization reactions. Type I involves
electron transfer to generate superoxide and hydroxyl radicals. Type II
transfers energy to molecular oxygen, producing singlet oxygen (^1O₂).
This dual mechanism makes hypericin one of the most efficient natural
photosensitizers known.

**Antiviral activity:** Hypericin exhibits broad-spectrum antiviral activity
in vitro against enveloped viruses including HIV-1, hepatitis C, influenza,
and [[cannabis-antiviral-herpes-simplex-blesching]] virus. The mechanism involves photodynamic disruption of
viral envelopes and inactivation of viral particles. In vivo efficacy is
limited by poor bioavailability and photosensitivity toxicity.
