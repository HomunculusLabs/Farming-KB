# Psilocybe subfimetaria

**Psilocybe subfimetaria** is a psilocybin-containing mushroom species originally described from Australia. As the specific epithet suggests, it is closely related to and morphologically similar to *Psilocybe fimetaria*, a species first described from Europe. The species is part of a group of coprophilous and coprophilous-associated *Psilocybe* mushrooms found in temperate and subtropical regions of the [[psilocin]] as its principal psychoactive alkaloids. Analytical data on exact alkaloid concentrations are limited due to the relative rarity of collections, but the species is considered to be moderately psychoactive.

## Ecological Role

As a coprophilous fungus, *P. subfimetaria* plays a role in nutrient cycling by decomposing herbivore dung. The spores are adapted to survive passage through the digestive tracts of grazing animals, facilitating dispersal to new substrate. ## Conservation Status

*Psilocybe subfimetaria* has not been formally assessed for conservation status. Its dependence on pastoral habitats with herbivore dung means that changes in agricultural practices — including reduced grazing, vermiculture treatments, and the use of anti-helminthic drugs in livestock — could potentially affect its populations. However, the species appears to be naturally uncommon even in suitable habitat, and the extent to which it is threatened by agricultural changes is unknown.
## Seasonality and Fruiting

*Psilocybe subfimetaria* fruits during the cooler, wetter months in southern Australia, typically from autumn through spring (April to October). Peak fruiting generally occurs during the winter months of June through August, when consistent rainfall and moderate temperatures create optimal conditions. The species fruits directly on or near herbivore dung, appearing 3–10 days after significant rainfall events. Individual fruiting bodies are relatively short-lived, typically persisting for 5–10 days before deteriorating. The species may produce multiple flushes from the same dung pat over a period of several weeks during extended wet periods. Its preference for cooler temperatures distinguishes it from some tropical coprophilous species that require warmer conditions for fruiting.
## Identification Notes

Field identification of *Psilocybe subfimetaria* requires attention to several characters that distinguish it from other coprophilous species:
- Small cap (1–2.5 cm) with hygrophanous coloration: reddish brown to cinnamon when moist, fading to pale buff when dry
- Slightly viscid cap surface when wet, becoming dry and dull as it dries
- Blue-green bruising on the stipe, especially at the base, though this reaction can be modest
- Dark purplish brown spore print (distinct from the black spore print of Panaeolus species)
- Growth on or near herbivore dung in temperate pastures

The modest bluing reaction means that the psychoactive content is not always immediately apparent from handling. Spore print color is a critical diagnostic character — the dark purplish brown spore print distinguishes *P. subfimetaria* from the many dung-inhabiting species with black (Panaeolus) or brown (Conocybe) spore prints. The farinaceous (mealy) taste and odor provide supporting characters. Definitive identification requires microscopic examination, particularly of spore dimensions (9–13 × 6–8 µm), which are larger than those of many similar species.
