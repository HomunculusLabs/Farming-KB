# Tetrahydrocannabiphorol (THCP)

**Tetrahydrocannabiphorol** (THCP) is a phytocannabinoid identified in 2019 by a team of Italian researchers led by Cinzia Citti at the University of Modena and Reggio Emilia. It is notable for possessing a **pentyl side chain extended by two methylene units** to a heptyl chain (7 carbon atoms), making it the first naturally occurring **heptyl homolog** of Δ⁹-tetrahydrocannabinol (THC). This structural extension confers dramatically enhanced binding affinity at cannabinoid receptors — up to 33 times that of Δ⁹-THC at CB1.

## Discovery

THCP was isolated from a medicinal cannabis variety called **FM2**, produced by the Italian Military Pharmaceutical Institute in Florence. The discovery was part of a broader analytical campaign using advanced **LC-HRMS** (liquid chromatography–high-resolution mass spectrometry) and **NMR** (nuclear magnetic resonance) techniques to catalog the full spectrum of cannabinoids present in certified medical cannabis.

The research team identified two homologs:
- **Δ⁹-THCP** (Δ⁹-tetrahydrocannabiphorol) — the heptyl analog of Δ⁹-THC
- **Δ⁹-THCP-COOH** — the carboxylated (acid) precursor, analogous to THCA

Simultaneously, the same team identified **CBDP** (cannabidiphorol), the heptyl homolog of CBD, from the same plant material.

## Pharmacology

### Receptor Binding Affinity

The pharmacological significance of THCP lies in its extraordinary receptor affinity, which far exceeds that of Δ⁹-THC:

| Receptor | Ki (THCP) | Ki (Δ⁹-THC) | Relative Affinity |
|----------|-----------|-------------|-------------------|
| CB1 | 1.2 nM | 40 nM | ~33× higher |
| CB2 | 5.7 nM | 36 nM | ~6× higher |

This binding profile makes THCP one of the most potent naturally occurring cannabinoids ever characterized. The Ki value of 1.2 nM at CB1 places THCP among the most potent CB1 agonists known, rivaling certain synthetic cannabinoids.

### In Vivo Activity

The 2019 study demonstrated in vivo cannabinoid activity in mice using the classic **tetrad assay** (a battery of four behavioral tests predictive of cannabinoid activity):

- **Hypomobility**: Reduced locomotor activity at doses as low as 0.1 mg/kg (vs. 2.5 mg/kg for Δ⁹-THC)
- **Analgesia**: Significant antinociceptive response in the hot-plate test
- **Hypothermia**: Dose-dependent reduction in body temperature
- **Catalepsy**: Immobility on the ring test at higher doses

The effective dose of THCP was approximately **5–10 times lower** than Δ⁹-THC across all four measures, confirming that the enhanced CB1 affinity translates to greater in vivo potency.

### Proposed Contribution to "cannabis terpenes and the entourage effect"

A significant implication of THCP's discovery is its potential role in explaining the variable psychoactivity of different cannabis varieties. The Italian researchers hypothesized that THCP may be present at concentrations too low to detect by standard analytical methods, yet sufficient — given its 33× potency advantage — to contribute meaningfully to the overall pharmacological effect. This could partially explain why some cannabis samples produce unexpectedly strong effects despite modest Δ⁹-THC levels.

## Synthetic and Semi-Synthetic Production

### Laboratory Synthesis

THCP can be synthesized through several routes, most commonly by adapting established THC synthesis protocols with heptyl-substituted starting materials:

1. **Olivetol route**: Heptyl-substituted resorcinol (1,3-dihydroxy-5-heptylbenzene) is condensed with a suitable monoterpene (e.g., p-menthadienol or citral) under acidic conditions
2. **Cannabigerol-type route**: Heptyl-cannabigerolic acid is cyclized using an appropriate cyclase enzyme or acid catalyst
