# Leucocoprinus birnbaumii (Flowerpot Parasol)

**Leucocoprinus birnbaumii**, commonly known as the **flowerpot parasol**, **plantpot dapperling**, or **yellow houseplant mushroom**, is a species of agaric fungus in the family **Agaricaceae**. It is the most frequently encountered mycelium
- **Surface:** Smooth above the ring zone, finely scaly below
- **Ring:** Present as a thin, membranous, movable (annulate) ring that often falls away with age

### Flesh
- **Color:** Pale yellow, thin
- **Texture:** Soft, fragile
- **Odor:** Faint, not distinctive; sometimes slightly fruity
- **Taste:** Mild

### Spore Print
- **Color:** White
- **Spore dimensions:** 8–12 × 5–8 µm, ellipsoid to ovoid, smooth, with a germ pore

## Habitat and Distribution

*L. birnbaumii* is a **saprotrophic** species closely associated with:

- **Potted plants** and container gardens — its most common habitat
- **Commercial potting soils** and peat-based green-cannabis-rockwool-hydroponic-growing-media
- **Greenhouses** and conservatories worldwide
- **Compost piles** and organic debris in tropical and subtropical regions
- **Tropical and subtropical forests** in its native range

The species is **cosmopolitan** in distribution, found indoors on every inhabited continent. In nature, it occurs in tropical and subtropical regions of the Americas, Africa, Asia, and Australia. Its appearance in temperate indoor environments is a result of contaminated potting soil or sphagnum moss products. It fruits year-round indoors when conditions are warm and humid.

## Toxicity

**Leucocoprinus birnbaumii is poisonous and should not be consumed:**

- Contains **gastrointestinal irritants** that cause nausea, vomiting, abdominal cramps, and diarrhea
- The toxic principles have not been fully characterized but are likely similar to other *Leucocoprinus* species
- While rarely life-threatening, ingestion can cause significant discomfort
- Particularly dangerous in households with small children or pets who may be attracted to the bright coloration
- **No fatalities have been reported**, but medical attention should be sought if ingested
