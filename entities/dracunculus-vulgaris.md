## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Dracunculus vulgaris** Schott ex Targioni Tozzetti was described in the 19th century. It is the type species of the genus *Dracunculus*, which contains two recognized species within Araceae subfamily Aroideae.

The second species, *Dracunculus canariensis*, is endemic to the Canary Islands and differs in having a shorter spathe and more deeply divided leaves.

The plant produces a single large leaf, or rarely two, emerging from an underground tuber. The leaf is highly dissected and palmate, measuring 30–100 cm across, with 7–15 lanceolate segments on long petioles.

The tuber is large, often weighing 1–3 kg in mature specimens, and produces offsets that enable [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]] due to garden escape. These populations are generally localized and not considered ecologically invasive.

The species has been collected from the wild for ornamental use, and in some areas, wild populations have declined due to tuber harvesting. It is protected by law in parts of its native range.

## Pollination Biology

The inflorescence of **Dracunculus vulgaris** is thermogenic, producing metabolic heat during anthesis that volatilizes odor compounds. Spadix temperatures can exceed ambient by 10–15°C during peak odor production.

The primary odorants include dimethyl disulfide, dimethyl trisulfide, and oligosulfides, which closely mimic the scent of decaying flesh. This chemical profile is remarkably similar to that of true carrion.

The spathe surface is covered in microscopic papillae that enhance odor emission by increasing surface area and trapping heat.

Pollination is primarily carried out by flesh flies (Sarcophagidae), blow flies (Calliphoridae), and beetles (Staphylinidae, Silphidae). These insects are attracted to the inflorescence expecting a carcass for oviposition.

The spathe acts as a temporary trap, preventing insect escape during the female phase of anthesis. Insects dusted with pollen from a previous visit transfer pollen to receptive stigmas.

Research on thermogenesis in *Dracunculus* has contributed to broader understanding of plant respiration and metabolic regulation. The mitochondrial alternative oxidase pathway plays a central role in heat production.

## Cultivation

Dragon arum is widely grown as an ornamental for its dramatic, tropical-looking inflorescence. It is one of the easiest thermogenic aroids to cultivate in temperate gardens.

The plant is cold-hardy to approximately USDA Zone 6 when the tuber is planted deep enough. Mulching heavily in winter provides additional protection in colder regions.

Tubers should be planted 10–15 cm deep in well-drained, humus-rich soil. They prefer a site with summer drought and winter moisture, mirroring Mediterranean climate patterns.

Propagation is primarily by separation of tuber offsets in autumn or early spring. [[cannabis-seed-germination]] is possible but slow, often requiring 1–3 years to produce a flowering-sized tuber.
