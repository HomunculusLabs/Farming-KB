## Chemical Properties

The molecular formula of 4-AcO-DET is C18H24N2O2.

Its molecular weight is 300.40 g/mol.

The IUPAC name is [3-[2-(diethylamino)ethyl]-1H-indol-4-yl] acetate.

The compound exists as a crystalline solid at room temperature.

The 4-acetoxy group serves as an ester prodrug moiety, readily hydrolyzed in vivo.

This deacetylation converts 4-AcO-DET into its active metabolite, 4-HO-DET.

The acetate ester increases lipophilicity compared to the phenolic 4-HO form.

Structurally, it differs from 4-AcO-DMT by the replacement of N,N-dimethyl with N,N-diethyl groups.

The diethyl substitution increases molecular weight and slightly alters [[turner-harmala-alkaloids-preparation-and-dosage]].

As a prodrug, it requires metabolic deacetylation by esterases to produce the active 4-HO-DET.

This metabolic conversion is rapid, with onset of effects beginning within 15–30 minutes of ingestion.

The diethyl substitution on the terminal amine results in slightly lower potency than the dimethyl analogue (4-AcO-DMT).

Binding affinity at 5-HT2A is estimated to be in the low micromolar range based on structural analogues.

## Effects

The subjective effects of 4-AcO-DET are similar to other 4-substituted tryptamines.

Visual effects include color enhancement, pattern recognition, geometric hallucinations, and trails.

Users report mild to moderate introspective and cognitive effects, generally less intense than DMT or [[leary-psychedelic-experience-bardo-mapping]].

Duration of effects is approximately 4–6 hours, consistent with the oral tryptamine class.

The comedown is generally gradual with minimal residual stimulation or anxiety.

## Synthesis

4-AcO-DET is synthesized via acetylation of 4-HO-DET using acetic anhydride.

The [[shirota-4-hydroxyindole-acetyl-protection-psilocin-synthesis-starting-material]], 4-HO-DET, can be prepared from DET through a 4-position hydroxylation.

DET itself is derived from tryptamine via reductive N-alkylation with diethyl groups.
