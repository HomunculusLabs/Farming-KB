Campanula americana occurs across eastern North America from southern Ontario and Quebec southward through the Appalachian Mountains to northern Florida. Its range extends westward to eastern Kansas, Oklahoma, and eastern Texas.

**Cultivation:** Campanula americana is cultivated as an ornamental in woodland gardens, native plant landscapes, and shaded perennial borders. It thrives in part shade with moist, humus-rich soil and benefits from a layer of organic mulch.

Transplanted seedlings establish readily in spring after danger of frost has passed. The species naturalizes freely through self-seeding in suitable sites but is not considered aggressive or weedy.

Plants perform poorly in hot, dry sites and full sun, where foliage may yellow and flower production declines significantly. Consistent soil moisture is the most critical cultural factor for garden success.

## Uses

Campanula americana has limited documented ethnobotanical use among Indigenous peoples of eastern North America. The Cherokee prepared infusions of the aerial parts as a treatment for coughs and respiratory ailments.
