## Water Hyacinth (Eichhornia crassipes)

## Description

Water hyacinth ([[eichhornia-crassipes]]) is a free-floating perennial aquatic plant in the Pontederiaceae family, native to the Amazon basin of South America. Mollison describes it as a key species in [[biological-water-purification]], specifically noting its use for removing residual metals, surplus nutrients, and coliform bacteria (including E. coli) from the aerobic stage of sewage treatment systems. Despite its value in water treatment, water hyacinth is one of the world's most invasive aquatic plants.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Division**: Magnoliophyta
- **Class**: Liliopsida
- **Order**: Commelinales
- **Family**: Pontederiaceae
- **Genus**: Eichhornia
- **Species**: E. crassipes

## Key Facts

- Water hyacinth features glossy green rounded leaves, inflated petioles that act as floatation devices, and showy lavender flowers with a characteristic yellow spot on the upper petal.
- Individual plants can double their biomass in 6-18 days under optimal conditions, making it one of the fastest-growing plants known.
- Mollison identifies water hyacinth as a pond surface stabiliser alongside Azolla and water lilies, reducing turbidity and algal growth.
- In the aerobic treatment stage, water hyacinth removes residual heavy metals, surplus nutrients, and the E. coli group of bacilli from sewage lagoon effluent.
- The plant's extensive feathery root system hangs below the water surface, providing enormous surface area for nutrient uptake and bacterial habitat.
- Can accumulate heavy metals including chromium, copper, cadmium, zinc, lead, and mercury in its tissues, enabling phytoremediation of contaminated water.
- Mollison notes that harvested water hyacinth can be processed for mulch, compost, biogas feedstock, or animal fodder (with caution regarding toxin accumulation).
- Also used in Holland's sewage canal systems, where reeds and floating plants are harvested as green crop or craft supplies.

## Role in Water Purification

Mollison describes water hyacinth functioning at several stages of the biological water treatment process:

1. **Surface stabilisation**: Reduces wind-driven turbidity in settling and treatment ponds.
2. **Nutrient removal**: Absorbs dissolved nitrogen and phosphorus, preventing eutrophication of downstream waterways.
3. **Metal accumulation**: Concentrates heavy metals in root and leaf tissues, removing them from the water column.
4. **Pathogen reduction**: Root zone microbial communities associated with water hyacinth help eliminate E. coli and other coliform bacteria.
5. **Final polishing**: In the aerobic pond stage, water hyacinth provides the last biological treatment before water is released to rush beds or trickle irrigation.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Phytoremediation**: Water hyacinth is one of the most studied plants for wastewater treatment, capable of removing nutrients, metals, and organic pollutants.
- **Biogas production**: Harvested biomass can be fed into anaerobic digesters to produce methane, integrating water purification with energy production.
- **Compost and mulch**: Decomposed water hyacinth provides nutrient-rich organic matter for soil amendment.
- **Permaculture design**: Fits into zone 3-4 water management systems as a productive component of sewage lagoons, greywater treatment cells, and aquaculture ponds.
- **Caution required**: The invasive potential of water hyacinth demands careful containment in any designed system; never introduce to natural waterways.

## Ecological Warnings

- Listed among the top 10 most invasive species globally by the IUCN.
- Can completely cover water surfaces, blocking light, depleting oxygen, and killing fish and native aquatic organisms.
- Obstructs waterways, irrigation channels, and hydroelectric installations.
- Must be continuously harvested from treatment systems to prevent overgrowth and maintain treatment efficiency.
- Biological control agents (weevils Neochetina eichhorniae and N. bruchi) have been used with varying success.
