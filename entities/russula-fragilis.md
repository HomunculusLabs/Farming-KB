## Russula fragilis (Fragile Russula)

## Description

Russula fragilis is a small, brightly coloured species of Russula closely resembling Russula emetica but distinguished by its striate cap edge, more fragile stature, and immediately acrid taste. It is considered poisonous in the raw state, though cooking disperses the irritant substance.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Russulales
- **Family:** Russulaceae
- **Genus:** Russula
- **Species:** R. fragilis

## Morphology

The cap is typically bright red, though with a much wider range of colour variation than Russula emetica. It is usually smaller than R. emetica and has a striate edge — the key distinguishing feature. Average cap width is approximately 2.75 inches; stem height about 2 inches; stem width about 3/8 inch.

The stem is spongy then hollow, and very fragile (hence the name). The flesh is NOT pink under the skin of the cap, unlike R. emetica. The taste is immediately very acrid but not as durable/persistent as that of R. emetica. The smell is pleasant. Some colour forms have been given varietal names.

## Key Facts

- Closely resembles Russula emetica but is usually smaller
- Cap has a striate edge (distinguishing feature from R. emetica)
- Flesh is not pink under the cap skin (unlike R. emetica)
- Stem is very fragile, spongy then hollow
- Taste is immediately very acrid but less persistent than R. emetica
- Wide range of colour forms, some given varietal names
- Most common form has a red cap
- Grows in woods in summer and autumn

## Toxicity

Russula fragilis is considered poisonous when raw due to its intensely acrid taste. Like other acrid Russula species, the irritant substance is dispersed by cooking. However, it is best avoided. The toxicity is relatively mild compared to species like Amanita phalloides, causing primarily gastrointestinal distress if consumed raw.

## Distinguishing from Russula emetica

| Feature | R. fragilis | R. emetica |
|---------|-------------|------------|
| Cap edge | Striate | Smooth at first, furrowed later |
| Size | Usually smaller | Larger |
| Flesh under cap skin | Not pink | Pink |
| Taste onset | Immediate | After a few seconds |
| Taste persistence | Less durable | Very persistent |
| Stem | Very fragile, spongy | Rigid |

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Genus characteristics:** Demonstrates key Russula features — brittle flesh, no milk exudation (distinguishing from Lactarius), and acrid taste
- **Taxonomic complexity:** The wide range of colour forms with varietal names illustrates the phenotypic plasticity within Russula species
- **Taste-based taxonomy:** In the genus Russula, taste is used as a primary division in some classification systems
- **Foraging education:** Serves as an important lookalike species that must be distinguished from edible Russula species
- **Comparative morphology:** The comparison with R. emetica teaches critical observation skills for field mycology

## Habitat and Ecology

Occurs in woods during summer and autumn. Different colour forms may be associated with different tree partners, though this is less well documented than for R. emetica. The species is saprobic or mycorrhizal depending on growth stage and environment.

## Historical Notes

Described by Ramsbottom (1945) on Plate 8 alongside R. emetica, R. fellea, and R. drimeia. The species epithet "fragilis" refers to the very fragile nature of the stem and flesh.
