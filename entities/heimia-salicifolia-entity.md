# Heimia salicifolia
Heimia salicifolia is a perennial shrub in the family Lythraceae, native to
semi-tropical regions of the Americas from Mexico through Argentina. Commonly
known as sinicuichi or sun opener, it has a long history of use in traditional
Mesoamerican shamanic practices, where its dried leaves are prepared as a
fermented tea to produce auditory hallucinations, memory enhancement, and a
characteristic yellowing of vision. The plant contains several quinolizidine
alkaloids, with cryogenine (vertine) considered the primary active constituent
responsible for its psychoactive effects. Despite its ethnobotanical
significance, Heimia salicifolia remains poorly studied in modern
pharmacology, with most knowledge derived from traditional use reports and a
small body of mid-20th-century laboratory research.
## Taxonomy and Naming
- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Order:** Myrtales
- **Family:** Lythraceae
- **Genus:** Heimia
- **Species:** H. salicifolia
- **Authority:** Link
- **Synonyms:** Heimia salicifolia var. latifolia, Heimia myrtifolia
- **Common names:** Sinicuichi, sun opener, narrow-leaf heimia, abrecaminos
- **Related species:** Heimia montana (also used as sinicuichi in some
  regions), Heimia salicifolia var. microphylla (smaller-leaved form)
The genus Heimia was named in honor of Ernst Ludwig Heim (1747–1834), a
German physician and botanist. The specific epithet salicifolia refers to the
willow-like (Salix-like) shape of its narrow leaves.
## Description
### Growth Habit and Morphology
Heimia salicifolia is a deciduous or semi-evergreen shrub reaching 1–3 meters
in height under cultivation, occasionally taller in ideal tropical conditions.
The plant has an upright, branching habit with multiple stems arising from a
woody base. Young stems are green and slightly angular, becoming woody and
brown with age. The bark of mature stems is rough and fissured, shedding in
thin strips on older wood.
### Leaves
Leaves are simple, opposite or sub-opposite, lanceolate to narrowly elliptic,
3–8 cm long and 0.5–2 cm wide, with entire margins and a pronounced drip tip.
The upper surface is dark green and slightly glossy, while the lower surface is
paler with a finely reticulate venation pattern visible under magnification.
Petioles are short, 2–5 mm. When dried, leaves turn a characteristic
yellowish-brown and retain a slightly astringent aroma.
### Flowers
Flowers are produced in leaf axils, solitary or in small clusters of 2–3.
Each flower is 2–3 cm in diameter with six yellow petals that are slightly
crumpled in appearance, resembling small wild roses. The petals are obovate
and slightly unequal in size. Flowers bloom primarily in summer and persist
for several days. The calyx is campanulate with six lobes, persistent after
petal drop.
### Fruit and Seed
The fruit is a small, globose to ovoid capsule 5–8 mm in diameter, green when
immature, turning brown and woody at maturity. Each capsule contains numerous
small seeds (100–200 per capsule), which are reddish-brown, reniform, and
approximately 1 mm long. Seeds are dispersed by wind and water, and
germination rates improve with cold stratification.
## Distribution and Habitat
Heimia salicifolia has a broad neotropical distribution spanning Mexico,
Central America, the Caribbean, and South America through Argentina. It is
found from sea level to approximately 1500 meters in elevation.
The plant favors moist, well-drained soils in open or semi-shaded locations.
It is a pioneer species frequently found along roadsides, riverbanks, field
edges, and in recently disturbed ground. It tolerates periodic flooding and
is common in riparian zones. While preferring tropical and subtropical
climates, the plant can survive mild frosts and has been cultivated outdoors
in USDA hardiness zones 8–11.
## Chemistry and Active Compounds
The psychoactive properties of Heimia salicifolia are attributed to several
quinolizidine alkaloids found in the leaves and stems:
- **Cryogenine (vertine):** The principal alkaloid, comprising approximately
  0.05–0.5% of dry leaf weight. It is a quinolizidine alkaloid with mild
  sedative and muscle relaxant properties, considered the primary contributor
  to the auditory and visual effects of sinicuichi.
- **Lythrine:** A second major alkaloid, structurally related to cryogenine,
  found at similar concentrations. Its pharmacological profile is less well
  characterized but may contribute to the overall psychoactive effect.
- **Lyfoline (dihydrovertine):** A reduced form of cryogenine present in
  smaller quantities; may be a metabolic product or degradation product of
  cryogenine during the traditional fermentation process.
- **Heimidine:** A minor alkaloid isolated from the leaves, with limited
  pharmacological investigation.
- **3-Methoxyvertine:** A methylated derivative of cryogenine found in
  trace amounts.
Total alkaloid content varies significantly based on growing conditions,
harvest time, and preparation method. Traditional fermentation of the leaves
is reported to alter the alkaloid profile, potentially converting some
compounds into more active forms.
## Traditional and Ethnobotanical Use
Heimia salicifolia holds an important place in Mesoamerican ethnopharmacology:
- **Sinicuichi preparation:** Traditional use involves gathering fresh or
  dried leaves, crushing them, soaking them in water, and allowing the
  mixture to ferment in the sun for several hours to overnight. The
  resulting yellowish liquid is consumed as a tea.
- **Reported effects:** Users describe a state of tranquil intoxication
  characterized by auditory distortions — particularly a sense of hearing
  voices or conversations from the past, a yellowing or dimming of vision,
  drowsiness, and a nostalgic or dreamy mental state.
- **Ceremonial context:** Among indigenous communities in central Mexico,
  sinicuichi was used by shamans to revisit past events, communicate with
  ancestors, and receive divinatory messages. It was considered a tool for
  "opening the memory" rather than a purely recreational intoxicant.
- **Medicinal uses:** The plant has been employed traditionally as an
  anti-inflammatory, diuretic, and treatment for digestive complaints,
  fevers, and respiratory ailments.
## Cultivation Notes
- Seeds should be sown on the surface of a well-draining potting mix and
  kept consistently moist. Germination typically occurs within 2–4 weeks at
  temperatures of 20–25°C. Cold stratification at 4°C for 2–4 weeks prior
  to sowing can improve germination rates.
- Softwood cuttings root readily in a humid environment with bottom heat.
- The plant prefers full sun to partial shade and regular watering during the
  growing season. It is moderately drought-tolerant once established.
- In temperate climates, the plant may die back to the root crown in winter
  and resprout in spring. Container cultivation allows overwintering indoors.
- Plants begin flowering in their second year from seed. Leaves can be
  harvested throughout the growing season.
## Pharmacology and Research
Modern pharmacological research on Heimia salicifolia is limited but has
identified several notable properties:
- Cryogenine demonstrates skeletal muscle relaxant activity in animal models,
  approximately one-tenth the potency of meprobamate. It does not appear to
  bind to benzodiazepine or GABA-A receptor sites.
- Anti-inflammatory activity has been demonstrated in rodent models of
  carrageenan-induced paw edema, supporting traditional medicinal uses.
- Preliminary antibacterial screening has shown activity against several
  Gram-positive organisms, including Staphylococcus aureus.
- No significant toxicity has been reported at traditional doses. High doses
  in animal models produce sedation, ataxia, and mild hypothermia but no
  mortality at doses up to 500 mg/kg of crude extract.
- The auditory effects remain unexplained by modern neuroscience. Hypotheses
  include mild GABAergic modulation affecting auditory processing pathways
  or interaction with serotonergic systems.
## Legal Status
Heimia salicifolia is not scheduled or regulated in most countries. The plant,
its seeds, and dried leaves are generally legal to possess, cultivate, and
trade. The active alkaloids (cryogenine, lythrine) are not specifically
scheduled under international drug conventions.
