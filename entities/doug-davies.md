# Doug Davies

## Description

Doug Davies is an agricultural practitioner cited by Mollison (as a personal communication) regarding optimal spacing and management of tagasaste (*Chamaecytisus palmensis*) for different production purposes. His recommendations distinguish between spacing regimes for cut forage versus seed and honey production, providing practical guidance for farmers establishing tagasaste as a dryland forage crop.

## Classification

- **Type**: Person — agricultural practitioner and horticulturist
- **Field**: Tagasaste cultivation, dryland forage management
- **Communication**: Cited as personal communication (pers. comm.) by Mollison

## Key Facts

- Recommended 1.0 to 1.5 m spacing for cut forage production, with stems kept cut at 1 m height
- Recommended 4-5 m spacing for seed and bee forage production, which produces better forage per plant
- His spacing guidance enables farmers to select establishment patterns based on their primary production goal
- Practical experience informed by hands-on management of tagasaste stands

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

Davies' recommendations are critical for anyone establishing tagasaste as a forage system:

1. **Cut forage regime (1.0-1.5 m spacing)**: Dense planting maximizes leaf production per hectare. Regular cutting at 1 m height maintains the plants in a productive coppiced state, producing succulent regrowth accessible to livestock or suitable for cut-and-feed systems. This spacing produces approximately 8,000-10,000 plants per hectare.

2. **Seed and honey regime (4-5 m spacing)**: Wider spacing allows plants to develop mature canopy and flower prolifically. Each plant produces more total forage, seed for poultry, and nectar for honeybees. At 4-5 m spacing, this represents approximately 400-625 plants per hectare — a much lower establishment cost with diversified yields.

3. **Dual-purpose transition**: The guidance suggests that stands could potentially be managed for both purposes by establishing at wider spacing initially for seed and honey income, then infilling or allowing natural seeding to achieve denser stands for cut forage as the system matures.

## Practical Application

Davies' spacing recommendations pair with Matthew Carpenter's field-drilling methodology and the seed-shaker tray system for seed collection, providing a complete establishment protocol for tagasaste in dryland temperate regions.
