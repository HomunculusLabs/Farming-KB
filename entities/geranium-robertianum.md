## Description

**Geranium robertianum**, commonly known as herb-Robert, is a species of flowering plant in the family Geraniaceae. It has been studied as a model organism for understanding how collembolan grazing on [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi]] affects plant growth.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Clade**: Angiosperms
- **Clade**: Eudicots
- **Order**: Geraniales
- **Family**: Geraniaceae
- **Genus**: Geranium
- **Species**: G. robertianum

## Key Facts

- Harris and Boerner (1990) studied collembolan grazing effects on its mycorrhizal associations and plant growth
- Growth was maximal at low collembolan densities, compared to either high densities or absence of [[collembola]]
- Intensity of mycorrhizal colonization was inversely related to collembolan density
- Plants with higher root colonization had the best growth
- Mycorrhizal benefit may have been through nutrients other than phosphorus

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

G. robertianum serves as a model for understanding the three-way interaction between plants, arbuscular [[mycorrhizal-fungi]], and soil [[microarthropods]]. The finding that intermediate collembolan densities optimize plant growth has practical implications for cultivation systems where soil fauna management is important. The observation that mycorrhizal benefits may operate through nutrients other than phosphorus challenges the conventional focus on P nutrition in mycorrhizal research.

## Ecological Role

G. robertianum is a common woodland plant in temperate regions, often found in shaded, nutrient-poor soils where mycorrhizal associations are particularly important. Its interaction with collembola and mycorrhizal fungi exemplifies complex multitrophic interactions in soil ecosystems.
