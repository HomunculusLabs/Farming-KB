# Eucalyptus obliqua

## Description

Eucalyptus obliqua, commonly known as **messmate stringybark** or simply **messmate**, is a tall to very tall tree widespread across southeastern Australia. It is one of the most commercially important hardwood species in Australia, particularly in Victoria and Tasmania. The species produces moderately durable timber and is a major source of pulpwood and sawlogs.

## Classification

- **Family:** Myrtaceae
- **Genus:** Eucalyptus
- **Species:** E. obliqua
- **Common names:** Messmate, messmate stringybark, brown top, stringybark

## Key Facts

- Tall to very tall tree, typically 30–60 m in height, occasionally exceeding 80 m
- Rough, stringy bark persisting to the smaller branches
- Widespread across southeastern Australia — Victoria, Tasmania, NSW, South Australia, and southern Queensland
- One of the most abundant eucalypt species in temperate forests
- Timber is pale brown, moderately durable, and widely used for construction and pulp
- Prefers moist, well-drained soils in high-rainfall areas
- Important component of wet and dry sclerophyll forests

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

- **Timber:** Major hardwood for sawlogs, construction timber, flooring, and joinery (pages 20, 95, 117)
- **Pulpwood:** Significant source of woodchips and pulp for paper production
- **Firewood:** Good quality firewood (pages 20, 95)
- **Honey:** Produces nectar for [[faires-beekeeping-and-honey-production]]
- **Shelter and windbreaks:** Tall growth suits it for large-scale shelter plantings in temperate areas
- **[[gadd-fungal-carbon-sequestration]]:** Large biomass potential for [[fungal-peat-decomposition-and-carbon-storage]]

## Cultivation Notes

- Prefers moist, well-drained loamy or gravelly soils
- Requires moderate to high rainfall (600–1500 mm annually)
- Fast-growing in suitable conditions
- Moderately frost-tolerant
- Can regenerate after wildfire through epicormic growth and seed
- Best propagated from seed
