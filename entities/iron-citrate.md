## Iron Citrate

## Description
Iron citrate is a chelated form of iron where citric acid binds to iron ions, improving the solubility and bioavailability of iron in soil and foliar applications. It is used as a micronutrient supplement in agriculture and horticulture to address iron deficiency (chlorosis) in plants. In the context of Elaine Ingham's compost tea methodology, iron citrate is mentioned as a foliar nutrient additive that can be combined with compost tea applications. The citrate chelation keeps iron in a plant-available form even under alkaline soil conditions where inorganic iron rapidly becomes unavailable.

## Classification
- **Chemical type**: Chelated micronutrient complex
- **Active ingredient**: Iron (Fe) chelated with citric acid
- **Common forms**: Ferric citrate (Fe(III) citrate), ferrous citrate (Fe(II) citrate)
- **Solubility**: Highly water-soluble compared to inorganic iron salts
- **Application**: Foliar spray, soil drench, hydroponic nutrient solution

## Key Facts

### Role in Plant Nutrition
- Iron is essential for chlorophyll synthesis — deficiency causes interveinal chlorosis (yellowing between leaf veins while veins remain green)
- Required for electron transport in photosynthesis and respiration
- Involved in nitrogen fixation and hormone synthesis
- Iron is immobile in plants — deficiency symptoms appear first on new growth
- Chelation with citrate prevents iron from precipitating as insoluble oxides or hydroxides in soil

### Chelation Chemistry
- Citric acid forms stable complexes with iron ions, keeping them in solution
- Chelated iron remains available at higher pH levels where inorganic iron becomes unavailable
- Particularly useful in alkaline or calcareous soils where iron availability is limited
- The citrate ligand is biodegradable, making it environmentally friendly compared to synthetic chelates like EDTA
- Natural chelation mimics biological processes — soil microbes produce organic acids that chelate minerals naturally

### Use in Compost Tea Programs
- Elaine Ingham lists iron citrate as an additive for foliar nutrient applications alongside AACT
- Can be tank-mixed with compost tea to provide both beneficial organisms and micronutrients
- Supports plant health by addressing iron deficiency that can weaken plants and make them susceptible to disease
- Foliar application of iron citrate provides rapid correction of deficiency symptoms
- Compatible with biological approaches — does not harm beneficial organisms at appropriate rates

### Application Considerations
- Foliar application: rapid uptake through leaf surfaces, provides quick correction
- Soil application: improves iron availability in the root zone, longer-lasting effect
- Rate varies by crop and severity of deficiency
- Avoid application during hot, sunny conditions to prevent leaf burn
- Can be combined with other micronutrients in a balanced foliar program
- Monitor soil pH — high pH (>7.0) reduces iron availability regardless of application method

### Biological Interactions
- Soil bacteria and fungi play a role in iron cycling — siderophore-producing microbes enhance iron availability
- Mycorrhizal fungi can improve iron uptake by extending the root absorption zone
- Healthy soil biology (as promoted by AACT) improves natural iron cycling and availability
- Adding iron citrate to compost tea may support both the organisms and the plants simultaneously

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology
- **Micronutrient management**: Iron deficiency is one of the most common micronutrient deficiencies in crops worldwide
- **Compost tea integration**: Can be used as part of a comprehensive foliar spray program with AACT
- **Soil biology connection**: Healthy soil food webs improve natural iron availability through microbial siderophore production
- **Chelation principles**: Understanding iron chelation is fundamental to plant nutrition in biological farming systems
- **Sustainable agriculture**: Biodegradable chelate (citrate) aligns with organic and biological farming principles
- **Mushroom cultivation**: Iron is a cofactor for many fungal enzymes involved in substrate decomposition
