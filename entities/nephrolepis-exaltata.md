## Overview

**Nephrolepis exaltata**, commonly known as the **Boston fern** or **sword fern**, is a tropical evergreen fern in the family **Nephrolepidaceae**. It is among the most popular and widely cultivated indoor plants in the world, prized for its graceful, arching fronds.

The species is native to tropical and subtropical regions of the Americas, Africa, and Polynesia. The **'Bostoniensis' cultivar**, discovered in Boston in 1894, launched the Boston fern as a household plant and remains the standard form sold in the horticultural trade.

Boston fern is recognized for its **air-purifying properties**, having been shown in NASA studies to effectively remove **formaldehyde, xylene, and toluene** from indoor air. It is listed among the top plants for improving indoor air quality.

## Ecological Role

In its native tropical habitats, *Nephrolepis exaltata* contributes to **epiphytic community diversity** on forest trees. Its dense frond masses provide microhabitat for invertebrates including mites, springtails, and small insects.

The species serves as a **reservoir for moisture** [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi-in-tropical-forest-restoration]] canopies. Its water-retentive frond mats trap rainfall and dew, creating damp microenvironments that support other epiphytic organisms including mosses, liverworts, and algae.

In Florida's native ecosystems, Boston fern provides **cover and foraging habitat** for small birds, tree frogs, and anoles. The dense root masses in tree crotches collect organic debris, contributing to canopy soil development.
