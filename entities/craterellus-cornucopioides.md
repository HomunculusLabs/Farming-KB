# Craterellus cornucopioides (Black Trumpet)

## Overview

**Craterellus cornucopioides**, commonly known as the black trumpet, horn of plenty, or trumpet of the dead, is an edible ectomycorrhizal basidiomycete mushroom in the family Cantharellaceae. Despite its unassuming dark appearance — resembling a small, brittle black funnel or fallen leaf — it is one of the most highly prized wild mushrooms in European and North American cuisine. Its rich, smoky, and fruity flavor profile makes it a favorite among chefs and foragers, and it is considered one of the finest mushrooms for drying, as the flavor intensifies considerably.

- **Asia:** Reported in Japan, Korea, and parts of China
- **Africa:** Rare but documented in North Africa (Morocco, Algeria)

The species is an obligate ectomycorrhizal associate, forming [[query-what-are-the-best-mushrooms-for-beginners-to-grow]] for drying — the flavor concentrates dramatically. Dried specimens can be ground into a powerful umami seasoning powder.
- **Pairings:** Complements cream, butter, garlic, thyme, poultry, game meats, and egg dishes particularly well.
