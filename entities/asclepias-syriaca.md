# Common Milkweed (*Asclepias syriaca* L.)

## Overview

*Asclepias syriaca* (common milkweed) is a perennial herbaceous plant native to
eastern and central [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]]. It is the primary larval host plant for the
monarch butterfly (*Danaus plexippus*) and produces cardenolide glycosides that
make both the plant and its insect feeders toxic to most vertebrate predators.

Beyond its ecological role, milkweed has historical uses as a fiber crop,
rubber source, food (with proper preparation), and medicinal plant. In

- ⚠️ **Invasive tendency:** Spreads aggressively via rhizomes; contain with
  root barriers
- Best in dedicated pollinator strips, hedgerows, and marginal areas
- Compatible with native grasses in meadow plantings

## Traditional and Medicinal Uses

- **Indigenous use:** Root poultices for wounds and [[cannabis-for-skin-conditions-detailed]]; leaf
  tea for digestive complaints (after thorough cooking to denature
  cardenolides)
- **Historical European use:** Root tea as an expectorant and diaphoretic
  for respiratory infections
- **Safety:** Internal use is NOT recommended due to cardiac glycoside
  toxicity; can cause nausea, vomiting, cardiac arrhythmias
