     7|     7|# W.Y. Evans-Wentz
     8|     8|
     9|     9|## Description
    10|    10|
    11|    11|Walter Yeeling Evans-Wentz (1878–1965) was an American anthropologist, writer, and pioneering scholar of Tibetan Buddhism. He is best known as the editor and publisher of "The Tibetan Book of the Dead" (1927), based on the translation by [[lama-kazi-dawa-samdup]]. This work became the foundational text through which Western readers — including [[timothy-leary]], [[r-c-zaehner]], and Aldous Huxley — first encountered Tibetan Buddhist teachings on consciousness, death, and the intermediate state.

    20|    20|## Key Facts
    21|    21|
    22|    22|- Born in Trenton, New Jersey; educated at Stanford University and Oxford
    23|    23|- Traveled extensively in India, Sikkim, and Tibet in the early 20th century
    24|    24|- Met Lama Kazi Dawa-Samdup in Gangtok, Sikkim, and collaborated on the English translation of the Bardo Thodol
    25|    25|- Published "The Tibetan Book of the Dead" in 1927 as part of his Oxford University Press series on Eastern spiritual texts
    26|    26|- Also published "Tibetan Yoga and Secret Doctrines" and "The Great Tibetan Yogin Milarepa"
    27|    27|- His editions included extensive introductions, notes, and commentaries contextualizing Tibetan practices for Western readers
    28|    28|- His editorial framing was influenced by Theosophical ideas and comparative religion

    30|    30|## Role in the Psychedelic Tradition
    31|    31|
    32|    32|Evans-Wentz's edition of the Tibetan Book of the Dead was the direct source text for Leary, Metzner, and Alpert's "The Psychedelic Experience" (1964):
    33|    33|
    34|    34|- Leary explicitly modeled his psychedelic session guide on the structure of Evans-Wentz's Bardo Thodol
    35|    35|- The three Bardo phases (Chikhai, Chonyid, Sidpa) were directly adapted as First, Second, and Third Bardos
    36|    36|- Evans-Wentz's commentary tradition was built upon (and sometimes challenged) by Leary's psychological reinterpretation
    37|    37|- The Evans-Wentz edition included Carl Jung's "Psychological Commentary," which Leary also engaged with
    38|    38|
    39|    39|## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology
    40|    40|
    41|    41|Evans-Wentz's editorial work is historically significant as the bridge between Tibetan Buddhist consciousness teachings and the Western psychedelic movement:
    42|    42|
    43|    43|- Provided the textual framework that Leary adapted for guiding psychedelic sessions with LSD, psilocybin, and mescaline
    44|    44|- His publication made Tibetan meditation and consciousness-mapping techniques available to researchers studying entheogenic plants and fungi
    45|    45|- The conceptual vocabulary of bardos, clear light, and ego-loss that he helped introduce became standard in psychedelic discourse
    46|    46|- Influenced how researchers and practitioners understand and frame the subjective effects of psilocybin mushrooms and other entheogens
