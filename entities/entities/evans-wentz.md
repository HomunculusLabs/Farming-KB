# W.Y. Evans-Wentz

## Description

Walter Yeeling Evans-Wentz (1878–1965) was an American anthropologist, writer, and pioneering scholar of Tibetan Buddhism. He is best known as the editor and publisher of "The Tibetan Book of the Dead" (1927), based on the translation by Lama Kazi Dawa-Samdup. This work became the foundational text through which Western readers — including Timothy Leary, R.C. Zaehner, and Aldous Huxley — first encountered Tibetan Buddhist teachings on consciousness, death, and the intermediate state.

## Classification

- **Category**: Person — Anthropologist / Editor / Religious Scholar
- **Tradition**: Theosophy / Tibetan Buddhist Studies
- **Era**: Late 19th – mid 20th century
- **Nationality**: American

## Key Facts

- Born in Trenton, New Jersey; educated at Stanford University and Oxford
- Traveled extensively in India, Sikkim, and Tibet in the early 20th century
- Met Lama Kazi Dawa-Samdup in Gangtok, Sikkim, and collaborated on the English translation of the Bardo Thodol
- Published "The Tibetan Book of the Dead" in 1927 as part of his Oxford University Press series on Eastern spiritual texts
- Also published "Tibetan Yoga and Secret Doctrines" and "The Great Tibetan Yogin Milarepa"
- His editions included extensive introductions, notes, and commentaries contextualizing Tibetan practices for Western readers
- His editorial framing was influenced by Theosophical ideas and comparative religion

## Role in the Psychedelic Tradition

Evans-Wentz's edition of the Tibetan Book of the Dead was the direct source text for Leary, Metzner, and Alpert's "The Psychedelic Experience" (1964):

- Leary explicitly modeled his psychedelic session guide on the structure of Evans-Wentz's Bardo Thodol
- The three Bardo phases (Chikhai, Chonyid, Sidpa) were directly adapted as First, Second, and Third Bardos
- Evans-Wentz's commentary tradition was built upon (and sometimes challenged) by Leary's psychological reinterpretation
- The Evans-Wentz edition included Carl Jung's "Psychological Commentary," which Leary also engaged with

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Evans-Wentz's editorial work is historically significant as the bridge between Tibetan Buddhist consciousness teachings and the Western psychedelic movement:

- Provided the textual framework that Leary adapted for guiding psychedelic sessions with LSD, psilocybin, and mescaline
- His publication made Tibetan meditation and consciousness-mapping techniques available to researchers studying entheogenic plants and fungi
- The conceptual vocabulary of bardos, clear light, and ego-loss that he helped introduce became standard in psychedelic discourse
- Influenced how researchers and practitioners understand and frame the subjective effects of psilocybin mushrooms and other entheogens
