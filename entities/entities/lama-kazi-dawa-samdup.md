# Lama Kazi Dawa-Samdup

## Description

Lama Kazi Dawa-Samdup (1868–1922) was a Tibetan Buddhist scholar and translator who produced the first English translation of the [[bardo-thodol]] (commonly known as "The Tibetan Book of the Dead"). His translation, edited and published by W.Y. [[evans-wentz]] in 1927, became the foundational text through which Western audiences — including [[timothy-leary]] — encountered Tibetan Buddhist teachings on consciousness, death, and the intermediate state (bardo).

## Classification

- **Category**: Person — Translator / Lama / Scholar
- **Tradition**: Tibetan Buddhism
- **Era**: Late 19th – early 20th century
- **Nationality**: Sikkimese (of Tibetan descent)

## Key Facts

- Served as headmaster of the Bhutia Boarding School in Gangtok, Sikkim
- Produced the landmark English translation of the Bardo Thodol, which Evans-Wentz edited and published
- His translation became the basis for virtually all subsequent Western engagement with Tibetan death and consciousness teachings
- Held that the Adi-Buddha and all Dharma-Kaya deities are not personal deities but personifications of primordial and universal forces, laws, or spiritual influences
- Died in 1922 before his translation was published; Evans-Wentz brought it to publication in 1927

## Dawa-Samdup's Key Philosophical Positions

As cited in Leary's "The Psychedelic Experience," Dawa-Samdup held that:

- The Adi-Buddha and all deities associated with the Dharma-Kaya are personifications of primordial and universal forces, not personal gods
- "In the boundless panorama of the existing and visible universe, whatever shapes appear, whatever sounds vibrate, whatever radiances illuminate, or whatever consciousnesses cognize, all are the play of manifestation in the Tri-Kaya"
- The Tri-Kaya (Three-fold Principle) is the Cause of All Causes, the Primordial Trinity
- "Impenetrating all, is the All-Pervading Essence of Spirit, which is Mind. It is uncreated, impersonal, self-existing, immaterial and indestructible"
- The Tri-Kaya corresponds esoterically to the exoteric trinity of Buddha, the Scriptures, and the Priesthood (or in Leary's psychedelic adaptation: your own divinity, this manual, and your companions)

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Dawa-Samdup's translation is the single most important textual source for Leary's "The Psychedelic Experience," which directly maps Tibetan bardo teachings onto psychedelic sessions. His work is therefore foundational to:

- The entire framework of using Tibetan Buddhist consciousness maps for understanding psychedelic experiences
- The concept of ego-loss as analogous to the death-and-rebirth process described in the Bardo Thodol
- Psychological models for understanding the stages of psychedelic sessions, including those involving psilocybin mushrooms and other entheogenic substances
- The interpretive tradition that sees visionary experiences as projections of internal mental states
