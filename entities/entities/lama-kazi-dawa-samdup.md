     7|     7|# Lama Kazi Dawa-Samdup
     8|     8|
     9|     9|## Description
    10|    10|
    11|    11|Lama Kazi Dawa-Samdup (1868–1922) was a Tibetan Buddhist scholar and translator who produced the first English translation of the [[bardo-thodol]] (commonly known as "The Tibetan Book of the Dead"). His translation, edited and published by W.Y. [[evans-wentz]] in 1927, became the foundational text through which Western audiences — including [[timothy-leary]] — encountered Tibetan Buddhist teachings on consciousness, death, and the intermediate state (bardo).

    20|    20|## Key Facts
    21|    21|
    22|    22|- Served as headmaster of the Bhutia Boarding School in Gangtok, Sikkim
    23|    23|- Produced the landmark English translation of the Bardo Thodol, which Evans-Wentz edited and published
    24|    24|- His translation became the basis for virtually all subsequent Western engagement with Tibetan death and consciousness teachings
    25|    25|- Held that the Adi-Buddha and all Dharma-Kaya deities are not personal deities but personifications of primordial and universal forces, laws, or spiritual influences
    26|    26|- Died in 1922 before his translation was published; Evans-Wentz brought it to publication in 1927
    27|    27|
    28|    28|## Dawa-Samdup's Key Philosophical Positions
    29|    29|
    30|    30|As cited in Leary's "The Psychedelic Experience," Dawa-Samdup held that:
    31|    31|
    32|    32|- The Adi-Buddha and all deities associated with the Dharma-Kaya are personifications of primordial and universal forces, not personal gods
    33|    33|- "In the boundless panorama of the existing and visible universe, whatever shapes appear, whatever sounds vibrate, whatever radiances illuminate, or whatever consciousnesses cognize, all are the play of manifestation in the Tri-Kaya"
    34|    34|- The Tri-Kaya (Three-fold Principle) is the Cause of All Causes, the Primordial Trinity
    35|    35|- "Impenetrating all, is the All-Pervading Essence of Spirit, which is Mind. It is uncreated, impersonal, self-existing, immaterial and indestructible"
    36|    36|- The Tri-Kaya corresponds esoterically to the exoteric trinity of Buddha, the Scriptures, and the Priesthood (or in Leary's psychedelic adaptation: your own divinity, this manual, and your companions)
    37|    37|
    38|    38|## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology
    39|    39|
    40|    40|Dawa-Samdup's translation is the single most important textual source for Leary's "The Psychedelic Experience," which directly maps Tibetan bardo teachings onto psychedelic sessions. His work is therefore foundational to:
    41|    41|
    42|    42|- The entire framework of using Tibetan Buddhist consciousness maps for understanding psychedelic experiences
    43|    43|- The concept of ego-loss as analogous to the death-and-rebirth process described in the Bardo Thodol
    44|    44|- Psychological models for understanding the stages of psychedelic sessions, including those involving psilocybin mushrooms and other entheogenic substances
    45|    45|- The interpretive tradition that sees visionary experiences as projections of internal mental states
