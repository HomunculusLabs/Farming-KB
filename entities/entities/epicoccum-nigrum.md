     7|     7|## Description
     8|     8|
     9|     9|Epicoccum nigrum (syn. Epicoccum purpurascens) is a ubiquitous saprotrophic fungus found on a wide range of plant substrates including living leaves, dead plant material, soil, and indoor environments. It is one of the most commonly encountered phylloplane fungi and is known for its rapid colonization of leaf surfaces and production of distinctive dark sporodochia (cushion-shaped spore masses). The species has attracted interest for its biocontrol potential against plant pathogens and its role as an opportunistic human pathogen in immunocompromised individuals.

    23|    23|## Key Facts
    24|    24|
    25|    25|- One of the most common phylloplane fungi found on the surfaces of living leaves across diverse plant species and ecosystems.
    26|    26|- Magan et al. (1995) observed that E. nigrum increased on Scots pine needles under ozone (O₃) exposure, suggesting ozone-induced damage to needle surfaces enhances colonization opportunities.
    27|    27|- Produces a wide range of secondary metabolites including antibiotics (flavipin, epicorazine) with activity against bacterial and fungal plant pathogens.
    28|    28|- Pieckenstain et al. (2001) demonstrated its efficacy as a biocontrol agent against Sclerotinia head rot of sunflower.
    29|    29|- Tolerant of a wide range of environmental conditions including temperature, humidity, and substrate type.
    30|    30|- Known allergen that can cause respiratory sensitization and, rarely, invasive infections in immunocompromised patients.

    32|    32|## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology
    33|    33|
    34|    34|Epicoccum nigrum is significant in both ecological research and applied mycology:
    35|    35|
    36|    36|1. **Biocontrol applications:** E. nigrum produces antifungal compounds effective against Sclerotinia, Botrytis, and other pathogens, making it a candidate for [[integrated-pest-management]] in agriculture.
    37|    37|2. **Pollution bioindicator:** Its increased abundance on ozone-exposed foliage makes it useful for monitoring atmospheric pollutant effects on plant surface microbial communities.
    38|    38|3. **Phylloplane ecology:** As a dominant leaf surface colonizer, E. nigrum competes with pathogenic fungi for space and resources, contributing to natural disease suppression on foliage.
    39|    39|4. **Substrate versatility:** The species grows on virtually any plant-derived substrate, making it easy to culture and study in laboratory settings.
    40|    40|5. **Allergen awareness:** Indoor environmental testing may detect Epicoccum spores, requiring interpretation in context — high counts may indicate moisture problems or water damage rather than specific health threats.
    41|    41|6. **Secondary metabolite research:** The diverse antibiotic compounds produced by E. nigrum are of interest for pharmaceutical and agricultural applications.
