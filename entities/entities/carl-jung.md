     7|     7|# Carl Jung
     8|     8|
     9|     9|## Description
    10|    10|
    11|    11|Carl Gustav Jung (1875–1961) was a Swiss psychiatrist and psychoanalyst who founded analytical psychology. His theories of the collective unconscious, archetypes, individuation, and psychological types profoundly influenced 20th-century psychology, religion, and the arts. Jung wrote the "Psychological Commentary" for [[evans-wentz]]'s edition of "The Tibetan Book of the Dead," which Leary directly references in "The Psychedelic Experience."

    20|    20|## Key Facts
    21|    21|
    22|    22|- Originally a close collaborator of [[sigmund-freud]] (1907–1913), later broke with Freud over theoretical differences
    23|    23|- Developed the concepts of the collective unconscious, archetypes, persona, shadow, anima/animus, and the Self
    24|    24|- Wrote extensively on Eastern philosophy, alchemy, and comparative religion
    25|    25|- His "Psychological Commentary" in Evans-Wentz's Tibetan Book of the Dead provided the psychoanalytic framework for interpreting Eastern consciousness teachings
    26|    26|- Suggested that Freud's theory of the unconscious was an attempt from below (from the instinctual) to explore the same territory that Tantric Lamaism approaches from above (from the transcendent)
    27|    27|
    28|    28|## Jung's Commentary Referenced by Leary
    29|    29|
    30|    30|In "The Psychedelic Experience," Leary cites Jung's observation that:
    31|    31|
    32|    32|- Freud's theory is the first Western attempt to investigate, "as if from below, from the animal sphere of instinct, the psychic territory that corresponds in Tantric Lamaism to the [[sidpa-bardo]]"
    33|    33|- The vision of seeing parents in sexual intercourse (primal scene) described in the Sidpa Bardo corresponds exactly to the psychoanalytic concept of the primal scene
    34|    34|- This convergence between Eastern and Western psychology demonstrates remarkable parallels in understanding unconscious processes
    35|    35|- The Sidpa Bardo visions map directly onto the psychoanalytic theory of the Oedipus Complex
    36|    36|
    37|    37|## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology
    38|    38|
    39|    39|Jung's analytical framework is relevant to understanding psychedelic experiences in several ways:
    40|    40|
    41|    41|- His archetype theory provides a framework for understanding the universal visionary content reported in psychedelic sessions, including those with psilocybin mushrooms
    42|    42|- The concept of the collective unconscious helps explain cross-cultural similarities in entheogenic visionary experiences
    43|    43|- His individuation process parallels the ego-death-and-reintegration model central to Leary's psychedelic framework
    44|    44|- The Jungian understanding of the shadow and its integration informs approaches to "bad trips" and challenging psychedelic experiences
    45|    45|- His commentary on the Bardo Thodol bridged Eastern and Western psychology in ways that remain influential in psychedelic-assisted therapy research
