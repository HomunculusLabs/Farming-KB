# Right Livelihood Award

## Description

The Right Livelihood Award is an international award established in 1980 by Swedish-German philanthropist Jakob von Uexkull to honor and support those offering practical and exemplary answers to the most urgent challenges facing the world today. Often referred to as the "Alternative Nobel Prize," it was created after the Nobel Foundation rejected Uexkull's proposal to establish two new Nobel Prizes in environment and development. [[bill-mollison]] received the award in 1981 for his work in environmental design through permaculture.

## Classification

- **Founder:** Jakob von Uexkull

## Key Facts

- Established in 1980 by Jakob von Uexkull, a Swedish-German philatelist and philanthropist.
- Created as an alternative to the Nobel Prize after the Nobel Foundation declined to add prizes for environment and development.
- Presented annually at a ceremony in the Swedish Parliament building in Stockholm, usually the day before the Nobel Prize ceremony.
- Awarded to individuals and organizations working in areas including environmental protection, human rights, sustainable development, health, and peace.
- Bill Mollison received the award in 1981 — one of the earliest recipients — for his pioneering work in [[permaculture-design]].
- The award brought international recognition to permaculture as a legitimate approach to sustainable land management.
- As of recent years, over 180 laureates from more than 70 countries have received the award.



## Significance for Permaculture

Mollison's receipt of the Right Livelihood Award in 1981 was a watershed moment for the permaculture movement:

- **Legitimization:** The award provided scientific and institutional credibility to permaculture at a critical early stage in its development.
- **Visibility:** International media attention brought permaculture concepts to a global audience.
- **Networking:** The award connected Mollison with a network of sustainability practitioners and thinkers worldwide.
- **Funding:** Prize money supported further development and dissemination of permaculture education.
- **Timing:** The 1981 award came just as the first cohort of 26 permaculture design course students was establishing practice, lending momentum to the movement's expansion.



## The Award's Ethos and Permaculture

The Right Livelihood Award's philosophy aligns closely with [[permaculture-ethics]]:

1. **Care of the Earth:** The award recognizes environmental stewardship — permaculture's first ethic.
2. **Care of People:** Many laureates work on social justice and human well-being.
3. **Setting Limits:** The award acknowledges work addressing overconsumption and sustainability.

The award itself exemplifies "right livelihood" — a Buddhist concept of earning one's living in an ethical manner that does not harm others. This principle mirrors permaculture's emphasis on self-reliance, ethical design, and working with rather than exploiting natural systems.



## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- The award's recognition of ecological design validates integrated cultivation approaches over industrial monoculture.
- Right Livelihood laureates have included numerous practitioners of sustainable agriculture and ecological land management.
- The award highlights the importance of recognizing and supporting alternative approaches to food production and land stewardship.
- Permaculture's award-endorsed framework provides a comprehensive design system applicable to cultivation and mycological operations.
