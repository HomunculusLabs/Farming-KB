## Joseph Fels

## Description

Joseph Fels (1853–1914) was a wealthy American businessman, best known as the manufacturer of **Fels-Naptha soap**, who became a passionate advocate for small farming and land access reform. In the early [[coleman-louis-savier-twentieth-century-maraicher]], he invested his personal fortune in establishing a practical demonstration of [[french-intensive-market-gardening]] in England, purchasing 600 acres at **Mayland, Essex** to be subdivided into numerous mini-farms for intensive French-style vegetable production.

## Classification

- **Type:** Person — Businessman, philanthropist, agricultural reformer

## Key Facts

- Made his fortune through **Fels-Naptha soap**, a well-known household cleaning product
- Believed firmly in the benefits of **small farming** and easier access for people to agricultural land
- Purchased **600 acres at Mayland, Essex, England** specifically to be divided into mini-farms for intensive French-style vegetable production
- His project aimed to demonstrate that unemployed factory workers could become **self-supporting on the land** through intensive cultivation
- His foreman, **Joseph Smith**, wrote *French Gardening*, one of the best practical instruction books on the French intensive system, with an introduction by Prince [[peter-kropotkin]]
- The timing aligned with widespread early-20th-century interest in helping unemployed factory workers return to agricultural self-sufficiency
- The French gardening movement in England that Fels helped finance was accompanied by sensational claims, with books titled *Gold Producing Soil* creating unrealistic economic expectations
- Many French gardens in England ultimately **failed through incompetence** or did not meet economic expectations, partly due to hype obscuring the actual skill and labor requirements

## Relevance to Cultivation

Fels represents an important chapter in the **transmission [[coleman-british-adoption-of-french-intensive-gardening]] market gardening techniques** to the English-speaking world. His investment demonstrated both the potential and the pitfalls of trying to transfer a highly skilled agricultural system to a new context. The failure of many of his projects illustrates that intensive market gardening requires deep expertise and enormous labor commitment — lessons that remain relevant today. Despite the mixed results, the books and documentation generated by his project (particularly Joseph Smith's *French Gardening*) preserved practical knowledge that would later inform the modern small-farm revival.
