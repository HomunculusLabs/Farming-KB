# Salvinorin B

**Salvinorin B** is a neoclerodane diterpenoid compound found in the
leaves of *Salvia divinorum* (Epling & Játiva), a psychoactive mint
family plant endemic to the Sierra Mazateca region of Oaxaca, Mexico.

It is the deacetylated metabolite of salvinorin A, the primary
psychoactive component of *S. divinorum*. While salvinorin A is one
of the most potent naturally occurring psychedelics known (active at
200-500 µg), salvinorin B is pharmacologically distinct, showing
markedly reduced affinity for the kappa-opioid receptor.

Salvinorin B is of significant pharmacological interest as a
structure-activity relationship (SAR) reference compound for
understanding the neoclerodane pharmacophore.

## Chemical Properties

- **Chemical class:** Neoclerodane trans-clerodane diterpenoid
- **Molecular formula:** C₂₀H₂₈O₆
- **Molecular weight:** 364.44 g/mol
- **IUPAC name:** (2S,4aR,6aR,7R,9S,10aS,10bR)-9-(acetyloxy)-
  2-(furan-3-yl)-6a,10b-dimethyl-4,10-dioxododecahydro-1H-
  benzo[f]isochromene-7-carboxylic acid
- **Appearance:** White crystalline solid
- **Solubility:** Soluble in acetone, ethyl acetate, chloroform;
  poorly soluble in water

Salvinorin B differs from salvinorin A by the absence of the C-2
acetyl group, bearing a hydroxyl instead. This single modification
dramatically reduces kappa-opioid receptor affinity.

## Pharmacology

Salvinorin B acts as a weak partial agonist at the kappa-opioid
receptor (KOR), the same receptor target as salvinorin A, but with
substantially lower potency and efficacy.

- **KOR binding affinity (Ki):** Approximately 1,000-5,000 nM,
  compared to ~1-10 nM for salvinorin A — representing a 100-5,000
  fold reduction in affinity
- **Efficacy:** Low partial agonist; does not produce full receptor
  activation even at saturating concentrations
- **Selectivity:** Retains KOR selectivity over mu- and delta-opioid
  receptors, consistent with the neoclerodane scaffold

The dramatic loss of activity upon removal of the C-2 acetyl group
demonstrates that this moiety is critical for high-affinity KOR
binding in the salvinorin pharmacophore.

## Biosynthesis

Salvinorin B is a natural intermediate in the biosynthetic pathway
to salvinorin A in *Salvia divinorum*. It is formed via oxidation
and cyclization of clerodane precursor diterpenes, and subsequently
acetylated at C-2 by a specific acetyltransferase to produce
salvinorin A.

It can also be produced by hydrolysis (deacetylation) of salvinorin
A, either chemically (base hydrolysis) or enzymatically. This
conversion is one of the primary metabolic pathways of salvinorin A
in vivo.

## Effects

Salvinorin B produces minimal psychoactive effects at doses where
salvinorin A is profoundly active. When isolated and administered,
it does not produce the characteristic visionary, dissociative
experience associated with salvinorin A.

- **Psychoactive threshold:** Not reliably established; likely
  inactive at doses under 10 mg (vs. 200-500 µg for salvinorin A)
- **Subjective effects:** Mild relaxation reported at very high
  doses; no visionary or dissociative effects
- **Duration:** Not well characterized due to low potency

The compound's primary significance is as a negative control and
SAR reference rather than as a pharmacologically active substance.

## Role in Salvinorin A Metabolism

Salvinorin B is a major metabolite of salvinorin A in humans.
After administration of salvinorin A (smoked or sublingual), the
C-2 acetyl group is rapidly cleaved by esterases in blood and
tissues, producing salvinorin B as the primary circulating
metabolite.

The SAR data demonstrate that C-2 substitution is the dominant
determinant of KOR affinity in the salvinorin series.

## Legal Status

Salvinorin B is not specifically scheduled in most jurisdictions.
Its legal status generally follows that of *Salvia divinorum* and
salvinorin A, which are controlled in many countries including
Australia, several EU member states, and numerous US states.

In jurisdictions where *S. divinorum* is unscheduled, salvinorin
B exists in a legal grey area as an unregulated natural compound
with negligible psychoactivity.
