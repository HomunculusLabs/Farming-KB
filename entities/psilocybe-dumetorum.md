# Psilocybe dumetorum

**Psilocybe dumetorum** is a tropical [[guzman-allen-gartz-psilocybe-section-relationships-trans-pacific-disjunctions]] psilocybe-sect.-psilocybe, the section containing the bluing, psychoactive species. The taxonomic status of *P. dumetorum* has been discussed in the context of Southeast Asian Psilocybe diversity, where several species show overlapping morphological characteristics.
Corner initially described the species in the context of his broader work on Malaysian agarics, a monumental multi-volume series documenting the fungal diversity of the region. The type specimen was collected from a forested area in Java and deposited at the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew herbarium. Subsequent authors, including Guzmán in his world monograph of Psilocybe, have examined the type material and corroborated Corner's original observations. No molecular phylogenetic studies have yet specifically sequenced *P. dumetorum*, leaving its precise evolutionary relationships within the genus unresolved.

## Description

Basidiocarps are small. The pileus is convex to plano-convex, 8 to 20 mm in diameter, hygrophanous, and brownish to dark reddish brown when moist, becoming much paler, often yellowish tan, upon drying. The pileus surface is smooth and may be slightly viscid when wet. The margin is translucently striate when moist. The lamellae are adnate to adnexed, close to subdistant, grayish initially, maturing to dark purplish brown. The stipe is 25 to 50 mm long, 1 to 2 mm thick, central, equal, pallid to brownish, and develops noticeable blue bruising when damaged. A fibrillose partial veil may be present, occasionally leaving a slight annular zone. Spore prints are dark purplish brown. Basidiospores are ellipsoid to broadly ellipsoid, measuring approximately 9 to 12 µm by 6 to 7.5 µm, with moderately thick walls and a germ pore. Cheilocystidia are lageniform to cylindrical with a narrow or slightly clavate apex. Pleurocystidia are less common.
### Microscopic Features

Basidiospores are ellipsoid to slightly amygdaliform in face view, measuring 10 to 13 µm long by 6 to 8 µm wide, with moderately thickened walls and a distinct germ pore. The spore wall is smooth and brownish in deposit. In side view, the spores are inequilateral to broadly elliptical. The germ pore is apical and conspicuous in Melzer's reagent.
Cheilocystidia are abundant on the lamellar edges, ventricose to lageniform in morphology, measuring 15 to 25 µm in total length. They possess a narrow neck, typically 1.5 to 2.5 µm wide at the apex, and a slightly enlarged basal portion. The cheilocystidial walls are thin and hyaline. Pleurocystidia are sparse to absent on the lamellar faces, and when present they are similar in form to the cheilocystidia but generally shorter and less regularly shaped.
Basidia are 4-spored, clavate, measuring 18 to 25 µm long by 5 to 7 µm wide, arising from a dense subhymenial layer. Sterigmata are slender, approximately 2 to 3 µm long. Basidioles are frequent among the mature basidia, particularly in younger basidiocarps, and are similar in dimensions to the basidia but lack sterigmata.

## Distribution and Habitat

### Geographic Range

*Psilocybe dumetorum* is known from tropical Southeast Asia, with confirmed records from Java, Indonesia, and possibly other islands in the Malay Archipelago. Unverified reports and herbarium specimens suggest the species may also occur on Sumatra and Borneo, though these records require molecular confirmation given the complexity of the regional Psilocybe flora. The species was first and most thoroughly documented from western Java, where Corner's original collections were made. Its full range within the region remains poorly understood due to limited systematic surveying of psychoactive agarics across the archipelago.
The Malay Archipelago is one of the most biodiverse regions for fungi globally, yet its mycoflora remains significantly underdocumented. Many tropical Psilocybe species from this region are known from single or very few collections, and their true distributions may extend across multiple islands. The logistical challenges of fieldwork in remote tropical forests, combined with the ephemeral nature of fungal fruiting, have contributed to the patchiness of collection records.
### Ecology

The species is saprotrophic, growing on rich soil in humid tropical forests, forest margins, and disturbed areas with dense herbaceous vegetation. It is commonly found on soil enriched with decomposing plant material, often along trails or in clearings within primary and secondary forest. Fruiting occurs during the wet season months when humidity is consistently high, typically between November and March in Java's monsoonal climate. *P. dumetorum* is associated with decomposing litter and herbaceous debris rather than woody substrates, distinguishing it from some of its congeners that fruit directly on decaying wood or dung. The altitudinal range extends from lowland to lower montane forests, approximately from sea level to 1,200 meters.
The species has been observed fruiting gregariously or in scattered groups, with individual basidiocarps often emerging from bare soil between patches of leaf litter. The preference for exposed mineral soil enriched with organic matter suggests that small-scale soil disturbance, such as that caused by falling branches or animal activity, may create favorable microsites for fruiting. Temperature requirements appear to be consistently tropical, with no records from cooler high-elevation sites above 1,200 meters.
