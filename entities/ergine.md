# Ergine

Ergine, also known as d-lysergic acid amide (LSA) and N,N-diethyllysergamide, is a naturally occurring ergoline alkaloid found in several plant species and the ergot fungus Claviceps purpurea. It is a close structural analog of LSD (lysergic acid diethylamide), differing only by the substitution of two ethyl groups with two amide hydrogen atoms. First isolated from ergot in 1947 by Hofmann and coworkers, ergine has been used for millennia as an entheogenic substance through the consumption of seeds from plants in the Convolvulaceae (morning glory) family. Despite being approximately 10–30 times less potent than LSD by weight, ergine produces qualitatively similar psychedelic effects including visual distortions, altered cognition, and mild euphoria, though with a heavier sedative and somatic component.

Ergine is the simplest naturally occurring lysergamide, serving as the parent compound for a family of related ergoline alkaloids. The ergoline ring system consists of a tetracyclic structure: a benzene ring fused to an indole, with additional ring closures forming the characteristic bicyclic amine portion. This scaffold is shared across pharmaceutically important compounds including ergotamine, pergolide, and cabergoline.

The seeds of Argyreia nervosa (Hawaiian baby woodrose) contain by far the highest natural concentration of ergine, typically 5–10 times higher than morning glory species. This has made A. nervosa the most common botanical source for ergine-containing preparations.

## Pharmacology

| Property | Detail |
|---|---|
| Receptor activity | Partial agonist at 5-HT₂A receptors; also binds 5-HT₁A, 5-HT₂C, and dopamine D₂ receptors |
| Mechanism | Primarily 5-HT₂A receptor agonism, similar to LSD but with lower intrinsic activity |
| Potency | Active oral dose: 200–500 µg (threshold); 500–1500 µg (moderate); 1500–3000 µg (strong) |
| Duration | 4–8 hours (shorter than LSD's 8–12 hours) |
| Onset | 30–90 minutes after oral ingestion |
| Metabolism | Hepatic metabolism via CYP450 enzymes; N-deethylation and oxidation |

Ergine's pharmacological profile is qualitatively similar to LSD but quantitatively weaker across all measures. The 5-HT₂A partial agonism produces the characteristic psychedelic effects, while additional affinity for 5-HT₁A receptors may contribute to the anxiolytic and sedative components that distinguish ergine from LSD. The D₂ receptor affinity is shared with many ergot-derived pharmaceuticals and may contribute to the somatic and gastrointestinal effects commonly reported with ergine.

## Subjective Effects

- **Visual**: Mild to moderate visual enhancements
including color intensification, pattern recognition, geometric patterning with eyes closed, and mild trailing effects. Significantly less intense than LSD or psilocybin at equivalent subjective levels.
- **Cognitive**: Altered thought patterns,
increased introspection, enhanced appreciation of music and art, mild synesthesia at higher doses. Thought loops and confusion are less common than with LSD.
- **Somatic**: This is the primary distinguishing
feature — ergine produces notable physical effects including nausea, abdominal discomfort, lethargy, heaviness in the limbs, and a strong sedative/dreamy quality. Many users describe a "drunken" or "delirious" body feel at higher doses.
- **Emotional**: Mild euphoria, emotional
openness, and peacefulness. Anxiety and paranoia are less common than with LSD, but the sedative quality can feel unpleasant to some users.

## Historical and Ethnobotanical Context

Ergine's entheogenic use predates modern chemistry by centuries. The Aztec and Mazatec peoples of Mexico consumed seeds of Turbina corymbosa (ololiuqui) and Ipomoea species (tlitliltzin) as sacred medicines in divination and healing rituals. Spanish chroniclers including Francisco Hernández (1577) documented ololiuqui use among the Aztecs. Albert Hofmann's identification of ergine and ergonovine as the active principles of ololiuqui in 1960 resolved centuries of botanical uncertainty about the identity of the Aztec sacrament.

In the 1960s, morning glory seeds became popular as a legal psychedelic, leading to commercial seed suppliers coating seeds with toxic fungicides to discourage consumption. Hawaiian baby woodrose seeds subsequently became the preferred botanical source due to higher ergine concentrations requiring fewer seeds.

## Extraction and Preparation

Cold water extraction is the most common method for preparing ergine from plant seeds, as the alkaloids are water- soluble while many seed lipids and irritant compounds are not. A typical preparation involves crushing seeds (5–15 HBWR seeds or 100–300 morning glory seeds), soaking in cold water for several hours with periodic agitation, filtering the liquid, and consuming the filtrate directly. More refined extraction methods can reduce gastrointestinal distress from non-alkaloid seed constituents.

## Legal Status

| Jurisdiction | Status |
|---|---|
| United States | Unscheduled; some states restrict sale of ergine-containing seeds |
| United Kingdom | Controlled under the Psychoactive Substances Act 2016 |
| Germany | Controlled under BtMG (narcotics act) |
| Australia | Schedule 9 (prohibited substance) |
| Canada | Unscheduled; seeds available commercially |

The legal status of ergine is complex and varies by jurisdiction. In most countries, the pure compound is controlled or prohibited, but the botanical sources (seeds) remain legal to possess and sell as ornamental plant material. This creates a legal gray zone that has persisted for decades.

## Safety and Toxicology

- **Toxicity**: No documented human fatalities
from ergine alone. LD₅₀ in animal models is significantly higher than active doses. Chronic toxicity data is limited.
- **Nausea**: The most common adverse effect,
caused by non-alkaloid seed constituents. Ranges from mild discomfort to significant vomiting depending on preparation method and individual sensitivity.
- **Uterine stimulation**: Ergot-derived compounds
can stimulate uterine contractions. Ergine-containing seeds are contraindicated during pregnancy.
- **Psychological risks**: As with all
psychedelics, individuals with personal or family history of psychosis or severe anxiety disorders should exercise caution. The sedative quality of ergine may increase vulnerability to dysphoric experiences.
- **Drug interactions**: Serotonergic drug
- **Drug interactions**: Serotonergic drug interactions are possible due to 5-HT receptor activity. Avoid combining with SSRIs, MAOIs, or other serotonergic substances.

## Analytical Detection

Ergine can be detected in biological samples using liquid
chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) and gas chromatography-mass
spectrometry (GC-MS) methods. It is not included in standard
workplace or forensic drug screening panels, but can be identified
with targeted analysis. Detection windows vary by sample type:
blood (6–12 hours), urine (24–48 hours), and hair (weeks to months).
Standard reference materials are available from several chemical
suppliers for analytical calibration.
