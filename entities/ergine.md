## Overview

Ergine, also known as d-lysergic acid amide (LSA) and lysergamide, is a
naturally occurring psychedelic alkaloid of the ergoline family. It is a
direct structural precursor to LSD (lysergic acid diethylamide), differing
only by the substitution of a diethylamide group with a simple amide. Ergine
occurs naturally in the seeds of several *Convolvulaceae* (morning glory)
species and in the fungal ergot *Claviceps purpurea*.

Ergine was first isolated in 1947 from Hawaiian baby woodrose (*Argyreia nervosa*)
and morning glory (*Ipomoea tricolor*) seeds. Albert Hofmann identified it during
his early investigations into ergot alkaloids, work that also led to his
accidental discovery of LSD-25 in 1943.

While ergine is approximately 10–30 times less potent than LSD by weight,
it produces qualitatively similar effects including visual alterations, mood
elevation, and cognitive shifts. The experience is generally described as more
sedating and dreamlike than LSD, with a shorter visual component.

## Chemical Properties

Ergine has the molecular formula C₁₆H₁₇N₃O and a molecular weight of
267.32 g/mol. The IUPAC name is (6aR,9R)-N,6-diethyl-7-methyl-4,6,6a,7,8,
9,10-hexahydroindolo[4,3-fg]quinoline-9-carboxamide. It is the monoamide
derivative of lysergic acid.

The compound exists as a white crystalline solid with limited water solubility.
It is soluble in ethanol, methanol, and slightly soluble in ether. Ergine
contains two stereocenters (C6a and C9) with the naturally occurring
bioactive isomer having the 6aR,9R configuration.

Ergine is chemically unstable in aqueous solution at low pH, where it
undergoes isomerization to the inactive isoergine (l-lysergic acid amide).
This isomerization is a significant factor in the variable potency of
crude seed preparations.

## Natural Sources

Ergine is found in multiple plant species across the Convolvulaceae family
and in ergot fungi:

- **Hawaiian baby woodrose** (*Argyreia nervosa*): The richest natural source
  at 0.3–0.9% ergine by seed weight; seeds also contain ergine isomers
- **Morning glory** (*Ipomoea tricolor*, *I. violacea*): 0.05–0.1% ergine
  by seed weight; used traditionally by Aztec and Zapotec peoples as
  *tlitliltzin* ("black thing")
- **Ololiuqui** (*Rivea corymbosa*): 0.01–0.06% ergine; the original
  Aztec entheogen identified by Hofmann and Schultes in 1960

The seeds of these species also contain related alkaloids including
isoergine, chanoclavine, elymoclavine, and lysergol, which may modulate the
overall psychoactive profile. *Claviceps purpurea* (ergot fungus) produces
ergine as an intermediate in the ergotamine biosynthetic pathway.

## Pharmacology

Ergine is a partial agonist at serotonin 5-HT₂A receptors, the primary
mechanism underlying its psychedelic effects. Its affinity (Kᵢ) at 5-HT₂A
is approximately 3–10 nM, which is comparable to LSD's affinity at this
receptor. However, ergine shows lower functional efficacy (intrinsic activity)
than LSD, which partially explains its reduced subjective potency.

Additional receptor interactions include:

- **5-HT₁A**: Moderate partial agonism, contributing to anxiolytic effects
- **5-HT₂C**: Weak partial agonism
- **Dopamine receptors**: Minimal direct activity, unlike LSD which has
  significant D₂ agonism
- **TAAR1**: Trace affinity

Ergine is metabolized by hepatic enzymes with an estimated half-life of
3–4 hours. The onset of effects occurs within 30–90 minutes, peaking at
3–5 hours with a total duration of 6–10 hours.

## Effects and Dosage

The threshold dose of pure ergine is approximately 200 µg, with common
doses ranging from 500 µg to 1.5 mg. When consumed as crushed seeds, a
typical dose is 50–300 Hawaiian baby woodrose seeds or 200–500 morning
glory seeds, though alkaloid content varies significantly by batch.

Subjective effects include:

- Mild to moderate visual distortions with enhanced color saturation
- Dreamy, introspective mental state with reduced analytical thinking
- Sedation and physical heaviness, especially during onset
- Mild euphoria and emotional openness
- Altered thought patterns with increased associative thinking
- Nausea is common, attributed to non-psychoactive seed constituents

The experience is consistently described as more sedating, less stimulating,
and less visually intense than LSD. The "headspace" is shallower, with less
ego dissolution and fewer mystical-type experiences at equivalent subjective
intensity levels.

## Legal Status

Ergine is unscheduled in most jurisdictions but is indirectly regulated through
controls on its source materials. In the United States, morning glory seeds and
Hawaiian baby woodrose seeds are not specifically controlled under the CSA,
though several states have enacted restrictions on their sale or cultivation.

The 1970 Controlled Substances Act's "analogue provisions" have been
intermittently applied to ergine-containing preparations. Internationally,
ergine is not listed under the UN Convention on Psychotropic Substances. Some
countries, including Australia and Poland, have specifically scheduled ergine
as a controlled substance.
