# Lepista nuda (Wood Blewit)

**Lepista nuda**, commonly known as the **wood blewit** or **blue stalk mushroom**, is an edible species of agaric fungus in the family **Tricholomataceae** (sometimes placed in **Lyophyllaceae**). It is widely distributed across [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]] of the Northern Hemisphere and is one of the most recognizable late-season [[palmer-edible-mushrooms-north-america]], appreciated for its distinctive bluish-violet coloration and pleasant aroma.

## Taxonomy and Nomenclature

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Tricholomataceae (or Lyophyllaceae)
- **Genus:** *Lepista*
- **Species:** *L. nuda*

The species was originally described as **Agaricus nudus** by the Swedish mycologist **Elias Magnus Fries** in 1818. It was later transferred to the genus *Lepista* by **Mordecai Cubitt Cooke** in 1871. The genus name derives from the Greek *lepistes*, meaning "wine vessel," referring to the purplish color. The epithet *nuda* means "naked" or "bare," referring to the smooth, hairless cap surface.

Some authorities place this species in the genus **Clitocybe** as *[[clitocybe-nuda]]*, reflecting ongoing taxonomic revision within the Tricholomataceae complex.

## Description

### Cap
- **Diameter:** 4–15 cm
- **Shape:** Convex when young, expanding to broadly convex or nearly flat with age, sometimes developing a shallow central depression
- **Color:** Distinctive violet-blue to lilac when fresh, fading to brownish or tan with age; often with a wavy, inrolled margin
- **Surface:** Smooth, bare (nuda), slightly tacky when moist; may develop radial wrinkles

### Gills
- **Attachment:** Adnexed to slightly decurrent
- **Color:** Pale lilac to pinkish when young, becoming buff to pale tan with age
- **Spacing:** Crowded, with many short gills (lamellulae)
- **Edge:** Even, sometimes slightly serrate

### Stipe (Stem)
- **Dimensions:** 4–10 cm tall × 1–3 cm thick
- **Shape:** Cylindrical to slightly clavate, solid
- **Color:** Bluish-violet when young, fading to pale lilac or brownish with age
- **Surface:** Smooth to finely fibrillose, often with a slightly swollen base
- **Ring:** Absent

### Flesh
- **Color:** Pale lilac to whitish
- **Texture:** Firm, thick
- **Odor:** Distinctive, pleasant, fruity or perfumed — often described as reminiscent of frozen orange juice concentrate or violet flowers
- **Taste:** Mild, slightly nutty

### Spore Print
- **Color:** Pale pink to salmon-pink
- **Spore dimensions:** 6–8 × 3.5–5 µm, elliptical, smooth, slightly thick-walled

## Habitat and Distribution

*Lepista nuda* is a **saprotrophic** species, decomposing leaf litter and other [[maintaining-organic-matter-in-vegetable-gardens]]:

- **Deciduous and mixed woodlands**, particularly under oak, beech, and birch
- **Coniferous forests**, especially with spruce and pine
- **Leaf litter piles**, compost heaps, and garden mulch in urban settings
- **Grasslands** and hedgerows adjacent to wooded areas

It is found across **Europe, [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]], Asia, and parts of Australasia**. Fruiting occurs in **autumn through early winter** (September to January in the Northern Hemisphere), often persisting through light frosts. It is one of the few edible mushrooms that continues fruiting well into cold weather.

## Culinary Value

The wood blewit is considered a **good edible mushroom** when properly cooked:

- **Must be cooked thoroughly** — raw or undercooked specimens can cause gastrointestinal distress in some individuals
- **Flavor profile:** Mild, nutty, and slightly sweet with a pleasant aromatic quality
- **Texture:** Firm and meaty, holds up well in cooking
- **[[amanita-muscaria-preparation-methods]]:** Sautéing, braising, soups, stews, and drying for later use
