## Description

### Cap
- **Diameter:** 4–15 cm
- **Shape:** Convex when young, expanding to broadly convex or nearly flat with age, sometimes developing a shallow central depression
- **Color:** Distinctive violet-blue to lilac when fresh, fading to brownish or tan with age; often with a wavy, inrolled margin
- **Surface:** Smooth, bare (nuda), slightly tacky when moist; may develop radial wrinkles

### Gills
- **Attachment:** Adnexed to slightly decurrent
- **Color:** Pale lilac to pinkish when young, becoming buff to pale tan with age
- **Spacing:** Crowded, with many short gills (lamellulae)
- **Edge:** Even, sometimes slightly serrate

### Stipe (Stem)
- **Dimensions:** 4–10 cm tall × 1–3 cm thick
- **Shape:** Cylindrical to slightly clavate, solid
- **Color:** Bluish-violet when young, fading to pale lilac or brownish with age
- **Surface:** Smooth to finely fibrillose, often with a slightly swollen base
- **Ring:** Absent

### Flesh
- **Color:** Pale lilac to whitish
- **Texture:** Firm, thick
- **Odor:** Distinctive, pleasant, fruity or perfumed — often described as reminiscent of frozen orange juice concentrate or violet flowers
- **Taste:** Mild, slightly nutty

### Spore Print
- **Color:** Pale pink to salmon-pink
- **Spore dimensions:** 6–8 × 3.5–5 µm, elliptical, smooth, slightly thick-walled

## Habitat and Distribution

*Lepista nuda* is a **saprotrophic** species, decomposing leaf litter and other [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]], Asia, and parts of Australasia**. Fruiting occurs in **autumn through early winter** (September to January in the Northern Hemisphere), often persisting through light frosts. It is one of the few edible mushrooms that continues fruiting well into cold weather.
