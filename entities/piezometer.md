## Description

A piezometer is a diagnostic instrument and technique used to measure groundwater pressure and depth at specific points in the soil profile. In [[permaculture-site-analysis]]

## How Piezometers Work

A piezometer is installed by:

1. Drilling or augering a bore hole to a specific depth in the soil profile
2. Installing a sealed pipe with a perforated section at the target depth
3. The pipe allows water to enter only at the targeted depth
4. Water level in the pipe indicates the hydraulic pressure at that specific point
5. Multiple piezometers at different depths create a profile of groundwater conditions

## Key Facts

- Essential diagnostic tool for determining whether salting originates from deep aquifers or shallow seepage
- Allow precise measurement of water table depth and fluctuation over time
- Multiple installations at different depths reveal vertical groundwater movement patterns
- Data from piezometers determines the appropriate remediation strategy:
  - Deep seepage rising: [[interceptor-banks]] plus trees
  - Shallow seepage above B horizon: interceptor banks to cut off throughflow
  - Deep permeable soils with no cemented layer: tree planting may suffice alone
- Should be installed before any earthworks or tree planting in salt-affected areas
- Combined with backhoe pit observations for comprehensive site assessment

## Relevance to Cultivation

Piezometers are critical for informed design decisions in dryland and salt-affected agricultural systems:

- Prevent costly mistakes from applying inappropriate [[soil-pollution-and-remediation-strategies]]
- Enable monitoring of groundwater response to interceptor banks and tree planting
- Provide early warning of rising water tables before surface symptoms appear
- Allow quantitative assessment of remediation effectiveness over time
- Essential for any serious dryland [[salinity-management-in-aquaculture-ponds]] program
- Relatively inexpensive compared to the cost of failed tree plantings or earthworks

## Application in Permaculture Design

- Install at key points in the landscape: ridgelines, midslope, valley floor
- Read regularly (weekly to monthly) to build a picture of seasonal groundwater dynamics
- Use data to design interceptor bank placement and spacing
- Monitor after earthworks construction to verify effectiveness
- Combined with soil pits for complete sub-surface assessment
- Mollison emphasizes: dig backhoe pits and insert piezometers in key areas before choosing corrective strategies
