# Chlamydomonas

## Description

Chlamydomonas is a genus of green algae (Chlorophyta) identified by Mollison as one of the four almost universal sewage lagoon algae. It is a biflagellate single-celled organism that plays a crucial role in the biological treatment of wastewater through photosynthetic oxygen production in faculative and aerobic sewage treatment ponds.

## Classification

- **Domain**: Eukaryota
- **Kingdom**: Plantae (Viridiplantae)
- **Division**: Chlorophyta
- **Class**: Chlorophyceae
- **Order**: Chlamydomonadales
- **Family**: Chlamydomonadaceae
- **Genus**: Chlamydomonas
- **Functional group**: Phytoplankton / green alga

## Key Facts

- One of four universal sewage lagoon algae alongside Euglena, Chlorella, and Scenedesmus
- Biflagellate — possesses two flagella for active motility in the water column
- Contains a single large cup-shaped chloroplast for efficient photosynthesis
- Capable of both autotrophic (photosynthetic) and heterotrophic (absorptive) nutrition
- Reproduces rapidly through cell division, enabling quick colonisation of nutrient-rich environments
- Some species produce haemoglobin, giving them a reddish colour under stress conditions
- Model organism in cell biology and genetics research, with C. reinhardtii being the most studied
- Fixes carbon and releases oxygen, driving the bacterial-algal symbiosis essential for sewage purification

## Role in Sewage Treatment

Chlamydomonas is part of the algal community that transforms faculative and aerobic sewage ponds:

- **Oxygen production**: Photosynthesis releases dissolved oxygen that aerobic bacteria require to decompose organic matter
- **Nutrient uptake**: Absorbs nitrogen and phosphorus from wastewater, reducing eutrophication potential
- **Carbon fixation**: Converts inorganic carbon to algal biomass, reducing carbon load
- **Food web support**: Serves as food for zooplankton (rotifers, cladocerans, copepods) that further process nutrients

The algal-bacterial symbiosis in treatment ponds is one of the most energy-efficient purification mechanisms available, requiring only sunlight as an energy input.
