## Overview

Sanionia uncinata, commonly known as the hook-leaved moss or Sanionia moss, is
a widespread pleurocarpous moss in the family Hypnaceae. It is one of the most
abundant and ecologically important mosses in Arctic and alpine tundra ecosystems,
forming extensive carpets that influence soil thermal regimes [[mollison-designers-fish-pond-fertiliser-and-nutrient-cycling]].

The species is recognized by its strongly falcate-secund leaves with a single
long costa, plicate leaf surface, and hooked leaf tips when dry. Its remarkable
cold tolerance and capacity for photosynthesis at near-freezing temperatures
make it a keystone species in polar and high-mountain environments.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Sanionia uncinata was originally described as Hypnum uncinatum by Johann
Hedwig in 1801. Lars Hedenäs transferred the species to Sanionia in 1997 based
on detailed morphological and phylogenetic analysis, distinguishing it from
related genera in the Hypnaceae sensu lato.

Plants form extensive, dense mats and turfs that can cover large areas of tundra
and alpine terrain. Stems are 5 to 15 centimeters long, irregularly branched,
with a distinctive golden-green to yellowish coloration that is diagnostic in
the field.

Leaves are 2 to 3.5 millimeters long, broadly ovate-lanceolate, strongly
falcate-secund, and plicate with longitudinal folds. The costa is strong and
extends well beyond mid-leaf, often reaching near the apex.

Laminal cells are short-rectangular to rhomboidal, 20 to 40 micrometers long,
with moderately thickened walls. The plicate leaf surface creates channels that
channel water toward the stem and enhance moisture retention in cold, dry
environments.

The seta is 15 to 30 millimeters long, reddish-brown, with a cylindrical,
erect capsule 1.5 to 2.5 millimeters in length. The operculum is conical and
the double peristome is well developed with reflexed teeth when dry.

## Distribution and Habitat

Sanionia uncinata has a circumpolar Arctic-alpine distribution, occurring
throughout the Arctic tundra, subarctic regions, and alpine zones of temperate
mountain ranges worldwide. It is one of the most widely distributed mosses on
Earth, found on every continent including Antarctica.

In the Arctic, the species is a dominant ground cover component across tundra
types, from wet sedge meadows to dry heath tundra and polar deserts. It occurs
from sea level to over 2,500 meters elevation in mountainous regions.

The moss colonizes a broad range of substrates including mineral soil, [[humus]],
gravel, rock crevices, and decaying wood. In tundra environments, it frequently
grows over permafrost soils where its insulating mat influences active layer
depth and thaw dynamics.
