# Soybean Meal

## Description

**Soybean meal** is an all-purpose organic fertilizer (N-P-K 7-2-1) valued for its balanced nutrient profile, high carbon content, ease of application, and lack of unpleasant odor. It rapidly attracts both fungi and bacteria to the soil, which slowly release its nutrients over 3 to 4 months. It is produced from ground soybeans after oil extraction and serves dual purposes as both a soil amendment and an animal feed ingredient.

## Classification

- **Category:** Natural organic fertilizer / Plant by-product
- **N-P-K:** 7-2-1
- **Nutrient Availability:** Moderate, slow-release (3–4 months)
- **Application Form:** Dry meal, spreader or hand-broadcast
- **Domain:** Organic gardening, horticulture, livestock feed

## Key Facts

- Contains 7% nitrogen, 2% phosphorus, and 1% potassium — a well-balanced moderate N-P-K profile.
- Supplies substantial **carbon** alongside nitrogen, feeding the soil food web and building soil organic matter.
- **Odorless** — unlike many animal-derived organic fertilizers, soybean meal does not produce unpleasant smells.
- **Easy to handle** — stores well, works in mechanical spreaders, and can be broadcast by hand.
- Quickly attracts both **fungi and bacteria**, which slowly mineralize its nutrients over 3 to 4 months.
- Versatile all-purpose fertilizer suitable for vegetables, perennials, lawns, trees, and shrubs.

## Cautions and Considerations

- **GMO concern:** Soybeans are one of the most widely genetically modified crops globally. GMO soybeans contain inserted bacterial genes that produce novel proteins not historically present in the plant.
- These novel proteins may require specialized soil microbes for decomposition, potentially affecting the soil food web's ability to cycle the nutrients.
- Some researchers link GMO soy to increased soy allergies and autoimmune/digestive problems in humans and animals.
- Studies on cattle, poultry, and swine suggest greater caution should be used with GMO crops.
- Organic-certified soybean meal is available but more expensive.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- The ability of soybean meal to attract both fungi and bacteria makes it an excellent amendment for building balanced soil food web populations.
- The carbon content of soybean meal directly feeds saprophytic fungi, supporting the decomposer networks that cycle organic matter.
- Soybean meal can be used as a nitrogen supplement in mushroom substrate formulations, where its moderate N-P-K profile and fungal attractiveness support mycelial colonization.
- In grain spawn preparation for mushroom cultivation, soybean meal is sometimes added to grain to boost protein and nitrogen content for more vigorous mycelial growth.
- The GMO issue intersects with fungal cultivation: mushroom mycelium growing on GMO soybean substrate may interact differently with the novel proteins, though this area is under-researched.
