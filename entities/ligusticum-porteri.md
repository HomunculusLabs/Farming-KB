# Ligusticum porteri (J.M.Coult. & Rose) J.M.Coult. & Rose

## Overview

*Ligusticum porteri*, commonly known as **osha** or **bear root**, is a
perennial herbaceous plant in Apiaceae native to the Rocky Mountains and
adjacent high-elevation regions of western [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]].

Osha is among the most important medicinal plants in [[halpern-sacramental-peyote-and-native-american-church]]
herbal traditions, used for centuries as a treatment for respiratory
infections, sore throats, digestive complaints, and musculoskeletal pain.

Its intensely aromatic root, blending notes of celery, licorice, and
pine, contains volatile oils and coumarins that underlie its broad
pharmacological activity.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

- **Order:** Apiales | **Family:** Apiaceae | **Genus:** *Ligusticum*
- **Species:** *L. porteri* J.M.Coult. & Rose
- **Synonyms:** *Cymopterus porteri* T.J.Howell, *Leptotaenia porteri* Jeps.
- **Etymology:** Honors Thomas Conrad Porter (1822–1901), American botanist.

*L. porteri* grows 60–150 cm tall from a thick, branching taproot
exceeding 5 cm in diameter. Leaves are compound, 15–30 cm long, with
serrated leaflets giving a lacy, fern-like appearance.

Flowers are small and white in compound umbels 5–10 cm across.
Bloom period: July–August. Fruit is a ribbed schizocarp 4–6 mm,
splitting into two mericarps at maturity.

The dark brown root with cream-white interior is the primary
medicinal part, reaching harvestable size at 3–5 years.

## Distribution and Habitat

Native to the Rocky Mountain corridor from southern Montana and Wyoming
through Colorado, New Mexico, Arizona into northern Mexico (Chihuahua,
Sonora, Durango).

Grows at elevations of 1,800–3,600 meters in subalpine meadows, aspen
groves, mixed conifer forests, and riparian corridors.
