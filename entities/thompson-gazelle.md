# Thompson Gazelle

## Description

The Thompson gazelle (Eudorcas thomsonii) is a small to medium-sized gazelle native to
East African savannahs and grasslands. Mollison references the Thompson gazelle in the
stock unit equivalence system, noting that 25 Thompson gazelles equal one standard stock
unit (one 450 kg European cow and calf). As a native East African species, it represents
part of the diverse herbivore guild that naturally produces higher yields per unit area
than monoculture livestock systems, all without external energy inputs or soil degradation.

Kangaroo at 58% assimilable protein versus cattle at 35% illustrates the superior protein
conversion of well-managed wild species over domestic livestock in appropriate environments.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Animalia
- **Class:** Mammalia
- **Order:** Artiodactyla
- **Family:** Bovidae
- **Genus:** Eudorcas (formerly Gazella)
- **Species:** E. thomsonii
- **Common names:** Thomson's gazelle, Tommie
- **Size:** 55–65 cm shoulder height, 15–25 kg adult weight

## Key Facts

- 25 Thompson gazelles equivalent to one standard stock unit (450 kg cow and calf)
- Small, swift antelope of East African grasslands and savannahs
- One of the most abundant gazelle species in all of Africa
- Primarily a grazer but also browses on shrubs and forbs when needed
- Can survive without free surface water for extended periods
- Extremely fast — capable of 80 km/hour sustained running speed
- [[savory-predator-prey-and-herding-behavior]]; forms groups from 5 to 1,000+ individuals seasonally
- Males establish and defend territories during breeding season
- Key prey species for cheetahs, the primary specialized predator
- Part of the diverse herbivore guild of the African savannah ecosystem

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Rangeland diversity:** Part of native herbivore communities sustaining ~500 kg/ha
- **[[dighton-selective-grazing-fungal-community-shifts]]:** Feeds on short grasses, complementing taller-grass grazers
- **[[fungal-single-cell-protein-production]]:** Efficient species produce more assimilable protein than cattle/ha
- **Dung ecology:** Dung supports coprophilous [[biodiversity-fungi-soil-fungal-communities]] for [[aact-nitrogen-forms-microbial-nutrient-cycling-ingham]]
- **Soil fertility:** Concentrated grazing and dunging create nutrient hotspots on range
- **Game farming:** Pioneer game farms demonstrate benefits of native species management

## Ecological Relationships

- Coexists with 11 grazer species, 5 browser species, and 4 mixed feeders on savannah
- Selective grazing on short grasses creates habitat heterogeneity across the range
- Primary prey of cheetahs; also taken by lions, wild dogs, leopards, and hyenas
- Stotting behavior (bounding leaps) signals fitness to pursuing predators
- Seasonal movements follow rainfall and fresh grass availability across range
- Mixed herds with other grazers reduce individual predation risk through vigilance

## Stock Unit Context (from Mollison)

The standard stock unit is a European cow and calf at 450 kg liveweight. This equals:
2 African cows; 5 European sheep; 20 African sheep; 25 Thompson gazelles; or 125 dikdik
antelope. Mollison argues that protein production from small well-managed species like
gazelle far exceeds cattle per unit area. A judicious balance of species will always
exceed monoculture in total yield and maintain better rangeland condition.

## Design Implications

- Natural multi-species systems outperform monoculture on all productivity metrics
- Small species can be harvested more continuously with lower [[fertilizer-pollution-environmental-impact]]
- Stock unit calculations enable integration of native and domestic species
- Importing European livestock prevented use of better-adapted native antelope
- Efficient food conversion makes gazelles potentially superior for protein per area
