## Overview

**Actinidia deliciosa**, commonly known as the fuzzy kiwifruit or simply kiwi, is a deciduous woody vine in the family Actinidiaceae.

It is native to southern China and is the most commercially important species in the genus *Actinidia*.

The fruit is a large berry with brown fuzzy skin, green flesh, and a characteristic sweet-tart flavor.

Global kiwifruit production exceeds 4 million tonnes annually, with New Zealand, Italy, Chile, and China as leading producers.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:** Kingdom Plantae → Order Ericales → Family Actinidiaceae → Genus *Actinidia* → *A. deliciosa*.

The species was originally described by Augustin Abel Hector Léveillé in 1906 as *A. chinensis* var. *deliciosa* and elevated to species rank by Chevalier in 1940.

**Growth habit** is a vigorous, climbing deciduous vine with stems that can reach 9–15 meters in length.

The vine climbs by twining and requires a strong support structure such as a pergola, trellis, or wire system.

**Leaves** are alternate, broadly ovate to cordate, 8–15 cm long and 7–12 cm wide, with serrate margins and a prominent drip tip.

The upper [[bloomfield-rust-germ-tube-tactile-sensing-leaf-surface-navigation]] is dark green and often sparsely covered with reddish-brown hairs.

**Flowers** are white to cream-colored, approximately 3–5 cm in diameter, borne in axillary cymes of 1–3 flowers.

The species is functionally dioecious, with separate male and female plants required for fruit production.

Flowers appear in spring (May–June in the Northern Hemisphere) shortly after leaf emergence.

**Fruit** is a large, ovoid to oblong berry, 5–8 cm long and 4.5–6 cm in diameter.

The skin is brown and covered with dense, stiff, tan hairs, giving the fruit its characteristic fuzzy appearance.

Flesh is bright green with a central white core radiating numerous small black seeds.

Seeds are approximately 2–3 mm long, numerous (up to 1,400 per fruit), and edible.

## Distribution and Habitat

*A. deliciosa* is native to the forested mountains of eastern and southern China, particularly in the provinces of Hubei, Sichuan, Shaanxi, and Fujian.

In the wild, it grows as a forest-edge vine at elevations of 600–2,000 meters in mixed deciduous forests.

It prefers deep, fertile, well-drained loamy soils with adequate moisture and partial shade.

The species is naturalized in several countries including New Zealand, Italy, the [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]] (California), and Chile.

Naturalized populations occasionally establish from discarded fruit in mild [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]].

It requires a [[savory-growing-season-and-non-growing-season-management]] of 200–240 frost-free days and approximately 600–1,200 mm of annual rainfall.

Winter chilling requirements are 600–900 hours below 7°C to ensure proper bud break and flowering.

## Cultivation

Kiwifruit vines are typically trained onto a T-bar or pergola trellis system at spacing of 4–5 meters between vines.

A male-to-female planting ratio of approximately 1:8 is standard, with male vines distributed evenly through the orchard.

Pruning is critical for productivity and is performed in both winter (structural) and summer (vigorous shoot removal).

Flowering occurs in spring, and pollination is primarily by honeybees, though wind plays a minor role.

Fruit matures in autumn, typically 150–180 days after flowering, and is harvested when soluble solids reach 6.2–6.5%.

Commercial cultivars are predominantly selections from the 'Hayward' clone, which dominates global production.

The 'Hayward' cultivar was selected in New Zealand in the 1920s by Hayward Wright and remains the industry standard.

Major pests include root-knot nematodes, leafroller caterpillars, and the bacterial canker *[[pseudomonas]] syringae* pv. *actinidiae*.
