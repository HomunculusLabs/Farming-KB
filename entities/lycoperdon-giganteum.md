## Lycoperdon giganteum (Giant Puffball)

## Description

Lycoperdon giganteum, commonly known as the giant puffball (now classified as [[calvatia-gigantea]]),
is one of the largest fungi in the world, producing [[bloomfield-asterophora-and-mycoparasites-of-fruiting-bodies]] that can reach up to 70 cm
(28 inches) in diameter and weigh over 20 kg (44 lbs). When young and edible, the interior is
pure white and firm, resembling a dense marshmallow. The skin color varies from pure white to
almost black. As it matures, the interior turns yellow and eventually brown, becoming a mass of
dry [[spore]] powder.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Agaricaceae
- **Genus:** Calvatia (modern) / Lycoperdon (historical, Palmer"s usage)
- **Species:** Calvatia gigantea

## Key Facts

- Palmer considered puffballs "the safest mushrooms for the beginner."
- The critical test for edibility: slice open and check interior color. If pure white throughout,
  it is safe. If the knife reveals any yellow stain, it is too old to eat.
- No poisonous fungus resembles the giant puffball — making it one of the most confidently
  recommended species for novice foragers.
- Must be cooked immediately after gathering; changes very rapidly and should only be eaten in
  its freshest state.
- Young specimens resemble oversized puffballs; the infant state shows smooth skin and pure white
  interior.
- Skin color is not an indicator of quality — varies from white to nearly black regardless of
  edibility.

## Edibility

Excellent edible when young and white throughout. Palmer stated "you may eat it without fear"
if the interior is pure white. The flesh has a mild, pleasant flavor and dense texture that
absorbs cooking fats and seasonings well. The rapid deterioration after picking means it must
be prepared promptly. All white puffballs may be safely used according to Palmer, provided they
are cooked at once.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- One of the safest species for beginners due to the absence of poisonous lookalikes.
- The simple "white inside = safe" test exemplifies the clearest edibility rule in [[mycology]].
- The rapid post-harvest deterioration illustrates the importance of freshness in fungal food
  safety — a theme Palmer emphasized throughout his work.
- Giant puffballs can produce trillions of spores from a single [[bloomfield-fungal-fruiting-body-morphology-ecological-function]], making them
  subjects of [[clarke-marijuana-botany-sex-determination-reproductive-biology]] studies.
- The species has been investigated for [[cotter-mushroom-nutrition-medicinal-compounds]] including calvacin, which showed
  antitumor activity in early research.
- Cultivation has been attempted but is challenging; most specimens are wild-foraged.

## Identification Notes

- Fruit body: spherical to broadly ovoid; 10-70 cm diameter.
- Skin (peridium): smooth, white to brownish; thin and papery.
- Interior (gleba): pure white and firm when young; yellow then olive-brown when old.
- If cut and any yellow shows, discard — too old for consumption.
- No stem; attached to substrate by a cord-like mycelial root.
- Habitat: grasslands, meadows, pastures, sometimes forest edges.
- Season: late summer to fall.

## Distinguishing from Lookalikes

- Earthballs (Scleroderma spp.) have a dark, marbled interior even when young — not pure white.
- [[amanita]] "eggs" (unexpanded Amanita fruiting bodies) are differentiated by cutting vertically
  to reveal the developing [[biology-of-fungi-and-mushroom-structure]] inside (gill outlines, stem).
- Palmer noted that no poisonous fungus resembles puffballs, making them uniquely safe.

## Historical Notes

Palmer listed puffballs first among his four grand divisions of fungi (gill-bearing, pore-bearing,
tooth-bearing, and puffballs), recommending them as the starting point for beginners. His simple
rule — white inside and fresh equals safe — has remained essentially unchanged in modern foraging
guides.
