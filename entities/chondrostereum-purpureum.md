# Chondrostereum purpureum

## Overview

*Chondrostereum purpureum* is a basidiomycete fungus and the causal agent of silver leaf disease in woody plants, particularly stone fruits and hardwoods.

It is notable both as a plant pathogen and as a registered bioherbicide used for stump treatment to prevent re-sprouting of unwanted woody species.

The fungus produces a characteristic lilac-purple, leathery, shelf-like fruiting body on dead wood.

Its white-rot decay mechanism and ability to colonize freshly cut stumps make it a valuable tool in vegetation management and forest regeneration.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Polyporales
- **Family:** Chondrostereaceae
- **Genus:** Chondrostereum
- **Species:** *C. purpureum* (Pers.) Pouzar (1959)
- **Synonyms:** *Thelephora purpurea* Pers. (1798), *Stereum purpureum* (Pers.) Fr. (1838)
- **Etymology:** *Chondro-* (cartilage) + *stereum* (firm); *purpureum* (purple), referring to the color of the fruiting body.

### Fruiting Body

- **Type:** Effused-reflexed to pileate (shelf-like), semicircular
- **Size:** 2–8 cm across, 1–4 cm radial extension
- **Upper surface:** Concentrically zoned, greyish-brown with fine hairs, margin often purple-tinted
- **Hymenium (underside):** Smooth to slightly wrinkled, distinctly lilac to purple
- **Flesh:** Tough, leathery, corky when dry, 1–2 mm thick
- **Spore print:** White to cream
- **Spores:** Cylindrical to allantoid, smooth, 5–7 × 2–3 µm, inamyloid
- **Basidia:** Clavate, four-spored, 25–35 × 4–5 µm

### Mycelial Characteristics

- Colonies on malt extract agar are white, cottony, becoming cream-colored with age.
- Aerial mycelium is moderately dense; growth rate 3–5 mm/day at 20–25°C.
- Hyphae are clamp-bearing, septate, 2–4 µm diameter.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Chondrostereum purpureum* has a broad circumpolar distribution across temperate regions of Europe, [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]], Asia, and parts of the [[guzman-allen-gartz-africa-southern-hemisphere-underexplored-neurotropic-fungi]].

It occurs as a saprotroph on dead hardwood branches, trunks, and stumps.

Primary hosts include *Prunus* species (cherry, plum, peach, apricot), apple (*Malus domestica*), poplar (*Populus* spp.), birch (*Betula* spp.), willow (*Salix* spp.), and numerous other hardwoods.

The fungus enters living trees through pruning wounds, frost cracks, or other bark injuries, establishing a systemic infection that spreads through the xylem.

Infected branches develop a characteristic silver sheen in the leaves due to separation of the palisade mesophyll from the underlying tissue — the diagnostic symptom of silver leaf disease.

## Pathogenicity and Silver Leaf Disease

Silver leaf disease progresses in three phases.

In the **invasive phase**, the fungus colonizes the xylem vessels of a living host, producing toxins that cause leaf discoloration and silvering.

During the **stress phase**, the host's defenses slow fungal spread, and symptoms may partially recede if the tree is vigorous.

In the **degradation phase**, the fungus transitions to a white-rot saprotroph, decomposing the lignin and cellulose of dead wood.

ⓘ In stone fruit orchards, silver leaf can cause significant economic losses.

Infected branches should be pruned at least 15 cm below visible symptoms, and wounds treated with fungicidal wound paint.

## Bioherbicide Applications

*C. purpureum* is one of the most widely used fungal bioherbicides globally.

Commercial formulations (e.g., Chontrol® in Canada, BioChon® in Europe) are applied as mycelial inoculum to freshly cut stumps.

The fungus colonizes the stump cambium and root system, preventing re-sprouting and killing the root system over 1–3 growing seasons.

Key advantages over chemical herbicides include target specificity (affects only treated stumps), no soil or water contamination, and compatibility with organic certification.

It is particularly effective on species prone to prolific re-sprouting such as aspen (*Populus tremuloides*), willow (*Salix* spp.), and alder (*Alnus* spp.).

### Application Considerations

- Optimal application within 24 hours of cutting for maximum colonization.
- Effective stump diameter range: 5–30 cm.
- Inoculum is typically formulated as a paste or granular preparation containing colonized grain or sawdust.
- Reduced efficacy in very dry conditions or on stumps exposed to direct sunlight.

## Wood Decay Mechanism

*C. purpureum* produces a classic white-rot decay, characterized by simultaneous degradation of lignin and cellulose.

It secretes lignin peroxidase (LiP), [[fungal-manganese-peroxidase-remediation]] (MnP), and [[singh-fungal-laccase-enzymes-bioremediation]] that break down the lignin polymer matrix.

[[brown-rot-fungi-cellulose-degradation-wood-decay]] is mediated by endoglucanases, cellobiohydrolases, and β-glucosidases.

The combined enzymatic attack results in bleached, fibrous, and eventually spongy wood that is structurally compromised.

This white-rot capability places *C. purpureum* among the ecologically important decomposers of hardwood lignin in forest ecosystems.
