## Ephedra sinica

**Ephedra sinica**, known as **Ma Huang** (麻黄) in traditional Chinese medicine, is a gymnosperm shrub in the family Ephedraceae. It is the principal natural source of ephedrine and pseudoephedrine — alkaloid sympathomimetic amines that have been used medicinally for over 5,000 years and remain important in modern pharmacology. Native to [[neurotropic-fungi-japan-east-asia-species-cultural-context]].

## Chemistry

Ephedra sinica produces a characteristic profile of alkaloids concentrated in the aerial stems:

| Compound | % dry weight | Activity |
|----------|-------------|----------|
| Ephedrine | 0.5–1.5% | α/β-adrenergic agonist |
| Pseudoephedrine | 0.2–0.8% | α/β-adrenergic agonist |
| Norephedrine | Trace | Weaker sympathomimetic |
| Norpseudoephedrine (cathine) | Trace | Stimulant |
| Methylephedrine | Trace | Minor |

**Ephedrine** (C₁₀H₁₅NO) is a direct and indirect sympathomimetic amine that:
- Stimulates the release of norepinephrine from sympathetic nerve endings
- Directly activates α- and β-adrenergic receptors
- Produces bronchodilation, vasoconstriction, and CNS stimulation
- Structurally related to amphetamine and methamphetamine

## Traditional Use

Ephedra sinica has one of the longest documented histories of medicinal plant use:
- **Shennong Ben Cao Jing** (~2700 BCE): Listed as a superior herb for treating respiratory ailments
- **Chinese pharmacopeia:** Used for asthma, bronchitis, nasal congestion, colds, fever, and chills
- **Ayurveda:** Related Ephedra species used in similar contexts
- **Method:** Stems dried and decocted; often combined with other herbs in classical formulas

The most famous traditional formula is **Ma Huang Tang** (Ephedra Decoction), dating to the Shang Han Lun (~200 CE), used for wind-cold conditions with fever and body aches.

## Modern Pharmacology

Ephedrine derived from E. sinica was first isolated by Nagai Nagayoshi in 1887. Its modern [[medicinal-mushroom-dietary-supplements]] were banned in the [[blesching-cannabis-hypertension-cardiovascular-effects]]:** Tachycardia, hypertension, arrhythmias
- **CNS effects:** Insomnia, anxiety, tremor, headache
- **[[mao-inhibitors-and-psychedelics]], caffeine, and other stimulants

Pseudoephedrine remains available in OTC decongestants but is regulated behind pharmacy counters in many countries due to its use as a methamphetamine precursor (Combat Methamphetamine Epidemic Act of 2005, US).
