# Conocybe smithii

**Conocybe smithii** is a small, fragile, psilocybin-containing mushroom in the family Bolbitiaceae. It is one of the lesser-known psychoactive *Conocybe* species, found in grasslands and disturbed soils in North America and Europe. The species was named in honor of the American mycologist **Alexander H. Smith**, one of the most prolific agaric taxonomists of the 20th century. Due to its extremely small size, inconspicuous appearance, and tendency to grow among other small grassland mushrooms, *C. smithii* is rarely noticed by casual observers and is among the most underreported [[psilocybin-mushrooms]].

## Taxonomy and Etymology

- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Bolbitiaceae
- **Genus:** Conocybe
- **Species:** C. smithii
- **Authority:** Watling

The genus *Conocybe* (sometimes called "cone heads") is characterized by small, conic-capped mushrooms with rusty brown spore prints and a preference for grassland habitats. Many species in the genus contain **phaeocystins** and other unique alkaloids. The specific epithet *smithii* honors Alexander H. Smith (1904–1986), who described over 2,000 fungal species during his career at the University of Michigan. Some authorities consider North American and European collections to represent different taxa, and the name *C. smithii* has been applied somewhat inconsistently in the literature.

## Morphology

### Cap (Pileus)
- 5–20 mm diameter (very small)
- Conic to campanulate (bell-shaped), sometimes with a pronounced acute apex
- Surface smooth, silky, or slightly fibrillose
- Color: ochraceous brown to tan when moist; pale buff to cream when dry
- Hygrophanous: fades dramatically as it dries
- Margin often slightly striate when moist
- Often with a small papilla (nipple-like protrusion) at the center

### Gills (Lamellae)
- Adnexed to narrowly adnate
- Close to subdistant
- Color: pale brown when young, becoming rusty brown (cinnamon to ferruginous) at maturity
- Spore print: rusty brown to orange-brown

### Stipe (Stem)
- 25–70 mm long, 0.5–2 mm thick (extremely fragile)
- Equal, hollow, very slender
- Surface: whitish to pale buff, sometimes with a slight pruina (frosty coating) near the apex
- No annulus or partial veil (distinctive from some other *Conocybe* species)
- Base may develop bluish-green discoloration, though this is often absent or barely visible

### Flesh
- Extremely thin, almost membranous
- Whitish to pale brown
- Odor and taste not distinctive or slightly mealy
- Bruising: may show faint blue-green at the base, but this is inconsistent

### Spores
- Spore print: rusty brown
- Spores: 8–12 × 5–7 µm, ellipsoid to lemon-shaped (limoniform)
- Thick-walled with a distinct germ pore
- Smooth to finely roughened
- Dextrinoid (staining reddish brown in Melzer's reagent)

### Microscopic Features
- Cheilocystidia: abundant, clavate to cylindrical, often with a swollen apex
- Pleurocystidia: absent or rare
- Pileipellis: a cutis (repent hyphae) with occasional pileocystidia

## Distribution and Habitat

*Conocybe smithii* is found in:

- **North America:** Pacific Northwest, California, the Great Plains, eastern seaboard; particularly common in the Pacific Northwest
- **Europe:** Scattered reports from the British Isles, France, Germany, and Scandinavia
- **Possible range:** Other temperate regions

It is a **grassland and dung-inhabiting species** found in:

- Rich pastures and meadows
- Lawns and parks (especially those with fertile, well-maintained grass)
- Compost heaps and enriched garden soils
- Occasionally on horse or cow dung
- Disturbed ground with abundant organic matter

Fruiting occurs from late spring through autumn, typically in brief flushes following rain. The fruiting bodies are extremely ephemeral, often lasting only 24–48 hours before shriveling or being consumed by invertebrates.

## Chemistry

| Compound | Concentration (% dry weight) |
|----------|------------------------------|
| Psilocybin | 0.05 – 0.60 (variable) |
| Psilocin | trace – 0.10 |
| Baeocystin | trace – 0.05 |

Potency varies significantly between collections. Some reports suggest Pacific Northwest collections may be more potent than those from other regions. The species was confirmed to contain psilocybin through GC-MS analysis by **Guzmán** and colleagues. The thin, fragile fruiting bodies make collection of sufficient material for chemical analysis challenging.

## Identification Challenges

*Conocybe smithii* is extremely difficult to identify in the field due to:

1. **Small size:** Caps under 15 mm are easily overlooked in grass
2. **Similar appearance:** Dozens of small brown *Conocybe* species look nearly identical macroscopically
3. **Microscopic identification required:** Definitive ID requires measuring spores and examining cystidia
4. **Ephemeral nature:** Fruitbodies may be gone within hours of appearing
5. **Inconsistent bruising:** Blue staining is unreliable as a field marker
6. **Toxic lookalikes:** Some *Conocybe* species contain dangerous levels of **amatoxins** or other toxins

## Toxic Lookalikes — Critical Warning

Several *Conocybe* species are **dangerously toxic** and can be easily confused with *C. smithii*:

- **Conocybe filaris:** Contains amatoxins (the same toxins as *[[amanita-phalloides]]*); can cause fatal liver and kidney failure
- **Conocybe apala:** Common lawn species; non-psychoactive but not dangerous
- **Conocybe lactea:** Small white species; non-psychoactive
- **Conocybe albipes:** Very common lawn species; non-psychoactive

The risk of confusing *C. smithii* with toxic *Conocybe* species makes it one of the **most dangerous** psilocybin mushrooms to forage. Microscopic examination is strongly recommended before any consumption.

## Ecological Role

*Conocybe smithii* is a **saprotrophic** species that decomposes organic matter in grassland soils. Its role in nutrient cycling includes:

- Breaking down grass clippings and thatch
- Decomposing dung and manure deposits
- Contributing to soil humus formation

The species appears to thrive in nitrogen-rich environments, which explains its association with fertilized lawns and manured pastures.

## Cultivation

*Conocybe smithii* has been cultivated on agar and sterilized grain substrates but is not cultivated for any practical purpose:

- Very small fruiting bodies make yields negligible
- Requires grass-based substrates for reliable fruiting
- Prefers warm temperatures (20–25°C) and high humidity
- Often outcompeted by faster-growing fungi in cultivation

## Similar Species

- **Conocybe cyanopus:** Also psychoactive; distinguished by more prominent blue bruising and larger spores
- **Conocybe kuehneriana:** European species; reported to contain psilocybin; very similar morphology
- **Conocybe siligineodes:** Small species found in similar habitats; psychoactive status uncertain
- **Conocybe filaris:** Toxic amatoxin-containing species; visually very similar; must be distinguished microscopically
