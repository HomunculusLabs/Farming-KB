# MDMA (3,4-Methylenedioxymethamphetamine)

**MDMA** (3,4-methylenedioxymethamphetamine) is a synthetic psychoactive compound of the **substituted amphetamine** class, classified primarily as an **entactogen** or **empathogen**. It is best known for producing profound feelings of emotional openness, empathy, connection, and euphoria, along with mild stimulant and psychedelic effects. MDMA has been the subject of intensive clinical research for its potential therapeutic applications, particularly in the treatment of **post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD)**.

## Chemical Identity

- **IUPAC name:** (RS)-1-(1,3-benzodioxol-5-yl)-N-methylpropan-2-amine
- **Chemical formula:** C₁₁H₁₅NO₂
- **Molecular weight:** 193.25 g/mol
- **CAS number:** 42542-10-9 (racemate)
- **SMILES:** CNC(C)CC1=CC(=C(C=C1)O2)OCO2
- **Classification:** Substituted amphetamine; phenethylamine derivative; entactogen
- **Appearance:** White crystalline powder (pure form); commonly encountered as tablets, capsules, or powder

MDMA is structurally related to both **amphetamine** (through the alpha-methyl group) and **mescaline** (through the 3,4-methylenedioxy substitution pattern on the phenyl ring). It exists as two enantiomers: **(R)-(−)-MDMA** and **(S)-(+)-MDMA**, with the S-enantiomer being more potent in releasing serotonin.

## Pharmacology

### Mechanism of Action

MDMA acts primarily as a **releasing agent and reuptake inhibitor** of three monoamine neurotransmitters:

1. **Serotonin (5-HT):** Primary mechanism — MDMA enters serotonergic neurons via the serotonin transporter (SERT) and causes massive, non-exocytotic serotonin release by reversing the transporter's normal direction. It also inhibits tryptophan hydroxylase (the rate-limiting enzyme in serotonin synthesis) and activates 5-HT₂ receptors. This serotonergic action is responsible for the characteristic empathogenic and mood-elevating effects.

2. **Dopamine (DA):** MDMA reverses the dopamine transporter (DAT), causing dopamine release in the mesolimbic pathway. This contributes to the stimulant and euphoric effects, reward signaling, and some of the neurotoxic potential.

3. **Norepinephrine (NE):** Reversal of the norepinephrine transporter (NET) produces sympathomimetic effects — increased heart rate, blood pressure, body temperature, and alertness.

4. **Additional receptor interactions:**
   - Partial agonist at **5-HT₂A** receptors (subtle psychedelic-like effects)
   - Release of **oxytocin** and **vasopressin** (pro-social and bonding effects)
   - Increased **prolactin** and **cortisol** levels

### Pharmacokinetics

- **Route of administration:** Primarily oral; also nasal insufflation (powder) and rarely intravenous
- **Onset:** 30–60 minutes (oral); 5–15 minutes (nasal)
- **Peak plasma concentration:** 1.5–2.5 hours (oral)
- **Duration of effects:** 3–6 hours (oral); shorter with insufflation
- **Half-life:** 7–9 hours
### Metabolism: Primarily hepatic via **CYP2D6** (N-demethylation to MDA), **CYP3A4**, and **COMT** (O-demethylenation to HHMA)
- **Active metabolites:** **MDA** (3,4-methylenedioxyamphetamine)
- **Excretion:** Renal (~65% as unchanged drug and metabolites)

## Subjective Effects

The MDMA experience is typically described in phases:

### Onset (30–90 minutes)
- Anticipation, "butterflies," tingling sensations
- Pupil dilation, jaw tension (gurning)
- Initial mood elevation and sensory enhancement

### Peak (1.5–3 hours)
- **Profound empathy and emotional openness** — the signature effect
- Enhanced tactile sensitivity, intensified music/art appreciation
- Reduced fear, anxiety, and defensiveness; talkativeness and emotional expressiveness
- Possible mild visual distortions (trailing, color shifting) at higher doses

### Comedown (4–6 hours)
- Gradual return to baseline; possible fatigue, emotional flatness, or mild dysphoria
- Muscle tension, jaw soreness, appetite suppression

### Common Physical Effects
- Pupillary dilation, increased heart rate and blood pressure, jaw clenching (gurning)
- Hyperthermia (elevated body temperature), nausea (less common), sweating, loss of appetite

## Dosage

| Route | Threshold | Light | Common | Strong | Heavy |
|-------|-----------|-------|--------|--------|-------|
| Oral | 30 mg | 50–75 mg | 80–120 mg | 120–160 mg | 160+ mg |
| Nasal | 15 mg | 25–50 mg | 50–80 mg | — | — |

- **Therapeutic dose (clinical trials):** 80–120 mg, with optional supplemental half-dose (40 mg) at 1.5–2 hours
- **Recreational:** Often consumed in 80–150 mg range, though street tablets vary widely in actual MDMA content
- **Harm reduction:** Reagent testing (Marquis, Mecke, Simons) recommended; pill testing services available in many countries

## Neurotoxicity and Safety

### Acute Risks
- **Hyponatremia** — potentially fatal; caused by excessive water intake + MDMA-induced SIADH; most common cause of MDMA-related death
- **Hyperthermia** — dangerous in hot environments; risk of rhabdomyolysis, organ failure
- **Cardiovascular events** — tachycardia, hypertension, arrhythmias (rare; higher risk with pre-existing cardiac conditions)
- **Serotonin syndrome** — rare but life-threatening when combined with MAOIs, SSRIs, or other serotonergic agents

### Neurotoxicity and Harm Reduction
- Animal studies show dose-dependent serotonergic axon damage with high-dose/frequent/hyperthermic exposure; clinical significance in humans debated
- Moderate, infrequent use in controlled settings poses minimal long-term risk
- **Harm reduction:** Test substances with reagent kits; moderate dosing (80–120 mg) with 6–8+ week intervals; stay cool and hydrate appropriately (250–500 mL/hour); avoid mixing with alcohol, MAOIs, SSRIs, stimulants; have sober companions present

## Clinical Research and Therapeutic Applications

### PTSD Treatment (Phase 3 Clinical Trials)

MDMA-assisted psychotherapy is among the most promising applications in modern psychedelic medicine:

- **Protocol:** Three 8-hour sessions (80–120 mg + 40–60 mg supplemental) with preparatory and integration psychotherapy
- **Phase 2 (Mithoefer et al.):** ~67–68% no longer met PTSD criteria vs. ~25–32% placebo
- **Phase 3 (2021–2024):** ~71% clinically significant improvement vs. 48% placebo; significant CAPS-5 reductions
- **Mechanism:** Reduces **amygdala** activity (fear) while enhancing **prefrontal cortex** activity (rational processing)
- **Regulatory status:** FDA advisory committee (2024) returned negative recommendation; resubmission ongoing

### Other Investigated Applications
- **Social anxiety in autism spectrum disorder**, **end-of-life anxiety**, **couples therapy**, **treatment-resistant depression**, and **alcohol/substance use disorder**

## History

- **1912:** First synthesized by **Anton Köllisch** at **Merck** in Darmstadt, Germany; patented as a hemostatic precursor. Never developed commercially.
- **1965–1970:** **Alexander Shulgin** synthesized and documented its psychoactive effects (published in PIHKAL, 1991)
- **1970s–1977:** Used by psychotherapists as an adjunct to therapy; recreational use began spreading after 1977 Chicago seizure
- **1985:** Emergency scheduled as **Schedule I** in the US by the DEA; also scheduled under UN Convention
- **1980s–2000s:** Widespread rave culture use ("Ecstasy"); adulteration with cathinones became common
- **2010s–2024:** Clinical renaissance — MAPS-sponsored PTSD research; FDA advisory committee voted against approval (2024); resubmission ongoing

## Legal Status

- **United States:** Schedule I (DEA) — high potential for abuse, no accepted medical use
- **United Nations:** Schedule I of the Convention on Psychotropic Substances (1971)
- **Australia:** Rescheduled to Schedule III for PTSD treatment (2023) — first country to approve medical MDMA
- **Canada, EU, Netherlands:** Generally prohibited; clinical research permitted under specific approvals
