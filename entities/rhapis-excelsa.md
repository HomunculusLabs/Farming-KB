## Overview

Rhapis excelsa, commonly known as the lady palm or broadleaf lady palm, is a species of small fan palm native to southern China and Taiwan. It is one of the most popular indoor palms in the world, widely cultivated for its tolerance of low light and indoor conditions.

The species forms dense clumps of slender, bamboo-like stems covered in fibrous leaf sheaths. Its glossy, dark green fan leaves create an elegant, tropical appearance that complements both traditional and contemporary [[biodiversity-fungal-genetic-diversity]] in cultivated forms, particularly in leaf variegation patterns. This diversity reflects centuries of selective breeding by Chinese and Japanese horticulturists, making it one of the most genetically variable cultivated palms.

Rhapis excelsa has been cultivated in China and Japan for centuries and was historically reserved for aristocratic households. The inflorescence is interfoliar, branched to two orders, and produces small, creamy-yellow flowers. Male and female flowers are borne on separate plants, making Rhapis excelsa a dioecious species, which is relatively uncommon among cultivated palms.

Male inflorescences are more branched and produce larger quantities of pollen, while female inflorescences bear fewer flowers but develop into fruit after successful pollination. In cultivation, the dioecious habit means [[coleman-sunlight-and-winter-crop-growth-rates]] are notably slow, with mature height typically reached after 20 to 30 years in cultivation. This slow growth contributes to the plant's longevity and reduces maintenance requirements for indoor and landscape specimens.

## Distribution and Habitat

Rhapis excelsa is native to southern China, primarily in Guangxi, Guangdong, and Hainan provinces, as well as Taiwan. Wild populations are now rare due to centuries of collection and habitat modification.

The species grows naturally in the understory of subtropical broadleaf forests at elevations from 100 to 800 meters. It is adapted to low light conditions beneath taller canopy trees and thrives in humid, shaded environments.
