## Description

The Rig Veda is an ancient Indian sacred text and one of the oldest known religious writings in any Indo-European language. It contains thousands of hymns devoted to soma, a sacred hallucinogenic plant or beverage. The descriptions of soma in the Rig-Veda have been crucial to identifying its probable botanical identity as [[allegro-amanita-muscaria-properties-and-pharmacology]] (fly agaric mushroom).

## Classification

- **Category**: Sacred text / Religious scripture
- **Cultural origin**: Ancient Vedic (Aryan) civilization of India
- **Language**: Vedic Sanskrit
- **Date**: Composed approximately 1500-1200 BCE
- **Contents**: Over 1,000 hymns (suktas) in 10 books (mandalas)

## Key Facts

- Contains thousands of hymns devoted to soma, describing the plant and its effects
- Soma was considered a divine substance, literally a god in plant form
- The hymns describe soma ceremonies where Aryan conquerors drank the sacred substance
- References to ceremonial urine drinking are a critical clue linking soma to Amanita muscaria
- No other proposed candidate plant matches all the descriptions found in the hymns
- For over 2,000 years after soma use died out, more than 100 plants were proposed as candidates
- The descriptions in the Rig-Veda are detailed enough to rule out most candidates

## Significance to Ethnomycology

The Rig-Veda is arguably the most important text in the history of ethnomycology:

- Provides the most detailed ancient description of a [[emcdda-barriers-to-hallucinogenic-mushroom-trend-diffusion]]
- The soma mystery drove ethnomycological research for over a century
- R. Gordon Wasson's identification of soma as A. muscaria was partly based on Rig-Veda descriptions
- The text describes effects consistent with muscimole intoxication
- References to [[siberian-fly-agaric-shamanism-urine-recycling]] match the known pharmacokinetics of muscimole

## Soma Hymns

Many hymns in the Rig-Veda describe:
- The appearance of the soma plant (consistent with A. muscaria morphology)
- The method of preparation (drying, pressing, filtering)
- The effects (ecstasy, divine inspiration, immortality)
- The ritual consumption (ceremonial drinking, including recycled urine)
- The divine status of soma (worshiped as a god)

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

The Rig-Veda is relevant to mycological studies:

- Documents one of the oldest known relationships between humans and psychoactive fungi
- Provides evidence for the antiquity of [[health-risks-and-harm-reduction-for-hallucinogenic-mushroom-use]]
- Links Indo-European religious traditions to [[fly-agaric-siberian-shamanic-practice]] practices
- Illustrates the role of fungi in the development of religious concepts including deity
