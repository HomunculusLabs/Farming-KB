## Overview

Gladiolus communis, commonly called common gladiolus or corn flag, is a cormous perennial native to the Mediterranean basin. It produces spikes of deep magenta-pink flowers on tall, sturdy stems that make it a striking presence in meadow and garden settings.

Unlike the large-flowered hybrid gladioli bred for the cut-flower trade, Gladiolus communis retains the graceful, wild character of the species. Its smaller flowers are more weather-resistant and naturalize freely in suitable climates.

The species has a long history of cultivation in European gardens, documented since the 16th century. It was one of the parent species used in early Gladiolus breeding programs.

The species name has been confused in horticultural literature, with subsp. byzantinus frequently sold under various names including Gladiolus byzantinus and Gladiolus communis var. byzantinus.

Gladiolus communis subsp. byzantinus is sometimes treated as a separate species and is the form most commonly encountered in cultivation. It is distinguished by slightly larger flowers and a more robust growth habit.

The species has naturalized widely outside its native range, particularly in western Europe, California, and parts of Australia. It spreads by corm offsets and self-seeding in favorable sites.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Family:** Iridaceae
- **Genus:** Gladiolus
- **Species:** Gladiolus communis L.
- **Subspecies:** subsp. communis; subsp. byzantinus (Mill.) A.P.Hammond
- **Synonyms:** Gladiolus byzantinus Mill.; Gladiolus insignis

The genus Gladiolus contains approximately 260 species, with the center of diversity in southern Africa. Gladiolus communis belongs to a small group of Eurasian and Mediterranean species.

The species epithet "communis" means "common," reflecting its widespread occurrence in the Mediterranean region. Subspecies byzantinus honors the Byzantine region of its original description.

Molecular phylogenetic studies place Gladiolus communis in a Eurasian clade distinct from the majority of African species. This clade represents an early northward migration of the genus from Africa into Eurasia.

Chromosome number is 2n = 60, a relatively high count resulting from ancient polyploidization events in the genus.

## Morphology

Plants grow 60–120 cm tall from globose, compressed corms 2–4 cm in diameter. The corm tunic is fibrous, composed of parallel vertical strands from old leaf bases.

Leaves are equitant, sword-shaped, 3–5 per plant, 30–60 cm long and 1–2.5 cm wide. The foliage is gray-green and slightly glaucous, providing an attractive foil for the flowers.

The flowering spike is unilateral, bearing 6–15 flowers on one side of the stem. Each flower is subtended by a green bract shorter than the tube.

Individual flowers are 4–6 cm long, funnel-shaped, in shades of deep magenta to purplish-pink. The three lower tepals often display a pale central streak or white blotch with darker veining.

The perianth tube is curved, 2–3 cm long. Three stamens are inserted unequally in the tube, and the style arches over them toward the lower lip.

Flowers open sequentially from bottom to top over a 2–3 week bloom period in late spring to early summer. Each flower lasts 3–5 days depending on temperature.

The fruit is an ellipsoid capsule 1.5–2.5 cm long containing numerous flat, winged seeds. Seeds are light brown, approximately 5–8 mm across, adapted for wind dispersal.

Corms persist in the soil during summer dormancy, protected by the fibrous tunic. This adaptation allows the species to survive extended drought periods characteristic of Mediterranean summers.

## Distribution and Habitat

Gladiolus communis is native to the Mediterranean basin, from Spain and Morocco eastward to Turkey and the Levant. It occurs throughout southern Europe, North Africa, and the eastern Mediterranean.

The species inhabits dry, open habitats including maquis, garrigue, olive groves, cornfields, and rocky hillsides. It prefers well-drained, calcareous soils in full sun to light shade.

Populations occur from sea level to 1200 m elevation, most commonly in areas with hot, dry summers and mild, wet winters. The species follows the classic Mediterranean geophyte [[ricinus-communis]]
