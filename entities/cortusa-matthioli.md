## Overview

Cortusa matthioli (Alpine bells) is a low-growing perennial herb in the Primulaceae family native to the mountains of central and southern Europe. It is valued for its nodding, bell-shaped pink to magenta flowers and rounded, scalloped leaves that form attractive [[query-how-to-protect-plants-from-frost-and-extend-the-growing-season]]. It prefers neutral to slightly acidic, [[humus]]-rich substrates.

Populations are typically small and scattered, reflecting the specialized habitat requirements of the species. Large colonies are rare and usually found in particularly favorable sites with reliable seepage.

The species shows a preference for north- and east-facing slopes where shade and moisture are most consistent. Populations on south-facing slopes are confined to deeply shaded rock crevices and overhangs.

Disjunct populations in the Carpathians are genetically distinct from Alpine populations and may warrant subspecific recognition. These eastern populations tend to have slightly larger flowers.

## Cultivation

Cortusa matthioli is a choice plant for the alpine house, shady rock garden, or peat bed. It requires partial to full shade, constant moisture, and excellent drainage — a combination that can be challenging to achieve in practice.

Plants thrive in a soil mix of equal parts loam, [[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]] when new growth emerges. Regular application of organic slug controls is recommended in gardens where Cortusa is cultivated.

## Ecological Role

The nodding bell-shaped flowers are adapted for buzz pollination by bumblebees, which vibrate the flowers to release pollen. This specialized pollination mechanism is unusual within Primulaceae.

Observations in the Austrian Alps have documented visitation primarily by Bombus lucorum and B. pascuorum. The flowers produce no nectar, relying entirely on pollen as a pollinator reward.
