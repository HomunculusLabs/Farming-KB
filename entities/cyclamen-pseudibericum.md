## Overview

Cyclamen pseudibericum is a tuberous perennial herb in the family Primulaceae, native to the mountains of eastern Turkey and the Caucasus. It is commonly known as the eastern cyclamen or false Iberian cyclamen.

The species produces large, fragrant magenta flowers in spring, emerging before or alongside the new foliage. It is among the most cold-hardy members of the genus Cyclamen.

Cyclamen pseudibericum was once classified as a subspecies of Cyclamen ibericum but has since been recognized as a distinct species. The two taxa differ in flower shape, petal size, and chromosomal number.

Chromosomal studies revealed that Cyclamen pseudibericum is a diploid with 2n = 30, while Cyclamen ibericum has a different chromosome count. This cytological distinction supported the elevation to full species status.

The species is highly regarded by cyclamen enthusiasts for its robust constitution and the intensity of its flower color. It has received the Royal Horticultural Society's Award of Garden Merit.

Flowers are large for the genus, measuring 2–3.5 cm long with petals that are broadly obovate and slightly twisted. The corolla color is a deep magenta or rose-purple with a darker basal blotch.

A distinctive dark purple or magenta band encircles the mouth of the corolla tube. This feature distinguishes Cyclamen pseudibericum from the closely related Cyclamen ibericum.

The petals are prominently reflexed, giving the flower a swept-back appearance typical of many Cyclamen species. The fragrance is sweet and noticeable, especially on warm days.

Each flower is held on a slender pedicel that arises from the tuber and coils tightly after fertilization. This coiling mechanism draws the developing seed capsule down to ground level for ant dispersal.

The flowering period extends from late winter through mid-spring in cultivation. Individual plants may produce 10 to 20 flowers per season under optimal conditions.

## Distribution and Habitat

Cyclamen pseudibericum is native to northeastern Turkey, particularly the Pontic Mountains near the Black Sea coast, and adjacent areas of the western Caucasus. It occurs at elevations of 200–1,200 meters.

The species grows in deciduous woodlands, rocky slopes, and shaded gullies. It is commonly found under beech, oak, and hornbeam trees in areas with rich, well-drained forest soils.

Its native climate is characterized by warm, wet summers and cold, snowy winters. This seasonal pattern differs from the Mediterranean climate preferred by most Cyclamen species.

Populations are locally abundant in suitable habitat, forming extensive carpets of flowering plants in spring. The species is threatened in some areas by forest clearance and overcollection of wild tubers.
