## Description

*Ampelomyces* is a genus of fungi best known as a **mycoparasite** — a fungus
that parasitizes other fungi. It is one of the most studied biological control
agents against **powdery mildew** (order Erysiphales). *Ampelomyces quisqualis*
is the most commonly referenced species and is commercially available as a
biocontrol product. In the context of compost tea production, *Ampelomyces* is
considered a highly beneficial organism that should be present in well-made,
aerobic compost.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Division**: Ascomycota
- **Class**: Dothideomycetes
- **Order**: Pleosporales
- **Family**: Phaeosphaeriaceae
- **Genus**: *Ampelomyces*
- **Type species**: *Ampelomyces quisqualis* Ces. ex Schltdl.

## Key Facts

### Mycoparasitic Activity

- *Ampelomyces* actively parasitizes powdery mildew fungi (order Erysiphales),
  penetrating their hyphae and conidiophores, and producing spores inside the
  host's structures.
- The more powdery mildew is present, the more *Ampelomyces* proliferates — it
  increases in proportion to its host's abundance, making it a self-regulating
  biocontrol agent.

### Habitat and Conditions

- Found naturally in **aerobic, not-overheated** compost piles.
- **Heat-sensitive**: *Ampelomyces* is not tolerant of high temperatures. If
  compost temperatures rapidly exceed 155–160 °F (68–71 °C), this beneficial
  fungus is killed. Slow heating to 165 °F may allow it to sporulate first,
  surviving in spore form until conditions improve.
- **Preferred conditions**: High humidity, high organic matter, and abundant
  fungal biomass — conditions that should naturally exist in a well-managed
  compost pile.
- Can be encouraged by adding powdery-mildew-affected leaves to compost, which
  provides host material for the parasite to feed on and multiply.

### Role in Compost Tea

- *Ampelomyces* should be present in most properly prepared, aerobic compost and
  will transfer into [[actively-aerated-compost-tea]] (AACT) made from that compost.
- It contributes to the foliar disease-suppressive properties of AACT when
  applied to plants affected by or vulnerable to powdery mildew.
- Ingham recommends inoculating compost with *Ampelomyces*-containing inoculum
  until the fungus is established in the compost system.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

### Biocontrol Applications

- **Powdery mildew management**: *Ampelomyces* offers a biological alternative to
  fungicides for powdery mildew on grapes, cucurbits, strawberries, roses, and
  many other crops.
- **Integrated approach**: Most effective when combined with good compost tea
  practices that maintain overall foliar biology, rather than applied as a
  standalone product.
- **Compost integration**: Building *Ampelomyces* populations in compost is a
  low-cost, sustainable way to maintain biocontrol capacity.

### Compost Management Implications

- Compost piles that heat too rapidly (a common mistake) may lose *Ampelomyces*
  and other heat-sensitive beneficial organisms.
- Slow, controlled heating with adequate moisture preserves a wider diversity of
  beneficial fungi including this mycoparasite.
- This is one reason why thermal compost management requires careful temperature
  monitoring — overheating destroys valuable biocontrol agents.

### Research and Commercial Status

- *Ampelomyces quisqualis* is available as commercial biocontrol formulations
  (e.g., AQ10).
- Field efficacy varies with environmental conditions — humidity and moderate
  temperatures favor the parasite.
- Best results come from preventive or early-season applications when powdery
  mildew pressure is still low.
