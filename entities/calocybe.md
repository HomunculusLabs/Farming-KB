# Calocybe

## Description

Calocybe is a small genus of mushrooms that includes [[calocybe-indica]], commonly known as the milky white mushroom or Indian mushroom. It is a tropical to subtropical species that has gained commercial importance as a warm-weather alternative to [[agaricus-bisporus]]. Like Agaricus, Calocybe can be cultivated on composted substrates and benefits from casing, but thrives at higher temperatures (25-35°C), making it suitable for tropical and subtropical climates.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Phylum**: Basidiomycota
- **Class**: Agaricomycetes
- **Order**: Agaricales
- **Family**: Lyophyllaceae
- **Genus**: Calocybe

## Key Facts

- Calocybe indica is the primary cultivated species in the genus
- Fruits on pasteurized agricultural waste products, similar to Pleurotus
- [[grain-spawn]] can be used directly for Calocybe on pasteurized substrates indoors
- Produces large, white, fleshy fruitbodies with a mild flavor
- Requires warm temperatures for fruiting (25-35°C / 77-95°F)
- One of the few mushrooms commercially cultivated at high ambient temperatures

## Relevance to Cultivation

Calocybe indica represents an important alternative crop for growers in warm climates where temperate species like Agaricus bisporus struggle. Its ability to fruit on pasteurized [[agricultural-waste-substrates]] reduces the need for energy-intensive sterilization. The cultivation process parallels that of other compost-grown mushrooms but with a simpler substrate preparation pipeline.

## Relevance to Mycology

The genus contributes to understanding tropical fungal ecology and thermotolerant fungal metabolism. Research into Calocybe cultivation provides insights into warm-climate mycology and adaptation of fungal fruiting mechanisms to higher temperature regimes.

## Techniques Referenced

- [[grain-spawn-production]] for direct inoculation of pasteurized substrates
- Pasteurized agricultural waste cultivation
- Casing soil application for improved fruiting
