## Overview

Cantharellus cinereus, commonly known as the ashen chanterelle or grey chanterelle,
is a species of ectomycorrhizal fungus in the family Cantharellaceae.

It is closely related to the golden chanterelle (C. cibarius) but is distinguished
by its darker, grey-brown to cinereous pileus and somewhat more muted coloration.

Cantharellales → Cantharellaceae → Cantharellus → C. cinereus.

The species was originally described by Persoon and has undergone several
taxonomic revisions as the genus Cantharellus has been redefined through
molecular phylogenetics.

The pileus ranges from 2 to 8 cm in diameter, convex when young and developing
a depressed center with age. The cap surface is smooth to finely tomentose,
colored grey-brown to dark smoky brown, often with paler margins.

The hymenophore consists of blunt, shallow, forked ridges that run decurrent
down the stipe, similar to other Cantharellus species but often less pronounced.

The stipe is 3 to 6 cm long and 0.5 to 1.5 cm thick, solid, and colored
similarly to the pileus or slightly paler, tapering toward the base.

hyaline, and inamyloid. The [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]], it occurs in northeastern and midwestern regions, particularly
in association with oak, beech, and spruce forests on well-drained acidic soils.

The species fruits singly or in scattered groups from mid-summer through autumn,
preferring moist, mossy forest floors with good [[ectomycorrhizal-host-tree-partnerships]] with enhanced nutrient
uptake, particularly phosphorus, in exchange for photosynthetic carbohydrates.

Molecular studies have confirmed that C. cinereus belongs to the C. cibarius
species complex, which includes several morphologically similar taxa across
different geographic regions.

This complex has undergone significant cryptic speciation, with molecular
phylogenetics revealing multiple distinct lineages previously lumped under
a single species name.
