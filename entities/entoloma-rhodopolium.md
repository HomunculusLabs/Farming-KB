The species is ectomycorrhizal, forming associations with hardwoods including
Quercus, Fagus, Betula, and Carpinus. It also occurs under conifers in mixed
woodland. It fruits in small groups on forest floors with well-developed humus
layers, preferring calcareous or neutral soils.

## Toxicity

E. rhodopolium is inedible and potentially toxic. Symptoms include nausea,
vomiting, diarrhea, and abdominal cramps, onset within 30 minutes to 2 hours.
Toxic principles are not fully characterized but may include unstable
gyromitrin-like compounds or lectins.

The primary danger is its resemblance to deadly E. sinuatum, which contains
significant toxic proteins and can cause severe, potentially fatal
gastrointestinal poisoning. Distinguishing between the two requires careful
examination of cap color, gill attachment, odor, and microscopic features.
