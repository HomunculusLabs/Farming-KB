## Overview

**Sideritis scardica** is a perennial herbaceous plant in the family Lamiaceae, commonly known as **Greek mountain tea** or **Pirin mountain tea**.

It is native to the mountainous regions of the Balkan Peninsula, where it has been consumed as a traditional herbal tea for centuries.

The species is valued for its anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and mild anxiolytic properties, and has attracted research attention as a potential cognitive enhancer.

*Sideritis scardica* is one of the most commercially important *Sideritis* species, forming the basis of a growing export market from Greece and Bulgaria.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Phylum:** Angiosperms
- **Class:** Eudicots
- **Order:** Lamiales
- **Family:** Lamiaceae
- **Genus:** *Sideritis*
- **Species:** *S. scardica*

Plants grow 20–50 cm tall with erect, woody-based stems that are densely white-tomentose.

Leaves are opposite, ovate to lanceolate, 2–5 cm long, with crenate margins and a woolly white underside.

Inflorescences are terminal, congested verticillasters forming a dense spike-like raceme, 3–10 cm long.

Calyx is campanulate, 5–7 mm, densely covered in white hairs, with five nearly equal teeth.

Corolla is yellow or pale yellow, 8–12 mm long, bilabiate with a curved upper lip and spreading lower lip.

Flowering occurs from June to August; fruit is a group of four brown nutlets, each approximately 2 mm long.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Sideritis scardica* is endemic to the mountainous regions of the central and southern Balkans.

Its native range extends through the Pirin, Rila, Rhodope, and Pindus mountain ranges across Bulgaria, Greece, North Macedonia, and Albania.

The species grows at elevations of 800–2,200 meters on limestone and siliceous substrates.

Preferred habitats include dry rocky slopes, alpine meadows, scree fields, and open forest clearings.

It is well-adapted to harsh mountain conditions including intense UV exposure, temperature extremes, and poor, shallow soils.

Wild populations have declined due to overharvesting, and the species is classified as near-threatened in parts of its range.
