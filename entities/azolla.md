# Azolla

## Description

Azolla is a genus of small, free-floating aquatic ferns that are among the most efficient nitrogen-fixing organisms known. Each Azolla plant hosts a symbiotic cyanobacterium (Anabaena azollae) within its leaf cavities that converts atmospheric nitrogen into forms usable by plants. In permaculture and sustainable agriculture, Azolla is valued as a living nitrogen factory, [[green-manure]], animal fodder, and water purification agent.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Division**: Polypodiophyta (ferns)
- **Class**: Polypodiopsida
- **Order**: Salviniales
- **Family**: Salviniaceae
- **Genus**: Azolla
- **Common names**: Mosquito fern, duckweed fern, fairy moss, water fern

## Species of Agricultural Importance

- **Azolla pinnata**: Most commonly used in Asian rice paddies
- **Azolla caroliniana**: Native to the Americas, cold-tolerant
- **Azolla filiculoides**: Large, robust species; widespread globally
- **Azolla microphylla**: Tropical species used in Southeast Asian agriculture

## Key Facts

- Fixes atmospheric nitrogen through symbiotic relationship with Anabaena azollae cyanobacteria
- Can fix 1.0-2.6 kg nitrogen per hectare per day under optimal conditions
- Doubles its biomass every 2-5 days under favorable conditions
- Contains 25-35% protein on a dry weight basis
- Used in rice cultivation for thousands of years in Asia
- Can be grown on water surfaces, making it a dual-purpose crop (water and fertility)
- Tolerates a wide range of water conditions from freshwater to slightly brackish
- Mollison notes that nitrogen can be supplied by water plants such as Azolla inoculated with rhizobia or fixed by algae

## Growth Requirements

- **Water**: Still or slow-moving fresh water, 5-15 cm depth ideal
- **Light**: Full sun to partial shade
- **Temperature**: Optimal 20-30 degrees C; varies by species
- **pH**: 5.0-7.0 preferred
- **Nutrients**: Requires phosphorus for optimal growth; minimal other inputs needed
- **Salinity**: Cannot tolerate high salinity

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

### Nitrogen Fixation
- Primary agricultural value is biological nitrogen fixation
- Can supply 20-40 kg N/ha per crop cycle when incorporated into rice paddies
- Eliminates or greatly reduces need for synthetic nitrogen fertilizers
- Nitrogen released slowly as Azolla decomposes, matching plant uptake patterns
- Continues to release nitrogen even after the host plants die and decompose

### Rice Paddy Integration
- Grown as a companion crop in flooded rice fields worldwide
- Suppresses weeds by forming a dense mat on the water surface
- Reduces water evaporation from rice paddies
- Provides habitat for beneficial aquatic organisms
- Traditional practice in Vietnam, China, and other Asian countries for centuries

### Animal Fodder
- High protein content (25-35% dry weight) makes it excellent livestock feed
- Suitable for poultry, pigs, fish, and cattle
- Can be harvested daily due to rapid growth rate
- Contains essential amino acids, vitamins, and minerals

### Green Manure and Compost
- Can be harvested and incorporated directly into soil as green manure
- Excellent addition to compost piles for nitrogen enrichment
- Decomposes rapidly, releasing nutrients quickly
- Improves soil organic matter content

### Water Purification
- Absorbs excess nutrients (nitrogen, phosphorus) from eutrophic water
- Can be used in constructed wetlands and greywater treatment systems
- Helps reduce algal blooms by competing for nutrients
- Bioaccumulates heavy metals (useful for bioremediation but caution needed)

### Mycological Connections
- Azolla can be composted to create nitrogen-rich substrates for mushroom cultivation
- Decomposing Azolla supports diverse microbial communities including beneficial fungi
- Can be incorporated into outdoor mushroom beds as a nitrogen supplement
- The rapid decomposition makes it suitable for warming compost in [[mushroom-substrate-preparation]]
