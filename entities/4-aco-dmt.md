# 4-AcO-DMT

## Overview

4-AcO-DMT (4-acetoxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine), also known as psilacetin, O-acetylpsilocin, or synthetically as 4-AcO-DMT, is a synthetic psychedelic tryptamine. It is a structural analog and putative prodrug of [[psilocin]] (4-HO-DMT), the active metabolite of psilocybin. First synthesized by Albert Hofmann at Sandoz Laboratories in the 1960s, 4-AcO-DMT produces subjective effects nearly identical to those of psilocybin mushrooms and is widely regarded as one of the closest synthetic equivalents to natural psilocybin. It has gained significant attention as a research chemical and in clinical contexts as a potential alternative to naturally derived psilocybin.

## Chemical Properties

- **IUPAC Name:** 3-[2-(Dimethylamino)ethyl]-1H-indol-4-yl acetate
- **Molecular Formula:** C₁₄H₁₈N₂O₂
- **Molecular Weight:** 246.31 g/mol
- **CAS Number:** 92292-87-6
- **Appearance:** White to off-white crystalline powder
- **Solubility:** Soluble in ethanol, DMSO, and dilute acids; moderately soluble in water
- **Stability:** Relatively stable as a solid under cool, dark, dry storage. The acetoxy group is susceptible to hydrolysis in aqueous solution, particularly under acidic or basic conditions.

Structurally, 4-AcO-DMT is identical to [[psilocin]] (4-HO-DMT) and acetic acid. This conversion is rapid and efficient, meaning the subjective experience is essentially that of psilocin.
- **Primary receptor activity (via psilocin):** Partial agonist at the 5-HT₂A serotonin receptor — the primary site responsible for classical psychedelic effects.
- **Additional receptor interactions:** 5-HT₁A (partial agonist), 5-HT₂C (agonist), and 5-HT₂B (weak agonist) — all mediated through the psilocin metabolite.
- **Direct receptor activity:** Some evidence suggests 4-AcO-DMT may have weak direct receptor affinity before deacetylation, though this is considered negligible compared to its activity as a prodrug.

The pharmacological profile is essentially indistinguishable from that of psilocybin, which itself is a prodrug of psilocin (dephosphorylated rather than deacetylated).

## Dosage and Administration

- **Threshold dose:** 2–5 mg, orally
- **Light dose:** 5–10 mg, orally
- **Common dose:** 10–25 mg, orally
- **Strong dose:** 25–40 mg, orally
- **Heavy dose:** 40+ mg, orally

Oral administration is the most common route. The compound is typically consumed as freebase powder, though fumarate and hydrochloride salt forms are also available. The fumarate salt is approximately 28% less potent by weight than the freebase due to the mass of the fumarate counterion.

## Effects Profile

The subjective effects of 4-AcO-DMT are consistently reported as nearly identical to those of psilocybin mushrooms:

- **Visual effects:** Enhanced colors, geometric patterns, visual distortions, closed-eye imagery, and at higher doses, immersive hallucinatory states
- **Cognitive effects:** Altered thought patterns, enhanced introspection, philosophical or spiritual insights, synesthesia-like experiences, and shifts in perception of time
- **Emotional effects:** Euphoria, anxiety (dose-dependent), sense of connectedness, emotional release, and in therapeutic contexts, profound cathartic experiences
- **Somatic effects:** Nausea (common during onset, diminishing as experience progresses), pupil dilation, changes in body temperature perception, yawning, and restlessness
- **Duration:** 4–7 hours, depending on dose and individual metabolism
- **Onset:** 20–60 minutes after oral administration
- **Peak:** 1.5–3 hours post-ingestion
- **Comedown:** 2–4 hours with gradual return to baseline

## Comparison with Psilocybin

The relationship between 4-AcO-DMT and psilocybin has been extensively discussed in the research chemical and psychedelic communities:

- **Prodrug status:** Both compounds serve as prodrugs of psilocin, but via different metabolic pathways: 4-AcO-DMT is deacetylated, while psilocybin is dephosphorylated. The end result is pharmacologically identical.
- **Subjective equivalence:** The overwhelming consensus among experienced users is that the subjective effects are indistinguishable from psilocybin mushrooms. Some report that 4-AcO-DMT is slightly "cleaner" — with less body load and nausea — possibly due to the absence of the additional alkaloids (baeocystin, norbaeocystin) present in natural mushroom preparations.
- **Onset:** Some reports suggest a marginally faster onset compared to psilocybin, consistent with the generally faster enzymatic hydrolysis of ester bonds compared to phosphate ester bonds.
- **Dose equivalence:** Approximately equimolar potency with psilocybin by weight when both are in freebase/acid form, though exact equivalencies depend on the salt form used.
- **Purity advantage:** As a synthesized compound, 4-AcO-DMT offers precise dosing control without the variability in alkaloid content that characterizes natural mushroom material.

## History and Discovery

- **1960s:** First synthesized by Albert Hofmann and Franz Troxler at Sandoz Laboratories as part of their systematic exploration of tryptamine derivatives. The compound was assigned the Sandoz research code but was not developed commercially, as Sandoz focused its attention on psilocybin itself.
- **1999:** The compound was independently investigated by David E. Nichols and colleagues at Purdue University, who characterized its pharmacological properties and confirmed its prodrug status.
- **2000s:** 4-AcO-DMT emerged in the research chemical market as a legal alternative to psilocybin in jurisdictions where it was not specifically scheduled. It gained popularity through online communities and harm reduction resources like Erowid.
- **2010s–2020s:** Growing interest in clinical applications as a potential substitute for psilocybin in therapeutic research, given its identical pharmacological profile and advantages in terms of synthesis, purity, and dosing precision. The compound is referenced in Alexander Shulgin's TiHKAL, which documents tryptamine chemistry.

## Synthesis

The synthesis of 4-AcO-DMT involves the acetylation of 4-hydroxyindole or psilocin:

- **From psilocin:** Direct acetylation of the 4-hydroxyl group using acetic anhydride or acetyl chloride under basic conditions yields 4-AcO-DMT. This is the most straightforward route.
- **From 4-acetoxyindole:** The indole can be functionalized at the 3-position to introduce the dimethylaminoethyl side chain, as described in the synthetic routes documented in Shulgin's TiHKAL for related 4-acetoxy tryptamines.
- **Synthetic accessibility:** 4-AcO-DMT is considered easier and more cost-effective to synthesize than psilocybin, as the acetoxy group is simpler to introduce than the phosphate ester. This has implications for clinical research where GMP-grade material is required.

### Related 4-Acetoxy Tryptamines

Shulgin's TiHKAL documents several related 4-acetoxy-substituted tryptamines that provide context for understanding the pharmacology of 4-AcO-DMT:

- **4-AcO-DET** (4-acetoxy-N,N-diethyltryptamine): Synthesized from 4-acetoxyindole via the indoleglyoxylamide intermediate and LAH reduction. Higher doses required (>20 mg) and limited subjective effects in trials.
- **4-AcO-DiPT** (4-acetoxy-N,N-diisopropyltryptamine): Referenced in PIHKAL's psychoactive matrix as a known compound, though detailed pharmacological characterization remains limited.
- **4-AcO-MET** (4-acetoxy-N-methyl-N-ethyltryptamine): A closely related compound with similar prodrug properties, producing effects comparable to 4-AcO-DMT with slight differences in duration and intensity.
- The systematic exploration of 4-substituted tryptamines by the Shulgins and subsequent researchers has established that the 4-acetoxy substitution consistently serves as an effective prodrug strategy for delivering 4-hydroxy tryptamines to the brain.

## Legal Status

Legal status varies significantly by jurisdiction and has evolved rapidly:

- **Controlled in many countries:** Often scheduled as a psilocybin analog under analog drug laws, or specifically listed as a controlled substance. Controlled in the UK (Class A), Australia, New Zealand, and several European countries.
- **United States:** Not specifically scheduled at the federal level under the Controlled Substances Act, but may be prosecuted under the Federal Analog Act as a controlled substance analog of psilocybin. Several states have specifically scheduled it.
- **Research exemption:** The compound's status as a potential clinical tool has led to discussions about regulatory frameworks that would allow its use in therapeutic research under controlled conditions.

## Safety and Harm Reduction

- **Physical safety:** 4-AcO-DMT has low physiological toxicity. No deaths have been directly attributed to the compound. However, as with all serotonergic psychedelics, there are theoretical risks for individuals with personal or family history of psychotic disorders or severe cardiovascular conditions.
- **Psychological safety:** As with psilocybin, the most significant risk factor is the set (mindset) and setting (environment). Challenging experiences ("bad trips") can occur, particularly at high doses, in unprepared individuals, or in unsupportive environments.
- **Harm reduction practices:** Start with low doses, use in a safe and comfortable environment, have a trusted sitter present, avoid combining with other substances (particularly alcohol, stimulants, and other serotonergic drugs), and allow adequate time between experiences.
- **Testing:** Reagent testing can help identify 4-AcO-DMT, though results may be similar to other indole-based compounds. Analytical testing (GC-MS, LC-MS) is required for definitive identification and purity assessment.

## Research and Clinical Potential

4-AcO-DMT has attracted growing interest in the clinical psychedelic research community as a potential alternative to naturally derived psilocybin:

- **Manufacturing advantages:** Synthetic production is more cost-effective and scalable than extraction from mushrooms, with better batch-to-batch consistency and easier GMP compliance
- **Pharmacological equivalence:** The identical end-product (psilocin) means clinical data from psilocybin trials is directly applicable to 4-AcO-DMT research
- **Dosing precision:** Pure synthetic material eliminates the variability in alkaloid content inherent in mushroom preparations
- **Regulatory pathway:** Some researchers argue that 4-AcO-DMT could follow a simplified regulatory path as a new molecular entity, avoiding the complex legal status of Schedule I psilocybin in some jurisdictions
- **Ongoing research:** Several research organizations are investigating 4-AcO-DMT for use in psychedelic-assisted psychotherapy, though no large-scale clinical trials had been published as of 2025
