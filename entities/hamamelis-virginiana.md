# Hamamelis virginiana (Witch Hazel)

## Overview

*Hamamelis virginiana*, commonly known as witch hazel, is a deciduous
shrub or small tree native to [[polypore-fungi-eastern-north-america]] America and the sole
[[growing-gourmet-north-american-reishi-species]] representative of the Hamamelidaceae family. It is
among the most widely used botanicals in commercial skincare and
topical therapeutics, valued for its astringent, anti-inflammatory,
and antioxidant properties. Witch hazel hydrosol (distillate) is one
of the few botanical products that remains a standard ingredient in
both conventional dermatology and traditional [[childrens-herbal-medicine]].

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Division:** Magnoliophyta
- **Class:** Magnoliopsida
- **Order:** Fagales
- **Family:** Hamamelidaceae
- **Genus:** *Hamamelis*
- **Species:** *H. virginiana* L. (1753)

**Etymology:** "Hamamelis" derives from Greek *hama* (together) and
*melon* (fruit), referring to the simultaneous presence of flowers and
fruit. "Virginiana" denotes its origin in colonial Virginia.

## Morphology

**Habit:** Deciduous multi-stemmed shrub or small tree, 3–6 m tall,
occasionally to 9 m. Spreading, irregular crown.

**Bark:** Smooth, thin, grayish-brown on young branches, becoming
scaly on older trunks.

**Leaves:** Simple, alternate, 8–15 cm long, broadly ovate with an
uneven cordate base. Coarsely serrate margins. Dark green above,
paler below with stellate pubescence along veins. Yellow-orange
to red autumn coloration.

**Flowers:** The most distinctive feature — fragrant, spider-like
flowers with 4 narrow, crumpled yellow petals (1.5 cm long) blooming
in October–November, after leaf drop. Each flower has 4 short stamens.
Blooming occurs when most other plants are dormant.

**Fruit:** Woody, two-valved capsule, 1–1.5 cm long, maturing the
following year. Explosive dehiscence ejects shiny black seeds up to
several meters.

## Distribution and Habitat

Native to eastern [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]], from Nova Scotia west to Minnesota,
south to eastern Texas and northern Florida.

Found in understory of deciduous forests, woodland margins, stream
banks, and moist slopes. Tolerates a range of soil conditions from
sandy loams to clay, preferring slightly acidic soils.

Hardy to USDA zones 3–8. Shade-tolerant but flowers more prolifically
in partial sun.

## Phytochemistry

The primary bioactive constituents are tannins (5–10% in bark):
- **Hamamelitannin** — a galloylated proanthocyanidin, unique to
  *Hamamelis* species, responsible for the primary astringent activity
- **Gallotannins** — hydrolyzable tannins contributing to astringency
- **Proanthocyanidins** — oligomeric polyphenols with antioxidant
  and anti-inflammatory activity
- **Flavonoids** — [[quercetin]], [[kaempferol]], and their glycosides
- **Volatile oils** — trace amounts contributing to the characteristic
