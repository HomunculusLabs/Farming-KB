# Ganoderma resinaceum

## Description

Ganoderma resinaceum is a lacquered [[polypore]] mushroom in the Ganoderma genus, classified among the reishi group of medicinal fungi. The species name "resinaceum" refers to the resinous, varnished appearance of the cap surface, a hallmark of the Ganoderma genus. Found in temperate regions, G. resinaceum grows as a wood-decay fungus on hardwood trees and is valued alongside other reishi species for its medicinal properties. It is one of several Ganoderma species commonly cultivated for both medicinal extract production and ornamental fruitbody development. Like all reishi species, it has been revered in traditional Chinese medicine as one of the "mushrooms of immortality" for thousands of years, particularly beneficial for those who suffer from sugar regulation complications such as diabetes, liver ailments, and immune dysfunction.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Phylum**: Basidiomycota
- **Class**: Agaricomycetes
- **Order**: Polyporales
- **Family**: Ganodermataceae
- **Genus**: Ganoderma
- **Species**: G. resinaceum

## Key Facts

- One of the common reishi species listed alongside G. applanatum (artist conk), G. curtisii, G. lucidum, G. oregonense, and G. tsugae (hemlock reishi)
- Shares the genus-wide difficulty ratings: outdoor cultivation difficulty 1, indoor cultivation difficulty 2
- Versatile hardwood substrate compatibility, consistent with most Ganoderma species except G. tsugae which prefers hemlock
- Produces the characteristic lacquered, varnished cap surface typical of reishi mushrooms
- [[mycelium]] is white and linear, hugging agar surfaces flat, with age producing a signature yellow metabolite darkening to orange
- Fruitbody begins as an antler with whitish tip, flattening to an off-center cap with white pores underneath
- [[spore-print]] is cinnamon to chocolate brown
- Aggressive mycelium with inherent antibiotic and antifungal properties that outcompete wild organisms
- The growing edge is white or off-yellow when actively growing, dulling and darkening at full maturity
- Spores typically cover the entire cap surface due to convecting air currents

## Mycelium and Spawn

The mycelium is white and linear, lying almost flat on agar surfaces. With age it produces a signature yellow metabolite darkening to orange. The smell can be sour and somewhat unpleasant, similar to other Ganoderma species. Commercial spawn is typically available in the form of cereal grains, supplemented sawdusts, and wooden dowels for outdoor cultivation. Cloning from the stem attachment point works best. Spore germinations are possible but less reliable and typically covered with contaminating yeast. The culture should be transferred from agar before it reaches plate edges, as it becomes difficult to cut cleanly and usually peels up when dragged with a scalpel.

## Cultivation Notes

### Outdoor Cultivation

The mushroom will not fruit aboveground, so buried hardwood logs and stumps are essential. Inoculate with sawdust, plug, or cardboard spawn. The trenched log method works well: inoculate small-diameter logs, colonize for six to eight months, then completely bury them packed tightly together as a raft. Antlers typically form at the interface between ground and wood on the shadier sides. Inoculation to fruiting: ten to twelve months. Reishis typically fruit in the spring and keep producing all summer, persisting into the fall. Expected outdoor yields are good; reishi mushrooms are dense and prolific fruiters.

### Indoor Cultivation

Indoor cultivation on supplemented sawdust in autoclave-sterilized bags produces antlers in as little as two to three weeks after colonization. Antlers elongate inside closed bags under elevated carbon dioxide until the bag is opened for cap flattening and sporulation. Preferred spawn types are grain or supplemented sawdust spawn. Expected indoor yields are good. Spawn can transform into solid bricks at room temperature; some growers have made bricks from the mycelium and used them to construct living structures demonstrating the fungus's strength.

## Harvesting and Storage

Mushrooms are slow to grow and can be harvested at any stage. Antlers may contain more beneficial medicinal properties than fully flattened specimens, though this has not been substantiated. Mature specimens with growing edge still present, harvested just before spore release, dry and preserve well. Fresh reishi keeps for one month at 38 to 42 F; under prolonged refrigeration molds can infect the tube layer. The best preservation method is to dry and powder or make medicinal extracts while fresh.

## Marketing

These mushrooms may look odd to first-time users. Bringing a fruiting kit of beautiful antlers and describing how to make tinctures is effective. At organic growers' conferences medicinal species sell poorly, whereas at herbal conferences or natural healing seminars reishi stock usually sells out. Citing scientific journals for health benefits helps the public gain respect for these fungi.

## Medicinal Properties

Ganoderma species have one of the highest beta-glucan levels of any medicinal fungus. Traditional Chinese medicine uses include treatment of diabetes through blood sugar regulation, liver ailments, immune dysfunction, and as a general cure-all. Studies demonstrate immune modulation, blood pressure regulation, cholesterol management, cardiovascular and digestive support, antiviral and antibacterial properties, anti-inflammatory compounds, and suppression of multiple cancer cell lines. Blood sugar regulation is the best-known mechanism, making this mushroom one of the best natural treatments for diabetes patients.

## Mycoremediation Applications

The mycelium has extremely high tensile strength and an affinity for inhibiting and lysing bacterial cells, making it suitable for [[mycofiltration-of-water]]. Myceliated substrate can form an almost rubber-like material that can be dried and cut into any shape for manufactured filter modules. Its ability to bind substrate tenaciously helps keep biofilters from disintegrating.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Ganoderma resinaceum expands the genetic diversity of reishi available to cultivators. Its ease of cultivation, aggressive mycelial growth, and strong medicinal profile make it a valuable addition to any medicinal mushroom operation. The species is a great starting fungus for gaining experience with the fungal life cycle before taking on more difficult species. Even growers without the luxury of a laboratory can expand a bag of supplemented sawdust spawn to wood chips and achieve near-commercial-grade fruiting yields.
