## Overview

**Coffea canephora**, known as Robusta coffee, is a species of flowering plant in the family Rubiaceae native to [[fungal-biodiversity-tropical-forests]] of Central and West Africa.

It is the second most widely cultivated coffee species in the world after [[coffea-arabica]], accounting for approximately 40% of global coffee production.

The species is valued for its hardiness, disease resistance, higher caffeine content, and ability to grow at lower elevations than Arabica.

It is the primary source of instant coffee, espresso blends, and contributes body and crema in traditional Italian espresso.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Coffea canephora** Pierre ex A.Froehner was described by Pierre in 1895.

The genus Coffea contains approximately 124 species, though only C. arabica and C. canephora are commercially significant at scale.

Coffea canephora is a diploid species (2n = 22), whereas C. arabica is an allotetraploid (2n = 44).

The plant grows as a shrub or small tree, typically reaching 3–10 meters in height under cultivation.

In the wild, it can exceed 15 meters, forming part of the sub-canopy in tropical lowland forests.

Leaves are evergreen, elliptic to oblong, 10–20 cm long and 4–8 cm wide, with a pronounced central vein.

Flowers are produced in axillary clusters, white to cream-colored, with a tubular corolla approximately 1–1.5 cm long.

Flowers are hermaphroditic and primarily self-incompatible, requiring cross-pollination for optimal fruit set.

Fruit is a drupe (cherry), 10–14 mm in diameter, ripening from green to red or purple over 8–11 months.

Each cherry contains two seeds (beans), flat on one side with a characteristic central groove.

Robusta beans are smaller and rounder than Arabica beans, with a straighter central crease.

## Distribution and Habitat

The species is native to a belt of equatorial African forest stretching from Guinea and Liberia in the west to Uganda and western Tanzania in the east.

It occurs naturally in lowland tropical forests from sea level to approximately 1200 meters elevation.

The center of [[biodiversity-fungal-genetic-diversity]] is in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Uganda, and Cameroon.

It grows as an understory shrub in its native habitat, tolerating partial shade and higher temperatures than C. arabica.

Optimal growing temperatures range from 24–30°C, with minimum temperatures not below 15°C.

Annual rainfall requirements are 1200–2500 mm, distributed relatively evenly throughout the year.

It is cultivated extensively in Vietnam (the world's largest Robusta producer), Brazil, Indonesia, Uganda, India, and West Africa.

## Cultivation

Coffea canephora is propagated both by seed and vegetatively through cuttings and somatic embryogenesis.

Clonal propagation through cuttings is widely practiced, particularly in Vietnam and Brazil, to maintain selected genotype characteristics.

Selected clones (e.g., the Conilon varieties in Brazil) offer improved yield, disease resistance, and cup quality.

Plants begin bearing fruit at 2–3 years of age and reach full production by 5–7 years.

Yields under good [[mollison-game-species-management-range]] from 1.5–3.0 tonnes of green bean per hectare per year, substantially exceeding typical Arabica yields.

The species is significantly more resistant to coffee leaf rust (Hemileia vastatrix) and coffee berry disease than C. arabica.

It is also more tolerant of root-knot nematodes and the coffee borer beetle (Hypothenemus hampei).

Harvesting is typically done by stripping all cherries from the branch at once, as Robusta cherries ripen less uniformly than Arabica.

Post-harvest processing is primarily by the dry method (natural), though wet processing is also used for premium Robusta lots.
