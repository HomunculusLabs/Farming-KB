## Stropharia

## Description

Stropharia is a genus of mushrooms that includes Stropharia rugoso-annulata, commonly known as the [[king-stropharia]] or [[wine-cap]] mushroom. It is one of the largest cultivated mushrooms and is popular for outdoor cultivation on wood chips and straw. Stropharia species are notable for requiring or benefiting from microbial interactions in [[casing-soil]] to trigger fruitbody formation, making them a model system for studying fungal-microbial symbiosis in mushroom cultivation.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Phylum**: Basidiomycota
- **Class**: Agaricomycetes
- **Order**: Agaricales
- **Family**: Strophariaceae
- **Genus**: Stropharia

## Key Facts

- Stropharia rugoso-annulata (king stropharia / wine cap) is the primary cultivated species
- One of the largest edible mushrooms, with caps reaching 30 cm (12 inches) or more
- Fruits on wood chips, straw, and lignocellulosic substrates, often outdoors
- Requires or benefits from microbial casing interactions to trigger fruiting
- Used as a model organism for dilution-to-extinction assays to identify fruiting-triggering microbes
- Can be fruited from sterilized sawdust topped with microbially-treated casing soil
- Distinctive ring (annulus) features on the stem give the species its name

## Relevance to Cultivation

Stropharia cultivation is unique among wood-decomposing species because it benefits from microbial casing soil, similar to Agaricus. The dilution-to-extinction method using serial dilutions of soil from wild stropharia stem bases has been demonstrated as an effective technique for identifying microbial communities that trigger fruiting. This makes Stropharia important for developing microbial inoculation strategies applicable to other difficult-to-cultivate mushrooms.

## Relevance to Mycology

Stropharia serves as a key model for understanding fungal-microbial interactions in fruiting. The observation that sterilized casing prevents fruiting while pasteurized or microbially-enriched casing promotes it provides evidence for complex signaling mechanisms between fungi and microbial partners. Research using PCR and DGGE on Stropharia casing soils helps identify keystone microbial species involved in fruiting triggers.

## Techniques Referenced

- [[dilution-to-extinction]] for microbial isolation
- Casing dilution assay (12-dilution serial protocol)
- Microbial slurry preparation from stem base soil
- Sterilized sawdust cultivation with casing
