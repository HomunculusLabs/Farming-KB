Marginal teeth are prominent, triangular, and coral-colored, spaced 10–15 mm apart.

Leaf margins are lined with a pinkish or reddish edge, especially when grown in full sun.

Internal leaf gel is clear to pale yellow, mucilaginous, and bitter-tasting.

### Flowers

Inflorescences are terminal, racemose panicles rising 40–80 cm above the foliage.

Tubular flowers are 30–35 mm long, bright red-orange to scarlet, rarely yellow.

Flowering occurs primarily in winter (June–August in the [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-south-america]].

In Japan, it has been cultivated for centuries and is one of the most commonly grown medicinal plants in household gardens.

## Bioactive Compounds

The leaf gel contains polysaccharides, predominantly acemannan (acetylated polymannan), which is responsible for many immunomodulatory effects.

Anthraquinone derivatives including aloin (barbaloin), aloe-emodin, and chrysophanol are concentrated in the leaf exudate (latex) beneath the rind.

[[aact-tannins-terpenes-phenolic-compounds-wood-composting-ingham]] include flavonoids, chromones, and coumarins.

Minerals present include calcium, magnesium, zinc, and selenium in biologically significant concentrations.

Vitamins A, C, E, and B-complex vitamins have been detected in leaf gel preparations.
