## Description

**[[metarhizium]] flavoviride** is an entomopathogenic fungus in the family Clavicipitaceae that has been investigated as a biocontrol agent against ticks and other arthropod pests. It has shown superior efficacy against ticks compared to the more commonly studied M. anisopliae.









## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Phylum**: [[ascomycota]]
- **Class**: Sordariomycetes
- **Order**: Hypocreales
- **Family**: Clavicipitaceae
- **Genus**: Metarhizium
- **Species**: M. flavoviride









## Key Facts

- Onofre et al. (2001) demonstrated superior efficacy against the cattle tick [[boophilus-microplus]] compared to M. anisopliae
- LD50 values ranged from 3.4 x 10^5 to 4.2 x 10^5 conidia/ml (approximately 100x more potent than M. anisopliae)
- Reduces both larval emergence from tick eggs and reproductive efficiency
- At 10^8 conidia/ml, achieved 63-78% control of engorged adult ticks
- Two isolates (CG-291 and MF-3) showed consistently high virulence









## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

M. flavoviride represents a promising biocontrol agent with demonstrated superiority over the more widely used M. anisopliae for certain target pests. For mycologists, it illustrates the importance of species-level and isolate-level selection in biocontrol development. The significant difference in LD50 between species highlights the need for systematic screening of [[fungal-biodiversity]] for biocontrol applications.









## Ecological Role

As a soil-dwelling entomopathogenic fungus, M. flavoviride contributes to natural regulation of arthropod populations. Its conidia persist in the soil environment and infect suitable hosts upon contact, demonstrating the ecological role of fungi as natural enemies of arthropods.
