## Design Principles

A duck tractor is a lightweight, predator-proof enclosure that can be moved
by one person across a landscape. Key design elements include:

- **Floorless base** — allows direct contact between ducks and soil,
  enabling foraging, manure deposition, and pest consumption
- **Weather protection** — enclosed or partially enclosed shelter area
  providing shade, windbreak, and rain protection
- **Mobility** — handles, wheels, or skids allowing daily or every-other-day
  relocation across the target area
- **Predator security** — hardware cloth or welded wire mesh on all openings,
  secure latches, and a fully enclosed run area to protect from raccoons,
  foxes, and aerial predators
- **Water access** — ducks require water for drinking and foraging; shallow
  containers or splash pools are typically included within or adjacent to
  the tractor

Typical dimensions are 1.5–3 m wide by 2–4 m long, housing 4–12 ducks
depending on size. The structure should provide a minimum of 0.4 m² per bird
in the enclosed area.

## Species Selection

Several duck breeds are well-suited to tractor systems:

- **Khaki Campbell** — prolific egg layer (250–340 eggs/year), active
  forager, moderate size, widely available
- **Indian Runner** — upright stance, excellent forager, good egg
  production (200–300 eggs/year), low feed requirements
- **Welsh Harlequin** — calm temperament, good dual-purpose bird,
  240–330 eggs/year
- **Muscovy** — large, quiet, excellent insect forager, low water
  requirements, good meat bird, roosts on perches
