## Paraglomus occultum

## Description

*[[paraglomus]] occultum* is a species of [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-colonization-patterns]] (AM) fungus in the genus *Paraglomus*, family Paraglomaceae. It is one of only two species in this small but phylogenetically significant genus within the order Glomales. Along with *[[archaeospora]]*, *Paraglomus* represents a deeply divergent lineage of AM fungi that was only recognized as distinct through molecular phylogenetic analysis of ribosomal DNA sequences.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Zygomycota / Glomeromycota
- **Order:** Glomales
- **Family:** Paraglomaceae
- **Genus:** *Paraglomus*
- **Species:** *P. occultum*

## Key Facts

- One of only two described species in the genus *Paraglomus*
- The family Paraglomaceae contains a single genus (*Paraglomus*) and was erected based on distinct rDNA primer sequences (Morton and Redecker 2001)
- Distributed globally, similar to other phylogenetically ancient AM fungal species
- The genus was delimited only recently using selective rDNA primer sequences in polymerase chain reactions
- Additional species presently classified in other genera are expected to be transferred to *Paraglomus* as molecular analyses continue
- The species epithet "occultum" (hidden) reflects the difficulty of detecting these fungi using traditional morphological methods alone

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

*Paraglomus occultum* is significant for [[mycological-research-institutions]] because:

- It represents one of the most basal lineages within Glomales, providing critical data for reconstructing the evolutionary history of AM symbiosis
- Its discovery through molecular rather than morphological methods highlights the limitations of traditional taxonomy for AM fungi
- Demonstrates that cryptic, phylogenetically distinct lineages of AM fungi exist globally but remain undersampled and poorly characterized
- Relevant to biodiversity surveys, as *Paraglomus* species are frequently detected in trap cultures from diverse habitats (see Table 15.1 in source)
- The genus illustrates the ongoing taxonomic revision of Glomales driven by molecular phylogenetics
