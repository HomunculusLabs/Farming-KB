# Camellia sinensis
Camellia sinensis, the tea plant, is an evergreen shrub or small tree in the
family Theaceae native to East and Southeast Asia. It is the source of all
true teas—white, green, oolong, black, and pu-erh—differentiated by their
processing methods rather than botanical variety. The plant has been cultivated
for over 4,000 years and is now grown commercially across tropical and
subtropical regions worldwide, making it one of the most economically
important beverage crops on Earth. Tea is consumed by an estimated 2.5 billion
people daily, and global production exceeds 6 million tonnes annually. The
plant's pharmacological significance derives from its rich polyphenol content,
particularly catechins and caffeine, which underpin both its stimulating and
health-promoting properties.
## Taxonomy and Naming
- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Division:** Magnoliophyta
- **Class:** Magnoliopsida
- **Order:** Ericales
- **Family:** Theaceae
- **Genus:** Camellia
- **Species:** C. sinensis (L.) Kuntze, 1887
- **Basionym:** Thea sinensis L., 1753
- **Common names:** Tea plant, tea shrub, tea tree (not to be confused with
  Melaleuca alternifolia)
- **Two major varieties:**
  - *C. sinensis* var. *sinensis* — Chinese type, smaller leaves, more
    cold-tolerant, used for green and white teas
  - *C. sinensis* var. *assamica* (J.W. Mast.) Kitam. — Assam type, larger
    leaves, tropical, used for black tea and pu-erh
## Description
### Growth Habit and Morphology
- **Habit:** Evergreen shrub, 1–2 m when cultivated; can reach 10–15 m as
  an unpruned tree in the wild
- **Leaves:** Alternate, elliptic to oblong, 4–15 cm long, 2–5 cm wide;
  leathery, serrate margin with 7–12 teeth per cm; dark glossy green
  above, paler below; young leaves are pubescent (hairy), a trait prized
  in some tea grades
- **Flowers:** Solitary or in clusters of 2–3 in leaf axils; white to
  pale pink, 2.5–4 cm diameter; 5–7 petals, numerous yellow stamens;
  fragrant; blooming in autumn
- **Fruit:** Capsule, 1.5–2 cm diameter, 3-lobed, containing 1–3
  brown spherical seeds
- **Roots:** Taproot system in seedlings; lateral root dominance in
  vegetatively propagated plants
- **Bark:** Grey-brown, rough on older stems
### Environmental Requirements
| Parameter | Optimal Range | Notes |
|-----------|--------------|-------|
| Temperature | 10–30°C annual | Var. sinensis tolerates -5°C; var. assamica requires >15°C |
| Rainfall | 1150–1400 mm/year | Even distribution preferred; dry season tolerated |
| Elevation | 0–2200 m | Higher altitude = slower growth, more catechins |
| Soil pH | 4.5–5.5 (acidic) | Intolerant of alkaline soils and waterlogging |
| Sunlight | Full sun to partial shade | Shade-growing increases theanine, reduces catechins |

## Distribution and Habitat
- **Native range:** East Asia, spanning from the eastern Himalayas through
  southern China, northern Myanmar, northern Laos, and northern Vietnam
- **Current cultivation:** Over 50 countries across six continents;
  major producers include China, India, Kenya, Sri Lanka, Turkey, Vietnam,
  Indonesia, and Japan
- **Habitat:** Montane and submontane forests, forest margins, and
  stream banks; thrives in humid, foggy microclimates
- **Introduced regions:** East Africa (Kenya, Malawi), South America
  (Argentina), the Caucasus, and parts of Oceania
