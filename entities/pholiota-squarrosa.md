Although P. squarrosa is technically edible after thorough cooking, it is
generally considered unpalatable due to its bitter taste and slimy texture.
Some individuals report adverse reactions, and it should be approached with
caution by foragers.

## Taxonomy and Classification

Basidiomycota → Agaricomycetes → Agaricales → **Strophariaceae → *Pholiota → *P. squarrosa***

- **Author:** (Pers.) P. Kumm. (1871).
- **Basionym:** Agaricus squarrosus O.F. Müll. (1774).
- **Common names:** Shaggy scalycap, scaly Pholiota, tufted scalycap.
- The species epithet *squarrosa* refers to the upright, recurved scales
  covering the cap surface.

## Morphology

- **Cap:** 3–10 cm diameter, convex to broadly convex, sometimes with a
  slight umbo. Surface covered with erect, pointed, tawny to brown recurved
  scales on a pale yellow to buff background. Scales are dry and do not wash
  off easily in rain — a key distinction from the similar P. squarrosoides.
- **Gills:** Adnexed to adnate, initially pale yellow, becoming rusty brown
  to cinnamon-brown with maturity as spores mature. Crowded.
- **Stipe:** 4–12 cm long, 0.5–1.5 cm thick, cylindrical with a slightly
  clavate base. Surface pale yellow below the ring zone, covered with scales
  similar to those on the cap. [[comparison-spore-print-collection-vs-spore-syringe-production]]:** Rusty brown to cinnamon-brown (Ferruginous).
- **Spores:** Smooth, ellipsoid, 5.5–7 × 3.5–4.5 µm, with a germ pore.
- **Chemical reactions:** Flesh may stain brownish with KOH.
