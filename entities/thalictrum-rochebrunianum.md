## Overview

*Thalictrum rochebrunianum* Franch. & Sav., commonly known as lavender mist meadow-rue or Japanese meadow-rue, is a tall herbaceous perennial in the family Ranunculaceae. It is native to Japan, where it grows in mountainous woodlands and moist meadows.

The species is prized for its airy sprays of small, pendulous, lavender-purple flowers held on slender, dark stems. The overall effect in the garden is a delicate cloud of color, giving rise to the common name "lavender mist."

Unlike many *Thalictrum* species, it is dioecious, with separate male and female plants. Both sexes produce attractive flowers, though male plants tend to have showier, more pendulous blooms.

The plant forms elegant clumps of finely divided, maidenhead fern-like foliage. The leaves provide a textural contrast to bolder-leaved perennials in shaded borders.

Flowering occurs in mid to late summer (July–August), a valuable time for garden color. The blooms are followed by attractive seed heads on female plants.

The species was introduced to Western horticulture in the late 19th century and has since become a garden favorite. Its airy texture and tall stature make it irreplaceable in the shaded border.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:** Kingdom Plantae → Order Ranunculales → Family Ranunculaceae → Genus *Thalictrum* → Section *Leptostigma* → Species *T. rochebrunianum*. The species is named after Alphonse Trémeau de Rochebrune (1840–1912), a French botanist.

Plants reach 120–200 cm in height, making this one of the taller meadow-rue species. Stems are slender, dark purple-green, glabrous, and branched in the inflorescence.

Leaves are 2–4 times pinnate with small, rounded, blue-green leaflets. The leaflets are 5–15 mm broad, cuneate at the base, and shallowly lobed at the apex.

The inflorescence is a large, open, branching panicle 30–60 cm across. Individual flowers are small (8–12 mm), pendulous, with 4–5 lavender-purple sepals and no petals.

Male flowers have numerous pendulous stamens with conspicuous, club-shaped, lavender-purple filaments. Female flowers have several pistils with styles that elongate into feathery structures at maturity.

The fruit is an ovoid achene 3–4 mm long with prominent longitudinal ribs. Achenes are ridged and slightly winged, aiding wind dispersal.

The roots are fibrous and form a dense, non-invasive clump over time. The root system does not produce stolons or runners, keeping the plant well-behaved in garden settings.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Thalictrum rochebrunianum* is endemic to Japan, occurring on the islands of Honshu, Shikoku, and Kyushu. It is absent from Hokkaido and the Ryukyu Islands.

The species grows in montane deciduous forests, forest margins, and moist mountain meadows. Elevational range is 500–2,000 meters.

It prefers cool, moist, [[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]] when new shoots are just emerging. Large clumps can be divided every 4–5 years to maintain vigor.
