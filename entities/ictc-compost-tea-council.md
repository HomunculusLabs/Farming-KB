## Description

The ICTC (International Compost Tea Council) is an organization referenced by [[elaine-ingham]] as a potential body for coordinating and funding research on [[compost-tea-application-methods]] [[soil-microscopy-and-biological-assessment]]. Ingham envisioned the ICTC as capable of organizing testing programs — such as assessing the effect of sprayer passage on compost tea organisms — and distributing research funds to the most appropriate laboratories regardless of which assay methodology they specialize in. This inclusive approach to research funding reflects Ingham's philosophy that different [[fungal-biodiversity-assessment-methods]] (direct counts, [[aact-plate-counts-vs-direct-microscopy-molecular-dna-testing-ingham]], enzyme assays) each have their appropriate applications.

## Classification

- **Category**: Industry organization / Research coordination body
- **Focus**: Compost tea standards, research funding, and methodology validation
- **Approach**: Multi-method, inclusive of different assay types
- **Relationship**: Connected to SFI ([[sfi-soil-foodweb-inc]].) and broader compost tea research community

## Key Facts

- Referenced as capable of organizing and funding biological testing programs
- Could distribute research money to labs using the most appropriate methods
- Supports a multi-method approach — not limited to one assay type
- Could fund plate count research when plate counts are the most appropriate tool
- Part of the effort to establish scientific standards for [[actively-aerated-compost-tea]] (AACT)
- Envisioned as playing a role in validating sprayer effects on compost tea organisms
- Reflects Ingham's pragmatic stance: use whichever assay gives the information needed

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

The ICTC represents the push toward professionalization and standardization of compost tea practices. For cultivators, the existence of an organization focused on compost tea standards and research funding means that the ad hoc, testimonial-based approach to compost tea is being replaced with rigorous scientific validation. The specific research questions the ICTC could address — such as whether sprayer pumps damage beneficial organisms during application — have direct practical implications for how cultivators prepare and apply compost tea. The ICTC's willingness to fund different types of assays (not just their preferred method) is also important for the credibility of compost tea research within the broader [[fukuoka-critique-agricultural-science-laws-liebig]] community.

## Source Context

Ingham references the ICTC in the context of Q&A about funding biological assessments of sprayer effects on compost tea organisms, presenting it as an organization that could coordinate testing and distribute research funds to the most qualified labs regardless of their preferred methodology.
