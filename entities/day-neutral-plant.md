## Description

Day-neutral plants are species whose flowering and seed-setting is not controlled by day length (photoperiod). Unlike short-day or long-day plants, day-neutral species will flower whenever other environmental factors such as temperature, moisture, and nutrition are satisfactory, regardless of the number of daylight hours.

## Classification

- **Category:** Photoperiodic classification
- **Domain:** Plant physiology, horticulture, [[permaculture]]
- **Contrasted with:** Short-day plants, long-day plants
- **Mechanism:** Flowering not triggered by critical day/night length

## Key Facts

### Photoperiodism Overview

Flowering plants are adapted to bloom and set seed in response to specific day lengths and seasonal changes:

- **Short-day plants:** Flower when nights exceed a critical length. Typical of tropical and subtropical origins.
- **Long-day plants:** Flower when days exceed a critical length. Typical of temperate origins.
- **Day-neutral plants:** Flower independent of photoperiod when other conditions are met.

### Practical Applications

Understanding [[photoperiodism]] allows permaculture designers to exploit day-length responses:

1. **Tropical corn in temperate climates:** Transferring a short-day tropical corn variety to a long-day hot-summer climate produces a good green-leaf fodder crop (vegetative growth continues without flowering).
2. **Tobacco in cool areas:** Moving tobacco from temperate to cool areas shifts production toward leaf rather than seed.
3. **New Guinea highlands example:** Cabbages from long-day climates may never flower under short-day conditions at equatorial highlands. Some Brassicas grow to 1-3 m tall, with leaves plucked at intervals for vegetable fodder, cut down only when too tall to reach.

### Latitude and Day Length

- **Low latitudes (0-30 degrees):** Tropical climates. Near-equal days and nights year-round.
- **Mid latitudes (30-50 degrees):** Cool to temperate climates. Long summer days and short winter days.
- **High latitudes (above 50 degrees):** Very long summer days, very short winter days.

### Design Implications

Mollison emphasizes that the photoperiod response is the reason for choosing varieties from local growers, or selecting for flowering in new introductions so that a local seed source is available for all crops desired in seed.

## Relevance to Cultivation

1. **Variety selection:** Matching plant photoperiod type to local latitude ensures reliable flowering and seed production.
2. **Food production strategy:** Exploiting photoperiod mismatches can yield useful biomass even without seed.
3. **Seed saving:** Day-neutral varieties are generally easier for seed saving across latitudes.
4. **Extended harvest:** Day-neutral varieties produce over a longer season compared to short-day or long-day types.
5. **Climate change adaptation:** As growing zones shift, day-neutral cultivars offer more flexible planting windows.
