## Juncus

## Description

*Juncus* is a genus of rush-forming perennial herbs in the family Juncaceae, commonly known as rushes. Mollison identifies *Juncus* species as indicators of advanced [[hordeum-maritimum]]*) and buckthorn plantain (*[[interceptor-banks]])

- **Crop production no longer viable** on the affected site without major earthworks

## Permaculture Applications

Despite being a salinity indicator, *Juncus* has functional uses in permaculture:

- **Bioindicator:** Reliable visual indicator of waterlogging and salinity
- **Erosion control:** Dense root mats stabilize wet, eroding soils
- **Water filtration:** Absorbs nutrients and some contaminants from waterlogged soils
- **Craft material:** Stems used for traditional basket weaving and thatching
- **Habitat:** Provides cover for wetland wildlife and nesting birds
- **Constructed wetlands:** Useful in greywater and wastewater treatment systems

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

*Juncus* represents an advanced warning of salinity problems that have already caused significant land degradation. For permaculture designers, its appearance indicates that interceptor banks and major earthworks are urgently needed — simple tree planting will no longer suffice. However, rushes also serve functional roles in constructed wetland systems and provide craft materials, making them productive components of water management design in permaculture systems.
