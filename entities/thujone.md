## Chemical Properties

- **Chemical class:** Monoterpene ketone (bicyclic)
- **Molecular formula:** C₁₀H₁₆O
- **Molecular weight:** 152.23 g/mol
- **IUPAC name:** (1S,4R,5R)-1-isopropyl-4-methylbicyclo[3.1.0]hexan-3-one
- **CAS number:** 546-80-5 (alpha-thujone); 471-15-8 (beta-thujone)
- **Appearance:** Colorless to pale yellow liquid
- **Odor:** Camphoraceous, herbaceous, aromatic

**Diastereomers:**
- **Alpha-thujone:** More pharmacologically active, more toxic
- **Beta-thujone:** Less active, minor component in most plant sources

Alpha-thujone is typically the dominant isomer in *Artemisia absinthium*
(approximately 60–90% of total thujone content).

## Pharmacology

**GABA_A receptor antagonism:** Thujone acts as a non-competitive
antagonist at the GABA_A receptor chloride channel, blocking
GABA-induced chloride currents. The IC₅₀ for alpha-thujone is
approximately 21 µM. This mechanism is shared with picrotoxin.

The GABA antagonism produces dose-dependent CNS stimulation:
- Low doses: mild stimulation, reported clarity
- Moderate doses: anxiety, restlessness, tremors
- High doses: tonic-clonic seizures (potentially fatal)

**5-HT3 receptor:** Some evidence of antagonism at serotonin 5-HT3
receptors, contributing to antiemetic properties.

**Cannabinoid receptors:** Early speculation about CB1 activity has been
largely disproven; thujone shows negligible affinity for CB1/CB2.

## Toxicity

**LD50 (oral, rat):** ~500 mg/kg (alpha-thujone)

**Neurotoxicity:** The primary concern is seizure induction via GABA
antagonism. Convulsive threshold varies significantly between individuals.

**NOAEL (no observed adverse effect level):** EU Scientific Committee on
Food established 0.11 mg/kg body weight/day for alpha-thujone.

**Regulatory limits:**
- EU: Maximum 35 mg/kg in food and beverages containing wormwood
