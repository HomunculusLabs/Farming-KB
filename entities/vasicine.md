# Vasicine

## Overview

Vasicine (also known as peganine) is a quinazoline alkaloid found primarily in
*Adhatoda vasica* (Malabar nut) and related species of the Acanthaceae family.
It is the principal bioactive constituent responsible for the bronchodilatory,
expectorant, and antitussive properties that have made vasaka a cornerstone of
Ayurvedic and Unani respiratory medicine for over 3,000 years. Vasicine's
pharmacological profile includes bronchial smooth muscle relaxation, uterine
stimulant activity, and antimicrobial effects.

## Chemical Identity

- **Chemical class:** Quinazoline alkaloid (benzopyrimidine)
- **IUPAC name:** (S)-1,2,3,9-Tetrahydro-2-methylpyrido[2,3-b]quinazolin-4-one
- **Molecular formula:** C₁₁H₁₂N₂O
- **Molecular weight:** 188.23 g/mol
- **CAS Registry Number:** 6159-55-3
- **Appearance:** Colorless to pale yellow crystalline solid
- **Melting point:** 205–207°C
- **Solubility:** Sparingly soluble in water; soluble in ethanol, methanol,
  chloroform, and dilute acids
- **Optical activity:** Levorotatory; [α]D = −46° (in ethanol)

## Chemical Structure

Vasicine contains a quinazolin-4-one core fused to a piperidine ring with a
methyl substituent at C-2. The (S) stereochemistry is pharmacologically relevant;
the (R)-enantiomer (vasicinine) has reduced bioactivity.
