## Description

**Agalychnis callidryas**, the red-eyed tree frog, is an iconic species of tree frog in the family Phyllomedusidae native to tropical lowland rainforests from southern Mexico to Colombia. It suffers significant egg mortality from filamentous ascomycete fungal infections in Panama.









## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Animalia
- **Phylum**: Chordata
- **Class**: Amphibia
- **Order**: Anura
- **Family**: Phyllomedusidae
- **Genus**: Agalychnis
- **Species**: A. callidryas









## Key Facts

- Warkentin et al. (2001) documented ascomycete fungal infection on 7% of egg clutches
- Fungal infection accounted for 40% of egg deaths in affected clutches
- A filamentous ascomycete fungus was identified as the pathogen
- Egg masses in both laboratory cultures and natural populations were affected
- Native to Panamanian rainforests experiencing amphibian population declines









## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

A. callidryas illustrates that fungal pathogens affect amphibians at all life stages. The 40% egg mortality rate demonstrates the significant ecological impact of fungi on vertebrate populations. For mycologists, this highlights the diversity of fungal pathogenic strategies, including infection of eggs in moist tropical environments.









## Ecological Role

The red-eyed tree frog is a canopy-dwelling species in Neotropical rainforests. It lays eggs on leaves overhanging water, and fungal infection of egg masses represents an important source of pre-hatching mortality. The species is an important indicator of tropical forest health.
