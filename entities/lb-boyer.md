# L. B. Boyer

## Description

L. B. Boyer was a psychoanalyst and anthropological researcher who conducted pioneering studies of Apache shamanism on the Mescalero Indian Reservation. Boyer applied psychoanalytic methods — particularly Rorschach projective testing — to the study of Native American shamans, producing some of the most detailed personality assessments of indigenous religious practitioners in the anthropological literature. His work on Mescalero Apache shamanism and peyote use forms a significant chapter in Harner's *Hallucinogens and Shamanism*.

## Classification

- **Profession:** Psychoanalyst, psychiatric researcher
- **Research focus:** Apache personality structure, shamanism, peyote use
- **Ethnic groups studied:** Mescalero Apache, Chiricahua Apache, Lipan Apache
- **Methodology:** Psychoanalytic interview, Rorschach projective testing, participant observation
- **Collaborators:** Ruth M. Boyer (spouse, anthropologist), B. Klopfer, Florence B. Brawer, H. Kawai

## Key Facts

- Conducted extensive psychoanalytic research on the Mescalero Apache Reservation
- Used Rorschach inkblot tests to compare personality structures of Apache shamans vs. pseudoshamans
- Found that authentic shamans scored higher on creativity and reality-testing than their culture mates
- Discovered that pseudoshamans had impoverished personalities and confided doubts about their own claimed supernatural powers
- Studied the relationship between peyote use and Apache shamanistic ritual
- Documented how peyote camps led to intra-group violence due to released hostilities among competing shamans
- Collaborated with Klopfer on Rorschach studies comparing shamans and pseudoshamans (1964)
- His work was frequently misunderstood — he did not consider shamans psychologically abnormal in the simple sense
- Documented the case studies of specific Apache shamans including "Black Eyes" and "Ancient One"
- Published extensively from 1961 through 1972 on Apache psychology, shamanism, and folk psychiatry

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Boyer's work is relevant to entheogenic plant studies because:

1. **Shaman personality profiling:** His Rorschach-based personality assessments of peyote-using shamans provide unique data on the psychological characteristics of hallucinogen practitioners
2. **Peyote prohibition dynamics:** His documentation of why the Apache banned peyote from shamanistic rituals provides a rare case study of a culture actively restricting hallucinogen use
3. **Authentic vs. fraudulent practitioners:** His distinction between shamans and pseudoshamans is relevant to understanding who actually uses entheogenic plants versus who claims to
4. **Psychoanalytic methodology:** His application of clinical tools to the study of hallucinogen-using cultures demonstrated that rigorous psychological assessment could be conducted cross-culturally
