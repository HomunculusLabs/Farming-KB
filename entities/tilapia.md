## Tilapia

## Description
Tilapia is a common name for nearly a hundred species of cichlid fish from the tilapiine cichlid tribe. It is one of the most important freshwater food fish in tropical and subtropical aquaculture systems worldwide. In [[permaculture-design]], Tilapia is valued as a core species in pond polycultures due to its herbivorous feeding habits, rapid growth, and tolerance of a wide range of water conditions.

## Classification
- **Kingdom**: Animalia
- **Phylum**: Chordata
- **Class**: Actinopterygii
- **Order**: Cichliformes
- **Family**: Cichlidae
- **Tribe**: Tilapiini
- **Key species mentioned**: *Sarotherodon mossambicus* (Mozambique tilapia)

## Key Facts

### Environmental Tolerance
- **Temperature**: Optimal growth at 20-28degC; dies below 12degC
- **Salinity**: Euryhaline -- tolerates freshwater to brackish conditions
- **Oxygen**: Moderately tolerant of low dissolved oxygen, though growth declines below 5 ppm
- **pH**: Prefers 6.0-8.5; tolerates moderately acidic conditions

### Role in Polyculture
- Primarily herbivorous, feeding on algae and higher aquatic vegetation
- Used as a component in rice paddy-fish culture systems in Southeast Asia
- In pond polycultures, typically stocked at 50% of total fingerling density alongside other species
- Combines well with *Helostoma temmincki*, *Osteochilus hasselti*, *Puntius gonionotus*, and *[[cyprinus-carpio]]*

### Production
- In tropical ponds, reaches pan size (approximately 300 g) in 10-12 weeks
- In cooler climates (e.g., Japan), may take 2-3 summer seasons
- Integrated with higher vegetation in fertilised ponds for optimal yield

### Integration Benefits
- Helps control aquatic weed growth in ponds
- Manures from ducks, pigs, and chickens enhance Tilapia production
- Pond water enriched by Tilapia waste benefits downstream land crops

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology
- While not directly related to mycology, Tilapia aquaculture shares principles of integrated nutrient cycling with [[mushroom-cultivation]]
- Pond sediments and organic waste from Tilapia systems can be composted for use as mushroom substrate
- The polyculture design philosophy mirrors the multi-species approach used in mycological cultivation systems
- [[water-management]] skills in aquaculture translate to humidity and substrate moisture management in mushroom growing
