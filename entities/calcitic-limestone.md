## Calcitic Limestone

## Description

Calcitic limestone is a naturally occurring sedimentary rock composed primarily of calcium carbonate (CaCO3). In agriculture and horticulture, finely ground calcitic limestone is the most commonly used liming agent to raise soil pH and address soil acidity. It is distinguished from dolomitic limestone by its lower magnesium content, making it the preferred liming material for soils that already have adequate or excessive magnesium levels.

## Classification

- **Type:** Agricultural amendment / liming agent
- **Chemical formula:** CaCO3 (calcium carbonate)
- **Mineral:** Calcite
- **Origin:** Sedimentary rock (limestone)
- **NPK value:** None (not a fertilizer in the traditional sense)

## Key Facts

- Primary use is to **raise soil pH** by neutralizing hydrogen ions (acidity) in the soil solution.
- When applied to acidic soils, calcitic limestone reacts with hydrogen ions to form carbonic acid (H2CO3), which further breaks down into carbon dioxide (CO2) and water (H2O).
- Best used when soils have **high magnesium levels**, since it does not add additional magnesium (unlike dolomitic limestone).
- Application rate is determined by the soil's **buffer pH** (a measure of the soil's resistance to pH change), not just the current pH.
- Particle size matters: finer grind reacts faster but may need more frequent application; coarser grind acts more slowly but lasts longer.
- Calcium from calcitic limestone also serves as an essential plant nutrient, supporting cell wall structure, cell division, and membrane integrity.
- Over-application can raise pH too high, making certain micronutrients (iron, manganese, boron, copper, zinc) less available.
- In organic gardening, calcitic limestone is an approved natural amendment for pH correction.

## Chemical Reaction in Soil

The neutralization reaction proceeds as follows:

1. CaCO3 (calcitic limestone) is applied to acidic soil.
2. The carbonate (CO3 2-) reacts with hydrogen ions (H+) in the soil solution.
3. This forms carbonic acid (H2CO3), which is unstable.
4. Carbonic acid decomposes into carbon dioxide (CO2) and water (H2O).
5. Net result: hydrogen ions are removed from solution, pH increases.
6. Calcium (Ca2+) is released and becomes available as a nutrient or occupies cation exchange sites.

## When to Use Calcitic vs. Dolomitic Limestone

- **Use calcitic limestone when**:
  - Soil test shows adequate or high magnesium levels.
  - Soil test specifically recommends calcium without additional magnesium.
  - You are correcting pH in a soil where excess magnesium is already competing with calcium uptake.
- **Use dolomitic limestone when**:
  - Soil test shows low magnesium levels in addition to low pH.
  - Both calcium and magnesium supplementation are needed simultaneously.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Substrate pH adjustment**: For cultivation of organisms that prefer neutral to slightly alkaline conditions, calcitic limestone can be used to adjust substrate pH.
- **Mushroom cultivation**: Some mushroom species (e.g., Agaricus bisporus) benefit from substrates with added calcium carbonate for pH buffering. Calcitic limestone is used in casing layers and compost formulations.
- **Casing layer formulation**: Calcium carbonate is a common component of casing soil mixes, providing both calcium nutrition and pH buffering for pinning and fruiting.
- **Soil preparation**: In outdoor cultivation beds, calcitic limestone can correct soil acidity to create favorable conditions for both plants and beneficial soil fungi.
- **Compost pH management**: Maintaining appropriate pH during composting supports optimal microbial activity; calcitic limestone can be added if the compost becomes too acidic.
- **Nutrient availability**: By raising pH to the optimal range (6.0-7.0 for most plants), calcitic limestone indirectly improves the availability of phosphorus, calcium, magnesium, and molybdenum.

## Application Guidelines

- Always conduct a soil test before applying calcitic limestone.
- Apply based on buffer pH recommendations, not just current pH.
- Incorporate into soil when possible for faster reaction.
- Effects are not instantaneous—pH changes occur over weeks to months.
- Avoid over-application: excessively high pH creates its own set of nutrient availability problems.
- Can be applied in fall for spring gardens, allowing time for the pH adjustment to take effect.

## Relationships

- **Related amendment**: Dolomitic limestone (CaMg(CO3)2)
- **Affected nutrients**: Calcium (increases), hydrogen ions (decreases), all pH-sensitive nutrients (indirectly)
- **Opposite amendment**: Sulfur (lowers pH)
- **Related concepts**: Soil pH, buffer pH, cation exchange capacity, nutrient availability
