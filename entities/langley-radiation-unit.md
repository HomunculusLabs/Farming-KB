# Langley (Radiation Unit)

## Description

The Langley (Ly) is a unit of solar radiation measurement, defined as one gram calorie per square centimetre of surface area (1 g-cal/cm2). It is named after Samuel Pierpont Langley, a 19th-century American astronomer and physicist who pioneered solar radiation research. In [[permaculture]] and agricultural science, Langleys provide a standardized way to quantify incoming solar energy at ground level for site design and crop planning.

## Classification

- **Type:** Unit of measurement
- **Category:** Radiation energy flux density
- **Definition:** 1 gram calorie per square centimetre (1 g-cal/cm2)
- **SI equivalent:** 1 Langley = 41,840 J/m2 = 41.84 kJ/m2
- **Named after:** Samuel Pierpont Langley (1834-1906)

## Key Values

- **Solar constant (outer atmosphere):** The sun provides approximately 2 Langleys per minute to the outer atmosphere
- **At ground level:** Varies widely due to atmospheric filtering
  - **Sun directly overhead, clear day:** Only about 22% of radiant energy penetrates (1 atmosphere depth)
  - **Polar regions (sun at 5 degrees elevation):** As little as 1% of incoming energy reaches ground (11 atmospheres depth)
- **Slope effects:** Pole-facing slopes receive dramatically less energy than equator-facing slopes

## Factors Affecting Langley Reception at Ground Level

Mollison identifies three primary factors that determine the Langleys received at any given site:

1. **Latitude and season:** Determine the depth of atmosphere that solar radiation must traverse (the atmospheric path length)
2. **Slope angle and aspect:** Affects the angle of incidence, which in turn affects reflection and absorption ratios
3. **Atmospheric conditions:** Ice crystals, water vapour, dust, and cloud cover attenuate incoming radiation

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

### Site Energy Assessment

Most homes receive enough sunlight on sun-facing areas to heat water and living space if capture and storage systems are properly designed. Langleys help quantify the solar energy budget available for [[passive-solar-design]], [[solar-water-heating]], and greenhouse planning. South-facing slopes (Northern Hemisphere) or north-facing slopes (Southern Hemisphere) receive significantly more energy.

### Heat Transfer Applications

Understanding radiation in Langleys underpins three grades of heat transfer relevant to [[permaculture-design]]:

1. **Low-grade heat (conduction):** Solid-to-solid contact, trapped by insulation
2. **Medium-grade heat (convection):** Fluid/gas movement, conserved by draught-proofing
3. **High-grade heat (radiation):** Straight-line transmission, conserved by reflective surfaces

### Design Implications

1. **House siting:** [[thermal-belt]] positions on sun-facing slopes maximize Langley reception
2. **Greenhouse orientation:** Align glazing perpendicular to incoming radiation
3. **Solar water heating:** Flat plate collectors and solar ponds capture Langley energy for thermosiphon circulation
4. **Reflective enhancement:** White paint, mirrors, and reflective insulation can redirect radiation to shaded areas

## Key Facts

- 1 Langley = 1 gram calorie per cm2
- Sun provides approximately 2 Ly/minute at outer atmosphere
- Only 22% penetrates at 1 atmosphere depth (overhead sun)
- As little as 1% reaches ground at polar latitudes
- Named after Samuel Pierpont Langley, American astronomer
- Critical for passive solar design calculations in permaculture

## Practical Conversion

- 1 Langley = 41.84 kJ/m2
- 1 Langley is approximately 0.0116 kWh/m2
- Daily total solar radiation in temperate regions: typically 200-600 Ly/day in summer
