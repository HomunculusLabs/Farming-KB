# ALEPH (DOT; PARA-DOT)

**Chemical name:** 2,5-Dimethoxy-4-methylthioamphetamine
**PiHKAL entry:** #3
**Molecular formula:** C12H19NO2S (freebase), C12H20ClNO2S (HCl salt)

## Overview

ALEPH was probably the first sulfur-containing phenethylamine evaluated as a CNS stimulant or psychedelic. Named after the first letter of the Hebrew alphabet, the ALEPH family represents the original exploration of thio-substituted amphetamines. The 2-carbon homologues of ALEPH became the far more thoroughly explored 2c-t-2-entity|2C-T family.

The original name DOT (desoxy- with a thiomethyl group at the 4-position) follows the dom-entity|DOM naming convention. ALEPH is specifically the *para*-isomer (4-position substitution); two positional isomers are cataloged as ORTHO-DOT and META-DOT.

The compound was a complete unknown when first explored — no sulfur-containing phenethylamine had ever been evaluated as a psychoactive substance. The extreme caution of the initial bioassay reflects this uncertainty.

## Dosage

| Level | Amount (mg) |
|-------|------------|
| Threshold | 3-5 |
| Light | 5 - 7 |
| Common | 7 - 10 |
| Strong | 10+ |
| Heavy | Unknown |

Shulgin's published dosage range: **5–10 mg**, with a duration of **6–8 hours**.

The dose-response relationship is erratic and unpredictable between individuals — the defining characteristic of the entire ALEPH family. At 7 mg one experienced only a +1, while at 10 mg a full +3 was achieved. However, an experienced colleague reported only mild, uninteresting effects at 10 mg. This inter-individual variability means no reliable dosage guidance can be given.

## Qualitative Effects

- Physical warmth spreading from legs to entire body
- Intense intellectual stimulation with non-verbal cognitive activity
- Empathic afterglow persisting well beyond the acute effects
- Frame-of-reference shifts making ordinary tasks feel alien
- Enhanced interpersonal interaction (telephone conversation manageable)
- Mechanical tasks become impossible (arranging flowers, alphabetizing)
- Driving explicitly described as impossible
- Writing and note-taking prolific during acute effects

## Shulgin's Qualitative Comments

### Selected PiHKAL Trip Reports

**5 mg:** "The initial hints of action were physical — warming of first the legs, and then a comfortable warmth spread over the entire body. Intense intellectual stimulation, one that inspired the scribbling of some 14 pages of handwritten notes. Which is a pretty good record for an experience that is almost entirely non-verbal. The afterglow was benign and rich in empathy for everything. And by the sixth hour I was quite hungry."

**10 mg:** "There was a rapid shift of frame of reference that made simple tasks such as reading and tuning the radio quite alien. I happened to catch the eyes of Pretty Baby, the cat, at the same moment she looked at me, and she turned and fled. I am able to interact with people on the telephone quite well but mechanical things, such as arranging flowers or alphabetizing names, are beyond me. Driving would be impossible."

## Synthesis (from PiHKAL)

The synthesis starts from 2,5-dimethoxy-4-(methylthio)benzaldehyde (see 2c-t-2-entity|2C-T for preparation of this intermediate):

1. **Nitrostyrene formation:** Benzaldehyde (2.3 g) + nitroethane (7.5 mL) + NH₄OAc (0.45 g), heated on steam bath for 6 h. Crude orange crystals recrystallized from 140 mL boiling MeOH to give 1.8 g bright orange crystals, mp 137–138 °C.

2. **LAH reduction:** Nitrostyrene (1.8 g) in THF added dropwise to LAH (1.4 g) in Et₂O/THF under inert atmosphere at reflux. Heated 7 h additional. Standard aqueous workup (H₂O, NaOH, H₂O sequence). Residue: 1.3 g colorless oil that solidified, mp 90–93 °C (recrystallized from hexane: 91–93 °C).

3. **Salt formation:** Freebase in IPA + concentrated HCl (0.57 mL), diluted with anhydrous Et₂O → 1.2 g HCl salt, mp 200–201 °C (analytical sample from IPA: 204–205 °C).

## The First Bioassay: Extreme Caution

The first trials of ALEPH were conducted with extraordinary caution, reflecting the complete unknown of introducing a sulfur heteroatom into a psychoactive phenethylamine framework:

- **September 3, 1975, 11:30 AM:** First trial at 0.25 micrograms (sub-microgram level)
- **18 trials** were required to work the experimental dosage up to a single milligram
- In retrospect, Shulgin acknowledged this was "overly cautious" — but the uncertainty was genuine

Part of the extreme caution was due to strange manic excitement during product isolation in the laboratory. Although certainly a placebo response, Shulgin was "jumpy and unable to stay in the lab for more than a few minutes at a time." Possible explanations considered: dust in the air, skin contact with the free base. Everything was washed down as a precaution.

## The 5 mg Experience and Chapter 14

The 5 milligram experiment is detailed in Chapter 14 of PiHKAL as an example of thought processes during psychedelic intoxication, ego-inflation, and mania. Shulgin wrote 14 pages of handwritten notes during an "almost entirely non-verbal" experience. The experience featured intense intellectual stimulation and could not be duplicated on subsequent trials — a hallmark of peak experiences catalyzed by drugs.

## The Erratic Nature of the ALEPH Family

The defining characteristic of the ALEPH family is its unpredictable inter-individual variability in response:

- At **5 mg**: Full experience with intellectual stimulation, writing, empathy
- At **7 mg**: Only a +1 (uneventful)
- At **10 mg**: Full +3 experience for one person, but only "mild effects which he found relatively uninteresting" for an experienced colleague
- One person at **4 mg** of ALEPH-2 maintained a fetal position in hibernation for several hours with substantial amnesia, while another at **8 mg** of ALEPH-2 experienced extreme intoxication but "almost no visual phenomena"

This erratic dose-response relationship led to the abandonment of systematic exploration of the ALEPH series, though ALEPH-2, ALEPH-4, ALEPH-6, and ALEPH-7 were all synthesized and entered in PiHKAL.

## Structure-Activity Relationships

ALEPH represents the 3-carbon (amphetamine) analogue of 2c-t-2-entity|2C-T, carrying a methylthio group at the 4-position of the 2,5-dimethoxyphenyl ring. Compared to the classic DO-series compounds (dom-entity|DOM, dob-entity|DOB, doi-entity|DOI), which carry alkyl or halogen substituents at the same position, ALEPH introduces a sulfur atom as the linking heteroatom.

The erratic inter-individual variability in response is the defining characteristic of the ALEPH family and distinguishes it from the more predictable halogen-substituted DO series. The same variability, though less pronounced, can be seen in some members of the 2C-T series such as 2c-t-4-entity|2C-T-4.

### The 2-Carbon vs. 3-Carbon Homologues

While the ALEPH (amphetamine) series proved erratic and unpredictable, the corresponding 2-carbon phenethylamine homologues — the 2c-t-2-entity|2C-T family — proved to have "much less uncertainty as to reasonable dosages, and much more richness in the subjective nature of the experience." This is why the 2C-T series became far more thoroughly explored than the ALEPH series.

### Numbering Convention

The numbering of both ALEPH and 2C-T compounds follows strict historical order of conception (like house numbers in residential Tokyo), with no structural significance in the number assignment. Thus ALEPH-3, ALEPH-5, ALEPH-16, and ALEPH-20 were assigned but never completed, while ALEPH-2, ALEPH-4, ALEPH-6, and ALEPH-7 were fully synthesized and bioassayed.

## Safety Warnings

- Dose-response is highly unpredictable between individuals — the same dose may produce no effects for one person and overwhelming effects for another
- The sulfur-containing phenethylamine class was entirely unexplored at the time of first synthesis
- At 10 mg, ordinary tasks like reading and mechanical operations become very difficult
- Driving is explicitly described as impossible
- The 2-carbon homologues (2C-T series) proved more predictable and should be preferred
- See [[psychedelic-harm-reduction]] for general harm reduction guidelines
