## Overview

Iris cristata, commonly known as the dwarf crested iris or crested iris, is a low-growing rhizomatous perennial native to [[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]] blooms and spreading habit. Its flowers feature distinctive crested falls with white or pale blue standards, often marked with orange-yellow ridges.

First described by Carl Linnaeus in 1753, the species has remained a staple of [[jeavons-climate-adaptation-growing-seasons]].

The root system is fibrous and relatively shallow, typically concentrated in the top 10 cm of soil. This shallow rooting makes the species easy to transplant but somewhat vulnerable to drought.

Propagation is by division of rhizomes in late summer or by seed. Seed requires cold stratification for 60–90 days and may take 2–3 years to reach flowering size.
