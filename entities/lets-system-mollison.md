## Description

The [[let-system]] (Local Employment Trading System) is a community-based currency and exchange network that enables members to trade goods and services using local "green" dollars rather than conventional national currency. Described by [[bill-mollison]] as a key strategy for building self-reliant communities within [[permaculture-design]].

## Classification

- **Category**: [[informal-and-formal-community-economics]] / Alternative currency
- **Domain**: Social and financial permaculture
- **Scale**: Community-level (6-600 people optimal)

## How LETS Works

The LET System centres on a community network where every joining member agrees to trade in local green dollars. The system operates on several key principles:

1. **No interest**: Green dollars carry no interest charges
2. **Cost-based administration**: Admin costs charged on a "cost of service" basis
3. **Transparency**: Any member can know the turnover or balance of any other member
4. **Periodic statements**: Every member receives periodic account statements
5. **Non-convertible**: The currency cannot be issued or cashed in — it is earned through service

Green dollars are "earned" by providing goods or services to other members and "lost" by using services or goods. All trade or credit standing is a public act referring to the community as a whole.

## Key Features

- Members can spend across the entire range of services or goods offered
- Production is never limited by lack of money
- Businesses can charge federal currency for spare parts and green dollars for labour
- Local components increase; local businesses thrive
- Charities and local farms benefit greatly as funds are likely to return
- Anyone wanting work can offer services without waiting for formal "jobs"
- Community account remains balanced at all times

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

LETS is directly relevant to permaculture communities for several reasons:

- **Farm labour exchange**: Members can trade farm work, harvesting, and garden maintenance without cash
- **Seed and produce trading**: Surplus produce, seeds, and plant materials circulate within the community
- **Tool sharing**: Equipment hire and sharing becomes feasible through credit systems
- **Skill exchange**: Propagation skills, soil knowledge, pruning expertise traded as services
- **Community food security**: Removes barrier of cash scarcity from local food production

## Advantages Over Conventional Money

Conventional money derives from external agencies and circulates through all communities, tending to accumulate in cities and multinational coffers. [[community-money-and-asset-categories]] (credit) is not usable outside the community, so it circulates indefinitely, providing a constantly available resource. This "recycling" of value mirrors the [[permaculture-principle-apply-self-regulation]] of energy cycling within systems.

## Integration with Permaculture Design

The LETS concept aligns with the permaculture ethic of "fair share" and the principle of using biological and [[permaculture-principle-use-and-value-renewable-resources]]. It treats money as a flow — like water in landscape — where the number of cycles and uses matters more than the total quantity.

## Historical Context

LETS emerged in the 1980s as communities sought alternatives to conventional banking. Mollison saw these systems as essential infrastructure for bioregional self-reliance, alongside ethical banking, credit unions, and cooperatives.

## How LETS Works

A Local Employment Trading System (LETS) is a
community-based mutual credit network where members
trade goods and services using a local unit of account
rather than national currency. Transactions are recorded
in a central ledger, with debits and credits balanced
across the community over time.

## Implementation Steps

1. Define the geographic boundary of the LETS network
2. Create a directory of offered goods and services
3. Establish a unit of exchange (often time-based)
4. Set up a recording system for transactions
5. Hold regular market days for face-to-face trading
6. Review and adjust the system quarterly

## Benefits for Permaculture Communities

LETS networks strengthen local resilience by keeping
value circulating within the community. They enable
skill-sharing, reduce dependence on external economies,
and create social bonds through exchange relationships.
Members can trade garden produce, repair services,
teaching, and labor without needing cash.
