# Vitex agnus-castus

Vitex agnus-castus, commonly known as chaste tree, chasteberry, Abraham's balm, or monk's pepper, is a deciduous shrub or small tree in the mint family (Lamiaceae), native to the Mediterranean basin and parts of western Asia. One of the most extensively researched medicinal plants in modern phytotherapy, chaste tree has been used for over 2,500 years to address women's health concerns, particularly menstrual irregularities, premenstrual syndrome (PMS), and infertility. The species name *agnus-castus* derives from the Latin *castus* (chaste) and *agnus* (lamb), a reference to the historical belief that the plant suppressed sexual desire — a belief that gave rise to its use by monks in medieval monasteries and the common name "monk's pepper." Modern pharmacological research has validated several of its traditional uses, particularly through the dopaminergic activity of its diterpene compounds on the pituitary gland.

## Morphology and Growth Characteristics

### Plant Structure
Chaste tree is a multi-stemmed deciduous shrub that can develop into a small tree reaching 3–8 m (10–26 feet) in height under favorable conditions, though it more commonly grows as a 2–5 m (6.5–16 foot) rounded shrub. The growth habit is open and spreading, with a rounded crown and somewhat irregular branching pattern. In colder climates, the plant may die back to the ground in winter and regenerate from the rootstock, behaving effectively as a herbaceous perennial.

### Leaves
The leaves are compound (palmately compound), typically with 5–7 lanceolate to elliptic leaflets radiating from a central point like fingers from a palm. Individual leaflets are 4–10 cm (1.6–4 inches) long, dark green above and gray-green to silvery below due to a dense covering of fine hairs (tomentose indumentum). The aromatic foliage, when crushed, releases a distinctive spicy-peppery fragrance. Leaf arrangement is opposite along the stems.

### Flowers
The inflorescence is a terminal, erect spike (raceme) 10–25 cm (4–10 inches) long, bearing numerous small, fragrant flowers densely packed along the axis. Individual flowers are bilabiate (two-lipped), approximately 1–1.5 cm (0.4–0.6 inches) long, with colors ranging from pale lilac and lavender-blue to deep violet-blue or white in some cultivars. Flowering occurs from midsummer through early autumn (July–September in temperate regions), providing an exceptionally long bloom period of 8–12 weeks.

### Fruit
The fruit is a small, hard, spherical drupe (berry-like) 3–4 mm (0.12–0.16 inches) in diameter, resembling black pepper corns — hence the common name "monk's pepper." Fruits ripen from green to dark brown or black and contain four small seeds. The dried ripe fruits (chasteberry) are the primary medicinal plant part.

## Medicinal Properties

### Traditional Uses
Chaste tree has one of the longest documented histories of medicinal use in Western herbalism:

- **Menstrual disorders:** Used for irregular menstruation, amenorrhea (absent periods), menorrhagia (heavy bleeding), and dysmenorrhea (painful periods) for over 2,500 years
- **Premenstrual syndrome (PMS):** The most extensively studied and clinically validated modern indication — systematic reviews and meta-analyses have confirmed its efficacy for PMS symptoms including breast tenderness (mastalgia), mood disturbance, headache, and bloating
- **Infertility:** Traditional use for promoting fertility, particularly in cases of luteal phase deficiency (inadequate progesterone production)
- **Lactation suppression:** Historically used to reduce or suppress breast milk production (counter to its modern use in supporting fertility)
- **Menopausal symptoms:** Used for hot flashes, night sweats, and mood changes during perimenopause, though evidence is less robust than for PMS
