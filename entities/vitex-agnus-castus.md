# Vitex agnus-castus (Chaste Tree)

## Overview

**Vitex agnus-castus**, commonly known as chaste tree, monk's pepper, or vitex, is a deciduous shrub in the family Lamiaceae native to the Mediterranean region and western Asia.

It has been used for over 2,500 years in European traditional medicine, primarily for gynecological complaints including PMS, menstrual irregularity, and fertility support.

The plant's medicinal reputation stems from dopaminergic D2 receptor agonist activity in the anterior pituitary, which modulates prolactin secretion and influences progesterone levels.

Its dried ripe fruits (berries) are the primary plant part used therapeutically, standardized to contain a minimum of 0.5% agnuside in pharmaceutical preparations.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae — **Order:** Lamiales — **Family:** Lamiaceae
- **Genus:** Vitex — **Species:** V. agnus-castus L.
- **Authority:** Carl Linnaeus, *Species Plantarum* (1753)
- **Synonyms:** Vitex agnus L., Agnus-castus officinalis (L.) Caruel, Vitex verticillata Lam.

**Etymology:** "Vitex" derives from Latin *vieo* (to weave), referring to flexible branches used in basketry. "Agnus-castus" combines *agnus* (lamb) and *castus* (chaste), from the ancient Greek belief that the plant promoted chastity during Demeter's Thesmophoria festivals.

**Common names:** Chaste tree, chaste berry, monk's pepper, Abraham's balm, lilac chaste tree, wild pepper, vitex.

## Morphology

**Growth habit:** Deciduous, multi-stemmed shrub or small tree, 3-6 m height.
**Branches:** Long, slender, flexible, quadrangular; young shoots gray-tomentose.

**Leaves:** Opposite, palmately compound, 5-7 lanceolate leaflets per leaf.
Each leaflet 4-10 cm × 1-3 cm, dark green above, gray-tomentose beneath.
Margins serrate, apex acute to acuminate.

**Flowers:** Terminal panicles or cymes, 10-25 cm long, mid-to-late summer.
Individual flowers 6-8 mm, tubular, two-lipped, fragrant.
Color: lavender-blue to violet-blue; white-flowered cultivars exist.

**Fruit:** Small drupe, 3-4 mm diameter, black when ripe, containing 4 seeds.
Dried fruit has a peppery, slightly aromatic odor and flavor.

## Distribution and Habitat

**Native range:** Mediterranean Basin, from the Iberian Peninsula through southern Europe, North Africa, and western Asia to the Caucasus.
**Naturalized:** Widely planted in temperate and subtropical regions worldwide (USDA zones 6-9).

**Habitat:** Riverbanks, rocky hillsides, dry gullies, and waste places, 0-1,000 m elevation.
Tolerates drought, poor soils, and coastal conditions. Prefers full sun and well-drained soils.

## Phytochemistry

**Diterpenes:** Rotundifuran (major), 6β,7β-diacetoxy-13-hydroxy-labda-8,14-diene, vitexilactone, viteagnusin A/B/C.

**Iridoid glycosides:** Agnuside (marker compound), aucubin, catalpol.

**Flavonoids:** Casticin, apigenin, luteolin, vitexin, orientin.

**Essential oil (0.5-1.2%):** α-pinene, β-pinene, 1,8-cineole, sabinene, limonene, germacrene D, β-caryophyllene.

## Pharmacology and Clinical Applications

### Dopaminergic Activity (Primary Mechanism)

Vitex extracts are D2 receptor agonists in the anterior pituitary, inhibiting prolactin secretion. This underpins efficacy in PMS/PMDD, conditions often associated with latent hyperprolactinemia.

A 2001 double-blind RCT (n=170) found vitex extract significantly reduced PMS symptoms including breast tenderness, edema, and psychological complaints over three cycles. A 2013 systematic review of 12 RCTs (n > 3,000) confirmed efficacy comparable to conventional treatments.

### Menstrual Cycle Regulation

Demonstrated luteal phase-supporting effects through increased progesterone secretion. Clinical studies report normalization of irregular cycles, particularly with latent hyperprolactinemia or corpus luteum insufficiency.

### Mastalgia (Breast Pain)

Cyclical mastalgia responds favorably in multiple clinical trials. Response rates of 60-80% reported, with improvements typically within 2-3 cycles.

### Fertility Support

Preliminary evidence for fertility improvement in women with luteal phase defects, though larger RCTs are needed.

⚠️ **Contraindications:** Pregnancy, lactation. Do not combine with dopamine agonists, oral contraceptives, or HRT without medical supervision.

## Cultivation

**Propagation:** Softwood cuttings in summer (preferred) or seeds (stratify 4°C for 30 days). Seed germination: 30-60% over 2-8 weeks, often irregular.
**Growing conditions:** Full sun, well-drained soil. Drought-tolerant once established. Suitable for xeriscaping.
**Pruning:** Hard prune in early spring to maintain shape; flowers on new wood. Train as single tree or multi-stemmed shrub.
**Hardiness:** USDA zones 6-9. Root-hardy to -15°C.
**Pests:** Generally pest-free. Occasional spider mites, whiteflies, or powdery milded in humid conditions.
**Yield:** Mature plants (4+ years) produce 0.5-2 kg dried fruit per season. Harvest fully ripe berries in autumn.

## Permaculture Applications

- **Ornamental/nectar plant:** Provides nectar for bees, butterflies, and hummingbirds; listed as Mediterranean bee-forage.
- **Drought-tolerant hedge:** Suitable as a dryland screening hedge in permaculture designs.
- **Food forest shrub layer:** Medicinal yield alongside food-producing species.
- **Erosion control:** Deep taproot stabilizes soil on slopes and riverbanks.
- **Companions:** Performs well with [[lavandula-angustifolia-entity]], [[rosmarinus-officinalis-entity]], [[salvia-officinalis]].

## Historical Use

**Ancient Greece:** Used in Thesmophoria festivals; Hippocrates recommended it for uterine disorders.
**Dioscorides (1st c. CE):** Prescribed vitex seeds in wine for uterine inflammation.
**Medieval Europe:** Monks consumed berries to suppress sexual desire — hence "monk's pepper."
**Modern:** German Commission E and ESCOP have published positive monographs. Widely prescribed in Germany, Austria, and the UK.

## Standardized Preparations

- **ZE 440** (Bionorica): Standardized extract used in most clinical trials; 20 mg/day.
- **BNO 1095** (Bionorica): Liquid preparation standardized to 0.08% essential oil.
- Typical dose: 20-40 mg/day standardized extract, or 3-4 mL/day 1:2 tincture.
- Onset: Gradual, typically requiring 2-3 menstrual cycles for significant improvement.

## Related Species

- **Vitex negundo** (Chinese chaste tree): Used in Ayurveda and TCM; distinct phytochemical profile.
- **Vitex trifolia** (simple-leaved chaste tree): Tropical Asian species, less studied medicinally.
- **Vitex rotundifolia** (beach vitex): Coastal East Asian species; invasive in the southeastern US.
