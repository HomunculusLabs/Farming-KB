## Description

Mesembryanthemum (commonly known as ice plants or fig marigolds) is a genus of succulent plants in the family Aizoaceae, native primarily to southern Africa but widely naturalized in coastal regions worldwide. Their fleshy, water-storing leaves and vigorous spreading habit make them valuable as ground-cover components of coastal windbreak systems in [[permaculture-design]].

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Family:** Aizoaceae
- **Genus:** Mesembryanthemum
- **Common names:** Ice plant, fig marigold, Hottentot fig, highway ice plant
- **Growth form:** Prostrate to trailing succulent perennial; forms dense mats

## Key Facts

### Coastal Windbreak Role

Mollison includes Mesembryanthemum alongside [[rhagodia]], Tetragonia, and Carpobrotus as tough semi-succulents that can mound over mesh fences, rock walls, and tyre barriers on salt-laden coasts. These species create living fedge systems, tolerate salt spray and sandblast, form dense mats that reduce ground-level wind velocity, and provide bee forage with showy flowers.

### Notable Species

- **Mesembryanthemum crystallinum** (common ice plant): Annual or biennial with glistening bladder cells on leaves that reflect sunlight. Edible leaves with salty flavor. Naturalized widely on coastal dunes.
- **Mesembryanthemum edule** (now Carpobrotus edulis): Hottentot fig with large yellow flowers and edible fruit.
- **Mesembryanthemum aitonis:** Low-growing species used for dune stabilization.

### Adaptations

- **Succulent leaves:** Store water in bladder-like cells; the crystalline appearance gives the common name "ice plant."
- **Salt accumulation:** Some species accumulate salt and can be used to desalinate soils.
- **CAM photosynthesis:** Crassulacean Acid Metabolism allows extremely efficient water use.
- **Rapid colonization:** Spreads vegetatively, covering bare sand and rock quickly.

### Caution

Some Mesembryanthemum species are aggressive invaders outside their native range, particularly in Mediterranean climate regions. They can smother native coastal plant communities. Responsible planting requires species selection appropriate to the local ecology.

## Relevance to Cultivation

1. **Coastal windbreak establishment:** Ground-level components protect the base of windbreaks and reduce sand movement around young tree plantings.
2. **Living mulch:** Dense mats suppress weeds and retain soil moisture beneath taller windbreak species.
3. **Edible ground cover:** Leaves and fruit of some species are edible, adding food yield.
4. **Dune stabilization:** Critical for preventing wind erosion of sandy coastal soils in early site establishment.
5. **Bee forage:** Prolific flowering provides nectar and pollen in harsh coastal environments.
