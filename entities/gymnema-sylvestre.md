## Gymnema sylvestre

## Overview

*Gymnema sylvestre* is a woody climbing shrub in the family Apocynaceae,
widely known as **gurmar** (Hindi: "sugar destroyer") for its unique
ability to temporarily suppress sweet taste perception.

Native to tropical and subtropical regions of India, Africa, and
Australia, it has been used for over 2,000 years in Ayurvedic medicine
for diabetes, [[datura-metel-old-world-ethnobotany]].

## Morphology

**Habit:** Large, woody, deciduous climbing shrub, growing 3–6 meters
when supported by trees or structures.

**Leaves:** Opposite, elliptic-ovate, 3–6 cm long, 1.5–3 cm wide,
short petiole. Surface smooth, glossy, dark green above. Margins
entire.

**Stems:** Slender, pubescent when young, exuding milky latex when cut.

**Flowers:** Small, yellowish-green, axillary or terminal umbellate
cymes. Corolla campanulate with 5 lobes.

**Fruit:** Paired follicles, lanceolate, 5–8 cm, with numerous flat
seeds bearing silky coma.

**Flowering:** May to September.

## Distribution and Habitat

*G. sylvestre* is native to [[psilocybe-southeast-asia-pacific]], tropical Africa, and northern Australia.

In India, it occurs in dry deciduous forests up to 600 meters in
Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Kerala, and Tamil Nadu.

It thrives in well-drained sandy to loamy soils with partial shade,
commonly climbing trees at forest edges and along stream banks.

## Bioactive Compounds

The primary bioactive constituents are **gymnemic acids**, triterpenoid
saponins structurally similar to glucose.
