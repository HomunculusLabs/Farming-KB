## Overview

*Orthotrichum diaphanum* Schrad. ex Brid., commonly known as the window screen
moss or transparent orthotrichum, is a widespread epiphytic moss in the family
Orthotrichaceae. It is among the most commonly encountered epiphytic mosses in
[[query-how-do-green-roofs-and-living-walls-benefit-urban-environments]]. This tolerance has made it a valuable bioindicator for
studying [[fukuoka-natural-farming-in-tropical-regions]].

Plants form loose to dense mats on bark, typically 0.5–2.0 cm tall. Stems are
erect, sparingly branched, with a central strand. The appearance is silvery-
grey when dry due to abundant hair-points on the leaf tips.

Leaves are 2.5–4.0 mm long, ovate-lanceolate, with recurved margins in the
upper portion. The leaf apex terminates in a long, hyaline hair-point
comprising one or more elongated cells. This hair-point is a key diagnostic
feature distinguishing it from related species.

Spore capsules are cylindrical to ellipsoidal, 1.5–2.5 mm long, on a very
short seta that leaves the capsule immersed or barely emergent from the
perichaetial leaves. Eight peristome teeth (single peristome) are present,
divided into two segments.

The calyptra is mitrate, covering only the upper portion of the capsule, and
conspicuously hyaline and papillose. Spores are spherical, 10–16 μm in
diameter, and released from late winter through spring.

## Distribution and Habitat

*O. diaphanum* has a broad distribution across Europe, western and central
Asia, and [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]], it occurs
primarily in the eastern and Pacific coastal states.

The species is primarily corticolous, growing on the bark of deciduous and
coniferous trees. Preferred host trees include oak, ash, elm, maple, willow,
and various [[comparison-pruning-fruit-trees-vs-grafting-fruit-trees]]. It also occasionally colonizes fence posts, stone
walls, and rock surfaces.

It shows a strong preference for trunks and branches with moderate to high
light exposure, typically on the trunk base and main branches. It avoids
deeply shaded, humid understory positions favored by more sensitive epiphytes.

Urban tolerance is a distinguishing characteristic. *O. diaphanum* persists in
city centers, along roadsides, and in industrial areas where sulfur dioxide
and nitrogen oxide pollution exclude most epiphytic mosses. It is often the
only epiphytic bryophyte present in heavily polluted urban settings.
