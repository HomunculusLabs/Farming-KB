# Ocimum basilicum

Ocimum basilicum, commonly known as sweet basil, is an aromatic annual herb
in the mint family (Lamiaceae). Native to tropical South and [[psilocybe-southeast-asia-pacific]],
it has been cultivated for over 5,000 years and is now one of the most widely
grown [[holzer-medicinal-and-culinary-herbs-compendium]] worldwide. The species is central to Mediterranean,
Italian, Thai, Vietnamese, and Indian cuisines, and holds deep cultural
significance in Hindu traditions.
Basil is rich in volatile [[comparison-essential-oils-vs-neem-oil-vs-insecticidal-soap]] — primarily linalool, eugenol, and
methyl chavicol — that confer its characteristic aroma and many medicinal
properties. It has a long history in Ayurveda, Unani, and European folk
herbalism as a digestive aid, carminative, and anti-inflammatory agent.
Modern research has validated antioxidant, antimicrobial, and anxiolytic
properties. The genus contains over 60 species; basil is also valued in
[[comparison-companion-planting-reference-vs-jadam-monoculture-critique]] and permaculture systems for [[foliar-pest-deterrence-aact-mechanisms]] and
pollinator attraction.

## Taxonomy and Naming

- **Order:** Lamiales | **Family:** Lamiaceae | **Tribe:** Ocimeae | **Genus:** Ocimum
- **Species:** O. basilicum L. | **Authority:** Carl Linnaeus, 1753
- **Etymology:** Greek "basilikos" (βασιλικός) = "royal"; Hindi name "tulsi"
  derives from Sanskrit
- **Synonyms:** O. album (Mill.), O. bullatum (M.Bieb.), Plectranthus barbasii
- **Related species:** O. tenuiflorum (holy basil), O. gratissimum (African
  basil), O. americanum, O. kilimandscharicum (camphor basil)

## Description

Ocimum basilicum is a fast-growing, tender annual herb reaching 20–60 cm in
height (up to 1 m in some cultivars). The stem is square (characteristic of
Lamiaceae), green to purplish-green, branching from the base, and densely
covered in fine glandular hairs that secrete the aromatic essential oils.
Leaves are opposite, decussate, ovate to broadly lanceolate, 2–8 cm long and
1–4 cm wide, with serrate margins, acuminate tip, and cuneate base.
Leaf color ranges from bright green to deep purple depending on cultivar.
When crushed, leaves emit a warm, sweet, spicy-camphoraceous aroma. The root
system is fibrous and shallow, concentrated in the top 15–20 cm of soil.

## Flower and Fruit

Flowers are borne in whorled verticillasters along terminal racemes,
each whorl subtended by a small bract. Individual flowers are bilabiate,
zygomorphic, approximately 1–1.5 cm long, with a 5-toothed calyx (upper lip
entire, lower lip 4-toothed). The corolla is white to pinkish-white, with
a 4-lobed upper lip and a single-lobed lower lip. Four didynamous stamens
with the longer pair exserted. The superior ovary develops into four small
nutlets (mericarps), each approximately 1.5–2 mm, ovoid, dark brown to black.
Flowering is indeterminate from mid-summer to first frost. In culinary
cultivation, flower buds are typically pinched to prolong leaf production.

## Distribution and Habitat

- **Native range:** Tropical/subtropical South Asia; earliest cultivation
  records from India circa 3000 BCE
- **Naturalized:** Mediterranean basin, Southern Europe, North Africa, Middle
  East, South America, Pacific Islands, southern US
- **Cultivation:** Worldwide; summer annual in temperate zones, perennial only
  in frost-free climates (USDA zones 10–11)
- **Habitat:** Full sun, well-drained soils; garden escape in disturbed sites
- **Climate:** 20–30°C optimal; killed by frost, damaged below 10°C

## Chemistry and Active Compounds

The essential oil varies significantly by chemotype and cultivar:
- **Linalool:** 20–55% of essential oil in sweet basil (Genovese type);
  monoterpene alcohol; sedative, anxiolytic, anti-inflammatory via
  GABA_A receptor modulation
- **Eugenol:** 5–75% depending on chemotype; analgesic, antioxidant,
  antimicrobial; predominant in O. tenuiflorum
- **Methyl chavicol (estragole):** 0–88% in European "French" basil;
  phenylpropanoid; sweet anise-like aroma; hepatotoxic at high doses (IARC 2B)
- **Citral (geranial + neral):** 1–5% (up to 75% in lemon basil); antimicrobial
- **Rosmarinic acid:** Major phenolic acid (non-volatile); antioxidant,
  anti-inflammatory, COX-2 inhibition
- **β-Caryophyllene:** 1–8%; sesquiterpene; CB2 receptor agonist
- **Flavonoids:** Orientin, vicenin, apigenin-7-O-glucoside, quercetin
  derivatives; antioxidant, hepatoprotective
- **Vitamins:** Rich in vitamin K (414 µg/100g), vitamin A, vitamin C,
  calcium, iron, magnesium

## Traditional and Modern Uses

- **Ayurveda:** O. tenuiflorum (tulsi) is a rasayana (rejuvenative); used for
  respiratory conditions, fever, digestive complaints, stress; O. basilicum
  shares many properties but is considered less potent
- **European folk medicine:** Carminative for flatulence, galactagogue, mild
  diuretic, topical relief for wasp stings
- **Modern herbalism:** Tincture or infusion for nervous tension, anxiety,
  digestive spasm; essential oil in aromatherapy for mental fatigue
- **Antimicrobial:** Active against S. aureus, E. coli, P. aeruginosa, and
  C. albicans in vitro

## Cultivation and Harvesting

- **Propagation:** Seed (indoors 6–8 weeks before last frost at 20–25°C) or
  softwood cuttings; germination in 5–14 days
- **Soil:** Well-drained fertile loam; pH 6.0–7.0; avoid waterlogging
- **Light:** Full sun, minimum 6–8 hours; tolerates partial shade with
  reduced yield
- **Spacing:** 20–30 cm between plants; 30–45 cm between rows
- **Pinching:** Pinch tips once 4–6 true leaf pairs form; remove flower buds
  throughout season
- **Harvest:** Begin 60–90 days from sowing at 15–20 cm tall; cut 10 cm above
  ground for regrowth; final harvest before first frost
- **Preservation:** Dried, frozen (in oil), or processed into pesto
- **Pests:** Japanese beetles, aphids, Fusarium wilt, [[cervantes-downy-mildew-fungal-blight]]
  (Peronospora belbahrii — major emerging disease)

## Companion Planting and Permaculture Role

Basil is widely recommended in companion planting, though evidence for many
claims remains limited:
- **Tomato partnership:** Classic companion; may repel hornworms and whiteflies
- **Pollinator attraction:** Flowers attract bees, butterflies, beneficial wasps
- **Pest deterrence:** Volatile oils may mask crop scents from pests
- **Permaculture:** Intercrop in food forests, keyhole gardens, herb spirals;
  self-seeds in warm climates
