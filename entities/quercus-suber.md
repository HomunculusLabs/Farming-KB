# Quercus suber

**Type:** Species (Tree)
**Category:** [[permaculture-livestock-forage-systems]], high-grade
charcoal, and serves as an effective firebreak due to low
combustibility.

## Taxonomy
- **Order:** Fagales
- **Family:** Fagaceae
- **Genus:** Quercus
- **Species:** Q. suber
- **Section:** Cerris (ring-cupped oaks)
- **Common relatives:** Q. robur (English oak), Q. ilex (holm oak),
  Q. petraea (sessile oak)

## Key Details
- **Growth rate:** Fairly slow growing
- **Height:** Up to 20 m, often as wide as tall
- **Lifespan:** Very long (200-300+ years of productive cork
  harvesting)
- **Climate:** Hardy to -15C; tolerates extreme heat; drought
  tolerant once established
- **Soil preference:** Prefers deep soil with good drainage; thrives
  in poor, acid, sandy soils; requires sunny position
- **Root system:** Develops long tap root (up to 1 m before showing
  above ground); this is critical for drought resistance
- **Cork production:** First harvest at approximately 20 years; then
  harvested on a 10-year cycle for hundreds of years; average
  yield approximately 240 kg/ha/year in Portugal; highest quality
  cork comes from dry rocky soils
- **Acorn production:** One recorded tree yielded 840 litres of
  acorns in a single season, enough to feed a pig for 120 days
  and add 59 kg of live weight
- **Acorn uses:** Roasted for human consumption; acclaimed stock
  feed; coffee substitute
- **Fire resistance:** Low combustibility makes it valuable as a
  firebreak species in Mediterranean landscapes
- **Other products:** High-grade charcoal from wood; tannin from
  bark

## Cultivation
Must be planted in permanent position as the long tap root makes
transplanting difficult or fatal. Acorns require immediate planting
in damp soil or storage in damp peat under refrigeration to prevent
drying. Best established where the tree is intended to grow
permanently. Propagation is by acorn. The species does not
transplant well after the first year of growth.

## Relevance
Cork oak is one of the most sustainably productive trees in
Mediterranean permaculture. Its renewable bark harvest, combined
with acorn production for livestock, [[mollison-tree-crop-systems-permaculture]], demonstrating the
permaculture principle of designing for permanence.
