# Cladobotryum

## Description

Cladobotryum is a large anamorphic genus of mycoparasitic fungi in the Hypocreales ([[ascomycota]]), serving as the primary anamorph (asexual stage) of the teleomorphic genus Hypomyces. Rogerson and Samuels (1993) expanded the genus to incorporate anamorphs from 12 previously separate genera, making it one of the most morphologically diverse anamorph genera among [[fungicolous-fungi]]. Species of Cladobotryum are obligate or facultative mycoparasites that colonize and destroy sporocarps of aphyllophorales, agarics, and other macrofungi.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Eukaryota
- **[[kingdom-fungi]]
- **Division:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Sordariomycetes
- **Order:** Hypocreales
- **Family:** Hypocreaceae
- **Genus:** Cladobotryum (anamorph)
- **Teleomorph:** Hypomyces

## Key Species

- **C. dendroides** (teleomorph Hypomyces rosellus) — One of the most common species, found on various aphyllophorales
- **C. varium** (teleomorph H. aurantius) — Disrupts host cytoplasm and causes irreversible changes in host cells (Kellock and Dix 1984)
- **C. fungicola** (teleomorph H. semitranslucens) — Found on Aphyllophorales; also reported on Auricularia species
- **C. clavisporum** (teleomorph H. polyporinus) — Mostly on Trametes versicolor
- **C. polypori** (teleomorph H. mycophilus) — On various aphyllophorales, often Polyporus species; reported from Auricularia polytricha
- **C. stereicola** — Preferentially grows on Chondrostereum purpureum
- **C. orthosporum** (teleomorph H. orthosporus) — On various aphyllophorales
- **C. mycophilum** (teleomorph H. odoratus) — On various aphyllophorales

## Morphological Characteristics

- **Conidiophores:** Phialidic, showing extensive morphological variation across species (illustrated in Rogerson and Samuels 1993, Fig. 17.2)
- **Conidia:** Hyaline, variable in shape from ellipsoidal to fusiform, produced in slimy heads or chains
- **Mycelium:** White to pinkish, spreading over host sporocarps
- **Synanamorphs:** Some species also produce chlamydosporic, aleurial, or bulbillar synanamorphs

## Taxonomic History

The genus was significantly expanded by Rogerson and Samuels (1993), who combined anamorphs from:

1. Arnoldiomyces
2. Eurasina
3. Helminthophora
4. Pseudohansfordia
5. Sibirina
6. Sympodiophora
7. Part of Trichothecium
8. Several other former genera

This consolidation was based on morphological study and teleomorph connections to Hypomyces species.

## Ecology and Host Range

Cladobotryum species parasitize two major host groups:

1. **Aphyllophorales:** The largest group of Hypomyces/Cladobotryum associations (23 species). Generally not host-specific. Found on Stereum, Trametes, Polyporus, Piptoporus, and many other genera.

2. **Agarics:** Tend to be more host-specific, often restricted to a host family or genus. Particularly notable as parasites of cultivated mushrooms.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Cobweb disease:** Cladobotryum species (particularly C. dendroides and related taxa) are causal agents of cobweb disease in commercial mushroom cultivation, where they colonize and destroy mushroom fruiting bodies
- **Biocontrol potential:** Some species show antagonism toward pathogenic fungi and could have applications in biological control
- **Economic impact:** Infections can cause significant losses in button mushroom (Agaricus bisporus) and specialty mushroom operations
- **Taxonomic importance:** The connection between Cladobotryum anamorphs and Hypomyces teleomorphs is a model system for understanding fungal life cycles

## Notes

The morphological diversity within Cladobotryum reflects the heterogeneity of its teleomorph genus Hypomyces. Species identification requires careful microscopic examination of conidiophore and conidial morphology. Molecular studies are increasingly important for accurate species delineation, particularly in the agriculturally significant species that affect mushroom cultivation.
