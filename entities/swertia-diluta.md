## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Scientific classification:**
- Kingdom: Plantae
- Clade: Angiosperms
- Clade: Eudicots
- Order: Gentianales
- Family: Gentianaceae
- Genus: *Swertia*
- Species: *S. diluta* (Batalin) T.N.Ho & S.W.Liu

Basionym: *Ophelia diluta* Batalin (1894). Transferred to Swertia by Ho and Liu in 1993. The species epithet "diluta" means "pale" or "diluted," referring to the pale flower color.

The plant grows 20–60 cm tall with an erect, simple or branched stem. Basal leaves are oblong-lanceolate, 2–5 cm long, forming a rosette that withers early. Stem leaves are opposite, linear-lanceolate, sessile, 1–4 cm long.

Inflorescences are terminal or axillary cymes bearing numerous small flowers. Each flower is 1–1.5 cm across with four pale violet to nearly white petals bearing two nectar glands each.

The corolla lobes have distinctive fringed nectary scales at the base, characteristic of the genus Swertia. Stamens are four, inserted on the corolla tube. The ovary is superior, unilocular.

Flowering occurs from July to September. Fruits are ovoid capsules 6–8 mm long, dehiscing along two valves to release numerous small, brown, angular seeds.

## Distribution and Habitat

Swertia diluta is native to China, with a broad distribution spanning Heilongjiang, Jilin, Liaoning, Inner Mongolia, Hebei, Shanxi, Shaanxi, Gansu, Qinghai, Sichuan, Yunnan, and Hubei provinces.

The species also occurs in Korea, Japan, and parts of the Russian Far East. It inhabits grasslands, forest margins, hillsides, meadows, and disturbed roadside habitats at 300–3,000 meters.

Unlike many Swertia species restricted to alpine or wetland habitats, Swertia diluta is relatively adaptable. It tolerates a range of soil types but prefers well-drained, slightly acidic to neutral substrates.

Populations are often large and locally abundant, especially in disturbed habitats. The species behaves as a pioneer, colonizing open ground after disturbance in montane grasslands.

## Phytochemistry and Pharmacology

The primary [[ursolic-acid]] have been isolated from aerial parts. Flavonoids such as swertisin and isovitexin contribute additional anti-inflammatory properties.

Pharmacological studies demonstrate hepatoprotective effects against carbon tetrachloride-induced liver damage in animal models. The plant extract shows significant choleretic activity, increasing bile production.

Anti-inflammatory activity has been confirmed through inhibition of NF-κB [[psilocybin-therapeutic-applications]] include treating icteric hepatitis, cholecystitis, dysentery, and fever. The herb is typically prepared as a decoction or tincture using dried whole plants harvested during flowering.

Modern clinical applications in China include treatment of chronic hepatitis B, liver fibrosis, and gallstone disease. Several commercial pharmaceutical preparations containing Swertia diluta extract are marketed in China.

The recommended daily dose in TCM practice is 3–9 grams of dried herb as a decoction. Preparations should be used cautiously in patients with cold-deficiency spleen patterns. The herb is contraindicated during pregnancy.

## Cultivation and Conservation

Swertia diluta is cultivated on a limited scale in China for medicinal use. Most commercial material is still wild-collected from natural populations across the central and western provinces.

Cultivation requires cool-temperate conditions, well-drained soil, and partial shade. Seeds require cold stratification and surface sowing. The species is grown as an annual crop in Heilongjiang and Jilin provinces.
