# Pyrostegia (Flame Vine)

## Description

Pyrostegia is a genus of vigorous evergreen climbing vines in the Bignoniaceae family, most notably represented by *Pyrostegia venusta* (syn. P. ignea), the flame vine or golden shower vine. Native to tropical and subtropical South America, it is widely cultivated for its spectacular displays of brilliant orange tubular flowers. Mollison recommends Pyrostegia as a perennial non-invasive vine that can be run completely across rooftops for climate moderation in desert homes, and as a cooling vine for western aspects of buildings.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Family:** Bignoniaceae
- **Genus:** Pyrostegia
- **Species:** P. venusta (most cultivated)
- **Common names:** Flame vine, golden shower vine, flaming trumpet
- **Growth habit:** Vigorous evergreen woody vine (liana)

## Key Facts

- Native to Brazil, Paraguay, Argentina, and Bolivia
- Produces dense curtains of brilliant orange-red tubular flowers, 5-8 cm long
- Flowers in late winter to early spring, providing vivid color during otherwise drab seasons
- Leaves are compound with 2-3 leaflets, each 4-8 cm, plus a tendril for climbing
- Climbs via tendrils and can reach 10-20 m in height on suitable support
- Extremely vigorous growth; can cover large structures rapidly
- Moderately drought-tolerant once established
- Prefers full sun to partial shade and well-drained soils
- Hardy to light frost; performs best in frost-free subtropical and tropical climates
- Attracts hummingbirds and other nectar-feeding birds

## Role in Permaculture Design

Mollison positions Pyrostegia within a suite of desert-climate vines:

- **Roof coverage:** Can be run completely across rooftops to moderate building temperature
- **Western aspect shading:** Dense evergreen foliage blocks harsh afternoon sun
- **Non-invasive:** Unlike [[dolichos]], or Pelargonium) standing out from wall
- **Rooftop:** Pyrostegia running across entire roof surface
- **Eastern side:** Winter-deciduous or summer-deciduous vine on screen fixed out from eastern sun
- **Rear door area:** Closed trellis with thick bark mulch below, water sprays for cooling
- Combined, these create a significantly cooler microclimate around and within the building

## Cultivation Notes

- Propagated from semi-hardwood cuttings or layering
- Plant in well-drained soil with full sun exposure for best flowering
- Requires strong support structure; mature vines become heavy
- Prune after flowering to maintain shape and prevent overgrowth
- Moderate water needs; drought-tolerant once established but flowers better with some irrigation
- May need tying to support in early establishment phase

## Relevance to Cultivation

Pyrostegia exemplifies the [[vetiver-grass]] - ground-level companion for cooling systems

## Pruning and Maintenance

Pyrostegia requires specific management to maintain its dual ornamental and functional roles:

- Major pruning immediately after the winter-spring flowering period
- Remove dead, diseased, and crossing stems to maintain airflow
- Thin excessive growth to prevent self-shading and reduce structural load on trellis
- Shorten lateral branches to encourage dense canopy for optimal shade
- Avoid pruning in late summer-autumn to preserve flowering wood for the next season
- Root pruning can control overly vigorous plants in confined spaces

## Climate Adaptation

While Pyrostegia is subtropical, it has shown adaptability:

- Grows well in Mediterranean climates with warm, dry summers
- Tolerates coastal conditions including salt-laden winds
- Performs best above 10C; suffers leaf damage below 2-3C
- In marginal frost zones, plant against warm north-facing walls (southern hemisphere: north-facing)
- Established plants recover from light frost damage within a single growing season
- In desert applications, protection from hot dry winds during establishment is beneficial
