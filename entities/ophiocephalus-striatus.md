## Ophiocephalus striatus (Striped Snakehead)

## Description
*Ophiocephalus striatus* (now reclassified as *Channa striata*), the striped snakehead, is a predatory air-breathing freshwater fish native to South and Southeast Asia. It is a key species in tropical pond polyculture systems, serving as the predator component that controls overpopulation of smaller fish. Its ability to breathe atmospheric air makes it invaluable in oxygen-depleted tropical aquaculture systems.

## Classification
- **Kingdom**: Animalia
- **Phylum**: Chordata
- **Class**: Actinopterygii
- **Order**: Anabantiformes
- **Family**: Channidae
- **Genus**: *Ophiocephalus* (syn. *Channa*)
- **Species**: *O. striatus*

## Key Facts

### Air-Breathing Adaptation
- Possesses a specialised suprabranchial organ (labyrinth organ) for aerial respiration
- Can survive in completely [[aact-ph-and-anaerobic-conditions]] (below 1 ppm dissolved oxygen)
- Survives in weedy, stagnant tropical shallows where most fish die
- Capable of overland migration during wet weather using sustained wriggling motion

### Role in Polyculture
- Used as a predator in Thai and Malaysian rice paddy systems
- Controls overpopulation of smaller fish species, preventing stunting
- Stocked alongside weed-eating species (*Trichogaster pectoralis*, *[[clarias]] macrocephalus*, *Clarias batrachus*, *Anabas testudineus*)
- Functions analogously to cage-reared predatory fish used to cull pond-bred fish

### Environmental Tolerance
- **Oxygen**: Survives complete anoxia; air-breathing capability
- **Temperature**: Tropical warm-water species
- **Habitat**: Stagnant ponds, swamps, rice paddies, canals, flooded fields
- **Hardiness**: Extremely resilient; survives out of water for extended periods

### Production
- Valued food fish across Southeast Asia
- Used in traditional medicine for wound healing (contains albumin and amino acids)
- Can be cultured in derelict swampy ponds that are unusable for other aquaculture

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology
- The predator-prey dynamic in snakehead polycultures parallels the ecological balance needed in [[mushroom-cultivation-environments]]
- Snakehead ponds produce rich organic sediments useful as compost base for [[mushroom-substrates]]
- Air-breathing adaptation exemplifies selecting organisms matched to site conditions -- a principle equally important in choosing mushroom species for specific growing environments
- The integrated rice-fish-predator model demonstrates multi-trophic system design used in mycorestoration
