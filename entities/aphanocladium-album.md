## Aphanocladium album

**Type:** Species (Ascomycete)
**Category:** Mycoparasite / Hyperparasite Complex
**Also known as:** Includes Lecanicillium dimorphum, L. aphanocladii (see taxonomy)

## Overview
Aphanocladium album is a complex of closely related ascomycete
fungi with remarkably diverse mycoparasitic lifestyles. Modern
taxonomic revision has revealed that what was historically called
A. album actually comprises multiple distinct species with
different [[bloomfield-slime-molds-myxomycetes-plasmodial-protists]]), rust hyperparasites, and mushroom
pathogens. This taxonomic complexity means that much of the older
literature requires careful reevaluation.

The name Aphanocladium album has been applied to several distinct
taxa. The true A. album, in the strict sense, is a
myxomyceticolous species that attacks only noncalcareous
myxomycetes (Rogerson and Stephenson 1993). Lecanicillium
dimorphum, formerly identified as A. album, is a necrotrophic
parasite that efficiently invades aecidiospores of the stem rust
fungus Puccinia graminis (Koc and Defago 1983).

Lecanicillium aphanocladii, another former A. album, is a
polyphagous species reported to infect mushroom sporocarps and
reduce yield in cultivated mushroom operations, particularly in
Australia (van Zaayen and Gams 1982). The transfer of these
species to Lecanicillium reflects molecular phylogenetic evidence
placing them closer to entomopathogenic Verticillium-like fungi
than to Aphanocladium.

## Taxonomy and Classification
- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Sordariomycetes
- **Order:** Hypocreales
- **Family:** Cordycipitaceae
- **Genus:** Aphanocladium / Lecanicillium (revised)
- **Species:** A. album sensu stricto / L. dimorphum / L. aphanocladii

The taxonomic history of the A. album complex illustrates the
challenges of classifying morphologically similar fungi. Three
distinct ecological types have been separated: myxomyceticolous
(true A. album), rust hyperparasitic (L. dimorphum), and
mushroom-pathogenic (L. aphanocladii). Each occupies a
fundamentally different ecological niche despite their
morphological similarity under light microscopy.

## Key Details

### Myxomyceticolous Parasitism
The true A. album attacks only noncalcareous myxomycetes, growing
on their [[fungi-cultivated-mushrooms-pathogens-competitors]] such as [[foliar-epiphytic-fungi-sooty-mildews-powdery-mildews]] than the specialized
[[ampelomyces-quisqualis]], which dominates this particular niche as
the primary powdery mildew hyperparasite worldwide.
