# Ramaria botrytis (Coral Mushroom)

## Overview

**Ramaria botrytis**, commonly known as the clustered coral mushroom, pink-tipped coral, or rosy coral, is an edible basidiomycete mushroom in the family Gomphaceae. It is one of the most visually striking forest mushrooms, producing large, cauliflower-like fruiting bodies with branching coral-like structures that are white to cream-colored at the base, transitioning to beautiful pinkish-rose at the branch tips. Despite its showy appearance, it is often overlooked because it grows on the forest floor partially buried in [[comparison-fruiting-body-vs-mycelium-extracts-medicinal-mushrooms]] may become brownish or ochraceous. The flesh is white.
- **Flesh:** Firm and white when young, becoming soft and crumbly with age. The flesh has a mild, pleasant odor and a mildly peppery or slightly bitter taste.
- **[[beta-glucans]] and other polysaccharides contribute to immune system stimulation
- **Antimicrobial:** Some Ramaria species show activity against Gram-positive bacteria and select fungi

The pink pigmentation of the branch tips is due to carotenoid compounds, which may have additional antioxidant properties. The specific carotenoid profile of R. botrytis has not been fully characterized.

## Ecological Significance

As an ectomycorrhizal fungus, Ramaria botrytis contributes to [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]] through decomposition of fruiting bodies
- Provides food habitat for invertebrates, particularly beetle larvae that develop within the fleshy branches
- Serves as a food source for small mammals, including squirrels and rodents
- The presence of diverse Ramaria species is an indicator of mature, undisturbed forest ecosystems with rich mycorrhizal communities

## Conservation Status

Ramaria botrytis is not currently listed as threatened, but like many ectomycorrhizal species, it is sensitive to:

- Forest clearing and habitat fragmentation
- Soil compaction from logging equipment
- Air pollution and acid deposition
- Changes in forest composition due to [[mollison-pdc-forest-management-products]]. Collection for food should be done sustainably, leaving some specimens to complete their [[ectomycorrhizal-associations]] — Mycorrhizal ecology and forest dynamics
