## Monomethylhydrazine

## Description

Monomethylhydrazine (MMH) is a volatile, highly toxic chemical compound with the formula CH₃NHNH₂. It is a colorless, hygroscopic liquid with a faint ammonia-like odor. MMH is primarily known as a hypergolic rocket propellant, used extensively in spacecraft thruster systems because it ignites spontaneously upon contact with oxidizers such as nitrogen tetroxide. In [[mycology]], MMH is notable as the toxic hydrolysis product of gyromitrin, a compound found in false morel mushrooms of the genus Gyromitra.

## Classification

- **Chemical formula:** CH₃NHNH₂
- **Molecular weight:** 46.07 g/mol
- **CAS number:** 60-34-4
- **Compound class:** Hydrazine derivative
- **Appearance:** Colorless liquid

## Key Facts

### Origin in False Morels

Gyromitra species (false morels) produce gyromitrin, a water-soluble hydrazine compound. When false morels are consumed or heated, gyromitrin hydrolyzes into monomethylhydrazine. This [[decomposition]] occurs through the following pathway:

1. Gyromitrin (N-methyl-N-formylhydrazone) present in raw mushroom tissue
2. Hydrolysis releases monomethylhydrazine (MMH)
3. MMH is volatile and toxic, responsible for false morel poisoning

### Toxicity

Monomethylhydrazine is a potent toxin with multiple mechanisms of harm:

- **Hepatotoxic:** Causes liver damage through metabolic interference
- **Neurotoxic:** Affects the central nervous system
- **Carcinogenic:** Classified as a probable human carcinogen (IARC Group 2B)
- **Hemolytic:** Can cause destruction of red blood cells
- **Vitamin B6 antagonist:** Interferes with pyridoxine metabolism, potentially causing seizures

Symptoms of gyromitrin/MMH poisoning from false morel consumption include nausea, vomiting, dizziness, diarrhea, and in severe cases, seizures, coma, and death. The toxin is partially removed by parboiling false morels in well-ventilated areas, though this practice remains controversial.

### Rocket Fuel Applications

Despite its toxicity, MMH has critical aerospace applications:

- **Hypergolic propellant:** Ignites on contact with nitrogen tetroxide (N₂O₄) oxidizer
- **Spacecraft thrusters:** Used in orbital maneuvering systems and reaction control systems
- **Space shuttle:** The Space Shuttle OMS (Orbital Maneuvering System) used MMH
- **Satellite propulsion:** Common in satellite station-keeping thrusters

### Dual-Use Organism Connection

Cotter highlights the remarkable dual-use potential of false morels (Gyromitra spp.) in the context of space cultivation. These mushrooms simultaneously produce:

- **Food:** High-protein mushrooms that are edible once the hydrazine is removed through proper preparation
- **Fuel:** MMH, a component of some rocket propulsion systems

This makes Gyromitra uniquely suited as a combined food and fuel source for long-duration space missions, where resource efficiency is paramount.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

### False Morel Identification

Understanding MMH and its origin in Gyromitra species is critical for forager safety. False morels can be confused with true morels ([[morchella]] spp.), with potentially fatal consequences. Key identification differences include:

- False morels have irregular, brain-like caps; true morels have honeycomb-patterned caps
- False morels are chambered inside; true morels are hollow
- False morels cap attaches only at the top; true morels cap attaches along the entire stem

### Processing for Edibility

In some European culinary traditions, [[gyromitra-esculenta]] is consumed after careful preparation:

- Parboiling in abundant water (at least 3:1 water-to-mushroom ratio)
- Boiling for at least 5-10 minutes in a well-ventilated space (MMH is volatile)
- Discarding the boiling water (contains dissolved MMH)
- Repeating the process for additional safety

However, many authorities recommend against consumption entirely due to residual toxin risk and individual sensitivity variation.

### Space Agriculture Considerations

For space cultivation, Gyromitra's production of both food protein and rocket fuel precursor in a single organism represents an elegant closed-loop resource strategy, though the extreme toxicity of MMH presents significant handling challenges in confined environments.

## Safety Considerations

- MMH is extremely toxic by inhalation, ingestion, and skin absorption
- Proper ventilation is essential when processing Gyromitra species
- Never eat raw false morels
- MMH is flammable and reacts violently with oxidizers
- Personal protective equipment is mandatory in industrial handling
