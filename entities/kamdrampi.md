## Kamdrampi

## Description

Kamdrampi is the Campa (Asháninka) name for ayahuasca, the hallucinogenic brew used in shamanic ceremonies among the [[campa-people]] of the Peruvian Amazon. The Campa also refer to the brew as "kampocamakenti," meaning "to vomit," reflecting its extremely bitter and sometimes emetic qualities. Kamdrampi is central to the Campa shamanistic ceremony described by [[gerald-weiss]].

## Classification

- **Category**: Hallucinogenic brew / entheogen
- **Botanical origin**: [[banisteriopsis-caapi]] vine combined with leaves of Psychotria viridis (called horéva in Campa)
- **Chemical class**: Beta-carboline and tryptamine alkaloids (harmine, harmaline, DMT)
- **Cultural context**: Campa (Asháninka) people, Peruvian Amazon

## Preparation

Kamdrampi is prepared by boiling fragments of the ayahuasca vine (Banisteriopsis caapi), which the Campa find growing wild and transplant to the vicinity of their settlements. The vine preparation is combined with leaves from an uncultivated tree bearing the Campa name horéva — identified as Psychotria viridis, the same DMT-containing plant used in many ayahuasca preparations across the Amazon.

## The Kamdrampi Ceremony

The Campa kamdrampi ceremony follows a distinctive format:

1. **Preparation**: At nightfall, participants convene, arranging themselves sitting or lying on mats in the settlement clearing or under a house roof, with women separated from men in Campa fashion
2. **Distribution**: The shaman drinks from a small gourd bowl, then gives each participant a drink — repeated at intervals until the supply is consumed
3. **Onset**: About half an hour later, the drug takes effect and the shaman begins to sing
4. **Duration**: The shaman sings one song after another as long as he is under the influence, and the séance may last until dawn

## Ceremonial Characteristics

### Atmosphere
The entire atmosphere of the ceremony is one of "decorum without frenzy," even though the shaman is in a drugged trance. The ceremony follows a definite, if simple, format, presenting the appearance of a group reverently making contact with good spirits under religious leadership.

### Spirit Communication
The ceremony involves communication with "good spirits" who visit the group in human form, festively attired. They sing and dance before the assembled mortals, but only the shaman perceives them clearly. When the shaman sings, he is understood to be repeating what he hears the spirits sing.

### Shamanic vs. Priestly Elements
The kamdrampi ceremony exhibits a notable ambivalence between shamanistic séance and priestly ritual:
- **Shamanistic aspect**: Spirits communicate through the shaman to spectators
- **Priestly aspect**: The ceremony involves worship and adoration of the good spirits
- This duality is described as a "Necker illusion" — the same behavior looks like a séance one moment and worship the next

## Spirit Songs

The ceremony includes specific songs marking the appearance of spirit beings:

### Kodkiti (Hawk Spirit) Song
> Tobacco, tobacco, pure tobacco
> It comes from River's Beginning
> Kodkiti, the hawk, brings it to you
> Its flowers are flying, tobacco
> It comes to your aid, tobacco
> Kodkiti, the hawk, is its owner

### Hummingbird Spirit Song
> Hummingbirds, hummingbirds, they come running
> Hummingbirds, hummingbirds, dark appearance
> Hummingbirds, hummingbirds, all our brothers
> Hummingbirds, hummingbirds, they all hover
> Hummingbirds, hummingbirds, group without blemish

## Key Facts

- The Campa transplant wild ayahuasca vines to their settlements, showing deliberate cultivation
- Psychotria viridis (horéva) leaves are the DMT-containing additive
- The ceremony explicitly lacks spirit possession — the shaman merely relays spirit songs
- Soul-flight is an optional element, not central to the ceremony
- A regional variant involves all men taking turns singing, emphasizing worship over shamanic mediumship

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

The Campa practice of transplanting wild ayahuasca vines to settlements represents an early form of cultivation of Banisteriopsis caapi. Combined with the use of Psychotria viridis (horéva), the kamdrampi preparation follows the classic ayahuasca formula found across the Amazon. The ceremony provides important ethnographic context for understanding the religious and social dimensions of ayahuasca use.
