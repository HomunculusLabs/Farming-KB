# Oyster Mushroom (*Pleurotus ostreatus*)

## Overview

The oyster mushroom (*Pleurotus ostreatus*) is one of the most widely cultivated and ecologically versatile mushrooms in the world. Named for its oyster shell-shaped cap, it is prized for its culinary qualities, ease of cultivation, and remarkable environmental applications. Native to temperate and subtropical forests worldwide, [[cultivation-of-oyster-mushrooms]] play a critical role in forest decomposition and have become a cornerstone species in mycoremediation and [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-permaculture-design-principles]].

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Pleurotaceae
- **Genus:** *Pleurotus*
- **Species:** *P. ostreatus*

### Notable Relatives in the Genus
- ***P. pulmonarius*** ([[growing-gourmet-phoenix-oyster-pulmonarius-cultivation]]) — similar, prefers warmer temperatures
- ***P. eryngii*** (King oyster) — thick-stemmed, meaty texture
- ***P. citrinopileatus*** (Golden oyster) — bright yellow, nutty flavor
- ***P. djamor*** (Pink oyster) — vibrant pink, tropical, fast-growing
- ***P. tuber-regium*** (King tuber) — produces edible sclerotia in tropical Africa

## Identification

### Macroscopic Features
- **Cap:** 5–25 cm diameter; fan-shaped to oyster-shaped; smooth surface; convex when young, becoming flat and sometimes funnel-shaped with age
- **Color:** Highly variable — white, gray, tan, brown, or blue-gray depending on strain and growing conditions
- **Gills:** Decurrent (running down the stem); white to cream; close, narrow gills
- **Stem:** Short, lateral or eccentric (off-center); 1–5 cm; white to pale; often absent in wild specimens growing directly from wood
- **Flesh:** White, firm, and thick when young; softens with age
- **Spore print:** White to lilac-gray
- **Odor:** Mild, pleasant, anise-like in some strains
- **Taste:** Mild, slightly sweet, and nutty

### Habitat
- **Substrate:** Dead or dying hardwood — oak, beech, aspen, birch, maple, poplar, willow
- **Growth pattern:** Shelf-like clusters (caespitose) on standing dead trees, fallen logs, and stumps
- **Season:** Autumn through spring in [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]]; year-round in tropical/subtropical areas
- **Ecological role:** Primary wood decomposer (white rot fungus)

## Bioactive Compounds

### Lovastatin
Oyster mushrooms naturally produce **lovastatin**, a cholesterol-lowering compound also found in red yeast rice. Levels range from 10–100 mg/kg dry weight, making oyster mushrooms a functional food for cardiovascular health.

### Beta-Glucans
Contains 1,3/1,6-β-glucans at concentrations of 8–25% of dry weight:
- Potent immunomodulators
- Activate macrophages and NK cells
- Anti-tumor activity demonstrated in animal studies
