# Muscimol

**Muscimol** is a potent psychoactive isoxazole alkaloid and the principal bioactive constituent of *[[amanita-muscaria]]* (fly agaric) and related *Amanita* species. It acts as a direct orthosteric agonist at GABA-A receptors, producing sedative, dissociative, and hallucinogenic effects. One of the few naturally occurring GABA-A agonists, muscimol has served as an important pharmacological research tool for over five decades.

## Chemical Identity

- **IUPAC Name**: 5-(aminomethyl)-3-hydroxyisoxazole
- **Molecular Formula**: C4H6N2O2
- **Molecular Weight**: 114.10 g/mol
- **CAS Number**: 2763-96-4
- **Chemical Class**: Isoxazole alkaloid; GABA analog
- **Appearance**: White to off-white crystalline solid
- **Melting Point**: 172-174 degrees C (decomposes)
- **Solubility**: Soluble in water, ethanol, DMSO

## Chemical Structure

Muscimol is a 5-membered heterocyclic compound containing an isoxazole ring (an oxygen-nitrogen heterocycle) bearing a hydroxyl group at position 3 and an aminomethyl group at position 5. The isoxazole ring serves as a bioisostere for the carboxylate group of GABA, allowing muscimol to bind the same receptor site while being metabolically more stable and capable of crossing the blood-brain barrier.

## Natural Sources

Muscimol is the principal psychoactive constituent of several *Amanita* mushroom species:

- **Amanita muscaria** (fly agaric) — the most well-known source; contains muscimol at approximately 0.03-0.11% of fresh weight
- **[[amanita-pantherina]]** (panther cap) — contains significant muscimol and ibotenic acid
- **Amanita gemmata** (gemmed amanita)
- **Amanita regalis** (royal fly agaric)

In fresh mushrooms, ibotenic acid predominates. Drying and heating convert ibotenic acid to muscimol via decarboxylation, which is why traditional preparation methods involve parboiling or drying before consumption.

## Biosynthesis

In *Amanita* species, muscimol is produced through a dedicated biosynthetic pathway:

1. **Precursor**: L-Glutamic acid
2. **Hydroxylation**: Formation of (3S)-3-hydroxyglutamic acid
3. **Oxidation/cyclization**: Conversion to ibotenic acid (C5H6NO4)
4. **Decarboxylation**: Yields muscimol (C4H6N2O2)

This step occurs both enzymatically (via a specific decarboxylase) and non-enzymatically through heat, explaining why fresh mushrooms are more toxic while properly prepared dried material is richer in muscimol.

## Pharmacology

### Mechanism of Action

Muscimol is a direct orthosteric agonist of the GABA-A receptor — one of the most potent known:

- **Binding Site**: Same as endogenous GABA on the beta+/alpha- subunit interface of the GABA-A receptor
- **Mechanism**: Channel opening allows chloride influx, hyperpolarizing the neuron
- **EC50**: ~10-50 nM at GABA-A receptors, more potent than GABA itself
- **Subtype Selectivity**: Preference for alpha1, alpha4, beta2/3, and gamma2 subunits
- **GABA-B**: Weak partial agonist at very high concentrations only
- **Blood-Brain Barrier**: Crosses readily unlike GABA
- **Onset**: 30-90 minutes; duration 4-10 hours

### Comparison to Other GABAergic Drugs

| Agent | Mechanism | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Muscimol | Direct GABA-A agonist | Orthosteric site; strongest activation |
| Benzodiazepines | Positive allosteric modulator | Requires GABA presence |
| Barbiturates | Positive allosteric modulator | Can directly open channel |
| GABA | Endogenous agonist | Does not cross BBB |

## Relationship to Ibotenic Acid and GABA

Ibotenic acid is a glutamate receptor agonist (excitatory neurotoxin) responsible for many unpleasant effects of raw *Amanita* ingestion including nausea, agitation, and confusion. Decarboxylation converts it to muscimol, changing the pharmacological profile from excitotoxic to inhibitory. Muscimol is a structural analog of GABA where the carboxylic acid group is replaced by a 3-hydroxyisoxazole ring, preserving hydrogen-bonding capacity while providing metabolic stability, enhanced lipophilicity, and greater receptor potency.

## Effects and Stages of Intoxication

*Amanita muscaria* intoxication (driven primarily by muscimol) is traditionally described in four stages:

### Stage 1 — Onset (30-120 minutes post-ingestion)

Nausea, perspiration, salivation, tingling or numbness in extremities, mild visual distortions, dizziness, and pupil dilation.

### Stage 2 — Sedation (1-3 hours)

Deep somnolence, dream-like hypnagogic imagery, auditory distortions, wavering between wakefulness and sleep, reduced motor coordination, and sensations of heaviness or floating.

### Stage 3 — Dissociation (3-6 hours)

Marked dissociation and depersonalization, altered perception of time and space, vivid hallucinations, macropsia or micropsia ("Alice in Wonderland syndrome"), euphoria or dysphoria, and clumsy wobbly gait.

### Stage 4 — Resolution (6-12+ hours)

Profound amnesia of the middle phases, confusion upon waking, prolonged sleep, residual ataxia, and gradual return to baseline.

Effects vary enormously by dose, preparation method, and the muscimol-to-ibotenic acid ratio.

## Toxicology

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| **LD50 (mouse, i.p.)** | ~2.5-4.5 mg/kg |
| **LD50 (mouse, oral)** | ~45 mg/kg |
| **LD50 (rat, oral)** | ~38 mg/kg |

Muscimol is strongly synergistic with other CNS depressants including alcohol, benzodiazepines, and barbiturates. Flumazenil has limited efficacy as an antidote since muscimol binds the orthosteric GABA site. Picrotoxin, a GABA-A channel blocker, is more effective as an experimental antagonist. Human fatalities are exceedingly rare and no established dependence syndrome has been documented.

## Chemical Synthesis

Multiple synthetic routes to muscimol have been developed since its isolation in the 1960s. The simplest route is thermal or chemical decarboxylation of ibotenic acid, consistent with the natural pathway. Other approaches include aminomethylation of 3-hydroxyisoxazole derivatives using Mannich-type reactions, and construction of the isoxazole ring via cycloaddition with hydroxylamine followed by functionalization. The first total synthesis was reported in the late 1960s. Modern syntheses typically proceed in 3-6 steps with moderate overall yields.

## History of Use

### Siberian Shamanism

Indigenous peoples of Siberia — including the Koryak, Chukchi, Itelmen, and Kamchadal — used *Amanita muscaria* as a sacramental intoxicant. Shamans consumed the mushroom to enter trance states for divination, healing, and spiritual journeys. A notable practice was urine recycling: since muscimol is excreted largely unmetabolized, tribesmen would drink the shaman's urine to experience the effects through several recycles, each producing a cleaner intoxication.

### European Folklore

*A. muscaria* appears throughout European fairy tales and iconography, and was likely the inspiration for the caterpillar's mushroom in Lewis Carroll's *Alice's Adventures in Wonderland* (1865). The "Alice in Wonderland syndrome" (micropsia/macropsia) is a recognized neurological phenomenon associated with muscimol intoxication.

### Soma Hypothesis

R. Gordon Wasson proposed in *Soma: Divine Mushroom of Immortality* (1968) that muscimol-containing *Amanita* mushrooms were the identity of Soma, the sacred drink of Vedic Hinduism. This hypothesis remains debated but influential in entheogen scholarship.

The active constituents were isolated in the 1960s: ibotenic acid in 1964 and muscimol in 1965 by independent Japanese and European groups.

## Legal Status

Muscimol is unscheduled and legal in most countries including the US, UK, Canada, Germany, and the Netherlands. Products containing it may fall under drug or novel food regulations in some jurisdictions.

## Research Applications

Muscimol is a gold-standard pharmacological tool used to characterize GABA-A receptor subtypes, study GABAergic neurotransmission, and as a radioligand for binding assays. Direct brain microinjection reversibly silences specific brain regions as a pharmacological lesion technique. Emerging therapeutic investigations include insomnia, anxiety, epilepsy, chronic pain, and substance use disorders.
