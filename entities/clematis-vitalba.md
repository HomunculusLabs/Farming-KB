C. vitalba is considered an invasive weed in New Zealand and parts of [[clematis-integrifolia]] var. integrifolia

Stems are slender, ribbed, and climb by means of twisting leaf petioles around supports.

Mature vines can reach 20–30 m in length with a stem diameter of up to 10 cm.

Leaves are opposite and pinnately compound, with 3–5 leaflets per leaf.

Leaflets are ovate to cordate, 5–12 cm long, with dentate margins and a bright green upper surface.

Flowers are creamy-white, 15–25 mm in diameter, with four prominent sepals and no true petals.

They are arranged in open panicles and bloom from July to September.

Male and female flowers are borne on separate plants (dioecious).

Fruit is an achene, 3–5 mm long, with a persistent plumose style 30–50 mm long forming the characteristic "beard."

These feathery seed heads give the plant its common name and provide winter visual interest.

## Distribution and Habitat

C. vitalba is native to Europe, from the UK and France eastward to the Caucasus and western Asia.

It occurs in hedgerows, woodland edges, riverbanks, and disturbed ground at low to mid elevations.

The species tolerates a wide range of soil types including chalk, clay, and sandy loams.

It prefers partial shade but grows aggressively in full sun at forest margins.

## Cultivation

Old man's beard is hardy to USDA zones 5–9 and is adaptable to most [[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]] by cutting back to low buds.

In cultivation, C. vitalba is sometimes used as a rootstock for more ornamental Clematis cultivars.

Its vigorous growth makes it suitable for covering large walls, fences, and pergolas.

The ornamental value is primarily in the fluffy seed heads rather than the modest flowers.

## Ecological Role

In its native range, C. vitalba provides late-season nectar for bees, hoverflies, and butterflies.

The dense foliage offers nesting habitat and cover for small birds.
