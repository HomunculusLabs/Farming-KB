## Benlate (Benomyl)

## Description

Benlate is the brand name for benomyl, a systemic fungicide that was widely used in agriculture and horticulture. In [[mushroom-cultivation]], Benlate (specifically the 50% wettable powder formulation) is used in antibiotic agar formulations to suppress mold contaminants while allowing mushroom [[mycelium]] to grow. The antibiotic agar formula containing Benlate was recommended by Dr. Bob Blanchette from the University of Minnesota for screening and purifying bacterial-contaminated mushroom cultures.

## Classification

- **Category**: Chemical agent
- **Type**: Fungicide / laboratory reagent
- **Chemical class**: Benzimidazole fungicide
- **Formulation**: 50% wettable powder

## Key Facts

- Benlate is the brand name for benomyl, a systemic benzimidazole fungicide
- Used at 0.06 grams (50% wettable powder) per 1,000 ml of antibiotic agar
- Part of an antibiotic agar formula for screening and purifying mushroom cultures
- The formula also includes malt extract (15 g), agar (15 g), yeast extract (2 g), [[streptomycin-sulfate]] (0.01 g), and lactic acid (2 ml)
- Added to the base medium before autoclaving; [[streptomycin]] and lactic acid added after cooling to 113-122°F (45-50°C)
- Effective against a broad spectrum of fungi including many mold contaminants
- Benomyl was withdrawn from the market in many countries but the chemical and similar benzimidazole fungicides remain available

## Relevance to Cultivation

Benlate is used in the [[antibiotic-agar-sandwich]] technique for rescuing mushroom cultures from mold and bacterial contamination. In this technique, contaminated mycelium is placed on antibiotic-supplemented agar and then covered with a wedge of the same agar, creating a sandwich. The mycelium can drill through the agar, but bacteria cannot accompany it through the antibiotic-laced medium. After 1-2 transfers through this medium, cultures are switched back to antibiotic-free medium for normal maintenance.

## Relevance to Mycology

Benlate/benomyl targets fungal microtubule formation by binding to beta-tubulin, disrupting mitosis. In laboratory mycology, selective media containing benomyl or similar benzimidazole compounds are used to isolate specific fungal groups by suppressing others. The development of benomyl resistance in field populations of plant pathogenic fungi has been an important model for studying the evolution of fungicide resistance.

## Safety Note

Benomyl has been associated with reproductive toxicity and was voluntarily withdrawn from the US market by DuPont in 2001. Appropriate personal protective equipment should be used when handling any fungicide in the laboratory. Modern alternatives with similar activity profiles may be preferred.
