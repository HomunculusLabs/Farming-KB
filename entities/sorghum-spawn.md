# Sorghum Spawn (Grain Spawn)

## Description

Sorghum spawn is mushroom mycelium grown on sorghum ([[mushroom-substrate-bags]]. Sorghum grain is widely used in tropical and subtropical regions as a spawn carrier because of its availability, nutritional suitability, and physical properties. The sorghum-based grain spawn represents the critical intermediate stage between laboratory tissue culture on PDA and the final fruiting substrate [[mushroom-cultivation-disabilities-nine-week-training-program]], the spawn multiplication step on sorghum represents a skill that can be mastered by trainees with various disabilities. The manual emphasizes that understanding the full spawn production cycle — from tissue culture through sorghum spawn to substrate inoculation — gives mushroom farmers greater self-sufficiency and control over their operation quality.

Self-produced spawn significantly reduces costs compared to purchasing commercial spawn, improving the profitability of small-scale mushroom enterprises. The ability to produce quality spawn is a key differentiator between subsistence and profitable mushroom farming operations.

## Quality Control

Good sorghum spawn should colonize evenly, break apart without becoming wet paste, and recover quickly after shaking.
Any sour smell, green mold, black spots, bacterial slime, or uncolonized wet grain is a warning sign.
Because spawn multiplies into many substrate bags, a small contamination problem at this stage can become a large crop failure.
Careful labeling by strain, date, and batch helps growers compare performance and avoid using old or suspect spawn.
For training programs, these checks are practical skills as important as the laboratory steps themselves.
