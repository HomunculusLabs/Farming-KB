# Anadenanthera colubrina (Vell.) Brenan

**Common names:** Cebil, Vilca, Huilco, Angico

**Family:** Fabaceae (Leguminosae)

## Overview

Anadenanthera colubrina is a mimosa-like tree native to [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-south-america]] that has
played a central role in the shamanic and ceremonial practices of indigenous
peoples across the southern continent for millennia. Its seeds, rich in
[[hplc-analysis-of-tryptamine-alkaloids]], are ground into a potent snuff known as Cebil or Vilca,
making it one of the most significant hallucinogenic plants of the New World.

## Taxonomy

Anadenanthera colubrina was originally described under the genus Mimosa and
later transferred to Piptadenia before being placed in its current genus.
The species authority is (Vell.) Brenan. A notable botanical synonym includes
the variety A. colubrina var. cebil, which reflects the common name used by
many indigenous groups of the region.

The genus Anadenanthera belongs to the subfamily Mimosoideae within the large
and economically important family Fabaceae, also known as the legume or pea
