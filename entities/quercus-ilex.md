## Quercus ilex

## Overview

**Quercus ilex** L., commonly known as holm oak, holly oak, or evergreen oak, is a large evergreen tree native to the Mediterranean Basin. It is one of the dominant species of Mediterranean sclerophyllous forests and a keystone species of the dehesa agroforestry system in the Iberian Peninsula.

The species is economically significant for acorn production (pig fattening), cork-quality bark, and hardwood timber. It is also widely planted as an ornamental and street tree across temperate regions worldwide.

- **Order:** Fagales
- **Family:** Fagaceae
- **Genus:** Quercus, subgenus Quercus (white oaks, section Quercus)
- **Species:** Q. ilex L., authority: Carl Linnaeus, 1753

Two subspecies are recognized: subsp. *ilex* (eastern Mediterranean, narrower leaves) and subsp. *rotundifolia* (western Mediterranean, broader rounded leaves; formerly described as Q. rotundifolia Lam.).

## Morphology

Holm oak is a medium to large evergreen tree reaching 20–25 m in height, occasionally to 28 m. The trunk is stout, reaching 1.5 m in diameter, with dark grey to blackish, deeply fissured bark.

The crown is dense, broadly domed, and symmetrical. In open conditions it develops a massive spreading canopy; in dense forests the crown is more columnar.

**Leaves** are evergreen, coriaceous, alternate, 3–7 cm long and 1.5–4 cm wide. The leaf margin is typically spiny-toothed in younger shoots (resembling holly, hence the common name) and entire or shallowly toothed on mature branches.

## Cultivation and Uses

### Timber

Holm oak wood is extremely dense, hard, and durable (air-dry density ~0.95–1.10 g/cm³ — it sinks in water). Heartwood is brown to dark brown with a fine, interlocked grain.

The wood is prized for fuelwood and [[quercus-suber]] is the primary cork oak), and firewood.
