# Carex lasiocarpa

## Overview

Carex lasiocarpa is a rhizomatous perennial sedge in the family Cyperaceae, commonly known as woolly sedge or slender sedge.

It is named for its densely woolly perigynia, which give the inflorescence a distinctive fuzzy appearance.

The plant forms extensive colonies in boreal and temperate wetlands across northern [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]] and Eurasia.

It is a dominant component of fen and marsh communities, providing habitat structure and forage for wildlife.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

- Kingdom: Plantae
- Order: Poales
- Family: Cyperaceae
- Genus: Carex
- Species: Carex lasiocarpa Ehrh.

The species was described by Jakob Friedrich Ehrhart in 1789.

Stems are slender and triangular in cross-section, reaching 30–100 cm in height.

Leaves are narrow (1–3 mm wide) and shorter than the stems, with a characteristic M-shaped cross-section.

The inflorescence consists of a terminal spike with 1–3 lateral spikes on short peduncles.

Perigynia are 3–5 mm long, lanceolate, and densely covered with long white hairs.

Achenes are lens-shaped, approximately 1.5–2 mm long, enclosed within the persistent perigynium.

## Distribution and Habitat

Carex lasiocarpa has a circumboreal distribution across northern North America and Eurasia.

In North America, its range extends from Alaska through Canada to the northern [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]], including New England and the Great Lakes states.

Isolated populations occur in southerly mountainous regions including the Rockies and Appalachians.

Eurasian populations span Scandinavia, northern Russia, Siberia, and extend into northern China and Mongolia.

It is an obligate wetland plant found in fens, marshes, bogs, and shallow water margins.

It prefers calcareous or neutral substrates with standing water 0–30 cm deep.

It commonly grows alongside [[carex-stricta]] and [[carex-aquatilis]] in mixed sedge meadow communities.

## Ecology and Ecological Role

Carex lasiocarpa is a dominant or codominant species in many northern fen communities, particularly rich fens with circumneutral pH.

It spreads primarily through rhizomatous clonal growth, forming dense mats that stabilize substrate and reduce erosion.

These mats create microhabitat for invertebrates, amphibians, and small mammals.

The species is important forage for waterfowl during spring migration when new shoots are most palatable.

Muskrats and other semi-aquatic herbivores regularly consume both rhizomes and aerial shoots.

It provides nesting cover for marsh birds including Virginia rail, sora, and American bittern.

The woolly perigynia may aid hydrochory, floating on water surfaces to disperse seeds across connected wetlands.

## Cultivation and Uses

Carex lasiocarpa is occasionally cultivated for wetland restoration and native landscaping projects.

It is valued in [[comparison-constructed-wetlands-vs-bioswales]] for dense root mats that filter nutrients and trap sediments.

Propagation is typically from rhizome divisions or seed, with seed requiring 30–60 days cold stratification.
