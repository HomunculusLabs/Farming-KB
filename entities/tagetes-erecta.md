# Tagetes erecta

## Overview

*Tagetes erecta*, commonly known as African marigold or Aztec marigold, is an
annual herbaceous plant in the family Asteraceae. Native to Mexico and Central
America, it is cultivated worldwide as an ornamental, medicinal, and
agricultural companion plant.

The species is one of the most economically important marigolds, valued for
its large, vibrant flower heads rich in lutein and other carotenoid pigments
used in food coloring and poultry feed supplements.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Division:** Magnoliophyta
- **Class:** Magnoliopsida
- **Order:** Asterales
- **Family:** Asteraceae
- **Genus:** *Tagetes*
- **Species:** *T. erecta* L.

The genus *Tagetes* comprises approximately 50 species distributed across
the Americas. *T. erecta* is closely related to *T. patula* (French marigold)
and *T. minuta*, with which it shares many bioactive properties.

## Morphology

Plants reach 30 to 100 cm in height with erect, branching stems. Leaves are
pinnately compound, 5 to 15 cm long, with serrated leaflets arranged in
opposite pairs along the rachis.

Flower heads are large, 5 to 12 cm in diameter, composed of both ray and
disc florets. Ray florets are typically orange, yellow, or gold, though
cultivated varieties range from pale lemon to deep mahogany.

The root system is fibrous and moderately deep, reaching 30 to 50 cm.
Secretory cavities in roots and foliage produce thiophene compounds with
nematicidal properties.

## Distribution and Habitat

Native to the highlands of central and southern Mexico, *T. erecta* has been
naturalized across tropical and subtropical regions worldwide. It thrives in
full sun and well-drained soils, tolerating a range of pH conditions.

The species is frost-sensitive and grows as an annual in temperate climates.
In tropical lowlands it can persist year-round with continuous flowering.

## Bioactive Compounds

The essential oil contains **tagetone**, **ocimene**, **limonene**, and
**dihydrotagetone** as major constituents. Thiophene derivatives including
alpha-terthienyl are concentrated in roots and exhibit strong nematicidal
and insecticidal activity.

Flower pigments are dominated by **lutein** (up to 85% of total
carotenoids), with **zeaxanthin**, **antheraxanthin**, and **beta-cryptoxanthin**
also present. These xanthophylls are commercially extracted for use as natural
food colorants (E161b) and nutritional supplements.

Flavonoids identified include **quercetagetin**, **patulitrin**, and
**quercimeritrin**, which contribute antioxidant and anti-inflammatory
activities demonstrated in vitro.

## Cultivation and Permaculture Applications

In permaculture and organic farming systems, *T. erecta* is widely used as
a companion plant. Root exudates repel root-knot nematodes (*Meloidogyne*
spp.), making it effective as an intercrop with tomatoes, peppers, and
eggplant.

The species also attracts beneficial insects including hoverflies, parasitic
wasps, and lady beetles while deterring whitefly and aphid populations through
volatile compound emissions. Dense planting as a border crop creates a
protective insectary buffer.

Seeds are direct-sown after frost risk passes. Germination occurs within
5 to 10 days at 20 to 25°C. Flowering begins 45 to 60 days from sowing and
continues until frost. Deadheading extends the bloom period significantly.

## Ethnobotanical and Medicinal Use

In Mexican traditional medicine, *T. erecta* flowers are prepared as
infusions for digestive complaints, respiratory infections, and menstrual
discomfort. The Aztecs used the plant in ritual ceremonies and as a treatment
for fever and cold symptoms.

Preliminary pharmacological studies support anti-inflammatory, antioxidant,
and hepatoprotective activities, though clinical validation remains limited.
The flowers are used in some commercial herbal tea blends and topical skin
care formulations.
