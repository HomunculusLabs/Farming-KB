# Amoghasiddhi

## Description
Amoghasiddhi is the Dhyani Buddha of the Northern Realm, known as the "Almighty Conqueror" and associated with the element of air — the principle of movement, extension, life, and breath (prana). In *The Psychedelic Experience*, Lama Govinda describes Amoghasiddhi as representing "the mysterious activity of spiritual forces, which work removed from the senses, invisible and imperceptible, with the aim of guiding the individual towards the maturity of knowledge and liberation."

## Classification
- **Category:** Deity — Dhyani Buddha
- **Domain:** Tibetan Buddhism, Vajrayana Buddhism, Bardo Thodol
- **Tradition:** Mahayana / Vajrayana Buddhism

## Key Facts
- One of the Five Dhyani Buddhas, associated with the North direction
- Realm: "Successful Performance of Best Actions" (Karma-vistara)
- Name means "Almighty Conqueror" or "Unfailing Success"
- Associated element: Air — principle of movement, extension, life, and breath
- Color association: Green ("the serene mystic green of Amoghasiddhi")
- Associated with the "yellow light of an inner sun invisible to human eyes"
- Accompanied by a Divine Mother and two Bodhisattvas representing "equilibrium, immutability, and almighty power" and "clearer of obscurations"
- Appears on the fifth day of the Chonyid Bardo in the Bardo Thodol

## Role in the Psychedelic Bardo Framework
Amoghasiddhi relates to **Vision 2: The Internal Flow of Archetypal Processes** — the intellectual and conceptual aspect of the Second Bardo visions. He represents the invisible spiritual forces that guide consciousness toward liberation. The "serene mystic green" and connection to air/breath makes him relevant to the breath-focused techniques used in psychedelic guidance.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology
The element of air (prana/breath) is directly relevant to psychedelic session guidance. Breath awareness is one of the primary techniques for managing difficult experiences during psilocybin sessions. The Amoghasiddhi archetype — invisible forces working toward liberation — parallels the set-and-setting principle that invisible contextual factors profoundly shape the psychedelic experience.
