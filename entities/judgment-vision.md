## Judgment Vision

## Description

The Judgment Vision is a characteristic Third Bardo experience described in *The Psychedelic Experience* and the original Bardo Thodol. During this vision, the voyager encounters a judgment scene where their life deeds are weighed and evaluated. In the Tibetan tradition, a "good genius" counts good deeds with white pebbles and an "evil genius" counts evil deeds with black pebbles. Leary interprets this psychologically as the emergence of conscience in its stern aspect, with the "Mirror of Karma" representing memory itself.

## Classification

- **Tradition**: Tibetan Buddhism / Cross-cultural Religious Phenomenon
- **Category**: Third Bardo Vision / Archetypal Experience
- **Bardo Phase**: Third Bardo (Sidpa Bardo, Period of Re-Entry)
- **Psychological Correlate**: Guilt, conscience, self-evaluation

## Key Facts

### Tibetan Buddhist Description

- The "Lord of Death" presides as judge — symbolizing conscience in its aspect of impartiality and love of righteousness
- The "Mirror of Karma" (analogous to the Christian Judgment Book) reflects all past actions clearly — memory cannot be deceived
- Different parts of the ego come forward: some offer excuses, others ascribe baser motives to past deeds, still others offer justifications or requests for pardon
- Lying and subterfuge are futile before the Mirror of Karma
- The "Executive Furies of the Robot Lord of Death" may appear as tormenting, torturing, or mocking figures

### Western Manifestation

- Westerners are most likely to experience the judgment vision in Christian form
- The vision can assume culturally specific forms depending on the voyager's background
- Tormenting figures may be internal hallucinations or may involve the people around the voyager, seen as pitiless, mocking, and superior
- The guilt-producing machinery is recognized as a self-created hallucination: "Your own guilt machine"

### Psychological Interpretation

- The Judge symbolizes conscience itself — the internalized critical voice
- The Mirror of Karma is memory — the inability to escape one's own self-knowledge
- The vision represents the ego's attempt to reconstitute itself through guilt and self-judgment
- The personality is described as "a collection of thought-patterns and void" that "cannot be harmed or injured — swords cannot pierce it, fire cannot burn it"
- The vision is triggered by the ego's resistance to dissolution during the re-entry phase

### Instructions for Navigation

- "Be not frightened, tell no lies, face truth fearlessly"
- Recognize the judgment scene as a Third Bardo manifestation — a self-created hallucination
- "In reality there is no such thing as the Lord of Death, or a justice-dispensing god or demon or spirit"
- Meditate on the void nature of the frightening imagery
- The "Voidness" is not nothingness but a state where consciousness shines more clearly and lucidly
- This connects to the Trikaya doctrine: Dharma-Kaya (Void), Sambhoga-Kaya (Brightness), Adi-Kaya (primordial consciousness), Nirmana-Kaya (radiant power)

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- Judgment visions are commonly reported during high-dose psilocybin sessions, particularly among individuals with significant guilt or shame
- Modern psychedelic-assisted therapy for addiction and depression frequently encounters this archetype — patients confront their "black pebbles" in a therapeutic context
- The instruction to "face truth fearlessly" parallels the therapeutic approach of encouraging patients to sit with difficult material rather than avoid it
- The insight that "swords cannot pierce it, fire cannot burn it" — that the core self is invulnerable to judgment — is a powerful therapeutic reframe used in modern psychedelic integration
- The observation that the judgment vision occurs during re-entry (Third Bardo) rather than peak experience suggests it emerges as the ego reconstitutes — consistent with the clinical observation that difficult material often surfaces during the comedown phase
