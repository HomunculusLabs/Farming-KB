# Arugula

## Description

Arugula (*Eruca vesicaria* subsp. *sativa*, synonym *Eruca sativa*), also known as rocket or roquette, is a peppery, cold-hardy leafy green in the Brassicaceae family. In [[eliot-coleman]]'s [[winter-harvest-system]], arugula is listed as a cool-house crop — one of the vegetables that benefits from minimal supplementary heat during winter — and also appears as an early spring crop from both cold and cool houses. Its versatility across seasons makes it a valuable crop for four-season market gardens.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Family**: Brassicaceae (mustard / [[cabbage-family-pests]])
- **Genus**: *Eruca*
- **Species**: *Eruca vesicaria* subsp. *sativa*
- **Common names**: Arugula, rocket, roquette, rucola
- **Category**: Cool-season leafy green

## Key Facts

### Botanical Characteristics

Arugula is an annual or biennial plant with deeply lobed, dark green leaves that grow in a low rosette. The leaves have a distinctive peppery, slightly bitter, nutty flavor that intensifies with heat and age. Yellowish-white flowers with purple veins are produced on tall stalks and are also edible.

### Cold Hardiness

Arugula is notably cold-hardy and continues to produce harvestable leaves well into the winter months under protection. In Coleman's system, it is classified as a cool-house crop rather than a cold-house crop, meaning it benefits from some minimal heat input during the coldest months to maintain active growth.

### Culinary Profile

- **Flavor**: Peppery, nutty, slightly bitter; milder when young, more pungent when mature
- **Use**: Salad green (raw), pizza topping, pasta addition, pesto base, cooked green
- **Pairing**: Classic combination with Parmesan cheese, prosciutto, lemon, and olive oil
- **Baby leaf**: Often included in mesclun and salad mixes for its distinctive flavor contribution

### Growing Characteristics

- **Germination**: 5-7 days at 50-65F soil temperature
- **Days to maturity**: 30-50 days for baby leaves, 50-60 days for full size
- **Regrowth**: Excellent [[cut-and-come-again]] potential; regrows after harvest
- **Bolting**: Tendency to bolt in warm weather and long days; best in cool seasons

## Relevance to Cultivation

### Winter and Spring Production

In Coleman's four-season system, arugula serves dual roles:
- **Cool house (winter)**: Listed among winter-harvested crops including radishes, turnips, turnip greens, Swiss chard, watercress, and parsley
- **Cold and cool houses (spring)**: Listed as an early spring crop alongside carrots, baby beets, baby new potatoes, broccoli, fennel, kale, lettuce, and turnips

### Salad Mix Component

Arugula's distinctive flavor makes it a valuable component of baby-leaf salad mixes. Coleman's salad mix was described by customers as a healthy fast food because it looks so inviting and is easy to prepare. The peppery kick of arugula provides contrast to milder greens in the mix.

### Market Appeal

Arugula is one of the crops that has grown significantly in popularity in [[growing-gourmet-north-american-reishi-species]] markets. Its inclusion in the [[cold-house-winter-harvest-system]] reflects both its agronomic suitability for protected winter cultivation and its strong market demand as a premium salad ingredient.

### Rotation Considerations

As a member of the Brassicaceae (cabbage) family, arugula must be rotated with crops from other families to prevent buildup of Brassica-specific [[mushroom-pests-and-diseases]]. In Coleman's cool-house rotation, brassica crops are alternated with lettuce family (Compositae) crops and warm-weather crops (cucumbers, peppers, eggplants) to maintain a complex rotation.
