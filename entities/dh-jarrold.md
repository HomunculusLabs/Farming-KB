## D.H. Jarrold

## Description

D.H. Jarrold is the author of "Indoor Mushroom Growing Technique for the Boil-A-Bag," a practical guide for amateur mushroom cultivation using inexpensive household materials. Jarrold developed the Boil-A-Bag technique as an accessible method for home cultivators, emphasizing the use of readily available materials like plastic boil bags, marmite, straw, and vermiculite. His guide provides detailed step-by-step instructions with accompanying illustrations for every stage of the cultivation process.

## Key Facts

- Author of the Boil-A-Bag indoor mushroom growing technique
- Developed a method using standard kitchen boil bags as cultivation containers
- Emphasized accessibility and low cost throughout his guide
- Used marmite (yeast extract) to accelerate spore germination
- Recommended Lakeland Plastics boil bags (8x12 inch, 20x30cm) costing approximately 2.50 GBP per pack of 50
- Provided detailed instructions for every stage from substrate preparation to spore print collection
- Designed a filter can system using recycled food cans for gas exchange

## The Boil-A-Bag Innovation

Jarrold's key innovation was recognizing that commercially available "boil-a-bag" roasting bags could serve as inexpensive, disposable cultivation containers. This eliminated the need for expensive canning jars or specialized autoclave bags, making mushroom cultivation accessible to anyone with a pressure cooker. The bags could be sterilized at 15 psi for 60 minutes and then inoculated through the plastic using a syringe needle.

## Cultivation Philosophy

Jarrold's approach prioritized practicality and cost-effectiveness over technical sophistication. His guide used household materials wherever possible: food cans as filters, masking tape for sealing, fish tanks as humidity chambers, and marmite as a nutrient supplement. This philosophy of making mushroom cultivation accessible to ordinary people echoes the broader democratization of mycological knowledge that has characterized the hobby cultivation movement.

## Detailed Method Documentation

Jarrold's guide is notable for its exceptional level of detail, with step-by-step photographs illustrating every stage of the process. From chopping straw to taking spore prints, each step is documented with both text and images. This makes the guide particularly accessible to complete beginners who may have no prior experience with mushroom cultivation.

## Spore Print Technique

Jarrold described a complete lifecycle approach to cultivation that includes taking spore prints from mature mushrooms to produce the next generation of cultures. His spore print technique involved sterilizing glass jars in an oven, using flame-sterilized tweezers and scissors to harvest caps, and allowing spores to settle and dry over 48 hours. This self-sustaining cycle eliminated the need to repeatedly purchase spores or cultures.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Jarrold's Boil-A-Bag technique represents a significant contribution to the amateur mycology movement, providing an accessible entry point for home cultivators. While the technique is simpler than professional methods, it incorporates sound mycological principles including substrate sterilization, aseptic inoculation technique, proper gas exchange, humidity management, and lifecycle sustainability through spore printing.
