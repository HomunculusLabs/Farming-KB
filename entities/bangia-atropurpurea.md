Bangia atropurpurea is a filamentous red alga in the family Bangiaceae. It is one of the simplest morphological forms among the Bangiales, consisting of uniseriate filaments attached to substrates by a basal holdfast. The species is intertidal and brackish, commonly found on rocks, shells, and wooden structures in temperate coastal waters.

Despite its diminutive appearance, B. atropurpurea is phylogenetically significant as a basal member of the Bangiophyceae. This group includes the economically important nori genus *Porphyra* sensu lato (now *Pyropia* and related genera).

The alga is easily recognized by its dark purple to nearly black coloration in the field. Filaments typically reach 1–6 cm in length but can extend to 12 cm under favorable conditions.

The species is euryhaline, tolerating salinities from approximately 5 to 35 PSU. This adaptability allows it to colonize estuaries, river mouths, and brackish lagoons in addition to fully marine habitats.

Typical substrates include hard surfaces such as rock, mollusk shells, concrete pilings, and wooden dock structures. It occupies the mid-to-upper intertidal zone, often forming dense tufts or mats.

## Reproduction and Life Cycle

The life cycle of B. atropurpurea involves an alternation between a macroscopic filamentous gametophyte and a microscopic filamentous conchocelis phase. This biphasic cycle is shared with other Bangiales.

Gametophytes are dioecious, with separate male and female filaments. Spermatangia release non-motile spermatia, and carpogonia receive these for fertilization. The resulting zygotes develop into carposporangia, which release diploid carpospores.

Carpospores settle on calcareous substrates, often empty mollusk shells, and germinate into the microscopic conchocelis phase. This phase bores into the calcium carbonate substrate and can persist through unfavorable conditions for months.

Asexual reproduction occurs via monospores released directly from vegetative cells. These settle and germinate into new filamentous gametophytes, enabling rapid colonization of suitable substrates without sexual recombination.

## Ecology and Interactions

Bangia atropurpurea is an early colonizer of bare intertidal surfaces. Its tolerance of desiccation and temperature fluctuation gives it a competitive advantage in the high intertidal zone.

The species provides substrate and shelter for small invertebrates including harpacticoid copepods, nematodes, and larval gastropods. Dense mats of Bangia can create microhabitats in otherwise barren intertidal areas.

## Culinary and Nutritional Use

Bangia atropurpurea is consumed as a minor edible seaweed in parts of Japan, Korea, and coastal China. It is typically gathered wild from intertidal rocks, rinsed, and consumed fresh in salads or lightly blanched.
