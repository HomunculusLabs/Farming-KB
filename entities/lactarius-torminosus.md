---# Lactarius torminosus

**Lactarius torminosus**, commonly known as the woolly milkcap, is a basidiomycete
fungus in the family Russulaceae. It is mycorrhizal with birch (*Betula* spp.) and
found across temperate and boreal regions of Europe, Asia, and [[ethnomycology-cultural-significance-sacred-fungi]] in Scandinavia and Eastern
Europe where it is parboiled and prepared as a traditional food.

## Taxonomy

Basidiomycota → Agaricomycetes → Russulales → **Russulaceae → *Lactarius → *L. torminosus***

Described by Jacob Christian Schäffer in 1774. The epithet *torminosus* derives from
the Latin *tormina* (colic), directly referencing its gastrointestinal effects when
eaten raw. Multiple infraspecific taxa have been proposed; current consensus
recognizes it as a single variable species, though phylogenetic studies suggest
a complex of closely related taxa across its range.

## Description

- **Cap:** 4–12 cm, convex becoming depressed at center with age; margin incurved
  and conspicuously shaggy-hairy. Surface pale orange to salmon-pink with
  concentric zonations. The distinctive woolly margin is a key field character.
- **Gills:** Adnate to slightly decurrent, crowded, narrow; cream to pale buff,
  often with a pinkish tinge. Cross-veined (anastomosing) between gills.
- **Stem:** 4–10 cm × 1–2 cm, cylindrical, hollow; pale flesh-colored to pinkish,
  smooth or slightly scurfy near base. No ring.
- **Flesh:** Whitish to buff, firm, brittle (typical Russulaceae texture). No
  significant odor when fresh; slowly acrid taste.
- **Latex (milk):** White, unchanging, extremely acrid and peppery. This acridity
  persists even after prolonged drying and is the primary reason for toxicity.
- **Spores:** White print; ellipsoid, 7–9 × 6–7 µm, ornamented with distinct
  warts and ridges forming a partial reticulum.

## Toxicity

Raw or inadequately cooked *L. torminosus* causes severe gastrointestinal poisoning
within 30 minutes to 3 hours of ingestion. Symptoms include nausea, vomiting,
abdominal cramps, and profuse diarrhea. The acrid latex (primarily the sesquiterpene
lactone strobilol and related velutinal esters) is responsible for the irritant
effect. Cooking destroys much of the toxicity, and in Finnish, Karelian, and
Russian tradition the mushrooms are parboiled (discarding the water), then
