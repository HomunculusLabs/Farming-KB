# Microberlinia bisulcata

## Description

Microberlinia bisulcata is a tropical tree species in the family Fabaceae
(subfamily Detarioideae) native to the rainforests of Cameroon, Central Africa.
It is an ectomycorrhizal canopy tree that has been studied in the context of
density-dependent seedling survival under conspecific adult trees. Newbery et
al. (2000) documented its seedling survival rates as part of research on
Janzen-Connell dynamics in African tropical forests. M. bisulcata is also a
commercially important timber species (zebrano).

## Classification

- **Domain:** Eukaryota
- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Clade:** Angiosperms, Eudicots, Rosids
- **Order:** Fabales
- **Family:** Fabaceae
- **Subfamily:** Detarioideae
- **Genus:** Microberlinia
- **Species:** M. bisulcata

## Key Facts

- **Density-dependent survival:** Newbery et al. (2000) found that seedling
  survival of M. bisulcata after 16 months was 25% at low adult stem density
  and 33% at high adult stem density, a modest improvement.
- **Ectomycorrhizal association:** Forms ectomycorrhizal associations that
  connect to shared fungal networks with other tropical legume trees in
  Cameroon rainforests.
- **Lowest seedling survival:** Among the tropical ectomycorrhizal trees
  studied by Newbery et al. (2000), M. bisulcata showed the lowest seedling
  survival rates at both low and high adult densities (25% and 33%
  respectively), compared to Tetraberlinia moreliana (70% and 76%).
- **Timber species:** M. bisulcata produces zebrano wood, a commercially
  valuable timber known for its distinctive striped figure, used in furniture,
  veneers, and woodturning.
- **Conservation concern:** Over-exploitation for timber has led to population
  declines, making conservation of remaining stands important for maintaining
  tropical forest biodiversity and ectomycorrhizal network integrity.
- **Forest dynamics role:** As a canopy tree, M. bisulcata influences light
  regimes, litter quality, and mycorrhizal network structure in the forest
  understory, affecting regeneration of multiple tree species.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Tropical ectomycorrhizal ecology:** M. bisulcata provides data points for
  understanding how ectomycorrhizal networks influence seedling survival
  differently across tropical tree species.
- **Species-specific responses:** The modest increase in seedling survival with
  adult density (compared to T. bifoliata which showed a doubling) demonstrates
  that mycorrhizal benefits are species-specific and depend on the biology of
  individual tree species.
- **Forest conservation:** Understanding the mycorrhizal ecology of M.
  bisulcata informs conservation strategies for this commercially exploited
  species and its forest habitat.
- **Timber sustainability:** Sustainable harvesting requires maintaining
  ectomycorrhizal network integrity to support natural regeneration of harvested
  species.

## Ecological Interactions

- Forms ectomycorrhizal associations with shared fungal networks in Cameroon
  rainforests.
- Seedling survival modestly enhanced at higher conspecific adult density.
- Shares habitat with Tetraberlinia, Paraberlinia, Afzelia, and Brachystegia
  in mixed ectomycorrhizal tropical forest.
- Produces leaf litter that contributes to forest floor nutrient cycling and
  supports saprotrophic fungal communities.
- Canopy structure influences understory light and microclimate conditions.
