# Phyllostachys bambusoides

## Description

Phyllostachys bambusoides, commonly known as **madake** or **Japanese timber bamboo**, is a large, running bamboo species native to China and widely cultivated in Japan. It is one of the most important bamboo species for construction and craft, producing strong, thick-walled culms that can reach 20 m in height. The species is renowned for its use in traditional Japanese architecture, crafts, and [[community-garden-design]].

## Classification

- **Family:** Poaceae (Gramineae)
- **Subfamily:** Bambusoideae
- **Genus:** Phyllostachys
- **Species:** P. bambusoides
- **Common names:** Madake, Japanese timber bamboo, giant timber bamboo

## Key Facts

- Large running bamboo, typically 10–20 m tall with culms 5–12 cm in diameter
- Thick-walled culms provide exceptional strength for a bamboo
- Native to China, extensively cultivated in Japan for centuries
- One of the strongest and most durable bamboo species for construction
- Culms emerge in spring, reaching full height in approximately 2 months
- Can spread aggressively through underground rhizomes (running bamboo)
- Young shoots are edible and prized in Japanese cuisine

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

- **Construction:** Primary bamboo for structural use — scaffolding, buildings, bridges, and furniture (pages 97, 136)
- **Craft and artisanry:** Used for baskets, fencing, musical instruments, and traditional crafts (page 97)
- **Edible shoots:** Young shoots are a premium vegetable in Asian cuisine
- **Windbreaks:** Dense, tall growth creates effective windbreaks (page 97)
- **[[comparison-vetiver-grass-vs-bamboo-for-erosion-control]]:** Extensive rhizome network stabilises soils on slopes
- **Garden stakes and poles:** Strong culms ideal for garden staking and trellising (page 97)
- **[[gadd-fungal-carbon-sequestration]]:** Rapid growth captures significant carbon biomass

## Cultivation Notes

- Running bamboo — requires rhizome barriers to contain spread
- Prefers moist, well-drained, fertile soils
- Thrives in full sun to partial shade
- Suited to temperate [[query-how-to-grow-rare-tropical-fruits-in-marginal-and-subtropical-climates]]
- Hardy to approximately -15°C
- Responds well to regular watering and fertilisation
- Culms are harvested at 3–5 years for optimal strength
- Best propagated by division of rhizome sections
