# Scutellospora persica

## Description

Scutellospora persica is a species of arbuscular mycorrhizal fungus (AMF) in the genus Scutellospora, family Gigasporaceae, order Glomales. The species is notable in the history of AMF taxonomy because the originally described species Gigaspora tuberculata was found to be synonymous with S. persica after detailed morphological comparison revealed that the type specimens of G. tuberculata were parasitized spores with abnormal ornamentation.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Eukarya
- **Phylum:** Zygomycota (Glomeromycota in modern taxonomy)
- **Order:** Glomales
- **Suborder:** Gigasporineae
- **Family:** Gigasporaceae
- **Genus:** Scutellospora
- **Species:** Scutellospora persica

## Key Facts

- **Taxonomic synonymy:** Gigaspora tuberculata (described by Neer et al. 1993) was synonymized with S. persica by Bentivenga and Morton (1995) after the original description was found to be based on parasitized spores with atypical surface ornamentation.
- Belongs to Scutellospora, a genus with 28 described species characterized by bilayered spore walls and the production of auxiliary cells (rather than vesicles) in the [[mycorrhiza]].
- Members of the suborder Gigasporineae (Scutellospora and Gigaspora) do not produce vesicles; instead, they form auxiliary cells that are transitory lipid-storage structures.
- Spores are typically large (often >200 µm) with complex wall ornamentation.

## Spore Morphology

- Scutellospora persica produces spores with characteristic ornamentation patterns on the outer laminate wall layer.
- The synonymy with G. tuberculata illustrates a common problem in AMF taxonomy: parasitized or degraded spores can exhibit morphological features (such as abnormal surface warts or tubercles) that lead to misidentification and description of spurious species.
- Healthy spores from trap cultures are essential for accurate species determination.

## Ecological Distribution

- Found in sand dune habitats and other coastal environments.
- Like other Scutellospora species, tends to be found in mesic to wet habitats rather than arid environments.
- Spores can be locally abundant when present, but the species may have a patchy, aggregated distribution.

## Taxonomic Lessons from the G. tuberculata Synonymy

The synonymization of Gigaspora tuberculata with Scutellospora persica carries important lessons for AMF taxonomy:

1. **Parasitism effects:** [[fungal-parasites]] can alter spore morphology dramatically, creating false diagnostic characters.
2. **Healthy material required:** Species descriptions should be based on healthy spores from trap cultures whenever possible.
3. **Comparative analysis needed:** Detailed comparison of spore wall development, not just surface features, is necessary for reliable species discrimination.
4. **Revision frequency:** As more material becomes available from trap cultures, previously described species may prove to be synonyms.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Taxonomic rigor:** The S. persica / G. tuberculata case underscores the importance of using healthy, cultured specimens for AMF species descriptions.
- **Biodiversity surveys:** Accurate species lists depend on correct identification, which requires awareness of parasitism-induced morphological variation.
- **Culture collections:** Institutions like INVAM maintain authenticated specimens that serve as references for resolving taxonomic disputes.
- **Species boundaries:** The case illustrates the challenge of defining species boundaries in a group with simple morphological structures and high phenotypic plasticity.

## Identification

- Spores are large, typically 200–350 µm in diameter.
- Outer wall surface with characteristic ornamentation visible under differential interference contrast (DIC) optics.
- Auxiliary cells produced on external hyphae, not within roots.
- Best identified from healthy spores produced in pot cultures.
