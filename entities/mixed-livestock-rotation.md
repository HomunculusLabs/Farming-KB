# Mixed Livestock Rotation

## Description

Mixed livestock rotation is a permaculture grazing strategy that sequences multiple animal species (typically horse/cow/sheep/goose/pig or similar combinations) over mixed forb/grass/legume/forage plantings. Each animal species prepares the sward or browse for its successor, creating a multi-species succession that attempts to reproduce the high yields and ecological balance of natural wildlife systems on managed farmland. Mollison notes this approach is "not much used as yet for more than 2-3 species" but has significant unrealized potential.

## Classification

- **Category**: Agricultural technique — multi-species rotational grazing
- **System type**: Sequential animal species rotation over mixed plantings
- **Species count**: 4-8 animal species recommended
- **Key proponent**: J. Savage (Victoria, Australia) — cited as successful practitioner
- **Integration**: Combines livestock management with permanent forage plantings

## Key Facts

- Uses horse/cow/sheep/goose/pig succession (or similar combinations)
- Each animal species prepares the sward or browse for its successor
- Comfrey identified as a key element, along with lucerne and grasses
- The "pig tractor" system is integral — pigs plough ground for new sowings and root sets
- J. Savage in Victoria, Australia demonstrated successful implementation
- Attempts to reproduce the high yields of natural wildlife systems
- Recommended for trials in any area with appropriate species selection
- Requires optimum densities and intervals tailored to local conditions
- Not widely practiced beyond 2-3 species rotations as of the Manual's publication

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

Mixed livestock rotation is arguably the most holistic of Mollison's seven grassland management strategies because it most closely mimics natural ecosystem processes:

1. **Complementary grazing pressure**: Each species selects different forage components — horses graze tall coarse grass, cattle take medium-height forage, sheep prefer short fine grass and forbs, geese graze the shortest sward and consume slugs/snails, and pigs root for below-ground material. This sequential defoliation creates space and light for regrowth of all plant species in the sward.

2. **Soil preparation cycle**: Pigs (the "pig tractor") initiate the cycle by rooting and clearing ground. This is followed by sowing of comfrey, sunroot, lucerne, and other deep-rooted forages. Cattle and sheep then graze the established sward, with each species stimulating different regrowth patterns.

3. **Parasite management**: Multi-species rotation naturally breaks parasite cycles because most livestock parasites are host-specific. When cattle follow sheep, cattle do not ingest sheep parasites (and vice versa), reducing the need for anthelmintic treatments.

4. **Nutrient distribution**: Each species deposits manure with different nutrient profiles and distribution patterns. The cumulative effect is more even nutrient cycling than single-species grazing.

5. **Yield optimization**: Natural ecosystems achieve high total biomass through diverse herbivore guilds. The mixed rotation aims to capture this effect by ensuring no single forage component is underutilized or overexploited.

## Implementation Considerations

- **Species selection**: Choose 4-8 species with genuinely different grazing habits and forage preferences
- **Stocking density**: Calculate per-species density based on forage availability and paddock size
- **Rotation timing**: Base on observed regeneration of browse, not fixed calendar schedules
- **Fencing**: Requires permanent electric fencing for flexible paddock management (15-18 fields recommended for full system)
- **Forage base**: Establish permanent deep-rooted forages (comfrey, lucerne) that persist through multiple animal rotations
- **Integration with tree forage**: Hedgerow and browse strip plantings provide supplemental forage during deficit periods

## Relationship to Other Grazing Methods

Mixed livestock rotation represents the most complex end of Mollison's grazing spectrum, combining elements of:
- **Pig tractor** (biological ploughing)
- **Short-rotation stocking** (frequent moves)
- **Cut and feed** (manure return to land)
- **Strip grazing** (controlled access)

It requires the most management skill but offers the highest ecological and economic resilience.
