# Eucalyptus regnans

## Description

Eucalyptus regnans, commonly known as **mountain ash** or **Victorian ash**, is the tallest flowering plant in the world and one of the tallest tree species on Earth, capable of exceeding 100 metres in height. Native to cool, mountainous regions of southeastern Australia (Victoria and Tasmania), it is a fast-growing, straight-trunked tree that produces high-quality pale timber.

## Classification

- **Family:** Myrtaceae
- **Genus:** Eucalyptus
- **Species:** E. regnans
- **Common names:** Mountain ash, Victorian ash, swamp gum, stringy gum

## Key Facts

- The tallest flowering plant species, with recorded heights exceeding 100 m
- Native to the cool, moist mountain forests of Victoria and Tasmania
- Fast-growing — can reach 60 m in 50 years under optimal conditions
- Bark is smooth, greyish-white to light brown, shedding in long ribbons
- Timber is pale cream to light brown, straight-grained, and relatively easy to work
- Short-lived for a eucalypt (typically 200–400 years), killed by intense wildfire
- Prefers deep, fertile, well-drained soils in high-rainfall areas (over 1000 mm)
- Ecologically important as a keystone species in montane ash forests

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

- **Timber:** Major commercial species for sawlogs, pulpwood, and woodchips; pale timber prized for flooring, furniture, and joinery (pages 18, 20)
- **Firewood:** Moderate-quality firewood; fast growth compensates for lower density (page 20)
- **[[gadd-fungal-carbon-sequestration]]:** Exceptionally fast [[soil-temperature-plant-growth-rate-doubling-rule]] makes it one of the most effective species for carbon capture
- **[[mycofiltration-watershed-protection]]:** Critical for maintaining water quality and yield in forested catchments
- **[[hemenway-backyard-wildlife-habitat]]:** Provides essential nesting hollows for threatened species like Leadbeater's possum

## Cultivation Notes

- Requires cool, moist mountain conditions with high rainfall
- Prefers deep, fertile, well-drained loamy soils
- Extremely fast growth in suitable sites
- Intolerant of drought and extended dry periods
- Sensitive to repeated wildfire (obligate seeder — does not resprout from lignotuber)
- Plantations established for timber on 30–40 year rotations
- Frost-tolerant once established, but seedlings need protection
