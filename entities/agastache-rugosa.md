# Korean Mint (*Agastache rugosa* (Fisch. & C.A.Mey.) Kuntze)

## Overview

*Agastache rugosa*, commonly known as Korean mint, is a perennial aromatic
herb in the mint family (Lamiaceae). Native to East Asia, it has been used for
centuries in Traditional Chinese Medicine as Huo Xiang (藿香) and in Korean
herbal medicine.

The aerial parts contain essential oil (dominated by methylchavicol and
menthone) alongside flavonoids with documented antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory,
and gastrointestinal benefits. It is cultivated as both a culinary and
medicinal plant.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Division:** Angiospermae
- **Class:** Magnoliopsida
- **Order:** Lamiales
- **Family:** Lamiaceae
- **Genus:** Agastache
- **Species:** A. rugosa
- **Authority:** (Fisch. & C.A.Mey.) Kuntze (1891)
- **Common names:** Korean mint, wrinkled giant hyssop, purple giant
  hyssop, Huo Xiang (Chinese)
- **Etymology:** *Agastache* from Greek *agan* (much) + *stachys* (ear of
  grain); *rugosa* meaning wrinkled, describing the leaf texture

## Morphology

- **Growth habit:** Upright perennial, 60–120 cm tall, strongly aromatic
- **Stems:** Square (Lamiaceae), pubescent, green to purplish
- **Leaves:** Opposite, ovate to cordate, 4–9 cm, serrate, wrinkled upper
  surface, glandular-punctate beneath
- **Inflorescence:** Dense terminal spikes, 5–15 cm, whorled verticillasters
- **Flowers:** Bilabiate, tubular, violet-blue to purple, 8–12 mm;
  calyx 5-toothed; flowering July–September
- **Fruit:** Four nutlets, ~1.5 mm, dark brown
- **Root:** Fibrous with short rhizomes; hardy to USDA zones 5–9

## Distribution and Habitat

- Native to East Asia: China, Korea, Japan, eastern Russia
- Found in mountain meadows, forest margins, and stream banks at 100–2000 m
- Prefers well-drained loamy soils, full sun to partial shade
- Drought-tolerant once established; USDA zones 5–9

## Essential Oil Composition

Oil yield: 0.5–2.0% (v/w) of dried aerial parts. Composition varies by
chemotype and harvest time:

- **Methylchavicol (estragole):** 40–80% — sweet anise-like aroma
- **Menthone:** 5–20% — minty, cooling character
- **Pulegone:** Trace to 5% — ⚠️ potentially hepatotoxic at high doses
- **Limonene:** 1–5%; **β-Caryophyllene:** 1–3%; **Linalool:** trace to 2%

Three chemotypes: estragole-dominant (most common), menthone-dominant
(Korean populations), and mixed.

## Phytochemistry (Non-volatile)

### Flavonoids

- **Acacetin:** Major flavone; anti-inflammatory, antitumor (in vitro)
- **Tilianin:** Acacetin-7-glucoside; cardioprotective in animal studies
- **Luteolin and apigenin derivatives:** Antioxidant, anti-inflammatory

### Phenolic Acids and Other Compounds

- **Rosmarinic acid:** 1.5–3.0% dried weight; antioxidant, antiviral
- **Iridoids:** Agastachoside (characteristic of genus)
- **Triterpenoids:** Oleanolic acid, ursolic acid

## Pharmacology and Traditional Use

### Gastrointestinal (Primary TCM Indication)

- **TCM:** Resolves dampness, harmonizes middle jiao, stops vomiting; used
  for summer-heat, nausea, and poor appetite
- Antispasmodic and antiemetic activity validated in rodent models
- Carminative: relieves bloating and flatulence

### Antimicrobial Activity

- Essential oil active against *S. aureus*, *E. coli*, *B. subtilis* (MIC
  0.125–1.0 mg/mL)
- Rosmarinic acid demonstrates antiviral activity against HSV-1 (in vitro)

### Anti-inflammatory

- Inhibits COX-2, iNOS, and NF-κB; reduces TNF-α, IL-6, and PGE₂ in vitro
- Topical use for insect bites and skin inflammation

### Cardiovascular

- Tilianin reduces blood pressure in hypertensive rat models via NO/cGMP pathway

## Cultivation

- **Propagation:** Seed (no stratification needed); softwood cuttings; division
- **Spacing:** 30–45 cm; harvest aerial parts at early flowering
- **Yield:** 2–4 kg dried herb per 10 m²; essential oil 8–20 L/ha
- **Drying:** ≤35°C to retain volatile oils
- **Pollinator value:** Highly attractive to bees, butterflies, and
  hummingbirds; excellent late-season nectar source

## Culinary Use

- Fresh leaves used in Korean, Chinese, and Vietnamese cuisine
- Anise-mint flavor complements fish, pork, soups, and stir-fries
- Leaves used in tea blends (Korean mint tea — common cold remedy)
- Flowers edible as garnish with mild sweet flavor

## Safety and Contraindications

- **Estragole content:** Suspected hepatocarcinogen at very high doses;
  typical culinary use considered safe
- ⚠️ Avoid high-dose internal use during pregnancy
- Pulegone-containing chemotypes: use with caution (hepatotoxic risk)
- Generally well-tolerated at normal therapeutic doses
