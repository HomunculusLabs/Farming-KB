# Korean Mint (*Agastache rugosa* (Fisch. & C.A.Mey.) Kuntze)

## Overview

*Agastache rugosa*, commonly known as Korean mint, is a perennial aromatic
herb in the mint family (Lamiaceae). Native to [[neurotropic-fungi-japan-east-asia-species-cultural-context]], it has been used for
centuries in Traditional Chinese Medicine as Huo Xiang (藿香) and in Korean
[[ursolic-acid]]

## Pharmacology and Traditional Use

### Gastrointestinal (Primary TCM Indication)

- **TCM:** Resolves dampness, harmonizes middle jiao, stops vomiting; used
  for summer-heat, nausea, and poor appetite
- Antispasmodic and antiemetic activity validated in rodent models
- Carminative: relieves bloating and flatulence

### Antimicrobial Activity

- Essential oil active against *S. aureus*, *E. coli*, *B. subtilis* (MIC
  0.125–1.0 mg/mL)
- Rosmarinic acid demonstrates antiviral activity against HSV-1 (in vitro)

### Anti-inflammatory

- Inhibits COX-2, iNOS, and NF-κB; reduces TNF-α, IL-6, and PGE₂ in vitro
- Topical use for insect bites and skin inflammation

### Cardiovascular

- Tilianin reduces blood pressure in hypertensive rat models via NO/cGMP pathway

## Cultivation

- **Propagation:** Seed (no stratification needed); softwood cuttings; division
- **Spacing:** 30–45 cm; harvest aerial parts at [[equatorial-photoperiod-early-flowering]]
- **Yield:** 2–4 kg [[dried-herb-storage]] per 10 m²; essential oil 8–20 L/ha
- **Drying:** ≤35°C to retain volatile oils
- **Pollinator value:** Highly attractive to bees, butterflies, and
  hummingbirds; excellent late-season nectar source

## Culinary Use

- Fresh leaves used in Korean, Chinese, and Vietnamese cuisine
- Anise-mint flavor complements fish, pork, soups, and stir-fries
- Leaves used in tea blends (Korean mint tea — common cold remedy)
- Flowers edible as garnish with mild sweet flavor

## Safety and Contraindications

- **Estragole content:** Suspected hepatocarcinogen at very high doses;
  typical culinary use considered safe
- ⚠️ Avoid high-dose internal use during pregnancy
- Pulegone-containing chemotypes: use with caution (hepatotoxic risk)
- Generally well-tolerated at normal therapeutic doses
