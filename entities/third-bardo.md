# Third Bardo

## Description

The Third Bardo, as described in Timothy Leary's *The Psychedelic Experience* (a reinterpretation of the Tibetan Book of the Dead), is the final stage of a psychedelic session during which the voyager's consciousness begins to seek re-entry into ordinary ego-based reality. Also called the "Bardo of Seeking Rebirth" or the "Period of Re-Entry," it is characterized by confusion, wandering thoughts, mood swings, vivid hallucinations, and the emergence of powerful emotional forces including desire, fear, anger, and self-pity. The Third Bardo is considered the last opportunity for liberation before consciousness returns to mundane waking reality.

## Classification

- **Category:** Consciousness state / Psychedelic experience phase
- **Domain:** Psychopharmacology, transpersonal psychology, contemplative tradition
- **Source tradition:** Tibetan Book of the Dead (Bardo Thodol)
- **Western interpretation:** Timothy Leary, Ralph Metzner, Richard Alpert (1964)

## Key Facts

### Position in the Bardo Sequence

In Leary's framework, the psychedelic experience progresses through three bardos:

1. **First Bardo:** The period of ego-loss and Clear Light, experienced at peak intensity
2. **Second Bardo:** Period of hallucinatory visions when ego-loss is not maintained, featuring peaceful and wrathful deities/archetypes
3. **Third Bardo:** The re-entry period when consciousness seeks to re-establish ego identity

### Characteristic Experiences

The Third Bardo is marked by several distinctive phenomena described in the source text:

- **Confusion and bewilderment:** The voyager may question their sanity, feel unable to communicate with companions, or wonder if they will ever return to normal consciousness
- **Dream-like perception:** Familiar places, people, and objects appear as in a dream or "through a glass darkly"
- **Physical sensations:** Feelings of being squeezed between rocks, trapped in a cage, or oppressed
- **Gray, murky light:** A dull, gray luminescence suffusing all objects
- **Mood swings:** Sharp emotional oscillations "like the stretching and relaxing of a catapult," determined by karma (mental set)
- **Judgment visions:** Experiences of guilt, judgment, and confrontation with one's actions
- **Sexual visions:** Hallucinations of mating couples and orgiastic scenes representing the pull of biological drives
- **Future self-images:** Different versions of one's future personality appear, with the most likely one seen most clearly

### Four Methods of Preventing Re-Entry

The text provides four meditation techniques for navigating the Third Bardo:

1. **Meditation on the Buddha:** Visualizing a protective figure as apparent yet non-existent, "like the reflecting of the moon in water," then dissolving the visualization into the Void Clear Light
2. **Meditation on Good Games:** Forming a single firm resolve to pursue positive post-session goals, described as "directing the course of a horse by the use of the reins"
3. **Meditation on Illusion:** Recognizing all phenomena as unreal, "like dreams, like echoes, like cities of clouds, like mirages, like mirrored forms, like phantasmagoria, the moon seen in water"
4. **Meditation on the Void:** Resting in the uncreated state, "like the pouring of water into water"

### Re-Entry and Personality Selection

As the Third Bardo concludes, the voyager must choose their post-session personality. The text instructs: "Whatever you choose, choose impartially, without attraction or revulsion. Enter into game-existence with good grace. Voluntarily and freely." The mental state during re-entry is believed to influence subsequent waking consciousness.

### Role of the Guide

Throughout the Third Bardo, the guide (trip sitter) plays a critical role by reading instructions, providing reassurance, offering physical objects for concentration, and reminding the voyager of the teachings. The guide is instructed to hand the wanderer objects to concentrate on, show mirrors to demonstrate altered self-perception, and repeatedly remind them of the manual's instructions.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Psychedelic mushroom context:** The Third Bardo framework is directly relevant to experiences with psilocybin mushrooms, which are central to mycological and psychedelic cultivation communities.
- **Integration practice:** Understanding the Third Bardo helps cultivators and practitioners prepare for and integrate psychedelic experiences, particularly those involving cultivated fungal medicines.
- **[[set-and-setting]]:** The emphasis on preparation, guidance, and trust in the Third Bardo instructions informs best practices for safe psychedelic mushroom sessions.
- **Cultural significance:** Leary's interpretation of the Tibetan Book of the Dead has deeply influenced psychedelic culture, including the community surrounding [[psilocybin-mushroom-cultivation]] and use.
- **Therapeutic applications:** Modern clinical research on psilocybin therapy echoes many Third Bardo principles—integration of difficult experiences, importance of guidance, and the therapeutic value of ego dissolution.
