## Overview

**Quercus montana**, the chestnut oak, is a medium to large deciduous tree native to the [[bolete-fungi-appalachian-region]] and adjacent areas of [[polypore-fungi-eastern-north-america]] America.

It is a major component of dry oak forests on ridges and upper slopes, valued for its rot-resistant wood and wildlife acorn production.

The species typically reaches 18–22 m (60–72 ft) in height with a rounded crown.

Its leaves superficially resemble those of American chestnut (*[[castanea-dentata]]*), giving the tree its common name.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Quercus montana** Willd. belongs to the family Fagaceae, genus *Quercus*, section Quercus (white oaks).

It was described by Carl Ludwig Willdenow in 1805.

The species has also been widely known as *Quercus prinus* L., though that name is now considered a nomenclatural synonym of uncertain application.

Chestnut oak is placed in the white oak group, sharing the characteristic lobed leaves and sweet acorns of that clade.

It frequently hybridizes with [[quercus-alba]] (white oak), [[quercus-rubra]] (northern red oak), and [[quercus-coccinea]] (scarlet oak).

### Bark and Form

Bark is dark gray to nearly black, forming deep, wide vertical fissures that create a distinctive ridged appearance.

The bark pattern is often described as resembling that of white oak but with broader, more pronounced ridges.

The crown is rounded and relatively dense, becoming more irregular with age.

The trunk is typically short and stout, especially on exposed ridge-top sites.

### Leaves

Leaves are alternate, simple, 10–20 cm (4–8 in) long and 5–10 cm (2–4 in) wide.

The margin has rounded teeth (crenate-dentate) with 10–14 shallow lobes, closely resembling chestnut leaves.

The upper surface is dark yellow-green and glossy; the lower surface is paler and may have sparse stellate hairs.

Leaf base is rounded to cordate; the apex is acute to rounded.

Petioles are stout, 1–3 cm long, and yellow-green.

Autumn color ranges from yellow-orange to russet brown, occasionally developing red tones.

### Flowers and Fruit

Chestnut oak is monoecious, producing separate male and female flowers on the same tree in mid-spring.

Male catkins are pendulous, 5–10 cm (2–4 in) long, yellowish-green.

Female flowers are short spikes with a single ovule, appearing in leaf axils near branch tips.

Acorns are 1.5–3 cm (0.6–1.2 in) long, ovoid, with a shallow, bowl-shaped cup enclosing one-third to one-half of the nut.

The cup scales are reddish-brown and tightly appressed.

Acorns mature in one season (annual), dropping in early autumn.

They are among the sweetest of the white oak acorns, with relatively low tannin content.

## Distribution and Habitat

Chestnut oak occurs from southern Maine and southeastern Pennsylvania south to northern Georgia and Alabama, west to southern Ohio and eastern Tennessee.

Its range is centered on the Appalachian Mountains, where it is one of the dominant ridge-top species.

The species grows best on dry, rocky, well-drained soils derived from sandstone, shale, and acidic igneous rocks.

It is most abundant on west- and south-facing upper slopes and ridge crests between 300–1,200 m (1,000–4,000 ft) elevation.
