---# Overview

**Phyllostachys nigra** is a running bamboo species native to the Hunan and Jiangxi provinces of southern China, renowned for the striking dark purple-to-black coloration of its mature culms. It is one of the most widely recognized ornamental bamboos globally, prized for its dramatic color contrast against green foliage and its elegant, arching habit.

The species has been cultivated in Japan for centuries, where it holds significant cultural importance in garden design, tea ceremony aesthetics, and traditional crafts. Its jet-black culms make it an iconic feature plant in both formal and naturalistic landscape designs.

## Morphology

**Culms:** Erect to slightly arching, typically 3–6 m tall, 1–3 cm in diameter. New culms emerge green and progressively darken to deep purple or jet-black over 1–3 years as the waxy cuticle develops pigmentation.

The color transition is one of the most [[savory-growing-season-and-non-growing-season-management]]. Rhizome spread is somewhat less aggressive than [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]], and other [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]].

Hardy in USDA zones 6–10, tolerating temperatures to approximately −15°C (5°F). Culms may suffer cold damage below −10°C but the rhizome system survives and resprouts.

Prefers rich, well-drained loamy soils with consistent moisture but tolerates clay, sandy soils, and periods of drought once established. Best coloration develops with full sun exposure.

## Cultivation and Uses

**Oramental planting:** The premier black-stemmed bamboo for landscape use. Used as specimen plants, in Japanese-style gardens, meditation gardens, and modern architectural landscapes.

The dark culms create dramatic contrast against light-colored walls, gravel, and green foliage, making it a favorite of landscape designers.

**Traditional crafts:** In Japan, black bamboo is used for tea ceremony utensils (chashaku), flower arrangement tools (kenzan bases), fishing rods, walking sticks, and umbrella handles.

**Musical instruments:** Culms are used in the construction of shakuhachi (Japanese bamboo flute), though the species *P. bambusoides* is more commonly used for this purpose.

**Fencing and garden structures:** Split and whole culms are used for decorative fencing, trellises, and garden edging in Japanese and Chinese garden traditions.

**[[permplant-willows-salix-erosion-control-fodder]]:** The dense root and rhizome network stabilizes soil on slopes and stream banks, though it is less commonly used for this purpose than larger bamboos.

**Shoot production:** Young shoots are edible but less commonly harvested for food than other *Phyllostachys* species. Shoots have a mild, slightly bitter flavor.
