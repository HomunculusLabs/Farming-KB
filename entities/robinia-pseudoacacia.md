# Robinia pseudoacacia

**Type:** Species
**Category:** Deciduous tree, nitrogen-fixing legume
**Also known as:** Black locust, false acacia, yellow locust

## Overview
Robinia pseudoacacia is a fast-growing, nitrogen-fixing leguminous tree
native to the Appalachian and Ozark mountains of the United States. It
can reach heights of 25 meters and live for up to 200 years. Widely
planted worldwide for erosion control, shelter belts, fodder, and
timber production, it is one of the most valuable pioneer species in
temperate [[permaculture]] systems.

## Key Details

### Botany
- **Family:** Fabaceae (Papilionaceae)
- **Form:** Deciduous tree to 25 m
- **Lifespan:** Up to 200 years
- **Root system:** Deep, with nitrogen-fixing nodules
- **Thorns:** Present on branches (protects from stock browsing)

### Growth Conditions
- Hardy and adaptable to a wide range of temperate climates
- Tolerant of poor soils, drought, and cold conditions
- Prefers well-drained soils but adapts to many site types
- Spreads by suckering; can become invasive if unmanaged

### Fodder Value
- Foliage is readily eaten by livestock
- Seed is suitable as poultry feed
- Estimated nitrogen yield: 1,600 kg/ha
- High protein content in leaves

### Timber Properties
- Hard, heavy, durable, and very rot-resistant
- Used extensively for fence posts, poles, railway sleepers,
  stakes, and mine timbers
- Excellent firewood; burns like coal, slow to ignite
- Well suited to charcoal production

### Products and Uses
- **Honey:** Produces honey regarded as one of the finest in the
  world; excellent bee forage
- **Erosion control:** Commonly planted for slope stabilization
- **Shelter belts:** Widely used in windbreak systems
- **Wildlife habitat:** Provides food and cover for birds and
  mammals
- **Coppice:** Coppices well for sustainable firewood production

### Management Considerations
- Suckering habit can make it a pest if not properly managed
- Thorns require careful planning in grazing systems
- Non-suckering cultivars are available
- Can be confined by livestock in integrated systems

## Relevance
Black locust is one of the most important pioneer species for
temperate permaculture and regenerative agriculture. Its combination
of rapid growth, nitrogen fixation, high-quality timber, excellent
firewood, and premier honey production makes it a multi-functional
cornerstone of food forest and silvopastoral systems. Its ability to
coppice and its longevity ensure productive yields for generations.

### Propagation
- Easily propagated from seed (scarification recommended)
- Also propagated from root cuttings and suckers
- Seed requires cold stratification or boiling water treatment
- Rapid early growth makes it effective for quick site establishment
