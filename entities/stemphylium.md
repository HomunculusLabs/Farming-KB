## Description

**Stemphylium** is a genus of filamentous fungi in the phylum Ascomycota, belonging to the family Pleosporaceae. The genus includes both saprotrophic and plant-pathogenic species. In the context of fungal physiology research, Stemphylium has been studied for its **carbon and phosphorus translocation mechanisms** through hyphal systems, contributing to our understanding of nutrient movement in fungal mycelia.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Dothideomycetes
- **Order:** Pleosporales
- **Family:** Pleosporaceae
- **Genus:** Stemphylium

## Key Facts

- **Diffusion-based translocation:** Olsson and Jennings (1991) demonstrated that the translocation of 14C (carbon-14) and 32P (phosphorus-32) through hyphal systems of Stemphylium occurs by diffusion, along with Rhizopus and Trichoderma.
- **Nutrient movement model:** The diffusion-based translocation mechanism in Stemphylium contrasts with the slower-than-diffusion translocation observed for 137Cs in [[schizophyllum-commune]], suggesting species-specific differences in how different elements move through fungal hyphae.
- **Plant pathogenicity:** Several Stemphylium species are significant plant pathogens causing leaf spots and other diseases on a wide range of crops, including tomatoes, onions, garlic, and asparagus.

## Ecological Role

Stemphylium species function in dual ecological roles:

1. **Saprotrophs:** Decompose dead plant material in soil and on plant surfaces
2. **Plant pathogens:** Cause stemphylium leaf blight and other diseases on economically important crops

The genus contributes to carbon [[mollison-designers-fish-pond-fertiliser-and-nutrient-cycling]] through its saprotrophic activities, while its pathogenic members can significantly impact [[fukuoka-agricultural-production-net-energy-deduction]].

## Relevance to Mycology and Cultivation

- **Translocation physiology:** Research on Stemphylium contributed to establishing diffusion as a primary mechanism of nutrient translocation in filamentous fungi.
- **Crop pathology:** Several Stemphylium species (S. lycopersici, S. vesicarium, S. botryosum) are significant pathogens of vegetable crops, relevant to cultivation management.
- **Comparative mycology:** Understanding diffusion-based translocation in Stemphylium provides a baseline for comparing translocation mechanisms across fungal genera.

## Distinguishing Features

- Dark olive to brown colony coloration
- Percurrently proliferating conidiophores (elongating through previous scar)
