# Avicennia marina (Grey Mangrove, White Mangrove)

## Description

Avicennia marina is the predominant mangrove tree of estuaries, river banks, and sheltered coasts across Australia. These trees obtain oxygen through specialised aerial roots (pneumatophores) that thrust upward from the mud. The soil is typically soft, moist, saline, and without developed profiles. A keystone species of coastal wetland ecosystems.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Family**: Acanthaceae
- **Genus**: Avicennia
- **Species**: A. marina
- **Common names**: Grey mangrove, white mangrove
- **Type**: Evergreen mangrove tree

## Key Facts

- Habitat: Estuaries, river banks, sheltered coasts
- Root system: Specialised pneumatophores for oxygen uptake
- Soil: Soft, moist, saline; no profile development
- Frost tolerance: Tolerates some frost
- Distribution: Widespread along Australian coastlines; Indo-Pacific
- Companion species: Casuarina glauca (south), Ceriops tagal (north)

## Uses

### Culinary

- Seeds, after three boilings, taste similar to avocado
- Golden-yellow flowers yield nectar for light amber honey (mid-autumn to early winter)

### Fodder

- Leaves palatable to stock

### Timber

- Extremely tough, hard wood
- Density approximately 880 kg/cu m
- Used for knees and elbows in boat building

### Ecological

- Critical fish and crustacean nursery habitat
- [[query-why-are-mangroves-critical-for-climate-change-adaptation-and-coastal-protection]] from storm surges
- Carbon sequestration in mangrove sediments

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

In permaculture, mangroves are essential for coastal zone design. Avicennia marina provides coastal protection, food (processed seeds and honey), aquaculture habitat, durable timber, and fodder. They represent the productive edge between land and sea.

## Cultivation Notes

- Requires tidal inundation or saline waterlogged conditions
- Propagated from propagules (viviparous seedlings)
- Cannot tolerate prolonged freshwater conditions
- Legal protections apply in many jurisdictions

## Related Species

- Ceriops tagal - companion northern mangrove
- Casuarina glauca (swamp oak) - companion on higher ground
- Rhizophora spp. - tropical mangrove genera
