# Ashwagandha (*[[[[adaptogenic-herbs-overview|adaptogenic]]-herbs-overview|Withania somnifera]]*)

## Overview

Ashwagandha (*Withania somnifera*) is one of the most important herbs in Ayurvedic medicine, classified as a *Rasayana* (rejuvenative). Native to the dry regions of India, North Africa, and the Middle East, this evergreen shrub in the nightshade family (Solanaceae) has been used for over 3,000 years to relieve stress, increase energy, and improve concentration. Its thick, tuberous roots contain steroidal lactones called withanolides, which are responsible for most of its pharmacological activity. Modern clinical research has validated its use as an adaptogen, with strong evidence for stress and anxiety reduction, sleep improvement, and testosterone support.

**Common names:** Ashwagandha (Sanskrit: *aśva* = horse, *gandhā* = smell), Indian ginseng, winter cherry, poison gooseberry. The species epithet *somnifera* means "sleep-bearing" in Latin.

## Description

An evergreen-to-deciduous woody shrub growing 35–75 cm tall (up to 150 cm under cultivation). Leaves are simple, ovate, dull green (4–10 cm), covered in fine stellate hairs giving a greyish-green appearance. Flowers are small, bell-shaped, green to pale yellow, borne in axillary cymes. Fruit is a small orange-red berry (~6 mm) enclosed in an inflated papery calyx. The thick, tuberous, fleshy roots are creamy-white to light brown and emit a characteristic horse-like odor.

## Native Range & Distribution

Native to dry regions of India (Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat, Punjab), North Africa (Egypt, Morocco, Tunisia), and the Middle East (Jordan, Israel, Yemen). Naturalized across the Mediterranean, Canary Islands, East and Southern Africa, Nepal, Sri Lanka, and China. Thrives in arid and semi-arid regions on sandy or loamy well-drained soils at 500–1,500 m altitude. India dominates global production (~70% of supply).

## Active Compounds

### Withanolides (Steroidal Lactones)
Over 40 withanolides identified. Key compounds:
- **Withaferin A:** Most studied; cytotoxic, anti-inflammatory, NF-κB pathway inhibitor
- **Withanolide D:** Neuroprotective, GABA-mimetic
- **Withanolide A:** Neuroregenerative, promotes neurite outgrowth
- **Withanone:** Anti-cancer, anti-stress

### Other Constituents
- **Alkaloids** (~0.13–0.31%): Ashwagandhine, somniferine, withanine
- **Saponins:** Sitoindosides VII–X, withanosides I–X
- **Iron:** ~3% in roots — among the highest of any medicinal plant
- **Amino acids:** Including tryptophan
- **Phenolic compounds:** Flavonoids with antioxidant activity

Commercial extracts are typically standardized to 2.5–5% total withanolides (HPLC).

## Ayurvedic Traditional Uses

Classified as *Medhya Rasayana* (cognitive rejuvenative). Properties: bitter and astringent taste, light and oily quality, heating potency, pungent post-digestive effect. Balances Vata and Kapha doshas. Classical applications include:
- **Balya** — strength promoter for weakness, debility, convalescence
- **Vajikarna** — aphrodisiac for low libido and male sexual dysfunction
- **Medhya rasayana** — cognitive enhancer for memory, learning, concentration
- **Nidrajanana** — sleep promoter for insomnia and restlessness
- **Arthritis and joint pain** — anti-inflammatory applications
- **Anemia** — attributed to high iron content

Traditional preparations include churna (powder), arishta (fermented liquid), ghrita (medicated ghee), and ksheerapaka (milk decoction).

## Modern Clinical Evidence

### Stress & Anxiety (Strong Evidence)
Multiple RCTs and meta-analyses demonstrate significant reductions in perceived stress and anxiety scores. A 2019 meta-analysis (Pratte et al.) of 12 studies found significant anxiolytic effects (Hedges' g = −0.56). Mechanisms include HPA axis regulation, cortisol reduction, GABA-mimetic activity, and serotonergic modulation.

### Sleep Improvement (Moderate–Strong Evidence)
RCTs using actigraphy and sleep questionnaires show improvements in sleep onset latency, sleep efficiency, total sleep time, and sleep quality. Langade et al. (2019) found significant improvement vs. placebo in both healthy subjects and insomnia patients at 300 mg/day.

### Testosterone & Male Fertility (Moderate Evidence)
A 2019 meta-analysis of 5 RCTs found significant testosterone increases (pooled SMD = 1.084). Studies show improved sperm count, motility, and morphology, particularly in men with stress-related infertility.

### Cognitive Function (Moderate Evidence)
Preclinical studies show neuroprotection against beta-amyloid plaques and tau protein aggregation. Clinical trials demonstrate improved memory, reaction time, executive function, and attention. Mechanisms include antioxidant activity, neurite outgrowth promotion, and acetylcholinesterase inhibition.

### Physical Performance (Moderate Evidence)
Wankhede et al. (2015) found significant increases in muscle strength (bench press and leg extension) and body composition improvements at 600 mg/day in resistance-trained men.

## Dosage

| Preparation | Typical Dose |
|-------------|-------------|
| Root powder (churna) | 1,000–6,000 mg/day |
| Standardized extract (2.5% withanolides) | 300–600 mg/day |
| KSM-66® extract (~5% withanolides) | 300–600 mg/day |
| Sensoril® (full-spectrum) | 125–250 mg/day |
| Tincture (1:5) | 1–3 mL/day |

Most clinical trials show effects within 4–12 weeks. Traditionally taken with warm milk and honey to enhance bioavailability of fat-soluble withanolides.

## Safety & Interactions

Generally safe at typical doses for up to 3 months. Rare side effects: gastrointestinal upset, nausea, drowsiness. **Contraindicated in pregnancy** (abortifacient activity in animal studies).

**Key drug interactions:**
- **Thyroid medications:** May increase T3/T4 levels; caution in hyperthyroidism
- **Sedatives/benzodiazepines:** Additive CNS depression
- **Immunosuppressants:** May counteract immunosuppressive therapy
- **Antidiabetic drugs:** May potentiate blood sugar-lowering effects
- **Anticoagulants:** Monitor INR if on warfarin

## Cultivation

Optimal temperature: 20–35°C. Annual rainfall: 500–750 mm. Sandy loam to light red soils, pH 7.5–8.0. Propagated primarily by seed (germination in 2–3 weeks); transplanted at 30–40 days with 30–40 cm spacing. Minimal irrigation after establishment. Roots harvested at 150–180 days (1,500–2,500 kg dry/ha). Shade-drying at ≤40°C is critical to preserve withanolide content. Improved varieties include 'Poshita,' 'Pratap,' 'Rakshita,' and 'Jawahar-20.'
