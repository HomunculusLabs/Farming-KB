# Withania somnifera (Ashwagandha)

## Overview

*Withania somnifera*, commonly known as **Ashwagandha** (Sanskrit: "smell of a horse," referring to both its aroma and the traditional belief that it imparts the vigor of a horse), is one of the most important herbs in **Ayurvedic medicine**. Classified as a *rasayana* (rejuvenating herb), it has been used for over 3,000 years to relieve stress, increase energy, and improve concentration.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Clade:** Tracheophytes
- **Clade:** Angiosperms
- **Clade:** Eudicots
- **Clade:** Asterids
- **Order:** Solanales
- **Family:** Solanaceae (nightshades)
- **Genus:** *Withania*
- **Species:** *W. somnifera*

### Notable Varieties

- **KSM-66** — Root extract standardized to ≥5% withanolides; the most studied commercial extract
- **Sensoril** — Root + leaf extract standardized to 10% withanolides; more potent anxiolytic effect
- **Shoden** — High-potency extract standardized to 35% withanolides

## Botanical Description

Ashwagandha is an evergreen **shrub** growing 35–75 cm tall. The leaves are dull green, elliptic, and up to 10 cm long. The flowers are small, green, and bell-shaped. The fruit is an orange-red berry enclosed in an inflated calyx. The **root** — thick, fleshy, tuberous, and pale brown on the outside, creamy white within — is the primary part used medicinally.

## Distribution

Native to the dry regions of India, North Africa, and the Middle East. India is the largest commercial producer, particularly the states of Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, and Gujarat. It thrives in sandy, well-drained soils and arid conditions.

## Phytochemistry

### Withanolides (Steroidal Lactones)

The primary bioactive constituents are **withanolides**, a group of naturally occurring steroidal lactones:

- **Withaferin A** — Anticancer, anti-inflammatory; induces apoptosis in tumor cells
- **Withanolide D** — Neuroprotective; promotes neurite outgrowth
- **Withanoside IV and VI** — Cognitive enhancement; anti-amnesic effects
- **Withaferin A** — Inhibits NF-κB signaling pathway, reducing inflammation

### Alkaloids

- **Ashwagandhine** — Mild sedative properties
- **Sominine** — CNS depressant activity
- **Anahygrine** — Stimulant at low doses

### Other Compounds

- **Sitoindosides IX and X** — Antioxidant flavonoids
- **Iron** — Root contains approximately 3 mg/g
- **Tannins** — Astringent, antimicrobial properties

## Traditional Ayurvedic Uses

Ashwagandha is classified as a *medhya rasayana* (brain tonic) in Ayurveda:

- **Vata dosha** — Primary indication; reduces anxiety, insomnia, and nervous exhaustion
- **Kapha dosha** — Used in excess can aggravate; generally balancing in moderated doses
- **Pitta dosha** — Cooling preparation (milk decoction) recommended to avoid aggravation

### Classical Preparations

- **Ashwagandharishta** — Fermented decoction with 20+ herbs
- **Ashwagandha Churna** — Dried root powder
- **Ashwagandha Ghrita** — Root cooked in ghee (clarified butter)
- **Brihat Ashwagandha Ghrita** — Enhanced formula with additional herbs

## Modern Clinical Evidence

### Stress and Cortisol Reduction

Multiple double-blind, placebo-controlled trials demonstrate significant reductions in cortisol (14–23%) and perceived stress scores with 300–600 mg/day of standardized root extract over 60 days (Chandrasekhar 2012, Lopresti 2019).

### Sleep Quality

A 2019 randomized trial (Langade et al.) found that patients receiving 300 mg Ashwagandha root extract twice daily for 10 weeks showed significant improvements in sleep quality (Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index), sleep onset latency, and sleep efficiency compared to placebo.

### Athletic Performance

Meta-analyses show consistent improvements in:

- VO₂ max (+4.5% average)
- Strength and power output
- Recovery markers (reduced creatine kinase, reduced muscle damage)
- Body composition (slight lean mass increase, fat reduction)

### Testosterone and Male Fertility

A 2019 meta-analysis (Lopresti et al.) of 4 RCTs found Ashwagandha supplementation significantly increased testosterone levels in men (particularly those with suboptimal baseline levels) and improved sperm quality parameters.

### Thyroid Function

Preliminary evidence suggests Ashwagandha may normalize thyroid function in subclinical hypothyroidism, though caution is advised for hyperthyroid patients as it may further elevate thyroid hormones.

## Dosage Guidelines

| Form | Standard Dose | Withanolide Content |
|------|--------------|---------------------|
| Root powder (churna) | 1,000–6,000 mg/day | ~0.3% |
| KSM-66 extract | 300–600 mg/day | ≥5% |
| Sensoril extract | 125–250 mg/day | ≥10% |
| Shoden extract | 30–60 mg/day | ≥35% |

Timing: Traditionally taken at night for sleep, or split morning/evening for stress.

## Safety and Contraindications

- **Pregnancy and lactation** — Contraindicated; abortifacient in animal studies
- **Autoimmune conditions** — May stimulate immune activity; theoretical risk
- **Hyperthyroidism** — May further increase thyroid hormone levels
- **Drug interactions** — Potential interactions with immunosuppressants, sedatives, thyroid medications, and antidiabetic drugs

Adverse effects are generally mild and include gastrointestinal discomfort, headache, and drowsiness at higher doses.

## Cultivation

Ashwagandha is cultivated from seed, typically sown in nursery beds and transplanted after 25–30 days. The crop matures in 150–180 days. Roots are harvested after the plant dries post-leaf fall. Dried root yields 150–200 kg/acre on average. India produces approximately 4,000 metric tons annually.
