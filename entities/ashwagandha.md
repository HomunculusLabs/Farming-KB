## Overview

**Ashwagandha** (*Withania somnifera*) is one of the most important herbs in Ayurvedic medicine, where it has been used for over 3,000 years. Known as "Indian ginseng" or "winter cherry," it is classified as a **rasayana** (rejuvenating) herb. The name translates from Sanskrit as "smell of a horse," referring both to its aroma and the traditional belief that it imparts the vigor and strength of a stallion.

## Botanical Description

- **Growth habit**: Evergreen shrub, 35–75 cm tall
- **Leaves**: Dull green, elliptic, 10–12 cm long
- **Flowers**: Small, green, bell-shaped, in axillary clusters
- **Fruit**: Orange-red berry enclosed in an inflated calyx
- **Root**: Thick, fleshy, tuberous — primary medicinal part
- **Root morphology**: Cream-brown exterior, white interior, distinct horse-like odor

## Active Compounds

The therapeutic activity is primarily attributed to a group of steroidal lactones called **withanolides**:

- **Withaferin A**: Anti-inflammatory, anti-tumor, apoptosis-inducing
- **Withanolide D**: Cytotoxic, immunomodulatory
- **Withanolide A**: Neuroprotective properties
- **Withanine**: Mild sedative, adaptogenic activity
- **Somniferine**: Alkaloid component
- **Anaferine**: Cardiovascular effects
- **Total withanolide content**: Typically 0.3–2.5% in dried root, standardized to 2.5–5% in extracts

## Pharmacology

Ashwagandha's adaptogenic effects are mediated through modulation of the **hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis**. Key mechanisms include:

- **Cortisol regulation**: Reduces serum cortisol levels by 14–23% in clinical trials
- **GABAergic activity**: Binds GABA-A receptors, producing anxiolytic effects
- **Anti-inflammatory**: Inhibits NF-κB signaling pathway and COX-2 expression
- **Antioxidant**: Scavenges free radicals, increases superoxide dismutase and catalase
- **Thyroid modulation**: Enhances T3 and T4 production in subclinical hypothyroidism
- **Neuroprotection**: Promotes neurite outgrowth and synaptic reconstruction

## Traditional Ayurvedic Uses

- **Kapha and Vata imbalance**: Primary doshic indication
- **Brihana** (strengthening) and **Vajikarna** (aphrodisiac) therapy
- **Rasayana**: Anti-aging, longevity promotion
- **Medhya rasayana**: Cognitive enhancement and memory support
- Classical formulations: *Ashwagandharishta*, *Ashwagandha Churna*, *KSM-66*

## Standardized Extracts

### KSM-66 (Ixoreal Biomed)
- Root-only extract, full-spectrum
- Standardized to ≥5% withanolides
- Most clinically studied extract globally
- Dose: 300–600 mg/day

### Sensoril (Natreon)
- Root + leaf extract (8:1 ratio)
- Standardized to ≥10% withanolides
- Higher potency, lower effective dose
- Dose: 125–250 mg/day

- **Timing**: Traditionally taken with warm milk before bed; modern use split AM/PM
- **Onset**: Anxiolytic effects noticeable within 2–4 weeks; full benefits at 8–12 weeks
- **Cycling**: No required cycling; safe for continuous use per available data

## Cultivation

- **Climate**: Semi-arid to subtropical, drought-tolerant once established
- **Soil**: Sandy loam, pH 7.5–8.0, well-drained
- **Propagation**: Seeds (direct sowing) or tissue culture
- **Spacing**: 30–45 cm between plants
- **Harvest**: Roots at 150–180 days after planting
- **Yield**: 200–500 kg dried root per hectare
- **Major producers**: India (Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh), Nepal, China
- **Post-harvest**: Roots washed, cut, dried at 35–40°C, milled to powder

## Contraindications & Interactions

- **Pregnancy**: Contraindicated — traditional abortifacient properties
- **Autoimmune conditions**: May stimulate immune activity
- **Thyroid disorders**: Caution in hyperthyroidism; potentiates levothyroxine
- **Diabetes medications**: Additive hypoglycemic effect
- **Immunosuppressants**: May reduce efficacy of prednisone, azathioprine
- **Sedatives/barbiturates**: Additive CNS depression
- **Children**: Limited safety data; not recommended under 12 years

## Safety Profile

Ashwagandha has an excellent safety profile in clinical trials. The **NOAEL** (No Observed Adverse Effect Level) in animal studies is 2,000 mg/kg body weight. Mild gastrointestinal discomfort is the most commonly reported adverse effect (<5% incidence). Hepatotoxicity is rare but has been documented in isolated case reports, primarily with multi-ingredient formulations rather than ashwagandha alone.
