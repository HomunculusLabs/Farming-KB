# Ashwagandha (*Withania somnifera*)

## Overview

ashwagandha-at-[[query-how-to-grow-ashwagandha-at-home]]|Ashwagandha]] (*Withania somnifera*, Solanaceae) is one of the most important herbs in Ayurvedic medicine, classified as a *Rasayana* (rejuvenating) herb. The name derives from Sanskrit: *ashwa* (horse) + *gandha* (smell), referring to both the root's distinct aroma and the traditional belief that it imparts the vigor and strength of a horse. Sometimes called "Indian Ginseng" or "Winter Cherry," ashwagandha has been used continuously for over 3,000 years across the Indian subcontinent.

## Botanical Description

Ashwagandha is an evergreen shrub growing 35–75 cm tall, native to dry regions of India, North Africa, and the Middle East. Key botanical features:

- **Leaves**: Ovate, dull green, 5–10 cm long, covered in fine trichomes
- **Flowers**: Small, bell-shaped, greenish-yellow, borne in axillary clusters
- **Fruit**: Small orange-red berry enclosed in an inflated calyx (lantern-like)
- **Root**: Thick, fleshy taproot — the primary medicinal part
- **Seeds**: Small, yellow, kidney-shaped

The plant thrives in arid, [[query-how-to-grow-rare-tropical-fruits-in-marginal-and-subtropical-climates]] and is drought-tolerant once established. It grows as a perennial in tropical zones and as an annual in [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]].

## Phytochemistry

### Withanolides

The primary bioactive class, withanolides are C28 steroidal lactones built on an ergostane skeleton. Over 40 withanolides have been isolated from *W. somnifera*:

- **Withaferin A**: The most studied; potent anti-inflammatory, anti-cancer, and immunomodulatory activities
- **Withanolide D**: Cytotoxic against multiple cancer cell lines
- **Withanone**: Cytoprotective, neuroprotective
- **Withanolide A**: Promotes neurite outgrowth and synaptic reconstruction

### Alkaloids

- Somniferine, somnine, somniferinine — contribute to sedative properties
- mckenna witch ointments tropane alkaloids flying salves (trace amounts): cuscohygrine, anaferine

### Other Constituents

- **Sitoindosides IX and X**: Unique glycowithanolides with significant anti-stress activity
- **Iron**: Notably high concentration in roots (contributing to traditional use for anemia)
- **Flavonoids**: Kaempferol, quercetin derivatives
- **Tannins and essential oils**

## Pharmacological Properties

### Adaptogenic Activity

Ashwagandha's defining property is its adaptogenic action — normalizing physiological processes regardless of the direction of deviation. This is mediated through modulation of the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis:

- Reduces cortisol levels by 14–28% in chronically stressed individuals
- Normalizes DHEA-S and testosterone ratios
- Modulates stress-responsive transcription factors (NF-κB, AP-1)

### Neurological Effects

- **Anxiolysis**: GABA-mimetic activity comparable to lorazepam in animal models without sedative side effects at therapeutic doses
- **Cognitive enhancement**: Improved memory, attention, and information processing speed in [[cancer-medicinal-mushrooms-schizophyllan-spg-clinical-trials]]
- **Neuroprotection**: Protection against β-amyloid and glutamate-induced neurotoxicity
- **Sleep improvement**: Significant improvement in sleep quality and sleep onset latency ( root extract 300 mg bid)

### Musculoskeletal Effects

- Increases VO₂ max and cardiovascular endurance by 13–15%
- Enhances muscle strength and recovery when combined with resistance training
- Increases testosterone levels by 15–40% in men with subclinical deficiency
- Improves sperm quality parameters (count, motility, morphology)

### Immunomodulation

- Increases immunoglobulin production (IgG, IgM)
- Enhances macrophage and NK cell activity
- Paradoxically, acts as both immunostimulant and immunosuppressant depending on context

### Anti-inflammatory

Withaferin A inhibits NF-κB signaling, reducing expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6). COX-2 inhibition has been demonstrated at physiologically relevant concentrations.

## Cultivation

### Growing Conditions

| Parameter | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Climate | Arid to semi-arid, tropical/subtropical |
| Temperature | 20–38°C optimal |
| Rainfall | 500–750 mm annually |
| Soil | Well-drained, sandy loam, pH 7.5–8.0 |
| Altitude | Sea level to 1500 m |
| Propagation | Direct seeding or transplanting |

### Agronomic Notes

- Seeds require no special pretreatment; germination in 7–14 days at 25°C
- Plant spacing: 30 × 60 cm (commercial) or 45 × 60 cm (seed production)
- Minimal fertilizer requirements; excess nitrogen reduces withanolide content
- Root harvest at 5–6 months, when leaves begin to yellow
- Average root yield: 400–600 kg/ha (dry weight)

### Key Cultivation Regions

India (Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Andhra Pradesh) produces the majority of the world's ashwagandha supply. Cultivation has expanded to the US, Australia, and parts of Africa as demand has surged.

## Extraction and Standardization

- **KSM-66**: Full-spectrum root extract standardized to 5% withanolides; most clinically studied extract
- **Sensoril**: Root and leaf extract standardized to 10% withanolides; higher potency, more sedating
- **[[kava-traditional-preparation-ceremonial-context]]**: Root powder (churna) — 3–6 g/day in warm milk with honey

## Dosage and Safety

| Form | Dose Range | Frequency |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| Root powder | 3–6 g/day | Divided doses |
| Standardized extract (5%) | 300–600 mg/day | 1–2 doses |
| Standardized extract (10%) | 125–250 mg/day | 1 dose |
| Tincture (1:5) | 2–4 mL/day | Divided doses |

### Contraindications

- Pregnancy (emmenagogue and uterotonic activity — risk of miscarriage)
