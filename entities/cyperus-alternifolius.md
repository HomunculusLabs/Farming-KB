## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Cyperus alternifolius** was described by the Swedish botanist Carl Linnaeus in 1753 in Species Plantarum. It is the type species of Cyperus subgenus Pycreus, though some modern treatments place it in Cyperus subgenus Chlorocyperus.

The specific epithet *alternifolius* refers to the alternate arrangement of the spikelets around the inflorescence axis, which creates the characteristic whorled, umbrella-like appearance.

Plants grow 60–150 cm tall from a short, stout rhizome. Culms are solid, triangular to subcylindrical, and smooth, arising in dense tufts from the base.

The rhizome is short, thick, and covered with dark brown scale-like sheaths from previous seasons. Under favorable conditions, a single clump may produce 50–100 culms per [[fukuoka-natural-farming-tropical-climates]], with peak flowering during warm, wet months.

Two subspecies are generally recognized: subsp. *alternifolius* (the typical form) and subsp. *flabelliformis*, which has a more [[mycofiltration-of-agricultural-runoff]].

In its introduced range in Florida, Cyperus alternifolius is most commonly encountered in the southern and central peninsula counties. Populations are concentrated in the Everglades Agricultural Area and suburban canal systems of the southeast coast.

It is frost-sensitive and does not persist in areas with regular freezing temperatures unless cultivated as an indoor or greenhouse plant. In temperate regions, it is grown exclusively as a container or houseplant.

Plants overwinter successfully indoors with bright indirect light and consistently moist soil. Growth resumes rapidly when temperatures exceed 18°C in spring.

## Cultivation and Uses

Cyperus alternifolius is one of the most widely cultivated ornamental sedges globally. It is grown as a houseplant, patio plant, and water garden feature [[comfrey-tea-liquid-fertilizer]] during [[cyperus-papyrus]].

The species is increasingly used in green roof and bioswale installations in tropical urban areas, where its tolerance of saturated substrates and rapid growth provide effective stormwater retention and filtration.

## Ecology

In naturalized range, Cyperus alternifolius can form dense stands that exclude native wetland vegetation. The thick rhizomatous mat creates a physical barrier to seedling establishment of other species.

The dense root system contributes to [[comparison-vetiver-grass-vs-bamboo-for-erosion-control]] along waterway margins but can also obstruct water flow in narrow drainage channels and ditches, creating maintenance challenges in managed waterways.
