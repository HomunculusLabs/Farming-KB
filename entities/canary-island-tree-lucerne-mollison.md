## Canary Island Tree Lucerne (Chamaecytisus proliferus)

## Description

The Canary Island tree lucerne, also known as tagasaste, is a fast-growing, nitrogen-fixing evergreen leguminous tree native to the Canary Islands. [[bill-mollison]] identifies it as the "number one chicken seed forage" among old-time poultry keepers, noting that abandoned chicken yards across West Australia can still be identified by the surviving tree lucerne plants.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Family**: Fabaceae
- **Genus**: Chamaecytisus
- **Species**: C. proliferus
- **Common Names**: Tree lucerne, tagasaste, escofbn
- **Type**: Evergreen nitrogen-fixing tree/shrub
- **Permaculture Role**: High-protein forage, soil nitrogen enrichment, pioneer species

## Key Facts

- Historically the premier chicken forage seed tree among Australian poultry keepers.
- Survives as a marker species at abandoned chicken yards, testament to its hardiness and persistence.
- As a legume, fixes atmospheric nitrogen, enriching soil in integrated forage systems.
- Evergreen habit provides year-round forage availability, unlike deciduous forage trees.
- Fast-growing, suitable for rapid establishment of forage systems.
- Provides both leaf forage and seed for poultry consumption.
- Adapted to Mediterranean and [[fukuoka-natural-farming-temperate-climates]].

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Soil Fertility**: Nitrogen fixation supports [[endorhiza-rhizosphere-bulk-soil-microbial-communities]], including beneficial [[mycorrhizal-fungi]].
- **Agroforestry Design**: Functions as a mid-canopy nitrogen fixer in food forest systems.
- **Compost Input**: High-nitrogen leaf litter accelerates compost decomposition and can supplement mushroom substrate formulations.
- **Poultry-Manure Cycling**: Manure from tree lucerne-fed poultry is nitrogen-rich and valuable for composting.
- **Erosion Control**: Fast root establishment protects soil structure and fungal hyphae networks.
