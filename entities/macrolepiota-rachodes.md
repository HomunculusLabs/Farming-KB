---# Macrolepiota rachodes (Shaggy Parasol)

## Overview

**Macrolepiota rachodes** is a large, saprotrophic agaric mushroom in the family *Agaricaceae*, widely distributed across [[macrolepiota-procera]]* (the true parasol) and *Macrolepiota mastoidea*. The species is valued as an [[guzman-chlorophyllum-molybdites-toxic-edible-paradox-yoruba-divinatory-use]]*, which causes severe gastrointestinal illness.

The taxonomic history has been complex. The species was originally described as *Agaricus rachodes* by Vittadini in 1835, later transferred to *Lepiota*, and finally placed in *Macrolepiota*. Some authorities still treat it as *Chlorophyllum rachodes* or *Lepiota rachodes*, reflecting ongoing phylogenetic debate about whether it belongs in *Macrolepiota* or *Chlorophyllum*. Molecular studies suggest it is more closely related to *Chlorophyllum* species than to *Macrolepiota procera*.

## Habitat and Distribution

*Macrolepiota rachodes* is a **saprotrophic species** found in a variety of habitats:

- **Deciduous and mixed woodlands** — fruiting among leaf litter and woody debris
- **Coniferous forests** — particularly under pines and spruces
- **Grasslands adjacent to woodlands** — parks, pastures, hedgerows
- **Compost heaps and enriched garden soils** — common in cultivated landscapes
- **Roadsides and disturbed ground** — often fruiting along paths

The species is **cosmopolitan in temperate zones**:
- Europe — widespread and common, especially in western and central regions
- [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-south-america]] — occasional reports from temperate zones

Fruiting season extends from **late summer through autumn** (August to November in the Northern Hemisphere), with peak abundance in September and October following warm, wet periods.
