## Overview

**Drosera peltata**, commonly known as the shield sundew or pale sundew, is a tuberous sundew native to Australia, New Zealand, and parts of [[savory-growing-season-and-non-growing-season-management]].

Stems are erect to semi-erect, glabrous, and green to reddish in color. They arise from the tuber and bear alternate, peltate leaves along the upper half. Each leaf is 5–12 mm in diameter with a central petiole attachment.

The lamina is circular to kidney-shaped, with tentacles on both the upper surface and the margins. Tentacles on the margins are typically longer than those on the surface, creating a distinctive fringed appearance.

Flowers are white to pale pink, 8–12 mm in diameter, and borne in terminal or axillary racemes. Each flower has five petals, five stamens, and a superior ovary with three styles. Flowering occurs in spring.

The flowers open sequentially from the base of the inflorescence upward, with each individual flower lasting 1–3 days. Self-pollination is effective, and seed set is typically high even in cultivation.

## Distribution and Habitat

D. peltata is found across eastern and southern Australia, from Queensland through New South Wales, Victoria, South Australia, and Tasmania. It also occurs in New Zealand and has been recorded in Papua New Guinea, Indonesia, and the Philippines.

The species inhabits a wide range of open, seasonally moist habitats. These include heathlands, open woodlands, montane bogs, roadside seepages, and the margins of swamps and streams. It is one of the most habitat-tolerant tuberous sundews.

Elevation ranges from near sea level to approximately 1500 m. Montane populations at higher elevations tend to produce more robust growth forms with taller stems and larger leaves.

The species is well-adapted to fire-prone landscapes. The underground tuber survives fire events, and the plants often emerge in greater numbers following controlled or natural burns. This fire adaptation is shared with many other Australian tuberous Drosera species.

Soil preferences include sandy loams, peaty sands, and lateritic gravels. The species requires a distinct dry period for tuber dormancy, and populations in areas with year-round moisture may decline without seasonal drying.

In New Zealand, D. peltata is found primarily on the North Island and northern South Island in open, boggy areas. The New Zealand populations are geographically disjunct from the Australian mainland range.

## Trap Mechanism and Prey

The trapping mechanism of D. peltata follows the flypaper model shared by all Drosera species. Glandular tentacles on the [[query-can-fungi-break-down-plastic-pollution]] the insect exoskeleton and soft tissues. Nutrient absorption occurs over 3–7 days depending on prey size.

Common prey items include small flies, gnats, ants, and springtails. The upright growth form of D. peltata positions the trapping leaves above ground-level vegetation, intercepting flying insects more effectively than ground-hugging rosette species.

The mucilage produced by D. peltata contains [[drosera-rotundifolia]]**, **[[query-how-to-attract-native-bees-and-beneficial-insects]] and hoverflies. The flowers are self-compatible, which ensures seed set even in the absence of pollinators. Seed capsules contain numerous small seeds that are dispersed by wind and water.

The seasonal growth cycle of D. peltata is synchronized with the Mediterranean-type climate of southern Australia. Active growth occurs during the cool, moist winter and spring months, with dormancy initiated as soils dry in summer.

Plants typically emerge 2–4 weeks after the first significant autumn rains. The above-ground growth phase lasts approximately 4–6 months before senescence and return to dormancy.
