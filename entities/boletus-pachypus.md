# Boletus pachypus

## Description

Boletus pachypus is a bolete mushroom characterized by its exceptionally thick, stout stem (the name deriving from Greek "pachy" meaning thick, and "pus" meaning foot). The species was known to 19th-century mycologists as a distinctive bolete with robust morphology and a powerful, nutty flavor and aroma. Palmer noted that the aroma was potent enough to transfer to other foods stored nearby without direct contact — a remarkable demonstration of the species' volatile aromatic compounds.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Boletales
- **Family:** Boletaceae
- **Genus:** Boletus
- **Species:** B. pachypus

## Morphology

- **Cap:** Medium to large, typically brown to reddish-brown; surface may be smooth to slightly velvety
- **Pore surface:** Yellow, turning blue when bruised or cut
- **Stem:** Exceptionally thick and robust, the defining characteristic; gives the species its name
- **Flesh:** Firm, pale, staining blue on exposure to air
- **Aroma:** Strong, distinctive nutty fragrance permeating surrounding area
- **[[spore-print]]:** Olive-brown (typical of Boletus)

## Key Facts

- Characterized by a notably thick, robust stem — among the most stoutly built boletes
- Possesses a strong, distinctive nutty flavor that is immediately recognizable
- Aroma is powerful enough to permeate other foods stored in proximity without any physical contact
- Turns blue when cut or bruised, a characteristic shared with many bolete species
- Was not traditionally listed among esculents by English mycologists
- Despite this exclusion from European edibility lists, was consumed safely by experienced foragers including a Harvard professor
- Shows the blueing reaction typical of many Boletus species, which does not necessarily indicate toxicity
- The species name has been applied to various thick-stemmed boletes; exact modern taxonomic correspondence may vary

## Historical Context

Julius A. Palmer documented that B. pachypus communicated its strong nutty flavor to potatoes placed beside it in his refrigerator, without any direct contact between the mushrooms and the potatoes. This demonstrated the presence of potent volatile aromatic compounds in the species.

Palmer also recounted that a Harvard professor, "used to the exact definitions of German philosophy," mistook B. pachypus for an edible boletus and consumed all he could find, distributing them to friends — without requiring the services of the undertaker. This was despite the species never having been included among the esculents by English mycologists and its tendency to change to blue on being cut.

Palmer used this case to illustrate two important principles: that the blueing reaction alone should not be used as a test for toxicity, and that species excluded from European edibility lists may still be perfectly safe for consumption.

## Ecology and Distribution

- Likely mycorrhizal with hardwood trees
- Found in forested areas of North America
- Details of habitat preference are poorly documented due to taxonomic ambiguity
- Season: Late summer to autumn

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Aromatic compounds:** Demonstrates the presence of potent volatile flavor compounds in bolete species capable of permeating sealed environments
- **Blueing reaction:** Another example showing blue coloration upon cutting is not a reliable indicator of toxicity
- **Taxonomic ambiguity:** The name has been applied to various thick-stemmed boletes by different authors; exact modern correspondence may vary and requires further investigation
- **Cultural mycology:** Illustrates the significant gap between European mycological tradition and actual American foraging experience
- **Sensory identification:** The powerful nutty aroma serves as a secondary identification aid beyond morphological features
- **Cautionary note:** The species' identity remains uncertain; modern foragers should not rely on historical accounts alone for edibility assessment
