# Australorp (Chicken Breed)

## Description

The Australorp is a breed of domestic chicken (*Gallus gallus domesticus*) developed in Australia, referenced by [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-animal-systems-in-permaculture]] in *Permaculture: A Designer's Manual* (Chapter 3, Section 3.2) as an example of breed characteristics relevant to [[permaculture-design]]. Mollison uses the Australorp to illustrate how intrinsic breed traits — particularly size and weight — directly affect behavior, space requirements, and containment strategies.

## Classification

- **Type:** Chicken Breed (Livestock)
- **Species:** *Gallus gallus domesticus*
- **Origin:** Australia (developed from Black Orpington stock)
- **Category:** Heavy breed / Dual-purpose (meat and eggs)
- **Recognition:** Australian national breed

## Key Characteristics

### Physical Traits
- **Size:** Large, heavy-bodied bird
- **Color:** Predominantly black with green sheen
- **Feathering:** Thick, dense plumage
- **Weight:** Heavy — cocks 3.4-4.0 kg, hens 2.5-3.0 kg

### Behavioral Traits (Mollison's Observations)
- **Flight capability:** Cannot fly over a 1.2 meter (4 foot) fence due to heavy body weight
- **Heat tolerance:** Moderate — thick feathering provides cold tolerance but dark color absorbs heat
- **Foraging behavior:** Good foragers suitable for free-range systems
- **Temperament:** Generally docile and easy to manage

### Production
- **Eggs:** Excellent layer — set world records (364 eggs in 365 days)
- **Egg color:** Brown
- **Meat:** Good table bird with substantial body weight

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

### Containment Design
Mollison specifically notes that heavy breeds like the Australorp **cannot fly over a 1.2m fence**, while lighter breeds like Leghorns clear the same fence easily. This is critical [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-guild-design-in-permaculture]]formation:

- **Low fencing sufficient** — reduces infrastructure costs
- **No roofed enclosures needed** in many situations
- **Orchard integration** is straightforward — birds stay within designated areas
- **Rotational grazing cells** can use simple portable fencing

### System Integration
As a dual-purpose breed, the Australorp fits well in integrated systems:

- **Orchard [[bill-mollison-permaculture-a-designers-manual-wildfire-management-in-permaculture]]:** Foraging for insects, weed seeds, fallen fruit
- **Manure production:** Substantial nitrogen-rich manure for composting
- **Food waste recycling:** Consume kitchen scraps and garden waste
- **Glasshouse heating:** Body heat from confined birds can warm greenhouse environments

### Breed [[mollison-designers-mixed-orchard-design-species-selection]] Criteria
The Australorp exemplifies Mollison's design principle of matching breed characteristics to system needs:

- **Intrinsic characteristics** (color, form, weight) affect behavior and space requirements
- **Climate compatibility** — thick feathering suits cooler climates; dark coloration may stress in extreme heat
- **Functional placement** — knowing a breed's physical limits determines integration [[fukuoka-food-strategy-rice-seed-weapon-seed-war-food-ecosystem]]
