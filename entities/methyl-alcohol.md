# Methyl Alcohol (Methanol)

## Description

Methyl alcohol, also known as methanol or wood alcohol, is a simple alcohol compound (CH₃OH) used as a solvent in alkaloid extraction from [[amanita-muscaria-legal-alternative-psilocybin-mushrooms-europe]]. In the context of the Field Guide to the Psilocybin Mushroom, methanol is described as the primary solvent for extracting psilocybin from dried, powdered mushroom material. The extraction process involves soaking the mushroom powder in methanol for several days, followed by straining and evaporation to yield a concentrated alkaloid residue.

## Classification

- **Category**: Chemical solvent
- **Chemical formula**: CH₃OH
- **Common names**: Methanol, wood alcohol, methyl hydrate
- **Properties**: Colorless, volatile, flammable liquid
- **Toxicity**: Highly toxic if ingested

## Key Facts

- Methyl alcohol is used as an [[everclear-extraction-solvent]] for [[hplc-analysis-of-psilocybin-and-psilocin]] from dried mushroom material.
- The extraction process involves soaking dried, ground mushroom powder in methanol for several days.
- After soaking, the alcohol is strained off and the liquid evaporated in a shallow dish.
- The residue contains concentrated alkaloids and is scraped up for storage.
- Further refinement can be achieved by repeating the extraction with pentane as a secondary solvent.
- **Critical safety note**: All methyl alcohol solvent must be completely evaporated before any product is consumed, as methanol is highly toxic to humans.
- Methanol poisoning can cause blindness, organ failure, and death.
- Methyl alcohol is more toxic than ethyl alcohol and requires stricter handling precautions.
- The evaporation process must be conducted in a well-ventilated area due to both flammability and toxicity of fumes.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Methyl [[ethyl-alcohol-extraction]] represents one of the earliest documented methods for concentrating psilocybin from mushrooms, appearing in 1970s-era field guides. While effective as a solvent, methanol's toxicity makes it significantly more dangerous than ethanol-based [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-spore-extraction-methods]]. The technique demonstrates the historical interest in creating standardized psilocybin preparations from wild-harvested mushrooms. Modern extraction methodology generally favors food-grade ethanol (ethyl alcohol) over methanol for safety reasons, though methanol remains a common laboratory solvent for analytical extraction of alkaloids.

## Extraction Process (Historical)

1. Dry collected mushroom specimens completely.
2. Grind dried mushrooms into a fine powder.
3. Soak powder in methyl alcohol for several days.
4. Strain off the alcohol through a filter.
5. Evaporate the strained alcohol in a shallow dish.
6. Scrape up the alkaloid residue for storage at cool temperature.
7. Optionally refine by repeating the process with pentane solvent.

## Safety Warnings

- **Toxicity**: Methanol is metabolized to formaldehyde and formic acid in the body, causing metabolic acidosis, blindness, and potentially death.
- **Flammability**: Methanol burns with an invisible flame — extra caution around ignition sources.
- **Ventilation**: Always use in a well-ventilated area or under a fume hood.
- **Complete evaporation**: Ensure absolutely all solvent has evaporated before any product is handled for consumption.
- **Storage**: Store in a clearly labeled, sealed container away from heat and flames.

## Role in Alkaloid Extraction Chemistry

Methanol is a polar protic solvent that effectively dissolves
both [[psilocybin]] and [[psilocin]] from dried mushroom
tissue. Its small molecular size and polarity allow it to
penetrate the chitinous cell walls of fungal material,
liberating the indole alkaloids into solution. Multiple
extraction cycles with fresh solvent increase yield. The
process parallels the [[psilocybin-extraction-guide]] methodology
described in ethnobotanical field guides of the era.

## Modern Alternatives

Contemporary practitioners increasingly favor food-grade
ethanol over methanol for safety reasons. The
[[psilocybin-alcohol-extraction]] method using 190-200 proof
ethyl alcohol produces comparable yields without the severe
toxicity risks associated with methanol residue. Supercritical
CO2 extraction and ultrasonic-assisted extraction represent
further advances in laboratory settings, offering higher
purity and better control over the final product.
