## Natural Occurrence

Tabernanthine is found in the root bark of *Tabernanthe iboga* at concentrations typically 5-10 times lower than ibogaine. The total alkaloid content of dried iboga root bark is approximately 5-6%, with ibogaine constituting 50-60% of the total alkaloid fraction.

The compound has also been identified in:

- **Voacanga africana** bark and seeds — alongside voacangine and voacamine, as a minor alkaloid constituent
- **[[tabernaemontana-divaricata]]** (crape jasmine) — leaves and bark contain trace amounts
- **Tabernaemontana penduliflora** — root bark alkaloid fraction

The distribution pattern suggests that tabernanthine is a common intermediate in iboga-type alkaloid biosynthesis across the Apocynaceae family, present wherever the iboga pathway is active but typically in low concentrations relative to the pathway end-products.

### Extraction and Isolation

Tabernanthine is typically isolated from iboga root bark through acid-base extraction. Dried root bark is powdered and extracted with methanol or ethanol, followed by acidification and extraction with chloroform. Column chromatography on silica gel separates tabernanthine from ibogaine, voacangine, and other co-occurring alkaloids.

Modern analytical methods (HPLC, LC-MS) allow rapid identification and quantification of tabernanthine in plant material and preparations. These techniques have been applied to standardize iboga root bark preparations used in traditional medicine and to detect adulteration in commercial iboga products.

## Synthesis and Metabolism

Total synthesis of tabernanthine has been accomplished through several routes, primarily building upon the iboga skeleton from tryptamine precursors via biomimetic pathways.

These syntheses are of academic interest but have no practical application due to the availability of the natural product from iboga root bark. The most efficient synthetic routes proceed through a key Pictet-Spengler cyclization followed by polyene cyclization to construct the complex iboga ring system.

The metabolic fate of tabernanthine in mammals has not been fully elucidated. By analogy with ibogaine, expected metabolic pathways include O-demethylation, N-demethylation, and hydroxylation, primarily via hepatic CYP2D6 and CYP3A4 enzymes.

The compound is not commercially available as a research chemical or pharmaceutical agent. Its limited availability has constrained pharmacological research compared to ibogaine, which has been more extensively studied for its anti-addictive properties.

In animal studies, tabernanthine has shown preliminary evidence of reducing morphine and cocaine self-administration in rodent models, consistent with the broader iboga alkaloid class. However, these studies used crude plant extracts rather than isolated tabernanthine, making it difficult to attribute effects to this specific compound.
