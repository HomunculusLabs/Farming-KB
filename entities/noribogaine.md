# Noribogaine

**Noribogaine** (12-hydroxyibogamine) is the principal psychoactive metabolite of ibogaine, the indole alkaloid found in the root bark of [[tabernanthe-iboga]].
It is formed in vivo through O-demethylation of ibogaine by cytochrome P450 enzymes, primarily CYP2D6.
Noribogaine is pharmacologically significant because its long elimination half-life (approximately 28–49 hours) means it persists in the body well after ibogaine has been cleared, and it contributes substantially to ibogaine's prolonged anti-addictive effects.

## Chemical Properties

**IUPAC name:** (12S)-12-Hydroxy-6-ethyl-6,7,8,9,10,12,13,14-octahydro-2-methoxy-5H-6,9-epiminobenzo[h]pyrido[1,2-ij]isoquinoline

**Chemical formula:** C₁₉H₂₆N₂O₂

**Molecular weight:** 314.43 g/mol

**Structure type:** Indole alkaloid; iboga-type. Structurally identical to ibogaine except for the replacement of the 12-methoxy group with a hydroxyl group.

**Appearance:** White crystalline solid (as hydrochloride salt)

**Solubility:** Freely soluble in water as hydrochloride salt; soluble in ethanol and methanol; poorly soluble in nonpolar solvents.

**pKa:** Approximately 8.5 (tertiary amine), consistent with basic indole alkaloid pharmacokinetics.

The conversion from ibogaine to noribogaine is catalyzed primarily by CYP2D6, with contributions from CYP3A4 and CYP2C19.
Individuals with CYP2D6 poor metabolizer phenotypes show reduced noribogaine formation and may experience different clinical outcomes from ibogaine treatment.

## Pharmacology

Noribogaine has a complex receptor binding profile that differs importantly from the parent compound [[ibogaine]].

**Serotonergic system:** 5-HT₂A agonist (psychedelic effects, neuroplasticity); 5-HT₂C agonist (mood/anxiety modulation); serotonin reuptake inhibition (SRI, Ki ~3.8 µM, ~10-fold weaker than ibogaine).

**Opioid system:** μ-opioid receptor (MOR) agonist/partial agonist — a critical distinction from ibogaine, which shows minimal direct opioid activity.
This MOR agonism is considered the major contributor to noribogaine's ability to suppress opioid withdrawal.
κ-opioid receptor activity has also been reported.

**Other targets:** NMDA receptor antagonist (IC50 ~10 µM); sigma-2 receptor agonist; nicotinic α3β4 receptor antagonist (blocks nicotinic reward pathways); dopamine transporter inhibition at high concentrations.

The combination of MOR agonism, SRI, and NMDA antagonism creates a unique pharmacological signature that may be responsible for noribogaine's extended therapeutic window.

## Pharmacokinetics

**Formation:** Primarily via hepatic O-demethylation of ibogaine by CYP2D6, with contributions from CYP3A4 and CYP2C19.
Peak noribogaine concentrations occur 4–6 hours post-dose; often exceed ibogaine concentrations within 4 hours.

**Distribution:** Volume of distribution ~7 L/kg; crosses blood-brain barrier; accumulates in liver, kidney, and brain.

**Elimination:** Half-life 28–49 hours (vs. ibogaine's 4–7 hours); clearance ~0.5 L/h/kg; excreted renally as unchanged drug and glucuronide conjugates.
The long half-life is clinically significant: therapeutic concentrations persist 3–7 days after a single ibogaine dose.

**Drug interactions:** CYP2D6 inhibitors reduce formation; combination with serotonergic drugs carries serotonin syndrome risk.

## Effects

Noribogaine is not typically administered as a primary substance; its effects are experienced as the extended post-acute phase of ibogaine treatment.

**Subjective effects:** Prolonged sense of well-being and emotional stability lasting days to weeks; reduced drug craving and withdrawal symptoms; mild sedation; vivid dreaming.
No acute psychedelic intensity — this distinguishes noribogaine from the ibogaine experience itself.

## Addiction Treatment Applications

Noribogaine is of particular interest as the key mediator of ibogaine's anti-addictive properties.

**Opioid dependence:** Suppresses acute withdrawal for 3–7 days post-ibogaine; reduces craving; may facilitate transition to naltrexone.

**Stimulant dependence:** Animal studies show reduced cocaine self-administration; dopaminergic modulation may normalize reward pathway dysfunction.

**Pharmaceutical development:** Phase I clinical trials have evaluated noribogaine safety in healthy volunteers.
Development by DemeRx Inc. offers potential advantages: no acute psychedelic burden, no QT-prolongation risk at therapeutic doses, wider therapeutic index than ibogaine.

## Synthesis and Legal Status

**Synthesis:** Noribogaine is produced biologically through CYP2D6-mediated O-demethylation of ibogaine.
Chemical synthesis via BBr₃ or AlCl₃ demethylation of ibogaine; semi-synthesis from voacangine.
Available as research chemical and clinical trial material from specialized suppliers.

**Legal status:**
- **International:** Not scheduled under the UN Convention on Psychotropic Substances
- **United States:** Not scheduled; exists in a regulatory gray area as an ibogaine metabolite
- **EU/Australia:** Generally unscheduled in most jurisdictions
- **Brazil:** Follows ibogaine controls
The uncontrolled status contrasts with ibogaine's Schedule I (US) status, creating potential pharmaceutical development advantages.

## Safety Profile

Noribogaine appears to have a more favorable cardiac safety profile than ibogaine:
- Does not produce the significant QT-interval prolongation associated with ibogaine
- Lower affinity for hERG potassium channels compared to ibogaine
- Animal toxicology studies show wider therapeutic index

**Known risks:**
- Serotonin syndrome risk when combined with serotonergic medications
- Variable pharmacokinetics due to CYP2D6 genetic polymorphism
- Limited long-term safety data
- Hepatic metabolism concerns in patients with liver impairment
