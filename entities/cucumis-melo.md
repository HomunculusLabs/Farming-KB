## Overview

*Cucumis melo* is an annual trailing vine in the family Cucurbitaceae, widely
cultivated for its sweet, fleshy fruit. Commonly known as melon, the species
encompasses a diverse group of cultivars including cantaloupe, honeydew,
muskmelon, and casaba.

Melon is one of the most important global [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]] are the
leading producers.

The species originated in Africa or Southwest Asia and has been cultivated for
over 4,000 years. Its remarkable morphological diversity has produced numerous
distinct cultivar groups adapted to varied climates and culinary traditions.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

- Kingdom: Plantae; Order: Cucurbitales; Family: Cucurbitaceae
- Genus: *Cucumis*; Species: *C. melo* L., 1753

The genus *Cucumis* includes approximately 50 species, with *C. melo* and
*C. sativus* (cucumber) being the two most economically important.

The vine grows 1.5–3 m long with branched tendrils and shallow, fibrous roots.
Stems are angular, hairy, and typically prostrate unless trellised.

Leaves are simple, broadly ovate to kidney-shaped, 8–20 cm diameter, with 3–7
shallow lobes and coarsely dentate margins. Both surfaces are covered with
short, stiff hairs.

Flowers are monoecious, with separate male and female flowers on the same
plant. Male flowers appear first in axillary clusters; female flowers are
solitary, with an inferior ovary. Flowers are yellow, 1.5–3 cm diameter.

Fruit is a large pepo (modified berry), varying enormously by cultivar from
5 cm to over 30 cm in diameter. Shape ranges from spherical to oblong, with
skin that may be smooth, ribbed, netted (reticulated), or warty.

Flesh color varies from green to orange, salmon, or white. The central cavity
contains numerous seeds enclosed in a gelatinous placental tissue.

## Distribution and Habitat

## Cultivation

Melon is typically direct-seeded or transplanted after 2–3 weeks in nursery
trays. Seeds germinate in 3–10 days at soil temperatures above 20°C.

1.5–2.5 m between rows for bush types; wider spacing for vining types.
