# Usnea spp.

## Description

*Usnea* is a genus of fruticose (shrubby) lichens commonly known as old man's beard or beard lichens. These pendulous or erect lichens are characterized by their hair-like growth form, flexible central cord, and sensitivity to air pollution. Species are found worldwide, particularly in humid forested environments where they hang from tree branches and trunks.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Lecanoromycetes
- **Order:** Lecanorales
- **Family:** Parmeliaceae
- **Genus:** *Usnea*

## Key Facts

- Maximum atmospheric SO2 tolerance of approximately 30 mg/m³, making them moderately sensitive bioindicators of air quality (Richardson, 1988).
- Among the first lichen genera to disappear from areas with elevated sulfur dioxide levels.
- *[[usnea-hirta]]* has been shown to be less tolerant of heavy metal exposure than *Hypogymnia physoides* and *[[pseudevernia-furfuracea]]* in Finnish smelter studies (Fritze et al., 1989).
- Species in the genus accumulate heavy metals at levels significantly higher than mosses, attributable to the fungal mycelial component (Kosta-Rick et al., 2001).
- Traditionally used in [[herbal-medicine]] due to the presence of [[usnic-acid]], a lichen secondary metabolite with antimicrobial properties.
- The genus shows a hierarchy of ozone sensitivity: *Usnea yasudae* is moderately sensitive, ranking between more and less sensitive species (Hur and Kim, 2000).

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

*Usnea* species are among the most widely recognized and ecologically significant lichen genera. Their high sensitivity to air pollution, particularly SO2 and ozone, makes them premier bioindicator organisms. In mycology, they are important for studying the fungal–algal symbiotic relationship and the ecological physiology of stress responses. Usnic acid extracted from *Usnea* has pharmaceutical and antimicrobial applications.

## Ecological Role

- Primary bioindicator of atmospheric SO2 and ozone pollution
- Important component of forest canopy biodiversity in humid regions
- Provides nesting material for birds and habitat for microinvertebrates
- Accumulates heavy metals from atmospheric deposition
- Contributes to nutrient cycling through nitrogen fixation (in species with cyanobacterial photobionts)
