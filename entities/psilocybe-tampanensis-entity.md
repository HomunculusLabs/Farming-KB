# Psilocybe tampanensis

**Type:** Species (Fungi, Basidiomycota, [[agar]]icales)
**Category:** Psilocybin-containing mushroom / sclerotia producer
**Also known as:** magic truffles, Philosopher's Stones

## Overview
Psilocybe tampanensis is a rare psychedelic mushroom known primarily for
its ability to produce sclerotia (dense, truffle-like masses of mycelium)
in culture. Discovered by Stephen Pollock and [[gary-lincoff]] near Tampa,
Florida in 1977, it has only been collected in the wild twice despite
extensive searching. The species survives today entirely through
cultivated strains, and its sclerotia are commercially sold as "magic
truffles" or "Philosopher's Stones" in countries where they remain legal.

## Key Details

### Taxonomy
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Hymenogastraceae
- **Genus:** Psilocybe
- **Authority:** Guzman and Pollock

### Morphology (Mushroom)
- **Cap:** 1-2.4 cm broad, convex, expanding to plane or slightly
  umbilicate with age. Surface smooth, subviscid when moist, soon dry.
  Ochraceous brown to straw brown, hygrophanous, fading to light straw
  to yellowish gray with slight bluish tones.
- **Gills:** Adnexed, brownish to dark violet brown with paler edges.
- **Stem:** 20-60 mm long by 1-2 mm thick, equal to enlarging near base.
  Covered with fibrillose patches near apex, adorned with whitish
  mycelium at base, sometimes bluish toned. Yellowish brown to reddish
  brown. Flesh whitish to yellowish, bruising bluish.
- **Spores:** Purplish brown in deposit, subellipsoid in side view,
  subrhomboid in face view, 8-10 (12) by 6-8.8 um.
- **Basidia:** 4-spored.

### Morphology (Sclerotia)
- Dense, hard, potato-like masses of compacted mycelium.
- Form underground in response to environmental stress or nutrient
  depletion.
- Range from pea-sized to golf ball-sized.
- Light brown to golden in color, with a rough, irregular surface.

### Habitat and Distribution
- Only twice collected in the wild: near Tampa, Florida, and near
  Mississippi.
- Original habitat: sandy soils in subtropical environments.
- Fruiting season: fall in the southeastern United States.

### Chemistry
- Moderately potent. Gartz et al. (1994) found up to 0.68% psilocybin
  and 0.32% psilocin in sclerotia.
- Sclerotia retain potency well in storage.

## Relevance
P. tampanensis is uniquely important in mycology and psychedelic culture
as the source of commercially cultivated "magic truffles." Its extreme
rarity in the wild (only two confirmed collections) contrasts sharply
with its widespread availability through cultivation, making it a
remarkable case study in fungal conservation through artificial
propagation. The species was first described and cultivation methods
were published by Stamets and Chilton (1983). In the Netherlands, P.
tampanensis sclerotia were legally sold in smart shops for years before
the 2019 ban, contributing significantly to psychedelic research and
recreational use. Its sclerotia-producing ability, shared with
P. mexicana, makes it valuable for understanding fungal stress
responses and survival strategies.
