## Overview

Cynomorium songaricum, commonly known as suo yang (锁阳), is a holoparasitic
flowering plant in the monogeneric family Cynomoriaceae. It is one of the most
valued tonic herbs in Traditional Chinese Medicine and Mongolian folk medicine,
prized as a yang-tonifying remedy for kidney deficiency and sexual dysfunction.

The species is entirely parasitic on the roots of desert shrubs, primarily
species of Nitraria (Nitrariaceae). It produces no chlorophyll and is visible
above ground only during its flowering and fruiting period.

C. songaricum occupies a unique [[neurotropic-fungi]]-japan-east-asia-species-cultural-context, where it has been used medicinally for
over 1,200 years.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Clade:** Tracheophytes
- **Clade:** Angiosperms
- **Clade:** Eudicots
- **Order:** Saxifragales
- **Family:** Cynomoriaceae
- **Genus:** Cynomorium
- **Species:** C. songaricum
- **Authority:** Rupr. (1869)

The family Cynomoriaceae is monogeneric, containing only Cynomorium. A second
species, C. coccineum (Maltese mushroom), occurs in the Mediterranean region
and was historically used in European folk medicine.

The taxonomic placement of Cynomoriaceae has been debated, with various
classifications placing it near Balanophoraceae or Saxifragales. Current
molecular phylogenetics supports placement in Saxifragales.

## Morphology

The plant is a fleshy, cylindrical to clavate parasitic stem emerging from the
ground, measuring 5–20 cm long and 2–5 cm in diameter.

The above-ground portion is dark red to purplish-red, covered with small,
imbricate scale leaves. It is sometimes called "desert ginseng" due to its
resemblance to ginseng root.

The inflorescence is a dense, oblong spike at the apex of the stem, 3–10 cm
long, composed of numerous small flowers embedded in the axis. Individual
flowers are unisexual (plants are dioecious), with male and female flowers on
separate plants.

Male flowers have 1–2 stamens; female flowers have a superior ovary with a
short style. Flowering occurs from May to June.

The fruit is a small nutlet. The underground portion consists of a tuberous
body attached to host roots via haustoria.

## Distribution and Habitat

C. songaricum is distributed across arid and semi-arid regions of Central and
East Asia, including western China (Gansu, Xinjiang, Inner Mongolia, Ningxia,
Qinghai), Mongolia, and parts of Central Asia.

It grows in desert, semi-desert steppe, and sandy/gravelly habitats at
elevations of 250–3,100 m. The species requires extremely well-drained soils
and cannot tolerate waterlogged conditions.

The primary host plants are Nitraria tangutorum and N. sibirica (desert
buckthorn), both halophytic shrubs adapted to saline, arid soils. C.
songaricum may also parasitize [[tamarix]] and other desert shrubs.
