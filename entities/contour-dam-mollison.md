## Description

A contour dam is a shallow earth dam with a large surface area, constructed by building an earth wall along a contour line on relatively flat terrain. Unlike valley dams that block a watercourse, contour dams perch on knolls or gentle slopes where the land is least sloping. The dam wall follows the natural contour and then swings back to ground level, creating a broad, shallow impoundment. They are among the cheapest forms of water storage per volume captured.

## Classification

- **Category**: Water storage / Earthworks
- **Type**: Rolled earth dam, barrier dam along contour
- **Scale**: Small to medium (up to ~6 feet high wall)
- **Terrain**: Flat to gently sloping land, knolls, ridges
- **Cost**: Very cheap — maximum water per earth moved

## Construction

Contour dams are built on the least sloping sites available:

1. **Site selection**: Choose flattish areas, even up high on the landscape. The flatter the floor being flooded, the more water obtained per dollar spent.
2. **Earth wall**: Build a simple earth wall along the contour. The wall may be rougher than valley dams — some grass or rock inclusions are tolerable.
3. **Compaction**: Roll the wall down tightly with machinery. The wall need not be perfectly uniform.
4. **Diversion drains**: Run standard diversion drains to feed water into the dam from surrounding catchment.
5. **Height**: Typically up to 6 feet high, though this varies with site conditions.

## Key Facts

- **Very cheap per volume**: For the amount of earth moved, contour dams capture significantly more water than valley dams on sloping terrain.
- **Shallow and broad**: Large surface area with shallow depth makes them ideal for evaporation management and wildlife habitat.
- **Multiple functions**: Serve water storage, wildlife habitat, water table recharge, and fire protection simultaneously.
- **Leakage benefit**: Contour dams leak slightly, which significantly increases the water table in the surrounding area.
- **No flood risk**: Being low structures with large spillways, they safely overflow without structural risk.
- **Flexible siting**: Can be built on ridge points, open fields, or anywhere with adequate catchment — not restricted to valleys.

## Effects on Landscape

Contour dams produce several beneficial effects:

1. **Water table increase**: Leakage from contour dams raises the surrounding water table, improving conditions for downslope vegetation.
2. **Runoff reduction**: By intercepting and holding water, they decrease the rush-off of storm water, giving water far more time on the landscape.
3. **Wildlife habitat**: The shallow, broad profile creates excellent habitat for waterfowl, amphibians, and aquatic plants.
4. **Fire protection**: Water stored at multiple points across the landscape provides fire suppression resources.

## Relevance to Cultivation

- **Orchard irrigation**: Contour dams can gravity-feed irrigation to orchards and gardens below.
- **Aquaculture**: The shallow, warm water is suitable for fish production, water chestnuts, wild rice, and crayfish.
- **Pasture improvement**: Raised water table from dam leakage benefits downslope pastures without active irrigation.
- **Nutrient capture**: Low dams collect dissolved solids and nutrients from uphill runoff, acting as biological filters.
