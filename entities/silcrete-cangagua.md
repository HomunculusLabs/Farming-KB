## Description

Silcrete, also known as **cangagua** in South America, is a grey to red shiny hard layer that develops below some tropical forest soils. It forms when silica deposits cement soil particles together, creating a glassy, impermeable surface when exposed. The condition is also referred to as a silica-cemented non-wetting horizon.

## Classification

- **Category**: Geological / Soil formation
- **Type**: Silica-cemented concretion (hardpan)
- **Composition**: Silica (SiO2), earthy and brittle texture
- **Colour**: Grey to red, glassy when exposed
- **Related terms**: Cangagua, duricrust, silica pan
- **Occurrence**: Volcanic areas primarily; red-brown hardpans also found in semi-arid non-volcanic soils

## Key Facts

1. **Formation**: Silica dissolved in groundwater precipitates and cements soil particles into a hard, impermeable layer. Most common in volcanic regions.
2. **Depth**: In Ecuadorian tropical forests, cangagua lies approximately 3 m below the surface, following hill contours.
3. **Thickness**: Variable — from 10 cm to as much as 30 m (4 inches to 98 feet) in extreme cases.
4. **Deforestation consequence**: When forests overlaying silcrete are cleared, topsoil is rapidly lost, exposing the glassy, impermeable silcrete surface. This effectively destroys the land for agricultural use.
5. **Volcanic association**: True silcrete is found only in volcanic areas, though similar red-brown hardpans occur in many soil types in semi-arid regions.
6. **Irreversibility**: Once exposed, the silcrete surface is extremely difficult to rehabilitate for productive use.
7. **Non-wetting properties**: The silica-cemented surface is hydrophobic, making re-vegetation nearly impossible without extensive intervention.

## Management and Remediation

- **Prevention**: The primary management strategy is to avoid clearing forests where silcrete is known or suspected to exist below the surface.
- **Permanent forestry**: Where silcrete underlies tropical forests, the only sustainable land use is perpetual forestry for non-timber products (honey, fruits, medicines).
- **Mechanical breaking**: If exposure has already occurred, mechanical disruption combined with deep organic matter incorporation may partially restore productivity.
