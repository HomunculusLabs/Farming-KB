---# Ullucus tuberosus

**Ulluco**, **ulluku**, **melloco**, **olloc**, **rubas**

*Ullucus tuberosus* is a perennial herbaceous plant in the family Basellaceae native to the Andean highlands of Peru, Bolivia, Ecuador, and Colombia. It is one of the most important traditional root crops of the Andes, cultivated alongside potato, oca, and mashua as part of the Andean tuber complex. Ulluco produces small, brightly colored tubers ranging from yellow and orange to magenta, red, and purple, often with contrasting pigmented bands or spots. It is the single most cold-tolerant of all cultivated [[cannabis-controlled-pollination-seed-production]] is rare in cultivated varieties. Propagation is almost entirely vegetative, through tuber planting.

## Natural Range and Distribution

Ulluco is native to the Andean cordillera from southern Colombia to northern Argentina and Chile, cultivated primarily between 2,800 and 4,200 meters elevation. The center of diversity is in the Lake Titicaca basin, where the greatest morphological variety of tuber colors and shapes exists. It has been cultivated for at least 5,000–7,000 years, making it one of the oldest domesticated crops in the Americas.

It is virtually unknown outside the Andes, though small-scale cultivation has been introduced to New Zealand and parts of Europe and [[growing-root-vegetables-guide]]:

- **Carbohydrates**: 13–15% (primarily starch, with some fructans and mucilage)
- **Protein**: 1.5–2.0% — higher than potato on a fresh-weight basis
- **Vitamin C**: 20–50 mg/100g (variable by variety; some purple varieties extremely high)
- **Carotenoids**: Yellow-fleshed varieties contain significant β-carotene
- **Anthocyanins**: Red and purple varieties are rich in cyanidin and pelargonidin glycosides
- **Calcium**: 15–25 mg/100g
- **Iron**: 0.8–1.5 mg/100g
- **Fiber**: 1.5–2.5 g/100g
- **Mucilage**: Soluble polysaccharides contributing to its distinctive texture

The leaves are also nutritious, containing higher concentrations of vitamin C, vitamin A, and iron than the tubers.

## Culinary Uses

Ulluco is consumed in multiple forms throughout the Andes:

- **Fresh**: Boiled, steamed, or roasted — the crisp texture and mild, slightly sweet flavor are appreciated directly
- **Thawed (*ch'uño de ulluco*)**: Tubers are exposed to nightly frosts and daily sun for several days, then stomped to remove water, producing a dehydrated product that stores for years
- **Pickled (*ulluco en escabeche*)**: Popular in Ecuadorian and Colombian cuisine
- **Soups and stews**: Added to *chupe* and other traditional Andean preparations
- **Salads**: Fresh slices add color and crunch

The mucilaginous quality (similar to okra) is either appreciated as a thickening agent or, by some palates, considered undesirable. Selecting less mucilaginous varieties and avoiding overcooking reduces this characteristic.

## Cultivation

Ulluco is adapted to the extreme conditions of high-altitude Andean agriculture:

- **[[extending-growing-season]]**: 5–8 months from [[guzman-allen-gartz-africa-southern-hemisphere-underexplored-neurotropic-fungi]]). Plants are spaced 30–50 cm apart in rows 60–80 cm apart. No seed varieties exist in commercial cultivation — all propagation is clonal, maintaining named varieties selected over generations.
