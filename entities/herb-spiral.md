## Description

The Herb Spiral is a three-dimensional permaculture garden design evolved by Bill Mollison in 1978 as a kitchen-door planting system. It consists of an ascending spiral of earth built on a 2-metre-wide base, rising to approximately 1 to 1.3 metres high. The spiral ramp provides variable microclimates — sunny, dry sites at the top for oil-rich Mediterranean herbs, and moist, shaded sites at the base for green foliage herbs. All plants are accessible from the perimeter, and the entire structure can be irrigated with a single 2-metre sprinkler.

## Classification

- **Type**: Permaculture design pattern / garden structure
- **Origin**: Bill Mollison, 1978
- **Scale**: Small (2 m diameter base, 1–1.3 m height)
- **Category**: Three-dimensional earth construct, kitchen garden, microclimate management

## Key Facts

- Base diameter: 2 metres; height: 1 to 1.3 metres
- Spiral ramp and walls exceed 9 metres of planting space in a compact 2-metre footprint
- Requires only one 2-metre sprinkler for complete irrigation — significant water savings versus row planting
- Provides variable drainage: dry at top, moist at base
- Provides variable aspect: full sun on top and south-facing sides, shade on north-facing lower sections
- Accommodates all basic culinary herbs in a single structure
- Rare example of a three-dimensional earth construct at garden scale

## Planting Strategy

The spiral creates distinct microclimate zones along its gradient:

**Top / Sunny / Dry zones:**
- Thyme (*Thymus* spp.) — oil-rich, drought-tolerant
- Sage (*Salvia officinalis*) — Mediterranean, prefers dry conditions
- Rosemary (*Salvia rosmarinus*) — woody, sun-loving, well-drained

**Middle / Moderate zones:**
- Basil (*Ocimum basilicum*) — warm, moderate moisture
- Oregano (*Origanum vulgare*) — well-drained, sunny

**Base / Shaded / Moist zones:**
- Mint (*Mentha* spp.) — moisture-loving, can be invasive
- Parsley (*Petroselinum crispum*) — cool, moist conditions
- Chives (*Allium schoenoprasum*) — moderate moisture
- Coriander / Cilantro (*Coriandrum sativum*) — cool, moist, shaded

## Design Principles

The herb spiral applies several permaculture principles simultaneously:

1. **Edge maximisation**: The spiral form maximises the planting edge within a minimal footprint
2. **Microclimate creation**: Variable height, aspect, and drainage create multiple growing conditions in one structure
3. **Zone planning**: Placed at the kitchen door (Zone 0–1) for maximum accessibility of frequently-used herbs
4. **Energy efficiency**: Single water source irrigates the entire structure; compact form reduces path space
5. **Stacking functions**: The earth mound itself provides thermal mass, drainage variation, and vertical growing space

## Variations and Adaptations

- **Rooftop / balcony**: Adapted as stepped walls, wall shelves, window boxes, or stacked pots in earth mounds for above-ground kitchens
- **Larger spirals**: The principle scales up for other crops; circle-mulch-grow pits of 1.8 m for bananas, 1.8–3 m for coconuts
- **Material options**: Stone, brick, reclaimed materials for the retaining walls; filled with quality soil and compost

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

The herb spiral is one of the most widely-adopted permaculture design patterns because it demonstrates core principles in a compact, replicable form. It illustrates how three-dimensional thinking and pattern application can compress linear systems (a 9-metre herb row) into a compact, efficient form (a 2-metre spiral). The design reduces water use by approximately 60% compared to equivalent row planting, and makes all plants accessible without entering the garden bed.
