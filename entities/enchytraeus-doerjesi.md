# Enchytraeus doerjesi

## Description

Enchytraeus doerjesi, commonly known as the **pot worm**, is a small oligochaete worm found primarily in forest soils. Much smaller than the familiar garden earthworm, pot worms are only a few millimeters to a few centimeters in length. They are ecologically significant because they succeed and replace [[earthworms]] in acid forest soils, environments that earthworms typically shun. Pot worms are also well known in the aquarium hobby as a favorite live food for tropical fish.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Animalia
- **Phylum:** Annelida
- **Class:** Clitellata
- **Order:** Enchytraeida
- **Family:** Enchytraeidae
- **Genus:** Enchytraeus
- **Species:** E. doerjesi

## Key Facts

- **Common name:** Pot worm, grindiworm
- **Size:** A few millimeters to a few centimeters in length — much smaller than traditional garden earthworms
- **Habitat:** Forest soils, particularly acidic environments where earthworms cannot thrive
- **[[ecological-niche]]:** Fills the same functional role as earthworms (shredding, decomposition, [[aact-nitrogen-forms-microbial-nutrient-cycling-ingham]]) but in acid soil conditions
- **Aquarium trade:** Cultured and sold as live food for tropical fish
- **Family Enchytraeidae:** Contains over 600 species of microdrile oligochaetes found worldwide

## Ecological Role

### Acid Soil Specialists
Pot worms are a critical component of [[the-soil-food-web]] in forest ecosystems where low pH prevents earthworm establishment. They perform the same shredding and decomposition functions that earthworms serve in garden soils:
- Break down [[savory-soil-organic-matter-and-decomposition-dynamics]] leaf litter
- Create micro-tunnels that aerate soil
- Produce castings that enrich soil with nutrients
- Serve as prey for larger soil organisms including predatory nematodes and microarthropods

### Nutrient Cycling
Like earthworms, pot worms consume bacteria, fungi, [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]]. Their digestive processes convert locked-up nutrients into plant-available forms. In acid forest soils, they are the primary macrofaunal decomposers.

### Soil Food Web Position
- **Trophic level:** Primary decomposer / shredder
- **Food sources:** Bacteria, fungi, organic matter, protozoa
- **Predators:** Predatory nematodes, microarthropods, some ground beetles
- **Role:** Bridge between [[cannabis-cultivar-microbial-community-effects]] and higher [[ingham-soil-foodweb-trophic-levels-protozoa-nematodes]] in acidic soils

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

1. **Forest soil health indicator:** Presence of pot worms indicates a functioning [[soil-food-web]] in acidic forest environments
2. **Mycorrhizal support:** By aerating forest soils and cycling nutrients, pot worms create conditions favorable for mycorrhizal fungal colonization of tree roots
3. **Acidic cultivation systems:** In cultivation systems using acidic substrates (e.g., blueberries, rhododendrons, certain mushroom species), pot worms may play a role analogous to earthworms in neutral soils
4. **Composting:** Some Enchytraeus species are cultured for vermicomposting on a small scale, particularly in Europe
5. **Research organism:** E. doerjesi and related species (particularly E. albidus, the white worm) are used in soil ecotoxicology studies
