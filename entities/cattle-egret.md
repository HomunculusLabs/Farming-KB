## Description

The cattle egret (*Bubulcus ibis*) is a small white heron that has developed a commensal relationship with large grazing mammals, particularly cattle. The birds follow livestock to catch insects disturbed by the animals' movement through grass. Mollison cites the cattle egret as a textbook example of how evolving plant and animal systems create niches for new species — the egret has specifically adapted to exploit the foraging niche created by large herbivores.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Animalia
- **Phylum**: Chordata
- **Class**: Aves
- **Order**: Pelecaniformes
- **Family**: Ardeidae
- **Genus**: *Bubulcus*
- **Species**: *B. ibis*
- **Common names**: Cattle egret, buffalo egret, elephant bird

## Key Facts

- Originally native to parts of Africa, Asia, and southern Europe
- Underwent one of the fastest and most dramatic natural range expansions of any bird species
- First recorded in South America in the 1870s; reached North America in the 1940s and Australia by the 1940s–50s
- Feeds primarily on insects (grasshoppers, crickets, flies, beetles) and other small invertebrates flushed by livestock
- Often seen perched on the backs of cattle, also removing ticks and external parasites
- Colonial nester — breeds in large groups, often alongside other heron species
- Adult length: 46–56 cm; wingspan: 88–96 cm; weight: 270–512 g
- Non-breeding plumage is all white; breeding adults develop orange-buff plumes on head, breast, and back

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- Cattle egrets serve as biological pest control in pasture systems, reducing insect populations
- Their presence indicates healthy pasture ecosystems with diverse food webs
