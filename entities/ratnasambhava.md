# Ratnasambhava

## Description
Ratnasambhava is the Dhyani Buddha "born of a jewel," associated with the Wisdom of Equality and the element of earth. In *The Psychedelic Experience*, Leary identifies Ratnasambhava as the Peaceful Deity personifying **Vision 5: The Vibratory Waves of External Unity** — the emotionally tinged vision where the wave-nature of reality produces either ecstatic oneness with all energy or the terrifying "plastic doll" experience of lifelessness and isolation.

## Classification
- **Category:** Deity — Dhyani Buddha
- **Domain:** Tibetan Buddhism, Vajrayana Buddhism, Bardo Thodol
- **Tradition:** Mahayana / Vajrayana Buddhism

## Key Facts
- One of the Five Dhyani Buddhas, associated with the South direction
- Name means "Jewel-Born" or "Origin of Jewels"
- Embodies the Wisdom of Equality — seeing all beings as equal manifestations of the same energy
- Element: Earth — "carries and nourishes all beings with the equanimity and patience of a mother, in whose eyes all beings, borne by her, are equal" (Govinda)
- Color association: Yellow (associated with the jewel)
- Accompanied by the Divine Mother "She of the Buddha Eyes"
- Accompanied by Bodhisattvas: "womb of the sky," "All-good," and those holding incense and rosary

## Role in the Psychedelic Bardo Framework
Ratnasambhava represents **Vision 5: The Vibratory Waves of External Unity** (eyes open, emotional aspects). The positive expression is a profound sense of one-ness — all superficial differences (role, caste, status, sex, species, form) dissolve before "the ecstatic union of all in one." Communication becomes unnecessary because complete communion exists. The negative trap produces the "plastic doll" phenomena: other people appear as lifeless mannequins, the world seems a stage set, and the voyager feels dead and unable to feel.

## The Plastic Doll Phenomenon
This is one of the most disturbing negative visions in the Bardo framework. The voyager perceives all forms as artificial, mass-produced, and lifeless. Other people appear as "wooden, waxen, feelingless, cold, grotesque, maniacal, space-fiction monsters." This represents the failure to accept the dissolution of ego boundaries — the ego clings to separateness but in doing so renders everything dead.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology
The Ratnasambhava vision maps onto a common psilocybin phenomenon: the sense of profound connection with nature and all living things. The "plastic doll" counterpart resembles depersonalization/derealization, a known temporary effect of psychedelics. Understanding this archetype helps guides distinguish between genuine mystical unity and its pathological mirror, and provides language for reassuring subjects experiencing derealization.
