## Overview

**Nepenthes lowii** is a tropical pitcher plant endemic to the highland mountains of Borneo, found at elevations between 1,650 and 2,600 meters.

It is one of the most morphologically distinctive *Nepenthes* species, producing upper pitchers with a greatly reduced peristome and a reflexed, domed lid that exudes a sugary exudate.

The species is famous for its unusual mutualistic relationship with mountain tree shrews (*Tupaia montana*), which feed on the lid exudate and defecate into the pitcher, providing the plant with nitrogen.

This toilet-pitcher strategy is unique among carnivorous plants and represents one of the most striking examples of plant-vertebrate mutualism.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:** Plantae → Angiosperms → Ericales → Nepenthaceae → *Nepenthes* → *N. lowii*.

**Authority:** Hook.f. (1859). Named in honor of Hugh Low, a British colonial administrator and naturalist who collected extensively in Borneo.

The species is placed in the *Montanae* clade, closely related to *N. epiphytica*, *N. macrophylla*, and *N. rajah*.

*Nepenthes lowii* is a scrambling climber or epiphyte. Stems can reach 5–10 meters in length, often ascending into the canopy of mossy montane forest.

Lower pitchers are ovate to urceolate, 6–12 cm tall, with a well-developed peristome and wings. They are briefly produced on juvenile plants before the characteristic upper pitchers appear.

Upper pitchers are dramatically different: they are bulbous at the base and constricted near the mouth, forming a distinctive hourglass shape.

The lid is enormous and reflexed, arching backward over the pitcher opening. Its undersurface is covered with bristly hairs and secretes copious white, sugary exudate.

The peristome of upper pitchers is greatly reduced and inner-angled, creating a funnel-like opening. This morphology is adapted for receiving tree shrew feces rather than trapping insects.

Coloration varies from green with red speckling to entirely red or purple. Highland forms tend to be more heavily pigmented than those from lower elevations.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Nepenthes lowii* is restricted to several isolated mountain ranges in Borneo, including Mount Kinabalu, Mount Tambuyukon, Mount Trus Madi in Sabah, and Mount Mulu in Sarawak.

It has also been recorded from Mount Murud and several peaks in Kalimantan, though populations are highly fragmented across its range.

The species occupies upper montane mossy forest and subalpine scrub, typically above 1,800 meters. It frequently grows as an epiphyte on mossy tree trunks and branches.

Cloud forests with persistent fog and high humidity provide ideal conditions. Mean annual precipitation exceeds 3,000 mm at most known sites.

Temperatures at these elevations are consistently cool, with daytime highs of 15–22°C and nighttime lows of 8–14°C. Frost is rare but has been recorded on the highest peaks.

Soils are typically acidic, nutrient-poor, and derived from ultramafic or sandstone substrates. The species also grows terrestrially on moss-covered cliff faces and ridges.

## Ecology and Mutualism

The most remarkable aspect of *N. lowii* ecology is its mutualistic relationship with the Bornean mountain tree shrew (*Tupaia montana*).

The oversized, reflexed lid of the upper pitcher exudes a sugary substance rich in glucose and fructose, which the tree shrew feeds on while perched on the pitcher rim.

While feeding, the tree shrew defecates into the pitcher. The fecal pellets are rich in nitrogen, which the plant absorbs as its primary nutrient source.
