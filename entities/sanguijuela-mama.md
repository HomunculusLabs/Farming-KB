# Sanguijuela Mama

## Description
The sanguijuela mama (mother leech) is a colossal supernatural leech described in Amazonian folklore as approximately twelve meters long, with four antennae and no mouth. It inhabits isolated jungle lakes (cochas bravas) and kills by draining all the blood from its victims using electromagnetic waves emanating from its head.

## Classification
- **Type**: Mythological creature / cryptid
- **Origin**: Amazonian mestizo folklore
- **Domain**: Aquatic / lacustrine
- **Tradition**: General Amazonian folklore / vegetalismo

## Key Facts
- The sanguijuela mama is described as a giant leech approximately twelve meters in length.
- It has four antennae on its head but no mouth, feeding instead through electromagnetic waves.
- It inhabits cochas bravas, isolated lakes deep in the jungle where enormous beasts are said to live.
- The creature kills by drawing all the blood from victims' bodies using electromagnetic waves emanating from its head.
- In the story recounted by Amaringo, eight hunters camped near a lake. One man awoke at midnight under a full moon to see the sanguijuela mama attacking a companion in his hammock.
- The creature had the power to make everyone sleep deeply, similar to the yana-puma (black jaguar) and Sachamama.
- When the survivor screamed, no one woke. He fled to the forest and returned the next morning to find all his comrades dead, drained of blood.
- Amaringo initially dismissed this as a story but became convinced of the creature's existence after seeing it in an [[ayahuasca-vision-art-and-shipibo-tradition]].
- The sanguijuela mama is associated with the same class of sleep-inducing predators as the yana-puma and Sachamama.
- The story originates from Amaringo's grandfather, who lived near a brook called Quincha, a tributary of the Fanacha River.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology
- The sanguijuela mama embodies the Amazonian understanding that remote waterways harbor dangerous and unknown life forms.
- The creature's electromagnetic feeding mechanism parallels actual biological electromagnetic sensitivity in some aquatic organisms.
- Stories of the sanguijuela mama serve as cautionary tales about camping near isolated jungle lakes, reflecting practical ecological knowledge.
- The creature's existence in both folklore and [[amaringo-ayahuasca-visions-shipibo-iconography]] illustrates how Amazonian people validate mythological beings through visionary experience.

## Cultural Context
The sanguijuela mama appears in Vision 22 of [[ayahuasca-preparation-pablo-amaringo-ucayali-shamanism]]'s Ayahuasca Visions. Amaringo explicitly states that he initially considered the creature mythological but was convinced of its reality through direct ayahuasca experience, illustrating the role of the brew in validating traditional knowledge in Amazonian culture.
