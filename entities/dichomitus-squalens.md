## Taxonomy

- Kingdom: Fungi
- Phylum: Basidiomycota
- Class: Agaricomycetes
- Order: Polyporales
- Family: Polyporaceae
- Genus: Dichomitus
- Species: D. squalens (P. Karst.) D.A. Reid

The species was originally described as Polyporus squalens by Petter Karsten in 1881.

It was transferred to the genus Dichomitus by Derek Reid in 1965.

Synonyms include Polyporus squalens, Poria squalens, and Coriolellus squalens.

## Morphology

Basidiocarps are annual, effused-reflexed to pileate, forming semicircular or shelf-like brackets.

The upper surface is whitish to cream-colored, often developing concentric zones and a zonate appearance.

Surface texture is hairy to tomentose when young, becoming smoother with age.

The pore surface is white to pale cream, with pores that are angular to round, 2-4 per millimeter.

Context is white, tough, and corky, typically 2-5 mm thick.

Tubes are concolorous with the pore surface, up to 3 mm in depth.

D. squalens has a wide distribution across temperate and [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]], and Asia, with scattered records from Australasia.

The species primarily colonizes fallen logs, stumps, and dead branches of coniferous trees.

Preferred hosts include species of Pinus, Picea, Abies, and Larix.

Occasionally found on hardwood substrates, particularly Quercus and Betula.

Fruiting occurs from late summer through autumn, though in mild climates it may persist year-round.
