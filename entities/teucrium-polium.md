## Overview

Teucrium polium, commonly known as germander or golden germander, is a perennial herbaceous plant in the family Lamiaceae native to the Mediterranean region and western Asia.

It has a long history of use in traditional medicine across the Middle East, North Africa, and southern Europe. The species is valued for its essential oil and bioactive diterpenoid compounds.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

T. polium was described by Carl Linnaeus in 1753. It belongs to the subfamily Ajugoideae within Lamiaceae, distinct from the Nepetoideae subfamily that includes most aromatic herbs.

The plant forms a low, woody-based subshrub growing 10–40 cm tall. Stems are erect to ascending, densely white-tomentose, giving the plant a silvery-grey appearance.

Leaves are opposite, ovate to oblong, 1–3 cm long, with crenate to dentate margins. Both surfaces are covered in dense white pubescence.

Flowers are arranged in compact, axillary or terminal clusters. The calyx is tubular and two-lipped, approximately 5–7 mm long.

The corolla is white to pale yellow, 8–12 mm long, with the upper lip reduced or absent — a characteristic feature of the genus Teucrium. The lower lip has three lobes.

The absence of a prominent upper corolla lip distinguishes Teucrium from most other Lamiaceae genera, which typically have bilabiate flowers with both lips well-developed. The stamens are exserted, protruding well beyond the corolla.

Blooming occurs from May to July. The fruit consists of four nutlets, each approximately 2 mm long, brown to dark brown at maturity.

Pollination is primarily by small bees and flies. The flowers produce modest nectar rewards and are not major honey sources despite their abundance in some regions.

## Distribution and Habitat

T. polium is distributed across the Mediterranean basin, from Portugal and Morocco east to Turkey, Iran, and the Caucasus. It also occurs in parts of Central Asia and Pakistan.

The species inhabits dry, rocky slopes, garigue, scrubland, and limestone outcrops. It is a characteristic component of Mediterranean phrygana and garrigue communities.

It grows at elevations from near sea level to approximately 2,000 meters. The plant is highly drought-tolerant and thrives in full sun with minimal soil moisture.

The species is calcicole, preferring alkaline limestone substrates. It is often found growing in rock crevices and on stony ground where competition from taller vegetation is limited.

Several subspecies have been recognized, including subsp. polium (western Mediterranean) and subsp. capitatum (eastern Mediterranean and western Asia). These differ in inflorescence structure and [[antifungal-secondary-metabolites-coprophilous-fungi]], including diterpenes, flavonoids, phenolic acids, and essential oil components.

The most notable compounds are neo-clerodane diterpenoids, including teucvin, teucvidin, and poliumoside. These compounds are responsible for many of the species' pharmacological activities.

The essential oil is dominated by monoterpenes, particularly α-pinene, β-pinene, and sabinene. Sesquiterpenes and oxygenated compounds contribute to the oil's complex aroma.

Flavonoid glycosides, including luteolin and apigenin derivatives, have been isolated from aerial parts. Phenolic acids such as caffeic acid and rosmarinic acid are also present.

Total phenolic content varies considerably with geography, harvest time, and plant part. Aerial parts harvested during flowering typically contain the highest concentrations of [[cordyceps-cancer-bioactive-compounds]].

Volatile oil composition shows significant chemotype [[bloomfield-fungal-pathogens-and-parasites]].

However, several cases of hepatotoxicity have been associated with T. polium consumption, particularly with prolonged use. The hepatotoxic effects are attributed to specific neoclerodane diterpenoids.

Hepatotoxicity cases have been documented primarily in patients using T. polium as a weight-loss aid or diabetes treatment. Liver enzyme elevations typically resolve after discontinuation.

In severe cases, fulminant hepatic failure requiring transplantation has been reported. These [[blesching-cannabis-safety-adverse-effects-evidence]] have significantly reduced the commercial availability of T. polium supplements in Europe.

The species has been investigated for potential antidiabetic properties, with animal studies showing reductions in blood glucose levels. Human clinical data remains limited and inconclusive.

Anti-inflammatory effects have been attributed to inhibition of cyclooxygenase (COX) and lipoxygenase (LOX) enzyme pathways. The antioxidant activity is linked to total phenolic content.

In Jordanian and Palestinian traditional medicine, T. polium tea is used to treat kidney stones and urinary tract infections. In Morocco, it is consumed as a digestive aid and appetite stimulant.

Due to hepatotoxicity concerns, several European health authorities have issued warnings against internal use of Teucrium species. External applications (compresses, poultices) are considered lower risk.
