## Overview

Jealousy is a modern indica-dominant hybrid cannabis strain bred by Seed Junky Genetics, known for its potent relaxing effects and visually striking purple-hued buds.

The strain has gained significant popularity in the licensed cannabis market since its release, becoming a staple in dispensaries across the United States.

Jealousy is characterized by a complex aroma blending sweet dessert notes with earthy, peppery undertones.

With THC content regularly testing between 20–28%, it is considered a high-potency strain best suited for experienced consumers.

## Lineage

Jealousy was created by crossing **Sherbet Bx1** with a select cut of **Gelato 41**.

The Sherbet lineage contributes deeply relaxing body effects and a complex sweet-and-funky aroma profile.

Gelato 41 adds potency, dense bud structure, and the characteristic dessert-like terpene expression.

Both parent strains trace back to the influential Girl Scout Cookies (GSC) genetic line through Sunset Sherbet and Thin Mint Cookies.

This GSC-dominant pedigree places Jealousy firmly within the "Cookies family" of modern West Coast cannabis genetics.

The strain is estimated at approximately 70% indica / 30% sativa based on its growth characteristics and effects profile.

## Appearance

Jealousy produces medium to large-sized flowers with a dense, chunky structure typical of indica-dominant cultivars.

The buds are notable for their coloring, often displaying deep purple and violet hues mixed with forest green.

Orange to rust-colored pistils weave through the calyxes, providing striking visual contrast.

A thick coating of milky-white trichomes covers the flower surface, giving buds a frosted, icy appearance.

The density and resin production make Jealousy popular for hash and concentrate extraction.

## Aroma and Flavor

The aroma of Jealousy is complex and layered, dominated by sweet, creamy notes reminiscent of its Sherbet parentage.

Secondary notes include earthy, peppery, and slightly funky undertones contributed by the Gelato lineage.

Some phenotypes express a distinct grape or berry sweetness, particularly when cured properly.

The flavor profile follows the aroma closely, with a smooth, creamy smoke carrying sweet dessert-like qualities on the inhale.

The exhale often reveals more earthy and peppery notes with a subtle menthol coolness.

## Terpene Profile

- **Caryophyllene:** Dominant terpene, contributing peppery, spicy notes and potential anti-inflammatory effects.
- **Limonene:** Secondary terpene, adding citrus sweetness and mood-elevating properties.
- **Myrcene:** Present at moderate levels, enhancing sedative and relaxing body effects.
- **Linalool:** Contributes floral, lavender-like undertones and calming effects.

Minor terpenes may include humulene, pinene, and trace amounts of ocimene depending on the specific phenotype.

## Cannabinoid Profile

- **THC:** 20–28% (common), with select phenotypes testing above 30%.
- **CBD:** Typically below 0.1%, classifying it as a THC-dominant cultivar.
- **Minor cannabinoids:** Varying levels of CBG and CBC depending on cultivation conditions.

The high THC content makes Jealousy suitable primarily for consumers with established cannabis tolerance.

## Effects

The onset of effects is moderately fast, beginning with a cerebral euphoria that lifts mood and promotes relaxation.

As the high progresses, a deep physical body stone sets in, often described as heavy and enveloping.

Common effects include deep relaxation, increased appetite (munchies), and a general sense of contentment.

Many users report Jealousy is effective for stress relief, anxiety reduction, and managing mild to moderate pain.

At higher doses, the sedative effects become pronounced, making it well-suited for evening or nighttime use.

## Cultivation

Jealousy can be grown both indoors and outdoors, with indoor cultivation generally producing higher quality flowers.

Flowering time is approximately 8–9 weeks when grown indoors from clones or feminized seeds.

The strain responds well to low-stress training (LST) and scrog methods to maximize light exposure and yield.

Indoor yields average 350–450 g/m², while outdoor plants can produce 400–600 g each when conditions are favorable.

The plants are moderately resistant to common pests and molds but benefit from good airflow management due to dense bud structure.

The purple coloration is enhanced by cooler nighttime temperatures during the final weeks of flowering.

## Notable Phenotypes and Derivatives

Jealousy has become a parent strain in its own right, used in several crosses since its release.

**Jealousy #1** and **Jealousy #3** are select cuts that have been distributed by Seed Junky as breeding stock.

Several Jealousy crosses have emerged in the market, combining its potency with other desirable flavor profiles.
