# Paullinia cupana

Paullinia cupana, commonly known as guaraná, is a climbing shrub in the
family Sapindaceae, native to the Amazon basin of Brazil. It is renowned
for its seeds, which contain the highest natural concentration of
[[theobromine]], theophylline, and a unique profile of
tannins and saponins that modify caffeine absorption, producing a
smoother, more sustained stimulant effect compared to coffee. Modern
research supports cognitive-enhancing, fatigue-reducing, and potential
weight-management properties.

## Taxonomy and Naming

- **Kingdom:** Plantae | **Division:** Magnoliophyta
- **Class:** Magnoliopsida | **Order:** Sapindales
- **Family:** Sapindaceae | **Genus:** Paullinia
- **Species:** P. cupana Kunth (var. sorbilis) / P. cupana var. typica
- **Common names:** guaraná, Brazilian cocoa, warana

Two varieties are recognized: P. cupana var. sorbilis (cultivated) and
P. cupana var. typica (wild, less important economically). The name
"guaraná" derives from the Tupi-Guarani "warana," meaning "fruit like
the eyes of the people" — the red aril partially opens to reveal a dark
seed with a white hilum resembling an eye.

## Description

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Habit | Woody climbing vine/shrub, 2–12 m long |
| Stem | Tendril-bearing, twining, woody at base |
| Leaves | Compound, 5-foliate (digitate), leaflets 15–30 cm |
| Leaflets | Oblong-lanceolate, serrate, glossy dark green |
| Inflorescence | Axillary panicles, 10–30 cm long |
| Flowers | Small, yellow-white, dioecious (separate male/female) |
| Fruit | Capsule, trilocular, 2.5–3.5 cm, bright red when ripe |
| Seed | Dark brown to black, 8–10 mm, with white aril "eye" |
| Root system | Deep taproot with extensive lateral roots |

## Distribution and Habitat

- **Native range:** Amazon basin, primarily Madeira and Tapajós river
  basins in Brazil. Also found in Venezuela, Colombia, Peru, Guianas.
- **Cultivation:** Predominantly Amazonas state, Brazil (Maués
  region), with increasing production in Bahia and other tropical
  countries.
- **Habitat:** Tropical lowland rainforest understory, 0–500 m.
  Prefers rich alluvial soils along riverbanks with partial shade.
- **Ecology:** Climbs canopy trees using tendrils. Dioecious
  reproduction requires both sexes. Pollinated by bees, butterflies.
  Seeds dispersed by birds and mammals.
- **Climate:** 2000–3000 mm rainfall, 24–28°C, 80–90% humidity.
  Intolerant of frost and drought.

## Chemistry and Active Compounds

### Xanthine Alkaloids

| Compound | Concentration (% dry weight) | Activity |
|----------|---------------------------|----------|
| Caffeine | 2.5–7.0% | CNS stimulant, adenosine antagonist |
| Theobromine | 0.02–0.07% | Mild stimulant, vasodilator |
| Theophylline | Trace | Bronchodilator |
| Theacrine | Trace | Novel purine alkaloid, anxiolytic |

Guaraná's caffeine content is the highest of any known plant, 2–5×
that of coffee beans, making it the most potent natural caffeine source.

### Non-Alkaloid Compounds

- **Tannins:** High polyphenol content (8–12%), primarily condensed
  tannins that slow caffeine absorption, producing the characteristic
  sustained release profile.
- **Saponins:** Triterpenoid saponins with immunomodulatory potential.
- **Flavonoids:** Catechins, epicatechins, proanthocyanidin oligomers.
- **Minerals:** Calcium, potassium, magnesium, phosphorus.
