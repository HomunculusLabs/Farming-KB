## Description

An abort (spelled "abhort" in [[pf-tek]] documentation) is an immature mushroom fruiting body that ceases growing, develops convoluted caps and gnarly stems, and becomes stunted. In PF TEK cultivation of *Psilocybe cubensis*, aborts are of particular significance because they are among the most potent specimens in terms of psychoactive alkaloid content — "even more powerful in magic than the stately beauties that will soon dominate the cake." Over half of the initial primordia that form will abort during the first flush.

## Classification

- **Category:** Developmental anomaly / growth stage
- **Type:** Arrested [[fruiting-body-development]]
- **Organism:** *Psilocybe cubensis* (PF TEK context)
- **Appearance:** Convoluted caps, gnarly stems, stunted growth
- **Potency:** Significantly higher than mature specimens

## Key Facts

- Over half of the small primordia that first form will abort (cease growing, convolute, and deform).
- Aborts are characterized by convoluted caps, gnarled stems, and stunted growth.
- Despite their unappealing appearance, aborts are "primo in magic alkaloids."
- They are described as "even more powerful in magic than the stately beauties that will soon dominate the cake."
- Tiny "baby mushroom" aborts are likewise highly potent.
- Blobs of fungus with little or no cap are another form of mutant growth that is also good for harvesting.
- Aborts should be harvested before their heads turn black.
- A pointed knife blade is recommended for removing high-potency aborts and primordia from the cake.

## Types of Abnormal Fruiting Bodies

The PF TEK identifies several categories of non-standard fruiting bodies:

1. **Aborts (abhorts):** Convuluted caps, gnarly stems, stunted growth. Highest potency. Harvest before heads turn black.
2. **Baby mushroom aborts:** Tiny aborted specimens. Also highly potent.
3. **Mutant blobs:** Fungal masses with little or no cap. Good for harvesting.
4. **Normal sporocarps:** Perfect specimens — the standard mature mushrooms.

## Harvest Protocol

- Monitor the cake closely once primordia begin forming.
- Identify aborts as soon as it can be determined they are not developing into normal specimens.
- Pick aborts before their heads turn black — blackening indicates advanced decomposition.
- Use a pointed knife blade for precise removal without damaging adjacent growth.
- Collect aborts from multiple cakes — "they seem tiny, but several cakes will produce quite a pile of these little ones (several dried grams!)."
- Several dried grams of aborts can be harvested from multiple cakes.

## Potency Comparison

The PF TEK establishes a clear potency hierarchy:

| Specimen Type | Relative Potency | Description |
|---------------|-----------------|-------------|
| Young primordia (aborted) | Highest | Picked before heads turn black |
| Young aborts | Very high | Stunted but healthy, pre-blackening |
| Baby mushroom aborts | High | Tiny aborted specimens |
| Mutant blobs | High | Fungal masses, little/no cap |
| Young normal mushrooms | Moderate-high | Unbroken veils, conical caps |
| Mature mushrooms | Moderate-low | Open caps, dark gills, sporulating |

## Why Are Aborts More Potent?

The PF TEK explains the potency differential through several factors:

- "It has been scientifically proven that the small immature specimens are significantly more potent than the larger mature specimens."
- As the mushroom cap expands like an umbrella, the mushroom's size increases but potency per unit weight decreases.
- The active compounds (psilocybin and psilocin) are concentrated in the early developmental stages.
- Pre-sporulation specimens retain maximum alkaloid content.
- This potency pattern is "probably true for all other magic mushroom species — including wild specimens."

## Identification Guidelines

Healthy aborts suitable for harvest:
- Pure white mycelial base, no discoloration
- No colored contamination
- Flesh is firm, not mushy
- Heads have not yet turned black

Aborts to discard:
- Heads have turned black (advanced decomposition)
- Any signs of colored contamination (pastels to black)
- Soft, mushy texture indicating bacterial rot
- Foul odor

## Relevance to Cultivation

Understanding aborts is important for cultivators because:

- **Yield optimization:** Harvesting aborts recovers significant alkaloid content that would otherwise be lost.
- **Quality over quantity:** "By harvesting only the young and abhortive specimens, the yield will be significantly lower, but the quality will be excellent and the potency can be similar to P. semilanceata."
- **Potential equal to Liberty Caps:** With proper harvest timing, *P. cubensis* aborts can achieve potency comparable to *Psilocybe semilanceata* (approximately 1% psilocybin content).
- **Environmental indicators:** Excessive aborting may indicate low humidity, temperature stress, or other environmental issues in the terrarium.
- **Deformed aborts** can indicate low humidity in the fruiting chamber.
