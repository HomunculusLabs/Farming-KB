## Description

Designer compost, also known as bokashi in Japan, is an enhanced compost product that goes beyond traditional decomposition by incorporating [[beneficial-indigenous-microorganisms-bim]] (BIM), bionutrients, and tailored nutrient profiles. The term "designer" reflects the ability to customize the compost's nutrient composition for specific agricultural goals—emphasizing potassium for flowering, nitrogen for vegetative growth, or other specific nutrient targets.

## Classification

- **Category**: Fermented soil amendment / enhanced compost
- **Origin**: Japan (bokashi), adapted in Korea and Philippines
- **System**: [[korean-natural-farming]], Japanese Natural Farming
- **Process**: [[jadam-anaerobic-fermentation]] with microbial inoculation

## Key Facts

- Designer compost is bokashi—fermented organic matter naturally fortified with macro and micronutrients, bionutrients, and biodiversed beneficial indigenous microorganisms.
- The basic formulation mirrors animal feed ratios: 80% carbon source (carbohydrate), 17% nitrogen (protein), 3% trace elements (vitamin/mineral).
- Philippine recipe: rice bran (10 kg), copra meal (20 kg), coco peat (20 kg), chicken manure (30 kg), charcoal dust (20 kg), BIM (1 liter), molasses (1 kg), bionutrient (1 liter).
- US substitutes: wheat bran for rice bran, corn/soybean meal for copra meal, peat moss/sawdust for coco peat, any animal manure for chicken manure.
- Charcoal dust provides carbon and serves as a microbial substrate for beneficial organism proliferation.
- Bionutrient composition can be customized: high-potassium extracts for flowering emphasis, high-nitrogen (fish emulsion) for vegetative growth emphasis.
- Rock dusts can be added and fermented for mineral enrichment.
- The finished product can also be brewed into compost tea for liquid application.
- Chicken manure is preferred for its potent macronutrient profile (N-P-K-Ca) and micronutrient content.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- Designer compost preparation parallels [[fungicide-treated-seed-hazard-mushroom-substrate-preparation]]: organic base materials are inoculated with beneficial organisms and fermented/conditioned.
- The anaerobic fermentation process involves lactic acid bacteria, yeasts, actinomycetes, and fermenting fungi—all relevant to mycological study.
- Charcoal (biochar) as a microbial substrate mirrors its use in [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]] moisture retention and microorganism habitat.
- Customizable nutrient profiles allow cultivators to create specialized soil conditions for different crops or mushroom species.
- The principle of inoculating substrate with cultured microorganisms is fundamental to both composting and mushroom cultivation.
