# Typha (Cattail / Bulrush)

## Description

Typha is a genus of marginal aquatic perennial plants commonly known as
cattails (North America) or bulrushes (some regions, though this name also
applies to unrelated [[permaculture]]. [[permaculture-design]].

### Permaculture Design Roles

- **Water filtration:** Root zones absorb excess nitrogen, phosphorus,
  and contaminants from greywater and animal runoff
- **Erosion control:** Dense root mats stabilise pond banks and swale edges
- **Habitat creation:** Shelter and nesting sites for waterfowl and frogs
- **Biomass production:** Rapid growth yields large quantities of mulch
- **Pig wallow remediation:** Natural succession plant for rested wallows
- **Fibre and craft material:** Leaves harvested for basketry and thatch

### Cultivation Notes

- Plant rhizomes 10-15 cm deep in saturated soil or shallow water (0-30 cm)
- Prefers full sun; tolerates partial shade with reduced vigour
- Thrives in standing water, muddy margins, and seasonally flooded areas
- Extremely low maintenance once established
- Can become invasive in small ponds—manage by rhizome barriers or regular
  harvesting
- Propagates easily from rhizome division or seed
- Hardy across most temperate and subtropical climates

### Companion Planting

In designed wetlands, Typha pairs well with Sagittaria (duck potato),
water chestnut, watercress, and other marginal aquatics. In permaculture
animal systems, cattail ponds serve ducks and geese that forage on the
invertebrate life the plants support.

## Summary

Typha is a powerhouse wetland genus that Mollison integrates into
permaculture animal systems through its placement in rested pig wallows.
Beyond this specific application, cattails offer extraordinary food
productivity, water purification, habitat creation, and material yields,
making them one of the most valuable plants in temperate and subtropical
permaculture water design.
