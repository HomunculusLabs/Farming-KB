## Description

The **Von Kármán trail** (or Von Kármán vortex street) is a repeating pattern
of swirling vortices caused by the unsteady separation of flow around blunt
bodies. In permaculture, Mollison identifies this as a fundamental **natural
pattern** that appears across multiple scales and substrates — from tidal flows
around islands to lava flows, estuary currents, and even the imprint of leaves
on one another. Understanding this pattern informs the design of water
harvesting, airflow, and energy systems.




## Classification

- **Category**: Pattern — Fluid dynamics / Natural pattern
- **Domain**: Physics, Geomorphology, Permaculture design
- **Named after**: Theodore von Kármán (Hungarian-American physicist)
- **Scale**: Universal — micro to macro




## Key Facts

- A **vortex street** forms when fluid flows past a blunt obstacle, creating
  alternating circular vortices downstream.
- Named after **Theodore von Kármán** (1881–1963), who described the
  mathematical conditions under which these vortex patterns form.
- Mollison documents Von Kármán trails in multiple natural settings:
  - **Estuaries** (Victoria, Australia) — flow patterns in tidal waterways
  - **Lava flows** — patterns frozen in volcanic rock
  - **Tidal narrows** (Deception Pass, Washington, USA) — flow between Whidbey
    and Orcas Islands
  - **Agave leaves** — the imprint of adjacent leaves reveals Von Kármán-like
    patterns of decreasing magnitude
- The pattern shows **increasing (or decreasing) magnitudes** of flow along the
  trail, with each successive vortex being larger or smaller than the previous.
- Relevant to permaculture design for understanding how **water, air, and
  energy** flow around obstacles in designed systems.




## Occurrences in Nature

1. **Water flow** around rocks, islands, and headlands
2. **Air currents** around buildings, trees, and terrain features
3. **Lava flow** patterns preserved in volcanic rock
4. **Leaf arrangement** imprints (e.g., agave rosettes)
5. **Cloud streets** visible from aerial observation




## Relevance to Cultivation / Mycology

- Understanding Von Kármán patterns informs **mushroom growing room design** —
  airflow around obstacles (racks, bags, columns) creates turbulence that
  affects humidity distribution and fresh air exchange.
- In **outdoor cultivation**, placement of mushroom logs or beds relative to
  wind flow obstacles can be optimised using vortex pattern understanding.
- **Swale and channel design** benefits from knowledge of how water will flow
  around check dams, rocks, and vegetation, ensuring even moisture distribution
  for fungal habitat.
- The alternating vortex pattern mirrors the **pulsing nature of biological
  systems** — mycelial growth fronts exhibit similar oscillating patterns at
  the microscopic scale.
