## Eucalyptus cornuta

## Description

Eucalyptus cornuta, commonly known as **yate** or **horned mallee**, is a medium-sized tree or mallee native to southwestern Western Australia.
It is named for its distinctive horn-shaped flower buds, with *cornuta* meaning horned.
The species produces hard, durable timber and is valued for its ornamental qualities, including showy yellow-green flowers.
In dry Mediterranean-climate plantings, it offers a combination of visual interest, nectar, shelter, and practical wood.
Its unusual buds make it easy to recognize compared with many more typical eucalypts.

## Classification

- **Family:** Myrtaceae
- **Genus:** Eucalyptus
- **Species:** E. cornuta
- **Common names:** Yate, horned mallee
- **Plant type:** Small to medium evergreen tree or mallee
- **Climate fit:** Mediterranean and near-coastal dryland regions
- **Primary yields:** Timber, nectar, ornamental flowers, shelter

## Key Facts

- Medium tree or mallee, typically 5–15 m tall
- Distinctive long, horn-shaped flower buds in clusters
- Smooth bark, pale grey to white
- Native to the southern coastal and near-coastal areas of Western Australia
- Produces hard, durable timber
- Flowers are yellow-green, appearing in large showy clusters
- Adapted to Mediterranean climate conditions
- Smaller stature than many timber eucalypts makes it easier to place in mixed plantings

## Cultivation Notes

- Prefers well-drained soils including sandy loams and gravels
- Suited to Mediterranean climates with 400–700 mm annual rainfall
- Moderately drought-tolerant
- Light frost tolerance
- Relatively fast-growing
- Best propagated from seed
- Responds well to pruning and shaping
- Avoid persistently wet or poorly drained soils
- Establish during the cool wet season where summers are dry
- Protect young plants from grazing and wind scorch until rooted
- Space according to final use: closer for shelter, wider for ornamental form or timber stems

## Design Considerations

Yate is especially useful where the design calls for a smaller eucalypt with both productive and aesthetic value.
Its horned buds and clustered flowers give it a distinctive presence near paths, field edges, and shelterbelts.
Because it is adapted to coastal and near-coastal Western Australian conditions, it should be matched to similar climates rather than treated as a universal eucalypt.
The tree can be combined with shrubs and groundcovers to create a layered windbreak that slows wind without creating turbulence.
In erosion-prone sandy or gravelly sites, its roots can help hold soil while the canopy reduces drying winds.
The species also supports pollinator-oriented plantings connected to [[fukuoka-phyllotaxy-natural-branching-law]] habit and flower display.
Timber harvest is usually small-scale, focused on posts, poles, or durable construction pieces.
Flowers should be considered a yield in their own right where bees are present.
In mixed plantings, monitor competition with nearby crops because even smaller eucalypts can compete strongly for water.
