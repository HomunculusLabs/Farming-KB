# Eucalyptus cornuta

## Description

Eucalyptus cornuta, commonly known as **yate** or **horned mallee**, is a medium-sized tree or mallee native to southwestern Western Australia. It is named for its distinctive horn-shaped flower buds (cornuta = horned). The species produces hard, durable timber and is valued for its ornamental qualities, including showy yellow-green flowers.

## Classification

- **Family:** Myrtaceae
- **Genus:** Eucalyptus
- **Species:** E. cornuta
- **Common names:** Yate, horned mallee

## Key Facts

- Medium tree or mallee, typically 5–15 m tall
- Distinctive long, horn-shaped flower buds in clusters
- Smooth bark, pale grey to white
- Native to the southern coastal and near-coastal areas of Western Australia
- Produces hard, durable timber
- Flowers are yellow-green, appearing in large showy clusters
- Adapted to Mediterranean climate conditions

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

- **Timber:** Hard, durable timber suitable for fence posts and construction (pages 22, 93, 116)
- **Ornamental:** Showy flowers and interesting bud shape make it a desirable garden specimen
- **Honey:** Produces nectar for [[faires-beekeeping-and-honey-production]] (pages 22, 93, 116)
- **Shelter:** Suitable for windbreaks and shelterbelts in Mediterranean climates
- **[[comparison-vetiver-grass-vs-bamboo-for-erosion-control]]:** Useful for stabilising coastal and near-coastal soils

## Cultivation Notes

- Prefers well-drained soils including sandy loams and gravels
- Suited to Mediterranean climates with 400–700 mm annual rainfall
- Moderately drought-tolerant
- Light frost tolerance
- Relatively fast-growing
- Best propagated from seed
- Responds well to pruning and shaping
