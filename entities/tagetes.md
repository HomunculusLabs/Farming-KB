# Tagetes (Marigold)

## Description

Tagetes is a genus of annual and perennial herbaceous plants in the aster family (Asteraceae), commonly known as marigolds. In permaculture and organic cultivation systems, Tagetes species are among the most important companion plants, valued for their ability to suppress nematodes, repel pests, attract beneficial insects, and provide ornamental value in food-producing gardens.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Family**: Asteraceae
- **Subfamily**: Asteroideae
- **Genus**: Tagetes
- **Number of species**: Approximately 50 species
- **Common names**: Marigold, African marigold, French marigold, Mexican marigold
- **Growth habit**: Annual herbs (most cultivated species), some perennials

## Key Facts

- **Nematode suppression**: Tagetes roots release thiophene compounds (particularly alpha-terthienyl) that are toxic to root-knot nematodes (Meloidogyne spp.) and other [[soil-nematodes]], making them one of the most effective biological nematode controls available
- **[[keyhole-bed]] designs
- Tolerant of the high temperatures and light conditions found in desert gardens
- Requires minimal water once established

### Soil and Root Health

The nematode-suppressing properties of Tagetes operate through:

- Release of nematicidal thiophene compounds from roots into surrounding soil
- Acting as a "trap crop" — nematodes enter Tagetes roots but are unable to reproduce
- Accumulating nematicidal compounds in soil over time with repeated plantings
- Particularly effective against root-knot nematodes (Meloidogyne spp.) and lesion nematodes

## Species of Cultivation Interest

- **Tagetes erecta** (African marigold): Tall-growing, large flowers; excellent for border plantings and cut flowers
- **Tagetes patula** (French marigold): Compact habit, ideal for interplanting among vegetables; strongest nematode suppression
- **[[keyhole-bed-design]]

## Integration in Keyhole Bed Systems

Mollison's keyhole bed design specifically incorporates Tagetes as an interplant. Keyhole beds are raised, densely planted beds designed for maximum access with minimum path-to-bed ratio. In these systems:

- 20-50 plants arranged in 3 rows deep (1.5 m wide)
- Tagetes interplanted among tomatoes and peppers for nematode control
- Wind shelter provided by border of sunflower, sunroot, or Crotalaria
- Basil included as culinary companion
- Dense mulch covers entire bed surface

This integrated approach demonstrates the permaculture principle of stacking functions, where each plant serves multiple purposes within the guild.
