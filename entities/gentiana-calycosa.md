# Gentiana calycosa

## Overview

Gentiana calycosa is a perennial herb in the Gentianaceae family, native to subalpine and alpine regions of western North America.

Known as the explorer's gentian or Wyoming gentian, it produces deep blue to violet-blue funnel-shaped flowers in late summer.

The species is valued in alpine and rock gardens for its intense flower color and compact growth habit.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Gentiana calycosa was described by August Grisebach in 1837 from specimens collected in the Rocky Mountains.

It belongs to Gentiana section Pneumonanthe, a group of autumn-flowering gentians with funnel-shaped corollas and lanceolate calyx lobes.

The plant grows 10–30 cm tall with erect to ascending stems that are simple or sparingly branched.

Leaves are opposite, lanceolate to ovate-lanceolate, 3–7 cm long, with three to five prominent veins.

The calyx is a distinctive feature — lobes are lanceolate and shorter than the corolla tube, typically 12–18 mm long.

Corollas are deep blue to violet-blue, funnel-shaped, 3–5 cm long, with fringed throats in some forms.

Flowering occurs from July through September, peaking in August at mid-elevations.

## Distribution and Habitat

The species ranges from British Columbia and Alberta south through Washington, Oregon, Idaho, Montana, Wyoming, and Colorado.

It occurs in the Cascades, Olympic Mountains, Rocky Mountains, and associated subalpine ranges.

Gentiana calycosa inhabits moist meadows, streambanks, seeps, and wet alpine slopes at elevations of 1,500–3,200 meters.

Populations prefer acidic to neutral soils derived from granitic or volcanic parent material.

The species is locally common in suitable habitat but can be absent from intervening dry areas.

Disjunct populations occur in the Olympic Mountains of Washington, isolated from the main Cascade Range populations.

In the southern Rocky Mountains, it is replaced by the closely related [[query-how-to-protect-plants-from-frost-and-extend-the-growing-season]], providing nectar for bumblebees (Bombus spp.) when few other alpine plants are blooming.

The deep corolla tube restricts pollination access to long-tongued bees and bee flies.

Seed dispersal is by wind, with small winged seeds released from capsules in early autumn.

The species functions as a moisture indicator in alpine plant communities.

It commonly associates with Veronica wormskjoldii, [[plants-without-mycorrhizal-associations]] with arbuscular fungi enhance phosphorus uptake in nutrient-poor alpine soils.

## Cultivation

Gentiana calycosa requires cool, moist conditions and well-drained [[humus]]-rich soil in partial shade to full sun.

It is hardy to USDA zones 3–6, tolerating winter temperatures below −30 °C with snow cover.

Propagation is primarily by seed, which requires cold stratification for 4–8 weeks at 0–4 °C.

Seeds should be surface-sown on moist, acidic [[compost]] and kept in cool, bright conditions.

Division of mature clumps in early spring is possible but plants resent root disturbance.

The species is rarely available commercially and is most often grown by specialist alpine plant enthusiasts.

[[jadam-pesticide-for-slugs-and-snails]] can damage new growth in spring; copper barriers provide effective protection in cultivation.
