## Description

**Polyommatus icarus**, the common blue butterfly, is a species of lycaenid butterfly widely distributed across Europe and Asia. It has been used to study how mycorrhizal colonization of host plants indirectly affects above-ground herbivore performance through changes in plant chemistry.









## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Animalia
- **Phylum**: Arthropoda
- **Class**: Insecta
- **Order**: Lepidoptera
- **Family**: Lycaenidae
- **Genus**: Polyommatus
- **Species**: P. icarus









## Key Facts

- Goverde et al. (2000) fed larvae with [[lotus-corniculatus]] plants inoculated with different mycorrhizal treatments
- Survival on plants colonized by both mycorrhizal species was 3.8x higher than single-species inoculation
- Larvae fed mycorrhizal plants had double the weight of those on nonmycorrhizal plants after 11 days
- Mycorrhizal plants had 3x higher leaf phosphorus concentration and higher C/N ratio
- Herbivore performance is mycorrhizal species-specific









## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

P. icarus provides evidence for the indirect effects of [[mycorrhizal-fungi]] on herbivore populations through modification of host plant chemistry. For mycologists, this illustrates that mycorrhizal effects extend far beyond plant nutrition to influence entire above-ground food webs. The species-specific effects of different mycorrhizal inocula have implications for selecting mycorrhizal partners in restoration and agriculture.









## Ecological Role

The common blue butterfly is a widespread polyphagous lycaenid that feeds on various Fabaceae species. Its interaction with mycorrhizal fungi through shared host plants demonstrates the interconnectedness of above- and below-ground ecological communities.
