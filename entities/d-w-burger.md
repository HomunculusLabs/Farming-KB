# D. W. Burger

## Description

D. W. Burger was a researcher at the Department of Environmental Horticulture, University of California, Davis, and co-author (with G. W. Forister) of a detailed [[holzer-terrace-construction-guide]] for [[comparison-laminar-flow-hood-vs-fruiting-chamber]] hoods used in plant [[cervantes-tissue-culture-micropropagation]] laboratories. The paper provides complete construction specifications for building two HEPA-filtered laminar flow hoods connected to a single blower, including fan housing assembly, filter mounting, plexiglass hood fabrication, electrical switch installation, and detailed cost analysis.

## Key Facts

- Co-authored the [[laminar-flow-hood-construction]] guide with G. W. Forister at UC Davis, based on a conceptual design from Meyer (1986).
- The construction guide includes specifications for the fan housing (3/4" plywood with rabbet joints), filter lids (loose-fit for filter replacement access), and the plexiglass hood enclosure (1/4" clear plexiglass with 1" angle aluminum frame).
- The HEPA filters specified are 24"×30"×6" units sourced from air and gas filtration suppliers (found under Yellow Pages "Filters-Air & Gas"), with pre-filters (16"×20"×2" extended surface air filters by Dayton) protecting the main HEPA elements.
- The blower specified is a Dayton #5C094 direct-drive unit, controlled by an ELECTROSWITCH part #21301A rotary switch (series 21, 1 deck, 4-hole, 1-7 throw).
- Total materials cost for two complete hoods plus one fan housing: $903 (historical pricing).

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Burger's co-authored construction guide provides a proven, cost-effective blueprint for building laminar flow hoods — one of the most critical pieces of equipment in [[mushroom-cultivation]]. Mycological work requires the same sterile conditions as plant tissue culture: agar plate pouring, [[duggar-mushroom-life-cycle-spore-germination-fruiting]], mycelium transfer, [[liquid-culture]] work, and substrate inoculation all benefit enormously from [[laminar-flow-hood]] protection. The detailed [[oss-oeric-equipment-and-materials-list]] and construction sequence make this design accessible to amateur and professional mycologists alike.
