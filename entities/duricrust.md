# Duricrust

## Description

A duricrust is a general term for any hard, cemented surface or near-surface layer formed by the accumulation of minerals (silica, iron, calcium, or aluminium) through soil-forming processes. Duricrusts form hard caps on hills in deserts and eroded landscapes, creating impermeable barriers that profoundly limit plant growth and water infiltration. They represent the end-product of long-term mineral accumulation and cementation at or near the soil surface.

## Classification

- **Category**: Geological formation / soil feature
- **Composition**: Variable — silica, iron, calcium, or aluminium compounds
- **Subtypes**: Silcrete (silica), ferricrete (iron), calcrete (calcium), alcrete (aluminium)
- **Formation context**: Deserts, eroded landscapes, tropical regions, arid zones
- **Position**: Surface or near-surface hard cap

## Key Facts

- Hard silica-iron caps form on hills in desert environments
- Any or all subtypes (calcrete, silcrete, ferricrete) may form duricrusts in eroded areas
- Under tropical rainforest (e.g. Ecuador), iron-silica pans follow hill contours at depth (cangagua)
- Exposing duricrusts through deforestation or erosion creates permanent barren surfaces
- All duricrust types require ripping, explosive shattering, or deep mulch pits for tree establishment
- Sodium-rich soils may develop a "collapsed" cemented greyish gravelly pan (solcrete) impermeable to water

## Subtypes and Their Characteristics

- **Calcrete**: Calcium-cemented; common on coral islands and desert borders; effervesces in acid
- **Ferricrete**: Iron-cemented; caps desert hills; includes laterites and iron-manganese nodules
- **Silcrete**: Silica-cemented; forms beneath tropical forests; irreversible exposure damage (cangagua)
- **Solcrete**: Sodium-cemented; greyish, gravelly, impermeable; relieved by deep-rooted trees and humus

## Management in Permaculture

- Identify duricrust type and depth before any land use decisions
- Never clear vegetation over known duricrusts — exposure may be irreversible
- Ripping, explosive shattering, or deep mulch pits required for tree establishment
- For sodium-based solcrete: deep drainage of 1–2 m is essential; only deep-rooted trees, salt reduction, and humus can relieve the condition
- Where duricrusts are deep, use shallow-rooted crops and raised bed systems

## Relevance to Cultivation

Duricrusts are among the most serious permanent soil limitations in permaculture. Their irreversible nature when exposed makes them a critical factor in site assessment, particularly in tropical and arid regions. Mollison emphasises that many duricrust-prone landscapes should remain under perpetual forest because the cost and difficulty of rehabilitation far exceeds the value of any alternative land use. Understanding duricrusts is essential for any permaculture designer working in desert margins, tropical forest edges, or coral island environments.
