## Helvella lacunosa (*Elfin Saddle*)

## Overview

Helvella lacunosa is a species of ascomycete fungus in the family Helvellaceae, widely distributed across [[growing-gourmet-north-american-reishi-species]] segregate).

Molecular studies confirm *H. lacunosa* as a species complex with multiple cryptic lineages corresponding to geographic regions.

## Morphology

**Cap (apothecium):** 1–5 cm broad, irregularly saddle-shaped to lobed or undulate, often with two or three prominent lobes.

Surface is smooth to slightly wrinkled, dark grey to brownish-black when mature, becoming paler at margins.

The hymenium (fertile surface) lines the upper surface of the cap lobes.

**Stipe:** 3–10 cm tall, 1–2.5 cm thick, cylindrical to slightly clavate.

The stipe is deeply ribbed with prominent longitudinal ridges and cross-chambers (lacunae), giving it a honeycomb-like appearance in cross-section.

Interior is hollow with chambered structure.

Color is whitish to greyish, often with brownish tints at the base.

**Flesh:** Thin, brittle, waxy in the cap, somewhat tougher in the stipe.

**Spores:** Ellipsoid to fusoid, 17–22 × 10–13 µm, smooth, hyaline, containing two guttules.

Spores are produced in asci that are operculate, 8-spored, measuring 250–300 × 14–17 µm.

Paraphyses are filiform, septate, slightly enlarged at tips.

**Microchemical reactions:** Melzer's reagent negative on asci (inoperculate treatment errors are common in older texts).

## Distribution and Habitat

**Geographic range:** Widely distributed across Europe, [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]] and Rocky Mountains), and parts of Asia.

Also reported from North Africa and [[douglas-fir]], pine, spruce) and mixed woodlands.

Also found along forest trails, roadcuts, disturbed ground, and occasionally in urban park settings.

**Season:** Fruiting from late spring through autumn (May–October in the Northern Hemisphere), with peak fruiting in late summer to early fall.

In Mediterranean climates, fruiting may extend into winter months following rain.
