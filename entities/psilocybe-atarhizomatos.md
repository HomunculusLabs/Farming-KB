# Psilocybe atarrhizomatos

## Overview

*Psilocybe atarrhizomatos* is a rare bluing *Psilocybe* species originally described from Mexico by Guzmán in 1973. It is classified in section *Stuntzii* (or *Mexicanae* in some treatments), a group of species sharing small stature, rhizomorphic base attachments, and moderate psilocybin content.

The species is of primary interest to mycologists studying the systematics of the *Psilocybe* genus and to psychonautic mycophiles seeking lesser-known active taxa.

Its distribution is restricted to subtropical and montane forests of central and southern Mexico, where it fruits in small clusters on disturbed ground and decaying plant material.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Hymenogastraceae
- **Genus:** *Psilocybe* (Fr.) P. Kumm.
- **Species:** *P. atarrhizomatos* Guzmán

The epithet "atarrhizomatos" derives from Greek *a-* (without) + *tarri-* (roots) + *homatos* (mucus), referencing the distinctive rhizomorphic basal [[mycelium-structure-and-growth]].

Section placement has varied between *Stuntzii* and *Mexicanae* across different taxonomic treatments, reflecting the ongoing revision of *Psilocybe* infrageneric classification.

## Morphology

### Pileus

- Cap 5–20 mm diameter, convex to broadly convex, sometimes with a slight umbo
- Surface smooth, hygrophanous, reddish-brown to ochraceous when moist
- Fading to pale straw-yellow or cream upon drying
- Margin sometimes appendiculate with [[partial-veil-tropism-protection-before-spore-release]] remnants in young specimens
- [[field-guide-psilocybin-bluing-reaction-identification]] moderate on handling or bruising

### Lamellae

- Gills adnate to adnexed, subdistant to close
- Color grayish to dark purplish-brown with spore maturity
- Edge whitish, often with cheilocystidia visible as a sterile edge

### Stipe

- Stem 30–60 mm long, 1–2 mm thick, cylindrical, equal or slightly enlarged at base
- Surface smooth to finely fibrillose, white to pale brownish
- Base with conspicuous [[mycelium-running-rhizomorphic-mycelium-structural-strength-erosion-control]] (a key diagnostic feature)
- Context stuffed or hollow, bluing strongly where handled
- Partial veil evanescent, sometimes leaving a faint annular zone

### Microscopic Features

- Basidiospores 10–13 × 6.5–8.5 µm, ellipsoid to rhomboid in face view
- Spore wall thickened, with a distinct germ pore
- Basidia 2- or 4-spored, clavate
- Cheilocystidia ventricose to lageniform, forming a sterile band on gill edges
- Pleurocystidia absent or very rare

### Chemical Reactions

- [[metol-testing-bluing-reaction-mushrooms]] with bruising on cap, gills, and stipe (indicates psilocybin/psilocin presence)
- No reaction with KOH or ammonia on cap surface

## Distribution and Habitat

Found in subtropical and temperate montane forests [[psychoactive-cacti-of-central-mexico]], at elevations of 1,500–2,500 meters above sea level.

Fruits in small groups or scattered clusters on soil, often in disturbed areas including trailsides, pastures, and forest clearings.

Associates with decaying herbaceous plant material and grasses in cloud forest and pine-oak woodland ecotones.

Fruiting season coincides with the rainy period from June through September in its native range.

The species has not been confirmed outside of Mexico, though similar habitats [[neurotropic-fungi-in-central-america]] may harbor undiscovered populations.
