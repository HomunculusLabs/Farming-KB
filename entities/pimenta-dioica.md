The fruit is a globose berry 5–8 mm in diameter, green when unripe, turning
purple-black at maturity. The commercially harvested "berries" are the
dried unripe green fruits, which retain the characteristic allspice aroma
upon drying.

## Distribution and Habitat

Pimenta dioica is native to the Caribbean islands of Jamaica, Cuba, Hispaniola,
and Puerto Rico, as well as southern Mexico (Veracruz, Tabasco), Belize,
Guatemala, and Honduras. The species grows naturally in moist limestone
forests and woodlands at elevations of 100–800 meters.

Jamaican allspice is considered the world's finest, particularly from the
parishes of St. Ann, St. Mary, and Portland on the island's north coast. The
unique terroir of Jamaican pimento — volcanic-derived limestone soils,
consistent humidity, and specific temperature regimes — produces berries with
higher eugenol content and more complex flavor than material from other origins.

## Chemistry and Pharmacology

The dried berries contain 3–5% essential oil, with the major constituents
being eugenol (65–85%), methyleugenol (5–12%), 1,8-cineole (2–5%), and
α-terpineol (1–3%). Jamaican allspice oil is characteristically high in
eugenol (>80%), distinguishing it from Central American material which
contains more methyleugenol.

The eugenol content is responsible for allspice's clove-like aroma and much
