# DPT (N,N-Dipropyltryptamine)

TIHKAL entry #9. An orally active psychedelic tryptamine with a history of clinical use as an adjunct to psychotherapy, particularly with alcoholic and terminal patients. Adopted as a sacrament by the Temple of the True Inner Light in New York City.

## Chemical Names

- Tryptamine, N,N-dipropyl
- Indole, 3-[2-(dipropylamino)ethyl]
- N,N-Dipropyltryptamine

## Dosage

| Route | Dose Range |
|-------|-----------|
| Oral | 100–250 mg |
| Intramuscular | 15–125 mg |
| Intravenous | 12–36 mg |
| Smoking | 100+ mg |

## Duration

2–4 hours (oral/i.m.); approximately 20 minutes (smoked). Onset from injection occurs between 10 and 15 minutes.

## Qualitative Comments

- **200 mg, orally**: "This started sooner and was a lot stronger than I had expected. I had trouble talking and I felt very uncomfortable. I think physically I was in a chair but I was on a kind of mountain surrounded by clouds."
- **250 mg, orally**: "I was seeing the Light real strongly. The Light sort of looked like bright bursts of Light but also like a kind of Spiritual Tunnel, and I saw a Human form... seemed much larger than me."
- **100 mg, i.m.**: "I was being led by the hand of a wise old man who I know was God, and we went off to the front of the synagogue. I was handed a Torah for me to carry as a sign that I had been accepted."
- **36 mg, i.v.**: "This was a very intense experience, every bit as powerful as this amount of DMT."
- **500 mg, orally** (from commentary): At this [[hallucinogenic-mushroom-dose-effects-clinical]] become intensely visual and exhaustingly long (up to 12 hours).

## Clinical History

DPT has been used clinically at lower dosages (15-30 mg i.m.) as an adjunct to psychotherapy with alcoholic patients, where its enhancement of recall, emotional expressiveness, and self-exploration -- combined with [[savory-short-duration-grazing-and-cell-systems]] -- made it attractive. Higher doses (75-125 mg i.m.) have been explored in psychotherapy for peak experiences, including work with dying patients. The combination of short duration, manageable intensity, and enhancement of emotional expression made it a valuable clinical tool before scheduling restrictions.

## Religious Use

The Temple of the True Inner Light in New York City uses DPT as its Eucharist, referring to it as "a powerful Angel of the Host." The sacrament is administered by either smoking or drinking. The congregation has reportedly not been interfered with by federal agencies. This religious use parallels other entheogenic traditions, placing DPT in a unique position among synthetic tryptamines.

## Extensions and Commentary

DPT represents a point on the N-alkyl chain-length series: DMT (dimethyl, entry #6), DET (diethyl, entry #3), DPT (dipropyl, entry #9), DBT (dibutyl, entry #2). Activity is present across this series but drops off as chains grow longer. All are orally active, contradicting the long-standing myth that only parenteral routes are effective for N,N-dialkyltryptamines beyond dimethyl.

The 4-hydroxy analogue (4-HO-DPT, entry #20) is also psychedelic. The 5-methoxy analogue (5-MeO-DPT) has been explored and shows the expected increase in potency from the 5-methoxy substitution pattern seen throughout the tryptamine series.

## Synthesis Notes

DPT can be synthesized by two routes: from tryptamine (via indol-3-ylglyoxylamide intermediate, mp 172-174 C, reduced with LAH) or from indole (via the same [[shirota-glyoxalylamide-formation-oxalyl-chloride-indole]] pathway). HCl salt mp 174-176 C (from tryptamine) or 178-179 C (from indole). MS (m/z): parent ion 246.

## The N-Alkyl Chain Length Series

DPT sits at a key point in the systematic exploration of N-alkyl chain length effects on tryptamine activity:

- **DMT** (dimethyl, entry #6): Active orally only with MAOI; smoked 20-60 mg; i.v. active at 1 mg
- **DET** (diethyl, entry #3): Orally active at 50-100 mg; longer, richer experience than DMT
- **DPT** (dipropyl, this entry): Orally active at 100-250 mg; i.m./i.v. active; pronounced mystical/spiritual qualities
- **DBT** (dibutyl, entry #2): Activity drops off with longer chains; less commonly explored

The progression shows increasing oral dose requirements with chain length, but all four are orally active -- contradicting the long-standing myth that only DMT required parenteral routes. The i.v. potency remains high across the series (12-60 mg range), suggesting that first-pass metabolism is the limiting factor for [[tihkal-oral-activity-mao-inhibition]].

### Route Comparison

DPT is unusual among tryptamines for having well-documented experience reports across four different routes of administration. The smoked route produces the shortest experience (~20 minutes) but requires much higher doses (100+ mg) due to incomplete vaporization. Intravenous administration is the most efficient (12-36 mg) and produces effects comparable to DMT. Intramuscular injection (15-125 mg) was the route used in clinical psychotherapy settings.

## Clinical Psychotherapy Applications

The use of DPT in psychotherapy deserves emphasis. At lower clinical doses (15-30 mg i.m.), DPT enhances recall, emotional expressiveness, and self-exploration without overwhelming the patient. Its short duration (2-4 hours) made it more practical than LSD (8-12 hours) for therapeutic sessions. Higher doses (75-125 mg i.m.) produced peak experiences used in working with terminal patients, paralleling the [[metzner-psycholytic-psychedelic-therapy-models]] model developed with LSD in the 1960s.

## The Temple of the True Inner Light

The religious use of DPT by the Temple of the True Inner Light in New York City is a unique case in the history of synthetic tryptamines. While many indigenous traditions use natural psychedelic plants as sacraments (peyote, ayahuasca, [[forensic-identification-methods-psilocybin-mushrooms-tlc-gc-hplc]]), few synthetic compounds have been adopted for ritual purposes. The Temple administers DPT as its Eucharist, referring to it as "a powerful Angel of the Host." The sacrament is administered by either smoking or drinking, and the congregation has reportedly not been interfered with by federal agencies. This raises interesting legal and religious freedom questions under the Religious Freedom Restoration Act.
