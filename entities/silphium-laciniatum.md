## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Division:** Magnoliophyta
- **Class:** Magnoliopsida
- **Order:** Asterales
- **Family:** Asteraceae
- **Genus:** *Silphium*
- **Species:** *S. lacinatum* L. (1753)

**Synonyms:** *Silphium laciniatum* (alternative orthography, less common),
*[[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]], from Ohio and Ontario west to
the Great Plains (North Dakota to Texas), and south to Alabama.

Occurs in tallgrass prairies, savannas, glades, and open woodland
edges. Prefers deep, loamy soils but tolerates clay and sandy soils.

Full sun exposure required. Hardy to USDA zones 4–9.

## Ethnobotany and Medicinal Use

[[halpern-sacramental-peyote-and-native-american-church]] tribes (Lakota, Pawnee, Omaha) used the dried resinous
sap from the stem as a chewing gum, respiratory remedy, and wound
treatment. The sap was collected by scoring the stem base in late
summer.

Early European settlers adopted the plant as a substitute for chewing
tobacco and used preparations for intermittent fever, liver complaints,
and as a general tonic.
