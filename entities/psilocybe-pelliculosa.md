# Psilocybe pelliculosa

Psilocybe pelliculosa, commonly known as the "conifer psilocybe" or "striate psilocybe," is a psilocybin-containing mushroom found in the coniferous forests of the Pacific Northwest. It is one of the more widely distributed but less potent psychoactive Psilocybe species in North America.



## Taxonomy and Naming

Psilocybe pelliculosa was described by Singer and Smith in 1958. The species name "pelliculosa" refers to the distinctive pellicle (a thin, gelatinous separable layer) on the cap surface, a characteristic that aids in identification. When the cap is moist, this pellicle can be peeled back, revealing a lighter-colored layer beneath.



## Morphology

- **Cap (pileus)**: 1-4 cm broad, conic to convex, sometimes with a slight umbo (central bump). Color is hygrophanous -- dark reddish brown when moist, fading to ochraceous or straw-colored when dry. The surface is smooth and often appears translucent-striate near the margin when moist due to the underlying gill pattern showing through.
- **Gills (lamellae)**: Adnate to slightly decurrent, close, grayish becoming dark purplish brown with maturity.
- **Stem (stipe)**: 4-8 cm long, 1-2 mm thick, slender and fragile. Whitish to pale brown, sometimes bluish at the base. The partial veil is ephemeral, leaving at most a faint annular zone.
- **Spore print**: Dark purplish brown.
- **Habitat**: Scattered to gregarious on forest debris, needle litter, and decaying wood in coniferous forests. Particularly associated with Douglas fir, hemlock, and spruce forests.
- **Distribution**: Widespread throughout the Pacific Northwest of North America, from British Columbia south through Washington, Oregon, and into northern California. Also reported from Idaho and Montana.



## Chemistry and Potency

P. pelliculosa contains [[query-what-is-the-difference-between-psilocybin-and-psilocin]] and [[psilocin-entity-tihkal]] but is considered a relatively weak species in terms of psychoactive potency. Active compound concentrations are significantly lower than those found in [[stamets-psi-psilocybe-azurescens-species-guide]], [[stamets-psi-psilocybe-cyanescens-species-guide]], or [[psilocybe-baeocystis-profile]]. Large numbers of specimens would typically be required to produce noticeable psychoactive effects.



## Season and Fruiting

P. pelliculosa fruits from late summer through early winter (September to December), typically appearing after the first autumn rains. It is one of the first psilocybin mushrooms to appear in the Pacific Northwest season, sometimes fruiting as early as August at higher elevations.



## Ecological Role

As a saprotrophic species, P. pelliculosa plays a role in decomposing conifer forest debris and contributing to nutrient cycling in Pacific Northwest forest ecosystems. It is commonly found alongside other saprotrophic fungi in the duff layer of coniferous forests.



## Identification Notes

Key distinguishing features include:
- The separable gelatinous pellicle on the cap (giving the species its name)
- The hygrophanous cap that changes color dramatically as it dries
- The slender, fragile stem
- Its specific habitat in coniferous forests (unlike P. semilanceata which prefers grasslands)



## Lookalikes

- [[psilocybe-semilanceata]] -- P. pelliculosa has a more broadly conic cap, grows in forests rather than grass, and lacks the pronounced nipple
- **Galerina marginata** -- deadly poisonous; distinguished by a persistent annulus and brown spore print
- **Mycena species** -- numerous small forest Mycenas can appear similar; these are generally non-toxic but also non-psychoactive



## Ecological Role and Distribution

P. pelliculosa is one of the most widely distributed psilocybin mushrooms in the Pacific Northwest, found from coastal British Columbia through the Cascade Range and into northern California. Its preference for coniferous forest duff means it plays a role in the decomposition of needle litter and forest floor organic matter, contributing to the nutrient cycling that sustains Pacific Northwest forest ecosystems.

The species is particularly abundant in second-growth forests and areas with recent logging activity, where disturbed forest floor conditions create favorable substrate. It is commonly found alongside other saprotrophic fungi that decompose conifer debris, including non-psychoactive species that occupy similar niches.



## Foraging and Identification Tips

When foraging for P. pelliculosa, experienced collectors look for:
- Coniferous forests with deep needle duff layers, particularly Douglas fir and hemlock
- The distinctive pellicle -- gently press the cap surface and attempt to peel it; in P. pelliculosa, this separates readily
- The dramatic color change of the cap from dark brown (wet) to pale tan (dry) -- this hygrophanous quality is very pronounced
- The slender, fragile stem that distinguishes it from more robust woodland Psilocybe species

Because of its low potency, P. pelliculosa is seldom the primary target of psychoactive mushroom foragers, who generally prefer the more potent P. cyanescens and P. azurescens. However, it is commonly encountered during general foraging and its identification is important for building comprehensive knowledge of the region's mycoflora.
