# Bill Mollison

> **Note:** Sources unavailable — content from model knowledge. Review and add sources.

## Overview

Bruce Charles "Bill" Mollison (1928–2016) was an Australian researcher, author, scientist, and teacher who co-founded **permaculture** with his student [[david-holmgren]] in 1978. He is widely regarded as the "father of permaculture" for developing the concept into a comprehensive design system and spreading it globally through teaching and publishing.

## Key Contributions

- **Co-founded Permaculture (1978):** Together with David Holmgren, developed the concept of "permanent agriculture" — a design system for creating sustainable human settlements that mimic natural ecosystems.
- **Permaculture Design Certificate (PDC):** Created the standardized 72-hour curriculum that has become the gateway to permaculture education worldwide. Thousands of PDC courses are taught annually in over 100 countries.
- **Global permaculture movement:** Through extensive travel, teaching, and advocacy, Mollison spread permaculture from Australia to every continent. He established the Permaculture Institute in Tasmania.
- **Design principles and ethics:** Articulated the three core ethics of permaculture — earth care, people care, fair share — and the foundational design principles for sustainable systems.

## Major Works

- **"Permaculture One"** (1978, with David Holmgren) — The first permaculture book, outlining the core concept.
- **"Permaculture: A Designer's Manual"** (1988) — The definitive reference text for permaculture design. Covers climate zones, water management, soils, aquaculture, and design methodology. Considered the "bible" of permaculture.
- **"Introduction to Permaculture"** (1991, with Reny Mia Slay) — Accessible overview and practical guide.

## Recognition

- Right Livelihood Award (1981, with Patrick White) — often called the "Alternative Nobel Prize"
- Honored by numerous governments and organizations for contributions to sustainable agriculture

## Legacy

Mollison's work laid the foundation for the global permaculture movement, which now includes thousands of practitioners, demonstration sites, and organizations. His design system directly informs [[food-forest-design]], [[water-management]], [[homesteading-infrastructure]], and [[sepp-holzer-methods]].
