The presence of a sterile margin on the pore surface and the absence of a stipe are additional diagnostic features.

## Human Uses

The tinder conk (*F. fomentarius*) has one of the longest histories of human use of any mushroom. Otzi the Iceman (ca. 3300 BCE) carried pieces of *F. fomentarius*, along with flint and pyrite, suggesting its use as a fire-starting kit. The processed inner tissue (called "amadou") was used to carry embers between camps. The fungus has also been used in traditional medicine as an anti-inflammatory, hemostatic, and wound dressing. Contemporary research has investigated its antimicrobial and immunomodulatory properties.

Amadou production involves a laborious preparation process. The tough outer crust is removed, and the fibrous inner context is beaten, soaked in a solution of wood ash (potash), and dried repeatedly until it becomes soft, felt-like, and highly absorbent. This processed material can smolder for hours while holding an ember, making it invaluable for transporting fire in pre-industrial societies. Amadou was also used historically as a padding material for clothing and as a wound dressing due to its absorbent and hemostatic properties.
