## Overview

*Campanula morettiana* Rehb., commonly called Moretti's bellflower, is a dwarf alpine perennial endemic to the Dolomite mountains of northeastern Italy. It is one of the most sought-after species for alpine trough and crevice gardens.

The plant forms compact cushions of small, rounded leaves smothered in starry violet-blue flowers in midsummer. Its diminutive stature (5–10 cm) and floriferous habit make it a favorite among alpine plant enthusiasts.

It is named after Giuseppe Moretto, an Italian botanist who collected extensively in the Dolomites during the early [[amanita-muscaria]]. It is one of the most photographed alpine plants in the eastern Alps during its July flowering peak.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

*Campanula morettiana* belongs to section *Campanula* within the genus. It is a member of the high-alpine species complex that includes *[[campanula-cenisia]]*.

The plant forms dense, low cushions or mats of small, rounded, crenate leaves 5–10 mm long. Basal rosettes are tightly packed, creating a moss-like appearance when not in flower.

Flowering stems are short (3–8 cm), ascending, and bear one to three flowers each. The flowers are broadly campanulate to almost stellate, 1.5–2.5 cm across, bright violet-blue with a whitish center.

The corolla is divided to approximately half its length into five spreading, acute lobes. The calyx lobes are narrow and acute, approximately one-third the corolla length.

Leaves are cordate at the base, crenate-dentate along the margins, and sparsely pubescent on both surfaces. The foliage turns bronze-purple in autumn and persists through winter in sheltered locations.

Capsules are small, ovoid, and dehisce by three small basal pores. Seeds are minute (0.4–0.6 mm), pale brown, and produced in moderate quantities per capsule.

The chromosome number is 2n=34. The species is closely related to *Campanula cenisia* of the western Alps but differs in its broader corolla lobes and lower growth habit.

In the field, the two species can be distinguished by flower orientation: *Campanula morettiana* has upward-facing to slightly nodding flowers, while *Campanula cenisia* bears distinctly nodding blooms. Leaf shape also differs, with *C. morettiana* having broader, more cordate leaves.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Campanula morettiana* is endemic to the Dolomite range in northeastern Italy, occurring in South Tyrol, Trentino, and Belluno provinces. Its range is limited to limestone massifs between 1,800 and 3,000 m elevation.

It grows exclusively on calcareous rock faces, in crevices, and on thin ledges where snow lies late into spring. The species is a strict chasmophyte, requiring vertical or near-vertical limestone substrates.

The Dolomite climate features long cold winters with 6–8 months of snow cover and short cool summers. Annual precipitation ranges from 800–1,200 mm, much of it falling as snow.

Populations are scattered across the major Dolomite groups including the Sella, Marmolada, Pale di San Martino, and Tre Cime di Lavaredo. Individual colonies may contain dozens to hundreds of plants.

The species shows high site fidelity, rarely colonizing areas far from established populations. Genetic studies suggest limited gene flow between mountain groups, with each major massif hosting partially differentiated populations.

The species co-occurs with other Dolomite endemics such as *Saxifraga burseriana*, *Potentilla nitida*, and *Physoplexis comosa*. These assemblages constitute some of the most specialized alpine plant communities in Europe.
