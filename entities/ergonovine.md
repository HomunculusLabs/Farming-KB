## Overview

Ergonovine, also known as ergometrine, is a naturally occurring lysergamide ergot alkaloid produced by *[[cannabis-root-rot-prevention-and-treatment]] of postpartum hemorrhage.

Beyond its [[oner-sativa-medical-applications]], ergonovine has psychoactive properties at doses exceeding therapeutic ranges. It was among the compounds Albert Hofmann self-experimented with during his investigation of ergot alkaloids at Sandoz Laboratories in the 1940s, contributing to the broader research program that led to the discovery of LSD-25.

## Chemical Properties

**IUPAC Name:** (8β)-9,10-didehydro-6-methyl-8-[(s)-1-hydroxyethyl]ergoline-8-carboxamide

**Molecular Formula:** C₁₉H₂₃N₃O₂

**Molecular Weight:** 325.41 g/mol

**Structure Type:** Lysergamide (tetracyclic ergoline ring system with C-8 amide substituent)

Ergonovine is the simplest naturally occurring ergot alkaloid in terms of its C-8 substituent. Unlike the ergopeptines (ergotamine, ergocristine, ergocryptine, ergocornine), which carry a tripeptide moiety, ergonovine bears only a single carboxamide group. This structural simplicity results in different pharmacological properties and metabolic stability.

The compound contains two stereocenters at C-5 and C-8, with the naturally occurring form being the 5R,8R configuration. The maleate salt (ergonovine maleate) is the standard pharmaceutical form, with improved water solubility for injectable formulations.

## Pharmacology

Ergonovine acts primarily as an agonist at α-adrenergic receptors and serotonin (5-HT) receptors, with additional activity at dopamine D₂ receptors. Its uterotonic effects are mediated through direct smooth muscle stimulation of uterine tissue, with partial contribution from α-adrenergic receptor activation.

The compound exhibits partial agonist activity at 5-HT₁A, 5-HT₂A, and 5-HT₂C receptor subtypes. This [[plant-gravitropism-and-spatial-awareness]]. The experience is generally described as substantially weaker than LSD or psilocybin, with more somatic (body-focused) effects.

Albert Hofmann documented his self-experiment with ergonovine in his autobiography, describing it as producing "a slight dizziness and a feeling of heaviness in the legs" without the full psychedelic spectrum of LSD. This relatively mild psychoactivity is consistent with the compound's lower affinity for 5-HT₂A receptors.

## Medical Applications

Ergonovine's primary clinical use is in obstetrics for the management of postpartum hemorrhage, the leading cause of maternal mortality globally. It is administered intramuscularly or intravenously immediately after delivery of the placenta. It is included on the WHO Model List of Essential Medicines for this indication.

The drug is also used diagnostically in cardiac stress testing. The ergonovine provocation test (also called the methylergonovine test) was historically used to provoke coronary artery spasm in patients with suspected variant angina. This application has declined with the availability of alternative diagnostic methods.

## History

The isolation of ergonovine from ergot was accomplished independently by four research groups in 1935: Dudley and Moir in England, Thompson and colleagues in the United States, Kharasch and Legault in Canada, and Wollensak in Germany. The simultaneous discovery reflected the intense pharmaceutical interest in ergot alkaloids during the interwar period.

Moir demonstrated ergonovine's uterotonic activity in 1935 when he administered a crude extract to a patient with postpartum hemorrhage, observing rapid contraction of the uterus. This clinical observation rapidly translated into widespread obstetric use, making ergonovine one of the first ergot alkaloids to enter routine medical practice.

Albert Hofmann's self-experimentation with ergonovine at Sandoz in 1943 occurred alongside his more famous work with LSD-25. While ergonovine proved to have only weak psychoactive properties, it was an important stepping stone in understanding the structure-activity relationships of lysergamide compounds. Hofmann noted that the simple amide group limited central nervous system penetration compared to the diethylamide group of LSD.
