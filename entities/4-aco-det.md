## Overview

**4-AcO-DET** (4-acetoxy-N,N-diethyltryptamine) is a synthetic psychedelic tryptamine and a close structural analogue of psilocin.

It functions primarily as a prodrug for 4-HO-DET (4-hydroxy-diethyltryptamine), which is the active metabolite produced through in vivo deacetylation.

The compound was first synthesized and characterized by Alexander and Ann Shulgin, who documented its synthesis and subjective effects in **TiHKAL** (Tryptamines I Have Known and Loved), entry #29.

4-AcO-DET is classified as a substituted tryptamine, with an acetoxy group at the 4-position of the indole ring and diethyl substitution on the terminal amine.

It is considered a designer drug and research chemical, with limited formal pharmacological study compared to classical psychedelics like psilocybin or LSD.

The compound is controlled or scheduled in several jurisdictions, though its legal status varies by country.

## Chemical Properties

**Systematic name:** 3-[2-(Diethylamino)ethyl]-1H-indol-4-yl acetate

**Molecular formula:** C₁₆H₂₂N₂O₂

**Molecular weight:** 274.36 g/mol

**IUPAC name:** [3-[2-(diethylamino)ethyl]-1H-indol-4-yl] acetate

**SMILES:** CCN(CC)CCC1=CNC2=C1C(=CC=C2)OC(=O)C

**Chemical class:** Substituted tryptamine, specifically a 4-substituted acetylated tryptamine.

The compound is structurally related to 4-AcO-DMT, differing only in the substitution pattern on the terminal amine (diethyl vs. dimethyl).

This difference in alkyl chain length influences both receptor affinity and metabolic stability.

4-AcO-DET is typically encountered as a white to off-white crystalline powder or as a freebase oil.

It is soluble in organic solvents such as ethanol, methanol, and DMSO, and is stable at room temperature when stored protected from light and moisture.

## Pharmacology

4-AcO-DET acts primarily as a **5-HT2A receptor agonist**, which is the primary mechanism underlying its psychedelic effects.

As a prodrug, it is rapidly deacetylated in vivo by esterases to produce **4-HO-DET**, which is the pharmacologically active species responsible for most of the observed psychoactivity.

**Receptor binding profile (estimated based on structural analogues):**

- **5-HT2A:** High affinity — primary mediator of hallucinogenic effects, visual distortions, and altered cognition.
- **5-HT1A:** Moderate affinity — may contribute to anxiolytic and mood-elevating aspects of the experience.
- **5-HT2C:** Moderate affinity — may influence visual patterning and somatic effects.

The diethyl substitution on the amine nitrogen generally reduces 5-HT2A potency relative to dimethyl analogues but may increase lipophilicity and membrane permeability.

The acetoxy group at the 4-position of the indole ring increases metabolic stability compared to the free phenol (4-HO-DET), resulting in a slightly delayed onset and extended duration.

## Effects

**Dose ranges (oral, estimated):**

- **Threshold:** 5–10 mg
- **Light:** 10–15 mg
- **Common:** 15–25 mg
- **Strong:** 25–35 mg
- **Heavy:** 35+ mg

**Duration:** Total experience lasts approximately 4–7 hours, with onset at 20–60 minutes, peak effects at 2–4 hours, and gradual comedown over 2–3 hours.

**Subjective effects profile:**

- Visual effects include geometric patterning, color enhancement, trailing, and in higher doses, immersive hallucinatory states.
- Cognitive effects include altered thought patterns, philosophical ideation, ego dissolution, and enhanced introspection.
- Emotional effects range from euphoria and connectedness to anxiety and existential distress, depending on dose and set-and-setting.
- Somatic effects include nausea (common during onset), pupil dilation, increased heart rate, and mild temperature dysregulation.

**Comparison to related compounds:**

4-AcO-DET is generally described as somewhat less intense and less visual than 4-AcO-DMT at equivalent doses, with a slightly longer duration.

It is considered gentler and more contemplative than psilocin, with users reporting a clearer headspace and less cognitive confusion.

The experience is often compared to a milder, more drawn-out version of the psilocybin experience with distinct character.

## Synthesis

The Shulgin synthesis, as described in TiHKAL, begins with 4-hydroxyindole or a suitably protected derivative.

The key steps involve:

1. Alkylation of the indole nitrogen with 2-chloro-N,N-diethylamine or equivalent to install the diethylaminoethyl side chain.
2. Acetylation of the 4-hydroxy group using acetic anhydride or acetyl chloride under basic conditions.
3. Purification via column chromatography or recrystallization.

The synthesis requires moderate organic chemistry laboratory skills and access to controlled precursor materials.

## Legal Status

4-AcO-DET is not specifically listed in the UN Convention on Psychotropic Substances.

In the United States, it may be prosecuted under the Federal Analogue Act as a Schedule I analogue of a controlled tryptamine.

In the United Kingdom, it is controlled as a Class A drug under the Psychoactive Substances Act and earlier misuse of drugs regulations.

Legal status varies significantly across jurisdictions, with many countries not specifically scheduling the compound.
