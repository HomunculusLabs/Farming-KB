# Zingiber zerumbet (Shampoo Ginger)

## Overview

Zingiber zerumbet is a perennial herbaceous plant in the family Zingiberaceae, widely distributed across tropical Asia and the Pacific. It is commonly known as shampoo ginger, pinecone ginger, or awapuhi, reflecting its most distinctive feature: a conical inflorescence filled with a fragrant, soap-like fluid.

The species is valued across its native range for its medicinal rhizomes, edible flower bracts, and cosmetic applications. In Hawaiian traditional medicine (*la'au lapa'au*), it is one of the most important medicinal plants and was among the canoe plants carried by Polynesian voyagers.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Order:** Zingiberales
- **Family:** Zingiberaceae
- **Genus:** Zingiber
- **Species:** Z. zerumbet (L.) Sm.

The specific epithet *zerumbet* derives from the Arabic *zerumbad*, referring to a related aromatic root. The Hawaiian name *awapuhi* is also applied to other Zingiber species introduced to the islands.

The species is closely related to Z. officinale (common ginger) and Z. mioga (myoga ginger). Hybrids between Z. zerumbet and Z. officinale have been reported in cultivation but are rare in the wild.

Plants grow 0.8–1.5 m tall from a branching rhizome system. Leaf blades are lanceolate, 25–40 cm long and 5–8 cm wide, glabrous above and sparsely pubescent beneath. The pseudostem is formed by tightly rolled leaf sheaths.

The inflorescence is the most recognizable feature: a cone-like structure 8–15 cm tall, composed of overlapping, fleshy bracts that are green at emergence, turning brilliant red as they mature. Each bract axil bears a pale yellow to white flower. The cone exudes a clear, viscous, fragrant fluid traditionally used as a hair wash.

Rhizomes are fleshy, branching, pale yellow internally with a ginger-like aroma though milder and less pungent than Z. officinale. They are smaller than commercial ginger, typically 2–5 cm in diameter.

The root system is fibrous with occasional spherical root tubers that serve as additional storage organs. ## Distribution and Habitat

Zingiber zerumbet is native to tropical and subtropical Asia, with a range spanning India, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh, Myanmar, Thailand, Vietnam, southern China, Malaysia, Indonesia, and the Philippines. It has been widely introduced throughout the Pacific islands, including Hawaii, Fiji, Samoa, and Tahiti.

The species naturalizes readily in disturbed habitats, forest margins, stream banks, and roadside areas. It grows from sea level to approximately 1200 m elevation. In Hawaii it has become naturalized in wet forests and valleys on all major islands.

In some Pacific islands, Z. zerumbet is considered moderately invasive due to its vigorous rhizomatous spread. It can form dense monocultures that suppress understory regeneration in disturbed forest areas, though it is generally less aggressive than other invasive Zingiberaceae species.

**Key pharmacological activities documented in the literature:**

- **Anti-inflammatory:** Zerumbone inhibits NF-κB, STAT3, and COX-2 signaling pathways. Oral administration reduces carrageenan-induced paw edema in rodent models.
- **Anticancer:** Zerumbone induces apoptosis and cell cycle arrest in multiple cancer cell lines. It suppresses tumor growth in xenograft models of colon and breast cancer.
- **Antioxidant:** Rhizome extracts scavenge DPPH and ABTS free radicals in vitro. Zerumbone upregulates endogenous antioxidant enzymes including SOD and catalase.
- **Antimicrobial:** Essential oil inhibits growth of [[staphylococcus-aureus]], Escherichia coli, and [[mushroom-cancer-clinical-trials-overview]] remain limited. The rhizome is generally recognized as safe for food use in traditional practice, but standardized safety data for therapeutic doses are not available.

Zerumbone has shown promise in chemoprevention studies, particularly for colon and skin cancers. Mechanism studies indicate it modulates multiple signaling cascades including PI3K/Akt and MAPK pathways, giving it a broad-spectrum effect on tumor cell proliferation.

Topical formulations containing Z. zerumbet extract have been studied for wound healing and anti-aging applications. The cone fluid demonstrates moisturizing properties and contains compounds that may protect against UV-induced skin damage.

## Cultivation and Uses

Zingiber zerumbet is cultivated commercially for its rhizomes in Thailand, Indonesia, and India. The species is also widely grown as an ornamental plant in tropical and subtropical gardens for its striking inflorescence.

Propagation is primarily by rhizome division. Planting is done at the start of the wet season with rhizome pieces placed 5–8 cm deep and spaced 30–50 cm apart. The crop tolerates a range of soil types but performs best in rich, well-drained loams with partial shade.
