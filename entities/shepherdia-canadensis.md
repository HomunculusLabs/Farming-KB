## Shepherdia canadensis

## Overview

**Soopolallie**, **Canada buffaloberry**, or **russet buffaloberry**
(*Shepherdia canadensis*) is a deciduous dioecious shrub in the family
Elaeagnaceae, native to boreal and montane regions [[tropical-rainforest-seedling-mycorrhiza-ecological-restoration]], *Shepherdia canadensis* is valued as
a nitrogen-fixing pioneer species, [[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]] before leaf
emergence. Female plants produce drupes 4–6 mm diameter, bright orange-red to
scarlet, ripening in mid-to-late summer.

## Distribution and Habitat

Canada buffaloberry ranges from Alaska and the Yukon across Canada to
Newfoundland, extending south through the Rocky Mountains to New Mexico and
through the Great Lakes region to Pennsylvania. Disjunct populations occur in
the Black Hills of South Dakota.

The species is a characteristic plant of open boreal forest, montane slopes,
stream banks, rocky outcrops, and disturbed sites including burns, avalanche
tracks, and road cuts. Elevation ranges from near sea level to 3,000 m.

It tolerates a remarkably wide range of soil conditions, from sandy gravel to
heavy clays, and thrives on nitrogen-poor substrates where its nitrogen-fixing
symbiosis provides a competitive advantage. It is among the most cold-hardy
[[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]]. In riparian zones, its
dense root system stabilizes streambanks and reduces erosion.
