# Soil Arthropods

## Description

Soil arthropods are invertebrate animals with segmented bodies, jointed appendages, and hard exoskeletons that inhabit the soil environment. They include insects (beetles, ants, springtails), arachnids (spiders, mites), myriapods (centipedes, millipedes), and crustaceans (woodlice/sowbugs). In [[the-soil-food-web]], arthropods occupy multiple trophic levels: some are predators that hunt nematodes, protozoa, and other arthropods; others are shredders that break down organic matter into smaller pieces for fungal and bacterial decomposition; and still others are herbivores that feed on living plant roots. Their movement through soil creates channels for air and water exchange, directly contributing to soil structure.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Soil zoology
- **Phylum:** Arthropoda
- **Category:** Soil mesofauna and macrofauna
- **Key groups:** Insects, Arachnids, Myriapods, Crustaceans
- **Defining features:** Segmented body, jointed appendages, exoskeleton (chitin)

## Key Facts

1. Arthropods are defined by three characteristics: segmented bodies, jointed appendages, and a hard outer covering called an exoskeleton made primarily of chitin.
2. Familiar arthropods include insects (beetles, ants, springtails), spiders, centipedes, millipedes, and even shrimp and lobsters (aquatic relatives).
3. In the [[soil-food-web]], arthropods feed on [[teaming-with-microbes-protozoa-and-nematodes]], which in turn have fed on bacteria and fungi — placing arthropods at the upper levels of the soil food web's microbial [[holmgren-trophic-pyramid-energy-hierarchy-societies]].
4. Soil arthropods also eat each other, and themselves serve as prey for snakes, birds, moles, and other larger animals, connecting the below-ground food web to above-ground ecosystems.
5. As arthropods move through soil searching for food and protection, their movement creates physical channels and pathways that allow air and water to enter and move through the soil profile.
6. Many soil arthropods function as shredders: they tear organic matter (leaves, dead roots, animal remains) into smaller pieces, dramatically increasing the surface area available for fungal and bacterial colonization and decomposition.
7. Beetles and their larvae are particularly important shredders in the soil, breaking down organic matter that worms have pulled into the soil from the surface.
8. Springtails (Collembola) are among the most abundant soil arthropods, feeding on fungi [[mollison-humus-and-organic-matter-in-soils]] in the rhizosphere and contributing to nutrient cycling.
9. Mites (Acari) are extremely diverse in soil, with species occupying every trophic level: decomposers, fungivores, bacterivores, predators, and parasites.
10. Rototilling and soil disturbance devastate arthropod populations by crushing their bodies, destroying their habitat structure, and disrupting the food chains they depend on.

## Ecological Roles

| Role | Examples | Function |
|------|----------|----------|
| Shredders | Millipedes, sowbugs, beetle larvae | Break organic matter into smaller pieces for microbial decay |
| Predators | Centipedes, ground beetles, pseudoscorpions | Control populations of herbivores and other soil organisms |
| Fungivores | Springtails, fungal-feeding mites | Graze on fungal hyphae, releasing nutrients |
| Herbivores | Root-feeding insects, some nematodes | Feed on living plant tissue (can be pests) |
| [[fungal-mycelial-networks-ecosystem-engineers]] | Ants, termites | Create extensive tunnel systems that aerate soil and modify drainage |

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Soil arthropods are essential partners in organic cultivation. As shredders, they accelerate the conversion of organic amendments (compost, mulch, cover crop residues) into forms accessible to bacteria and fungi. As predators, they help regulate populations of root-feeding pests and maintain the competitive balance that suppresses soil-borne diseases. In mycology, fungivorous arthropods like springtails and mites interact directly with fungal mycelium — they can spread fungal spores through soil, graze on pathogenic fungi, and influence fungal [[core-endorhiza-bacterial-community-composition-cannabis]] through selective feeding. A healthy, diverse arthropod population is a strong indicator of a functioning soil food web and good soil health.
