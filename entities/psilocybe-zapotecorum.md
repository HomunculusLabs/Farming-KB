## Psilocybe zapotecorum

*Psilocybe zapotecorum* is a psilocybin-containing mushroom in the family
Hymenogastraceae. It is known by the common name **pajaritos de monte** (little
birds of the woods) and is notable for its prominent, acutely papillate cap
and preference for muddy clay soils in subtropical coffee-growing regions.
The species was first described by [[psilocybe-semperviva]]* Heim and Callieux, is considered by
many mycologists to be conspecific with this species. The epithet
"zapotecorum" honors the Zapotec people of Oaxaca, Mexico, among whom the
mushroom has been used ceremonially. The species was originally collected and
described by Heim based on specimens from the Zapotec region of southern
Mexico. Singer's later interpretation helped clarify the species concept and
distinguished it from similar taxa in [[spore]]
development.

### Stem

(30) 50–90 (110) mm long by 1–3 mm thick. The stem is equal to slightly
thickened near the base, flexuous, and sometimes twisted. Color is whitish
overall, often brownish red near the base. The stem bruises readily bluish to
bluish black when handled, a key indicator of psilocybin content.

### Partial Veil

Thinly cortinate, fragile, and soon disappearing. Does not typically leave a
persistent annular zone on the stem.

### Microscopic Features

Spores are dark purplish brown in deposit, rhomboid to subrhomboid, measuring
(5) 6.5–8 (9.6) × 4–5.6 µm. Basidia are 4-spored, rarely 2-spored.
Pleurocystidia measure 16–36 × 8–12 µm and are ventricose to clavate, often
irregular in form. Cheilocystidia measure (15) 19–35 × 4.4–6.6 µm,
lageniform, narrowing into a long neck of 1–3 µm, either acute or
subcapitate at the apex.

## Habitat and Distribution

*Psilocybe zapotecorum* grows solitary to gregarious in muddy clay soils,
typically in subtropical coffee plantations. It fruits in June and July in
Mexico (Puebla, Oaxaca, and Chiapas) and in February in Argentina. The species
has also been reported from Brazil and Colombia, indicating a broad Neotropical
distribution. Elevational range is 1000–1800 meters above sea level. Its
association with disturbed coffee-growing habitats suggests it may benefit from
the cleared, moist, clay-rich soils found in these agroecosystems.
