## Overview

**Lysimachia terrestris** is a species of herbaceous perennial plant in the family Primulaceae, native to northern [[growing-gourmet-north-american-reishi-species]] flora. Modern phylogenetic studies place it within Lysimachia section Lysimachia, the core yellow-flowered clade.

The plant produces erect, unbranched stems reaching 60–120 cm in height. Stems are smooth, slightly angular, and green to reddish-green in color, with a characteristic slight wing along each angle.

Leaves are lanceolate to narrowly elliptic, measuring 5–12 cm long and 1–3 cm wide. They are arranged in opposite or whorled pairs along the upper stem and are sessile or very short-petiolate.

The leaf surface is glabrous to sparsely hairy, with a prominent midrib and fine lateral veins. Leaf margins are entire or very finely toothed near the apex.

The inflorescence is a terminal leafy spike measuring 5–15 cm long, bearing numerous flowers. Each flower has five yellow petals and five green sepals, with a distinct orange or reddish ring at the base of the petals.

Petals are 8–12 mm long and overlap slightly at the base. The flowers are subtended by small bracts and are densely packed along the spike, giving it a candle-like appearance.

The fruit is a small globose capsule approximately 3–4 mm in diameter containing numerous fine seeds. Seeds are brown, oblong, and released when the capsule dehisces at maturity.

Below ground, the plant develops a shallow network of rhizomes that enable gradual clonal expansion. Older rhizome segments can survive for several years, contributing to the longevity of established populations.

The root system is fibrous and relatively shallow, adapted to saturated soils where deep rooting is neither necessary nor possible. Root biomass is concentrated in the upper 15 cm of the substrate.

## Distribution and Habitat

Lysimachia terrestris ranges across northern North America from Newfoundland and Labrador west to Manitoba and south through the Great Lakes region to New England, New York, and the Appalachian Mountains.

Lysimachia terrestris is not considered invasive outside its native range, though it can form substantial colonies in favorable wetland conditions. Its growth is naturally limited by competition and water availability.

In the northeastern United States, it is often found growing alongside other wetland perennials including [[iris-versicolor]], and various Carex species. It typically occupies the shallowest zones of marsh vegetation gradients.

## Ecology and Interactions

The bright yellow flowers of Lysimachia terrestris are primarily pollinated by medium to large bees, particularly bumblebees and honeybees. The flowers produce modest quantities of nectar accessible to short-tongued insects.

The orange ring at the base of the petals serves as a nectar guide, directing pollinators toward the floral center. Flowers are protogynous, with the stigma becoming receptive before pollen is released.

Butterflies, syrphid flies, and small beetles also visit the flowers as secondary pollinators. The extended bloom period of 4–6 weeks provides a reliable midsummer nectar resource in wetland habitats.

The foliage is palatable to mammalian herbivores including deer and muskrat, though [[comparison-constructed-wetlands-vs-bioswales]] where consistent soil moisture can be maintained. It is valued for its vertical accent and reliable midsummer bloom.

Propagation is by seed, which germinates readily under moist, cool conditions without stratification. Division of established clumps in early spring is an effective vegetative method.

Seeds can be sown directly into wet soil in autumn or started indoors in trays with consistently moist medium. Transplanting is most successful when done in early summer before peak bloom.

The species requires minimal maintenance once established in appropriate wet conditions. It spreads slowly by rhizomes and self-seeds moderately, but does not typically become aggressive in garden settings.
