## G. M. Kuaeva

## Description

G. M. Kuaeva is a snow science researcher whose work on the physical properties of snow cover in the Greater Caucasus mountains was translated and published by the USDA. Kuaeva's research provided foundational data on snow physics, avalanche dynamics, and the behavior of snow in mountain environments — data that Mollison drew upon extensively in his treatment of cold climate [[permaculture-design]].

## Classification

- **Category**: Researcher / Scientist
- **Field**: Snow physics and glaciology
- **Region**: Greater Caucasus (Soviet Union / Russia)
- **Period**: 1975 publication

## Key Facts

- Published *Physical Properties of Snow Cover of the Greater Caucasus* in 1975
- The work was translated into English by the USDA, indicating its international scientific significance
- Research focused on the physical characteristics of snow in the Caucasus mountain range
- Data from Kuaeva's work was used by [[bill-mollison]] in the cold climate sections of the *Permaculture: A Designer's Manual*
- The research contributed to understanding of snow movement modes: microcreep, snow board slip, snow mound flow, snow body mass movement, snow slides, and avalanches
- Provided quantitative data on avalanche velocities (up to 6.5 m/s for snow avalanches, with air blast waves reaching 330 m/s)

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Kuaeva's snow research has indirect but meaningful relevance:

1. **Cold-climate soil biology**: Understanding snow insulation properties helps predict soil microbial and fungal activity beneath snowpack — many cold-climate fungi remain active under insulating snow layers thicker than 15 cm
2. **Permafrost and mycorrhizae**: Research on frozen ground dynamics informs understanding of mycorrhizal survival in permafrost zones
3. **Forest management**: Avalanche data helps in siting and maintaining forest shelterbelts that protect cultivated areas and fungal habitats
4. **[[water-management]]**: Snow melt patterns determine spring soil moisture, critical for mushroom fruiting in cold climates
5. **Seasonal cultivation planning**: Understanding snow physics enables better planning of cold-climate cultivation schedules

## Research Contributions Referenced by Mollison

- Snow movement classification: microcreep, slip (snow boards), snow mound, snow bodies, snow slides, and avalanches
- Avalanche speed data: systematic avalanches occur yearly, episodic avalanches every approximately 35 years
- Compressed air blast dynamics preceding avalanche masses
- Snow radiation properties: albedo values from 20% (muddy slush) to near 100% (fresh dry snow)
- Snow as black body radiation emitter: values 0.962-0.986
