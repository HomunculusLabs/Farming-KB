The species is absent from the Great Plains and the arid Southwest. It becomes increasingly localized at the southern edge of its range, where it is restricted to cooler, moister sites.

It inhabits moist to wet deciduous woodlands, floodplain forests, stream banks, swamp margins, and damp meadows. Soils are typically loamy or silty, with moderate to high organic content.

The species tolerates seasonal flooding and is a common component of bottomland hardwood forests. It rarely occurs in permanently standing water but thrives in seasonally saturated soils.

Elevation range extends from near sea level to approximately 1,200 meters in the Appalachian Mountains. Populations at higher elevations tend to be smaller and more scattered than lowland populations.

## Ecology

Lysimachia ciliata flowers from June through August, with peak bloom in mid-July. The nodding yellow flowers are pollinated primarily by small bees, especially species of Halictidae (sweat bees).

The flowers produce floral oils collected by specialized oil-collecting bees of the genus Macropis. This oil-bee relationship is characteristic of several Lysimachia species and represents a specialized pollination syndrome.

Vegetative spread through rhizomes is the primary mode of colony expansion. Individual genets may extend over several square meters in favorable sites, forming dense stands.

The species is moderately tolerant of shade but flowers more prolifically in brighter sites. In deep shade, plants tend to be taller and more sparsely leaved.

The species serves as a larval host plant for several moth species. Its dense growth provides cover for small wildlife in wetland edge habitats.
