## Description

Banisteriopsis quitensis is a species of woody vine in the Malpighiaceae family, native to the Amazon basin and Andean foothills. It is one of several Banisteriopsis species identified as being used in the preparation of ayahuasca and related psychoactive preparations in the Amazon region. The species was catalogued by Friedberg (1965) as one of the four major Banisteriopsis species employed in Amazonian hallucinogenic practices.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Family:** Malpighiaceae
- **Genus:** Banisteriopsis
- **Species:** B. quitensis
- **Etymology:** The specific epithet "quitensis" refers to Quito, Ecuador, suggesting the species was first documented in or near the Ecuadorian capital
- **Related species:** B. caapi, B. inebrians, B. rusbyana
- **Geographic affinity:** Upper Amazon and Andean foothills near Quito, Ecuador

## Key Facts

- One of four major Banisteriopsis species delineated by Friedberg (1965) for the Amazon region
- The four species are B. caapi, B. quitensis, B. inebrians, and B. rusbyana
- Used traditionally in the preparation of ayahuasca and related psychoactive brews
- The species name suggests an association with the Quito region of Ecuador, indicating a geographic distribution that extends into the upper Amazon and Andean foothills
- Like other Banisteriopsis species, contains beta-carboline alkaloids that serve as MAO inhibitors
- The presence of MAOI compounds enables the oral activity of DMT when combined with appropriate admixture plants
- Traditional healers may select different Banisteriopsis species depending on regional availability and specific ceremonial purposes
- Less commonly discussed in the ethnobotanical literature than B. caapi, which is the most widely known and cultivated species
- The geographic range extending to the Andean foothills near Quito suggests potential adaptation to higher elevations

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Banisteriopsis quitensis is relevant to cultivators as one of the lesser-known but traditionally used Banisteriopsis species. While B. caapi dominates the cultivation and commercial market for ayahuasca vines, the traditional use of multiple species suggests that B. quitensis may offer different alkaloid profiles or growing characteristics worth investigating. The species" association with the Quito region implies it may be adapted to slightly higher elevations than lowland Amazonian species, potentially expanding the range of cultivation environments. For researchers and cultivators interested in preserving the full diversity of traditional ayahuasca plant knowledge, B. quitensis represents an important but under-studied species that merits conservation and further botanical investigation. Precise species identification is critical for accurate alkaloid profiling and pharmacological research.

## Cultivation Considerations

- Likely requires tropical or subtropical conditions similar to other Banisteriopsis species
- May tolerate slightly cooler temperatures given its association with the Quito region (high elevation)
- As a woody climbing vine, requires support structures for optimal growth
- Propagation methods likely similar to B. caapi (seed or cutting)
- Conservation status and wild population data are limited in the source material
- The higher-elevation habitat may make this species more accessible to cultivators outside the lowland tropics

## Chemical Profile

- Presumed to contain beta-carboline alkaloids similar to other Banisteriopsis species
- Specific alkaloid ratios may differ from B. caapi, B. inebrians, and B. rusbyana
- Functions as MAO inhibitor enabling oral DMT activity in ayahuasca preparations
- Further phytochemical analysis needed to characterize the specific profile

## Botanical Description

Banisteriopsis quitensis is a woody climbing vine in the Malpighiaceae family, likely similar in morphology to the better-known B. caapi. The species epithet "quitensis" indicates a connection to Quito, Ecuador, suggesting that the species was first collected or described from populations growing in the upper Amazon basin or Andean foothills of Ecuador. This geographic association implies adaptation to slightly different ecological conditions than lowland Amazonian species, potentially including higher elevations and cooler temperatures.

## Geographic Distribution and Ecology

The Quito-associated name suggests B. quitensis may be found in:
- The upper Amazon basin of Ecuador
- Andean foothills and cloud forest transitions
- Areas with higher elevation than typical lowland Amazon habitat
- Potentially overlapping ranges with B. caapi in transitional zones

This distribution pattern has implications for cultivation, as it may indicate broader environmental tolerance than exclusively lowland species.

## Ethnobotanical Significance

The identification of four distinct Banisteriopsis species used in ayahuasca preparation (B. caapi, B. quitensis, B. inebrians, and B. rusbyana) demonstrates the sophistication of indigenous and mestizo botanical knowledge. Healers can distinguish between these species and select appropriate vines based on tradition, availability, and intended therapeutic effect. The use of multiple species suggests that each may contribute distinct qualities to the ayahuasca preparation, whether in terms of alkaloid profile, potency, duration, or qualitative effects on the visionary experience.

## Research Priorities

B. quitensis represents several important gaps in the current literature:
