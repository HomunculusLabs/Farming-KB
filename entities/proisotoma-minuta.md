## Description

**Proisotoma minuta** is a species of collembolan (springtail) in the family Isotomidae. It is a small soil-dwelling arthropod commonly used in laboratory studies of fungal-faunal interactions due to its grazing behavior on [[fungal-hyphae]] and spores.









## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Animalia
- **Phylum**: Arthropoda
- **Class**: Collembola
- **Order**: Entomobryomorpha
- **Family**: Isotomidae
- **Genus**: Proisotoma
- **Species**: P. minuta









## Key Facts

- Collembolan species frequently used in choice chamber experiments to study fungal feeding preferences
- Hiol Hiol et al. (1994) demonstrated that P. minuta significantly slowed the growth rate of [[ectomycorrhizal-fungi]] including [[suillus-luteus]], [[pisolithus-tinctorius]], [[thelephora-terrestris]], and Laccaria laccata
- Capable of reducing mycorrhizal development on roots of loblolly pine seedlings in controlled experiments
- Feeding preferences shift over time when offered combinations of fungal food sources, indicating flexible dietary behavior
- Schultz (1991) showed altered feeding behavior in pairwise combinations of ectomycorrhizal fungi over time









## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Proisotoma minuta is significant in mycological research as a model organism for studying collembolan-fungal interactions. Its grazing on ectomycorrhizal fungi can reduce colonization of plant roots, potentially impacting seedling establishment in forestry and cultivation systems. Understanding the density-dependent effects of collembolan grazing on mycorrhizal fungi is critical for soil management in mushroom cultivation and plant production systems. At optimal low densities, collembola may stimulate compensatory hyphal growth, while high densities reduce mycorrhizal benefit to host plants.









## Ecological Role

As a fungal grazer, P. minuta plays a role in regulating fungal biomass and community composition in soil ecosystems. Its selective feeding preferences influence which fungal species dominate in the rhizosphere. The species contributes to nutrient cycling by consuming fungal tissue and redistributing nutrients through soil via fecal deposition.
