The species is notable for its tall stature, reaching up to 3 meters, and its bright yellow ray florets surrounding a distinctive greenish central disk.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Rudbeckia laciniata** was described by Linnaeus in 1753.

The genus honors Olof Rudbeck the Younger (1660–1740), a Swedish botanist and professor at Uppsala University.

The specific epithet *laciniata* means "slashed" or "cut into lobes," referring to the deeply divided leaves.

Plants produce stout, erect stems 1–3 meters tall, glabrous or sparsely hairy near the inflorescence.

Leaves are alternate, broadly ovate to triangular in outline, 10–40 cm long, pinnately lobed with 3–7 lanceolate segments.

Lower leaves are long-petiolate; upper leaves become smaller and sessile toward the inflorescence.

Flower heads are borne singly or in loose corymbose clusters at the stem tips.

The involucre is hemispheric, 1.5–2.5 cm across, with reflexed phyllaries that often have a dark purplish base.

Ray florets are 6–16, bright yellow, 3–6 cm long, typically reflexed and drooping.

The central disk is globe-shaped, green to yellowish-green, 1.5–2.5 cm in diameter, composed of numerous small tubular florets.

Achenes are dark brown to black, 4–5 mm long, quadrangular, and lack a pappus.

The fruit is a dark achene, 3–4 mm long, lacking a pappus or with a short crown-like remnant.

The root system consists of a fibrous root crown with short, thick rhizomes. Established plants develop a substantial root mass that contributes to [[polypore-fungi-eastern-north-america]] America, from Quebec and Ontario south to Georgia and west to Minnesota and Oklahoma.
