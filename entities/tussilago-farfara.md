## Tussilago farfara

Tussilago farfara, commonly known as coltsfoot, is a low-growing herbaceous perennial in the aster family (Asteraceae), native to Europe and parts of Asia. One of the earliest flowering plants in temperate regions, coltsfoot sends up dandelion-like yellow flowers in late winter and [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]]:** Established in eastern Canada and the northeastern and midwestern [[fukuoka-textdoc-agricultural-land-law-obstruction-return-farming]]:** A persistent weed of arable fields, particularly on heavy clay soils

### Invasive Potential
Coltsfoot is classified as an invasive or noxious weed in several jurisdictions, including parts of Canada (Ontario, Manitoba) and the northeastern United States. Its aggressive rhizomatous spread allows it to form dense, monocultural mats that exclude native vegetation. In permaculture contexts, the species should be contained using root barriers (minimum 30 cm depth) and managed through regular harvesting to prevent uncontrolled spread.

### Early Pollinator Resource
The early flowering period (February–April) provides a critical nectar and pollen source for emerging pollinators, particularly bumblebee queens (*Bombus* spp.) establishing spring nests. In temperate climates, coltsfoot is one of the few native plants in bloom during the earliest weeks of spring, making it ecologically valuable despite its weedy tendencies.
