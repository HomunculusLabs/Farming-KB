# Cortinarius brunneus

## Description

*Cortinarius brunneus* (commonly known as the brown webcap) is a medium-sized basidiomycete fungus in the family Cortinariaceae, order Agaricales. It produces fruiting bodies with dark brown, hygrophanous caps (3–8 cm) that fade to paler brown as they dry. The gills are initially pale brown, darkening with age to rusty-brown. The stem is relatively slender, pale brown at the top and darker below, with remnants of the cortina (cobweb veil). This ectomycorrhizal species associates primarily with coniferous trees, especially spruce (*Picea*) and pine (*Pinus*), in acidic soils.

## Key Facts

- Recorded as a high **radiocesium accumulator** in Belgian forest ecosystems following Chernobyl, with levels of **7,700–27,000 Bq/kg** (Fraiture et al., 1990).
- Among the top radiocesium-accumulating fungi measured in the Belgian survey, second only to *[[cortinarius-armillatus]]* in some samples.
- The wide range (7,700–27,000 Bq/kg) demonstrates significant **within-species variation** in radiocesium accumulation.
- As an ectomycorrhizal associate of conifers, it forms mutualistic relationships with economically important timber species.
- Common and widespread in coniferous and mixed forests across Europe and North America.
- The genus *Cortinarius* is the largest genus of mushrooms globally, with over 2,000 described species.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

*Cortinarius brunneus* contributes to understanding radiocesium dynamics in forest ecosystems:

- **Radiocesium monitoring**: As a consistent high-level radiocesium accumulator, it provides reliable data for monitoring radioactive contamination in coniferous forests.
- **Ectomycorrhizal cesium transport**: The high accumulation by mycorrhizal *Cortinarius* species (vs. lower levels in saprotrophs like *[[agaricus]]*) supports the hypothesis that [[mycorrhizal-fungi]] accumulate radiocesium through active nutrient transport mechanisms shared with potassium uptake.
- **Forest food webs**: High radiocesium levels in *C. brunneus* represent a pathway for radiation transfer to fungivorous mammals (deer, wild boar, rodents) in contaminated forests.
- **Species identification challenges**: The *Telamonia* subgenus contains many morphologically similar brown species, making field identification difficult — a concern for foragers in radiation-affected areas.
- **Post-Chernobyl ecology**: Data from *C. brunneus* and related species has been essential for modeling long-term radiocesium cycling in European forests, showing that fungal contamination can persist for decades.

The species illustrates the critical role that [[ectomycorrhizal-fungi]] play as concentrators of environmental radionuclides, creating ecological pathways for radiation transfer that persist long after initial contamination events.
