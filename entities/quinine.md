# Quinine

## Overview

**Quinine** (C₂₀H₂₄N₂O₂) is a quinoline alkaloid and one of the most important antimalarial drugs in history. It is extracted from the bark of *Cinchona* species and was the primary treatment for malaria for over three centuries before the development of synthetic alternatives.

Quinine remains clinically significant today, particularly for treating *Plasmodium falciparum* malaria in regions with chloroquine resistance. It also retains use as a treatment for nocturnal leg cramps and as a bittering agent in tonic water.

## Chemical Properties

- **Molecular formula:** C₂₀H₂₄N₂O₂
- **Molecular weight:** 324.42 g/mol
- **IUPAC name:** (8S,9R)-6'-methoxycinchonan-9-ol
- **Structure type:** Quinoline alkaloid with a quinuclidine ring system
- **Appearance:** White to colorless, crystalline, needle-like powder with an intensely bitter taste
- **Melting point:** 177 °C (decomposes)
- **Solubility:** Slightly soluble in water (0.5 g/L at 25 °C); soluble in ethanol, chloroform, and dilute acids
- **pKa:** 4.2 (quinoline nitrogen), 8.5 (quinuclidine nitrogen)
- **CAS number:** 130-95-0
- **Optical rotation:** [α]D²⁰ = −168° (in ethanol)

Quinine contains four stereocenters and is the (-)-isomer of the cinchona alkaloid diastereomer pair. The related compound quinidine is the (+)-isomer and shares many pharmacological properties.

## Pharmacology

### Mechanism of Action

Quinine kills malaria parasites through multiple mechanisms:

- **Heme polymerization inhibition** — accumulates in the parasite food vacuole and prevents detoxification of heme into hemozoin, leading to toxic heme buildup
- **DNA intercalation** — binds to parasitic DNA, inhibiting replication
- **Sporozoite inhibition** — disrupts parasite metabolism at multiple stages

Quinine is primarily effective against the asexual erythrocytic stages of *Plasmodium* species. It has minimal activity against liver-stage (exoerythrocytic) forms and gametocytes.

### Pharmacokinetics

- **Absorption:** Nearly complete [[first-pass-metabolism-and-oral-bioavailability]] (~80%). Peak plasma levels in 1–3 hours.
- **Half-life:** 8–14 hours in healthy adults; prolonged in severe malaria (up to 26 hours)
- **Volume of distribution:** 1.5–3.0 L/kg
- **Plasma protein binding:** ~70% (primarily to alpha-1-acid glycoprotein)
- **Metabolism:** Hepatic via CYP3A4 to 3-hydroxyquinine (major metabolite, less active) and other oxidative products
- **Excretion:** Renal (~20% unchanged); metabolites excreted in urine

### Additional Pharmacology

- **Anti-pyretic** — reduces fever through action on the hypothalamic heat-regulating center
- **Skeletal muscle relaxant** — reduces muscle excitability, basis for treating leg cramps
- **Oxytocic** — stimulates uterine contractions at high doses
- **Cardiac effects** — prolongs QT interval; blocks sodium and potassium channels

## Effects

### Therapeutic Effects

- Rapid reduction of *P. falciparum* parasitemia within 48 hours
- Resolution of fever and malaria symptoms
- Reduction of nocturnal leg cramps frequency

### Adverse Effects

- Cinchonism: tinnitus, headache, nausea, blurred vision, and dizziness (dose-dependent)
- Hypoglycemia (especially in severe malaria and pregnancy)
- QT prolongation and cardiac arrhythmias
- Thrombocytopenia and hemolytic-uremic syndrome
- Hypersensitivity reactions, including severe skin reactions
- Severe hemolysis in G6PD-deficient patients
