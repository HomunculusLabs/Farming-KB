# Trichostrongylus colubriformis

## Description

Trichostrongylus colubriformis is an intestinal parasitic nematode (strongyle)
that infects ruminant animals, particularly sheep and goats. It is one of the
most economically important gastrointestinal parasites in livestock worldwide.
The free-living stages develop in dung pads on pasture, where they are vulnerable
to predation by nematophagous fungi, particularly Duddingtonia flagrans.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Eukaryota
- **Kingdom:** Animalia
- **Phylum:** Nematoda
- **Class:** Secernentea
- **Order:** Strongylida
- **Family:** Trichostrongylidae
- **Genus:** Trichostrongylus
- **Species:** T. colubriformis

## Key Facts

- **Intestinal parasite:** Adult worms live in the small intestine of sheep, goats,
  and other ruminants, causing damage to the intestinal mucosa leading to protein
  loss, diarrhea, and weight loss.
- **Free-living stage:** Eggs develop through two free-living larval stages (L1, L2)
  to the infective L3 stage in dung on pasture. This stage is the target of
  nematophagous fungal biocontrol.
- **Duddingtonia flagrans control:** Faedo et al. (1997) demonstrated oral
  administration of D. flagrans spores to sheep significantly reduced T.
  colubriformis larval development in feces.
- **Spore survival:** D. flagrans conidia and chlamydospores survive passage through
  the ruminant gut, germinating in dung to trap developing nematode larvae.
- **Economic impact:** Causes significant production losses in sheep and goat
  industries globally through reduced weight gain, lower wool quality, and mortality.
- **Anthelmintic resistance:** Has developed widespread resistance to multiple
  classes of chemical anthelmintics, increasing urgency for biological alternatives.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Biocontrol target:** Primary target organism in Duddingtonia flagrans biocontrol
  research for livestock parasites.
- **Fungal delivery system:** D. flagrans spores surviving gut passage represents a
  novel biocontrol delivery mechanism exploiting the nematode life cycle.
- **Sustainable parasite management:** Offers non-chemical alternative for managing
  T. colubriformis, reducing reliance on anthelmintic drugs.
- **Livestock industry impact:** Effective biocontrol could significantly improve
  profitability and sustainability of sheep and goat production worldwide.

## Ecological Interactions

- Free-living larval stages in dung trapped and consumed by Duddingtonia flagrans
  and Arthrobotrys species nematophagous fungi.
- Population dynamics influenced by pasture management, stocking density, and climate.
- Dung pat microecosystem supports complex interactions between nematodes, fungi,
  coprophilous organisms, and dung beetles.

## Pitfalls and Research Gaps

- D. flagrans spore survival through the ruminant gut varies with animal species,
  diet, and fungal isolate, requiring strain selection for optimal performance.
- Reduced ability to recover fungi from feces of animals in the tropics suggests
  biocontrol may be restricted to temperate and subtropical regions.
- Integration with existing anthelmintic treatment schedules requires careful
  timing to avoid negative interactions between treatments.
- Long-term effects of continuous D. flagrans supplementation on pasture ecology
  and non-target soil organisms have not been assessed.
