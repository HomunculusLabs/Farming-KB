# Nucleic Acid

## Description

Nucleic acids — DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) and RNA (ribonucleic acid) — are the special molecules that hold the genetic code, the instructions used to build cells and everything in them. Together with carbohydrates, lipids, and proteins, nucleic acids are one of the four fundamental groups of molecules that constitute all [[savory-living-organisms-as-tools]]. Among their most critical roles is the duplication of proteins, which despite their immense numbers and importance cannot duplicate themselves.

## Classification

- **Type**: Macromolecule / biomolecule
- **Category**: One of the four [[molecules-of-life-in-plants]]
- **Composition**: Carbon (C), Hydrogen (H), Oxygen (O), Phosphorus (P), Nitrogen (N)
- **Forms**: DNA and RNA

## Structure

Nucleic acids are built from smaller units called nucleotides, each consisting of:
- A sugar molecule (ribose in RNA, deoxyribose in DNA)
- A phosphate group
- A nitrogen base

Only five nucleotides combine in various arrangements to encode all the genetic information for all life:
1. **Adenine** — found in both DNA and RNA
2. **Thymine** — found only in DNA
3. **Cytosine** — found in both DNA and RNA
4. **Guanine** — found in both DNA and RNA
5. **Uracil** — found only in RNA (replaces thymine)

## DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid)

- The master blueprint of the cell, stored and protected in the nucleus
- Holds the complete genetic code for building every protein the organism needs
- Famous double-helix shape is created by hydrogen bonds between complementary nucleotide pairs

## RNA (Ribonucleic Acid)

RNA exists in several functional forms that carry out different roles in protein synthesis:

### Messenger RNA (mRNA)

- Copies the genetic instructions from DNA in the nucleus
- Carries these instructions through nuclear pores to ribosomes in the cytoplasm
- The ribosome reads the mRNA sequence to determine the order of amino acids in a protein
- A well-fed plant has abundant tRNA relative to mRNA, ensuring smooth [[fungal-single-cell-protein-production]]

### Transfer RNA (tRNA)

- Brings specific amino acids to the ribosome during protein synthesis
- Each tRNA carries one specific amino acid matching the mRNA code
- Acts as the adapter molecule between nucleotide code and amino acid sequence
- Present in much greater numbers than mRNA in well-nourished plants

### Ribosomal RNA (rRNA)

- Structural and catalytic component of ribosomes
- Produced in the nucleolus along with other ribosome subunits

## Key Facts

- Only five nucleotides encode all genetic information for all life on Earth
- The double helix of DNA is held together by hydrogen bonds — the same weak bonds that hold water molecules together
- Nucleic acids are among the largest molecules in the cell
- Proteins cannot duplicate themselves — they depend entirely on nucleic acids for their reproduction
- The sequence of nucleotides determines the sequence of amino acids in every protein
- Nucleic acids contain phosphorus as a structural component, linking phosphorus nutrition directly to genetic function

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- Phosphorus is a structural component of all nucleic acids — [[query-how-to-fix-phosphorus-deficiency-in-cannabis]] directly impairs DNA replication and RNA transcription, slowing growth and reproduction
- Nitrogen is a major component of nucleotide bases — nitrogen deficiency reduces nucleic acid production and limits protein synthesis
- Rapidly dividing cells (meristems, root tips) have the highest demand for nucleic acid building blocks
- In [[mycology]], fungal DNA/RNA drives the production of digestive enzymes that break down organic substrate
- Understanding nucleic acid metabolism helps explain why nitrogen and phosphorus are the two most critical macronutrients for plant growth
- [[genetic-improvement-agaricus-bisporus-molecular-approaches]] of cultivars operates at the level of DNA — selecting or engineering favorable nucleotide sequences for desired traits
