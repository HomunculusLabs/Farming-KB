# J (BDB; 2-Amino-1-(3,4-methylenedioxyphenyl)butane)

J, also known as BDB, is a phenethylamine compound and the alpha-ethyl homologue of [[2c-b-pihkal-entry-20]] #94.

## Chemical Classification

J belongs to the phenethylamine class of chemical compounds.

It is formally named 2-amino-1-(3,4-methylenedioxyphenyl)butane.

The code name BDB derives from the IUPAC nomenclature using 1,3-benzodioxole: 1-(1,3-benzodioxol-5-yl)-2-butanamine.

J is the alpha-ethyl analogue of [[mda]], but with a similar chronology of effects.

All subjects who explored J accepted it and commented favorably.

## Qualitative Effects

At 175 mg, the first effects are evident within 30 minutes as pleasant feelings without untoward body effects.

The experience reaches a plus two level and levels off there within an hour.

There are no visual distortions, no giddiness, and no significant introspective urges.

The experience does not rise to a full psychedelic intoxication.

After about 90 minutes at this level, the effects gradually decline over two hours, followed by fatigue and languor.

At 200 mg with a 75 mg supplement, subjects reported a very strong climb with a good interior feeling described as having some mdma.

## Nomenclature

Two nomenclature systems exist for naming J and related compounds.

The traditional system calls the methylenedioxy ring a substituent on phenyl, giving 2-amino-1-(3,4-methylenedioxyphenyl)butane.

The modern Chemical Abstracts system uses 1,3-benzodioxole as the parent, giving 1-(1,3-benzodioxol-5-yl)-2-butanamine, from which the code BDB is derived.

The "J" code name follows Shulgin's alphabetical naming system for MDA homologues.
