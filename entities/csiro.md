# CSIRO

## Description

The Commonwealth Scientific and Industrial Research Organisation (CSIRO) is Australia's national science agency and one of the largest and most diverse research agencies in the world. It is mentioned in [[bill-mollison]]'s *Permaculture: A Designers' Manual* (1988) as the organization where Mollison worked from 1954 in the Wildlife Survey Section, conducting field research on rabbits, locusts, muttonbirds, and forest regeneration problems with marsupials across remote Australian locations — experiences that formed the empirical foundation for his later development of permaculture.

## Classification

- **Type**: Government research agency, statutory authority
- **Founded**: 1916 (as Advisory Council of Science and Industry); 1926 (as CSIR); 1949 (renamed CSIRO)
- **Headquarters**: Canberra, Australian Capital Territory, Australia
- **Domain**: All fields of scientific and industrial research
- **Budget**: Approximately AUD $1.7 billion (recent years)
- **Employees**: Approximately 5,000+

## Key Facts

- Australia's national science agency, founded in 1916 and established in its modern form in 1949.
- Bill Mollison joined the CSIRO Wildlife Survey Section in 1954, working as a biologist for nine years across many remote locations in Australia.
- Mollison's CSIRO fieldwork included studies of rabbit populations, locust plagues, muttonbird (short-tailed shearwater, *Ardenna tenuirostris*) ecology, and forest regeneration with respect to marsupial browsing.
- This period of field observation of natural systems across diverse Australian ecosystems directly informed Mollison's understanding of ecological interactions, which later crystallized into [[invasive-species-management]], disease ecology

## Notable Agricultural Contributions

- Development of improved crop varieties adapted to Australian conditions
- Soil mapping and classification systems
- Water-use efficiency research for irrigation
- Biological control programs (e.g., biological control of prickly pear cactus, rabbit calicivirus)
- [[pasture-management]] and rotational grazing research
- Climate modeling and seasonal forecasting for agricultural planning

## After CSIRO: Mollison's Path

After leaving CSIRO in 1963, Mollison:
- Spent time at the Tasmania Museum in curatorial duties
- Worked with the Inland Fisheries Commission surveying macrofauna of inland waters and estuaries
- Studied food chains and water conditions in Tasmanian rivers and lagoons
- Returned to university study, completing a degree in biogeography
- Was appointed to the University of Tasmania, developing the unit of Environmental Psychology
- In 1974, with David Holmgren, developed and refined the permaculture concept

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

CSIRO's research directly supports cultivation and mycology through:
- Soil microbiology research including mycorrhizal associations
- Development of biological control agents including entomopathogenic fungi
- Climate data and seasonal forecasting essential for cultivation planning
- Water management research applicable to irrigation and humidity control
- Biodiversity research supporting integrated pest and disease management
- Ongoing work on soil health, carbon sequestration, and regenerative agriculture
