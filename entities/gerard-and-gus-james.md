## Description

**Gerard and Gus James** are agricultural practitioners from the **Pyramid Lake
Paiute Tribe** reservation in Nevada, USA, who worked with Bill Mollison on
**Indian rice grass** (*Oryzopsis hymenoides* / *Achnatherum hymenoides*)
re-seeding trials in tribal rangelands. Their work demonstrates traditional
ecological knowledge combined with modern range management techniques to restore
cold desert ecosystems and provide staple food sources.






## Classification

- **Category**: People — Agricultural practitioners / Tribal land managers
- **Domain**: Dryland range management, Native seed restoration
- **Location**: Pyramid Lake, Nevada, USA
- **Affiliation**: Pyramid Lake Paiute Tribe






## Key Facts

- Based at **Pyramid Lake** in the Great Basin desert of Nevada — a cold desert
  environment with extreme temperatures and low precipitation.
- Conducted **Indian rice grass re-seeding** trials on tribal rangelands.
- The seed treatment process they used:
  1. Seed is **acid-treated** to break dormancy
  2. Buried at **0.3 m depth** for stratification
  3. **Glumes flamed** off to prepare seed for germination
- *Oryzopsis* (Indian rice grass) is a **staple food of the Shoshone people**
  in this region.
- Indian rice grass is a **perennial range grass** adapted to cold deserts,
  providing both human food and livestock forage.
- The grass naturally spaces itself at approximately **0.6 m intervals** as
  bunches — an efficient pattern for arid land colonisation.
- Their work represents the integration of **indigenous food sovereignty**
  with permaculture land management principles.






## Relevance to Cultivation / Mycology

- Native bunch grasses like Indian rice grass form extensive **mycorrhizal
  associations** that maintain soil structure and nutrient cycling in arid
  ecosystems.
- Re-seeded grasslands develop **fungal-dominated soil food webs** over time,
  transitioning from degraded bare soil to biologically active range.
- The bunch growth habit (0.6 m spacing) creates **root zone islands** of
  biological activity — mycorrhizal networks connect adjacent bunches through
  common soil hyphal networks.
- **Acid treatment** of seed mirrors processes used in fungal substrate
  preparation — controlled acidification to break down protective barriers.
- Restored grasslands provide **organic matter inputs** (root exudates, leaf
  litter, dead root turnover) that feed saprophytic fungi and build soil
  carbon reserves.
- Traditional Shoshone use of Indian rice grass seed as staple food parallels
  modern interest in **fungally-processed grain substrates** for mushroom
  cultivation.
