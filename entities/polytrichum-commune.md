## Overview

**Polytrichum commune** (common haircap moss) is one of the largest and most recognizable moss species in the world. It forms dense, tall tufts reaching 10–40 cm in height, giving it a miniature forest appearance on the forest floor.

The species is widespread across boreal and temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere. It is a conspicuous component of coniferous and mixed woodlands, often dominating acidic, nutrient-poor substrates.

Its common name derives from the dense, hair-like calyptra that covers the immature spore capsule. The genus name *Polytrichum* means "many hairs" in Greek, referring to this distinctive feature.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

*Polytrichum commune* belongs to the family Polytrichaceae within the class Polytrichiopsida.

**Classification:**
- **Division:** Bryophyta
- **Class:** Polytrichiopsida
- **Order:** Polytrichales
- **Family:** Polytrichaceae
- **Genus:** *Polytrichum*
- **Species:** *P. commune* Hedw.

The gametophyte is the dominant phase, consisting of an erect, unbranched stem with narrow, lanceolate leaves arranged spirally. Leaves are 6–10 mm long with a toothed margin and a conspicuous nerve (costa) extending beyond the leaf tip as a colorless hair point.

Stems are reddish-brown at the base, darkening with age. The root-like rhizoids at the stem base anchor the plant and absorb water from the substrate. Mature colonies form dense, interwoven mats up to several centimeters thick.

A unique feature of Polytrichaceae is the presence of **lamellae** — vertical plates of photosynthetic tissue on the upper leaf surface. These lamellae increase the photosynthetic area and help regulate gas exchange in the compact leaf structure.

The sporophyte consists of a long seta (stalk) topped by a cylindrical, angular capsule. The capsule is covered by a hairy calyptra in early development. Spores are released through 32–64 peristome teeth at the capsule mouth.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Polytrichum commune* has a circumboreal distribution across Europe, Asia, and [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-central-america-caribbean]] and East Africa.

The species colonizes a wide range of acidic substrates. It is most common on well-decomposed humus in coniferous forests, along stream banks, on road embankments, and in peatland margins.

It shows a strong preference for moist, shaded microsites but tolerates moderate exposure. Dense colonies develop on rotting logs, stumps, and mineral soil where competition from vascular plants is reduced.

The species is moderately shade-tolerant but also colonizes open, disturbed sites. It frequently dominates the bryophyte layer in managed conifer plantations throughout northern Europe and Canada.

## Ecology and Interactions

As a relatively large moss, *P. commune* plays a significant role in forest floor microhabitats. Its dense turfs retain moisture and moderate temperature fluctuations, creating favorable conditions for invertebrates and microorganisms.

The species contributes substantially to organic matter accumulation. Decomposition of its robust shoots adds humus to forest soils, particularly in boreal ecosystems where vascular plant litter inputs are low.

*Polytrichum commune* supports diverse microfaunal communities. Tardigrades, nematodes, and springtails are commonly found within its dense mats. The lamellate leaf structure provides sheltered microhabitats for these organisms.
