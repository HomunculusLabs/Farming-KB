## Overview

Gastrodia elata, known as Tianma in Chinese, is a fully mycoheterotrophic orchid in the family Orchidaceae.

It is one of the most important medicinal plants in traditional Chinese medicine.

The species entirely lacks chlorophyll and depends on [[auxin-production-by-symbiotic-fungi]] for nutrition throughout its life cycle.

The dried tuber of Gastrodia elata has been used medicinally for over 2,000 years in East Asia.

It is native to mountainous regions of China, Japan, Korea, and the Russian Far East.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Gastrodia elata was first described by Carl Peter Thunberg in 1783.

The genus name derives from the Greek gastrodes, meaning "pot-bellied," referring to the shape of the tuber.

The species belongs to the subfamily Epidendroideae within the tribe Gastrodieae.

Plants grow 30–150 cm tall from a fleshy, elongated tuber.

The tuber is cylindrical to irregular, 5–15 cm long and 2–5 cm in diameter.

The stem is erect, pale yellow to reddish-brown, and bears scale-like leaves.

True foliage leaves are absent; the leaf-like structures are reduced to membranous sheaths.

The inflorescence is a terminal raceme bearing 20–70 flowers.

Flowers are 8–12 mm long, with a dull yellow to brownish perianth.

The labellum is white with purple streaks and forms a short tube with the column.

The ovary is inferior and contains thousands of microscopic dust-like seeds.

The plant produces no green tissues at any stage of its life cycle.

## Distribution and Habitat

Gastrodia elata is native to East Asia.

In China, the species occurs across most provinces south of the Yellow River.

Its range extends through Korea and Japan to the Russian Far East and the Himalayan region.

The plant grows in mountainous forests at elevations of 400–3,000 meters.

It is typically found in humus-rich soils under deciduous and coniferous forest canopies.

Gastrodia elata requires the presence of its fungal partner Armillaria mellea, the honey mushroom.

The Armillaria mycelium colonizes the tuber and provides all nutritional requirements.

Soils must be well-drained and slightly acidic for optimal growth.

Natural populations have declined significantly due to overharvesting of wild tubers.

Wild Gastrodia elata is now rare and is classified as Vulnerable in China.

## Bioactive Compounds and Pharmacology

The primary bioactive compound in Gastrodia elata is gastrodin, a glucoside of 4-hydroxybenzyl alcohol.

Gastrodin is the major water-soluble component and serves as a chemical marker for quality control.

Additional bioactive constituents include parishin, gastrodigenin, vanillyl alcohol, and bis(4-hydroxybenzyl) ether.

The tuber also contains [[aact-tannins-terpenes-phenolic-compounds-wood-composting-ingham]], organic acids, and polysaccharides.

Gastrodin has demonstrated neuroprotective effects in numerous preclinical studies.
