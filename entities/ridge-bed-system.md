# Ridge Bed System

## Description

The ridge bed system is one of the two primary bed construction methods used in [[mushroom-cultivation]], historically favored by French growers and practiced extensively in the limestone caves around Paris. Ridge beds are constructed as raised, peaked mounds of compost that maximize surface area for mushroom production in low-ceilinged growing spaces. The system is also known as the French bed method.

## Classification

- **Category**: Cultivation infrastructure
- **Subcategory**: Bed construction method
- **Geographic association**: France (Paris caves), Europe
- **Alternative name**: French bed system
- **Primary use**: Low-ceilinged caves and cellars

## Bed Dimensions and Construction

Duggar provides specific construction parameters for ridge beds:

- **Base width**: 20 to 24 inches
- **Top width**: No more than 6 inches across
- **Height**: 12 to 14 inches
- **Arrangement**: Groups of two beds, with 10-12 inches of floor space between groups
- **Post-casing spacing**: Reduced to approximately 9 inches after casing application
- **Bed contact**: The two beds in each group may contact each other throughout their length
- **Profile**: Gentle slope from crest to floor

### Construction Method

1. Compost is pitched into the designated area
2. Material is arranged and compressed with blows from a shovel or a compressing board
3. Beds are shaped to the specified dimensions with gradual tapering from base to top
4. The gentle slope is critical — steeper slopes cause problems with mushroom development

## Advantages

1. **Surface area**: Greater surface area per unit of floor space than flat beds
2. **Sanitation**: Permits more effective sanitation through improved airflow around the bed surfaces
3. **Space efficiency**: The only practical system in low-roofed caves and cellars
4. **Yield potential**: May increase yield per unit of floor area
5. **Drainage**: Good drainage characteristics prevent waterlogging
6. **Air circulation**: Triangular cross-section promotes natural convection

## Disadvantages

1. **Labor**: Significantly more labor-intensive in both construction and ongoing maintenance
2. **Crop uniformity**: First and last portions of the crop tend to produce lighter-weight mushrooms compared to continuous flat beds
3. **Slope sensitivity**: If slopes are too steep, mushrooms are smaller and clusters detach more readily from spawn
4. **Watering complexity**: More complex moisture management due to the angled surfaces
5. **Maintenance difficulty**: Harder to access, water, and harvest than flat beds

## Slope Considerations

Duggar emphasizes that the gentle slope from crest to floor is essential for optimal production:

- Proper slope permits production of heavy clusters
- Steeper slopes cause smaller mushrooms because:
  - Mushrooms are subject to more rapid drying out on steep surfaces
  - Heavy clusters pull away from their attachment to the spawn below
  - Mushrooms cannot reach maximum possible size before detaching
- The same gentle slope principle applies to unsided flat beds

## Historical Context

The ridge bed system was the dominant cultivation method in the famous mushroom caves of Paris, where low ceilings made the [[flat-bed-system]] impractical. The caves of Paris (carrières) were extensive underground limestone quarries that provided stable temperature and humidity conditions ideal for mushroom cultivation. The ridge bed system evolved specifically to maximize production in these constrained vertical spaces.

Duggar notes that in "the relatively low-roofed caves of Paris the ridge bed system alone is practised, and there it has its distinct advantages."

## Comparison with Flat Bed System

| Feature | Ridge Bed | Flat Bed |
|---------|-----------|----------|
| Surface area | Higher per floor unit | Slightly lower |
| Labor required | More | Less |
| Mushroom weight | Variable (lighter at crop edges) | More uniform, heavier |
| Space requirement | Low ceiling compatible | Requires headroom for tiers |
| Geographic preference | France | England, United States |
| Sanitation | Better airflow | Adequate |
| Construction | More complex | Simpler |

## Modern Relevance

The ridge bed system is less common in modern commercial operations but remains relevant in:
- Small-scale and traditional mushroom cultivation
- Operations with limited vertical space
- Regions maintaining traditional French cultivation methods
- Educational demonstrations of historical mushroom growing techniques

The principle of maximizing surface area through bed geometry continues to influence modern mushroom farm design, though contemporary operations more commonly achieve similar goals through shelving and tray systems.
