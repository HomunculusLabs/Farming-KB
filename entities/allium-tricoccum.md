## Overview

**Allium tricoccum**, commonly known as ramps, wild leeks, or wild garlic, is a perennial bulbous plant native to the deciduous forests of [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]].

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Allium tricoccum** Aiton belongs to the family Amaryllidaceae, tribe Allieae.
It was first described by William Aiton in 1789 in Hortus Kewensis.
The species is diploid with 2n = 2x = 14 chromosomes.

A closely related taxon, *Allium burdickii*, has narrower leaves and a less pungent bulb. Some authorities treat it as *A. tricoccum* var. *burdickii*, while others recognize it at species level.

The bulb is white, ovoid, and 1–3 cm in diameter.
Each bulb produces a cluster of fibrous roots from its base.
Typically 2–3 lanceolate leaves emerge per bulb, measuring 10–30 cm long and 3–7 cm wide.

Leaves are deep green and smooth-margined, with a distinct reddish-purple lower stem near ground level. This coloration is the most reliable field character for distinguishing ramps from toxic look-alikes such as lily of the valley.

The scape (flower stalk) emerges after leaves have senesced in early summer.
It is 15–40 cm tall, smooth, and bears a single terminal umbel of 20–50 flowers.
Flowers are white to creamy-white, 4–6 mm across, with six tepals and protruding stamens.
The fruit is a three-lobed capsule containing shiny black seeds approximately 3 mm in diameter.

## Distribution and Habitat

Ramps range from central Canada (Ontario, Quebec) south through the Appalachian Mountains to Georgia.
Their range extends west to Minnesota, Iowa, and Missouri.

The species is most abundant in the southern and central Appalachians, where large colonies can cover hectares of forest floor.

Ramps grow in rich, moist, well-drained soils of deciduous forests, typically at elevations of 200–1500 m.
They are most commonly associated with maple-beech-hemlock or mixed [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]] (5–15%) and a pH of 5.5–7.0 for optimal growth.
