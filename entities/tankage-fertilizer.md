## Description

Tankage (sometimes called blood-and-bone or meatmeal) is an animal-based organic fertilizer produced from slaughterhouse byproducts. It is a concentrated nitrogen source used to boost the nitrogen content of seedmeal-based fertilizer blends. In Steve Solomon's COF formula, tankage is used as an optional supplement at 1/2 part by volume alongside 3 parts seedmeal.

## Classification

- **Type**: Organic fertilizer / Nitrogen booster
- **Source**: Slaughterhouse byproducts (rendered animal tissue, blood, bone)
- **Primary nutrients**: High nitrogen, moderate phosphorus
- **Typical analysis**: ~10-4-0 NPK (varies by source)
- **Form**: Dry meal / powder

## Key Facts

- Tankage is the product of the slaughterhouse industry, made from the whole animal minus the parts that go into human food and the hide
- The typical analysis of tankage Solomon used was approximately 10-4-0 NPK
- It is used in COF as a nitrogen booster alongside seedmeal when higher nitrogen is desired
- The higher-nitrogen option (3 parts seedmeal + 1/2 part tankage instead of 4 parts seedmeal alone) is slightly better for leafy crops in spring
- If tankage is unavailable or the gardener is concerned about disease transmission, it can be omitted entirely with the consequence only that the COF will be slightly lower in nitrates, requiring a bit more total application
- Tankage quality and analysis can vary significantly between sources

## Relevance to Cultivation

- Provides a high-nitrogen organic alternative to chemical nitrogen fertilizers
- Particularly beneficial for leafy crops in spring when rapid nitrogen availability supports vigorous vegetative growth
- As a slow-release organic source, tankage avoids the boom-bust nitrogen cycle of synthetic fertilizers
- The organic nitrogen from tankage is released through microbial decomposition, feeding soil biology while nourishing plants
- When used in COF, it contributes to the complete nutritional profile that produces more nutritious vegetables

## Safety and Handling

- Some gardeners may have concerns about disease transmission from animal byproducts
- Solomon notes that in over 20 years of use, he encountered no disease problems
- If concerned, tankage can be safely omitted from the COF formula with only a modest reduction in nitrogen potency
- Store in a dry, sealed container like other organic fertilizer ingredients

## Nutrient Composition

Tankage typically provides an NPK ratio of 6-6-0 to 9-5-0 depending
on the source material and processing method. It is one of the most
concentrated organic nitrogen sources available.

## Processing and Safety

Modern tankage is processed through rendering at temperatures above
130 degrees Celsius, eliminating pathogens and reducing odor. The
resulting product is stable, dry, and safe for garden use.

## Application Guidelines

Apply tankage at 0.5-1 kg per 10 square meters. It is particularly
effective as a pre-plant soil amendment for heavy-feeding crops.
Avoid use on root crops immediately before planting.

## Historical Use in Agriculture

Tankage has been used as a fertilizer since the 19th century when
meatpacking byproducts were first recognized for their agricultural
value. Early American farming manuals frequently recommended
tankage as a primary nitrogen source.

## Comparison with Blood Meal

Tankage provides both nitrogen and phosphorus, while blood meal
is primarily nitrogen (12-0-0). For balanced fertility, tankage
is often preferred as a standalone organic amendment.
