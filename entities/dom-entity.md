# DOM (STP)

**Chemical name:** 2,5-Dimethoxy-4-methylamphetamine

**PiHKAL #68** | [[alexander-shulgin]]'s rating: +++

## Overview

DOM is a potent psychedelic amphetamine of the DO series, infamous for its widespread distribution as "STP" in the 1967 Haight-Ashbury scene. It produces powerful visual and emotional effects with a very long duration. The "R" isomer is the more active form, while the "S" isomer is largely inactive as a psychedelic (though it may contribute to physical side effects). DOM served as the template for the entire 2,5-dimethoxy-4-substituted amphetamine family.

## Qualitative Effects

- Extremely strong visual effects, described as transformative in quality
- Color perception fundamentally altered — "more of them," "pulsations," "extreme pastels"
- Deep cognitive and philosophical effects
- Emotional range from euphoria to profound anxiety and negative feelings
- Physical tremor, tight jaw, and body load
- Strong enhancement of music appreciation
- Nausea possible during onset (may clear with regurgitation)
- Erotic enhancement reported
- At high doses: visual hallucinations, alternation between joy and discomfort

## Shulgin's Qualitative Comments

At 4 mg: "THE CLOUDS!!! No visual experience has ever been like this. The meaning of color has just changed completely." At 5 mg: alternating between joy and discomfort; "I knew what it was like to look across the brink to insanity." At 8 mg: "rich curly-imaged eyes-closed show that interlocks closely with music." At 10 mg: "DOM has the glory and the doom sealed up in it." At 12 mg: extremely rapid onset, "body tremor feels like poisoning," then exceptional music, erotic, and fantasy experiences.

## Extensions and Commentary

**The STP Disaster:** DOM was distributed in 1967 Haight-Ashbury under the name STP (originally from a motor oil additive name). Initial tablets contained 20 mg; later reduced to 10 mg. Both were "thoroughly whopping" doses. The slow onset (2 hours to full effect) led users familiar with LSD's rapid onset to take additional doses, resulting in overdoses of up to 30 mg. The slow onset combined with the remarkably long duration caught many users unprepared.

Clinical studies documented rapid tolerance: five volunteers given 6 mg daily for three days showed progressively diminished responses, from "extremely intense" on day one to "felt absolutely nothing" by day three.

The compound was known to the military with chemical warfare code EA-1306, and was part of the Edgewood Arsenal human studies program.

## Safety Warnings

- **Very slow onset** (2 hours to full effect) creates serious redosing danger
- **Extremely long duration** — 14-20 hours
- Physical side effects: tremor, jaw clenching, nausea
- High doses can produce frightening experiences
- Rapid tolerance development
- See [[psychedelic-harm-reduction]] for general harm reduction guidelines
- Legal status: Schedule I in the US and most countries
