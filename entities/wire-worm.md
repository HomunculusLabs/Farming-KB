## Wire Worm

## Description

Wire worms are the larval stage of click beetles (family Elateridae). They are significant soil-dwelling agricultural pests that feed on seeds, roots, and underground plant parts. Wire worms are particularly damaging to germinating seeds and young seedlings, and they can persist in soil for multiple years as larvae. [[molasses-and-sugars-in-compost-tea-brewing]] research, wire worms are notable because they can be managed through biological soil approaches including [[comparison-trichoderma-vs-entomopathogenic-fungi]] delivered via compost tea.

## Classification

- **Order**: Coleoptera
- **Family**: Elateridae
- **Common Genera**: Agriotes, Limonius, Melanotus, Conoderus
- **Life Stage**: Larval (wire worm), Adult (click beetle)

## Key Facts

- Wire worm larvae can live in soil for 2-6 years depending on species, making them persistent pests
- They feed on seeds, roots, tubers, and other underground plant parts
- Wire worms are attracted to reduced oxygen conditions in the range of 5-6 ppm
- They can be managed through entomopathogenic fungi, particularly Beauveria species
- Adding excess sugar to soil can temporarily drive wire worms away by creating [[aact-ph-and-anaerobic-conditions]], but this approach risks damaging seeds and roots
- The fungi that prey on wire worms require aerobic conditions to function effectively

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

### Biological Control via Compost Tea
- AACT can contain Beauveria and other entomopathogenic fungi that are parasitic on wire worm larvae
- Beauveria may be available as a commercial inoculum and can be added directly to AACT
- The fungus should be applied as a soil drench in fall so it has winter to establish and attack larval stages
- Spring applications of 150 liters per hectare provide additional suppression
- Fungi must be active and ready to attack wire worms on contact

### Sugar-Based Management Limitations
- Adding simple sugars feeds bacteria, which consume oxygen and create localized anaerobic zones
- This temporarily drives wire worms away, but does not kill them
- When oxygen returns, wire worms move back to the root zone
- The anaerobic zone can also harm germinating seeds [[soil-moisture-management-and-root-growth]]
- This approach only works in compacted soils and fails when soil has good structure

### Integrated Soil Management
- Maintaining good soil structure with adequate aeration is the foundation of wire worm management
- [[ingham-aact-beauveria-fruit-fly-beneficial-bacteria-inoculant]] in aerobic conditions make the environment unpleasant for wire worms
- As bacteria form glues and microaggregates, they [[query-how-do-fungi-improve-soil-structure]] and oxygen diffusion
- Predator organisms return to well-structured soil, providing additional pressure on wire worms
- Monitoring oxygen levels and soil biology helps predict wire worm pressure

### Practical Application Protocol
- Apply Beauveria-containing compost tea as soil drench
- Fall application is critical — allows fungi to work through winter
- Avoid excessive sugar additions that could create harmful anaerobic conditions
- Monitor soil biology to ensure adequate predator populations are present
- In severely infested fields, combine biological approaches with cultural controls
