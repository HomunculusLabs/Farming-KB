## Exacum affine

## Overview

Exacum affine is a small, fragrant annual or short-lived perennial in the family Gentianaceae, widely sold as the Persian violet or exacum.

It is native to the island of Socotra (Yemen), where it grows as an understory herb in semi-arid shrubland. The species produces masses of small, star-shaped, deep violet-blue flowers with prominent yellow stamens.

Exacum affine is one of the most popular flowering houseplants worldwide. Its compact habit, long bloom period, and fragrance make it a staple of the floriculture industry.

The species was introduced to horticulture in the late 19th century. Modern cultivars have been selected for larger flowers, [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]]. Several million plants are sold annually through [[psilocybe-southeast-asia-pacific]].

Exacum affine is closely related to Exacum arabicum and Exacum molle from the Arabian Peninsula. Molecular phylogenetics places it in a clade of Socotran endemics.

The species epithet "affine" refers to its close relationship (affinity) with other Indian Ocean Exacum species. No subspecies or varieties are currently recognized.

Plants form compact mounds 15–30 cm tall and 15–25 cm wide. Stems are quadrangular, branching freely from the base, and glabrous to sparsely pubescent.

Leaves are opposite, ovate to elliptic, 1–3 cm long, dark glossy green, with three prominent veins. Leaf margins are entire and slightly revolute.

Flowers are solitary or paired in upper leaf axils, 1–2 cm in diameter, with five spreading corolla lobes. The corolla is deep violet-blue to lavender, fading to white at the center.

Five prominent yellow stamens form a conspicuous central cone. The flowers emit a sweet, vanilla-like fragrance, especially in warm conditions.

The fruit is a small, globose capsule containing numerous dust-like brown seeds. Seed count per capsule ranges from 50 to several hundred.

Seeds are among the smallest in the Gentianaceae family. They lack elaiosomes and are primarily dispersed by wind and water.

## Distribution and Habitat

Exacum affine is endemic to the island of Socotra, Yemen, in the western Indian Ocean. The island is a UNESCO World Heritage Site known for its unique flora.

On Socotra, the species grows in shaded rock crevices, on moist banks, and in semi-shaded shrubland. It occurs at elevations from sea level to about 700 m.

The climate is tropical semi-arid with seasonal monsoon influence. Rainfall is sporadic, and the species survives dry periods as seed.

Natural habitat receives approximately 150–300 mm of annual rainfall. Plants grow primarily during the cooler, moister winter season on the island.

Wild populations are not considered threatened. The species is common in suitable habitat across Socotra and is cultivated globally.

Socotra's isolation has produced one of the most remarkable island floras on Earth. About one-third of its plant species are endemic, including Exacum affine.

## Cultivation

Exacum affine is grown as a summer annual or seasonal flowering pot plant in [[comfrey-tea-liquid-fertilizer]] every 2 weeks during active growth. Avoid high-nitrogen feeds that promote leaf growth at the expense of flowers.

Pinching young plants promotes branching and a fuller habit. Most commercial varieties are self-branching and do not require pinching.

Common pests include aphids, whiteflies, and spider mites. Good air circulation and regular inspection prevent serious infestations.

## Ornamental Use

The Persian violet is primarily sold as a disposable flowering houseplant. Its bloom period extends 4–8 weeks under good indoor conditions.

Popular cultivars include 'Midget' (compact, deep blue), 'Blue Champion' (large flowers), and 'White Champion' (pure white flowers). 'Tiddly Winks' is a popular dwarf series in multiple colors.

The species is also used in mixed container plantings, window boxes, and shaded annual borders. In USDA zones 10–11, it can be grown as a perennial ground cover.

Cut flowers last 5–7 days in water. The fragrance makes them suitable for small table arrangements and posies.
