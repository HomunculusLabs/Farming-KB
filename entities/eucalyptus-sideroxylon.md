# Eucalyptus sideroxylon

## Description

Eucalyptus sideroxylon, commonly known as **red ironbark** or **mugga ironbark**, is a medium to tall tree native to eastern Australia. It is distinguished by its persistent, dark, deeply furrowed bark (ironbark) and is renowned for producing one of the hardest and most durable timbers of any eucalypt. The species also features showy pink, red, or white flowers, making it valuable as both a timber and ornamental tree.

## Classification

- **Family:** Myrtaceae
- **Genus:** Eucalyptus
- **Species:** E. sideroxylon
- **Common names:** Red ironbark, mugga ironbark, mugga

## Key Facts

- Medium to tall tree, typically 15–35 m in height
- Dark, deeply furrowed, persistent bark (characteristic ironbark type)
- Showy flowers ranging from pink to red to white, appearing autumn to spring
- Native to inland New South Wales and Queensland, extending into Victoria
- Produces extremely dense, hard, dark red timber
- Adapted to a range of soils including heavy clays and poor sandy loams
- Drought-tolerant and suitable for low-rainfall areas
- Important honey tree, yielding dark, strong-flavoured honey

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

- **Timber:** One of the hardest and most durable Australian timbers; used for sleepers, heavy construction, fence posts, and engineering applications (pages 94, 96)
- **Firewood:** Extremely dense wood provides excellent, long-burning firewood (pages 94, 96)
- **Honey:** Significant nectar source producing dark, robust honey (pages 94, 96)
- **Ornamental:** Showy pink-red flowers make it a popular street and garden tree
- **Shelter and windbreaks:** Dense canopy and [[dighton-fungal-drought-tolerance-plant-water-relations]] suit it for shelterbelt plantings
- **[[comparison-vetiver-grass-vs-bamboo-for-erosion-control]]:** Deep root system helps stabilise soils on eroded sites

## Cultivation Notes

- Tolerates a wide range of soils from heavy clays to sandy loams
- Highly drought-tolerant once established
- Suited to areas with 400–800 mm annual rainfall
- Frost-tolerant to approximately -7°C
- Relatively slow-growing but develops extremely durable timber
- Responds well to coppicing
- Best established from seed or tubestock
