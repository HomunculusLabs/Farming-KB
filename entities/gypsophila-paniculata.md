In the Great Lakes dune systems, Gypsophila paniculata can produce over 13,000 seeds per plant per season. This prolific [[cannabis-controlled-pollination-seed-production]] drives its invasive potential in sandy, open habitats.

## Cultivation

In gardens, Gypsophila paniculata requires full sun and very well-drained, alkaline to neutral soil. It tolerates drought, poor soils, and exposed sites but resents wet conditions, especially in winter.

The deep taproot makes transplanting difficult, so plants should be placed in their permanent position when young. Established plants should not be disturbed once the root system has developed.

Key cultivars include 'Bristol Fairy' (double white, the commercial standard), 'Perfecta' (large double flowers), and 'Flamingo' (double pink). 'Bristol Fairy' accounts for the majority of commercial cut-flower production.

For cut-flower use, stems are harvested when approximately one-third of the buds have opened. Vase life is 7–10 days for fresh stems; dried stems retain their form indefinitely.

The dried flower industry relies heavily on Gypsophila paniculata, with stems air-dried or silica-dried for permanent arrangements. Properly dried flowers maintain their white color for several years.

Plants are hardy to USDA zones 3–9 and require good winter drainage to prevent crown rot. Heavy mulching is not recommended, as it can promote rot at the crown.
