# Isaac Asimov

## Description

Isaac Asimov (1920–1992) was a Russian-American author, biochemist, and one of the most prolific and influential science writers of the 20th century. Mollison quotes Asimov's restatement of the [[laws-of-thermodynamics]] in *Permaculture: A Designers' Manual*: "The total energy of the universe is constant and the total entropy is increasing." This concise formulation introduces the thermodynamic constraint that all [[permaculture-design]] must work within — energy cannot be created from nothing, and all energy transformations produce waste heat (entropy), making efficient energy capture and storage essential.

## Key Facts

- Born Isaak Yudovich Ozimov on January 2, 1920, in Petrovichi, Russia; died April 6, 1992, in New York City.
- Wrote or edited more than 500 books across nearly every category of the Dewey Decimal System.
- Authored *Foundation* series, *I, Robot*, and *The End of Eternity* — among the most influential works in science fiction.
- Earned a PhD in biochemistry from Columbia University in 1948 and served as professor of biochemistry at Boston University.
- His popular science books explained complex scientific concepts — including thermodynamics, genetics, and physics — to millions of general readers.
- The quote cited by Mollison (from *Asimov, 1970*) elegantly summarizes both the first law (energy conservation) and second law (entropy increase) in a single sentence.
- Coined or popularized terms including "robotics," "positronic," and "psychohistory."

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Asimov's thermodynamic formulation has practical implications for cultivation system design:

- **Energy budget constraint:** Since total energy is conserved but entropy increases, every cultivation system must work within the solar energy budget available to it. No amount of technology creates new energy — it can only capture, transform, and store existing energy more or less efficiently.
- **Entropy in agriculture:** Every agricultural operation (tillage, transport, refrigeration, fertilizer manufacture) increases entropy. Industrial agriculture loses enormous energy to entropy. Permaculture design minimizes entropy by stacking functions and reducing unnecessary energy conversions.
- **[[biological-efficiency]]:** Photosynthesis and [[fungal-decomposition]] are among the most efficient energy-capture processes known. Maximizing biological energy conversion (via diverse plantings and healthy soil biology) is thermodynamically superior to mechanical approaches.
- **Soil organic matter:** Each gram of soil organic matter represents captured solar energy. Losing topsoil is not just losing nutrients — it is losing stored energy that took years of biological work to accumulate.
- **Closed-loop thinking:** Asimov's first law (energy conservation) implies that waste from one process must become input for another. In cultivation, this means every output (crop residue, manure, prunings) should feed another process (compost, mulch, animal feed, mushroom substrate).

Mollison uses Asimov's quote as a concise statement of the physical laws that permaculture design must respect and work within.
