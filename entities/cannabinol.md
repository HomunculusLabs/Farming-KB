## Description

Cannabinol (CBN) is a non-psychoactive cannabinoid found in Cannabis that forms as a degradation product of tetrahydrocannabinol (THC). As Cannabis ages or is improperly stored, THC gradually converts to CBN, resulting in decreased psychoactive potency. CBN is one of the first cannabinoids to be isolated and identified from the plant.

## Classification

- **Category**: Cannabinoid (degradation product)
- **Chemical class**: Terpenophenolic compound / Dibenzopyran
- **Molecular formula**: C21H26O2
- **Source**: Cannabis species (formed from THC degradation)
- **Activity**: Non-psychoactive (or minimally active)

## Key Facts

- Degradation product of tetrahydrocannabinol (THC)
- Forms when THC oxidizes over time
- Inactive or minimally psychoactive compared to THC
- The conversion pathway: cannabidiolic acid -> THC -> cannabinol
- This degradation occurs faster in tropical than cooler climates
- Presence of high CBN levels indicates aged or poorly stored Cannabis
- One of several related constituents found in Cannabis resin

## Degradation Pathway

1. Fresh plant contains mainly cannabidiolic acid (inactive precursor)
2. Over time, cannabidiolic acid converts to active THC
3. With further aging, THC degrades to inactive cannabinol
4. This explains why older Cannabis material is less potent
5. The rate of conversion depends on [[spawn-storage-conditions]] and temperature
