## Overview

Anemone nemorosa is a perennial herbaceous plant in the family Ranunculaceae, widely known as wood anemone or windflower. It is native to deciduous woodlands across temperate Europe and western Asia.

The species is a classic spring ephemeral, completing its above-ground [[mandrake-mandragora-officinarum-in-european-folklore]] and traditional herbalism.

The species is exceptionally long-lived for a herbaceous perennial, with individual clones persisting for centuries. Genetic studies have confirmed that some extensive woodland carpets represent single genets that may exceed 1,000 years in age.

In the language of flowers popularized during the Victorian era, the wood anemone symbolized anticipation and fragility. Its tendency to close its petals at night and before rain gave rise to weather-forecasting folklore.

Basal leaves are palmately divided into three deeply toothed segments, each 2 to 5 centimeters long. Stem leaves are arranged in a whorl of three below the single terminal flower.

Flowers are solitary, 2 to 4 centimeters in diameter, with 5 to 7 white petal-like sepals. The sepals are typically white above and often tinged with pink or lavender beneath.

A yellow center formed by the dense cluster of stamens creates a contrasting bullseye pattern visible to pollinating insects. This color contrast enhances pollinator recognition and visitation rates.

Each flower contains numerous stamens surrounding a cluster of free carpels. After pollination, the carpels develop into small achenes approximately 2 to 3 millimeters long, each tipped with a short persistent style.

The achene cluster forms a distinctive spherical head that persists for several weeks after petal drop. Each achene contains a single seed with a small elaiosome that attracts ants for dispersal.

Flowering typically occurs from March through May, with the exact timing dependent on latitude, elevation, and seasonal temperature accumulation. Flowers close at night and during wet weather, opening fully in direct sunlight.

## Distribution and Habitat

Anemone nemorosa is native to most of Europe, extending from the British Isles and the Iberian Peninsula eastward to the Caucasus and western Siberia. It is absent from the Mediterranean extreme south and from Scandinavia north of the Arctic Circle.

In the British Isles, the species is most abundant in southern and central England, becoming scarcer in northern Scotland and western Ireland. Its presence is a key criterion in ancient woodland designation surveys.

The species is an indicator of ancient deciduous woodlands, rarely colonizing recently established tree plantations. It thrives under oak, ash, beech, and hazel on moist but well-drained soils.

Preferred soils are neutral to slightly acidic loams rich in leaf litter [[comparison-honey-bees-vs-native-solitary-bees]], hoverflies, and small beetles.

The above-ground growth period lasts approximately 6 to 8 weeks from emergence to senescence. During this brief window, the plant must photosynthesize enough to sustain the rhizome through the remaining year.
