# Carex rostrata

## Overview

Carex rostrata, commonly known as beaked sedge or blunt-broom sedge, is a perennial rhizomatous sedge in the family Cyperaceae.

It is distinguished by its inflated, bottle-shaped perigynia that taper to a distinct beak.

The species is widespread across boreal and [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]] of the Northern Hemisphere, where it forms extensive sedge mats in shallow water and marshes.

It plays a significant ecological role in [[soil-protozoa-nutrient-cycling-and-mineralization]] carbon storage in northern peatlands.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

- Kingdom: Plantae
- Order: Poales
- Family: Cyperaceae
- Genus: Carex
- Species: Carex rostrata Stokes

The species was described by Jonathan Stokes in 1787.

Stems are stout and triangular, typically 40–90 cm tall, arising from a creeping rhizome.

Leaves are 2–5 mm wide, flat to slightly M-shaped, and usually equal to or slightly shorter than the stems.

The inflorescence is compound, with 2–5 lateral spikes on short peduncles below an elongate terminal spike.

Perigynia are conspicuously inflated, 5–8 mm long, obovate to broadly elliptic, with a biconvex shape and a short, abrupt beak.

The perigynia surface is smooth or slightly papillose, maturing from green to pale brown.

Achenes are tightly enclosed within the inflated perigynium, approximately 2–2.5 mm long.

## Distribution and Habitat

Carex rostrata has a circumboreal distribution across northern North America and Eurasia.

In North America, it ranges from Alaska across Canada to the northern tier of the United States, including the [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]], Great Lakes region, and New England.

Eurasian populations extend from Scandinavia and the British Isles across Russia to Siberia, Mongolia, and northern Japan.

It is one of the most widely distributed sedges in the arctic and subarctic zones.

The species typically grows in shallow standing water, marshes, fens, and along lake margins.

It tolerates water depths from 0 to 50 cm and prefers mineral-rich, circumneutral substrates.

It frequently codominates with Carex aquatilis and Carex lasiocarpa in northern wetland complexes.

## Ecology and Ecological Role

Carex rostrata is a keystone species in boreal fen ecosystems, contributing significantly to primary productivity.

Its creeping rhizomes form dense floating or anchored mats that create stable substrates for other plant species.

These mats slow water flow, trap organic matter, and promote peat accumulation over time.

The species is an important forage resource for moose, muskrats, and waterfowl.

Geese and swans feed on both the rhizomes and young shoots during spring migration.

It provides critical nesting habitat and cover for marsh birds including red-winged blackbird and common snipe.

The inflated perigynia float readily, enabling effective waterborne seed dispersal to new wetland sites.

## Cultivation and Uses

Carex rostrata is used in [[tropical-rainforest-seedling-mycorrhiza-ecological-restoration]] projects aimed at re-establishing native wetland vegetation.

Its tolerance of fluctuating water levels makes it suitable for stormwater treatment wetlands and constructed marshes.
