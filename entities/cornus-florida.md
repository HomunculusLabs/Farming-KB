## Ecology

The drupes are a critical food resource for at least 36 species of birds and numerous mammals. Wild turkey, northern cardinal, robin, and cedar waxwing are among the most frequent avian consumers. White-tailed deer, gray fox, and black bear also feed on the fruit.

Deer browsing of seedlings and saplings is intense and represents a major recruitment bottleneck. In forests with high deer density, dogwood regeneration is often severely limited, shifting the age structure toward older, senescing individuals.

Exclosure studies have demonstrated dramatic recovery of dogwood recruitment when deer are excluded. In one long-term study, dogwood seedling density increased 40-fold within 15 years of deer exclusion in a Pennsylvania forest.

The foliage serves as a larval host for the spring azure butterfly (*Celastrina ladon*) and several moth species. The flowers provide nectar and pollen for early-season native bees and flies when few other woody plants are in bloom.

Fallen dogwood leaves decompose rapidly and contribute significant calcium to the forest floor. This [[bolete-fungi-appalachian-region]] and spread throughout most of the species' range.

The disease spreads through rain-splashed spores and is most virulent during cool, wet spring conditions. Trees growing in full sun or on dry sites generally suffer less damage than those in deep shade.
