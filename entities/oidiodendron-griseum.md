# Oidiodendron griseum

## Description

*Oidiodendron griseum* is a dematiaceous (darkly pigmented) mitosporic fungus in the family Myxotrichaceae, order Onygenales. It is an **ericoid mycorrhizal** fungus, forming symbiotic associations with plants in the Ericaceae (heath family) such as *Calluna*, *Vaccinium*, and *Rhododendron*. The species produces characteristic arthroconidia (segmented spores) in chains and has grey to dark olive colonies on culture media. It is commonly isolated from acidic, peaty soils in heathland and boreal forest ecosystems.

## Key Facts

- Screened among 21 mycorrhizal fungi for **PCB (polychlorinated biphenyl) degradation** capability (Donnelley and Fletcher, 1994).
- As an ericoid mycorrhizal species, it was less effective at PCB degradation than the top-performing ectomycorrhizal species (*[[radiigera-atrogleba]]* and *[[hysterangium-gardneri]]*, which degraded 80% of 2,2'-dichlorobiphenyl).
- Related species *O. maius* produces the enzyme **polygalacturonase** (which degrades pectin) at increased levels in the presence of zinc and cadmium (Martino et al., 2000a).
- Commonly found in heathland soils alongside other ericoid mycorrhizal species.
- Populations of *Oidiodendron* species were shown to **decrease** along heavy metal pollution gradients in Swedish forests (Rühling and Söderström, 1990), indicating sensitivity to metals.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

*Oidiodendron griseum* is significant in several areas of mycological research:

- **Ericoid mycorrhizal ecology**: As a symbiont of Ericaceae, it plays a critical role in nutrient acquisition for heathland plants growing in nutrient-poor, acidic soils.
- **Heavy metal tolerance**: The ericoid mycorrhizal association enables host plants to survive in metal-contaminated soils, with the fungus sequestering heavy metals and preventing translocation to plant tissues.
- **Bioremediation potential**: While less effective than ectomycorrhizal species for PCB degradation, its enzyme systems contribute to understanding how mycorrhizal fungi process recalcitrant organic pollutants.
- **Enzyme physiology**: The enhanced production of polygalacturonase in response to heavy metals in related species suggests adaptive enzyme regulation under stress conditions.
- **Pollution bioindicators**: The decline of *Oidiodendron* populations along pollution gradients makes it useful for monitoring ecosystem health.

The genus *Oidiodendron* represents an important group of ericoid mycorrhizal fungi that enable heathland plants to colonize extreme environments, including metal-contaminated and nutrient-poor sites, making them relevant to both ecological restoration and understanding plant-fungal coevolution.
