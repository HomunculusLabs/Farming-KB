## Tetragonia tetragonioides (Warrigal Cabbage, New Zealand Spinach)

## Description

Tetragonia tetragonioides, commonly known as warrigal cabbage or New Zealand spinach, is a shrubby, creeping herb found in sheltered beaches, salt marshes, arid woodlands, and plains extending into central Australia. It is also native to the coast of New Zealand. It holds the distinction of being the only plant native to Australia to be cultivated internationally as a vegetable. The plant was famously used by Captain Cook's crew to prevent scurvy during their voyage.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Family**: Aizoaceae
- **Genus**: Tetragonia
- **Species**: T. tetragonioides
- **Common names**: Warrigal cabbage, New Zealand spinach, Botany Bay spinach, sea spinach
- **Type**: Prostrate/creeping perennial herb

## Key Facts

- Growth habit: Shrubby, creeping herb forming low mats
- Habitat: Sheltered beaches, salt marshes, arid woodlands, and plains
- Native range: Coastal and inland Australia; coastal New Zealand
- Salt tolerance: Well adapted to coastal and saline conditions
- The only Australian native plant cultivated internationally as a vegetable
- Leaves cooked similarly to spinach
- Succulent young leaves can be eaten raw in salads
- Historically used by Captain Cook's crew to prevent scurvy

## Uses

### Culinary

- Leaves cooked similarly to spinach (blanching recommended)
- Young succulent leaves suitable for raw salads
- Historically adopted by early European settlers as a spinach substitute
- Rich in vitamins (particularly vitamin C) and minerals
- Seeds can also be eaten

### Permaculture Applications

- Ground cover for coastal and saline sites
- Habitat restoration on dunes and salt-affected land
- Edible landscaping in coastal gardens
- Low-maintenance perennial vegetable crop for marginal soils
- Living mulch that suppresses weeds while providing food

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

Warrigal cabbage is a remarkable perennial vegetable for permaculture systems in coastal and semi-arid zones. Its ability to thrive in saline soils and tolerate drought makes it a resilient food source where conventional vegetables struggle. As a creeping ground cover, it helps stabilise soil while producing an edible harvest. It is an excellent candidate for food forests in Mediterranean [[container-growing-vegetables-herbs]] in non-coastal areas
- Generally pest-free and disease-resistant

## Related Species

- Atriplex nummularia (old man saltbush) - saline-tolerant edible shrub
- Suaeda australis (sea blight) - coastal saline vegetable
