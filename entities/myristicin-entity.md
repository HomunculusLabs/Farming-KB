## Myristicin
Myristicin is a naturally occurring phenylpropene compound and the principal
psychoactive constituent of nutmeg ([[alexander-shulgin]].

- **IUPAC name:** 1-Allyl-3-methoxy-4,5-methylenedioxybenzene
- **Molecular formula:** C11H12O3

## Pharmacology
Myristicin exhibits a complex pharmacological profile combining hallucinogenic,
anticholinergic, and monoamine oxidase inhibitory activity:
- **Monoamine oxidase inhibition (MAOI):** Myristicin is a reversible
  inhibitor of MAO-A with moderate potency (IC50 ≈ 50–100 μM). MAO-B
  inhibition is weaker. This activity may contribute to psychoactive effects
  by increasing synaptic monoamine levels, and poses a theoretical risk of
  serotonin syndrome when combined with serotonergic drugs.
- **5-HT2A receptor activity:** Weak partial agonist activity has been
  reported at the 5-HT2A receptor, the primary target of classical
  psychedelics, though affinity is low (Ki > 10 μM).
- **Anticholinergic effects:** Myristicin produces significant
  anticholinergic symptoms in vivo, suggesting muscarinic receptor
  antagonism or inhibition of acetylcholinesterase.
- **GABA-A receptor modulation:** Weak positive allosteric modulation of
  GABA-A receptors has been demonstrated in vitro, which may contribute to
  the sedative and anxiolytic effects reported at low doses.
- **Metabolism:** Hepatic metabolism via CYP1A2, CYP2E1, and CYP3A4.
  O-demethylation yields MMDA (3-methoxy-4,5-methylenedioxyamphetamine),
  a psychedelic amphetamine. This metabolic conversion is inefficient,
  requiring large doses of myristicin to produce pharmacologically
  relevant MMDA levels.
## Psychoactive Effects and Dosage
Recreational nutmeg intoxication, attributed primarily to myristicin, has
been documented since the 16th century:
- **Threshold dose:** Approximately 5–15 g of whole nutmeg seed (containing
  roughly 25–150 mg myristicin). Effects are highly variable between
  individuals due to differences in metabolism and body weight.
