## Chisel Plough Soil Rehabilitation

## Description

Chisel plough soil rehabilitation is a non-inversion tillage technique central to [[earthworms]], bacteria, and fungi multiply in the aerated, organic-matter-rich environment.
- **Root penetration**: Loose, aerated soil allows plant roots to penetrate 30-50 cm in pasture and deeper in forests, accessing water and nutrients previously unavailable.
- **Soil temperature regulation**: Aerated soil stays warmer in winter and cooler in summer, extending growing seasons.
- **Erosion prevention**: Because the surface is little disturbed, roots hold against erosion even after fresh chisel ploughing.

## Pattern and Implementation

- The fundamental pattern rule: drive slightly downhill from valleys to ridges, creating herring-bone patterns. Spines are the valleys; ribs slope outward and downhill.
- This pattern allows water to move subsurface from valleys to ridges, reversing the typical erosion flow pattern.
- Ideal timing is early spring at the end of a gentle rainy period, when soil is neither too dry (producing clods) nor too wet (collapsing back).
- Follow up with permanent deep-rooted plants (forests or prairies) and minimum tillage management to prevent re-compaction.
- Not suitable for coarse sandy soils, cinder soils, dunes, very stony or boulder-soil mixtures, or steep slopes — use biological (root penetration) methods instead.

Effective management requires monitoring soil indicators and responding to changes in
soil structure, organic matter content, and biological activity. Key strategies include
minimizing soil disturbance, maintaining ground cover, and incorporating diverse plantings.
