## Overview

β-Caryophyllene (BCP) is a bicyclic sesquiterpene found in the essential oils of numerous plants, most notably black pepper (*Piper nigrum*), cloves (*Syzygium aromaticum*), cinnamon (*Cinnamomum* spp.), rosemary (*Rosmarinus officinalis*), hops (*Humulus lupulus*), and cannabis (*Cannabis sativa*). It is one of the most abundant terpenes in cannabis, typically comprising 5–15% of the essential oil profile in strains like Girl Scout Cookies, OG Kush, and Bubba Kush.

BCP is distinguished from other terpenes by its unique chemical structure: a cyclobutane ring fused to a trans-cyclooctene ring, making it one of the few natural products containing a cyclobutane moiety. This structural feature is critical to its biological activity.

## Pharmacology

### CB2 Receptor Selectivity

The most pharmacologically significant property of β-caryophyllene is its role as a selective agonist of the cannabinoid CB2 receptor (Gertsch et al., 2008). This landmark discovery established BCP as the first known "dietary cannabinoid" — a compound that activates the endocannabinoid system through dietary consumption without psychoactive effects.

- **CB2 binding**: EC₅₀ ≈ 155 nM, with functional selectivity over CB1 (no significant CB1 binding at concentrations up to 10 µM)
- **Functional activity**: Full or partial agonist depending on assay system; promotes Gi/o protein signaling, inhibiting adenylate cyclase
- **In vivo effects**: CB2-mediated anti-inflammatory and analgesic effects demonstrated in mouse models of carrageenan-induced paw edema, neuropathic pain, and colitis
- **Receptor specificity confirmed**: Effects are absent in CB2-knockout mice, confirming CB2-dependent mechanism

This CB2 selectivity is therapeutically significant because CB2 activation provides anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory benefits without the psychoactive effects associated with CB1 activation.

### Other Receptor Targets

- **PPAR-α**: BCP functions as a PPAR-α agonist, contributing to anti-inflammatory and lipid-metabolism effects
- **PPAR-γ**: Partial agonist activity reported, relevant to anti-diabetic and anti-inflammatory pathways
- **5-HT₁A**: Weak partial agonist activity, may contribute to anxiolytic effects
- **TRP channels**: Weak agonist at TRPV1 and TRPA1, possibly contributing to sensory modulation

## Biological Activities

### Anti-inflammatory

BCP suppresses pro-inflammatory mediators including TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, NF-κB, COX-2, and iNOS expression in multiple cell types (macrophages, microglia, fibroblasts). In animal models, BCP reduces inflammation in arthritis, colitis, pancreatitis, and neuroinflammation. These effects are primarily CB2-mediated but also involve PPAR-α and direct NF-κB inhibition.

### Analgesic

Demonstrated analgesic effects in models of inflammatory pain, neuropathic pain, and visceral pain. The CB2-mediated mechanism avoids opioid-like side effects and addiction potential. Synergistic effects with other cannabis terpenoids and cannabinoids have been reported in the "entourage effect."

### Gastroprotective

BCP reduces gastric lesions, inhibits gastric acid secretion, and increases gastric mucus production. The gastroprotective mechanism involves antioxidant activity, anti-inflammatory action via CB2, and modulation of prostaglandin synthesis.

### Neuroprotective

CB2 activation by BCP reduces neuroinflammation in models of Alzheimer's disease, multiple sclerosis, and Parkinson's disease. BCP reduces microglial activation, amyloid-β accumulation, and neuronal apoptosis in preclinical studies.

### Hepatoprotective

Demonstrated protective effects against acetaminophen-induced, alcohol-induced, and carbon tetrachloride-induced liver injury. Mechanisms include antioxidant enzyme induction (SOD, CAT, GPx), anti-inflammatory activity, and reduction of lipid peroxidation.

### Antimicrobial

BCP exhibits antimicrobial activity against Gram-positive bacteria (*Staphylococcus aureus*, *Bacillus subtilis*), some Gram-negative bacteria, and *Candida albicans*. Activity is moderate compared to conventional antibiotics but may contribute to the overall antimicrobial profile of essential oils containing BCP.

## Occurrence in Cannabis

In *Cannabis sativa*, β-caryophyllene is biosynthesized from farnesyl pyrophosphate (FPP) via the enzyme β-caryophyllene synthase. It is one of the dominant sesquiterpenes in most cannabis chemotypes, contributing to:

- **Aroma**: Woody, peppery, spicy notes with earthy undertones
- **Entourage effect**: CB2 activation modulates the overall pharmacological profile, potentially enhancing anti-inflammatory properties of THC and CBD
- **Strain markers**: High-BCP strains include Girl Scout Cookies, OG Kush, Bubba Kush, Skywalker OG, and Super Lemon Haze
- **Caryophyllene oxide**: The oxidation product (epoxide) of BCP, is the primary marker used in drug-sniffing dogs to detect cannabis

## Safety and Toxicology

BCP is classified as GRAS (Generally Recognized As Safe) by the US FDA as a food additive and flavoring agent. It has a long history of human dietary exposure through consumption of black pepper, cloves, cinnamon, and other common spices. Oral LD₅₀ in rats exceeds 5 g/kg. No significant toxicity or mutagenicity has been reported at normal dietary or therapeutic doses.

## Metabolism

BCP is metabolized primarily by cytochrome P450 enzymes (CYP2C9, CYP2C19) to β-caryophyllene oxide (caryophyllene epoxide) and subsequently to diols and hydroxy acids. The oxide metabolite retains biological activity and is detectable in plasma for several hours after oral administration. Bioavailability is relatively low (estimated 10–30% oral) due to first-pass metabolism, but tissue distribution is broad with significant accumulation in the liver, brain, and adipose tissue.

## Biosynthesis

In plants, β-caryophyllene is synthesized from farnesyl pyrophosphate (FPP) by the enzyme β-caryophyllene synthase (TPS21 in Arabidopsis, various TPS family members in other species). The cyclization mechanism involves initial ionization of FPP, followed by C1–C10 cyclization to form the germacradienyl cation, and then a second C1–C6 cyclization to form the characteristic cyclobutane ring. This bicyclic architecture is unusual among sesquiterpenes and is responsible for many of BCP's unique biological properties.

## Agricultural and Industrial Applications

Beyond its pharmacological importance, β-caryophyllene has significant commercial applications:

- **Food and beverage industry**: Widely used as a flavoring agent, contributing spicy, woody, and peppery notes to foods, beverages, and alcoholic drinks. FDA GRAS status supports its use as a food additive (CFR 21 §172.515).
- **Fragrance industry**: A common ingredient in perfumes, soaps, and cosmetics, valued for its warm, spicy, woody aroma with balsamic undertones.
- **Insecticidal and repellent**: Demonstrates insect repellent and anti-feedant activity against mosquitoes, ticks, and agricultural pests. The mechanism involves disruption of insect olfactory receptors and GABA-gated chloride channels.
- **Preservative**: Antimicrobial properties make it useful as a natural food preservative, inhibiting growth of spoilage organisms and foodborne pathogens.
- **Cannabis industry**: As a major cannabis terpene, BCP is increasingly isolated and used in terpene formulations, vape products, and topical preparations. Its CB2 activity adds therapeutic claims to cannabis wellness products.
- **Crop protection**: BCP emission from plants serves as an indirect defense mechanism, attracting natural enemies of herbivorous insects (parasitic wasps, predatory mites) through tritrophic interactions.

## Drug Interactions

Given BCP's activity at multiple molecular targets, theoretical drug interactions should be considered:

- **CYP2C9/2C19 substrates**: BCP may inhibit these enzymes, potentially increasing plasma concentrations of drugs metabolized by them (warfarin, phenytoin, omeprazole, diazepam)
- **CB2-active compounds**: Additive or synergistic effects with other CB2 agonists, including CBD and Δ9-THC
- **Anticoagulants**: Enhanced antiplatelet effects possible due to reported antiplatelet aggregation activity
- **P-glycoprotein**: Limited evidence suggests BCP may modulate P-gp efflux, potentially affecting drug bioavailability

These interactions remain largely theoretical at dietary exposure levels but may be relevant at supplemental or therapeutic doses.
