## Description

Skunk cabbage refers primarily to the eastern skunk cabbage ([[philodendron-selloum]].

### Pollination Strategy

The combination of heat and odor serves sophisticated pollination functions:

1. **Fly attraction:** The warmth and unpleasant smell attract flies and heat-seeking insects to the pollen.
2. **Vortex pollination:** The shape of the first leaf creates a vortex from wind that is contained within the hot leaf. This vortex carries pollen down to the unpollinated lower flowers, achieving fertilization in cold winds without the presence of insects.
3. **Scent dispersal:** The heat acts as a thermal chimney, dispersing odorous scents over greater distances.

### Related Thermogenic Plants

Mollison groups several thermogenic species:

- **Arum lilies (Arum spp.):** Most or all species store fats for thermogenesis.
- **Philodendron selloum:** Flowers may register 46 C (115 F) when ambient air is 4 C (39 F).
- **Crocuses:** Heat up to 15 C (27 F) above ambient air temperature.

All share the strategy of generating heat to attract pollinating flies in cold conditions.

### Ecology

- **Habitat:** Wetlands, seeps, swamp margins, stream edges. Requires consistently moist soil.
- **Emergence:** One of the first plants to emerge in late winter/early spring in its native range.
- **Decay odor:** The common name derives from the skunk-like odor of the flowers and crushed leaves.
- **Contractile roots:** Pull the stem deeper into the soil each year, making established plants very difficult to transplant.
