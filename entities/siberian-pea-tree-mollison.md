# Siberian Pea Tree (Caragana arborescens)

## Description

The Siberian pea tree is a hardy, nitrogen-fixing deciduous shrub or small tree extensively used in permaculture as a poultry forage crop. [[bill-mollison]] identifies it as a critical survival food source for chickens during periods when other forage is unavailable, noting that peasants in Siberia historically fed their poultry on this tree alone.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Family**: Fabaceae (legume family)
- **Genus**: Caragana
- **Species**: C. arborescens
- **Common Names**: Siberian pea tree, Siberian peashrub, Caragana

## Key Facts

- **Nitrogen fixation**: As a member of the Fabaceae family, the Siberian pea tree fixes atmospheric nitrogen through root-associated Rhizobium bacteria, improving soil fertility in its vicinity.
- **Seed production**: Produces abundant pea-like pods containing small seeds that are highly nutritious for poultry and other birds. Chickens access the seeds directly from the tree or from fallen pods.
- **Hardiness**: Extremely cold-hardy, native to Siberia and Central Asia. Tolerates severe winters, drought, and poor soils, making it suitable for harsh climates where few other forage trees survive.
- **Protein content**: Seeds contain significant protein, comparable to or exceeding grain-based feeds.
- **Seasonal reliability**: Provides seed forage during periods when mulberries and other seasonal forages are unavailable.

## Role in Permaculture Systems

Mollison describes the Siberian pea tree as a key component of zone two forage systems designed for poultry:

- **Poultry forage**: Chickens go on and off the tree, paying it less attention when other food sources like mulberries are abundant, but relying on it heavily during lean periods.
- **Self-sustaining feed**: The tree reduces or eliminates the need to purchase external grain feed for poultry flocks.
- **Soil improvement**: Nitrogen fixation benefits surrounding plants, particularly in integrated chicken-orchard systems where fruit trees and forage shrubs are interplanted.
- **Drought resilience**: Described by Mollison as the species that "should carry us through a drought."

## Cultivation Notes

- Readily established from seed or cuttings.
- Tolerates a wide range of soil conditions including alkaline soils.
- Can be planted as hedgerows or scattered through chicken forage areas.
- Minimal maintenance required once established.

## Ecological Significance

Represents a prime example of Mollison's philosophy of designing integrated animal-forage systems that replace conventional grain-based feeding with perennial, self-maintaining food sources.
