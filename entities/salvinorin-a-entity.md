# Salvinorin A

## Overview

**Salvinorin A** (systematic name: (2S,4aR,6aR,7R,9S,10aS,10bR)-9-(acetyloxy)-2-(furan-3-yl)-6a,10b-dimethyl-4,10-dioxododecahydro-1H-benzo[f]isochromene-7-carboxylic acid methyl ester) is a neoclerodane-type diterpene and the principal psychoactive compound in *Salvia divinorum*, a mint family (Lamiaceae) plant endemic to the Sierra Mazateca of Oaxaca, Mexico. It is the most potent naturally occurring psychedelic compound known, with effective doses in the 200–500 µg range when vaporized. Unlike most psychedelics, salvinorin A is not a tryptamine or phenethylamine and does not act on serotonin receptors — instead, it is a highly selective agonist at the kappa-opioid receptor (KOR).

## Chemical Identity

- **Molecular Formula:** C₂₃H₂₈O₈
- **Molecular Weight:** 432.47 g/mol
- **IUPAC Name:** Methyl (2S,4aR,6aR,7R,9S,10aS,10bR)-9-(acetyloxy)-2-(furan-3-yl)-6a,10b-dimethyl-4,10-dioxo-2,3,4,4a,5,6,6a,7,8,9,10,10a,10b-tetradecahydro-1H-benzo[f]isochromene-7-carboxylate
- **CAS Number:** 83729-01-7
- **Chemical Class:** Neoclerodane trans-clerodane diterpenoid
- **Appearance:** White crystalline powder
- **Solubility:** Soluble in acetone, ethyl acetate, chloroform; poorly soluble in water

### Chemical Structure
The neoclerodane skeleton is a rearranged clerodane diterpene featuring a trans-decalin core fused to a lactone ring, with characteristic acetoxy and furanyl substituents. The C-2 furan ring and the C-4 carbonyl group are critical pharmacophores; modification of either significantly reduces KOR affinity.

## Pharmacology

### Receptor Pharmacology
- **Primary Target:** κ-opioid receptor (KOR, KOR-1/OPRK1) — full agonist
- **Ki at KOR:** ~1 nM (exceptionally high affinity)
- **EC₅₀ at KOR:** ~1–10 nM
- **Selectivity:** Highly selective — negligible affinity for µ-opioid receptor (MOR), δ-opioid receptor (DOR), 5-HT2A, dopamine D2, or cannabinoid CB1 receptors
- **Intrinsic Activity:** Full agonist at KOR (Emax ≈ 100% relative to U-50488)

### Mechanism of Action
Salvinorin A binds to KOR with remarkable potency and selectivity. KOR activation produces:
- **Dissociative effects:** Profound alteration of consciousness, sense of bodily boundary, and spatial orientation
- **Introspective states:** Deeply altered internal experiences often described as "contacting other realities"
- **Anti-addictive potential:** KOR agonism modulates dopamine release in the mesolimbic pathway, counteracting the rewarding effects of drugs of abuse
- **Neuroendocrine effects:** Increases prolactin release and modulates HPA axis activity

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Route of administration:** Most commonly smoked/vaporized (sublingual and buccal absorption possible but less efficient due to poor water solubility); oral bioavailability is extremely low due to extensive first-pass metabolism
- **Onset (smoked):** 20–60 seconds
- **Peak effects:** 1–3 minutes post-inhalation
- **Duration:** 5–15 minutes (smoked); 30–60 minutes (sublingual/chewed quid method)
- **Metabolism:** Esterase-mediated hydrolysis of the C-2 acetate group produces salvinorin B (inactive), followed by hepatic glucuronidation
- **Half-life:** ~60 minutes (rapid clearance)

### Behavioral Effects in Humans
- **Dose-dependent spectrum:**
  - **Light (200–500 µg):** Mild perceptual changes, laughter, altered spatial perception
  - **Moderate (500 µg–1 mg):** Significant dissociation, vivid closed-eye imagery, altered sense of self, synesthesia-like experiences
  - **Strong (1–2 mg+):** Complete dissociation from consensus reality, experiences of merging with objects or environments, ego dissolution, experiences of "other dimensions" or entities; amnesia for the experience is common
- **Reversal:** Effects can be partially or fully attenuated by KOR antagonists such as naltrexone

## Natural Source

**Salvia divinorum** (Epling & Játiva-M.) is the sole known natural source of salvinorin A:
- A perennial herb in the Lamiaceae (mint) family, growing to ~1 m tall
- Endemic to a small region of the Sierra Mazateca in Oaxaca, Mexico, at elevations of 300–1800 m
- The Mazatec people call it *ska María Pastora* ("leaves of Mary the Shepherdess") or *ska Pastora*
- Traditionally used by Mazatec curanderos in divinatory rituals by chewing fresh leaves or preparing an aqueous infusion (though infusion is notably less effective)
- Dried leaf typically contains 0.1–0.4% salvinorin A by dry weight
- Propagated almost exclusively through vegetative cloning; the plant rarely sets viable seed and is believed to be a cultivar of human origin

## Legal Status

Salvinorin A and *Salvia divinorum* have been scheduled or regulated in numerous jurisdictions:
- **United States:** Not federally scheduled under the Controlled Substances Act (as of 2026), but regulated or banned in many states; the DEA has listed it as a "drug of concern"
- **Australia:** Schedule 9 (prohibited substance) since 2002
- **Many European countries:** Regulated or banned, including Germany, Italy, Sweden, Denmark, Finland, Latvia, Lithuania, Romania, and others
- **Japan:** Regulated since 2015
- **United Kingdom:** Controlled under the Psychoactive Substances Act 2016

## Research and Therapeutic Potential

### Preclinical Findings
- **Anti-addiction:** Demonstrated reduction in cocaine, nicotine, and alcohol self-administration in rodent models via KOR-mediated modulation of mesolimbic dopamine
- **Anti-inflammatory:** KOR activation produces anti-inflammatory and neuroprotective effects in animal models
- **Depression models:** Mixed results — KOR agonism can produce both antidepressant and pro-depressant (dysphoric) effects depending on context and brain region
- **Neuropathic pain:** Analgesic properties demonstrated in rodent models

### Clinical Research Status
- No large-scale human clinical trials of salvinorin A have been completed as of 2026
- The extremely short duration of action and intense subjective effects present practical challenges for clinical use
- Semi-synthetic KOR agonists derived from salvinorin A (e.g., ethoxymethyl-salvinorin B, herkinorin analogs) are under investigation as potential therapeutic agents with modified pharmacokinetic profiles
- Interest in salvinorin A as a tool for understanding consciousness and KOR neuropharmacology remains high

### Synthetic Analogs
Numerous semi-synthetic derivatives have been prepared for structure-activity relationship studies:
- **Salvinorin B:** Deacetylated metabolite; essentially inactive at KOR (Ki > 10 µM)
- **2-Methoxymethyl-salvinorin B:** Increased potency and duration relative to salvinorin A
- **Herkinorin:** Modified at C-2 with a phenylacetyl group; acts as a µ-opioid receptor agonist — a remarkable switch in receptor selectivity
- **β-Tetrahydropyran salvinorin B:** Extended duration of action in animal models

## Safety Profile

- **Physical toxicity:** Low — salvinorin A shows no significant acute toxicity in animal models; LD50 in mice exceeds 10 mg/kg (far above effective human doses)
- **Psychological risks:** The extreme intensity and disorienting nature of the experience can cause significant anxiety, panic, and dysphoria, particularly at high doses or in unprepared users
- **Dependence potential:** No evidence of physical dependence; KOR agonists are generally considered to have anti-addictive rather than addictive properties
- **Harm potential:** Primarily psychological — risk of accidental injury during dissociation; no confirmed deaths attributable to salvinorin A alone
