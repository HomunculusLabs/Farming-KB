# Artemisia absinthium

**Artemisia absinthium**, commonly known as **wormwood**, **absinthe wormwood**, or **grand wormwood**, is a herbaceous perennial plant in the family Asteraceae. Native to temperate regions of Eurasia and Northern Africa, it is best known as the primary flavoring ingredient of the legendary spirit absinthe and as a traditional medicinal herb with a long history of use across European, Middle Eastern, and Asian herbal traditions. The plant thrives in dry, open, well-drained habitats including roadsides, waste ground, abandoned fields, rocky slopes, and disturbed sites. ## Economic and Commercial Importance

Beyond absinthe production, wormwood has significant commercial value in several industries.
The essential oil is used in the fragrance and cosmetics industry as a component of perfumes, soaps, and aromatherapy blends, valued for its fresh, herbaceous, and slightly bitter aromatic profile.
The dried herb is commercially available as a culinary bittering agent, a tea herb, and a botanical ingredient in vermouth, bitters, and other aperitif formulations.
Global demand for wormwood-derived products has increased in recent years, driven by the craft spirits revival and the growing popularity of absinthe, herbal bitters, and botanical spirits.
Commercial cultivation of wormwood for essential oil production is centered in Europe (particularly France, Spain, and Hungary) and Central Asia, with the essential oil commanding prices of $50–150/kg depending on quality and thujone content.
