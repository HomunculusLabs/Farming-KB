## Psilocybe subcaerulipes

**Psilocybe subcaerulipes** is a rare and little-known psychoactive species of mushroom in the family Hymenogastraceae. It is native to [[psilocybe-caerulipes]], from which it is distinguished by subtle but consistent differences in cap morphology, stipe characteristics, and microscopic features, as well as by its distinct geographic distribution separated by the Pacific Ocean. The prefix *sub-* denotes its status as a closely allied species rather than a variety, subspecies, or form of [[peter-mantle-liberty-cap-chromatographic-discovery-1969]]), [[psilocybe-caerulipes]], which is named for its characteristically blue-staining foot). There is no annulus (ring) at any stage of development.

### Spores and Microscopic Features
Spores are broadly ellipsoid to slightly rhomboid in face view and ellipsoid in side view, smooth, with a distinct germ pore at the apex. They measure approximately 9 to 12 µm by 6 to 7.5 µm, placing them in the medium size range for the genus. The [[spore]] deposit is dark purplish-brown to reddish-brown, typical of section Psilocybe. Basidia are four-spored and clavate, measuring 16 to 22 µm. Cheilocystidia are abundant at the gill edges, ventricose to lageniform (flask-shaped), with swollen bases and narrow, elongated necks, measuring 20 to 35 µm. Pleurocystidia are similar in shape but less common, scattered on the gill faces. The pileipellis is a cutis with occasional erect elements.

## Distribution and Habitat

P. subcaerulipes is known primarily from Japan, where it has been collected in various forested regions of the country including Honshu, Shikoku, and Kyushu. It has also been reported from other parts of East Asia, including South Korea and possibly parts of northeastern China, though documented collections from outside Japan remain scarce and poorly documented.
