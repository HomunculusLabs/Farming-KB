# Interceptor Drain

## Description

An interceptor drain is a specialised earthwork designed to prevent overland waterflow and waterlogging in dryland and semi-arid landscapes. Unlike diversion drains or swales, interceptor drains are specifically engineered to totally intercept overland flow and redirect it to streams or valley run-offs. They were developed by Harry Whittington of West Australia as a response to desertification caused by rising saline water tables in agricultural land.

## Classification

- **Category**: [[water-management]] earthwork
- **Function**: Subsoil water interception and salinity control
- **Design lineage**: Permaculture earthworks / dryland rehabilitation

## Design Specifications

- **Depth**: Always 1.5–2.5 m (5–8 feet) deep, deeper than swales or diversion drains
- **Gradient**: Made to flow at 1:600 to 1:1500 cross-slope
- **Construction**: Involves ramming subsoil layers hard against the downhill bank using a bulldozer blade, effectively sealing the downhill wall
- **Spacing**: Blocks of 100 m (330 feet) to maximum 300 m (985 feet) wide
- **Orientation**: Cross-slope, similar to swales but with sealed downhill wall

## Key Facts

- The sealed downhill bank prevents or impedes water seepage, isolating large blocks of soil from waterlogging and salt seepage
- Effectively stops both overland flow and salt water seepages in shallow sand seams
- After installation, trees can be planted in previously desertified soils
- Deeper sand seams carrying salty water can be trenched and blocked with vertical plastic barriers backed by compacted clay
- Cuts off seepage from salt lakes and can divert early salted overland flow around saltpans, allowing later fresh floodwater to fill pans or shallow lakes

## Relevance to Cultivation

Interceptor drains are critical for rehabilitating salt-affected and desertified farmland. By preventing saline water tables from rising through capillary action into the root zone, they restore soil productivity. The technique is especially relevant in:

- **Dryland agriculture** where rising saline groundwater threatens crop production
- **Desertification reversal** in semi-arid regions with shallow saline aquifers
- **Permaculture design** as a primary earthwork for water and salinity management
- **Agroforestry establishment** in degraded landscapes where trees previously could not survive

The interceptor drain represents one of the most effective large-scale remediation strategies for landscapes damaged by over-clearing and irrigation-induced salinity, a widespread problem in Australia, the Middle East, and parts of Central Asia.

## Relationship to Other Earthworks

Interceptor drains differ from swales and diversion drains in three key ways: they are always deep (1.5–2.5 m), their downhill bank is mechanically compacted to be impermeable, and their purpose is water exclusion rather than water harvesting. They work in conjunction with spreader banks, which distribute overflow water evenly downslope.
