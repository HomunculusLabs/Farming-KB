## Humic Acid

## Description

Humic acids are a group of complex organic molecules that form as a result of the decomposition and transformation of plant, animal, and microbial residues. They are a principal component of humus, the dark brown to black material in soil that results from the breakdown of organic matter. Humic acids are extracted from humus using alkaline solutions and are characterized by their high molecular weight, complex aromatic ring structures, and ability to chelate (bind) minerals and nutrients. In compost tea and soil management, humic acid serves as both a fungal food source and a chelating agent that improves nutrient availability.

## Classification

- **Category**: Humic substance / soil organic matter component
- **Chemical nature**: High molecular weight organic polymer (10,000 to 100,000+ Da)
- **Components**: Part of the humic substance family alongside fulvic acid and humin
- **Solubility**: Soluble in alkaline solutions, insoluble in acidic conditions (precipitates below pH 2)
- **Source materials**: Leonardite (oxidized lignite), compost, peat, vermicompost

## Key Facts

- Humic acids contain functional groups (carboxyl, phenolic, hydroxyl, quinone) that enable them to chelate metal ions, making them central to [[nutrient-cycling-in-soil]] systems.
- They are extracted commercially primarily from leonardite, a naturally occurring oxidized form of lignite coal found in deposits across North Dakota and other regions.
- Elaine Ingham's field guide extensively covers humic acid as an AACT ingredient, noting its dual role as both a fungal food and mineral chelator.
- Humic acids stimulate fungal growth in compost tea more effectively than bacterial growth, making them ideal for producing fungal-dominant teas.
- The chelation properties of humic acid improve plant uptake of micronutrients including iron, zinc, manganese, and copper by keeping them in soluble, plant-available forms.
- Humic acids buffer soil pH, improve soil structure through aggregation, and increase water-holding capacity.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

### Compost Tea Ingredient

- Humic acid is listed as a key ingredient in fungal-dominant compost tea recipes in Ingham's guide, typically added at rates of 0.5–2% of total tea volume.
- When combined with fish hydrolysate, humic acid provides an excellent fungal food base that promotes rapid hyphal growth in AACT.
- The guide notes that humic acid extraction methods matter — cold-water extracted humic acids retain more biological activity than heat-processed products.

### Chelation and Nutrient Availability

- Humic acids chelate mineral nutrients, forming organic complexes that plants can absorb more readily than inorganic salts.
- This chelation effect is particularly important for iron availability in alkaline soils where iron tends to precipitate as unavailable forms.
- The guide includes detailed discussion of chelation in plant physiology, explaining how humic acid facilitates mineral transport across cell membranes.

### Fungal Growth Promotion

- Humic acid molecules serve as a carbon substrate that preferentially supports fungal rather than bacterial growth.
- In combination with Beauveria (entomopathogenic fungus), humic acid enhanced fungal colonization and insect suppression in studies cited by Ingham.
- This makes humic acid an essential ingredient for growers producing fungal-dominant compost teas for perennial crops, trees, and woody plants.

### Soil Application Benefits

- Improves soil aggregation and structure through interaction with clay particles and cations.
- Increases [[cation-exchange-capacity]] (CEC), helping soils retain nutrients against leaching.
- Stimulates root growth and branching, improving plant access to water and nutrients.

### Distinction from Related Materials

- **Humus vs. Humic Acid**: Humus is the complete decomposed organic matter in soil, while humic acid is a specific extractable fraction of humus.
- **Fulvic Acid**: A lower molecular weight humic substance that is soluble at all pH levels and more biologically active in some contexts.
- **Humin**: The insoluble fraction of humic substances that remains in soil as a stable carbon pool.

### Quality Assessment

- High-quality humic acid products should have a humic acid content of 12–15% or higher.
- The guide emphasizes testing humic acid sources for biological activity, as processing methods can destroy beneficial properties.
- Color, solubility, and smell are practical indicators of humic acid quality.
