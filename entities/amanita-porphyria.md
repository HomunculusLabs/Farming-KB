## Overview

**Amanita porphyria** is a medium-sized [[ectomycorrhizal-mushroom-fruiting-ecology]] in the family
Amanitaceae. It is widely distributed across temperate and [[fungal-diversity-boreal-forests-taiga]] of
Europe and Asia.

The species is inedible and causes gastrointestinal distress when consumed.
It is frequently confused with the deadly **[[amanita-phalloides]]** and the
edible **[[amanita-rubescens]]** by inexperienced foragers.

The epithet *porphyria* refers to the purplish-grey coloration of the cap
surface, which is the most reliable [[palmer-mushroom-hunting-field-identification]] character.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:**

- Kingdom: Fungi
- Division: Basidiomycota
- Class: Agaricomycetes
- Order: Agaricales
- Family: Amanitaceae
- Genus: *Amanita*
- Species: *A. porphyria*

Authority: (Alb. & Schwein.) P. Kumm. (1871).

Synonyms include *Agaricus porphyrius* Alb. & Schwein. and *Amanita
porphyria* var. *porphyria*.

The basidiomata are medium-sized, with caps reaching 5–10 cm in diameter.
Young caps are convex to subconical, becoming plano-convex or depressed at
maturity.

The cap surface is distinctly grey to purplish-grey, darker at the center
and paler toward the margin. It is covered with thin, appressed, greyish
volval remnants that often wash off with rain.

The gills are free, crowded, and white to cream-colored. They do not stain
on bruising, which distinguishes this species from *Amanita rubescens*.

The stipe is 6–12 cm tall and 1–2 cm thick, white to pale grey, and bears
a membranous, pendant annulus near the apex. The base is bulbous and
surrounded by a short, membranous, sac-like volva.

[[comparison-spore-print-collection-vs-spore-syringe-production]] is white. Basidiospores are broadly ellipsoid to subglobose,
measuring 7–9 × 5.5–7.5 µm. Spores are inamyloid and thin-walled.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Amanita porphyria* is found across much of temperate Europe and northern
Asia. Its range extends from Scandinavia and the British Isles eastward
through central Europe to Siberia and Japan.

The species is most common in coniferous and mixed forests dominated by
spruce (*Picea* spp.), fir (*Abies* spp.), and pine (*Pinus* spp.). It also
occurs under birch (*Betula* spp.) and oak (*Quercus* spp.).

Fruiting occurs from late summer through autumn, typically August to
October in northern Europe. It is more common in cool, moist seasons and at
higher elevations.

The mushroom forms [[ectomycorrhizal-associations]] with a broad range of
forest trees. It prefers acidic to neutral soils with well-developed humus
layers.

## Toxicity

*Amanita porphyria* is poisonous. Consumption causes primarily
gastrointestinal symptoms including nausea, vomiting, abdominal cramps, and
diarrhea within 30 minutes to 2 hours of ingestion.
