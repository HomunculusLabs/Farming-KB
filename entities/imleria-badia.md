# Imleria badia

## Overview

Imleria badia, the bay bolete, is an edible [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]]. It fruits from mid-summer through late autumn beneath conifers
and hardwoods, and remains one of the most commonly collected wild edible
mushrooms in central and northern Europe.

Long classified as *Xerocomus badius*, it was moved to *Imleria* by Vizzini
in 2014 based on multigene phylogenetic analysis.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Taxonomy:** Basidiomycota → Agaricomycetes → Boletales → Boletaceae →
Imleria badia (Fr.) Vizzini. Synonyms include *Boletus badius* Fr. (1821)
and *Xerocomus badius* (Fr.) Kühner ex Gilb. (1931).

**Cap:** 4–15 cm, convex to plano-convex, smooth to finely velvety when
young, often cracking with age. Color bay-brown to chestnut-brown,
darkening when wet.

**Pore surface:** Adnate to slightly decurrent, pale yellow becoming
olive-yellow. Pores 1–2 per mm, angular. Bruises slowly bluish-green.

**Stipe:** 5–12 cm × 1–3 cm, cylindrical, pale brown with fine
longitudinal striations. Context white to pale yellow, firm.

**Spores:** 10–14 × 4–5.5 µm, fusoid to ellipsoid, smooth. Print
olive-brown. Flesh bluing faintly; taste mild, odor pleasant.

## Distribution and Habitat

Broad circumboreal distribution: Europe, Asia (China, Japan, Korea), and
[[picea-abies]]*), fir (*Abies* spp.), and also birch
and oak in mixed woodlands. Fruits on acidic to neutral forest soils from
July through November, most abundantly in wet autumns following warm summers.

## Edibility and Culinary Use

An excellent edible with mild, pleasantly nutty flavor. Cap flesh is firm
and holds texture well during cooking — suitable for sautéing, drying, and
preserving. The stipe becomes fibrous in mature specimens and is often used
for stock or discarded.

Unlike some boletes, the bay bolete does not develop bitterness when dried,
making it one of the best candidates for preservation. Dried caps rehydrate
well with intensified umami character. Pore tubes of mature specimens can
become slimy; removing them before cooking is recommended.

## Bioactive Compounds

- **Badione A and B:** Bis-p-quinone pigments with moderate antibacterial
  activity against Gram-positive bacteria.
- **Variegatic acid and xerocomic acid:** Pulvinic acid derivatives
  responsible for bruising color changes.
- **[[ergosterol]]:** Predominant sterol and provitamin D₂ precursor.
- **[[aact-tannins-terpenes-phenolic-compounds-wood-composting-ingham]]:** Catechol derivatives with antioxidant activity.
- **Polysaccharides:** Beta-glucan fractions showing immunomodulatory
  activity, stimulating macrophage response in vitro.

## Cultivation

Cultivation remains challenging due to obligate ectomycorrhizal lifestyle.
Cannot be grown on sterile substrate like saprotrophic species.
