# Master Cho Han-Kyu

Master Cho Han-Kyu (1935-2018) was a Korean farmer and educator who developed Korean Natural Farming (KNF), a complete agricultural system that uses naturally occurring microorganisms and plant-derived inputs instead of chemical fertilizers and pesticides. He founded the Janong Natural Farming Institute in Korea.

## Background

- Born in Korea, 1935
- Studied agriculture in Japan (where he was exposed to EM technology by Teruo Higa)
- Returned to Korea and developed his own approach, emphasizing locally sourced inputs
- Founded the Janong Natural Farming Institute
- Trained thousands of farmers across Asia, the Pacific, and eventually worldwide
- His son Cho Youngsang continues to promote KNF globally
- Passed away in 2018

## Core Philosophy

Cho's system rests on several principles:

1. **Use what nature provides locally** — No imported inputs; everything comes from the farm or immediate surroundings
2. **Observe and follow nature** — Let nature's timing guide farming decisions
3. **Indigenous microorganisms (IMO)** — Capture and multiply the microorganisms already adapted to your local environment
4. **Fermentation over decomposition** — Fermented inputs are more stable, more bioavailable, and don't attract pests
5. **Plants feed microbes, microbes feed plants** — The plant-microbe partnership is central
6. **Zero waste** — Every output becomes an input somewhere else
7. **Minimize external inputs** — The farm should become increasingly self-sufficient

## Key Innovations

### Indigenous Microorganisms (IMO)
- Collect microorganisms from your local environment (forest soil, leaf mold)
- Culture them on cooked rice, then expand on brown sugar
- IMO contains bacteria, fungi, actinomycetes adapted to local conditions
- More effective than commercial EM because it's locally adapted
- See [[indigenous-microorganisms-imo]] for full protocol

### Fermented Plant Juice (FPJ)
- Extract plant sap from fast-growing plant tips
- Ferment with brown sugar at 1:1 ratio
- Provides growth hormones, enzymes, and nutrients
- Different plants for different growth stages (see [[nutritive-cycle-theory]])

### Oriental Herbal Nutrients (OHN)
- Fermented extracts of garlic, ginger, licorice, cinnamon, Angelica, and other herbs
- Used for plant immunity and pest deterrence
- Functions like a plant immune booster

### Fish Amino Acid (FAA)
- Fermented fish waste provides nitrogen and amino acids
- Particularly effective during vegetative growth phase

### Natural Farming Input System
Cho developed a complete toolkit of inputs for every stage of plant growth and every farming need. See [[knf-preparations]] for the full catalog.

## The Nutritive Cycle Theory

Cho's most distinctive contribution is the Nutritive Cycle — matching fertilizer inputs to the plant's natural growth rhythm:

1. **Vegetative/Reproductive (V/R)** — Early growth: nitrogen-dominant
2. **Changeover** — Transition to flowering: calcium, phosphorus
3. **Reproductive/Storage (R/S)** — Fruiting and maturation: potassium, phosphorus
4. **Senescence** — End of life: minimal inputs, prepare for next cycle

See [[nutritive-cycle-theory]] for detailed application.

## Global Influence

KNF has spread to:

- **Hawaii** — where it's widely adopted by both conventional and organic growers
- **Thailand, Vietnam, Philippines** — significant adoption in Southeast Asia
- **East Africa** — promoted as a low-cost alternative to chemical agriculture
- **United States** — growing interest among cannabis growers and market gardeners
- **Japan** — influenced and was influenced by EM technology

## Differences from Similar Systems

| Feature | KNF | JADAM | EM/Bokashi | Fukuoka |
|---------|-----|-------|------------|---------|
| Microbe source | Local IMO | Local JMS | Purchased EM | Natural succession |
| Inputs | Many fermented | Fewer, simpler | EM + bran | No inputs |
| Approach | Systematic | Simplified KNF | Inoculation | Minimalist |
| Complexity | Medium-high | Low | Low | Very low |
| Origin | Korea | Korea (student of Cho) | Japan | Japan |

Cho's system sits between Fukuoka's radical minimalism and conventional organic's input-heavy approach. It offers a systematic, repeatable methodology while keeping inputs local and affordable.
