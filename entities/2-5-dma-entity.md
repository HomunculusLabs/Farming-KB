# 2,5-DMA

2,5-DMA (2,5-Dimethoxyamphetamine; DMA) is an amphetamine derivative that serves as a critical precursor to several potent [[doc]]|DOC, and [[don-entity]]|DON.

Radio-halogenation of N-substituted homologues of 2,5-DMA with hypoiodite or hypofluorite has been used in the search for radio-labeled brain blood flow agents.

## Industrial Significance

Shulgin noted a mystery regarding the commercial production of 2,5-DMA.

Drug Enforcement Administration production quotas regularly listed quantities of 2,5-DMA "in the many tens of millions of grams," rivaling medical mainstays such as codeine and morphine.

This staggering quantity far exceeds any conceivable demand for research or clandestine use.

The compound has been reported to have applications in the photographic industry, though Shulgin had no definitive confirmation of this use.

## Synthesis

The synthesis begins with 2,5-dimethoxybenzaldehyde, which is condensed with nitroethane and ammonium acetate in acetic acid.

The resulting 1-(2,5-dimethoxyphenyl)-2-nitropropene is reduced with lithium aluminum hydride in ether.

The free base is converted to the hydrochloride salt by saturation with anhydrous HCl gas.

The nitrostyrene intermediate has been commercially available from various sources at different times.

## Comparison with DMA Isomers

There are six possible dimethoxyamphetamine isomers.

2,4-DMA (meta-orientation) shows primarily stimulant effects with increased susceptibility to electrophilic attack at the 5-position.

3,4-DMA (catecholamine pattern) is the positional isomer of dopamine/norepinephrine and has been explored for psychedelic activity at high doses.

2,3-DMA, 2,6-DMA, and 3,5-DMA have been studied pharmacologically in animals but not in humans.

Among all DMA isomers, only 2,5-DMA has achieved significant commercial production and wide availability.
