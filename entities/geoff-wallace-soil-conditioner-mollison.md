# Geoff Wallace Soil Conditioner

## Description

The [[geoff-wallace]] Soil Conditioner is a specialised agricultural implement designed for non-inversion soil aeration and rehabilitation. Described extensively by [[bill-mollison]] in the [[permaculture]] Designers' Manual, it represents a key technology for broadacre soil restoration without the destructive effects of conventional ploughing. The implement creates air pockets beneath the soil surface, stimulating biological activity and root growth while leaving the surface largely undisturbed.

## Classification

- **Type**: Agricultural implement / soil conditioning machinery
- **Category**: Non-inversion tillage equipment, soil rehabilitation tool
- **Origin**: Australia
- **Inventor**: Geoff Wallace

## Key Facts

- The Wallace Soil Conditioner uses a circular coulter to slit the ground, followed by a steel shoe that opens the soil to form an air pocket without turning the soil over — preserving surface structure, organic matter, and existing vegetation.
- The soil must be at the correct moisture level for operation: neither too dry (which produces clods) nor too wet (which causes the soil to collapse back).
- Seed can be dropped into the thin furrows created by the coulter, allowing beans, corn, and other crops to be seeded directly through existing grass without bare cultivation.
- No fertiliser or top-dressing is required — the beneficial effect comes primarily from entrapped air beneath the earth and the subsequent biological activity of [[soil-life]] and plant roots.
- Mollison records that Geoff Wallace measured up to 13°C (25°F) temperature increase in treated versus untreated soils in autumn, generated by biological activity and the insulating air pockets.
- The implement is part of Mollison's broader soil rehabilitation sequence: chisel at shallow depth (5-10 cm), graze or cut, then chisel again at increasing depth (23-30 cm), repeating through a one-year cycle.
- After a year of this treatment, a 23 cm (9 inch) blanket of aerated, living soil is created, with thousands of subsurface tunnels leading from valley to ridge for water infiltration.

## Relevance to Cultivation

- **Soil rehabilitation**: Provides a mechanised approach to restoring compacted, degraded soils on a broadacre scale without the damage of conventional ploughing.
- **Pasture improvement**: Can be used on existing pasture without taking land out of production; the pasture thickens and weeds are swamped by grasses and legumes.
- **[[water-management]]**: The herring-bone pattern of chisel lines (running slightly downhill from valleys to ridges) creates subsurface channels that direct water into the soil profile rather than allowing overland flow.
- **Minimum tillage cropping**: Grain growers can use the Wallace conditioner to move to minimum tillage methods with fallows of pasture between crops.
- **Thermal management**: Increased soil temperature from aeration and biological activity enables earlier planting and more frost-sensitive crops.
- **Nitrogen fixation**: Nodulation of nitrogen-fixing bacteria is greatly increased by the improved aeration and soil structure.

## Design Principles

- Drive the tractor or team slightly downhill, making herring-bones of the land: the spines are the valleys and the ribs slope out and down-slope.
- Start in the high valley bottoms and drive slightly downhill to the ridges.
- Begin with shallow passes (5-10 cm) and increase depth in subsequent passes (15-30 cm).
- Time operations for early spring at the end of a gentle rainy period.
- Can be combined with seed boxes for direct seeding of tree seed, soybeans, millet, sunflowers, melons, and other crops.
