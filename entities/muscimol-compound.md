# Muscimol

## Overview

**Muscimol** (5-aminomethyl-3-hydroxyisoxazole) is the principal psychoactive compound in *Amanita muscaria* (fly agaric) and related *Amanita* species. Unlike tryptamine-based psychedelics (psilocybin, DMT, LSD), muscimol acts as a potent and selective **GABA_A receptor agonist**, producing sedation, dissociation, altered perception, and vivid dreamlike states. Muscimol has experienced a resurgence of interest due to commercial *Amanita muscaria* products and emerging therapeutic research.

Muscimol is a bicyclic compound with a 3-hydroxyisoxazole ring and aminomethyl group — a rare naturally occurring isoxazole.

## Biosynthesis and Occurrence

### Natural Source

Muscimol is produced by **decarboxylation of ibotenic acid** (α-amino-3-hydroxy-5-isoxazoleacetic acid), the primary amino acid in fresh *Amanita* mushrooms:

- **Ibotenic acid** → (decarboxylation) → **Muscimol**
- Conversion occurs upon drying, heating, or aging of mushroom tissue
- Proper preparation (drying at 70–80°C, boiling, extended air-drying) converts most ibotenic acid to muscimol, reducing toxicity while preserving psychoactive effects

### Concentration in *Amanita muscaria*

| Tissue | Ibotenic Acid | Muscimol |
|--------|--------------|----------|
| Fresh cap | 0.03–0.12% | Trace |
| Dried cap | 0.01–0.05% | 0.01–0.06% |
| Dried, heated cap | <0.01% | 0.05–0.1% |

Also found in *A. pantherina*, *A. gemmata*, and *A. regalis*.

## Pharmacology

### Mechanism of Action

Muscimol is a **direct GABA_A receptor agonist** — one of very few naturally occurring compounds with this mechanism:

- Binds the GABA binding site on GABA_A receptors, mimicking endogenous GABA
- Activates chloride ion channels, producing neuronal hyperpolarization
- Unlike benzodiazepines (allosteric modulators), muscimol directly opens the channel
- Selective for GABA_A over GABA_B; some affinity for GABA_C
- Distinct from classical psychedelics (5-HT2A agonism), explaining sedative-dissociative character

### Pharmacokinetics

| Parameter | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| Absorption | Well absorbed orally; onset 30–90 minutes |
| Distribution | Crosses blood-brain barrier efficiently |
| Peak plasma | 1–3 hours post-ingestion |
| Duration | 4–8 hours (oral) |
| Metabolism | Hepatic glucuronidation |
| Half-life | ~2–3 hours |

### Comparison to Related Compounds

| Compound | Mechanism | Key Difference |
|----------|-----------|----------------|
| **Muscimol** | Direct GABA_A agonist | Natural; dissociative-sedative |
| **Ibotenic acid** | Glutamate receptor agonist | Excitatory precursor; causes nausea |
| **Gaboxadol (THIP)** | GABA_A agonist (δ-selective) | Synthetic analog; former sleep aid |
| **Zolpidem** | GABA_A allosteric modulator | Synthetic; more amnestic |
| **Psilocybin** | 5-HT2A agonist | Serotonergic; classic psychedelic |

## Effects

### Subjective Experience

**Common effects (moderate doses):**
- Warmth and heaviness in the body; drifting dreamlike cognitive state
- Altered perception of size and distance (micropsia/macropsia)
- Vivid closed-eye imagery; time distortion; enhanced music appreciation

**Higher dose effects:** Deep dissociation; lucid-dream-like scenarios; amnesia, possible delirium

**After-effects:** Lingering calm (12–24 hours); improved sleep; residual drowsiness

### Dose Range (Dried *A. muscaria*)

| Dose | Equivalent Muscimol | Effect |
|------|---------------------|--------|
| 0.5–2 g | ~0.5–2 mg | Threshold; mild relaxation |
| 3–5 g | ~3–5 mg | Light; dreamy, enhanced imagery |
| 5–10 g | ~5–10 mg | Moderate; dissociation, altered cognition |
| 10–20 g | ~10–20 mg | Strong; deliriant potential |

Doses vary enormously by mushroom potency and preparation. Always start low.

## Safety and Toxicology

### Acute Toxicity

- LD50 (rats, oral): ~45 mg/kg — relatively wide therapeutic index
- Most adverse effects from *A. muscaria* are attributable to **ibotenic acid**, not muscimol

### Adverse Effects and Contraindications

- Nausea/vomiting (especially with high ibotenic acid content)
- Dizziness, ataxia, confusion, dissociative panic
- Theoretical respiratory depression at very high doses
- **Do NOT combine** with other GABAergic substances (benzodiazepines, alcohol, gabapentinoids)
- Avoid with severe liver/kidney impairment or seizure disorders
- Pregnancy and breastfeeding — insufficient safety data

### Legal Status

- Unscheduled in most jurisdictions; not controlled under UN Convention
- *A. muscaria* legal in most countries (exceptions: Australia, Netherlands); legal ≠ safe

## Emerging Therapeutic Research

- **Sleep disorders**: GABA_A agonism suggests insomnia potential; gaboxadol (synthetic analog) briefly marketed as sleep aid
- **Anxiety and alcohol use disorder**: Anxiolytic via GABAergic mechanism; preclinical evidence for reduced craving and withdrawal
- **Microdosing**: Anecdotal sub-threshold use for anxiety and sleep — no clinical evidence yet
