# Hericium erinaceus

**Hericium erinaceus**, commonly known as **lion's mane mushroom**, **bearded tooth fungus**, **satyr's beard**, or **pom pom blanc**, is a species of edible and medicinal mushroom belonging to the tooth fungus group. Renowned for both its culinary value and its remarkable neuroprotective properties, lion's mane has become one of the most studied functional mushrooms in modern mycological and biomedical research. Its cascading white spines give it a striking resemblance to a lion's mane, and its ability to stimulate **Nerve Growth Factor (NGF)** synthesis makes it unique among natural products.

## Taxonomy and Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Russulales
- **Family:** Hericiaceae
- **Genus:** *Hericium*
- **Species:** *H. erinaceus*

The genus *Hericium* contains several species, including *H. coralloides* (coral hedgehog), *H. americanum* (bear's head), and *H. alpestre* (mountain lion's mane). *H. erinaceus* is the most widely cultivated and commercially available species in the genus.

### Synonyms and Common Names
- **English:** Lion's mane, bearded tooth, bearded hedgehog, pom pom mushroom, white hedgehog
- **Japanese:** Yamabushitake (山伏茸, "mountain priest mushroom")
- **Chinese:** Hóutóugū (猴头菇, "monkey head mushroom")
- **Korean:** Norugungdengi-i (노루궁뎅이)
- **German:** Igelstachelbart ("hedgehog beard")
- **French:** Crinière de lion

## Morphology

*Hericium erinaceus* is one of the most distinctive mushrooms in the fungal kingdom:

- **Fruiting body:** 5–25 cm (2–10 in) in diameter, irregularly spherical to hemispherical. Composed of cascading, soft, pendant spines rather than gills or pores.
- **Spines:** 1–5 cm long, 1–2 mm thick, white to cream-colored, hanging downward in tufted clusters. The spines are fleshy and edible, not brittle.
- **Stipe:** Short or absent; fruiting body attaches directly to the substrate or develops a small lateral base.
- **Flesh:** White, firm when young, becoming softer and stringy with age. Mild, sweet, seafood-like flavor (often compared to lobster or crab).
- **Spore print:** White to pale cream
- **Spores:** Spherical to broadly ellipsoid, smooth, 5–7 × 5–6 µm, amyloid

Young specimens are entirely white. With age, spines may yellow or brown slightly. The mushroom is a **saprotroph** that decomposes dead and dying hardwood.

## Distribution and Habitat

Native to North America, Europe, and Asia. Found growing on:

- **Substrates:** Dead or dying hardwood trees and logs, especially oak (*Quercus* spp.), walnut (*Juglans* spp.), beech (*Fagus* spp.), maple (*Acer* spp.), sycamore (*Platanus* spp.), and birch (*Betula* spp.)
- **Habitat:** Temperate and boreal hardwood forests, particularly old-growth areas with abundant fallen timber
- **Season:** Late summer through autumn (August–November in the Northern Hemisphere)
- **Ecology:** Primary wood decomposer (white rot fungus); breaks down cellulose and lignin

The species is relatively uncommon in the wild and can be confused with other *Hericium* species. All *Hericium* species are edible, reducing the risk of dangerous misidentification.

## Phytochemistry

Lion's mane contains several classes of bioactive compounds:

### Hericenones (from the fruiting body)
- **Hericenones C–H:** Aromatic compounds isolated from the fruiting body
- **Hericenone C and D:** Identified as stimulators of NGF synthesis in vitro
- **Structure:** Complex benzyl alcohol derivatives with unique terpenoid-like side chains

### Erinacines (from the mycelium)
- **Erinacines A–I:** Cyathane-type diterpenoids isolated primarily from the mycelial culture
- **Erinacine A:** The most potent NGF stimulator identified; crosses the blood-brain barrier in animal models
- **Structure:** Cyathane skeleton with various oxygen-containing functional groups

### β-Glucans (Polysaccharides)
- **(1→3)-β-D-glucan with (1→6)-β branches:** Primary immunomodulatory compounds
- Concentration: 15–30% of dry weight in quality extracts
- Mechanism: Activate macrophages, NK cells, and dendritic cells via Dectin-1 receptor

### Other Compounds
- **Hericerin:** Antioxidant sterol
- **4-Hydroxyphenethyl alcohol:** Antimicrobial
- **Palmitoylethanolamide (PEA):** Endogenous fatty acid amide with anti-inflammatory and neuroprotective properties
- **Various amino acids, minerals (especially zinc, selenium, germanium)**

## Neuroprotective and Cognitive Effects

The most significant and extensively studied property of lion's mane is its neuroprotective activity:

### Nerve Growth Factor (NGF) Stimulation
Lion's mane is one of the few natural substances demonstrated to stimulate NGF synthesis:

- **Mechanism:** Hericenones and erinacines activate the JAK/STAT and PI3K/Akt signaling pathways in astrocytes, upregulating NGF mRNA expression and protein secretion
- **NGF function:** Essential for the survival, maintenance, and regeneration of neurons; promotes neurite outgrowth and synaptic plasticity
- **Blood-brain barrier:** Erinacine A has been confirmed to cross the BBB in rodent models, reaching the hippocampus and cortex

### Cognitive Enhancement
Animal and preliminary human studies suggest cognitive benefits:

- Improved spatial and visual recognition memory in mice (Mori et al., 2009)
- Enhanced learning performance and hippocampal neurogenesis in aged rats
- A 2009 Japanese clinical trial (Mori et al.) showed significant cognitive improvement in adults with mild cognitive impairment (MCI) after 16 weeks of lion's mane supplementation (3 g/day dried powder), with benefits declining after cessation
- A 2023 systematic review found moderate evidence for cognitive enhancement in older adults

### Neurodegenerative Disease Research
- **Alzheimer's disease:** Amyloid-β plaque reduction and improved cognitive function in transgenic mouse models. Mechanism involves reduced neuroinflammation and enhanced cholinergic neurotransmission.
- **Parkinson's disease:** Protective effects against dopaminergic neuron loss in 6-OHDA and MPTP mouse models
- **Peripheral neuropathy:** Preclinical evidence of improved nerve regeneration after peripheral nerve injury
- **Multiple sclerosis:** Immunomodulatory effects that may reduce neuroinflammation

## Immunomodulatory Effects

Lion's mane exhibits broad immunomodulatory activity:

- Activation of innate immune cells (macrophages, NK cells, neutrophils)
- Enhanced production of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, TNF-α) at appropriate doses
- Anti-tumor activity in vitro and in animal models (colon cancer, gastric cancer, leukemia) — attributed primarily to β-glucan-mediated immune activation
- Reduced systemic inflammation markers (IL-6, CRP) in clinical studies

## Gastrointestinal Benefits

- **Stomach mucosa protection:** Lion's mane extract has been shown to protect against ethanol-induced gastric ulcers in animal models, attributed to increased mucus secretion and antioxidant activity
- **Gut microbiome modulation:** Preliminary evidence suggests prebiotic effects, promoting growth of beneficial *Bifidobacterium* and *Lactobacillus* species
- **Inflammatory bowel disease:** Anti-inflammatory effects in colitis models

## Cultivation

Lion's mane is commercially cultivated worldwide and is relatively straightforward to grow:

### Substrate Methods
- **Hardwood sawdust:** Oak, beech, or maple sawdust supplemented with wheat bran (75:25 ratio) is the standard commercial substrate
- **Supplemented blocks:** 5 kg blocks with 65% moisture content
- **Log cultivation:** Inoculated into hardwood logs (oak, beech); 1–2 year fruiting cycle

### Cultivation Parameters
- **Inoculation:** Grain spawn (rye, millet, or wheat) at 5–10% inoculation rate
- **Spawn run:** 14–21 days at 20–24°C (68–75°F), high humidity (85–95%), fresh air exchange
- **Pinning:** Temperature drop to 16–20°C (61–68°F), high humidity (90–95%), increased fresh air exchange and indirect light
- **Fruiting:** 7–14 days from pinning to harvest
- **Yield:** Biological efficiency of 80–120% (0.8–1.2 kg fresh per kg dry substrate)
- **Harvest:** When spines begin to elongate and before they turn brown

## Culinary Uses

Lion's mane is highly regarded in the kitchen:

- **Flavor profile:** Mild, sweet, slightly earthy with a texture remarkably similar to crab or lobster meat when cooked
- **Preparation:** Best when pan-fried in butter or oil until golden brown. Also excellent in soups, stews, tempura, and as a seafood substitute
- **Texture:** Firm and meaty when young; can become stringy and tough if overcooked or harvested late
- **Nutrition:** Rich in protein, fiber, potassium, zinc, and various B vitamins; low in calories and fat
- **Storage:** Fresh mushrooms refrigerate for 7–10 days. Dried mushrooms store for 1–2 years.

## Dosage for Supplementation

Based on clinical and preclinical evidence:

- **Dried fruiting body powder:** 1,000–3,000 mg/day (divided doses)
- **Dual extract (fruiting body + mycelium):** 500–1,500 mg/day
- **Standardized extract (for erinacines/hericenones):** Follow manufacturer instructions; typically 500–1,000 mg/day
- **Duration:** Minimum 4–8 weeks for cognitive benefits; sustained use recommended

## Safety

- **Generally recognized as safe (GRAS):** Lion's mane has a long history of culinary use with no significant toxicity concerns
- **Allergic reactions:** Rare cases of contact dermatitis or respiratory allergy in mushroom handlers
- **Bleeding risk:** Theoretical concern due to anticoagulant effects of some constituents; caution with blood-thinning medications
- **Autoimmune conditions:** Immune-stimulating effects may theoretically exacerbate autoimmune conditions (limited evidence)
- **Pregnancy and lactation:** Insufficient safety data; consult healthcare provider
