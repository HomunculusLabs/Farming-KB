# Hericium erinaceus (Lion's Mane Mushroom)

## Overview

**Hericium erinaceus**, commonly known as lion's mane mushroom, is an edible and medicinal fungus belonging to the tooth fungus group (*Hydnaceae* family). It is recognized by its distinctive cascading white spines that resemble a lion's mane, and has been used for centuries in traditional Chinese and Japanese medicine. The species is native to North America, Europe, and Asia, where it grows as a saprotroph on hardwood trees, particularly oaks, walnuts, and beeches.

## Morphology

Lion's mane forms large, spherical fruiting bodies measuring 5–25 cm in diameter. The spines (teeth) are 1–5 cm long, white to cream-colored when fresh, and turn yellowish-brown with age. The flesh is firm and white, with a texture often compared to seafood — particularly crab or lobster — when cooked. Spores are white, roughly spherical, and approximately 5–7 µm in diameter.

Unlike most mushrooms with gills or pores, *Hericium* species produce spores on hanging teeth-like structures (an *hydnoid* hymenophore), a trait shared with other members of the *Hericiaceae* and related families.

## Key Bioactive Compounds

### Hericenones (Erinacines)

Hericenones are fatty acid derivatives isolated from the fruiting body, while erinacines are cyathane-type terpenoids found primarily in the mycelium. Both classes are small enough to cross the blood-brain barrier and are the primary compounds responsible for lion's mane's neurotrophic effects.

### β-Glucans (Polysaccharides)

The fruiting body contains significant quantities of (1→3)(1→6)-β-D-glucans, which modulate the immune system through interaction with macrophages, NK cells, and dendritic cells. These polysaccharides are responsible for the species' recognized immunomodulatory properties.

### Other Constituents

- **Hericenones A–H**: Terpenoid compounds from the fruiting body
- **Erinacines A–I**: Cyathane-type sesquiterpenes from mycelial culture
- **Oleanolic acids**: Anti-inflammatory triterpenoids
- **Amino acids**: Contains all essential amino acids, particularly high in glutamic acid

## Pharmacology & Health Effects

### Neurotrophic Activity

Lion's mane is one of the few known natural sources of compounds that stimulate **Nerve Growth Factor (NGF)** synthesis. Erinacines and hericenones promote neuronal differentiation and neurite outgrowth in vitro. Animal studies demonstrate improved cognitive function, memory retention, and nerve regeneration following peripheral nerve injury.

### Neuroprotective Effects

Research suggests potential benefits in neurodegenerative conditions:

- **Alzheimer's disease**: Amyloid-β plaque reduction observed in murine models; small human trials show modest cognitive improvement
- **Parkinson's disease**: Dopaminergic neuron protection demonstrated in cell culture studies
- **Peripheral neuropathy**: Nerve regeneration accelerated in animal wound models

### Cognitive Enhancement

Several double-blind, placebo-controlled human trials have reported:

- Improved scores on the Revised Hasegawa Dementia Scale (HDS-R)
- Enhanced recognition memory in healthy older adults
- Reduced mild cognitive impairment (MCI) symptoms after 16 weeks of supplementation

### Immune Modulation

β-glucan fractions exhibit immunostimulatory activity, enhancing macrophage activation and cytokine production. This has led to investigation as an adjunct therapy in oncology, particularly for gastric and colorectal cancers.

### Gastrointestinal Health

Traditional use for gastric ulcers is supported by studies showing:

- Protection of gastric mucosa from ethanol-induced damage
- Reduction of *Helicobacter pylori* colonization
- Anti-inflammatory effects on intestinal epithelial cells

## Cultivation

### Substrate Requirements

Lion's mane is cultivated on supplemented hardwood sawdust, typically a mix of:

- Oak or beech sawdust (base substrate)
- Wheat bran or rice bran (supplement, 15–20% dry weight)
- Gypsum (calcium sulfate, 2% for pH buffering)

Optimal substrate moisture content is 60–65%. The species is a primary decomposer, meaning it can colonize fresh wood without prior decomposition by other fungi.

### Environmental Parameters

| Parameter | Range |
|-----------|-------|
| Spawn run temperature | 20–25°C |
| Fruiting temperature | 16–20°C |
| Relative humidity | 85–95% |
| Fresh air exchange (FAE) | Moderate to high |
| Light | Indirect ambient (50–200 lux) |
| pH (substrate) | 5.0–6.0 |
| CO₂ (fruiting) | < 800 ppm |

### Fruiting Cycle

Spawn colonization takes 14–21 days. After full colonization, a cold shock (12°C for 24–48 hours) can initiate pinning. Primordia form within 3–7 days, and fruiting bodies mature in 7–14 days. Expected biological efficiency is 100–150% (fresh weight of mushrooms per dry weight of substrate).

### Harvesting & Storage

Harvest when spines begin to elongate but before they turn brown. Fresh mushrooms store 7–14 days refrigerated. Dehydration at 35–40°C preserves bioactive compounds better than higher temperatures. Dried lion's mane reconstitutes well and is stable for 12+ months when stored airtight and away from light.

## Culinary Uses

Lion's mane is a choice edible with mild, sweet flavor and seafood-like texture. Preparations include sautéing in butter, breaded frying as crab cake substitute, pulling for plant-based seafood, and drying for teas.

## Safety & Contraindications

Lion's mane is generally recognized as safe (GRAS). Exercise caution with mushroom allergies; rare contact dermatitis reported. Potential anticoagulant interactions. No established safe dosage for pregnancy.

## Conservation Status

Wild *Hericium erinaceus* has declined in parts of Europe and North America due to habitat loss and overharvesting; listed as **Vulnerable** in several European countries. Cultivation is strongly encouraged over wild harvesting.
