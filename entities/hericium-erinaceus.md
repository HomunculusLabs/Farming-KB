# Hericium erinaceus

**Lion's Mane** | **Monkey's Head Mushroom** | **Yamabushitake** (山伏茸)

Hericium erinaceus is a species of edible and medicinal mushroom belonging to the tooth fungus group (family Hericiaceae, order Russulales). Renowned for its cascading white spines that resemble a lion's mane, it has been used for centuries in traditional Chinese and Japanese medicine and has become one of the most researched nootropic fungi in modern science.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Russulales
- **Family:** Hericiaceae
- **Genus:** *Hericium*
- **Species:** *H. erinaceus*

First described by Carl Peter Thunberg in 1783 as *Hydnum erinaceus*, it was later reclassified into the genus *Hericium* by Persoon in 1797. Several cryptic species within the *Hericium erinaceus* complex have been proposed based on phylogenetic analyses, though the taxonomy remains unsettled.

## Morphology

The fruiting body is distinctive and unlikely to be confused with toxic species:

- **Form:** Rounded to irregular mass, 5–25 cm across
- **Spines:** Long (1–6 cm), white to cream, cascading downward, borne from a single branching base
- **Flesh:** White, firm when young, becoming tough and fibrous with age
- **Spore print:** White
- **Odor:** Pleasant, mildly fruity or seafood-like when fresh
- **Habitat:** Saprotrophic and parasitic on hardwoods, especially oak (*Quercus* spp.), walnut (*Juglans*), beech (*Fagus*), and maple (*Acer*)

The spines hang in tufted clusters, differentiating it from other *Hericium* species like *H. coralloides* (which has branched spines) and *H. americanum* (which has more robust, shorter spines growing from a branched base).

## Distribution and Ecology

Native to North America, Europe, and Asia, *H. erinaceus* is found in temperate and boreal forests. It fruits in late summer through autumn, typically after cool rains. The fungus plays an important ecological role as a primary decomposer of dead hardwood and can also act as a wound parasite on living trees, causing a white rot of the heartwood.

In permaculture and forest gardening, Lion's Mane is valued for its ability to break down lignin and cellulose in woody substrates, converting low-nutrient timber into protein-rich biomass. It can be cultivated on supplemented hardwood sawdust or logs.

## Bioactive Compounds

The medicinal properties of *H. erinaceus* are attributed to two major classes of compounds:

### Erinacines

Erinacines (A–I, K, and S) are cyathane-type sesquiterpenoids from the **mycelium**. Erinacine A is the most potent NGF stimulator, active at nanomolar concentrations.

### Hericenones

Hericenones (C–H) are aromatic compounds from the **fruiting body**. Hericenones C and D stimulate NGF synthesis in cultured astrocytes.

### β-Glucans

Significant (1→3)-β-D-glucan and (1→6)-β-D-glucan polysaccharides, activating macrophages, NK cells, and dendritic cells.

### Other Compounds

- **Hericenols:** Antioxidant aromatic compounds
- **4-Hydroxy-2-ethoxy-5-methoxybenzaldehyde:** Anti-inflammatory
- Antimicrobial peptides and proteins

## Pharmacology and Clinical Research

### Neuroprotective Effects

- **NGF stimulation:** Erinacines and hericenones cross the blood-brain barrier, upregulating NGF synthesis, promoting neurite outgrowth and myelination.
- **Cognitive function:** Double-blind RCTs in Japan showed significant improvements in MCI patients after 16 weeks at 3 g/day (Mori et al., 2009).
- **Neurodegeneration:** Protective effects against amyloid-β toxicity, MPTP-induced Parkinson's pathology, and ischemic damage in animal models.
- **Peripheral nerve regeneration:** Promotes regeneration of crushed nerves in rat models.

### Anti-inflammatory and Immunomodulation

- Reduces pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6); modulates NF-κB and MAPK pathways
- Activates complement system; anti-tumor activity through immune potentiation

### Gastrointestinal Health

- Gastroprotective effects against ulcers; inhibits *H. pylori* in vitro
- Protects intestinal mucosa and modulates gut microbiota

## Cultivation

*Hericium erinaceus* is commercially cultivated worldwide and is one of the more rewarding species for home cultivation:

### Substrate

- Hardwood sawdust (oak, beech, or maple) supplemented with wheat bran (10–20%) and gypsum (1–2%)
- Moisture content: 60–65%
- Bag or bottle cultivation at 20–25°C
- Fruiting temperature: 15–20°C (cool temperatures essential for quality)
- High humidity (85–95%) and fresh air exchange required during fruiting
- Harvest 7–10 days after primordia appear, before spines elongate excessively

### Log Cultivation

- Inoculate oak or walnut logs (10–20 cm diameter) in spring
- Incubation: 12–18 months
- Force fruiting by soaking logs in cold water for 24–48 hours

## Culinary Uses

When young and fresh, Lion's Mane has a texture and flavor often compared to lobster or crab meat:

- Sautéed in butter with garlic and thyme
- Breaded and fried as a seafood substitute
- Added to soups and cream sauces
- Dried and powdered for supplementation

The flavor is mild, sweet, and slightly earthy. Overcooking makes the texture rubbery. Fresh specimens are vastly superior to dried for culinary purposes.

## Safety and Contraindications

*H. erinaceus* is generally recognized as safe (GRAS). However:

- Rare cases of allergic contact dermatitis from handling fresh specimens
- Theoretical risk in individuals with bleeding disorders due to antiplatelet activity
- Limited safety data for pregnant and breastfeeding women
- May interact with anticoagulant and immunosuppressant medications

## Conservation Status

While commercially cultivated and not considered threatened globally, wild populations face pressure from habitat loss and overharvesting. Sustainable foraging practices include cutting rather than pulling fruiting bodies and leaving smaller specimens to sporulate.
