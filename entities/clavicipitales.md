# Clavicipitales

## Description

The Clavicipitales (now placed within the order Hypocreales in modern taxonomy)
is an order of ascomycete fungi that includes some of the most ecologically and
economically significant plant-associated fungi in the world. Members of this
order include grass endophytes (*Epichloe*, *Neotyphodium*, *Balansia*), ergot
fungi (*Claviceps*), insect pathogens (*Cordyceps*, *Beauveria*), and plant
pathogens of both grasses and dicots. The unifying characteristic of many
Clavicipitales is their production of biologically active secondary metabolites,
particularly alkaloids, that profoundly influence the ecology of their hosts and
the herbivores that feed on them.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Sordariomycetes
- **Order:** Clavicipitales (now subsumed into Hypocreales in contemporary
  classification)
- **Key Families:** Clavicipitaceae, Cordycipitaceae, Ophiocordycipitaceae

## Key Facts

- Members of the Clavicipitales are the dominant [[fungal-endophytes]] of
  cool-season grasses (Poaceae, subfamily Pooideae). Clay (1990) listed
  *Acremonium* (*Neotyphodium*), *Atkinsoniella*, *Balansia*, *Epichloe*, and
  *Myriogenospora* as five genera in the Clavicipitales that act as grass
  endophytes.
- These fungi produce a diverse array of alkaloids including ergot alkaloids,
  indole-diterpenes (lolitrems), loline alkaloids, and peramine, that deter
  insect and mammalian herbivory.
- *Epichloe* species can suppress host plant reproduction (causing "choke
  disease") while simultaneously enhancing vegetative growth, representing a
  conditional mutualism.
- *Balansia* species infect warm-season grasses and similarly produce alkaloids
  that reduce herbivory while sometimes suppressing flowering.
- The Clavicipitales-grass symbiosis has been cited as a model system for
  understanding how fungal endophytes can exert effects upward through entire
  ecological communities (Clay, 1997).
- Bacon and White (2000) reviewed the broader interactions among plants and
  their bacterial and fungal endophytes, situating Clavicipitales within a
  wider context of plant-microbe mutualisms.

## Ecological Role

- Endophytic Clavicipitales modify host plant palatability, competitive
  ability, and stress tolerance, thereby influencing grassland community
  composition and dynamics.
- The alkaloids produced affect multiple trophic levels: they deter insect
  oviposition and feeding, reduce mammalian grazing, and can be toxic to
  livestock (e.g., tall fescue toxicosis, ryegrass staggers).
- In the entomopathogenic branch of the order, *Cordyceps* species are
  important density-dependent regulators of insect populations in forest
  ecosystems, with *C. militaris* causing periodic population fluctuations
  in beech caterpillar (*[[syntypistis-punctatella]]*).
- The evolutionary trajectory of Clavicipitales-plant associations appears to
  be driven by herbivory pressure, with increasing endophyte infection rates
  correlated with higher herbivore intensity over evolutionary time.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- Clavicipitales endophytes are central to forage grass breeding programs
  aimed at balancing pest resistance with livestock safety.
- *Cordyceps* species within the order are valued in traditional medicine and
  are increasingly studied for their bioactive compounds (e.g., cordycepin).
- The order provides key insights into the evolution of fungal secondary
  metabolism, the ecology of plant-fungal mutualism, and the continuum between
  mutualism and parasitism.
- Species like *Baccharis* produce macrocyclic trichothecenes toxic to cattle,
  illustrating the broad phylogenetic reach of Clavicipitales chemistry.
