
# Meripilus giganteus

## Overview

**Meripilus giganteus**, the giant polypore or black-staining polypore, is a
large bracket fungus in the family Meripilaceae that parasitizes root systems
and butts of hardwood trees, especially beech (*[[fruiting-body-development]] structure.

## Morphology

Basidiocarps are among the largest produced by any temperate fungus, forming
massive rosette-like clusters of overlapping fan-shaped caps from a short,
stout central stem or directly from the substrate. Individual caps measure
10–40 cm across and 1–3 cm thick, collectively reaching 50–80+ cm. The upper
surface is concentrically zoned with alternating bands of brown, ochre, and
cream, finely velvety to nearly smooth. The **pore surface** is white to cream,
staining distinctly **black when bruised or cut** — the most reliable field
diagnostic feature. Pores are circular to angular, 3–5 per mm. Context is
white, soft and spongy when young, becoming tough and fibrous. **Spores** are
globose to broadly ellipsoid, 5–7 × 4.5–6 µm, smooth, hyaline, non-amyloid.
Hyphal system is monomitic with clamped generative hyphae. Fruitbody darkens
significantly on drying. Fruiting occurs August–November, almost exclusively
at the base of hardwood trees or on stumps and roots.

## Distribution and Habitat

Widely distributed across temperate Europe (Scandinavia to the Mediterranean),
[[holzer-sweet-chestnut-and-nut-trees]], and walnut. Fruits at
the base of living trees, on stumps, or on buried roots in mature deciduous
forests and old parkland. Prefers well-drained soils with moderate to high
rainfall.

## Pathology

*M. giganteus* is a **brown-rot** fungus that selectively degrades cellulose
and hemicellulose while leaving lignin largely intact, producing dry, cracked,
brown wood. The fungus enters through root wounds, root grafts, or the root
collar, progressively decaying heartwood and butt. Key impacts include:

- **Structural failure:** Infected trees develop brittle heartwood at the
  base, susceptible to windthrow or stem breakage. [[bioremediation-fungal-iron-reduction-chelation-metal-solubilization]] generating hydroxyl radicals that depolymerize
cellulose — of interest in industrial biomass processing.

## Ecological Role

A major agent of [[mollison-designers-food-forest-succession-design]] and structural diversity. Large, long-lasting
basidiocarps provide habitat for [[grifola-frondosa]]*** (Maitake) — Superficially similar multi-capped
  structure; smaller; pores do not bruise black; highly valued edible.
