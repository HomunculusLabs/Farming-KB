# Lucuma

**Type:** Species (Plant)
**Category:** Evergreen fruit tree
**Also known as:** Pouteria lucuma, Pouteria obovata, lucmo, lucumo,
lucmarucma, mamon, eggfruit

## Overview
Lucuma (pronounced luke-mah) is an attractive evergreen medium-sized
tree (8-15 m) native to the subtropical Andes of South America. It
produces distinctive fruit with smooth bronze skin and bright yellow-
orange flesh that can smell and taste like maple syrup. A single tree
can produce up to 500 fruit per year, each weighing up to 1 kg. The
fruit is rich in provitamin A, vitamin B3, carbohydrates, and iron, and
can be dried into a flour that stores for years.

## Key Details

### Botanical Profile
- **Family:** Sapotaceae
- **Height:** 8-15 m
- **Origin:** Subtropical Andes, South America
- **Habit:** Evergreen tree
- **Frost tolerance:** Killed below -5 degrees C
- **Blooming/fruiting:** Year-round in native range

### Growth Requirements
- Climate: Adapted to fairly dry conditions; tolerates seasonal rains
  but not waterlogging or extended humid weather
- Temperature: As a rule, grows wherever a lemon will grow
- Soil: Most soil types if well-drained; yields best in deep alluvial
  soils rich in organic matter
- Water: Drought-adapted once established

### Fruit Characteristics
- Size: Up to 1 kg per fruit
- Skin: Smooth, bronze coloured
- Flesh: Bright yellow to orange
- Flavour: Variable; often described as maple syrup-like
- Yield: Up to 500 fruit per tree per year
- Harvest: Fruit falls when ready; stored in dry hay or sawdust until
  soft

### Nutritional Value
- Rich in provitamin A (carotene)
- Rich in vitamin B3 (niacin)
- Good source of carbohydrates and calories
- Good source of iron

### Uses
- **Fresh eating:** Flesh scooped out and eaten; often blended with
  milk for milkshakes
- **Dried flour:** Fruit dried and ground into flour; keeps for years;
  added to other flours for desserts
- **Animal forage:** Valuable chicken forage; promotes eggs with bright
  orange yolks
- **Timber:** Wood used in construction
- **Cultural:** Important traditional fruit in Peru, Ecuador, and
  Chile; widely used in desserts and ice cream

### Propagation
- Seed and cuttings
- Grafting reported as difficult

## Relevance
Lucuma is a significant traditional Andean fruit tree with exceptional
storage properties through drying. Its ability to produce year-round,
tolerate dry conditions, and store as flour for years makes it
valuable for food security in subtropical [[permaculture]] systems. The
unique maple-syrup flavour and high nutritional value add diversity to
food forest designs. Its use as chicken forage that enhances yolk
colour adds a multi-function element.
