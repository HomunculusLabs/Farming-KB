## Overview

**Pachyphytum oviferum** (syn. *Echeveria oviferum*), commonly known as **moonstones** or **sugar almond plant**, is a small succulent in the family Crassulaceae. It is prized for its plump, egg-shaped leaves covered in a thick powdery bloom that gives them a moonlit, pastel appearance.

The species is native to northeastern Mexico, where it grows on rocky cliffs and ledges at moderate elevations. It has become one of the most popular collector succulents worldwide due to its distinctive form and ease of cultivation.

Its common name "moonstones" refers to the rounded, silvery-blue leaf shape that resembles polished stones. In Japan, where succulent collecting is a major hobby, selected cultivars with enhanced pink and lavender tones command premium prices.

In cultivation, several named cultivars have been selected for enhanced coloration. "Variegatum" displays creamy-white leaf margins, while "Pink Moonstones" develops intense rose-pink tones under stress conditions.

The species has received the Royal Horticultural Society Award of Garden Merit (AGM), confirming its reliability and ornamental value in temperate collections.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Pachyphytum oviferum was first described by J.A. Purpus and later transferred to the genus Pachyphytum by Charles Lemaire. The genus name derives from Greek *pachys* (thick) and *phyton* (plant), referencing the fleshy leaves.

The genus Pachyphytum contains approximately 15–20 recognized species, all endemic to Mexico. Pachyphytum oviferum belongs to the section Diotostemon, characterized by pendulous flowers and loosely arranged rosettes.

Leaves are obovate to nearly spherical, arranged in loose rosettes reaching 10–15 cm across. Each leaf measures 2–4 cm long and 1.5–3 cm wide, with colors ranging from pale blue-grey to lavender-pink depending on light exposure and temperature.
