# Poria cocos

**Type:** Species
**Category:** Basidiomycete (Polyporales)
**Also known as:** Fu Ling, Indian bread, Tuckahoe

## Overview

Poria cocos is a non-edible medicinal mushroom that ranks among the
10 most important medicinal mushrooms listed in the source literature.
China produces approximately 10,000 tons annually, making it a major
producer of this non-edible medicinal species. The mushroom has been
used extensively in traditional Chinese medicine for centuries, where
it is known as Fu Ling and is one of the most commonly prescribed
herbal medicines.

## Key Details

### Taxonomy and Classification
Poria cocos is a basidiomycete fungus in the order Polyporales. The
currently accepted synonym is Wolfiporia cocos, reflecting revised
taxonomic understanding. Unlike most mushrooms, Poria cocos grows
underground as a sclerotium (a compact mass of hardened mycelium) on
the roots of pine trees and other conifers. The sclerotium is the part
harvested for medicinal use, rather than a typical above-ground
fruiting body.

### Active Compounds
The primary bioactive compound in Poria cocos is Pachymaran, a
beta-glucan with documented immunomodulatory and antitumour activity.
This polysaccharide has been the subject of extensive pharmacological
investigation. A chemically modified derivative, carboxymethyl Poria
cocos polysaccharide (CMPLP), has also been studied and shows enhanced
solubility and bioactivity compared to the native compound.

Research has also identified connections to PSP (polysaccharopeptide)
compounds, which are structurally related bioactive molecules found in
other medicinal mushrooms such as Trametes versicolor. These
polysaccharopeptide complexes contribute to the immunomodulatory
effects of Poria cocos extracts.

### Medicinal Properties
Poria cocos has a broad range of medicinal applications in traditional
Chinese medicine. It is classified as a tonic that strengthens the
spleen, calms the mind, and promotes diuresis. Modern research has
validated several of these traditional uses, demonstrating
immunomodulatory, antitumour, and anti-inflammatory activities.

The mushroom is used as a sedative and anxiolytic agent in traditional
practice, and research has confirmed that certain triterpenoid
compounds isolated from Poria cocos have central nervous system
depressant effects. It is also valued for its diuretic properties and
is commonly included in traditional formulations for oedema and
urinary disorders.

### Production
China is the dominant producer of Poria cocos, with annual production
of approximately 10,000 tons. Cultivation involves inoculating pine
tree roots or prepared substrate with the fungus and allowing the
sclerotium to develop over several months. The harvested sclerotia are
dried, sliced, and processed for use in traditional medicine
formulations and modern pharmaceutical preparations.

### Non-Edible Status
Unlike many other medicinal mushrooms, Poria cocos is classified as a
non-edible medicinal mushroom, meaning it is used exclusively as a
medicine rather than as a food. This distinction is important for
regulatory purposes and affects how the mushroom is cultivated,
processed, and marketed. The sclerotium has a tough, woody texture
and bland taste that make it unsuitable for culinary use.

## Relevance

Poria cocos is one of the most important medicinal mushrooms in
traditional Chinese medicine and continues to be widely prescribed in
modern clinical practice. For growers, it represents a specialised
medicinal crop with well-established market demand. The extensive
pharmacological research on its beta-glucan and polysaccharopeptide
compounds makes it a model organism for studying fungal-derived
immunomodulators. Its non-edible status and underground growth habit
present unique cultivation challenges and opportunities compared to
typical above-ground fruiting mushrooms.
