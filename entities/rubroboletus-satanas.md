---# Rubroboletus satanas

## Overview

Rubroboletus satanas, commonly known as the Devil's bolete, is a
basidiomycete fungus in the family Boletaceae. Despite its sinister name, the
mushroom is not lethally poisonous but causes severe gastrointestinal
toxicity when consumed raw or undercooked. It is native to southern and
central Europe and is recognized by its pale whitish to grey cap, red-orange
pore surface, and distinctive red reticulate pattern on the stipe. Molecular
phylogenetics placed it in the genus *Rubroboletus*, which groups red-pored
boletes containing boleivic acid derivatives. Due to its toxicity and
potential confusion with edible boletes, it is important for foragers to
recognize.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi → **Division:** [[basidiomycota]] → **Class:** Agaricomycetes
  → **Order:** Boletales
- **Family:** Boletaceae → **Genus:** *Rubroboletus* → **Species:**
  *R. satanas*
- **Basionym:** *Boletus satanas* Lenz (1831)
- **Synonyms:** *Boletus satanas*, *Suillellus satanas*, *Tubiporus satanas*
- Transferred to *Rubroboletus* in 2014 based on multigene phylogenetic
  analysis by Vizzini et al.
- The epithet "satanas" was given due to its unsettling appearance and toxic
  reputation, not because of any actual danger to life — no fatalities have
  been documented.
- The genus *Rubroboletus* currently contains ~15 species, mostly from the
  Northern Hemisphere.

## Morphology

- **Cap:** 8–25 cm, broadly convex to plane, sometimes slightly depressed;
  surface smooth, dry, finely felted, whitish-grey to pale ochre; often with
  a greenish or yellowish tint in age; margin thick and inrolled when young.
- **Pore surface:** Bright orange to red-orange when young, becoming more
  ochre-yellow with maturity; pores round to angular, ~1–2 per mm; does NOT
  bruise blue (key distinguishing feature from *[[comparison-spore-print-collection-vs-spore-syringe-production]]:** Olive-green to olive-brown.
- **Spores:** 10–15 × 5–7 µm, ellipsoid to fusiform, smooth.
- **Odor:** Distinctive and unpleasant — often described as reminiscent of
  old ham or putrid, especially in mature specimens.
- **Taste:** Mild to slightly acidic.

## Distribution and Habitat

- Primarily distributed in southern and central Europe: France, Italy, Spain,
  Germany, Austria, the Balkans, extending eastward to Ukraine and Turkey.
- Less common in northern Europe; rare in Scandinavia and the British Isles.
- Ectomycorrhizal, associated primarily with broadleaf trees: oak (*Quercus*),
  beech (*Fagus*), hornbeam (*Carpinus*), and linden (*Tilia*).
- Prefers warm, calcareous (lime-rich) soils in deciduous and mixed forests.
- Fruits from June to October, particularly during warm, humid summers; can
  be locally abundant in favorable years.
