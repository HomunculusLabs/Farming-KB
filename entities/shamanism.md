## Description

Shamanism is humanity's oldest known form of natural spirituality, centered on the individual's direct experience of altered [[mckenna-food-gods-designer-states-of-consciousness]]—typically induced by plant entheogens—to achieve spiritual insight, healing, and preparation for death. It predates all organized religions and is considered the common ancestral source of the world's mythologies involving ascension beyond death and the quest for immortality.

## Classification

- **Category**: Spiritual practice / ethnoreligious tradition
- **Scope**: Global (found on every inhabited continent)
- **Antiquity**: Pre-literate, potentially Paleolithic origins (30,000+ years)

## Key Facts

- Shamanism is defined by the practitioner's direct, individual experience of the "[[shamanism-death-and-rebirth]]" through plant-induced altered states, as practice for conquering actual physical death.
- The core practice involves deep psychological introspection and judgment through plant-induced visionary states, preparing the individual for the inevitability of death.
- Shamanism directly opposes patriarchal, dogmatic religious systems that suppress individual spiritual experience, sexuality, and entheogen use.
- *Amanita muscaria* is considered the cornerstone of Shamanism, producing the quintessential death and [[shamanism-and-the-death-rebirth-experience]].
- Shamanic teachings are transmitted individually, not through institutional authority—each person experiences heaven, hell, and all points between directly.
- The shamanic worldview holds that disease results from imbalance and disharmony (yin/yang concept), and healing comes from restoring balance.
- Shamanism is considered the cornerstone of all religions, though organized religion has lost the natural indigenous freedom it originally imparted.
- The practice recognizes five natural human instincts that institutional religion suppresses: (1) expanding consciousness, (2) heightening awareness, (3) experiencing plant substances, (4) freely enjoying sexuality, (5) engaging in rhythmic dance and trance.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- Shamanism is the primary [[ghouled-field-guide-historical-context]] for the cultivation and ritual use of entheogenic mushrooms, particularly *Amanita muscaria* and *Psilocybe* species.
- Understanding shamanic practice is essential for contextualizing the ethnobotanical significance [[history-of-mushroom-cultivation]].
- The shamanic tradition of combining multiple plants in brews (e.g., Soma, Ayahuasca) reflects sophisticated knowledge of synergistic plant chemistry, including the MAO inhibitor + DMT combination principle.
- Modern mycology [[controlled-substances-act-and-psychedelic-research]] trace their cultural lineage to shamanic plant knowledge.

## Historical Figures

- R. Gordon Wasson—proposed *Amanita muscaria* as Soma, linking mushrooms to shamanic practice
- Terence McKenna—championed *Psilocybe* mushrooms as a shamanic tool and consciousness-expanding agent
- Wilhelm Reich—critiqued cultural repression of natural spirituality and sexuality
