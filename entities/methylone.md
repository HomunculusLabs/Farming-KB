## Pharmacology

Methylone acts as a **non-selective monoamine transporter substrate**, promoting the release of serotonin, dopamine, and norepinephrine from presynaptic nerve terminals. In vitro studies using rat brain synaptosomes have established the following rank order of transporter potency: SERT > DAT > NET.

**Transporter release potency (EC₅₀, rat synaptosomes):** SERT ≈ 51 nM, DAT ≈ 182 nM, NET ≈ 426 nM. By comparison, MDMA shows EC₅₀ values of approximately 67 nM (SERT), 122 nM (DAT), and 283 nM (NET), indicating methylone has slightly lower DAT potency but similar SERT affinity.

The compound is a substrate for all three monoamine transporters rather than a reuptake inhibitor. Upon entering the presynaptic terminal via reverse transport, it disrupts vesicular storage through VMAT2 interaction, causing cytoplasmic monoamines to be released through the transporter in reverse.

The β-ketone group reduces lipophilicity compared to MDMA, resulting in lower blood-brain barrier penetration and reduced central potency per milligram. This pharmacokinetic difference is the primary reason methylone requires higher doses than MDMA to achieve comparable subjective effects.

## Effects

The subjective profile of methylone is qualitatively similar to MDMA, combining stimulant effects (increased energy, elevated mood, enhanced sociability) with mild empathogenic qualities (emotional openness, increased tactile sensitivity). However, users consistently report that the effects are less pronounced and shorter-lasting than MDMA.

**Onset:** 15–30 minutes after oral administration.

**Duration:** 3–5 hours, significantly shorter than MDMA's 4–6 hour duration.

**Dosage range:** 100–250 mg orally. The lower CNS penetration of the β-ketone analogue necessitates doses approximately 1.5–2× higher than MDMA by weight for comparable subjective intensity.

Common reported effects include euphoria, increased talkativeness, enhanced music appreciation, mild visual perceptual changes, and increased libido. Negative effects at higher doses include tachycardia, hypertension, anxiety, insomnia, jaw clenching, and dehydration.

## Metabolism

Methylone undergoes N-demethylation to form methcathinone, N-demethylation combined with O-demethylenation to form cathinone, and reduction of the ketone to form the corresponding alcohol (corresponding to MDMA metabolites).
