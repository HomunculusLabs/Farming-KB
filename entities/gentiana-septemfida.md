## Overview

**Gentiana septemfida**, commonly called the crested gentian or every-seventh gentian, is a herbaceous perennial in the family Gentianaceae. It is native to the Caucasus Mountains and adjacent alpine regions of Turkey and Iran.

The species produces trailing stems that form mats of lance-shaped foliage. In late summer it bears clusters of brilliant blue, trumpet-shaped flowers with distinctive fringed coronas inside the corolla throat.

Gentiana septemfida is among the easiest alpine gentians to cultivate in temperate gardens. Its adaptability and long bloom season have made it a staple of rock gardens worldwide.

The bloom period extends from July through September in the Northern Hemisphere. This late-summer flowering fills a niche when most other alpine plants have finished blooming.

The specific epithet "septemfida" refers to the seven-part division of the corolla crest. This feature distinguishes it from the similar Gentiana lagodechiana, which has a less fringed corona.

In the horticultural trade, it is sometimes confused with Gentiana septemfida var. cordifolia. This variety has heart-shaped basal leaves and a more compact habit, but is not universally accepted as distinct.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Family:** Gentianaceae
- **Genus:** Gentiana
- **Species:** G. septemfida
- **Authority:** Pallas (1789)

The plant grows 10–20 cm tall with procumbent to ascending stems that root at the nodes. Stems are typically 20–40 cm long, forming loose mats 15–30 cm across.

Leaves are opposite, lanceolate to ovate-lanceolate, measuring 2–5 cm long and 0.5–1.5 cm wide. Basal leaves are smaller and form a compact rosette that persists through winter.

The foliage is dark green and glossy on the upper surface. Leaf margins are entire and slightly revolute, an adaptation that reduces water loss in exposed alpine conditions.

Flowers are borne in terminal clusters of two to seven blooms. The corolla is deep blue to azure, 3–5 cm long, with seven fringed lobes forming a crest-like plicate structure.

The calyx is tubular, 1.5–2 cm long, with five narrow linear lobes. Fruit is a two-valved capsule containing numerous small, light brown, winged seeds approximately 1 mm long.

The root system consists of a short, slender rhizome with fibrous roots. In cultivation, plants gradually spread by rhizome extension, forming loose clumps.

## Distribution and Habitat

Gentiana septemfida is native to the Greater and Lesser Caucasus range. Its range spans Georgia, Armenia, Azerbaijan, and northeastern Turkey.

Isolated populations extend into the Alborz and Zagros Mountains of northern Iran. The species has also been recorded from the Pontic Alps of northern Turkey.

It inhabits alpine meadows, scree slopes, and rocky crevices at elevations of 1,800–3,500 m. Populations prefer moist, well-drained calcareous soils in full sun to partial shade.

The species is most abundant on north- and east-facing slopes where snow linger provides protection. It avoids south-facing exposures where desiccation risk is high.

Plants grow in areas with cold winters and cool, moist summers. Snow cover persists for five to seven months in its native range.

The species occurs sympatrically with Gentiana pontica and [[gentiana-gelida]] in the eastern Caucasus. Hybridization events have been suggested where populations overlap.

## Cultivation

Gentiana septemfida thrives in rock garden settings with gritty, [[humus]]-rich soil. It requires excellent drainage and consistent moisture during [[query-how-to-protect-plants-from-frost-and-extend-the-growing-season]].

Full sun or light afternoon shade produces the best flower color. The species is hardy to USDA zones 4–7 and tolerates winter temperatures below −30 °C.

Propagation is by seed sown fresh in autumn, or by division of established clumps in spring. Seeds require cold stratification for 6–8 weeks to germinate reliably.

Cuttings taken from non-flowering shoots in early summer root readily in sand. This method preserves cultivar characteristics better than [[green-cannabis-grow-bible-cloning-and-seed-propagation]].

Plants benefit from a neutral to slightly acidic pH (6.0–7.0). A mulch of gritty sand or fine gravel around the crown prevents crown rot in wet winters.

The cultivar 'Purple Pendula' features nodding flowers with a purple-blue tinge. Other selections vary in flower size and mat density.

## Ecological Role

The trumpet-shaped flowers attract bumblebees (Bombus spp.), which are the primary pollinators in alpine environments. The flowers produce nectar at the base of the corolla tube.

In its native Caucasus, the species contributes to alpine meadow biodiversity. It co-occurs with other alpine specialists including saxifrages, sedums, and dwarf willows.

Gentiana septemfida is not considered threatened globally. However, overgrazing and alpine habitat degradation in the Caucasus pose localized pressures on some populations.

The species serves as an indicator of healthy alpine meadow ecosystems. Its presence often correlates with intact turf and moderate grazing intensity.
