## Boyce Thompson Institute for Plant Research

## Description

The Boyce Thompson Institute (BTI) is a premier independent plant research institute located on the campus of Cornell University in Ithaca, New York. Founded in 1924 by William Boyce Thompson, the institute is dedicated to advancing understanding of plant biology, plant-microbe interactions, and the role of plants in sustaining human life. BTI researchers have made landmark contributions to the study of [[mycorrhizal-symbiosis]], plant pathology, and fungal genomics.

## Key Facts

- **Mycorrhizal research**: BTI is a leading center for arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) symbiosis research, with researchers including Maria J. Harrison, Jinyuan Liu, Melina Lopez-Meyer, and Ignacio Maldonado-Mendoza contributing to genomic insights into AM development
- **Genomics**: Pioneering use of genomic tools to dissect the molecular basis of plant-fungal interactions
- **Plant pathology**: Significant contributions to understanding fungal plant pathogens and disease resistance mechanisms
- **Interdisciplinary approach**: Combines molecular biology, genetics, biochemistry, and computational biology
- **Training**: Provides research training for graduate students and postdoctoral fellows from around the world

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

BTI's research on [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-symbiosis]] is directly relevant to cultivation science. Mycorrhizal fungi form mutualistic associations with the vast majority of plant species, facilitating nutrient uptake (especially phosphorus), improving drought resistance, and enhancing plant health. Understanding the genomic basis of these associations, as studied at BTI, opens possibilities for optimizing mycorrhizal inoculants in agriculture, reducing fertilizer dependence, and improving crop resilience. The institute's work on plant immune responses and fungal pathogenesis also informs disease management strategies in cultivation systems.
