# Juglans regia

**Type:** Species (Tree)
**Category:** Nut-bearing tree
**Family:** Juglandaceae
**Also known as:** Persian walnut, English walnut

## Overview
Juglans regia is a slow-growing, broad-crowned deciduous tree reaching
up to 24 m in height. It is one of the most commercially important
walnut species worldwide, prized for both its nutritious nuts and its
exceptionally decorative timber. The species has been cultivated for
thousands of years across Europe, the Middle East, and Asia, with
Persia (modern Iran) being its probable centre of origin. The name
"English walnut" is a misnomer arising from its introduction to
England via Roman trade routes.

## Taxonomy
- **Order:** Fagales
- **Family:** Juglandaceae
- **Genus:** Juglans
- **Species:** J. regia
- **Common relatives:** J. nigra (black walnut), J. cinerea
  (butternut), J. venezuelensis (Venezuelan walnut)

## Key Details
- **Growth rate:** Slow growing
- **Height:** Up to 24 m with broad spreading crown
- **Yield:** 150-200 kg per tree recorded in commercial settings
- **Nutritional value (per 100g edible portion):** Energy 651 cal,
  Fat 64.0 g, Protein 14.8 g, Carbohydrate 15.8 g, Calcium 99 mg,
  Phosphorus 380 mg, Potassium 450 mg, Magnesium 131 mg, Iron
  3.1 mg, Vitamin A 30 IU, Thiamin 0.33 mg, Niacin 0.9 mg
- **Digestion time:** 3 hours
- **Culinary uses:** Nuts eaten raw, roasted, and in virtually
  every form of cooking; unripe fruits added to jams, preserved
  in vinegar or syrup, made into brandy; oil used for cooking
  and as a salad dressing
- **Timber qualities:** The most sought-after walnut timber; most
  decorative grain, durable, takes a high polish, not prone to
  splitting; preferred for prestige car trim, gun butts (valued
  for resilience), and fine cabinetry
- **Medicinal:** Leaf tea is a stomach tonic and appetite promoter;
  decoctions used for rheumatism, gout, glandular swelling,
  scrofula, gum problems, sweaty feet, skin problems including
  acne and dandruff; decoction of green shell used for failing
