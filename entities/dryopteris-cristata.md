## Overview

Dryopteris cristata is a semi-evergreen fern in the family Dryopteridaceae, commonly known as the crested wood fern or crested shield fern. It is recognized by the conspicuously crested tips of its pinnae, which give the frond a ruffled or tasseled appearance.

The species was described by the Swedish botanist Carl Linnaeus in 1753 under the basionym Polypodium cristatum. It was later transferred to the genus Dryopteris by Adanson in 1763.

## Distribution and Habitat

Dryopteris cristata ranges across northern North America from Alaska to Labrador, south through the Great Lakes region to New England, and along the Appalachian Mountains to Georgia. Disjunct populations occur in the Black Hills of South Dakota.

## Cultivation and Uses

Dryopteris cristata is cultivated as an ornamental fern for shaded, moist garden settings. Its crested pinnae tips provide textural interest in woodland plantings and rain gardens.

The species is more cold-hardy than most cultivated ferns, tolerating temperatures to minus 35 degrees Celsius or lower. This makes it a reliable choice for northern gardens where other ferns may be marginal.
