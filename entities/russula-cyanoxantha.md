# Russula cyanoxantha (Charcoal Burner)

## Overview

Russula cyanoxantha, commonly known as the charcoal burner, is a widely
distributed [[bloomfield-boletus-satanus-edible-mushroom-hazards]] in the Russulaceae family. It is considered one of
the finest edible species in the large genus *Russula*, prized for its mild,
nutty flavor and firm texture that holds up well to cooking. Found across Europe,
Asia, and [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]], it fruits from early summer through autumn in deciduous
and mixed woodlands, forming [[ectomycorrhizal-associations]] primarily with oaks
and beech. The species epithet *cyanoxantha* refers to its variable cap coloration
combining blue-green and pink-purple tones. It is one of the few *Russula*
species widely recommended for beginners due to its reliable edibility and
[[metzner-three-distinctive-features-shamanic-entheogenic-ceremonies]].

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi → **Division:** Basidiomycota → **Class:** Agaricomycetes →
  **Order:** Russulales → **Family:** Russulaceae → **Genus:** *Russula* →
  **Species:** *R. cyanoxantha*

## Macroscopic Features

- **Cap:** 5–15 cm diameter; convex when young, expanding to flat or slightly
  depressed with a shallow central depression; margin smooth to slightly
  furrowed at maturity; cuticle peels to two-thirds or more
- **Cap color:** Highly variable — dark blue-grey, violet, purple, green,
  olive, or pink; often mottled or with concentric color zones; fades with age
  (the "charcoal burner" name refers to this smoky, mottled appearance)
- **Gills:** Adnexed to slightly decurrent; crowded, white to cream; soft and
  flexible even when mature (critical distinguishing feature — most *Russula*
  species have brittle gills that snap like chalk)
- **Stem:** 4–10 cm × 1–3 cm; cylindrical, slightly clavate; white, smooth,
  firm; turns slowly grey-brown with age or handling
- **Flesh:** White, firm, thick; does not change color when cut or bruised;
  mild, slightly nutty taste and fruity odor
- **[[comparison-spore-print-collection-vs-spore-syringe-production]]:** White to pale cream (3a–3b in Romagnesi color scale)
- **Spores:** Globose to broadly ellipsoid, 6.5–9 × 5.5–7.5 µm; ornamented
  with isolated warts connected by fine ridges forming a partial reticulum

## Microscopic Features

- **Basidia:** Clavate, 4-spored, 35–50 × 8–12 µm
- **Cystidia:** Cheilocystidia abundant, fusiform to lanceolate, 40–70 × 7–12 µm;
  pleurocystidia less common, similar morphology
- **Pileipellis:** Cutis composed of repent hyphae with embedded gelatinized
  hyphae; contains intracellular pigments giving the variable cap colors
- **Sphaerocysts:** Abundant globose cells in the trama (characteristic of the
  genus), giving the flesh its distinctive brittle yet in this case surprisingly
  pliable texture

## Key Identification Features

- **Flexible gills:** The single most reliable diagnostic feature; gills can be
  bent without snapping, unlike brittle gills of other *Russula* species
- **Peeling cap cuticle:** Peels readily, often in irregular patches
- **No latex:** Does not exude latex when cut or damaged (distinguishing it from
  Lactarius species)
- **No color change:** Flesh does not stain or discolor when cut or bruised
- **Habitat:** Under oaks (*Quercus*) and beech (*Fagus*); rarely under conifers
- **Odor and taste:** Mild, pleasant, fruity — no acrid or peppery taste
