## Microscopic Features

- **Basidia:** Clavate, 4-spored, 35–50 × 8–12 µm
- **Cystidia:** Cheilocystidia abundant, fusiform to lanceolate, 40–70 × 7–12 µm;
  pleurocystidia less common, similar morphology
- **Pileipellis:** Cutis composed of repent hyphae with embedded gelatinized
  hyphae; contains intracellular pigments giving the variable cap colors
- **Sphaerocysts:** Abundant globose cells in the trama (characteristic of the
  genus), giving the flesh its distinctive brittle yet in this case surprisingly
  pliable texture

## Key Identification Features

- **Flexible gills:** The single most reliable diagnostic feature; gills can be
  bent without snapping, unlike brittle gills of other *Russula* species
- **Peeling cap cuticle:** Peels readily, often in irregular patches
- **No latex:** Does not exude latex when cut or damaged (distinguishing it from
  Lactarius species)
- **No color change:** Flesh does not stain or discolor when cut or bruised
- **Habitat:** Under oaks (*Quercus*) and beech (*Fagus*); rarely under conifers
- **Odor and taste:** Mild, pleasant, fruity — no acrid or peppery taste
