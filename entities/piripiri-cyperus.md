# Piripiri (Cyperus sp.)

## Description
Piripiri is the common name for several species of sedge in the genus *Cyperus* used extensively in Amazonian vegetalismo as both a physical medicine and a magical plant-teacher. Multiple distinct varieties exist, each with specific medicinal and shamanic applications. Piripiri is one of the most important additive plants in Amazonian plant medicine.

## Classification
- **Type**: Medicinal plant / plant-teacher
- **Scientific name**: *Cyperus* sp. (Cyperaceae family)
- **Common names**: Piripiri, caballo piripiri (horse piripiri)
- **Origin**: Amazon Basin, tropical lowlands
- **Tradition**: Vegetalismo (Amazonian plant medicine)

## Key Facts
- *Cyperus* is a large genus of sedges with many species distributed throughout the tropics and subtropics.
- The caballo piripiri (horse piripiri) is prepared in a mixture with jagua or huito (*[[genipa-americana]]*) to confer great strength upon the user.
- The preparation involves mixing jagua in careful, exact proportions with the piripiri, then pouring it over the entire body in the morning.
- A strict eight-day diet follows application: avoiding sun, salt, sweets, garlic, liquor, pig fat, sex, and socializing with sexually active people.
- The day after application, the skin turns black as if dyed with black ink. By the eighth day, the coloration disappears.
- The yahuar-piripiri (*[[eleutherine-bulbosa]]*) is mentioned as nourishment for the yahuar-toro (blood bull), and its icaro cures malaire and harm from malevolent beings.
- Piripiri is listed among the key additives used by Shipibo shamans alongside ayahuasca, toe (*Brugmansia* sp.), and chirik-sanango (*Brunfelsia grandiflora*).
- Various piripiri species have different shamanic applications, each with its own icaro (sacred song).

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology
- *Cyperus* species are relatively easy to cultivate in tropical and subtropical conditions, preferring moist to wet soils and full to partial sun.
- Piripiri's extensive use in Amazonian medicine makes it a priority species for ethnobotanical conservation and cultivation.
- The plant's combination of physical [[amanita-muscaria-medicinal-properties-heart-anxiety-dreams]] with shamanic applications illustrates the holistic approach of Amazonian plant medicine.
- Understanding piripiri cultivation requirements can support ethnobotanical gardens and medicinal plant conservation efforts.

## Cultural Context
Piripiri appears in Visions 4, 13, and throughout [[ayahuasca-preparation-pablo-amaringo-ucayali-shamanism]]'s [[amaringo-ayahuasca-visions-shipibo-iconography]] as a fundamental plant in the vegetalista's pharmacopoeia. Its use extends beyond the Amazon to other [[fukuoka-natural-farming-in-tropical-regions]] where *Cyperus* species are employed in traditional medicine. The strict dietary restrictions associated with its use reflect the broader Amazonian concept of *dieta* as essential to effective plant medicine practice.
