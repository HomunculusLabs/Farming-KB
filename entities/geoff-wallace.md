# Geoff Wallace

## Description

Geoff Wallace is an Australian agricultural innovator and inventor of a highly effective soil conditioning implement referenced extensively by [[bill-mollison]] in *Permaculture: A Designers' Manual*. His soil conditioner represents a key mechanical technology for rehabilitating compacted and degraded soils without inverting or destroying soil structure. The Wallace conditioner is distinguished from conventional ploughs by its approach of lifting and fracturing soil in place while preserving surface vegetation and soil biological communities.

## Classification

- **Category:** Person — Agricultural Engineer / Inventor
- **Domain:** Soil science, permaculture, broadacre agriculture
- **Geographic context:** Australia
- **Era:** Mid-to-late 20th century

## Key Facts

- Designed and produced a purpose-built soil conditioner that uses a circular coulter to slit the ground, followed by a steel shoe that opens the soil to create an air pocket without turning the soil over.
- The implement works on the principle that entrapped air beneath the earth surface stimulates biological activity, root growth, and [[soil-life]] — without the need for fertiliser or top-dressing.
- His system enables seed to be dropped into thin furrows created by the machine; beans, corn, and other crops can grow through existing grass cover.
- Recorded as much as 13°C (25°F) increase in soil temperature on treated versus untreated soils in autumn — a result generated by both biological activity and the air pockets left at various depths by the chisel points.
- This temperature increase enables earlier planting and supports more frost-sensitive crops.
- The approach produces permanent soil structure change if the soil is not subsequently compacted by heavy traffic, over-stocking, or chemical treatments.

## Soil Rehabilitation Method

Wallace's soil conditioning follows a sequential graze-or-cut-and-chisel cycle:

1. **First pass:** Chisel plough at 5–10 cm depth after rain when soil will carry a tractor.
2. **Graze or cut:** New growth is hard-grazed or cut and left as [[green-manure]]. Shocked plants shed root mass, adding organic matter to soil.
3. **Second pass:** Chisel again at 23–30 cm on the same pattern.
4. **Repeat:** Over approximately one year, a 23 cm (9 inch) blanket of aerated, living soil develops.
5. **Result:** Myriad subsurface tunnels carry water from valleys to ridges. Earthworms breed in green manure. Bacteria multiply. Pasture thickens and weeds are swamped.

## Equipment Design

The Wallace soil conditioner consists of a rectangular steel frame (tool bar) towed by tractor or draught animals. Shanks are narrow-edge, forward-curved vertical flat bars with slip-on steel shoes at the points. The shanks clamp to the tool-bar frame, and the points attach to the shanks. A single implement with 5 shanks requires only a 25–50 horsepower tractor and covers significant ground efficiently. The circular coulter slits the ground first, and the steel shoe follows to open the slit into an air pocket without inverting the soil.

## Results of Treatment

The documented outcomes of Wallace's soil conditioning include:

- A 23 cm (9 inch) blanket of aerated, living soil develops over 1–2 growing seasons.
- Soil temperature increases up to 13°C on treated versus untreated soils in autumn.
- Dramatically improved water infiltration — all water flows into soil rather than running off.
- Increased nitrogen-fixing bacteria nodulation in legume roots.
- Breakdown of subsoil and rock particles by carbonic and humic acids.
- Previously boggy country supports vehicle traffic without sinking.
- Water filtered through conditioned soil runs clear into dams and rivers.
- Trees show greatly increased growth due to combined factors of warmth, water, root run, and deep nutrients.

## Broader Impact

The Wallace conditioner demonstrated that soil rehabilitation does not require the wholesale destruction of existing vegetation. This insight was radical in an era dominated by conventional ploughing, where bare earth was considered normal agricultural practice. The method has influenced subsequent minimum-tillage and no-till farming movements worldwide, and remains one of the most effective tools for transitioning compacted agricultural land to productive permaculture systems.

## Design Considerations

When applying Wallace's methods, several factors must be considered:

- **Soil moisture:** Ground must be neither too dry nor too wet for effective treatment.
- **Timing:** Early spring at the end of a gentle rainy period is ideal.
- **Pattern:** Implements are driven slightly downhill from valley to ridge, creating herringbone drainage patterns.
- **Subsequent management:** The advantage of looseness can be quickly lost through over-stocking, heavy traffic, or heavy-hooved animals in wet weather.
- **Long-term solution:** Final stability depends on following up with permanent deep-rooted plants — forests or prairies.

## Relevance to Cultivation

Wallace's soil conditioner is one of the most important mechanical tools in the permaculture toolkit for broadacre soil rehabilitation. It addresses the fundamental problem of soil compaction — one of the most widespread forms of agricultural degradation — without the destructive effects of conventional ploughing. For cultivators working degraded land, the Wallace conditioner offers a pathway from compacted, lifeless soil to productive, living earth within a single growing season.
