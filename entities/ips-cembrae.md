## Description

**Ips cembrae**, the larch bark beetle, is a species of bark beetle in the family Curculionidae (subfamily Scolytinae) that attacks larch trees (Larix spp.). It is a vector of the fungal pathogen [[ceratocystis-lariciola]], causing dieback and death of larches in a density-dependent manner.









## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Animalia
- **Phylum**: Arthropoda
- **Class**: Insecta
- **Order**: Coleoptera
- **Family**: Curculionidae
- **Subfamily**: Scolytinae
- **Genus**: Ips
- **Species**: I. cembrae









## Key Facts

- Transmits the fungal pathogen Ceratocystis lariciola to larch trees
- Redfern (1989) described its role as a vector of larch fungal disease
- Damage severity is density-dependent: high populations cause severe attack and tree death
- Shares larch hosts with the wood wasp [[urocerus-gigas]] which transmits [[amylostereum-chailletii]]
- Disease severity depends on interactions between the two insect vectors and their respective fungal pathogens









## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

I. cembrae illustrates the bark beetle-fungal pathogen vector relationship, one of the most economically important fungal-insect interactions in forestry. The density-dependent nature of damage demonstrates threshold effects in disease epidemiology relevant to fungal disease management in any host system.









## Ecological Role

As a bark beetle attacking larch, I. cembrae contributes to natural forest thinning and gap dynamics in larch-dominated stands. Its fungal symbiont accelerates tree death and initiates wood [[decomposition]]. The beetle-fungus complex is part of the natural disturbance regime in European larch forests.
