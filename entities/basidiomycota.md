## Description

Phylum Basidiomycota is the second largest phylum of Kingdom Fungi, with approximately 23,000 described species (Hawksworth et al. 1995). It includes mushrooms, shelf fungi, jelly fungi, rusts, smuts, puffballs, stinkhorns, and many other familiar macroscopic fungi. Basidiomycetes are characterized by the production of meiospores (basidiospores) on club-shaped cells called basidia. This phylum contains the majority of commercially cultivated mushroom species.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Phylum**: Basidiomycota
- **Estimated species**: ~23,000 described
- **Sister phylum**: Ascomycota (together form crown fungi with Glomales)
- **Major classes**: Ustilaginiomycetes, Urediniomycetes, Hymenomycetes

## Key Facts

### Defining Characteristic

- Production of basidiospores on club-shaped basidia
- Basidium morphology historically central to classification:
  - **Phragmobasidiomycetes**: septate basidia (ancestral state)
  - **Homobasidiomycetes**: nonseptate basidia
- Dolipore septum (flared cell walls at septal pore with parenthesome membranes) unites Hymenomycetes

### Three Major Classes

#### Ustilaginiomycetes (Smuts)

- Characterized by teliospores (diploid overwintering spores)
- Dimorphic life histories: saprobic yeast state + pathogenic filamentous state
- Three major subclasses: Entorrhizomycetidae, Ustilaginiomycetidae, Exobasidiomycetidae
- Teliospore and "smut" morphology are convergent—not synapomorphic
- Not a separate phylum (Ustomycota); confirmed as members of Basidiomycota
- Numerous taxa formerly in Ustilaginales now placed in Urediniomycetes (e.g., *Microbotryum*)

#### Urediniomycetes (Rusts and Relatives)

- Large group of dimorphic, yeastlike fungi
- Subclasses: Urediniomycetidae (Uredinales, Septobasidiales), Microbotryomycetidae, Agaricostilbomycetidae
- Filamentous forms have septate mycelia with simple pores (no dolipore, no parenthesomes)
- Uredinales: complex life cycles—heteroecious (two hosts) and macrocyclic (up to five spore states) in extreme cases
- Septobasidiales: extreme specialization on scale insects
- *Mixia*: fern parasite, enigmatic member previously classified in Ascomycota (Taphrinales)
- Basidiomycete yeasts are polyphyletic across multiple clades

#### Hymenomycetes (Mushrooms and Relatives)

- Most familiar fungi: mushrooms, jelly fungi, shelf fungi
- United by dolipore septum (imperforated parenthesome appears ancestral)
- Eight major homobasidiomycete clades: polyporoid, euagaric, bolete, thelephoroid, russuloid, hymenochaetoid, cantharelloid, gomphoid-phalloid
- All eight clades include species with crust/corticioid morphology
- Basidiocarp morphology is not phylogenetically informative at higher levels due to convergent and divergent evolution

### Key Ecological Groups

- **Mycorrhizal fungi**: *Russula*, *Lactarius* (Russulales—actually separate from Agaricales), *Amanita*, many Boletaceae
- **Wood decomposers**: polyporoid clade, many Agaricales
- **Plant pathogens**: *Rhizoctonia* (Ceratobasidiales, Tulasnellales), rusts (Uredinales)
- **Insect symbionts**: Septobasidiales (scale insects), Termitomyces (termites)

### Agaricales (Euagarics Clade)

- Best-studied group phylogenetically (Moncalvo et al. 2000, 2002; Hibbett and Thorn 2001)
- Traditionally: all gilled mushrooms + fleshy poroid Boletaceae
- Molecular evidence: five to six separate clades of gilled mushrooms
- *Russula* and *Lactarius* NOT members of Agaricales—placed in Russulales
- Not all Agaricales have lamellate hymenophores
- Many major families monophyletic (Amanitaceae, Lepiotaceae); some polyphyletic (Cortinariaceae, Tricholomataceae)
- Convergent evolution: bird's nest fungi (*Cyathus*) and puffballs (*Lycoperdon*) independently lost forcible spore discharge

### Gomphoid-Phalloid Clade

- Most basal lineage of homobasidiomycetous Hymenomycetes
- Not anticipated from gross morphology; strongly supported by molecular data
- Contains six of seven major basidiocarp types
- Suggests most major basidiocarp morphologies present since early evolutionary history

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Basidiomycota contains virtually all major commercially cultivated mushroom species: *Agaricus bisporus* (button mushroom), *Pleurotus* species (oyster mushrooms), *Lentinula edodes* (shiitake), *Ganoderma* species (reishi/lingzhi), *Grifola frondosa* (maitake), *Hericium erinaceus* (lion's mane), and *Flammulina velutipes* (enokitake). Understanding that basidiocarp morphology is phylogenetically unreliable at higher levels has practical implications: unrelated species with similar macroscopic appearance may require very different cultivation approaches. The distinction between Agaricales and morphologically similar groups (Russulales, bolete clade) affects substrate and environmental parameter selection.
