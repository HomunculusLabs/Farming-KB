## Chemical Family and Structural Context

pyr-T is unique among the tryptamines explored in TIHKAL for its cyclic
nitrogen substitution. Rather than having two freely rotating alkyl groups
on the tryptamine nitrogen, pyr-T joins them into a pyrrolidine ring, creating
a rigid, cyclic diamine structure.

This approach -- constraining flexible substituents into rings -- is a well-
established strategy in medicinal chemistry. Rigid structures typically show
greater receptor subtype selectivity because they cannot adopt the multiple
conformations available to flexible molecules. However, in the case of pyr-T,
the constraint appears to have reduced rather than enhanced activity at
psychedelic receptors, possibly because the locked conformation is suboptimal
for 5-HT2A binding.

The pyrrolidine ring is found in numerous pharmacologically active compounds
across diverse therapeutic areas, including [[dipt]] (N,N-diisopropyl): Active at 25-75 mg, unique auditory distortions

- pyr-T (pyrrolidine): Active at 200-250 mg, mild entactogen-like, rigid ring

- 4-HO-pyr-T: Active at >20 mg, bizarre, long latency

- 5-MeO-pyr-T: Active at 0.5-2 mg, very negative, amnestic

The pyrrolidine series demonstrates that constraining N-alkyl groups into a
ring fundamentally alters the pharmacological profile, with effects ranging
from gentle warmth (pyr-T) through bizarre unpleasantness (4-HO-pyr-T) to
negative amnestic states (5-MeO-pyr-T).
