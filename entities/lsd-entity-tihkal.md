# LSD-25 (Lysergic Acid Diethylamide)

TIHKAL entry #26. The prototypical psychedelic compound, discovered by Albert Hofmann in 1943. LSD remains the most potent known psychedelic by weight (active at 60-200 micrograms orally), with a complex pharmacology that has driven decades of research into consciousness, serotonin receptors, and the neuroscience of perception.

## Chemical Names

- D-Lysergic acid diethylamide
- Lysergide
- D-Lysergamide, N,N-diethyl
- 9,10-Didehydro-N,N-diethyl-6-methylergoline-8b-carboxamide

| Route | Dose Range |
|-------|-----------|

8 -- 12 hours.

## Stability and Storage

LSD is an unusually fragile molecule with three points of instability:

1. **Epimerization** (high pH): The 8-position can scramble, producing iso-LSD (biologically inactive).
2. **Lumi-LSD formation** (light/water): Water or alcohol can add across the 8-9 double bond, especially under UV light, producing inactive lumi-LSD.
3. **Chlorine destruction**: Even trace chlorine in tap water will destroy LSD on contact.

As a salt, in cold water, free from air and light, it is stable indefinitely. A totally pure tartrate salt, when dry and shaken in the dark, will emit small flashes of white light.

## Origin of the Name

"LSD" comes from the German "Lysergsaure Diethylamid." The number "25" was assigned because it was the 25th substance in Hofmann's series of lysergic acid derivatives (1938), not the 25th attempt or form.

## Synthesis

All syntheses start from lysergic acid. The POCl3 method (from lysergic acid + diethylamine) is clean and fast, and is the preferred process. The synthesis involves hydrolyzing ergotamine tartrate with KOH, acidifying to precipitate lysergic acid, then coupling with diethylamine using POCl3 in an inert atmosphere. The product is isolated as the tartrate salt.
