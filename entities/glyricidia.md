# Glyricidia (Gliricidia sepium)

## Description

Glyricidia (Gliricidia sepium), commonly known as quickstick, madre de cacao, or gliricidia, is a fast-growing, nitrogen-fixing leguminous tree native to Central America and Mexico. In permaculture, Mollison repeatedly recommends it as one of the most versatile windbreak and agroforestry species, serving simultaneously as wind shelter, nitrogen fixer, fodder crop, mulch source, and living fence. Its ability to be propagated from large cuttings and coppice vigorously makes it one of the most practical multipurpose trees for tropical permaculture systems.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Family**: Fabaceae (Leguminosae)
- **Subfamily**: Faboideae
- **Genus**: Gliricidia
- **Species**: G. sepium
- **Common names**: Quickstick, madre de cacao, kakawate, mata raton, gliricidia
- **Growth form**: Medium deciduous tree, 10-15 m

## Key Facts

- Native to seasonally dry tropical forests from Mexico to Colombia; now naturalised throughout the tropics as one of the most widely planted agroforestry species.
- A medium-sized deciduous tree reaching 10-15 m in height, with an open, spreading crown that allows filtered light through to understorey crops.
- **Nitrogen fixation**: Forms root nodules with Rhizobium bacteria, contributing significant nitrogen to surrounding soils. Critical for interplanted crops and reducing dependency on synthetic nitrogen inputs.
- Rated by Mollison as a **core windbreak species** for multiple configurations:
  - **Configuration D**: Permeable low hedgerow of legumes for grass and crop growth, allowing air movement to reduce frosts.
  - **Configuration E2**: Interplanted within tree crops (citrus, avocado, banana) as shelter and mulch in complete or near-complete crown cover systems.
  - **Border plantings**: Alongside Casuarina, low palms, Leucaena, and Prosopis as wind-fast borders.
- Provides **excellent fodder**: leaves contain 20-30% crude protein, palatable to cattle, goats, and sheep. Regular lopping produces continuous high-quality feed.
- **Mulch potential**: Heavy leaf drop during dry season provides nutrient-rich mulch for understory crops, recycling nitrogen and phosphatic fertilisers into the root zone.
- Can be established from **large cuttings** (quickset): 1-2 m long hardwood cuttings planted directly into the ground root readily, providing rapid windbreak effect within one growing season.
- Coppices vigorously, tolerating frequent lopping every 3-6 months for continuous fodder and mulch harvest without tree death.
- **Living fence posts**: Cut branches root directly when planted as fence posts, creating living fences that grow stronger over time rather than rotting.
- Flowers are attractive to bees, supporting apiary integration within agroforestry systems and providing an additional honey yield.

## Role in Permaculture Design

Mollison places Glyricidia among the most important multi-functional windbreak species. Its specific roles include:

- **Nitrogen fixation**: Enriches soil for adjacent crop trees, reducing or eliminating synthetic nitrogen inputs in integrated orchard systems.
- **Frost protection**: When interplanted with susceptible crops (avocado, citrus, macadamia, chestnuts), the canopy prevents bare-ground radiation frost at night by trapping long-wave radiation.
- **Firewood source**: Provides excess fuel for cooking crop products, an important factor in subsistence and third-world agriculture where fuel scarcity limits food processing.
- **Compatibility**: Works synergistically with Casuarina (border), Acacia, Albizia, Inga, and Leucaena in compound windbreak and orchard systems.
- **Integral with crop**: Unlike single-purpose windbreaks that merely occupy space, Glyricidia is fully integrated within productive orchards, occupying space that adds to total yield rather than deducting from it.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Tropical agroforestry**: One of the most widely adopted agroforestry trees worldwide, used in alley cropping, live fencing, and shade systems for coffee and cacao plantations.
- **Mycorrhizal partnerships**: As a legume, forms both rhizobial root nodules and arbuscular mycorrhizal associations, building complex soil food webs that benefit all neighbouring plants.
- **Soil building**: The combination of nitrogen fixation, leaf-litter mulch, and root turnover creates fertile, humus-rich soils in otherwise degraded tropical sites within 3-5 years.
- **Mushroom substrate potential**: The nutrient-rich leaf litter and woody material from coppicing could serve as substrate components for tropical saprophytic mushroom cultivation, particularly for Pleurotus and Volvariella species that grow on supplemented agricultural wastes.
- **Mushroom log culture**: Branches of appropriate diameter from coppiced Glyricidia may serve as substrate logs for tropical wood-decomposer mushroom species, integrated with the agroforestry cycle.

## Cultivation Notes

- Tropical species; damaged by frost below approximately 5 degrees C. Suited to USDA Zones 10-12 and elevations from sea level to 1500 m.
- Tolerates poor, acidic, and degraded soils; thrives in well-drained sites from sandy loams to heavy clays.
- Extremely drought-tolerant once established due to deep taproot; survives extended dry seasons by shedding leaves.
- Propagation: direct seeding, bare-root seedlings, or large hardwood cuttings (1-2 m long) planted directly in the field.
- Coppice every 3-6 months for continuous mulch and fodder production; annual biomass yields of 10-20 tonnes dry matter per hectare are achievable.
- Can become weedy in some contexts; manage through regular lopping and prevent seed spread to non-target areas.
- Generally pest-free; occasionally attacked by stem-borers in stressed trees. No serious disease problems reported.
