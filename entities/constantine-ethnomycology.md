## Description

Constantine the Great (Latin: Flavius Valerius Constantinus, c. 272–337 CE) was Roman Emperor from 306 to 337 CE and the first Roman emperor to convert to Christianity. In ethnomycological and historical analysis, Constantine is identified as the political architect who transformed Christianity from a persecuted minority sect into the state religion of the Roman Empire — a process characterized as the creation of a "One World Religion" for political control.

## Classification

- **Category**: Historical figure
- **Role**: Roman Emperor (306–337 CE)
- **Dynasty**: Constantinian dynasty
- **Key actions**: Edict of Milan (313 CE), Council of Nicaea (325 CE), foundation of Constantinople
- **Religious significance**: First Christian Roman Emperor, saint in Eastern Orthodoxy

## Historical Context

### Political Motivation

The source text characterizes Constantine as understanding that "one of the most basic tenets of government was the control of its people," and that "governmental control of people becomes much easier and effective when that government is able to also assume a "divine' authority." According to this analysis, Christianity was deliberately constructed as a political tool — a "One World Religion" designed to unify the empire under both secular and sacred authority.

### The Council of Nicaea (325 CE)

Constantine convened the First Council of Nicaea, where Christian bishops were gathered to establish orthodox doctrine. In this analysis, the council:

- **Acquired and examined** as many of the world"s religious doctrines as could be found
- **Selected elements** from Egyptian mythology, Mithraism, Hinduism, Buddhism, the Jewish Torah, and many other traditions
- **Altered** the selected doctrines as desired for political purposes
- **Discarded** material that didn't serve the agenda
- **Compiled** the resulting plagiarized and altered writings into the Catholic ("universal" — "one from all") Bible

### Suppression of Entheogenic Knowledge

The text argues that this political construction of Christianity served a specific purpose regarding entheogenic plants:

- **Removed the individual"s ability** to experience direct communion with God (gnosis) through entheogenic substances
- **Replaced sacramental mushrooms** with symbolic substitutes (the wafer of Trans-Substantiation)
- **Condemned the very substances** that inspired the religion's root symbolism
- Made the **priest class essential** as intermediaries between the individual and the divine
- By denying the source (entheogenic plants), religion "becomes lies"

## Ethnomycological Significance

### The Corruption Thesis

The central ethnomycological argument about Constantine"s Christianity is one of **systematic corruption**:

1. **Original source material** from older traditions contained genuine [[soma]], the Fruit of the Tree, the Hidden Manna, the Small White Stone
- The cross as a mushroom symbol
- The "Body of Christ" (Soma = body = mushroom)
- The Eucharist as sacramental mushroom ingestion
- References to the "anointing" (Chrisma) and the "comforter" dwelling "in you"

### The One World Religion

The Catholic ("catholic" = universal) Church"s ambition to be the single world religion is framed as the continuation of Constantine's original political project — centralized control through spiritual authority that denies direct spiritual experience.
