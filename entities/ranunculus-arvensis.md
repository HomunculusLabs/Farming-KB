## Overview

Ranunculus arvensis L., the corn buttercup or field buttercup, is an annual herb in the family Ranunculaceae. It is native to Europe and western Asia but has spread widely as an agricultural weed across [[query-how-to-protect-plants-from-frost-and-extend-the-growing-season]].

Cauline leaves are sessile and progressively reduced upward, with deeply divided narrow segments. The uppermost leaves are filiform, barely 1 mm wide.

Flowers are 8–15 mm in diameter with five glossy, pale to golden-yellow petals. The five sepals are reflexed against the pedicel at anthesis.

Each petal bears a basal nectary covered by a small scale. Petals are obovate, 5–8 mm long, and slightly overlapping at the margins.

The fruiting receptacle forms a globose head 6–10 mm across. Each achene is 3–4 mm, flattened, with a broad marginal wing bearing sharp, hooked spines.

The spiny achenes are diagnostic for the species and distinguish it from all other European Ranunculus. They resemble tiny burs and are readily dispersed by attachment to fur and clothing.

Chromosome number is 2n = 32, placing it in the [[psilocybin-psilocin-variation-across-flushes]] its wide European range.

## Distribution and Habitat

Ranunculus arvensis is native across Europe from Portugal and the British Isles eastward to the Urals and the Caucasus. Its range extends into western Siberia, northern Iran, and the mountains of Central Asia.

It has been introduced to [[comparison-honey-bees-vs-native-solitary-bees]] (Halictidae). However, the species is not a major nectar resource compared to other field-margin flora.
