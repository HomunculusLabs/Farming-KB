## High Tunnel

## Description

A high tunnel (also known as a hoop house) is an unheated or minimally heated greenhouse structure consisting of a metal or wooden hoop frame covered with a single layer of greenhouse plastic. High tunnels are the primary protected-culture structure in [[eliot-coleman]]'s [[winter-harvest-system]], providing the outer layer of protection that, when combined with an inner layer of row covers, creates the microclimate necessary for winter vegetable production in cold climates.

## Classification

- **Category**: Agricultural infrastructure / protected cultivation
- **Type**: Unheated polyethylene-covered greenhouse
- **Alternative names**: Hoop house, poly tunnel, cold greenhouse
- **Scale**: Typically 30-foot wide by 96-foot long in Coleman's system

## Key Facts

### Design Specifications

Coleman's commercial-scale high tunnels are:
- **Width**: 30 feet
- **Length**: 96 feet
- **Frame**: Metal hoop construction
- **Covering**: Single layer of greenhouse-grade polyethylene
- **Mobility**: Designed to be movable between field sites

### Cold House vs. Cool House

Coleman distinguishes two types of high tunnel management:

**Cold House**: Completely unheated structure relying solely on passive solar gain and [[double-coverage]] (inner row covers) for frost protection. Produces a Zone 8 equivalent climate inside. Crops include carrots, spinach, mache, leeks, scallions, tatsoi, pak choi, and other Asian greens.

**Cool House**: Minimally heated structure with supplementary heat (propane heater) maintaining temperatures just above critical thresholds. Enables production of a wider variety of crops through the coldest months including radishes, turnips, turnip greens, Swiss chard, watercress, parsley, and arugula.

### Latitude and Climate Independence

A key insight from Coleman's work is that high tunnels can produce winter crops across a wide range of latitudes because the limiting factor for winter growing is day length (light), not temperature. Since the entire continental U.S. shares similar or better day-length conditions than much of Europe where winter production is traditional, high tunnels make winter harvests possible in any part of the country.

### The Movable Advantage

Coleman's high tunnels are designed to be moved between alternate field sites using a metal sled-runner system pulled by a tractor. This mobility addresses the historical problem of soil degradation in permanent greenhouse structures and enables field-scale crop rotation under cover.

## Relevance to Cultivation

### Four-Season Production Calendar

The high tunnel enables a year-round production schedule:
- **Fall (September-November)**: Fall crops sown for harvest through December
- **Early winter (December)**: Maximum production for pre-Christmas market
- **Vacation (late December-late January)**: Only candy carrots and overwintered leeks harvested
- **Late winter (February-March)**: Replanted crops begin maturing; spinach harvest resumes
- **Spring (April-May)**: Early spring crops and tomato transplanting
- **Summer (June-August)**: Heat-loving crops — tomatoes, peppers, eggplants, cucumbers, melons

### Economic Efficiency

Coleman chose the 30-by-96-foot dimension as the most efficient commercial size. The economics of the system are enhanced by using the same structure for both winter and summer production, distributing the capital cost across twelve months of output rather than a single season.

### Crop Diversity

The high tunnel environment supports a remarkable diversity of crops across the year. Over the course of twelve months, a single structure might produce spinach, carrots, potatoes, melons, tomatoes, lettuce, brassicas, and green manures — far more intensive than any outdoor field could sustain.

## Comparison with Other Structures

- **Cold frame**: Smaller, individual beds; high tunnels are field-scale
- **Heated greenhouse**: High tunnels use no or minimal heat, dramatically reducing operating costs
- **[[floating-row-cover]]**: Used inside high tunnels as the inner layer of double coverage
- **Dutch lights**: Historical glass-paned precursor to modern poly-covered high tunnels

## Relationship to Other Concepts

- **Movable greenhouse**: High tunnels designed to be relocated between sites
- **Double coverage**: High tunnel as the outer layer with row covers as the inner layer
- **Cold house / cool house**: Two management regimes for high tunnels
- **Crop rotation**: Mobility enables rotation of the entire structure between field sites
- **Winter harvest**: The primary production system enabled by high tunnels
