## Phanerochaete velutina

Phanerochaete velutina is a cord-forming basidiomycete fungus and a
significant wood decomposer in forest ecosystems. It has been
extensively studied as a model organism for understanding mycelial
foraging behavior, [[staycare-phanerochaete-chrysosporium-in-bioremediation]] (the
white-rot model fungus), though the two species differ in their
ecological strategies and [[fungal-mycelial-networks-nutrient-translocation]] optimized for local resource exploitation.

### Imaging Techniques

The mycelial network of P. velutina has been studied using advanced
imaging techniques including confocal laser scanning microscopy for
visualizing vacuolar dynamics and cytoplasmic streaming within hyphal
compartments, photon-counting scintillation imaging for tracking
radiolabeled nutrient translocation through intact mycelial networks,
and network graph analysis for quantifying branching patterns and
connectivity.

### Network Analysis

Bright-field imaging of P. velutina growing from beech-block inocula
over compressed soil has been used to construct network graphs at
multiple time points (9, 25, and 39 days). Branch points and
anastomoses are manually coded as nodes connected by links, allowing
quantitative analysis of network topology.
