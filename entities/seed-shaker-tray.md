# Seed Shaker Tray

## Description

The seed shaker tray is a simple mechanical device for collecting tree seed from the soil beneath mature trees. It consists of a sequence of three shaker trays in one frame, operated electrically, through which soil scooped from beneath seed-bearing trees is processed to separate clean seed from soil, grass sod, and debris. Mollison describes this as the cheapest method for obtaining large quantities of tree seed for field-drilling operations.

## Classification

- **Category**: Agricultural tool — seed collection and processing
- **Type**: Electrically operated vibrating screen system
- **Components**: Three graduated shaker trays in a single frame
- **Function**: Separate tree seed from soil matrix beneath mature trees
- **Cost**: Very low — the "most cheaply obtained" seed source method

## Key Facts

- Uses three sequentially finer shaker trays in one frame
- Electrically operated for continuous processing
- Soil is simply shovelled onto the top tray
- Separates seed from grass sod and soil through graduated sieving action
- Provides the cheapest seed source for tree forage establishment
- Designed for collecting tagasaste seed and similar tree forage species
- Enables field-drilling of tree seed using standard crop seed drills

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

The seed shaker tray represents a key enabling technology for low-cost tree forage establishment:

1. **Eliminates nursery costs**: Instead of buying nursery-grown tree seedlings, farmers can collect seed directly from beneath mature trees on their own or neighbouring properties. The shaker tray makes processing large volumes of soil-seed mixture practical.

2. **Direct seeding**: The collected clean seed can be field-drilled using a standard seedbox designed for conventional crops. Mollison notes that tagasaste seed can be mixed with daikon radish or turnip seed for effective grass suppression while the trees establish — the fast-growing radish provides initial ground cover and the tree seedlings emerge through the dying radish canopy.

3. **Adaptation and self-selection**: Seed collected from mature trees growing successfully in local conditions carries the genetic adaptation to that specific climate, soil, and moisture regime. This local ecotype selection is superior to nursery seed of unknown provenance.

4. **Scale and accessibility**: The device is simple enough to be fabricated on-farm with basic materials (mesh of graduated sizes, frame, small electric motor). This makes tree forage establishment accessible to small farmers in developing regions.

5. **Integration with field drilling**: Matthew Carpenter's successful field-drilling of tagasaste on the Canterbury Plain would have relied on seed collection methods like the shaker tray. The complete system — shaker tray for collection, standard seed drill for establishment — uses existing farm equipment rather than requiring specialized machinery.

## Design and Construction

The three-tray system uses progressively finer mesh sizes:
- **Top tray**: Coarse mesh removes large debris, stones, and grass clumps
- **Middle tray**: Medium mesh captures seed-sized particles while passing finer material
- **Bottom tray**: Fine mesh catches the clean seed while allowing dust and fine soil to pass through

The electric motor vibrates the trays, causing material to stratify by size and density. Seed is collected from the appropriate tray, cleaned if necessary, and stored cool and dry until drilling time.

## Construction and Design

Basic design uses mesh-bottomed trays that allow seed separation from chaff and debris.
Materials include wood frames with hardware cloth or specialized seed cleaning screens.
Multiple screen sizes accommodate different seed types from fine herbs to larger grains.

## Operating Techniques

Rhythmic shaking motion separates seed from chaff based on size and weight differences.
Angle adjustment controls flow rate and separation efficiency for different seed types.
Batch processing with progressive screen sizes achieves clean seed samples.

## Integration with Seed Saving

Essential tool for small-scale seed saving and heritage variety preservation.
Enables efficient processing of seed crops for storage and future planting.
Supports local seed sovereignty by making seed cleaning accessible to small producers.
