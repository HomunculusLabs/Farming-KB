## Overview

Crassula ovata, commonly called jade plant, jade tree, or money tree, is a woody-stemmed succulent shrub in the family Crassulaceae. Native to the Eastern Cape and KwaZulu-Natal provinces of South Africa, it develops thick, egg-shaped dark green leaves and a tree-like habit with age.

The species is among the most popular houseplants globally, valued for its architectural form, longevity, and cultural associations with prosperity in Chinese and Japanese traditions. Mature specimens can live for decades and reach 1–2 meters in height.

It produces clusters of star-shaped pink or white flowers in winter under short-day conditions. First described by Albrecht Roth in 1821 as Cotyledon ovata, it was later transferred to Crassula by George Claridge Druce. The genus Crassula contains approximately 200 species, with the center of diversity in southern Africa.

The species has naturalized widely outside its native range due to its popularity in cultivation. It is one of the most commercially significant succulent species, with millions produced annually by wholesale nurseries worldwide.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:**
- Kingdom: Plantae
- Order: Saxifragales
- Family: Crassulaceae
- Genus: Crassula
- Species: C. ovata (Mill.) Druce

Plants develop a thick, woody trunk with age, reaching 1–2 m tall in cultivation. Leaves are opposite, obovate to broadly elliptic, 3–9 cm long, dark glossy green, often with red margins when stressed by sun or drought.

The [[epinephrine-stress-response-endocannabinoid-modulation-blesching]] is caused by anthocyanin production and is often considered ornamental by growers.

Flowers are borne in terminal thyrses with 3–5 branches, each bearing 8–15 small star-shaped flowers. Petals are white to pale pink, 5–7 mm long. Flowering occurs in late autumn to winter, triggered by short days and cool nights.

The roots are fibrous and shallow, spreading laterally rather than deeply. This root morphology is adapted to capturing brief rainfall in rocky, well-drained soils.

Stems become woody and corky with age, developing a brown bark texture. Young stems are green and fleshy, gradually lignifying from the base upward over 2–3 years of growth.

## Distribution and Habitat

Crassula ovata is endemic to southern Africa, occurring naturally in the Eastern Cape and KwaZulu-Natal provinces of South Africa. It grows in valley bushveld and thicket vegetation on rocky hillsides and exposed outcrops.

The native climate is subtropical with summer rainfall (500–800 mm annually) and dry, mild winters. Temperatures rarely drop below 5°C in its natural range. Plants grow in full sun to partial shade, often rooted in crevices.

Soils in native habitats are shallow, rocky, and well-drained, derived from sandstone and quartzite. The species tolerates a wide pH range from slightly acidic to mildly alkaline (pH 5.5–7.5).

The species is naturalized in parts of Australia, New Zealand, and the Mediterranean, where it can become invasive in coastal scrub. In California, escaped specimens are occasionally found in disturbed coastal habitats.

Within its native range, C. ovata co-occurs with other succulents including [[aloe-ferox]], Euphorbia species, and Pelargonium. The valley thicket vegetation type where it grows is considered threatened by agricultural expansion and overgrazing.

## Cultivation

Jade plants require well-drained soil and bright light, ideally 4–6 hours of direct sun daily. A gritty succulent mix with added perlite or pumice prevents root rot, the most common problem in cultivation.

Water only when the soil is completely dry, typically every 1–2 weeks in summer and monthly in winter. The leaves will wrinkle slightly when the plant needs water, providing a reliable visual indicator.

Propagation is easy from stem or leaf cuttings. Stem cuttings root within 2–3 weeks in moist sand. Leaf cuttings take longer, producing roots and a tiny plantlet at the base within 4–8 weeks.

Seed propagation is possible but rarely practiced due to the ease of vegetative methods. Seeds germinate in 7–14 days at 20–25°C on moist, well-drained substrate under light.

Common cultivars include 'Hummel's Sunset' (golden variegated leaves), 'Gollum' and 'Hobbit' (tubular, trumpet-shaped leaves), and 'Tricolor' (cream and green variegation). Over 20 named cultivars exist.

Pests include mealybugs, scale insects, and spider mites. Mealybugs cluster at leaf axils and can be treated with [[isopropyl-alcohol]]. Good [[duggar-mushroom-insect-pests-and-fungal-diseases]].

Cold tolerance extends briefly to -2°C, but prolonged frost causes leaf drop and stem dieback. In USDA zones 10–11, jade plants can be grown outdoors year-round; elsewhere they require indoor protection during winter.
