# Distichlis palmeri (Palmer Saltgrass)

## Description

Distichlis palmeri is a perennial grass growing to approximately 0.5 m, native to tidal flats and salty marshlands off the Gulf of California. It thrives with full seawater inundation, making it one of the most salt-tolerant grain crops known. The seed is traditionally harvested by the Seri Indians of the Sonoran coast.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Family**: Poaceae
- **Genus**: Distichlis
- **Species**: D. palmeri
- **Common names**: Palmer saltgrass
- **Type**: Perennial rhizomatous grass

## Key Facts

- Height: Approximately 0.5 m
- Habitat: Tidal flats and salty marshlands
- Native range: Gulf of California, Sonoran Desert coast
- Salt tolerance: Thrives with full seawater inundation
- Spreads by rhizomes forming extremely dense stands
- Traditional harvest: Seri Indians collect seeds washed ashore
- Field trials: Hybrid cultivars producing 1 tonne grain per hectare
- Once established, does not need replanting

## Uses

### Grain Production

- Edible grain comparable to conventional cereals
- Hybrid cultivars yielding 1 tonne/ha
- No replanting required - perennial crop

### Land Rehabilitation

- Extremely salt-tolerant for saline land rehabilitation
- Dense rhizomatous growth stabilises tidal soils

### Fodder

- Related D. spicata cultivated on 20,000 ha of salt flats near Mexico City for cattle fodder
