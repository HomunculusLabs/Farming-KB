# Eucalyptus maculata (Spotted Gum)

## Description

Eucalyptus maculata, commonly known as spotted gum, is a large Australian native tree species valued for its timber, revegetation potential, and remarkable adaptability to climates outside its natural range. It is one of the most vigorous and valuable farm timber trees identified in [[permaculture]] and farm forestry contexts.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Family:** Myrtaceae
- **Genus:** Eucalyptus
- **Species:** E. maculata
- **Common names:** Spotted gum

## Natural Distribution

Spotted gum is native to the east coast of Australia, a region dominated by summer rainfall patterns. Its natural range extends along coastal and near-coastal areas of New South Wales and Queensland, where it forms tall, straight-boled forests on a variety of soil types.

## Key Facts

- **Exceptional climate adaptability:** One of the most significant findings from Holmgren's revegetation research is that spotted gum performs successfully well outside its natural climatic range. It has proven to be one of the most vigorous and valuable farm timber trees in parts of southern Australia that are dominated by winter rainfall — the opposite seasonal pattern to its native east coast habitat.
- **Timber value:** Spotted gum produces high-quality hardwood timber prized for its strength, durability, and attractive appearance. The timber is used in construction, flooring, tool handles, and engineering applications.
- **Revegetation success:** The species has demonstrated that planting species from dissimilar climates can yield surprising and valuable results, challenging the conventional wisdom of only planting locally sourced species.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

Eucalyptus maculata serves as a compelling case study in permaculture Principle 10 (Use and Value Diversity). Its unexpected success in winter-rainfall regions of southern Australia demonstrates several key permaculture concepts:

1. **Adaptive diversity:** The species illustrates how random diversity in plantings — even those driven by incomplete knowledge — can generate surprising successes that would never have been discovered through strictly climate-matched planting strategies.

2. **Farm forestry integration:** As a fast-growing, high-quality timber species, spotted gum is well-suited to integrated farm forestry designs, providing economic returns while contributing to shelter, biodiversity, and landscape function.

3. **Revegetation strategy:** Its performance supports the "try everything" approach to revegetation, where maximum unselected diversity is planted to discover which species adapt best to local conditions — a strategy that embraces uncertainty as a source of new adaptive diversity.

4. **Challenge to orthodoxy:** The success of spotted gum outside its natural range challenges the conservation movement's emphasis on local provenance only, suggesting that broader genetic and species diversity in revegetation can reveal latent adaptive capacities.

## Cultivation Notes

- Adaptable to a range of soil types including poor soils
- Tolerates winter-rainfall Mediterranean climates despite summer-rainfall origins
- Straight bole form makes it suitable for timber production
- Useful in shelterbelt and farm forestry designs
