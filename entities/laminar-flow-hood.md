## Description

A laminar flow hood (also called a laminar flow cabinet or clean bench) is an enclosed work station that uses HEPA-filtered air to create a sterile working environment. Air flows in a uniform direction (laminar flow) at a velocity of approximately 0.5 m/s, creating a particle-free zone for sterile operations.

## Classification

- **Category**: Laboratory equipment / Sterile work enclosure
- **Types**: Horizontal flow (air moves back to front) and Vertical flow (air moves top to bottom)
- **Application**: Mycology, tissue culture, microbiology, pharmaceutical compounding

## Key Facts

- Consists of three main components: pre-filter pad, blower (fan), and HEPA filter
- Air velocity in working area should be approximately 0.5 m/s
- Removes all airborne contamination through HEPA filtration
- Horizontal flow: air moves from back wall toward the operator
- Vertical flow: air moves from the top down through holes in the base
- Recommended construction material: 19 mm press boards with plexiglass top for light

## Advantages Over Steam Method

- Larger sterile working area
- Plant/fungal material can remain in the sterile zone without overheating
- Easier to use larger flasks and containers with wide openings
- Lower contamination rate, especially with wide-mouthed vessels
- Better visibility during operations

## Disadvantages

- More expensive to build (~304 EUR total cost for DIY)
- Requires more space than simple [[growing-gourmet-atmospheric-steam-sterilization]]
- Needs electricity to operate the blower

## Construction

A DIY laminar flow hood can be built for approximately 304.37 EUR:
- Blower (G2E140-AI28-01): 114.30 EUR
- Filter pad (E360): 15.08 EUR
- HEPA filter (HS Mikro SF 305 x 610 x 78 mm): 131.08 EUR
- Press boards: 23.91 EUR
- Small parts (screws, etc.): 20.00 EUR

## Usage Protocol

1. Turn on blower and wipe sterile area with alcohol-soaked paper
2. Let blower run continuously for 30 minutes
3. Re-wipe the sterile area with alcohol-soaked paper
4. Begin sterile work using cleaned glass petri dishes as cutting surfaces

## Airflow Calculation

The required airflow is calculated as:
- Airflow = width x height x air speed
- Example: 0.305 m x 0.61 m x 0.5 m/s = 0.093 m3/s = 334.89 m3/h

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Laminar flow hoods are the gold standard for sterile work [[contamination-prevention-in-mushroom-cultivation]]. They are essential for:

- **Agar culture work**: Transferring and isolating mycelium
- **Grain-to-grain transfers**: Expanding spawn without contamination
- **Tissue cloning**: Isolating mushroom tissue on sterile media
- **Spore germination**: Working with spore prints and syringes
- **Slant making**: Creating long-term [[cotter-culture-storage-senescence]]

For cultivators producing more than personal-use quantities, a laminar flow hood is strongly recommended over the steam method or still air boxes.
