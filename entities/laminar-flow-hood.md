## Description

A laminar flow hood (also called a laminar flow cabinet or clean bench) is an enclosed work station that uses HEPA-filtered air to create a sterile working environment. Air flows in a uniform direction (laminar flow) at a velocity of approximately 0.5 m/s, creating a particle-free zone for sterile operations.

## Classification

- **Category**: Laboratory equipment / Sterile work enclosure
- **Types**: Horizontal flow (air moves back to front) and Vertical flow (air moves top to bottom)
- **Application**: Mycology, tissue culture, microbiology, pharmaceutical compounding

## Key Facts

- Consists of three main components: pre-filter pad, blower (fan), and HEPA filter
- Air velocity in working area should be approximately 0.5 m/s
- Removes all airborne contamination through HEPA filtration
- Horizontal flow: air moves from back wall toward the operator
- Vertical flow: air moves from the top down through holes in the base
- Recommended construction material: 19 mm press boards with plexiglass top for light

## Advantages Over Steam Method

- Larger sterile working area
- Plant/fungal material can remain in the sterile zone without overheating
- Easier to use larger flasks and containers with wide openings
- Lower contamination rate, especially with wide-mouthed vessels
- Better visibility during operations
