## Chemical Properties

Nuciferine (IUPAC: 1,2-dimethoxyaporphine) has the molecular formula
C19H21NO2 and a molecular weight of 295.38 g/mol. It exists as two
enantiomers, with (−)-nuciferine being the naturally occurring and
pharmacologically active form.

The structure consists of a tetracyclic aporphine skeleton with two
methoxy substituents at positions 1 and 2. The compound is a weak base
(pKa ~7.5) and is moderately lipophilic (logP ~3.1), facilitating blood-
[[psilocybin-pharmacology-and-serotonin-receptors]], nuciferine shows antagonist activity at 5-HT2A
and 5-HT2C subtypes. This antagonist profile at 5-HT2A distinguishes it
from classical psychedelics, which act as agonists or partial agonists
at this receptor.

Additional receptor activities include antagonism at α1-adrenergic
receptors and histamine H1 receptors. These activities likely contribute
to its reported sedative and antihistaminergic effects in traditional use.

Bhattacharya et al. (1978) demonstrated that (−)-nuciferine produced
dose-dependent sedation and catalepsy in rodents, effects that were
reversed by dopaminergic antagonists, confirming a dopaminergic
mechanism of action.

Recent research has identified anti-inflammatory and potential
antitumor properties. Nuciferine inhibits NF-κB signaling and induces
apoptosis in several cancer cell lines, though these effects require
significantly higher concentrations than those achievable through
traditional lotus preparations.
