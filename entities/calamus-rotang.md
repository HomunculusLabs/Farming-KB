## Overview

**Calamus rotang** (common rattan) is a climbing palm species native to South and [[fungal-biodiversity-tropical-forests]] from sea level to 800 meters elevation.

The species grows as an understory climber in primary and secondary rainforests, seasonally dry forests, and swamp forests. It requires support vegetation to climb and reach the canopy for adequate light.

Commercial harvesting involves cutting mature stems at the base, stripping the leaf sheaths, and processing the canes. Fresh rattan is highly perishable and must be treated with preservatives within days of harvest.

Processing includes boiling, drying, sanding, and bending into desired shapes. The outer bark may be peeled to produce finer rattan for basketry, while the inner core serves as a material for furniture framing.

Yields from managed plantations average 1.5–3.0 metric tons of dried rattan per hectare per year. Sustainable management requires rotational harvesting to allow stem regrowth.

## Uses and Applications

Rattan furniture is the single largest commercial use, valued for its flexibility, light weight, and natural aesthetic. The global rattan furniture market is concentrated in Indonesia, the Philippines, Vietnam, and China.

Basketry and wickerwork represent traditional uses dating back thousands of years across South and Southeast Asia. Rattan baskets, mats, and containers are produced for both domestic and export markets.

Construction applications include structural elements for vernacular architecture, rope and cable alternatives, and reinforcement in concrete. The high tensile strength of rattan makes it suitable for load-bearing applications.

In rural communities, rattan serves as a material for tools, weapons, fishing equipment, and musical instruments. The inner pith is edible and consumed as a vegetable in some regions of India and Indonesia.

Rattan stems are used as binding material in horticulture and agriculture. The split cane is valued for its uniformity and workability in handicraft production.

Synthetic rattan alternatives made from polyethylene and polypropylene have captured a significant share of the outdoor furniture market. However, natural rattan retains advantages in indoor applications due to its breathability and traditional appeal.

## Ecological Role and Conservation

Calamus rotang provides structural habitat in tropical forests by forming dense climbing networks. These networks serve as arboreal pathways for mammals, reptiles, and amphibians navigating the forest canopy.

The fruit is consumed by birds, bats, and primates, making the species an important food resource. [[clay-pellet-seed-dispersal]] is facilitated by frugivorous animals that carry fruits away from the parent plant.
