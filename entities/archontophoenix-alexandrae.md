# Archontophoenix alexandrae

## Overview

Archontophoenix alexandrae, commonly known as the Alexandra palm or king palm,
is a solitary pinnate-leaved palm native to the coastal rainforests of
northeastern Australia. It is among the most widely cultivated ornamental palms
in subtropical and tropical landscapes worldwide.

The species reaches 15 to 25 meters in height, with a slender grey ringed trunk
20 to 30 centimeters in diameter. Its prominent crownshaft and gracefully
arching fronds make it a hallmark of resort and boulevard planting.

In parts of its introduced range, particularly southeastern Queensland, the
species has naturalized and is considered an environmental weed. It readily
colonizes riparian areas, competing with native understory species.

The genus name derives from Greek *archon* (chief) and *phoenix* (date palm).
The specific epithet *alexandrae* commemorates Princess Alexandra of Denmark.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:** Plantae → Tracheophyta → Liliopsida → Arecales → Arecaceae
→ Arecoideae → Areceae → Archontophoenicinae → **Archontophoenix alexandrae**
(F.Muell.) H.Wendl. & Drude (1875). Basionym: Ptychosperma alexandrae F.Muell.
(1866), from collections near the Johnstone River, Queensland.

**Trunk:** Solitary, erect, grey to light brown, prominently ringed. Height in
natural habitat ranges from 10 to 20 meters.

**Crownshaft:** Approximately 1 meter long, smooth, green to purplish-green,
notably broader than that of the closely related Archontophoenix cunninghamiana.

**Leaves:** Pinnate, 3 to 4 meters long. Leaf segments regularly arranged, 60 to
90 centimeters long, dark green above with silvery underside. Leaf base sheaths
are not prickly, distinguishing it from some other Archontophoenix species.

**Inflorescence:** Borne below the crownshaft, protandrous, with a woody densely
tomentose spathe. Flowers cream to pale purple, arranged in triads.

**Fruit:** Ovoid drupe, 1.2 to 1.5 centimeters long, ripening green to red to
bright crimson. Contains a single seed, attractive to frugivorous birds.

## Distribution and Habitat

Archontophoenix alexandrae occurs naturally along the central and northern
coast of Queensland, from the Tully River north to Cape York Peninsula, at
approximately 17°S to 19°S latitude.

The species grows in lowland tropical and subtropical rainforests, typically
below 500 meters elevation. It favors moist, well-drained soils along riparian
corridors, in coastal swamps, and on rainforest margins.

It often grows as a subcanopy to canopy element in mesophyll vine forests and
is particularly abundant in disturbed or regenerating rainforest margins.

Outside its native range, the species has naturalized in eastern Australia,
Hawaii, southern Florida, and parts of Southeast Asia and East Africa.

## Cultivation

Alexandra palm is among the most popular ornamental palms for tropical and
subtropical landscaping, valued for its slender profile, self-cleaning habit,
and relatively fast growth rate.

**Climate:** Thrives in USDA Zones 10B through 11, tolerating brief exposure to
−1°C. Performs best with annual rainfall exceeding 1200 millimeters.

**Soil:** Prefers rich, well-drained loamy soils but adapts to sandy and clay
soils with adequate drainage. Tolerates pH 5.5 to 7.0.

**Propagation:** Primarily from seed, germinating within 2 to 6 months under
warm, moist conditions. Seed should be cleaned of pulp and sown shallowly.

**Growth rate:** Moderate to fast, with 30 to 60 centimeters of trunk growth per
year. Full height is typically reached within 15 to 25 years.

**Pests:** Relatively pest-free. Pink rot (Thielaviopsis paradoxa) and bud rot
(Phytophthora palmivora) can affect stressed specimens.

## Ecology and Conservation

Within its native habitat, Alexandra palm provides food for frugivorous birds
and mammals. The bright red fruits are consumed by spectacled flying foxes,
fruit doves, and cassowaries, which facilitate seed dispersal.

The species serves as habitat for epiphytic ferns, orchids, and bromeliads in
mature rainforest canopies. Its crownshaft provides a moist microhabitat
supporting diverse invertebrate communities.

Archontophoenix alexandrae is assessed as Least Concern by the Queensland
Government due to its wide native distribution and stable populations.

In its introduced range in southeastern Queensland, the species has become
invasive along waterways, forming dense monocultures that shade out native
riparian vegetation. Several local councils actively manage wilding populations.

## Uses

The primary use is as an ornamental landscape tree, extensively planted along
boulevards, in parks, resorts, and residential gardens throughout tropical and
subtropical regions.

The heart of the palm (terminal bud) is edible and was traditionally harvested
by Indigenous Australian peoples, though harvest kills the plant.

The wood is soft and not used commercially. Indigenous communities used leaves
for thatching and leaf sheaths for cordage. In the nursery trade, Alexandra palm
is a significant commercial crop in Florida, Hawaii, and northern Australia.
