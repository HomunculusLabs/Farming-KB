# Exobasidiales

## Description

The Exobasidiales is an order of plant-parasitic [[singh-basidiomycete-fungi-in-petroleum-hydrocarbon-cleanup]]
the class Ustilaginomycetes (phylum [[basidiomycota]]), closely related to
the [[smut-fungi]]. Unlike typical smuts, which produce powdery teliospore
masses in sori, Exobasidiales are characterized by **basidia that
develop directly on the surface of infected host tissues** without
forming enclosed fruiting bodies or sori.

Modern molecular analyses (ultrastructural, biochemical, and nucleotide
sequence studies by Bauer et al. 2001) have shown that Exobasidiales is
**phylogenetically nested within the smut fungi clade**, specifically
closely related to *Tilletia* and allied taxa. This finding resolved a
longstanding taxonomic question about the relationship between these
morphologically distinct groups.

The order comprises four families, with the largest being
Exobasidiaceae, dominated by the type genus *[[exobasidium]]*.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Ustilaginomycetes
- **Order:** Exobasidiales

### Families

1. **Exobasidiaceae** — largest family; includes *Exobasidium*,
   *Kordyana*, *Muribasidiospora*
2. **Brachybasidiaceae** — small, predominantly tropical genera
   causing leaf spots
3. **Cryptobasidiaceae** — small genera associated with hypertrophied
  host tissue, primarily on Lauraceae
4. **Graphiolaceae** — smutlike pathogens of palms; exclusively
  tropical/subtropical

## Key Facts

### Genus Exobasidium

- Species are **parasitic on angiosperms**, with preferred hosts being
  temperate Ericales (heath family relatives: rhododendrons,
  azaleas, blueberries, camellias)
- Infected host tissues on leaves, succulent shoots, flowers, and
  fruits form **misshapen galls or blisters**, often discolored white
  or pink
- **Basidia** develop in a dense layer on the outer surfaces of
  infected host tissues, often protruding from stomata or from between
  epidermal cells (Savile 1959; Donk 1966; Blanz 1978)
- A **yeastlike single-cell stage** develops from microconidial
  propagation; this stage is readily cultured in vitro (Graafland 1953;
  Sundström 1964)
- Overmature *Exobasidium* colonies are often overgrown by hyphomycetes,
  which may make identification difficult
- In [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]], symptoms are most visible at **midsummer**

### Other Notable Genera

- **Kordyana:** [[copelandia-cyanescens-pantropical-distribution-and-ecology]] on Commelinaceae (spiderworts
  and relatives)
- **Muribasidiospora:** Causes foliage spots on *Rhus* (sumac) and
  *Celtis* (hackberry)
- **Brachybasidium:** Occurs on Palmae (palm family)
- **Proliferobasidium:** Monotypic genus (*P. heliconiae*) found on
  *Heliconia*
- **Ceraceosorus bombacis:** Causes serious disease of economically
  important hardwood *Bombax* (Bombacaceae) in India

### Graphiolaceae (Palm Pathogens)

- Exclusively tropical and subtropical distribution
- The genus *Graphiola* includes nine species pathogenic on palms
- Early stages appear as **dark, 1–3 mm diameter subepidermal pustules**
  surrounded by pale yellow halos on palm leaves
- Mature pustules have a hard, dark outer peridium and thin hyaline
  inner peridium surrounding masses of smooth, yellow, thick-walled
  spores
- Cause **premature shedding of leaves** by date palms
- Species on date palms are restricted mainly to coastal areas

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

### Cultivation Impact

- *Exobasidium vaccinii* infects **blueberry and cranberry** crops,
  causing reddish leaf and fruit galls that reduce yield and quality
- *Exobasidium camelliae* causes **camellia leaf gall**, a concern for
  ornamental nursery production
- In ericaceous plant cultivation (blueberries, rhododendrons, azaleas),
  *Exobasidium* species can be managed through cultural practices:
  removing and destroying galled tissues, improving air circulation,
  and applying fungicides during susceptible growth stages

### Research Value

- The phylogenetic position of Exobasidiales within Ustilaginomycetes
  provides a model for studying **morphological evolution** in fungal
  pathogens — how basidia-on-surface morphology evolved from sorus-
  forming ancestors
- The yeastlike culturable stage of *Exobasidium* makes it more
  amenable to laboratory study than many smut fungi, which are
  obligate biotrophs
- The group illustrates the **diversity of biotrophic strategies**
  within Basidiomycota, from obligate smuts to the gall-forming
  exobasidialean fungi

### Taxonomic Significance

- The inclusion of Exobasidiales within the smut clade (Bauer et al.
  2001) resolved a major taxonomic question and demonstrated that
  morphological simplicity in Exobasidiales represents **derived
  reduction** rather than a primitive state
- Conversely, smutlike pathogens in *Microbotryum* were shown to be
  more closely related to rust fungi (Urediniomycetes) than to true
  smuts, illustrating convergent evolution in smutlike morphology
