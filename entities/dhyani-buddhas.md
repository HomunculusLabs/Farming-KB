# Dhyani Buddhas

## Description
The Dhyani Buddhas (also called Five Tathagatas or Five Wisdom Buddhas) are a set of five celestial or meditational Buddhas in Vajrayana Buddhism who represent different aspects of enlightened consciousness and cosmic principles. In *The Psychedelic Experience*, Leary maps the five Dhyani Buddhas to the visionary experiences of the Second Bardo, using them as archetypal frameworks for the different types of hallucinatory and revelatory phenomena that arise during the psychedelic state.

## Classification
- **Domain:** Buddhist Theology / Vajrayana Iconography
- **Category:** Concept — archetypal figures
- **Tradition:** Tibetan Vajrayana Buddhism, Tantric Buddhism
- **Geographic origin:** Indian Buddhism, systematized in Tibet

## Key Facts

### The Five Dhyani Buddhas
Each Buddha occupies a cardinal direction in the mandala and embodies a specific wisdom, element, color, and aspect of consciousness:

#### 1. Vairochana (Center)
- **Wisdom:** Dharmadhatu Wisdom (Wisdom of the Pure Universe)
- **Element:** Space / Ether
- **Color:** White / Blue
- **Direction:** Center
- **Function:** Manifestation of phenomena; source of all organic life
- **Role in The Psychedelic Experience:** Corresponds to Vision 1 — "The Source," the undifferentiated creative energy of the First Bardo. "In him all things visible and invisible have their consummation and absorption."
- **Leary's interpretation:** The Central Realm of the Densely-Packed, where "the seed of all universal forces and things are densely packed together."

#### 2. Akshobhya (East)
- **Wisdom:** Mirror-like Wisdom
- **Element:** Water
- **Color:** Blue
- **Direction:** East
- **Function:** Reflecting reality as it is, without distortion
- **Role in The Psychedelic Experience:** Corresponds to Vision 4 — "The Wave-Vibration Structure of External Forms," where all phenomena are perceived as shimmering wave patterns.
- **Lama Govinda quote:** "Things are freed from their 'thingness,' their isolation, without being deprived of their form; they are divested of their materiality, without being dissolved."

#### 3. Ratnasambhava (South)
- **Wisdom:** Wisdom of Equality
- **Element:** Earth
- **Color:** Yellow
- **Direction:** South
- **Function:** Equanimity, treating all beings equally
- **Role in The Psychedelic Experience:** Corresponds to Vision 5 — "The Vibratory Waves of External Unity," the experience of ecstatic oneness where all beings are recognized as equal manifestations of one energy.
- **Lama Govinda quote:** "The earth carries and nourishes all beings with the equanimity and patience of a mother, in whose eyes all beings, borne by her, are equal."

#### 4. Amitabha (West)
- **Wisdom:** Discriminating Wisdom (All-Discriminating Awareness)
- **Element:** Fire
- **Color:** Red
- **Direction:** West
- **Function:** Discriminating perception, boundless light, feeling
- **Role in The Psychedelic Experience:** Corresponds to Vision 3 — "The Fire-Flow of Internal Unity," the emotional experience of organic unity and pulsing love.
- **Associated with:** Bodhisattva Chenrazee (Avalokiteshvara), embodiment of compassion.
- **Lama Govinda quote:** "The deep red light of discriminating inner vision shines forth from his heart... fire corresponds to him and thus... the eye and the function of seeing."

#### 5. Amoghasiddhi (North)
- **Wisdom:** All-Accomplishing Wisdom
- **Element:** Air / Wind
- **Color:** Green
- **Direction:** North
- **Function:** Accomplishment of all actions, movement and extension
- **Role in The Psychedelic Experience:** Corresponds to Vision 2 — "The Internal Flow of Archetypal Processes," the movement of consciousness through evolutionary and cosmological imagery.
- **Lama Govinda quote:** "The mysterious activity of spiritual forces, which work removed from the senses, invisible and imperceptible... On the elementary plane this all-pervading power corresponds to the element of air — the principle of movement and extension, of life and breath (prana)."

### The Mandala Structure
- The five Dhyani Buddhas are arranged in a mandala — a sacred geometric diagram representing the structure of enlightened consciousness.
- Each is typically depicted with a consort ( Divine Mother / prajna), Bodhisattva attendants, and symbolic objects.
- The mandala serves as both a cosmological map and a meditation tool.

### Wrathful Counterparts
- Each Peaceful Deity has a wrathful counterpart — terrifying forms that represent the same wisdom principles appearing in an aspect designed to shock the practitioner out of complacency.
- In *The Psychedelic Experience*, the "[[wrathful-deities]]" correspond to the negative, frightening versions of each vision (e.g., the "plastic doll" phenomenon as the wrathful counterpart of unity).

### Use in Psychedelic Guidance
- Leary uses the Dhyani Buddha system as a typology for the various visionary states that can arise during psychedelic sessions.
- This provides a framework for the guide to identify what type of experience the voyager is having and to offer appropriate instructions.
- The system allows Western practitioners to benefit from the Tibetan psychological map without requiring belief in literal deities.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology
The Dhyani Buddhas have no direct connection to mushroom cultivation. Their relevance to the mycological community lies in the realm of psilocybin experience:
- The five-vision typology provides a practical framework for categorizing and navigating the diverse effects of psilocybin mushrooms at different dose levels.
- Cultivators who consume their product may encounter the full range of Dhyani Buddha correspondences — from source-energy experiences (Vairochana) to internal biological visions (Amoghasiddhi) to emotional unity (Amitabha).
- Understanding this typology can help session guides provide appropriate support during psilocybin experiences.
