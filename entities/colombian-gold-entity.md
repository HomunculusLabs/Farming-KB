# Colombian Gold

## Overview

Colombian Gold is a legendary pure sativa landrace cannabis strain that
originated in the Santa Marta Mountains of Colombia's Caribbean coast.

Along with Thai, Acapulco Gold, and Afghani, it formed one of the four
foundational pillars of the 1960s–1970s imported cannabis trade that shaped
modern Western cannabis genetics.

The strain was prized for its energetic, cerebral high, sweet tropical
aroma, and towering plant structure. It contributed genetics to countless
hybrid strains developed during the "landrace rush" of the 1970s and 1980s.

True, unhybridized Colombian Gold is considered functionally extinct in its
original form, though close approximations and preserved lines exist among
collector-growers.

## Lineage

Colombian Gold is a **pure landrace sativa** with no known intentional
hybridization in its original form.

The strain adapted naturally to the tropical climate of the Sierra Nevada de
Santa Marta, a unique coastal mountain range rising to 5,700 m near the
Caribbean coast. This isolation produced distinct phenotypes adapted to
equatorial day-length patterns.

Genetic contributions to modern hybrids:

| Descendant Strain | Contribution |
|---|---|
| Skunk #1 | Parent line (via Colombian Gold × Afghani cross) |
| Original Haze | One of three founding sativa parents |
| Northern Lights | Possible sativa contributor in early breeding |
| Big Bud | Colombian sativa influence in original crosses |

Skunk #1 alone, which derives from Colombian Gold × Afghani genetics, underpins
an estimated 30–40% of all modern hybrid cannabis strains.

## Appearance

In its authentic landrace form, Colombian Gold produced large, airy, spear-
shaped buds characteristic of equatorial sativas.

Flowers were elongated and loosely structured, with pale green to golden-
yellow coloration that gave the strain its name. Pistils were abundant,
ranging from white to rust-orange at maturity.

Trichome coverage was moderate by modern standards — visible frost was
present but less dense than contemporary hybrids. The overall impression
was of a fluffy, light bud with golden undertones.

Leaves were long, narrow-bladed, and fingered (9–13 leaflets), displaying
the classic sativa morphology. Plants developed intense purple and red
pigmentation in the calyxes and petioles, especially when exposed to cool
mountain nights.

## Aroma and Flavor

The terpene profile was dominated by tropical, sweet, and earthy notes
distinctive to the Santa Marta landrace.

- **Primary:** Sweet mango, ripe banana, and tropical fruit
- **Secondary:** Earthy, woody, and spicy undertones
- **Tertiary:** Skunky, lemon zest, and floral notes

Smoke was described as smooth, sweet, and expansive in the lungs. The
flavor retained tropical fruit character through the exhale with an
earthy, slightly skunky finish.

## Terpene and Cannabinoid Profile

As a landrace adapted to traditional cultivation methods, Colombian Gold
had moderate cannabinoid content by modern standards.

- **THC:** 8–14% in original landrace populations
- **CBD:** <0.5% (virtually absent)
- **Dominant terpenes:** Myrcene, limonene, caryophyllene, pinene
- **Secondary terpenes:** Linalool, humulene, terpinolene

The moderate THC range and complex terpene profile produced effects that
were considered clear-headed and energetic rather than overwhelmingly
sedative, consistent with the effects profile of equatorial sativas.

## Effects

The high was characteristically sativa: cerebral, uplifting, and energetic.

- **Onset:** 10–20 minutes when smoked
- **Peak:** 45–90 minutes
- **Duration:** 2–4 hours
- **Character:** Euphoric, creative, talkative, and socially engaging

Users reported enhanced focus, musical appreciation, and creative ideation.
The strain was popular among artists, musicians, and counter-culture figures
of the 1960s and 1970s.

Physical effects were minimal: mild body stimulation without sedation or
couch-lock. At higher doses, some anxiety or paranoia was reported, typical
of high-THC sativa strains.

## Cultivation

As a tropical equatorial sativa, Colombian Gold had very specific
cultivation requirements that made it challenging to grow outside its
native range.

- **Flowering time:** 12–16 weeks (extremely long by modern standards)
- **Height:** 2.5–4.0 m outdoors; easily exceeded 2 m indoors without training
- **Yield:** Moderate (400–500 g/m² indoors), generous outdoors in tropical
  climates
- **Climate:** Requires warm, humid, equatorial conditions; photoperiod
  sensitivity makes it difficult to flower at temperate latitudes without
  light deprivation techniques

The long flowering time was a major reason breeders crossed Colombian Gold
with fast-flowering indicas — to capture the sativa effects and flavor
while reducing the flowering window to 8–10 weeks.
