The pharmacological profile of kratom is unusual in that it produces both stimulant-like effects at lower doses and opioid-like effects at higher doses. This biphasic action has generated significant scientific and public health interest, particularly as kratom has been increasingly used in Western countries as a self-managed treatment for chronic pain, anxiety, and opioid dependence. The US DEA estimated approximately 10–15 million kratom users in the United States as of the mid-2020s.

Traditional use of kratom in Southeast Asia predates written records, with ethnographic accounts describing its use by manual laborers, farmers, and fishermen as a stimulant to increase work capacity and endurance. In Thailand and Malaysia, fresh kratom leaves have historically been chewed or brewed into tea, often with lime (calcium hydroxide) to enhance alkaloid extraction. The transition from traditional fresh-leaf consumption to commercially processed powdered leaf products represents a significant change in exposure patterns and potential health risks.

Kratom's legal status varies significantly across jurisdictions. It remains unscheduled at the federal level in the United States following a public backlash against the DEA's 2016 proposal to emergency-schedule it. It is controlled in several countries including Thailand (decriminalized in 2021 after decades of prohibition), Malaysia, Australia, and several EU member states. The FDA has not approved kratom for any medical use and has issued warnings about its safety.

## Botanical Characteristics

**Family:** Rubiaceae (coffee family)

**Genus:** *Mitragyna*

**Species:** *M. speciosa* Korth. (1836)

**Common names:** Kratom, ketum, ithang, kakuam, thom, biak-biak

Kratom is an evergreen tree reaching heights of 4–16 meters in its native habitat, with a straight trunk up to 60 cm in diameter. The leaves are dark green, glossy, ovate-acuminate, measuring 10–20 cm in length and 6–12 cm in width. Leaf morphology varies among regional populations, with three recognized leaf vein color varieties: red, green, and white, each associated with different subjective effect profiles by users.

The inflorescences are terminal, globose heads of small, yellowish flowers. The fruit is a small capsule containing numerous tiny seeds. Vegetative propagation through stem cuttings is the primary method of cultivation, as seed germination rates are low and seed viability is short. The plant thrives in humid, tropical conditions with well-drained, fertile soil and partial shade.

The natural range of kratom extends from the Malay Peninsula south through Sumatra, Borneo, Sulawesi, and the Philippines. Within this range, considerable genetic and chemical variation exists among regional populations. Indonesian kratom (particularly from Borneo and Sumatra) is the most widely exported, while Thai and Malaysian populations have been historically most important for traditional use.

Kratom belongs to the same family as coffee (*Coffea* spp.), gardenia (*Gardenia* spp.), and cinchona (*Cinchona* spp., the source of quinine). The genus *Mitragyna* contains several species, though *M. speciosa* is the only one widely consumed for psychoactive effects. Other *Mitragyna* species such as *M. javanica* and *M. hirsuta* are used locally in Southeast Asia but contain significantly lower concentrations of active alkaloids.

## Chemical Composition

Kratom leaves contain over 40 structurally related indole alkaloids, with the following being the most pharmacologically significant:

- **Mitragynine:** The predominant alkaloid, constituting approximately 66% of total alkaloid content (1–1.5% of dry leaf weight). Acts as a partial agonist at mu-opioid receptors (MOR) and delta-opioid receptors, and as an antagonist at kappa-opioid receptors. Also exhibits adrenergic and serotonergic activity.
- **7-Hydroxymitragynine:** A minor constituent (<2% of total alkaloids) but 30–46 times more potent than mitragynine at MOR. Considered the primary contributor to the opioid-like analgesic effects of kratom. More effective at activating G-protein [[cannabis-terpene-entourage-effect]] — the synergistic interaction of multiple kratom alkaloids producing effects greater than the sum of individual components — is an area of active research interest. This phenomenon may explain why whole-leaf kratom preparations produce a different subjective experience than isolated mitragynine at equivalent doses, and has implications for the development of standardized pharmaceutical preparations.

Additional alkaloids include [[mushroom-storage-and-processing-methods]]. Young leaves generally contain higher mitragynine concentrations than mature leaves.

The commercial kratom market distinguishes between several product forms: dried leaf powder, crushed leaf, extracts (water-based and solvent-based), enhanced leaf (powder fortified with extract), and capsules. Extracted and enhanced products typically contain substantially higher mitragynine concentrations than plain leaf, and are associated with a disproportionate share of adverse events and dependence cases reported in the literature.

## Pharmacology

Kratom's pharmacological profile is complex and not fully characterized, involving multiple receptor systems simultaneously. The primary mechanisms of action include:

- **Mu-opioid receptors (MOR):** Mitragynine acts as a partial agonist with lower intrinsic efficacy than [[7-hydroxymitragynine]] is a potent MOR agonist with a strong bias toward G-protein signaling over β-arrestin recruitment, potentially explaining the reduced respiratory depression observed in animal models.
- **Adrenergic receptors:** Mitragynine exhibits α2-adrenergic agonist activity, contributing to its stimulant-like effects at lower doses and potentially to its reported efficacy in managing opioid withdrawal symptoms (similar to clonidine).
- **Serotonergic receptors:** Weak agonist activity at 5-HT2A and 5-HT2C receptors may contribute to mood elevation and anxiolytic effects, while partial agonism at 5-HT1A may reduce anxiety.

The unique biased signaling profile of mitragynine at MOR represents a significant pharmacological distinction from classical opioids. In animal models, mitragynine produces potent antinociception with substantially less respiratory depression, constipation, and physical dependence than morphine at equianalgesic doses. This has generated interest in kratom alkaloids as potential leads for novel analgesic drug development with improved safety profiles.

Mitragynine undergoes hepatic metabolism primarily via CYP3A4 and CYP2D6, with the major metabolite being 7-hydroxymitragynine. The elimination half-life of mitragynine is approximately 7–9 hours, though significant interindividual variability has been observed. O- and N-demethylation, oxidation, and [[glucuronidation]] produce numerous [[antifungal-secondary-metabolites-coprophilous-fungi]], some of which retain pharmacological activity.

Recent preclinical research has identified mitragynine's biased agonism at MOR as a potential paradigm for developing safer analgesics. By preferentially activating G-protein signaling (which mediates analgesia) over β-arrestin recruitment (which mediates respiratory depression and tolerance), mitragynine and its derivatives may offer a therapeutic window not available with conventional opioid analgesics. Several pharmaceutical companies are actively developing synthetic mitragynine analogues for clinical use.
