## Taxonomy and Morphology

Malvales → Malvaceae → Byttnerioideae → **Theobroma → *T. cacao***

Described by Linnaeus in 1753. Three major cultivar groups are recognized:
**Criollo** (pale beans, mild flavor, disease-susceptible), **Forastero**
(darker beans, hardier, bolder flavor, ~80% of global production), and
**Trinitario** (Criollo × Forastero hybrid, fine-flavor production).

Trees reach 4–8 m in cultivation (up to 15 m wild). Leaves are alternate,
entire, 20–35 cm, with new foliage pale pink before greening. Flowers are
borne directly on trunk and branches (cauliflory), small (1–2 cm), purplish-pink,
requiring midge pollination (*Forcipomyia* spp.).

Fruits are large ellipsoid pods (10–25 cm), ripening yellow to orange or
reddish-purple. Each pod contains 30–50 seeds embedded in sweet white pulp.
Pods mature in 4–6 months.

## Culinary and Nutritional Use

Processed into cocoa liquor, cocoa butter, and cocoa powder forming the basis
of all chocolate products. Per 100 g, dark chocolate (70–85%) provides ~598 kcal,
7.8 g protein, 46 g fat, 11 g fiber, iron (11.9 mg), magnesium (228 mg), and
copper (1.8 mg).
