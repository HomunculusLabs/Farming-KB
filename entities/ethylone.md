# Ethylone

**Ethylone** (systematic name: **3,4-methylenedioxy-N-ethylcathinone**) is a synthetic stimulant and entactogen of the **cathinone class**. Structurally, it is the β-keto (β-ketone) analog of **MDEA** (3,4-methylenedioxy-N-ethylamphetamine), and belongs to the substituted cathinone family alongside mephedrone, methylone, and butylone. Ethylone acts primarily as a releasing agent and reuptake inhibitor of serotonin, dopamine, and norepinephrine, producing effects broadly similar to MDMA but with notable pharmacological and experiential differences.

## Chemical Profile

### IUPAC Name

1-(1,3-benzodioxol-5-yl)-2-(ethylamino)propan-1-one

### Chemical Formula

- **Molecular formula:** C₁₂H₁₅NO₃
- **Molecular weight:** 221.25 g/mol
- **CAS Number:** 842212-02-0

### Structural Classification

Ethylone is a **substituted cathinone** with the following structural features:

- **β-keto group** (carbonyl at the β-position relative to the nitrogen) — the defining feature that distinguishes cathinones from amphetamines
- **N-ethyl substitution** on the amine nitrogen (vs. N-methyl in methylone, N-methyl in MDMA)
- **3,4-methylenedioxy** substitution on the phenyl ring (shared with MDMA, MDA, methylone, and the entire MDxx family)

### Structural Relationship to Other Compounds

```
MDA → MDMA (N-methyl) → MDEA (N-ethyl) → Ethylone (β-keto MDEA)
                                       → Methylone (β-keto MDMA)
                                       → Butylone (β-keto MBDB)
```

This places ethylone as the **cathinone homolog of MDEA**, just as methylone is the cathinone homolog of MDMA.

## Pharmacology

### Mechanism of Action

Ethylone acts as a **triple monoamine releasing agent (TMRA)** and reuptake inhibitor, with activity at three major monoamine transporters:

1. **Serotonin transporter (SERT):** Moderate to strong serotonin release and reuptake inhibition. This mechanism is primarily responsible for the entactogenic (empathogenic) effects.
2. **Dopamine transporter (DAT):** Moderate dopamine release and reuptake inhibition. Contributes to stimulant and euphoric effects.
3. **Norepinephrine transporter (NET):** Norepinephrine release and reuptake inhibition. Produces peripheral stimulant effects (increased heart rate, blood pressure, temperature).

### Receptor Affinity Profile

The N-ethyl substitution shifts the monoamine release profile compared to methylone:

- **SERT affinity:** Lower than methylone, resulting in less pronounced serotonergic effects
- **DAT/NET affinity:** Relatively higher ratio compared to SERT, producing a more **stimulant-dominant** profile
- **Net effect:** Ethylone is generally considered more stimulating and less empathogenic than methylone or MDMA, with a **higher ratio of dopaminergic to serotonergic activity**

### Pharmacokinetics

- **Onset:** 20–60 minutes after oral administration
- **Peak effects:** 1.5–2.5 hours
- **Duration:** 3–5 hours (oral)
- **Half-life:** Approximately 3–5 hours (limited human data)
- **Metabolism:** Hepatic, primarily via N-deethylation (to methylone and other metabolites), O-demethylenation (to catechol metabolites), and β-ketone reduction

### Major Metabolites

Identified in human urine and plasma studies:

- **Methylone** (via N-dealkylation) — an active metabolite with its own pharmacological profile
- **4-Hydroxy-3-methoxyethylcathinone** (via O-demethylenation)
- **Norethylone** (via N-deethylation)
- **Hydroxyethylcathinone glucuronide** (Phase II conjugate)

## Subjective Effects

### Desired Effects

- **Mild euphoria** — less intense than MDMA or methylone
- **Empathy and sociability** — present but attenuated compared to more serotonergic compounds
- **Stimulation** — more prominent than with methylone; energy, alertness, reduced fatigue
- **Enhanced music appreciation** — audio enhancement is notable
- **Mild sensory enhancement** — tactile sensitivity, visual acuity

### Undesired Effects

- **Tachycardia** (elevated heart rate) — more pronounced than with MDMA due to higher dopaminergic/noradrenergic activity
- **Vasoconstriction** — jaw tension, teeth grinding (bruxism)
- **Hyperthermia** — risk elevated in dance/party environments, though generally lower than with MDMA at comparable doses
- **Anxiety and restlessness** — the stimulant-dominant profile can produce uncomfortable overstimulation
- **Insomnia** — difficulty sleeping for 4–8 hours after effects subside
- **Cognitive impairment** — residual "fog" or difficulty concentrating the following day
- **Appetite suppression** — typical of stimulant cathinones

### Dose Range (Oral)

| Category | Dose |
|----------|------|
| Threshold | 50–80 mg |
| Light | 80–120 mg |
| Common | 120–180 mg |
| Strong | 180–250 mg |
| Heavy | 250+ mg |

**Note:** Dose information is drawn from self-reported user data and has not been verified in controlled clinical settings. Ethylone is frequently misidentified or adulterated in illicit markets, making accurate dosing unreliable.

## History and Legal Status

### Discovery and Emergence

Ethylone was first synthesized and characterized in the **2010–2011 timeframe**, emerging in the global research chemical market as part of the wave of novel psychoactive substances (NPS) that followed mephedrone's scheduling. It was first identified by forensic laboratories in Europe and Japan in 2011 and appeared in the United States shortly thereafter.

### Market Context

Ethylone was marketed online as a "legal high" under various names including:

- **NRG-2** (in Europe)
- **Aquamarine** (limited reports)
- Sold as "bath salts," "plant food," or "research chemical"

It was frequently misrepresented as MDMA or methylone in the illicit market, contributing to unexpected and sometimes adverse experiences among users who believed they were consuming a different substance. This misrepresentation remains a significant public health concern.

### Legal Status (as of 2026)

| Jurisdiction | Status |
|-------------|--------|
| United States | Schedule I (temporarily scheduled in 2014, permanently scheduled 2021 via the Cathinone类 scheduling act) |
| United Kingdom | Class B under the Psychoactive Substances Act 2016 |
| European Union | Controlled under individual member state legislation; EU-wide early warning monitoring |
| Canada | Schedule I under the Controlled Drugs and Substances Act |
| Australia | Schedule 9 (Prohibited Substance) |
| China | Controlled substance since 2015 |

## Toxicology and Harm Reduction

### Acute Toxicity

Reported adverse effects include:

- Severe agitation and anxiety
- Hypertension and tachycardia requiring emergency treatment
- Hyponatremia (rare, but possible with excessive water intake)
- Serotonin syndrome — risk is lower than MDMA due to reduced SERT activity, but remains possible, especially when combined with other serotonergic substances
- Seizures — reported in cases of extreme overdose

### Dangerous Interactions

- **MAOIs:** Potentially life-threatening serotonin syndrome — absolute contraindication
- **Other serotonergic drugs:** SSRIs, SNRIs, tramadol, tryptamines — increased serotonin syndrome risk
- **Stimulants:** Cocaine, amphetamines, caffeine — additive cardiovascular strain
- **Alcohol:** Masks stimulant effects, increases dehydration risk
- **Antidepressants:** Chronic SSRI use significantly blunts effects

### Harm Reduction Practices

- **Test your substance:** Reagent testing (Marquis, Mecke, Simons) can help identify but cannot distinguish ethylone from other cathinones. GC-MS or LC-MS is required for definitive identification.
- **Start low, go slow:** Begin with a low test dose due to potency variability and potential misidentification
- **Stay hydrated:** Drink water at a normal rate (250–500 mL/hour); avoid excessive water intake
- **Control temperature:** Take breaks from dancing, cool down regularly
- **Avoid redosing:** The initial dose produces diminishing returns on redose; redosing significantly increases cardiovascular strain and neurotoxicity risk
- **Space usage:** Wait a minimum of 4–6 weeks between uses to allow monoamine recovery
