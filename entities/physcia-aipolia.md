## Overview

**Physcia aipolia** (Ehrh. ex Humb.) Fürnr. is a foliose lichen in the
family Physciaceae, commonly known as the **hammer lichen** or **hammered
candleflame lichen**. It is widely distributed in temperate regions and is
one of the most frequently encountered corticolous lichens in urban and
suburban environments.

The species is named for its characteristic maculate thallus surface,
which bears scattered white punctate markings resembling hammer
indentations. It is moderately pollution-tolerant and serves as a
bioindicator for intermediate [[psilocybe-semilanceata-worldwide-temperate-distribution-guzman-1998]], occurring
across Europe, North America, eastern Asia, and parts of the Southern
Hemisphere. In Europe it is one of the most common foliose lichens on
tree bark.

The species is almost exclusively corticolous, growing on the bark of
deciduous and coniferous trees. It is particularly abundant on nutrient-
enriched bark in urban parks, gardens, hedgerows, and along roadsides.
