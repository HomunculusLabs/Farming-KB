## Appearance

Thai Stick was visually distinctive from other landrace strains of its era.

Buds were medium-density, elongated, and often displayed striking purple and
red coloration, especially in calyxes and small sugar leaves. The color was
more intense than most contemporaneous strains.

The iconic "stick" preparation involved selecting large cola tips, tying
them to thin bamboo skewers (~15–20 cm), and drying them in this form.
Premium versions were then dipped in cannabis hash oil, creating a sticky,
potent product.

Trichome density was high for the era, giving buds a frosty appearance.
Leaves were classically sativa: long, narrow, and heavily fingered.

## Aroma and Flavor

The terpene profile was distinctive, characterized by pungent tropical
and musky notes.

- **Primary:** Pungent skunk, tropical fruit, and black pepper
- **Secondary:** Woody, earthy, and citrus undertones
- **Tertiary:** Chocolate and floral notes (especially in Chocolate Thai)

The smoke was famously harsh and expansive — experienced consumers
describe it as "explosive" in the lungs, with a lingering spicy-pepper
aftertaste. The hash oil dipping amplified both flavor intensity and
potency significantly.

## Terpene and Cannabinoid Profile

Thai Stick was considered exceptionally potent for its era, though modern
hybrids have since surpassed it in THC content.

- **THC:** 10–18% in landrace populations; hash oil-dipped sticks reached
  considerably higher effective potency
- **CBD:** <0.5%
- **Dominant terpenes:** [[acapulco-gold]] — Mexican landrace sativa popular during the same period
