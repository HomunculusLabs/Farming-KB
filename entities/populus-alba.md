## Overview

**Populus alba** (white poplar, silver poplar, abele) is a medium to large deciduous tree native to central and southern Europe, western and central Asia, and northern Africa. It is widely planted as an ornamental and shelterbelt tree and has become naturalized and invasive in many temperate regions worldwide.

The species is easily recognized by its distinctive leaves — dark green and deeply lobed above, bright white and densely tomentose beneath. Wind causes the white undersides to flash, creating a shimmering silver appearance that gives the tree its common name.

White poplar has been cultivated since antiquity and was described by both Theophrastus and Pliny the Elder. Its rapid growth and striking appearance made it a popular landscape tree throughout the Roman Empire and medieval Europe.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Order:** Malpighiales
- **Family:** Salicaceae
- **Genus:** Populus
- **Species:** P. alba L. (1753)

The species is highly variable, and several cultivars are recognized:

- 'Nivea' — leaves nearly white on both surfaces
- 'Pyramidalis' — fastigiate (columnar) growth habit
- 'Raket' — strong [[populus-tremuloides]] produces P. × canescens (gray poplar), a naturally occurring hybrid that is common in Europe and has been introduced to [[populus-deltoides]] and native willow species.

These monoclonal stands reduce biodiversity by creating simple [[dighton-fungal-biomass-production-aquatic-ecosystems]] on short rotation cycles of 3–5 years.

The primary management challenge in cultivation is controlling root suckering. Unchecked suckering can produce dense thickets extending 10–20 m from the parent tree. Root barriers and regular removal of suckers are necessary for landscape maintenance.
