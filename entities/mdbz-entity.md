# MDBZ (N-Benzyl-MDA; 3,4-Methylenedioxy-N-benzylamphetamine)

MDBZ is a substituted amphetamine compound and the N-benzyl derivative of [[fentanyl]], where replacement of the phenethyl group on the nitrogen with a benzyl group virtually eliminates analgesic potency.

The N-benzyl analogue of MDA similarly shows little if any central activity.

## Synthesis

MDBZ is synthesized by the reductive amination of 3,4-methylenedioxyphenylacetone with benzylamine hydrochloride.

The reductive amination uses sodium cyanoborohydride in methanol at pH 6, maintained by the addition of HCl over several days.

A significant challenge in this synthesis is the separation of the starting benzylamine from the product MDBZ.

The workup involves acid-base extraction: the reaction mixture is acidified and extracted to remove neutral impurities, then basified and extracted to isolate the product.

The separation exploits the different acid-base properties of the starting amine and the product.

The hydrochloride salt melts at 170-171 degrees C (with prior shrinking at 165 degrees C).

The yield was quite small (0.55 g), reflecting the difficulty of separating product from starting material.
