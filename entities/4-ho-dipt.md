# 4-HO-DiPT

## Overview

**4-HO-DiPT** (4-hydroxy-N,N-diisopropyltryptamine), also known as **Iprocin**, is
a synthetic substituted tryptamine with psychedelic properties. It is a
hydroxylated analogue of DiPT, first synthesized and characterized by Alexander
Shulgin and documented in TIHKAL (Tryptamines I Have Known and Loved).

The compound is notable for its distinctive auditory effects, which are
reported to be more prominent than its visual effects compared to other
psychedelic tryptamines. It remains primarily a research chemical with limited
history of traditional or ceremonial use.

## Chemical Properties

**IUPAC name:** 3-[2-(Diisopropylamino)ethyl]-1H-indol-4-ol.

**Molecular formula:** C₁₆H₂₄N₂O.

**Molecular weight:** 260.38 g/mol.

**SMILES:** CC(C)N(C)CCc1c[nH]c2ccc(O)cc12.

4-HO-DiPT is a member of the 4-hydroxy [[tryptamine-family-overview]], sharing the
indole-4-ol core with psilocin (4-HO-DMT). The distinguishing structural
feature is the diisopropyl substitution on the terminal amine nitrogen, which
significantly alters the pharmacological profile relative to simpler alkyl
substitutions.

The freebase form is an oily liquid at room temperature, while the
phosphate or fumarate salt forms are crystalline solids suitable for
laboratory handling. The compound is sensitive to light, heat, and oxidation,
requiring storage under inert atmosphere at low temperatures.

## Pharmacology

4-HO-DiPT acts primarily as a serotonin 5-HT₂A receptor agonist, the same
mechanism underlying the psychedelic effects of psilocin, LSD, and DMT. The
diisopropyl substitution pattern likely influences [[tihkal-receptor-binding-serotonin]] affinity
and selectivity relative to the unsubstituted parent compound.

The compound also shows affinity for 5-HT₁A receptors, contributing to
mood-modulating properties. Partial agonism at 5-HT₂C receptors has been
hypothesized based on structural analogy to related tryptamines.

Metabolism is presumed to occur via hepatic monoamine oxidase (MAO-A)
oxidative deamination, consistent with other primary tryptamines. The
4-hydroxy group may also undergo [[glucuronidation]], analogous to psilocin's
[[gluconeogenesis-biochemistry-metabolic-pathway]].

## Effects

The subjective effects of 4-HO-DiPT are qualitatively distinct from those of
psilocin despite their structural similarity. The most notable characteristic
is a pronounced alteration of auditory perception.

**Auditory effects** include pitch distortion, frequency shifting, and a
general warping of sound quality. Music may sound deeper, slower, or
reverberant. These auditory phenomena are often more prominent than the
visual component, which is unusual among psychedelic tryptamines.

**Visual effects** are present but typically milder than those of psilocin at
equivalent doses. Closed-eye visuals include geometric patterns and color
shifting. Open-eye visuals are subtle at common doses.

**Dose range** (oral, freebase equivalent):

- **Threshold:** 5–10 mg
- **Light:** 10–20 mg
- **Common:** 15–30 mg
