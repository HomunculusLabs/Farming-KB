# Tetrahydroharmine — TIHKAL #54

## Compound Identity

- Full name: 7-Methoxy-1,2,3,4-tetrahydroharman; 1,2,3,4-tetrahydroharmine

- Synonyms: 7-MeO-THH, Leptaflorine, 7-Methoxy-1-methyl-1,2,3,4-tetrahydro-beta-carboline

- Class: Beta-carboline, tetrahydro derivative

- Source: [[tihkal-introduction-and-methodology]]

## Dosage and Duration

- Dosage: 300 mg orally (synthetic racemate)

- Duration: Unknown

- Active routes: oral

## Qualitative Effects

At 300 mg (oral): Subjective effects were described as similar to those experienced
with 100 mg of [[harmaline-entity-tihkal]]. Only a single trial
exists, with a single volunteer.

## Natural Occurrence

Tetrahydroharmine is one of the three principal beta-carboline alkaloids
found in [[mckenna-tryptamine-ayahuasca-and-dmt-combinations]] preparations, along with
[[harmine-entity-tihkal]] and [[harmaline-entity-tihkal]].
It is present in [[peganum-harmala]] (Syrian rue) and [[banisteriopsis-caapi]] (ayahuasca
vine). In analyses of ayahuasca samples, tetrahydroharmine has been reported
at levels equal to those of harmine, while harmaline is usually present in
much smaller amounts.

It has also been identified in Calliandra pentandra, a plant sometimes used
as an ayahuasca component in place of the usual [[psychotria-viridis]]. In one
analyzed brew using C. pentandra, a component initially identified as DMT
by HPLC proved to be tetrahydroharmine upon GCMS analysis — yet the brew
still exhibited psychopharmacological activity.

## Pharmacological Importance

Tetrahydroharmine is an effective monoamine oxidase inhibitor (MAOI), which
is critical for its role in ayahuasca. MAO inhibition allows orally inactive
compounds like [[alpha-n-dmt-entity-tihkal]] to become orally active by preventing
their metabolic breakdown in the gut and liver. While [[harmine-entity-tihkal]]
and [[harmaline-entity-tihkal]] are better-studied MAOIs, tetrahydroharmine
may be an extremely important — and underappreciated — factor in the overall
pharmacology of ayahuasca preparations.

The natural isolate is the dextrorotatory isomer ([a]D +32°), whereas the
existing human trial used the synthetic racemate.

## Synthesis

From [[harmaline-entity-tihkal]] hydrochloride via catalytic hydrogenation
with PtO2 and NaBH4. The crude product (mp 187-190°C) is recrystallized from
methanol. The hydrochloride salt (mp 232-234°C) is obtained in 75% yield.
IR fingerprint: 804, 902, 922, 943, 1036, 1157 cm-1.

## History of Discovery

The first isolation from Banisteriopsis caapi (originally called Banisteria)
revealed harmine as the major alkaloid. Harmaline was reported years later.
In the 1950s, careful chromatographic analysis revealed a third alkaloid.
Initially thought to be 6-methoxy-DMT based on apparent optical activity,
it was subsequently identified as tetrahydroharmine when the racemate was
synthesized and found to be spectroscopically identical, with identical IR
spectrum and melting point, and no depression in mixed melting point.

## Expanded Pharmacological Context

Tetrahydroharmine's role in ayahuasca pharmacology is increasingly recognized
as more complex and potentially more important than previously appreciated.
While harmine and harmaline are potent reversible inhibitors of MAO-A
(the enzyme primarily responsible for serotonin and DMT metabolism),
tetrahydroharmine contributes to the overall MAO inhibition profile and
may have additional pharmacological activities that shape the ayahuasca
experience.

Modern research has identified several potential pharmacological roles
for tetrahydroharmine:

- MAO-A inhibition: Potent but less well-characterized than harmine/harmaline

- Serotonin reuptake inhibition (SRI): Tetrahydroharmine has been shown to
inhibit the serotonin transporter (SERT), potentially contributing to the
antidepressant effects reported with ayahuasca use

- Additional receptor interactions: The compound may interact with other
neurotransmitter systems, including dopamine and noradrenaline pathways

The combination of MAO inhibition and serotonin reuptake inhibition in a
single molecule is pharmacologically interesting and may contribute to
ayahuasca's unique therapeutic profile. This dual mechanism is shared by
few other naturally occurring compounds.

## Comparison to Related Beta-Carbolines

- Harmine: Fully aromatic 7-methoxy-beta-carboline, potent MAO-A inhibitor,
traditional ayahuasca component

- Harmaline: 7-hydroxy dihydro-beta-carboline, potent MAO-A inhibitor,
more visual/trippy in traditional use

- Tetrahydroharmine: 7-methoxy tetrahydro-beta-carboline, MAOI + SRI,
present at levels equal to harmine in ayahuasca

- 6-MeO-THH: 6-methoxy isomer, endogenous, untested in humans

- THBC (tetrahydro-beta-carboline): Unsubstituted, endogenous, present in
many foods and beverages

## Harm Reduction and Safety Considerations

- As an MAO inhibitor, tetrahydroharmine can interact dangerously with
tyramine-containing foods (aged cheese, cured meats, fermented products)
and with certain medications

- The combination of MAO inhibition and serotonin reuptake inhibition
creates particular risk of serotonin syndrome when combined with SSRIs,
other MAOIs, or serotonergic medications

- The single human trial at 300 mg used the racemate; the natural
dextrorotatory isomer may have different potency and effects

- Individuals taking antidepressants, particularly SSRIs or MAOIs,
should avoid tetrahydroharmine-containing preparations

- The compound's effects in isolation are poorly characterized; most
experience comes from its presence in ayahuasca brews where multiple
active compounds interact synergistically
