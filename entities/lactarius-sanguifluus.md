# Lactarius sanguifluus (Red Pine Milky Cap)

**Lactarius sanguifluus** (Paulet) Fr., commonly known as the **red pine milky cap**, **bloody milk cap**, or **weeping milk mushroom**, is an edible ectomycorrhizal basidiomycete in the family **Russulaceae** (order Russulales). It is highly prized as an [[bloomfield-boletus-satanus-edible-mushroom-hazards]] in Southern Europe, particularly in Spain, France, and Italy, where it is collected from pine forests and sold in local markets. The species is distinguished by its striking wine-red to orange-red latex ("milk") that exudes from cut or broken flesh, and its association with **mountain pine** (*Pinus* spp.) forests. The epithet *sanguifluus* combines Latin *sanguis* (blood) and *fluere* (to flow), referring to the blood-red latex.

Originally described by *Paulet* in the late 18th century and validated by Elias Fries in his Systema Mycologicum (1821). The subgenus *Dapetes* contains species with brightly colored, often vinaceous latex and associations with conifers. *Lactarius sanguifluus* belongs to the *L. sanguifluus* species complex, which includes several closely related taxa that have been variously treated as separate species or subspecies:

- *L. sanguifluus* sensu stricto (Mediterranean/North African populations)
- *L. deliciosus* var. *sanguifluus* (sometimes merged)
- *L. vinosus* (Q) — treated as separate by some authorities
- [[growing-gourmet-north-american-reishi-species]] populations may represent distinct species (*L. sanguifluus* sensu Berglund & Miller)

Molecular phylogenetic studies (Nuytinck 2005, 2008) support recognition of *L. sanguifluus* and *L. deliciosus* as distinct species, despite their close morphological similarity and long history of [[neurotropic-fungi-taxonomic-confusion-and-identification]].

## Macroscopic Description

- **Cap**: 5–12 cm diameter, convex becoming depressed with age, often with a small central umbo; surface smooth to slightly wrinkled when wet, azonate (lacking concentric zones) or with very faint zones; orange to vinaceous-red, sometimes greenish where bruised or aged; margin incurved, eventually becoming wavy
- **Flesh**: Firm, brittle (typical Russulaceae sphaerocyst-dominated tissue); white to pale cream; staining vinaceous or greenish-blue on exposure, especially in the stem base
- **Latex**: Abundant, **blood-red to wine-red** when first exuded, slowly turning **greenish** after 10–30 minutes on exposure to air; mild to slightly bitter taste; this is the key diagnostic feature
- **Gills**: Adnate to slightly decurrent, crowded, narrow; pale salmon-orange to pinkish-orange, sometimes with greenish tints where cut; staining greenish when damaged
- **Stem**: 3–7 cm × 1–2.5 cm, equal to slightly tapering downward; solid becoming hollow with age; surface smooth to slightly pitted (scrobiculate at base); pale orange to vinaceous, staining greenish where handled
- **Spore print**: Pale yellowish-cream to pale salmon
- **Odor**: Pleasant, fruity or slightly resinous
- **Taste**: Mild, slightly bitter when raw; pleasantly peppery after cooking

## Microscopic Features

- **Basidiospores**: Broadly ellipsoid, 7–9 × 5.5–7 µm; ornamented with a partial reticulum connected by ridges, with isolated warts; amyloid ornamentation (turns blue-black in Melzer's reagent)
- **Basidia**: Clavate, 4-spored, 38–48 × 8–10 µm
- **Cheilocystidia**: Fusiform to ventricose, 30–50 × 5–8 µm; abundant, forming sterile gill edges
- **Pleurocystidia**: Similar but less abundant; cylindrical to lanceolate
- **Pileipellis**: An ixocutis (gelatinized cutis) with embedded hyphae 3–5 µm wide; globose cells (sphaerocysts) present in the trama (characteristic of Russulaceae)
- **Lactiferous hyphae**: Present throughout the context; contain the colored latex
- **Hyphal system**: Monomitic; clamp connections absent (characteristic of Russulaceae)

## Habitat and Distribution

*Lactarius sanguifluus* is an obligate **ectomycorrhizal species** forming associations exclusively with **pines** (*Pinus* spp.), particularly:

- **Primary associates**: Scots pine (*[[pinus-sylvestris]]*), maritime pine (*Pinus pinaster*), Aleppo pine (*Pinus halepensis*), stone pine (*[[pinus-pinea]]*), mountain pine (*Pinus mugo*), black pine (*[[pinus-nigra]]*)
- **Elevation**: Lowland to montane, typically 200–1,800 m a.s.l.
- **Soil preference**: Acidic to neutral soils; sandy or loamy; well-drained
- **Season**: Late summer to late autumn (September–December in Mediterranean climate)

[[geographic-distribution-of-psilocybe-species]] is centered on the **Mediterranean basin**:

- **Europe**: Spain (widespread, especially Catalonia, Pyrenees), France (southern, especially Provence and Alps), Italy (widespread), Portugal, Greece, Balkans
- **North Africa**: Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia (Atlas Mountains)
- **Asia Minor**: Turkey, Cyprus
- **[[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]]**: Reported from western North America, though taxonomic status of these populations is debated
