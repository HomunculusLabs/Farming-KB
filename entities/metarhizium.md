# Metarhizium

**Metarhizium** is a genus of [[singh-entomopathogenic-fungi-biocontrol-agents]] in the family Clavicipitaceae
(order Hypocreales) that infect and kill insects, while also living as soil
saprotrophs and plant root colonizers. First described by Sorokin in 1883 from
the green muscardine disease of beetles, the genus contains approximately 12-15
accepted species and is among the most widely used fungi in [[cervantes-biological-pest-control-predators]]
control worldwide. The 2009 taxonomic revision by Bischoff, Rehner & Humber
split the former *M. anisopliae* species complex into multiple distinct species
based on multilocus phylogenetics.

## Taxonomy and Classification

| Family | Clavicipitaceae |

The genus was originally established when Sorokin renamed *Oospora destructor*
Metschnikoff (1879) to *[[metarhizium-anisopliae]]*, with the species epithet
referring to the host beetle genus *Anisoplia*. The teleomorph (sexual stage)
has been linked to *Metacordyceps* species, though [[fungal-life-cycles-sexual-reproduction-metamorphosis]] is
rarely observed. Most reproduction is clonal via conidia.

## Major Species

The genus underwent a landmark taxonomic revision in 2009 that elevated many
former varieties to full species status:

- ***M. robertsii*** — formerly *M. anisopliae* var. *anisopliae*; broad host
  range, common in soil, and an active plant root colonizer
- ***M. brunneum*** — formerly *M. anisopliae* var. *majus*; the species used
  in the commercial biopesticide Met52 for tick and [[query-how-to-use-beneficial-nematodes-for-soil-pest-control]]
- ***M. anisopliae*** (sensu stricto) — the type species of the genus
- ***M. acridum*** — formerly *M. anisopliae* var. *acridum*; specialized on
  Orthoptera (locusts and grasshoppers), used in the LUBILOSA program in Africa
- ***M. rileyi*** — formerly *Nomuraea rileyi*; a lepidopteran specialist that
  infects noctuid caterpillar pests
- ***M. guizhouense***, ***M. globosum***, ***M. pinghaense***, ***M. album***,
  and ***M. lepidiotae*** — additional species with varying host specificities

## Morphology

Colonies on PDA are olive-green to dark green, velvety to floccose, reaching
3-7 cm in 14 days at 25C. Conidia are ovoid to cylindrical (5-8 x 2-3 um),
hydrophobic, and produced on phialides in densely packed columns; some species
form synnemata. Hyphae are septate and hyaline when young (1-3 um wide). In
infected insects, hyphae produce yeast-like blastospores in the hemocoel.

## Infection Mechanism

The infection process follows six stages: (1) **Adhesion** — hydrophobic
conidia attach to the insect epicuticle using MAD1/MAD2 hydrophobins; (2)
**Germination** — conidia germinate within 12-24h at >=85% RH, forming
appressoria; (3) **Cuticle penetration** — proteases (Pr1), chitinases, and
lipases combined with mechanical pressure (up to 8 MPa) breach the cuticle;
(4) **Blastospore proliferation** — yeast-like blastospores multiply in the
hemolymph, evading immunity via antioxidant enzymes; (5) **Host death** —
toxins and nutrient depletion kill the insect in 3-10 days, mummifying the
cadaver; (6) **Sporulation** — under high humidity, green conidia emerge from
the cadaver and disperse by wind, rain, or contact to infect new hosts
