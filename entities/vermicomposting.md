# Vermicomposting

## Overview

Vermicomposting is the bioconversion of organic waste into nutrient-rich
vermicast ([[ingham-vermicompost-worm-castings]]) using epigeic earthworm species, primarily
*Eisenia fetida* (red wiggler) and *Eisenia andrei* (red tiger worm).
It is a mesophilic process operating at 15–25°C, distinguishing it
from thermophilic composting which relies on microbial heat generation.

## Key Organism Species

### *Eisenia fetida* (red wiggler, tiger worm)

- **Class:** Clitellata, family Lumbricidae
- **Size:** 5–10 cm, 2–3 mm diameter
- **Optimal temperature:** 15–25°C (survives 0–35°C)
- **Optimal moisture:** 70–80% (by weight)
- **Processing capacity:** Consumes approximately 50–100% of body
  weight per day in organic matter
- **Reproduction:** Hermaphroditic; cocoon production 1–2 per week
  under optimal conditions; 3–7 hatchlings per cocoon; reaches
  sexual maturity in 40–60 days
- **Lifespan:** 3–5 years

### *Eisenia andrei* (red tiger worm)

Closely related to *E. fetida* with nearly identical requirements but
slightly faster reproduction and higher tolerance of warmer
conditions (up to 30°C). Often found in mixed populations with
*E. fetida*. Identifiable by more uniform reddish coloration without
the alternating banding pattern of *E. fetida*.

### Other species used

- **Perionyx excavatus** (Indian blue worm) — tropical, faster but
  temperature-sensitive
- **Eudrilus eugeniae** (African nightcrawler) — tropical, rapid
  processing, less cold-tolerant
- **Lumbricus rubellus** (red marsh worm) — temperate, slower but
  hardy

## Process Parameters

**Feedstock:** Fruit and vegetable scraps, coffee grounds, tea bags,
crushed eggshells (calcium source), shredded newspaper/cardboard
(carbon bedding), aged horse/cow manure. Avoid: meat, dairy, oils,
excessive citrus, alliums, pet waste.

**Carbon-to-nitrogen ratio:** Initial feedstock C:N of 25:1 to 30:1
is optimal. Higher C ratios slow processing; lower ratios risk
anaerobic conditions and ammonia toxicity.

**Moisture:** 70–80% moisture content. Squeeze test: a handful of
bedding should yield 1–2 drops of water when firmly squeezed.

**pH:** 6.0–8.0. Worms tolerate pH 5.0–9.0 but feed most actively
near neutral.

**Aeration:** Adequate oxygen is essential. Compaction, excess
moisture, and overfeeding create anaerobic zones producing hydrogen
sulfide and ammonia, which are toxic to worms.

**Temperature:** 15–25°C for optimal processing. Below 10°C, feeding
drops dramatically. Above 30°C, worms migrate or die.

## Vermicast Properties

Vermicast differs from conventional compost in several important ways:

- **Nutrient availability:** Higher plant-available nitrogen, phosphorus,
  potassium, calcium, magnesium compared to thermophilic compost
- **Microbiology:** Enormous diversity including nitrogen-fixing bacteria
  (*Azotobacter*, *Azospirillum*), phosphate-solubilizing bacteria,
  actinomycetes, beneficial fungi (*Trichoderma*, *Pseudomonas*)
- **Plant growth hormones:** Auxins, gibberellins, cytokinins produced
  during gut transit
- **Humic substances:** 6–20% humic and fulvic acids, chelating
  micronutrients and improving [[cation-exchange-capacity]]
- **Disease suppression:** Antibiotic-producing organisms suppress
  soilborne pathogens (*Pythium*, *Rhizoctonia*, *Fusarium*)

## Vermicomposting Systems

**Bin systems:** Plastic or wooden bins (30–60 L) for household scale.
Layers of bedding and feedstock, 15–20 cm depth. Processing: 2–4
months.

**Flow-through systems:** Continuous-feed reactors; castings harvested
from below while worms remain in feeding zone. Higher throughput than
batch bins. Popular models: Worm Factory, Urban Worm Bag.

**Windrows:** Outdoor windrows (0.6–1.2 m wide, 0.3–0.6 m deep)
for agricultural scale. Covered with shade cloth. Requires climate-
appropriate site selection.

**Vermifiltration:** Worms process wastewater and sewage sludge in
engineered biofilters, reducing BOD, COD, and pathogen loads.

## Permaculture Applications

- **Closed-loop cycling:** Converts kitchen/garden waste into plant-
  available fertilizer on-site, eliminating external inputs
- **Compost tea substrate:** Preferred substrate for aerated compost
  tea (ACT) due to superior microbial diversity
- **Potting mix amendment:** 5–10% vermicast by volume dramatically
  improves plant growth and disease resistance
- **Top-dressing:** 1–2 cm on garden beds provides slow-release
  nutrition and moisture retention
- **Chicken integration:** Chickens pre-process scraps, reducing
  particle size and increasing palatability for worms
