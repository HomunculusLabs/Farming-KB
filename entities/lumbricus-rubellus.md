## Description

Lumbricus rubellus is a species of earthworm known for its medicinal properties, particularly as the source of lumbrokinase — a potent fibrinolytic enzyme used to treat strokes and coronary thrombosis. In ZERI integrated farming systems, L. rubellus feeds on [[spent-mushroom-substrate]], linking vermiculture directly to fungal cultivation.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Animalia
- **Phylum**: Annelida
- **Class**: Clitellata
- **Order**: Opisthopora
- **Family**: Lumbricidae
- **Genus**: Lumbricus
- **Species**: L. rubellus
- **Common name**: Red earthworm / Red wiggler

## Key Facts

- Source of lumbrokinase, a potent fibrinolytic enzyme that dissolves blood clots.
- Lumbrokinase is used clinically to treat strokes and coronary thrombosis.
- Characterized as a source of the enzyme by Mihara et al. (1991) in the *Japanese Journal of Physiology*.
- In ZERI systems, earthworms feed on spent mushroom substrate rich in fungal mycelium.
- Earthworms transmute mushroom mycelium into worm biomass, which can feed fish or chickens.
- The remaining substrate becomes vermicompost — a nutrient-rich natural fertilizer.
- [[gunter-pauli]] highlighted the value of earthworms as sources of useful enzymes beyond their composting role.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Lumbricus rubellus is directly linked to mushroom cultivation through integrated waste processing. After mushrooms fruit on agricultural waste, the spent substrate still contains significant mycelial protein and nutrients. Feeding this to L. rubellus produces three outputs: worm biomass (for animal feed), vermicompost (for soil amendment), and potentially lumbrokinase (for pharmaceutical extraction). This makes the mushroom-to-earthworm pipeline a triple-value system that enhances the economics of mushroom cultivation operations.
