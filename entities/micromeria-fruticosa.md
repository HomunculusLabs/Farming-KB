## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Micromeria fruticosa** was originally described as *Satureja fruticosa* by Linnaeus in 1753, later transferred to *Micromeria* by Robert Druce in 1916. Some authorities still recognize it under the original combination.

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Order:** Lamiales
- **Family:** Lamiaceae
- **Subfamily:** Nepetoideae
- **Tribe:** Mentheae
- **Genus:** *Micromeria*
- **Species:** *M. fruticosa*

The plant is a compact, woody-based subshrub reaching 30–60 cm in height. Stems are erect, branched, and covered with fine white hairs giving a greyish appearance.

Leaves are small, ovate to lanceolate, 8–18 mm long and 4–10 mm wide. The upper surface is greyish-green while the lower surface is densely white-tomentose, giving the plant its common name.

Flowers are arranged in whorls in the leaf axils, forming interrupted spike-like inflorescences. The calyx is tubular, 5–7 mm long, with a characteristic curved upper lip.

The corolla is white to pale pink, 8–12 mm long, with a two-lipped structure typical of the Lamiaceae. Flowering occurs from March to July, depending on altitude and latitude.
