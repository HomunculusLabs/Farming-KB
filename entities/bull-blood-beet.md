## Description

'Bull's Blood' is a beet (*[[beta-vulgaris]]*) cultivar grown primarily for its deeply colored leaves rather than its roots. In winter greenhouse production, it is harvested at the baby-leaf stage for both salad mix and braising mix, where its intense maroon-red color provides striking visual contrast.

## Classification

- **Type**: Cultivar (Beet / Leaf beet)
- **Species**: *Beta vulgaris*
- **Category**: Salad green, braising green, baby-leaf crop
- **Primary use**: Leaf production (not root production)

## Key Facts

- Leaves exhibit a deep maroon color that intensifies under cool winter greenhouse conditions
- Serves as the primary red-color component in Eliot Coleman's baby-leaf salad mix
- Replaces radicchio in the salad mix — preferred because radicchio's bitterness can become objectionable under poor growing conditions and ruin an entire mix
- Also included in the braising mix alongside spinach, chard, and Asian greens
- Harvested at baby-leaf size (3 inches or less for salad, 4 inches or less for braising)
- Cool winter temperatures enhance both color depth and flavor quality

## Cultivation Notes

- Hardy enough for cold-house (unheated) production through winter in Zone 5 Maine
- Does not require the cool-house (minimally heated) protection that lettuce, endive, and arugula need
- Leaves can be harvested repeatedly through regrowth
- Red-leaved mustards were trialed for the braising mix but lacked sufficient color in winter, making 'Bull's Blood' the preferred red-leaf ingredient

## Relevance to Cultivation

'Bull's Blood' beet exemplifies the principle of selecting crops for specific functional roles in a mixed-product system. Rather than growing it for roots, it is cultivated exclusively for its leaves, where it serves dual purposes: visual appeal (deep maroon color) and mild flavor (no bitterness). Its exceptional cold hardiness makes it a reliable winter producer in unheated structures, and the cool conditions actually improve its quality. This makes it a cornerstone crop in the [[four-season-farm]] winter production system.
