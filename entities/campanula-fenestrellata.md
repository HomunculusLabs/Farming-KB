## Overview

Campanula fenestrellata, the Adriatic bellflower or windowed bellflower, is a low-growing perennial species in the family Campanulaceae. It is endemic to the Dinaric Alps of the western Balkans, where it grows on limestone cliffs and rocky outcrops.

The species produces star-shaped, violet-blue flowers with distinctive reflexed petal tips. Its compact cushion habit and profuse blooming make it one of the most desirable alpine campanulas for rock gardens.

Campanula fenestrellata is named for the small fenestrae or "windows" in the corolla tube, which are translucent patches visible when backlit. These thin areas in the petal tissue allow light to pass through.

The species is closely related to [[campanula-portenschlagiana]], two other Adriatic endemics popular in cultivation. All three share a compact cushion habit and star-shaped flowers.

Campanula fenestrellata received the Royal Horticultural Society's Award of Garden Merit in 1993. This recognition confirms its reliability and ornamental value in cultivation.

The species has been featured on postage stamps from Croatia and Slovenia, reflecting its [[mckenna-club-des-hachischins-and-nineteenth-century-literary-hashish]] and has remained a rock-garden staple.

Plants require well-drained, gritty, alkaline soil in a sunny or partially shaded position. A limestone chip mulch improves drainage and maintains root zone pH.

Hardy to USDA zone 5, the species tolerates cold winters with reliable snow cover. It struggles in hot, humid summer climates where crown rot is a recurring problem.

Propagation is straightforward from seed or division. Seed sown in spring germinates in 2–3 weeks without pretreatment.

## Ecological Role

The flowers attract a range of pollinators including small bees, hoverflies, and butterflies. The star-shaped corolla provides an accessible landing platform for diverse insect visitors.

Endemic to limestone cliffs, Campanula fenestrellata is part of a specialized chasmophyte community. These cliff-dwelling plants are ecologically significant as relicts of ancient Mediterranean flora.
