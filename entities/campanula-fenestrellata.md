## Overview

Campanula fenestrellata, the Adriatic bellflower or windowed bellflower, is a low-growing perennial species in the family Campanulaceae. It is endemic to the Dinaric Alps of the western Balkans, where it grows on limestone cliffs and rocky outcrops.

The species produces star-shaped, violet-blue flowers with distinctive reflexed petal tips. Its compact cushion habit and profuse blooming make it one of the most desirable alpine campanulas for rock gardens.

Campanula fenestrellata is named for the small fenestrae or "windows" in the corolla tube, which are translucent patches visible when backlit. These thin areas in the petal tissue allow light to pass through.

The species is closely related to [[campanula-garganica]] and [[campanula-portenschlagiana]], two other Adriatic endemics popular in cultivation. All three share a compact cushion habit and star-shaped flowers.

Campanula fenestrellata received the Royal Horticultural Society's Award of Garden Merit in 1993. This recognition confirms its reliability and ornamental value in cultivation.

The species has been featured on postage stamps from Croatia and Slovenia, reflecting its [[amanita-muscaria]] as a symbol of the Dinaric karst landscape.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Campanula fenestrellata was described by Heinrich Feer in 1890. It belongs to Campanula series Garganicae, a group of southeastern European species adapted to limestone cliff habitats.

Plants form dense, mat-forming cushions 5–12 cm tall and 15–30 cm across. The root system is fibrous, spreading horizontally in crevices and thin soil.

Stems are short, procumbent to ascending, and sparingly branched. They are glabrous or sparsely hairy with short, appressed white hairs.

Leaves are small, ovate to cordate, 5–15 mm long, with crenate margins and long petioles. Basal leaves are rounded, while stem leaves are more pointed and smaller.

Flowers are stellate, 15–25 mm across, with five broadly lanceolate lobes. The corolla is violet-blue to lavender-blue, with translucent patches near the base of each lobe.

Each lobe tip is slightly reflexed, giving the flower an open, star-like appearance. The style is exserted, with three recurved stigmatic lobes.

Flowering occurs from June through August. Capsules are pendulous, ovoid, dehiscing by three pores near the base.

Seeds are small, pale brown, ellipsoid, and slightly flattened. Germination occurs readily without cold stratification in cultivation.

The chromosome number is 2n = 34. Three subspecies are recognized, differing in leaf shape and flower size.

## Distribution and Habitat

Campanula fenestrellata is endemic to the Dinaric Alps of Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Slovenia, and Montenegro. It is a classic limestone endemic with a narrow geographic range.

It grows on vertical and overhanging limestone cliffs, in rock crevices, and on stabilized scree at elevations of 500–2,000 meters. Plants are restricted to calcareous substrates.

The species occupies cool, north-facing cliffs where humidity remains high. Shading from overhanging rock protects plants from intense midday sun.

Rainfall in its native range averages 1,500–2,500 mm annually. Winter snow cover provides insulation against freezing temperatures.

Population sizes vary from a few individuals on isolated cliffs to extensive colonies covering large rock faces. Dispersal between cliff systems is limited.

The total area of occupancy is estimated at less than 2,000 square kilometers. Despite this restricted range, the species is not considered immediately endangered due to the inaccessibility of most cliff habitats.

## Cultivation

Campanula fenestrellata is among the most popular alpine campanulas in cultivation. It was introduced to horticulture in the late [[mckenna-club-des-hachischins-and-nineteenth-century-literary-hashish]] and has remained a rock-garden staple.

Plants require well-drained, gritty, alkaline soil in a sunny or partially shaded position. A limestone chip mulch improves drainage and maintains root zone pH.

Hardy to USDA zone 5, the species tolerates cold winters with reliable snow cover. It struggles in hot, humid summer climates where crown rot is a recurring problem.

Propagation is straightforward from seed or division. Seed sown in spring germinates in 2–3 weeks without pretreatment.

Division of established clumps is best done in [[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]]. Each division should include healthy roots and several growing points.

Plants benefit from shearing after flowering to maintain compact form. Without trimming, centers may open and become unsightly in older clumps.

The species hybridizes readily with Campanula garganica in cultivation, producing intermediate forms. These hybrids are sometimes sold under the name Campanula × hydrocotifolia.

## Ecological Role

The flowers attract a range of pollinators including small bees, hoverflies, and butterflies. The star-shaped corolla provides an accessible landing platform for diverse insect visitors.

Endemic to limestone cliffs, Campanula fenestrellata is part of a specialized chasmophyte community. These cliff-dwelling plants are ecologically significant as relicts of ancient Mediterranean flora.
