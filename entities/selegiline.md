## Selegiline

**Type:** Chemical / Pharmaceutical / MAO Inhibitor
**Category:** Selective MAO-B inhibitor / Neuroprotective agent
**Also known as:** L-Deprenyl, Eldepryl, Emsam, Zelapar

## Overview

Selegiline (L-deprenyl) is a selective irreversible monoamine oxidase-B
(MAO-B) inhibitor developed by Joseph Knoll in Hungary in the 1960s. It was
originally synthesized as a derivative of amphetamine and methamphetamine
but was found to possess unique neuroprotective and neurorestorative
properties independent of its MAO-B inhibition.

Selegiline is FDA-approved for Parkinson's disease as an adjunct to
levodopa therapy and for major depressive disorder via the transdermal
patch formulation (Emsam). At low doses, it selectively inhibits MAO-B,
preserving MAO-A function and avoiding the dietary tyramine restrictions
associated with non-selective [[first-pass-metabolism-and-oral-bioavailability]]: 10% (extensive first-pass metabolism)
- Transdermal bioavailability: ~60-70% (bypasses first-pass)
- Onset: 1-3 hours (oral), within hours (transdermal)
- Half-life: 1.5-2 hours (parent); 18-20 hours (MAO-B inhibition)
- Metabolism: Hepatic via CYP2B6 and CYP3A4
- Excretion: Renal (metabolites)

## Clinical Applications

### Parkinson's Disease

Selegiline is used as an adjunct to levodopa/carbidopa therapy. By
inhibiting dopamine breakdown, it extends the effective duration of each
levodopa dose and may delay the need for levodopa initiation in early
disease. Studies suggest a modest reduction in disease progression when
started early, though this remains debated.
