# 5-MeO-DIPT (Foxy Methoxy)

TIHKAL entry #37. An orally active psychedelic tryptamine with pronounced erotic enhancement properties. Known colloquially as "Foxy" or "Foxy Methoxy." Notable for producing a fast-acting, relatively short psychedelic experience with minimal visual effects but strong sensory and sexual enhancement. Originally co-published with Michael Carter in 1981.

## Chemical Names

- Tryptamine, N,N-diisopropyl-5-methoxy
- N,N-Diisopropyl-5-methoxytryptamine
- 3-[2-(diisopropylamino)ethyl]-5-methoxyindole

## Dosage

| Route | Dose Range |
|-------|-----------|
| Oral | 6–12 mg |

## Duration

4–8 hours

## Erotic Enhancement

The aphrodisiac properties of 5-MeO-DIPT are among its most remarked-upon features. At low doses (6-7 mg), the experience is characterized by a generalized sensual enhancement rather than overt visual psychedelic effects. The drug produces a state Shulgin describes as "a marvelous, sexy place" where "everything was shaded with eroticism." One subject at 12 mg described "a wonderful sexual turn on, where my entire body was alive and alert" that continued to "fill out fuller and fuller for a couple of hours." This property distinguishes it from most other tryptamine psychedelics and has contributed significantly to its popularity.

## Musical Distortion

Musical sound distortions are noted with 5-MeO-DIPT, reminiscent of its analogue without the 5-methoxy group, [[5-meo-dmt-entity-tihkal]] is not orally active at all, and 5-MeO-DET (entry #36) requires MAOI co-administration, 5-MeO-DIPT is readily orally active at 6-12 mg. The bulkier diisopropyl groups appear to confer metabolic stability that allows first-pass survival.

## Dose-Response Characteristics

The dose-response curve shows a relatively narrow window between the sensual low-dose experience (6-7 mg) and the potentially uncomfortable high-dose experience (12+ mg). At the lower end, effects are described as warm, sensual, and social. At the higher end, "body load and discomfort" can dominate, including increased peristalsis, muscle spasms, and hypertension. This narrow therapeutic window may contribute to the mixed reputation of the compound.

## Scheduling and Legal History

5-MeO-DIPT was placed on the U.S. Schedule I list in 2004, following its emergence as a "designer drug" in the club and online marketplace in the early 2000s. The street name "Foxy" or "Foxy Methoxy" became widely known during this period. Its scheduling followed the typical pattern Shulgin described: availability, promotion, broadcast usage, spectacular claims, prohibition, illegality.
