# Methedrine (Methamphetamine)

## Description

Methedrine is a brand name for methamphetamine, a powerful central nervous system (CNS) stimulant of the phenethylamine and amphetamine classes. Originally developed in the early 20th century, methamphetamine produces pronounced effects including euphoria, increased alertness, elevated energy, enhanced focus, and elevated mood. It has been used medically for conditions including narcolepsy, attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), and obesity, though its medical use is now severely restricted due to its high abuse potential.

In *The [[leary-psychedelic-experience-bardo-mapping]]*, Leary references Methedrine in two distinct contexts: as a pre-session mood enhancer to establish a pleasant euphoric baseline, and as a "set-breaker" during sessions to interrupt repetitive game-routines. Critically, Leary specifies that its use must always be with the person's full knowledge and consent.

## Classification

- **Chemical class:** Phenethylamine / amphetamine derivative (N-methylated amphetamine)
- **Pharmacological class:** CNS stimulant; indirect dopamine, norepinephrine, and [[serotonin]] agonist (releasing agent and reuptake inhibitor)
- **Legal status:** Schedule II controlled substance in the United States (accepted medical use with severe restrictions); highly regulated worldwide
- **Therapeutic use (historical/limited):** ADHD (brand name Desoxyn), narcolepsy, obesity (rarely prescribed)

## Dosage Information (per Leary)

The Psychedelic Experience describes two dosage protocols for Methedrine:

| Context | Dosage | Route | Purpose |
|---------|--------|-------|---------|
| Pre-session mood enhancement | Unspecified (low dose) | Likely oral or IM | Induce pleasant euphoric mood prior to session |
| Session set-breaker | 25 mg | Intramuscular (IM) injection | Break repetitive game-routines during [[bardo-instruction-texts-psychedelic-sessions]] |

These doses are consistent with the lower therapeutic range for methamphetamine, producing stimulant and euphoriant effects without the extreme intoxication associated with recreational abuse doses.

## Pharmacokinetics

- **[[mushroom-taste-route-of-administration-and-consumption-barriers]]:** Oral, intranasal, intramuscular, intravenous, or smoked (Leary's context specifies IM)
- **Onset (IM):** Rapid, within minutes
- **Peak effects:** 1–3 hours (IM)
- **Duration of action:** 8–12 hours (significant pharmacological activity)
- **Half-life:** 9–15 hours (highly variable; dependent on urine pH and individual metabolism)
- **Metabolism:** Hepatic, primarily via CYP2D6; significant renal excretion

## Key Facts

1. Methamphetamine was first synthesized in 1893 by Japanese chemist Nagai Nagayoshi from [[ephedrine]].
2. It was widely used by military forces during World War II by both Axis and Allied troops to promote wakefulness, confidence, and aggression.
3. The compound acts primarily by forcing the release of dopamine, norepinephrine, and serotonin from presynaptic vesicles, producing massive increases in synaptic neurotransmitter concentrations.
4. Methamphetamine is more potent than amphetamine due to greater lipid solubility, enabling more efficient blood-brain barrier penetration.
5. The brand name "Methedrine" (Burroughs Wellcome) was one of several pharmaceutical methamphetamine preparations available before scheduling restrictions tightened.
6. Chronic use produces severe neurotoxicity, cognitive impairment, dental decay ("meth mouth"), and profound psychological disturbance including stimulant psychosis.

## Relevance to Psychedelic Studies

Leary's inclusion of Methedrine in *The Psychedelic Experience* reflects a pragmatic, pharmacologically sophisticated approach to managing the set and trajectory of psychedelic sessions. Two distinct applications are described:

### Pre-Session Mood Enhancement

Before the psychedelic session begins, Methedrine may be used to establish a positive, euphoric emotional baseline. This application reflects the principle that the emotional state at onset significantly influences the entire trajectory of the psychedelic experience. A voyager who enters the session in a state of pleasant euphoria and optimistic energy is more likely to have a positive, expansive experience than one who enters in depression, anxiety, or emotional flatness.

This practice parallels the use of [[librium]] for anxious individuals — both are pharmacological tools for optimizing the pre-session psychological state. Where Librium calms and steadies, Methedrine energizes and elevates.

### Set-Breaker During Sessions

At 25 mg IM, Methedrine serves as a "set-breaker" — a tool for interrupting the repetitive thought-patterns, behavioral loops, or "game-routines" that can trap a voyager during the psychedelic experience. In [[jung-and-the-tibetan-book-of-the-dead]] of the Dead framework that Leary employs, these repetitive patterns represent the ego's desperate attempts to reconstitute itself by cycling through familiar defensive structures.

The stimulant properties of Methedrine — heightened alertness, increased cognitive flexibility, elevated mood — can shatter these repetitive loops by forcing the psyche into a new energetic register. This is functionally similar to the use of [[dmt]] as a set-breaker, though through a different pharmacological mechanism (dopaminergic stimulation rather than serotonergic intensification).

### Ethical Framework: Consent

Leary's explicit requirement that Methedrine use always involve the person's full knowledge and consent reflects an important ethical principle. The administration of a stimulant to someone already under the influence of a powerful psychedelic without their awareness would constitute a serious violation of autonomy and trust. This consent requirement distinguishes Leary's approach from the coercive practices documented in programs like MKUltra.

## Modern Perspective and Caveats

From a contemporary standpoint, the use of methamphetamine in psychedelic session work would be considered highly controversial and potentially dangerous. The combination of a powerful stimulant with an ego-dissolving psychedelic could theoretically increase the risk of hypertensive crisis, cardiac arrhythmia, and stimulant psychosis. The neurotoxic potential of methamphetamine is well-established, and modern [[metzner-amplifier-microscope-metaphors-psychedelic-therapy]] protocols do not employ stimulant adjuncts.

Leary's recommendations must be understood in their [[ghouled-field-guide-historical-context]]: the early 1960s, when pharmaceutical methamphetamine was legally available and its long-term risks were not yet fully appreciated.

## Safety Considerations

- Cardiovascular risk: elevated heart rate, blood pressure, risk of arrhythmia or cardiac event.
- Combining methamphetamine with psychedelics increases sympathetic nervous system activation.
- Additive effects with other stimulants or MAO inhibitors can be dangerous.
- Potential for psychological dependence and neurotoxicity with repeated use.
- Modern clinical practice would not endorse this combination.
