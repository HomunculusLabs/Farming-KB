## Overview

**Cladonia chlorophaea**, commonly known as the mealy pixie-cup or green pixie-cup lichen, is a widespread fruticose lichen in the family Cladoniaceae. It forms distinctive cup-shaped podetia arising from a squamulose primary thallus, closely resembling the better-known *Cladonia pyxidata*.

The species is circumpolar in distribution and occurs across boreal and temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere. It is a common component of forest floor communities, particularly in coniferous and mixed woodlands.

*C. chlorophaea* is chemically variable, containing several depsidones and depsides that have historically been important in lichen taxonomy. These [[guzman-allen-gartz-africa-southern-hemisphere-underexplored-neurotropic-fungi]], scattered populations occur in montane South America, New Zealand, and southeastern Australia. These disjunct populations may reflect ancient Gondwanan distributions.

The species is also recorded from rocky outcrops, peatland margins, and acidic soils. It occasionally colonises tree bases and rotting stumps in old-growth forests.

Altitudinal range extends from sea level to approximately 2000 m in mountainous regions. At higher elevations, it is often replaced by more cold-tolerant *Cladonia* species such as *C. rangiferina*.

## Chemistry

The species is known for chemical polymorphism, with several chemotypes recognised based on their secondary metabolite profiles.

The most common chemotype contains **fumarprotocetraric acid** as the major substance, sometimes accompanied by protocetraric acid. This chemotype reacts C+ red and P+ yellow-orange in spot tests.

A second chemotype contains **fumarprotocetraric acid** plus **cryptochlorophaeic acid** or **grayanic acid**. These depsidones are more reactive in spot tests and give stronger colour reactions.

Trace amounts of thamnolic acid, squamatic acid, and usnic acid have been reported from some populations. These minor constituents are useful for fine-scale chemotaxonomic discrimination.

[[mycelium-running-mushroom-antimicrobial-properties-species-chart]]. Fumarprotocetraric acid shows moderate activity against Gram-positive bacteria and some [[bloomfield-fungal-pathogens-and-parasites]].

These lichen acids may also serve defensive functions against herbivory and microbial attack. The chemical variability within the species may reflect local adaptation to different environmental pressures.

## Ecology and Biomonitoring

*Cladonia chlorophaea* is an important ground-dwelling lichen in boreal and temperate forest ecosystems. It contributes to soil stabilisation and moisture retention on the forest floor.

The species is moderately sensitive to air pollution, particularly sulphur dioxide. Its presence in an area indicates relatively clean air, though it is more tolerant than many other *Cladonia* species.

Lichenologists have used *C. chlorophaea* as a bioindicator in air quality monitoring programmes across northern Europe. Its decline in industrial regions documented the impact of acid rain in the 1970s and 1980s.

The species provides microhabitat for various invertebrates, including tardigrades and mites. It is occasionally grazed by reindeer and caribou, though it is less preferred than *Cladonia rangiferina*.

Recovery of *C. chlorophaea* populations has been documented following reductions in industrial emissions. This recolonisation is slower in areas with severely depleted soil lichen communities.

The photobiont partner is typically a unicellular green alga of the genus *Asterochloris*. Photobiont identity can influence the growth rate and chemical profile of the lichen thallus.

In permafrost-affected soils of the boreal zone, *C. chlorophaea* contributes to the development of biological soil crusts. These crusts play a role in nitrogen and carbon cycling in cold ecosystems.
