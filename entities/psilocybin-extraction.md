# Psilocybin Extraction

## Description

Psilocybin extraction refers to the general process of isolating psilocybin and psilocin alkaloids from mushroom fruiting bodies using various solvent systems. Multiple extraction methodologies exist, ranging from simple alcohol-based extractions suitable for home use to laboratory-grade purification processes using specialized solvents. The goal is to concentrate the active alkaloids into a form that is more stable, precisely dosable, and easier to store than raw mushroom material.

## Classification

- **Category**: Alkaloid isolation technique
- **Target compounds**: Psilocybin (4-PO-DMT) and psilocin (4-HO-DMT)
- **Primary solvents**: Ethyl alcohol, methyl alcohol, water, methanol
- **Secondary solvents**: Pentane (for refining)
- **Difficulty**: Moderate to advanced (varies by method)

## Key Facts

- Psilocybin is soluble in both water and alcohol, making extraction accessible with common solvents.
- The two primary alkaloids — psilocybin and psilocin — have different stability profiles; psilocin is more fragile and degrades quickly, while psilocybin is relatively stable.
- Alcohol-based extraction (ethyl or methyl alcohol) is the most common home extraction method.
- Methyl alcohol extraction involves soaking dried, ground mushroom material in methanol for several days, straining, and evaporating.
- Further refinement can be achieved by repeating the extraction process with pentane as a secondary solvent.
- The residue from extraction contains concentrated alkaloids and must be stored at cool temperatures.
- Ensuring all solvent has fully evaporated before ingestion is a critical safety requirement.
- Potency varies considerably between specimens, making consistent dosing a challenge without careful measurement.

## Extraction Methods

### Ethyl Alcohol Method
- Uses 200 proof anhydrous ethanol as solvent
- 24-hour soak with agitation
- Multiple filtration passes
- Fan-assisted evaporation to desired concentration
- Produces a drinkable liqueur

### Methyl Alcohol Method
- Uses methanol (wood alcohol) as solvent
- Several-day soak period
- Strain and evaporate in shallow dish
- Can be refined using pentane as secondary solvent
- Produces a solid or paste residue

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Psilocybin extraction is relevant to cultivation because it provides a method for preserving and standardizing the output of mushroom grows. Rather than consuming variable-potency dried mushrooms, extraction allows for the creation of concentrated preparations with more predictable dosing. The technique also has historical significance in ethnobotanical research, as it parallels the methods used by early researchers like Albert Hofmann to isolate and characterize psilocybin from mushroom specimens collected by R. Gordon Wasson in Mexico.

## Safety Considerations

- Methanol is toxic — all solvent must be completely evaporated before any product is consumed.
- Potency of extracted material is highly variable and should be tested with extreme caution.
- Proper ventilation is essential during all evaporation steps.
- All solvents are flammable and must be kept away from ignition sources.
- Starting material quality directly affects extraction yield — young, pre-sporulation specimens produce the best results.

## Solvent Selection and Chemistry

The choice of solvent significantly impacts extraction quality
and safety. [[methyl-alcohol]] is highly effective at
dissolving alkaloids but poses serious toxicity risks if
any residue remains in the final product. [[psilocybin]]
is readily soluble in both water and polar organic solvents,
while [[psilocin]] is less stable and degrades rapidly when
exposed to heat, light, or oxygen. Food-grade ethanol
provides a safer alternative for home extraction, though
it is more expensive and may be harder to obtain at high
proof concentrations. Water extraction is possible but
produces a less stable product due to faster degradation.

## Storage of Extracted Material

Extracted alkaloid residue should be stored in a cool, dark
environment to minimize degradation. The concentrated form
is more susceptible to oxidation than whole dried mushrooms
due to increased surface area. Small amber glass vials or
sealed containers stored in a refrigerator or freezer
provide the best long-term preservation. Properly stored
extracts can retain potency for months, though potency
declines gradually over time regardless of storage method.

## Historical Context

Early extraction methods were documented in 1970s field
guides alongside the work of [[albert-hofmann]], who first
isolated [[psilocybin]] from specimens collected by
[[r-gordon-wasson]] in Mexico. These home extraction techniques
represent a grassroots parallel to the pharmaceutical
purification processes developed at Sandoz Laboratories.
