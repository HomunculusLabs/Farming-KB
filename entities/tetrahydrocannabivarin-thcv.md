# Tetrahydrocannabivarin (THCV)

**Type:** Chemical Compound (Minor Cannabinoid / Varinoid)
**Category:** Phytocannabinoid
**Also known as:** THCV, tetrahydrocannabivarin, delta-9-THCV

## Overview
Tetrahydrocannabivarin (THCV) is a propyl analogue of THC, classified
as a varinoid cannabinoid due to its three-carbon (propyl) side chain
rather than the five-carbon (pentyl) side chain found in most major
cannabinoids. THCV is considered a minor cannabinoid because it
typically occurs in much lower concentrations than THC or CBD in most
[[african-landrace-sativa-modern-hybrids]] varieties,
particularly from southern and central Africa, can contain relatively
higher THCV levels.

## Key Details

### Chemical Properties
- **Molecular formula:** C19H26O2
- **Classification:** Phytocannabinoid (varinoid series)
- **Side chain:** Propyl (3-carbon), distinguishing it from THC's
  pentyl (5-carbon) chain
- **Molecular weight:** 286.4 g/mol

### Pharmacology
THCV has a complex and dose-dependent pharmacological profile that
differs significantly from THC:

- **CB1 receptor:** At low doses, THCV acts as a CB1 antagonist
  (blocks receptor activity), while at higher doses it can act as a
  partial CB1 agonist. This dual activity makes it unique among
  cannabinoids
- **CB2 receptor:** THCV acts as a partial agonist at CB2 receptors
  in the immune system
- **Non-psychoactive at low doses:** Due to its CB1 antagonism at
  low concentrations, THCV does not produce intoxication at typical
  dietary or low therapeutic doses
- **Appetite suppression:** Unlike THC which stimulates appetite,
  THCV has been investigated for its potential to reduce appetite,
  making it a candidate for weight management applications

### Therapeutic Potential
- **[[haze-lineage-and-legacy-in-modern-breeding]] programs have begun crossing these landrace
varieties with high-THC or high-CBD cultivars to produce strains
with enhanced THCV profiles for therapeutic use. Detection and
quantification of THCV requires specialized analytical methods due
to its low concentrations in most strains.

## Relevance
THCV represents a new frontier in cannabinoid therapeutics because
its pharmacological profile differs fundamentally from THC despite
structural similarities. The CB1 antagonism at low doses means THCV
can modulate the endocannabinoid system without causing intoxication,
which is valuable for [[cannabis-minor-cannabinoids]]
currently under investigation.
