## Description

Annidation is a design and pattern strategy in permaculture involving the "nesting" or stacking of one element, function, or species within another — like a bowl within a bowl, or a vine growing within a tree canopy. The term originates from ecology, where it describes the way organisms occupy nested niches within a larger ecosystem. In [[companion-planting]], [[succession-planting]]:** Following spring peas with summer squash in the same bed

### Functional Annidation
- **Pond as multi-function:** Aquaculture + irrigation + fire protection + microclimate + wildlife
- **Hedge as multi-function:** Windbreak + fodder + privacy + wildlife corridor + bee forage

## Relevance to Cultivation

- Maximizes yield per unit area — critical for small-scale and urban permaculture
- Reduces competition by placing elements in complementary rather than overlapping niches
- Creates beneficial microclimates through layered plantings (shade, wind protection, humidity)
- Increases total system resilience through functional redundancy and diversity
- Enables the "unlimited yield" concept by finding new niches to fill within existing designs
- Applicable to both plant and animal systems, and to integration of the two
- Essential design principle for food forest architecture with 7+ productive layers

## Design Application

When applying annidation, designers should consider:

1. **Vertical space:** Canopy, understory, shrub, herbaceous, ground cover, rhizosphere, vine layers
2. **Temporal windows:** Spring, summer, autumn, winter production niches
3. **Root zones:** Shallow, medium, and deep-rooted species occupying different soil strata
4. **Light requirements:** Sun-loving canopy species creating dappled shade for understory
5. **Functional stacking:** Each element should serve at least 2–3 functions
