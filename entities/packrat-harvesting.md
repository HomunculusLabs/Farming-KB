## Packrat Harvesting

## Description

Packrat harvesting is an innovative, biologically-based crop collection technique described by Mollison in which packrats (genus Neotoma) are recruited as harvesting agents for wild rice ([[zizania-lacustris]]). The rodents naturally carry and store large quantities of sound, cleaned grain into artificial shelters, where it can be collected by the grower. Mollison proposes this as potentially the most efficient and cleanest harvesting method for wild rice, dramatically outperforming expensive mechanical harvesters.

## Classification

- **Category:** Biological harvesting technique
- **Target crop:** Wild rice (Zizania lacustris)
- **Harvesting agent:** Packrats (Neotoma spp.)
- **Type:** Animal-assisted crop collection
- **Geographic context:** Minnesota / North America

## Key Facts

- Packrats naturally carry and store large quantities of sound, cleaned wild rice grain
- Grain is stored in artificial shelters (e.g., engine manifolds or purpose-built chambers)
- Storage sites are insulated and plugged with Typha (cumbungi, cattail) seed silk
- Artificial shelters are placed along narrow fields to attract packrat use
- Breeding packrats can be supplied with alternative winter food, or allowed to keep 15% of stores
- Mechanical harvesters costing $300,000+ gather less than 10% of available grain — packrats may vastly outperform this
- The grain collected by packrats is notably clean and sorted

## The Method in Detail

1. **Provide artificial shelters:** Place suitable chambers (engine manifolds, pipes, or purpose-built boxes) at intervals along narrow wild rice fields
2. **Insulation material:** Packrats naturally use Typha (cattail/cumbungi) seed silk to insulate and plug their storage sites
3. **Collection phase:** Packrats gather grain during the 3–4 week ripening period, carrying it to the artificial shelters
4. **Harvest:** Grower collects from the storage chambers, leaving approximately 15% for the packrats
5. **Sustainability:** Breeding packrats can be maintained with alternative winter food, ensuring a resident population for subsequent seasons

## Parallel Technique: Squirrel Harvesting

Mollison draws a direct parallel with [[bill-mackently]]"s grey squirrel harvesting method for hickory nuts and acorns:

- Grey squirrels collect and store nuts in buried pipes provided by the harvester
- Mackently takes approximately 85% of the harvest, leaving 15% for the squirrels
- Same principle: exploit natural caching behavior, provide convenient storage sites, harvest sustainably

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

Packrat harvesting represents a radical application of [[permaculture]] thinking:

1. **Biological solutions over technological ones:** A free rodent population outperforms a $300,000 machine
2. **Work with nature:** Instead of fighting "pest" rodents, redirect their natural behavior into productive harvesting
3. **Multiple function:** Packrats serve as harvesters, sorters (selecting only sound grain), and cleaners
4. **Fair share (ethic):** The 85/15 split respects the needs of all participants in the system
5. **Appropriate scale:** The method works for small to medium operations where mechanical harvesting is uneconomical
6. **Zero energy input:** No fuel, no electricity, no maintenance — the packrats power themselves

This technique challenges the industrial assumption that mechanization is the path to efficiency. For wild rice specifically, where seed ripens gradually over 3–4 weeks and shatters easily, the packrats' patient, continuous collection matches the crop's natural biology far better than a single mechanical pass.

## Economic Comparison

| Method | Cost | Yield | Cleanliness |
|--------|------|-------|-------------|
| Mechanical harvester | $300,000+ | <10% of grain | Moderate |
| Traditional canoe | Labor-intensive | ~50% of grain | Good |
| Packrat harvesting | Near-zero | Potentially highest | Excellent |
