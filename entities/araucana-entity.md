## Araucana Chicken

## Description

The Araucana is a distinctive breed of domestic chicken originating from South
America, specifically the Araucanía region of Chile. It is most famous for
laying eggs with blue or green-tinted shells, a trait that has earned it and
its derivatives the popular nickname "Easter Egg chicken." The breed's origins
are linked to the indigenous Mapuche people of Chile, who kept these birds for
centuries before they were introduced to the wider world in the early twentieth
century.

Araucanas are medium-sized birds with a compact, upright carriage. They may
exhibit a range of plumage colours including black, black-breasted red, golden
duckwing, silver duckwing, and white. In the United States and some other
standards, the term "Araucana" refers specifically to rumpless birds (lacking
tail vertebrae) with tufted ears, while the more commonly encountered
tailed, bearded variety is classified as the "Ameraucana." In other countries,
particularly the United Kingdom and Australia, the breed standard is broader.

## Key Facts

- Mollison identifies the Araucana as a distinct breed originating from South
  America, separate from the Mediterranean light breeds and the heavy breeds
  derived from the Chinese fowl/Langshan lineage.
- The blue egg gene is dominant and is one of the breed's most distinctive
  genetic traits. When crossed with brown-egg layers, the resulting offspring
  often produce green-shelled eggs.
- True Araucanas (as defined by the US standard) are rumpless and may have ear
  tufts — small feather clusters growing from a fleszy peduncle near the ear.
  The ear tuft gene is lethal when homozygous, meaning chicks inheriting two
  copies of the gene die in the shell.
- The breed is moderately hardy and adapts well to free-range conditions,
  making it a good candidate for [[integrated-pest-management]]:** When rotated through garden beds, orchards,
  or [[food-forest]] zones, Araucanas help control insect pests, slugs, and weed
  seeds while contributing manure to soil fertility.

## Design Considerations

Include Araucanas in diverse poultry flocks for visual interest, unique egg
colour, and reliable foraging behaviour. They pair well with heavier breeds in
mixed flocks, where their alertness provides early warning of aerial predators.
Plan poultry rotations through Zones I–III using mobile housing (chicken
tractors or range shelters) to maximise their soil-building and pest-control
contributions while protecting crops from scratching damage. Ensure adequate
shelter from extreme weather, as the breed's moderate size means it is less
cold-hardy than the heaviest breeds.
