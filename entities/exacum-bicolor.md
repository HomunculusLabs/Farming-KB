## Overview

**Exacum bicolor** is a small herbaceous annual or short-lived perennial in the family Gentianaceae, native to tropical South and [[psilocybe-southeast-asia-pacific]].

It is notable for its attractive two-toned flowers, with white petals that grade to lavender or purple at the tips, giving rise to the specific epithet "bicolor."

The species is occasionally cultivated as a bedding or container plant in tropical and subtropical gardens.

Exacum bicolor is less well known in horticulture than its relative *[[exacum-affine]]* (Persian violet) but shares many ornamental qualities.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Exacum bicolor belongs to the genus Exacum, which comprises approximately 70 species distributed across the Old World tropics.

Plants grow 10–30 cm tall with erect to ascending, somewhat branching stems.

Leaves are opposite, ovate to elliptic, 1–3 cm long, dark green, and sessile or with very short petioles.

Flowers are terminal or axillary, 1–2 cm across, with four (occasionally five) petals.

The petals are white at the base grading to lavender-purple at the tips and margins, creating a distinctive bicolor effect.

The flower center features prominent yellow anthers that contrast sharply with the pale petals.

Flowering occurs throughout the year in tropical conditions but peaks during the wet season.

Fruits are small ovoid capsules 3–5 mm long containing numerous tiny seeds.

The root system is fibrous and relatively shallow, adapted to the thin soils of rocky habitats.

Stems are somewhat succulent and brittle, easily broken but capable of rooting at lower nodes where they contact moist soil.

## Distribution and Habitat

Exacum bicolor is native to tropical Asia, including Sri Lanka, southern India, Bangladesh, Myanmar, and Thailand.

It grows at low to moderate elevations (0–800 meters) in open, moist habitats.

Typical habitats include grassy slopes, stream banks, forest clearings, and rocky outcrops in seasonal forests.

The species prefers well-drained soils with consistent moisture and partial shade to full sun exposure.

In Sri Lanka, it is found in the wet zone and intermediate zone, often growing on rock surfaces in streamside habitats.

Populations in the Western Ghats of India occur in grassy balds and disturbed forest margins.

## Ecology

Exacum bicolor is an annual or short-lived perennial that behaves as a pioneer species in disturbed sites.

It colonizes open areas after monsoon rains, completing its life cycle during the wet season.

The flowers are visited by small bees and flies attracted to the contrasting yellow anthers and pale petals.
