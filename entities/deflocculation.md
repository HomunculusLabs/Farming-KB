## Description

Deflocculation is a chemical process in soil science where the aggregate (crumb) structure of clay soils breaks down due to the displacement of calcium ions by sodium ions on clay particle surfaces. When sodium exceeds approximately 15% of exchangeable cations, the attractive forces between clay platelets are disrupted, causing them to disperse rather than flocculate (aggregate). This process is central to the mechanism of [[soil-collapse]] in arid and semi-arid regions.

## Chemical Process

1. **Normal soil structure**: Calcium ions (Ca²⁺) bridge between negatively charged clay platelets, creating stable crumbs with pore spaces for air and water infiltration.
2. **Sodium introduction**: When sodium chloride (NaCl) from salt deposits or rising groundwater dissolves, it releases sodium ions (Na⁺) and chloride ions (Cl⁻).
3. **Ion exchange**: Sodium ions, being more mobile, displace calcium ions from clay particle surfaces.
4. **Structural collapse**: With calcium displaced, the clay platelets repel each other (due to their negative charges no longer being bridged) and disperse into individual platelets.
5. **Sealing and hardpan formation**: Dispersed clay particles migrate downward with percolating water and cement the subsoil into a dense, hydrophobic (water-repellent) block.
6. **Cascade effect**: The sealed subsoil prevents deep infiltration, increasing surface ponding and evaporation, which concentrates more salt at the surface — a self-reinforcing cycle.

## Key Facts

- The 15% sodium threshold is a critical tipping point known to soil scientists as the Exchangeable Sodium Percentage (ESP).
- Deflocculation is irreversible without intervention — it does not self-correct when salt levels decrease.
- The process affects both the surface soil (sealing) and the subsoil (hardpan formation), creating a double barrier to water infiltration.
- Mollison describes the result as "a cement without the air spaces of true soil."

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

- **Gypsum application**: Adding gypsum (calcium sulphate) supplies calcium ions that can displace sodium and restore flocculation. This is the standard remediation treatment for sodic soils.
- **Prevention over cure**: Once deflocculation occurs, remediation is extremely difficult and expensive. Maintaining tree cover and preventing salt mobilisation is far more effective.
- **[[interceptor-banks]] and careful water management are essential in salt-affected landscapes — concentrated water flow through sodic soils accelerates the process.
- **[[coleman-compost-and-organic-fertility-management]] matter**: High organic matter content buffers against deflocculation by maintaining soil structure through biological rather than purely chemical bonds.
