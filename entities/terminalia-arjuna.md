# Terminalia arjuna

## Overview

Terminalia arjuna, commonly known as the Arjuna tree, is a large evergreen tree belonging to the family Combretaceae.
It is one of the most revered medicinal plants in Ayurveda, where its bark has been used as a cardiotonic for over 2,500 years.
The tree holds deep cultural and religious significance in Hinduism, appearing prominently in the epic Mahabharata.
Its use as a heart remedy is documented in some of the oldest classical texts of Indian medicine, and modern pharmacological research has validated many of its traditional cardioprotective claims.
This has led to its incorporation into contemporary herbal formulations for cardiovascular health worldwide.
The Arjuna tree is sometimes referred to as the "guardian of the heart" in Ayurvedic literature, reflecting its singular status among medicinal plants for cardiovascular indications.
Its bark remains one of the most commercially significant Ayurvedic raw materials in global trade.

The crown is spreading and forms a dense canopy that provides substantial shade.
The bark is the most distinctive and medicinally important feature of the tree.
It is smooth, thick, and grey-pink to pinkish-brown in color, exfoliating in large, thin, papery sheets.
The outer bark is grey, while the inner bark is smooth and pinkish-red, yielding a characteristic appearance when fresh sections are cut.
Beneath the bark, the wood is hard, durable, and whitish to pale yellow, making it commercially valuable for furniture and construction.
The characteristic peeling bark gives the tree a distinctive mottled appearance identifiable even from a distance.
Leaves are simple, oblong or elliptic-oblong, 10 to 25 cm long and 5 to 12 cm wide, arranged suboppositely on slender branchlets.
They are coriaceous (leathery) with a glossy dark green upper surface and a paler lower surface.
New leaves are often coppery-red before maturing to their characteristic dark green, and a pair of small glands is typically present near the base of the leaf blade.
Flowers are small, sessile, and creamy-white to pale yellow, borne in dense axillary or terminal spikes 7 to 15 cm long.
Each flower has a cupular calyx with five lobes, absent petals, and ten stamens in two whorls.
Flowering typically occurs from March to June, though timing varies with geography and local climate.
The fruit is a drupaceous, five-winged nut, 2.5 to 5 cm in diameter, pale green when immature and turning brown or reddish-brown at maturity.
The five wings are fibrous and papery, aiding in wind dispersal, and each fruit contains a [[kava-traditional-preparation-ceremonial-context]] is arjuna kshara, an alkaline extract produced by burning the bark and dissolving the resulting white ash in water, historically used for abdominal tumors, urinary disorders, and metabolic conditions.
The bark was also boiled in milk to prepare "arjuna ksheerapaka," a decoction believed to be particularly effective as a heart tonic and nervine.
