# Chicken Forage System (Mollison Zone Two)

## Description

A comprehensive, integrated poultry forage system designed by [[bill-mollison]] for zone two permaculture design. The system replaces conventional grain feeding with a diverse perennial forage polyculture that provides year-round nutrition for chickens while simultaneously improving soil, managing pests, and producing commercial nut crops. Mollison asserts that "never in the history of the world has anybody designed and implemented animal forage systems" of this type.

## Classification

- **Category**: Integrated permaculture design system
- **Zone**: Zone two
- **Primary animal**: Chickens (Gallus gallus domesticus)
- **Complementary animals**: Ducks (with sheep), pigeons, quail

## Key Components

### Forage Trees and Shrubs
- **White mulberry**: 17% protein, primary forage during bearing season. As good as a double crop of grain.
- **Siberian pea tree**: Drought-period forage; peasants fed poultry on it alone.
- **Black locust**: Best fence post material, provides 10,000+ pound leaf drop per acre.
- **Hawthorn group**: Great winter forage.
- **Mountain ash**: Winter forage.
- **Sunflower**: Seed forage, used in mud balls for frost-heave areas.

### Ground Forage
- **Comfrey**: High-protein greens for summer drought periods.
- **Cleavers**: Summer greens forage.
- **Chard**: Grown more for chickens than for humans in some gardens.
- **Jerusalem artichoke**: Tubers brought into forage system.
- **Clover**: Planted in frost-heave patches.

### Commercial Crops (Integrated)
- **Hazelnuts**: Yield quadruples inside chicken forage areas compared to outside.
- **Chinese chestnuts**: High nitrogen demand, high commercial value.
- **Apples**: Windfalls consumed by chickens; chicken-orchard integration.

## System Design

### Stocking Rates
- **Maximum**: 400 chickens per acre (break-even point for bare soil prevention).
- **Optimal**: 50-60 chickens per flock, up to 80 maximum.
- **Flock separation**: 150 feet between chicken houses (200 feet ideal).
- **Roosters**: 5 per flock to prevent hen wandering.
- **Multiple breeds**: Four different breeds can run on one acre.

### Health Benefits
- Chickens maintained on forage systems show minimal disease.
- Layers continue producing for 6+ years (vs. conventional 3-year systems).
- Often die at roost having laid the day before.
- No need to cull layers.

### Duck Integration
- Ducks with sheep are excellent — ducks eat shallow water snails (fluke carriers) and fluke eggs.
- Ducks are great fly catchers.
- Ducks don't compete with sheep for food.
- Creates a high-hygienic situation.

## Nutrient Cycling

- **Chicken manure**: A grain-fed animal's manure keeps four chickens on grain content alone.
- **Insect production**: Dead animals hung in chicken runs convert into larvae, sharply reducing local fly populations.
- **Parasite control**: Chickens eat encysted parasite eggs from pasture.
- **Pest reduction**: Chickens with plum and cherry trees sharply reduce crawling and flying pests.

## Economic Analysis

- A half-acre system produces 5,000+ pounds of chicken forage annually.
- Plus 2,000-3,000 pounds of protein from insects and forages.
- Can sustain 40 chickens year-round or more with seasonal supplementation.
- Eliminates need for threshing, bagging, and purchasing grain.
- Combined with a Fukuoka grain plot, provides complete poultry feed system.
