# Pyridoxamine Phosphate

Pyridoxamine phosphate is the aminated phosphate form of vitamin B6.
It is usually abbreviated PMP in enzyme-mechanism literature.
The name pyridoxamine 5'-phosphate indicates that the phosphate is attached to the 5-hydroxymethyl position of pyridoxamine.
PMP is closely paired with [[pyridoxal-phosphate]] in aminotransferase chemistry.
Its molecular formula is C8H13N2O5P.
Its molecular weight is about 248.17 g/mol.
PubChem lists pyridoxamine phosphate as compound CID 1053.
Its InChIKey is ZMJGSOSNSPKHNH-UHFFFAOYSA-N.
The listed IUPAC name is [4-(aminomethyl)-5-hydroxy-6-methyl-3-pyridinyl]methyl dihydrogen phosphate.
PMP is best understood as a coenzyme state rather than as a dietary supplement category.

## Structural Features

PMP shares the substituted pyridine ring scaffold of the vitamin B6 family.
It contains a phosphate ester, a phenolic hydroxyl group, a methyl group, and a ring nitrogen.
The defining feature is an aminomethyl group where PLP has an aldehyde.
This aminomethyl group is the chemical record of an amino group accepted during transamination.
The phosphate group keeps PMP strongly polar and supports tight binding in enzyme active sites.
The ring system still provides conjugation and acid-base behavior relevant to catalysis.
Compared with PLP, PMP is less electrophilic because it lacks the aldehyde carbonyl.
That difference explains why PMP is a donor form in transamination rather than the initial imine-forming acceptor.
In enzyme-bound form, PMP sits in the same [[cofactor]] pocket used by PLP.
The surrounding protein controls when PMP releases its amino group to a keto acid substrate.

## Relationship to PLP

PMP and PLP are interconverted during aminotransferase catalytic cycles.
PLP begins many transamination reactions as an aldehyde-containing amino-group acceptor.
An [[fish-amino-acid]] leaves as an alpha-keto acid.
The cofactor is then present as PMP.
PMP subsequently reacts with a second alpha-keto acid.
It donates the amino group to that keto acid to form a new [[fish-amino-acid-faa]].
The donation regenerates PLP and completes the catalytic cycle.
This ping-pong logic lets aminotransferases move nitrogen without releasing free ammonia.
The PLP-PMP pair is therefore central to reversible nitrogen exchange in metabolism.

## Transamination Context

Alanine aminotransferase cycles between PLP and PMP while interconverting alanine and pyruvate.
Aspartate aminotransferase cycles between PLP and PMP while interconverting aspartate and oxaloacetate.
Branched-chain aminotransferases use the same cofactor logic for leucine, isoleucine, and valine metabolism.
Tyrosine aminotransferase and many microbial aminotransferases also rely on the PLP-PMP couple.
The overall reaction normally transfers an amino group from one carbon skeleton to another.
No net oxidation or reduction is required in the basic aminotransferase cycle.
Instead, PMP functions as a temporary carrier of the amino group.
The amino group is stored on the cofactor long enough for the first product to leave and the second substrate to enter.
This makes PMP a chemically precise shuttle, not merely a passive intermediate.
The reversibility of transamination makes PMP relevant to both [[cho-fish-amino-acid-preparation]] synthesis and [[knf-fish-amino-acid]] degradation.

## Mechanistic Role

Formation of PMP usually follows a sequence of external aldimine, quinonoid, ketimine, and hydrolysis steps.
PLP first forms an external aldimine with the amino acid substrate.
After proton transfer and electron delocalization, a ketimine intermediate can form.
Hydrolysis of the ketimine releases the corresponding keto acid and leaves PMP bound to the enzyme.
In the second half-reaction, the sequence runs in the opposite direction with a keto acid substrate.
PMP's amino group is transferred to the keto acid through enzyme-guided imine chemistry.
The enzyme active site prevents the amino group from being lost randomly to solvent.
Specific acid-base residues control proton movements that would be slow or unselective in solution.
The same cofactor scaffold can support many substrate pairs because proteins tune recognition.
PMP's importance is therefore inseparable from the enzyme architecture that holds it.
