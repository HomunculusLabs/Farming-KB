## Arundo donax

## Overview

Arundo donax, commonly known as giant reed, is a tall [[cannabis-controlled-pollination-seed-production]] has been documented in the field, meaning every
  wild population is a clone derived from human introduction.

## Morphology

- **Height:** 3–10 m (10–33 ft), forming dense bamboo-like cane stands up to
  50,000 stems per hectare.
- **Stems (culms):** Erect, hollow, 2–3 cm diameter, segmented with visible
  nodes; light green to grey-green, becoming woody and tan with age; walls
  3–5 mm thick.
- **Leaves:** Alternate, 30–60 cm long × 2–6 cm wide, lanceolate, with a
  conspicuous midrib; margins rough with silica deposits; ligule membranous,
  2–4 mm.
- **Inflorescence:** Large feathery panicle, 30–60 cm long, silvery-white to
  purplish, appearing late summer to autumn; often sterile.
- **Roots:** Extensive rhizome system extending 1+ meters deep and spreading
  laterally several meters per year; roots can penetrate 3–4 m into soil,
  forming dense mats.
- **Reproduction:** Produces few or no viable seeds; spreads by rhizome and
  stem fragment dispersal — stem pieces as short as 5 cm can root when in
  contact with moist soil.

## Distribution and Habitat

- **Native range:** Eastern Mediterranean, Middle East, and South Asia.
- **Introduced/invasive:** Widely naturalized in [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-south-america]], Australia, South Africa,
  and [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-hawaii-pacific-islands]].
- Grows in riparian zones, floodplains, wetlands, and disturbed areas;
  tolerates saline (up to 20 dS/m), alkaline, and waterlogged soils.
- Hardy to USDA zones 7–10; tolerates light frost but dies back in severe
  cold, regrowing from rhizomes in spring.
- In California alone, an estimated 40,000+ hectares are infested, with
  annual control costs exceeding $25 million.

## Uses

- **Bioenergy:** High biomass productivity (20–40 tonnes dry matter/ha/year)
  makes it a leading candidate for cellulosic ethanol, biogas, and pellet
  production; requires low nitrogen input (30–60 kg N/ha).
