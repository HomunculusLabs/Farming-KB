## Overview

Canavalia gladiata, commonly known as the sword bean, is a perennial climbing
legume in the family Fabaceae. Native to tropical Asia and possibly Africa, it
is cultivated across the tropics for its edible seeds, green pods, and as a
[[cover-crop]] [[comparison-arid-vs-tropical-permaculture]] designs.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Kingdom:** Plantae — **Family:** Fabaceae — **Subfamily:** Faboideae — **Genus:** Canavalia

Canavalia gladiata (Jacq.) DC. was described by Augustin Pyramus de Candolle.
The genus Canavalia contains approximately 60 species distributed across tropical
and subtropical regions worldwide. C. gladiata is closely related to the jack
bean (C. ensiformis), and hybridization between the two species has been reported.

The plant is a vigorous perennial vine with twining stems reaching 3–10 meters
in length. Stems are angular, pubescent when young, becoming glabrous with age.

Leaves are trifoliate with ovate to rhomboid leaflets 7–15 cm long and 5–10 cm
wide. The terminal leaflet is larger than the lateral pair. Leaflet texture is
smooth with prominent venation on the abaxial surface.

Inflorescences are axillary racemes bearing large, papilionaceous flowers.
Flowers are 2–3 cm long with a broad standard petal, typically white to pale
pink, sometimes marked with a purple keel. Flowering occurs 90–120 days after
sowing.

Pods are linear-oblong, 20–35 cm long and 2.5–4 cm wide, with a pronounced
beak at the distal end. Each pod contains 5–15 large seeds, ellipsoid to
reniform, 1.5–2.5 cm long. Seed color varies from white through pink, red,
and mottled patterns.

## Nutritional Value and Antinutrients

Dry seeds contain 22–30% protein, 55–60% carbohydrate, and 1–2% fat. The
protein is relatively rich in lysine but deficient in sulfur-containing amino
acids methionine and cysteine.

Major antinutritional factors include concanavalin A (a lectin), canavanine
(a non-protein amino acid), trypsin inhibitors, and phytic acid. Raw seed
consumption causes gastrointestinal distress and potential toxicity.
