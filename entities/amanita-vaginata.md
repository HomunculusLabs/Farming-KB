## Overview

*Amanita vaginata* (Bull.) Lam. is an edible basidiomycete mushroom commonly
known as the grisette. It belongs to *Amanita* section *Vaginatae* and is
widely distributed across temperate and [[boil-a-bag-spore-print-technique-jarrold]].
Unlike many *Amanita* species, *A. vaginata* lacks both a universal veil and
a [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]], though foragers must exercise caution to distinguish it
from toxic lookalikes in the *Amanita* genus.

## Taxonomy and Classification

Basidiomycota → Agaricomycetes → Agaricales → **Amanitaceae → *Amanita***
→ section *Vaginatae* → ***A. vaginata***

- **Basionym:** *Agaricus vaginatus* Bull. (1784); **Authority:** (Bull.) Lam. (1783)
- **Synonyms:** *Amanitopsis vaginata* (Bull.) Roze, *Vaginata plumbea* Schaeff.
- **Common names:** Grisette, grissette (French), grauer Scheidling (German)

Section *Vaginatae* is characterized by the absence of a volva and the
presence of a sac-like volva remnant (saccate volva) or its complete absence.
*A. vaginata* is the type species of the section.

## Morphology

**Cap:** 4–12 cm, ovate to convex becoming plano-convex to depressed at
center; surface smooth, slightly sticky when wet, gray to brownish-gray,
sometimes with a darker umbo; margin striate, extending to one-third of the
cap radius; flesh white, thin, and fragile.

**Gills:** Free to subfree, crowded, white to cream; lamellulae numerous.

**Stipe:** 8–16 × 0.8–2 cm, central, cylindrical, slightly tapering upward;
surface white, fibrillose, sometimes with grayish scales below the annulus;
base bulbous, with a saccate volva that is often buried and easily detached;
[[partial-veil-tropism-protection-before-spore-release]] leaving a thin, fragile, evanescent annulus that frequently
falls away.

**Spores:** 8–12 × 6–9 μm, broadly ellipsoid to ellipsoid, smooth, thin-
walled, hyaline, non-amyloid; [[spore-print]] white.

**Odor and taste:** Mild, pleasant mushroomy odor; taste mild, sometimes
slightly nutty.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Amanita vaginata* has a broad circumboreal distribution, found throughout
Europe, Asia, and [[huckleberries-and-wild-berries-of-north-america]]. In North America it occurs from Alaska to
Mexico, though western and eastern populations may represent distinct
phylogenetic species within the *A. vaginata* complex.

The species forms [[ectomycorrhizal-associations]] with a wide range of trees
including *Betula* (birch), *Quercus* (oak), *Pinus* (pine), *Picea*
(spruce), *Populus* (poplar/aspen), and *Fagus* (beech). It fruits singly or
in scattered groups from summer through autumn, preferring moist forest
floors with duff or moss cover.
