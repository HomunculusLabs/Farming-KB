## Overview

**Carex vesicaria**, commonly known as bladder sedge, is a robust perennial sedge of the family Cyperaceae. It has a circumboreal distribution across Europe, northern Asia, and North America.

The species is named for its conspicuously inflated utricles, which are among the largest and most distinctive of any European sedge. These balloon-like fruits make the plant readily identifiable in the field.

Bladder sedge is a plant of wetland margins, shallow water, and damp meadows. It is a characteristic component of fen and marsh vegetation in the boreal and temperate zones.

The species is ecologically important as a provider of habitat structure in wetland plant communities. Its tall, sturdy culms create microhabitats for invertebrates and emerging amphibians.

The specific epithet vesicaria derives from the Latin vesica, meaning bladder, referring to the inflated utricles. This character is unique among European Carex species of similar size and habitat.

Hybridisation with Carex rostrata has been documented in areas where the two species co-occur. The hybrid, known as Carex × forficula, is intermediate in morphology and partially sterile.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Carex vesicaria** L. was described by Linnaeus in 1753. It is placed in Carex section Vesicariae within subgenus Carex, alongside other large-utricle species such as Carex rostrata.

The plant grows 60–120 cm tall from short, stout rhizomes. Culms are thick, trigonous, and conspicuously leafy towards the base, giving the plant a tussock-like appearance.

Leaf blades are 5–12 mm wide, flat, and bright green. They are distinctly rough along the margins, a characteristic that aids identification when the inflorescence is not present.

The inflorescence is large and conspicuous, consisting of 3–6 elongated spikes. The terminal spike is male, and the lateral spikes are female, pendulous, and well-separated on long peduncles.

Glumes are pale green with a brownish midrib. Uricles are the most diagnostic feature: they are 8–12 mm long, broadly inflated, and prominently nerved with a glossy surface.

The inflated utricles are pale green when immature, turning brown at maturity. They remain attached to the spike for an extended period, providing a persistent identification feature.

Flowering occurs from May to July, with fruits persisting on the plant well into autumn. The large, buoyant utricles can float on water, aiding dispersal in wetland habitats.

## Distribution and Habitat

Bladder sedge has a wide circumboreal distribution. In Europe, it occurs from Scandinavia and the British Isles southward to the Mediterranean mountains and eastward through Russia to Siberia.

In North America, it ranges from Alaska across Canada to the northeastern and north-central United States. Isolated populations occur in the Rocky Mountains and the [[query-how-to-protect-plants-from-frost-and-extend-the-growing-season]]. Beyond this depth, the species cannot establish from seed.

In Scandinavia, bladder sedge occurs along the margins of boreal lakes and slow-flowing rivers. It forms extensive stands in shore meadows that are periodically inundated.

Soil preferences include mineral-rich clays, silts, and organic muck soils. It is absent from pure peat substrates and strongly acidic organic soils.

## Ecology

**Carex vesicaria** is a prominent species in fen and marsh plant communities across its range. In the British National Vegetation Classification, it is characteristic of several fen meadow and swamp communities.

The tall, dense growth form provides important structural habitat for wetland invertebrates. Dragonfly nymphs, water beetles, and aquatic snails use the submerged portions of the culms as perching and feeding sites.

The inflated utricles are an effective dispersal mechanism in [[nutrient-cycling-in-soil]] wetland ecosystems through its substantial annual litter production. Decomposing Carex vesicaria leaf litter releases nutrients that support algal and [[constructed-wetland-design]] naturalistic water gardens. Its tall stature and architectural form make it an effective accent plant at pond margins.
