# Red Thread Disease

## Description

Red thread is a common turfgrass disease caused by fungi in the genus *Laetisaria*, most notably *Laetisaria fuciformis*. It is easily recognized by the pinkish-red, thread-like fungal structures (stromata) that extend from infected leaf blades. In the soil food web framework, red thread is among the diseases suppressed by fungally dominated compost teas, which establish competitive fungal communities that prevent *Laetisaria* from colonizing grass tissues.

## Classification

- **Causal organism:** *Laetisaria* spp. (primarily *L. fuciformis*, formerly *Corticium fuciforme*)
- **Type:** Foliar turfgrass disease
- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Basidiomycota
- **Visual diagnostic:** Pink-red, gelatinous, thread-like stromata extending from leaf blades
- **Host range:** Most cool-season turfgrass species

## Key Facts

- Caused by *Laetisaria* species — basidiomycete fungi that produce distinctive red, thread-like fruiting structures
- Easily identified by pinkish-red, gelatinous threads (stromata) protruding from affected leaf blades
- Affects most cool-season turfgrasses including red fescue, perennial ryegrass, and Kentucky bluegrass
- Favored by cool, humid conditions with extended leaf wetness (spring and autumn)
- Most damaging in nitrogen-deficient turf — low nitrogen availability weakens grass and favors the pathogen
- Suppressed by fungally dominated compost teas through competitive exclusion on leaf surfaces
- The disease is often a symptom of underlying nutrient deficiency or poor soil biology
- Cultural controls include:
  - Adequate nitrogen fertilization (organic sources)
  - Improved air circulation and drainage
  - Reduced leaf wetness duration
  - Maintaining active soil food web biology

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Red thread disease connects to mycological practice in several ways:

- **Basidiomycete pathogen:** As a basidiomycete, *Laetisaria* is related to many beneficial decomposer fungi — understanding its ecology informs both disease management and appreciation of basidiomycete diversity
- **Stromata production:** The visible red threads are specialized fungal structures (stromata/coralloid mycelium) that produce spores — understanding this life cycle stage helps with timing of compost tea applications
- **Nitrogen connection:** Red thread is unusual among turf diseases in being associated with nitrogen deficiency rather than excess — soil food web management must balance fungal and bacterial nitrogen cycling
- **Biological suppression:** Fungal teas introduce competing fungi that occupy leaf surfaces, preventing *Laetisaria* colonization through competitive exclusion
