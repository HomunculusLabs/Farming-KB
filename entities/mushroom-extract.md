## Description

Mushroom extracts (also known as tinctures) are concentrated liquid preparations made by extracting bioactive compounds from mushrooms using alcohol, hot water, or a combination of both. They are used primarily for medicinal purposes but are often expensive and difficult to quality-assess when purchased commercially, motivating many cultivators to produce their own. Tradd Cotter describes two primary extraction methods: alcohol extraction (preferred for safety) and hot water extraction (used as a supplementary step), as well as an alternative method using grain spawn as the source material.

## Classification

- **Category**: Value-added mushroom product / Medicinal preparation
- **Type**: Liquid extract / Tincture
- **Primary methods**: Alcohol extraction, hot water extraction, dual extraction
- **Shelf life**: Several years (if alcohol content maintained and container sealed)
- **Storage**: Dark container, refrigerated

## Extraction from Fresh or Dried Mushrooms

### Alcohol Extraction (Recommended)
1. Clean and chop or blend mushrooms (fresh or dried)
2. Place in glass or ceramic container with lid
3. Add high-proof alcohol (95% ABV preferred, such as Everclear)
4. Dosage: 1 gram dried mushroom (or fresh equivalent) per teaspoon of alcohol
5. For a 1-liter bottle (~200 teaspoons): ~200 grams dried mushroom needed
6. Example: Oyster mushrooms (90% water when fresh) → ½ cup dried/powdered or 4¼ cups fresh per liter
7. Let sit for at least 14 days at room temperature (65–80°F / 18–27°C)
8. Strain out mushrooms
9. Dilute down to 20–23% alcohol by volume to prevent degradation of active ingredients
10. With 95% alcohol: dilute at ratio of 1 part extract concentrate to 4 parts distilled water

### Hot Water Extraction (Supplementary)
- Performed on spent mushrooms after alcohol extraction to capture water-soluble constituents
- Cover spent mushrooms with boiling water by about 1 inch
- Cover container and let sit for several hours to overnight
- Strain and use liquid to dilute alcohol extract
- This dual-extraction approach captures both alcohol-soluble and water-soluble compounds

### Alternative Alcohols
- Any drinkable alcohol can be used, not just Everclear
- Lower-proof alcohols require longer extraction time
- The alcohol needs time to break down mycelium molecular bonds and release active constituents
- Higher proof = faster and more complete extraction

## Safety Considerations

- Alcohol extraction is preferred over hot water alone because pathogenic bacteria can survive hot water treatment but are killed by alcohol
- Bacteria on mushroom tissue can pass through filters into hot-water-only extracts
- Always dilute final extract to 20–23% ABV to prevent alcohol from degrading active ingredients
- Store in dark container in refrigerator
- Seal container to maintain alcohol content

## Extraction from Grain Spawn

A more complex method (briefly referenced by Cotter) uses grain spawn as the source material rather than fruiting bodies. This approach allows extraction from mycelium-colonized grain, which may contain different concentrations of bioactive compounds compared to fruiting bodies.

## Dosage and Usage

- Therapeutic dose based on original mushroom concentration
- 1 gram dried mushroom equivalent per teaspoon at preparation strength
- After dilution, adjust dosage accordingly
- Can be taken directly or added to beverages

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Mushroom extracts represent one of the highest-value products that cultivators can produce from their crops. By converting fresh or dried mushrooms into shelf-stable medicinal tinctures, growers can access the supplement and natural medicine market, which commands premium pricing. The dual extraction method ensures maximum recovery of both alcohol-soluble compounds (like triterpenes in reishi) and water-soluble compounds (like beta-glucans), creating a more complete medicinal product. Home preparation allows quality control that commercial products often lack, giving cultivators a competitive advantage through transparency and freshness.
