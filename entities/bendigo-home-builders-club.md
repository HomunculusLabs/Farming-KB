# Bendigo Home Builder's Club

## Description

The Bendigo Home Builder's Club is a community labour exchange organization based in
Victoria, Australia, documented by [[bill-mollison]] as a model for informal community
economics. Comprising 35 member families, the club operates on a system of mutual labour
credits where members build each other's homes using a standardized hour-based exchange
system. It exemplifies the [[permaculture-principle-apply-self-regulation]] of cooperative [[mycelial-network-resource-sharing-plants]] and
demonstrates how communities can meet fundamental needs (shelter) without reliance on
conventional financial systems.

## Classification

- **Category:** Community organization / Labour exchange
- **Domain:** Permaculture -- [[informal-economy-and-community-currencies]], community cooperation
- **Location:** Bendigo, Victoria, Australia
- **Scale:** 35 member families
- **Function:** Mutual aid in housing construction

## Organizational Structure

- **Membership:** 35 families
- **Annual Dues:** $5 per family per year (covers printing and newsletter distribution only)
- **Roles:** Each member can be either a recipient or donor of labour
- **Coordination:** A designated Labour Organiser manages the balance of payments and
  dispatches labourers to recipients

## How the Labour Exchange Works

1. **Unit of Exchange:** Hours of labour -- all labour is considered equal regardless of
   the type of work performed
2. **Legal Framework:** Uses a standard labour exchange form that is legally binding
3. **Accounting:** For every hour of work performed, the recipient is debited and the
   donor is credited
4. **Minimum Credit Requirement:** A recipient must have at least 60 hours in credit
   before they can receive labour from the group
5. **Labour Organiser Role:** Sorts out balance of payments and dispatches workers to
   where they are needed

## Key Facts

- Founded on the principle that all labour hours are of equal value
- The $5 annual fee is minimal, covering only administrative costs
- The legally binding exchange form ensures accountability
- The 60-hour credit threshold ensures commitment before receiving benefits
- Members build individual homes (not communal housing)
- The system enables families to construct homes without mortgages or large cash outlays

## Operating Principles

- **Reciprocity:** Every hour given is an hour earned; the system balances over time
- **Equality:** No distinction between skilled and unskilled labour in accounting
- **Self-governance:** Members manage the system internally through the Labour Organiser
- **Minimal overhead:** Near-zero administrative cost keeps the system accessible
- **Legal accountability:** Binding forms prevent free-riding and ensure follow-through

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Infrastructure Building:** The same labour exchange model can be applied to building
  cultivation infrastructure -- greenhouses, shade houses, [[mushroom-fruiting-chambers]],
  [[composting-systems]], and irrigation installations
- **Community Farms:** Groups of growers can adapt the Bendigo model to collectively
  establish market gardens, mushroom yards, or community orchards
- **Seasonal Labour:** Cultivation has peak labour demands (planting, harvesting, substrate
  preparation) that are ideal for round-robin labour exchanges
- **Processing Facilities:** Shared construction of drying rooms, canning kitchens, or
  [[mushroom-substrate-sterilization-and-pasteurization]] facilities through labour credits
- **Land Preparation:** Swales, contour banks, raised beds, and other [[permaculture-earthworks]]
  can be built cooperatively using the same hour-based exchange
- **Skill Transfer:** New cultivators benefit from experienced members' knowledge during
  cooperative work sessions, combining labour exchange with mentorship
- **Resilience:** Communities using labour exchange systems maintain food production capacity
  regardless of economic conditions, since cultivation depends on shared labour not cash

## Broader Application

The Bendigo model demonstrates several [[mollisonian-permaculture-principles]]:

- **Relative Location:** Members live close enough to assist each other
- **Each Element Performs Multiple Functions:** Labour exchange builds homes, creates
  community bonds, transfers skills, and provides meaningful work simultaneously
- **Small-Scale Intensive:** 35 families is within the optimal group size (30-40) that
  Mollison identifies as capable of covering most human functions
- **Diversity:** The system works because members bring different skills and availability
- **Edge Effect:** The model sits at the intersection of formal (legally binding) and
  informal (community trust) economics

## Related Models

- **Round-Robin Work Groups:** 3-5 person episodic groups that cycle through members'
  projects until all needs are met
- **Community Barter Clubs:** Broader exchange systems covering goods and services beyond
  construction labour
- **LET System:** Local Employment Trading System using green dollars instead of hour credits
- **[[mondragon-cooperatives]]:** Larger-scale worker cooperative model from Basque Spain

## Implementation Notes

- Works best in communities where members live within reasonable travel distance
- Requires at least one committed coordinator (Labour Organiser)
- The 60-hour credit threshold is key to preventing exploitation
- Legal forms provide recourse if commitments are not honored
- Best combined with a newsletter or communication system for coordination
