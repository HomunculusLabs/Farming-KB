# Psilocybe atlantis

**Psilocybe atlantis** is a rare [[emcdda-psilocybin-non-addictive-profile-tolerance-dynamics]] in the family Hymenogastraceae. It is one of several *Psilocybe* species known to produce **sclerotia** — dense, hardened masses of mycelium that store nutrients and allow survival during unfavorable conditions. In commercial contexts, sclerotia of *P. atlantis* and closely related species are sold as "magic truffles" or "philosopher's stones," particularly in the Netherlands where they remain legally available (as of 2026) despite restrictions on the mushrooms themselves.

## Taxonomy and Nomenclature

- **Family:** Hymenogastraceae
- **Genus:** Psilocybe
- **Species:** P. atlantis

The species was described by Gastón Guzmán in 2003 from specimens collected in Fulton County, Georgia, USA. The epithet *atlantis* references the specimen's New World origin (a metaphorical Atlantis). Some mycologists have suggested *P. atlantis* may be conspecific with *P. galindoi* and *P. mexicana*; the taxonomy of sclerotia-producing *Psilocybe* species remains actively debated.

### The P. mexicana Complex
The sclerotia-producing psilocybes form a closely related group:

- ***[[dark-incubation-sclerotia-production-psilocybe-mexicana-tampanensis]]*** — the type species, described by Heim from Mexico
- ***Psilocybe galindoi*** — described from Mexico
- ***[[sclerotia-maturation-timeline-psilocybe-tampanensis-mexicana]]*** — described from Florida, USA
- ***Psilocybe atlantis*** — described from Georgia, USA

Molecular phylogenetic studies suggest some of these taxa may represent a single polymorphic species complex with incomplete lineage sorting. The commercial "magic truffle" trade often conflates them under various marketing names.

## Description

### Sclerotia (Truffles)
The sclerotia are the primary form encountered commercially:

- **Size:** 0.5–3 cm in diameter, occasionally larger
- **Shape:** Irregular, roughly spherical to lobed, resembling small potatoes or nuts
- **Color:** Dark brown to golden-brown externally; lighter tan to cream internally
- **Texture:** Hard and dense when dried; rubbery-firm when fresh
- **Water content:** Approximately 30–70% by weight fresh
- **Storage:** When dried, become hard and can be stored for extended periods

### Mushroom (Carpophore)
The actual mushroom is less commonly encountered than the sclerotia:

- **Cap (Pileus):** 1–4 cm diameter, convex to plane, sometimes with a slight umbo. Hygrophanous — ochraceous-brown when moist, fading to pale yellowish-tan when dry. Viscid from a gelatinous pellicle when wet. Partial veil remnants may persist on the cap margin.
- **Gills (Lamellae):** Adnate to adnexed, moderately crowded, grayish to purplish-brown with age, whitish edges.
- **Stipe:** 3–7 cm long, 2–4 mm thick, pale yellowish to brownish. Persistent annulus (ring) from the partial veil. Fibrous and tough.
- **Flesh:** Thin, pale, bruising distinctly **blue to blue-green** when handled or damaged. This [[field-guide-psilocybin-bluing-reaction-identification]] is due to oxidation of psilocin.
- **Spore print:** Dark purplish-brown.
- **Spores:** 11–15 × 7–9 µm, elliptical to subrhomboidal, thick-walled, with a broad germ pore. Slightly roughened to nearly smooth.

## Distribution and Habitat

Originally described from the southeastern United States (Georgia). Natural distribution includes the southeastern US and possibly central Mexico, though confusion with related species complicates mapping. The species grows in rich pastures, disturbed grassy areas, and subtropical to warm-temperate environments, often in association with enriched soils.

## Chemistry and Potency

Contains the classic psilocybin mushroom alkaloids:

- **Psilocybin** — primary prodrug, converted to psilocin in the body
- **Psilocin** — the active psychoactive compound
- **Baeocystin** — a minor alkaloid contributing to the overall effect profile
- **Norbaeocystin** — present in trace amounts

### Potency
Sclerotia contain approximately 0.3–0.7% psilocybin + psilocin by dry weight. Generally considered among the more potent sclerotia-forming species, though potency varies with genetics, growing conditions, and handling. In the Dutch market, *P. atlantis* is typically regarded as more potent than *P. mexicana* but less potent than *P. tampanensis* or *P. hollandia* sclerotia.

## Cultivation

Cultivated primarily for sclerotia, which form over weeks to months in the substrate:

- **Substrate:** Rye grain, grass seed (*Lolium* spp.), or millet; sterilized before inoculation
- **Temperature:** Optimal colonization at 24–27°C (75–80°F)
- **Incubation:** Sclerotia begin forming after 4–8 weeks of colonization
- **Total production:** 2–4 months from inoculation to harvest
- **Humidity:** Moderate (60–75%) during [[growing-gourmet-sclerotia-formation-biology-mushrooms]]
