# Concrete Sprayer

## Description

A concrete sprayer is a type of hand pump sprayer designed for applying concrete sealers and coatings, repurposed as an ideal tool for applying actively aerated compost tea. Concrete sprayers look exactly like home garden pump sprayers but feature fewer bends in the plumbing, larger orifices, and nozzles that can handle larger particles — making them superior to standard garden sprayers for compost tea application.

## Classification

- **Category:** Application equipment / spray tool
- **Type:** Hand pump pressure sprayer
- **Primary use (original):** Concrete sealer application
- **Adapted use:** Compost tea application to soil and foliage

## Key Facts

- Resembles standard home garden pump sprayers in appearance but with critical design differences.
- Fewer bends in the internal plumbing reduce clogging by compost particles.
- Larger orifices and nozzles allow compost particles, fungal hyphae, and nematodes to pass through without damage.
- Available at builders' supply stores, concrete contractors, concrete supply stores, and sand and gravel companies.
- Avoids the problem of standard garden sprayers clogging with compost particulate matter.
- Must still be operated at pressures below 70 PSI to avoid killing microbes through shear forces.
- An affordable alternative to commercial compost tea spraying equipment for small to medium applications.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

The method of applying compost tea is as important as the tea itself. Standard garden sprayers have narrow orifices and multiple bends that clog with compost particles and can damage delicate fungal hyphae and nematodes through shear forces. Concrete sprayers solve this problem while remaining affordable and widely available.

For cultivators applying compost tea as a foliar spray, the concrete sprayer allows the full diversity of the tea — bacteria, fungi, protozoa, and nematodes — to reach the leaf surface intact. This is critical because foliar application requires 70% leaf surface coverage for effective pathogen competition, and the larger organisms (particularly fungal hyphae) play a key role in establishing protective colonies on leaf surfaces.

The sprayer should be used at low pressure with the spray head turned upward so droplets "parachute" down gently, avoiding forceful impact that can kill the microbes being applied.
