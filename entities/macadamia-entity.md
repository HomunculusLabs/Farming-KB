# [[macadamia]]

**Type:** Species (Plant)
**Category:** Nut-producing evergreen tree
**Also known as:** Macadamia spp, Queensland nut, bush nut,
bauple nut, maroochi nut

## Overview
Macadamia is a small to medium, long-lived evergreen tree and the
first Australian native developed into a commercial tree crop. It
typically begins nut production between years 4 and 6, with individual
trees producing up to 40 kg of nuts by age 15. The nuts are
exceptionally hard-shelled, rich in healthy fats (71.6% fat content),
and remain fresh for years. Six species are known, with two having
commercial significance.

## Key Details

### Botanical Profile
- **Family:** Proteaceae
- **Height:** 15-20 m depending on species
- **Origin:** Australia (eastern coast, subtropical rainforests)
- **Habit:** Long-lived evergreen tree
- **Frost tolerance:** Hardy to about -4 degrees C
- **Spacing:** Recommended 9 m x 9 m

### Commercial Species
- **M. integrifolia** (smooth-shelled macadamia): To 15 m; primary
  commercial species; grows best in coastal areas; pale green new
  leaves; creamy white flowers; round smooth-shelled nuts
- **M. tetraphylla** (rough-shelled macadamia): To 20 m; rough
  textured shell; red new leaves; pinkish hairy flowers; more adapted
  to inland conditions; more resistant to Phytophthora trunk canker;
  often used as rootstock

### Growth Requirements
- Soil: Deep, fertile, well-drained; avoid acid soils (treat with
  lime or dolomite)
- Water: Fairly drought tolerant; irrigation improves reliability;
  critical watering in spring and early summer
- Wind: Prone to losing limbs; sheltered sites recommended
- Pests: Phytophthora cinnamomii causes trunk canker on damaged roots
- Interplanting with tagasaste, small acacias, and cassias reduces
  wind damage and root fungus risk

### Nutritional Composition (per 100 g)
- Energy: 691 calories
- Fat: 71.6 g
- Protein: 7.8 g
- Carbohydrate: 15.9 g
- Calcium: 48 mg
- Potassium: 264 mg
- Iron: 2.0 mg

### Propagation
- Seed: Soak overnight in warm water; discard floaters; keep in barely
  damp warm peat moss until sprouting
- Grafting: Many grafted cultivars available; recommended for
  consistent production
- Cross-pollination: Planting diverse cultivars improves yield
  reliability across varying weather conditions

## Relevance
Macadamia is a high-value [[permaculture]] crop for subtropical and
tropical food forests. Its long productive lifespan, extremely hard
storage nuts, and high fat content make it both a commercial crop and
a valuable food security tree. The ability to interplant with nitrogen-
fixing nurse species during the establishment phase demonstrates good
[[permaculture-succession]] design.
