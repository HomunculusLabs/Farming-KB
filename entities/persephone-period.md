## Description

The Persephone period is a concept used in winter harvest planning to describe the time of year when daylength drops below the minimum threshold (approximately 10 hours) required for active plant growth. Named after the Greek goddess who descended to the underworld for winter, it marks the period when crops in unheated greenhouses essentially stop growing and enter a holding pattern until days lengthen again.

## Classification

- **Type**: Agricultural concept / Planning calendar marker
- **Category**: Photoperiod management, winter production scheduling
- **Named for**: Persephone (Greek mythology)

## Key Facts

- Defined as the period when daylight hours fall below approximately 10 hours per day
- During this period, plant growth in unheated structures slows to near-zero regardless of temperature
- Crops planted before the Persephone period must reach harvestable size before it begins
- Growth resumes after the Persephone period ends as daylength increases above the threshold
- Different regions have different Persephone period dates based on latitude
- Serves as a critical planning deadline for winter harvest schedules

## Practical Application

- All winter crops must be sown early enough to reach harvestable size before the Persephone period begins
- During the Persephone period, crops are essentially in storage — alive but not growing
- After the Persephone period ends, crops resume growth and new sowings can begin again
- The end of the Persephone period is a trigger for several management changes:
  - Begin monitoring for overheating under row covers
  - Resume irrigation (low evaporation during the period means minimal water needs)
  - Plan for removing inner covers on sunny days by March 15 at the latest

## Regional Variation

The Persephone period dates vary significantly by latitude. In Zone 5 Maine (approximately 44°N), the period is longer than in Zone 7 Virginia or Zone 8 Pacific Northwest. Growers must calculate their local Persephone dates using daylength tables to create accurate planting calendars.

## Relevance to Cultivation

The Persephone period is the single most important scheduling concept for winter greenhouse production. Understanding it allows growers to work with nature's rhythm rather than against it. Instead of trying to force growth during the darkest weeks (which would require supplemental lighting and heat), the [[winter-harvest-system]] is designed around having crops reach harvestable size before growth stops, then maintaining them in a fresh state through the dark period. This approach requires zero energy input during the coldest, darkest months and is the key to the economic viability of unheated winter production. The concept applies everywhere — only the calendar dates change with latitude.
