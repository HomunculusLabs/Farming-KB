## Description

**Urocerus gigas**, the giant wood wasp or giant horntail, is a large species of sawfly in the family Siricidae. The female carries symbiotic fungi in specialized mycangia and injects them into wood during oviposition, where the fungi decompose wood tissue to provide nutrition for developing larvae.









## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Animalia
- **Phylum**: Arthropoda
- **Class**: Insecta
- **Order**: Hymenoptera
- **Family**: Siricidae
- **Genus**: Urocerus
- **Species**: U. gigas









## Key Facts

- Transmits the fungal pathogen [[amylostereum-chailletii]] to larch trees
- Redfern (1989) described its role alongside the bark beetle [[ips-cembrae]] in damaging larch
- Female wasps carry fungal symbionts in mycangia and inject them during egg-laying
- Larvae feed on fungus-degraded wood tissue
- Damage severity is density-dependent on insect population levels









## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

U. gigas represents the wood wasp-[[fungal-symbiosis]] strategy, where fungi pre-digest wood for insect nutrition. This is analogous to leaf-cutter ant fungus gardens but occurs within wood substrate. For mycologists, the siricid-Amylostereum symbiosis demonstrates how fungi can be employed as external digestive systems, a principle that underpins [[mushroom-cultivation]] on woody substrates.









## Ecological Role

The giant wood wasp is a native species in Eurasian conifer forests attacking weakened, dying, or recently felled trees. Its fungal symbiont initiates wood decomposition, making the wasp an early successional species in forest nutrient cycling.
