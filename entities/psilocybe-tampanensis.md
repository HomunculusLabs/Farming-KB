# Psilocybe tampanensis

## Overview

**Psilocybe tampanensis** is a psilocybin-producing mushroom notable as one of
the few **Psilocybe** species that produces prominent **sclerotia** — dense,
hardened mycelial masses serving as nutrient reserves. Commonly called "magic
truffles," these sclerotia contain the same psychoactive alkaloids as the
fruiting bodies.

The species was first collected near Tampa, Florida in 1977 by Steven Pollock
and described by Guzmán and Pollock in 1978. It is extremely rare in the wild,
having been documented from its type locality only once. Nearly all material in
circulation today derives from that single 1977 collection. It remains popular
in the Netherlands, where fresh sclerotia are legally sold through smart shops.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Hymenogastraceae
- **Genus:** Psilocybe
- **Species:** P. tampanensis Guzmán & S.H. Pollock (1978)

No significant synonyms exist. The commercial name "Philosopher's Stone" is not
a taxonomic designation. The species belongs to section Mexicanae alongside
P. mexicana and P. galindoi.

## Morphology

### Fruiting Body

- **Cap:** 1–2.4 cm diameter, convex to plane, hygrophanous, reddish-brown
  when moist fading to yellowish-brown when dry
- **Gills:** Adnexed to adnate, close, grayish becoming dark purple-brown
- **Stipe:** 4–6 cm × 1–2 mm, equal, hollow, silky-fibrillose, bruising blue
- **Partial veil:** Cortinate, leaving a sparse membranous annular zone
- **Spore print:** Dark purple-brown
- **Spores:** Ellipsoid, 9–12 × 6–8 μm, thick-walled with distinct germ pore

### Sclerotia

- **Form:** Irregular to roughly spherical, resembling small stones or tubers
- **Size:** 0.5–3 cm diameter in cultivation
- **Surface:** Rough, knobby, dark brown to reddish-brown
- **Interior:** Firm, rubbery, cream to light tan
- **Texture:** Dense when fresh, hardening when dried

Sclerotia are the primary commercial product, forming reliably in vitro without
requiring standard fruiting conditions.

## Distribution and Habitat

Originally collected from a sandy lawn near Tampa, Florida in a subtropical
climate zone. The single confirmed wild collection came from disturbed, grassy
soil. Despite extensive searching, the species has never been re-collected
from the wild. Its natural distribution and habitat preferences remain
essentially unknown, though the type locality suggests warm, sandy soils
associated with grasses.

## Cultivation

Cultivated primarily for sclerotia rather than fruiting bodies:

- **Substrate:** Rye grass seed (Elymus spp.) preferred; brown rice flour and
  rye grain also work but produce less sclerotia
- **Colonization:** 2–4 weeks at 24–26°C
- **Sclerotia formation:** Begins 4–12 weeks after colonization at 20–22°C;
  total cultivation time typically 3–4 months
- **Yield:** Approximately 10–30% of substrate dry weight as sclerotia
- **Fruiting:** Requires casing layer and 90–95% humidity; considered
  unreliable compared to other Psilocybe species

The long production timeline makes it less commercially attractive than faster
alternatives like P. mexicana or P. galindoi.

## Bioactive Compounds

- **Psilocybin:** 0.14–0.42% dry weight (sclerotia), 0.11–0.34% (fruiting)
- **Psilocin:** 0.03–0.11% dry weight (sclerotia), 0.05–0.09% (fruiting)
- **Baeocystin:** 0.01–0.04% dry weight (trace amounts)
- **Norbaeocystin:** Trace quantities in some analyses

Total tryptamine content is moderate by Psilocybe standards, comparable to
mid-potency P. cubensis strains. Fresh sclerotia are 70–80% water,
concentrating alkaloids 4–5× upon drying.

## Pharmacology and Effects

Mediated primarily by 5-HT2A receptor agonism:

- **Onset:** 20–60 minutes
- **Peak:** 2–3 hours
- **Duration:** 4–6 hours
- **Dose (fresh):** 5–15 grams
- **Dose (dried):** 3–7 grams

Effects are similar to other psilocybin mushrooms: visual distortion, altered
time perception, emotional sensitivity, cognitive changes. Users commonly
report a "smoother" or "more grounded" experience compared to cubensis,
though controlled comparisons are lacking.

## Legal Status

In the Netherlands, fresh sclerotia were exempted from the 2008 dried
mushroom ban and remain legally sold through licensed smart shops. Dried
material is controlled. In most other countries, both sclerotia and fruiting
bodies are scheduled as controlled substances.
