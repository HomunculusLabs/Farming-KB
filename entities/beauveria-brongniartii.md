---# Taxonomy and Classification

Division: Ascomycota → Subdivision: Pezizomycotina → Class: Sordariomycetes → Order:
Hypocreales → Family: Cordycipitaceae → Genus: *Beauveria* → Species: *B. brongniartii*.

The species epithet honors the French entomologist Henri Brongniart. Molecular phylogenetic
studies using ITS, TEF, and Bloc gene regions confirm *B. brongniartii* as a distinct
lineage within the *B. bassiana* species complex, separate from *B. bassiana* sensu stricto
and *B. malawiensis*.

Synonyms include *Isaria brongniartii* (Sacc.) and *Sporotrichum brongniartii* Sacc.,
reflecting its historical placement in multiple genera before modern taxonomic revision.

## Morphology

Colonies on potato dextrose agar (PDA) grow moderately fast, reaching 25-35 mm diameter
in 14 days at 25°C. Colony texture is cottony to powdery, white to pale cream in color,
with age sometimes developing a pale yellow or pinkish tint on reverse.

Conidiophores are irregularly branched, bearing whorls of conidiogenous cells. Conidia are
globose to subglobose, measuring 2.0-3.0 × 2.0-2.8 μm, produced singly on sympodial
conidiogenous cells with a distinctive zigzag rachis. Conidia are hyaline and
single-celled, forming powdery white masses on infected cadavers.

Blastospores may form in [[enzymatic-degradation-in-mycoremediation]] of the epicuticle. The fungus proliferates as blastospores in the
hemocoel, producing toxins including beauvericin and bassianolide. Death typically occurs
within 3-7 days post-infection, followed by emergence of conidiophores through the
cadaver integument.

Its host range is narrower than *B. bassiana*, which is advantageous for biocontrol
applications targeting specific pests without harming [[comparison-beneficial-insects-guide-vs-integrated-pest-management]].

## Distribution and Ecology

*B. brongniartii* is found worldwide in temperate and subtropical soils, particularly in
agricultural and grassland habitats with high populations of scarab larvae. It persists
in soil as conidia or as dormant hyphal bodies within infected cadavers.
