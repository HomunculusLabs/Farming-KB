## Oryzopsis (Rice-Grass)

## Description

Oryzopsis, commonly known as rice-grass or Indian ricegrass, is a genus of perennial bunch grasses native to arid and semi-arid regions of North America. The most significant species, *Oryzopsis hymenoides* (now reclassified as *Achnatherum hymenoides*), produces edible seeds that were a major staple food for Indigenous peoples of the Great Basin and Southwestern deserts. Mollison cites rice-grass seed alongside [[permaculture]] principle of designing food systems that combine wild harvesting with intensive gardening. The grass also serves critical ecological functions in soil stabilization and erosion control, making it valuable for both food production and land restoration in arid environments. Its modern use in reclamation work demonstrates how Indigenous food plants can serve multiple functions in designed systems.

## Harvesting and Processing

Traditional methods for harvesting rice-grass seed:

- Harvest by beating mature seed heads into tightly woven baskets
- Timing is critical: seeds shatter readily when fully ripe, requiring careful observation
- Some groups harvested before full maturity and allowed seed to after-ripen
- Processing involves winnowing to remove chaff, followed by parching
- Parching over hot coals or in a hot pan removes remaining moisture and makes grinding easier
- Seeds ground on metates (stone grinding slabs) into fine flour
- Flour mixed with water to form cakes, then baked on hot stones or dried in the sun

## Potential as a Cultivated Crop

Modern interest in Oryzopsis/Achnatherum as a dryland cereal:

- Highly drought-tolerant perennial that could reduce irrigation needs for grain production
- Deep root system builds soil carbon and prevents erosion
- No tillage required; perennial growth eliminates annual planting costs
- Seed yield under cultivation remains a research challenge
- Breeding and selection could improve seed size, retention, and yield
- Potential as a dual-purpose grain and soil conservation crop for semi-arid rangelands
