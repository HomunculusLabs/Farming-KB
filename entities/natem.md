## Natem

## Description

Natem (also spelled Natem, natemdé) is the Jivaro name for the ayahuasca brew used by Jivaro shamans of the Upper Amazon region of Ecuador. It serves as the primary hallucinogenic sacrament and central pharmacological tool in Jivaro shamanism, described as "the chemical door to the otherwise invisible world of the Jivaro shaman."

## Classification

- **Type:** Hallucinogenic plant brew ([[ayahuasca-preparation]])
- **Botanical source:** [[banisteriopsis-caapi]] vine combined with admixture plants
- **Chemical class:** [[beta-carboline]] and tryptamine alkaloids (harmine, harmaline, N,N-dimethyltryptamine)
- **Cultural context:** Jivaro (Shuar) people, Upper Amazon, Ecuador

## Preparation

Natem is prepared by boiling fragments of the ayahuasca vine (Banisteriopsis caapi) with additional plant materials. The brew is boiled down, cooled, and then consumed. The preparation process is overseen by the shaman, who checks the appearance by blowing away the froth on the bubbling brew.

## Role in Jivaro Shamanism

Natem occupies a central role in virtually every aspect of Jivaro shamanic practice:

- **Supernatural perception:** Shamans drink natem to perceive the "true" supernatural reality that is invisible in ordinary consciousness
- **Tsentsak revelation:** Under natem"s influence, magical darts (tsentsak) reveal their true nature as spirit helpers — giant butterflies, jaguars, monkeys, and other zoomorphic forms with distinct names
- **Medical diagnosis:** Curing shamans use natem to "see into the body of the patient as though it were glass," identifying intruding objects with clarity sufficient to determine whether the sickness can be cured
- **Bewitching:** Bewitching shamans drink natem before secretly approaching victims to launch magical darts
- **Spiritual combat:** Shamans use natem to perceive and battle enemy spirit helpers (pasuk, wakani birds) sent by bewitchers

## Initiation Practice

During shamanic initiation, a novice drinks natem every evening for ten days after receiving tsentsak from a donor shaman. The novice must remain inactive and abstain from sexual intercourse for at least three months, ideally a full year, to develop sufficient power as a curer or bewitcher.

## Ongoing Use

Practicing shamans must drink natem regularly to:

- Keep their tsentsak fed and active
- Maintain contact with the supernatural reality
- Keep their spirit helpers alert for defense against enemy magical darts
- Periodically refresh their visionary capacity

Natem is often combined with green tobacco juice and piripiri extract in shamanic practice. The three substances work synergistically: tobacco juice maintains a narcotized state that keeps tsentsak ready, piripiri provides additional power, and natem opens the door to full supernatural perception.

## Pharmacological Significance

The brew"s psychoactivity results from the interaction between beta-carboline MAO inhibitors (harmine, harmaline) from the Banisteriopsis vine and N,N-dimethyltryptamine (DMT) from admixture plants such as [[psychotria-viridis]]. The beta-carbolines render DMT orally active by inhibiting its breakdown by monoamine oxidase enzymes in the gut.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Natem represents one of the most sophisticated plant-based psychedelic preparations in indigenous American pharmacology. The Jivaro understanding of the synergistic combination of plants — developed without modern chemistry — demonstrates advanced ethnobotanical knowledge. The Banisteriopsis caapi vine cultivation and the identification of appropriate admixture plants reflect centuries of empirical botanical research by Amazonian peoples.

## Cultural Context

The Jivaro distinguish between the ordinary material world perceived without natem and the "true" supernatural reality revealed through the brew. In their cosmology, the supernatural aspects of objects and beings revealed by natem are considered more real than their everyday appearances. This dual-reality framework underlies the entire Jivaro shamanic system of bewitching, curing, and spiritual combat.
