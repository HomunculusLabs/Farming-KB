# White Mulberry (Morus alba)

## Description

The white mulberry is a fast-growing deciduous tree prized in permaculture as one of the highest-value poultry forage crops. Bill Mollison rates it as "as good as a double crop of grain" due to its 17% protein content in fruit and its extended bearing season. It forms a cornerstone of zone two forage system design for integrated poultry management.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Family**: Moraceae
- **Genus**: Morus
- **Species**: M. alba
- **Common Names**: White mulberry, silkworm mulberry

## Key Facts

- **Protein content**: Fruit is 17% protein, making it equivalent to a double grain crop in feed value.
- **Extended season**: Chickens continue foraging on dropped fruit and seed long after the main mulberry harvest ends.
- **Rapid establishment**: Large plants can be grown from four-foot cuttings of 1.5-inch diameter, reaching full production within two years.
- **Multiple varieties**: Planting two or three varieties extends the harvest season significantly.
- **American cultivars**: Mollison notes the United States has "one of the very best black mulberries in the world," with black mulberries (M. nigra) being a closely related high-value species.

## Role in Permaculture Systems

Mollison positions white mulberry as the primary forage tree in zone two chicken-orchard systems:

- **Primary poultry feed**: Replaces grain-based feed with a perennial, self-maintaining food source.
- **Seasonal bridge**: Provides food during the bearing period and beyond, as chickens continue finding dropped fruit.
- **Companion planting**: Works alongside Siberian pea tree (for lean periods), comfrey (for greens), and sunflower (for additional seed).
- **Orchard integration**: Planted within chicken forage areas alongside hazelnuts, Chinese chestnuts, and fruit trees.

## Cultivation Notes

- Can be propagated from large cuttings (up to 4 feet long, 1.5 inch diameter).
- First autumn rooting produces several usable trees.
- Completely fills an area and reaches full mulberry production by the second year.
- Minimal maintenance once established.

## Comparison with Grain Feed

Mollison's argument: 60% of the world's grains are fed to livestock. A half-acre integrated forage system including white mulberry can produce 5,000+ pounds of chicken forage annually — higher in protein than wheat and far more varied nutritionally — without threshing, bagging, or purchasing grain.
