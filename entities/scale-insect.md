# Scale Insect

## Description

Scale insects are small, sap-sucking insects belonging to the superfamily Coccoidea within the order Hemiptera. They are significant agricultural and horticultural pests that attach to plant stems, leaves, and fruits, feeding on plant sap and often producing honeydew as a byproduct. In compost tea research, scale insects are notable because they can be managed through biological approaches using [[actively-aerated-compost-tea-aact-brewing]] tea (AACT).

## Classification

- **Order**: Hemiptera
- **Suborder**: Sternorrhyncha
- **Superfamily**: Coccoidea
- **Common Types**: Armored scale (Diaspididae), Soft scale (Coccidae), Mealybugs (Pseudococcidae)

## Key Facts

- Scale insects produce honeydew, a sugary excretion that promotes the growth of sooty mold on plant surfaces
- They have protective waxy or armored coverings that make chemical control difficult
- Scale infestations weaken plants by draining sap and reducing photosynthetic capacity
- [[fukuoka-textdoc-arrowhead-scale-ladybug-biological-control]] using compost tea has shown success against scale on rhododendron and apple
- Good fungal biomass in compost tea is essential for scale management success
- The mechanism involves both direct biological competition and repulsion of the insects

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

### Compost Tea Applications
- AACT with excellent fungal biomass has been demonstrated to cause scale on rhododendron and apple to disappear
- Fungal-dominant compost tea is preferred for scale management
- Applications should begin in fall before leaf drop and continue through [[query-how-to-protect-plants-from-frost-and-extend-the-growing-season]]
- The beneficial fungi in the tea create an environment that is repugnant to scale insects

### Honeydew and Sooty Mold Connection
- Scale insects produce honeydew that serves as food for sooty mold fungi
- In biologically active systems, beneficial organisms consume the honeydew before sooty mold can establish
- This three-way relationship (scale - honeydew - sooty mold) can be disrupted by restoring beneficial biology
- When beneficial organisms are present, honeydew is consumed and sooty mold does not develop

### Biological Control Agents
- [[beauveria-bassiana]] and other entomopathogenic fungi can parasitize scale insects
- These fungi can be incorporated into [[compost-tea-for-specific-crops]] enhanced pest management
- Bruce Elliott has worked on fungal species specific to different insect pests
- Local, indigenous parasites and diseases should be prioritized for biocontrol

### Integrated Management
- Scale management requires consistent application — a single spray is insufficient
- Conventional chemical approaches kill beneficial organisms, making the problem worse over time
- Restoring the full soil food web provides long-term, self-sustaining pest management
- [[beneficial-insects-and-biological-pest-control]] serve as "taxi-cabs" to transport compost tea organisms to new growth
