## Overview

**DET** (N,N-diethyltryptamine) is a synthetic psychedelic tryptamine and close structural analog of DMT (N,N-dimethyltryptamine).

It was first synthesized in the 1950s and later documented in [[alexander-shulgin]]'s TiHKAL (entry #39), where it received a ++ rating for psychedelic activity.

The compound acts primarily as a 5-HT2A receptor agonist, producing visual and cognitive effects similar to DMT but with a longer duration and different character.

DET occurs rarely in nature, having been detected in trace amounts in certain plant species and as a possible minor alkaloid in some *[[acacia]]* species.

## Chemical Properties

- **IUPAC Name:** N,N-diethyl-1H-indole-3-ethanamine
- **Molecular Formula:** C14H20N2
- **Molecular Weight:** 216.32 g/mol
- **CAS Number:** 2261-65-0
- **SMILES:** CCN(CC)CCc1c[nH]c2ccccc12
- **Appearance:** White crystalline powder or freebase oil

The freebase form is an oily liquid at room temperature, while the hydrochloride salt is a white crystalline solid.

The compound is soluble in organic solvents (ethyl acetate, chloroform, DMSO) and slightly soluble in water.

DET differs from DMT by the replacement of two methyl groups with two ethyl groups on the terminal nitrogen, increasing lipophilicity.

## Pharmacology

DET binds to the 5-HT2A receptor with moderate affinity, acting as a full or partial agonist depending on the assay system.

The ethyl substitution relative to DMT results in slightly reduced receptor potency but increased metabolic stability and longer duration of action.

Additional serotonergic activity includes binding to 5-HT1A, 5-HT2C, and 5-HT7 receptor subtypes, though with lower affinity than at 5-HT2A.

The compound shows minimal affinity for dopamine, norepinephrine, and histamine receptors at typical psychedelic doses.

Metabolism occurs primarily via monoamine oxidase (MAO-A) oxidative deamination, similar to other simple tryptamines.

The increased steric bulk of the ethyl groups confers partial resistance to MAO degradation compared to DMT, contributing to the longer duration.

## Effects

The subjective effects of DET are broadly similar to those of DMT but are generally described as smoother, less intense, and more drawn out.

Visual effects include geometric patterns, color intensification, and altered perception of surfaces and textures, typically developing over 15–30 minutes.

Cognitive effects range from heightened introspection and philosophical thinking to mild [[psilocybin-ego-dissolution-mechanisms]] at higher doses.

Users frequently report a greater sense of mental clarity and groundedness compared to DMT, which is sometimes described as more chaotic.

The experience is often characterized as a "middle ground" between DMT and longer-acting psychedelics like LSD or psilocybin.

Somatic effects include mild nausea, pupil dilation, increased heart rate, and tactile sensitivity, particularly during onset.

## Dosage and Duration

- **Threshold dose:** 20–30 mg (oral or vaporized)
- **Light dose:** 30–50 mg
- **Common dose:** 50–80 mg
- **Strong dose:** 80–120 mg

Oral administration without an MAOI produces mild effects; combination with a β-carboline MAOI (e.g., [[harmine]]) significantly potentiates and extends the experience.

Vaporized or insufflated routes produce more rapid onset (5–15 minutes) with shorter overall duration.

Duration of effects is approximately 2–4 hours when vaporized, extending to 6–8 hours when taken orally with an MAOI.

Tolerance develops rapidly, with near-complete tolerance observed after 2–3 consecutive days of use, requiring 5–7 days for baseline sensitivity to return.
