# Mentha piperita

## Overview

Mentha piperita (peppermint) is a sterile natural hybrid of
watermint (*M. aquatica*) and spearmint (*M. spicata*), among
the most widely cultivated aromatic herbs. Valued for high
menthol content, ginseng calendula cultivation and medicinal uses properties, and culinary
versatility. The global [[permaculture-designers-manual-essential-oil-plants]] industry exceeds several
thousand tonnes annually, with major production in the US,
India, and Eastern Europe. A sterile allotetraploid (2n = 72),
it produces no viable seeds and spreads only vegetatively.

## Taxonomy and Naming

- **Kingdom:** Plantae — **Family:** Lamiaceae
- **Genus:** Mentha — **Species:** *M. piperita* L.
- **Parentage:** *M. aquatica* × *M. spicata*
- **Chromosome count:** 2n = 72; parents each 2n = 48
First described by Linnaeus in 1753. Epithet *piperita*
refers to pepper-like taste from high menthol content.

## Botanical Description

**Morphology:** Perennial herb, 30–90 cm tall. Square stems,
often purplish at nodes. Strongly aromatic when crushed.
**Leaves:** Opposite, ovate-lanceolate, 4–9 cm, serrate,
dark green with reddish veins, pubescent. **Flowers:** Small,
pale purple, in verticillasters; corolla 3–5 mm; terminal
spikes 3–7 cm. Blooms July–September. Sterile.
