It is a brown-rot decomposer found almost exclusively on dead or dying birch trees (*Betula* spp.) across the temperate Northern Hemisphere.

The species has a long history of ethnobotanical use, valued for both its [[cancer-medicinal-mushrooms-chaga-and-birch-polypore]] is one of the most recognizable bracket fungi in boreal and temperate forests due to its distinctive hoof-shaped [[fomitopsis-pinicola]]* and *Fomitopsis rosea*.

The species epithet *betulina* refers to its exclusive association with birch trees.

### Fruiting Body

The fruiting body is a sessile or laterally stipitate bracket, 5–25 cm across and 2–6 cm thick at the base.

The upper surface is smooth to slightly wrinkled, pale grey-brown when young, darkening to dark brown or nearly black with age.

Concentric zone lines are often visible on the cap surface, and the margin is rounded and inrolled when young.

### Pore Surface

The pore surface is white to cream-colored, bruising brownish on handling.

Pores are circular to angular, 3–5 per mm, with tubes 1–4 cm deep.

Spores are cylindrical to allantoid, 5–6 × 1.5–2 µm, white in deposit, inamyloid.

### Flesh and Odor

The context is tough, corky, and pale cream, composed of a distinct dual structure.

The upper layer is darker and harder while the lower layer near the pores is softer and whiter.

The flesh has a faintly fungoid odor and a mildly acidic, astringent taste when fresh.

## Distribution and Habitat

Fomitopsis betulina has a circumboreal distribution across Europe, Asia, and [[fungal-role-in-carbon-cycling]] in boreal [[antifungal-secondary-metabolites-coprophilous-fungi]] with documented pharmacological activities.

### Polysaccharides

Beta-glucans and other polysaccharides isolated from the fruiting body demonstrate immunomodulatory activity.

In vitro studies show stimulation of macrophage activity and enhancement of natural killer cell cytotoxicity.

These polysaccharides are primarily water-soluble and are concentrated in the inner context tissue.
