## Overview

5-MeO-DPT (5-methoxy-N,N-dipropyltryptamine) is a synthetic psychedelic tryptamine compound closely related to DMT and 5-MeO-DMT.

It was first synthesized and characterized by Alexander Shulgin and is documented in TiHKAL (Tryptamines I Have Known and Loved) as entry #50.

The compound is known for its [[savory-short-duration-grazing-and-cell-systems]], intense psychedelic effects, and relatively limited history of human use compared to more well-known tryptamines.

## Chemical Properties

- **IUPAC name:** N,N-dipropyl-5-methoxy-1H-indole-3-ethanamine
- **Molecular formula:** C17H26N2O
- **Molecular weight:** 274.40 g/mol
- **CAS number:** 4023-35-8
- **SMILES:** COc1c[nH]c2ccc(cc12)CCN(CCC)CCC
- **Appearance:** Typically encountered as a freebase oil or crystalline hydrochloride salt

The 5-methoxy substitution on the indole ring and the N,N-dipropyl substitution on the terminal amine distinguish 5-MeO-DPT from both DMT (dimethyl) and 5-MeO-DMT.

The propyl groups increase lipophilicity compared to the methyl groups of DMT, potentially affecting blood-brain barrier penetration and [[tihkal-receptor-binding-serotonin]] affinity.

## Pharmacology

5-MeO-DPT acts primarily as a serotonergic psychedelic, with agonist or partial agonist activity at the 5-HT2A receptor, the primary receptor responsible for classical psychedelic effects.

The compound also shows affinity for other serotonin receptor subtypes, including 5-HT1A and 5-HT2C, which may contribute to its unique subjective profile.

The dipropyl substitution pattern may result in different receptor selectivity and functional efficacy compared to the dimethyl analog 5-MeO-DMT.

Limited pharmacological data are available compared to more widely studied tryptamines, and specific Ki values for receptor binding remain incompletely characterized in the published literature.

## Effects

### Subjective Effects

Users report rapid onset of effects within 5–15 minutes following insufflation or oral administration, with a total duration of 2–4 hours.

The experience is generally described as less visually intense than DMT or 5-MeO-DMT but with pronounced cognitive and emotional effects.

Common effects include [[psilocybin-ego-dissolution-mechanisms]], altered perception of time and space, enhanced introspection, and mood elevation.

Some users describe a dreamy, introspective quality with less overwhelming sensory distortion than other 5-methoxy tryptamines.

Open-eye visual effects are typically mild to moderate, with geometric patterns, color enhancement, and wavering of visual fields.

### Dosage

- **Insufflation threshold:** 5–10 mg
- **Insufflation light:** 10–20 mg
- **Insuffilation common:** 20–35 mg
- **Insufflation strong:** 35–50 mg
- **Oral:** 40–80 mg (with MAOI potentiator)

Dosages are approximate and vary significantly based on individual sensitivity, body weight, and prior experience with psychedelics.

## Legal Status

5-MeO-DPT is unscheduled in most jurisdictions, though it may fall under analog act provisions in countries with blanket tryptamine bans.

In the [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]], the compound is not specifically listed on the Controlled [[controlled-substances-act-and-psychedelic-research]] but could potentially be prosecuted under the Federal Analog Act.

Several European countries have placed the compound under controlled substance schedules as part of broader research chemical bans enacted in the 2010s.

## History

5-MeO-DPT was first synthesized by Alexander and Ann Shulgin, with initial human bioassays conducted by the Shulgin research group.

The compound received limited attention in the research chemical community and has remained relatively obscure compared to more popular tryptamines like 5-MeO-DMT or 4-AcO-DMT.

Its availability increased briefly in the mid-2000s through online research chemical vendors but has remained a niche substance within the psychedelic community.
