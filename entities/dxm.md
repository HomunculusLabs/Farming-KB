In 2022, the FDA approved a combination of dextromethorphan and bupropion
(Auvelity) for treatment-resistant major depressive disorder, marking a
significant new clinical application.

## Chemical Properties

- **Molecular formula:** C₁₈H₂₅NO
- **Molecular weight:** 271.40 g/mol
- **IUPAC name:** 3-Methoxy-17-methylmorphinan
- **CAS number:** 125-71-3
- **Structure class:** Morphinan alkaloid (synthetic)
- **Appearance:** White crystalline powder

DXM is the dextrorotatory enantiomer of the racemic compound methorphan.
Its levorotatory counterpart, levomethorphan, is an opioid analgesic with
abuse potential. DXM itself has negligible affinity for classical opioid
receptors at standard doses.

It is metabolized primarily by cytochrome P450 2D6 (CYP2D6) to its active
metabolite dextrorphan (DXO). Individuals with CYP2D6 deficiency (poor
metabolizers) experience higher and more prolonged DXM blood levels.

## Pharmacology

DXM binds to multiple receptor systems, producing a complex pharmacological
profile that varies significantly with dosage.

**Primary targets:**
- NMDA receptor antagonist (Ki ≈ 0.1–1 μM) — primary mechanism for
  dissociative effects
- Sigma-1 receptor agonist (Ki ≈ 0.05 μM) — modulates calcium signaling
  and neuroprotection
