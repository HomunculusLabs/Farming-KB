## Trichomes

## Description

Trichomes are tiny hair-like structures that grow from the epidermis of plant leaves, stems, and sometimes roots. These specialized epidermal outgrowths serve multiple functions including transpiration enhancement, [[dighton-endophyte-alkaloid-grass-herbivore-defense]], and water loss reduction. Trichomes are important diagnostic features in plant identification and play significant roles in plant–environment interactions.

## Classification

- **Tissue Origin:** Epidermal tissue (specialized outgrowths)
- **Structure Type:** Hair-like epidermal appendages
- **Types:** Glandular (secretory) and non-glandular (structural)
- **Location:** Leaf surfaces (upper and lower), stems, and occasionally other plant parts

## Structure

Trichomes are extensions of epidermal cells that project outward from the plant surface. They vary widely across species:

- **Simple trichomes:** Single-celled or multicellular hair-like projections
- **Glandular trichomes:** Possess secretory glands producing chemical compounds
- **Branched trichomes:** Multi-branched structures forming dense coverings (tomentose surfaces)

## Key Facts

- Trichomes help draw evaporated water up and away from the leaf body, speeding transpiration
- Some trichomes possess glands producing anti-grazing substances for herbivore defense
- Other trichomes act as foils, decreasing airflow over the plant surface and slowing water loss
- Found on both upper and lower leaf surfaces in many species
- Part of the epidermal tissue system involved in transpiration regulation
- Cannabis trichomes produce and store cannabinoids and terpenes, making them commercially critical

## Functions

### Transpiration Enhancement
Trichomes near stomata help channel water vapor away from leaf surfaces, maintaining the concentration gradient that drives transpiration and pulls water through the xylem.

### Herbivore Defense
Glandular trichomes secrete anti-grazing compounds including sticky resins, toxic alkaloids, and irritant compounds that deter insects and other herbivores.

### Water Conservation
Dense trichome coverings reduce wind speed at the leaf surface, creating a boundary layer that slows evaporative water loss — important in arid environments.

### UV Protection
In some species, trichomes scatter or absorb ultraviolet radiation, providing protection against DNA damage.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Cannabis cultivation:** Trichome density and development are primary indicators of cannabinoid maturity; harvest timing is based on trichome color (clear to milky to amber)
- **Pest management:** Glandular trichomes provide natural pest resistance; environmental stress can increase trichome production
- **Plant health indicator:** [[cannabis-trichome-development-cannabinoid-biosynthesis-sites]] reflects light intensity, humidity, [[chelation-and-nutrient-availability]], and stress conditions
- **Fungal interaction:** Trichomes can trap fungal spores before they reach the leaf surface, providing a physical barrier against pathogens
- **[[holzer-climate-zone-microclimate-creation]]:** Dense trichome layers create humid microenvironments on leaf surfaces

## Environmental Influences

- **Light:** Higher UV-B exposure typically increases trichome density as a protective response
- **Temperature:** Moderate stress temperatures can trigger increased trichome production
- **Nutrients:** Adequate potassium and silicon support trichome structural integrity
- **Humidity:** Lower humidity often promotes denser trichome development

## Trichome Diversity Across Species

Trichomes vary enormously across the plant kingdom:

- **Cannabis:** Capitate stalked and sessile glandular trichomes produce cannabinoids and terpenes
- **Tomato:** Type VI glandular trichomes produce [[chamovitz-plant-scent-communication-volatile-organic-compounds]] that attract predators of herbivores
- **Mint family (Lamiaceae):** Peltate trichomes produce essential oils (menthol, thymol)
- **Cotton:** Seed trichomes (cotton fibers) are the longest single cells in the plant kingdom
- **[[stinging-nettle]]:** Silica-tipped trichomes inject histamine and formic acid when touched
- **Sundew (Drosera):** Glandular trichomes produce sticky digestive enzymes for carnivory

## Research Applications

Trichomes are model systems for studying:

- Cell differentiation and development
- [[fungal-elicitors-enhanced-secondary-metabolite-production]] biosynthesis
- Plant-insect interactions
- Glandular secretion mechanisms
- Industrial production of valuable plant compounds
