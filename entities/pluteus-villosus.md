# Pluteus villosus

**Pluteus villosus** is a species of pluteus mushroom found on decaying hardwood
in temperate forests of [[hplc-analysis-of-tryptamine-alkaloids]].

## Taxonomy and Naming

Pluteus villosus was first described by the Finnish mycologist Petter Adolf Karsten in
1879. The specific epithet *villosus* means "hairy" or "shaggy" in Latin, referring to
the distinctive fuzzy or villose texture of the cap surface. The genus Pluteus is
placed in the family Pluteaceae and is notable for its free gills (not attached to the
stipe), which is relatively uncommon among agarics and helps distinguish the genus from
look-alikes.

The taxonomy of Pluteus has undergone significant revision in recent years based on
molecular phylogenetic studies. The genus is divided into several sections based on
morphological and genetic characters, with P. villosus falling within the section
Hispidoderma, which includes species with hairy or scaly cap surfaces and rhombic to
broadly ellipsoid spores.

## Morphology

### Cap
The cap measures 3 to 8 cm in diameter, convex when young, expanding to broadly convex
or plane with age, sometimes with a slight depression at the center. The surface is
distinctly fuzzy or hairy, covered with erect to appressed fibrils that give it a
velvety to shaggy appearance. The cap color is variable, ranging from grayish-brown to
dark brown, often with a lighter margin. The surface is dry and non-hygrophanous, and
the hairs are more prominent near the center of the cap.

### Gills
The gills are free from the stipe, close to subdistant, and broad. They are initially
white but become pinkish to salmon-pink as spores mature. This color change from white
to pink is a hallmark of the genus Pluteus and is one of the most useful field
identification features. The gill edges are entire or slightly fringed.

### Stipe
The stipe measures 4 to 9 cm in length and 4 to 10 mm in thickness, cylindrical,
equal or slightly tapering upward, and solid becoming hollow with age. The surface is
smooth to slightly fibrillose, white to pale gray, and often with a downy or pruinose
coating near the apex. The base may be slightly enlarged or bulbous. No annulus is
present.

This species is exclusively saprotrophic on hardwood, growing on decaying logs, stumps,
and fallen branches of broadleaf trees. Common host trees include oak (Quercus spp.),
beech (Fagus spp.), maple (Acer spp.), ash (Fraxinus spp.), and elm (Ulmus spp.). It
fruits from early summer through autumn, typically June through October, often appearing
3 to 7 days after periods of sustained rain. Unlike some Pluteus species that favor
conifer wood, P. villosus is strongly associated with hardwood substrates and is most
frequently encountered in mature deciduous forests with abundant fallen timber. The
species shows a preference for well-decomposed wood in intermediate to advanced stages
of decomposition and is often found on the upper surfaces of logs that receive some
ambient light.
