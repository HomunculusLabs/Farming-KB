## Carex digitata

## Overview

Carex digitata, commonly called fingered sedge, is a small, tufted perennial sedge in the family Cyperaceae. It is widely distributed across temperate Eurasia, from western Europe to the Russian Far East.

The species is a characteristic component of calcareous beech and oak woodlands, where it forms small, discreet tussocks in the herb layer. Its slender, dark green foliage and compact habit make it one of the more easily overlooked woodland sedges.

Carex digitata is distinguished from similar species by its combination of few-flowered spikes and downward-pointing (deflexed) perigynia. These features are visible only with careful examination using a hand lens.

Despite its wide distribution, the species is locally uncommon in many parts of its range. It is considered an indicator of ancient woodland in some European countries, where its presence suggests long-established forest cover.

In Germany and Switzerland, Carex digitata is used as a diagnostic species in phytosociological classifications of the Carpinion and Fagion alliances. Its fidelity to these communities makes it a useful indicator in [[fagus-sylvatica]]) woodlands, oak-hornbeam forests, and mixed deciduous woods with diverse spring-flowering herb layers. It grows in dappled to moderate shade and avoids both deep shade and fully exposed sites.

Soil preferences include well-drained, calcareous loams with moderate humus content. The species is intolerant of waterlogging and acidic conditions, restricting it to base-rich microsites.

In southern and Mediterranean parts of its range, the species is confined to cooler, north-facing slopes and higher elevations. In Scandinavia, it occurs in lowland sites with suitable calcareous geology.

The species provides habitat structure for small invertebrates in the woodland herb layer. Ground-dwelling spiders and beetles use the tussock bases as shelter during dry periods and winter months.

As a wind-pollinated species, Carex digitata does not provide nectar resources for pollinators. However, the foliage is grazed by some woodland Lepidoptera larvae, particularly species adapted to woodland sedge habitats.

The species is considered an ancient woodland indicator in the United Kingdom and parts of northern Europe. Its poor dispersal ability and habitat specificity mean it persists primarily in long-established forest sites.

Genetic studies using microsatellite markers have revealed limited gene flow between isolated Carex digitata populations. This fragmentation pattern is typical of ancient woodland indicators that depend on continuous forest cover for dispersal.

## Similar Species

Carex digitata can be confused with Carex umbrosa (shaded sedge), which shares a similar woodland habitat and [[fukuoka-ideal-rice-plant-morphology-compact-growth-high-yield]] form. Carex umbrosa has broader perigynia (3.5–5 mm) that are ascending rather than deflexed at maturity.

Carex ericetorum (heath sedge) also produces few-flowered spikes but occurs in acidic heathland rather than calcareous woodland. The habitat difference is usually sufficient to separate the two species in the field.

The closely related Carex pediformis from East Asian woodlands is sometimes considered part of a broader Carex digitata complex. These taxa are separated primarily by geographic range and subtle perigynium morphology differences.
