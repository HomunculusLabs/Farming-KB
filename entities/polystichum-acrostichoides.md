## Overview

**Polystichum acrostichoides**, commonly known as the **Christmas fern**, is an evergreen fern native to [[polypore-fungi-eastern-north-america]] America. It belongs to the family **Dryopteridaceae** and is one of the most recognizable woodland ferns in its range.

The species earns its common name from its fronds, which remain green through the winter and were historically used in Christmas decorations. It is a clump-forming fern reaching 30–80 cm in height.

Christmas fern is widely cultivated as a [[hamilton-shade-garden-plants]] plant for its reliability, evergreen foliage, and tolerance of a range of soil conditions. It serves as a larval host for several moth species in the wild.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Taxonomy:**

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Clade:** Polypodiopsida
- **Order:** Polypodiales
- **Family:** Dryopteridaceae
- **Genus:** *Polystichum*
- **Species:** *P. acrostichoides* (Michx.) Schott

**Synonyms:** *Aspidium acrostichoides* Michx., *Polystichum acrostichoides* var. *incisum* Farw.

The species was first described by André Michaux in 1803 as *Aspidium acrostichoides* and later transferred to *Polystichum* by Heinrich Wilhelm Schott in 1834. No major taxonomic revisions have split the species, though several infraspecific varieties have been described and subsequently merged.

Molecular phylogenetic studies place *P. acrostichoides* within a clade of primarily [[growing-gourmet-north-american-reishi-species]] *Polystichum* species. It is most closely related to *P. munitum* and *P. lemmonii* based on chloroplast DNA sequence data.

**Morphology:**

Fronds are **pinnately compound**, 30–80 cm long and 10–20 cm wide, with 20–40 pairs of pinnae per frond. Each pinna is **lanceolate**, 3–8 cm long, with a distinctive **auricle** (ear-shaped lobe) at the base pointing toward the frond tip.

The **rachis** (central stem) is covered with brown, scales that are lighter at the base. Fronds arise from a **short, erect rhizome** covered with persistent old frond bases, forming a dense crown.

**Fertile fronds** are similar in size to sterile fronds but bear **sori** (spore clusters) on the undersides of the pinnae. The sori are covered by a **peltate indusium** (shield-shaped membrane) and are arranged in two rows near the midvein of each pinna.

## Distribution and Habitat

Christmas fern is native to eastern North America, ranging from **Nova Scotia west to eastern Minnesota**, south to **eastern Texas** and **northern Florida**. It is absent from most of the Gulf Coastal Plain and the Great Plains.

The species occurs primarily in **deciduous and mixed forests**, often on **rocky slopes, stream banks, and ravines**. It tolerates both acidic and circumneutral soils, preferring well-drained loams with high [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]].

Elevation range extends from sea level to approximately **1,200 m** in the southern Appalachian Mountains. It is considered an **obligate upland species**, rarely found in wetlands or flood-prone areas.

Christmas fern frequently grows alongside other woodland ferns including [[athyrium-filix-femina]], and [[dryopteris-filix-mas]]** within Dryopteridaceae, but can be distinguished by the presence of auricles on the pinnae bases and the peltate (not reniform) indusium covering the sori.
