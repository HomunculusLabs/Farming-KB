## Overview

**Polystichum acrostichoides**, commonly known as the **Christmas fern**, is an evergreen fern native to [[hamilton-shade-garden-plants]] plant for its reliability, evergreen foliage, and tolerance of a range of soil conditions. It serves as a larval host for several moth species in the wild.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Taxonomy:**

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Clade:** Polypodiopsida
- **Order:** Polypodiales
- **Family:** Dryopteridaceae
- **Genus:** *Polystichum*
- **Species:** *P. acrostichoides* (Michx.) Schott

**Synonyms:** *Aspidium acrostichoides* Michx., *Polystichum acrostichoides* var. *incisum* Farw.

The species was first described by André Michaux in 1803 as *Aspidium acrostichoides* and later transferred to *Polystichum* by Heinrich Wilhelm Schott in 1834. No major taxonomic revisions have split the species, though several infraspecific varieties have been described and subsequently merged.

Molecular phylogenetic studies place *P. acrostichoides* within a clade of primarily [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]].

Elevation range extends from sea level to approximately **1,200 m** in the southern Appalachian Mountains. It is considered an **obligate upland species**, rarely found in wetlands or flood-prone areas.

Christmas fern frequently grows alongside other woodland ferns including [[dryopteris-filix-mas]]** within Dryopteridaceae, but can be distinguished by the presence of auricles on the pinnae bases and the peltate (not reniform) indusium covering the sori.
