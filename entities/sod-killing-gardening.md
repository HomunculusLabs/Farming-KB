## Sod Killing (Gardening)

## Description

Sod killing is the process of converting an established grass-covered area into a plantable garden bed. Grass forms a dense network of tough roots that holds soil firmly together and aggressively regrows after disturbance. Successfully eliminating sod requires either mechanical destruction (rototilling or hand-digging) or chemical intervention (glyphosate), followed by decomposition time before planting can proceed. This is typically the first and most labor-intensive step in establishing a new vegetable garden.

## Classification

- **Category:** Garden establishment technique
- **Type:** Land preparation / sod elimination
- **Methods:** Rotary cultivation, hand-digging, herbicide, solarization, [[sheet-mulching]]
- **Timing:** Best done in autumn for spring planting; possible year-round in mild climates

## Key Facts

- **Grass persistence:** Grass will not die from a single turning; it regrows aggressively and must be killed through repeated disturbance or chemical means
- **Optimal timing:** End of summer / early autumn is ideal — gives sod time to decompose over winter for spring planting
- **Site preparation:** Mow grass as close to ground as possible before beginning; remove tall clippings
- **Moisture requirement:** Soil must be at correct moisture for working — use the "ready-to-till test"
- **Hand-digging method:** Three-turn process over ~3 weeks — first turn damages grass, second turn (1 week later) kills most of it, third turn produces crumbly soil
- **Rototiller method:** Multiple shallow passes, cross-tilling pattern; wait 3-7 days between sessions
- **Glyphosate option:** Spraying glyphosate 4-8 weeks before digging allows sod to die and partially rot, making subsequent work at least twice as fast
- **Bed sizing:** Work in 100-square-foot sections (5 ft x 25 ft) — manageable units that prevent overwhelm
- **Amendment incorporation:** Spread lime, COF, manure/compost before digging so they are mixed in during the sod-killing process
- **Labor estimate:** ~90 minutes for experienced gardener to hand-dig one 125 sq ft section of new sod; ~6 days for 725 sq ft

## Methods Comparison

| Method | Speed | Cost | Soil Health | Depth | Plow Pan Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hand-digging | Slow (90 min/125 sq ft) | Free | Excellent | Full blade depth | None |
| Rototiller | Fast (~1,000 sq ft/hr) | Rental + fuel | Moderate | 5 inches | High |
| Glyphosate + dig | Medium | Herbicide cost | Poor (chemical) | Full blade depth | None |
| Solarization | Very slow (6-8 weeks) | Plastic sheet | Good | Surface only | None |
| Sheet mulch | Slow (months) | Carbon sources | Excellent | Surface only | None |

## Hand-Digging Procedure (Solomon Method)

1. **Mow:** Scalp grass to ground level
2. **Amend:** Spread lime, COF, manure/compost over the area
3. **Mark beds:** 5 ft wide x 25 ft long sections with pegs and string
4. **First dig (Week 1):** Nibble small chunks, place upside down or on sides in trench
5. **Second dig (Week 2):** Turn again, breaking chunks smaller, blending amendments
6. **Third dig (Week 3):** Final turn; remove surviving grass clumps to compost heap

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **[[growing-gourmet-outdoor-mushroom-bed-establishment]]:** Sod must be fully killed and decomposed before establishing [[cotter-outdoor-mushroom-garden-mulch-beds]] beds to prevent grass competition
- **Mycorrhizal disruption:** Sod killing disrupts existing [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-networks]]; re-inoculation may be needed
- **Soil biology reset:** Decomposing sod releases nutrients and stimulates microbial activity beneficial [[grass-seed-substrate-for-mushroom-cultivation]]
- **Sheet mulching alternative:** Cardboard + substrate + mulch can both kill grass and establish [[psilocybin-mushroom-habitat-and-ecology]] simultaneously
- **Weed seed management:** Turning sod brings weed seeds to surface where they germinate; subsequent digging kills seedlings
