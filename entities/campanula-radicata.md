## Overview

Campanula radicata Poir. is a perennial herbaceous plant in the family Campanulaceae. It is commonly known as the rooted bellflower and is native to the eastern Mediterranean region.

The species is distributed primarily in Greece, the Aegean islands, and western Anatolia. It is characterized by its basal rosette of hairy leaves and tall, slender flowering stems bearing nodding blue-violet bells.

Campanula radicata inhabits rocky, limestone-dominated habitats from lowland to montane elevations. It is a characteristic species of Greek phrygana and open scrub communities.

The species is cultivated occasionally in alpine and rock gardens for its compact foliage and elegant flower display. It is well adapted to dry Mediterranean summer conditions.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Campanula radicata was described by Jean Louis Marie Poiret in 1789. The specific epithet "radicata" refers to the prominent basal rootstock from which new growth emerges each season.

The species belongs to Campanula sect. Quinqueloculares, a group of Mediterranean bellflowers characterized by their rotate-campanulate corollas. Molecular studies confirm its close relationship to other eastern Mediterranean species in the section.

It is a perennial herb with a woody, branched rootstock. The rootstock produces a tight basal rosette of leaves and multiple flowering stems.

Basal leaves are oblanceolate to spathulate, 3–10 cm long, covered with soft spreading hairs. They are petiolate with crenate to dentate margins and form a compact rosette close to the ground.

Flowering stems are erect, slender, 20–60 cm tall, typically unbranched. They bear a few reduced, bract-like leaves and terminate in a loose raceme of 3–15 flowers.

Flowers are nodding, campanulate, bright blue-violet, 1.5–2.5 cm long. The corolla is divided about one-third of its length into five spreading lobes.

The calyx lobes are linear-lanceolate, about half the corolla length, and spreading to slightly reflexed in fruit. The style is exserted and curved downward.

The fruit is a nodding, ovoid capsule dehiscing by three valves near the base. Seeds are small, ellipsoid, light brown, and approximately 0.8 mm long.

The chromosome number is 2n = 34, consistent with the base number of x = 17 for the genus. This diploid condition is shared with most Mediterranean Campanula species.

The species is relatively long-lived for a herbaceous perennial, with individual plants persisting for 10–15 years in favorable conditions. The woody rootstock allows regrowth after grazing or drought stress.

## Distribution and Habitat

Campanula radicata occurs primarily in Greece, including the mainland, the Peloponnese, and numerous Aegean islands. Its range extends eastward into western Anatolia in Turkey.

The species is calcicolous, strongly associated with limestone and marble substrates. It grows in rock crevices, on cliff faces, in stony phrygana, and in open pine woodlands.

Elevational range extends from near sea level to approximately 1800 m in the mountains of central Greece. Populations on the Aegean islands are typically at lower elevations.

The species is well adapted to the Mediterranean climate with hot, dry summers and mild, wet winters. It is summer-dormant in the hottest parts of its range.

Campanula radicata is not considered globally threatened. However, some small island populations may be vulnerable to habitat disturbance from tourism development.

On popular tourist islands such as Rhodes and Crete, coastal development has reduced habitat at lower elevations. Higher-elevation populations remain relatively secure due to steep terrain and limited access.

The species is listed on Annex II of the EU Habitats Directive as part of the habitat type "Mediterranean calcareous cliff communities." This provides indirect protection for its habitat within Natura 2000 sites.

## Cultivation and Uses

Campanula radicata is cultivated as a rock garden plant in regions with Mediterranean or dry-summer climates. It is suitable for USDA zones 7–9 and requires excellent drainage.

The species performs best in full sun to light shade in alkaline to neutral, well-drained soils. It tolerates drought well once established but resents winter wet and heavy clay soils.

Propagation is by seed sown in autumn or spring. Seeds germinate within 2–4 weeks without special pretreatment. Cuttings from basal shoots can be rooted in summer.

The species benefits from a dry summer rest period in cultivation. Overwatering during summer dormancy is the most common cause of loss in garden settings.

In the garden, it combines well with other Mediterranean perennials such as thymes, helianthemums, and dwarf cistus species. The species is relatively pest-free when grown in appropriate conditions.

The species has limited ethnobotanical significance. In some rural Greek communities, the young leaves have been occasionally used as a potherb, though this use is uncommon and poorly documented.

The species has ornamental potential that remains underexploited in horticulture. Its tolerance of drought and alkaline soils makes it suitable for water-wise gardening in Mediterranean climate regions worldwide.

## Ecological Role

Campanula radicata is a component of the diverse phrygana and garrigue communities of the eastern Mediterranean. These shrubland communities are among the most species-rich plant assemblages in Europe.

The flowers are visited by a variety of native bees, particularly solitary bees in the families Megachilidae and Anthophoridae. The pendant flower form provides some protection from rain while allowing bee access.

Seed dispersal occurs through passive release from the nodding capsules. Gravity and wind move seeds short distances from parent plants, often into nearby rock crevices.

Despite limited dispersal distance, the species has colonized most suitable limestone habitats across the Aegean. Island-hopping dispersal during lower sea levels in the Pleistocene likely facilitated its current distribution pattern.

The species contributes to the biodiversity of limestone cliff communities in Greece. These habitats support numerous endemic species and are priority conservation targets under European habitat directives.

Its deep rootstock allows survival through extended summer drought. This adaptation makes it an important species for understanding plant responses to Mediterranean-type climates.

The species shows morphological variation across its range, particularly in leaf hairiness and flower size. Aegean island populations tend to be smaller and more compact than mainland Greek populations.

## Related Species

Campanula radicata belongs to a group of Mediterranean bellflowers that includes several closely related species. Taxonomic boundaries within this group have been debated and revised based on molecular evidence.

Campanula versicolor is a similar species from southern Greece that differs in its more deeply divided corolla and denser inflorescence. The two species are partially sympatric in the Peloponnese.

Campanula lyrata is another eastern Mediterranean species with a similar growth form. It can be distinguished by its lyrate-pinnatifid basal leaves, which are more deeply lobed than those of Campanula radicata.

Campanula carpathica (formerly included in C. rotundifolia) is a more widespread European species that differs in its smooth, glabrous leaves and smaller flowers. It occurs in more northern and montane habitats.

The phylogenetic relationships within this Mediterranean group continue to be refined. Ongoing molecular studies using next-generation sequencing are expected to clarify several taxonomic uncertainties.
