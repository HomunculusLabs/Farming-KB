# N-Methyl-PEA

N-Methyl-PEA (N-Methylphenethylamine) is a substituted phenethylamine that occurs both endogenously in the human body and as a minor component of some dietary sources.

It is documented in the context of PiHKAL's foundational compounds.

## Chemical Identity

Full chemical name: N-Methylphenethylamine.

Molecular formula: C9H13N.

N-Methyl-PEA is the simplest N-substituted [[phenethylamine-chemistry-basics]], consisting of the basic phenethylamine structure with a single methyl group on the amine nitrogen.

The compound is intermediate in structure between pea-entity|PEA (phenethylamine) and amphetamine (alpha-methylphenethylamine).

The hydrochloride salt melts at 104-105 degrees Celsius.

## Endogenous Occurrence

N-Methyl-PEA occurs naturally in the human body as a trace amine.

It is produced through the N-methylation of phenethylamine by the enzyme phenylethanolamine N-methyltransferase (PNMT).

PNMT is better known for its role in converting norepinephrine to epinephrine in the adrenal medulla.

N-Methyl-PEA has been detected in human urine and brain tissue.

The compound has also been identified in various plant and food sources, though typically at low concentrations.

## Dosage and Duration

N-Methyl-PEA is only weakly psychoactive when administered orally due to rapid metabolism by monoamine oxidase enzymes.

Effective oral dosage: not well established (likely greater than 500 mg without MAO inhibition).

Duration: very short (minutes) due to rapid metabolic clearance.

Like pea-entity|PEA, N-Methyl-PEA requires MAO inhibition to produce significant central effects.

## Qualitative Effects

When effective (with MAO-B inhibition), N-Methyl-PEA produces mild stimulant effects.

The experience is described as similar to a very mild amphetamine, with increased alertness and subtle mood elevation.

Effects are generally less pronounced than those of pea-entity|PEA at equivalent doses.

The compound is not considered recreational or psychedelic at any accessible dose.

## Pharmacological Context

N-Methyl-PEA acts primarily through the release of catecholamines (dopamine and norepinephrine) from presynaptic terminals.

The compound has lower affinity for monoamine transporters than either pea-entity|PEA or amphetamine.

N-Methyl-PEA also acts as an agonist at trace amine-associated receptors (TAARs), though with lower potency than pea-entity|PEA at TAAR1.

The compound's pharmacological profile is intermediate between pea-entity|PEA and amphetamine, consistent with its structural position.

## Significance in Structure-Activity Relationships

N-Methyl-PEA occupies a pivotal position in the phenethylamine structure-activity relationship.

The progression from pea-entity|PEA (no alpha-methyl, no N-methyl) through N-Methyl-PEA (N-methyl only) to amphetamine (alpha-methyl only) and methamphetamine (both alpha-methyl and N-methyl) demonstrates how each substitution contributes to potency and character.

N-Methyl-PEA's weak activity shows that N-methylation alone, without alpha-methylation, does not produce a potent stimulant.

The alpha-methyl group is the critical determinant of stimulant potency in this series.

## Role in PiHKAL Context

While N-Methyl-PEA is not itself a psychedelic compound, it serves as a reference point for understanding the N-substitution patterns explored throughout PiHKAL.

The transition from N-H to N-CH3 to N-C2H5 and beyond is a recurring theme in Shulgin's structure-activity explorations.

N-Methyl-PEA demonstrates that N-substitution, in the absence of aromatic ring substitution, does not produce significant psychoactivity.

## Legal Status

N-Methyl-PEA is not a controlled substance in most jurisdictions.

It is available as a research chemical and dietary supplement in some countries.

Its status as a naturally occurring trace amine contributes to its unregulated status.

## Harm Reduction

N-Methyl-PEA is generally considered safe at normal dietary and endogenous levels.

High doses, particularly when combined with MAO inhibitors, could produce cardiovascular effects.

The compound should not be combined with MAO-A or MAO-B inhibitors without medical supervision. For general harm reduction principles, see [[psychedelic-safety-and-harm-reduction]].

N-Methyl-PEA has not been extensively studied for long-term effects at supraphysiological doses.
