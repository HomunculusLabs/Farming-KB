# Peltigera canina

**Peltigera canina**, the dog lichen, is a foliose lichen-forming ascomycete in the family Peltigeraceae.

It is notable for its role in advancing understanding of lichen hydrophobin biology and water relations.

The genus *Peltigera* includes species with both green algal and cyanobacterial photobionts.

P. canina has been an important subject for studying how lichen fungi manage water at their symbiotic interfaces.

## Taxonomy and Classification

P. canina belongs to the class Lecanoromycetes within the [[water-management]] in [[staycare-degradation-of-plant-cell-wall-polymers-by-fungi]] layers form the inner boundary.

The mycobiont-derived rodlet layer of hydrophobin controls water movement between the symbiotic partners.

Gas-filled cortical cells recognized by Goebel (1926) in P. canina provide additional structural complexity at the interface.

## Poikilohydric Adaptation

Like other lichen-forming fungi, P. canina exhibits poikilohydric water relations.

Desiccated cells can survive temperature extremes unharmed in a state of dormancy.

The species is adapted to continuous wetting and drying cycles as experienced during diurnal and seasonal fluctuations in water availability.

However, lichen-forming fungi typically die off under continuously moist conditions or when prevented from drying at elevated temperatures.

## Photobiont Diversity in Peltigera

The genus Peltigera exhibits photobiont diversity relevant to understanding P. canina.

Approximately 85 percent of lichen-forming ascomycetes have green algal photobionts (chlorolichens).

About 10 percent have cyanobacterial photobionts (cyanolichens).

About 4 percent have both simultaneously.

Some Peltigera species are cephalodiate lichens with a green algal partner providing fixed carbon and a diazotrophic cyanobacterial partner delivering fixed nitrogen.

In such triple symbioses, the fungal partner creates microaerobic conditions around the cyanobacterial colony.

This results in increased heterocyst frequencies and enhanced [[comparison-biological-nitrogen-fixation-vs-synthetic-fertilizer]].

## Distribution and Habitat

Peltigera species, including P. canina, are commonly found on soil, moss, and at the bases of trees in moist habitats.

They are particularly characteristic of nutrient-enriched sites.

They are often found in association with nitrogen-fixing cyanobacteria.

The genus is widely distributed in temperate and boreal regions.
