# Overview

*Trametes gibbosa* is a polyporoid bracket fungus in the family Polyporaceae, widely distributed across temperate and [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]].

It is a white-rot decomposer commonly found on dead hardwood, particularly beech, oak, and birch.

The species is notable for its lumpy, uneven pore surface and concentrically zoned upper surface, which give it a distinctive appearance among bracket fungi.

Though not widely consumed as an edible, it has attracted interest for its bioactive metabolites and potential [[bloomfield-mushroom-formation-fruiting-body-development-cultivation]] is a semicircular to fan-shaped bracket, 5–15 cm wide and 1–4 cm thick at the base.

The upper surface is concentrically zoned in shades of white, grey, and pale brown, often with a whitish bloom.

Surface texture is finely tomentose when young, becoming smoother with age.

The pore surface is white to cream, with large, angular and irregular (daedaleoid) pores that distinguish it from the more regular pores of *T. versicolor*.

Pores are 1–3 per mm, labyrinthine in maturity.

The context is white, tough, and corky, with a pleasant fungal odor.

Spores are cylindrical to allantoid, 5–7 × 1.5–2.5 µm, hyaline, smooth, and inamyloid.

The hyphal system is dimitic, with generative hyphae bearing clamp connections and skeletal hyphae.

Cystidia are absent or very rare on the hymenium.

## Bioactive Compounds

Like other *Trametes* species, *T. gibbosa* produces a range of bioactive [[antifungal-secondary-metabolites-coprophilous-fungi]].

Polysaccharides isolated from the fruiting body have demonstrated immunomodulatory activity in vitro, stimulating macrophage function and cytokine production.

Triterpenoids present in the context show anti-inflammatory properties in preliminary assays.
