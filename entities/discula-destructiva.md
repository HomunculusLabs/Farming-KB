## Discula destructiva

## Description

**Discula destructiva** is an ascomycete fungus that causes dogwood anthracnose, a serious disease of flowering dogwood (*[[cornus-florida]]*) and Pacific dogwood (*Cornus nuttallii*) in North America. First identified in the late 1970s, the disease has caused extensive mortality in both forest and ornamental dogwood populations, particularly in the eastern United States. The fungus infects leaves, twigs, and branches, producing necrotic lesions that can girdle stems and kill entire trees, especially in cool, moist conditions at higher elevations.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Eukaryota
- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** [[ascomycota]]
- **Class:** Sordariomycetes
- **Order:** Diaporthales
- **Family:** Valsaceae
- **Genus:** *Discula*
- **Species:** *D. destructiva*

**Note:** The teleomorph stage has been placed in the genus *Neoanova*, though the anamorph name *Discula destructiva* remains more commonly used in plant pathology literature.

## Key Facts

- First observed in the northeastern United States in the late 1970s and has since spread throughout the range of flowering dogwood
- Causes leaf spots, blotches, and stem cankers that can girdle and kill branches
- Most severe at higher elevations (>300m) and in shaded, moist forest understory sites
- Kills trees of all ages, but seedlings and stressed trees are most vulnerable
- Spread by rain-splashed conidia during wet weather
- Overwinters in infected twigs and fallen leaves
- Has killed millions of dogwood trees across the Appalachian region
- Threatens both ecological integrity of eastern deciduous forests and ornamental landscaping
- Fungicide treatments (chlorothalonil, mancozeb) can protect high-value trees but are not practical for forest settings

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

*Discula destructiva* is relevant to cultivation and [[mycology]]:

1. **Ornamental Horticulture:** Flowering dogwood is one of the most popular ornamental trees in the United States. Dogwood anthracnose has severely impacted nursery production and landscape management.

2. **Forest Ecology:** Dogwood is a keystone understory species in eastern forests, producing high-quality fruit for wildlife. Its decline affects birds, mammals, and nutrient cycling patterns.

3. **Anthracnose Diseases:** Dogwood anthracnose is part of a broader group of anthracnose diseases (caused by various *Discula*, *Colletotrichum*, and *Glomerella* species) that affect many important plant species.

4. **Climate and Disease:** The disease's severity at cooler, moist sites demonstrates how microclimate conditions influence fungal pathogen dynamics — relevant to understanding climate change impacts on forest health.

5. **Breeding for Resistance:** Efforts to develop resistant dogwood cultivars (particularly *C. florida* x *C. kousa* hybrids like the Rutgan series) integrate pathology with horticultural breeding.

## Ecological Context

In Dighton's *Fungi in Ecosystem Processes*, *Discula destructiva* is referenced in the index as a pathogen affecting hardwood forest trees, contributing to forest decline phenomena alongside other introduced pathogens like chestnut blight and Dutch elm disease.
