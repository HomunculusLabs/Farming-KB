# Elsinoë

## Description

*Elsinoë* is a genus of plant-pathogenic ascomycete fungi in the order
Dothideales (formerly Myriangiales) that causes **scab diseases** on a
wide range of economically important tropical and subtropical crops.
The genus is the teleomorphic (sexual) state, while the associated
anamorphic (asexual) stage belongs to the genus **Sphaceloma**.

*Elsinoë* species produce **scab symptoms** — roughened, crustlike
diseased areas on fruits, leaves, and stems that reduce crop quality,
marketability, and yield. In tropical agriculture, *Elsinoë* scabs are
among the most widespread and damaging diseases of fruit and plantation
crops.

## Classification

- **[[kingdom-fungi]]
- **Phylum:** [[ascomycota]]
- **Class:** Dothideomycetes
- **Order:** Dothideales (Myriangiales)
- **Family:** Elsinoaceae
- **Genus:** *Elsinoë*
- **Anamorph:** *Sphaceloma*

The placement in Myriangiales vs. Dothideales has varied with different
taxonomic treatments; von Arx (1963) treated the group as Myriangiales.
The genus is characterized by **globose, pulvinate, or discoid ascomata**
with multiple locules containing bitunicate asci.

## Key Facts

### Important Species and Diseases

- **Elsinoë fawcettii** (anamorph: *Sphaceloma fawcettii*) — causes
  **citrus scab**, one of the most important diseases of citrus worldwide,
  producing raised, corky, scabby lesions on fruit, leaves, and twigs
- **Elsinoë australis** — causes **sweet orange scab**, affecting
  primarily oranges and mandarins; less common than citrus scab but
  important in South America
- **Elsinoë mangiferae** — causes **mango scab**, producing grayish-brown
  corky lesions on mango fruits and leaves
- **Elsinoë veneta** — causes **raspberry cane spot**, affecting
  caneberries in temperate regions
- **Elsinoë ampelina** (anamorph: *Sphaceloma ampelinum*) — causes
  **grapevine anthracnose** (also called bird's-eye rot), producing
  characteristic circular lesions with dark margins and lighter centers
- **Elsinoë pyri** — causes **pear scab**
- **Elsinoë perseae** — affects **avocado**

### Host Range

*Elsinoë* scabs occur on a wide range of agriculturally important
tropical and subtropical hosts including:
- **Avocado** (*[[persea-americana]]*)
- **Cashew** (*Anacardium occidentale*)
- **Castor** (*Ricinus communis*)
- **Groundnut/peanut** (*Arachis hypogaea*)
- **Papaw/papaya** (*Carica papaya*)
- **Poinsettia** (*Euphorbia pulcherrima*)
- **Soybean** (*Glycine max*)
- **Citrus** (*Citrus* spp.)
- **Mango** (*Mangifera indica*)
- **Grape** (*Vitis* spp.)

### Symptoms

- **Scab lesions** are roughened, crustlike, or warty areas on fruits,
  leaves, and young stems
- On fruit, lesions may be **raised and corky** or **sunken and scabby**
- On leaves, infections appear as **small, raised, corky spots** that
  may coalesce
- Young, actively growing tissues are most susceptible
- Severe infections can cause **leaf distortion, premature drop**, and
  significant reduction in fruit quality

### Environmental Conditions

- *Elsinoë* species require **prolonged wetness** for infection,
  typically 4–12 hours of free moisture on plant surfaces
- Optimal temperatures for most species are **20–28°C**
- Infection is most severe during **warm, wet growing seasons** with
  frequent rainfall or overhead irrigation
- Conidia (produced by *Sphaceloma* anamorphs) are dispersed primarily
  by **rain splash** and wind-driven rain

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

### Tropical Agriculture

- Citrus scab and mango scab are among the **most important diseases**
  in tropical and subtropical fruit production
- Management relies on **protective fungicide applications** during
  susceptible growth stages (flush growth, fruit set, early fruit
  development)
- **Cultural practices** — removing infected debris, improving air
  circulation, avoiding overhead irrigation — reduce disease pressure
- **Resistant cultivars** are available for some crops (e.g., citrus
  varieties vary widely in susceptibility to scab)

### Scab vs. Anthracnose

- *Elsinoë* scabs are often confused with **anthracnose** caused by
  *Colletotrichum* species, as both produce raised, crusty lesions on
  fruit
- Key distinction: *Elsinoë* scab lesions tend to be **more superficial
  and corky**, while *Colletotrichum* anthracnose produces **deeper,
  sunken lesions** with characteristic pink-orange conidial masses under
  humid conditions
- Correct identification is essential for effective fungicide selection

### Substrate Considerations

- Fruit and plant material from *Elsinoë*-infected crops can be used as
  mushroom substrate after proper composting
- *Elsinoë* is a **facultative parasite** that cannot survive extended
  saprotrophic growth, so thorough composting eliminates inoculum risk

### Quarantine Significance

- Some *Elsinoë* species are **regulated quarantine pests** in
  international trade
- *Elsinoë australis* (sweet orange scab) is listed as an **A1
  quarantine pest** by EPPO and is subject to phytosanitary regulations
  in many citrus-producing countries
- Accurate identification to species level is critical for regulatory
  compliance
