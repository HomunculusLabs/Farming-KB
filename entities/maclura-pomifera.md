## Maclura pomifera

## Overview

- **Common names:** [[extreme-heat-preparedness]],
  cold, drought, and poor soils
- **Root system:** Deep taproot with extensive lateral roots;
  soil-binding and erosion-controlling
- **[[dighton-fungal-decomposition-leaf-litter-dynamics]] return
and deep mineral mining from subsoil layers.

## Taxonomy

- **Binomial:** Maclura pomifera (Raf.) C.K.Schneid.
- **Family:** Moraceae (mulberry family)
- **Authority:** Charles Konstantin Gabriel Schneid., 1906
  (basionym: Ilex pomifera Raf., 1818)
- **Order:** Rosales
- **Synonyms:**
  - Maclura aurantiaca Nutt.
  - Toxylon pomiferum Raf.
  - Ilex pomifera Raf.
  - Joxylon pomiferum (Raf.) Skeels
  - Cardioxyylon aurantiacum (Nutt.) Prenant
- **Type:** Collected in Arkansas; original Rafinesque
  description based on Osage Nation material

## Description and Morphology

- **Tree size:** 30-60 ft tall, 1-2 ft DBH; open crown,
  rounded to irregular form
- **Bark:** Dark brown to orange-brown, deeply furrowed
  with ridges, scaly on mature trunks
- **Branches:** Stout, zigzag at nodes, armed with 0.5-1 in
  sharp thorns (especially on young growth)
- **Leaves:** Simple, alternate, 3-5 in x 2-3 in; ovate to
  lanceolate; glossy dark green above, paler below; entire
  or slightly serrate near apex; pinnately veined; milky
  latex on break
- **Flowers:** Dioecious; apetalous, greenish, in tight
  globular clusters (May-June); male flowers in racemes,
  female flowers in globular heads
- **Fruit:** Large globose syncarp, 4-6 in diameter, bright
  green turning yellow-green; rough-textured, citrus-scented;
  aggregate of many small drupelets around a central core;
  not edible raw (causes GI distress); seeds dispersed by
  former megafauna
- **Wood:** Bright yellow-orange heartwood deepening to amber
  with age; extremely dense (SG 0.77-0.86); hard, heavy,
  strong; highest rot resistance of any [[north-american-dmt-containing-plants-phalaris-desmanthus-peganum]] trees,
colonizing disturbed ground rapidly via root sprouts and seed
distribution (originally by extinct megafauna, now primarily
by water and human activity).
