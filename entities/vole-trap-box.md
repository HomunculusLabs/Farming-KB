## Vole Trap Box

**Classification:** Pest management technique / Physical trap design  
**Category:** Technique

## Description

The Vole Trap Box is a non-chemical pest management device developed by [[eliot-coleman]] at [[four-season-farm]] for controlling vole (*Microtus* spp.) populations in market gardens and greenhouses. The design exploits a fundamental vole behavioral trait—their instinctive tendency to enter small, dark holes—eliminating the need for bait entirely.

## Design and Construction

The trap box is a simple wooden enclosure with the following specifications:

- **Dimensions:** Approximately 12 inches long × 8 inches wide × 6 inches deep
- **Construction:** Wooden box with attached bottom and removable top for easy access
- **Entry holes:** Two mouse-sized holes drilled at floor level, one at each 8-inch end
- **Traps:** Standard snap traps placed inside each entrance hole, unbaited

## How It Works

1. Voles naturally investigate small, dark openings as part of their burrowing behavior
2. They enter the box through the drilled holes and trigger the snap trap inside
3. No bait is required—the dark hole itself is the attractant
4. Once the first vole is caught, the box accumulates vole scent, making it even more attractive to subsequent voles
5. Traps are checked each morning, emptied, and reset

## Key Facts

- **Bait-free operation:** Eliminates the cost, mess, and maintenance of baiting
- **Behavioral exploitation:** Uses innate vole behavior rather than food attraction
- **Self-reinforcing:** Vole scent from previous captures increases effectiveness
- **Maintenance:** Daily checking; lift removable top, empty traps, reset
- **Effectiveness:** Coleman describes results as "impressively effective" and ongoing

## Advantages Over Baited Traps

- No need to purchase, store, or handle bait materials
- No bait degradation or spoilage concerns
- No non-target attraction (birds, raccoons, etc.)
- Simpler and more reliable over time
- Voles have no aversion to previously occupied traps

## Relevance to Cultivation

Voles can cause significant damage to vegetable crops by gnawing roots, stems, and tubers. In greenhouse environments, they can be particularly problematic during winter months when outdoor food sources are scarce. The trap box design exemplifies Coleman's broader philosophy of working with natural behaviors rather than against them—a physical, non-chemical approach that aligns with organic farming principles.

This technique demonstrates the principle that understanding pest biology and behavior leads to more elegant, effective, and environmentally sound solutions than chemical intervention.
