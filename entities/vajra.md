## Vajra (Dorje)

## Description

The Vajra (Sanskrit: वज्र, "thunderbolt" or "diamond"; Tibetan: Dorje) is a ritual
implement used in Vajrayana Buddhism. A small metal object, round and bulbous at both
ends with a narrow connecting piece in the middle, it is held during meditation and
ritual practice. The Vajra gives Vajrayana Buddhism its name — the "lightning-fast" or
"diamond vehicle" path to enlightenment.

## Classification

- **Category:** Ritual object / religious symbol
- **Cultural origin:** Tibetan Buddhism (Vajrayana), also Hinduism
- **Sanskrit meaning:** Thunderbolt, lightning, diamond, indestructible
- **Tibetan name:** Dorje
- **Ethnomycological interpretation:** Stylized [[metzner-non-drug-methods-consciousness-transformation]]
- **Death of the ego:** Vajrayana "death yoga" parallels A. muscaria ego-death
- **Secret knowledge:** Highest initiations involve restricted knowledge

### Connection to Stupa
The Stupa is shaped like a mushroom with fire emerging from top. Stupa and Vajra
are complementary Vajrayana symbols linked to the same referent.

## Cross-Cultural Parallels

- **Norse — Thor's Hammer (Mjolnir):** Similar shape, lightning, divine power
- **Hindu — Indra's Vajra:** Ancestral to Buddhist object
- **Greek — Zeus's Thunderbolt:** Lightning weapon, sudden divine manifestation
- **Egyptian — Djed Pillar:** Structurally similar stacked-column symbol
