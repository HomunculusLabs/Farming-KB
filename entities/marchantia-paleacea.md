The species forms prostrate, dichotomously branched thalli 1–2 cm wide and 2–5 cm long. It is distinguished from Marchantia polymorpha by its more delicate texture, thinner thallus margins, and distinctive crescent-shaped gemmae cups.

Marchantia paleacea is less commonly encountered than its famous relative but serves as an important subject in bryological research. Its cellular anatomy and [[capillary-action-in-soils]].

Oil bodies are present in all thallus cells, appearing as small, ovoid inclusions visible under high magnification. These structures contain terpenoid compounds characteristic of the Marchantiales.

The thallus anatomy includes a dorsal epidermis, air chamber layer, photosynthetic filament layer, and storage parenchyma. This differentiated internal structure is a hallmark of the Marchantiales.

## Distribution and Habitat

Marchantia paleacea has a broad distribution across temperate Eurasia, North Africa, and parts of North America. It is particularly common in Mediterranean and subtropical regions of Europe, including Italy, Spain, Greece, and Turkey.

In North America, it occurs sporadically in the southeastern United States and along the Pacific coast. It is less common in northern latitudes than Marchantia polymorpha, preferring milder winter climates.

The species grows on damp, calcareous or base-rich soils in partially shaded sites. Typical habitats include stream banks, moist rock crevices, garden paths, greenhouse floors, and earthworks.

It is somewhat calciphilic (lime-loving), favoring substrates with higher pH than the acidophilic Marchantia polymorpha. This substrate preference partially explains the ecological separation between the two species.

Populations are often ephemeral, appearing after disturbance and declining as competitive vascular plants establish. The species can colonize bare soil rapidly through gemmae dispersal.

In urban settings, Marchantia paleacea can appear in nursery pots, greenhouse beds, and irrigated plantings. Its presence in horticultural contexts sometimes leads to confusion with Marchantia polymorpha.

## Reproduction

Marchantia paleacea reproduces both sexually and asexually. Asexual reproduction occurs through gemmae produced in crescent-shaped cups on the dorsal thallus surface.

Gemmae are dispersed by raindrops splashing them from the cups. Each gemma can develop into a new genetically identical thallus upon landing on suitable substrate. Gemmae production occurs year-round in favorable conditions.

Sexual reproduction involves archegoniophores (female reproductive structures) and antheridiophores (male reproductive structures) borne on specialized stalks. The species is dioicous, with male and female structures on separate thalli.

Sporophytes develop from fertilized eggs and consist of a capsule, seta, and foot. Spores are released from the capsule and germinate into a protonemal stage before developing into mature thalli. [[spore]] production is less frequent than asexual reproduction in most populations.

Spores are small (12–20 μm in diameter), thin-walled, and adapted for wind dispersal. They remain viable for several months under cool, dry conditions.
