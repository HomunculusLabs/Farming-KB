# [[moringa]] oleifera

**Type:** Species
**Category:** Small deciduous tree
**Also known as:** Horseradish tree, drumstick tree, ben oil tree,
  moringa, malunggay

## Overview
Moringa oleifera is a small deciduous tree native to India, growing to
8 meters tall and 3 meters wide. Nearly every part of the tree is
useful: leaves are eaten as a nutritious vegetable, pods are used in
soups and curries, seeds yield a non-rancid oil, and the root is used
as a condiment. The leaves contain 7-10% protein, making it one of
the most nutritious plant foods available.

## Key Details

### Botany
- **Family:** Moringaceae
- **Form:** Small deciduous tree to 8 m tall, 3 m spread
- **Origin:** Native to India
- **Hardiness:** Drought-tender and frost-tender

### Edible Parts and Uses
- **Young leaves and twigs:** Mustard flavour; eaten in salads and as
  a cooked vegetable
- **Long bean-like fruit:** Used in soups and curries
- **Immature seeds:** Used like peas in cooking
- **Mature seeds:** Roasted or fried and eaten like peanuts
- **Root:** Used as a condiment similar to horseradish
- **Young seedlings:** Cooked as a vegetable

### Nutritional Profile
- Leaves contain 7-10% protein
- Rich in vitamins A, C, and calcium
- Considered a "superfood" in modern nutrition circles
- One of the most nutrient-dense leafy greens available

### Oil (Ben Oil / Behen Oil)
- Extracted from the seeds
- Notably never goes rancid due to unique fatty acid composition
- Used for salads and cooking
- Known as "watchmakers oil" for use in precision machinery
- Historically valued for cosmetic and medicinal applications

### Water Purification
- Seed powder used traditionally for water purification
- Proteins in seeds act as natural flocculants
- Removes turbidity and bacteria from drinking water
- Recognized by WHO as a low-cost water treatment option

### Traditional Medicine
- Extensive use in Ayurvedic medicine for over 4,000 years
- Anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and antimicrobial properties
- Used to treat malnutrition in developing countries

### Growth and Cultivation
- Extremely fast-growing; can reach harvestable size in months
- Drought-tolerant once established but prefers regular water
- Propagation from seed or cuttings; seeds germinate readily
- Coppices readily after cutting for leaf harvest
- Often grown as a hedge or living fence in the tropics
- Leaves can be harvested multiple times per year

### Modern Applications
- Widely promoted for malnutrition treatment in developing countries
- Leaf powder exported internationally as a nutritional supplement
- Gaining popularity in temperate regions as an annual crop

## Relevance
Moringa is one of the most important multipurpose trees for tropical
[[permaculture]] and subsistence agriculture. Its exceptional nutritional
value, fast growth, drought tolerance, and the edibility of nearly
every plant part make it a cornerstone species for food forests, home
gardens, and community nutrition programs. The non-rancid seed oil and
water purification properties add unique functional value.
