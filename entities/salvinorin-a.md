# Salvinorin A

## Overview

Salvinorin A is a psychoactive neoclerodane diterpene and the principal active compound found in *Salvia divinorum*, a species of sage in the mint family (Lamiaceae) traditionally used by the Mazatec people of Oaxaca, Mexico. Isolated and characterized in 1982 by Alfredo Ortega and colleagues at the University of Mexico City, salvinorin A is the most potent naturally occurring psychedelic compound known, with effective doses in the 200–500 µg range when vaporized. Uniquely among classical psychedelics, salvinorin A is a non-nitrogenous compound — it contains no nitrogen atoms — and exerts its effects primarily as a highly selective and potent agonist at the kappa-opioid receptor (KOR), distinguishing its pharmacological profile from the 5-HT₂A-mediated mechanisms of tryptamines (psilocybin, DMT) and lysergamides (LSD).

## Pharmacology

### Pharmacodynamics

Salvinorin A's mechanism of action is fundamentally distinct from other psychedelic compounds:

- **Kappa-opioid receptor (KOR) agonist**: Salvinorin A is a highly selective KOR agonist with an EC₅₀ of approximately 1 nM, making it the most potent known selective KOR agonist. It shows negligible affinity for mu-opioid receptors (MOR), delta-opioid receptors (DOR), 5-HT₂A receptors, dopamine receptors, or cannabinoid receptors.
- **Selectivity**: KOR selectivity is >10,000-fold over MOR and DOR, making it an extraordinarily clean pharmacological tool for studying the kappa-opioid system.
- **KOR signaling**: KOR activation leads to G-protein-mediated signaling through Gᵢ/Gₒ proteins, inhibiting adenylyl cyclase and reducing cAMP production. KOR activation also recruits β-arrestin, which mediates many of the aversive and dysphoric effects.
- **Downstream effects**: KOR activation modulates dopaminergic signaling in the mesolimbic pathway, particularly reducing dopamine release in the nucleus accumbens. This is thought to underlie both the dissociative psychedelic effects and the dysphoric/aversive components of the experience.
- **Rapid desensitization and internalization**: Salvinorin A causes rapid KOR internalization and desensitization, contributing to the extremely short duration of action despite high receptor affinity.

| Duration (buccal absorption) | 30–60 minutes |
| Half-life | Approximately 60 minutes |
| Metabolism | Hepatic, via CYP2D6 and CYP2C19; ester hydrolysis to salvinorin B |

The extremely short duration of action when vaporized is both distinctive and practically significant — the entire subjective experience typically concludes within 20 minutes, with return to baseline within 30–45 minutes. This contrasts sharply with psilocybin (4–6 hours), LSD (8–12 hours), and DMT (15–30 minutes when smoked).

## Subjective Effects

### Psychedelic Experience

The salvinorin A experience is qualitatively different from other classical psychedelics and is often described as more dissociative, disorienting, and intense:

- **Intenseness**: Despite its short duration, salvinorin A produces one of the most intense psychedelic experiences known at breakthrough doses. Users frequently report complete detachment from reality, profound alterations in body perception, and immersive hallucinatory states.
- **Dissociation**: Unlike the ego dissolution of 5-HT₂A psychedelics, salvinorin A often produces a sense of being transported to entirely different realities or dimensions. Users may lose awareness of their body, identity, and surroundings.
- **Geometric and organic visuals**: Complex, rapidly shifting visual hallucinations including geometric patterns, organic/tendril-like forms, and landscapes that appear fully real and immersive.
- **Somatic effects**: Loss of motor coordination, perceived merging with objects, gravitational sensations, and extreme distortions of body image (feeling extremely large, small, flat, or non-existent).
- **Amnesia**: Partial or complete amnesia for the experience is common, particularly at higher doses. Users may not remember what happened during the peak.

## Therapeutic Research

While clinical research with salvinorin A is in early stages compared to psilocybin and MDMA, several therapeutic applications are under investigation:

- **Treatment-resistant depression**: KOR antagonists have shown antidepressant effects in animal models, and the acute KOR agonist effects of salvinorin A may produce rapid-acting antidepressant effects through downstream neuroplastic changes.
- **Substance use disorders**: The dysphoric properties of KOR activation have been explored for their potential to reduce drug-seeking behavior and craving. Salvinorin A analogs with modified pharmacokinetics are being developed for this purpose.
- **Neuropathic pain**: KOR agonists have demonstrated analgesic properties, though the psychedelic side effects of salvinorin A limit direct therapeutic application. Non-psychedelic analogs are in development.
- **Analogs under development**: Researchers have developed semi-synthetic salvinorin A analogs (e.g., herkinorin, ethoxymethyl-salvinorin B, 2-oxo-prerinamine) that modify the pharmacokinetic profile and receptor selectivity for potential therapeutic use.

## Natural Occurrence

Salvinorin A is found exclusively in *Salvia divinorum* (Epling and Játiva-M.), a perennial herbaceous plant endemic to the Sierra Mazateca region of Oaxaca, Mexico. The compound is concentrated in the glandular trichomes on the leaves and stems. Concentration varies by leaf position and plant maturity, typically ranging from 0.08–0.37% of dry leaf weight. The plant contains over 20 related salvinorin compounds (salvinorins B through Y), though salvinorin A is the only one with significant psychoactivity.

## Cultural and Historical Context

- **Mazatec tradition**: *Salvia divinorum* has been used for centuries by the Mazatec people in divination rituals and healing ceremonies. The plant is known as "ska María Pastora" (leaves of Mary the Shepherdess) or "hojas de la Pastora."
- **Western discovery**: First brought to the attention of Western ethnobotanists by R. Gordon Wasson and Albert Hofmann in the 1950s and 1960s.
- **Modern popularity**: Gained widespread recreational popularity in the early 2000s through internet distribution and was briefly available legally in many countries before being scheduled.
- **Ethical concerns**: Commercialization of *S. divinorum* has raised concerns about biopiracy and cultural appropriation of Mazatec traditional knowledge.

## Safety Considerations

- **Physical safety**: Salvinorin A has no known lethal dose and is not considered physiologically toxic. No deaths have been directly attributed to salvinorin A.
- **Psychological safety**: The intense, disorienting nature of the experience makes it potentially frightening, especially for unprepared users. A sitter is strongly recommended.
- **Dependence potential**: No evidence of physical dependence or addiction. KOR agonists are generally considered to have anti-addictive properties.
- **Drug interactions**: No known dangerous pharmacological interactions, though combining with other psychoactive substances is strongly discouraged.
- **Contradictions with other psychedelics**: Salvinorin A's KOR-mediated mechanism means it should not be grouped with 5-HT₂A psychedelics in harm reduction frameworks. The experience profile, risks, and integration needs are fundamentally different.
