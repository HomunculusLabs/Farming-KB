# Gymnopilus luteus

**Gymnopilus luteus** is a large, orange to yellow **Gymnopilus** species found growing
on hardwood logs and stumps in [[polypore-fungi-eastern-north-america]] America and parts of Europe. It is closely
related to the well-known psychoactive species [[gymnopilus-spectabilis-entity]], and like those species, it contains the psychoactive
compounds psilocybin and psilocin, though typically in low concentrations.

## Taxonomy and Naming

Gymnopilus luteus was originally described by Charles Horton Peck in 1872 from specimens
collected in New York state. The specific epithet *luteus* refers to the distinctive
yellow coloration of the [[comparison-fruiting-body-vs-mycelium-extracts-medicinal-mushrooms]]. It belongs to the family Hymenogastraceae
(formerly placed in Cortinariaceae) within the order Agaricales. The genus Gymnopilus
is sometimes called the "big laughing gym" group due to the psychoactive properties of
several of its members.

## Morphology

### Cap
The cap (pileus) ranges from 5 to 20 cm in diameter, convex when young, expanding to
broadly convex or plane with age. The surface is dry and covered with fine fibrils or
small scales, especially near the center. Color is variable, ranging from bright
orange-yellow to deep orange with golden tones. The cap margin is often slightly
incurved in young specimens and may develop slight radial splitting with maturity.

### Gills
The gills are adnate to slightly decurrent, close to subdistant, and initially yellowish
before maturing to a rusty orange or cinnamon-brown color as spores develop. This
rust-colored spore deposit is characteristic of the genus Gymnopilus and helps
distinguish it from superficially similar species in other genera.

### Stipe
The stipe measures 6 to 15 cm in length and 1 to 3 cm in thickness, typically central
and equal or slightly tapered toward the base. It is solid, fibrous, and colored
similarly to the cap, ranging from yellow to orange. A rudimentary cortina-like veil
may be present in young specimens but typically disappears quickly, leaving no
persistent annulus in most collections.

### Spores and Microscopic Features
Spores are elliptical to amygdaliform, roughened with warty ornamentation, measuring
approximately 7 to 9 µm by 4 to 5 µm. [[comparison-spore-print-collection-vs-spore-syringe-production]] is rusty orange to cinnamon-brown,
characteristic of the genus. Basidia are four-spored, clavate, measuring 22 to 30 by 6
to 8 micrometers. Cheilocystidia are variable in form, ranging from clavate to
ventricose to lageniform, measuring 18 to 32 by 5 to 10 micrometers. Pleurocystidia
are absent or rare. The gill trama is regular (parallel hyphae), composed of long,
slender hyphae that are yellowish to brownish in KOH solution. The pileipellis is a
cutis of repent to interwoven hyphae, sometimes with scattered erect elements that give
rise to the fibrillose or scaly cap surface. Clamp connections are present at the
septa. The combination of rough-walled spores, rusty spore print, and regular gill
trama distinguishes Gymnopilus from genera with smooth spores or irregular gill trama
such as Cortinarius.

## Distribution and Habitat

Gymnopilus luteus is primarily found in eastern North America, with documented
collections from the northeastern [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]] (New York, Pennsylvania, Massachusetts,
Vermont, and Maine) and southeastern Canada (Ontario and Quebec). Its range extends
