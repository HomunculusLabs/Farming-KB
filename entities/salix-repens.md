# Salix repens

## Description

*Salix repens* (creeping willow) is a low-growing deciduous shrub in the family Salicaceae. It is distinguished by its ability to form dual mycorrhizal associations with both arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) and ectomycorrhizal (ECM) fungi, making it a model species for studying the comparative functional roles of different [[mycorrhizal-types]] on a single host plant.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Clade**: Tracheophytes
- **Clade**: Angiosperms
- **Clade**: Eudicots
- **Order**: Malpighiales
- **Family**: Salicaceae
- **Genus**: *Salix*
- **Species**: *S. repens*

## Key Facts

- Capable of forming associations with both arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) and ectomycorrhizal (ECM) fungi simultaneously.
- Research by van der Heijden (2001) demonstrated functionally distinct outcomes from AM vs. ECM associations on the same genotype.
- AM fungus *Glomus mosseae* showed low root colonization but significant short-term effects on shoot growth and root length.
- ECM fungus *Hebeloma leucosarx* showed high root colonization and improved host plant growth over a longer term.
- AM colonization resulted in higher shoot phosphorus uptake, particularly in December-collected cuttings vs. March cuttings.
- ECM and non-mycorrhizal treatments showed no seasonal difference in plant response.
- Demonstrates that dual mycorrhizal capability provides complementary nutrient acquisition strategies across seasons.
- Native to European coastal dunes, heathlands, and wet grasslands.

## Dual Mycorrhizal Functional Comparison

The differential effects of the two mycorrhizal types on *S. repens* relate to the availability of nutrients at different times of year and the inherent differences in function between AM and ECM fungi. Arbuscular mycorrhizae excel at rapid inorganic phosphorus uptake, particularly during active growing seasons, while ectomycorrhizae provide sustained nutrient access through enzyme-mediated organic nutrient mobilization and extensive mycelial networks.

| Parameter | AM (*G. mosseae*) | ECM (*H. leucosarx*) |
|-----------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Colonization | Low | High |
| Growth effect | Short-term | Long-term |
| P uptake | High (seasonal) | Moderate (consistent) |
| Seasonal response | Strong | None |

## Ecological Role

*S. repens* occupies pioneer habitats including coastal dunes and disturbed sites, where its dual mycorrhizal strategy provides resilience across variable nutrient conditions. The species contributes to dune stabilization and early successional community development. Its ability to partner with diverse mycorrhizal fungi makes it ecologically flexible and competitive in heterogeneous environments.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Dual mycorrhizal model**: The most significant research value lies in its ability to host both AM and ECM fungi, enabling direct comparison of mycorrhizal functional types.
- **Ecosystem restoration**: Useful for dune stabilization and riparian restoration due to its hardy nature and flexible mycorrhizal partnerships.
- **Mycorrhizal research**: Provides a natural system for investigating how plants allocate resources between different mycorrhizal partners.
- **Climate adaptation**: Seasonal variation in mycorrhizal benefit may inform predictions of plant response to changing climate conditions.
- **Phytoremediation**: Willow species are widely used for soil remediation, and understanding mycorrhizal partnerships enhances application potential.

## Habitat and Distribution

*S. repens* is native to western and central Europe, where it inhabits coastal dunes, sandy heaths, wet grasslands, and peat bogs. It is particularly characteristic of fixed dune and dune slack communities along the Atlantic coast. The species is well-adapted to nutrient-poor, free-draining sandy soils and tolerates both drought and periodic inundation.

## Morphology and Growth

- Low-growing, creeping shrub typically reaching 30-100 cm in height.
- Produces extensive underground rhizome systems enabling clonal spread.
- Narrow, silky-haired leaves adapted to reduce water loss in exposed coastal habitats.
- Catkins appear in early spring before or with the leaves.

## Conservation Status

*S. repens* is an important component of coastal dune ecosystems, which are priority habitats under the EU Habitats Directive. Its dual mycorrhizal strategy may be a key factor in its ability to establish in the challenging conditions of coastal dune systems, where nutrient availability and moisture levels fluctuate dramatically.

## Research Significance

The *Salix repens* - *Hebeloma leucosarx* - *Glomus mosseae* system studied by van der Heijden (2001) is one of the few natural systems where the functional consequences of dual mycorrhizal associations have been experimentally demonstrated on the same host genotype. This makes it a reference system for understanding the ecological significance of mycorrhizal diversity.
