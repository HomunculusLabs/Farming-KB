## Description

Nixi pae ([[cashinahua]]: "the vine drunkenness") is the Cashinahua
name for the hallucinogenic brew prepared from *Banisteriopsis*
liana stalks combined with *Psychotria* leaves. It is the
Cashinahua variant of the pan-Amazonian drink widely known as
[[ayahuasca]]. Nixi pae is distinguished by its communal use
pattern and its role in providing revelatory information for
both individual and collective decision-making.

## Classification

- **Type:** Hallucinogenic plant preparation / entheogenic brew
- **Culture of origin:** Cashinahua (southeastern Peru)
- **Botanical ingredients:** *Banisteriopsis* spp. + *Psychotria*
  (*nai kawa*)
- **Synonyms:** Ayahuasca (Quechua/Peruvian), yagé (Colombia),
  caapi (Brazil), natemä (Jivaro)

## Key Facts

### Botanical Composition

The brew combines two plants:
1. **Banisteriopsis** stalks — one to two meters of liana,
   cut into 6–8 inch segments and lightly pounded
2. **Psychotria** (*nai kawa*) — three to five branches;
   leaves and buds stripped and added to the pot

This combination is notable for its clear dual-plant
formulation, with the Cashinahua explicitly recognizing
Banisteriopsis as the base and Psychotria as an essential
additive that intensifies and prolongs the experience.

### Active Constituents

- **From Banisteriopsis:** Harmine and harmaline
  (β-carboline [[mao-inhibitors-and-psychedelics]])
- **From Psychotria:** Dimethyltryptamine (DMT)
  (fast-acting [[mckenna-tryptamine-hallucinogen-classification-four-groups-plants]])

This combination represents the classic ayahuasca
pharmacological synergy: the MAO inhibitors in the vine
render orally inactive DMT bioavailable.

### Preparation Method

The host selects and cuts plant material in the jungle without
ritual or ceremony. Back at the house:
1. Vine segments pounded lightly with a rock
2. Placed in a clay cooking pot (2–4 gallon capacity)
3. Psychotria leaves and buds stripped and added
4. Pot filled with water
5. Fire lit around the base; water brought near boiling
6. Brew steeped for approximately one hour
7. Ladled into smaller pots to cool

### Dosage and Administration

Each participant dips out approximately one pint of liquid.
Before drinking, each man sings or chants over the brew,
asking it to "show him many things." After 15 minutes, a
second pint may be consumed for enhanced effects ("to have a
good trip" — *nixi paewen en bai wai pe*).

### Communal Setting

Nixi pae sessions are intensely communal:
- Multiple men participate simultaneously
- Physical contact is maintained throughout (men line up
  on logs, wrapping arms and legs around each other)
- Nixi pae is NEVER taken alone
- The group provides contact with "the real world" as
  protection against the terrors of the [[yakuruna-aquatic-spirit-world]]
- Only experienced ("strong") men may sit without
  physical contact

### Timing

- Sessions occur no more frequently than every other week
- Always after dark, beginning around 8 PM
- Lasting until 2–3 AM
- Timing determined by group consensus on need/advisability

### Experiential Character

Despite its central cultural role, the Cashinahua find nixi
pae deeply unpleasant. The most persistent informant comment
is: "It is a fearsome thing, I was very much afraid." Few
ever describe it as pleasant. They endure the experience
specifically to obtain urgently desired revelations from
the spirit world—hallucinations are viewed as experiences
of the *nama yuxin* (dream spirit).

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Nixi pae provides one of the clearest ethnographic examples
of the Banisteriopsis + Psychotria combination with explicit
indigenous recognition of each plant's role. The Cashinahua
understand that without Psychotria, hallucinations are "less
vivid and of shorter duration"—a precise folk-pharmacological
observation matching modern understanding of DMT's contribution.
The preparation method (decoction rather than cold water
extraction) and [[blesching-cannabis-health-index-dosage-guidelines-medical]] (1–2 pints) offer practical
data for understanding traditional [[ayahuasca-pharmacology]].
