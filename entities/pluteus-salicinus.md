# Pluteus salicinus

**Pluteus salicinus** (Pers.: Fr.) P. Kumm. is a small, wood-inhabiting
mushroom in the family Pluteaceae confirmed to contain [[baeocystin]]. It is one of the least studied psychoactive agarics, known
primarily from Europe and [[psilocybian-fungi-law-australia-nz]]. The Pluteaceae
are closely related to the Amanitaceae, but unlike the mycorrhizal
[[bloomfield-mushroom-formation-fruiting-body-development-cultivation]] is white, though these still display characteristic
blue staining at the stem base.

### Gills (Lamellae)

The gills are free from the stem, a diagnostic feature of the Pluteaceae.
Young gills are white, turning pink to salmon-pink as spores mature. This
pink spore print is a key distinguishing characteristic that separates
*Pluteus* from all other psilocybin-containing genera, which have dark
spore prints ranging from purplish-brown to black.

### Stem (Stipe)

The stipe measures 3 to 6 cm in length and 2 to 5 mm in thickness. It is
cylindrical, white to pale gray, and may show blue-green staining at the
base. No annulus or volva is present. The stem is relatively fragile
compared to more robust psychoactive species.

### Microscopic Features

- **Spore print:** Pink to salmon-colored
- **Spore dimensions:** 6 to 8 by 5 to 6 micrometers, ellipsoid to
  broadly ellipsoid, smooth and thin-walled
- **Cheilocystidia:** Numerous, ventricose to cylindrical with a narrow
  neck, sometimes capitate — a key diagnostic feature for the genus
- **Pleurocystidia:** Similar to cheilocystidia but less numerous
- **Pileipellis:** Cutis of cylindrical hyphae with embedded pigment

## Distribution and Habitat

*Pluteus salicinus* has a broad but scattered distribution across the
Northern Hemisphere, documented from North America, Europe, Asia, and
Africa. In the Guzman, Allen, and Gartz [[fungal-succession-wood-substrates]] make it easy to overlook in
the field.
