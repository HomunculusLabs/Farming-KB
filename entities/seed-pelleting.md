## Description

Seed pelleting is a technique for embedding seeds in a protective capsule of soil amendments, nutrients, and protective agents to improve establishment success in difficult or degraded soils. The process overcomes local soil deficiencies at the microsite level, giving each seed a tailored microenvironment for germination and early growth. It is particularly valuable for pioneering soil rehabilitation and stabilisation projects.

## Classification

- **Category**: Permaculture technique / Seed treatment
- **Type**: Physical seed encapsulation
- **Purpose**: Improve seedling establishment in deficient or hostile soils
- **Scale**: Garden to broadacre
- **Equipment**: Meat mincer (blades removed), shaker table, drying area

## Key Facts

1. **Three-stage process**: Seed pelleting consists of three sequential stages — seed pretreatment, inoculation, and pelleting — each addressing a specific barrier to establishment.
2. **Seed pretreatment**: Thick-coated seeds or those requiring heat, cold, or scarification to break dormancy must be treated before pelleting. This ensures the protective coating does not prevent necessary dormancy-breaking cues.
3. **Inoculation**: Legume seeds are inoculated with appropriate microbial or fungal spores (rhizobia, mycorrhizae) by soaking in inoculant solution and drying before pelleting.
4. **Pellet composition**: The core pellet is made from lime, clay, and trace rock flour (calcium or phosphate) mixed into a damp but plastic slurry around the seed.
5. **Extrusion method**: The slurry-encased seeds are extruded through a meat mincer with the cutting blades removed, producing uniform pellets.
6. **Shaker table**: Extruded pellets are rolled on a slightly inclined tray covered with dust, which both dries and shapes the pellets to a desirable size.
7. **Outer coat ingredients**: The dust coating should incorporate:
   - Soil conditioning gel or polymer (for water retention)
   - Colloid-forming substance such as fine graphite
   - Bird repellent — green dye helps repel birds
   - Insect repellent — powdered neem tree leaf ([[melia-azedarach-entity|melia-azedarach]]) or diatomaceous earth
   - Swelling clay such as bentonite (for moisture management)
8. **Deployment**: Dried pellets are scattered, drilled, or sown on sites to await rain. The protected seed germinates when the pellet absorbs water, with the emerging root encountering a favourable microenvironment.

## Process Steps

1. **Pretreat seeds** — scarify, stratify, or heat-treat as needed for the species
2. **Inoculate** — soak legume seeds in rhizobial/mycorrhizal inoculant solution, then dry
3. **Mix primary coat** — combine lime, clay, rock flour, and phosphate with seed in damp slurry
4. **Extrude** — pass through meat mincer without blades to form pellets
5. **Shape on shaker table** — roll on inclined dust-covered tray, adding outer coating ingredients
6. **Dry** — air dry completely before storage or sowing
7. **Deploy** — scatter or drill onto rehabilitation site
