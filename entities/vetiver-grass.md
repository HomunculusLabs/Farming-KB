## Vetiver Grass (Chrysopogon zizanioides)

## Description

Vetiver grass is a perennial bunch grass widely recommended by Bill Mollison for erosion control and bank stabilization in permaculture earthworks. It is used in cross-swale plantings to trap silt and spread water across eroded landscapes. Vetiver forms dense, deeply rooted hedges that slow water flow, trap sediment, and stabilize soil on slopes ranging from moderate to very steep.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Family:** Poaceae (grass family)
- **Genus:** Chrysopogon
- **Species:** C. zizanioides
- **Common Names:** Vetiver, khus, vetiver grass
- **Growth Form:** Perennial bunch grass

## Key Facts

- Roots can penetrate 3-5 meters deep in the first year, providing exceptional slope stability
- Forms dense hedges when planted in close rows, creating effective barriers to water flow
- Traps sediment behind its clumps, gradually building up natural terraces
- Tolerant of extreme conditions: drought, flood, fire, heavy metal contamination
- Non-invasive in most cultivated varieties (does not produce viable seed)
- Used in cross-swale plantings to spread water and trap silt on eroded sites

## Role in Permaculture Earthworks

Mollison specifically recommends vetiver for:
1. **Erosion face stabilization:** Planted on steep slopes alongside pampas grass, bamboo, and lemongrass
2. **Cross-swale plantings:** Small wire netting fences combined with vetiver swales trap silt and spread water
3. **Bank stabilization:** Used on dam walls and embankments where trees are unsuitable
4. **Revegetation of degraded sites:** Effective on overgrazed, eroded, mined, or bulldozed land
5. **Gully erosion control:** Halting and reversing gully formation on hillsides

## Propagation and Establishment

- Propagated vegetatively by splitting clumps (tillering)
- Planted in dense rows (10-15 cm apart) along contour lines
- Establishes quickly in warm climates
- Can be cut back periodically; regrows vigorously

## Additional Uses

- **Essential oil:** Roots produce vetiver oil, used in perfumery
- **Mulch and thatch:** Leaves provide durable thatching material
- **Water purification:** Roots can absorb heavy metals and pollutants
- **Animal fodder:** Young growth is palatable to livestock

## Relevance to Cultivation

Vetiver grass is one of the most powerful tools available for permaculture designers working on eroded or degraded landscapes. Its combination of deep roots, sediment trapping, and non-invasive growth habit makes it ideal for the transition zone between earthworks and natural landscape. In the context of Mollison's earthworks philosophy, vetiver serves as the living component that makes earthworks permanent and self-maintaining.
