## Overview

Nephroma laevigatum, commonly known as the smooth kidney lichen, is a
large foliose cyanolichen in the family Nephromataceae. It forms circular
to irregular thalli loosely attached to mossy bark, rocks, and soil in
humid forests.

The species is closely related to N. arcticum and N. expansible but
differs in its smooth, glossy upper surface and preference for more
oceanic climates. It is widely distributed across [[comparison-biological-nitrogen-fixation-vs-synthetic-fertilizer]]. It is
considered an indicator of long-term forest continuity and clean air.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Nephroma laevigatum was described by Erik Acharius in 1810. It belongs to
the order Peltigerales, family Nephromataceae, genus Nephroma.

The thallus is large, 5–15 cm in diameter, forming a loosely attached,
circular to irregularly shaped rosette. The upper surface is smooth and
glossy, brown to dark brown when dry, becoming darker olive-green when
wet. It lacks soredia and isidia.

The lower surface is pale tan with a net-like vein pattern and sparse,
simple rhizines concentrated near the lobe center. The margin is often
upturned and slightly lobed, exposing the pale undersurface.

Apothecia are common on the lower surface, borne on short stalks or
directly on the medulla. They are kidney-shaped (reniform) with a brown
to red-brown disc, 2–5 mm in diameter. Ascospores are ellipsoid,
hyaline, 1-septate, measuring 15–25 × 5–8 µm.
