# Albedo

## Description

Albedo is the **ratio of light reflected by a surface to the total light received** by that surface. It is a fundamental concept in climatology, energy design, and [[permaculture]] that describes how different materials and surfaces interact with incoming solar radiation. Albedo values range from near 0 (complete absorption, a "black body") to near 1.0 (near-complete reflection, such as a mirror). Understanding albedo is critical for designing buildings, gardens, and landscapes that effectively manage solar energy.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Physics — Radiative Transfer and Climatology
- **Category:** Physical property / Design parameter
- **Units:** Dimensionless ratio (0.0 to 1.0)
- **Measurement context:** Visible and near-visible wavelengths of electromagnetic radiation

## Key Facts

- Different natural surfaces have dramatically different albedo values, affecting local and global climate.
- **Forests** generally have low albedo (absorb more radiation), converting it to heat and growth energy.
- **Snow and ice** have high albedo, reflecting most incoming radiation — a key factor in polar climate stability.
- **Water surfaces** have variable albedo depending on the angle of incoming radiation; low sun angles reflect more, high angles absorb more.
- Albedo is directly affected by human activities: **deforestation** increases albedo (less absorption), while **industrial pollution** can decrease it (dark particles on ice).
- Mollison emphasizes that albedo changes from deforestation and agriculture are among the **human activities that locally and globally affect climate**.

## Radiation Interactions

Incoming solar radiation encountering a surface undergoes one of four fates:

1. **Reflected** — turned away as light (high albedo surfaces, white walls, mirrors)
2. **Refracted** — bent or curved (light through water, waves around headlands)
3. **Absorbed** — converted from short-wave (light) to long-wave (heat) radiation (dark surfaces, black bodies)
4. **Transmitted** — passed through the material (glass, some plastics)

All absorbed light is re-emitted as heat. This principle governs microclimate design in permaculture.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

Albedo is a powerful design tool in permaculture:

- **Microclimate modification**: Placing reflective surfaces (light walls, white stones) near plants can increase available light for photosynthesis in shaded areas.
- **Heat management**: Dark surfaces (charcoal, dark rock, evergreen trees) absorb and re-radiate heat, creating warm microclimates for frost-sensitive plants.
- **Building design**: Understanding albedo informs wall color, roof material, and paving choices for energy-efficient housing.
- **Forest design**: Forest edges with mixed albedo surfaces create diverse microclimates.
- **Water bodies**: Ponds and water surfaces serve as both absorbers and reflectors depending on angle, contributing to thermal mass in landscape design.

Mollison stresses that albedo, along with forest cover, industrial pollutants, and agricultural practices, is one of the primary ways that **local actions affect global and local climate**. Permaculture designers manipulate albedo consciously to direct energy flows on site.
