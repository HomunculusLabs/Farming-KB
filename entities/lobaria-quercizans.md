## Overview

Lobaria quercizans, commonly known as the oak lungwort, is a large foliose
cyanolichen in the family Lobariaceae. It forms broad, loosely attached
thalli on the bark of mature hardwood trees in humid, old-growth forests.

The species is named for its association with oak (Quercus) trees, though
it also grows on maple, beech, and other deciduous species. It is one of
the largest foliose lichens in temperate North America and Europe.

Like [[lobaria-pulmonaria]], L. quercizans harbors both green algal and
cyanobacterial photobionts, making it a tripartite lichen with significant
nitrogen-fixing capability. It is considered an indicator of forest
continuity and ecological health.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Lobaria quercizans was described by [[chamovitz-julius-von-sachs-blue-light-phototropism]] Flotow in 1848. It belongs
to the order Peltigerales, family Lobariaceae, genus Lobaria.

The thallus is large, reaching 15–30 cm in diameter, with broad, rounded
lobes 2–5 cm wide. The upper surface is pale gray to brownish-gray with a
distinctly ridged or reticulate texture, sometimes appearing puckered or
wrinkled.

The lower surface is pale tan to brown with a simple tomentum and sparse,
short rhizines. The margin is usually entire to slightly crenulate, lacking
soredia. The lobe tips often curl upward, revealing the pale lower surface.

Apothecia are common, located along lobe margins or on the upper surface.
They are lecanorine with a brown disc and a prominent thalline margin.
Ascospores are ellipsoid to fusiform, hyaline, 3-septate, measuring
30–45 × 4–6 µm.

Cephalodia are present as discrete, dark green to blackish warts on the
upper surface, containing the Nostoc cyanobacterial photobiont.

## Distribution and Habitat

Lobaria quercizans is found in temperate deciduous forests of eastern
North America, from southern Ontario and Quebec south to the Appalachian
Mountains and west to the Ozarks. In Europe, it occurs in scattered
oceanic locations.

The species is restricted to old-growth or late-successional forests with
high humidity and minimal [[air-pollution-fungal-community-responses]]. It grows on the bark of mature
oaks, maples, beeches, and basswood, typically on trunks and large
branches at 1–5 m height.

It is most abundant in cove forests, northern hardwood forests, and
montane oak-hickory forests. Elevational range is typically 300–1500 m.
The species has declined significantly in areas with acid rain and
industrial pollution.

## Ecology and Symbiosis

Lobaria quercizans is a tripartite lichen with two photobionts: a green
alga (Dictyochloropsis-type) in the primary photobiont layer, and Nostoc
cyanobacteria in external cephalodia.

The cyanobacterial symbiont fixes atmospheric nitrogen, contributing to
the nitrogen economy of [[dighton-fungi-acidifying-pollutants-forest-ecosystems]]. In old-growth stands, Lobaria
species can contribute substantially to throughfall nitrogen inputs.

The species is highly sensitive to air pollution, particularly sulfur
dioxide and acid deposition. Its decline in [[polypore-fungi-eastern-north-america]] America since
the mid-20th century mirrors the decline of L. pulmonaria in Europe.

L. quercizans provides habitat for microarthropods and serves as a
substrate for [[lichenicolous-fungi]]. Its large, loosely attached thalli
create humid microenvironments that support diverse [[cannabis-rhizosphere-microbial-communities]].
