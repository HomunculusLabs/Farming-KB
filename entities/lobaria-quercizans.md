## Taxonomy and Morphology

Lobaria quercizans was described by [[air-pollution-fungal-community-responses]]. It grows on the bark of mature
oaks, maples, beeches, and basswood, typically on trunks and large
branches at 1–5 m height.

It is most abundant in cove forests, northern hardwood forests, and
montane oak-hickory forests. Elevational range is typically 300–1500 m.
The species has declined significantly in areas with acid rain and
industrial pollution.

## Ecology and Symbiosis

Lobaria quercizans is a tripartite lichen with two photobionts: a green
alga (Dictyochloropsis-type) in the primary photobiont layer, and Nostoc
cyanobacteria in external cephalodia.

The cyanobacterial symbiont fixes atmospheric nitrogen, contributing to
the nitrogen economy of [[polypore-fungi-eastern-north-america]] America since
the mid-20th century mirrors the decline of L. pulmonaria in Europe.

L. quercizans provides habitat for microarthropods and serves as a
substrate for [[cannabis-rhizosphere-microbial-communities]].
