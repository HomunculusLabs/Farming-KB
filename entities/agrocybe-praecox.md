# Agrocybe praecox

**Agrocybe praecox** (Pers.) Fayod, commonly known as the **early parasol** or **spring agrocybe**, is a saprotrophic basidiomycete fungus in the family Strophariaceae.
It fruits in spring and early summer on wood chips, mulch, and disturbed ground in [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]].
The species is edible when thoroughly cooked but easily confused with several toxic lookalikes, making it a mushroom of caution for foragers.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Strophariaceae
- **Genus:** Agrocybe
- **Species:** A. praecox (Pers.) Fayod (1889)

**Basionym:** Agaricus praecox Pers. (1793)

**Synonyms:**
- Pholiota praecox (Pers.) P. Kumm. (1871)
- Naucoria praecox (Pers.) Quél. (1872)

Agrocybe praecox is the type species of the genus Agrocybe and has served as the reference for morphological delimitation within the group.
Molecular phylogenetics has confirmed its placement in Strophariaceae and revealed a species complex with several cryptic taxa across Europe and [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]].

## Morphology

**Cap:** 3–8 cm diameter, convex to plano-convex, sometimes with a low umbo.
Surface is hygrophanous, smooth to slightly wrinkled when dry, pale tan to ochraceous-brown, fading to buff with age.
The cap margin is often appendiculate with [[partial-veil-tropism-protection-before-spore-release]] remnants in young specimens.

**Gills:** Adnexed to adnate, initially pale grayish-white, darkening to cinnamon-brown with spore maturity.
Gill edges are floccose in youth, becoming smoother with age.

**Stipe:** 4–10 cm tall, 4–10 mm thick, cylindrical, slightly tapered at the base.
Surface is white to pale brown, silky-fibrillose above the annulus zone.
A [[membranous-annulus-loss-brassinosteroid-induced-morphological-change]] is present but evanescent, often disappearing in mature specimens.
The stipe base may show white mycelial strands.

**Flesh:** Thin, white to pale buff, with a mild to slightly farinaceous odor and taste.

**Spore Print:** Dark cinnamon to tobacco-brown.

**Spores:** 9–13 × 6–8 µm, elliptical to broadly ellipsoid, smooth, thick-walled, with a distinct germ pore.
Cheilocystidia are ventricose with a narrow neck, often numerous and conspicuous.

## Distribution and Habitat

Agrocybe praecox has a broad distribution across temperate zones of the Northern Hemisphere.
It is commonly found throughout Europe, North America, and parts of Asia.
The species fruits from April through June, with peak fruiting in May in most temperate locales.

It is strongly associated with disturbed habitats and anthropogenic environments:
- Wood chip mulch in gardens, parks, and landscaped areas
- Sawdust piles and wood waste
- Rich garden soil amended with [[fukuoka-textdoc-composting-critique-futility-prepared-organic-matter]]
- Occasionally on decaying hardwood logs and stumps

This preference for mulched and cultivated ground makes it one of the most frequently encountered agarics in urban and suburban settings during spring months.

## Bioactive Compounds and Pharmacology

Agrocybe species have attracted research interest for their bioactive metabolites, though most studies have used related species (A. cylindracea, A. aegerita) rather than A. praecox specifically.

**Polysaccharides:**
- β-glucans with immunomodulatory properties isolated from [[bloomfield-asterophora-and-mycoparasites-of-fruiting-bodies]] and mycelial culture
- Water-soluble heteroglycans demonstrating antitumor activity in murine Sarcoma 180 models
- (1→3)-β-D-glucan and (1→6)-β-D-glucan linkages confirmed through structural characterization
