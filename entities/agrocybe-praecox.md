## Agrocybe praecox

**Agrocybe praecox** (Pers.) Fayod, commonly known as the **early parasol** or **spring agrocybe**, is a saprotrophic basidiomycete fungus in the family Strophariaceae.
It fruits in spring and early summer on wood chips, mulch, and disturbed ground in [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]].

## Morphology

**Cap:** 3–8 cm diameter, convex to plano-convex, sometimes with a low umbo.
Surface is hygrophanous, smooth to slightly wrinkled when dry, pale tan to ochraceous-brown, fading to buff with age.
The cap margin is often appendiculate with [[membranous-annulus-loss-brassinosteroid-induced-morphological-change]] is present but evanescent, often disappearing in mature specimens.
The stipe base may show white mycelial strands.

**Flesh:** Thin, white to pale buff, with a mild to slightly farinaceous odor and taste.

**Spore Print:** Dark cinnamon to tobacco-brown.

**Spores:** 9–13 × 6–8 µm, elliptical to broadly ellipsoid, smooth, thick-walled, with a distinct germ pore.
Cheilocystidia are ventricose with a narrow neck, often numerous and conspicuous.

## Distribution and Habitat

Agrocybe praecox has a broad distribution across temperate zones of the Northern Hemisphere.
It is commonly found throughout Europe, North America, and parts of Asia.
The species fruits from April through June, with peak fruiting in May in most temperate locales.

It is strongly associated with disturbed habitats and anthropogenic environments:
- Wood chip mulch in gardens, parks, and landscaped areas
- Sawdust piles and wood waste
- Rich garden soil amended with [[bloomfield-asterophora-and-mycoparasites-of-fruiting-bodies]] and mycelial culture
- Water-soluble heteroglycans demonstrating antitumor activity in murine Sarcoma 180 models
- (1→3)-β-D-glucan and (1→6)-β-D-glucan linkages confirmed through structural characterization
