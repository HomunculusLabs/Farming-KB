## Eudarluca caricis

**Type:** Species (Ascomycete)
**Category:** Rust Mycoparasite / Hyperparasite
**Also known as:** Anamorph: Sphaerellopsis filum (Biv.-Bern. ex Fr.) B. Sutton

## Overview
Eudarluca caricis is a cosmopolitan ascomycete and the most
widely distributed and commonly encountered mycoparasite of rust
fungi (Uredinales). The species occurs on rusts worldwide and has
been reported on over 300 rust species, making it the most
polyphagous rust hyperparasite known. Its teleomorph (Eudarluca)
produces pycnidia, while the anamorph (Sphaerellopsis filum)
produces conidia for asexual dispersal.

E. caricis parasitizes all stages of the rust [[permaculture-designers-manual-climatic-zones-tropical]] reflects the [[fukuoka-textdoc-biological-control-fallacy-pheromone-critique]] agent against cereal rusts, which are among
the most devastating diseases of wheat, barley, and other grain
crops worldwide. By reducing urediniospore production, E. caricis
can slow the epidemic development of rust diseases and reduce
yield losses in susceptible cereal crops.

E. caricis is one of relatively few mycoparasites for which both
the teleomorph (Eudarluca) and anamorph (Sphaerellopsis filum)
are well characterized. The dual naming reflects the historical
separation of sexual and asexual morphs in [[savory-growing-season-and-non-growing-season-management]].

## Key Details

### Host Range
E. caricis has been reported on over 300 rust species spanning
multiple genera including Puccinia, Uromyces, Phragmidium, and
Melampsora. This extraordinarily broad host range makes it the
most polyphagous rust mycoparasite known. The fungus does not
appear to be strongly host-specific at the rust species level,
though infection efficiency may vary among different rust species
and their developmental stages.

### Parasitic Mechanism
E. caricis infects rust sori (spore-producing structures) by
penetrating urediniospores and teliospores with germ tubes that
enter through the spore wall. Within the infected spores, the
fungus develops pycnidia (in the teleomorph stage) or conidiomata
(in the anamorph stage), producing spores for dispersal to new
rust infections. Infected urediniospores are often discolored and
fail to germinate, reducing the rust's epidemic potential.

### Effect on Rust Epidemics
In field conditions, E. caricis can reduce urediniospore
production by up to 50% in infected rust populations (Kranz
1981). This level of reduction can significantly slow epidemic
development, particularly when infection occurs early in the
epidemic cycle. However, the impact of E. caricis on overall
disease development is often modest because the fungus typically
arrives after the rust epidemic is well established.
