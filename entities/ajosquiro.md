## Ajosquiro (*Gallizia corazema*)

## Description

Ajosquiro is the common Amazonian name for *Gallizia corazema*, a tree species recognized in Amazonian vegetalismo as one of the four primary kapukiri-producing trees. The kapukiri produced by ajosquiro causes a distinctive set of symptoms characterized by extreme nervousness, fear, and the sensation of being bitten by worms. The tree is also mentioned as a source of knowledge for huaira-kuros sorcery (worms of the air), which sorcerers use to enter victims through the nose and ears.

## Classification

- **Scientific name:** *Gallizia corazema*
- **Common names:** Ajosquiro, ajo-quiro
- **Growth form:** Tropical tree
- **Habitat:** Amazon basin forests

## Relation to Sorcery

The same tree is linked to huaira-kuros, the worms of the air.

This association gives ajosquiro a double role: it is part of environmental pathology and part of aggressive magical practice.

A sorcerer may use knowledge from the tree to send intrusive entities through a victim's nose and ears.

For a healer, identifying the tree behind the attack helps determine the proper song and countermeasure.

## Comparison with Other Kapukiri Trees

- Puka-lupuna emphasizes swelling, smarting sensations, and pregnancy danger.
- [[catahua-negra]] emphasizes dryness, contraction, fever, and stuttering.
- Huairacaspi is the fastest and most lethal, with grey skin and heavy vomiting.
- Ajosquiro emphasizes fear, nervousness, and worm-like biting sensations.

This symptom profile makes ajosquiro psychologically distinctive among the kapukiri trees.
