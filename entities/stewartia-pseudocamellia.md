## Overview

Stewartia pseudocamellia (Japanese stewartia) is a deciduous ornamental tree in the family Theaceae, native to Japan and Korea. It is prized for its exfoliating bark, camellia-like white flowers, and reliable red-orange autumn color.

The species epithet "pseudocamellia" references its flowers' resemblance to those of Camellia, a close relative within the same family. Mature trees reach 9–15 m in height with a pyramidal to oval crown.

It is considered one of the finest four-season [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]].

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Family:** Theaceae
- **Genus:** Stewartia
- **Species:** S. pseudocamellia (Maxim.) Maxim.
- **Synonyms:** Stuartia pseudocamellia

Bark is smooth and exfoliating in irregular patches, revealing layers of gray, orange, tan, and brown beneath. This bark character provides exceptional winter interest and is often described as the tree's most striking feature.

Leaves are elliptic to ovate, 4–9 cm long, dark green and glossy above, with crenulate margins and pubescent midribs beneath. They turn vivid shades of orange, red, and burgundy in autumn.

Flowers are solitary, 5–7 cm across, with five white petals surrounding a cluster of golden-yellow stamens. Each bloom lasts approximately one week, and the tree flowers progressively over 2–4 weeks in mid-summer (July–August).

The fruit is a woody, five-valved capsule 1.5–2 cm long that persists through winter. Seeds are winged and dispersed by wind from autumn into early winter.

Chromosome number is 2n = 30, consistent across studied Stewartia species. This karyotype is shared with other members of Theaceae, supporting the monophyly of the tribe Stewartieae.

## Distribution and Habitat

Native to mountainous regions of Japan (Honshu, Shikoku, Kyushu) and South Korea. Populations occur at elevations of 300–1,500 m in moist, well-drained deciduous and mixed forests.

The species grows as an understory or sub-canopy tree in association with Acer palmatum, Fagus crenata, Quercus crispula, and Tsuga sieboldii. It prefers partial shade in youth but tolerates full sun with adequate moisture.

Soils in native habitats are acidic, humus-rich, and well-drained, typically derived from granite or volcanic substrates. Annual precipitation in native range is 1,200–2,000 mm with warm summers and cold winters.

Korean populations are more scattered and occur at slightly lower elevations. These populations may represent introgression with the closely related Stewartia koreana, though taxonomic treatment varies among authorities.

The species has been naturalized sparingly in parts of western Europe and the northeastern United States. However, it does not exhibit invasive tendencies and is not considered a ecological risk outside its native range.

## Ecological Role and Uses

In native forests, Stewartia pseudocamellia contributes to mid-canopy diversity and provides pollen for native bee species during its summer flowering period. The flowers produce moderate nectar volumes attractive to bumblebees and syrphid flies.

The wood is fine-grained, hard, and valued in Japan for small specialty items, tool handles, and turnery. However, commercial harvest is limited by the tree's relatively small size and slow growth rate.

In horticulture, it is widely planted as a specimen tree in botanical gardens and private collections worldwide. The Royal Horticultural Society has awarded it the Award of Garden Merit (AGM).

It pairs well with shade-loving ornamentals such as hostas, ferns, and rhododendrons in woodland garden settings. The tree's relatively pest-free nature adds to its landscape appeal and long-term maintenance simplicity.
