## Sectoring (Culture Sectoring)

## Description
Sectoring is the technique of selecting and isolating individual strains of mushroom [[mycelium]] from a mixed culture on an agar plate. When multiple genetically distinct strains of the same species grow on a single plate, they form visible islands of mycelium with distinct morphologies and growth rates. Sectoring allows cultivators to isolate the most vigorous strains.

## Classification
- **Category**: Cultivation technique / Strain selection
- **Domain**: Mycology, [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]]
- **Function**: Isolating individual strains from mixed spore germinations

## Key Facts

### When Sectoring Occurs
- Most commonly seen on spore streak plates where multiple spores have germinated
- Each spore produces a genetically unique individual
- Different strains develop as islands of mycelium on the agar plate
- Strains may have completely different morphology, climate preferences, and metabolic requirements

### Identifying Strain Boundaries
- Mycelium will not fuse with mycelium that is not genetically identical
- Where different strains collide, they build walls with thickening lines of division
- Colonies differentiate visibly — some surge ahead, others fall behind
- The pattern resembles a race between competing strains

### Sectoring Technique
1. Observe the mixed culture plate and identify distinct colonies/sectors
2. Look for the most aggressive, fastest-growing sectors
3. Excise a small bit of the outermost mycelium (hyphal tips)
4. Transfer to a fresh agar plate
5. The resulting culture should show more even, symmetrical growth
6. Even symmetrical growth is a textbook sign of a pure strain isolated

### Selection Criteria
- Aggressive growth rate (colonies that surge ahead)
- Healthy, cottony mycelial appearance
- Uniform morphology on the transfer plate
- Species-appropriate characteristics

### Importance for Strain Development
- Allows discovery of superior strains from wild spore germinations
- Different sectors may have different fruiting temperatures, yields, or substrate preferences
- Essential for developing new cultivars with desirable characteristics
- The foundation of strain improvement and breeding programs

## Relevance to Cultivation
Sectoring is the primary method by which cultivators develop new strains and select for desirable traits. The technique enables discovery of strains with better yields, faster colonization, broader climate tolerance, or other commercially valuable characteristics.
