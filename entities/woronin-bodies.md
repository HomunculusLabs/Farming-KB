## Description

Woronin bodies are specialized, dense-core peroxisome-derived organelles found exclusively in filamentous ascomycete fungi. They are positioned on either side of septal pores — the channels that connect adjacent hyphal compartments — and function as biological "plugs" that seal the pore when a neighboring hyphal compartment is damaged or stressed. This sealing mechanism prevents catastrophic loss of cytoplasm and allows the fungal mycelium to maintain compartmentalization, which is essential for the multicellular lifestyle of filamentous fungi.

## Classification

- **Type**: Sub-cellular organelle (peroxisome-derived)
- **Taxonomic distribution**: Exclusive to Pezizomycotina (filamentous ascomycetes)
- **Location**: Septal pores between hyphal compartments
- **Composition**: Dense proteinaceous core surrounded by a single-unit membrane

## Key Facts

- Woronin bodies are unique to filamentous ascomycete fungi and are not found in yeasts, basidiomycetes, or other organisms.
- They are derived from peroxisomes and retain peroxisomal matrix proteins, but have evolved a specialized dense core composed primarily of the HEX protein (Hex1 in [[neurospora-crassa]]).
- The organelles are approximately 0.1–0.5 μm in diameter and are physically tethered near septal pores.
- Upon hyphal damage or cellular stress, Woronin bodies rapidly move to and occlude the septal pore, preventing cytoplasmic bleeding and containing the damage.
- This pore-plugging function is essential for maintaining the integrity of the multicellular hyphal network, effectively allowing the mycelium to behave as a coordinated multicellular organism.
- Beyond emergency pore plugging, Woronin bodies may also play roles in regulating intercompartmental transport, maintaining differentiation between hyphal compartments, and supporting polarized growth.
- In "Fungi in the Environment" (Chapter 2 by Gregory Jedd), Woronin bodies are discussed in depth as structures that support the multicellular lifestyle of filamentous ascomycetes.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- Understanding Woronin body function is fundamental to comprehending how filamentous fungi maintain hyphal integrity — directly relevant to mushroom cultivation where mycelial health determines yields.
- The septal pore plugging mechanism protects mycelial networks from mechanical damage during substrate colonization, a consideration in cultivation practices involving stirring, mixing, or disturbance of growing media.
- Woronin body biology informs understanding of [[fungal-stress-responses]], which are critical when cultivated fungi encounter environmental challenges such as contamination, desiccation, or temperature stress.
- The study of Woronin bodies in model ascomycetes (e.g., Neurospora crassa, Aspergillus nidulans) provides transferable knowledge about hyphal compartmentalization that applies to commercially important cultivated fungi.
- Research into these organelles may eventually inform bioengineering approaches to improve the robustness of cultivated fungal strains.
