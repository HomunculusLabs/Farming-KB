# 5-MAPB

## Overview

**5-MAPB** (1-(1-benzofuran-5-yl)-N-methylpropan-2-amine) is a synthetic **benzofuran**
compound with entactogen and empathogen effects, structurally and pharmacologically related
to **MDMA** and **6-APB** (benzofury). It acts primarily as a **serotonin releaser** and
**reuptake inhibitor**, producing euphoria, increased sociability, emotional openness,
and mild stimulation. 5-MAPB emerged in the research chemical market around 2010 and was
subsequently controlled in several jurisdictions.

## Chemical Properties

- **Chemical name:** 1-(1-benzofuran-5-yl)-N-methylpropan-2-amine
- **Molecular formula:** C₁₂H₁₅NO
- **Molecular weight:** 189.26 g/mol
- **CAS number:** 1390110-10-6
- **Structure:** Benzofuran-substituted phenethylamine. The benzofuran ring (fused
  benzene-furan) replaces the methylenedioxyphenyl ring of MDMA, increasing
  lipophilicity and altering metabolic profile. 5-position substitution on the
  benzofuran ring is analogous to the 3,4-positions of MDMA's phenyl ring.
- **Appearance:** Freebase is pale yellow to brownish oil; HCl salt is white
  crystalline powder, freely soluble in water.

## Pharmacology

- **Serotonin release (SERT):** Potent serotonin-releasing agent via transporter
  reversal — the primary mechanism driving entactogen effects. In vitro potency
  comparable to or slightly greater than MDMA.
- **Dopamine and norepinephrine release:** Also reverses DAT and NET transporters,
  contributing stimulant and empathogenic components. DAT activity lower than SERT,
  giving a more serotonionic profile than purely stimulant compounds.
- **Receptor interactions:** Weak 5-HT₂A and 5-HT₂C agonism may contribute to mild
  visual effects and characteristic entactogenic headspace.
- **Reuptake inhibition:** Contributes to sustained effect, though releasing activity
  predominates.

| Property | MDMA | 5-MAPB |
|----------|------|--------|
| Primary mechanism | 5-HT release | 5-HT release |
| Reuptake inhibition | Moderate | Moderate |
| 5-HT₂A activity | Minimal | Weak agonist |
| Lipophilicity | Lower | Higher |
| Duration | 3–5 hours | 5–8 hours |
| Neurotoxicity concern | Established | Unknown |

## Dosage

- **Threshold:** 50–70 mg (oral)
- **Light:** 70–100 mg (oral)
- **Common:** 100–130 mg (oral)
- **Strong:** 130–160 mg (oral) — not recommended

Almost exclusively administered orally as HCl salt in capsules. Insufflation is painful
due to the benzofuran ring. Onset 30–60 minutes; duration 5–8 hours with gradual
comedown. Redosing at 3–4 hours extends effects but increases serotonergic strain.

## Effects

**Positive:** Euphoria, emotional openness, increased empathy and sociability, enhanced
tactile and auditory perception, mild visual distortions (color enhancement, pattern
recognition), sense of inner peace, musical appreciation.

**Neutral:** Pupil dilation, increased heart rate, jaw clenching (bruxism), reduced
appetite, sweating, restless legs, difficulty sleeping.

**Negative:** Anxiety during onset, nausea (more common than MDMA), cognitive fog during
comedown, emotional flattening the following day, headaches, difficulty concentrating.

## Toxicity and Harm Reduction

- **Hyponatremia:** As with all serotonergic releasers, excessive water consumption
  without electrolytes can cause dangerous sodium dilution. Sip water moderately.
- **Serotonin syndrome risk:** Dangerous with MAOIs, SSRIs, or other serotonergic drugs.
  Minimum 2-week washout after SSRI discontinuation.
- **Cardiovascular strain:** Tachycardia and hypertension at common doses; avoid with
  cardiac conditions.
- **Unknown long-term toxicity:** No controlled human studies. Animal data for related
  benzofurans suggest potential serotonergic neurotoxicity at high/repeated doses.
  Limit frequency to no more than once per month.
- **Purity concerns:** As an unregulated substance, samples may be adulterated.
  Reagent testing recommended.

## Legal Status

- **UK:** Class B (since 2014, following temporary class drug order in 2013).
- **EU:** Controlled under national legislation; scheduled in Germany (BtMG Anlage II),
  Austria, and several member states.
- **USA:** Not specifically scheduled at federal level, but prosecutable under the
  Federal Analog Act as an MDMA analogue.
- **China:** Controlled narcotic (2015).
- **Japan:** Controlled under Pharmaceutical Affairs Law.
- **Australia:** Border-controlled drug.

## History

5-MAPB first appeared on the online research chemical market circa 2010–2012, sold
alongside 6-APB ("benzofury") as a legal MDMA alternative. It gained popularity in
the European festival and club scene before the UK ACMD identified it in their 2013
report on benzofuran derivatives, recommending Class B control. Academic interest has
focused on its pharmacological profile as a probe for studying serotonin transporter
function and potential as a tool compound in neuropharmacology.

## Related Compounds

- **6-APB** — positional isomer (6-position benzofuran); commonly sold as "benzofury."
- **6-MAPB** — N-methylated 6-APB analogue; reportedly longer duration.
- **5-APB** — non-methylated precursor; slightly less potent.
- **MDMA** — prototypical entactogen from which benzofurans derive.
- **MBDB** — non-neurotoxic MDMA analogue with similar structural motif.
