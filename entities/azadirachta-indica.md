# Azadirachta indica

**Type:** Species
**Category:** Evergreen to deciduous tree
**Also known as:** Neem, nimtree, Indian lilac, margosa

## Overview
Azadirachta indica (neem) is a deep-rooted, broad-leafed tree native to
the dry forests of India through to Burma. It is potentially among the
most valuable of all arid-zone trees, providing insecticidal compounds,
medicinal products, timber, firewood, and soil improvement across a
remarkable range of harsh conditions.

## Key Details

### Botany
- **Family:** Meliaceae
- **Form:** Usually evergreen; may lose leaves during drought
- **Root system:** Deep taproot for accessing subterranean water
- **Growth:** Moderately fast; coppices readily

### Growth Conditions
- **Rainfall:** Optimal 450-1,150 mm; tolerates as low as 130 mm
- **Heat tolerance:** Withstands extreme heat and long dry seasons
- **Frost:** Not frost-hardy; killed by freezing temperatures
- **Soils:** Grows on most soils including dry, stony, clay, and
  shallow soils; not seasonally waterlogged or deep dry sands below
  18 m water table
- **pH:** Optimum 6.2; grows well at pH 5 and brings soil toward
  neutral through leaf litter
- **Salinity:** Not tolerant of saline soils

### Key Compounds
- Seeds and leaves yield azadirachtin (azadarachtin), a potent
  naturally occurring insecticide
- Used for pest control in agriculture and stored grain protection
- Oil from seed used medicinally for skin diseases

### Timber Properties
- Similar to Cuban mahogany in appearance and quality
- Resists decay and insects; tougher than teak
- Termite attack is rare
- Excellent for construction and furniture making
- Valuable firewood with calorific value approaching that of coal

### Traditional Uses
- **Leaves:** Dried and placed in books, grain, and clothes to repel
  insects
- **Twigs:** Used as toothbrushes (traditional oral hygiene)
- **Bark:** Decoction for fevers, nausea, liver complaints, and
  jaundice; poultice for wounds and skin diseases
- **Bark oil:** Rub for rheumatism
- **Fruit:** Used for urinary complaints, piles, and worms; considered
  purgative and emollient
- **Seed oil:** Applied topically as a cure for skin diseases

### Cultural Significance
- One of five sacred trees planted in Indian sacred groves
- Companion planting with mango and Ficus religiosa
- Propagation by seed; seed is very short-lived (2-3 weeks)

## Relevance
Neem is arguably the single most important tree for sustainable
agriculture in arid and semi-arid tropics. Its insecticidal properties
provide a natural alternative to synthetic pesticides, while its timber,
firewood, and medicinal uses make it economically valuable. In
permaculture design, neem serves as a multipurpose arid-zone pioneer
with applications ranging from integrated pest management to soil
rehabilitation.
