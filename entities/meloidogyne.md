## Meloidogyne

## Description

*Meloidogyne* (root-knot nematodes) is a genus of sedentary endoparasitic nematodes that are among the most economically damaging plant pathogens worldwide. They infect thousands of plant species, causing characteristic root galls (knots) that disrupt water and nutrient transport. Viaene and Abawi (1998) demonstrated that [[nematophagous-fungi]] of the genera *Arthrobotrys* and *Monacrosporium* attack juvenile *Meloidogyne* nematodes, while parasitic fungi of the genera *Fusarium*, *Verticillium*, and *Alternaria* infect egg masses — indicating a diverse community of fungal natural enemies that could be harnessed for biocontrol.

## Key Facts

- **Global significance**: Root-knot nematodes are the single most important group of plant-parasitic nematodes, causing crop losses estimated at $100 billion annually worldwide. The four major species (*M. incognita*, *M. javanica*, *M. arenaria*, *M. hapla*) collectively parasitize virtually every crop species.
- **Sedentary endoparasitism**: Second-stage juveniles (J2) penetrate root tips and migrate to the vascular cylinder, where they induce the formation of giant cells (feeding sites) through injection of effector proteins. The nematode becomes sedentary, swells, and induces the surrounding root tissue to form a gall.
- **Reproduction**: Most economically important species reproduce by mitotic parthenogenesis, meaning a single juvenile can found a population — making management extremely challenging.
- **Fungal parasites**: Viaene and Abawi (1998) found that egg masses of *M. hapla* in New York organic soils showed 13–30% infection by parasitic fungi, while 5–24% of J2 juveniles were attacked by nematophagous fungi. This demonstrates significant natural biological control potential.
- **Egg mass parasitism**: *Fusarium*, *Verticillium*, and *Alternaria* species were identified as egg mass parasites, while *Arthrobotrys* and *Monacrosporium* trapped and killed mobile juveniles.

## Ecological Role

- Alters root architecture and function, reducing host plant water and nutrient uptake, stunting growth, and increasing susceptibility to other pathogens.
- Root galls disrupt the rhizosphere microbial community, potentially shifting the balance between beneficial and pathogenic soil organisms.
- Fungal parasites of *Meloidogyne* represent an important component of [[soil-food-web]] and contribute to natural nematode population regulation.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Soil solarization**: Combining solarization with organic amendments can enhance biological control of *Meloidogyne* by promoting antagonistic fungi.
- **Fungal biocontrol agents**: *Paecilomyces lilacinus*, *Pochonia chlamydosporia* (formerly *Verticillium chlamydosporium*), and *Arthrobotrys* spp. are commercially available biocontrol agents for root-knot nematode management.
- **Resistant cultivars**: Tomato cultivars carrying the *Mi* gene provide resistance to *M. incognita*, *M. javanica*, and *M. arenaria*, but resistance breaks down at soil temperatures above 28°C.
- **Crop rotation**: Incorporating non-host crops (e.g., marigold *Tagetes* spp., which produce nematicidal thiophenes) can reduce *Meloidogyne* populations between susceptible crops.
