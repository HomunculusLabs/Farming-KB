# Taxonomy

Aureobasidium pullulans (de Bary) G. Arnaud is a yeast-like
fungus
belonging to the phylum Ascomycota, class Dothideomycetes, order
Dothideales, and family Dothioraceae. The species has recently
been
reassigned to the family Aureobasidiaceae based on phylogenetic
analyses. A. pullulans is a polymorphic fungus capable of
switching
between yeast-like and [[bloomfields-orchard-hyphal-growth-and-invasive-biology]] forms depending on
environmental
conditions. The species is remarkable for its production of
pullulan,
an extracellular polysaccharide with significant industrial
applications.

# Overview

Aureobasidium pullulans is a cosmopolitan fungus found across
diverse
habitats including leaf surfaces, [[dighton-fungal-decomposition-leaf-litter-dynamics]], soil, rock
surfaces,
and aquatic environments. In [[fungal-stress-responses]] and is a key component of
phyllosphere and endolithic [[biodiversity-of-fungi-soil-fungal-communities-agriculture]].

The ecological breadth of A. pullulans reflects its physiological
versatility and ability to survive under conditions that inhibit
many
other fungal species. Its presence in leaf litter fungal
communities,
phyllosphere habitats, and endolithic rock communities
demonstrates
the capacity of this species to bridge aboveground and
belowground
fungal networks.

# Pollution and Radiation Tolerance

One of the most striking characteristics of Aureobasidium
pullulans
documented in Dighton's textbook is its tolerance to atmospheric
pollutants and ionizing radiation. In sulfur dioxide fumigation
experiments, the respiration of A. pullulans was unaffected by
SO2
concentrations that significantly reduced the respiratory
activity of
other fungal species. This exceptional pollution tolerance
suggests
that A. pullulans may become relatively more dominant in fungal
communities exposed to anthropogenic air pollution, potentially
