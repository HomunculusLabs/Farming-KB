## Psilocybe angustispora

**Type:** Species (Fungus)
**Category:** Basidiomycota, Agaricales, Strophariaceae, Psilocybe
**Also known as:** None widely used

## Overview
Psilocybe angustispora is a petite, uncommon dung-inhabiting mushroom
originally described from western [[challenges-neurotropic-fungi-chemical-analysis-taxonomic-identification]]. Its small size makes it easily
overlooked in the field.

## Key Details
- **Cap:** 0.2-0.6 cm broad. Acutely conic to conic-campanulate.
  Margin decorated with whitish, fibrillose veil remnants. Dark
  reddish brown when moist, fading to pale pinkish tan. Surface
  smooth and viscid from thick gelatinous separable pellicle. Flesh
  very thin and pliant.
- **Gills:** Subdecurrent, distant to subdistant, broad. Dark
  purple-brown in age with whitish fringed edges.
- **Stem:** 10-20 (40) mm long by 0.5-2 mm thick. Equal, flexuous.
  Pallid pinkish tan or nearly concolorous with cap. Surface covered
  with pallid fibrils, lower portions with minute fibrillose scales.
  Partial veil thin, cortinate. Base adorned with tufts of mycelium.
- **Spores:** Dark purplish brown in deposit, narrowly ellipsoid,
  12-15 x 5-8 um. Basidia 4-spored. Pleurocystidia absent.
  Cheilocystidia present.
- **Habitat:** Single to several on dung of sheep, cows, horses, elk,
  marmots, and other animals during spring and fall. Originally
  reported from western Washington, Oregon, Idaho, and Colorado.
  Probably more widely distributed.
- **Chemistry:** Not yet analyzed. Stamets suspects activity based on
  morphology and affinity to known active species, but no chemical
  data exists. The species warrants further study given its
  phylogenetic position within the genus.
