# Psilocybe angustispora

**Type:** Species (Fungus)
**Category:** Basidiomycota, Agaricales, Strophariaceae, Psilocybe
**Also known as:** None widely used

## Overview
Psilocybe angustispora is a petite, uncommon dung-inhabiting mushroom
originally described from western North America. Named for its narrow
spores (angustispora = "narrow spore"), it is a classically shaped
mycenoid Psilocybe. Though activity is suspected based on its
morphology and phylogenetic placement, no specimens have yet been
submitted for chemical analysis. Its small size makes it easily
overlooked in the field.

## Key Details
- **Cap:** 0.2-0.6 cm broad. Acutely conic to conic-campanulate.
  Margin decorated with whitish, fibrillose veil remnants. Dark
  reddish brown when moist, fading to pale pinkish tan. Surface
  smooth and viscid from thick gelatinous separable pellicle. Flesh
  very thin and pliant.
- **Gills:** Subdecurrent, distant to subdistant, broad. Dark
  purple-brown in age with whitish fringed edges.
- **Stem:** 10-20 (40) mm long by 0.5-2 mm thick. Equal, flexuous.
  Pallid pinkish tan or nearly concolorous with cap. Surface covered
  with pallid fibrils, lower portions with minute fibrillose scales.
  Partial veil thin, cortinate. Base adorned with tufts of mycelium.
- **Spores:** Dark purplish brown in deposit, narrowly ellipsoid,
  12-15 x 5-8 um. Basidia 4-spored. Pleurocystidia absent.
  Cheilocystidia present.
- **Habitat:** Single to several on dung of sheep, cows, horses, elk,
  marmots, and other animals during spring and fall. Originally
  reported from western Washington, Oregon, Idaho, and Colorado.
  Probably more widely distributed.
- **Chemistry:** Not yet analyzed. Stamets suspects activity based on
  morphology and affinity to known active species, but no chemical
  data exists. The species warrants further study given its
  phylogenetic position within the genus.

## Distribution and Ecology
Psilocybe angustispora was originally collected from the mountainous
regions of western North America, specifically Washington, Oregon,
Idaho, and Colorado. Its presence across both coastal (Pacific NW)
and interior (Idaho, Colorado) mountain ranges suggests adaptability
to different moisture regimes and forest types. The species fruits
during both spring and fall, indicating a bimodal fruiting pattern
possibly tied to seasonal livestock grazing cycles in alpine and
subalpine meadows. Its occurrence on dung of diverse animals
(sheep, cows, horses, elk, marmots) demonstrates broad substrate
tolerance among herbivore manures, a trait shared with many
coprophilous Panaeolus and Psilocybe species.

## Relevance
Psilocybe angustispora represents a gap in the chemical
characterization of Psilocybe species. Its mycenoid form (small
size, conic cap, narrow stem) places it among the less conspicuous
psilocybin mushrooms, easily overlooked in the field. Its presence
on diverse animal dung across western North American mountain and
coastal regions suggests wide ecological tolerance. The lack of
chemical analysis highlights the need for systematic study of
Psilocybe diversity, as many similar small species may harbor
undiscovered psychoactive compounds. Its affinity to P. semilanceata
and allies suggests it may be a potent species once analyzed.
