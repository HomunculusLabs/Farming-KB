## Suillellus luridus

## Overview

Suillellus luridus (formerly Boletus luridus), commonly known as the lurid
bolete, is a basidiomycete fungus in the family Boletaceae. It is a widely
distributed [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]], and
parts of Asia. The species is notable for its striking red-to-orange pore
surface and reticulate stipe, but requires careful preparation — it is mildly
toxic when raw and can cause gastrointestinal distress if consumed undercooked
or in combination with alcohol. Despite these cautions, it is considered a good
edible when thoroughly cooked and is collected by experienced foragers in many
European countries, particularly in Slavic and Scandinavian traditions where it
is a valued culinary mushroom.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota → **Class:** Agaricomycetes → **Order:** Boletales
- **Family:** Boletaceae → **Genus:** *Suillellus* → **Species:** *S. luridus*
- **Basionym:** *Boletus luridus* Schaeff. (1774)
- **Synonyms:** *Boletus luridus*, *Tubiporus luridus*,
  *Suillellus luridus* f. *luridus*
- The species was transferred to *Suillellus* based on molecular phylogenetics
  that separated it from the core *Boletus* sensu stricto clade.
- Several varieties have been described including f. *rubriceps* (redder cap)
  and f. *luridus*, though these are not widely recognized in modern taxonomy.

## Morphology

- **Cap:** 6–20 cm, hemispherical to convex, becoming plano-convex with age;
  surface smooth, tacky when wet, olive-brown to dark brown, sometimes with
  a reddish tint; margin inrolled when young.
- **Pore surface:** Bright red to orange when young, turning olive-yellow with
  age; pores angular, 1–3 per mm; bruises prominently and immediately blue
  when touched.
- **Hymenophore:** Tubes adnate to slightly decurrent, yellow, turning blue
  on cutting.
- **Stipe:** 6–12 cm × 2–5 cm, club-shaped to bulbous; surface covered in
  a fine red reticulum (net pattern) over a yellow ground color; solid, firm.
- **Flesh:** Pale yellow, turning vivid blue when cut or bruised; red just
  beneath the pileipellis (cap skin).
- **[[polypore-fungi-eastern-north-america]] America (rare) and parts of
  Asia (China, Korea, Japan).
- Ectomycorrhizal, forming associations primarily with broadleaf trees: oak
  (*Quercus*), beech (*Fagus*), hornbeam (*Carpinus*), birch (*Betula*), and
  occasionally conifers such as spruce (*Picea*).
- Fruits singly or in small scattered groups from July to October (Northern
  Hemisphere), preferring warm, humid summers.
- Found in deciduous and mixed forests, often on calcareous or neutral soils;
  fruits more abundantly after warm rainy periods.
- Prefers well-drained soils but tolerates heavier clay soils in lowland
  forests.
