## LET System (Local Employment Trading System)

## Description

The LET System (Local Employment Trading System) is a community-based economic framework
that enables members to trade goods and services using [[mollison-local-currency-systems-and-community-banking]] units, often called
"green dollars," rather than conventional national currency. It was designed to keep wealth
circulating within a community and to provide economic access for people regardless of their
access to formal money. [[bill-mollison]] highlights the LET System as a key strategy for building
self-reliant communities in [[permaculture-design]].

## Classification

- **Category:** Economic system / Community currency
- **Domain:** [[permaculture]] -- [[informal-and-formal-community-economics]], bioregional finance
- **Origin:** Community-based, grassroots economic innovation
- **Scope:** Local community scale (neighborhoods, towns, bioregions)

## How It Works

1. **Membership:** Every joining member must be willing to trade in local green dollars
2. **No Interest:** LET dollars carry no interest, removing the debt-spiral of conventional banking
3. **Administration:** Costs are charged on a "cost of service" basis only
4. **Transparency:** Any member can know the turnover or balance of any other member
5. **Periodic Statements:** Every member gets periodic account statements
6. **Currency Issuance:** Green dollars are earned by providing goods or services to others
7. **Spending:** Green dollars are spent by using services or goods from other members
8. **Public Accounting:** All trade or credit standing is a public act referring to the community

## Key Principles

- The currency is equivalent to legal tender but not issued and cannot be cashed in
- Unlike simple barter, a member in credit can spend across the full range of services or goods offered
- Production (time spent by members in service to others) is never limited by lack of money
- Businesses can charge federal currency for spare parts and green dollars for labour
- Foreign goods become more expensive while local components increase
- Local businesses thrive under the system

## Key Facts

- The community account is always balanced since only members trade with each other
- Any member effectively issues their own currency by working or selling
- Could return any community to full employment
- An ideal member has many transactions but accumulates modest debits and credits
- Charities and local farms benefit greatly as donors can see funds likely to return
- Anyone who wants work can offer services without waiting for formal "jobs"

## Advantages for Community

- Prevents wealth leakage from the community to distant banks and corporations
- Creates constantly available economic resource that circulates indefinitely
- Supports unemployed and underemployed community members
- Makes local goods and services more competitive than imported ones
- Builds social cohesion through economic interdependence
- Enables full employment at community level

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Farm Labour Access:** Farmers and cultivators can access labour without needing cash,
  exchanging produce or future harvests for help with planting, harvesting, or processing
- **Local Food Systems:** LET systems naturally favor local food production over imported food,
  supporting market gardens, [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]], and community orchards
- **Tool Sharing:** Expensive [[gottlieb-psilocybin-cultivation-equipment-and-facilities]] (dehydrators, pressure cookers, substrate
  sterilizers) can be shared through LET credits rather than purchased individually
- **Land Access:** Members can trade labour on each other's cultivation plots, enabling more
  land to be productively managed without monetary wage costs
- **Seed and Spawn Exchange:** Genetic materials like seeds, [[mushroom-spawn]], and plant
  cuttings can circulate through the LET economy, increasing local biodiversity
- **Knowledge Trade:** Mycology workshops, cultivation consultations, and permaculture
  design services can be offered through LET credits, democratizing access to expertise
- **Resilience:** In economic downturns, communities with LET systems maintain food
  production capacity since cultivation labour is not dependent on cash availability

## Comparison with Other Systems

| Feature | LET System | Conventional Money | Simple Barter |
|---------|-----------|-------------------|---------------|
| Scope | Community-local | Global | 1-to-1 only |
| Interest | None | Variable | N/A |
| Transparency | Full | Private | Private |
| Flexibility | Multi-party | Multi-party | Bilateral |
| Wealth retention | High (local) | Low (leaks out) | High (local) |

## Implementation Notes

- Requires a trusted administrator or coordinating body
- Works best in communities of 30-600 people where members know each other
- Can be supplemented with computerized accounting for larger communities
- Should be paired with other community structures (cooperatives, credit unions)
- Most effective when combined with local production capacity (farms, workshops)
