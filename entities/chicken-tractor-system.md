# Chicken Tractor System

## Description

The chicken tractor system is a permaculture land preparation technique where mobile, floorless chicken enclosures (pens) are systematically rotated across garden or farm land. The confined chickens simultaneously clear vegetation, consume weeds and insect pests, scratch and aerate soil, and deposit manure — preparing each area for immediate planting to subsequent crops. After 4-6 weeks in one position, the pen is moved to fresh ground, and the cleared, fertilised area is limed and sown to vegetables, small fruit, or forage crops. The system integrates livestock management with soil preparation and crop rotation in a single closed-loop process, eliminating the need for mechanical cultivation, herbicides, and synthetic fertilisers.

## Classification

- **Category**: Permaculture technique
- **Subcategory**: [[cyperus-rotundus]]), Kikuyu grass, onion weed (Allium triquetrum), and Oxalis species — plants that defeat most other control methods.
- At densities of 120-180 chickens per hectare, the system specifically targets windfall cleanup and orchard fertilisation, reducing codlin moth and fruit fly populations to insignificant proportions over a few years.
- After chickens clear a pen, the ground is limed and immediately sown to forage crops such as buckwheat, wheat, sunflower, millet, black nightshade, cleavers, chard, and mustard — given 2-3 months to mature before chickens return.
- The system produces three simultaneous yields: eggs and meat from the chickens, pest and weed control services, and prepared, fertilised soil ready for crop planting.
