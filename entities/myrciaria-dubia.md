# Myrciaria dubia

## Overview

**Myrciaria dubia**, known as **camu camu**, is a small shrub or tree in
the family Myrtaceae, native to the Amazon basin. It produces berry-like
fruits with the highest recorded natural vitamin C content of any plant,
reaching 2,000–3,000 mg per 100 g of fresh fruit — approximately 30–60
times the concentration in oranges.

The species grows along riverbanks and in floodplain forests throughout
the Amazon, from Peru and Brazil to Colombia, Ecuador, and Bolivia.

Camu camu has gained international attention as a "superfruit" and is
increasingly cultivated for the nutraceutical, beverage, and cosmetic
industries.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

- **Kingdom:** Plantae → **Division:** Angiosperms → **Class:** Eudicots
- **Order:** Myrtales → **Family:** Myrtaceae → **Subfamily:** Myrtoideae
- **Tribe:** Myrteae → **Genus:** *Myrciaria*
- **Species:** *M. dubia* (Kunth) McVaugh (1963)

The basionym is *Eugenia dubia* Kunth (1823). Rogers McVaugh transferred
it to *Myrciaria* in 1963. Molecular phylogenetic studies support this
placement.

**Growth habit:** Evergreen shrub to small tree, 1–8 m tall, typically
2–4 m in cultivation. Multi-stemmed with a spreading canopy.

**Leaves:** Opposite, elliptic to obovate, 3–8 cm long, leathery,
glossy dark green above. New growth is often reddish-bronze.

**Flowers:** Small, white to pale pink, 8–12 mm diameter, with prominent
stamens. Born in axillary clusters of 2–5. Primarily bee-pollinated.

**Fruit:** Spherical berry, 1.5–3.5 cm diameter. Skin smooth, thin,
green turning reddish-purple at maturity. Pulp juicy, pale yellow to
pink, highly acidic. Contains 1–4 seeds per fruit.

**Root system:** Shallow and fibrous, adapted to periodic flooding.

## Distribution and Habitat

*M. dubia* is native to the western Amazon basin, found along river
margins, lakes, and streams in periodically flooded igapó and várzea
forest types.

Its distribution extends from the Putumayo and Napo rivers in Peru and
Ecuador through the Solimões and Amazon rivers in Brazil to the Orinoco
basin in Colombia and Venezuela.

The plant is adapted to flooded conditions and can survive partial
submersion for weeks during the annual flood cycle. Optimal growth
occurs in full sun with consistently moist or waterlogged soils.

Altitudinal range is from sea level to approximately 500 m. Annual
rainfall in native habitats ranges from 1,500 to 3,500 mm.

## Nutritional Profile

Camu camu is most notable for its extraordinary vitamin C content:

- **Vitamin C:** 1,800–2,800 mg/100g fresh weight (highest of any
  known fruit)
- **Anthocyanins:** 50–200 mg/100g, primarily cyanidin-3-glucoside
