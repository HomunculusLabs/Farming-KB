## Description

Walter Clay Lowdermilk (1888–1974) was an American soil conservationist and Assistant Chief of the Soil Conservation Service at the United States Department of Agriculture (USDA). In 1928 and 1929, he conducted pioneering surveys of ancient agricultural civilisations in the Middle East, Israel, and dryland China, examining the archaeological evidence for how land use practices contributed to the rise and fall of civilisations. His work directly connected soil erosion, deforestation, and [[permaculture]] principle of designing for permanence.
- **Policy implications**: His work at USDA demonstrated that government policy on soil conservation is essential — a theme Mollison strongly endorses.

## Legacy

Lowdermilk's work remains foundational to the understanding that soil conservation is not merely an agricultural technique but a civilisational imperative. His surveys influenced subsequent soil conservation policy worldwide and are cited extensively in permaculture literature as historical justification for redesigning agricultural systems.
