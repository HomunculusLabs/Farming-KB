# Cedrus libani

## Overview

**Cedrus libani**, the cedar of Lebanon, is a large evergreen conifer in
the family Pinaceae. It is native to the mountains of the eastern
Mediterranean and is one of the most culturally significant trees in
human history.

The cedar of Lebanon has been harvested for its exceptionally durable
timber since at least the third millennium BCE. Ancient Egyptian, Mesopotamian,
and Phoenician civilizations prized cedar wood for shipbuilding, temple
construction, and mummification.

The species is the national emblem of Lebanon and appears on the
country's flag and coat of arms. Despite its iconic status, wild
populations have been severely reduced by centuries of exploitation and
now occupy a fraction of their original range.
