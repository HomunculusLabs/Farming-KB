## Echinopsis bridgesii (Bolivian Torch)

**Echinopsis bridgesii**, commonly known as the **Bolivian Torch cactus**, is a fast-growing columnar cactus species in the family Cactaceae, native to the Bolivian Andes. It is one of the principal "san pedro-type" mescaline-containing cacti, valued both as a traditional entheogen and as an ornamental succulent in cultivation. The species is closely related to *[[trichocereus-mescaline-cacti-san-pedro-peruvian-torch-halpern]]), and is often discussed alongside them in ethnobotanical literature, though it is genetically and morphologically distinct.

The taxonomic history of *E. bridgesii* is complex. It has been variously classified as *[[trichocereus-bridgesii]]*.

## Taxonomy and Classification

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Order:** Caryophyllales
- **Family:** Cactaceae
- **Subfamily:** Cactoideae
- **Tribe:** Trichocereeae
- **Genus:** Echinopsis (syn. Trichocereus)
- **Species:** E. bridgesii (Salm-Dyck) Friedrich & G.D. Rowley

The species was first described by Joseph zu Salm-Dyck in 1850 as *Cereus bridgesii*, named in honor of the English botanist and plant collector George Bridges (1806–1883), who collected cacti in [[soil-temperature-plant-growth-rate-doubling-rule]] is fast for a cactus — 30–60 cm per year under optimal conditions — making it one of the most vigorous Trichocereus species.

### Ribs
The cactus typically has 4–8 ribs (most commonly 5), which is fewer than *E. pachanoi* (typically 6–9 ribs). The ribs are broad and rounded, with deep furrows between them. This relatively low rib count is a key diagnostic feature distinguishing it from related species.

### Areoles and Spines
Areoles are large and prominent, spaced approximately 1.5–3 cm apart along the ribs. Spines are variable:
- **Radial spines:** 3–7, slender, yellowish-brown to honey-colored, 0.5–2 cm long
- **Central spines:** 1–4 (usually 1–2), strong, yellowish to brown, 2–6 cm long
- Spine length is highly variable; some populations produce notably long central spines (up to 10 cm), while spineless or nearly spineless forms also exist in cultivation
- Spines turn gray with age

### Flowers
Large, showy flowers emerge from near the apex of mature stems. Flowers are:
- **Size:** 15–22 cm long, 10–14 cm in diameter
- **Color:** White with a greenish throat, sometimes with pinkish-brown outer petals
- **Form:** Funnel-shaped, with numerous petals and a prominent cluster of stamens and style
- **Blooming:** Nocturnal to crepuscular, opening in the evening and remaining open through the following morning
- **Fragrance:** Sweet, floral fragrance that attracts pollinating moths and bats

### Fruit
Oval to club-shaped, green to yellowish-green, 5–7 cm long, splitting longitudinally when ripe to reveal white pulp containing numerous small, black seeds.

### Distinguishing Features from Related Species
| Feature | E. bridgesii | E. pachanoi | E. peruvianus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ribs | 4–8 (usually 5) | 6–9 | 5–8 (usually 6–7) |
| Spine length | Moderate to long | Short | Long |
| V-notching | Absent | Present | Present |
| Growth rate | Very fast | Fast | Moderate |
| [[gottlieb-peyote-botany-and-mescaline-content]] | Moderate-high | Moderate | Variable |
| Areole spacing | 1.5–3 cm | 1–2 cm | 1–2 cm |

The absence of V-shaped notches between areoles on the ribs is a reliable distinguishing feature — *E. pachanoi* and *E. peruvianus* both exhibit characteristic V-notching.

### Distribution and Habitat
Native to Bolivia (departments of La Paz, Cochabamba, Santa Cruz), growing at 2,000–3,000 meters in Andean foothills on rocky hillsides, dry inter-Andean valleys with seasonal rainfall, and well-drained volcanic soils.
