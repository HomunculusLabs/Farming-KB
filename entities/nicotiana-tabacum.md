## Overview

**Nicotiana tabacum**, commonly known as cultivated tobacco, is an annual herbaceous plant in the family Solanaceae. It is the most widely cultivated species of the genus Nicotiana and the primary commercial source of tobacco products worldwide. The species is believed to be an allopolyploid hybrid originating from crosses between [[canary-grass-phototropism-model-organism]] in plant molecular biology and genetics research.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Nicotiana tabacum L. belongs to the family Solanaceae, subfamily Nicotianoideae, tribe Nicotianeae. The species was formally described by Carl Linnaeus in 1753 in Species Plantarum. It is an allotetraploid (2n = 48), resulting from natural hybridization events in [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]].

Major producers include China, India, Brazil, the United States, and Zimbabwe. China alone accounts for roughly 40 percent of global tobacco leaf production. The crop is cultivated from sea level to approximately 1500 meters elevation.

The plant thrives in well-drained, fertile soils with a pH of 5.5 to 6.5. Sandy loams with high [[query-how-to-protect-plants-from-frost-and-extend-the-growing-season]]. Drought stress during the rapid growth phase can significantly reduce leaf quality and yield.

Tobacco is relatively intolerant of waterlogged soils and poorly aerated root zones. Raised beds and well-drained field sites are preferred in regions with heavy rainfall or high water tables.

Frosts are lethal at all growth stages, making the crop suitable only for frost-free [[aact-tannins-terpenes-phenolic-compounds-wood-composting-ingham]], flavonoids, carotenoids, and volatile terpenes that contribute to flavor and aroma.

Nicotine binds to nAChR subtypes with high affinity, particularly the alpha-4-beta-2 subtype in the ventral tegmental area. Activation produces dopaminergic stimulation in the mesolimbic pathway, which underlies the reinforcing and addictive properties of tobacco use.
