## Overview

Ranunculus flammula, the lesser spearwort, is a slender perennial herb in the family Ranunculaceae. It is considerably smaller than its relative [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]] of the Northern Hemisphere. It inhabits wet meadows, stream margins, pond edges, and other damp habitats from sea level to subalpine elevations.

Like other buttercups, it contains protoanemonin and is toxic to livestock if ingested in quantity. The species epithet *flammula* means "small flame," likely referring to the bright yellow flowers.

It is sometimes grown in wildlife ponds and rain gardens as a native marginal plant. Its compact size makes it more suitable for small water features than the larger Ranunculus lingua.

The species shows considerable morphological plasticity depending on growing conditions. Submerged forms develop elongated stems with narrower leaves, while emergent plants are more compact and bushy.

The species was described by Linnaeus in Species Plantarum (1753). It belongs to Ranunculus sect. Ranunculus and is closely allied with Ranunculus lingua.

Two varieties are sometimes recognized: var. *flammula* with narrower leaves and var. *minimus* (F.W. Schmidt) Beck, a dwarf alpine form. The taxonomic status of these varieties varies among regional floras.

Hybridization with Ranunculus lingua produces Ranunculus × godronii, a sterile intermediate. This hybrid is rare in the wild and generally confined to sites where both parent species co-occur.

The chromosome number is 2n = 32 in most populations. Polyploid forms have been reported from northern Scandinavia but are not widely accepted as distinct taxa.

## Morphology

Ranunculus flammula is an erect to ascending perennial with slender, solid stems 15–60 cm tall. The plant grows from a short, fibrous rootstock without prominent rhizomes.

Basal leaves are lanceolate to narrowly elliptic, 2–8 cm long and 3–10 mm wide, with entire margins. Stem leaves are similar but smaller, sessile, and progressively reduced upward.

The leaves are typically dark green and glossy on the upper surface. When crushed they release a faint acrid odor characteristic of buttercups containing ranunculin.

Flowers are solitary or in loose 2–5-flowered cymes. Each flower is 10–18 mm in diameter with 5 bright glossy yellow petals and 5 spreading sepals.

The fruiting head is ovoid, composed of numerous small achenes. Each achene is 1.5–2.5 mm long, smooth or sparsely hairy, with a short straight beak about 0.5 mm long.

The flowering period extends from May through September, peaking in June and July. Plants at higher elevations flower later and more briefly than lowland populations.

The roots are fibrous and relatively shallow, spreading horizontally in the upper soil layers. This root system makes the plant effective at stabilizing stream banks and pond margins.

## Distribution and Habitat

Ranunculus flammula occurs throughout Europe, including Iceland and the Faeroe Islands, and extends eastward across northern Asia to Japan and Kamchatka. In [[juncus-effusus]], Carex nigra, and various Sphagnum species.

The species tolerates moderate salinity and can be found in brackish marshes near the coast. This [[clay-pellet-seed-dispersal]]. It can form dense stands in disturbed wetland areas before being succeeded by taller competitors.

Seeds are dispersed by water and by adhesion to the feet and feathers of waterfowl. This dispersal mechanism enables rapid colonization of newly created wetland habitats.
