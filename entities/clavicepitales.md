# Clavicepitales

## Description

The Clavicipitales (now classified within Hypocreales) is a fungal order
containing many of the most important endophytic and pathogenic fungi associated
with grasses and other plants. Members of this order, including the genera
Epichloe, Balansia, Claviceps, Neotyphodium, Atkinsoniella, and
Myriogenospora, are renowned for their production of alkaloid secondary
metabolites that defend host plants against herbivores. Clay (1990) reviewed the
effects of Clavicipitalean endophytes on plant growth, reproduction, and
herbivore resistance.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Eukaryota
- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Sordariomycetes
- **Order:** Hypocreales (formerly Clavicipitales)
- **Family:** Clavicipitaceae

## Key Facts

- **Endophyte-grass associations:** Members of the Clavicipitales,
  particularly Neotyphodium and Epichloe, form intimate associations with
  cool-season grasses, growing systemically within above-ground plant tissues
  without causing apparent damage.
- **Alkaloid production:** These fungi produce diverse alkaloids including
  ergot alkaloids (ergovaline), lolines, peramine, and lolitrems that are toxic
  or deterrent to herbivorous insects and mammals.
- **Effects on plant growth:** Clay (1990) documented that Clavicipitalean
  endophytes increase vegetative growth in Acremonium (100 host species),
  Atkinsoniella (4 species), Balansia (100 species), and Epichloe (100 species).
- **Effects on reproduction:** Some genera suppress host reproduction
  (Atkinsoniella, Balansia, Epichloe, Myriogenospora), representing a
  partial sterility trade-off for the benefits of endophyte association.
- **Herbivore resistance:** All major Clavicipitalean endophyte genera confer
  herbivore resistance to their host plants, making them valuable for pasture
  management and pest control.
- **Transmission strategies:** Include both vertically transmitted species
  (Neotyphodium, via seeds) and horizontally transmitted species (Epichloe, via
  stromata and spores), representing different evolutionary strategies.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Pasture management:** Clavicipitalean endophytes are central to pasture
  grass breeding programs, particularly for tall fescue and perennial ryegrass
  used in livestock production.
- **Novel endophyte technology:** Breeding programs select for strains
  producing insect-deterrent but non-livestock-toxic alkaloid profiles.
- **Biological pest control:** Prestidge and Ball (1997) demonstrated broad
  adverse effects on 12 beetle species, 2 fly species, 16 Hemiptera/Homoptera
  species, and 8 lepidopteran larvae.
- **Evolutionary ecology:** The Clavicipitales serve as a model system for
  studying the evolution of mutualism, vertical versus horizontal transmission,
  and the costs and benefits of endophytic associations.

## Ecological Interactions

- Endophytic species grow within grass tissues, forming systemic infections
  that can be seed-transmitted or spore-transmitted.
- Alkaloids deter insect herbivores (aphids, armyworms, beetles) and grazing
  mammals (cattle, sheep).
- Some species form visible stromata on flowering tillers, producing sexual
  spores for horizontal transmission.
- Interact with the full community of leaf surface microorganisms, competing
  with pathogens for nutrients and space.
