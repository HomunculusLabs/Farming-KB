## Lentinula (Genus)

## Description

Lentinula is a small genus of wood-inhabiting mushrooms best known for containing [[lentinula-edodes]], the shiitake mushroom — arguably the most recognized specialty mushroom in the world. The genus has been cultivated in Asia for possibly thousands of years and underwent a global cultivation revolution in the late 1970s that challenged the century-long dominance of white button mushrooms (Agaricus). Shiitakes are now cultivated worldwide and are prized for their rich nutritional profile, medicinal properties, and relative ease of cultivation, especially on hardwood logs outdoors.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Omphalotaceae
- **Genus:** Lentinula

## Key Species

- **L. edodes** — Shiitake mushroom, the sole commercially cultivated species in the genus

## Key Facts

- Shiitake is one of the most recognized and widely cultivated specialty mushrooms globally
- Cultivated in Asia for possibly thousands of years
- Revolution in the late 1970s expanded cultivation worldwide
- Protein content: 16–22 percent (dry weight)
- Contains nearly all essential amino acids — close to a "complete protein"
- Difficulty level: Outdoor cultivation 1/5, Indoor cultivation 3/5
- Mycelium is white and linear, eventually becoming bumpy and turning slightly brown
- Spawn develops a dark, chocolate-colored crust when exposed to air or after prolonged storage
- Distinct shiitake odor in fresh spawn confirms viability
- [[spore-print]] is creamy white
- Cap honey brown to dark chocolate, nearly black in cold weather
- Sometimes covered with whitish fibrils or tufts arranged in zones

## Cultivation Notes

### Strain Categories

Shiitake strains are categorized by their fruiting temperature windows:

- **Warm strains:** Fruit above 75°F (24°C), suitable for tropical climates or summer months in temperate zones. Have shown prolific fruiting even during extreme drought conditions at 105°F (41°C) with supplemental watering and shading.
- **Cold strains:** Fruit between 35–50°F (2–10°C). Cold shock below 50°F for several days can initiate production year-round. Darker caps at colder temperatures — possibly the origin of the name "black forest mushroom."
- **Wide-range strains:** Fruit at 55–80°F (13–27°C). Fill spring and fall gaps in temperate climates. Most common strains worldwide, being hybridized for specific wood types and substrates.
- **Indoor strains:** Bred for supplemented sawdust blocks. Selected for enzymatic efficiency and thick outer rind formation during browning. Best at 60–70°F (15–21°C), flushing approximately every 21 days.

### Outdoor Cultivation (Logs)

- Grow on hardwood logs or rounds matching strain preference
- Fresh wood (cut no more than one month before inoculation)
- Preferred spawn: sawdust, plug, or cardboard spawn
- Inoculation to fruiting time: six to ten months
- Soak colonized logs for 12–24 hours to initiate fruiting
- Maintain daily misting during primordia formation
- Allow logs to dry between flushes (8–10 week intervals)
- Yields: Very good — 1–2 pounds per log (3 ft x 6–8 in diameter) per year
- Two to three flushes annually

### Indoor Cultivation (Sawdust Blocks)

- Use supplemented sawdust sterilized in autoclave for two hours
- Press blocks tightly in filter bags
- Preferred spawn: grain or supplemented sawdust spawn
- Inoculation to fruiting time: seven to ten weeks

### The Browning Phase

A unique fruiting stage for shiitakes where the outer mycelial surface forms a brownish rind from which primordia develop:

- Triggered by increased oxygen exposure under high humidity
- Secondary metabolite is a reddish-brown fluid
- Takes about two weeks to complete
- Can occur inside or outside the bag, each with advantages
- Maintain high humidity (80–90%) and slightly lower temperature during browning
- Blocks become corky or spongy when ready for fruiting

### Wild Spawn Expansion

- Stem bases do not clone well on cardboard (unlike [[oyster-mushrooms]])
- Dense fibrous stems rarely produce mycelium
- Disk stack method effective: stack new wood rounds on colonized pieces
- Use wet cardboard between layers to maintain moisture
- Shiitake mycelium is slower growing and weaker than most cultures in the lab

### Laboratory Culture

- Can be cultured by tissue cloning or spore germination
- Spore dilutions on agar can train strains to new substrates
- Cannot overexpand spawn on same media more than five times without risking [[strain-senescence]] (loss of vigor)

## Harvesting

- Form singly or in small clusters
- Pull entire mushroom including attachment point from substrate
- Trim before storing
- Best harvested just before cap edges turn upward
- Curled-in, dense caps of younger specimens preferred over mature wavy-margined ones

## Storage

- Fresh: two to three weeks at 38–42°F (3–6°C)
- Dries well but tends to reconstitute chewy
- Powdering dried mushrooms is often better than reconstitution
- Sautéing and freezing preserves natural rich cap color

## Marketing

- Shiitakes enjoy widespread commercial recognition and demand
- Log-grown shiitakes command higher prices (higher cap-to-stem ratio)
- Many growers sell colonized logs at markets for customers to fruit at home
- Worldwide commercial cultivation for over 25 years

## Nutritional and Medicinal Properties

- Protein content: 16–22 percent (dry weight)
- Contains nearly all essential amino acids — near-complete protein comparable to meat
- Boosts and modulates immune system function
- Helps regulate blood pressure and sugar levels
- Lowers cholesterol
- Provides cardiovascular and digestive support
- Contains antiviral and antibacterial properties
- Anti-inflammatory compounds present
- Suppresses many cancer cell lines

## Mycoremediation Potential

- Antibiotic properties make shiitakes suitable for biological water filters
- Can filter water contaminated with human pathogens and parasites
- Dense mycelial mats effective at trapping and neutralizing bacterial contaminants

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Lentinula edodes (shiitake) is foundational to specialty [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]] worldwide. Its nutritional completeness — approaching a true complete protein with all essential amino acids — sets it apart from most other cultivated mushrooms. The species' adaptability, with strains bred for every climate and condition, makes it accessible to cultivators everywhere. The unique browning phase of indoor cultivation and the well-established outdoor log methods provide multiple pathways to production. Its medicinal properties, particularly immune modulation and cancer cell suppression, continue to drive research interest.
