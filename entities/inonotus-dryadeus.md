# Inonotus dryadeus

## Overview

**Inonotus dryadeus** (Pers.) Murrill, commonly known as the **willywidow fungus**, **weeping polypore**, or **oak bracket**, is a perennial polypore fungus in the family Hymenochaetaceae.

It is a root and butt rot pathogen primarily associated with mature oak trees (*Quercus* spp.), causing a distinctive [[quercus-robur]]*, *Q. petraea*, *Q. ilex*, *Q. suber*, *Q. coccifera*) and occasionally with other Fagaceae (*[[fagus-sylvatica]]*).

Basidiocarps appear at the base of living or dead standing trees, typically emerging from root flares or from soil-covered buttress roots.

## Pathology and Wood Decay

**Inonotus dryadeus** is one of the most significant root-rot pathogens of mature oaks in Europe.

Infection occurs through root contact or wounds near the base of the tree. The fungus colonizes the heartwood, causing a selective white rot that preferentially degrades lignin while leaving cellulose relatively intact.

Decayed wood becomes lighter in color, soft, and stringy, eventually reducing to a fibrous or spongy mass.

Infected trees may show canopy thinning, premature autumn leaf coloration, reduced shoot extension, and eventual branch dieback. Structural failure at the root collar is a significant hazard in parkland and urban settings.

The fungus can persist for decades within the root system of a living host, making eradication impractical once established.

Risk assessment in arboriculture involves visual inspection for basidiocarps, probing for soft wood at the root collar, and resistograph or sonic tomography testing.

## Bioactive Compounds

Like other Inonotus species, *I. dryadeus* produces a range of bioactive [[aact-tannins-terpenes-phenolic-compounds-wood-composting-ingham]]:** hispidin analogues, fuscopol, and related pigments
- **Sterols:** ergosterol, ergosterol peroxide

Preliminary studies show moderate cytotoxic activity against human [[blesching-cannabis-apoptosis-and-cancer-cell-death]] lines, though research lags behind that of *I. obliquus* (chaga).
