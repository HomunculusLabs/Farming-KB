## Overview

**Eucalyptus camaldulensis**, commonly known as the river red gum, is the most widely distributed eucalypt species in Australia and one of the most extensively planted eucalypts worldwide.

It is a large evergreen tree typically reaching 20–35 m in height, occasionally exceeding 45 m under optimal conditions. The species is named after the Camaldoli monastery near Naples, Italy, where a specimen was grown in the early 19th century.

River red gum is ecologically significant as a dominant riparian tree across inland Australia, forming the structural framework of floodplain and riverbank ecosystems.

The species is valued for timber, essential oil production, land reclamation, and ornamental planting in Mediterranean and subtropical climates globally.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Taxonomy:** Eucalyptus camaldulensis Dehnh. (1832), family Myrtaceae, subfamily Myrtoideae, tribe Eucalypteae. The specific epithet refers to the Camaldoli collection.

The bark is characteristic: smooth, mottled white, grey, and reddish-brown on the upper trunk and branches, with rough, fibrous, grey to black bark at the base.

Adult leaves are alternate, lanceolate to falcate, 10–30 cm long and 1–3 cm wide, glossy green to blue-grey, with a prominent midrib and intramarginal vein.

Juvenile leaves are opposite, ovate to broadly lanceolate, and often slightly glaucous, distinguishing them from the adult canopy.

Flowers are white to creamy, borne in axillary or terminal panicles of 5–11 flowers per umbel.

The woody fruit (gum nut) is hemispherical to conical, 5–8 mm in diameter, with a prominent raised disc and 3–4 exserted valves.

Seeds are small, brown to yellow-brown, and retain viability for several years under dry [[comparison-steam-distillation-vs-co2-extraction-for-essential-oils]].

Essential oil composition is dominated by 1,8-cineole (eucalyptol), typically comprising 60–75% of the total oil, with α-pinene, limonene, and p-cymene as secondary constituents.

In agroforestry, the species is planted for windbreaks, [[biodiversity-of-fungi-ecosystem-services-agriculture]] including water filtration, bank stabilization, and [[comparison-nutrient-cycling-vs-nutrient-dense-gardening]] in natural river red gum populations.
