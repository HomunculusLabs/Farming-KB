## Overview

**Ulva lactuca** is a [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-europe]], Asia, and the [[guzman-allen-gartz-trans-pacific-species-pairs-psilocybe-biogeography]]lucent green

## [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-classification-system]]:**

- Kingdom: Chromista
- Division: Chlorophyta
- Class: Ulvophyceae
- Order: Ulvales
- Family: Ulvaceae
- Genus: *Ulva*
- Species: *U. lactuca*

Authority: L. (1753).

The thallus is a flat, leaf-like blade, 10–30 cm long and up to 20 cm
wide, though specimens can exceed 60 cm under favorable conditions. It
is attached to the substrate by a small discoid holdfast.

The thallus is composed of only two cell layers (distromatic), making it
translucent when held against light. The edge may be smooth or deeply lobed
depending on environmental conditions.

Cell dimensions are approximately 10–30 µm in length. The two cell layers
are separated by a thin layer of extracellular matrix. Chloroplasts are
cup-shaped and parietal, characteristic of the Ulvophyceae.

The genus *Ulva* has undergone significant taxonomic revision with
molecular phylogenetics. Some previously described species have been
synonymized, and *U. lactuca* is now considered a species complex.

## Nutritional and [[guzman-allen-gartz-four-chemical-groups-neurotropic-fungi]] Composition

*Ulva lactuca* is approximately 15–25% protein (dry weight), 40–50%
carbohydrate, 1–3% lipid, and 10–15% ash (mineral content). The protein
is relatively complete, containing most essential amino acids.
