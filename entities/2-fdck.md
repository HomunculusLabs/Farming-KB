## Overview

2-Fluorodeschloroketamine (2-FDCK) is a synthetic dissociative substance belonging to the arylcyclohexylamine chemical class. It is a structural analog of ketamine with a fluorine atom substituted at the 2-position of the phenyl ring in place of the chlorine atom.

The compound emerged on the research chemical market around 2016 as a ketamine alternative. It is sold primarily through online vendors and has no accepted medical use.

2-FDCK is sometimes marketed under the names "2-FK" and "2-fluoroketamine." It produces dissociative, hallucinogenic, and anesthetic effects similar to but generally longer-lasting than ketamine.

The substance is part of a growing family of fluorinated arylcyclohexylamine derivatives. These compounds are designed to exploit legal gray areas while mimicking the effects of controlled dissociatives.

Recreational use of 2-FDCK has been documented primarily in Europe, [[neurotropic-fungi-japan-east-asia-species-cultural-context]], and [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]]. Online drug monitoring services report that it is one of the more commonly encountered novel dissociatives.

The lack of clinical safety data makes 2-FDCK riskier than well-characterized dissociatives. Users serve as unwitting test subjects for a compound with no toxicological baseline.

## Chemical Properties

2-FDCK has the molecular formula C₁₃H₁₆FNO and a molecular weight of 221.27 g/mol. Its IUPAC name is 2-(2-fluorophenyl)-2-(methylamino)cyclohexan-1-one.

The compound is structurally identical to ketamine except for the 2-fluoro substitution replacing the 2-chloro group. This seemingly minor change produces meaningful differences in pharmacokinetics and [[beta-glucan-receptor-binding]].

2-FDCK exists as a racemic mixture in most commercial preparations. The R and S enantiomers likely differ in potency and subjective effects, as they do with ketamine.

The substance typically appears as a white to off-white crystalline powder. It is soluble in water and ethanol, facilitating both insufflation and oral routes of administration.

The fluorine substitution is a common medicinal chemistry strategy used to improve metabolic stability. In the case of 2-FDCK, this modification appears to significantly extend the duration of subjective effects.

No pharmaceutical-grade synthesis standards exist for 2-FDCK. Products sold online may contain impurities, incorrect isomers, or entirely different compounds mislabeled as 2-FDCK.

## Pharmacology

2-FDCK acts primarily as an NMDA receptor antagonist, the same mechanism shared by ketamine, PCP, and [[methoxetamine]]. By blocking NMDA-mediated glutamate signaling, it produces dissociation, analgesia, and perceptual distortions.

The fluorine substitution may affect the compound's metabolic stability compared to ketamine. Preliminary evidence suggests 2-FDCK has a longer duration of action, potentially due to slower hepatic metabolism.

Onset of effects is typically 5–15 minutes via insufflation and 20–40 minutes orally. Total duration ranges from 2–5 hours, significantly longer than ketamine's typical 45–90 minute duration.

The compound likely shares ketamine's effects on opioid receptors and monoamine transporters. However, detailed [[tihkal-receptor-binding-serotonin]] profiles have not been published for 2-FDCK specifically.

Metabolic pathways are not fully characterized but are expected to involve N-demethylation and ring hydroxylation analogous to ketamine metabolism. The fluorine atom may resist oxidative metabolism, contributing to the extended duration.

The compound crosses the blood-brain barrier efficiently due to its moderate lipophilicity. Brain concentrations are expected to parallel plasma levels with slight delay.

Animal studies on related fluorinated arylcyclohexylamines suggest similar neurotoxicity profiles to ketamine at equivalent doses. However, species-specific metabolic differences limit extrapolation to humans.

## Effects

Subjective effects at common doses (30–80 mg insufflated) include dissociation from the physical body, visual and auditory distortions, and a sense of floating or detachment. The experience is frequently described as dreamlike.

Compared to ketamine, users report a more gradual onset, smoother peak, and longer tail of effects. The "hole" experience (complete dissociation) typically requires higher doses of 80–150 mg.

Cognitive effects include impaired working memory, reduced verbal fluency, and temporal distortion. These cognitive impairments resolve gradually over several hours after peak effects subside.

Physical effects include analgesia, ataxia, and mild tachycardia at lower doses. Nausea and vertigo are commonly reported during onset, particularly with insufflation.

Psychological risks include anxiety, panic, and depersonalization during the experience. Prolonged derealization lasting days to weeks has been reported with heavy use.

The harm profile of 2-FDCK is not well characterized due to limited clinical data. Risks are assumed to be similar to ketamine, including urinary tract toxicity with chronic heavy use.

[[adverse-effects-and-harm-reduction-psilocybin]] guidance for 2-FDCK emphasizes starting with very low doses due to potency uncertainty. Testing products with reagent kits can help rule out misidentified substances but cannot confirm purity.

Combining 2-FDCK with depressants such as alcohol increases the risk of respiratory depression and loss of consciousness. Mixing with stimulants may mask sedative effects, leading to accidental overdose.

## History

2-FDCK first appeared on the online research chemical market around 2016. Early vendor listings described it as a "legal ketamine alternative" without providing pharmacological details.

The compound gained popularity following ketamine scheduling in various Asian countries. Online drug forums began documenting user experiences around 2017, with detailed reports appearing on platforms like Erowid and Reddit.

Analytical identification of 2-FDCK in seized drug samples was first reported by European forensic laboratories in 2018. The EMCDDA has monitored it since 2019 as part of its early warning system.

Academic interest in 2-FDCK has focused on its forensic detection rather than pharmacology. Several research groups have published methods for identifying the compound in biological and seized material samples.
