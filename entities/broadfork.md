## Broadfork

## Description

The broadfork (also known as a U-bar digger or grelinette) is a human-powered deep soil aeration tool used extensively at [[four-season-farm]] as the first step in preparing greenhouse beds for replanting between succession crops. Unlike a rototiller, the broadfork loosens and aerates soil without inverting it, preserving soil structure, microbial communities, and natural layering. It is the primary deep-soil intervention in Coleman's greenhouse soil preparation workflow.

## Classification

- **Category**: Soil preparation tool
- **Type**: Manual deep cultivator / aerator
- **Use case**: Deep aeration of raised beds before replanting
- **Power source**: Human

## Physical Specifications (Four Season Farm model)

- **Width**: 30 inches (matches bed width)
- **Handles**: Two handles, each 4 feet long, standing upright at either end of the crossbar
- **Crossbar**: 30-inch-wide metal bar
- **Tines**: 7 soil-penetrating metal tines
- **Tine length**: 10 inches each
- **Material**: Steel construction

## Operating Technique

1. Place broadfork at one end of the bed with tines resting on soil surface
2. Apply downward pressure with one foot to press tines into the soil to full depth
3. Pull back on handles — just enough to lift and loosen the lower soil slightly
   - **Critical**: Do NOT flip or invert the soil; the goal is lifting and cracking, not turning
4. Raise tines out of the soil
5. Step back 6 inches
6. Repeat the sequence down the full length of the bed

The result is a bed with loosened, aerated subsoil that retains its natural layering and structure.

## Role in the Soil Preparation Sequence

At Four Season Farm, the broadfork is used as the first mechanical step after crop removal in greenhouse beds:

1. **Clear old crop** — remove plant debris
2. **Broadfork** — deep aeration (the step described here)
3. **Tilther** — shallow incorporation of amendments into top 2 inches
4. **Compost** — applied to surface, mixed shallowly
5. **Rake** — smooth bed surface with Austrian hay rake
6. **Roll** — firm surface with mesh roller for seeding

The broadfork addresses the lower soil layers (below 2 inches) while the Tilther handles the surface zone. This complementary pairing ensures full-profile soil preparation without deep disruption.

## Design Philosophy

The broadfork embodies several key organic farming principles:

- **Non-inversion tillage**: Soil layers remain in their natural order, preserving microbial habitat zonation
- **Preservation of mycorrhizal networks**: Fungal hyphae are cracked and loosened but not severed and buried
- **No compaction**: Unlike rototiller tines, which can create a hardpan at their maximum working depth, broadfork tines fracture compaction layers
- **Scalable effort**: The operator controls depth and force, adapting to variable soil conditions bed by bed

## Comparison to Alternatives

| Tool | Depth | Soil Impact | Energy Source | Compaction Risk |
|------|-------|-------------|---------------|-----------------|
| Broadfork | 10 inches | Loosens without inverting | Human | None |
| Rototiller | 6–8 inches | Inverts and pulverizes | Gas/electric | Hardpan at max depth |
| Spading fork | 8–12 inches | Similar but narrower | Human | None (but slower) |
| Double dig | 12–24 inches | Full inversion | Human | None (but very laborious) |

## Relevance to Cultivation

The broadfork is essential in systems that:

- Practice intensive succession cropping (frequent replanting of beds)
- Need to maintain long-term soil health in permanent raised beds
- Work in protected structures where gas-powered tools are impractical
- Prioritize biological soil management over mechanical intervention

For greenhouse and high tunnel growers, the broadfork provides deep aeration without the drawbacks of powered equipment — no fumes, no noise, no hardpan, and no disruption of [[soil-food-web]]. It is particularly valuable in no-till and minimum-till systems where soil structure is a primary asset.
