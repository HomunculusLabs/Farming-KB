## Description

Cherimoya (*Annona cherimola*) is a subtropical to mild-temperate fruit tree in the family Annonaceae, prized for its large, heart-shaped, green-skinned fruit with creamy white, custard-like flesh. Often called the "ice cream fruit" for its rich, sweet flavor blending notes of banana, pineapple, papaya, and vanilla. Native to the Andean highlands of Ecuador, Colombia, Peru, and Bolivia, it thrives at elevations of 1,000–2,000 meters where conditions are cool but frost-free.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Family:** Annonaceae (custard apple family)
- **Genus:** *Annona*
- **Species:** *A. cherimola*
- **Common names:** Cherimoya, chirimoya, custard apple (shared with related species)
- **Growth habit:** Semi-deciduous tree, 5–9 m tall
- **Climate zone:** Subtropical to mild temperate (USDA Zones 9–11)

## Key Facts

- Fruit weighs 200–1,000 g with a characteristic scale-like or fingerprint-patterned green skin
- Flesh is white, creamy, and aromatic with embedded dark brown to black seeds
- Seeds are toxic if crushed and ingested (contain annonacin, a neurotoxin)
- Trees begin bearing 3–5 years after planting from seed
- Pollination is often hand-assisted because natural pollinators (certain beetles) may be absent outside native range
- Grows best in well-drained soils with pH 6.5–7.5
- Sensitive to waterlogging and extended drought
- Fruit is highly perishable — shelf life of 2–7 days at room temperature
- Rich in vitamin C, B vitamins, potassium, and dietary fiber
