# Eucalyptus salmonophloia

## Description

Eucalyptus salmonophloia, commonly known as **salmon gum** or **salmon Gum**, is a medium to large tree native to the wheatbelt region of Western Australia. It is distinguished by its striking smooth salmon-pink bark and is one of the most visually impressive mallee and woodland eucalypts. The species produces extremely hard, durable timber that has been heavily exploited for construction and fuel.

## Classification

- **Family:** Myrtaceae
- **Genus:** Eucalyptus
- **Species:** E. salmonophloia
- **Common names:** Salmon gum

## Key Facts

- Medium to large tree, typically 10–30 m tall with a spreading crown
- Named for its distinctive smooth salmon-pink bark that ages to grey
- Native to the semi-arid interior of Western Australia, particularly the goldfields and wheatbelt
- Produces extremely dense, hard timber — among the hardest of all eucalypts
- Well-adapted to arid and semi-arid conditions with annual rainfall as low as 250 mm
- Important species in the goldfields woodland ecosystem
- Slow to moderate [[soil-temperature-plant-growth-rate-doubling-rule]]

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

- **Timber:** Extremely hard and durable timber used for fence posts, sleepers, and mining timbers (pages 94, 95, 96, 117)
- **Firewood:** Among the best firewood species due to extremely dense wood (pages 94, 95, 96, 117)
- **Shade and shelter:** Provides excellent shade in [[neem-azadirachta-indica-arid-zone-multipurpose-tree]] plantings
- **[[comparison-vetiver-grass-vs-bamboo-for-erosion-control]]:** Deep root system stabilises soils in semi-arid regions
- **Honey:** Produces nectar for [[faires-beekeeping-and-honey-production]] in suitable flowering years (pages 94, 95)
- **Ornamental:** Striking salmon-pink bark makes it a desirable specimen tree for arid zone landscaping
- **Drought fodder:** Can serve as emergency fodder during severe drought

## Cultivation Notes

- Extremely drought-tolerant once established
- Prefers well-drained, moderately fertile soils including clay loams
- [[plants-tolerant-of-alkaline-soils]] soils
- Suited to low-rainfall areas (250–500 mm annually)
- Slow initial growth but develops into a very long-lived tree
- Frost-tolerant once established
- Best planted in autumn or winter in Mediterranean climates
