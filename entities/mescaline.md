## Overview

**Mescaline** (3,4,5-trimethoxyphenethylamine) is a naturally occurring psychedelic alkaloid of the phenethylamine class. It is the principal psychoactive compound found in several species of Cactaceae, most notably peyote (*[[lophophora-williamsii]]*), San Pedro (*[[echinopsis-pachanoi]]*), and the Peruvian torch (*[[echinopsis-peruviana]]*). Mescaline is one of the oldest known psychoactive substances, with evidence of ceremonial use spanning over 5,700 years in the archaeological record.

The compound produces profound alterations in consciousness, including vivid visual hallucinations, altered perception of time and space, and deep introspective experiences. It acts primarily as a serotonin 5-HT2A receptor agonist, sharing this mechanism with other classical psychedelics such as [[lsd]] and [[psilocin]]. Despite its long history of human use, mescaline remains one of the least studied classical psychedelics in modern clinical research.

Mescaline is classified as a Schedule I controlled substance under the United Nations Convention on Psychotropic Substances (1971). However, exemptions exist for the traditional ceremonial use of peyote by members of the [[halpern-sacramental-peyote-and-native-american-church]] in the United States, a protection codified in the American Indian Religious Freedom Act Amendments of 1994.

## Chemical Properties

**IUPAC name:** 2-(3,4,5-trimethoxyphenyl)ethan-1-amine

**Molecular formula:** C11H17NO3

**Molecular weight:** 211.26 g/mol

**SMILES:** COc1cc(cc(c1OC)OC)CCN

**CAS number:** 54-04-6

Mescaline is a white crystalline solid at room temperature, soluble in water, ethanol, and slightly soluble in diethyl ether. The freebase form is an oily liquid at room temperature, though it is almost exclusively encountered as the hydrochloride or sulfate salt in both research and traditional contexts. The hydrochloride salt melts at 181–182 °C.

The three methoxy groups at the 3, 4, and 5 positions on the phenyl ring are essential for psychedelic activity. Removal or repositioning of these substituents dramatically reduces or abolishes 5-HT2A receptor affinity. The ethylamine side chain is also critical, as extension or branching modifies both potency and pharmacokinetic profile.

Mescaline is a chiral molecule, though it is typically encountered as the racemate. The (R)-enantiomer has been reported to be modestly more potent than the (S)-enantiomer in animal behavioral models, though clinical differences in humans are not well characterized. The compound is stable under normal storage conditions and does not degrade significantly when kept dry and protected from light.

The trimethoxy substitution pattern is shared with several other psychoactive phenethylamines, though the specific 3,4,5-trimethoxy arrangement is unique to mescaline and its direct analogues. This substitution pattern maximizes [[mckenna-fungal-fossil-gap-spore-electron-density-and-metal-hardness]] on the phenyl ring, which correlates with increased 5-HT2A receptor binding affinity relative to mono- or dimethoxy-substituted compounds.

## Pharmacology

Mescaline acts primarily as a **partial agonist at the serotonin 5-HT2A receptor** with a Ki of approximately 10–30 µM, which is notably lower affinity than [[lsd]] (Ki ≈ 1–3 nM) or [[psilocin]] (Ki ≈ 5–10 nM). This lower receptor affinity is consistent with mescaline's higher effective dose range of 200–500 mg, compared to LSD's 50–200 µg range.

Additional receptor interactions contribute to its full pharmacological profile:

- **5-HT2C receptor:** Partial agonism with moderate affinity, contributing to anxiety and stimulant-like effects at higher doses. 5-HT2C activation also modulates dopamine release in mesolimbic pathways.
- **5-HT1A receptor:** Weak partial agonism, potentially contributing to the anxiolytic and contemplative aspects of the mescaline experience at moderate doses.
- **Alpha-1 adrenergic receptor:** Weak antagonism, which may account for the mild cardiovascular effects and peripheral vasoconstriction sometimes reported.

Mescaline undergoes hepatic metabolism primarily via **oxidative deamination** by monoamine oxidase (MAO-A) and **O-demethylation** by cytochrome P450 enzymes (predominantly CYP2D6). The major metabolites include 3,4,5-trimethoxyphenylacetic acid (TMPAA), 3,4-dimethoxy-5-hydroxyphenethylamine, and various demethylated conjugates excreted renally.

The elimination half-life of mescaline is approximately 6 hours, with total duration of effects lasting 8–12 hours depending on dose and individual metabolism. Peak plasma concentrations are reached 1.5–2.5 hours after oral administration. Bioavailability is reduced by first-pass metabolism, though this is partially mitigated by consuming the alkaloid in plant material, which contains MAO-inhibiting compounds such as [[harmine]]-related beta-carbolines.

Interindividual variation in CYP2D6 metabolism significantly affects mescaline pharmacokinetics. Poor CYP2D6 metabolizers may experience higher and more prolonged plasma concentrations, while ultra-rapid metabolizers may require larger doses for equivalent effects. This polymorphism may partially explain the wide variability in subjective dose-response reported in ethnographic and clinical literature.

## Effects

**Typical oral dose range:** 200–500 mg (as hydrochloride salt)

**Duration:** 8–14 hours, with onset at 45–90 minutes, peak effects at 2–4 hours, and gradual return to baseline over 6–10 additional hours.

**Subjective effects profile:**

- **Visual hallucinations:** Rich, colorful geometric patterns, enhanced color perception, and complex imagery. Visual effects are consistently described as more organic and flowing compared to the synthetic sharpness of [[lsd]].
- **Altered perception:** Significant distortions of time perception (time dilation), synesthesia (especially sound-to-color), and altered body image. Spatial perception changes are common and can be disorienting at higher doses.
- **Emotional effects:** Wide range from euphoria and profound emotional openness to anxiety and existential distress. Set and setting heavily influence the valence of the experience.
- **Cognitive effects:** Deep introspective thinking, enhanced pattern recognition, philosophical ideation, and altered self-awareness. Users frequently report mystical-type experiences at moderate-to-high doses.

**Common side effects:** Nausea and vomiting (especially during onset, partly attributable to other cactus alkaloids), tachycardia, hypertension, mydriasis, tremor, hyperthermia, headache, and dizziness. The emetic effect is particularly pronounced with crude cactus preparations.

**Aftereffects:** Residual visual disturbances ("visual snow") may persist for 1–3 days. Emotional sensitivity, fatigue, and altered sleep patterns are common for 24–72 hours following the experience. Persistent perception disorder (HPPD) is rare but documented with heavy chronic use, and tolerance to mescaline's effects develops rapidly with consecutive daily dosing.

## Toxicology

The therapeutic index of mescaline is relatively wide compared to many psychoactive substances. The LD50 in mice is approximately 370 mg/kg (intraperitoneal) and 1320 mg/kg (oral), suggesting a substantial safety margin at recreational doses of 200–500 mg. No confirmed human fatalities from mescaline overdose alone have been documented in the medical literature.
