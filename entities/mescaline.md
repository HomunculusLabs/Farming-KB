## Description

Mescaline is a naturally occurring hallucinogenic phenylethylamine alkaloid found primarily in the peyote cactus ([[lophophora-williamsii]]) and other cacti of the San Pedro group. It is one of the most well-studied psychedelic compounds and is notable for its [[serotonin-psilocin-structural-similarity-hallucinogenic-mechanism]] to the neurohormone norepinephrine, a relationship that parallels the structural similarity between indolic hallucinogens and serotonin.

## Classification

- **Category**: Hallucinogenic alkaloid
- **Chemical class**: Phenylethylamine base
- **Molecular formula**: C11H17NO3
- **Source organisms**: Lophophora williamsii (peyote), [[trichocereus-pachanoi]] (San Pedro), other cacti

## Key Facts

- Hallucinogenic phenylethylamine base found in peyote cactus
- Structurally similar to the neurohormone norepinephrine
- This structural similarity may help explain its hallucinogenic activity
- One of the most important naturally occurring psychedelics
- Has been used for thousands of years in Native American [[entheogenic-folk-religious-ceremonies]]
- Paramount among hallucinogens of religious significance

## Pharmacological Effects

- Potent visual hallucinations with vivid colors and geometric patterns
- Altered perception of time and space
- Synesthesia (crossing of sensory modalities)
- Nausea and vomiting (especially in early phases with peyote ingestion)
- Effects typically last 8-12 hours
- Acts primarily on serotonin 5-HT2A receptors despite phenylethylamine structure

## Structural Significance

The source text highlights a key chemical parallel:
- **Mescaline is to norepinephrine** as **indolic hallucinogens are to serotonin**
- This dual relationship between hallucinogenic compounds and neurohormones provides insight into the mechanisms of hallucinogenic activity
- Mescaline represents a different structural class (phenylethylamine) from most other hallucinogens (indoles/tryptamines)

## Cultural Significance

- Central sacrament of the [[metzner-peyote-ceremonies-native-american-church-and-huichol-traditions]] peyote ceremony
- Used by indigenous peoples of Mexico for thousands of years
- Paramount among hallucinogens of religious significance worldwide
- Depicted in the Aztec statue of Xochipilli (Prince of Flowers)

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

While mescaline is a plant (cactus) alkaloid, it is relevant to mycological studies:

- Represents a non-indole hallucinogen for comparison with fungal tryptamines
- Understanding different hallucinogen structural classes enriches mycological pharmacology knowledge
- Peyote cultivation shares techniques with mushroom cultivation (environmental control)
- Important for comprehensive understanding of naturally occurring psychoactive compounds
