# Parasitella parasitica

## Description

*Parasitella parasitica* is a zygomycete fungus in the order Mucorales that parasitizes other Mucorales, notably *Absidia glauca*. The species is remarkable for forming anchoring cells (sikyotic cells) that establish cytoplasmic continuity with the host, inducing gall formation. Most significantly, *P. parasitica* has been shown experimentally to transfer genetic material between host and parasite — one of the few documented cases of natural horizontal gene transfer between fungi, with potentially profound evolutionary implications.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Eukarya
- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Zygomycota
- **Order:** Mucorales
- **Genus:** *Parasitella*
- **Species:** *P. parasitica*

## Key Facts

- *P. parasitica* forms sikyotic ("sucker" or "anchor") cells that create a wide cytoplasmic connection with the host fungus *Absidia glauca*.
- The parasite induces gall formation in the host, a visible morphological response to infection.
- Mating-type factors determine the compatibility between host and parasite.
- Horizontal gene transfer (HGT) from host to parasite has been demonstrated experimentally (Kellner et al. 1993; Wöstemeyer et al. 1995):
  - The prototrophic parasite compensated for artificially induced deficiencies in the host.
  - This compensation became established in 0.4% of uninucleate sporangiospores of the offspring.
  - Transfer of a neomycin-resistance plasmid was also observed.
  - Although the "pararecombinants" were unstable, this demonstrates a natural gene transfer mechanism.
- The cytoplasmic continuity established by the sikyotic cell is the likely conduit for DNA transfer.
- *P. parasitica* is considered a semi-biotrophic parasite — it maintains a living relationship with the host initially but may become saprotrophic later.
- The HGT mechanism could have important evolutionary implications, potentially explaining morphological similarities between unrelated mycoparasites and their hosts.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

*Parasitella parasitica* is significant for several reasons:
- **Evolutionary biology:** Provides one of the clearest examples of natural horizontal gene transfer between fungi, suggesting that mycoparasitism may be an important driver of fungal genome evolution.
- **Mucorales ecology:** As a parasite of *Absidia* and other Mucorales, *P. parasitica* regulates populations of these fast-growing primary colonizers.
- **Morphological convergence:** The HGT mechanism may explain cases where mycoparasites (e.g., tremelloid fungi) show morphological similarities to their hosts, previously attributed to convergent evolution.
- **Genetic research:** The sikyotic cell interface provides a model for studying intercellular communication and genetic exchange in fungi.
