# Voacangine

## Overview

Voacangine is an indole alkaloid found in the root bark of several
Apocynaceae species, most notably *Voacanga africana* and *Tabernanthe
iboga*. It is a close structural relative of ibogaine, the principal
psychoactive alkaloid of the iboga plant.

Voacangine serves primarily as a biosynthetic precursor to ibogaine
and iboga-related alkaloids, but also possesses intrinsic
pharmacological activity including serotonergic receptor modulation,
mild psychedelic effects, and potential anti-addictive properties. It
has been used as a starting material in the semi-synthesis of ibogaine
for clinical research.

## Chemical Properties

- **Molecular formula:** C₂₂H₂₈N₂O₃
- **Molecular weight:** 368.47 g/mol
- **Structure type:** Indole alkaloid (iboga-type)
- **Appearance:** White to off-white crystalline solid
- **Solubility:** Soluble in chloroform, methanol, DMSO; poorly
  soluble in water

Voacangine belongs to the iboga class of monoterpenoid indole
alkaloids, characterized by a fused isoquinuclidine-indole ring
system. It differs from ibogaine by the absence of a hydroxyl group
at C-12 and the presence of a methoxy group at C-16.

## Pharmacology

- **5-HT₂A:** Weak partial agonist/antagonist; contributes to mild
  psychedelic effects at high doses
- **5-HT₂C:** Moderate affinity; may influence appetite and mood
- **SERT:** Moderate inhibition; less potent than ibogaine
- **DAT:** Weak inhibition
- **NMDA receptor:** Weak affinity; glutamatergic modulation may
  contribute to anti-addictive potential

Voacangine's profile is broadly similar to ibogaine but consistently
less potent across all targets. The weaker serotonergic activity means
voacangine produces minimal perceptual disturbance at doses where
ibogaine would be fully psychedelic, suggesting it as a potentially
safer scaffold for anti-addiction drug development.

## Effects

**Subjective effects** (20–50 mg oral, limited self-report data):
Mild sedation within 30–60 minutes, subtle visual changes (increased
color saturation, mild pattern enhancement), light oneirogenic quality,
reduced anxiety and introspective state. Unlike ibogaine, voacangine
typically does not cause severe nausea or ataxia at moderate doses.

**Duration:** 4–8 hours, significantly shorter than ibogaine's
12–24+ hour duration.

**Dose range:** Limited clinical data. Estimated threshold 15–20 mg,
moderate at 30–50 mg, strong above 50 mg. Based on traditional use
and self-report, not controlled studies.

## Natural Sources

- **Voacanga africana** Stapf — root bark 0.5–1.5% voacangine,
  plus voacamine, voacamidine. West and Central Africa.
- **Tabernanthe iboga** Baill. — minor constituent (<0.3%);
  ibogaine is dominant
- **Voacanga thouarsii** — Madagascar and East Africa
- **Tabernaemontana divaricata** — crepe jasmine; trace amounts

*V. africana* root bark is the primary commercial source, harvested,
dried, and extracted for voacangine and related alkaloids.

## Synthesis

Voacangine is extracted from *V. africana* root bark via acid-base
extraction and chromatographic isolation. It serves as a key precursor
in ibogaine semi-synthesis: the isolated voacangine undergoes
oxidative demethylation and rearrangement to produce ibogaine. This
route avoids complex total synthesis and provides better batch
consistency than direct *T. iboga* extraction.

## History

First isolated from *V. africana* in the 1950s by French and Belgian
chemists studying West African medicinal plants. The iboga alkaloids
were first investigated by Édouard Landrin in 1901, with ibogaine
isolated by Raymond Hamet. *V. africana* bark has traditional use in
West African ethnomedicine as a stimulant, aphrodisiac, and ceremonial
intoxicant in Ghana, Nigeria, and Cameroon.

## Legal Status

- **United States:** Not specifically scheduled; sale for human
  consumption may be prosecuted under the Federal Analog Act
- **UK/Canada:** Uncontrolled
- **International:** Not listed on UN Convention on Psychotropic
  Substances

## Related Compounds

- **Ibogaine** — principal iboga alkaloid; fully psychedelic at
  10–20 mg/kg, potent anti-addictive
- **Ibogamine** — non-psychoactive, 18-desmethoxy derivative
- **Noribogaine** — active ibogaine metabolite; may mediate
  anti-addictive effects
- **Voacamine** — bisindole alkaloid from *V. africana* with
  antiplasmodial activity
- **18-MC** — synthetic iboga derivative designed to retain anti-
  addictive properties without psychedelic or cardiotoxic effects
