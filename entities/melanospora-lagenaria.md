# Melanospora lagenaria

## Description

Melanospora lagenaria is a mycoparasitic fungus in the family Ceratostomataceae (Sordariales, [[ascomycota]]). It is one of the most commonly encountered species of Melanospora found on polypore bracket fungi, where it produces distinctive perithecia on the hymenophore and surface of host sporocarps. The species is frequently found in association with other [[fungicolous-fungi]], particularly [[ophiostoma-polyporicola]] and Hypocrea pulvinata, forming a characteristic fungal community on Fomitopsis pinicola.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Eukaryota
- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Sordariomycetes
- **Order:** Sordariales
- **Family:** Ceratostomataceae (Melanosporaceae)
- **Genus:** Melanospora
- **Species:** M. lagenaria

## Morphological Characteristics

- **Perithecia:** Dark, flask-shaped ascomata produced on the surface and hymenophore of host polypores. Characteristic of the Ceratostomataceae, with dark-colored walls
- **Asci:** Unitunicate, evanescent at maturity
- **Ascospores:** Darkly pigmented, typically one-celled, with germ pores
- **Anamorph:** Not well documented for this species; other Melanospora species have Harzia (syn. Acremoniella) anamorphs

## Ecology and Host Range

Melanospora lagenaria has been recorded on a wide range of polypore hosts:

1. **Fomitopsis pinicola** — The most commonly reported host, where it frequently co-occurs with Ophiostoma polyporicola and Hypocrea pulvinata (Cannon and Hawksworth 1982)
2. **Bjerkandera species**
3. **Trametes species**
4. **Polyporus species**
5. **Piptoporus species**
6. **Stereum species**

The species grows on the hymenophore and surface of bracket fungi, where its dark perithecia are visible against the lighter host tissue.

## Associated Fungal Community

On Fomitopsis pinicola, M. lagenaria is part of a characteristic fungicolous assemblage:

- **Ophiostoma polyporicola** — Often found together, with Ophiostoma ascomata hidden inside pores of the bracket fungus
- **Hypocrea pulvinata** — Forms stromata on the same host, sometimes O. polyporicola grows out from its stromata

This three-species community represents one of the best-documented examples of multiple mycoparasites co-occurring on a single host sporocarp.

## Mycoparasitic Behavior

- Colonizes the sporocarps of polypores, growing on and within the hymenophore tissue
- Classified among the mycoparasitic Ceratostomataceae, a family containing many fungicolous species (Cannon and Hawksworth 1982)
- The degree of host specificity varies, with the species found on multiple polypore genera but showing a preference for Fomitopsis

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Forest pathology:** May influence the decomposition dynamics of bracket fungi in forest ecosystems
- **Community ecology:** The co-occurrence with other mycoparasites on F. pinicola provides a model system for studying fungal community assembly
- **Taxonomic significance:** Illustrates the mycoparasitic lifestyle within the Sordariales, a primarily saprotrophic order
- **Biocontrol potential:** Melanospora species have been investigated as potential biocontrol agents against pathogenic fungi

## Key References

- Cannon and Hawksworth (1982) — revision of the Ceratostomataceae including mycoparasitic species
- Helfer (1991) — European fungicolous fungi records

## Notes

Melanospora lagenaria is one of the more conspicuous mycoparasites on polypores due to its dark perithecia. The species is relatively easily identified in the field when growing on Fomitopsis pinicola. The related species M. caprina is found primarily on Tomentella species and other tomentelloid fungi, showing the host segregation within the genus.
