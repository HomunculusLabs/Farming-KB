# Sporophagomyces

## Description

Sporophagomyces is a genus of mycoparasitic fungi in the family Hypocreaceae (Hypocreales, [[ascomycota]]). The genus is characterized by an acremonium-like anamorph and a distinctive subiculum (fungal mat) that physically traps host basidiospores. These trapped spores serve as the primary nutrient source for the parasite. The most well-known species, S. chrysostomus, produces conspicuous fanlike colonies that hang beneath the basidiomata of its [[ganoderma]] hosts.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Eukaryota
- **[[kingdom-fungi]]
- **Division:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Sordariomycetes
- **Order:** Hypocreales
- **Family:** Hypocreaceae
- **Genus:** Sporophagomyces

## Key Species

- **S. chrysostomus** — The type and best-documented species. Produces fanlike colonies hanging under basidiomata of [[ganoderma-applanatum]]. The brownish color of the subiculum is caused by trapped basidiospores of the host embedded in the fungal mat. Hosts are mostly members of the Ganodermataceae (Põldmaa et al. 1999).

## Morphological Characteristics

- **Subiculum:** A distinctive fungal mat that covers and surrounds the host basidiomata. The subiculum physically entraps host basidiospores, which are visible as brownish inclusions and serve as nutrient sources
- **Anamorph:** Acremonium-like, with phialidic conidiophores producing hyaline conidia
- **Teleomorph:** Perithecial ascomata embedded in or arising from the subiculum
- **Colony morphology:** Fanlike, spreading growth pattern beneath host brackets

## Ecology and Habitat

Sporophagomyces species are obligate mycoparasites found on:

- **Ganodermataceae:** Primarily associated with Ganoderma species, especially G. applanatum
- The fungus colonizes the lower surface and pore layer of perennial bracket fungi
- Fanlike colonies are visible hanging beneath the host basidiomata in the field

## Mycoparasitic Mechanism

The genus employs a unique spore-trapping strategy:

1. **Subiculum formation:** A dense fungal mat grows over the hymenophore of the host
2. **Spore entrapment:** Host basidiospores are physically trapped within the subiculum as they are released from the pore surface
3. **Nutrient extraction:** The trapped basidiospores serve as a concentrated nutrient source
4. **Sporocarp development:** The parasite produces its own reproductive structures on or within the subiculum

This mechanism is distinct from most other mycoparasites, which typically parasitize hyphae rather than trapping spores.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Ganoderma pathology:** Could potentially affect cultivated Ganoderma species used in medicinal mushroom production
- **Unique biology:** The spore-trapping mechanism represents a specialized evolutionary adaptation among mycoparasites
- **Taxonomic significance:** Shares characters with Hypocrea and Hypomyces but is distinguished by its subiculum-based spore-trapping strategy
- **Ecological role:** May influence Ganoderma population dynamics and [[spore-dispersal]] in forest ecosystems

## Key References

- Põldmaa et al. (1999) — description and revision of the genus
- Rogerson and Samuels (1993) — taxonomic framework for Hypocreaceae mycoparasites

## Notes

Sporophagomyces represents one of the more specialized mycoparasitic strategies in the Hypocreaceae. The brownish subiculum color caused by trapped host spores is a diagnostic field character. The genus is relatively rarely collected, possibly due to its specialized host range on Ganodermataceae and the need to examine the undersides of bracket fungi carefully. Whether the genus is distinct from Hypocrea requires further molecular study.
