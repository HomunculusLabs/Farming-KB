## Overview

Buphedrone is a synthetic stimulant belonging to the substituted [[cathinone]] class of chemicals. It is structurally related to methcathinone, differing only by the addition of an alpha-methyl group extending the carbon chain.

The compound emerged in the early 2010s as part of the "bath salts" wave of novel [[savory-short-duration-grazing-and-cell-systems]] and high compulsion to redose.

Buphedone has never been investigated in formal [[blesching-cannabis-safety-profile]].

The compound's legal status varies by jurisdiction, with most countries having enacted specific or generic controls. Online vendors continue to market analogs designed to circumvent existing legislation.

## Chemical Properties

Buphedone has the molecular formula C₁₁H₁₅NO and a molecular weight of 177.24 g/mol. Its IUPAC name is 2-(methylamino)-1-phenylbutan-1-one.

The structure consists of a phenyl ring attached to a ketone-bearing carbon chain with a methylamino group. It is an alpha-ethyl analog of methcathinone and a beta-keto analog of methamphetamine.

Buphedone exists as a racemic mixture in most commercial preparations. The hydrochloride salt form is the most commonly encountered physical form, appearing as a white crystalline powder.

The compound is soluble in water and organic solvents. Its melting point is approximately 180–185°C for the hydrochloride salt.

Spectral data for identification include characteristic IR absorption at the carbonyl stretch and specific mass fragmentation patterns detectable by GC-MS.

Buphedone can be distinguished from mephedrone by mass spectrometry due to the absence of the 4-methyl group on the phenyl ring. This structural difference reduces molecular weight by 14 mass units.

The compound's structural simplicity makes it relatively easy to synthesize from commercially available precursors. This accessibility contributed to its initial appearance on the recreational market.

## Pharmacology

Buphedone acts as a releasing agent and reuptake inhibitor at monoamine transporters. It primarily affects dopamine and norepinephrine systems, with weaker effects on serotonin transport.

In vitro studies indicate buphedrone is a potent dopamine and norepinephrine releaser. Its serotonergic activity is modest compared to entactogenic cathinones like mephedrone.

The potency at dopamine transporters is comparable to methamphetamine. This strong dopaminergic action explains the intense euphoria and high addiction potential reported by users.

Onset of effects is rapid via insufflation, typically 5–15 minutes. Oral onset is slower at 20–45 minutes. Duration of effects is notably short at 1.5–3 hours, shorter than mephedrone.

The short duration drives compulsive redosing behavior. Users frequently report consuming their entire supply within a single session, a pattern that increases toxicity risk.

Metabolism involves N-demethylation, reduction of the beta-keto group, and aromatic hydroxylation. Major metabolites are detectable in urine for 24–48 hours after ingestion.

The beta-keto group distinguishes cathinones from amphetamines in both structure and metabolism. This modification reduces blood-brain barrier penetration relative to the corresponding amphetamine.

Cross-tolerance with other stimulants develops with regular buphedone use. Users report diminished response to cocaine and amphetamine after periods of heavy cathinone consumption.

## Effects

At common doses (20–50 mg insufflated), buphedone produces intense euphoria, increased energy, and elevated mood. Users describe a rush of confidence and sociability similar to but shorter than mephedrone.

Physical effects include tachycardia, hypertension, pupillary dilation, and elevated body temperature. Jaw clenching and teeth grinding (bruxism) are commonly reported.

The comedown is characterized by depression, anxiety, and fatigue. These after-effects are often described as disproportionately severe relative to the brief duration of positive effects.

Psychological risks include anxiety, paranoia, and psychosis at higher doses. The combination of strong dopaminergic stimulation and short duration creates a particularly [[emcdda-psilocybin-non-addictive-profile-tolerance]].

Chronic use is associated with cardiovascular strain, sleep disruption, and cognitive impairment. Injecting buphedone carries additional risks of vascular damage and infection.

Overdose symptoms include severe hypertension, hyperthermia, seizures, and in extreme cases, stroke or cardiac arrest. No specific antidote exists, and treatment is supportive.

Harm reduction approaches for buphedone emphasize avoiding redosing, staying hydrated, and never mixing with other stimulants or alcohol. Users should be aware of the high addiction potential before experimenting.

## Legal Status

Buphedone is controlled as a scheduled substance in most jurisdictions. Many countries regulate it under generic cathinone or phenethylamine legislation.

The United Kingdom controls buphedone as a Class B drug under the Misuse of Drugs Act. It was among the cathinones specifically scheduled in the 2010 generic cathinone ban.

The United States prosecutes buphedone under the Federal Analogue Act as a Schedule I substance. Specific state-level scheduling varies, with most states enacting controls.
