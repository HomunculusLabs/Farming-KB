## Overview

**Echium pininana**, commonly known as the **tower of jewels** or **pine echium**, is a monocarpic biennial or triennial herb in the family Boraginaceae. It is endemic to the laurel forests of La Palma in the Canary Islands, where it grows at mid-elevations in humid, shaded ravines.

The species is renowned among horticulturists for producing one of the tallest flower spikes of any temperate garden plant. A single flowering stalk can exceed 4 meters in height, covered in thousands of blue-violet blossoms that attract pollinators in extraordinary numbers.

In cultivation, Echium pininana has become a signature plant of coastal and mild-climate gardens worldwide. It has naturalized in parts of southwestern Britain, Ireland, and coastal California, where mild winters allow self-seeding.

The species epithet *pininana* refers to its pine-like stature when in full flower. Within its native range, it inhabits steep, shaded ravines and north-facing slopes at elevations of 500–1,200 meters. The species favors humid microsites with persistent cloud cover, well-drained volcanic soils, and protection from direct afternoon sun.

Associated species in its native habitat include *Laurus novocanariensis*, *Persea indica*, and *Myrica faya*, all characteristic of the Canarian laurel forest canopy. The understory typically features ferns, mosses, and shade-tolerant herbs that share the moist ravine environment.

The laurel forest habitat is critically fragmented due to historical clearing for agriculture and grazing. Echium pininana populations have declined significantly, and the species is considered vulnerable in its native range.

Outside its native habitat, the species has naturalized in parts of southwestern England, western Ireland, coastal California, and New Zealand. These populations persist in gardens and along sheltered coastlines where winter temperatures remain above −5 °C.

Increasing drought frequency and reduced cloud immersion threaten the laurel forest's moisture regime, which the species depends upon.

## Cultivation

Echium pininana requires a sheltered position in full sun to partial shade with well-drained soil. It thrives in mild maritime climates where winters rarely drop below −5 °C (USDA hardiness zones 8–10).

Seeds should be sown in late spring or early summer in a well-drained seed compost. Germination typically occurs within 2–4 weeks at 15–20 °C, and seedlings grow rapidly once established.

Seedlings develop a deep taproot early and resent root disturbance, so pricking out should be done while plants are small. Direct sowing in situ is often more successful than transplanting from seed trays.

Young plants are susceptible to slug damage and benefit from physical barriers during the rosette stage. The bristly foliage of mature plants provides some herbivore deterrence, but young leaves are tender and palatable.

In colder regions, the rosette can be protected with a dry mulch or fleece during winter. Plants grown in containers should be moved to a frost-free location, as container-grown roots are more vulnerable to freezing.
