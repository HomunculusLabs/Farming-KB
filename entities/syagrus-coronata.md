# Syagrus coronata

## Overview

Syagrus coronata, the licuri palm or ouricuri palm, is a medium-sized solitary
palm endemic to the Caatinga and Atlantic Forest transition zones of
northeastern Brazil. It is an ecological keystone species, providing critical
food and habitat for several threatened species.

The palm reaches 8 to 15 meters in height with a robust trunk 20 to 30
centimeters in diameter. Persistent leaf bases form a distinctive spiral pattern
on the trunk, giving the species its specific epithet *coronata* (crowned).

Licuri palm is best known as the primary food source for the Spix's macaw
(Cyanopsitta spixii), one of the world's rarest birds. Although currently

density varies enormously: some Bahia sites exceed 200 individuals per hectare,
while others are fragmented and declining due to land conversion.

## Ecology and Conservation

Syagrus coronata is a keystone species in the Caatinga. Its fruits are consumed
by Spix's macaw, Lear's macaw (Anodorhynchus leari), and numerous parrots,
toucans, and mammals.

The Lear's macaw is heavily dependent on licuri palm nuts. Colonies of this
Endangered species nest in sandstone cliffs adjacent to licuri stands in Bahia.
