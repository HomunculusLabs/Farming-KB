## Overview

**Fermentative Compost Tea (FCT)** is compost tea produced through anaerobic or partially anaerobic fermentation of compost in water, without active aeration. Classified in Ingham's taxonomy in *The Field Guide for Actively Aerated Compost Tea*.

## Characteristics

- No mechanical aeration during brewing
- Fermentative organisms dominate (lactic acid bacteria, yeasts)
- pH drops as organic acids are produced
- Represents the historical approach to compost tea used for centuries

## Comparison with AACT

| Parameter | FCT | AACT |
|-----------|-----|------|
| Aeration | None | Continuous |
| Dominant organisms | LAB, yeasts | Aerobic bacteria, fungi |
| Oxygen | Depleted (<2 ppm) | Maintained (>6 ppm) |
| Pathogen risk | Higher | Lower |
| Odor | Sour/fermented | Earthy |

## Potential Benefits

- Low cost — no aeration equipment required
- Organic acid production can suppress certain pathogens
- Nutrient solubilization through fermentation
- Plant growth hormones from fermentative organisms
- Centuries of traditional use

## Concerns

Ingham and other soil biologists raise concerns about pathogen proliferation in anaerobic conditions, variable quality, alcohol and organic acid phytotoxicity, reduced organism diversity, and loss of beneficial aerobes.

## Related Techniques

- Korean Natural Farming (KNF) — fermented plant juices and LAB cultures
- Effective Microorganisms (EM) — Teruo Higa's proprietary fermentative cultures
- Jadam Organic Farming — anaerobic fermentations for pest control
- Biodynamic preparations — Rudolf Steiner's fermented amendments

## Relevance to Mycology

- Fermentative fungi (yeasts) play roles in FCT
- Organic acids can suppress pathogenic fungi on leaf surfaces
- Fermentation techniques are used to prepare mushroom cultivation substrates
- Fermentative organisms can outcompete mold contaminants
- Understanding aerobic vs. anaerobic composting is essential for mushroom compost production
