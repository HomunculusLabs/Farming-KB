# Tremella fuciformis

**Type:** Species
**Category:** Basidiomycete (Tremellales)
**Also known as:** Snow fungus, White jelly mushroom, Silver ear

## Overview

Tremella fuciformis is a jelly fungus that has been cultivated in
China since approximately 1800 AD. It is recognised as one of the
important [[medicinal-mushrooms]] and has been used in traditional Chinese
medicine for centuries. The fungus produces distinctive white, gelatinous
fruiting bodies and is valued both as a culinary delicacy and a
therapeutic agent with documented health benefits.

## Key Details

### Taxonomy and Classification
Tremella fuciformis belongs to the family Tremellaceae within the
order Tremellales of the Basidiomycota. It is a parasitic fungus that
typically grows on dead or dying branches of broadleaf trees,
especially species of Sterculiaceae. The fruiting body is soft,
gelatinous, and translucent white, forming lobed or brain-like
structures that can reach several centimetres in diameter.

### Active Compounds
The primary bioactive compounds in Tremella fuciformis are
polysaccharides, with glucuronoxylomannan being the most significant.
Several specific bioactive fractions have been identified:

- Glucuronoxylomannan: the major bioactive polysaccharide
- T-7 and T-19: exopolysaccharides with immunomodulatory activity
- Mannose, xylose, and glucuronic acid: constituent sugars released
  from polysaccharide complexes

Submerged mycelial biomass culture has been shown to produce
glucuronoxylomannan, making liquid fermentation a practical approach
for commercial production. The liquid cultured broth also yields
xylose, glucuronic acid, and mannose, which have individual
therapeutic applications.

### Medicinal Properties
Tremella fuciformis possesses several documented medicinal properties.
It has demonstrated antitumour activity, making it relevant to cancer
research and adjunctive therapy. The mushroom also functions as a
kidney tonic and nerve tonic in traditional Chinese medicine
classifications.

The antidiabetic properties of Tremella fuciformis have been noted in
pharmacological studies. Additionally, the mushroom has been shown to
reduce both total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol, making it a
functional food for cardiovascular health management.

### Traditional Use
In traditional Chinese medicine, Tremella fuciformis has been used for
centuries to nourish the lungs, strengthen the kidneys, and improve
skin health. It is commonly consumed in sweet soups and desserts in
Chinese cuisine, reflecting its dual role as both food and medicine.
The mushroom is believed to promote longevity and vitality.

### Cultivation
Tremella fuciformis has been cultivated in China for over two
centuries. Traditional cultivation involves inoculating logs with the
fungus, while modern methods use sterile substrate bags. The ability
to produce key bioactive compounds through submerged fermentation makes
this species attractive for industrial-scale production of
polysaccharides for pharmaceutical and nutraceutical applications.

## Relevance

Tremella fuciformis is significant for its well-established dual role
as both an edible and medicinal mushroom. For growers, it offers a
specialty crop with established market demand in Asian markets and
growing interest globally. The capacity for submerged fermentation
production of bioactive polysaccharides makes it particularly relevant
for biotechnology applications. Its cholesterol-lowering and
antidiabetic properties position it well within the expanding functional
foods market.
