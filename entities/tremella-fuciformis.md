## Overview

**Tremella fuciformis**, commonly known as snow fungus, silver ear, or white
jelly mushroom, is an edible jelly fungus in the family Tremellaceae.

It forms translucent, gelatinous, brain-like [[bloomfield-asterophora-and-mycoparasites-of-fruiting-bodies]] that are widely
consumed as both a culinary delicacy and [[query-how-do-i-make-a-medicinal-mushroom-double-extraction-tincture]] in [[neurotropic-fungi-japan-east-asia-species-cultural-context]].

The species has been used in traditional Chinese medicine for over 1,800
years, valued for its purported effects on lung health, skin vitality, and
immune function.

Modern research has identified bioactive polysaccharides, particularly
tremellan, as the primary compounds responsible for its immunomodulatory and
antioxidant activities.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:** Fungi → Basidiomycota → Tremellomycetes → Tremellales →
Tremellaceae → *Tremella fuciformis* Berk.

**Synonyms:** *Tremella fuciformis* var. *fuciformis*, *Tremella
fuciformis* Berk. ex Cooke, *Naematelia fuciformis* (Berk.) G.W. Martin.

The [[comparison-fruiting-body-vs-mycelium-extracts-medicinal-mushrooms]] is gelatinous, soft, and translucent white to pale yellow,
reaching 2–15 cm in diameter. It has a lobed, undulating, or brain-like
morphology with no clearly defined cap or stipe.

The basidium is cruciately septate, producing four epibasidia. Basidiospores
are hyaline, broadly ellipsoid, and measure 7–10 × 6–8 µm, germinating by
repetitive budding or ballistoconidia production.

The hyphal system consists of thin-walled, branched, clamped generative hyphae.
Haustorial cells penetrate the hyphae of its host fungus, *Annulohypoxylon*
species, from which it derives nutrients as a mycoparasite.

In nature, *T. fuciformis* is an obligate mycoparasite and cannot complete
its [[agaricus-campestris-biology-and-life-cycle]] without an appropriate host fungus. This parasitic relationship
is essential for [[fungal-life-cycles-sexual-reproduction-metamorphosis]] and fruiting [[suppressed-incomplete-fruit-body-formation-brassinosteroid-treatment]].

## Distribution and Habitat

*T. fuciformis* is widely distributed across tropical, subtropical, and warm
[[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]] worldwide, with natural populations in Asia, Oceania, the
Americas, and Africa.

It grows on dead or dying branches of broadleaf trees, particularly oaks
(*Quercus* spp.), but is dependent on the presence of its host fungus
*Annulohypoxylon* (formerly *Hypoxylon*) species within the same wood.

Fruiting occurs primarily during warm, rainy periods from late spring through
autumn. The gelatinous fruiting bodies swell dramatically after rainfall and
can persist for several days in humid conditions.

In commercial cultivation, the species is grown on a substrate of hardwood
sawdust and bran, inoculated with a [[fukuoka-rice-barley-dual-culture]] of *T. fuciformis* and its
required host fungus.
