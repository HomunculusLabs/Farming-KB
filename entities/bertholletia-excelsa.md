## Overview

**Bertholletia excelsa**, the Brazil [[nut-tree-cultivation-for-permaculture-food-forests]], is one of the largest and longest-lived canopy trees of the Amazon rainforest.

It produces the commercially important Brazil nut, the most economically significant non-timber forest product harvested from Amazonian forests.

The tree can exceed 50 meters in height and live for over 500 years.

Its ecological relationships are extraordinarily specialized, particularly its reliance on a single genus of bees for pollination and agoutis for [[clay-pellet-seed-dispersal]].

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:**

- Kingdom: Plantae
- Order: Ericales
- Family: Lecythidaceae
- Genus: Bertholletia
- Species: B. excelsa Humb. & Bonpl.

The genus is monotypic, containing only this single species.

No subspecies or varieties are formally recognized, though genetic variation exists across its range.

**Morphology:**

The trunk is straight and cylindrical, reaching 1-2 meters in diameter at breast height.

Bark is grayish-brown, rough, and deeply fissured on mature specimens.

Leaves are simple, oblong to elliptical, 20-35 cm long and 10-15 cm wide, with entire margins.

Flowers are large (3-4 cm), zygomorphic, creamy-white to yellowish, borne in panicles on old wood.

The fruit is a large woody capsule (10-15 cm diameter, weighing up to 2 kg) containing 12-24 triangular seeds (the Brazil nuts).

## Distribution and Habitat

Native to the Amazon Basin, occurring across Brazil, Peru, Bolivia, Colombia, Venezuela, Ecuador, and the Guianas.

Found primarily in non-flooded (terra firme) forests at elevations below 500 meters.

The tree is absent from seasonally flooded várzea forests and white-sand igapó habitats.

It forms groves or "castanhais" of 20-200 individuals, reflecting patterns of past indigenous planting and management.

Population densities are typically 1-10 adult trees per hectare in natural forest.

## Ecological Relationships

Pollination is exclusively by large-bodied euglossine bees of the genera Eulaema and Xylocopa.

These bees require the complex floral morphology to access nectar; smaller pollinators are physically excluded.

The flowering period lasts 2-4 weeks, typically during the dry season, creating a narrow reproductive window.

[[seed-dispersal-ecology-and-mechanisms]] depends entirely on agoutis (Dasyprocta spp.), which bury excess nuts and forget some caches.

This scatter-hoarding behavior is the only natural mechanism for seedling establishment.

Without agoutis, virtually no natural regeneration occurs beneath mature canopy trees.

## Nutritional Profile

Brazil nuts are among the richest dietary sources of selenium on Earth, containing 68-91 µg per gram.

A single nut (approximately 5 g) provides 100-175% of the recommended daily selenium intake.
