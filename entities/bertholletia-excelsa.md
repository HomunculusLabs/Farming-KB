**Classification:**

- Kingdom: Plantae
- Order: Ericales
- Family: Lecythidaceae
- Genus: Bertholletia
- Species: B. excelsa Humb. & Bonpl.

The genus is monotypic, containing only this single species.

No subspecies or varieties are formally recognized, though genetic variation exists across its range.

**Morphology:**

The trunk is straight and cylindrical, reaching 1-2 meters in diameter at breast height.

Bark is grayish-brown, rough, and deeply fissured on mature specimens.

Leaves are simple, oblong to elliptical, 20-35 cm long and 10-15 cm wide, with entire margins.

Flowers are large (3-4 cm), zygomorphic, creamy-white to yellowish, borne in panicles on old wood.

The fruit is a large woody capsule (10-15 cm diameter, weighing up to 2 kg) containing 12-24 triangular seeds (the Brazil nuts).

## Distribution and Habitat

Native to the Amazon Basin, occurring across Brazil, Peru, Bolivia, Colombia, Venezuela, Ecuador, and the Guianas.

Found primarily in non-flooded (terra firme) forests at elevations below 500 meters.

The tree is absent from seasonally flooded várzea forests and white-sand igapó habitats.

It forms groves or "castanhais" of 20-200 individuals, reflecting patterns of past indigenous planting and management.

Population densities are typically 1-10 adult trees per hectare in natural forest.
