# OG Kush

**OG Kush** is a legendary indica-dominant hybrid cannabis strain that originated in Southern California during the early 1990s. It is widely considered the genetic backbone of West Coast cannabis culture and has served as a parent to dozens of commercially significant strains.

Its complex aroma of earth, pine, and lemon, combined with potent cerebral-euphoric effects, established a new standard for cannabis quality in the American market.

## Strain Classification

- **Type:** Indica-dominant hybrid (approximately 55% indica / 45% sativa)
- **Lineage:** Chemdawg × Lemon Thai × Hindu Kush (most widely accepted)
- **Breeder:** Unknown; propagated through clone-only cuttings in Florida and California
- **THC content:** 19–26% (average ~23%)
- **CBD content:** Low (<0.3%)

## Aroma and Flavor Profile

- **Primary aromas:** Earthy, pine, wood, lemon
- **Secondary notes:** Fuel, spice, citrus zest
- **Taste:** Smooth woody inhale with a sharp lemon-pine exhale; lingering herbal aftertaste
- **Key terpenes:** Myrcene, limonene, β-caryophyllene, linalool

The aroma is immediately recognizable and has become a benchmark against which other "Kush" strains are measured. The fuel-like undertone comes from high concentrations of myrcene and caryophyllene working in concert.

## Effects and Pharmacology

- **Onset:** Rapid, within 2–5 minutes of inhalation
- **Duration:** 2–3 hours
- **Primary effects:** Euphoric uplift, creative stimulation, mood elevation
- **Body effects:** Deep physical relaxation spreading from the head downward; heavy limbs at higher doses
- **Medical applications:** Stress relief, mild depression, chronic pain, migraine, insomnia (evening use)
- **Side effects:** Dry mouth, dry eyes; anxiety or paranoia at high doses in susceptible individuals

OG Kush is known for its biphasic effect profile. Lower doses produce clear-headed, social euphoria while higher doses shift toward sedative couch-lock. This versatility has contributed to its enduring popularity across both recreational and medical markets.

## Growing Characteristics

- **Flowering time:** 8–9 weeks
- **Yield:** 300–500 g/m² indoors; 400–600 g/plant outdoors
- **Height:** 90–160 cm indoors
- **Difficulty:** Moderate; requires attention to feeding and humidity
- **Climate:** Warm, Mediterranean; thrives in 20–28°C range
- **Resistance:** Moderate mold resistance; susceptible to spider mites
- **Nutrient needs:** Heavy feeder during flowering; benefits from calcium and magnesium supplementation
- **Training:** LST and SCROG recommended to manage stretch; responds well to topping

Because OG Kush was clone-only for decades, phenotypic variation is minimal. However, this also means cuttings can carry latent pathogens. Tissue culture is recommended for commercial cultivation to ensure vigor and disease-free stock.

## Genetic Relatives and Crosses

- **Chemdawg:** Contributed potency and the characteristic fuel aroma
- **Lemon Thai:** Provided the citrus terpene profile and cerebral sativa effects
- **Hindu Kush:** Contributed indica density, resin production, and physical relaxation
- **Notable crosses:** Girl Scout Cookies, Headband, Tahoe OG, Skywalker OG, SFV OG, Ghost OG

OG Kush is arguably the most influential genetic platform in modern cannabis breeding. The "OG" designation has been appended to hundreds of descendant strains, many of which share only a distant genetic relationship to the original clone.

## Cultural Significance

OG Kush emerged from the 1990s Southern California cannabis scene and became synonymous with Los Angeles hip-hop culture. Snoop Dogg, Dr. Dre, and other West Coast figures referenced it extensively, cementing its status as a cultural icon.

It won the High Times Cannabis Cup and has been featured in numerous films, albums, and television shows. The debate over the meaning of "OG" — whether it stands for "Original Gangster," "Ocean Grown," or refers to the original grower — remains unresolved.

## Legal Status

OG Kush is classified as cannabis and is subject to the legal regulations of each jurisdiction. Legal in Canada, Uruguay, and numerous U.S. states with adult-use or medical cannabis programs. Remains a Schedule I substance under U.S. federal law as of 2026.

## Appearance

- **Bud structure:** Dense, medium-to-large calyxes with tight internodal spacing
- **Color:** Vibrant green with occasional amber pistils; leaves can show purple hues in cool temperatures
- **Trichome coverage:** Extremely heavy; buds appear frosted with a thick resin layer
- ** Pistil density:** Moderate orange-brown pistils interspersed among calyxes

The visual presentation of OG Kush is distinctive: chunky, resinous colas with a pale green hue that darkens toward the calyx tips. Trichome density is among the highest of any classic-era strain, contributing to its reputation for hash-making potential.

## Phytochemical Notes

Beyond the dominant terpenes, OG Kush produces measurable quantities of nerolidol, bisabolol, and guaiol — sesquiterpenes associated with sedative and anti-inflammatory properties. The cannabinoid ratio is heavily skewed toward THC with trace amounts of THCV detected in some phenotypes, which may contribute to the strain's reported appetite-stimulating effects.

Total terpene content typically ranges from 1.5–2.5%, placing OG Kush in the moderate-to-high terpene tier. This terpene concentration, combined with high THC, produces what consumers describe as a "full-spectrum" entourage effect.
