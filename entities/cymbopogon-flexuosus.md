# Cymbopogon flexuosus

**Cymbopogon flexuosus**, commonly known as East Indian lemongrass, Malabar
lemongrass, or Cochin lemongrass, is a perennial aromatic grass in the family
Poaceae. Native to South and [[permaculture-designers-manual-essential-oil-plants]] — one of the primary commercial sources of citral. The species is
widely used in perfumery, food flavoring, traditional medicine, and permaculture
systems for its pest-deterrent properties.

## Taxonomy

Poaceae → Panicoideae → Andropogoneae → **Cymbopogon → *C. flexuosus***

The genus *Cymbopogon* comprises approximately 55 species of aromatic grasses.
*C. flexuosus* is one of two major lemongrass species cultivated for essential oil,
the other being *C. citratus* (West Indian lemongrass). The two are morphologically
similar but differ in chromosome number (*C. flexuosus* is tetraploid, 2n=40;
*C. citratus* is diploid, 2n=20), growth habit, and citral isomer ratio.
*C. flexuosus* is sometimes confused with *C. citratus* in commerce, though
their oils have slightly different organoleptic profiles.

## Description

- **Growth form:** Perennial bunching grass, forming dense clumps 0.6–1.5 m tall
  and 0.5–1 m wide. Rhizomatous, spreading slowly by short underground rhizomes.
- **Leaves:** Linear, 40–90 cm long × 1–2 cm wide, glaucous green, drooping at
  tips. Leaf sheaths purplish or reddish at base (a key distinction from
  *C. citratus*, which has green sheaths). Strong lemon fragrance when crushed.
- **Stems:** Erect culms, solid or slightly hollow, 3–6 mm diameter at base.
- **Inflorescence:** Large, nodding, compound panicle, 30–60 cm long, with
  purplish racemes. Flowers rarely set viable seed in cultivation.
- **Roots:** Dense fibrous root system extending 30–60 cm deep; effective at
  soil stabilization and [[query-how-to-grow-rare-tropical-fruits-in-marginal-and-subtropical-climates]].
Optimal growth at 25–35°C with annual rainfall of 250–330 cm. Tolerates poor,
sandy, and lateritic soils but performs best in well-drained loams with pH 5.5–7.0.

## Essential Oil Composition

The essential oil is steam-distilled from fresh or dried aerial parts, yielding
0.5–1.5% by weight. The oil is composed of:

- **Citral** (75–85%): A mixture of geranial (citral A, ~40–50%) and neral
  (citral B, ~30–40%). This is the primary aromatic and bioactive component.
- **Myrcene** (10–20%): Monoterpene contributing to the fresh green aroma.
- **[[linalool]]** (trace), **[[limonene]]** (trace).
- Minor constituents: farnesol, nerol, methyl heptenone.

The citral content of *C. flexuosus* oil is typically higher than that of *C. citratus*,
making it preferred for industrial citral extraction. Citral is a precursor for
the synthesis of ionones, vitamin A, and retinol.
