## Morning Glory Seeds

## Description

Morning glory seeds, particularly from the species [[ipomoea-violacea]] (syn. I. tricolor) and [[turbina-corymbosa]] (ololiuqui), contain ergot alkaloids including LSA ([[lysergic-acid-amide]], also called ergine) and are one of the traditional natural sources of psychedelic compounds in the Americas. Leary mentions morning glory seeds in "The Psychedelic Experience" in the context of dosage and side effects, noting that — like peyote — they can cause physiological nausea, as distinct from the primarily psychological nausea associated with pure LSD or psilocybin.

## Classification

- **Category**: Plant / Seeds — Entheogen
- **Primary Species**: Ipomoea violacea (common morning glory), Turbina corymbosa (ololiuqui)
- **Family**: Convolvulaceae
- **Primary Active Compounds**: LSA ([[lysergic-acid]] amide), iso-LSA, and other ergot alkaloids
- **Related Substances**: LSD (synthetically derived from related ergot alkaloids)

## Key Facts

- Turbina corymbosa (ololiuqui) was used by Aztec and Maya peoples in divination and healing rituals
- The Aztec name "ololiuqui" (round thing) refers to the seeds of T. corymbosa
- Contains LSA (lysergic acid amide), a naturally occurring compound structurally related to LSD
- LSA is approximately 1/10th to 1/20th the potency of LSD
- Common garden morning glory varieties (Heavenly Blue, Pearly Gates, Flying Saucers) contain the highest concentrations of active alkaloids
- Seeds are often treated with fungicides by commercial suppliers, making them potentially toxic unless properly sourced

## In Leary's Framework

Leary mentions morning glory seeds specifically regarding side effects:

- Like peyote, morning glory seeds can cause physiological (not just psychological) nausea
- This distinguishes them from pure compounds like LSD and psilocybin, where nausea is primarily a mental symptom indicating fear
- Anti-nauseant drugs may be taken beforehand to prevent physiological nausea from seed consumption
- This practical advice reflects the early era of psychedelic research when natural plant sources were commonly used alongside pure compounds

## Traditional Use

- Central American indigenous use of ololiuqui dates back centuries, with documented use by the Zapotec, Mazatec, and other peoples
- Spanish colonial records describe the use of "ololiuqui" in divination and healing ceremonies
- The seeds were ground and prepared as a drink or paste
- Traditional use often occurred in a ritual context similar to mushroom veladas
- [[albert-hofmann]] isolated and identified LSA from morning glory seeds in 1960

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Morning glory seeds are relevant to cultivation and mycology as:

- A natural entheogenic plant that complements the fungal entheogens (psilocybin mushrooms) in the broader psychedelic toolkit
- An example of how ergot alkaloids (the family from which LSD was derived) occur naturally in multiple plant species
- Part of the traditional Mesoamerican pharmacopeia that includes psilocybin mushrooms, salvia, and datura
- The relationship between LSA (natural) and LSD (synthetic) parallels the relationship between psilocybin (natural mushroom compound) and its synthetic analogs
- Understanding the cultivation and preparation of morning glory seeds provides context for the broader world of entheogenic plant cultivation
