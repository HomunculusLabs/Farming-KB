## Agaricus haemorrhoides

## Description

*[[agaricus]] haemorrhoides* (also known as *Agaricus moelleri* in some classifications) is a medium-sized saprotrophic basidiomycete fungus in the family Agaricaceae, order Agaricales. It is characterized by its whitish to greyish cap (5–10 cm) that is covered with grey-brown scales. The species name "haemorrhoides" refers to the distinctive **bright red or blood-red staining** that appears when the flesh is cut or bruised, particularly at the base of the stem. It has a strong, unpleasant phenol-like odor similar to ink or carbolic acid. Found in deciduous and coniferous forests across Europe and parts of North America.

## Key Facts

- Contains a mixture of **inorganic arsenic, methylarsonic acid (MA), dimethylarsenic acid (DMA), and arsenobetaine (AB)** in its fruiting bodies (Byrne et al., 1995).
- Shares the same arsenic compound profile as *A. placomyces* and *[[sarcodon-imbricatus]]*, suggesting a common arsenic metabolic pathway in these species.
- The presence of arsenobetaine (AB) — the same arsenic compound found in marine fish and crustaceans — in a terrestrial fungus is notable and biochemically significant.
- Known to accumulate heavy metals, including silver, cadmium, and copper, consistent with the general pattern of metal accumulation in the genus *Agaricus*.
- Considered **poisonous** by some authorities due to the presence of phenolic compounds (suggested by the phenol odor) that can cause gastrointestinal distress.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

*Agaricus haemorrhoides* contributes to understanding fungal arsenic chemistry:

- **Arsenic speciation**: The co-occurrence of four arsenic species in *A. haemorrhoides* demonstrates that terrestrial fungi possess complex arsenic metabolic pathways involving methylation, demethylation, and arsenobetaine synthesis.
- **Biochemical mystery**: The production of arsenobetaine (typically associated with marine organisms) by a terrestrial fungus raises questions about the evolutionary origins and ecological functions of this compound.
- **Toxicology**: As a potentially poisonous species that closely resembles edible *Agaricus* species (including the cultivated *A. bisporus*), it represents an identification challenge for foragers.
- **Environmental monitoring**: The red-staining reaction, while diagnostic for identification, may also reflect chemical responses to environmental metal exposure.
- **Section Placomyces chemistry**: The shared arsenic metabolic profile between *A. haemorrhoides* and *A. placomyces* suggests section-level biochemical traits in *Agaricus* that could be useful for chemotaxonomy.

The study of this species alongside *A. placomyces* has been instrumental in revealing the sophisticated arsenic transformation capabilities of common woodland fungi, with implications for food safety, bioremediation, and understanding arsenic cycling in terrestrial ecosystems.
