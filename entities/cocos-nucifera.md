It is cultivated throughout the humid tropics between 23°N and 23°S latitude, often described as the "tree of life" due to its extraordinary range of uses.

Over 80 million tonnes of coconuts are produced annually across more than 90 tropical countries, with Indonesia, the Philippines, and India as the leading producers.

Every part of the palm provides economic value, including the fruit (copra, oil, water, milk), trunk (timber), leaves (thatching, weaving), and husk (coir fiber).

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:** Kingdom Plantae → Arecales → Arecaceae → Arecoideae → Cocoeae → *Cocos* L.

*Cocos nucifera* L. (1753). The genus *Cocos* is monotypic, containing only this species.

Two major cultivar groups are recognized: the **Tall** type (cross-pollinating, heterozygous, hardier) and the **Dwarf** type (self-pollinating, homozygous, earlier-bearing).

**Morphology:** The [[oner-southeast-asian-sativa-heritage-thai-vietnamese-cambodian]] origin) and the Atlantic group (Indian Ocean origin), with subsequent human-mediated dispersal.

Coconuts spread across the Pacific through Austronesian voyaging beginning approximately 3,500 years ago, reaching Polynesia and Hawaii.

Portuguese and Spanish explorers facilitated further dispersal to the Caribbean, West Africa, and the Americas during the 16th century.

The species now occupies coastal sandy soils throughout the tropics and is naturalized on many islands and coastal plains.

## Cultivation

Coconut palms grow best in well-drained sandy or loamy soils within 20 km of the coast, at elevations below 600 m.

Optimal annual rainfall ranges from 1,000 to 2,500 mm, though established palms tolerate considerable seasonal drought.

Mean daily temperatures of 25–29°C are ideal, with minimum temperatures above 15°C and full sun exposure.

Palms begin bearing fruit 5–7 years after planting for Tall types and 3–4 years for Dwarf types, with productive life spans of 60–80 years.

Yields range from 30 to 100 nuts per palm per year depending on variety, management, and environmental conditions.

Major pests include the coconut rhinoceros beetle (*Oryctes rhinoceros*), coconut mite (*Aceria guerreronis*), and the lethal yellowing phytoplasma disease.
