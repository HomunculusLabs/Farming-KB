## Sanghuangporus villosus

## Overview

Sanghuangporus villosus is a perennial polypore fungus in the family Hymenochaetaceae, order Hymenochaetales. It is one of several species collectively known as "sanghuang" in traditional Chinese medicine, a group of woody bracket fungi valued for their potent anti-inflammatory, immunomodulatory, and antitumor properties.

The species was originally described in the genus Inonotus but was transferred to the newly erected genus Sanghuangporus following molecular phylogenetic studies. The genus name derives from "sanghuang," the Chinese term for these medicinally important mushrooms used for over two millennia.

Sanghuangporus villosus is distinguished from other sanghuang species by its velvety to tomentose pileus surface, relatively small pores, and specific substrate preferences. Like other sanghuang species, it forms perennial, woody, hoof-shaped fruiting bodies on the trunks of living or dead hardwood trees.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Sanghuangporus villosus (L.W. Zhou & Y.C. Dai) L.W. Zhou & Y.C. Dai was first described in 2012 as Inonotus villosus and transferred to Sanghuangporus in 2019. The genus Sanghuangporus currently contains approximately 14 species, most of which are found in East and Southeast Asia.

The basidiocarp is perennial, sessile to effused-reflexed, and woody-corky in consistency. The pileus surface is distinctly velvety to tomentose when young, becoming zonate and eventually glabrous with age. Color ranges from dark brown to nearly black on the upper surface.

The pore surface is initially reddish-brown to cinnamon, darkening with age. Pores are circular to angular, numbering 5-7 per millimeter. The context is duplex, with a thin, dark, crust-like upper layer and a lighter, fibrous lower layer. Tubes are stratified, indicating the perennial growth habit.

Microscopically, the hyphal system is dimitic, with generative hyphae bearing clamp connections and skeletal hyphae that are thick-walled and golden-brown in Melzer's reagent. Basidiospores are broadly ellipsoid to subglobose, hyaline, thick-walled, and measure 5-6.5 by 4.5-5.5 micrometers. Setal hyphae are present in the context and tubes.

## Distribution and Habitat

Sanghuangporus villosus is distributed in East Asia, with confirmed records from China, Japan, and Korea. In China, the species occurs primarily in temperate and subtropical forests of the eastern provinces, including Zhejiang, Fujian, Anhui, and Hunan.

The species is a white-rot saprotroph and facultative parasite, fruiting on the trunks and branches of broadleaf trees. Preferred hosts include species of Quercus, Castanopsis, and Betula. Fruit bodies typically develop on standing dead trees, fallen logs, and occasionally on living trees with compromised heartwood.

The fungus fruits in cooler months, typically from autumn through early spring in its native range. It requires high humidity and moderate temperatures for [[antifungal-secondary-metabolites-coprophilous-fungi]] consistent with the sanghuang group. Polysaccharides are the primary bioactive constituents, with beta-glucans representing the major immunomodulatory fraction. Water-soluble polysaccharides from the fruiting body show significant immunostimulatory activity.

Triterpenoids isolated from the species include inotodiol, trametenolic acid, and several lanostane-type triterpenes unique to the Sanghuangporus genus. These compounds demonstrate anti-inflammatory activity through inhibition of NF-kappaB signaling and suppression of pro-inflammatory cytokine production.

Polyphenolic compounds include hispidin and its derivatives, which exhibit potent antioxidant and antitumor properties. Hispidin analogs from sanghuang species have shown selective cytotoxicity against human cancer cell lines including hepatoma, breast cancer, and leukemia cells in vitro.

Protein-bound polysaccharides from Sanghuangporus species activate macrophages and enhance natural killer cell activity. This immunomodulatory profile underpins the traditional use of sanghuang as an adjunct therapy in cancer treatment, particularly in integrative oncology protocols combining conventional chemotherapy with medicinal mushroom extracts.

Hepatoprotective effects have been documented in animal models of chemically-induced liver injury, where sanghuang extracts reduced serum transaminase levels and improved histopathological scores. Antifibrotic activity through inhibition of hepatic stellate cell activation represents a promising research direction.

Antiviral activity against hepatitis B virus and human immunodeficiency virus has been reported for sanghuang-derived polysaccharides and triterpenoids. The proposed mechanisms include viral entry inhibition, reverse transcriptase suppression, and enhancement of host immune surveillance against viral-infected cells.

## Traditional Medicine

Sanghuang has been used in Chinese medicine for over 2,000 years, first recorded in the Shennong Bencao Jing circa 200 CE. The classical text describes sanghuang as a remedy for gynecological disorders, hemorrhage, and "stagnant blood," reflecting its traditional use as a blood-regulating herb.

In contemporary Chinese herbal medicine, sanghuang preparations are prescribed as immunomodulators, anti-inflammatory agents, and adjunctive cancer treatments. The fungus is commonly administered as a decoction of dried fruiting body slices, with typical dosages of 6-15 grams per day in divided doses.
