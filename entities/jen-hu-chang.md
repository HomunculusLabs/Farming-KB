## Jen-hu Chang

## Description

Jen-hu Chang is a researcher who in 1968 described and named the "clothesline
effect" — the phenomenon where advected dry hot air blowing into crop areas causes
accelerated water loss far exceeding normal evapotranspiration predictions. His
work is fundamental to understanding wind-irrigation interactions in arid
agriculture and is cited by Mollison as a key principle in desert settlement design.

## Classification

- **Category**: Person — agricultural climatologist
- **Field**: Agricultural meteorology, micrometeorology
- **Key publication**: 1968 paper on the [[permaculture-design]] manual
- Underpins windbreak spacing and depth calculations
- Demonstrated field size irrelevance under oasis effect

## Relevance to Cultivation

Provides the scientific basis for critical [[permaculture]] design decisions in arid
lands. Without understanding the [[clothesline-effect]], designers place ineffective
narrow windbreaks and wonder why crops still suffer excessive water loss. Chang's
work shows that perimeter defense is far more effective than distributed shelter.

## References

- Chang, Jen-hu (1968). Original research on the clothesline effect.
- Mollison, B. (1988). Permaculture: A Designers' Manual, p. 387.

Chang's 1968 research remains foundational reading for anyone designing
windbreak systems or planning desert agriculture at any scale.
