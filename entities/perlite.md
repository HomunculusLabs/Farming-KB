## Perlite

## Description

Perlite is a naturally occurring volcanic glass that, when heated to high
temperatures, expands into a lightweight, porous, bright-white granular
material. In [[mushroom-cultivation]], perlite is widely used as a passive
humidification medium due to its excellent water uptake and transpiration
properties. Unlike [[vermiculite]], perlite does not absorb and hold water
within its structure; instead, it wicks moisture through its porous surface
and gradually evaporates it into the surrounding air.

## Classification

- **Category**: Horticultural substrate component
- **Type**: Amorphous volcanic glass (expanded)
- **Industry**: Agriculture, horticulture, mycology
- **Appearance**: Bright white, lightweight granules

## Key Facts

- Perlite provides passive humidification in terrariums and fruiting chambers
  without the need for electric humidifiers.
- The material works by wicking water upward through [[soil-capillary-action-and-water-movement]]
  slowly evaporating it, maintaining high relative humidity (90%+ RH).
- A layer of wetted perlite at the bottom of a fruiting chamber can sustain
  adequate humidity for weeks, suitable for multi-flush growing cycles.
- Perlite does not actually absorb water like [[vermiculite]]; instead, water
  adheres to its porous surface and gradually transpires.
- In the [[pf-tek]] method, [[perlite-humidification]] was found to be more
  effective than aquarium bubbler systems alone.
- Cakes can be placed directly on the perlite layer, allowing additional
  wick-like moisture transfer directly to the substrate.
- Available at any plant shop or garden center, typically sold as a potting
  soil additive for aeration.
- The "Rice Krispies effect" describes the characteristic sound perlite makes
  when water is added, indicating its porous structure is active.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Perlite is one of the most important low-tech tools in home mushroom
cultivation. It enables growers to maintain the high humidity environment
(typically 95-100% RH) required for pinning and fruit body development
[[jadam-anti-complexity-farming-without-expensive-equipment]]. The [[pf-tek]] dual-chambered terrarium uses
perlite as its primary humidification method, demonstrating that effective
cultivation can be achieved with minimal investment. Perlite's inert nature
means it does not support mold growth or interact chemically with the
substrate, making it a clean and reliable humidification medium.

## Properties

- **pH**: Neutral (approximately 6.5-7.5)
- **Water retention**: Moderate surface retention; high evaporation rate
- **Sterilization**: Can be moistened with [[cervantes-hydrogen-peroxide-sterilization]] solution to
  reduce contamination
- **Reusability**: Can be rinsed and reused between grows
- **Safety**: Non-toxic, inert; dust can be an irritant (wear mask when
  handling dry)

## Usage in Cultivation

1. Pour a 1-2 inch layer of dry perlite into the bottom of the fruiting
   chamber.
2. Moisten with water (or diluted hydrogen peroxide) until saturated but
   not flooded.
3. Place substrate cakes or containers on top of the perlite layer.
4. Maintain the chamber by misting the perlite periodically to replenish
   moisture.
5. The perlite will provide continuous passive humidification for weeks.

## Perlite vs Vermiculite

While both are mineral-based horticultural additives, perlite and
[[vermiculite]] serve fundamentally different functions in cultivation.
Perlite excels at aeration and surface evaporation, making it ideal for
[[pf-tek-dual-chambered-terrarium-design-humidity-management]]. [[vermiculite]] absorbs and retains water within its
layered structure, making it better suited as a substrate moisture reservoir.
In the [[pf-tek]] method, [[vermiculite]] is mixed with [[brown-rice-flour]]
to form the substrate cake, while perlite lines the fruiting chamber floor
for humidification. Using perlite in the substrate or [[vermiculite]] in the
humidification layer would produce inferior results.

## Sourcing and Grades

Perlite is available in several grades, from fine to coarse. Coarse-grade
perlite is preferred [[grass-seed-substrate-for-mushroom-cultivation]] humidification because the
larger particles provide better airflow and evaporation rates. Fine-grade
perlite can compact and reduce humidity output. Most garden center perlite
is suitable, though some products contain added fertilizers that should be
avoided. Unscented, additive-free horticultural perlite is the standard.

## Hydrogen Peroxide Enhancement

Many cultivators moisten perlite with a diluted hydrogen peroxide solution
(typically 3% H2O2 diluted 1:10 with water) instead of plain water. The
peroxide provides mild antiseptic properties that help suppress airborne
contaminant spores in the fruiting chamber. This technique is especially
useful in the [[pf-tek]] method, where the fruiting chamber is opened
regularly for misting and fanning, exposing the interior to potential
contamination.
