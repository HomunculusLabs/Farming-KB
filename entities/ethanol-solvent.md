## Ethanol (Ethyl Alcohol)

## Description

Ethanol (C₂H₅OH), also called ethyl alcohol or pure grain alcohol, is a versatile polar solvent with a boiling point of 78.5°C. In cannabis and natural product extraction, ethanol is considered one of the most desirable solvents due to its extraction properties being similar to methanol but with significantly lower toxicity. However, its availability is restricted because it is the primary active ingredient in alcoholic beverages and is subject to heavy taxation and regulatory control in most jurisdictions.

## Classification

- **Category**: Chemical / Solvent
- **Chemical type**: Primary alcohol (two carbon)
- **Molecular formula**: C₂H₅OH
- **Molecular weight**: 46.07 g/mol
- **Boiling point**: 78.5°C
- **CAS number**: 64-17-5
- **Appearance**: Colorless liquid with characteristic odor

## Key Facts

- Produces clean cannabis extracts with extraction properties very similar to methanol, but without the severe toxicity concerns of methanol.
- Difficult to obtain in pure form because it is heavily taxed as the active ingredient in liquor. Pure ethanol requires special permits or licenses in most countries.
- Denatured ethanol, widely available at hardware stores and pharmacies, contains non-removable toxic additives (such as methanol, isopropanol, or methyl ethyl ketone) that evaporate at the same temperature as pure ethanol, making it permanently unfit for human consumption.
- Used in the isomerization process: cannabis oil is dissolved in absolute ethanol (1g oil : 10g solvent) with sulfuric acid catalyst to convert cannabidiol to THC and promote higher-rotating isomers.
- 95% ethanol was used by Roger Adams in his landmark 1940 countercurrent extraction of wild Minnesota hemp, producing concentrated solutions of 23.1 gm solids per 100 cc.
- Pure ethanol may be produced from liquor or fermented material through [[cannabis-reefers-and-tincture-preparation]]**: Ethanol is the standard solvent for making medicinal mushroom tinctures (dual extraction with water). Tinctures of Reishi, Lion's Mane, Chaga, and Turkey Tail all use ethanol to extract triterpenes, sterols, and other non-water-soluble compounds.

## Safety Considerations

- **Flammability**: Flash point of 13°C (55°F). Highly flammable — use away from open flames and sparks.
- **Toxicity**: Low acute toxicity compared to methanol, but ingestion of large amounts causes alcohol poisoning. Denatured forms are toxic.
- **Water content**: Ethanol containing water (e.g., 95%) co-extracts water-soluble tars and plant constituents, producing a less pure extract with lower potency by weight.
- **Absolute (anhydrous) ethanol**: Required for isomerization reactions where water would interfere with the acid catalyst. Denatured ethanol may be used if it does not contain water.
