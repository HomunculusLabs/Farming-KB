# S-Adenosylmethionine
S-Adenosylmethionine is a ubiquitous sulfonium metabolite and one of biology's main activated methyl donors.
It is commonly abbreviated SAM, SAMe, or AdoMet, depending on biochemical, clinical, or supplement context.
The molecule links ATP, methionine, sulfur metabolism, and one-carbon transfer chemistry in a single cofactor.
It is especially relevant to [[staycare-fungal-biomethylation-of-metals-and-metalloids]], where SAM-dependent methyl transfer helps explain volatile organometalloid formation.
## Core Identity
- Entity class: small-molecule metabolite and enzymatic cosubstrate.
- PubChem CID: 34755.
- Molecular formula: C15H22N6O5S.
- Molecular weight: approximately 398.4 g/mol.
- Chemical family: sulfonium betaine derived from methionine and adenosine.
- Physiological role: human metabolite and universal methyl-group donor.
- Common biological abbreviation: SAM.
- Commercial spelling: SAMe or SAM-e.
## Structure and Activation
SAM is formed when the sulfur atom of methionine is bonded to the adenosyl group from ATP.
This creates a positively charged sulfonium center.
The sulfonium center makes the attached methyl group chemically activated.
Enzymes can then transfer that methyl group to oxygen, nitrogen, sulfur, carbon, and other acceptor atoms.
The molecule stores transfer potential rather than simply acting as a passive carbon source.
Its adenosyl portion helps enzymes recognize and position the molecule in active sites.
Its methionine-derived portion supplies the transferable methyl group.
After methyl donation, SAM becomes S-adenosylhomocysteine.
S-adenosylhomocysteine must be cleared because it can inhibit many methyltransferases.
## Biosynthesis
SAM is synthesized from methionine and ATP.
The enzyme class responsible is methionine adenosyltransferase.
The reaction is unusual because all three phosphate groups of ATP are displaced during sulfonium compound formation.
Most organisms maintain tight regulation of SAM pools.
High SAM can signal methionine sufficiency.
Low SAM can limit methylation capacity and disrupt downstream sulfur metabolism.
In mammals, the liver is a major site of SAM production and consumption.
In microbes, SAM availability is coupled to methionine and cysteine metabolism.
In plants, SAM also feeds hormone biosynthesis pathways.
## Methyl Donor Functions
SAM participates in dozens of methyltransferase reactions.
Major methylated targets include DNA, RNA, proteins, phospholipids, hormones, and secondary metabolites.
DNA methyltransferases use SAM to place methyl groups on cytosine or adenine bases.
Histone methyltransferases use SAM to modify lysine or arginine residues in chromatin.
RNA methyltransferases use SAM to tune tRNA, rRNA, and mRNA stability and function.
Small-molecule methyltransferases use SAM to alter solubility, volatility, reactivity, and toxicity of metabolites.
These reactions connect SAM to gene regulation, development, detoxification, and cellular identity.
## Role in Biomethylation
SAM is the methyl donor invoked in many models of biological methylation of metals and metalloids.
In fungal biomethylation, methyl groups can be transferred onto arsenic, selenium, tellurium, mercury, or related substrates.
The products often become more volatile than their inorganic precursors.
For arsenic, sequential methylation can lead toward methylarsonic species, dimethylarsinic species, and ultimately [[trimethylarsine]].
For selenium, methylation can produce dimethyl selenide and dimethyl diselenide.
For tellurium, analogous reactions can produce dimethyl telluride compounds.
The same general logic appears in microbial, fungal, and plant volatilization systems.
Enzymes and intermediates differ across organisms, so SAM should not be treated as proof of a single universal pathway.
SAM acts as the biochemical bridge between central metabolism and metalloid transformation.
## Transsulfuration and Polyamine Links
SAM is not only a methyl donor.
It is connected to transsulfuration through S-adenosylhomocysteine and homocysteine metabolism.
Homocysteine can be remethylated to methionine, closing the methionine cycle.
Alternatively, homocysteine can enter cysteine and glutathione synthesis.
SAM can also be decarboxylated for aminopropylation reactions.
Those reactions contribute to polyamine biosynthesis.
Polyamines such as spermidine and spermine support growth, translation, and stress responses.
Thus SAM sits at the junction of methylation, sulfur balance, antioxidant capacity, and cellular proliferation.
## Regulatory Importance
Because SAM reflects methionine and ATP status, cells use it as a metabolic signal.
In bacteria, SAM can bind riboswitches that regulate genes for methionine or cysteine biosynthesis.
In eukaryotes, SAM availability influences epigenetic methylation capacity.
Diet, stress, disease, and microbial metabolism can all affect SAM-dependent pathways.
Excessive methylation capacity can be as biologically consequential as insufficient methylation capacity.
The SAM to S-adenosylhomocysteine ratio is often treated as an indicator of cellular methylation potential.
## Plant and Fungal Relevance
In plants, SAM is a precursor for ethylene biosynthesis.
Ethylene regulates ripening, senescence, stress response, and developmental signaling.
In fungi, SAM-dependent methylation contributes to secondary metabolism, detoxification, and environmental transformations.
Fungal production of volatile organometalloids depends on access to methyl donor chemistry of this kind.
That makes SAM relevant to [[phytovolatilization]], [[fungal-metal-redox-transformations-detailed]], and bioremediation pages.
SAM also helps connect biochemical methylation to practical questions about contaminant mobility.
## Medical and Supplement Context
SAMe is sold in some jurisdictions as a dietary supplement or medicinal product.
It has been studied in relation to depression, osteoarthritis, and liver disease.
Evidence and regulatory status vary by country and indication.
The biochemical importance of SAM should not be confused with proof that supplementation benefits every condition associated with methylation.
Clinical use requires attention to interactions, psychiatric history, pregnancy, and underlying disease.
The wiki should keep supplement claims separate from core biochemical functions.
