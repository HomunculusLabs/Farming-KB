# Gentiana pneumonanthe

## Overview

Gentiana pneumonanthe, the marsh gentian, is an erect perennial herb producing striking deep-blue trumpet flowers in late summer and autumn. It grows in wet heathlands, bogs, and fen meadows across temperate Europe and western Asia.

The species is notable as the larval host plant of the Alcon blue butterfly (*Phengaris alcon*), a globally threatened lycaenid. This ecological relationship makes Gentiana pneumonanthe a flagship species for wet heathland conservation.

Marsh gentian has declined dramatically across its European range due to drainage of wetlands, agricultural intensification, and abandonment of traditional heathland management. It is legally protected in most European countries where it occurs.

The species is listed on the IUCN Red List as Least Concern globally, but many national Red Lists classify it as Vulnerable or Endangered. In the United Kingdom, it is a Priority Species under the UK Biodiversity Action Plan.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Scientific classification:**
- Kingdom: Plantae
- Family: Gentianaceae
- Genus: *Gentiana*
- Species: *G. pneumonanthe* L.
- Authority: Carl Linnaeus (1753)

**Synonyms:** *Gentiana linearis* Froel., *Gentiana nana* Gilib.

Plants grow 15–60 cm tall with erect, unbranched stems arising from a short, stocky rhizome. Leaves are opposite, linear-lanceolate, 3–8 cm long, with a pronounced midrib and slightly inrolled margins.

Flowers are solitary or in clusters of 2–5 at stem apices, campanulate, 3–5 cm long. The corolla is deep blue to violet-blue with five spreading lobes and conspicuous greenish spots inside the throat.

The calyx tube is 12–20 mm long with five narrow lanceolate lobes. Capsules are ellipsoid, splitting by two valves to release numerous small winged seeds approximately 1.5 mm long.

Flowering occurs from July to October, making it one of the latest-flowering European gentians. Late flowering is an adaptation to wet heathland habitats where competitive grasses senesce early.

The rhizome system is more extensive than in alpine gentians, producing both flowering and non-flowering shoots. This clonal growth allows persistence under unfavorable conditions.

## Distribution and Habitat

Gentiana pneumonanthe occurs across temperate Europe from Ireland and Portugal east to the Urals and the Caucasus. Its northern limit reaches southern Scandinavia and the Baltic states.

The species is restricted to wet, oligotrophic habitats including lowland heaths, valley bogs, transition mires, and damp acid grasslands. It requires seasonally waterlogged, acidic to neutral soils (pH 4.5–6.5).

Key habitat requirements include low [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]] and northeastern states with suitable acidic, moist conditions.

## Ecological Role

The most significant ecological relationship is the obligate dependency of the Alcon blue butterfly (*Phengaris alcon*) on Gentiana pneumonanthe as its sole larval food plant. Female butterflies lay eggs on flower buds, and caterpillars feed on developing seeds.

After the third instar, Alcon blue caterpillars drop to the ground where they are adopted by *Myrmica* ants and carried into the ant nest. The caterpillars complete development by feeding on ant brood, a remarkable example of social parasitism.

This tripartite relationship (plant–butterfly–ant) is one of the most studied [[fungal-conservation-biology]]. Loss of any one component causes cascade failures in the system.
