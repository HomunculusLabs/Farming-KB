# Ephedrine

Ephedrine is a naturally occurring alkaloid and sympathomimetic amine
found primarily in plants of the Ephedra genus (Ephedraceae). It has
been used in traditional Chinese medicine for over 5,000 years under
the name ma huang and remains one of the most pharmacologically
significant plant-derived stimulants. Chemically classified as a
phenethylamine, ephedrine acts directly and indirectly on adrenergic
receptors, producing effects including bronchodilation, vasoconstriction,
and central nervous system stimulation. It is a structural precursor to
methamphetamine and has been subject to extensive regulatory control due
to its role as a clandestine synthesis precursor.

Ephedrine contains two chiral centers, yielding four stereoisomers:
(1R,2S)-ephedrine, (1S,2R)-ephedrine, (1R,2R)-pseudoephedrine, and
(1S,2S)-pseudoephedrine. Natural ephedrine from Ephedra species is
primarily the (1R,2S) enantiomer, also known as L-ephedrine. The
diastereomer pseudoephedrine differs in pharmacological potency and
is widely used as a nasal decongestant.

## Biosynthesis and Natural Sources

Ephedrine is biosynthesized in Ephedra species through the condensation
of phenylpyruvic acid with an amine donor via a transamination pathway.
The pathway proceeds through phenylacetaldoxime intermediates and
involves specific P450 enzymes and transaminases unique to the
Ephedraceae family.
