## Overview

Potato Dextrose Agar (PDA) is one of the most widely used solid nutrient media in mycology for cultivating the mycelia of higher fungi. It consists of potato extract, dextrose (glucose), and agar, providing a balanced nutrient profile that supports robust mycelial growth. PDA is a standard medium for growing stock inocula of mushroom species including *[[brassinosteroid-morphological-effects-psilocybe-cubensis-fruiting]]*, *[[agaricus-bisporus]]*, and many others.

## Classification

- **Type**: Microbiological growth medium
- **Category**: Solid agar medium
- **Primary use**: Cultivation of fungal mycelia
- **Availability**: Pre-mixed from scientific supply companies or homemade

## Composition

### Standard Recipe

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Potatoes | 250 g |
| Agar | 15 g |
| Dextrose | 10 g |
| Nutritional yeast (or yeast extract) | 1.5 g |
| Water | 1 liter |

### Optional Enrichment

- **Yeast extract / nutritional yeast**: 1.5 g — provides additional proteins and B vitamins for enhanced mycelial vigor. This addition is recommended by Oss & Oeric for both PDA and MEA media.

## Preparation Procedure

1. **Shred potatoes** into a colander and rinse for 30 seconds with cold tap water
2. **Boil potatoes** in 1 liter of water for 30 minutes at a gentle boil
3. **Filter broth** through muslin or cheesecloth; discard the potato solids
4. **Add dry ingredients** (pre-weighed and mixed agar, dextrose, and yeast) to the hot potato broth while stirring gently
5. **Gently boil** for 10 minutes or until the solution is clear, taking care not to allow boilover
6. **Adjust volume** by adding water to return to 1 liter total
7. **Pour** into petri plates, baby food jars, or slant culture tubes while still hot — fill to approximately ¼ inch depth in plates, or ⅓ full in tubes
8. **Sterilize** at 15–20 lbs pressure for 45 minutes to 1 hour

## Sterilization

Proper sterilization is critical:

- **Pressure**: 15–20 lbs (corresponding to approximately 250°F / 121°C)
- **Duration**: 45 minutes to 1 hour (longer than the standard 15 minutes to ensure complete sterilization)
- **Steam management**: Allow a strong head of steam to build up in the pressure cooker before closing the stopcock — this ensures wet heat sterilization rather than dry heat, which is less effective
- **Cooling**: Allow the cooker to cool to room temperature before opening the stopcock, to prevent the medium from boiling over
- **Plastic plates**: If using pre-sterilized plastic plates, pour the medium after sterilizing — do not autoclave plastic plates

## Storage

- Unused sterilized medium in sealed flasks can be stored in the refrigerator indefinitely
- Stored medium can be reliquified over heat and reused
- Prepared plates should be used within a reasonable timeframe to prevent dehydration

## Usage in Mushroom Cultivation

### Stock Inocula
PDA is used to grow and maintain dikaryotic mycelial cultures that serve as the source of inoculum for [[grain-spawn-production]]. A small piece of mycelial tissue or mycelium-covered agar is transferred to fresh PDA plates, where the mycelium grows radially across the surface, covering it completely within 7–20 days.

### Culture Characteristics
- **Healthy mycelium**: Pure white, fluffy/thread-like appearance, growing radially from inoculation point
- **Growth time**: 3–5 days for initial growth to be visible; 7–20 days for complete plate coverage
- **Contamination indicators**: Blue-green, black, yellow, or dirty-gray growths appearing as dots with colored centers, growing more rapidly than the white mycelium

### Relationship to MEA
Oss & Oeric recommend alternating between PDA and [[malt-extract-agar]] (MEA) when preparing batches of plates. This practice:

- Prevents the mycelium from becoming accustomed to one type of medium
- Forces the fungus to use different parts of its genome in adapting to different nutrient profiles
- Helps prevent "senescence factor" — the tendency of mycelium to age physiologically and lose vigor over time on a single medium type

## Comparison with Malt Extract Agar

| Property | PDA | MEA |
|---|---|---|
| Primary nutrient source | Potato starch/dextrose | Malt extract |
| Preparation complexity | Moderate (requires boiling potatoes) | Simple (dissolve in water) |
| Growth vigor | Good | Excellent (may promote faster colonization) |
| Recommended for | General cultivation, stock maintenance | Vigorous growth, [[grain-spawn-preparation]] |
| pH | Slightly acidic | Slightly acidic to neutral |

Oss & Oeric note that MEA is preferable to PDA when preparing cultures for grain inoculation, as the richer nutrient profile of MEA promotes more vigorous mycelial growth.
