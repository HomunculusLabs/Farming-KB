## Description

[[soil-ph|soil-ph]], making it particularly valuable for treating sodic (sodium-rich) clays and improving soil structure in alkaline conditions.

## Classification

- **Category**: Soil amendment / Mineral fertiliser
- **Chemical formula**: CaSO4·2H2O
- **Type**: Calcium sulphate (does not raise pH significantly)
- **Appearance**: White to grey crystalline mineral
- **Solubility**: Moderately soluble in water

## Key Facts

1. **Sodic clay treatment**: Gypsum is the primary remedy for sodium-rich clays that seal on the surface in light rains and "melt" when wet. Applied at 2–3 handfuls per square metre.
2. **Sodium displacement**: Gypsum works by displacing sodium ions from clay particles with calcium ions. The displaced sodium is then leached away as sodium sulphate by flushing with fresh water.
3. **pH neutral**: Unlike limestone, gypsum does not raise soil pH significantly, making it safe for use on alkaline soils that already have high pH.
4. **Clay improvement**: Calcium from gypsum acts as a flocculating agent, improving soil structure by binding clay particles into larger aggregates, improving aeration and water penetration.
5. **Cracking clays**: For deep cracking clays and lumpy soils, the recommended treatment is a sand layer, scattered gypsum at a handful per square metre, plus mulch.
6. **Salty soil remediation**: On alkaline salty soils, gypsum is applied followed by flushing with fresh tank water to remove sodium as sodium sulphate.
7. **Application rate**: Typically 1–3 handfuls per square metre (approximately 50–150 g/m²), depending on soil severity.
8. **Complementary use**: Used alongside deep drainage (1–2 m), organic matter incorporation, and mulching for comprehensive soil rehabilitation.

## Application Protocols

- **Sealing clays**: Add gypsum at 2–3 handfuls/m² and flush with fresh water to remove sodium as sulphate
- **Cracking clays**: Add sand layer, scatter gypsum at 1 handful/m², and mulch heavily
- **Alkaline salty soils**: Apply gypsum, flush with fresh water, continue using lime if needed
- **Deep sands**: Not typically needed; use clay and mulch instead

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Soil structure improvement**: By improving clay structure, gypsum creates better habitat for soil fungi and other microorganisms through improved aeration and water movement.
- **Mycorrhizal benefit**: Displacing sodium and improving soil structure directly benefits mycorrhizal fungal networks that struggle in sodic, poorly structured soils.
- **Compost integration**: Gypsum can be added to compost piles as a source of calcium and sulphur, supporting the microbial community during decomposition.
- **Drainage enhancement**: Improved soil structure from gypsum treatment enhances drainage, preventing the waterlogged conditions that inhibit fungal activity.
- **Reclamation tool**: Essential for reclaiming degraded, salinised soils for productive cultivation including mushroom production.
