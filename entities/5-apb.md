## Overview

**5-APB** (6-aminobenzofuran; 1-benzofuran-5-ylpropan-2-amine) is a synthetic
entactogenic compound of the benzofuran class. It acts primarily as a serotonin
releaser and reuptake inhibitor, producing subjective effects broadly similar to
MDMA including euphoria, empathy, and mild stimulation.

The compound emerged as a designer drug in the United Kingdom around 2010, marketed
initially as a "research chemical" and sold through online vendors. It was developed
as an alternative to MDMA following MDMA's scheduling and the scheduling of related
compounds such as mephedone and methylone.

5-APB is structurally related to MDMA, with the benzodioxole ring replaced by a
benzofuran ring system. This substitution alters the pharmacokinetic profile,
including increased metabolic stability and a longer duration of action compared
to MDMA.

## Chemical Properties

- **IUPAC name:** 1-(1-benzofuran-5-yl)propan-2-amine
- **Molecular formula:** C₁₁H₁₃NO
- **Molecular weight:** 175.23 g/mol
- **CAS number:** 286834-85-3
- **Appearance:** White to off-white crystalline powder

The benzofuran scaffold differentiates 5-APB from the classical phenethylamine
and amphetamine structures. The furan oxygen occupies the 1-position of the fused
ring system, while the 5-position bears the aminopropyl side chain.

The compound is a free base at physiological pH. Its hydrochloride salt is more
commonly encountered in powdered form due to improved stability and solubility.
The hydrochloride form has a reported melting point of approximately 142–144°C.

## Pharmacology

5-APB functions as a **triple monoamine releaser** with preferential activity at the
serotonin transporter (SERT). Its primary mechanism involves reversal of SERT,
VMAT2, and to a lesser extent the dopamine transporter (DAT) and norepinephrine
transporter (NET).

In vitro studies indicate the following approximate rank order of potency:
SERT > DAT > NET. This serotonin-dominant profile is responsible for the
characteristic entactogenic effects, which closely parallel those of MDMA.

The compound shows moderate affinity for 5-HT₂A, 5-HT₂B, and 5-HT₂C receptors as
an agonist. Activity at 5-HT₂B is a noted safety concern, as chronic agonism at
this receptor is associated with valvular heart disease — the same mechanism
implicated in the fenfluramine/phen-fen cardiac toxicity cases.

## Effects

The subjective effect profile of 5-APB closely resembles that of MDMA:

- **Onset:** 30–60 minutes following oral administration
- **Duration:** 5–8 hours (significantly longer than MDMA's 3–5 hours)
- **Comeup:** Characterized by tingling sensations, pupil dilation, and increased
  heart rate; may include mild nausea in some users
- **Peak effects:** Last approximately 2–3 hours; include strong euphoria,
  emotional openness, enhanced tactile sensation, and moderate stimulation

The comedown phase is noted for being smoother than MDMA's, with less acute
serotonin depletion in the immediate aftermath. However, the longer duration means
users may experience more fatigue the following day.

Common after-effects include residual stimulation, difficulty sleeping (if dosed
late), mild mood suppression for 1–3 days, and reduced appetite. These effects are
consistent with post-synaptic serotonin depletion following robust release.

## Dosage

- **Threshold:** 50–70 mg (oral)
- **Light dose:** 70–100 mg
- **Common dose:** 100–130 mg
- **Strong dose:** 130–160 mg
- **Heavy dose:** 160+ mg (not recommended)

Oral administration is the most common route. Insufflation produces faster onset
(10–20 minutes) but significantly shorter duration (3–4 hours) and more intense
nasal discomfort due to the benzofuran structure.

Redosing is common due to the long duration, but each additional dose shows
diminishing returns and increases cardiovascular strain. Harm reduction guidance
advises waiting at minimum 2–3 months between uses.

## Legal Status

5-APB is controlled as a Class B drug in the United Kingdom under the
Psychoactive Substances Act amendments (2014). In the United States, it is a
Schedule I substance under the Federal Analog Act and is specifically listed on
the DEA's Schedule I controlled substances.

The compound is also controlled in China, Germany, Australia, New Zealand, and
most EU member states. Its scheduling generally falls under designer drug or new
psychoactive substance legislation rather than traditional drug scheduling
frameworks.

## Related Compounds

- **6-APB** (6-aminobenzofuran) — positional isomer with slightly different
  receptor affinity profile; generally considered more euphoric and less
  stimulating
- **5-MAPB** — 5-(2-aminopropyl)benzofuran N-methylated analogue; more
  serotonergic, closer to MDMA in character
- **MDMA** — the prototype entactogen to which all benzofurans are compared
- **Methylone** — cathinone-derived entactogen with similar triple releaser profile
- **6-MAPB** — N-methylated 6-APB; longer duration, more empathogenic quality
