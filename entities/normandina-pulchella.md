## Overview

**Normandina pulchella**, commonly known as the shell lichen or button lichen, is a small foliose lichen in the family Parmeliaceae.

It is widespread in [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]], and parts of Asia.

The species forms distinctive rounded, shield-like thalli that superficially resemble tiny seashells on bark and rock surfaces.

It is easily recognized by its pale gray to white coloration, button-shaped lobes, and frequent sorediate margins.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Normandina pulchella** (Borrer) Nyl. was originally described as *Lichen pulchellus* by William Borrer and later transferred to *Normandina* by William Nylander.

The genus *Normandina* is monotypic, containing only this single species.

Some authorities have placed it in *Physcia* or *Pannaria*, but molecular phylogenetic studies support its placement in Parmeliaceae as a distinct genus.

The thallus forms small, orbicular rosettes typically 5–20 mm in diameter.

Individual lobes are 1–3 mm wide, convex, and closely appressed to the substrate.

The upper surface is pale gray to whitish, often with a pruinose or powdery coating.

The lower surface is pale with sparse, simple rhizines.

Apothecia are rare but when present are lecanorine, with a pale thalline margin and brown disc.

Soralia develop along the lobe margins, producing coarse, farinose soredia for vegetative reproduction.

The photobiont partner is a green alga of the genus *Trebouxia*.

Spores are hyaline, ellipsoid, and approximately 10–14 × 6–8 μm in size.

## Distribution and Habitat

**Normandina pulchella** has a broad distribution across temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere.

In Europe it occurs from Scandinavia and the British Isles south to the Mediterranean basin.

It is found throughout much of North America, particularly in the eastern and Pacific coastal states.

The species also occurs in temperate regions of [[air-pollution-fungal-community-responses]].

Elevation range extends from near sea level to approximately 1500 m in mountainous areas.

## Chemistry

**Normandina pulchella** is characterized by the presence of zeorin as its primary medullary compound.

The cortex contains atranorin, which gives the upper surface its grayish coloration.

Spot tests typically yield K+ yellow (cortex) and C– and KC– (medulla), with P– reactions.

Thin-layer chromatography confirms zeorin as the diagnostic [[fungal-elicitors-enhanced-secondary-metabolite-production]].

The species does not produce usnic acid, distinguishing it from many superficially similar Parmeliaceae members.

The zeorin chemotype is consistent across its geographic range, with little chemical variation reported.

Some specimens may contain trace amounts of atranorin degradation products or additional triterpenes.
