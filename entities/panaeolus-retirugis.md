## Panaeolus retirugis

Panaeolus retirugis is a psychoactive mushroom in the family
Panaeolaceae, known to contain psilocybin and related tryptamine
compounds. It is a coprophilous (dung-inhabiting) species found
primarily in tropical and subtropical regions. The species was
originally described from African specimens and has since been
reported across parts of Asia, Oceania, and the Americas. Though
less well-known than [[gartz-panaeolus-subbalteatus-taxonomy-and-intoxication-history]] complex
- **Type locality:** Tropical Africa
- **Notes:** The genus Panaeolus is characterized by
  mottled gills (spotted with mature and immature spores
  simultaneously), a feature visible in P. retirugis
## Description
### Cap
- Size: 15–40 mm diameter
- Shape: Convex to broadly convex, sometimes slightly
  umbonate at maturity
- Surface: Smooth to finely wrinkled (rugose), hygrophanous;
  drying from a dark brown to a pale tan or greyish-brown
- Margin: Slightly striate when moist, often with remnants
  of the partial veil at the margin in young specimens
- Color: Dark brown when wet, fading to greyish-brown or
  pale fawn as it dries
### Gills
- Attachment: Adnexed to adnate, moderately close
- Color: Grey initially, developing a distinctive mottled
  pattern as spores mature (characteristic of Panaeolus)
- Edge: Even, becoming darkened with mature spore deposits
### Stem (Stipe)
- Length: 40–80 mm
- Thickness: 2–4 mm
- Structure: Hollow, equal or slightly thickened at the base
- Surface: Smooth, finely pruinose near the apex, pale to
  brownish
- Ring: Absent or evanescent, sometimes leaving a faint
  annular zone

- **Substrate:** Exclusively coprophilous, found on the dung
  of herbivorous mammals (cattle, horses, elephants)
- **Seasonality:** Fruiting during warm, wet seasons; most
  commonly observed in the rainy periods of tropical climates
- **Habitat:** Open grasslands, pastures, and savannas where
  herbivore dung is abundant; rarely in forested areas
- **Frequency:** Widespread within range but locally
  uncommon; typically found as scattered individuals or
  small groups rather than in large clusters
