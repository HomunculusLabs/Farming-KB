# Panaeolus retirugis

Panaeolus retirugis is a psychoactive mushroom in the family
Panaeolaceae, known to contain psilocybin and related tryptamine
compounds. It is a coprophilous (dung-inhabiting) species found
primarily in tropical and subtropical regions. The species was
originally described from African specimens and has since been
reported across parts of Asia, Oceania, and the Americas. Though
less well-known than Panaeolus cyanescens (Copelandia cyanescens),
P. retirugis occupies a similar ecological niche on herbivore dung
and is recognized among psychotropic fungi surveys for its
documented neurotropic chemistry. The species epithet "retirugis"
refers to the characteristic wrinkled or rugose surface features
of the cap, which help distinguish it from close relatives in the
genus.
## Taxonomy and Naming
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Panaeolaceae
- **Genus:** Panaeolus (Fr.) Quélet
- **Species:** P. retirugis
- **Authority:** Originally described from tropical Africa
- **Synonyms:** Historically placed in broader Panaeolus
  circumscriptions; some authors have considered it closely
  allied to the Panaeolus subbalteatus complex
- **Type locality:** Tropical Africa
- **Notes:** The genus Panaeolus is characterized by
  mottled gills (spotted with mature and immature spores
  simultaneously), a feature visible in P. retirugis
## Description
### Cap
- Size: 15–40 mm diameter
- Shape: Convex to broadly convex, sometimes slightly
  umbonate at maturity
- Surface: Smooth to finely wrinkled (rugose), hygrophanous;
  drying from a dark brown to a pale tan or greyish-brown
- Margin: Slightly striate when moist, often with remnants
  of the partial veil at the margin in young specimens
- Color: Dark brown when wet, fading to greyish-brown or
  pale fawn as it dries
### Gills
- Attachment: Adnexed to adnate, moderately close
- Color: Grey initially, developing a distinctive mottled
  pattern as spores mature (characteristic of Panaeolus)
- Edge: Even, becoming darkened with mature spore deposits
### Stem (Stipe)
- Length: 40–80 mm
- Thickness: 2–4 mm
- Structure: Hollow, equal or slightly thickened at the base
- Surface: Smooth, finely pruinose near the apex, pale to
  brownish
- Ring: Absent or evanescent, sometimes leaving a faint
  annular zone
### Spores and Microscopy
| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| Spore print | Black |
| Spore shape | Ellipsoid to lemon-shaped |
| Spore size | 12–16 x 8–10 µm |
| Spore wall | Smooth, thick-walled |
| Germ pore | Present, distinct |
| Basidia | Two- and four-spored |
| Cheilocystidia | Ventricose, sometimes with a narrow neck |
| Pleurocystidia | Rare or absent |
## Distribution and Habitat
- **Geographic range:** Tropical and subtropical Africa, parts
  of Southeast Asia, and reported in Oceania; occasional
  reports from Central and South America
- **Substrate:** Exclusively coprophilous, found on the dung
  of herbivorous mammals (cattle, horses, elephants)
- **Seasonality:** Fruiting during warm, wet seasons; most
  commonly observed in the rainy periods of tropical climates
- **Habitat:** Open grasslands, pastures, and savannas where
  herbivore dung is abundant; rarely in forested areas
- **Frequency:** Widespread within range but locally
  uncommon; typically found as scattered individuals or
  small groups rather than in large clusters
## Chemistry and Active Compounds
Panaeolus retirugis is confirmed to contain psychoactive
tryptamine alkaloids, placing it among the neurotropic fungi
surveyed by Guzmán, Allen, and Gartz. The principal active
compounds detected include:
- **Psilocybin:** The primary psychoactive compound;
  concentrations reported are moderate compared to other
  Panaeolus species, generally in the range of 0.1–0.4%
  of dry weight
- **Psilocin:** Present in lower concentrations than
  psilocybin; some degradation of psilocybin to psilocin
  may occur in dried specimens
- **Baeocystin:** Minor amounts detected in some analyses
- **Norbaeocystin:** Trace amounts reported
- **Note:** Potency data should be interpreted with caution
  as many published analyses are from herbarium specimens
  stored for extended periods, which significantly
  underestimate fresh potency

## Field Identification Tips
- Look for the characteristic mottled gill pattern that
  defines the genus Panaeolus
- The finely wrinkled cap surface (rugose texture) is a key
  distinguishing feature of this species
- The black spore print separates Panaeolus from many other
  dung-inhabiting genera
- B bruising may be subtle or absent, unlike the more
  dramatic blueing seen in Copelandia cyanescens
- Always confirm identification with microscopy, as several
  Panaeolus species are morphologically very similar
- The combination of rugose cap, moderate size, and dung
  habitat narrows identification considerably
## Conservation Status
Not formally assessed by the IUCN. As a coprophilous species
dependent on herbivore dung, populations are likely stable
wherever livestock or wild herbivores are present. No
specific conservation concerns have been identified for this
species. Habitat loss affecting grazing mammals could
indirectly impact local populations.
