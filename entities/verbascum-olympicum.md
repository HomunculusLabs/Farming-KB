## Overview

**Verbascum olympicum**, commonly known as **Olympus mullein** or **Greek mullein**, is a striking biennial or short-lived perennial in the family Plantaginaceae. It is native to the mountains of western Turkey, particularly the Olympus region, where it grows on dry, stony slopes and open pine forests.

The species is prized in horticulture for its enormous, branching flower panicles covered in bright yellow blossoms. A single flowering stem can produce hundreds of flowers over a 6–8 week period in mid-summer, creating a spectacular candelabra effect.

## Distribution and Habitat

Verbascum olympicum is endemic to northwestern Turkey, primarily in the Uludağ (Mount Olympus of Bithynia) region and surrounding mountain ranges. It occurs at elevations of 800–2,200 meters on dry, stony slopes and in openings within Anatolian black pine forests.

The species favors well-drained, calcareous or volcanic substrates with full sun exposure. It is adapted to the harsh conditions of Mediterranean mountain climates, with hot, dry summers and cold, wet winters.

In its native habitat, Verbascum olympicum grows alongside other Turkish endemics and Mediterranean montane species. These communities are adapted to seasonal drought, intense solar radiation, and thin, nutrient-poor soils.

## Cultivation

Verbascum olympicum thrives in full sun and sharply drained, alkaline to neutral soil. It tolerates poor, sandy, or gravelly soils and is an excellent choice for Mediterranean-style gardens, gravel gardens, and xeriscapes.
