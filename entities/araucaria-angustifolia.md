## Araucaria angustifolia

## Overview

Araucaria angustifolia, commonly known as the Brazilian pine or Paraná pine, is a large evergreen conifer in the family Araucariaceae. It is the most economically and ecologically important tree species in southern Brazil.

The species once dominated the Mixed Ombrophilous Forest (Araucaria forest) across the southern Brazilian plateau. Intensive logging during the [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-south-america]]. They serve as critical habitat for endangered species including the red-spectacled parrot (Amazona pretrei) and the brown howler monkey (Alouatta guariba).

The forest type transitions ecologically with altitude, forming mosaics with grasslands (campos) and subtropical Atlantic forest. These ecotonal zones support exceptionally high plant species richness.

Altitudinal gradients create distinct vegetation bands, with pure Araucaria stands at higher elevations giving way to mixed associations with [[clay-pellet-seed-dispersal]] and forest regeneration.

The species supports diverse epiphyte communities including bromeliads, orchids, and lichens. Fallen branches and decomposing needles create microhabitat heterogeneity essential for ground-dwelling invertebrates.

Araucaria forests regulate regional hydrological cycles, maintaining streamflow in the Paraná and Uruguay river basins. Their role in [[psilocybe-southeast-asia-pacific]].

Resin extracted from the bark has traditional applications in folk medicine and varnish production. The aromatic quality makes it a potential source of terpenoid compounds for pharmaceutical use.
