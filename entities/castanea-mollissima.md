## Castanea mollissima

## Overview

Castanea mollissima, the Chinese chestnut, is a deciduous tree in the
Fagaceae family native to China, Korea, and Taiwan. It is the most widely
cultivated chestnut species globally, valued for its large, sweet nuts,
timber, [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]].

China produces over 80% of the world's chestnuts (~2M tonnes/year),
the vast majority from C. mollissima orchards. Other significant
producers: Turkey, South Korea, Italy, Bolivia.

The species prefers deep, well-drained, acidic soils (pH 5.5–6.5) and full
sun. Tolerant of drought once established. Hardy to USDA zones 4–8.

## Nutritional Profile

Chinese chestnuts are nutritionally distinct from other nuts:

- **Low fat:** ~2% (vs. 60%+ in walnuts, almonds)
- **High carbohydrate:** ~45% starch, comparable to cereal grains
- **Protein:** ~6–8%
- **Vitamin C:** 40–50 mg/100g (retained after cooking — unusual for nuts)
- **Potassium:** ~600 mg/100g
- **B vitamins:** Significant folate, B6, thiamine
- **Calories:** ~180 kcal/100g raw

This profile makes chestnuts function more like a starchy staple than a
fatty nut. Dried and ground into flour, they serve as a gluten-free,
nutrient-dense carbohydrate source.
