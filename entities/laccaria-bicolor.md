# Laccaria bicolor

**Type:** Species (Fungus)
**Category:** Ectomycorrhizal Basidiomycete
**Also known as:** Two-toned Laccaria

## Overview
Laccaria bicolor is an ectomycorrhizal basidiomycete fungus that forms
associations with a broad range of tree species, including conifers and
hardwoods. It was the first ectomycorrhizal fungus to have its genome
fully sequenced, and has been extensively studied for its biocontrol
properties against plant pathogens and its role in forest ecosystem
dynamics.

## Taxonomy and Classification
- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Hydnangiaceae
- **Genus:** Laccaria
- **Species:** L. bicolor

## Key Details

### Morphology
- Cap is 1 to 5 cm, convex to plane, with a distinctive bicolored
  appearance (purplish center fading to tan at margins)
- Gills are broadly attached, lavender to pinkish in color
- Stem is fibrous, scaly at the base, purplish
- Spores are broadly elliptical and spiny
- Spore print is white to pale pink

### Host Range
- Associates with a wide range of tree hosts including pine, spruce,
  fir, birch, poplar, and oak
- Commonly used in commercial mycorrhizal inoculants for forestry
  and reforestation
- L. laccata and L. proxima are closely related species with similar
  ecology

### Biocontrol Properties
- Suppresses Fusarium oxysporum on Douglas fir seedlings (Sylvia and
  Sinclair, 1983)
- Protects Pinus banksiana (jack pine) seedlings from Fusarium
  pathogens (Chakravarty and Hwang, 1991)
- Biocontrol mechanism includes both physical root colonization that
  excludes pathogens and production of antifungal compounds
- Effective in nursery conditions; field efficacy less well documented

### Phosphorus Solubilization
- Capable of solubilizing complex inorganic phosphate forms including
  calcium phytate and tricalcium phosphate
- Shows moderate phosphate solubilization (100 units against calcium
  phytate in the absence of soluble P)
- Activity is downregulated when soluble orthophosphate is abundant
  through negative feedback mechanisms (Lapeyrie et al., 1991)

### Nitrogen Utilization
- Belongs to a group of ectomycorrhizal fungi that rely primarily on
  enhancing nutrient uptake from mineralization by saprotrophic
  microbial communities (Read, 1991b)
- Considered a relatively poor enzyme producer compared to species
  like Paxillus involutus and Amanita spp.

### Heavy Metal Interactions
- Closely related L. amethystina accumulates dimethylarsenic acid
  (DMA) in fruit bodies
- Polyphenols such as catechin and quercetin can inhibit its growth,
  affecting competitive dynamics in soil

### Ecological Role
- An early-stage colonizer commonly appearing in young forests and
  disturbed sites
- Important for seedling establishment after disturbance
- Affinity for mineral soils and post-disturbance conditions
- Induces higher tissue phosphorus content in willow (Salix repens)
  compared to other mycorrhizal species

## Relevance
Laccaria bicolor is a commercially important ectomycorrhizal fungus used
in forestry inoculants worldwide. Its broad host range and biocontrol
properties make it valuable for sustainable forestry and reforestation
projects. In permaculture, it represents a model organism for understanding
how mycorrhizal associations can reduce dependency on chemical pesticides
and fertilizers in tree crop systems.
