## Overview

**Carex pilosa** is a perennial sedge of the family Cyperaceae, native to central and eastern Europe.

It grows in deciduous woodlands on calcareous or neutral soils, forming dense tussocks that contribute to the herbaceous ground layer.

The species is a characteristic associate of beech and oak-hornbeam forests across its range.

It is commonly known as hairy sedge, referring to the hairy sheaths and leaf margins.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Carex pilosa Scop. belongs to section **Montanae** within subgenus **Carex**.

The specific epithet *pilosa* derives from Latin *pilosus* (hairy), describing the conspicuously pubescent sheaths and leaf blades.

Plants form loose to dense tussocks reaching 30–80 cm in height.

Culms are trigonous, slender, and rough-textured toward the apex.

Leaf blades are 3–6 mm wide, flat, with long white hairs along the margins and on the adaxial surface near the base.

Sheaths are conspicuously hairy, a key diagnostic feature distinguishing it from similar woodland sedges.

Inflorescences are androgynous, with a terminal male spike and 2–4 lateral female spikes.

Female glumes are ovate, brownish-green, 4–5 mm long, with a short awn.

Utricles are broadly ovoid, 3.5–4.5 mm long, greenish-brown, prominently nerved, and gradually tapering into a bidentate beak.

Stigmas are three, confirming placement in the subgenus Carex.

## Distribution and Habitat

Carex pilosa occurs across central and eastern Europe, from northern Italy and the Balkans north to Poland and the Czech Republic.

Its range extends eastward through Romania, Ukraine, and into western Russia.

In western Europe it is absent or very rare, reaching its western limit in eastern France and southern Germany.

The species is a characteristic component of the herb layer in **Carpinion betuli** (oak-hornbeam) forest communities.

It also occurs in **Fagetum** (beech) forests on warm, south-facing slopes.

It prefers calcareous to neutral loamy or clay soils with moderate moisture.

Elevation ranges from lowlands to approximately 1,200 m in the Carpathians and Balkan mountains.

## Ecology

Carex pilosa is a shade-tolerant species of the [[fungal-ecology-forest-floor]], active in [[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]] before full canopy closure.

Its tussock growth form creates microsites for seedling establishment of other herbaceous species.

The species contributes significantly to aboveground biomass in oak-hornbeam forests, particularly in unmanaged stands.

It is a host for several sedge-specific fungi, including **Puccinia caricina** and **Anthracoidea caricis**.

Leaf litter from Carex pilosa decomposes relatively slowly, contributing to the humus layer.

The seeds are dispersed by epizoochory, attaching to the fur of passing mammals.

## Ethnobotany and Uses

Carex pilosa has limited direct economic use but serves as a traditional thatch and stuffing material in rural European communities.

In parts of the Carpathian region, dried sedge tussocks were historically used as mattress filling.

The species has value as an indicator of ancient, undisturbed woodland communities.

It is occasionally used in [[tropical-rainforest-seedling-mycorrhiza-ecological-restoration]] projects targeting oak-hornbeam forest reestablishment.
