## Description

The Bwiti cult is a syncretic religious movement native to Gabon and the Congo in Central Africa, centered around the sacramental use of iboga ([[mckenna-iboga-bwiti-fang-tabernanthe-iboga]]). The Bwiti cult is the most famous of several [[entheogenic-knowledge-secret-societies]] in the region that use iboga root as their primary sacrament. Entrance into the cult requires "seeing" the god plant Bwiti, which is accomplished through the visionary effects of iboga.

## Classification

- **Category**: Syncretic religion / Secret society
- **Geographic origin**: Gabon and Congo, Central Africa
- **Sacrament**: Iboga (Tabernanthe iboga) root
- **Active compound**: Ibogaine (principal [[aminopropyl-silica-gel-chromatography-indole-alkaloid-purification]])

## Key Facts

- Most famous secret society using iboga in Gabon and the Congo
- Entrance conditional on having "seen" the god plant Bwiti through iboga visions
- Provides the strongest single force against the spread of Christianity and Islam in the region
- Discovered by Europeans toward the middle of the 19th century
- The "Payment of the Ancestors" ceremony takes place between shrubby bushes of Tabernanthe iboga
- Ibogaine, the principal alkaloid, induces unworldly visions in large doses

## Religious Practices

- Initiation rites involve consuming large doses of iboga root
- Initiates must experience visions of the Bwiti god to gain entry
- "Sorcerers" take the drug to seek information from ancestors and the spirit world
- The Fang Cult of Bwiti conducts elaborate ceremonies with iboga bushes present
- The religion blends animist African traditions with iboga-centered sacramental use

## Sacramental Use of Iboga

- Yellowish root of the iboga plant is the primary sacramental material
- Large doses induce profound visionary experiences
- Hunters use iboga as a stimulant to stay awake all night
- The drug has a reputation as a powerful stimulant and aphrodisiac
- Ibogaine is the principal indole alkaloid among a dozen others found in iboga

## Pharmacological Context

- Ibogaine is the principal indole alkaloid
- In large doses: strong central nervous system stimulant
- Can lead to convulsions, paralysis, and arrest of respiration at extreme doses
- Beyond hallucinogenic effects, ibogaine has been studied for anti-addiction properties
- The Bwiti cult's controlled use demonstrates sophisticated indigenous pharmacological knowledge

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

While iboga is a plant sacrament, the Bwiti cult is relevant to mycological studies:

- Demonstrates indigenous sacramental use of [[cultural-evolution-and-psychoactive-substances]] parallel to [[sacred-mushroom-ceremonies]]
- The controlled dosing practices show sophisticated traditional pharmacological knowledge
- Ibogaine's indole structure connects it to the same chemical family as fungal hallucinogens
- The Bwiti tradition parallels Mesoamerican mushroom ceremonies in structure and purpose
