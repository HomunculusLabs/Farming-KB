# Phyllanthus emblica (Amla)

## Overview

Phyllanthus emblica (amla, Indian gooseberry) is a deciduous tree in the
Phyllanthaceae family, native to tropical South and [[psilocybe-southeast-asia-pacific]]. Its fruit
is among the richest natural sources of ascorbic acid and contains a complex
array of tannins, flavonoids, and polyphenols. Amla is a premier rasayana
(rejuvenative) herb in Ayurvedic medicine with over three millennia of
documented use across South Asian medical traditions.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae — **Order:** Malpighiales — **Family:** Phyllanthaceae
- **Genus:** Phyllanthus — **Species:** P. emblica L.
- **Authority:** Carl Linnaeus (1753)
- **Synonyms:** [[emblica-officinalis]] Gaertn.
- **Common names:** Amla (Hindi), Indian gooseberry, Āmalakī (Sanskrit),
  Nelli (Tamil), Yiu-zi-tau (Chinese)

The genus name derives from Greek *phyllon* (leaf) + *anthos* (flower),
referring to flowers borne on leaf undersides. The epithet *emblica* follows
the Arabic/Persian name for the fruit.

## Morphology

- **Habit:** Small to medium deciduous tree, 8-18 m tall
- **Bark:** Grey-brown, exfoliating in thin flakes
- **Leaves:** Simple, subsessile, oblong-elliptic, 10-25 × 5-10 mm;
  phyllanthoid branching gives pinnate appearance
- **Flowers:** Greenish-yellow, unisexual, in axillary fascicles; male in
  clusters of 6, female solitary; entomophilous pollination
- **Fruit:** Globular berry, 15-25 mm diameter, pale green-yellow when ripe,
  six-lobed with vertical grooves; 3-6 trigonous seeds
- **Taste:** Extremely sour and astringent; one of the most astringent
  commonly consumed fruits
- **Fruiting:** October-March in native range

## Distribution and Habitat

- **Native range:** India, Sri Lanka, Nepal, Bangladesh, Myanmar, Thailand,
  southern China, Malaysia, Indonesia
- **Introduced:** Pakistan, Iran, tropical Africa, Caribbean, [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-hawaii-pacific-islands]]
- **Climate:** Tropical and subtropical; tolerates 5-47 °C
- **Altitude:** Sea level to 1,800 m
- **Soil:** Wide-ranging tolerance; prefers well-drained loam but survives in
  sandy, rocky, and saline conditions
- **[[dighton-fungal-drought-tolerance-plant-water-relations]]:** High once established

## Phytochemistry

Amla fruit contains one of the highest vitamin C concentrations of any fruit
(400-700 mg/100 g fresh weight), stabilized by tannin complexes that prevent
oxidative degradation during drying and processing.

| Compound Class | Key Constituents | Content |
|---------------|-----------------|---------|
| Vitamin C | L-Ascorbic acid | 400-700 mg/100g fresh |
| Tannins | Emblicanin A & B, Punigluconin | 28-35% dry weight |
| Flavonoids | Quercetin, Kaempferol, Rutin | 0.5-1.5% |
| Phenolic acids | Gallic acid, Ellagic acid | 1-3% |
| [[stable-carbon-isotope-signature-fatty-acids-mycorrhizal-carbon-tracking]] | Linoleic, Oleic (seed oil) | 25-35% seed |

The gallotannin-ellagitannin complex (emblicanins) synergizes with ascorbic
acid, protecting it from thermal degradation — a unique stabilization
mechanism not found in other vitamin C-rich fruits.
