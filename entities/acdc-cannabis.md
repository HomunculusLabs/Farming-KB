# ACDC (Cannabis Strain)

## Overview

ACDC is a high-CBD, low-THC cannabis strain that has become one of the most widely recognized and medically significant cultivars in the therapeutic cannabis market. Bred to maximize cannabidiol (CBD) content while minimizing psychoactive tetrahydrocannabinol (THC), ACDC typically produces flowers with a CBD:THC ratio of 20:1, allowing patients to access the therapeutic benefits of cannabis without significant intoxication.

The strain is classified as a phenotype of Cannatonic, itself a cross between MK Ultra and G13 Haze, and was selectively bred by Dr. William Courtney and colleagues in Northern California during the late 2000s and early 2010s. Its development represented a pivotal moment in the medical cannabis movement, demonstrating that cannabis could be therapeutically effective without producing a "high."

## Genetic Lineage

- **Lineage:** Cannatonic phenotype (MK Ultra × G13 Haze)
- **Type:** Sativa-dominant hybrid (approximately 60/40 sativa-indica ratio, though the distinction is less relevant given its cannabinoid profile)
- **Breeder:** Originally stabilized by Dr. William Courtney's breeding program; widely distributed through numerous seed banks and clone programs
- **Landrace ancestry:** The G13 Haze component traces back to Colombian, Mexican, Thai, and Afghani landraces through the Haze lineage

ACDC is a phenotype, meaning it was selected from a population of Cannatonic seeds for its exceptionally high CBD expression. True ACDC cuttings are clone-only, though several seed companies offer ACDC crosses and approximations. Genetic stability is maintained through vegetative cloning rather than seed production.

## Cannabinoid Profile

ACDC's defining characteristic is its extraordinary CBD-to-THC ratio:

- **CBD (Cannabidiol):** 15–20% (typically 16–19%)
- **THC (Δ9-Tetrahydrocannabinol):** 0.5–1.5% (rarely exceeding 2%)
- **CBD:THC Ratio:** Approximately 20:1
- **CBC (Cannabichromene):** 0.5–1.2%
- **CBG (Cannabigerol):** 0.3–0.8%
- **THCA (Tetrahydrocannabinolic acid):** <1.5%
- **CBDA (Cannabidiolic acid):** 15–20%

The acidic precursors (CBDA and THCA) predominate in fresh or gently cured flower. Decarboxylation through heating converts these to their neutral forms. The high CBDA content is particularly relevant for anti-inflammatory applications, as CBDA has shown potent COX-2 inhibitory activity in preclinical studies, potentially exceeding the anti-inflammatory effects of CBD itself.

## Terpene Profile

ACDC produces a distinctive terpene profile dominated by:

- **Myrcene:** 0.4–1.1% — sedating, anti-inflammatory, muscle relaxant
- **Pinene (α-pinene and β-pinene):** 0.2–0.6% — bronchodilator, memory-enhancing, anti-inflammatory
- **Limonene:** 0.1–0.5% — mood-elevating, anxiolytic, anti-fungal
- **Caryophyllene:** 0.1–0.4% — CB2 receptor agonist, anti-inflammatory, analgesic
- **Linalool:** 0.05–0.3% — anxiolytic, sedating, anti-convulsant
- **Humulene:** 0.05–0.2% — appetite suppressant, anti-inflammatory

The terpene composition contributes to the entourage effect, in which terpenes and cannabinoids act synergistically to enhance therapeutic outcomes. The relatively low total terpene content compared to THC-dominant strains gives ACDC a mild, earthy aroma rather than the pungent, complex bouquet of recreational cannabis.

## Aroma and Flavor

- **Aroma:** Mild, earthy, and slightly sweet with subtle floral and woody notes. Lacks the strong, skunky, or diesel-like odors common in high-THC strains.
- **Flavor:** Smooth, herbal, and slightly piney on the inhale with a faintly sweet, floral finish. The low terpene content results in a gentle, approachable taste that is well-tolerated by patients sensitive to strong cannabis flavors.
- **Smoke:** Light and smooth, with minimal harshness. The low resin content relative to high-THC strains results in thinner, less irritating smoke.

## Medical Applications

ACDC is primarily cultivated and consumed for medical purposes. Its therapeutic profile is supported by the growing body of evidence for CBD and the entourage effects of minor cannabinoids and terpenes:

### Pain and Inflammation
- Chronic pain management (neuropathic pain, fibromyalgia, arthritis)
- Anti-inflammatory applications leveraging both CBD and CBDA's COX-2 inhibition
- Topical preparations for localized pain and inflammation

### Neurological Conditions
- Epilepsy and seizure disorders — ACDC's high-CBD profile aligns with the mechanism of action of Epidiolex (FDA-approved CBD for Dravet syndrome and Lennox-Gastaut syndrome)
- Multiple sclerosis symptom management (spasticity, neuropathic pain)
- Parkinson's disease tremor reduction (anecdotal reports, limited clinical data)
- Neuroprotection through antioxidant and anti-inflammatory pathways

### Mental Health
- Anxiety reduction without sedation or cognitive impairment
- PTSD symptom management
- Mood stabilization (the anxiolytic effects of CBD are well-documented)
- Sleep improvement secondary to anxiety reduction

### Oncology Support
- Anti-nausea effects (synergistic with pinene's anti-emetic properties)
- Appetite stimulation
- Potential anti-tumor activity of CBD (preclinical only)
- Management of chemotherapy side effects

### Pediatric Applications
The low THC content makes ACDC one of the few cannabis strains considered appropriate for pediatric use under medical supervision, particularly for:
- Treatment-resistant epilepsy
- Autism spectrum disorder behavioral symptoms
- Pediatric anxiety and sleep disorders

## Growing Information

### Cultivation Parameters
- **Flowering time:** 65–70 days indoors; late October outdoors (Northern Hemisphere)
- **Yield:** Moderate, 10–14 oz/m² indoors
- **Height:** Medium-tall, reaching 120–180 cm outdoors
- **Difficulty:** Moderate — requires attention to CBD phenotype stability

### Cultivation Notes
- True ACDC must be grown from verified clones to maintain the 20:1 CBD:THC ratio
- Seed-grown ACDC will show significant phenotypic variation in cannabinoid profile
- The strain is susceptible to powdery mildew in high-humidity environments
- Prefers moderate nutrient levels; over-fertilization can reduce CBD content
- Performs well in both soil and hydroponic systems
- Light stress and cold temperatures during late flowering can enhance purple coloration without significantly affecting cannabinoid content
- Regular testing of finished flower is essential to verify CBD:THC ratios for medical compliance

### Legal Considerations
In jurisdictions where hemp is defined as cannabis with ≤0.3% THC dry weight, ACDC may qualify as hemp depending on the specific harvest. However, some phenotypes can push above 0.3% THC, requiring careful compliance monitoring. In the United States, ACDC is grown under both hemp and medical cannabis licenses depending on the regulatory framework of the producing state.

## Side Effects

ACDC is well-tolerated by most users due to its low THC content. Reported side effects are generally mild:
- Dry mouth (xerostomia)
- Mild drowsiness (particularly at higher doses)
- Slight lightheadedness (usually dose-dependent)
- Potential drug interactions via CYP450 enzyme inhibition (particularly CYP3A4 and CYP2C19) — patients on anticoagulants, antiepileptics, or immunosuppressants should consult their physician

ACDC does not produce the anxiety, paranoia, tachycardia, or cognitive impairment commonly associated with THC-dominant strains, making it suitable for daytime use and patients who are cannabis-naïve or sensitive to THC.
