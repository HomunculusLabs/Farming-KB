# ACDC (Cannabis Strain)

**ACDC** is a high-CBD, low-THC cannabis strain that has become one of the most widely recognized CBD-dominant cultivars in the medical cannabis community. Renowned for its remarkably high cannabidiol (CBD) content and minimal psychoactive effects, ACDC is favored by patients seeking therapeutic benefits without intoxication.

## Genetic Lineage

ACDC is a **phenotype of Cannatonic**, a balanced hybrid created by crossing **MK Ultra** (Indica-dominant, G13 × LA Confidential) with **Reina Madre** (a landrace-leaning Sativa). The specific ACDC phenotype was selected and stabilized for its extreme CBD-to-THC ratio, typically producing CBD:THC ratios of 20:1 or higher. Some tested samples have shown CBD concentrations exceeding 20% by dry weight with THC below 1%.

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Type** | Sativa-dominant hybrid (~60/40) |
| **Lineage** | Cannatonic phenotype (MK Ultra × Reina Madre) |
| **Breeder** | Originally selected by Dr. William Courtney; popularized by Dark Heart Nursery |
| **CBD:THC Ratio** | Typically 20:1 (range 15:1 to 30:1) |
| **Flowering Time** | 8–9 weeks indoors |
| **Yield** | Moderate (300–400 g/m² indoor) |

## Cannabinoid Profile

The defining characteristic of ACDC is its cannabinoid profile:

- **CBD (Cannabidiol):** 15–20% (dominant cannabinoid)
- **THC (Δ⁹-Tetrahydrocannabinol):** 0.5–1.5% (minimal intoxication)
- **THCA:** Trace
- **CBDA:** Present in significant quantities in raw flower
- **CBG, CBC:** Present in minor amounts

The low THC content means ACDC does not produce the characteristic cannabis "high." Users report feeling clear-headed and functional, making it suitable for daytime use and for individuals who are THC-sensitive or required to avoid psychoactive substances.

## Terpene Profile

ACDC's aromatic and therapeutic profile is shaped by its terpene composition:

- **Myrcene** (dominant): Herbal, earthy, slightly fruity. Contributes to sedative and muscle-relaxant properties. The most common terpene in cannabis and also abundant in hops, mango, and lemongrass.
- **Pinene** (α-pinene and β-pinene): Piney, fresh. Associated with bronchodilation, memory retention, and anti-inflammatory effects. Pinene is theorized to counteract some of the cognitive impairment associated with THC — less relevant for ACDC but notable pharmacologically.
- **Caryophyllene**: Spicy, peppery. A dietary cannabinoid that binds directly to CB₂ receptors, contributing anti-inflammatory and analgesic effects without psychoactivity.
- **Limonene** (minor): Citrus notes. Mood elevation, anxiolytic properties.
- **Humulene** (minor): Woody, earthy. Anti-inflammatory and appetite-suppressant.

## Therapeutic Applications

ACDC is primarily used for its **anti-inflammatory, analgesic, anxiolytic, and anticonvulsant** properties. It has gained particular attention in the following clinical contexts:

### Epilepsy and Seizure Disorders

ACDC was one of the strains used in early patient-driven research into CBD's anticonvulsant effects. The story of **Charlotte Figi**, while primarily associated with the Stanley Brothers' Charlotte's Web strain, parallels the broader movement of CBD-dominant cultivars like ACDC being used to treat **Dravet syndrome** and other treatment-resistant epilepsies. This patient-driven evidence ultimately contributed to the FDA's approval of Epidiolex (purified CBD) in 2018.

### Chronic Pain and Inflammation

The combination of high CBD with anti-inflammatory terpenes (caryophyllene, pinene, myrcene) makes ACDC a popular choice for patients with:

- Fibromyalgia
- Rheumatoid arthritis
- Multiple sclerosis-related neuropathic pain
- Chronic lower back pain

### Anxiety and Mood Disorders

CBD has demonstrated anxiolytic effects in multiple clinical trials, and ACDC's negligible THC content avoids the anxiety that some users experience with THC-rich strains. It is used for generalized anxiety disorder, social anxiety, and PTSD-related symptoms.

### Cancer Symptom Management

ACDC is used adjunctively in cancer care for:

- Nausea reduction (though less effective than THC for this indication)
- Pain management as an opioid-sparing agent
- Sleep improvement without sedation
- Appetite stimulation (modest)

## Cultivation Notes

ACDC can be grown indoors, outdoors, or in greenhouse settings. It is considered moderately difficult to cultivate, largely because maintaining the high CBD:THC ratio requires careful phenotype selection and environmental control.

- **Temperature:** Prefers 65–80°F (18–27°C)
- **Humidity:** 40–60% during flowering (mold-resistant but not immune)
- **Nutrients:** Moderate feeder; responds well to organic soil cultivation
- **Training:** LST (low-stress training) and SCROG improve yield
- **Harvest timing:** Later harvest (amber trichomes) can slightly increase CBD content

Growers should source verified clones from reputable breeders, as seed-grown ACDC may not maintain the extreme CBD:THC ratio due to genetic variation.

## Legal Status

The legal status of ACDC varies by jurisdiction. In the United States, hemp-derived CBD strains (defined as cannabis with <0.3% THC by dry weight under the 2018 Farm Bill) are federally legal, placing ACDC in a favorable regulatory position. However, state laws vary, and some states classify all cannabis-derived products the same regardless of THC content. In many countries, ACDC is only available through medical cannabis programs.
