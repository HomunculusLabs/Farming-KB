# Psilocybe subcubensis

**Type:** Species (Fungus)
**Category:** [[psilocybin]]-containing mushroom
**Also known as:** little Cuban Psilocybe

## Overview
Psilocybe subcubensis is a subtropical psilocybin mushroom described
by Guzman that is virtually identical in appearance to the well-known
Psilocybe cubensis. The two species are differentiated primarily by
microscopic features: P. subcubensis has smaller spores (9.9-13 by
6.1-7.1 microns versus the larger spores of P. cubensis) and slightly
smaller pleurocystidia. Despite being less famous, P. subcubensis has a
broad geographic distribution across the neotropics.

## Key Details

### Taxonomy
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Hymenogastraceae
- **Genus:** Psilocybe
- **Authority:** Guzman

### Morphology
Virtually identical to Psilocybe cubensis in macroscopic features.
Distinguished only by microscopic characteristics:
- **Spores:** (9.9) 11-13 (14) by 6.1-7.1 microns (smaller than P.
  cubensis). Dark purplish brown in deposit.
- **Pleurocystidia:** Slightly smaller than those of P. cubensis.
- General appearance matches P. cubensis: golden to caramel cap,
  bruising blue, growing on dung or enriched soils in tropical and
  subtropical environments.

### Habitat and Distribution
Reported from throughout much of subtropical Mexico, Colombia, Bolivia,
Ecuador, Honduras, El Salvador, Venezuela, and Australia. Probably
more widely distributed across tropical and subtropical regions
worldwide. Reports from California (Stamets 1978; Ott and Bigwood 1978)
are believed to be from outdoor cultivated specimens rather than wild
populations.

### Chemistry
Active, containing psilocybin and [[psilocin]]. Potency profile is presumed
to be similar to P. cubensis given their close relationship, though
specific quantitative analyses of P. subcubensis are limited.

## Relevance
Psilocybe subcubensis is a cryptic species that demonstrates how
microscopic examination is essential for accurate identification within
the Psilocybe genus. Its nearly identical appearance to P. cubensis
means it is likely frequently encountered but misidentified by casual
observers. The distinction between these two species rests entirely on
spore size and pleurocystidia dimensions, requiring access to a
microscope.

The broad geographic range across Central and South America suggests it
is one of the more widespread psilocybin species in the neotropics. Its
presence in Australia indicates potential for intercontinental dispersal
through cattle trade or other anthropogenic means, similar to the spread
of P. cubensis itself. Reports from California are attributed to
outdoor cultivated specimens rather than naturalized populations.

For cultivators and researchers, P. subcubensis represents an
understudied close relative of the most widely cultivated psilocybin
mushroom. Its growth parameters, potency profile, and cultivation
characteristics remain largely undocumented, presenting opportunities
for further investigation.
