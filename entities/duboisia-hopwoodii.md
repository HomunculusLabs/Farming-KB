# Duboisia hopwoodii F. v. Muell.

## Common Names

Pituri, Pitcheri, Pituri Bush, Native Tobacco

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Order:** Solanales
- **Family:** Solanaceae (Nightshade Family)
- **Subfamily:** Nicotianoideae
- **Genus:** Duboisia
- **Species:** D. hopwoodii F. v. Muell.

## Description

Duboisia hopwoodii is an erect or spreading shrub of the arid and
semi-arid regions of central Australia. The plant typically grows
to a height of 3 to 10 ft (1 to 3 m) and is recognized by its
slender, somewhat drooping branches and narrow, lanceolate leaves.
The foliage is pale green to grayish-green in color.

The leaves are the most economically and culturally significant part
of the plant. They are linear to narrow-lanceolate, 1 to 3.5 in
(2.5 to 9 cm) long, and contain the psychoactive alkaloid complex
that has made this species central to Aboriginal Australian culture
for tens of thousands of years.

The flowers are small and white, borne in loose clusters. The fruit
is a small berry. The root system is extensive, allowing the plant
to survive in extremely arid conditions and contributing to its
ability to persist across vast stretches of the Australian interior.

## Distribution

Duboisia hopwoodii is endemic to Australia, occurring across the
arid and semi-arid interior of the continent. Its range extends
through central Australia, from Western Australia through the
Northern Territory, South Australia, and into southwestern
Queensland. The plant is adapted to dry, sandy soils and rocky
outcrops in desert and semi-desert environments.

## Chemistry

Duboisia hopwoodii contains a variety of powerful and stimulating
but toxic alkaloids. The principal constituents identified in the
leaves include:

- **D-nor-nicotine:** This compound appears to be the main active
  constituent of Pituri leaves. D-nor-nicotine is a demethylated
  analog of nicotine, possessing stimulant properties similar to
  those of nicotine but with a distinct pharmacological profile.

- **Nicotine:** The well-known alkaloid of commercial tobacco
  (Nicotiana tabacum), present in significant quantities in Pituri
  leaves.

- **Piturine:** An alkaloid originally isolated from D. hopwoodii
  and named after the plant's common Aboriginal designation.

- **Dubosine:** Another alkaloid characteristic of the Duboisia
  genus, contributing to the overall psychoactive profile.

In addition to the leaf alkaloids, hallucinogenic tropane alkaloids
have been discovered in the roots of Duboisia hopwoodii:

- **Hyoscyamine**
- **Scopolamine**

These tropane alkaloids are characteristic of the Solanaceae
family and are found in many related psychoactive plants including
[[datura-innoxia]] and other members of the nightshade lineage.
The presence of both nicotine-type and tropane-type alkaloids in
different parts of the same plant makes D. hopwoodii chemically
remarkable.

## Ethnobotanical Uses

Pituri leaves have been used for at least 40,000 years in
Australian Aboriginal rituals, representing one of the longest
continuous traditions of psychoactive plant use documented
anywhere in the world. The leaves serve both medicinal and
pleasurable purposes within Aboriginal society.

The traditional method of Pituri consumption involves mixing the
dried, crushed leaves with alkaline plant ashes derived from
specific woody species. This alkaline admixture serves to
liberate the free base form of the alkaloids, facilitating
absorption through the buccal mucosa when the mixture is chewed,
much like chewing tobacco. The prepared quid is held in the cheek
or behind the lip for extended periods.

The effects of Pituri include the removal of hunger and thirst,
a valuable property in the harsh Australian desert environment
where food and water are scarce. More significantly from a cultural
standpoint, Pituri induces intense visionary dreams and altered
states of consciousness.

## Cultural Significance

In Aboriginal magic and cosmology, entering the dream state, the
transcendent state, is an essential part of ritual practice. The
concept of the Dreamtime (or Alcheringa) is central to Aboriginal
worldview, representing the ancestral creation period during which
the land, its features, and its living inhabitants were formed.

Pituri serves as a tool for accessing this transcendent dream
state, facilitating communication with ancestral spirits, obtaining
healing knowledge, and performing divination. Shamans and elders
use Pituri in initiation ceremonies, healing rituals, and other
important cultural events.

The Pituri trade routes extended across vast distances of the
Australian continent, forming one of the most extensive prehistoric
trade networks documented. Dried Pituri leaves were transported
from collection areas in the interior to coastal regions hundreds
of miles away, exchanged for tools, ochre, shells, and other
valued commodities.

Duboisia hopwoodii is one of the most important psychoactive plants
of Aboriginal Australian culture and represents the most significant
and ancient psychoactive plant use in Australia.
