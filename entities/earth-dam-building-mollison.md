# Earth Dam Building

## Description

Earth dam building is the construction of water-retaining structures using compacted soil, primarily clay and earth materials, to create on-farm water storages. Small earth dams are among the most cost-effective water storage methods available, storing approximately 100 times more water per unit cost than concrete tanks. They are a cornerstone of permaculture water management and Keyline design systems.

## Classification

- **Domain**: Water management / Earthworks
- **Category**: Water storage construction
- **Related techniques**: Keyline design, swale systems, gley sealing, bentonite sealing
- **Scale**: Farm-scale (typically 3–6 m depth, up to 4.5 million litres)

## Key Facts

### Site Selection Criteria
Successful earth dam construction depends on careful site evaluation across several factors:

1. **Soil type**: Core sample pits assess clay fractions; tamped earth with clay fractions better than 50% creates waterproof barriers
2. **Grade behind wall**: Lower slopes provide greatest capacity for earth moved
3. **Downstream safety**: A key factor for large dams; failure risk must be assessed
4. **Height above use points**: Gravity flow is desirable for distribution
5. **Available catchment or diversion**: Sufficient water must be available to fill storage

### Construction Specifications
- **Maximum economic height**: 3.6 m (12 feet) of retaining wall for tamped earth; practical depths of 4.5–6 m (15–20 feet) for small earth dams
- **Compaction**: Every 25 cm (10 inches) of earth must be rolled or tamped by bulldozer tracks or rollers
- **Slope ratios**: 3:1 (inner/wet side) and 2:1 to 2.5:1 (outer/dry side)
- **Freeboard**: 0.9 m (3 feet) minimum above spillway
- **Key trench**: 0.6–0.9 m (2–3 feet) deep for walls over 1.8 m (6 feet) high to prevent shear and base seepage
- **Shrinkage**: Well-compacted dams show less than 1% shrinkage

### Critical Construction Details
- Slopes to crest should be concave for stability
- Boulders, logs, grass clumps, and topsoil must be excluded from the wall
- Earth must be neither so dry as to crumble nor so wet as to slump under the roller
- A key trench prevents shear and cuts off base seepage
- In suspect soils, the whole core can be constructed of imported clay
- The wall can curve outward or inward if carefully made

### Spillway and Outlet Design
- **Spillway base**: Carefully surveyed at 1 m below crest, positioned away from the wall itself
- **Base outlet (lock pipe)**: Fitted with baffle plates to draw off water
- **Siphon**: Used for smaller dams as an overflow over the dam wall crest

### Dam Efficiency
The key to efficiency is the ratio of dam wall length to the length of water impounded. Short dams at constricted sites with flat valleys behind them are the most cost-effective. A careful survey of grade plus dam length determines value before construction begins.

## Sealing Methods for Leaky Dams

Five primary methods are described by Mollison:

1. **Gley**: Fermented green plant material producing bacterial slime seal
2. **Bentonite**: Volcanic ash-derived clay powder, rototilled 5–7 cm deep
3. **Explosives**: 3–5 stick dynamite charges to compact dam sides
4. **Clay**: Spread and rolled 23–30 cm thick over suspect areas
5. **Impermeable membranes**: Welded plastic, neoprene, or concrete

## Cost Comparisons

- Water stored in soil and humus: cheapest, greatest volume
- Surface dams: next cheapest
- Tanks: more expensive but still cheaper than piped mains water
- A 22,500 litre tank costs the same to build as an earth dam storing 2,500,000 litres (100× ratio)
- Earth tanks excavated below grade cost one-tenth the price of concrete tanks above grade
- Dams above 3 m wall height see rapidly escalating costs

## Relevance to Cultivation and Land Management

Earth dams are central to permaculture because they provide:

1. **Low-cost water storage** enabling drought-proofing of farms and settlements
2. **Gravity-fed irrigation** when positioned above use points
3. **Biological water purification** when fenced and planted to 30–60 m of forest surround
4. **Microclimate modification** through water body thermal mass
5. **Aquaculture opportunities** with proper sealing and stocking
6. **Fire protection** when integrated with spill gate and sheet irrigation systems

The combination of dams with Keyline design, swales, and diversion drains creates a complete water management system that captures, stores, and distributes water across the landscape using only gravity and natural processes.
