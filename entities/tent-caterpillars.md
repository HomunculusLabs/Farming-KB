# Tent Caterpillars

## Description

Tent caterpillars are the larval stage of moths in the genus *Malacosoma*
(family Lasiocampidae) that build conspicuous silk tents in the crotches of
trees. The eastern tent caterpillar (*Malacosoma americanum*) is the most
common species, building tents in cherry, apple, and crabapple trees. Forest
tent caterpillars (*M. disstria*) do not build tents but form mats on trunks
and branches. They are significant defoliators that can strip trees of
foliage during outbreak years.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Animalia
- **Phylum**: Arthropoda
- **Class**: Insecta
- **Order**: Lepidoptera
- **Family**: Lasiocampidae
- **Genus**: *Malacosoma*
- **Key species**: *M. americanum* (eastern tent caterpillar),
  *M. disstria* (forest tent caterpillar), *M. californicum* (western tent
  caterpillar)

## Key Facts

- Larvae build communal silk tents in tree branch crotches, emerging to feed
  on foliage during the day and returning to the tent at night.
- Eastern tent caterpillar prefers cherry, apple, and crabapple trees; forest
  tent caterpillar attacks a wider range including aspen, oak, and maple.
- A single colony can defoliate small trees; large outbreaks can defoliate
  entire stands.
- Caterpillars are social insects that use pheromone trails to coordinate
  foraging.
- One generation per year; eggs overwinter in shiny dark masses encircling
  twigs.
- Defoliation rarely kills healthy trees but reduces vigor, growth, and
  [[berry-fruit-production-cool-humid-climates]].
- Natural enemies include birds, predatory wasps, parasitic flies, and
  various entomopathogenic organisms.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

### Compost Tea Applications

[[elaine-ingham]] includes tent caterpillars among the pests manageable through
[[actively-aerated-compost-tea]] (AACT) applications:

1. **Foliar deterrent**: AACT applied to foliage alters the [[aact-foliar-sticker-spreaders-leaf-surface-attachment-ingham]]
   chemistry and [[cannabis-cultivar-microbial-community-effects]], making treated leaves less palatable
   or attractive to feeding caterpillars.
2. **Entomopathogenic organisms**: Well-made compost may contain beneficial
   organisms such as *[[beauveria-bassiana]]* that can infect and kill
   caterpillars.
3. **Predator support**: AACT applications support a diverse foliar
   community that attracts and sustains predatory insects and parasitoids
   that attack caterpillars and their eggs.
4. **Plant [[mycorrhiza-induced-resistance-defense-priming]]**: Beneficial microbes on leaf surfaces can
   trigger [[endophytic-mycorrhizal-induced-systemic-resistance]], enhancing the plant's natural
   chemical defenses against herbivory.

### Integrated Pest Management

Ingham's approach emphasizes building a complete food web where pest
populations are naturally regulated by their predators and parasites, rather
than relying on pesticides that disrupt beneficial organisms.

## Management Notes

- Apply AACT preventively in early spring as caterpillars emerge from eggs.
- Remove and destroy egg masses during winter pruning.
- Remove small tents manually in early spring before caterpillars disperse.
- Encourage bird populations with habitat features.
- Maintain overall tree health through soil [[soil-food-web-management-for-farmers]].
