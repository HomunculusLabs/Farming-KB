## Description

**Bipolaris sorokinaianum** (syn. Cochliobolus sativus, anamorph Helminthosporium sativum) is a fungal plant pathogen that causes spot blotch and common root rot in cereal crops. It is notable for being lethal to collembolan species when consumed, demonstrating toxic defenses against soil microarthropod grazers.









## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Phylum**: [[ascomycota]]
- **Class**: Dothideomycetes
- **Order**: Pleosporales
- **Family**: Pleosporaceae
- **Genus**: Bipolaris
- **Species**: B. sorokinaianum









## Key Facts

- Demonstrated to be lethal to all collembolan species tested in feeding experiments
- Produces toxic compounds that kill soil microarthropod grazers upon consumption
- Acts as both a plant pathogen and an arthropod-toxic fungus
- Illustrates that some fungi have evolved chemical defenses against invertebrate grazing









## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

B. sorokinaianum demonstrates that fungi can defend themselves against invertebrate grazing through toxic secondary metabolites. This has implications: (1) it illustrates the evolutionary arms race between fungal prey and microarthropod predators, (2) it suggests some fungal metabolites may have insecticidal properties useful for pest management, and (3) it highlights the importance of fungal community composition in determining collembolan populations in cultivation systems.









## Ecological Role

As a plant pathogen, B. sorokinaianum causes significant disease in wheat, barley, and other cereals. Its toxicity to [[collembola]] suggests an additional ecological role as a grazing-defended fungus, potentially allowing it to persist at higher biomass levels in soil than palatable species.
