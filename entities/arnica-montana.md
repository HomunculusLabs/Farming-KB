The species is critically endangered in several European countries due to

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Division:** Tracheophyta
- **Class:** Magnoliopsida
- **Order:** Asterales
- **Family:** Asteraceae
- **Genus:** Arnica
- **Species:** A. montana L. (1753)
- **Common names:** Mountain arnica, leopard's bane, wolfsbane (historical),
  arnica flower, Arnika (German), Arnique (French)
- **Etymology:** "Arnica" derives from the Greek "arni" (lamb), referring to
  the soft, hairy leaves. "Montana" refers to its mountain habitat.

## Morphology

- **Habit:** Perennial herb, 20–60 cm tall, with a single erect stem.
- **Leaves:** Basal rosette of 4–8 spatulate to obovate leaves, 4–12 cm long,
  covered in fine arachnoid hairs giving a grey-green appearance. Cauline
  leaves smaller, sessile, bract-like.
- **Stem:** Unbranched or sparingly branched above, glandular-pubescent.
- **Flower heads:** Solitary terminal, 4–7 cm diameter. Ray florets orange-
  yellow, 15–25, ligulate, 15–25 mm long. Disk florets tubular, yellow.
  Involucral bracts in 2–3 rows, lanceolate, with glandular hairs.
- **Roots:** Cylindrical rhizome with numerous fibrous secondary roots.
- **Fruit:** Achenes, 5–7 mm, cylindrical, dark brown, with a white pappus
  of 20–30 bristles.
- **Flowering:** June to August.
- **Pollination:** Insect-pollinated, primarily by bees and hoverflies.

## Distribution and Habitat

Arnica montana is native to mountainous regions of central and southern Europe,
extending from the Iberian Peninsula through the Alps and Carpathians to
southern Scandinavia. It occurs at 600–2,800 meters elevation.

Preferred habitats include nutrient-poor acidic soils in mountain meadows
and open coniferous woodlands. It requires full sun and is intolerant of heavy
grazing, fertilization, and competition from aggressive grasses. Populations
have declined significantly due to agricultural intensification.

## Chemistry and Pharmacology

### Primary Bioactive Compounds

- **Helenalin:** The principal sesquiterpene lactone, responsible for most
  anti-inflammatory and analgesic activity. Contains reactive α-methylene-
  γ-lactone and cyclopentenone moieties that alkylate cellular thiols,
  inhibiting NF-κB transcription factor activation.
- **11α,13-Dihydrohelenalin:** Reduced derivative of helenalin with lower
  potency but improved tolerability.
- **Dihydrohelenalin acetate:** Acetylated derivative; contributes to
  anti-edema effects.
