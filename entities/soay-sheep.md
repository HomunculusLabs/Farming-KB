# Soay Sheep

## Description

The Soay sheep is a primitive, hardy breed of domestic sheep (Ovis aries) originating from the island of Soay in the St Kilda archipelago of Scotland. Mollison mentions Soay sheep in the context of aquatic and coastal polyculture systems, noting that they — along with turtles and Galapagos iguanas — browse large seaweeds of the seashore and seagrasses, demonstrating the potential for integrating livestock with coastal and aquatic food production.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Animalia
- **Class:** Mammalia
- **Order:** Artiodactyla
- **Family:** Bovidae
- **Genus:** Ovis
- **Species:** O. aries (domestic sheep)
- **Breed:** Soay
- **Origin:** St Kilda archipelago, Scotland
- **Conservation status:** Rare breed; feral population on Soay island

## Key Facts

- One of the most primitive surviving sheep breeds, largely unchanged since the Bronze Age
- Naturally self-shedding wool (no shearing required)
- Extremely hardy, surviving on minimal forage in harsh maritime conditions
- Small stature: adults typically 25–40 kg
- Willing to browse seaweeds and marine algae along coastlines
- Can thrive without supplementary feeding in marginal environments
- Naturally resistant to many common sheep diseases and foot problems
- Seasonal breeders with strong maternal instincts

## Ecological Role in Coastal Systems

Mollison positions Soay sheep within a broader discussion of aquatic polyculture, noting that several species naturally browse marine vegetation:

- **Soay sheep:** Browse large seaweeds and seagrasses at coastal margins
- **Turtles:** Graze seagrass beds and macroalgae in shallow marine environments
- **Galapagos iguana:** The only known marine lizard, feeds on underwater algae

This coastal browsing behavior suggests potential for integrated land-sea polyculture designs where livestock harvest marine vegetation as supplementary fodder.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

The Soay sheep exemplifies several [[permaculture-principles]] through its natural characteristics:

1. **Multiple function:** Provides meat, wool, and vegetation management simultaneously
2. **Low input:** Self-shedding, disease-resistant, and capable of foraging on marginal vegetation including seaweed
3. **Edge utilization:** Naturally exploits the productive land-sea interface, one of the most biologically rich zones
4. **Adaptation over modification:** The breed's primitive genetics mean it is already adapted to harsh conditions rather than requiring modified environments
5. **Integration potential:** Seaweed-browsing ability creates a natural bridge between terrestrial livestock and marine/aquatic production systems

For coastal [[permaculture]] designs, Soay sheep could serve as:
- Low-maintenance lawn mowers for coastal pastures
- Harvesters of washed-up seaweed (converting marine biomass to terrestrial fertility)
- Cleaners of rocky shorelines where machinery cannot operate
- A hardy livestock option for exposed maritime sites where conventional breeds fail

## Traditional and Historical Context

The Soay sheep represents a living connection to prehistoric agriculture. Its primitive characteristics — self-shedding fleece, small size, hardiness, and browsing behavior — reflect the ancestral sheep type before selective breeding for wool production and docility. This makes it a valuable genetic resource for low-input, sustainable livestock systems.
