## Indole

**Indole** (2,3-benzopyrrole) is an aromatic heterocyclic [[lsd-lysergic-acid-diethylamide-experience-guide]]. The indole ring system is one of the most pharmacologically significant heterocycles in nature.

## Chemical Properties

- **Chemical formula:** C₈H₇N (C₈H₅NH for the parent)
- **Molecular weight:** 117.15 g/mol
- **IUPAC name:** 1H-Indole
- **Structure:** Fused bicyclic system — benzene ring fused to pyrrole at the 2,3-positions
- **pKa:** ~16.97 (N-H), making it a very weak base and non-basic at physiological pH
- **Solubility:** Slightly soluble in water (1.9 g/L at 25°C); soluble in organic solvents (ethanol, ether, chloroform)
- **Odor:** Characteristic jasmine-like fragrance at low concentrations; unpleasant fecal odor at high concentrations

### Electronic Structure

The indole ring system has 10 π-electrons distributed over the bicyclic framework, making it aromatic. The nitrogen lone pair participates in the aromatic sextet of the pyrrole ring (unlike pyridine), which is why indole is not basic. The most nucleophilic position is C-3, making it the preferred site for electrophilic substitution reactions — a feature exploited in the biosynthesis of indole alkaloids.

## Natural Occurrence

Indole is widely distributed in nature:

- **Plant volatiles:** Jasmine (where it was first identified), orange blossom, narcissus, and other flowers
- **Mammalian chemistry:** Produced in the gut by tryptophanase activity of intestinal bacteria from [[serotonin]], [[psilocin]] | Active metabolite of psilocybin |
| 4-PO₄-N⁺(CH₃)₃ | Phosphoryloxy + quaternary N | [[5-meo-dmt-compound-profile]] | Potent psychedelic |
| 5-OCH₃ | Methoxy + ethylamide | [[serotonin]] and [[5-meo-dmt-compound-profile]], DPT, DiPT, 4-HO-MET, 4-AcO-DMT
- **Antidepressants:** Indole-3-propionic acid derivatives; vilazodone (5-HT₁A partial agonist + SSRI)
- **Anti-inflammatory:** Indomethacin, sulindac, etodolac — NSAIDs built on indole scaffolds
- **Antimicrobials:** Indole-containing β-lactam antibiotics
- **Anticancer agents:** Vinca alkaloids (vinblastine, vincristine from Catharanthus roseus) and their synthetic analogues; sunitinib (a kinase inhibitor)
- **Plant hormones:** Indole-3-acetic acid (IAA, auxin) and synthetic auxins (2,4-D, dicamba, picloram)
- **Cardiovascular:** Pimobendan (inodilator), ergot alkaloid derivatives

The pharmacological versatility of the indole ring stems from its ability to participate in hydrogen bonding (via N-H), π-stacking (via the aromatic system), and hydrophobic interactions, making it an ideal scaffold for drug design.

## Indole in Neurotransmission and Psychedelic Pharmacology

The indole ring is the defining structural feature that distinguishes tryptamine neurotransmitters and psychedelics from other monoamine classes (catecholamines like dopamine/norepinephrine and the ethylamine class). The flat, planar indole ring is critical for binding within the orthosteric site of 5-HT receptors:

- The indole NH forms a hydrogen bond with a conserved serine residue (Ser5.46 in the 5-HT₂A receptor)
- The aromatic ring participates in π-π stacking with phenylalanine residues in the binding pocket
- Substitutions on the indole ring (4-OH for [[serotonin]] and exogenous psychedelics share this exact indole scaffold is no coincidence — it is the structural basis for their shared ability to activate serotonergic receptors and modulate consciousness.

## Historical Context

Indole was first isolated in 1866 by Adolf von Baeyer from the indigo dye reduction product indoxyl. The name derives from "indigo" + "oleum" (oil), reflecting its origin from indigo processing. The Fischer indole synthesis, developed by Emil Fischer in 1883, became the foundational method for constructing indole rings and enabled the systematic exploration of indole [[tryptophan]] — indole-3-substituted essential amino acid; biosynthetic precursor to all natural indole alkaloids
- [[dimethyltryptamine]] — 4-substituted indole with N,N-dimethyl; endogenous psychedelic
- [[psilocybin]] — indole-based mushroom alkaloid and 5-HT₂A agonist
- [[serotonin]] — indole-based neurotransmitter and structural template for psychedelics
