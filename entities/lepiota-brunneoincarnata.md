# Lepiota brunneoincarnata

## Overview

Lepiota brunneoincarnata is a dangerously toxic agaric mushroom in the family
Agaricaceae. It contains lethal concentrations of amatoxins — the same
peptide toxins found in *[[query-how-to-identify-and-avoid-the-death-cap-amanita-phalloides]] poisoning.
There is no specific antidote, though supportive care and liver transplantation
improve survival odds.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

### Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Agaricaceae
- **Genus:** Lepiota
- **Species:** L. brunneoincarnata (Chodat & C. Martín) J.E. Lange

The species was originally described by Robert Chodat and Carlos Martín in
1889 and later transferred to *Lepiota* by Jakob Emanuel Lange.

### Physical Description

The cap measures 20–50 mm in diameter, initially hemispherical before
expanding to broadly convex or plano-convex. The cap surface is covered with
concentrically arranged brown to reddish-brown scales on a pale cream to
whitish background.
