# PsiD (L-Tryptophan Decarboxylase)

**PsiD** is an aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase and the first enzyme in the psilocybin biosynthetic pathway. It catalyzes the decarboxylation of L-tryptophan to produce tryptamine, the foundational scaffold from which all downstream psilocybin-related alkaloids are derived.








## Biochemical Function

PsiD removes the α-carboxyl group from L-tryptophan via a pyridoxal-5'-phosphate (PLP)-dependent mechanism, yielding tryptamine (indole-3-ethylamine) and carbon dioxide. This reaction is analogous to that performed by mammalian aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase (AADC), but PsiD has evolved specificity within fungal secondary metabolism rather than neurotransmitter biosynthesis.








## Position in the Psilocybin Pathway

PsiD occupies the committed entry point of the psilocybin biosynthetic gene cluster:

1. **L-Tryptophan** → (PsiD) → **Tryptamine** → (PsiH) → 4-Hydroxytryptamine → (PsiK) → Norbaeocystin → (PsiM) → Baeocystin → Psilocybin

Without PsiD activity, no psilocybin, psilocin, baeocystin, or norbaeocystin can be produced. The enzyme therefore functions as the metabolic gatekeeper for the entire pathway.








## Enzymatic Properties

- **Cofactor:** Pyridoxal-5'-phosphate (PLP / vitamin B6)
- **Substrate specificity:** L-tryptophan; does not efficiently decarboxylate other aromatic amino acids under physiological conditions
- **Reaction type:** Oxidative decarboxylation (α-decarboxylation)
- **Localization:** Cytosolic, co-expressed with other pathway enzymes from the psilocybin gene cluster








## Genetic Context

The *psiD* gene is part of a conserved gene cluster found in psilocybin-producing fungi, including *Psilocybe cubensis*, *Psilocybe cyanescens*, *Psilocybe serbica*, and *Inocybe corydalina*. The cluster typically contains *psiD*, *psiH*, *psiK*, and *psiM* in a co-regulated arrangement, suggesting coordinated transcriptional control during fruiting body development.








## Biotechnological Applications

Heterologous expression of PsiD in microbial hosts (e.g., *E. coli*, *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*) is a key strategy in synthetic biology efforts to produce tryptamine and downstream psilocybin derivatives at scale. The enzyme's PLP dependence makes cofactor supply a consideration in fermentation optimization.
