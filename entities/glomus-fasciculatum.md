## Glomus fasciculatum

## Description

*Glomus fasciculatum* (syn. *Rhizophagus fasciculatus*) is an arbuscular mycorrhizal fungus (AMF) in the family Glomeraceae. It is one of the most widely studied and distributed AMF species globally, forming mutualistic associations with the roots of an enormous range of host plants. This species produces intraradical arbuscules and vesicles as well as extraradical hyphae that extend into soil.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Glomeromycota
- **Class:** Glomeromycetes
- **Order:** Glomerales
- **Family:** Glomeraceae
- **Genus:** *Glomus*
- **Species:** *G. fasciculatum*

## Key Facts

- Used successfully as inoculum in surface lignite mine restoration, significantly increasing survival, biomass, nitrogen, and phosphorus content of three grass species (Call and Davies, 1988).
- Sideoats grama inoculated with *G. fasciculatum* showed 97% survival and 25 g/plant biomass vs. 86% and 15 g for uninoculated controls.
- Indian grass inoculated with *G. fasciculatum* achieved 92% survival and 36 g/plant biomass vs. 72% and 21 g for controls.
- Klein grass inoculated with *G. fasciculatum* showed 61% survival and 19 g/plant biomass vs. 47% and 10 g for controls.
- Arbuscular mycorrhizal colonization enhanced Cd uptake through fungal hyphae but reduced transfer from fungus to plant, retaining Cd in the fungal component and thus detoxifying the system (Joner and Leyval, 1997).

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

*Glomus fasciculatum* is one of the most commercially important AMF species for inoculant production. Its proven effectiveness in mine overburden restoration and heavy metal tolerance makes it a primary candidate for reclamation projects. The species' ability to enhance [[plant-nutrient-uptake]] while sequestering toxic metals in its own biomass represents a key mechanism for phytoremediation. It is widely used in agriculture, horticulture, and restoration ecology.

## Ecological Role

- Arbuscular mycorrhizal symbiont with extremely broad host range
- Critical for plant establishment in degraded and contaminated soils
- Sequesters heavy metals in fungal tissues, reducing plant toxicity
- Enhances phosphorus uptake in nutrient-poor soils
- Produces glomalin, a glycoprotein that improves soil aggregation
