# Ecology Action

## Description

Ecology Action is a non-profit organization founded in 1971 that developed and promotes the GROW BIOINTENSIVE sustainable mini-farming method. Originally located in Palo Alto, California, using corporation land in the Stanford Industrial Park, the organization later moved to Willits, California. Ecology Action has spent over 50 years researching, developing, and teaching sustainable agriculture methods that enable people to grow complete diets on very small land areas using minimal resources.

## Key Facts

- Founded in 1971 in Palo Alto, California
- Used Stanford Industrial Park corporation land for initial research gardens
- Published the first book on Biointensive techniques in 1974 (How to Grow More Vegetables)
- Research from 1972-1978 demonstrated economic mini-farm viability on 1/4 acre
- Showed complete balanced diet possible from 2,800 square feet
- Cosponsored Third International Conference on Small-Scale and Intensive Food Production (1981)
- U.S. Secretary of Agriculture Bob Bergland called their work "10 years ahead of its time" (1981)
- GROW BIOINTENSIVE trademark registered in 1999
- Over 500,000 copies of How to Grow More Vegetables sold worldwide

## Research Milestones

Ecology Action's research has produced significant findings: University of California soil science tests showed accelerated humified carbon buildup; research demonstrated 67-88% water consumption reduction, 50+% fertilizer reduction, 99% energy reduction, and 200-400% caloric production increase per unit area. The organization published a Preliminary Research Report in 1973 outlining initial findings, followed by decades of continuing research and data collection.

## Educational Publications

Ecology Action has produced an extensive library of publications including How to Grow More Vegetables (their flagship book, published in multiple editions), the Self-Teaching Mini-Series booklets, conference proceedings, and research reports. By 1981, twenty books by other organizations using Biointensive techniques based on Ecology Action publications had appeared, demonstrating the organization's catalytic role in sustainable agriculture education.

## International Reach

Ecology Action's work has had global impact. Projects based on their methods were established in India (Shri A.M.M. Murugappa Chettier Research Centre), China, and numerous other countries. The Third International Conference attracted participants from 14 countries. The organization's publications have been translated into multiple languages, and their methods have been adapted to diverse climates and cultures worldwide.

## The Mini-Farm Research Program

Central to Ecology Action's work is their research mini-farm in Willits, California, where they conduct long-term experiments in sustainable food production. The mini-farm serves as both a research facility and a demonstration site, providing data on yields, water consumption, soil building, and economic viability. The research program has been running continuously since the early 1970s, making it one of the longest-running sustainable agriculture research projects in the world.

## Relevance to Cultivation

Ecology Action's compost-intensive approach and emphasis on soil biology create conditions highly favorable for beneficial fungi. Their deep soil preparation methods encourage mycorrhizal networks, and their compost production methods parallel mushroom substrate preparation techniques. The organization's whole-system approach to farming recognizes the importance of soil microorganisms in nutrient cycling.

## The Self-Teaching Mini-Series

Ecology Action published a series of practical booklets designed for self-directed learning. Titles included Soybeans, The U-Bar, Food from Your Backyard Homestead, The Self-Fertilizing Herbal Lawn, Beginning to Mini-Farm, Modular Multi-Use Mini-Greenhouse Plans, A 10-Crop 5-Year Learning and Test Workbook, and A Perspective. These publications made sustainable agriculture education accessible to individuals without access to formal training programs.

## Move to Willits, California

Ecology Action relocated from Palo Alto to Willits, California (in Mendocino County) to establish a permanent research facility. The Willits site provides a longer growing season and rural setting more suitable for long-term agricultural research. The move also reflected the organization's growth from a small research project to a significant force in sustainable agriculture education.

## Conference Sponsorship and Networking

Ecology Action cosponsored the Third International Conference on Small-Scale and Intensive Food Production, which attracted 100 participants from 14 countries including Mainland China. The conference proceedings, published as "Intensive Food Production on a Human Scale," helped establish an international network of sustainable agriculture practitioners. This conference also catalyzed a biointensive project in China, demonstrating the cross-cultural applicability of the method.

## Soil Science Research

Ecology Action's research attracted academic attention from an early stage. University of California graduate students in soil science tested garden beds at the research site and found an "unexpected accelerated rate of humified carbon buildup, a process that normally takes hundreds of years." Two beds from the research site were moved to a protected area for future monitoring, indicating the significance of the soil building results. This finding of accelerated soil formation is one of the most remarkable claims of the GROW BIOINTENSIVE system.

## Science '80 Recognition

A major article about Ecology Action's work appeared in the premier issue of Science '80 magazine, bringing the organization to national scientific attention. This coverage was significant because it presented biointensive methods to a scientific audience, not just the organic gardening community. The recognition helped establish Ecology Action as a serious research organization rather than merely an advocacy group.
