## Overview

Ranunculus glacialis, the glacier buttercup or glacier crowfoot, is a herbaceous perennial in the family Ranunculaceae. It holds the distinction of being the highest-altitude flowering plant in Europe, recorded at 4,275 meters on the Finsteraarhorn in the Swiss Alps.

## Physiology and Adaptations

The species exhibits extreme freezing tolerance, surviving temperatures below -30°C in laboratory tests. Ice nucleation occurs extracellularly, preventing intracellular ice crystal formation that would rupture cell membranes.

Photosynthesis operates efficiently at low temperatures (5–10°C), with the photosynthetic apparatus adapted to function in cold conditions. The plant maintains positive net photosynthesis at temperatures as low as 2°C.

Ranunculus glacialis accumulates soluble sugars and antifreeze proteins as cryoprotectants during the winter dormant period. These compounds lower the freezing point of cell sap and stabilize membranes during freeze-thaw cycles.

The compact cushion growth form reduces wind exposure and creates a favorable microclimate within the rosette. Temperatures within the cushion can be 5–10°C above ambient air temperature on sunny days.
