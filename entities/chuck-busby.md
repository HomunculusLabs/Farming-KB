## Description

**Chuck Busby** is a permaculture practitioner based in **Kualapuu, Moloka'i,
Hawaii**, recognised by Bill Mollison for developing innovative **mulched circle
garden** systems. Busby created productive garden nuclei in exhausted pineapple
farm soils on Moloka'i, using compact mulching and watering techniques within
circle garden designs. He is part of the broader Hawaiian permaculture community
that demonstrated tropical food system design.

## Key Facts

- Developed **mulched circle gardens** on exhausted pineapple farm soils in
  Kualapuu, Moloka'i — some fitted with trellises for climbing crops.
- Used **banana grass** (*Pennisetum*) as shelter hedging around circle gardens.
- Noted that several yam and bean species can use banana grass as support or
  trellis, creating productive vertical growing space.
- Operated within the **Hawaiian Homelands** area on Moloka'i.
- Featured in Mollison's photographic plates alongside other Hawaiian
  permaculture pioneers.
- Demonstrated that compact, mulch-intensive garden design can rehabilitate
  degraded tropical agricultural land.








## Relevance to Cultivation / Mycology

- Mulched circle gardens create concentrated zones of **organic matter
  decomposition** driven by fungal and bacterial activity.
- The deep mulch layer in circle gardens provides ideal habitat for
  **saprophytic fungi** that break down organic matter and release nutrients.
- Exhausted pineapple farm soils (typically acidic and depleted from years of
  monoculture) require **biological rehabilitation** — fungal inoculation and
  mycorrhizal establishment are key to this process.
- Banana grass shelter creates humid microclimates favourable for fungal
  fruiting and mycelial growth in adjacent garden beds.
- Trellis systems using banana grass supports can be combined with **shade-loving
  mushroom cultivation** (e.g., *Pleurotus* spp.) on logs or substrate blocks
  positioned at the grass base.
