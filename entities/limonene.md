# Limonene

Limonene (1-methyl-4-(1-methylethenyl)cyclohexene) is a cyclic monoterpene and one of the most common terpenes in nature, widely recognized for its characteristic citrus aroma. In cannabis (*Cannabis sativa*), limonene is a major terpene constituent that contributes significantly to the plant's therapeutic profile, with well-documented anti-inflammatory, anxiolytic, gastroprotective, and potential anticancer properties. Its presence in cannabis is strongly associated with mood elevation, stress relief, and focus-enhancing effects.





## Chemical Properties

Limonene has the molecular formula C10H16 and a molecular weight of 136.23 g/mol. It exists in two enantiomeric forms: R-(+)-limonene (d-limonene), which is found in citrus fruits and has a characteristic orange-lemon scent, and S-(−)-limonene (l-limonene), which occurs in conifers and has a more piney, turpentine-like aroma. The R-enantiomer is far more abundant in nature and is the predominant form found in cannabis.

Limonene is a colorless liquid at room temperature with a boiling point of approximately 176°C. It is highly lipophilic, volatile, and relatively stable under normal storage conditions. The compound is classified as a monoterpene, being composed of two isoprene units.





## Natural Occurrence

Limonene is the second most widely distributed terpene in nature and is found in significant quantities in citrus fruits (d-limonene constitutes 90–95% of orange peel essential oil), cannabis (a major terpene in citrus-scented cultivars such as Lemon Skunk, Super Lemon Haze, and Sour Diesel), conifer trees, herbs and spices (rosemary, juniper, peppermint, caraway), and numerous other plant species.

Industrial production of limonene is primarily through extraction from citrus peel waste generated by the juice industry, making it one of the most commercially available terpenes.





## Role in Cannabis

### Aroma and Flavor Contribution

Limonene is a primary contributor to the citrus, lemon, and orange aromas that characterize many popular cannabis cultivars. Its presence is easily detected organoleptically and serves as a key trait in cultivar identification and consumer preference.

### Pharmacological Synergy with Cannabinoids

Limonene participates in the entourage effect through enhanced transdermal absorption (increasing skin permeability), anxiolytic potentiation (complementing CBD's anti-anxiety effects), and antidepressant synergy (enhancing THC's uplifting effects through its own mood-elevating properties).

### Cultivar Characterization

Limonene-dominant cultivars are typically classified as sativa-leaning in traditional cannabis taxonomy, and their effects are generally described as uplifting, creative, and focused.





## Pharmacological Properties

### Anxiolytic and Antidepressant Effects

Limonene has demonstrated significant anxiolytic and antidepressant effects in preclinical studies through [[serotonin]] and dopamine modulation, GABAergic activity, and HPA axis regulation reducing stress-related cortisol elevation.

### Anti-inflammatory Activity

Limonene exhibits potent anti-inflammatory effects through COX-2 inhibition, NF-κB pathway suppression, cytokine modulation (reducing TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6), and inhibition of inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS).

### Gastroprotective Effects

Limonene's gastroprotective activity is among its most clinically established properties. It has been investigated as a treatment for gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD), protects the gastric mucosa from damage, and has demonstrated antiulcer effects in animal models.

### Anticancer Properties

D-limonene has been extensively studied for its anticancer properties with promising results. It prevents the initiation and promotion of carcinogenesis in multiple animal models, inhibits cancer cell growth through cell cycle arrest and apoptosis, enhances the efficacy of conventional chemotherapeutic agents, and has shown partial regression of breast cancer tumors in clinical studies. Mechanisms include inhibition of protein prenylation (particularly Ras protein farnesylation), induction of detoxification enzymes, modulation of apoptosis-regulatory proteins, and anti-angiogenic effects.

### Immunomodulatory Effects

Limonene modulates immune function through enhancement of phagocytic activity, modulation of lymphocyte proliferation, and regulation of cytokine production.





## Safety and Therapeutic Dosing

Limonene is classified as GRAS by the FDA and is widely used as a flavoring agent and fragrance ingredient. Oral doses used in clinical studies have ranged from 0.5 to 15 grams per day, with minimal adverse effects. Common side effects at higher doses include mild gastrointestinal discomfort and heartburn.

Limonene is metabolized primarily in the liver through oxidation by cytochrome P450 enzymes to carveol, perillyl alcohol, and limonene-1,2-diol, which may themselves possess biological activity.
