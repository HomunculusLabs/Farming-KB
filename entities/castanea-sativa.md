# Castanea sativa

**Type:** Species (Tree)
**Category:** Nut-bearing tree
**Family:** Fagaceae
**Also known as:** Spanish chestnut, [[dighton-fungal-drought-tolerance-plant-water-relations]]:** High once established
- **Soil preference:** Well-drained, light soils; fair lime tolerance;
  does not tolerate salinity
- **Nutritional value (fresh, per 100g):** Energy 194 cal,
  Carbohydrate 42.1 g, Fat 1.5 g, Protein 2.9 g, Potassium 454 mg,
  Phosphorus 88 mg, Vitamin B1 0.22 mg, Vitamin B2 0.22 mg
- **Nutritional value (dried, per 100g):** Energy 377 cal,
  Carbohydrate 78.6 g, Fat 4.1 g, Protein 6.7 g, Potassium 875 mg,
  Iron 3.3 mg, Phosphorus 162 mg
- **Digestion time:** 2.75 hours (fresh)
- **Yield:** Up to 5,000 kg per hectare from mature groves; 0.77
  t/ha minimal irrigation, 1.7 t/ha full irrigation by year 10
- **Timber:** Young timber is tough and durable; [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]]; destroyed by bark fungus (chestnut blight) in
  early 1900s; nuts said to be sweetest of any species

## Relevance
Sweet chestnut is a cornerstone species in European agroforestry and
a valuable addition to temperate food forests. Its ability to coppice
for centuries while producing both nuts and timber makes it one of
the most sustainable tree crops available. The high carbohydrate
content of dried chestnuts made them a staple food across southern
Europe for centuries, and they remain an important element of
traditional food systems.
