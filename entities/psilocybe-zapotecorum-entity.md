# Psilocybe zapotecorum

**Type:** Species (Fungi, Basidiomycota, Agaricales)
**Category:** Psilocybin-containing mushroom
**Also known as:** badao Zoo, badoo, bei, be-meeche, beya-zoo,
bi-neechi, zapos

## Overview
Psilocybe zapotecorum is a large, strongly bluing psychedelic mushroom
found throughout southern Mexico and Central and South America. Named
after the Zapotec people of Oaxaca, it is one of the most culturally
significant species in Mesoamerican mushroom traditions. The species
is noted for its asymmetrical, often convoluted caps and its habit of
sometimes growing inside the mud houses of native peoples, a phenomenon
described as "spiritually prophetic."

## Key Details

### Taxonomy
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Hymenogastraceae
- **Genus:** Psilocybe
- **Authority:** Heim emend. Guzman

### Morphology
- **Cap:** 1-3 (11) cm, highly variable in form, conic to convex to
  subumbonate, sometimes papillate and convoluted in age. Surface
  smooth, translucent-striate near margin when moist. Reddish brown,
  hygrophanous, fading to beige, orangish rose to straw in drying,
  quickly bruising blue to green to blackish where injured.
- **Gills:** Sinuate to adnate, pale brown to violet brown to violet
  purple with edges concolorous or slightly paler.
- **Stem:** (40) 100-200 mm long by (3) 5-10 (12) mm thick, equal to
  lightly expanded at base, sometimes with pseudorhiza. White to grayish
  to variably reddish brown or vinaceous, bluing when touched or injured
  with blackish violet tones. Surface floccose above and strongly
  scabrous-strigose near base.
- **Spores:** Purplish in deposit, violet brown, oblong-ellipsoid,
  (5.5) 6.6-7 (8.8) by 3.8-4.4 (5.5) um.
- **Basidia:** 2-spored.
- **Pleurocystidia:** 20-38 by 5.5-14 um, variable in form.
- **Cheilocystidia:** 13-27 by 3.5-6 um, ventricose to fusoid to
  lageniform with extended neck.

### Habitat and Distribution
- Cespitose to gregarious, rarely scattered, in swampy or muddy soils,
  in humus rich with leaves and wood debris, in marshy deciduous forests,
  and in coffee plantations.
- Frequently found on faces of ravines with exposed soils.
- Range: Southern Mexico (600-1800 m), extending through Colombia,
  Ecuador, Peru, Brazil, and Argentina.
- Also reported from Bolivia in January.
- Sometimes found inside the mud houses of Zapotec peoples.

### Chemistry
- Moderately to highly active.
- Stijve and de Meijer (1993) found up to 0.30% psilocybin and 1.0%
  psilocin in Brazilian specimens.
- Heim and Hofmann (1958) found only 0.05% psilocybin and no psilocin
  in two-year-old specimens (likely degraded).
- The high psilocin content accounts for the strong bluing reaction.

## Relevance
P. zapotecorum is one of the most important species in Mesoamerican
ethnomycology, held in high esteem by both Mazatec and Zapotec peoples.
Its large size and high psilocin content make it a potent ceremonial
species. The curious phenomenon of it growing inside indigenous dwellings
has spiritual significance in local cultures. Heim and Callieux (1959)
successfully fruited this species in laboratory conditions, contributing
to early cultivation knowledge. The species has been frequently confused
with P. caerulescens in the literature, and photographs in Ott and
Bigwood (1978) and Stamets (1978) labeled as P. caerulescens are in
fact P. zapotecorum.
