# Tulsi (Ocimum tenuiflorum)

Tulsi, also known as holy basil or sacred basil, is an aromatic perennial
herb in the family Lamiaceae, native to the Indian subcontinent and
cultivated throughout [[ursolic-acid]],
rosmarinic acid, linalool, carvacrol, and beta-caryophyllene. Three
distinct chemotypes exist: Krishna tulsi (purple-leaved), Rama tulsi
(green-leaved), and Vana tulsi (wild-type, [[permaculture-designers-manual-essential-oil-plants]] profile and therapeutic emphasis.

## Taxonomy and Classification

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Phylum:** Angiosperms
- **Class:** Eudicots
- **Order:** Lamiales
- **Family:** Lamiaceae
- **Genus:** Ocimum
- **Species:** O. tenuiflorum (syn. O. sanctum)

## Description

### Morphological Characteristics

| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| Growth habit | Upright bushy annual or short-lived perennial, 30-60 cm |
| Stem | Square (Lamiaceae), branching, pubescent |
| Leaves | Opposite, ovate to elliptic, 2-5 cm long, serrate margins |
| Leaf color | Green (Rama), dark purple-red (Krishna), grey-green (Vana) |
| Flowers | Small bilabiate, whorled racemes on terminal spikes |
| Flower color | White to pale purple, sometimes tinged pink |
| Calyx | Tubular, 5-toothed, persistent after corolla falls |
| Roots | Fibrous, shallow, extensive lateral root system |
| Seeds | Small, black, ovoid, 1-1.5 mm, in nutlets |

### Chemotype Varieties

| Variety | Leaf Color | Key Oil Constituents | Traditional Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Krishna Tulsi | Dark purple | Eugenol (up to 70%), beta-caryophyllene | Meditation, respiratory |
| Rama Tulsi | Bright green | Eugenol, linalool, rosmarinic acid | Daily wellness, tea |
| Vana Tulsi | Grey-green | Eugenol, germacrene D, limonene | Wild adaptogen, immunity |

## Distribution and Habitat

Tulsi is native to the Indian subcontinent including India, Nepal,
Bangladesh, Pakistan, and Sri Lanka. It has been naturalized throughout
tropical and subtropical regions of Southeast Asia, Africa, the
Caribbean, and parts of Central and [[query-how-to-grow-rare-tropical-fruits-in-marginal-and-subtropical-climates]] (USDA zones 10-12 perennial,
  zones 5-9 as annual)
- Well-drained loamy to sandy soils with pH 6.0-7.5
- Full sun to partial shade, requiring 6+ hours of direct sunlight
- Annual rainfall of 500-2000 mm; moderately drought-tolerant once
  established
- Elevations from sea level to 1500 m
