# Suillus pungens

## Overview

**Suillus pungens** is an ectomycorrhizal bolete fungus in the family Suillaceae,
notable for its strong, pungent odor and exclusive association with Monterey pine
(*[[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]] due to
its acrid, almost chemical fragrance that can be detected from several meters away.

The species was first collected and documented in the Monterey Peninsula region,
where Monterey pine forms natural stands. Despite its penetrating smell, S. pungens
is considered edible when thoroughly cooked, though it is not widely collected for
the table.

## Taxonomy and Classification

Basidiomycota → Agaricomycetes → Boletales → **Suillaceae → *Suillus → *S. pungens***

- **Basionym:** Described by H.D. Thiers in 1975.
- **Type locality:** Monterey County, California.
- **Common name:** Pungent slippery jack (rarely used).
- No significant synonyms or recent taxonomic revisions; placement in Suillus
  is stable based on both morphological and molecular phylogenetic data.

## Morphology

- **Cap:** 4–12 cm diameter, convex to broadly convex, sometimes plano-convex
  with age. Surface smooth and glossy when young, becoming sticky to viscid in
  wet weather. Color ranges from pale yellow-brown to warm cinnamon-brown,
  often with a slightly darker disc. Cuticle peels easily from the margin.
- **Pore surface:** Adnexed to slightly decurrent, bright yellow when young,
  becoming olive-yellow with age. Pores angular, 1–2 per mm. Stains brownish
  when bruised.
- **Stipe:** 4–9 cm long, 1–2 cm thick, cylindrical or slightly tapered
  toward the base. Solid, pale yellow above the annulus, darker below.
  Surface covered with small brownish glandular dots below the ring.
- **Annulus:** Membranous, often collapsed and appressed to the stipe, yellowish,
  sometimes disappearing with age.
- **Flesh:** Pale yellow, soft, unchanging or slowly browning when cut.
- **Odor:** Very strong, pungent, and distinctive — often described as
  acrid, fruity, or chemical-like. This is the most reliable field character.
- **Taste:** Mild to slightly acidic.
- **[[shipibo-san-francisco-ceremonial-center-ucayali]] Bay Area south to San Luis Obispo County.
- **Habitat:** Exclusively ectomycorrhizal with Monterey pine (*Pinus radiata*).
  Found in coastal pine forests, often in sandy or loamy soils.
- **Season:** Late autumn through early winter (November–February), appearing
  with the onset of seasonal rains.
- **Frequency:** Locally common within its range but restricted to Monterey pine
  forests, limiting its overall distribution.

## Ecological Role

S. pungens forms obligate [[arbuscule-isolation-and-nutrient-exchange-mechanisms]] between the [[fungal-mycelium-hyphae-and-the-feeding-phase]] and host root
system. The fungus aids in phosphorus and nitrogen uptake for the tree while
receiving photosynthetically derived carbon compounds.
