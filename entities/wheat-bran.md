## Description

**Wheat bran** (coarse, unrefined) is a key supplementary ingredient in mushroom cultivation substrates, particularly for *Grifola frondosa* (maitake) synthetic-log cultivation. Comprising 18-23% of the total substrate formulation, wheat bran serves as the primary source of thiamine (vitamin B₁) and other nutrients essential for fruiting body formation. It is added to the [[hardwood-sawdust-substrate]] basal ingredient along with calcium compounds and sucrose. Wheat bran is a by-product of milling [[triticum-aestivum]] (common wheat) and is one of the most widely used substrate supplements across many mushroom species.

## Classification

- **Category:** Substrate supplement / Nutrient additive
- **Type:** Agricultural by-product
- **Source:** Outer layer of wheat grain (Triticum aestivum)
- **Form used:** Coarse, not refined
- **Proportion in substrate:** 18-23% by dry weight

## Key Facts

- Wheat bran is the essential source of thiamine (vitamin B₁) required for fruiting body formation in *Grifola frondosa*.
- Coarse, unrefined wheat bran is preferred over refined bran for better texture and aeration in the substrate.
- In the standard maitake substrate formula: hardwood sawdust 75%, wheat bran 23%, sucrose 1%, calcium compound 1%.
- In the recycled substrate formula, wheat bran (or a substitute) is included at 10% alongside 20% spent substrate.
- Wheat bran also provides protein, lipids, minerals, and additional carbohydrates that support vigorous mycelial colonization.
- The bran contributes to the overall moisture-holding capacity of the substrate due to its absorptive properties.
- Calcium from bran, combined with added lime or [[gypsum]], contributes to differentiation of basidiocarps (fruiting bodies).

## Nutritional Composition

Wheat bran provides a rich and diverse nutritional profile that makes it an ideal substrate supplement:

### Macronutrients
- **Protein:** 14-16% crude protein — a critical nitrogen source for mycelial growth
- **Fat:** 4-5% crude fat — provides lipids that serve as an energy reserve
- **Carbohydrates:** 20-25% — includes starch, cellulose, and hemicellulose
- **Dietary fiber:** 40-50% — contributes to substrate structure

### Vitamins
- **Thiamine (B₁):** 0.5-0.8 mg/100g — the most critical vitamin for basidiomycete fruiting
- **Riboflavin (B₂):** 0.3-0.5 mg/100g
- **Niacin (B₃):** 10-15 mg/100g
- **Pyridoxine (B₆):** 0.5-1.0 mg/100g

### Minerals
- **Phosphorus:** 1.0-1.3% — essential for ATP production and cell membrane integrity
- **Potassium:** 1.0-1.5% — osmotic regulation and enzyme activation
- **Magnesium:** 0.4-0.6% — cofactor for numerous enzymes
- **Calcium:** 0.1-0.2% — cell wall structure and signaling

## Role in Mushroom Substrate

### Thiamine Requirement
Many basidiomycete fungi, including *G. frondosa*, have an absolute requirement for exogenous thiamine (vitamin B₁) for fruiting body development. Without adequate thiamine, mycelium may grow vegetatively but fail to produce mushrooms. Wheat bran is the most practical and economical source of this essential vitamin in commercial substrate formulations. This requirement is common across many wood-decomposing species discussed in mushroom cultivation fundamentals.

### Nitrogen Enrichment
The nitrogen in wheat bran helps narrow the carbon-to-nitrogen (C:N) ratio of the substrate from approximately 500:1 (pure hardwood sawdust) to 30-50:1 (supplemented substrate). This range is optimal for most wood-loving mushroom species. The improved C:N ratio accelerates colonization and supports more vigorous fruiting.

### Physical Substrate Benefits
Coarse bran particles serve several physical functions in the substrate:
- **Porosity maintenance:** The irregularly shaped particles resist compaction, maintaining air channels for gas exchange.
- **Moisture buffering:** Bran absorbs and holds water, acting as a mini-reservoir that releases moisture during the spawn run.
- **Nucleation sites:** Bran particles provide colonization points where [[mycelium]] can rapidly establish before expanding into the surrounding sawdust.

## Application Across Species

While primarily discussed here in the context of [[maitake-cultivation]], wheat bran is used as a supplement in the cultivation of many species:
- **Shiitake (*Lentinula edodes*):** 10-20% bran in sawdust blocks, as detailed in shiitake mushroom cultivation and medicinal properties
- **Oyster mushroom (*Pleurotus* spp.):** 5-15% bran in straw or hull substrates; see oyster mushroom cultivation
- **Reishi (*Ganoderma lucidum*):** 10-20% bran; covered in reishi mushroom cultivation and medicinal properties
- **Enoki (*Flammulina velutipes*):** 10-15% bran in supplemented sawdust
- **Wood ear (*Auricularia* spp.):** See wood ear auricularia cultivation for formulations

## Substrate Formulations

### Standard Maitake Formula
- Hardwood sawdust: 75%
- Wheat bran: 23%
- Sucrose: 1%
- Calcium compound (lime/gypsum): 1%

### Recycled Substrate Formula
- Hardwood sawdust: 65%
- Spent substrate: 20%
- Wheat bran: 10%
- Sucrose: 1%
- Calcium compound: 1%

When cottonseed hulls are substituted for 25% of the sawdust (see [[cottonseed-hulls]]), the bran percentage remains at 18-23%.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Thiamine requirement:** Many basidiomycete fungi, including *G. frondosa*, have an absolute requirement for exogenous thiamine (vitamin B₁) for fruiting body development. Wheat bran is the most practical and economical source.
- **Nutritional enrichment:** Beyond thiamine, wheat bran provides a broad spectrum of nutrients (protein, B-vitamins, minerals) that accelerate mycelial growth and improve yield.
- **Substrate structure:** Coarse bran particles help maintain substrate porosity, improving gas exchange — critical for the highly aerobic maitake.
- **Cost-effectiveness:** As an agricultural by-product, wheat bran is inexpensive and widely available, making it ideal for commercial substrate formulation.
- **Substitution options:** In the recycled substrate formula, wheat bran can be partially substituted, offering flexibility for substrate optimization.
