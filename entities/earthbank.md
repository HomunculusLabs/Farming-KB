## Earthbank

## Description

Earthbank is a community [[ethical-investment-and-earth-banking]] and financial education concept developed by
the [[permaculture-institute]] following seminars with the E. F. Schumacher group in the USA
in 1983. Documented by [[bill-mollison]] in *[[permaculture]]: A Designer's Manual*, an Earthbank
group begins as a research, teaching, and seminar-convening organization that evolves into
an active community financial system as local money systems are established. The Earthbank
concept represents the Permaculture Institute's practical approach to "banking on the earth"
-- redirecting public money toward ecological and social good.

## Classification

- **Category:** Community financial system / Ethical investment movement
- **Domain:** Permaculture -- [[informal-and-formal-community-economics]], ethical investment, earth care finance
- **Origin:** Permaculture Institute, 1983 (following E. F. Schumacher group seminars, USA)
- **Scope:** Local community to bioregional scale
- **Evolution:** Research group to active financial services organization

## How It Works

1. **Initial Phase:** A local Earthbank group starts as a research, teaching, and
   seminar-convening organization
2. **Data Collection:** The group assembles a resource list of data from existing community
   financial models
3. **Education:** Invites fund managers to visit the region to give seminars on ethical
   investment and [[community-finance-and-alternative-economies]]
4. **System Design:** Adopts or devises local financial recycling systems, ethical brokerages,
   or non-monetary community [[arbuscule-isolation-and-nutrient-exchange-mechanisms]]
5. **Operational Phase:** As local money systems are established, some members obtain
   employment running financial, exchange, or barter systems
6. **Networking:** Connects with annual seminars and other Earthbank groups for knowledge
  sharing and mutual support

## Annual Seminars

Earthbank groups convene or participate in annual seminars:

- **UK:** "The Other Economic Summit" (TOES)
- **USA, Australia, Canada:** Regional ethical investment conferences
- **Attendance:** Started with 12 people in Australia; grew to 60-100 interested people
  and organizations
- **Participants:** Banks, insurance companies, cooperatives, credit unions send
  representatives to assist new groups

## Ethical Investment Criteria

Earthbank groups develop investment criteria based on three tests:

1. **Economic Audit (European Audit):** "Where did the money go? Was it honestly used?
   Is the system economically viable?"
2. **Ethical Audit (Iroquois Audit):** "Was the enterprise concerned with its ethical
   (people care) accounting? Did the enterprise benefit people in the long run?"
3. **Resource Audit (Life Increase Audit):** Did the investment increase or decrease
   biological resources and natural capital?

## Positive Investment Priorities

The Earthbank movement evolved from negative screening (what not to fund) to positive
investment in enterprises that:

- Assist conservation and reduce waste or energy use
- Grow clean food free of biocides or dangerous contaminants
- Are involved in community reafforestation
- Build energy-conserving houses or villages
- Produce clean transport or energy systems
- Assist people's self-reliance
- Found cooperatives, self-employed ventures, or profit-sharing systems
- Produce durable, sound, useful, and necessary products

## Investment Time Horizon Model

Mollison recommends staggering investments by return timeline:

**Short Term (1-2 years):**
- Draught-proofing and insulation
- Attached glasshouses
- Clean water tanks
- Trickle irrigation and dam building
- Good local industries with careful management

**Medium Term (3-7 years):**
- Bee, chicken, and pig forage systems plus stock
- Large water catchments for aquaculture and irrigation
- Nurseries, subdivisions, tours and tourism
- Environmental rehabilitation and farm resale
- Clean power systems

**Long Term (7-20 years):**
- Town and city reconstruction or development
- Fuel crop and processing
- Small farm development
- Large-scale property retrofit
- [[fukuoka-citrus-orchard-establishment-bare-hillside]]
- Research into new energy forms

**Permanent:**
- Forestry and natural remnant forest purchase
- Wildlife reserves and rainforest preservation
- Shares tradeable as values increase over the long term

## Proportional Investment Recommendation

For a $100 investment, Mollison suggests:
- 10% to risk ventures (new ideas, new ventures)
- 10-20% to a local S.H.A.R.E. programme or credit union as community development funds
- 10-20% in any existing clean public power utility
- 20-40% in a social investment fund
- 10% to a public interest investment (school, hospital, research centre)

## Key Facts

- Started in 1983 by the Permaculture Institute
- Inspired by E. F. Schumacher group seminars in the USA
- By 1988, hundreds of ethical financial organizations existed worldwide
- Ethical brokerages handled over $160 billion annually in the USA alone
- The movement's growth is described as exponential
- The Permaculture Institute began with 12 people in Australia; grew to 60-100 attendees
- Existing banks, credit unions, and cooperatives began rewriting charters to include
  earth care and people care values

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Cultivation Finance:** Earthbank-style funds can finance [[mushroom-cultivation]] start-ups,
  greenhouse construction, and substrate processing equipment through community ethical
  investment pools
- **Clean Food Investment:** The movement's positive criteria directly support investment
  in organic cultivation, pesticide-free mushroom growing, [[soil-health-ethics-and-sustainable-agriculture]]
- **Reforestation Funding:** Community reafforestation projects funded through Earthbank
  groups create future mushroom foraging habitat and substrate material sources
- **Nursery Finance:** Plant and spawn nurseries qualify as ethical investments that assist
  people's self-reliance and produce necessary biological resources
- **Research Funding:** Mycological research, cultivar development, and growing technique
  innovation fit the Earthbank criteria for investment in useful knowledge
- **Cooperative Support:** Earthbank principles directly fund mushroom growing cooperatives,
  community composting operations, and shared-use processing facilities
- **Energy-Efficient Systems:** Investment in solar-powered fruiting chambers, passive
  climate control for mushroom houses, and energy-efficient dehydration systems aligns
  with the conservation priority
