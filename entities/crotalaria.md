# Crotalaria

## Description

Crotalaria is a large genus of leguminous plants in the family Fabaceae, commonly known as rattlepods. In permaculture and dryland garden systems, Crotalaria species serve as multi-functional companion plants providing nitrogen fixation, nematode suppression, wind shelter, and mulch biomass.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Family**: Fabaceae (Leguminosae)
- **Subfamily**: Faboideae
- **Genus**: Crotalaria
- **Number of species**: Approximately 600-700 species worldwide
- **Common names**: Rattlepod, rattlebox, showy crotalaria
- **Growth habit**: Annual or perennial herbs, shrubs; varies by species

## Key Facts

- **Nitrogen fixation**: As a legume, Crotalaria forms symbiotic associations with rhizobia bacteria in root nodules, converting atmospheric nitrogen into plant-available forms. This enriches soil fertility for neighboring crops
- **Nematode suppression**: Crotalaria species produce compounds toxic to root-knot nematodes (Meloidogyne spp.), making them valuable companion plants for nematode-susceptible crops like tomatoes, peppers, and carrots
- **Wind shelter**: Tall-growing species provide effective windbreaks for vegetable beds, reducing wind damage to onions and other wind-sensitive crops
- **Mulch production**: Produces substantial above-ground biomass that can be cut and used as garden mulch
- **[[nematode-management]]**: Interplanted with tomatoes and peppers to suppress nematode populations in [[crotalaria-juncea]]* (sunn hemp): Widely used as [[green-manure]] [[cover-crop]], producing 3-4 tons of dry matter per hectare
- *Crotalaria spectabilis* (showy crotalaria): Used for nematode control in crop rotations
- *Crotalaria retusa* (rattleweed): Common in tropical pastures

## Toxicity Warning

Many Crotalaria species contain pyrrolizidine alkaloids, which are toxic to livestock and humans. They should not be used as forage for grazing animals. This toxicity, however, contributes to their nematode-suppressing properties. Handle with care and do not consume any part of the plant.

## Management Notes

- Easy to establish from seed in warm-season plantings
- Requires full sun and well-drained soil
- Some species are annuals, others short-lived perennials
- Allow to grow to sufficient height before cutting for mulch
- Can become weedy in some environments — manage seed set if spreading is a concern
