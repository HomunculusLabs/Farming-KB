## Distribution and Habitat

Carex dioica has a wide but discontinuous distribution across Europe, from the British Isles and Scandinavia eastward to Siberia and the Russian Far East. It occurs in mountainous regions of central and southern Europe, including the Alps, Carpathians, and Balkans.

In the British Isles, it is predominantly a northern and western species, found in Scotland, northern England, Wales, and Ireland. It is rare and declining in lowland England due to drainage of calcareous fens.

The species is strongly associated with calcareous wetland habitats, including spring-fed fens, flushes on limestone slopes, and damp calcareous grassland. It requires permanently moist, base-rich soils.

It is a characteristic species of the Caricion davallianae alliance, a community type of calcareous spring fens. It frequently grows alongside [[pinguicula-vulgaris]].

Soil pH preference is distinctly calcareous, with optimal growth at pH 6.5 to 8.0. The species is absent from strongly acidic peat bogs and oligotrophic wetlands.

Hydrological requirements include a consistently high water table within 10 centimetres of the soil surface. The species is intolerant of both prolonged desiccation and deep submersion.

## Ecology

The dioecious sedge is an important component of species-rich calcareous fen communities. Its diminutive stature allows it to occupy the ground layer beneath taller fen herbs and grasses.

Because male and female flowers occur on separate plants, successful reproduction requires both sexes to be present in close proximity. This reproductive constraint limits colonisation ability and contributes to the species' patchy distribution.

Flowering occurs from April through June, with wind pollination. Male spikes release pollen earlier than female stigmas become receptive, promoting outcrossing between individuals.

The species supports a specialised invertebrate fauna. Sedge-feeding moth larvae and leaf-mining flies have been recorded on Carex dioica, though the small stature of the plant limits the invertebrate biomass it can support.

Conservation value is high. Carex dioica is listed as a species of conservation concern in several European countries and is a positive indicator for calcareous fen habitat quality assessments.

The species is sensitive to drainage, eutrophication, and grazing abandonment. Decline has been noted in many European countries where traditional low-intensity management of wet meadows has ceased.

In the European Red List, Carex dioica is assessed as Least Concern globally but is regionally threatened in several countries including the Netherlands, Germany, and lowland England. Habitat loss through agricultural intensification is the primary threat.

## Cultivation and Uses

Carex dioica is occasionally cultivated in specialist alpine and wetland plant collections. It is not widely available in [[tropical-rainforest-seedling-mycorrhiza-ecological-restoration]] of calcareous fen habitats. However, successful establishment requires carefully matched hydrological and soil chemistry conditions.

Traditional uses are essentially absent due to the small stature of the plant. The thin leaves are too short and fine for thatching, basketry, or any practical application.

No pharmacological properties have been documented. The species has no known uses in [[childrens-herbal-medicine]] or any other applied context.
