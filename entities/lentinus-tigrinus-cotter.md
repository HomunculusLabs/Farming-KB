## Lentinus tigrinus (Tiger Sawgill)

## Description

Lentinus tigrinus, commonly known as the tiger sawgill, is an edible wood-decay fungus characterized by its distinctive tough, leathery fruiting body with a scaly cap surface. The species produces small to medium-sized mushrooms with serrated (saw-like) gill edges and a fibrous stem. It is found worldwide on dead hardwood, particularly oak and other broadleaf trees, and plays an important ecological role as a decomposer in forest ecosystems.

## Key Facts

- Primordia can be initiated on agar at localized sites of hormonal stimulus, demonstrating that fruiting can be spatially controlled.
- Used as a demonstration species for [[primordial-hormone-extract]] techniques, where hormone concentrations from primordia trigger site-specific fruiting.
- Possesses a tough, leathery texture due to its high chitin content, typical of the genus Lentinus.
- A white-rot fungus capable of decomposing lignin in hardwood substrates.
- Found on dead stumps, logs, and fallen branches of hardwood trees in temperate and tropical regions worldwide.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Lentinus tigrinus serves as a model species for studying primordial hormone extracts and their role in inducing mushroom fruiting. Its responsiveness to hormonal stimulus at specific loci on agar plates demonstrates that fruiting can be site-specifically controlled through hormone application. This technique has potential applications in commercial cultivation for controlling the placement and timing of mushroom production, optimizing space utilization in fruiting rooms and complementing production cycles. The species also represents the Lentinus genus, which includes economically important cultivated species such as shiitake (Lentinula edodes).
