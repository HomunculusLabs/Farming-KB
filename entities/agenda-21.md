## Description

Agenda 21 is the comprehensive global action plan for sustainable development adopted at the 1992 [[new-united-nations-concept]] Conference on Environment and Development (Earth Summit) in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil. The "21" refers to the 21st century. It was the first global plan to comprehensively address the relationship between [[fungal-melanin-environmental-protection-stress-resistance-gadd]] and economic development at local, national, and international levels.

## Classification

- **Type**: International policy framework / UN action plan
- **Adopted**: 1992, UN Earth Summit, Rio de Janeiro
- **Scope**: Global sustainable development
- **Follow-up**: 2002 World Summit on Sustainable Development (Johannesburg)

## Key Facts

- Revealed at the 1992 UN Earth Summit as the first global plan for sustainable development.
- The "21" designation refers to preparation for the 21st century.
- Called for full implementation at the 2002 World Summit on Sustainable Development in Johannesburg.
- Paired with the Millennium Development Goals (target date 2015), which included: eradicating poverty and hunger, universal primary education, gender equality, reduced child mortality, improved maternal health, combating diseases, environmental sustainability, and global partnership.
- The UN declared a "Decade of Education for Sustainable Development" starting January 2005 to promote understanding of Agenda 21 and the Millennium Development Goals.
- Prompted scores of governmental and non-governmental organizations to work toward sustainability in economic, ethical, social, technological, and environmental domains.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Agenda 21 created the international policy context for promoting [[mushroom-cultivation]] as a sustainable development tool. Under its framework, organizations like ZERI developed projects that use [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]] to address multiple Millennium Development Goals simultaneously — poverty reduction, food security, environmental sustainability, and economic development. The Hong Kong government's substitution of [[mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities-training-program]] [[natural-free-range-poultry-farming]] after the avian flu crisis exemplifies an Agenda 21-aligned response.

## Historical Context and Development

The Agenda 21 emerged from international deliberation processes
involving multiple stakeholders and national representatives.
The document reflects the political and scientific consensus
of its era while establishing frameworks for future action.

## Key Principles and Recommendations

Central to the Agenda 21 are principles of intergenerational
equity and ecological stewardship. The recommendations
address both immediate policy needs and long-term systemic
transformation toward sustainable practice.

## Legacy and Continuing Influence

The influence of the Agenda 21 extends far beyond its original
publication context. Subsequent international agreements
and national policies continue to reference its core
principles as foundational to environmental governance.
