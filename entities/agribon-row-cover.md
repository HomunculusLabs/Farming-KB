## Agribon Row Cover

## Description

Agribon is a brand of spun-bonded polypropylene row cover fabric used extensively in protected-culture agriculture. In the [[winter-harvest-system]] developed by [[eliot-coleman]], Agribon serves as both an inner greenhouse layer and a field-level covering for quick-hoops, providing frost protection and creating microclimates that enable winter vegetable production without supplemental heat.

## Classification

- **Entity Type:** Agricultural Material / Frost Protection Fabric
- **Category:** Row Cover, [[floating-row-cover]], Frost Blanket
- **Material:** Spun-bonded polypropylene
- **Primary Distributor:** Johnny's Selected Seeds

## Key Properties

Agribon row covers function as permeable barriers that:

1. **Trap radiant heat** — Reduce heat loss from the soil and plant canopy during cold nights
2. **Allow light transmission** — Permit photosynthetically active radiation (PAR) to reach crops
3. **Permit air and water exchange** — The spun-bonded structure allows moisture and gases to pass through, preventing condensation buildup and reducing disease pressure
4. **Provide wind protection** — Create a calm microenvironment that reduces desiccation and physical damage to tender crops

## Application in Winter Harvest Systems

### Greenhouse Inner Layer

In Coleman's system, Agribon is deployed as an inner covering inside unheated greenhouse structures, creating a double-layer protected environment:

- The outer greenhouse glazing (typically polyethylene film) provides the first layer of protection
- The inner Agribon layer provides a second insulating barrier
- The air space between the two layers acts as additional insulation
- This double-layer system protects winter salad crops to temperatures as low as approximately 15°F (-9°C) inside the greenhouse

### Quick-Hoops Field Covering

Agribon is also used as the covering fabric for quick-hoops — low tunnel structures made from bent metal conduit positioned over field beds:

- Quick-hoops extend the growing season in open field conditions
- The Agribon covering provides several degrees of frost protection
- Crops under quick-hoops can be harvested weeks later than uncovered plantings
- The system is inexpensive, temporary, and easily managed by a single person

## Practical Considerations

- **Weight grades:** Agribon is available in different weights (grams per square meter), with heavier grades providing more frost protection but less light transmission
- **Durability:** With proper care, Agribon covers can last multiple seasons
- **Storage:** Should be stored dry and out of direct sunlight when not in use to extend lifespan
- **Deployment timing:** Covers should be deployed before hard freezes to allow the soil to retain accumulated heat
- **Ventilation:** On sunny winter days, temperatures under row covers can rise rapidly; vents or temporary removal may be needed

## Temperature Management Context

In the winter harvest system, Agribon row covers work within a carefully managed temperature framework:

- **75°F (24°C)** — Optimum photosynthesis and plant growth
- **55°F (13°C)** — Average daytime greenhouse temperature during coldest months
- **35°F (2°C)** — Ideal minimum greenhouse air temperature (no freezing of crops)
- **15°F (-9°C)** — Lower limit for quality harvest of most winter salad crops

The row covers extend the range of conditions under which quality harvest is possible.

## Key Facts

- Purchased primarily from Johnny's Selected Seeds in the Coleman system
- Essential component of the low-cost winter harvest approach that eliminates the need for supplemental heating
- Functions as part of a layered protection strategy combining greenhouse glazing, row covers, and the insulating properties of snow
- Available in multiple weight grades to suit different protection needs

## Relevance to Cultivation

Agribon row covers are one of the key enabling technologies for four-season harvesting in cold climates. Their importance to cultivation includes:

- **Season Extension:** Allow growers to produce marketable crops through winter months in regions previously considered too cold
- **Energy Independence:** Eliminate the need for fossil-fuel heating in greenhouse systems
- **Crop Quality:** Maintain crop quality by preventing freeze damage while allowing continued slow growth
- **Economic Viability:** The low cost of row cover systems compared to heated greenhouse infrastructure makes winter production economically accessible to small farms
- **Scalability:** Row cover systems can be scaled from small garden beds to commercial acreage with minimal capital investment
