# Cosmos bipinnatus (*Cosmos bipinnatus*)

## Overview

Cosmos bipinnatus, commonly known as the garden cosmos or Mexican aster, is an
annual herbaceous plant in the family Asteraceae. Native to the meadowlands and
scrub of Mexico, it has become one of the most widely cultivated ornamental
annuals worldwide. The species is valued for its delicate, finely divided
foliage and prolific production of daisy-like flowers in shades of pink, white,
and mauve.

It grows rapidly from seed, reaching heights of 60–150 cm depending on
cultivar and growing conditions. Garden cosmos thrives in poor to average soils
and is notably drought-tolerant once established, making it a staple of
low-maintenance gardens and wildflower meadows. The flowers are attractive to
bees, butterflies, and other pollinators throughout its long blooming season
from midsummer to first frost.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Clade:** Tracheophytes, Angiosperms, Eudicots, Asterids
- **Order:** Asterales
- **Family:** Asteraceae
- **Tribe:** Coreopsideae
- **Genus:** Cosmos
- **Species:** C. bipinnatus

The specific epithet *bipinnatus* refers to the bipinnately compound leaves.
The species was first described by Antonio José Cavanilles in 1791 based on
collections from Mexico. Synonyms include *Coreopsis bipinnata* Cav.

Notable cultivars include 'Sensation' (tall, mixed colors), 'Sonata' (dwarf
series, 30 cm), 'Purity' (pure white), 'Dazzler' (vivid pink), and the
'Seashells' series with uniquely tubular ray florets.

## Morphology

### Growth Habit

Cosmos bipinnatus is an erect, branching annual [[herb-growing-guide]] 60–150 cm tall.
The stems are slender, green to purplish, and sparsely covered with fine hairs.
Branching is primarily in the upper portion, producing an open, airy habit.

### Leaves

The leaves are bipinnately dissected into narrow, linear segments 2–8 mm wide,
giving the foliage a delicate, fern-like appearance. Individual leaves are 5–15
cm long and arranged oppositely on the lower stem, becoming alternate higher
up. The leaf segments are bright green and slightly hairy.

### Flowers

Capitula (flower heads) are 5–10 cm in diameter, solitary or in loose
corymbose clusters. Each head bears 8–13 ray florets, each 2–4 cm long, in
shades of white, pink, rose, or lavender. The disc florets are numerous,
tubular, yellow to orange, forming a central dome 1–2 cm across.

### Fruit and Seed

Achenes are linear, 10–15 mm long, dark brown to black, with a slender beak
and two awns 3–6 mm long. Each achene weighs approximately 2–3 mg. A single
plant can produce 5,000–10,000 seeds under favorable conditions. Seeds remain
viable for 2–3 years under cool, dry storage.

## Distribution and Habitat

Cosmos bipinnatus is native to Mexico, ranging from the central highlands to
southern states including Morelos, Guerrero, and Oaxaca. It occurs naturally in
open meadows, pine-oak woodlands, disturbed sites, and along roadsides at
elevations of 800–2,400 meters.
