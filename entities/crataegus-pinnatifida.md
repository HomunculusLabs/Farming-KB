## Overview

**Crataegus pinnatifida**, known as Chinese hawthorn or shanzha (山楂), is a deciduous tree or large shrub in the family Rosaceae.

It is native to China, Korea, and Mongolia, where it has been cultivated for its edible fruit [[neurotropic-fungi-japan-east-asia-species-cultural-context]].

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:** Plantae → Tracheophyta → Magnoliopsida → Rosales → Rosaceae → Crataegus → C. pinnatifida.

The species was described by Alexander von Bunge in 1835. The epithet *pinnatifida* refers to the pinnately lobed leaf margins.

Chinese hawthorn is a small deciduous tree reaching 4–8 meters in height with a dense, rounded crown.

The bark is grey-brown, developing shallow fissures and becoming scaly with age on mature trunks.

Branches are typically armed with stout spines 1–3 cm long, though cultivated varieties are often spineless.

Leaves are alternate, broadly ovate, 5–10 cm long and 4–8 cm wide, with 3–5 pairs of irregular lobes along the margins.
