## Overview

Cyclone Tracy was a **Category 4 tropical cyclone** that struck Darwin, Northern Territory, Australia, on **24-25 December 1974**. Referenced on page 87 of Holmgren's *Permaculture: Principles and Pathways Beyond Sustainability*, it serves as a case study in vulnerability, resilience, and self-reliance.

## The Event

- Wind gusts up to 240 km/h before instruments were destroyed
- 71 people killed; over 70% of Darwin's buildings destroyed or severely damaged
- Approximately 30,000 of 47,000 residents evacuated — largest mass evacuation in Australian history

## Ecological Impact

Most trees stripped, snapped, or uprooted. Significant wildlife mortality. Coastal landforms altered. The destruction created conditions for ecological succession, similar to the "release" phase in Holling's adaptive cycle.

## Relevance to Permaculture

Holmgren references Cyclone Tracy in the context of **Self-Reliance and Disaster Preparedness** (page 87):

- **Distributed infrastructure** is more resilient than centralized systems
- **Redundancy** in essential systems (water, food, shelter)
- **Community self-organization** accelerates recovery
- **Building design** for local conditions dramatically reduces damage
- Catastrophic disturbances are natural and can be designed for

## Lessons for Cultivation

- Wind-resistant plantings and diversity ensure some crops survive extreme events
- Infrastructure designed for local extreme weather
- Seed saving maintains genetic resources after catastrophic loss
- Ground covers and mulch prevent soil loss during extreme rainfall
