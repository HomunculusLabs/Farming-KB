## Description

Gabion dams are permeable barriers constructed from rock-filled wire mesh baskets (gabions) placed across eroding valleys in dryland regions. Unlike conventional dams designed to impound water, gabion dams create silt fields and spread water across the landscape. They are primarily erosion control and water-spreading structures rather than water storage structures. In permaculture design, they serve as critical tools for rehabilitating degraded dryland landscapes and re-establishing productive soil moisture regimes.

## Classification

- **Category**: Earthworks / Erosion control structure
- **Domain**: Permaculture, dryland restoration, civil engineering
- **Structure type**: Permeable barrier dam
- **Primary function**: Silt capture and water spreading
- **Climate applicability**: Drylands primarily

## Key Facts

### Construction

- Built from steel wire mesh units ("baskets") filled with rocks, stones, or shingle
- Individual gabion units are strong wire baskets typically rectangular in form
- Units are assembled in place and filled with locally available stone
- Wall heights for farm construction: 0.5–2 m (2–6.5 feet)
- Resistant to flood forces due to permeability and mass

### Primary Functions

1. **Silt capture**: Create flat areas where silt loads deposit usefully
2. **Water spreading**: Distribute flood flows across valley floors rather than concentrating erosion
3. **Absorption bed creation**: Form zones where flood water infiltrates into soils
4. **Erosion stabilization**: Arrest gully and valley erosion in degraded landscapes

### Design Principles

- Unlike storage dams, gabion dams are intentionally permeable
- Water passes through rather than being impounded
- Silt accumulates behind the structure over time
- The resulting silt field becomes a moisture-rich planting zone
- Multiple gabion structures can be placed in series along a valley

### Materials

- Steel or galvanized wire mesh for baskets
- Locally sourced rocks, stones, or shingle for fill
- No concrete or specialized construction materials required
- Can be assembled by hand with minimal equipment

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Gabion dams create moist, silt-rich deposition zones that can support cultivation in otherwise arid landscapes. The accumulated silt and organic matter behind gabion structures creates ideal substrate conditions for moisture-loving fungi. In dryland mycorestoration projects, gabion-sealed valleys provide the sustained soil moisture needed for fungal mycelium to establish and propagate through degraded soils.

The silt fields created by gabion dams progressively build soil depth and organic matter content, transforming eroded gullies into productive growing areas. This has direct application for mycoremediation of eroded landscapes, establishing fungal networks in degraded dryland soils, creating moist microsites for mushroom cultivation in arid regions, and building soil carbon through combined silt capture and biological activity.
