## Overview

**Saruma henryi** is the sole species in the monotypic genus *Saruma*, belonging to the family Aristolochiaceae. It is a herbaceous perennial endemic to central and southwestern China, where it grows in shaded forest understories and rocky ravines.

The genus name is an anagram of its close relative *Asarum*, reflecting the taxonomic proximity of the two genera within Aristolochiaceae.

*Saruma henryi* is distinguished from *Asarum* species by its erect, leafy stems (rather than stemless habit), larger and more showy flowers, and its three-merous flowers with distinct petals.

The species is cultivated as an ornamental shade plant in temperate gardens and has received the Royal Horticultural Society's Award of Garden Merit.

It was named in honor of Augustine Henry (1857–1930), an Irish botanist who collected extensively in China and discovered many new species.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

*Saruma henryi* Oliv. was described by Daniel Oliver in 1889 based on specimens collected by Augustine Henry in Hubei province.

The genus *Saruma* was established simultaneously with the species description.

Molecular phylogenetic studies place *Saruma* as sister to the *Asarum*-*Hexastylis* clade within the subfamily Asaroideae of Aristolochiaceae.

The anagrammatic genus name is one of several examples in botanical nomenclature where letters of a related genus were rearranged.

Growth habit is an upright, clump-forming herbaceous perennial reaching 30–60 cm in height.

Stems are erect, branching, pubescent, and covered with cordate to ovate leaves.

Leaves are alternate, cordate at the base, 5–12 cm long and 4–10 cm wide, with dentate margins.

## Cultivation

*Saruma henryi* has been cultivated in western gardens since its introduction to Europe in the late 19th century.

It is valued as an unusual and attractive shade perennial with long-lasting flowers and interesting foliage.

The plant is hardy to USDA Zone 6 (approximately -23°C) when grown in sheltered positions.

It prefers partial to full shade, consistent soil moisture, and a humus-rich, well-drained [[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]] or autumn.
