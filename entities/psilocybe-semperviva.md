# Psilocybe semperviva

**Psilocybe semperviva** is a rare psilocybin-containing mushroom species documented from Europe. The species name "semperviva" translates from Latin as "always living" or "evergreen," likely referring to its persistent fruiting habit or the resilient nature of its mycelial network. The species was identified through systematic mycochemical surveys rather than through traditional ethnomycological knowledge.

## Taxonomy

- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Hymenogastraceae
- **Genus:** Psilocybe
- **Authority:** Described from European collections

The species belongs to the bluing, psilocybin-producing section of Psilocybe, though its exact sectional placement is subject to revision as molecular phylogenetics reshapes the genus. The genus Psilocybe has undergone significant taxonomic reorganization, with DNA sequencing revealing that many traditional species groups are polyphyletic.

Within the European Psilocybe flora, P. semperviva contributes to the recognition that Europe harbors a more diverse assemblage of psychoactive fungi than was appreciated in earlier mycological literature. It sits alongside better-known species such as [[psilocybe-semilanceata]], [[psilocybe-cyanescens]], and [[psilocybe-serbica]].

## Description

### Cap (Pileus)

The pileus is small to medium, typically 1 to 3 cm in diameter.

- **Shape:** Conic to broadly conic when young, with a pronounced umbo; expanding to convex or plano-convex with age
- **Surface:** Smooth to slightly viscid when moist; dry and silky when dry
- **Color:** Hygrophanous; dark chestnut-brown or olive-brown when fresh, fading to straw-brown or buff when dry
- **Margin:** Slightly translucent-striate when moist; may show bluing on handling

### Gills (Lamellae)

- **Attachment:** Adnate to broadly adnexate
- **Spacing:** Moderately close to subdistant
- **Color:** Grayish when young; darkening to purplish-brown to nearly black with maturity
- **Edges:** Slightly paler than faces; may be fimbriate from cheilocystidia

### Stipe (Stipe)

- **Dimensions:** 3 to 6 cm long; 1 to 3 mm thick
- **Shape:** Cylindrical; equal or slightly thickened at base
- **Surface:** Finely pruinose near apex; smooth below
- **Color:** Whitish to pale brown; often darker at base
- **Bluing:** Frequently observed, particularly at base and on handling
- **Veil:** Partial veil evanescent; little or no annular remnant

### Spores and Microscopy

| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| Spore print | Dark purplish-brown to nearly black |
| Spore shape | Ellipsoid to subamygdaliform |
| Spore size | ~10-14 x 6-9 µm |
| Spore wall | Thick with distinct germ pore |
| Basidia | Typically four-spored |
| Cystidia | Pleurocystidia and cheilocystidia present |

## Distribution and Habitat

Psilocybe semperviva has been documented from European collections, though its full geographic range remains incompletely known. The species is typically found in:

- Grassy habitats including pastures, meadows, and parklands
- Areas with accumulations of organic debris
- Disturbed ground and margins of paths and clearings

Fruiting is triggered by cool, damp conditions, typically occurring in autumn in temperate European climates. Its rarity may reflect genuine scarcity or simply the difficulty of distinguishing it from closely related species in the field.

## Chemistry

The species has been confirmed to contain [[psilocybin]] and [[psilocin]] as its primary psychoactive alkaloids. The bluing reaction is caused by oxidative degradation of psilocin, which is produced by enzymatic dephosphorylation of psilocybin.

Specific alkaloid concentrations have not been extensively documented. Potency likely falls within the moderate range typical of European Psilocybe species, though significant variation between collections is common.

Minor alkaloids that may be present include:

- [[baeocystin]] — A biosynthetic precursor of psilocybin
- [[norbaeocystin]] — A related tryptamine alkaloid found in many psilocybin-containing species

## Similar Species

P. semperviva may be confused with several other European Psilocybe species:

| Species | Distinguishing Features |
|---|---|
| [[psilocybe-semilanceata]] | More conic cap with pronounced papilla; robust curved stipe |
| [[psilocybe-serbica]] | Generally larger and more robust |
| [[psilocybe-bohemica]] | Central European; larger stature |
| [[psilocybe-cyanescens]] | Wood-inhabiting; strongly hygrophanous with intense bluing |

Microscopic examination of spore morphology and cystidia is typically required for confident species-level identification. The bluing reaction indicates psychoactive potential but is not definitive for species identification.

## Cultural Significance

The species was documented in Jochen Gartz's comprehensive survey *Magic Mushrooms Around the World*, reflecting the growing recognition through the 1980s and 1990s that Europe harbors greater diversity of psilocybin-containing fungi than previously appreciated. Earlier literature focused primarily on a few well-known species, and systematic chemical screening has steadily expanded the known list.

## Legal Status

As a confirmed psilocybin-containing species, P. semperviva is controlled in most jurisdictions that regulate psilocybin mushrooms. Legal restrictions generally apply regardless of whether the species is specifically named in legislation.
