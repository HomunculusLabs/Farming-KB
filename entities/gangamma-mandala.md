## Description

**Gangamma's Mandala** is a circular permaculture garden design pattern named
after Gangamma, an Indian permaculture student. It represents an idealised
tropical home garden layout based on **zero-runoff** principles, using keyhole
beds, banana circles, and concentric planting zones arranged in a mandala
pattern. The design integrates everyday food production, greywater recycling,
mulch generation, and animal management into a compact, accessible system.

## Classification

- **Category**: Technique — Garden design / Permaculture pattern
- **Domain**: Tropical and subtropical home gardening
- **Origin**: Indian permaculture tradition, adapted by Mollison
- **Scale**: Home garden to community kitchen garden

## Key Facts

- **Core assembly**: 4–5 **banana circles** forming the garden centre, each
  capable of containing a small pond for frogs and water convolvulus or taro.
- **Surrounding beds**: 8–12 **keyhole beds** radiating from the centre,
  accessed by narrow paths.
- **Keyhole access**: A keyhole path accesses the central banana circles.
- **Zero runoff**: All water is captured within the system — rainwater, tank
  overflow, and greywater irrigate the garden.
- **Vertical integration**: *Leucaena* or palms planted at keyhole junctions
  for high shade and mulch production.
- **Hedge surround**: Outer barrier hedge provides annual mulch, animal
  exclusion, and wind protection.
- **Scalable**: Design can be scaled up for community kitchens at schools,
  villages, and rural centres (e.g., Karnataka state, India).

## Planting Zones (Concentric Rings)

- **Inner**: Banana circles with compost pits, small ponds, papaya
- **Pathside**: Herbs and greens picked daily (chard, perennial spinaches)
- **Narrow beds**: Frequently picked vegetables (tomatoes, eggplant, peppers,
  beans, kale, corn, okra) — all within reach from paths
- **Outer beds**: Root crops and cut-and-remove crops (potatoes, sweet potato,
  carrots, cauliflower, cabbage) — one step onto bed to harvest
- **Hedge surround**: [[outdoor-mushroom-cultivation]]** on buried substrate blocks.
- The deep mulch layers (30 cm / 1 foot of trodden, wetted mulch) create
  excellent fungal habitat and support rapid **soil food web development**.
- Worm populations build up over 9–15 months, coexisting with fungal
  decomposers in the mulch layer.
- **Comfrey** in the hedge surround acts as a dynamic accumulator, drawing
  up minerals that feed both plants and soil microorganisms.
- The system's emphasis on **no-dig gardening** preserves mycorrhizal
  networks and mycelial mats throughout the beds.
