## Mushroom Bed Spawning

## Description

Mushroom bed spawning is the process of inoculating prepared compost beds with mushroom mycelium (spawn) to initiate colonization and subsequent fruiting. This is a critical step in [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]] that requires careful attention to temperature, moisture, and technique. Benjamin Minge Duggar provided detailed early 20th-century instructions for spawning that established principles still followed in modified form today.

## Classification

- **Category**: Cultivation technique / Inoculation process
- **Type**: Mechanical procedure with biological timing requirements
- **Timing in crop cycle**: Between composting/bed installation and casing
- **Equipment**: Spawn (brick or flake), hands or spawning tools

## Key Facts

- Beds should be spawned only after the compost temperature falls to 65-75 degrees F (18-24 degrees C), with 65-70 degrees F being preferable when maintaining 55 degrees F during the growing period.
- Spawning at temperatures of 85-90 degrees F (29-32 degrees C), as advised in some earlier guides, is fatal if maintained for extended periods.
- Spawning at 50-65 degrees F is acceptable but results in slower growth; below 50 degrees F growth is very slow.
- When using [[brick-spawn]], each brick should be broken into 9-12+ pieces (each about the size of a hen egg), with one piece per square foot of bed space.
- Flake spawn requires a portion about half the size of one fist per inoculation point.
- Spawn pieces should be inserted deep enough to be covered with at least 1 inch of compost and pressed firmly.
- An aerating passage left beside the inserted spawn piece promotes mycelial growth.
- Under proper conditions, spawn begins visible growth within 7-10 days of insertion.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

### Spawning Procedure

The spawning technique described by Duggar involves a specific method:

1. **Lift the compost** with one hand to create an opening
2. **Insert the spawn piece** deep enough with the other hand
3. **Cover with 1 inch of compost** over the spawn piece
4. **Press firmly** over the inserted spawn to ensure good contact
5. **Leave an aerating passage** to one side of the spawn piece

This method ensures:
- Good mycelium-substrate contact for rapid colonization
- Protection from drying at the surface
- Adequate aeration for mycelial respiration
- Prevention of contamination at the spawn site

### Pre-Spawning Assessment

Before spawning, growers must verify:

- **Temperature**: Compost temperature has peaked and is declining toward 70 degrees F
- **Moisture content**: The hand test — compressed compost should moisten the hand without releasing drops of water (approximately 65% moisture)
- **Ammonia level**: Should be absent or minimal; residual ammonia will kill spawn
- **Mold assessment**: Some mold growth before spawning is acceptable, but heavy colonization suggests the compost is not ready or conditions need adjustment

### Post-Spawning Care

Between spawning and casing:
- Monitor spawn run — visible white mycelial growth spreading from inoculation points
- Maintain bed moisture without direct watering of spawn
- Wait until spawn is running well before applying casing layer
- Early casing may be necessary only to maintain moisture in dry conditions
- If profuse mold growth occurs after spawning, further compression or early casing may help

### Spawn Types Referenced

Duggar distinguishes between:
- **Brick spawn**: Compressed blocks of mycelium on substrate, broken into pieces for spawning
- **Flake spawn**: Loose, flaky mycelium on grain or straw substrate
- **Green spawn**: Freshly prepared spawn that may produce premature small mushrooms (undesirable)

### Temperature-Moisture Interactions

The success of spawning depends critically on the interplay between temperature and moisture:

- Too hot + too wet = bacterial bloom suppressing mycelium
- Too hot + too dry = spawn desiccation
- Too cold + proper moisture = slow but acceptable growth
- Proper temperature + proper moisture = optimal colonization in 7-10 days
