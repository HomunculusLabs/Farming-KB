## Overview

Orson K. Miller Jr. (1930–2006) was an American mycologist whose published botanical descriptions provided key identification criteria for *[[brassinosteroid-morphological-effects-psilocybe-cubensis-fruiting]]* (syn. *[[stropharia-cubensis]]*). His book *Mushrooms of North America* served as the primary taxonomic reference cited in early cultivation guides for magic mushrooms.

## Key Contributions

### Taxonomic Descriptions

Miller provided the botanical description of *Stropharia cubensis* that became the standard identification reference in the cultivation community. His description, as quoted in the *Magic Mushroom Grower's Guide* (Oss & Oeric, 1976), includes:

- **Cap**: 1.5–8 cm broad, conic to bell-shaped, convex in age, viscid, without hairs, whitish to pale yellow, light brownish in age, stains bluish in age
- **Flesh**: Firm, white, bruises blue
- **Gills**: Adnate (attached) to adnexed (notched), close, grey to violet-grey in age with white edges
- **Stalk**: 4–15 cm long, 4–14 mm thick, enlarging toward base, dry, without hairs, white staining blue when bruised
- **Veil**: White, leaving a superior membranous ring
- **Spores**: 10–17 × 7–10 µm, elliptical to oval in side-view, thick-walled, with a large pore at apex, purple-brown [[spore-print]]
- **Cystidia**: Sterile cells on gill edge, club-shaped with rounded heads

### Genus Placement

Miller placed this species in the genus *Psilocybe*, following the reclassification by [[rolf-singer]]. This reclassification from *Stropharia* to *Psilocybe* reflected evolving taxonomic understanding of the Strophariaceae. Both names — *Stropharia cubensis* and *Psilocybe cubensis* — continue to be used in different contexts, with the older *Stropharia* name being more common in cultivation literature.

## Published Works

- **Mushrooms of North America** — A comprehensive field guide that became a standard reference for [[mushroom-identification]] in North America. This work provided the detailed morphological description of *Stropharia cubensis* that was used by cultivators for field identification.

## Impact on Mushroom Cultivation

Miller's taxonomic work had an outsized influence on the practical cultivation of psilocybin mushrooms:

- His species description provided the clear, standardized morphological criteria needed for reliable field identification of *P. cubensis*
- The emphasis on blue-staining as a diagnostic feature helped cultivators verify specimens before collecting spores
- His measurements of spore dimensions and other microscopic features supported microscopic verification

## Broader Mycological Career

While best known in the cultivation community for his description of *P. cubensis*, Miller was a prolific mycologist with broader contributions to North American fungal taxonomy. He described numerous species and contributed significantly to the understanding of fungal diversity across the continent.

## Legacy

Miller's descriptions continue to be referenced in modern cultivation guides and online resources. His clear, detailed morphological descriptions set a standard for practical field identification that remains relevant despite advances in molecular taxonomy.
