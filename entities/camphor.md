## Chemical Properties

- **IUPAC name:** 1,7,7-Trimethylbicyclo[2.2.1]heptan-2-one
- **Molecular formula:** C10H16O
- **Molecular weight:** 152.23 g/mol
- **CAS number:** 76-22-2 (racemic); 464-48-2 ((+)-camphor); 464-49-3 ((−)-camphor)
- **Appearance:** Colorless to white crystalline solid, translucent waxy lumps
- **Melting point:** 175–177 °C
- **Boiling point:** 204 °C (sublimes)
- **Odor:** Strong, penetrating, characteristic aromatic odor
- **Solubility:** Slightly soluble in water (~0.7 g/L at 25 °C); soluble in
  ethanol, ether, chloroform, and most organic solvents
- **Optical rotation:** [α]D = +44.2° (natural (−)-camphor)

Camphor belongs to the bornane skeleton class of monoterpenes. The bicyclic
framework consists of a cyclohexane ring fused to a cyclopentane ring with a
ketone at the C-2 position. This rigid structure is responsible for its high
melting point relative to other monoterpenes and its ability to sublime at
moderate temperatures.

## Pharmacology and Effects

Camphor produces multiple pharmacological effects through several receptor systems:

**TRP channel modulation:**
- Potent agonist of TRPV3 and TRPV1 (transient receptor potential channels)
- Activation of TRPV1 underlies the characteristic warming sensation
- TRPV3 activation in keratinocytes contributes to skin penetration effects

**Central nervous system effects:**
- Low doses: mild stimulant, increases alertness and respiratory drive
- Moderate doses: antispasmodic, counterirritant, mild local anesthetic
- High doses: generalized CNS depression, seizures (dose-dependent biphasic effect)

**Antimicrobial activity:**
- Active against Gram-positive bacteria (*[[candida-albicans]]* and dermatophytes
- Insecticidal and moth-repellent properties (basis of naphthalene replacement)

**Topical effects:**
- Counterirritant via TRP channel activation, producing rubefacient warmth
- Mild local anesthetic action at concentrations of 3–11%
- Reduces pruritus through TRPA1/TRPV1 desensitization

⚠️ **Toxicity:** The lethal dose in adults is approximately 50–500 mg/kg.
Seizures can occur at doses as low as 2 g in children. Topical formulations
exceeding 11% camphor are restricted in many jurisdictions.
