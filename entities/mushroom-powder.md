## Description

Mushroom powder is a value-added product created by drying and grinding mushrooms into a fine powder. It serves multiple purposes: long-term storage, nutritional supplementation, culinary ingredient, and medicinal delivery system. Powdering dried mushrooms increases their surface area, allowing for more intense flavor and different texture characteristics compared to fresh mushrooms. [[tradd-cotter]] describes it as one of the best ways to extend the use and sales of mushrooms, particularly for utilizing "waste" — perfectly fine but unsightly mushrooms culled from production lines.

## Classification

- **Category**: Value-added mushroom product
- **Type**: Food product / Storage medium / Culinary ingredient
- **Shelf life**: Years (if kept dry in airtight container)
- **Protein content**: 20–30% by dry weight (retained through powdering)

## Production Method

1. Dry mushrooms in a dehydrator or solar dryer until brittle
2. Break up mushrooms by hand into small pieces
3. Reduce to fine powder using blender or mortar and pestle
4. Store in airtight container (mason jar); optional: add small bag of rice to absorb moisture
5. Powder keeps for years if kept dry

## Culinary Applications

- Dust on scallops or fish, sear in hot oil, then bake gently for mushroom-encrusted dishes
- Mix directly into homemade pasta or bread recipes (replace up to 25% of flour)
- Use as breading for fish, chicken, and vegetables
- Overcome texture objections from people who dislike the soft/slippery texture of fresh mushrooms
- More pungent varieties (shiitake, maitake, almond portabella, morels) work especially well with pastas
- Can be used in ravioli dough and fillings

## Storage and Preservation

- Mushroom powder can be mixed into raw honey for long-term storage
- Honey is naturally antibiotic and resists spoilage, preserving the mushroom powder virtually indefinitely
- Ancient Egyptians stored mushroom extracts in honey; analysis showed retained potency after 4,000+ years
- In disaster relief contexts, powdering enables indefinite storage when sun-drying is not feasible
- Powder renders mushrooms into protein-rich flour usable for pastries and breads

## Binding Considerations

- Mushrooms do not bind as well as wheat flour
- If substituting more than 25% flour with mushroom powder, increase binding agents (egg whites)
- Experiment with smaller quantities if breads or pastas fail to hold together

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Mushroom powder transforms the economics of [[mushroom-cultivation]] by creating a high-value product from what would otherwise be waste. Culled, damaged, or surplus mushrooms that cannot be sold fresh become a marketable product with extended shelf life. This is particularly important for small-scale cultivators who may experience production surges or aesthetic quality variations. The technique also provides a solution for off-season storage, allowing year-round mushroom consumption from seasonal production. In humanitarian contexts, powdering is the key to preserving overproduction for lean periods.
