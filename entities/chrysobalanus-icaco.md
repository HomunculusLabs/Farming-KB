## Chrysobalanus icaco (Coco Plum, Icaco)

## Description

Chrysobalanus icaco is an extremely hardy shrub or small tree reaching up to 12 m, with branches that creep in all directions. Native to south Florida and the Caribbean, it grows in dense clumps on the seashore and in both fresh and saline swamps, extending well inland into mountain regions. The fruit was an important food of the Seminole Indians.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Family**: Chrysobalanaceae
- **Genus**: Chrysobalanus
- **Species**: C. icaco
- **Common names**: Coco plum, icaco
- **Type**: Evergreen shrub to small tree

## Key Facts

- Height: Up to 12 m
- Growth habit: Shrubby with creeping branches forming dense clumps
- Native range: South Florida, Caribbean, tropical Americas
- Habitat: Seashore, fresh and saline swamps; extends into mountains
- Extremely salt tolerant including coastal spray
- Likely frost tolerant given mountain range extension
- Important Seminole Indian food source

## Uses

### Culinary

- Fruit eaten raw
- Stewed with sugar as dessert
- Dried like prunes
- Made into jellies
- Seed kernels roasted and eaten
- Unique preserve technique: fruit pierced to kernel, flavour penetrates seed

### Landscaping

- Excellent coastal hedge plant
- Dense windbreak and privacy screen
- Soil stabilisation on dunes and swamps
