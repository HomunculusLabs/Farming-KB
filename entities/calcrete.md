## Description

Calcrete (also known as caliche, platin, or kunkar) is a hard, mainly level subsurface soil concretion typically found 0.5–1.0 metres below granular or sandy topsoil. It forms from the cementation of soil particles by calcium carbonate and calcium triphosphate deposits. Calcrete is characteristic of coral islands and the downwind areas of desert borders, where evaporation exceeds rainfall and dissolved minerals concentrate at a specific depth below the surface.

The concretion creates an impermeable barrier that prevents both root penetration and water movement between surface soil and deeper subsoil layers. On coral atolls, where calcrete is widespread, fresh-water lenses develop below the caliche layer, making it a dual barrier — blocking roots from reaching water while also protecting the freshwater lens from surface contamination.

## Classification

- **Category:** Soil concretion / hardpan
- **Composition:** Calcium triphosphate, calcium/magnesium concretions
- **Colour:** Whitish to creamy
- **Depth:** Typically 0.5–1.0 m below surface
- **Geographic occurrence:** Coral atolls, desert margins, dry islands, arid coasts
- **Chemical test:** Releases bubbles of carbon dioxide when treated with acid (vinegar)
- **Alternative names:** Caliche, platin, kunkar

## Key Facts

- Roots cannot penetrate calcrete, causing trees to spread laterally and become susceptible to wind-throw
- Must be broken open mechanically before planting trees successfully
- Broken calcrete can serve as a building material and forms safe roofs for tunnels or dugouts
- Worsened by the addition of superphosphate fertilisers, which promote additional calcium cementation
- Fresh-water deposits often develop below calcrete layers on atolls
- Occurs at relatively shallow depths (0.5–1.0 m) beneath topsoil
- Related to but distinct from [[ferricrete]] (iron-cemented) hardpans
- Vinegar test produces CO2 bubbles, distinguishing calcrete from other concretions

## Relevance to Cultivation

Calcrete presents a significant barrier to plant establishment in [[casuarina]], palm, and household wastes, is a proven strategy for production on calcrete-affected atoll soils.

The addition of superphosphate should be avoided on calcrete-prone soils as it worsens the cementation process. This has significant implications for conventional agriculture on tropical islands where superphosphate is routinely applied.

## Management Approaches

- Mechanical ripping or explosive shattering of the hardpan
- Deep mulch pits to establish trees above the concretion
- Use of deep-rooted pioneer species once the layer is breached
- Avoid superphosphate, which can worsen calcium concretion
- Grow pit method for atoll and island cultivation
- Building material extraction can serve dual purpose (construction + soil preparation)
