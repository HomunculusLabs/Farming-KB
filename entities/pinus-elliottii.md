## Overview

Pinus elliottii, commonly known as slash pine, is a fast-growing coniferous tree native to the southeastern [[dmt-maoi-plants-united-states]].

It is one of the most commercially important timber species in the American South, widely planted for pulpwood, sawtimber, and naval stores production.

The species was named in honor of the American agriculturist Stephen Elliott, who made significant botanical contributions to the early study of southeastern flora.

Slash pine is a key component of the flatwoods and wet savanna ecosystems of the Gulf and Atlantic coastal plains.

Two varieties are recognized: typical Pinus elliottii var. elliottii and the South Florida slash pine, Pinus elliottii var. densa.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Pinus elliottii was first described by George Engelmann in 1880.

The authority is commonly cited as Engelm. (Engelmann).

It belongs to Pinus subsection Australes, the southern yellow pines.

Mature trees of the typical variety reach 25 to 35 meters in height with diameters of 50 to 80 centimeters.

The South Florida variety is generally smaller, reaching 15 to 21 meters.

The crown is conical to rounded and relatively open, with ascending branches.

Bark on mature trees is thick, platy, and reddish-brown, divided into large, irregular plates.

Needles are borne in fascicles of two or three, measuring 18 to 30 centimeters in length.

They are dark green, stout, and flexible, persisting for 2 years.

Cones are glossy, lustrous brown, and armed with a sharp prickle at the tip of each scale.

They measure 7 to 15 centimeters in length and are sessile or nearly so.

Cones are serotinous in typical slash pine, remaining closed for several years until opened by fire.

The South Florida variety is less serotinous, with cones opening at maturity.

Seeds are small, 5 to 7 millimeters long, with wings of 15 to 25 millimeters.

## Distribution and Habitat

Pinus elliottii var. elliottii occurs naturally along the coastal plain from South Carolina through Georgia, northern Florida, Alabama, Mississippi, and eastern Louisiana.

Pinus elliottii var. densa is restricted to southern Florida, including the Everglades region and the Florida Keys.

The species has been widely planted outside its native range throughout the southeastern [[hallucinogenic-plants-native-to-united-states]] and in many subtropical countries worldwide.

Natural stands typically occupy flatwoods, wet prairies, and poorly drained sites.

Slash pine tolerates periodic flooding and waterlogged soils better than most other southern pines.

It also grows on well-drained upland sites, though it is most competitive on moist, acidic, sandy or loamy soils.

Elevational range is from sea level to approximately 150 meters.

The species is intolerant of frost and is restricted to areas with mild, short winters.

Plantations have been established successfully in Brazil, Argentina, South Africa, Australia, and China.

## Ecology and Silviculture

Slash pine is an intolerant, fast-growing species that requires full sunlight for optimal growth.

In natural stands, it is a pioneer species that colonizes disturbed sites following fire or logging.

Seedling growth is rapid, with trees reaching 15 to 20 meters in 20 years on favorable sites.
