## Overview

Pinus elliottii, commonly known as slash pine, is a fast-growing coniferous tree native to the southeastern [[hallucinogenic-plants-native-to-united-states]] and in many subtropical countries worldwide.

Natural stands typically occupy flatwoods, wet prairies, and poorly drained sites.

Slash pine tolerates periodic flooding and waterlogged soils better than most other southern pines.

It also grows on well-drained upland sites, though it is most competitive on moist, acidic, sandy or loamy soils.

Elevational range is from sea level to approximately 150 meters.

The species is intolerant of frost and is restricted to areas with mild, short winters.

Plantations have been established successfully in Brazil, Argentina, South Africa, Australia, and China.

## Ecology and Silviculture

Slash pine is an intolerant, fast-growing species that requires full sunlight for optimal growth.

In natural stands, it is a pioneer species that colonizes disturbed sites following fire or logging.

Seedling growth is rapid, with trees reaching 15 to 20 meters in 20 years on favorable sites.
