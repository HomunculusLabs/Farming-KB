# Phyllosphere

## Description

The phyllosphere is the total above-ground surface area of plants, primarily the leaf surfaces, that serves as a habitat for microorganisms. Analogous to the rhizosphere in the root zone, the phyllosphere supports a distinct [[cannabis-cultivar-microbial-community-effects]] that plays a critical role in plant health. Leaves produce exudates — chemical compounds secreted through their surfaces — that attract [[forest-and-bamboo-beneficial-microorganisms]] in the same way root exudates recruit soil biology.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Plant microbiology / Phyllosphere ecology
- **Category:** Plant surface microenvironment
- **Subcategory:** Aerial microbial habitat

## Key Facts

- Leaves produce exudates that attract microorganisms identically to the way roots do, forming a protective biological layer on leaf surfaces.
- Bacteria and fungi colonize the phyllosphere, forming protective layers that act as physical barriers to invasion by pathogenic organisms.
- Some phyllosphere bacteria and fungi produce inhibitory compounds including vitamins and antibiotics that help maintain or improve plant health.
- The phyllosphere microbiome competes with pathogenic organisms for space, nutrients, and exudates on leaf surfaces, preventing disease-causing organisms from establishing.
- Penicillin (produced by the soil-borne fungus Penicillium) and [[streptomycin]] (produced by the soil-borne bacterium Streptomyces) are examples of antibiotic compounds produced by microorganisms that can also operate in phyllosphere contexts.
- When phyllosphere [[cannabis-rhizosphere-microbial-communities]] are disrupted or diminished, plants become vulnerable to foliar diseases and pest establishment.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

The phyllosphere concept explains why foliar sprays of compost tea, [[bokashi-composting-and-effective-microorganisms]] (EM), or beneficial fungal spores can protect plants from disease — they are reinforcing the natural protective microbiome on leaf surfaces. In mycology, the phyllosphere represents an aboveground fungal habitat where endophytic and epiphytic fungi coexist with plants. Foliar applications of mycorrhizal and beneficial fungal spores, whey-based sprays, [[teaming-with-microbes-compost-tea-and-microbial-inoculants]] all target the phyllosphere. Understanding this zone helps cultivators move beyond root-focused soil management to whole-plant biological [[frost-protection-strategies]].
