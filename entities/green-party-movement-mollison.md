## Green Party Movement

## Description

The Green Party movement is a global political movement based on ecological principles, grassroots democracy, social justice, and nonviolence. Bill Mollison references the Green Party in *Permaculture: A Designers' Manual* (Chapter 14.4) as a vehicle for achieving the political changes necessary to bring about ecological transformation, noting its growing representation in Europe by the mid-1980s.

## Classification

- **Category**: Political movement / Organisation
- **Founded**: First Green Party established in Tasmania, 1972; German Greens (Die Grünen) founded 1980
- **Key figures referenced**: Not named in this section; Die Grünen at Bundestag, Bonn, West Germany
- **Philosophy**: Ecological, socially egalitarian, grassroots democratic, non-growth

## Key Facts

- Mollison notes that by 1983, the German Green Party (Die Grünen) held 23 federal and 48 state seats — significant representation for an ecological party at that time.
- He provides the address of the International Green Party as 113 29th St, Newport Beach, California 92663, and Die Grünen at Bundestag, Bonn, West Germany.
- Mollison advocates two strategies for political change: mobilising ground support for any candidate taking a stand on good ecology, and forming local Ecological or Green Parties or bioregional groups.
- The Green Party's four core aims — ecological, socially egalitarian, grassroots democratic, and non-growth — align directly with permaculture's ethical framework.
- Mollison suggests that intentional groups should affiliate and subscribe to a common policy, arguing that a common policy statement sums up the skills of all groups and is difficult for small isolated groups to develop alone.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- Political advocacy through Green Party channels can support policies beneficial to [[mushroom-cultivation-for-sustainable-agriculture]]: pesticide regulation, [[fukuoka-critique-organic-certification]] standards, and protection of old-growth forests as mycological habitats.
- The non-growth principle aligns with permaculture's emphasis on steady-state, regenerative food systems rather than agricultural expansion.
- Grassroots democratic structures support community-level food sovereignty initiatives, seed libraries, and local composting programmes.
- Political representation for ecological policies can secure funding for soil conservation, water quality protection, and biological research including mycological surveys.

## Context in Permaculture Design

Mollison presents the Green Party as one of several strategies (alongside bioregional groups and tribal congresses) for moving from individual ethical action to collective political influence. He frames it as part of a broader transition from power-centred politics to information-centred, life-centred governance.
