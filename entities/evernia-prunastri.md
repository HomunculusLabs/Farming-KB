## Overview

Evernia prunastri, commonly known as oakmoss, is a fruticose lichen in the family Parmeliaceae, widely distributed across [[fungal-taxonomy-and-kingdom-fungi]] > Phylum Ascomycota > Class Lecanoromycetes > Order Lecanorales > Family Parmeliaceae > Genus Evernia.

**Authority:** (L.) Ach. (1810). Basionym: Lichen prunastri L. (1753).

The thallus is fruticose and bushy, forming irregularly branched clumps 3–10 cm long.

Branching is predominantly dichotomous (forking into two equal branches), with the thallus attached to the substrate at a single basal holdfast.

Lobes are flat to slightly canaliculate (channelled), 1–3 mm wide, with a gray-green upper surface and white to pale green lower surface.

The upper surface is often dusted with a fine white pruina, while the lower surface is paler with no rhizines.

Unlike the closely related genus Pseudevernia, E. prunastri has a white lower surface rather than a black one.

Apothecia are rare, lecanorine, brown, up to 8 mm in diameter, and borne terminally on branch tips.

Spores are hyaline, ellipsoid, 7–10 × 4–6 µm, simple and non-septate.

Pycnidia are common, producing conidia that facilitate [[conidia-and-asexual-reproduction-in-filamentous-fungi]].

## Distribution and Habitat

E. prunastri has a wide distribution across Europe, North Africa, temperate Asia, and [[corylus-avellana]]), pine (Pinus spp.), fir (Abies spp.), and [[cervantes-air-quality-ventilation]] and is less common in densely shaded interiors of closed-canopy forests.

## Chemistry

E. prunastri produces a distinctive profile of lichen [[antifungal-secondary-metabolites-coprophilous-fungi]], dominated by evernic acid (a depside) and atranorin (a depsidone) in the cortex.

Additional compounds include usnic acid, chloroatranorin, and various thamnolic acid derivatives.

Spot test reactions are K+ yellow → red (medulla, evernic acid), KC+ yellow, C−, and Pd+ (medulla).

The cortex is K+ yellow due to atranorin, while the medullary reaction to K (turning yellow then red) is diagnostic for evernic acid.

Usnic acid contributes both antimicrobial activity and UV-protective properties to the thallus.

The species contains atranorin, which has been investigated for anti-inflammatory, analgesic, and cytotoxic activities in laboratory studies.

The complex chemistry of E. prunastri is responsible for its distinctive earthy, mossy aroma that makes it valuable in perfumery.

## Traditional Uses

E. prunastri has been used in European traditional medicine for centuries, particularly as an astringent, antiseptic, and wound-healing agent.

It was employed in folk medicine as a treatment for respiratory ailments, digestive complaints, and topical skin infections.

The lichen was historically steeped in alcohol or boiled in water to prepare decoctions and tinctures for medicinal use.
