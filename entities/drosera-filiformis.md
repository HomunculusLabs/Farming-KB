## Overview

Drosera filiformis, commonly known as the thread-leaved sundew, is a temperate carnivorous plant native to [[growing-gourmet-north-american-reishi-species]] sundews. Its unusual [[geographic-distribution-of-psilocybe-species]] and flower morphology.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

D. filiformis was first described by Constantine Samuel Rafinesque in 1808. The specific epithet "filiformis" refers to the thread-like (filiform) leaves that characterize the species.

Two subspecies are currently recognized. Subspecies *filiformis* occurs in the northeastern [[query-how-to-grow-and-use-oca-in-temperate-climates]] or in unheated greenhouses.

Subspecies *tracyi* is somewhat easier to cultivate than the nominate form, being more tolerant of warm summer conditions. The nominate subspecies can suffer in extended heat above 32°C.

Plants should be allowed to enter winter dormancy naturally. The hibernaculum protects the crown during freezing temperatures, and many populations regularly experience ice formation in their native bogs.

## Conservation

D. filiformis is considered vulnerable or imperiled across much of its range due to habitat loss. Coastal development, drainage of wetlands, and fire suppression have all contributed to population declines.

The nominate subspecies has been extirpated from several historic locations in the northeastern United States. Subspecies *tracyi* faces similar pressures from coastal development along the Gulf Coast.

Prescribed burning is an important [[savory-fire-as-management-tool]] for maintaining suitable habitat. Many natural populations occur in fire-adapted ecosystems where periodic burning prevents woody encroachment and maintains open bog conditions.

## Flowers and Reproduction

D. filiformis produces small, self-pollinating flowers on tall scapes that emerge in mid-summer. The flowers of subspecies *filiformis* are typically pink to magenta, while those of subspecies *tracyi* are white to pale pink.

Each scape bears 5–15 flowers arranged along the upper portion. Individual flowers measure 1–2 cm in diameter and open sequentially over a period of several weeks.

Seed capsules mature in late summer and release numerous tiny seeds. Wind dispersal and water transport both contribute to seed distribution within and between suitable habitats.

Vegetative reproduction occurs through division of the root crown, which naturally produces multiple growing points over time. Large, established clumps may contain dozens of rosettes.

## Hybridization and Related Species

D. filiformis is known to hybridize naturally with several other Drosera species. The hybrid with D. intermedia (D. × hybrida) occurs where the two species co-occur along the Atlantic coast.

The hybrid with D. rotundifolia has been reported from New England bogs. These inter-specific hybrids typically show intermediate leaf morphology and may backcross with either parent species.
