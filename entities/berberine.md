## Berberine

## Overview

Berberine is a quaternary ammonium isoquinoline alkaloid found in roots, rhizomes,
stems, and bark of numerous plant species. Used in Ayurveda and Traditional
Chinese Medicine for millennia, it is one of the most extensively researched
plant-derived [[cannabis-metabolic-syndrome-research]], cardiovascular disease, [[blesching-cannabis-gastrointestinal-disorders]], and
microbial infections.

## Chemical Identity

- **Chemical class:** Benzylisoquinoline alkaloid
- **IUPAC name:** 5,6-Dihydro-9,10-dimethoxybenzo[g]-1,3-benzodioxolo[5,6-a]quinolizinium
- **Molecular formula:** C₂₀H₁₈NO₄⁺
- **Molecular weight:** 336.37 g/mol
- **CAS Registry Number:** 2086-83-1
- **Appearance:** Yellow crystalline solid; strong yellow fluorescence under UV
- **Melting point:** 145°C (decomposes)
- **Solubility:** Sparingly soluble in water; soluble in ethanol, methanol,
  dilute acids; insoluble in non-polar solvents

## Chemical Structure

Berberine consists of an isoquinoline core fused to a methylenedioxybenzene ring,
with two methoxy substituents and a positively charged nitrogen. The quaternary
ammonium structure is responsible for its poor oral bioavailability and its
ability to interact with nucleic acids and enzymes.

### Pharmacokinetics

- **Oral bioavailability:** Very low (<1%) due to P-glycoprotein efflux
  and extensive first-pass metabolism (CYP3A4)
- **Peak plasma:** 1–2 hours post-administration; half-life: 3–5 hours
