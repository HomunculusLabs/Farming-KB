## Description

**Agrostis tenuis** (common bentgrass, synonym: *Agrostis capillaris*) is a perennial grass species in the family Poaceae. It is a widespread and ecologically important grass found in upland and grassland ecosystems across Europe. In mycological research, A. tenuis has been extensively studied for its interaction with arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) fungi and the resulting effects on **radiocesium uptake and translocation**.

## Key Facts

- **Reduced cesium uptake with [[mycorrhizae]]:** Berreck and Hasselwandter (2001) demonstrated that AM development in A. tenuis roots reduced cesium uptake by the plant at moderate soil nutrient levels.
- **Mechanism of protection:** The protective mechanism is attributed to sequestration of Cs in the extraradical hyphae of the AM fungus, with reduced translocation into the host plant.
- **Potassium fertilization effects:** Studies showed that adding potassium to reduce Cs uptake did not provide additional benefit for this fungal-plant interaction.
- **Enhanced shoot translocation:** Dighton and Terry (1996) showed enhanced translocation of radiocesium into shoots of AM-colonized A. tenuis, indicating that while overall uptake may be moderated, the proportion translocated to above-ground tissues can increase.

## Mycorrhizal Associations

A. tenuis forms arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) symbioses, which are critical for its nutrient acquisition in nutrient-poor upland soils. The AM association creates a complex dynamic for radionuclide uptake:

1. Extraradical hyphae can sequester radiocesium, reducing plant uptake
2. However, internal redistribution within mycorrhizal plants may increase the proportion of Cs translocated to shoots
3. The net effect depends on soil nutrient status, particularly potassium availability

## Ecological Role

A. tenuis is a common component of upland grasslands, heathlands, and disturbed sites. Its mycorrhizal associations are essential for nutrient acquisition in the acidic, nutrient-poor soils typical of these habitats. The species plays a role in radionuclide cycling in contaminated ecosystems, as demonstrated by post-Chernobyl research.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Phytoremediation model:** The A. tenuis–AM fungus system serves as a model for understanding how mycorrhizal associations can be leveraged in phytoremediation strategies for radioactively contaminated soils.
- **Mycorrhizal ecology:** Illustrates the dual nature of mycorrhizal effects — both protective (reduced total uptake) and potentially detrimental (enhanced shoot translocation) — for plant contamination.
- **Potassium–cesium competition:** Research on this species contributed to understanding the competitive relationship between potassium and cesium in plant–fungus systems.
