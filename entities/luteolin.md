## Overview

Luteolin is a naturally occurring flavone, a subclass of flavonoids, found
widely in plants including celery, parsley, peppermint, and chamomile flowers.
Its chemical name is 3',4',5,7-tetrahydroxyflavone, and it is one of the most
studied flavonoids for its anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and neuroprotective
properties.

Unlike many flavonoid glycosides, luteolin typically occurs in plants as both
the aglycone and various glycosylated derivatives (luteolin-7-O-glucoside,
luteolin-7-O-rutinoside). It has a long history of use in traditional Chinese
medicine and is the subject of extensive modern pharmacological research.

## Chemical Properties

**Chemical name:** 2-(3,4-Dihydroxyphenyl)-5,7-dihydroxy-4H-chromen-4-one

**Molecular formula:** C₁₅H₁₀O₆

**Molecular weight:** 286.24 g/mol

**CAS number:** 491-70-3

**Structure type:** Flavone (2-phenyl-1,4-benzopyrone)

Luteolin features a flavone backbone with hydroxyl groups at the 5, 7, 3',
and 4' positions. The catechol group on the B-ring (3',4'-dihydroxy) is
critical for its radical scavenging activity. The 2,3-double bond in
conjugation with the 4-carbonyl group contributes to its ability to
stabilize [[reactive-oxygen-species-and-oxidative-stress]].

The compound is yellow crystalline at room temperature, with limited water
solubility (approximately 5.6 mg/L at 25°C). It is soluble in ethanol,
DMSO, and alkaline aqueous solutions. The melting point is 328–330°C
(with decomposition).

## Pharmacology

Luteolin exhibits multiple pharmacological activities through diverse
molecular mechanisms.

**Anti-inflammatory:** Luteolin inhibits NF-κB activation by blocking IκB
kinase (IKK) phosphorylation, reducing pro-inflammatory cytokine production
including TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, and COX-2. It also suppresses MAPK signaling
pathways (ERK, JNK, p38).

**Antioxidant:** The catechol B-ring structure enables direct scavenging of
reactive oxygen and nitrogen species. Luteolin upregulates endogenous
antioxidant defenses via Nrf2/ARE pathway activation, increasing
superoxide dismutase, catalase, and glutathione peroxidase expression.

**Neuroprotective:** In microglial cells, luteolin reduces
lipopolysaccharide-induced neuroinflammation by inhibiting TLR4 signaling
and subsequent pro-inflammatory mediator release. It also modulates
MAO-A and MAO-B enzyme activity, and has demonstrated protective effects
in animal models of Alzheimer's and Parkinson's diseases.

**Anticancer:** Luteolin induces apoptosis in [[blesching-cannabis-apoptosis-and-cancer-cell-death]] lines through
multiple pathways including PI3K/Akt inhibition, cell cycle arrest at G2/M
phase, and suppression of VEGF-mediated angiogenesis. IC₅₀ values vary
widely across cell lines (15–100 µM range).

## Dietary Sources and Bioavailability

Luteolin is present in a variety of common foods, though concentrations
vary considerably.

**Rich sources (>1 mg/g dry weight):**
- Celery (*[[apium-graveolens]]*) leaves — up to 9.7 mg/g
- Peppermint (*Mentha × piperita*) dried leaves — 5.0–8.5 mg/g
- Chamomile (*[[matricaria-chamomilla]]*) flowers — 3.0–5.0 mg/g
