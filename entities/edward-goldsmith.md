# Edward Goldsmith

## Description

Edward "Teddy" Goldsmith (1928–2009) was a British environmentalist, writer, and founding editor of *The Ecologist* magazine. In Mollison's *Permaculture: A Designer's Manual*, Goldsmith is quoted extensively from his 1981 essay "Thermodynamics or Ecodynamics" published in *The Ecologist*, arguing that the biosphere (Gaia) systematically builds up complexity, diversity, and stability, and criticizing scientists who refuse to consider the function of life in natural systems.

## Classification

## Key Facts

- Founded *The Ecologist* magazine in 1970, one of the earliest and most influential environmental periodicals.
- Authored *The Way: An Ecological World-View* (1992), proposing that traditional societies operated within ecological limits and that modern industrial civilization is fundamentally unsustainable.
- Edited the landmark *A Blueprint for Survival* (1972), which argued for the necessity of transitioning to a decentralized, ecologically stable society and was signed by 36 leading scientists and intellectuals.
- His concept of "ecodynamics" proposed that living systems follow thermodynamic principles distinct from those governing inert matter — that life creates order, complexity, and stability in apparent defiance of entropy.
- Argued that quantifiable scientific concepts often "correspond to nothing whatsoever in the world of living things," a critique that resonates deeply with permaculture's preference for holistic observation over reductionist analysis.
- Was a vocal critic of large-scale development projects, industrial agriculture, and economic growth dogma.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Goldsmith's ecodynamic framework underpins several core permaculture cultivation principles:

- **Biospheric complexity**: His argument that natural systems build complexity supports the permaculture strategy of designing diverse polycultures that become more stable and productive over time.
- **Anti-reductionism**: Goldsmith's critique of reductionist science aligns with the permaculture approach of observing whole-system interactions rather than optimizing individual variables in isolation.
- **Thermodynamic accounting**: The energy accounting framework presented in Mollison's Chapter 1 (which compares conventional agriculture vs. permaculture across energy, soil, water, pollution, social, and life quality metrics) directly applies Goldsmith's thermodynamic critique to practical farm design.
- **[[building-soil]] biology through composting, mulching, and mycorrhizal associations.
