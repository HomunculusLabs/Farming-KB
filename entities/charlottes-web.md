## Overview

**Charlotte's Web** is a high-CBD, low-THC [[cannabis-cultivar-effects-soil-microbiome]] developed by the Stanley Brothers in Colorado, notable for its role in treating pediatric epilepsy.

The strain gained international attention in 2013 following a CNN documentary featuring Charlotte Figi, a child with Dravet syndrome whose seizure frequency was dramatically reduced by its use.

It is one of the most commercially significant CBD-dominant strains in history, spawning a major hemp-derived CBD product brand.

Unlike most [[cannabis-microbiome-otu-abundance-vs-presence-cannabis-strains]], Charlotte's Web was specifically bred to maximize CBD content while minimizing psychoactive THC.

Its [[cannabidiol-cbd-therapeutic-profile-blesching]] has influenced medical cannabis [[mushroom-legislation-worldwide]] and contributed to the rescheduling of CBD-derived pharmaceuticals.

## Lineage

Charlotte's Web was bred from a cross between unknown hemp-type genetics and a high-CBD chemotype of Jamaican landrace origin.

The exact parentage is proprietary to the Stanley Brothers, but genetic analysis places it as an indica-dominant hybrid with sativa-influenced morphology.

The strain emerged from a [[cervantes-breeding-program-design-goal-setting]] focused on isolating plants with CBD:THC ratios exceeding 20:1 from a population of Rocky Mountain-grown cannabis.

It is not directly related to other named commercial strains like ACDC or Harlequin, though all share the trait of CBD dominance.

Subsequent breeding has produced several phenotypic variants, including Charlotte's Web Original, Charlotte's Web Maximum Strength, and Charlotte's Web Calm.

## Cannabinoid and Terpene Profile

**Cannabinoid content:** CBD typically ranges from 15–20%, while THC is consistently below 0.3%, qualifying the cultivar as industrial hemp under the 2018 US Farm Bill.

The CBD:THC ratio is approximately 30:1, with trace amounts of CBC (0.5–1.0%) and CBG (0.2–0.5%).

THCa levels are negligible, as the hemp is typically harvested at full maturity when most cannabinoids have decarboxylated.

**Dominant terpenes:** Myrcene (herbal, sedative), pinene (pine, focus), caryophyllene (peppery, anti-inflammatory), and limonene (citrus, mood-elevating).

The terpene profile contributes to an [[cannabis-terpene-entourage-effect-profiles]] that enhances the anti-seizure and anxiolytic properties of CBD.

Total terpene content is moderate (1.0–1.5%), lower than many THC-dominant cultivars, reflecting the hemp-type genetics.

## Effects and Medical Applications

The primary therapeutic application is in treatment-resistant epilepsy, particularly Dravet syndrome and Lennox-Gastaut syndrome.

In the landmark 2017 New England Journal of Medicine study (Devinsky et al.), pharmaceutical CBD derived from similar high-CBD genetics reduced seizure frequency by 39% in Dravet syndrome patients.

Charlotte's Web has also been reported effective [[cannabis-for-anxiety-disorders]], [[cannabis-chronic-pain-management]], inflammation, sleep disturbances, and PTSD.

Users consistently report no psychoactive intoxication at standard doses, making it suitable for children, [[cannabis-elderly-patients]], and THC-sensitive individuals.

Common subjective effects include mild relaxation, improved mood, reduced anxiety, and enhanced sleep quality without cognitive impairment.

[[blesching-cannabis-safety-adverse-effects-evidence]] are generally mild: dry mouth, drowsiness, diarrhea, and changes in appetite or weight at higher doses.

## Cultivation

Charlotte's Web is cultivated outdoors and in greenhouses across Colorado, Kentucky, and other hemp-producing US states.

It grows as a tall, slender plant with indica-leaf morphology, reaching heights of 1.5–2.5 meters outdoors.
