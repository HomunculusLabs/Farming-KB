# Gymnopilus viridans

**Gymnopilus viridans** is a large, orange-capped mushroom in the family Cortinariaceae (or Hymenogastraceae under recent taxonomy) that contains psilocybin and psilocin. It is one of several *Gymnopilus* species known to produce psychoactive compounds, though it is relatively uncommon and less well-documented than its close relatives *G. spectabilis* and *G. junonius*. The species was described by American mycologist **Murrill** from collections in the eastern United States.

## Taxonomy and Etymology

- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Hymenogastraceae (traditionally Cortinariaceae)
- **Genus:** Gymnopilus
- **Species:** G. viridans
- **Authority:** Murrill

The genus name *Gymnopilus* derives from Greek *gymnos* (naked) and *pilus* (cap), referring to the bare or smooth cap surface characteristic of the genus. The specific epithet *viridans* refers to the greenish coloration that develops on the gills and flesh with age or upon bruising.

## Morphology

### Cap (Pileus)
- 30–120 mm diameter
- Convex to broadly convex, sometimes plane in age
- Surface dry, smooth to finely fibrillose
- Color: bright orange to rusty orange, fading to yellowish with age
- Margin incurved at first, becoming straight; sometimes slightly appendiculate with veil remnants

### Gills (Lamellae)
- Adnate to subdecurrent
- Close to crowded
- Color: yellow-orange when young, becoming greenish with age (the distinguishing feature)
- Spore print: rusty brown to ferruginous (orange-brown)

### Stipe (Stem)
- 40–100 mm long, 8–20 mm thick
- Equal or slightly enlarged at the base
- Fibrous, solid to stuffed
- Surface: pale yellow to orange, often with fine fibrils
- Partial veil forms a fragile, evanescent ring zone on the stipe
- Context bruises greenish, especially at the base

### Flesh
- Yellowish to orange
- Turns greenish on exposure, particularly in the gills and lower stipe
- Odor mild to slightly mealy or farinaceous
- Taste bitter to extremely bitter (a characteristic of the genus)

### Spores
- Spores: 7–10 × 5–7 µm
- Shape: broadly ellipsoid, roughened to warty
- Color in deposit: rusty orange-brown
- Features a small germ pore

## Distribution and Habitat

*Gymnopilus viridans* is found in:

- **Eastern North America:** From the Great Lakes region south to the Gulf Coast, extending eastward to the Atlantic
- **Central America:** Reported from Mexico
- **Possible range:** Other temperate regions, though records are scarce

It is a **wood-decomposing species** (saprotrophic) that fruits on:

- Dead hardwood logs and stumps (oak, maple, beech, hickory)
- Occasionally on conifer wood
- Fallen branches and buried wood
- Fruiting in late summer through autumn (August–November)

The species is relatively uncommon compared to other *Gymnopilus* species and may be underreported due to confusion with lookalikes.

## Chemistry

| Compound | Concentration (% dry weight) |
|----------|------------------------------|
| Psilocybin | 0.02 – 0.10 (variable) |
| Psilocin | trace – 0.02 |
| Baeocystin | trace |

*Gymnopilus viridans* is one of the weaker psilocybin-containing species in the genus. Potency varies significantly between collections. The bitter taste, shared by all *Gymnopilus* species, is not related to psilocybin content but rather to other compounds (likely sesquiterpenes) that may be toxic at high doses. This bitterness serves as a deterrent to casual consumption.

## Distinguishing Features

The key identifying feature of *G. viridans* is the **greenish coloration** that develops on the gills, flesh, and sometimes the stipe surface with age or bruising. This separates it from:

- **Gymnopilus spectabilis:** Does not develop greenish tones; gills remain rusty orange; generally more robust
- **Gymnopilus junonius:** Larger species; does not turn green; found on a wider range of substrates
- **Gymnopilus luteofolius:** Has a purple-violet tint to the gills when young, distinct from the green of *G. viridans*
- **Gymnopilus aeruginosus:** Cap has blue-green tones from the start; does not develop green with age in the same way

## Safety and Edibility

*Gymnopilus viridans* is **not recommended for consumption** despite its psychoactive properties:

1. **Bitter compounds:** All *Gymnopilus* species contain bitter principles that may cause gastrointestinal distress
2. **Toxicity risk:** Some *Gymnopilus* species contain compounds that are not fully characterized; the risk of consuming unknown toxic alkaloids alongside psilocybin is real
3. **Confusion risk:** Several *Gymnopilus* species are non-psychoactive and potentially more toxic; accurate identification requires microscopic examination
4. **Gastrointestinal effects:** Nausea and vomiting are commonly reported even from confirmed psychoactive collections

## Similar Species

- **Gymnopilus spectabilis:** "Big Laughing Gym"; the most well-known psychoactive species in the genus; lacks green coloration
- **Gymnopilus validipes:** Small, orange species; reported to be weakly psychoactive
- **Cortinarius species:** Share the rusty spore print; many are dangerously toxic; the lack of a well-developed cortina (web-like veil) in *Gymnopilus* helps distinguish them

## Notes

The green coloration in *G. viridans* is thought to result from the oxidation of psilocybin and related indole compounds, though the exact pigments responsible have not been fully characterized. This feature makes it one of the few mushrooms where the presence of psychoactive alkaloids can be partially inferred from macroscopic appearance. However, chemical confirmation is always recommended before drawing conclusions.
