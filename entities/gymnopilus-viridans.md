# Gymnopilus viridans

**Gymnopilus viridans** is a large, orange-capped mushroom in the family Cortinariaceae (or Hymenogastraceae under recent taxonomy) that contains [[hplc-analysis-of-psilocybin-and-psilocin]]. It is one of several *Gymnopilus* species known to produce psychoactive compounds, though it is relatively uncommon and less well-documented than its close relatives *G. spectabilis* and *G. junonius*. The species was described by American mycologist **Murrill** from collections in the eastern [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]].

## Taxonomy and Etymology

- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Hymenogastraceae (traditionally Cortinariaceae)
- **Genus:** Gymnopilus
- **Species:** G. viridans
- **Authority:** Murrill

The genus name *Gymnopilus* derives from Greek *gymnos* (naked) and *pilus* (cap), referring to the bare or smooth cap surface characteristic of the genus. The specific epithet *viridans* refers to the greenish coloration that develops on the gills and flesh with age or upon bruising.

## Morphology

### Cap (Pileus)
- 30–120 mm diameter
- Convex to broadly convex, sometimes plane in age
- Surface dry, smooth to finely fibrillose
- Color: bright orange to rusty orange, fading to yellowish with age
- Margin incurved at first, becoming straight; sometimes slightly appendiculate with veil remnants

### Gills (Lamellae)
- Adnate to subdecurrent
- Close to crowded
- Color: yellow-orange when young, becoming greenish with age (the distinguishing feature)
- [[comparison-spore-print-collection-vs-spore-syringe-production]]: rusty brown to ferruginous (orange-brown)

### Stipe (Stem)
- 40–100 mm long, 8–20 mm thick
- Equal or slightly enlarged at the base
- Fibrous, solid to stuffed
- Surface: pale yellow to orange, often with fine fibrils
- [[partial-veil-tropism-protection-before-spore-release]] forms a fragile, evanescent ring zone on the stipe
- Context bruises greenish, especially at the base

### Flesh
- Yellowish to orange
- Turns greenish on exposure, particularly in the gills and lower stipe
- Odor mild to slightly mealy or farinaceous
- Taste bitter to extremely bitter (a characteristic of the genus)

### Spores
- Spores: 7–10 × 5–7 µm
- Shape: broadly ellipsoid, roughened to warty
- Color in deposit: rusty orange-brown
- Features a small germ pore

## Distribution and Habitat

*Gymnopilus viridans* is found in:

- **[[polypore-fungi-eastern-north-america]] America:** From the Great Lakes region south to the Gulf Coast, extending eastward to the Atlantic
- **[[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-central-america-caribbean]]:** Reported from Mexico
- **Possible range:** Other [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]], though records are scarce

It is a **wood-decomposing species** (saprotrophic) that fruits on:

- Dead hardwood [[query-how-to-grow-gourmet-mushrooms-outdoors-on-logs-and-stumps]] (oak, maple, beech, hickory)
- Occasionally on conifer wood
- Fallen branches and buried wood
- Fruiting in late summer through autumn (August–November)

The species is relatively uncommon compared to other *Gymnopilus* species and may be underreported due to confusion with lookalikes.

## Chemistry

| Compound | Concentration (% dry weight) |
|----------|------------------------------|
| Psilocybin | 0.02 – 0.10 (variable) |
| Psilocin | trace – 0.02 |
| Baeocystin | trace |

*Gymnopilus viridans* is one of the weaker psilocybin-containing species in the genus. Potency varies significantly between collections. The bitter taste, shared by all *Gymnopilus* species, is not related to psilocybin content but rather to other compounds (likely sesquiterpenes) that may be toxic at high doses. This bitterness serves as a deterrent to casual consumption.
