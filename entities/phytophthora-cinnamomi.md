# Phytophthora cinnamomi

## Description

**Phytophthora cinnamomi** is an oomycete (water mold) pathogen belonging to the genus *Phytophthora*. It is one of the most destructive plant pathogens worldwide, causing root rot and dieback in hundreds of plant species. Despite its species name suggesting an association with cinnamon (*Cinnamomum*), its host range is extraordinarily broad, affecting both woody and herbaceous plants across many families. It is classified as an oomycete rather than a true fungus, though it shares many ecological and morphological similarities with fungi and is traditionally studied within mycology.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Eukaryota
- **Kingdom:** Chromista (Straminipila)
- **Phylum:** Oomycota
- **Class:** Oomycetes
- **Order:** Peronosporales
- **Family:** Peronosporaceae
- **Genus:** *Phytophthora*
- **Species:** *P. cinnamomi*

## Key Facts

- Listed as one of the world's worst invasive species by the IUCN Invasive Species Specialist Group
- Causes devastating jarrah dieback in Western Australia, threatening entire ecosystems
- Host range exceeds 3,000 plant species, including economically important crops
- Spreads primarily through infected soil, water movement, and human activity (vehicles, footwear)
- Produces both chlamydospores (thick-walled resting spores) and sporangia containing motile zoospores
- Zoospores are attracted to plant [[root-exudates]], enabling active host-seeking behavior
- Thrives in warm, moist conditions; poorly drained soils favor disease development
- Can survive in soil for years as dormant chlamydospores even without a host
- Impacts both agricultural systems and native ecosystems globally

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

*Phytophthora cinnamomi* is of critical concern in cultivation and mycology for several reasons:

1. **Crop Disease Management:** It causes root rot and crown rot in avocado, pineapple, chestnut, and many ornamental plants. Management requires integrated approaches including resistant rootstocks, phosphonate fungicides, and cultural practices like improved drainage.

2. **Ecosystem Impact:** Its role in forest dieback (particularly in Australian eucalypt forests) demonstrates how a single pathogen can reshape entire ecosystems, affecting fungal community structure and mycorrhizal associations.

3. **Biocontrol Interactions:** [[ectomycorrhizal-fungi]] can provide some protection against *P. cinnamomi* infection. *[[laccaria-laccata]]* and other mycorrhizal species have been investigated as biological control agents.

4. **Diagnostic Importance:** Distinguishing *Phytophthora* root rot from fungal diseases is essential for effective treatment, as oomycete-specific fungicides (phosphonates, metalaxyl) differ from those targeting true fungi.

5. **Research Model:** Studied extensively as a model for understanding plant-pathogen interactions, zoospore biology, and the ecological distinctions between oomycetes and true fungi.

## Ecological Role in Fungal Communities

As referenced in Dighton's *Fungi in Ecosystem Processes*, *P. cinnamomi* interacts with mycorrhizal fungal communities. Disease outbreaks can reduce mycorrhizal colonization in affected plants, while healthy [[mycorrhizal-networks]] may confer some disease resistance through improved plant nutrition and physical barriers to pathogen entry.
