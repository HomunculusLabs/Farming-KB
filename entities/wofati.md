## Design and Construction

A wofati is built into an earth berm on a hillside, with the building
partially set into the ground. The design follows a set of rules codified
by Wheaton, known informally as the "Wofati Code."

The core structural elements are round wood poles (typically un-milled
timber harvested on-site) arranged in an A-frame or modified post-and-
beam configuration. No concrete foundation is required; posts are set
directly on or into the earth.

The roof is the defining feature: a thick layer of living earth (minimum
18 inches / 46 cm) supported by a waterproof membrane and wood framing.
The earth roof provides insulation, thermal mass, and integrates the
structure into the landscape.

The south-facing wall is largely open, usually as large windows or a
glass wall, oriented to maximize winter solar gain. This glazing is the
primary heat source during cold months.

Earth is bermed against the north, east, and west walls to below the
roofline, insulating those walls with soil. The south face remains open
to sunlight and views.

## Materials and Techniques

Wofatis emphasize locally sourced, natural, and often salvaged materials.
The primary construction materials include round wood poles, earth, straw
bales (for insulation), and a waterproof membrane for the roof.

The roof waterproofing typically uses heavy-duty [[comparison-lime-plaster-vs-earthen-plaster]] (cob plaster) over
the insulating layer. Floors are often poured earth, flagstone, or
salvaged materials.

No mechanical heating or cooling system is required in the design.
Thermal performance depends on proper orientation, adequate earth berm
depth, and quality of the roof insulation layer.
