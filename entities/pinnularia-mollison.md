# Pinnularia

## Description

Pinnularia is a genus of pennate (bilaterally symmetrical) freshwater diatoms recognized by their elongated, naviculoid (boat-shaped) frustules with prominent longitudinal ribs (raphe). Mollison lists Pinnularia as one of the typical diatom organisms present in open sewage treatment ponds, contributing to biological wastewater processing.

## Classification

- **Domain**: Eukaryota
- **Kingdom**: Chromista
- **Phylum**: Ochrophyta
- **Class**: Bacillariophyceae (diatoms)
- **Order**: Naviculales
- **Family**: Pinnulariaceae
- **Genus**: *Pinnularia*

## Key Facts

- Pinnularia species are among the largest freshwater diatoms, with some species reaching 200-300 um in length.
- They are primarily freshwater organisms, preferring slightly acidic to neutral waters with low mineral content.
- Their distinctive raphe system allows them to glide along substrates, a form of motility unique to pennate diatoms.
- In sewage lagoon systems, they contribute to the phytoplankton community that oxygenates water and consumes dissolved nutrients.
- Pinnularia frustules are heavily silicified, making them well-preserved in fossil records and sediment cores.
- They are often found in benthic (bottom-dwelling) habitats but also occur in the plankton of ponds and lakes.

## Role in Wastewater Treatment

Mollison includes Pinnularia among the typical organisms in faculative and aerobic sewage treatment ponds. Its contributions include:

1. **Oxygen production**: Photosynthetic activity releases oxygen into the water column, supporting aerobic bacteria.
2. **Nutrient uptake**: Absorbs dissolved nitrogen and phosphorus from wastewater.
3. **Food web support**: Serves as food for zooplankton grazers such as Daphnia and copepods.
4. **Bioindicator**: Its presence indicates adequate oxygen levels and moderate nutrient loading.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Constructed wetlands and reed beds**: Diatoms like Pinnularia contribute to biofilm communities on substrate surfaces, enhancing filtration and nutrient removal.
- **Water quality assessment**: Pinnularia species are used in bioassessment protocols to evaluate freshwater ecosystem health.
- **Integrated systems**: In permaculture designs incorporating aquaculture, diatom populations support the food chains that sustain fish and other aquatic organisms.
- **Soil-water interfaces**: Diatoms at the soil-water interface in flooded systems contribute to nutrient cycling relevant to mycorrhizal and microbial soil communities.
