## 5-MeO-DET — TIHKAL #36

## Compound Identity

- Full name: N,N-Diethyl-5-methoxytryptamine

- Synonyms: 3-[2-(diethylamino)ethyl]-5-methoxyindole

- Class: Tryptamine, 5-methoxy substituted, N,N-diethyl

- Source: [[5-meo-dipt]]): Positive, LSD-like, 8-12 mg

- DD (5-MeO-DPT): Neutral balance, 6-10 mg

The challenge: can CC (5-MeO-DET) be modified to eliminate the vertigo while
maintaining the erotic and potentially psychedelic properties?

## Synthesis

Two routes are described: from 5-methoxytryptamine via alkylation with bromoethane
and diisopropylethylamine (35% yield), and from 5-methoxyindole via the oxalyl
chloride/LAH reduction sequence (74% yield). The hydrochloride salt has mp
190-191°C. A byproduct, N-ethylmelatonin, proved extremely difficult to hydrolyze. (see [[tryptamine-family-overview]]).

The compound's designation as "CC" in Shulgin's systematic code reflects its
position in the matrix of 5-methoxylated tryptamines organized by nitrogen
substitution. The "CC" indicates two ethyl groups on the nitrogen atom, placing
it between the dimethyl (BB, 5-MeO-DMT) and diisopropyl (EE, 5-MeO-DIPT)
variants.

The vertigo/erotic combination is unique to 5-MeO-DET among the Shulgin
tryptamines and suggests a distinctive receptor interaction profile that
simultaneously affects vestibular processing and sexual arousal pathways.

## Detailed Pharmacological Discussion

5-MeO-DET's extreme oral potency (1-3 mg) rivals or exceeds that of many
classic psychedelics including LSD (60-200 mcg) and DOM (3-10 mg). The
compound's potency reflects optimal engagement with the 5-HT2A receptor,
the primary target of psychedelic tryptamines.

The prominent vertigo is a pharmacologically distinctive feature that may
