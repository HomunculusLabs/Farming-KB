# 5-MeO-DET — TIHKAL #36

## Compound Identity

- Full name: N,N-Diethyl-5-methoxytryptamine

- Synonyms: 3-[2-(diethylamino)ethyl]-5-methoxyindole

- Class: Tryptamine, 5-methoxy substituted, N,N-diethyl

- Source: [[tihkal-introduction-and-methodology]]

## Dosage and Duration

- Dosage: 1-3 mg orally

- Duration: 3-4 hours

- Active routes: oral

## Qualitative Effects

At 2 mg: Tinnitus becomes prominent and inescapable. A lightheaded "funny"
quality — not hypotension, not dizziness — possibly related to the inner
ear. Intoxication is clear. Sexual enhancement is notable at this dose, described
as "I may be spacey in the head, but my body knows where it is at."

At 3 mg: Rapid onset within 20 minutes. The dominant experience is vertigo/lightheadedness
that blocks other effects and creates discomfort. However, behind the vertigo,
another dimension becomes accessible. The experience is described as "erotic
beyond belief, gorgeous, warm and glowing." The vertigo component is a consistent
and distinctive feature that may limit the compound's appeal.

## Distinctive Properties

The most striking feature of 5-MeO-DET is its extreme oral potency (1-3 mg)
combined with a distinctive vertigo/lightheadedness. This vertigo is not
merely dizziness — it appears to be a specific pharmacological property related
to the combination of diethyl substitution with the 5-methoxy group. The
erotic component is equally prominent and consistent.

Shulgin organized the 5-methoxylated tryptamines by increasing mass to identify
patterns:

- BB ([[5-meo-dmt]]): Positive psychedelic, 6-20 mg

- BE ([[5-meo-mipt]]): Mixed, complex, 4-6 mg

- CC (5-MeO-DET): Negative (vertigo), erotic, 2-3 mg

- EE (5-MeO-DIPT): Positive, LSD-like, 8-12 mg

- DD (5-MeO-DPT): Neutral balance, 6-10 mg

The challenge: can CC (5-MeO-DET) be modified to eliminate the vertigo while
maintaining the erotic and potentially psychedelic properties?

## Synthesis

Two routes are described: from 5-methoxytryptamine via alkylation with bromoethane
and diisopropylethylamine (35% yield), and from 5-methoxyindole via the oxalyl
chloride/LAH reduction sequence (74% yield). The hydrochloride salt has mp
190-191°C. A byproduct, N-ethylmelatonin, proved extremely difficult to hydrolyze.

## Chemical Family Context

5-MeO-DET is a member of the 5-methoxytryptamine family, which represents
one of the most potent classes of oral tryptamine psychedelics. The 5-methoxy
group dramatically enhances potency compared to unsubstituted tryptamines,
with most members active in the 1-20 mg range orally.

The compound's designation as "CC" in Shulgin's systematic code reflects its
position in the matrix of 5-methoxylated tryptamines organized by nitrogen
substitution. The "CC" indicates two ethyl groups on the nitrogen atom, placing
it between the dimethyl (BB, 5-MeO-DMT) and diisopropyl (EE, 5-MeO-DIPT)
variants.

The vertigo/erotic combination is unique to 5-MeO-DET among the Shulgin
tryptamines and suggests a distinctive receptor interaction profile that
simultaneously affects vestibular processing and sexual arousal pathways.

## Detailed Pharmacological Discussion

5-MeO-DET's extreme oral potency (1-3 mg) rivals or exceeds that of many
classic psychedelics including LSD (60-200 mcg) and DOM (3-10 mg). The
compound's potency reflects optimal engagement with the 5-HT2A receptor,
the primary target of psychedelic tryptamines.

The prominent vertigo is a pharmacologically distinctive feature that may
reflect activity at 5-HT1A receptors in the vestibular nucleus, or at
serotonin receptors on inner hair cells. The tinnitus reported at 2 mg
further supports an inner ear/vestibular mechanism. This side effect profile
is unique among tryptamines and significantly limits the compound's
therapeutic or recreational utility.

The erotic enhancement is another distinctive feature. The disconnect
between the "spacey head" and the aware body -- "I may be spacey in the
head, but my body knows where it is at" -- suggests a dissociation between
cognitive and somatic processing that may reflect differential effects
on cortical versus spinal serotonin pathways.

## Comparison to Related Compounds

- 5-MeO-DMT: 6-20 mg (smoked), intense psychedelic, no vertigo

- 5-MeO-MIPT: 4-6 mg, mixed effects, complex character

- 5-MeO-DET: 1-3 mg, vertigo + erotic, 3-4 h

- 5-MeO-DIPT: 8-12 mg, LSD-like, no prominent vertigo

- 5-MeO-DPT: 6-10 mg, neutral balance

The progression from BB through EE shows that N-substitution dramatically
affects both quantitative potency and qualitative character in the
5-methoxytryptamine series, with no simple trend relating substituent
size to specific effect profiles.

## Harm Reduction and Safety Considerations

- The extreme potency (1-3 mg active dose) makes accurate dosing critical;
a milligram scale is essential

- The vertigo component could be hazardous in environments requiring balance

- The erotic component, while potentially positive, could lead to risky
behavior in inappropriate contexts

- Standard tryptamine precautions apply: avoid MAO inhibitors, SSRIs,
lithium, and other serotonergic medications

- The compound's effects are not fully characterized; individual sensitivity
may vary significantly

## Legal Status

5-MeO-DET is not specifically scheduled in most jurisdictions. As a
5-methoxy-substituted tryptamine, it could potentially fall under analogue
legislation related to 5-MeO-DMT, which is controlled in several countries.
