## Distribution and Habitat

*Dactylorhiza fuchsii* occurs throughout most of Europe, from Ireland and Portugal eastward to the Urals and western Siberia. It is absent from the Mediterranean lowlands and the far north.

The species occupies a wide range of habitats including neutral and calcareous grasslands, marshy meadows, dune slacks, woodland clearings, roadside verges, and railway embankments. It shows a preference for moist, base-rich soils.

Elevational range extends from sea level to approximately 2,000 m in montane regions of central Europe. Populations are particularly abundant in areas with traditional low-intensity grazing or hay meadow management.

It is one of the most frequently encountered wild orchids in the United Kingdom, found in every county. In continental Europe, population densities are highest in the Alps and Carpathians.

The species has expanded its range in some northern areas during the 20th century, colonizing roadside verges and railway embankments created by modern infrastructure. These linear habitats serve as important dispersal corridors.

## Ecology

Pollination is primarily by generalist insects including bees, flies, and beetles. The flowers produce limited nectar, and the species may rely partly on deceptive pollination strategies.

Individual plants may persist for 10–20 years under favorable conditions, though above-ground shoots may not appear every year. Dormancy periods of one to several seasons have been documented in marked population studies.

The species is listed on Appendix II of CITES and is protected under the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981 in the United Kingdom.
