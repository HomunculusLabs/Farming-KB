# David Nutt

David Nutt (born 1951) is a British neuropsychopharmacologist and
professor at Imperial College London. He is best known for his
evidence-based drug harm rankings, his dismissal as the UK
government's chief drug adviser, and his advocacy for psychedelic
research.

## Early Career

Nutt studied medicine at the University of Cambridge and completed
his psychiatric training at Oxford. His doctoral research focused
on benzodiazepine receptors and the neurobiology of anxiety
disorders.

He held academic positions at the University of Bristol and Oxford
before joining Imperial College London. He published extensively on
serotonin receptors, GABA function, and the neurochemistry of mood
disorders.

## Drug Harm Rankings and ACMD

In 2008, Nutt chaired the UK Advisory Council on the Misuse of
Drugs (ACMD). He published a paper in *The Lancet* comparing the
harms of 20 common drugs using multi-criteria decision analysis.

The study ranked alcohol and tobacco as more harmful than several
Schedule I substances including [[lsd]] and [[psilocybin]]. His
public statements that cannabis and ecstasy were less dangerous
than alcohol drew political controversy.

In 2009, Home Secretary Alan Johnson dismissed Nutt from the ACMD.
The dismissal triggered widespread protest from the scientific
community and resignations of several ACMD members in solidarity.

His 2010 paper expanded the assessment to 16 harm criteria
covering both harm to the user and harm to others. Alcohol scored
highest overall, while psilocybin mushrooms and [[lsd]] ranked
near the bottom.

## Psychedelic Research Advocacy

Nutt co-founded the Beckley/Imperial Research Programme with
Amanda Feilding. This collaboration produced pioneering
neuroimaging studies of [[psilocybin]] and [[lsd]] effects on the
brain using fMRI and magnetoencephalography.

The research demonstrated reduced blood flow in the default mode
network during psychedelic states. His team's 2016 psilocybin
trial for treatment-resistant depression showed promising results.

Nutt argues that psychedelics represent a paradigm shift in
psychiatric treatment. He continues to advocate for using
psychedelics to treat depression, addiction, and anxiety.

His public advocacy has made him one of the most recognizable
scientists in the global drug policy debate. He appears regularly
in media explaining the neuroscience of psychedelics to general
audiences.

## Drug Science Foundation

Following his ACMD dismissal, Nutt founded Drug Science in 2010.
Drug Science operates as an independent scientific body providing
evidence-based information on drugs free from political influence.

The organization publishes research reviews, hosts conferences,
and advises policymakers. Its reports on cannabis-based medicines,
psychedelic therapy, and harm reduction have influenced policy
discussions across Europe and beyond.

## Selected Publications

- *Drugs Without the Hot Air* (2012) — evidence-based guide to
  drug harms and policy
- *The Lancet* drug harm study (2007, 2010) — multi-criteria harm
  rankings
- *Drink?* (2020) — science of alcohol and its effects

## Legacy

Nutt's work fundamentally shifted the public conversation about
drug harms from moral panic to evidence-based assessment. His
dismissal became a cause célèbre for scientific independence from
political interference.

He demonstrated that rigorous science could challenge
prohibition-era drug policy with empirical data. His popular
writing makes complex pharmacological science accessible to
general audiences and policymakers alike.

He continues to publish and advocate at Imperial College London.
His current research focuses on the therapeutic potential of
psychedelics for depression and addiction. He remains one of the
most prominent scientific voices calling for drug policy reform
grounded in evidence rather than ideology.
