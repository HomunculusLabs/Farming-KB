# Leucaena

## Description


Leucaena is a genus of fast-growing tropical leguminous trees and shrubs in the family Fabaceae, subfamily Mimosoideae. The most widely known species is *Leucaena leucocephala*, commonly called lead tree or white popinac. Native to Central America and Mexico, Leucaena species have been introduced throughout the tropics and subtropics for fodder, fuelwood, shade, and soil improvement.

## Classification


- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Family**: Fabaceae (Leguminosae)
- **Subfamily**: Mimosoideae
- **Genus**: Leucaena
- **Notable species**: *L. leucocephala*, *L. diversifolia*, *L. trichandra*

## Key Facts


- Fast-growing nitrogen-fixing tree capable of reaching 5–10 m in height within a few years.
- Forms symbiotic associations with Rhizobium bacteria, fixing atmospheric nitrogen and enriching soils naturally.
- Highly productive leaf biomass makes it one of the most important tropical green manure and mulch sources.
- Foliage contains mimosine, a toxic amino acid that limits its use as sole fodder for non-ruminants; ruminants can tolerate it with gradual adaptation.
- Deep root system accesses nutrients from subsoil layers, acting as a nutrient pump for surface soils through leaf fall.
- Coppices vigorously after cutting, allowing repeated harvest for mulch, fodder, or fuelwood.
- Tolerates a wide range of soil conditions including acidic and moderately alkaline soils.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture


Mollison identifies Leucaena as a critical species for restoring tropical soil fertility. In the humid tropics chapter, he specifically recommends Leucaena and tree legumes generally for restoring humus to degraded tropical soils. Key permaculture applications include:

- **Soil restoration**: Planted as a green manure crop to rebuild humus layers in deeply leached tropical soils where cation exchange capacity has been depleted by clearing.
- **Intercropping**: Used as a perennial nitrogen-fixing companion in multi-storied tropical polycultures, providing shade, mulch, and nitrogen simultaneously.
- **Swale plantings**: Listed among productive species for tropical swale systems, contributing biomass and nitrogen to adjacent crop rows.
- **Erosion control**: Fast establishment on degraded sites stabilizes soils and begins the process of ecological succession.
- **Animal forage**: Provides high-protein fodder for cattle, goats, and other ruminants in integrated crop-livestock systems.

Mollison emphasizes that in tropical soils where most nutrients are held in biomass rather than the soil itself (85–90% in vegetation), Leucaena's rapid biomass production and nitrogen fixation make it indispensable for sustainable land management.

## Ecological Considerations


- Can become invasive in some tropical regions due to prolific seed production and rapid growth.
- Seed longevity in soil can exceed 10 years, requiring management in sensitive ecosystems.
- Some Leucaena species are frost-sensitive, limiting use in subtropical areas with cold winters.

## Cultivation Notes


- Propagates readily from seed; scarification improves germination rates.
- Tolerates regular coppicing at 1–2 m height for continuous mulch production.
- Inoculation with appropriate Rhizobium strains ensures effective nitrogen fixation.
- Spacing of 1–3 m between trees in hedgerow or alley cropping configurations is typical.
