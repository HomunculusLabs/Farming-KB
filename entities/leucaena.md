## Description

*Leucaena* is a genus of fast-growing leguminous trees and shrubs in the family Fabaceae, widely valued in permaculture and agroforestry for their multiple functions. The most commonly used species is *Leucaena leucocephala*, known as lead tree or ipil-ipil. In Mollison's design framework, Leucaena is cited as a prime example of a multi-functional windbreak species that provides edible leaves for fodder while simultaneously serving as a wind barrier.








## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Family:** Fabaceae (Leguminosae)
- **Subfamily:** Mimosoideae
- **Genus:** Leucaena
- **Key species:** L. leucocephala, L. diversifolia, L. esculenta
- **Growth form:** Small to medium fast-growing tree or large shrub
- **Native range:** Americas (Mexico to Central America)








## Key Facts

- Nitrogen-fixing legume that improves soil fertility through symbiotic association with Rhizobium bacteria in root nodules.
- Produces edible leaves high in protein, widely used as fodder for cattle, goats, and other livestock.
- Capable of extremely rapid growth under favorable conditions, making it one of the fastest-growing tropical legumes.
- Mollison specifically recommends Leucaena for windbreak plantings that serve multiple functions simultaneously.
- Leaves contain mimosine, a toxic amino acid that limits use as sole fodder source; however, rumen bacteria in cattle can degrade mimosine.
- Produces abundant seed and can naturalize aggressively in tropical and subtropical regions, leading to invasive potential in some contexts.








## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

- **Multi-functional windbreaks:** Mollison highlights Leucaena alongside tagasaste as a windbreak species providing edible leaves, exemplifying the permaculture principle that every element should serve multiple functions.
- **Nitrogen fixation:** As a legume, Leucaena contributes fixed nitrogen to the soil, benefiting neighboring crops and supporting polyculture designs.
- **Living fence and fodder bank:** Can be managed as a hedgerow for periodic coppicing, providing a sustainable source of high-protein fodder.
- **Alley cropping:** Widely used in tropical alley cropping systems where rows of Leucaena are interspersed with crop rows, providing shade, mulch, and nitrogen.
- **Erosion control:** Fast establishment and deep root systems make it effective for stabilizing slopes and preventing soil erosion.
- **Caution required:** The invasive potential of *L. leucocephala* is well-documented; permaculture designers should consider local conditions and potentially use less aggressive species within the genus.








## Ecological Considerations

Leucaena species have been introduced throughout the tropics for fodder, fuelwood, and reforestation. While their benefits are substantial, their capacity for rapid spread and monoculture formation means they must be managed carefully within designed systems. In mixed polycultures with appropriate grazing or coppicing regimes, they contribute positively without becoming dominant.
