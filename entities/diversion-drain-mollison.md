## Description

Diversion drains are gently sloping excavated channels used to redirect water away from valleys and streams into storages, irrigation systems, sand beds, or swales for absorption. They are the essential connective infrastructure of the Keyline water management system, linking dam series so that overflow from one dam enters the feeder channel of the next. Unlike swales (which hold water without flow), diversion drains are built to flow after rain, serving as the arterial water transport network in designed landscapes.

## Classification

- **Category**: Earthworks / Water conveyance
- **Domain**: Permaculture, Keyline design, farm water management
- **Structure type**: Gently sloping open channel
- **Primary function**: Water transport and redistribution
- **Climate applicability**: All climates

## Key Facts

### Design Specifications

- Base slopes as little as 1:6000 in fine sands (extremely gentle gradient)
- Cut at 1:250–500 rising grade for hill runoff capture
- Carefully surveyed and planned routes
- Dam crests and irrigation drains need equally careful placement

### Functions

1. **Lead water to storages**: Direct overland flow and stream water into dams and tanks
2. **Connect dam series**: Link Keyline dams so overflow feeds downstream storages
3. **Feed irrigation systems**: Deliver water from storages to crop land
4. **Direct water to absorption zones**: Lead water into sand beds or swales for infiltration
5. **Intercept overland flow**: Gather water from as little as 1–1.5 cm of rain over 24 hours

### Distinction from Swales

| Feature | Diversion Drain | Swale |
|---------|----------------|-------|
| Flow | Designed to flow | Designed to hold (no flow) |
| Purpose | Transport water | Store and infiltrate water |
| Survey | Gentle slope | Dead level |
| Compaction | May be compacted | Never compacted |
| Connection | Links storages | Standalone or fed by drains |

### Enhanced Functionality

When low earth walls are raised across the flow channels of larger diversion drains, they create a series of mini-swales for specific tree sites while surplus water flows on to storages. This hybrid approach combines conveyance with targeted infiltration.

### Water Capture Performance

Even without a stream intake, diversion drains gather water from overland flow in minimal rainfall events: as little as 1–1.5 cm of rain in 24 hours produces usable flow. This makes them effective even in quite dry country. Isolated dams should always be fitted with diversion drains.

### Construction Variations

- Standard earth channels in soil
- Simple concrete or stone walls across solid rock faces (e.g., Canary Islands)
- Broad level swales in drylands (as destination)
- Combined with mini-swatched earth walls for tree site creation

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Diversion drains are the distribution arteries that deliver water from storage to point-of-use in permaculture systems. For mushroom cultivation, they can direct water from keypoint dams or other storages to log soaking areas for shiitake and other wood-loving species, substrate preparation stations, mist irrigation zones for outdoor mushroom beds, and moisture maintenance in mushroom cultivation tunnels or shade houses.

The ability to capture water from minimal rainfall events makes diversion drains essential for dryland mycology where every drop of water must be efficiently captured and directed to productive use.
