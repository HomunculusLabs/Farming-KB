# Dano

## Description

Dano (Spanish: "damage" or "harm", written as dano) is a category of magical illness recognized throughout the Peruvian Amazon, particularly in urban slum communities such as Belen in Iquitos. It refers to illness caused by the malicious intent of another person — typically through envy (envidia) or vengeance (despecho). Dano is one of the principal categories of illness treated by ayahuasqueros and represents a core concept in Amazonian magical healing.

## Classification

- **Category:** Culture-bound syndrome / magical illness
- **Causation:** Interpersonal malice — envy, hatred, or desire for revenge channeled through witchcraft
- **Geographic distribution:** Peruvian Amazon, particularly Iquitos and Belen
- **Healing tradition:** Treated by ayahuasqueros using ayahuasca diagnosis and magical countermoves

## Key Facts

- Dano is caused when someone envies or harbors vengeance toward the victim and employs a witch (brujo) to inflict magical harm
- A witch may prepare a powerful potion slipped into the victim's drink or thrown across their doorstep at night
- Witches and some ayahuasqueros are believed to control spirit familiars that can be dispatched to inflict harm
- A chonta (thorn) carrying disease-producing substances can be magically shot through the air at enemies
- Dano can manifest as a wide range of symptoms: hemorrhaging, muscular pain, loss of consciousness, suffocation, tumors, and persistent bad luck (saladera)
- The critical diagnostic feature is the cause (magical attack) rather than the specific manifestation of symptoms
- It is imperative that a person suffering from dano seek a healer to neutralize the harm before it becomes fatal
- Some ayahuasqueros report being approached by clients seeking to cause dano to others, not merely to be healed
- The condition illustrates the deep entanglement of social conflict, projection, and psychosomatic illness in Amazonian communities

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Dano is central to understanding the therapeutic use of ayahuasca because:

1. **Diagnostic use of ayahuasca:** Ayahuasqueros use the vine to identify the cause of dano — revealing in visions the person responsible for bewitching the patient
2. **Neutralization:** The healer uses ayahuasca visions to determine the specific magical attack and then performs counter-rituals to neutralize it
3. **Social function:** The concept of dano channels social conflict and anxiety into a culturally manageable framework, with the ayahuasquero serving as mediator
4. **Psychosomatic healing:** Many dano symptoms have psychosomatic dimensions that can respond to culturally appropriate therapeutic intervention
5. **Ethnopharmacological context:** The use of plant hallucinogens for both causing and curing dano reveals the dual nature (healing/harming) of Amazonian plant knowledge
