## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Phoenix roebelenii** O'Brien belongs to the family Arecaceae, subfamily Coryphoideae, tribe Phoeniceae.

The genus Phoenix comprises approximately 14 species distributed across Africa, southern Asia, and the Mediterranean.

The trunk is slender, covered with persistent leaf bases, reaching 2–4 meters in height and 8–12 centimeters in diameter.

Multiple stems often arise from the base, forming attractive clustering habit in cultivated specimens.

The crown is dense and graceful, consisting of 15–25 pinnate fronds arching outward from the apex.

Fronds are 1–1.5 meters long, with approximately 100–120 narrow, glossy green leaflets per side.

The leaflets are arranged in a single plane with a slight twist, creating a feathery appearance.

Leaflets near the base of the frond are modified into sharp, persistent spines along the petiole.

Inflorescences are interfoliar, branched, and reach 30–50 centimeters in length.

Flowers are small, cream-colored, and arranged in clusters along the rachillae.

The species is dioecious, with separate male and female individuals required for [[fungal-biodiversity-tropical-forests]].

The species grows at elevations from 100 to 1,200 meters above sea level.

It thrives in warm, humid conditions with partial shade, particularly as an understory palm in forest settings.

Native populations are increasingly rare due to habitat destruction and over-collection for the ornamental trade.

The palm has been widely introduced and naturalized in tropical and subtropical regions worldwide.

## Cultivation

Phoenix roebelenii is propagated primarily from seed, which germinates within 2–4 months under warm conditions.

Seedlings grow moderately fast, reaching a sellable size in 2–3 years in nursery production.

The species prefers well-drained soils rich in [[fukuoka-natural-farming-temperate-climates]].

It is highly adaptable to container cultivation and is a popular choice for indoor and patio plantings.

Fertilization with a balanced palm fertilizer every 2–3 months promotes vigorous growth.

Pruning is limited to removing dead or damaged fronds; excessive pruning weakens the palm.
