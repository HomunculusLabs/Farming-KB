## Description

Keypoint dams are water storage structures located in the valleys of secondary streams in humid landscapes, positioned at the critical transition point where a stream profile changes from convex to concave. This geomorphic feature is called the "keypoint" in P.A. Yeomans' Keyline design system. Keypoint dams are the highest practical construction point in the hill profile for irrigation storage, and their placement is fundamental to the Keyline approach of whole-farm water management.

## Classification

- **Category**: Earthworks / Dam type
- **Domain**: Permaculture, Keyline design, farm water management
- **Landscape position**: Valley of secondary streams at convex-to-concave transition
- **Primary use**: Irrigation water storage
- **Climate applicability**: Humid landscapes primarily

## Key Facts

### Site Identification

- Located where the stream profile changes from convex to concave slope
- This transition point can be judged by eye in the field
- A descending contour from the identified keypoint picks up all other keypoints on the main valley
- Keypoints represent the boundary between steeper upper catchment and flatter lower slopes

### Design and Construction

- Built at the highest practical construction point in the valley
- Can be constructed in series: second and third series of dams can be built below the primary series
- The spillway of the last dam in a series can be returned "upstream" to meet the main valley
- This effectively spills surplus water back to streams, maintaining downstream flow

### Placement in the Landscape

Keypoint dams sit between saddle dams / ridgepoint dams (higher) and contour dams (lower):

| Dam Type | Landscape Position | Primary Use |
|----------|-------------------|-------------|
| Saddle dam | Highest, on skyline saddles | Wildlife, stock, high storage |
| Ridgepoint dam | Sub-plateaus of flattened ridges | Runoff storage, limited irrigation |
| **Keypoint dam** | **Valley at convex-concave transition** | **Irrigation water storage** |
| Contour dam | Slopes 8% or less | Irrigation, aquaculture, flood basins |
| Barrier dam | Across flowing streams | Energy, irrigation above valley floor |

### Role in Keyline System

The keyline (heavy contour line) links keypoints in primary valleys. Keypoint dams form the backbone of the Keyline irrigation system by capturing runoff from upper catchments at the optimal elevation, storing water high enough for gravity-fed irrigation of lower slopes, enabling sequential use of water through dam series, and integrating with diversion drains that connect dams in the Keyline layout.

### Historical Context

P.A. Yeomans developed the Keyline system including keypoint dam placement on his property "Yobarnie" in Australia. After 17 years of Keyline irrigation development on approximately 307 ha (758 acres), the property demonstrated drought-proofing with very low maintenance and operating costs. Yeomans stated that if 10–15% of a normal humid lowland or foothill landscape were fitted with small earth storages, floods, drought, and fire threat could be eliminated.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Keypoint dams provide reliable, gravity-fed irrigation water storage at the optimal landscape position for productive land use. The consistent moisture availability supports diverse cropping systems, including mushroom cultivation on logs or beds in the irrigated zones below the dam. The Keyline system's emphasis on soil creation and water retention creates conditions favorable for soil fungal networks and mycorrhizal associations.
