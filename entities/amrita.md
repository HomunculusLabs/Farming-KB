# Amrita (Amrta)

## Description

Amrita (Sanskrit: अमृत, meaning "immortality" or "deathless") is the elixir of
immortality in Hindu and Buddhist mythology. Produced by the churning of the cosmic
ocean (Samudra Manthana), it is the most sacred substance in Vedic tradition. Amrita
is functionally equivalent to Soma and in many contexts the terms are used
interchangeably. In Buddhist tradition, Amrta represents the nectar of enlightenment
consumed during highest-level initiatory practices.

## Classification

- **Category:** Mythological / ritual substance
- **Cultural origin:** Hinduism (Vedic), Buddhism (Vajrayana)
- **Sanskrit etymology:** A- (without) + mrta (death) = immortal
- **Related concepts:** Soma, Haoma, Ambrosia, Tchefau, Eucharist
- **Ethnomycological identification:** [[allegro-amanita-muscaria-properties-and-pharmacology]] (proposed)

## Key Facts

- Produced through the Churning of the Milky Ocean (Samudra Manthana).
- 54 gods and 54 demons pull the Naga serpent Vasouki, wrapped around Mount Mandera,
  to churn the cosmic ocean.
- Vishnu steadies the mountain as the tortoise Kurma (earth's axial wobble through
  precession of the equinoxes).
- The myth encodes astronomical knowledge: Milky Way spiral and precession.
- In Buddhism, Amrta is consumed during Vajrayana initiations.
- Identified with the "wish-fulfilling jewel" (Cintamani) in Tibetan tradition.
- The torma cakes used in Tibetan ritual symbolize Amrta.

## The Churning of the Milky Ocean

Depicted monumentally at Angkor Wat and Angkor Thom, Cambodia:

1. **Vasouki (Naga serpent):** Churning rope around Mount Mandera
2. **Mount Mandera:** Cosmic axis pivot
3. **54 Devas + 54 Asuras:** Pull alternately (108 total, a sacred number)
4. **Vishnu (tortoise Kurma):** Supports the mountain base
5. **Product:** Amrita, the elixir of immortality

The temple architecture represents a megalithic pine forest in stone, with mushroom
imagery at the base of the pine-tree towers.

## Ethnomycological Significance

- **Pine forest association:** Angkor Wat designed as a stone pine forest; A. muscaria
  grows mycorrhizally with conifers. "Testing of aspirants" occurs at the base of
  pine towers, where mushrooms are found in nature.
- **Duality of food and deity:** Like Soma, Amrita is both substance consumed and
  divine being — the [[psilocin-accumulation-pattern-across-flushes]] Egyptian, Christian, and Mithraic traditions.
- **Lightning association:** Amrta linked to Vajra (lightning bolt); folk mythology
  holds mushrooms grow where lightning strikes.
- **Hidden knowledge:** True identity reserved for highest initiatory levels.
