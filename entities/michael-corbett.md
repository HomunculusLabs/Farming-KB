## Description

**Michael Corbett** is a designer and developer best known as the creator of
**Village Homes** in Davis, California, USA — a pioneering suburban development
that integrated [[passive-solar-design]], common orchard areas, and natural drainage. Village Homes
is one of the most-cited examples of ecological suburban design in the
permaculture literature.

## Classification

- **Category**: Person — Designer / Developer
- **Domain**: Urban planning, Ecological design, Permaculture
- **Location**: Davis, California, USA
- **Notable work**: Village Homes (built 1970s–1980s)

## Key Facts

- Designer and developer of **Village Homes**, Davis, CA — a 70-acre residential
  development featuring integrated swale drainage, passive solar orientation,
  common food-producing landscapes, and pedestrian-priority layout.
- Village Homes features an **extensive swale system** that captures road runoff
  and infiltrates it into the soil, creating a deepening soil-stored water
  reservoir beneath the development in what would otherwise be a desert climate.
- The swales are heavily shaded by trees nourished by swale soakage, creating
  lush microclimates that contrast dramatically with surrounding conventional
  developments.
- The development predates modern "green building" standards by decades.
- Cited by Mollison as a key example of **swale-based water harvesting** in
  suburban settings.

## Design Innovations at Village Homes

1. **Swale drainage**: Gravel-based swales collect road runoff, infiltrating
   water into soil rather than piping it to storm drains.
2. **Passive solar**: All homes oriented for solar access; deciduous trees
   provide summer shade and winter sun.
3. **Common areas**: Shared orchards, vineyards, and garden spaces maintained
   by community.
4. **Pedestrian priority**: Narrow streets, extensive bike paths, limited car
   access to create safe, quiet neighbourhoods.
5. **Food production**: Almond orchards, community gardens, fruit trees
   integrated throughout the development.

## Relevance to Cultivation / Mycology

- The swale systems at Village Homes create **fungal-friendly soil conditions**
  through constant moisture infiltration and organic matter accumulation.
- Tree-lined swales produce significant **leaf litter** that feeds decomposer
  fungal communities in the soil.
- The integrated food-producing landscapes (orchards, vineyards) benefit from
  **mycorrhizal networks** supported by the swale irrigation system.
- Demonstrates how **suburban-scale water harvesting** can maintain soil
  moisture levels favourable to fungal activity even in semi-arid climates.
- The mulched swale bases provide habitat for **saprophytic fungi** that
  decompose organic matter and support soil structure.
