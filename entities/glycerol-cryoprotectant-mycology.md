## Description

[[glycerol]] (also known as glycerin) is a simple polyol compound used in mycological laboratories as a cryoprotectant for preserving fungal cultures through freezing. At a 5% concentration, glycerol prevents ice crystal formation from rupturing mycelial cells during the freezing process, enabling near-indefinite storage of valuable culture collections.

## Classification

- **Category**: Laboratory reagent / Cryoprotectant
- **Chemical name**: Glycerol / Glycerin (propane-1,2,3-triol)
- **Concentration used**: 5% solution in sterile water or culture broth
- **Function**: Prevents intracellular ice crystal formation during freezing

## Key Facts

- Used at a 5% concentration (e.g., 5 ml glycerol per 95 ml culture slurry or water)
- Must be sterilized separately before mixing with culture materials
- [[liquid-culture]] must be pre-chilled before adding glycerol; adding glycerol to warm culture can damage [[mycelium]] of some species
- Compatible with colonized agar cubes or grain submerged in the glycerol solution
- Professional laboratories prefer a slurry method: blending colonized agar with sterile water, adding to weak nutrient broth, agitating for 1-3 days to allow mycelial recovery, then adding glycerol

## Preparation Methods

### Method 1: Direct Submersion
1. Sterilize water and glycerol separately
2. Refrigerate both before mixing
3. Fill test tubes half full with sterile water
4. Add glycerol to achieve 5% concentration
5. Agitate to distribute
6. Add colonized agar cubes or grain
7. Freeze with caps loose, then seal tight once frozen

### Method 2: Slurry Method (Professional)
1. Blend colonized agar with sterile water to make slurry
2. Transfer to liquid broth with 10-20% of original nutrient/antibiotic formula
3. Agitate with magnetic stirrer for 1-3 days (allows mycelium to recover from blender damage)
4. Pre-chill mixture on ice or in refrigerator
5. Add glycerol to 5% concentration (e.g., 95 ml slurry + 5 ml glycerol)
6. Transfer to sterile storage tubes and freeze

## Thawing Protocol

1. Place frozen vials in warm water bath at 77-86 F (25-30 C) for 2-5 minutes
2. Transfer immediately to fresh agar plate
3. Observe recovery growth; should resemble original pre-frozen culture
4. Verify purity via microscopic examination (check for clamp connections)
5. Test viability monthly during initial freeze testing

## Relevance to Cultivation

Glycerol-based cryopreservation is the gold standard for long-term [[fungal-culture-storage]], enabling cultivators to maintain libraries of valuable isolates almost indefinitely. For commercial operations and serious cultivators, the ability to freeze and reliably recover pure cultures is essential for maintaining consistent production quality, preserving wild isolates, and protecting against catastrophic loss of culture collections. The technique is particularly valuable for preserving generation 0 isolates at the peak of their genetic vigor.
