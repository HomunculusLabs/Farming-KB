## Taxonomy [[trichome-development-and-morphology]]

Weigela florida was described by Prince Maximilian zu Wied-Neuwied in 1844 and
placed in the family Caprifoliaceae. The genus contains approximately 12 species
native to eastern Asia.

The shrub is rounded to spreading, typically reaching 1.5 to 3 meters in height
with a similar spread. The branches are arching and form a dense, bushy habit.

Leaves are opposite, simple, ovate to oblong, and 5 to 10 centimeters long.
They have finely serrated margins and are dark green above with paler
undersides. Foliage quality varies among cultivars.

Flowers are borne in short cymose clusters along the previous year's growth.
Each flower is tubular-funnel-shaped, approximately 2.5 to 3.5 centimeters
long, with five rounded lobes.

## Ecology and Pollinators

The tubular flowers of Weigela florida are highly attractive to pollinators.
Hummingbirds are particularly drawn to the red and pink-flowered cultivars,
while bees and butterflies visit all color forms.

The nectar is produced at the base of the long corolla tube, favoring
long-tongued pollinators. This specialized floral morphology reflects
coevolution with native Asian pollinator communities.

In its native range, the shrub grows in mixed deciduous scrub and provides
cover and nesting habitat for small birds. The dense branching offers
protection from predators and weather.

In [[berry-growing]], Weigela is not considered invasive. It spreads primarily
through cultivation rather than naturalization. Occasional seedling volunteers
appear near planted specimens but rarely establish persistent populations.

The foliage provides browse for deer, though it is not a preferred food source.
