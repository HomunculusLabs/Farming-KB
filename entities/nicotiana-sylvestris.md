It is prized in ornamental horticulture for its tall, elegant habit and intensely fragrant, tubular white flowers that open in the evening.

The species is of significant scientific importance as the maternal parent of [[chiric-caspi-brunfelsia-south-american-hallucinogen]] group within the genus.

Nicotiana sylvestris forms a basal rosette of large, broadly elliptic leaves 15 to 30 centimeters long during its vegetative phase.

The flowering stem rises to 100 to 180 centimeters, producing a tall, open, somewhat branching inflorescence.

Individual flowers are tubular, approximately 5 to 7 centimeters long, pure white, and strongly reflexed at the mouth.

Flowers open in late afternoon and evening, releasing a powerful jasmine-like fragrance throughout the night.

The floral tube is notably long relative to other Nicotiana species, an adaptation to pollination by long-tongued hawkmoths.

Each flower produces a capsule containing numerous tiny seeds approximately 0.5 millimeters in diameter.

## Distribution and Habitat

Nicotiana sylvestris is native to the Andean foothills and adjacent lowlands of northwestern Argentina, specifically in the provinces of Salta, Jujuy, and Tucuman.

It typically grows in semi-shaded habitats along streambanks, in ravines, forest margins, and disturbed sites at elevations between 1000 and 2500 meters.

The species prefers moist, fertile, well-drained soils in positions with partial shade, though it adapts to full sun in cultivation.

In its native habitat, it often colonizes disturbed sites and forest clearings where competition from taller vegetation is reduced.

The natural range is relatively restricted, making the species potentially vulnerable to habitat loss in its native Andean populations.

It has naturalized occasionally in [[query-how-to-grow-and-use-oca-in-temperate-climates]].

Seeds require light for germination and should be pressed into the soil surface without covering.

Germination occurs in 10 to 21 days at temperatures of 18 to 22 degrees Celsius.

The species is grown as an annual in most temperate gardens, though it is technically a tender perennial hardy only in USDA zones 10 to 11.

Plants prefer rich, moist, well-drained soil in partial shade to full sun, with afternoon shade beneficial in hot climates.

Regular watering and fertilization promote vigorous growth and abundant flowering throughout the summer and into early autumn.

Deadheading spent flowers encourages continued bloom but reduces seed production for those wishing to save seed.

The species is relatively pest-free in garden conditions, though aphids and tobacco hornworms may occasionally feed on the foliage.

No named cultivars are widely available, though white-flowered selections with enhanced fragrance exist in the specialty seed trade.

## Chemistry and Genetics

Like all Nicotiana species, Nicotiana sylvestris produces nicotine as its primary alkaloid, concentrated in the leaves at concentrations of approximately 0.5 to 2 percent of dry weight.
