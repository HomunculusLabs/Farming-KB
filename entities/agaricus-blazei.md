# Agaricus blazei

**Type:** Species
**Category:** Basidiomycete
**Also known as:** Himematsutake, Almond Portobella, Royal Sun Agaricus

## Overview

Agaricus blazei is a medicinal mushroom native to the mountainous
region near Sao Paulo, Brazil. First discovered in the USA during the
1940s, it is now commercially cultivated in Japan and Brazil, where it
ranks among the most expensive medicinal mushrooms available. Native
Brazilian populations historically showed low cancer incidence, which
has been correlated with regular consumption of this mushroom.

## Key Details

### Taxonomy and Classification
Agaricus blazei belongs to the family Agaricaceae within the
Basidiomycota phylum. It is a saprophytic fungus that thrives in
tropical and subtropical climates. The species is closely related to
the common button mushroom but is distinguished by its almond-like
aroma and superior medicinal properties.

### Active Compounds
The mushroom contains a rich profile of bioactive polysaccharides and
proteoglucans with demonstrated antitumour and immune-enhancing
properties. Key active compounds include:

- Beta-(1,3)-D-glucan: antitumour and immune enhancing
- Beta-(1,4)-D-glucan: antitumour and immune enhancing
- Beta-(1,6)-D-glucan: antitumour and immune enhancing
- Proteoglucans: antitumour activity

Several specific bioactive fractions have been isolated and studied:
FI1-a-beta (beta-glucan), FIII2-beta (beta-glucan-protein complex),
FA-1a-beta (hetero-beta-glucan), FA-2b-beta (RNA fraction), and FV-1
(insoluble beta-glucan).

### Novel Polysaccharide Complexes
A novel polysaccharide-protein complex designated ATOM
(Agaricus blazei Ortiz Mayor) has shown high activity against
xenografts (Ito et al., 1997). Submerged mycelial biomass culture
produces both ATOM, a glucomannan-protein complex, and AB-FP, a
mannan-protein, making liquid fermentation a viable production method
for these therapeutically active compounds.

### Medicinal Properties
Epidemiological studies confirm the immunostimulatory and antitumour
action of Agaricus blazei. The mushroom is recognised as an important
source of antitumour polysaccharides and has demonstrated significant
activity in various experimental models. It is widely used in Japan as
an adjunct to conventional cancer therapies.

### Cultivation
Agaricus blazei is cultivated commercially on composted substrates
similar to those used for [[agaricus-bisporus]], though requiring warmer
temperatures. Brazil and Japan are the primary production centres.
Commercial cultivation has expanded significantly due to growing demand
for medicinal mushroom supplements.

## Relevance

Agaricus blazei represents one of the most commercially significant
medicinal mushrooms in the global market. Its high price in Japan
reflects strong demand driven by documented health benefits. For
growers and mycologists, this species offers a high-value crop with
established therapeutic applications. The ability to produce bioactive
compounds through submerged fermentation also makes it attractive for
biopharmaceutical production, bypassing the need for fruiting body
cultivation.
