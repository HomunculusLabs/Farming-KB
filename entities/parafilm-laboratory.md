## Description

Parafilm is a flexible, stretchable, self-sealing laboratory film used extensively in [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]] to seal petri plates, test tubes, and other culture containers. It creates a semi-permeable barrier that allows limited gas exchange while preventing desiccation and contamination of agar cultures and stored specimens. In mushroom laboratories, Parafilm is one of the most frequently used consumable supplies, appearing at virtually every stage of culture work from isolation through storage.

## Classification

- **Category**: Laboratory supply / Culture sealing material
- **Material**: Paraffin wax-based film with plastic backing
- **Function**: Sealing culture containers while allowing gas exchange
- **Manufacturer**: Originally American Can Company, now Bemis Company / Amcor
- **Standard widths**: 2-inch (for petri plates) and 4-inch (for larger containers)

## Key Facts

- Used to seal agar plates after inoculation, preventing contamination while allowing the mycelium to breathe
- Also used to seal test tubes for [[agar-slant]] storage and [[submerged-agar-cube-storage]]
- Stretchable and self-adhering; wraps tightly around containers of various shapes and sizes
- Semi-permeable: allows some gas exchange while blocking airborne contaminants and reducing moisture loss
- Replaced by tape in some applications where Parafilm is not available, though tape provides a less effective seal
- Can be cut to size or stretched directly from the roll
- Important caveat: alcohol dissolves marker labeling written on plates sealed with Parafilm, which can destroy critical identification information

## Applications in Mushroom Cultivation

Parafilm appears throughout the mushroom cultivation workflow:

- **Petri plate sealing**: After tissue transfer or [[spore-streaking]], plates are sealed with Parafilm to prevent contamination and desiccation
- **Agar slant storage**: Tubes of agar slants are sealed with Parafilm before refrigeration to maintain sterility and moisture
- **Frozen culture tubes**: After freezing, tube caps are sealed with Parafilm for an additional layer of protection against moisture and contamination
- **Contaminated plate handling**: Used to seal contaminated plates during transport and disposal, preventing the spread of mold spores
- **[[streak-test]] plates**: Sealed with Parafilm during incubation to prevent external contamination while allowing gas exchange
- **Submerged agar storage**: Test tubes containing submerged agar cubes are sealed with Parafilm

## Handling Notes

- Stretch slightly when applying to create a tight, conforming seal around the container edge
- Apply with clean or gloved hands to avoid introducing contamination
- Alcohol dissolves permanent marker labeling; be careful when cleaning sealed plates with alcohol, as it can remove critical identification data
- Available in various widths; 2-inch width is standard for petri plates, wider rolls for tubes and flasks
- Can be reused on the same plate if carefully removed, though fresh film is recommended for best results
- Store in a cool, dry place; the film can become brittle in very cold conditions

## Relevance to Cultivation

Parafilm is one of the most commonly used consumable supplies in mushroom cultivation laboratories. Proper sealing of agar plates and culture containers is fundamental to maintaining sterile cultures and preventing cross-contamination between isolates. While tape can substitute in many applications, Parafilm's flexibility, self-sealing properties, and semi-permeability make it the preferred choice for professional and hobbyist cultivators alike. Cotter references Parafilm throughout his protocols for sealing plates, slants, and frozen culture tubes, making it one of the essential supplies for any mushroom cultivation laboratory.
