# Picamilon

**Type:** Chemical / Pharmaceutical / Niacin-GABA Prodrug
**Category:** Nootropic vasodilator
**Also known as:** Nicotinoyl-GABA, Pikamilon

## Overview

Picamilon is a synthetic compound formed by combining niacin (nicotinic
acid, vitamin B3) with GABA (gamma-aminobutyric acid) via a peptide bond.
Developed in the Soviet Union in 1969 at the All-Union Vitamin Research
Institute, it was designed to overcome GABA's inability to cross the
blood-brain barrier. The niacin moiety provides lipophilicity for CNS
penetration, where the compound hydrolyzes to release both GABA and
niacin independently.

In Russia and neighboring countries, picamilon is prescribed as a
medication for cerebrovascular conditions including stroke recovery,
migraine, and chronic cerebral insufficiency. In Western markets, it has
been sold as a dietary supplement for cognitive enhancement and anxiety
reduction. The FDA ruled in 2015 that picamilon does not qualify as a
dietary supplement, removing it from the US market.

## Chemical Identity

- Molecular formula: C10H12N2O3
- Molecular weight: 208.21 g/mol
- IUPAC name: 4-(pyridine-3-carbonylamino)butanoic acid
- CAS number: 769-78-8
- Appearance: White to slightly yellowish crystalline powder
- Solubility: Freely soluble in water; sparingly soluble in ethanol

Picamilon is a prodrug that undergoes enzymatic hydrolysis after crossing
the blood-brain barrier. The cleavage products — GABA and nicotinic acid —
exert their pharmacological effects independently. This dual-release
mechanism distinguishes picamilon from simple GABA derivatives like
phenibut, which acts primarily on GABA receptors.

## Pharmacology

### Mechanism of Action

Picamilon's effects derive from its two metabolites acting in concert.
GABA provides anxiolytic and mild sedative effects through GABA-A and
GABA-B receptor activation. Niacin produces significant cerebral
vasodilation through activation of the GPR109A receptor, increasing
cerebral blood flow and oxygen delivery.

The vasodilatory effect is the key differentiator from phenibut. While
phenibut acts almost exclusively through GABA-B receptors, picamilon's
niacin component addresses cerebrovascular insufficiency directly. This
dual mechanism makes it particularly suited for conditions where reduced
cerebral blood flow contributes to cognitive and mood symptoms.

### Pharmacokinetics

- Oral bioavailability: High (prodrug design enables BBB penetration)
- Onset: 30-60 minutes
- Duration: 4-6 hours
- Half-life: Data limited; estimated 1-2 hours (parent compound)
- Metabolism: Hydrolysis to GABA and nicotinic acid in brain tissue
- Excretion: Renal

## Dosage and Effects

### Dosage Ranges

- **Cerebrovascular disorders:** 50-100 mg 2-3 times daily
- **Migraine prevention:** 50 mg 2-3 times daily
- **Anxiolytic/nootropic:** 50-100 mg once or twice daily
- **Maximum daily dose:** 300 mg (Russian prescribing guidelines)

### Subjective Effects

- **Vasodilatory:** Sensation of warmth, flushing (niacin effect), mild
  headache during initial use as cerebral blood flow adjusts
- **Anxiolysis:** Mild anxiety reduction, comparable to low-dose phenibut
  but generally less potent
- **Cognitive:** Improved mental clarity and focus in some users,
  attributed to enhanced cerebral perfusion
- **Mood:** Slight mood elevation and emotional stability

## Safety and Tolerance

### Tolerance and Dependence

Picamilon carries significantly lower tolerance and dependence risk
compared to phenibut. The GABA component produces mild receptor
modulation rather than the strong GABA-B agonism of phenibut, resulting
in slower tolerance development. Most users report no withdrawal symptoms
after discontinuation from standard therapeutic doses.

However, long-term daily use at maximum doses may produce mild tolerance
to the anxiolytic component. Russian medical guidelines recommend
treatment cycles of 2-4 weeks with intervening breaks.

### Adverse Effects

- Niacin flushing (warmth, redness, tingling — dose-dependent)
- Mild headache and dizziness during initial treatment
- Gastrointestinal discomfort at higher doses
- Transient hypotension from vasodilation
- Insomnia in some individuals (paradoxical effect)
