# Eucalyptus melliodora

## Description

Eucalyptus melliodora, commonly known as **yellow box** or **honey box**, is a medium to tall tree widespread across southeastern Australia. It is regarded as one of the best honey-producing eucalypts in Australia, yielding a pale, high-quality honey that is considered premium. The species is well-adapted to a range of conditions from temperate to semi-arid zones.

## Classification

- **Family:** Myrtaceae
- **Genus:** Eucalyptus
- **Species:** E. melliodora
- **Common names:** Yellow box, honey box

## Key Facts

- Medium to tall tree, typically 15–30 m in height
- Rough, fibrous bark on the lower trunk, smooth pale above
- Widespread across inland New South Wales, Victoria, and Queensland
- Adapted to a wide range of soils including heavy clays, loams, and alluvial flats
- One of the premier honey trees in Australia — the species epithet "melliodora" means "honey-scented"
- Drought-tolerant, surviving in areas with 400–800 mm annual rainfall
- Timber is hard, dense, and durable

## Design Considerations

For permaculture use, Eucalyptus melliodora is most appropriate where there is enough space for a long-lived canopy tree.
It is not a small-garden species, but it can be valuable on farms, shelterbelts, apiary sites, and restoration blocks.
Its tolerance of clay and alluvial soils makes it useful in places where more delicate timber or fruit species struggle.
However, site selection should account for mature height, root competition, and fire behavior.
The tree's value to [[farm-forestry-design-mollison]] terms, its multiple yields make it a classic stacking species: honey, shade, habitat, posts, and fuel.

## Management Notes

Seed propagation is straightforward, but young plants benefit from weed control and protection from browsing.
Once established, the species becomes notably drought hardy.
Growth rate varies with soil depth, rainfall, and competition.
Plantings should preserve genetic suitability by sourcing seed from climates similar to the planting site.
In restoration contexts, yellow box is often paired with native grasses and other woodland trees rather than dense underplanting.
