# Eucalyptus melliodora

## Description

Eucalyptus melliodora, commonly known as **yellow box** or **honey box**, is a medium to tall tree widespread across southeastern Australia. It is regarded as one of the best honey-producing eucalypts in Australia, yielding a pale, high-quality honey that is considered premium. The species is well-adapted to a range of conditions from temperate to semi-arid zones.

## Classification

- **Family:** Myrtaceae
- **Genus:** Eucalyptus
- **Species:** E. melliodora
- **Common names:** Yellow box, honey box

## Key Facts

- Medium to tall tree, typically 15–30 m in height
- Rough, fibrous bark on the lower trunk, smooth pale above
- Widespread across inland New South Wales, Victoria, and Queensland
- Adapted to a wide range of soils including heavy clays, loams, and alluvial flats
- One of the premier honey trees in Australia — the species epithet "melliodora" means "honey-scented"
- Drought-tolerant, surviving in areas with 400–800 mm annual rainfall
- Timber is hard, dense, and durable

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

- **Honey:** Considered one of the top honey-producing eucalypts; yields premium light-coloured honey (pages 94, 116)
- **Timber:** Hard, dense timber suitable for fence posts, sleepers, and heavy construction (pages 94, 116)
- **Firewood:** Dense wood makes excellent, long-burning firewood (pages 94, 116)
- **Shade and shelter:** Well-suited to farm shelterbelts and windbreaks
- **Fodder:** Leaves occasionally browsed by livestock during drought
- **[[hemenway-backyard-wildlife-habitat]]:** Provides important nectar and pollen source for [[query-how-to-attract-native-bees-and-beneficial-insects]], birds, and mammals

## Cultivation Notes

- Tolerates a wide range of soils including heavy clays
- Drought-tolerant once established
- Suited to temperate and semi-arid zones with 400–800 mm rainfall
- Moderate frost tolerance
- Moderate [[soil-temperature-plant-growth-rate-doubling-rule]]
- Long-lived species
- Best propagated from seed
