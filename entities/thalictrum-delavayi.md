## Thalictrum delavayi

## Overview

Thalictrum delavayi, commonly known as Chinese meadow rue or Delavay's meadow rue, is an elegant herbaceous perennial in the family Ranunculaceae.

Native to southwestern China, it is prized for its airy sprays of pendulous lavender flowers and delicate, maidenhair-fern-like foliage. The species has become increasingly popular in temperate gardens worldwide as a reliable, long-blooming shade perennial.

It was named in honor of Père Jean Marie Delavay (1834-1895), a French Catholic missionary and plant collector who made extensive botanical collections in Yunnan province during the late 19th century.

Leaves are 2-3 times ternately compound, with small, rounded leaflets 5-15 mm wide, reminiscent of maidenhair fern (Adiantum). The foliage is glaucous blue-green, providing textural contrast in the garden even when the plant is not in bloom.

Flowering stems reach 90-150 cm tall, branching into airy panicles of nodding flowers. Each flower lacks true petals, instead displaying showy lavender-mauve sepals 6-10 mm long surrounding prominent pendulous stamens with pale yellow anthers.

The stamens are the primary ornamental feature, creating a fluffy, cloud-like effect when viewed en masse. Bloom period extends from midsummer to early autumn, making it one of the latest-flowering Thalictrum species.
