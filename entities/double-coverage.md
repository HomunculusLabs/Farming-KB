# Double Coverage

## Description

Double coverage is the practice of using two layers of transparent protective material — an outer greenhouse or hoop house and an inner layer of cold frames or floating row covers — to create a protected microclimate significantly warmer than ambient outdoor conditions. Discovered by [[eliot-coleman]] in the winter of 1980, double coverage is the foundational principle of the [[winter-harvest-system]], enabling cold-hardy vegetable production in unheated structures through the coldest months.

## Classification

- **Category**: Agricultural technique / season-extension method
- **Type**: Passive solar microclimate management
- **Layers**: Two (outer structure + inner covers)
- **Energy input**: None (entirely solar-powered)

## Key Facts

### The Discovery

In the winter of 1980, Coleman erected cold frames inside an unheated greenhouse constructed in a traditional Dutch style. The result was dramatic: plants effectively moved an additional 500 miles south to a Zone 8 climate, with the added benefit of a snow-shedding roof. This was the great leap forward in the winter harvest concept.

### How It Works

Each layer of protection provides approximately 1.5 USDA hardiness zones of climate moderation:
- **Single layer** (greenhouse only): Moves plants ~500 miles south (1.5 zones)
- **Double layer** (greenhouse + inner covers): Moves plants ~1,000 miles south (3 zones)

At Coleman's Maine farm (Zone 5), double coverage creates an effective Zone 8 microclimate, comparable to the coastal Pacific Northwest or parts of the southeastern United States.

### Progressive Protection Effect

The effectiveness of the inner layer increases progressively as ambient temperature drops:
- At 25F (-4C) outside: Inner layer adds 6-7F (3.5C) of protection
- At -15F (-26C) outside: Inner layer adds 30-35F (16-19C) of protection

This means double coverage is most effective precisely when it is most needed — during the coldest nights. The protective blanket becomes dramatically more effective just below the point at which tender crops freeze.

### Inner Layer Materials

The inner layer has evolved over time:
- **Original**: Glass cold frames (Dutch lights) — excellent protection but costly and labor-intensive
- **Current**: Floating row covers on flat-topped wire wickets — self-venting, lighter, easier to manage, less expensive

Row covers are held approximately 12 inches (one foot) above the soil surface, creating an insulating air gap between the cover and the crop.

## Relevance to Cultivation

### Soil Temperature Management

The soil under the inner layer of cold greenhouses experiences no more than light surface freezing even on the coldest nights. With the possible exception of one or two days each winter when an extremely cold night follows a heavily clouded day, the soil in the beds is always unfrozen by 10 AM and ready to rake into a fine new seedbed for replanting.

### The Diurnal Heat Cycle

The key mechanism is diurnal — happening in the daytime. Solar energy warms the covered beds above freezing during the day, and this stored heat is retained through the night by the double layers. This daily influx of the sun's heat is the key to successful winter production in cold houses.

### Replanting Through Winter

Double coverage makes it possible to replant right through the winter as spaces open up following harvest. This would be impossible outdoors during a Maine winter, but the protected soil remains workable throughout the season.

### The Harvest Season vs. Growing Season Distinction

Double coverage does not extend the growing season in the sense of maintaining summer growth rates through winter. Rather, it extends the harvest season — plants grow to harvestable size during the fall, then maintain their quality in a semi-dormant state through the Persephone period, ready for harvest whenever needed.

## Relationship to Other Concepts

- **Cold frame**: The original inner layer in the double-coverage system
- **[[floating-row-cover]]**: The modern replacement inner layer
- **Persephone days**: Double coverage cannot overcome the light limitation of the Persephone period
- **USDA hardiness zones**: Each coverage layer shifts effective zones by approximately 1.5
- **Cold house**: An unheated greenhouse using double coverage
- **Cool house**: A minimally heated greenhouse that may or may not use double coverage
