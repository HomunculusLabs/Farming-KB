# Chamaecrista fasciculata (Partridge Pea)

**Chamaecrista fasciculata**, commonly known as **partridge pea**, **sensitive pea**, **showy partridge pea**, or **prairie senna**, is an annual herbaceous legume native to eastern and central [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]]. It is highly valued in permaculture, [[tropical-rainforest-seedling-mycorrhiza-ecological-restoration]], and native landscaping for its nitrogen-fixing capability, pollinator support, wildlife value, and ornamental display of bright yellow flowers.

## Taxonomy and Nomenclature

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Division:** Magnoliophyta
- **Class:** Magnoliopsida
- **Order:** Fabales
- **Family:** Fabaceae (subfamily **Caesalpinioideae**)
- **Genus:** *Chamaecrista*
- **Species:** *C. fasciculata*

The species was formerly classified as **Cassia fasciculata** and is still widely referenced under that name in older literature and horticultural sources. The genus was reclassified based on molecular phylogenetic studies that separated the genus *Chamaecrista* from *Cassia* sensu stricto. The common name "partridge pea" refers to the seeds' use as food for northern bobwhite quail and other game birds. "Sensitive pea" reflects the compound leaves' tendency to fold when touched (thigmonastic response), similar to *[[tompkins-mimosa-pudica-sensitive-plant-experiments]]*.

## Description

### Growth Habit
- **[[agaricus-campestris-biology-and-life-cycle]]:** Annual (summer annual)
- **Height:** 30–150 cm (1–5 ft), typically 60–90 cm
- **Form:** Erect to ascending, much-branched, bushy appearance
- **Root system:** Taproot with abundant nitrogen-fixing nodules; roots can extend 30+ cm deep

### Stems
- **Color:** Light green to reddish-green, sometimes with purple striations
- **Texture:** Smooth to slightly hairy (pubescent)
- **Branching:** Highly branched from the base, giving an open, airy appearance

### Leaves
- **Type:** Alternate, even-pinnately compound
- **Leaflets:** 8–15 pairs per leaf, oblong to linear-oblong, 6–15 mm long
- **Color:** Medium green, folding shut when disturbed
- **Stipules:** Small, lance-shaped, often with dark glandular dots near the base
- **Movement:** Thigmonastic — leaflets fold within seconds of being touched

### Flowers
- **Inflorescence:** Axillary clusters (fascicles) of 1–6 flowers
- **Size:** 2–3 cm across
- **Color:** Bright yellow petals with reddish-purple markings at the base
- **Structure:** 5 yellow petals (slightly unequal), 5 sepals, 10 stamens (heterostylous — some long, some short)
- **Blooming period:** June through October (peak July–September)
- **Pollination:** Primarily by bumblebees (*Bombus* spp.); also visited by honeybees, sweat bees, and other insects

### Fruit and Seeds
- **Type:** Linear, flattened legume (pod)
- **Size:** 5–10 cm long × 4–6 mm wide
- **Color:** Green turning dark brown at maturity
- **Seeds:** 12–20 per pod, dark brown to black, flattened, oval
- **[[clay-pellet-seed-dispersal]]:** Explosive dehiscence (pods twist open and eject seeds); also dispersed by birds and water
- **Seed viability:** Can persist in soil [[query-how-do-i-create-a-seed-bank-and-start-seed-saving]] for 5–10+ years

## Distribution and Habitat

*C. fasciculata* is native to:

- **Eastern and central United States** — from Massachusetts to Florida, west to Minnesota, Nebraska, Colorado, and New Mexico
- **Eastern Canada** — southern Ontario
- **Northern Mexico**

It is adapted to a wide range of habitats:

- **Prairies and open grasslands**
- **Savannas and woodland edges**
- **Old fields and disturbed sites**
- **Roadsides and railway embankments**
- **Sandy or rocky soils** with low fertility
