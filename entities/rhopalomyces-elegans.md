## Description

**Rhopalomyces elegans** is a nematode-egg parasitic fungus that attacks the eggs of plant-parasitic nematodes. It has been identified as a potentially important natural regulator of nematode populations in agricultural soils.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Phylum**: [[ascomycota]]
- **Class**: Sordariomycetes
- **Order**: Coniochaetales
- **Family**: Coniochaetaceae
- **Genus**: Rhopalomyces
- **Species**: R. elegans

## Key Facts

- Mankau (1981) identified it as a nematode egg-parasitic fungus
- Capable of infecting nematode eggs, potentially reducing population recruitment
- Related cyst-pathogenic fungi (Verticillium and Cylindrosporium species) may infect 14-50% of cysts and 70-90% of eggs
- Limited information available on effects on nematode population regulation

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Nematode-egg parasitic fungi like R. elegans offer a targeted approach to managing plant-parasitic nematodes. Unlike trapping fungi that target mobile nematodes, egg parasites prevent population recruitment by attacking the reproductive stage. The high infection rates of related species (70-90% of eggs) suggest significant biocontrol potential.

## Ecological Role

R. elegans contributes to natural regulation of nematode populations in soil ecosystems. By parasitizing nematode eggs, it reduces recruitment into the nematode population, providing top-down control complementary to host plant resistance.
