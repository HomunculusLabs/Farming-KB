# Eucalyptus cladocalyx

## Description

Eucalyptus cladocalyx, commonly known as **sugar gum**, is a medium to tall tree native to South Australia, particularly Kangaroo Island and the southern Flinders Ranges. It is named for the sweet, sugary exudate produced by its leaves and branchlets. The species produces extremely hard, dense timber and is widely planted for shade, shelter, and fuel in southern Australia.

## Classification

- **Family:** Myrtaceae
- **Genus:** Eucalyptus
- **Species:** E. cladocalyx
- **Common names:** Sugar gum

## Key Facts

- Medium to tall tree, typically 15–35 m in height
- Smooth bark, grey to white, sometimes with a rough stocking at the base
- Native to South Australia (Kangaroo Island, southern Flinders Ranges, Eyre Peninsula)
- Named for the sugary exudate on leaves that attracts bees and insects
- Produces extremely dense, hard timber — one of the heaviest eucalypt woods
- Drought-tolerant and suited to areas with 350–600 mm annual rainfall
- Has become naturalised in parts of Victoria and Western Australia

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

- **Timber:** Extremely hard and dense timber used for fence posts, sleepers, and heavy construction (pages 94, 116)
- **Firewood:** One of the best firewood species due to extremely dense wood (pages 94, 116)
- **Shelter and windbreaks:** Widely planted for farm shelter in southern Australia
- **Honey:** Produces good nectar flow for [[faires-beekeeping-and-honey-production]]
- **[[comparison-vetiver-grass-vs-bamboo-for-erosion-control]]:** Deep root system helps stabilise eroded farmland
- **Ornamental:** Attractive smooth bark and dense canopy make it a popular [[mollison-designers-legume-shade-tree-pasture-systems]]

## Cultivation Notes

- Highly drought-tolerant once established
- Tolerates a wide range of soils including poor sands and heavy clays
- Suited to areas with 350–600 mm annual rainfall
- Moderate frost tolerance
- Fast-growing in favourable conditions
- Can become weedy outside its native range
- Best propagated from seed
