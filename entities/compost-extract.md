## Compost Extract

## Description

Compost extract is produced by soaking compost in water for a couple of weeks or more without active aeration. The result is an anaerobic liquid with perhaps a bit of aerobic activity on the surface. Compost extracts represent an older, traditional approach to using compost in liquid form, distinct from modern [[actively-aerated-compost-tea]] (AACT).

## Classification

- **Category:** Compost derivative / liquid amendment
- **Type:** Anaerobic preparation
- **Related to:** [[compost-leachate]], [[manure-tea]], AACT

## Key Facts

- Made by soaking compost in water for two or more weeks — no aeration is provided during the steeping period.
- The end result is predominantly anaerobic, with limited aerobic activity possible only at the surface.
- Loss of aerobic microbial diversity is a significant concern; anaerobic conditions favor different organisms than those beneficial to most plants.
- Risk of containing anaerobic pathogens and alcohols produced by fermentation under low-oxygen conditions.
- Generally not considered safe or advisable for use in gardens or on crops.
- Differs from AACT which uses active aeration to maintain aerobic conditions and promote beneficial microbial growth.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Compost extracts illustrate the importance of aeration in preparing biological inoculants. Anaerobic conditions select for organisms that produce alcohols and other compounds toxic to plant roots. The pathogens that can flourish in anaerobic extracts — including E. coli and other harmful bacteria — pose risks to both plants and human health.

For cultivators seeking to build [[soil-food-web]] biology, the distinction between anaerobic extracts and aerobic compost teas is critical. Only AACT reliably produces the high concentrations of beneficial bacteria, fungi, protozoa, and nematodes needed for effective nutrient cycling, disease suppression, and soil structure improvement.
