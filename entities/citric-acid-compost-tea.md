# Citric Acid

## Description
Citric acid (C6H8O7) is a naturally occurring organic acid found in citrus fruits and produced by many microorganisms through fermentation. In compost tea and soil management, citric acid serves multiple functions: pH adjustment, nutrient chelation (particularly iron and other micronutrients), and as a carbon source for beneficial microorganisms. Elaine Ingham includes citric acid as a component in the AACT (Actively Aerated Compost Tea) ingredient list, where it plays a role in nutrient availability and microbial support during the brewing process.

## Classification
- **Chemical formula**: C6H8O7
- **Chemical class**: Tricarboxylic acid (organic acid)
- **Molecular weight**: 192.12 g/mol
- **Appearance**: White crystalline powder
- **Solubility**: Highly soluble in water
- **Natural sources**: Citrus fruits, fermentation products of Aspergillus niger

## Key Facts

### Role in Compost Tea Production
- Acts as a mild pH adjuster — can lower pH of tea or soil to favor fungal growth
- Functions as a chelating agent, binding to metal ions (iron, calcium, magnesium) and keeping them in solution
- Provides a carbon source that supports beneficial microbial growth during brewing
- Helps maintain nutrient solubility in the tea solution throughout the brewing cycle
- Ingham lists citric acid among the ingredients for AACT formulation

### Chelation Properties
- Forms stable complexes with di- and trivalent metal ions
- Iron citrate (iron chelated with citrate) is a highly bioavailable form of iron for plants
- Prevents precipitation of micronutrients in alkaline conditions
- Biodegradable chelate — decomposes naturally, unlike synthetic chelates (EDTA, DTPA)
- Particularly important for iron, manganese, and zinc availability in growing systems

### Biological Functions
- Citric acid is an intermediate in the Krebs (TCA) cycle — fundamental to cellular respiration in all aerobic organisms
- Soil microorganisms produce citric acid as a metabolic byproduct
- Mycorrhizal fungi and other beneficial fungi secrete organic acids to solubilize minerals from rock and soil
- Citric acid-producing bacteria contribute to nutrient cycling in the rhizosphere

### Agricultural Applications
- Foliar spray additive to improve micronutrient delivery
- Soil drench component for correcting micronutrient deficiencies
- pH adjustment in hydroponic nutrient solutions
- Component of organic-approved fertilizer formulations
- Used in post-harvest treatments to reduce fungal spoilage on stored produce

### pH Management
- Citric acid can lower pH of alkaline soils or growing media
- Fungal-dominated compost teas benefit from slightly acidic conditions (pH 5.5-6.5)
- Helps create conditions favorable for beneficial fungi over bacteria when used appropriately
- More gentle and biodegradable than mineral acids (sulfuric, hydrochloric)

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology
- **Compost tea formulation**: Listed ingredient in Elaine Ingham's AACT recipes for chelation and pH management
- **Nutrient availability**: Essential for maintaining micronutrient solubility in biological growing systems
- **Microbial support**: Provides carbon substrate and chelation for beneficial organisms
- **Iron management**: Citric acid chelation of iron is critical for preventing chlorosis in alkaline conditions
- **Organic certification**: Natural, organically approved compound suitable for all biological farming systems
- **Mushroom cultivation**: pH adjustment in substrate preparation; organic acid metabolism is fundamental to fungal physiology
