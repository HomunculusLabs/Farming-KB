# Brahea edulis

## Overview

Brahea edulis, the Guadalupe palm, is a medium-sized fan palm endemic to
Guadalupe Island off the Pacific coast of Baja California, Mexico. It is one of
the most cold-tolerant Brahea species and a popular ornamental in Mediterranean
and warm-temperate climates.

The species reaches 10 to 13 meters in height with a robust trunk 35 to 45
centimeters in diameter covered in persistent fibrous leaf bases.

Guadalupe Island was historically overrun by feral goats that devastated the
island's endemic vegetation. Following complete goat eradication by 2007,
Brahea edulis has shown remarkable recovery, making it one of the most notable
conservation success stories among island endemic palms.

The edible fruits were an important food source for 19th-century sealers and
whalers visiting the island. The specific epithet *edulis* refers to the
palatability of the fruit.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:** Plantae → Tracheophyta → Liliopsida → Arecales → Arecaceae
→ Coryphoideae → Trachycarpeae → **Brahea edulis** H.Wendl. ex S.Watson
(1879). Described from collections on Guadalupe Island.

**Synonyms:** Erythea edulis (H.Wendl. ex S.Watson) S.Watson, Brahea dulcis
var. edulis (H.Wendl. ex S.Watson) Moore.

**Trunk:** Solitary, erect, 10 to 13 meters tall, 35 to 45 centimeters in
diameter. Covered in a dense mat of brown to grey fibrous leaf bases that sheds
to reveal a smooth grey trunk.

**Leaves:** Palmate, 1.2 to 1.5 meters in diameter, on a petiole 60 to 100
centimeters long. Divided into 40 to 55 segments, each 50 to 80 centimeters
long. Stiff, dark green above, glaucous green below. Not conspicuously
blue-silver like [[brahea-armata]].

**Inflorescence:** Interfoliar, erect, 1.0 to 1.5 meters long, branching to
two or three orders. Flowers small, cream to yellowish, male and female on
the same inflorescence.

**Fruit:** Spherical drupe, 1.5 to 2.0 centimeters in diameter, ripening from
green to brownish-black. Mesocarp is thin and sweet when ripe. Seed spherical,
1.0 to 1.4 centimeters in diameter.

## Distribution and Habitat

Brahea edulis is endemic to Guadalupe Island, approximately 260 kilometers west
of the Baja California peninsula. The island is roughly 35 kilometers long and
9 kilometers wide.

The species occurs primarily on north-facing slopes and in canyon bottoms
between 200 and 1200 meters elevation. It grows in mixed chaparral and pine
forest communities alongside island oak (Quercus tomentella) and island pine
([[pinus-radiata]] var. binata).

The island has a Mediterranean climate with cool wet winters and warm dry
summers. Annual precipitation ranges from 200 to 400 millimeters, primarily as
winter rain and fog drip.

Prior to goat eradication, Brahea edulis was restricted to inaccessible cliffs.
Post-eradication surveys have documented extensive juvenile recruitment in
formerly degraded areas.

## Conservation

For over 150 years, feral goats reduced Brahea edulis to a relict population on
cliffs. A systematic goat eradication program completed by 2007 led to dramatic
recovery within a decade.
