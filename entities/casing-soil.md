## Casing Soil

## Description

Casing soil is a non-nutritive or low-nutritive layer of material applied over colonized bulk substrates to stimulate mushroom fruiting. Casing soils create a high-humidity microenvironment that protects developing primordia from drying out and, for many species, provides the microbial interactions necessary to trigger fruitbody formation. Casing is a critical technique for cultivating terrestrial mushroom species such as [[agaricus]], Stropharia, Morchella, and others that require or benefit from microbial partnerships.

## Classification

- **Category**: Cultivation technique
- **Type**: Substrate amendment / fruiting trigger
- **Application**: Post-colonization, pre-fruiting stage

## Key Facts

- Casing soils are typically made from peat moss, coconut coir, vermiculite, perlite, sand, lime, and gypsum
- A high-quality casing has high water retention, good aeration, and is pH neutral to slightly acidic
- Applied at approximately 1 inch depth per 6-8 inches of colonized media below
- The casing structure should be loose with minute air pockets, not packed down tight
- Surface should be raked or dibbled to create ridges where primordia form in the hollows
- Casing can be pasteurized (130-145°F / 54-63°C for 6-8 hours) to preserve beneficial microbes
- Sterilized casing eliminates all microbes and may prevent fruiting in species requiring microbial triggers
- Most wood-decomposing mushrooms (shiitake, oyster) do NOT require casing

## Relevance to Cultivation

Casing is essential for reliable commercial production of [[agaricus-bisporus]] and beneficial for many other terrestrial species. The casing layer creates a critical microclimate transition zone where mycelium shifts from vegetative colonization to reproductive fruiting. As mycelium rises from dense substrate into less dense casing, it becomes rhizomorphic (strand-like), channeling resources upward. At the surface, growth shifts horizontally to form a dense mat and cross connections before initiating primordia.

## Relevance to Mycology

The discovery that sterilized casing prevents fruiting while pasteurized casing promotes it in Agaricus and other species reveals a fundamental aspect of fungal biology: many mushrooms require microbial partners to complete their life cycle. This has profound implications for understanding [[fungal-ecology]], decomposition, and the co-evolution of fungi with soil microbial communities.

## Moisture Testing

Squeeze a handful of casing: if a few drops come out, it is perfect. A steady stream means too wet. No drips means too dry. Some media (peat, coir) can be hydrophobic initially and need time to absorb water.
