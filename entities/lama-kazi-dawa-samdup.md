## Lama Kazi Dawa-Samdup

## Description

Lama Kazi Dawa-Samdup (1868–1922) was a Tibetan Buddhist scholar and translator.

He produced the first English translation of the [[bardo-thodol]] (Tibetan Book of the Dead).

His translation was published posthumously by W.Y. [[evans-wentz]] in 1927.

Dawa-Samdup held that Adi-Buddha and [[dharma-kaya]] deities are personifications of primordial universal forces, not personal deities.

## Classification

- **Type**: Historical person — translator and Buddhist scholar
- **Tradition**: Tibetan Buddhism
- **Nationality**: Tibetan/Sikkimese
- **Key Work**: English translation of the *Bardo Thodol*
- **Role**: Primary cultural mediator between Tibetan Buddhism and the English-speaking world

## Key Facts

1. Lama Kazi Dawa-Samdup was born in 1868 in Sikkim to a Tibetan Buddhist family.

2. He served as headmaster of the Bhutia Boarding School in Gangtok, Sikkim.

3. He was fluent in Tibetan, English, Sanskrit, and several other languages.

4. His translation of the Bardo Thodol was the first complete English version of this foundational text.

5. The translation was edited and published by W.Y. Evans-Wentz after Dawa-Samdup"s death in 1922.

6. He held that Adi-Buddha and Dharma-Kaya deities are personifications of primordial universal forces.

7. He explicitly stated these are not personal deities but expressions of fundamental cosmic principles.

8. His interpretive framework shaped how Western readers understood Tibetan Buddhist concepts.

9. Evans-Wentz's edition, built on Dawa-Samdup's translation, became the basis for [[carl-jung]]'s psychological commentary.

10. Without Dawa-Samdup's translation, the Bardo Thodol would not have reached the Western audience that inspired *The Psychedelic Experience*.

## The Translation of the Bardo Thodol

Dawa-Samdup's translation work was meticulous, combining scholarly precision with deep experiential understanding.

He worked directly from Tibetan texts, providing not just literal translation but contextual interpretation.

His notes and commentary clarified the philosophical and practical dimensions of the Bardo teachings.

The Evans-Wentz edition added supplementary material but relied entirely on Dawa-Samdup's core translation.

## Philosophical Contributions

His view that Dharma-Kaya deities are personifications rather than personal gods was a crucial interpretive insight.

This perspective allowed for a psychological rather than merely theological reading of the Bardo Thodol.

It opened the door for Carl Jung's psychological commentary and, ultimately, for Leary's psychedelic adaptation.

## Relevance to Psychedelic Studies

Lama Kazi Dawa-Samdup's translation is the foundational text upon which *The Psychedelic Experience* was built.

Without his work, [[timothy-leary]] would not have had access to the Bardo Thodol's detailed psychological map.

His non-theistic interpretation of Dharma-Kaya deities made the text compatible with psychological and psychedelic frameworks.

## Relation to Other Entities

- **Dharma-Kaya**: Dawa-Samdup's key interpretive contribution regarding its non-theistic nature.
- **Adi-Kaya**: His understanding of Adi-Buddha as a personification of primordial force.
- **Carl Jung**: Wrote the psychological commentary for the edition containing Dawa-Samdup's translation.
- **W.Y. Evans-Wentz**: Editor and publisher of Dawa-Samdup's translation.
- **Lama Govinda**: Fellow Tibetan Buddhist scholar cited alongside Dawa-Samdup.
