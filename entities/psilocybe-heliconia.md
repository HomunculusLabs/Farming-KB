# Psilocybe heliconia

Psilocybe heliconiae is a neotropical psychoactive mushroom species in the
family Strophariaceae, first described by Guzmán, Saldarriaga, Pineda, García
and Velázquez. It belongs to the diverse Psilocybe flora of the northern Andes
and is one of the lesser-known bluing Psilocybe species from South America.
The species is named for its association with Heliconia plants in its tropical
habitat. Like other bluing Psilocybes, it contains psilocybin and/or psilocin
as its primary active compounds.

## Taxonomy and Naming

- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Strophariaceae
- **Genus:** Psilocybe
- **Species:** P. heliconiae Guzmán, Saldarriaga, Pineda, García & Velázquez
- **Authority:** Guzmán et al. (1994)
- **Species number 104** in Guzmán's worldwide checklist of neurotropic fungi
- The epithet "heliconiae" refers to the genus of tropical flowering plants
  (Heliconia, family Heliconiaceae) associated with the habitat where this
  species was originally collected

## Description

### Macroscopic Features

Cap: Small, conic to campanulate, expanding with age. Surface viscid when
moist, hygrophanous. Coloration typically brownish to fuliginous, often with
a lighter margin that becomes more translucent-striate when wet. The cap
surface may show remnants of the partial veil in young specimens.
Gills: Adnexed to adnate, close to subdistant. Grayish when young, darkening
to purplish brown or nearly black with spore maturity, with whitish edges
that become mottled as spores mature.
Stem: Cylindrical, equal to slightly thickened at the base, hollow. Surface
pruinose above, silky to striate below the annular zone. Color pallid to
brownish, bruising bluish where handled or injured.
Partial veil: Cortinate, leaving a faint annular zone on the stem.
Bruising: Strong bluing reaction upon handling or injury, characteristic of
psilocybin-containing species.

### Microscopic Features

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Spore print | Dark purplish brown |
| Spores | Ellipsoid to subellipsoid, thick-walled |
| Spore size | 8-12 × 5-7 µm (typical for Section Psilocybe) |
| Basidia | 4-spored, clavate |
| Cheilocystidia | Ventricose to lageniform, abundant |
| Pleurocystidia | Present, similar to cheilocystidia |
| Pileipellis | Cutis to ixocutis, with gelatinized hyphae |

Note: Detailed microscopic measurements are drawn from closely related
Colombian Psilocybe species in the same section, as P. heliconiae has not
been as thoroughly characterized as more widely distributed species.

## Distribution and Habitat

Psilocybe heliconiae is known from Colombia, where it was originally described.
- **Country:** Colombia (Antioquia region)
- **Habitat:** Found in association with tropical plants, particularly in or
  near stands of Heliconia. Grows on soil enriched with organic matter in
  montane and premontane tropical forests.
- **Elevation:** Mid-elevation Andean slopes, typical of the Colombian
  coffee-growing belt (1,000-2,000 m)
- **Seasonality:** Presumably during the rainy season when humidity is high,
  though specific seasonal data is limited.
- **Substrate:** Terricolous (growing on soil), likely in association with
  decaying plant material and root zones of Heliconia and other tropical
  understory plants.

The species was described as part of a series of Colombian Psilocybe taxa
by Guzmán and colleagues in 1994, alongside P. guatapensis and other species
from the same region. Colombia is notable for its high diversity of
Psilocybe species, with at least 12 species recorded from the country.

## Chemistry and Active Compounds

- **Psilocybin:** Present (characteristic bluing reaction confirms presence)
- **Psilocin:** Likely present, contributing to the strong bluing
- **Baeocystin:** Not specifically reported for this species
- **Norbaeocystin:** Not reported
- **Potency:** Not quantitatively analyzed in published literature

The strong bluing reaction noted in related Colombian species such as
P. caerulescens and P. weilii suggests moderate to high potency, but no
analytical studies have been published specifically for P. heliconiae.

## Ecological Role

As a saprophytic fungus, Psilocybe heliconiae contributes to nutrient cycling
in tropical forest ecosystems by decomposing organic matter in soil. Its
apparent association with Heliconia plants may indicate a specific
ecological niche, possibly involving decomposition of Heliconia litter or
root exudates. The relationship, if any, between the fungus and living
Heliconia tissue has not been investigated.

## Conservation Status

No formal conservation assessment has been made for Psilocybe heliconiae.
Its known range is restricted to Colombian Andean forests, a region that
has experienced significant deforestation for agriculture and cattle grazing.
The species may be threatened by habitat loss, though too little is known
about its distribution to make a definitive assessment.

## Historical Context

Psilocybe heliconiae was described by Gastón Guzmán and colleagues in 1994,
during a period of intensive taxonomic work on Colombian Psilocybe species.
Guzmán's 1995 monograph "The Genus Psilocybe" recognized the species as one
of 144 taxa in the genus. The 1994 paper also described P. guatapensis from
the same region, reflecting the underexplored mycoflora of the northern
Andes. Despite the species being described over 30 years ago, it remains
poorly known, with few subsequent collections reported in the literature.

## Field Identification Tips

- Look for small, bluing Psilocybes in tropical Andean forests of Colombia
- Association with Heliconia plants is a key habitat indicator
- Strong bluing reaction upon handling distinguishes it from non-psychoactive
  lookalikes in the same habitat
- Viscid cap that becomes translucent-striate when wet
- Dark purplish brown spore print
