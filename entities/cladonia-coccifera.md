## Overview

**Cladonia coccifera** (Scop.) Hook. is a fruticose lichen in the family Cladoniaceae, widely recognized by its brilliant red apothecia borne on short, grey-green podetia. Common names include the scarlet-cup lichen and the red-fruited cladonia. It is among the most conspicuous Cladonia species in montane and boreal heathlands across the Northern Hemisphere.

The species is notable for its role as an indicator of [[ph-and-nutrient-availability-garden-soils]]. In montane environments it occurs on exposed ridges and windswept plateaus above the treeline.

Elevation ranges from sea level in boreal regions to approximately 2,500 m in alpine zones of central Europe. It is absent from heavily polluted urban areas and intensively managed agricultural landscapes.

## Chemistry

*Cladonia coccifera* contains usnic acid as the major [[dighton-nitrogen-deposition-mycorrhizal-community-shifts]] and responds negatively to eutrophication. Declining populations in lowland heathlands across western Europe have been linked to increased nitrogen availability from agricultural runoff.

Grazing animals, particularly reindeer and caribou in boreal regions, occasionally consume *C. coccifera* as a minor component of their winter diet. However, it is far less palatable than *Cladonia rangiferina* and other dominant reindeer lichens.

Associations with bryophytes such as *Pleurozium schreberi* and *Dicranum scoparium* are common in boreal forest-floor communities. These mosses create humid microenvironments that support lichen hydration cycles.

## Related Species

*Cladonia coccifera* is most easily confused with *Cladonia pleurota*, which shares red apothecia but differs in having broader, more extensively squamulose podetia and containing fumarprotocetraric acid rather than thamnolic acid.

*Cladonia deformis* is another similar species with red apothecia, but it typically produces more robust, cup-forming podetia and has a different chemistry profile including homosekikaic acid.

The boreal species *Cladonia amaurocraea* produces brown apothecia rather than red ones, and its podetia are more slender and extensively branched. It occupies similar heathland habitats but tolerates slightly wetter conditions.

*Cladonia bellidiflora* produces pink apothecia and has a distinctive granular-sorediate podetial surface, separating it readily from the smooth-surfaced *C. coccifera*.

## Uses

*Cladonia coccifera* has limited direct economic value but serves as an important bioindicator species for air quality monitoring programs across northern Europe. Its sensitivity to nitrogen and sulfur dioxide makes it useful for assessing habitat integrity.

In traditional Scandinavian folk medicine, the species was occasionally used as a poultice for wounds, though this practice was not widespread. Its bright red apothecia may have contributed to its recognition in folk taxonomy.

The scarlet apothecial pigments have been investigated as potential natural dye sources for wool and textile applications. However, the small size of individual thalli limits practical harvest volume for commercial dyeing operations.

The species contributes to the aesthetic and ecological value of heathland landscapes. Conservation efforts targeting *C. coccifera* often focus on preserving open heath habitats and reducing atmospheric nitrogen deposition.

It is occasionally cultivated in lichen gardens and [[comparison-green-roof-vs-earth-sheltered]] projects designed to support acidophilic lichen communities. Its tolerance of full sun and drought makes it suitable for extensive green roof substrates.

## Conservation

Population declines have been documented across lowland western Europe, particularly in the Netherlands, northern Germany, and southern England. These declines are primarily attributed to atmospheric nitrogen deposition exceeding critical loads for oligotrophic lichen communities.
