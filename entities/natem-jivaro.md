# Natem (Jivaro)

## Description

Natem (also written natéma) is the **[[jivaro]] name** for their hallucinogenic beverage, a variant of the pan-Amazonian brew more widely known as ayahuasca. Natem is central to Jivaro shamanism and worldview, enabling practitioners to enter the supernatural world that the Jivaro consider the **only true reality**. The Jivaro of eastern Ecuador use natem to practice both bewitching and curing shamanism.

## Classification

- **Type:** Hallucinogenic beverage / decoction
- **Culture of origin:** Jivaro Indians, eastern Ecuador
- **Pronunciation:** Accent on the penultimate syllable (na-TEM)
- **Synonyms:** Ayahuasca (Quechua/Peru), yagé (Colombia), caapi (Brazil), [[nixi-pae]] (Cashinahua)

## Preparation

Natem is prepared from:
- Segments of ** *[[banisteriopsis-caapi]]*** vine (Malpighiaceae family)
- Leaves of a **similar vine**, probably another *Banisteriopsis* species
- The two are boiled together to produce a tea

## Chemistry

The brew contains a combination of powerful hallucinogenic alkaloids:
- **Harmaline** — β-carboline MAOI
- **Harmine** — β-carboline MAOI
- **d-Tetrahydroharmine** — β-carboline
- **N,N-Dimethyltryptamine (DMT)** — fast-acting tryptamine hallucinogen (probable)

The combination of β-carboline MAOIs with DMT enables oral activity of DMT, which is normally inactive when taken by mouth due to degradation by monoamine oxidase in the gut.

## Role in Jivaro Culture

- Natem makes it possible for **almost anyone** to achieve the trance state essential for shamanism.
- Approximately **1 in 4 Jivaro men** is a shaman, a proportion directly attributed to the availability and power of natem.
- The Jivaro take natem **individually** (unlike the communal Cashinahua/Sharanahua practice), preferring to use it to cope supernaturally with enemies.
- Under natem, shamans can perceive **tsentsak** (spirit helpers/darts) — the invisible forces believed to cause illness and death.
- The drink enables shamans to see the true cause of illness (e.g., a poisonous serpent sent by an enemy shaman) within a patient's body.

## Experiential Effects

[[michael-harner]]'s firsthand account describes: faint lines and forms appearing in darkness, shrill music of spirit helpers, visions of anacondas transmuting into crowns of gold, giant butterflies singing with their wings, a thousand eyes appearing on arms as demon helpers emerge, and the sound of rushing water filling the ears — a sign of possessing the power of Tsungi, the first shaman.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Natem is the Jivaro-specific preparation of the *Banisteriopsis* brew, significant for its role in producing one of the highest densities of shamans in any documented culture. The preparation demonstrates how a local psychoactive plant can shape an entire society's metaphysical framework, medical system, and power structures. The pharmacological principles of natem (β-carboline MAOIs enabling oral DMT activity) are identical to those in modern ayahuasca preparations studied in clinical settings.
