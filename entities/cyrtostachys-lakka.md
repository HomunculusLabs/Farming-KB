## Cyrtostachys lakka

## Description

Cyrtostachys lakka (also widely known as Cyrtostachys renda), commonly known as **sealing wax palm** or **lipstick palm**, is a slender, multi-stemmed palm native to peat swamp forests of peninsular Thailand, Malaysia, Sumatra, and Borneo. It is one of the most striking ornamental palms in the world, distinguished by its brilliant scarlet-red crownshaft and leaf sheaths that resemble sealing wax.

## Classification

- **Family:** Arecaceae (Palmae)
- **Genus:** Cyrtostachys
- **Species:** C. lakka (syn. C. renda)
- **Common names:** Sealing wax palm, lipstick palm, red palm, maharajah palm

## Key Facts

- Slender, multi-stemmed palm, typically 5–12 m tall
- Crownshaft and leaf sheaths are brilliant scarlet red — the most vividly coloured palm
- Native to peat swamp forests of [[community-garden-design]]:** Creates dramatic colour accent in tropical and subtropical gardens
- **Palm collection:** Highly valued by palm collectors and botanical gardens
- **Wet area planting:** Suited to planting in waterlogged and swampy areas in tropical zones
- **Container specimen:** Can be grown in large containers in warm, humid indoor environments

## Cultivation Notes

- Strictly tropical — requires temperatures above 15°C year-round
- Demands consistently moist to wet soil conditions
- Prefers partial shade when young, full sun once established
- Requires high humidity for optimal growth and colour
- Best in peaty, acidic soils
- Slow-growing, especially in suboptimal conditions
- Propagated by seed or division of clumps
- Cold-sensitive — damaged below 10°C
