# Feather Meal

## Description

**Feather meal** is a slow-release nitrogen fertilizer (N-P-K 7-12-0-0) produced as a by-product of the poultry industry. It is made from processed poultry feathers, which are composed primarily of the structural protein **keratin** — a complex, tough protein that requires extensive microbial digestion to decompose. This keratin content makes feather meal one of the slowest-release natural nitrogen fertilizers available, providing a sustained nitrogen source for 6 or more months after application.

## Classification

- **Category:** Natural organic fertilizer / Animal by-product
- **N-P-K:** 7 to 12-0-0 (varies by processing method)
- **Nutrient Availability:** Very slow release (6+ months)
- **Application Form:** Dry meal
- **Domain:** Organic gardening, poultry industry by-product utilization

## Key Facts

- Feather meal contains 7–12% nitrogen by weight, depending on processing methods.
- The nitrogen source is **keratin**, a highly complex structural protein that is resistant to rapid microbial decomposition.
- This is one of the **slowest-release natural fertilizers** available — far slower than bat guano or blood meal.
- Provides nitrogen continuously for **6 months or longer**, making it ideal for long-season crops or perennials.
- As a poultry industry by-product, it represents industrial waste stream valorization — converting feathers that would otherwise be discarded into a valuable agricultural input.

## Cautions and Considerations

- **May attract dogs, raccoons, and bears** — animal-attracting properties should be considered in areas with wildlife.
- The very slow release rate means it is not suitable for quick nitrogen corrections; use faster-acting fertilizers (blood meal, bat guano) for immediate needs.
- Best used as part of a fertilization program that includes both slow and fast nitrogen sources to provide continuous nutrition.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- Feather meal is used in some mushroom compost formulations as a slow-release nitrogen source, particularly for Agaricus bisporus (button mushroom) production where extended nutrient availability is beneficial.
- The keratin protein requires specialized **keratinolytic fungi and bacteria** for decomposition — these organisms are naturally present in healthy soil food webs.
- In soils with robust microbial populations, feather meal provides a slow, steady nitrogen release that supports sustained mycelial growth of beneficial fungi.
- The decomposition of feather meal by keratinolytic organisms demonstrates the importance of biological diversity in soil: specific microbes are needed to unlock specific nutrient sources.
