## Dactylium dendroides

## Taxonomy

Dactylium dendroides Fr. is a fungal contaminant classified within the Fungi
Imperfecti (Deuteromycota), order Moniliales, family Moniliaceae. The genus name
Dactylium comes from the Greek "daktylos," meaning finger, in reference to the
forking pattern of the conidiophore. The genus Dactylaria is considered synonymous
with Dactylium. Dactylium dendroides is recognized as the asexual (conidial) form of
Hypomyces rosellus, a teleomorphic fungus in the Hypocreales. Some species of
Hypomyces are known to attack wild mushrooms, particularly Lactarius, Russula,
Agaricus, and Amanita species. D. dendroides is commonly known by several names
including Cobweb Mold, Downy Mildew, and Soft Mildew, all of which describe its
characteristic appearance in [[duggar-mushroom-growing-history-and-methods]] rooms and casing layers.

## Role in Mushroom Cultivation

In The Mushroom Cultivator, Dactylium dendroides is identified as one of the most
troublesome casing layer contaminants in mushroom cultivation. It is listed in the
[[cannabis-growing-environment-setup]] to
  prevent further spread.
- **Lowering relative humidity** to create conditions less favorable for cobweb mold
  growth.
- **Increasing air circulation** to reduce the stagnant, moist conditions that favor
  Dactylium development.
- **Careful examination** of casing soil components for hygienic quality before use.
- **Pasteurization of casing soil**, which generally prevents the occurrence of
  Dactylium contamination.
- **Direct treatment** by covering cobweb mold patches with salt, baking soda, or any
  highly alkaline compound to halt growth.

Spores of Dactylium dendroides are killed when exposed to temperatures of 115-122
degrees F for only 30 minutes (Anderson, 1956). This relatively low thermal death
point means that proper pasteurization of [[mushroom-cultivator-casing-materials-buffering-agents]] is an effective preventive
measure.
