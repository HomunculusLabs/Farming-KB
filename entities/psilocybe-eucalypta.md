The placement of *P. eucalypta* within the section *Cyanescens* of the genus *Psilocybe* has been proposed based on its morphological similarity to *P. cyanescens* and *P. subaeruginosa*. Molecular phylogenetic studies of Australasian Psilocybe species remain limited, and definitive placement awaits DNA sequencing of authenticated collections.

## Distribution

### Australia
- **New South Wales**: Near Canberra at Tidbinbilla Nature Reserve; near Queanbeyan at Talaganda Forest Reserve; near Sydney at Mt. Wilson
- **Tasmania**: Possibly present, given the distribution pattern of related species

### New Zealand
- **North Island**: Northwest of New Plymouth

## Habitat

*Psilocybe eucalypta* grows solitary or in small groups among grassy woody debris or among mosses in shallow groves of eucalypt forests. This habitat preference — forest floor among woody debris and mosses, rather than on dung — places it in the same ecological category as [[psilocybe-subaeruginosa]], distinct from the coprophilous species introduced with cattle.

The species fruits in April (autumn in the southern hemisphere), coinciding with the onset of cooler, wetter weather that triggers fruiting in many temperate Australian fungi.

The eucalypt forest floor provides a rich substrate of decomposing leaf litter, bark fragments, and woody debris. Eucalypt leaves are slow to decompose due to their essential oils and tough cuticle, creating a persistent litter layer that supports a diverse fungal community. The mossy microhabitats where *P. eucalypta* is found likely provide consistent moisture and protection from temperature extremes.

## Description

Allen's guide provides limited morphological detail but notes the similarity to *Psilocybe cyanescens*. Key characteristics based on the comparison:

- Likely features the characteristic wavy cap margin and caramel-brown coloration of the *P. cyanescens* group
- Presumably exhibits blue bruising reaction typical of psilocybin/psilocine-containing species
- Grows in the forest floor microhabitat rather than the woodchip/urban garden habitat favored by *P. cyanescens* in the northern hemisphere

The cap is likely hygrophanous, changing color as it dries — a characteristic feature of many Psilocybe species. The gills are presumably adnate to adnexed, darkening to purplish-brown with spore maturity. The stipe would be expected to be slender, equal, and bluing when handled.
