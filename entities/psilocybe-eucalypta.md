**Psilocybe eucalypta** Guzmán & Watling is a bluing psilocybin-containing mushroom species endemic to eastern Australia. Commonly called the **eucalyptus Psilocybe**, it was first described by Guzmán and Watling and is closely associated with Eucalyptus forests. The species belongs to Psilocybe (Section Cyanescens) and is thought to be moderately active, though formal chemical analyses have not yet been published (Stamets, 1996).

## Taxonomy and Classification

*P. eucalypta* was described by Guzmán and Watling based on collections from New South Wales, Australia. It is placed within Psilocybe (Section Cyanescens), a group of primarily lignicolous bluing species that includes [[psilocybe-azurescens]], and [[psilocybe-tasmaniana]], arguing they represent a single variable species. However, Guzmán, Bandala, and King (1993) rejected this proposal, noting the difficulty of accepting that a single species could be both coprophilic and lignicolous.

Guzmán and Stamets both maintain these taxa as separate species pending further study. The species is listed as number 91 in Guzmán, Allen, and Gartz's worldwide catalog of neurotropic fungi.

## Morphology

The cap measures 1.5–3.8 cm broad, starting convex and expanding to broadly convex or nearly plane, often with a shallow central umbo. The surface is smooth to slightly translucent-striate near the margin, viscid when moist. Color is reddish brown when young, becoming ochraceous, and hygrophanous, fading to dull straw-colored in drying. The flesh is whitish and bruises bluish where injured.

The gills are adnate in attachment, dull brown at first, becoming purplish brown and eventually dark violaceous gray with whitish edges at maturity. The stem measures 65–86 mm long by 2–2.5 mm thick, covered with silky fibrils, equal to slightly swollen at the base (4–5 mm thick) with whitish rhizomorphs attached. A cortinate partial veil leaves a fugacious fibrillose annular zone in the upper stem region.

Spores are purplish brown in deposit, subellipsoid, measuring 9–18 × 5.5–6.6 μm. Basidia are 4-spored. Pleurocystidia measure 17–30 × 5.5–7.7 μm, fusoid-ventricose to mucronate to sublageniform with short necks. Cheilocystidia measure 15–25 × 4.4–6.6 μm, fusoid-ventricose to sublageniform with elongated necks.

## Distribution and Habitat

*P. eucalypta* has been reported from New South Wales in eastern Australia, where it grows solitary to gregarious on soils rich in woody debris. It is frequently found in grassy areas associated with Eucalyptus trees. Collections from Australia have been documented by Margot and Watling (1981), Guzmán (1983), and Chang and Mills (1992).

The species has also been reported from Papua New Guinea (Allen et al., 1991; Guzmán et al., 1993b), suggesting a wider distribution across Australasia. The association with Eucalyptus is notable, as several other Australian Psilocybe species share this ecological preference, including [[psilocybe-tasmaniana]].

## Chemical Constituents

*P. eucalypta* is thought to be moderately active based on its strong bluing reaction and taxonomic placement among known psychoactive species. However, no formal chemical analyses quantifying [[psilocin]], or [[psilocybe-subaeruginosa]], from which it can be distinguished by its smaller stature and different spore morphology.

The relationship between *P. eucalypta* and Eucalyptus trees may be more than incidental. The species appears adapted to the specific soil chemistry and microclimate created by Eucalyptus leaf litter decomposition, similar to how [[psilocybe-tasmaniana]] reflect the broader challenges of classifying morphologically variable fungi across wide geographic ranges.

The species was included in Allen's comprehensive survey of Australian and New Zealand psychoactive fungi, contributing to the documentation of the southern hemisphere's indigenous neurotropic mycoflora. Its recognition by Guzmán in the authoritative worldwide distribution study cemented its status as a validated psychoactive species.

## Differentiation from Related Species

Distinguishing *P. eucalypta* from closely related Australian species requires
careful microscopic examination. Key differentiating features include:

- **Versus *P. subaeruginosa***: *P. subaeruginosa* is typically larger
  (cap to 6+ cm), with a more pronounced umbo and longer stem. Its spores
  are also generally larger (11–15 × 7–9 μm). *P. subaeruginosa* fruits
  more abundantly in autumn, while *P. eucalypta* has a broader fruiting window
- **Versus *P. australiana***: *P. australiana* was described from dung-rich
  substrates and shows a coprophilic tendency, whereas *P. eucalypta* is
  strictly lignicolous. The cheilocystidia morphology also differs
- **Versus *P. tasmaniana***: *P. tasmaniana* has a more southern distribution
  centered on Tasmania and tends to fruit in cooler conditions. Its spore
  size range partially overlaps but is generally smaller on average
