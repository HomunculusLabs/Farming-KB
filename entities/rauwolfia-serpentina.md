# Rauwolfia serpentina

**Rauwolfia serpentina** (Indian snakeroot) is an evergreen perennial shrub in the family Apocynaceae, native to the Indian subcontinent and [[tryptamine]]-class phytochemistry.

## Key Details

### Botanical Description

- **Family:** Apocynaceae (dogbane family)
- **Growth habit:** Evergreen perennial shrub, 30–60 cm tall
- **Leaves:** Whorled (3–5 per node), elliptic-lanceolate, 7–17 cm, glossy dark green
- **Flowers:** Greenish-white to pinkish, cymose inflorescences; corolla tubular, 8–12 mm
- **Fruit:** Drupe, 5–8 mm, dark purple at maturity; usually two-seeded
- **Roots:** Tuberous, irregular, yellowish-brown; strongly bitter — the primary medicinal part
- **Chromosome number:** 2n = 22

### Habitat and Distribution

Native to tropical and subtropical regions of the Indian subcontinent and Southeast Asia:

- **India:** Sub-Himalayan tract from Punjab to Assam; peninsular India in deciduous forests up to ~1000 m elevation. Abundant in Bihar, Odisha, West Bengal, and the Western Ghats.
- **Bangladesh, Nepal, Sri Lanka:** Common in lowland tropical forests and foothills.
- **Southeast Asia:** Present in Myanmar, Thailand, Indonesia, and Malaysia.
- **Naturalized range:** Introduced in parts of Africa, the Caribbean, and tropical South America.

Prefers shaded or semi-shaded conditions in moist, well-drained forest soils rich in [[query-what-are-the-main-bioactive-compounds-in-medicinal-plants]]:

### Reserpine

The most significant alkaloid (0.1–0.7% of dry root weight). A pentacyclic indole alkaloid (C₃₃H₄₀N₂O₉), reserpine was the first drug to demonstrate lasting antipsychotic effects from a natural product. It irreversibly inhibits the vesicular monoamine transporter (VMAT), preventing packaging of [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]].
- **1954:** Nathan Kline demonstrated reserpine's efficacy in treating schizophrenia.
- **1955:** Reserpine became one of the first drugs widely used for outpatient hypertension management.

### Nobel Prize Connection

The isolation of reserpine contributed to the broader psychopharmacology revolution of the 1950s. While no Nobel Prize was awarded specifically for reserpine, its development alongside chlorpromazine fundamentally transformed psychiatry from institutionalization to pharmacological treatment. The work on Rauwolfia alkaloids was part of the same scientific current recognized by subsequent Nobel prizes in neurotransmitter research.

## Medical Applications

- **Hypertension:** First-line antihypertensive for over two decades at 0.05–0.25 mg/day. Commonly combined with diuretics and vasodilators. Largely replaced by newer agents but remains on the WHO Essential Medicines List for its [[jadam-ultra-low-cost-agriculture]] and proven efficacy.
- **Cardiac arrhythmia:** Ajmaline is used clinically as a class Ia antiarrhythmic, particularly valuable in Brugada syndrome diagnosis (marketed as Gilurytmal in Europe).
- **Anxiety and insomnia:** Ayurvedic preparations use the plant's sedative and anxiolytic properties, attributed to central serotonin and catecholamine depletion. Standardized extracts are available as herbal remedies in some countries.
- **Schizophrenia (historical):** Effective from 1954 until the late 1960s, abandoned due to high incidence of severe depression and suicidal ideation.

## Toxicity and Side Effects

Reserpine and crude Rauwolfia preparations carry a significant side-effect burden:

- **Depression:** Most serious adverse effect. Depletes central monoamines producing a syndrome indistinguishable from major depressive disorder. At higher doses, up to 20% develop significant depression; suicidal ideation is a recognized risk.
- **Extrapyramidal symptoms:** Parkinsonism (tremor, bradykinesia, rigidity) from central dopamine depletion. Dose-dependent and may persist after discontinuation.
- **Gastrointestinal effects:** Nausea, vomiting, abdominal cramps, diarrhea, increased gastric acid secretion. Can exacerbate peptic ulcer disease.
- **Nasal congestion:** Due to peripheral catecholamine depletion in nasal blood vessels.
- **Orthostatic hypotension:** Syncope and dizziness upon standing, related to sympathetic blockade.
- **Endocrine effects:** Gynecomastia, galactorrhea, menstrual irregularities from prolactin elevation secondary to dopamine depletion in the tuberoinfundibular pathway.
