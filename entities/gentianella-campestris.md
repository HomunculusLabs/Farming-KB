## Overview

**Gentianella campestris**, the field gentian, is a small annual or biennial herb in the family Gentianaceae. It is native to Europe, with a distribution centered on the Nordic and Baltic regions.

The plant grows 5–25 cm tall with slender, erect stems. Flowers are typically lilac-purple to deep violet, sometimes white, with four corolla lobes.

Gentianella campestris is distinguished from true gentians (Gentiana) by its four-lobed corolla and fringed scales at the corolla throat. The species was formerly placed in Gentiana but reclassified based on floral morphology.

It is a characteristic species of calcareous grasslands and alpine meadows in northern Europe. The plant is considered declining in parts of its range due to agricultural intensification.

The genus Gentianella was separated from Gentiana in the early 20th century. The distinction is based on the presence of fringed nectar scales and the four-lobed corolla.

Field gentian is one of the most widespread Gentianella species in Europe. Its range spans over 30 degrees of latitude from Iceland to the Mediterranean.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Family:** Gentianaceae
- **Genus:** Gentianella
- **Species:** G. campestris
- **Authority:** (L.) Börner (1912)

Plants grow 5–25 cm tall with one to several erect, simple or branched stems. The stem is typically reddish at the base.

Leaves are opposite, ovate to lanceolate, 0.5–2.5 cm long. Basal leaves form a rosette, while stem leaves are smaller and more pointed.

Flowers are terminal and axillary, 1.5–2.5 cm long, with four corolla lobes. The corolla is lilac-purple, violet, or rarely white.

The calyx has four lobes, two broad and two narrow, an important diagnostic character. Fringed nectar scales are present at the corolla throat.

The fruit is a capsule containing numerous small, brown, reticulate seeds. Seeds are approximately 0.5 mm long and require light for germination.

Two subspecies are recognized: subsp. campestris and subsp. baltica. They differ in calyx lobe shape and [[jeavons-climate-adaptation-growing-seasons]].

## Distribution and Habitat

Gentianella campestris is widespread across northern and central Europe. Its range extends from Iceland and the British Isles through Scandinavia to the Baltic states.

Southern populations occur in the Alps, Carpathians, and Pyrenees at higher elevations. Isolated stations exist in central Spain and the Apennines.

The species inhabits dry grasslands, calcareous pastures, dune slacks, and alpine meadows. It grows at elevations from sea level to 2,500 m in mountainous regions.

Plants prefer open, short-turf conditions on calcareous or base-rich soils. It is intolerant of shade and tall, rank vegetation.

In Scandinavia, it is a common component of species-rich meadow communities. It associates with Galium, Thymus, and Helianthemum species.

The species shows considerable morphological [[agaricus-campestris-biology-and-life-cycle]] and specific habitat requirements make it challenging to maintain.

Seed should be sown in autumn on the surface of gritty, calcareous [[compost]]. Seeds require light and a period of cold stratification.

Germination rates are typically 40–60% under optimal conditions. Seedlings are tiny and vulnerable to competition from larger plants.

Plants need full sun and excellent drainage. A neutral to alkaline pH (7.0–8.0) is essential [[query-how-to-manage-ph-for-healthy-cannabis-plants]] growth.

The species self-seeds readily in suitable conditions. It can be naturalized in rock gardens or calcareous meadow plantings.

## Ecological Role

The flowers are visited by small bees, flies, and butterflies. The open corolla allows access to a wider range of pollinators than the tubular flowers of Gentiana.

Unlike many gentians, the flowers remain open on overcast days. This increases foraging opportunities for pollinators in variable weather.

The species is an indicator of species-rich calcareous grassland. Its presence suggests low [[arid-soil-fertility-and-mineral-management]] appropriate grazing management.

Gentianella campestris is a hemiparasitic plant that forms connections with neighboring plants through root haustoria. This partial parasitism supplements its [[aact-microbial-chelation-of-minerals-ingham]] and [[brix-testing-foliar-nutrient-uptake]].
