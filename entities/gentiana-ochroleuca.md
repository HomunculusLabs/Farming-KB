## Overview

Gentiana ochroleuca, commonly called the cream gentian or yellowish gentian, is a perennial herb native to central and [[cannabis-seed-germination]]. Germination requires cold stratification and occurs on bare soil in spring.

The species is a component of the late-season prairie forb community. It flowers alongside goldenrods, asters, and blue lettuce, providing sequential nectar resources.

Herbivory is minimal due to the bitter secoiridoid glycosides in foliage. Grasshoppers occasionally feed on petals but rarely cause significant damage.

## Cultivation

Cream gentian requires moist, well-drained soil in full sun to light shade. It is adaptable to a range of soil types but performs best on neutral to alkaline loams.

Seeds need 60–90 days of cold-moist stratification for germination. Fall sowing in prepared seedbeds yields the most reliable results.

Seedlings grow slowly, typically not flowering until the third year. Transplant bare-root plants in early spring before growth resumes.
