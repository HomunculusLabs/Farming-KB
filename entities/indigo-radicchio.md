# Indigo Radicchio

## Description

'Indigo' is a radicchio variety (chicory, Cichorium intybus) grown on Eliot Coleman's farm as part of the diverse selection of cold-hardy winter vegetables. Radicchio is a bitter leafy vegetable in the chicory family that performs exceptionally well in unheated winter greenhouse environments, developing deeper color and improved flavor in cool conditions.

## Classification

- **Category**: Crop variety
- **Domain**: Winter vegetable production, greenhouse growing
- **Species**: Cichorium intybus (chicory family, Asteraceae)
- **Type**: Radicchio (heading chicory)

## Key Facts

- Listed among the cold-hardy, colorful winter crops grown on Coleman's Four Season Farm in Maine
- Radicchio varieties are notably cold-tolerant and can survive hard frosts with minimal protection
- Cool temperatures (below 50F/10C) enhance the deep red/purple coloration that gives 'Indigo' its name
- Bitter compounds (intybin and lactucin) become milder in cold weather, improving palatability
- Marketed as part of winter salad mixes alongside other cold-hardy greens
- Commands premium prices in winter markets when fresh salad greens are scarce

## Relevance to Cultivation

Radicchio 'Indigo' exemplifies the winter harvest strategy: selecting crop varieties that not only survive but actually improve in quality during the cold season when conventional vegetable production is impossible. For greenhouse and hoophouse growers, radicchio is an ideal winter crop because it requires minimal heat input, the cool conditions improve its eating quality, and it provides visual appeal and nutritional diversity to winter salad offerings. The deep pigmentation indicates high levels of anthocyanins and antioxidants.

## Growing Notes

- Sow in late summer for winter harvest
- Performs well in unheated greenhouse or with row cover protection
- Color development is temperature-dependent -- best color below 50F
- Harvest as headed radicchio or as individual leaves for salad mix
- Stores well after harvest, maintaining crispness in cold storage

## Source Context

Mentioned in Eliot Coleman's "The Winter Harvest Handbook" as part of the farm's cold-hardy vegetable selection.
