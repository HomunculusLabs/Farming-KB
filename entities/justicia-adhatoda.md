# Justicia adhatoda

## Overview

*Justicia adhatoda* (syn. *Adhatoda vasica*), commonly known as vasaka or Malabar
nut, is an evergreen shrub in the family Acanthaceae, widely distributed across
tropical and subtropical Asia. It is one of the most important medicinal plants
in Ayurvedic, Unani, and Siddha medicine, with over 3,000 years of documented
respiratory therapeutic use. The leaves contain the quinazoline alkaloids vasicine
and vasicinone, providing bronchodilatory, expectorant, antitussive, and
antimicrobial activities. The plant is also valued in permaculture for erosion
control, hedge establishment, and as a botanical insecticide.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae → **Division:** Angiospermae → **Class:** Eudicotyledones
- **Order:** Lamiales → **Family:** Acanthaceae
- **Genus:** *Justicia* L. → **Species:** *J. adhatoda* L. (1753)
- **Synonyms:** *Adhatoda vasica* Nees (1832); *Adhatoda zeylanica* Medic.
- **Etymology:** *Justicia* honors Scottish botanist James Justice (1698–1763).
  *Adhatoda* derives from Tamil *aduthodai* (destroyer of diseases). Sanskrit
  *vasaka* means "that which removes disease."
- **Common names:** Vasaka, Malabar nut, adulsa, arusa (Hindi), basanga (Bengali)

## Morphology

### Growth Habit

- Evergreen, much-branched shrub, 1.5–3.0 m tall
- Dense, bushy crown with multiple stems from the base
- Slow to moderate growth rate; deep taproot
- Lifespan: 15–25 years under cultivation

### Vegetative Structures

- **Leaves:** Opposite, decussate, broadly lanceolate to ovate, 10–20 ×
  4–8 cm; dark green above, paler below; leathery texture; margin entire
  or slightly crenate; petiole 1–2 cm, pubescent
- **Stems:** Erect, cylindrical, 1–3 cm diameter; bark pale gray-brown,
  rough and fissured with age; young branches quadrangular, pubescent
- **Roots:** Deep taproot with extensive lateral root development

### Reproductive Structures

- **Inflorescence:** Dense terminal or axillary spikes, 5–15 cm long
- **Flowers:** Bisexual, zygomorphic, white with purple/violet streaks
  on lower lip; calyx 5-lobed; corolla bilabiate, 1.5–2.5 cm;
  autumn blooming (October–November)
- **Fruit:** Capsule, oblong-clavate, 2–3 cm, 4-valved, tapering to long beak
- **Seeds:** 8–16 per capsule, suborbicular, 3–4 mm, brown, mucilaginous coat

## Distribution and Habitat

- **Native range:** Indian subcontinent, Myanmar, Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia
- **Introduced:** Tropical Africa, Caribbean, parts of South America
- **Climate:** Tropical/subtropical; 10–40°C; frost sensitive but tolerates brief
  exposure; altitude 0–1,400 m
- **Rainfall:** 500–2,500 mm annually; drought-tolerant once established
- **Soil:** Wide range, sandy loams to heavy clays; pH 5.5–8.0; tolerates
  saline/alkaline conditions
- **Habitat:** Roadsides, waste places, forest edges, scrubland; common along
  watercourses and disturbed areas

## Phytochemistry

### Major Alkaloids

- **Vasicine (peganine):** 0.5–1.5% dried leaves; primary bronchodilator
  and expectorant; quinazoline alkaloid
- **Vasicinone:** 0.2–0.5%; oxidation product of vasicine with retained
  pharmacological activity
- **Deoxyvasicine:** Trace amounts; structurally related

### Other Constituents

- **Flavonoids:** Apigenin, luteolin, kaempferol glycosides (antioxidant,
  anti-inflammatory)
- **Essential oil:** 0.1–0.3%; β-caryophyllene, linalool, α-pinene
- **Tannins:** 2–4% (astringent properties)
- **Vitamin C:** 30–50 mg/100g fresh leaf weight

## Medicinal Uses

### Respiratory System

- **Asthma:** Leaf preparations act as bronchodilator; vasicine relaxes
  bronchial smooth muscle comparable to theophylline
- **Bronchitis:** Expectorant properties reduce sputum viscosity; widely used
  in Ayurvedic formulations for chronic bronchitis
- **Cough:** Antitussive efficacy comparable to bromhexine and codeine in
  clinical trials for productive and dry cough
- **Tuberculosis:** Adjunctive use; vasicine shows in vitro activity against
  *M. tuberculosis*

### Other Uses

- **Hemorrhagic disorders:** Astringent/hemostatic for menorrhagia, epistaxis
- **Fever/malaria:** Leaf decoction used as antipyretic; mild antimalarial
  activity in vitro
- **Topical:** Leaf paste for wounds, skin infections, inflammatory conditions
- **Uterine disorders:** ⚠️ Uterine stimulant — contraindicated in pregnancy

## Cultivation

- **Propagation:** Seeds (germination 15–25 days) or hardwood cuttings
  (60–80% rooting under mist)
- **Spacing:** 60 × 60 cm for hedges; 1.5 × 1.5 m for open field
- **Irrigation:** Essential at establishment; drought-tolerant thereafter
- **Fertilizer:** 10–15 t/ha FYM at planting; NPK (20:40:20) kg/ha annually
- **Harvest:** Leaves harvested 2–3 times/year starting 12 months; air-dried
  in shade to preserve alkaloid content
- **Yield:** 2,000–3,000 kg dried leaves/ha/year

## Permaculture Applications

- **Living fence:** Dense, thorny branches form effective livestock barrier
- **Erosion control:** Deep root system stabilizes slopes and embankments
- **Botanical insecticide:** Leaf extracts toxic to aphids, whiteflies,
  caterpillars; used as natural pesticide in organic systems
- **Windbreak:** Low-height windbreak for vegetable gardens in tropics
- **Bee forage:** Autumn flowers attract pollinators
