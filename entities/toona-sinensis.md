# Toona sinensis

Toona sinensis, commonly known as Chinese mahogany, Chinese toon, or red toon, is a
deciduous tree species in the family Meliaceae. Native to China and widely cultivated
apermaculturend Southeast Asia, it is valued both as a timber tree and, uniquely among
mahoganies, for its edible young leaves and shoots, which are a prized ingredient in
Chinese cuisine. The species is increasingly recognized in permaculture for its dual
production of high-quality timber and nutritious spring greens.

## Taxonomy and Nomenclature

The genus Toona is closely related to Cedrela (Spanish cedar), and the two were
historically conflated. Toona sinensis was previously classified as Cedrela sinensis. The
family Meliaceae also includes the important tropical timber genus Swietenia (true
mahogany). The species epithet sinensis denotes its Chinese origin. Common names include
Chinese mahogany (referring to the timber quality), Chinese toon (from the pronunciation
of the Chinese name xiangchun), and red toon (referring to the reddish spring foliage). In
Chinese, the tree is called xiangchun, meaning fragrant spring, reflecting the aromatic
quality of the young shoots that emerge at that season.

## Botany and Identification

Toona sinensis is a medium to large deciduous tree, reaching 15 to 25 meters in height
with a straight, cylindrical trunk that can attain 60 to 80 cm in diameter. The bark is
dark brown to grayish-brown, fissured into longitudinal strips on mature trees, with
occasional plate-like scaling. Young branches are reddish-brown and initially pubescent,
becoming glabrous with age. The tree produces a characteristic strong, aromatic odor when
leaves or bark are crushed.

The leaves are pinnately compound, 30 to 60 cm long, with 10 to 20 pairs of leaflets.
Leaflets are lanceolate to ovate-lanceolate, 6 to 15 cm long and 2 to 5 cm wide, with
acuminate tips and serrate margins. The base of each leaflet is asymmetric (oblique), a
useful identification feature. New growth in spring emerges with a distinctive reddish-
purple to burgundy coloration, which gradually turns green as the leaves mature. This
reddish flush is the primary harvest period for edible use.

The flowers are pentamerous, white to pale green, approximately 5 mm across, borne in
large terminal panicles 30 to 50 cm long. Each flower has five white petals, five sepals,
and a superior ovary. Flowering occurs in May to June, with the emerging panicles
appearing simultaneously with or slightly before the new foliage. The inflorescences are
conspicuous and slightly fragrant. The fruit is a cylindrical woody capsule, 2 to 3.5 cm
long and 4 to 5 mm in diameter, containing numerous winged seeds. Capsules ripen from
September to October, turning from green to brown, and dehisce along five valves to
release the flat, papery seeds which are wind-dispersed. Each capsule contains 50 to 100
seeds.

## Distribution and Habitat

Toona sinensis is native to a large region of eastern Asia, spanning from northeastern
China (Heilongjiang, Jilin, Liaoning) through central and southern China to Korea, Japan,
and northern Southeast Asia. It grows naturally in mixed deciduous forests on mountain
slopes, valley bottoms, and along watercourses at elevations from near sea level to
approximately 2,000 meters. The species has been widely planted as a timber and vegetable
tree throughout its native range and has been introduced to other temperate and
subtropical regions for cultivation.

## Edible Shoots and Leaves

The young, emerging leaves and shoots of Toona sinensis are a traditional spring
delicacy in Chinese cuisine, known as xiangchun. The tender reddish-purple new growth is
harvested in early spring before the leaves fully expand and toughen. The optimal harvest
window is typically 2 to 4 weeks in duration. The flavor is complex and distinctive,
described as a combination of onion, garlic, and shallot with an aromatic, slightly
peppery quality. This characteristic aroma is attributed to sulfur-containing volatile
compounds, primarily various sulfides, which are also responsible for the similar flavor
profile of Allium species.

Culinary applications include stir-frying with eggs (a classic Chinese dish, xiangchun
chao jidan), braising with tofu, incorporating into dumplings and omelets, pickling in
salt or soy sauce, and using as a flavoring garnish for soups and rice dishes. The shoots
are rich in vitamins A, C, and E, and contain significant amounts of protein
(approximately 6 to 8 grams per 100 grams fresh weight), calcium, iron, and phosphorus for
a leafy green. Research has identified numerous bioactive compounds including quercetin,
kaempferol, rutin, and various phenolic acids with demonstrated antioxidant, anti-
inflammatory, and antimicrobial properties. The total phenolic content of young shoots is
significantly higher than that of many common vegetables.

In traditional Chinese medicine, Toona sinensis leaves are used to stimulate appetite,
promote digestion, clear heat, and treat infections. The bark has been used as an
astringent, antidiarrheal, and detoxifying agent. Modern pharmacological studies have
confirmed antibacterial activity against several pathogenic bacteria, anti-inflammatory
effects, and potential antitumor properties of extracts from various plant parts.

## Timber Properties

The wood of Toona sinensis is classified as a true mahogany, closely related to and
sometimes confused with Toona ciliata (Australian red cedar). The heartwood is reddish-
brown to deep mahogany-red, often with a distinctive aromatic scent when freshly cut that
fades with seasoning. The grain is straight to interlocked, with a medium to coarse
texture and a natural luster.

Timber properties include good workability with both hand and machine tools, moderate
density (approximately 480 to 560 kg per cubic meter at 12 percent moisture content), good
dimensional stability, and natural resistance to decay and insect attack due to the
presence of repellent secondary compounds. The wood seasons well with minimal warping when
properly stacked and stickered. It takes glue, nails, screws, and finishes satisfactorily.
The timber is commercially important in China and is considered one of the finest native
hardwoods, used for high-quality furniture, cabinetry, interior paneling, musical
instruments, carving, boat building, and decorative veneer.

## Cultivation and Growth

Toona sinensis is adaptable to a wide range of climatic conditions, growing well in
temperate, subtropical, and tropical montane environments. It is hardy to approximately
-25 degrees Celsius when dormant, making it suitable for cultivation in USDA hardiness
zones 5 to 10. The tree prefers deep, fertile, well-drained loamy soils but tolerates
clay, sandy, and slightly acidic to alkaline soils (pH 5.5 to 8.0). It does not tolerate
waterlogging or saline conditions.

Growth rates are moderate to fast under favorable conditions, with trees reaching 8 to
12 meters in 10 years. Full sun is preferred, though the tree tolerates partial shade,
especially when young. Water requirements are moderate, and established trees show
reasonable drought tolerance. The tree is relatively wind-resistant due to its flexible
wood and deep root system. For seed saving of woody perennials, see [[permaculture-seed-saving]].

## Propagation

Propagation is achieved through seed, root cuttings, or [[tissue-culture-cloning]]. Seed
germination is relatively straightforward, though viability declines rapidly after 6 to 12
months, and fresh seed is preferred. Seeds should be sown in spring in well-drained seed
beds, lightly covered, and kept consistently moist. Germination typically occurs within 2
to 4 weeks with rates of 60 to 80 percent for fresh seed. Root suckers are readily
produced and can be separated for vegetative propagation, which maintains the desirable
characteristics of selected cultivars, particularly those bred for enhanced shoot
production, leaf color, or growth form. Grafted specimens of superior shoot-producing
cultivars are widely available in Chinese nurseries, with several named cultivars selected
for heavy, early, or particularly flavorful shoot production.
