## Nigella sativa

**Black seed** (also known as **kalonji**, **black cumin**, **fennel flower**, or **Roman coriander**) is an annual flowering plant in the family Ranunculaceae, native to southern Europe, North Africa, and southwest Asia. The small black seeds of N. sativa have been used as both a culinary spice and medicinal remedy for over 3,000 years, with a particularly [[cuminum-cyminum]], Apiaceae) or to Bunium bulbocastanum (also called black cumin). The seeds are sometimes called "onion seed" in South Asian cuisines, though the plant is not related to onions.

## Botanical Description

Nigella sativa grows 20–30 cm tall as an erect, branching annual herb. The stem is finely grooved and glaucous (bluish-green) in color.

**Leaves** are finely divided, 2–3 times pinnatisect, with linear segments 1–3 mm wide, giving a feathery, fennel-like appearance.

**Flowers** are solitary, terminal, pale blue to white, 2–3 cm diameter, with 5–7 petal-like sepals (true petals are reduced to small nectariferous structures). Blooming occurs from June to August.

**Fruit** is a large, inflated capsule (2–3.5 cm long) composed of 5–7 fused carpels, each containing numerous small trigonous seeds. The capsule turns brown and splits open when ripe.

**Seeds** are small (2–3 mm), matte black, triangular in cross-section, with a rough surface. They have a characteristic pungent, peppery-oregano aroma and a slightly bitter, warming flavor.

## Distribution and Habitat

Native to the Mediterranean basin, extending east through the Middle East to northern India. Cultivated widely in Egypt, India, Pakistan, Iran, Turkey, and Syria. Naturalized in parts of Europe and Central Asia. Grows in well-drained, sandy or loamy soils in full sun. Drought-tolerant once established. Hardy to about -5°C.

## Cultivation

- **Soil:** Well-drained, moderately fertile soil. Tolerates poor, calcareous soils. Avoid waterlogging.
- **Light:** Full sun required.
- **Propagation:** Direct sowing in spring after last frost. Seeds germinate in 7–14 days at 15–20°C. Do not transplant well — sow in situ.
- **Spacing:** 15–25 cm between plants.
- **Water:** Low to moderate. Drought-tolerant; reduce watering as plants mature.
- **Harvest:** Capsules are ready 100–120 days after sowing. Harvest when capsules turn brown and begin to split. Cut whole stems and hang upside down to dry. Thresh to collect seeds.
- **Yield:** 500–1,500 kg seed per hectare.

### Fixed Oil Composition

The cold-pressed fixed oil is rich in:

- **Linoleic acid** (C18:2 ω-6) — ~55%
- **Oleic acid** (C18:1 ω-9) — ~23%
- **Palmitic acid** (C16:0) — ~12%
- **Stearic acid** (C18:0) — ~3%
- **Eicosadienoic acid** — trace amounts

### Other Constituents

- **Saponins:** α-hederin and melanthin
- **Alkaloids:** Nigellidine, nigellicine, nigellicimine
- **Proteins:** 20–21% seed protein with a balanced [[cho-fish-amino-acid-preparation]] profile
- **Minerals:** Iron, calcium, potassium, zinc, magnesium, selenium
- **Vitamins:** Vitamin E (tocopherols), vitamin A (β-carotene)
