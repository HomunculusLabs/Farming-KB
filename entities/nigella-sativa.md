# Nigella sativa

**Black seed** (also known as **kalonji**, **black cumin**, **fennel flower**, or **Roman coriander**) is an annual flowering plant in the family Ranunculaceae, native to southern Europe, North Africa, and southwest Asia. The small black seeds of N. sativa have been used as both a culinary spice and medicinal remedy for over 3,000 years, with a particularly rich history in Islamic traditional medicine (Tibb al-Nabawi) and Ayurveda.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Order:** Ranunculales
- **Family:** Ranunculaceae
- **Genus:** Nigella
- **Species:** N. sativa

**Note on nomenclature:** The common name "black cumin" is a source of persistent confusion. Nigella sativa is unrelated to true cumin (Cuminum cyminum, Apiaceae) or to Bunium bulbocastanum (also called black cumin). The seeds are sometimes called "onion seed" in South Asian cuisines, though the plant is not related to onions.

## Botanical Description

Nigella sativa grows 20–30 cm tall as an erect, branching annual herb. The stem is finely grooved and glaucous (bluish-green) in color.

**Leaves** are finely divided, 2–3 times pinnatisect, with linear segments 1–3 mm wide, giving a feathery, fennel-like appearance.

**Flowers** are solitary, terminal, pale blue to white, 2–3 cm diameter, with 5–7 petal-like sepals (true petals are reduced to small nectariferous structures). Blooming occurs from June to August.

**Fruit** is a large, inflated capsule (2–3.5 cm long) composed of 5–7 fused carpels, each containing numerous small trigonous seeds. The capsule turns brown and splits open when ripe.

**Seeds** are small (2–3 mm), matte black, triangular in cross-section, with a rough surface. They have a characteristic pungent, peppery-oregano aroma and a slightly bitter, warming flavor.

## Distribution and Habitat

Native to the Mediterranean basin, extending east through the Middle East to northern India. Cultivated widely in Egypt, India, Pakistan, Iran, Turkey, and Syria. Naturalized in parts of Europe and Central Asia. Grows in well-drained, sandy or loamy soils in full sun. Drought-tolerant once established. Hardy to about -5°C.

## Cultivation

- **Soil:** Well-drained, moderately fertile soil. Tolerates poor, calcareous soils. Avoid waterlogging.
- **Light:** Full sun required.
- **Propagation:** Direct sowing in spring after last frost. Seeds germinate in 7–14 days at 15–20°C. Do not transplant well — sow in situ.
- **Spacing:** 15–25 cm between plants.
- **Water:** Low to moderate. Drought-tolerant; reduce watering as plants mature.
- **Harvest:** Capsules are ready 100–120 days after sowing. Harvest when capsules turn brown and begin to split. Cut whole stems and hang upside down to dry. Thresh to collect seeds.
- **Yield:** 500–1,500 kg seed per hectare.

## Phytochemistry

### Thymoquinone and Related Compounds

The primary bioactive constituent of N. sativa seed [[permaculture-designers-manual-essential-oil-plants]] is **thymoquinone** (2-isopropyl-5-methyl-1,4-benzoquinone), comprising 30–48% of the volatile oil fraction. Related quinones include:

- **Thymohydroquinone** (dihydrothymoquinone) — antioxidant
- **Dithymoquinone** (nigellone) — antispasmodic, originally isolated as a bronchodilator
- **p-Cymene** — major hydrocarbon in the essential oil
- **Carvacrol** — antimicrobial phenolic monoterpene
- **4-Terpineol** — antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory
- **Longifolene** — sesquiterpene

### Fixed Oil Composition

The cold-pressed fixed oil is rich in:

- **Linoleic acid** (C18:2 ω-6) — ~55%
- **Oleic acid** (C18:1 ω-9) — ~23%
- **Palmitic acid** (C16:0) — ~12%
- **Stearic acid** (C18:0) — ~3%
- **Eicosadienoic acid** — trace amounts

### Other Constituents

- **Saponins:** α-hederin and melanthin
- **Alkaloids:** Nigellidine, nigellicine, nigellicimine
- **Proteins:** 20–21% seed protein with a balanced [[cho-fish-amino-acid-preparation]] profile
- **Minerals:** Iron, calcium, potassium, zinc, magnesium, selenium
- **Vitamins:** Vitamin E (tocopherols), vitamin A (β-carotene)

## Pharmacological Properties

### Anti-inflammatory

Thymoquinone suppresses NF-κB and MAPK signaling pathways, reducing pro-inflammatory cytokine production (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6). These effects have been demonstrated in numerous animal models of inflammatory disease, including arthritis, colitis, and encephalomyelitis.

### Antioxidant

Thymoquinone and thymohydroquinone are potent free radical scavengers that increase endogenous antioxidant enzyme activity (SOD, catalase, glutathione peroxidase) while reducing lipid peroxidation. The combined essential oil and fixed oil fractions provide both lipophilic and hydrophilic antioxidant protection.

### Immunomodulatory

N. sativa exhibits bidirectional immunomodulation: it enhances cell-mediated immunity (increasing CD4+ and CD8+ T-cell activity, NK cell cytotoxicity) while suppressing humoral hyperactivity (reducing IgE and histamine release). This dual action underlies its traditional use for allergic conditions.

### Antidiabetic

[[mushroom-cancer-clinical-trials-overview]] and animal studies demonstrate significant blood glucose reduction, improved insulin sensitivity, and protection of pancreatic β-cells. Thymoquinone activates AMPK and upregulates PPAR-γ expression.

### Antimicrobial

Broad-spectrum activity against bacteria (including MRSA), fungi ([[candida-albicans]]), and parasites (Schistosoma mansoni). Carvacrol and 4-terpineol contribute significantly to antimicrobial effects.

### Respiratory

Nigellone (dithymoquinone), isolated in 1959, was one of the first constituents studied for bronchodilatory activity. Modern clinical trials support the use of N. sativa oil for asthma, allergic rhinitis, and COPD.

### Hepatoprotective

Thymoquinone protects against toxin-induced liver injury (CCl₄, acetaminophen, thioacetamide) by reducing [[blesching-cannabis-oxidative-stress-and-antioxidant-defense]] and inhibiting inflammatory cascades. Clinical evidence supports use in non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD).

## Traditional and Historical Uses

- **Islamic medicine (Prophetic medicine):** "Use the black seed, for indeed it is a cure for all diseases except death" (Sahih Bukhari 5688). Used for headaches, toothaches, nasal congestion, intestinal worms, and [[cannabis-for-skin-conditions-detailed]].
- **Ayurveda:** Known as *krishna jiraka*; used as a lactation stimulant, digestive, and treatment for piles and renal calculi.
- **Unani medicine (Tibb):** Classified as hot and dry in temperament (*har mizaj*). Used as a diuretic, carminative, and emmenagogue.
- **Middle Eastern cuisine:** Kalonji sprinkled on naan bread, flatbreads, pickles, and savory pastries.
- **South Asian cuisine:** Key ingredient in panch phoron (Bengali five-spice blend), added to curries, dals, and pickles.

## Clinical Evidence

- **Asthma:** A 2017 meta-analysis of 5 RCTs found N. sativa significantly improved asthma control, FEV1, and peak expiratory flow.
- **Diabetes:** Multiple RCTs demonstrate fasting blood glucose and HbA1c reduction with 1–3 g/day seed powder.
- **Hypertension:** 2016 meta-analysis of 11 RCTs found significant reduction in both systolic and diastolic blood pressure.
- **Obesity:** Clinical evidence supports modest weight loss and reduction in waist circumference with daily supplementation.
- **Dyslipidemia:** Consistent reductions in triglycerides, LDL-C, and total cholesterol across multiple trials.

## Cautions

- **Pregnancy:** Traditional emmenagogue and abortifacient; contraindicated during pregnancy due to uterine stimulant activity.
- **Anticoagulant interaction:** May potentiate warfarin and other anticoagulants.
- **Hypotension:** Additive blood pressure-lowering effects with antihypertensive medications.
