# Dave Jacke

## Overview

Dave Jacke is an American permaculture designer, educator, and author
best known as the primary author of *Edible Forest Gardens* (2005), the
definitive two-volume reference on temperate-climate food forest design.

His work bridges the gap between ecological theory and practical
agroforestry, providing the most comprehensive framework available for
designing polycultural food-producing ecosystems in temperate regions.

Jacke's approach is notable for its rigorous grounding in community
ecology, succession dynamics, and landscape-level pattern understanding,
distinguishing his work from more casual permaculture design manuals.

## Biography

Jacke holds a bachelor's degree in environmental studies and a master's
degree in landscape design from the Conway School of Landscape Design in
Massachusetts. His academic background combines ecology, design, and
land stewardship.

He has lived and worked primarily in the northeastern United States,
with particular focus on the New England bioregion. His practical
experience includes designing and installing food forests, teaching
permaculture design courses, and consulting on ecological land
management projects.

Before focusing on edible forest gardens, Jacke worked in landscape
design, environmental education, and natural areas management. These
experiences informed his systems-level approach to food forest design.

## Major Contributions

### Edible Forest Gardens (2005)

The two-volume *Edible Forest Gardens*, co-authored with Eric Toensmeier,
is Jacke's seminal contribution. Published by Chelsea Green, the work
remains the standard reference for temperate food forest design.

**Volume 1: Ecological Vision and Theory** establishes the scientific
foundation. It covers community ecology principles, forest architecture,
plant succession, polyculture design theory, soil ecology, and the
ecological patterns underlying productive forest gardens. This volume is
notable for its depth — it applies peer-reviewed ecological science to
agricultural design rather than relying solely on permaculture lore.

**Volume 2: Ecological Design and Practice** provides the applied
framework. It includes a comprehensive plant species matrix (over 600
species with ecological functions), guild design methodology, site
assessment protocols, implementation guidelines, and maintenance
strategies for maturing food forests.

Together, the volumes represent approximately 1,000 pages of dense,
carefully referenced material that has educated a generation of
permaculture designers and food forest practitioners.

## Design Principles

Jacke's approach to forest garden design is distinguished by several
key principles that differentiate it from broader permaculture practice:

**Ecological fidelity:** Designs should mimic the structure and function
of natural forest communities. This includes vertical canopy layering,
successional pathways, nitrogen and nutrient cycling, and mutualistic
species interactions.

**Guild-based design:** Species are assembled into functional guilds
based on their ecological roles (nitrogen fixers, dynamic accumulators,
insectary plants, canopy species, ground covers) rather than solely on
crop value.

**Site-specific analysis:** Rigorous observation and documentation of
site conditions — soils, microclimates, existing vegetation, water
flows — precede any design decisions. Jacke emphasizes that generic
designs fail without site-specific calibration.

**Succession awareness:** Forest gardens are understood as successional
systems. Designers must plan for how polycultures will evolve over
decades, including the eventual canopy closure and understory shifts
that accompany maturing forest ecosystems.

**Pattern literacy:** Designs should replicate ecological patterns at
multiple scales — from individual plant root zones to landscape-level
water and nutrient flows.

## Influence and Legacy

Jacke's *Edible Forest Gardens* has become required reading in
permaculture design certificate (PDC) courses worldwide and is cited as
a foundational text by food forest practitioners across temperate
regions.

His work influenced the resurgence of interest in food forestry in the
2010s and 2020s, contributing to the establishment of hundreds of
public and private food forest projects in North America and Europe.

The plant species matrix in Volume 2 remains one of the most comprehensive
temperate food-forest plant databases available, bridging ecological
function data with horticultural practicality.

Jacke's emphasis on ecological rigor has helped elevate permaculture
design discourse beyond intuition-based practice toward evidence-based
agroecology, establishing standards for design documentation and
long-term monitoring.
