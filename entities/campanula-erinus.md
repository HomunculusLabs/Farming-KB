# Campanula erinus

## Overview

Campanula erinus is a small annual bellflower native to the Mediterranean basin and western Asia. It is one of the few annual species in a genus dominated by perennials, completing its entire lifecycle within a single [[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]] before the onset of summer drought. This adaptation allows it to exploit brief windows of moisture in arid Mediterranean habitats.

The species has naturalized in parts [[query-how-do-green-roofs-and-living-walls-benefit-urban-environments]], it is a characteristic component of Mediterranean wall flora, growing alongside Cymbalaria muralis, Parietaria judaica, and various fern species. These micro-habitats provide the sharp drainage and [[hamilton-rock-garden-design]] designs.

The species has been featured in Mediterranean wildflower seed mixes intended for dry garden borders and [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]], flowering is delayed until April or May.

Plants are hardy to approximately USDA Zone 7 and will not survive prolonged freezing. In colder climates, the species can be grown as a winter annual in an [[comparison-honey-bees-vs-native-solitary-bees]] (Hylaeus, Lasioglossum) and hoverflies (Syrphidae), which are attracted to the accessible nectar. The open corolla shape allows a broad range of small pollinators to access the rewards.

Autogamy (self-pollination) has been documented in isolated populations where pollinator visits are infrequent. This reproductive assurance strategy ensures seed set even in unfavorable conditions.

As a winter annual, Campanula erinus contributes to early-season nectar availability before most perennials have begun flowering. This makes it ecologically significant in Mediterranean plant communities where winter-active insects depend on scarce floral resources.
