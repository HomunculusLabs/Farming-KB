## Psilocybe ovoideocystidiata

## Taxonomy

- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Hymenogastraceae (formerly Strophariaceae)
- **Genus:** Psilocybe
- **Species:** P. ovoideocystidiata
- **Authority:** Guzmán (1979)
- **Synonyms:** None widely accepted

## Description

### Cap (Pileus)
- 1.5 to 5 cm in diameter
- Convex to broadly convex, becoming plane with age
- Surface smooth, hygrophanous, often slightly viscid when moist
- Coloration ranges from chestnut brown to ochraceous brown when hydrated, fading to pale straw or whitish as it dries
- Often develops greenish or bluish bruising with age or handling
- Margin sometimes adorned with remnants of the partial veil

### Gills (Lamellae)
- Adnate to adnexed, becoming subdecurrent with maturity
- Close to crowded
- Color initially grayish, progressing to purplish brown to dark purple brown as spores mature
- Edges often whitish and fringed

### Stipe (Stem)
- 3 to 8 cm long, 2 to 5 mm thick
- Equal to slightly enlarged at the base
- Hollow, fibrous
- Surface whitish to pale yellowish, bruising bluish when handled
- Partial veil leaving a persistent annular zone that is sometimes prominent

### Spores
- Spore print dark purple brown to nearly black
- Spores ellipsoid to rhomboid in face view, slightly thick walled
- 11 to 15 × 6.5 to 8 µm
- Cheilocystidia distinctive: ovate to flask shaped with a long neck, giving the species its name

- Active at typical [[psilocybe-caerulipes]] but differs in seasonality, geographic range, and microscopic features
- The persistent annular zone on the stipe is more prominent than in many other Psilocybe species
- The chestnut brown cap that fades to pale is characteristic when fresh

## Cultivation

- Can be cultivated on standard lignicolous substrates including hardwood sawdust and wood chip based preparations
- Prefers hardwood supplemented substrates over grain only approaches
- Fruitbody formation is triggered by temperature drops and high humidity, mimicking its natural spring fruiting conditions
- Similar cultivation parameters to [[psilocybe-cyanescens]] — more robust, wavy cap margin, different cystidia, and typically autumn fruiting
- [[raw-cannabis-thca-non-psychoactive-therapeutic-properties]] Galerina and Stropharia species share similar habitat and should be carefully distinguished
