# Tetrahydroharmine

## Overview

**Tetrahydroharmine** (THH) is a β-carboline alkaloid and one of the primary
active constituents of *Banisteriopsis caapi*, the principal admixture plant
in the Amazonian psychoactive brew ayahuasca.

It is a fully hydrogenated derivative of harmine, with the pyridine ring
reduced to a tetrahydropyridine. This structural difference confers distinct
pharmacological properties compared to harmine and harmaline.

THH is typically the most abundant β-carboline in *B. caapi*, often present
at concentrations 2–10 times higher than harmine or harmaline depending on
the variety and preparation method.

Its primary pharmacological actions include weak monoamine oxidase inhibition
(selective for MAO-A) and serotonin reuptake inhibition, making it a unique
contributor to the overall psychoactive profile of ayahuasca.

## Chemical Properties

- **IUPAC Name:** 1-Methyl-2,3,4,9-tetrahydro-1H-pyrido[3,4-b]indole
- **Molecular Formula:** C₁₃H₁₄N₂
- **Molecular Weight:** 198.26 g/mol
- **CAS Number:** 526-45-6
- **Appearance:** White to off-white crystalline solid
- **Solubility:** Soluble in ethanol, methanol, and dilute acids;
  poorly soluble in water

Tetrahydroharmine is structurally related to harmine, differing by the
saturation of the pyridine nitrogen ring (tetrahydro vs. fully aromatic).

This reduction makes THH less planar than harmine, which affects its
binding affinity at various receptor sites and contributes to its
distinct pharmacological profile.

The compound can be synthesized by catalytic hydrogenation of harmine
or harmaline, though it is most commonly obtained through extraction
from *Banisteriopsis caapi* bark.

## Pharmacology

### Monoamine Oxidase Inhibition

THH is a reversible inhibitor of monoamine oxidase type A (MAO-A), but
is significantly less potent than harmine or harmaline in this regard.

IC₅₀ values for MAO-A inhibition: THH is approximately 10–50 times less
potent than harmine, which has an IC₅₀ in the low nanomolar range.

This relatively weak MAO inhibition means THH alone would be insufficient
to produce the oral activity of DMT in ayahuasca. Its role is primarily
contributory, working synergistically with the more potent harmine and
harmaline.

### Serotonin Reuptake Inhibition

A distinguishing feature of THH is its activity as a serotonin reuptake
inhibitor (SRI). This action is not shared by harmine or harmaline at
comparable concentrations.

By blocking the serotonin transporter (SERT), THH increases synaptic
serotonin levels, potentially contributing to the mood-elevating and
anxiolytic effects reported by ayahuasca users.

This SRI activity may also explain why ayahuasca preparations with higher
THH content (such as certain *B. caapi* varieties) are often described
as having a "softer" or more euphoric character.

### Other Receptor Interactions

THH shows weak binding to 5-HT₂A and 5-HT₂C receptors, though at
concentrations unlikely to be pharmacologically relevant at typical
ayahuasca doses.

It also acts as a weak antagonist at the GABA-A receptor, which may
contribute to the mild stimulant effects sometimes reported during
ayahuasca ceremonies.

## Occurrence and Sources

Tetrahydroharmine is found predominantly in:

- ***Banisteriopsis caapi***: The primary source. Concentrations in
  dried bark range from 0.05% to 0.5% depending on variety. The
  "Oni" and "Cielo" varieties tend to be highest in THH.
- ***Banisteriopsis inebrians***: A related South American species
  used in similar preparations.
- ***Peganum harmala*** (Syrian rue): Contains only trace amounts
  of THH compared to *B. caapi*.

THH is not found in *Psychotria viridis* or *Diplopterys cabrerana*,
the DMT-containing admixture plants commonly added to ayahuasca.

In prepared ayahuasca brew, THH content varies widely based on the
specific *B. caapi* variety used, brewing duration, pH, and
extraction method. Typical concentrations range from 50–500 mg/L.

## History and Cultural Significance

Tetrahydroharmine was first isolated and characterized from
*Banisteriopsis caapi* in the early 1970s by Rivier and Lindgren,
who were mapping the alkaloid chemistry of Amazonian psychoactive
plants.

The recognition of THH as the most abundant β-carboline in *B. caapi*
helped explain why the ayahuasca experience differs qualitatively
from synthetic harmine-DMT combinations used in clinical research.

Indigenous Amazonian peoples have long recognized qualitative
differences between *B. caapi* varieties, classifying them by the
nature of the visionary experience they produce. Modern analysis
suggests THH content is a key variable underlying these traditional
classifications.

Recent clinical interest in ayahuasca for treating depression, PTSD,
and substance use disorders has renewed attention on THH, as its SRI
activity may contribute to therapeutic outcomes alongside the
psychedelic effects of DMT.
