## Description

The Berchtesgarten Syndrome is a term coined by Bill Mollison to describe a specific Type One Error in permaculture design: the urge to site a dwelling on the highest point of a landscape — a ridge top or hill summit. The name references Adolf Hitler"s Berchtesgaden, a massive concrete blockhouse built on top of a crag where, as Mollison quipped, the occupant "could have perished of thirst." It represents the human desire to climb high and look out upon the landscape, which leads to multiple compounding design problems.

## Classification

- **Category**: Permaculture design principle / Type One Error
- **Originator**: Bill Mollison
- **Named after**: Berchtesgaden (Hitler"s mountain retreat)
- **Error type**: Site selection / Dwelling placement

## The Problems of Ridge-Top Siting

Mollison identifies multiple interconnected problems with hilltop dwellings:

1. **Water insecurity**: All water must be pumped uphill. The clean water storage is below the dwelling, requiring energy for distribution. "He could have perished of thirst."
2. **Fire vulnerability**: Fire sweeps uphill with fantastic rapidity. A ridge-top dwelling faces fire from two fronts simultaneously — there is nowhere to retreat. "Good-bye Berchtesgarten."
3. **Thermal exposure**: Ridge tops lack the thermal belt that exists just below the keypoint. Occupants experience extreme temperature fluctuations.
4. **Wind exposure**: Hilltops receive the full force of prevailing winds, increasing heating costs and structural stress.
5. **Frost exposure**: Cold air and frost flow over ridge tops. The dwelling sits above the protective thermal belt.
6. **High energy in perpetuity**: Once the error of ridge-top placement is made, "everything else you attempt will remain difficult forever. You invite a high energy situation for your client in perpetuity."

## The Correct Alternative: The Thermal Belt

Mollison recommends siting dwellings within the thermal belt, just below the keypoint:

- **Thermal advantage**: A zone of warm air exists between the frost above the keypoint and the cold valley floor below. This is where warm air is pushed out at the bottom of the forest above.
- **Water advantage**: Clean water storage is above the dwelling, providing gravity-fed supply without pumping.
- **Fire control**: Below the forest, fire can be controlled easily. The forest above acts as a buffer.
- **Seasonal advantage**: The thermal belt is where the first buds of spring break out and the last place where productivity disappears in autumn. "If you race up and down the hills, this is the best place to get started early in the spring."
- **Temperature differential**: Several degrees warmer than surrounding areas due to the insulating effect of the forest above.

## Key Facts

- **Type One Error**: Once this error is made, all subsequent design decisions are compromised. "You invite a high energy situation for your client in perpetuity. They are always going to be in trouble."
- **Multiple manifestations**: The syndrome includes any dwelling placed where water must be pumped up and fire sweeps from below.
- **Common among clients**: Many clients instinctively want to build on the highest point with the best views.
- **Another Type One Error**: "A little camp in the woods" is identified as a separate but related error — siting in an isolated forest location with similar compounding problems.

## Relevance to Permaculture Design

This concept teaches one of the most fundamental lessons in permaculture site analysis:

1. **Water flows downhill**: Design with gravity, not against it. Place storages high, uses low.
2. **Frost behaves like treacle**: Cold air is viscous and pools in valleys but flows over ridge tops. The thermal belt between is the ideal zone.
3. **Fire runs uphill**: A dwelling below the forest with water above can be protected. A dwelling above has no defense.
4. **Every placement decision cascades**: The first decision (where to site the dwelling) determines the energy budget of the entire system forever.
