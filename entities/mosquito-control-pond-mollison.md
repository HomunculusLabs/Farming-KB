## Mosquito Control Pond System (Mollison)

## Description

A biological mosquito control system designed around small garden ponds (4 feet across, 18 inches deep) using garlic oil, backswimmer insects, and frog populations. [[bill-mollison]] describes a comprehensive design that achieves near-100% mosquito larva kill rate while preserving tadpoles and other beneficial organisms. The system was developed by a Ph.D. in mosquito control.

## Classification

- **Category**: Biological pest control / pond management technique
- **Primary organisms**: Garlic (Allium sativum), backswimmers (Notonectidae), frogs (Anura), tadpoles

## Key Facts

- **Garlic oil**: The primary control method — squeeze garlic bulbs into the pond. Garlic oil floats on the surface and kills mosquito larvae with approximately 100% effectiveness.
- **Tadpole-safe**: Garlic oil does not harm tadpoles, which continue to develop and contribute to mosquito control as adult frogs.
- **Backswimmers**: Secondary control — backswimmer insects (Notonectidae) are introduced to ponds. They fly in naturally or can be collected and added. They prey on mosquito larvae but do not affect tadpoles.
- **Frog production**: A 4-foot pond produces approximately 200-300 frogs twice per summer. Tadpoles develop in the pond; adult frogs live in surrounding cabbages, lettuces, and mulch.
- **Not assassin bugs**: Mollison specifically notes to use backswimmers, NOT the large assassin bugs, for this purpose.

## Pond Design

- **Size**: 4 feet across, 18 inches deep.
- **Construction**: Old stock tanks, baths, brick/plaster, or dug into clay and rammed.
- **Egress**: Must provide a way for frogs to exit — frogs will drown without escape routes. Build up edges with stones, use sweet alyssum trailing into water as a ladder.
- **Micro-habitat**: Place sweet alyssum, thyme, and garlic between paving stones around the pond. Alyssum trails into the water edge, providing frog exit routes.
- **Stone pile**: Build a small stone pile in the pond for additional frog habitat.

## Integrated Design

- Ponds serve dual purpose: mosquito control and food production (high-yielding pond plants).
- Frogs return to the pond to breed, creating a self-maintaining control cycle.
- Some pond plants belong in the annual garden as high-turnover crops.
- Perennial pond plants integrate with the broader garden system.
