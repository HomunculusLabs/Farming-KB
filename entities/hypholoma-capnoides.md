# Hypholoma capnoides

## Introduction

**Hypholoma capnoides** (Fries) Quelet, commonly known as the Brown Gilled
Clustered Wood Lover, Smoky Gilled Hypholoma, or Elsie's Edible, is a gourmet
mushroom that grows on conifer wood. Also known as *Naematoloma capnoides* (Fr.)
Karst., this species is one of the few gourmet mushrooms adaptive to conifer
stumps and logs, making it a valuable candidate for recycling conifer stumps
after logging operations.

**CAUTION:** The sister species *Hypholoma fasciculare* (Sulphur Tuft) is
poisonous and difficult to distinguish until the gills are examined. *H.
capnoides* has smoky brown gills while *H. fasciculare* has bright greenish
yellow to dingy yellow gills. Additionally, *H. fasciculare* is extremely
bitter whereas *H. capnoides* is mild. Foragers should also be aware that
[[galerina-autumnalis]], a deadly poisonous mushroom, shares the same habitat.
Several species can inhabit a single stump. Danger is entirely avoided by
growing indoors on sterilized sawdust or chips.

## Description

The cap is orange to orangish yellow to orangish brown to dull brown, measuring
2-7 cm broad. It is convex with an incurved margin at first, soon becoming
broadly convex to almost flattened, occasionally with an obtuse umbo. The cap
margin is often adorned with fine partial veil remnants. The surface is smooth,
moist, and lacks a separable gelatinous pellicle.

The gills are close, white at first, soon grayish, and eventually develop a
smoky grayish purple brown color. The stem measures 5-9 cm, is enlarged at the
base, and is covered with fine hairs. The partial veil is cortinate, sometimes
leaving a faint annular zone that is dusted purple brown with spores. The
mushroom usually grows in dense clusters, and the overall appearance is
attractive and easily recognized by experienced foragers.

**Microscopic features:** Spores are purple brown in mass, 6-7 x 4.0-4.5 μm,
ellipsoid, smooth, with a germ pore. Cheilocystidia, pleurocystidia, and clamp
connections are present. The context is monomitic.

## Distribution

*Hypholoma capnoides* is widely distributed across North America, particularly
in the western United States. It occurs throughout temperate Europe and is
probably widely distributed through similar ecological zones worldwide. Its
preference for conifer substrates means it is especially abundant in forested
regions with significant conifer populations.

## Natural Habitat

This species is a lover of conifer wood, especially Douglas fir. It is found on
stumps or logs and is often encountered in "beauty bark" (landscaping bark). It
has also been successfully grown on sterilized wood chips of *Alnus rubra* (red
alder). The mycelium is white, silky, rhizomorphic, and exquisitely formed,
growing in distinct zonations. Yellow tones develop with age (notably, unlike
the rusty brown tones seen in [[hypholoma-sublateritium]]). The fragrance is
fresh, sweet, and forest-like, similar to [[stropharia-rugoso-annulata]].

## Cultivation

*Hypholoma capnoides* is best suited for outdoor cultivation on large diameter
conifer stumps and logs, which can produce crops every season for more than a
decade. Clusters hosting dozens of fruitbodies and weighing up to 4 pounds have
been collected from well-colonized stumps. It is considered the premier species
for recycling the millions of conifer stumps left after logging operations.

Yields on supplemented sawdust are modest at 0.10-0.25 lbs per 5 lbs of moist
unsupplemented sawdust. Notably, yield is not enhanced by bran supplements. The
species is currently better grown outdoors than indoors and is not yet
commercially marketed. Indoor cultivation strategies parallel those used for
[[flammulina-velutipes]]: stems elongate in response to elevated CO2 levels,
and cap development is influenced by light and CO2 concentration.

**Cropping Cycle:** 2 crops, approximately 4 weeks apart.

## Medicinal Properties

No formal medicinal properties have been documented for *Hypholoma capnoides*.
However, [[paul-stamets]] recommends examining this species for anti-bacterial
and medicinal properties given its woodland habitat and competitive success
against other fungi on conifer substrates. Its ability to thrive on conifer
wood suggests unique enzymatic capabilities worth investigating.

## Flavor and Preparation

*Hypholoma capnoides* has a nutty flavor and is excellent in stir fries.
Harvest when caps are convex for the best texture and flavor. The mushroom is
best prepared simply to appreciate its delicate taste. It pairs well with
garlic, butter, and light cream sauces, and can also be dried for later use
in soups and stews.
