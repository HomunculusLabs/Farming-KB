## Description

Lanzarote pit cultivation is a traditional dryland farming technique from the Canary Islands in which large pits (8-10 m across and 1-3 m deep) are excavated, a single vine or tree is planted in each, and the surface is covered with a layer of volcanic cinder. The pits function as night condensers, trapping cold night air and harvesting atmospheric moisture from sea winds, enabling productive agriculture in extremely arid conditions.

## Classification

- **Category**: Agricultural technique / Traditional farming system
- **Domain**: Dryland agriculture / Condensation harvesting
- **Geographic origin**: Lanzarote, Canary Islands

- **Climate**: Arid volcanic islands

## Key Facts

### Pit Construction

- Pits are 8-10 meters across and 1-3 meters deep.
- One vine or tree planted per pit.
- Surface carefully covered with volcanic cinder (scoria) raked over the pit surface.

### Moisture Harvesting Mechanism

- Large pits act as night condensers, trapping cold night air.
- Condensation from sea winds wets root areas.
- Cinder contains many air pockets and gas bubbles that retain moisture from dews and condensation.
- Cinder allows rain infiltration but prevents erosion and soil overheating.

### Applications

- Used for vine cultivation (primary use on Lanzarote).
- Also used for potato ridges.
- Orchards in sloping country.
- Home gardens wherever cinder is plentiful.
- Applicable over clay or loam soils where cinder is available.

### Environmental Benefits

- Cinder allows rain to penetrate while preventing erosion.
- Prevents soil overheating in the intense island sun.
- Air pockets in cinder retain atmospheric moisture.
- Pits provide wind protection at ground level.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- The humid microclimate at the bottom of the pits creates favorable conditions for [[saprophytic-fungi]].
- The temperature moderation effect of the pits creates stable conditions for fungal network development.
- Similar pit techniques could be adapted for [[stone-mulch]] or gravel can substitute.
- Particularly relevant for arid coastal regions where sea winds provide atmospheric moisture.
- Can be combined with [[drip-irrigation]] for initial plant establishment.
- Scale can be reduced for home garden applications using smaller pits.
