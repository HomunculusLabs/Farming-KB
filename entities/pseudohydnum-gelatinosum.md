## Overview

**Pseudohydnum gelatinosum**, commonly known as the toothed jelly fungus or
false hedgehog mushroom, is a species of jelly fungus in the order
Tremellales.

It is distinguished from other jelly fungi by the combination of its
gelatinous, translucent [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]], though it is not widely commercially cultivated.

*P. gelatinosum* is unusual among jelly fungi in forming an ectomycorrhizal
association with coniferous trees, rather than growing as a saprotroph on dead
wood.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:** Fungi → Basidiomycota → Tremellomycetes → Tremellales →
Pseudohydnaceae → *Pseudohydnum gelatinosum* (Scop.) P. Karst.

**Synonyms:** *Tremella gelatinosa* Scop., *Hydnum gelatinosum* (Scop.) Pers.,
*Merisma gelatinosum* (Scop.) Gray, *Exidia gelatinosa* (Scop.) G. W. Martin.

The fruiting body is gelatinous to rubbery in texture, translucent whitish to
pale gray or tan, and 1–5 cm tall. It is typically shelf-like or laterally
stipitate with a cap-like upper portion.

The most distinctive feature is the spore-bearing surface, which is covered
with small, crowded, conical teeth (spines) 2–5 mm long, hanging downward
from the underside. These teeth give the fungus its common name and superficial
resemblance to hedgehog mushrooms (*Hydnum* spp.).

The context is gelatinous and translucent, with a more firm central columella
in well-developed specimens. The flesh is tasteless and odorless when raw.

Basidiospores are broadly ellipsoid to subglobose, hyaline, and measure
7–10 × 6–8 µm. Basidia are tremelloid, cruciately septate, producing
epibasidia that bear the spores.
