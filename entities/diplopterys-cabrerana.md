# Diplopterys cabrerana

**Diplopterys cabrerana** (Cuatr.) B. Gates is a psychoactive plant species native to the Amazon basin, best known as one of the principal admixture plants added to **Ayahuasca** ([[banisteriopsis-caapi]]). Among the western Tukanoan Siona of the Colombian Putumayo, it is known as **Oco-yaje**. The leaves are added to the Ayahuasca brew to provide the visionary DMT component.

### Role in the Brew

While Banisteriopsis caapi provides the beta-carboline alkaloids (harmine, harmaline) that inhibit monoamine oxidase and allow DMT to be orally active, it is the admixture plants like D. cabrerana that supply the DMT itself. Without these additives, Ayahuasca would lack its characteristic visionary effects.

The synergistic interaction between the MAO-inhibiting beta-carbolines of Banisteriopsis and the DMT of Diplopterys is one of the most remarkable examples of ethnopharmacological knowledge in the plant kingdom. Indigenous Amazonian peoples discovered this pharmacological synergy through centuries or millennia of empirical experimentation, a discovery that was not replicated in Western laboratories until the mid-20th century.
