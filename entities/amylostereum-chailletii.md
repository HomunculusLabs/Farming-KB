## Description

**Amylostereum chailletii** is a wood-decay fungus in the family Amylostereaceae that is vectored by wood wasps (Siricidae). It causes dieback and death of larch trees (Larix spp.) and is notable for its symbiotic relationship with the giant wood wasp [[urocerus-gigas]].









## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Phylum**: Basidiomycota
- **Class**: Agaricomycetes
- **Order**: Russulales
- **Family**: Amylostereaceae
- **Genus**: Amylostereum
- **Species**: A. chailletii









## Key Facts

- Transmitted by the wood wasp Urocerus gigas to larch trees
- Causes dieback and death of larches, with severity depending on insect density
- Redfern (1989) described its interaction with the wood wasp vector
- Damage to trees is density-dependent: high insect populations cause severe attack and likely tree death
- Shares larch hosts with [[ceratocystis-lariciola]] (vectored by bark beetle [[ips-cembrae]])









## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

A. chailletii illustrates the principle of fungal-insect mutualism in wood decay. The density-dependent nature of damage is relevant to understanding threshold effects in pathogen epidemiology. For mycologists, the species is of interest as a wood-decay basidiomycete with specific insect dispersal mechanisms. The competition between multiple fungal pathogens vectored by different insects on the same host demonstrates [[ecological-niche]] partitioning.









## Ecological Role

As a wood-decay fungus, A. chailletii contributes to nutrient cycling in forest ecosystems by decomposing lignin and cellulose in dead and dying larch wood. Its dependence on wood wasp vectors for dispersal creates an ecological linkage between insect population dynamics and fungal distribution patterns.
