## Overview

**Lamium purpureum**, commonly known as red dead-nettle or purple dead-nettle,
is an annual herb in the family Lamiaceae. It is native to Europe and western
Asia and has become a widespread weed in [[urtica-dioica]]*) without any stinging hairs.

Red dead-nettle is one of the most recognizable early-spring weeds in gardens,
agricultural fields, and waste areas. Despite its weedy status, it has value as
an edible plant and an early-season nectar source for pollinators.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

*Lamium purpureum* L. was described by Linnaeus in *Species Plantarum* (1753).
The species epithet *purpureum* refers to the distinctive purple coloration of
the upper stem and leaves, which is most pronounced during cool weather.

The plant is an erect annual reaching 10–30 cm in height. The square stems are
branching from the base, green in the lower portion and becoming purple-red
toward the tips. The stem is pubescent with retrorse hairs.

Leaves are opposite, ovate to broadly cordate, 1.5–4 cm long, with crenate
to serrate margins. The upper leaves are often purple-tinted and crowded at the
stem apex, while the lower leaves are green with longer petioles.

Flowers are borne in whorls of 4–8 in the upper leaf axils, subtended by
purple-tinged bracts. The corolla is 10–15 mm long, two-lipped, with a
pinkish-purple to crimson tube and a pale upper lip. Flowering occurs from
March through November.

The calyx is 5-toothed, approximately 5–6 mm long, with teeth that are
triangular and slightly unequal. Each flower produces four brown, ovoid
nutlets approximately 1.5 mm long.

## Distribution and Habitat

Red dead-nettle is native to Europe, North Africa, and western Asia. It has
been introduced to [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-south-america]], eastern Asia, Australia, and
New Zealand, where it is now naturalized and often common.

The species is highly adaptable and colonizes disturbed ground, cultivated
fields, gardens, roadsides, waste areas, and woodland edges. It is one of
the most common arable weeds in temperate cereal farming systems.

It prefers nutrient-rich, moist soils but tolerates a wide range of
conditions. The plant germinates in both autumn and spring, producing
distinct flushes of growth. Autumn-germinated plants overwinter as rosettes
and flower early the following spring.

Red dead-nettle grows from sea level to approximately 1,800 meters elevation.
It is a common component of the *Chenopodietum* weed community in European
arable fields.

## Edibility and Culinary Use

Red dead-nettle is widely recognized as a safe and palatable edible weed. The
young leaves and tender upper stems are the most commonly consumed parts,
