# Magnaporthe grisea

**Type:** Species (Fungus)
**Category:** Plant Pathogenic Ascomycete
**Also known as:** [[bloomfield-rice-blast-appressorium-mechanics]] fungus, Magnaporthe oryzae (current name),
  M. grisea (T.T. Hebert) M.E. Barr

## Overview
Magnaporthe grisea (now reclassified as Magnaporthe oryzae) is the most
destructive pathogen of rice worldwide, causing the disease known as rice
blast. It was the first fungal plant pathogen to have its genome fully
sequenced (Dean et al., 2005) and serves as the principal model organism
for elucidating the molecular basis of fungal plant disease. Rice blast
destroys enough rice annually to feed approximately 60 million people.

## Taxonomy and Classification
- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Sordariomycetes
- **Order:** Magnaporthales
- **Family:** Magnaporthaceae
- **Genus:** Magnaporthe
- **Species:** M. grisea / M. oryzae

Note: Taxonomic revision has separated the rice-infecting isolates as
M. oryzae from the broader M. grisea species complex that infects
grasses and other cereals.

## Key Details

### Infection Mechanism
- Infects plants through a specialized infection structure called an
  appressorium
- The appressorium generates enormous [[bloomfield-turgor-pressure-and-hyphal-invasion]] (up to 8 MPa)
  to physically breach the plant cuticle
- Differentiates on the leaf epidermis before penetration
- Two of three MAP kinase pathways control appressorium development,
  making virulence a tightly regulated genetic process

### Genomic Features
- First fungal plant pathogen to have its genome sequenced
- Contains 76 G-protein coupled receptors (GPCRs), significantly more

## Relevance
Magnaporthe grisea represents one of the most significant threats to
global food security among [[bloomfield-fungal-pathogens-and-parasites]]. As a model organism, it has
provided fundamental insights into fungal pathogenicity, environmental
sensing, and genome evolution. Understanding its infection biology informs
both disease [[cannabis-disease-pest-resistance-breeding-clarke]] and the development of novel antifungal
strategies in agriculture.
