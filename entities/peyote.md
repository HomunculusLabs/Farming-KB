# Peyote

## Description

Peyote ([[lophophora-williamsii]]) is a small, spineless cactus native to the southwestern United States and northern Mexico, containing the psychedelic alkaloid mescaline. It has been used for thousands of years by Indigenous peoples of the Americas in religious and healing ceremonies, particularly within the [[native-american-church]]. Leary mentions peyote in "The Psychedelic Experience" in the context of dosage and side effects, noting that nausea associated with peyote use can have a physiological cause (unlike most psychedelic substances where nausea is primarily psychological).

## Classification

- **Category**: Plant — Cactus / Entheogen
- **Scientific Name**: Lophophora williamsii
- **Family**: Cactaceae
- **Primary Active Compound**: Mescaline (3,4,5-trimethoxyphenethylamine)
- **Related Substances**: Mescaline, San Pedro cactus ([[echinopsis-pachanoi]])

## Key Facts

- Small, spineless, globe-shaped cactus growing in the Chihuahuan Desert
- Contains over 50 alkaloids, with mescaline being the primary psychoactive compound
- Mescaline content: approximately 0.4% by dry weight (varies by specimen)
- Has been used ceremonially for at least 5,500 years (archaeological evidence from caves in Texas)
- Central sacrament of the Native American Church (NAC), which has legal protection for peyote use in the United States
- Leary lists mescaline dosage at 600-800 mg for transcendental experience, 300-500 mg for experienced users

## Peyote in Leary's Framework

Leary mentions peyote specifically in the context of physical side effects:

- Most psychedelic nausea is psychological (indicating fear), but peyote is an exception where nausea can have a genuine physiological cause
- The cactus material itself (not just the mescaline) causes gastrointestinal distress in many users
- Anti-nauseant drugs such as Marezine, Bonamine, Dramamine, or Tigan may be taken beforehand to prevent physiological nausea
- Onset time for mescaline (oral, empty stomach): 1-2 hours (longer than LSD at 20-30 minutes)
- Duration: 8-10 hours (similar to LSD)

## Traditional and Cultural Context

- Indigenous use dates back millennia among the Huichol, Tarahumara, Cora, and other peoples of Mexico
- The Native American Church incorporates peyote as a sacrament in all-night prayer ceremonies
- Traditional peyote ceremonies involve singing, prayer, and communal spiritual practice
- The "peyote road" emphasizes living an ethical, harmonious life
- Legal protections exist for NAC members under the American Indian Religious Freedom Act

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Peyote is relevant to the broader context of entheogenic cultivation:

- Unlike [[psilocybin-mushrooms]] (fungi), peyote is a slow-growing cactus requiring 10-30 years to reach maturity in the wild
- Cultivation techniques include grafting peyote onto faster-growing cactus rootstock (e.g., San Pedro) to accelerate growth
- The conservation concerns around wild peyote populations parallel concerns about wild mushroom habitats
- Peyote's mescaline is structurally similar to the neurotransmitters affected by psilocybin
- Understanding peyote traditions provides cultural context for the broader entheogenic movement that includes [[mushroom-cultivation]]
- The legal landscape around peyote (protected for NAC use) provides a model for potential psilocybin regulatory frameworks
