## Ferula drudeana

**Ferula drudeana** J.B. Korovin is a recently rediscovered perennial plant
species in the family Apiaceae that has attracted significant scientific and
public attention as a potential botanical match for the legendary ancient Greek
plant silphium. The species was originally collected in central Anatolia (modern
Turkey) in the early 20th century by botanist Boris Fedtschenko but was
subsequently overlooked for decades until its rediscovery on a Turkish mountain
slope.

Phytochemical analysis has revealed the presence of psychoactive compounds
in the plant's resin, lending plausibility to historical accounts that
silphium possessed [[medicinal-herb-growing]] 1.5 to 2.5 meters tall with a
thick, fleshy taproot. The stem is stout, hollow, and finely ribbed, arising
from a basal rosette of large compound leaves. The overall growth habit is
similar to other large Ferula species, with a single flowering stalk produced
per season.

Basal leaves are large, tripinnate, with segments 5 to 20 cm long and 2 to 8
cm wide, glaucous green with a waxy surface. The inflorescence is a large,
compound umbel up to 50 cm in diameter, composed of multiple umbels bearing
small yellow flowers. The fruit is a schizocarp, 12 to 18 mm long, with
prominent dorsal ridges and lateral wings.

## Distribution and Habitat

F. drudeana is known from a limited number of populations in central Anatolia,
Turkey, growing at elevations between 1,500 and 2,000 meters on rocky, calcareous
slopes. The species was rediscovered in 2021 on Mount Hasan in the Aksaray
Province by researcher Mahmut Miski, who had been searching for a botanical
candidate for silphium for over three decades.

ferula compounds. Gas chromatography-mass spectrometry analysis has identified
at least 15 major compounds, several of which are shared with F. assafoetida
and F. persica.
