## Overview

**[[agaricus]] xanthodermus**, commonly known as the yellow-stainer, is a basidiomycete
fungus in the family Agaricaceae. It is one of the most commonly encountered toxic
mushrooms in the genus *Agaricus*, frequently mistaken for the edible field mushroom
(*A. campestris*) or cultivated [[european-mushroom-poisoning-history]] incidents involving
the *Agaricus* genus, though its effects are generally gastrointestinal rather than
life-threatening. Rapid yellowing of the flesh upon cutting is the key diagnostic
character separating it from edible relatives.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:**
- Kingdom: Fungi
- Division: Basidiomycota
- Class: Agaricomycetes
- Order: Agaricales
- Family: Agaricaceae
- Genus: *Agaricus*
- Species: *A. xanthodermus*

The species was originally described by Joseph-Henri Léveillé in 1884. The epithet
*xanthodermus* derives from Greek *xanthos* (yellow) and *derma* (skin), referring to
the distinctive yellowing reaction. The lectotype was designated by Pegler in 1977.

**Cap:** 5–15 cm diameter, convex to plane-convex, dry, white to cream, sometimes
with a pale brown disc. Surface is smooth or finely fibrillose. The cap does not
typically stain yellow on its own but may show faint yellow tones in age.

**Gills:** Free, crowded, initially white then progressing through pink to
chocolate-brown at maturity. This pink stage is characteristic of the genus and
helps distinguish *Agaricus* from amanitas (which always have white gills).

**Stipe:** 5–12 cm tall, 1–3 cm thick, cylindrical with a slight bulbous base.
Ring is membranous, single, white, often with a yellowish tinge on the underside.
The stipe flesh turns vivid chrome-yellow when cut or bruised, especially at the
base.

**Flesh:** White, thick, turning bright chrome-yellow almost immediately when cut
or bruised. This reaction is most intense at the base of the stipe. The yellowing
fades gradually over 30–60 minutes.

**Spores:** 5.5–7.5 × 4–4.5 µm, ellipsoid, smooth, with a small germ pore. Spore
print is dark chocolate-brown.

**Odor:** Characteristic and distinctive — described as phenolic, inky, or like
library paste or iodine. This phenolic odor is considered the second most important
identification feature after the yellowing reaction.

## Distribution and Habitat

*A. xanthodermus* has a [[psilocybe-semilanceata]] and is found across temperate and
subtropical regions worldwide. It is particularly common in Europe, [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]],
Australia, and parts of Asia. Originally described from Europe, the species complex
has since been recognized as containing multiple cryptic taxa.

The mushroom fruits in disturbed habitats, particularly gardens, parks, lawns, and
along roadsides. It grows solitary to gregarious on the ground, often in enriched
soils containing compost, mulch, or manure. Fruiting occurs from late spring through
autumn, with peak abundance in summer and early fall.

The species has a strong association with human-altered landscapes and is rarely
found in old-growth forests or undisturbed [[agaricus-arvensis]]
