## Overview

Cercis chinensis, commonly known as the Chinese redbud, is a small deciduous tree in the family Fabaceae. It is native to central and southern China and is widely cultivated as an ornamental tree for its profuse display of pink to magenta flowers.

The species is closely related to the eastern redbud ([[cercis-canadensis]]) [[amanita-muscaria]] in traditional Chinese horticulture and medicine. The bark, flowers, and leaves have been used in traditional Chinese medicine for various ailments.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Taxonomy:**

- Kingdom: Plantae
- Order: Fabales
- Family: Fabaceae
- Subfamily: Cercidoideae
- Genus: Cercis
- Species: C. chinensis Bunge

The species was first described by Alexander Bunge in 1833 based on specimens collected in northern China. Two varieties are commonly recognized: var. *chinensis* (the typical variety) and var. *avenna* (with narrower leaves), though some authorities treat the latter as a synonym.

Recent molecular phylogenetic studies place Cercis in the subfamily Cercidoideae, which is distinct from the Faboideae. This subfamily is one of the earliest-diverging lineages within the Fabaceae.

**Morphology:**

Chinese redbud is a small tree or large shrub with a rounded to spreading crown. The bark is dark gray to brown, smooth when young and becoming shallowly fissured with age.

Leaves are simple, cordate (heart-shaped), 6 to 14 centimeters long and wide, with a rounded to slightly acuminate apex. The leaf surface is dark green and glabrous above, paler beneath with sparse pubescence along the veins.

The flowers appear in March to April before the leaves, borne in fascicles of 4 to 8 on bare wood of the previous year's growth and sometimes directly on the trunk. Each flower is papilionaceous, 10 to 15 millimeters long, with a pink to magenta standard petal and paler wings.

The fruit is a flattened legume pod 5 to 12 centimeters long and 1.3 to 1.5 centimeters wide. Pods are green initially, turning brown to reddish-brown at maturity, and typically contain 4 to 8 flat, oblong seeds.

## Distribution and Habitat

Cercis chinensis is native to central and southern China, occurring in the provinces of Hebei, Shanxi, Shaanxi, Gansu, Henan, Hubei, Sichuan, Guizhou, Yunnan, and Guangxi. Its range extends from approximately 32°N to 40°N latitude.

The species grows in a variety of habitats including forest margins, thickets, mountain slopes, and valley bottoms. It is found at elevations ranging from 500 to 2,000 meters above sea level.

Chinese redbud prefers well-drained, loamy to sandy soils and tolerates both acidic and alkaline conditions. It grows best in full sun to partial shade with moderate moisture but is reasonably drought-tolerant once established.

The natural climate in its range is temperate to subtropical, with cold winters and warm, moist summers. It is hardy to approximately USDA zone 6, tolerating winter temperatures to -23°C.

The species has been widely planted beyond its native range as an ornamental tree throughout [[organic-propagation-methods]].

The tree is relatively low-maintenance and adaptable to a range of soil conditions. It performs best with regular watering during establishment but is drought-tolerant once mature. Pruning should be done after flowering to avoid removing next year's flower buds.

Several cultivars have been developed for ornamental use. 'Avondale' is a compact, freely flowering selection popular in the nursery trade. 'Don Egolf' is a sterile triploid cultivar that produces abundant flowers without setting seed pods.

**Traditional Medicine:**

In traditional Chinese medicine, the bark of Cercis chinensis (known as zi jing pi) is used to promote blood circulation, relieve pain, and reduce swelling. It is prescribed for conditions including traumatic injuries, rheumatism, and menstrual disorders.

The flowers are also used medicinally, primarily as a remedy for coughs and [[tropical-rainforest-seedling-mycorrhiza-ecological-restoration]] plantings.
