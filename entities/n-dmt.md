# N,N-Dimethyltryptamine

## Overview

N,N-Dimethyltryptamine (DMT or N,N-DMT) is a naturally occurring psychedelic compound of the tryptamine class, widely regarded as one of the most powerful psychoactive substances known. Found in a remarkable diversity of plant species across the globe, as well as in trace amounts in mammals including humans, DMT has been used for millennia in indigenous shamanic practices — most notably as the primary active compound in ayahuasca, a brew consumed in Amazonian ceremonial contexts. When vaporized and inhaled, DMT produces an overwhelmingly intense, rapid-onset psychedelic experience characterized by profound alterations in consciousness, vivid geometric visual imagery, encounters with seemingly autonomous entities, and experiences of ego dissolution and mystical union. Despite its current Schedule I controlled substance status in most countries, DMT has attracted intense scientific interest in recent years for its potential therapeutic applications in treating depression, anxiety, addiction, and end-of-life distress, as well as for the fundamental questions it raises about the nature of consciousness itself.

## Chemical Identity

- **IUPAC name:** N,N-Dimethyl-1H-indole-3-ethanamine
- **Molecular formula:** C₁₂H₁₆N₂
- **Molecular weight:** 188.27 g/mol
- **CAS number:** 61-50-7
- **SMILES:** CNC(=O)CC1=CNC2=C1C=CC=C2
- **Chemical class:** Tryptamine derivative (substituted dimethyltryptamine)
- **Structural relationship:** Structurally similar to serotonin (5-HT), differing by two methyl groups on the terminal amine. Also closely related to psilocin (4-HO-DMT), bufotenin (5-HO-DMT), and 5-MeO-DMT.
- **Physical properties:** Crystalline solid at room temperature. White to off-white crystals when pure. Freely soluble in acidic aqueous solutions, soluble in organic solvents including ethanol, methanol, and chloroform. Slightly soluble in water at neutral pH. Melting point: 44–46 °C. Decomposes at higher temperatures.
- **Stability:** Relatively stable as a salt (DMT fumarate, DMT hydrochloride). The freebase form is sensitive to oxidation and light. Should be stored in a cool, dark, airtight container.

## Biosynthesis and Natural Occurrence

DMT is one of the most widely distributed psychedelic compounds in nature, occurring across thousands of plant species and in numerous animal taxa:

### Plant sources

- **Leguminosae (Fabaceae):** The richest botanical source. Mimosa tenuiflora (Mimosa hostilis) root bark contains 0.5–1.0% DMT. Anadenanthera peregrina and A. colubrina seeds (used as yopo/vilca snuff) contain DMT along with 5-MeO-DMT and bufotenin. Acacia species (A. maidenii, A. phlebophylla, A. confusa) bark and leaves.
- **Malpighiaceae:** Diplopterys cabrerana (chacruna) leaves, a principal ayahuasca admixture, containing 0.1–0.6% DMT. Banisteriopsis caapi, the ayahuasca vine, contains harmine and harmaline (MAOIs) rather than DMT itself.
- **Rubiaceae:** Psychotria viridis (chacruna) leaves, another primary ayahuasca admixture.
- **Other families:** Desmanthus illinoensis (prairie mimosa), Phalaris grasses, Petalodium species, and numerous others spanning over 80 plant families.

### Endogenous occurrence

- DMT has been detected in human blood, urine, cerebrospinal fluid, and brain tissue, though at very low concentrations (picomolar to low nanomolar range).
- The functional significance of endogenous DMT remains debated. Proposed roles include acting as a trace amine neuromodulator, a neuroprotectant, and potentially a regulator of the sigma-1 receptor.
- The biosynthetic pathway in mammals involves the enzymes tryptophan decarboxylase and indolethylamine N-methyltransferase (INMT), which sequentially decarboxylates tryptophan to tryptamine and then N-methylates it to DMT.
- The pineal gland hypothesis (popularized by Rick Strassman) suggesting the pineal as the primary site of DMT synthesis has been contested; INMT expression is widespread in the brain and peripheral tissues.

## Pharmacology

### Receptor interactions

- **5-HT2A receptor (primary site):** DMT is a potent agonist at the 5-HT2A serotonin receptor, which is considered the principal receptor responsible for its psychedelic effects. Activation of cortical 5-HT2A receptors on layer V pyramidal neurons leads to increased glutamate release, thalamocortical dysrhythmia, and the characteristic disruption of default mode network activity observed under psychedelics.
- **5-HT1A receptor:** Partial agonist activity, contributing to anxiolytic and mood-elevating effects.
- **5-HT2C receptor:** Agonist activity, potentially contributing to visual effects and changes in appetite.
- **Sigma-1 receptor:** Agonist activity, which may modulate neuroplasticity, calcium signaling, and cellular stress responses.
- **TAAR1 (trace amine-associated receptor 1):** DMT is an endogenous agonist, suggesting a potential role as a trace amine neuromodulator.
- **Other receptors:** Interactions with 5-HT2B, 5-HT6, 5-HT7, and alpha-2 adrenergic receptors at higher concentrations.

### Pharmacokinetics

- **Oral bioavailability:** Nearly zero when consumed orally without a monoamine oxidase inhibitor (MAOI), due to rapid first-pass metabolism by peripheral MAO-A enzymes in the gut and liver. This is why ayahuasca combines DMT-containing plants with MAOI-containing Banisteriopsis caapi.
- **Inhalation:** Rapid onset (15–60 seconds), intense peak at 2–5 minutes, total experience duration 15–45 minutes. Nearly 100% bioavailability via vaporization.
- **Intravenous injection:** Similar rapid onset and short duration to inhalation.
- **Ayahuasca (oral with MAOI):** Onset at 30–60 minutes, peak at 1–2 hours, total duration 4–6 hours. The MAOI allows DMT to cross the gut barrier and reach systemic circulation.
- **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized by MAO-A to indole-3-acetic acid (IAA) and by N-oxide formation. Secondary metabolites include DMT-N-oxide and N,N-dimethyltryptamine-N-oxide.

## Subjective Effects

### Onset and progression (smoked/vaporized)

- **0–30 seconds:** A "rush" or "loading" sensation, often described as a high-frequency vibration or buzzing. Body feels light or dissolves.
- **30 seconds – 5 minutes:** Complete breakthrough. Intense geometric visual imagery, colors of impossible saturation, dissolution of self-other boundaries. Many users report encountering seemingly autonomous beings or entities in hyperdimensional spaces.
- **5–20 minutes:** Gradual return to ordinary consciousness. Visual complexity decreases, thought patterns normalize, residual body effects fade.
- **20–45 minutes:** Aftereffects — residual visual patterning (especially with eyes closed), introspective mood, sense of significance or awe.

### Characteristic phenomena

- **Ego dissolution:** Complete loss of the sense of a separate self, replaced by experiences of unity, boundlessness, or identification with fundamental aspects of reality.
- **Entity encounters:** A remarkably consistent feature of DMT experiences, where users report interacting with intelligent, communicative beings described variously as jesters, aliens, machine elves, or spiritual entities. This phenomenon has been the subject of serious academic investigation.
- **Hyperspace:** The term commonly used to describe the immersive, seemingly alien environments experienced during breakthrough DMT states. These spaces are often described as having their own internal logic, architecture, and physics.
- **Ineffability:** Users consistently report that the experience defies adequate description in ordinary language, a characteristic shared with other mystical experiences and described by Aldous Huxley as "the reducing valve" of ordinary consciousness being removed.

## Therapeutic Research

- **Depression and anxiety:** Clinical research on DMT-assisted therapy is in early stages, with preliminary studies showing rapid and sustained reductions in depressive symptoms following single or limited doses, consistent with findings for psilocybin and classical psychedelics more broadly.
- **Addiction:** Ayahuasca has shown promise in treating substance use disorders, including alcoholism and cocaine dependence, in observational studies and preliminary clinical trials conducted in South America.
- **End-of-life distress:** The intense mystical-type experiences produced by DMT may help terminal patients confront mortality, reduce death anxiety, and improve quality of remaining life — a hypothesis under investigation at several research centers.
- **Neuroplasticity:** DMT has been shown to promote structural and functional neuroplasticity in preclinical models, including increased dendritic spine density and BDNF expression, similar to effects observed with other serotonergic psychedelics.
- **Legal research frameworks:** As of the mid-2020s, clinical research with DMT has been approved or is underway in the United States, United Kingdom, Brazil, Switzerland, and several other countries.

## Legal Status

- **United States:** Schedule I under the Controlled Substances Act. Not approved for any medical use. DMT-containing plants occupy a legal gray area, though extraction of DMT is explicitly prohibited. Religious exemption for ayahuasca use granted to specific churches (UDV, Santo Daime) under RFRA.
- **International:** Listed in Schedule I of the UN Convention on Psychotropic Substances (1971). Most countries classify DMT as a controlled substance, though enforcement regarding natural plant sources varies significantly.
- **Ayahuasca-specific exceptions:** Several South American countries (Brazil, Peru, Colombia) recognize traditional ayahuasca use. Religious exemptions exist in the US, Netherlands, and several other countries.

## Preparations and Routes of Administration

### Ayahuasca

- **Traditional preparation:** A decoction typically combining Banisteriopsis caapi bark (source of harmine, harmaline, and tetrahydroharmine — β-carboline MAOIs) with Psychotria viridis or Diplopterys cabrerana leaves (source of DMT). The plants are boiled together for several hours, often with repeated reduction and addition of fresh plant material.
- **Mechanism:** The β-carboline alkaloids in B. caapi are reversible inhibitors of MAO-A, the enzyme responsible for oxidative deamination of DMT in the gut and liver. By inhibiting MAO-A, DMT becomes orally bioavailable and can cross the blood-brain barrier in sufficient concentrations to produce psychedelic effects.
- **Variations:** Numerous indigenous and neo-indigenous recipes exist. Amazonian tribes use various DMT-containing admixture plants and MAOI sources. Some preparations include additional plants for flavor, purification, or specific ceremonial purposes (e.g., tobacco mapacho, datura, coca).
- **Ceremonial context:** Ayahuasca is traditionally consumed in ritual ceremonies led by experienced practitioners (ayahuasqueros, curanderos, or taitas) who sing icaros (healing songs) throughout the experience. The ceremony typically involves periods of introspection, vomiting (considered purgative and cleansing), and visual experiences.

### Smoked or vaporized DMT

- **Freebase DMT:** The most common form for vaporization. White to yellowish crystalline powder, typically vaporized at 60–80°C using a glass pipe, vaporizer, or "DMT machine" (a specialized glass apparatus). Dosage: 20–60 mg for threshold to full breakthrough.
- **DMT-infused herbs (changa):** A smokable blend of DMT-infused herbs, typically on a base of caapi leaf or mullein. The caapi leaf provides harmine alkaloids that extend and modify the experience. Changa typically contains 20–40% DMT by weight. Dosage: 50–150 mg of blend.
- **Enhanced leaf:** DMT deposited onto a smokable herb such as parsley, mint, or mullein. Typically 10–20% DMT by weight. Easier to dose and smoke than pure freebase crystals.

### Other preparations

- **Pharmahuasca:** Oral administration of pharmaceutical-grade DMT (usually as DMT fumarate) combined with a pharmaceutical MAOI such as moclobemide or harmine hydrochloride. Provides more controlled dosing compared to traditional ayahuasca.
- **Yopo and vilca:** Snuff preparations made from Anadenanthera seeds, containing DMT along with 5-MeO-DMT and bufotenin. Blown into the nostrils through a tube. Traditional to indigenous peoples of the Orinoco basin and Amazon.
- **Injections:** Intravenous and intramuscular administration has been used in clinical research settings, providing precise pharmacokinetic control for experimental purposes.

## Safety Profile and Risks

### Physiological safety

- **Toxicity:** DMT has a remarkably high safety margin physiologically. The LD50 in animals is extremely high relative to psychoactive doses. No confirmed deaths have been attributed solely to DMT toxicity. The primary risks are psychological rather than physiological.
- **Cardiovascular effects:** DMT produces transient increases in blood pressure, heart rate, and body temperature. These effects are generally mild with smoked DMT but more pronounced with ayahuasca (due to β-carboline MAOIs). Individuals with cardiovascular conditions should exercise caution.
- **Serotonin syndrome risk:** Theoretical risk when combined with serotonergic medications (SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs, tramadol). Caution is advised when combining DMT-containing preparations with prescription antidepressants.
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding:** Contra-indicated. Both DMT and the β-carboline MAOIs in ayahuasca are uterotonic and may pose risks during pregnancy.

### Psychological risks

- **Challenging experiences ("bad trips"):** Intense fear, confusion, paranoia, and existential distress can occur, particularly at high doses, in unprepared users, or in unsupportive settings. The rapid onset and intensity of smoked DMT can be particularly overwhelming for inexperienced users.
- **HPPD risk:** Hallucinogen Persisting Perception Disorder has been rarely reported with DMT, though the risk appears lower than with LSD or psilocybin.
- **Pre-existing conditions:** Individuals with personal or family history of psychotic disorders, schizophrenia, or bipolar disorder with psychotic features may be at elevated risk for adverse psychological reactions.
- **Set and setting:** As with all psychedelics, the psychological context ("set" — mindset, expectations, mental state) and physical/social environment ("setting") are critical determinants of the experience quality and safety.
