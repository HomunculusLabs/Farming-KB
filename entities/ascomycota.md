## Description

Phylum Ascomycota is the largest phylum of Kingdom Fungi, encompassing approximately 32,000 described species (Hawksworth et al. 1995). Ascomycetes are characterized by the production of meiospores (ascospores) within sac-shaped cells called asci. The phylum includes yeasts, molds, morels, truffles, cup fungi, and many economically important genera such as *Penicillium*, *Aspergillus*, *Saccharomyces*, and *Cordyceps*.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Phylum**: Ascomycota
- **Estimated species**: ~32,000 described
- **Sister phylum**: Basidiomycota (together form the crown fungi with Glomales)
- **[[ascomycota-phylogeny-major-classes-lineages]]**: Archiascomycetes, Saccharomycetes, Euascomycetes

## Key Facts

### Defining Characteristic

- Production of ascospores within asci (sac-shaped meiosporangia)
- Asci may be operculate (lid-like opening) or inoperculate
- Asci are borne in fruiting structures called ascomata (sporocarps)

### Three Major Classes

#### Archiascomycetes (Basal Ascomycetes)

- Paraphyletic assemblage of basal taxa recognized by rDNA phylogeny (Nishida and Sugiyama 1993, 1994; Taylor et al. 1994)
- Includes yeastlike, filamentous, and possibly sporocarp-producing species
- Largest order: Taphrinales (~100 species of plant pathogens)
- Notable members: *Pneumocystis jiroveci* (pneumonia agent), *Schizosaccharomyces* (fission yeasts), *Neolecta* (stipitate, tonguelike sporocarps)
- Taphrinales are dimorphic: saprobic yeast phase + filamentous pathogenic phase

#### Saccharomycetes (True Yeasts)

- Monophyletic clade sharing a most recent common ancestor with Euascomycetes (Berbee and Taylor 1993b; Kurtzman and Robnett 1995)
- Paramount in food production: *[[saccharomyces-cerevisiae]]* (bakers' and brewers' yeast)
- Pathogens: *[[bloomfield-candida-albicans-yeast-hyphal-dimorphism]]* (facultative human pathogen)
- Mutualists: numerous endosymbionts of arthropods
- No true yeast produces a sporocarp; life histories include budding growth phase
- Some members display filamentous growth (e.g., *Ascoidea* species)

#### Euascomycetes (Filamentous Ascomycetes)

- Largest class; includes filamentous, sporocarp-producing taxa and asexual relatives (anamorphs)
- Arguably the most successful group of fungi ecologically
- Includes parasites, pathogens, mutualists of plants/algae/animals, and saprobes
- Major lineages include Pyrenomycetes, Plectomycetes, Helotiales, Pezizales, Lecanorales, and Loculoascomycetes
- Primitive sporocarp morphology: apothecium (exposed hymenium)
- Convergent evolution in ascus morphology is common

### Key Orders and Groups

- **Pezizales**: Largest group of apothecial fungi; includes *Morchella* (morels), *Gyromitra*, *Helvella*, truffles; operculate asci; probably contains majority of ectomycorrhizal ascomycete species
- **Helotiales**: Inoperculate discomycetes; endophytes, [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi]], plant pathogens, saprobes; grossly polyphyletic
- **Pyrenomycetes (Sordariomycetes)**: Perithecial (flask-shaped) ascomycetes; includes Hypocreales, Sordariales, Xylariales, Ophiostomatales; also includes cleistothecial fungi and yeastlike endosymbionts
- **Plectomycetes (Eurotiomycetes)**: Eurotiales + Onygenales; includes *[[penicillium-chrysogenum]]* (penicillin source), *[[coccidioides-immitis]]* (valley fever), *Elaphomyces* (ectomycorrhizal truffles)
- **Lecanorales s.l.**: Lichenized ascomycetes; >7,000 species; two main groups—apothecial Lecanorales and Arthoniales
- **Loculoascomycetes**: Ascostromatic development; includes Pleosporales, Dothideales, Chaetothyriales; at least two independent origins of ascostromatic development

### Lichenization

- Gain of lichenization occurred early and infrequently during Euascomycete evolution
- Some major nonlichenized lineages (Plectomycetes, Chaetothyriales) may be derived from loss of lichenization
- Loss of lichenization was not predicted from previous studies—novel evolutionary hypothesis

### Phylogenetic Challenges

- Many monophyletic lineages at approximately order level have been discerned
- Traditional groupings (plectomycetes, pyrenomycetes, discomycetes, loculoascomycetes) based on sporocarp morphology do not correspond to natural groups
- Convergent morphological features are common throughout
- Relationships among monophyletic groups not resolved in most cases
- RNA polymerase II and protein-coding genes (β-tubulin, EF-1α) hold promise for resolving deep nodes

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Ascomycota includes many commercially cultivated and collected species: morels (*Morchella*), truffles (*Tuber*, *Terfezia*), cordyceps (*Cordyceps*), and cup fungi. The class Saccharomycetes underpins brewing, baking, and industrial fermentation. Understanding Ascomycota phylogeny informs taxonomy, substrate preferences, and life cycle management for cultivation. The dimorphic life histories seen in Archiascomycetes (yeast + filamentous phases) parallel strategies used in cultivation of other fungi. The phylum also includes critical contaminant genera (*Aspergillus*, *Penicillium*) that cultivators must manage.
