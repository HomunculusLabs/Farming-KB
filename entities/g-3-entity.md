# G-3

G-3 (2,5-dimethoxy-3,4-(trimethylene)amphetamine; 5-(2-aminopropyl)-4,7-dimethoxyindane) is a psychedelic amphetamine and a member of the Ganesha family described in PiHKAL entry #82.

It is the three-carbon (amphetamine) homologue of [[2c-g-3-entity]], carrying a trimethylene bridge fused across the 3 and 4 positions of the aromatic ring to create an indane framework.

The Ganesha series is named for the Hindu deity associated with new beginnings, reflecting the novel structural territory these compounds explore.

## Dosage and Duration

Dosage range: 12 to 18 milligrams.

Duration: 8 to 12 hours.

## Qualitative Effects

At 12 milligrams, a warmth and mellowness develop.

No body disturbance occurs, but no visuals are produced either, which the subject found disappointing on that particular occasion.

The day was spent reading with complete identification with the fictional hero, described as "a different form of fantasy."

The subject noted a preference for music as a structural basis for fantasy over literary immersion.

At 18 milligrams, a full plus three is achieved, though the subject was uncertain why it qualified as such intensity.

Eyes-closed imagery consists of "puffy clouds" without drama.

Music is not exciting.

Easy eroticism is available but without push in that direction.

Appetite is not greatly affected.

Moving about and doing things produces no difficulty, but lying still and surveying the body suggests some neurological sensitivity.

The following morning the subject moved more slowly than usual.

The overall assessment was uncertainty about whether going higher would provide reward.

## Comparison with 2C-G-3

In direct comparison between the two-carbon phenethylamine (2C-G-3) and the three-carbon amphetamine (G-3), the preference goes to the shorter-chain compound.

With the first Ganesha pair ([[2c-g-entity]] and [[ganesha-entity]]), the comparison was a stand-off in both potency and quality of effects.

Here, with the somewhat bulkier trimethylene bridge at the definitive 3,4-positions, the nod goes to the shorter chain for the first time in the series.

The potency differences are small, and maybe the amphetamine is still a bit more potent.

But there are hints of discomfort with G-3 that seem to be absent with the phenethylamine.

The more highly substituted compounds in the series more clearly define these differences between the 2-carbon and 3-carbon chain lengths.

## The Ganesha Series

The Ganesha compounds are characterized by increasingly bulky ring-substitution patterns built from bridged carbon structures.

G-3 carries a three-carbon bridge (trimethylene) across the 3,4-positions of the benzene ring, creating an indane framework.

This is the second compound in the series after GANESHA (the dimethylene bridge analogue) and before G-4 (tetramethylene) and G-5 (benzonorbornane).

The indane ring system is found in various natural products and pharmaceuticals, but its application to psychedelic amphetamine design was novel.

## Status

Active psychedelic. Potent but with hints of discomfort that make the phenethylamine homologue generally preferred.

The indane ring system produces a compound that is clearly psychoactive but lacks the compelling qualities of some other members of the Ganesha family.

The structural exploration of bridged ring systems on the phenethylamine/amphetamine framework represents one of the more inventive aspects of the Shulgin research program.
