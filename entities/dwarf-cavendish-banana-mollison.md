# Dwarf Cavendish Banana (Greenhouse Production)

## Description

The Dwarf Cavendish banana (Musa acuminata 'Dwarf Cavendish') is a compact banana variety recommended by Bill Mollison for greenhouse production in temperate climates. Two plants are sufficient to supply all the bananas a family needs, and the dwarf variety is specifically suited to the height constraints of passive-solar glasshouses.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Family**: Musaceae
- **Genus**: Musa
- **Cultivar**: Dwarf Cavendish
- **Common Names**: Dwarf Cavendish banana, Chinese banana

## Key Facts

- **Family-scale production**: Two banana plants normally supply all the bananas a household would use.
- **Compact size**: Dwarf variety suitable for single-story glasshouses.
- **Vegetative propagation**: Bananas send up shoots (pups) that can be given to friends to establish new plants.
- **Self-seeding**: In tropical climates, papaws (papayas) are self-seeding and come up everywhere; bananas spread via offshoots.
- **Cold sensitivity**: Cannot tolerate frost; must be grown in greenhouses in temperate zones.

## Greenhouse Design Considerations

Mollison describes two approaches for accommodating bananas in temperate greenhouses:

1. **Sunken floor**: Build a single-story glasshouse and drop the floor one story below ground level (must be well-drained). The below-grade position acts as a heat buffer while providing vertical space for tall plants.
2. **Two-story glasshouse**: Build upward with a full second story. However, Mollison warns about severe thermal stratification problems in two-story designs — a single story with dwarfs performs much better.

## Role in Permaculture Food Systems

Mollison argues that growing tropical/subtropical crops in temperate-zone greenhouses is a moral imperative:

- Food imports from the third world divert peasant economies from food self-sufficiency to export crop production.
- Growing coffee, tea, cinnamon, ginger, vanilla, and bananas locally reduces this burden.
- Each banana plant produces fruit continuously — cut the main stem after fruiting and allow a pup to replace it.

## Other Dwarf Varieties Mentioned

- Various others suitable for greenhouse culture
