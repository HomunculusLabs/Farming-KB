## Description

Phallin is a glucoside toxin first isolated from *Amanita phalloides* and other poisonous *Amanita* species. It was the first poisonous principle to be extracted from the Death Cap and was initially assumed to be the primary active poison. However, subsequent research revealed that phallin attacks the red blood corpuscles (haemolysis) and is readily destroyed by heat and digestive juices — characteristics that do not match the clinical presentation of *Amanita phalloides* poisoning, which is not primarily haemolytic in nature.

## Classification

- **Type:** Mycotoxin (haemolysin)
- **Chemical family:** Glucoside
- **Source organisms:** *Amanita phalloides*, *Amanita verna*, *Amanita virosa*
- **Target:** Red blood corpuscles (erythrocytes)
- **Stability:** Destroyed by heat and digestive juices

## Key Facts

- First toxin to be isolated from *Amanita phalloides*, leading early researchers to believe it was the cause of death.
- Acts as a haemolysin, destroying red blood corpuscles.
- Readily destroyed by heat and by digestive juices, meaning it cannot survive normal cooking or the passage through the stomach.
- The symptoms of *Amanita phalloides* poisoning are not those of haemolysis, which ultimately disproved phallin as the primary lethal agent.
- The true lethal toxin was later identified as amanitin (α-amanitin), which is heat-stable and resistant to digestive enzymes.
- Phallin's discovery nonetheless represented an important early step in understanding the complex toxin profile of deadly *Amanita* species.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Phallin illustrates the challenge of identifying the active principles in complex natural toxins. Early mycological research often isolated a single compound and attributed all effects to it, only to discover later that multiple toxins with different properties and targets coexist in the same organism. For modern mycotoxicology, phallin serves as a historical lesson in toxin identification methodology. The fact that *Amanita phalloides* contains at least three distinct toxins (phallin, amanitin, and phalloidin) with different mechanisms, stability profiles, and clinical effects demonstrates why mushroom poisoning can present in varied and confusing ways.

## Related Toxins

- **Amanitin (α-amanitin):** The true lethal principle; heat-stable, resists digestive juices, destroys liver and kidney cells.
- **Phalloidin:** A polypeptide toxin; quickly active but destroyed by heat. Binds actin and causes liver damage.
