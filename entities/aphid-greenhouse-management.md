# Aphid (Greenhouse Management)

## Description

Aphids are the primary plant pest encountered in winter greenhouse growing, specifically appearing on spinach crops in late winter. In Coleman's experience, aphid outbreaks serve as a biological indicator of excess soil nitrogen rather than a problem to be treated with insecticides -- organic or otherwise. The solution was not pest control but environmental management through irrigation.

## Classification

- **Category**: Pest / Biological indicator
- **Domain**: Greenhouse growing, integrated pest management
- **Target crops**: Spinach (primarily), other winter greens
- **Order**: Hemiptera, family Aphididae

## Key Facts

- Aphids appear on greenhouse spinach starting in early to mid-February in Coleman's zone
- Primary causal factor: excess nitrogen buildup in unirrigated winter greenhouse soil
- Nitrogen accumulates when no rain or irrigation flushes the soil from mid-November to early March
- Solution: thorough weekly irrigation on sunny mornings starting mid-January to flush excess nitrogen
- After implementing the irrigation regime, the aphid problem was eliminated entirely
- Aphid predators eventually appeared, but their carcass debris on leaves was a secondary quality problem
- The irrigation practice caused no new problems and continues to the present

## Relevance to Cultivation

This case study illustrates Coleman's "first rule of biological agriculture" -- pests are symptoms, not causes. Rather than treating aphids with sprays (even organic ones like garlic spray or pyrethrum), the correct approach is to identify and correct the underlying soil condition that made the plant vulnerable. For greenhouse growers specifically, the lesson is that winter irrigation management directly affects pest pressure. The tendency to reduce winter greenhouse watering (because evaporation is low and soil appears moist) can create nitrogen accumulation that triggers aphid outbreaks.

## Management Protocol

1. Monitor greenhouse spinach for aphid appearance starting early February
2. If aphids appear, check winter irrigation history -- likely the soil has not been flushed
3. Begin thorough weekly irrigation on sunny mornings starting mid-January
4. Do not use insecticidal sprays -- correct the cause (excess nitrogen) rather than the symptom
5. Continue irrigation practice preventatively in subsequent seasons
