## Overview

**Helleborus argutifolius** Viv., commonly called the Corsican hellebore or holly-leaved hellebore, is an evergreen perennial in the family Ranunculaceae. It is one of the larger hellebore species, forming robust clumps 60–90 cm tall and up to 120 cm across.

Native to Corsica, Sardinia, and the Balearic island of Majorca, it grows in Mediterranean scrub, rocky slopes, and open woodland. The species produces pale green, cup-shaped flowers from late winter through mid-spring.

Like all hellebores, H. argutifolius contains cardiac glycosides (hellebrin, helleborin) and protoanemonin. All parts are toxic if ingested, and sap may cause skin irritation in sensitive individuals.

The species is closely related to Helleborus [[helleborus-lividus|lividus]] from Majorca, and some authorities treat them as conspecific. Their hybrid, Helleborus × sternii, is widely grown in horticulture and combines the hardiness of the Corsican parent with the pink-flushed foliage of the Balearic parent.

**Synonyms:** *Helleborus corsicus* Willd., *[[helleborus-lividus]]* subsp. *corsicus* (Willd.) Tutin & T.R.Deng.

**Morphological description:**

Leaves are palmately divided into three coarsely toothed, spiny-margined leaflets, 10–20 cm long, leathery and dark green. The foliage is evergreen and distinctly prickly, resembling holly.

Stems are erect, stout, and branching, bearing terminal cymes of 3–12 flowers. Each flower is 3–5 cm across with five petal-like sepals in shades of pale apple-green, sometimes flushed pink on the outer surface.

True petals are reduced to tubular nectaries. Flowers are subtended by conspicuous bracts that persist through fruiting. Fruits are follicles containing numerous dark brown to black seeds.

Seeds are elaiosome-bearing, indicating ant-mediated dispersal (myrmecochory) in the wild. Germination is epigeal and typically occurs the spring after sowing, following a period of warm then cold stratification.

## Distribution and Habitat

Endemic to the western Mediterranean islands of Corsica (France), Sardinia (Italy), and Majorca (Spain). Populations are scattered across rocky limestone and granite substrates from sea level to 1500 m elevation.

The species occupies Mediterranean maquis, garigue, and open [[extending-growing-season]]. Mature plants are remarkably drought-resistant, reflecting their Mediterranean origins.

**Maintenance:**

Remove old or damaged leaves in late autumn to early winter before flower stems emerge. Cut spent flower stems to the base after seed set to prevent unwanted self-seeding.

Divide clumps in [[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]] or autumn if desired, though established plants resent disturbance. Propagate by seed sown fresh in late spring, or by careful division.

## Ecology and Applications

**Ecological role:**

The winter-blooming habit provides an important nectar source for early-emerging bees, particularly Bombus species and honeybees on mild winter days. The foliage is generally avoided by deer and rabbits due to toxicity.

In its native range, H. argutifolius contributes to Mediterranean scrubland biodiversity. It hybridizes readily with Helleborus lividus in cultivation, producing Helleborus × sternii, a popular garden plant with pink-tinged foliage.

**Ornamental value:**

Awarded the RHS Award of Garden Merit. Valued for its architectural, evergreen foliage and reliable winter blooms. The pale green flowers complement spring bulbs and early perennials.
