## Overview

**Trollius europaeus** (globeflower) is a perennial herbaceous plant in the family Ranunculaceae, native to Europe and northern Asia.

It is one of the most distinctive wildflowers of European mountain meadows, recognized by its globe-shaped bright yellow flowers.

The genus name Trollius likely derives from the Swiss-German word *Trollblume*, referring to the round flower heads that resemble the hats of mythical trolls.

The species is a valuable nectar source for pollinators in subalpine and alpine meadow communities.

**Trollius europaeus** was described by Carl Linnaeus in 1753.

## Pollination and Reproduction

**Trollius europaeus** exhibits a highly specialized pollination system centered on small flies (Diptera) of the genera Chiastocheta and other flower fly groups.

The globe-shaped flower structure creates a microclimate inside the flower that attracts and retains small flies.

Flies enter through gaps between the staminodes and feed on pollen and nectar within the enclosed chamber.

While inside, flies pick up pollen on their bodies and transfer it between flowers.

This fly-pollination syndrome is relatively uncommon among temperate herbs and represents a significant evolutionary specialization.

The species also reproduces vegetatively through its creeping rootstock, forming slowly expanding clumps.

Flowering occurs from May to August depending on elevation and latitude.
