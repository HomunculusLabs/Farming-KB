# 4-Acetoxy-N,N-Dimethyltryptamine (4-AcO-DMT)

4-Acetoxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine (4-AcO-DMT), also known as O-acetylpsilocin or synthetically as "psilacetin," is a synthetic tryptamine compound and a prodrug of [[psilocin]]. It is classified as a psychedelic substance and is considered an acetylated analog of [[psilocin]], structurally related to both [[psilocybin]] and [[dimethyltryptamine]].

## Chemical Structure and Properties

4-AcO-DMT is an indole-ethylamine compound with the molecular formula C₁₄H₁₈N₂O₂ and a molecular weight of 246.31 g/mol. The IUPAC name is [3-[2-(dimethylamino)ethyl]-1H-indol-4-yl] acetate. The structure consists of an indole ring with a dimethylaminoethyl chain at the 3-position and an acetoxy group at the 4-position of the benzene ring.

The acetoxy group at the 4-position is a key structural feature that distinguishes 4-AcO-DMT from [[psilocybin]] (which bears a phosphoryloxy group at the same position) and from [[psilocin]] (which bears a free hydroxyl group). The compound is typically encountered as a freebase oil or as a crystalline salt (commonly the fumarate or hydrochloride).

## Pharmacology

4-AcO-DMT is widely regarded as a prodrug of [[psilocin]]. Upon ingestion, it is believed to undergo rapid deacetylation by esterases in the body, yielding [[psilocin]] as the active metabolite. This mechanism parallels the metabolic dephosphorylation of [[psilocybin]] to [[psilocin]], though the deacetylation of 4-AcO-DMT is generally considered to occur more rapidly.

The pharmacological activity is therefore primarily attributable to [[psilocin]], which acts as a partial agonist at the 5-HT₂A serotonin receptor. This receptor interaction is the primary mechanism responsible for the psychedelic effects. Additional activity at 5-HT₁A, 5-HT₂B, 5-HT₂C, and other serotonin receptor subtypes has been proposed but is less well characterized.

## History and Discovery

4-AcO-DMT was first synthesized and described in the literature by Albert Hofmann and colleagues at Sandoz Laboratories in the late 1950s or early 1960s, during the same research program that produced many of the synthetic [[psilocybin]] analogs. It was investigated as a potential alternative to [[psilocybin]] for clinical and psychotherapeutic research but did not receive the same level of attention as the naturally occurring compound.

The compound remained relatively obscure for decades before resurfacing in the research chemical market in the 2000s and 2010s. It has gained popularity as a "legal alternative" to [[psilocybin]] in jurisdictions where the natural compound is controlled, owing to its synthetic origin and sometimes ambiguous legal status.

## Effects

The subjective effects of 4-AcO-DMT are reported to be nearly indistinguishable from those of [[psilocybin]], consistent with the prodrug hypothesis. Users describe visual and auditory hallucinations, altered perception of time, enhanced introspection, and mystical-type experiences. The onset is typically 20 to 40 minutes after oral ingestion, with effects lasting 4 to 8 hours depending on dosage.

Some users and researchers have reported subtle qualitative differences between 4-AcO-DMT and [[psilocybin]], including a smoother onset, reduced body load, and slightly different visual characteristics. However, these subjective distinctions remain anecdotal and have not been rigorously investigated in controlled clinical settings.

## Synthesis

The synthesis of 4-AcO-DMT is described in the patent and academic literature as involving the acetylation of [[psilocin]] or its precursors. One route involves reacting 4-hydroxyindole with acetic anhydride to form 4-acetoxyindole, followed by Mannich reaction or reductive amination to introduce the dimethylaminoethyl side chain. Alternatively, direct acetylation of [[psilocin]] using acetic anhydride or acetyl chloride in the presence of a base yields 4-AcO-DMT.

The concise large-scale synthesis of [[psilocin]] and [[psilocybin]] described by Shirota, Hakamata, and Goda provides relevant methodology for the preparation of the 4-hydroxyindole intermediate, which can be diverted to 4-AcO-DMT production.

## Dosage

Oral dosages reported in anecdotal literature typically range from 8 to 30 mg for a full psychedelic experience. A common threshold dose is approximately 5 mg, while doses above 35 mg are considered strong. These figures are approximate and highly variable based on individual sensitivity, set, and setting.

## Legal Status

The legal status of 4-AcO-DMT varies by jurisdiction. In many countries, it exists in a legal gray area due to its synthetic nature and absence from explicit scheduling. However, several nations have enacted analogue or blanket bans on tryptamine derivatives that would encompass 4-AcO-DMT. In the United States, it may fall under the Federal Analogue Act as an analogue of [[psilocybin]] or [[dimethyltryptamine]], though enforcement has been inconsistent.

In the United Kingdom, the Psychoactive Substances Act 2016 potentially covers 4-AcO-DMT, though its explicit scheduling status has been debated. In Canada, it is not specifically listed in the Controlled Drugs and Substances Act but could be considered an analog of a Schedule III substance. Users and researchers should consult local regulations before acquiring or working with this compound.

## Comparison with Psilocybin

Several lines of evidence support the prodrug hypothesis for 4-AcO-DMT and argue against significant intrinsic activity of the acetylated form:

- **Esterase studies**: In vitro incubation of 4-AcO-DMT with human or animal serum rapidly produces [[psilocin]], with half-lives on the order of minutes.
- **Pharmacokinetic studies**: Plasma levels of 4-AcO-DMT decline rapidly after administration, while [[psilocin]] levels rise correspondingly, consistent with metabolic conversion.
- **Receptor binding**: 4-AcO-DMT itself shows very low affinity for 5-HT₂A and other relevant receptors in radioligand binding assays, while its metabolite [[psilocin]] shows high affinity.

Despite these data, some researchers have argued that the acetylated form may have unique pharmacological properties not accounted for by simple prodrug conversion. These might include differences in absorption, distribution, or first-pass metabolism that could affect the time course or character of the psychedelic experience.

## Variants and Homologs

Several structural variants of 4-AcO-DMT have been synthesized and investigated:

- **4-AcO-MET**: The N-methyl-N-ethyl analog, reportedly producing effects similar to 4-AcO-DMT but with a longer duration.
- **4-AcO-MiPT**: The N-methyl-N-isopropyl analog, reported to have reduced visual effects compared to 4-AcO-DMT.
- **4-AcO-DiPT**: The N,N-diisopropyl analog, known for producing primarily auditory distortions with minimal visual effects.
- **4-AcO-DET**: The N,N-diethyl analog, with a slightly longer duration than 4-AcO-DMT.

These variants illustrate the structure-activity relationships within the 4-substituted tryptamine class, where modifications to the amine nitrogen alter potency, duration, and qualitative character of effects while the 4-acetoxy pharmacophore remains constant.

## Therapeutic Research

The modern resurgence of interest in psychedelic-assisted therapy has led to renewed investigation of 4-AcO-DMT as a potential clinical tool. The compound's pharmacological similarity to [[psilocybin]], combined with practical advantages in synthesis and storage, has attracted the attention of both academic researchers and pharmaceutical companies. Several factors make it an interesting candidate for therapeutic development:

- **Stability**: The fumarate salt of 4-AcO-DMT is more stable than [[psilocybin]] under ambient conditions, simplifying storage and handling in clinical settings.
- **Synthetic accessibility**: 4-AcO-DMT can be synthesized more readily and at lower cost than [[psilocybin]], which requires extraction from natural sources or more complex synthesis.
- **Regulatory exploration**: In some jurisdictions, 4-AcO-DMT occupies a more favorable regulatory position than [[psilocybin]], potentially facilitating clinical research.

Clinical trials directly comparing 4-AcO-DMT to [[psilocybin]] in controlled settings have not yet been published, but preclinical and anecdotal evidence suggests comparable efficacy. As regulatory landscapes evolve, 4-AcO-DMT may emerge as a practical alternative for therapeutic applications.

## Metabolism and Pharmacokinetics

The pharmacokinetics of 4-AcO-DMT have been partially characterized through both preclinical studies and anecdotal reports:

- **Absorption**: Oral bioavailability is high, with rapid absorption from the gastrointestinal tract. Sublingual and intranasal routes have also been reported, with faster onset but shorter duration.
- **First-pass metabolism**: The compound undergoes extensive first-pass metabolism, primarily via deacetylation to [[psilocin]]. This process is rapid and efficient, with the majority of the dose converted within the first pass through the liver and intestinal mucosa.
- **Distribution**: [[psilocin]] generated from 4-AcO-DMT distributes throughout the body, with significant penetration into the central nervous system due to its lipophilic nature.
- **Elimination**: The primary route of elimination is renal excretion of [[psilocin]] and its glucuronide conjugate. The elimination half-life of [[psilocin]] is approximately 2 to 3 hours.

## Forms and Preparations

4-AcO-DMT is encountered in several forms in the research chemical and underground markets:

- **Freebase**: An oily liquid, rarely encountered in commercial preparations due to handling difficulties.
- **Fumarate salt**: The most common crystalline form, appearing as white to off-white crystals or powder. This is the most stable and well-characterized form.
- **Hydrochloride salt**: A crystalline powder, more hygroscopic than the fumarate.
- **Hemifumarate**: Some preparations use a hemifumarate salt, which has different solubility properties.

Purity of commercial preparations varies widely and should be verified by analytical testing before use in research settings. Adulterants and misidentified compounds have been reported in unregulated markets.

## Tolerance and Cross-Tolerance

As a prodrug of [[psilocin]], 4-AcO-DMT exhibits tolerance and cross-tolerance patterns consistent with the 5-HT₂A receptor-mediated psychedelic class:

- **Acute tolerance**: Significant tolerance develops rapidly, with diminished effects observed if the compound is re-dosed within 24 to 48 hours. This tolerance is primarily due to 5-HT₂A receptor downregulation and internalization.
- **Cross-tolerance**: Full cross-tolerance exists between 4-AcO-DMT, [[psilocybin]], [[psilocin]], LSD, and other classical psychedelics that act primarily as 5-HT₂A agonists.
- **Tolerance duration**: Tolerance typically resolves within 7 to 14 days of abstinence, though individual variation is considerable.
- **Harm reduction**: These tolerance properties have implications for safe use patterns, as escalating doses to overcome tolerance can increase the risk of adverse psychological reactions without proportionally increasing the desired effects.
