# Harmane

## Overview

**Harmane** (1-methyl-9H-pyrido[3,4-b]indole) is a tricyclic
β-carboline alkaloid found in numerous plant species, foods, tobacco
smoke, and mammalian tissues including the human brain.

Its pharmacological significance centers on reversible MAO-A
inhibition, [[serotonin-receptor-interference-psychedelic-mechanism]] agonism, and neurotoxic properties at
elevated concentrations. A principal β-carboline in *Banisteriopsis
caapi*, harmane enables [[tihkal-oral-activity-mao-inhibition]] of DMT in ayahuasca by
inhibiting peripheral MAO-A.

## Chemical Properties

**Chemical name:** 1-Methyl-9H-pyrido[3,4-b]indole

**Molecular formula:** C₁₂H₁₀N₂ | **MW:** 182.22 g/mol

**Structure:** Tricyclic β-carboline (indole + pyridine fusion) with
methyl substituent at N-1 position.

**CAS:** 486-84-0

**Physical properties:** Yellowish crystalline solid. MP 228–230°C
(dec.). Slightly soluble in water; soluble in ethanol, methanol,
chloroform, dilute acids. pKa 7.7.

**Spectroscopic:** UV-Vis λmax (MeOH) 253, 280, 330, 370 nm.
Fluorescence: ex 370 nm, em 440 nm.

## Pharmacology

**MAO inhibition:** Competitive, reversible MAO-A inhibitor (RIMA
profile), Ki 0.5–5 µM. Weak MAO-B inhibition (Ki > 50 µM).

By inhibiting peripheral MAO-A, allows DMT oral bioavailability in
ayahuasca — the pharmacological basis for the brew's oral activity.

**[[psilocybin-pharmacology-and-serotonin-receptors]]:** Agonist at multiple subtypes:
- **5-HT₂A** (Ki ~1–5 µM) — mild psychoactive/hallucinogenic effects
- **5-HT₂B** — cardiac valvulopathy risk with chronic use
- **5-HT₂C** — appetite and mood effects

**Other interactions:** Weak GABA_A benzodiazepine site binding
(Ki > 100 µM); imidazoline I₂ [[blesching-cannabis-oxidative-stress-and-antioxidant-defense]] and mitochondrial dysfunction.
Elevated levels found in postmortem brain tissue of essential tremor
and Parkinson's patients; causality not established.

## Effects

**[[cannabis-therapeutic-index-overview]]. Above
100 mg: severe nausea, hypertensive crisis risk (tyramine + MAOI),
neurotoxic potential. Not used recreationally as standalone drug.
