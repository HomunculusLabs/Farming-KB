## Overview

Pinus coulteri, commonly known as Coulter pine or big-cone pine, is a large coniferous tree native to the mountains of central and southern California and northern Baja California.

It is renowned for producing the heaviest cones of any pine species, which can weigh up to 5 kilograms and are armed with formidable hooked spines.

The species was named in honor of the Irish botanist and physician Thomas Coulter, who collected it in California during the early 1830s.

Coulter pine is an ecologically significant component of the mixed-conifer and pine-oak woodlands of the Transverse and Peninsular Ranges.

The species is relatively uncommon compared to its more widespread relatives, ponderosa and Jeffrey pines.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Pinus coulteri was first described by David Douglas in 1832 and formally named by George Gordon in 1849.

The authority is commonly cited as D.Don ex Gordon.

It belongs to Pinus subsection Ponderosae, closely related to Jeffrey pine and ponderosa pine.

Mature trees reach 20 to 30 meters in height, with trunk diameters of 60 to 90 centimeters.

The crown is open and irregularly rounded, with ascending branches that become spreading at maturity.

Bark on young trees is dark and scaly, becoming thick, deeply furrowed, and reddish-brown on mature trunks.

Needles are borne in fascicles of three, measuring 20 to 38 centimeters in length.

They are stout, stiff, and yellowish-green to dark green, persisting for 3 to 4 years.

Cones are the most distinctive feature of the species, measuring 20 to 40 centimeters in length and weighing 2 to 5 kilograms.

They are broadly ovoid, sessile, and densely armed with stout, recurved spines on each scale that can inflict serious injury.

Cone maturation requires 2 years, and the cones typically persist on the tree for several years before opening.

Seeds are large, 12 to 15 millimeters long, with thick wings of 20 to 25 millimeters.

## Distribution and Habitat

Pinus coulteri occurs in a relatively restricted range within the California Floristic Province.

The northern extent of the range is near Mount Diablo in the Diablo Range of central California.

Populations extend southward through the Transverse Ranges and into the Peninsular Ranges of southern California.
