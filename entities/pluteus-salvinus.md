## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Pluteaceae
- **Genus:** *Pluteus* section *Pluteus*
- **Species:** *P. salvinus* Singer (1958)

The genus *Pluteus* is characterized by free gills (not attached to
the stipe), pink [[fungal-succession-woody-debris-ecology]].
Most species in the genus are saprotrophic and not psychoactive; the
presence of psilocybin in *P. salvinus* is a secondary biochemical
feature not shared with the majority of its congeners.

The species is placed in section *Pluteus* based on its smooth,
non-veil-covered cap surface and absence of cystidia types
characteristic of other sections within the genus.

## Morphology

The pileus is convex to broadly convex, 2–5 cm in diameter, with a
smooth, slightly viscid surface when moist. The cap color is
greyish-brown to olive-brown, often with a slightly paler margin.
The surface may develop fine radial fibrils with age.

Gills are free from the stipe, crowded, and white when young, turning
pinkish as spores mature — a diagnostic feature of the Pluteaceae.
The stipe is central, 3–6 cm long and 3–6 mm thick, cylindrical, and
colored whitish to pale grey, often with a slightly bulbous base.

### Microscopic Features

- **Basidiospores:** Broadly ellipsoid, 6–8 × 5–6.5 µm, smooth,
  thin-walled, pinkish in deposit.
- **Basidia:** Clavate, 20–28 × 6–8 µm, four-spored.
- **Cheilocystidia:** Abundant, diverse in shape — fusiform,
  lageniform, or utriform, with apical projections.
- **Pleurocystidia:** Present, similar to cheilocystidia but less
  abundant; characteristic of the genus *Pluteus*.
- **Pileipellis:** A cutis of interwoven hyphae, with occasional
  erect elements forming fine surface fibrils.

No [[partial-veil-tropism-protection-before-spore-release]] or annulus is present. The flesh is thin, white, and
has no distinctive odor.
