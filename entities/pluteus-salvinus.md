# Pluteus salvinus (*Pluteus salvinus* Singer)

## Overview

*Pluteus salvinus* is a rarely documented species of agaric mushroom in
the family Pluteaceae, known primarily from tropical and subtropical
regions of Mesoamerica. It is one of the few *Pluteus* species confirmed
to contain psilocybin, placing it among the lesser-known psychoactive
fungi alongside more famous genera such as *Psilocybe* and *Gymnopilus*.

The species was originally described by Rolf Singer in 1958 from
collections in Guatemala, associated with investigations into the
traditional use of psychoactive mushrooms by indigenous peoples of the
region. It is named in honor of Salvador, likely referencing the
Salvadoran context of the original investigations.

*P. salvinus* remains poorly known in the mycological literature, with
relatively few confirmed collections and limited modern [[challenges-neurotropic-fungi-chemical-analysis-taxonomic-identification]]
of its psilocybin content.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Pluteaceae
- **Genus:** *Pluteus* section *Pluteus*
- **Species:** *P. salvinus* Singer (1958)

The genus *Pluteus* is characterized by free gills (not attached to
the stipe), pink spore prints, and growth on wood or [[fungal-succession-woody-debris-ecology]].
Most species in the genus are saprotrophic and not psychoactive; the
presence of psilocybin in *P. salvinus* is a secondary biochemical
feature not shared with the majority of its congeners.

The species is placed in section *Pluteus* based on its smooth,
non-veil-covered cap surface and absence of cystidia types
characteristic of other sections within the genus.

## Morphology

The pileus is convex to broadly convex, 2–5 cm in diameter, with a
smooth, slightly viscid surface when moist. The cap color is
greyish-brown to olive-brown, often with a slightly paler margin.
The surface may develop fine radial fibrils with age.

Gills are free from the stipe, crowded, and white when young, turning
pinkish as spores mature — a diagnostic feature of the Pluteaceae.
The stipe is central, 3–6 cm long and 3–6 mm thick, cylindrical, and
colored whitish to pale grey, often with a slightly bulbous base.

### Microscopic Features

- **Basidiospores:** Broadly ellipsoid, 6–8 × 5–6.5 µm, smooth,
  thin-walled, pinkish in deposit.
- **Basidia:** Clavate, 20–28 × 6–8 µm, four-spored.
- **Cheilocystidia:** Abundant, diverse in shape — fusiform,
  lageniform, or utriform, with apical projections.
- **Pleurocystidia:** Present, similar to cheilocystidia but less
  abundant; characteristic of the genus *Pluteus*.
- **Pileipellis:** A cutis of interwoven hyphae, with occasional
  erect elements forming fine surface fibrils.

No partial veil or annulus is present. The flesh is thin, white, and
has no distinctive odor.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Pluteus salvinus* is known from tropical and subtropical forests of
Mesoamerica, with confirmed collections from Guatemala and neighboring
regions of southern Mexico. The species has also been tentatively
reported from parts of South America, though these records require
molecular confirmation.

It grows as a saprotroph on decaying wood and woody debris, particularly
on fallen logs and branches of hardwood trees in humid tropical forest
environments. Collections have been made at elevations ranging from
500 to 1,500 meters above sea level.

Fruiting occurs during the rainy season, typically from June to October
in its known range. The species appears to prefer undisturbed or
semi-disturbed forest habitats with high humidity and abundant woody
debris on the forest floor.

## Chemistry and Effects

*P. salvinus* has been confirmed to contain psilocybin, the primary
psychoactive [[aminopropyl-silica-gel-chromatography-indole-alkaloid-purification]] found in [[emcdda-hallucinogenic-mushrooms-emerging-trend-europe]]. The
original analysis by Singer and colleagues detected psilocybin in
basidiocarp tissue using paper chromatography methods.

Quantitative analyses of psilocybin and psilocin content are limited.
Based on available data from related psychoactive *Pluteus* species,
psilocybin content is likely in the low to moderate range (estimated
0.1–0.5% dry weight), making it significantly less potent than
concentrated cultivated *[[brassinosteroid-morphological-effects-psilocybe-cubensis-fruiting]]* strains.

The psychoactive effects are consistent with classical tryptamine
psychedelics: visual and auditory alterations, altered perception of
time, synesthetic experiences, and changes in mood and cognitive
processing. Onset occurs within 20–60 minutes of ingestion, with
effects lasting 4–6 hours.

⚠️ **Legal status:** Psilocybin and psilocybin-containing mushrooms
are classified as Schedule I substances in many jurisdictions. The
legal status of *P. salvinus* varies by country.

## Similar Species

*Pluteus salvinus* can be confused with other *Pluteus* species that
lack psychoactive properties, as well as with potentially toxic
mushrooms from other genera.

- **[[inocybe-corydalina]]** — Another psilocybin-containing
  species found on wood, but with adnate gills, brown spore print, and
  distinctive odor; some *Inocybe* species contain dangerous levels of
  muscarine.
