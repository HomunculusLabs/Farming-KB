# Cordon Fruit Training

## Description

Cordon fruit training is a space-efficient espalier technique where fruit trees (typically apples and pears) are grown as single stems trained along wires or fences. Bill Mollison recommends cordon training for zone one permaculture designs where space is limited, noting a California practitioner who grows 150 apple varieties using this method and produces "bushels and bushels" from a tiny backyard.

## Classification

- **Category**: Horticultural Technique / Pruning System
- **Primary Species**: Malus domestica (apple), Pyrus communis (pear)
- **Domain**: Zone One Design, Intensive Orcharding
- **Permaculture Principle**: Edge effect, space optimization, [[gaias-garden-stacking-functions]]

## Key Facts

- Single cordons are trained as individual stems approximately four feet long, each grafted to a different variety.
- Whip grafting is the recommended technique for establishing cordons.
- A cordon fence can be composed of five or more different apple varieties in the space of a single standard tree.
- A California grower maintains 150 varieties using cordon systems in a small backyard.
- Mollison recommends cordon training specifically for apples and pears in zone one.
- Dwarf fruit trees are recommended for other species (peaches, citrus) in the same zone.
- The technique is described as "extraordinarily high-value" per unit area.
- Combines with multi-graft approaches for maximum diversity in minimum space.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Root Zone Management**: Cordon training concentrates root systems in narrow spaces, requiring careful attention to soil biology and mycorrhizal health.
- **Fungal-Friendly Pruning**: The intensive pruning regime creates woody debris that can feed saprophytic fungal communities in the mulch layer below.
- **[[holzer-climate-zone-microclimate-creation]]**: Cordon fences create sheltered microclimates beneficial to nearby mushroom beds and compost areas.
- **Biodiversity**: Multiple varieties in a small space support diverse pollinator communities and associated soil microbiota.
- **Space Efficiency**: Frees ground area for other uses including mushroom cultivation, herb spirals, and composting systems.
