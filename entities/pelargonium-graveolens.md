## Taxonomy and Morphology

### Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Division:** Angiosperms
- **Class:** Eudicots
- **Order:** Geraniales
- **Family:** Geraniaceae
- **Genus:** *Pelargonium*
- **Species:** *P. graveolens* L'Hér.
- **Authority:** L'Héritier de Brutelle (1792)

The species belongs to section *Pelargonium* within the genus. Several
cultivars and chemotypes exist, with significant variation in essential
oil composition. The commercial cultivar 'Bourbon', from Réunion Island,
is considered the gold standard for essential oil quality.

### Morphology

*P. graveolens* forms an erect, branching shrub reaching 60–100 cm in
height. Stems are hairy and slightly succulent at the base, becoming
woody with age.

Leaves are palmately lobed with 5–7 deeply incised lobes, 3–6 cm wide,
covered in soft glandular trichomes that secrete the aromatic essential
oil. Leaf color ranges from gray-green to soft green with a silvery
sheen from the trichome layer.

Flowers are small, pink to lavender, borne in umbel-like clusters. Each
flower has five petals with darker veining, approximately 1–1.5 cm in
diameter. The blooming period extends from spring through late summer.

## Distribution and Cultivation

The species is indigenous to the Cape Floristic Region of South Africa,
specifically the Western and Eastern Cape provinces. It grows naturally
in sandy, well-drained soils in fynbos and renosterveld vegetation types.

Commercial cultivation is concentrated in Egypt (the world's largest
producer), Réunion Island, China, India, Algeria, and Morocco. The plant
is propagated vegetatively through stem cuttings to maintain chemotype
consistency.
