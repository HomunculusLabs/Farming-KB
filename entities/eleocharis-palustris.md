## Overview

**Eleocharis palustris** (common spikerush) is a widespread perennial emergent aquatic plant in the sedge family (Cyperaceae). It occurs in marshes, shallow wetlands, and along the margins of lakes and streams across much of the Northern Hemisphere.

The species is ecologically important as a primary colonizer of disturbed wetland substrates and a stabilizer of shorelines and streambanks. Its dense rhizomatous mats provide [[growing-gourmet-north-american-reishi-species]] plants are sometimes treated as E. palustris ssp. palustris.

The genus Eleocharis contains approximately 250 species worldwide. E. palustris is one of the most widely distributed members of the genus.

Related species that frequently co-occur with E. palustris include E. acicularis (needle spikerush), E. obtusa (blunt spikerush), and E. erythropoda (red-rooted spikerush). Field identification often requires examination of achene and tubercle morphology under magnification.

## Morphology

Common spikerush is a rhizomatous perennial producing dense tussocks of cylindrical, unbranched stems. Stems are 15–80 cm tall, 1.5–4 mm in diameter, bright green to yellowish-green, and smooth to slightly roughened toward the apex.

The rhizome system is extensive and creeping, producing new shoots at regular intervals. A single genet can spread over several square meters, forming dense monospecific stands that exclude competing vegetation through both physical crowding and allelopathic [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]] content, though the species readily colonizes disturbed sites with exposed mineral soil.

## Ecological Role

Common spikerush is a primary producer in wetland ecosystems and contributes significantly to primary productivity in emergent marsh communities. Dense stands provide important cover and foraging habitat for waterfowl, shorebirds, and wading birds.

The dense rhizome network stabilizes wetland substrates and reduces erosion from wave action and water flow. This makes the species valuable in [[mollison-designers-wastewater-treatment-wetlands]]. Its root zone provides substrate for microbial communities involved in nitrogen and phosphorus removal from contaminated water.
