# 3,4-DMA

3,4-DMA (3,4-Dimethoxyamphetamine) is an amphetamine derivative with the same oxygen substitution pattern as the natural catecholamine neurotransmitters.

It is documented as entry #55 in PiHKAL by Alexander Shulgin.

## Chemical Identity

Full chemical name: 3,4-Dimethoxyamphetamine.

Molecular formula: C11H17NO2 (free base).

The 3,4-orientation of the two methoxy groups mirrors the positional substitution found in dopamine, norepinephrine, and epinephrine (noradrenaline and adrenaline).

This makes 3,4-DMA a direct analogue of the endogenous catecholamine system, with the hydroxyl groups replaced by methoxy groups.

The hydrochloride salt melts at 144-145 degrees Celsius as beautiful white crystals.

## Dosage and Duration

Oral active dosage: approximately 160 mg and above.

Intravenous dosage producing mescaline-like effects: approximately 0.04 mM/kg.

The compound appears to have moderate potency, falling somewhere between mescaline-entity|mescaline and mda-entity|MDA when administered orally.

Duration is not well characterized but appears to be moderate.

## Qualitative Effects

The qualitative comments for 3,4-DMA come from clinical studies, not from Shulgin's research group.

At 0.004 mM/kg intravenously, patients exhibited only "slight increase in psychiatric symptoms."

At 0.04 mM/kg intravenously (approximately 700 mg), a definite "mescaline-like" state was induced.

Symptoms included colored hallucinations of geometric figures and occasional structured forms.

One patient experienced visual distortions, notable after-imagery, feelings of unreality, and paranoid ideas.

Marked mydriasis and gross body tremors occurred in both patients.

## Military Research History

The clinical studies of 3,4-DMA were conducted by the chemical warfare group within the U.S. Army at the New York State Psychiatric Institute in the early 1960s.

The Edgewood Arsenal code name for 3,4-DMA was EA-1316.

These experiments were conducted on psychiatric patients without informed consent, representing what Shulgin described as "one of the most outrageous chapters in the books on military medicine."

The military tested many potential psychedelics by administering them to "innocent patients" without any thought of obtaining informed consent.

## The Catecholamine Question

Shulgin questioned the common assumption that the 3,4-oxygen pattern (catecholamine pattern) should produce the most interesting psychedelic activity.

The reasoning goes that since nature placed the oxygen groups in this position in brain neurotransmitters, compounds with the same pattern might be the most psychoactive.

Shulgin found this logic unconvincing, noting that both matching and non-matching substitution patterns could be used to explain activity.

The fundamental question of how psychedelic drugs produce their effects remains unanswered, regardless of the oxygen positioning.

## Synthesis

The synthesis begins with veratraldehyde (3,4-dimethoxybenzaldehyde), which is condensed with nitroethane.

The nitropropene intermediate crystallizes as yellow crystals with a melting point of 70-71 degrees Celsius.

Reduction with lithium aluminum hydride in ether yields 3,4-DMA as the free base.

An alternative synthesis route using n-amylamine as a catalyst instead of ammonium acetate gave a superior yield of the nitropropene intermediate (29.0 g vs 14.8 g from 33.2 g aldehyde).

## Essential Oils Connection

3,4-DMA is classified as one of the "Ten Essential Amphetamines," meaning it can theoretically arise from the amination of one of the ten essential oils found in the spice and herb trade.

In this case, the essential oil precursors are methyl eugenol and methyl isoeugenol.

This classification connects 3,4-DMA to the broader natural product chemistry of aromatic compounds.

## DMA Isomer Family

There are six possible dimethoxyamphetamine isomers.

The 3,4-isomer has always been the most appealing to life scientists due to its catecholamine-like pattern.

2,4-DMA and 2,5-DMA have been separately discussed and studied.

2,3-DMA, 2,6-DMA, and 3,5-DMA have been made and studied pharmacologically in animals but not in humans.

The bromination products of all six DMA isomers are discussed under META-DOB.

## Legal Status

3,4-DMA is likely controlled as a dimethoxyamphetamine derivative in most jurisdictions.

In the United States, it would likely fall under analogue drug legislation.

The compound has never been widely available as a recreational substance.

## Harm Reduction

The military studies used intravenous administration, which is significantly more dangerous than oral dosing.

The high doses required for oral activity (160+ mg) produce significant cardiovascular effects including tremors and mydriasis.

The compound has not been studied for long-term toxicity or neurotoxicity.
