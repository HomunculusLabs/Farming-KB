# Roundup (Glyphosate) Impact on Soil Biology

## Description

Roundup (active ingredient: glyphosate) is a broad-spectrum systemic herbicide whose impact on soil biological communities is a significant concern in biological farming. Elaine Ingham's field guide addresses Roundup as a chemical that is biologically detrimental to [[soil-food-web]], affecting beneficial microorganisms critical for nutrient cycling and plant health.

## Classification

- **Category**: Agricultural chemical - herbicide
- **Active ingredient**: Glyphosate (N-phosphonomethyl glycine)
- **Mode of action**: Inhibits EPSP synthase in the shikimate pathway
- **Impact classification**: Biologically detrimental

## Key Facts

- **Broad-spectrum effect**: While designed as a plant herbicide, glyphosate has documented effects on soil microorganisms including bacteria, fungi, and protozoa
- **Mycorrhizal damage**: Glyphosate can reduce mycorrhizal fungal colonization of plant roots, disrupting the critical plant-fungal symbiosis
- **Nutrient cycling disruption**: By affecting microbial communities, Roundup can impair decomposition and mineralization of organic matter
- **Chelation properties**: Glyphosate is a strong chelator of micronutrients (iron, manganese, zinc, copper), potentially making these nutrients unavailable to plants and microorganisms
- **Persistence**: While glyphosate adsorbs to soil particles, its breakdown product AMPA (aminomethylphosphonic acid) can persist for extended periods
- **Root exudate alteration**: Plants treated with glyphosate alter their root exudates, changing the rhizosphere microbial community

## Impact on Soil Food Web

### Direct Effects
- **Bacterial communities**: Shifts in bacterial community composition and diversity
- **Fungal communities**: Reduction in saprophytic and mycorrhizal fungal populations
- **Protozoa**: Reduced populations of beneficial protozoa that cycle nutrients by consuming bacteria
- **Nematodes**: Negative impacts on beneficial nematode populations

### Indirect Effects
- **Nutrient mineralization**: Reduced microbial activity leads to slower nutrient cycling
- **Disease suppression**: Loss of beneficial organisms reduces natural disease suppression
- **Soil structure**: Reduced fungal hyphae and glomalin production affects soil aggregation
- **Water infiltration**: Degraded soil structure reduces water infiltration and retention

## Relevance to Compost Tea and Biological Farming

Elaine Ingham specifically identifies Roundup among chemicals that are biologically detrimental:

- **Recovery challenge**: Soils treated with Roundup may require intensive biological remediation through compost tea applications and compost amendments
- **Compost tea role**: AACT can help restore biological diversity after chemical damage, but repeated Roundup applications will continue to undermine recovery
- **Integration issue**: Roundup use is fundamentally incompatible with biological farming relying on soil food web health
- **Alternative weed control**: Biological farming uses competitive exclusion, cover crops, and proper nutrient balance rather than herbicidal weed control

## Weed Control Through Biology

Ingham addresses root causes of weed pressure rather than using Roundup:

- **High nitrate levels**: Excess nitrate favors weed species; proper biology converts nitrogen to protein forms
- **Low calcium**: Calcium deficiency promotes weed growth; proper mineral balance favors desired crops
- **Anaerobic conditions**: Anaerobic soil conditions favor weeds; good soil biology maintains aerobic conditions
- **Competitive exclusion**: A healthy soil food web supports vigorous crop growth that outcompetes weeds

## Comparison with Other Detrimental Chemicals

| Chemical | Impact | Notes |
|----------|--------|-------|
| Roundup (glyphosate) | Biologically detrimental | Chelates micronutrients, damages mycorrhizae |
| Copper compounds | Toxic to microorganisms | Accumulates in soil over time |
| Sulfur | Fungicidal at high rates | Less persistent than copper |
| Lime sulfur | Broad toxicity | Damages beneficial and pathogenic organisms |
| Inorganic fertilizers (high rates) | Disrupts biology | Osmotic shock to organisms |

## Recovery Strategies

1. **Stop applications**: Discontinue Roundup and other biologically harmful chemicals
2. **Apply compost**: High-quality thermal compost reintroduces diverse microbial communities
3. **Brew AACT**: Apply compost tea to rapidly boost biological activity
4. **Mycorrhizal inoculation**: Reintroduce mycorrhizal fungi that may have been lost
5. **Cover crops**: Establish diverse cover crops to feed soil biology and build organic matter
6. **Monitor recovery**: Use soil biology testing to track the return of beneficial organisms
