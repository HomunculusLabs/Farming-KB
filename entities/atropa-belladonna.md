## Atropa belladonna L.

**Common names:** [[harner-european-witchcraft-hallucinogens]], folk medicine, and classical toxicology.
Native to Europe, North Africa, and parts of Asia, this perennial herbaceous
plant contains potent tropane alkaloids, principally [[hyoscyamine]],
and [[aact-foliar-sticker-spreaders-leaf-surface-attachment-ingham]] is dark green above and
paler beneath, with a slightly unpleasant odor when crushed.

The flowers are one of the most [[metzner-three-distinctive-features-shamanic-entheogenic-ceremonies]] of the plant. They are
bell-shaped, clearly displaying the characteristic corolla form of the
Nightshade family. The flowers are typically dull purplish-green or brownish-
purple in color, approximately 0.75 to 1 inch (2 to 2.5 centimeters) long,
borne singly or in pairs in the axils of the leaves.

The fruit is a glossy, black berry approximately 0.5 inches (1.25
centimeters) in diameter, resembling small cherries. The berries are
extremely toxic, particularly to children, who may be attracted by their
sweet taste and appealing appearance. All parts of the plant contain
tropane alkaloids, with the berries and leaves being the most commonly
encountered sources of accidental poisoning.

## Distribution and Habitat

Atropa belladonna is native to Europe, North Africa, and western Asia. It is
widely distributed across central and southern Europe, from the British
Isles and Scandinavia in the north to the Mediterranean region in the south.
The plant extends eastward through the Balkans, Turkey, and into the Caucasus
and Himalayan regions.

Deadly Nightshade typically grows in shaded or semi-shaded habitats,
including woodland edges, clearings, hedgerows, and disturbed ground. It
prefers rich, moist, calcareous soils and is often found in association
with other woodland plants. In some regions, it has become naturalized far
beyond its native range.
