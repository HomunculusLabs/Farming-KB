## Henri Michaux

## Description
Henri Michaux (1899–1984) was a Belgian-born French poet, writer, and painter, widely recognized for his experimental literary works and his investigations into consciousness through art and psychedelics. Leary credits Michaux with coining the phrase "retinal circus" to describe the kaleidoscopic visual phenomena that occur during psychedelic experiences.

## Classification
- **Category:** Person — Artist / Writer / Poet
- **Domain:** Literature, visual arts, psychedelic experience documentation
- **Era:** 20th century (1899–1984)

## Key Facts
- Born in Namur, Belgium; became a naturalized French citizen
- One of the most important French-language poets of the 20th century
- Experimented extensively with mescaline beginning in the 1950s
- Documented his mescaline experiences in *Miserable Miracle* (1956), which Leary cites as the source of "retinal circus"
- Also authored *The Infinite Turbulence* and other works on altered consciousness
- His artwork (India ink drawings) was directly influenced by his psychedelic visions
- His writing style was characterized by spare, precise language attempting to capture ineffable experience
- Refused the Grand Prix National des Lettres in 1965

## The "Retinal Circus" Phrase
Leary borrows Michaux's evocative term for **Vision 6** of the Second Bardo — the kaleidoscopic, ever-changing panorama of visual phenomena that fills consciousness during psychedelic experiences. Michaux's phrase captures the quality of endless, autonomous visual production that the unprepared mind finds overwhelming but the prepared mind finds delightful. The "circus" metaphor suggests both spectacle and chaos.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology
Michaux's documentation of mescaline visions provides detailed phenomenological descriptions that are applicable to understanding [[psilocybin]] visual effects. His work demonstrates that the visual phenomena of psychedelics are not random hallucinations but organized, patterned experiences with consistent features across individuals. Artists and writers working with psilocybin often report similar visual cascades.
