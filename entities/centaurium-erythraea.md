## Centaurium erythraea

## Overview

Centaurium erythraea, commonly known as common centaury, European centaury, or feverwort, is an annual or biennial herbaceous plant in the family Gentianaceae. It is native to Europe, western Asia, and North Africa.

The species has been used in European herbal medicine for over two millennia as a digestive tonic and bitter stimulant. Its dried flowering tops are the source of the pharmacopoeial drug Centaurii herba.

Centaury derives its common name from the centaur Chiron of Greek mythology, who reputedly used the plant to heal wounds. The specific epithet erythraea refers to the reddish coloration of the stems and flowers.

The plant is commercially harvested from both wild and cultivated sources for the herbal medicine and bitter liqueur industries. It remains one of the most widely used bitter herbs in European phytotherapy.

The intense bitterness of centaury is perceptible at dilutions as low as 1:10,000. This extreme bitterness is the basis of its classification as a "simple bitter" in pharmacopoeial monographs.

Centaurii herba has been included in the pharmacopoeias of numerous European countries and is listed in the European Pharmacopoeia. [[centaurium-umbellatum]] and Erythraea centaurium.

## Distribution and Habitat

Centaurium erythraea is widely distributed across Europe from the British Isles to the Caucasus. It extends southward through the Mediterranean region to North Africa and eastward into western and central Asia.

The species occupies a broad range of open habitats including grasslands, heathland, coastal dunes, woodland margins, and disturbed ground. It prefers sandy or calcareous soils with low to moderate fertility.

It is found from sea level to approximately 2,000 meters elevation. The plant is characteristic of nutrient-poor, open habitats and declines under heavy shading or nutrient enrichment.

## Pharmacology and Traditional Use

Centaurium erythraea is classified as a bitter tonic in European herbal medicine. The secoiridoid glycosides stimulate bitter taste receptors on the tongue, reflexively increasing gastric secretion and digestive motility.

The European Medicines Agency recognizes Centaurii herba as a traditional herbal medicine for the relief of temporary loss of appetite and dyspeptic complaints. It is typically administered as a tea, liquid extract, or tincture.

Traditional uses extend beyond digestion to include treatment of fever, anemia, snakebite, and wound healing. The species name "feverwort" reflects its historical use as an antipyretic in European folk medicine.

Clinical studies on the digestive effects of centaury extracts have demonstrated increased salivary and gastric secretion. The bitter response is mediated through the gustatory nerve and vagal reflex pathways.
