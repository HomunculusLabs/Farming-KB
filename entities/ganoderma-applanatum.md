# Ganoderma applanatum

**Type:** Species (Fungus)
**Category:** Wood-Decaying Polypore Basidiomycete
**Also known as:** Artist's conk, artist's bracket

## Overview
Ganoderma applanatum is a large, perennial polypore basidiomycete
that causes [[fungicolous-fungi]], including
Sporophagomyces chrysostomus, and serves as a substrate for a diverse
community of mycoparasites and successor fungi.

## Taxonomy and Classification
- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Polyporales
- **Family:** Ganodermataceae
- **Genus:** Ganoderma
- **Species:** G. applanatum

## Key Details

### Morphology
- Basidiocarps are perennial, sessile to effused-reflexed,
  typically 10 to 50 cm or more in width
- Upper surface is concentrically zoned, with a hard, crusty
  cuticle ranging from gray-brown to reddish-brown
- Pore surface is white to cream, bruising distinctly brown upon
  handling or scratching
- Pores are small (4 to 6 per mm), circular, and the tube layer
  is stratified in perennial specimens
- Context is corky to woody, with a dark brown to cinnamon
  brown color
- Basidiospores are brown, ovate, with a truncated apex,
  approximately 7 to 10 by 5 to 7 micrometers, with a double
  wall

### Ecology and Habitat
- Found on standing and fallen trunks of hardwood trees,
  particularly oak, maple, beech, and willow
- Also occurs on some conifer species
- Causes a slow-progressing white rot, preferentially degrading
  lignin over cellulose
- Fruit bodies persist for multiple years, adding new tube layers
  each [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]] of [[gonatobotryum-fuscum]] can grow on Ganoderma among other
  poroid fungi
- A yeast isolated from a G. applanatum sporocarp was identified
  as [[biodiversity-of-fungi-pcr-molecular-methods-fungal-diversity]] (Prillinger et al.
  1989)

### Succession Dynamics
- Channels left in wood by mycelial strands of G. applanatum
  may serve as canals for hyphae of succeeding fungi
- Successor fungi may fruit on dead basidiocarps, complicating
  interpretation of parasitic versus saprotrophic succession
- An observed succession in fruiting does not necessarily indicate
  selective parasitism (Niemela et al. 1995)
- The species complex of Ganodermataceae hosts is particularly
  targeted by Sporophagomyces chrysostomus
