## Overview

*Campanula sabatia* De Not., the Savona bellflower, is a perennial herb endemic to the Liguria region of northwestern Italy. It is one of the rarest and most threatened species in the genus *Campanula*.

The plant produces slender stems bearing clusters of pale blue to lavender campanulate flowers in late spring. Its restricted range and small population size have earned it Endangered status on the IUCN Red List.

It grows to 15–40 cm tall, forming loose clumps from a slender woody rootstock. The species is named after the Sabatia district of Liguria, where it was first collected.

First described by Agostino Naccari's colleague Giuseppe De Notaris in 1844, it has been the subject of intensive conservation efforts. It represents one of Italy's most significant floristic endemics.

The species has become a flagship for plant conservation in Liguria, featured in regional environmental education programs. Public awareness campaigns have helped reduce illegal collection pressure on wild populations.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

*Campanula sabatia* belongs to section *Homentoloba* of the genus *Campanula*, a group of mostly Mediterranean species with hairy ovaries. It is closely related to *Campanula macrorhiza* and other Ligurian endemics.

Stems are slender, erect to ascending, pubescent with short spreading hairs. Basal leaves are ovate to cordate, 1.5–3 cm long, crenate-dentate, and form a loose rosette that may wither before flowering.

Cauline leaves are progressively smaller, sessile, and lanceolate, decreasing in size toward the apex. Both leaf surfaces bear a sparse indumentum of simple, multicellular trichomes.

Flowers are campanulate, 1.5–2.5 cm long, pale blue to lavender, arranged in loose terminal panicles or racemes of 3–8 blooms. The corolla is divided to approximately one-third of its length into five acute lobes.

The calyx lobes are linear-lanceolate, pubescent, approximately half the corolla length. The ovary is inferior, densely pubescent — a key diagnostic character for section *Homentoloba*.

Capsules are ovoid, dehiscing by three small basal pores near the calyx base. Seeds are ellipsoid, light brown, and approximately 0.6–0.8 mm in length.

The chromosome number is 2n=34 (diploid), consistent with the base number x=17 for the genus.

The species can be confused with *Campanula macrorhiza* from the Maritime Alps, but differs in its more compact habit and broader corolla lobes. The pubescent ovary distinguishes both species from the common *[[cold-frame-construction]].

Propagation is by seed, which benefits from a period of cold stratification. Germination is often irregular and seedling survival can be low without careful attention to drainage.

## Conservation

*Campanula sabatia* is listed as Endangered (EN) on the IUCN Red List under criterion D (population size estimated at fewer than 250 mature individuals in some assessments). It is also protected under Appendix I of the Bern Convention and the EU Habitats Directive.

Primary threats include habitat loss from coastal development, road construction, and quarrying. Tourism pressure on cliff habitats and competition from invasive species also contribute to population decline.

Ex situ conservation collections are maintained at several European botanical gardens, including the Royal Botanic Gardens Kew and the Hanbury Botanical Gardens in La Mortola. Seed banking provides additional insurance against extinction.
