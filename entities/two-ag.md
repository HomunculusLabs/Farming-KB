## 2-Arachidonoylglycerol (2-AG)

**Type:** Chemical / Endocannabinoid
**Category:** Endogenous cannabinoid (produced by the human body)
**Also known as:** 2-AG, 2-arachidonoyl glycerol

## Overview
2-Arachidonoylglycerol (2-AG) is an endogenous cannabinoid produced
naturally by the human body, alongside anandamide (AEA). It is one of
five identified endocannabinoids and is considered the most abundant
endocannabinoid in the central nervous system. 2-AG functions as a full
agonist at both CB1 and CB2 [[cannabinoid-receptors-cb1-cb2]], playing critical
roles in immune function, pain signaling, and neuroprotection.

## Key Details

### Chemical Properties
- Full name: 2-arachidonoyl glycerol
- Endogenous lipid mediator
- Full agonist at both CB1 and CB2 receptors
- Most abundant endocannabinoid in the brain
- Broken down primarily by monoacylglycerol lipase (MAGL)
- Also metabolized by cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) and other enzymes

### Five Identified Endocannabinoids
The human body produces at least five distinct endocannabinoids:
1. Anandamide (N-arachidonoylethanolamide) (AEA)
2. 2-arachidonoyl glycerol (2-AG)
3. 2-arachidonoyl-glyceryl-ether ([[staycare-chlorophenol-degradation-pathways-white-rot-fungi]].

### Biological Function
2-AG is produced on-demand from membrane lipid precursors and serves as
a retrograde signaling molecule in synaptic transmission. Key functions
include:
- Retrograde neurotransmitter signaling at CB1 receptors
- [[tetrahydrocannabinol]]
- [[lysergic-acid-amide-containing-plants-halpern]] hydrolase (FAAH)
