## Overview

**Raphia taedigera** (moriche palm, raffia palm) is a large, clustering palm species native to tropical Africa and the Neotropics.

It is the primary source of commercial raffia fiber, harvested from the epidermis of young leaflets.

The species can reach heights of 15–25 meters and forms extensive stands in freshwater swamps and tidal zones.

Raphia taedigera is ecologically important as a mangrove-associate and provides critical habitat for wildlife.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Raphia taedigera** (Mart.) belongs to the family Arecaceae, subfamily Calamoideae, tribe Raphieae.

The genus Raphia comprises approximately 20 species distributed across tropical Africa and Madagascar.

The trunk is massive, solitary or clustered, reaching 15–25 meters in height and 30–50 centimeters in diameter.

It is covered with persistent leaf bases and prominent, dark brown leaf scars.

Leaves are the largest of any palm, reaching 15–20 meters in length including the petiole.

Each leaf bears up to 200 pairs of leaflets arranged regularly along the rachis.

Young expanding leaves produce a whitish epidermal layer that is stripped to yield commercial raffia fiber.

The petiole is heavily armed with rows of dark, recurved spines along both margins.

Inflorescences are massive, pendulous, and terminal, reaching 2–3 meters in length.

The palm is hapaxanthic, flowering once and dying after [[query-how-do-i-prune-brambles-for-maximum-fruit-production]].

Fruits are exceptionally large, scaly drupes up to 8–10 centimeters long, brown and covered in glossy scales.

## Distribution and Habitat

Raphia taedigera has a disjunct distribution across two continents.

In Africa, it occurs from Senegal eastward through the Gulf of Guinea countries to Angola and the Congo Basin.

In the Americas, it is found along the Caribbean and Pacific coasts from Nicaragua to Colombia and Ecuador.

The species grows in freshwater swamps, tidal estuaries, riverine floodplains, and mangrove-fringe habitats.

It thrives in permanently waterlogged or seasonally inundated soils with high organic content.

Elevation range is from sea level to approximately 200 meters.

The palm tolerates brackish water conditions, allowing it to colonize mangrove transition zones.

It often forms monodominant stands in low-lying swamp forests behind mangrove belts.

[[neurotropic-fungi-in-central-america]], it is particularly associated with coastal plain wetlands and river deltas.

## Cultivation and Uses

Raffia fiber is harvested by stripping the epidermis from the undersides of young, unexpanded leaflets.

Each leaf can yield up to 1–2 meters of usable fiber strips.

The fiber is air-dried, sorted by quality, and used for weaving, basketry, rope-making, and textile crafts.

Raffia is exported globally as a natural tying and weaving material for horticulture, fashion, and packaging.

The palm sap is tapped for wine production (palm wine) across West and Central Africa.

Sap collection involves cutting the inflorescence and collecting the flowing juice in containers.
