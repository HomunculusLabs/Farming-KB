# Psilocybin

## Overview

**Psilocybin** (3-[2-(dimethylamino)ethyl]-1H-indol-4-yl dihydrogen phosphate) is a naturally occurring psychedelic prodrug and tryptamine alkaloid produced by 200+ fungal species in *Psilocybe* and related genera. Upon ingestion, alkaline phosphatase dephosphorylates psilocybin to **psilocin** (4-hydroxy-DMT), the pharmacologically active metabolite. Psilocybin is classified as a Schedule I controlled substance internationally, though its legal status is under increasing review as clinical evidence for therapeutic applications accumulates.

## Chemical Properties

- **Molecular Formula:** C₁₂H₁₇N₂O₄P | **MW:** 284.25 g/mol | **CAS:** 520-52-5
- **Structure:** Tryptamine derivative with indole ring, phosphate ester at position 4, and dimethylaminoethyl side chain.
- **Appearance:** White to off-white crystalline powder.
- **Solubility:** Water-soluble; slightly soluble in ethanol; insoluble in non-polar solvents.
- **Stability:** Degrades with heat, light, and alkaline conditions. Store at -20°C in amber vials.

### Biosynthesis

From L-tryptophan through four enzymatic steps: decarboxylation → tryptamine, N-methylation → DMT, hydroxylation → psilocin, phosphorylation → psilocybin. The *psi* gene cluster was characterized in *P. cubensis* by Fricke et al. (2017) and is believed acquired via horizontal gene transfer from soil bacteria.

## Pharmacokinetics

- **Absorption:** Rapid GI absorption. Peak psilocin plasma in 40–90 minutes. Oral bioavailability ~50%.
- **Distribution:** Readily crosses blood-brain barrier. Volume of distribution ~2.5 L/kg.
- **Metabolism:** Hepatic. Primary pathway: glucuronidation to psilocin-O-glucuronide (UGT1A10, UGT2B7). Secondary: MAO-A deamination to 4-HIAA.
- **Elimination:** Renal. Psilocin half-life ~2–3 hours. Complete elimination within 24–48 hours.

## Pharmacodynamics

Psilocin is a **serotonin receptor agonist**; 5-HT₂A is the primary mediator of psychedelic effects:

| Receptor | Ki (nM) | Activity |
|----------|---------|----------|
| 5-HT₂A | 6–12 | Full agonist |
| 5-HT₂C | 8–25 | Agonist |
| 5-HT₁A | 50–200 | Weak agonist |
| 5-HT₂B | 10–30 | Agonist |

**Key mechanisms:**
- **Glutamate release:** 5-HT₂A activation increases cortical glutamate via PLC → IP₃/DAG signaling.
- **DMN disruption:** Reduces default mode network connectivity, correlating with "ego dissolution."
- **Cross-modal connectivity:** Increases connectivity between normally segregated brain regions.
- **BDNF upregulation:** Increases neurotrophic factor expression, contributing to neuroplastic changes.

Ketanserin (5-HT₂A antagonist) completely blocks subjective effects without altering psilocin plasma levels.

## Subjective Effects and Dose Response

| Dose (*P. cubensis*) | Psilocybin (mg) | Experience |
|----------------------|-----------------|------------|
| 0.5–1.0 g | 2–5 | Mild enhancement, subtle visuals |
| 1.0–2.5 g | 5–15 | Moderate psychedelic, prominent visuals/emotion |
| 2.5–5.0 g | 15–30 | Strong psychedelic, possible ego dissolution |
| 5.0+ g | 30+ | Intense mystical-type experience |

**Timeline:** Onset 20–60 min, come-up 60–120 min, peak 2–4 hours, comedown 4–6 hours. Aftereffects may persist 12–72 hours.

## Therapeutic Applications

### Treatment-Resistant Depression

Carhart-Harris et al. (2016, 2021) demonstrated rapid, sustained antidepressant effects. COMPASS Pathways Phase IIb (2022): single 25 mg dose with psychological support produced significant MADRS reductions at 3 weeks, maintained at 12 weeks. Mechanism hypothesized: DMN reset, enhanced emotional processing, cognitive flexibility.

### End-of-Life Anxiety and Depression

Griffiths et al. (2016, Johns Hopkins): single dose with psychotherapy significantly reduced depression/anxiety in terminal cancer patients. Effects persisted at 6-month and 4.5-year follow-up. Participants rated it among the most meaningful experiences of their lives.

### Substance Use Disorders

Bogenschutz et al. (2015, 2021): psilocybin-assisted therapy significantly reduced alcohol dependence, with abstinence rates >2× placebo at 36 weeks. Research ongoing for nicotine, cocaine, and opioid use disorders.

### OCD

Moreno et al. (2006): open-label study found acute OCD symptom reduction in all participants, lasting hours to days.

## Legal Status

**International:** Schedule I (1971 UN Convention). Exceptions include Netherlands (truffles legal), Portugal (decriminalized), Canada (Special Access Programme since 2022), and Australia (rescheduled to Schedule 8 for treatment-resistant depression, July 2023).

**United States:** Federal Schedule I. FDA rejected COMPASS NDA in 2024, requesting additional Phase III data. State-level: decriminalized in Oregon, Colorado, California, Washington, Massachusetts, and numerous municipalities. Oregon's Measure 109 (2020) established licensed psilocybin therapy; Colorado's Prop 122 (2022) created a regulated natural medicine program.

## Harm Reduction

### Toxicity

Exceptionally high safety margin: LD₅₀ (IV, rat) 285 mg/kg — ~100–200× typical dose. No verified human fatalities from overdose. Primary risk is accidental injury during altered states.

### Psychological Risks

- **Challenging experiences:** Anxiety, paranoia, confusion, especially in unprepared individuals or unsafe settings.
- **HPPD:** Hallucinogen Persisting Perception Disorder — rare (1–4.2%), persistent visual disturbances.
- **Psychosis risk:** Elevated in personal/family history of schizophrenia or bipolar disorder.
- **Contraindications:** Active psychosis, bipolar (manic phase), severe personality disorders, pregnancy, lithium, MAOIs.

### Harm Reduction Principles

Use in safe, familiar environment with trusted sitter. Start low, go slow. Avoid alcohol, stimulants, other psychedelics. Screen for psychiatric history. Allow 1–2 weeks integration between sessions. "Set and setting" are the most important determinants.
