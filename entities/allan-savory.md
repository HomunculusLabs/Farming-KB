# Allan Savory

## Overview

Allan Savory (born September 15, 1935, in Bulawayo, Southern Rhodesia, now
Zimbabwe) is a Zimbabwean ecologist, livestock farmer, and environmentalist
best known as the originator of [[savory-holistic-management-overview]] and Holistic Planned
Grazing. His central thesis, developed over decades of observation and
experimentation in the grasslands of Africa and beyond, argues that properly
managed livestock can reverse desertification, restore degraded grasslands,
sequester atmospheric carbon into soils, and improve water cycles. His 2013 TED
Talk has been viewed over 8 million times and brought his ideas to a global
audience.

## Biography

### Early Life and Education

Born in Bulawayo, Southern Rhodesia, Savory was raised in a family with deep
connections to the African landscape. His childhood experiences in the bushveld
instilled an early fascination with ecology, wildlife, and the relationships
between land, animals, and people. He attended the University of Natal in South
Africa, studying zoology, botany, and ecology.

### Early Career in Rhodesia

Savory began his career as a game ranger in what is now Hwange National Park,
where he managed wildlife populations and developed culling programs. His key
observation was that land protected from grazing by fencing did not necessarily
recover — in many cases, it continued to deteriorate. Conversely, land under
properly managed herds of large herbivores often showed signs of recovery. This
observation formed the foundation of his lifelong work challenging conventional
[[mollison-pdc-range-management-wildlife]] theory.

### Political Involvement and Exile

Savory served as a member of the Rhodesian Parliament, where he worked on
environmental policy. His opposition to the white minority government's
policies eventually led to his exile. He moved to the United States and later
to other countries, continuing to develop and teach his management framework
while working with ranchers and land managers across multiple continents.

## Holistic Management Framework

### Core Philosophy

Holistic Management is a decision-making framework that treats the ecosystem
as an interconnected whole. Practitioners define a [[savory-holistic-goal-definition]] encompassing
quality of life, forms of production, and the future resource base that must
be sustained. All management decisions are tested against this holistic goal
using a set of seven testing guidelines that consider social, economic, and
ecological factors simultaneously.

### Holistic Planned Grazing

The grazing component is the most widely known aspect:

- **High animal density, [[savory-short-duration-grazing-and-cell-systems]]**: Livestock are concentrated in
  large herds that move frequently, mimicking natural herding herbivores.
  Stocking density is high but duration of occupation is brief.
- **Long recovery periods**: Plants are given extended recovery periods of
  60-120 days or more before being regrazed.
- **Complete rest is destructive**: A central tenet is that complete rest of
  grasslands from grazing is as destructive as chronic overgrazing. Grasses
  co-evolved with large herding herbivores and require periodic disturbance.
- **Planning and monitoring**: Grazing plans are developed annually based on
  monitoring data, with real-time adjustments for changing conditions.

## Scientific Evidence and Debate

### Supporting Evidence

Multiple long-term case studies have documented improvements in soil organic
carbon, water infiltration rates, vegetation cover, and species diversity on
ranches practicing Holistic Planned Grazing. Research at Texas A&M, Colorado
State, and Michigan State has shown that high-density, short-duration grazing
improves soil aggregate stability, microbial biomass, and [[comparison-nutrient-cycling-vs-nutrient-dense-gardening]].
The Africa Centre for Holistic Management in Zimbabwe has demonstrated
restoration of severely degraded land through planned grazing.
