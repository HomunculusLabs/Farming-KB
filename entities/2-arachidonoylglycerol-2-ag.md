# 2-Arachidonoylglycerol (2-AG)

**Type:** Chemical Compound (Endocannabinoid)
**Category:** Endogenous Cannabinoid
**Also known as:** 2-AG, 2-arachidonoyl glycerol

## Overview
2-Arachidonoylglycerol (2-AG) is one of the two primary endogenous
cannabinoids (endocannabinoids) produced naturally by the human body,
alongside anandamide (AEA). First identified in 1995, 2-AG is now
recognized as the most abundant endocannabinoid in the body, present
at concentrations significantly higher than anandamide in most
tissues. It functions as a full agonist at both CB1 and CB2 receptors
and plays a central role in the endocannabinoid system's regulation of
numerous physiological processes.

## Key Details

### Chemical Properties
- **Molecular formula:** C23H38O4
- **Classification:** Endocannabinoid (monoacylglycerol)
- **Molecular weight:** 378.5 g/mol
- **Abundance:** The most abundant endocannabinoid in mammalian
  tissues, typically found at concentrations far exceeding anandamide
- **Structure:** A glycerol molecule esterified with arachidonic acid

### Endocannabinoid System Role
2-AG is a full agonist at both CB1 and CB2 cannabinoid receptors,
meaning it fully activates these receptors upon binding. This
distinguishes it from anandamide, which is a partial agonist. 2-AG is
produced on-demand from membrane lipid precursors in response to
cellular signals, rather than being stored preformed. Its rapid
synthesis and degradation (primarily by MAGL, monoacylglycerol
lipase) allow for precise temporal control of endocannabinoid
signaling.

### Physiological Functions
- **Immune regulation:** 2-AG is a key immunomodulator through CB2
  receptor activation on immune cells, regulating inflammation and
  immune cell migration
- **Neuroprotection:** As a CB1 agonist in the central nervous system,
  2-AG helps protect neurons from excessive excitation and damage
- **Pain signaling:** Modulates pain perception through both CB1 and
  CB2 pathways, contributing to the body's natural analgesic systems
- **Appetite and metabolism:** Involved in the hypothalamic regulation
  of food intake and energy balance
- **Neuroinflammation:** Regulates microglial cell activity in the
  brain, playing a role in neuroinflammatory conditions

### Relationship to Cannabis
2-AG shares the same receptor targets as THC, the primary psychoactive
constituent of cannabis. THC mimics the action of 2-AG (and anandamide)
at CB1 and CB2 receptors. Understanding 2-AG's physiological roles
helps explain why cannabis has therapeutic effects across such a wide
range of conditions, as the plant cannabinoids effectively modulate
the same signaling pathways that 2-AG regulates naturally.

### Five Identified Endocannabinoids
The human body produces at least five distinct endocannabinoids:
1. Anandamide (N-arachidonoylethanolamide, AEA)
2. 2-Arachidonoyl glycerol (2-AG)
3. 2-Arachidonoyl-glyceryl-ether (Noladin ether)
4. O-Arachidonoyl-ethanolamine (Virodhamine)
5. N-Arachidonoyl-dopamine (NADA)

2-AG is by far the most abundant and pharmacologically significant
of these five molecules.

## Relevance
2-AG is the most important signaling molecule in the human
endocannabinoid system by virtue of its abundance and full agonist
activity at both receptor types. Its discovery helped establish the
endocannabinoid system as a fundamental physiological regulatory
network. For cannabis medicine, 2-AG is relevant because plant
cannabinoids essentially modulate the same pathways that 2-AG
governs, and the therapeutic effects of cannabis can be understood
through the lens of enhancing or supplementing 2-AG signaling.
