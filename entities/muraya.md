# Muraya

## Description

The muraya is a type of shaman in the Amazonian vegetalismo hierarchy, ranking above ordinary vegetalistas but below bancos and sumirunas. Murayas are distinguished by their mastery of perfume-based spiritual power and their ability to travel to subaquatic worlds. They are considered practitioners of great spiritual power who specialize in working with water spirits and deep healing work.

## Classification

- **Domain**: Amazonian vegetalismo / mestizo [[shamanism]]
- **Category**: Shamanic rank/title, mid-to-high level practitioner
- **Hierarchy**: Above vegetalista, below banco and sumiruna
- **Specialization**: Perfume mastery, subaquatic travel, water spirit work

## Key Facts

- The muraya dominates through perfume, possessing great spiritual power. Perfume and camphorated water are key tools in their practice, blown onto patients during ayahuasca sessions.
- Murayas use the [[yakumama]] to descend to the deepest parts of rivers in visionary travel, where the great serpent transforms into a beautiful submarine as their vehicle.
- They are received by mermaids who serve as guides to golden cities beneath the waters when entering the subaquatic world.
- Their guardian animals include the tibemama (osprey, Pandion haliaetus), sarara (Anhinga anhinga), and tibe negro (Neotropic cormorant).
- Murayas work alongside bancos (who possess diamond swords and flying wheels) and sumirunas in healing ceremonies.
- They receive the powers of [[queen-inamullo]] through their mouths, obtaining colored [[mariri]] (magical phlegm) cords including the yura-mariri (white) and puka-mariri (red).

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

The muraya's specialization in aromatic and perfume-based medicine connects directly to the use of fragrant plants in Amazonian healing. Plants such as albahaca (Ocimum micranthum), alcanfor (Zanthoxylum sp.), and various aromatic species are central to the muraya's practice. The muraya's focus on perfume as a spiritual technology reflects the Amazonian understanding that volatile aromatic compounds in plants carry spiritual and [[raw-cannabis-thca-non-psychoactive-therapeutic-properties]]. For ethnobotanical research, muraya practices highlight the importance of aromatic plants in traditional Amazonian pharmacology and the role of olfaction in plant-mediated healing experiences.
