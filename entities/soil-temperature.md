## Soil Temperature

## Description

Soil temperature is a critical environmental factor that directly controls the rate of plant growth, seed germination, and root development. Unlike air temperature, soil temperature determines the actual metabolic speed of root systems and consequently the entire plant. Understanding soil temperature dynamics is essential for timing plantings and optimizing growth rates.

## Classification

- **Category**: Environmental growing condition
- **Measurement**: Degrees Fahrenheit or Celsius
- **Instrument**: Soil thermometer
- **Depth measured**: Top few inches (varies by crop)
- **Importance**: Primary growth rate determinant

## Key Facts

- Plant growth is mainly done at night — plants store energy as sugar during the day, then burn that sugar to grow at night.
- For every 10F (5C) increase in temperature, growth speed doubles exponentially.
- At 50F (10C) nighttime low: plant grows one size larger overnight.
- At 60F (15C): plant doubles that growth (two sizes).
- At 70F (21C): plant quadruples the growth (four sizes).
- At 80F (26C): plant grows eight sizes overnight — explains hearing corn growing on hot nights.
- Some seeds require warm soils to germinate and will rot if too cold.
- Root growth speed is also determined by soil temperature.
- A soil thermometer is one of the most educational tools a gardener can own.

## Practical Implications

1. In cool climates, cold soil is a major limiting factor — not just air temperature.
2. Sowing too early in cold soil leads to poor germination and wasted seed.
3. Raised beds warm faster in spring, extending the effective growing season.
4. Mulches insulate soil — keeping it cooler in summer but delaying spring warming.
5. Choose varieties that germinate in lower soil temperatures for cool climates.

## Relevance to Cultivation

Soil temperature is perhaps the most underappreciated factor in garden success. The exponential relationship between temperature and growth rate means small differences in soil warming have enormous effects on productivity.
