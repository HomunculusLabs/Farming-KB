# Pertusaria amara (Bitter Wart Lichen)

## Overview

*Pertusaria amara* is a crustose lichen in the family Pertusariaceae, widely recognized for its intensely bitter taste caused by the depside picrolichenic acid. It forms pale grey to white, warty thalli on the bark of mature trees and occasionally on siliceous rock.

The species is among the most chemically distinctive lichens in its genus, containing bitter depsides absent from most other *Pertusaria* species. Its name "amara" directly references this bitterness.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Pertusaria amara* has a broadly [[polypore-fungi-eastern-north-america]] America, primarily in the [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]] of [[fagus-sylvatica]]*, *Acer platanoides*, *Fraxinus excelsior*, and *Ulmus glabra*.

Occasional substrate extensions include old wooden fence posts, weathered timber, and siliceous rock outcrops in humid, sheltered locations. It is most abundant in old-growth forests with high bark pH and good air quality.

The species is characteristically found in well-lit situations on trunks and main branches, from the base up to approximately 2 meters. It avoids deep shade and extremely exposed, desiccation-prone sites.

## Chemistry and Secondary Metabolites

*Pertusaria amara* is chemically defined by the presence of picrolichenic acid, a bitter depside that is the primary compound responsible for its distinctive taste. Picrolichenic acid occurs in concentrations of 2–8% dry weight.

Secondary compounds detected include stictic acid, cryptostictic acid, and norstictic acid in some populations. The stictic acid chemotype is more common in Mediterranean populations.

The bitter depside profile is relatively unusual within *Pertusaria*, as most species in the genus produce terpenoids or orcinol depsidones rather than bitter depsides. This chemical distinctiveness makes spot tests useful for field identification.

Spot test reactions: K+ yellow to orange (picrolichenic acid), C−, KC−, P+ pale yellow, UV−. The K+ yellow reaction is particularly diagnostic and separates *P. amara* from most morphologically similar congeners.

## Traditional and Medicinal Uses

*Pertusaria amara* has a documented history of use as a bitter tonic in Scandinavian and Central European herbal medicine. The lichen was harvested from tree bark, dried, and steeped to produce bitter infusions.

In Sweden and Norway, bitter lichen preparations were traditionally consumed before meals to stimulate appetite and aid digestion. The practice was common in rural communities through the 19th century.

The active principles responsible for the bitter tonic effect are the picrolichenic acid compounds. These depsides stimulate gustatory bitter receptors, triggering salivation and gastric secretion.

Historical pharmaceutical texts list *P. amara* under the name "Cetraria amara" or "bitter tree lichen" as an ingredient in compound bitters and stomachic preparations. Commercial harvesting continued into the early 20th century.

No modern clinical studies have validated the traditional uses. The species is not currently used in mainstream phytotherapy, though it retains interest as a historical ethnobotanical resource.

⚠️ Lichen compounds can cause contact dermatitis in sensitive individuals. Internal use should be approached with caution due to limited toxicological data.
