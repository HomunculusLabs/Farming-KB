## Overview

**Caryota urens** (fishtail palm, toddy palm, or kitul palm) is a species of flowering palm native to tropical South and [[fungal-calcium-oxalate-crystals]] that are severely irritating to the skin and mucous membranes.

The genus name Caryota derives from the Greek word "karyon" meaning nut, referring to the hard-shelled fruits. The specific epithet "urens" means burning in Latin, a reference to the caustic oxalate crystals found throughout the plant.

## Distribution and Habitat

Caryota urens is native to India, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh, Myanmar, Thailand, and the Andaman Islands. It is most abundant in the Western Ghats of India and the wet zone of Sri Lanka.

The species grows naturally in moist deciduous and semi-evergreen forests at elevations from sea level to 1,500 meters. ## Cultivation and Tapping

Propagation is from seed, which germinates within 2–6 months under warm, moist conditions. Seeds lose viability rapidly and should be sown within 2–3 months of collection from ripe fruits.

Seedlings require 2–3 years of nursery care before field planting at spacings of 5–8 meters. Stems begin producing sap-yielding inflorescences at 12–15 years and continue for 2–4 years before dying.

Toddy tapping involves cutting the tip of the developing inflorescence and collecting the exuding sap in clay pots or bamboo containers. Traditional tappers use curved knives to make precise cuts, refreshing the wound twice daily.

A single stem can yield 5–20 liters of sap per day during peak flow. Total annual yield per clump depends on the number of flowering stems, typically ranging from 500 to 1,500 liters.

Fresh sap is highly perishable and must be processed within hours of collection. It is consumed directly as a sweet beverage, fermented into toddy, or boiled to produce jaggery (palm sugar).

## Uses and Ethnobotany

Palm sugar (jaggery) from Caryota urens is considered superior in quality to sugarcane-derived products in many South Asian culinary traditions. The sugar has a rich, caramelized flavor and commands premium prices in domestic and international markets.

Toddy from the fishtail palm is consumed fresh or fermented into vinegar. In Sri Lanka, fermented toddy is distilled to produce arrack, a traditional spirit with significant cultural and commercial value.

The palm heart (terminal bud) is edible and considered a delicacy. Harvesting the palm heart kills the stem, so it is typically collected only from stems that are about to flower or have been damaged.

The pith from felled stems is rich in starch and is processed into sago-like flour. This flour is used in traditional puddings, flatbreads, and as a thickening agent in curries throughout South Asia.

In traditional Ayurvedic medicine, the roots are used to treat digestive complaints and joint pain. These include gallic acid, catechin, and epicatechin, which contribute to reported anti-inflammatory and hepatoprotective properties.
