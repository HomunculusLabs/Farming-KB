# Lespedeza bicolor

**Type:** Species
**Category:** Leguminous shrub
**Also known as:** Miracle plant, bush clover, shrub lespedeza

## Overview
Lespedeza bicolor is a leguminous shrub growing to 3 meters, native to
Japan and widely cultivated in Korea for soil reclamation. Named the
"miracle plant" for its remarkable ability to restore degraded land, it
combines efficient nitrogen fixation, high-protein fodder, quality
firewood, and medicinal properties in a single hardy shrub.

## Key Details

### Botany
- **Family:** Fabaceae (Papilionaceae)
- **Form:** Leguminous shrub to 3 m
- **Origin:** Native to Japan; widely grown in Korea
- **Root system:** Long shallow roots spreading along the ground surface

### Nitrogen Fixation
- Root nodules are very efficient at nitrogen fixation
- The spreading root system distributes fixed nitrogen across a wide
  area
- Makes nitrogen available to companion plants and [[soil-food-web]]

### Growth Conditions
- Adaptable to a range of temperate conditions
- Tolerant of poor, degraded, and nutrient-deficient soils
- Used specifically for soil reclamation and land rehabilitation
- Planted as a nurse crop to pine trees in Korean forestry

### Fodder Value
- High protein content makes it excellent livestock feed
- Seeds are good poultry feed
- Readily browsed by sheep, goats, and cattle

### Firewood Production
- High quality firewood
- In Korea, yields of 2.9 tonnes of firewood per hectare in the first
  year are typical
- Rapid biomass production for energy applications

### Bee Forage
- Considered a good honey plant
- Late-season flowers provide nectar when other sources are scarce

### Medicinal Uses
- Leaves and roots used in traditional Japanese medicine
- Contains isoflavones and other bioactive compounds

### Management
- Fast establishment on degraded sites
- Spreading root habit helps stabilize soil
- Can be cut back and will regrow from crown and roots
- Provides soil cover and organic matter inputs
- Suitable for integration into silvopastoral systems
- Responds well to periodic cutting for fodder harvest

## Relevance
Lespedeza bicolor earns its "miracle plant" name through its exceptional
soil reclamation capabilities combined with multiple yields. In
[[permaculture]] systems, it serves as a pioneer species for restoring
degraded land, providing nitrogen fixation, livestock fodder, firewood,
and bee forage simultaneously. Its use as a nurse crop to pine trees
demonstrates its value in succession-based forest establishment.
