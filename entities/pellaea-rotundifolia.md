## Overview

Pellaea rotundifolia, commonly known as the button fern, is an evergreen fern native to New Zealand. It is widely cultivated as a houseplant and is valued for its tolerance of indoor conditions and its neat, [[mushroom-natural-habitats]] include podocarp-hardwood forests, kauri forests, and mixed broadleaf forests where humidity levels remain consistently high. The species avoids exposed sites and is most common in deeply shaded gorges and ravine systems.

Within its native range, button fern is often associated with other shade-tolerant ferns including Asplenium bulbiferum, Blechnum discolor, and various Hymenophyllum species. It is particularly abundant on limestone and basalt substrates in the North Island.

Rainfall in its native range varies from 800 to over 2,500 millimeters annually. The species is most abundant in areas with consistent year-round precipitation and minimal dry season duration.

The species has naturalized in parts of southeastern Australia and in the British Isles near coastal gardens. Escaped populations are typically small and associated with garden waste disposal sites.

## Cultivation

Pellaea rotundifolia is one of the most widely cultivated New Zealand ferns and a standard offering in the international houseplant trade. Its popularity stems from its compact size, attractive foliage, and relative ease of cultivation compared to many other ferns.

A well-drained potting mix containing peat, perlite, and pine bark provides suitable growing conditions. Standard houseplant fertilization at half-strength during [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]] the species is grown exclusively as a houseplant or conservatory specimen.

The species is relatively pest-resistant, though mealybugs and scale insects may colonize the frond bases in stagnant indoor air. Good air circulation around the plant reduces pest incidence and prevents fungal leaf spot diseases.

Repotting is needed every 2 to 3 years as the rhizome fills the container. Plants benefit from being slightly root-bound, and over-potting in excessively large containers can lead to root rot in the unused moist potting medium.

## Ecology and Propagation

In its native forest habitat, Pellaea rotundifolia plays a modest ecological role as a substrate stabilizer on rock faces. The fibrous root system helps retain organic debris in rock crevices, contributing to the development of micro-soil pockets.

The species supports limited epiphytic growth on its fronds, primarily by bryophytes and lichens in very humid forest interiors. Its value as a food source for native fauna is minimal, as the leathery fronds are unpalatable to most herbivorous insects.

New Zealand native snails of the genus Powelliphanta occasionally shelter among the frond bases of forest-dwelling Pellaea. These interactions are incidental rather than ecological dependencies.
