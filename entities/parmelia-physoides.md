## Parmelia physoides

## Description

*Parmelia physoides* (currently accepted as *[[flavoparmelia-caperata]]* in some taxonomic treatments) is a foliose lichen species in the family Parmeliaceae. It is a broadly distributed epiphytic lichen found on tree bark, rocks, and walls in temperate regions. Like other *Parmelia* species, it has been used extensively as a bioindicator of air quality, particularly sulfur dioxide pollution.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Lecanoromycetes
- **Order:** Lecanorales
- **Family:** Parmeliaceae
- **Genus:** *Parmelia*
- **Species:** *P. physoides*

## Key Facts

- Documented by De Wit (1976) as showing differential sensitivity to atmospheric pollutants depending on substrate.
- Less sensitive to pollutants on oak stems than on poplar or elm, demonstrating that lichen pollution tolerance is modified by the bark substrate.
- Has a maximum SO2 tolerance of approximately 40 mg/m³, placing it in the moderate sensitivity range.
- Used in transect studies to map pollution gradients from urban centers.
- Demonstrates recovery of lichen communities where industrial SO2 emissions have declined, as documented in the greater London area (Bates et al., 2001).

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

*Parmelia physoides* is an important bioindicator species in environmental monitoring. Its sensitivity to sulfur dioxide and other atmospheric pollutants makes it valuable for assessing air quality and ecosystem health. Understanding its substrate-dependent tolerance helps refine pollution mapping methodologies. In mycology, it represents an important model for studying fungal–algal symbiosis and stress physiology.

## Ecological Role

- Epiphytic bioindicator of atmospheric SO2 levels
- Contributes to nutrient cycling through atmospheric deposition capture
- Provides habitat for microinvertebrates on tree bark
- Plays a role in primary succession on bare substrates
- Component of lichen community recovery monitoring programs
