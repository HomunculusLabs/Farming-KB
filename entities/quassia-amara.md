The species was among the first New World medicinal plants adopted into European pharmacopoeias during the colonial period, appearing in the London Pharmacopoeia by the late 18th century as "Quassia lignum."

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Quassia amara was described by Linnaeus in 1759 and placed in the family Simaroubaceae. The genus name honors Graman Quassi, while the specific epithet amara refers to the intensely bitter taste of the wood and bark.

The plant is an evergreen shrub or small tree reaching 3–6 meters in height with a slender, straight trunk. The pinnately compound leaves are arranged alternately, with 3–5 pairs of glossy, elliptic leaflets.

Each leaflet measures 5–12 cm in length with a smooth margin and prominent midrib. Young leaves are often tinged with reddish bronze before maturing to dark green.

The inflorescences are terminal or axillary panicles bearing showy, bright scarlet flowers approximately 2 cm long. Each flower has five petals and prominent exerted stamens that attract hummingbird pollinators.

The fruit is a small drupe, 1–2 cm long, ripening from green to glossy black, containing a [[iboga-root-bark-preparation]] contains the highest concentration of bioactive quassinoids.
