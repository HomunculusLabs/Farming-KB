## Soil Block Transplant

**Classification:** Transplanting technique / Propagation method  
**Category:** Technique

## Description

[[soil-block]] Transplanting is a propagation method in which seedlings are grown in compressed blocks of growing medium rather than in plastic containers, cell packs, or pots. The technique eliminates the need for disposable plastic containers and produces seedlings with superior root systems that establish quickly after transplanting. Eliot Coleman adopted this system at [[four-season-farm]] and found it necessary to modify his transplanting tools to accommodate the blocks.

## How Soil Blocks Are Made

1. A specialized soil block maker (also called a "blocker") is pressed into a moistened growing medium
2. The blocker compresses the medium into cuboid blocks with a small depression on top for seed placement
3. Blocks are placed on open trays (no cells or containers needed)
4. Seeds are placed in the top depression and covered
5. Blocks are watered gently and kept in appropriate germination conditions

## Key Facts

- **No containers required:** Eliminates plastic waste, storage space, and container costs
- **Air pruning:** Roots reach the edge of the block and stop growing (air pruning), preventing root circling
- **Faster establishment:** Transplanted blocks root out quickly into surrounding soil
- **Growing medium:** Typically a blend of peat, compost, perlite, and other organic materials
- **Block sizes:** Available in various sizes (micro 3/4", mini 1-1/2", standard 2", and larger)

## Advantages Over Container Transplanting

- **Root health:** No root circling, girdling, or binding common in plastic containers
- **Cost:** No ongoing container purchase or replacement
- **Environmental:** Eliminates single-use plastic from the propagation chain
- **Transplant shock:** Minimal because the entire root system is intact and undisturbed
- **Storage:** No empty containers to store between seasons

## Tool Modification for Soil Blocks

When Coleman transitioned from bare-root transplants to soil block seedlings, the standard transplant trowel position became uncomfortable. His solution was to modify a bricklayer's trowel:

- Shortened the blade
- Changed the handle angle to parallel the blade
- Created a tool comfortable to hold blade-down for jabbing and pulling
- The modified tool made a perfect hole for inserting a soil block

This tool innovation exemplifies the iterative refinement approach Coleman advocates throughout his work.

## Relevance to Cultivation

Soil blocks are central to intensive market gardening and four-season growing systems. They allow efficient production of large numbers of transplants for succession planting and winter harvest systems. The technique aligns with organic farming principles by eliminating synthetic containers, promoting biological root development, and producing vigorous plants better able to resist pests and diseases.

The method is particularly valuable for winter harvest operations where precise transplant timing is essential and healthy, well-rooted seedlings must establish quickly in cooler soil conditions.
