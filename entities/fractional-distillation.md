# Fractional Distillation

## Description

Fractional distillation is a separation technique that exploits differences in boiling points to separate the components of a liquid mixture. In cannabis processing, fractional distillation under reduced pressure (vacuum) is used as an [[high-volume-cannabis-extraction-advanced-refinement]] technique to separate THC and related cannabinoids from less volatile impurities. The technique produces oil up to twice as strong as the ether-phase extract and can yield nearly pure THC when combined with cannabidiol isolation and isomerization.

## Classification

- **Category**: Separation / purification technique
- **Method type**: Vacuum fractional distillation
- **Related techniques**: Simple distillation, short-path distillation, wiped-film evaporation
- **Core principle**: Differential vaporization under controlled temperature and reduced pressure

## Key Facts

- Requires heating cannabis oil to **high temperatures under reduced pressure** (vacuum), causing cannabinoid fractions to vaporize at specific temperature/pressure combinations.
- Vapors are condensed on a cooled surface and collected as purified fractions; impurities that do not vaporize remain in the heating flask.
- Produces oil **up to twice as strong** as the ether-phase extract from basic [[pf-tek-alcohol-extraction-method]] and petroleum ether purification.
- Can be used to produce **nearly pure THC** when combined with prior chemical isolation of pure cannabidiol followed by isomerization.
- First systematically applied to cannabis oil by **Roger Adams in 1940**, published in the *Journal of the American Chemical Society*, Vol. 62, p. 198.
- Adams used wild Minnesota hemp (1938 season), extracted with 95% ethyl alcohol using a 4-crock countercurrent system, then performed fractional distillation under gradually increasing temperature (90°C to 200°C) with progressively reduced pressure (down to 30 mm Hg).
- The technique requires **sophisticated equipment**: Claisen flask, vacuum pump, fractionating column, temperature and pressure monitoring, and glass wool packing material.
- Temperature must never exceed 50°C during solvent evaporation (concentration step) to avoid degrading heat-sensitive cannabinoids.
- The concentrated extract contained 23.1 gm of solids per 100 cc of 95% ethanol, with 1 cc representing 4.13 gm of hemp equivalent.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Processing

Fractional distillation represents the most advanced refinement technique in the *[[cannabis-alchemy-book]]* workflow. While basic extraction produces a dark oil containing 30–60% THC (after petroleum ether [[cannabis-oil-purification-and-isomerization]]), fractional distillation can further concentrate the active constituents. The technique is positioned as the cutting edge of cannabis chemistry — the text notes that "these advanced [[mushroom-spawn]] are probably beyond the reach of the starting alchemist but are important in the sense that they lead to the production of the THC of highest purity." Modern cannabis processors use closely related techniques (short-path distillation, wiped-film evaporation) to produce commercial-grade distillates exceeding 90% THC.

## Roger Adams' Experimental Protocol

1. Ethanolic extract placed in 1-liter Claisen flask with glass wool packing
2. Temperature raised gradually from 90°C to 140°C under slightly reduced pressure
3. Distilled ethanol discarded; flask refilled to two-thirds capacity
4. Process repeated until 1600 cc of extract processed
5. Temperature raised to 200°C until ethanol traces ceased
6. Bath lowered to 180°C, pressure reduced to 30 mm Hg
7. Fractions collected at specific temperature/pressure breakpoints

## Modern Equivalents

| Technique | Era | Purity Achievable |
