## Ricardo Semler

## Description

Ricardo Semler is a Brazilian businessman and former CEO of Semco, a manufacturing company that became famous for its radical approach to workplace democracy and organisational transformation. Holmgren cites Semler's turnaround of Semco as "perhaps the most dramatic and positive example of creative top-down change in business." Under Semler's leadership, Semco transformed from a failing giant into a thriving, innovative enterprise by instituting management transparency, flattening hierarchies, and empowering workers to participate in decision-making.

## Classification

- **Category:** Person — Business Leader / Organisational Innovator
- **Domain:** Business management, organisational design, workplace democracy
- **Nationality:** Brazilian
- **Period:** Active 1980s–2000s

## Background

Semler inherited Semco from his father, Antonio Semler, in 1980. At the time, Semco was a traditional, hierarchical Brazilian manufacturing company specialising in marine and food processing equipment. The company was struggling financially and losing market share. Rather than continuing with conventional management approaches, Ricardo Semler embarked on a programme of radical organisational reform that would eventually make Semco one of the most studied companies in management theory.

## Key Reforms at Semco

1. **Management transparency** — All financial information was made available to workers, creating trust and shared understanding of the company's situation.

2. **Flattened hierarchy** — Unnecessary layers of management were removed, speeding decision-making and reducing bureaucratic overhead.

3. **Human-scale workplaces** — Industrial-scale environments were replaced with smaller, more comfortable workspaces that respected human dignity.

4. **Worker share ownership** — Employees were given stakes in the company, aligning individual interests with collective success.

5. **Participatory decision-making** — Workers were involved in decisions about their work, schedules, and even hiring of managers.

## Key Facts

- The transformation rescued Semco from near-failure and made it highly profitable.
- Semler's approach demonstrated that treating workers as co-participants rather than subordinates could revitalise a failing organisation.
- His methods challenged the then-dominant command-and-control management orthodoxy.
- Documented in his bestselling book *Maverick* (1993).
- The Semco model has been widely studied in business schools but rarely replicated at scale.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

- **Self-regulation and feedback:** Semco's management reforms embody the permaculture principle of applying self-regulation and accepting feedback (Principle 4). By flattening hierarchies and making information transparent, the organisation could respond more effectively to changing conditions.

- **Cooperative design:** The shift from top-down control to participatory management mirrors permaculture's emphasis on working with natural systems rather than dominating them.

- **Scale-appropriate solutions:** Semler's move toward human-scale workplaces aligns with permaculture's principle of using small and slow solutions (Principle 9).

- **Systems thinking:** The Semco example illustrates how understanding systemic relationships — between management and workers, between parts and the whole — can lead to more resilient and productive systems.

- **Creative use of change:** Holmgren uses Semco as an example of how deliberate, creative change in large-scale, complex systems is possible even when it seems most fraught with difficulty (Principle 12).

## Connections

- His management philosophy parallels permaculture design principles applied to social and economic systems
