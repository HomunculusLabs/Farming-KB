## Overview

**Leptospermum scoparium** J.R.Forst. & G.Forst., commonly known as mānuka, New Zealand tea tree, or kahikātoa, is an evergreen shrub or small tree in the family Myrtaceae. It is native to New Zealand and southeastern Australia.

The species is world-renowned for the medicinal honey produced from its nectar, known as mānuka honey, which exhibits potent antibacterial activity attributed to methylglyoxal. This honey commands premium prices in international markets.

Mānuka is also valued for its essential oil, which contains triketones with strong antimicrobial and insecticidal properties. The oil is used in aromatherapy, cosmetics, and topical antiseptic formulations.

The plant typically grows 2–5 meters tall as a multistemmed shrub, occasionally reaching 10–15 meters as a small tree in favorable conditions. It is one of the most widespread native shrub species in New Zealand.

Leptospermum scoparium was first described by Johann and Georg Forster in 1776 from specimens collected in New Zealand during Captain Cook's second voyage. The genus Leptospermum comprises approximately 80–90 species distributed across Australasia and Southeast Asia.

## Cultivation and Uses

Leptospermum scoparium is primarily cultivated for mānuka honey production, which has become a major agricultural industry in New Zealand. The mānuka honey industry is valued at approximately NZ$300 million annually.

Commercial mānuka plantations have been established on thousands of hectares across New Zealand, with breeding programs selecting for high dihydroxyacetone (DHA) content in nectar. DHA converts to methylglyoxal in honey, conferring the unique antibacterial activity.

The UMF (Unique Mānuka Factor) and MGO (methylglyoxal) grading systems are used to certify the antibacterial potency of mānuka honey. Premium grades with high MGO levels command prices exceeding NZ$500 per kilogram.

Mānuka honey is used therapeutically for wound care, burn treatment, digestive health, and sore throat relief. Clinical studies have demonstrated efficacy against antibiotic-resistant bacteria including MRSA.

The essential oil, steam-distilled from leaves and branchlets, contains triketones (flavesone, leptospermone, iso-leptospermone) with potent antimicrobial properties. The oil is used in skincare products, acne treatments, and insect repellents.

## Ecology

Leptospermum scoparium is a keystone species in New Zealand's native vegetation, playing critical roles in primary succession, soil stabilization, and habitat provision. It is often the first woody species to colonize disturbed landscapes.

The dense, low canopy provides shelter for native seedlings of taller forest trees, facilitating [[mollison-designers-food-forest-succession-design]]. Mānuka scrub serves as a nurse crop for species such as kāmahi, tawa, and podocarps.

Flowers are an important nectar source for native bees, flies, beetles, and moths. The prolonged flowering period (spring to early summer) supports pollinator populations during a critical seasonal transition.

The foliage provides browse for introduced herbivores, particularly possums and goats, which can severely damage mānuka stands. Heavy browsing can suppress regeneration and alter successional trajectories in native forests.

Mānuka-dominated vegetation provides habitat for native birds including fantails, silvereyes, and warblers. Dense mānuka thickets offer nesting sites and protection from predators for small bird species.

The species contributes to soil formation and stabilization on erosion-prone slopes and riverbanks. Its extensive root network binds loose substrates, reducing landslide and erosion risk in hill country catchments.

Mānuka is also important in riparian zone restoration, where its tolerance of waterlogged soils and rapid growth make it effective for stream bank stabilization. Plantings reduce sediment runoff and improve water quality in agricultural watersheds.
