# N,N-Dimethyltryptamine (DMT)

## Overview

**N,N-Dimethyltryptamine (DMT)** is a naturally occurring tryptamine alkaloid and powerful psychedelic compound found across the plant and animal kingdoms. It is the primary psychoactive constituent of **ayahuasca**, a traditional Amazonian brew used in shamanic ceremonies for millennia. DMT produces profound alterations in consciousness, including vivid visual hallucinations, ego dissolution, and mystical-type experiences, typically lasting 5–15 minutes when smoked or 4–6 hours when consumed as ayahuasca (with MAO inhibition). Its presence in the human brain has led to extensive research into its role as an endogenous neurochemical.

## Chemical Identity

- **IUPAC Name:** N,N-Dimethyl-1H-indole-3-ethanamine
- **Chemical Formula:** C₁₂H₁₆N₂
- **Molecular Weight:** 188.27 g/mol
- **CAS Number:** 54-11-5
- **SMILES:** CNC(C)CCc1c[nH]c2ccccc12
- **Structural Class:** Tryptamine derivative (substituted at the amine nitrogen)

### Chemical Structure
DMT consists of an indole ring system (the core of tryptophan and serotonin) with a two-carbon ethylamine chain at the 3-position, where the terminal amine is dimethylated. This structural similarity to serotonin (5-HT) and the neurotransmitter dimethyltryptamine is central to its pharmacological activity.

## Pharmacology

### Mechanism of Action
DMT acts primarily as a **non-selective serotonin 5-HT receptor agonist**, with highest affinity at:

| Receptor | Ki (nM) | Functional Effect |
|----------|---------|-------------------|
| 5-HT2A | 30–50 | Primary psychedelic effect |
| 5-HT2C | 50–100 | Anxiety, modulatory |
| 5-HT1A | 100–200 | Anxiolytic, mood |
| Sigma-1 | 100–200 | Neuroprotective, modulation |
| TAAR1 | 500+ | Trace amine activity |
| 5-HT2B | 200+ | Cardiovascular (valvulopathy risk low at DMT doses) |

The 5-HT2A receptor activation, particularly in the prefrontal cortex, is considered the primary driver of psychedelic effects. fMRI studies show that DMT dramatically increases neural entropy — the brain enters a hyperconnected, less modular state characterized by increased global connectivity and disrupted default mode network (DMN) activity.

### Pharmacokinetics

| Parameter | Smoked/Vaped | Oral (with MAOi) |
|-----------|-------------|-------------------|
| Onset | 10–30 seconds | 30–60 minutes |
| Peak | 2–5 minutes | 1.5–2 hours |
| Duration | 5–15 minutes | 4–6 hours |
| Bioavailability | 40–50% | ~3% (without MAOi); ~60% (with MAOi) |
| Metabolism | MAO-A (first-pass), CYP2D6, CYP1A2 | MAO-A inhibited; hepatic CYP metabolism |
| Half-life | ~15 minutes | ~3 hours |

### Endogenous Production
DMT is naturally produced in the human body:

- **Pineal gland:** Demonstrated in rat pineal tissue; speculated (not confirmed) in humans
- **Cerebral cortex:** Detected in human brain tissue
- **Blood and CSF:** Present at nanomolar concentrations in living humans (Barker et al., 2019)
- **Biosynthesis:** From tryptophan → tryptamine → NMT → DMT, via indolethylamine N-methyltransferase (INMT)
- **Physiological role:** Hypothesized functions include neuroprotection, modulation of sigma-1 receptor signaling, and a potential role in near-death experiences and dreaming — all remain speculative

## Natural Occurrence

### Plant Sources
DMT is found in over 65 plant species across multiple families:

- **Leguminosae (Fabaceae):** *Mimosa tenuiflora* (jurema), *Anadenanthera peregrina* (yopo), *Desmanthus illinoensis*
- **Malpighiaceae:** *Banisteriopsis caapi* (ayahuasca vine — contains harmine/harmaline MAOIs, not DMT itself, but is the ayahuasca admixture)
- **Rubiaceae:** *Psychotria viridis* (chacruna), *Psychotria carthaginensis*
- **Solanaceae:** *Diplopterys cabrerana* (chaliponga)
- **Euphorbiaceae:** Various *Croton* species
- **Gramineae (Poaceae):** *Phalaris brachystachys*, *Phalaris arundinacea*

### Animal Sources
- Detected in mammalian brain tissue, blood, urine, and cerebrospinal fluid
- Present in amphibian skin secretions (*Bufo alvarius* — contains 5-MeO-DMT, not DMT)
- Found in some marine invertebrates

## Preparations and Routes of Administration

### Ayahuasca
The most well-known DMT preparation, ayahuasca combines DMT-containing plants (*Psychotria viridis*) with MAO-inhibiting plants (*Banisteriopsis caapi*). The harmala alkaloids (harmine, harmaline, tetrahydroharmine) inhibit monoamine oxidase A in the gut and liver, preventing the rapid breakdown of DMT and enabling oral activity. This preparation has been used by indigenous Amazonian peoples for at least 1,000 years, possibly much longer.

### Changa
A smoking blend combining DMT extracted from plant material with harmala alkaloids (often infused into *Banisteriopsis caapi* leaf or other herbs). The harmalas provide mild MAO inhibition, extending and smoothing the DMT experience to 15–30 minutes.

### Freebase DMT
Extracted DMT in its freebase form, vaporized or smoked. Produces the most intense, rapid-onset experience — often described as a "breakthrough" at higher doses.

### Pharmaceutical (IV/IM)
Clinical research uses synthetic DMT fumarate administered intravenously (0.1–0.3 mg/kg) or intramuscularly (0.5–1.0 mg/kg). Imperial College London's Centre for Psychedelic Research has conducted pioneering DMT clinical trials using these methods.

## Subjective Effects

### Common Experiences
- Rapid onset of geometric visual patterns, kaleidoscopic imagery, and color intensification
- Alterations in sense of time (time dilation or dissolution)
- Ego dissolution or "ego death" — loss of subjective self-boundary
- Encounters with seemingly autonomous entities or beings
- Profound emotional experiences: awe, fear, love, grief, insight
- Mystical-type experiences: unity, transcendence of space/time, ineffability
- Synesthetic blending of sensory modalities

### Dose-Response

| Level | Dose (smoked) | Characteristics |
|-------|--------------|-----------------|
| Threshold | 5–10 mg | Subtle visual shimmer, mood shift |
| Light | 10–20 mg | Clear geometric patterns, enhanced colors |
| Moderate | 20–40 mg | Immersive visuals, ego softening, emotional intensity |
| Strong | 40–60 mg | Complete ego dissolution, entity contact, "breakthrough" |
| Heroic | 60+ mg | Overwhelming non-dual experience, total reality dissolution |

## Clinical Research

### Current Trial Areas
- **Treatment-resistant depression:** Open-label studies at Imperial College show rapid antidepressant effects persisting 1–2 weeks post-session
- **Alcohol use disorder:** Phase II trials evaluating ayahuasca-assisted therapy
- **End-of-life distress:** Ongoing studies at Johns Hopkins and Imperial College
- **Trauma and PTSD:** Preliminary studies with veterans using ceremonial ayahuasca contexts

### Neuroimaging Findings
- Dramatic increase in signal diversity (a measure of neural complexity) — the highest of any psychedelic studied
- Disruption of the default mode network (DMN) and increased global brain connectivity
- Increased communication between brain regions that normally don't interact
- Changes in alpha and theta oscillations correlated with mystical experience intensity

## Legal Status

- **United States:** Schedule I under the Controlled Substances Act (no accepted medical use)
- **UN Convention:** Schedule I of the 1971 Convention on Psychotropic Substances
- **Brazil:** Legal for religious use by recognized ayahuasca churches (Santo Daime, União do Vegetal)
- **Peru:** Decriminalized for traditional/ceremonial use
