## Description

The hex-1 gene encodes the HEX1 protein, which is the major structural core protein of Woronin bodies in Euascomycete fungi. HEX1 self-assembles into the dense, crystalline core that characterizes these unique organelles. The hex-1 gene is considered diagnostic of the Euascomycete lineage, as it is present in all Euascomycete genomes examined (including the earliest-branching lineages) but absent from other known fungal genomes. Its discovery provided definitive molecular evidence that Woronin bodies are peroxisome-derived organelles.

## Classification

- **Category**: Gene / Protein
- **Protein product**: HEX1 (Woronin body core protein)
- **Taxonomic distribution**: Euascomycetes (a major clade within [[ascomycota]])
- **Organelle association**: Woronin bodies
- **Cellular localization**: Peroxisome-derived organelle core

## Key Facts

- The hex-1 gene is found exclusively in Euascomycete genomes, making it a reliable molecular marker for this fungal lineage.
- HEX1 protein spontaneously self-assembles into a dense, crystalline core structure that gives Woronin bodies their characteristic electron-dense appearance under transmission electron microscopy.
- The gene phylogenetic distribution supports a single evolutionary origin of Woronin bodies in the ancestral Euascomycete.
- hex-1 is present even in the earliest-branching Euascomycete lineages, indicating that Woronin body evolution was an early innovation in this group.
- The protein is targeted to peroxisomes via a peroxisomal targeting signal (PTS), confirming the peroxisomal origin of Woronin bodies.
- HEX1 self-assembly into a crystalline core represents a unique example of protein-based organelle biogenesis in eukaryotes.

## Biological Function

The HEX1 protein serves several critical functions through its role in Woronin body structure:

1. **Pore sealing**: The dense HEX1 core enables Woronin bodies to physically plug septal pores when hyphal damage occurs, preventing cytoplasmic loss.
2. **Tethering**: Woronin bodies are positioned near septal pores by tethering filaments, ensuring rapid response to cellular damage.
3. **Organelle biogenesis**: HEX1 self-assembly drives the formation of the Woronin body as a distinct peroxisome-derived organelle.

## Evolutionary Significance

The hex-1 gene represents a key innovation in [[fungal-evolution]]:

- Its origin coincides with the evolution of complex multicellular organization in the Euascomycete lineage.
- The presence of hex-1 correlates with the ability to produce complex multicellular fruiting bodies (ascocarps).
- The absence of hex-1 in other fungal groups (Basidiomycota, Zygomycota, Chytridiomycota) confirms that Woronin bodies are unique to Euascomycetes.
- Basidiomycetes evolved an independent solution (septal pore caps) for septal pore regulation, demonstrating convergent evolution of septal pore management.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

While hex-1 is specific to Euascomycetes and therefore not directly present in basidiomycete cultivated mushrooms, understanding this gene has broad implications:

- **Biotechnology**: HEX1 has been explored as a model for protein self-assembly and organelle engineering.
- **Pathology**: Many pathogenic ascomycetes (e.g., Aspergillus, Candida) possess Woronin bodies dependent on hex-1, and disrupting this gene affects virulence.
- **Phylogenetics**: hex-1 serves as a molecular marker for phylogenetic studies of Euascomycete relationships.
- **Comparative biology**: Understanding hex-1 and Woronin bodies informs the broader question of how all fungi manage septal pore function and intercellular transport.
