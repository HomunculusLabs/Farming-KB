# Jagadis Chandra Bose

Sir jagadis chandra bose Bose (1858–1937) was a Bengali polymath and pioneering
scientist who made foundational contributions to both physics and [[plant-electrophysiology]], and his philosophical position —
that there is no hard boundary between life and non-life in stimulus response
— informs contemporary discussions of plant sentience and biosemiotics.

## Controversies

Bose’s priority over Marconi in wireless signaling remains debated. Bose used
millimeter waves (short range within a room) while Marconi developed practical
long-range wireless telegraphy. Bose chose not to pursue commercial applications
or vigorously press his priority claims, though Lord Kelvin publicly
acknowledged his contributions after the 1897 Royal Institution demonstration.
His claims about plant sentience — that plants can be “drugged” and show
“fatigue” — were controversial among Western scientists who accused him of
anthropomorphizing plant responses, a debate partly colored by colonial attitudes.

## Cultural Significance

A close friend of Rabindranath Tagore, Bose shared the poet’s vision of unity
between science, art, and philosophy. He wrote *Niruddesher Kahini* (1896),
considered one of the first Bengali science fiction stories. As a figure of the
Bengal Renaissance, Bose demonstrated that world-class science was possible under
colonial constraints. His career embodied scientific self-reliance: he built his
own equipment, founded India-funded research institutions, and insisted on equal
treatment in the colonial academy, inspiring generations of Indian scientists.
