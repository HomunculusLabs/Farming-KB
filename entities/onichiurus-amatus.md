## Description

**Onichiurus amatus** (syn. *Onychiurus amatus*) is a species of collembolan (springtail) found in forest soils that feeds preferentially on fungal mycelia. It has been extensively studied for its clear and consistent hierarchy of fungal feeding preferences, making it one of the most important model organisms for understanding how soil microarthropod grazing influences fungal community composition and ecosystem processes.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Eukaryota

## Key Facts

### Fungal Feeding Preference Hierarchy

Shaw (1988) established a clear, consistent hierarchy of fungal preferences. The most preferred was *Marasmius androsaceus* (72.2% colony consumed), followed by *Mycena galopus* (68.2%), *Lactarius rufus* (55.7%), *Suillus luteus* (50.7%), and *Laccaria proxima* (41.4%). The least preferred were *Hebeloma crustuliniforme* (1.7%), *Clitocybe sp.* (1.2%), and *Pisolithus tinctorius* (0.2%). There is over a 60-fold difference in consumption between the most and least preferred species.

### Avoidance of Toxic Fungi

Parkinson et al. (1979) demonstrated that *O. amatus* can actively detect and avoid toxic fungi, avoiding a particular basidiomycete that caused death even without ingestion. This indicates chemical sensing capabilities that allow assessment of fungal quality before feeding.

### Ecological Impact

The selective feeding by *O. amatus* has significant ecosystem consequences:

1. **Competitive shifting:** Preferential grazing on *Marasmius androsaceus* allows less-preferred *Mycena galopus* to dominate
2. **Habitat redistribution:** Grazing forces fungal species into suboptimal habitats, potentially reducing functional efficiency
3. **Decomposition modification:** Changes in fungal community composition alter decomposition rates
4. **Nutrient mineralization:** Grazing stimulates nutrient turnover through consumption and excretion of fungal biomass

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

### Understanding Fungal Palatability

The preference hierarchy provides insights for:

1. **Substrate design:** Knowing which fungi are most palatable helps predict which species face highest grazing pressure
2. **Companion fungi:** Less palatable species may coexist better with collembola in soil-based systems
3. **Biological indicators:** Collembola feeding behavior can indicate substrate fungal community composition

### Soil Fauna Management in Cultivation

*O. amatus* and related collembola are common in cultivation substrates:

- Can damage mycelial networks of cultivated species, particularly those ranked high in palatability
- May be beneficial at moderate densities by suppressing competitor molds
- Management requires balancing their decomposer role against potential damage to cultivated mycelium

### Research Applications

*O. amatus* is valuable for laboratory studies because:

- Shows clear, quantifiable, and reproducible feeding preferences
- Can be maintained in laboratory culture
- Responds to fungal chemical cues in measurable ways
- Represents an important functional group (fungivorous collembola) in soil ecosystems
