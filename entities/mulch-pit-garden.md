## Description

The Mulch-Pit Garden (also called circle-mulch-grow pit) is a permaculture garden design that uses a central composting pit surrounded by a planting shelf and spill bank to achieve dramatic water savings compared to row cropping. The design consists of a central mulch pit approximately 60 cm wide and deep, surrounded by a planting shelf and spill bank totalling 1.2 metres (4 feet) across. The circular form has a 3.8-metre (12-foot) perimeter but can be efficiently watered with a single low-pressure sprinkler, whereas an equivalent 3.8-metre straight row requires three sprinklers.

## Classification

- **Type**: Permaculture garden design / water-efficient planting system
- **Origin**: Bill Mollison, described in *Permaculture: A Designer's Manual*
- **Scale**: Variable — 1.2 m diameter for herbs to 3 m diameter for tree crops
- **Category**: Circular gardening, water conservation, mulching technique

## Key Facts

- Central pit: 60 cm wide × 60 cm deep
- Total diameter including planting shelf and spill bank: 1.2 m (4 feet)
- Perimeter: 3.8 m (12 feet)
- Requires only one low-pressure sprinkler vs. three for equivalent row crop — 60% water savings
- Plants eventually overshade the centre pit, preventing evaporation
- Scales from herb gardens (1.2 m) to banana plantings (1.8 m) to coconut circles (1.8–3 m)
- Out-produces row cropping for approximately one-third the water use

## Design Specifications by Crop

**Herb / Vegetable Scale (1.2 m / 4 feet):**
- Central mulch pit: 60 cm wide × 60 cm deep
- Planting shelf surrounds the pit
- Spill bank forms the outer ring
- Single sprinkler provides complete irrigation

**Banana Circles (1.8 m / 6 feet):**
- Larger pit to accommodate banana biomass and organic matter
- Central mulch pit absorbs banana stems, leaves, and kitchen scraps
- Bananas planted around the rim

**Coconut Circles (1.8–3 m / 6–10 feet):**
- Largest scale variant
- Central pit serves as organic matter sump
- Coconut palms planted around perimeter
- Multiple circles can be established in series for orchard-scale production

## How It Works

The mulch-pit garden creates a self-improving system through several mechanisms:

1. **Central organic matter sump**: Kitchen scraps, garden waste, and crop residues are placed in the central pit where they decompose, releasing nutrients to surrounding plants
2. **Moisture concentration**: The pit design captures and retains water in the root zone rather than allowing runoff
3. **Self-shading**: As plants mature, they overshade the central pit, dramatically reducing evaporation
4. **Efficient irrigation**: Circular form allows one sprinkler to cover the entire growing area
5. **Root zone feeding**: Decomposing material in the centre feeds plants from below through natural leaching

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

The mulch-pit garden is a practical application of several core permaculture principles:

1. **Circular pattern application**: Converting linear row systems to circular forms reduces path space, land area, and water requirements
2. **Multiple functions**: The central pit serves as compost bin, water reservoir, nutrient source, and evaporation shield simultaneously
3. **Energy efficiency**: Gravity and natural decomposition do the work of fertilising and watering
4. **Scale appropriateness**: The design scales from household herb gardens to orchard-level production
5. **Fail-safe design**: The self-mulching, self-shading system becomes more effective as plants mature
