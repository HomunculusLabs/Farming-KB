## Description

"Joi Choi' is a pak choi (bok choy) cultivar (*[[brassica-rapa]]* subsp. *chinensis*) noted for its exceptional cold tolerance and bolt resistance in winter greenhouse production. It was identified as the most bolt-resistant of the Asian greens trialed by [[eliot-coleman]] for late-winter harvest, continuing to grow productively well after other varieties had gone to seed.

## Classification

- **Type**: Cultivar (Pak choi / Bok choy)
- **Species**: *Brassica rapa* subsp. *chinensis*
- **Family**: Brassicaceae (mustard family)
- **Category**: Asian green, stir-fry/braising green
- **Origin**: F1 hybrid variety

## Key Facts

- Outstanding bolt resistance compared to other pak choi cultivars in winter production
- In trials sown October 25, 'Joi Choi' was the only cultivar that did not go to seed within a week of the February 15 harvest
- Continued growing after February 15 and was sold bunched as "baby pak choi" through the end of March
- Other trial cultivars ("Yu Choi', 'Mei Qing Choi', tatsoi, mizuna) all bolted by late February
- Produces compact, upright plants suitable for close spacing under row covers
- Hardy enough for unheated cold-house production through Zone 5 winters

## Cultivation Notes

- Sown October 25 for mid-February harvest in Eliot Coleman's trials
- Unlike other Asian greens in the same trial, maintained vegetative growth rather than bolting in response to lengthening days
- Marketed as "baby pak choi" — whole bunched heads rather than cut leaves
- The extended harvest window (mid-February through March) provides weeks of additional revenue compared to bolting-prone varieties

## Comparison with Other Cultivars

In the October 25 sowing trial:
- **'Joi Choi'**: Did not bolt; sold through March as baby pak choi
- **'Yu Choi'** (edible rape): Bolted within a week of Feb 15
- **'Mei Qing Choi'** (baby pak choi): Bolted within a week of Feb 15
- **Tatsoi**: Bolted within a week of Feb 15
- **Mizuna**: Bolted within a week of Feb 15

## Relevance to Cultivation

'Joi Choi' demonstrates the critical importance of cultivar selection for specific production windows. While most pak choi and Asian green cultivars bolt as days lengthen in late winter, 'Joi Choi' provides an extended harvest window that fills the gap between deep-winter crops and spring plantings. This bolt resistance translates directly into extended market availability and revenue. For winter growers, identifying and using the most bolt-resistant cultivar for each sowing date is as important as selecting for cold hardiness.
