## Panaeolus fimicola

**Panaeolus fimicola**, commonly known as the **dung mottlegill**, is a small mushroom in
the family Bolbitiaceae found worldwide on dung and enriched soils. While primarily
known as a common and widespread non-psychoactive species, [[tryptamine]] distribution among [[macrofungal-documentation-photography-spore-prints]]. The
genus belongs to the family Bolbitiaceae within the order Agaricales. P. fimicola
is one of the most common and widely distributed members of the genus, though its
identification is complicated by the existence of several closely related taxa.

Some authorities have recognized Panaeolus fimicola var. fimicola and Panaeolus fimicola
var. ater as distinct varieties, with the latter being darker and more uniformly colored.
The taxonomic status of these varieties remains debated, with some mycologists
considering them synonymous with the type variety.

The species has also been placed in the genus Anellaria by some historical authorities,
though this placement is no longer accepted. The name Anellaria separata has been applied
to what is now considered P. fimicola by various authors. The genus Anellaria was
distinguished from Panaeolus primarily by the presence of a more persistent annulus (ring),
but this character was found to be too variable to support generic separation.

The genus name Panaeolus derives from Greek "pan" (all) and "aiolos" (variegated or
changing), referring to the mottled appearance of the gills.

## Description

**Cap:** Convex to campanulate (bell-shaped) when young, becoming broadly convex to
nearly plane with age. Surface is smooth, hygrophanous, and typically brownish to
grayish-brown or dark brown when moist. The cap surface often has a silky or slightly
shiny appearance, particularly in young specimens. As it dries, the cap fades to a pale
grayish or whitish color, sometimes with a darker disc (center) that persists. Cap
diameter is small, ranging from 10 to 30 mm. The margin is often slightly striate when
moist.

**Gills:** Adnate to slightly adnexed, close to subdistant. The gills display the
characteristic mottled or spotted pattern typical of the genus Panaeolus, with dark
patches developing unevenly as spores mature on different parts of the gill surface at
different rates. At maturity, the gills appear black with lighter edges formed by sterile
cheilocystidia. This mottled appearance is the single most reliable field character for
the genus.

**Stem:** Cylindrical, equal, hollow, and fragile. Surface is smooth and silvery-white
to pale gray, sometimes with a slightly powdery coating near the apex. The base may be
slightly thickened or discolor brownish from the substrate. A thin annular zone may be
present but is typically evanescent. Length ranges from 30 to 70 mm, thickness 1.5 to 3
mm.

character should not be used as the sole identification criterion.

**Microscopic features:** Basidia are typically two-spored, though four-spored basidia
also occur. Cheilocystidia are abundant, ventricose to lageniform, with narrow necks.
The pileipellis is a cutis of narrow, parallel hyphae. [[basidiomycete-sexual-reproduction-and-clamp-connections]] are absent.
