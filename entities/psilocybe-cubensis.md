# [[stamets-psi-psilocybe-cubensis-species-guide]]

[[tropisms-psilocybe-cubensis-gravitropism]] is the most widely known and cultivated psilocybin-containing mushroom [[allegro-death-and-resurrection-in-the-mushroom-cult]] world. Originally described from Cuba by Franklin Sumner Earle in 1906, it has since been found throughout tropical and subtropical regions globally. The species owes its [[stamets-psylo-global-distribution-ecology]] in no small part to its intimate association with bovine dung — a trait that allowed it to spread wherever cattle ranching took hold.

## [[yeast-diversity-taxonomy-and-inventory-methods]] Naming

The epithet *cubensis* refers to Cuba, where the type specimen was first collected. The species has accumulated numerous synonyms over the decades, including *[[stropharia-cubensis]]* and *Naematoloma caerulescens*. It was formally placed in the genus *Psilocybe* by Rolf Singer and Alexander H. Smith in 1958. Common names include "golden teacher," "cubes," and "gold caps," the latter referring to the characteristic caramel-gold color of the cap in mature specimens.

## Morphology

The cap (pileus) ranges from 1.6 to 8 cm in diameter and is convex when young, expanding to broadly convex or nearly plane with age. The cap surface is smooth, hygrophanous, and typically golden-brown to pale yellow when moist, fading to nearly white when dried. The gills are adnate to adnexed, grayish at first, darkening to deep purple-brown or nearly black as spores mature. The stipe is 4–15 cm tall, 0.4–1.4 cm thick, hollow, and whitish, often bruising bluish where handled. The veil forms a transient partial veil that leaves a membranous annulus on the stipe, though this can be fragile and evanescent in some collections. Spore prints are dark purplish-brown, and spores measure approximately 11.5–17 × 8–11.5 µm.

## Distribution and Habitat

*P. cubensis* is pantropical, occurring naturally in the Gulf Coast states of the US, throughout Central and South America, the Caribbean, Southeast Asia, Australia, and sub-Saharan Africa. It is a strict coprophile, fruiting almost exclusively from the dung of herbivores — primarily cattle, but also horses, elephants, and other large grazers. Fruiting occurs during warm, humid conditions, typically following substantial rainfall when temperatures remain above 20°C. The species can also colonize enriched soils adjacent to dung deposits.

## Chemistry

*P. cubensis* contains the indole alkaloids psilocybin and psilocin as its primary psychoactive constituents. [[baeocystin]] and norbaeocystin are also present in trace amounts. Typical concentrations range from 0.5–1.2% psilocybin and 0.1–0.5% psilocin by dry weight, though this varies significantly depending on genetics, growing conditions, and harvest timing. Younger specimens and the mycelium itself may contain different alkaloid ratios than mature fruiting bodies.

## Cultivation

*P. cubensis* is the single most cultivated psilocybin mushroom, owing to its relatively fast colonization speed, moderate environmental requirements, and substantial yield potential. It was the species used in the landmark PF Tek method popularized by Robert McPherson (Psilocybe Fanaticus) in the 1990s, which made home cultivation accessible to a wide audience. The species colonizes a broad range of substrates including brown rice flour, whole grains (rye, millet, wheat), straw, compost, and supplemented sawdust. Under optimal conditions — temperatures of 24–27°C for colonization and 22–26°C with high humidity for fruiting — the species can complete a full growth cycle from inoculation to harvest in approximately 4–6 weeks.

## Notable Strains

Numerous domesticated strains exist within the species, reflecting selective breeding and natural variation. These include Golden Teacher, B+, Ecuador, Cambodian, Thai Koh Samui, Penis Envy (known for unusually thick stipes and reportedly elevated potency), and AA+ (Albino A+). Strain differences manifest primarily in morphology, growth speed, and yield, with chemical composition being more variable and less consistently differentiated between strains.

## Ethnomycological Significance

*P. cubensis* has been used ceremonially by indigenous peoples in Mesoamerica for millennia. The Mixtec and Zapotec peoples of Oaxaca, Mexico, traditionally used related *Psilocybe* species, and *P. cubensis* was likely among those employed. María Sabina, the Mazatec curandera who introduced psilocybin mushrooms to the Western world, worked primarily with *P. caerulescens* and *P. mexicana*, but *P. cubensis* is the species that subsequently became most widely known internationally.
