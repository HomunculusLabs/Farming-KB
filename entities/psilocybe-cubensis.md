# Psilocybe cubensis

## Overview

**Psilocybe cubensis** is a species of psychedelic mushroom whose principal active compounds are psilocybin and psilocin. It is the most widely known and cultivated psilocybin-containing mushroom, owing to its relatively large size, ease of cultivation, and potent psychoactive properties. Originally described from Cuba, it grows throughout tropical and subtropical regions worldwide, particularly in the dung of herbivorous animals.

### Synonyms and Former Classifications

- *Stropharia cubensis* (Earle) Singer (1949)
- *Naematoloma caerulescens*
- *Psilocybe cubensis* var. **cyanescens** (not to be confused with *P. cyanescens*)
- Numerous strain names exist in the cultivation community (Golden Teacher, B+, Ecuador, Thai, etc.) but these are not formally recognized taxonomic varieties

## Morphology

- **Cap (pileus)**: 1.5–8 cm diameter; convex to broadly convex, often with a distinct umbo in maturity; color ranges from golden-brown to pale yellow, hygrophanous (becoming lighter when drying); surface smooth to slightly viscid when moist
- **Gills (lamellae)**: Adnate to adnexed, grayish when young, darkening to purplish-black with spore maturity
- **Stem (stipe)**: 4–15 cm long, 0.4–1.4 cm thick; hollow; white to yellowish; bruises blue prominently where damaged
- **Veil**: Partial veil present, leaving a persistent annular zone on the stem
- **Spore print**: Dark purplish-brown
- **Spores**: Ellipsoid, 11.5–17 × 8–11.5 μm
- **Blueing reaction**: Intensive blue bruising on cap, gills, and stem when handled — indicative of psilocin oxidation

## Distribution and Habitat

- **Geographic range**: Pantropical — found throughout Central and South America, the Caribbean, Southeast Asia, Australia, and subtropical regions of the United States (Gulf Coast states, Hawaii)
- **Substrate**: Primarily coprophilous — grows on dung of ruminants (cattle, horses, goats); also found on enriched soils and decaying plant matter in pastures
- **Climate**: Thrives in warm, humid environments; optimal growth at 24–28°C with high humidity
- **Season**: Year-round in tropical regions; late spring through autumn in subtropical areas
- **Symbiosis**: Saprotrophic; no known mycorrhizal associations

## Psychoactive Compounds

### Psilocybin

- **Chemical name**: O-phosphoryl-4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine
- **Molecular formula**: C₁₂H₁₇N₂O₄P
- **Molecular weight**: 284.25 g/mol
- **Mechanism**: Prodrug — dephosphorylated by alkaline phosphatase enzymes in the body to active psilocin
- **Potency**: Typically 0.5–1.0% dry weight in *P. cubensis*

### Psilocin

- **Chemical name**: 4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine (4-HO-DMT)
- **Molecular formula**: C₁₂H₁₆N₂O
- **Molecular weight**: 204.27 g/mol
- **Mechanism**: Primary psychoactive agent; partial agonist at serotonin 5-HT₂A receptors, with additional affinity for 5-HT₁A, 5-HT₂B, 5-HT₂C, and 5-HT₇ receptors

### Baeocystin and Norbaeocystin

- Minor alkaloids present at lower concentrations
- Baeocystin (4-phosphoryloxy-N-methyltryptamine): ~0.1–0.3% dry weight
- Norbaeocystin: Even lower concentrations; pharmacological role not fully understood
- May contribute to the overall entourage effect

## Clinical Research and Therapeutic Applications

### Treatment-Resistant Depression

Landmark studies at Imperial College London (Carhart-Harris et al., 2016) demonstrated that a single dose of psilocybin (10 mg and 25 mg, one week apart) produced rapid and sustained antidepressant effects in patients with treatment-resistant depression. Follow-up studies showed benefits persisting at 6-month and 12-month marks.

### End-of-Life Anxiety

Studies at Johns Hopkins University and NYU Grossman School of Medicine have shown that psilocybin-assisted psychotherapy significantly reduces death-related anxiety and depression in patients with life-threatening cancer diagnoses, with effects lasting 4–5 years post-treatment.

### Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder

Pilot studies suggest psilocybin may reduce OCD symptoms, possibly through disruption of rigid cognitive patterns mediated by the default mode network (DMN).

### Substance Use Disorders

Research at Johns Hopkins has demonstrated high rates of smoking cessation following psilocybin-assisted treatment (80% abstinence at 6-month follow-up in initial study). Similar approaches are being investigated for alcohol use disorder.

### Cluster Headaches

Anecdotal reports and limited observational data suggest psilocybin may interrupt cluster headache cycles, though controlled trials remain lacking.

### Neural Mechanisms

- **Default Mode Network (DMN) suppression**: Psilocybin decreases connectivity within the DMN, associated with ego dissolution and increased cognitive flexibility
- **Global connectivity increase**: Functional MRI shows increased connectivity between brain regions that don't normally communicate
- **Neuroplasticity**: Promotes dendritic spine growth and increased synaptic density in cortical neurons (via 5-HT₂A-mediated mTOR signaling)
- **Entropic brain hypothesis**: Psilocybin increases the entropy (complexity) of brain activity, allowing novel cognitive patterns to emerge

## Cultivation

### PF Tek Method

The most accessible method for home cultivation:
- Brown rice flour and vermiculite substrate in half-pint mason jars
- Sterilization via pressure cooker (90 min at 15 psi)
- Inoculation with spore syringe in a still-air box
- Colonization at 24–27°C in darkness (2–4 weeks)
- Fruiting chamber with perlite for humidity (95%+ RH)

### Monotub Cultivation

Advanced bulk method:
- Grain spawn (rye, oats, or millet) colonized in jars
- Spawned to bulk substrate (coconut coir + vermiculite + gypsum)
- Colonization in a modified plastic storage tub (10–14 days)
- Initiate fruiting with fresh air exchange and light introduction
- Yields of 2–6 oz dry weight per tub are typical

## Harm Reduction and Safety

- **Set and setting**: Psychological preparation and a comfortable environment are critical determinants of experience quality
- **Dosage**: Typical dried dose ranges from 1–5 g; higher doses carry greater risk of challenging experiences
- **Contraindications**: Personal or family history of psychosis or schizophrenia; cardiovascular conditions; current use of SSRIs or MAOIs
- **Screening**: Clinical protocols use rigorous psychiatric screening to exclude vulnerable individuals
- **Integration**: Post-experience integration therapy significantly improves long-term therapeutic outcomes
- **Toxicity**: Psilocybin has extremely low physiological toxicity (LD₅₀ estimated at > 1000× typical recreational dose in animals)
- **No physical dependence**: No evidence of addiction or withdrawal syndrome
