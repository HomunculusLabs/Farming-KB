# Psilocybe cubensis

Psilocybe cubensis is a psychoactive mushroom in the family Hymenogastraceae,
known for its psilocybin and psilocin content. It is the most widely cultivated
and recognized psychedelic mushroom worldwide.

## Taxonomy

| Rank | Classification |
|------|---------------|
| Kingdom | Fungi |
| Division | Basidiomycota |
| Class | Agaricomycetes |
| Order | Agaricales |
| Family | Hymenogastraceae |
| Genus | Psilocybe |
| Species | P. cubensis |

First described by Franklin Sumner Earle in 1906 in Cuba. The epithet
"cubensis" refers to its Cuban type locality.

## Morphology

- **Cap**: 1.5–8 cm, convex to plane, often with a distinct umbo; golden-brown
  to pale yellow when dry
- **Gills**: Adnate to adnexed, gray at first, darkening to purple-black
- **Stipe**: 4–15 cm long, 0.5–1.5 cm thick, hollow, white to yellowish,
  bruises blue when handled
- **Spore print**: Dark purple-brown
- **Veil**: Universal veil forms a persistent white annulus on the stipe

## Identification

Key identifying features include blue bruising (psilocin oxidation), dark
purple-brown spore print, growth on herbivore dung or enriched substrates, and
gills progressing from gray to deep purple-black.

## Distribution and Habitat

- **Native range**: Subtropical and tropical regions worldwide, especially
  Neotropical areas
- **Habitat**: Grasslands and pastures; commonly on bovine dung
- **Geographic spread**: Central and South America, Southeast Asia, Australia,
  southern United States
- **Climate**: Warm and humid (70–85°F / 21–29°C) with regular rainfall

## Lifecycle

1. **Spore germination**: Haploid spores form monokaryotic mycelium
2. **Mating**: Compatible monokaryotic hyphae fuse into dikaryotic mycelium
3. **Colonization**: Mycelium colonizes substrate via extracellular enzymes
4. **Fructification**: Triggered by reduced temperature, high humidity (~95%),
   light, and fresh air exchange
5. **Maturity**: Pins develop into fruiting bodies over 5–14 days
6. **Spore release**: Spores discharged from basidia for wind/animal dispersal

## Cultivation

P. cubensis is the most commonly cultivated psychedelic mushroom due to simple
requirements and robust growth.

### Common Substrates

| Substrate | Suitability | Notes |
|-----------|------------|-------|
| Brown rice flour + vermiculite | Beginner | PF Tek; sterilized jars |
| Rye grain | Intermediate | Spawn production; high nutrient |
| Coco coir + vermiculite | All levels | Bulk substrate; pasteurized |
| Manure-based | Intermediate | Horse/cow manure; pasteurized |
| Straw | Intermediate | Chopped wheat straw; pasteurized |

### Cultivation Parameters

- **Colonization temp**: 75–80°F (24–27°C)
- **Fruiting temp**: 70–75°F (21–24°C)
- **Relative humidity**: 90–99% for fruiting
- **Light**: Indirect ambient, 12h on/off cycle
- **Fresh air exchange**: Critical during fruiting for pinning

## Chemistry

- **Psilocybin** (O-phosphoryl-4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine): Prodrug
  converted to psilocin in vivo; stable, water-soluble
- **Psilocin** (4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine): Primary active metabolite;
  crosses blood-brain barrier; 5-HT2A receptor agonism
- **Baeocystin** / **norbaeocystin**: Minor alkaloids; role not fully
  characterized
- **Typical psilocybin content**: 0.5–1.2% dry weight, varying by strain,
  conditions, and maturity

## Legal Status

- **UN**: Scheduled under 1971 Convention on Psychotropic Substances (I)
- **US**: Schedule I; spores legal in most states (not CA, GA, ID)
- **Netherlands**: Fresh truffles legal; dried mushrooms banned since 2008
- **Canada**: Schedule III under CDSA; therapeutic exemptions for trials
- **Brazil**: Not explicitly criminalized
- **Jamaica**: Legal for religious and therapeutic use
- **Portugal**: Decriminalized for personal use

## History and Research

Indigenous Mesoamerican cultures used psilocybin mushrooms for millennia in
spiritual ceremonies (Mazatec people of Oaxaca). R. Gordon Wasson published a
famous *Life* article in 1957 after a Mazatec velada with Maria Sabina. Albert
Hofmann isolated psilocybin in 1958 from *P. mexicana*. The strain was
popularized in the 1960s by Timothy Leary and Richard Alpert.

Modern research includes FDA breakthrough therapy designation for
treatment-resistant depression (Compass Pathways, 2018), studies on
neuroplasticity and synaptic connectivity, microdosing investigations for
cognitive enhancement, and preliminary evidence for cluster headache relief.

## Safety

- **Toxicity**: Very low physiological toxicity; LD50 >285 mg/kg (rodent, IV)
- **Psychological risks**: Anxiety, paranoia, or challenging experiences at
  high doses or in uncontrolled settings
- **Contraindications**: History of schizophrenia, bipolar, or psychotic
  disorders; serotonin syndrome risk with MAOIs/SSRIs
- **Set and setting**: Mindset, environment, and integration support are critical
- **Dependence**: No known addiction risk or withdrawal syndrome
