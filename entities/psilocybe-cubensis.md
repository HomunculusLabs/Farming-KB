# Psilocybe cubensis

## Overview

**Psilocybe cubensis** is a species of psychedelic mushroom whose principal active compounds are **psilocybin** and **psilocin**. It is the most widely known and cultivated psilocybin mushroom, belonging to the family **Hymenogastraceae** within the order **Agaricales**. The species is coprophilous, naturally occurring in subtropical and tropical environments across the Americas, Southeast Asia, and Australia.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Hymenogastraceae
- **Genus:** *Psilocybe*
- **Species:** *P. cubensis*

First described in 1906 by Franklin Sumner Earle in Cuba, the species epithet *cubensis* refers to its type locality. Multiple synonyms exist in the literature, including *Stropharia cubensis* and *Naematoloma caerulescens*, reflecting historical reclassifications as taxonomic understanding evolved.

## Morphology

### Cap (Pileus)

The cap ranges from 1.5–8 cm in diameter, convex when young, expanding to broadly convex or plane with age. Coloration is highly variable: cinnamon-brown to golden when young, fading to pale yellow or nearly white as the mushroom matures. The surface is smooth to slightly viscid when moist, with a distinct **remnant universal veil** that often appears as a darker ring zone on the cap margin.

### Gills (Lamellae)

Gills are adnate to adnexed, close to subdistant, initially grayish, darkening to deep purplish-brown to nearly black with spore maturity. This dark spore print color is a key diagnostic feature of the genus *Psilocybe*.

### Stipe (Stem)

The stipe is 4–15 cm long, 0.4–1.4 cm thick, equal to slightly swollen at the base. Color is white to yellowish, bruising prominently **blue** when handled — a characteristic reaction caused by the oxidation of psilocin. A persistent **annulus** (ring) from the partial veil is typically present, though it can be fragile and easily lost.

### Spores

Spores are elliptical to subrhomboidal, 11.5–17.3 × 7–12 μm, with a thick wall and a distinct **germ pore**. The spore print is dark purplish-brown.

## Chemical Constituents

The primary psychoactive compounds are:

| Compound | Class | Approximate Content (% dry weight) |
|----------|-------|-----------------------------------|
| Psilocybin | Tryptamine alkaloid | 0.5–1.2 |
| Psilocin | Tryptamine alkaloid | 0.1–0.4 |
| Baeocystin | Tryptamine alkaloid | Trace |
| Norbaeocystin | Tryptamine alkaloid | Trace |

Psilocybin (O-phosphoryl-4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine) is a prodrug, dephosphorylated *in vivo* by alkaline phosphatase enzymes to the pharmacologically active psilocin, which acts as a partial agonist at **5-HT2A serotonin receptors**.

## Distribution and Habitat

*P. cubensis* is found across the globe in tropical and subtropical regions between approximately 25°N and 35°S latitude. It grows predominantly on:

- Dung of herbivores (especially cattle, horses, and elephants)
- Enriched grassland soils
- Subtropical pastures and meadows

The species thrives in warm, humid conditions with temperatures between 24–30°C (75–86°F) and high ambient humidity (>85%). Fruiting typically follows heavy rainfall.

## Notable Strains

Numerous cultivated strains have been isolated and stabilized, differing in growth characteristics, potency, and morphology:

- **Golden Teacher** — Widely distributed, moderate potency, reliable cultivar; favored by beginners.
- **B+** — Large fruiting bodies, resilient to suboptimal conditions; one of the oldest domesticated strains.
- **Penis Envy** — Characteristically thick, dense stipes; reported higher-than-average psilocybin content.
- **Cambodian** — Fast-colonizing strain originating from Angkor Wat region; moderate potency.
- **Ecuador** — Thick-stemmed, large caps; robust and cold-tolerant relative to other strains.
- **Thai** — Long, thin stipes, moderate potency; associated with the "Koh Samui" variety.

## Cultivation

*P. cubensis* is the most commonly cultivated psilocybin mushroom due to its relatively fast growth and forgiving nature. Standard methods include:

### PF Tek (Psilocybe Fanaticus Technique)
A beginner-friendly method using brown rice flour and vermiculite in half-pint wide-mouth mason jars, sterilized by pressure cooking and inoculated with spore syringes.

### Bulk Monotub Cultivation
Spawn (colonized grain) is mixed with a bulk substrate — typically a combination of coconut coir, vermiculite, and gypsum (CVG) — in a modified plastic storage bin. This method yields significantly larger harvests.

### Key Parameters

| Parameter | Optimal Range |
|-----------|---------------|
| Substrate pH | 5.5–6.5 |
| Colonization temperature | 24–27°C (75–80°F) |
| Fruiting temperature | 22–26°C (72–79°F) |
| Relative humidity (fruiting) | 85–95% |
| Fresh air exchange | 1–4 exchanges/hour |
| Light cycle | 12 hours on / 12 hours off (indirect) |

## Legal Status

The legal status of *P. cubensis* varies significantly by jurisdiction:

- **United States:** Schedule I controlled substance at the federal level. Decriminalized in several cities (Denver, Oakland, Santa Cruz, Ann Arbor, Washington D.C.). Oregon Measure 109 (2020) permits supervised therapeutic use.
- **Netherlands:** While psilocybin-containing mushrooms were banned in 2008, psilocybin-containing **truffles** (sclerotia) remain legally sold in smart shops.
- **Brazil:** Sale and cultivation are illegal, but possession and consumption of fresh mushrooms are not explicitly criminalized.
- **Jamaica:** Legal to cultivate and consume; retreat centers operate openly.
- **Bahamas:** Legal and unregulated.
- **Portugal:** Decriminalized (personal use, all drugs since 2001).

## Pharmacology and Effects

Onset of effects occurs within 20–60 minutes of ingestion, peaking at 2–3 hours and lasting 4–6 hours total. Subjective effects include:

- Altered perception of time and space
- Visual and auditory hallucinations (at moderate–high doses)
- Synesthesia (rare, dose-dependent)
- Enhanced introspection and emotional processing
- Ego dissolution (at high doses)

## Safety Profile

*P. cubensis* has a high therapeutic index (LD₅₀ in mice: 280 mg/kg psilocybin IV). No confirmed human fatalities from psilocybin overdose have been recorded. Psychological risks include anxiety, paranoia, and triggering latent psychiatric conditions — particularly in individuals with personal or family history of psychosis or schizophrenia.
