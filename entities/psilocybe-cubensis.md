# Psilocybe cubensis

## Overview

**Psilocybe cubensis** is a species of psychedelic mushroom whose principal active compounds are psilocybin and psilocin. It is the most widely known and cultivated psychoactive mushroom, belonging to the family Hymenogastraceae in the order Agaricales. The species is native to subtropical and tropical environments and has been used in indigenous Mesoamerican spiritual practices for millennia.

Originally described by Franklin Sumner Earle in 1906 from specimens collected in Cuba, the species has since been found across the Gulf Coast of the United States, throughout Central and South America, Southeast Asia, and subtropical regions of Australia. The epithet "cubensis" refers to its type locality in Cuba.

## Morphology

### Cap (Pileus)

The cap ranges from 1.5–8 cm in diameter, beginning convex to conical when young and expanding to broadly convex or plane with age. Coloration varies from light brown to golden or pale yellow, often with a darker center (umbo). The surface is smooth and hygrophanous, becoming lighter as it dries. A distinct membranous partial veil covers the gills in immature specimens, leaving a persistent annular ring on the stipe after rupture.

### Gills (Lamellae)

Gills are adnate to adnexed, close to subdistant, and grayish when young, darkening to purplish-brown or nearly black with spore maturity due to the dark spore print.

### Stipe (Stem)

The stipe measures 4–15 cm tall and 0.4–1.5 cm thick, cylindrical, and often slightly enlarged at the base. It is white to yellowish, bruising prominently blue when damaged — a characteristic reaction attributed to the oxidation of psilocin and related compounds.

### Spores

Spores are ellipsoidal to rhomboid, 11.5–17.3 × 8–11.5 µm, with a dark purplish-brown spore print. The presence of a germ pore is a key microscopic feature.

## Chemistry

The primary psychoactive constituents are:

- **Psilocybin** (O-phosphoryl-4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine): A prodrug that is dephosphorylated by alkaline phosphatase in vivo to yield psilocin. Typical dried-weight concentration: 0.5–1.2%.
- **Psilocin** (4-hydroxy-N,N-dimethyltryptamine): The pharmacologically active metabolite responsible for the subjective psychedelic effects. Typical dried-weight concentration: 0.1–0.5%.
- **Baeocystin** and **norbaeocystin**: Minor alkaloids whose pharmacological contributions remain under investigation.

Both psilocybin and psilocin are tryptamine derivatives structurally analogous to serotonin (5-HT). They act primarily as agonists at the serotonin 5-HT₂A receptor, with additional affinity for 5-HT₁A, 5-HT₂C, and other receptor subtypes.

## Pharmacology

Psilocybin is orally active with an onset of 20–60 minutes, peak effects at 60–120 minutes, and a total duration of 4–6 hours. The compound crosses the blood-brain barrier readily. The effective oral dose ranges from 6–20 mg of psilocybin, though individual sensitivity varies considerably based on body weight, metabolism, set, and setting.

### Mechanism of Action

The psychedelic effects are primarily mediated by 5-HT₂A receptor agonism in the prefrontal cortex. Functional MRI studies show that psilocybin decreases default mode network (DMN) activity and increases global brain connectivity, which correlates with subjective reports of ego dissolution and mystical-type experiences.

## Clinical Research

Modern clinical investigation of psilocybin has experienced a renaissance since the mid-2000s:

- **Treatment-resistant depression**: Phase II and III trials at institutions including Imperial College London and Johns Hopkins have demonstrated rapid and sustained antidepressant effects from single or limited-dose regimens.
- **End-of-life anxiety**: Studies in patients with terminal cancer diagnoses show significant reductions in anxiety and depression lasting 6+ months.
- **Substance use disorders**: Pilot studies suggest efficacy in reducing alcohol and tobacco dependence.
- **Obsessive-compulsive disorder**: Case series and open-label trials have shown symptom reduction.

In 2018, the U.S. FDA granted Breakthrough Therapy designation to psilocybin-assisted therapy for treatment-resistant depression, accelerating its regulatory pathway. Compass Pathways and the Usona Institute are leading commercial and non-profit development efforts, respectively.

## Cultivation

*P. cubensis* is relatively easy to cultivate, which accounts for its status as the most commonly grown psychoactive mushroom. Cultivation methods include:

- **Brown rice flour/vermiculite (PF Tek)**: A beginner-friendly method using sterilized substrate in half-pint mason jars.
- **Monotub/SGFC (Shotgun Fruiting Chamber)**: Bulk cultivation using grain spawn inoculated with a master culture, then transferred to a bulk substrate (typically coco coir + vermiculite + gypsum).
- **Liquid culture**: Mycelium propagated in a nutrient broth for rapid expansion.

Optimal fruiting conditions include 23–27°C, 90–95% relative humidity, and indirect light on a 12/12 cycle. The species is a secondary decomposer, fruiting on a variety of organic substrates including grain, straw, composted manure, and wood-based substrates.

## Legal Status

Psilocybin and psilocin are listed in Schedule I of the UN Convention on Psychotropic Substances (1971). National legal status varies:

- **United States**: Schedule I under the Controlled Substances Act. However, Oregon Measure 109 (2020) legalized supervised psilocybin use, and several cities have deprioritized or decriminalized possession.
- **Netherlands**: Fresh psilocybin mushrooms (paddos) were banned in 2008, though psilocybin-containing truffles (*sclerotia*) remain legally sold in smart shops.
- **Brazil, Jamaica, Bahamas**: Psilocybin mushrooms are not specifically scheduled and may be legally possessed or sold.

## Notable Cultivars

Numerous cultivated varieties exist, selected for growth characteristics, potency, or morphology:

- **Golden Teacher**: Popular beginner variety with moderate potency and large fruiting bodies.
- **B+**: Robust grower tolerant of varied conditions; large caps.
- **Penis Envy**: Characteristically thick stipe and underdeveloped cap; reportedly higher potency.
- **Cambodian**: Fast colonizer originating from specimens collected near Angkor Wat.
- **African Transkei**: Known for visually intense effects; collected from wild specimens in South Africa.
