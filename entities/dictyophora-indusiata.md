# Dictyophora indusiata

## Overview

Dictyophora indusiata, commonly known as the bamboo mushroom, veiled lady mushroom, or long net stinkhorn, is a saprotrophic basidiomycete fungus in the family Phallaceae. It is distinguished by its striking fruiting body — a white, cylindrical receptacle topped with a conical cap and draped in a lacy, net-like indusium (veil) that extends to the ground. Native to tropical and subtropical regions of Asia, Australia, and the Americas, it is highly prized in Chinese cuisine for its delicate texture and is the subject of growing pharmacological research.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Phallales
- **Family:** Phallaceae
- **Genus:** Dictyophora
- **Species:** D. indusiata (Vent.) Desv. (1809)

### Synonyms

- *Phallus indusiatus* Vent. (1798) — original basionym
- *Dictyophora duplicata* (Bosc.) E. Fisch. (1888) — often treated as separate
- *Dictyophora multicolor* Berk. & Broome (1868) — sometimes considered conspecific
- *Phallus indusiatus* f. *roseus* — pink-tinged form

### Etymology

The genus name derives from Greek *dictyon* (net) and *phora* (bearing), referring to the characteristic reticulate veil. The specific epithet *indusiata* comes from Latin *indusium* (undergarment or veil), describing the conspicuous net-like structure hanging from the pileus.

## Morphology

### Fruiting Body Structure

- **Immature stage (egg stage):** Ovoid to subglobose, 3–5 cm diameter, white to cream-colored, attached to white rhizomorphs at the base; contains the fully formed immature structures within a gelatinous peridium.
- **Mature receptacle (stipe):** Cylindrical, hollow, 12–25 cm tall × 2–4 cm diameter, white to pale cream, spongy texture, arising from a volva at ground level.
- **Pileus (cap):** Conical to bell-shaped, 2–4 cm wide, covered with a reticulate greenish-brown to olive gleba on the exposed upper surface.
- **Indusium (veil):** Reticulate, skirt-like mesh extending from the underside of the pileus to near ground level, 10–20 cm long; white to cream, composed of a delicate open mesh network — the most distinctive feature.
- **Gleba:** Olive-green to brown, slimy, foul-smelling at maturity, coating the upper surface of the pileus; contains spores and attracts insect dispersers.
- **Volva:** Cup-shaped at base, 3–5 cm wide, persistent, white, often buried in substrate.

### Microscopic Features

- **Basidiospores:** Ellipsoidal to cylindrical, smooth, 3.5–5.0 × 1.5–2.0 µm, hyaline, inamyloid.
- **Basidia:** Clavate, 4-spored, 12–16 × 4–5 µm.
- **Hymenium:** Located on the exposed upper surface of the pileus, irregular and convoluted.

## Distribution and Habitat

- **Geographic range:** Widely distributed in tropical and subtropical regions. Found throughout southern China, Japan, Korea, Taiwan, Southeast Asia (Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia, Philippines), northeastern Australia, tropical Africa, and parts of the Americas (southern United States through Central and [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-south-america]]).
- **Habitat:** Terrestrial, saprotrophic. Grows on decomposing woody material, bamboo debris, leaf litter, and rich organic soils in forests, gardens, and bamboo groves.
- **Substrate association:** Strongly associated with bamboo (Bambusoideae) litter but not obligate; also found on other hardwood debris in mixed forests.
- **Seasonality:** Fruiting occurs during warm, wet periods — typically late spring through early autumn depending on latitude; most abundant during the monsoon season in tropical Asia.
- **Ecological role:** Primary decomposer of lignocellulosic material, particularly bamboo. The foul-smelling gleba attracts flies and beetles, which disperse spores to new substrates.

## Cultivation

### Commercial Production

Dictyophora indusiata is commercially cultivated in China, particularly in Fujian, Guizhou, and Yunnan provinces, where it is considered a delicacy.

- **Substrate:** Traditionally grown on bamboo sawdust mixed with rice bran (80:20 ratio). Alternative substrates include hardwood sawdust (beech, oak), wheat straw, [[fungi-in-composting-and-agricultural-waste-conversion]] supplemented with nitrogen sources.
- **[[spawn-preparation]]:** Grain spawn (wheat, rye, or sorghum) inoculated from pure culture. [[brassinosteroid-psilocybe-cubensis-mycelial-growth-research]] rate is moderate — full colonization of substrate bags takes 25–35 days at 22–26°C.
[[fruiting-conditions]] ions:** Requires high humidity (85–95%), temperature 22–28°C, diffuse light, and adequate fresh air exchange. A cold shock or temperature fluctuation can stimulate [[growing-gourmet-primordia-formation-pinning]].
- **Casing:** Not always required but improves yield. A thin layer of sterilized soil or peat-based casing is sometimes applied.
- **Y [[stamets-biological-efficiency-yield]] ficiency typically 40–60% over 2–3 flushes. First flush produces the largest [[bloomfield-asterophora-and-mycoparasites-of-fruiting-bodies]].
- **Growth cycle:** From inoculation to first harvest is approximately 50–70 days. Total cropping cycle is 3–4 months.
- **Challenges:** Susceptible to contamination by Trichoderma and other molds during spawn run. The egg stage is vulnerable to insect larvae and mites.

## Nutritional Composition

Dictyophora indusiata is valued nutritionally as a low-calorie, high-fiber food with notable bioactive content.
