# Eleusinian Mysteries

## Description
The Eleusinian Mysteries were initiatory rites held annually in ancient Greece at Eleusis (near Athens), representing one of the most important and long-lasting mystery cults of the ancient world. In *The Psychedelic Experience*, Leary cites the Eleusinian Mysteries as one of the historical traditions that practiced "the consciousness-expansion experience — the pre-mortem death and rebirth rite," drawing a direct parallel between these ancient initiations and the psychedelic experience.

## Classification
- **Category:** Historical Tradition / Mystery Cult
- **Domain:** Ancient Greek religion, initiation rites, consciousness expansion
- **Era:** circa 1500 BCE – 392 CE (nearly 2,000 years of continuous practice)

## Key Facts
- Held at Eleusis, approximately 18 km northwest of Athens
- Dedicated to Demeter (goddess of agriculture) and Persephone (queen of the underworld)
- Initiation involved a symbolic death and rebirth experience
- The secret rites (telete) were held annually in September/October
- Open to all who spoke Greek and had not committed murder — including women and slaves
- The kykeon, a ceremonial drink consumed during initiation, is widely theorized to have contained ergot alkaloids (related to LSD)
- Initiation was said to change one's relationship with death: "beautiful indeed was the vision...we dwelt in pure light" (fragment attributed to an initiate)
- Jung connects the Eleusinian initiation to the Bardo Thodol's purpose: "an initiation of the living was a preparation for the Beyond"

## Connection to the Bardo Framework
Leary places the Eleusinian Mysteries alongside Vedic sages and Tantric practitioners as keepers of "the consciousness-expansion experience — the pre-mortem death and rebirth rite." The secret, passed down for over 2,500 years, was "that it is possible to cut beyond ego-consciousness, to tune in on neurological processes which flash by at the speed of light, and to become aware of the enormous treasury of ancient racial knowledge welded into the nucleus of every cell in your body." Modern psychedelic chemicals, Leary argues, provide a key to this same forgotten realm.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology
The Eleusinian Mysteries are directly relevant to understanding the cultural and historical context of entheogenic mushroom use. The theory that kykeon contained ergot ([[claviceps-purpurea]], a fungus parasitic on grains) links the Mysteries to fungal pharmacology. More broadly, the Mysteries demonstrate that structured, ritual use of consciousness-altering substances has ancient precedent, supporting the argument for careful, guided psychedelic use in modern therapeutic contexts.
