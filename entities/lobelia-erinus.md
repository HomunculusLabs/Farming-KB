## Overview

Lobelia erinus, commonly called edging lobelia or garden lobelia, is a tender herbaceous plant in the family Campanulaceae. It is native to southern Africa, where it occurs from South Africa to Mozambique and Malawi.

The species is one of the most widely cultivated summer bedding plants in [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]] worldwide. Its profusion of small, vividly blue flowers makes it indispensable for [[hamilton-hanging-baskets-organic]], window boxes, and edging.

Most garden lobelias are treated as annuals in temperate zones, though the species is perennial in frost-free climates. Hundreds of cultivars have been bred, ranging from deep blue to white, pink, and purple.

The plant contains toxic alkaloids, including lobeline, which has a history of use in respiratory medicine. However, ornamental cultivars are bred for flower production, not alkaloid content.

The genus Lobelia is named after Matthias de l'Obel (1538–1616), a Flemish botanist and physician to King James I of England. The specific epithet "erinus" refers to the plant's resemblance to species in the genus Erinus (Plantaginaceae).

Lobelia erinus was introduced to European horticulture in the mid-18th century and rapidly became a staple of Victorian bedding schemes. Breeding efforts have produced over 200 named cultivars to date.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:**
- Kingdom: Plantae
- Family: Campanulaceae
- Subfamily: Lobelioideae
- Genus: Lobelia
- Species: L. erinus L.

The plant forms a low, spreading mound 10–15 cm tall and 15–30 cm wide. Trailing cultivars can spread to 45 cm or more in hanging baskets.

Leaves are small, ovate to lanceolate, 5–15 mm long, dark green or bronze-tinted, and arranged alternately on slender stems. The foliage is slightly glossy.

Flowers are produced in profusion from early summer to first frost. Each flower is 1–1.5 cm across, tubular with a two-lipped corolla split on the upper side.

The lower lip has three prominent lobes, while the upper lip has two smaller reflexed lobes. The most common color is intense blue with a white eye, though cultivars span the full spectrum.

The fruit is a small ovoid capsule containing numerous dust-like seeds. Seeds are among the smallest in the horticultural trade, with approximately 30,000 seeds per gram.

Chromosome number is 2n = 14. The species is diploid and shows moderate genetic variability across its native range in southern Africa.

## Distribution and Habitat

Lobelia erinus is native to the summer rainfall regions of southern Africa, from the Eastern Cape through KwaZulu-Natal to Mpumalanga, Mozambique, and the highlands of eastern Zimbabwe and Malawi.

It grows naturally along stream banks, in damp grasslands, and on forest margins at elevations of 200–2000 m. The species favors moist, partially shaded sites with well-drained soils.

In its native habitat, the plant behaves as a short-lived perennial, regrowing from the base after winter dormancy. It is evergreen in frost-free locations.

The species has naturalized in parts of Australia, New Zealand, and coastal California. These introductions resulted from garden escapes and are generally not considered invasive.

Naturalized populations are typically found near streams, drainage ditches, and other consistently moist sites. The plant lacks the vegetative aggressiveness of weedy Lobelia species.

Genetic analysis of African populations reveals two distinct clades: a southern clade from the Eastern Cape and KwaZulu-Natal, and a northern clade from Mpumalanga to Malawi. These may represent incipient speciation driven by habitat differences.

## Cultivation

Garden lobelia is universally grown as a half-hardy annual from seed sown indoors 8–12 weeks before the last frost. Seeds require light for germination and should be surface-sown on moist compost.

Germination occurs in 10–21 days at 18–21 °C. Seedlings are tiny and require careful handling during pricking out and transplanting.

The species prefers rich, moisture-retentive soil in full sun to partial shade. Consistent watering is essential — plants wilt rapidly when dry and may not recover fully.

Hardy only to USDA zones 10–11, the plant is killed by light frost. In cooler zones, it is treated as a summer annual and replaced each year.

Trailing cultivars are the standard for hanging baskets and containers. Compact mounding types are preferred for formal bedding and edging schemes.

Common cultivar series include 'Crystal Palace' (deep blue, bronze foliage), 'Cambridge Blue' (light blue), 'String of Pearls' (mixed colors, trailing), and 'Regatta' ([[equatorial-photoperiod-early-flowering]], trailing).

## Ecological Role

In its native southern Africa, Lobelia erinus is visited by a range of small bees, flies, and butterflies. The tubular flowers with a landing platform on the lower lip facilitate pollination by short-tongued insects.

The small, lightweight seeds are dispersed primarily by wind and water. In streamside habitats, water carries seeds downstream to establish new colonies along the banks.

Garden plantings provide nectar for bees and hoverflies throughout the summer months. The long bloom period makes lobelia a reliable food source in [[query-how-do-green-roofs-and-living-walls-benefit-urban-environments]].

The species produces pyrrolizidine alkaloids as a chemical defense against herbivory. These compounds render the foliage unpalatable to most mammalian and insect browsers.
