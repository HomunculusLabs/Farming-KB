### Mud Planter Technique

- Used for palms and trees near groundwater in sandy soils.
- Pit or slot-planted in a "mud planter" hole.
- Avoids sandblast, conserves water (in mud and sand).
- Escapes excessive heat of shallow sands (which can reach 70°C).
- Can be line, drip, or pot-irrigated.
- Mud must be locally available; worthwhile only if trees are of great value.

### Calcrete Soil Treatment

- In calcrete soils of coasts and islands, the concreted layer must be broken open at every tree site.
- A mulch pit prepared with elemental sulphur and mineral elements.
- A few vigorous-rooted species will sit on calcrete but are at risk from drought and windthrow.
- Tree growth keeps the pattern open and cracked for interplant.
- Fast method uses auger and about one-third of a plug of dynamite.
- Same technique for fence posts or shattering concretions in shales and mudstones.

### Companion Planting for Establishment

- When bulldozer is available, rip-lines made for tree lines.
- Companion crop of small legumes seeded between trees along the rip.
- Desert fenugreek, lucerne, tagasaste, and gourds intercropped with palms, *Casuarina*, or jujube.

### Soil Amendments for Establishment

- **Gypsum**: Helps roots penetrate clay soils.
- **Bentonite**: Assists sands to hold moisture.
- **Dried seaweed crumble**: Added to planting holes, forms a gel in rain, enables seedlings to penetrate to deeper levels in first season.
- **Commercial soil gel additives**: Available for potted plants in nursery; some function for many months.

### Morning Shade Principle

- Pits and hollows shaded from morning sun are superior planting sites.
- Night condensation stays longer in morning shade, enabling plants to use moisture longer.
- In unshaded sites, morning sun evaporates night dews and plants suffer longer water deprivation.
- Heat itself is less a factor than water availability early in the day.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- Tree establishment pits create organic matter accumulation zones that support fungal colonization.
- Companion legume intercropping promotes mycorrhizal networks that benefit all plants in the association.
- Mulch pits around establishing trees can be inoculated with mycorrhizal fungi to accelerate root colonization.
- Mud planters provide a moist, organic-rich medium for fungal hyphae to establish alongside tree roots.
- Seaweed crumble in planting holes introduces beneficial marine-derived fungi and bacteria.
- The layered establishment approach (pit, mulch, shade, companion) creates diverse fungal niches.
- Established tree root systems become fungal highways connecting surface mulch to deep soil moisture.

## Species Mentioned for Intercropping

- Desert fenugreek (*Trigonella* spp.)
- Lucerne/alfalfa (*Medicago sativa*)
- Tagasaste (*Chamaecytisus palmensis*)
- Gourds (*Cucurbitaceae*)
- Palms (*Arecaceae*)
- *Casuarina* spp.
- Jujube (*Ziziphus* spp.)
