## Overview

**Piper betle** L. (betel pepper) is an evergreen dioecious vine in the
family Piperaceae, cultivated across South and [[antifungal-secondary-metabolites-coprophilous-fungi]] with documented antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory,
antioxidant, and antidiabetic properties.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

- **Kingdom:** Plantae; **Order:** Piperales
- **Family:** Piperaceae; **Genus:** Piper; **Species:** *P. betle* L.
- **Synonyms:** *Chavica betle* (Miq.) Miq.

The plant is a climbing or scrambling vine reaching 1–5 m in length.
Stems are green to greenish-brown, cylindrical, and jointed at nodes
where adventitious roots form for attachment to supports.

Leaves are alternate, simple, cordate (heart-shaped), 5–14 cm long and
3–9 cm wide, with an entire margin and deeply cordate base. The upper
surface is glossy green; the lower is paler with prominent venation.
Leaves exude a characteristic pungent aroma when crushed.

Inflorescences are cylindrical spikes 3–7 cm long, opposite to leaves.
Male spikes are slender; female spikes are thicker and produce small
drupaceous fruits 3–5 mm in diameter.

## Distribution and Habitat

Native to the Indian subcontinent, Sri Lanka, Malaysia, and Indonesia.
Widely naturalized throughout tropical and subtropical Asia, from India
eastward through Thailand, Vietnam, and southern China.

It thrives in warm, humid tropical climates below 1,000 m elevation.
Optimal growth occurs in partial shade with 2,000–3,000 mm annual
rainfall and temperatures of 20–35°C. Prefers rich, well-drained
loamy soils with pH 5.5–7.0 and high [[jeavons-seed-propagation-techniques]] is unreliable and slow.

Support structures (called "boroj" in Bengal) are essential for
commercial cultivation. Live supports from *[[sesbania-grandiflora]]*
or *Erythrina* spp. are traditional; concrete or bamboo trellises
are used in modern systems.

Leaves are harvested 60–90 days after planting, then every 15–20 days.
Annual yield ranges from 80–150 leaves per plant. Major varieties
include 'Bangla,' 'Calcutta,' 'Kapoori,' 'Magduma,' and 'Sanchi,'
differing in leaf size, aroma intensity, and pungency.
