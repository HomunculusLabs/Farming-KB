# Pinus banksiana

## Overview

**Pinus banksiana**, the jack pine, is a small to medium conifer native to
boreal and sub-boreal regions [[berry-growing]].

It is one of the most widely distributed pines in Canada, forming extensive
forests across the boreal shield and northern prairie regions.

Jack pine is distinguished by its serotinous cones, which remain sealed with
resin for decades and open primarily in response to wildfire heat.

This fire-adapted strategy makes jack pine a classic post-fire colonizer and
a defining species of the boreal fire regime.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Division:** Pinophyta
- **Class:** Pinopsida
- **Order:** Pinales
- **Family:** Pinaceae
- **Genus:** Pinus
- **Subgenus:** Pinus (Diploxylon / hard pines)
- **Species:** *Pinus banksiana* Lamb.

Described by Aylmer Bourke Lambert in 1803, named for Sir Joseph Banks, the
English botanist who accompanied Captain Cook.

Jack pine is placed in subsection *Australes*, most closely related to
[[pinus-contorta]], with occasional hybridization where ranges overlap.

## Morphology

Jack pine typically reaches 9–18 m in height and 20–30 cm DBH on typical
boreal sites; on favorable sites it can reach 24 m.

The crown is narrow, irregular, and often sparsely branched, with crooked or
leaning stems common.

Bark is thin, dark gray to reddish-brown, divided into irregular scaly ridges.

Needles occur in fascicles of two, 2–4 cm long and 1–1.5 mm wide — among
the shortest of all [[growing-gourmet-north-american-reishi-species]] pines.

They are yellow-green to dark green, slightly twisted, and persist 2–3 years.

Cones are strongly serotinous, curved, 3–5 cm long, often clustered in
whorls on branch tips.

Cone scales are thick, armed with small prickles, sealed with resin that
requires temperatures above 50°C to melt.

Non-serotinous cones also occur on some trees as a backup reproductive
strategy.

Seeds are small (3–4 mm), dark brown, with a 6–10 mm wing.

The root system is shallow and widespreading, adapted to thin boreal soils
and permafrost-affected substrates.

## Distribution and Habitat

Jack pine ranges from the Mackenzie River delta eastward across Canada to
Nova Scotia, and south through the Great Lakes region to northern Minnesota,
Wisconsin, and Michigan.

It is most abundant on the Canadian Shield, occupying well-drained to
excessively drained sandy and gravelly soils.

The species is extremely cold-hardy, tolerating winter temperatures below
-50°C across its northern range.
