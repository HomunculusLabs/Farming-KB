## Overview

Wes Jackson (1936–) is an American plant geneticist and agronomist referenced on page 36 of David Holmgren's *Permaculture: Principles and Pathways Beyond Sustainability*. He founded **The Land Institute** in Salina, Kansas, and pioneered the development of **perennial grain crops** as an alternative to annual agriculture.

## Background

Born on a farm near Topeka, Kansas, Jackson studied botany and genetics at Kansas State University and earned his doctorate from North Carolina State University. He founded The Land Institute in 1976.

## The Land Institute

The Land Institute develops agricultural systems based on natural ecosystems rather than annual monoculture. Key programs include perennial wheat breeding, **Kernza** (a perennial grain from intermediate wheatgrass), and perennial sorghum, sunflower, and rice breeding.

## Core Philosophy

- Annual crops require soil disturbance every year, causing erosion
- Nature's ecosystems are predominantly perennial
- Agriculture should mimic natural patterns
- Soil is essentially non-renewable on human timescales

## Connection to Permaculture

Jackson's work aligns with permaculture's emphasis on perennial food systems, working with nature, and rebuilding natural capital (Holmgren page 36). His approach models agricultural systems on natural prairie ecosystems.

## Key Publications

- *New Roots for Agriculture* (1980)
- *Altars of Unhewn Stone* (1987)
- *Consulting the Genius of the Place* (2010)

## Relevance to Mycology

- Perennial root systems support extensive mycorrhizal fungal networks
- No-till perennial systems preserve soil structure and fungal hyphae
- Prairie ecosystem models include important fungal decomposer communities
- Kernza creates stable root zone environments for mycorrhizal associations

## Legacy

Jackson received the Right Livelihood Award (1992). Kernza is now grown commercially. His vision of perennial agriculture continues to influence ecological design and regenerative agriculture.
