The species is closely related to and often confused with lion's mane ([[panax-ginseng]], including neuroprotective and immunomodulatory compounds shared with other Hericium species.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Russulales
- **Family:** Hericiaceae
- **Genus:** Hericium
- **Species:** H. laciniatum

**Basionym:** Hydnum laciniatum Leers (1778).

**Synonyms:**

- Hericium ramosum (Merat) Banker (1906), widely used in [[telluride-mushroom-festival-modern-mycological-culture]] literature.

## Morphology

**Fruiting body:** Soft-fleshy, initially compact, expanding into a branched, coral-like structure. Individual branches arise from a short, thickened base or a rudimentary stipe. The overall mass can reach 5–20 cm across.

**Spines (teeth):** The most distinctive feature — long, slender, tapered spines measuring 5–25 mm in length and 0.5–1 mm in diameter. They hang downward in curtains or tufts from the branches and branch tips. Spines are white when fresh, turning yellowish-brown with age.

**Flesh:** White, soft, and watery when young. Becomes tough and fibrous in maturity. Has a mild, pleasant odor reminiscent of seafood, similar to H. erinaceus.

**Spore print:** White to cream.

**Spores:** Globose to subglobose, smooth, 5–7 × 5–6 µm, hyaline, amyloid, occurring on basidia at the tips of the spines.

**Chemical reactions:** Flesh stains brownish to reddish-brown when cut or bruised, especially in age. KOH on flesh produces a negative to weakly pinkish reaction.

## Distribution and Habitat

Hericium laciniatum has a broad distribution across [[mushroom-species-cultivation-parameters]]. It is considered more challenging to fruit reliably in artificial conditions.

**Substrate:** Supplemented hardwood sawdust (oak, beech, or maple) at 65–75% moisture content. Wheat bran or rice bran supplementation at 10–20% improves yields. Some growers use a mixed substrate with small amounts of soy meal.

**Spawn run:** Optimal temperature 20–25°C. Full colonization typically takes 18–25 days, slightly slower than H. erinaceus.

**[[biological-efficiency]] ranges from 50–85%, lower than the 100–150% achievable with H. erinaceus. The species is sensitive to contamination, particularly Trichoderma, during the spawn run.

**Harvest:** Fruit bodies should be harvested when spines are fully elongated but still white. Post-harvest shelf life is limited — 5–7 days refrigerated.
