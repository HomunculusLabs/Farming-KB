# Monacrosporium haptotylum

**Monacrosporium haptotylum** (synonym: *Dactylaria candida*) is a nematophagous ascomycete fungus that captures and kills nematodes using adhesive knob traps.

It has become a model organism for understanding the molecular mechanisms of fungal predation.

The species has been the subject of extensive genomic studies including EST database construction and cDNA microarray analysis.

## Trap Morphology and Infection Mechanism

M. haptotylum infects nematodes using a specialized adhesive knob structure.

During growth in liquid cultures with heavy aeration, the connections between the traps (knobs) and mycelium can be broken easily.

The knobs can be separated from the mycelium by filtration.

The isolated knobs retain their function as infection structures and can independently capture and infect nematodes.

This makes them useful for experimental study.

When knobs are incubated with *Caenorhabditis elegans* on water [[agar]] plates, approximately 30 to 35 percent of nematodes become non-motile after 4 hours of infection.

After 24 hours, approximately 80 to 85 percent are immobile, with fungal hyphae growing inside the infected nematodes.

The two-phase infection process allows researchers to study early and late molecular events during pathogenesis separately.

## EST Database and Genomics

An EST (Expressed Sequence Tag) database has been generated for M. haptotylum using four directional cDNA libraries constructed from:

- Mycelium
- Isolated knobs
- Knobs infecting *C. elegans* for 4 hours
- Knobs infecting *C. elegans* for 24 hours

In total, 8463 ESTs were sequenced and assembled into 3121 contigs that putatively represent unique genes and/or transcripts.

Between 5 and 37 percent of assembled sequences displayed high similarity to sequences in the GenBank nr protein database.

A large fraction (38 to 60 percent) showed no homology to known protein sequences.

The highest proportion of orphans was found in the knob (58 percent) and 24-hour infection (60 percent) libraries.

The high proportion of orphan sequences suggests that nematode trapping and infection involve a substantial number of novel genes not found in other fungal lifestyles.

## Comparative Gene Expression

Genes differentially regulated in the adhesive knobs of M. haptotylum show striking parallels with appressoria of plant pathogenic fungi.

These include [[magnaporthe-grisea]] and [[blumeria-graminis]].

Key expression patterns include:

- **Upregulated in knobs**: Serine protease (subtilisin), peptidyl-prolyl cis-trans isomerase, gEgh16 (also upregulated in M. grisea and B. graminis appressoria)
- **Downregulated in knobs**: Chitinase, translation elongation factor (TEF1), glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate dehydrogenase, enolase, GTPase Rho1, profilin

The similarity between knob formation in nematophagous fungi and appressorium formation in plant pathogens suggests convergent evolution of infection structures across different fungal lifestyles.

Both processes involve an extensive synthesis and turnover of proteins.

Both generate physical forces for host penetration.

The upregulation of serine proteases in both contexts indicates that proteolytic degradation of host structural proteins is a shared strategy for tissue invasion.

## Phylogenetic Position

Phylogenetic analysis based on 18S rDNA sequences shows that nematode-trapping fungi form a monophyletic clade among an unresolved cluster of apothecial ascomycetes.

The phylogenetic pattern within the clade is concordant with the morphology of the traps.

Related nematophagous species in the same clade include:

- [[arthrobotrys-oligospora]] (hyphal network traps)
- [[arthrobotrys-musiformis]]
- *Arthrobotrys pyriformis*
- *Arthrobotrys superba*
- *Monacrosporium ellipsosporum*
- *Monacrosporium gephyropagum*
- *Monacrosporium psychrophilum*
- *Dactylella oxyspora*
- *Dactylella rhopalota*
- [[duddingtonia-flagrans]]

All branches within the clade have bootstrap support values above 50, confirming the monophyly of nematode-trapping fungi.

The concordance between trap morphology and phylogeny supports the hypothesis that diverse trapping mechanisms evolved as specializations within a single predatory lineage.

## Model Organism for Pathogenesis

M. haptotylum has proven tractable as a model for studying fungal infection mechanisms.

The nematode *Caenorhabditis elegans* serves as a convenient host for pathogenesis assays.

The genome of C. elegans is fully sequenced, facilitating molecular analysis of host-pathogen interactions.

The ability to isolate functional trap structures independently of the mycelium makes this system uniquely amenable to controlled infection experiments.

## Ecological Role in Soil

Nematophagous fungi like M. haptotylum occupy an important position in soil food webs.

They regulate nematode populations through predation.

Their activity contributes to the suppression of plant-parasitic nematodes in natural ecosystems.

This has been explored for application in sustainable agriculture as an alternative to chemical nematicides.
