## Description

A "cool greenhouse" is Eliot Coleman"s term for a minimally heated greenhouse structure where the thermostat is set just above freezing (approximately 35°F / 1.5°C). This minimal heat input expands the range of crops that can be grown through the coldest winter months beyond what is possible in unheated "cold houses," while keeping energy costs minimal.

## Classification

- **Type**: Agricultural structure / Growing system
- **Category**: Protected cultivation, minimally heated greenhouse
- **Temperature range**: Nighttime minimum ~35°F (1.5°C) to 32°F (0°C)
- **Energy input**: Minimal (propane, wood, or waste oil)

## Key Facts

- Thermostat set at 35°F (1.5°C) — just warm enough to prevent freezing
- Creates the equivalent of "the month of May" compared to outdoor winter conditions
- Doubles the number of winter harvests compared to unheated cold houses
- The cost of a heater is much less than building a new greenhouse to gain equivalent production
- Double-covered (two layers of plastic with air inflation) for 40% fuel savings
- Coleman describes the minimal heat as "a different kind of protective layer"
- Now used on only one small area at [[four-season-farm]], as the farm has moved toward fully unheated systems

## Crops Enabled by Cool House

Crops that cannot be quality-harvested in cold houses during December–February but succeed in the cool house:
- Turnips (e.g., 'Hakurei')
- Radishes (e.g., 'Tinto', 'Shunkyo')
- 'Bianca Riccia' endive
- Standard arugula
- Lettuce (especially oak-leaf types)
- Broccoli transplants

## Heating Options Explored

- **Propane heaters**: Standard, simple, moderate cost
- **Wood furnace**: Lower operating cost, more labor; installed at Four Season Farm's packing house
- **Wood-fired hot-water boiler**: Very high initial cost (10x propane heaters), would heat all cool houses
- **Used cooking oil**: Ingenious direct-burn system used by Laughing Stock Farm
- **Earth tubes**: Passive geothermal — fan-blown air through buried pipes (considered but not implemented)

## Temperature Research Findings

- Informal trials suggest hardy crops may tolerate temperatures down to 26°F (-3°C) without cosmetic damage
- However, temperatures just below freezing slow growth for up to a week afterward
- Maintaining nighttime minimums above 32°F (0°C) prevents this growth setback
- Soil temperature in minimally heated houses drops to 42°F (6°C) at 4-inch depth by midwinter

## Relevance to Cultivation

The cool greenhouse represents the hybrid approach to winter production — combining the simplicity of unheated structures with minimal energy input to expand crop variety and harvest frequency. Coleman likens this to the hybrid car concept: a small amount of supplementary energy (like a gasoline engine) combined with the primary passive system (like an electric motor) may be more economically efficient than going fully one direction. However, Coleman's personal preference has evolved toward fully unheated systems, using the cool house only for limited trials. For growers in Zones 3-6, the cool greenhouse approach may be a practical necessity for certain crops during the coldest months.
