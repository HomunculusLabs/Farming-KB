## Overview

Convolvulus prostratus, commonly known as Shankhpushpi, is a perennial creeping herb in the family Convolvulaceae, native to the Indian subcontinent. It is one of the most important medhya rasayanas (cerebral tonics) in Ayurvedic medicine, traditionally used to enhance cognition, memory, and mental clarity.

The plant is a low-growing, spreading herb with small elliptical leaves and pale blue to white funnel-shaped flowers. It thrives in sandy, well-drained soils across plains and low hills of northern and central India.

Shankhpushpi holds a central place in classical Ayurvedic formulations, particularly those targeting neurological and psychological conditions. Modern pharmacological research has validated several traditional uses, including anxiolytic, memory-enhancing, and antidepressant activities.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Magnoliopsida → Solanales → **Convolvulaceae → *Convolvulus → *C. prostratus***

The taxonomy of Shankhpushpi has been contentious, with the name applied to multiple species across different regions of India. The most commonly accepted [[dmt-botanical-sources-pharmacology]] include Convolvulus prostratus (syn. C. pluricaulis), Evolvulus alsinoides, and [[clitoria-ternatea]].

The accepted name C. prostratus (Forssk.) Choisy reflects its prostrate growth habit. Some authorities maintain C. microphyllus and C. pluricaulis as separate species, while others treat them as synonyms within a morphologically variable complex.

The plant forms a dense prostrate mat, with stems 20–60 cm long rooting at nodes. Leaves are small, 0.5–2 cm long, elliptic to oblong, glabrous to sparsely pubescent. The characteristic flowers are funnel-shaped, 8–12 mm long, pale blue to white, borne singly in leaf axils.

The fruit is a globular capsule approximately 4 mm in diameter containing 2–4 seeds. Rooting is adventitious at stem nodes, enabling rapid vegetative spread. Flowering occurs from July to October, with fruiting through November.

## Distribution and Habitat

C. prostratus is native to the Indian subcontinent, found predominantly in the plains and foothills of northern and central India, extending into Pakistan, Nepal, and Bangladesh. It is cultivated in several Indian states including Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, and Gujarat.

The species favors sandy or sandy-loam soils in open, sunny locations. It is commonly found in wastelands, grasslands, and along field margins at elevations up to approximately 1,500 meters. It tolerates semi-arid conditions and moderate drought once established.

Wild populations are under pressure from overharvesting for the herbal trade. Cultivation practices have been developed to meet demand while reducing pressure on natural stands.

## Bioactive Constituents

The principal bioactive constituents include a range of alkaloids, flavonoids, and coumarins. The indole alkaloids shankhpushpine, convoline, convolidine, and convolvine are considered the primary neuroactive compounds.

Flavonoid glycosides, including kaempferol and quercetin derivatives, contribute to the antioxidant activity. Scopoletin, a coumarin, has been identified as a significant constituent with anti-inflammatory and anxiolytic properties.

Triterpenoids, including β-amyrin and its derivatives, have been isolated from aerial parts. The total alkaloid content of dried aerial parts ranges from 0.1–0.5%, depending on the source and processing method.

The roots contain a higher concentration of alkaloids compared to aerial parts, though the whole plant is traditionally used in formulations. Seasonal variation affects constituent profiles, with peak alkaloid content during the flowering period.

## Pharmacology and Traditional Use

In Ayurveda, Shankhpushpi is classified as a medhya rasayana — a rejuvenator of intellect and nervous tissue. It is a key ingredient in classical formulations such as Shankhpushpi Churna, Brahmi Ghrita, and Saraswatarishta, used for memory enhancement, anxiety, and insomnia.

Modern research has validated its anxiolytic effects through GABAergic modulation, with alkaloid fractions demonstrating benzodiazepine-like activity in animal models. Antidepressant effects mediated through monoamine oxidase inhibition and serotonin reuptake modulation have been documented.

Memory-enhancing properties are attributed to cholinergic modulation — extracts increase acetylcholinesterase inhibition and enhance cholinergic transmission in the hippocampus. Studies in scopolamine-induced amnesia models show significant reversal of cognitive deficits.

Anti-inflammatory activity occurs through inhibition of COX-2 and TNF-α pathways. Antioxidant effects are mediated by flavonoid content, with significant DPPH and superoxide radical scavenging activity.

The plant also demonstrates anticonvulsant, hepatoprotective, and hypolipidemic activities in preclinical studies. [[mushroom-cancer-clinical-trials-overview]] in humans have shown improvements in attention, memory, and anxiety scores with standardized extracts.

## Cultivation

C. prostratus is propagated through stem cuttings and seeds, with [[hamilton-vegetative-propagation]] preferred for maintaining consistent alkaloid profiles. Cuttings of 10–15 cm from healthy stock plants root readily in sandy nursery beds under mist.
