## Melia azedarach (White Cedar / Chinaberry)

## Description

melia azedarach entity, commonly known as white cedar, chinaberry, Persian
lilac, or bead tree, is a deciduous tree in the mahogany family (Meliaceae)
native to South and Southeast Asia but widely naturalised in tropical and
subtropical regions worldwide. It is valued in permaculture primarily for
its potent insecticidal properties—[[azadirachta-indica]]), sharing many bioactive properties
- Fast-growing, reaching useful size within 3-5 years
- Hardy in USDA zones 7-11, tolerating drought, heat, and poor soils
- Timber is hard, reddish, and resistant to termites
- Berries historically used to make beads and necklaces (hence "bead tree")

## Relevance to Cultivation

### Insecticidal Use in Permaculture

Mollison specifically recommends Melia azedarach oil as one of three
insecticidal oils (alongside neem and pyrethrum) applied to hessian
rubbing posts for lice control on pigs. The system works as follows:

- Hessian-wrapped posts are installed in pig ranges
- The hessian is treated with a mixture including Melia azedarach oil
- Pigs rub against the posts naturally to relieve skin irritation
- The insecticidal oil transfers to the pig's skin and coat
- Lice and other ectoparasites are killed or repelled by the compounds

This represents a self-administering parasite control system that requires
no handling of the animals, embodying [[permaculture-principles]] of designing
solutions that work with natural animal behaviour.

### Active Compounds

The insecticidal properties of Melia azedarach derive from several
bioactive limonoids and triterpenoids:

- **Meliatoxins:** Potent insect growth regulators and antifeedants
- **Azadirachtin-related compounds:** Similar mode of action to neem
- **Toosendanin:** A potent insecticide found in the bark
- These compounds disrupt insect moulting, feeding, and reproduction

### Permaculture Design Roles

- **Parasite control:** Oil used in rubbing posts for livestock lice management
- **Shade and shelter:** Spreading canopy provides shade for animals
- **Termite-resistant timber:** Useful for fence posts and structural elements
- **Ornamental value:** Attractive flowers and foliage in landscape design
- **Windbreak component:** Fast growth makes it useful in shelter plantings

### Companion Integration

Melia azedarach is used alongside neem (Azadirachta indica) and pyrethrum
(Tanacetum cinerariifolium) in Mollison's integrated parasite management
strategy. All three provide insecticidal oils but with different active
compounds, reducing the likelihood of parasite resistance developing.

### Cultivation Notes

- Propagates readily from seed or cuttings
- Prefers full sun and well-drained soils
- Tolerates drought once established
- Fast initial growth slows as tree matures
- Prune to maintain accessible shape if using for rubbing post material
- Fruit and seeds are toxic—site away from areas where livestock might
  consume fallen berries

### Toxicity Warning

All parts of Melia azedarach, especially the berries, are toxic to humans
and some mammals if ingested. Livestock poisoning has been reported from
consumption of fallen fruit. However, the topical use of oil preparations
for parasite control is well-established in traditional practice. Always
exercise caution and keep berries away from curious animals and children.

## Summary

Melia azedarach is a fast-growing insecticidal tree that Mollison
integrates into permaculture animal systems through its use in pig rubbing
posts for lice control. Combined with neem and pyrethrum oils, it forms
part of a self-administering, chemical-free parasite management system that
leverages the tree's potent natural insecticidal compounds.
