# Eleocharis dulcis (Chinese Water Chestnut)

## Description

Eleocharis dulcis is a grass-like aquatic plant cultivated for its edible corms, commonly known as Chinese water chestnuts. It is one of the most productive aquatic food crops in permaculture pond systems, thriving in shallow water environments where it produces crisp, sweet corms valued across Asian cuisines worldwide.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Family**: Cyperaceae (sedge family)
- **Genus**: Eleocharis
- **Species**: E. dulcis
- **Common names**: Chinese water chestnut, water chestnut, matai (Chinese: 马蹄)

## Key Facts

- Grows in shallow ponds, typically 2-60 cm of water depth
- Produces underground corms that are the primary edible portion
- Corms are crisp, sweet, and retain their crunch even after cooking due to unique cell structure containing ferulic acid cross-links
- Widely cultivated throughout southern China, Southeast Asia, and increasingly in temperate permaculture systems
- Harvested by draining ponds and hand-digging the corms from mud
- Can be grown as part of polyculture systems alongside fish, frogs, and other aquatic plants

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

In permaculture design, Eleocharis dulcis is a keystone shallow-pond crop. Mollison specifically recommends it for small garden ponds of 2-60 cm depth, where it thrives alongside kangkong (Ipomoea aquatica), watercress (Rorippa aquatica), taro (Colocasia esculenta), frogs, and small fish.

The plant serves multiple functions in the pond ecosystem:

- **Food production**: Corms provide starchy carbohydrate and can be eaten raw, boiled, or stir-fried
- **Water filtration**: Dense growth helps absorb excess nutrients from pond water
- **Habitat structure**: Provides cover and spawning substrate for small fish and invertebrates
- **Edge effect enhancement**: Contributes to the productive margin zone of pond systems

The corms can also be processed into flour or starch, adding versatility to their food value. In integrated systems, boiled taro, cassava, or plantain can supplement fish food, and water chestnut plays a similar supplementary role.

## Cultivation Notes

- Requires warm growing season; corms are planted in spring in mud or shallow water
- Prefers rich, organic muck substrate
- Full sun to partial shade
- Corms mature in approximately 6-7 months
- Yields can reach several kilograms per square metre in well-managed systems

## Related Species

- Eleocharis obtusa (spike rush) -- related species used in wetland restoration
- Eleocharis palustris (common spike-rush) -- widespread in temperate pond margins

## Traditional Uses

Chinese water chestnuts have been cultivated for over 3,000 years in southern China. The corms feature prominently in Cantonese cuisine, where their crisp texture is prized in stir-fries, dim sum fillings, and soups. In traditional Chinese medicine, water chestnut is considered cooling and is used to treat coughs, hypertension, and digestive complaints.

The plant also plays a role in biogas production systems where pond vegetation is harvested and fed into digesters, producing methane for cooking and heating while the nutrient-rich effluent fertilises additional pond or garden crops.

## Ecological Role

In natural wetland systems, Eleocharis species provide critical habitat for waterfowl, amphibians, and aquatic invertebrates. The dense sedge growth creates sheltered microhabitats within the shallow water zone, supporting populations of Gambusia and other small forage fish that in turn feed larger predatory species in the pond food web.
