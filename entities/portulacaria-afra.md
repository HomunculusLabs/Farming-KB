## Overview

**Portulacaria afra** (Jacq.) DC., commonly known as **elephant bush** or **pork bush**, is a fleshy-leaved evergreen succulent shrub native to southern Africa. It belongs to the family Didiereaceae, having been reclassified from Portulacaceae based on molecular phylogenetics.

The species is widely cultivated as an ornamental houseplant and bonsai subject worldwide. In its native South Africa, it is a dominant component of Albany thickets and serves as a primary food source for elephants and other browsing herbivores.

Portulacaria afra has gained attention for its exceptional carbon sequestration capacity in degraded thicket ecosystems. It is also edible for humans, with a tart, acidic flavor used in salads and traditional medicine.

The species has naturalized in parts of California, the Mediterranean, and Australia where it has escaped cultivation. In favorable conditions it can form dense thickets 3–4 m tall. It is not considered invasive in most regions due to its dependence on well-drained soils and limited frost tolerance.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:**
- Kingdom: Plantae
- Clade: Tracheophytes
- Clade: Angiosperms
- Clade: Eudicots
- Order: Caryophyllales
- Family: Didiereaceae
- Genus: *Portulacaria*
- Species: *P. afra*

**Authority:** (Jacq.) DC., published in Prodromus Systematis Naturalis Regni Vegetabilis (1828). Basionym: *Crassula afra* Jacq.

**Morphological characteristics:**
- Growth form: Erect to spreading shrub, 2–5 m tall in habitat
- Branches: Reddish-brown, woody at maturity, smooth bark
- Leaves: Opposite, ovoid to nearly round, 0.5–1.5 cm long, bright green to glossy
- Leaf texture: Fleshy, smooth, with a slightly sticky surface
- Flowers: Small, star-shaped, pink to mauve, borne in terminal panicles
- Bloom time: Late summer to autumn (February–April in [[guzman-allen-gartz-africa-southern-hemisphere-underexplored-neurotropic-fungi]])

The variegated cultivar 'Variegata' (also sold as 'Aurea') has cream-edged leaves and a more compact habit. Several prostrate and miniature forms exist in cultivation.

**Cultivar 'Limpopo':** A large-leaved form collected near the Limpopo River with leaves up to 2.5 cm long. This form grows more vigorously and develops thicker, more sculptural trunks than the standard type.

The species superficially resembles *[[crassula-ovata]]* (jade plant) but differs in having opposite (rather than alternate) leaf arrangement, reddish-brown (rather than grey) mature bark, and much smaller flowers. The leaves are also thinner and more pliable than the stiff, leathery leaves of Crassula ovata.

## Distribution and Habitat

Portulacaria afra is endemic to southern Africa, with a distribution centered on the Eastern Cape and KwaZulu-Natal provinces of South Africa. It also occurs in parts of Eswatini and southern Mozambique.

The species is a keystone component of **Albany thickets**, a vegetation type characterized by dense, impenetrable succulent shrubland. It grows on rocky hillsides, dry river valleys, and arid slopes from sea level to approximately 1,000 m elevation.

Albany thickets have experienced severe degradation from overgrazing by domestic goats, which has converted large areas from dense Portulacaria-dominated shrubland to open, eroded landscapes. This degradation has released significant stored carbon into the atmosphere.

Rainfall in the native range is 250–600 mm annually, falling predominantly in summer. The species tolerates extended drought by shedding leaves and relying on water stored in its succulent stems. Fog and coastal mist provide supplementary moisture in many populations along the Indian Ocean coast.

Soils in the native habitat are typically shallow, rocky, and alkaline, derived from shale or sandstone parent material. Portulacaria afra shows a strong preference for south-facing slopes in the southern hemisphere, where it receives maximum sun exposure.

## Cultivation

Portulacaria afra is one of the easiest succulents to grow, making it popular with beginners and bonsai enthusiasts alike.

Unlike many succulents, Portulacaria afra performs well in high-humidity tropical environments if given adequate drainage. It is also more shade-tolerant than most Crassulaceae, thriving in bright indirect light where jade plants would etiolate.

**Growing requirements:**
- Light: Full sun to bright indirect light; tolerates partial shade
- Water: Drought-tolerant; water thoroughly then allow soil to dry completely
- Soil: Well-draining succulent mix; pH 6.0–7.0
- Temperature: Hardy to approximately −2 °C (USDA Zone 9b); protect from frost
- Propagation: Extremely easy from stem cuttings; roots within 2–3 weeks

As a bonsai subject, Portulacaria afra develops thick, gnarled trunks with careful pruning. It responds well to root restriction and container cultivation. The species grows vigorously in warm conditions and may require regular pruning to maintain shape.

[[query-how-to-grow-and-use-oca-in-temperate-climates]], Portulacaria afra is grown exclusively as a houseplant or greenhouse specimen. It benefits from summer placement outdoors after all danger of frost has passed, which strengthens growth and improves leaf coloration. Plants moved outdoors must be gradually acclimated to direct sun to avoid sunburn.

Repotting is best done in spring, using a container only slightly larger than the root ball. The species has a relatively shallow root system compared to other succulent shrubs. Overpotting in large containers leads to excess soil moisture and potential root rot.

Indoor specimens should receive at least 4–6 hours of bright light daily. Insufficient light causes etiolation with elongated internodes and pale foliage. Mealybugs and [[cervantes-spider-mites-identification-control]] are the most common pests, especially on stressed indoor plants.
