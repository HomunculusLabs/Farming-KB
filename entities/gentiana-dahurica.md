## Gentiana dahurica

## Overview

Gentiana dahurica, the Dahurian gentian, is a perennial herbaceous plant in the family Gentianaceae. It is native to northern China, Mongolia, and southern Siberia, where it inhabits alpine meadows and grassy slopes.

The species is one of several gentians used in traditional Chinese medicine under the name "Qin Jiao." Its roots contain iridoid glycosides with documented anti-inflammatory properties.

Gentiana dahurica produces attractive blue-violet flowers on upright stems from July to September. It is occasionally cultivated as an ornamental in alpine and rock gardens.

The species was first described by the German-Russian botanist Friedrich Ernst von Fischer in 1812. The specific epithet refers to the Dauria region of southeastern Siberia, the center of its distribution.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:** Kingdom Plantae → Order Gentianales → Family Gentianaceae → Genus Gentiana → Section Cruciata → Species G. dahurica Fisch.

Stems are erect, simple or sparingly branched, reaching 20–60 cm in height. The stem is glabrous, striate, and typically produces 1–3 flowering branches.

Basal leaves are lanceolate to linear-lanceolate, 5–12 cm long, with three prominent parallel veins. They form a tufted rosette at the base.

Cauline leaves are opposite, sessile, and gradually reduced in size upward. Upper leaves are shorter and broader relative to the basal leaves.

The root system consists of a stout, fleshy taproot with lateral branches. Roots are yellowish-brown externally and pale yellow internally, reaching 20–30 cm in depth.

Flowers are terminal or axillary, clustered in whorls at the upper nodes. The corolla is tubular-campanulate, 2–3 cm long, with four to five blue-violet lobes.

The calyx tube is shorter than the corolla tube, with linear-lanceolate lobes. Corolla lobes are ovate-oblong with fringed margins between the lobes.

Flowering occurs from July to September. The fruit is an ellipsoid capsule containing numerous small, winged seeds.

Chromosome number is 2n = 26. The species belongs to Gentiana section Cruciata, characterized by whorled flowers and linear leaves.

## Distribution and Habitat

Gentiana dahurica is native to northern China (Heilongjiang, Jilin, Liaoning, Inner Mongolia, Hebei, Shanxi), Mongolia, and the Russian Far East (Dauria region, Transbaikalia).

It occurs at elevations of 400–2,400 m, inhabiting mountain meadows, grassy slopes, forest edges, and steppe margins. The species prefers well-drained, calcareous to neutral soils.

Populations are most abundant in the Dahurian steppe-forest transition zone. Here the species grows in open grasslands interspersed with birch and larch woodlands.

The species is relatively cold-hardy, tolerating winter temperatures below −30°C. Deep snow cover provides natural insulation for the root crown during severe winters.
