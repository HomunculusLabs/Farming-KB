## Cyprinus carpio (Common Carp)

## Description
*Cyprinus carpio*, commonly known as the common carp, is a widespread freshwater fish of the family Cyprinidae. It is one of the most important aquaculture species globally and a staple of Asian polyculture pond systems. In [[permaculture]], carp are valued for their omnivorous diet, hardiness, and ability to thrive in a wide range of water conditions including sewage-fed canals.

## Classification
- **Kingdom**: Animalia
- **Phylum**: Chordata
- **Class**: Actinopterygii
- **Order**: Cypriniformes
- **Family**: Cyprinidae
- **Genus**: *Cyprinus*
- **Species**: *C. carpio*

## Key Facts

### Environmental Tolerance
- **Temperature**: Broad tolerance; optimal growth at 20-28degC
- **Oxygen**: Tolerant of moderately low dissolved oxygen; adapts to eutrophic conditions
- **pH**: 6.0-8.5 preferred
- **Salinity**: Primarily freshwater but tolerates low salinity

### Role in Polyculture
- Stocked at 10-15% of fingerling density in diverse polyculture mixes
- Effective combinations include:
  - *Sarotherodon mossambicus* (50%), *Helostoma temmincki* (20%), *Osteochilus hasselti* (15%), *Puntius gonionotus* (15%), *Cyprinus carpio* (10%)
  - *Helostoma temmincki* (35%), *Osteochilus hasselti* (15%), *Cyprinus carpio* (15%), *Puntius gonionotus* (35%)
- Omnivorous -- consumes detritus, insects, plant matter, and benthic organisms

### Production Efficiency
- Feed conversion ratio of approximately 1.5-2.3 kg feed to 1 kg fish flesh
- Far more efficient than terrestrial livestock (beef, mutton, pork require 2-3x the feed)
- Can be cultured in sewage/stream systems at extremely low energy cost (<0.004 MJ/g)
- Sewage canal culture of carp in cages achieves production costs as low as 8 kcal/oz

### Culture Systems
- Grown in rice paddies, terraced ponds, canals, and cage systems
- Benefits from liming, manuring, and aeration
- Compatible with duck, pig, and chicken manure fertilisation systems

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology
- Carp pond sediments are rich in [[savory-soil-organic-matter-and-decomposition-dynamics]] can be harvested as [[compost]] or mushroom substrate
- The integrated polyculture model parallels multi-trophic cultivation systems in [[mycology]]
- Nutrient cycling from animal manure through carp to land crops demonstrates closed-loop design used in mushroom-plant integrated systems
