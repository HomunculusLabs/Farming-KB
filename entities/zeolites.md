# Zeolites (Compost Tea Amendment)

## Description

Zeolites are naturally occurring or synthetically produced microporous aluminosilicate minerals with a crystalline structure that creates a vast internal surface area of pores and channels. In compost tea brewing and soil food web management, zeolites serve as fungal food and habitat — their porous structure provides surfaces for fungal hyphae to attach to and grow along, while their mineral composition supplies nutrients that favor fungal over bacterial growth.

## Classification

- **Type:** Microporous aluminosilicate mineral
- **Crystal structure:** Framework of tetrahedra (SiO₄ and AlO₄) creating uniform pores and channels
- **Common species:** Clinoptilolite, mordenite, chabazite
- **Origin:** Natural (volcanic) and synthetic
- **Role in compost tea:** Fungal food and attachment substrate

## Key Facts

- Microporous mineral structure with high surface area provides ideal attachment surfaces for fungal hyphae
- Classified as good fungal food that does not support populations of bacteria — selectively promotes fungal growth
- Used alongside kelp (*Ascophyllum nodosum*), humic acids, fulvic acids, and rock dust in fungal-dominant compost tea recipes
- Cation exchange capacity (CEC) allows zeolites to hold and slowly release mineral nutrients
- Porous structure mimics the physical habitat that fungi naturally colonize in soils (pore spaces between soil particles)
- Help shift the microbial balance of compost tea toward fungal dominance, which benefits perennials, trees, shrubs, and other woody plants
- Also used in agriculture for:
  - Soil amendment to improve nutrient retention
  - Water retention in sandy soils
  - Heavy metal remediation
  - Odor control in composting operations

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Zeolites are relevant to mycological practice in several ways:

- **Fungal habitat provision:** The porous crystalline structure mimics soil pore spaces where fungi naturally grow — providing this in compost tea gives hyphae a physical substrate to colonize
- **Selective fungal promotion:** Unlike simple sugars (which feed bacteria), zeolites provide mineral nutrition and physical structure that selectively benefits fungi
- **Mineral nutrition:** Zeolites contain silica, aluminum, and various exchangeable cations that support fungal metabolic processes
- **Compost tea formulation:** Combined with kelp, humic acids, and fish hydrolysate, zeolites are part of the toolkit for creating fungally dominated compost teas
- **Soil structure analog:** Understanding how zeolites provide fungal habitat helps explain why soil structure (aggregate formation, pore space) is critical for fungal communities
