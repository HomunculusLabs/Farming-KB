---# Overview

*Xanthosoma sagittifolium* (malanga, tannia, yautia) is a tropical herbaceous perennial in the family Araceae, cultivated primarily for its starchy underground cormels and edible young leaves.

The species is a major staple [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-south-america]] and [[colocasia-esculenta]]*).

## Taxonomy

Monocots → Commelinids → Alismatales → Araceae → Aroideae → Caladieae → ***Xanthosoma* → *X. sagittifolium***

- **Authority:** (L.) Schott (1832)
- **Basionym:** *Arum sagittifolium* L. (1753)
- **Synonyms:** *Xanthosoma violaceum* Schott, *Xanthosoma atrovirens* Kunth, *Xanthosoma Caribe* (horticultural)
- **Note:** Often confused with *Colocasia esculenta* (taro); the two are in different genera despite similar common names and uses.
- **Common names:** malanga, tannia, yautia, new cocoyam, macabo, taioba (Brazil), mangarito (South America), otoe (Panama), cocoyam (West Africa)

## Morphology

*X. sagittifolium* is a robust herbaceous perennial, 1–2.5 m tall, with large arrow-shaped leaves arising from a central corm.

- **Leaves:** Sagittate (arrow-shaped), 30–100 cm long and 20–60 cm wide; glossy dark green, with peltate attachment; petioles 60–120 cm long, often with a distinctive purplish tinge at the base; margins entire to slightly wavy.
- **Corm:** Central underground storage organ, 10–25 cm diameter, rough and brown-skinned; flesh white to cream or pale yellow; firm and dense.
- **Cormels:** Secondary cormels (5–20 per plant) develop around the central corm; these are the primary edible organs; each cormel 5–20 cm long, 3–8 cm diameter, ovoid to cylindrical.
- **Inflorescence:** Spathe 15–25 cm long, green to purplish on the outside, cream to yellowish inside; spadix shorter than spathe, male and female zones separated by a sterile band.
- **Roots:** Fibrous adventitious roots arising from the corm; some develop into thickened contractile roots that pull the plant deeper into the soil.

## Distribution and Habitat

*X. sagittifolium* is native to tropical lowland regions of Central and northern South America, with the center of domestication in the northern Amazon basin and Caribbean.

The species has been widely dispersed through human migration and the slave trade, becoming naturalized and cultivated across the tropics.

- **[[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]]; tolerates acidic soils (pH 4.5–6.5) and moderate waterlogging better than most [[cannabis-soil-and-nutrient-requirements]]:** Responds well to organic manure and moderate NPK application; potassium is particularly important for cormel development.
- **[[fukuoka-natural-weed-management]]:** Rapid canopy closure (2–3 months) suppresses weeds effectively; hand-weeding needed during early establishment.
- **Pests:** The most damaging pest is the taro beetle (*Papuana uninodis*); nematodes (*Meloidogyne* spp.) and the golden apple snail (*Pomacea canaliculata*) can cause significant losses.
