## Hebeloma cylindrosporum

## Description

*Hebeloma cylindrosporum* is an ectomycorrhizal basidiomycete fungus in the family Hymenogastraceae. It is one of the most extensively studied model organisms for ectomycorrhizal symbiosis research, particularly in European forest ecosystems. The species forms characteristic [[ectomycorrhizal-associations]] with a range of coniferous and deciduous tree species, especially pines (*Pinus* spp.) and birches (*Betula* spp.).

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Phylum**: Basidiomycota
- **Class**: Agaricomycetes
- **Order**: Agaricales
- **Family**: Hymenogastraceae
- **Genus**: *Hebeloma*
- **Species**: *H. cylindrosporum*

## Key Facts

- Produces acid phosphatase and phytase enzymes enabling access to organic phosphorus sources in forest soils.
- Capable of solubilizing complex inorganic phosphate forms including calcium phosphate, particularly in the presence of ammonium nitrogen.
- Enzyme production is regulated by external orthophosphate concentration via product suppression mechanisms.
- Dissolution of complex phosphate forms is enhanced in the absence of available orthophosphate.
- Functions as both an efficient inorganic nutrient scavenger and an organic nutrient mobilizer in forest floor environments.
- Frequently studied in controlled experiments on phosphate uptake kinetics and enzyme regulation in ectomycorrhizal systems.
- One of several *Hebeloma* species exhibiting pre-adaptation to utilize nitrogen from proteins and glutamic acid released during soil freeze-thaw cycles.
- Shows moderate phosphatase activity compared to species like *Entoloma sericeum* and *Cenococcum geophilum*.

## Enzyme Activity Profile

Based on research by Antibus et al. (1992), *H. cylindrosporum* shows moderate mycelial surface phosphatase activity and low phytase enzyme production. Its ability to incorporate phosphorus from organic sources is notable but less pronounced than that of *Cenococcum geophilum* or *Entoloma sericeum*. The species demonstrates measurable solubilization of calcium phytate, CaHPO4, and Ca3(PO4)2.

## Ecological Role

In forest ecosystems, *H. cylindrosporum* contributes to nutrient cycling by accessing phosphorus from both organic and inorganic pools in the soil. It is classified as a moderately enzyme-competent ectomycorrhizal species, occupying an intermediate position between highly enzyme-active late-successional species and less enzyme-capable early-successional species. This positions it within the mycorrhizal succession framework where it appears in intermediate stages of forest development.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Model organism**: Widely used in laboratory studies of ectomycorrhizal physiology, enzyme production, and nutrient uptake.
- **Forestry applications**: Potential inoculant for reforestation projects, particularly on nutrient-poor or disturbed sites.
- **Phosphorus cycling**: Demonstrates the importance of mycorrhizal enzyme competence in maintaining forest soil fertility.
- **Cold adaptation**: Studies on its nitrogen utilization at low temperatures (6C) provide insights into mycorrhizal function in boreal and arctic ecosystems.
- **Research significance**: Understanding its enzyme regulation mechanisms informs broader knowledge of fungal nutrient acquisition strategies.

## Habitat and Distribution

*H. cylindrosporum* is primarily found in temperate and boreal forests of Europe, where it associates with both coniferous and deciduous hosts. It prefers acidic to neutral forest soils with moderate organic matter content. The species is particularly abundant in pine forests on sandy soils, where phosphorus availability is often limiting and the enzyme capabilities of ectomycorrhizal partners become critical for tree nutrition.

## Research Methodology

The species is commonly maintained in pure culture on modified Melin-Norkrans (MMN) agar medium, making it accessible for controlled experiments. Its relatively fast growth rate in culture and reliable formation of mycorrhizal structures with host seedlings in greenhouse conditions have made it a standard organism in ectomycorrhizal research laboratories worldwide.

## Phosphorus Solubilization Data

From Lapeyrie et al. (1991), solubilization of complex inorganic phosphate forms (percentage of control):
- Ca phytate: 120 (minus P) / 120 (plus P)
- CaHPO4: 120 / 120
- Ca3(PO4)2: 80 / not determined
- Ca5(PO4)3OH: 80 / 50

The data show consistent solubilization across phosphate forms with moderate suppression by orthophosphate.
