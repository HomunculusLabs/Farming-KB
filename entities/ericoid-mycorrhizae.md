## Ericoid Mycorrhizae

## Description

Ericoid mycorrhizae are a specialized type of mycorrhizal association formed exclusively with plants in the order Ericales (family Ericaceae and related families), which includes blueberries, cranberries, rhododendrons, azaleas, heathers, and heaths. These fungi penetrate the cortical cells of the fine hair roots characteristic of Ericaceae, forming dense coils of hyphae within the cells. Ericoid mycorrhizae are critical for plant survival in the acidic, nutrient-poor soils where Ericaceae typically grow, enabling access to organic nitrogen and other nutrients that would otherwise be unavailable.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi (primarily Ascomycota)
- **Type**: Endomycorrhiza (ericoid type)
- **Distinguished from**: VAM (arbuscular structures, broader host range), Ectomycorrhizae (mantle and Hartig net)
- **Host plants**: Ericaceae family: blueberries (Vaccinium), cranberries, rhododendrons, azaleas, heather (Calluna), heaths (Erica)
- **Key structures**: Intracellular hyphal coils in root cortical cells, fine hair roots

## Key Facts

- Ericoid mycorrhizae are one of three major [[mycorrhizal-types]] distinguished by SFI in root analysis, alongside VAM and ectomycorrhizae.
- They are uniquely adapted to acidic, low-nutrient soils (pH 3.5-5.5) where most other mycorrhizal types function poorly.
- Ericoid fungi possess enzymes (particularly nitrogen acquisition enzymes) that allow them to decompose organic matter directly, accessing nitrogen from organic compounds that plants cannot use alone.
- Dr. [[elaine-ingham]]'s SFI technicians are trained to differentiate ericoid mycorrhizal colonization from VAM and ectomycorrhizal colonization in root samples.
- The assessment of ericoid colonization follows similar threshold principles to VAM: low colonization correlates with poor plant performance, while adequate colonization supports health and disease resistance.
- Ericaceae plants without adequate ericoid mycorrhizal partners show nutrient deficiency symptoms (particularly nitrogen and iron chlorosis) even when nutrients are present in the soil.
- The hair roots of Ericaceae are extremely fine (sometimes barely visible) and require careful microscopic examination to assess ericoid colonization status.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Blueberry cultivation**: Commercial blueberry production benefits significantly from ericoid [[mycorrhizal-inoculation-guide]], especially in soils that have been fumigated or heavily disturbed.
- **Cranberry farming**: Cranberry bogs rely on ericoid mycorrhizae for nitrogen acquisition in the acidic peat soils where these crops are grown.
- **Ornamental horticulture**: Rhododendrons, azaleas, and heathers in landscapes and nurseries establish more successfully when ericoid mycorrhizal inoculum is present.
- **Soil acidification management**: When growing Ericaceae, maintaining appropriate [[soil-ph]] (4.5-5.5) supports ericoid mycorrhizal function. Excessive liming can harm the fungal symbionts.
- **Compost tea application**: Compost teas containing ericoid-compatible fungal species can be applied to establish or enhance mycorrhizal colonization in Ericaceae plantings.
- **Reforestation and restoration**: Restoring heathland or acidic peatland communities requires attention to ericoid mycorrhizal presence. Without the fungal partners, Ericaceae transplants may fail.
