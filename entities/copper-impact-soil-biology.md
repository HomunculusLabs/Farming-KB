## Copper Impact on Soil Biology

## Description

Copper-based fungicides have significant negative impacts on soil biological communities, a concern highlighted in Elaine Ingham's field guide on compost tea. While copper compounds are effective against fungal pathogens, they are broadly toxic to beneficial soil organisms and can accumulate in soils to levels that permanently damage [[soil-food-web]].

## Classification

- **Category**: Agricultural chemical - fungicide
- **Common forms**: Copper sulfate (Bordeaux mixture), copper hydroxide, copper oxide, copper octanoate
- **Impact**: Biologically detrimental - toxic to beneficial microorganisms
- **Persistence**: Copper accumulates in soil; does not break down

## Key Facts

- **Non-selective toxicity**: Copper kills beneficial fungi, bacteria, and other organisms alongside target pathogens
- **Soil accumulation**: Copper is an element and does not degrade; repeated applications build up soil copper levels over years
- **Mycorrhizal damage**: Copper is particularly toxic to mycorrhizal fungi, disrupting essential plant-fungal symbioses
- **Earthworm toxicity**: Copper is toxic to earthworms and other macrofauna at relatively low concentrations
- **Microbial biomass reduction**: Copper-treated soils show reduced total microbial biomass and activity
- **Enzyme inhibition**: Copper interferes with soil enzyme activity, impairing nutrient cycling processes

## Impact on Soil Organisms

### Fungi
- **Mycorrhizal fungi**: Highly sensitive; colonization rates drop significantly after copper application
- **Saprophytic fungi**: Beneficial decomposer fungi are killed along with pathogenic species
- **Trichoderma spp.**: These beneficial biocontrol fungi are sensitive to copper

### Bacteria
- **Diversity reduction**: Copper reduces bacterial species diversity in soil
- **Nitrifying bacteria**: Copper inhibits nitrification, affecting nitrogen cycling
- **Decomposer bacteria**: Reduced populations slow organic matter decomposition

### Other Organisms
- **Protozoa**: Populations decline, reducing nutrient cycling through the microbial loop
- **Nematodes**: Beneficial nematode species are affected
- **Earthworms**: Avoid copper-contaminated soil; populations decline

## Copper and Sulfur Combinations

Ingham notes the combined use of copper and sulfur in vineyards as particularly problematic:

- **Copper + sulfur**: The traditional Bordeaux mixture combines copper sulfate with lime
- **Copper + sulfur + Kaligreen**: Triple-combination applications create compounding toxicity
- **Vineyard soils**: Historic vineyard soils often have dangerously high copper levels from decades of Bordeaux mixture application

## Soil Copper Levels

| Copper Level (mg/kg) | Impact |
|-----------------------|--------|
| < 20 | Normal background; minimal biological impact |
| 20-50 | Reduced microbial diversity; mycorrhizal damage begins |
| 50-100 | Significant biological impairment; earthworm avoidance |
| 100-200 | Severe biological damage; difficult to restore |
| > 200 | Toxic to most soil organisms; may require remediation |

## Relevance to Compost Tea and Biological Recovery

### Recovery Challenges
- **Persistent damage**: Unlike organic pesticides that break down, copper damage persists indefinitely
- **Slow recovery**: Soil biology may take years to recover even after copper use stops
- **Compost tea role**: AACT applications can help restore some biological activity, but cannot overcome severe copper toxicity
- **Organic matter**: Adding compost helps bind copper and reduce its bioavailability while supporting biology recovery

### Management Strategy
1. **Stop copper applications**: Transition to biological disease management
2. **Test soil copper**: Determine current copper levels before planning recovery
3. **Apply compost**: High-quality compost adds organic matter that chelates copper
4. **AACT applications**: Regular compost tea applications to reintroduce beneficial organisms
5. **Mycorrhizal inoculation**: Reintroduce mycorrhizal fungi that copper has eliminated
6. **pH management**: Maintaining neutral to slightly alkaline pH reduces copper availability
7. **Monitor**: Regular soil biology testing to track recovery

## Alternatives to Copper

| Alternative | Efficacy | Biological Impact |
|-------------|----------|-------------------|
| Compost tea (AACT) | Good disease suppression | Beneficial |
| Potassium bicarbonate (Kaligreen) | Moderate | Low impact |
| Biological controls | Variable | Beneficial |
| Sulfur (alone) | Moderate | Low-moderate |
| Plant resistance varieties | Good | None |

## Regulatory Context

- Many organic certification programs limit copper application rates
- European Union has strict limits on copper use in organic farming (typically 4-6 kg/ha/year)
- Some regions require soil copper testing before additional applications are permitted
- Environmental agencies may restrict copper use near waterways
