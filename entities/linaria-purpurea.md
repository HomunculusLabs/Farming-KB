## Overview

*Linaria purpurea* (Linnaeus) Miller is a slender, upright perennial in the family Plantaginaceae, native to the Mediterranean region. It is valued in gardens for its long, graceful spikes of small, purple, snapdragon-like flowers that bloom over many weeks in summer.

The species grows 60–120 cm tall with narrow, grey-green foliage and wiry stems that sway in the breeze. The delicate, airy habit makes it an excellent filler plant in perennial borders and cottage gardens.

Individual flowers are 1–1.5 cm long, two-lipped, and spurred, resembling miniature snapdragons. The color ranges from soft lavender to deep violet-purple, with a pale yellow or white palate on the lower lip.

*Linaria purpurea* is drought-tolerant, deer-resistant, and long-blooming, making it a low-maintenance choice for Mediterranean-climate gardens. It is hardy in USDA zones 5–9.

The species has naturalized widely in the British Isles, where it has been cultivated since the 16th century. It is commonly found on old stone walls, ruined buildings, and railway embankments throughout southern and central England.

The species self-seeds freely in favorable conditions, sometimes aggressively. Gardeners should be prepared to thin seedlings or remove spent flower stems before seed set if spread is not desired.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

*Linaria purpurea* belongs to the genus *Linaria* within tribe Antirrhineae of the family Plantaginaceae. The genus comprises approximately 150 species of annual and perennial herbs native to Europe, Asia, and North Africa.

The species was first described by Linnaeus in 1753 and later transferred to its current combination by Philip Miller in 1768. The genus name *Linaria* derives from *linum* (flax), referring to the flax-like leaves of some species.

Stems are erect, slender, and slightly winged, branching primarily near the inflorescence. The plant is glabrous or sparsely glandular-pubescent, with a grey-green, glaucous appearance.

Leaves are linear to linear-lanceolate, 2–5 cm long and 1–3 mm wide, arranged alternately or in whorls of three on the lower stem. They are sessile, entire, and slightly fleshy, an adaptation to dry conditions.

The inflorescence is a terminal, elongated raceme bearing 15–40 flowers. Each flower has a slender spur 8–12 mm long projecting backward from the corolla tube. The spur contains nectar accessible only to long-tongued pollinators.

The fruit is a globose capsule 4–6 mm in diameter that dehisces by two pores near the apex. Seeds are disc-shaped, 1–1.5 mm wide, with a broad, membranous wing that aids wind dispersal.

The chromosome number is 2n = 12, a base number shared with many Mediterranean *Linaria* species.
