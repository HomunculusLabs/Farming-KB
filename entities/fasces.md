# Fasces

## Description

The Fasces (Latin: fascis, "bundle"; plural: fasces) is an ancient Roman symbol
consisting of a bundle of wooden rods tied with straps and an axe blade projecting
from one side. It represents authority, centralized power, and the principle that a
group bound together is stronger than individuals. The Fasces gave its name to
"Fascism" and is displayed in government buildings worldwide.

## Classification

- **Category:** Political symbol / governmental emblem
- **Cultural origin:** Ancient Rome (Etruscan antecedents)
- **Modern usage:** U.S. House of Representatives and other government buildings
- **Political significance:** Namesake of Fascism
- **Symbolic meaning:** Centralized authority, strength through unity, judicial power

## Physical Description

- Bundle of straight wooden rods (typically birch or elm)
- Leather or rope straps binding rods at intervals
- Single axe head (securis) affixed near the top
- Sometimes with axe blade emerging, sometimes with handle visible

## Key Facts

- Displayed on both sides of the Rostrum in the U.S. House of Representatives.
- "Fascism" derives directly from fasces.
- Commonly associated only with Mussolini and Hitler, but the pattern of governance
  (control through oppression of sexuality and consciousness-repression) is argued to
  be far older.
- Carried by Roman lictors as symbol of magistrate's power to punish and execute.
- The bundle metaphor: individual rods break easily, the bundle does not.
