## Laetiporus persicinus

## Description

Laetiporus persicinus is a species of [[chicken-of-the-woods]] mushroom recognized by its peach-colored to pinkish cap surface, which distinguishes it from the more common orange and yellow Laetiporus species. It is one of several species in the genus, classified alongside L. sulphureus, L. conifericola, and L. gilbertsonii based on differences in cap color, pore color, and host tree preference. Like its relatives, it forms large bracket-shaped or rosette-like fruiting bodies on wood.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Polyporales
- **Family:** Fomitopsidaceae
- **Genus:** Laetiporus
- **Species:** L. persicinus

## Key Facts

- Distinguished from other Laetiporus species by peach to pinkish cap coloration
- Produces large, fleshy bracket or rosette fruiting bodies
- [[mycelium]] is orange and powdery with a sulfur-like odor
- [[spore-print]] is white
- Part of the chicken of the woods complex
- Difficulty level: Outdoor cultivation 3/5, Indoor cultivation 5/5
- Shares general genus characteristics for form and ecology
- Best harvested young when silky and tender

## Cultivation Notes

### Substrate Preferences

As with all Laetiporus species, strains are best isolated from wild fruitings and matched to their preferred wood type. Each strain is adapted to specific wood chemistry and density. Confirm original wood type when sourcing spawn.

### Outdoor Cultivation

- Best suited for outdoor stump or round cultivation
- Use fresh wood (cut no more than one month before inoculation)
- Layer supplemented sawdust spawn between rounds
- Ground contact enhances moisture availability
- Inoculation to fruiting time: one to two years
- Expected yields: Low — typical of the genus

### Indoor Cultivation

Indoor cultivation is not recommended for any Laetiporus species. The fungus may require microbial symbionts to complete its reproductive cycle. No reliable indoor fruiting methods have been developed.

### Laboratory Culture

- Clone from slightly older, drier specimens for best results
- Crack open flesh to find dry, powdery tissue with bright color
- Orange powdery mycelium with distinctive sulfur smell
- Antibiotic extracts can be produced from colonized grain and sawdust blocks without fruiting

### Spawn Types

- Sawdust spawn
- Plug spawn
- Cardboard spawn

## Harvesting

- Cut young, silky outer tips with a sharp knife
- Flesh becomes tough and fibrous near the attachment point
- Can also bend tips to snap at the natural break point between edible flesh and [[compost]]

## Storage

- Fresh: two to three weeks at 38–42°F (3–6°C)
- Cooks and freezes well
- Does not reconstitute well when dried

## Nutritional and Medicinal Properties

- Protein content approximately 12–14 percent (dry weight), consistent with the genus
- May help regulate blood pressure and sugar levels
- Contains high levels of anti-inflammatory compounds
- Shows strong activity against Staphylococcus bacteria
- Promising candidate for systemic staph infection research

## Mycoremediation Potential

- Known for enzymatic degradation of chemical dyes
- Candidate for mycofiltration units in textile operations
- Useful for solid-state decolorization processes
- Antibiotic properties suggest potential for water filtration applications

## Identification

- Peach to pinkish upper cap surface (distinguishing feature)
- White, orange, or yellow pore surface beneath
- Large bracket or rosette growth form on wood
- White [[spore]] print
- Orange, powdery mycelium with sulfur odor

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

L. persicinus is a less commonly encountered member of the chicken of the woods group, notable for its distinctive peach-pink coloration. While sharing the cultivation challenges of the genus — difficulty fruiting indoors and low outdoor yields — it shares the same valuable medicinal and mycoremediation properties. Its unique coloration makes it an interesting subject for taxonomic study and a visually distinctive addition to outdoor mushroom gardens.
