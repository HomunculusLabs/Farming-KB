# Eupatorium perfoliatum

## Overview

**Eupatorium perfoliatum**, known as boneset or common boneset, is a perennial
herbaceous plant in the aster family (Asteraceae). Native to eastern and
central North America, it was one of the most widely used medicinal plants
in 19th-century American herbalism, particularly for treating fevers, colds,
and influenza-like illnesses.

The common name "boneset" does not refer to bone healing but to "break-bone
fever," an older term for dengue-like fevers characterized by severe bone and
muscle pain. The plant was the primary herbal remedy for such conditions in
pre-modern American medicine.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:**

- Kingdom: Plantae
- Division: Magnoliophyta
- Class: Magnoliopsida
- Order: Asterales
- Family: Asteraceae
- Tribe: Eupatorieae
- Genus: Eupatorium
- Species: E. perfoliatum L. (1753)

**Stem:** Erect, 0.5–1.5 m tall, cylindrical, covered in fine white hairs.
The distinctive feature is the leaves appearing to be perforated by the stem —
the opposite leaves are joined at their bases (perfoliate), clasping the stem.

**Leaves:** Opposite, lanceolate to ovate, 8–20 cm long and 3–8 cm wide,
with serrate margins. Surface is rough-textured above, finely hairy beneath.
Vein pattern is prominently triple-nerved from the base.

**Flowers:** Small, white to pale pink, arranged in dense, flat-topped
corymbose clusters 10–20 cm across. Each flower head contains 10–20 disc
florets; ray florets are absent. Blooms July–September.

**Root:** Fibrous, with a short rhizomatous crown. Spreads slowly by
rhizomes to form loose clumps.

**Fruit:** Small achenes (2–3 mm), black, with a white pappus for wind
dispersal.

## Distribution and Habitat

Eupatorium perfoliatum is native to eastern and central North America,
ranging from Quebec to Manitoba, south to Florida and Texas. It is hardy
in USDA zones 3–8.

It grows naturally in:
- Low meadows and damp pastures
- Stream banks and wetland margins
- Bottomland hardwood forests
- roadside ditches and disturbed wet areas

The plant prefers full sun to partial shade and moist to wet soils rich in
organic matter. It tolerates seasonal flooding and clay soils, making it
adaptable to riparian [[pollinator-habitat]] and [[companion-planting]]** — its bitter compounds may deter certain pest
  insects when interplanted with vegetables
