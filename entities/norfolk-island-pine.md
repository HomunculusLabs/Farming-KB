## Description

The Norfolk Island Pine (Araucaria heterophylla) is a large evergreen conifer native to Norfolk Island in the Pacific Ocean. It is widely planted in subtropical and tropical coastal regions worldwide as a windbreak tree, valued for its exceptional salt tolerance, symmetrical form, and ability to grow tall in harsh coastal conditions.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Family:** Araucariaceae
- **Species:** Araucaria heterophylla (Salisb.) Franco
- **Common names:** Norfolk Island pine, Norfolk pine, star pine
- **Origin:** Norfolk Island (Pacific Ocean, between Australia and New Zealand)
- **Growth form:** Tall, columnar to broadly conical evergreen tree; can reach 50-65 m in native habitat

## Key Facts

### Windbreak Role

Mollison identifies the Norfolk Island pine as one of the hardy pioneer species that can be nursed to grow to windward on salt-laden coasts. Key characteristics:

- **Salt tolerance:** Withstands direct salt spray that kills most other tree species.
- **Height achievement:** Can climb above the salt-burn zone to establish an upper canopy windbreak.
- **Columnar form:** Dense, symmetrical branching provides effective wind reduction.
- **Slow establishment:** Growth from ground level in direct coastal exposure is slow, but when given a head start behind a fence, building, earth bank, or barrier, height is achieved with far less lateral spread.

### Coastal Windbreak Strategy

On dry salt-wind coasts, tree canopies typically gain only about 46 cm (18 inches) of height per 1 m (3 feet) of width. Mollison notes that providing a starting barrier gives the Norfolk Island pine a great start for far less spread, making it one of the few species that can eventually form a tall windbreak canopy in extreme coastal conditions.

### General Characteristics

- **Needle-like branchlets:** Whorled, symmetrical arrangement. Reduced leaf surface area minimizes water loss.
- **Windfirm root system:** Deep, anchoring roots resist uprooting in cyclone-prone regions.
- **Growth rate:** Moderate; faster in sheltered sites with adequate moisture.
- **Hardiness:** USDA Zones 10-11; frost-tender when young.
- **Saltburn recovery:** Can recover from salt damage to lower branches while continuing upward growth.

### Uses Beyond Windbreak

- **Ornamental:** Iconic silhouette used in seaside landscaping worldwide.
- **Timber:** Lightweight wood used in craft on Norfolk Island.
- **Container plant:** Young specimens widely grown as indoor Christmas trees.
- **Wildlife habitat:** Provides nesting sites for seabirds in coastal environments.

## Relevance to Cultivation

1. **Coastal food production:** As a tall windbreak pioneer, creates the sheltered microclimate necessary for less salt-tolerant food crops behind it.
2. **Cyclone resilience:** Among the most wind-resistant tall trees, maintaining windbreak function after severe storms.
3. **Multi-tier windbreak design:** Eventual height allows vertical stacking of windbreak function, protecting progressively taller productive trees.
