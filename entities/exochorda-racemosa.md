## Overview

**Exochorda racemosa**, commonly called pearl bush or common pearl bush, is a deciduous flowering shrub in the family Rosaceae. It is native to central and eastern China, where it grows in mountain thickets and open woodlands.

The species is renowned for its abundant pure white flowers that appear in pendulous racemes during mid-spring. Each flower has five rounded petals and measures approximately 3–4 cm in diameter.

Pearl bush earned its common name from the pearl-like buds that precede flowering. The round, silvery-white buds create a striking display even before the petals fully expand.

The genus belongs to the subfamily Amygdaloideae and is closely related to *Oemleria* and *Prinsepia*. Molecular studies have confirmed its placement within the tribe Osmaronieae.

Pearl bush is among the earliest of the large white-flowered spring shrubs, typically blooming in late April to early May. Its flowering period overlaps with late-flowering forsythias and early rhododendrons.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

The genus *Exochorda* contains four species, all native to eastern Asia. *Exochorda racemosa* was described by John Lindley in 1837 and remains the most widely cultivated member of the genus.

The shrub forms an upright, spreading mound reaching 2–4 meters in height with a similar spread. Young branches are green and slightly hairy, maturing to smooth gray-brown bark.

Leaves are alternate, ovate to elliptic, 4–8 cm long, with finely serrate margins. The upper leaf surface is dark green and glabrous, while the underside is paler with fine pubescence along the veins.

Flowers are borne in 5–10 flowered terminal racemes on short lateral branches. The pure white petals are broadly obovate and overlap slightly, creating a full, rounded floral display.

Each flower produces a five-chambered capsule that dehisces at maturity. The fruit is a small, dry, papery capsule approximately 8–12 mm in diameter.

Autumn color is generally unremarkable, with foliage turning yellowish-green before dropping. The primary ornamental value is concentrated in the spring flowering display.

## Cultivation

Pearl bush performs best in full sun to partial shade with well-drained, moderately fertile soils. It is adaptable to a range of soil pH levels, from slightly acidic to slightly alkaline.

The shrub is cold-hardy to USDA Zone 4, tolerating temperatures to -34°C. In colder regions, flower buds may be damaged by late spring frosts, which can reduce the seasonal display.

Pruning should be performed immediately after flowering, as the species blooms on the previous year's wood. Removing one-third of the oldest stems annually encourages vigorous new growth from the base.

Overgrown specimens respond well to renovation pruning in early spring, though this sacrifices the current season's flowers. The shrub regrows quickly from established root systems.
