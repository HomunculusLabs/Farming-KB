## Cewey Mushroom Spawn

## Description

Cewey (also spelled "Cewey's") was a commercial brand of artificial mushroom spawn available in 19th-century England, referenced by James Cuthill in his 1870 treatise on mushroom cultivation. Cewey's spawn was one of the commercially available brick spawns that growers could purchase as an alternative to generating their own natural spawn from horse droppings. Cuthill mentioned it as an option for growers who needed to use artificial spawn when their natural spawn had been destroyed by overheating.

## Classification

- **Type**: Commercial product / agricultural input
- **Historical period**: 19th century (1870)
- **Form**: Brick spawn (dried mycelium compressed into bricks)
- **Purpose**: Inoculation of mushroom beds with Agaricus campestris mycelium

## Key Facts

- Commercially available brand of mushroom spawn in 19th-century England
- Used when natural spawn was unavailable or had been destroyed by overheating
- Considered expensive by Cuthill, who advised cottagers to avoid reliance on it
- Represented the commercial spawn industry that was developing alongside traditional spawn-saving practices
- Cuthill noted that using artificial spawn was "to be avoided on account of the expense"
- Alternative to saving spawn from old mushroom beds, which Cuthill considered superior

## Commercial Spawn Industry

The availability of branded spawn products like Cewey's reflected the development of a commercial mushroom spawn industry in 19th-century England. Before commercial spawn, all spawn was either collected from the wild or saved from previous crops. The commercialization of spawn production represented a significant advancement, as it provided reliable, standardized inoculum that could be purchased when natural spawn failed. However, the cost of commercial spawn was a barrier for working-class growers, which is why Cuthill emphasized self-sufficiency in spawn production.

## Brick Spawn Format

Commercial spawn in Cuthill's era was typically sold as "brick spawn" — mycelium grown on a substrate of manure and loam, compressed into brick-shaped blocks and dried for storage and transport. Growers would break the bricks into pieces and insert them into prepared beds at regular intervals. The brick format allowed for standardized dosing and reliable storage, but the quality of brick spawn varied considerably between manufacturers, making brand reputation important.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

The Cewey brand represents the early commercialization of mycelial products, a practice that has evolved into the modern spawn industry where specialized companies produce pure culture spawn on sterilized grain. The transition from cottage-level spawn saving through branded brick spawn to modern laboratory-produced spawn illustrates the progressive professionalization and scientific advancement of mushroom cultivation technology over the past 150 years.
