It is one of the most important traditional respiratory remedies of the
Plains and Inter-Mountain West, used by both Indigenous peoples and
later Eclectic physicians as an expectorant and antispasmodic.

The species is distinguished by its resinous, strongly aromatic flower
heads with prominently recurved involucral bracts that give the plant
its common name.

Grindelia's medicinal reputation rests on its diterpene resin acids and
flavonoids, which have demonstrated bronchodilator and anti-inflammatory
activity in pharmacological studies.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:**

- Kingdom: Plantae
- Division: Magnoliophyta
- Class: Magnoliopsida
- Order: Asterales
- Family: Asteraceae
- Tribe: Astereae
- Genus: *Grindelia*
- Species: *G. squarrosa* (Pursh) Dunal (1817)

**Synonyms:** *Grindelia nuda* var. *squarrosa* (Pursh) H.M. Hall,
*Grindelia subdecurrens* A. Nelson.

**Growth habit:** Erect, branching perennial, 30–100 cm tall.
Stems are glabrous to sparsely hairy, often whitish with a resinous
coating.

**Leaves:** Alternate, sessile or [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]]-petiolate, oblong to lanceolate,
2–8 cm long, 0.5–2 cm wide. Margins are irregularly toothed.
Surfaces are green and resinous-sticky.

**Flower heads:** Solitary or in corymbose clusters, 1.5–3 cm diameter.
Involucral bracts are lanceolate and strongly reflexed at tips, a key
diagnostic feature. Ray florets yellow, 8–15; disk florets yellow.

**Fruit:** Achenes 3–4 mm, with a pappus of 2–3 awns.
Flowering July to September.
