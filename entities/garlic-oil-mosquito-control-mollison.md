## Garlic Oil Mosquito Control

## Description

A biological mosquito larvicide technique using garlic (Allium sativum) bulbs crushed directly into pond water. Bill Mollison describes this as the primary and most effective mosquito control method for permaculture pond systems, claiming approximately 100% kill rate of mosquito larvae while being harmless to tadpoles and other beneficial organisms.

## Classification

- **Category**: Biological Pest Control Technique
- **Active Ingredient**: Garlic oil (allicin and related sulfur compounds)
- **Target**: Mosquito larvae (Culicidae)
- **Permaculture Application**: Pond management, zone one design

## Key Facts

- Garlic bulbs are squeezed directly into pond water; the garlic oils float on the surface.
- Achieves approximately 100% kill rate of mosquito larvae.
- Does not harm tadpoles, making it fully compatible with frog-based mosquito management.
- More efficient than oil-based treatments (which also kill larvae but are less selective).
- Leaves other beneficial organisms unharmed.
- Part of a two-method control strategy: garlic oil for larvae, backswimmers (Notonectidae) for additional predation.
- A formal mosquito control standard design was written by a Ph.D. in mosquito control for the permaculture system but was never formally published.
- Simple, low-cost, and requires no specialized equipment or chemicals.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Outdoor Cultivation**: Enables mosquito-free pond systems near outdoor mushroom beds, log cultures, and composting areas.
- **Water Quality**: Unlike chemical larvicides, garlic oil does not contaminate water used for irrigation of mushroom substrates or garden beds.
- **Ecosystem Health**: Preserves aquatic food webs including tadpoles, which mature into frogs that provide additional pest control in surrounding crops.
- **Integrated Design**: Supports the permaculture principle of [[gaias-garden-stacking-functions]] — ponds serve as frog habitat, thermal mass, irrigation reservoir, and aesthetic features without becoming mosquito breeding grounds.

## Source Reference

Bill Mollison, *Permaculture Design Course Pamphlet IX — [[urban-permaculture-techniques]]*, page 5. Mollison states: "That kills the larva. Just float off your garlic oils. It"s about 100% kill. The garlic doesn't kill tadpoles."
