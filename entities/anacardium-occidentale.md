## Overview

**Anacardium occidentale** L., the cashew, is a tropical evergreen tree in
the family Anacardiaceae, native to northeastern Brazil. It is cultivated
primarily for its seed — the cashew nut — one of the most economically
important tree nuts in global trade, with production exceeding 4 million
tonnes annually. The tree also produces a fleshy peduncle known as the
cashew apple, which is rich in vitamin C and used in beverages, preserves,
and traditional medicine.

The cashew nut shell liquid (CNSL), a byproduct of nut processing, is a
significant industrial raw material containing anacardic acid, cardanol,
and cardol — compounds with applications in resins, friction linings, and
pesticides. The species is increasingly valued in tropical agroforestry
and permaculture systems for its [[dighton-fungal-drought-tolerance-plant-water-relations]], soil stabilization,
and multi-product yield.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Taxonomy:** Plantae → Tracheophyta → Magnoliopsida → Sapindales →
**Anacardiaceae → *Anacardium → *A. occidentale***.

The cashew is a medium-sized, spreading evergreen tree, typically 8–12 m
tall but capable of reaching 15 m under favorable conditions. The trunk
is short and often gnarled, with a low-branching, irregular canopy.

Leaves are simple, alternate, obovate to elliptic, 10–22 cm long and
5–10 cm wide, leathery, with entire margins and prominent venation.
New foliage is copper-colored, turning glossy dark green.

The inflorescence is a terminal panicle bearing small, pinkish-green
flowers. The fruit is distinctive: a kidney-shaped drupe (the true fruit)
borne atop a fleshy, pear-shaped peduncle (the "cashew apple," 5–10 cm
long, yellow to red). The drupe contains the [[cervantes-selfing-single-seed-descent]] (cashew nut)
encased in a thin shell containing the vesicant CNSL.

## Distribution and Habitat

Native to the coastal tablelands of northeastern Brazil (Piauí, Ceará,
Rio Grande do Norte), where wild populations still exist. Portuguese
colonizers introduced cashew to Mozambique and India in the 16th century;
from India, cultivation spread throughout [[psilocybe-southeast-asia-pacific]] and Africa.

Today, major producers include Vietnam, India, Nigeria, Côte d'Ivoire,
and Brazil. The tree thrives in tropical lowlands (0–700 m elevation)
with annual rainfall of 800–1,500 mm and temperatures of 24–32°C. It
is notably drought-tolerant, surviving dry seasons of 4–6 months on
deep, well-drained sandy soils.

## Bioactive Compounds

The tree's chemistry is dominated by phenolic lipids from the Anacardiaceae:

- **Anacardic acids** — [[comparison-jasmonate-vs-salicylic-acid-defense-signaling]] derivatives with C15 alkyl side
  chains (primarily C15:0, C15:1, C15:2, C15:3); strong antibacterial,
  anti-inflammatory, and tyrosinase-inhibiting activity. Concentrated in
  the nut shell liquid (25–35%).
- **Cardanol** — the decarboxylated product of anacardic acid, used
  industrially as a phenolic resin precursor.
- **Cardol** — a dihydroxyphenol with strong vesicant properties.
