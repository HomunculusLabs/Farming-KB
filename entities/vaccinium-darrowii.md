It belongs to *Vaccinium* section *Cyanococcus*, the true blueberries, alongside the highbush and lowbush blueberries.

Plants are low-growing shrubs, typically 30–90 cm tall with a dense, rounded crown and multiple stems.

Leaves are small, 6–20 mm long, elliptic to obovate, glossy dark green above, and paler below.

The leaf margins are entire or very finely serrulate, and leaves persist through mild winters, giving the species its evergreen character.

Flowers are urn-shaped, 4–6 mm long, white to pink, and borne in axillary racemes of 2–6 flowers.

Blooming occurs from February to April, one of the earliest flowering periods among Vaccinium species.

Fruits are small blue-black berries, 5–8 mm in diameter, with a waxy bloom, ripening from May to July.

The fruit contains numerous small seeds and has a sweet, mildly tart flavor.

Roots are fine and fibrous, forming associations with [[plant-nutrient-uptake-mechanisms]] in acidic soils.

## Distribution and Habitat

The native range extends from southeastern Louisiana through Mississippi, Alabama, Georgia, and northern Florida.

Populations are concentrated in the Gulf Coastal Plain and the Atlantic Coastal Plain, particularly in Florida's sandhill and scrub ecosystems.

Typical habitats include sandy pine flatwoods, turkey oak barrens, xeric scrub, and dry sandy prairies.

The species is adapted to acidic, well-drained sandy soils with pH 4.0–5.5 and low [[coleman-hardy-winter-vegetables-cold-tolerance-mechanisms]] compared to northern blueberry species.

## Cultivation

**Vaccinium darrowii** is commercially important as a breeding parent rather than as a direct crop species.

Its low-chill requirement (200–400 chill hours) enables southern highbush blueberry cultivars to fruit in mild winter climates.

Key southern highbush cultivars with *V. darrowii* parentage include 'Emerald', 'Jewel', 'Sharpblue', and 'Star'.

Breeding programs at the University of Florida and USDA-ARS have extensively utilized the species for heat and disease resistance.

In cultivation, the species requires acidic soil (pH 4.0–5.5), good drainage, and full sun to partial shade.

It is drought-tolerant once established but benefits from supplemental irrigation during fruit development.

Propagation is by softwood cuttings under mist, with rooting typically occurring in 8–12 weeks.
