# 5,6-MeO-MIPT

5,6-Dimethoxy-N-Isopropyl-N-Methyltryptamine

## Chemical Identity

5,6-MeO-MIPT is 5,6-dimethoxy-N-isopropyl-N-methyltryptamine, a synthetic tryptamine carrying the methyl-isopropyl nitrogen substitution pattern combined with methoxy groups at both the 5 and 6 positions of the indole ring.

The full chemical name is 5,6-dimethoxy-3-[2-(isopropylmethylamino)ethyl]indole. The compound is also designated as 5,6-dimethoxyindole-3-ethaneamine, N-isopropyl-N-methyl.

The free base has a melting point of 71-73 degrees Celsius after crystallization from hexane. Mass spectral data shows the characteristic C5H12N+ fragment at m/z 86 (100%), the indolemethylene+ fragment at m/z 190 (4%), and the parent ion at m/z 276 (9%).

This compound belongs to the 5,6-dimethoxy series of tryptamines, which has been systematically explored but has consistently failed to demonstrate significant psychoactivity. The methyl-isopropyl nitrogen pattern is the same one found in the highly active compounds [[5-meo-mipt-entity-tihkal]] and [[4-ho-mipt-entity-tihkal]], making the inactivity of 5,6-MeO-MIPT all the more striking.

5,6-MeO-MIPT is documented in [[tihkal-introduction-and-methodology]] as entry #41.

## Dosage and Administration

The estimated threshold dosage is greater than 75 milligrams when taken orally.

Duration is unknown.

At 35 mg orally, the subject reported "Nothing at all."

At 75 mg orally, the subject reported "There was a vague awareness of something at about the one-hour point. Not enough to even be called a threshold effect."

## Pharmacology and Activity

5,6-MeO-MIPT shows no detectable psychoactivity even at the milligram per kilogram level. At 75 milligrams orally, only a vague and barely perceptible awareness was reported, which falls far short of any threshold psychedelic effect.

This compound effectively "condemns the 5,6-dimethoxy indole pattern" as a pharmacologically inactive substitution pattern for tryptamine psychedelics. The evidence extends beyond this single compound to a series of closely related derivatives that have been synthesized:

- 5,6-MeO-DMT (N,N-dimethyl): the simplest member

- 5,6-MeO-DET (N,N-diethyl): the diethyl homologue

- 5,6-MeO-DBT (N,N-dibutyl): the dibutyl homologue

- 5,6-MeO-pyr-T (pyrrolidyl): the pyrrolidine analogue

- 5,6-MeO-pip-T (piperidyl): the piperidine analogue

- 5,6-MeO-mor-T (morpholyl): the morpholine analogue

All of these were synthesized and characterized in the 1960s, but Shulgin has no record of any having been tried in human subjects prior to 5,6-MeO-MIPT.

A desoxy analogue, 5-methoxy-6-methyl-DMT (also known as 5-MeO-6,N,N-TMT), where the 6-methoxy group is replaced with a 6-methyl group, has also been tested. At 15 milligrams by smoking, nothing was noted. This level would have been dramatic had there been no substitution at the 6-position, further supporting the conclusion that the 6-position is unfavorable for tryptamine psychoactivity.

The consistent inactivity across the entire 5,6-dimethoxy series stands in contrast to the high potency of 5-methoxy-substituted tryptamines and the significant activity of 4-substituted tryptamines. The presence of a substituent at the 6-position appears to be strongly detrimental to the psychedelic activity of tryptamines, a finding that is discussed in more detail in the commentary for [[alpha-n-dmt-entity-tihkal]] (6-HO-DMT).

## Synthesis

The synthesis of 5,6-MeO-MIPT begins with 5,6-dimethoxyindole. The indole is suspended in diethyl ether, stirred, and cooled with an external ice bath. Oxalyl chloride in ether is added dropwise over 20 minutes, and the mixture is stirred for an additional 20 minutes. The glyoxyl chloride crystallizes as a red solid, which is collected by filtration, washed with ether, and dried under vacuum.

The red solid acid chloride is suspended in ice-cold dry tetrahydrofuran under nitrogen and treated with a 30% solution of methyl isopropyl amine in ether until the pH exceeds 9. After removal of solvents under vacuum, the residue is partitioned between chloroform and water. The organic phase is decolorized with charcoal, stripped of solvent, and the solid residue recrystallized from ethyl acetate and hexane to give 5,6-dimethoxy-N-isopropyl-N-methylindoleglyoxylamide with a melting point of 204-206 degrees Celsius in 40% yield.

The glyoxylamide is reduced with lithium aluminum hydride in anhydrous tetrahydrofuran under reflux for 30 minutes. After workup, the oily residue is crystallized from hexane to give 5,6-MeO-MIPT with a melting point of 71-73 degrees Celsius in 71% yield.

## The 6-Position Problem

The consistent inactivity of 5,6-disubstituted tryptamines raises an important question about the role of the 6-position in tryptamine pharmacology. Whether the substituent is a methoxy group, a methylenedioxy bridge, or a methyl group, the presence of anything at the 6-position appears to significantly reduce or eliminate psychedelic activity.

This is in contrast to the 5-position, where methoxylation produces some of the most potent tryptamine psychedelics known (such as [[5-meo-dmt-entity-tihkal]]), and the 4-position, where hydroxylation produces the psilocybin/psilocin family of compounds.

Some historical background on an oxygen at the 6-position is discussed in the entry for 6-HO-DMT, which has its own entity page as [[alpha-n-dmt-entity-tihkal]] in the TIHKAL series. The 6-position appears to be a "kiss of death" for tryptamine psychedelic activity, a finding that has important implications for understanding the structural requirements for binding at serotonin 5-HT2A receptors and related sites.
