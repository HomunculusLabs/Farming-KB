# Salvia divinorum

**Diviner's Sage** | **Ska María Pastora** | **Seer's Sage** | **Sally-D**

*Salvia divinorum* is a psychoactive perennial herb in the mint family (Lamiaceae) native to the cloud forests of the Sierra Mazateca in Oaxaca, Mexico. It is the only natural source of salvinorin A, a neoclerodane diterpenoid that is the most potent naturally occurring non-nitrogenous psychedelic known — active at doses as low as 200–500 μg. Used for centuries by Mazatec curanderos in divinatory and healing rituals, *S. divinorum* has become one of the most widely used legal (in many jurisdictions) psychedelics in the 21st century.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Clade:** Angiosperms, Eudicots, Asterids
- **Order:** Lamiales
- **Family:** Lamiaceae (mint family)
- **Genus:** *Salvia* (sages)
- **Species:** *S. divinorum* Epling & Játiva
- **First described:** Carl Epling & Carlos Játiva, 1962

*S. divinorum* is one of approximately 1,000 species in the genus *Salvia*. Its closest relatives are *S. connivens* and *S. fulgens*, both non-psychoactive Mexican species. The species epithet means "of the diviners," referencing its use in Mazatec shamanic practice. The plant is sterile in the wild — it almost never produces viable seeds and reproduces exclusively through vegetative propagation, suggesting it is a human-cultivated clone that has been maintained by Mazatec communities for centuries or possibly millennia.

## Botanical Description

- **Growth habit:** Herbaceous perennial, 0.5–1.5 m tall (up to 2.5 m in cultivation)
- **Stems:** Square (characteristic of Lamiaceae), hollow, green to purplish, branching
- **Leaves:** Opposite, ovate to broadly lanceolate, 10–25 cm long × 5–12 cm wide, serrate margin, pubescent (hairy) on both surfaces, dark green with a lighter underside; distinctive depressed vein pattern giving a quilted appearance
- **Flowers:** White to blue-violet, bilabiate (two-lipped, typical of mint family), arranged in whorls along terminal spikes 20–40 cm long; calyx green-purple, corolla 2–3 cm; blooms from October through June in its native range
- **Root system:** Fibrous, shallow; roots easily at stem nodes when in contact with soil
- **Propagation:** Primarily through stem cuttings — stems root readily in water or moist soil within 2–4 weeks

## Distribution and Habitat

- **Native range:** Endemic to a small area of the Sierra Mazateca, Oaxaca, Mexico, between 300–1,800 m elevation
- **Habitat:** Shaded, moist ravines and stream banks in cloud forest and tropical montane forest; often grows along trails and near waterfalls
- **Climate:** Humid subtropical to temperate; prefers filtered light, consistent moisture, and temperatures of 15–25 °C
- **Introduced range:** Widely cultivated globally as an ornamental and psychoactive plant; naturalized in limited areas (Hawaii, parts of Central America)
- **Cultivation:** Easily grown indoors or in subtropical gardens; requires high humidity (60–90%), well-draining rich soil, and protection from frost; does not tolerate drought or full sun

## Active Compounds

### Salvinorin A (the primary psychoactive constituent)
- **Chemical class:** Neoclerodane diterpenoid (trans-clerodane skeleton)
- **Empirical formula:** C₂₃H₂₈O₈
- **Molecular weight:** 432.47 g/mol
- **CAS:** 83729-01-5
- **Appearance:** White crystalline powder
- **Active dose:** 200–500 μg (submilligram — among the most potent natural psychedelics)
- **Content in dried leaf:** 0.08–0.37% by dry weight (varies by clone, harvest, and processing)

### Other Salvinorins
- **Salvinorin B:** Inactive metabolite of salvinorin A (deacetylated); also found naturally in small amounts
- **Salvinorin C, D, E, F, G:** Minor diterpenoids isolated from leaf; none have significant psychoactivity at natural concentrations
- **Salvinorin ethoxymethyl ether:** Semi-synthetic analog with ~10× the potency of salvinorin A (active at ~20 μg)

**Notable:** Unlike virtually all other classical psychedelics (LSD, psilocybin, DMT, mescaline), salvinorin A contains no nitrogen and is not a tryptamine, phenethylamine, or lysergamide. It is pharmacologically unique.

## Pharmacology

### Receptor Pharmacology
- **Kappa-opioid receptor (KOR):** Potent and selective agonist — EC₅₀ ~1–10 nM. This is the primary mechanism responsible for its psychoactive effects. Unlike μ-opioid agonists (morphine, heroin), KOR agonists do not produce euphoria, addiction, or respiratory depression.
- **No significant activity at:** 5-HT₂A (the primary target of classical psychedelics), μ-opioid receptors, δ-opioid receptors, dopamine receptors, cannabinoid receptors, or NMDA receptors
- **D2 receptor:** Very weak partial agonist (not clinically significant)

### Mechanism of Action
Salvinorin A's KOR agonism produces a profoundly altered state of consciousness that is qualitatively different from other psychedelics:
- Rapid activation of KOR in the ventral tegmental area (VTA) and prefrontal cortex
- Disruption of thalamocortical connectivity and sensory integration
- Modulation of dopamine release in mesolimbic pathways
- Induction of an intense dissociative, introspective, and visionary state

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Onset:** 20–60 seconds when smoked or vaporized; 5–10 minutes when sublingual (chewed as quid)
- **Peak effects:** 1–3 minutes (smoked); 15–30 minutes (sublingual)
- **Duration:** 5–15 minutes (smoked); 30–60 minutes (sublingual); 1–2 hours (oral tincture)
- **Metabolism:** Rapidly deacetylated to salvinorin B by esterases in the gut and liver; first-pass metabolism is extensive
- **Half-life:** ~60 minutes (salvinorin A); rapid clearance contributes to short duration

## Subjective Effects

The effects of *S. divinorum* are qualitatively distinct from other psychedelics and are dose-dependent:

### Threshold to Moderate Doses
- Altered perception of space, time, and gravity
- Uncontrollable laughter
- Visual distortions (geometric patterns, color intensification)
- Slight dissociation and dreaminess
- Enhanced appreciation of music and art

### Strong Doses (Breakthrough)
- Complete dissociation from consensus reality
- Vivid, immersive visionary landscapes and encounters
- Sense of merging with objects, environments, or alternate dimensions
- Loss of bodily awareness and ego dissolution
- Amnesia of the experience during and immediately after
- Experience of being "pulled," "rotated," or "folded" by an external force
- Communication with perceived entities or presences

### Aftereffects
- Residual calm, introspection, and altered perspective lasting hours
- No significant hangover or physical comedown
- Brief residual visual distortions in some users
- Potential for integration difficulties similar to other psychedelics

## Indigenous Use

The Mazatec people of Oaxaca have used *S. divinorum* for at least several centuries, possibly much longer:
- **Healing ceremonies (veladas):** Curanderos guide patients through diagnostic and therapeutic sessions, interpreting the visionary experience to identify illness causes (often supernatural)
- **Divination:** Used to locate lost objects, predict outcomes, and receive guidance
- **Method:** Traditional preparation involves pairing fresh leaves in the mouth (quid method) — 6–28 large leaves chewed slowly without swallowing, held against the cheek and gums for 15–30 minutes; the bitter juice is absorbed sublingually
- **Cultural context:** Considered an intermediary plant spirit — less powerful than mushrooms (*hongos*) but more accessible; used for "lesser" ailments and divination; often employed when mushrooms are out of season

## Legal Status

- **United States:** Unscheduled at the federal level; regulated or banned in 29+ states (CA, FL, IL, NY, OH, TX, and others vary). Not regulated by the DEA Controlled Substances Act.
- **Mexico:** Not specifically scheduled; restricted in some states
- **Australia:** Schedule 9 (prohibited substance) since 2002
- **UK:** Class B controlled substance since 2016 (Psychoactive Substances Act)
- **Germany:** Controlled under the BtMG (NPS legislation)
- **Canada:** Unscheduled but under regulatory review
- **Most other countries:** Legal or unscheduled, though rapidly changing
