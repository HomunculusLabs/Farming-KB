# Salvia divinorum

*Salvia divinorum* is a [[psychedelic-psychotherapy|psychoactive]] perennial herb in the mint family (Lamiaceae), endemic to a small region of the Sierra [[psychedelic-1960s-counterculture|Mazatec]]a in Oaxaca, Mexico. It is the only known natural source of [[psychedelic-mushroom-dosage-guide|salvinorin]] A, a neoclerodane diterpene and the most potent naturally occurring non-nitrogenous [[weil-psychedelic-experience-set-and-setting|psychedelic]] compound known. The Mazatec people have used the plant in divination and healing rituals for generations. Among approximately 700 *Salvia* species distributed worldwide, *S. divinorum* is unique in its psychoactive properties. It was scientifically described by Carl Epling and Carlos D. Játiva-M. in 1962 from specimens collected by R. Gordon Wasson near Huautla de Jiménez, Oaxaca, published in *Brittonia* 14(2): 154–159.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Order:** Lamiales
- **Family:** Lamiaceae (mint family)
- **Subfamily:** Nepetoideae
- **Tribe:** Mentheae
- **Genus:** *Salvia*
- **Species:** *Salvia divinorum* Epling & Játiva-M.
- No subspecies or varieties are formally recognized. Some researchers have proposed it may be a hybrid of *S. concolor* and *S. fulgens*, though this remains unproven.

The specific epithet *divinorum* is the Latin genitive plural of *divinus* ("divine"), meaning "of the diviners." Wasson proposed the name to Epling in reference to the Mazatec practice of using the plant for divination. In Nahuatl-related scholarship, the plant has been hypothesized to be the colonial-era *pipilzintzintli* ("the most noble little prince"), though this identification is speculative and controversial.

## Botany

*S. divinorum* grows as a large perennial herb reaching 0.5–2+ meters tall with hollow, square stems characteristic of Lamiaceae. Leaves are large (up to 30 cm), ovate, finely dentate, hairy, dark green with a lighter underside. Flowers are white to blue-violet with purple calyces, borne in whorled verticillasters on tall inflorescences up to 41 cm. Flowering occurs under short-day photoperiods, typically October–December in its native range. The plant rarely produces viable seed and is almost exclusively propagated through stem cuttings, leading most researchers to consider it a **cultigen** — a plant that exists only through human cultivation and may have lost the ability to reproduce sexually through long-term human selection. This near-sterility, combined with its remarkably restricted native range, suggests centuries of clonal propagation by Mazatec communities. Native habitat is cloud forest edges and shaded humid ravines at 300–1500 m elevation.

## Cultivars

All extant cultivated clones trace back to a very small number of introductions from Mexico, resulting in extremely low genetic diversity worldwide:

- **Wasson-Hofmann (W-H):** The original clone collected by Wasson and Hofmann in 1962. Most widely distributed clone worldwide and the basis for most research and commercial material.
- **Blosser ("Blosser's Palatable"):** Introduced by Sean Blosser from a Mazatec source. Reported to be less bitter than W-H when chewed in the traditional quid method. Slightly smaller, more elongated leaves.
- **Luna:** Collected from Mazatec territory by Kathleen Harrison (wife of Terence McKenna). Reportedly more vigorous with distinct growth characteristics.
- **Appaloosa:** Named for its variegated white/cream mottled foliage. Less common, generally less vigorous due to reduced chlorophyll.

## Chemistry

### Salvinorin A

The principal psychoactive compound is salvinorin A (C₂₃H₂₈O₈, MW 432.46 g/mol), a trans-neoclerodane diterpene with a furan ring, lactone ring, and two acetate esters. It contains no nitrogen atom, making it unique among major psychedelics. First isolated by **Alfredo Ortega** at UNAM in 1982 (originally named "divinorin A"). Daniel Siebert subsequently conducted systematic analytical work quantifying salvinorin A content across tissue types (~0.089% dry leaf weight on average; higher in younger leaves and apical tips). Bryan Roth's lab at UNC Chapel Hill confirmed salvinorin A as a highly selective kappa opioid receptor (KOR) agonist (EC₅₀ ~1–10 nM) in a seminal 2002 PNAS paper, showing no significant binding to 5-HT2A, mu-opioid, delta-opioid, or cannabinoid receptors.

### Other Compounds

Salvinorins B through M and beyond have been identified as minor constituents. Salvinorin B (C₂₃H₃₀O₇), also isolated by Ortega, is the primary metabolite and is pharmacologically inactive at KOR. **Divinatorins A–E**, isolated by Leander J. Valdés III in the early 2000s, form a separate class of neoclerodane diterpenes with weak or no KOR activity. Thomas Prisinzano's lab at the University of Kansas identified salvinicins A and B as in vivo metabolites and synthesized numerous analogs including herkinorin (a mu-opioid agonist derived from salvinorin A).

## Pharmacology

Salvinorin A's mechanism as a selective KOR agonist distinguishes it from virtually all other classical hallucinogens, which act primarily on 5-HT2A serotonin receptors. KOR activation produces dissociative, hallucinatory, and often dysphoric effects qualitatively different from classical psychedelics. Onset and duration depend heavily on route: vaporized (20–60 second onset, 5–20 minute duration), sublingual quid (5–15 minute onset, 30–90 minute duration). Oral bioavailability is extremely low due to first-pass metabolism. Half-life is approximately 1 hour. Primary metabolism is via hepatic CYP2D6 and esterase hydrolysis, with salvinorin B as the major inactive metabolite. No significant tolerance develops with repeated use.

## Traditional Mazatec Use

The Mazatec call the plant *ska María Pastora* ("leaves of Mary the Shepherdess") or *hojas de la Pastora* in Spanish, reflecting syncretic Catholic-Mazatec religious blending. It is employed in divination rituals (*veladas*) led by a curandero/a to diagnose illness, locate lost objects, or divine future events. The traditional method involves chewing fresh leaf pairs (quid method) with juice held in the mouth for buccal absorption. María Sabina, the famous Mazatec curandera, described it as a substitute for sacred mushrooms: "When there are no mushrooms and I want to heal someone who is sick, then I must fall back on the leaves of pastora. When you grind them up and eat them, they work just like the *niños*. But, of course, pastora has nowhere near as much power as the mushrooms." The plant may also represent the Aztec *pipilzintzintli* mentioned in colonial Inquisition records from 1696–1706, though this identification remains debated.

## Legal Status

Not scheduled at the US federal level but listed as a "drug of concern" by the DEA. As of the early 2020s, 28+ US states have enacted restrictions. Internationally, it is banned in Australia (since 2002), Denmark, Finland, Germany (under NpSG since 2019), Italy, Japan, Latvia, Lithuania, Poland, Romania, South Korea, Sweden, and the UK (Psychoactive Substances Act 2016). It remains legal or unscheduled in the Netherlands, New Zealand (Restricted Substance), Switzerland, and parts of Latin America. Mexico has not scheduled it at the federal level.

## Cultivation

Propagation is almost exclusively through stem cuttings, which root readily in water or moist medium within 2–4 weeks. The plant prefers partial to full shade, rich well-draining soil (pH 6.0–7.0), high humidity (60–80%+), and temperatures of 15–27°C. It is frost-sensitive (hardy only in USDA zones 10–11) and can be grown as a houseplant with humidity supplementation. Pests include spider mites, aphids, and whiteflies. The plant's global cultivation is widespread despite legal restrictions, distributed through informal networks of cuttings.


## Effects Spectrum

The subjective effects of salvinorin A are qualitatively distinct from classical psychedelics due to the KOR mechanism:

- **Mild doses** (sub-threshold to threshold): subtle visual shifts, laughter, bodily heaviness, warmth
- **Moderate doses**: strong visual transformations, sense of merging with objects, altered gravity perception, entity encounters, reality texture changes described as mechanical or fabric-like
- **Strong doses**: complete dissociation, perceived reality dissolution, amnesia of the experience, encounters with "impossible" geometries and spaces, sensation of being pulled, rotated, or folded into other dimensions
- **Common themes**: cartoony or absurd imagery, reliving memories, perception of becoming objects, a sense of conscious awareness being "reset" or "rebooted"

The characteristic dysphoria, disorientation, and sometimes terrifying nature of the experience may be explained by kappa agonism, which in animal models is associated with dissociative and aversive states. Effects are typically described as more physically immersive and less emotionally euphoric than 5-HT2A-mediated psychedelics.

## Research and Therapeutic Potential

Preclinical research has explored several therapeutic applications. Salvinorin A has shown antidepressant-like effects in animal models at sub-psychedelic doses, and reduces cocaine and alcohol self-administration in rats, consistent with KOR involvement in stress-induced relapse. Antinociceptive effects have been demonstrated in neuropathic pain models. Thomas Prisinzano's lab has synthesized numerous semisynthetic analogs (e.g., herkinorin, β-tetrahydropyran salvinorin B) exploring the structure-activity relationship for potential therapeutic leads with longer duration or reduced dysphoria. No human clinical trials have been completed.
