## Pluteus americanus

**Pluteus americanus** is a psilocybin-containing mushroom in the genus *Pluteus*, found growing on dead and decaying hardwood in [[growing-gourmet-north-american-reishi-species]] origin. Some authorities treat *P. americanus* as a synonym or variety of *P. salicinus*, while others maintain it as a distinct species based on spore morphology and DNA sequence data.

## Morphology

### Cap (Pileus)
- 15–60 mm diameter
- Convex to broadly convex, sometimes with a slight umbo
- Surface smooth to slightly silky-fibrillose
- Color: grayish brown to dark brown, often with a bluish or greenish tint when young or when bruised
- Hygrophanous: lighter when dry, often appearing silvery gray
- Margin smooth, sometimes slightly translucent-striate when moist

### Gills (Lamellae)
- **Free** (not attached to the stipe) — a key diagnostic feature of the genus
- Close to subdistant
- White when young, becoming pink to salmon-pink at maturity (pink [[spore-print]])
- Edges often white and slightly fimbriate (fringed)

### Stipe (Stem)
- 30–80 mm long, 2–6 mm thick
- Equal or slightly enlarged at the base
- Solid to hollow
- Surface: white to pale gray, sometimes with bluish staining at the base
- Smooth to slightly fibrillose
- No ring or partial veil

### Flesh
- Thin, white to pale grayish
- May develop bluish-green discoloration where cut or bruised, especially at the base
- Odor mild or slightly mealy
- Taste mild to slightly raphanoid (radish-like)

### Spores
- Spore print: pink to salmon-pink
- Spores: 6–9 × 5–7 µm, broadly ellipsoid to subglobose
- Smooth, thin-walled
- No germ pore (distinguishing feature from many [[polypore-fungi-eastern-north-america]] America:** From Ontario and Quebec south to Florida and west to the Great Plains
- **Western North America:** Reports from the [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]], though these may represent distinct taxa
- **Northeastern Mexico:** Rare reports from montane forests

It is a **wood-decomposing saprotroph** found on:

- Dead hardwood logs and stumps (especially oak, maple, ash, elm)
- Fallen branches in deciduous forests
- Wood chips in landscaping and garden settings
- Rarely on conifer wood

Fruiting occurs from late spring through autumn (May–October), typically after periods of sustained rainfall. The species prefers shaded, moist environments and is often found on wood that is well-rotted and covered with moss or other fungi.

## Chemistry

| Compound | Concentration (% dry weight) |
|----------|------------------------------|
| Psilocybin | 0.05 – 0.30 |
| Psilocin | trace – 0.05 |
| Baeocystin | trace |

Potency is generally low to moderate, comparable to *[[gartz-pluteus-salicinus-wood-inhabiting-psilocybin]]*. The blue-staining reaction, when present, correlates with higher psilocybin content. However, not all collections show significant bluing, and potency can vary considerably based on substrate, age, and environmental conditions.
