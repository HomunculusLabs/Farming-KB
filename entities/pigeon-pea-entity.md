# Pigeon Pea (Cajanus cajan)

## Description

Pigeon pea ([[bill-mollison]] includes pigeon pea among the
seed-bearing trees and shrubs recommended for poultry forage in
permaculture systems, recognising its dual value as both human food and
animal feed.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Order:** Fabales
- **Family:** Fabaceae
- **Subfamily:** Faboideae
- **Genus:** Cajanus
- **Species:** C. cajan

## Key Facts

- Short-lived perennial shrub, typically 1-4 metres tall
- Nitrogen-fixing legume improving soil fertility in mixed plantings
- Produces pods containing 2-9 seeds each, harvested as pulses
- Seeds contain 20-25% protein and are rich in B vitamins and minerals
- Deep taproot system (up to 2 metres) accesses subsoil moisture and nutrients
- Drought-tolerant, thriving in areas with 600-1500 mm annual rainfall
- Fast-growing, reaching maturity in 5-8 months from seed
- Can be harvested multiple times per year in suitable climates
- Widely adapted to soil types including poor, degraded soils
- Important [[cover-crop]] in rotational systems

## Relevance to Cultivation

### Poultry Forage

Mollison lists pigeon pea as a seed-bearing shrub for poultry range. The
seeds provide high-protein feed that falls naturally from mature pods,
creating a self-service feeding system for free-range chickens and other
poultry. The foliage is also palatable to poultry when kept at browsing
height, making it a productive component of orchard-poultry integration
designs.

### Human Food Crop

Pigeon pea is a staple pulse crop across South Asia, Africa, and the
Caribbean. The dried seeds (dal) are cooked in soups, stews, and curries.
Young green pods can be eaten as a vegetable. This dual-purpose nature
aligns with [[permaculture-principles]] of stacking functions.

### Permaculture Design Roles

- **Poultry forage:** Seed drop and browse foliage for free-range fowl
- **Food crop:** Protein-rich pulses for human consumption
- **Nitrogen fixer:** Enriches soil through rhizobium symbiosis
- **Windbreak and hedge:** Woody growth forms useful shelter belts
- **Mulch producer:** Pruned foliage provides abundant green mulch
- **Alley cropping:** Compatible with cereal and vegetable intercropping

### Companion Planting

Pigeon pea integrates well with other poultry forage species such as
Coprosma repens, Caragana, tagasaste, boxthorn (Lycium spp.), and
mulberry. As a nitrogen fixer, it benefits neighbouring fruit trees and
vegetables. In tropical home gardens, it is commonly interplanted with
cassava, maize, and sweet potato.

### Cultivation Notes

- Direct sow seeds 2-5 cm deep after frost danger has passed
- Prefers full sun and well-drained soils; tolerates pH 5.0-7.5
- Minimal fertiliser needed due to nitrogen fixation
- Prune to 1-2 metres to maintain poultry-accessible browse height
- Susceptible to wilt diseases (Fusarium) in waterlogged conditions
- Harvest pods when dry and brittle; seeds store well for months
- In frost-free areas, plants persist 3-5 years with ongoing production

### Climate Suitability

Pigeon pea excels in tropical and subtropical climates (USDA zones 9-12).
It is killed by frost but can be grown as an annual in temperate regions
with long warm seasons. Its drought tolerance makes it particularly
valuable in semi-arid permaculture designs.

## Summary

Pigeon pea is a quintessential permaculture plant, combining nitrogen
fixation, human food production, and poultry forage in a single hardy
species. Its role in Mollison's poultry forage palette highlights its
value as a seed-bearing shrub that sustains free-range fowl while
simultaneously producing food for people and building soil fertility.
