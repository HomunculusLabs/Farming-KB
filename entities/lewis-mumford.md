# Lewis Mumford

## Description

Lewis Mumford (1895–1990) was an American historian, sociologist, and philosopher of technology regarded as one of the 20th century's most important public intellectuals on cities, technology, and human settlement. David Holmgren cites Mumford's *The City in History* (1961, Penguin) as providing "the classic overview" of urban development.

## Background and Classification

- **Type:** Person — historian, philosopher, urban theorist, cultural critic
- **Nationality:** American
- **Lifespan:** 1895–1990
- **Key works:** *The City in History* (1961), *Technics and Civilization* (1934), *The Myth of the Machine* (1967–1970)
- **Domain:** Urban history, philosophy of technology, cultural criticism

## Key Facts

- Authored over 30 books spanning urban planning, technology criticism, architecture, and cultural history
- *The City in History* traces the evolution of human settlements from prehistoric villages to modern megacities
- Coined "megatechnics" to describe technological systems accumulating power beyond human control
- Advocated for human-scale, regionally appropriate technology and settlement patterns
- Influenced organic architecture, regional planning, and ecological design thinking
- Received the National Medal of Arts (1986)

## Relevance to Permaculture and Cultivation

- **Settlement pattern thinking:** His analysis of city growth and transformation provides a framework for Permaculture's bioregional approach
- **Appropriate technology:** His critique of over-scaled technology parallels Permaculture Principle 9 (Use Small and Slow Solutions)
- **Energy and civilisation:** His analysis of how energy sources shape settlement patterns informs Holmgren's energy descent scenarios
- **Urban design:** Understanding historical city evolution helps urban Permaculture designers identify leverage points
- **Systems thinking:** Mumford's holistic approach to cities as living organisms anticipates systems ecology
- **Military and urban development:** Holmgren notes the connection between permanent armies and the first cities in the Middle East — explored in Mumford's work
