## Overview

Jasione laevis, commonly called smooth sheep's-bit or perennial sheep's-bit, is a low-growing perennial herb in the bellflower family.

It is native to mountainous regions of central and southern Europe, where it inhabits rocky slopes and alpine meadows.

The species forms compact cushions of narrow basal leaves topped by rounded heads of pale blue flowers.

It is valued in rock garden cultivation for its neat habit and long flowering period from late spring through midsummer.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Jasione laevis belongs to family Campanulaceae, tribe Jasioneae, genus Jasione.

The genus Jasione comprises approximately 15 species distributed across Europe and the Mediterranean basin.

Jasione laevis was first described by Jean-Baptiste Lamarck and is sometimes listed under the synonym [[jasione-montana]] but differs in its perennial habit and larger flower heads.

The species forms a compact cushion or tuft of basal rosettes 5–15 cm tall in vegetative state.

Flowering stems are erect, slender, and leafless or with few reduced bracts, reaching 15–30 cm in height.

Basal leaves are linear to narrowly spatulate, 1–4 cm long, entire, and glabrous or sparsely hairy.

Flower heads are capitulum-like, 1.5–3 cm in diameter, consisting of numerous small flowers.

Individual flowers are 5–8 mm across with five pale blue to lavender-blue corolla lobes.

Each flower has five stamens that form a tube around the style, characteristic of the Campanulaceae.

## Distribution and Habitat

Jasione laevis is native to the mountains of central and southern Europe, including the Alps, Pyrenees, Carpathians, and Balkan ranges.

Its range extends from eastern Spain through southern France, Switzerland, Italy, and into the Balkan Peninsula.

Populations occur at elevations ranging from 500 to 2,500 meters, primarily in subalpine and alpine zones.

The species inhabits rocky slopes, scree, limestone pavements, and alpine grasslands with thin, well-drained soils.

It is a strict calcicole, found almost exclusively on limestone and other base-rich substrates.

Habitats include windswept ridges, cliff ledges, and crevices where competition from taller vegetation is minimal.

The species is locally common within its range but absent from siliceous mountain ranges.

## Ecology

Jasione laevis is a long-lived perennial that persists in harsh alpine conditions through its [[extending-growing-season]].

[[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]].

It requires full sun, excellent drainage, and a neutral to alkaline soil mix with added grit or limestone chips.

Propagation is by seed sown in late winter or by careful division of established cushions in early spring.

The species is relatively pest-free and disease-resistant when grown in appropriate conditions.

It associates well with other alpine cushion plants such as Saxifraga, Androsace, and Silene species.
