# Baeocystin

**Baeocystin** (4-phosphoryloxy-N-methyltryptamine) is a naturally occurring indole alkaloid found in psilocybin-producing mushrooms. It is a direct biosynthetic intermediate between norbaeocystin and psilocybin, and is the monomethyl analog of psilocybin (which carries an N,N-dimethyl substitution). Baeocystin is consistently detected as a minor to moderate constituent of psilocybin mushroom alkaloid profiles across the genus *Psilocybe* and related genera.






## Chemical Identity

- **IUPAC name:** [3-[2-(Methylamino)ethyl]-1H-indol-4-yl] dihydrogen phosphate
- **Molecular formula:** C₁₁H₁₅N₂O₄P
- **Molecular weight:** 270.23 g/mol
- **CAS number:** 520-53-6
- **Structure:** Tryptamine backbone with a phosphate ester at C4 of the indole ring and a single N-methyl group on the ethylamine side chain






## Relationship to Psilocybin

Baeocystin occupies a unique position in the psilocybin alkaloid family. It is the N-monomethyl intermediate in the pathway from tryptophan to psilocybin:

- **Norbaeocystin** (desmethyl) → psim-enzyme|PsiM → **Baeocystin** (monomethyl) → psim-enzyme|PsiM → **Psilocybin** (dimethyl)

Unlike psilocybin, which is fully dephosphorylated to the active psilocin in vivo, baeocystin's pharmacology after oral administration is less well characterized. It is presumed to undergo dephosphorylation to 4-hydroxy-N-methyltryptamine, which may contribute to the overall psychoactive profile of mushroom preparations.






## Natural Occurrence

Baeocystin was first isolated and identified from *Psilocybe baeocystis* (hence the name) and has since been detected in most psilocybin-containing species studied. Typical baeocystin levels range from trace amounts to approximately 0.1–0.3% dry weight, though some species and individual specimens can accumulate higher concentrations.

Notable species containing baeocystin:
- *Psilocybe cubensis* — commonly detected as a minor alkaloid
- *Psilocybe semilanceata* — consistently present
- *Psilocybe azurescens* — present alongside very high psilocybin levels
- *Panaeolus cyanescens* — detected in varying amounts
- *Inocybe aeruginascens* — co-occurs with the related compound aeruginascin






## Pharmacology

The pharmacological profile of baeocystin remains less thoroughly investigated than that of psilocybin. Key considerations:

- **Receptor affinity:** Expected to act as a partial agonist at 5-HT2A receptors following dephosphorylation, similar to psilocin but with potentially different potency and efficacy due to the N-monomethyl substitution
- **Prodrug behavior:** Like psilocybin, baeocystin likely functions as a prodrug, with alkaline phosphatases cleaving the phosphate ester to release the active hydroxylated amine
- **Contribution to mushroom effects:** Baeocystin may modulate the overall subjective experience when consumed as part of whole mushroom preparations, potentially contributing to the qualitative differences reported between isolated psilocybin and whole-mushroom experiences
- **Potential entourage effect:** The presence of baeocystin, norbaeocystin, and other minor alkaloids in mushroom tissue may produce pharmacological effects not replicated by pure psilocybin alone






## Analytical Detection

Baeocystin is routinely detected in mushroom alkaloid analyses using HPLC-UV, HPLC-MS/MS, and GC-MS methods. It is typically resolved from psilocybin and norbaeocystin using reversed-phase chromatography. The compound is less stable than psilocybin under acidic extraction conditions, requiring careful sample preparation to avoid degradation or artifact formation.
