The compound acts primarily as an NMDA receptor antagonist, producing characteristic
dissociative effects including [[methoxetamine]]),
and 3-MeO-PCP. The key structural feature is the 3-methoxy substitution on
the phenyl ring, which distinguishes it from PCE and significantly alters
both potency and subjective effects.

## Pharmacology

The primary mechanism of action is non-competitive antagonism of the NMDA receptor,
blocking glutamate signaling at the PCP binding site within the ion channel pore.
This produces the characteristic dissociative state common to all arylcyclohexylamines.

Additional pharmacological activity includes:

- **Sigma-1 receptor agonism:** Contributes to the psychotomimetic and
  antidepressant-like effects observed in animal models. Sigma-1 activity is
  thought to modulate the emotional and hallucinatory dimensions of the experience.
- **Dopamine reuptake inhibition:** Minor but measurable DAT inhibition may
  contribute to stimulant-like side effects (tachycardia, restlessness).
- **Serotonergic activity:** Weak affinity for SERT has been reported but is
  not considered clinically significant at recreational doses.
