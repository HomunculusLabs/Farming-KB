## Overview

3-Methoxyeticyclidine (3-MeO-PCE) is a dissociative anesthetic compound belonging
to the arylcyclohexylamine class. Structurally, it is the 3-methoxy analog of
eticyclidine (PCE), itself a close derivative of phencyclidine (PCP). 3-MeO-PCE
emerged on the research chemical market in the mid-2010s and has gained popularity
among dissociative enthusiasts for its long duration, potency, and complex
headspace — often described as a hybrid between [[ketamine]] and MXE-like effects.

The compound acts primarily as an NMDA receptor antagonist, producing characteristic
dissociative effects including [[psilocybin-ego-dissolution-mechanisms]], sensory disconnection, and
analgesia. It is not approved for medical use in any jurisdiction.

## Chemical Properties

- **IUPAC name:** 1-(1-(3-methoxyphenyl)cyclohexyl)ethan-1-amine
- **Molecular formula:** C₁₅H₂₃NO
- **Molecular weight:** 233.35 g/mol
- **CAS number:** Unregistered (research chemical)
- **Appearance:** White to off-white crystalline powder
- **Solubility:** Soluble in ethanol, moderately soluble in water

3-MeO-PCE is structurally related to several known dissociatives. It shares
the arylcyclohexylamine scaffold with PCP, ketamine, MXE ([[methoxetamine]]),
and 3-MeO-PCP. The key structural feature is the 3-methoxy substitution on
the phenyl ring, which distinguishes it from PCE and significantly alters
both potency and subjective effects.

## Pharmacology

The primary mechanism of action is non-competitive antagonism of the NMDA receptor,
blocking glutamate signaling at the PCP binding site within the ion channel pore.
This produces the characteristic dissociative state common to all arylcyclohexylamines.

Additional pharmacological activity includes:

- **Sigma-1 receptor agonism:** Contributes to the psychotomimetic and
  antidepressant-like effects observed in animal models. Sigma-1 activity is
  thought to modulate the emotional and hallucinatory dimensions of the experience.
- **Dopamine reuptake inhibition:** Minor but measurable DAT inhibition may
  contribute to stimulant-like side effects (tachycardia, restlessness).
- **Serotonergic activity:** Weak affinity for SERT has been reported but is
  not considered clinically significant at recreational doses.

## Effects and Dosage

### Dosage Ranges

- **Threshold:** 5–10 mg (insufflated)
- **Light:** 10–20 mg
- **Common:** 15–30 mg
- **Strong:** 25–40 mg
- **Heavy:** 40+ mg (high risk of [[blesching-cannabis-safety-adverse-effects-evidence]])

[[first-pass-metabolism-and-oral-bioavailability]] is significant but onset is slower (45–90 minutes vs.
5–15 minutes insufflated). Oral doses are typically 1.5–2× the insufflated dose.

### Effects Profile

- **Onset:** 5–15 minutes (insufflated), 45–90 minutes (oral)
- **Peak:** 1–2 hours
- **Duration:** 4–8 hours (significantly longer than ketamine)
