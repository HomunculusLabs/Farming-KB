**Parkia biglobosa**, commonly known as the **African locust bean tree**, is a perennial leguminous tree in the family Fabaceae, subfamily Mimosoideae.

**Tree morphology:**

- Large deciduous tree reaching 10–20 meters in height, with a spreading, umbrella-shaped crown.
- Bark is dark grey to brown, rough and fissured, exuding a reddish gum when cut.
- Leaves are bipinnately compound, 20–40 cm long, with 8–20 pairs of pinnae, each bearing numerous small leaflets.
- Leaflets are tiny (3–6 mm), oblong, and fold at night (nyctinastic movement).
- Flowers are arranged in large, dense, hanging heads (racemes) up to 20 cm long, with bright red stamens that create a showy, brush-like appearance.
- Pods are long, linear, and slightly curved, 30–50 cm long and 2–3 cm wide, containing 15–30 seeds embedded in a sweet, powdery yellow pulp.
- Seeds are hard, glossy, brown to black, approximately 1 cm long.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Parkia biglobosa* is native to the Sudanian and Guinean savanna zones of sub-Saharan Africa.

Its natural range extends from Senegal in the west through Mali, Burkina Faso, Ghana, Nigeria, Cameroon, Chad, and Sudan.

It is highly drought-tolerant and can survive extended dry seasons of 5–7 months.

## Uses and Cultural Significance

**Food:**

The sweet yellow pulp surrounding the seeds is eaten fresh as a snack and is rich in sugars (up to 40% sucrose), vitamin C, and minerals.

The seeds are the primary economic product, fermented to produce dawadawa, a strong-smelling, protein-rich condiment essential to West African cooking.

Dawadawa is used to flavor soups, stews, and sauces, providing both umami depth and significant nutritional value.

Fermented seeds contain approximately 35–40% protein, making them one of the most important plant-based protein sources in the region.

The seeds are also roasted and used as a coffee substitute in some areas.
