## Overview

Andreaea rupestris, commonly known as rock moss or granite moss, is a species of moss in the family Andreaeaceae.

It is one of the most widespread species within its genus, occurring across boreal, montane, and arctic regions of the Northern Hemisphere.

The species is characteristically found growing on exposed acidic rock surfaces, particularly granite and quartzite outcrops.

Andreaea rupestris belongs to the class Andreaeopsida, an early-diverging group of mosses that lack a peristome and possess unique [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]], the species ranges from Greenland and the Canadian Arctic southward through the Rocky Mountains and Appalachian Mountains.

The species is also reported from mountainous regions of central and southern Europe, including the Alps, Carpathians, and Pyrenees.

Its preferred habitat is exposed, acidic rock surfaces, particularly granite, gneiss, and quartzite.

It is commonly found at elevations between 500 and 2500 meters, though it can occur from sea level in arctic regions to alpine zones.

The moss tolerates extreme desiccation and temperature fluctuations, making it well-suited to exposed rock surfaces.

It typically grows in dry, well-drained microsites where competition from vascular plants is minimal.
