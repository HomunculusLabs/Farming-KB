# Medlar (Mespilus germanica)

## Description

The medlar is a small deciduous tree in the family Rosaceae, cultivated since ancient times across Europe, the Mediterranean, and western Asia for its distinctive pome fruit. Mespilus germanica is native to southwest Asia and southeast Europe, and has been grown for at least 3,000 years, with references in Greek, Roman, and [[solanaceous-hallucinogens-of-medieval-european-witchcraft]] literature. The fruit is unique among temperate pome fruits in requiring bletting — a post-harvest ripening process through which the hard, astringent fruit softens to a custard-like consistency with a complex spiced apple-pear flavour. In [[food-forest]] plantings where longevity and reliability are valued.
- Medlar flowers appear late in spring compared to other pome fruits, significantly reducing frost damage risk and making the species reliable in frost-prone sites and marginal growing areas.
- The tree is relatively pest and disease resistant compared to apples, fitting well into organic orchard systems that aim to minimise or eliminate chemical biocide use through selection of resistant varieties.
- The bletted fruit provides a unique late-autumn to winter food source when few other fresh fruits are available, extending the seasonal food production window on the homestead.
- Medlar was historically one of the most important winter fruits in Europe before the widespread adoption of cold storage and imported tropical fruits; it declined in popularity during the Victorian era but is experiencing a revival among heritage fruit enthusiasts.

## Cultivation Requirements

Medlar is adaptable to a wide range of soil types including clay, preferring slightly acidic to neutral pH. It tolerates partial shade but fruits best in full sun. The tree is drought-tolerant once established and has low fertiliser requirements. Propagation is by grafting onto hawthorn (Crataegus), quince (Cydonia), or pear rootstocks. Little pruning is required — the tree naturally forms an attractive spreading shape. Fruit is harvested in late autumn after exposure to frost, then bletted in cool, dry conditions before eating fresh or using in preserves, jellies, and desserts.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Medlar is an excellent candidate for diversified permaculture orchards seeking to extend the fruit season and reduce reliance on spray programs. Its late flowering and natural disease resistance make it a low-risk addition to polyculture orchard guilds. As a member of the Rosaceae, it shares many guild associations with apples and pears, benefiting from the same insectary plants (umbellifers, compositae), nitrogen fixers (clovers, [[comfrey]]) in the orchard understory. The tree's deep root system and longevity contribute to soil stability and long-term soil organic matter development. Its cultural and culinary uniqueness adds value to farm-gate sales and specialty markets.
