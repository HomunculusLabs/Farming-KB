## 4,5-MDO-DMT

4,5-Methylenedioxy-N,N-Dimethyltryptamine

## Chemical Identity

4,5-MDO-DMT is N,N-dimethyl-4,5-methylenedioxytryptamine, a synthetic tryptamine carrying a methylenedioxy bridge spanning the 4 and 5 positions of the indole ring. This is the substitution pattern that covers both of the aromatic positions most strongly associated with psychedelic activity in tryptamines: the 4-position (the site of hydroxylation in [[tryptamine-chemistry-psilocin-and-psilocybin-synthesis]]) and the 5-position (the site of the potent 5-methoxy substitution in compounds like 5-MeO-DMT).

The full chemical name is 3-[2-(dimethylamino)ethyl]-4,5-methylenedioxyindole. The compound is also designated as 5H-1,3-dioxolo-[4,5-e]indole-7-ethaneamine, N,N-dimethyl.

The free base has a melting point of 93-95 degrees Celsius.

4,5-MDO-DMT is documented in [[4-5-mdo-dipt-entity-tihkal]] - the N,N-diisopropyl 4,5-methylenedioxy analogue
- [[5-meo-dmt-entity-tihkal]] - 5-methoxy-DMT, the 5-methoxy compound

## Historical Significance

4,5-MDO-DMT represents one of the most theoretically promising unexplored tryptamines in the Shulgin catalogue. The combination of the two most pharmacologically significant positions on the indole ring (4 and 5) with the methylenedioxy bridge pattern that has proven so productive in the phenethylamine series makes this compound a particularly compelling target for future research.

The fact that it remains untested despite its theoretical appeal speaks to the practical limitations of the Shulgin research program and the vast number of compounds that await exploration.
