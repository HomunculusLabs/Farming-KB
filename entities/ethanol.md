# Ethanol

## Description

Ethanol ([[pf-tek-200-proof-ethyl-alcohol-procurement-state-permit]], pure grain alcohol, C₂H₅OH) is a primary alcohol and one of the most desirable solvents for [[cannabis-extraction-basics]]. It possesses extraction properties very similar to methanol but with significantly lower toxicity. However, its status as the primary psychoactive ingredient in alcoholic beverages means it is heavily taxed and regulated, making pure (non-denatured) ethanol difficult to obtain for extraction purposes.

## Classification

- **Category**: Primary alcohol solvent
- **Chemical formula**: C₂H₅OH
- **Molecular weight**: 46.07 g/mol
- **Boiling point**: 78.5°C (173°F)
- **CAS Number**: 64-17-5
- **Appearance**: Colorless liquid

## Key Facts

- **Extraction properties very similar to methanol** — effectively dissolves cannabinoid-rich [[comparison-essential-oils-vs-neem-oil-vs-insecticidal-soap]] without significant co-extraction of water-soluble impurities.
- **Significantly less toxic** than methanol, making it inherently safer for use in consumable product extraction.
- Boiling point of 78.5°C allows for controlled evaporation and solvent recovery through distillation.
- **Very difficult to obtain in pure form** due to alcohol taxation and regulation. Pure ethanol is a controlled substance in many jurisdictions.
- Denatured ethanol (available in hardware stores and pharmacies) contains non-removable poisons that evaporate at the same temperature as pure ethanol, rendering the ethanol unfit for consumption and unsafe for extract production.
- Pure ethanol can be produced by distillation from fermented material, though this process is regulated in many countries.
- Used both as a primary extraction solvent and as a solvent for isomerization reactions (converting CBD to THC using [[fukuoka-textdoc-fertilizer-soil-destruction-sulfuric-acid]] catalyst).
- Absolute ethanol (100%, water-free) is required for the isomerization process; any water present interferes with the acid-catalyzed conversion.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Processing

Ethanol is considered the gold standard among common alcohol solvents for cannabis extraction. Its low toxicity means that trace residues in the final product are far less dangerous than methanol residues. In the extraction workflow, ethanol functions identically to methanol: powdered cannabis is refluxed in ethanol for 3–4 hours, the solution is filtered, and the solvent is distilled off. The resulting crude oil can then be purified through petroleum ether partitioning. Ethanol is also the solvent of choice for the isomerization step, where sulfuric acid in ethanol catalyzes the conversion of cannabidiol to THC and simultaneously rotates THC to its more potent isomeric form.

## Regulatory and Availability Challenges

| Form | Availability | Suitability for Extraction |
|---|---|---|
| Pure grain alcohol (95%+) | Restricted, heavily taxed | Excellent — preferred solvent |
| Denatured ethanol | Hardware stores, pharmacies | Unusable — contains non-removable poisons |
| Distilled from fermented material | Home production (regulated) | Good — requires distillation setup |
| Everclear (190 proof) | Limited states (US) | Good — high purity grain alcohol |

## Safety Considerations

- The least toxic of the common extraction alcohols, but still flammable.
- Denatured ethanol must never be used for producing consumable extracts — the denaturants cannot be separated.
- When used for isomerization, absolute (anhydrous) ethanol is required; residual water will interfere with the acid catalyst.
- Standard reflux [[mushroom-edibility-testing-safety-protocols]] apply: sealed apparatus, water bath heating, no open flames.
