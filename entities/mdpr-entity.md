# MDPR

MDPR (N-Propyl-MDA; 3,4-Methylenedioxy-N-propylamphetamine) is an N-propyl substituted analogue of mda-entity|MDA that is notable primarily for its remarkable properties as a psychedelic primer.

It is documented as entry #118 in PiHKAL by [[alexander-shulgin]].

## Chemical Identity

Full chemical name: 3,4-Methylenedioxy-N-propylamphetamine.

Molecular formula: C13H19NO2 (free base).

MDPR is the N-propyl homologue of mda-entity|MDA, with a three-carbon propyl chain on the nitrogen atom.

The hydrochloride salt melts at 190-192 degrees Celsius, with recrystallization raising this to 194-195 degrees Celsius.

The NMR spectrum was confirmed as completely consistent with the assigned chemical structure.

## Dosage and Duration

Dosage: greater than 200 mg (as a standalone compound).

Duration: unknown.

As a standalone compound, MDPR is essentially inactive at doses up to 200 mg.

However, its true significance lies not in its standalone activity but in its ability to dramatically potentiate other psychedelics when taken as a primer.

## Qualitative Effects (Standalone)

At 200 mg, the only effects reported are "the slightest hints of physical response, maybe a smidgin of a lightheadedness at the one hour point."

Perhaps slight teeth clenching was noted, but "certainly there is no central mental effect."

Reports in the 150-200 mg range suggested "something was taking place in the periphery" without clear central effects.

The term "body window" was coined to describe this peripheral opening, analogous to the "window" term used for mdma-entity|MDMA in the 1970s.

## The Primer Effect

MDPR's most significant pharmacological property is its ability to serve as a "primer" for subsequent psychedelic experiences.

When 160 mg of MDPR is taken and followed two hours later by 60 micrograms of LSD, the results are extraordinary.

One report described "visual phenomena that were extraordinary" at the beach south of Mendocino, with "endlessly deep microcosm of detail" in natural objects.

Another report described a "startling folie-a-deux" involving reliving the origins of human arrival on earth, a shared eyes-closed fantasy that "seemed to be the same script for both of us."

At 160 mg MDPR followed by 100 micrograms of LSD, the experience was "almost too intoxicating."

One subject was able to temporarily suppress the experience to perform in a string quartet, demonstrating "the mind has a remarkable ability to control the particular place the psyche is in."

After returning home and allowing 20 minutes, the subject was "back into a rolling +++."

## Drug-Drug Interaction Concepts

MDPR research contributed to the development of several important concepts in psychopharmacology.

The "primer" concept involves taking an inactive drug followed by an active drug, where the inactive drug may enhance the active drug's effects.

The "piggyback" concept involves adding a second drug during the latter part of an active drug's effectiveness.

The "synergism" concept involves simultaneous administration of two active drugs.

MDPR proved to be an "extraordinary primer" for LSD at modest doses, even though it was completely inactive on its own.

## Comparison with MDAL

MDAL (N-allyl-MDA, entry #101) has a nearly identical N-substitution pattern (three-carbon allyl vs three-carbon propyl).

However, while MDPR at 180 mg is a "fantastic enhancer of LSD action," the same weight of MDAL not only does not enhance but actually "seems to somewhat antagonize the action of LSD."

This dramatic difference from just a couple of hydrogen atoms (C13H18ClNO2 vs C13H20ClNO2) illustrates the exquisite sensitivity of receptor interactions to subtle structural changes.

## Synthesis

The synthesis begins with propylamine, which is converted to its hydrochloride salt by addition of concentrated HCl.

The propylamine hydrochloride is reacted with 3,4-methylenedioxyphenylacetone (the ketone precursor to mdma-entity|MDMA) and sodium cyanoborohydride in methanol.

The pH is maintained at approximately 6 throughout the reaction.

The product is isolated as the free base by distillation (bp 85-90 degrees C at 0.2 mm/Hg) and converted to the hydrochloride salt.

## Pharmacological Context

MDPR represents an important but underexplored area of psychedelic pharmacology: the interaction between inactive receptor ligands and active compounds.

The compound appears to bind to receptor sites without activating them, yet somehow sensitizes or primes these sites for subsequent activation by LSD.

This suggests that MDPR may be an antagonist or partial agonist at one or more serotonin receptor subtypes.

The "body window" effect suggests peripheral effects on serotonin receptors outside the central nervous system.

## Harm Reduction

While MDPR is inactive alone, its primer effect can dramatically amplify the effects of subsequently administered psychedelics.

Users should be aware that combining MDPR with any psychedelic may produce effects far beyond what would be expected from the psychedelic alone.

The dose of the subsequent psychedelic should be significantly reduced when MDPR is used as a primer.

MDPR has not been extensively studied for safety or toxicity at the high doses (160-200 mg) used in primer experiments.
