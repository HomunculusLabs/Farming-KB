## Overview

Primula cortusoides is a perennial herbaceous plant in the family Primulaceae, native to Siberia and the Ural Mountains. It is commonly known as the Cortusa primrose or Siberian primrose.

The species produces rounded, softly hairy leaves in basal rosettes and bears loose umbels of pink to lilac flowers in late spring. It is valued in rock gardens and alpine plantings.

Primula cortusoides belongs to the Cortusoides section of the genus, a group of Asian species characterized by rounded, often hairy leaves and corymbose or umbellate inflorescences. The section includes approximately 20 species distributed across Siberia, Central Asia, and China.

Several other species in this section, such as Primula saxatilis and Primula matthioli, are also cultivated as ornamentals. Primula cortusoides is the most widely grown member of the section in western gardens.

The plant is relatively compact, typically reaching 15–30 cm in height including the flower stalks. Its modest size makes it suitable for small garden spaces and container cultivation.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Primula cortusoides was described by Linnaeus in 1753. The specific epithet refers to the genus Cortusa, to which the species was originally assigned before being transferred to Primula.

The basal rosette consists of broadly ovate to orbicular leaves measuring 4–10 cm long and 3–8 cm wide. The leaf blade is shallowly cordate at the base with crenate to dentate margins.

Both leaf surfaces are covered with soft, glandular hairs, giving the foliage a slightly sticky texture. The upper surface is medium green, while the underside is paler with more prominent venation.

The flowering stem is 10–25 cm tall, bearing an umbel of 5–15 flowers at its apex. Each flower measures 12–18 mm in diameter with five notched petals.

Petal color ranges from soft pink to deep rose or lilac, with a yellow or white eye at the center. The calyx is campanulate with lanceolate lobes and is typically covered in glandular hairs.

The flowers are lightly fragrant, with a subtle sweet scent that is most noticeable on calm, warm days. This fragrance enhances the species' appeal as a garden plant near seating areas.

The style is slender and extends slightly beyond the corolla tube. The species is distylous, with long-styled and short-styled morphs occurring in natural populations.

The rootstock is a short, thick rhizome producing fibrous roots. The plant slowly forms clumps through offset rosettes produced at the base of mature plants.

Individual plants typically live for five to ten years under garden conditions. Periodic division rejuvenates older clumps and maintains flowering vigor.

## Distribution and Habitat

Primula cortusoides is native to southern Siberia, the Ural Mountains, and adjacent regions of northern Kazakhstan and Mongolia. It occurs at elevations of 300–1,800 meters.

The species performs best in well-drained, humus-rich soil with a slightly acidic to neutral pH. Heavy clay soils should be amended with compost or leaf mold before planting.

A surface mulch of chopped leaves or pine needles helps maintain soil moisture and moderate soil temperature. This is particularly important during the first winter after planting.

Propagation by seed is straightforward, with best results from fresh seed sown in autumn. Cold stratification improves germination rates significantly.
