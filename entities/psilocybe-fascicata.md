## Psilocybe fascicata

*Psilocybe fascicata* is a bluing psilocybin-containing mushroom species in the family Hymenogastraceae.
It was originally described from Mexico and is one of the less commonly encountered psychoactive Psilocybe species, known primarily from subtropical and tropical regions of [[forensic-identification-methods-psilocybin-mushrooms-tlc-gc-hplc]].

## Morphology

### Macroscopic Features

- **Cap**: 5 to 20 mm in diameter, conic to convex, expanding to broadly convex with age. Surface is hygrophanous, brownish to ochraceous when moist, fading to pale tan or straw-colored as it dries. Surface is smooth to slightly viscid when wet, becoming matte as it dries. The margin is often slightly translucent-striate when moist.
- **Gills**: Adnate to adnexed, grayish initially, darkening to purplish brown with spore maturity. Gills bruise bluish-green when damaged, sometimes slowly but distinctly. Gill edges are typically lighter (whitish) than the gill faces, creating a two-toned appearance. The gills are subdistant to close, with 20 to 30 gills reaching the stem and occasional lamellulae interspersed.
- **Stem**: 30 to 70 mm long, 1 to 2 mm thick, equal to slightly enlarged at the base, hollow. White to pale brown, staining intensely blue where handled or injured. The stem surface may show fine longitudinal fibrils, especially near the apex. The stem base may be slightly mycelioid or covered with fine rhizomorphs anchoring the cluster to the substrate.
- **Partial veil**: When present, it is ephemeral and often leaves no persistent annulus on the stem. Cortinate veil remnants may be observed on the cap margin in young specimens as fine fibrillose threads connecting the cap edge to the stem.
- **Flesh**: Thin, whitish to pale brownish in the cap, bruising slowly to distinctly blue when cut or damaged. The bluing reaction may take several minutes to develop fully, beginning as a greenish tint that deepens to blue-black.

### Microscopic Features

- **Spores**: Approximately 10 to 13 by 6 to 8 micrometers, elliptical to rhomboidal in shape, with a distinct apical germ pore approximately 1 to 1.5 micrometers in diameter. Spore print is dark purplish brown to nearly black.
- **Basidia**: Typically 4-spored, though 2-spored basidia may occur occasionally. Clamp connections are absent at the base of basidia, consistent with the genus Psilocybe.
- **Cheilocystidia**: Present on the gill edges, ventricose to lageniform (flask-shaped), often with a narrow neck. These sterile cells are hyaline (transparent) and thin-walled, measuring approximately 15 to 25 by 5 to 8 micrometers.
- **Pleurocystidia**: Less common than cheilocystidia, when present they are similar in morphology but generally fewer in number.
- **Pileipellis**: A cutis composed of interwoven hyphae, with some erect terminal elements giving a slightly felted appearance under magnification. The hyphae are cylindrical, 3 to 6 micrometers in diameter, and may be slightly gelatinized.

### Bluing Reaction

The blue staining reaction is prominent and serves as the primary field indicator of psychoactive content.
The bluing occurs on the stem (especially near the base), gills, and sometimes the cap flesh when bruised, indicating the presence of [[comparison-nutrient-cycling-vs-nutrient-dense-gardening]] and carbon release in montane forest ecosystems.

The species has a strong preference for partially decayed hardwood, particularly fallen branches and logs in advanced stages of decomposition.
It may also colonize wood-chip mulch and landscaping debris in disturbed areas near forest edges, though natural forest occurrences are more typical.

Its clustered growth form may provide a competitive advantage by concentrating enzymatic activity at a single point on the substrate, enabling more efficient colonization of dense woody material.
Associations with specific tree species have not been well documented, but collections have frequently been made in proximity to oaks (*Quercus* spp.) and pines (*Pinus* spp.) in mixed pine-oak forests.

## Cultivation

*Psilocybe fascicata* is not commonly cultivated, as it is less well-known and less widely distributed than species like psilocybe-mexicana-cultivation.
However, its wood-inhabiting nature suggests it could potentially be grown on sterilized wood-based substrates using techniques similar to those developed for other lignicolous psilocybin mushrooms.

Successful cultivation would likely require a supplemented hardwood sawdust substrate, high humidity, moderate temperatures (20 to 25 degrees Celsius), and adequate fresh air exchange.
Initiation of fruiting may require a temperature drop or increase in humidity, mimicking natural conditions in its montane habitat.
