## Mycena polygramma

## Description

*Mycena polygramma* (the grooved bonnet) is a small saprotrophic basidiomycete mushroom in the family Mycenaceae. It is characterized by a bell-shaped grey-brown cap and a distinctive longitudinally grooved or striate stipe. This species is commonly found on decaying wood and leaf litter in temperate forests and has been identified as one of the highest accumulators of radiocesium among [[saprotrophic-fungi]].

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Mycenaceae
- **Genus:** *Mycena*
- **Species:** *M. polygramma*

## Key Facts

- Identified as having one of the highest rates of radiocesium uptake and incorporation among fungi tested in [[liquid-culture]] (Clint et al., 1991).
- Among three saprotrophic basidiomycetes (*M. polygramma*, *[[cystoderma-amianthinum]]*, *M. sanguinolenta*) with the highest radiocesium accumulation rates on a biomass basis.
- Despite being saprotrophic, showed higher accumulation rates than many ectomycorrhizal species in laboratory conditions.
- Uptake rates among fungal species varied between approximately 100 and 250 nmol/g dry weight per hour for radiocesium.
- Contributes to understanding of radionuclide cycling in forest ecosystems through [[saprotrophic-decomposition]] pathways.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

*Mycena polygramma* is significant as a model saprotrophic fungus for studying radionuclide dynamics in forest soils. Its high radiocesium accumulation rate challenges the assumption that only [[mycorrhizal-fungi]] are important vectors for radionuclide cycling. The species' saprotrophic lifestyle means it processes organic matter directly, potentially mobilizing radionuclides bound in decomposing litter and wood. Understanding its uptake mechanisms contributes to models of radionuclide movement in contaminated forest ecosystems.

## Ecological Role

- Saprotrophic decomposer of woody debris and leaf litter in temperate forests
- High-capacity radiocesium accumulator, contributing to radionuclide cycling
- Part of the fungal community responsible for organic matter turnover in forest floor
- Links decomposition processes to radionuclide mobilization in contaminated ecosystems
