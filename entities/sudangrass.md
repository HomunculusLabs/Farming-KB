# Sudangrass (Sorghum sudanense)

## Description

Sudangrass (*Sorghum sudanense*) is a C4 grass species widely used as the standard host plant for culturing arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) fungi in greenhouse pot cultures. It is the routine culture host at INVAM (International Culture Collection of Vesicular Arbuscular Mycorrhizal Fungi) due to its exceptional compatibility with the broadest range of AM fungal species of any tested host.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Family:** Poaceae
- **Genus:** *Sorghum*
- **Species:** *S. sudanense*
- **Common name:** Sudangrass
- **Photosynthetic pathway:** C4

## Key Facts

- More than 1,000 isolates representing 98 species across all genera of Glomales can grow and sporulate on Sudangrass (Morton et al. 1993)
- Roots of Sudangrass accommodate colonization by as many as 10 AM fungal species simultaneously in trap pot cultures using field soil as inoculum
- Identified as the most compatible and productive host for AM fungal culture, likely because C4 grasses produce abundant root systems and partition photosynthetic carbon efficiently
- At INVAM, Sudangrass is overseeded to produce shorter, more uniform plants and ensure widespread root infiltration throughout the pot
- Used in trap cultures to bait and induce sporulation of both sporulating and nonsporulating AM fungi from field-collected roots and soil
- Standard growth period is a minimum of 4 months under controlled greenhouse conditions

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Sudangrass is arguably the single most important tool for AM fungal cultivation and taxonomy. Its role includes:

- **Monospecific culture establishment:** Enables isolation of individual AM fungal species from mixed field communities
- **Inoculum multiplication:** Supports production of large volumes of clean inoculum for agricultural and ecological applications
- **Biodiversity surveys:** Used in trap cultures worldwide to recover AM fungal species not detectable by direct field spore extraction
- **Experimental microcosms:** Serves as host in pot-culture experiments examining fungal community dynamics, species interactions, and sporulation patterns

The choice of Sudangrass as a universal host has standardized AM fungal research methodology across laboratories globally, enabling meaningful comparisons of species composition and function across studies.
