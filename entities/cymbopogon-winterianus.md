## Overview

**Cymbopogon winterianus**, commonly known as **Java citronella**, is a tropical [[psilocybe-southeast-asia-pacific]].

## Taxonomy and Morphology

*Cymbopogon winterianus* was described by Jowitt in 1888, though its taxonomic history has been intertwined with *C. nardus* due to morphological similarity.

It belongs to the genus *Cymbopogon* (lemongrasses), subfamily Panicoideae, tribe Andropogoneae.

The plant forms dense tussocks 1–1.5 meters tall, with robust cane-like stems arising from a short rhizome.

Leaf blades are linear, 40–90 cm long and 1–2 cm wide, with a greyish-green color and slightly rough margins.

The leaves are highly aromatic when crushed, emitting a strong, sweet citrus scent distinct from the lemon aroma of lemongrass.

Inflorescence is a large, open panicle 30–60 cm long, with pairs of spikelets borne on slender pedicels.

The sessile spikelets contain a single floret with a callus of short, silky hairs at the base.

Rhizomes are short and thickened, producing dense clumps that can persist for 5–8 years under cultivation.

## Distribution and Habitat

*C. winterianus* is believed to be native to Southeast Asia, likely originating in Indonesia (Java and Sumatra), though wild populations are rarely distinguished from *C. nardus*.

The species is now cultivated throughout the tropics and subtropics, with major production in Indonesia, India, Brazil, China, and Vietnam.

It thrives in warm, humid climates with annual rainfall of 1,500–2,500 mm and mean temperatures of 25–35 °C.

Optimal growth occurs on well-drained sandy loam to loamy soils with pH 5.0–7.0, though the species tolerates moderately acidic conditions.

The plant is frost-sensitive and suffers damage below 10 °C, limiting cultivation to tropical and subtropical zones.

It performs best in full sun but tolerates partial shade, particularly during establishment.
