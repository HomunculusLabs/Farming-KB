# White Mulberry (Morus alba) — Permaculture Forage

## Description

White mulberry (Morus alba) is a fast-growing deciduous tree renowned in permaculture as one of the highest-value chicken forage plants. Bill Mollison rates it as "as good as a double crop of grain" at 17% protein content. The mulberry's extended fruiting season provides sustained forage for poultry well beyond the harvest period, as chickens continue finding dropped fruit and seed long after the main crop.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Family**: Moraceae
- **Genus**: Morus
- **Species**: M. alba
- **Type**: Deciduous fruit tree
- **Permaculture Role**: Premium poultry forage, multi-season food source

## Key Facts

- 17% protein content in fruit, comparable to grain crops.
- Mollison rates it as "as good as a double crop of grain" for poultry feed value.
- Chickens continue feeding on fallen fruit and seed long after the visible berry crop is gone.
- Can be propagated from large cuttings (up to 4 feet long, 1.5 inch diameter) that root readily.
- Fill an area with cuttings and achieve full mulberry production by the following year.
- The United States has excellent black mulberry varieties (M. nigra) as well.
- Two to three varieties extend the harvest season significantly.
- Part of an integrated chicken-orchard system including hazelnuts, chestnuts, and vine crops.
- Hens prefer mulberries to other forage when available.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Mycorrhizal Host**: Morus species form ectomycorrhizal associations, supporting soil fungal networks.
- **Leaf Litter**: Mulberry leaves decompose readily and contribute to mulch layers, supporting saprophytic fungal activity.
- **Integrated Systems**: In chicken-orchard systems, poultry manure creates nutrient cycling that benefits plants and soil microbiota.
- **Agroforestry**: Serves as an upper-canopy tree in food forest designs with poultry ranging beneath.
- **Compost Input**: Fallen fruit and leaf material provide carbon and nitrogen inputs for composting.
