## Overview

Brassica rapa is a diverse species in the family Brassicaceae encompassing
numerous economically important vegetable crops. It includes turnips (root
morphotype), bok choy and Chinese cabbage (leafy morphotypes), and rapeseed
(oilseed morphotype).

The species originated in the Mediterranean region and western Asia, with
secondary diversification in [[growing-root-vegetables-guide]] to [[canary-grass-phototropism-model-organism]] for studying plant domestication.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Brassica rapa L. (syn. Brassica campestris L.) belongs to the family
Brassicaceae, order Brassicales. The species includes several subspecies
and morphotype groups recognized by crop scientists.

The turnip subspecies (B. rapa subsp. rapa) produces an enlarged hypocotyl
root that is the harvested organ. Root shape ranges from flat to globular to
elongated, with skin colors of white, purple, red, or yellow.

Chinese cabbage (B. rapa subsp. pekinensis) forms an elongated, cylindrical
head of overlapping pale green leaves. It is one of the most widely consumed
vegetables in East Asia.

Bok choy (B. rapa subsp. chinensis) has smooth, dark green leaf blades on
thick, crisp white petioles arranged in a loose rosette rather than a tight
head.

Rapeseed types (B. rapa subsp. oleifera) are grown for their small, oily
seeds. Modern cultivars produce canola-quality oil low in erucic acid and
glucosinolates.

Plants are typically biennials requiring vernalization for flowering, though
annual forms exist. Basal leaves form a rosette, and the flowering stem
(bolting) produces yellow racemes.

The root system of turnip types consists of a prominent taproot with
secondary fibrous roots. Leafy types have shallower but more extensive
fibrous root systems.

Flowers are typical Brassica-type: four yellow petals in a cross arrangement,
six stamens (four long, two short), and a superior ovary with two carpels.

## Cultivation

Brassica rapa is a cool-season crop with optimal growth at 15–20°C. Most
morphotypes tolerate light frost, and some turnip varieties are winter-hardy
to -10°C.

Turnips mature rapidly, reaching harvestable size in 35–60 days from sowing.
This fast growth makes them valuable as catch crops and for [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]].

Chinese cabbage and bok choy require 50–80 days to maturity. These crops
are sensitive to bolting triggered by temperatures below 10°C during early
growth, which limits their production window [[query-how-to-grow-and-use-oca-in-temperate-climates]].

All morphotypes prefer fertile, well-drained loam soils with pH 6.0–7.5.
Consistent moisture is critical for preventing root cracking in turnips and
tipburn in leafy types.
