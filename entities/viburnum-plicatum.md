## Viburnum plicatum

## Overview

**Viburnum plicatum**, commonly known as Japanese snowball bush, is a deciduous shrub in the family Caprifoliaceae. It is native to Japan, Korea, and Taiwan, where it grows in mountainous forests and open woodlands.

The species is prized for its spectacular display of rounded white flower clusters that resemble snowballs in late spring. The layered branching habit gives it strong architectural presence in the landscape even when not in bloom.

Mature plants reach 2.5–4 meters in height and spread with a distinctly tiered, horizontal branching pattern. The specific epithet "plicatum" refers to the pleated or folded appearance of the leaves.

Two forms are widely grown: the sterile-flowered f. plicatum (Japanese snowball) and the fertile-flowered f. tomentosum (doublefile viburnum). The latter produces lacecap-style flower clusters with an outer ring of showy sterile florets surrounding fertile central flowers.

The species was introduced to Western horticulture by Philipp Franz von Siebold in the 1840s and quickly became a staple of Victorian shrub borders.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Family:** Caprifoliaceae
- **Genus:** Viburnum
- **Species:** Viburnum plicatum Thunb.
- **Synonyms:** Viburnum tomentosum Thunb.

Leaves are opposite, broadly ovate, 7–15 cm long, with deeply veined, pleated surfaces and serrated margins. They emerge bright green in spring, maintain good dark green color through summer, and develop reddish-purple to burgundy autumn tones.

Flowers of f. plicatum are all sterile, forming dense, rounded globes 5–8 cm across in pure white. Flowers of f. tomentosum form flat-topped lacecap cymes 8–15 cm wide, with a ring of large sterile florets surrounding small fertile flowers.

Fruit is an ovoid drupe, initially red, maturing to black, 6–8 mm long. Only f. tomentosum produces fruit reliably, as f. plicatum lacks fertile flowers. Fruit persists into early winter, providing visual interest and bird forage.

Stems are stout and develop a distinctive horizontal layering as the plant matures. Bark is grey-brown and relatively smooth on older branches, with prominent lenticels.

The root system is fibrous and relatively shallow, making transplanting relatively easy when plants are young. Mature specimens resent being moved and may sulk for a season after transplanting.

## Distribution and Habitat

Viburnum plicatum is native to Japan (Honshu, Shikoku, Kyushu), Korea, and Taiwan. It grows in deciduous and mixed forests at elevations of 100–1,500 meters, often along forest margins and stream banks.

In its native habitat, the species experiences warm, humid summers and cool winters with reliable snow cover. It is well adapted to the monsoon climate of East Asia, tolerating both heavy summer rainfall and seasonal dryness.

## Ecological Role

The fertile form (f. tomentosum) attracts a wide range of pollinators including small bees, hoverflies, and butterflies during its spring bloom period. The nectar-rich fertile flowers provide an important early food source.

Berries of f. tomentosum are consumed by robins, cedar waxwings, and other fruit-eating birds in autumn. The dense branching provides cover and nesting sites for small songbirds throughout the year.

The plant has no serious disease problems in cultivation. [[growing-gourmet-north-american-reishi-species]] gardens, it serves as a host plant for the spring azure butterfly (Celastrina ladon) in some regions.
