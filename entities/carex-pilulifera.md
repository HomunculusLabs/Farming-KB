## Overview

Carex pilulifera, commonly known as the pill sedge, is a perennial herbaceous sedge in the family Cyperaceae. It is native to Europe and western Asia, where it occurs in acidic heathlands, dry grasslands, and open woodland margins.

The species epithet pilulifera means "bearing pills" and refers to the distinctive globose female flower clusters. Pill sedge is a characteristic species of Nardus stricta grasslands and [[festuca-ovina]].

The species tolerates moderate grazing and benefits from periodic disturbance that prevents competitive dominance by coarser grasses. It declines under heavy fertilisation or cultivation.

Light availability is an important factor, with the species preferring open or partially shaded conditions. Complete canopy closure by trees or tall shrubs leads to gradual decline and local disappearance.

## Ecology

Pill sedge provides a modest forage resource for grazing mammals, though its palatability is low compared to grasses. The dense tussocks offer microhabitat for invertebrates.

The seeds are dispersed locally by wind and rain splash. The globose female spikes aid in short-distance seed distribution, though long-range dispersal is limited.

Carex pilulifera is a mycorrhizal associate, forming relationships with [[ground-beetles-carabidae-and-biological-pest-control]] and spiders.

The species is an indicator of relatively undisturbed acidic grassland and [[tropical-rainforest-seedling-mycorrhiza-ecological-restoration]] projects. It is not widely available in the commercial horticultural trade.

Propagation is primarily by seed, which requires a period of cold stratification to break dormancy. Seed germination rates are moderate under optimal conditions.

The species is valued in habitat restoration for its role in recreating species-rich acidic grassland communities. It establishes well on appropriately prepared substrates with low nutrient status.

Traditional uses are limited. Some sources note that sedges were historically used for thatching and basket-making, though Carex pilulifera is too small-statured to have been a significant resource for these purposes.

It has no known pharmacological properties and is not used in herbal medicine. The plant is generally considered of negligible economic importance.
