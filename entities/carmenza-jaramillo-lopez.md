## Carmenza Jaramillo López

## Description

Carmenza Jaramillo López is a Colombian mycologist and mushroom cultivation specialist who served as a mycological consultant for Sustainable Communities/ZERI-New Mexico (SCZ-NM). She conducted a groundbreaking project in Chinchiná, Colombia — a coffee-growing region — that demonstrated the viability of growing shiitake mushrooms on coffee waste substrates. Her work exemplifies the application of ZERI principles to create economic opportunities for impoverished farming communities through mushroom cultivation.

## Classification

- **Role:** Mycologist, mushroom cultivation specialist, ZERI consultant
- **Nationality:** Colombian
- **Affiliation:** Sustainable Communities/ZERI-New Mexico (SCZ-NM)
- **Field:** Mushroom cultivation, agricultural waste valorization, sustainable development
- **Project Location:** Chinchiná, Colombia

## Key Facts

- Served as one of the mycological consultants for SCZ-NM, providing expertise on mushroom cultivation using waste substrates.
- Conducted a mushroom cultivation project in Chinchiná, Colombia, located in a coffee-growing region where low-income peasant farmers face precarious livelihoods due to volatile coffee prices.
- Demonstrated that shiitake mushrooms can be successfully cultivated on coffee waste substrates, achieving a remarkable Biological Efficiency of 75%.
- Her project addressed the fundamental inefficiency of coffee production, where the final coffee beans represent only a small percentage of the total agricultural biomass, with huge amounts of coffee waste traditionally discarded.
- The Chinchiná project provided coffee growers with an additional source of income and nutrition, demonstrating mushroom cultivation as a tool for poverty reduction in agricultural communities.
- Her research was published alongside collaborators Rodríguez and V.S.T. Chang (Jaramillo et al., 2004), documenting the techniques and results of shiitake cultivation on coffee waste.
- Worked within the ZERI framework, applying the principle of transforming agricultural waste into a valuable resource — turning coffee processing byproducts into marketable shiitake mushrooms.
- Her collaboration with SCZ-NM connected Colombian agricultural innovation with U.S. Forest Service restoration programs, demonstrating the international applicability of integrated fungal cultivation systems.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Carmenza Jaramillo López"s work is highly relevant to mushroom cultivation, particularly in tropical and developing-world contexts:

1. **Coffee waste substrate** — Her demonstration that coffee waste can serve as an effective substrate for shiitake cultivation at 75% Biological Efficiency opens up a vast, previously underutilized substrate resource in coffee-growing regions worldwide.

2. **Economic diversification for farmers** — Her model provides coffee growers with a secondary income stream from the same land and labor base, reducing vulnerability to coffee price volatility while adding nutritional value.

3. **Poverty reduction through cultivation** — The Chinchiná project is a concrete example of how mushroom cultivation can serve as a development tool, providing income and nutrition for impoverished farming communities.

4. **Substrate innovation** — Her work expands the catalog of viable substrates for shiitake cultivation, providing cultivators with additional options beyond traditional hardwood substrates.

5. **ZERI implementation** — Her project serves as a model for implementing ZERI design principles in real-world agricultural settings, demonstrating how "waste" from one process becomes the input for a valuable new product.
