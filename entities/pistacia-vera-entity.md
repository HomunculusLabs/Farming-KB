# Pistacia vera
**Pistachio / Green Almond / Fistık**
- **Division:** Magnoliophyta
- **Class:** Magnoliopsida
- **Order:** Sapindales
- **Family:** Anacardiaceae
- **Authority:** L. (1753)
- **Chromosome number:** 2n = 30

## Overview
Pistacia vera is a small deciduous tree in the cashew family (Anacardiaceae)
cultivated for its edible seeds — the pistachio nut — one of the world's
most valuable nut crops. Native to Central Asia and the Middle East
(Iran, Turkmenistan, northern Afghanistan), pistachio has been cultivated
for over 3,000 years. In permaculture and [[agroforestry]] systems.
Productive lifespan exceeds 100 years in suitable conditions.

## Morphology
- **Habit:** Deciduous tree, 5–8 m in cultivation (to 12 m wild)
- **Trunk:** Short, gnarled, gray-brown rough bark
- **Root system:** Deep taproot (3–5 m+) with extensive laterals;
  confers exceptional [[dighton-fungal-drought-tolerance-plant-water-relations]] and [[permplant-willows-salix-erosion-control-fodder]]
- **Leaves:** Pinnately compound, 10–20 cm, 3–5 pairs of glossy
  leathery leaflets; new foliage bronze-tinted, maturing deep green
- **Flowers:** Dioecious (separate male/female trees); wind-pollinated,
  apetalous; male catkins 3–6 cm, brownish; flowering March–April
- **Fruit:** Drupe, 1.5–2.5 cm; green hull turning reddish-yellow at
  maturity; hard cream shell naturally splits to reveal pale green
  kernel with reddish-violet seed coat

## Habitat and Distribution
- **Native range:** Central Asia — eastern Iran, Turkmenistan,
  northern Afghanistan; wild stands in Kopet-Dag mountains
- **Major producers:** Iran (~55% world production), USA/California
  (~27%), Turkey (~8%), China, Syria, Italy/Sicily, Australia
- **[[mollison-climate-zones-permaculture-strategies]]:** Mediterranean, semi-arid, continental dry;
  USDA zones 7–10
- **Temperature:** Hot summers (30–38°C optimal); 800–1000 chilling
  hours required; winter cold below -20°C damages trees
- **Water:** 200–400 mm annual rainfall sufficient; one of the most
  drought-tolerant nut crops
- **Soil:** Well-drained sandy-loam, pH 6.5–8.0; tolerant of alkaline,
  calcareous, and moderately saline soils; intolerant of waterlogging

## Cultivation
### Planting and Establishment
- **Pollination:** Male-female ratio 1:8 to 1:12 required; males
  planted in grid pattern throughout orchard
- **Spacing:** 6–8 m × 6–8 m (150–250 trees/ha)
- **Rootstock:** P. vera seedlings standard; P. atlantica rootstock
  improves drought/salinity tolerance
- **First harvest:** 4–5 years small crop; 7–8 years commercial;
  12–15 years peak production

### Orchard Management
- **Training:** Modified [[comparison-central-leader-vs-open-center-fruit-tree-training]] or open vase; annual pruning
- **Irrigation:** Drip standard; deficit irrigation reduces water 30–40%
  with minimal yield loss
- **Fertilization:** Low-moderate N; K and P important for nut fill;
  B and Zn supplementation in calcareous soils
- **Pests:** Navel orangeworm, hemispheric scale, Botryosphaeria blight,
  Verticillium wilt; IPM approaches increasingly standard
- **Yield:** 2–5 kg/tree/year at maturity; 2,500–5,000 kg/ha
  (California); pronounced alternate bearing

## Nutritional Profile
- **Protein:** ~20% (higher than almonds, cashews)
- **Fat:** ~45% (55% oleic acid, 30% linoleic acid)
- **Fiber:** ~10%
- **Key micronutrients:** Vitamin B6 (25% DV/28g), thiamine, copper,
  manganese, phosphorus, potassium (~1025 mg/100g), vitamin K
- **Antioxidants:** γ-Tocopherol, lutein, zeaxanthin, anthocyanins;
  highest antioxidant capacity among tree nuts
- **Unique:** High phytosterols (β-sitosterol) and arginine for
  cardiovascular health

## Propagation and Processing
### Propagation Methods
- **Seed:** Cold stratification 2–3 months; seed-grown trees variable,
  used primarily for rootstock production
- **Grafting:** T-bud or chip bud onto seedling rootstock; standard for
  commercial cultivars; scions taken late winter, grafted spring
- **Cuttings:** Semi-hardwood under mist with IBA; low success (~20–30%)

### Harvest and Post-Harvest
- **Harvest:** Mechanical shaking or hand-pick when hulls split
  (August–September); timing critical for aflatoxin prevention
- **Processing:** Hull within 24 hours to prevent staining and mold;
  dry to 5–7% moisture; sort by size/grade; roast and salt most common
- **Storage:** In-shell 1–2 years at 0–4°C, <65% RH; shelled kernels
  require refrigeration or nitrogen flushing

## Permaculture Applications
- **Food forest role:** Mid-canopy tree in Mediterranean/dryland
  systems; dappled shade for understory herbs and N-fixing shrubs. See [[drought-tolerant-planting-design]] for companion strategies.
- **Guild companions:** Alfalfa, clover, comfrey, rosemary, lavender,
  fig, pomegranate, olive — shared drought/heat tolerance
- **Soil ecology:** Forms vesicular-arbuscular mycorrhizal (VAM)
  associations; facilitates [[comparison-nutrient-cycling-vs-nutrient-dense-gardening]] in poor soils
- **Erosion control:** Deep roots stabilize slopes in arid landscapes
- **Windbreak:** Mid-layer component in multi-strata shelterbelts
- **Biodiversity:** Early pollen for insects; nuts for birds/wildlife;
  canopy habitat for invertebrates
