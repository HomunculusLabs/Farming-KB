# Leucaena leucocephala (*Lead Tree*)

## Overview

*Leucaena leucocephala* is a fast-growing, nitrogen-fixing leguminous tree in
the family Fabaceae, native to [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-central-america-caribbean]] and southern Mexico.

Widely recognized as one of the most versatile multipurpose agroforestry trees
in the tropics, it provides high-protein forage, fuelwood, timber, [[coleman-mobile-greenhouse-green-manure-soil-building]],
and [[permplant-willows-salix-erosion-control-fodder]].

Its rapid [[soil-temperature-plant-growth-rate-doubling-rule]] — up to 3 m per year under optimal conditions — and
ability to fix atmospheric nitrogen make it a cornerstone species in tropical
permaculture and [[biochar]] systems.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Order:** Fabales
- **Family:** Fabaceae
- **Subfamily:** Mimosoideae
- **Genus:** Leucaena
- **Species:** L. leucocephala (Lam.) de Wit

**Synonyms:** *Acacia leucocephala* (Lam.) Link, *Mimosa leucocephala* Lam.,
*Leucaena glauca* (L.) Benth.

**Etymology:** *Leucaena* derives from Greek *leukos* (white), referring to
the white flowers. *Leucocephala* combines *leukos* (white) and *kephale*
(head), describing the white, globular flower heads.

## Morphology

Leucaena is a small to medium-sized tree with a spreading, often multi-stemmed
canopy.

- **Height:** 5–20 m depending on ecotype and conditions; giant types reach
  20 m, shrubby types remain under 5 m.
- **Leaves:** Bipinnately compound, 15–25 cm long, with 4–9 pairs of pinnae,
  each bearing 10–25 pairs of small leaflets (3–5 mm each). Leaves fold at
  night (nyctinasty).
- **Flowers:** White, globular heads 1–2 cm diameter, borne on peduncles 2–4
  cm long in axillary clusters. Self-compatible, primarily outcrossing.
- **Fruit:** Flat, linear pods 10–20 cm × 1.5–2 cm, brown at maturity,
  containing 15–30 seeds per pod.
- **Seeds:** Brown, glossy, ovoid, 6–8 mm long, with a hard seed coat
  requiring scarification for germination.
- **Root system:** Deep taproot (up to 5 m) with extensive lateral roots and
  prolific nodulation by *Rhizobium* strains.

## Cultivation

Leucaena is exceptionally easy to establish and manage, tolerating a wide range
of tropical conditions.

- **Propagation:** Seed (scarify with hot water or acid), [[comparison-seed-starting-indoors-vs-direct-seeding-techniques]] or
  nursery transplant. Also propagated by stem cuttings.
- **Climate:** Tropical and subtropical; optimal growth at 25–30°C annual
  mean. Intolerant of frost; damaged below 5°C.
- **Rainfall:** 500–3,000 mm annual; most productive above 1,000 mm.
- **Soil:** Adapted to neutral to alkaline soils (pH 6.0–8.0); intolerant of
  waterlogging. Tolerates moderate salinity.
- **Growth rate:** Exceptionally fast; 3+ m/year height gain in favorable
  conditions. Can reach harvestable size in 3–5 years for fuelwood.
- **[[comparison-biological-nitrogen-fixation-vs-synthetic-fertilizer]]:** Fixes 100–500 kg N/ha/year, depending on stand
  density and rhizobial effectiveness.
- **Yield:** Leaf yield 5–15 t DM/ha/year; wood yield 10–30 m³/ha/year.
- **Pests:** Susceptible to psyllid (*Heteropsylla cubana*) — a major
  pest causing defoliation since its spread from [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-caribbean-central-america]] in the 1980s.
