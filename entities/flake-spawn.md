## Description

Flake spawn is a traditional form of mushroom inoculum consisting of thin, flattened pieces of substratum—typically dried manure-based material—that have been colonized by the [[agaricus-campestris]]* (syn. *A. bisporus*). Unlike the more compact [[mushroom-spawn]] / inoculum
- **Form:** Loose flakes or thin compressed pieces of colonized substratum
- **Substratum:** Dried, fermented manure-based material
- **Inoculant:** Pure culture mycelium of *Agaricus campestris*

## Key Facts

- Flake spawn was historically the older form of commercial spawn, predating brick spawn in common usage.
- Duggar (1915) specified that when using flake spawn, an amount approximately half the size of one's fist should be inserted at each spawning point—larger than the hen's-egg-sized pieces used for brick spawn.
- The larger piece size for flake spawn compensates for its lower density and looser structure compared to compressed brick spawn.
- Flake spawn distributes mycelium somewhat differently than brick spawn, with more surface area in contact with the surrounding compost.
- Like brick spawn, flake spawn must be inserted beneath the compost surface and covered to maintain proper moisture and temperature conditions for mycelial growth.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Historical cultivation method:** Flake spawn represents an earlier approach to mushroom inoculum production and distribution, reflecting the evolution of spawn technology in commercial cultivation.
- **Regional preferences:** Flake spawn was historically more common in certain European mushroom-growing traditions, while brick spawn became more standardized in English and American practice.
- **Inoculation density:** The larger quantity of flake spawn required per insertion point (compared to brick spawn) means that a given volume of flake spawn covers less bed area than the same volume of brick spawn.
- **Mycelial distribution:** The flaky structure can promote rapid mycelial spread through the compost due to the greater surface area of contact, though this advantage is offset by the lower density of viable mycelium per unit volume.
- **Storage and handling:** Flake spawn requires careful storage to prevent breakage and excessive drying, as the thinner pieces are more susceptible to moisture loss than compressed bricks.

## Historical Context

Flake spawn was among the earliest standardized forms of mushroom inoculum, used by European mushroom growers before the development and widespread adoption of brick spawn. Duggar's 1915 *Mushroom Growing* describes both spawn types as commercially available options, reflecting a transitional period in spawn technology. The subsequent development of grain spawn in the mid-20th century would eventually replace both flake and brick spawn in most commercial operations, offering faster colonization and more reliable results.

## Cautions

- Flake spawn is more susceptible to drying during storage than brick spawn and should be kept in sealed, moisture-controlled conditions.
- The loose structure makes flake spawn more vulnerable to contamination if packaging is compromised.
