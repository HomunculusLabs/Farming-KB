## Description

The [[permaculture-plant-propagation-guide]].
- **Nucleus expansion strategy**: The nest acts as a nutrient nucleus from which vegetation expands outward, mirroring the permaculture approach of starting with small nuclei and gradually expanding the perimeter.
- **Desert reforestation model**: Mollison cites the mallee fowl's compost nest as inspiration for human desert reforestation strategies using buried organic matter, manure, and wood chips in contour swales.
- **Mycelial web development**: The decomposing mound creates ideal conditions for fungal networks, demonstrating the importance of mycelial webs in supporting tree establishment in arid reforestation.

## Application to Desert Reforestation

Mollison proposes a direct application of the mallee fowl model for creating forests in drylands:

- Grade wide hollows (5-10 m) on contour from outwash runnels, leaving 30-100 m bare strips between swales.
- At 10-20 m intervals in swales, bury truckloads of mixed chips, logs, manure, and straw under 1.0-1.5 m of sand.
- Wait for rain, then plant pioneer legume and nurse crop trees into the buried organic matter.
- The deep mulch holds water at tree roots, while the forest soil develops mycelial web for roots.
- Inter-swale desert runoff should supply each swale with an effective 50-300 cm of water per year.

## Conservation Status

The mallee fowl is listed as vulnerable in several Australian states due to habitat loss from land clearing, predation by introduced foxes and cats, and altered fire regimes that disrupt recruitment of new generations. The species' reliance on specific conditions for mound construction makes it particularly sensitive to landscape changes. Its decline serves as an indicator of broader arid ecosystem degradation.
