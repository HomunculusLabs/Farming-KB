## Overview

**2C-T-2** (2,5-dimethoxy-4-ethylthiophenethylamine) is a synthetic **psychedelic phenethylamine** of the 2C family. It was first synthesized by [[alexander-shulgin]] and documented in PIHKAL (Phenethylamines I Have Known and Loved), published in 1991.

The compound produces visual and auditory hallucinations, altered perception of time, and emotional effects characteristic of classical psychedelics. Its pharmacological profile is similar to other 2C compounds, acting primarily as a **5-HT2A receptor agonist**.

2C-T-2 gained popularity as a "research chemical" in the early 2000s and was subsequently scheduled in several countries. It is a **thio** analog of 2C-E, with an ethylthio substituent at the 4-position of the phenyl ring.

## Chemical Properties

**Chemical name:** 2,5-dimethoxy-4-(ethylthio)phenethylamine

**Molecular formula:** C12H19NO2S

**Molecular weight:** 241.34 g/mol

**IUPAC name:** 2-(2,5-dimethoxy-4-(ethylthio)phenyl)ethan-1-amine

**SMILES:** CCSC1=C(C=C(C=C1OCC)OC)CCN

**Appearance:** White crystalline powder or fine crystals. Freebase is an oily liquid at room temperature. Typically encountered as the hydrochloride salt, which is stable and water-soluble.

**Solubility:** Hydrochloride salt is freely soluble in water, ethanol, and DMSO. The freebase is soluble in organic solvents including chloroform and diethyl ether.

**Storage:** Stable at room temperature when protected from light and moisture. No known decomposition products under normal [[vegetable-storage-conditions-by-temperature-and-humidity]].

## Pharmacology

2C-T-2 acts as a **partial agonist** at the **5-HT2A [[serotonin-receptor-interference-psychedelic-mechanism]]**, the primary receptor responsible for classical psychedelic effects. Binding affinity is comparable to other members of the 2C family, with reported Ki values in the low nanomolar range.

The compound also shows activity at **5-HT2C** and **5-HT1A** receptors, which may contribute to its overall effect profile. Limited data suggest weak activity at α2-adrenergic receptors.

The ethylthio substituent at the 4-position increases lipophilicity compared to 2C-B (which has a bromine at this position), potentially affecting blood-brain barrier penetration and duration of action.

## Effects

**Dosage range:** 10–25 mg orally. A threshold dose is approximately 5 mg. Doses above 30 mg significantly increase risk of unpleasant physical side effects.

**Duration:** 4–8 hours, with onset at 30–60 minutes, peak effects at 2–3 hours, and gradual comedown over 2–4 hours.

**Subjective effects include:**

- **Visual:** Color enhancement, geometric patterning, tracers, synesthesia at higher doses.
- **Cognitive:** Altered thought patterns, enhanced introspection, time distortion.
- **Emotional:** Euphoria, emotional openness, occasional anxiety at higher doses.
- **Physical:** Mild stimulation, pupil dilation, increased body temperature.

## Synthesis

2C-T-2 is synthesized from **2,5-dimethoxybenzaldehyde** via a sequence involving nitrostyrene formation (Henry reaction), reduction to the phenethylamine, and introduction of the ethylthio group.

The ethylthio group is typically introduced at the **asarylaldehyde** stage before nitrostyrene formation, using ethanethiol in the presence of a Lewis acid. Alternative routes introduce the thioether after amine formation via electrophilic aromatic substitution.

The synthesis requires intermediate-level [[pericyclic-reactions-organic-chemistry]] skills and access to controlled precursors. Shulgin's original route is documented in PIHKAL with full experimental details.

## Legal Status

2C-T-2 is controlled in many jurisdictions. In the [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]], it is a **Schedule I** substance under the Federal Analog Act due to its structural similarity to 2C-B. It was specifically listed as a Schedule I drug in 2004.

Internationally, 2C-T-2 is controlled in the **United Kingdom** (Class A), **Germany** (BtMG Anlage I), **Canada** (Schedule III), **Australia** (Schedule 9), and most EU member states following EMCDDA recommendations.

In countries where it remains unscheduled, it may be prosecuted under analog acts or general drug legislation targeting [[cultural-evolution-and-psychoactive-substances]].

## History and Cultural Context

Alexander and Ann Shulgin first synthesized and tested 2C-T-2 in the 1970s. It was assigned entry #33 in PIHKAL, where Shulgin described moderate doses as producing "color enhancement and a pleasant light-headed feeling."

The compound remained obscure until the early 2000s, when online vendors began offering it as a "research chemical" following the scheduling of 2C-B. It appeared in recreational contexts at music festivals and underground psychedelic gatherings.

Several adverse incidents were reported in the mid-2000s, including hospitalizations involving severe agitation and cardiovascular symptoms. These cases contributed to emergency scheduling in multiple countries.
