Carex pulicaris belongs to Carex section Paludosae, a group of small, tufted sedges adapted to acidic mire conditions. The species typically flowers from April through June.

Despite its small stature, Carex pulicaris is an ecologically important component of acidic mire communities across much of its European range.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Carex pulicaris** L. was described by Linnaeus in 1753 in *Species Plantarum*.

It is a small rhizomatous perennial forming dense tufts, typically 10 to 30 cm tall. The species is among the smallest Carex species in Europe, giving it a delicate appearance.

Culms are slender, trigonous, and smooth. They are shorter than or equal in length to the leaves, contributing to the compact growth form.

Leaf blades are 1 to 2 mm wide, channelled, and often curved or arching at the tips. The leaves are a yellowish-green to dark green color.

The inflorescence consists of 2 to 5 small, dense female spikes clustered near the apex of the culm, plus a terminal male spike. The female spikes are ovoid, 4 to 8 mm long, and dark brown to blackish at maturity.

This dark coloration and dense clustering of the female spikes gives the inflorescence its flea-like appearance. The contrast with the green foliage makes flowering plants conspicuous.

Glumes are dark brown to blackish, ovate, and shorter than the utricles. Utricles are 2.5 to 3.5 mm long, broadly ovate, planoconvex, and dark brown with a pale beak.

The utricle beak is short and bidentate. Stigmas are two per flower.

Nutlets are biconvex, approximately 1.5 mm long, and dark brown at maturity. The small seed size may limit seedling establishment in competitive mire environments.

Vegetative spread through rhizome extension is the primary means of local colonization. Seed production supplements vegetative spread and enables longer-distance dispersal.

The rhizome network is dense and mat-forming, creating a compact root system adapted to thin peat layers. This growth form helps anchor the plant in waterlogged, unstable substrates.

Establishment from seed is likely episodic, occurring during years when water table conditions are favorable. Seed banks in peat may provide a buffer against local population extinction.

## Distribution and Habitat

Carex pulicaris is distributed across much of Europe, from Scandinavia and the British Isles south to the Iberian Peninsula and eastward to western Siberia.

The species occurs in strongly acidic wetland habitats including ombrotrophic bogs, wet heaths, peaty hollows, and damp moorland margins. It is also found in acid flushes on upland slopes.

It favors waterlogged, nutrient-poor peat soils with a well-developed Sphagnum moss layer. The species is intolerant of base-rich or nutrient-enriched conditions.

In the British Isles, Carex pulicaris is widespread but local in distribution. It is most common in the uplands of northern and western Britain, occurring in blanket bogs and wet heath.

The species becomes increasingly scarce in lowland England and is absent from much of intensively farmed eastern England. Drainage and agricultural improvement have eliminated many lowland populations.

Elevational range extends from near sea level in oceanic regions to approximately 1000 m in continental mountain areas. It is most abundant at low to moderate elevations in western oceanic regions.

## Ecology and Ecological Role

Carex pulicaris is a characteristic species of acidic mire and wet heath plant communities. It is often found in association with Sphagnum mosses, occupying the wetter parts of the mire microtopography.

## Conservation and Threats

Carex pulicaris is assessed as Least Concern at the European level but has experienced significant regional declines. Local extinctions have occurred in intensively drained lowland areas.
