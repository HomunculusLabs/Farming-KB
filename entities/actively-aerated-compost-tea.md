## Overview

**Actively Aerated Compost Tea (AACT)** is a liquid biological amendment produced by extracting and multiplying beneficial microorganisms from high-quality compost in oxygenated water. AACT is the central subject of Dr. Elaine Ingham's *The Field Guide for Actively Aerated Compost Tea* (2004).

## Definition and Classification

AACT is defined by **active aeration** during brewing. Ingham's taxonomy distinguishes:

- **AACT** — Continuously aerated, dissolved oxygen above 6 ppm, promoting aerobic organisms
- **FCT (Fermentative Compost Tea)** — No aeration, fermentative organisms dominate
- **LBCT (Long-Brewing Compost Tea)** — Extended brewing, often partially anaerobic
- **NACT (Not-Aerobic Compost Tea)** — No aeration mechanism

AACT is distinct from compost extract, compost leachate, plant tea, and manure tea.

## Brewing Process

Critical parameters: quality compost, dechlorinated water, microbial food sources (molasses, kelp, fish hydrolysate), continuous aeration (>6 ppm dissolved oxygen), temperature 15-24 degrees C, duration 18-36 hours, clean food-grade equipment.

## Mechanisms of Impact

### Disease Suppression
- Outcompeting disease organisms for niches on leaf surfaces and root zones
- Predatory protozoa and beneficial fungi consuming pathogens
- Natural antibiotic production by beneficial microorganisms

### Nutrient Cycling
- Retaining nutrients in microbial biomass preventing leaching
- Converting nutrients to plant-available forms at rates matching plant demand
- Mineralization of organic matter

### Soil Structure
- Bacterial slime and fungal hyphae bind soil particles into aggregates
- Improved porosity, water infiltration, and root penetration

### Toxin Decomposition
- Certain microorganisms break down organic contaminants and pesticide residues

## Application Methods

Soil drench, foliar spray, seed treatment, transplant dip. Rates vary by industry: agriculture 10-20 gal/acre soil; 5-10 gal/acre foliar.

## Food Web Assessment

Direct microscopy assesses compost tea quality by identifying and counting organisms, assessing bacterial vs. fungal biomass balance, and screening for pathogens. This contrasts with plate counts which significantly underestimate microbial diversity.

## Relevance to Mycology

- **Fungal-dominant teas** brewed with appropriate food sources favor fungal growth
- AACT supplements mycorrhizal inoculation by providing supportive food web context
- Beneficial organisms suppress competitor molds on mushroom substrates
- Foliar applications prevent fungal and bacterial diseases without synthetic chemicals

## Cautions

- Pathogen risk with improperly brewed tea
- Anaerobic conditions promote harmful organisms
- Must be used within hours of brewing completion
- Starting compost quality is the primary determinant of tea quality
