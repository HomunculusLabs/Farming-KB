# Octotemnus glabriculus

## Description

Octotemnus glabriculus is a small ciid beetle (family Ciidae) that specializes in feeding on the fruiting bodies of wood-decomposing [[polypore]] fungi. This species is one of the most intensively studied fungivorous beetles in the context of fungal–insect interactions. Research by Guevara et al. (2000) demonstrated that Octotemnus glabriculus can significantly reduce the reproductive output (fecundity) of the wood-rotting fungus [[coriolus-versicolor]] (now Trametes versicolor), with reductions of up to 58% in reproductive potential. This finding challenged the prevailing assumption that invertebrate grazing on fruiting bodies was inconsequential to fungal fitness.





## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Animalia
- **Phylum**: Arthropoda
- **Class**: Insecta
- **Order**: Coleoptera
- **Family**: Ciidae
- **Genus**: Octotemnus
- **Species**: O. glabriculus





## Key Facts

- A minute ciid beetle (typically 1–3 mm in length) that feeds within and on the fruiting bodies of polypore bracket fungi.
- Specializes in colonizing the fruiting bodies of Coriolus versicolor (Trametes versicolor), a common white-rot wood-decomposing basidiomycete.
- Reduced the reproductive potential of Coriolus versicolor by 58% in studies by Guevara et al. (2000) — one of the highest documented impacts of any fungivorous insect on fungal fecundity.
- Co-occurs with another ciid beetle, Cis boleti, which also feeds on Coriolus versicolor but causes a comparatively lower reduction in fecundity (30%).
- The impact of these beetles on fungal reproduction may be ecologically significant, potentially reducing the colonization potential of affected fungal species in the environment.
- Ciid beetles in general are among the most specialized fungivorous insects, with larval development occurring entirely within the fruiting body of the host fungus.
- The larvae tunnel through and consume the trmal tissue of bracket fungi, degrading spore-producing structures.





## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- Serves as a key example of how even small invertebrate grazers can significantly impact fungal reproduction, challenging assumptions about the insignificance of fruiting-body grazing.
- Relevant to [[outdoor-mushroom-cultivation]] and forest-based cultivation systems where bracket fungi and polypores are part of the ecosystem.
- Demonstrates that fungal–insect interactions on fruiting bodies can reduce spore output and thereby affect the dispersal and colonization success of wood-decomposing fungi.
- Understanding ciid beetle ecology is relevant for managing insect pests in log-based [[mushroom-cultivation]] (e.g., shiitake on logs).
- The species illustrates the concept that fungi–insect associations can range from commensalistic to strongly antagonistic, depending on the intensity of grazing and the specific biology of the interaction.





## Ecological Impact on Fungal Reproduction

| Beetle Species | Reduction in Fungal Fecundity |
|---------------|-------------------------------|
| Octotemnus glabriculus | 58% |
| Cis boleti | 30% |

The combined impact of both species on Coriolus versicolor suggests that ciid beetles as a guild can substantially reduce the reproductive fitness of their host fungi, with potential consequences for fungal population dynamics and wood decomposition processes in forest ecosystems.





## Ecological Interactions

- **Coriolus versicolor** (Trametes versicolor): Primary host fungus; beetles feed on and develop within fruiting bodies, significantly reducing spore production.
- **Cis boleti**: Sympatric ciid beetle species sharing the same fungal host; both species contribute to reduced fungal fecundity.
- **Wood decomposition**: By reducing the fecundity and colonization potential of white-rot fungi, these beetles may indirectly influence the rate of wood decomposition in forest ecosystems.
