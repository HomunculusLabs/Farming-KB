# Mycena cyanorrhiza
**Mycena cyanorrhiza** is a small, distinctive mushroom in the family
Mycenaceae confirmed to contain [[psilocin]]. It is
notable among the genus Mycena for its pronounced bluing reaction when
bruised, making it one of the more easily recognized psychoactive members of
this large and diverse genus. The species was first described from North America
and has since been documented in other temperate regions, representing one of
the more accessible examples of psilocybin production outside the traditional
psychoactive genera.
## Taxonomy and Classification
Mycena cyanorrhiza is classified in the order Agaricales, family
Mycenaceae, genus Mycena. The genus contains over 500 described species
and is one of the most species-rich genera of agaricoid fungi, found in
virtually every terrestrial habitat worldwide. Most Mycena species are small,
saprotrophic mushrooms that decompose leaf litter, wood, or other plant debris.
The discovery of psilocybin in certain Mycena species was significant
because it expanded the known taxonomic distribution of psychoactive fungi
beyond Psilocybe, Panaeolus, Gymnopilus, Conocybe, Pluteus,
and Inocybe.
The phylogenetic implications are particularly interesting because Mycena is
not closely related to other psilocybin-producing genera, suggesting either
independent evolution of the biosynthetic pathway or ancient horizontal gene
transfer. The presence of psilocybin in distantly related lineages supports
the hypothesis that the psilocybin biosynthetic gene cluster may have been lost
in many lineages and retained only in scattered groups across the Agaricales.
## Morphology
### Cap (Pileus)
The cap is small, ranging from 5 to 20 mm in diameter, among the smallest of
commonly encountered psilocybin-containing mushrooms. It is campanulate to
conical when young, becoming broadly convex to slightly umbonate with age while
often retaining a small central papilla. The cap surface is smooth, distinctly
hygrophanous, and usually striate especially near the margin when moist.
Coloration is typically brownish to reddish-brown when moist, fading to a
lighter tan or grayish-buff as it dries. The center is often darker than the
margins. The flesh is thin, fragile, and watery.
### Gills (Lamellae)
The gills are adnate to slightly decurrent, well-spaced to subdistant, and
narrow — typically 12 to 18 reach the stipe, with 1 to 3 tiers of lamellulae
between them. They are initially white to pale gray, developing a pinkish to
salmon-buff color as spores mature. The gills may exhibit bluish
discoloration when bruised, though this reaction is often more prominent on the
stipe. The gill edges are typically even and paler than the gill faces.
### Stipe (Stem)
The stipe is the most diagnostically useful feature and the primary field
identification character. It is central, slender, fragile, hollow, ranging from
20 to 60 mm in length and 1 to 2 mm in thickness. The stipe is typically
covered with a dense, conspicuous coating of white, downy to cottony hairs
(tomentum), particularly in the lower half — unusual in the genus. The base is
often covered with long, white, coarse rhizomorphic strands that extend into
the substrate.
When cut, handled, or bruised, the stipe develops an intense blue to
blue-green discoloration among the most dramatic bluing reactions observed in
any psilocybin-containing mushroom, including members of Psilocybe. This
reaction typically develops within seconds and is initially vivid blue, deepening
to blue-green or teal over several minutes. The intensity suggests relatively
high [[psilocybin]] and
[[psilocybe-azurescens]]
but comparable to moderate species like [[mycena-purpureofusca]], [[mycena-citrinomarginata]] (disputed). Variable reports may reflect genuine
variation, methodological differences, or cryptic species complexes.
## Similar Species
The key distinguishing feature is the intense blue bruising on the stipe,
unusual in the genus. [[mycena-galopus]] has
characteristic milky latex when cut. The white, downy stipe base with
rhizomorphic strands is a useful macroscopic characteristic for narrowing
identification.
## Cultivation
M. cyanorrhiza has not been successfully cultivated. Its specific
environmental requirements, combined with small size and moderate potency,
have discouraged cultivation efforts.
## Legal Status
As a confirmed psilocybin-containing species, M. cyanorrhiza is subject to
legal controls in many jurisdictions. The species is so small and inconspicuous
that identification and enforcement would be extremely difficult in practice.
