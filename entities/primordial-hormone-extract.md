## Primordial Hormone Extract

## Description

Primordial hormone extract is a concentrated biological preparation derived from mushroom primordia (the earliest visible stage of fruitbody formation) that contains the naturally occurring hormones mushrooms produce to orchestrate their fruiting cycles. When applied as a mist or liquid fertilizer to colonized mushroom biomass, these hormones can help induce fruiting, control the location and timing of fruitbody formation, and focus primordia development at specific sites. The technique was developed and described by Tradd Cotter as a method for controlling mushroom production in cultivation settings.

## Classification

- **Category**: Cultivation Technique / Biological Preparation
- **Source material**: Mushroom primordia
- **Active components**: Fruiting hormones concentrated in primordial tissue
- **Application method**: Mist or liquid application to colonized biomass

## Key Facts

- Mushrooms, like plants, produce hormones that orchestrate their fruiting cycles, and these hormones are concentrated in the primordia.
- The extract can be applied as a mist or liquid fertilizer to a colonized biomass to help induce fruiting.
- It can be used to control both the location and timing of fruiting, focusing primordia development at sites where cultivators want the fungi to fruit.
- The treatment can be used to induce fruiting to complement production cycles.
- Premature induction before the myceliated biomass has charged up enough nutrients will result in reduced yields (Mau and Beelman, 1996).
- Tiger sawgill (Lentinus tigrinus) primordia have been shown to initiate on agar at loci of hormonal stimulus, demonstrating site-specific fruitbody formation.

## Preparation Protocol

The preparation of primordial hormone extract follows a specific laboratory procedure:

1. **Harvest primordia**: Collect a few primordia from cultivated mushrooms, cleaning away any substrate debris.
2. **Surface sterilize**: Swirl the primordia gently in ethanol for 30 seconds, then rinse with distilled water; repeat three times.
3. **Blend**: Process the surface-sterilized primordia with water in a sterilized blender to create a slurry.
4. **Vacuum filter**: Remove particulates from the slurry by vacuum filtration.
5. **Centrifuge**: Transfer the filtered slurry to a centrifuge and spin at 10,000 x g for 5 minutes.
6. **Collect supernatant**: Withdraw the supernatant into a sterile syringe.
7. **Sterilize**: Fit the syringe with a syringe filter to sterilize the fluid.
8. **Store**: Collect the sterilized fluid in a sterile vial.

## Relevance to Cultivation

Primordial hormone extract offers cultivators a biological tool for managing fruiting:

- **Site-specific fruiting**: By applying the extract to specific locations on the colonized substrate, cultivators can control where mushrooms form, potentially allowing for organized patterns across the casing surface.
- **Timing control**: The extract can be used to synchronize fruiting across a crop, helping to coordinate harvest timing.
- **Production optimization**: Can be used to induce fruiting to complement existing production cycles.
- **Species-specific application**: The dilution rate must be determined experimentally for each species, based on the volume of primordia used to make the extract.

## Limitations

- Requires a supply of primordia from the target species, creating a chicken-and-egg problem for first-time cultivators.
- The volume of extract produced is very small compared to the starting material.
- Nutrient charging time cannot be skipped; premature induction reduces yields.
- Rate of application must be determined through experimentation for each species.
- Requires basic laboratory equipment (centrifuge, syringe filters, vacuum filtration setup).

## Research Context

The study of fungal fruiting hormones parallels research in plant hormone biology, where hormones such as auxins, gibberellins, and cytokinins orchestrate developmental transitions. In fungi, the hormonal signals that trigger primordia formation and fruitbody development are concentrated in the earliest fruiting structures, making primordia the ideal source material for extraction. The ability to harvest and concentrate these signals opens possibilities for precise control over mushroom production cycles.
