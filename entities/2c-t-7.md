
# 2C-T-7 (2,5-Dimethoxy-4-propylthiophenethylamine)

## Broader 2C-T Series Context

The 2C-T series represents Shulgin's exploration of thioether substitution at the 4-position of the
2,5-dimethoxyphenethylamine scaffold. Other members include 2C-T-2 (methylthio), 2C-T-4 (ethylthio), 2C-T-21
(isopropylthio), and compounds with longer or branched alkyl chains. The series demonstrates a general trend of
increasing duration with alkyl [[phenethylamine-chain-length-effects]], along with varying potency and effect character. The thioether series
overall tends to produce longer durations and heavier body loads than the halogenated 2C compounds, with the safety
concerns of 2C-T-7 serving as a cautionary example of the unpredictable pharmacology that can emerge from relatively
minor structural modifications. 2C-T-2, with its shorter methylthio chain, is somewhat shorter-acting and has a
somewhat better safety reputation, though it too carries risks.

## Routes of Administration

2C-T-7 is unusual among psychedelic phenethylamines in being commonly administered via insufflation (nasal
insufflation) in addition to the standard oral route. Insufflated doses of 2C-T-7 are approximately half the oral dose
(5–15 mg vs. 10–30 mg), with a faster onset (10–20 minutes) but a significantly increased risk of adverse
effects. The insufflation route was implicated in several of the serious adverse events that led to the compound's
scheduling. The rapid absorption via the nasal mucosa produces a steep rise in blood levels, potentially overwhelming
metabolic pathways and leading to dangerously high concentrations of the parent compound and its metabolites. Most and [[harm-reduction-guide]] guidance strongly advises against the insufflation of 2C-T-7 and related thioether compounds.
