# 2C-T-7 (2,5-Dimethoxy-4-propylthiophenethylamine)

2C-T-7 is a synthetic psychedelic phenethylamine belonging to the 2C-T subfamily, a series of compounds in which the
4-position substituent is an alkylthio (thioether) group. First synthesized by [[alexander-shulgin]] and documented in
*PiHKAL*, 2C-T-7 is distinguished by its exceptionally long duration, strong visual effects, and a complicated safety
profile that ultimately led to its control in multiple jurisdictions.

## Chemical Properties

2C-T-7 is 2,5-dimethoxy-4-propylthiophenethylamine, with the molecular formula C₁₃H₂₁NO₂S and a molecular
weight of 255.38 g/mol. The defining feature of the 2C-T series is the replacement of the 4-position halogen atom found
in compounds like [[2c-b]] (bromine) or [[2c-i]] (iodine) with a thioether linkage (–S–R), where R is an alkyl
group. In 2C-T-7, this alkyl group is a propyl chain (–S–CH₂CH₂CH₃). The sulfur atom introduces unique
electronic and conformational properties compared to the halogenated 2C compounds, affecting receptor binding and
metabolic pathways. The compound is typically encountered as a white crystalline powder, with an effective oral dose
range of 10–30 mg. It is also active via insufflation at lower doses (5–15 mg), though this route is associated
with increased risk.

## Pharmacology

2C-T-7 acts as a 5-HT₂A receptor agonist, the primary mechanism underlying its psychedelic effects. The thioether
linkage confers distinct pharmacokinetic properties compared to halogenated analogues. Notably, the propylthio group is
susceptible to metabolic oxidation by cytochrome P450 enzymes (particularly CYP2D6), which can produce reactive
metabolites including sulfoxide and sulfone derivatives. This metabolic pathway is a key factor in the compound's
complex toxicity profile, as the oxidative metabolites may have biological activity distinct from the parent compound.
Drug interactions involving CYP2D6 inhibition — such as those with certain antidepressants, antihistamines, and other
substances — can dramatically alter 2C-T-7 metabolism and increase the risk of adverse effects.

## Metabolic Considerations

The metabolic fate of 2C-T-7 is more complex than that of the halogenated 2C compounds. The thioether sulfur can
undergo sequential oxidation to form sulfoxide (2C-T-7-SO) and sulfone (2C-T-7-SO₂) metabolites, both of which may
retain psychoactive and physiological activity. This multi-step metabolic pathway has several important implications:
the active metabolites may contribute to the overall duration and character of the experience; competition between
metabolic pathways can lead to unpredictable blood levels; and inhibition of CYP2D6 (a highly polymorphic enzyme with
significant inter-individual variation in activity) can cause dramatic, potentially dangerous increases in parent
compound levels. Poor CYP2D6 metabolizers may experience significantly more intense and prolonged effects at the same
dose as extensive metabolizers.

## Subjective Effects

The subjective effects of 2C-T-7 are characterized by intense, colorful visual hallucinations often described as
"liquid" or "flowing" in quality, with pronounced color shifting and patterning. Cognitive effects include enhanced
introspection, a sense of mental clarity unusual among psychedelics, and occasionally profound emotional experiences.
The body load is more pronounced than with many 2C compounds, with common reports of significant physical stimulation,
vasoconstriction, nausea, and temperature dysregulation. The most notable pharmacological characteristic of 2C-T-7 is
its exceptional duration — effects can persist for 12–16 hours or longer, particularly at higher doses, making it
one of the longest-lasting orally active phenethylamine psychedelics. The extended duration, combined with the
significant body load, can make the experience physically taxing.

## Safety Concerns and Controversy

2C-T-7 acquired a reputation as one of the more dangerous members of the 2C family following several hospitalizations
and at least two fatalities in the early 2000s. The deaths were associated with polydrug use, insufflation, and
possible MAO inhibitor interactions. The compound's susceptibility to dangerous drug interactions — particularly with
MAOIs, which can prevent the oxidative metabolism of the thioether group — is a significant safety concern.
Additionally, the combination of long duration and heavy body load means that adverse effects are sustained over many
hours, compounding their impact. These incidents led to emergency scheduling of 2C-T-7 in the United States (2002) and
several other countries. The case of 2C-T-7 is frequently cited as an example of the risks inherent in novel
psychoactive substances whose pharmacology is incompletely understood.

## Broader 2C-T Series Context

The 2C-T series represents Shulgin's exploration of thioether substitution at the 4-position of the
2,5-dimethoxyphenethylamine scaffold. Other members include 2C-T-2 (methylthio), 2C-T-4 (ethylthio), 2C-T-21
(isopropylthio), and compounds with longer or branched alkyl chains. The series demonstrates a general trend of
increasing duration with alkyl chain length, along with varying potency and effect character. The thioether series
overall tends to produce longer durations and heavier body loads than the halogenated 2C compounds, with the safety
concerns of 2C-T-7 serving as a cautionary example of the unpredictable pharmacology that can emerge from relatively
minor structural modifications. 2C-T-2, with its shorter methylthio chain, is somewhat shorter-acting and has a
somewhat better safety reputation, though it too carries risks.

## Routes of Administration

2C-T-7 is unusual among psychedelic phenethylamines in being commonly administered via insufflation (nasal
insufflation) in addition to the standard oral route. Insufflated doses of 2C-T-7 are approximately half the oral dose
(5–15 mg vs. 10–30 mg), with a faster onset (10–20 minutes) but a significantly increased risk of adverse
effects. The insufflation route was implicated in several of the serious adverse events that led to the compound's
scheduling. The rapid absorption via the nasal mucosa produces a steep rise in blood levels, potentially overwhelming
metabolic pathways and leading to dangerously high concentrations of the parent compound and its metabolites. Most harm
reduction guidance strongly advises against the insufflation of 2C-T-7 and related thioether compounds.
