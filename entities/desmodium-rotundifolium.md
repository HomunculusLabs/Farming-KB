## Overview

Desmodium rotundifolium, commonly known as roundleaf ticktrefoil or prostrate ticktrefoil, is a perennial herbaceous legume native to [[mckenna-club-des-hachischins-and-nineteenth-century-literary-hashish]]. It belongs to the family Fabaceae, subfamily Faboideae, and tribe Desmodieae.

The genus Desmodium contains over 300 species distributed in tropical and [[query-how-to-protect-plants-from-frost-and-extend-the-growing-season]].

Flowers are borne in axillary racemes on short peduncles arising from the leaf axils. Each raceme contains 5 to 15 flowers with papilionaceous corollas approximately 8 to 12 millimeters long.

Petals are pink to purplish-pink, with the standard petal slightly darker than the wings and keel. Flowering occurs from June through August across most of the species' range, with southern populations blooming slightly earlier.

Fruits are flattened loments composed of 3 to 6 jointed segments, each approximately 5 to 8 millimeters long. The outer surface of each segment is covered with minute hooked hairs that readily adhere to fur, feathers, and clothing.

Roots form associations with Bradyrhizobium bacteria, producing nodules on the root system for atmospheric nitrogen fixation. The root system is relatively shallow but well-branched, consistent with the plant's prostrate growth habit.

## Distribution and Habitat

Desmodium rotundifolium is native to the eastern United States, ranging from Pennsylvania and New Jersey west to Missouri and eastern Oklahoma, and south to northern Florida and eastern Texas. It is most abundant in the Ohio River Valley and central Appalachian region.

The species occurs in a variety of partially shaded habitats including dry to mesic oak-hickory forests, upland savannas, woodland edges, rocky bluffs, and along stream terraces. It is more tolerant of shade than most prairie legumes.

Roundleaf ticktrefoil is commonly found on well-drained loam or sandy-loam soils with moderate fertility. It tolerates acidic conditions better than many legumes, occurring on soils with pH as low as 4.8 in some forested sites.

Elevation ranges from near sea level in the Atlantic coastal plain to approximately 800 meters in the southern Appalachians. It is most characteristic of the Interior Low Plateau, Ozark Plateau, and Ridge and Valley provinces.

In forest communities, roundleaf ticktrefoil is a component of the herbaceous understory beneath oak, hickory, and pine canopies. It often grows alongside other woodland legumes such as [[dighton-fungal-drought-tolerance-plant-water-relations]] once established, though prolonged dry periods can cause partial senescence of trailing stems. It recovers quickly with resumed rainfall, producing new growth from rooted nodes and the crown.

Fire response is generally positive in savanna and open woodland settings. Plants resprout from the root crown and rooted stem nodes following low-intensity prescribed burns, and post-fire conditions often favor increased flowering and seed production.

## Ecology and Interactions

As a nitrogen-fixing legume, Desmodium rotundifolium contributes significantly to the nitrogen economy of forest and savanna ecosystems. Fixed nitrogen becomes available to neighboring plants through leaf litter decomposition and root exudation.

The sticky loment segments are adapted for epizoochory, dispersing by attaching to the fur of mammals and feathers of birds. White-tailed deer, raccoons, wild turkeys, and various songbirds all serve as dispersal vectors.

The foliage provides palatable browse for white-tailed deer and is consumed by cottontail rabbits during the growing season. The low growth habit offers cover for ground-dwelling invertebrates and small mammals.

Several species of native bees visit the flowers for nectar and pollen, including bumblebees and long-tongued solitary bees. Butterflies and skippers are also frequent flower visitors during the midsummer bloom period.

Roundleaf ticktrefoil serves as a larval host plant for some skipper butterfly species. The foliage provides suitable food for caterpillars of the silver-spotted skipper and other hesperiids.

## Cultivation and Uses

Desmodium rotundifolium can be established from seed in native woodland and savanna restoration projects. Seed scarification improves germination rates, which are otherwise low due to the hard seed coat.

Seeding rates for ecological plantings range from 3 to 6 kilograms per hectare. Seeds should be planted shallowly in areas with partial to full shade, as the species struggles in open, full-sun conditions with heavy competition from tall grasses.

Once established, roundleaf ticktrefoil is a long-lived perennial that persists with minimal management. It spreads gradually by both seed dispersal and vegetative rooting at stem nodes.

In native landscaping, the species is used as a shade-tolerant groundcover for woodland gardens and naturalized areas. Its trailing habit and late-summer flowers provide visual interest beneath taller perennials and shrubs.

The species has been investigated for use in agroforestry systems as an understory legume beneath nut and timber trees. Its shade tolerance and nitrogen-fixing ability make it a candidate for temperate [[alley-cropping]] configurations.
