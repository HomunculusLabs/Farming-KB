## Overview

**Litsea glauca** is an evergreen tree species in the family Lauraceae, native to tropical and subtropical forests of Southeast Asia. The specific epithet *glauca* refers to the glaucous (bluish-grey) appearance of the leaf undersides.

It is a medium-sized canopy tree reaching heights of 10–20 meters in natural forest settings. The species is valued locally for its aromatic timber and traditional medicinal applications.

The crown is dense and broadly conical in younger trees, becoming more rounded with maturity. Branching is ascending to spreading, with slender, glabrous twigs that are green when young and brown with age.

In its native range, the species is known by several common names reflecting its aromatic foliage and traditional uses. Local communities in Yunnan refer to it by names that translate roughly to "fragrant mountain laurel."

**Litsea glauca** (Sieb. ex Nees) Nees belongs to the order Laurales, a basal angiosperm lineage. The genus *Litsea* comprises approximately 200–400 species distributed across tropical and subtropical Asia, Australia, and the [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-hawaii-pacific-islands]].

Molecular phylogenetic studies place *Litsea* within the core Laureae clade of Lauraceae, closely related to *Lindera* and *Neolitsea*. Species-level taxonomy within the genus remains challenging due to morphological plasticity and overlapping character states.

The bark is smooth and grey-brown when young, becoming fissured with age. Leaves are alternate, elliptic to oblong-lanceolate, measuring 8–18 cm long and 3–6 cm wide.

The upper leaf surface is dark green and glossy, while the lower surface is distinctly glaucous with a whitish bloom. Leaf margins are entire, and petioles are 1–2 cm long with a slight channel on the upper surface.

When crushed, the leaves emit a pleasant camphoraceous aroma characteristic of many Lauraceae species. This scent is most pronounced in young foliage and diminishes as leaves mature.

Inflorescences are axillary umbels with 3–6 flowers. Flowers are small, yellowish-white, and functionally unisexual in a dioecious arrangement.

Male flowers typically possess 9–12 stamens arranged in four whorls, while female flowers contain a superior ovary with a single ovule. Floral morphology is consistent with the generalized Lauraceae pollination syndrome involving small generalist insects.

Fruits are spherical drupes approximately 8–10 mm in diameter, ripening from green to dark purple or black. Each fruit contains a single seed enclosed in a thin endocarp.

The fruit pulp is thin and aromatic, surrounding a hard, smooth seed approximately 5–7 mm in diameter. Seed viability declines rapidly after removal from the fruit, necessitating prompt sowing for propagation.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Litsea glauca* occurs naturally across southern China (Yunnan, Guangxi, Guangdong), northern Vietnam, Laos, Thailand, and Myanmar. Populations are found at elevations ranging from 200 to 1500 meters above sea level.

The species represents one of the more widespread *Litsea* taxa in mainland Southeast Asia. Herbarium records document occurrences in primary and secondary forests across the Indo-Burma biodiversity hotspot.

The species grows in broadleaved evergreen forests, often on slopes and ridges with well-drained soils. It prefers acidic to neutral soils derived from granite or sandstone parent material.

It is a component of mid-successional forest communities, commonly associated with *Castanopsis*, *Schima*, and other Lauraceae genera. In disturbed habitats, it can persist as a secondary growth species.
