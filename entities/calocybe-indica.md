## Calocybe indica

## Overview

Calocybe indica is a tropical edible mushroom native to the Indian subcontinent, widely known as the Milky Mushroom or Dudh Chatta (Hindi: "milk umbrella"). It belongs to the family Lyophyllaceae within the order Agaricales. Among commercially [[volvariella-volvacea]]. In domestic markets it commands a price premium over paddy straw mushroom due to its meatier consistency and longer post-harvest viability.

## Taxonomy and Naming

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Lyophyllaceae
- **Genus:** Calocybe
- **Species:** C. indica (P. Karst. & Massee) Pegler

The species was originally described under a different genus before being transferred to Calocybe. The epithet *indica* refers to its Indian origin. Several synonyms exist in older literature, which has occasionally caused confusion with other pale-colored Calocybe species.

Molecular phylogenetic studies place it within a tropical clade of Calocybe, separate from the temperate European species C. gambosa (St. George's mushroom). Its closest known relatives within the genus are also tropical Asian taxa, though the genus-level taxonomy of Lyophyllaceae remains under active revision.

## Description

### Macroscopic Features

The fruiting body is robust and entirely white when fresh, darkening to cream or pale tan with age. It is classified as a medium-to-large agaric, comparable in size to commercial button mushrooms but with a sturdier stipe.

Individual caps can weigh 30–80 g at harvest. The overall appearance resembles a small Agaricus but the absence of a ring and volva, combined with the sinuate gill attachment, readily separates it in the field.

- **Pileus:** 5–12 cm diameter, convex to plano-convex, sometimes with a low umbo; surface smooth, dry, white aging to creamish; margin incurved then decurved, occasionally slightly striate at maturity.
- **Lamellae:** Adnexed to sinuate, crowded, white to pale cream, with lamellulae of varying lengths; edges even.
- **Stipe:** 4–9 cm long, 1–2 cm thick, central, cylindrical, solid; surface smooth, white, sometimes with a mealy coating near the apex; no annulus or volva.
- **Flesh:** Thick, firm, white, unchanging on exposure; odor mild and pleasant, reminiscent of fresh meal; taste mild, slightly farinaceous.
- **Spore print:** White.

### Microscopic Features

| Feature | Description |

## Distribution and Habitat

Calocybe indica is native to the Indian subcontinent and is found across India, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh, and parts of Southeast Asia. It has also been reported in tropical Africa.

Natural fruiting occurs during the monsoon season (June–September in India) on decomposing organic matter in open grasslands, cultivated fields, and forest margins.

The species is a primary decomposer (saprotroph) of lignocellulosic substrates, particularly paddy straw, sugarcane bagasse, and various agricultural residues. In the wild it is most commonly found in soil-rich environments with high humidity and warm temperatures. Unlike many temperate agarics, it does not require a cold shock for fruiting initiation.

Collections have been documented from elevations ranging from sea level to approximately 800 m, with optimal natural fruiting in the humid lowland tropics. It is not known to form mycorrhizal associations and appears entirely saprotrophic throughout its life cycle.

## Cultivation Notes

C. indica is one of the easiest tropical mushrooms to cultivate on a commercial scale, requiring no sophisticated [[stamets-cultivator-humidity-management-fogging-systems]]. It is particularly suited to low-technology farming systems.

- **Substrates:** Paddy straw (most common), wheat straw, sugarcane bagasse, maize stover, cotton waste, and combinations thereof. Supplements such as rice bran (10–15%) significantly improve yields.
- **Pasteurization:** Hot water treatment (80°C for 1–2 hours) or steam pasteurization. No sterilization required for standard straw-based cultivation.
- **Spawn:** Grain spawn (sorghum or wheat) is standard. Spawn rate is typically 5–10% (wet weight basis) of the substrate. Spawn run takes 15–20 days at 28–32°C. Mycelial growth rate peaks around 30°C and drops sharply below 20°C.
- **Casing:** Not strictly required, but a thin casing layer of sterilized soil or coir pith can improve pinning uniformity and yield by 10–20%.
- **Fruiting temperature:** 28–35°C; no cold shock needed. Relative humidity 80–95%.
- **Crop cycle:** 50–60 days from spawning to final harvest. Two to three flushes are typical, with the first flush producing 60–70% of total yield.

## Nutritional Value

Calocybe indica is a nutritious food source with a favorable protein-to-carbohydrate ratio. Fresh fruiting bodies contain approximately 85–90% moisture. Values per 100 g dry weight (approximate ranges from multiple studies):

- **Protein:** 18–26 g
- **Carbohydrates:** 45–55 g
- **Crude fiber:** 8–12 g
- **Total lipids:** 2–4 g
- **Ash:** 6–9 g
- **Energy:** 280–340 kcal
