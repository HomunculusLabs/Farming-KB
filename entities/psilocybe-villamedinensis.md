# Psilocybe villamedinensis

**Psilocybe villamedinensis** is a tropical [[guzman-allen-gartz-psilocybe-section-relationships-trans-pacific-disjunctions]] psilocybe-sect.-psilocybe, which encompasses the majority of bluing, psychoactive taxa in the genus. Molecular data for *P. villamedinensis* remain limited, and its phylogenetic position relative to other neotropical species has not been fully resolved.

Spore prints are dark purplish brown. Basidiospores are ellipsoid to slightly rhomboid, approximately 10 to 14 µm long by 6.5 to 8.5 µm wide, with thick walls and a prominent germ pore.
Cheilocystidia are lageniform to ventricose with a narrow neck, often abundant. Pleurocystidia are similar but less frequent.

### Microscopic Features
Basidiospores measure 10 to 14 µm long by 6.5 to 8.5 µm wide, ellipsoid to broadly ellipsoid in face view, with a prominent apical germ pore and moderately thickened walls. The spore surface is smooth to very finely roughened.
Cheilocystidia are ventricose to lageniform, measuring 18 to 26 µm in total length, with a narrow cylindrical neck that may be slightly capitate at the tip. They are numerous on the lamellar edges, forming a conspicuous sterile band.
Basidia are 4-spored, clavate, measuring 18 to 25 µm long. Pleurocystidia are occasionally present on the lamellar faces, similar in morphology to the cheilocystidia but less regularly shaped and more sparsely distributed.
The pileipellis is a cutis of hyaline, filamentous hyphae 2 to 4 µm wide, with occasional gelatinous intrusions. Clamp connections are present at the hyphal septa. The occasional presence of pleurocystidia is a notable character that may aid in distinguishing this species from closely related Venezuelan taxa.

## Distribution and Habitat

### Geographic Range
*Psilocybe villamedinensis* is known from the Andean highlands of western Venezuela, with confirmed collections from Trujillo state near the city of Valera. Additional collections have been made in the neighboring state of Mérida in the northern Andes.
The species is likely endemic to the Venezuelan Andes, one of the most mycologically diverse but under-surveyed regions in South America. Its true range may extend further along the Andean cordillera into Colombia, but no confirmed records exist from outside Venezuela.

### Ecology
The species inhabits montane cloud forests and disturbed pastures in the Andean highlands, at altitudes ranging from approximately 1,500 to 2,800 meters above sea level. It grows on soil rich in organic matter, often in association with decomposing plant material along forest trails, at the margins of cultivated land, and in highland pastures that have replaced original forest cover.
Fruiting occurs during the wet season, typically from May through October, when persistent cloud cover, frequent mist, and high humidity provide the moisture regime favorable for basidiocarp development.
The species appears to tolerate some degree of habitat disturbance, as it has been found in both primary cloud forest and secondary grassland environments. This ecological flexibility may help the species persist in a landscape increasingly modified by human activity. However, the long-term viability of populations in secondary habitats compared to primary cloud forest remains uncertain and would benefit from comparative ecological study.
