# Dolomitic Limestone

## Description

Dolomitic limestone is a naturally occurring sedimentary rock composed of calcium magnesium carbonate (CaMg(CO3)2). It serves a dual purpose in agriculture and horticulture: raising soil pH (liming) while simultaneously supplying both calcium and magnesium—two essential macronutrients. Dolomitic limestone is the preferred liming material for soils that are both acidic and deficient in magnesium, conditions that frequently co-occur in sandy, leached, or heavily weathered soils.

## Classification

- **Type:** Agricultural amendment / liming agent
- **Chemical formula:** CaMg(CO3)2 (calcium magnesium carbonate)
- **Mineral:** Dolomite
- **Origin:** Sedimentary rock
- **NPK value:** None (not a traditional fertilizer)

## Key Facts

- Contains approximately 54% calcium carbonate and 46% magnesium carbonate by molecular weight, though commercial products vary.
- Provides both calcium (Ca) and magnesium (Mg) in a single application—two of the three major cation nutrients (along with potassium).
- Used specifically when soil tests indicate low pH AND low magnesium—a common combination in acidified, leached soils.
- Reacts more slowly than pure calcitic limestone because dolomite is less soluble, providing a more gradual pH adjustment.
- Application rate depends on the soil's buffer pH (resistance to pH change) and its current magnesium status.
- Magnesium is the central atom in the chlorophyll molecule and is essential for photosynthesis, phosphate metabolism, and enzyme activation.
- Over-application can lead to excessive magnesium, which competes with calcium for uptake and can worsen calcium deficiency despite adequate calcium in soil.
- In soils with already high magnesium, calcitic limestone (CaCO3) should be used instead.

## Chemical Reaction in Soil

1. CaMg(CO3)2 is applied to acidic soil.
2. Carbonate ions (CO3 2-) neutralize hydrogen ions (H+) in the soil solution.
3. Carbonic acid (H2CO3) forms and decomposes into CO2 and H2O.
4. Both Ca2+ and Mg2+ are released into the soil solution.
5. These cations occupy exchange sites on clay and organic matter particles.
6. Net result: pH increases, and both calcium and magnesium become available.

## Calcium-Magnesium Balance

The ratio of calcium to magnesium in soil is critical:

- Excess magnesium from overuse of dolomitic lime can reduce calcium availability, leading to blossom end rot in tomatoes, bitter pit in apples, and poor cell wall development.
- Ideal Ca:Mg ratio targets vary by soil type but generally fall between 3:1 and 7:1 on a base saturation basis.
- Too much calcium (from over-liming with calcitic limestone) can also reduce magnesium availability, causing interveinal chlorosis.
- Soil testing is essential to determine which liming material is appropriate.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Mushroom substrate supplementation**: Magnesium is a cofactor for many fungal enzymes. Dolomitic limestone can provide both calcium and magnesium to mushroom substrates when magnesium is deficient.
- **Casing layer formulation**: Dolomitic limestone is used in casing soil mixes for Agaricus and other mushroom species, providing pH buffering and mineral nutrition.
- **Outdoor bed preparation**: For outdoor mushroom beds and garden plots, dolomitic limestone corrects acidity while supplying magnesium for both plants and soil fungi.
- **Compost pH buffering**: Adding dolomitic limestone to compost piles can prevent excessive acidification during decomposition.
- **Mycorrhizal health**: Appropriate soil pH supports mycorrhizal fungal associations, which are critical for phosphorus uptake.
- **Caution**: Excessive magnesium can create hard, compacted soil structure, reducing aeration and drainage—both critical for fungal mycelium growth.

## Application Guidelines

- Conduct a soil test before application to determine pH, buffer pH, calcium, and magnesium levels.
- Use dolomitic limestone only when both pH and magnesium are low.
- If pH is low but magnesium is adequate or high, use calcitic limestone instead.
- Apply in fall for spring gardens to allow gradual pH adjustment.
- Incorporate into soil for faster results; surface application works but is slower.
- Monitor pH trends over multiple seasons and re-test annually after initial application.

## Relationships

- **Related amendment**: Calcitic limestone (CaCO3)
- **Opposite amendment**: Sulfur / sulfuric acid (lowers pH)
- **Key nutrients supplied**: Calcium (Ca2+), Magnesium (Mg2+)
- **Related concepts**: Soil pH, buffer pH, Ca:Mg ratio, base saturation, cation exchange capacity
