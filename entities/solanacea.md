## Description

The Solanaceae (referred to as Solanacea in Palmer"s text) are a family of flowering plants commonly known as the nightshade family. In the context of mushroom poisoning, this family is critically important because it furnishes the most trustworthy antidotes to Amanitine poisoning. The active principles extracted from several Solanaceae species — particularly atropine from [[atropa-belladonna]] — have been used to counteract the deadly alkaloids found in Amanita mushrooms.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Order:** Solanales
- **Family:** Solanaceae
- **Common name:** Nightshade family
- **Key antitoxin-producing genera:** Atropa, Datura, Nicotiana

## Key Facts

- Palmer identifies the Solanaceae as providing "the most trustworthy antidotes" for Amanitine poisoning
- Three specific alkaloids from this family are cited as antidotes:
  1. **Daturine** — from Datura species
  2. **Nicotine** — from Nicotiana (tobacco)
  3. **Atropine** — from Atropa belladonna (ranked most effective)
- Atropine used via subcutaneous injection is identified as the most promising treatment
- The physiological antagonism between Solanaceae alkaloids and Amanita alkaloids was demonstrated experimentally

## Pharmacological Significance

The Solanaceae-Amanita antagonism represents one of the earliest documented examples of specific pharmacological counteraction:

- Schmiedeberg and Koppe demonstrated that a frog's heart, silenced by muscarine (from [[amanita-muscaria]]), resumes beating when treated with atropine
- Animals pre-treated with atropine survive otherwise fatal doses of muscarine
- A consulting physician independently suggested belladonna treatment after observing that the mushroom's effects on the patient mirrored belladonna's known pharmacological profile
- This independent observation confirmed the theoretical basis for the antidote

## Notable Solanaceae Species in This Context

### Atropa belladonna (Deadly Nightshade)
- Source of atropine, the primary antidote
- Tincture of belladonna was used clinically in the documented 1876 poisoning case
- The patient appeared to improve under its influence, though treatment may have started too late

### Datura species
- Source of daturine
- Less prominently discussed but listed among the antidotal alkaloids

### Nicotiana (Tobacco)
- Source of nicotine
- Palmer personally suggested tobacco for hypodermic use based on his own experience with its effects and his knowledge of Amanitine's action
- He was not a tobacco user, lending objectivity to his observation

## Clinical Application

The recommended approach for using Solanaceae-derived antidotes:

1. Administer via subcutaneous injection for most direct systemic reach
2. Begin treatment as early as possible — delay reduces effectiveness as the alkaloid is absorbed
3. Balance the dose carefully — too much atropine can itself become poisonous
4. Combine with enemata (warm water, soap and water, oil of sweet almonds or olive oil) for symptomatic relief
5. Avoid emetics and cathartics, which may worsen absorption

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Understanding the Solanaceae antidotes is essential for:

- **Emergency preparedness:** Any mycologist or forager working with Amanita species should know that atropine is the primary pharmacological antidote
- **Historical toxicology:** The Solanaceae-Amanita relationship was one of the first well-documented poison-antidote pairs in modern medicine
- **Plant-mushroom pharmacology:** The fact that two entirely different kingdoms produce compounds that physiologically antagonize each other has implications for understanding alkaloid evolution
- **Modern medicine:** Atropine remains a component of treatment protocols for certain types of mushroom poisoning today
