# Boil-A-Bag Technique

## Description

The Boil-A-Bag technique is an indoor mushroom cultivation method developed by D.H. Jarrold that uses standard kitchen roasting bags as cultivation containers. The technique provides a complete, low-cost method for growing mushrooms at home using readily available materials. It encompasses substrate preparation, sterilization, inoculation, colonization, fruiting, and spore print collection in a self-sustaining cycle.

## Classification

- **Type**: Cultivation technique / method
- **Skill level**: Amateur / beginner
- **Setting**: Indoor / home
- **Cost**: Ultra-low (approximately 2.50 GBP for 50 bags)
- **Scale**: Small-scale personal use

## Key Facts

- Uses kitchen boil bags (20x30cm) that withstand pressure cooker temperatures at 15 psi
- Substrate: chopped straw soaked in marmite/boiling water, supplemented with brown rice flour
- Sterilization: 60 minutes at 15 psi in a pressure cooker
- Inoculation: spore syringe through the bag wall (short needle) or through filter can (long needle)
- Gas exchange provided by a homemade filter can (food can filled with vermiculite and rock wool)
- Colonization: bags kept in cardboard box in warm location
- Fruiting: bags placed in humidity chamber (fish tank with hydro pellets)
- Spore prints collected for self-sustaining cultivation cycle

## Materials Required

The technique requires minimal specialized equipment: boil bags from Lakeland Plastics, a pressure cooker, spore syringe, propyl alcohol, spirit burner, vermiculite or rock wool, masking tape, straw, brown rice flour, marmite, glass jars, tweezers, scissors, and a syringe with needle. Most materials are household items or available at local shops.

## Substrate Preparation

Straw is chopped to 3-10cm lengths and soaked in boiling water with dissolved marmite for approximately 2 hours. The marmite (yeast extract) provides nutrients that accelerate spore germination. After soaking, the straw is drained in a bathtub for 1 hour, with excess water squeezed out by hand. Brown rice flour is sprinkled over the straw (3-4 handfuls for 6 bags) and mixed in as a nutritional supplement.

## Sterilization Process

Filled bags are placed in a pressure cooker with water and brought to 15 psi for 60 minutes. Jarrold noted that some cultivators may prefer to split this into two 30-minute stages. After sterilization, bags must cool completely before inoculation. The filter cans fitted into the bag tops allow gas exchange during and after sterilization while maintaining sterile conditions.

## Inoculation Methods

Jarrold described two inoculation methods. The short needle method involves swabbing inoculation points with propyl alcohol, heating the needle until red to penetrate the bag, and injecting 5cc of spore solution at each point. The long needle method is preferred as "much easier, quicker and more trouble free" with the needle passed through the filter can and 10cc of spore solution inoculated directly into the substrate.

## Fruiting and Humidity Management

Fully colonized bags are transferred to a humidity chamber made from a 4ft by 2ft fish tank with soaked hydro pellets at the bottom. Fresh air is introduced by fanning the tank twice daily. When pinning begins, bags are cut down the sides to expose the substrate. For cold climates, Jarrold designed a warm humid air delivery chamber using a water container with heater and fan.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

The Boil-A-Bag technique represents one of the most accessible methods for amateur mushroom cultivation, lowering the barrier to entry by eliminating the need for expensive specialized equipment. While simpler than professional techniques, it incorporates all the essential principles of mushroom cultivation including sterilization, aseptic technique, gas exchange, humidity management, and lifecycle sustainability.
