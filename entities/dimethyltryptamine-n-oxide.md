# N,N-Dimethyltryptamine N-Oxide

N,N-Dimethyltryptamine N-oxide (DMT N-oxide) is an oxidative metabolite of [[dimethyltryptamine]] except for the addition of an oxygen atom bonded to the tertiary amine nitrogen, forming an N-oxide functional group. The IUPAC name is N,N-dimethyl-2-(1H-indol-3-yl)ethanamine N-oxide.

The N-oxide group is a polar, zwitterionic functionality that significantly alters the physicochemical properties of the molecule compared to [[dimethyltryptamine]] by flavin-containing monooxygenase enzymes, particularly FMO3 in the human liver. This is a phase I metabolic reaction that converts the lipophilic tertiary amine into a more polar N-oxide for potential excretion.

## Occurrence in Nature

DMT N-oxide has been detected in several natural sources, contributing to the complex alkaloid profiles of these organisms:

- **Psychotria viridis and Diplopterys cabrerana**: The two primary DMT-containing admixture plants used in ayahuasca preparations have been found to contain DMT N-oxide alongside [[dimethyltryptamine]] during the preparation of ayahuasca or other plant-based preparations, potentially contributing to the overall DMT content of the brew.

- **HPLC**: With appropriate chromatographic conditions, DMT N-oxide can be separated from DMT based on its greater polarity and shorter retention time on reverse-phase columns.
