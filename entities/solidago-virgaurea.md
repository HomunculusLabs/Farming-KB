## Solidago virgaurea

## Overview

Solidago virgaurea, the European goldenrod or woundwort, is a perennial herbaceous plant in the family Asteraceae. It is native to Europe and western Asia and widely valued for its [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]].

The plant grows 30–100 cm tall from a woody, creeping rhizome. Stems are erect, unbranched or sparsely branched above, and slightly hairy below the inflorescence.

Basal leaves form a rosette that withers before flowering. Cauline leaves are alternate, lanceolate to ovate, 4–10 cm long with serrate margins, and progressively reduced upward along the stem.

The inflorescence is a dense, cylindrical or slightly pyramidal panicle of small flower heads. Each head contains 5–12 bright yellow ray florets surrounding 6–15 tubular disc florets.

Flowering occurs from July to October depending on latitude and elevation. The achenes are cylindrical, approximately 2 mm long, with a pappus of white hairs facilitating wind dispersal.

Root and rhizome morphology includes a horizontal rhizome system producing fibrous adventitious roots. Underground biomass can constitute a significant proportion of total plant mass, particularly in established populations.

## Traditional and Modern Uses

European goldenrod has a long history of medicinal use dating to classical antiquity. Dioscorides and Pliny the Elder documented its wound-healing properties, giving rise to the common name "woundwort."
