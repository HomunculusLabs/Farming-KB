## Overview

**Oudemansiella mucida** (Schrad.) Höhn., commonly known as the porcelain mushroom or poached egg fungus, is a basidiomycete fungus in the family Physalacriaceae. It is one of the most visually distinctive mushrooms of temperate European forests, producing clusters of translucent, white to cream-colored fruiting bodies on the dead or dying branches of living beech trees (*[[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]]:** Rarely reported, with some collections from eastern Canada; these may represent a distinct but closely related species.
- **Asia:** Reported from Japan and China, though Asian material may represent different taxa within the genus complex.

The species is **strongly associated with beech** (*[[fagus-sylvatica]]* in Europe). It is almost always found growing on:

- Dead or dying branches of living beech trees, typically 2–10 meters above ground
- Fallen beech trunks and large branches
- Occasionally on other hardwoods, but with very low frequency

Fruiting occurs from late summer through late autumn (August–November), with peak abundance in September and October. It prefers humid, shaded forest interiors with good air circulation.

## Ecology and Life History

*Oudemansiella mucida* occupies an interesting ecological niche as both a **saprotroph** and a **weak parasite**:

1. **Parasitic phase:** The fungus colonizes the bark of living beech branches, penetrating the periderm and establishing in the cambial region. This colonization can cause local dieback of smaller branches.
2. **Saprotrophic phase:** After the branch or section of bark dies, the fungus continues to decompose the dead wood, contributing to [[fukuoka-pine-forest-ecosystem-collapse-cascade-matsutake]].

Fruiting typically occurs after periods of cool, wet weather and can be triggered by sudden drops in temperature. The fungus often fruits in large, spectacular clusters that can cover entire sections of dead beech branches.

## Edibility

*Oudemansiella mucida* is considered a good edible mushroom, particularly in Central and Eastern European culinary traditions:

- **Preparation:** The thick, glutinous slime layer must be thoroughly removed before cooking. This is typically done by peeling the cap or wiping with a damp cloth. The stipe is often tough and fibrous, so only caps and upper stipes are used.
- **Culinary qualities:** The flesh has a mild, pleasant flavor with a slightly nutty undertone. It holds its texture well when cooked and is suitable for soups, stews, and sautés.
- **Preservation:** The species can be pickled, dried, or frozen after blanching. Pickled porcelain mushrooms are considered a delicacy in some regions.

**Cautions:**

- The slimy cap surface can harbor small insects and debris that must be cleaned thoroughly.
- As with all wild mushrooms, positive identification is essential before consumption.
- Some individuals may experience mild gastrointestinal discomfort.
