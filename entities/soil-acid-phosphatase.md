## Soil Acid Phosphatase

## Description

Soil acid phosphatase is an enzyme produced by plant roots, [[mycorrhizal-fungi]], and soil microorganisms that catalyzes the hydrolysis of organic phosphorus compounds into plant-available inorganic phosphate (orthophosphate) under acidic soil conditions (optimal pH ~4–6.5). It plays a critical role in phosphorus cycling in terrestrial ecosystems, particularly in forest soils where organic phosphorus often represents the dominant phosphorus pool. Fungi, especially mycorrhizal species, are major contributors to soil acid phosphatase activity.

## Classification

- **Category**: Enzyme / Phosphohydrolase
- **EC Number**: EC 3.1.3.2
- **Optimal pH Range**: 4.0–6.5 (acidic)
- **Reaction**: Organic phosphate + H₂O → alcohol + inorganic phosphate (Pi)
- **Primary Producers**: Mycorrhizal fungi, plant roots, soil bacteria

## Key Facts

- Mycorrhizal colonization is positively correlated with soil acid phosphatase activity (Arnebrant et al., cited in Dighton).
- [[ectomycorrhizal-fungi]] secrete acid phosphatase from their extramatrical mycelium to mobilize phosphorus from organic matter in forest soils.
- Nitrogen amendments reduce mycorrhizal colonization and consequently decrease acid phosphatase activity in forest soils (Arnebrant, 1994).
- [[phosphorus-availability]] is often the limiting nutrient in forest ecosystems, making phosphatase activity a key regulator of ecosystem productivity.
- The enzyme hydrolyzes phosphate monoesters including phytate, nucleotides, and phospholipids.
- Acid phosphatase activity is used as a standard biochemical indicator of soil quality and microbial functional diversity.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Mycorrhizal Function**: Acid phosphatase secretion is one of the primary mechanisms by which mycorrhizal fungi deliver phosphorus to host plants. Understanding and optimizing this process is essential for effective [[mycorrhizal-inoculation-guide]] in cultivation systems.
- **Fertilizer Interactions**: Nitrogen fertilization reduces mycorrhizal abundance and acid phosphatase activity, potentially creating phosphorus limitation even when organic phosphorus is abundant in the soil.
- **Soil Health Indicator**: Acid phosphatase activity levels serve as a proxy for the functional health of the soil fungal community and its capacity to support plant nutrition.
- **Organic Farming**: In systems that prohibit soluble phosphate fertilizers, mycorrhizal phosphatase activity becomes the primary mechanism for phosphorus acquisition, making fungal management critical.
- **Forest Management**: Acid deposition and heavy metal contamination suppress phosphatase activity, reducing phosphorus availability and contributing to forest decline syndromes.

## Mechanism of Action

Acid phosphatase enzymes are secreted into the rhizosphere and mycorrhizosphere where they encounter organic phosphorus compounds. The enzyme cleaves phosphate ester bonds, releasing orthophosphate (H₂PO₄⁻/HPO₄²⁻) that can be absorbed by [[fungal-hyphae]] and transported to host plant roots. This process is particularly important in acidic forest soils where inorganic phosphorus is rapidly immobilized by iron and aluminum oxides, leaving organic phosphorus as the primary phosphorus reserve.

## Environmental Sensitivity

Soil acid phosphatase activity is sensitive to environmental stressors. Heavy metal contamination (copper, chromium, arsenic from timber preservatives) reduces phosphatase activity (Bardgett et al., 1994). Acid deposition can both directly inhibit the enzyme and indirectly reduce its production by diminishing mycorrhizal fungal biomass. These feedbacks create a vicious cycle where environmental degradation reduces phosphorus availability, further stressing plant and fungal communities.
