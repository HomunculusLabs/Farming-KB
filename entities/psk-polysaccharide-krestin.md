# PSK (Polysaccharide-K)

**Type:** Chemical Compound
**Category:** Protein-bound polysaccharide (polysaccharopeptide)
**Also known as:** Krestin, PSK, Polysaccharopeptide K

## Overview
PSK (Krestin) is a water-soluble protein-bound polysaccharide extracted
from mycelial cultures of Trametes versicolor (Turkey Tail mushroom).
It is one of the most commercially successful mushroom-derived
pharmaceuticals, at its peak accounting for 25% of total anti-cancer
agent expenditure in Japan (1987). PSK acts through dual pathways:
directly on tumour cells and indirectly through host immune activation.

## Key Details

### Chemical Structure
- Type: Protein-bound beta-glucan polysaccharide
  (polysaccharopeptide)
- Source: Mycelial cultures of Trametes versicolor
- Solubility: Water-soluble
- Composition: Beta-glucan polysaccharide covalently bound to
  protein component (approximately 25-38% protein)
- Trade name: Krestin

### Mechanism of Action
PSK operates through a dual mechanism distinguishing it from purely
immunostimulatory compounds:
- **Direct action:** Cytostatic and cytotoxic effects on tumour cells
  through induction of apoptosis
- **Indirect action:** Potent immunostimulation via T-cells and
  antigen-presenting cells (monocytes and macrophages)
- Increases white blood cell counts
- Stimulates interferon-gamma and interleukin-2 production
- Enhances delayed-type hypersensitivity reactions
- Shows antiviral activity through interferon production stimulation

### Clinical Applications
- Phase I, II, and III clinical trials completed successfully
- Effective against cancers of the stomach, oesophagus, nasopharynx,
  colon, rectum, lung, and subsets of breast cancer
- Protects against immunosuppression from surgery and long-term
  chemotherapy
- Used extensively in Japan as an adjunct to standard radiotherapy
  and chemotherapy
- Reduces side effects of conventional cancer treatments when used
  as a co-therapy
- No significant increase in side effects when combined with
  chemotherapy
- Gives protection against the immunosuppression normally associated
  with surgery and long-term chemotherapy

### Market and Production
Commercial name: Krestin. Produced from controlled mycelial
fermentation of T. versicolor under aseptic conditions. Available
in Japan as a prescription drug and increasingly in Western markets
as a dietary supplement for cancer support. PSP (Polysaccharide-
Peptide) is a related compound from the same source, developed
independently by Chinese researchers.

### Comparison with PSP
Both PSK and PSP derive from T. versicolor mycelium and are potent
immunostimulators. PSK was developed in Japan, PSP in China. Both
show activity for T-cells and antigen-presenting cells. PSP has
been recognised as a drug by the Chinese Ministry of Public Health.
Neither compound shows significant side effects.

## Relevance
PSK represents the most commercially successful mushroom-derived
anticancer compound to date. Its dual mechanism of action, combining
direct tumour cell effects with host immune activation, distinguishes
it from purely immunostimulatory compounds like Lentinan. The
commercial success of Krestin established the economic viability of
mushroom-derived pharmaceuticals and paved the way for development
of PSP, Grifron-D, and next-generation fungal immunomodulators.
