## Overview

**Dendrobium nobile** Lindl., the noble dendrobium, is an epiphytic orchid in the family Orchidaceae. It is one of the most commercially important species in the genus, valued both as an ornamental plant and a traditional medicine.

The species produces upright cane-like pseudobulbs bearing clusters of fragrant flowers in shades of white, pink, and purple. Flowering occurs in late winter to spring, making it a popular plant for seasonal display.

*Dendrobium nobile* is a major source of the traditional Chinese medicine Shi Hu (石斛), documented in the Chinese Pharmacopoeia. The stems contain dendrobine and other bioactive alkaloids with documented pharmacological activities.

The species has been cultivated in China for over 2,000 years and features prominently in the tradition of Chinese orchid appreciation. Its ease of cultivation has made it one of the most widely grown dendrobiums worldwide.

The genus name *Dendrobium* derives from the Greek *dendron* (tree) and *bios* (life), reflecting the epiphytic growth habit. The species epithet *nobile* means "noble" or "distinguished."

In the Victorian era, *Dendrobium nobile* was among the first tropical orchids introduced to European horticulture. It remains one of the most recognizable species in the genus.

The species is listed on CITES Appendix II, reflecting concerns about trade-driven population declines in wild habitats.

**Morphology:**

Plants produce erect to arching pseudobulbs (canes) 30–90 cm tall and 1–2 cm in diameter. Canes are segmented, yellow-green when young, becoming yellowish with age.

Leaves are oblong-lanceolate, 5–10 cm long, arranged alternately along the upper portions of the canes. They are deciduous, dropping in winter before flowering.

Flowers are produced in clusters of 1–3 from nodes along the upper leafless canes. Each flower is 5–8 cm across, with white to pale pink petals and a darker pink to purple labellum.

The labellum is broadly ovate with a dark purple blotch at the base and ruffled margins. Flowers are intensely fragrant, producing a sweet raspberry-like scent.

Roots are thin, wiry, and covered with a silvery velamen. The plant attaches firmly to tree bark and rock surfaces in its natural habitat.

Reintroduction programs using tissue-cultured seedlings have been initiated in several Chinese nature reserves. These efforts aim to restore wild populations while reducing pressure from commercial collection.

The IUCN Red List assessment classifies the species as Vulnerable due to ongoing population declines from habitat loss and over-harvesting throughout its range.

## Ecology and Cultivation

**Ecology:**

*Dendrobium nobile* employs a CAM (Crassulacean Acid Metabolism) photosynthetic pathway as an adaptation to its epiphytic habit. This allows efficient water use during dry periods.

Pollination in the wild is performed by [[cannabis-controlled-pollination-seed-production]].

The species forms [[cannabis-seed-germination]]. Fungi in the genus *Tulasnella* and *Ceratobasidium* have been identified as symbionts.
