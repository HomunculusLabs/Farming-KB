# Mushroom-Infused Beer

## Description

Mushroom-infused beer (also called mycobrew or medicinal mushroom beer) is an alcoholic beverage that incorporates mushrooms or mushroom extracts into the traditional beer brewing process. The practice combines the health benefits of [[medicinal-mushroom-compounds]]—such as [[beta-glucans]], triterpenes, and polysaccharides—with the flavor complexity and cultural appeal of craft beer. Tradd Cotter pioneered the commercial concept with an international debut at the 2012 Telluride Mushroom Festival ("Shroomfest") in Colorado, where 240 gallons sold out in under 48 hours.

## Classification

- **Category:** Fermented alcoholic beverage
- **Type:** Specialty craft beer / functional beverage
- **Key ingredients:** Malted grains, hops, yeast, mushrooms or mushroom extracts
- **Subcategories:** Mushroom ale, mushroom lager, mushroom stout

## Key Facts

### Brewing Methods

Mushrooms can be incorporated into beer at several stages:

1. **Grain mash addition:** Adding fresh, dried, or powdered mushrooms to the grain and mash. Extracts flavors, bitterness, and water-soluble medicinal compounds during the cook at approximately 160°F (71°C).

2. **"Dry shrooming":** The mushroom equivalent of dry hopping—adding powder or whole dried fruitbodies to the fermentation container alongside hops. Imparts complex aromas, unique flavors, and varied bitterness depths depending on species.

3. **Extract addition:** Adding alcohol-based mushroom extracts at kegging or just before bottling. Preferred for medicinal potency because alcohol extracts better release beta-glucans and other molecules trapped in the chitin matrix.

4. **Powdered mushroom addition:** Dried, powdered mushrooms provide maximum surface area for extraction of flavor and medicinal compounds. Powder can be gently roasted before addition for unique flavor enhancement.

### Sterilization Requirement

Fresh and dried mushrooms can carry wild yeasts and bacteria that may spoil beer. Cotter recommends autoclaving mushrooms for one hour at 250°F (121°C) before adding them to any brew, since wort temperature (160°F/71°C) is insufficient to sterilize fruitbodies. For spirit infusions (vodka, etc.), the high alcohol concentration eliminates contamination risk, making pre-sterilization unnecessary.

### Species Selection Criteria

Choosing a mushroom for brewing involves balancing five factors:

**Flavor:** Mushrooms provide preliminary and lingering flavors. Some species are savory and hearty (suited for stouts), while others offer pungency and sweetness (suited for brown ales and lagers). Start with small amounts in small batches.

**Aroma:** Many mushrooms contain aromatic essential oils that mix with hop oils. Birch polypore is noted for vibrantly aromatic oils. Consider the combined olfactory experience.

**Medicinal Properties:** Reishi and shiitake are primary medicinal brewing species. Beer serves as an effective delivery system for extracts.

**Bitterness:** Polypores are naturally "hoppy" and can partially or entirely substitute for hops. Tylopilus species are extremely bitter—use sparingly. Test bitterness by chewing a fresh specimen for 60 seconds.

**Cost:** Use powdered mushrooms or extracts rather than bulk pieces to minimize cost. Powder provides more surface area and efficient extraction.

### Notable Recipes

**Turkey Tail Ale** (golden pale ale)
- 6 lb extra-light malted grain
- 4 oz fresh or 2 oz dried turkey tail (Trametes versicolor)
- 1 oz hops, ale yeast

**Iceman Amber** (amber ale)
- 6 lb extra-light malted grain, 1 lb amber malt
- 12 oz organic carrot juice
- 4 oz fresh or 2 oz dried birch polypore (Piptoporus betulinus)
- 4 oz fresh or 2 oz dried amadou (Fomes fomentarius)
- 1 oz hops, ale yeast

**Reishi Red** (natural red lager)
- 6 lb extra-light malted grain, 1 lb amber malt
- 12 oz red beet juice
- 4 oz fresh or 2 oz dried reishi (Ganoderma spp.)
- 1 oz hops, ale yeast

**Agarikon Stout** (dark, earthy brew)
- 1 lb chocolate malt, 1 lb crystal malt, 5 lb pale malt
- 2 oz liquid agarikon extract (Laricifomes officinalis)
- 1 oz hops, Trappist yeast

### Medicinal Considerations

Cotter recommends moderation in consuming mushroom-infused beer:

- Drink weekly rather than daily
- Alternate recipes to expose the body to different mushroom properties
- Observe individual responses to different formulas
- Try mushrooms or extracts alone (without alcohol) to compare effects
- Use for anti-inflammatory benefits (e.g., arthritis relief)

### Yeast and Grain Sourcing

Quality brewing inputs are essential:

- **Yeast:** Use pure cultures from reliable suppliers; liquid yeast preferred for quick delivery, dry yeast for remote locations. Maintain yeast cultures using same principles as mushroom cultures—keep masters and backups, alternate media to prevent strain senescence.
- **Grains:** Organic malted grains recommended despite higher cost. Can source from local brewing companies.
- **Hops:** Organic hops are expensive due to powdery mildew vulnerability. Growing your own from rhizomes is cost-effective. Use beneficial insects (ladybugs, predatory mites) and organic fungicides (liquid copper) for disease management.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

### Value-Added Product Development

Mushroom beer represents a significant value-added opportunity for cultivators. A small amount of dried mushroom powder or extract transforms standard craft beer into a premium functional beverage. The 2012 Telluride festival's 240-gallon sellout demonstrates strong consumer demand.

### Hops Substitute Potential

With hops disease problems increasing in commercial production and global demand rising, mushrooms offer a potential partial or complete hops substitute. Polypores with natural bitterness could reduce brewery dependence on conventional hops.

### Mushroom Wine and Spirits

The infusion concept extends beyond beer:

- **Wine:** Add sterilized mushroom powder to fermentation tanks or aging barrels (5g per liter as starting dose)
- **Spirits:** Infuse whole or chopped mushrooms in high-proof spirits (vodka, etc.) for approximately one month. Add spices (lemon peel, hot peppers, cinnamon). Strain when mushrooms sink. Infused mushrooms can be eaten as "mushroom shots" or cooked to remove alcohol.
