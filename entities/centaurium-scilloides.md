## Centaurium scilloides

## Overview

Centaurium scilloides is a perennial herb in the Gentianaceae family, native to coastal regions of western Europe.

Known as the perennial centaury, it is distinguished from annual centaury species by its persisting basal rosette and perennial rootstock.

The species produces dense clusters of pink to rose-pink flowers in late summer, attracting butterflies and bees.

Centaurium scilloides is a coastal specialist, largely restricted to cliff tops, sand dunes, and maritime grasslands.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Centaurium scilloides was described by Carl von Linné the Younger in 1782.

The species belongs to Centaurium section Centaurium, characterized by perennial habit and cymose inflorescences.

It grows 10–30 cm tall with erect flowering stems arising from a persistent basal rosette.

Basal leaves are obovate to spathulate, 1–3 cm long, forming a tight evergreen rosette.

Stem leaves are smaller, ovate to lanceolate, and clasping at the base.

Flowers are pink to rose-pink, 8–12 mm in diameter, with five spreading corolla lobes.

The calyx is shorter than the corolla tube, with narrow lanceolate lobes.

Flowering peaks from July to September, with seed capsules maturing in October.

## Distribution and Habitat

The species occurs along the Atlantic coasts of Britain, Ireland, France, Spain, and Portugal.

In Britain, it is concentrated in southwest England, Wales, and scattered sites in Scotland.

It grows in short maritime grassland, cliff ledges, sand dune slacks, and coastal heath.

Centaurium scilloides prefers well-drained, neutral to slightly acidic sandy or loamy soils.

Populations are typically small and localized, restricted to areas with minimal competition from taller vegetation.

The species benefits from moderate grazing that maintains open turf conditions.

In Ireland, populations occur along the western and southern coasts, from Donegal to Cork.

Some inland populations exist in western Ireland on limestone pavement, representing relict coastal occurrences.

## Ecology

Flowers are visited by a range of pollinators including hoverflies, small bees, and butterflies.

The perennial rootstock allows plants to survive winter and regenerate from the base each spring.

Centaurium scilloides acts as an indicator species for species-rich maritime grassland communities.

Associated species include Thymus polytrichus, [[clay-pellet-seed-dispersal]] is primarily by wind, with small seeds released from dehiscent capsules in autumn.

Germination in the wild typically occurs in spring, with seedlings establishing in bare patches of open turf.

The species shows some tolerance to salt spray, an adaptation shared with other maritime gentian relatives.

## Cultivation

The species is suitable for rock gardens, gravel gardens, and coastal-themed plantings.

It requires full sun and well-drained, poor to moderately fertile soil.

Centaurium scilloides is hardy to approximately USDA zone 7, tolerating light frost but not prolonged wet freezing.
