# 5,6-MDO-DIPT

5,6-Methylenedioxy-N,N-Diisopropyltryptamine

## Chemical Identity

5,6-MDO-DIPT is N,N-diisopropyl-5,6-methylenedioxytryptamine, a synthetic tryptamine carrying both a methylenedioxy bridge across the 5 and 6 positions of the indole ring and diisopropyl substituents on the side chain nitrogen.

The full chemical name is 3-[2-(diisopropylamino)ethyl]-5,6-methylenedioxyindole. The compound is also known as 5H-1,3-dioxolo-[4,5-f]indole-7-ethaneamine, N,N-diisopropyl. The molecular framework combines the methylenedioxy substitution pattern (famous from the phenethylamine series with compounds like MDA and MDMA) with the diisopropyl nitrogen substitution that has proven effective in compounds like [[dipt-entity-tihkal]] and [[4-ho-dipt-entity-tihkal]].

The free base crystallizes with a melting point of 93-94 degrees Celsius.

5,6-MDO-DIPT is documented in [[tihkal-introduction-and-methodology]] as entry #29. It has not been tested in humans at active levels, and its pharmacological activity remains unknown.

## Dosage and Administration

Dosage is unknown. The compound has not been explored up to an active level in human subjects.

Duration is unknown.

No qualitative comments from human trials are available.

## Pharmacology and Rationale

Despite the lack of human testing data, 5,6-MDO-DIPT represents an "appealing target" in the systematic exploration of tryptamine structure-activity relationships. The compound is part of a "methylenedioxy trilogy" that includes [[5-6-mdo-dmt-entity-tihkal]] and [[5-6-mdo-mipt-entity-tihkal]], all carrying the 5,6-methylenedioxy substitution but with different nitrogen substitution patterns.

The rationale for investigating this series comes from a well-established pattern in tryptamine chemistry. With both unsubstituted and 5-methoxy-substituted tryptamines, the progression from dimethyl to methylisopropyl to diisopropyl nitrogen substituents produces a characteristic pattern of activity: the dimethyl compound is quite potent but requires parenteral administration, the methylisopropyl compound is highly potent and orally active, and the diisopropyl compound is also quite potent and orally active.

If this pattern were maintained with the 5,6-methylenedioxy substitution, 5,6-MDO-DIPT (as the diisopropyl member) would be expected to be orally active. However, the 6-position substitution appears to be detrimental to tryptamine activity based on other evidence, and none of the three 5,6-methylenedioxy compounds have shown significant activity in preliminary testing.

The methylenedioxy bridge is a structural feature of great importance in the phenethylamine world, where it produces the characteristic activity of MDA and MDMA. Transposing this feature to the tryptamine skeleton was an obvious experimental question, but the results suggest that the 5,6-position may not be optimal for this substitution in the tryptamine series.

## Synthesis

The synthesis of 5,6-MDO-DIPT begins with 5,6-methylenedioxyindole (see [[5-6-mdo-mipt-entity-tihkal]] for its preparation). The indole is dissolved in anhydrous diethyl ether and cooled in an external ice bath. Oxalyl chloride in ether is added dropwise over 20 minutes, forming the glyoxyl chloride as red crystals which are collected by filtration, washed with ether, and dried under vacuum.

The crude acid chloride is dissolved in anhydrous tetrahydrofuran, cooled under nitrogen to 0 degrees Celsius, and treated with a diethyl ether solution of diisopropylamine until the reaction mixture remains basic (pH greater than 9 to external pH paper). After removal of solvents under vacuum, the residue is partitioned between water and chloroform, and the organic phase is dried over anhydrous magnesium sulfate.

The residue is recrystallized from ethyl acetate and hexane to yield 5,6-methylenedioxy-N,N-diisopropylglyoxylamide with a melting point of 278-280 degrees Celsius in 38% yield.

The glyoxylamide is reduced with lithium aluminum hydride in dry tetrahydrofuran under reflux for 2 hours. After careful hydrolysis and workup, the residue is distilled by Kugelrohr and the colorless distillate recrystallized from diethyl ether and hexane to give 5,6-MDO-DIPT with a melting point of 93-94 degrees Celsius in 60% yield.

## Related Compounds

5,6-MDO-DIPT is part of a series of three 5,6-methylenedioxy N,N-disubstituted tryptamines:

- [[5-6-mdo-dmt-entity-tihkal]] - the N,N-dimethyl member
- [[5-6-mdo-mipt-entity-tihkal]] - the N-methyl-N-isopropyl member
- 5,6-MDO-DIPT - the N,N-diisopropyl member (this entry)

Three additional heterocyclic analogues have been described but not tested in humans:

- 5,6-MDO-pyr-T (pyrrolidine analogue, mp 110-112 degrees Celsius)

- 5,6-MDO-pip-T (piperidine analogue, mp 150-152 degrees Celsius)

- 5,6-MDO-mor-T (morpholine analogue, mp 117-119 degrees Celsius)

None of these three heterocyclic compounds has ever been administered to human subjects.

The 4,5-methylenedioxy isomer series, including [[4-5-mdo-dipt-entity-tihkal]], represents the alternative positioning of the methylenedioxy bridge and may hold more promise for psychoactivity given the importance of the 4- and 5-positions in tryptamine pharmacology.

## Commentary on the 5,6-Position

The 5,6-methylenedioxy substitution pattern on tryptamines has been largely disappointing. While the 5-position is one of the two aromatic positions associated with psychedelic activity in tryptamines (the other being the 4-position), the presence of a substituent at the 6-position appears to significantly reduce or eliminate psychoactivity.

This stands in contrast to the phenethylamine world, where the methylenedioxy bridge between the 3 and 4 positions of the benzene ring (corresponding roughly to the 5,6-positions of the indole ring) produces the profoundly active compounds MDA and MDMA. The positional analogy between the two molecular families does not hold for this particular substitution pattern.
