# Acremonium lolii

## Description

*Acremonium lolii* (now reclassified as *Neotyphodium lolii*) is a fungal
endophyte most commonly associated with perennial ryegrass (*Lolium perenne*).
It is a member of the order Clavicipitales and grows systemically within the
tissues of its grass host without causing visible symptoms of disease. The
fungus is transmitted vertically through host seeds, ensuring that each new
generation of grass is colonized from germination.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Sordariomycetes
- **Order:** Hypocreales (Clavicipitales sensu lato)
- **Family:** Clavicipitaceae
- **Genus:** *Acremonium* (sect. *Albo-lanosa*), reclassified to *Neotyphodium*
- **Species:** *A. lolii* / *N. lolii*

## Key Facts

- Grows intercellularly within the leaves, stems, and seeds of perennial
  ryegrass (*Lolium perenne*), never forming external fruiting structures.
- Produces a suite of alkaloid secondary metabolites, including lolitrem B
  (a neurotoxin responsible for ryegrass staggers in grazing livestock),
  peramine (an insect feeding deterrent), and ergovaline.
- The presence of *A. lolii* in ryegrass confers strong resistance to a range
  of insect herbivores, including the Argentine stem weevil
  (*Listronotus bonariensis*) and various aphid species.
- Vertical transmission via infected seeds ensures near-100% colonization rates
  in commercial ryegrass cultivars when endophyte-positive seed is planted.
- The relationship is considered unconditionally mutualistic: the host plant
  gains antiherbivore defense and potential drought tolerance, while the fungus
  gains a protected habitat and reliable transmission to offspring.
- Clay (1990) identified *A. lolii* as one of approximately 100 host-specific
  *Acremonium* endophyte species in grasses, with effects including increased
  vegetative growth and enhanced herbivore resistance.
- Wilson (1993) showed that the improved fitness conferred by alkaloid
  production has led to vertical transmission of the endophyte from plant to
  plant via seed, so all new plants can start life with the endophyte.

## Ecological Role

- Alters the palatability of host grass tissue to mammalian and insect
  herbivores through alkaloid production.
- Can influence grassland community composition by providing a competitive
  advantage to infected plants over uninfected conspecifics and other species.
- The endophyte-grass association represents a model system for studying the
  continuum between mutualism and parasitism in plant-fungal interactions.
- Table 5.11 in the source shows endophyte effects reducing feeding,
  oviposition, survival, and development rate across multiple insect species
  including *Rhopalosiphum padi*, *Spodoptera frugiperda*, and *Crambus* spp.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- In pasture management, endophyte-positive ryegrass is valued for persistence
  and pest resistance, but lolitrem B toxicity poses risks to grazing livestock.
- Selective breeding programs have developed "novel endophyte" strains that
  retain insect deterrence (e.g., peramine production) while eliminating or
  reducing livestock-toxic alkaloids.
- Understanding *A. lolii* biology is critical for forage crop improvement and
  for broader studies of fungal endophyte ecology and evolution.
- The species exemplifies how fungal secondary metabolism can shape multitrophic
  interactions across grasslands, affecting plants, herbivores, and the food web.
