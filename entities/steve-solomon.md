# Steve Solomon

Steve Solomon is a garden writer, seed saver, and advocate for nutrient-dense food production. He founded the Territorial Seed Company in 1979 and authored several influential gardening books including "Growing Vegetables West of the Cascades" and "Gardening When It Counts."

## Background

- Founded Territorial Seed Company (1979), one of the first regional seed companies focused on varieties adapted to the Pacific Northwest
- Later left the seed business to focus on writing and soil research
- Spent years studying William Albrecht's work on soil mineral balancing
- Lived and gardened extensively in Oregon, then moved to Tasmania, Australia

## Core Contributions

### Complete Organic Fertilizer (COF)
Solomon's most widely adopted contribution is his COF recipe — a complete mineral fertilizer using organic ingredients:

- Seed meal + lime + bone meal + kelp meal
- Provides all 13 essential plant nutrients, not just NPK
- Designed to be mixed at home and applied at modest rates
- See [[nutrient-dense-gardening]] for the full recipe

### Nutrient-Dense Food Movement
- Argued that modern organic gardening focuses too much on NPK and compost
- Championed the idea that trace mineral deficiencies in soil produce nutritionally poor food
- Promoted brix testing as a simple way to measure produce quality
- Connected soil mineralization to pest resistance, taste, and storage life

### Regional Gardening
- "Growing Vegetables West of the Cascades" was groundbreaking in addressing the specific challenges of maritime Pacific Northwest gardening (cool summers, dry summers, acid soils)
- Emphasized that gardening advice must be adapted to local climate and soil conditions

### Irrigation Philosophy
- In "Gardening When It Counts," Solomon argues that intensive raised-bed gardening with frequent irrigation creates dependent, shallow-rooted plants
- Advocates wider spacing, less frequent but deeper watering, and drought-adapted varieties
- Believes gardeners should prepare for economic uncertainty by learning to grow food with minimal inputs

## Key Books

1. **Growing Vegetables West of the Cascades** — Regional guide for PNW gardeners
2. **Gardening When It Counts: Growing Food in Hard Times** — Low-input, high-nutrient gardening
3. **The Intelligent Gardener: Growing Nutrient-Dense Food** — Complete mineralization approach (with Steve Tekulsky)
4. **Water-Wise Vegetables** — Drought-resistant gardening methods
5. **Seed to Seed** — Seed saving techniques (contributor/related work)

## Philosophy

Solomon's approach combines several threads:

1. **Self-reliance** — Garden as if inputs (fertilizer, water, seeds) might become scarce
2. **Scientific rigor** — Use soil tests, measure results, don't rely on dogma
3. **Nutritional focus** — The point of gardening is nutritious food, not just yield
4. **Regional adaptation** — What works in one climate may fail in another
5. **Mineral completeness** — Plants need 13 nutrients, not just three

## Criticisms and Debates

- Some permaculture advocates disagree with his skepticism about raised beds and intensive spacing
- His criticism of John Jeavons' biointensive method sparked ongoing debate
- Some organic purists object to including any mined minerals (rock phosphate, lime) in "organic" systems
- His low-irrigation approach may not suit all climates
