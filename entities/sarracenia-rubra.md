## Overview

**Sarracenia rubra**, commonly known as the sweet pitcher [[query-what-is-the-difference-between-aerobic-compost-tea-and-fermented-plant-extracts]] or red pitcher plant, is a carnivorous perennial in the family Sarraceniaceae.

It is native to the southeastern [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]], where it grows in nutrient-poor wetlands and bogs.

The species is valued by carnivorous plant collectors for its slender, brightly colored pitchers and fragrant flowers.

Several subspecies have been recognized, each adapted to specific geographic regions within its range.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:** Kingdom Plantae → Order Ericales → Family Sarraceniaceae → Genus *Sarracenia* → *S. rubra*.

The species was first described by Thomas Walter in 1788 in his *Flora Caroliniana*.

**Pitchers** are narrow and tubular, typically 20–45 cm tall and 2–4 cm in diameter at the mouth.

The pitcher tube is green to reddish, often with pronounced red venation and a hooded apex that arches over the opening.

A prominent wing (alata) runs along the front of the tube from base to near the opening.

The interior surface is [[query-how-do-i-choose-between-a-cold-frame-row-cover-and-low-tunnel]]ed with downward-pointing hairs that prevent prey from escaping.

**Phyllodia** (flat, non-carnivorous leaves) are produced in late summer and persist through winter.

These are sickle-shaped and serve a photosynthetic function during the dormant season.

**Flowers** emerge on scapes 30–50 cm tall before new pitchers in spring.

The flowers are approximately 4–6 cm in diameter, with deep red to maroon petals and an unusual umbrella-like style.

Flowers are mildly fragrant, often described as similar to cherry or violet.

Five subspecies are currently recognized: subsp. *rubra*, subsp. *gulfensis*, subsp. *wherryi*, subsp. *alabamensis*, and subsp. *jonesii*.

Subsp. *alabamensis* and subsp. *jonesii* are critically endangered and federally protected.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Sarracenia rubra* is found across the southeastern [[dmt-maoi-plants-united-states]], from southeastern Virginia south to Florida and west to eastern Louisiana.

Each subspecies occupies a more restricted range within this broader distribution.

Subsp. *rubra* occurs in the coastal plain from [[north-carolina-mycodiversity]] to Georgia [[query-what-is-the-difference-between-biointensive-and-no-till-gardening]]rthern Florida.

Subsp. *gulfensis* is found in the Florida panhandle and adjacent Alabama.

Subsp. *wherryi* ranges from southwestern Georgia to the Florida panhandle.

Subsp. *alabamensis* is restricted to a few sites in central Alabama.

Subsp. *jonesii* is limited to mountain bogs in western North Carolina and upstate South Carolina.

The species inhabits sunny, open wetlands including bogs, seepage slopes, and [[query-best-cover-crops-for-sandy-soil]] wet savannas.

It requires consistently moist, acidic soils low in nutrients, particularly nitrogen and phosphorus.

## Cultivation

*S. rubra* is considered one of the easier Sarracenia species for cultivation, suitable [[query-what-is-the-best-cannabis-grow-medium-for-beginners]].

It requires a dormancy period of 3–5 months with temperatures [[query-difference-between-white-rot-and-brown-rot-fungi]] 2–10°C.

Plants should be grown in a peat-based medium mixed with sand or [[query-what-is-the-difference-between-perlite-and-vermiculite]] for drainage.

The standard mix is approximately 1:1 sphagnum [[query-what-is-the-difference-between-coco-coir-and-peat-moss]] to silica sand or horticultural perlite.

Plants should be watered with rainwater, distilled water, or reverse osmosis water to avoid mineral buildup.
