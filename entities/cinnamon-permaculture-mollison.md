# Cinnamon (Cinnamomum verum) — Permaculture Greenhouse Production

## Description

Cinnamon is a tropical evergreen tree that Mollison identifies as an ideal greenhouse crop for temperate permaculture systems. It is easily propagated from cuttings, coppices readily (producing four new shoots for every stem cut), and requires only about one square foot of pot space to produce enough cinnamon for an entire neighborhood. Mollison argues that growing cinnamon locally is a moral imperative to reduce exploitation of third-world peasant economies.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Family**: Lauraceae
- **Genus**: Cinnamomum
- **Species**: C. verum (true cinnamon)
- **Common Names**: Cinnamon, Ceylon cinnamon

## Key Facts

- **Propagation**: Grows readily from cuttings — cut a cinnamon stick and four more shoots emerge.
- **Coppicing**: Operates on a two-year cycle — harvest a stem and four replacements grow back.
- **Space efficiency**: A single pot taking up about one square foot in a greenhouse can produce cinnamon for an entire local area.
- **Roadside plant**: A common roadside plant in near-tropical regions, indicating its ease of cultivation.
- **Multiple products**: Bark is the cinnamon spice; leaves can be distilled for cinnamon oil; leaves serve as high-value fertilizer.
- **Cold tolerance**: More cold-hardy than typically assumed; can be grown in sheltered positions in mild temperate climates.

## Greenhouse Production

- Grow in a pot in the greenhouse occupying minimal space.
- Harvest stems on a two-year rotation.
- Distill leaves for essential oils as a secondary product.
- Use spent leaves as high-value mulch and fertilizer.

## Ethical Argument

Mollison positions cinnamon alongside coffee, tea, ginger, and vanilla as crops that temperate-zone consumers import at great human cost:

- Third-world peasant economies are converted from food self-sufficiency to export production.
- Growing these crops in home greenhouses directly reduces demand for exploitative import systems.
- One cinnamon plant can supply 100 households, making neighborhood-scale production practical.
