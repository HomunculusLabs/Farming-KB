## Overview

**Calvatia cyathiformis**, commonly known as the **purple-spored puffball** or **pestle puffball**, is a saprotrophic gasteroid fungus in the family Lycoperdaceae. It is distinguished from other puffballs by its prominent purple-brown spore mass at maturity, a feature that makes field identification straightforward once the gleba is exposed.

The species fruits singly or in small groups on soil in grasslands, lawns, and disturbed ground. It is widely distributed across [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-central-america-caribbean]], and has been reported in Australia.

The shape ranges from roughly spherical to pear-shaped or inverted-conical (pestle-shaped), giving rise to the common name.

The outer surface (peridium) is smooth to finely warted when young, white to cream-colored, becoming tan to brownish with age. It dehisces irregularly at maturity to release spores.

The gleba is firm and white when immature, gradually turning yellowish, then olive-gray, and finally a distinctive deep purple-brown as spores mature. This purple gleba is the key diagnostic feature.

Spores are spherical to broadly ellipsoidal, 5–8 µm in diameter, ornamented with fine spines or warts, and purple-brown in mass deposit. Capillitial threads are slender, branched, and septate.

## Culinary and Foraging Notes

Immature Calvatia cyathiformis with firm white gleba is considered edible
and choice by many foragers, with a mild flavor and pleasant texture when
sautéed or used in soups. The key foraging rule is to only collect
specimens with entirely white, firm interior flesh. Any specimen showing
yellow, olive, or purple discoloration of the gleba should be avoided,
as it is either past its prime or may harbor developing spores and
compounds that can cause gastrointestinal distress. Some foragers
consider the flavor superior to the giant puffball when prepared
fresh, noting a mild, earthy taste that pairs well with butter
and simple seasonings. The texture is firm and slightly spongy
when cooked, holding its shape better than many other puffballs.

## Seasonality

Calvatia cyathiformis typically fruits from late summer through autumn,
with peak fruiting occurring from August to November in eastern North
America. The species prefers warm, humid conditions and often appears
after heavy rainfall. In southern portions of its range, fruiting may
extend into December during mild, wet years. Individual fruiting bodies
persist for several days to a week before dehiscing to release spores.
In warmer southern regions, a smaller secondary fruiting may occur
in spring following heavy rains, though autumn remains the primary
fruiting season across most of the species' range.
