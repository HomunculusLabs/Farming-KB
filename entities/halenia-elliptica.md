## Taxonomy and Morphology

Halenia elliptica was first described by David Don in 1836 based on [[psilocybe-zapotecorum-section-zapotecorum-americas-pacific-distribution-guzman-1998]].

The species grows 20–60 cm tall with erect, simple or branched stems that are quadrangular in cross-[[comparison-white-rot-fungi-vs-brown-rot-fungi-biodegradation]] seeds.

Blooming occurs from July to September, with fruiting following in September and October.

It is commonly found in disturbed ground and along trails at high elevations.

Populations in the eastern Himalayas tend to be more robust than those in drier Tibetan plateau habitats.

## Chemistry and Pharmacology

Halenia elliptica contains xanthones and secoiridoid glycosides characteristic of the Gentianaceae family.

Major xanthones include halenin, 1-hydroxy-3,5,8-trimethoxyxanthone, and bellidifolin with anti-inflammatory activity.

Secoiridoid glycosides include sweroside and amarogentin, bitter compounds with choleretic effects.

Extracts of the whole plant have shown antioxidant activity in DPPH and ABTS assays.

Anti-inflammatory activity has been demonstrated in carrageenan-induced paw edema models in rats.

Clinical validation in humans remains limited, with most evidence from in vitro and animal model studies.

## Ethnobotanical Uses

In traditional Chinese medicine, Halenia elliptica is known as *huā zhǎn huā* (花盏花) and classified as a liver-clearing herb.

Tibetan medicine practitioners use the whole plant to treat jaundice, hepatitis, and digestive disorders.

The bitter aerial parts are prepared as decoctions or tinctures for stimulating appetite and promoting bile flow.
