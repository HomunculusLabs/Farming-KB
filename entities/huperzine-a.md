## Overview

**Huperzine A** is a naturally occurring sesquiterpene [[aminopropyl-silica-gel-chromatography-indole-alkaloid-purification]] and potent acetylcholinesterase inhibitor isolated from the club moss *Huperzia serrata* (Thunb. ex Murray) Trevis.

It has been used for centuries in traditional Chinese medicine under the name **Qian Ceng Ta** (千层塔, "thousand-layer pagoda").

The compound is one of the most potent naturally occurring cholinesterase inhibitors known, with reversible binding affinity comparable to synthetic pharmaceutical agents used in Alzheimer's [[ingham-root-disease-treatment]].

## Chemical Properties

Huperzine A has the molecular formula **C₁₅H₁₈N₂O** and a molecular weight of **242.32 g/mol**.

Its IUPAC name is (1R,9S,11E,13S,14R,16R,17S)-14-acetyl-2,3,10,11,12,13,14,15,16,17-decahydro-1-methoxy-5H-17-methyl-13-ethyl-5,9-diazatetracyclo[9.4.0.0¹,⁸.0¹⁵,¹⁷]pentadeca-11-ene-4,6-dione.

The compound features a complex tetracyclic structure classified as a **lycopodium alkaloid**, a group of nitrogen-containing natural products derived from lycophyte plants.

It is sparingly soluble in water but dissolves readily in chloroform, methanol, and DMSO. The stereochemistry is critical for biological activity — only the (-)-enantiomer exhibits significant acetylcholinesterase inhibition.

Synthetic routes exist but remain complex due to the dense ring system and multiple stereocenters. Commercial production relies largely on extraction from cultivated *Huperzia serrata* biomass.

## Pharmacology

Huperzine A acts as a **reversible, competitive acetylcholinesterase (AChE) inhibitor** with an IC₅₀ of approximately 82 nM for electric eel AChE, making it roughly 10× more potent than [[galantamine]] and comparable to donepezil.

Unlike many AChE inhibitors, huperzine A shows high selectivity for AChE over butyrylcholinesterase (BuChE), which reduces peripheral cholinergic side effects.

It also exhibits **neuroprotective activity** through multiple mechanisms: NMDA receptor antagonism, reduction of [[blesching-cannabis-oxidative-stress-and-antioxidant-defense]] via mitochondrial pathways, and attenuation of amyloid-beta-induced neurotoxicity.

Animal studies demonstrate that huperzine A can reduce neuronal apoptosis, improve synaptic plasticity, and upregulate nerve growth factor (NGF) expression in the hippocampus.

## Effects and Dosage

In human [[mushroom-cancer-clinical-trials-overview]], huperzine A has shown significant improvements in memory, cognition, and behavioral function in patients with Alzheimer's disease and vascular dementia.

Common supplemental doses range from **50–200 μg/day**, typically divided into two doses taken with meals. Cognitive enhancement in healthy individuals has been reported at 50–100 μg/day.

The onset of cognitive effects is gradual, typically requiring 2–4 weeks of consistent use before noticeable improvement.

Duration of action is approximately 8–10 hours, longer than many synthetic AChE inhibitors, which allows for less frequent dosing.

Common side effects at standard doses are mild and include nausea, dizziness, and mild gastrointestinal discomfort. At higher doses, bradycardia, muscle cramps, and excessive salivation may occur.

## Clinical Research

Multiple randomized controlled trials have evaluated huperzine A in Alzheimer's disease. A 2018 Cochrane-style meta-analysis of 20 trials (n = 1,824) found significant improvement in MMSE scores compared to placebo at 12 and 16 weeks.

Effects on vascular dementia have also been studied, with moderate evidence for improved cognitive function and activities of daily living in patients with cerebrovascular disease.

Comparative trials against donepezil show roughly equivalent efficacy with a more favorable side-effect profile, particularly regarding gastrointestinal tolerance and sleep disturbance.

Some evidence suggests huperzine A may benefit cognitive performance in healthy young adults, though sample sizes are small and results require replication.

## Legal Status

Huperzine A is available as a **dietary supplement** in the [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]], Canada, the UK, and most of the EU under the category of nootropic compounds.

It is classified as a prescription drug in China, where it is approved under the brand name **Shuangyiping** for the treatment of Alzheimer's disease.
