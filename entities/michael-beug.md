# Michael W. Beug

Michael W. Beug is an American mycologist and chemist at The Evergreen State College (Olympia, Washington), known for his collaborative analytical work with [[jeremy-bigwood]] on psilocybin and psilocin quantification, and for his extensive contributions to mushroom toxicity education and identification.

## Key Research

### Psilocybin and Psilocin Quantification (with Bigwood)

**Beug & Bigwood (1982):** "Psilocybin and Psilocin Levels in Twenty Species from Seven Genera of Wild Mushrooms in the Pacific Northwest, U.S.A." (*Journal of Ethnopharmacology* 5:271-285) — The first systematic quantitative survey of psychoactive alkaloid levels across multiple species of wild mushrooms. Surveyed 20 species from 7 genera, establishing baseline potency data.

**Bigwood & Beug (1982):** "Variation of Psilocybin and Psilocin Levels with Repeated Flushes" (*Journal of Ethnopharmacology* 5:287-291) — Demonstrated that psilocybin levels vary unpredictably across flushes while psilocin builds from zero to maximum by the fourth flush. Found 4× variation in controlled cultures and 10× variation in street samples.


### Key Findings from Flush Variation Study

- **Psilocybin:** Varied unpredictably across flushes (4× range in controlled cultures, 10× range in street samples), generally similar in first and last flush
- **Psilocin:** Typically absent in first 1-2 flushes, reached maximum by fourth flush, then declined — suggesting biosynthetic maturation of the fungus
- **Cap vs stem distribution:** Caps contained ~2× the psilocybin of stems; small amounts of psilocin present were entirely in stems (in some strains)
- **Amazon strain:** Trace of psilocin in cap but not in stem; equal psilocybin in cap and stem
- **Street samples:** Highly variable psilocybin (0.7–6.2 mg/g), consistently low psilocin — users cannot predict dosage from dry weight
- **Methods:** Rye-grain miniculture in half-pint jars, cased with peat/calcium carbonate/perlite; 4-5 weeks from inoculation to first flush; average 2.7g dry weight per jar; continued fruiting 20+ weeks

### Species Survey (20 species, 7 genera)

- Surveyed Pacific Northwest wild mushrooms for psilocybin and psilocin levels using HPLC calibrated against NIDA standards
- Confirmed *Psilocybe semilanceata* as the most potent Pacific Northwest species (up to 0.93% psilocybin in Northwestern US samples)
- *Conocybe cyanopus* contained 0.33–0.55% psilocybin with trace psilocin (confirmed by Norwegian researchers 1982/83)
### Earlier Biosynthetic Work

Co-authored "Psilocin, Bufotenine and Serotonin: Historical and Biosynthetic Observations" (Chilton, Bigwood & Beug 1979, *Journal of Psychedelic Drugs* 11:61-69) — tracing the biosynthetic relationships between these indole compounds.

## Mushroom Toxicity Expertise

Beug has been a prominent educator on mushroom poisoning and identification safety. His photographic contributions appear in [[paul-stamets]]'s *Psilocybin Mushrooms of the World*. He has served as a resource for poison control centers and mycological societies on the identification of toxic mushroom species.

## Analytical Methods

Beug's work employed reversed-phase HPLC calibrated against National Institute on Drug Abuse standards, with TLC confirmation using butanol-acetic acid-water (12:3:5). This methodology became a standard for psychoactive mushroom analysis.
