# 2C-I

2C-I is a psychedelic phenethylamine and a member of the 2C family of compounds.

It is the 4-iodo analogue of 2,5-dimethoxyphenethylamine, first synthesized and bioassayed by [[alexander-shulgin]] and documented as entry #33 in PiHKAL.

## Chemical Identity

Full chemical name: 2,5-Dimethoxy-4-iodophenethylamine.

Molecular formula: C10H15INO2.

The compound belongs to the 2C family (2-carbon phenethylamines) and is structurally analogous to 2c-b-entity|2C-B, with an iodine atom replacing the bromine at the 4-position of the aromatic ring.

Iodine sits directly below bromine in the mollison soil elements, and Shulgin noted that the two compounds share many qualitative similarities while maintaining distinct pharmacological profiles.

## Dosage and Duration

Effective oral dosage: 14 - 22 mg.

Duration of effects: 6 - 10 hours.

The compound has a relatively slow onset, with effects typically becoming noticeable within 40-60 minutes and peaking around the 2-hour mark.

The comedown is often described as abrupt, with effects dropping off sharply around the 5.5 to 6-hour mark.

## Qualitative Effects

At threshold and low doses (14-16 mg), users report comfortable onset with enhanced color perception and a general sense of well-being.

Visual patterning is commonly described as Persian carpet-like, similar to 2c-b-entity|2C-B, with vivid and active colors.

At moderate doses (16-20 mg), effects intensify to include clear visual distortions, enhanced appreciation of art and music, and a sense of emotional openness and communicative clarity.

Body load is generally reported as minimal, with no significant nausea, tremor, or discomfort.

Users consistently describe enhanced sensory perception without the physical tension sometimes associated with 2c-e-entity|2C-E or 2c-b-entity|2C-B.

The compound is frequently noted for being "good on the body" with a gentle transition period.

## Synthesis

The synthesis described in PiHKAL proceeds through several steps.

2,5-Dimethoxyphenethylamine (the free base of 2C-H) is first converted to its N-phthalimide derivative by heating with phthalic anhydride.

The phthalimide is then iodinated using iodine monochloride in acetic acid, introducing the iodine atom at the activated 4-position.

The N-phthalimide protecting group is removed with hydrazine hydrate to yield 2C-I as the hydrochloride salt.

The final product is obtained as white microcrystalline solid with a melting point of 246-247 degrees Celsius.

## Pharmacological Context

2C-I is one of the most widely known and used members of the 2C family.

It was among the last of the 2C compounds to gain significant popularity as a research chemical, with widespread availability in the early 2000s.

Like other members of the DOX and 2C families, 2C-I acts primarily as a 5-HT2A receptor agonist, though its full pharmacological profile may involve activity at other serotonin receptor subtypes.

The heavy iodine atom makes 2C-I and its 3-carbon analogue [[doi-entity]]|DOI natural candidates for radio-labelling experiments used in brain imaging and serotonin receptor mapping studies.

doi-entity|DOI (2,5-dimethoxy-4-iodoamphetamine) has been extensively used as a radioligand in neuroscience research.

## N-Hydroxy Analogue

Shulgin described a derivative called 2CI-2ETO (2-ethoxy-4-iodo-5-methoxyphenethylamine), known as "Iodotweetio."

This compound showed threshold activity at 5 mg but failed to produce effects beyond a plus-one level even at doses up to 50 mg, with the primary effect being a stretching of the threshold duration.

The limited activity of this homologue suggests the importance of the precise substitution pattern at the 2-position.

## Comparison with Related Compounds

2C-I is frequently compared with 2c-b-entity|2C-B due to their structural similarity and overlapping visual effects.

Users generally find 2C-I to have a longer duration, slightly less sensual character, and a more "head-oriented" experience compared to 2C-B.

The body load and physical discomfort associated with 2C-B transitions are typically less pronounced with 2C-I.

Compared to 2c-e-entity|2C-E, 2C-I is shorter-acting, less intense, and considered more forgiving in terms of both dosage sensitivity and physical side effects.

## Legal Status

2C-I is classified as a Schedule I controlled substance in the United States.

It is also controlled or banned in many other countries, including the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, and throughout much of the European Union.

The compound was specifically scheduled in the United States as part of the and psychedelic research Act amendments targeting 2C compounds.

## Harm Reduction

As with all [[optical-isomers-psychedelic-phenethylamines]], users should exercise careful dose measurement.

The relatively wide active dose range (14-22 mg) can lead to unexpectedly intense experiences at the upper end.

And setting considerations are important, particularly given the 6-10 hour duration.

2C-I is not recommended for individuals with a personal or family history of psychotic disorders or severe cardiovascular conditions.

The compound should not be combined with MAOIs, tramadol, or other serotonergic substances due to the risk of serotonin syndrome.

## Cultural Significance

2C-I became one of the most popular research chemicals in the early 2000s, widely discussed on online forums including the Bluelight community and Erowid.

Its relative and safety profile and predictable effects made it a common introduction to the 2C family for recreational users.

The compound's popularity contributed significantly to the eventual scheduling of the 2C family as a whole in multiple jurisdictions.
