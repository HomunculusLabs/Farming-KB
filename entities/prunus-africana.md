# Prunus africana

## Description

*Prunus africana* (also known as African cherry, red stinkwood, or Pygeum) is an
evergreen tree native to sub-Saharan Africa and the island of Madagascar. It is a
medium-to-large forest tree that can reach heights of 25-40 metres, characterised
by its dark brown to blackish bark, glossy dark green leaves, and small white or
cream flowers. The species is found across a range of forest types from highland
forests to montane and upper montane rainforests, typically at elevations between
1,000 and 2,500 metres.

The tree is of significant economic and medicinal importance. Its bark has been
harvested for centuries in traditional African medicine and more recently has become
a globally traded commodity for the pharmaceutical industry. The bark extract is used
in the treatment of benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH), a common condition in older
men, and has been the subject of considerable pharmaceutical research and commercial
exploitation.

In the [[preface-and-author-context-of-permaculture-designers-manual]] and agroforestry in Africa, *Prunus africana* is
identified as a valuable longer-lived, non-leguminous tree species that is being
encouraged as part of agroforestry systems alongside faster-growing leguminous
pioneer species.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Family**: Rosaceae
- **Genus**: Prunus
- **Species**: P. africana
- **Common Names**: African cherry, red stinkwood, Pygeum, iron wood
- **Native Range**: Sub-Saharan Africa, Madagascar
- **Habitat**: Montane and highland forests

## Key Facts

- The bark of *Prunus africana* contains phytosterols, pentacyclic triterpenes,
  and ferulic acid esters, compounds with documented anti-inflammatory and
  anti-androgenic properties used in treating prostate disorders.
- The species is threatened by overexploitation for its bark, which is harvested
  and sold as a medicinal remedy for prostate disorders on international markets.
- Bark harvesting, if done sustainably (stripping only portions of the trunk
  rather than girdling the tree), can allow recovery, but unsustainable practices
  have led to significant population declines.
- Listed on CITES Appendix II since 1995, regulating international trade in the
  species and its products.
- In agroforestry systems, it serves as a longer-lived, non-leguminous tree that
  provides long-term structure and shade in mixed planting systems.
- Research and extension work in Kenya is encouraging the planting of *Prunus
  africana* alongside fast-growing leguminous species to create multi-strata
  agroforestry systems that build soil fertility over time.
- The species represents an important case study in balancing medicinal plant
  commercialisation with conservation and sustainable management.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Agroforestry Integration**: As a longer-lived canopy tree, *Prunus africana*
  provides shade [[tree-temperature-effects-and-microclimate-modification]] that can benefit understory crops
  and fungal cultivation in agroforestry systems.
- **Soil Health**: The tree contributes to soil organic matter through leaf litter
  and [[root-exudates]], supporting [[endorhiza-rhizosphere-bulk-soil-microbial-communities]] including mycorrhizal
  fungi.
- **Mycorrhizal Associations**: As a member of the Rosaceae family, *Prunus
  africana* forms vesicular-arbuscular mycorrhizal (VAM) associations that
  enhance nutrient uptake and contribute to forest soil health.
- **Forest Ecosystem Function**: In its native habitat, the tree supports complex
  forest ecosystems where [[fungal-decomposition]] and [[mycorrhizal-networks]] play
  essential roles [[comparison-soil-protozoa-vs-nematodes-in-nutrient-cycling]].
- **Sustainable Harvesting**: The challenge of sustainable bark harvesting
  parallels sustainable management of fungal resources, requiring understanding
  of regeneration biology and long-term population dynamics.
- **Cultivation Potential**: Efforts to cultivate *Prunus africana* in agroforestry
  systems may reduce pressure on wild populations and provide structured habitats
  beneficial for [[classification-of-cultivated-mushroom-species]].
