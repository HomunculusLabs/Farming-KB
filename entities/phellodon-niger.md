## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Thelephorales
- **Family:** Bankeraceae
- **Genus:** Phellodon
- **Species:** P. niger
- **Authority:** (Fr.) P. Karst. (1881)

The basionym is Hydnum niger Fries (1821). The genus name Phellodon derives
from Greek "phellos" (cork) and "odon" (tooth), referring to the corky texture
of the teeth-bearing surface.

P. niger is closely related to P. melaleucus, with which it has sometimes been
confused, though P. niger is darker and typically associated with conifers.

## Morphology

The fruiting body is a tough, leathery, funnel-shaped to centrally depressed
basidiocarp measuring 3–8 cm in diameter.

The upper surface (pileus) is dark brown to black, smooth to finely felted,
often with concentrically zoned margins. The margin is initially inrolled and
may become wavy with age.

The undersurface bears crowded, decurrent teeth (spines) that are dark grey to
black, measuring 2–5 mm long. These teeth are the spore-producing hymenophore.

The flesh is tough, corky, and dark brown throughout, with no distinct zone
between pileus and hymenophore. Context is thin (1–2 mm).

The odor is strongly spicy, peppery, or like fenugreek, which is a key field
character for identification. The taste is acrid and peppery.

Spores are spherical to broadly ellipsoidal, ornamented with tuberculate
spines, measuring 4–5.5 μm in diameter. Spore print is white to pale buff.

## Distribution and Habitat

P. niger is widely distributed across temperate Europe, from Scandinavia to
the Mediterranean, and extends into parts of western Russia. Records from North
America exist but may represent a distinct species complex.

The fungus is mycorrhizal, forming obligate associations with coniferous trees
especially pine ([[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-networks]].
