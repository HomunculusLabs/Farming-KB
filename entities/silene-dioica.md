# Silene dioica (Red Campion)

## Overview

Silene dioica (L.) Clairv., commonly known as red campion, is a herbaceous perennial wildflower in the family Caryophyllaceae. It produces vivid rose-pink to magenta flowers on upright stems reaching 30–90 cm in height.

The species is native to Europe and western Asia, where it inhabits woodlands, hedgerows, and damp meadows. It is valued as an ornamental garden plant and serves as an important nectar source for long-tongued bumblebees and moths.

It is dioecious, with male and female flowers borne on separate plants. This sexual dimorphism is reflected in its specific epithet "dioica" and has made it a subject of study in plant [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]] worldwide. Recent molecular studies have supported the inclusion of former Lychnis species within an expanded Silene.

The species exhibits notable morphological variation across its range. Northern populations tend to be smaller and more compact, while southern populations produce taller, more robust stems with larger flowers.

The plant forms a basal rosette of ovate to heart-shaped leaves. Stem leaves are opposite and softly hairy, with a grey-green appearance.

Flowers are 18–25 mm in diameter with five deeply notched petals. Male flowers have ten stamens and a cylindrical calyx.

Female flowers possess five styles and an inflated calyx that becomes angular as the fruit develops. The fruit is an ovoid capsule containing numerous reniform seeds approximately 1.2–1.5 mm in diameter.

Seed production is prolific, with a single female plant capable of producing several thousand seeds per season. This high reproductive output contributes to the species' ability to colonize disturbed habitats rapidly.

The root system consists of a stout taproot with fibrous lateral roots. This allows the plant to persist as a short-lived perennial, typically surviving three to five years.

## Distribution and Habitat

Silene dioica is widely distributed across Europe from Iceland and Scandinavia south to the Mediterranean. Its range extends eastward through western Asia to the Altai Mountains.

Silene dioica hybridizes readily with the closely related white campion (Silene latifolia) where their ranges overlap. The resulting fertile hybrids display pale pink flowers and intermediate morphological traits.

This hybrid zone has been extensively studied as a model for speciation and reproductive isolation. Genetic introgression between the two species occurs preferentially in one direction due to cytoplasmic-nuclear interactions.

Seeds are dispersed primarily by wind. The capsule opens at the apex through ten recurved teeth, releasing seeds gradually over several weeks.

Ants also contribute to secondary seed dispersal through elaiosome attachment on some seeds. This dual dispersal mechanism allows colonization of both open and sheltered microsites within woodland habitats.

Plants typically flower from May through September, with peak bloom in June and July. The extended flowering period makes red campion a valuable resource for pollinators throughout the summer.

Foliage serves as a food plant for several moth species including the lychnis (Hadena bicruris) and campion (Sideridis rivularis) moths. These herbivore interactions contribute to the species' role in woodland food webs.

## Cultivation

Red campion is easily grown from seed sown in spring or autumn. Seeds require light for germination and should be surface-sown on moist compost at temperatures of 15–20 °C.

Germination typically occurs within 14–30 days. Plants flower in their second year from seed.

The species thrives in partially shaded positions with moist, well-drained soil. It is suitable for woodland gardens, wildflower meadows, and naturalistic plantings.

Cultivars are limited, though selections with deeper flower color and longer bloom periods have been developed. The plant self-seeds freely in favorable conditions and can form large colonies over time.
