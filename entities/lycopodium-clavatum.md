L. clavatum is a perennial, evergreen lycophyte with a creeping,
dichotomously branching rhizome that extends up to 3 meters or more.
Upright aerial shoots arise at intervals, reaching 10–30 cm in height.

The microphylls (leaves) are simple, lanceolate, 3–8 mm long, spirally
arranged, and densely overlapping along the stem. They are dark green,
lustrous above, and paler beneath.

Strobili (cone-like reproductive structures) are borne on unbranched
peduncles 5–20 cm long. Each strobilus is cylindrical, 2–5 cm long,
composed of sporophylls bearing kidney-shaped sporangia.

Spores are tetrahedral, approximately 25–40 μm in diameter, with a
distinctive three-branched tetrad scar (trilete mark). The spore surface
is finely reticulate. Spores are produced in enormous quantities — a
single strobilus can release millions of spores.
