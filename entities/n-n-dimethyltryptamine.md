# N,N-Dimethyltryptamine (DMT)

## Overview

**N,N-Dimethyltryptamine (DMT)** is a naturally occurring tryptamine alkaloid and powerful psychedelic compound found in numerous plants, animals, and potentially produced endogenously in the human body. First synthesized in 1931 by Canadian chemist Richard Manske and later isolated from *Anadenanthera peregrina* by Hungarian chemist Stephen Szára in 1955, DMT is renowned for producing intense, short-acting visionary experiences. It is the primary psychoactive component of **ayahuasca**, a traditional Amazonian entheogenic brew used for millennia in shamanic ceremonies.

## Chemical Properties

### Structure and Classification

- **IUPAC Name:** N,N-Dimethyl-1H-indole-3-ethanamine
- **Molecular Formula:** C₁₂H₁₆N₂
- **Molecular Weight:** 188.27 g/mol
- **Chemical Class:** Tryptamine (substituted indolethylamine)
- **CAS Number:** 61-50-7

DMT is structurally analogous to serotonin (5-HT), differing only by the addition of two methyl groups on the terminal amine. This structural similarity enables DMT's activity at serotonin receptors.

### Animal Sources

DMT has been detected in the following organisms:
- **Rat brain:** Trace levels in pineal gland, cerebral cortex, and retina
- **Human brain:** Detected in pineal gland, cerebral cortex (variable results)
- **Rabbit brain:** Detected in pineal tissue
- **Human blood, urine, and CSF:** Endogenous presence confirmed via GC-MS and LC-MS/MS
- **Bufo alvarius (Colorado River toad):** 5-MeO-DMT in venom glands (not DMT itself, but closely related)

### Endogenous Production Hypothesis

The presence of DMT in human tissues has led to the hypothesis that it may function as an endogenous neurotransmitter or neuromodulator:
- **Biosynthetic pathway:** Tryptophan → Tryptamine → N-methyltryptamine (NMT) → DMT
- **Enzymes:** Indolethylamine N-methyltransferase (INMT) catalyzes N-methylation; aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase (AADC) decarboxylates tryptophan
- **INMT expression:** Found in human pineal gland, retina, and cerebral cortex
- **The "Spirit Molecule" hypothesis (Strassman):** Proposed that the pineal gland may release DMT during extraordinary states such as near-death experiences (NDEs), mystical experiences, and REM sleep — remains speculative and controversial

## Pharmacology

### Receptor Binding Profile

| Receptor | Ki (nM) | Functional Activity |
|----------|---------|-------------------|
| 5-HT2A | 0.084–0.376 | Full agonist (primary psychedelic mechanism) |
| 5-HT2C | 0.14–0.46 | Full agonist |
| 5-HT1A | 0.107–1.1 | Agonist (contributing to anxiolytic effects) |
| 5-HT1B | 0.058–1.2 | Agonist |
| Sigma-1 receptor | 3.1–7.4 | Agonist (neuroprotective, anti-inflammatory) |
| TAAR1 | ~2.0 | Agonist |
| 5-HT2B | ~1.0 | Agonist |

DMT shows the highest affinity for 5-HT1B and 5-HT2A receptors among serotonin receptor subtypes. Its psychoactive effects are primarily mediated through 5-HT2A receptor activation in the prefrontal cortex, similar to other classical psychedelics (LSD, psilocybin, mescaline).

### Pharmacokinetics

| Parameter | Smoked/IV | Oral (with MAOI) |
|-----------|-----------|-----------------|
| Onset | 10–60 seconds | 20–60 minutes |
| Peak | 2–5 minutes | 60–120 minutes |
| Duration | 15–45 minutes | 2–6 hours |
| Bioavailability | ~45% (IV ~100%) | Variable (enhanced by MAOI) |
| Metabolism | MAO-A (first-pass, gut/liver) | MAO inhibited by β-carbolines |
| Elimination half-life | ~15 minutes (IV) | ~3 hours (oral) |
| Active metabolite | DMT-N-oxide | DMT-N-oxide |
**Route-dependent metabolism:**
- **Smoked/Vaporized:** Bypasses first-pass metabolism; rapid onset and intense peak
- **Intravenous:** Most controlled route; used in clinical research settings
- **Oral (alone):** Rapidly metabolized by monoamine oxidase-A (MAO-A) in the gut and liver; ~0% psychoactive bioavailability
- **Oral (with MAOI):** β-Carboline alkaloids (harmine, harmaline, tetrahydroharmine) from *Banisteriopsis caapi* inhibit MAO-A, allowing DMT to reach systemic circulation — this is the ayahuasca formulation

## Subjective Effects

### Psychological Effects

**Common experiences:**
- Rapid onset of intense visual hallucinations: geometric patterns, fractals, entity encounters
- Altered perception of time and space (time dilation, ego dissolution)
- Profound mystical or spiritual experiences
- Encounters with seemingly autonomous entities ("beings," "entities," "machine elves")
- Synesthesia (cross-sensory blending)
- Life-changing philosophical or personal insights
- Feelings of interconnectedness and unity
- Intense emotional experiences (awe, fear, joy, grief)

**Dosage-dependent effects (smoked/IV):**
| Dose (mg, smoked) | Intensity | Description |
|-------------------|-----------|-------------|
| 5–10 | Light | Mild visual distortion, body sensation |
| 10–20 | Moderate | Vivid geometric visuals, altered thought patterns |
| 20–40 | Strong | Complete immersive hallucination, ego dissolution |
| 40–60 | Breakthrough | Complete separation from consensus reality, entity contact |
| 60+ | Heroic | Maximum intensity; overwhelming visionary experience |

### Physiological Effects

- **Cardiovascular:** Transient hypertension (+20–40 mmHg systolic), tachycardia
- **Pupillary:** Mydriasis (pupil dilation)
- **Body temperature:** Mild increase (0.5–1°C)
- **Serum cortisol and prolactin:** Significant elevation during experience
- **Anti-inflammatory:** Reduced pro-inflammatory cytokines post-experience (ayahuasca studies)

## Clinical Research

### Current Therapeutic Investigations

| Application | Status | Key Findings |
|------------|--------|-------------|
| Treatment-resistant depression | Phase II trials (Imperial College London) | Rapid antidepressant effects within 24h; sustained 2+ weeks |
| Substance use disorders | Open-label studies | Reduced alcohol, tobacco, cocaine craving and use |
| PTSD | Phase I/II | Preliminary evidence of symptom reduction |
| End-of-life anxiety | Early-stage research | Reduced death anxiety and improved quality of life |
| Cluster headache | Self-report studies | Rapid relief reported by ~70% of users |

### Neuroimaging Findings

- **fMRI:** Decreased activity in the default mode network (DMN); increased global connectivity
- **MEG:** Increased signal diversity (higher entropy) correlated with subjective intensity
- **PET:** 5-HT2A receptor occupancy of ~60–80% at psychedelic doses
- **ASL-MRI:** Increased cerebral blood flow in anterior cingulate, insula, and hippocampus
- **EEG:** Decreased alpha power, increased theta and gamma oscillations

## Harm Reduction

### Physiological Safety
- **Lethal dose:** No confirmed human fatalities from DMT overdose alone
- **Cardiovascular risk:** Monitor blood pressure in at-risk individuals
- **Serotonin syndrome risk:** Theoretical with concurrent serotonergic medications (SSRIs, MAOIs, tramadol)
- **Pregnancy:** Contraindicated; β-carbolines in ayahuasca are uterotonic

### Psychological Safety
- **Set and setting:** Critical determinants of experience quality
- **Integration:** Post-experience processing recommended (therapy, journaling, community)
- **Screening:** Exclude individuals with personal/family history of psychosis, schizophrenia, or bipolar I disorder
- **Contrast sensitivity:** No evidence of persistent HPPD (Hallucinogen Persisting Perception Disorder) at typical doses, unlike LSD
- **Substance testing:** Always verify DMT purity and identity; synthetic DMT should be free of N,N-dimethylamide (safrole) and other synthesis byproducts
