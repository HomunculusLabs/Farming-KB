## Distribution and Habitat

Populus grandidentata ranges from Nova Scotia and New Brunswick west to southeastern Manitoba and Minnesota, and south through the Great Lakes region to Iowa, Missouri, and the mountains of [[savory-growing-season-and-non-growing-season-management]].

The root system of bigtooth aspen is shallow and widespread, with lateral roots extending 10–20 meters from the parent tree. These roots form interconnected networks within clones, allowing [[fukuoka-textdoc-microclimate-mite-scale-population-dynamics]] in the Great Lakes region. Beaver prefer aspen for [[jeavons-climate-adaptation-growing-seasons]].

## Cultivation and Uses

Bigtooth aspen is primarily managed for pulpwood production, where its rapid growth and relatively short rotation make it economically attractive. Pulp yields from aspen are comparable to softwood species, and the light-colored wood produces high-quality printing and writing paper.

Timber value is limited due to the species' small size, tendency to develop heartrot, and difficulty in seasoning without warping. However, aspen lumber finds use in pallets, crates, matchsticks, and engineered wood products. The wood has moderate strength-to-weight ratio and takes paint and stain well.

Aspen wood has a low density (approximately 380 kg/m³ at 12% moisture content) and an even, fine texture. These properties make it suitable for oriented strand board (OSB) production, where aspen has become a preferred feedstock. The wood is also used for animal bedding and as a [[grass-seed-substrate-for-mushroom-cultivation]] cultivation.

Silvicultural management typically employs clearcutting at 40–50 year rotations, relying on natural root suckering for stand regeneration. Pre-commercial thinning of dense suckering stands improves growth rates and reduces self-thinning mortality. The species responds well to fertilization on nutrient-poor sites.
