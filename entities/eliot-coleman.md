## Comparison with Heated Greenhouses

A key argument in Coleman"s work is the economic and ecological superiority of unheated winter growing over heated greenhouse production:

- **Energy costs**: Eliminating heating fuel eliminates one of the largest costs in conventional greenhouse operations.
- **Capital costs**: The simple hoop house structures he advocates cost a fraction of conventional glass greenhouses.
- **Crop quality**: Crops grown in cool conditions with natural light tend to be sweeter, more flavorful, and more nutritious than those grown in heated greenhouses.
- **Environmental impact**: The carbon footprint of unheated winter growing is minimal compared to importing produce from distant warm climates or growing in heated structures.
- **Simplicity**: The systems require less technical infrastructure and are accessible to small-scale growers.

Coleman acknowledges that heated greenhouses are necessary for warm-season crops like tomato and [[carrot-growing-guide]], parsnip, and kale.

## The 18-Mile Diet

Coleman and his wife, Barbara Damrosch, have championed the concept of eating locally and seasonally, demonstrating through their own lives that it is possible to eat a varied, nutritionally complete diet from local sources year-round in cold climates. Their farm supplies produce to local restaurants, stores, and a year-round farmers market in their community on the Blue Hill Peninsula in Maine.

## Greenhouse Design Specifications

The Winter Harvest Handbook provides detailed construction guidance for the movable greenhouse system:

- **Dimensions**: Typically 30 feet wide by 48 feet long, providing 1,440 square feet of growing space.
- **Structure**: Galvanized steel hoops anchored to a ground-level rail system, allowing the entire structure to be winched along tracks.
- **Covering**: Double-layer inflated polyethylene with an air gap providing insulation equivalent to a single glass layer.
- **Interior protection**: Wire-supported row covers of 1.25 oz spun-bonded polypropylene laid directly over crops within the greenhouse.
- **Cost**: Significantly lower than permanent glass greenhouse structures, with estimated construction costs a fraction of conventional heated greenhouse operations.

## Influence and Legacy

Coleman's work has had far-reaching impact on the local food movement and sustainable agriculture:

- He helped establish the Maine Organic Farmers and Gardeners Association (MOFGA), one of the oldest and largest state-level organic farming organizations in the United States.
- His techniques have been adopted by thousands of small-scale farmers across temperate climates worldwide, enabling local food production where it was previously considered impossible year-round.
