# 2C-B-FLY

## Overview / Summary
2C-B-FLY (2,5-dimethoxy-4-(2-fluoroethylthio)benzeneethylamine) is a synthetic psychedelic phenethylamine and a fluorinated analogue of 2C-B.
It belongs to the 2C family of substituted phenethylamines originally described by Alexander Shulgin in PiHKAL.
The "FLY" designation is borrowed from the difuranyl ring substitution pattern associated with Bromo-DragonFLY, though 2C-B-FLY itself has a simpler 2-fluoroethylthio substituent at the 4-position.
2C-B-FLY is known for producing vivid visual and cognitive effects with a longer duration than its parent compound 2C-B.
It emerged as a designer drug in the late 2000s and gained notoriety due to a fatal mislabeling incident in 2009.
Despite being less potent than Bromo-DragonFLY, 2C-B-FLY remains active at low milligram doses and carries significant risk if misidentified or adulterated.

## Chemical Identity
- **IUPAC Name:** 1-(2,5-dimethoxy-4-((2-fluoroethyl)thio)phenyl)propan-2-amine
- **Molecular Formula:** C11H16FNO2S
- **Molecular Weight:** 245.32 g/mol
- **SMILES:** COc1cc(C[C@H](N)C)c(Sc2cc(F)ccc2F)cc1OC
- **CAS Number:** Not definitively registered; often discussed by informal identifiers
- **Chemical Class:** Substituted phenethylamine, specifically a 2,5-dimethoxy-4-substituted phenethylamine
- **Structural Features:** 2,5-dimethoxy groups on the benzene ring with a 2-fluoroethylthio chain at the 4-position
- The fluorinated ethylthio side chain is the key structural departure from 2C-B (which has a bromine atom at the 4-position)
- This substitution is responsible for both the extended duration and altered receptor binding profile

## History & Discovery
2C-B-FLY was first synthesized by David E. Nichols and colleagues at Purdue University in the context of structure-activity relationship (SAR) studies on serotonin receptor agonists.
Nichols' research group was investigating how modifications to the phenethylamine scaffold affected 5-HT2A receptor affinity and functional activity.
The compound was described in academic literature as part of a broader effort to understand the pharmacophore responsible for psychedelic activity.
It was not included in Shulgin's original PiHKAL (1991), though it is catalogued in extended online PiHKAL databases such as Isomer Design.
2C-B-FLY first appeared on the recreational drug market in the mid-to-late 2000s as a novel psychoactive substance (NPS) sold through online research chemical vendors.
Its availability was short-lived in the mainstream designer drug market, largely due to the 2009 mislabeling crisis that led to emergency scheduling in several countries.

## Pharmacology
2C-B-FLY acts primarily as a serotonin 5-HT2A receptor agonist, which is the principal mechanism underlying its psychedelic effects.
Activation of the 5-HT2A receptor in the prefrontal cortex is believed to produce the characteristic visual and cognitive alterations of classical psychedelics.
The compound also shows affinity for other serotonin receptor subtypes, including 5-HT2C and possibly 5-HT1A, though its selectivity profile has not been exhaustively characterized.
The fluorinated ethylthio side chain increases lipophilicity compared to 2C-B, contributing to a longer duration of action and potentially altered receptor kinetics.
Functional assays suggest that 2C-B-FLY is a partial to full agonist at 5-HT2A, with efficacy comparable to but distinct from other 2C-family compounds.
As with all serotonergic psychedelics, there is a theoretical risk of serotonin syndrome if combined with MAOIs, SSRIs, or other serotonergic substances.

## Dosage & Administration
2C-B-FLY is administered orally, typically in the form of pressed tablets, capsules, or powder.
Dose ranges are approximate and can vary significantly based on individual sensitivity, body weight, and set and setting.
- **Threshold:** ~8 mg
- **Light:** 8–12 mg
- **Common:** 12–15 mg
- **Strong:** 15–20 mg
- **Heavy:** 20+ mg (not recommended; increased risk of adverse effects)
Doses above 20 mg are associated with significantly more intense experiences and a higher likelihood of challenging psychological effects.
Insufflation (nasal) has been reported anecdotally but is not well-documented and is strongly discouraged due to unknown bioavailability and potential for nasal tissue damage.
Accurate dosing is critical; the 2009 poisoning incident underscores the lethal danger of mislabeled materials.
There is no established LD50 for 2C-B-FLY in humans.

## Effects & Experience
2C-B-FLY produces effects broadly consistent with the classical psychedelic phenethylamine profile.
- **Duration:** 6–10 hours, with onset at 30–90 minutes, peak at 2–4 hours, and gradual comedown over the final hours
- **Visual effects:** Enhanced color saturation, geometric patterning (particularly with eyes closed), fractal visuals, mild tracers, and warping of surfaces
- **Cognitive effects:** Altered thought patterns, enhanced introspection, increased philosophical or creative thinking, occasional ego dissolution at higher doses
- **Emotional effects:** Euphoria, empathogenic qualities (less pronounced than MDMA but present at light doses), emotional amplification, and occasional anxiety or paranoia at higher doses
- **Physical effects:** Pupil dilation, mild tachycardia, increased body temperature, nausea (more common during onset), mild muscle tension
- **After-effects:** Residual stimulation, mild visual "breathing" of surfaces, and emotional reflectivity that may persist for several hours post-peak
Users frequently describe 2C-B-FLY as smoother and longer-lasting than 2C-B, with a somewhat gentler comedown.
The experience is often compared to a blend of 2C-B's visual richness with the extended duration more typical of LSD or DOB.
Reports suggest that 2C-B-FLY has a more pronounced body component than 2C-B, with users noting increased tactile sensitivity and a warm, pleasurable physical sensation during the peak.

## Toxicity & Harm Reduction
The toxicological profile of 2C-B-FLY has not been formally studied in humans or animals.
There are no confirmed deaths directly attributable to 2C-B-FLY when correctly identified and dosed.
The primary toxicity concern is not the compound itself but the risk of misidentification, adulteration, or substitution with far more potent and dangerous compounds (see Bromo-DragonFLY incident below).
- Use reagent testing (e.g., Marquis, Mecke, Ehrlich) to help identify substances, though these cannot distinguish 2C-B-FLY from all analogues
- Start with a low dose if the substance's identity cannot be independently verified
- Avoid combining with MAOIs, SSRIs, tramadol, or other serotonergic drugs due to serotonin syndrome risk
- Avoid combining with stimulants (cocaine, amphetamines) which increase cardiovascular strain
- Ensure adequate hydration and a comfortable, safe environment
- Individuals with personal or family history of psychotic disorders should exercise extreme caution with all serotonergic psychedelics
- Individuals with cardiovascular conditions (hypertension, heart disease) are at elevated risk due to sympathomimetic effects
- There is no known dependence potential, but tolerance builds rapidly with repeated use; allow at least 1–2 weeks between experiences

## The 2009 Bromo-DragonFLY Mislabeling Incident
In 2009, multiple European countries (notably Denmark, Germany, and the United Kingdom) reported severe poisonings and at least one confirmed death linked to a substance sold as "2C-B-FLY."
Subsequent forensic analysis revealed that the material was actually Bromo-DragonFLY, a far more potent and toxic psychedelic with an extremely long duration (up to 3 days) and a narrow therapeutic index.
Bromo-DragonFLY is active at sub-milligram doses (200–800 µg), meaning users who took what they believed to be a standard 2C-B-FLY dose (10–15 mg) were ingesting 15–75 times a normal Bromo-DragonFLY dose.
Symptoms of Bromo-DragonFLY overdose included severe vasoconstriction, peripheral limb ischemia (leading to gangrene and amputations in several cases), prolonged hallucinations, tachycardia, hypertension, and in at least one case, death.
This incident is one of the most well-documented examples of the lethal consequences of mislabeled research chemicals.
It led to emergency scheduling of both 2C-B-FLY and Bromo-DragonFLY in several jurisdictions and prompted broader regulatory action on designer phenethylamines.
The incident remains a defining cautionary tale in the harm reduction community regarding the dangers of unregulated research chemical markets.
It also accelerated the development of more sophisticated forensic drug checking programs across Europe.

## Legal Status
The legal status of 2C-B-FLY varies significantly by jurisdiction.
- **United States:** Not explicitly scheduled at the federal level, but may be prosecuted under the Federal Analogue Act as an analogue of 2C-B (Schedule I) due to substantially similar chemical structure and effects.
- **United Kingdom:** Controlled as a Class A drug under a generic phenethylamine catch-all provision following the 2009 incident.
- **Denmark:** Emergency scheduled in 2009 following the mislabeling poisonings; subsequently placed under permanent control.
- **Germany:** Scheduled under the NpSG (New Psychoactive Substances Act) as of 2016.
- **Australia:** Covered under state and territory drug analogues legislation.
- **Sweden:** Classified as a hazardous substance under narcotic control laws.
Many other countries have enacted blanket bans on substituted phenethylamines or novel psychoactive substances that would encompass 2C-B-FLY.
Users should consult local regulations, as legal status changes frequently.

## Relationship to Other Compounds
- **2C-B (2,5-dimethoxy-4-bromophenethylamine):** The direct parent compound; 2C-B-FLY replaces the bromine atom with a 2-fluoroethylthio group, resulting in longer duration and altered potency.
- **Bromo-DragonFLY:** A far more potent difuranyl-substituted phenethylamine with bromine; its mislabeling as 2C-B-FLY caused the 2009 poisoning crisis. Active at microgram doses vs. milligram doses for 2C-B-FLY.
- **DOB (2,5-dimethoxy-4-bromoamphetamine):** An amphetamine-class psychedelic related to the 2C series; shares the 2,5-dimethoxy-4-halogenated phenethylamine core but is a full amphetamine, contributing to significantly greater stimulant effects and longer duration.
- **5-MeO-2C-B-FLY (Nexus Flipping):** A tryptamine analogue of 2C-B-FLY incorporating a 5-methoxytryptamine core with a similar fluorinated side chain; reported to be highly potent with unique pharmacological properties. Also known colloquially as "Nexus Flipping." Some reports suggest it may share the vasoconstrictive risks associated with Bromo-DragonFLY.
- **2C-I (2,5-dimethoxy-4-iodophenethylamine):** Another 2C-family compound with an iodo substituent; shares a similar duration and visual profile but differs in subjective character and receptor affinity.
