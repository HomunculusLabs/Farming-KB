# Persea americana

Persea americana, the avocado, is a large evergreen tree in the laurel
family (Lauraceae) native to south-central Mexico. Cultivated for over
10,000 years, it produces one of the most nutritionally dense fruits known —
rich in monounsaturated fatty acids, dietary fiber, potassium, and vitamins
E, K, and B6. The creamy, lipid-rich mesocarp has made it a globally
important food crop, with annual production exceeding 8 million tonnes. In
permaculture, avocado serves as a productive canopy tree, shade provider,
windbreak component, and wildlife habitat. Its deep root system, evergreen
canopy, and high leaf litter production contribute significantly to soil
building and microclimate moderation in food forest systems.

## Taxonomy and Naming

- **Order:** Laurales
- **Family:** Lauraceae
- **Genus:** Persea
- **Species:** P. americana Mill. (1768)
- **Common names:** Avocado, aguacate, alligator pear, butter fruit
- **Botanical varieties:**
  - *P. americana* var. *americana* (West Indian) — tropical, lowland
  - *P. americana* var. *drymifolia* (Mexican) — cold-tolerant, anise-scented
  - *P. americana* var. *guatemalensis* (Guatemalan) — subtropical, thick skin

The three varieties hybridize freely; most commercial cultivars are hybrids.
'Hass' (dominant global cultivar) is a Mexican × Guatemalan hybrid selected
by Rudolph Hass in California in the 1920s. The genus name derives from a
Greek name for an Egyptian tree, though the avocado is New World in origin.

## Description

### Tree Form and Foliage

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Height** | 9–20 m, occasionally to 25 m in wild stands |
| **Canopy** | 6–12 m wide, dense, rounded to irregularly spreading |
| **Bark** | Grey-brown, rough and fissured with age |
| **Leaves** | Alternate, simple, elliptic to obovate, 10–25 × 5–12 cm |
| **Leaf texture** | Leathery, glossy dark green above, lighter below |
| **Leaf scent** | Var. drymifolia has anise-like aroma when crushed |
| **Root system** | Coarse, shallow feeders with deep taproot; sensitive to waterlogging |

### Flowers and Fruit

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Inflorescence** | Panicles of greenish-yellow flowers, 5–15 cm long |
| **Flowers** | Perfect, 6 tepals in two whorls, 9 stamens in 3 whorls |
| **Pollination** | Protogynous dichogamy; Type A or B flowering patterns. |
| | Cross-pollination recommended for maximum fruit set |
| **Fruit** | Single-seeded berry, 7–20 cm long, 5–12 cm wide |
| **Skin** | Thin to thick, smooth to pebbly, green to purple-black |
| **Flesh** | Creamy, pale yellow-green, 60–75% lipid content |
| **Seed** | Spherical to ovoid, 3–6 cm diameter, 15–25% of fruit weight |

### Climatic Requirements

| Parameter | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Temperature** | 15–30°C optimal; var. drymifolia tolerates brief -4 to -6°C |
| **Frost** | Young trees killed at -1°C; mature var. drymifolia to -6°C |
| **Rainfall** | 1000–1500 mm annually; well-drained soil essential |
| **USDA zones** | 9–11 (var. americana); 8b–11 (var. drymifolia) |
| **Wind** | Sheltered position essential; causes fruit drop and branch breakage |
| **Altitude** | Sea level to 2500 m (var. drymifolia in Mexican highlands) |

## Distribution and Habitat

- **Native range:** South-central Mexico to Guatemala, in cloud forests
  and subtropical highlands of the Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt.
- **Current distribution:** Tropical and subtropical regions worldwide.
  Major producers: Mexico (~30% global), Colombia, Dominican Republic,
  Peru, Indonesia, Kenya, Brazil. Significant US production in California,
  Florida, Hawaii.
- **Naturalized:** Central/South America, Africa (Kenya, South Africa),
  Southeast Asia, Mediterranean climates (Israel, Spain, Australia).

## Permaculture Functions

- **Food production:** One mature tree produces 150–500 fruits annually.
  High-calorie, nutrient-dense, providing essential fatty acids and fiber.
- **Canopy/shade:** Evergreen canopy provides year-round shade for
  understory crops and livestock. Upper canopy layer in food forests.
- **Windbreak:** Dense foliage useful in subtropical orchard shelterbelts.
- **Soil building:** High leaf litter (C:N ~25:1) supports rapid
  decomposition and nutrient cycling.
- **Wildlife habitat:** Flowers provide nectar for bees; fruit feeds birds
  and mammals. Dense canopy offers nesting sites for songbirds.
- **Mulch source:** Pruned branches and leaves provide substantial mulch.
- **Microclimate:** Transpirational cooling reduces understory temperatures
  by 3–5°C, benefiting shade-loving crops.

## Cultivation Notes

- **Propagation:** Grafted onto seedling rootstocks ('Duke 7' or 'Thomas'
  for Phytophthora resistance). Seed-grown trees viable but variable.
- **Spacing:** 6–10 m within rows, 8–12 m between rows. High-density
  plantings (3–5 m) used commercially with aggressive canopy management.
- **Irrigation:** 25–50 mm per week during growing season. Drip irrigation
  preferred to avoid Phytophthora crown infection.
- **Fertilization:** Young trees need regular nitrogen; mature trees benefit
  from balanced NPK with zinc and boron. Ammonium sulfate preferred.
- **Pruning:** Annual pruning for canopy openness, deadwood removal, height
  management. Stumping practiced to rejuvenate tall trees.
- **Major threats:** Root rot (Phytophthora cinnamomi) is the most serious
  disease. Also avocado thrips, persea mite, sunblotch viroid, anthracnose.

## Notable Cultivars

- **'Hass':** Mexican × Guatemalan; dominant global cultivar, year-round,
  thick pebbly purple-black skin, 18–22% oil. ~80% of global production.
- **'Fuerte':** Mexican × Guatemalan; pear-shaped, green skin, excellent
  flavor, moderate cold tolerance.
- **'Bacon':** Cold-hardy, medium round fruit, green skin. Good for cooler
  coastal locations.
- **'Reed':** Guatemalan; round, large, thick green skin, compact tree.
- **'Pinkerton':** Elongated pear, pebbly green skin, small seed, heavy
  bearing.
