## Description

The Sustainable Studies Institute (SSI) is a research and educational organization founded by Dr. Elaine Ingham, focused on advancing understanding of soil foodweb biology and promoting sustainable land management practices. SSI served as the primary institutional home for the development and dissemination of Actively Aerated Compost Tea (AACT) methodology and soil biology assessment techniques.

- **Category**: Organization / Research institute
- **Domain**: Soil microbiology, sustainable agriculture, education

## Key Facts

- Dr. Elaine Ingham served as President of the Sustainable Studies Institute.
- Co-published *The Field Guide I for Actively Aerated Compost Tea (AACT)* in 2004 along with Nature Technologies International LLC.
- The institute operated alongside two other Ingham-led organizations: Soil Foodweb Institute Pty. Ltd. (Australia) and Soil Foodweb Inc. (US-based research entity).
- Contact: david@sustainablestudies.org, www.sustainablestudies.org, phone 541-257-2614.
- SSI's work bridged academic research (Ingham's affiliation with Oregon State University, Dept. Forest Science) with practical field application.
- The institute focused on translating soil foodweb science into actionable protocols for farmers, landscapers, and compost tea practitioners.
- SSI was part of a network of organizations promoting biological approaches to soil management as alternatives to conventional chemical agriculture.

## Research Programs

SSI's primary research programs included: characterization of optimal soil foodweb structures for different crop types, development of standardized compost tea brewing protocols, documentation of disease suppression mechanisms through biological inoculation, and creation of soil biology assessment benchmarks for agricultural consulting.

## Educational Outreach

The institute offered workshops, online courses, and field training programs teaching soil foodweb assessment techniques. Programs ranged from introductory courses for farmers to advanced microscopy certification for professional consultants. The educational approach emphasized hands-on microscopy skills combined with ecological understanding.

## Organizational Network

SSI operated as part of a three-organization network: the Sustainable Studies Institute (research and education, Corvallis OR), Soil Foodweb Inc. (US-based research entity), and Soil Foodweb Institute Pty. Ltd. (commercial testing services, Australia). This network allowed Ingham's methodology to be applied across multiple continents.

## Publications and Resources

Beyond the 2004 Field Guide, SSI produced educational materials including soil biology assessment protocols, compost tea recipe guides, and crop-specific biological management recommendations. These publications helped standardize the emerging biological agriculture consulting industry.

## Historical Context

The work documented in this entry emerged during a pivotal period in biological agriculture, when conventional chemical-based approaches were increasingly questioned in favor of biological and ecological methods. The principles and practices described here represent part of a broader shift toward understanding soil as a living ecosystem rather than an inert substrate for chemical inputs.

## Contemporary Relevance

The concepts and organizations described in this entry continue to influence modern regenerative agriculture, soil health movements, and biological farming practices. Current research in soil microbiome science increasingly validates the biological approaches pioneered during this era, confirming the importance of fungal networks, bacterial diversity, and protozoan grazing in maintaining productive soil ecosystems.
