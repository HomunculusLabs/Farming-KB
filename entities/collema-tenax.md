# Collema tenax

## Description

*Collema tenax* is a gelatinous cyanolichen that forms a critical component of [[biological-soil-indicators]] (biocrusts) in arid and semi-arid regions worldwide. It consists of a symbiotic association between a fungus (mycobiont) and a cyanobacterium, typically *Nostoc* spp. (photobiont). The species is one of the most important nitrogen-fixing [[lichens]] in desert ecosystems and plays a foundational role in soil stabilization, nutrient cycling, and primary production in landscapes where vascular plant cover is sparse.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Division**: [[ascomycota]]
- **Class**: Lecanoromycetes
- **Order**: Peltigerales
- **Family**: Collemataceae
- **Genus**: *Collema*
- **Species**: *C. tenax*

## Key Facts

- Forms gelatinous, dark olive-green to black thalli that swell significantly when wet, enabling rapid water absorption from infrequent rainfall events.
- Studied by Lange et al. (1998) for its photosynthetic response to water content, light, and temperature in arid lands of southern Utah.
- Capable of fixing atmospheric nitrogen through its cyanobacterial partner (*Nostoc*), contributing 1-13 kg N ha-1 yr-1 to desert crust communities (Belnap, 2002).
- Contributes to biological soil crust communities that stabilize desert soils against wind and water erosion.
- Exhibits photosynthetic activity only when hydrated; the cyanobacterial component produces fixed carbon while the fungal matrix provides structural support and moisture retention.

## Physiological Ecology

The CO2 exchange of *C. tenax* is strongly regulated by thallus water content:

- **Minimum water content**: Photosynthesis begins at very low water content, a critical adaptation to brief dew or fog events.
- **Optimal hydration**: Maximal net photosynthesis occurs when the thallus is fully saturated, typically after rain.
- **Temperature response**: Photosynthesis is active across a wide temperature range (approximately -5 to 30 C), with optimal rates around 15-20 C.
- **Desiccation tolerance**: Can survive extended periods of complete desiccation, rapidly reactivating metabolic processes upon rehydration.

## Ecological Role

As a dominant component of biocrusts in the deserts of the American Southwest:

1. **Nitrogen input**: Symbiotic nitrogen fixation provides the primary source of new nitrogen to many desert ecosystems.
2. **Soil stabilization**: Gelatinous thalli bind soil particles, reducing wind erosion and creating microtopographic features that trap additional organic material.
3. **Carbon fixation**: Contributes measurably to net primary production in ecosystems where vascular plant productivity is low.
4. **Water infiltration**: Crust communities enhance soil water-holding capacity and reduce runoff.
5. **Successional foundation**: Often among the first colonizers of disturbed desert soils, facilitating establishment of later successional organisms.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Desert restoration**: *C. tenax* and related biocrust lichens are targets for restoration ecology in degraded arid lands.
- **Nitrogen fixation model**: The cyanobacterial-fungal partnership provides a model for studying nitrogen fixation in non-legume systems.
- **Desiccation biology**: Research into how *C. tenax* survives extreme dehydration has implications for understanding stress tolerance in cultivated fungi.
- **Climate change indicator**: Sensitivity of biocrust lichens to warming and altered precipitation makes them important bioindicators.
