N. maxima frequently hybridizes in the wild, particularly with N. neoguineensis in New Guinea and N. tentaculata in Sulawesi. Natural hybrids produce intermediate pitcher morphology and are often locally common.

## Hybridization and Horticultural Significance

N. maxima is one of the most important parent species in Nepenthes horticulture. Its genetic contribution appears in hundreds of registered hybrids, valued for vigor, pitcher size, and heat tolerance.

Notable hybrids include N. × maxima (with N. northiana), N. × hookeriana (with N. rafflesiana), and numerous complex crosses such as N. × ventrata (N. ventricosa × N. maxima).
