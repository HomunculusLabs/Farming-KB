## Description

"Dwarf Scotch Curled' is a traditional kale cultivar (*[[brassica-oleracea]]* var. *sabellica*) noted for its deeply frilled, finely curled blue-green leaves and compact growth habit. In Eliot Coleman's winter production system, it is valued for producing exceptionally sweet inner leaves during the coldest months under inner row-cover protection.

## Classification

- **Type**: Cultivar (Kale)
- **Species**: *Brassica oleracea* var. *sabellica*
- **Family**: Brassicaceae (mustard family)
- **Category**: Winter green, braising/salad green
- **Origin**: Scotland (traditional variety)

## Key Facts

- Described as "the best eating of the traditional kale varieties" grown under inner cover at [[four-season-farm]]
- Sown September 1 for February 15 harvest of baby inner leaves
- Very sweet inner leaves produced during deep winter under protected cultivation
- Produces full-size kale for market sales from early to mid-March
- Begins bolting (going to seed) by the third week of March
- Compact growth habit makes it suitable for close planting under row-cover wickets
- Hardy enough for unheated cold-house production in Zone 5 Maine

## Cultivation Notes

- September 1 sowing schedule produces harvestable baby leaves by mid-February
- Inner leaves harvested at baby-leaf stage are extremely sweet — cold temperatures convert starches to sugars
- Full plants produce marketable kale through March before bolting
- One of the few traditional European kale varieties that performs well under inner row-cover protection
- Frilled leaf texture adds visual appeal to mixed green products

## Harvest Schedule (Four Season Farm)

- **Sowing**: September 1
- **Baby-leaf harvest**: mid-February (inner leaves for salad/stir-fry mix)
- **Full-size harvest**: early to mid-March
- **Bolting**: third week of March

## Relevance to Cultivation

'Dwarf Scotch Curled' kale demonstrates the winter sweetening phenomenon common to brassicas: cold temperatures trigger starch-to-sugar conversion, resulting in leaves that are significantly sweeter in winter than in warmer months. This cultivar's reliable performance under inner row covers in unheated structures makes it a valuable component of the winter production calendar. Its dual-purpose nature — baby leaves for mixes in deep winter, full leaves for bunched sales in early spring — exemplifies the succession-harvest approach that maximizes revenue from protected growing space.
