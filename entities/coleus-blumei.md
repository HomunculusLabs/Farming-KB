## Coleus blumei

*Coleus blumei* (syn. *Plectranthus scutellarioides*), commonly known as painted nettle or simply coleus, is a species of flowering plant in the family Lamiaceae. While primarily cultivated as an ornamental plant for its strikingly colorful foliage, it has been occasionally reported as a mild psychoactive plant in the ethnobotanical literature, earning it a minor but notable place in discussions of psychoactive flora.

## Taxonomy and Description

*Coleus blumei* is native to Southeast Asia, particularly Malaysia and parts of Indonesia, but has been widely cultivated and naturalized in tropical and subtropical regions worldwide. The plant is a perennial herb or subshrub growing to approximately 30–60 cm in height, with opposite, toothed leaves that display remarkable variation in color—including combinations of green, red, purple, orange, yellow, and brown—due to the presence of anthocyanin pigments.

The taxonomic history of the genus has been complex. Many species formerly classified in *Coleus* have been reassigned to *Plectranthus* or *Solenostemon*, and modern molecular phylogenetic studies have further refined the classification. The currently accepted name for the cultivated coleus is often given as *Plectranthus scutellarioides*, though *Coleus blumei* remains widely used in the horticultural and ethnobotanical literature.

## Ornamental Cultivation

Coleus is one of the most popular ornamental foliage plants in the world, valued for its ease of cultivation and the dramatic color variation of its leaves. Thousands of cultivars have been developed, with leaf colors ranging from deep burgundy and near-black to bright chartreuse and vivid pink. The plant is propagated readily from stem cuttings and grows well in both garden beds and containers.

In temperate climates, coleus is typically grown as an annual, as it is sensitive to frost. In tropical and subtropical regions, it can persist as a perennial. The plant prefers partial shade and consistently moist, well-drained soil, though many modern cultivars have been bred for greater sun tolerance.

## Psychoactive Properties

Reports of psychoactive activity in *Coleus blumei* are scattered and somewhat ambiguous. The plant has occasionally been mentioned in ethnobotanical and underground literature as producing mild hallucinogenic or sedative effects when the fresh leaves are chewed or consumed, particularly the more colorful varieties.
