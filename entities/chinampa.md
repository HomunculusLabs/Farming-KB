## Chinampa (Floating Garden System)

## Description
A chinampa is a traditional Mesoamerican agricultural system consisting of artificial islands or raised beds built on shallow lake beds, used for intensive, year-round cultivation. In [[permaculture]] literature, [[chinampas]] are cited as an exemplary model of edge-maximising design that integrates aquaculture and agriculture in a highly productive, sustainable system. The chinampa represents one of the most productive agricultural systems ever devised by humans.

## Classification
- **Type**: Traditional agricultural/aquacultural technique
- **Origin**: Mesoamerica (Aztec civilisation, Lake Texcoco, modern Mexico City)
- **Design category**: Edge maximisation, polyculture, water-land integration
- **Era**: Pre-Columbian to present (over 1,000 years of continuous use in Xochimilco)

## Key Facts

### Design Principles
- Rectangular plots of fertile land surrounded by narrow canals
- Maximises the edge between land and water -- the most productive interface in ecology
- Mud from canal bottoms is periodically dredged and placed on beds as fertiliser
- Willows and other trees planted at bed edges stabilise the structure and provide shade
- Canals provide irrigation, transportation, and aquatic habitat simultaneously

### Productivity
- Among the most productive agricultural systems per unit area ever recorded
- Year-round production due to constant moisture and nutrient availability
- Supports diverse polycultures: vegetables, flowers, fish, waterfowl
- Canals between beds yield fish, axolotls, and aquatic plants as additional crops

### Permaculture Applications
- Mollison references chinampa systems as models for pond and canal edge design
- Sinuous canals in flat landscapes provide maximum edge for fish stocking
- Maximum edge is assured by either [[swale]], canal, or chinampa systems
- Harvesting simplified by skimming, floating, or trapping products in flow
- Canals enable low-energy transportation by barge, flatboat, or float

### Modern Relevance
- Xochimilco chinampas in Mexico City are a UNESCO World Heritage Site
- Principles adapted in modern permaculture for aquaculture pond design
- Canal-based systems allow sequential pond management with gravity flow

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology
- The constant moisture environment of chinampa beds is analogous to controlled mushroom growing conditions
- Mud and organic dredgings from chinampa canals would serve as excellent mushroom substrate material
- The edge-maximisation principle applies directly to mushroom bed and [[fruiting-chamber]] design
- Integrated multi-trophic systems parallel the multi-organism approach in mycorestoration
