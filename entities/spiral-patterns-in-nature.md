## Description

**Spiral patterns** are among the most fundamental geometric forms in nature, appearing across all scales from molecular DNA double-helices to galactic structures. In Mollison's pattern understanding framework, spirals arise as a consequence of flow deformation over surfaces and are classified as either dynamic (revolving) or static (fixed). Natural spirals manifest in plant phyllotaxis, mollusc shells, weather systems, water flow, DNA, and planetary orbits. The spiral is presented as one of the primary pattern templates available to [[overbeck-jet]], D'Arcy Thompson

## Key Facts

- Spirals may be revolving (dynamic, like atmospheric circulation) or fixed (static, like snail shells).
- Arise as a consequence of deformations in flow, or as an intrinsic property of a specific velocity of flow over surfaces.
- Spiral paths are traced by orbiting bodies over time, or developed by organisms creating compact forms (e.g., molluscs) analogous to annidation.
- Made visible in plants as whorls of leaves and branches.
- D'Arcy Thompson (1952, *On Growth and Form*) discusses quantitative and geometric qualities of spiral phenomena.
- A long spiral in section is the "S" form of humid landscape slopes (and the yin-yang symbol).
- Three-dimensional spirals form long ribbons of complex shape.
- DNA reveals a double-helix form — spirals exist even at the molecular level.
- Spirals are single streamlines of vortices, tori, or sap flows.

## Fibonacci Series in Plant Spirals

Storl (1978, *Culture and Horticulture*) describes spiral leaf arrangement where leaves are positioned at fractional turns around the stem:
- Leaf placements progress in Fibonacci ratios: 1:2, 1:3, 2:5, 3:8, 5:13, 8:21, 13:34...
- Each ratio is the sum of the two preceding: 1:2 + 1:3 = 2:5; 1:3 + 2:5 = 3:8.
- Venus forms five loops (retrogressions) below the ecliptic in eight years.
- Natural spirals are never "perfect" but show slight progression and gradually lose phase over long periods.
- Storl sees a relation between the forms of plants and of planets in these progressions.

## Applications of Spiral Form

- **Compact storage**: Mollusc shells spiral to fit large surface area in small volume.
- **Efficient array**: Leaf spirals maximize light capture per unit stem.
- **Flow guidance**: Screws, propellers, impellers, turbines, gears.
- **Exchange surface**: Some sharks and invertebrates develop spiral gut lining to increase absorption.
- **Transport**: Spiral cilia convey mucus, food, or particles in or out of organisms.
- **Anchoring**: Plants like *Convolvulus* use spiral anchors in earth; some parasites spiral into flesh.
- **Landscape design**: Spiral herb gardens, spiral paths, and spiral water features.
