# DOAM

**Chemical name:** 2,5-Dimethoxy-4-(n)-amylamphetamine
**PiHKAL entry:** #61

## Overview

DOAM is a member of the [[2c-family-compounds-overview]] of psychedelic amphetamines, carrying a five-carbon (amyl/pentyl) chain at the 4-position of the 2,5-dimethoxyamphetamine skeleton. It represents the longest-chain alkyl substituent in the DOx series that was actually bioassayed, and demonstrates the dramatic loss of potency that occurs as the 4-position alkyl chain extends beyond the optimal length.

DOAM was one of the compounds "actually completed" in the series stemming from the original discovery of dom-entity|DOM. Shulgin's "Ten Classics" research program explored systematically extending the alkyl chain at this position.

| Level | Amount (mg) |

**Effective psychedelic dose:** Greater than 10 mg (potency "way down from the lower homologues")

Duration: Unknown at active levels.

## Effects

At the 10 mg test dose, effects were subtle and primarily stimulant-like:

- **Mood:** Quite gay and voluble at lunch, "bubbled on into the afternoon with puns and high spirits."
- **Motor effects:** Some motor incoordination noted in handwriting.
- **Driving:** A "strange tenseness during driving" was noted.
- **Aftermath:** No sequelae, no trouble sleeping.
- **Observer's conclusion:** "With this potency way down from the lower homologues, I have no pressing desire to take this compound to a higher dose."

## The DOM Homologue Series

DOAM fits into the systematic exploration of 4-position alkyl chain length on the 2,5-dimethoxyamphetamine skeleton:

| Compound | 4-Substituent | Dosage (mg) | Duration |
|----------|--------------|-------------|----------|
| don-entity|DON | NO2 | 3.0-4.5 | 8-15 h |
| dom-entity|DOM | CH3 | 3-10 | 8-12 h |
| [[doet-compound-profile]] | C2H5 | 2-5 | 14-20 h |
| dobu-entity|DOBU | C4H9 | ~2.5 | Very long |
| DOAM | C5H11 | >10 | Unknown |
| dopr-entity|DOPR | C3H7 | 2.5-5.0 | 20-30 h |

The potency does not follow a simple pattern with chain length, reflecting the complex interplay of lipophilicity, steric factors, and metabolic stability.

## Synthesis Notes

The synthesis follows the general pattern for DOx compounds:

1. **Acylation:** p-Dimethoxybenzene is acylated with valeric acid using polyphosphoric acid to give 2,5-dimethoxyamylophenone.
2. **Reduction:** Clemmensen reduction with amalgamated zinc and HCl gives 2,5-dimethoxy-(n)-amylbenzene.
3. **Formylation:** Vilsmeier-Haack reaction (POCl3 + N-methylformanilide) yields the aldehyde.
4. **Nitrostyrene formation:** Condensation with nitroethane in acetic acid.
5. **Reduction:** LAH reduction provides the amine hydrochloride (mp 145-146 deg C from acetonitrile).

## Pharmacology

The very low potency of DOAM suggests that the five-carbon chain may be too long for optimal binding at the [[phenethylamine-receptor-pharmacology]] or that metabolic processing of the long chain produces inactive or less active metabolites. The compound's existence demonstrates that there is a practical limit to the 4-position alkyl chain length for psychedelic activity.

## Significance

DOAM serves as an important boundary marker in the structure-activity relationship studies of the DOx series. It helps define the practical limits of 4-position alkyl substitution and demonstrates that beyond a certain chain length, the compounds lose their psychedelic potency dramatically.
