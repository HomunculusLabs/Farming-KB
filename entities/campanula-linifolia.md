## Overview

**Campanula linifolia** is a slender perennial bellflower in the family Campanulaceae, native to montane regions of central and southern Europe. The species epithet *linifolia* refers to its narrow, linear leaves that resemble those of flax (Linum).

It produces upright stems bearing loose racemes of small, pale blue to violet campanulate flowers. The plant is valued for its delicate, airy appearance in rock gardens and border front positions.

Campanula linifolia is less commonly cultivated than many of its congeners but is appreciated by collectors of European alpine plants for its graceful form.

It is sometimes sold under the synonym [[campanula-rapunculoides]] subsp. linifolia in European nurseries. The species has naturalized sparingly in parts of northern Europe outside its native range.

- **Genus:** Campanula
- **Species:** Campanula linifolia Lam.

The species was described by Jean-Baptiste Lamarck in the late 18th century. It belongs to Campanula section Eucodon, a diverse group of herbaceous bellflowers with rotate to campanulate corollas.

Campanula linifolia has sometimes been confused with [[campanula-persicifolia]] in [[biodiversity-fungal-herbarium-collections]]. The two species differ in leaf width, stem branching, and corolla shape.

No widely accepted subspecies or varieties are currently recognized. Some populations from the southeastern part of the range show variation in flower size and stem height.

The species is diploid with 2n = 34 chromosomes. This chromosome number is consistent across its range, supporting treatment as a single variable species rather than a species complex.

## Morphology

The plant forms a compact clump reaching 20–40 cm in height. Stems are erect, slender, and unbranched or sparsely branched above, typically green and glabrous or sparsely pubescent.

Basal leaves are spoon-shaped to narrowly elliptic, forming a low rosette. Stem leaves are linear to narrowly lanceolate, 2–5 cm long and 2–5 mm wide, giving the plant its characteristic grass-like foliage.

Flowers are borne in loose, terminal racemes of 3–10 blooms. Each flower is 12–18 mm long, broadly campanulate, pale blue to violet with a lighter center.

The corolla is divided to approximately one-third of its length into five broadly triangular lobes. The calyx lobes are narrow and spreading, about one-third the length of the corolla.

The style is exerted and bears a three-lobed stigma. Capsules are ovoid to hemispherical, dehiscing by three small basal pores.

Seeds are elliptic, light brown, and approximately 0.8 mm long. Flowering occurs from June through August.

## Distribution and Habitat

Campanula linifolia is native to montane and subalpine regions of central and southern Europe. Its range extends from the Pyrenees through the Alps to the Carpathians and mountains of the northern Balkans.

The species occurs between 500 and 2,000 meters elevation. It grows in rocky meadows, open woodlands, and along forest margins on well-drained soils.

It favors calcareous to neutral substrates but can tolerate mildly acidic conditions where limestone is absent. The species prefers sites with good drainage and moderate moisture during [[query-how-to-protect-plants-from-frost-and-extend-the-growing-season]].

Populations in the western part of the range tend to occur at lower elevations. Eastern populations are more commonly found in subalpine grasslands and on rocky slopes.

The species can be locally abundant in suitable habitat but is never dominant. It typically occurs as scattered individuals or small groups among other meadow forbs.

## Cultivation

Campanula linifolia prefers a sunny to partially shaded position in well-drained soil. It tolerates a range of soil pH from slightly acidic to neutral.

The plant is suitable for rock gardens, trough plantings, and the front of herbaceous borders. Its airy flower stems add vertical interest without dominating neighboring plants.

Propagation is straightforward from seed sown in spring or by division in autumn. Basal cuttings taken in late spring also root readily.

The species is hardy to USDA zones 4–8. It requires no special winter protection when grown in well-drained conditions.

No serious pests or diseases affect Campanula linifolia. Aphids may occasionally colonize young shoots but rarely cause significant damage.

The plant self-seeds moderately in favorable conditions. Deadheading after flowering prevents unwanted seedlings if a tidy appearance is desired.
