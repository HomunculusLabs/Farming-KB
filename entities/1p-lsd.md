# 1P-LSD

**1-Propionyl-D-lysergic acid diethylamide** (abbreviated **1P-LSD**), also known as **1-propionyl-LSD**, is a semisynthetic psychedelic substance of the lysergamide class. It is a derivative and presumed prodrug of **lysergic acid diethylamide (LSD)** in which a propionyl group is attached to the indole nitrogen at position 1 of the LSD molecule. 1P-LSD first appeared on the research chemical market in early 2015 and rapidly became one of the most widely distributed novel psychoactive substances (NPS) in the lysergamide category, marketed as a "legal alternative" to LSD in jurisdictions where it remained unscheduled.

## Chemical Structure and Properties

**IUPAC name:** (6aR,9R)-N,N-diethyl-7-propionyl-6,6a,8,9,10,10a-hexahydro-4H-indolo[4,3-fg]quinoline-9-carboxamide

**Molecular formula:** C₂₁H₂₇N₃O₂

**Molecular weight:** 349.46 g/mol

**SMILES:** CCC(=O)N1[C@@H](C2=CNC3=C2C=C(C=C3)C(=O)N(CC)CC)[C@H](CN1C)C1CCCC1

The propionyl group at the 1-position of the indole nitrogen is the sole structural difference from LSD (which bears a hydrogen at this position). This modification increases the molecular weight by 56 Da and adds lipophilicity, but the core tetracyclic ergoline scaffold, diethylamide substituent at C-8, and stereochemistry at C-6a and C-9 are identical to LSD.

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Appearance | White crystalline powder |
| Solubility | Soluble in ethanol, DMSO; poorly soluble in water |
| Melting point | ~180–185°C (decomposes) |
| Stability | Sensitive to light, heat, and chlorine; store dark, cold, dry |

## Pharmacology

### Mechanism of Action

1P-LSD is believed to function primarily as a **prodrug** of LSD. The propionyl group is metabolically cleaved in vivo, yielding pharmacologically active LSD. This has been confirmed through both in vitro and in vivo studies:

- **In vitro [[beta-glucan-receptor-binding]]:** Brandt et al. (2016) demonstrated that 1P-LSD shows negligible direct binding to the 5-HT₂A receptor at concentrations up to 10 µM, while LSD binds with high affinity (Kᵢ ≈ 1–3 nM). This suggests that 1P-LSD itself is pharmacologically inactive at [[psilocybin-pharmacology-and-serotonin-receptors]].
- **In vivo metabolism:** Wagmann et al. (2019) identified LSD as the predominant metabolite in human and animal samples after 1P-LSD administration, confirming the prodrug hypothesis.
- **Hydrolysis:** The propionyl group can also be cleaved non-enzymatically under acidic conditions, explaining why some users report effects from 1P-LSD stored in acidic solutions.

### Pharmacokinetics

- **Onset of effects:** 45–90 minutes after oral administration (slightly delayed compared to LSD due to the metabolic conversion step)
- **Duration:** 8–12 hours, consistent with the pharmacokinetic profile of LSD once converted
- **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic, via esterases and amidases. The propionyl ester is cleaved to yield LSD, which is then metabolized via N-demethylation, N-deethylation, and hydroxylation pathways identical to those for LSD
- **Excretion:** Primarily renal, with metabolites detectable in urine for 24–72 hours post-dose

### Receptor Pharmacology (via LSD metabolite)

Once converted to LSD, the active compound acts as a **potent partial agonist** at the 5-HT₂A receptor, with additional activity at:
- **5-HT₂B** (agonist) — associated with cardiac valvulopathy risk at chronic high doses
- **5-HT₂C** (agonist)
- **5-HT₁A** (partial agonist)
- **D₂/D₃ dopamine receptors** (partial agonist)
- **TAAR1** (agonist)

The psychedelic effects are primarily mediated through 5-HT₂A receptor activation in the prefrontal cortex, leading to increased glutamate release, thalamocortical dysrhythmia, and altered functional connectivity in the default mode network.

## Subjective Effects

The [[dosage-unpredictability-psilocybin-subjective-experience]] of 1P-LSD is reported by users as essentially indistinguishable from LSD at equivalent doses:

- **Visual effects:** Geometric patterns, color enhancement, trailing/afterimages, texture flowing, synesthesia
- **Cognitive effects:** Enhanced creativity, introspective thought, altered sense of time, ego dissolution at higher doses, increased philosophical or spiritual ideation
- **Physical effects:** Pupil dilation, increased heart rate, elevated body temperature, nausea (more common in early phase), reduced appetite, vasoconstriction
- **Emotional effects:** Euphoria, anxiety ([[set-and-setting]] dependent), feelings of unity/connectedness, enhanced empathy

Common dosages reported by users:

| Dose Range | Effect Level |
|------------|-------------|
| 20–50 µg | Threshold — subtle changes in perception and mood |
| 50–100 µg | Light — noticeable visual and cognitive effects |
| 100–200 µg | Moderate — full psychedelic experience, significant visual and philosophical effects |
| 200–400 µg | Strong — intense experience, potential for ego dissolution |
| 400 µg+ | Heavy — overwhelming for most users, high risk of anxiety |

## History and Legal Status

### Emergence

1P-LSD was first detected by the European Monitoring Centre for Drugs and Drug Addiction (EMCDDA) in 2015, appearing simultaneously on online research chemical markets and as "legal LSD" on blotter paper sold through various channels. Its rapid emergence followed the scheduling of other LSD analogues such as AL-LAD and ETH-LAD.

### Legal Status (as of 2026)

- **United States:** The Federal Analog Act potentially covers 1P-LSD as an analog of LSD (Schedule I), though enforcement has been inconsistent. Some states have explicitly scheduled it.
- **United Kingdom:** Controlled as a Class A drug under the Psychoactive Substances Act 2016 and the Misuse of Drugs Act 1971 (as a lysergamide).
- **Germany:** Controlled under the NpSG (Neue-psychoaktive-Stoffe-Gesetz) since 2016.
- **Switzerland:** Explicitly controlled under the Narcotics Act (Betäubungsmittelgesetz).
- **EU-wide:** Subject to drug control frameworks across member states; most have scheduled it as a controlled substance.
- **Japan:** Controlled as a designated substance.
- **Canada:** Controlled under the Controlled Drugs and Substances Act as an analog.

### Analytical Detection

Standard drug screening tests do not detect 1P-LSD or its LSD metabolite. Specialized analytical methods are required:
- **LC-MS/MS** — most common, can distinguish 1P-LSD from LSD and other lysergamides
- **GC-MS** — requires derivatization due to thermal instability
- **HRMS** — high-resolution mass spectrometry for forensic identification

The development of standardized analytical methods for 1P-LSD has been an active area of forensic chemistry research, with multiple methods published in *Drug Testing and Analysis* and *Forensic Science International*.

## Harm Reduction and Safety

1P-LSD carries the same risk profile as LSD once metabolized:

- **Toxicity:** LSD has a very high therapeutic index. The LD₅₀ in humans is estimated at >12,000 µg (based on animal extrapolation), making fatal overdose extremely unlikely.
- **Psychological risks:** The primary risks are psychological — anxiety, panic reactions, triggering of latent psychiatric conditions (especially schizophrenia-spectrum disorders), and HPPD (hallucinogen persisting perception disorder) in rare cases.
- **Vasoconstriction:** Chronic use may carry cardiovascular risk due to 5-HT₂B agonism.
- **Set and setting:** The importance of psychological preparation, safe environment, and trusted companions ("trip sitter") cannot be overstated.
- **Interactions:** Dangerous interactions with lithium (risk of seizures), tramadol (serotonin syndrome risk), and other serotonergic drugs. Do not combine with MAOIs.

## Analytical Notes for Forensic Context

1P-LSD is commonly encountered on blotter paper at doses of 100–150 µg per tab, visually indistinguishable from LSD blotter. Key analytical markers:
- **Molecular ion:** m/z 350 (M+H)⁺
- **Fragment ions:** m/z 280 (loss of propionyl group, yielding LSD fragment), m/z 265, m/z 221
- **Distinction from LSD:** Presence of the propionyl fragment (m/z 57) and the M+H at 350 vs. 324 for LSD
