# Leersia hexandra

## Description

Leersia hexandra, commonly known as southern cutgrass, is a perennial aquatic to semi-aquatic grass found in tropical and subtropical wetlands worldwide. It is a stoloniferous species that forms dense mats in shallow water and along the margins of ponds, streams, and marshes. The species is notable for its ability to maintain arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) associations in semi-aquatic habitats, making it relevant to studies of [[fungal-symbiosis]] under waterlogged conditions.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Division:** Magnoliophyta (Angiosperms)
- **Class:** Liliopsida (Monocots)
- **Order:** Poales
- **Family:** Poaceae
- **Subfamily:** Oryzoideae
- **Genus:** Leersia
- **Species:** L. hexandra

Leersia is a small genus of about 18 species in the rice subfamily (Oryzoideae), closely related to Oryza (rice). L. hexandra is the most widely distributed species in the genus, occurring pantropically.

## Morphology

- **Height:** 30–100 cm, decumbent to ascending, often rooting at lower nodes
- **Stems:** Stoloniferous, forming extensive mats; nodes glabrous
- **Leaves:** Linear-lanceolate, 10–30 cm long, 5–12 mm wide, scabrous margins (cutting texture, hence the common name)
- **Ligule:** Membranous, 2–5 mm long
- **Inflorescence:** Open, nodding panicle, 8–20 cm long, with slender branches
- **Spikelets:** Laterally compressed, 4–6 mm long, single-flowered, lacking glumes
- **Roots:** Fibrous, arising from nodes of stolons and at the base; capable of forming AMF associations

## Ecology and Habitat

L. hexandra occupies a distinctive [[ecological-niche]] in tropical and subtropical freshwater ecosystems:

- **Aquatic habitats:** Shallow water of ponds, lake margins, slow-moving streams, irrigation ditches, and rice paddies
- **Wetland types:** Freshwater marshes, wet prairies, swamp margins
- **Water regimes:** Semi-aquatic to fully aquatic; roots typically submerged or in saturated soils
- **Geographic range:** Pantropical — found throughout the southeastern U.S., Central and South America, Africa, Asia, and Australia
- **Growth habit:** Forms dense floating or emergent mats that can cover large areas of water surface

## Key Facts

- **Mycorrhizal colonization under inundation:** Miller (2000) found that L. hexandra maintains arbuscular mycorrhizal infection in its roots even under semi-aquatic conditions, though colonization decreases as water depth increases.
- **Water depth control:** Water depth was identified as the primary factor controlling mycorrhizal colonization in L. hexandra, more significant than interspecific plant differences.
- **Weed significance:** In some regions, L. hexandra is considered a noxious weed in rice paddies and irrigation canals, where its dense mats impede water flow.
- **Forage value:** In other contexts, it serves as a palatable forage grass for livestock when accessible in pastures and wet meadows.
- **Habitat creation:** Dense stands provide important habitat for aquatic invertebrates, fish fry, and waterfowl.

## Mycorrhizal Associations

L. hexandra maintains functional arbuscular mycorrhizal associations even in flooded conditions:

- **Colonization type:** Arbuscular mycorrhizal with typical structures (arbuscules, vesicles, hyphae)
- **Depth response:** Colonization decreases with increasing water depth, reflecting reduced oxygen availability
- **Ecological function:** Mycorrhizal associations likely enhance phosphorus acquisition in the typically nutrient-poor sediments of tropical wetlands
- **Comparison:** Shows similar patterns of depth-dependent colonization to co-occurring [[panicum-hemitomon]] in southeastern U.S. wetlands

## Interactions with Other Organisms

- **[[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi]]:** Functional AMF associations persist under semi-aquatic conditions
- **Aquatic fauna:** Dense mats provide habitat and refugia for fish, amphibians, and invertebrates
- **Waterfowl:** Seeds and vegetative parts consumed by various waterfowl species
- **Livestock:** Palatable forage when accessible, though dense wetland growth limits grazing access

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

L. hexandra has relevance across several applied and research domains:

- **Mycorrhizal ecology research:** A model species for studying the persistence and function of arbuscular mycorrhizae under anaerobic, waterlogged conditions
- **Rice paddy ecology:** As a member of the Oryzoideae and common rice paddy associate, studies of its mycorrhizal status inform understanding of fungal symbiosis in cultivated rice systems
- **Wetland restoration:** Dense mat-forming habit makes it potentially useful for bank stabilization and wetland creation projects
- **[[invasive-species-management]]:** Understanding its mycorrhizal dependencies may inform management strategies where it is a problematic weed
