# AL

AL (4-allyloxy-3,5-dimethoxyphenethylamine) is a psychedelic phenethylamine and one of the most potent 3,4,5-trisubstituted phenethylamines yet described.

It appears as PiHKAL entry #2, first explored by the Czech chemist Otakar Leminger in Prague.

AL represents one of the few simple phenethylamines with an allyloxy group that demonstrates significant central activity.

The allyloxy functional group (-O-CH2-CH=CH2) contains a double bond that distinguishes it from the more common alkoxy substituents found in mescaline analogues.

## Dosage and Duration

Dosage range: 20 to 35 milligrams.

Duration: 8 to 12 hours.

Dosages above 35 milligrams remain unexplored.

The compound was reported by Leminger as the most active of all the compounds he had studied, with activity at 20 milligrams, perceptual changes, color enhancement, and difficult dreams during sleep that night.

Some effects persisted for more than 12 hours.

## Qualitative Effects

At 24 milligrams, onset occurs within 10 minutes as a pleasant increase in energy.

The development is "nice, smooth."

Positive and negative feelings develop simultaneously during the first hour.

Following a suggestion, the subject ate food despite not being hungry, and "to my surprise all the negative feelings dropped away."

This allowed free-flowing creative expression and deep laughter described as "working through buried depressions inside me and freeing me."

The experience is characterized by clear-headedness and abundant energy persisting throughout the day and evening.

One subject reported a "profound sense of the Presence and great love and gratitude for the place, the people, and the activities taking place."

The comedown is gradual and smooth; food tastes wonderful, and sleep arrives readily despite continuing energy.

A second 24-milligram report describes extremely gradual and graceful onset, with the first clear alert at approximately 50 minutes.

The peak arrives at one hour and fifteen minutes.

All sensory modalities remain functional with cogent, fluid speech and unusual ease of free association.

Affect is pleasant but not exalting, and the material is described as "wholly social in nature."

No visual, auditory, or olfactory sharpening occurs.

The plateau is unusually long. Sleep was difficult for several hours despite taking 25 mg of Librium.

The following day was "lethargic and slow" with a sense that effects had not worn off until midday.

At 35 milligrams, the subject reached a distinct +1 within 35 minutes and +2 by the end of the first hour.

The experience was "somewhat dissociated" with an inability to connect with feelings, despite a clear mind.

A hangover feeling developed by the end of the experiment.

## Historical Context

AL was first explored in Prague by Otakar Leminger, who published synthetic details and activity data in a Czech-language paper.

Leminger worked for years in industry and published this research only upon retirement.

He lived at Usti (called the Elbe in German), directly north of Prague.

Shulgin speculated that there might be other unpublished discoveries from Leminger's laboratory sitting in some farm house attic in Northern Czechoslovakia.

A young man named Stanislov Wistupkin brought the compound to Shulgin's attention, claiming to have discovered several new psychedelic drugs.

Whether Wistupkin was a student of Leminger's remains unknown.

The Czechoslovakian publication that presented AL and MEPEA also described [[escaline-entity]], [[proscaline]], and the allyloxy analogue (AL).

This is the only published material dealing with psychedelic drugs that Shulgin was ever able to find from the laboratory of Otakar Leminger.
