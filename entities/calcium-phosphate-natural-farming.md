## Description

Calcium phosphate is a bionutrient used in Korean Natural Farming as a **stage-specific supplement** applied during the critical changeover period when plants transition from vegetative growth to flowering and fruiting. Based on the theory of Nutrioperiodism (Yasushi Inoue, 1930s), calcium phosphate addresses the plant's "morning sickness" — the increased nutritional demand during the shift to reproductive growth.

## Classification

- **Domain**: Agricultural input / Mineral bionutrient
- **Chemical**: Ca₃(PO₄)₂ (tricalcium phosphate in natural form)
- **Origin**: Korean Natural Farming / Nutrioperiodism theory
- **Primary Use**: Changeover period nutrition for flowering/fruiting support

## Key Facts

- Calcium phosphate is considered critical during the **changeover period** — the transition between vegetative growth and reproductive (flowering/fruiting) stages.
- The changeover period is described as the stage that most determines the **quality of the final reproductive output** (fruit/flower quality and yield).
- Natural farmers describe the plant's need at this stage as analogous to **"morning sickness"** — the plant needs additional nutrition to support the emerging reproductive process.
- Applied as a foliar spray or soil drench during the changeover period to improve plant health and productivity.
- The preparation method transforms common waste materials (eggshells) into a potent, bioavailable calcium phosphate solution.

## Preparation Method (Natural)

1. **Collect eggshells** — a readily available household waste material
2. **Roast the eggshells** thoroughly to generate ashes (this converts calcium carbonate to calcium oxide and releases phosphorus compounds)
3. **Submerge roasted eggshells** in approximately equal visual volume of vinegar (acetic acid)
4. **Allow to react** for a couple of weeks until the eggshells are practically dissolved/broken down by the vinegar acids
5. **Dilute the resulting solution** with 20 parts water
6. **Apply** by spraying on plant foliage or watering into soil during the changeover period

## The Chemistry

- Eggshells are primarily **calcium carbonate** (CaCO₃)
- Roasting converts some carbonate to oxide and releases bound minerals
- Vinegar (acetic acid) dissolves the calcium compounds, creating **calcium acetate** in solution
- The acidic extraction also solubilizes **phosphorus** present in the eggshell matrix
- The resulting solution provides bioavailable calcium and phosphate ions

## Role in Nutrioperiodism

According to Inoue's theory, nutrient requirements follow a stage-specific pattern:

| Stage | Primary Nutrient | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Vegetative | Nitrogen | Leaf and stem growth |
| Changeover | **Calcium Phosphate** | Transition support |
| Reproductive | Potassium | Flowering and fruiting |

Calcium phosphate fills the gap between the nitrogen-dominant vegetative phase and the potassium-dominant reproductive phase. Without it, the transition can result in reduced flower quality, poor fruit set, or overall diminished reproductive output.

## Alternative Sources

- **Soybean stem ash** is described as an excellent source of calcium phosphate for this purpose
- Rock phosphate can also be fermented with BIM to create bioavailable calcium phosphate solutions

## Relevance to Cultivation

For cultivators, calcium phosphate prepared by this method provides:
- A **zero-cost calcium and phosphorus supplement** from waste eggshells
- **Stage-specific nutrition** that supports the critical transition to flowering/fruiting
- Improved plant health during a vulnerable developmental period
- A natural alternative to commercial calcium-phosphorus fertilizers
- Compatible with organic and natural farming certification standards

The principle of using eggshell-derived calcium is widely applicable beyond Korean Natural Farming — many traditional farming systems recognize the value of eggshell amendments for soil calcium supplementation.
