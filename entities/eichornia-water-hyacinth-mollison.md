## Description


Eichornia (commonly spelled Eichhornia), or water hyacinth, is a free-floating aquatic perennial plant native to the Amazon basin but now widespread across tropical and subtropical waterways worldwide. In permaculture systems, despite being one of the world's most invasive aquatic weeds, water hyacinth is strategically harvested and used as a valuable mulch and nutrient source for tropical gardens.

## Classification


- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Clade**: Tracheophytes
- **Clade**: Angiosperms
- **Clade**: Monocots
- **Order**: Commelinales
- **Family**: Pontederiaceae
- **Genus**: Eichhornia
- **Notable species**: Eichhornia crassipes (water hyacinth)

## Key Facts


- Water hyacinth is one of the fastest-growing plants known, capable of doubling its population in as little as 6-18 days
- Each plant can produce thousands of seeds per year, and the seeds can remain viable for up to 30 years
- It forms dense floating mats that block sunlight, deplete oxygen, and obstruct waterways
- Despite its invasiveness, water hyacinth has been successfully used as crop mulch in many tropical areas
- The plant accumulates nutrients (especially nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium) from the water column, making it an effective bioaccumulator
- Water hyacinth biomass has a high moisture content (approximately 95%) and decomposes rapidly when used as mulch

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology


- **Mulch source**: Harvested water hyacinth is applied directly to garden beds as nutrient-rich mulch, particularly valuable in areas where organic matter is scarce
- **Nutrient cycling**: The plants absorb dissolved nutrients from eutrophic waterways; when transferred to gardens, these nutrients are returned to the soil
- **Weed suppression**: Thick applications of water hyacinth mulch effectively suppress garden weeds while retaining soil moisture
- **Compost ingredient**: Water hyacinth can be composted with dry carbon-rich materials (sawdust, straw) to create balanced compost
- **Bioremediation**: Can be used to clean nutrient-polluted water before the biomass is repurposed as mulch
- **Biogas production**: Water hyacinth can be processed in anaerobic digesters to produce biogas, with the remaining slurry used as fertiliser
- **Fungal habitat**: Decomposing water hyacinth provides habitat for diverse saprophytic fungi involved in nutrient cycling

## Management Considerations


- Must be harvested regularly to prevent re-infestation of waterways
- Should be dried or composted before transport to prevent accidental spread
- Seeds remain viable even after drying; hot composting (above 55C) is recommended to destroy seeds
- Some regions legally classify water hyacinth as a noxious weed; check local regulations before cultivation
- Best used as a "harvest and remove" resource from existing infestations rather than deliberately cultivated
