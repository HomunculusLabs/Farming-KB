## Spider Mite

## Description

Spider mites are tiny arachnid pests belonging to the family Tetranychidae. They are among the most common and destructive foliar pests in agriculture, horticulture, and greenhouse production. Spider mites feed by piercing plant cells and extracting cell contents, causing characteristic stippling, yellowing, and eventually leaf death. [[molasses-and-sugars-in-compost-tea-brewing]] research, spider mites are notable because they are consistently reported to decline when plants are treated with [[actively-aerated-compost-tea-aact-brewing]] tea (AACT).

## Classification

- **Class**: Arachnida
- **Order**: Trombidiformes
- **Family**: Tetranychidae
- **Common Species**: Two-spotted spider mite (Tetranychus urticae), European red mite (Panonychus ulmi)

## Key Facts

- Spider mites thrive in hot, dry conditions and reproduce rapidly, completing a life cycle in as few as 7 days under optimal conditions
- They produce fine webbing on leaf undersides, which protects them from predators and [[hamilton-organic-approved-chemical-sprays]]
- Nearly everyone who works with AACT reports that foliar insects including spider mites disappear when spraying begins
- The evidence for spider mite control via compost tea is largely anecdotal — controlled scientific studies with entomologists are still needed
- Multiple mechanisms may explain mite reduction: repugnant biology on leaf surfaces, elevated CO2 levels, consumption of stress-produced alcohols, and improved plant health

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

### Mechanisms of Mite Reduction via Compost Tea
1. **Repulsion**: Biology applied to the leaf surface may be repugnant to spider mites, preventing landing and colonization
2. **CO2 Disruption**: Beneficial organisms elevate CO2 on leaf surfaces, which may provide incorrect attraction signals for mites
3. **Stress Alcohol Consumption**: Tea organisms consume alcohols produced by stressed plants, removing the attractant that draws mites
4. **Improved Plant Health**: Tea organisms help plants become less stressed, making them less susceptible to mite attack

### Research Needs
- Controlled studies with entomologists are needed to document the reduction and disappearance of spider mite populations
- The specific organisms responsible for mite repulsion have not been fully identified
- Dosage, frequency, and timing protocols need scientific validation
- All four proposed mechanisms may be operating simultaneously

### Biological Control Integration
- Local, indigenous parasites and diseases of mites should be identified and encouraged
- A local microbiologist or SFI advisor can assist with field-level assessment
- Plate count methods are inadequate for detecting active organism changes related to mite control
- [[aact-plate-counts-vs-direct-microscopy-molecular-dna-testing-ingham]] assessment of active organisms is the preferred monitoring method

### Management Considerations
- Mite problems often indicate an imbalance in the predator-prey food web
- Pesticide applications that kill mite predators frequently exacerbate mite outbreaks
- Restoring the complete food web including mite predators provides sustainable control
- Fungal-dominant compost tea appears most effective for foliar [[aact-mite-management-and-biocontrol-fungi]]
