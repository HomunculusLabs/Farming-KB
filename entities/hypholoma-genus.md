## Hypholoma (Genus)

## Description

Hypholoma is a genus of wood-inhabiting mushrooms that includes several cultivatable species, the most recognizable being the brick top ([[hypholoma-sublateritium]]). These mushrooms fruit on hardwood logs, stumps, and wood chip substrates, forming singly or in small clusters that tend to fuse at the base. The genus is valued for both culinary and medicinal applications, with a mild nutty flavor and crunchy texture that makes them versatile in cooking.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Strophariaceae
- **Genus:** Hypholoma

## Key Species

- **H. sublateritium** — Brick top mushroom, the most commonly cultivated species
- **H. capnoides** — Similar in appearance but with smoky gray gills
- **H. fasciculare** — Sulfur tuft, considered poisonous and a dangerous lookalike

## Key Facts

- Wood-inhabiting genus fruiting on hardwood logs, stumps, and supplemented substrates
- Mushrooms form singly or in small clusters, often fusing at the base
- Similar in texture to black poplar mushrooms ([[agrocybe-aegerita-detailed]])
- Mild nutty flavor with a crunchy bite
- Protein content: 20–30 percent (dry weight) — among the most protein-rich of all [[fungi-cultivated-mushrooms-pathogens-competitors]]
- Requires cooler fruiting temperatures, below 60°F (16°C)
- Difficulty level: Outdoor cultivation 2/5, [[cannabis-sativa-indoor-cultivation-techniques]] 3/5

## Cultivation Notes

### Substrate Preferences

- Hardwood logs, stumps, and wood chips for outdoor cultivation
- [[oyster-mushroom-supplemented-sawdust-blocks]] for indoor cultivation
- Prefers high-density fruiting substrates

### Fruiting Conditions

- Requires temperatures below 60°F (16°C) for [[fruiting-initiation]]
- Expose rested and hydrated containers to cool temperatures at intervals coinciding with flushing records
- Expect two flushes several weeks apart
- Yields rated as fair

### Outdoor Cultivation

- Grow on logs, stumps, or wood chip beds
- Use fresh wood (cut no more than one month before inoculation)
- Preferred spawn types: sawdust, plug, or cardboard spawn
- Inoculation to fruiting time varies with conditions

### Indoor Cultivation

- Use supplemented sawdust or sterilized wood-based substrates
- Grain or supplemented sawdust spawn preferred
- Requires cold shock to initiate fruiting
- Maintain high humidity during [[growing-gourmet-primordia-formation-pinning]]

## Harvesting

- Pull entire attachment of mushrooms out of substrate
- Trim excess waste away before storing
- Mushrooms tend to fuse at base into clusters

## Storage

- Fresh: two to three weeks at 38–42°F (3–6°C)
- Dried caps rehydrate well
- Stems can be powdered into flour for breads, pastas, soups, and sauces

## Marketing

- Similar in texture to Agrocybe aegerita (black poplar mushroom)
- Mild nutty flavor with a crunchy bite
- Delicious sautéed, in stir-fries, and warm salads
- Sell wrapped in bundles, since they tend to fuse at the base into clusters
- The "brick top" common name is recognizable and marketable

## Nutritional and Medicinal Properties

- Protein content: 20–30 percent (dry weight) — exceptionally high among cultivated mushrooms
- Contains antiviral, antibacterial, and anti-inflammatory compounds
- Shown to suppress many cancer cell lines
- Fibrous texture contributes to dietary fiber content
- Among the most protein-rich of all cultivated mushrooms

## Mycoremediation Potential

- Cooler fruiting temperature window makes this genus suitable for mycoremediation in cooler climates
- Ability to inhibit bacteria at lower temperatures
- Can degrade chemicals at lower temperatures than many other genera
- The entire genus should be explored for mycoremediation projects in cool climate applications

## Identification

- Brick-colored to reddish-brown caps (in H. sublateritium)
- Gills attached, light-colored when young, darkening with age
- White [[spore-print]]
- Grows in clusters on dead or dying hardwood
- Caution: Must be distinguished from poisonous H. fasciculare (sulfur tuft)

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Hypholoma species represent an underutilized genus in commercial [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]]. Their exceptional protein content (among the highest of any cultivated mushroom) and strong medicinal properties make them excellent candidates for expanded cultivation. The genus's ability to fruit at cooler temperatures and its antibacterial properties make it particularly promising for [[mycoremediation-applications]] in temperate and cold climates. The brick top (H. sublateritium) is the most commercially viable species in the genus.
