# Trichoderma virens

**Type:** Species (Ascomycete)
**Category:** Mycoparasitic Biocontrol Agent
**Also known as:** Gliocladium virens (Miller, Giddens and Foster) von Arx

## Overview
Trichoderma virens is a polyphagous mycoparasitic ascomycete and
one of the most effective [[fukuoka-textdoc-biological-control-fallacy-pheromone-critique]] agents against soil-
borne plant pathogens. Formerly known as Gliocladium virens, the
species was reclassified following modern phylogenetic analysis.
T. virens has been widely tested against [[pythium-ultimum]] damping-
off in cotton (Howell 1982, 1987, 1991) and other crops,
demonstrating consistent efficacy across diverse agricultural
systems.

The species produces gliotoxin, a member of the
epipolythiodioxopiperazine class of fungal metabolites, which is
a potent antibiotic effective against a wide range of fungal
pathogens. The toxinogenic T. virens efficiently suppressed
Pythium ultimum in cotton fields (Howell 1991), and suppression
of damping-off of peas was achieved in a commercial [[solomon-potting-mix-and-seedling-soil-recipe]]
(Howell 1991).

T. virens is also rhizosphere-competent, able to colonize plant
roots and persist in the soil environment. It is isolated from
soil using medium with benomyl, sodium propionate, rose bengal,
and antibacterial antibiotics supplemented with gliotoxin and
acriflavine (Park et al. 1992). This selective medium exploits
the species' resistance to its own toxin.

## Taxonomy and Classification
- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Sordariomycetes
- **Order:** Hypocreales
- **Family:** Hypocreaceae
- **Genus:** Trichoderma
- **Species:** T. virens

T. virens was historically classified as Gliocladium virens
(Miller, Giddens and Foster) von Arx. The transfer to Trichoderma
reflects molecular phylogenetic evidence placing the species
within the Trichoderma/Hypocrea clade. The old name still appears
extensively in biocontrol literature, and the species is
sometimes discussed alongside the closely related T. harzianum
and T. viride.

## Key Details

### Gliotoxin Production
Gliotoxin is the hallmark [[fungal-elicitors-enhanced-secondary-metabolite-production]] of T. virens and a
member of the epipolythiodioxopiperazine (ETP) class. The
compound contains a disulfide bridge that is essential for its
biological activity. Gliotoxin inhibits a broad range of
microorganisms including bacteria, fungi, and even mammalian
immune cells, though its plant toxicity is relatively low at
concentrations relevant to biocontrol.

The production of gliotoxin gives T. virens a significant
competitive advantage in soil, as it can suppress competitors
before direct hyphal contact. The species' resistance to its own
toxin is an important trait that allows it to occupy niches where
other fungi are excluded by gliotoxin accumulation.

### Biocontrol Mechanisms
T. virens employs multiple complementary mechanisms for
biocontrol: mycoparasitism (direct hyphal attack on pathogen
hyphae), antibiosis (gliotoxin production), competition for
nutrients and space, and [[endophytic-mycorrhizal-induced-systemic-resistance]] resistance in host
plants. The combination of these mechanisms provides robust
protection against diverse soil-borne pathogens.

### Efficacy Against Soil Pathogens
T. virens efficiently suppressed Pythium ultimum in cotton fields
(Howell 1991). Suppression of damping-off of peas was achieved in
a commercial potting mix. Alongside [[coniothyrium-minitans]], T.
virens was identified as one of the most active agents against
[[sclerotinia-sclerotiorum]], both singly and in combination (Whipps
and Budge 1990).
