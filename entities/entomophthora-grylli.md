## Description

**Entomophthora grylli** is an entomopathogenic fungus in the phylum Entomophthoromycota that primarily infects grasshoppers and related orthopteran insects. It is notable for causing dramatic population crashes in grasshopper species, particularly during periods of high humidity and precipitation.









## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Phylum**: Entomophthoromycota
- **Class**: Entomophthoromycetes
- **Order**: Entomophthorales
- **Family**: Entomophthoraceae
- **Genus**: Entomophthora
- **Species**: E. grylli









## Key Facts

- Caused near-complete eradication of the grasshopper [[cammula-pellucida]] in Saskatchewan in 1963 (Pickford and Riegert, 1964)
- High incidence correlated with unusually high precipitation and humidity in June and July
- Functions as a density-dependent population regulator of grasshoppers
- Infection requires specific environmental conditions: warm, wet conditions significantly increase entomopathogenic fungal activity
- Has been investigated as a potential biocontrol agent for grasshopper pests









## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

E. grylli demonstrates the potential of [[entomopathogenic-fungi]] as natural biocontrol agents against insect pests. For mushroom cultivators, understanding entomopathogenic fungi is relevant for managing fungus gnat (Mycetophilidae) and other dipteran pests that infest cultivation facilities. The species' dependence on high humidity for infection parallels conditions in many [[mushroom-growing-environments]], suggesting both opportunity and risk for natural pest control in cultivation settings.









## Ecological Role

E. grylli serves as a natural population regulator of grasshopper populations in grassland ecosystems. Its epizootic outbreaks can dramatically reduce herbivore pressure on vegetation, indirectly affecting plant community composition and nutrient cycling. The fungus exemplifies how entomopathogenic fungi can exert top-down control on herbivore populations, with cascading effects throughout the ecosystem.
