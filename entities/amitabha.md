# Amitabha

## Description

Amitabha is the Buddha of Infinite Light in the Tibetan Buddhist pantheon.

He is one of the five [[dhyani-buddhas]] of the [[sambhoga-kaya]].

Amitabha is associated with the fire element and the color red.

He represents the transmutation of desire and attachment into discriminating wisdom.

In the [[bardo-thodol]], Amitabha appears on a specific day of the Second Bardo visions.

## Classification

- **Tradition**: Tibetan Buddhism (Vajrayana) / Mahayana Buddhism
- **Category**: Dhyani Buddha — second of five
- **Element**: Fire
- **Color**: Red
- **Direction**: West
- **Wisdom**: Discriminating Awareness

## Key Facts

1. Amitabha means "Infinite Light" — he embodies the boundless radiance of awakened awareness.

2. He is the red Buddha, associated with the fire element and the western direction.

3. Amitabha transmutes the poison of desire/attachment into the wisdom of discrimination.

4. In the Bardo Thodol, Amitabha appears accompanied by his consort and attending deities.

5. He is one of the most widely revered Buddhas in East Asian Buddhism, particularly in Pure Land traditions.

6. In *The Psychedelic Experience*, Amitabha is associated with Vision 3 instructions.

7. The fire element connection links Amitabha to transformative, purifying energy in the psychedelic context.

8. His pure land is Sukhavati — a realm of bliss accessible through devotion and meditative focus.

9. Amitabha's wisdom addresses the challenge of desire and attachment that arises during psychedelic experiences.

10. Recognition of Amitabha in the Bardo visions represents liberation through the transmutation of desire.

## Relevance to Psychedelic Studies

In *The Psychedelic Experience*, Amitabha appears as one of the archetypal visions of the Second Bardo.

The red, fiery quality of Amitabha corresponds to the intense emotional and sensory experiences during psychedelic sessions.

The instruction for Amitabha's vision guides the voyager to recognize the fire-like quality of psychedelic perception as an expression of discriminating wisdom.

Rather than being consumed by desire or overwhelmed by sensory intensity, the practitioner is instructed to merge with the light of Amitabha.

This represents a key principle: the energies that bind us in suffering are the same energies that can liberate us when properly recognized.

The Amitabha vision is particularly relevant to experiences of overwhelming bliss, beauty, or sensory richness.

## Relation to Other Entities

- **Dharma-Kaya**: The formless ground from which Amitabha emanates as a Sambhoga-Kaya expression.
- **Sambhoga-Kaya**: The visionary body through which Amitabha manifests.
- **Tri-Kaya**: The three-fold doctrine within which Amitabha operates as a Second Bardo deity.
- **Herukas**: The wrathful counterparts to peaceful deities like Amitabha.
- **Clear Light**: The primordial light from which Amitabha's "Infinite Light" emanates.

## Psychological Significance

Amitabha's association with fire and desire speaks to one of the most powerful aspects of psychedelic experience.

The overwhelming sensory richness and emotional intensity can either bind or liberate the voyager.

Amitabha's wisdom of discrimination provides the key: discerning the nature of desire without being consumed by it.

This is the alchemical transformation — the base metal of craving becomes the gold of wisdom.

## Broader Buddhist Context

In Pure Land Buddhism, Amitabha is the most widely practiced devotional figure in the Buddhist world.

Practitioners recite his name (Nembutsu: "Namu Amida Butsu") to attain rebirth in his Western Pure Land.

This devotional tradition represents a different approach to the same goal: liberation through Amitabha's infinite light.

In the Vajrayana context of the Bardo Thodol, Amitabha appears as one of five Dhyani Buddhas.
