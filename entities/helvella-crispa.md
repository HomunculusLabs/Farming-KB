## Overview

**Helvella crispa**, commonly known as the **white saddle** or **fluted white helvella**, is a species of
cup fungus in the family Helvellaceae (order Pezizales).
It is widely distributed across [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]] of the Northern Hemisphere and is one of the more
frequently encountered species in the genus Helvella.

The mushroom is distinctive for its saddle-shaped, irregularly lobed cap and its deeply ribbed,
fluted stipe. It is generally considered edible when thoroughly cooked, though some authorities
recommend caution due to variable reports of gastrointestinal upset in sensitive individuals.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** [[ascomycota]]
- **Class:** Pezizomycetes
- **Order:** Pezizales
- **Family:** Helvellaceae
- **Genus:** Helvella
- **Species:** H. crispa (Scop.) Fr. (1822)

The basionym is *Peziza crispa* Scop. (1772). Elias Magnus Fries transferred it to *Helvella* in his
Systema Mycologicum (1822). Several infraspecific taxa have been described but none are widely
accepted; the species is considered monotypic.

## Morphology

### Cap

The cap is 1–5 cm in diameter, saddle-shaped when young, becoming irregularly lobed and often
contorted with maturity. The upper surface is smooth to slightly wrinkled, whitish to cream-colored,
occasionally developing pale tan or pinkish tones in age. The margin is free from the stipe and
often undulating or lobed. The hymenium (fertile surface) covers the upper surface of the cap.

### Stipe

The stipe is 3–8 cm tall and 1–2 cm wide, prominently fluted or ribbed with deep longitudinal
grooves, giving it a ornate, architectural appearance.
It is hollow and chambered internally. Color ranges from white to pale cream, sometimes with a
blush of pinkish or tan, especially at the base. The ribs are typically whitish.

### Flesh

The flesh is thin, brittle, and waxy in the cap. It is whitish and hollow in the stipe.
It has a mild, indistinct odor and a mildly pleasant taste.

### Microscopic Features

Asci are 8-spored, operculate, measuring 200–280 × 12–16 µm. Ascospores are ellipsoid, smooth,
hyaline, containing one or two large guttules, measuring 17–22 × 11–14 µm. Paraphyses are
filamentous, septate, slightly clavate at the tips, 2–4 µm wide.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Helvella crispa* has a broad distribution across temperate regions of Europe, [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]], and
Asia. In Europe, it is one of the most commonly collected Helvella species, found from Scandinavia
to the Mediterranean.

It fruits on the ground in deciduous and mixed forests, particularly in association with oaks
(*Quercus* spp.), beech (*Fagus* spp.), and birch (*Betula* spp.). It often grows along forest
paths, in disturbed soil, and in mossy areas. Fruiting occurs from late summer through autumn,
typically August to November, with peak abundance in September and October.
