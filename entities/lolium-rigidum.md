## Lolium rigidum

## Description

*Lolium rigidum*, commonly known as Wimmera rye grass or annual ryegrass, is an annual grass species in the family Poaceae. Mollison lists it among salt-tolerant plants capable of thriving in soils with more than 0.4% sodium chloride, useful for maintaining productive pasture on salt-affected agricultural land in dryland regions.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Family:** Poaceae (grass family)
- **Genus:** *Lolium*
- **Species:** *L. rigidum*
- **Common names:** Wimmera rye grass, annual ryegrass, stiff darnel
- **Growth habit:** Annual grass, 20–80 cm tall

## Salt Tolerance

Mollison includes *Lolium rigidum* among plants that can thrive at **>0.4% sodium chloride**:

- More salt-tolerant than most improved pasture grasses
- Maintains reasonable production on moderately saline soils
- Useful as a transitional pasture species while salinity is being addressed
- Responds well to improved drainage and fresh water flushing

## Agricultural Significance

*Lolium rigidum* is one of the most important pasture grasses in Australian agriculture:

- **Winter-active annual:** Provides high-quality winter and spring feed
- **High nutritive value:** Excellent digestibility, high crude protein
- **Rapid establishment:** Germinates quickly with autumn rains
- **High productivity:** Produces significant dry matter per hectare
- **Palatability:** Highly palatable to all classes of livestock

## Role in Dryland Salinity Management

Mollison includes Wimmera rye grass alongside other salt-tolerant species for supplanting affected crops:

- **Saltbushes (*Atriplex*)**
- **Acacias**
- **[[maireana]]* spp.)**
- **Samphire (*Crithmum*, *Salicornia*)**
- **Tall wheat grass (*Agropyron*)**

Rye grass fills the annual pasture niche on mildly to moderately saline sites.

## Herbicide Resistance Note

*Lolium rigidum* has developed widespread herbicide resistance in Australian agriculture, making it simultaneously one of the most valuable pasture species and one of the most problematic crop weeds. This duality is relevant to [[interceptor-banks]] are being installed
- **Winter feed:** Provides high-quality cool-season forage in dryland systems
- **Soil building:** Dense root mass adds organic matter and improves soil structure
- **Understory grass:** Compatible with tree belts and windbreak systems
- **Seed production:** Self-seeding reduces need for reseeding

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

*Lolium rigidum* provides permaculture designers with a productive annual pasture option for the early stages of dryland salinity management. It is most valuable on mildly saline sites where interceptor banks have been installed and soil conditions are improving. Its high feed quality and self-regenerating habit make it suitable for low-input grazing systems. However, consistent with Mollison's broader philosophy, it should complement rather than replace structural solutions to the underlying salinity problem.
