## Paraglomus

## Description

Paraglomus is a small genus of [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi]] (AMF) belonging to the family Paraglomaceae within the order Glomales. Like Archaeospora, Paraglomus was delimited recently based on differences in selective rDNA primer sequences. The genus contains only two described species and represents a phylogenetically ancient lineage within the Glomales. [[paraglomus-occultum]], the type species, is found globally across diverse habitats and continents.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Eukarya
- **Phylum:** Zygomycota (Glomeromycota in modern taxonomy)
- **Order:** Glomales
- **Family:** Paraglomaceae
- **Genus:** Paraglomus

## Key Facts

- Contains only **two described species**.
- Placed in the family Paraglomaceae, one of two new families (along with Archaeosporaceae) established to accommodate phylogenetically ancient glomalean lineages.
- Paraglomus occultum is **globally distributed** across tropical and temperate latitudes, arid and mesic climates, and a wide range of habitats.
- Delimited based on selective rDNA primer sequences in PCR analyses (Morton and Redecker 2001).
- Additional species currently classified in other genera are expected to be transferred as new molecular analyses are conducted.

## Phylogenetic Significance

Paraglomus represents one of the most basal lineages within the Glomales. Its separation from other genera is supported by distinct 18S rDNA sequences detectable only through selective primer PCR. The global distribution of P. occultum, combined with its phylogenetically ancient status, suggests that this lineage has persisted since the early evolution of the [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-symbiosis]] over 400 million years ago.

## Ecological Distribution

- Found on all continents (except Antarctica) across tropical and temperate latitudes.
- Occurs in diverse habitats: deserts, grasslands, tropical forests, sand dunes, agricultural fields, and acid mine soils.
- Frequently recovered in trap cultures from sites in Kenya, Venezuela, Namibia, Arizona, Minnesota, and Virginia.
- Typically represented by one species per site in community surveys.
- Appears in first-cycle trap cultures, suggesting moderate to high inoculum potential.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Evolutionary biology:** Paraglomus provides evidence for the deep evolutionary history of the AM symbiosis and the ancient divergence of glomalean lineages.
- **Biogeography:** The pantropical and pan-temperate distribution of P. occultum makes it one of the most widely distributed AMF species, useful for comparative biogeographic studies.
- **Molecular diagnostics:** The reliance on selective PCR primers for identification highlights the importance of molecular methods in modern AMF taxonomy.
- **Community ecology:** The consistent presence of Paraglomus at low species richness (one species per site) across diverse habitats suggests ecological generalism within the genus.

## Isolation and Culture

- Cultured successfully on Sudangrass (Sorghum sudanense) in greenhouse pot cultures.
- Detected in trap-pot cultures from diverse global habitats.
- Standard wet-sieving and sucrose-density gradient centrifugation for spore extraction.
- Molecular identification using selective rDNA primer sequences recommended for reliable species-level identification.
