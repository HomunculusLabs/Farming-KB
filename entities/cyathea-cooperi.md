## Overview

**Cyathea cooperi**, commonly known as **Australian tree fern**, **cooper tree fern**, or **straw tree fern**, is a fast-growing tree fern in the family Cyatheaceae. Endemic to eastern Australia, it is one of the most commonly cultivated tree ferns in tropical and subtropical gardens worldwide.

The species produces a tall, slender trunk up to 15 m high, crowned with a spreading rosette of large, tripinnate fronds reaching 4–6 m long. Its rapid [[soil-temperature-plant-growth-rate-doubling-rule]] of up to 30 cm per year in ideal conditions makes it one of the fastest-growing tree fern species.

It has become a problematic invasive species in several regions outside its native range. In Hawaii, La Réunion, and parts of New Zealand, it displaces native vegetation in wet forests.

The trunk is covered with persistent leaf bases that form a distinctive lattice pattern. These leaf bases are light brown and create the characteristic straw-like appearance referenced in its common name.

Despite its common name, *Cyathea cooperi* is not closely related to the New Zealand tree fern *[[coleman-hardy-winter-vegetables-cold-tolerance-mechanisms]].

The species was named in honor of Daniel Cooper, a nineteenth-century Australian botanical collector. It was formally described by William Jackson Hooker in 1854.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Division:** Polypodiophyta
- **Family:** Cyatheaceae
- **Genus:** *Cyathea*
- **Species:** *Cyathea cooperi* (Hook. ex F.Muell.) Domin
- **Synonyms:** *Alsophila cooperi* Hook. ex F.Muell., *Sphaeropteris cooperi* (Hook. ex F.Muell.) R.M.Tryon

The trunk is erect, 8–15 cm in diameter, and bears conspicuous oval leaf scars. Fronds are bipinnate to tripinnate, bright green, and covered with pale scales and occasional prickles on the stipe.

Sori are round, positioned near the midvein on the undersides of fertile pinnae. Indusia are absent or rudimentary, a feature distinguishing it from many other *Cyathea* species.

Scales on the croziers and stipe bases are glossy brown, ovate, and entire. The crozier unfurling mechanism is circinate, typical of all ferns.

Spores are trilete, approximately 50–70 μm in diameter, and pale yellow when mature.

Phylogenetic analyses place *Cyathea cooperi* in the *Sphaeropteris* clade, sometimes recognized as a separate genus. This group is characterized by scaly trunks and lack of true indusia.

The species can be distinguished from the similar *[[cyathea-australis]]* by its smoother trunk, lighter-colored scales, and more finely divided fronds.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Cyathea cooperi* is native to the coastal ranges and tablelands of eastern Australia. It occurs from southeastern Queensland through New South Wales to eastern Victoria.

It grows in wet sclerophyll forest, subtropical and temperate rainforest, and along stream margins. The species prefers sheltered gullies with high humidity and consistent moisture.

Altitudinal range extends from near sea level to approximately 1,200 m. At higher elevations, plants are more compact and frost-tolerant.

The species thrives in organically rich, well-drained soils. It tolerates a range of soil types from volcanic loams to alluvial clays.

Rainfall in its native habitat ranges from 1,000 to 2,500 mm annually. The species requires reliable moisture throughout the year and does not tolerate prolonged drought.

In Australia, it is common along road cuttings in the Blue Mountains and Dorrigo Plateau. These populations show natural variation in frond size and trunk thickness.

## Cultivation

Australian tree fern is widely cultivated in tropical and subtropical gardens for its dramatic architectural form. It requires consistent moisture, humidity, and protection from strong winds.

It grows best in partial shade but tolerates full sun in [[berry-fruit-production-cool-humid-climates]]. In cooler regions it can be grown in containers and moved to shelter during winter.

Propagation is exclusively from spores, as the species does not produce offsets. [[organic-nutrients-vs-synthetic-nutrients]].

The species is hardy to approximately USDA zone 9b. Frost damage causes frond dieback but the trunk usually survives and resprouts.

The species responds well to balanced, slow-release fertilizers during [[stamets-growing-room-air-circulation-co2-management]].

## Invasiveness

*Cyathea cooperi* has naturalized and become invasive in Hawaii, La Réunion, Jamaica, and the Azores. In Hawaiian wet forests, it forms dense stands that shade out native understory species.
