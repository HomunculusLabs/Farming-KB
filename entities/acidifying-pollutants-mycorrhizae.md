# Acidifying Pollutants and Mycorrhizae

## Description

The interaction between acidifying pollutants (acid rain, sulfur dioxide, nitrogen oxides, and ozone) and mycorrhizal fungi is a major area of study in fungal ecology. Acidifying pollutants alter mycorrhizal community structure, colonization rates, fruiting body production, and the functional capacity of mycorrhizal symbioses in forest ecosystems. The effects are mediated through changes in soil chemistry, direct toxicity to fungal hyphae, and indirect effects via reduced host plant photosynthesis.

## Classification

- **Category:** Environmental stress factor / ecological interaction
- **Pollutant types:** Sulfur dioxide (SO2), nitrogen oxides (NOx), ozone (O3), sulfuric acid (H2SO4), ammonium nitrate (NH4NO3)
- **Affected systems:** Ectomycorrhizal, arbuscular mycorrhizal, and ericoid mycorrhizal associations

## Key Facts

### Pollutant Effects on Ectomycorrhizal Fungi

**Sulfur dioxide (SO2):**
- Field fumigation experiments (McLeod et al., 1992) showed SO2 reduced Paxillus involutus mycorrhizae on Scots pine
- Sulfite (SO3²⁻) at 12.5–100 mM negatively affected mycelial growth and spore germination of saprotrophic fungi (Dursun et al., 1996a)
- SO2 at 40 nl l⁻¹ reduced respiration of Mycena galopus on Sitka spruce litter by over 50%

**Nitrogen deposition:**
- Addition of ammonium sulfate to Norway spruce forests decreased fine root biomass but not overall ectomycorrhizal colonization (Kåren and Nylund, 1997)
- Nitrogen addition significantly reduced fruit body production of basidiomycete ectomycorrhizal species (Termorshuizen, 1990)
- High N loading (3x and 9x ambient) in beech woodlands caused near-complete cessation of mycorrhizal fungal fruiting (Rühling and Tyler, 1991)
- Specific responses varied: Cortinarius and Russula declined; Lactarius showed little change; Paxillus involutus and Lactarius rufus increased (Brandrud, 1995)

**Ozone (O3):**
- Ozone-tolerant provenances of loblolly pine showed less reduction in root surface area and mycorrhizal colonization than ozone-intolerant provenances (Qui et al., 1993)
- Ozone altered arbuscular mycorrhizal structure in sugar maple by reducing arbuscules but increasing vesicles and hyphal coils (Duckmanton and Widden, 1994)
- This shift likely represents a stress response, as vesicles are less energy-demanding than arbuscules

### Aluminum Toxicity

- Aluminum at 146 mM suppressed hyphal growth of Cenococcum, Pisolithus, and Thelephora (Thompson and Medve, 1984)
- Suillus showed no growth reduction below 1000 mM Al concentration
- Al:Ca, Al:Mg, and Al:PO4 ratios are critical determinants of fungal growth
- Low Al:PO4 ratios improved growth even at high Al concentrations, explained by Al absorption into polyphosphate droplets in fungal hyphae (Kottke and Oberwinkler, 1990)

### Phosphorus Cycling Disruption

- Acidifying pollutants increase P sorption capacity and decrease labile P concentration (Carreira et al., 2000)
- Acid phosphatase activity in soil decreased under acid misting (263 vs. 382 mg pNP h⁻¹ g⁻¹), increasing organic P pools
- Nitrogen saturation leads to reduced P availability, creating secondary nutrient limitation
- Fungal species with high phosphatase production may be favored in N-saturated soils

### NITREX Experiments

The NITREX (Nitrogen Saturation Experiments) project in Sweden provided key data:
- Control plots received natural N deposition; treatment plots received additional 35 kg ha⁻¹ y⁻¹ N
- Roofed plots excluded N deposition entirely
- PCA analysis showed shifts in ectomycorrhizal community structure with N addition
- Molecular analysis of root tips showed only 1–4% correspondence between fruit body species and mycorrhizae on roots (Jonsson, 1998)
- This poor correspondence complicates interpretation of fungal community surveys

### Mycorrhizal Diversity and Function

Baxter and Dighton (2001) demonstrated using controlled ectomycorrhizal diversity experiments that:
- Mycorrhizal diversity per se was a better determinant of improved birch seedling nutrient content than species composition
- The diversity effect was stronger for phosphorus than nitrogen uptake
- This supports the hypothesis that diverse mycorrhizal communities provide functional insurance for nutrient acquisition

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Understanding pollutant-mycorrhizae interactions is essential for:
1. **Site selection** for outdoor cultivation — areas with high atmospheric N deposition may have impaired mycorrhizal function
2. **Soil amendment strategies** — liming can offset acidification but shifts fungal community structure toward deciduous woodland assemblages
3. **Mycorrhizal inoculum selection** — species differ in tolerance to acidity, aluminum, and nitrogen loading
4. **Monitoring** — fruit body surveys may not accurately reflect below-ground mycorrhizal status
5. **Nutrient management** — excess nitrogen can suppress phosphorus acquisition by reducing phosphatase activity
