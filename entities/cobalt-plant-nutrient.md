## Description

Cobalt (Co) is an essential micronutrient for nitrogen fixation in legumes. It is a critical component of vitamin B12 (cobalamin), which is required by rhizobia bacteria in legume root nodules for the nitrogenase enzyme complex to function. Cobalt deficiency produces symptoms identical to nitrogen deficiency because it prevents nitrogen fixation at the biochemical level.

## Classification

- **Category**: Essential micronutrient (for nitrogen-fixing systems)
- **Symbol**: Co
- **Atomic number**: 27
- **Role**: Component of cobalamin (vitamin B12) required by nitrogen-fixing bacteria
- **Primary indicator**: Severe nitrogen deficiency in legumes despite adequate inoculation

## Key Facts

1. **Nitrogen fixation bottleneck**: Cobalt is required by rhizobia bacteria to synthesise vitamin B12, which is essential for nitrogenase function. Without cobalt, nodulated legumes cannot fix nitrogen despite having active rhizobia present.
2. **Misdiagnosis risk**: Cobalt deficiency is easily misdiagnosed as nitrogen deficiency because the symptoms are identical — pale, yellowing, spindly legume plants. The difference is that nitrogen fertiliser provides only a temporary fix while cobalt addresses the root cause.
3. **Peat and coastal soils**: Most common in peaty soils and coastal soils where cobalt has been leached or is naturally low.
4. **Legume-specific**: Cobalt is only essential for plants hosting nitrogen-fixing bacteria. Non-legumes have very low cobalt requirements.
5. **Application protocol**: For severe nitrogen deficiency with poor clover growth on peaty or coastal soils, apply cobalt at very low dilutions — 1 g per 10 square metres every 10 years.
6. **Manganese interaction**: High manganese levels can exacerbate cobalt deficiency. In high-manganese soils, adding lime at one handful per square metre helps restore the cobalt-manganese balance.
7. **Indicator plants**: Catchfly (Silene) species are biological indicators for cobalt in soils, concentrating the element from underlying deposits.

## Application Protocol

- **Rate**: Extremely low — 1 g per 10 square metres
- **Frequency**: Every 10 years approximately
- **Alkaline soils**: Apply as very dilute foliar spray
- **Acid soils**: Add lime to improve availability and reduce manganese interference
- **Timing**: Apply before sowing legumes in suspect soils

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Rhizobia support**: Cobalt directly enables the bacterial component of the nitrogen-fixing symbiosis that supports organic growing systems.
- **Mycorrhizal network health**: Legumes with functional nitrogen fixation (enabled by cobalt) contribute nitrogen to [[saprophytic-fungi|saprophytic-fungi]].
- **Compost nitrogen**: Legume biomass from cobalt-sufficient plants produces nitrogen-rich compost, the backbone of fungal-friendly growing media.
- **Biological farming**: Understanding cobalt is essential for biological farming systems that rely on legume-based nitrogen rather than synthetic fertilisers.
- **Mushroom substrate**: Cobalt levels in substrate materials affect the microbial community that supports mushroom mycelium during colonisation.
