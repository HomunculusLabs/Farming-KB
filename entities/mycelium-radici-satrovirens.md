## Description

**[[mycelium]] radicis atrovirens** (MRA) is a dark-pigmented (dematiaceous) ectomycorrhizal fungal species that forms associations with tree roots. It has been studied for its distinctive effects on the microbial community structure in the mycorrhizasphere, particularly on protozoan species composition.









## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Phylum**: Ascomycota
- **Class**: Dothideomycetes (provisional; identity of MRA complex debated)
- **Form genus**: Mycelium radicis atrovirens (morphotype designation)









## Key Facts

- Ingham and Massicotte (1994) showed it alters protozoan species composition in the mycorrhizasphere
- Grouped with Rhizopogon vulgaris, R. rubescens, and [[thelephora-terrestris]] in principal coordinate analysis of protozoan communities
- Colonizes roots of ponderosa pine ([[pinus-ponderosa]])
- Supports different bacterial communities than other ectomycorrhizal species on the same host
- The distinct protozoan communities suggest mycorrhizal species-specific effects on the broader [[soil-food-web]]









## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

MRA demonstrates that different mycorrhizal fungal species create distinct microbial communities in the rhizosphere through cascading effects on bacteria and protozoa. For mycologists and plant cultivators, this illustrates that the choice of mycorrhizal inoculant shapes the entire soil microbial ecosystem, not just plant nutrition. Understanding these multi-trophic effects is important for holistic soil management.









## Ecological Role

As an ectomycorrhizal fungus, MRA exchanges soil nutrients for plant-derived carbon. Its distinctive effect on protozoan and bacterial communities suggests it creates a unique microhabitat in the rhizosphere, influencing nutrient cycling rates and [[soil-food-web-structure]]. The species contributes to below-ground biodiversity supporting forest ecosystem function.
