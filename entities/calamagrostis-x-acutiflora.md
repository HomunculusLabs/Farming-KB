## Taxonomy and Morphology

*Calamagrostis × acutiflora* (DC.) Trin. belongs to the tribe Poeae within the subfamily Pooideae of Poaceae.

The × symbol in the name denotes its hybrid origin, first described by the Scottish botanist George Arnott Walker-Arnott.

Plants form dense, upright clumps reaching 1.5 to 2.0 meters in height when in flower.

Leaves are narrow, linear, and deep green, approximately 60 cm long and 8 to 12 mm wide.

The inflorescence is a narrow, feathery panicle 20 to 35 cm long, appearing in early to mid-summer.

Flowers are initially tinged with purple or pink, turning a warm straw-gold as they mature through autumn.

The panicles remain upright and attractive through winter, providing strong vertical structure in the garden.

Roots are fibrous and deep, forming a dense, non-invasive clump that does not spread by rhizomes.

## Distribution and Habitat

The parent species *C. epigejos* ranges across much of Europe and temperate Asia, while *C. arundinacea* is native to European woodlands and forest margins.

The hybrid itself occurs naturally where the ranges of the parent species overlap in central and eastern Europe.

It has been widely introduced in cultivation across [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-south-america]], Australia, and New Zealand.

In natural settings, the parent species occupy meadows, forest clearings, riparian margins, and disturbed ground.

The hybrid is not considered invasive in any region, unlike some closely related *Calamagrostis* species.

## Cultivation

Feather reed grass is exceptionally adaptable, thriving in USDA hardiness zones 4 through 9.

It prefers full sun but tolerates partial shade, particularly in hotter climates.

Plants accept a wide range of soil types including clay, loam, and sandy soils, provided drainage is adequate.

Once established, it is notably drought-tolerant, requiring minimal supplemental irrigation in [[fungal-roles-in-old-growth-forests]] to 5 to 10 cm above ground in late winter before new shoots emerge.

Plants are generally pest-free and resistant to most common grass diseases.

The cultivar 'Karl Foerster' is sterile and produces no viable seed, eliminating any risk of self-seeding.

Propagation is by division in spring or autumn, typically every 3 to 5 years to maintain vigor.

## Ecology and Applications

In designed landscapes, feather reed grass serves as a vertical accent, mass planting, or informal hedge.

Its narrow profile makes it effective for screening without consuming excessive ground area.

The dense root system provides soil stabilization on slopes and banks, reducing erosion.

Stems and dried panicles offer winter habitat and forage for birds and small mammals in garden settings.

The grass is increasingly used in green infrastructure projects including bioswales and rain gardens.

Its tolerance of periodic waterlogging and occasional drought makes it versatile in sustainable [[holzer-permaculture-landscape-design]].

Landscape architects frequently specify it for public plantings, commercial landscapes, and residential gardens.
