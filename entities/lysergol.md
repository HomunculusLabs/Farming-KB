The compound was first isolated and characterized from *Ipomoea* species
in the early 1970s. It occupies a critical node in ergoline biosynthesis,
bridging the simple clavine alkaloids (such as [[agroclavine]] and [[elymoclavine]])
with the more complex [[lysergic-acid]] derivatives.

## Chemical Properties

- **Molecular formula:** C₁₆H₁₈N₂O
- **Molecular weight:** 254.33 g/mol
- **IUPAC name:** (6aR,9R)-7-methyl-4,6,6a,7,8,9-hexahydroindolo[4,3-fg]quinoline-9-ol
- **Structure type:** Tetracyclic ergoline (clavine alkaloid subclass)
- **Solubility:** Sparingly soluble in water; soluble in ethanol, chloroform,
  and dilute acids
- **Melting point:** 210–212 °C (decomposes)

Lysergol features the characteristic tetracyclic ergoline ring system
comprising an indole moiety fused to a quinoline-derived bicyclic amine.
The 8β-hydroxyl group is the key distinguishing feature that differentiates
lysergol from agroclavine (which lacks the hydroxyl). This hydroxyl group
is introduced by the cytochrome P450 enzyme cloA in the fungal
biosynthetic pathway, converting the 8-position to an alcohol before
subsequent oxidation to the lysergic acid carboxyl group.

## Biosynthesis and Occurrence

Lysergol is produced as an intermediate in the ergoline alkaloid pathway,
primarily by sclerotia-forming *[[ipomoea-tricolor]]* ([[morning-glory-lsa-and-ergoline-alkaloids]]. The presence of lysergol in
plant seeds is attributed to symbiotic [[sacred-mexican-morning-glories-ololiuqui-and-tlitliltzin]] is now understood to
originate from seed-transmitted [[fungal-endophytes]] related to
*Periglandula* spp. (*Clavicipitaceae*). These endophytes colonize the
shoot apical meristem and produce the full spectrum of ergoline alkaloids
found in the seeds, including lysergol.

## Pharmacology

Lysergol acts as a partial agonist at serotonin 5-HT₂A receptors, though
with substantially lower affinity and efficacy than LSD or LSA.

- **5-HT₂A affinity (Kᵢ):** ~2,500 nM (vs. LSD ~3–10 nM)
- **5-HT₁A affinity:** Weak (estimated >10,000 nM)
- **Dopamine receptor activity:** Negligible

The mild psychoactive effects reported for lysergol-containing plant
preparations are primarily attributable to co-occurring alkaloids
