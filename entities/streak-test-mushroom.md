## Description

The [[streak-test]] is a simple but powerful diagnostic technique used in [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]] to screen liquid cultures for contamination. By dragging a sterilized loop dipped in [[liquid-culture]] across a fresh agar plate in a Z pattern, cultivators can visually identify whether bacteria or mold contaminants are present alongside the target mycelium. The technique is recommended before any expansion of liquid cultures into grain spawn to prevent propagating hidden contamination through large volumes of substrate.

## Classification

- **Category**: Diagnostic technique / Laboratory protocol
- **Purpose**: Contamination screening for liquid cultures and grain spawn slurries
- **Equipment**: Sterilized inoculation loop, fresh agar plate (no antibiotics), [[laminar-flow-hood]], alcohol lamp
- **Time to results**: Several days of incubation

## Key Facts

- Performed using a sterile loop dipped into liquid culture or grain spawn prepared as a slurry
- The loop is dragged across a fresh agar plate in a large Z pattern
- Must use agar with no added antibiotics to ensure all bacteria present will grow visibly
- [[malt-extract-agar]] is preferred over potato-based agar, since many bacteria species do not propagate well on potato media
- Plate is sealed with Parafilm or tape and incubated for several days
- A clean result shows a solid line of mycelium along the streak; any additional colonies indicate contamination
- If contamination is found, the entire lineage should be tested to locate the source
- Cotter recommends performing streak tests routinely before all liquid culture expansions

## Procedure

1. Prepare a fresh agar plate using malt extract agar (no antibiotics, no potato base)
2. Sterilize an inoculation loop in the flame of an alcohol lamp until red-hot, then cool
3. Dip the cooled loop into the liquid culture to be tested
4. Drag the loop across the agar surface in a large Z pattern
5. Seal the plate with Parafilm or tape
6. Incubate at appropriate temperature for several days
7. Observe for solid mycelial growth along the streak line
8. Any bacterial or mold colonies in addition to the mycelial streak indicate contamination

## Tracing Contamination Sources

When a streak test reveals contamination, the cultivator should conduct a systematic investigation:

1. Repeat the streak test on every preceding expansion generation of the contaminated culture
2. Work backwards from the original isolate (tissue culture or spore germination) through each expansion level
3. Identify the specific generation at which contamination was introduced
4. Revert to the last uncontaminated generation to maintain purity in the culture lines
5. Test agar media by transferring a wedge to a fresh plate
6. Test air filtration by leaving a fresh petri plate open for 60 seconds in the cleaned laminar workspace
7. If contamination appears on test plates, the HEPA filter may need replacement or the hood may need resealing

## Relevance to Cultivation

Streak testing is an essential quality control step before expanding liquid cultures into grain spawn. Using contaminated liquid culture to inoculate grain would propagate bacteria or mold through hundreds of pounds of substrate, wasting significant time and resources. The streak test provides a relatively quick and inexpensive way to verify culture purity before committing resources to large-scale spawn production. It is considered a standard best practice in both hobbyist and commercial mushroom cultivation operations.
