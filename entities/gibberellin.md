## Description

Gibberellin is a plant growth hormone first isolated from the fungus that causes **bakanae disease** ("foolish seedling disease") in rice. The hormone was discovered by Japanese researcher **[[eiichi-kurosawa]]**, who extracted it from the fungal culture — making gibberellin one of the first plant growth hormones to be identified. Fukuoka studied under Kurosawa at the Yokohama Customs Bureau research laboratory.

## Discovery and Properties

- **Discovered by**: Eiichi Kurosawa at the Yokohama Customs Bureau research laboratory
- **Source organism**: A fungus (later identified as *Gibberella fujikuroi*) that causes bakanae disease in rice
- **Effect on rice plants**: When a small amount is absorbed by young rice plants, it causes abnormally tall growth; in excess, it retards growth (the opposite reaction)
- **Global impact**: While initially ignored in Japan, the discovery became a topic of active research overseas. An American scientist later used gibberellin to develop the **seedless grape**

## Mechanism of Action

Gibberellin acts as a potent growth regulator with dose-dependent effects:

1. **Low doses**: Stimulate elongation of rice stems, causing the characteristic "foolish seedling" appearance of bakanae disease
2. **High doses**: Paradoxically retard plant growth
3. **Commercial applications**: Used to produce seedless grapes, promote uniform germination, and control plant height in various crops

## Bakanae Disease

Bakanae ("foolish seedling") disease is a condition of rice plants caused by infection with *Gibberella fujikuroi*. The fungus produces gibberellin, which causes infected seedlings to grow abnormally tall and spindly, often leading to plant death. The disease was the pathway through which gibberellin was discovered.

## Classification

- **Category**: Plant growth hormone / Chemical compound
- **Chemical class**: Diterpenoid acid
- **Source organism**: *Gibberella fujikuroi* (teleomorph of *Fusarium fujikuroi*)
- **Applications**: Agriculture, viticulture, horticulture

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Gibberellin occupies a unique position at the intersection of mycology and plant science. Its discovery from a pathogenic fungus demonstrated that fungal metabolites can profoundly influence plant growth and development. This understanding is relevant to:

- **[[mushroom-cultivation]]**: Understanding fungal metabolites and their effects on plant substrates
- **[[substrate-preparation]]**: Knowledge of how fungal enzymes and growth regulators modify organic matter
- **[[natural-farming]]**: Fukuoka's experience with gibberellin research informed his later understanding that natural systems self-regulate without human chemical intervention

## Key Facts

- First plant growth hormone isolated from a fungus
- Discovered by Eiichi Kurosawa in Japan
- Extracted from *Gibberella fujikuroi*, cause of bakanae disease in rice
- Low doses cause tall growth; high doses retard growth
- Used commercially to produce seedless grapes
- Fukuoka studied under Kurosawa at Yokohama Customs Bureau
- Initially ignored in Japan but became internationally significant
