# gamma-2C-T-4 (2,6-Dimethoxy-4-isopropylthiophenethylamine)

gamma-2C-T-4 is a phenethylamine compound and a member of the 2C-T family of psychedelic substances. It is notable as a "pseudo-psychedelic," representing the 2,4,6-trisubstitution pattern rather than the classic 2,4,5 pattern. The compound is listed as PIHKAL entry #42.

## Chemical Classification

gamma-2C-T-4 belongs to the phenethylamine class of chemical compounds.

It is formally named 2,6-dimethoxy-4-(i)-propylthiophenethylamine.

The "gamma" prefix indicates that the substitution pattern is 2,4,6 (the "pseudo" arrangement) rather than the classic 2,4,5 arrangement found in most psychedelic phenethylamines.

It is a sulfur-containing phenethylamine in the 2C-T series.

## Pharmacological Profile

gamma-2C-T-4 is an active compound, though the exact effective dosage has not been fully established.

At 8 mg, subjects reported a plus one level with a very good feeling that faded away, possibly of short duration.

At 12 mg, subjects noted awareness of activity beginning around 25 minutes with progression over the following quarter hour, but without a distinct "sparkle."

The dosage is listed as "above 12 mg" and the duration is "probably short."

## Qualitative Effects

The effects at threshold levels include pleasant feelings and general awareness of altered state.

There is no prominent body load or nausea reported at the explored doses.

The experience is described as gentle and smooth but lacking in dramatic psychedelic qualities.

The compound appears to produce a subtle intoxication rather than a full psychedelic experience at the doses explored.

## The Concept of Pseudo-Psychedelics

gamma-2C-T-4 is presented by Shulgin as an example of an important class of compounds called "pseudo-psychedelics."

In chemistry, "pseudo-" is used when a second form or isomer of a known compound lacks its own common name. The 2,4,6-trisubstituted counterparts of the 2,4,5-trisubstituted psychedelics constitute the pseudo-psychedelic family.

Almost all of the 2,5-dimethoxy-4-something-or-other compounds (both phenethylamines and amphetamines) are active and interesting. Their 2,6-dimethoxy counterparts are expected to be just as active and interesting.

## Synthetic Challenges

Chemically, the 2,6-dimethoxy compounds are much more difficult to synthesize than their 2,5-dimethoxy counterparts.

The 2,5-dimethoxy orientation makes the 4-position a natural and easy target for electrophilic substitution.

The 2,6-dimethoxy orientation pushes for 3-substitution, and the 4-position is completely unnatural, requiring chemical tricks to access.

The synthesis of gamma-2C-T-4 demonstrates one such trick using ortho-lithiation chemistry.

## Synthesis

The synthesis begins with 3,5-dimethoxy-1-chlorobenzene and isopropylsulfide, which undergo a lithium-halogen exchange reaction.

The intermediate is formylated using DMF to produce 2,6-dimethoxy-4-(i-propylthio)benzaldehyde.

This aldehyde is condensed with nitromethane to form the corresponding nitrostyrene.

The nitrostyrene is reduced using LAH to yield the final amine product.

The hydrochloride salt melts at decomposition and the product is obtained in small quantities.
