# Honey Oil

## Description

Honey oil (also called translucent oil, amber oil) is one of the most potent and visually distinctive forms of cannabis concentrate. It is a thick, translucent, amber-colored oil with the appearance and consistency of dark honey. Honey oil is produced by removing colored impurities from cannabis oil that has already been purified by re-extraction with petroleum ether, using granulated activated charcoal as a decolorizing agent. The term "honey oil" has been in use since at least the 1970s and remains one of the most recognized cannabis concentrate categories.

## Classification

- **Category**: Cannabis concentrate
- **Type**: Charcoal-filtered petroleum ether extract
- **Appearance**: Thick, translucent, amber (dark honey-like)
- **Consistency**: Viscous, pourable when warm; thick when cool
- **Potency**: Very high (depends on [[hofmann-lsd-chemical-modifications-derivatives]])

## Key Facts

- Produced by dissolving **petroleum ether-purified cannabis oil** in ten times its volume of pure alcohol, then treating with granulated activated charcoal (Norit brand referenced).
- Activated charcoal quantity: **half the weight of the oil** in granulated form.
- After charcoal treatment, the solution is filtered through fine filter paper and the alcohol is removed by evaporation.
- The result is a **translucent amber oil** with the appearance and consistency of dark honey — significantly more visually appealing and pure-looking than the crude dark oil from initial extraction.
- The oil may or may not be **isomerized or acetylated** prior to the charcoal filtration step — both processes increase potency but are independent of the decolorization.
- Originally and most famously extracted from **Afghanistan hashish**, which was noted as a premium source material for honey oil production.
- The charcoal filtration removes pigmented impurities (primarily chlorophyll derivatives and oxidized resins) without significantly affecting cannabinoid content.
- Norit (now Cabot Norit) is a well-known brand of activated carbon used in laboratory and pharmaceutical purification processes.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Processing

Honey oil represents the aesthetic and practical pinnacle of the basic [[cannabis-advanced-refinement-fractional-distillation]]). The charcoal filtration step transforms the dark, opaque crude extract into a visually appealing translucent product that is easier to handle, dose, and consume. The technique is notable for its simplicity — activated charcoal is widely available and the process requires no specialized equipment beyond standard filtration supplies. In modern cannabis processing, similar principles are applied using activated carbon columns to "scrub" extracts of pigments and undesirable compounds, though modern producers typically use closed-loop systems and more refined filtration media.

## Production Process

1. Begin with cannabis oil purified by petroleum ether re-extraction
2. Dissolve oil in 10× its volume of pure alcohol
3. Add granulated activated charcoal (Norit) equal to ½ the oil's weight
4. Mix thoroughly to allow charcoal to adsorb colored impurities
5. Filter through fine filter paper to remove charcoal
6. Evaporate alcohol to recover purified oil
7. Result: translucent amber honey oil
