# Pleurotus citrinopileatus

**Golden Oyster Mushroom**

*Author: Singer*

## Introduction

pleurotus citrinopileatus, commonly known as the Golden Oyster Mushroom or Ii'mak
in the Soviet Far East, is one of the most brilliantly colored members of the
[[pleurotus-ostreatus]] complex. It is closely allied to [[pleurotus-eryngii]] and
other oyster species within the genus *Pleurotus*.

First formally described by Singer, pleurotus citrinopileatus is often considered
a variety of *P. cornucopiae* (Paulet) Roll. Moser (1978) and Singer (1986)
distinguished *P. cornucopiae* var. *cornucopiae* as tawny brown versus the
unmistakably brilliant yellow of *P. citrinopileatus*.

Petersen's (1993) interfertility studies demonstrated that Chinese collections of
*P. citrinopileatus* are sexually compatible with European *P. cornucopiae*,
suggesting a close evolutionary relationship between the two taxa. Despite this
genetic compatibility, the striking morphological differences in cap color and
cluster morphology have led most cultivators and mycologists to treat them as
distinct entities.

## Description

Caps are golden to bright yellow, measuring 2 to 5 cm in diameter. They are convex
when young, expanding to plane at maturity. The flesh is thin relative to other
[[oyster-mushroom-species]]. Gills are decurrent, and stems are white and centrally
attached.

Fruiting bodies grow in large clusters arising from a single joined base. Clusters
of 50 to 100 or more individual mushrooms are not uncommon, creating spectacular
golden bouquets. As strains senesce through repeated cultivation cycles, the
characteristic yellow color is gradually lost and caps become beige.

## Distribution

Native to the forested subtropics of China, southern Japan, and adjacent regions
of eastern Asia. The species is neither found growing wild in North America nor
naturalized outside its native range in Asia.

## Natural Habitat

pleurotus citrinopileatus is a saprophyte of Asian hardwoods, especially oaks,
elm, beech, and poplars. It decomposes dead and dying wood in subtropical forest
ecosystems, playing an important role in nutrient cycling within these habitats.

**Microscopic features:**

Spores are pale pinkish buff, measuring 7.5 to 9.0 x 3.0 to 3.5 um. Clamp
connections are present. The hyphal system is dimitic.

**Mycelium:**

Cottony, whitish, often with tufts of dense growth. The mycelium sometimes shows
yellowish tones and occasionally forms rhizomorphic strands. Primordia appear
yellow at first formation.

**Fragrance:**

Astringent, acrid, nutty, sometimes described as "fishy."

## Cultivation

pleurotus citrinopileatus grows quickly through pasteurized straw and sterilized
sawdust substrates. It thrives at high temperatures, fruiting readily above 65 degrees
F (18 degrees C). Per cubic meter of elm wood, yields average 17 to 22 kg (Hilber
1982).

In China it is also grown on cottonseed hulls, sugarcane bagasse, straw, and sawdust.
In the United States, wheat straw or hardwood sawdust are the most common
substrates. Grain spawn is sown directly into sterilized sawdust or pasteurized
substrates.

Similar cultivation methods apply to related species like [[pleurotus-pulmonarius]]
and [[pleurotus-djamor]].

## Medicinal Properties

While specific medicinal studies on *P. citrinopileatus* are limited, oyster
mushrooms in general are recognized for their nutritional and functional food
properties. The species contributes dietary fiber, protein, and various bioactive
compounds. Like other [[pleurotus-ostreatus]] species, it contains [[beta-glucans]]
associated with immune modulation.

Oyster mushrooms have been studied for their potential cholesterol-lowering
effects, antitumor activity, and antimicrobial properties. Further
pharmacological research on the Golden Oyster specifically is warranted.

## Flavor and Preparation

The Golden Oyster is extremely fragile post-harvest, which limits its commercial
distribution and shelf life. Raw mushrooms taste spicy and bitter at first, but
develop a strong nutty flavor upon thorough cooking.

It is one of the most brilliantly colored oyster mushrooms and makes an excellent
visual addition to dishes. Best prepared by sauteing thoroughly in butter or oil,
and pairs well with light sauces, eggs, and vegetable dishes.
