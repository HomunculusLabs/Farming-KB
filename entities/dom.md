# DOM (4-Methyl-2,5-dimethoxyamphetamine)

DOM is a potent psychedelic amphetamine and the prototype of the DOx series — compounds that combine the psychedelic
2,5-dimethoxy-4-substituted phenethylamine pharmacophore with the alpha-methyl group characteristic of amphetamines.
Originally developed in the 1960s, DOM gained notoriety as a street drug under the name "STP" and remains one of the
most potent and longest-lasting orally active psychedelics known.

## Chemical Properties

DOM is 4-methyl-2,5-dimethoxyamphetamine, with the molecular formula C₁₂H₁₉NO₂ and a molecular weight of
209.28 g/mol. Structurally, it is identical to 2C-D (2,5-dimethoxy-4-methylphenethylamine) with the addition of an
alpha-methyl group on the ethylamine side chain. This alpha-methyl substitution converts the compound from a simple
phenethylamine to an amphetamine, conferring dramatically increased metabolic stability, potency, and duration by
protecting the molecule from rapid oxidative deamination by MAO enzymes. DOM is typically encountered as a white powder
or in tablet form. The effective oral dose is remarkably low at 3–10 mg, making it approximately 20–40 times more
potent than [[mescaline]] by weight. Duration extends to 14–20 hours or longer at higher doses.

## Pharmacology

DOM acts primarily as a potent agonist at 5-HT₂A serotonin receptors, with additional activity at 5-HT₂C receptors.
The alpha-methyl group dramatically increases binding affinity and duration compared to the non-methylated analogue
2C-D. DOM also has some affinity for trace amine-associated receptors (TAARs) and may have weak monoamine-releasing
properties at higher doses, though these effects are minimal compared to classical stimulant amphetamines. The compound
is metabolized primarily via hepatic N-dealkylation, O-demethylation, and hydroxylation. The alpha-methyl group
provides substantial resistance to MAO metabolism, which is the primary reason for the compound's extended duration.
The 4-methyl substituent is relatively small compared to the halogen substituents found in other DOx compounds, which
contributes to DOM's somewhat lower potency relative to DOB or DOI.

## Subjective Effects

The DOM experience is characterized by intense, long-lasting visual hallucinations with a quality often described as
"sharp" and "crystalline." Visual effects include complex geometric patterns, color enhancement and distortion,
significant depth perception changes, and persistent afterimages. Cognitive effects include pronounced stimulation,
enhanced analytical thinking, and sometimes a rigid or强迫 quality to thought patterns that some users find
uncomfortable. The stimulant component is significant and can include sustained physical energy, jaw clenching,
insomnia, and elevated heart rate and blood pressure. The extremely long duration — with aftereffects and difficulty
sleeping persisting well beyond the primary experience — is the most frequently cited challenge. Users often report
that the experience outlasts their willingness to continue, making set and setting management critical given that one
cannot simply "wait it out" in a few hours as with shorter-acting psychedelics.

## History and Street Drug Era

DOM was first synthesized by [[alexander-shulgin]] in 1963. In 1967, it appeared on the streets of San Francisco's
Haight-Ashbury district as "STP," allegedly standing for "Serenity, Tranquility, and Peace" (though the name was a
tongue-in-cheek reference to the motor oil additive). The initial tablets, distributed by the Hells Angels, contained
an estimated 10–20 mg — doses at the high end of the effective range, which produced overwhelming experiences
lasting up to 24 hours. Many users, expecting an LSD-like 8–12 hour experience, redosed during the peak, resulting in
extremely prolonged and often difficult trips. The STP episode became a cautionary tale in psychedelic drug culture and
contributed to the growing backlash against street psychedelics that culminated in their scheduling under the
[[controlled-substances-act-and-psychedelic-research]] of 1970.

## The STP Incident and Its Legacy

The STP episode is historically significant as one of the earliest examples of the dangers of novel psychoactive
substances in unregulated distribution. The problems with STP were multifaceted: the dose was too high for most users;
the duration far exceeded user expectations; there was no quality control or dosage information provided; and the drug
was distributed in a context of escalating social and political tension. These factors combined to produce a wave of
bad experiences and medical incidents that were widely reported in the media. The STP [[gaias-garden-flowering-tree-desert-restoration-case-study]] is frequently
referenced in discussions of [[magic-mushroom-prohibition-and-uk-drug-policy]], [[hallucinogenic-mushroom-health-risks-and-harm-reduction]], and the ethics of novel psychoactive substance distribution.
It illustrates the critical importance of accurate dosage information and user education — principles that remain
central [[turner-brief-history-psychedelics-ancient-to-modern]] harm reduction practice.

## DOx Series

DOM is the prototype of the DOx family, which includes DOB (4-bromo-2,5-dimethoxyamphetamine), DOI
(4-iodo-2,5-dimethoxyamphetamine), DOC (4-chloro-2,5-dimethoxyamphetamine), and others. The DOx compounds share the
common feature of combining the psychedelic 2,5-dimethoxyphenyl substitution with the amphetamine alpha-methyl group,
resulting in a series of extremely potent, long-lasting psychedelics with significant stimulant properties. Potency
varies across the series: DOB and DOI are active at sub-milligram doses, DOC is slightly less potent than DOM, and DOC
is active at 1–3 mg. All DOx compounds share the characteristic of very long duration (12–30+ hours), which
distinguishes them from virtually all other psychedelic drug classes and makes dosing precision especially important.

## Misrepresentation as LSD

A persistent concern with DOx compounds, particularly DOC and DOI, is their occasional misrepresentation as LSD in
illicit markets. Because these compounds are active at low doses and are available as blotter paper, they can be
difficult to distinguish from LSD without chemical analysis. This misrepresentation creates significant risks: users
expecting a 10–12 hour LSD experience may instead face 20–30 hours of psychedelic effects with pronounced stimulant
properties. Drug checking services and reagent testing can help identify DOx compounds — DOI and DOB, for example, do
not produce the typical color reactions with Ehrlich's reagent that LSD does. The misrepresentation issue is one of the
strongest arguments for the availability of drug checking services at festivals and events.

## Safety and Harm Reduction

DOM's safety profile is characterized by the challenges inherent to its extreme duration and stimulant properties. The
most significant risks include cardiovascular stress from prolonged sympathetic activation, dehydration and exhaustion
from extended wakefulness, psychological distress from the inability to "wait out" the experience, and dangerous drug
interactions, particularly with MAOIs. There are no confirmed deaths directly attributable to DOM at recreational
doses, though the STP-era hospitalizations demonstrate that high doses can produce overwhelming and potentially
dangerous experiences. Harm reduction recommendations for DOx compounds include starting with very low doses (1–2
mg), never redosing, having a sober companion present, and preparing for an experience that may last well beyond 24
hours.
