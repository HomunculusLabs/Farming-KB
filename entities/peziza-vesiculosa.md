## Taxonomy

Ascomycota → Pezizomycetes → Pezizales → Pezizaceae → *Peziza* →
*P. vesiculosa* Bull. (1789).

The species was originally described by Jean-Baptiste François Bulliard in
1789. The epithet "vesiculosa" refers to the blistered or vesiculate
appearance of the hymenial surface. It is the type species of the genus
*Peziza* in some historical treatments, though modern phylogenetics has
refined generic boundaries within the Pezizaceae. Several morphologically
similar taxa have been separated or synonymized over time, reflecting the
complex species-level taxonomy within *Peziza* sensu lato.

## Morphology

- **Apothecium:** Cup-shaped when young, becoming more irregularly
  disc-shaped with age; 2–6 cm diameter, occasionally up to 8 cm
- **Outer surface (excipulum):** Pale brown to tan, finely tomentose,
  often dusted with soil particles; margin incurved when young,
  becoming undulating and uplifted at maturity
- **Inner surface (hymenium):** Smooth, brown to dark brown, distinctly
  wrinkled or blistered (vesiculate) — the defining feature of the
  species; this sets it apart from smoother-cupped relatives
- **Flesh:** Thin, brittle, pale brownish; no distinctive odor; taste
  mild but unremarkable
- **Ascospores:** Elliptical, smooth, hyaline, 17–22 × 10–13 μm,
  containing two large oil droplets (guttulate); asci operculate,
  typical of the [[comparison-spore-print-collection-vs-spore-syringe-production]] white
- **Paraphyses:** Filamentous, slightly clavate at tips, septate,
  brown-pigmented at apices
- **Microscopic features:** Excipulum composed of textura angularis
  (angular-globose cells); medullary excipulum of interwoven hyphae;
  ectal excipulum with globose to subglobose cells 15–30 μm diameter

## Distribution and Habitat

*Peziza vesiculosa* has a broad distribution across [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]] of
Europe, [[fukuoka-textdoc-sawdust-wood-chips-orchard-soil-acidification-experiment]], and
its preference for nutrient-rich disturbed ground makes it
one of the first cup fungi to colonize newly turned soil.

The species is remarkably tolerant of high nitrogen environments,
including areas with manure or composted kitchen waste. It has been
recorded fruiting indoors in potting soil and greenhouse beds. In
warmer climates, fruiting can occur nearly year-round wherever
moisture and organic substrate are available.
