# Gentiana waltonii

## Overview

Gentiana waltonii is a perennial herb in the Gentianaceae family, endemic to the Tibetan Plateau and adjacent high-altitude regions of western China.

The species produces deep blue trumpet-shaped flowers that emerge from rosettes of leathery leaves in late summer.

Named after the British plant collector H.J. Walton, who collected in Tibet during the early 20th century.

Gentiana waltonii is prized by alpine gardeners for its intense blue flowers and compact cushion-forming habit.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Gentiana waltonii was described by Isaac Henry Burkill in 1899 from specimens collected in southern Tibet.

It belongs to Gentiana section Frigida, a group of high-altitude gentians with solitary or few-flowered stems.

The plant forms a compact cushion or rosette 5–12 cm tall with short, procumbent flowering stems.

Basal leaves are crowded, oblong to spathulate, 1–3 cm long, with prominent white cartilaginous margins.

Flowers are solitary, terminal, deep blue to ultramarine, funnel-shaped, 3–5 cm long.

The corolla lobes are ovate and spreading, with a white-spotted throat in some populations.

Flowering occurs from July to September, depending on elevation and seasonal snowmelt timing.

[[herbarium-specimens-fungal-collections]] show variability in corolla size across the elevational range.

Plants from higher elevations tend to have proportionally shorter stems and larger flowers relative to the rosette.

## Distribution and Habitat

The species is native to southern Tibet, Qinghai, Sichuan, and Yunnan provinces of China.

It occurs on the Tibetan Plateau at elevations of 3,500–5,200 meters, among the highest-elevation gentians.

Gentiana waltonii grows in alpine meadows, moraines, scree slopes, and stabilized gravel patches.

Populations prefer calcareous substrates, often growing in association with Kobresia cushion communities.

The species experiences intense UV radiation, strong winds, and diurnal temperature fluctuations exceeding 30 °C.

Winter snow cover provides critical insulation, protecting the rosette from freeze-desiccation at extreme altitudes.

## Ecology

Flowers are pollinated by bumblebees adapted to high-altitude conditions, notably Bombus rufofasciatus.

The deep blue corolla color may enhance heat absorption, warming the flower interior for pollinator attraction and pollen development.

Rosettes trap windblown debris and fine particles, creating a nutrient-enriched microsite.

The species is a component of the alpine cushion-plant community, providing shelter for invertebrates.

[[cannabis-controlled-pollination-seed-production]] is relatively low compared to lower-elevation gentians, with many flowers failing to set seed.

Vegetative reproduction through side rosettes occurs occasionally, allowing clonal patch expansion.

The species demonstrates classic alpine cushion-plant adaptations including [[fukuoka-ideal-rice-plant-morphology-compact-growth-high-yield]] and hairy leaf margins that reduce transpiration.

## Cultivation

Gentiana waltonii requires extremely well-drained, gritty, alkaline soil in full sun with protection from winter wet.

It is rated hardy to USDA zones 4–7, though winter damp poses a greater threat than cold temperatures.

Propagation from seed requires cold stratification and surface sowing on limestone chippings.

Germination rates are typically low (10–30%), reflecting the challenging alpine adaptation of the species.
