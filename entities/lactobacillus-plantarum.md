# Lactobacillus plantarum

*Lactobacillus plantarum* is a widespread Gram-positive lactic acid bacterium and one of the most versatile species in the genus *Lactobacillus*. It is found in a diverse range of environmental niches including fermented vegetables, dairy products, meat, fish, and the gastrointestinal tracts of humans and animals. Its metabolic flexibility allows it to thrive under a broad range of conditions, making it a cornerstone organism in both traditional fermentation and modern probiotic applications.









## Taxonomy and Classification

*Lactobacillus plantarum* belongs to the phylum Bacillota, class Bacilli, order Lactobacillales, family Lactobacillaceae. The species was originally described in 1919 and has since been reclassified several times as phylogenetic methods have evolved. In 2020, the genus *Lactobacillus* underwent extensive taxonomic revision, with many species reclassified into new genera; however, *L. plantarum* retained its original name. The species encompasses a remarkably diverse genomic repertoire, with strain-level variation that reflects its adaptation to many different ecological niches.









## Role in Fermentation

*L. plantarum* is one of the principal organisms responsible for lactic acid fermentation in vegetable-based foods. It plays a central role in the production of kimchi, sauerkraut, pickles, and many traditional fermented foods across Asia, Africa, and Europe. During fermentation, *L. plantarum* converts available sugars—primarily glucose, fructose, and sucrose—into lactic acid, acetic acid, ethanol, carbon dioxide, and various aromatic compounds that contribute characteristic flavors and textures to fermented products.

The organism is facultatively heterofermentative, meaning it can produce both lactic acid and additional metabolites (acetic acid, ethanol, CO₂) depending on the available carbon source. Under aerobic conditions, it can also utilize oxygen via flavin oxidases, contributing to the preservation of anaerobic conditions favorable for the overall fermentation community. Its high acid tolerance (surviving at pH values below 3.5) and ability to grow at temperatures ranging from 15°C to 45°C make it particularly robust in natural fermentation environments.









## Use in Indigenous Microorganism (IMO) Preparations

In Korean natural farming and other indigenous microorganism (IMO) systems, *L. plantarum* is deliberately cultured and applied as a key component of biological inputs. It is commonly propagated using rice wash water or steamed rice as a substrate, collected from the surface of fermented rice or from forest soils. The cultured organisms are then combined with brown sugar and further fermented to produce a concentrated inoculant known as "LAB serum" (lactic acid bacteria serum).

In agricultural applications, *L. plantarum* preparations are applied to soil, compost, and plant foliage to suppress pathogenic microorganisms, improve nutrient availability, and enhance crop health. The lactic acid produced by the bacteria helps solubilize minerals in the soil, making phosphorus, calcium, and trace elements more accessible to plant roots. Additionally, *L. plantarum* produces bacteriocins—antimicrobial peptides—that inhibit the growth of competing bacteria, including plant pathogens such as *Erwinia* and *Pseudomonas* species.









## Probiotic Properties

*L. plantarum* is one of the most extensively studied probiotic species. Clinical research has demonstrated its ability to survive transit through the gastrointestinal tract, adhere to intestinal epithelial cells, and modulate immune function. Strains of *L. plantarum* have been investigated for their potential benefits in managing irritable bowel syndrome (IBS), reducing symptoms of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), lowering serum cholesterol, and preventing antibiotic-associated diarrhea.

The probiotic activity of *L. plantarum* is attributed to multiple mechanisms: production of lactic acid and bacteriocins that inhibit pathogens, competitive exclusion of harmful bacteria at adhesion sites, enhancement of intestinal barrier function, and modulation of host immune responses through interaction with pattern recognition receptors. Genome sequencing has revealed that *L. plantarum* possesses an unusually large number of genes involved in carbohydrate utilization, adhesion, and stress response compared to other lactobacilli, which may partially explain its ecological success.









## Metabolic Characteristics

*L. plantarum* exhibits a remarkably flexible metabolism. It can ferment over 50 different carbohydrates, including pentoses and hexoses, due to its large complement of phosphotransferase systems (PTS) and sugar transporters. The organism produces both D- and L-lactate isomers, a trait that distinguishes it from many other lactobacilli that produce only one isomer. This metabolic versatility allows *L. plantarum* to dominate complex fermentation environments where multiple sugar sources are available simultaneously.

In addition to lactic acid, *L. plantarum* produces a range of bioactive metabolites including short-chain fatty acids (acetate, propionate), exopolysaccharides (EPS), gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA), and various vitamins. The EPS produced by certain strains contributes to the texture of fermented foods and has prebiotic properties, supporting the growth of beneficial gut bacteria.
