# Lactobacillus plantarum

Lactobacillus plantarum (recently reclassified as Lactiplantibacillus
plantarum) is a widespread Gram-positive, facultative heterofermentative
lactic acid bacterium. It is one of the most versatile species in the genus
Lactobacillus, found in a diverse range of ecological niches including the
human gastrointestinal tract, fermented foods, soil, and plant material. It
is one of the most extensively studied lactic acid bacteria due to its
significant roles in food fermentation and its probiotic properties.

## Taxonomy and Classification

L. plantarum was originally described in 1919 and was long classified within
the genus Lactobacillus. In 2020, a comprehensive taxonomic revision of the
genus Lactobacillus based on whole-genome phylogenetic analysis led to the
reclassification of L. plantarum into the new genus Lactiplantibacillus.
The species retains its well-established identity in both scientific
literature and food microbiology despite this nomenclatural change. It belongs
to the Lactobacillaceae family within the order Lactobacillales.

## Metabolic Pathways

L. plantarum is a facultative heterofermentative bacterium, meaning it can
metabolize sugars through both homofermentative and heterofermentative
pathways depending on substrate availability. Under homofermentative
conditions, it converts hexose sugars primarily to lactic acid via the
Embden-Meyerhof-Parnas (EMP) pathway, producing two moles of lactic acid
per mole of glucose. Under heterofermentative conditions, it utilizes the
phosphoketolase pathway to convert pentose sugars into lactic acid, acetic
acid, ethanol, and carbon dioxide.

This metabolic flexibility allows L. plantarum to thrive in diverse
environments and substrates. It can metabolize a wide range of carbohydrates
including glucose, fructose, sucrose, lactose, maltose, and cellobiose. The
species possesses an unusually large genome for a lactic acid bacterium
(approximately 3.3 Mbp), encoding a remarkable array of sugar transporters,
regulatory proteins, and metabolic enzymes.

## Fermentation Applications

L. plantarum is one of the most important species in industrial and
traditional food fermentation. It plays a central role in the production of:

- **Vegetable fermentations** — L. plantarum is the dominant organism in the
  fermentation of sauerkraut, kimchi, pickled vegetables, and traditional
  [[query-how-to-make-sourdough-bread-at-home]], traditional African fermented cereals, and grain-based
  beverages.
- **Silage fermentation** — L. plantarum is widely used as an inoculant in
  agricultural silage production, improving preservation and nutritional
  quality of animal feed.

## Probiotic Properties

L. plantarum has been extensively studied for its probiotic characteristics:

- **Gastrointestinal survival** — Many strains exhibit high tolerance to
  gastric acid and bile salts, enabling survival transit through the stomach.
- **Competitive exclusion** — L. plantarum strains can inhibit pathogenic
  bacteria through production of bacteriocins (particularly plantaricins),
  organic acids, and [[cancer-immune-modulation-mechanisms]]** — Research has demonstrated that certain strains can
  modulate immune responses, enhancing intestinal barrier function.
- **Anti-inflammatory effects** — Some strains have shown anti-inflammatory
  properties in clinical studies, potentially beneficial for conditions such
  as [[blesching-cannabis-inflammatory-bowel-disease]] disease.
