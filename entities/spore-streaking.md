# Spore Streaking

## Description

Spore streaking is a fundamental laboratory technique in mycology for initiating mushroom cultures from spores. The method involves transferring spores from a spore print onto an agar plate by dragging a sterilized tool through the agar in a defined pattern (typically a Z pattern). This technique distributes spores in a tight line, facilitating mating between compatible spore types and allowing cultivators to observe sectoring—the emergence of different strains (ecotypes) from the same spore germination.

## Classification

- **Category:** Laboratory cultivation technique
- **Field:** Mycology / Tissue culture
- **Purpose:** Starting cultures from spores (sexual reproduction)
- **Contrast with:** Tissue culturing (cloning / asexual reproduction)

## Key Facts

- Spore streaking involves dragging a metal tool (inoculation loop, scalpel, or wire) loaded with spores across the surface of an agar plate in a Z pattern.
- The Z pattern keeps spores in a tight line, which helps compatible spores find suitable mates nearby for successful germination.
- Streaking allows observation of sectoring—different strains of the same mushroom separating from the pack, with the healthiest and most efficient strains surging outward faster than inferior strains.
- The technique requires an alcohol lamp for flame sterilization of tools, nitrile or latex gloves, freshly poured agar plates, and tape or Parafilm for sealing.
- Spore prints should be stored on aluminum foil or glass slides inside sealed bags and opened only during the actual transfer.
- The workspace (preferably in front of a laminar flow hood) should be prepared an hour in advance with the hood running and surfaces sanitized with 70% isopropyl alcohol.
- After streaking, plates are sealed with tape or Parafilm and incubated at approximately 77°F (25°C) in a dark cabinet or insulated box.
- Most species show visible mycelial growth within 7 to 10 days, though some (like morels and paddy straw) germinate faster.
- Spores offer genetically variable offspring—mushrooms cultured from spores can be identical to or remarkably different from the parent in shape, taste, and fruiting parameters.
- Spore germinations are ideal for breeding or creating strains specific to a region or growing substrate, enabling selective culture of specimens expressing desired traits.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Spore streaking is one of the two primary methods for starting mushroom cultures (the other being tissue cloning). Unlike cloning, which produces genetically identical copies, spore germination introduces genetic diversity, making it essential for breeding programs and strain development. Cultivators use spore streaking to develop new strains adapted to local substrates, climates, or cultivation parameters. The technique is also the starting point for genetic improvement programs, where cultivators selectively transfer the fastest-growing, most robust sectors from spore germination plates. Mastery of spore streaking is a foundational skill for any serious mushroom cultivator or mycological researcher.

## Step-by-Step Protocol

1. **Prepare workspace:** Turn on laminar flow hood one hour before work. Sanitize all surfaces and tools with 70% isopropyl alcohol.
2. **Organize materials:** Arrange tools from sterile (near filter) to non-sterile (near user). Place agar plates against the filter face.
3. **Sterilize tool:** Hold inoculation loop or scalpel in the alcohol lamp flame until tip glows red.
4. **Cool tool:** Lift petri plate lid slightly and dip the hot tool into the agar until it stops popping. Close lid.
5. **Load spores:** Touch the wet tool tip to the spore print. Spores adhere to the moist surface.
6. **Streak spores:** Lift petri plate lid at an angle and drag the tool through the agar in a wide Z pattern. Close lid within 3 seconds.
7. **Seal plate:** Wrap the seam with tape or Parafilm to prevent contamination.
8. **Label plate:** Record date, agar formula, spore type, and other tracking information.
9. **Incubate:** Store at 77°F (25°C) in darkness. Monitor for mycelial growth within 7-10 days.
