## Description

'Bianca Riccia' is an Italian endive cultivar (*Cichorium endivia*) grown for baby-leaf salad production. Its full Italian name is 'Indivia Bianca Riccia da Taglio', meaning "white curly endive for cutting." Sold by Johnny's Selected Seeds, it produces light-colored, frilly leaves ideal for [[mesclun]] mixes during the winter harvest season.

## Classification

- **Type**: Cultivar (Endive)
- **Species**: *Cichorium endivia*
- **Category**: Salad green, baby-leaf crop
- **Growth habit**: Cutting type (regrows after harvest)

## Key Facts

- Produces attractive light-colored frilly leaves at the baby-leaf stage
- Grown specifically for winter salad mix production in protected structures
- Not sufficiently cold-hardy for unheated cold houses during the coldest months (January–February in Zone 5)
- Requires minimal heat protection (cool house, ~35°F thermostat) during peak winter to maintain quality
- Harvested at 3 inches or less for salad mix inclusion
- Regrows after cutting, allowing multiple harvests from the same planting

## Cultivation Notes

- One of three salad mix components (along with lettuce and arugula) that does not consistently meet quality standards under inner row covers during the deepest cold
- Quality is maintained in a minimally heated "cool house" set just above freezing
- [[eliot-coleman]]'s farm traditionally gives this cultivar cool-house protection during January and February
- Used as a replacement for radicchio in baby-leaf mixes, providing visual appeal without radicchio's bitter edge

## Relevance to Cultivation

'Bianca Riccia' represents the strategy of selecting specific cultivars optimized for winter baby-leaf production rather than using mature-head endive varieties. Its frilly leaf texture and light color add visual diversity to mesclun mixes. The cultivar illustrates a key principle in winter harvesting: matching crop hardiness to the available protection level, and using minimal heat strategically to extend the range of cultivars beyond what unheated structures alone can support.
