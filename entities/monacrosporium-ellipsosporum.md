## Description

**Monacrosporium ellipsosporum** is a nematode-trapping fungus that captures soil nematodes using specialized adhesive network structures. It occurs in the rhizosphere of plants where it is positioned to prey on plant-parasitic nematodes, and has been investigated as a potential biocontrol agent.









## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Phylum**: Ascomycota
- **Class**: Orbiliomycetes
- **Order**: Orbiliales
- **Family**: Orbiliaceae
- **Genus**: Monacrosporium
- **Species**: M. ellipsosporum









## Key Facts

- Identified by Mankau (1981) as a nematode-trapping fungus occurring in the plant rhizosphere
- Well-positioned to predate plant-parasitic nematodes due to rhizosphere habitat
- Produces adhesive trapping networks to capture nematodes
- Related to other trapping fungi including [[arthrobotrys-dactyloides]] and [[dactylaria-brochopaga]]
- Limited success in practical biocontrol applications despite demonstrated predation ability









## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

M. ellipsosporum and related [[nematophagous-fungi]] have potential applications in managing plant-parasitic nematodes in agricultural and mycological contexts. In [[mushroom-cultivation]], nematodes can contaminate substrates. Nematophagous fungi offer a biological approach to nematode management. The rhizosphere occurrence suggests compatibility with plant-fungal systems.









## Ecological Role

As a predatory fungus, M. ellipsosporum helps regulate nematode populations in soil, particularly in the nutrient-rich rhizosphere zone. Its trapping structures represent an evolutionary adaptation to a predatory lifestyle, allowing fungi to exploit nematodes as a supplementary nitrogen source.
