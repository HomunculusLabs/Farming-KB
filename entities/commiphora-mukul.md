## Overview

Commiphora mukul, known as Guggul or Indian Bdellium, is a flowering shrub or
small tree in the family Burseraceae. It is the source of guggul resin, one of
the oldest and most important medicaments in Ayurvedic medicine.

The species is native to [[fukuoka-natural-farming-in-arid-regions]] of India, Pakistan, and Bangladesh. It
produces a yellowish-brown oleoresin (guggul) from its bark, which has been
used for over 3,000 years in traditional Indian medicine for treating
[[cannabis-for-inflammatory-conditions]], obesity, and lipid disorders.

Modern pharmacological research has validated the resin's hypolipidemic and
anti-inflammatory properties, leading to the development of standardized
extracts such as Guggulipid. The compound guggulsterone has been identified as
a key bioactive constituent with farnesoid X receptor (FXR) antagonism.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:** Kingdom Plantae → Division Magnoliophyta → Class
Magnoliopsida → Order Sapindales → Family Burseraceae → Genus
*Commiphora* → Species *C. mukul*.

**Authority:** (Hook. ex Stocks) Engl. (1883). Basionym: *Balsamodendron
mukul* Hook. ex Stocks (1849). The taxonomy of *Commiphora* species has been
historically complex, with several names applied to what is now considered
a single polymorphic species.

The plant is a thorny shrub or small tree reaching 1.5–4 meters in height.
The branches are irregular, knotted, and divaricate. Bark is thin, papery,
peeling in flakes, and pale gray to brown.

Leaves are trifoliate (rarely 1-foliate), alternate, with leaflets 1–5 cm
long, ovate to elliptic, and sessile. The leaf margin is entire or slightly
serrate. Young leaves are pubescent, becoming glabrous with age.

Flowers are small, brownish-red, borne in axillary fascicles. The fruit is
a small drupe, 6–8 mm in diameter, red when ripe, containing a single
seed. The resin ducts in the bark produce guggul when the plant is wounded.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Commiphora mukul* is native to the arid and semi-arid regions of the
Indian subcontinent, particularly Rajasthan, Gujarat, Maharashtra, and
Karnataka. It also occurs in Pakistan and Bangladesh.

The species thrives in rocky, sandy, and gravelly soils in arid zones with
annual rainfall of 100–500 mm. It is commonly found on rocky hillsides,
dry deciduous forests, and degraded scrubland at elevations up to 800 meters.

Propagation is through stem cuttings or seeds. [[cannabis-seed-germination-methods]] is slow and
irregular; stem cuttings root more reliably. The plant is drought-tolerant
and well-adapted to extreme temperatures.

Overharvesting of wild populations has raised conservation concerns. The
species is classified as vulnerable in parts of its range due to unsustainable
resin collection practices.

## Bioactive Compounds

The guggul resin contains a complex mixture of diterpenoids, triterpenoids,
sterols, and sesquiterpenes. The major bioactive constituents are the
guggulsterones (E- and Z-isomers of 4,17(20)-pregnadiene-3,16-dione).

Guggulsterone E (cembrenone) and Z-isomer are plant sterols with a unique
pregnane skeleton. Total guggulsterone content in standardized extracts
ranges from 2.5–5.0%. These are the primary hypolipidemic compounds.
