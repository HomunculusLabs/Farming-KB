# Roger Adams

## Description

Roger Adams (1889–1971) was an American organic chemist whose pioneering research on cannabis constituents in the 1930s and 1940s laid the groundwork for understanding cannabinoid chemistry. His work on the [[fractional-distillation]] and structural elucidation of cannabis compounds, published in the *Journal of the American Chemical Society*, represents some of the earliest rigorous [[challenges-neurotropic-fungi-chemical-analysis-taxonomic-identification]] of cannabis oils. Adams developed the potency scale that bears his name, used to rate the relative strength of cannabis extracts and [[blesching-cannabis-synthetic-cannabinoids]].

## Key Facts

- Conducted groundbreaking research on cannabis chemistry in **1940**, published on page 198 of volume 62 of the *Journal of the American Chemical Society* (JACS).
- Performed **[[cannabis-advanced-refinement-fractional-distillation]] of cannabis red oil** under reduced pressure (vacuum), successfully separating cannabinoid fractions from wild Minnesota hemp.
- Used wild hemp grown in Minnesota during the 1938 season, harvested after flowering began but before seed set in female tops, dried for six weeks.
- Employed **countercurrent extraction** using four 20-gallon crocks, each with a capacity of 23 pounds of material and 61 liters of solvent (95% ethyl alcohol).
- His extraction protocol obtained approximately **2 grams of solids per 100 cc** in the most concentrated extract.
- Developed the **Adams potency scale** for rating THC and its derivatives — on this scale, THC rates 7.3 while [[thc-acetate]] rates 14.6 (twice as potent).
- His work demonstrated that cannabis oil could be refined through vacuum distillation to yield products up to twice as strong as the crude ether-phase extract.
- Showed that totally pure THC (a thin transparent oil) could theoretically be produced by chemically isolating pure cannabidiol and then isomerizing it to THC.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Processing

Roger Adams' research is the scientific foundation upon which practical cannabis [[high-volume-cannabis-extraction-and-refinement]] techniques are built. His systematic approach — countercurrent extraction, vacuum concentration at controlled temperatures (never above 50°C), and fractional distillation — established the methodological framework that *Cannabis Alchemy* adapts for kitchen and laboratory use. The Adams potency scale remains a referenced benchmark for comparing the relative strength of different cannabis preparations and derivatives. His proof that cannabidiol could be isomerized to THC directly enabled the isomerization techniques described in the book.

## Research Methodology Highlights

- **Plant material**: Wild Minnesota hemp, 1938 season
- **Drying**: 6 weeks with fan circulation, no molding
- **Processing**: Stems removed; leafy material separated by shaking
- **Extraction**: 95% ethyl alcohol, countercurrent (4-crock system)
- **Concentration**: Vacuum evaporation at ≤50°C (30°C preferred)
- **Yield**: 23.1 gm solids per 100 cc concentrated solution; 1 cc = 4.13 gm hemp equivalent
- **Distillation**: Gradual temperature increase from 90°C to 200°C under reduced pressure

## Historical Significance

Adams' work represents one of the earliest systematic chemical investigations of cannabis, conducted at a time when cannabis research was both legally and socially constrained. His publications in JACS provided a rare legitimate scientific framework for understanding cannabinoid chemistry that would not be significantly expanded upon until the isolation and synthesis of Δ⁹-THC by Raphael Mechoulam in 1964.
