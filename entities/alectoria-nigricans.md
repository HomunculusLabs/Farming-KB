## Alectoria nigricans (Black Witch's Hair Lichen)

## Overview

*Alectoria nigricans* is a distinctive fruticose lichen in the family Parmeliaceae, forming dark brown to blackish, hair-like tufts on rocks and tree bark in Arctic and high alpine environments. It is one of the most cold-tolerant lichen species in the Northern Hemisphere.

The species is characterized by its conspicuously dark thallus, which distinguishes it from the more common and lighter-colored *[[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-north-america]], it occurs across the Canadian Arctic Archipelago, northern Alaska, Greenland, and southward in alpine zones of the Rocky Mountains and Coast Ranges.

In Eurasia, the species is found across Svalbard, Novaya Zemlya, northern Scandinavia, and the Kola Peninsula. Alpine populations extend through the Urals, Altai, and other Asian mountain ranges.

The species colonizes both lithic (rock) and corticolous (bark) substrates. On rock, it grows on granitic and basaltic outcrops, boulders, and stone surfaces in tundra landscapes. On bark, it prefers *Salix arctica*, *Betula nana*, and dead standing wood.

It is found from sea level in high Arctic regions to approximately 2,800 m in more southerly mountain ranges. The species tolerates extreme cold, prolonged darkness, and desiccation.

*A. nigricans* is characteristic of exposed, wind-swept tundra habitats and fell-field communities. It is less frequent in sheltered valleys or areas with persistent snow cover that limits light availability.

## Ecology and Symbiosis

*Alectoria nigricans* associates with a trebouxioid green algal photobiont, typical of Parmeliaceae. The symbiosis is adapted to extreme photophysiological conditions including prolonged [[arbuscule-isolation-metabolic-activity-assays]] during brief [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]] accumulation. Dead thallus material breaks down into humus that supports tundra vegetation.

The species hosts [[lichenicolous-fungi]] including *Arthonia alectoriicola* and *Endococcus alectoriae*. These parasites are widespread but seldom cause significant thallus mortality.

## Chemistry and Secondary Metabolites

The primary secondary metabolite of *Alectoria nigricans* is alectoronic acid, a depsidone that is characteristic of the genus *Alectoria*. Some populations additionally contain α-collatolic acid.

Spot test reactions: K+ yellow, C−, KC+ yellow, Pd+ orange. The chemistry separates *A. nigricans* from *Bryoria* species that contain physodic or norstictic acid.

Usnic acid is absent from *A. nigricans*, which contrasts with *A. sarmentosa* where usnic acid is a major cortical compound. The absence of usnic acid contributes to the darker, less yellow coloration.
