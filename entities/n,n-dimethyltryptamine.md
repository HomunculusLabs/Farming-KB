# N,N-Dimethyltryptamine (DMT)

## Overview

N,N-Dimethyltryptamine (DMT or N,N-DMT) is a potent, short-acting psychedelic tryptamine compound found naturally in numerous plant species and animals, including humans. It is one of the most powerful psychoactive substances known, capable of inducing profound alterations in consciousness, perception, and the sense of self at even sub-milligram doses. DMT has been used for centuries in the form of ayahuasca — a brew prepared from *Banisteriopsis caapi* and *Psychotria viridis* — by indigenous peoples of the Amazon basin. In 1956, Hungarian chemist and psychiatrist Stephen Szára became the first to describe the subjective effects of synthesized DMT administered intramuscularly.

## Chemical Properties

### IUPAC and Structural Information

- **IUPAC name:** N,N-Dimethyl-1H-indole-3-ethanamine
- **Molecular formula:** C₁₂H₁₆N₂
- **Molecular weight:** 188.27 g/mol
- **SMILES:** CN(C)CCC1=CNC2=C1C=CC=C2
- **CAS Number:** 61-50-7
- **Appearance:** White or off-white crystalline powder (freebase); colorless to yellow crystals or crystalline solid (fumarate salt)

### Physicochemical Properties

|----------|-------|

| Boiling point | ~160 °C (decomposes) |
| Density | ~1.099 g/cm³ |
| Solubility (water) | Slightly soluble |

### Biosynthetic Pathway
DMT is biosynthesized from the essential amino acid L-tryptophan through two enzymatic steps:

1. **Tryptophan decarboxylase** converts L-tryptophan → tryptamine
2. **Indolethylamine N-methyltransferase (INMT)** methylates tryptamine → N-methyltryptamine (NMT) → N,N-dimethyltryptamine (DMT)

This pathway is active in mammalian brain tissue, particularly in the pineal gland, cerebral cortex, and choroid plexus.

## Pharmacology

### Receptor Binding Profile

| Receptor | Affinity (Ki) | Functional Activity |
|----------|--------------|-------------------|
| 5-HT2A | 0.50 μM | Full/partial agonist — primary mediator of psychedelic effects |
| 5-HT1A | 1.10 μM | Agonist — contributes to anxiolytic and anti-stress effects |
| 5-HT2C | 2.20 μM | Agonist |
| Sigma-1 | 3.30 μM | Agonist — neuroprotective, modulates calcium signaling |
| 5-HT7 | 1.20 μM | Agonist |
| TAAR1 | Unknown | Possible agonist — modulates monoamine release |
| SERT | ~2.0 μM | Substrate/inhibitor — blocks serotonin reuptake |

### Pharmacokinetics

| Parameter | Intravenous | Intramuscular | Oral (with MAOI) | Vaporized |
|-----------|------------|--------------|-------------------|-----------|
| Onset | 10–30 seconds | 2–5 minutes | 20–60 minutes | 10–30 seconds |
| Peak | 2–5 minutes | 5–15 minutes | 60–120 minutes | 1–3 minutes |
| Duration | 15–30 minutes | 30–60 minutes | 3–6 hours | 15–45 minutes |
| Bioavailability | ~100% | ~80% | ~5% (without MAOI) | ~40–50% |

**Critical pharmacokinetic note:** When administered orally, DMT is rapidly metabolized by **monoamine oxidase A (MAO-A)** in the gastrointestinal tract and liver, resulting in only ~5% bioavailability. Ayahuasca overcomes this by combining DMT-containing plants (*Psychotria viridis*) with MAO-inhibiting plants (*Banisteriopsis caapi*, rich in β-carboline alkaloids: harmine, harmaline, and tetrahydroharmine). This synergistic combination has been used for millennia.

### Metabolism
DMT is primarily metabolized by MAO-A to the inactive metabolite **indole-3-acetic acid (IAA)** via the intermediate 3-indoleacetaldehyde. A secondary pathway involves N-oxidation to **DMT N-oxide**, which may have weak pharmacological activity and can be reduced back to DMT in vivo.

### Endogenous Occurrence
DMT has been detected in human tissues and body fluids:

- **Brain tissue:** Detected in pineal gland, cerebral cortex, and other regions
- **Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF):** Present at concentrations of ~0.05–0.10 ng/mL
- **Blood plasma:** Detected at similar concentrations; levels may increase during stress, near-death experiences, or REM sleep
- **Urine:** Excreted as a normal metabolic product

The physiological role of endogenous DMT remains a subject of active research and scientific debate. Proposed (speculative) roles include acting as a neurotransmitter or neuromodulator, a neuroprotective agent, and a regulator of the sigma-1 receptor.

## Subjective Effects

The DMT experience is commonly described in terms of distinct phases:

### 1. Onset / Come-up (0–2 minutes, vaporized)
- Rapid visual distortion — geometric patterns, color intensification
- Body sensations: vibration, pressure, warmth
- Auditory changes: humming, buzzing, heightened acuity

### 2. Peak / "Breakthrough" (2–15 minutes, vaporized)
- Complete dissolution of ego and body boundaries
- Encounter with ostensibly autonomous entities or beings ("machine elves," "teachers," geometric intelligences)
- Vision of ineffably complex landscapes, hyperbolic geometry, and self-transforming recursive structures
- Profound emotional states: awe, terror, bliss, cosmic insignificance
- Experience of dying, rebirth, or ontological transformation

### 3. Resolution / Come-down (15–45 minutes, vaporized)
- Gradual return of normal perception and self-awareness
- Lingering afterglow: sense of peace, renewed perspective, emotional openness
- Integration period: hours to days of processing

### Subjective Intensity Scale
DMT is considered by many researchers and experienced psychonauts to be the most intense psychedelic substance per unit dose, exceeding even that of LSD (lysergic acid diethylamide) and mescaline. A breakthrough dose of vaporized DMT (40–60 mg) typically produces effects comparable in intensity to a high-dose LSD (500+ μg) or psilocybin (5+ g dried) experience, compressed into a fraction of the duration.

## Clinical Research

### Modern Clinical Trials

1. **University of New Mexico (1990s):** Rick Strassman conducted the first modern clinical trials of intravenous DMT, administering 0.1–0.4 mg/kg to healthy volunteers. Results were published in *The Spirit Molecule* (2001).

2. **Imperial College London (2020s):** Chris Timmermann and Robin Carhart-Harris conducted studies using intravenous DMT fumarate to model near-death experiences (NDEs) and investigate the neural correlates of the DMT state using fMRI and EEG.

3. **Brazilian Research Groups:** Ongoing research on ayahuasca's therapeutic potential for treatment-resistant depression, anxiety, and substance use disorders at institutions including UNIPOP (Federal University of Rio Grande do Norte) and the University of São Paulo.

### Therapeutic Potential
Preliminary research suggests potential applications for:

- **Treatment-resistant depression:** Rapid and sustained antidepressant effects observed in open-label ayahuasca studies
- **Substance dependence:** Reduced craving and increased motivation for abstinence following ayahuasca ceremonies
- **PTSD and trauma processing:** DMT's ability to facilitate ego dissolution and emotional confrontation
- **End-of-life distress:** Reduction in death anxiety and existential distress
- **Cluster headaches:** Anecdotal reports and limited clinical evidence of efficacy

## Safety and Toxicity

- **LD50 (IV, mice):** ~47 mg/kg — approximately 100x a typical breakthrough dose, providing a wide safety margin
- **Cardiovascular:** Transient increases in heart rate and blood pressure; contraindicated for those with cardiovascular disease
- **Serotonin syndrome risk:** Theoretical risk when combined with SSRIs or other serotonergic medications
- **Psychological risks:** Acute anxiety, panic, dissociation; rare cases of prolonged psychosis in predisposed individuals
- **No evidence of physical dependence or addiction:** DMT does not produce compulsive drug-seeking behavior
