## Anadenanthera peregrina (L.) Speg.

## Common Names

Yopo, Cohoba, Parica, Jopa, Niopo, Huilca, Sebil, Juju

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Order:** Fabales
- **Family:** Fabaceae (Leguminosae)
- **Subfamily:** Mimosoideae
- **Genus:** Anadenanthera
- **Species:** A. peregrina (L.) Speg.

## Synonyms

- [[mimosa]] peregrina L.
- [[acacia]] peregrina (L.) Willd.
- Piptadenia peregrina (L.) Benth.

## Description

Anadenanthera peregrina is a mimosa-like tree, occurring mainly in
open grasslands and savanna habitats. It attains an impressive height
of up to 65 ft (20 m) and develops a substantial trunk reaching
approximately 2 ft (60 cm) in diameter. The bark is characteristically
blackish and coarsely armed with conical mucronate projections, giving
the tree a distinctive appearance in the landscape.

The leaves are bipinnately compound, bearing 15 to 30 pairs of pinnae,
each pinna carrying many very small, hairy leaflets. This finely
divided foliage creates a feathery canopy typical of many mimosoid
legumes. The inflorescences consist of minute white flowers arranged
in dense spherical heads, which are pollinated by insects.

The fruit is a flattened, woody legume pod typical of the family
Fabaceae, containing the seeds that are the primary psychoactive
part of the plant. The seeds are broadly oval, flat, and dark brown
to nearly black when mature.

## Distribution

Anadenanthera peregrina is native to the Orinoco basin of Venezuela
and extends across northern [[mollison-designers-gallery-forests-and-valley-bottomland-systems]], and disturbed areas.

## Chemistry

The active principles of Anadenanthera peregrina belong to both
open-chained and ringed tryptamine derivatives, all members of the
class of [[indole]] alkaloids. The principal open-chained tryptamines are:

- **N,N-dimethyltryptamine (DMT):** A potent hallucinogenic compound
  found in numerous plant species and produced endogenously in mammals.

- **[[bufotenine]] (5-hydroxy-DMT):** A tryptamine derivative also found
  in the skin secretions of toads of the genus Bufo, from which its
  name is derived. Bufotenine contributes significantly to the
  psychoactive effects of [[ayahuasca-preparation-and-ceremony]]
- [[anadenanthera-colubrina]]
- Virola
- DMT
- Bufotenine
- Ayahuasca
