## Eleutherococcus senticosus

Eleutherococcus senticosus, commonly known as [[query-what-are-the-main-bioactive-compounds-in-medicinal-plants]] called eleutherosides.
It is one of the most extensively studied [[psilocybin-clinical-research-history]] spanning over 60 years since Soviet-era investigations
into its performance-enhancing properties.

The plant gained prominence in the 1960s when Soviet scientist Israel
Brekhman classified it as an adaptogen — a substance that increases
non-specific resistance to stress. It was subsequently used by Soviet
athletes, cosmonauts, and military personnel. Today it is widely
available as a dietary supplement and remains a staple of traditional
Chinese medicine (where it is known as ci wu jia) and Russian herbalism.

## Distribution and Habitat

- **Native range:** Northeastern China (Heilongjiang, Jilin, Liaoning),
  the Russian Far East (Primorsky and Khabarovsk krais), Korea, and
  northern Japan (Hokkaido)
- **Habitat:** Mixed and coniferous forests, forest edges, thickets,
  and mountain slopes from 200–800 m elevation
- **Climate:** Cold-hardy to USDA Zone 3; prefers temperate to boreal
  conditions with cold winters and warm, moist summers
- **Soil:** Adaptable but prefers well-drained, loamy soils with
  moderate fertility and slightly acidic to neutral pH
- **Shade tolerance:** Tolerates partial shade but fruits more
  abundantly in full sun
