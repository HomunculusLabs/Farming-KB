# Arkana

## Description
Arkana (from Quechua *arkay* = to defend/protect) is the term for spiritual defenses or protective shields in Amazonian vegetalismo. Arkanas are energetic barriers maintained by shamans to protect themselves and their patients from sorcery, malevolent spirits, and hostile spiritual attacks during ayahuasca sessions.

## Classification
- **Type**: Shamanic technique / spiritual defense
- **Origin**: Amazonian mestizo and indigenous practice (Quechua language roots)
- **Domain**: Protective / defensive
- **Tradition**: Vegetalismo (Amazonian plant medicine)

## Key Facts
- Arkanas are spiritual defenses that protect practitioners and patients during ceremony and healing work.
- Different types of arkanas manifest as different serpents or animals, reflecting the practitioner's level of power and specialization.
- A white serpent of the white rainbow serves as the arkana for a shaman solely dedicated to healing the sick.
- An ochre-yellow boa serves as the arkana for a healer of average ability.
- A red boa that can journey into the waters serves as the arkana for a healer who can also kill.
- A golden boa serves as the arkana for a Conibo shaman with many powers who can both heal and kill.
- Black boas serve as the arkana for a Shetebo sorcerer who only kills and possesses all knowledge of witchcraft.
- The yana-cocodrilo (black crocodile) has its icaro controlled by the suamis or sumis.
- Arkanas can be placed on newborns to ensure strength.
- Aquatic arkanas are guarded by yana-yaku-lobos (black water wolves) who move swiftly in the water.
- The strength and nature of a practitioner's arkana directly reflects their moral character and level of shamanic attainment.
- Arkanas from plants like the tian camuri fairies provide protective power derived from specific [[dmt-botanical-sources-pharmacology]].

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology
- The concept of arkana reflects the Amazonian understanding that plant medicine work requires active protection, a principle applicable to any serious engagement with psychoactive plants.
- The relationship between a practitioner's moral character and their arkana emphasizes the ethical dimension of Amazonian plant medicine practice.
- Different plant species contribute different protective qualities, reinforcing the importance of plant diversity in the vegetalista's practice.

## Cultural Context
Arkanas appear throughout [[ayahuasca-preparation-pablo-amaringo-ucayali-shamanism]]'s [[amaringo-ayahuasca-visions-shipibo-iconography]], most prominently in Vision 11 (The Powers of the Pipes) where the relationship between shamanic rank and arkana type is explicitly detailed. The concept of arkana is fundamental to Amazonian vegetalismo, reflecting the constant spiritual warfare that practitioners navigate in their healing work.
