## Description

Jace Callway is a researcher mentioned in the context of entheogenic plant studies, specifically recognized for research demonstrating that DMT-containing plants are mixed with MAO inhibitor-containing plants to produce entheogenic brews such as ayahuasca. His work contributes to the understanding of how [[ethnobotanical-use-of-sacred-mushrooms-in-indigenous-cultures]] discovered and systematized plant combinations for visionary experiences.

## Classification

- **Category**: Person / Researcher
- **Field**: Ethnopharmacology / Psychoactive plant research
- **Research Focus**: DMT-containing plants, MAO inhibitors, ayahuasca analogs

## Key Facts

- Conducted research demonstrating that DMT (Dimethyltryptamine) containing plants are mixed in brews with MAO (Monoamine Oxidase) inhibitor containing plants
- His research helped establish the pharmacological basis for ayahuasca's effects: DMT is orally active only when combined with an MAO inhibitor
- Identified plant combinations including Banisteriopsis caapi (MAO inhibitor source) with various DMT-containing plants
- His work supports the understanding that indigenous peoples independently discovered these complex pharmacological combinations through traditional knowledge systems
- The research is referenced in the context of understanding the Soma mystery — the proposed five-plant admixture theory that combines Amanita muscaria, Psilocybe mushrooms, Syrian Rue, Opium Poppy, and green plants including Cannabis

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Plant-Fungus Interactions**: Callway's research on plant combinations for entheogenic effects connects to the broader field of how fungi and plants interact chemically, including [[teaming-with-microbes-fungi-and-mycorrhizal-relationships]] and the production of [[antifungal-secondary-metabolites-coprophilous-fungi]].
- **Ayahuasca Pharmacology**: The DMT-MAOI combination principle that Callway researched is fundamental to understanding ayahuasca, which itself is associated with the fungus-rich Amazonian ecosystem.
- **Ethnomycological Context**: His work contributes to ethnomycology by demonstrating that psychoactive plant knowledge often involves sophisticated multi-ingredient preparations, suggesting that mushroom use was likely part of broader plant-fungus pharmacological traditions.
- **Soma Research**: Callway's findings on plant admixtures are relevant to theories about the ancient Soma brew, which may have combined multiple psychoactive plants and fungi including [[amanita-muscaria-and-ibotenic-acid-containing-fungi]] Psilocybe species.
- **Chemical Ecology**: Understanding how plants produce compounds like DMT and beta-carbolines (MAO inhibitors) relates to chemical ecology — the study of how organisms use chemicals to interact, a field that includes [[biodiversity-fungal-secondary-metabolites]].
