## Overview

**Bufo bufo**, the common toad, is a large, widely distributed amphibian found across much of Europe and western Asia. It is one of the most familiar and recognizable amphibians in the Palearctic region.

Adults typically measure 6–10 cm in snout-vent length, with females reaching up to 13 cm. The species has a robust, warty body with a distinctive rounded snout and prominent parotoid glands behind the eyes.

## Ecology and Behavior

Common toads are crepuscular and nocturnal, foraging primarily between dusk and dawn. Their diet consists of invertebrates including slugs, earthworms, beetles, spiders, ants, and caterpillars.

Prey is captured by extending a sticky tongue or by lunging forward and seizing items with the jaws. Toads consume relatively large prey items compared to frogs of similar size.

A single adult toad may consume hundreds of invertebrates per night during peak activity periods. This voracious appetite makes them beneficial in gardens and agricultural settings as [[cervantes-biological-pest-control-predators]] control agents.

Spring migration to breeding ponds is triggered by rising temperatures above 5–8°C and increased humidity. Males arrive first and may wait several days for females, during which they attempt to grasp any moving object.

Males produce a low, rasping release call when clasped by another male. The advertisement call, when produced, is a quiet, sustained trill that carries only short distances.

Females lay 3,000–6,000 eggs in two gelatinous strings that may stretch 3–5 meters in length. The strings wrap around aquatic vegetation at varying depths.

Tadpoles develop over 8–12 weeks, reaching metamorphosis at approximately 10–12 mm in length. Newly metamorphosed toadlets are small, dark, and highly active.

Common toads hibernate from October to March, typically in frost-free locations underground, in compost heaps, or among tree roots. They may hibernate communally in large aggregations.
