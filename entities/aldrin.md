# Aldrin

## Description

Aldrin is an organochlorine insecticide that was widely used in agriculture from the 1950s through the 1970s before being banned or severely restricted in most countries due to its extreme persistence, bioaccumulation, and toxicity. Mentioned by Mollison as one of the persistent biocides that has contaminated orchards, sugarcane areas, pineapple, cotton, tobacco, and banana plantations to such an extent that even 18-20 years after cessation of use, soils may still produce unsafe residue levels in food products.

## Classification

- **Category**: Chemical / Organochlorine pesticide
- **Chemical name**: Hexachloro-hexahydro-dimethanonaphthalene
- **Chemical formula**: C12H8Cl6
- **CAS number**: 309-00-2
- **Related compound**: Dieldrin (Aldrin rapidly converts to Dieldrin in the environment)

## Key Facts

- Banned in most countries since the 1970s-1980s but persists in soils for decades
- Rapidly converts to Dieldrin in the environment through epoxidation
- Apple orchards in West Australia still produce unsafe levels in free-range chicken eggs 18-20 years after cessation of chemical use
- Attempts to grow prawns in ponds on former agricultural land failed due to residual Dieldrin levels
- Highly lipophilic: accumulates in fatty tissues of animals and moves through food chains
- Subject to global ban under the Stockholm Convention on Persistent Organic Pollutants (2001)
- Applied as persistent biocide alongside DDT, Dieldrin, BHC, and other organochlorines

## Environmental Persistence

- Half-life in soil: 1-6 years depending on conditions
- Does not readily break down in anaerobic (waterlogged) conditions
- Binds strongly to soil organic matter, especially in clay soils
- More mobile in acid sandy soils where it may leach to 50-60 cm depth
- Not significantly removed by leaching from clays or organic profiles (10-20 cm binding depth)
- Phosphate application may mobilize bound arsenic and other contaminants to deeper levels

## Health Effects

- Neurotoxin affecting the central nervous system
- Suspected carcinogen (EPA classification: probable human carcinogen)
- Disrupts endocrine function
- Accumulates in liver, fat, and nervous tissue
- In animals: causes toxaemic jaundice (liver poisoning), blood in urine
- Even low chronic exposure poses risks through bioaccumulation

- Former orchards, cane fields, and plantations are high-risk sites for Aldrin/Dieldrin contamination
