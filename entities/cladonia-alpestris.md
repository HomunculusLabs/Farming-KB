# Cladonia alpestris

**Cladonia alpestris** (syn. Cladonia stellaris) is a fruticose lichen in the family Cladoniaceae, commonly known as the grey reindeer lichen or reindeer moss. It forms extensive mats in boreal and arctic tundra ecosystems and is one of the most ecologically important lichen species in northern regions, serving as a primary food source for caribou and reindeer and exerting significant allelopathic effects on forest regeneration.

## Taxonomy and Distribution

Cladonia alpestris is a member of the Cladoniaceae, a family of lichenized fungi
in the Ascomycota. The species has been subject to taxonomic revision and is
sometimes treated as Cladonia stellaris. It is circumpolar in distribution,
forming extensive ground cover in [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]], Europe, and Asia, where it can
dominate the forest floor for hundreds of square kilometers.

## Lichen Structure and Nutrient Cycling

In the classification of lichen growth forms, Cladonia represents a mat-forming genus (Type 3 lichens) that contributes significantly to soil surface ecology. Unlike some other lichen types with more structured internal differentiation, Cladonia has a more diffuse association with the substrate, with elements distributed throughout the thallus rather than concentrated in specific layers. This diffuse organization may influence [[dighton-fungal-biomass-production-aquatic-ecosystems]] varying considerably between sites. The relatively slow growth rates of mat-forming lichens mean that recovery from disturbance (such as fire or grazing) can take decades, making these communities particularly vulnerable to land use changes.

## Allelopathic Effects on Trees

### Nursery Studies

Brown and Mikola (1974) demonstrated that the addition of Cladonia alpestris lichen to the soil surface significantly reduced the growth and survival of pine and spruce seedlings in a tree nursery. The lichen mulch had little effect on birch growth or survival, suggesting species-specific allelopathic sensitivity. This differential sensitivity may explain why birch can successfully establish in lichen-dominated communities while conifers struggle.

### Threshold Effect in Natural Ecosystems

In natural ecosystems, a ground cover of greater than 10% Cladonia alpestris was shown to reduce Scots pine seedling growth compared to similar or higher ground cover of Cladonia rangiferina or Arctostaphylos uva-ursi (bearberry). This threshold effect suggests that even moderate lichen cover can significantly impact tree regeneration, and that the allelopathic effect of Cladonia alpestris is stronger than that of some other common ground cover species.

### Phosphorus Uptake Reduction

Fisher (1979) conducted detailed studies of the allelopathic effects of Cladonia lichens on tree seedlings. White spruce and Jack pine seedlings were grown in the greenhouse in acidic sandy soil with mulches of Cladonia rangiferina or Cladonia alpestris. After 15 weeks of growth, the soil was spiked with radioactively labeled phosphorus (32P-PO4), and uptake was measured in plants harvested at 17 weeks.

The presence of Cladonia alpestris lichen mulch significantly reduced both tree seedling growth and phosphate uptake. Quantitative effects on Jack pine:

- Dry weight reduced to 56% of control (without lichen mulch)
- Phosphorus uptake reduced to 62% of control
- Nitrogen content reduced to 85% of control
- Phosphorus content reduced to 81% of control

Quantitative effects on white spruce:

- Dry weight reduced to 58% of control
- Phosphorus uptake reduced to 48% of control
- Nitrogen content reduced to 77% of control
- Phosphorus content reduced to 62% of control

### Phosphorus-Specific Inhibition

Nitrogen content was consistently less reduced than phosphorus content across both tree species, and potassium, calcium, and magnesium plant content were not affected by the lichen mulch. This indicates that the allelopathic effect specifically targets phosphorus nutrition rather than general [[query-how-does-phosphorus-availability-change-across-soil-ph-levels]] in the rhizosphere.

### Comparison with Cladonia rangiferina

Both Cladonia alpestris and Cladonia rangiferina showed allelopathic effects,
but the magnitude varied by tree species. For Jack pine, C. alpestris was
slightly less inhibitory than C. rangiferina for dry weight (56% vs 60% of
control) but slightly more inhibitory for phosphorus uptake (62% vs 56%). For
white spruce, C. alpestris was slightly less inhibitory for dry weight (58% vs
64%) but more inhibitory for phosphorus content (62% vs 54%).
