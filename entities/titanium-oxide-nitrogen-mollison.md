# Titanium Oxide Nitrogen Fixation (Mollison's System)

## Description

A catalytic nitrogen fixation system described by Bill Mollison in which titanium oxide (TiO2), a common white paint pigment, catalyzes the conversion of atmospheric nitrogen into ammonia in the presence of sunlight. Mollison presents this as a low-cost method for producing nitrogen fertilizer without industrial processes.

## Classification

- **Category**: Chemical catalysis / soil amendment technique
- **Type**: Photocatalytic nitrogen fixation
- **Materials**: White sand, titanium oxide (TiO2), water

## Key Facts

- **Catalytic reaction**: Titanium oxide catalyzes atmospheric N2 into NH3 (ammonia) when exposed to sunlight. Neither the sand nor the TiO2 is consumed.
- **Water solubility**: The resulting ammonia is highly water-soluble and can be flushed from the system with water to fertilize gardens directly.
- **Scale**: Three acres of desert under this system would supply as much nitrogen as a commercial fertilizer plant. Practically, three square feet suffices.
- **Desert relevance**: In desert environments, nitrogen naturally evaporates and rain carries ammonia back down, explaining desert plant growth.
- **Scientific basis**: Noted in Chemical Abstracts circa 1977, with implications for atmospheric circulation.

## System Design

1. Build a sand box with a trickle-in water system and subsurface barriers.
2. Fill with white sand. Add 0.25 ounce of titanium oxide.
3. Expose to sunlight for catalytic ammonia production.
4. Periodically flush with water (not continuous, to prevent algae).
5. Use ammonia solution to water gardens.

## Applications

- Small-scale household gardening
- Sand dune rehabilitation (1-2 lbs TiO2 per dune area)
- Desert agriculture without nitrogen-fixing pioneer species
- Third-world subsistence farming

## Ecological Significance

Mollison frames this as an example of natural factories working everywhere — titanium oxide catalysis occurs naturally in desert sands worldwide. The technique represents permaculture emphasis on harnessing natural processes over industrial agriculture.
