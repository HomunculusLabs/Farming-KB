## Overview

**Platanus × acerifolia**, the London planetree, is a hybrid deciduous tree resulting from a cross between *P. orientalis* (oriental planetree) and *P. occidentalis* (American sycamore).

It is one of the most widely planted urban trees in temperate cities worldwide, valued for its tolerance of pollution, compaction, and pruning.

The species forms a large, broad-crowned tree reaching 20–35 m (65–115 ft) in height with a trunk diameter exceeding 1.5 m (5 ft).

Its distinctive mottled bark and large palmate leaves make it immediately recognizable in streetscapes and parks.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Platanus × acerifolia** (Aiton) Willd. is a natural hybrid of *[[platanus-orientalis]]* L. and *[[platanus-occidentalis]]* L.

It was first described in the late 17th century from specimens growing in the Oxford Botanic Garden and the Vauxhall Gardens in London.

The hybrid is sometimes treated as *Platanus × hispanica* Münchh. in European literature.

It is partially fertile and occasionally produces viable seed, though most propagation is vegetative.

### Bark

The most distinctive feature is the exfoliating bark, which flakes away in irregular plates to reveal creamy white, olive green, and pale brown patches beneath.

This camouflage-like pattern develops after approximately 5–10 years of growth.

Young bark is smooth and grayish-green, lacking the characteristic flaking.

### Leaves

Leaves are alternate, palmately lobed, 15–25 cm (6–10 in) wide and long with 3–5 pointed lobes.

The leaf base is truncate to slightly cordate, and margins are coarsely toothed.

The upper surface is dark green and rough to the touch due to pubescence on veins.

Autumn color is typically yellow-brown, rarely producing the vivid displays of maples or ashes.

### Flowers and Fruit

Flowers are borne in dense, spherical heads 1–2 cm in diameter, appearing with the leaves in mid-spring.

The species is monoecious, with separate male and female flower heads on the same tree.

Fruit is a globose aggregate achenes, 2.5–3.5 cm (1–1.4 in) in diameter, hanging on long peduncles.

Each ball contains 600–1,000 tiny seeds, which are dispersed by wind over considerable distances.

## Distribution and Habitat

The London planetree is widely naturalized across [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]] of Europe, [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]], Australia, and parts of [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-south-america]].

It is not found in the wild as a native species; all populations originate from cultivated or naturalized plantings.

The tree thrives in [[query-how-do-green-roofs-and-living-walls-benefit-urban-environments]] including streets, plazas, parking lots, and parkland.

It tolerates a wide range of soil types from sandy loams to heavy clays, provided drainage is adequate.

Optimal growth occurs in deep, moist, fertile soils near waterways.

The hybrid is hardy to USDA Zone 5 (−23 °C / −10 °F) and performs well in heat Zone 7.

## Ornamental and Urban Use

London planetree is the preeminent street tree in cities such as London, Paris, New York, and Melbourne.

Its tolerance of atmospheric pollution, road salt, compacted soils, and repeated [[cervantes-root-pruning-rootbound-management]] makes it unmatched for harsh urban conditions.

The tree casts dense shade, reducing urban heat island effects and lowering building cooling costs.

Large mature specimens are significant landscape features, with spreading crowns spanning 15–20 m (50–65 ft).
