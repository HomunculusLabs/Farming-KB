# Eucalyptus wandoo

## Description

Eucalyptus wandoo, commonly known as **wandoo** or **white gum**, is a medium-sized tree endemic to Western Australia. It is a key species of the wheatbelt and goldfields regions, producing dense, durable timber and thriving in semi-arid conditions. The pale, smooth bark gives it the common name white gum. It is closely related to the powderbark wandoo (E. wandoo subsp. pulverea).

## Classification

- **Family:** Myrtaceae
- **Genus:** Eucalyptus
- **Species:** E. wandoo
- **Common names:** Wandoo, white gum

## Key Facts

- Medium-sized tree typically 10–25 m tall
- Smooth pale grey to white bark, sometimes with rough stocking at base
- Native to the wheatbelt and adjacent areas of southwestern Western Australia
- Produces hard, dense, pale-coloured timber of excellent durability
- Adapted to semi-arid conditions with 300–600 mm annual rainfall
- Prefers clay loams and heavy soils, often on valley floors and lower slopes
- Important component of wandoo woodland ecosystems
- Good honey producer with light-coloured honey

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

- **Timber:** Dense, durable timber used for fence posts, sleepers, flooring, and mining timbers (pages 94, 96, 117)
- **Firewood:** Excellent firewood with high density and calorific value (pages 94, 96, 117)
- **Honey:** Produces good quality, light-coloured honey valued by beekeepers (pages 94, 96)
- **Shelter and shade:** Well-suited to semi-arid shelterbelt plantings
- **Soil conservation:** Deep root system helps manage dryland salinity and soil erosion
- **[[hemenway-backyard-wildlife-habitat]]:** Provides important habitat and food source for native fauna

## Cultivation Notes

- Highly drought-tolerant once established
- Prefers heavy soils — clay loams and gravels
- Suited to semi-arid zones with 300–600 mm annual rainfall
- [[plants-tolerant-of-alkaline-soils]] soils
- Moderate frost tolerance
- Slow to moderate [[soil-temperature-plant-growth-rate-doubling-rule]]
- Best planted in autumn–winter in Mediterranean climates
