## Fagopyrum esculentum (Buckwheat)

## Overview

Buckwheat is an annual fast-growing plant in the family Polygonaceae, domesticated in Southwest China around 6000 BCE. Despite its common name, buckwheat is not related to wheat (Poaceae); it is a pseudocereal, producing grain-like seeds used as a gluten-free flour source. In permaculture and sustainable agriculture, buckwheat is valued primarily as a quick-cover crop, phosphorus-scavenging [[mollison-designers-bee-forage-tree-planting]].

## Botanical Description

Buckwheat grows 60–120 cm tall with heart-shaped leaves on slender, branching stems. Flowers are white to pink, arranged in clusters, and produce both nectar and pollen abundantly. The growing [[bionutrients-lab-calcium-phosphate-calamansi]], iron phosphate, and aluminum phosphate compounds that are inaccessible to most crop roots. This process:

- Increases plant-available P in the root zone
- Makes scavenged phosphorus available to subsequent crops when buckwheat residues decompose
- Is particularly effective on acidic, high-fixation soils where P is readily locked into insoluble forms

## Permaculture Applications

- **Smother crop:** Rapid germination and dense canopy (within weeks) suppresses weeds without herbicides
- **Phosphorus mining:** Solubilizes bound phosphorus for subsequent crop uptake
- **Bee forage:** Produces exceptionally high-quality, dark, strongly flavored honey; one of the best nectar sources for honeybees
- **Quick green manure:** Can produce 2–3 tonnes of biomass per hectare in 6–8 weeks; incorporated into soil before seed set
- **Emergency cover:** Useful for filling gaps in crop rotations, preventing soil erosion on exposed beds
- **Food production:** Gluten-free grain rich in rutin (a flavonoid glycoside), magnesium, and essential amino acids; used for soba noodles, kasha, pancakes, and sprouts
- **Biofumigation:** Residues release allelopathic compounds that suppress some soil-borne pathogens and weed seeds

## Cultivation

Buckwheat is direct-seeded at 40–60 kg/ha, 2–5 cm deep, after soil temperatures reach 10°C. It performs best in well-drained sandy loams with pH 5.0–7.0 but tolerates poor, acidic, and low-fertility soils. Sensitive to frost; killed by temperatures below -2°C. Successive sowings every 2–3 weeks provide continuous cover through [[query-how-to-attract-native-bees-and-beneficial-insects]] invertebrates
- Seeds provide food for game birds and small mammals
