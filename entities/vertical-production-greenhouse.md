# Vertical Production (Greenhouse)

## Description

Vertical production is a [[greenhouse-growing]] technique that maximizes yield per square foot by training vining or tall-statured crops upward on trellises, strings, or supports. By exploiting the vertical dimension of greenhouse airspace, growers can dramatically increase productivity in the valuable protected growing environment. This approach is particularly important for high-value summer crops grown in rotation with winter greens.

## Classification

- **Category**: Agricultural technique — spatial optimization
- **Environment**: Greenhouse, [[high-tunnel]], polytunnel
- **Crops**: Tomatoes, cucumbers, peppers, beans, and other vining crops
- **System**: Intensive protected cultivation

## Key Facts

- Greenhouse floor space is the most expensive square footage on a market garden — vertical production maximizes return on that investment
- Tomatoes, cucumbers, and peppers are the primary vertical crops in protected cultivation
- Crops are typically trained to single or double stems on strings attached to overhead wires
- Side shoots (suckers) are removed to direct growth energy into fruit production
- Height is managed by lowering and leaning plants as they grow, keeping fruit within reach
- Vertical production can yield 3–5x more per square foot compared to unsupported field culture
- Requires regular attention — training, pruning, and harvesting at least weekly
- Essential technique for making greenhouse summer production economically viable
- Combines well with winter production: vertical summer crops rotate with low-profile winter greens in the same greenhouse space

## Crop-Specific Methods

**Tomatoes**:
- Indeterminate varieties trained to single stem
- Clip or twine to overhead support wire at 6–7 feet
- Remove all side shoots below the growing tip
- Lower and lean as plants exceed support height
- Harvest 15–30 pounds per plant over the season

**Cucumbers**:
- European greenhouse types (parthenocarpic) trained vertically
- Plants can reach 10+ feet in height
- Prune to single stem, remove lateral branches
- Harvest frequently to maintain production

**Peppers**:
- Trained to 2–4 main stems with vertical support
- Less aggressive vertical growth than tomatoes or cucumbers
- Support prevents lodging under fruit weight

## Relevance to Cultivation

Vertical production is critical for greenhouse economics in market gardens:

- **Summer greenhouse use**: When winter greens are not being grown, the same greenhouse space generates income from high-value summer fruiting crops
- **Income justification**: The high yields from vertical production justify the capital investment in greenhouse infrastructure
- **Rotation integration**: Vertical summer crops complement low-profile winter crops in the same beds, creating year-round greenhouse utilization
- **[[crop-rotation-benefits]]**: Alternating between fruiting crops and leafy greens helps break pest and disease cycles
- **Labor planning**: Vertical crops require consistent attention during summer months when winter crops are not occupying the greenhouse

## Infrastructure Requirements

- Overhead support wires at 6–7 feet height
- Tomato clips or twine for plant attachment
- Sturdy end-post anchors to support crop weight
- Adequate headroom in [[greenhouse-design]]
- Access aisles for harvesting and training
