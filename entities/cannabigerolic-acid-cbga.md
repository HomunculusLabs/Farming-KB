# Cannabigerolic Acid (CBGA)

**Type:** Chemical Compound (Cannabinoid Acid Precursor)
**Category:** Phytocannabinoid (Mother Cannabinoid)
**Also known as:** CBGA, the "mother cannabinoid," cannabigerol acid

## Overview
Cannabigerolic acid (CBGA) is the foundational acidic precursor from
which all major cannabinoids are biosynthesized in the cannabis plant.
Often called the "mother cannabinoid," CBGA sits at the top of the
cannabinoid biosynthetic pathway and is enzymatically converted into
THCA, CBDA, and CBCA by their respective synthase enzymes. Without
CBGA, none of the other cannabinoids would exist in the plant. It is
also the acidic precursor to cannabigerol (CBG).

## Key Details

### Chemical Properties
- **Molecular formula:** C22H32O4
- **Classification:** Phytocannabinoid (central acidic precursor)
- **Biosynthetic role:** Ultimate precursor to all major cannabinoids
- **Enzymatic conversions:** CBGA is the substrate for THCA synthase,
  CBDA synthase, and CBCA synthase

### Biosynthesis Pathway
CBGA is produced in the trichome cells of cannabis from olivetolic acid
and geranyl pyrophosphate (GPP). Once formed, the relative activity of
three competing enzymes determines the final cannabinoid profile of
the plant:

1. **THCA synthase** converts CBGA to THCA (THC precursor)
2. **CBDA synthase** converts CBGA to CBDA (CBD precursor)
3. **CBCA synthase** converts CBGA to CBCA (CBC precursor)

The dominant synthase enzyme determines whether a strain is
THC-dominant (Type I), CBD-dominant (Type II/III), or balanced.
Genetic breeding programs select for strains with desired synthase
ratios to target specific cannabinoid profiles.

### Pharmacology
- **COX enzyme inhibition:** Alongside THC-acid, CBD-acid, CBD, and
  CBG, CBGA has demonstrated the ability to inhibit cyclooxygenase
  (COX) enzymes, contributing to anti-inflammatory effects
- **CB1 and CB2 receptor interaction:** Limited direct binding in its
  acidic form, but its decarboxylated product (CBG) has significant
  receptor activity
- **Anti-proliferative effects:** Laboratory studies have shown that
  CBGA, together with other cannabinoids, can inhibit [[cannabis-sativa-flowering-cycle-management]] and gradually decrease as
the plant converts it to downstream cannabinoids. [[cannabis-terpene-entourage-effect-profiles]]. The therapeutic potential
of CBGA itself, independent of its downstream products, is an active
area of investigation that may yield new non-psychoactive treatments
for inflammation and other conditions.
