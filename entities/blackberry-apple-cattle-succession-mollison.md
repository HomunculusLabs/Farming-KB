# Blackberry-Apple-Cattle Succession (Mollison)

## Description

A self-organizing ecological succession pattern described by Bill Mollison in which blackberry brambles, apple trees, and cattle interact to produce a productive, low-maintenance orchard system. The pattern begins with a pig pen and blackberry clump, progresses through apple tree establishment, and culminates in cattle-managed blackberry suppression with massive apple production. Mollison cites Geoff Wallace's deliberate application of this system.

## Classification

- **Category**: Ecological succession / integrated design pattern
- **Key organisms**: Blackberry (Rubus fruticosus), Apple (Malus domestica), Cattle (Bos taurus)

## The Succession Pattern

### Stage 1: Origin (Year 0)
- A pig pen exists with a blackberry clump in one corner.
- Apple seeds are deposited (via pig feed containing apples).
- An apple seedling emerges within the blackberry protection.

### Stage 2: Growth (Years 1-14)
- The apple tree grows up through the blackberry canopy.
- Blackberry protects the young tree from grazing and grass competition.
- Blackberry continually mulches the tree, keeping its root system free of grass.
- Blackberry growth pre-prunes the tree to a standard form, preventing low branching.
- The tree crowns into a classical British-type round crown with strong trunk.

### Stage 3: Production (Years 14-17)
- The tree drops 30+ bushels of apples.
- Apples ferment in the blackberries.
- Cattle smell the fermenting apples and wade chest-deep into blackberries to reach them.
- Cattle tread heavily on blackberry canes, smashing them flat.
- Partial shading from the now-large apple tree begins to suppress blackberries.
- Cattle become unable to stay out — the attraction is irresistible.

### Stage 4: Maturity (Year 17+)
- Result: A single massive apple tree, 70 feet across, 60 feet high.
- Eight feet clear of branches (blackberry-pre-pruned standard).
- Yielding 70 bushels of apples (40 for cattle, 30 for harvest).
- Blackberries suppressed by shade and cattle trampling.

## Ecological Functions

- **Grass suppression**: Blackberry prevents grass competition at tree roots. Grasses produce chemicals hostile to trees — brambles help trees win the grassland-forest conflict.
- **Mulching**: Blackberry continually drops leaf litter, creating thick mulch around the tree base.
- **Pre-pruning**: Blackberry growth removes lower branches, creating a clean standard trunk.
- **Crown shaping**: Results in classical round crown form.
- **Fire resistance**: Bramble-covered systems have very low litter accumulation, reducing fire risk.

## Geoff Wallace's Application

Mollison notes that Geoff Wallace applies this pattern deliberately, having "completely run out of blackberries, wiped blackberries right off his property" through managed succession with cattle and fruit trees.
