## Jude Hobbs

Jude Hobbs is an American permaculture designer, educator, and ecological land-use consultant based in the Pacific Northwest (Cascadia bioregion). She is the founder and director of **Cascadia [[permaculture-institute]]** and has been teaching permaculture design courses (PDCs) since the early 1990s, making her one of the most experienced permaculture educators in [[pdc-water-harvesting-earthworks-design]], and wildlife habitat corridors.

Hobbs holds qualifications in permaculture design, horticulture, and landscape architecture. She has served on the board of several permaculture organizations and has been an active voice in the professionalization of permaculture design as a discipline.

## Teaching and Educational Philosophy

Hobbs is widely regarded as one of the most effective and experienced permaculture teachers in North America, having taught over 50 Permaculture Design Courses since the early 1990s.

Her teaching philosophy centers on several key principles:

**Observation-first approach:** Students are trained to spend extensive time observing site conditions — soil, water flow, existing vegetation, microclimates, wildlife patterns — before beginning any design work. This contrasts with template-based approaches that overlay standard designs onto sites.

**Integration of native ecology:** Unlike some permaculture educators who focus primarily on cultivated systems, Hobbs emphasizes understanding and working with existing native plant communities. She teaches that native plant knowledge is essential for creating resilient, site-adapted designs.

**Experiential learning:** Her courses emphasize fieldwork, site visits, and hands-on design exercises over lecture-based instruction.
Students complete multiple full site designs during a standard PDC.

**Accessible design language:** Hobbs is known for her ability to communicate complex ecological concepts in clear, practical terms. Her teaching style makes permaculture accessible to diverse audiences including farmers, homeowners, and community organizers.

She has taught at the University of Oregon, Oregon State University, and numerous community colleges, in addition to her independent PDC offerings through Cascadia Permaculture Institute.

## Key Principles

Several design principles recur throughout Hobbs' teaching and consulting work:

**Start with water:** Every design begins with mapping and managing the water cycle on site. Swales, keyline patterns, and retention landscapes form the backbone of her designs.

**Edge is where the action is:** She emphasizes maximizing productive edges between ecosystems — forest-field transitions, riparian zones, windbreak-field interfaces — as the most biodiverse and productive areas.

**Function stacking:** Each element in a design should serve multiple functions. A hedgerow provides wind protection, wildlife habitat, nitrogen fixation, pollinator forage, and potentially food harvest simultaneously.
