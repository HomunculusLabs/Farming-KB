# Diazotrophs

## Description

Diazotrophs are microorganisms — primarily [[teaming-with-microbes-bacteria-and-archaea-in-soil]] — capable of **nitrogen fixation**: the process of breaking the extremely strong triple covalent bonds of atmospheric nitrogen (N₂) and converting it into biologically available forms such as ammonium (NH₄⁺) and nitrate (NO₃⁻). These organisms are the biological foundation of the [[nitrogen-cycle]] and are responsible for making atmospheric nitrogen accessible to plants and all higher life forms. Before the invention of the Haber-Bosch process in the early 1900s, diazotrophs were the only significant source of fixed nitrogen for life on Earth.

## Classification

- **Domain**: Bacteria and Archaea (diazotrophs span both domains)
- **Functional Group**: Nitrogen-fixing microorganisms
- **Key Enzyme**: Nitrogenase (breaks N≡N triple bonds)
- **Categories**:
  - **Symbiotic diazotrophs**: Live in mutualistic association with plants (e.g., Rhizobia, Frankia)
  - **Free-living diazotrophs**: Independent soil organisms (e.g., Azotobacter)
  - **Associative diazotrophs**: Loosely associated with plant roots (e.g., Azospirillum)

## Key Facts

1. The term "diazotroph" literally means "nitrogen eater" (from Greek *dia* = through, *aza* = nitrogen, *troph* = nourishment)
2. Atmospheric nitrogen (N₂) makes up **78% of Earth's atmosphere** but is unavailable to most organisms due to the extremely strong triple [[covalent-bond]]
3. Diazotrophs possess the enzyme **nitrogenase**, which catalyzes the energy-intensive reduction of N₂ to NH₃
4. Nitrogenase is **oxygen-sensitive** — it functions only in low-oxygen environments, which is why many diazotrophs form nodules or live in anaerobic microsites
5. Up to **50% of the nitrogen in a garden** can come from nitrogen-fixing organisms
6. Before the Haber-Bosch process (early 1900s), atmospheric nitrogen bonds could only be broken by biological means via diazotrophs
7. Today, [[nitrogen-fixing-bacteria-and-biological-nitrogen-fixation]] accounts for roughly **50–60% of available nitrogen**; the rest comes from industrial fixation
8. A tiny fraction of nitrogen is also fixed by **electrical storms and snow storms**, but this is negligible compared to biological fixation

## Major Groups of Diazotrophs

### Symbiotic Diazotrophs
- **Rhizobia**: Soil bacteria forming symbiotic relationships with legumes in root nodules; specific Rhizobia species must match specific legume species
- **Frankia**: Filamentous actinomycetes that associate with actinorhizal plants (alders, bayberry, sea buckthorn, oleaster)

### Free-Living Diazotrophs
- **Azotobacter**: Free-living soil bacteria capable of independent nitrogen fixation
- **Clostridium**: Anaerobic nitrogen fixers
- **Cyanobacteria**: Photosynthetic bacteria (e.g., Nostoc, Anabaena) that fix nitrogen in aquatic and terrestrial environments

### Associative Diazotrophs
- **Azospirillum**: Bacteria that colonize the rhizosphere of grasses and cereals
- **Klebsiella**: Found in association with various plant roots

## The Nitrogen Fixation Process

1. Diazotrophs use the enzyme nitrogenase to break N≡N triple bonds
2. The reaction requires substantial energy: N₂ + 8H⁺ + 8e⁻ + 16 ATP → 2NH₃ + H₂ + 16 ADP
3. In symbiotic systems, the host plant provides carbon-based exudates as energy for the diazotroph
4. Root nodules create the low-oxygen environment necessary for nitrogenase function
5. Up to 20% excess nitrogen is produced by Rhizobia and moves into [[the-soil-food-web]]
6. Much of this excess nitrogen is transported to other plants by **mycorrhizal fungi**

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- Diazotrophs are the biological alternative to synthetic nitrogen fertilizers produced by the Haber-Bosch process
- Inoculating crops and soils with appropriate diazotrophs can significantly reduce fertilizer requirements
- Cover [[mollison-avenue-cropping-with-legumes]] harnesses Rhizobia to build soil nitrogen for subsequent crops
- Diazotroph activity is central to **[[eliot-coleman-deep-organic-farming-and-winter-harvest]] regenerative agriculture** systems
- Mycorrhizal fungi serve as conduits, moving nitrogen fixed by diazotrophs from the rhizosphere to non-leguminous plants
- [[contamination-prevention-in-mushroom-cultivation]] on nitrogen-poor substrates, diazotrophs may contribute to nitrogen availability
- Diazotroph populations are sensitive to soil pH, moisture, and the presence of synthetic nitrogen (which suppresses biological fixation)

## Relationships

- **Rhizobia**: The most familiar group of diazotrophs; symbiotic with legumes
- **Frankia**: Actinomycete diazotrophs; symbiotic with actinorhizal plants
- **Azotobacter**: Free-living diazotroph genus found in soils worldwide
- **Nitrogenase**: The enzyme responsible for nitrogen fixation in all diazotrophs
- **Mycorrhizal Fungi**: Distribute biologically fixed nitrogen from diazotrophs to host plants
- **Haber-Bosch Process**: Industrial alternative to biological nitrogen fixation
