## Overview

**Phellodendron amurense**, the **Amur cork tree**, is a deciduous tree species
in the family Rutaceae native to northeastern China, Korea, Japan, and the
Russian Far East. It is one of two commercially important *Phellodendron*
species, the other being *P. chinense*.

The thick, corky bark (known as **Huang Bo** or **Phellodendri Cortex** in
traditional Chinese medicine) has been used medicinally for over two millennia.
It is one of the "Three Yellows" of TCM, prized for its heat-clearing,
damp-drying, and fire-purging properties. The bark is rich in [[berberine]], a
potent isoquinoline alkaloid with broad pharmacological activity.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:** Plantae > Magnoliophyta > Magnoliopsida > Sapindales >
Rutaceae > *Phellodendron*.

**Habit:** A medium to large deciduous tree reaching 15-25 m in height with a
broad, spreading crown. Bark is thick, corky, and deeply furrowed, gray-brown
externally with bright yellow inner bark characteristic of the genus.

**Leaves:** Odd-pinnately compound, 20-40 cm long, with 5-13 leaflets.
Leaflets are ovate to lanceolate, 5-12 cm long, with serrate margins and
acute apices. The foliage is lustrous dark green above, paler beneath,
turning yellow in autumn.

**Flowers:** Small, dioecious, greenish-yellow, borne in axillary panicles.
Male flowers have 5-6 stamens; female flowers bear a superior ovary. Flowering
occurs in May to June.

**Fruit:** A small drupe, 8-10 mm in diameter, black at maturity, containing
2-5 seeds. Fruits ripen in September to October and are dispersed by birds.

## Distribution and Habitat

*P. amurense* is native to the Amur River basin, Manchuria, the Korean
Peninsula, and northern Japan. It occurs in mixed hardwood forests at
elevations from 200 to 1500 m, often on well-drained mountain slopes and
river valleys.

The species is cold-hardy to USDA zone 3, making it one of the most cold-
tolerant members of Rutaceae. It prefers deep, fertile, well-drained loamy
soils with slightly acidic to neutral pH. It tolerates partial shade when
young but requires full sun for optimal growth and bark production.

## Chemistry and Pharmacology

The bark contains **berberine** (1-5%) as the primary bioactive alkaloid,
along with **palmatine**, **jatrorrhizine**, **phellodendrine**, and
**candicine**. Flavonoids including phellamurin and amurensin are also
present.

Berberine demonstrates antibacterial, antifungal, antiviral, anti-
inflammatory, antidiabetic, and cardioprotective activities in preclinical
studies. It activates AMPK signaling, improves insulin sensitivity, and
reduces hepatic glucose output. [[cannabis-metabolic-syndrome-research]].

Palmatine contributes additional antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory effects.
The bark extract as a whole shows synergistic activity exceeding individual
isolated compounds, consistent with the polypharmacological approach of TCM.
