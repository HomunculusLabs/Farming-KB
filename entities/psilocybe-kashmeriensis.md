# Psilocybe kashmeriensis

**Type:** Species (Fungus)
**Category:** Basidiomycota, Agaricales, Strophariaceae, Psilocybe
**Also known as:** None widely used

## Overview
Psilocybe kashmeriensis is a Psilocybe species first described from
Kashmir, India, by Abraham (1995). It grows gregariously on the cut
aerial clumps of lemon grass (Cymbopogon jawarancusa), an economically
important plant for citronella and other essential oils. The species is
especially prolific the second and third year after planting. Activity
has not been documented but is considered possible, as it does not
exhibit bluing despite its affinity for tall grass habitats.

## Key Details
- **Cap:** 3-4 cm broad. Campanulate at first, expanding to convex
  with distinct acute umbo. Ochraceous to chrome yellow, fading to
  pale brown. Surface smooth, viscid when moist, often cracking.
  Appendiculate with whitish veil remnants on margin. Margin
  incurved when young, often elevated with age. The chrome-yellow
  coloration of fresh specimens is distinctive among Psilocybe.
- **Gills:** Adnate to adnate-sinuate, distant. Dull yellow, darkening
  with age, becoming dark purplish brown at maturity, often mottled.
- **Stem:** 40-80 mm long by 6-8 mm thick. Even, swelling towards
  base which radiates clusters of white rhizomorphs. Partial veil
  cortinate, leaving apical fibrillose zone darkened with spores.
- **Spores:** Dark purplish brown in deposit, ellipsoid, 8-10.5 x
  5-7 um. Basidia 4-spored. Pleurocystidia common, similar to
  cheilocystidia. Cheilocystidia fusoid to clavate to capitate,
  sometimes forked, 20-60 x 5-8 um.
- **Habitat:** Gregarious on camel grass or lemon grass (Cymbopogon
  jawarancusa). Especially prolific second and third year after
  planting. First reported from Kashmir, India.
- **Chemistry:** Activity not documented. Does not bruise bluish,
  though several grass-loving Psilocybe species (P. semilanceata,
  P. strictipes) can be potent without strong bluing due to low
  psilocin content.

## Distribution and Ecology
Psilocybe kashmeriensis was first described from Kashmir, India
(Abraham 1995), representing one of the few Psilocybe species
documented from the Indian subcontinent. Its association with
Cymbopogon jawarancusa (lemon grass) is highly specialized, growing
on cut aerial clumps of this economically important plant. The
species is especially prolific during the second and third year after
grass planting, suggesting the mycelium requires time to establish
extensive networks in the grass root zone. The lemon grass substrate
provides a unique combination of nutrients from decomposing grass
tissues and the microclimate created by dense grass clumps.

## Relevance
Psilocybe kashmeriensis is significant as one of the few
Psilocybe species documented from the Indian subcontinent. Its
specialized association with commercially cultivated lemon grass
creates a unique ecological interaction between a potentially
psychoactive fungus and an economically important crop. The
species' prolific fruiting on cut grass clumps suggests it may
play a role in decomposition of grassy substrates in tropical
and subtropical Asian environments. Its relationship to P.
squamosa and the absence of bluing despite possible activity
parallels patterns seen in other grassland Psilocybe species,
reinforcing that bluing reaction alone is an unreliable indicator
of psychoactive content.
