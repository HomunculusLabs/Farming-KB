## Psilocybe kashmeriensis

**Type:** Species (Fungus)
**Category:** Basidiomycota, Agaricales, Strophariaceae, Psilocybe
**Also known as:** None widely used

## Overview
Psilocybe kashmeriensis is a Psilocybe species first described from
Kashmir, India, by Abraham (1995). It grows gregariously on the cut
aerial clumps of lemon grass (Cymbopogon jawarancusa), an economically
important plant for citronella and other [[juncao-grass-substrate-technique]]
provides a unique combination of nutrients from decomposing grass
tissues and the microclimate created by dense grass clumps.

## Relevance
Psilocybe kashmeriensis is significant as one of the few
Psilocybe species documented from the Indian subcontinent. Its
specialized association with commercially cultivated lemon grass
creates a unique ecological interaction between a potentially
psychoactive fungus and an economically important crop. The
species' prolific fruiting on cut grass clumps suggests it may
play a role in decomposition of grassy substrates in tropical
