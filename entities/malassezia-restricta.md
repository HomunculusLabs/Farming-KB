# Malassezia restricta

**Malassezia restricta** is a lipophilic (lipid-dependent) [[biodiversity-yeast-diversity-ecology]].

## Morphology and Physiology

### Cellular Morphology
- **Cell Shape:** Ovoid to ellipsoidal, approximately 2 to 4 by 3 to 5 μm
- **Reproduction:** Reproduces asexually by budding, producing daughter cells with a narrow base of attachment (unipolar budding)
- **Cell Wall:** The cell wall contains specific lipid and carbohydrate components adapted to its skin surface habitat

### Lipid Dependence
The most distinctive physiological characteristic of *Malassezia* species is their absolute requirement for exogenous lipids:

- **Culture Requirements:** Cannot be cultured on standard mycological media. Requires supplementation with lipid sources such as Tween, oleic acid, or cholesterol for growth
- **Mechanism:** The genus has lost the ability to synthesize [[fungal-commensal-vertebrate-associations]].
- **Strain-Level Diversity:** Multilocus typing and whole-genome sequencing are revealing previously unrecognized strain-level diversity within *M. restricta*
- **[[ribosomal-rna-sequencing-yeast-identification]] and proteomic studies are characterizing the gene expression programs of *M. restricta* under different [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]] show detectable phylogenetic structure, suggesting some geographic population differentiation, though human migration and global travel complicate these patterns
- **Scalp versus Body Sites:** Strains isolated from the scalp may exhibit genetic and physiological differences from those found on other body sites, reflecting adaptation to the specific microenvironment of the scalp (high sebum production, hair coverage, distinct [[greg-green-temperature-and-humidity-control]])

## Biochemical Compounds and Metabolites

*Malassezia restricta* produces several biochemically significant compounds:

- **Indole Derivatives:** Metabolomic studies have identified indole-containing compounds produced by *M. restricta* that can influence aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR) signaling in skin cells, potentially modulating immune responses and barrier function
- **Lipase Enzymes:** Secreted lipases (LIP1, LIP2 families) hydrolyze sebum triglycerides into free fatty acids, including oleic acid, which triggers the inflammatory cascade associated with dandruff and seborrheic dermatitis
- **Sphingolipids:** *Malassezia* species produce unique sphingolipids that are not found in other fungi, reflecting their adaptation to the skin lipid environment. These sphingolipids may contribute to cell wall integrity and host immune interactions
- **Malassezin:** A tryptophan-derived metabolite identified in *Malassezia* cultures that has been investigated for potential immunomodulatory and neuroactive properties, with structural similarity to the mammalian signaling molecule endocannabinoid
