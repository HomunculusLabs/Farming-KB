## Leaf Mold (JADAM)

## Description

In the JADAM organic farming system, leaf mold (forest floor decomposing organic matter) is the foundational model for ideal soil and the primary source of beneficial microorganisms. Youngsang Cho"s core principle of soil management is to "make your soil like leaf mold" — replicating the conditions found on the forest floor where diverse communities of fungi, bacteria, actinomycetes, and other organisms create optimal growing conditions naturally. Leaf mold serves as the inoculant for JADAM Microorganism Solution (JMS) and as the philosophical template for the entire JADAM approach to soil health.

- **Type**: Soil amendment / microbial inoculant / soil model

## Key Facts

- The core model for JADAM soil management: "make your soil like leaf mold"

- Used as seed and seedling treatment medium to establish beneficial microbial populations on young plants
- Represents the natural state of healthy soil that JADAM seeks to replicate in agricultural settings
- Free and universally available in forested areas
- The decomposing organic matter on the forest floor creates a self-sustaining system of nutrient cycling that requires no external inputs
- JADAM explicitly rejects conventional composting in favor of approaches that mimic leaf mold decomposition processes

## The Leaf Mold Principle

Youngsang Cho"s observation that the forest floor — where leaf mold accumulates and decomposes — represents the ideal model for agricultural soil is the foundation of JADAM soil management. In the forest, leaves fall and are decomposed by a succession of microorganisms, creating a rich, well-structured growing medium without any human intervention. The JADAM approach seeks to replicate this natural process on the farm by introducing leaf mold microorganisms (via JMS), adding organic matter, avoiding soil disturbance that disrupts microbial communities, and maintaining conditions that favor beneficial fungal and bacterial growth.

## JADAM Microorganism Solution (JMS)

JMS is made by culturing microorganisms from leaf mold in water, feeding them potatoes or other starchy medium. The resulting solution is a powerful soil quality improver that can also be used as a foliar spray for keeping pathogens in check. JMS contains the diverse microbial communities found in healthy forest soil, including bacteria, fungi, actinomycetes, and protozoa. When applied to agricultural soil, these organisms establish populations that improve soil structure, enhance nutrient cycling, suppress pathogens, and promote plant growth.

## Rejection of Conventional Composting

JADAM explicitly advocates for getting "rid of the compost method" in favor of approaches that more closely mimic natural leaf mold decomposition. Conventional composting, with its focus on achieving high temperatures through thermophilic decomposition, is seen as destructive to beneficial microbial communities. Instead, JADAM promotes anaerobic fermentation and the direct incorporation of organic matter into the soil, processes that more closely resemble the gradual decomposition that occurs on the forest floor. This approach preserves a wider diversity of beneficial organisms, including many fungal species that are killed by the high temperatures of conventional composting.

## Seed and Seedling Treatment

Leaf mold is used directly as a seed and seedling treatment, coating seeds and young plant roots with beneficial microorganisms before planting. This establishes a protective microbial community on the plant from the earliest stages of growth, providing ongoing protection against pathogens and enhancing nutrient uptake through mycorrhizal associations. This practice mirrors the natural process by which seeds in the forest germinate in contact with the leaf mold layer and immediately establish symbiotic relationships with soil microorganisms.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Leaf mold is fundamentally a mycological resource. The decomposing organic matter on the forest floor is processed primarily by fungal mycelia, which break down cellulose and lignin that bacteria cannot efficiently decompose. By using leaf mold as the source of microorganisms and as the model for ideal soil, JADAM implicitly recognizes the central role of fungi in creating and maintaining healthy soil. The JADAM approach to soil management is essentially an application of forest mycology to agricultural practice, seeking to recreate the diverse fungal communities and mycorrhizal networks that characterize healthy forest ecosystems in agricultural settings.
