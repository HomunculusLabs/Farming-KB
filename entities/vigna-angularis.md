## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:**

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Order:** Fabales
- **Family:** Fabaceae
- **Subfamily:** Faboideae
- **Genus:** Vigna
- **Species:** V. angularis (Willd.) Ohwi & H. Ohashi

**Synonyms:** Phaseolus angularis (Willd.) W.F. Wight, Dolichos angularis Willd., Azukia angularis (Willd.) Ohwi.

Adzuki bean is an erect, bushy annual herb typically 30–60 cm tall, though some climbing varieties can reach 90 cm. The stems are slender, green to purplish, and sparsely pubescent.

Leaves are trifoliate with ovate to rhomboid leaflets 5–12 cm long. The terminal leaflet is larger than the lateral pair. Petioles are 5–15 cm long with stipels at the base.

Flowers are papilionaceous, yellow to pale orange, borne in axillary racemes of 5–20 flowers. Flowering is self-pollinating, which favors genetic uniformity in cultivated varieties.

Pods are cylindrical, 5–13 cm long, and contain 5–12 seeds. Mature pods are black or dark brown and may shatter at maturity in wild types.

Seeds are small (4–6 mm long), typically red but ranging from white, black, mottled, to green in different cultivars. The hilum is white and conspicuous.

## Cultivation

Adzuki bean is primarily grown in East Asia, with China accounting for approximately 70% of global production. Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, and Thailand are other major producers.

The crop grows well in temperate to [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]] and during the monsoon season in tropical areas. Seed rate is 20–30 kg/ha with [[comparison-biological-nitrogen-fixation-vs-synthetic-fertilizer]]. The crop fits well in rotation with rice, wheat, or barley.

## Nutritional Value

Adzuki beans contain approximately 19–25% protein on a dry weight basis. They are notably lower in fat than many other legumes, with only 0.5–2% lipid content.

The seeds are rich in dietary fiber (15–17%), particularly soluble fiber including resistant starch. This contributes to their low glycemic index and prebiotic properties.

**Key nutrients per 100 g dry seeds:**

- Protein: 19–25 g
- Dietary fiber: 15–17 g
- Carbohydrates: 55–60 g
- Fat: 0.5–2 g
- Iron: 4–5 mg
- Potassium: 1,250–1,400 mg
- Folate: 220–250 µg

Polyphenols, including proanthocyanidins and catechins, are concentrated in the seed coat.

Red varieties show significantly higher antioxidant capacity than white-seeded types.

Sprouting adzuki beans increases vitamin content and reduces trypsin inhibitor activity by 50–70%.

## Ecology and Applications

Adzuki bean forms effective nitrogen-fixing symbioses with Bradyrhizobium, contributing 40–80 kg N/ha to the soil. This makes it a valuable break crop in cereal-based farming systems.
