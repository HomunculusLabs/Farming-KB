## Overview

**Abies nordmanniana**, the Nordmann fir or Caucasian fir, is a large evergreen conifer in the family Pinaceae. It is native to the mountains of the western Caucasus and adjacent parts of northeastern Turkey.

The species is among the most important [[fagus-orientalis]]*), Caucasian spruce (*[[carpinus-betulus]]*) at lower elevations.

Disjunct populations have been reported in the mountains of northern Iran, though their taxonomic status remains debated among botanists.

## Cultivation and Silviculture

Nordmann fir is widely cultivated as a Christmas tree across Europe, particularly in Denmark, Germany, Belgium, and the United Kingdom.

Christmas tree production is a major horticultural industry; Denmark alone produces approximately 8–10 million Nordmann fir trees annually for export.

Seedlings require 8–12 years in the field to reach typical Christmas tree height (1.5–2 meters). The species has excellent needle retention, lasting 4–6 weeks after cutting.

Cultivation requires well-drained, slightly acidic soils and protection from hot, drying winds. It is less cold-hardy than many other firs, hardy to approximately USDA zone 5.

Timber cultivation is practiced on a smaller scale, primarily in its native range and in experimental plantations in western Europe.
