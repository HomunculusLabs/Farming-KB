# BOH

**PIHKAL Entry #15** -- beta-Methoxy-3,4-methylenedioxyphenethylamine

## Chemical Name

beta-Methoxy-3,4-methylenedioxyphenethylamine

## Dosage and Duration

- **Dosage:** 80 - 120 mg

- **Duration:** 6 - 8 h

## Qualitative Effects

BOH produces a mild mood enhancement with primarily physical effects at low doses (20-40 mg). At higher doses (80-120 mg), users report:

- Distinct body awareness and warmth

- Skin prickling, cold feet

- A "real plus two" experience with dilated pupils and thirst, but no visuals

- Good humor without particular insightfulness

- The compound has been described as a "strange mix of some sort of altered state along with bodily discomfort" at psychedelic-seeking doses

At 90 mg, one subject noted threshold effects that were mostly physical: inside warmth, skin prickling, loose bowels, and anorexia. The experience was "good humored but not insightful." At 100 mg, vague nausea and chilling of feet preceded a plus-two state described as being "very turned on" but without being stoned or visual effects.

## Synthesis

Synthesized from piperonal via formation of 3,4-methylenedioxy-beta-nitrostyrene, followed by sodium methoxide addition to yield 1-methoxy-1-(3,4-methylenedioxyphenyl)-2-nitroethane, and final reduction with LAH in the presence of sulfuric acid. The hydrochloride salt crystallized with a mp of 152-153 degrees C (anhydrous form).

## Extensions and Commentary

BOH is a member of the BOX family (Benzyl + Oxy prefix). The name follows the convention discussed in the BOD recipe, with RHS for homopiperonylamine as the simplest member of the Muni-Metro family. The demethylated homologue of BOH is BOHH, the methylenedioxy analogue of norepinephrine. BOH may hydrolytically open in the body to provide this neurotransmitter, acting as a "pro-drug" -- a compound not intrinsically active but metabolizing to provide an active drug.

Shulgin noted an uncanny analogy between BOH and the [[tryptamine]] alpha,O-dimethylserotonin (a,O-DMS), both being pro-drugs that mask polar functions with ether groups to gain brain access. Only the racemic mixture has been tasted; Shulgin speculated that one optical isomer might produce the body effects while the other produces the mental effects.

## Chemical Family Context

BOH belongs to the BOX family of phenethylamines, a group exploring beta-oxygenation
of the phenethylamine backbone. This structural modification is unusual in
psychedelic chemistry, where most research has focused on ring substitution
rather than aliphatic chain modification.

The BOX family includes:

- BOB: beta-methoxylated with bromine ring substitution

- BOD: beta-methoxylated with dimethoxy ring substitution

- BOM: beta-methoxylated with trimethoxy (mescaline pattern)

- BOH: beta-methoxylated with methylenedioxy ring substitution

The pro-drug hypothesis is particularly interesting for BOH, as hydrolytic
cleavage of the beta-methoxy group would yield the methylenedioxy analogue
of norepinephrine -- a catecholamine that could have its own pharmacological
activity at adrenergic receptors. This dual-action potential (direct effects
plus metabolite effects) makes the BOX family pharmacologically complex.

## Pharmacological Discussion

The pro-drug hypothesis for BOH suggests that its effects may be mediated
not by the compound itself but by its metabolic conversion to a catecholamine-like
species. This would explain the predominantly physical effects (warmth, skin
prickling, cold feet) with minimal visual or cognitive psychedelic effects --
these physical symptoms are more consistent with adrenergic receptor activation
than with 5-HT2A agonism.

The comparison to alpha,O-dimethylserotonin (a,O-DMS) in the tryptamine
series strengthens the pro-drug interpretation. Both compounds mask polar
groups with ether linkages, potentially allowing brain penetration followed
by enzymatic cleavage to release the active species.

If the pro-drug hypothesis is correct, the optical isomers of BOH could have
different pharmacological profiles: one isomer might be preferentially
metabolized to the catecholamine, while the other might resist metabolism
and produce direct (but different) effects. This speculation remains untested.

## Comparison to Related Compounds

- MDA (3,4-methylenedioxyamphetamine): More potent, more visual, longer duration

- MDPEA (homopiperonylamine): The demethylated precursor, mild stimulant

- BOM (beta-methoxymescaline): Same beta-methoxy pattern, different ring substitution

- Norepinephrine: The hypothetical metabolite of BOH's hydrolytic cleavage

BOH's effects profile -- physical warmth, prickling, mood elevation without
visuals -- is more similar to mild stimulants or empathogens than to classic
psychedelics, consistent with the pro-drug/catecholamine hypothesis.

## Harm Reduction and Safety Considerations

- The physical discomfort reported (nausea, cold feet, loose bowels) may indicate
adrenergic effects; cardiovascular monitoring is advisable

- The pro-drug hypothesis means the compound's effects may depend on individual
metabolic enzyme activity, leading to unpredictable inter-individual variation

- Standard phenethylamine precautions apply: avoid MAO inhibitors, SSRIs, and
other serotonergic or adrenergic medications

- The compound has not been extensively characterized for toxicity

- The racemic mixture tested may behave differently from individual enantiomers

## Legal Status

BOH is not specifically scheduled in most jurisdictions. As a methylenedioxy-substituted
phenethylamine, it could potentially fall under analogue legislation related to
MDA or MDMA (both Schedule I in the US). The compound's rarity and lack of
recreational use have kept it largely below regulatory attention.

## Cultural and Historical Significance

BOH's primary significance is in its contribution to the understanding of
pro-drug strategies in psychedelic chemistry. The concept that a compound
could be pharmacologically inactive in its administered form but active
through metabolic conversion has important implications for drug design,
both for therapeutic and research applications.

The analogy between BOH and a,O-DMS across the phenethylamine-tryptamine
divide illustrates the deep structural and pharmacological connections
between these two major classes of psychedelic compounds.
