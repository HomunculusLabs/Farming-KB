## Description

Muscarine is a parasympathomimetic alkaloid found in trace amounts in [[amanita-muscaria-and-ibotenic-acid-containing-fungi]] more abundantly in certain Inocybe, Clitocybe, and Omphalotus species. For over a century, muscarine was incorrectly believed to be the primary intoxicating principle of the fly agaric mushroom, but it is now known to be present in concentrations far too low to produce the characteristic psychoactive effects.

## Classification

- **Category**: Parasympathomimetic alkaloid
- **Chemical class**: Quaternary ammonium compound
- **Molecular formula**: C9H20NO2+
- **Source organisms**: Amanita muscaria (trace), Inocybe spp., Clitocybe spp.

## Key Facts

- Named after Amanita muscaria, from which it was first isolated (1869)
- Present in only minute concentrations in A. muscaria; insufficient for psychoactive effects
- For approximately 100 years, erroneously believed to be the active principle of fly agaric
- Activates muscarinic acetylcholine receptors (M1 through M5)
- SLUDGE syndrome: Salivation, Lacrimation, Urination, Defecation, Gastrointestinal distress, Emesis
- More clinically significant in Inocybe and Clitocybe poisoning than in Amanita muscaria ingestion
- Now recognized that muscimole, not muscarine, is the primary intoxicant of A. muscaria

## Pharmacological Effects

- Potent parasympathetic nervous system activator
- Does not cross the blood-brain barrier (quaternary ammonium structure)
- Causes SLUDGE syndrome in toxic doses
- Not hallucinogenic; its effects are purely peripheral autonomic
- Atropine is the specific pharmacological antagonist

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Understanding muscarine is important for:

- **Mushroom safety**: Recognizing and treating muscarine poisoning from Inocybe and Clitocybe species
- **[[growing-gourmet-pleurotus-sajor-caju-taxonomic-confusion]]**: Clarifying why A. muscaria was historically misunderstood
- **Toxicology education**: Distinguishing between different types of [[amanita-toxins-and-mushroom-poisoning]]
