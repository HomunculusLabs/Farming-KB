## Overview

**Gentiana farreri** (syn. *Gentiana hexaphylla* var. *farreri*) is a herbaceous perennial gentian in the family Gentianaceae.

It produces large, deep blue trumpet-shaped flowers that make it one of the most sought-after species for alpine rock gardens worldwide.

The species was named in honor of Reginald Farrer (1880–1920), the English plant collector and rock-garden writer.

Farrer encountered this gentian during his plant-hunting expeditions in the mountains of western China in the early [[gentiana-veitchiorum]]*, sharing their trailing growth habit and large blue flowers.

Plants form low, spreading mats of trailing stems 10–20 cm long that root at the nodes where they contact moist soil.

Leaves are lanceolate to elliptic, arranged in pairs along the stems, glossy green, and 1.5–3 cm in length.

Flowers are solitary, terminal, funnel-shaped, and 4–6 cm long, among the largest in section *Chondrophyllae*.

The corolla is deep cobalt-blue to gentian-blue with pale greenish speckling inside the throat.

Flowering occurs from late summer through autumn (August–October), providing late-season color in the alpine garden.

Each stem typically produces one flower, but well-grown mats may display dozens of blooms simultaneously.

The root system is fibrous and relatively shallow, adapted to the thin soils of alpine scree and rock crevices.

## Distribution and Habitat

The species is native to the mountains of western China, specifically Sichuan, Gansu, and Qinghai provinces.

It grows at elevations of 3,000–4,500 meters on rocky slopes, alpine meadows, and scree fields.

Gentiana farreri prefers cool, moist conditions with excellent drainage.

In its native habitat it experiences cold winters with reliable snow cover and short, cool summers with abundant monsoon moisture.

The species occurs in subalpine to alpine zones where it receives full sun to light shade.

It frequently grows among granite rocks and in well-drained moraine deposits alongside other high-altitude specialists.

Rainfall in its native range averages 600–1,000 mm annually, concentrated in the summer monsoon season.

Soils are typically acidic granitic sands with high organic content from decomposing alpine vegetation.

## Ecology and Cultivation

In the wild, Gentiana farreri grows in association with other alpine plants including saxifrages, primulas, and dwarf rhododendrons.

It serves as a late-season nectar source for alpine bumblebees and other pollinators active in late summer.

Cultivation requires careful attention to drainage and moisture balance.
