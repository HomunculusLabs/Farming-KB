## Overview

**Aulacomnium palustre** (riband bog-moss, marsh aulacomnium) is an acrocarpous moss in the family Aulacomniaceae, widespread across boreal and temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere. It is a characteristic species of wetland habitats including bogs, fens, marshes, and moist stream banks.

The species is recognized by its distinctive riband-like leaf arrangement on upright stems, with leaves that have strongly revolute margins creating a ribbon-like appearance. It forms loose to dense turfs in saturated or periodically flooded substrates.

Aulacomnium palustre plays an important ecological role in wetland ecosystems, contributing to peat formation, water filtration, and microhabitat creation. It is one of the more moisture-dependent forest and wetland mosses, often growing in association with Sphagnum species.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

- **Division:** Bryophyta
- **Class:** Bryopsida
- **Order:** Rhizogoniales
- **Family:** Aulacomniaceae
- **Genus:** *Aulacomnium* Schwägr.
- **Species:** *A. palustre* (Hedw.) Schwägr.
- **Authority:** Johann Hedwig, 1801 (basionym: *Mnium palustre* Hedw.)

Gametophytes form loose to moderately dense turfs 3 to 12 cm tall. Stems are erect, simple or sparingly branched, with a tomentose base of reddish-brown rhizoids that anchors the plant firmly in soft substrates.

Leaves are 3 to 5 mm long, broadly ovate-lanceolate, and conspicuously riband-like due to strongly revolute margins that curl backward from the leaf base nearly to the apex. This rolled margin creates a channel on the leaf underside.

The costa is strong and extends to the leaf tip. Leaf cells are linear-elongate in the upper lamina and shorter, more rectangular toward the base. The revolute margins bear distinctive, elongate cells that differ from the lamina cells.

The sporophyte bears a pendulous capsule on a curved seta 1.5 to 3 cm long. Capsules are 2 to 3 mm long, cylindrical, and distinctly ribbed or furrowed when dry. The operculum is long-rostrate, giving the capsule a beaked appearance.

A distinctive vegetative propagation structure, the propagulum, is produced in dense clusters at stem apices. These multicellular, bud-like structures detach readily and establish new colonies, serving as the primary mode of vegetative spread in wetland habitats.

When dry, the revolute leaf margins create a prominently ribbed, riband-like appearance that is visible to the naked eye. This distinctive character separates Aulacomnium from superficially similar wetland mosses such as Mnium and Rhizomnium.

The species is dioicous, with male and female reproductive structures on separate plants. Sporophyte production requires proximity of both sexes and a water film for fertilization, limiting capsule frequency in some populations.

## Distribution and Habitat

Aulacomnium palustre has a circumboreal distribution across Europe, Asia, and [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]] accumulation that characterizes northern peatland ecosystems.

The species provides important microhabitat for wetland invertebrates including water mites, nematodes, and various insect larvae. Its turfs create elevated platforms above the water table that serve as feeding and refuge sites for small arthropods.
