# Artemia (Brine Shrimp)

## Description

Artemia is a genus of aquatic crustaceans commonly known as brine shrimp, found in hypersaline water bodies worldwide. In aquaculture and [[permaculture]] systems, Artemia and their cysts are the most widely used live food organism for feeding larval and juvenile fish, prawns, and shrimp.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Animalia
- **Phylum**: Arthropoda
- **Subphylum**: Crustacea
- **Class**: Branchiopoda
- **Order**: Anostraca (fairy shrimp)
- **Family**: Artemiidae
- **Genus**: Artemia
- **Primary species**: A. salina, A. franciscana

## Key Facts

- Inhabit salt lakes, salt pans, and hypersaline environments worldwide (Great Salt Lake, San Francisco Bay salt ponds, etc.)
- Produce dormant cysts (eggs) that can remain viable for years in dry storage and hatch within 24-48 hours when placed in saltwater
- Nauplii (newly hatched larvae) are approximately 0.4 mm long -- ideal first food for many fish and crustacean larvae
- Adults reach 8-12 mm and can be fed to larger juvenile fish
- Filter feeders consuming microalgae, bacteria, and detritus
- Can survive in salinity ranges from 30-300+ ppt (seawater is approximately 35 ppt)

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

Mollison specifically mentions Artemia mixtures as a beneficial food source for prawns in pond systems:

### Live Food for Prawns
- Prawns benefit from Artemia (brine shrimp) mixtures as a food source
- Most fish species also benefit from shrimp in their diet, making Artemia a versatile live food
- In permaculture pond systems, Artemia can be cultured in dedicated saline ponds and harvested for feeding prawns and fish fry

### Broader Aquaculture Role
- The global aquaculture industry depends heavily on Artemia cysts for larval rearing of virtually all cultured fish and crustacean species
- Cysts are harvested commercially from salt lakes and sold worldwide
- Newly hatched nauplii are nutritionally superior due to their yolk reserves
- Can be enriched with essential fatty acids and vitamins before feeding to improve nutritional value

### Permaculture Integration
- Small-scale Artemia culture can be established in shallow saline ponds or tanks
- The cysts' ability to be stored dry makes them an excellent backup food source
- Can be integrated into sequential pond systems where saline ponds feed into fish production ponds
- Fits the permaculture principle of producing food at multiple trophic levels

## Cultivation Notes

- Cysts hatch in 24-48 hours at 25-28 degrees C in 25-35 ppt salinity with aeration and light
- Can be cultured to adult size in 2-3 weeks on a diet of microalgae or commercial feeds
- Hatching requires conical containers with vigorous aeration
- Cysts are commercially available and relatively inexpensive

## Life Cycle and Cyst Biology

Artemia have a remarkable dual reproductive strategy adapted to extreme environments:
- **Ovoviviparity**: In favourable conditions, females produce live nauplii that hatch internally
- **Oviparity**: Under stress (drying, extreme salinity, temperature shock), females produce dormant cysts encased in a tough chorion shell

These cysts can remain viable for decades in cool, dry storage. When rehydrated in saltwater with oxygen and light, they hatch within 18-36 hours, providing an on-demand live food supply. This "biological storage" capability makes Artemia uniquely suited to permaculture systems where seasonal food availability must be managed.

## Integration with Sequential Pond Systems

In Mollison's consecutive series pond designs, Artemia culture can be integrated as the first trophic level:
- Small saline ponds or tanks produce Artemia nauplii
- These flow into or are harvested for prawn and shrimp rearing ponds
- Prawns in turn provide food for larger fish in the next pond in the series
- This trophic ladder approach maximises energy capture at every level

The small scale of Artemia production ponds (one-seventh to one-ninth of the next pond in series) means they require minimal space while providing disproportionate value to the entire system's productivity.
