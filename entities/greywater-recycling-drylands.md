## Description

Greywater recycling in dryland permaculture refers to the systematic capture, treatment, and beneficial reuse of domestic wastewater (from sinks, showers, handbasins) for irrigation of productive vegetation. In desert settlements, greywater is not waste but a critical resource for establishing fuel forests, windbreaks, food gardens, and maintaining settlement vegetation. All settlements must close their water cycles to survive in arid environments.

## Classification

- **Category**: [[mycorrhizal-fungi]] associated with greywater-irrigated trees benefit from consistent moisture and nutrient availability.
- Anaerobic settling ponds for methane production support unique fungal communities.
- [[fungal-decomposition]] of greywater organics prevents clogging of irrigation lines and improves water quality.

## Design Principles

- Subsurface delivery is preferred to prevent human contact with greywater pathogens.
- Health and soil filtration are primary reasons for subsurface rather than surface application.
- All organic matter in greywater should be captured and cycled back to productive use.
- No settlement should waste water that can grow trees, food, or fuel.
- Systems must be designed for periodic maintenance and cleaning.

## Integration with Permaculture Zones

- **Zone 0 (House)**: Source of greywater from sinks, showers, basins
- **Zone 1 (Garden)**: Subsurface irrigation of food crops via slot-pipe or waste channels
- **Zone 2-3 (Orchard/Windbreak)**: Arbor system for productive trees
- **Zone 3-4 (Fuel forest)**: Ponded and settled greywater for coppice forestry
- **Zone 5 (Wild)**: final filtration and absorption
