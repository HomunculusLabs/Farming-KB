## Atriplex (Saltbush)

## Description

*Atriplex*, commonly known as saltbush, is a genus of salt-tolerant shrubs and subshrubs in the family Chenopodiaceae (now often placed in Amaranthaceae). Mollison identifies saltbush as one of the key plant groups capable of thriving in saline soils with more than 0.4% sodium chloride, making it valuable for supplanting salt-affected crops in dryland regions undergoing salinization.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Family:** Chenopodiaceae / Amaranthaceae
- **Genus:** *Atriplex*
- **Common name:** Saltbush
- **Growth habit:** Shrubs and subshrubs, typically 0.5–3 m tall
- **Species diversity:** Approximately 250–300 species worldwide

## Salt Tolerance

*Atriplex* species are among the most salt-tolerant vascular plants:

- Thrive in soils exceeding **0.4% sodium chloride**
- Tolerate both soil salinity and saline irrigation water
- Accumulate salt in specialized bladder cells on leaf surfaces, which are shed to excrete excess salt
- Maintain growth and nutritional value under saline conditions where conventional crops fail

## Role in Dryland Salinity Management

Mollison includes saltbush (*Atriplex*) among salt-tolerant plants that can be used to:

- **Supplant affected crops** in areas where soil salinity has progressed beyond the tolerance of conventional agriculture
- **Provide forage** for livestock on otherwise unproductive salted land
- **Stabilize soil** on salt-affected sites to reduce erosion
- **Serve as a transitional crop** while underlying salinity causes are addressed

However, Mollison warns that emphasis on salt-tolerant species alone "will simply delay investigation into basic causes and treatment." Saltbush should complement, not replace, structural solutions like [[permaculture-design]] for dryland and salinity-affected sites, *Atriplex* provides a productive intermediate layer between structural earthworks (interceptor banks) and long-term tree establishment. It offers immediate productive use of salted land while deeper remediation proceeds. Designers should integrate saltbush as part of a multi-strategy approach: interceptor banks to halt waterlogging, trees on upslope banks for groundwater management, and saltbush in transitional zones for forage production and soil stabilization.
