# Oscar Schmiedeberg

## Description

Oscar Schmiedeberg was a German chemist and pharmacologist who, together with his colleague Koppe, isolated the toxic alkaloid from *Amanita muscaria* (the fly agaric mushroom) and gave it the name **Muscarine**. His work in the late 19th century contributed significantly to the understanding of mushroom poisoning and the search for effective antidotes.

## Biographical Details

- **Nationality**: German
- **Field**: Pharmacology / Chemistry
- **Collaborator**: Koppe (co-researcher in muscarine isolation)
- **Era**: Late 19th century (referenced in Palmer's 1870–1879 writings)
- **Key institution**: Active in German pharmaceutical research

## Key Contributions

### Muscarine Isolation

Schmiedeberg and Koppe isolated the alkaloid from *Amanita muscaria* and named it Muscarine. This was one of several independent isolations of toxic compounds from *Amanita* species:

- **Letellier** (France): Named his isolate "Amanitine" (from multiple Amanita species)
- **Schmiedeberg & Koppe** (Germany): Named theirs "Muscarine" (from *A. muscaria*)
- **Boudier** (France): Named his "Bulosine" (from *A. bulbosa*)

Palmer noted that the effects of all three compounds were nearly identical and advocated for "Amanitine" as the umbrella term, as it was derived from the whole family rather than a single species.

### Atropine as Antidote Discovery

Schmiedeberg and Koppe made the crucial pharmacological discovery that **atropine** (the active principle of belladonna) acts as a physiological antagonist to muscarine:

- A frog's throbbing heart is silenced by a drop of muscarine
- The heart's action is restored by a drop of atropine
- Animals pre-treated with hypodermic injections of atropine are unharmed by otherwise fatal doses of muscarine

This antagonism between muscarine and atropine became one of the foundational examples of receptor pharmacology, demonstrating that certain drugs can block the effects of others at the physiological level.

## Historical Context

Schmiedeberg worked during a period when the chemistry of fungal toxins was poorly understood. French chemists were described as having "seldom been able to isolate" the subtle alkaloid, and no reliable reagent existed to detect its presence in either the fungus or the stomach of a victim. Schmiedeberg's isolation and characterization of muscarine represented a major advance in this field.

His advocacy for hypodermic (subcutaneous) injection of atropine as a treatment for Amanita poisoning was confirmed by independent clinical observation: a consulting physician who had never heard of Schmiedeberg's work independently arrived at the same treatment — using belladonna tincture injected hypodermically — by observing the patient's symptoms and comparing them with the known effects of belladonna.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Schmiedeberg's work is foundational to:

- **Mushroom toxicology**: Understanding the pharmacological action of *Amanita* toxins
- **Emergency medicine**: The atropine-muscarine antagonism remains relevant in treating certain types of mushroom poisoning
- **Pharmacology**: His discovery of drug antagonism at the receptor level became a cornerstone concept in modern pharmacology
- **Safe foraging**: His research reinforced the need for positive identification of all mushroom species before consumption
