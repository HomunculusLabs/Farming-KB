## Brown Rot

## Description

Brown rot is a destructive [[bloomfield-amphibian-decline-fungal-disease-climate-interactions]] affecting [[query-how-do-i-grow-stone-fruits-like-peaches-plums-and-cherries]] (Prunus species)
including cherries, peaches, plums, and apricots. It is caused primarily by
*Monilinia fructicola* and *Monilinia laxa*. The disease affects blossoms,
twigs, and fruit, causing blossom blight, twig cankers, and a characteristic
soft brown rot of mature fruit. [[elaine-ingham]] specifically discusses brown rot
on cherries in the context of [[compost-tea-disease-suppression]].

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Phylum**: Ascomycota
- **Class**: Leotiomycetes
- **Order**: Helotiales
- **Family**: Sclerotiniaceae
- **Genus**: *Monilinia*
- **Key species**: *M. fructicola*, *M. laxa*, *M. fructigena*

## Key Facts

- Infects blossoms, twigs, and fruit of stone fruits (cherries, peaches,
  plums, apricots, almonds).
- Blossom blight: flowers turn brown and die, often with tan-gray spore
  masses; infected blossoms may stick to twigs and form cankers.
- Fruit rot: begins as small brown spots that rapidly expand; fruit becomes
  soft, brown, and covered with tan-gray cushion-like spore masses.
- Infected fruit may mummify (dry and shrivel) and remain attached to the
  tree or drop to the ground, serving as overwintering inoculum.
- Spores are produced abundantly and spread by wind, rain splash, and insects.
- Optimal infection conditions: 20–25°C with moisture (rain or dew) for
  blossom infection; fruit become more susceptible as they ripen.
- Brown rot on cherries can destroy an entire crop within days during warm,
  wet harvest periods.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

### Compost Tea Applications

Elaine Ingham includes brown rot on cherries as a disease suppressible through
[[actively-aerated-compost-tea]] (AACT):

1. **Blossom protection**: AACT applied during bloom colonizes flower
   surfaces, preventing *Monilinia* spores from germinating and infecting
   delicate blossom tissue.
2. **Fruit surface colonization**: Regular [[aact-applications-guide-ingham]] maintain
  beneficial biofilms on developing fruit, creating barriers to infection
  as fruit ripens and becomes more susceptible.
3. **Mummy management**: AACT applied to the [[fukuoka-orchard-floor-management]] helps decompose
   mummified fruit more rapidly, breaking the disease cycle.
4. **Canopy biology**: Beneficial organisms in AACT colonize twig surfaces,
  suppressing canker development and reducing overwintering inoculum.

### Orchard Management

- Ingham's approach treats the entire orchard ecosystem — soil, floor,
  canopy, and fruit — as interconnected habitats where beneficial biology
  must be maintained.
- Prevention through biological coverage is emphasized over curative
  treatments.

## Management Notes

- Apply AACT at pink bud stage, full bloom, petal fall, and pre-harvest.
- Remove and destroy mummified fruit from trees and orchard floor.
- Prune out cankered twigs during dormant season.
- Use fungal-dominated [[compost-tea-for-specific-crops]] woody perennial applications.
- Maintain [[soil-food-web]] health to support overall tree vigor.
