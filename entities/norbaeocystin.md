## Norbaeocystin

Norbaeocystin (4-PO-T, O-phosphoryl-4-hydroxytryptamine) is a
naturally occurring **[[psilocybin-containing-mushrooms-in-us]]. It is the **demethylated precursor** to both
baeocystin and psilocybin in the fungal biosynthetic pathway, and
represents the simplest phosphorylated tryptamine alkaloid in the
series. First identified as a minor constituent of [[baeocystin-and-norbaeocystin-in-psilocybin-biosynthesis]] and the [[hplc-analysis-of-tryptamine-alkaloids]] found in psilocybin
mushrooms.

Like psilocybin and baeocystin, norbaeocystin is a **prodrug** that
requires dephosphorylation by alkaline phosphatase to yield its
putative active metabolite, **4-hydroxytryptamine (4-HT)** — also
known as **4-hydroxy-serotonin**. This metabolite is a positional
isomer of serotonin itself (which is 5-hydroxytryptamine, or 5-HT).
The difference between 4-HT and 5-HT is simply the position of the
hydroxyl group on the indole ring — para (4-position) versus meta
(5-position) relative to the amine-containing side chain.

Norbaeocystin is highly water-soluble due to its zwitterionic character
and phosphate group. It is detectable by HPLC-MS methods, typically
eluting before baeocystin and psilocybin on reversed-phase columns.
The [M+H]⁺ ion for norbaeocystin has an m/z of 257, which can be used
for mass spectrometric identification alongside characteristic fragment
ions including losses of H₃PO₄ and H₂O.

## Biosynthesis

Norbaeocystin occupies the **first committed step** in the psilocybin
biosynthetic pathway that distinguishes psilocybin-producing fungi from
other tryptamine-producing organisms. The complete pathway is:

1. L → tryptamine (via PsiD, decarboxylation)
2. Tryptamine → 4-hydroxytryptamine (via PsiH, P450 hydroxylation)
3. 4-hydroxytryptamine → **norbaeocystin** (via PsiK, phosphorylation)
4. Norbaeocystin → baeocystin (via PsiM, first N-methylation)
5. Baeocystin → psilocybin (via PsiM, second N-methylation)

The phosphorylation step catalyzed by **PsiK** is the key branching
point — without it, the pathway cannot produce psilocybin or its
analogs. PsiK is a 4-hydroxytryptamine kinase that transfers a
phosphate group from ATP to the 4-hydroxy position of the indole ring.
Norbaeocystin is the product of this phosphate transfer and serves as
the methyl acceptor substrate for PsiM.

The discovery that PsiM catalyzes two sequential methylations — first
of norbaeocystin to baeocystin, then of baeocystin to psilocybin —
explained why both intermediates accumulate in mushroom tissue. The
enzyme uses S-adenosyl methionine (SAM) as the methyl donor for both
reactions. Studies of PsiM kinetics suggest that the first methylation
(norbaeocystin → baeocystin) proceeds more efficiently than the second,
potentially explaining the relative [[psilocybe-cubensis-developmental-stages]] with reduced PsiM
expression might be expected to accumulate more norbaeocystin, but this
has not been systematically studied. There may also be developmental
regulation, with norbaeocystin levels peaking earlier in [[comparison-fruiting-body-vs-mycelium-extracts-medicinal-mushrooms]]
