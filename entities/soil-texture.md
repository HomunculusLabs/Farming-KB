# Soil Texture

## Description

Soil texture refers to the relative proportions of sand-, silt-, and clay-sized mineral particles in soil. Texture is determined entirely by particle size, not by chemical composition. It is one of the most fundamental soil properties because it governs water retention, drainage, aeration, nutrient-holding capacity, and the suitability of soil for different types of plant and microbial life.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Soil science / Pedology
- **Category:** Soil physical properties
- **Subcategory:** Particle size distribution

## Key Facts

- **Sand:** Particles range from 0.0625 to 2.0 mm in diameter. Visible to the naked eye. Gritty texture. Large pore spaces drain quickly (gravitational water) but hold little capillary water. Influenced by gravity; settles rapidly in water.
- **Silt:** Particles range from 0.004 to 0.0625 mm in diameter. Requires a microscope to see individual particles. Smooth, flour-like texture when rubbed between fingers. Holds more capillary water than sand. Also settles in water, but more slowly than sand.
- **Clay:** Particles are 0.004 mm or less in diameter. Requires an electron microscope to resolve individual particles. Plastic and slippery when wet. Composed of hydrous aluminum silicates, often with magnesium or iron. Tremendous surface area — a gram of clay one-particle thick would cover a basketball court. Holds the most nutrients and water of any particle type.
- **Size comparison:** If a clay particle were the size of a marigold seed, a silt particle would be a large radish, and a sand grain would be a large garden wheelbarrow.
- The soil texture triangle is used to classify any soil by plotting its sand, silt, and clay percentages.
- Texture has nothing to do with composition — sand particles are not necessarily quartz, and silt can have the same mineral constituents as sand, just smaller.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Soil texture directly determines what kind of soil food web a soil can support. Sandy soils drain too freely, losing nutrients and drying out; they tend to support less fungal biomass. Clay soils can become waterlogged and anaerobic, limiting oxygen for beneficial aerobic organisms. Loam, the balanced texture, provides the best conditions for diverse microbial communities, robust mycorrhizal networks, and healthy root development. Texture cannot be easily changed (it is a fundamental property of the mineral component), but it can be modified through organic matter additions that improve structure and water dynamics regardless of the underlying mineral texture.
