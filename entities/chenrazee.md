# Chenrazee (Avalokiteshvara)

## Description
Chenrazee (Tibetan name for Avalokiteshvara, also known as Chenrezig) is the Bodhisattva of Compassion, one of the most revered figures in Tibetan Buddhism. In *The Psychedelic Experience*, Chenrazee appears as a companion to Amitabbha in the Third Vision of the Second Bardo, embodying mercy and compassion — "the great pitier ever on the lookout to discover distress and to succour the troubled."

## Classification
- **Category:** Deity — Bodhisattva
- **Domain:** Tibetan Buddhism, Mahayana Buddhism, Bardo Thodol
- **Tradition:** Vajrayana Buddhism

## Key Facts
- Tibetan form of Avalokiteshvara, the Bodhisattva of Compassion
- Considered the patron deity of Tibet
- The Dalai Lama is considered an emanation of Chenrazee
- Name means "He who looks with an unwinking eye" — ceaseless compassion for all beings
- Associated with the mantra "Om Mani Padme Hum"
- Appears alongside Amitabbha in the Bardo Thodol's Fifth Day vision
- Accompanied by the "Glorious Gentle-voiced One" and female incarnates of "song" and "light"
- Represents the quality of mercy that arises during the Fire-Flow vision of internal unity

## Role in the Psychedelic Bardo Framework
Chenrazee appears as a supportive figure in Vision 3 (Fire-Flow of Internal Unity), representing the compassionate quality of consciousness that emerges when the ego dissolves into emotional unity with all life. His presence signals that the voyager's experience includes the quality of active compassion — not just passive merging, but a felt urge to relieve suffering in all beings.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology
In psychedelic therapy contexts, the compassionate quality represented by Chenrazee often manifests as profound empathy — the sense that one can directly feel others' emotions and suffering. This is one of the most therapeutically valuable aspects of psilocybin experiences. Cultivators and guides working in therapeutic settings may encounter this archetype as clients report feelings of universal compassion and connection.
