# Red Velvet

**Red Velvet** is an indica-dominant hybrid [[blesching-cannabis-strain-selection-receptor-targeting]] renowned for its striking visual appearance — dense, deep purple buds coated in fiery orange pistils that evoke the layered colors of a red velvet cake. Bred by crossing **[[tree-of-knowledge-and-forbidden-fruit-theory]]** (Cherry Pie × Tangie) with a select **Red Velvet Cake** phenotype, this strain combines bold cherry and citrus terpenes with heavy, sedating effects suitable for evening use.

## Genetics and Lineage

```
Cherry Pie × Tangie → Forbidden Fruit
          ↓
Forbidden Fruit × Red Velvet Cake selection
          ↓
      Red Velvet
```

- **Forbidden Fruit** contributes cherry, tropical, and musky grape terpenes alongside potent, relaxing body effects.
- **Red Velvet Cake** parent (itself a Cherry Pie descendant) reinforces the cherry terpene profile and deep purple bud coloration.
- The resulting hybrid is approximately **65–75% indica**, though the exact ratio varies by phenotype.

## Appearance

Red Velvet is a visually stunning strain that commands attention in any jar:

- **Buds:** Dense, medium-to-large, with a chunky, indica-typical structure. Calyxes are tightly packed and often exhibit dramatic purple coloring ranging from deep violet to near-black.
- **Pistils:** Abundant, long, bright orange to red-orange hairs that give the buds their namesake "velvet" quality.
- **Trichomes:** Heavy, frosty layer of milky-white trichomes with occasional amber heads, indicating full maturity and peak cannabinoid production.
- **Color:** The interplay of purple calyxes and orange pistils against a frosty trichome backdrop creates a genuinely eye-catching aesthetic.

## Aroma and Flavor

### Aroma

- **Primary:** Rich, sweet cherry with a creamy, bakery-like quality — the "red velvet cake" association is immediate and unmistakable.
- **Secondary:** Hints of earthy musk, sour citrus, and a subtle grape note inherited from the Forbidden Fruit lineage.
- **Tertiary:** Dried flowers and a faint peppery spice on the exhale when ground.

### Flavor

- **Inhalation:** Sweet cherry cream with mild citrus tang; smooth and dessert-like.
- **Exhalation:** Earthy, woody undertones emerge, with a lingering cherry sweetness on the palate. The flavor is notably persistent, coating the mouth.
- **Aftertaste:** Creamy vanilla-cherry notes that fade slowly, reminiscent of frosting.

## Terpene Profile

- **Myrcene** (dominant) — Musky, herbal, relaxing; responsible for the strain's sedative body effects.
- **Limonene** — Citrus brightness, mood elevation, stress relief.
- **Caryophyllene** — Peppery spice, anti-inflammatory, adds depth to the flavor.
- **Linalool** — Floral, lavender notes; contributes to calming, anxiolytic effects.
- **Humulene** — Earthy, woody; subtle appetite suppression.

## Cannabinoid Profile

- **THC:** 18–26% (phenotype-dependent; premium cuts consistently test above 22%)
- **CBD:** < 1% (negligible)
- **CBG:** Trace amounts
- **THCV:** Negligible

## Effects

### Onset and Duration

Effects begin within 5–10 minutes of inhalation and typically last 2–3 hours. The experience follows a classic indica-dominant trajectory.

### Experience

- **Initial (0–15 min):** A pleasant cerebral lift — mood elevation, mild euphoria, and a hazy, dreamy headspace. Colors may seem slightly more vivid and music more immersive. The onset is smoother than many high-THC indicas, with minimal initial anxiety.

- **Middle (15–45 min):** The body high emerges gradually, starting in the shoulders and neck before spreading as a warm, heavy relaxation. Physical tension dissolves, and a pleasant "couch-lock" sensation develops. Mental effects shift from stimulation to a contemplative, introspective calm.

- **Peak (45–90 min):** Deep physical sedation. Users report a "melting" sensation as muscle tension releases completely. The mental state becomes deeply relaxed, sometimes bordering on sleepy. Appetite stimulation is significant during this phase.

- **Comedown (90–180 min):** Gradual taper into sleepiness. Most users find Red Velvet transitions naturally into restful sleep if consumed in the evening.

### Medical Applications

- **Insomnia:** One of the strain's strongest medical applications. The sedative body high reliably promotes sleep onset.
- **[[cannabis-chronic-pain-management]]:** Myrcene-dominant profile provides significant analgesic effects for musculoskeletal pain, neuropathy, and arthritis.
- **Anxiety and stress:** Linalool and limonene contribute anxiolytic properties, though high doses may paradoxically increase anxiety in sensitive users.
- **Appetite stimulation:** Effective for nausea, appetite loss (cachexia), and eating disorders.
- **Muscle spasms:** Anti-spasmodic effects useful for MS, fibromyalgia, and menstrual cramps.

## Cultivation

### Growing Characteristics

- **Difficulty:** Moderate. Red Velvet can be finicky about nutrients and requires attention to environmental conditions for optimal color development.
- **Flowering time:** 8–9 weeks indoors.
- **Yield:** Moderate — 300–400 g/m² indoors; 350–500 g/plant outdoors.
- **Height:** Medium; reaches 100–150 cm indoors with training.
- **Stretch:** Moderate post-flower stretch (50–75%).

### Environmental Tips for Color

The deep purple coloration of Red Velvet is enhanced by:

- Cool nighttime temperatures (15–18°C) during the final 2–3 weeks of flowering.
- Slight [[query-how-to-fix-phosphorus-deficiency-in-cannabis]] (intentional, mild) can intensify anthocyanin production.
- UV-B supplementation (reptile lamp) during mid-flower may enhance both color and trichome density.

### Pests and Diseases

Moderate resistance to most common cannabis pathogens. Susceptible to [[cervantes-powdery-mildew-prevention-treatment]] in high-humidity environments due to dense bud structure. [[cervantes-spider-mites-identification-control]] and thrips should be monitored. Dense canopy requires adequate airflow management.

## Availability

Red Velvet is considered a relatively rare, boutique strain. It is more commonly found in licensed dispensaries on the US West Coast (California, Oregon, Washington) and in select markets in Colorado and Oklahoma. Availability fluctuates; it is not typically found as a consistent staple in most markets.
