## Description

Gilgais (also known as melonholes, crabholes, or self-mulching soils) are natural surface depressions or micro-relief features found in vertisolic (expanding clay) soils in arid, semi-arid, and seasonally wet environments. They form through the cyclical wetting and drying of high-clay-content soils, particularly those dominated by smectite clays that undergo significant volume change with moisture content. Gilgais function as natural water-harvesting swales, collecting runoff and supporting distinct vegetation communities within the surrounding arid landscape.

## Classification

- **Category**: Landform / micro-relief feature
- **Soil type association**: Vertisols (cracking clay soils)
- **Formation process**: Pedoturbation (soil self-mixing through wet-dry cycles)
- **Alternative names**: Melonholes, crabholes, self-mulching depressions, natural swales
- **Related features**: Claypans, saltpans, ephemeral wetlands

## Key Facts

- Form from pockets of expanding clays that swell when wet and shrink when dry, creating a pattern of mounds and depressions on the soil surface.
- The depressions collect and hold water after rainfall, creating temporarily inundated micro-wetlands in otherwise arid landscapes.
- Support specialised aquatic and semi-aquatic plant species adapted to hot, somewhat brackish, or salty water conditions.
- Function as natural analogues to human-constructed swales used in [[permaculture-water-harvesting]].
- Can vary in size from a few metres to tens of metres across, with depths ranging from centimetres to over a metre.
- The wetting-drying cycle self-mulches the soil, maintaining a loose, well-aerated surface layer that promotes infiltration.

## Ecological Function

Gilgais create a mosaic of microhabitats within arid landscapes:

- **Aquatic refugia**: During wet periods, gilgais harbour aquatic plants, invertebrates, and amphibians that would otherwise be absent from the surrounding desert.
- **Water filtration**: Plants growing in gilgais remove dissolved salts, nitrogenous compounds, and pollutants from collected water.
- **Seed banks**: The periodically inundated soils maintain seed banks of aquatic and semi-aquatic species that germinate when conditions are favourable.
- **Wildlife habitat**: Provide drinking water and food sources for desert animals, functioning as critical resource patches.

## Associated Species

Mollison notes that gilgai environments support specialised plant communities including:

- Perennial and annual grasses adapted to claypan and swale conditions
- Aquatic plants tolerant of hot, brackish water
- Rushes and sedges that remove dissolved salts and pollutants
- Dense algae capable of desalinating large volumes of water

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

Gilgais represent a natural model for water harvesting in arid permaculture design:

- **Biomimicry in earthworks**: The gilgai form -- a shallow depression in expansive clay that collects and infiltrates runoff -- is directly replicated in permaculture swale design.
- **Expanding clay management**: Understanding gilgai formation helps designers work with, rather than against, vertisolic soils that are often considered problematic in conventional agriculture.
- **Desalination potential**: The aquatic plant communities that naturally establish in gilgais can be deliberately cultivated as biological water treatment systems.
- **Aquaculture integration**: Mollison describes desert gilgais as ideal sites for small aquacultural assemblies and experiments, noting that desert aquatic habitats are naturally isolated -- there is no danger of species escaping to infest permanent streams.
- **Natural water filtration**: The model of gilgais as part-enclosed, part-open natural water filtration systems can be applied to remove faecal, nitrogenous, and metallic pollutants from water supplies.

## Design Applications

Permaculture designers can apply gilgai principles by:

- Replicating the mound-and-depression pattern in swale construction on clay soils
- Selecting plants from natural gilgai communities for rehabilitation of degraded vertisols
- Using gilgai-like depressions as quarantine aquaculture ponds in arid zones
- Incorporating the self-mulching dynamics of vertisols into soil management strategies
