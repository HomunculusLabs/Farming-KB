## Description

Thiobacillus is a genus of Gram-negative, rod-shaped bacteria that play a critical role in the sulphur cycle within soils and aquatic environments. These chemoautotrophic organisms derive energy from the oxidation of reduced sulphur compounds, converting them to sulphates and sulphuric acid. In permaculture and soil science, thiobacilli are significant because they contribute to soil acidification, mineral solubilisation, and the production of gases found in anaerobic soil microsites. Mollison references thiobacillus action as part of the complex biochemical processes operating in waterlogged and anaerobic soils.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Bacteria
- **Phylum:** Pseudomonadota (Proteobacteria)
- **Class:** Betaproteobacteria
- **Order:** Burkholderiales
- **Family:** Thiobacillaceae
- **Genus:** Thiobacillus
- **Metabolism:** Chemoautotrophic (sulphur-oxidising)

## Key Facts

### Sulphur Oxidation
- Thiobacillus species oxidise reduced sulphur compounds including hydrogen sulphide (H2S), elemental sulphur (S), thiosulphate (S2O3), and sulphite (SO3).
- The end product is sulphate (SO4) and sulphuric acid (H2SO4).
- This process releases energy that the bacteria use for carbon fixation and growth.

### Role in Soil Processes
- Thiobacilli are responsible for producing sulphuretted hydrogen (hydrogen sulphide) and sulphur dioxide in anaerobic soils and waters.
- These gases escape to the atmosphere as components of biogas or marsh gas.
- The sulphuric acid produced helps dissolve metallic oxides in soils, contributing to mineral nutrient availability.
- In anaerobic soils and waters, thiobacillus works alongside other processes producing carbon dioxide, methane, and ethylene.

### Habitat
- Found in soils, muds, hot springs, mine drainage waters, and any environment containing reduced sulphur compounds.
- Particularly active in waterlogged soils, hydrophobic clays in winter, and soils where crumb structure has been destroyed.
- Also found in salt-deflocculated collapsed soils and periodically flooded soils.
- Active in the mottled colour zones of hydromorphic soils.

### Agricultural Significance
- Thiobacillus action contributes to the acidification of soils, which in moderate amounts helps solubilise mineral nutrients.
- Excessive thiobacillus activity (e.g., from acid sulphate soils or mine drainage) can produce harmful levels of soil acidity.
- Their gas production (sulphuretted hydrogen, sulphur dioxide) is part of the natural soil atmosphere and contributes to the complex gas exchange processes in soil.
- The gases produced are found alongside humus decay products in anaerobic soil conditions.

### Connection to the Ethylene-Oxygen Cycle
- Thiobacilli are active in the same anaerobic microsites where ethylene production occurs.
- Their metabolic products contribute to the gas mixture found at oxygen-depleted soil sites.
- When oxygen returns to these sites (as part of the ethylene-oxygen cycle), aerobic organisms resume activity while thiobacilli become less dominant.

### Industrial and Environmental Context
- Thiobacillus species are used in biomining (bioleaching of ores).
- They contribute to acid mine drainage, a significant environmental problem.
- T. ferrooxidans also oxidises ferrous iron, linking sulphur and iron cycles in soil.
- Some species can fix atmospheric nitrogen, providing an additional nutrient source in extreme environments.

## Relevance to Cultivation

Thiobacillus bacteria illustrate the extraordinary chemical complexity occurring continuously in soil. While gardeners may never think about these organisms directly, their activity is essential to the sulphur cycle and to maintaining the availability of mineral nutrients. In permaculture systems where soils are kept alive with organic matter and minimal disturbance, thiobacilli function as part of the balanced soil ecosystem. Problems arise only when soils are severely degraded, waterlogged for extended periods, or exposed to acid sulphate conditions. Understanding thiobacillus helps practitioners appreciate why maintaining aerobic soil conditions through good drainage, crumb structure, and mulching is so important for overall soil health.

## Metabolic Pathways

Thiobacillus species oxidize reduced sulfur compounds (elemental sulfur,
thiosulfate, sulfide) to generate energy. T. ferrooxidans also oxidizes
ferrous iron. These chemoautotrophic reactions produce sulfuric acid,
significantly lowering soil pH in their microenvironment.

## Applications in Bioleaching

Thiobacillus bacteria are used industrially for bioleaching of metals from
low-grade ores. In permaculture, their acidifying activity can help reclaim
alkaline soils when managed carefully. Inoculation with Thiobacillus speeds
sulfur oxidation in alkaline soils requiring pH reduction.
