## Description

Bokashi is a Japanese term for "fermented organic matter" — a designer compost that is naturally fortified with macro and micro nutrients (bionutrients) and biodiversed [[beneficial-indigenous-microorganisms]] (BIM). Unlike traditional composting which relies on aerobic decomposition, bokashi uses **[[jadam-anaerobic-fermentation]]** to break down organic materials, resulting in a potent, microbially-rich soil amendment.

## Classification

- **Domain**: Agricultural inputs / Composting method

- **Type**: Fermented organic fertilizer

## Key Facts

- Bokashi is a **designer compost** — its formulation can be adjusted based on specific nutritional goals (e.g., higher potassium for flowering, higher nitrogen for vegetative growth).
- The basic formulation follows a **80% carbon / 17% nitrogen / 3% trace elements** ratio, mirroring the general animal feed formulation of 80% carbohydrate / 17% protein / 3% vitamin-mineral.
- Charcoal dust is included as a substrate because it provides an ideal [[query-how-do-i-design-habitat-for-beneficial-insects]] microorganism proliferation.
- Molasses serves as a food source for the [[forest-and-bamboo-beneficial-microorganisms]], boosting their population during fermentation.
- The finished product can also be used to make **compost tea** for foliar application.

## Preparation Principles

1. **Carbon source** (80%): Provides the bulk substrate and energy for microbial activity. Grain brans, peat, and sawdust serve this role.
2. **Nitrogen source** (17%): Manure and protein-rich meals fuel microbial protein synthesis and generate nitrogen-rich compost.
3. **Trace elements** (3%): Charcoal dust, rock dusts, and targeted bionutrient additions supply micronutrients.
4. **Microbial inoculant**: BIM provides the biodiversed beneficial microorganism population — the essential "workforce" of the bokashi.
5. **Microbial food**: Molasses feeds the microorganisms during the fermentation process.

## Customization

The recipe can be adjusted based on goals:
- **Higher potassium**: Emphasize bionutrients with fermented extracts high in potassium (banana, seaweed)
- **Higher nitrogen**: Emphasize fish emulsion or leguminous plant extracts in the bionutrient component
- **Rock dusts** can be added and fermented to increase mineral content

## Relevance to Cultivation

Bokashi represents a **farmer-empowering technology** that allows small-scale producers to create high-quality, microbially-rich compost from locally available materials without purchasing commercial inputs. The fermentation process preserves more nutrients than traditional hot composting and introduces a diverse population of beneficial microorganisms directly into the soil.

For cultivation specifically, bokashi provides:
- Immediate soil fertility improvement
- Beneficial microbial inoculation
- Improved soil structure and water retention
- Slow-release nutrition through microbial decomposition
- A vehicle for targeted nutrient delivery via bionutrient additions
