## Description

Vermicompost (also called worm castings or vermicast) is the product of organic matter decomposition processed through the digestive systems of earthworms. Unlike thermal composting, vermicomposting relies on worms and the bacteria inside them to break down materials at ambient temperatures, without the heat cycles of traditional composting. The resulting material is a nutrient-rich, microbially active soil amendment that is almost always **bacterially dominated**, making it particularly suited for vegetables, annuals, and other plants that prefer bacterial nitrogen cycling.

## Classification

- **Type**: Organic soil amendment
- **Category**: Compost derivative
- **Process**: Mesophilic bio-oxidative decomposition via earthworm digestion
- **Dominant biology**: Bacterial (with polysaccharide, carbohydrate, and protein coatings on castings)

## Key Facts

### Production Process
- Organic materials (food wastes, paper, cardboard, leaves, grass clippings) are fed to earthworms in a contained bin or system.
- **No heat is involved** — temperatures must remain within worm survival range (approximately 55–77°F / 13–25°C), as excessive heat kills the worms.
- Worms digest materials through their gut, where **symbiotic bacteria** perform the actual biochemical decomposition.
- Output consists of **worm castings** — coated with polysaccharides, carbohydrates, and simple proteins that support robust bacterial populations.

### Starting Materials
- **Recommended**: Food wastes (excluding fats and meats), paper, cardboard, leaves, green grass
- **Brown materials** must be shredded or broken up for worms to ingest efficiently.
- **Weed-containing materials** should be thermally composted first to destroy seeds before adding to worm bins.
- Materials should include some **microarthropods** to assist with physical breakdown before worm ingestion.
- Outdoor bins encourage natural arthropod and insect colonization.

### Bacterial Dominance
- Vermicompost is inherently **bacterially dominated** — fungi are minimally involved in worm digestion.
- Castings are coated with bacterial-supporting compounds (polysaccharides, carbohydrates, proteins).
- This makes vermicompost ideal for plants preferring bacterially-dominated soils: vegetables, annual flowers, and lawns.
- Not the preferred compost type for trees, shrubs, and perennials that benefit from fungal dominance.

### System Setup
- Can use **commercially available earthworms** (e.g., *[[eisenia-fetida]]* — red wigglers) specifically bred for vermicomposting.
- Bins range from simple wooden or plastic boxes to multi-tier stacking systems.
- Moisture management is critical — bins should be moist but not waterlogged.
- Aeration is important to maintain aerobic conditions and prevent odors.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

1. **Soil inoculation**: Vermicompost introduces high populations of beneficial bacteria to soils, establishing bacterial food webs that support vegetable and annual plant growth.
2. **Nutrient density**: Castings contain concentrated, plant-available nutrients including nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, calcium, and micronutrients in biologically available forms.
3. **Plant growth stimulation**: Contains plant growth hormones (auxins, cytokinins, gibberellins) produced during worm digestion that directly stimulate root development and plant vigor.
4. **Disease suppression**: Beneficial bacterial populations in vermicompost can outcompete soil-borne pathogens through competitive exclusion and production of antifungal compounds.
5. **Mycology applications**: While bacterially dominated (less ideal for fungal crops), vermicompost extracts can be used as bacterial supplements in [[substrate-preparation]] or as foliar sprays.
6. **Small-scale production**: Feasible for indoor or small-space production, making it accessible for home gardeners and small-scale cultivators.

### Comparison with Thermal Compost

| Feature | Vermicompost | [[thermal-compost]] |
|---------|-------------|-----------------|
| Temperature | Ambient (no heat) | 40–65°C thermophilic |
| Time | 2–6 months | 2 weeks – 1 year |
| Dominance | Bacterial | Fungal, bacterial, or balanced |
| Pathogen kill | Minimal | Excellent (thermophilic) |
| Scale | Small bins | Large piles |
| Weed seed kill | None | Yes (at 150°F/65°C) |

## Environmental Considerations

- **Temperature**: 55–77°F (13–25°C) — must not overheat
- **Moisture**: 70–80% (similar to wrung-out sponge)
- **pH**: Tends toward neutral (6.5–7.5)
- **Oxygen**: Aerobic conditions required; avoid waterlogging
