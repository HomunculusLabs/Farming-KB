## Stropharia caerulea

**Stropharia caerulea** is a saprotrophic basidiomycete and cord-forming fungus.

It has served as an important model organism for studies of [[coprinus-picaceus]]*, and *[[comparison-stinging-nettle-vs-horsetail]]) rhizomes and other small woody and non-woody litter.

This preference for nutrient-enriched substrates is consistent with its short-range foraging strategy.

These resources tend to be relatively abundant and homogeneously distributed rather than large and sparse.

## Temporal Changes in Mycelial Pattern

Mycelial pattern characteristics, including fractal dimension, change with time in S. caerulea as in other cord-forming fungi.

Systems tend to become more open (lower fractal dimension) over time.

Even mycelia that were initially surface fractal for several months become increasingly mass fractal in the long term.

Systems become more open as individual hyphae and minor cords regress, leaving a persistent network of thick cords behind the foraging margin.

Regressing mycelium allows redistribution of biomass and nutrients for further exploration by foraging fronts.

Radiotracer studies have demonstrated that nutrients are scavenged at foraging fronts and translocated from the resource base to support growth fronts.

## Response to Environmental Factors

[[brassinosteroid-psilocybe-cubensis-mycelial-growth-research]] (extension, biomass, D) is affected by many environmental variables.

These include:

- The nutrient status of the inoculum
- Nutrient status of newly encountered resources
- Nutrient status and composition of the soil
- Temperature, gaseous regime, water potential, and pH

Most species examined, although not S. caerulea, produce mycelia with greater space-filling from larger inocula.

S. caerulea mycelium produces greater space-filling from resources with a higher nutrient concentration.

Higher D values were often correlated with higher extension rates and hyphal coverage.

S. caerulea is responsive to changes in the environment and responds to elevated soil nutrient status by increasing branching, which allows increased uptake of nutrients.
