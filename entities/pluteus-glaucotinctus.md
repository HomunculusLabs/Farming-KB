# Pluteus glaucotinctus

**Pluteus glaucotinctus** is a wood-inhabiting mushroom in the family
Pluteaceae known to contain [[comparison-spore-print-collection-vs-spore-syringe-production]], and lack of both a volva and a persistent annulus.
P. glaucotinctus is placed in the section Celluloderma, which contains species
with a cellular (celluloderm) cap cuticle composed of globose to ellipsoid
cells rather than filamentous hyphae. This section includes several other
psilocybin-containing species, suggesting phylogenetic clustering of
psychoactive properties within the genus.

## Morphology

### Cap (Pileus)

The cap ranges from 15 to 50 mm in diameter. It is convex when young, becoming
broadly convex to plano-convex or slightly umbonate with maturity. The cap
surface exhibits a grayish to bluish-green (glaucous) tint most apparent when
young and moist, giving the species its specific epithet. This coloration is
particularly noticeable at the center and may fade to a muted grayish-brown
as the specimen dries. The surface is smooth to slightly silky-fibrillose,
sometimes with subtle radial fibril arrangement. The margin may be slightly
translucent-striate when moist.

### Gills (Lamellae)

The gills are free from the stipe, the defining characteristic of the genus
Pluteus. They are moderately crowded, white when young, developing a
pinkish to salmon color as spores mature. The gill edges are typically white
and finely fringed (fimbriate). The gills may show slight bluish
discoloration when bruised, though this reaction is often subtle and
inconsistent compared to species of the genus Psilocybe.

### Stipe (Stem)

The stipe is central, cylindrical, and equal or slightly tapering toward the
base, ranging from 25 to 70 mm in length and 2 to 5 mm in thickness. It is
white to pale gray, sometimes with a bluish or glaucous tint especially when
young and fresh. The surface is smooth to silky-fibrillose and may develop
subtle longitudinal striations with age. The stipe is firm and stuffed when
young, becoming hollow with maturity. It lacks both an annulus and a volva.

### Spores and Microscopic Features

Basidiospores are broadly ellipsoid to subglobose, smooth, and thin-walled,
measuring approximately 6 to 8 um by 5 to 7 um. Spore print color is pink to
salmon. Cheilocystidia are abundant, ranging from fusiform to lageniform
(flask-shaped), often with a distinctive capitate apex. Pleurocystidia are
also present, usually larger and more prominent. The pileipellis is a cellular
type (celluloderm) composed of globose to broadly ellipsoid cells measuring 15
to 40 um in diameter, arranged in a single or [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]]. It is saprotrophic and
lignicolous, growing on decaying hardwood logs, stumps, and fallen branches.
It has been documented on oak, beech, ash, and willow. The species shows a
preference for well-decayed wood. Fruiting typically occurs from late spring
through autumn during warm, wet weather. It is solitary to scattered and
considered uncommon to rare in most of its range.
