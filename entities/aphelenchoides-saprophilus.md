## Description

**Aphelenchoides saprophilus** is a species of fungivorous (mycophagous) nematode found in soil ecosystems. It feeds on a wide range of fungal species, including both saprotrophic and ectomycorrhizal fungi. This nematode has been extensively studied as a model organism for understanding fungal-feeding preferences and the impacts of soil fauna on fungal community dynamics.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Eukaryota
- **Kingdom:** Animalia
- **Phylum:** Nematoda
- **Class:** Secernentea
- **Order:** Aphelenchida
- **Family:** Aphelenchoididae
- **Genus:** *Aphelenchoides*
- **Species:** *A. saprophilus*

## Key Facts

### Feeding Preferences

Research by Ruess and Dighton (1996) demonstrated that *A. saprophilus* shows strong preferences among fungal food sources. When offered seven ectomycorrhizal and one ericoid fungal species, significant differences in final nematode populations were found:

- Highest population on *Laccaria laccata* (2.4 x 10^5) with 11.4% mature females
- High population on *Hymenoscyphus ericae* (2.0 x 10^5) with 6.8% females
- Moderate on *Cenococcum geophilum* (1.5 x 10^5) and *Lactarius rufus* (1.4 x 10^5)
- Low on *Amanita muscaria* (0.7 x 10^5) and *A. rubescens* (0.6 x 10^5)
- Could not maintain sustainable populations on *Paxillus involutus* alone

### Population Dynamics

- Produced the highest populations when feeding on *Laccaria laccata* and ericoid mycorrhizal *Hymenoscyphus ericae*
- Could not maintain sustainable populations on a diet of *Paxillus involutus* alone
- In mixed nematode communities, *A. saprophilus* dominated when feeding on preferred fungi (up to 100% of the community)
- The proportion of females in the population varies significantly depending on fungal food source

### Ecological Interactions

- Feeds on both ectomycorrhizal and saprotrophic fungi from various higher taxa
- Food preference shifts over time, possibly as a strategy to avoid accumulation of toxic fungal secondary metabolites
- Grazing pressure can potentially reduce mycorrhizal colonization of plant roots
- Part of complex soil food webs involving predation by mites and other microarthropods

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

### Impact on Mycorrhizal Function

The feeding activities of *A. saprophilus* on mycorrhizal fungi have implications for plant-mycorrhizal symbiosis:

1. **Grazing pressure:** Intensive feeding on extraradical hyphae can sever mycelial connections between host roots and soil
2. **Species selection:** Preferential feeding on certain mycorrhizal species may alter fungal community composition
3. **Nutrient mineralization:** Nematode grazing stimulates nutrient turnover by consuming and processing fungal biomass
4. **Population regulation:** Predator-prey dynamics with mites and other soil fauna regulate nematode populations

### Soil Food Web Dynamics

*A. saprophilus* plays a critical role in soil food webs:

- Serves as a trophic link between fungal primary production and higher trophic levels
- Grazing stimulates fungal activity through compensatory growth responses
- Nematode excretion releases mineralized nutrients (N, P) back into the soil
- Interacts with bacterial-feeding nematodes and their predators

### Research Applications

This species is valuable for laboratory studies because:
- Can be maintained in pure culture on fungal hosts
- Shows clear, measurable feeding preferences
- Responds rapidly to changes in food quality
- Represents an important functional group in decomposition and nutrient cycling studies
