It functions primarily as an NMDA receptor antagonist, producing dissociative, analgesic, and hallucinogenic effects similar to ketamine but with distinct pharmacokinetic and subjective properties.

DCK emerged as a designer drug / research chemical in the mid-2010s and has been subject to legal controls in numerous jurisdictions.

## Chemical Properties

- **IUPAC name:** 2-(Methylamino)-2-phenylcyclohexan-1-one
- **Molecular formula:** C₁₃H₁₇NO
- **Molecular weight:** 203.28 g/mol
- **CAS number:** 4681-68-9 (parent compound context)
- **Chemical class:** Arylcyclohexylamine
- **Structure:** Cyclohexanone ring substituted at the 2-position with a phenyl group and a methylamino group; lacks the 2-chloro phenyl substituent present in ketamine
- **Appearance:** White crystalline powder
- **Solubility:** Soluble in water, ethanol, and most organic solvents

### Structural Relationship to Ketamine

The only structural difference between DCK and ketamine is the absence of the chlorine atom at the 2-position of the phenyl ring.

This modification significantly alters binding affinity, duration, and metabolic fate while retaining the core arylcyclohexylamine pharmacophore responsible for NMDA antagonism.
