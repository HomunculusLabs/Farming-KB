# Goumi (Elaeagnus multiflora)

Goumi is a deciduous to semi-evergreen shrub in the family Elaeagnaceae,
native to East Asia, cultivated for its edible, antioxidant-rich fruit
and its nitrogen-fixing root symbiosis with Frankia actinorhizal
bacteria. The species is increasingly valued in permaculture and
agroforestry as a multi-functional plant providing food, wildlife
habitat, soil fertility, and erosion control. Goumi fruits are small
reddish-orange drupes with a tart-sweet flavor and high lycopene content
rivaling tomatoes, making them among the richest temperate-zone sources
of this antioxidant.

The species was introduced to Europe and North America in the 19th
century. In permaculture design, goumi is a "stacked function" plant
serving as food crop, nitrogen fixer, pollinator support, wildlife
habitat, and erosion control. Its ability to thrive in poor soils makes
it valuable for establishing food forests on degraded land.

## Taxonomy and Classification

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Phylum:** Angiosperms
- **Class:** Eudicots
- **Order:** Rosales
- **Family:** Elaeagnaceae
- **Genus:** Elaeagnus
- **Species:** E. multiflora

The genus Elaeagnus contains approximately 50-70 species. E. multiflora
is closely related to autumn olive (E. umbellata), silverberry (E.
commutata), and Russian olive (E. angustifolia). Goumi is smaller and
less invasive than autumn olive with superior fruit. The epithet
"multiflora" refers to the abundant spring flowers.

## Distribution and Habitat

Goumi is native to China, Korea, and Japan. It grows in forest margins,
riparian thickets, hillsides, and disturbed areas at 500-2000 m
elevation, in well-drained loamy to sandy soils (pH 5.5-7.5). In North
America it is cultivated in zones 4-8 but is not invasive, unlike
autumn olive. This makes it the preferred Elaeagnus for permaculture.

## Nitrogen Fixation

Goumi forms a symbiotic relationship with Frankia actinorhizal bacteria,
which colonize root nodules and convert atmospheric N2 to ammonia. Key
aspects include:
- **Fixation rates:** 15-40 kg N/ha/year depending on plant age and
  soil conditions
- **Nodules:** Coral-like structures on lateral roots, appearing in
  year two
- **Associative benefit:** Fixed nitrogen spreads to neighbors via
  root exudation, leaf litter, and mycorrhizal transfer
- **Frankia compatibility:** Strains are broadly compatible across the
  genus

This capacity makes goumi an excellent pioneer for productive
polycultures on nitrogen-poor soils.

## Nutritional Profile of Fruit

| Nutrient | Value (per 100 g fresh) | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| Energy | ~50-70 kcal | Low-calorie fruit |
| Lycopene | 15-50 mg | Exceptionally high; rivals tomatoes |
| Vitamin C | 15-30 mg | 25-50% daily value |
| Vitamin A | 200-400 IU | Beta-carotene contribution |
| Potassium | 200-300 mg | Important electrolyte |
| Calcium | 15-25 mg | Moderate mineral content |
| Iron | 0.5-1.0 mg | Enhanced by vitamin C |
| Fiber | 2-4 g | Digestive health |
| Sugar | 8-12 g | Fructose and glucose dominant |

The lycopene content is the most notable feature, associated with
reduced risk of cardiovascular disease, prostate cancer, and macular
degeneration. Bioavailability is enhanced by dietary lipids.

## Cultivation

Goumi is low-maintenance and well-suited to home gardens:
- **Propagation:** Softwood/hardwood cuttings (70-90% success); seed
  requires 60-90 days cold stratification
- **Planting:** Space 1.5-2.5 m apart; fruits best in full sun
- **Soil:** Highly adaptable; tolerates poor, sandy, and clay soils
- **Watering:** Drought-tolerant after 1-2 years
- **Pruning:** Minimal; remove dead branches in late winter. Fruit on
  previous year's wood.
- **Pests:** Generally pest-free; birds are the primary challenge
- **Yield:** 5-10 kg/plant annually at maturity (2-3 years to fruit)
- **Pollination:** Self-fertile; a single plant produces fruit
