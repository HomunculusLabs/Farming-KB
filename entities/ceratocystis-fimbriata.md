# Ceratocystis fimbriata

**Ceratocystis fimbriata** is a morphological species of ascomycete fungus that has proven to be a complex of multiple genetically and reproductively isolated species.

Originally described as a single entity, modern phylogenetic analysis has revealed it to harbour several cryptic species with distinct host specializations.

It is a landmark example of the power of molecular systematics in [[molecular-methods-fungal-taxonomy-and-diversity]].

## Taxonomy and Classification

C. fimbriata belongs to the order Microascales within the class Sordariomycetes.

The genus *Ceratocystis* includes many economically important plant pathogens.

Key taxonomic details include:

- **Phylum:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Sordariomycetes
- **Order:** Microascales
- **Family:** Ceratocystidaceae

The genus is characterized by the production of perithecia with long necks and hat-shaped ascospores.

## Species Complex

The morphological species C. fimbriata was shown by Engelbrecht and Harrington (2005) to harbour several genetically and reproductively isolated groups.

These represent multiple phylogenetic and biological species.

This discovery followed the pattern seen in other fungal species complexes such as histoplasma-capsulatum and coccidioides-immitis, where phylogenetic analysis revealed previously unrecognized species boundaries.

The recognition of cryptic species within C. fimbriata has important implications for plant quarantine regulations and [[plant-disease-management-solomon]].

## Host Specialization

Once phylogenetic groups within C. fimbriata were identified, phenotypic differences were found for each group.

The primary distinguishing phenotype is the preferred host:

- **[[comparison-cassava-vs-taro-vs-sweet-potato-vs-yam]]** associated clade
- **Sycamore** associated clade, described as the new species *C. platani*
- **Cacao** associated clade, described as the new species *C. cacaofunesta*

Additional phenotypic differences include the size and shape of meiosporic and mitosporic reproductive structures and spores.

Interestingly, *C. cacaofunesta* encompasses two genetically and reproductively isolated groups.

These show some morphological differences but are maintained as a single species because they both infest the same agricultural host (cacao) and the morphological differences were deemed trivial.

When more is known about the natural hosts and additional phenotypes of *C. cacaofunesta*, it seems likely that another species will be recognized.

## Geographic Distribution

C. fimbriata and its segregate species show correlations with geography.

A deep divergence in combined gene genealogies was consistent with a division between individuals collected in the Northern and Southern Hemispheres.

This pattern is similar to that seen in [[botrytis-cinerea]], [[magnaporthe-grisea]].

However, strong geographic correlation (endemism) is not always found.
