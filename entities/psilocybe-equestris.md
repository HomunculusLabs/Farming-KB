# Psilocybe equestris

Psilocybe equestris is a bluing psilocybin-containing mushroom in the
section Semilanceatae of the genus Psilocybe. It was described from
European collections and is closely related to the well-known liberty
cap, Psilocybe semilanceata. The species is relatively uncommon in the
mycological literature and remains poorly understood compared to its
more famous congeners. It produces psilocybin and psilocin as its
primary psychoactive constituents and bruises blue when damaged, a
hallmark of psilocybin-containing species.

## Taxonomy and Naming

- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Hymenogastraceae
- **Genus:** Psilocybe
- **Section:** Semilanceatae
- **Authority:** (Friedrich) Noordel.
- **Synonyms:** Psilocybe equestris var. equestris
- **Etymology:** The specific epithet "equestris" derives from Latin
  meaning "of or pertaining to horses," though the reason for this
  naming is not well documented in the original description.

The species was originally described under a different genus before
being transferred to Psilocybe. It shares the Semilanceatae section
with P. semilanceata, P. pelliculosa, P. sierrae, and P. strictipes,
all of which share morphological similarities including conic to
subconic pileus shapes and a tendency to grow in grassland habitats.

## Description

### Cap (Pileus)
The cap ranges from 5 to 25 mm in diameter, conic to subconic when
young, becoming broadly convex to umbonate with age. The surface is
hygrophanous, appearing chestnut-brown to olive-brown when moist and
paling to straw-yellow or cream as it dries. A distinct translucent
striate margin is often visible when wet, extending about one-third
to one-half the radius. The cap surface is smooth to slightly viscid
when moist, with a separable pellicle.

### Gills (Lamellae)
Gills are adnate to adnexed, moderately close to subdistant, with
2-3 tiers of lamellulae. Color is initially grayish-brown becoming
dark purple-brown to nearly black with spore maturity. Gill edges
are lighter and fringed, sometimes becoming mottled with white.

### Stipe (Stem)
The stipe is 40 to 90 mm long and 1 to 2.5 mm thick, equal to
slightly enlarged at the base. It is fibrous, hollow, and pale
yellowish to whitish, darkening with age or where handled. The
surface is smooth to finely pruinose near the apex. No annulus or
partial veil is present, though a faint cortina may be observed in
very young specimens. The stipe base often has thin, whitish
mycelial strands attached.

### Microscopic Features

| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| Spore print | Dark purple-brown to nearly black |
| Spores | Ellipsoidal to subellipsoidal, 10-14 x 6-8 µm |
| Spore wall | Thick-walled, smooth, with a distinct germ pore |
| Basidia | 2- and 4-spored, clavate, 14-20 x 4-6 µm |
| Cheilocystidia | Ventricose to lageniform, 15-25 x 4-7 µm |
| Pleurocystidia | Similar to cheilocystidia or absent |
| Pileipellis | Cutis with embedded hyphae, hyaline |

## Distribution and Habitat

- **Geographic range:** Europe, primarily northern and central
  regions. Reported from Scandinavia, the British Isles, and
  continental Europe. Exact range is poorly documented due to
  historical confusion with P. semilanceata.
- **Habitat:** Grasslands, pastures, meadows, and lawns. Often
  found in nutrient-poor, acidic soils typical of upland pastures.
- **Substrate:** Terrestrial, growing directly from soil among
  grasses. Not associated with dung or woody debris.
- **Season:** Late summer to autumn, typically fruiting from August
  through November depending on latitude and altitude.
- **Ecology:** Saprotrophic, decomposing grass and plant litter in
  the soil profile. May have weakly ectomycorrhizal associations.

## Chemistry and Active Compounds
- **Psilocybin:** Present in moderate concentrations. Exact values
  are poorly documented due to limited analytical studies on
  confirmed specimens. Estimated at 0.2-0.6% dry weight based on
  related Semilanceatae section members.
- **Psilocin:** Present as a degradation product of psilocybin and
  in trace amounts in fresh tissue.
- **Baeocystin:** Likely present in trace amounts, consistent with
  the pattern observed across the Semilanceatae section.
- **Norbaeocystin:** May be present in trace quantities.
- **Aeruginascin:** Not reported; this compound is more typical of
  P. cyanescens and closely related species.
Bluing reaction is moderate upon bruising of the stipe base and
gills, intensifying over several minutes after handling. The bluing
is less dramatic than in P. cyanescens or P. azurescens.

The most common confusion is with P. semilanceata, from which P.
equestris is differentiated by subtle morphological differences
including cap shape, stipe proportions, and microscopic characters.
Accurate identification often requires microscopic examination.
## Field Identification Tips
- Conic to subconic cap with hygrophanous coloration that fades
  from brown to pale yellow as it dries
- Translucent striate margin when wet
- Absence of an annulus or veil remnants on the stipe
- Moderate blue bruising, most evident at the stipe base
- Dark purple-brown spore print
- Found in grassland habitats, not on dung or wood
- Compare carefully with P. semilanceata — P. equestris tends to
  be smaller overall with a less pronounced umbonate peak
## Conservation Status
Psilocybe equestris has not been formally assessed by the IUCN. Like
many grassland fungi, it may be threatened by agricultural
intensification, habitat loss, and the use of fungicides. Its
restricted and poorly documented distribution makes population
assessment difficult. Collection for psychoactive use poses an
additional potential threat in areas where it occurs.
## Legal Status
Psilocybin and psilocin are Schedule I controlled substances in many
jurisdictions, including the United States under the Controlled
Substances Act. The species is therefore illegal to possess, cultivate,
or distribute in most countries. Legal status varies by jurisdiction;
some countries and municipalities have decriminalized or deprioritized
enforcement for personal possession of psilocybin mushrooms.
