# Psilocybe equestris

Psilocybe equestris is a bluing [[psilocybin]]-containing mushroom in the
section Semilanceatae of the genus Psilocybe. It was described from
European collections and is closely related to the well-known liberty
cap, [[hplc-analysis-of-psilocybin-and-psilocin]] as its
primary psychoactive constituents and bruises blue when damaged, a
hallmark of psilocybin-containing species.

## Taxonomy and Naming

- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Hymenogastraceae
- **Genus:** Psilocybe
- **Section:** Semilanceatae
- **Authority:** (Friedrich) Noordel.
- **Synonyms:** Psilocybe equestris var. equestris
- **Etymology:** The specific epithet "equestris" derives from Latin
  meaning "of or pertaining to horses," though the reason for this
  naming is not well documented in the original description.

The species was originally described under a different genus before
being transferred to Psilocybe. It shares the Semilanceatae section
with P. semilanceata, P. pelliculosa, P. sierrae, and P. strictipes,
all of which share morphological similarities including conic to
subconic pileus shapes and a tendency to grow in grassland habitats.

## Description

### Cap (Pileus)
The cap ranges from 5 to 25 mm in diameter, conic to subconic when
young, becoming broadly convex to umbonate with age. The surface is
hygrophanous, appearing chestnut-brown to olive-brown when moist and
paling to straw-yellow or cream as it dries. A distinct translucent
striate margin is often visible when wet, extending about one-third
to one-half the radius. The cap surface is smooth to slightly viscid
when moist, with a separable pellicle.

### Gills (Lamellae)
Gills are adnate to adnexed, moderately close to subdistant, with
2-3 tiers of lamellulae. Color is initially grayish-brown becoming
dark purple-brown to nearly black with [[spore]] maturity. Gill edges
are lighter and fringed, sometimes becoming mottled with white.

### Stipe (Stem)
The stipe is 40 to 90 mm long and 1 to 2.5 mm thick, equal to
slightly enlarged at the base. It is fibrous, hollow, and pale
yellowish to whitish, darkening with age or where handled. The
surface is smooth to finely pruinose near the apex. No annulus or

## Distribution and Habitat

- **Geographic range:** Europe, primarily northern and central
  regions. Reported from Scandinavia, the British Isles, and
  continental Europe. Exact range is poorly documented due to
  historical confusion with P. semilanceata.
- **Habitat:** Grasslands, pastures, meadows, and lawns. Often
  found in nutrient-poor, acidic soils typical of upland pastures.
- **Substrate:** Terrestrial, growing directly from soil among
  grasses. Not associated with dung or [[fungal-succession-woody-debris-ecology]].
- **Season:** Late summer to autumn, typically fruiting from August
  through November depending on latitude and altitude.
