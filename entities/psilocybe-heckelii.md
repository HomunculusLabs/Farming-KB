P. heckelii is closely allied with the **Psilocybe mexicana** species complex. It produces moderate levels of [[traditional-indigenous-use-sacred-fungi]] ceremonies in several Mesoamerican cultural contexts. The species is less well-characterized biochemically than its close relatives P. mexicana and P. caerulescens.

## Taxonomy

**Kingdom:** Fungi — **Phylum:** Basidiomycota — **Class:** Agaricomycetes

**Order:** Agaricales — **Family:** Hymenogastraceae — **Genus:** Psilocybe

**Species:** P. heckelii Guzmán

The species was formally described by Gastón Guzmán in 1976 based on specimens collected from the **Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta** in Colombia. The epithet honors the French mycologist **René Maire** (the original collector of related material). Some authorities have suggested P. heckelii may represent a tropical variant or subspecies of P. mexicana, but morphological and distributional differences support its status as a distinct species.

**Synonyms:** None currently accepted. Historically confused with P. mexicana in field identifications.

## Morphology

**Cap:** 8–25 mm diameter, conic to campanulate when young, expanding to broadly convex or plane with age. Surface is hygrophanous, brownish-orange to reddish-cinnamon when moist, fading to straw-yellow or pale ochre upon drying. Surface is smooth to slightly translucent-striate at the margin.

**Gills:** Adnexed to adnate, close to subdistant, grayish-brown becoming dark purplish-brown with spore maturity. Gill edges are fringed and whitish.

**Stipe:** 30–70 mm long, 1.5–3 mm thick, equal to slightly enlarged at the base. Color is pallid to yellowish, bruising distinctly blue to blue-green when handled. Surface is smooth, dry, with a persistent partial veil forming a thin annular zone.

**Spores:** Ellipsoid to somewhat rhomboid in face view, 8–11 × 5–7 μm, thick-walled with a distinct germ pore. Spore print is dark purple-brown.

**Taste and odor:** Farinaceous (mealy, flour-like), characteristic of the P. mexicana group.

## Distribution and Habitat

P. heckelii occurs in humid tropical and subtropical montane forests between 800–2,000 m elevation. It has been confirmed from **Mexico** (Chiapas, Oaxaca), **Guatemala**, **Costa Rica**, **Panama**, **Colombia**, and **Ecuador**.

The species is primarily a **dung-inhabiting** (coprophilous) fungus, fruiting on the dung of herbivorous mammals including cattle, horses, and deer. It also occurs on enriched soils and grasslands adjacent to forest margins.

Fruiting occurs during the rainy season, typically from May through October depending on latitude. Solitary to gregarious in habit.

## Chemistry and Pharmacology

The principal psychoactive constituents of P. heckelii are:

- **Psilocybin**: 0.25–0.55% dry weight (moderate potency)
- **Psilocin**: 0.02–0.10% dry weight
- **Baeocystin**: present in trace amounts
- **Norbaeocystin**: detected in some collections

Optimal [[growing-gourmet-fruiting-parameters]] include temperatures of 22–26°C and high relative humidity (>90%). The species is somewhat slower to colonize than P. cubensis, with full substrate colonization requiring 3–4 weeks from inoculation.

 sclerotium formation (the production of hard, underground "truffle-like" masses known as "magic truffles") has been reported for P. heckelii, as it has for other members of the P. mexicana group. Sclerotia contain lower concentrations of psilocybin than fruiting bodies but are easier to cultivate in vitro.
