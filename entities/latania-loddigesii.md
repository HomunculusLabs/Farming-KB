Leaves are palmate (fan-shaped), costapalmate, and 2–3 meters in diameter. The leaf blade is divided into 60–80 rigid, lanceolate segments, each 60–100 centimeters long. The upper leaf surface is covered with a distinctive waxy blue-gray indumentum, while the lower surface is silvery-white.

The petiole is stout, 1–1.5 meters long, armed with sharp, curved teeth along the margins, and is covered in a dense brown tomentum. The leaf base splits longitudinally, forming a fibrous sheath around the trunk.

Inflorescences are interfoliar, producing large, pendulous spikes up to 1 meter in length. The species is dioecious, with separate male and female plants. Male inflorescences are branched and produce copious pollen, while female inflorescences bear large, brown, ovoid fruits approximately 5 centimeters in diameter.

Female plants produce clusters of 10–30 fruits per inflorescence. The fruit has a thick, fibrous husk surrounding a hard, bony endocarp that contains one to three seeds. Germination is hypogeal, with the first leaf emerging below ground.

## Distribution and Habitat

Latania loddigesii is endemic to Réunion Island, a volcanic island approximately 800 kilometers east of Madagascar in the western Indian Ocean. Réunion is the largest of the Mascarene Islands, with an area of approximately 2,512 square kilometers.

The species historically occurred in lowland dry forests and coastal woodlands on the western and southern sides of Réunion. Today, wild populations are restricted to a handful of fragmented sites, primarily along the southwestern coast between Saint-Leu and Saint-Pierre.

Native elevational range is from sea level to approximately 500 meters, though most remaining individuals occur below 200 meters. The palm grows on well-drained basaltic and coralline soils in areas receiving 800–1500 millimeters of annual rainfall.

Réunion's original lowland dry forest has been almost entirely eliminated by agriculture, urbanization, and invasive species. The palm now survives mainly in steep, inaccessible ravines and on coastal cliffs where land conversion was impractical.

The climate on Réunion is tropical maritime, influenced by the southeast trade winds. A wet season from November to April brings heavy rainfall, while the dry season from May to October is cooler and relatively arid. The blue latan palm is adapted to both seasonal extremes.

## Cultivation

Latania loddigesii is highly valued in ornamental horticulture for its large, silvery-blue fan leaves and imposing stature. It serves as a dramatic specimen tree in tropical and subtropical landscapes, public gardens, and resort properties.

The species thrives in full sun on well-drained soils and is tolerant of coastal conditions, including salt spray and wind. [[query-how-do-i-prune-brambles-for-maximum-fruit-production]].

Seedling growth is moderately fast compared to many other palm species, particularly under warm, humid conditions. Young plants produce blue-tinted foliage from an early age, making them attractive even before trunk development begins.

Established plants are relatively drought-tolerant but benefit from regular irrigation during dry periods. Fertilization with a balanced palm fertilizer containing micronutrients supports vigorous growth and maintains the intense blue leaf coloration.

## Ecological Role

Latania loddigesii is a keystone species of the original lowland dry [[psidium-cattleianum]] (strawberry guava) and Rubus alceifolius, aggressively colonize disturbed areas and suppress native regeneration.

Conservation efforts by the Réunion National Park and local botanical institutions include habitat protection, invasive species removal, and ex situ cultivation. The species is well-represented in botanical gardens worldwide, providing an important genetic safeguard.
