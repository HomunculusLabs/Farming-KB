# Psilocybe tasmaniana

**Psilocybe tasmaniana** is a [[psilocybin]]-containing mushroom species native to the island state of Tasmania, Australia. It is one of the less well-documented psychoactive fungi in the Australasian region, though it has been confirmed to produce the indole alkaloids psilocybin and psilocin.

## Taxonomy and Classification

*Psilocybe tasmaniana* belongs to the family Hymenogastraceae, within the order Agaricales. It was identified as part of the broader mycoflora survey work conducted in Tasmania during the mid-20th century, when Australian mycologists began cataloguing the continent's psychoactive fungi. The species epithet refers to its Tasmanian provenance.
It is one of several psychoactive mushroom species endemic to or strongly associated with the island state.

The species was documented by Dr. Malcolm Hall and other Australian mycologists who surveyed the psychoactive mushroom species occurring in Tasmania and the Australian mainland. It appears in comprehensive field guides to Australian and New Zealand psychoactive fungi.

## Morphology

*Psilocybe tasmaniana* is a small to medium agaric mushroom. Like many Psilocybe species, it features a hygrophanous pileus (cap) that changes color as it dries, typically brownish when moist and lighter when desiccated. The cap is 10–30 mm in diameter, convex to plano-convex in maturity, sometimes with a slight umbo. The cap surface is smooth and slightly viscid when moist, becoming dry and dull as it dries. Gills are adnate to adnexed, darkening from pale gray to purplish-brown as spores mature, with lamellulae (short gills) present in a regular pattern.

The stipe (stem) is slender, cylindrical, 40–80 mm long and 1–3 mm thick, and typically exhibits bluish bruising when handled or damaged, which is characteristic of psilocybin-containing species. This bluing reaction is caused by the oxidation of psilocin and is a key field identification marker for psychoactive Psilocybe species. The stipe surface is smooth to finely pruinose (frosted) near the apex.

Spores are dark purplish-brown in deposit, ellipsoid to subellipsoid, and approximately 10–14 × 6–8 µm, consistent with the Psilocybe genus. Basidia are four-spored, clavate. Cheilocystidia are ventricose to lageniform, a feature shared with many other Psilocybe species.

## Distribution and Habitat

As the name suggests, *P. tasmaniana* is found in Tasmania, the southernmost state of Australia. It is also reported from parts of southeastern Australia, particularly in cool, temperate regions with reliable rainfall. The species favors dung-enriched substrates and grassy pastures, similar to the habitat preferences of [[psilocybe-subcubensis]] and [[psilocybe-semilanceata]].

Fruiting typically occurs in the cooler months of autumn and early winter, when moisture levels are high and temperatures range from approximately 10-18°C. In Tasmania's maritime climate, these conditions are met reliably from April through July.

## Chemistry and Potency

*Psilocybe tasmaniana* has been confirmed to contain [[psilocybin]] and [[psilocin]], the primary psychoactive tryptamine alkaloids. Analytical studies have verified the presence of these compounds through thin-layer chromatography (TLC) and more advanced analytical methods such as high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) and gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS).

The species is considered to be of moderate potency relative to other Australian psychoactive mushrooms. It is less potent than [[psilocybe-subaeruginosa]], which is regarded as one of the most potent species in the Australasian region, but comparable to [[psilocybe-cubensis]] in its overall alkaloid content per dry weight. Estimated alkaloid concentrations based on bioassay and limited analytical data are approximately 0.3–0.7% psilocybin and 0.05–0.2% psilocin per dry weight. [[baeocystin]] has been detected in trace amounts in related Tasmanian species and may also be present.

## Identification Notes

*Psilocybe tasmaniana* can be confused with several non-psychoactive or toxic species found in similar grassland habitats. The most critical lookalike is [[galerina-marginata]], which contains lethal amatoxins and can fruit in similar grassy, enriched-soil environments. Key distinguishing features include the Galerina's rusty-brown spore print (versus dark purplish-brown in *P. tasmaniana*) and the absence of significant blue bruising in Galerina species. Other lookalikes include [[panaeolina-foenisecii]] (the haymaker's mushroom), which is not psychoactive and has a dark brown spore print, and various inactive Psilocybe species that share the same grassland habitat but lack the characteristic bluing reaction.

The bluing reaction in *P. tasmaniana* is typically most pronounced at the base of the stipe and may be subtle in dried or older specimens. Fresh, young specimens collected during cool, wet conditions show the strongest bruising response. Microscopic examination of spore dimensions and cheilocystidia morphology is recommended for definitive identification.

## Distribution & Habitat

As the name suggests, *P. tasmaniana* is found in Tasmania, the southernmost state of Australia. It is also reported from parts of southeastern Australia, particularly in cool, temperate regions with reliable rainfall including Victoria and southern New South Wales. The species favors dung-enriched substrates and grassy pastures, similar to the habitat preferences of [[psilocybe-subcubensis]] and [[psilocybe-semilanceata]]. Fruiting typically occurs in the cooler months of autumn and early winter, when moisture levels are high and temperatures range from approximately 10–18°C. In Tasmania's maritime climate, these conditions are met reliably from April through July. The species has been collected from both lowland pastures and montane grasslands.

## Similar Species

*Psilocybe tasmaniana* can be confused with several non-psychoactive or toxic species found in similar habitats:
- **Galerina species**: Particularly [[galerina-marginata]], which contains lethal amatoxins. Distinguished by rusty-brown spore print.
- **Panaeolina foenisecii**: The haymaker's mushroom, common in lawns and pastures but not psychoactive. Has a dark brown spore print.
- **Psilocybe subaeruginosa**: Much more potent wood-inhabiting species. Usually larger with stronger bluing. See [[psilocybe-subaeruginosa]].
- **Psilocybe semilanceata**: The liberty cap, found in similar temperate grassland habitats in the Northern Hemisphere but not confirmed from Tasmania.
- **Inactive Psilocybe species**: Numerous brown-spored, non-psychoactive Psilocybe species share the same grassland habitat.

## Legal Status

In Australia, *Psilocybe tasmaniana* is classified as a prohibited plant under state and territory drug legislation. The possession, cultivation, and distribution of psilocybin-containing mushrooms is illegal throughout Australia, with penalties varying by jurisdiction. In Tasmania specifically, the species is covered under the Poisons Act and related drug misuse regulations.

## Seasonality

*P. tasmaniana* fruits during the cooler autumn and early winter months in Tasmania, typically from April through July. Peak fruiting occurs in May and June when temperatures range from 8–15°C and rainfall is consistent. The species benefits from Tasmania's reliable autumn rainfall and maritime climate, which provide extended periods of high humidity suitable for sustained fruiting. In favorable years, fruitings can extend into August during mild, wet conditions.
