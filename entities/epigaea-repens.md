## Overview

Trailing arbutus (*Epigaea repens*) is a low-growing evergreen subshrub in the heath family (Ericaceae). It is one of the earliest spring-blooming wildflowers in eastern North America, producing clusters of fragrant pink or white flowers in March and April. The species is celebrated for its intense spicy-sweet fragrance, which has made it a beloved symbol of spring in New England folklore.

The plant forms extensive mats of leathery evergreen leaves along the forest floor. Its creeping stems root at the nodes as they spread, creating dense carpets that persist year-round. The common name "trailing arbutus" reflects its prostrate growth habit and resemblance to the Mediterranean arbutus tree.

*E. repens* was designated the provincial flower of Nova Scotia in 1901 and is the state flower of Massachusetts.
