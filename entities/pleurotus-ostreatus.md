# Pleurotus ostreatus

**Pleurotus ostreatus**, the oyster mushroom, is one of the most widely cultivated edible mushrooms globally, belonging to the family Pleurotaceae. Beyond its nutritional value and culinary versatility, *P. ostreatus* has attracted significant scientific attention for its ability to produce statins, degrade environmental pollutants (mycoremediation), and modulate immune function. It serves as a bridge between medicinal mushroom research, environmental biotechnology, and sustainable agriculture.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

* **Division:** Basidiomycota
* **Class:** Agaricomycetes
* **Order:** Agaricales
* **Family:** Pleurotaceae
* **Genus:** *Pleurotus*

The oyster mushroom forms shelf-like, oyster-shaped or fan-shaped fruiting bodies with a short or absent stem. Caps are 5–25 cm wide, smooth, and range in color from white to gray, brown, or pink depending on strain and growing conditions. The gills are white to cream, decurrent (running down the stem), and closely spaced. The flesh is white, firm when young, and becomes tough with age. Spore print is white to lilac-gray.

*P. ostreatus* has a broad natural distribution across temperate and subtropical forests worldwide, growing saprotrophically on dead or dying hardwood trees, especially oak, beech, birch, and aspen. It fruits from autumn through winter and is one of the few cold-weather mushrooms, capable of fruiting at temperatures as low as 5°C. The species complex includes multiple genetically distinct populations adapted to different climatic conditions.

## Bioactive Compounds

### Lovastatin (Mevacor)

*P. ostreatus* is a natural producer of **lovastatin**, a competitive inhibitor of HMG-CoA reductase, the rate-limiting enzyme in cholesterol biosynthesis. Lovastatin content varies significantly witfruiting-body-developmentweight), substrate composition, and cultivation conditions. Rice straw and wheat straw substrates tend to produce higher lovastatin yields than wood-based substrates. The statin is produced during both mycelial growth and fruiting body development.

### Polysaccharides

Pleuran (also called pleuran-β-glucan) is a β-(1→3)(1→6)-D-glucan with demonstrated immunomodulatory activity. It is structurally similar to lentinan from shiitake but with different branching patterns and solubility characteristics.

### Additional Compounds

* **Pleurotin** — a furanone compound with antibiotic and antitumor properties
* **Ergothioneine** — a thiourea derivative of histidine with potent antioxidant properties; *Pleurotus* species are among the richest natural sources
* **Lectins** — hemagglutinating proteins with immunostimulatory activity
* **Phenolic compounds** — including p-hydroxybenzoic acid, p-coumaric acid, and gallic acid
* **Linoleic acid and its derivatives** — with reported antifungal and antimicrobial properties
* **Ergosterol** — provitamin D2, converting to vitamin D2 upon UV exposure

## Pharmacological Activities

### Cholesterol-Lowering Effects

The lovastatin content of *P. ostreatus* directly inhibits cholesterol synthesis in the liver, similar to pharmaceutical statins but at lower doses. Clinical studies have demonstrated:

* 10–20% reduction in total cholesterol after 4–8 weeks of consuming 10–50 g/day dried oyster mushroom
* Significant LDL cholesterol reduction (15–25%)
* Mild HDL cholesterol elevation
* Reduction in triglyceride levels (5–15%)

A randomized controlled trial by Schneider et al. demonstrated that 10 g/day of dried *P. ostreatus* powder was as effective as low-dose simvastatin (10 mg/day) in reducing LDL cholesterol over 8 weeks, without the muscle pain and liver enzyme elevations sometimes associated with pharmaceutical statins.

### Immunomodulatory Effects

Pleuran and other β-glucans from *P. ostreatus* modulate immune function through:

* Activation of macrophages and enhancement of phagocytic activity
* Stimulation of NK cell cytotoxicity against tumor cells
* Increased production of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, TNF-α, IL-6) and subsequent regulation
* Enhancement of secretory IgA production in the gut mucosa
* Clinical trials have shown pleuran supplementation reduces the incidence and severity of upper respiratory tract infections in athletes and children

### Antitumor Activity

In vitro and animal studies demonstrate:

* Direct cytotoxicity against breast cancer (MCF-7), colon cancer (HCT-116), and lung cancer (A549) cell lines
* Apoptosis induction through mitochondrial pathways and caspase activation
* Anti-angiogenic effects through suppression of VEGF
* Enhancement of chemotherapy efficacy when used as an adjunct
* In vivo tumor suppression in sarcoma-180 and Ehrlich ascites tumor models

### Antioxidant Activity

*P. ostreatus* demonstrates potent antioxidant capacity through multiple mechanisms:

* Ergothioneine scavenges reactive oxygen species (ROS) and protects cellular proteins from oxidative damage
* Phenolic compounds chelate metal ions (Fe²⁺, Cu²⁺) and prevent Fenton reactions
* Enzymatic antioxidants including superoxide dismutase (SOD), catalase, and glutathione peroxidase are upregulated by oyster mushroom extracts

## Nutritional Profile

Oyster mushrooms are nutritionally rich, containing 20–30% protein (dry weight) with a favorable amino acid profile. They are excellent sources of B vitamins (particularly niacin, riboflavin, thiamine, and folate), vitamin D2, potassium, phosphorus, iron, and zinc. The fiber content includes both insoluble dietary fiber and bioactive β-glucans.

## Cultivation

*P. ostreatus* is one of the easiest mushrooms to cultivate, making it ideal for small-scale and home cultivation. It grows on a wide variety of lignocellulosic substrates including:

* Hardwood sawdust (oak, beech, poplar)
* Agricultural residues (wheat straw, rice straw, corn cobs, sugarcane bagasse)
* Paper and cardboard waste
* Coffee grounds
* Cottonseed hulls
