## Description

[[rhagodia]] is a genus of evergreen shrubs in the family Chenopodiaceae (now often included in Amaranthaceae) native to Australia. These tough, sprawling to erect shrubs are valued in [[permaculture]] as frontline windbreak species on exposed coastal sites, where they tolerate salt spray, sandblast, and drought to form dense, semi-succulent barriers.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Family:** Chenopodiaceae / Amaranthaceae
- **Genus:** Rhagodia
- **Common name:** Saltbush, berry saltbush, climbing saltbush
- **Growth form:** Sprawling to erect evergreen shrubs; some species climb or scramble

## Key Facts

### Coastal Windbreak Role

Mollison lists Rhagodia among the key semi-succulent ground-cover species that can mound over mesh fences, rock walls, and tyre barriers on salt-laden coasts. Combined with Tetragonia, Carpobrotus, and Mesembryanthemum, Rhagodia helps create living barriers that withstand direct salt spray, form dense low-growing canopies, provide bee forage and edible berries, and require minimal maintenance once established.

### Notable Species

- **Rhagodia candolleana** (sea berry saltbush): Coastal species with fleshy red berries, found along southern Australian coastlines.
- **Rhagodia baccata** (berry saltbush): Western Australian species, erect shrub to 2 m with edible berries.
- **Rhagodia spinescens** (creeping saltbush): Low, spreading form useful as ground cover on dunes.

### Adaptations

- **Semi-succulent foliage:** Stores water, resists desiccation from salt and wind.
- **Deep root system:** Accesses subsoil moisture in sandy coastal soils.
- **Salt tolerance:** Excretes or compartmentalizes salt, surviving conditions that kill most species.
- **Rapid growth:** Quickly covers fences and barriers when planted in favorable microsites.

## Relevance to Cultivation

1. **Coastal food production:** Without hardy pioneers like Rhagodia, no productive food gardens can be established on exposed coasts.
2. **Living fences:** Rhagodia scrambles over mesh and rock to create fedge systems combining structural and living windbreak.
3. **Wildlife habitat:** Berries attract birds; dense growth shelters small animals and invertebrates.
4. **Erosion control:** Stabilizes coastal dunes and sandy soils against wind erosion.
5. **[[edible-landscaping]]:** Berries of some species are edible, adding food yield to windbreak infrastructure.
