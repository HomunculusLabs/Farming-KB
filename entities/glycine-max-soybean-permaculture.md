# Glycine max (Soybean)

## Overview

*Glycine max*, commonly known as the soybean, is a leguminous annual plant in the family Fabaceae. Originally domesticated in East Asia approximately 5,000–9,000 years ago, soybean has become one of the most important crop species globally, serving as a primary source of plant protein, vegetable oil, and animal feed. In the context of permaculture and sustainable agriculture, soybean is valued for its nitrogen-fixing capacity, versatility in polyculture systems, deep taproot architecture, and role as a dynamic accumulator of soil nutrients.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Clade:** Tracheophytes
- **Clade:** Angiosperms
- **Clade:** Eudicots
- **Clade:** Rosids
- **Order:** Fabales
- **Family:** Fabaceae
- **Subfamily:** Faboideae
- **Genus:** *Glycine*
- **Species:** *G. max*

The wild progenitor of cultivated soybean is *Glycine soja* (or *G. max* subsp. *soja*), a scrambling annual vine native to East Asia. The domestication bottleneck reduced genetic diversity by approximately 50%, but modern breeding programs have reintroduced alleles from wild populations to improve stress tolerance and nutritional quality.

## Morphology

### Growth Habit
- **Life cycle:** Annual (typically 90–150 days, depending on variety and photoperiod)
- **Growth habit:** Determinate or indeterminate, depending on cultivar
- **Height:** 20–180 cm
- **Root system:** Deep taproot (60–180 cm) with extensive lateral branching and nodulation

### Leaves
- **Type:** Trifoliate (three leaflets per leaf)
- **Leaflets:** Ovate to lanceolate, 6–15 cm long, 2–7 cm wide
- **Stipules:** Present, small, lanceolate
- **Color:** Green to dark green; some cultivars exhibit purple anthocyanin pigmentation

### Flowers
- **Type:** Papilionaceous (typical Fabaceae form)
- **Color:** White, pink, or purple
- **Arrangement:** Axillary racemes, 3–15 flowers per cluster
- **Pollination:** Predominantly self-pollinating (>95%); some cross-pollination by insects

### Pods and Seeds
- **Pod:** Pubescent, 3–8 cm long, containing 1–5 seeds
- **Seed:** Oval to spherical, 5–11 mm diameter
- **Seed color:** Yellow, green, brown, black (variety-dependent)
- **Seed composition (dry weight):** ~40% protein, ~20% oil, ~35% carbohydrate, ~5% ash

## Permaculture Functions

### 1. Nitrogen Fixation

Soybean forms a **symbiotic relationship** with *Bradyrhizobium japonicum* (or related *Bradyrhizobium* spp.) through root nodule formation. The biochemical process:
1. Root exudates (flavonoids and isoflavones) attract compatible rhizobial bacteria
2. Bacteria attach to root hairs and form an infection thread
3. Nodule primordia develop from root cortical cells
4. Inside the nodule, rhizobia differentiate into bacteroids expressing the **nitrogenase enzyme complex**
5. Nitrogenase catalyzes: N₂ + 8H⁺ + 8e⁻ + 16 ATP → 2NH₃ + H₂ + 16 ADP + 16 Pi
6. The plant provides photosynthates (malate, succinate) as the carbon and energy source

**Nitrogen fixation rates:** Well-nodulated soybean can fix 50–200 kg N/ha/year, with fixation efficiency varying based on soil conditions, rhizobial strain compatibility, and plant nutrition.

### 2. Dynamic Nutrient Accumulation

The deep taproot system of soybean accesses nutrients from subsoil layers that are unavailable to shallow-rooted crops:
| Nutrient | Accumulation Mechanism | Permaculture Benefit |
|----------|----------------------|---------------------|
| Phosphorus | Mycorrhizal associations; root exudate solubilization | Brought to surface via leaf litter and residue decomposition |
| Calcium | Deep root uptake from calcareous subsoils | Cycled back to topsoil through plant residue |
| Potassium | Subsoil mining | Returns to topsoil via mulch incorporation |
| Iron | Chelation by root exudates | Beneficial for intercropped species with Fe deficiency |

- **Companion with maize (three sisters adaptation):** Soybean fixes nitrogen for the heavy-feeding corn, while corn provides structural support
- **Intercropped with small grains:** Reduces pest pressure through habitat diversification
- **Repels certain pests:** Isoflavone compounds in root exudates may deter some soil-borne pathogens
- **Attracts beneficial insects:** Flowers provide nectar and pollen for parasitoid wasps, lady beetles, and other predatory insects

## Polyculture Integration Strategies

### Three Sisters System (East Asian Adaptation)
```
Soybean + Foxtail millet (Setaria italica) + Azuki bean (Vigna angularis)
```
This East Asian polyculture mirrors the Mesoamerican Three Sisters but substitutes regionally appropriate species. Soybean fixes nitrogen, millet provides structural support and grain yield, and azuki bean provides additional nitrogen fixation and a complementary protein source.

### Soybean + Maize Intercrop
- Plant soybean 2–4 rows between maize rows
- Spatial arrangement: alternating or strip intercropping
- Yield advantage (Land Equivalent Ratio): 1.15–1.40 (i.e., 15–40% more total production per unit area compared to sole cropping)
- Nitrogen transfer: 10–30% of fixed nitrogen may be transferred to adjacent maize via mycorrhizal networks or root exudate pathways

### Soybean + Cover Crop Sequence
A common permaculture rotation pattern:
```
Soybean (summer) → Winter rye + crimson clover (winter cover) → Sorghum or amaranth (following summer)
```
This sequence maximizes nitrogen fixation, soil coverage, and nutrient cycling across the annual cycle.

## Cultivation in Permaculture Systems

### Soil Requirements
- **pH:** 5.5–7.0 (optimal: 6.0–6.5)
- **Soil type:** Adapted to a wide range — sandy loams to clay loams; best in well-drained soils
- **Organic matter:** Moderate to high preferred
- **Phosphorus:** Important for nodulation and nitrogen fixation
- **Inoculation:** Seed inoculation with appropriate *Bradyrhizobium* strains is essential if soybean has not been grown previously in the site

### Planting
- **Timing:** After soil temperature reaches 15°C (59°F); in temperate zones, typically 2–4 weeks after last frost
- **Seeding depth:** 2.5–4 cm
- **Spacing:** 5–10 cm within rows; 40–75 cm between rows (depending on variety and system)
- **Seed rate:** 35–50 kg/ha (depending on seed size and row spacing)

### Water Requirements
- **Annual requirement:** 450–700 mm
- **Critical periods:** Flowering and pod-filling stages are most sensitive to water stress
- **Drought tolerance:** Moderate — deeper-rooted cultivars and landraces show improved drought resilience
- **Permaculture strategy:** Swale systems and mulching reduce irrigation needs significantly

### Pests and Diseases (Integrated Management)

| Pest/Disease | Integrated Management Approach |
|-------------|-------------------------------|
| Soybean aphid (*Aphis glycines*) | Encourage lady beetles, lacewings; neem oil spray |
| Japanese beetle (*Popillia japonica*) | Companion planting with garlic, catnip; floating row covers |
| Soybean cyst nematode (*Heterodera glycines*) | Crop rotation with non-host crops; marigold intercropping |
| Root rot (*Phytophthora sojae*) | Well-drained soil; biochar amendment; resistant varieties |
| Powdery mildew | Adequate spacing for air circulation; potassium bicarbonate spray |
| Deer and rabbit browsing | Physical barriers; repellent plants (garlic, onions) in border |

## Nutritional Value

Soybean is nutritionally exceptional among plant foods:
| Nutrient | Per 100g (dry) | Significance |
|----------|---------------|-------------|
| Protein | 36–40 g | Complete protein — contains all 9 essential amino acids |
| Fat | 18–20 g | Rich in polyunsaturated fatty acids (linoleic and linolenic acid) |
| Carbohydrates | 25–30 g | Includes dietary fiber and oligosaccharides |
| Iron | 15.7 mg | High bioavailability when fermented |
| Calcium | 277 mg | Enhanced by fermentation |
| Folate | 375 μg | Important B vitamin |
| Isoflavones | 0.5–3 mg/g | Genistein, daidzein — phytoestrogens with health-promoting properties |

### Soybean Products
- **Tofu** — Coagulated soy milk
- **Tempeh** — Fermented with *Rhizopus oligosporus*
- **Miso** — Fermented with *Aspergillus oryzae*
- **Natto** — Fermented with *Bacillus subtilis* natto
- **Soy sauce (shoyu)** — Fermented paste
- **Soy milk** — Extracted from ground beans
- **Edamame** — Harvested at green/immature stage

Fermentation improves protein digestibility, reduces anti-nutritional factors (trypsin inhibitors, phytic acid), and increases bioavailability of minerals and isoflavones.

## Environmental Impact and Sustainability

### Carbon Sequestration
Soybean contributes to soil carbon sequestration through root biomass turnover and residue incorporation, adding approximately 0.5–1.5 t C/ha/year to soil organic carbon pools in well-managed systems.

### Reduced Fertilizer Dependence
By fixing 50–200 kg N/ha/year, soybean can eliminate or drastically reduce the need for synthetic nitrogen fertilizers in rotation systems, which reduces greenhouse gas emissions associated with fertilizer manufacturing (Haber-Bosch process: ~1–2% of global energy consumption).

### Water Use Efficiency
Soybean has a water use efficiency (WUE) of approximately 10–15 kg grain per mm of water, comparable to other legumes and superior to many cereal crops when accounting for its protein output per unit of water.
