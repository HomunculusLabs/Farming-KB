# Richard Alpert

Richard Alpert (April 6, 1931 – December 22, 2019), later known as Ram Dass,
was an American spiritual teacher, psychologist, and author. He was a
prominent figure in the counterculture of the 1960s and became one of the
most influential voices in bringing Eastern spiritual practices to Western
audiences.

## Early Academic Career

Richard Alpert was born in Boston, Massachusetts, to a prominent Jewish family.
His father was George Alpert, a lawyer and railroad executive. Alpert earned
his bachelor's degree from Tufts University in 1952 and received a master's
degree from Wesleyan University in 1954. He earned his Ph.D. in psychology
from Stanford University in 1957, completing his doctoral work under the
guidance of prominent psychologists. His early academic interests focused on
human motivation, personality theory, and the psychology of consciousness.

## Harvard Research with Timothy Leary

In 1958, Alpert was appointed as an assistant professor of clinical psychology
at Harvard University, where he also served as the director of the Harvard
Psychedelic Research Project. He joined [[timothy-leary]], who had been
recruited to the Harvard faculty in 1960, and together they conducted
pioneering research on the psychological effects of [[psilocybin-compound]] and
[[hofmann-lsd-discovery-bicycle-day]]. Their studies explored the potential of psychedelics to induce
mystical experiences, alter personality structure, and facilitate therapeutic
breakthroughs.

The Harvard research became controversial. In 1962, the university
administration began investigating Alpert and Leary for administering
psychedelics to undergraduate students, which violated research protocols.
In 1963, both were dismissed from Harvard. Despite the controversial end to
their Harvard careers, their research became foundational to the emerging
field of psychedelic studies and influenced a generation of researchers.

## Spiritual Journey to India

In 1967, Alpert traveled to India seeking spiritual teachers who could help
him understand the states of consciousness he had encountered through
psychedelics. In India, he met Neem Karoli Baba (also known as Maharaj-ji),
a Hindu sage and devotee of Hanuman, at a temple in the Himalayan foothills.
Neem Karoli Baba gave Alpert the name "Ram Dass," meaning "Servant of God."
The encounter profoundly transformed Alpert, who found in Neem Karoli Baba's
teachings the spiritual framework he had been seeking.

During his time in India, Ram Dass studied with various teachers and immersed
himself in Hindu devotional practices, yoga, and meditation. The experience
shifted his focus from the use of external substances to achieve altered
states to the pursuit of spiritual awakening through disciplined inner
practice.

## Key Books and Publications

- **Be Here Now** (1971) — Ram Dass's most influential book, a seminal work
  of the spiritual counterculture that combined autobiographical narrative
  with teachings on meditation, yoga, and consciousness. The book sold over
  two million copies and introduced generations of readers to Eastern
  spiritual practices.
- **The Only Dance There Is** (1974) — A collection of talks exploring the
  integration of spiritual practice with everyday life.
- **Grist for the Mill** (1977, with Stephen Levine) — Teachings on using
  life's difficulties as material for spiritual growth.
- **How Can I Help?** (1985, with Paul Gorman) — An exploration of service
  as a spiritual practice.
- **Still Here: Embracing Aging, Changing, and Dying** (2000) — Reflections
  on aging and mortality informed by his own experience of a debilitating
  stroke in 1997.

## Later Life and Legacy

After suffering a stroke in 1997 that left him with significant physical
limitations, Ram Dass continued to teach and write, using his experience of
illness and recovery as a basis for spiritual instruction. He established the
Love Serve Remember Foundation and the Ram Dass Foundation to preserve and
share his teachings. He remained active in the interfaith dialogue movement
and collaborated with figures including the Dalai Lama and Thich Nhat Hanh.
Ram Dass died on December 22, 2019, at his home in Maui, Hawaii. His legacy
includes a vast body of teachings that bridged Eastern and Western spiritual
traditions and influenced the modern mindfulness movement, [[psychedelic-therapy-foundations]]
research, and contemplative education.
