# Golden Teacher

**Golden Teacher** is a widely recognized strain of *Psilocybe cubensis*, a
psychoactive basidiomycete fungus known for producing psilocybin and psilocin.
It is among the most popular and well-documented strains in the mycology
community, valued for its reliability and accessibility.

## Classification

Golden Teacher belongs to *Psilocybe cubensis* (Earle) Singer, family
Hymenogastraceae, order Agaricales. It represents a specific cultivar with
consistent morphological and psychochemical traits preserved through selective
cloning and multispore propagation.

## Origin and History

Golden Teacher first appeared in the late 1980s, likely originating from the
Gulf Coast of the United States or possibly Cuba. It gained prominence through
underground mycology networks and was distributed by spore vendors beginning in
the early 1990s. Its accessible growth requirements and reliable yields drove
rapid adoption by amateur cultivators worldwide. Exact genetic lineage remains
uncertain, though it likely descends from wild *P. cubensis* from subtropical
and tropical environments.

## Morphology

Golden Teacher produces large, robust fruiting bodies with distinctive
golden-amber caps (30–80 mm, convex to plane, often with a pronounced umbo)
and cylindrical stipes (60–150 mm × 5–15 mm) that bruise blue when damaged.
Gills are adnate to adnexed, darkening from grayish-white to deep purplish-
brown at maturity. A well-developed partial veil leaves a persistent
membranous annulus on the upper stipe. Spore prints are dark purplish-brown.

## Growth Characteristics

Regarded as one of the most beginner-friendly strains, Golden Teacher has
moderate colonization speed (10–14 days on grain), producing thick white
rhizomorphic mycelium with relative resistance to common contaminants. Fruiting
is robust and prolific, with first flushes yielding medium to large fruits and
2–4 subsequent flushes of smaller but abundant mushrooms.

## Spore Characteristics and Microscopy

Spores are dark purplish-brown, ellipsoid to subellipsoid, measuring
~11.5–17.5 × 8–11.5 µm. Basidia are club-shaped and typically four-spored.
Spore syringes and prints are widely available for microscopy and taxonomy
research.

## Popularity and Cultural Significance

Golden Teacher holds a central position in amateur mycology. It is the most
commonly recommended first strain for novice cultivators due to forgiving
growth parameters, reliable yields, and extensive documentation. Its moderate
potency suits all experience levels. Spores are among the most traded in the
microscopy community, widely referenced in forums and cultivation guides.

## Genetic Stability and Lineage

Golden Teacher demonstrates good genetic stability across multispore generations,
retaining golden cap coloration, moderate potency, and growth parameters. Some
phenotypic variation is expected; cloned sub-strains preserve traits more
consistently. Multiple lineages circulate under the Golden Teacher name due to
open spore trading, contributing to inter-vendor variability.
