# Gerronema strombodes

## Overview

**Gerronema strombodes** is a tropical agaric mushroom in the family
Omphalotaceae, widely distributed across lowland neotropical forests from
southern Mexico through [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-central-america-caribbean]] into northern [[guzman-psilocybin-mushrooms-south-america]].

It is recognized by its broadly umbilicate cap, pale cream-to-ochre coloration,
distantly spaced gills, and preference for decomposing hardwood on the forest
floor. The species is considered edible and is collected by indigenous
communities in parts of its range, though it is not commercially cultivated.

The epithet *strombodes* refers to the swirling, funnel-like shape of mature
caps, which can become notably depressed at the center.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:** [[fungal-taxonomy-and-kingdom-fungi]] → Basidiomycota → Agaricomycetes →
Agaricales → Omphalotaceae → Gerronema → G. strombodes

**Authority:** (Berk. & M.A. Curtis) Singer, 1951

**Basonym:** Agaricus strombodes Berk. & M.A. Curtis, 1868

The basidiocarp is small to medium. The cap measures 20–70 mm in diameter,
convex when young, expanding to broadly convex with a distinct central
depression or umbo in maturity. The cap surface is smooth, dry, and
glabrous, ranging from pale cream to buff or pale ochraceous.

The flesh is thin and membranous, white to cream, without a distinctive
odor or taste. Gills are decurrent, narrow, and subdistant, with 30–40
lamellae reaching the stipe and numerous lamellulae in between.

The stipe is 20–50 mm long and 2–5 mm thick, central or slightly
eccentric, cylindrical, equal, and solid. Its surface is smooth and
concolorous with the cap or slightly paler.

Spores are broadly ellipsoid, smooth, hyaline, non-amyloid, measuring
6.0–8.5 × 4.5–6.0 μm. Basidia are clavate, four-spored, 20–25 × 5–7 μm.

Cystidia are absent or very sparse on gill edges. The pileipellis is a
cutis of interwoven cylindrical hyphae 3–6 μm wide.

## Distribution and Habitat

G. strombodes occurs in humid tropical lowland forests from southern Mexico
(Veracruz, Chiapas) through Guatemala, Belize, Honduras, Costa Rica, Panama,
and into Colombia, Venezuela, and the Amazon basin.

Elevation ranges from sea level to approximately 800 meters. The species is
most commonly found on well-decomposed hardwood logs, branches, and woody
debris on the [[fungal-ecology-forest-floor]], particularly on Fabaceae, Moraceae, and
Sapotaceae substrates.

Fruiting occurs during the rainy season, typically from May through November
[[neurotropic-fungi-in-central-america]], with peak abundance during the wettest months.

## Ecology

G. strombodes is a primary white-rot decomposer of hardwood. It contributes
to [[comparison-nutrient-cycling-vs-nutrient-dense-gardening]] [[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi-in-tropical-forest-restoration]] ecosystems by breaking [[query-how-do-white-rot-fungi-break-down-lignin]] and
cellulose in fallen wood.

The species is relatively tolerant of fragmented forest habitats and has been
recorded in secondary growth and disturbed forest patches, suggesting moderate
ecological resilience compared to more specialized tropical fungi.

Associations with arthropods have been documented, with beetles and dipteran
larvae occasionally inhabiting the basidiocarps.
