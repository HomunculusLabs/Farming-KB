## Description

Java Tilapia (*Oreochromis mossambicus* or related Tilapia species) is a freshwater plankton-feeding fish widely regarded as one of the most productive species for aquaculture ponds. In Mollison's permaculture manual, it is highlighted as the top-yielding fish species when ponds are fertilised, producing far greater yields per hectare than predatory or omnivorous alternatives.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Animalia
- **Phylum**: Chordata
- **Class**: Actinopterygii (ray-finned fishes)
- **Order**: Cichliformes
- **Family**: Cichlidae
- **Genus**: *Oreochromis*
- **Trophic level**: Primary consumer (plankton feeder)

## Key Facts

- As a plankton feeder low on the trophic scale, Java Tilapia achieves the highest yields among commonly cultured fish species in fertilised ponds.
- Stocking rates of 5,000-10,000 fish per hectare are typical for Tilapia in production ponds.
- At 5,000/ha, yields of approximately 840 kg/ha are achievable; at 15,000/ha, yields can reach 1,387 kg/ha.
- Tilapia breeding in ponds can be controlled via sterile hybridisation techniques, producing one-sex or sterile male stock to prevent overpopulation.
- Overstocking beyond optimal rates produces high proportions of unmarketable small fish — at 10,000/ha, marketable percentage drops to approximately 50%.
- Fast-growing Tilapia strains can reach 200 g marketable size in about 6 months, while slower growers may take 13 months to reach the same weight.
- Tilapia can be successfully paired with channel catfish in polyculture; catfish food conversion efficiency (1.7:1) is unaffected by Tilapia presence.
- Combined catfish (4,400/ha) plus Tilapia (1,250/ha) yields can reach 1,834 kg/ha versus 1,400 kg/ha for catfish alone.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Tilapia is central to permaculture aquaculture design as a keystone polyculture species. Its role as a plankton converter makes it an efficient biological nutrient recycler. In integrated systems, Tilapia ponds can:

- Receive and process organic waste streams, converting them to protein.
- Produce nutrient-rich water for hydroponic or terrestrial crop irrigation.
- Function as part of a trophic ladder in consecutive pond series, where forage organisms flow to higher trophic levels.
- Integrate with mushroom cultivation systems where pond water provides mineral-rich irrigation for substrate preparation.

Tilapia exemplifies the permaculture principle of using biological resources at the lowest feasible trophic level for maximum energy efficiency — a concept equally applicable to fungal cultivation systems where substrate organisms break down organic matter before fungal colonisation.
