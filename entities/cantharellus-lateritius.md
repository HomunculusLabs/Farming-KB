# Cantharellus lateritius

**Cantharellus lateritius** (Berk.) Singer, commonly known as the **smooth chanterelle**, **yellow chanterelle**, or **late chanterelle**, is an edible ectomycorrhizal mushroom in the family Cantharellaceae. It is one of the most widespread and commonly foraged chanterelle species in [[polypore-fungi-eastern-north-america]], prized for its mild fruity aroma, peppery flavor, and firm texture. Often confused with the golden chanterelle (*[[cantharellus-cibarius-entity]]*), it is distinguished by its smoother hymenium (lacking the pronounced false gills of *C. cibarius*) and later fruiting season.

## Taxonomy and Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Cantharellales
- **Family:** Cantharellaceae
- **Genus:** Cantharellus
- **Species:** C. lateritius
- **Authority:** (Berk.) Singer, 1943; basionym: *Merulius lateritius* Berk., 1873
- **Synonyms:** *Cantharellus aurantiacus* (Peck) Shaffer, *Craterellus lateritius* (Berk.) Shaffer
- **Etymology:** *lateritius* means "brick-colored" in Latin, referring to the orange-red cap color

## Macroscopic Features

- **Cap:** 3–12 cm diameter, convex to depressed at center with age; margin inrolled when young, becoming wavy and irregular; surface smooth to slightly wrinkled, tacky when wet; color ranges from bright apricot-orange to deep brick-orange; develops brownish tones with age
- **Hymenium (underside):** Distinguishing feature — smooth to shallowly wrinkled or bluntly veined, lacking the well-developed forked ridges (false gills) characteristic of *C. cibarius*; color similar to cap or slightly paler
- **Stem:** 3–8 cm × 1–3 cm, solid, tapering downward; smooth to slightly longitudinally wrinkled; pale yellow to orange; often curved at base
- **Flesh:** Firm, pale yellow to white; odor mild and fruity (sometimes described as apricot-like); taste mildly peppery
- **Spore print:** Pale yellow to cream
- **Spores:** Ellipsoid, 7–10 × 4–6 µm, smooth, hyaline, inamyloid

## Microscopic Features

- **Basidia:** Clavate, 4-spored, 55–75 × 7–10 µm
- **Basidiospores:** Ellipsoid, smooth, thin-walled, containing one or more large oil droplets; 7–10 × 4.5–6 µm
- **Hyphal system:** Monomitic; generative hyphae with clamp connections
- **Pileipellis:** Cutis composed of repent hyphae; terminal cells cylindrical to slightly swollen

## Distribution and Habitat

- **Geographic range:** Eastern North America from Newfoundland to Florida, westward to the Great Plains; also reported in [[guzman-allen-gartz-neurotropic-fungi-central-america-caribbean]] and parts of Asia
- **Habitat:** Ectomycorrhizal with hardwoods — primarily oaks (*Quercus* spp.), but also found with hickories (*Carya*), beech (*Fagus*), tulip poplar (*Liriodendron*), and occasionally conifers
- **Season:** Summer through autumn (June–October), typically peaking later than *C. cibarius* — hence the common name "late chanterelle"
- **Ecology:** Ectomycorrhizal mutualist; forms nutrient-exchange relationships with tree root systems; contributes to forest [[comparison-nutrient-cycling-vs-nutrient-dense-gardening]] and soil health
- **Preferred conditions:** Warm, humid summers with adequate rainfall; often fruits in abundance following warm rain events in mid-to-late summer
- **Habitat types:** Oak-hickory forests, mixed hardwood stands, forest edges, disturbed woodland; occasionally found in urban parks with mature oaks

## Nutritional Composition

- **Macronutrients:** High protein (15–25% dry weight), low fat (<5%), moderate carbohydrates (40–55% dry weight), high dietary fiber
- **Vitamins:** Rich in B-complex vitamins (B1 thiamine, B2 riboflavin, B3 niacin, B5 pantothenic acid, B9 folate); contains vitamin D₂ (ergocalciferol) from ergosterol when exposed to UV light
- **Minerals:** Excellent source of potassium, phosphorus, and iron; good source of zinc, copper, manganese, and selenium
- **[[query-what-are-the-main-bioactive-compounds-in-medicinal-plants]]:** Ergosterol (pro-vitamin D₂), ergothioneine (antioxidant amino acid), β-glucans (immunomodulatory polysaccharides), lovastatin precursors
- **Antioxidant capacity:** High ORAC value; ergothioneine and selenium contribute to significant free-radical scavenging activity
- **Caloric value:** Approximately 25–35 kcal per 100 g fresh weight

## Distinguishing from Similar Species

- **Cantharellus cibarius (golden chanterelle):** Has prominent, bluntly forked false gills that run down the stem; *C. lateritius* has a smooth or shallowly wrinkled hymenium; *C. cibarius* typically fruits earlier in the season
- **[[cantharellus-cinnabarinus]] (cinnabar chanterelle):** Much smaller (cap 1–4 cm), bright red-orange, thin-fleshed; has blunt ridges; more likely to be confused with *C. minor*
- **[[hygrophoropsis-aurantiaca]] (false chanterelle):** True gills that are thin, forking, and easily separable; grows on decaying wood rather than soil; cap more orange and less apricot-colored
- **[[omphalotus-olearius]] (jack-o'-lantern):** TOXIC — has true gills, not ridges; grows in clusters on wood or buried roots; bioluminescent gills in darkness; causes severe gastrointestinal distress
- **Cantharellus floridensis:** Very similar species found in the southeastern US; differentiated by spore size and geographic distribution; some authorities consider it conspecific

## Culinary Value

- **Flavor profile:** Mild, fruity aroma with a slightly peppery, apricot-like taste; less intensely flavored than *C. cibarius* but more delicate and nuanced
- **Texture:** Firm and meaty when fresh; holds shape well during cooking; does not release excessive water
- **Cooking methods:** Excellent sautéed in butter, added to cream sauces, risottos, and pasta; pairs well with poultry, pork, eggs, and cream; can be dried for long-term storage and reconstituted
- **Preservation:** Dries well (retains flavor better than many mushrooms); also freezes well after light sautéing; suitable for pickling
- **Market value:** Less commercially valuable than *C. cibarius* due to less recognition; highly prized by foragers and sold at farmers' markets in regions where it is common
