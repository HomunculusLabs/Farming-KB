**Morphology:**
- Height: 5–15 cm, spreading to 50 cm or more
- Leaves: Rounded to cordate, 1–2.5 cm, crenate margins, bright green, semi-evergreen to evergreen
- Stems: Prostrate to ascending, rooting at nodes where they contact soil
- Flowers: Funnel-shaped to campanulate, 1.5–2.5 cm long, deep violet-purple, borne in terminal clusters of 2–5; corolla lobes less reflexed than in C. garganica
- Blooming period: May through July, with occasional rebloom in early autumn
- Fruit: Erect capsule dehiscing by three basal pores

The deeper purple flower color and less reflexed corolla lobes distinguish it from C. garganica, which has paler lavender-blue flowers with sharply recurved lobes.

Flower color can vary from deep violet-purple to lavender depending on growing conditions and genetic variation within populations. The species does not produce white-flowered forms in the wild, though pale variants occasionally appear in cultivation.

## Distribution and Habitat

Native to the Dinaric Alps of Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, and Montenegro. Found primarily in karst limestone regions along the eastern Adriatic coast and inland mountain ranges.

Grows on limestone cliffs, in rock crevices, on dry-stone walls, and in scree at elevations from sea level to approximately 1,500 meters. The species is a calcicole, strongly preferring alkaline substrates.

The native climate is sub-Mediterranean with warm, dry summers and cool, wet winters. Snow cover persists at higher elevations but the species is fully hardy and tolerates prolonged freezing.

Populations are locally abundant and not considered threatened. Much of the native range falls within national parks and protected areas in Croatia.

The karst limestone landscape of the Dinaric region provides ideal habitat with its extensive network of crevices, fissures, and thin soil pockets. The species is well adapted to the periodic drying that characterizes these free-draining substrates.

## Cultivation

Campanula portenschlagiana is among the easiest campanulas to grow and is recommended for beginners. It is hardy to USDA zones 3–8 and RHS hardiness rating H7 (below −20 °C).

**Growing requirements:**
- Soil: Well-drained, alkaline to neutral loam or gritty substrate; tolerates poor soils
- Light: Full sun to partial shade; flowers best in full sun
- Water: Moderate during growth; drought-tolerant once established
- Propagation: Division in spring; stem cuttings in early summer; seed in autumn

The species roots freely at stem nodes, allowing it to colonize crevices and gaps in walls effectively. Plants spread rapidly and can form mats over 1 meter across within 3–5 years.

**Notable cultivars:**
- 'Birch Hybrid' — compact habit with large deep violet flowers
- 'Reshold' — variegated foliage with cream margins

No significant pest or disease problems. Crown rot may occur in poorly drained, waterlogged soils.

The species tolerates salt spray and is suitable for coastal gardens in exposed positions. This [[cervantes-root-zone-heating]] cool. Foliage may become semi-evergreen in colder zones, dying back to the crown in severe winters and regrowing in spring.

## Ecological Role

The deep purple flowers are highly attractive to bees, particularly bumblebees and [[comparison-honey-bees-vs-native-solitary-bees]]. The tubular corolla provides access primarily to medium-tongued pollinators.
