# Lion's Mane Mushroom (*Hericium erinaceus*)

## Overview

Lion's Mane is a large, white, shaggy mushroom that resembles a lion's mane as it grows. It belongs to the tooth fungus group (*Hericiaceae*) and has been used for centuries in traditional Chinese and Japanese medicine. Native to North America, Europe, and Asia, it grows on hardwood trees, particularly oaks, walnuts, and beeches, typically in late summer and fall.

## Identification

- **Cap**: 5–25 cm diameter, white when fresh, yellowing with age, composed of cascading spines
- **Spines**: 1–5 cm long, white, hanging downward in tufts or a single mass
- **Flesh**: White, soft, and edible when young
- **Spore print**: White to cream
- **Odor**: Mild, pleasant, slightly seafood-like when cooked
- **Habitat**: Saprotrophic on dead or dying hardwoods; occasionally parasitic

Similar species include *Hericium coralloides* (coral hedgehog) and *Hericium americanum* (bear's head), which have branched fruiting bodies rather than a single mass.

## Active Compounds

### Hericenones (Found in the fruiting body)
Hericenones are aromatic compounds isolated from the fruiting body. At least eight hericenones (C–H) have been identified. Hericenones C, D, E, F, G, and H have been studied for their ability to stimulate nerve growth factor (NGF) synthesis in vitro.

### Erinacines (Found in the mycelium)
Erinacines are cyathane-type diterpenoids found primarily in the mycelium. Erinacine A is the most extensively studied and has demonstrated potent NGF-inducing activity. Erinacines S and A-1 have shown neuroprotective effects in animal models.

### β-Glucan Polysaccharides
Lion's Mane contains significant amounts of (1→3)-β-D-glucan and (1→6)-β-D-glucan, which contribute to its immunomodulatory properties by activating macrophages, NK cells, and dendritic cells.

## Health Benefits

### Neuroprotective Effects
- Stimulates synthesis of NGF and brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF)
- May improve mild cognitive impairment in older adults (double-blind clinical trial, Mori et al., 2009)
- Animal studies show protection against neuronal damage from amyloid-beta plaques
- Investigated for potential applications in Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease

### Cognitive Enhancement
- Improves memory and concentration in both animal and human studies
- May enhance spatial memory and recognition memory
- Clinical evidence supports improvement in cognitive function at 3 g/day over 16 weeks

### Mood and Anxiety
- Reduced symptoms of anxiety and depression in a 4-week clinical trial (Nagano et al., 2010)
- Mechanism may involve anti-inflammatory and NGF-enhancing pathways

### Immune System Support
- β-glucans activate innate immune responses
- Demonstrated antibacterial activity against *Helicobacter pylori*
- Antioxidant properties reduce oxidative stress markers

### Digestive Health
- Protects gastric mucosa from damage
- Anti-ulcer properties demonstrated in animal models
- Traditional use for gastritis and digestive complaints

## Cultivation

### Substrate Preparation
Lion's Mane can be cultivated on supplemented hardwood sawdust or grain spawn. Common substrate recipes:
- 75% hardwood sawdust (oak or beech), 24% wheat bran, 1% gypsum
- Moisture content: 60–65%

### Growing Conditions

| Parameter | Optimal Range |
|-----------|--------------|
| Temperature (colonization) | 20–25°C (68–77°F) |
| Temperature (fruiting) | 16–20°C (61–68°F) |
| Humidity | 85–95% |
| Fresh air exchange | High |
| Light | Indirect, low intensity |
| pH | 5.0–6.0 |

### Timeline
- Spawn run: 14–21 days
- Primordia formation: 3–7 days after cold shock
- Fruit body maturation: 7–14 days
- Total cycle: approximately 4–6 weeks

### Harvesting
Harvest when spines are fully elongated but before they turn brown. Young specimens have the best texture and flavor. Cut at the base with a clean knife.

## Culinary Uses

- Texture similar to crab or lobster meat when cooked
- Popular in vegetarian and vegan cooking as a seafood substitute
- Can be sautéed, grilled, roasted, or made into tempura
- Dried and powdered as a supplement or tea
- Pairs well with butter, garlic, soy sauce, and lemon

| Form | Dose | Frequency |

| Mycelium on grain | Follow manufacturer guidelines | Daily |

## Sustainability and Foraging

- When foraging, never harvest the entire cluster; leave at least 30% for spore dispersal
- Avoid collecting from polluted areas as fungi bioaccumulate heavy metals
- Cultivation is highly sustainable using agricultural waste substrates
- Can be grown on supplemented straw or sawdust from sustainable forestry operations

## Interactions and Precautions

- Generally recognized as safe (GRAS)
- Caution for individuals with bleeding disorders or on anticoagulants (mild antiplatelet activity)
- Rare cases of allergic contact dermatitis from handling
- May interact with diabetes medications due to blood sugar-lowering effects
- Insufficient safety data for pregnancy and breastfeeding — consult a healthcare provider
