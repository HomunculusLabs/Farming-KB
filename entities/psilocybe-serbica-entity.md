# Psilocybe serbica

**Type:** Species (Fungi, Basidiomycota, Agaricales)
**Category:** [[psilocybin]]-containing mushroom
**Also known as:** none widely used

## Overview
Psilocybe serbica is a moderately to highly active psychedelic mushroom
found in Central and Southeastern Europe. Described by Moser and Horak,
this wood-inhabiting species grows on rotting wood and woody debris in
deciduous and mixed forests. It is closely related to Psilocybe
bohemica and P. cyanescens, and is part of a complex of morphologically
similar European bluing Psilocybes that has generated considerable
taxonomic debate.

## Key Details

### Taxonomy
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Hymenogastraceae
- **Genus:** Psilocybe
- **Authority:** Moser and Horak
- **Complex:** Part of the P. cyanescens / P. bohemica / P. serbica
  species complex. Some authorities consider P. bohemica a synonym or
  variety of P. serbica.

### Morphology
- **Cap:** 1-3.5 cm broad, convex to campanulate, often twisted,
  expanding with age to broadly convex to plane. Surface viscid when
  moist from thin gelatinous pellicle, often not separable. Margin
  translucent-striate when moist, soon uplifting and irregular in age,
  bluish tinged or bruising bluish where injured. Reddish brown to
  brownish yellow, hygrophanous, fading to grayish yellow, ochraceous or
  straw colored in drying.
- **Gills:** Adnate to adnexed, purplish brown to chocolate brown with
  whitish margins.
- **Stem:** 20-60 mm long by 1.5-5 mm thick. Equal to slightly enlarged
  towards base, whitish to whitish with splotchy brown patches, bruising
  bluish where injured, covered with white fibrillose patches below.
- **Partial veil:** Finely cortinate, soon disappearing.
- **Spores:** Dark purplish brown in deposit, elongate-ellipsoid in face
  and side view, 10-13 by 5.5-7 um.
- **Basidia:** 4-spored.
- **Pleurocystidia:** Absent or rare, near gill edge, mucronate,
  16-25 by 4.4-7.7 um.
- **Cheilocystidia:** 20-36 by 6-11 um, lageniform with extended necks
  1.5-2.5 um, arising from cells parallel to gill edge.

### Habitat and Distribution
- Growing on rotting wood or in soils rich in woody debris, in deciduous
  or mixed forests.
- Beneath European beech (Fagus silvatica) and/or mixed with firs
  (Abies sp.).
- Range: Yugoslavia (Serbia), Slovakia, and the Czech Republic.
- Fruit body appears in late summer through autumn.
- Often found along river valleys and in mountainous forest regions.

### Chemistry
- Moderately to highly active based on observed bluing reactions.
- Exact chemical analyses are limited in the published literature.
- Expected to contain psilocybin and [[psilocin]] based on strong bluing
  response and phylogenetic placement.

## Relevance
P. serbica is the most well-documented naturally occurring psychedelic
mushroom in Central Europe, making it significant for European
mycology and ethnopharmacology. Its taxonomic relationship with
P. bohemica has been debated, with some researchers considering them
conspecific. The species is closely related to P. cyanescens but is
distinguished by microscopic features, particularly cheilocystidia
morphology. Its presence in European beech-fir forests connects it to
specific forest ecology and conservation concerns, as old-growth
forest management practices directly impact its habitat.
