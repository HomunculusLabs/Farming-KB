## Overview

**N,N-Dimethyltryptamine (DMT)** is a tryptamine-derived psychedelic compound with the molecular formula C₁₂H₁₆N₂. It is a structural analogue of serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine) and melatonin, acting primarily as a non-selective agonist at 5-HT2A receptors.

DMT occurs naturally in over 60 species of plants across at least 8 plant families, as well as in trace amounts in mammalian brain tissue. It is the primary psychoactive component of the traditional Amazonian brew ayahuasca (*Banisteriopsis caapi* admixed with DMT-containing plants).

The compound produces intensely vivid visual and auditory hallucinations, altered sense of time and space, and profound changes in consciousness at doses above 0.2 mg/kg intravenous. The subjective experience is typically brief — 5–15 minutes when smoked or injected — owing to rapid metabolism by monoamine oxidase (MAO).

## Chemical Properties

- **IUPAC name:** N,N-Dimethyl-2-(1H-indol-3-yl)ethan-1-amine
- **Molecular formula:** C₁₂H₁₆N₂
- **Molecular weight:** 188.27 g/mol
- **CAS number:** 61-50-7
- **SMILES:** CNC(C)CC1=CNC2=C1C=CC=C2
- **Appearance:** White to off-white crystalline powder or crystals
- **Melting point:** 44–46 °C
- **Boiling point:** 160 °C (at 0.05 mmHg, decomposes)
- **Solubility:** Freely soluble in water, ethanol, and chloroform; slightly soluble in ether

DMT exists as a free base (lipophilic, volatile) and as a salt (typically fumarate or hydrochloride, water-soluble, stable). The free base is typically vaporized for inhalation, while salt forms are used for oral or intravenous administration.

The compound contains an indole ring structure shared with serotonin, psilocin, and bufotenin, which is responsible for its serotonergic receptor affinity.

## Pharmacology

### Receptor Activity

DMT acts as a non-selective serotonergic receptor agonist with the following pharmacological profile:

- **5-HT2A:** Full agonist (Ki ≈ 0.5 µM) — primary receptor responsible for psychedelic effects
- **5-HT2C:** Full agonist (Ki ≈ 6.0 µM)
- **5-HT1A:** Partial agonist (Ki ≈ 1.0 µM) — may contribute to anxiolytic and antidepressant effects
- **Sigma-1 receptor:** Agonist (Ki ≈ 1.4 µM) — neuroprotective and anti-inflammatory modulation
- **TAAR1:** Agonist — trace amine-associated receptor involved in neuromodulation

### Pharmacokinetics

DMT is rapidly metabolized by monoamine oxidase A (MAO-A) in the liver and peripheral tissues, yielding N-methyltryptamine (NMT) as the primary metabolite, followed by indole-3-acetic acid and tryptophol.

- **Smoked/vaporized:** Onset 15–60 seconds, peak 2–5 minutes, duration 5–15 minutes. Bioavailability ~40–60%.
- **Intravenous:** Onset 10–30 seconds, peak 1–3 minutes, duration 10–15 minutes.
- **Oral (with MAOI):** Onset 20–60 minutes, peak 60–120 minutes, duration 2–6 hours. Oral bioavailability without MAOI is <1% due to first-pass metabolism.
- **Intramuscular:** Onset 2–5 minutes, peak 5–15 minutes, duration 30–60 minutes.

The short duration of smoked/injected DMT is due to rapid hepatic clearance, not receptor dissociation — functional MRI studies show sustained brain activity changes beyond the subjective experience.

## Effects

### Subjective Experience

The DMT experience is characterized by:
- **Visual phenomena:** Intensely colorful geometric patterns, fractals, and "hyperbolic" visual spaces. At higher doses, users report encounter-type experiences with perceived entities or beings.
- **Cognitive changes:** Dissolution of ego boundaries, altered sense of time (time dilation or timelessness), profound insight-type experiences, and emotional release.
- **Somatic effects:** Warmth, tingling, vibration-like sensations throughout the body, changes in perceived body size or boundaries.
- **Auditory phenomena:** Buzzing, humming, or complex soundscapes. Some users report "communication" with perceived entities.

The experience is commonly categorized into dose-dependent levels:
- **Threshold (2–5 mg smoked):** Mild visual enhancement, mood elevation
- **Moderate (10–20 mg smoked):** Vivid closed-eye visuals, altered thought patterns
- **Breakthrough (30–50+ mg smoked):** Complete immersion in an alternative reality, ego dissolution, entity encounters

### Physiological Effects

- Increased heart rate, blood pressure, and body temperature
- Pupillary dilation
- Hyperventilation and changes in respiratory pattern
- No significant toxicity at recreational doses; LD50 in rodents exceeds 110 mg/kg intravenous

## Natural Occurrence

DMT is biosynthesized from tryptophan via the pathway: tryptophan → tryptamine → N-methyltryptamine (NMT) → N,N-dimethyltryptamine (DMT). Two enzymes mediate this: aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase (AADC) and indolethylamine N-methyltransferase (INMT).

### Plant Sources

DMT occurs at concentrations of 0.1–1.0% dry weight in numerous plant species:

- **Leguminosae:** *Mimosa tenuiflora*, *Anadenanthera peregrina*, *Anadenanthera colubrina*
- **Malpighiaceae:** *Banisteriopsis caapi* (trace amounts), *Diplopterys cabrerana*
- **Rubiaceae:** *Psychotria viridis*, *Psychotria carthagenensis*
- **Solanaceae:** *Datura* species (trace amounts)
- **Poaceae:** *Phalaris arundinacea* and *Phalaris brachystachys* (0.05–0.3%)

### Endogenous Occurrence

DMT has been detected in mammalian brain tissue, cerebrospinal fluid, urine, and blood. INMT, the enzyme responsible for DMT biosynthesis, is expressed in the pineal gland, cerebral cortex, and choroid plexus.

The function of endogenous DMT remains debated. Proposed roles include: a neuroregulatory modulator of sigma-1 receptors, a neuroprotective agent against hypoxia, and a potential regulator of REM sleep and near-death experiences. Levels increase significantly under stress, cardiac arrest, and in rodent models of psychedelic states.

## Cultural and Historical Significance

DMT-containing preparations have been used for at least 3,000 years in South American indigenous cultures:

- **Ayahuasca:** The most well-known DMT preparation, combining *Banisteriopsis caapi* (β-carboline MAOI source) with DMT-containing leaves (typically *Psychotria viridis*). The MAOI prevents peripheral DMT degradation, enabling oral activity.
- **Virola snuff:** Indigenous peoples of the Amazon basin prepare DMT-rich resin from *Virola* species bark as a nasal insufflation.
- **Anadenanthera snuff:** *Anadenanthera peregrina* seeds (yopo) are toasted, ground, and snuffed, containing DMT along with 5-MeO-DMT and bufotenin.
- **Epená:** Prepared from *Virola theiodora* resin, used by Waika and other Yanomamö groups.

DMT was first synthesized in 1931 by Canadian chemist Richard Manske and first identified in a natural source (*Mimosa tenuiflora*) by Brazilian chemist Oswaldo Gonçalves de Lima in 1946.

## Legal Status

DMT is classified as a Schedule I controlled substance in the United States under the Controlled Substances Act, with no accepted medical use and high potential for abuse.

International scheduling varies:
- **UN Convention on Psychotropic Substances:** Listed in Schedule I (1971)
- **Brazil:** Legal for religious use in ayahuasca ceremonies (Santo Daime, União do Vegetal)
- **Peru:** Legal for traditional indigenous and religious use
- **Netherlands:** Legal to possess and use, though extraction and sale are restricted
- **Canada:** Schedule III under the Controlled Drugs and Substances Act

Despite Schedule I status, clinical research on DMT has expanded significantly since 2020, with FDA-granted breakthrough therapy designations for psilocybin and MDMA generating interest in the broader tryptamine class.

## Related Compounds

- **5-MeO-DMT:** More potent, shorter-acting tryptamine found in *Bufo alvarius* venom
- **Psilocin (4-HO-DMT):** Active metabolite of psilocybin, longer duration
- **Bufotenin (5-OH-DMT):** Naturally occurring in toad venom and some plants
- **N-Methyltryptamine (NMT):** Biosynthetic precursor with weaker activity
- **Ergoline:** Tryptamine-derived scaffold found in [[ergoline]] alkaloids and [[lysergic acid diethylamide|LSD]]
