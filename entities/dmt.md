# DMT

## Overview

N,N-Dimethyltryptamine (DMT) is a naturally occurring tryptamine alkaloid and one
of the most powerful psychedelic compounds known. It is found in a remarkable
diversity of plant and animal species, including humans, where it is produced
endogenously in the brain and peripheral tissues. DMT is the principal psychoactive
component of ayahuasca, a traditional Amazonian brew, and is also consumed as
pure compound via vaporization, producing extraordinarily intense, brief visionary
experiences.

## Chemical Properties

- **IUPAC Name:** N,N-dimethyl-1H-indole-3-ethanamine
- **Molecular Formula:** C12H16N2
- **Molecular Weight:** 188.27 g/mol
- **CAS Number:** 61-50-7
- **Appearance:** White to yellowish crystalline solid or powder
- **Melting Point:** 44 to 47 degrees Celsius (freebase)
- **Boiling Point:** 160 degrees Celsius (freebase, decomposes)
- **Solubility:** Freebase is soluble in organic solvents; salt forms are water
  soluble

DMT is the simplest N,N-dimethylated tryptamine and serves as the structural parent
of numerous related compounds including [[5-meo-dmt]], [[psilocin]], and
[[bufotenine]]. The freebase form is volatile and can be vaporized without
decomposition at moderate temperatures (approximately 60 to 80 degrees Celsius).

## Natural Occurrence

DMT is one of the most widely distributed psychedelic alkaloids in nature. It has
been identified in over 65 plant species spanning at least 8 plant families,
including Fabaceae (*Mimosa tenuiflora*, *Anadenanthera* spp.), Malpighiaceae
(*Diplopterys cabrerana*), Rubiaceae (*Psychotria viridis*), and Apocynaceae
(*Prestonia amazonica*). It was first chemically characterized in 1931 by the
Canadian chemist Richard Manske, who isolated it from the roots of *Mimosa
tenuiflora* (then known as *Mimosa hostilis*).

In animals, DMT has been detected in mammalian brain tissue, cerebrospinal fluid,
blood, and urine. The discovery of DMT, [[5-meo-dmt]], and [[bufotenine]] in the
human brain by Steven Barker and colleagues at Louisiana State University confirmed
its endogenous status. The functional significance of endogenous DMT remains a
subject of active research, with hypotheses ranging from a role in near-death
experiences and dreaming to a function as a neuromodulator or neuroprotectant.

## Pharmacology

DMT is a potent agonist at serotonin 5-HT2A receptors (Ki approximately 0.484
micromolar), with additional activity at 5-HT1A, 5-HT2C, 5-HT5A, 5-HT6, and 5-HT7
receptors, as well as sigma-1 and TAAR1 receptors. It also acts as a weak
reuptake inhibitor and releasing agent of serotonin, dopamine, and norepinephrine
through interactions with the SERT, DAT, and NET transporters.

DMT is rapidly inactivated by monoamine oxidase (MAO-A) when administered orally,
rendering it inactive by this route. This is why the traditional Amazonian brew
ayahuasca combines DMT-containing plants with MAO-inhibiting plants (typically
*Banisteriopsis caapi* containing harmine, harmaline, and tetrahydroharmine), which
allows the DMT to reach the systemic circulation. When vaporized and inhaled,
onset is nearly instantaneous (15 to 60 seconds), with peak effects at 2 to 5
minutes and total duration of 15 to 30 minutes.

## Effects and Dosage

DMT produces one of the most intense psychedelic experiences of any known substance.
Common effects include:

- Complex, immersive visual hallucinations including geometric patterns, entities,
  and "hyperspace" environments
- Complete dissolution of ego and sense of self
- Alterations in the perception of time (time dilation or apparent cessation)
- Encounters with seemingly autonomous entities or beings
- Profound emotional and mystical experiences
- Synesthesia and multisensory merging

Dosage varies significantly by route of administration. Vaporized or smoked doses
range from 20 to 60 mg of freebase for a full experience. Intramuscular injection
doses are approximately 30 to 60 mg. Oral doses in ayahuasca typically
deliver 30 to 100 mg of DMT in combination with MAO inhibition. The experience
profile is often described using the "breakthrough" model, where doses below
threshold produce mild effects, moderate doses produce intense visual and cognitive
alteration, and "breakthrough" doses produce complete ego dissolution and entry
into apparently alien environments.

## Traditional Use

DMT-containing preparations have been used by indigenous peoples of the Amazon
basin for centuries, possibly millennia. The two primary traditional preparations
are ayahuasca (an orally active brew combining DMT-containing plants with MAO
inhibitors) and various snuffs (rapé, yopo, ebena) prepared from the resin or
seeds of *Virola* and *Anadenanthera* species. These preparations are central to
the shamanic and healing practices of numerous indigenous groups throughout the
Amazon, where they are used for divination, diagnosis and treatment of illness,
communal ceremony, and spiritual development.

## Modern Research

DMT has been the subject of renewed clinical research interest in the 21st century.
Studies at Imperial College London, Johns Hopkins University, and other centers
have investigated DMT for treatment-resistant depression, anxiety associated with
life-threatening illness, and substance use disorders. Research by Rick Strassman
at the University of New Mexico in the 1990s was the first modern clinical
investigation of IV DMT in humans, published in the influential book "DMT: The
Spirit Molecule."

## Legal Status

DMT is classified as a Schedule I controlled substance under the United Nations
Convention on Psychotropic Substances (1971) and is a Schedule I drug in the US.
Despite this, the use of ayahuasca has been granted religious exemptions in
several countries, including the US (Uniao do Vegetal and Santo Daime churches),
Brazil, Peru, and the Netherlands. Natural plant materials containing DMT are not
universally regulated, creating a complex and variable legal landscape.
