# WISALT (West Australian Salt Land Reclamation)

## Description

WISALT (also rendered as W.A.L.S.T. or W.LS.A.L.T. in the text) is a group of Western Australian hill farmers who developed a unique and successful approach to reversing [[interceptor-banks]] — deep contour ditches that halt subsurface water movement and enable the recovery of salted, collapsed soils. [[water-conservation]]

## Key People Associated

Mollison credits several individuals for explaining their approach to land reclamation:

- **Harry Whittington** of Brookton — primary explainer of the interceptor bank approach
- **Gavin Drew** of Beermullah
- **James Gardener** of Bungulla
- **Mac Forsythe** of Kellerberrin
- **Laurie Anderson** of Quairading
- **Terry White** in Victoria — friend who provided constant inspiration and persistence
- **Lex Langridge** — transport and logistics support

## Core Approach

The WISALT method addresses [[soil-collapse]] at its source rather than treating downstream symptoms:

1. **Start at hilltops** — Begin 1–3 m below ridgelines with deep (2 m) interceptor ditches
2. **Contour construction** — Banks follow contour lines precisely
3. **Sealed compaction** — Bulldozer rams excavated subsoil against the downslope bank to create an impermeable barrier
4. **Progressive slope coverage** — Additional banks every 3 m of vertical height down the slope
5. **Natural waterway connection** — Lower banks grade to streams at 1:3000 slope
6. **Aquifer interception** — Shallow aquifers and sand seams are excavated and blocked with clay or plastic barriers

## Results Achieved

- Immediate cessation of overland flow and wind erosion upon construction
- Within 2–3 years: grass regeneration between banks, dying trees recovering
- Within 5–7 years: cash returns from increased livestock and crop yields pay for all earthwork
- Long-term (up to 10 years): full soil recovery and gully elimination

## Geographic Context

Western Australia has suffered extreme deforestation:

- Only **0.7%** of original tree cover remains in Western Australia
- **3%** in South Australia
- **13%** in Victoria
- Salt reserves to 20 m deep measure 3,400–19,000 tonnes per hectare over granite and gneissic areas
- Most salt lies below 2 m depth, below the forest root zone

## Philosophy

The WISALT approach embodies several [[permaculture-principles]]:

- **Problem at source:** Address hilltop water intake rather than valley salt pans
- **Careful observation:** Dig backhoe pits to understand local seepage patterns before designing intervention
- **[[appropriate-technology]]:** Uses bulldozers and simple earthworks rather than expensive pumping systems
- **Economic sustainability:** Farmers can fund the work from increased production returns

## Distinction from Other Approaches

- **Contour banks** (traditional): Shallow (0.5 m), address surface soil loss only, eventually collapse from salt
- **Tile drains and pumping:** Effective for wetland salting but impractical for dryland salting at aquifer scale
- **Tree planting alone:** Fails in already-collapsed soils — trees waterlog and die within 1–2 years
- **WISALT interceptor banks:** Deep, address subsurface water movement, enable subsequent tree establishment

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

Mollison describes the WISALT system as a "unique and successful" solution to soil collapse that he "cannot overstress the importance of, to every country." The approach is directly transferable to any dryland region worldwide where deforestation has led to rising water tables and soil salinization. Permaculture designers should study this method as a primary intervention for degraded dryland sites before attempting any tree planting or crop establishment.
