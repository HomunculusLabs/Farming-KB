The 4-hydroxytryptamine family demonstrates a remarkable pharmacological
consistency: regardless of the specific N-alkyl groups (within the constraint
of six or fewer total carbons on the nitrogen), all members are active in the

N-alkyl groups providing fine-tuning of the qualitative experience.

4-HO-MET is sometimes called "metocin" or "colour" in informal contexts, and
has appeared sporadically on research chemical markets since the mid-2000s.
Its effects are generally described as slightly more colorful and less
introspective than psilocin, with a comparable duration.

## Detailed Pharmacological Discussion

4-HO-MET acts primarily as a 5-HT2A receptor agonist, consistent with the
established mechanism of action for all 4-hydroxytryptamine psychedelics. The
ethyl group on the nitrogen atom slightly increases lipophilicity compared to
psilocin, which could affect both brain penetration kinetics and metabolic
clearance rates.

The compound is metabolized primarily by MAO-mediated oxidative deamination
and by cytochrome P450 enzymes, particularly CYP2D6. The N-ethyl group may
alter metabolic stability relative to psilocin, potentially contributing to
slight differences in duration and character.
