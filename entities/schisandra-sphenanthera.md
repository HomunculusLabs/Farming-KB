The fruit of *S. sphenanthera* contains a distinct lignan profile that differs from *S. chinensis*, with schisandrin A (schisandrol A) as the primary bioactive marker compound rather than schisandrin B. Despite this distinction, the two species have historically been used interchangeably in some TCM formulations, though modern pharmacopoeias treat them as separate drugs.

## Taxonomy

**Kingdom:** Plantae → **Division:** Magnoliophyta → **Class:** Magnoliopsida → **Order:** Austrobaileyales → **Family:** Schisandraceae → **Genus:** *Schisandra* → **Species:** *S. sphenanthera*

- **Chinese Pharmacopoeia designation:** 南五味子 (Nan Wu-wei-zi), distinct from 北五味子 (Bei Wu-wei-zi, *S. chinensis*)

## Morphology

**Vine:** Deciduous woody climber, typically 3–8 m long. Stems are cylindrical, brown to dark brown, with lenticels. Young stems are green and pubescent.

**Leaves:** Alternate, ovate to broadly elliptic, 5–12 cm long and 3–7 cm wide. Leaf margins are finely serrate. The upper surface is dark green and glabrous; the lower surface is lighter green with sparse pubescence along veins.

Petioles are 1–3 cm long, reddish at the base. Leaf arrangement is often in clusters on short spur shoots.

**Flowers:** Unisexual, with male and female flowers on separate plants (dioecious). Flowers are axillary, borne on 1–3 cm pedicels, with 6–9 cream-white to pale yellow tepals.

Female flowers have an apocarpous gynoecium of 12–35 free carpels arranged in a globose head. Blooming occurs in April to June.

**Fruit:** A subglobose aggregate berry, 4–8 mm in diameter, red to dark red at maturity. Each fruit contains 1–2 kidney-shaped seeds. Fruits ripen July to September and hang in dense clusters.

The fruit surface is smooth to slightly wrinkled, with a glossy red appearance when ripe. Taste is characteristically astringent, sweet, sour, bitter, and salty — the "five flavors" referenced in the Chinese name.

**Seeds:** Kidney-shaped, 3–5 mm long, brownish-yellow, with a smooth testa.

## Distribution and Habitat

Native to central and southwestern China, including the provinces of Hubei, Sichuan, Guizhou, Yunnan, Hunan, Guangxi, and Shaanxi. Found in mixed broadleaf forests at elevations of 600–2,000 m.

The species occurs as an understory climber in warm-temperate to subtropical forests, scrambling over shrubs and small trees along forest edges, ravines, and stream corridors.

It prefers partial shade to dappled sunlight, well-drained humus-rich soils, and moderate annual precipitation (800–1,500 mm). Frost tolerance is lower than [[dynamic-accumulator-plants-trace-minerals]].

Total lignan content is typically 3–7% dry weight, with schisandrin A constituting approximately 0.4–1.2%.

**Hepatoprotective activity:** Schisandrin A and related lignans protect hepatocytes from toxin-induced injury through antioxidant mechanisms, enhancement of glutathione levels, and inhibition of lipid peroxidation.

Studies demonstrate protection against carbon tetrachloride, acetaminophen, and alcohol-induced liver damage in animal models.

**Antioxidant effects:** Lignans from *S. sphenanthera* scavenge free radicals and upregulate endogenous antioxidant enzyme systems (superoxide dismutase, catalase, glutathione peroxidase).
