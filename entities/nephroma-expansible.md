# Nephroma expansum

## Overview

**Nephroma expansum**, commonly known as the **broad kidney lichen** or **expanded nephroma**, is a large foliose cyanolichen in the family Nephromataceae. It is recognized by its kidney-shaped lobes and distinctive reticulate vein pattern on the lower surface.

The species is widely distributed across boreal and temperate forests of the Northern Hemisphere. It is particularly associated with old-growth coniferous and mixed forests that maintain high humidity and stable microclimates.

*N. expansum* is ecologically significant as a nitrogen-fixing organism in forest ecosystems. Like other cyanolichens, it contributes to the nitrogen economy of old-growth forest floors.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

*Nephroma expansum* (Pers.) Arnold was originally described by Christiaan Hendrik Persoon and later transferred to *Nephroma* by Ferdinand von Arnold. The species epithet refers to its broadly expanded thallus lobes.

The thallus is large, reaching 10–25 cm in diameter, with broadly rounded, kidney-shaped lobes 2–6 cm wide. The upper surface is dark green to olive-brown when moist, becoming grayish-green to brown when dry.

The most distinctive feature is the lower surface, which has a well-developed network of raised, pale veins. These veins form a reticulate pattern that is characteristic of the genus *Nephroma*.

The lower surface between the veins is pale tan to cream-colored with a sparse tomentum. Rhizines arise from the vein intersections and help anchor the thallus to the substrate.

Apothecia are produced on the lower surface, which is unusual among foliose lichens. They are reddish-brown, 2–5 mm in diameter, and borne on short stalks arising from the vein margins.

The photobiont is the cyanobacterium *Nostoc*, housed in a distinct layer within the upper medulla. This cyanobacterial partnership enables atmospheric [[comparison-nutrient-cycling-vs-nutrient-dense-gardening]] on the forest floor.

*N. expansum* is moderately sensitive to air pollution, particularly sulfur dioxide and [[mycelium-running-mushroom-antimicrobial-properties-species-chart]].

In traditional Chinese medicine, related *Nephroma* species have been used to treat coughs, fever, and inflammation. Preparations typically involve boiling the dried lichen to extract water-soluble polysaccharides.

Modern chemical studies have identified several [[query-what-are-the-main-bioactive-compounds-in-medicinal-plants]] in *Nephroma* species, including nephroarctin and various phenolic acids. These compounds show moderate antimicrobial and antioxidant activity in laboratory assays.

## Chemistry and Secondary Metabolites

*Nephroma expansum* produces a distinctive secondary metabolite profile including nephroarctin, pannarin, and traces of usnic acid. These compounds are concentrated in the cortical and medullary layers.

Nephroarctin is a benzyl ester compound that is relatively rare in lichens outside the Nephromataceae. Its presence is a useful taxonomic character for identifying *Nephroma* species.

Standard spot tests typically yield C+ red to orange reactions from the medulla. K and PD reactions are usually negative or weakly positive, depending on the concentration of medullary compounds.

TLC analysis can reliably distinguish *N. expansum* from *N. arcticum* and *N. laevigatum* based on differences in their secondary metabolite profiles. Chemical identification is essential for accurate species determination.
