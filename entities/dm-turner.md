# D. M. Turner

D. M. Turner (1962–1996) was an American psychonaut and author of *The Essential Psychedelic Guide* (1994, Panther
Press, ISBN 0-9642636-1-0), one of the first comprehensive comparative guides covering multiple psychedelic substances
from a user's perspective. His work covered LSD, psilocybin mushrooms, [[mescaline]], DMT, MDMA, [[2c-b]], ketamine,
and harmala alkaloids with detailed dosage information, effects descriptions, and safety advice. Turner is remembered
both for his significant contributions to the documentation of psychedelic experiences and for his death, which became
a cautionary tale about the risks of solo psychedelic use.

## The Essential Psychedelic Guide

Turner's guide was written to "expand the body of knowledge available on psychedelics" and provided hard-to-find,
accurate data on dosage and administration for a wide range of entheogenic substances. What distinguished Turner's work
was his vivid, lucid narration from the frontier of consciousness itself — his trip reports conveyed the essence of
the psychedelic experience in a manner previously thought impossible using prose. He also presented novel philosophical
thinking about the bizarre, extreme, and sometimes sublime dimensions accessible through entheogens. The guide was
notable for its systematic approach: each substance received a standardized treatment covering dosage ranges for
different experience levels, onset and duration timelines, effects descriptions organized by sensory modality, and
practical advice for maximizing benefit and minimizing risk.

## Approach to Psychedelic Exploration

Turner's methodology was characterized by what might be called radical empiricism — a commitment to exploring the
full range of psychedelic experiences, including the most extreme and challenging territories, and reporting his
findings with precision and honesty. Unlike many psychedelic writers who focused primarily on lower to moderate doses
in supportive settings, Turner routinely explored high-dose experiences, including combinations of substances that most
practitioners would consider inadvisable. This approach yielded unique and valuable data about the phenomenology of
extreme psychedelic states, but it also placed Turner at significantly higher risk. His writing reflects both the
extraordinary insights and the genuine dangers of this approach, making his guide an essential but ambivalent document
in the psychedelic literature.

## CydelikSpace Framework

Turner explored the concept of "CydelikSpace" as a framework for understanding the spaces accessed through ketamine and
DMT experiences. His philosophical underpinnings regarding these visionary dimensions were considered highly original,
placing his work in a class by itself among psychedelic literature. CydelikSpace was conceived as a multi-dimensional
territory accessible through dissociative and tryptamine psychedelics, with distinct "zones" or "levels" characterized
by progressively more abstract and alien experiential content. Turner mapped these territories with the precision of a
cartographer, providing detailed descriptions of each level and the transitions between them. His framework anticipated
later discussions in psychedelic research about "entity encounters," "hyperspace," and the ontology of psychedelic
experiences.

## Ketamine Research and Evolving Views

Turner was one of the earliest psychonauts to conduct systematic explorations of ketamine's psychedelic potential at a
time when the drug was primarily known as a veterinary tranquilizer and anesthetic. His ketamine reports in the
Essential Guide are among the most detailed early accounts of the drug's visionary properties, describing experiences
of ego dissolution, encounters with apparently independent entities, and journeys through what he termed "
CydelikSpace." Over the course of his explorations, Turner's views on ketamine evolved from enthusiasm to increasing
caution. A friend's recollection noted that Turner had been revising his guide and that "his opinion of ketamine had
changed considerably. He was sensitive to safety issues, and was increasingly troubled by what he called the
'psychedelic heroin' properties of ketamine." This evolving perspective — recognizing both the profound experiential
depth and the addiction potential of dissociative anesthetics — was ahead of its time.

## Death and Legacy

On or about December 31, 1996, Turner prepared for what would be his final voyage. He was found dead, drowned in his
bathtub, with a vial of ketamine nearby. It appears he slid beneath the waterline while under ketamine's influence,
leaving himself unconscious in several inches of water. Turner was described as "the Chuck Yeager of psychonauts, a
test pilot's test pilot" — a hard-head who required much higher dosages than most to achieve desired effects. His
death has served as a cautionary tale about the importance of having a sitter present during psychedelic experiences,
particularly with dissociative anesthetics like ketamine that produce profound impairment of motor function and
awareness while maintaining a deceptive surface appearance of coherence.

## Comparative Substance Analysis

One of Turner's most valuable contributions was his systematic comparison of different psychedelic substances. Rather
than treating each compound in isolation, he drew explicit comparisons between substances, noting similarities and
differences in visual character, cognitive effects, body load, emotional tone, and spiritual depth. His comparisons
between DMT and [[5-meo-dmt]], between ketamine and classical psychedelics, and between different dosages of the same
substance provided a framework for understanding the pharmacological landscape that had not previously been available
in print. This comparative approach has influenced subsequent psychedelic literature and harm reduction resources.

## Historical Context

Turner's introduction included a comprehensive history of psychedelics "from the Creation of Gods to the Demise of
Psychedelic Reverence in Modern Times," documenting the trajectory from [[albert-hofmann]]'s discovery of LSD through
the 1960s counterculture to the prohibition era. His work referenced and built upon the contributions of
[[r-gordon-wasson]], [[richard-evans-schultes-updated]], [[alexander-shulgin]], and [[terence-mckenna]]. Turner
positioned himself within this intellectual tradition while extending it through his willingness to explore territories
that previous writers had approached more cautiously.
