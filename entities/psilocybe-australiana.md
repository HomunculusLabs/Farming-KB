## Taxonomy

The species was described by Guzman and Watling as part of their systematic study of Australian agarics. Photographs in Allen's guide are credited to C.J. King of the University of Auckland. The specific epithet *australiana* refers to its Australian origin. It is classified within the genus *Psilocybe* in the section containing wood-inhabiting bluing species.

## Distribution

### Australia
- **New South Wales**: Documented from multiple locations
- **Tasmania**: Recorded from various sites including Mt. Field National Park area

### New Zealand
- **North Island**: Documented from the Auckland area and other North Island locations

## Habitat

*Psilocybe australiana* is found in association with eucalypt forests and woody habitats. Unlike the dung-inhabiting [[cattle-dispersal-of-psychoactive-fungi]]).

## Research Needs

The species remains poorly studied pharmacologically. Accurate potency determinations, alkaloid profiling, and comparison with related species would contribute significantly to understanding the Psilocybe flora of Australasia. The reported high potency warrants further investigation.

## Legal Status

As a psilocybin-containing species, *P. australiana* is controlled under Australian state and federal drug legislation and New Zealand's Misuse of Drugs Act 1975. Collection, possession, and sale are prohibited.

## Foraging and Identification Notes

*Psilocybe australiana* is less commonly encountered by foragers than [[psilocybe-cubensis-potency-variation-by-flush]] ("gold tops"), *P. australiana* is largely unknown outside mycological and specialist entheogen circles. It has not been widely reported in Australian media accounts of psychoactive mushroom use, and its relatively restricted habitat (eucalypt forests) limits its accessibility to the general public. However, among knowledgeable foragers, it is valued for its reportedly high potency.
