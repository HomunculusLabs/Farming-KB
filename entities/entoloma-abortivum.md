## Overview

*Entoloma abortivum*, commonly known as the aborted entoloma or shrimp of
the woods, is an [[armillaria-mellea]]*
(honey fungus), which causes some [[polypore-fungi-eastern-north-america]] and parts of
Europe, fruiting from late summer through autumn.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Classification:** Basidiomycota → Agaricomycetes → Agaricales →
Entolomataceae → *Entoloma* → *E. abortivum*.
Authority: (E.M. Fries ex Schwein.) M.L. Berkeley (1859).

The species produces two distinct morphological forms. Normal fruiting
bodies feature a convex to broadly umbonate cap measuring 3–10 cm in
diameter, with a smooth, silvery-gray to brownish surface that becomes
slightly sticky when wet. Gills are adnate to sinuate, initially white
before turning pinkish as spores mature. The stipe is 4–10 cm tall,
0.8–2 cm thick, solid, and white to pale gray.

Aborted forms are irregular, contorted masses 3–15 cm across, lacking any
discernible cap, gills, or stipe structure. Their surface is white to
pale gray, and the interior is solid and marbled. These aborted forms are
the result of *Armillaria mellea* [[comparison-spore-print-collection-vs-spore-syringe-production]] is pinkish. Spores are angular to ellipsoid, measuring
7.5–10.5 × 6–8 µm, with 5–7 angles, which is characteristic of the
genus *Entoloma*.

## Distribution and Habitat

*E. abortivum* is found across eastern North America from eastern Canada
to the Gulf Coast, and extends westward to the Great Plains. It also
occurs in parts of Europe, though it is less commonly reported there.
