## Penicillium notatum

## Description

*Penicillium notatum* (now classified as *[[penicillium-chrysogenum]]* in modern taxonomy) is a filamentous ascomycete fungus in the family Aspergillaceae. It is one of the most historically significant fungi, being the original source of penicillin, the first widely used antibiotic, discovered by Alexander Fleming in 1928. Beyond its medical importance, *P. notatum* has been studied for its metal uptake physiology and ecological roles.

## Key Facts

- Studies of zinc uptake by *P. notatum* showed that zinc is an essential element for this fungus, with different uptake kinetics at low and high solution concentrations (Starling and Ross, 1991).
- Zinc uptake is competitively inhibited by cadmium and noncompetitively by copper, indicating different interaction mechanisms between metals and fungal physiology.
- Competitive inhibition by Cd suggests that Cd and Zn share the same transport system across the plasma membrane.
- Noncompetitive inhibition by Cu suggests Cu acts on a different aspect of the uptake mechanism, possibly affecting membrane function.
- Belongs to the genus *Penicillium*, which was observed to decrease in abundance under high heavy metal pollution in Swedish forest soils (Rühling and Söderström, 1990).
- The original source of penicillin (Fleming, 1929), revolutionizing medicine.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

*Penicillium notatum* is foundational to both medical [[mycology]] and industrial microbiology. Its metal uptake physiology provides insights into fungal heavy metal resistance mechanisms and bioremediation potential. The competitive/noncompetitive inhibition patterns between Zn, Cd, and Cu are important for understanding how fungi in contaminated environments manage multiple metal stressors. The species is also relevant to food mycology as a common spoilage organism and as a source of enzymes used in food processing.

## Ecological Role

- Ubiquitous soil fungus involved in [[decomposition]] and nutrient cycling
- Source of the antibiotic penicillin, transforming human medicine
- Model organism for studying fungal metal transport and homeostasis
- Common airborne fungus contributing to indoor and outdoor [[spore]] loads
- Some strains produce mycotoxins, relevant to food safety
