## Tubaria furfuracea (Scaly Twiglet)

*Tubaria furfuracea*, commonly known as the Scaly Twiglet or Scurfy Twiglet,
is a small saprotrophic agaric mushroom in the family Tubariaceae. It is
one of the most frequently encountered twig-inhabiting mushrooms in temperate
regions, characteristically fruiting on decaying twigs, small branches, and
[[fungal-wood-decomposition-spatial-variation]].

## Taxonomy

Basidiomycota → Agaricomycetes → Agaricales → Tubariaceae → *Tubaria* →
*T. furfuracea* (Pers.) Gillet (1876).

The species was originally described as *Agaricus furfuraceus* by
Christiaan Hendrik Persoon in 1801. It was transferred to *Tubaria* by
Claude-Casimir Gillet in 1876. The genus name *Tubaria* refers to the
habit of growing on tubes or hollow stems, while the epithet "furfuracea"
means "scurfy" or "bran-like," describing the characteristic surface
texture of the cap. Molecular phylogenetic studies have confirmed the
placement of *Tubaria* within the Tubariaceae, separate from the
Cortinariaceae where it was sometimes historically placed. The genus
contains approximately 20 described species worldwide.

## Morphology

- **Cap:** 1–4 cm diameter, broadly convex becoming plane or slightly
  depressed; surface dry, covered with fine scurfy scales (furfuraceous)
  giving a dusty or mealy appearance; coloration ochre-brown to
  cinnamon-brown, paler at the margin; margin often irregular and
  slightly striate when moist
- **Gills:** Adnate to slightly decurrent, moderately spaced, narrow;
  color pale brown to rust-brown, darkening with age as spores mature;
  edges finely crenulate
- **Stipe:** 3–6 cm × 0.2–0.4 cm, cylindrical, equal, hollow;
  surface fibrillose to scurfy like the cap, pale brown; no annulus
  or ring zone, though a faint cortinal remnant may persist in
  young specimens
- **Flesh:** Thin, pale brownish, watery; odor indistinct or faintly
  farinaceous; taste mild to slightly bitter

Australia and New Zealand. The species fruits from late summer through
autumn, with occasional fruitings in mild winters in maritime climates.

amatoxins identical to those found in [[amanita-phalloides]].
