## Lysimachia punctata

## Overview

Lysimachia punctata, commonly known as dotted loosestrife or yellow loosestrife, is a rhizomatous perennial herb in the family Primulaceae. It is native to central and southeastern Europe and western Asia, and widely cultivated as a garden ornamental.

The species is named for the small dark dots or glands that dot the undersides of its leaves. These glandular punctuations are a distinctive identifying feature that separates it from other yellow-flowered Lysimachia species.

Despite its common name, Lysimachia punctata is not closely related to the true loosestrife genus Lythrum. The shared common name reflects historical confusion between these two wetland plant genera rather than any taxonomic relationship.

Flowers are produced in axillary clusters or short racemes in the upper leaf axils, typically with three to seven flowers per node. Individual flowers are about 2 centimeters across, with five bright yellow petals that are slightly overlapping at the base.

The calyx is deeply five-lobed with reddish margins, and the corolla is cup-shaped with a conspicuous yellow eye. Blooming occurs from June through August, with individual flowers lasting three to five days before petal drop.

The androecium consists of five stamens that do not extend beyond the corolla lobes. The superior ovary is globose and develops into a one-celled capsule after successful fertilization by visiting insects.

Fruit is a small spherical capsule approximately 5 millimeters in diameter containing numerous fine seeds. The capsule turns brown at maturity and splits open to release seeds, which are dispersed by water splashing and animal movement.

The rhizome system consists of thick, fleshy underground stems that branch repeatedly at nodes. New aerial shoots emerge from rhizome buds in spring, while older rhizome segments gradually become woody and decompose.

The genus name Lysimachia commemorates Lysimachus, a companion of Alexander the Great, who was said to have used a plant from this genus to calm a panicked horse. This etymological anecdote has been attached to the genus since antiquity, though the specific identity of the plant remains uncertain.

## Distribution and Habitat

Lysimachia punctata is native to central and southeastern Europe, extending eastward through Turkey and the Caucasus to western Iran. In Europe, its native range includes the Alps, the Carpathians, and the Balkan Peninsula.

The species grows in damp meadows, stream banks, woodland clearings, and roadside ditches at elevations up to 1,500 meters. It prefers moist, nutrient-rich soils in partial shade but tolerates full sun where adequate moisture is available.
