# MDAL (N-Allyl-MDA; 3,4-Methylenedioxy-N-allylamphetamine)

MDAL is a substituted amphetamine compound and the N-allyl derivative of [[mda]], with an allyl (3-carbon unsaturated) group replacing the hydrogen on the nitrogen.

MDAL was synthesized as part of the systematic exploration of N-substituted MDA derivatives.

## Pharmacological Profile

MDAL has shown no significant psychoactive effects at the doses explored.

Dosage: Greater than 180 mg (no central effects observed).

Duration: Unknown.

At 180 mg, the compound was found to not enhance and actually seemed to somewhat antagonize the action of LSD.

## Comparison with MDPR

MDAL was explored as a potential primer for LSD, similar to the role explored for [[mdpr-entity]] at 180 mg was found to be a "rather fantastic enhancer of LSD action," functioning as an inactive compound that potentiates LSD effects.

MDAL at the same weight not only does not enhance LSD, but actually seems to antagonize its action.

The difference between MDAL and MDPR is striking given their structural similarity.

Both compounds have identical carbon atoms, identical oxygen atoms, and an identical nitrogen atom, all in identical positions.

The only difference is that MDAL is C13H18ClNO2 while MDPR is C13H20ClNO2, a difference of just two hydrogen atoms.

MDPR has a saturated N-propyl group while MDAL has an unsaturated N-allyl group.

This demonstrates how dramatically pharmacological properties can change with very minor structural modifications.

## Synthesis

MDAL is synthesized by the reductive amination of 3,4-methylenedioxyphenylacetone with allylamine hydrochloride.

Allylamine hydrochloride is prepared by introducing allylamine into concentrated HCl and removing volatiles.

The reductive amination uses sodium cyanoborohydride in methanol at pH 6, maintained by the addition of HCl as needed.

The reaction proceeds over about 5 days.

After workup, the product is distilled at 90-95 degrees C at 0.2 mm/Hg to give an off-white oil.

The hydrochloride salt melts at 174-176 degrees C.

The proton NMR spectrum confirmed that the allyl group remained intact through the synthesis.
