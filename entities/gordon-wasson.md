# Gordon Wasson

## Description

R. Gordon Wasson (1898–1986) was an American ethnomycologist and banker.

He is credited with discovering the use of [[psilocybin-mushrooms]] in indigenous Mexican ceremonies.

His 1957 *Life* magazine article "Seeking the Magic Mushroom" introduced psilocybin to the Western world.

Wasson's work is listed as recommended reading in *The Psychedelic Experience*.

## Classification

- **Type**: Historical person — ethnomycologist and author
- **Nationality**: American
- **Dates**: 1898–1986
- **Key Work**: *Seeking the Magic Mushroom* (1957, Life magazine)
- **Role**: Discoverer of [[psilocybin-mushroom]] ceremonies in Mexico

## Key Facts

1. R. Gordon Wasson was born in 1898 in Great Falls, Montana.

2. He was a vice president of J.P. Morgan & Company — banking was his professional career.

3. Together with his wife Valentina Pavlovna, he developed the field of ethnomycology.

4. In 1955, Wasson traveled to Huautla de Jiménez, Oaxaca, Mexico, seeking mushroom ceremonies.

5. He became the first outsider to participate in a Mazatec velada (mushroom ceremony).

6. The ceremony was conducted by the Mazatec curandera María Sabina.

7. His 1957 *Life* magazine article "Seeking the Magic Mushroom" was a watershed cultural moment.

8. The article introduced the term "magic mushroom" into the English language.

9. Wasson sent mushroom samples to [[roger-heim]], who identified them as *Psilocybe* species.

10. Heim then sent samples to [[albert-hofmann]] at Sandoz, who isolated and synthesized psilocybin.

## The Discovery

Wasson and his wife Valentina developed a theory that cultures could be divided into "mycophiles" (mushroom-loving) and "mycophobes" (mushroom-fearing).

Their research led them to suspect that mushroom veneration survived in remote areas of Mexico.

In 1955, guided by Mazatec informants, Wasson participated in a nighttime mushroom ceremony.

He described the experience as overwhelming and transformative — encountering visions of "palaces and gardens."

The subsequent *Life* article brought international attention to psilocybin mushrooms and indigenous shamanic practices.

## Impact on Ethnomycology

Wasson co-authored *Mushrooms, Russia and History* (1957) with his wife Valentina.

He later proposed the controversial theory that the Vedic soma was a mushroom (*Amanita muscaria*).

His work *The Wondrous Mushroom* (1980) continued his ethnomycological investigations.

Wasson's discoveries inspired a generation of researchers including Timothy Leary.

## Relevance to Psychedelic Studies

Without Wasson's discovery, the psychedelic movement as we know it would not exist.

His 1957 article directly inspired Timothy Leary to seek out psilocybin mushrooms.

Leary traveled to Mexico in 1960 and had his own transformative experience with psilocybin.

This experience launched Leary's career in psychedelic research, ultimately leading to *The Psychedelic Experience*.

Wasson's work is listed as recommended reading for understanding the cultural and botanical context of psilocybin.

## Relation to Other Entities

- **Timothy Leary**: Inspired by Wasson's discovery to begin psychedelic research.
- **Albert Hofmann**: Isolated and synthesized psilocybin from Wasson's mushroom samples.
- **María Sabina**: The Mazatec curandera who conducted the ceremony Wasson attended.
- **Gary Fisher**: Fellow psychedelic researcher who published dosage guidelines.
- **Alan Watts**: Recommended reading alongside Wasson in *The Psychedelic Experience*.
