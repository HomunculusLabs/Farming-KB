# Silver Iodide

## Description

Silver iodide (AgI) is a chemical compound used as the primary artificial ice nucleus in cloud seeding operations worldwide. Mollison discusses it in the context of regional intervention in the water cycle, comparing its effectiveness to natural biological nucleii produced by forests. While silver iodide has been the standard cloud seeding agent since the 1940s, Mollison reveals that natural organic particles from vegetation — particularly tea leaf litter — can outperform it as rain nucleii.

## Classification

- **Chemical formula:** AgI
- **Crystal structure:** Hexagonal (similar to ice, which enables ice nucleation)
- **Appearance:** Yellow solid
- **Solubility:** Practically insoluble in water
- **Category:** Ice nucleating agent / weather modification chemical

## Key Facts

- Silver iodide requires temperatures of -8 to -10 degrees Celsius to seed clouds effectively
- By comparison, Kenyan organic tea leaf litter causes water to freeze at only -5 degrees Celsius, making it a superior seeding agent
- Eucalyptus grove leaf litter requires -11 degrees Celsius for ice nucleation
- Local indigenous forest litter (Colorado) requires -8 degrees Celsius
- Silver iodide is deployed via aircraft, ground burners, or rockets into cumulus, cumulonimbus, or nimbostratus clouds
- Cloud seeding with silver iodide can increase precipitation by up to 30% initially, averaging 19% in Australia and 17% in Israel over weeks
- Effects spread over very wide areas and secondary effects persist for months


## Cloud Seeding Methods

Silver iodide is introduced into suitable cloud formations through several delivery mechanisms:

- **Aircraft dispersal:** Direct seeding into cloud formations at altitude
- **Ground burners:** Silver iodide is absorbed into coal dust and burned when clouds form on hill crests
- **Ground release:** Passive release systems positioned on strategic ridge lines
- **Rocket delivery:** For targeted cloud formation intervention



## Effectiveness Data

Recent analysis (as of Mollison's writing, citing *Ecos* 45, Spring 1985) showed:

- Initial precipitation increase: up to 30%
- Weekly average increase: 19% (Australia), 17% (Israel)
- Monthly average increase: declining to approximately 8%
- Effects spread across hundreds or thousands of square kilometres
- Secondary effects last for months due to persistent wind-carried nucleii



## Limitations and Risks

- Once initiated, cloud seeding effects cannot quickly be stopped
- Can create excessive rainfall in regions lacking adequate ground storage systems
- Requires pre-existing suitable cloud formations — cannot create rain from clear skies
- Does not address the underlying ecological degradation that reduces natural precipitation
- Must be accompanied by flood retardation basins, oversized swales, sand dams, and water spreading systems



## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

- **Natural alternatives:** Forests produce biological nucleii (pollen, bacteria, organic particles) that are more effective than silver iodide, reinforcing the case for reforestation over technological intervention
- **Design strategy:** Cross-wind tree belts of 12+ meters height create Ekman spirals that add approximately 40% to rainfall — a biological cloud seeding system
- **Cost comparison:** Ground-based biological strategies (forests, tree lines) are cheaper and more sustainable per unit area than ongoing silver iodide campaigns
- **Systems thinking:** Cloud seeding treats symptoms (insufficient rain) rather than causes (deforestation, soil loss, vegetation removal)
- **Integration:** In arid restoration projects, cloud seeding may serve as a bridge technology while biological infrastructure (forests, soil organic matter) is being re-established
