## Description

*Bassia quinquecuspis*, commonly known as Black Roly-poly, is a short-lived perennial shrub native to arid and semi-arid Australia. It is one of several "golem" species — plants that break off at the base when mature and roll across the landscape driven by wind, dispersing spiny seeds as they travel. Mollison describes it as a "defending pioneer" that plays a critical ecological role in protecting degraded soils.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Phylum**: Angiosperms
- **Class**: Eudicots
- **Order**: Caryophyllales
- **Family**: Amaranthaceae (formerly Chenopodiaceae)
- **Genus**: *Bassia*
- **Species**: *B. quinquecuspis*

Note: Some taxonomic authorities place this species in the genus *Sclerolaena*.

## Key Facts

- Short-lived perennial shrub that breaks off from its roots when mature, forming a rolling ball (golem) dispersed by wind.
- Spiny seeds are distributed as the plant rolls across the landscape.
- Typically occurs in dense stands only where overgrazing has degraded the landscape.
- Considered a "noxious weed" by conventional agriculture, though Mollison argues this label reflects ignorance of its [[bloomfield-fungal-fruiting-body-morphology-ecological-function]].
- Several related *Bassia* species (copperburrs) and salt bushes perform similar pioneer roles on scalded soils.

## Ecological Role as Pioneer

Mollison identifies *Bassia quinquecuspis* as a crucial pioneer species that protects soil in degraded landscapes:

- **Soil defence**: The dense, spiny mass protects underlying soil from wind erosion and further degradation.
- **Nurse crop**: Creates sheltered microenvironments under which more palatable or useful species can establish.
- **[[fukuoka-textdoc-composting-critique-futility-prepared-organic-matter]]**: As roly-poly plants accumulate and decompose in depressions, pits, and swales, they contribute organic matter to depleted soils.
- **Seed catch**: Wind-blown plants trap in landscape depressions, concentrating [[savory-soil-organic-matter-and-decomposition-dynamics]] seeds in low points where water also collects.

## Wraiths and Golems Strategy

Mollison categorises wind-dispersed arid plants into two types:

- **Golems**: Substantial rolling balls of whole plants (like *Bassia quinquecuspis*) that physically move across the landscape.
- **Wraiths**: Dry, light, airy panicles that blow as individual seed heads.

Both strategies exploit wind energy for seed dispersal [[mollison-humus-and-organic-matter-in-soils]] transport, effectively harvesting wind to redistribute biological material across the landscape.

## Relevance to Cultivation

- **Pioneer management**: Rather than eradicating roly-poly, land managers can use it as a protective nurse crop for establishing desired species beneath it.
- **Soil restoration indicator**: Dense stands of *Bassia* signal overgrazing but also indicate active natural repair processes underway.
- **Organic matter redistribution**: The wind-driven transport of whole plants naturally concentrates organic matter in swales, pits, and depressions — a process that can be mimicked in designed systems.
- **Rethinking "weeds"**: Mollison"s analysis challenges the categorisation of pioneer species as "noxious," arguing that the real problem is the [[integrated-land-management-and-polyculture-design]] that created the conditions they are attempting to heal.

## Related Species

- **Copperburrs** (*Bassia* spp.): Related species that also pioneer scalded (topsoil-removed) areas.
- **Salt bushes**: Co-pioneers on saline scalds.
- **Tumbleweed** (*Amaranthus albus*): Similar golem strategy in different genus.
