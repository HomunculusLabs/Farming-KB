## Paraberlinia bifoliolata

## Description

Paraberlinia bifoliolata is a tropical tree species in the family Fabaceae
(subfamily Detarioideae) native to the rainforests of Cameroon, Central Africa.
It is an ectomycorrhizal tree species that has been studied for its mycorrhizal
connections to other tropical tree species. Onguene and Kuyper (2002) used P.
bifoliolata seedlings in experiments demonstrating the benefits of mycorrhizal
network connectivity between tropical tree species.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Eukaryota
- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Clade:** Angiosperms, Eudicots, Rosids
- **Order:** Fabales
- **Family:** Fabaceae
- **Subfamily:** Detarioideae
- **Genus:** Paraberlinia
- **Species:** P. bifoliolata

## Key Facts

- **Mycorrhizal network connectivity:** Onguene and Kuyper (2002) demonstrated
  that P. bifoliolata seedlings in contact with the root and mycorrhizal system
  of adult Paraberlinia trees showed significantly higher shoot biomass (2.43g)
  compared to isolated seedlings (1.52g) after 8 months of growth.
- **Cross-species mycorrhizal benefits:** When P. bifoliolata seedlings were
  connected to [[mycorrhizal-networks]] of different adult tree species, all
  combinations showed enhanced growth compared to isolated seedlings: Afzelia
  (1.59g vs 1.16g isolated), Brachystegia (1.48g vs 1.24g), Tetraberlinia
  (2.19g vs 1.78g).
- **Ectomycorrhizal association:** As an ectomycorrhizal species, P.
  bifoliolata forms fungal sheaths around root tips and a [[hartig-net]] within the
  root cortex, facilitating bidirectional nutrient exchange.
- **Cameroon rainforest habitat:** Native to Central African rainforests where
  it co-occurs with other ectomycorrhizal Caesalpinioideae trees including
  Tetraberlinia, Microberlinia, Afzelia, and Brachystegia species.
- **Seedling establishment:** The species demonstrates that mycorrhizal
  connectivity between seedlings and adult trees of both the same and different
  species can significantly enhance seedling growth in tropical forests.
- **Carbon and nutrient transfer:** The enhanced growth of connected seedlings
  suggests active transfer of carbon and nutrients through the mycorrhizal
  network, supplementing photosynthetically derived resources.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Mycorrhizal network research:** P. bifoliolata is a key model species for
  demonstrating that mycorrhizal networks in tropical forests facilitate
  cross-species nutrient transfer and seedling establishment.
- **Tropical forest ecology:** Understanding mycorrhizal connectivity in
  species-rich tropical forests informs models of forest community assembly and
  maintenance of biodiversity.
- **Reforestation:** Results suggest that maintaining mycorrhizal networks
  during reforestation could significantly enhance seedling establishment and
  growth rates in degraded tropical forests.
- **Janzen-Connell refinement:** The species provides evidence that
  mycorrhizal benefits can offset negative density-dependent effects near
  parent trees, refining classical models of tropical forest diversity
  maintenance.

## Ecological Interactions

- Forms ectomycorrhizal associations shared with other tropical legume trees.
- Seedlings benefit from mycorrhizal connections to adult trees of multiple
  species, not just conspecific adults.
- Shoot biomass enhanced by 30-60% when seedlings are connected to adult tree
  mycorrhizal networks compared to isolated seedlings.
- Co-occurs with Afzelia, Brachystegia, Tetraberlinia, and Microberlinia
  species in mixed-species ectomycorrhizal forests of Cameroon.
