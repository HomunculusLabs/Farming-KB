## Description

*Natema* (also written *natemd*) is the Jivaro name for the hallucinogenic brew prepared from *[[banisteriopsis-caapi]]* vine and a closely related second vine, likely another *Banisteriopsis* species. It is the central psychoactive preparation in Jivaro shamanism, consumed by both bewitching and curing shamans to enter the supernatural world that the Jivaro consider the only true reality. The brew is the essential tool without which Jivaro shamanic practice — including the perception and manipulation of *tsentsak* (spirit helpers) — would be impossible.

## Classification

- **Type**: Psychoactive plant preparation / hallucinogenic beverage
- **Base ingredient**: *Banisteriopsis caapi* (Malpighiaceae) vine
- **Admixture**: Second *Banisteriopsis* species (exact identification pending)
- **Active alkaloids**: Harmaline, harmine, d-tetrahydroharmine, possibly N,N-dimethyltryptamine (DMT)
- **Cultural context**: Jivaro Indians, Ecuadorian Amazon
- **Related preparations**: Ayahuasca (Quechua/Peru), yagé/yajé (Colombia), caapi (Brazil), [[nixi-pae]] (Cashinahua)

## Key Facts

- Natema is prepared by boiling segments of *Banisteriopsis caapi* vine with leaves of a similar vine (likely another *Banisteriopsis* species). The resulting tea contains harmaline, harmine, d-tetrahydroharmine, and quite possibly DMT (Agurell, Holmstedt, & Lindgren, 1968; Der Marderosian, 1967; Friedberg, 1965; Poisson, 1965).
- The Jivaro accent falls on the penultimate syllable. A pair of dots over a vowel indicates it is unvoiced.
- Natema is considered essential for shamanic practice: it makes it possible for virtually anyone to achieve the trance state necessary for perceiving the supernatural world and the *tsentsak* that are normally invisible. Without the drug, Jivaro shamanic practice collapses.
- The brew produces dramatic visionary experiences including encounters with *pangi* (the anaconda, transmuted into a crown of gold), *wampang* (the giant butterfly singing with its wings), *makanchi* (a poisonous serpent representing witchcraft-induced illness), and multitudes of spirit helpers appearing as snakes, spiders, birds, bats, and eyes.
- The "sound of rushing water" is a hallmark of the natema experience, signifying that the drinker has acquired the power of *Tsungi*, the first shaman. This auditory phenomenon gives its name to Harner's seminal paper on Jivaro shamanism.
- [[michael-harner]] personally consumed the brew during fieldwork in 1961 with an upper Amazon tribe (not the Jivaro), experiencing bird-headed people, dragon-like creatures claiming to be "the true gods of this world," and attempts to fly through the galaxy — experiences that profoundly altered his understanding of the drug's impact on native ideology.
- Approximately one in four Jivaro men is a shaman, a proportion attributed largely to the accessibility and power of natema in producing shamanic trance states.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- Natema represents one of the simplest traditional ayahuasca-like preparations: primarily *Banisteriopsis caapi* with minimal admixture. This contrasts with multi-plant preparations used by other groups (e.g., Cashinahua adding *[[psychotria-viridis]]*), making the Jivaro tradition relevant to studying the effects of *Banisteriopsis* alone versus synergistic combinations.
- The brew's role in Jivaro culture demonstrates how a single psychoactive plant preparation can sustain an elaborate cosmological and medical system. The entire Jivaro supernatural worldview — the "ordinary world is a lie" philosophy — is constructed around and maintained by the natema experience.
- From a pharmacological perspective, natema provides β-carboline alkaloids (harmine, harmaline, d-tetrahydroharmine) that act as MAO-A inhibitors, potentially rendering endogenous tryptamines psychoactive even without added DMT from admixture plants.
- The natema tradition has contributed significantly to modern understanding of ayahuasca pharmacology and the role of β-carboline-tryptamine synergy in producing visionary experiences.
