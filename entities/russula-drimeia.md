# Russula drimeia (Pungent Russula)

## Description

Russula drimeia is a striking species of Russula with a bright purple or brown-violet cap, citron-yellow to sulphur-yellow gills, and very acrid taste. It is associated with coniferous woods and is considered poisonous when raw.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Russulales
- **Family:** Russulaceae
- **Genus:** Russula
- **Species:** R. drimeia

## Morphology

The cap is bright purple or brown-violet, darker in the middle, and decoloured by rain. It is convex with an incurved margin, then flat or depressed, and slightly viscid. Average cap width is approximately 4 inches; stem height about 3 inches; stem width about 0.75 inch.

The gills are adnate and somewhat crowded, citron-yellow at first then becoming sulphur-yellow. The stem is solid and firm, concolorous with the cap but paler. The flesh is compact, yellowish then white, purplish under the skin. The taste is very acrid; the smell is pleasant.

A variety (Queletii) has white gills instead of the typical yellow.

## Key Facts

- Bright purple or brown-violet cap colouration — distinctive among British Russula
- Gills transition from citron-yellow to sulphur-yellow (variety Queletii has white gills)
- Cap is decoloured by rain, indicating water-soluble pigments
- Flesh is compact, not as brittle as some other Russula species
- Stem is solid and firm (unlike the hollow stems of some acrid species)
- Very acrid taste with pleasant smell
- Associated with coniferous woods
- Grows in autumn
- Variety Queletii has white gills

## Toxicity

Considered poisonous when raw due to the very acrid taste. As with other acrid Russula species, the irritant principle is likely dispersed during cooking, but the species is best avoided. The purple coloration and acrid taste serve as warning characters.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Conifer association:** Unlike the other acrid Russula species described by Ramsbottom, R. drimeia is specifically associated with coniferous woods, suggesting a mycorrhizal relationship with conifers
- **Pigment biochemistry:** The water-soluble purple pigments that wash out in rain are of biochemical interest — this behaviour is shared with some other purple-capped Russula species
- **Gill colour variation:** The existence of variety Queletii with white gills demonstrates the genetic basis for gill pigmentation and the taxonomic challenges in Russula
- **Morphological distinctiveness:** The combination of purple cap + yellow gills + acrid taste makes this one of the more easily identified acrid Russula species
- **Flesh consistency:** The compact, firm flesh contrasts with the typically brittle flesh of many Russula species
- **Taxonomic note:** The species epithet "drimeia" relates to the Greek for "acrid" or "pungent"

## Habitat and Ecology

Grows in coniferous woods in autumn. The specific association with conifers distinguishes it from the beech-associated R. fellea and the more general woodland species R. emetica and R. fragilis. The firm, solid stem may be an adaptation to the often drier conditions of coniferous woodland floors.

## Historical Notes

Described and illustrated by Ramsbottom (1945) on Plate 8 alongside R. emetica, R. fragilis, and R. fellea. Ramsbottom notes the existence of variety Queletii with white gills, named in honour of the French mycologist Lucien Quélet.
