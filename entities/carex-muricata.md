## Overview

**Carex muricata** is a perennial sedge of the family Cyperaceae, native to much of Europe and extending into western Asia. It is commonly known as prickly sedge or rough sedge, referring to the distinctly roughened texture of its utricles.

The species is characteristic of damp meadows, marsh edges, and wet woodland clearings across lowland and montane habitats. It typically forms loose tussocks and flowers from May through July.

Carex muricata belongs to the large genus Carex, which contains approximately 2000 species worldwide. It is a member of Carex section Acutae, a group distinguished by two-stigmate utricles and acute beaks.

The species has been historically important as an indicator of semi-natural wet grassland communities in Europe. Its presence often signals moderately nutrient-rich conditions with seasonal waterlogging.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Carex muricata** L. was described by Carl Linnaeus in 1753 in *Species Plantarum*.

It is a rhizomatous perennial forming loose to moderate tussocks, typically 30 to 80 cm tall. The culms are sharply trigonous and slightly roughened above.

Leaf blades are 2 to 4 mm wide, flat or slightly canaliculate, and shorter than the culms. The ligule is longer than broad, typically 3 to 6 mm.

The inflorescence consists of 3 to 6 lateral female spikes and a single terminal male spike. Female spikes are cylindrical to ovoid, 1 to 3 cm long, loosely flowered, and usually pendulous on long peduncles.

Glumes are ovate to lanceolate, reddish-brown with a green midrib, and shorter than the utricles. Utricles are 3.5 to 5 mm long, broadly ovate, planoconvex, and densely covered with short papillae or tubercles — the source of the epithet "muricata."

The papillose utricle surface is a key diagnostic character separating this species from smooth-utricle relatives such as Carex pairae. This texture is best observed with a hand lens in the field.

The utricle beak is bidentate, with the teeth approximately 1 mm long. Stigmas are two per flower, a key diagnostic character for section Acutae.

The nutlet (achene) is biconvex, ellipsoid, and approximately 2 mm long. It is brown at maturity and fills the utricle body.

Seed dispersal is primarily by hydrochory in waterlogged habitats. Utricles float briefly and can be carried by slow-moving water to new sites.

Viable seed production is moderate, and the species also spreads vegetatively through its creeping rhizome system.

## Distribution and Habitat

Carex muricata is widely distributed across Europe, from the British Isles and Scandinavia south to the Mediterranean basin.

Its range extends eastward through central and eastern Europe into western Russia and the Caucasus region. It has been introduced in parts [[deschampsia-caespitosa]], and Juncus effusus. In fen communities, it may grow alongside Carex acutiformis and [[comparison-soil-protozoa-vs-nematodes-in-nutrient-cycling]] in wet [[cervantes-fertilizer-application-strain-feeding]] favors competitive grasses over sedges.

Abandonment of traditional hay meadow management has also reduced suitable habitat. Without annual cutting, more competitive tall herbs and grasses outcompete Carex muricata.
