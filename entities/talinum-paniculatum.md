## Overview

Talinum paniculatum, commonly known as fameflower, Jewels of Opar, or waterleaf,
is a succulent perennial herb in the family Talinaceae (formerly Portulacaceae).
Native to tropical and subtropical regions of the Americas, it is widely cultivated
and naturalized across Africa, Asia, and the Pacific for its edible leaves and
ornamental flowers.

The species produces clusters of small pink to carmine flowers on tall, airy
panicles, making it a popular garden ornamental. Its tender leaves are consumed
as a nutritious leafy vegetable in many tropical countries, particularly in
West Africa and [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]] through [[dighton-fungal-drought-tolerance-plant-water-relations]] through its succulent leaves and tuberous root system.
It performs best in full sun to partial shade at elevations up to 1,500 meters.

## Nutritional Profile

The leaves are a significant source of vitamins and minerals in traditional diets.
Protein content ranges from 2.5–4.0 g per 100 g fresh weight, which is high
relative to common leafy vegetables.

Vitamin A (as β-carotene) content is approximately 5,000–7,000 µg per 100 g
fresh weight, exceeding that of spinach. Vitamin C content ranges from 60–100
mg per 100 g, comparable to citrus fruits.

Mineral content includes calcium (180–250 mg/100 g), iron (3–5 mg/100 g),
phosphorus (40–60 mg/100 g), and potassium (300–450 mg/100 g). The calcium
bioavailability is enhanced by the low oxalate content relative to many
traditional leafy vegetables.

The tuberous roots contain 15–20% carbohydrate (primarily starch) and can be
consumed as a minor root crop, though they are smaller and less productive
than dedicated [[growing-root-vegetables-guide]].
