## Lactic Acid Bacteria in Composting

## Description

[[lactic-acid-bacteria]] (LAB) are fermentative bacteria used in [[composting]] and soil amendment production, including [[bokashi-composting-and-effective-microorganisms]] (EM) formulations. While they can prevent putrefactive nitrogen loss during initial composting phases, they do not promote [[brassinosteroid-fungal-growth-promotion]] and must be managed carefully.

## Classification

- **Type:** Bacterial functional group
- **Metabolism:** Facultative anaerobic, fermentative
- **Key Genera:** Lactobacillus, Pediococcus, Leuconostoc, Streptococcus
- **Ecological Role:** Fermentative decomposition, pH reduction

## Key Facts

- Not conducive to fungal growth, limiting their value in fungal-dominant composting
- Useful at the START of composting to prevent putrefactive losses of N, S, and P
- After the thermophilic phase, promoting fungi becomes more important
- Indigenous species are more valuable than purchased inocula
- Commercial EM products can start the process but should be replaced with locally adapted inocula
- LAB produce lactic acid which lowers pH, creating conditions less favorable for [[beneficial-fungi-trichoderma-and-mycorrhizae]]

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Over-reliance on LAB-dominated systems can suppress the fungal component essential for soil structure, nutrient retention, and disease protection. The recommended approach: use LAB only initially, then actively promote [[steroidal-regulation-fungal-development-growth]] during maturation. Develop locally adapted inocula over time.

## Relationships

- Related to Effective Microorganisms (EM) products
- Connected to bacterial vs. fungal balance in composting
- Relevant to anaerobic brewing concerns
- Contrast with [[saprophytic-fungi]] as the desired group in mature compost
