# DiPT

**N,N-Diisopropyltryptamine** is an unusual psychedelic tryptamine distinguished by its uniquely auditory-dominant effects — it is one of the very few psychedelics that produces profound auditory distortion with minimal visual or cognitive alteration. First synthesized by [[det]] (N,N-diethyl-
tryptamine), which is Schedule I. In practice, this means prosecution for
possession or sale is possible under analogue drug provisions. In the United
Kingdom, DiPT falls under the Psychoactive Substances Act 2016, making its
production, supply, and importation criminal offenses. Several other countries
including Germany, Japan, and Australia have also enacted controls through
generic tryptamine or psychoactive substance legislation. The compound's
relative obscurity compared to classical psychedelics has kept enforcement
activity limited, but forensic detection methods for DiPT and its metabolites
have been developed and are increasingly available to law enforcement.

## Analogues and Derivatives

Several structural analogues of DiPT have been synthesized and documented:
- **4-HO-DiPT** (a.k.a. "Iprocin"): Adds a 4-hydroxy group, producing more
  traditional visual psychedelic effects alongside the auditory distortions.
  Documented in TIHKAL with higher potency than the parent compound.
- **5-MeO-DiPT** (a.k.a. "Foxy Methoxy"): Adds a 5-methoxy group, producing
  pronounced serotonergic effects including visual disturbances and tactile
  enhancement. Widely encountered as a designer drug in the early 2000s.
- **5,6-MDO-DiPT**: Incorporates a methylenedioxy bridge, with limited
  documentation and unclear pharmacological profile.
These derivatives illustrate how subtle structural modifications to the
tryptamine skeleton can dramatically shift the sensory profile, with the
diisopropyl group consistently biasing effects toward auditory domains.

## Safety

- No deaths attributed to DiPT alone in literature
- Persistent auditory aftereffects are the primary concern — tinnitus-like symptoms reported in some users lasting days to weeks
- Limited toxicological data; standard tryptamine precautions apply
