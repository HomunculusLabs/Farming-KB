# Combination Shovel

## Description

The combination shovel is a versatile digging tool that combines the functions of both a standard shovel and a spade in a single implement. It features a slightly pointed cutting edge with a modestly curved blade — narrow enough to push into uncompacted earth without unusual effort, yet wide enough to move soil effectively. [[steve-solomon]] considers it the single best all-around digging tool for the home vegetable gardener.

## Classification

- **Category:** Garden hand tool
- **Type:** Digging implement (hybrid shovel/spade design)
- **Primary use:** General-purpose garden digging, bed preparation, soil turning
- **Skill level:** Suitable for all gardeners

## Key Facts

- **Design:** Slightly pointed cutting edge on a curved blade that is narrower than a standard shovel but wider than a spade
- **Dual function:** Can perform tasks of both a shovel (moving loose material) and a spade (cutting into compacted earth)
- **Blade width:** Narrow enough to push into soil without excessive upper body strength; a person weighing 100 pounds (45 kg) can manage it
- **Construction quality:** Should be sourced from trade/landscaping suppliers, not discount stores; look for solid handle attachment, good balance, and rolled-over blade top for foot pressure
- **Handle length:** Long-handled (standard) for adults; short-handled versions available but not recommended for regular garden use except by people under 5 feet tall
- **Sharpening:** Ships blunt from manufacturer; must be sharpened before use and periodically maintained with a file
- **Bevel angle:** ~15 degrees for the cutting edge (more acute than a hoe at ~20 degrees)
- **Durability:** A quality shovel can dig all season without dulling unless striking many rocks
- **Solomon recommendation:** "All things being equal, I recommend the combination shovel" as the primary garden digging tool

## Sharpening Procedure

1. Place shovel on edge of porch or ground, blade accessible
2. Press foot on handle behind blade socket to hold still (or have someone hold it)
3. Hold file at 15 degree angle to blade edge
4. Stroke from center outward, alternating sides
5. Maintain consistent angle throughout — do not vary
6. Continue until chisel-like bevel is achieved (may take hundreds of strokes on a new tool)
7. Flip shovel over; make a few light back-side strokes at ~5 degrees to remove wire edge
8. Most cutting occurs within 4 inches (10 cm) of center — focus effort there

## Quality Indicators

- Handle solidly attached to blade socket
- Light and well-balanced yet strong
- Top of blade rolled over for comfortable foot pressure
- Steel that cuts slowly when filed (indicates harder, better steel)
- Brand names used by tradespeople

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Bed preparation:** Essential for preparing garden beds and [[mushroom-cultivation]] beds by hand
- **Soil turning:** Used for double-digging and deep bed preparation methods
- **Compost management:** Effective for turning compost piles and moving finished compost
- **Substrate preparation:** Can be used for mixing bulk substrate materials in [[outdoor-mushroom-cultivation]]
- **No-till alternative:** Hand-digging with a combination shovel avoids the plow pan compaction created by rototillers, preserving deep soil structure that benefits both plant roots and mycelial networks

## Selection Criteria
Choose tools based on your soil type, garden size, and physical
capabilities. Quality construction with durable materials ensures
long service life. The right tool reduces effort and improves
results. Consider handle length and grip comfort for ergonomics.
Test weight and balance before purchasing.

## Maintenance and Care
Regular maintenance extends tool life and improves performance.
Clean tools after each use to prevent rust and disease transfer.
Sharpen cutting edges regularly using appropriate files or stones.
Oil metal parts periodically and store in a dry location.
Replace worn handles before they become safety hazards.

## Proper Technique
Using tools correctly prevents injury and maximizes efficiency.
Let the tool do the work rather than forcing it. Use your body
weight and leg muscles rather than arm strength alone. Take
breaks during extended use and stay hydrated. Alternate between
different tasks to prevent repetitive strain.
