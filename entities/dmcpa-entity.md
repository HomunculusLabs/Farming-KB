# DMCPA (2-(2,5-Dimethoxy-4-methylphenyl)cyclopropylamine)

DMCPA is a cyclopropylamine compound modeled after the structure of [[dom-entity]]. It represents a novel approach to psychedelic phenethylamine design by incorporating a cyclopropane ring. The compound is listed as PIHKAL entry #56.

## Chemical Classification

DMCPA belongs to a unique class of compounds combining phenethylamine and cyclopropylamine structural elements.

It is formally named trans-2-(2,5-dimethoxy-4-methylphenyl)cyclopropylamine.

The compound was modeled directly after the structure of [[dom-entity]], maintaining the 2,5-dimethoxy-4-methyl substitution pattern.

Instead of the typical isopropylamine group found in [[dom-entity]], DMCPA uses a cyclopropylamine ring.

## Pharmacological Profile

DMCPA is an active compound with a dosage range of 15 to 20 milligrams.

The duration of effects is 4 to 8 hours.

The compound is active at the trans-isomer on the racemic mixture; the cis-isomer remains unknown.

The optical isomers have been resolved but have not been compared in man.

## Qualitative Effects

At 10 mg, effects are quite real at one hour but very hard to define.

Nothing remained at four hours, but sleep was filled with bizarre and colorful dreams, suggesting the compound continued working at some level.

At 20 mg, subjects reported lightheadedness that was remarkably localized to the left side of the brain.

The experience was described as flighty, with reminders of the aura preceding a convulsion.

Subjects felt decoupled from their experience and environment.

After about an hour, complete control returned and a plus three level was achieved.

Easy fantasy, erotic content, considerable movement in the visual field, and mild anorexia were noted.

The residual hyperreflexive thinness was largely gone and not worrisome.

Several reports describe muscular tremor, especially in the earliest part of the experience, but this was never a serious concern.

The next day was without any residual effects.

## Relationship to Tranylcypromine

DMCPA is intimately related to tranylcypromine, a clinically proven antidepressant.

Tranylcypromine is a known monoamine oxidase inhibitor (MAOI), and it is possible that DMCPA shares some of this pharmacological property.

The hints of physical toxicity at the higher doses assayed might suggest MAOI activity.

The 2,5-dimethoxy-4-methyl substitution pattern was deliberately chosen to mirror [[dom-entity]].

A related compound, 3,4,5-trimethoxytranylcypromine (TMT), was evaluated at 13 milligrams orally with no hints of central activity.

## Synthesis

The synthesis of DMCPA begins with 2,5-dimethoxy-4-methylbenzaldehyde (prepared as described in the [[2c-d-entity]] recipe).

This is condensed with malonic acid via a Knoevenagel reaction to give 2,5-dimethoxy-4-methylcinnamic acid.

The cinnamic acid is converted to its tert-butyl ester and then subjected to a cyclopropanation using dimethylsulfoxonium methylide.

Hydrolysis gives the trans-cyclopropanecarboxylic acid.

The carboxylic acid is converted through a Curtius rearrangement (via azide and isocyanate intermediates) to the carbobenzoxy-protected amine.

Hydrogenolysis removes the protecting group to yield DMCPA as the hydrochloride salt with a melting point of 210-211 degrees C.

## Structural Innovation

DMCPA represents a novel approach to psychedelic drug design by replacing the flexible isopropylamine side chain of [[dom-entity]] with a rigid cyclopropane ring.

The cyclopropane ring constrains the geometry of the amine relative to the aromatic ring, which may influence receptor binding.

This approach explores whether the psychoactive properties of DOM can be preserved or modified by restricting the conformational flexibility of the side chain.

## Dosage and Duration

Dosage: 15 - 20 mg.

Duration: 4 - 8 h.

Active as the racemic trans-isomer.

## Safety and Toxicology

Muscular tremor was reported at higher doses, particularly in the early phase.

The possible MAOI activity of DMCPA raises potential safety concerns regarding dietary restrictions and drug interactions.

Limited human trials make comprehensive safety assessment difficult.
