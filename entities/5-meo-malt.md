## Overview

**5-MeO-MALT** (5-methoxy-N-methyl-N-allyltryptamine) is a novel synthetic tryptamine in the 5-MeO substituted family. It belongs to the broader class of N,N-dialkylated tryptamines that act as serotonin receptor agonists.

The compound is a structural analog of 5-MeO-DMT, differing in the replacement of one N-methyl group with an N-allyl substituent. This modification alters the compound's receptor binding profile and duration of action.

5-MeO-MALT emerged in the research chemical market in the late 2010s. It is valued in psychonaut communities for its short-duration psychedelic effects.

The compound remains relatively obscure compared to 5-MeO-DMT and 5-MeO-MiPT. Limited published research exists on its pharmacology, toxicology, or human effects.

5-MeO-MALT is sometimes referred to by the colloquial abbreviation "MALT" in online communities. This abbreviation should not be confused with the unrelated chemical maltol used in food flavoring.

## Chemical Properties

The IUPAC name of 5-MeO-MALT is N-[2-(5-methoxy-1H-indol-3-yl)ethyl]-N-methylprop-2-en-1-amine. Its molecular formula is C15H20N2O.

The molecular weight is 244.33 g/mol. The compound features a 5-methoxy indole core with a dialkylated terminal amine.

The allyl group on the nitrogen distinguishes it from the simpler 5-MeO-DMT. This structural feature increases lipophilicity and may affect metabolic stability.

The freebase form is typically a light-colored oil or waxy solid at room temperature. The hydrochloride salt is a white crystalline powder.

The compound is soluble in ethanol, DMSO, and other organic solvents. It is sparingly soluble in water as the freebase.

Melting point data has not been widely published for either the freebase or salt forms. Stability studies under various storage conditions are also lacking in the literature.

The compound's UV absorption spectrum is characteristic of 5-methoxy indole derivatives, with a peak near 315 nm. This property can be used for analytical detection via HPLC-UV methods.

## Pharmacology

5-MeO-MALT is presumed to act primarily as an agonist at the 5-HT2A receptor, similar to other 5-methoxy tryptamines. Full receptor binding profiles have not been published in peer-reviewed literature.

Based on structural analogy with 5-MeO-DMT and 5-MeO-MiPT, it likely also has affinity for 5-HT1A, 5-HT2C, and possibly sigma-1 receptors. The relative potency at each receptor subtype remains uncharacterized.

The N-allyl substitution may confer resistance to monoamine oxidase (MAO) degradation compared to 5-MeO-DMT. This could result in a longer duration of action when taken orally.

No formal dose-response studies have been conducted. Anecdotal reports suggest oral activity in the 10–30 mg range without an MAOI.

Metabolism is presumed to follow pathways similar to other dialkyl tryptamines. Hepatic CYP450 enzymes likely mediate oxidative deamination and N-dealkylation.

The exact metabolic fate of the N-allyl group has not been determined experimentally. It may undergo oxidation to form an epoxide intermediate, though this pathway remains speculative.

No controlled pharmacokinetic studies have been conducted in humans or animal models. All dosing information derives from uncontrolled self-reports in online forums.

## Effects

User reports describe 5-MeO-MALT as producing a gentle, warm psychedelic experience. Visual effects are typically mild, with color enhancement and subtle patterning.

The mental state is described as contemplative, euphoric, and mildly introspective. Many users report a warm body high accompanied by emotional openness.

Effects are reported to last 3–5 hours when taken orally. This is notably longer than 5-MeO-DMT, which typically lasts 15–45 minutes.

Onset occurs within 30–60 minutes of oral ingestion. Peak effects are reached around the 1.5–2 hour mark.

Common side effects include mild nausea during onset, elevated heart rate, and pupil dilation. Anxiety and confusion are reported at higher doses.

The compound is considered to have a favorable safety profile relative to other research chemicals. However, the absence of toxicological data means risks remain poorly characterized.

Long-term effects of 5-MeO-MALT use have not been studied. As with all novel psychoactive substances, caution and harm reduction practices are strongly advised.

No deaths have been directly attributed to 5-MeO-MALT in the published medical literature. However, the compound's novelty means adverse event reporting remains incomplete.

## Legal Status

5-MeO-MALT is not specifically scheduled under the UN Convention on Psychotropic Substances. Its legal status varies by jurisdiction.

In the United States, it may be prosecutable under the Federal Analog Act as an analog of 5-MeO-DMT, a Schedule I substance. Prosecution under this statute requires demonstration of human consumption intent.

Several European countries have enacted blanket bans on tryptamine derivatives that would encompass 5-MeO-MALT. The United Kingdom's Psychoactive Substances Act 2016 effectively prohibits its sale and supply.

In jurisdictions without specific legislation, it exists in a legal gray area typical of novel research chemicals. Users and researchers should verify local regulations before acquisition or use.

The rapid pace of novel tryptamine synthesis has outpaced regulatory frameworks worldwide. Many countries have responded with blanket generic legislation covering entire chemical families.

## Related Compounds

5-MeO-MALT is part of a growing family of N-allyl substituted tryptamines. The simpler analog 5-MeO-DALT (without the N-methyl group) was documented earlier and has more anecdotal reports.

5-MeO-MiPT ("moxy") is the N-methyl-N-isopropyl analog and is better characterized pharmacologically. It shares the warm, gentle quality attributed to 5-MeO-MALT but with somewhat different dosing parameters.

5-MeO-DMT remains the most potent and well-known member of the 5-methoxy tryptamine family. Its extreme potency and short duration contrast with the milder, longer-lasting profile of 5-MeO-MALT.

Other research chemical tryptamines including 4-HO-MET and 4-AcO-DMT have more established user communities. These compounds tend to produce more visually oriented experiences than the 5-MeO series.

The systematic exploration of N-substituted tryptamine analogs was pioneered by Alexander Shulgin. His TIHKAL publication documented many foundational compounds in this chemical space.
