## Description

Lagochilus inebrians, commonly known as Turkestan Mint, is a small shrub native to the dry steppes of Turkestan in Central Asia. For centuries it has served as a source of intoxication among the Tajik, Tartar, Turkoman, and Uzbek tribesmen. It is valued both as a folk medicine and as a mild hallucinogen.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Family**: Lamiaceae (Labiatae) - Mint family
- **Genus**: Lagochilus
- **Species**: L. inebrians
- **Common names**: Turkestan Mint, intoxicating sage

## Key Facts

- Small shrub of the dry steppes of Turkestan (Central Asia)
- Leaves are gathered in October, toasted, and sometimes mixed with stems, fruits, and flowers
- [[kava-traditional-preparation-ceremonial-context]] methods in activating or enhancing psychoactive properties
- Demonstrates Central Asian ethnobotanical traditions parallel to mushroom-based practices
