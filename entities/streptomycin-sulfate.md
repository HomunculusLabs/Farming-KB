## Streptomycin Sulfate

## Description

Streptomycin sulfate is an antibiotic compound used in [[mushroom-cultivation]] as a component of antibiotic agar formulations for purifying bacterial-contaminated cultures. It is added to [[malt-extract-agar]] after autoclaving and cooling to target and eliminate bacterial contaminants while allowing fungal mycelium to grow through the medium. In the [[antibiotic-sandwich]] technique, streptomycin-laced agar prevents bacteria from accompanying mycelium through the gel, effectively separating clean mycelium from bacterial contamination.

## Classification

- **Category**: Chemical agent
- **Type**: Antibiotic / laboratory reagent
- **Chemical class**: Aminoglycoside antibiotic
- **Source**: Produced by [[streptomyces-griseus]]

## Key Facts

- Used at 0.01 grams per 1,000 ml of antibiotic agar
- Added after autoclaving, once the medium has cooled to 113-122°F (45-50°C) — heat-liable
- Part of the antibiotic agar formula recommended by Dr. Bob Blanchette (University of Minnesota)
- Full formula per 1,000 ml: malt extract (15 g), agar (15 g), yeast extract (2 g), Benlate 50% WP (0.06 g), streptomycin sulfate (0.01 g), lactic acid (2 ml)
- Effective against a broad range of gram-negative and some gram-positive bacteria
- Works by inhibiting bacterial protein synthesis (binds to 30S ribosomal subunit)
- Does not affect fungal cells, which have 80S ribosomes

## Relevance to Cultivation

Streptomycin sulfate is a critical tool for rescuing mushroom cultures from bacterial contamination. In the [[antibiotic-agar-sandwich]] technique, contaminated mycelium is placed on the agar and covered with a wedge of the same medium. The mycelium drills through the agar, but bacteria cannot survive passage through the streptomycin-laced gel. The purified mycelium emerges on the outside of the agar layers and can be transferred to fresh antibiotic-free plates. After 1-2 transfers through streptomycin agar, cultures are returned to normal medium.

## Relevance to Mycology

Streptomycin was the first antibiotic effective against tuberculosis and has been historically significant in both medicine and laboratory science. In mycology, it enables the selective isolation and purification of fungal cultures from mixed microbial communities. The differential sensitivity of bacterial (70S) versus fungal (80S) ribosomes to aminoglycoside antibiotics is a fundamental principle exploited in mycological media design.

## Safety Note

Streptomycin can cause allergic reactions and is toxic to kidneys and inner ear with prolonged exposure. Handle with appropriate laboratory precautions including gloves and eye protection. Dispose of antibiotic-containing media according to institutional safety protocols.
