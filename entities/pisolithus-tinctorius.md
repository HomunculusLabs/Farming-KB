# Pisolithus tinctorius

**Type:** Species (Fungus)
**Category:** Ectomycorrhizal Basidiomycete
**Also known as:** Dyeball, Earthball, [[pisolithus-arhizus]]

## Overview
Pisolithus tinctorius is an ectomycorrhizal basidiomycete fungus widely
used in commercial forestry inoculants for reforestation and mine
reclamation. It forms associations with a broad range of tree species and
is particularly valued for its tolerance of stressed, degraded, and
acidic soils. The fungus produces distinctive club-shaped fruiting bodies
filled with powdery spore masses.

## Taxonomy and Classification
- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Boletales
- **Family:** Pisolithaceae
- **Genus:** Pisolithus
- **Species:** P. tinctorius

## Key Details

### Morphology
- Fruiting body is club-shaped to pear-shaped, 3 to 10 cm tall
- Outer surface is rough and scaly, yellow-brown to brown
- Interior is filled with powdery, rust-colored spore masses
  (peridioles) at maturity
- Does not have a typical mushroom cap and stem structure
- Spores are spherical, echinulate (spiny), and brown

### Host Range
- Associates with pine, eucalyptus, oak, birch, and many other trees
- Particularly effective with Pinus and Eucalyptus species
- One of the most widely used ectomycorrhizal inoculants in commercial
  forestry, especially in the southeastern United States

### Nutrient Mobilization
- Produces oxalate in the presence of mica minerals (phlogopite),
  facilitating mineral weathering (Paris et al., 1996)
- Oxalate production is greater with ammonium nitrogen than nitrate
  nitrogen
- Variable phosphate solubilization capacity among isolates, ranging
  from 0 to 100 units depending on the isolate (Lapeyrie et al., 1991)
- Isolates maintained longer on agar show enhanced organic nitrogen
  utilization (Anderson et al., 2001)

### Heavy Metal Tolerance and Detoxification
- Among the most tolerant ectomycorrhizal species to heavy metals
- 50% inhibition concentrations (mg/L): Al 200, Fe 400, Cu 200, Zn 200,
  Ni 10, Cd 10, Pb 200, Hg 1 (Tam, 1995)
- Cadmium and other heavy metals are complexed to polysaccharides and
  cysteine-rich proteins in the outer pigmented layer of the cell wall
  (Turneau et al., 1994)
- Metals are also sequestered in vacuoles as phosphate complexes
  (Turneau et al., 1993)
- Provides heavy metal detoxification for host plants

### Mycorrhizal Efficiency
- Infection rates of 69.5% on pine seedlings (Rousseau et al., 1994)
- Produces rhizomorphs: 0.36 m per gram of soil, facilitating
  long-distance nutrient transport
- Total fungal area: 47.4 mm2/g soil; total fungal length: 6.78 m/g
  soil in association with pine
- Effective at enhancing phosphorus uptake in low-phosphorus soils

### Ecological Role
- Considered an early-stage to mid-stage successional ectomycorrhizal
  fungus
- Commonly found in disturbed sites, mine tailings, and burned areas
- Readily colonizes nursery seedlings and outplanted trees

## Relevance
Pisolithus tinctorius is the most commercially important ectomycorrhizal
fungus for reforestation and mine reclamation worldwide. Its tolerance of
acidic, heavy-metal-contaminated, and nutrient-poor soils makes it
indispensable for ecological restoration. In permaculture, it serves as a
model for selecting fungal partners that improve tree survival and growth
on degraded lands without chemical amendments.
