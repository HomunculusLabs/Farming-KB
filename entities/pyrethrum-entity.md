# Pyrethrum (Tanacetum cinerariifolium)

## Description

Pyrethrum, now formally classified as Tanacetum cinerariifolium (formerly
Chrysanthemum cinerariifolium), is a perennial daisy native to the
Dalmatian region of Croatia. It is the primary natural source of
pyrethrin insecticides—potent neurotoxins to insects that are among the
safest insecticides for mammals and birds. [[permaculture]] animal
management systems.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Order:** Asterales
- **Family:** Asteraceae
- **Genus:** Tanacetum
- **Species:** T. cinerariifolium
- **Former names:** Chrysanthemum cinerariifolium, Pyrethrum cinerariifolium

## Key Facts

- Perennial herbaceous plant, 30-60 cm tall with grey-green divided foliage
- White daisy-like flowers with yellow centres, 3-5 cm diameter
- Flower heads contain the highest concentration of active pyrethrin compounds
- Six related pyrethrin compounds (pyrethrins I-VI) act as contact insecticides
- Non-persistent in the environment—degrades rapidly in sunlight (UV breakdown)
- Extremely low mammalian toxicity compared to synthetic alternatives
- Deadly to fish and aquatic invertebrates—use with caution near waterways
- Commercial pyrethrum production centred in Kenya, Tasmania, and Rwanda
- Dried flower powder has been used as insecticide for over 2000 years
- Basis for synthetic pyrethroid class of commercial insecticides

## Relevance to Cultivation

### Livestock Parasite Control

Mollison recommends pyrethrum oil as one of three insecticidal oils (along
with neem and [[companion-planting]]:** Repels pest insects from nearby vegetables
- **Income crop:** Dried flowers are a saleable commodity

### Cultivation Notes

- Prefers well-drained, alkaline to neutral soils in full sun
- Hardy in USDA zones 4-9; tolerates frost and dry conditions
- Propagate by division or seed; division maintains pyrethrin quality
- Plant 30-40 cm apart in rows for commercial production
- Flowers appear in second year from seed; peak production years 3-5
- Requires good air circulation to prevent fungal diseases
- Harvest on dry, sunny days when flowers are fully open

### Companion Integration

In Mollison's parasite management system, pyrethrum oil complements neem
and Melia azedarach oils. Using multiple insecticidal compounds reduces
the risk of parasites developing resistance—a principle that mirrors
resistance management in conventional agriculture.

## Summary

Pyrethrum is the world's most important natural insecticide plant, and
Mollison integrates it into permaculture animal management through
self-administering pig rubbing posts. Its rapid environmental breakdown,
low mammalian toxicity, and on-farm producibility make it an ideal
pest management tool for self-reliant permaculture systems.
