# Psilocybe thrausta

**Psilocybe thrausta** is a little-known [[psilocybin-compound]]-containing mushroom species originally described from Southeast Asian collections. It is a rarely collected species with limited documentation in the mycological literature.

## Taxonomy

- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Hymenogastraceae
- **Genus:** Psilocybe
- **Etymology:** From Greek *thraustos* (fragile/fractile), referring to the delicate nature of the basidiocarp.

## Description

### Cap (Pileus)
- Small, typically 5–20 mm in diameter
- Convex to campanulate, sometimes with a slight papilla
- Surface smooth, slightly viscid when wet
- Color reddish-brown to ochraceous-brown, hygrophanous
- Margin often lighter, sometimes striate at maturity

### Gills (Lamellae)
- Adnexed to adnate
- Pale grayish when young, darkening to dark purplish-brown with [[spore]] maturity
- Moderately spaced, with lamellulae in tiers

### Stipe (Stem)
- Slender, cylindrical, fragile
- Surface smooth to finely pruinose above, more fibrillose below
- Pallid to brownish
- Partial veil evanescent, sometimes leaving a fibrillose annular zone
- Bruises bluish when damaged, though the reaction may be slow or faint

### Spores
- [[spore-print]] dark purplish-brown
- Spores broadly ellipsoid to subamygdaliform
- Wall thickness moderate, with a visible germ pore
- Approximately 9–12 × 6–7.5 µm

## Distribution and Habitat

P. thrausta has been documented from tropical and subtropical regions of Southeast Asia, including collections from Thailand, Malaysia, and surrounding areas. It fruits on decaying plant material, including grasses and herbaceous debris, often in disturbed or agricultural environments. The species appears to favor warm, humid conditions and is typically found during monsoon or rainy seasons.

## Chemistry

Psilocybin and [[psilocin]] are present, consistent with the genus. The blue bruising reaction, while sometimes subtle, confirms the presence of these compounds. Quantitative data on alkaloid content is not available in published literature.

## Similar Species

The small size and reddish-brown cap coloration could lead to confusion with numerous small brown mushrooms, including inactive species of Psathyrella, Galerina, and other Psilocybe species. Microscopic features, particularly spore size and cheilocystidia morphology, are essential for accurate identification. P. thrausta is differentiated by its fragile stature, specific spore dimensions, and its Southeast Asian distribution.

## Notes

P. thrausta is one of the many understudied Psilocybe species from Asian tropical regions. The scarcity of collections and the lack of modern molecular data mean its phylogenetic relationships within the genus are poorly resolved. Further fieldwork in Southeast Asian mycoflora surveys would be valuable for better characterizing this species.
