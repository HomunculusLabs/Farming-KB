## Overview

**Dicksonia antarctica**, commonly known as **soft tree fern**, **man fern**, or **Tasmanian tree fern**, is a slow-growing tree fern in the family Dicksoniaceae. Endemic to southeastern Australia and Tasmania, it is the most cold-hardy tree fern in cultivation and a defining feature of temperate rainforests in the region.

The species produces a thick, fibrous trunk up to 15 m tall, though 3–8 m is typical in cultivated specimens. The crown bears a rosette of large, deeply divided fronds 2–5 m long that create a distinctive canopy.

Unlike the Australian tree fern (*[[guzman-allen-gartz-africa-southern-hemisphere-underexplored-neurotropic-fungi]]. *Dicksonia antarctica* is the largest and most southerly species in the genus.

The bivalve indusium is a key diagnostic feature separating Dicksoniaceae from Cyatheaceae. In *Cyathea*, the indusium is absent or cup-shaped rather than two-valved.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Dicksonia antarctica* is native to southeastern Australia, occurring in Victoria, New South Wales, southeastern Queensland, and Tasmania. It is a dominant component of cool temperate rainforests throughout this range.

It grows in moist, sheltered gullies, along stream banks, and on south-facing slopes. The species requires consistent moisture and high humidity.

Altitudinal range extends from near sea level to approximately 1,200 m in the Victorian highlands. At higher elevations, plants are shorter and more compact.

The species thrives in deep, organic soils derived from volcanic or sedimentary parent material. It tolerates a wide pH range from moderately acidic to near-neutral.

Annual rainfall in its native habitat ranges from 900 to 3,000 mm. The species relies on consistent moisture, including regular fog and mist at higher elevations.

In Tasmanian temperate rainforests, *Dicksonia antarctica* co-occurs with *Nothofagus cunninghamii*, *Atherosperma moschatum*, and *[[query-how-to-protect-plants-from-frost-and-extend-the-growing-season]].

The species is relatively pest-free in cultivation. Snails may damage young fronds in damp conditions.

In UK and European gardens, the species has become a popular focal point for jungle-style plantings. Specimens are frequently imported from Australian nurseries under CITES certification.

The trunk can be used as a [[cervantes-rockwool-growing-substrate]] for epiphytic plants. In cultivation, gardeners often plant *Asplenium*, *Hymenophyllum*, and mosses directly into the fibrous trunk surface.

## Conservation

*Dicksonia antarctica* is protected under Australian state legislation. Commercial harvesting is regulated through licensing systems in Tasmania and Victoria.
