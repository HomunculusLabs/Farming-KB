# Hypholoma popperianum

**Hypholoma popperianum** is a [[neurotropic-fungi]] cataloged by Guzmán,
Allen, and Gartz (2000). Originally described in the genus Psilocybe, it was later
transferred to Hypholoma based on taxonomic revisions reflecting its morphological
affinities. The species is found in neotropical and subtropical regions and grows on
decaying wood and [[fungal-succession-woody-debris-ecology]].

## Taxonomy and Nomenclature

*Hypholoma popperianum* was first described by the German-born mycologist Rolf Singer in 1973,
originally as *Psilocybe popperiana*. Singer, one of the most prolific taxonomists of
Agaricales in the 20th century, described numerous Psilocybe species from tropical
America during his extensive fieldwork. The species was later elevated to *Hypholoma
popperianum* in subsequent taxonomic revisions by Gastón Guzmán and other mycologists who
recognized its morphological affinity with the genus Hypholoma.

The transfer from Psilocybe to Hypholoma reflects the species' morphological
characteristics, particularly its wood-inhabiting habit, adnate gills, and certain microscopic
features that align more closely with Hypholoma than with the core [[psilocybin]] species that develop prominent blue bruising).
The odor is mild, sometimes slightly farinaceous (mealy or flour-like). The taste is mild
and not distinctive.

### Microscopic Features

The spore print is dark purplish-brown to dark brown, typical of the Strophariaceae.
Basidiospores are ellipsoid to slightly amygdaloid (almond-shaped) or broadly ellipsoid
in face view, and slightly inequilateral (asymmetric) in side view. They measure
approximately 10 to 13 μm long by 6 to 8 μm wide, which is slightly larger than the
average for many Psilocybe species. The spores have a distinct germ pore at the apical
end and a smooth, moderately thick wall. The spore wall is not ornamented and is
non-amyloid.

Cheilocystidia (sterile cells on the gill edges) are present and typically vary in shape,
ranging from ventricose (swollen) to lageniform (flask-shaped) with a narrow neck. They
measure approximately 15 to 30 by 4 to 8 μm. Pleurocystidia (sterile cells on the gill
faces) may be present but are less common. The pileipellis (cap cuticle) is a thin
ixocutis — a gelatinous layer of radially arranged, interwoven hyphae — which is
responsible for the viscid appearance of the cap when moist. Clamp connections are
present at the septa of hyphae.

## Distribution and Habitat

*H. popperianum* is a neotropical to subtropical species with a distribution centered in
Central America, the Caribbean, and northern South America. Documented collections include
Mexico, Guatemala, Costa Rica, Panama, Colombia, Venezuela, and various Caribbean islands.
The species may also occur in parts of Brazil and the Amazon basin, though records from
these areas are less well documented.

The species grows on decaying wood, fallen branches, and woody debris in humid forests. It
has been found in both primary (old-growth) and secondary (regrowth) tropical and subtropical
forests, often at low to mid-elevations (sea level to approximately 1500 meters). It shows
a preference for relatively hard, slow-decaying wood of broadleaf trees, though the specific
host tree species are not well documented. The mushrooms typically fruit in small clusters
or scattered groups on the same piece of wood, reflecting the saprotrophic mycelium's
colonization of the substrate.

Fruiting typically occurs during the rainy season when humidity is high and temperatures
are warm (20 to 30°C).

## Chemistry

*Hypholoma popperianum* contains [[psilocin]] as its principal psychoactive
alkaloids, consistent with its classification among psilocybin-containing [[challenges-neurotropic-fungi-chemical-analysis-taxonomic-identification]].
The presence of these compounds has been confirmed by chemical analysis.

The exact concentrations of [[psilocin]] in *H. popperianum* have not been
extensively studied or published in the quantitative literature. The species is reported to
be psychoactive, suggesting that alkaloid concentrations are sufficient to produce perceptible
effects upon ingestion. As with other wood-inhabiting psilocybian species, the alkaloid content
may vary depending on the substrate (wood species, degree of decomposition), environmental
conditions (temperature, humidity, light exposure), geographic origin, and the age of the
[[comparison-fruiting-body-vs-mycelium-extracts-medicinal-mushrooms]] at harvest.

Some species in the genus Hypholoma and related genera may also contain [[norbaeocystin]] as minor alkaloid constituents, though specific analyses of
*H. popperianum* for these compounds are not readily available.
