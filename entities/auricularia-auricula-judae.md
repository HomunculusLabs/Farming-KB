# Auricularia auricula-judae

**Wood Ear** · **Jew's Ear** · **Jelly Ear** · **黑木耳** (Mù'ěrguā)

## Taxonomy

Kingdom Fungi → Division Basidiomycota → Class Agaricomycetes → Order Auriculariales → Family Auriculariaceae → Genus *Auricularia* → Species *A. auricula-judae*. Described by Bulliard in 1789. The species epithet references the folk belief that Judas Iscariot hanged himself from an elder tree, the primary host. Cultivated in East Asia for over 1,000 years.

## Morphology

- **Fruiting body**: Ear-shaped to cup-shaped gelatinous basidiocarp, 2–10 cm across, 1–4 mm thick; upper surface brown to dark brown, velvety; lower surface smooth, purplish-grey with hymenial ridges (false gills)
- **Texture**: Rubbery and gelatinous when fresh; tough and shriveled when dry, reconstitutes to original form in water
- **Spores**: Allantoid (sausage-shaped), 12–16 × 4–5 µm, hyaline, smooth
- **Mycelium**: White, cottony to woolly; moderately fast colonizer on grain

## Habitat and Distribution

- **Primary hosts**: Elder (*Sambucus nigra*), alder, beech, oak, other hardwoods
- **Range**: Cosmopolitan temperate — Europe, Asia, North America, Africa, Australia; abundant in China and Southeast Asia
- **Ecology**: Saprotrophic on dead and dying hardwood branches and stumps; fruits spring through autumn after warm rains in shaded, moist microhabitats

## Bioactive Compounds

### Polysaccharides

- **Auricularian** (β-glucan complex): Primary immunomodulatory fraction; stimulates macrophage activity, enhances NK cell cytotoxicity
- **Acidic heteropolysaccharides**: Anticoagulant and antithrombotic properties

### Other Constituents

- **Adenosine**: Vasodilatory, antiplatelet, neuroprotective; contributes to cardiovascular benefits
- **Melanin**: Pigment in dark outer surface with free radical scavenging activity
- **Triterpenoids**: Anti-inflammatory; **Ergosterol**: Vitamin D2 precursor
- **Dietary fiber**: High chitin and β-glucan content

## Nutritional Profile (per 100g dried)

Protein 8–10 g, Carbohydrate 65–75 g, Fat 0.5–1.5 g, Iron 9–18 mg, Calcium 35–40 mg, Phosphorus 200–250 mg, Vitamins B1 0.15 mg, B2 0.55 mg. Extraordinarily high in iron for a fungal source — traditional food medicine for anemia in Chinese medicine.

## Traditional Use

- **TCM**: Used for 1,000+ years to "nourish the blood," improve circulation, and strengthen lungs; tonic for *xue* (blood) deficiency
- **European folk medicine**: Poultices for eye inflammation and sore throat
- **Culinary**: Staple in Chinese, Japanese, and Korean cuisine; prized for crisp, crunchy texture in soups, stir-fries, and cold dishes

## Modern Research

### Cardiovascular Health

Multiple studies demonstrate anticoagulant, antithrombotic, and cholesterol-lowering effects. Polysaccharides reduce platelet aggregation and improve blood rheology. Chinese clinical trials show reduced atherosclerosis risk with regular consumption.

### Blood Sugar and Gut Health

Polysaccharide extracts lower blood glucose and improve insulin sensitivity in diabetic animal models. High dietary fiber and prebiotic polysaccharides support beneficial gut microbiota; chitin content enhances mucosal immunity.

### Antioxidant

Melanin and phenolic fractions show significant DPPH and ABTS radical scavenging, comparable to synthetic antioxidants in vitro.

## Cultivation

### Substrate and Parameters

- **Natural logs**: Hardwood bolts (oak, beech, alder), 10–20 cm diameter; inoculate with plug or sawdust spawn in spring
- **[[oyster-mushroom-supplemented-sawdust-blocks]]**: Hardwood sawdust + 10–20% wheat bran, hydrated to 60–65%; sterilized bags
- **Spawn run**: 22–28 °C, 30–45 days (sawdust) or 6–12 months (logs)
- **Fruiting**: 15–25 °C, 85–95% RH, indirect light, tolerates high CO₂
- **Yield**: 80–120% [[stamets-biological-efficiency-yield]] on supplemented sawdust; logs produce for 3–5 years

Initiate fruiting by soaking or cold shock. Harvest in 7–14 days when ears are fully expanded but tender. Air-dry at 30–40 °C; reconstitutes 6–8× weight.

### Contamination and Hygiene

Wood ear is relatively contamination-resistant compared to other cultivated fungi, making it suitable for beginner cultivators. The gelatinous fruiting body is less susceptible to mold colonization. Common contaminants on sawdust substrate include *Trichoderma* (green mold) and *Coprinus* species. Maintain strict hygiene during inoculation but fruiting can tolerate less sterile conditions than oyster or [[gourmet-medicinal-mushroom-shiitake-cultivation-logs]].

## Culinary Applications

- **Preparation**: Must be cooked before eating; reconstitute dried specimens in warm water for 20–30 minutes
- **Texture**: Primary culinary appeal — crunchy, slightly slippery, absorbs surrounding flavors
- **Classical pairings**: Ginger, garlic, soy sauce, black vinegar, sesame oil
- **Iconic dishes**: Hot and sour soup (Chinese), wood ear salad with cilantro and chili, Buddha's delight (mixed vegetable stir-fry), Korean japchae
- **Storage**: Dried specimens keep for 2+ years in airtight containers away from moisture
- **Nutrition bonus**: Absorbs cooking liquids, making it an effective vehicle for nutrient-dense broths

## Permaculture Applications

- **Elder polyculture**: Dual yield of elderflowers/berries and wood ear mushrooms from the same tree
- **Log inoculation**: Low-input method using pruned hardwood branches
- **Food forest**: Grows on coppiced hardwoods in the understory layer without competing with herbs
- **Riparian buffer**: Thrives in moist microclimates along waterways

### Integration with Other Mushroom Species

Wood ear can be grown alongside other mushroom species on the same logs or in proximity:

- **Shiitake** (*Lentinula edodes*): Sequential inoculation possible; shiitake first (2–3 years), then wood ear colonizes as shiitake production declines
- **Oyster** (*Pleurotus ostreatus*): More competitive; avoid same log but complementary in same growing area with staggered harvest timing
- **Reishi** (*[[stamets-reishi-ganoderma-lucidum-cultivation]]*): Similar substrate preferences but longer rotation; interplant logs in the same shaded area

### Mycoremediation Potential

*Auricularia* species have demonstrated ability to bioaccumulate heavy metals from contaminated substrates. While this makes them unsuitable for food production on polluted sites, it opens applications for monitored bioremediation of heavy-metal-contaminated wood waste.

## Safety

Generally recognized as safe (GRAS). Raw consumption may cause GI discomfort. High oxalate content — moderate intake for kidney stone history. No significant drug interactions documented.
