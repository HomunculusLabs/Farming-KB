## Rhys Jones

## Description

Rhys Jones was an Australian prehistorian and archaeologist who coined the influential term "[[firestick-farming]]" to describe the systematic use of fire by Aboriginal Australians as a land management tool. His work was instrumental in overturning the perception of Aboriginal Australians as passive inhabitants of a wilderness, revealing instead a sophisticated, deliberate system of landscape management that shaped the entire Australian continent over tens of thousands of years.

## Classification

- **Type:** Person — prehistorian, archaeologist
- **Nationality:** Welsh-born, Australian
- **Born:** 1941, Wales
- **Died:** 2001, Australia
- **Fields:** Prehistoric archaeology, Australian Aboriginal studies
- **Institution:** Australian National University, Canberra

## Key Facts

- **Coined "firestick farming":** This term, perhaps his most enduring contribution, captured the essence of Aboriginal land management in a phrase that conveyed its deliberate, agricultural nature. By using the word "farming," Jones challenged the assumption that hunter-gatherer societies did not actively manage their environments.

- **Systematic management evidence:** Jones's research contributed to the body of evidence showing that Aboriginal burning was far from random or chaotic. The landscapes that Europeans encountered when colonising Australia could not have existed without great regularity and precision in the use of fire over thousands of years.

- **Challenged terra nullius thinking:** By demonstrating that the Australian landscape was a cultural product of Aboriginal management, Jones's work undermined the legal fiction of terra nullius (empty land) that had been used to justify European colonisation — well before the Mabo High Court decision formally overturned it in 1992.

- **Influence on [[permaculture]]:** Holmgren cites Aboriginal land management, including the firestick farming concept, as a direct inspiration in the original conception of Permaculture. Understanding that apparently natural landscapes were in fact the product of deliberate, long-term management by people was transformative for how permaculture designers think about the human-nature relationship.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

Rhys Jones's work is foundational to several permaculture concepts:

1. **Low-energy landscape management:** Firestick farming demonstrates that sophisticated, continent-scale land management is possible with minimal energy inputs — the most relevant model for [[permaculture-design]] in energy descent scenarios.

2. **Edge-based management:** The Aboriginal burning pattern described by Jones — where only the driest sites burn in the early dry season, creating self-reinforcing patterns of burnt and unburnt areas — is a masterclass in using edge to increase landscape productivity and diversity.

3. **Cultural embeddedness:** Jones's work shows that effective land management was embedded in cultural practice, not separated from it — a principle that permaculture seeks to restore through its integration of ethics, design, and daily practice.

4. **Pattern recognition:** The firestick farming concept exemplifies the permaculture principle of designing from patterns to details — the pattern being seasonal, targeted burning that creates productive edges at landscape scale.
