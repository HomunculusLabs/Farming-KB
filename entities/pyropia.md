The **conchocelis phase** is a microscopic, branched filamentous stage that
bores into the shells of mollusks or artificial calcium carbonate substrates.
Conchocelis filaments are 2–4 μm in diameter and can persist for months within
the substrate, producing asexual spores (conchospores) that germinate into new
gametophyte blades.

## Distribution and Habitat

Pyropia species are found in cold-temperate to tropical waters worldwide. The
highest species diversity occurs in the Northwest Pacific (Japan, Korea, China),
where most commercial species are native. Additional diversity centers exist
in the Northeast Atlantic and the Eastern Pacific.

Natural populations grow in the upper intertidal zone on rocky shores, often in
areas exposed to significant wave action and periodic desiccation. Species are
highly adapted to fluctuating environmental conditions including rapid salinity
changes, temperature extremes, and intense light during low tide.

*Cultivated species* include *P. yezoensis* (Japan, China, Korea — the dominant
commercial species), *P. haitanensis* (China — second most cultivated), *P.
tenera* (Japan — premium quality nori), and *P. pseudolinearis* (Korea).
