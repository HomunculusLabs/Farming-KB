# Bitterns (Mineral Residue)

## Description

Bitterns are the concentrated, bitter oily liquid residue remaining after common salt (sodium chloride) has crystallized out of evaporating seawater. They are rich in minor minerals, trace elements, and early-periodic-table elements including bromine. Mollison describes bitterns as a valuable but highly corrosive soil amendment and foliar spray material for supplying trace minerals to leached or deficient soils.

## Classification

- **Category**: Mineral Product / Soil Amendment
- **Source**: Seawater evaporation residue
- **Key constituents**: Magnesium salts, potassium salts, bromine, trace minerals, rare mineral salts
- **Physical form**: Bitter, oily, corrosive liquid
- **Evaporite sequence position**: Final residual liquid after calcite, dolomite, gypsum, anhydrite, and rock salt deposition

## Key Facts

- Remain after common evaporite minerals (calcite, dolomite, gypsum, anhydrite, rock salt) have crystallized out of seawater
- Contain rich concentration of minor minerals and trace elements from the early periodic table
- Include bromine and rare minerals not commonly available in standard fertilizers
- Extremely corrosive; must be handled in non-corrosive vessels (polyethylene pipes and drums)
- Sold commercially for dilution and incorporation into crop soils
- Applied as foliar spray at dilutions of 100-500:1, at rates of 1-20 liters per hectare
- Cheap and easily transported to leached inland areas
- Effects must be established by local trials on specific crops due to variable composition

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Trace mineral supply**: Provides a broad spectrum of trace elements essential for enzyme function in both plants and fungi
- **Soil remineralization**: Valuable for restoring mineral balance to heavily leached tropical or rain-washed soils
- **Foliar feeding**: Diluted bitterns can supply trace minerals directly to plant foliage for rapid uptake
- **Compost enrichment**: Can be added to compost systems to broaden the mineral profile
- **Mushroom cultivation**: Trace elements like zinc, copper, and selenium from bitterns may support fungal enzyme systems and fruiting body quality
- **Caution required**: High salinity and boron content can damage sensitive crops and fungal mycelium if over-applied
- **Soil biology**: Must be used at appropriate dilutions to avoid harming beneficial soil microorganisms and mycorrhizal networks
