## Description

**Sawdust spawn** is a type of mushroom inoculum consisting of vigorous mycelial growth on supplemented sawdust-bran substrates. It is one of the four primary spawn types used in mushroom cultivation, alongside [[liquid-spawn]], grain spawn, and wooden stick spawn. Sawdust spawn is particularly well-suited for wood-loving species such as *Grifola frondosa*, [[lentinula-edodes]] (shiitake), and other species cultivated on lignocellulosic substrates. It serves as the intermediate step between pure culture maintenance on agar and inoculation of production substrates in [[maitake-cultivation]] and other wood-decomposing mushroom operations.

## Classification

- **Category:** Spawn type / Cultivation material
- **Type:** Solid substrate mycelial inoculum
- **Base material:** Hardwood sawdust supplemented with bran
- **Use case:** Wood-loving mushroom species, synthetic-log cultivation

## Key Facts

- Sawdust spawn uses supplemented sawdust-bran substrates, typically hardwood sawdust amended with [[wheat-bran]] as a nutrient supplement.
- It is usually the spawn type of choice for *Grifola frondosa* (maitake) cultivation on synthetic logs.
- The sawdust substrate provides a closer nutritional and physical match to the natural wood substrate that wood-decomposing mushrooms colonize in nature.
- Spawn is produced under sterile conditions to maintain pure culture of the desired mushroom species before inoculating production substrates.
- Sawdust spawn colonizes production substrates more evenly than grain spawn in some applications, as the texture and particle size are more similar to the final substrate.
- Requires aseptic technique during handling and inoculation to prevent contamination by competing molds or bacteria.
- Can be produced by the grower or purchased from commercial spawn suppliers.

## Production Process

### Media Preparation
Sawdust spawn production begins with preparing a supplemented sawdust medium:
1. **Base substrate:** Hardwood sawdust (80-85%) provides the lignocellulosic base.
2. **Supplement:** [[wheat-bran]] (15-20%) adds nitrogen, vitamins (especially thiamine), and readily available carbohydrates.
3. **Moisture:** Water is added to achieve 55-60% moisture content.
4. **Optional additions:** Gypsum (1-2%) may be added to improve texture and buffer pH; calcium carbonate can help control acidity.

### Sterilization and Inoculation
- The prepared medium is filled into autoclavable bags or wide-mouth jars.
- Sterilize at 121°C for 60-90 minutes in an autoclave or pressure sterilizer.
- After cooling, the medium is inoculated with mycelium from agar culture or from master grain spawn under sterile conditions using a agar culture technique transfer.
- Inoculation is performed in a laminar flow hood or glove box to maintain sterility.

### Incubation
- Inoculated bags are incubated at the optimal temperature for the target species (22-25°C for most wood-loving species).
- Full colonization typically takes 14-30 days depending on species, inoculation rate, and temperature.
- Colonized spawn should show white, uniform mycelial growth with no colored patches (which indicate contamination).

## Advantages and Disadvantages

### Advantages Over Grain Spawn
- **Substrate compatibility:** Already adapted to lignocellulosic substrate, transitions readily to [[hardwood-sawdust-substrate]] production blocks.
- **Pest resistance:** Less attractive to rodents and insects compared to grain-based spawn.
- **Even colonization:** The similar texture to production substrate allows more uniform spread during the spawn run.
- **Cost-effective for wood-loving species:** The sawdust base is cheaper than grain for species that naturally grow on wood.

### Disadvantages
- **Slower colonization of production substrate:** Compared to [[liquid-spawn]], which percolates through the substrate more rapidly.
- **Inoculation requires more handling:** Solid spawn must be broken up and distributed manually, whereas liquid spawn can be poured or injected.
- **Limited to wood-decomposing species:** Not ideal for compost-grown species like [[agaricus-bisporus]] where grain spawn is preferred.

## Storage and Handling

- Sawdust spawn should be stored at 2-5°C (refrigeration) when not in immediate use.
- Under proper refrigeration, most species' sawdust spawn remains viable for 3-6 months.
- Before use, refrigerated spawn should be allowed to warm to room temperature for 12-24 hours to reactivate metabolism.
- Always inspect spawn for signs of contamination (green mold, slimy patches, unusual odors) before use.
- Handle with clean hands or gloves in a clean environment to prevent introducing contaminants.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Substrate compatibility:** Because sawdust spawn is already adapted to a lignocellulosic substrate, it transitions readily to synthetic-log substrates used in commercial maitake and shiitake production.
- **Nutritional buffering:** The bran supplement in sawdust spawn provides readily available nutrients that support vigorous mycelial growth during the initial colonization phase.
- **Storage and handling:** Sawdust spawn can be stored under refrigeration for extended periods, though viability should be checked before use.
- **Cost-effectiveness:** For species like maitake that are grown on sawdust-based substrates, sawdust spawn can be more economical and effective than grain spawn, which may attract pests more readily.
- **Scalability:** Sawdust spawn production can be scaled from small-batch lab operations to industrial-scale commercial spawn laboratories.
