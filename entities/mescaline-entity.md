# Mescaline (M)

**Chemical name:** 3,4,5-Trimethoxyphenethylamine

**PiHKAL #96** | [[alexander-shulgin]]'s rating: +++

**Military code:** EA-1306

**Natural source:** Lophophora williamsii (Peyote), Echinopsis pachanoi (San Pedro), Echinopsis peruviana, other cacti

## Overview

Mescaline is one of the oldest known psychedelics and the central prototype against which all other phenethylamine psychedelics are measured. It is the primary active compound of the peyote cactus (Lophophora williamsii, also known as Anhalonium lewinii), which has been used as a sacrament in Native American religious traditions for thousands of years. First isolated from peyote in 1897 and synthesized in 1919, mescaline became the standard reference compound in psychedelic research. In PiHKAL, [[alexander-shulgin]] describes it as "truly the central prototype against which everything else is measured." The Edgewood Arsenal established the "mescaline unit" (M.U.) system where all psychedelics were quantified relative to mescaline's activity at 3.75 mg/Kg.

## Qualitative Effects

- Intense color enhancement — subtle hue differences amplified to extreme contrast
- Phosphorescent intensity of colors
- Remarkable visual effects: moving mosaics, transforming objects, synesthetic experiences
- Extreme empathy for all living things — "impossible to harm anything"
- Deep introspective and spiritual experiences
- Nausea and vomiting common during onset (peyote tradition considers this purifying)
- Restlessness and hyper-energy at peak
- Enhanced sensory perception of sound, touch, and sight
- Transcendent experiences described in mystical terms
- Sense of connection to nature and all creation
- Can produce both euphoric heights and contemplative depths

## Shulgin's Qualitative Comments

At 300 mg: "quite intoxicated and hyper with energy... considerable agony, attempting to break through... a brilliant white light, that seemed to be where God was entering." At 350 mg: "intensification of color and considerable change in texture... desire to laugh hysterically at the completely ridiculous state of the entire world... extreme empathy felt for all small things; a stone, a flower, an insect." At 400 mg: "extreme empathy... it would be impossible to harm anything... the slightest difference of shade could be amplified to extreme contrast. Many subtle hues became phosphorescent in intensity."

## Extensions and Commentary

Mescaline is the major active component of peyote, a small dumpling cactus with tufts of soft fuzz instead of spines, growing wild in the Southwestern United States and Northern Mexico. It has been used as an intimate component of Native American religious traditions. The dried buttons (3-24 per ceremony) are consumed in ritual settings.

In clinical studies, mescaline was typically administered as the sulfate salt, which forms "magnificent crystals" from water but has a broad melting point dependent on hydration rate. The US Chemical Warfare program used mescaline as the reference standard (EA-1306) for quantitative comparisons of all substituted phenethylamines.

The compound has always been the central standard — "twice the potency of mescaline" or "twice as long-lived as mescaline" being the universal comparison. The "mescaline unit" (M.U.) system assigned mescaline a value of 1.0 at 3.75 mg/Kg (300 mg for an 80 Kg person).

## Safety Warnings

- Nausea and vomiting very common (considered part of the peyote experience in traditional use)
- Long duration (10-12 hours) requires planning
- Can produce challenging experiences, especially at higher doses
- Potential for anxiety and paranoia, particularly in uncontrolled settings
- Peyote is a controlled substance but is legally protected for Native American religious use in the US (American Indian Religious Freedom Act)
- See [[psychedelic-harm-reduction]] for general harm reduction guidelines
- Legal status: Schedule I in the US (with religious use exemption for Native American Church)
