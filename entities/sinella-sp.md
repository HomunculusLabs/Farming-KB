## Description

**Sinella** is a genus of collembolan (springtail) in the family Entomobryidae. Species in this genus are soil-dwelling [[microarthropods]] that graze on fungi, and have been studied for their density-dependent effects on arbuscular mycorrhizal colonization of plant roots.









## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Animalia
- **Phylum**: Arthropoda
- **Class**: [[collembola]]
- **Order**: Entomobryomorpha
- **Family**: Entomobryidae
- **Genus**: Sinella









## Key Facts

- Bakonyi et al. (2002) studied density effects on arbuscular mycorrhizal colonization
- At densities above 0.2 individuals per gram of soil, significant reductions in mycorrhizal colonization occurred
- At densities from zero to 0.2 individuals per gram, there was a significant increase in root colonization
- Demonstrates a threshold density effect with positive effects at low density and negative effects at high density
- Studied in microcosms with maize and red fescue









## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

The density-dependent effects of Sinella on mycorrhizal colonization illustrate that soil microarthropod populations have optimal densities for beneficial fungal interactions. Below the threshold, collembola may stimulate mycorrhizal colonization; above it, they reduce fungal benefit. This has direct implications for managing soil fauna in mycorrhizal-inoculated cultivation systems.









## Ecological Role

Sinella species are common soil collembola that participate in detrital food webs by grazing on fungi and other soil microorganisms. Their density-dependent effects on [[mycorrhizal-fungi]] suggest they play a regulatory role in plant-fungal symbioses.
