The species epithet "autumnalis" refers to the plant's characteristic autumnal flowering period.

Culms are slender, erect or ascending, and typically 5 to 30 centimeters tall, arising singly or in small tufts.

Leaves are basal, filiform to narrowly linear, 1 to 15 centimeters long and approximately 1 millimeter wide.

The inflorescence consists of a single terminal spikelet or a small cluster of 2 to 5 spikelets at the culm apex.

Spikelets are ovoid to lanceolate, 4 to 10 millimeters long, with overlapping reddish-brown to chestnut-colored glumes.

The style is 3-branched at the apex, a diagnostic feature distinguishing Fimbristylis from morphologically similar genera.

Achenes are small, approximately 1 millimeter long, broadly obovate, and white to pale brown in color.

Fimbristylis autumnalis has a broad distribution across the Americas, from southern Canada through the eastern and central United States to Central and South America.

In the United States, it occurs east of the Rocky Mountains from Maine to Florida and west to Texas and South Dakota.

## Cultivation and Management

Fimbristylis autumnalis is not commercially cultivated but may appear spontaneously in [[constructed-wetland-design]] rain gardens.

It is generally regarded as a minor weed in rice paddies and other irrigated agricultural systems in the southern United States.
