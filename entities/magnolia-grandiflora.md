Its range extends inland along river valleys into Arkansas, Oklahoma, and the eastern edge of Tennessee.

The species is most abundant in moist, well-drained bottomland soils and along the edges of swamps and streams.

It also grows on upland sites with sandy loam soils in the southeastern coastal plain.

Southern magnolia is hardy in USDA zones 7 through 10, though it can survive in protected locations in zone 6 with some winter injury.

It occurs naturally at elevations below 150 meters in most of its range, extending to approximately 300 meters in the Appalachian foothills.

## Ecology

The seeds of Magnolia grandiflora are dispersed by birds, mammals, and water. The bright red arils attract birds including northern flickers, yellow-bellied sapsuckers, and various songbirds.

White-tailed deer occasionally browse young foliage, though the leathery mature leaves are generally avoided.

The dense evergreen canopy provides year-round cover and nesting habitat for numerous bird species.

Southern magnolia is a member of climax forests in the lower coastal plain, associated with loblolly pine, water oak, sweetgum, and American beech.

The leaf litter decomposes slowly and forms a thick, acidic mulch layer that inhibits understory plant growth beneath mature trees.

The species is relatively free of serious pest problems, though scale insects and magnolia weevil can occasionally cause damage.

Root rot caused by Armillaria species can affect trees planted in poorly drained sites.

Southern magnolia has been introduced to cultivation in Europe, Asia, Australia, and [[hamilton-ornamental-trees]] in the southeastern United States and in warm-climate gardens worldwide.

It prefers full sun to partial shade and moist, well-drained, acidic soils rich in [[query-how-to-protect-plants-from-frost-and-extend-the-growing-season]].

Numerous cultivars have been selected, including 'Bracken's Brown Beauty' (compact, cold-hardy), 'D.D. Blanchard' (large, symmetrical), and 'Little Gem' (dwarf, early-blooming).

The dwarf cultivar 'Little Gem', reaching only 6 to 9 meters tall, has become one of the most popular selections for residential landscapes.

The wood is hard, heavy, and close-grained with a pale yellow to cream color. It is used for furniture, veneer, pallets, and pulpwood.
