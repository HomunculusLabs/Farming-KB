# Hypholoma sublateritium

## Introduction

**Hypholoma sublateritium** (Fries) Quelet, commonly known as Kuritake (Japanese
for "Chestnut Mushroom"), Brick Top, or Red Woodlover, is a flavorful edible
mushroom that fruits in the late summer and fall. It is synonymous with
*Naematoloma sublateritium* (Fr.) Karsten. Cultivation of this species was
pioneered at the Mon Mushroom Institute in Kiryu, Japan.

**CAUTION:** Alexander H. Smith (1949) notes that some European strains have
been implicated in poisonings. However, no recent reports suggest alarm, and
the species has long been enjoyed as a favorite edible in both [[edible-wild-mushrooms-by-region]], proper identification is essential.

## Description

The cap measures 2-7 cm broad, hemispheric to convex when young, expanding
with age to eventually become plane. Colors range from tan to brown to brick
red, darker towards the center and lighter yellow near the margin. The margin
is incurved and covered with floccose partial veil remnants, straightening and
becoming uplifted at maturity. The flesh is relatively thick.

The gills are close and bluntly attached, pallid at first and becoming dark
purple gray. The stem measures 5-10 cm x 5-10 mm, is solid, and is covered
with fibrillose veil remnants below the annular zone. The mushroom often forms
large clusters, and the brilliant yellowish zone around the cap margin
contrasts beautifully with the reddish brown coloration, making it
aesthetically pleasing.

**Microscopic features:** Spores are dark gray brown, 6.0-7.5 x 3.5-4.0 μm,
smooth, ellipsoid, with a faint germ pore. Cheilocystidia, pleurocystidia, and
clamp connections are present. The context is monomitic.

## Flavor and Preparation

*Hypholoma sublateritium* is excellent in stir fries or baked dishes. It is
sold both fresh and dried, and some mycophiles prefer to pickle it. In Japan,
it is primarily marketed fresh as Kuritake, where it commands a good price.
The mushroom has a rich, earthy flavor that pairs well with soy sauce, ginger,
and sesame oil.
