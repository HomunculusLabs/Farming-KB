# Bacillariophyta

## Description

**Bacillariophyta** (diatoms) is a division of photosynthetic microorganisms characterized by their unique cell walls made of biogenic silica (glass-like frustules). Diatoms are among the most important and prolific photosynthesizers on Earth, responsible for an estimated 20–30% of global oxygen production. While primarily aquatic, diatoms also occur in soils, especially in moist environments. Their fossilized remains form diatomaceous earth, a substance with significant applications in cultivation and pest management.

## Classification

- **Domain:** Eukarya
- **Kingdom:** Chromista (or Stramenopiles)
- **Division:** Bacillariophyta
- **Classes:** Coscinodiscophyceae (centric), Fragilariophyceae (araphid), Bacillariophyceae (raphid)

## Key Facts

- Cell walls (frustules) are made of hydrated silica (SiO₂·nH₂O) — essentially microscopic glass shells.
- Two major morphological groups: centric (radially symmetrical) and pennate (bilaterally symmetrical).
- Estimated 100,000+ species; among the most species-rich groups of microorganisms.
- Extremely prolific photosynthesizers — produce roughly 20–30% of Earth's oxygen.
- Frustules are intricately patterned with species-specific designs of extraordinary geometric beauty.
- Reproduce primarily by cell division; each daughter cell receives one half of the parent frustule.
- Over geological time, dead diatoms accumulate as **diatomaceous earth** — chalky sedimentary deposits.
- Found in virtually all aquatic habitats and many moist terrestrial environments.
- Important indicators of water quality and ecological health.

## Role in the Soil Food Web

In Lowenfels' soil food web, Bacillariophyta contribute through several pathways:

- **Primary production:** Soil-surface diatoms photosynthesize, converting CO₂ into organic matter that enters the soil food web.
- **Diatomaceous earth:** Fossilized diatom deposits, when applied to soil, contribute silica and can physically deter crawling pests.
- **Silicon cycling:** Diatoms are major participants in the global silicon cycle, converting dissolved silicate into biogenic silica.
- **Food source:** Soil diatoms are consumed by protozoa and small invertebrates.
- **Moisture indicators:** Presence of diatoms in soil indicates adequate surface moisture.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Diatomaceous earth (DE):** Widely used as a physical pest control agent. The microscopic silica shells abrade insect exoskeletons, causing desiccation. Effective against slugs, snails, ants, and various crawling insects without chemical toxicity.
- **Slug and snail control:** DE is particularly relevant for protecting mushroom beds and greenhouse crops from gastropod damage.
- **Soil amendment:** DE adds silica to soil and can improve soil structure and drainage in heavy clay soils.
- **Stored grain protection:** DE is used to protect stored grains and mushroom spawn from insect infestation.
- **Filter aid:** Diatomaceous earth is used in filtration systems, including some compost tea production setups.
- **Silica nutrition:** Some plants (especially grasses) benefit from silica uptake, which strengthens cell walls and improves pest and disease resistance.
- **Environmental monitoring:** Diatom species assemblages are used as biological indicators of soil and water quality.

## Environmental Conditions

- **Moisture:** Require moisture for locomotion and metabolism; active on moist soil surfaces.
- **Light:** Photosynthetic — require adequate light exposure.
- **Silica:** Require dissolved silicate in water or soil solution for frustule formation.
- **Temperature:** Most active in moderate temperatures; tolerant of wide ranges.
- **pH:** Most species prefer neutral to alkaline conditions.
