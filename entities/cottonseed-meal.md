## Cottonseed Meal

## Description

**Cottonseed meal** is a slow-release, acidic nitrogen fertilizer (N-P-K 6-0.4-1.5) produced from the ground remains of cotton seeds after oil extraction. It is valued for its slightly acidic nature, making it particularly suitable for acid-loving plants such as rhododendrons, azaleas, camellias, and blueberries. In addition to nitrogen, it provides trace elements including zinc, copper, manganese, and molybdenum.

## Classification

- **Category:** Natural organic fertilizer / Plant by-product
- **N-P-K:** 6-0.4-1.5
- **Nutrient Availability:** Slow release (~4 months)
- **pH Effect:** Slightly acidic
- **Application Form:** Dry meal
- **Domain:** Organic gardening, acid-soil horticulture

## Key Facts

- Contains 6% nitrogen with additional phosphorus (0.4%) and potassium (1.5%).
- Rich in trace elements: zinc, copper, manganese, and molybdenum — all essential micronutrients for plant growth.
- Naturally slightly acidic, making it ideal for plants that prefer lower pH soils.
- Provides nutrients for approximately 4 months after application.
- Especially well-suited for rhododendrons, azaleas, camellias, blueberries, and other acid-soil-loving species.

## Cautions and Considerations

- **Pesticide residue concern:** Cotton is one of the most heavily pesticide-treated crops in agriculture. Residues of these chemicals can persist in cottonseed meal, making it **unsuitable for use on vegetables and fruit crops** where food safety is a concern.
- **GMO concern:** Cotton is frequently genetically modified (glyphosate-resistant varieties). Cottonseed meal may not qualify as organic and may contain GMO-derived proteins.
- These two concerns significantly limit the application range of cottonseed meal compared to other organic nitrogen sources.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- The acidic nature of cottonseed meal mirrors the soil conditions favored by many fungi, including mycorrhizal species that prefer lower pH environments.
- The trace element content (Zn, Cu, Mn, Mo) supports enzymatic functions in both plants and soil microorganisms, as these elements serve as cofactors in critical enzyme systems.
- Cottonseed meal can be used in compost for acid-loving plants or mixed into potting substrates for ericaceous (heath family) species.
- The pesticide residue issue highlights the importance of sourcing organic-certified inputs when growing food crops or cultivating mushrooms for consumption.
