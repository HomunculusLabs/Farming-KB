The species is easily recognized by its large, glossy green thallus with a reticulate pattern of hexagonal air pores on the upper surface. Its strong, earthy scent when crushed gives rise to the common name great scented liverwort.

Conocephalum is a small genus containing only 2–3 accepted species worldwide. Conocephalum conicum is by far the most widespread and abundant member of the genus.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Conocephalum conicum was first described by Carl Linnaeus in 1753 as Marchantia conica. It was later elevated to its own genus by Weber in 1798.

The genus name Conocephalum derives from the Greek words konos and kephale, referring to the conical shape of the archegonial receptacles. The specific epithet conicum shares this etymological root.

The thallus is large and robust, reaching 1–2 cm wide and up to 15 cm or more in length. It forms extensive, overlapping mats on moist substrates.

The upper surface is dark green and glossy, with a distinctive network of raised hexagonal areas surrounding conspicuous air pores. Each air pore is surrounded by 4–6 concentric rows of specialized cells.

This reticulate surface pattern is unique among temperate thalloid liverworts and makes Conocephalum immediately recognizable even to novice bryologists.

When the thallus is viewed under a hand lens, the air pore complexes appear as small circular openings surrounded by radiating cell rows. This complex internal chamber system facilitates gas exchange while minimizing water loss.

The lower surface bears two rows of purple scales along the midrib and numerous rhizoids. Both smooth and pegged rhizoids are present.

A conspicuous midrib runs along the center of the thallus, visible as a raised ridge on both upper and lower surfaces. The thallus margins are wavy and often overlap adjacent thalli.

Male reproductive structures are stalked, disc-shaped receptacles borne on short stalks. Female receptacles are also stalked, with finger-like projections that radiate from the central stalk.

The sporophyte consists of a short seta and a spherical capsule that opens into four valves at maturity. Spores are yellow-brown and approximately 18–22 μm in diameter.

## Distribution and Habitat

Conocephalum conicum has a circumboreal distribution across Europe, Asia, and North America. It is widespread throughout the British Isles, continental Europe, eastern Asia, and [[basidiomycete-mating-systems-sexual-reproduction]] involves antheridia and archegonia produced on separate plants, making the species dioicous.

Sperm are dispersed by water splashes and require a continuous film of water to reach the archegonia. Sexual reproduction peaks in spring when moisture conditions are optimal.

The sporophyte develops after successful fertilization, with the capsule maturing over several weeks. Spore release occurs as the capsule splits into four valves.

Vegetative reproduction occurs through thallus fragmentation. Broken pieces of thallus readily regenerate into new individuals when they land on suitable moist substrates.

The species also produces gemma-like propagules from the thallus margins in some populations. These vegetative structures facilitate local colonization and population expansion.

Gemmae are not produced in specialized cups as in Lunularia and Marchantia. Instead, [[conidia-and-asexual-reproduction-in-filamentous-fungi]] Conocephalum relies primarily on mechanical fragmentation.

The life cycle from spore germination to spore production takes approximately 12–18 months under natural conditions. This relatively long generation time favors [[mollison-designers-fish-pond-fertiliser-and-nutrient-cycling]] within the bryophyte mat.

As a competitor for space on moist rock surfaces, Conocephalum can influence the composition of bryophyte communities. Its large thallus size and rapid vegetative growth give it a competitive advantage over smaller liverwort species.

The species plays a role in the chemical weathering of limestone substrates. Organic acids produced by the thallus contribute to slow dissolution of [[peat-calcium-carbonate-perlite-casing-psilocybe-cubensis-miniculture]] surfaces.
