# 5-MeO-DALT (N,N-Diallyl-5-methoxytryptamine)

## Overview

5-MeO-DALT is a synthetic psychedelic tryptamine first synthesized and characterized by Alexander Shulgin and described in *TiHKAL* (Tryptamines I Have Known and Loved). The compound features a 5-methoxy substitution on the indole ring and N,N-diallyl groups on the terminal amine — a substitution pattern that is rare among both natural and synthetic tryptamines. It is known for its relatively short duration, mild to moderate visual effects, and distinctive body load, making it an atypical member of the psychedelic tryptamine family.

## Chemical Information

### IUPAC Name
N,N-diallyl-5-methoxy-1H-indole-3-ethanamine

### Chemical Formula
C₁₇H₂₂N₂O

### Molecular Weight
270.37 g/mol

### SMILES
COc1ccc2[nH]c(CCN(C=CC)C=CC)c(C)c2c1

### Structural Features
- **Indole ring** with a **5-methoxy** substitution (positions analogous to psilocin/5-MeO-DMT)
- **N,N-diallyl substitution** on the ethylamine side chain — two allyl groups (CH₂-CH=CH₂) replacing the more common N,N-dimethyl groups
- The allyl groups significantly alter the compound's pharmacokinetics compared to N,N-dimethyl tryptamines

### Physical Properties
- Appearance: White to off-white crystalline powder or freebase oil
- Freebase form is an oily liquid at room temperature; commonly encountered as a hydrochloride or fumarate salt
- Slightly soluble in water (as salt); soluble in ethanol, DMSO, and lipid solvents

## Pharmacology

### Mechanism of Action
5-MeO-DALT acts primarily as a **5-HT₂A receptor agonist**, the primary mechanism underlying its psychedelic effects. Additional receptor interactions likely include:

- **5-HT₂C:** Moderate affinity; may contribute to the body load and anxiety some users report
- **5-HT₁A:** Weak affinity; may contribute to anxiolytic or calming effects
- **TAAR1 (Trace Amine-Associated Receptor 1):** Some tryptamines show activity here, though 5-MeO-DALT has not been specifically characterized

The N,N-diallyl substitution is pharmacologically significant. Compared to N,N-dimethyl groups, the allyl chains:
- Increase lipophilicity, potentially enhancing blood-brain barrier penetration
- Alter metabolic stability — allyl groups are metabolized by cytochrome P450 enzymes, particularly CYP2D6 and CYP3A4
- May confer partial agonist or biased signaling properties at 5-HT₂A that differ from DMT or psilocin

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Onset:** 15–45 minutes after oral administration
- **Duration:** 2–4 hours (notably shorter than psilocin at 4–6 hours or LSD at 8–12 hours)
- **Metabolism:** Presumed hepatic, via N-deallylation and O-demethylation. The allyl groups are susceptible to oxidative metabolism, producing diallylamine and 5-hydroxyindole metabolites.
- **Bioavailability:** Moderate when taken orally. The compound may also be vaporized/smoked, which produces a faster onset (1–5 minutes) and shorter duration (1–2 hours).

## Dosage

### Oral
- **Threshold:** 5–10 mg
- **Light:** 10–15 mg
- **Common:** 15–25 mg
- **Strong:** 25–40 mg
- **Heavy:** 40 mg+

### Vaporized/Smoked
- **Light:** 5–10 mg
- **Common:** 10–20 mg
- **Strong:** 20–30 mg

**Note:** Dosage ranges are derived from self-reporting in the research chemical community and have not been established through clinical trials. Individual sensitivity varies considerably.

## Effects

### Subjective Experience
The psychedelic effects of 5-MeO-DALT are generally described as:

- **Visuals:** Mild to moderate closed-eye visuals (CEVs) — geometric patterns, color shifts, flowing textures. Open-eye visuals are typically subtle, consisting of color enhancement, mild patterning, and softening of edges. Significantly less visual intensity than DMT, psilocin, or LSD at comparable perceived doses.
- **Head space:** Light to moderate cognitive alteration. Users frequently describe a "surface-level" psychedelic experience — thoughts are loosened and associative but ego dissolution is uncommon at typical doses. Some users find the headspace surprisingly clear, even analytical.
- **Body sensations:** A distinctive physical component is frequently reported — tingling, buzzing, or vibrating sensations in the extremities, mild to moderate nausea during onset, and a generalized sense of physical energy or restlessness.
- **Emotional effects:** Can range from euphoria and amusement to mild anxiety. The emotional tone is highly set-and-setting dependent.
- **Auditory effects:** Mild sound distortion and enhanced music appreciation.

### Comparison to Other Tryptamines
- **Versus 5-MeO-DMT:** Far less intense. 5-MeO-DMT often produces overwhelming, ego-dissolving experiences; 5-MeO-DALT is comparatively gentle and recreational.
- **Versus psilocin:** Shorter duration, less visual intensity, more body-focused effects.
- **Versus DMT:** Less visionary, less intense, longer duration, more controllable.
- **Versus 4-HO-DiPT:** Similar duration, but 5-MeO-DALT is considered less psychedelic and more "trippy-light."

## History

### Discovery
5-MeO-DALT was first synthesized by Alexander Shulgin and his research group. It is listed in *TiHKAL* (published 1997), where Shulgin provides limited qualitative commentary. The entry is notable for being relatively brief compared to other tryptamine entries, suggesting Shulgin himself did not extensively characterize the compound's subjective effects.

### Research Chemical Era (2004–2016)
5-MeO-DALT became available through online research chemical vendors in the mid-to-late 2000s, marketed as a "research chemical" or "not for human consumption." It gained moderate popularity on forums such as Bluelight, Erowid, and Drugs-Forum due to its:
- Legal status in many jurisdictions (not scheduled in most countries at the time)
- Short duration making it suitable for casual or evening use
- Readily available in powdered form from chemical suppliers

### Current Status
The compound has been scheduled in several countries including the UK (Class B, 2014), Japan, China, and parts of Australia. In the United States, it exists in a legal gray area — not specifically scheduled under the Controlled Substances Act but potentially prosecutable under the Federal Analog Act if intended for human consumption. It has largely fallen out of favor as newer compounds have appeared and legal pressure has increased.

## Toxicology and Safety

### Acute Toxicity
No formal LD₅₀ data are available for humans. Animal toxicity data are extremely limited. The compound is generally considered to have low acute toxicity at recreational doses, based on the absence of serious adverse events in the self-reporting literature.

### Known Risks
- **Nausea and vomiting:** Common during onset, particularly with oral administration
- **Anxiety and panic:** Possible at higher doses, particularly in inexperienced users or uncontrolled settings
- **Cardiovascular effects:** As a serotonergic psychedelic, 5-MeO-DALT may cause mild tachycardia, hypertension, and vasoconstriction. Individuals with cardiovascular conditions should exercise caution.
- **Serotonin syndrome:** Theoretical risk when combined with MAOIs, SSRIs, or other serotonergic drugs. No confirmed cases specifically with 5-MeO-DALT are documented.
- **Harm potential of allyl groups:** N-allyl compounds have been associated with hepatotoxicity in other chemical classes (e.g., allyl alcohol). The significance of the allyl groups in 5-MeO-DALT is unknown but warrants caution regarding liver health with frequent use.
- **Impurity risks:** Research chemical supplies may be adulterated or contain synthetic byproducts. Independent laboratory testing is recommended when possible.

### Harm Reduction
- Start with low doses, particularly if inexperienced with psychedelics
- Use in a safe, comfortable environment with a trusted person present
