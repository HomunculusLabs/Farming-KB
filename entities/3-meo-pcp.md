# 3-Methoxyphencyclidine (3-MeO-PCP)

## Overview

3-Methoxyphencyclidine (3-MeO-PCP) is a dissociative anesthetic of the
arylcyclohexylamine class, structurally related to phencyclidine (PCP) with
a methoxy substituent at the 3-position of the phenyl ring. First
synthesized in the 1970s as part of pharmaceutical research into analgesic
anesthetics, it emerged as a recreational research chemical in the early
2010s and has become one of the most widely used novel dissociatives.

3-MeO-PCP is significantly more potent than PCP or [[ketamine]], with active
doses in the low single-digit milligram range. It produces profound
dissociation, analgesia, mania, and euphoria, alongside substantial
neurotoxicity concerns at high or chronic doses.

## Chemical Properties

- **IUPAC name:** 1-(1-(3-methoxyphenyl)cyclohexyl)piperidine
- **Molecular formula:** C₁₈H₂₇NO
- **Molecular weight:** 273.42 g/mol
- **CAS number:** 1638268-90-2
- **SMILES:** COc1cccc(c1)C2(CCCCC2)N3CCCCC3

The 3-methoxy substitution on the phenyl ring increases lipophilicity
compared to PCP, enhancing blood-brain barrier penetration and
contributing to its higher potency and longer duration of action. It is a
free base at physiological pH, typically encountered as a white to
off-white crystalline powder.

## Pharmacology

3-MeO-PCP is a high-affinity **NMDA receptor antagonist** (non-competitive),
binding to the PCP site within the NMDA channel pore. This mechanism is
shared with ketamine, PCP, MXE, and all arylcyclohexylamine dissociatives.

Additional receptor interactions contribute to its unique profile:

- **Dopamine reuptake inhibition:** Moderate DAT activity, contributing to
  stimulant-like euphoria and manic/psychotic states at higher doses.
- **[[serotonin]] reuptake inhibition:** Weak SERT activity.
- **Sigma-1 receptor agonism:** Contributes to dissociative effects.
- **[[salvinorin-a-kappa-opioid-receptor-pharmacology]] activity:** Negligible.

The NMDA antagonism combined with dopamine reuptake inhibition
distinguishes 3-MeO-PCP from ketamine and DXM, blending dissociation with
significant stimulant properties.

## Effects

**Dose ranges (oral):**
- Threshold: 1–3 mg
- Light: 3–6 mg
- Common: 6–10 mg
- Strong: 10–15 mg
- Heavy: 15 mg+

**Onset:** 20–60 minutes (oral), 5–15 minutes (insufflated).
**Duration:** 5–8 hours (oral), 3–5 hours (insufflated).
**After-effects:** 2–12 hours of residual stimulation, brain fog, or
dysphoria.

**Physical effects:** Analgesia, numbness, ataxia, tachycardia,
hypertension, nystagmus, perspiration, vasoconstriction. Pupil dilation
is common but not pronounced.
