## Overview

*Antrodia cinnamomea* is a rare, endemic medicinal polypore fungus found exclusively in the mountainous forests of Taiwan, where it grows as a parasite and saprotroph on the inner cavity of the endemic tree *Cinnamomum kanehirai* (bull camphor tree).

The species is among the most valuable medicinal mushrooms in traditional Chinese medicine, commanding extraordinarily high market prices due to its scarcity, slow growth, and potent pharmacological activities.

## Taxonomy

Basidiomycota → Agaricomycetes → Polyporales → Fomitopsidaceae → ***Antrodia* → *A. cinnamomea***

- **Authority:** T.T. Chang & W.N. Chou (1995)
- **Synonyms:** *Antrodia camphorata* (M. Zang & C.H. Su) Sheng H. Wu, Z.H. Yu, Y.C. Dai & C.H. Su (2004); *Taiwanofungus camphoratus* (M. Zang & C.H. Su) Sheng H. Wu, Z.H. Yu, Y.C. Dai & C.H. Su (2004)
- **Note:** The taxonomic placement has been debated. Some authorities place it in the genus *Taiwanofungus* based on phylogenetic analysis, though *Antrodia cinnamomea* remains the most widely used name.

## Morphology

Fruiting body is sessile to effused-reflexed, hoof-shaped or shelf-like, growing from the inner wall of hollow *C. kanehirai* trunks.

- **Upper surface:** Orange-red to cinnamon-brown, concentrically zoned, with a rough to somewhat velvety texture; margin often lighter in color.
- **Pore surface:** Orange to reddish-brown, pores circular to angular, 2–5 per mm; tube layer up to 8 mm thick.
- **Context:** Corky to woody, light brown to cinnamon-colored, 3–15 mm thick.
- **Spores:** Cylindrical to ellipsoidal, 6–8 × 2–3 μm, hyaline, smooth, thin-walled, inamyloid.
- **Hyphal system:** Monomitic to dimitic; generative hyphae with clamp connections; skeletal hyphae present in mature specimens.

## Distribution and Habitat

*A. cinnamomea* is endemic to Taiwan, found at elevations of 450–2,000 m in old-growth and secondary broadleaf forests.

- **Host specificity:** Found almost exclusively on living or recently dead *Cinnamomum kanehirai* trees, growing within the hollowed trunk cavity.
- **Growth habit:** Primarily an internal parasite that transitions to saprotrophic decay after host death; fruiting bodies form on the inner wall of the trunk, rarely visible externally.
- **Habitat:** Humid, shaded mountain forests dominated by *C. kanehirai* and other Lauraceae species.
- **Conservation status:** Wild populations have declined dramatically due to overharvesting and habitat loss; the species is protected under Taiwanese law.

## Cultivation

Commercial cultivation has been achieved through several methods, though wild-harvested material still commands premium prices.

- **Solid-state fermentation:** Supplemented sawdust of *C. kanehirai* or other *Cinnamomum* species in bags or jars; fruiting takes 6–12 months.
- **Submerged fermentation:** Mycelial biomass cultured in liquid medium for production of bioactive metabolites; harvest in 5–10 days, used primarily for extract production.
- **Host log cultivation:** Inoculation of *C. kanehirai* logs, mimicking natural conditions; very slow, 1–2 years to fruiting.
- **Yield:** Fruiting body yields remain low (50–150 g fresh per log), contributing to high market value; submerged fermentation produces higher biomass but lower triterpenoid content.

## Bioactive Compounds

*A. cinnamomea* is exceptionally rich in bioactive secondary metabolites, with over 78 compounds isolated and characterized.

- **Triterpenoids:** The signature compound class, with over 30 ergostane-type triterpenoids identified; the "antcins" (antcin A, B, C, H, K) and "methyl antcinate" series are the most pharmacologically significant.
- **Polysaccharides:** β-glucan fractions with immunomodulatory activity; molecular weights range from 10 to 500 kDa.
- **Maleic/succinic derivatives:** Antroquinonol, antrocamphin A and B, 4-acetylantroquinonol B — unique to *Antrodia* with potent cytotoxic and anti-inflammatory effects.
- **Steroids:** Ergosterol and ergosterol peroxide with documented antitumor activity.
- **Benzenoids and ubiquinones:** Minor constituents with antioxidant properties.

## Pharmacology

*A. cinnamomea* has the most extensively studied pharmacological profile of any Taiwanese medicinal mushroom, with demonstrated activities across multiple therapeutic areas.

- **Hepatoprotective activity:** The most well-established traditional and modern use. Antcin K, antcin B, and antroquinonol protect against CCl₄-, acetaminophen-, and alcohol-induced liver injury in rodent models through suppression of hepatic inflammation, reduction of oxidative stress markers (MDA, ROS), and inhibition of stellate cell activation.
- **Anti-inflammatory effects:** Ethanol extracts inhibit NF-κB, MAPK, and JAK/STAT signaling pathways; reduce production of TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, and PGE₂ in LPS-stimulated macrophages at IC₅₀ values of 10–50 μg/mL.
- **Antitumor activity:** Antroquinonol, antcin K, and ethyl antcinate A show dose-dependent cytotoxicity against hepatoma (HepG2, Hep3B), breast cancer (MCF-7), lung cancer (A549), and colorectal cancer (HT-29) cell lines with IC₅₀ values of 5–40 μM; mechanisms include cell cycle arrest (G0/G1 and G2/M), induction of apoptosis via caspase activation, and inhibition of PI3K/Akt/mTOR signaling.
- **Antioxidant activity:** Crude extracts and purified polysaccharides show strong DPPH, ABTS, and superoxide radical scavenging; antroquinonol demonstrates potent Nrf2 pathway activation.
- **Immunomodulation:** Polysaccharide fractions enhance NK cell activity, promote T-cell proliferation, and stimulate dendritic cell maturation.
- **Anti-diabetic effects:** Antcin K improves insulin sensitivity and reduces fasting blood glucose in diabetic mouse models.

## Traditional Use

*A. cinnamomea* has been used by indigenous Taiwanese peoples (the Atayal and Saisiyat) and in traditional Chinese medicine for centuries.

- **Indications:** Liver disorders, fatigue, food poisoning, alcohol intoxication, abdominal pain, diarrhea, and hypertension.
- **Preparation:** Traditionally prepared as a decoction from dried fruiting bodies, often simmered with chicken or pork; modern use includes capsules, tablets, and tinctures from fruiting body or mycelial extracts.
- **Dosage (traditional):** 3–10 g dried fruiting body per day in decoction; commercial extracts typically standardized to 5–15% triterpenoid content.

## Legal Status and Conservation

Wild harvesting of *A. cinnamomea* has been severely restricted since 2005 under Taiwan's Wildlife Conservation Act, as the species is classified as endangered due to decades of unsustainable collection.

- **Wild harvest ban:** Collection from natural stands is prohibited without special permits; illegal harvesting carries significant fines.
- **Protected host tree:** *Cinnamomum kanehirai* itself is a protected species in Taiwan, further limiting access to the natural substrate.
- **Market price:** Wild-harvested material commands $15,000–25,000 USD per kilogram dried; cultivated material ranges from $500–5,000 USD per kg depending on method and triterpenoid content.
- **Cultivation as conservation:** Commercial cultivation programs are actively promoted by the Taiwanese government as a conservation strategy to reduce pressure on wild populations.
- **Red-list status:** Listed as endangered by the Taiwan Red List of Fungi (2017).

## Related Species

Several *Antrodia* species share morphological and phytochemical characteristics with *A. cinnamomea* but differ in distribution and host specificity.

- *Antrodia salmonicolor*: Found in Japan and Taiwan on coniferous wood; produces similar triterpenoids but with different spectral profiles.
- *Antrodia serialis*: Northern Hemisphere species on conifer logs; medicinally less studied.
- *Antrodia malicola*: Tropical species with broader host range; lacks the characteristic antcin compounds.
- *Taiwanofungus camphoratus*: A synonym used by some authorities; the same species under an alternative generic placement based on molecular phylogenetics.
