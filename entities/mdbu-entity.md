# MDBU (N-Butyl-MDA; 3,4-Methylenedioxy-N-butylamphetamine)

MDBU is a substituted amphetamine compound and the N-butyl derivative of [[mda-entity]]. It represents the exploration of longer N-alkyl chain substitutions on the MDA skeleton. The compound is listed as PIHKAL entry #102.

## Chemical Classification

MDBU belongs to the amphetamine class of chemical compounds.

It is formally named 3,4-methylenedioxy-N-butylamphetamine.

It is the N-butyl homologue of [[mda-entity]], with a four-carbon butyl chain replacing the hydrogen on the nitrogen atom.

MDBU was synthesized as part of the systematic exploration of N-alkyl chain length on the MDA scaffold.

## Pharmacological Profile

MDBU has shown no significant psychoactive effects at the doses explored.

Dosage: Greater than 40 mg (no central effects observed).

Duration: Unknown.

The compound is considered inactive as a centrally acting psychoactive substance.

## The N-Alkyl MDA Series

Straight chain homologues on the nitrogen atom of MDA longer than two carbons are probably not active.

This butyl compound provoked no interest, and although longer chain counterparts were synthesized, they were not tasted.

All mouse assays that compared this homologous series showed a consistent decrease in action (anesthetic potency and motor activity) as the alkyl chain on the nitrogen atoms was lengthened.

The pattern suggests that the N-hydrogen (as in MDA) or short N-alkyl chains (as in MDMA with N-methyl, or MDE with N-ethyl) are optimal for psychoactivity.

Chain extension beyond two carbons on the nitrogen progressively reduces central activity.

## Synthesis

MDBU is synthesized by the reductive amination of 3,4-methylenedioxyphenylacetone with butylamine hydrochloride.

Butylamine hydrochloride is prepared by introducing butylamine into concentrated HCl and removing volatiles.

The reductive amination uses sodium cyanoborohydride in methanol at pH 6, maintained by the addition of HCl as needed.

The reaction proceeds over about two days.

During the reaction, the mixture first becomes cottage-cheese-like, then thins out again.

After workup, the product is distilled at 90-100 degrees C at 0.15 mm/Hg to give a white oil.

The hydrochloride salt crystallizes spontaneously with a melting point of 200-200.5 degrees C.

## General Applicability of the Synthetic Method

The synthetic procedure using the hydrochloride salt of the amine and sodium cyanoborohydride in methanol is described as quite general.

Most of the MD-group of compounds in PIHKAL were made in this manner.

The method also works effectively with phenylacetone (P-2-P) as the starting ketone.

From phenylacetone and butylamine hydrochloride, N-butylamphetamine hydrochloride was produced (23.4 g from 16.3 g P-2-P).

From phenylacetone and ethylamine hydrochloride, N-ethylamphetamine was produced (22.4 g from 22.1 g P-2-P).

From phenylacetone and methylamine hydrochloride, N-methylamphetamine was produced (24.6 g from 26.8 g P-2-P).

The reaction with simple ammonia (as ammonium acetate) gives consistently poor yields.

## Relationship to Other N-Substituted MDA Compounds

[[mda-entity]] has N-H and is fully psychedelic.

[[mdma-entity]] has N-methyl and is a potent entactogen.

[[mde-entity]] has N-ethyl and is also active.

[[mdal-entity]] has N-allyl and is inactive, and appears to antagonize LSD.

[[mdpr-entity]] has N-propyl and is inactive alone but enhances LSD.

MDBU has N-butyl and is inactive.

The progression from active to inactive occurs somewhere between the ethyl and propyl chain lengths.

## Dosage and Duration

Dosage: Greater than 40 mg (no central effects observed).

Duration: Unknown.

The compound is considered inactive.
