# Earthnut Pea (Lathyrus tuberosus)

## Overview

Lathyrus tuberosus, commonly known as the earthnut pea, tuberous pea, or tine-tare, is a perennial climbing legume notable for producing edible underground tubers. Native to Europe and Western Asia, this species has a long history of human use as a food crop, particularly during periods of scarcity. Despite its historical importance, the earthnut pea has largely faded from commercial cultivation and is now considered an underutilized or orphan crop with significant potential for permaculture and [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]] worldwide. Lathyrus tuberosus was first formally described by Carl Linnaeus in his 1753 *Species Plantarum*, though the plant had been known and used by humans for millennia prior to formal taxonomic classification.
Several botanical varieties and subspecies have been described over the centuries, though modern taxonomic treatments tend to recognize L. tuberosus as a single, morphologically variable species without formal infraspecific taxa. The species has a chromosome number of 2n = 14, consistent with many other members of the genus. Molecular phylogenetic studies place L. tuberosus within the Lathyrus section Lathyrus, alongside species such as L. sativus and L. cicera, supporting the close evolutionary relationships inferred from morphology.

## Morphology

Lathyrus tuberosus is a herbaceous perennial vine that typically grows 30 to 120 centimeters in height, though it can exceed this range under ideal conditions with adequate support. The plant exhibits the following morphological characteristics:
**Stems:** Slender, climbing or trailing stems that are distinctly winged, a characteristic feature of many Lathyrus species. The wings run along the length of the stem's angles, providing [[dighton-fungal-drought-tolerance-plant-water-relations]].
**Rhizobial Symbiosis:** The roots form nitrogen-fixing nodules in association with Rhizobium leguminosarum biovar viciae, the same bacterial symbiont associated with peas, lentils, and vetches. Nodulation typically begins 3 to 4 weeks after seedling emergence and continues throughout [[polypore-fungi-eastern-north-america]], where it occasionally escapes cultivation and establishes self-sustaining populations. It has also been introduced to Australia and New Zealand, though it is less widespread in the [[coleman-louis-savier-twentieth-century-maraicher]] document the tubers being sold in local markets in rural Catalonia and Aragon, where they were valued as a winter food source when fresh vegetables were scarce.

In Eastern Europe, particularly in Poland, Ukraine, and Belarus, the earthnut pea was known as *groch grubowy* or *pestrushka* and held a place in traditional peasant cuisine. Tubers were commonly roasted alongside root vegetables during autumn harvest festivals. In Italy, the plant is called *cicerchia tuberosa* and was historically foraged in the Apennine foothills, where it grew abundantly in dry meadows and olive grove margins. Italian ethnobotanical records document its use in rustic breads and savory pies.
