## G. W. Forister

## Description

G. W. Forister was a researcher at the Department of Environmental Horticulture, University of California, Davis, and co-author (with D. W. Burger) of a detailed [[holzer-terrace-construction-guide]] for laminar flow hoods used in plant [[cervantes-tissue-culture-micropropagation]] laboratories. The paper describes the [[farm-pond-design-and-construction]] of two laminar flow hoods connected to a single blower unit via dryer vent hose, based on an earlier conceptual design by Meyer (1986, HortScience 21(4):1064-1065). The construction details include complete fan housing specifications, [[hepa-filter]] integration, plexiglass hood construction, and a cost analysis totaling approximately $903 for two hoods and one fan housing.

## Key Facts

- Co-authored a practical [[laminar-flow-hood-construction]] guide while at UC Davis, providing detailed step-by-step instructions suitable for laboratory self-construction.
- The design uses a Dayton #5C094 direct-drive blower connected via dryer vent hose and aluminum irrigation pipe fittings to two separate HEPA-filtered hood enclosures.
- The hood enclosures use 24"×30"×6" HEPA filters capable of removing 99.99% of all airborne materials including dust, spores, and mycelia.
- The total construction cost was approximately $903 (historical pricing), covering both hoods, the fan housing, blower, pre-filters, plexiglass, and all miscellaneous supplies.
- The completed units were validated using open petri plate contamination tests, confirming they provided a sterile working environment.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Forister"s [[laminar-flow-hood]] design is directly applicable to mycological work. Laminar flow hoods are essential equipment for [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]], providing the sterile environment needed for agar work, grain-to-grain transfers, [[comparison-agar-vs-liquid-culture]] inoculation, and other aseptic techniques. The DIY construction approach described by Forister and Burger makes professional-grade sterile workflow accessible to small-scale cultivators. The [[aact-brewer-design-principles-aeration-systems-ingham]] — HEPA filtration, proper air flow dynamics, and enclosure design — are identical whether the application is plant tissue culture or mushroom mycology.
