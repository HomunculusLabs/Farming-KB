## Foliar Pests and AACT

## Description

Foliar pests — including aphids, mites, thrips, whiteflies, caterpillars,
and various other leaf-feeding insects — represent a major category of crop
damage that [[elaine-ingham]] addresses through [[actively-aerated-compost-tea]]
(AACT) applications. Ingham dedicates a significant section of her field guide
to the mechanisms by which AACT suppresses and repels foliar insect pests,
including bulb mites, [[tent-caterpillars]], and leaf-feeding insects in general.

## Classification

- **Category**: Pest management technique
- **Approach**: [[fukuoka-textdoc-arrowhead-scale-ladybug-biological-control]] through foliar [[fukuoka-microbial-ecology-decomposition]]
- **Key organisms**: [[ingham-aact-beauveria-fruit-fly-beneficial-bacteria-inoculant]], fungi, protozoa, and nematodes
  applied via AACT

## Key Facts

- AACT applied as a foliar spray creates a living biological barrier on leaf
  surfaces that deters pest feeding and reproduction.
- Beneficial fungi such as *[[beauveria-bassiana]]* [[molasses-and-sugars-in-compost-tea-brewing]] can directly
  infect and kill various insect pests.
- Foliar microorganisms alter the chemical profile of leaf surfaces, making
  plants less attractive to ovipositing (egg-laying) insects.
- A diverse foliar food web supports predatory insects and parasitoids that
  provide additional pest control.
- Ingham describes specific mechanisms for controlling mites, caterpillars,
  root grubs, wireworms, and other common agricultural pests.
- Regular AACT applications maintain beneficial populations on foliage that
  degrade over time due to UV exposure, rain, and new leaf growth.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

### Mechanisms of Pest Suppression

Ingham outlines several mechanisms by which AACT controls foliar pests:

1. **Direct pathogenicity**: *Beauveria* species in compost tea infect insect
   cuticles and kill pests through mycosis. Combined with humic acid,
   *Beauveria* shows enhanced effectiveness against fruit fly larvae.
2. **Feeding deterrence**: Microbial metabolites on leaf surfaces alter taste
   and texture, making treated plants less palatable to herbivorous insects.
3. **Reproductive disruption**: Some beneficial organisms produce compounds
   that interfere with insect reproduction and development.
4. **Predator attraction**: Foliar biology produces [[mycovolatiles-fungal-volatile-compounds]] that
   attract predatory insects and parasitic wasps.
5. **Physical barrier**: Biofilm formation on leaf surfaces can impede small
   pests like mites and thrips from moving and feeding freely.

### Specific Pests Addressed

- **Bulb mites**: AACT applications to bulbs and surrounding soil reduce mite
  populations.
- **Tent caterpillars**: Foliar sprays deter feeding and support natural
  enemies.
- **Leaf-feeding insects**: General suppression through foliar biological
  coverage.
- **Root grubs and wireworms**: Soil drenching with AACT targets soil-dwelling
  pest larvae.
- **Fruit fly larvae**: *Beauveria* + humic acid combinations show
  effectiveness.

## Management Notes

- Apply AACT to foliage every 7–14 days during [[query-how-to-protect-plants-from-frost-and-extend-the-growing-season]].
- Use both foliar spray and soil drench for comprehensive pest management.
- Maintain compost diversity to ensure broad-spectrum beneficial organisms.
- Combine AACT with habitat features that support predatory insects.
- Monitor pest populations and beneficial organism coverage regularly.
- Adjust AACT recipe (bacterial vs. fungal dominance) based on target pests.
