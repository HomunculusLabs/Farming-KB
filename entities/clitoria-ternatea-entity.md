# Clitoria ternatea

## Overview

Clitoria ternatea, commonly known as butterfly pea, Asian pigeonwings, blue pea, or Darwin pea, is a perennial climbing plant in the family Fabaceae. Native to tropical equatorial Asia, it is widely cultivated across Southeast Asia, India, Africa, and the Americas for its striking blue flowers, medicinal properties, and utility as a nitrogen-fixing cover crop.

The plant is perhaps best known in the culinary world for its vivid blue flowers, which are used as a natural food colorant in traditional Southeast Asian beverages and desserts. The flowers contain anthocyanin pigments called ternatins, which produce an intense blue color that shifts to purple in the presence of acid.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Division:** Angiospermae
- **Class:** Eudicots
- **Order:** Fabales
- **Family:** Fabaceae
- **Subfamily:** Faboideae
- **Genus:** Clitoria
- **Species:** C. ternatea
- **Authority:** L. (1753)
- **Synonyms:** Clitoria pilosula (Raf.) Fernald

The genus name *Clitoria* refers to the flower's resemblance to human female genitalia, as noted by Linnaeus. The species epithet *ternatea* refers to Ternate Island in the Maluku Islands of Indonesia, where the species was first collected by European botanists.

## Morphology

### Growth Habit

A vigorous perennial vine climbing 1.5–3 m via twining stems. In tropical climates it is evergreen; in subtropical regions it may die back to the root crown during frost.

The root system develops a deep taproot with prolific nodulation by rhizobia, enabling significant atmospheric nitrogen fixation (estimates range from 50–200 kg N/ha/year).

### Leaves

Pinnately compound with 5–7 ovate leaflets, each 2.5–5 cm long. Leaflets are elliptic to oblong with entire margins and a mucronate tip.

The foliage is relatively palatable to livestock and serves as a useful forage component in mixed tropical pastures.

### Flowers

Solitary or paired, axillary, 4–5 cm long. The most common form is deep blue-violet with a white or pale yellow center, though white-flowered varieties also exist.

Flowers are papilionaceous (typical pea family form) with a standard, two wings, and a keel. They bloom prolifically throughout the warm season and are attractive to butterflies, bees, and other pollinators.

### Fruit and Seed

Linear pods, 5–12 cm long and 8–12 mm wide, containing 6–10 seeds. Pods are dark brown at maturity and dehiscent.

Seeds are ovoid, 5–6 mm, brown to black with a hard seed coat. Germination is improved by scarification or hot water treatment.

## Distribution and Habitat

Native to the tropical Asian mainland and archipelagos, including India, Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia, and the Philippines. Now widely naturalized across tropical Africa, the Americas, and northern Australia.

Thrives in warm, humid tropical and subtropical environments from sea level to 1,500 m elevation. Tolerates a wide range of soil types but prefers well-drained, sandy loams with pH 5.5–7.5.

Established plants are moderately drought-tolerant due to the deep taproot but perform best with regular rainfall (1,000–2,500 mm annually).

## Bioactive Compounds

### Ternatins

The primary anthocyanins responsible for the flower's blue color. Ternatins A1–A3 are polyacylated delphinidin glucosides unique to *C. ternatea*, with potent antioxidant activity.

### Cyclotides

Cyclic peptides (cyclotides) isolated from the plant exhibit insecticidal, antimicrobial, and anti-HIV properties in laboratory studies. Clitorin and cycloviolacin are among the notable cyclotides.

### Flavonoids and Saponins

Root extracts contain kaempferol, quercetin, myricetin, and various triterpenoid saponins. These contribute to the plant's anti-inflammatory, anxiolytic, and nootropic effects documented in traditional medicine.

## Pharmacology and Traditional Uses

In Ayurvedic medicine, the root is classified as a *medhya rasayana* (brain tonic) and is used to enhance memory and cognitive function. Modern animal studies have confirmed nootropic effects, including improved memory retention and anxiolytic activity comparable to diazepam.

The plant is used traditionally as an antipyretic, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial agent across South and Southeast Asian medicine systems. Root preparations are used for urinary tract infections and digestive complaints.

**Caution:** Cyclotide-containing plants can be toxic in large quantities. Use only at established therapeutic doses. The seeds contain antinutritional factors and are not recommended for consumption without processing.

## Permaculture and Agroforestry Applications

As a nitrogen-fixing legume, *C. ternatea* is an excellent cover crop and green manure for tropical agroecosystems. It can be intercropped with cereals, tubers, and tree crops to improve soil fertility and reduce weed pressure.

The vine provides living mulch, erosion control on slopes, and fodder for livestock. Its rapid growth makes it effective for suppressing invasive weeds in tropical field margins.

The flowers support pollinator populations, making it a valuable companion plant in mixed tropical gardens. The deep root system improves soil structure and accesses nutrients unavailable to shallow-rooted crops.

## Culinary Uses

The most prominent culinary application is the use of butterfly pea flowers as a natural blue food colorant. In Thailand, *nam dok anchan* (butterfly pea flower drink) is a popular iced beverage that is striking blue and turns purple when lemon juice is added due to pH-sensitive anthocyanins.

The flowers are used to color rice (blue rice), desserts, cocktails, and teas throughout Southeast Asia. Dried flowers retain their pigment and are commercially sold as tea and food coloring.

Young pods are edible when cooked and consumed as a vegetable in parts of India and Southeast Asia, similar to other leguminous vegetables. The flowers are also used in traditional Malay and Nyonya cuisine.
