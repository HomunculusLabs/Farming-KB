## Description

Gley is a traditional pond and dam sealing technique that uses anaerobically fermented green plant material to create an impervious bacterial slime layer. The method permanently seals soil, sand, or small gravels, making it possible to construct water-tight storages in porous substrates without synthetic liners. It is one of the most cost-effective and biologically appropriate methods for sealing leaky dams and ponds in permaculture design.

## Classification

- **Category**: Earthworks / Water storage technique
- **Domain**: Permaculture, civil engineering, water management
- **Method type**: Biological sealing (anaerobic fermentation)
- **Material basis**: Green plant matter + anaerobic decomposition

## Key Facts

### Process

1. A continuous layer of 15–23 cm (6–9 inches) of mashed, wet, green, sappy plant material is laid over the base and gently sloping sides (1:4 ratio) of a pond or dam
2. The green material is covered completely with earth, cardboard, thick wet paper, plastic sheets, or rolled clay
3. The covering is sealed off from air to promote anaerobic fermentation
4. Bacterial slime is produced that permanently seals soil, sand, or small gravels
5. Once fermentation occurs, the pond is pumped or hosed full of water
6. Paper or plastic covering can be removed after sealing is complete

### Preferred Materials

- Fresh green manure from cattle (most preferred)
- Shredded sappy vegetation
- Lawn or second-cut grasses
- Papaya and banana leaves
- Vegetable tops
- Green manure crops
- Dolichos bean (can be grown in situ and rolled flat)

### Timing

- Cold areas: 1–2 weeks for fermentation
- Tropical areas: as little as 1 day



### Modifications

- Pen and feed cattle in the dry dam until the bottom becomes a manurial pug
- Strew bales of green hay and manure on ponds that leak slightly to produce algae sealing minor cracks
- Sow green crop in the dry dam, spray irrigate, and feed it off regularly with cattle



## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Gley sealing is directly relevant to permaculture-based cultivation systems where water storage is essential. For mushroom cultivation and mycology, gley-sealed ponds provide a low-cost method for establishing reliable water supplies for substrate preparation, humidity management, and irrigation of mushroom logs or beds. The anaerobic biological process aligns with permaculture principles of working with natural systems rather than against them.

The technique enables creation of water storage in sandy, gravelly, or shale substrates where conventional earth dams would leak. This is particularly valuable in arid and semi-arid regions where water retention is critical for any form of cultivation. The biological nature of the seal means it integrates with rather than disrupts local ecosystems.



## Comparison with Other Sealing Methods

- **Bentonite**: Volcanic ash–derived clay powder; swells when wet; more expensive; less reliable in some soils
- **Clay**: Carted in and rolled 23–30 cm thick; expensive if not locally available
- **Explosives**: Dynamite compaction of dam sides; dangerous and unpredictable
- **Impermeable membranes**: Plastic, neoprene, or concrete; most expensive; enables steeper banks; not biological
