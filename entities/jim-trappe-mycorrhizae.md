# Jim Trappe (Mycorrhizal Researcher)

## Description

Dr. [[jim-trappe]] is a renowned mycologist and emeritus professor at Oregon State University, recognized as the leading authority on [[ectomycorrhizal-fungi]]. Dr. [[elaine-ingham]] describes him as "the guru of ectomycorrhizal fungi" and credits his expertise as invaluable for understanding mycorrhizal colonization dynamics in soil systems. His work spans decades of research on the ecology, taxonomy, and function of mycorrhizal symbioses in forest and agricultural ecosystems.

## Classification

- **Type**: Person / Researcher

- **Specialization**: Ectomycorrhizal fungi taxonomy and ecology
- **Status**: Retired (at time of Ingham's writing)

## Key Facts

- Recognized as the preeminent expert on ectomycorrhizal fungi, the mycorrhizal type most commonly associated with trees and woody plants.
- Based at Oregon State University, providing a regional connection to Pacific Northwest mycological research.
- Dr. Ingham relied on consultations with Trappe for information on both ectomycorrhizal and vesicular-arbuscular mycorrhizal (VAM) fungi.
- His graduate student and post-doctoral researcher, Dr. Efren Cazares, performed much of the VAM and ectomycorrhizal sample analysis and trained SFI technicians in identification and measurement.
- Trappe's expertise bridged multiple mycorrhizal types: ectomycorrhizae (tree symbionts), VAM/endomycorrhizae (crop and grassland symbionts), and ericoid mycorrhizae (Ericaceae family symbionts).
- His work provided the taxonomic foundation for understanding how different mycorrhizal types contribute to soil structure, nutrient cycling, and plant health.
- Dr. Ingham notes it was "very helpful" to be able to consult with Trappe and Bob Linderman when information on VAM, endos, or ectos was needed.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Mycorrhizal identification**: Trappe's taxonomic work enables accurate identification of mycorrhizal types in soil samples, which is essential for assessing soil biological quality.
- **SFI methodology**: Through Dr. Cazares, Trappe's expertise directly influenced [[soil-foodweb-institute]]'s protocols for mycorrhizal colonization assessment.
- **Mycorrhizal thresholds**: Research influenced by Trappe established that VAM colonization below 40% correlates with root disease problems, while colonization above 30-40% sees these problems diminish.
- **Forest ecology**: His ectomycorrhizal research is foundational for understanding forest soil health, mushroom cultivation, and the ecology of edible mycorrhizal mushrooms (truffles, chanterelles, boletes).
- **Practical application**: Growers assessing mycorrhizal colonization in their soil can trace assessment methodology back to Trappe's taxonomic framework.
