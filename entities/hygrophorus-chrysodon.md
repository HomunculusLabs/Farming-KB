## Overview

*Hygrophorus chrysodon* is a species of agaric fungus in the family Hygrophoraceae, commonly known as the golden tooth waxy cap.

It is an ectomycorrhizal associate of both coniferous and deciduous trees, found across temperate Europe and parts of [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]].

The species is most recognizable by its white to cream cap covered in minute golden-yellow granules, especially near the margin.

It is considered edible, though not widely collected due to its small size and rarity.

## Taxonomy and Classification

Basidiomycota → Agaricomycetes → Agaricales → **Hygrophoraceae → *Hygrophorus* → *H. chrysodon***

The species was originally described by August Karl Joseph Corda in 1836.

The specific epithet *chrysodon* derives from Greek *chrysos* (gold) and *odon* (tooth), referring to the golden granules on the cap surface.

It belongs to the subgenus *Hygrophorus*, which includes species with dry, often fibrillose or granulose cap surfaces.

No significant taxonomic synonyms are in current use; Corda's original name remains accepted.

## Morphology

**Cap:** 2–6 cm diameter, convex to plano-convex, sometimes with a low umbo. Surface is white to pale cream, covered with fine golden-yellow granules (especially near the margin).

These granules are the remnants of a universal veil and are the most diagnostic field character.

**Gills:** Adnexed to subdecurrent, moderately spaced, white to cream. Waxy in texture, typical of the genus.

Gill edges are even and concolorous with the gill face.

**Stipe:** 3–6 cm tall × 0.4–1 cm thick, cylindrical, white, dry. Surface may have fine golden granules in the lower portion, similar to the cap.

The stipe is solid to slightly hollow, with a fibrillose white surface.

**Flesh:** White, thin, unchanging when cut. Odor is mild and pleasant, taste slightly mealy but not unpleasant.

**Spores:** White spore print. Basidiospores are ellipsoid to broadly ellipsoid, 6.5–8.5 × 4.5–5.5 μm, smooth, inamyloid.
