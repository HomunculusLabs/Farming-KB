## Overview

Fabiana imbricata is a species of evergreen shrub in the family Solanaceae, native to Chile and adjacent regions of Argentina. It is commonly known as pichi, pichi romero, or huevil, and has a long history of use in traditional Chilean medicine.

The plant forms dense, heath-like mounds of small, imbricate leaves with an appearance resembling certain Ericaceae species. It produces tubular white to pale pink flowers in abundance during spring.

The imbricate leaf arrangement reduces surface area exposed to wind and sun, an efficient [[water-conservation]] strategy in dry habitats. This adaptation is convergent with similar leaf forms in unrelated Mediterranean-climate shrub lineages.

Fabiana belongs to the tribe Cestreae along with Cestrum and Sessea, all sharing tubular flowers and berry-like fruits. Molecular studies confirm the monophyly of this New World lineage.

The shrub typically grows 0.5–2.5 meters tall with a dense, rounded to columnar habit. Older plants develop a gnarled, picturesque form with dark brown, fissured bark.

In cultivation, mature specimens can reach 3 meters in height under ideal conditions. The plant is slow-growing, typically adding 10–15 cm of height per year.

Stems are numerous, slender, and densely clothed in small, overlapping leaves. The branching pattern is ascending, giving mature plants a candle-like silhouette.

Leaves are sessile, scale-like, imbricate, measuring 3–7 mm long and 2–4 mm wide. They are ovate to lanceolate, with a leathery texture and a glaucous or grayish-green coloration.

The leaf surface is covered with a waxy cuticle that reduces water loss, an adaptation to the dry conditions of the species' native habitat. Leaves are closely pressed to the stem, giving branches a rope-like appearance.

Flowers are tubular, 12–18 mm long, borne singly or in small clusters in the leaf axils. The corolla is white to pale pink, with five short lobes that spread slightly at the apex.

Flowering occurs from October to January in the [[hamilton-ornamental-shrub-care]] in Mediterranean-climate gardens and as a medicinal plant. It is propagated from seed or semi-hardwood cuttings.

Seeds germinate readily after cold stratification for 4–6 weeks. Cuttings taken in late summer root under mist within 6–8 weeks.

The shrub prefers full sun and well-drained soil. It is extremely drought-tolerant once established and requires minimal supplemental irrigation in Mediterranean climates.

In Chilean traditional medicine, the aerial parts of Fabiana imbricata are prepared as infusions or decoctions. The primary traditional use is as a diuretic and urinary antiseptic.

The plant is also used traditionally as a digestive aid, carminative, and anti-inflammatory. Mapuche communities have used pichi preparations for treating liver complaints and rheumatic conditions.
