## Description


Anabaena is a genus of filamentous nitrogen-fixing cyanobacteria that forms a symbiotic relationship with the aquatic water-fern Azolla. One species of Anabaena "nests" in the glutinous sacs within Azolla fronds, creating a mutually beneficial partnership that is exploited in tropical permaculture systems as a source of nitrogen-rich mulch and green manure.

## Classification


- **Domain**: Bacteria
- **Phylum**: Cyanobacteria
- **Order**: Nostocales
- **Family**: Nostocaceae
- **Genus**: Anabaena
- **Symbiotic partner**: Azolla (water-fern, several species)
- **Relationship type**: Endosymbiotic — Anabaena resides within cavities in Azolla leaves

## Key Facts


- Anabaena fixes atmospheric nitrogen (N2) into biologically available forms (ammonia), which is shared with the host Azolla plant
- The Azolla-Anabaena partnership can fix up to 1.1 kg N/ha/day under optimal conditions
- Azolla hosting Anabaena has largely replaced kukui (Aleurites moluccensis) as a taro mulch in Hawaii
- In the dry-wet tropics, shallow flood-water bunds collect or produce these plants; they can be gathered as rolls of dry material when water dries out in winter, providing abundant garden mulch
- The nitrogen provided by this symbiosis makes it one of the most valuable organic nitrogen sources in tropical agriculture
- Unlike chemical fertilisers, the nitrogen fixed by Anabaena is released slowly as the Azolla fronds decompose, reducing leaching losses

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology


- **Tropical mulch production**: Azolla-Anabaena mats are harvested from ponds and shallow bunds as nitrogen-rich mulch for vegetable gardens, taro terraces, and tree plantings
- **Soil building**: When incorporated into soil, the decomposing fern-cyanobacterium complex adds both organic matter (humus) and nitrogen, improving cation exchange capacity (CEC) in leached tropical soils
- **Fungal substrate enrichment**: The nitrogen-rich organic matter supports diverse soil microbial communities including beneficial fungi involved in decomposition and nutrient cycling
- **Water management**: Azolla-Anabaena can be cultivated in seasonally flooded areas, turning otherwise unproductive water surfaces into mulch factories
- **Integration with taro systems**: Traditional Hawaiian agriculture uses Azolla as a living mulch in taro terraces, where it suppresses weeds and provides nitrogen directly to the crop
- **Compost activator**: The high nitrogen content makes Azolla-Anabaena biomass an excellent compost activator, accelerating the breakdown of carbon-rich materials like palm fronds and sawdust

## Cultivation Notes


- Grows best in still or slow-moving water with partial to full sun
- Requires phosphorus in the water for optimal growth and nitrogen fixation
- Can double its biomass every 2-3 days under ideal conditions
- Tolerates a wide pH range but prefers slightly acidic to neutral water
- Dies back in cold temperatures; tropical and subtropical climates are ideal
- Can be established in rice paddies, pond margins, and specially constructed shallow bunds
