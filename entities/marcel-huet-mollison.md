## Description

[[marcel-huet]] was a prominent aquaculture scientist and author of *Textbook of Fish Culture* (Fishing News Books Ltd., Surrey, U.K., 1975), described by Mollison as an excellent text on breeding and rearing of fish and coldwater species. Huet's work on fish culture in canals and flowing water systems provided key data that Mollison incorporated into [[permaculture]] aquaculture design principles, particularly the finding that canal culture can outperform pond culture by 40-60%. This finding has profound implications for [[permaculture-design]], validating the principle that edge and flow are key productivity factors.

## Key Facts

- Author of the standard reference *Textbook of Fish Culture* (1975), published by Fishing News Books Ltd., Surrey, U.K.
- Reported good yields from trout canals in Switzerland, both fed and unfed systems
- Documented that canal-based fish culture achieves 40-60% greater yields than broad-pond culture
- His research validated the permaculture principle that edge and flow increase productivity
- His 1964 report on Swiss trout canals provided Mollison with key data on slope-based aquaculture
- Recognized as providing essential knowledge on breeding and rearing of both warm-water and coldwater fish
- Published by Fishing News Books Ltd., a leading fisheries publisher
- His findings apply to both intensive and extensive aquaculture systems

## Contribution to Permaculture Design

Huet's research directly influenced Mollison's aquaculture chapter through several findings:
- Canal culture on slopes can outperform pond culture by 40-60% for trout production
- Flow-through canal systems provide superior oxygenation compared to static ponds
- Hill canal aquaculture requires reliable water intake and clay soils for permanence
- Edge vegetation is a critical factor in nutrient supply for canal-based fish systems
- Both fed and unfed systems can be productive, depending on design
- Supports the permaculture design principle that edge is the most productive zone in any system

## Relevance to Cultivation

Huet's work is directly relevant to permaculture aquaculture design because:
- Validates canal and channel culture as superior to pond culture for certain species
- Demonstrates that flowing water systems can achieve higher yields than static ponds
- Provides practical guidance for slope-based aquaculture integration with agriculture
- His data supports the permaculture principle of maximizing edge (canal edges vs. pond edges)
- Informs design of integrated hillside water systems combining fish culture with terraces
- Essential reference for anyone designing coldwater fish production in permaculture systems
- Demonstrates that [[appropriate-technology]] (canals) can outperform intensive methods (ponds)

## Design Implications from Huet's Research

- Canal width of approximately 2 m is productive for fish culture
- Reliable water intake is essential for permanent systems
- Clay soils provide necessary water retention for canal systems
- Vegetation of edges, shallows, and margins is critical for nutrient supply
- Hill canal systems can integrate with terrace agriculture for maximum land-water productivity
- The 40-60% yield improvement over pond culture makes canals a superior choice where terrain permits

## Broader Influence

- Huet's work is cited as a basic text alongside Bardach, Ryther, and McLarney's *Aquaculture*
- His research complemented the FAO's growing body of aquaculture knowledge in the 1960s-70s
- The canal culture concept he documented has been applied to warm-water species beyond trout
- His findings support the economic case for small-scale, appropriately designed aquaculture
- The principle that flowing water outproduces still water has applications in hydroponics and aquaponics

## Practical Application Example

A permaculture designer with a sloped site and reliable spring or stream water can apply Huet's findings by constructing narrow (2 m wide) canal systems along contour lines. The canal edges are planted with productive marginal species that provide fish food and nutrient input. The system produces 40-60% more fish per unit area than a pond of equivalent size, while also integrating with upslope and downslope agricultural systems.

The key requirements from Huet's research are: reliable year-round water supply, clay soils or clay lining for water retention, productive edge vegetation, and appropriate fish species selection for the climate and water temperature.
