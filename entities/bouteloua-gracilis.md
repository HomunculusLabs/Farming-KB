## Overview

**Bouteloua gracilis** (Kunth) Lag. ex Griffiths, commonly known as **blue grama**, is a perennial warm-season bunchgrass native to the [[holmgren-soil-humus-carbon-storage-rebuilding]]. Blue grama grasslands store an estimated 25–30 tonnes of carbon per hectare.

The species provides habitat and food for numerous wildlife species. Prairie dogs, bison, pronghorn antelope, and elk all graze on blue grama. Ground-nesting birds including the mountain plover and long-billed curlew nest in blue grama-dominated grasslands.

Blue grama exhibits a dual reproductive strategy. It reproduces primarily by seed but also spreads vegetatively through short rhizomes and stolons. Seed viability in soil banks can persist for several years, facilitating recovery after drought.

Mycorrhizal associations are common and ecologically significant. Blue grama forms [[permplant-willows-salix-erosion-control-fodder]], and low-maintenance turf. Its [[dighton-fungal-drought-tolerance-plant-water-relations]], low fertility requirements, and grazing tolerance make it an excellent species for sustainable land management.

Seed should be planted at 2–4 kg per hectare in early summer when soil temperatures exceed 18 °C. Germination is typically slow and irregular, taking 14–30 days. Seedling establishment benefits from a season of reduced competition from weeds.

No irrigation or fertilizer is needed for range plantings once established. In turf applications, minimal supplemental irrigation (25–50 mm per month) maintains green cover through summer. Mowing at 5–8 cm height produces an acceptable low-input lawn.
