# Botrytis cinerea

**Type:** Species (Fungus)
**Category:** Necrotrophic Filamentous Ascomycete
**Also known as:** [[alpha-1-3-glucan-fungal-pathogen-cell-walls]].
It causes gray [[hamilton-composting-and-organic-matter-management]], transitioning between biotrophic and necrotrophic
phases during infection. B. cinerea is also extensively studied in the
context of [[biodiversity-fungicolous-mycoparasitic-fungi]] target
it as a host.

## Taxonomy and Classification
- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Ascomycota
- **Subdivision:** Pezizomycotina
- **Class:** Leotiomycetes
- **Order:** Helotiales
- **Family:** Sclerotiniaceae
- **Genus:** Botrytis
- **Species:** B. cinerea

## Key Details

### Morphology
- Characterized by profuse, gray-brown, branched conidiophores
  bearing clusters of conidia at their apices (botryose
  conidiation)
- Conidia are ellipsoidal to obovoid, hyaline to pale brown,
  typically 7 to 14 by 5 to 9 micrometers
- Hyaline, septate hyphae with multinucleate cells
- Produces hard, black, irregularly shaped sclerotia as
  survival structures (1 to 5 mm)
- Apothecia (cup-shaped ascomata) are occasionally produced
  from sclerotia under appropriate environmental conditions
- Ascospores are hyaline, unicellular, and ellipsoidal

### Ecology and Habitat
- [[jeavons-climate-adaptation-growing-seasons]]
- Airborne conidia serve as primary inoculum for new infections

### Disease and Economic Impact
- Causes gray mold on grapes (bunch rot), strawberries,
  tomatoes, lettuce, beans, and numerous ornamentals
- Post-harvest losses on soft fruits and vegetables can exceed
  50 percent in uncontrolled conditions
- On grapes, infection can lead to noble rot under specific
  conditions, valued in dessert wine production
- Estimated global economic losses exceed 100 billion dollars
  annually across all affected crops
- Resistance to multiple fungicide classes has emerged in many
  agricultural regions

### Mycoparasitic Interactions
- Hypomyces chrysospermus can grow biotrophically inside the
  cells of B. cinerea under certain conditions (Rudakov 1981)
- Papulaspora species have been recovered from B. cinerea
  cultures using PDA plates overgrown with the host fungus
- Clonostachys (Gliocladium) rosea has been isolated as a
  mycoparasite on B. cinerea in soil baiting experiments
- [[trichoderma-harzianum]] has been successfully applied as a
  biocontrol agent against B. cinerea on apples (Tronsmo
  and Ystaas 1980)
- The success of biocontrol applications is often limited by
  insufficient moisture on leaf surfaces
