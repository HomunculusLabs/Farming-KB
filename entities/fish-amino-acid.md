# Fish Amino Acid

**Fish Amino Acid (FAA)** is a Korean Natural Farming (KNF) liquid fertilizer produced by fermenting fish waste or whole small fish with brown sugar to extract amino acids, nitrogen compounds, minerals, and other nutrients. Developed by Master Han Kyu Cho as part of the Cho Global Natural Farming system, FAA serves as a rapid-acting nitrogen source that supports vegetative growth, fruit development, and overall plant vigor. The preparation is particularly valued for its accessibility — it transforms fish processing waste into a valuable agricultural input, aligning with KNF principles of waste recycling and zero external input dependency.

## Principle

Fish tissue is rich in protein (15–25% by weight), amino acids, omega-3 fatty acids, vitamins (particularly B12, D3, and A), minerals (calcium, phosphorus, iron, zinc, iodine), and enzymes. When fish material is combined with brown sugar, osmotic pressure extracts cellular fluids while naturally occurring microorganisms (from the fish gastrointestinal tract, skin surface, and the brown sugar) initiate a combined proteolytic and fermentative process:

1. **Proteolysis:** Fish enzymes (proteases, peptidases) and microbial proteases break down fish proteins into free amino acids, peptides, and polypeptides
2. **Fermentation:** Lactic acid bacteria (LAB) and other microorganisms ferment available sugars, producing lactic acid and other organic acids that preserve the mixture
3. **Lipid transformation:** Fish oils are partially hydrolyzed to fatty acids, some of which have antimicrobial properties

The result is a dark brown, strongly odorous liquid rich in free amino acids that plants can absorb directly through leaves and roots without further microbial processing.

## Raw Material Selection

### Fish Types

* **Whole small fish:** Anchovies, sardines, mackerel, herring, and other small pelagic fish are preferred because their small size means bones, scales, and viscera are included without additional processing. This provides calcium, phosphorus, and trace minerals from bones and cartilage.
* **Fish waste:** Heads, bones, viscera, skin, and trimmings from larger fish (salmon, tuna, cod, tilapia) are excellent FAA material and represent a valuable waste-to-resource conversion. Fish processing byproducts often contain higher concentrations of certain nutrients than muscle tissue.
* **Crustacean waste:** Shrimp shells, crab shells, and lobster carapaces provide chitin (which stimulates plant defense responses against fungal pathogens) and additional calcium carbonate.
* **Quality criteria:** Fresh or freshly frozen material is essential. Spoiled or rotten fish produces FAA with excessive putrefactive compounds (ammonia, amines, hydrogen sulfide) that can harm plants and create persistent foul odors. Avoid fish from polluted waters due to heavy metal and contaminant accumulation.

### Sugar Selection

* **Brown sugar (unrefined):** The standard choice in KNF. Contains molasses residue with additional minerals (iron, calcium, magnesium, potassium) that supplement the fish-derived nutrients.
* **Molasses:** An acceptable alternative, particularly cane or blackstrap molasses. Higher mineral content than brown sugar and supports vigorous microbial activity.
* **Ratio:** Fish to sugar at 1:1 by weight is the standard KNF ratio. Some practitioners use 1:0.7 to 1:0.8 (less sugar) for faster fermentation and more concentrated product, but this increases the risk of putrefaction.

## Preparation Protocol

### Method 1: Standard Fermentation

1. **Preparation:** If using whole fish, chop into 3–5 cm pieces to maximize surface area. Fish waste can be used as-is or coarsely chopped.
2. **Mixing:** Layer fish material and brown sugar alternately in a container (glass, ceramic, or food-grade plastic — avoid metal, which can react with fish acids). Final ratio should be 1:1 by weight.
3. **Aeration:** During the first 3–5 days, the mixture should be stirred 1–2 times daily. This is critical — initial fermentation produces CO₂ and if the mixture is not aerated, anaerobic putrefaction will dominate, producing foul-smelling amines and ammonia instead of desirable lactic acid fermentation.
4. **Container management:** Cover with breathable cloth. Do not seal airtight. The container should have adequate headspace (20–30%) for foaming during active fermentation.
5. **Fermentation period:** 14–30 days at ambient temperature (20–30°C). Fermentation is complete when the fish material is fully liquefied and the liquid has a dark brown color with a sweet-sour-umami aroma (not foul or putrid).
6. **Extraction:** Strain through cheesecloth or a coarse sieve. The liquid is FAA. Solid residues can be added to compost piles.
7. **Storage:** FAA can be stored at room temperature for 6–12 months. Refrigeration extends shelf life. Adding a layer of brown sugar to the storage container helps maintain preservation.

### Method 2: Quick Extraction (Modified)

For faster production, some practitioners blend the fish-sugar mixture before fermentation:

1. Blend fish and brown sugar in a food processor with minimal added water
2. Transfer to fermentation container and proceed as above
3. Blending dramatically increases surface area and accelerates both extraction and fermentation
4. FAA can be ready in 7–10 days using this method
5. Risk: blended material heats up rapidly during fermentation and may require more frequent stirring to manage temperature

### Odor Management

FAA production generates strong odors. Strategies for managing this:

* **Location:** Produce in a well-ventilated area, preferably outdoors or in a dedicated space
* **Charcoal filtration:** Some practitioners place a charcoal layer on top of the fermenting mixture to absorb volatile odors
* **Closed system with gas venting:** Use a container with a one-way gas vent (similar to homebrewing airlocks) to contain odors while allowing CO₂ release
* **EM (Effective Microorganism) addition:** Adding commercial EM cultures at the start of fermentation shifts the microbial balance toward lactic acid fermentation and reduces putrefactive odors

## Nutritional Composition

FAA is primarily a nitrogen source but contains a broad spectrum of nutrients:

* **Amino acids:** The dominant component, including glutamic acid, aspartic acid, alanine, glycine, leucine, lysine, arginine, and other proteinogenic amino acids. Free amino acid content typically ranges from 5–15% of the liquid volume.
* **Total nitrogen:** 2–5% as both amino nitrogen and ammonium nitrogen
* **Minerals:** Calcium (1–3%), phosphorus (1–2%), magnesium, iron, zinc, manganese, iodine, and selenium from fish bones and tissues
* **Fatty acids:** Omega-3 fatty acids (EPA, DHA) and other fish-derived lipids
* **Vitamins:** B-complex vitamins (particularly B12), vitamin D3, vitamin A
* **Chitin:** From crustacean shells (if used), which acts as a bioelicitor triggering plant defense responses
* **Organic acids:** Lactic acid, acetic acid, and other fermentation products

## Application Methods

### Foliar Spray (Primary Method)

* **Dilution:** 1:500 to 1:1000 (1–2 ml FAA per liter of water)
* **Timing:** Apply during early morning or late afternoon. Early morning application is preferred as stomata are open and amino acids can be absorbed efficiently.
* **Frequency:** Every 7–14 days during vegetative growth stages
* **Growth stages:**
  * **Seedling to vegetative:** Apply at 1:500 dilution every 10–14 days to support leaf growth, stem elongation, and chlorophyll production
  * **Pre-flowering:** Reduce or stop FAA application 1–2 weeks before flowering to prevent excessive vegetative growth at the expense of flower/fruit development
  * **Post-fruit set:** Resume FAA at 1:1000 dilution to support fruit development and nutrient accumulation

### Soil Drench

* **Dilution:** 1:500 to 1:1000
* **Application:** Pour 0.5–1 liter of diluted FAA at the base of each plant
* **Timing:** Apply during transplanting and earldecompositiongrowth
* **Caution:** Excessive soil application can attract pests (flies, raccoons, rcompostingd cause nitrogen burn in sensitive plants

### Compost Additive

* FAA at 1:100 dilution accelerates compost decomposition by providing nitrogen for microbial protein synthesis
* Particularly beneficial when composting high-carbon materials (wood chips, sawdust, straw) that have low nitrogen content

## Synergistic Combinations

FAA is most effective when combined with other KNF inputs:

* **FAA + FPJ (1:500 each):** Balanced nitrogen and potassium/growth hormone application for vegetative growth
* **FAA + OHN (1:1000 each):** Combines nitrogen supplementation with disease protection
* **FAA + WCA (1:1000 each):** During fruiting, FAA provides nitrogen while Water-Soluble Calcium supports cell wall strength and fruit quality
* **FAA + BRV (Brown Rice Vinegar):** BRV's acetic acid helps stabilize FAA and enhances nutrient absorption through mild acidification of leaf surfaces

## Precautions

* **Over-application:** Excessive FAA causes lush, soft vegetative growth that is more susceptible to pests and diseases, delays flowering, and reduces fruit quality
* **Pest attraction:** Fish-based products can attract flies, ants, and other pests. Apply in the evening when pest activity is lower. Soil applications may require protective barriers.
* **Phytotoxicity:** Undiluted FAA or over-concentrated applications (>1:200) can burn leaves and roots, particularly on sensitive crops (lettuce, herbs, seedlings)
* **Odor:** FAA retains a fishy odor even after dilution. Consider this when applying near residential areas or in greenhouse operations
* **Sodium content:** Marine fish FAA contains sodium that can accumulate in soil with repeated application. In salt-sensitive crops or saline soils, use freshwater fish or limit application frequency
