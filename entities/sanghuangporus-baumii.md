# Sanghuang (Sanghuangporus baumii)

## Overview

Sanghuang is a medicinal polypore mushroom deeply rooted in Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM). The name "Sanghuang" (桑黄) literally translates to "mulberry yellow," referring to the mushroom's preferred host tree and the yellowish color of its pore surface.

For over two millennia, Sanghuang has been valued as a potent herbal remedy in East Asian medical systems. Historically, it was prescribed for a range of conditions including chronic diarrhea, gastrointestinal hemorrhage, and various gynecological disorders.

In modern times, Sanghuang has attracted significant scientific attention due to its remarkable anti-tumor, immunomodulatory, and anti-inflammatory properties. The fungus belongs to the genus *Sanghuangporus*, which was erected in 2012 to clarify the long-standing taxonomic confusion surrounding Sanghuang mushrooms.

Among the species in this genus, *Sanghuangporus baumii* is one of the most widely studied and commercially significant, and it underpins most of the modern pharmacological research on Sanghuang. Wild Sanghuang is now one of the most expensive medicinal mushrooms in the world, with demand far exceeding natural supply.
The commercial Sanghuang market in China alone is estimated to exceed several billion yuan annually, reflecting growing consumer interest in functional foods and natural health products derived from this mushroom.

## Taxonomy

*Sanghuangporus baumii* occupies the following taxonomic position within the fungal kingdom:

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Hymenochaetales
- **Family:** Hymenochaetaceae
- **Genus:** Sanghuangporus
- **Species:** S. baumii

The species was originally described under the basionym *Polyporus baumii* by Pilát in 1940. For much of the 20th century, Sanghuang mushrooms were classified within the genus *Phellinus*, leading to widespread misidentification in both commercial products and scientific literature.

Molecular phylogenetic studies in the early 2010s demonstrated that Sanghuang-forming species formed a distinct clade separate from *Phellinus* sensu stricto, prompting the establishment of the new genus *Sanghuangporus* by Zhou et al. in 2016.

The type species of the genus is *S. lonicericola*, while *S. baumii* remains one of the most economically important members. Phylogenetic analyses based on ITS, nLSU, and tef1-α gene regions have confirmed the monophyly of *Sanghuangporus* and its position within the Hymenochaetaceae.
The genus currently contains approximately 15 recognized species, with new species continuing to be described as molecular surveys expand across East and Southeast Asia.
Several of these newly described species may also possess significant medicinal properties, though pharmacological data remain limited for most.

## Morphology

The basidiocarp (fruiting body) of *Sanghuangporus baumii* is a tough, woody polypore that forms perennial to semi-perennial conks on living or dead hardwood trees. Key morphological features include:

- **Macroscopic form:** The fruiting body is typically hoof-shaped (ungulate) when growing on vertical surfaces, or effused-reflexed (spread out with a raised margin) when developing on horizontal or inclined substrates. Specimens can reach 5–20 cm in width and 2–8 cm in thickness, with a hard, extremely durable consistency.
- **Upper surface (pileus):** The exterior surface is hard and woody, colored rust-brown to dark reddish-brown, often with a slightly glossy or varnished appearance. Concentric growth zones are usually visible, reflecting seasonal periods of development. The surface may develop radial cracks in older specimens.
- **Pore surface (hymenophore):** The underside of the conk bears a poroid hymenophore that is yellow-brown to rusty-brown in color. Pores are circular to angular, typically 4–6 per millimeter. The pore surface bruises darker brown when handled. Tubes are stratified in multi-layered specimens.
- **Context:** The interior flesh (context) is corky to woody and brown in color, composed of a dense dual hyphal system. There is typically no distinct layer of sterile tissue separating the tubes from the context.
- **Basidiospores:** Spores are ellipsoid to broadly ellipsoid, hyaline, thin-walled, and smooth, measuring approximately 4–6 × 3–4 μm. They are inamyloid and non-dextrinoid.
- **Hyphal system:** Dimitic, comprising generative hyphae with clamp connections and thick-walled skeletal hyphae. Setae (thick-walled, dark, spine-like sterile elements) are present in the hymenium, typically 15–30 μm long and prominently brown.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Sanghuangporus baumii* is distributed across East Asia, with confirmed records from China, Korea, and Japan. In China, the species is found in temperate and subtropical forested regions, particularly in the northeastern provinces (Heilongjiang, Jilin, Liaoning), as well as in Shandong, Zhejiang, and Sichuan.

Korean populations are concentrated in mountainous areas, while Japanese records come primarily from Honshu and Kyushu. The species is a white-rot saprotroph and facultative parasite, meaning it can decompose dead wood but also colonize weakened living trees.

Its preferred hosts are broadleaf hardwoods, most notably mulberry (*Morus spp.*), which gives the mushroom its Chinese common name. Other documented host genera include oak (*Quercus*), chestnut (*Castanea*), cherry and plum (*Prunus*), and occasionally elm (*Ulmus*) and poplar (*Populus*).

The fungus typically fruits in mid-summer to early autumn following periods of warm, humid weather. Wild Sanghuang is considered rare and has been overharvested in many regions, contributing to its high market value — premium wild-harvested specimens can command prices exceeding several thousand dollars per kilogram.
Conservation concerns have been raised regarding wild populations, and several East Asian countries have initiated programs to protect remaining natural habitats and promote sustainable harvesting practices.

## Traditional Use

Sanghuang has one of the longest recorded histories of use among medicinal mushrooms in East Asia. It is mentioned in the *Shennong Ben Cao Jing* (Divine Farmer's Materia Medica), one of the oldest Chinese pharmacological texts dating to approximately 200–250 CE, where it is classified among the middle-grade herbs.

In TCM, Sanghuang is described as having a bitter flavor and neutral to slightly cold nature, and it is associated with the liver, heart, and kidney meridians. Historical and contemporary TCM applications include:
The *Ben Cao Gang Mu* (Compendium of Materia Medica, 1578) by Li Shizhen further documents Sanghuang's applications, noting its effectiveness in treating hemorrhoids, stomach pain, and jaundice.

- **Gynecological disorders:** Treatment of amenorrhea (absence of menstruation), metrorrhagia (abnormal uterine bleeding), and menorrhagia (excessive menstrual bleeding). Sanghuang has traditionally been one of the primary herbal remedies for female reproductive hemorrhage.
- **Gastrointestinal conditions:** Prescribed for chronic diarrhea, dysentery, and bloody stools (hematochezia). Its astringent and hemostatic properties were well recognized by classical practitioners.
- **Leukorrhea:** Used to treat excessive vaginal discharge, often in combination with other herbs such as *Atractylodes* and *Dioscorea*.
- **Topical applications:** Applied externally to promote wound healing and reduce swelling from traumatic injuries, sprains, and contusions. Poultices of dried, powdered Sanghuang were traditionally applied to cuts and abrasions.
- **General tonification:** In Korean traditional medicine (Hanyak), Sanghuang has been used as a general tonic to restore vitality, reduce fatigue, and support overall health, particularly in postpartum women and the elderly. Korean royalty historically valued Sanghuang as a longevity-promoting substance.

## Chemical Constituents

Phytochemical investigations of *Sanghuangporus baumii* have revealed a diverse array of bioactive compounds, many of which contribute to its pharmacological activities:

- **Hispolon:** A phenolic compound and one of the most studied bioactive molecules from Sanghuang. Hispolon has demonstrated potent anti-tumor, anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant effects in preclinical studies, with IC₅₀ values in the low micromolar range against several cancer cell lines.
- **3,4-Dihydroxybenzaldehyde (protocatechualdehyde):** A simple phenolic aldehyde with antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and neuroprotective activities that contributes to the free radical scavenging capacity of Sanghuang extracts.
- **Phelligridins A–H:** A family of hispidin-like phenolic compounds isolated from *Sanghuangporus* species. Phelligridins exhibit significant antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and cytotoxic activities against various cancer cell lines, with phelligridin D and G being among the most pharmacologically active.
- **Polysaccharides:** High molecular weight polysaccharides, particularly β-glucans, are among the most important immunomodulatory constituents. These water-soluble compounds activate macrophages, NK cells, and T lymphocytes, and are responsible for much of the tumor-inhibitory activity in vivo. The polysaccharide fraction typically constitutes 3–8% of the dry weight.
- **Triterpenoids:** Including inotodiol and related lanostane-type triterpenes, which possess anti-tumor, anti-inflammatory, and hepatoprotective properties. Content varies significantly with growth conditions and substrate composition.
- **Ergosterol and sterols:** Ergosterol (provitamin D₂) is the predominant sterol, along with ergosterol peroxide and other sterol derivatives that exhibit anti-inflammatory and anti-tumor activities through modulation of PI3K/Akt and MAPK signaling pathways.
- **Other compounds:** Additional constituents include flavonoids, organic acids, lectins, and small peptides identified through HPLC-MS and NMR spectroscopy.

## Pharmacological Research

Modern pharmacological research on *Sanghuangporus baumii* has substantially validated many of its traditional uses and uncovered new therapeutic potentials:

### Anti-Tumor Activity

Sanghuang extracts have demonstrated broad-spectrum anti-tumor activity against numerous cancer cell lines in vitro, including those derived from lung, liver, breast, colon, gastric, and cervical cancers. The anti-tumor mechanisms are multifaceted:

Direct cytotoxic effects occur via apoptosis induction, with hispolon and phelligridins triggering mitochondrial-mediated apoptotic pathways and upregulating pro-apoptotic proteins (Bax, caspase-3, caspase-9) while downregulating anti-apoptotic Bcl-2.

Cell cycle arrest occurs at G2/M and G0/G1 phases, mediated through modulation of cyclin-dependent kinases and their regulatory partners.

Angiogenesis inhibition is achieved through suppression of VEGF signaling and downregulation of VEGF receptor expression on endothelial cells.

Anti-metastatic effects are mediated by downregulating matrix metalloproteinases (MMP-2 and MMP-9), which are critical for tumor cell invasion and extracellular matrix degradation.

In vivo studies using mouse xenograft models have confirmed significant tumor growth inhibition, especially with polysaccharide-rich fractions that operate primarily through immune-mediated mechanisms rather than direct cytotoxicity. Tumor inhibition rates of 40–70% have been reported in some studies.

### Immunomodulation

Polysaccharide fractions from *S. baumii* are potent immunomodulators. They enhance innate immune function by activating macrophages (increasing phagocytic activity, NO production, and cytokine secretion of TNF-α, IL-12, and IFN-γ), stimulating NK cell cytotoxicity, and promoting dendritic cell maturation.

Adaptive immune responses are also modulated through increased proliferation of T and B lymphocytes and enhanced antibody production. These immunomodulatory effects underpin much of the anti-tumor activity observed in animal models, as the activated immune system recognizes and destroys tumor cells more effectively.

Sanghuang polysaccharides have also been shown to enhance the efficacy of conventional chemotherapy drugs in combination therapy studies, suggesting potential as an adjuvant cancer treatment.

### Anti-Inflammatory Effects

Sanghuang extracts suppress pro-inflammatory mediators including TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, prostaglandin E₂ (PGE₂), and nitric oxide (NO) in lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-stimulated macrophages.

A key mechanism is the inhibition of NF-κB signaling pathway activation, which serves as a master regulator of inflammatory gene expression. Hispolon and phelligridins are the principal anti-inflammatory constituents, validated in animal models of carrageenan-induced paw edema, DSS-induced colitis, and adjuvant-induced arthritis.
Nitric oxide production is reduced through downregulation of inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS) expression at the transcriptional level.

Additional anti-inflammatory mechanisms include suppression of COX-2 expression and inhibition of MAPK signaling cascades (p38, JNK, ERK).

### Hepatoprotective Effects

*Sanghuangporus baumii* extracts protect the liver from chemical-induced injury caused by agents such as carbon tetrachloride (CCl₄), acetaminophen, and alcohol. Hepatoprotective mechanisms include reduction of serum transaminase levels (ALT, AST), attenuation of lipid peroxidation, enhancement of hepatic antioxidant enzyme activity (SOD, catalase, glutathione peroxidase), and suppression of hepatic stellate cell activation.

The suppression of hepatic stellate cell activation is particularly important, as it is central to the development of liver fibrosis and cirrhosis. Sanghuang extracts have shown anti-fibrotic effects in CCl₄-induced fibrosis models in rats.
