## Green Mulch

## Description

Green mulch refers to fresh, nitrogen-rich organic materials applied to the soil surface around plants to promote bacterial activity in [[the-soil-food-web]]. Unlike brown mulches (aged, carbon-rich materials that promote fungi), green mulches feed bacterial populations, making them the preferred mulch type for vegetable gardens, annual flower beds, and other plantings that thrive in bacterially dominated soils. Green mulch is one of the three primary [[soil-food-web]] management tools.

## Classification

- **Category**: Organic surface amendment
- **Type**: Bacteria-supporting mulch
- **Role**: Bacterial food source, weed suppression, moisture retention

## Key Facts

- Made from fresh, green plant materials that retain their sugar content even after cutting
- Lawn clippings are an ideal green mulch—they retain sugars even after chlorophyll fades and they turn brown
- Straw is also considered a green mulch because it contains residual sugars from when it was cut
- [[alfalfa-meal]] is another excellent green mulch material that strongly promotes bacteria
- Finer-textured green mulches support higher bacterial populations due to greater surface area
- Wetter (but not anaerobic) green mulches promote more bacterial growth
- Green mulch suppresses weed germination by blocking light and tying up nutrients at the soil-mulch interface
- Retains soil moisture by reducing evaporation
- The smell test determines proper moisture: if it smells bad, it is too wet and anaerobic—add air and reduce water

## Application in Vegetable and Annual Gardens

Green mulch is specifically recommended for plants that prefer bacterially dominated soils:

1. Apply around vegetables and annuals after the soil has warmed in spring
2. Layer 2–3 inches of green mulch around established plants, leaving space around stems
3. Replenish as the mulch decomposes and thins
4. Apply in autumn as well, so bacteria can work during winter to break down organic matter before spring planting
5. Side-dress with organic microbe food (NPK all below 10) before mulching
6. Follow with bacterially dominated compost tea as soil drench

## Why Green Mulch Works for Vegetables

Vegetables, annuals, and grasses evolved in early successional environments with bacterially dominated soils. Green mulch recreates these conditions by:

- Providing easily digestible organic matter that bacteria thrive on
- Promoting the bacterial decomposition pathway that produces nitrate nitrogen
- Supporting protozoa and nematode populations that graze on bacteria and release plant-available nutrients
- Maintaining the moist, nitrogen-rich surface conditions that bacteria prefer
- Preventing the fungal colonization that would shift soil toward ammonium nitrogen production

## Relationship to Brown Mulch

The green/[[brown-mulch]] distinction is fundamental to soil food web gardening:

| Feature | Green Mulch | Brown Mulch |
|---------|------------|-------------|
| Material | Fresh grass clippings, straw, alfalfa | Dry leaves, bark chips, twigs |
| C:N ratio | Lower (nitrogen-rich) | Higher (carbon-rich) |
| Promotes | Bacteria | Fungi |
| Best for | Vegetables, annuals, lawns | Trees, shrubs, perennials |
| Nitrogen form | Nitrate | Ammonium |
| Succession stage | Early | Late |

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Green mulch is the go-to surface management tool for annual food production using soil food web principles. It replaces the traditional practice of rototilling (which artificially created bacterial dominance through destruction) with a biological approach that builds bacterial populations while maintaining soil structure. For mycologists, green mulch conditions are useful to understand as the opposite of what most cultivated mushrooms require—green mulch actively discourages the fungal dominance that mushroom-producing fungi depend on.

## Relationship to Other Entities

- **Brown mulch**: The fungal counterpart; both are applied based on the target plant community
- **[[bacterial-compost]]**: Applied under green mulch as a soil inoculant
- **Compost tea**: Bacterially dominated tea complements green mulch applications
- **No-till gardening**: Green mulch enables bacterial dominance without destructive tilling
- **Lawn clippings**: The most readily available green mulch material for home gardeners
