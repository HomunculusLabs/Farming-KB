## Robert Rogers

## Description

Robert Rogers is an author, herbalist, and mycologist best known for his comprehensive reference work The Fungal Pharmacy: The Complete Guide to [[medicinal-mushrooms]] and [[lichens]] of North America. Rogers is recognized for his extensive documentation of the medicinal properties of fungi, providing detailed medical referencing for hundreds of mushroom species. [[tradd-cotter]] specifically recommends pairing his cultivation guide with Rogers' book for comprehensive coverage of both growing techniques and medicinal properties.

## Classification

- **Category**: Person — author and herbalist
- **Primary field**: Medicinal mycology and herbalism
- **Notable work**: The Fungal Pharmacy (2011)
- **Geographic focus**: North America

## Key Facts

- **Author of The Fungal Pharmacy**: This reference work covers the medicinal properties of mushrooms and lichens with extensive medical referencing, making it one of the most comprehensive guides to medicinal fungi available.
- **Cotter's recommended companion text**: Cotter explicitly states that he would strongly encourage pairing his growing guide with The Fungal Pharmacy by Robert Rogers, noting that Rogers has included the medical referencing in extensive detail.
- **Comprehensive medicinal documentation**: Where Cotter's work focuses on [[cannabis-sativa-indoor-cultivation-techniques]] and practical growing information, Rogers' work fills the gap by providing in-depth medicinal property references, chemical analyses, and traditional use documentation.
- **Bridges traditional and modern knowledge**: Rogers' work integrates traditional herbal knowledge with modern scientific research on fungal compounds.
- **Focus on North American species**: The book provides region-specific information valuable for cultivators and foragers working in North American ecosystems.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

### Essential Reference for Medicinal Cultivation

For cultivators growing mushrooms for their medicinal properties — such as Ganoderma (reishi), Trametes (turkey tail), Hericium (lion's mane), and the almond-scented Agaricus species — Rogers' book provides the detailed pharmacological and therapeutic context that cultivation guides typically omit.

### Complementary to Cultivation Guides

The relationship between Cotter's cultivation manual and Rogers' medicinal reference illustrates a key principle for serious cultivators: growing knowledge and medicinal knowledge are complementary but distinct domains. Cotter covers how to grow; Rogers covers why to grow — specifically the health benefits and [[cordyceps-cancer-bioactive-compounds]].

### Medicinal Property Categories Covered

Based on Cotter's references to the need for Rogers' work, the medicinal properties too extensive to list in a cultivation guide include:
- [[blesching-cannabis-immune-system-modulation]] and enhancement
- Blood pressure and blood sugar regulation
- Cholesterol reduction and cardiovascular support
- Antiviral and antibacterial properties
- Anti-inflammatory compounds
- Cancer cell line suppression data
- Traditional use in Chinese and other [[herbal-medicine]] systems
- [[dighton-fungal-nutritional-composition-animal-diets]] and protein content analysis

### Value for Commercial Cultivators

Market vendors and commercial growers who need to communicate the health benefits of their products — such as the almond portabella fetching $15 to $20 per pound partly on nutritional merits — will find Rogers' detailed referencing invaluable for marketing, labeling, and customer education.
