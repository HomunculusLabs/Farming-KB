## Caffeine

## Overview

**Caffeine** (1,3,7-trimethylxanthine) is a purine alkaloid and the most widely consumed psychoactive substance in the world. It is found naturally in over 60 plant species and acts as a central nervous system stimulant primarily through adenosine receptor antagonism. Global consumption exceeds 120,000 tonnes annually, with coffee, tea, and soft drinks serving as the primary dietary sources.

## Chemical Properties

- **Molecular formula:** C₈H₁₀N₄O₂
- **Molecular weight:** 194.19 g/mol
- **IUPAC name:** 1,3,7-trimethyl-3,7-dihydro-1H-purine-2,6-dione
- **Structure type:** Methylxanthine (purine derivative)
- **Appearance:** White, odorless, bitter-tasting crystalline powder
- **Melting point:** 238 °C (460 °F)
- **Solubility:** Moderately soluble in water (2.17 g/100 mL at 25 °C); more soluble in hot water and organic solvents
- **pKa:** 10.4 (at 25 °C)
- **CAS number:** 58-08-2

Caffeine is structurally related to the nucleosides adenosine and guanosine, which underlies its ability to interact with adenosine receptors.

## Pharmacology

### Receptor Activity

Caffeine is a non-selective competitive antagonist at all four adenosine receptor subtypes:

- **A₁ receptors** — blockade produces wakefulness, increased alertness, tachycardia, and diuresis. Located throughout the cortex, heart, and kidneys.
- **A₂A receptors** — blockade increases dopamine signaling in the basal ganglia, contributing to the reinforcing and motor-stimulating effects. Located in striatum and vasculature.
- **A₂B receptors** — less affinity; blockade may contribute to peripheral effects including bronchodilation.
- **A₃ receptors** — lowest affinity; role in caffeine pharmacology is minimal.

Caffeine has a Ki of ~44 μM at A₁ and ~40 μM at A₂A receptors, making it a relatively low-affinity antagonist that requires milligram-range doses for functional blockade.

### Additional Mechanisms

- **Phosphodiesterase inhibition** — inhibits cAMP and cGMP phosphodiesterases at high concentrations (millimolar range); not clinically relevant at typical dietary doses
- **Calcium mobilization** — releases calcium from intracellular stores via ryanodine receptors at supratherapeutic concentrations
- **GABA_A receptor antagonism** — weak blockade at high doses may contribute to anxiogenic effects
- **Acetylcholinesterase inhibition** — minimal effect at dietary doses

### Pharmacokinetics

- **Absorption:** Rapid and nearly complete via oral administration. Peak plasma levels in 30–60 minutes.
- **Bioavailability:** ~99% oral
- **Volume of distribution:** 0.6 L/kg
- **Plasma protein binding:** 17–36%
- **Half-life:** 3–7 hours in healthy adults (longer in pregnancy, liver disease, or with oral contraceptives)
- **Metabolism:** Primarily hepatic via CYP1A2 to paraxanthine (~84%), [[comparison-caffeine-plants-yerba-mate-vs-guayusa-vs-yaupon-vs-tea]]
