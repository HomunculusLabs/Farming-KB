## Description

*[[phyla-nodosa]]* (formerly *Lippia nodosa*) is a low-growing, mat-forming perennial plant recommended by [[bill-mollison]] as a nitrogenous mulch source for taro terrace bunds. It is a creeping herb of the Verbenaceae family that spreads along the ground, forming dense mats. In taro polyculture systems, it serves as living mulch on bunds between wet terraces, where it is naturally trimmed at the water level by fish such as [[tilapia]] and grass carp. This plant exemplifies the [[permaculture]] principle of self-managing elements within a designed system.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Family**: Verbenaceae (verbena family)
- **Genus**: Phyla
- **Species**: *P. nodosa* (syn. *Lippia nodosa*)
- **Growth form**: Low, creeping, mat-forming perennial herb
- **Habitat**: Moist to wet soils, margins of water bodies, bunds of terraces

## Key Facts

- Formerly classified as *Lippia nodosa*; reclassified to genus *Phyla*
- Spreads as a dense ground cover, remaining short and compact
- Functions as a nitrogenous mulch when cut or trimmed
- Grows on taro terrace bunds where it can be managed by grazing fish
- Tilapia and grass carp naturally trim the plant at the water line
- Provides soil stabilization on bunds and canal banks
- Contributes to the nitrogen budget of integrated aquaculture systems
- [[companion-planting]] with *Dolichos hosei* for additional nitrogen fixation
- Dense mat suppresses weeds on bund surfaces
- Requires no mowing or mechanical maintenance when integrated with fish

## Role in Taro Polyculture

In Hawaiian taro culture, Mollison recommends planting bunds to *Phyla nodosa* (and *Dolichos hosei*) as useful nitrogenous mulch sources. The plant:
- Remains short and naturally trimmed at the water level by herbivorous fish
- Fixes or accumulates nitrogen for the terrace system
- Provides habitat for beneficial insects along bund margins
- Stabilizes bund soil against erosion from water movement and heavy rainfall
- Requires no mechanical cutting when fish are present in the system
- Creates a living edge between aquatic and terrestrial zones

## Related Species

- *Phyla nodiflora* (syn. *Lippia nodiflora*) — common frogfruit, widely distributed in warm regions worldwide
- *Phyla lanceolata* — lanceleaf frogfruit, North American native used as ground cover
- The genus *Phyla* includes several species used as ground cover and forage plants
- All members of the genus share the creeping, mat-forming habit suited to marginal planting

## Relevance to Cultivation

*Phyla nodosa* exemplifies the permaculture principle of using plants that perform multiple functions:
- Living mulch on terrace bunds reducing maintenance needs
- Fish forage at water margins creating a food source for aquaculture species
- Nitrogen contribution to the system reducing external fertilizer needs
- Soil stabilization and erosion control protecting bund integrity
- Low maintenance through self-trimming when fish are present
- Weed suppression through dense mat formation reducing competition

This makes it an ideal component of integrated aquaculture-taro polyculture systems in tropical and subtropical regions, particularly where wet terrace cultivation is practiced.

## Integration Notes

- Plant on bunds between wet terraces for optimal positioning
- Combine with *Dolichos hosei* for diverse nitrogen sources and growth habits
- Introduce Tilapia or grass carp to the terrace system for natural trimming
- Can be used in any wetland margin system where low ground cover is needed
- Provides excellent erosion control on canal and ditch banks
- Suitable for chinampa and swamp garden systems as well as taro terraces
- Also appropriate for rice paddy bunds and aquaculture pond margins

## Ecological Functions

- Provides habitat and forage for beneficial insects near water margins
- Root systems help stabilize bund soil structure and prevent slumping
- Decomposing plant material contributes organic matter to adjacent water systems
- The dense mat creates microclimate benefits, reducing soil surface temperature
- Flowers provide nectar source for pollinators in aquatic margin zones

## Propagation and Establishment

- Propagates readily from stem cuttings placed in moist soil
- Establishes quickly in wet conditions, forming dense cover within weeks
- Can be planted along new bunds immediately after construction
- Vegetative spread means a small number of starter plants can cover extensive areas
- Tolerant of periodic flooding and water level fluctuations common in terrace systems
