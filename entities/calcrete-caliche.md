## Description

Calcrete, also known as **caliche**, **platin**, or **kunkar**, is a hard, mainly level subsurface concretion found approximately 0.5–1.0 metres below granular or sandy topsoil. It is composed primarily of calcium triphosphate and is characteristic of coral islands and the downwind areas of desert borders.

## Classification

- **Category**: Geological / Soil formation
- **Type**: Subsurface concretion (cemented soil layer)
- **Composition**: Calcium triphosphate, calcium/magnesium compounds
- **Colour**: Whitish to creamy
- **Related terms**: Caliche, platin, kunkar, duricrust

## Key Facts

1. **Formation**: Develops in arid and semi-arid environments where calcium carbonates or phosphates precipitate and cement soil particles together below the surface.
2. **Depth**: Typically occurs 0.5–1.0 m below the soil surface, forming a hard, impermeable barrier.
3. **Coral islands**: Particularly common on atolls where calcium triphosphate accumulates. Freshwater deposits develop below the caliche layer.
4. **Aggravation**: The condition is worsened by the addition of superphosphate fertilisers, which can accelerate cementation.
5. **Identification**: Calcrete releases bubbles of carbon dioxide when tested with acid (vinegar).
6. **Wind-throw risk**: Trees planted above unbroken calcrete develop shallow, lateral root systems and are highly susceptible to wind-throw.
7. **Building material**: Broken calcrete can be used as a construction material and forms a safe roof for tunnels or dugouts.
8. **Atoll cultivation**: On atolls, digging down to near the water table through the caliche and mulching thickly (using Casuarina, palm, and household waste) is the primary cultivation strategy.

## Management and Remediation

- **Mechanical breaking**: Calcrete must be broken open mechanically (ripping, explosive shattering) before planting trees to allow deep root penetration.
- **Deep mulch pits**: Excavating through the calcrete layer and filling with organic matter and compost enables productive gardening.
- **Grow pits**: On atolls, large grow pits measuring 3–6 m wide by 10–12 m long by 3 m deep are dug to access the water table below the caliche.
- **Avoid superphosphate**: Use of superphosphate should be avoided as it worsens calcium/magnesium concretion.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Root barrier**: The impermeable nature of calcrete prevents deep root penetration, severely limiting plant growth and mycorrhizal development.
- **Mycorrhizal impact**: Fungal networks cannot penetrate the cemented layer, reducing beneficial soil biology in affected zones.
- **[[water-management|water-management]]**: Calcrete layers impede drainage and can create perched water tables, affecting both plant and fungal communities.
- **Soil biology**: Breaking the calcrete and introducing organic matter, compost, and mulch can restore biological activity including [[fungal-decomposition|fungal-decomposition]] pathways.
- **Atoll food systems**: Understanding calcrete is essential for establishing productive food gardens on coral atolls and arid coastal soils.
