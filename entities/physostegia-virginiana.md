# Physostegia virginiana

## Overview

Physostegia virginiana, commonly called obedient plant or false dragonhead, is a rhizomatous perennial in the family Lamiaceae. It is native to [[polypore-fungi-eastern-north-america]], where it grows in moist meadows, prairies, and along stream margins from Quebec to Manitoba south to Florida and Texas.

The common name "obedient plant" refers to the curious property of individual flowers, which remain in position when pushed to one side on the inflorescence axis. This trait makes it a popular garden plant for children and floral designers.

The species produces showy spikes of tubular pink to lavender flowers in late summer through early autumn, a valuable nectar source during a period when few native perennials are in bloom. It reaches 60 to 120 cm in height with upright, square stems typical of the mint family.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

- Kingdom: Plantae
- Clade: Tracheophytes
- Clade: Angiosperms

- Clade: Asterids

- Species: *Physostegia virginiana* Benth.
- Synonyms: *Dracocephalum virginianum* L.

The leaves are lanceolate to ovate-lanceolate, 5 to 15 cm long, with serrate margins. They are arranged oppositely on square stems, a characteristic diagnostic feature of the Lamiaceae.

Flowers are tubular, two-lipped, approximately 2.5 cm long, and range in color from white to deep pink or lavender. They are arranged in dense terminal spikes 10 to 25 cm long.

The "obedient" property results from a specialized articulation at the base of each pedicel. When a flower is pushed sideways, the joint holds the new position without springing back.

Each flower produces four small nutlets as fruit. The seeds require no special treatment for germination, though cold stratification improves uniformity.

The root system consists of aggressive, spreading rhizomes that enable the plant to form large colonies over time. This spreading habit is beneficial for naturalized plantings but can be invasive in confined garden spaces.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Physostegia virginiana* is native to a broad range of eastern North America. Its distribution extends from southern Quebec and Ontario west to Minnesota and North Dakota, and south to Florida and Texas.

It occurs naturally in moist to wet habitats including prairie potholes, stream banks, marsh edges, and wet meadows. The species tolerates periodic flooding and seasonal soil saturation.

In prairie ecosystems, it is a component of mesic to wet-mesic tallgrass prairie communities. It grows alongside big bluestem, switchgrass, and various aster and goldenrod species.

The plant adapts readily to garden conditions, tolerating clay soils and periodic drought once established. It performs best in full sun to light shade in consistently moist soils.

Several subspecies are recognized across the native range, differing in flower color and pubescence. Subspecies *virginiana* is the most widespread, while subspecies *ledinghamii* occurs in the northern Great Plains.

## Cultivation

*Physostegia virginiana* is hardy in USDA zones 3 through 9, demonstrating excellent cold tolerance. It prefers full sun and moist, fertile soils but adapts to partial shade and average garden conditions.

The plant spreads aggressively via rhizomes, making it suitable for large-scale plantings and naturalized areas. In smaller gardens, rhizome barriers or container planting are recommended to control spread.

Propagation is easy by division in spring or autumn. Root cuttings taken in late winter also produce new plants reliably.

[[green-cannabis-grow-bible-cloning-and-seed-propagation]] is straightforward with fresh seed sown in autumn or stratified seed sown in spring. Germination occurs within 14 to 21 days at 20 to 25°C.

Several cultivars are available in the nursery trade. 'Vivid' has deep pink flowers on compact plants 45 to 60 cm tall.

'Rosea' bears soft pink flowers, while 'Alba' produces pure white blooms. 'Variegata' features creamy white leaf margins but is less vigorous than the species.

The species is relatively disease-free and deer-resistant. [[cervantes-powdery-mildew-prevention-treatment]] may appear in humid conditions with poor [[stamets-growing-room-air-circulation-co2-management]] but rarely causes serious damage.

## Uses and Ethnobotany

*Physostegia virginiana* is primarily valued as an ornamental perennial for moist borders, rain gardens, and native plant landscapes. Its late summer bloom time fills an important niche in the perennial [[cervantes-garden-calendar-checklist]].

The flowers are highly attractive to hummingbirds, bumblebees, and sphinx moths, making it a key species for pollinator gardens. Long-tongued bees are particularly effective pollinators.

In traditional Native [[tree-daturas-brugmansia-south-american-ethnobotany]], the Meskwaki people used a root infusion as a treatment for digestive ailments. The plant was also used ceremonially in some Plains cultures.

The cut flowers are long-lasting in arrangements, maintaining their "obedient" positioning. This unique characteristic makes them popular with florists for specialized designs.

Research has identified iridoid glycosides in *Physostegia* species, including physoside and related compounds. These constituents show anti-inflammatory activity in preliminary laboratory studies.
