## Conservation Status

No formal conservation assessment. Widespread and common across its
pantropical range. Its coprophilous lifestyle depends on large herbivores,
potentially affected by changes in pastoral land use.

## Historical Context

Copelandia tropicalis was described by Károly Oláh in 1969 as part of his
monographic work on Panaeolus, based on 2-spored basidia, internally
granulated spores, and tropical distribution. Later transferred to
Copelandia by Singer and Weeks. Rolf Singer's treatment of the
Copelandian Panaeoli established the classification framework still used
for tropical bluing Panaeolus species. The species is widespread but
less studied than its famous relative C. cyanescens.

## Field Identification Tips

- Small, slender mushrooms growing directly on dung in tropical regions
- Strong bluing when stem is touched or bruised
- Mottled gills (dark spots on grayish background) — characteristic of
  Panaeolus
- Lemon-shaped, internally granulated spores under microscopy
- Absence of partial veil or annulus
- Compare with C. cyanescens (larger, no granulation) and
  C. cambodginiensis (4-spored, darker pleurocystidia apices)
