4. **Nut:** Round, deeply corrugated shell with thick green husk

## Juglone and Allelopathy

### What is Juglone?

Juglone (5-hydroxy-1,4-naphthoquinone) is a phytotoxic compound produced by black walnut (and to a lesser extent by butternut, hickory, and pecan). It is present in all parts of the tree — roots, bark, leaves, nut hulls, and wood — but is most concentrated in the roots and in the soil immediately surrounding root zones.

### Mechanism of Action

Juglone acts as an **oxidation-reduction (redox) cycling agent** that interferes with cellular respiration:

### Soil Persistence

- Juglone is released from roots into the rhizosphere and from decomposing leaves and husks
- Juglone concentration in soil decreases with distance from the tree (strongest within the drip line)
- Soil half-life: approximately 30–60 days under aerobic conditions
- Persistence is longer in poorly drained, compacted, or alkaline soils
- Juglone can persist in soil for **1–2 years after tree removal**, as root decomposition releases stored compounds
