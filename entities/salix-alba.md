## Salix alba

**Type:** Species (Tree)
**Category:** Riparian tree ([[comparison-jasmonate-vs-salicylic-acid-defense-signaling]],
the precursor to aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid), making it one of the
most important medicinal trees in human history.

## Taxonomy
- **Order:** Malpighiales
- **Family:** Salicaceae
- **Genus:** Salix
- **Species:** S. alba
- **Common relatives:** S. caprea (goat willow), S. viminalis (osier),
  S. babylonica (weeping willow), Populus spp. (poplars)

## Key Details
- **Growth rate:** Very fast growing, among the fastest of
  temperate trees
- **Height:** Up to 25 m
- **Water use:** Large amounts; root systems known to extend 50 m
  laterally seeking moisture and nutrient
- **Flood tolerance:** Tolerates prolonged periods of flooding;
  naturally adapted to riparian environments
- **Erosion control:** Excellent for sheet, gully, and slump
  erosion; salinity control in waterlogged areas; root systems
  bind soil along watercourses
- **Root hazard:** Should not be planted close to gardens, septic
  systems, building foundations, or orchards due to aggressive
  root spread seeking water
- **Timber:** Soft, light, flexible; useful for fencing, basketry,
  cricket bats, light construction, and traditional crafts
- **Coppicing:** Coppices vigorously; sustainable biomass
  production on short rotations; living willow structures
- **Fodder:** Provides useful fodder for livestock, especially
  during periods when other forage is scarce
- **Medicinal (primary value):** Bark contains salicin, a glycoside
  that converts to salicylic acid in the body; precursor to modern
  aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid); traditional remedy for pain,
  fever, and inflammation; one of the oldest known herbal
  medicines, documented in ancient Egyptian, Greek, and Chinese
  medical texts
- **[[coleman-overwintered-onions-and-early-spring-crops]] catkins provide important pollen
  and nectar for bees

## Cultivation
Easily established from cuttings; willows are among the easiest trees
to propagate. Simply push live cuttings (30-60 cm) into wet ground
during the dormant season. Prefers moist to wet soils in open,
