## Kundalini

## Description
Kundalini (Sanskrit: "coiled snake") refers to a form of primal energy believed in Tantric yoga to lie dormant at the base of the spine. In *The Psychedelic Experience*, Leary describes the activation of Kundalini energy as a natural phenomenon that occurs during the First Bardo (ego-loss) phase, where ecstatic energy movement is felt rising from the base of the spine through ganglionic centers (chakras) to the crown of the head.

## Classification
- **Category:** Technique / Energy Concept
- **Domain:** Tantric Yoga, Kundalini Yoga, energy physiology
- **Tradition:** Hindu Tantra, Tibetan Vajrayana Buddhism

## Key Facts
- Described as "the Serpent Power" in Tantric traditions
- Said to reside coiled at the base of the spine (muladhara chakra)
- When activated, rises through seven primary chakras (energy centers) to the crown (sahasrara)
- Tantric adepts devote "decades of concentrated meditation" to the release of these energies
- Associated with the burning sensation at the base of the spine and top of the cranium
- In the Bardo context, described as producing "the most intense feelings of joy and illumination"
- Ill-prepared subjects may interpret the experience in pathological terms

## Kundalini in the Psychedelic Context
Leary presents Kundalini activation as a spontaneous event during the ego-loss phase of psychedelic experiences. The base of the backbone "seems to be melting or seems on fire." With quiet concentration, the energy flows upward through the chakras to the brain, producing a burning sensation at the crown. Unlike traditional yoga, which requires decades of practice, psychedelics can trigger this energy movement in a single session. The critical factor is preparation — those who understand the phenomenon experience joy and illumination; those who don't may interpret it as a medical emergency.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology
Kundalini-type sensations are commonly reported during psilocybin experiences, particularly at higher doses. Subjects may describe tingling at the base of the spine, energy flowing up the back, heat in the head, or full-body vibratory sensations. Understanding this phenomenon helps guides reassure subjects that these are normal and positive signs of consciousness expansion, not medical emergencies. The R.C. Zaehner episode (q.v.) is cited as a case where Kundalini-type sensations were misinterpreted pathologically.
