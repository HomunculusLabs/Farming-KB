## Overview

A "cold house" is Eliot Coleman's term for an unheated greenhouse used for [[coleman-french-historical-winter-vegetable-production]]. In contrast to traditional heated "hothouses" (maintained at 55-65°F for crops like tomatoes and lettuce), the cold house relies entirely on passive solar gain and an inner layer of [[floating-row-cover]] to create a climate suitable for cold-hardy vegetables through the winter months. At [[four-season-farm]] in Harborside, Maine (Zone 5), cold houses enable year-round production despite outdoor temperatures reaching -20°F (-29°C).

## Terminology Note

Coleman defines greenhouse terms differently from British horticultural convention:

| Term | British Definition | Coleman's Definition |
|------|--------------------|---------------------|
| Cold greenhouse | Minimum 32°F | Unheated (may have backup heater for early warm-season starts) |
| Cool greenhouse | Minimum 45°F | Maintained just above freezing with supplementary heat |
| Greenhouse/Tunnel/Hoop house | N/A | Used interchangeably for pipe-frame, plastic-covered structures |

## Structure and Design

### Basic Cold House at Four Season Farm
- **Dimensions**: 30 feet wide by 96 feet long (standard)
- **Style**: Gothic-style hoop house (peaked roof for snow shedding)
- **Frame**: Pipe-frame construction
- **Covering**: Single layer of UV-resistant plastic (double-layer air-inflated is unnecessary without heat)
- **Orientation**: East-west axis for optimal winter solar gain
- **Floor**: Bare soil (serves as thermal mass / heat storage medium)

### Inner Protection Layer
- **Material**: Lightweight floating row cover fabric
- **Support**: Flat-topped wire wickets at 12 inches (30 cm) above soil
- **Wicket Spacing**: Every 4 feet (120 cm) along 30-inch-wide (75 cm) growing beds
- **Function**: Creates a second microclimate inside the greenhouse microclimate

## Temperature Performance

The double-layer system provides dramatic temperature moderation:

| Condition | Temperature |
|-----------|-------------|
| Outdoor (cold night) | -15°F (-26°C) |
| Inside greenhouse (no inner layer) | Slightly above outdoor |
| Under inner row cover | 15-18°F (-10 to -8°C) |
| Total moderation | ~30°F gain vs. outdoor |

The soil beneath the inner layer serves as the heat-storage medium, absorbing solar energy during the day and releasing it slowly at night. This is the same principle as natural ground heat, simply amplified and protected.

## Crop Performance

### Cold House Yield
- **Crops per year**: 3-4 per bed
- **Best for**: Baby-leaf salads, braising mixes, spinach, leeks, Asian greens, winter carrots, spring crops
- **Limitation**: Periodic freezing limits production of more tender crops like baby turnips and radishes during the coldest months

### Cold-Hardy Crops (30+ species)
Arugula, beet greens, broccoli raab, carrots, chard, chicory, claytonia, collards, dandelion, endive, escarole, garlic greens, kale, kohlrabi, leeks, lettuce, mache, minutina, mizuna, mustard greens, pak choi, parsley, radicchio, radish, scallions, sorrel, spinach, tatsoi, turnips, watercress.

Most tolerate temperatures to 10°F (-12°C) or lower under protection, provided they are spared the desiccating effects of cold winter winds.

## Key Principles

1. **Soil as thermal mass**: No water or stone ballast; soil alone stores and releases heat
2. **Double coverage**: Two layers of protection (greenhouse + row cover) create synergistic [[coleman-double-layer-climate-modification-temperature-data]]
3. **[[pf-tek-dual-chambered-terrarium-design-humidity-management]]**: Inner layer increases relative humidity, providing additional freeze protection
4. **Wind exclusion**: More critical than temperature — desiccation kills faster than cold
5. **No complicated technology**: No pumps, fans, buried insulation, or space-age materials

## Comparison: Cold House vs. Cool House

| Feature | Cold House | Cool House |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| Heat source | None (passive solar) | Minimal propane (just above 32°F) |
| Crops/year | 3-4 | 5-6 |
| Best crops | Hardy salads, spinach, leeks, carrots | All cold-house crops plus tender turnips, radishes |
| Spring carrots | May 5 harvest | April 1 harvest (6 weeks earlier) |
| Cost | Very low | Propane expense offset by extra crop value |
| Infrastructure | Simple hoop house + row cover | Hoop house + heater + concrete floor area |

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

The cold house concept has direct applications to [[accessible-mushroom-cultivation-for-disabilities]]:

- **Winter mushroom production**: Cold houses can moderate temperatures for cold-tolerant mushroom species (oyster, enoki, nameko, shiitake in certain phases)
- **Spawn run chambers**: The double-layer system maintains stable temperatures for [[mycelial-colonization]]
- **Low-cost infrastructure**: Unheated structures reduce the capital and operating costs of mushroom production
- **Integrated systems**: Vegetable-mushroom intercropping under the same protected structure
- **Soil thermal mass**: Ground heat can be harnessed for substrate temperature moderation
