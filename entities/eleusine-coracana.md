# Eleusine coracana

## Overview

*Eleusine coracana* (finger millet, ragi, African millet) is an annual cereal grass in the family Poaceae, widely cultivated as a staple food grain in Sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia.

It ranks among the most climate-resilient cereal crops, tolerating drought, low soil fertility, and high temperatures while producing reliable yields under conditions where maize and wheat fail.

Finger millet is exceptionally nutritious, with high calcium, iron, methionine, and dietary fiber content relative to other major cereals. It serves as a critical food security crop for millions of smallholder farmers.

## Taxonomy

**Kingdom:** Plantae

**Division:** Magnoliophyta

**Class:** Liliopsida

**Order:** Poales

**Family:** Poaceae

**Subfamily:** Chloridoideae

**Genus:** Eleusine

**Species:** E. coracana

**Authority:** (L.) Gaertn. (1788)

**Synonyms:** *Cynosurus coracanus* L., *Eleusine coracana* subsp. *africana*

**Progenitor:** The wild progenitor is *Eleusine coracana* subsp. *africana*, native to East Africa. Domestication occurred approximately 5000 years ago, likely in the Uganda-Ethiopia region.

## Morphology

**Habit:** Annual bunchgrass, 30–150 cm tall, erect to semi-erect, with tillering capacity producing 5–15 culms per plant.

**Leaves:** Linear-lanceolate, 20–40 cm long, 0.5–1 cm wide, glabrous to pubescent. Ligule short and ciliate. Leaf sheaths are prominent and compressed.

**Inflorescence:** Digitate arrangement of 4–7 spike-like racemes at the terminus of the culm, resembling a bird's foot (hence "finger" millet). Each raceme is 5–15 cm long.

**Grain:** Elliptical to rounded, 1.0–1.5 mm diameter, approximately 1000–3000 grains per inflorescence. Seed coat color varies from white through red, brown, to nearly black depending on cultivar. Thousand-grain weight is 2.5–3.5 g.

**Root system:** Fibrous, with relatively shallow primary roots but extensive lateral branching, enabling efficient exploitation of surface soil moisture.

## Distribution and Cultivation

Finger millet is cultivated across a broad geographic belt from East Africa (Uganda, Ethiopia, Kenya, Tanzania) through South Asia (India, Nepal, Sri Lanka).

India is the world's largest producer, accounting for approximately 55% of global production, followed by Uganda, Nepal, and Ethiopia. Global production exceeds 4 million tonnes annually on approximately 4 million hectares.

The crop is grown primarily under rainfed conditions at elevations from sea level to 2400 m. It performs well on marginal soils including sandy loams, red laterites, and acidic soils with pH as low as 4.5.

## Nutritional Profile

Finger millet has superior [[coleman-winter-greens-nutritional-quality-cold-sweetening]] compared to most major cereals, making it particularly valuable for vegetarian and subsistence diets.

**Key nutrients (per 100 g dry grain):**

- **Calcium:** 344 mg (highest among all cereals, 5–30× that of rice or wheat)
- **Iron:** 3.9 mg
- **Protein:** 7.3 g, with high methionine content relative to other millets
- **Dietary fiber:** 11 g (high insoluble fiber content)
- **Minerals:** Rich in phosphorus, magnesium, and manganese

The grain is naturally gluten-free, making it suitable for celiac patients. It has a low glycemic index (GI ~68), beneficial for diabetes management.
