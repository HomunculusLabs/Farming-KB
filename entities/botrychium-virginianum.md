## Overview

Botrychium virginianum, commonly known as the rattlesnake fern, is a perennial
fern in the family Ophioglossaceae. It is one of the most widely distributed
moonwort species, found across [[growing-gourmet-north-american-reishi-species]] medicine,
particularly among the Iroquois and Cherokee, for treating snakebites and other
ailments. Its common name derives from this traditional association.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

Botrychium virginianum (L.) Sw. was described by Olof Swartz in 1801, based on
an earlier Linnaean basionym (Osmunda virginiana, 1753). It is the type species
of the genus Botrychium.

The species belongs to the order Ophioglossales, one of the most ancient
lineages of extant ferns. Molecular phylogenies place Ophioglossales as sister
to all other leptosporangiate ferns.

Fronds emerge singly from a short, erect rhizome in spring. The total frond
height ranges from 15 to 45 cm.

The sterile segment is a single, broadly triangular, deeply divided leaf blade.
It is typically 10–20 cm long and 8–16 cm wide, with 3–4 pairs of pinnae that
are themselves divided into pinnules with crenate or dentate margins.

The fertile segment is a sessile or short-stalked panicle that arises from the
base of the sterile blade at a 45–90 degree angle. It is 3–12 cm long and
bears numerous spore-bearing sporangia that ripen from June to August.

Spores are small (20–35 μm), yellow, and monolete. They are released
simultaneously as the sporangia dehisce.

## Distribution and Habitat

Botrychium virginianum has a circumboreal distribution across North America,
Europe, and eastern Asia. In North America, it ranges from Newfoundland and
Quebec west to British Columbia and south to Georgia, Texas, and Arizona.

The species is most common in rich, moist deciduous forests, particularly
maple-beech and maple-basswood associations. It is also found in mixed
conifer-hardwood stands, alluvial floodplains, and occasionally in open meadows
at higher elevations.

It prefers well-drained loamy soils with high [[jeavons-climate-adaptation-growing-seasons]], making population estimates difficult.

## Ecology and Interactions

Botrychium virginianum is obligately mycorrhizal, forming associations with
[[arbuscular-mycorrhizal-fungi]] (Glomeromycota) in its roots and subterranean
gametophyte stage. The fungal partner supplies phosphorus and organic nitrogen
to the fern throughout its life.
