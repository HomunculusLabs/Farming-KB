## Piloderma crocerum

## Description

*Piloderma crocerum* (also referred to as *Piloderma croceum*) is an ectomycorrhizal basidiomycete fungus in the family Atheliaceae. It is a common associate of forest trees in temperate and boreal ecosystems, particularly birch (*Betula* spp.) and conifers. The species is noted for its bright yellow-orange pigmentation and its role in forest floor nutrient cycling.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Phylum**: Basidiomycota
- **Class**: Agaricomycetes
- **Order**: Atheliales
- **Family**: Atheliaceae
- **Genus**: *Piloderma*
- **Species**: *P. crocerum*

## Key Facts

- Forms ectomycorrhizal associations with birch and conifer species in temperate and boreal forests.
- Studied in the context of organic nitrogen utilization alongside other ectomycorrhizal fungi by Bending and Read (1996).
- When tested for nitrogen acquisition from bovine serum albumin (BSA), accumulated no more nitrogen from BSA than from basal medium.
- Classified as having relatively low enzyme competency for protein nitrogen acquisition compared to species like *Suillus bovinus*.
- Produces characteristic yellow-orange pigmentation in mycelial mats, which can be a significant component of the forest floor biomass.
- The genus *Piloderma* is known for forming extensive mycelial networks and cord-like structures in organic soil horizons.
- Contributes to the mat-forming ectomycorrhizal communities that can constitute 10-20% of the top 10 cm of forest soil.

## Ecological Role

*P. crocerum* contributes to the ectomycorrhizal community diversity in forest soils, where it plays a role in inorganic nutrient scavenging and mycelial network formation. Its extensive mycelial systems help explore large soil volumes for mineral nutrients, complementing the enzyme-based organic nutrient mobilization carried out by more enzyme-competent species. The functional diversity between *P. crocerum* and its co-occurring fungal partners supports the concept of complementary mycorrhizal niches within forest ecosystems.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Functional diversity**: Illustrates the range of enzyme competencies within ectomycorrhizal communities and their complementary roles.
- **Forest soil biology**: Mat-forming mycelial networks contribute significantly to soil structure and organic matter dynamics.
- **Mycorrhizal succession**: As a mid-to-late successional species, informs understanding of fungal community development during forest maturation.
- **Bioindicators**: Yellow-orange pigmentation makes field identification possible, useful for ecological surveys.
- **Ecosystem function**: Demonstrates that not all ectomycorrhizal fungi need high enzyme activity to contribute meaningfully to forest nutrient cycling.

## Enzyme Competency Classification

Based on the enzyme competency framework proposed by Read (1991b) and supported by Bending and Read (1996):

- **Protease activity**: Low - poor utilization of protein nitrogen sources.
- **Phosphatase activity**: Moderate - contributes to organic phosphorus mobilization.
- **Overall classification**: Relies more on efficient inorganic nutrient uptake rather than enzymatic degradation of organic compounds.
- Position: Intermediate between high enzyme-competent species (*Paxillus involutus*, *Suillus* spp.) and low enzyme-competent species (*Laccaria laccata*, *Pisolithus tinctorius*).

## Mat-Forming Ecology

The genus *Piloderma* is recognized for forming dense mycelial mats in forest floor organic horizons. These mats:

- Can constitute 10-20% of the top 10 cm of forest soil volume.
- Account for 45-55% of total soil biomass in some forest types.
- Create distinct microenvironments with modified nutrient cycling rates.
- Influence soil pH, moisture retention, and microbial community composition.
- Represent significant carbon sinks in forest ecosystems.

## Pigmentation and Identification

The bright yellow-orange pigmentation of *P. crocerum* mycelium is due to the production of secondary metabolites, possibly including:
- Xanthocillin-type compounds
- Pulvinic acid derivatives
- Other phenolic pigments

This pigmentation serves as a useful field marker for identifying *Piloderma*-colonized soil horizons during ecological surveys.
