---# Pyridoxal Phosphate

Pyridoxal phosphate is the main catalytically active coenzyme form of vitamin B6.
It is usually abbreviated PLP in biochemistry and enzymology literature.
The name pyridoxal 5'-phosphate emphasizes that the phosphate is attached to the 5-hydroxymethyl substituent of pyridoxal.
PLP is a small organic cofactor rather than a protein or mineral nutrient.
Its formula is C8H10NO6P, and its PubChem compound identifier is CID 1051.
Its neutral molecular weight is about 247.14 g/mol.
PubChem lists the IUPAC name as (4-formyl-5-hydroxy-6-methyl-3-pyridinyl)methyl dihydrogen phosphate.
The InChIKey is NGVDGCNFYWLIFO-UHFFFAOYSA-N.
PLP belongs to the family of vitamin B6 vitamers, which also includes pyridoxine, pyridoxal, pyridoxamine, and their phosphate forms.
In organisms, these vitamers can be interconverted through salvage and phosphorylation reactions.

## Structural Features

PLP contains a substituted pyridine ring.
The ring nitrogen can participate in electron withdrawal when protonated in an enzyme active site.
A phenolic hydroxyl group sits on the ring and contributes to hydrogen bonding and tautomeric chemistry.
A methyl group marks the vitamin B6 scaffold and helps distinguish PLP from other pyridine cofactors.
The aldehyde group is the reactive handle that forms imines with amino groups.
The phosphate ester is not usually the main catalytic center, but it strongly anchors PLP in enzymes.
The phosphate group makes PLP more polar than free pyridoxal.
The phosphate also supplies binding energy through ionic interactions with arginine, lysine, serine, threonine, and backbone amides.
In many PLP enzymes, the cofactor is buried deeply enough that its reactivity is controlled by the surrounding protein.
The conjugated aldehyde-pyridine system is the basis of PLP's electron-sink behavior.

## Biological Role

PLP is best known as a coenzyme for [[fish-amino-acid]] reaction intermediates.
PLP stabilizes carbanion-like character that would otherwise be too reactive in water.
The cofactor therefore lets enzymes break bonds next to an amino group under physiological conditions.
PLP enzymes are central to nitrogen redistribution, neurotransmitter biosynthesis, sulfur [[serotonin]] biosynthesis pathways.
Histidine decarboxylase uses PLP to make histamine.
Serine hydroxymethyltransferase uses PLP in one-carbon metabolism linked to folate chemistry.
Cystathionine beta-synthase and cystathionine gamma-lyase use PLP in sulfur [[knf-fish-amino-acid]] metabolism.
Glycogen phosphorylase contains PLP, although in that enzyme the phosphate group has a distinctive catalytic role.
