# Eucalyptus saligna

## Description

Eucalyptus saligna, commonly known as **Sydney blue gum** or **blue gum**, is a tall to very tall tree native to coastal and near-coastal areas of New South Wales and southern Queensland. It is a fast-growing, straight-trunked species that produces high-quality pink to red-brown timber. Widely planted internationally for timber and pulpwood, it is one of the most commercially important eucalypts globally.

## Classification

- **Family:** Myrtaceae
- **Genus:** Eucalyptus
- **Species:** E. saligna
- **Common names:** Sydney blue gum, blue gum

## Key Facts

- Tall to very tall tree, typically 30–55 m in height
- Smooth bark, grey to white, with a rough stocking at the base
- Native to coastal NSW and southern Queensland
- Fast-growing, producing straight, cylindrical trunks
- Timber is pink to red-brown, moderately durable, and easy to work
- One of the most widely planted eucalypts internationally for timber and pulp
- Prefers moist, well-drained soils in high-rainfall areas

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

- **Timber:** Premium hardwood for construction, flooring, joinery, and furniture (pages 20, 94)
- **Pulpwood:** Widely used in pulp and paper production internationally
- **Firewood:** Good quality firewood (pages 20, 94)
- **Shelter and windbreaks:** Fast growth makes it suitable for tall shelterbelts
- **Reforestation:** Widely used in plantation forestry worldwide, especially in Brazil, South Africa, and Australia
- **[[gadd-fungal-carbon-sequestration]]:** Fast [[soil-temperature-plant-growth-rate-doubling-rule]] provides rapid carbon capture

## Cultivation Notes

- Prefers moist, deep, well-drained soils
- Requires good rainfall (minimum 700 mm annually)
- Extremely fast-growing — can reach sawlog size in 20–30 years
- Intolerant of drought, frost, and saline conditions
- Best in coastal and near-coastal climates
- Susceptible to a range of [[mushroom-pests-and-diseases]] including psyllids and [[bloomfield-fungal-pathogens-and-parasites]]
- Best propagated from seed
