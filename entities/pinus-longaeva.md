## Pinus longaeva

## Overview

**Pinus longaeva** D.K. Bailey, the Great Basin bristlecone pine, is a small, extremely long-lived evergreen conifer in the family Pinaceae. It is native to the high mountains of the western [[dighton-fungal-drought-tolerance-plant-water-relations]] but provides little fire protection.

Needles occur in fascicles of five, 2.5–4 cm long, 0.8–1 mm wide, and densely clustered on short shoots. They are dark green to blue-green, with persistent fascicle sheaths. Individual needles may remain alive for 15–45 years, the longest needle retention of any pine.

Pollen cones are reddish, 6–10 mm long, in dense clusters. Seed cones are cylindrical to ovoid, 5–10 cm long and 3–4 cm wide when open. Each scale bears a long, fragile bristle (6–12 mm) that gives the species its common name. Cones mature over two growing seasons.

Seeds are small, 5–6 mm long, with a 10–15 mm wing. The seeds contain wing-loaded resin vesicles that may aid in wind dispersal at high elevations. Cone production is sporadic, with good seed years occurring at intervals of 3–8 years.

Reproductive maturity is reached at approximately 20–40 years of age. Pollination occurs in July–August at high elevations, with seed maturation requiring two growing seasons.

## Distribution and Habitat

Great Basin bristlecone pine occurs in scattered populations across the high mountains of eastern California, Nevada, and western Utah. The most famous populations grow in the White Mountains of California (Inyo National Forest) and on Mount Washington in eastern Nevada.

The species occupies extremely harsh, high-elevation sites from 2,300 m to 3,700 m above sea level. It grows on dry, rocky slopes with thin, calcareous or dolomitic soils derived from limestone parent material.

Climate at bristlecone sites is among the most severe experienced by any tree species. Mean annual precipitation ranges from 250–450 mm, mostly as winter snow. Summer temperatures rarely exceed 15 °C, while winter temperatures may fall below −35 °C. The growing season is approximately 6–10 weeks.

Soils are typically shallow (10–30 cm deep), well-drained, alkaline (pH 7.0–8.5), and very low in [[picea-pungens]], [[cervantes-mealybugs-scale-insect-control]] (*Chionaspis pinifoliae*) is one of the few herbivores that regularly affects the species.
