# Margrit Kennedy

## Description

Margrit Kennedy was a German economist and author whose work on interest-free financial
systems and ecological economics is cited by [[bill-mollison]] in *Permaculture: A Designer's
Manual*. Her 1987 manuscript *Toward an Ecological Economy: Money, Land and Tax Reforms*
argued that an interest-free financial system is the sole sustainable medium for exchange,
and that such a "no growth" fiscal system would encourage and preserve all natural resources.
Mollison draws on her analysis to explain how hidden interest costs permeate all goods and
services, transferring wealth from the poor to the rich.

## Classification

- **Category:** Person / Economist
- **Domain:** Permaculture -- Ecological economics, monetary reform, interest-free finance
- **Nationality:** German (West Germany at time of publication)
- **Key Work:** *Toward an Ecological Economy: Money, Land and Tax Reforms* (1987)

## Key Work

**Toward an Ecological Economy: Money, Land and Tax Reforms** (October 1987)
- Published from Ginsterweg 45, D3074, Steyerberg, West Germany
- Argues for interest-free monetary systems as essential for ecological sustainability
- Demonstrates how hidden interest costs are embedded in all goods and services
- Proposes that "no growth" fiscal systems preserve natural resources

## Core Arguments

### Interest-Free Financial Systems

Kennedy was convinced that:

1. Interest-free financial systems are the sole sustainable medium for exchange
2. A "no growth" fiscal system encourages and preserves all natural resources
3. Such a system would abolish the condition where the third world and the poor in
   affluent countries pay out more in interest than they receive in loans
4. It would prevent the growth of a minority wealthy elite
5. It would stabilize resource use

### Hidden Interest Costs

Kennedy's analysis revealed that interest costs are embedded in all goods and services:

- **Garbage collection:** ~12% of charges are hidden interest costs
- **Drinking water:** ~38% of charges are hidden interest costs
- **Social housing:** ~77% of charges are hidden interest costs

These gains flow to the rich or lenders, and the losses fall to the poor or borrowers,
even though the earth provides the wealth of their labor in the production sphere. This
wealth is removed by interest charges in the fiscal or circulation sphere.

### Wealth Transfer Mechanism

Kennedy identified that wealth used by unethical investment strategies is rapidly transferred
via global stock or money markets to most efficiently exploit the poor. This creates a
structural mechanism where:

- The production sphere creates real wealth through labor and natural resources
- The circulation sphere (fiscal system) extracts that wealth through interest charges
- The gross imbalance of wealth promotes "big" spending in capital-consuming but publicly
  paid investments (big dams, big power stations, big housing corporations)
- Governments refer the costs to the people
- Eventually only military (waste) funding can absorb all the misbegotten wealth
- Sensible small-scale and cost-effective solutions are prevented or actively discouraged

## Key Facts

- Published her key work in 1987 from Steyerberg, West Germany
- Demonstrated that 12-77% of basic service costs are hidden interest charges
- Argued that interest-free systems would stabilize resource use and prevent wealth
  concentration
- Her analysis showed that the poor pay more in interest than they receive in loans
- She proposed that even a single town, region, or nation implementing interest-free
  systems could put right many social and ecological ills
- Referenced Austrian towns of the 1930s depression as successful examples of local
  interest-free currency systems

## The Austrian Town Precedent

Kennedy's work references successful experiments by small Austrian towns during the 1930s
depression. These towns implemented local interest-free currency systems that:

- Stabilized local economies during the Great Depression
- Demonstrated that communities could thrive outside the conventional interest-based
  financial system
- Showed that as such systems strengthen and grow, regions can stabilize and pay for
  all their essential long-term resources

## The "5% Solution"

Kennedy noted that goods and services need only about a 5% maintenance cost to pay for
repairs and people to run the system. This stands in stark contrast to the interest-laden
costs that currently inflate prices by 12-77%. Any town, region, or nation setting up a
constant system at this base rate could put right many social and ecological ills.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Input Cost Reduction:** Interest-free financial systems would dramatically reduce the
  cost of cultivation infrastructure (greenhouses, sterilization equipment, irrigation
  systems), making food and [[stamets-stump-cultivation-mushroom-production-forestry]] more accessible to small-scale growers
- **Fair Market Access:** Without hidden interest costs inflating distribution chains,
  cultivators could sell produce at lower prices while earning more, as the interest
  overhead of middlemen is eliminated
- **Community-Scale Production:** Interest-free lending enables community [[duggar-mushroom-growing-history-and-methods]]
  cooperatives, shared-use processing facilities, and bulk purchasing systems without the
  debt burden that typically crushes small agricultural enterprises
- **Resource Valuation:** Kennedy's ecological economy framework supports valuing natural
  resources (soil, forest, [[teaming-with-microbes-tilling-damage-to-fungal-networks]]) as capital to be preserved rather than income
  to be extracted, aligning with sustainable cultivation practices
- **Land Access:** Interest-free land purchase or trust systems would make cultivation land
  accessible to new growers without the crushing mortgage burden that currently prevents
  many from entering agriculture
- **Processing and Value-Add:** The 77% hidden interest in social housing suggests similar
  hidden costs in food processing infrastructure; interest-free systems could dramatically
  reduce the cost of community canning kitchens, drying facilities, and mushroom
  processing operations
- **Research Funding:** Interest-free research grants could fund mycological research,
  cultivar development, and growing technique innovation without the profit pressure of
  interest-bearing institutional loans

## Broader Significance

Kennedy's work provides the economic theoretical foundation for many of the community
financial systems described by Mollison, including:

- LET systems (no interest on green dollars)
- Community credit unions (low or no interest loans)
- Revolving loan funds (modest interest, community benefit)
- [[local-currencies]] (non-interest bearing by design)

All of these systems implement, in practice, the interest-free principles that Kennedy
articulated in theory.
