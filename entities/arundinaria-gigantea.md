## Overview

**Arundinaria gigantea** (giant cane, river cane) is the only native bamboo species of North America, belonging to the grass family Poaceae.

It forms dense thickets known as "canebrakes" that were once a dominant feature of the southeastern United States landscape, covering millions of hectares along river floodplains.

Canebrakes were largely destroyed by agriculture, logging, and fire suppression during the 18th and 19th centuries, making extant stands critically rare.

The species is valued in permaculture and [[permplant-willows-salix-erosion-control-fodder]] capabilities, and [[mycofiltration-of-agricultural-runoff]] before they reach waterways.

Indigenous peoples of the Southeast used river cane extensively for basketry, fishing weirs, blowguns, arrows, and structural framing.

The Cherokee, Choctaw, Creek, and Seminole peoples developed sophisticated split-cane basketry traditions that persist to the present day.
