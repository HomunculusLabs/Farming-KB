# Arthrobotrys

## Description

*Arthrobotrys* is a genus of nematode-trapping (nematophagous) fungi in the
phylum Ascomycota. These fungi are best known for their ability to capture and
consume free-living nematodes in soil using specialized hyphal structures:
constricting rings and adhesive networks. The genus has attracted significant
attention for its potential as a biological control agent against parasitic
nematodes of both plants and livestock.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Orbiliomycetes
- **Order:** Orbiliales
- **Family:** Orbiliaceae
- **Genus:** *Arthrobotrys*

## Key Facts

- *Arthrobotrys* species produce constricting rings, specialized hyphal cells
  that snap shut around nematodes within milliseconds via a tactile-induced
  change in turgor pressure, effectively creating a biological noose.
- Some species also produce adhesive hyphal networks (three-dimensional nets)
  that trap nematodes on contact via sticky secretions.
- Once a nematode is trapped, the fungus secretes digestive enzymes, penetrates
  the nematode cuticle, and uses the nematode body as a source of nitrogen and
  other nutrients.
- Conidia (asexual spores) of *Arthrobotrys* can survive passage through the
  gastrointestinal tract of ruminant animals, germinating in dung where
  free-living stages of parasitic nematodes develop (Faedo et al., 1997).
- *Arthrobotrys* spores show lower survival through the ruminant gut compared
  to *Duddingtonia flagrans*, which may limit their efficacy as feed-through
  biocontrol agents.
- Kerry and Jafee (1997) highlighted that the success of *Arthrobotrys* as a
  biocontrol agent depends on conidial density in soil, relating to
  density-dependent parasitism dynamics.
- Viaene and Abawi (1998) found that *Arthrobotrys* and *Monacrosporium*
  species were effective at reducing populations of juvenile root-knot nematodes
  (*Meloidogyne* spp.) in organic soils.

## Ecological Role

- *Arthrobotrys* plays a significant role in regulating free-living nematode
  populations in soil, contributing to the natural biological control of both
  plant-parasitic and animal-parasitic nematode species.
- In pasture ecosystems, *Arthrobotrys* traps the free-living larval stages of
  intestinal nematodes (e.g., *Trichostrongylus colubriformis*) in dung,
  potentially reducing the infective load for grazing livestock.
- The genus is part of a broader guild of nematophagous fungi that includes
  *Duddingtonia*, *Dactylella*, *Nematoctonus*, and egg-parasitic genera like
  *Fusarium* and *Verticillium*.
- Kerry (1988) identified approximately 150 fungal species with nematode-
  parasitizing properties, placing *Arthrobotrys* within a large and diverse
  functional group of soil-borne biological control agents.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- *Arthrobotrys* is one of the most studied genera for biological control of
  plant-parasitic nematodes in agriculture, offering an alternative to chemical
  nematicides.
- Its trapping mechanisms (constricting rings and adhesive nets) have been
  extensively studied as models for understanding fungal morphogenesis and the
  evolution of predation in fungi.
- Limitations for practical deployment include variable conidial survival,
  sensitivity to environmental conditions (especially in tropical soils), and
  the need for high inoculum densities to achieve effective nematode control.
- Manueli et al. (1999) found reduced fungal recovery from feces of animals in
  tropical conditions, suggesting geographic limits to biocontrol efficacy.
- Research into *Arthrobotrys* contributes to the broader field of using fungal
  biological control agents in integrated pest management (IPM) programs.
