## Overview

**Neolitsea aurata** is an evergreen tree in the family Lauraceae, native to broadleaved forests of central and southern China, Taiwan, and the Ryukyu Islands of Japan.

The species is distinguished by its golden-indumented branchlets and leaf undersides, from which the specific epithet "aurata" (golden) is derived. It typically reaches eight to fifteen meters in height with a dense, rounded canopy.

The specific epithet refers to the distinctive golden-brown tomentum covering young shoots and leaf undersides. This pubescence is most conspicuous in spring on new growth and gradually diminishes as leaves mature.

In traditional Chinese medicine, the leaves and bark are used to treat colds, coughs, and rheumatic pain. The wood is valued locally for tool handles and construction, while the aromatic foliage has potential as a source of [[comparison-essential-oils-vs-neem-oil-vs-insecticidal-soap]].

*Neolitsea aurata* is increasingly planted as an ornamental tree in parks and gardens for its glossy foliage, golden pubescence, and tolerance of partial shade.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

**Taxonomy:** Kingdom Plantae → Order Laurales → Family Lauraceae → Genus *Neolitsea* → Species *N. aurata*.

The species was first described by William Hemsley in 1891 based on specimens from central China. Several varieties have been recognized, including *N. aurata* var. *paracicatricosa* and *N. aurata* var. *chekiangensis*, though modern treatments often collapse these into the species.

Molecular phylogenetic studies place *Neolitsea* within the Laureae clade of Lauraceae, closely related to *Litsea* and *Actinodaphne*. The genus is distinguished by its paniculate inflorescences and two-flowered cymules.

The tree is evergreen, reaching eight to fifteen meters tall with a trunk diameter of twenty to forty centimeters. Young branchlets are densely covered with golden-brown tomentose indumentum, becoming glabrescent with age.

The trunk is straight with a compact, rounded crown. Bark on mature trees is dark gray to brown, developing longitudinal fissures with age. Branching is sympodial, producing a dense foliage canopy.

Leaves are alternate, oblong to elliptic-oblong, eight to eighteen centimeters long and three to six centimeters wide. The upper surface is dark green and glabrous, while the lower surface is covered with golden-brown pubescence, giving the canopy a distinctive golden sheen in wind.

The leaf margin is entire to slightly undulate, with a thickened revolute margin. The midrib is prominent on the lower surface, with lateral veins in eight to twelve pairs.

Leaf texture is coriaceous, becoming leathery with age. The petiole is stout, eight to fifteen millimeters long, with the same golden-brown tomentum as the branchlets.

Inflorescences are axillary, paniculate, with the panicle shorter than the leaves. Flowers are small, pale yellow, approximately four millimeters in diameter, with six tepals in two whorls.

The fruit is a subglobose drupe, eight to ten millimeters in diameter, ripening to red or dark purple. The fruiting cupule is shallow and cup-shaped.

Flowering occurs from October through February, with fruit development completing by August to October of the following year. The extended flowering period overlaps with the dormant season of many associated tree species.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Neolitsea aurata* occurs in central and southern China, including Hubei, Hunan, Sichuan, Guizhou, Guangxi, Guangdong, Fujian, and Zhejiang provinces. It is also native to Taiwan and the Ryukyu Islands of southern Japan.

The species grows in broadleaved evergreen and mixed evergreen-deciduous forests at elevations from three hundred to two thousand meters. It is most abundant in montane forests with high humidity and well-drained soils.

Common canopy associates include *Castanopsis* species, *Quercus* species, and *Schima superba*. In Taiwan, *Neolitsea aurata* is a characteristic component of mid-elevation cloud forests.

It prefers acidic to neutral sandy loam or clay loam soils with abundant [[fukuoka-textdoc-composting-critique-futility-prepared-organic-matter]]. The climate in its range is warm temperate to subtropical, with mean annual temperatures of twelve to twenty degrees Celsius.

*Neolitsea aurata* is shade-tolerant in youth but requires increasing light for optimal growth as it matures. It regenerates well in canopy gaps and along forest edges.

*Neolitsea aurata* is frost-hardy to approximately minus ten degrees Celsius, making it one of the more cold-tolerant members of the genus. This [[coleman-hardy-winter-vegetables-cold-tolerance-mechanisms]] allows cultivation in [[oyster-mushroom-log-cultivation-temperate-regions-short-log-method]] outside its native range.

In cultivation, the species has demonstrated adaptability to a range of soil types, including slightly alkaline substrates. However, chlorosis may develop on calcareous soils with high pH.

## Chemistry and Ethnobotany

The essential oil of *Neolitsea aurata* contains a complex mixture of monoterpenes and sesquiterpenes. Major constituents include [[sabinene]], alpha-pinene, [[limonene]], [[beta-caryophyllene]], and germacrene D.

The leaves contain flavonoids, including [[quercetin]] and [[kaempferol]] derivatives, which exhibit antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activities in vitro. Tannins and lignans have also been isolated from bark and wood samples.

In traditional Chinese medicine, leaves are decocted for treating coughs, colds, and bronchitis. Bark preparations are applied externally for rheumatic pain and joint inflammation.

The golden pubescence on young shoots has been used as a dye material in some rural communities, producing yellowish-brown tones on natural fibers.

Wood is moderately hard and durable, used locally for tool handles, furniture components, and small construction projects. The timber is not commercially significant due to the species' relatively small size.

Fuelwood value is low, as the wood burns with considerable smoke. However, the aromatic bark and leaves have been traditionally burned as insect-repellent incense in rural households.

## Ecology

*Neolitsea aurata* provides food and habitat for forest fauna. The fruits are consumed by birds including bulbuls, thrushes, and white-eyes, which disperse seeds throughout forest landscapes.

Several species of Lepidoptera use *Neolitsea* foliage as larval food plants. The dense canopy provides nesting sites for small birds and shelter for understory invertebrates.
