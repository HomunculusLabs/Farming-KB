## Calendula officinalis (Pot Marigold)

## Overview

Calendula officinalis, commonly known as pot marigold, is an annual or short-lived perennial herb in the aster family (Asteraceae). Native to the Mediterranean basin and Southern Europe, it has been cultivated for over 1,000 years for its medicinal, culinary, ornamental, and dye-producing properties. One of the most extensively studied [[comparison-essential-oils-vs-neem-oil-vs-insecticidal-soap]] that confer potent anti-inflammatory, wound-healing, antimicrobial, and antioxidant properties.

The genus name *Calendula* derives from Latin *calendae* (first day of each month), referring to the plant's tendency to bloom nearly year-round in mild climates. The specific epithet *officinalis* indicates its long-standing inclusion in official pharmacopoeias. "Pot marigold" distinguishes it from *Tagetes* (French and African marigolds), which it resembles superficially but to which it is not closely related.

## Description

### Growth Habit and Morphology

Calendula officinalis grows 30–60 cm (12–24 inches) tall with a bushy, branching habit. Stems are angular, slightly pubescent, green to dark green. Leaves are oblong-lanceolate, 5–15 cm long, slightly hairy, and arranged alternately with a sticky surface.

### Flower Structure

The flower heads (capitula) are typically 4–7 cm in diameter, with two types of florets:
- **Ray florets**: 15–30 fertile, ligulate florets forming the outer ring. Colors range from pale yellow to deep orange, with many cultivated varieties showing bicolored, double, or crested forms.
- **Disk florets**: Numerous tubular florets forming the central disk, yellow to orange-brown.
The involucre consists of 2–3 rows of pubescent, linear-lanceolate bracts.
