## Overview

**Ramalina canariensis** is a fruticose to subfruticose lichen in the family Ramalinaceae, endemic to the Canary Islands and adjacent Macaronesian archipelagos. It forms distinctive pale green to yellowish-green, strap-shaped tufts on the branches of trees in laurel forest and coastal scrub habitats.

The species is restricted to oceanic environments with high humidity and relatively stable temperatures. It is one of several *Ramalina* species that characterize the lichen communities of Macaronesian laurel forests, a threatened UNESCO World Heritage ecosystem.

## Taxonomy and Morphology

*Ramalina canariensis* Steiner is classified in the order Lecanorales, family Ramalinaceae. The species was first described from the Canary Islands and has been reported from Madeira and the Azores, though some of these records may represent closely related taxa.

The thallus forms erect to pendulous tufts 3–10 cm long, arising from a small basal holdfast. Branches are flattened, strap-like, and 1–3 mm wide, with angular to perforate margins in well-developed specimens.

Color ranges from pale gray-green when dry to bright green when wet, reflecting the presence of usnic acid in the cortex. The surface is smooth to slightly wrinkled, with pseudocyphellae sometimes present along branch margins.

Apothecia are terminal or lateral, with a pale to brown disc 1–3 mm in diameter. The thallus margin of the apothecium is typically well-developed and crenulate. Ascospores are ellipsoid, colorless, and measure approximately 10–15 × 5–7 µm.

The photobiont is a trebouxioid green alga. The medulla is white and loosely structured, containing the primary [[juniperus-phoenicea]]* and other shrubs in areas influenced by oceanic moisture. These coastal populations tend to be smaller and more compact than those in humid laurel forest.

## Ecology and Symbiosis

*Ramalina canariensis* contributes to the rich epiphytic lichen communities that characterize Macaronesian laurel forests. These forests host some of the highest lichen diversities in Europe, with over 500 species documented in the Canary Islands alone.

## Conservation Status

*Ramalina canariensis* is considered vulnerable due to its restricted geographic range and dependence on laurel forest habitat. The laurel forests of the Canary Islands have been extensively cleared for agriculture and settlement over the past five centuries.

## Reproduction and Dispersal

*Ramalina canariensis* reproduces through both sexual and vegetative means. Apothecia produce ascospores that require capture of compatible algal cells for successful establishment on new substrates.

[[fungal-dispersal-limitation-biogeographic-barriers]] may contribute to its restricted distribution within the Canary Islands.

Wind transport of lightweight fragments between forest patches on different islands is possible during storm events, but long-distance oceanic dispersal is considered unlikely. Island biogeography patterns in Macaronesian lichens suggest most inter-island colonization occurred via rare chance events over geological timescales.
