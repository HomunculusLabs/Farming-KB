## Description

Muscazone is a secondary psychoactive compound found in [[allegro-amanita-muscaria-properties-and-pharmacology]] (fly agaric mushroom). It is formed from [[amanita-muscaria-and-ibotenic-acid-containing-fungi]] through ultraviolet light exposure and occurs in lesser concentrations than either ibotenic acid or muscimole. Muscazone may contribute to the overall intoxication effects of fly agaric mushrooms.

## Classification

- **Category**: Psychoactive compound / Ibotenic acid derivative
- **Chemical class**: Related to the isoxazole group
- **Source organism**: Amanita muscaria
- **Formation**: UV-mediated conversion of ibotenic acid

## Key Facts

- Found in [[amanita-muscaria-in-eastern-religions]] lesser concentrations than muscimole or ibotenic acid
- Formed from ibotenic acid through exposure to ultraviolet light
- May contribute to the overall intoxicating effects of the mushroom
- Less pharmacologically characterized than muscimole
- One of several derivatives formed during the drying and processing of fly agaric

## Relationship to Other Compounds

- **Ibotenic acid**: Parent compound; UV light converts it to muscazone
- **Muscimole**: The primary active compound, formed from ibotenic acid via decarboxylation
- **Muscarine**: A separate, trace compound once erroneously thought to be the main active principle
