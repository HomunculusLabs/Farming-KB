# Cladosporium herbarum

## Overview

cladosporium herbarum is one of the most common and widely distributed fungal species in the world, belonging to the phylum [[ascomycota-phylogeny-major-classes-lineages]]. It is a ubiquitous saprotrophic mold found in almost every terrestrial environment and is among the most abundant components of the airborne spora globally. The species is ecologically significant as a decomposer, a plant surface colonizer, a participant in phosphate solubilization consortia, and a remarkably radiation-tolerant organism.

## Taxonomy

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Phylum:** Ascomycota
- **Class:** Dothideomycetes
- **Order:** Capnodiales
- **Family:** Cladosporiaceae
- **Genus:** Cladosporium
- **Species:** C. herbarum

The genus Cladosporium is one of the largest genera of dematiaceous (dark-pigmented) hyphomycetes, containing hundreds of described species. Many Cladosporium species are cosmopolitan and occupy diverse ecological niches. The teleomorph (sexual stage) of C. herbarum has been linked to Mycosphaerella tassiana (or Davidiella tassiana under some classifications).

## Morphology and Identification

cladosporium herbarum is a dematiaceous fungus recognized by the following morphological features:

- **Colony:** On standard media such as potato dextrose [[agar]] or malt extract agar, colonies grow moderately rapidly, reaching 3 to 6 cm in diameter within 14 days at 25 degrees Celsius. Colonies are olive-green to dark olive-brown or nearly black, with a velvety to velvety-felty surface texture. The colony reverse is similarly darkly pigmented.
- **Hyphae:** Septate, brown to dark brown, with smooth to slightly roughened walls.
- **Conidiophores:** Erect, arising from the substrate hyphae, more or less branched, brown to dark brown. Conidiophore nodes bear conidiogenous cells.
- **Conidia:** The most distinctive feature is the production of characteristic branching chains of conidia (ramoconidia). Primary conidia (ramoconidia) are large, ellipsoidal to lemon-shaped, and typically one- to three-celled, measuring 15 to 40 micrometers in length. Secondary conidia are smaller, one-celled, ellipsoidal, and measure 4 to 10 micrometers.
- **Dark pigmentation:** The dark coloration is due to the presence of melanin in the cell walls, which provides protection against ultraviolet radiation and other environmental stresses.
- **Teleomorph:** Pseudothecia (sexual fruiting bodies) are globose to subglobose, dark, containing bitunicate asci with ellipsoidal, hyaline, one-celled ascospores.

## Ecology and Habitat

cladosporium herbarum is one of the most cosmopolitan of all fungal species, found in virtually every terrestrial habitat on Earth:

- Air (as airborne spores, often the dominant component of outdoor air spora)
- Soil of all types (agricultural, forest, grassland, desert)
- Phyllosphere (leaf surfaces) of a vast range of plant species
- Dead and decaying plant material (leaves, stems, fruits, seeds)
- Stored food products and organic materials
- Building materials and indoor environments
- Extremes of environments including high-radiation sites

The species is particularly abundant during warm, humid conditions and is a major component of the atmospheric fungal spora in temperate regions during summer and autumn.

## Role in Ecosystem Processes

### Decomposition

cladosporium herbarum is a primary colonizer of dead plant material and plays a significant role in the early stages of decomposition. It readily colonizes senescing leaves on living plants and fallen litter, initiating the decomposition process. Its cellulolytic and pectinolytic enzyme systems break down plant cell wall components, releasing nutrients for subsequent microbial colonization and plant uptake.

### Phosphate Solubilization

A notable ecological function of cladosporium herbarum is its role in phosphate solubilization. As documented in Dighton (2003), Singh and Kapoor (1998) demonstrated that C. herbarum, in consortium with the arbuscular mycorrhizal fungus [[glomus-intraradices]] and the bacterium [[knf-hay-bacillus-disease-control]], enhanced [[phosphorus-availability]] to mung bean plants from rock phosphate. In this consortium, C. herbarum contributed to the solubilization of poorly available phosphorus sources, demonstrating the importance of microbial consortia in plant nutrition.

### Phyllosphere Colonization

As a common inhabitant of the phyllosphere (leaf surfaces), cladosporium herbarum contributes to the microbial ecology of above-ground plant surfaces. It participates in nutrient cycling on leaf surfaces, competes with other microorganisms for space and resources, and may influence the susceptibility of leaves to pathogen attack.

## Radiation Tolerance

cladosporium herbarum has demonstrated remarkable tolerance to ionizing radiation. Research at the Chernobyl Atomic Electric Station by Zhdanova and colleagues (2000), as cited in Dighton (2003), revealed the following:

- cladosporium herbarum was isolated from the walls of the reactor room, where radiation levels ranged from 1.5 to 25 mR per hour (lower zones) and 40 to 220 mR per hour (higher zones)
- Isolation frequency was 20.1 percent at lower radiation intensities (1.5 to 25 mR per hour)
- At higher radiation intensities (40 to 220 mR per hour), isolation frequency increased to 44.1 percent
- The species persisted in the presence of plutonium isotopes, americium-241, curium-244, and cesium-137

The increase in isolation frequency at higher radiation levels suggests that cladosporium herbarum is competitively favored under radiation stress, possibly due to the protective effects of its melanin pigmentation.

### Responses to Atmospheric Pollutants

Dighton (2003) also notes that cladosporium herbarum and related Cladosporium species were investigated for their sensitivity to atmospheric pollutants, particularly sulfur dioxide and ozone. Cladosporium species were found to be less common on leaf litter in fumigated environments, suggesting sensitivity to these pollutants. However, the specific effects varied depending on the litter substrate species.

## Interactions with Other Organisms

### Plant Interactions

cladosporium herbarum is primarily a saprotroph on plant surfaces but can act as a weak pathogen under certain conditions, particularly on stressed or damaged plant tissue. It has been associated with leaf spots and fruit rots on various hosts, though its pathogenic activity is generally secondary.

### Microbial Consortia

As demonstrated by the Singh and Kapoor (1998) study, cladosporium herbarum functions effectively as part of microbial consortia that enhance plant nutrition. Its synergistic interactions with [[mycorrhizal-fungi]] and phosphate-solubilizing bacteria highlight the importance of multi-trophic microbial interactions in ecosystem processes.

### Allergen Production

cladosporium herbarum is one of the most important fungal allergens, producing spores that trigger allergic reactions in sensitive individuals. It is a major cause of fungal allergy and asthma, particularly during peak spore seasons in late summer and autumn.

### Indoor Environments

cladosporium herbarum is one of the most common fungi found in indoor environments. It colonizes damp building materials, wallpaper, textiles, and food products. Its presence indoors is associated with:

- Moisture problems such as leaks, condensation, and high humidity
- Poor ventilation in buildings
- Water-damaged construction materials

Indoor exposure to Cladosporium spores has been linked to respiratory symptoms, allergic reactions, and exacerbation of asthma in sensitized individuals. The species is therefore an important consideration in indoor air quality assessment and building health management.

### Seasonal Dynamics

The atmospheric concentration of cladosporium herbarum spores shows pronounced seasonal patterns:

- Spore counts are typically lowest during winter months
- Spore concentrations increase in spring and peak during late summer and early autumn
- Spore release is triggered by changes in humidity and temperature
- Rainfall events can cause rapid spore discharge, leading to atmospheric spikes

These seasonal dynamics are important for understanding allergic disease patterns and for timing of antifungal treatments in agricultural settings.

## Economic and Practical Significance

- **Allergy and health:** As a major airborne allergen, cladosporium herbarum has significant public health implications, particularly for individuals with respiratory conditions.
- **Agriculture:** The species contributes to both beneficial (phosphate solubilization) and detrimental (post-harvest decay, plant disease) processes in agricultural systems.
- **Bioremediation:** Its radiation tolerance and ability to colonize contaminated environments suggest potential applications in bioremediation.
- **Food spoilage:** C. herbarum can cause spoilage of stored food products, particularly fruits and vegetables, under cool, damp storage conditions.
- **Biodeterioration:** The species contributes to the biodeterioration of building materials, textiles, and other organic substrates.

## Research Notes

Dighton (2003) references cladosporium herbarum in multiple contexts: as a component of phosphate-solubilizing microbial consortia (with Glomus fasciculatum and Bacillus circulans), as a radiation-tolerant species from Chernobyl, and as a component of phyllosphere fungal communities affected by atmospheric pollution. These diverse references underscore the ecological versatility and ubiquity of this species across a range of ecosystem processes.
