## Overview

**Nepenthes villosa** is a spectacular tropical pitcher plant endemic to Mount Kinabalu and Mount Tambuyukon in Sabah, Malaysian Borneo. It is one of the most iconic and sought-after species in the genus, renowned for its large, heavily ridged pitchers covered in dense, shaggy hairs.

The species is restricted to ultramafic substrates at elevations between 2300 and 3250 m, making it one of the most altitude-restricted Nepenthes species. It grows in subalpine scrub and mossy forest, where persistent cloud cover and cool temperatures create a perpetually humid environment.

N. villosa is closely related to **[[nepenthes-rajah]]**, and the three species sometimes grow in proximity on Mount Kinabalu. The distinctive pitcher morphology of N. villosa, with its highly developed peristome ridges and dense indumentum, makes it unmistakable in the field.

Despite its fame among carnivorous plant enthusiasts, N. villosa is considered vulnerable due to its extremely restricted geographic range. [[fungal-biosorption-of-heavy-metals-by-filamentous-fungi]] such as nickel and chromium, which most plants cannot tolerate.

Habitat types include subalpine scrubland, mossy upper montane forest, and exposed ridgelines above the [[nepenthes-lowii]]** at slightly lower elevations and **[[nepenthes-rajah]]** in mid-elevation sites, forming a vertical zonation pattern.

Several species of invertebrates inhabit the pitchers of N. villosa as part of the pitcher-organism food web. These include mosquito larvae, midge larvae, and various protozoa that feed on decomposing prey. Some of these organisms may be obligate inhabitants.

Mutualistic relationships with ants have been documented for some Nepenthes species, where ants feed on nectar and fallen prey without being trapped. Similar relationships may exist for N. villosa, though specific studies are lacking.

Mount Kinabalu is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, providing legal protection for N. villosa and its habitat. The species is also protected under Malaysian law, and collection from the wild is prohibited without permits.
