## Description

*Acacia tortilis*, the **umbrella thorn acacia**, is a iconic dryland tree of
Africa and the Middle East, used in permaculture systems as a **barrier plant**,
**nitrogen fixer**, and **soil stabiliser**. Mollison recommends it as one of
four ideal species for constructing bomas (thorn fences) to corral livestock.
Its distinctive flat-topped canopy provides dappled shade in arid landscapes.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Family**: Fabaceae (legume family)
- **Subfamily**: Mimosoideae
- **Genus**: *Acacia* (sensu lato) / *Vachellia* (recent reclassification)
- **Species**: *A. tortilis*
- **Common names**: Umbrella thorn, Israeli babool
- **Growth form**: Tree, 4–15 m tall with spreading flat crown

## Key Facts

- Native to arid and semi-arid regions of Africa, the Arabian Peninsula, and
  the Middle East.
- Iconic **flat-topped (umbrella-shaped) canopy** casts dappled shade, allowing
  understorey growth.
- Produces both long straight thorns and short hooked thorns (dimorphic),
  creating extremely dense, impenetrable branches.
- **Nitrogen-fixing**: root nodules host *Rhizobium* bacteria, enriching soil
  with fixed atmospheric nitrogen.
- Produces coiled pods that are nutritious fodder for livestock and wildlife.
- Extremely drought-tolerant; deep taproot system accesses groundwater.
- Key species in **thorn savanna** and **dryland agroforestry** systems.
- Used traditionally for fuelwood, construction, fodder, and medicine.

## Permaculture Functions

- **Barrier plant / boma construction**: Dense thorny branches for livestock
  enclosures and garden protection.
- **Nitrogen fixation**: Enriches soil for interplanted crops.
- **Soil stabilisation**: Deep roots prevent erosion in arid landscapes.
- **Fodder production**: Pods provide high-protein livestock feed.
- **Shade**: Dappled canopy allows understorey crop growth.
- **Fuelwood**: Dense wood burns hot and clean.

## Relevance to Cultivation / Mycology

- **Mycorrhizal associations**: Forms extensive ectomycorrhizal and
  [[mycorrhizal-networks]] connect tree and crop roots.
