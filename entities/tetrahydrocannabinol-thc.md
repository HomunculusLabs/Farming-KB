# Tetrahydrocannabinol (THC)

**Type:** Chemical / Phytocannabinoid
**Category:** Major [[blesching-cannabis-cannabinoid-terpene-entourage-effect]] (primary psychoactive)
**Also known as:** THC, delta-9-THC, dronabinol (synthetic isomer),
Marinol

## Overview
Tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) is the primary psychoactive constituent of
cannabis, responsible for the plant's mind-altering effects. First
isolated and its chemical structure discovered in 1964 by Israeli
scientists Raphael Mechoulam and Yechiel Gaoni, THC is the most studied
cannabis compound. It binds relatively equally to both CB1 and CB2
cannabinoid receptors, initiating simultaneous changes in the central
nervous system, immune system, and autonomic nervous system.

## Key Details

### Chemical Properties
- Molecular formula: C21H30O2
- Primary psychoactive component of cannabis
- Binds relatively equally to CB1 and CB2 receptors
- Exists as THC-acid (THCA) in raw, fresh cannabis
- THCA is non-psychoactive; becomes THC through decarboxylation
- Heat, drying, and UV light promote decarboxylation
- Content decreases over time with exposure to light and heat

### Mechanism of Action
THC mimics anandamide, the body's endogenous cannabinoid, binding to
both CB1 and CB2 receptors. CB1 activation in the central nervous system
produces psychoactive effects, while CB2 activation modulates immune
responses. THC also causes bronchodilation (enlargement of airways) and
stimulates appetite through hypothalamic pathways.

### Therapeutic Applications
- **Cardiovascular:** THC may protect the heart from damage and mitigate
  infarction damage. Reduces heart attack size in mice; protects heart
  cells against hypoxia damage via nitric oxide induction; neuroprotective
  via CB1.
- **Appetite stimulation:** FDA-approved (as Dronabinol/Marinol) for
  AIDS-related anorexia and cachexia. Causes weight gain and increases
  walking distance in COPD patients.
- **Anti-emetic:** Approved for nausea and vomiting from chemotherapy.
- **Anti-cancer:** Reduces human breast [[blesching-cannabis-apoptosis-and-cancer-cell-death]] proliferation through
  CB2 activation; induces apoptosis. THC and CBN retard [[psk-breast-lung-cancer-clinical-trials]] cell
  growth and reduce tumor size.
- **Antibacterial:** Potent activity against MRSA strains alongside other
  major cannabinoids.
- **Neurological:** Treatment of neurological disorders and PTSD
  (FDA-approved indications via synthetic formulations).

### Synthetic Pharmaceutical Forms
- **Dronabinol (Marinol):** FDA-approved synthetic THC isomer
- **Nabilone (Cesamet):** Synthetic cannabinoid for nausea/vomiting
- **Sativex:** Plant-derived THC:CBD tincture (not pure synthetic)

### Strain and Form Considerations
- Sativa strains: higher THC to CBD/CBN ratio (stimulating, uplifting)
- Indica strains: lower THC to CBD/CBN ratio (sedating, relaxing)
- Indoor-grown cannabis tends to have higher THC than outdoor crops
- Fresh cannabis contains non-psychoactive THCA
- Raw THCA has unique anti-inflammatory properties (inhibits TNF-alpha)
  without producing intoxication

## Relevance
THC is the cornerstone of medical cannabis pharmacology and the most
extensively researched phytocannabinoid. Its discovery in 1964 by
Mechoulam and Gaoni opened the field of cannabinoid science. THC's dual
CB1/CB2 [[tihkal-receptor-binding-serotonin]] makes it a uniquely versatile therapeutic
agent, though its psychoactive properties limit dosing in some patient
populations. The [[cannabis-terpene-entourage-effect-profiles]] between THC and other cannabinoids
(particularly CBD) is a key principle in whole-plant cannabis medicine.
