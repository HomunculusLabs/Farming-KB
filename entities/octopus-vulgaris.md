# Common Octopus (*Octopus vulgaris*)

## Overview

*Octopus vulgaris* is a widely distributed cephalopod mollusk found
throughout temperate and tropical coastal waters of the Atlantic,
Mediterranean, and Indo-Pacific. It is the most studied octopus species and
a [[plant-olfaction-chemical-sensing]].

The body lacks both internal and external shells, a defining feature of
octopods. The only hard structure is a chitinous beak resembling a
parrot's beak, used for crushing prey. A radula (toothed tongue) assists
in processing food. The ink sac produces melanin-based ink deployed as a
smokescreen against predators.

The nervous system contains approximately 500 million neurons, with roughly
two-thirds distributed among the arms. This decentralized organization
allows each arm to exhibit semi-autonomous sensory-motor control. The eyes
are camera-type with horizontal slit pupils and are among the most
sophisticated visual systems in invertebrates.

## Distribution and Habitat

*Octopus vulgaris* occupies coastal waters from the intertidal zone to
approximately 200 meters depth. In the Atlantic, it ranges from the British
Isles and Norway south to Senegal, and across to the Caribbean and Gulf of
Mexico. Mediterranean populations are particularly well-studied and
commercially significant. Indo-Pacific populations extend from East Africa
to Japan and Australia.

The species prefers rocky bottoms, coral reefs, seagrass beds, and areas
with abundant crevices or dens. Juveniles inhabit shallow subtidal zones,
migrating to deeper water as they mature. Preferred substrates provide both
shelter for den construction and access to productive foraging grounds.

### Den Construction

Octopuses are sedentary hunters that construct and defend dens, typically
in rock crevices, abandoned shell cavities, or excavated burrows. Dens
are identified by accumulated middens of discarded shells and carapaces.
Individuals may occupy the same den for weeks to months, modifying the
entrance with rocks and shells.
