## Description

**Ceratocystis lariciola** is a fungal pathogen in the family Ceratocystidaceae that causes dieback and death of larch trees (Larix spp.). It is vectored by the larch bark beetle ([[ips-cembrae]]) and represents one component of a two-pathogen disease complex affecting larch in European forests.









## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Fungi
- **Phylum**: [[ascomycota]]
- **Class**: Sordariomycetes
- **Order**: Microascales
- **Family**: Ceratocystidaceae
- **Genus**: Ceratocystis
- **Species**: C. lariciola









## Key Facts

- Transmitted to larch trees by the bark beetle Ips cembrae
- Causes dieback and death of larch trees, with severity dependent on insect density
- Redfern (1989) described its interaction with the bark beetle vector
- Shares larch host trees with [[amylostereum-chailletii]] (vectored by [[urocerus-gigas]])
- Disease severity depends on interactions between the two insect vectors and their respective fungal pathogens









## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

C. lariciola exemplifies the bark beetle-fungal pathogen vector system, an important fungal-insect interaction in forestry pathology. For mycologists, it demonstrates how fungal pathogens can be specialized for insect-vectored dispersal, producing sticky spores adapted for attachment to insect bodies. The density-dependent disease pattern illustrates epidemiological thresholds relevant to fungal disease management.









## Ecological Role

C. lariciola contributes to natural disturbance dynamics in European larch forests by killing stressed or weakened trees. Its dependence on bark beetle vectors creates a linkage between insect population dynamics and fungal disease incidence. The pathogen initiates wood decomposition in killed trees, contributing to nutrient cycling.
