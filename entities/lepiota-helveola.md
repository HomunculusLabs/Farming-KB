# Lepiota helveola (Poisonous Lepiota)

## Description

Lepiota helveola is a small, rare species of Lepiota with brownish-pink colouration. It belongs to a group of small Lepiota species that are brownish with an admixture of pink, all of which are rare in Britain and whose specific differences remain uncertain. One or more of these species cause symptoms resembling mild Amanita phalloides poisoning.

## Classification

- **Kingdom:** Fungi
- **Division:** Basidiomycota
- **Class:** Agaricomycetes
- **Order:** Agaricales
- **Family:** Agaricaceae
- **Genus:** Lepiota
- **Species:** L. helveola

## Morphology

The cap is rounded then flattened, sometimes with a slight central prominence. At first it is covered with an ochre-brown cuticle which becomes flushed with pink or lilac-colour, especially in damp weather. The cuticle tears into numerous scales except on the disk, showing a white or pinkish surface between them. The gills are white, then cream-coloured, crowded and have a fimbriate (fringed) edge. The stem is cylindrical, hollow, concolorous with the cap and covered with silky fibrils. The ring is in the form of an annular bracelet. Average cap width is approximately 1.25 inches; stem height about 1.5 inches; stem width about 0.25 inches.

## Key Facts

- Rare species in Britain, with ongoing taxonomic uncertainty about species boundaries within the group
- Contains toxins that produce symptoms similar to mild Amanita phalloides poisoning
- Cap flushes pink to lilac in damp conditions and breaks into scales
- Flesh is white, often becoming slightly pinkish when cut
- Has a pleasant smell and a sourish taste
- Grows among grass in autumn

## Toxicity

Lepiota helveola belongs to the same poisoning category as Amanita phalloides — those containing substances which, after a long incubation period, bring about the degeneration of the cells of the body. While it is classified among the deadly poisonous species, Ramsbottom notes it is "much less dangerous" than Amanita phalloides itself. Symptoms resemble mild phalloides poisoning, with a delayed onset of gastrointestinal distress and potential organ effects.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Taxonomic importance:** Represents a poorly understood group of small Lepiota species where specific boundaries remain uncertain
- **Toxicology:** Illustrates that the amatoxin-type poisoning mechanism is not limited to the genus Amanita
- **Foraging hazard:** Small size and rarity make it an unlikely candidate for accidental collection, but its presence in grassland habitats necessitates caution
- **Identification challenge:** The pinkish-brown coloration and scaly cap can superficially resemble other small Lepiota species, some of which may be edible
- **Research significance:** The uncertain species boundaries within this group highlight the need for modern molecular systematic studies

## Habitat and Ecology

Grows among grass in autumn. Rare in Britain. Part of a complex of small brownish-pink Lepiota species whose ecological roles and distributions are poorly documented due to taxonomic confusion.

## Historical Notes

Described and illustrated by John Ramsbottom in "Poisonous Fungi" (1945) on Plate 6 alongside Lepiota cristata. Ramsbottom provided an inclusive description covering multiple related species due to the prevailing taxonomic uncertainty.
