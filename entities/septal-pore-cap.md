## Description

The septal pore cap (SPC), also known as the parenthosome, is a membrane-bound organelle associated with the septal pore apparatus in basidiomycete fungi of the subclass Hymenomycetes. It sits atop the barrel-shaped swelling of the cell wall that surrounds the septal pore, functioning as a regulatory structure that controls the passage of materials between adjacent hyphal compartments. The SPC is a defining ultrastructural feature of the Hymenomycetes and plays a critical role in maintaining the balance between intercellular communication and cellular isolation.

## Classification

- **Category**: Organelle / Septal-pore-associated structure
- **Taxonomic distribution**: Hymenomycetes (a major group within Basidiomycota)
- **Alternative names**: Parenthosome
- **Related structures**: Woronin body (Euascomycetes), Woronin-body-like organelles (Urediniomycetes)

## Key Facts

- The SPC was first described by Bracker (1967), who termed it the parenthosome.
- It consists of an electron-dense core associated with a unit membrane, positioned like a cap over the septal pore apparatus.
- Electron microscopy reveals continuity between the SPC membrane and the nuclear envelope, suggesting the SPC is a specialized domain of the endoplasmic reticulum (Moore, 1975; Muller et al., 1998).
- The SPC is associated with cytoplasmic filaments and an osmiophilic material that appears to occlude the septal pore (Moore and Marchant, 1972).
- In Hymenomycetes, the septum features a barrel-shaped swelling of the cell wall surrounding the pore, distinguishing it from simpler pore structures found in other fungal groups.
- The SPC can rapidly seal the septal pore in response to damage, preventing catastrophic cytoplasmic loss from the hyphal compartment.

## Structural Features

- **Membrane**: Unit membrane continuous with the endoplasmic reticulum/nuclear envelope
- **Core**: Electron-dense material forming the body of the cap
- **Associated filaments**: Cytoplasmic filaments extending from the cap into the surrounding cytoplasm
- **Occlusive material**: Osmiophilic material that can block the septal pore
- **Position**: Sits atop the dolipore septum, which has a barrel-shaped swelling of the cell wall

## Phylogenetic Significance

The SPC is diagnostic of the Hymenomycetes, one of the major groups within the Basidiomycota that includes many mushroom-forming fungi. Its presence correlates with:

- Complex multicellular fruiting body production
- Sophisticated hyphal differentiation
- Advanced nutrient transport systems through corded mycelia

The SPC represents an independent evolutionary solution to the problem of septal pore regulation in [[basidiomycetes]], analogous but not homologous to the Woronin bodies found in ascomycetes.

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

Understanding the septal pore cap is important for cultivators working with basidiomycete species (which includes most cultivated mushrooms):

- **Damage response**: The SPC ability to rapidly seal septal pores protects the colony from cytoplasmic loss when hyphae are damaged during handling or substrate colonization.
- **Intercellular transport**: SPC regulation of septal pore patency directly influences nutrient and signal translocation through the mycelial network.
- **Species identification**: SPC ultrastructure is used as a taxonomic character in fungal classification.
- **Colony resilience**: The SPC contributes to the remarkable resilience of basidiomycete [[fungal-mycelial-networks-nutrient-translocation]] to physical disruption.
