# Bufotenin

**Bufotenin** (5-hydroxy-*N*,*N*-dimethyltryptamine, abbreviated **5-HO-DMT**) is a naturally occurring [[reversed-phase-hplc-tryptamine-alkaloid-quantification-methodology]] found in a wide range of organisms, including several species of toads, mushrooms, and plants. It is a close structural analog of both serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine, 5-HT) and the classical psychedelic *N*,*N*-dimethyltryptamine (DMT), differing from DMT by the addition of a single hydroxyl group at the 5-position of the indole ring.

## Chemical Profile

- **IUPAC Name:** 3-[2-(dimethylamino)ethyl]-1H-indol-5-ol
- **Molecular Formula:** C₁₂H₁₆N₂O
- **Molecular Weight:** 204.27 g/mol
- **CAS Number:** 487-93-4
- **SMILES:** CN(C)CCC1=CNC2=C1C=C(O)C=C2
- **Appearance:** White to off-white crystalline solid

Bufotenin is a Schedule I controlled substance in the United States and is similarly regulated in many other jurisdictions. It is not scheduled at the international level under the UN Convention on Psychotropic Substances, which has created inconsistent regulatory treatment across nations.

The compound was first isolated in 1934 by the Austrian chemist Handovsky from the skin secretions of the European toad (*Bufo bufo*), from which it derives its common name (from *Bufo*, the toad genus, combined with the tryptamine-derived suffix *-tenin*). The compound was subsequently identified in the venom of the Colorado River toad (*Incilius alvarius*, synonym *[[tryptamine-family-overview]] tree:

- **Serotonin (5-HT):** Bufotenin differs from serotonin only by the presence of two methyl groups on the terminal amine. This small structural change converts a neurotransmitter into a potent psychedelic.
- **DMT (N,N-dimethyltryptamine):** Bufotenin differs from DMT by the addition of a single hydroxyl group at the 5-position of the indole ring. This addition dramatically changes the compound's polarity, pharmacokinetics, and pharmacology.
- **5-MeO-DMT:** Another important related compound found in the same toad species, differing from bufotenin by having a methoxy group instead of a hydroxyl group at the 5-position.
- **Psilocin (4-HO-DMT):** The active metabolite of psilocybin, with the hydroxyl at the 4-position rather than the 5-position.

The 5-hydroxyl group is the critical structural feature that makes bufotenin significantly more polar and water-soluble than DMT, with profound implications for its pharmacokinetic behavior.

## Pharmacology

Bufotenin acts primarily as a **serotonin 5-HT₂A receptor agonist**, the same receptor primarily responsible for the psychedelic effects of psilocybin, LSD, and DMT. However, it also shows significant affinity for multiple other receptor subtypes:

- **5-HT₁A** — partial agonist; may contribute to anxiolytic and calming effects
- **5-HT₂B** — agonist; the clinical significance of this activity is not well characterized
- **5-HT₂C** — agonist; may contribute to appetite suppression and anxiety effects
- **5-HT₃** — the only ionotropic [[tihkal-receptor-binding-serotonin]] profile distinguishes bufotenin from DMT and contributes to its distinct subjective effects and side-effect profile.

### Pharmacokinetics

The 5-hydroxyl group dramatically affects bioavailability. Bufotenin does not readily cross the blood-brain barrier when taken orally due to its high polarity and poor lipid solubility. Oral ingestion of bufotenin-containing materials is generally considered ineffective for producing central psychedelic effects and instead produces primarily peripheral somatic effects including nausea, vasoconstriction, facial flushing, and a sensation of bodily heaviness.

Effective psychoactive use has primarily been documented via:
- **Insufflation** (snorting) — effective but irritating to nasal membranes
- **Smoking/vaporization** — the most effective route, producing rapid-onset effects
- **Intravenous injection** — effective but not traditionally used

When vaporized and inhaled, bufotenin produces rapid-onset psychedelic effects within seconds, peaking at 5–10 minutes and lasting approximately 15–30 minutes. The subjective profile is often described as more visually colorful than DMT but less cognitively immersive, with prominent geometric patterns, color enhancement, and a dreamy, sedative quality. Some users report significant physical discomfort including chest pressure, facial warmth, and nausea during the onset phase.

## Natural Sources

### Animal Sources
- **Incilius alvarius** (Colorado River toad / Sonoran Desert toad) — the most concentrated natural source, with bufotenin constituting up to 15% of dried venom alongside 5-MeO-DMT
- **Bufo bufo** (common toad) and various *Bufo* species — skin parotoid gland secretions
- **Rana species** — various frog species worldwide
