## Taxonomy and Morphology

- **Kingdom:** Plantae
- **Order:** Malpighiales
- **Family:** Linaceae
- **Genus:** Linum
- **Species:** L. usitatissimum L.

The plant grows 60–120 cm tall as a slender, unbranched or sparsely branched stem. Stems are cylindrical, glabrous, and green to blue-green, with a waxy cuticle. The bast fiber strands run the length of the stem just beneath the epidermis.

Leaves are alternate, lanceolate to oblong, 1–3 cm long and 2–5 mm wide, with a single prominent midrib. They are grayish-green with entire margins.

Flowers are 1.5–2.5 cm in diameter, borne in loose terminal clusters (cymes). Each flower has five petals, usually bright blue but occasionally white, pink, or red in ornamental varieties. The fruit is a spherical boll (capsule) 5–9 mm in diameter, containing 8–10 glossy, flattened seeds.

Two main cultivar groups are recognized: **fiber flax** (tall, slender stems, few branches, grown for fiber) and **oilseed flax** (shorter, more branched, larger seed yield). [[dmt-containing-plants-united-states-halpern]], and Ethiopia. It grows in USDA hardiness zones 3–9.

Flax prefers cool, moist growing conditions with well-drained loamy soils. Optimal growth occurs at 15–20°C during the [[aact-tannins-terpenes-phenolic-compounds-wood-composting-ingham]] (ferulic acid, p-coumaric acid).

The seed coat mucilage forms a gel in water, giving flaxseed its characteristic thickening and emulsifying properties. This mucilage layer also slows digestion, contributing to the seeds' low glycemic impact.

## Edible and Medicinal Use

Flaxseed is consumed whole, ground, or as oil.

Ground flaxseed is nutritionally superior to whole seeds, as the hard seed coat resists digestion. Recommended daily intake for cardiovascular benefits is 1–2 tablespoons (10–20 g) of ground flaxseed.

Clinical evidence supports several health benefits.

Regular flaxseed consumption lowers LDL cholesterol by 5–18% and blood pressure by 2–10 mmHg. The lignan content is associated with reduced risk of hormone-sensitive cancers (breast, prostate) in epidemiological studies, though randomized controlled trials are inconclusive.

Flaxseed mucilage has been used as a demulcent and laxative in traditional medicine.

The seeds, soaked in water, form a soothing gel used for gastrointestinal irritation, sore throats, and skin inflammations. Externally, flaxseed poultices have been applied to boils, burns, and joint pain.

**Flaxseed oil** is rich in ALA but lacks the fiber, lignans, and protein of the whole seed.

It is used as a nutritional supplement, in oil painting (as a drying oil), and in industrial applications (linoleum, varnishes). The oil must be refrigerated and protected from light to prevent rapid oxidation.
