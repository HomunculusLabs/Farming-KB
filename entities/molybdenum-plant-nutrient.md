## Description

Molybdenum (Mo) is an essential plant micronutrient that plays a critical role in nitrogen fixation and nitrate reduction. It is a key component of the enzyme nitrogenase, which enables rhizobia bacteria in legume root nodules to convert atmospheric nitrogen into plant-available forms. Molybdenum deficiency particularly affects legumes and is most common in acid, leached soils.

## Classification

- **Category**: Essential plant micronutrient
- **Symbol**: Mo
- **Atomic number**: 42
- **Role**: Enzyme cofactor for nitrogenase and nitrate reductase
- **Critical function**: Nitrogen fixation in legume root nodules
- **Deficiency indicator**: Poor clover growth and establishment

## Key Facts

1. **Nitrogen fixation**: Molybdenum is essential for the nitrogenase enzyme that enables rhizobia bacteria to fix atmospheric nitrogen. Without adequate molybdenum, legumes cannot form functional nodules.
2. **Legume indicator**: The primary indicator of molybdenum deficiency is poor establishment and growth of clover and other legumes, particularly in peaty or coastal soils.
3. **[[mycorrhizal-networks|mycorrhizal-networks]] that trade carbon for nutrients.
- **Soil food web**: Functional nitrogen fixation, enabled by molybdenum, is the foundation of biological nitrogen cycling in permaculture systems.
- **Fungal nitrogen sources**: [[cobalt-plant-nutrient]] — Companion micronutrient with similar careful application needs
- [[rock-phosphate|Rock Phosphate]] — Phosphorus source needed alongside nitrogen fixation
