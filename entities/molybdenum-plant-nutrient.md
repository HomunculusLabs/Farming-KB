## Description

Molybdenum (Mo) is an essential plant micronutrient that plays a critical role in nitrogen fixation and nitrate reduction. It is a key component of the enzyme nitrogenase, which enables rhizobia bacteria in legume root nodules to convert atmospheric nitrogen into plant-available forms. Molybdenum deficiency particularly affects legumes and is most common in acid, leached soils.

## Classification

- **Category**: Essential plant micronutrient
- **Symbol**: Mo
- **Atomic number**: 42
- **Role**: Enzyme cofactor for nitrogenase and nitrate reductase
- **Critical function**: Nitrogen fixation in legume root nodules
- **Deficiency indicator**: Poor clover growth and establishment

## Key Facts

1. **Nitrogen fixation**: Molybdenum is essential for the nitrogenase enzyme that enables rhizobia bacteria to fix atmospheric nitrogen. Without adequate molybdenum, legumes cannot form functional nodules.
2. **Legume indicator**: The primary indicator of molybdenum deficiency is poor establishment and growth of clover and other legumes, particularly in peaty or coastal soils.
3. **[[soil-ph|soil-ph]] connection**: Molybdenum availability increases with soil pH. Acid soils (pH < 5.5) strongly lock up molybdenum, making it unavailable even when present.
4. **Cobalt companion**: Severe nitrogen deficiency in legumes may actually be cobalt deficiency rather than molybdenum deficiency — both are required for functional nitrogenase.
5. **Tiny quantities needed**: Application rates are extremely low. Sodium molybdate at approximately 10 g mixed with 5 kg of sand, then further diluted to 1/100 of this mixture per square metre every 10 years.
6. **Manganese antagonism**: High manganese levels can interfere with molybdenum uptake. If manganese is high, adding lime to balance the soil (one handful per square metre) is recommended.
7. **Foliar application**: On alkaline soils, molybdenum can be applied as a very dilute foliar spray at 1 g per 10 square metres every 10 years.

## Application Protocol

- **Source**: Sodium molybdate
- **Mixing**: 10 g sodium molybdate mixed thoroughly with 5 kg sand
- **Dilution**: Take 1/100 of the sand mixture (weigh it) per square metre
- **Frequency**: Every 10 years
- **Acid soils**: Add lime first to raise pH and improve natural availability
- **High manganese soils**: Add lime at one handful per square metre to balance

## Relevance to Cultivation and Mycology

- **Rhizobia symbiosis**: Molybdenum is the key micronutrient enabling the bacterial-fungal-plant nitrogen fixation triangle that underpins organic fertility systems.
- **Legume-mycorrhizal partnership**: Legumes with adequate molybdenum fix more nitrogen, supporting the [[mycorrhizal-networks|mycorrhizal-networks]] that trade carbon for nutrients.
- **Soil food web**: Functional nitrogen fixation, enabled by molybdenum, is the foundation of biological nitrogen cycling in permaculture systems.
- **Fungal nitrogen sources**: [[mycorrhizal-fungi|mycorrhizal-fungi]] benefit from the nitrogen-rich root exudates of well-nodulated legumes.
- **Compost quality**: Legume biomass from molybdenum-sufficient plants produces nitrogen-rich compost that feeds decomposer fungi.

## Practical Applications

Understanding molybdenum plant nutrient is essential for optimizing plant health and crop yields.
Proper diagnosis and treatment of mineral imbalances can significantly improve agricultural
productivity while reducing unnecessary fertilizer applications.

## Diagnostic Methods

Visual symptom identification, soil testing, and tissue analysis are the primary methods
for assessing plant nutrient status. Early detection prevents yield loss and allows for
timely corrective action through soil amendments or foliar sprays.

capacity, and microbial activity. Deficiencies manifest as characteristic visual symptoms
