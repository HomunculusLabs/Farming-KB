# Rorippa aquatica (Watercress)

## Description

Watercress (Rorippa aquatica, formerly Nasturtium aquaticum or Nasturtium officinale) is a fast-growing aquatic or semi-aquatic perennial plant in the brassica family. It is one of the most nutritious leaf vegetables known, thriving in shallow flowing water and pond margins, making it an ideal crop for permaculture aquaculture systems.

## Classification

- **Kingdom**: Plantae
- **Family**: Brassicaceae (mustard/cabbage family)
- **Genus**: Rorippa (formerly Nasturtium)
- **Species**: R. aquatica / R. nasturtium-aquaticum
- **Common names**: Watercress, yellowcress

## Key Facts

- Perennial aquatic plant growing in shallow, clean flowing water
- Rich in vitamins A, C, K, iron, calcium, and iodine
- One of the most nutrient-dense foods per calorie measured
- Propagates readily from stem cuttings placed in water
- Harvested by cutting stems; regrows rapidly from remaining nodes
- Thrives in cool, clean water with some flow or movement

## Relevance to Cultivation and Permaculture

Mollison lists watercress alongside Chinese water chestnut (Eleocharis dulcis), kangkong (Ipomoea aquatica), and taro (Colocasia esculenta) as a key shallow-pond crop:

### Shallow Pond Integration
- Grows in water 2-60 cm deep, ideal for the shallow margins of garden ponds
- Provides continuous leaf crop throughout growing season
- Tolerates the fluctuating water levels common in permaculture pond systems
- Coexists with frogs, small fish, and other aquatic organisms

### Water Quality Benefits
- Absorbs excess nutrients (nitrogen, phosphorus) from pond water
- Helps prevent algal blooms by competing for dissolved nutrients
- Oxygenates water during daylight hours through photosynthesis
- Can be used in biofiltration systems downstream of fish ponds

### Food System Integration
- Fresh leaves used in salads, sandwiches, and soups
- Can be fed to fish as supplementary green fodder
- Rich nutrient profile complements the protein-heavy fish production
- Multiple harvests per year from the same planting

### Economic Value
- Watercress commands premium prices as a specialty salad green
- Can be sold at farmers markets alongside fish and other pond products
- Low maintenance once established; primarily requires clean flowing water

## Cultivation Notes

- Plant stem cuttings in shallow water with some flow
- Prefers pH 6.5-7.5 and cool water (10-20 degrees C optimal)
- Can be invasive in natural waterways -- contain within pond systems
- Requires clean water for food safety; avoid contaminated sources
- Harvest regularly to prevent flowering and maintain tender growth

## Historical and Cultural Significance

Watercress has been consumed as food and medicine since antiquity. The Greek physician Hippocrates is said to have located his first hospital near a stream specifically to ensure a supply of fresh watercress for patients. Roman soldiers ate it to maintain vigour, and in Victorian England, watercress sandwiches were a working-class staple sold fresh in the streets of London.

In the context of permaculture pond culture, watercress represents the productive edge zone between aquatic and terrestrial systems. Its ability to grow in both saturated soil and shallow water makes it ideal for the pond margin design that Mollison advocates, where maximum "edge effect" is achieved through elongated, convoluted pond shapes that maximise the linear metres of productive margin per unit of pond surface area.

## Nutritional Significance

Watercress consistently ranks as one of the most nutrient-dense foods in scientific analyses. It scores highest among fruits and vegetables on the CDC's nutrient density index, providing exceptional concentrations of:
- Vitamin K (critical for blood clotting and bone health)
- Vitamin C (immune function and antioxidant)
- Vitamin A (vision and cellular health)
- Glucosinolates (cancer-protective compounds shared with other brassicas)
- Iron and calcium in bioavailable forms
