# Psilocybe coprophila

## Overview

**Psilocybe coprophila** is a small, dung-inhabiting mushroom in the family Hymenogastraceae. It was one of the species included in Bigwood and Beug's comprehensive 1992 survey of psilocybin and psilocin levels across Psilocybe species. Unlike many well-known psychoactive Psilocybe species, *P. coprophila* is considered to be weakly active or of uncertain psychoactive status.

## Taxonomy and Classification

- **Order:** [[spore-print]]:** Dark purplish brown to nearly black, characteristic of the genus.
- **Spores:** Ellipsoidal to subellipsoidal, smooth, with a germ pore.
- **Microscopic Features:** Cheilocystidia and pleurocystidia may be present; chrysocystidia are sometimes observed but are not prominent.

## Habitat and Distribution

*Psilocybe coprophila* is a coprophilous (dung-inhabiting) species, growing primarily on the dung of herbivorous mammals, particularly cattle and horses. It is found in:

- Temperate grasslands and pastures
- Agricultural fields where livestock graze
- Areas with rich deposits of manure

The species has been reported from [[psilocybin-north-america-pacific-northwest]], Europe, and parts of Asia. It fruits primarily in late summer through autumn, following periods of rain. Its preference for nutrient-rich dung substrates distinguishes it from the many wood-inhabiting Psilocybe species.

## Chemistry and Psychoactivity

*Psilocybe coprophila* was included in the Bigwood and Beug (1992) study examining psilocybin and psilocin concentrations across the genus. Analytical results indicated:

- **Psilocybin content:** Very low or below detectable limits in most collections
- **Psilocybin concentration:** Generally reported at less than 0.01% dry weight
- **Psilocin content:** Not detected or trace amounts only

The species is generally regarded as non-psychoactive or only minimally active due to its extremely low alkaloid content. This distinguishes it sharply from more potent coprophilous relatives such as [[psilocybe-cubensis]]** — Much larger, notably psychoactive, also grows on dung but far more robust
- **deconica coprophila** — Some authorities place this species in Deconica; non-psychoactive members of the former Psilocybe sensu lato
- **[[psilocybe-fimetaria]]** — Dung-associated species with somewhat higher reported alkaloid levels

## Ethnobotanical and Legal Status

*P. coprophila* has no known history of traditional or recreational use. Due to its negligible psychoactive alkaloid content, it has not been sought after by foragers or ethnobotanists. Nonetheless, in jurisdictions where all Psilocybe species are controlled by name, it may technically fall under the same legal restrictions as its more potent relatives.

## Cultivation

There is no established cultivation methodology for *P. coprophila*. Its coprophilous nature suggests it would require pasteurized herbivore dung as a primary substrate, but its low alkaloid content means there has been essentially no interest in cultivating it.
