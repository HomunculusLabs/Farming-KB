# Argillic Horizon

## Description

The argillic horizon is a diagnostic subsurface soil layer characterized by the accumulation of clay (an "Argillic skin") plus iron and silica cements that together build up an impermeable layer blocking root penetration and water movement. Mollison describes this layer as a product of natural soil-forming processes that normally takes 10,000 or more years, but which can be dramatically accelerated by deforestation and agricultural practices that mobilize salt and cause clay [[deflocculation]].

## Classification

- **Category:** Soil diagnostic horizon / pedogenic feature
- **Soil taxonomy:** Argillic horizon (Bt horizon in soil profile notation)
- **Domain:** Soil science, dryland management
- **Related terms:** Clay pan, hardpan, cemented "B" horizon, duripan

## Formation Process

### Natural Formation (10,000+ years)

1. Clay particles translocate from upper soil layers (eluviation)
2. Clay accumulates in the B horizon (illuviation)
3. Iron oxides and silica cement the clay layer over time
4. The resulting layer becomes increasingly impermeable to roots and water

### Accelerated Formation (decades)

Agricultural practices that accelerate argillic horizon formation:

1. **Deforestation** removes deep-rooted vegetation that maintained soil structure
2. **Salt mobilization** from rising groundwater increases clay solubility
3. **Clay deflocculation** — sodium ions displace calcium, dispersing clay particles
4. **Humus loss** from fire, cropping, and overgrazing removes the polysaccharide glues that maintain [[interceptor-banks]]:** Cut through the perched water table and divert water to streams
- **Ripping:** Mechanical fracturing of the hardpan (expensive, temporary)
- **Gypsum application:** Displaces sodium on clay particles, promoting flocculation and improved structure where pH > 8.5
- **Deep-rooted perennials:** Gradually improve soil structure through root channels (only viable after waterlogging is controlled)
- **[[bill-mollison]], *Permaculture: A Designer's Manual*, Chapter 11 (Dryland Strategies), pages 440–448.
