## Overview

**Gracilaria verrucosa** (Hudson) Papenfuss is a red marine macroalga in the family Gracilariaceae. It ranks among the most commercially important agarophytes worldwide, cultivated extensively for agar extraction used in food, microbiology, and biotechnology.

The species forms erect, bushy thalli reaching 20–40 cm in length. It attaches to substrates via a discoid holdfast and branches dichotomously to form dense, three-dimensional frond structures.

G. verrucosa has a [[psilocybe-southeast-asia-pacific]], Australia, and both coasts of the Americas.

The species inhabits the intertidal and shallow subtidal zones, typically from 0 to 10 meters depth. It grows on rocky substrates, shells, pebbles, and artificial structures such as pier pilings and aquaculture nets.

Optimal growth occurs in estuaries and sheltered bays with salinities of 20–35 ppt and temperatures of 15–28°C. The species is notably euryhaline, tolerating salinities as low as 10 ppt in brackish environments.

## Chemistry and Nutrition

The primary commercial compound is agar, a sulfated galactan comprising 20–35% of dry weight. Agar from G. verrucosa has lower gel strength than Gelidium-derived agar but a higher melting temperature.

Alkaline pretreatment with NaOH before extraction improves gel strength by removing sulfate groups, producing food-grade agar.
The agarose fraction is used in molecular biology for electrophoresis.

Dried Gracilaria contains approximately 20–30% protein, 1–3% lipids, 40–55% carbohydrates, and 15–30% minerals.
